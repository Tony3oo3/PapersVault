## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting degrades in real-world scenes due to transient distractors (pedestrians, vehicles, shadows), and existing distractor-removal methods that rely on semantic priors from vision foundation models suffer from two inherent limitations: semantic mismatch (general-purpose semantic regions do not align with the binary static/transient distinction) and semantic fragility (perceptual features produce unstable responses to minor appearance perturbations during 3DGS optimization, misclassifying static regions as transient).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260584/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (downsampled 8×) with known camera parameters, where training images are disturbed by transient distractors. It produces binary static/transient masks at the patch level (default 16×16 patches) that are applied as spatial filters in the 3DGS photometric loss, updated every 100 iterations after a 500-step warm-up. The final output is a distractor-free 3DGS scene renderable at novel viewpoints; evaluation uses clean (distractor-free) test images.

## Method

HPC introduces two complementary designs. First, a patch-wise classification approach replaces external semantic region grouping (SAM2, superpixels, feature clustering) with regular non-overlapping patches, classifying each patch by its mean error via either percentile thresholding or a two-component 1-D GMM fit, leveraging local spatial consistency without semantic mismatch. Second, a hybrid classification metric computes both a photometric error map (RGB L1) and a perceptual error map (cosine similarity of multi-layer features from a vision foundation model such as DINOv2, VGG-16, or ResNet-18); the photometric GMM classification estimates the scene's static proportion, which then calibrates the percentile threshold for the perceptual classification, and the final mask is the intersection of both. This adaptive fusion corrects the semantic fragility of perceptual metrics in textureless regions while retaining their ability to separate color-similar distractors from static backgrounds.

## Results

On the RobustNeRF dataset (4 scenes, 8× downsampled), the best variant (ResNet) achieves 29.87 PSNR, 0.913 SSIM, and 0.073 LPIPS on average, outperforming all 10 baselines across all three metrics. The method improves over native 3DGS by 3.41 dB in PSNR and 0.025 in SSIM, and over the strongest prior 3DGS method (HybridGS) by 0.34 dB in PSNR and 0.007 in SSIM. Performance is remarkably consistent across three different vision foundation models (VGG-16, ResNet-18, DINOv2), with less than 0.06 dB PSNR variation, demonstrating generalizability without per-scene tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed HPC framework (best variant: ResNet) achieves the best average PSNR (29.87), SSIM (0.913), and LPIPS (0.073) on RobustNeRF, beating the closest competitor HybridGS (29.53 / 0.906 / 0.080) by 0.34 dB PSNR and 0.007 SSIM. Among semantic-prior-based 3DGS methods, SLS-mlp (29.51) and RobustSplat (29.36) trail by 0.36 and 0.51 dB respectively, while WildGaussians (27.88) and T-3DGS (28.37) lag further behind despite also using DINOv2 perceptual metrics. Against NeRF-based baselines, the gap is larger: NeRF-HuGS (28.50) and RobustNeRF (28.11) are outperformed by 1.37 and 1.77 dB. Notably, the method's advantage over native 3DGS (26.46) is 3.41 dB, and it maintains consistent performance across VGG-16, ResNet-18, and DINOv2 backbones (±0.06 dB), whereas baselines like RobustSplat show high LPIPS (0.135) indicating perceptual degradation despite competitive PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on native 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) as the rendering backbone and on the GMM-based error classification paradigm introduced by MemE-3DGS (Wang et al., 2024b) for separating static from transient pixels. It departs from the dominant trend of incorporating external semantic priors (SAM2, superpixels, DINOv2 feature clustering) for region grouping by replacing them with simple mechanical patch partitioning, arguing that general-purpose vision semantics are fundamentally misaligned with the binary static/transient distinction. On the metric side, it extends the perceptual-error line (WildGaussians, T-3DGS, RobustSplat) by calibrating perceptual classification with a photometric GMM estimate of the static proportion, addressing what the authors term 'semantic fragility' in textureless regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, efficient solution to a persistent real-world deployment problem for 3DGS: transient distractors (pedestrians, shadows, vehicles) that corrupt reconstruction. The key open question it highlights is the inherent ambiguity between rarely-observed static objects and true transients, which no error-based method can fully resolve without a paradigm shift. The finding that coarse patch boundaries are tolerable in 3DGS (unlike 2D segmentation) due to multi-view consistency is a useful practical insight, but the method still inherits 3DGS's inability to reconstruct unobserved content and struggles with sub-patch-scale distractors or highly reflective surfaces.
