## Problem

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has proven highly effective for training LLM reasoning, but it is fundamentally limited to tasks with ground-truth verifiers (math, code). Many high-impact reasoning domains—analytical writing, open-ended research, financial analysis—lack such verifiers, and the standard fallback of Supervised Fine-Tuning on expert demonstrations fails to elicit the same search-and-self-correction behaviors that RL produces. The paper asks whether expert demonstrations alone, without verifiers or human preference labels, can drive the emergence of strong reasoning capabilities.

## Input / Output

The policy takes a text question q and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace z followed by a final answer a, with a configurable reasoning token budget (256–4096 tokens in experiments). The relativistic critic takes a question q and two candidate answers (one expert, one policy-generated) and outputs a three-way classification: expert, policy, or tie. Training data consists of expert QA pairs (e.g., 131k Countdown problems, 103k DeepMath problems, 40k poems with auto-generated prompts); no preference annotations or verifier labels are required at training time.

## Method

RARO formulates reasoning training as Inverse Reinforcement Learning: rather than maximizing the intractable marginal likelihood over CoT traces, it learns a reward function r_φ such that the KL-regularized optimal policy under r_φ approximates the ML objective, yielding a closed-form gradient that decomposes into expert and policy terms. Concretely, a single LLM plays both roles—policy (generating CoT + answer) and relativistic critic (pairwise-comparing expert vs. policy answers with a tie option)—and both are updated jointly via GRPO in a mixed batch. The relativistic pairwise setup (rather than absolute classification) prevents critic degeneracy as the policy approaches expert quality, and the tie label provides stable rewards when the critic is uncertain. Key stabilization techniques include a replay buffer of past policy rollouts to prevent critic catastrophic forgetting, shared weights between policy and critic to reduce memory and promote generalization, over-length filtering, and removal of advantage/length normalization from GRPO.

## Results

On Countdown (1.5B, 2048-token budget), RARO achieves 54.4% accuracy, outperforming the best verifier-free baseline (SFT, 40.7%) by 13.7% and nearly matching the oracle RLVR baseline (57.7%). On DeepMath (7B), RARO reaches 57.5% accuracy versus 49.3% for the best baseline (RL-Logit), a +8.2% gain. On Poetry Writing (7B), RARO achieves a 25.0% win rate against expert poems and a 77.3/100 GPT-5 score, substantially surpassing all baselines (best: SFT at 5.9% win rate, 65.4 score). RARO also exhibits robust scaling with both reasoning budget (33.1%→61.3% on Countdown from 256→4096 tokens) and model size (7.8%→25.0% poetry win rate from 1.5B→7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RARO consistently outperforms all verifier-free baselines across every task and model scale. Against SFT (the strongest supervised baseline), RARO gains +13.7% on Countdown (54.4% vs 40.7%), +15.2% on DeepMath 7B (57.5% vs 42.3%), and +19.1% win rate on Poetry 7B (25.0% vs 5.9%). Against RL-Logit (the strongest RL-based verifier-free baseline), RARO leads by +3.6% to +8.2% on DeepMath across model sizes, but on Poetry Writing RL-Logit collapses to near-base performance (36.4 vs 35.0 score at 1.5B), while RARO achieves 67.8. Against Iterative DPO, RARO wins on DeepMath at all scales (e.g., 57.5% vs 36.9% at 7B) and on Poetry at 1.5B and 7B, though DPO Round 3 is competitive at 3B (69.8 vs 71.9 score). Against the oracle RLVR upper bound, RARO nearly matches on Countdown (54.4% vs 57.7%) but trails on DeepMath 7B (57.5% vs 66.2%), with the gap narrowing under test-time scaling (68.4% vs 76.9% at N=16). Rationalization performs poorly across all tasks (12.5% on Countdown, 48.6% on DeepMath 7B), confirming that naive CoT annotation does not elicit effective reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

RARO reframes reasoning training as an Inverse Reinforcement Learning problem, drawing directly on the GAIL adversarial imitation framework (Ho & Ermon, 2016) and the KL-regularized optimal policy characterization (Peng et al., 2019) to derive a principled reward gradient from expert demonstrations alone. The optimization is instantiated via GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), with the policy parameterized as a latent-variable CoT model (Phan et al., 2023). The key departure from prior non-verifiable reasoning work (Zhou et al., 2026; Gurung & Lapata, 2025; Jia et al., 2025; Gunjal et al., 2026) is the elimination of all external reward signals—no logit-based rewards, no LLM-as-judge, no rubrics, no reference answers—replacing them with a learned relativistic critic that performs pairwise expert-vs-policy comparisons with a tie option.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RARO opens a practical pathway for eliciting reasoning in the many real-world domains (analytical writing, financial analysis, open-ended research) where task-specific verifiers are unavailable and human preference collection is prohibitively expensive. The relativistic critic with tie option and replay buffer are concrete stabilization techniques that address a known failure mode in adversarial LLM training. Key limitations include reduced sample efficiency versus RLVR (the critic must first learn to discriminate before providing signal), observed reward oscillation in creative domains, and evaluation only up to 7B parameters. Open questions include whether the method scales to frontier model sizes, how to make the critic's preferences interpretable, and whether the adversarial dynamics can be further stabilized for long-form subjective tasks.
