## Problem

Existing prompt learning for vision-language models (VLMs) follows either end-to-end or decoupled routes, both of which optimize prompts in task-specific feature spaces and suffer from a base-new tradeoff. The paper identifies that sample-wise optimization traps models in local optima because the prompt-induced change is not consistently scaled across base and new tasks, and that decoupled methods require task identity at test time, which is impractical in open-world settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263161/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 visual encoder and text class prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a [CLASS].') encoded by the CLIP text encoder. Five learnable prompt tokens are injected into the visual encoder starting from layer 6, producing two branches: a class-token embedding and a mean-pooled prompt-token embedding, both projected to the shared image-text space and ℓ₂-normalized. Classification is performed via a temperature-scaled softmax over cosine similarities, yielding a single class label per image without requiring task identity at inference time.

## Method

AlignedNorm formulates prompt learning as constructing a Coupled Prompt Field (CPF), where the prompt-induced change u_f(x) = α(f_p(x) − f_c(x)) is a task-shared correction applied uniformly to both base and new classes under a single inference rule. The key insight is that norm drift in the projected prompt feature (ratio r(x) = ‖z_p(x)‖/‖z_c(x)‖) distorts this field across the base-new gap, and that small prompt-branch norms amplify stochastic gradient noise and destabilize the field. To stabilize the coupling, AlignedNorm adds two L1 norm-alignment losses: a post-projection term aligning ‖z_p(x)‖ to the class-token norm, and a layer-wise term aligning pooled prompt-token norms to class-token norms at each prompted layer (J through L). The layer-wise alignment specifically prevents 'entanglement collapse,' a phenomenon where randomly initialized prompt tokens with mismatched norms receive exponentially suppressed attention and fail to exchange semantic information with global tokens. The overall training objective is L_task + β·L_proj + γ·L_token, with no architectural changes, no additional parameters, and no decoupled inference at test time.

## Results

On the base-to-new generalization benchmark (11 datasets), AlignedNorm achieves an average harmonic mean (HM) of 81.45%, improving over MMRL++ without decoupled inference (80.71%) by +0.74 HM and over the strongest end-to-end method MicroPL (80.60%) by +0.85 HM. In the few-shot setting, AlignedNorm reaches 72.93% at 1-shot and 84.08% at 16-shot, matching or slightly exceeding MMRL++ (72.57% / 84.07%). For cross-dataset transfer, AlignedNorm attains 67.61% average accuracy across 11 target datasets, and for domain generalization it achieves 60.96% average over four shifted ImageNet variants, both marginally ahead of MMRL++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlignedNorm is compared against six baselines spanning end-to-end (MaPLe, PromptSRC, MicroPL) and decoupled (MMRL, MMRL++) paradigms. Against the strongest end-to-end method MicroPL, AlignedNorm gains +0.85 HM (81.45 vs 80.60) while also improving new-class accuracy by +1.29 points. Against the decoupled MMRL++ baseline (which requires task identity at test time), AlignedNorm achieves +0.74 HM over its non-decoupled variant and matches the full MMRL++ HM of 81.43 without any decoupled inference. In the few-shot regime, AlignedNorm leads at 1-shot (72.93% vs 72.57% for MMRL++) and 4-shot (79.11% vs 78.95%), with gains diminishing at higher shot counts. On cross-dataset transfer, AlignedNorm edges out MMRL++ by +0.01 average (67.61 vs 67.60) and outperforms PromptSRC by +1.80. On domain generalization, AlignedNorm achieves the best average (60.96%) versus MMRL++ (60.58%) and MMRL (60.40%), with the largest gap on ImageNet-V2 (+0.76 over MMRL++). The only notable loss is on FGVCAircraft new classes (−0.03 vs MMRL++ w/o decoupling), attributed to distribution mismatch with CLIP's pretraining data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MMRL++ (Guo & Gu, 2026), a decoupled deep prompt learning framework for CLIP, and reframes its two-branch inference as a Coupled Prompt Field (CPF) — a task-shared transformation over the feature space. The key conceptual departure from prior decoupled methods (DePT, DPC, MMRL, MMRL++) is eliminating the need for task identity at test time by enforcing a single unified inference rule, and identifying embedding norm drift as the mechanism that breaks this coupling. The norm-alignment regularizer (AlignedNorm) draws on the uniformity-tolerance analysis of contrastive representations (Wang & Isola, 2020; Wang & Liu, 2021) and the attention-sink / high-norm-token literature, but applies these ideas specifically to stabilize prompt-token interactions across transformer layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CPF formulation offers a cleaner conceptual lens for prompt learning: instead of defining an ideal final representation (which is undefinable across the base-new gap), one models the more transferable prompt-induced change as a shared field. Practically, AlignedNorm achieves competitive performance without decoupled inference, removing a key deployment limitation of prior decoupled methods. However, the method is tightly coupled to the MMRL++ architecture and CLIP-style contrastive VLMs; whether norm dynamics play an analogous role in generative multimodal models remains open. The single-step optimization and dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning (β, γ) also limit immediate broad applicability.
