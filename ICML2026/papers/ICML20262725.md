## Problem

Existing diffusion-based generative image steganography methods (CRoSS, DiffStega, Gaussian Shading, StegaDDPM) suffer from a key-dependency paradox: precise secret extraction requires sender and receiver to pre-share a private key or random seed to synchronize the stochastic denoising trajectory, creating a single point of failure and logistical overhead. This paper eliminates that dependency by grounding security in information-theoretic structural redundancy rather than exogenous cryptographic parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB secret image and a free-form text prompt as input. The secret is downsampled and halftoned into binary structural invariants, split via XOR into two statistically uniform shares, each mapped to a 64×64×4 Gaussian latent and rendered through Stable Diffusion v1.5 conditioned on the text prompt, producing a pair of 512×512 stego-images. Recovery XORs the two extracted binary shares and applies score-guided posterior sampling to reconstruct the continuous-tone secret at full resolution, requiring no shared key or seed.

## Method

KIStego is a training-free framework built on a pre-trained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5). The secret is projected into a binary manifold via spatial pooling and halftoning, then decomposed into two shares S₁ ~ U(F₂ᵏ) and S₂ = S₁ ⊕ B, each providing zero mutual information about B (Shannon perfect secrecy). An orthogonal matrix Q maps the standardized shares onto the Gaussian latent shell, ensuring stego-latents are statistically indistinguishable from i.i.d. noise. Recovery formulates inverse halftoning as a constrained inverse problem: a differentiable surrogate M_τ(x) = Sigmoid(τ(P(x) − d)) approximates the non-differentiable halftoning operator, and gradients through this surrogate steer the diffusion posterior sampling trajectory (using Tweedie estimates at each timestep) to reconstruct high-fidelity continuous-tone pixels from the sparse binary observation, mitigating ODE inversion drift without any task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

On the UniStega benchmark (100 image-prompt pairs at 512×512), KIStego achieves near-perfect concealment with a stego-vs-secret PSNR of only 9.51 dB and ID similarity of 0.011, far below CRoSS (19.53 dB, 0.462) and DiffStega (18.85 dB, 0.226), confirming complete semantic decoupling. For recovery fidelity, KIStego attains 24.25 dB PSNR, 0.728 SSIM, and 0.825 ID similarity, outperforming CRoSS (21.30 dB, 0.656, 0.644) and DiffStega (23.94 dB, 0.707, 0.780). Generative quality is also superior: KIStego scores 0.294 CLIP and 3.25 NIQE versus 0.237/4.20 (CRoSS) and 0.252/3.77 (DiffStega). Under channel degradations (blur, noise, JPEG, scaling), KIStego maintains 21.56–22.48 dB PSNR while CRoSS drops to 18.27–20.08 dB and DiffStega to 18.62–20.85 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KIStego outperforms all four baselines across every reported metric. Against CRoSS (Yu et al., 2023), KIStego improves recovery PSNR by 2.95 dB (24.25 vs. 21.30) and ID similarity by 0.181 (0.825 vs. 0.644), while reducing stego-secret PSNR from 19.53 to 9.51 dB—indicating CRoSS leaks substantial identity information into the stego-image. Against DiffStega (Yang et al., 2024), the gains are narrower but consistent: +0.31 dB PSNR, +0.021 SSIM, +0.045 ID Sim on recovery, and a 10× reduction in stego-secret ID similarity (0.011 vs. 0.226), showing DiffStega retains residual semantic coupling. Against HiNet (Jing et al., ICCV 2021), a modification-based invertible-network method, KIStego is dramatically more robust: under Gaussian blur HiNet collapses to 10.26 dB PSNR while KIStego holds 22.48 dB, and steganalysis detection accuracy for HiNet reaches 0.72–0.81 versus KIStego's near-ideal 0.50. Against Latent-DPS (Song et al., ICLR 2024) in the ablation, KIStego's differentiable halftoning guidance adds 5.69 dB PSNR over unguided latent DPS (24.25 vs. 18.56) and 3.13 dB over the STE-based error-diffusion variant (24.25 vs. 21.12), confirming that the measurement-conditioned posterior sampling is the critical component for high-fidelity inverse halftoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

KIStego departs from the dominant line of diffusion-based steganography (CRoSS, DiffStega, Gaussian Shading, StegaDDPM) that all require pre-shared cryptographic keys or random seeds to synchronize the denoising trajectory. It repurposes the bipartite XOR secret-sharing mechanism from visual cryptography (Yan et al., 2019) and couples it with diffusion posterior sampling (Chung et al., ICLR 2023) to replace exogenous key coordination with endogenous structural invariants. The halftoning-and-downsampling measurement operator, combined with a differentiable sigmoid surrogate for gradient-based guidance, is a novel architectural choice not present in prior GIS methods, which typically embed secrets directly into continuous latent coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating the out-of-band key-distribution bottleneck addresses a practical single-point-of-failure that has constrained real-world deployment of diffusion steganography, and the information-theoretic (Shannon) security guarantee is stronger than the computational security offered by seed-based schemes. However, the evaluation is confined to a single backbone (Stable Diffusion v1.5), a single resolution (512×512), and one benchmark (UniStega), leaving open questions about scalability to higher resolutions, alternative architectures (e.g., SDXL, DiT), and robustness against adaptive steganalysis. The iterative backpropagation cost of the posterior sampling phase also limits real-time applicability, and the 'fast mode' that bypasses gradient guidance is not quantitatively characterized in the main tables.
