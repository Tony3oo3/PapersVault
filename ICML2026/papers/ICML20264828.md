## Problem

Prior work establishes that the centered-scaled steady state of constant-stepsize stochastic approximation converges weakly to a Gaussian as the stepsize α→0, but provides no explicit error bound for any fixed α, leaving practitioners without a quantitative characterization of the pre-limit regime. This paper fills that gap by deriving non-asymptotic Wasserstein distance bounds and non-uniform tail probability bounds that vanish as α→0, and extends the analysis to Markovian noise and non-Gaussian (Gibbs) limits for general convex objectives.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes the SA recursion parameters—stepsize α, drift mapping F (gradient of a smooth strongly convex objective, a linear map, or a contractive nonlinear operator), and a noise sequence (i.i.d. with finite third moment, or Markovian with bounded Poisson solutions)—and produces explicit Wasserstein-1 distance bounds of order O(α^{1/2} log(1/α)) between the scaled steady state and its Gaussian limit, with fully specified constants depending on dimension d, the Hessian/Jacobian at the fixed point, and noise covariance. The tail bounds are non-uniform in the deviation level a, taking the form Φ^c(a) ± C_d α^{1/4} log^{1/2}(1/α)/a, so the error shrinks as one probes deeper into the tails. For general convex objectives with h-th order local growth, the output is a Wasserstein bound of order O(α^{1/h}) against a non-Gaussian Gibbs limit with density proportional to exp(−|y|^h).

## Method

The core technique is generator-based Stein's method: the one-step update operator of the SA chain is compared against the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck generator of the Gaussian limit via a second-order Taylor expansion, with remainder terms controlled by uniform moment bounds on the SA iterates and regularity bounds on the Stein solution (gradient, Hessian, and Hessian Hölder continuity). For Markovian noise, a Poisson equation technique re-expresses the temporally correlated noise in terms of a one-step transition, allowing the same generator-comparison framework to handle the additional linear and quadratic noise terms that do not vanish automatically. The tail bounds are then derived from the Wasserstein bound via a concentration argument that optimizes over a free parameter to yield the non-uniform 1/a decay. For general convex objectives, the method identifies the correct scaling α^{1/h} and a polynomial Gibbs target distribution, with the Stein equation corresponding to the one-dimensional Langevin diffusion whose invariant law is the Gibbs measure.

## Results

The paper establishes explicit pre-limit Wasserstein bounds of the form $c\alpha^{1/2}\log(1/\alpha)$ between the centered-scaled steady state $Y^{(\alpha)}$ and its Gaussian limit for three SA models (SGD on strongly convex objectives, linear SA, and contractive nonlinear SA), under both i.i.d. and Markovian noise. The corresponding non-uniform tail bounds take the form $C_d\,\alpha^{1/4}\log^{1/2}(1/\alpha)/a$, decaying in both the deviation level $a$ and stepsize $\alpha$. For general convex objectives with $h$-th order growth at the minimizer, the correct scaling is $\alpha^{1/h}$ (not $\sqrt{\alpha}$) and the limit is a non-Gaussian Gibbs law with Wasserstein error $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{1/h})$. All bounds vanish as $\alpha\downarrow 0$, recovering the prior weak convergence results of Chen et al. (2022) while providing the first explicit rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing algorithms; instead it improves upon prior theoretical results. Chen et al. (2022) established only weak convergence of $Y^{(\alpha)}$ to a Gaussian as $\alpha\downarrow 0$ under i.i.d. noise, with no explicit rate and a uniqueness conjecture left open. This paper resolves that conjecture (Proposition C.1) and provides the first explicit $\alpha$- and $d$-dependent Wasserstein rate $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{1/2}\log(1/\alpha))$. Zhang et al. (2024) achieved $W_2(\mathcal{L}(Y^{(\alpha)}),\mathcal{L}(Y))=o(1)$ for nonsmooth contractive SA but without an explicit convergence rate in $\alpha$ or $d$, making it inapplicable for fixed-stepsize algorithm design. The present work's bounds are strictly stronger in that they are non-asymptotic and fully explicit. The logarithmic factor $\log(1/\alpha)$ is acknowledged as a technical artifact of high-dimensional Stein's method; a concurrent work (Zhang & Xie, 2026) removes it for diminishing-stepsize CLT but the fixed-$\alpha$ setting remains open. For the general convex case, the paper's $\alpha^{1/h}$ scaling and Gibbs limit (Proposition 5.5) go beyond the Gaussian regime and are validated numerically, extending the heuristic observations in Chen et al. (2022, Section 3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the asymptotic characterization of constant-stepsize SA steady states from Chen et al. (2022), who proved weak convergence of the centered-scaled iterate to a Gaussian limit as α→0 under i.i.d. noise but provided no explicit error rate. The authors deploy generator-based Stein's method (originating from Barbour, 1990, and developed for queueing by Gurvich, 2014) combined with high-dimensional OU Stein regularity bounds (Gallouet et al., 2018) to obtain explicit non-asymptotic Wasserstein-1 bounds with clear α and d dependence. A key methodological extension is the use of Poisson equation techniques to handle Markovian (temporally correlated) noise, which prior SA fluctuation theory (e.g., Fort, 2015; Dieuleveut et al., 2020) addressed only in diminishing-stepsize CLT settings. The paper also resolves the uniqueness conjecture from Chen et al. (2022) by showing the characterizing PDE has a unique Gaussian solution via the method of characteristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit pre-limit Wasserstein and non-uniform tail bounds enable practitioners to quantify how well a Gaussian approximates the steady-state fluctuation of SGD or TD-learning at a fixed stepsize, supporting risk-sensitive RL and statistical inference on training dynamics. The non-Gaussian Gibbs limit for general convex objectives (Section 5) is the first constant-stepsize steady-state diffusion approximation yielding a non-Gaussian law with an explicit error bound, though it rests on two unproven conjectures. Open questions include removing the log(1/α) factor (acknowledged as a technical artifact of high-dimensional Stein's method), improving the polynomial dimension dependence to logarithmic, and completing the proof of the Gibbs-limit proposition. The results are primarily theoretical and do not directly change training practice, but they provide the quantitative foundation for understanding when and why constant-stepsize SGD behaves approximately Gaussian.
