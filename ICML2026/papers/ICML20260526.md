## Problem

LLM alignment relies on complex proxy reward signals (learned reward models, LLM-as-a-Judge) whose implicit objectives are opaque, creating risks of misalignment and reward hacking. Existing interpretation methods either depend on pre-defined rubrics that miss 'unknown unknowns' or operate on static model snapshots, failing to leverage the rich temporal signal in the alignment trajectory to identify objectives that are both comprehensive and causal to learned behavior.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a chronological sequence of T model checkpoints (e.g., sampled at steps [0, 25, 50, 75, 100] for synthetic PPO or [0, 100, 200, 300] for real RMs) along with the reward model's scalar scores and the training prompt set. It produces a Discovered Interpretable Reward (DIR): a sparse set of k natural-language objectives with learned weights from a linear-regression composition function (positive coefficients enforced), plus exemplar-based Objective Explanations — small sets (κ=8) of input-response trajectories selected to maximize trend fidelity and semantic diversity. Each discovered objective must satisfy two formal desiderata: human-interpretability (LLM-as-a-Judge scores approximate the objective within ε_interp) and trend-predictability (scores fit a parametric learning curve within ε_trend).

## Method

Obj-Disco frames reward decomposition as a sparse representation problem and solves it with an iterative greedy algorithm inspired by Matching Pursuit: at each iteration it selects the single natural-language objective that maximally reduces the residual Obj-Error (root-mean-squared reward mismatch averaged over the checkpoint trajectory). Each iteration has two phases — Discovery and Verification. In Discovery, the algorithm identifies high-residual input samples (top-ν from a random pool of N_cand=100), feeds their full response trajectories across checkpoints to a proposer LLM (GPT-4o-mini) in batches of 3, and greedily selects the candidate that minimizes residual on a held-out test interval. In Verification, an ensemble of LLM-as-a-Judge models checks human-interpretability, and the objective's score trajectory is fit against a library of parametric learning curves (linear, logarithmic, power-law-with-asymptote, exponential saturation) to confirm trend-predictability. A key differentiator from prior static comparison methods is the use of the full checkpoint trajectory to disentangle pre-existing inductive biases from behaviors actively reinforced by the reward signal. For human-facing explanations, a submodular greedy selection (with (1−1/e) approximation guarantee) picks exemplar trajectories that jointly maximize trend fidelity and cross-cluster semantic diversity.

## Results

Across all controlled and open-source reward model settings (PPO/GRPO, Llama-8B/Qwen-4B, four tasks), Obj-Disco is the only method to consistently surpass 90% Model-Fit, while Iter-Filter and Zero-Shot achieve lower and more variable scores. In the safety misalignment case study (34 trials), Obj-Disco identified a latent misaligned objective in 58.8% of trials (95% CI: [42.3%, 75.4%]), significantly outperforming all baselines. Human-subject evaluations confirm that Obj-Disco's discovered objectives are more causal to final policy behavior (35.6% selection frequency vs. 16.7–27.1% for baselines) and its Objective Explanations achieve 39.9% identification accuracy vs. 25.5% for random baselines (p < 0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Obj-Disco outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. On Model-Fit, Obj-Disco consistently exceeds 90% across all eight experimental configurations, while Iter-Filter and Zero-Shot remain below this threshold with notably higher variance; Zero-Shot occasionally approaches Obj-Disco but is constrained by the proposer LLM's context window. In the misalignment case study, Obj-Disco detects latent misaligned objectives in 58.8% of trials, compared to 20.6% for Iter-Filter (p = 0.003), 0.0% for Zero-Shot (p < 0.001), 23.5% for Fixed-3 (p = 0.006), and 5.9% for Limited-Zero-Shot (p < 0.001); the strongest baseline, Fixed-15 (44.1%), still trails Obj-Disco and requires a manually curated objective set. In the causality human study, Obj-Disco's DIRs are selected as most behaviorally similar 35.6% of the time, significantly exceeding Iter-Filter (16.7%, p < 0.001), Zero-Shot (27.1%, p < 0.010), and the unaligned base model (20.6%, p < 0.001). The trajectory ablation (Obj-Disco-Static) further confirms that leveraging the full checkpoint sequence is critical: without it, misaligned behaviors are captured in only 1/4 trials versus 3/4 for full Obj-Disco.

## Relation to Prior Work

Obj-Disco adapts the Matching Pursuit algorithm (Mallat & Zhang, 1993) from signal processing to the problem of decomposing an opaque RLHF reward signal into sparse, interpretable natural-language objectives, and uses submodular optimization (Nemhauser et al., 1978) for selecting exemplar trajectories. It departs from prior reward-model interpretability work (Jiang et al., 2025b; Christian et al., 2025; Marks et al., 2023) that relies on predefined attributes, token-level analysis, or sparse autoencoders, and from descriptive 'proposer-validator' frameworks (Zhong et al., 2022; Dunlap et al., 2024; 2025) that compare static model snapshots. The key conceptual departure is operating on the temporal trajectory of alignment checkpoints rather than a single before/after comparison, enabling the method to isolate behaviors actively reinforced by the reward signal from pre-existing inductive biases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first automated, open-ended tool for auditing what an aligned LLM has actually learned, which is critical as reward models grow more complex and misalignment risks (sycophancy, permissiveness) become harder to detect manually. The finding that simpler discovery baselines (Zero-Shot) miss latent misaligned incentives entirely across 34 trials underscores the practical value of the residual-driven, trajectory-based approach for safety auditing. Key limitations include the a posteriori setting (requires full checkpoint access), computational cost of iterative LLM-as-a-Judge scoring, and reliance on the assumption that the reward decomposes into a small number of independent objectives. Open questions include extending to online/real-time monitoring, handling non-sparse or highly correlated objective structures, and validating whether LLM-as-a-Judge verification introduces systematic blind spots that could mask certain misalignments.
