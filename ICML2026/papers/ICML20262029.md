## Problem

Multimodal thinking models produce long reasoning traces that should be grounded in visual evidence, but verifying this grounding is computationally prohibitive: faithful causal methods (perturbation, gradient) require repeated forward/backward passes whose cost scales linearly with trace length, while raw attention maps are instant but lack causal validity. This creates a faithfulness-efficiency trade-off that makes real-time, per-step visual attribution infeasible for interactive debugging of reasoning chains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262029/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 224×224 for DINOv3 feature extraction) and a text query as input to the VLM, while simultaneously caching text-to-vision cross-attention weights at each decoding step. The output is a stream of per-region attribution scores (one vector of K values per completed reasoning span, where K is adaptively determined between 16–128 via DINOv3 clustering), delivered asynchronously so that attribution for step t appears as the model begins generating step t+1. A secondary patch-level map is produced by redistributing region scores using the DINOv3 attention map as a spatial prior.

## Method

VSTREAM amortizes counterfactual ablation effects by training a linear estimator (L×H parameters, e.g., 784 for a 28-layer/28-head model) to predict log-probability drops from pooled cross-attention features. The key insight is that while individual attention weights are unreliable as causal explanations, the comprehensive attention pattern across all layers and heads contains sufficient signal to recover the counterfactual effect of masking a visual region. Vision tokens are first partitioned into semantically coherent regions via DINOv3-Large features and agglomerative clustering (Ward's linkage, cosine distance, threshold τ=0.5), yielding a membership matrix that defines K adaptive regions per image. For each (span, region) pair, mean attention is pooled and concatenated across all L·H layer-head pairs to form the feature vector; the estimator is trained on 2,000 examples (32 random region masks each) by maximizing Pearson correlation with ground-truth ablation effects computed via attention masking (setting scores to −∞). At inference, a producer-consumer architecture offloads attribution scoring to a background GPU thread, hiding the O(K·L·H) dot-product cost behind the latency of the next reasoning step and achieving up to 117× speedup over gradient-based baselines.

## Results

VSTREAM achieves an average LDS of 0.70–0.72 and Top-5 Drop of 0.98–1.02 across four reasoning VLMs (Qwen3-VL-8B, GLM-4.1V-9B, MiMo-VL-7B, Cosmos-Reason1-7B) and five task categories, matching or exceeding the strongest gradient- and perturbation-based baselines (AttnLRP, TAM) in 16/20 settings. Crucially, VSTREAM operates at 0.022–0.026 s per 10 tokens (≈117× faster than gradient-based methods at ~2.5–3.0 s), enabling real-time streaming attribution. The predicted-vs-actual ablation effect correlation reaches R² = 0.65, and cross-task transfer retains 75–90% of in-domain performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSTREAM matches or exceeds AttnLRP (the strongest gradient-based baseline) on LDS across all four models (e.g., 0.72 vs. 0.71 on Qwen3-VL) and surpasses it on Top-5 Drop in most settings (1.00 vs. 0.89 on Qwen3-VL), while running ~110× faster (0.024 s vs. 2.60 s per 10 tokens). Against TAM (the strongest perturbation-based baseline), VSTREAM achieves comparable or better Top-5 Drop (1.00 vs. 0.99 on Qwen3-VL; 1.02 vs. 0.95 on MiMo-VL) with a ~70× speedup. InputGrad is consistently outperformed on both metrics (e.g., LDS 0.72 vs. 0.64, Top-5 Drop 1.00 vs. 0.76 on Qwen3-VL) and is ~117× slower. Raw Attention and Random baselines are far below all causal methods, confirming that attention alone is an unfaithful proxy. The key differentiator is that VSTREAM achieves faithfulness on par with expensive backward-pass or multi-forward-pass methods while adding negligible overhead to the decoding loop, making per-step streaming attribution feasible for the first time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the amortized attribution paradigm (Jethani et al., 2021; Covert et al., 2024) from text to visual tokens in multimodal reasoning models, and builds directly on the concurrent AT2 work (Cohen-Wang et al., 2025) that uses attention features for text attribution. The ablation mechanism (attention masking to −∞) is adopted from Geva et al. (2023), while the semantic region unitization via DINOv3 features and agglomerative clustering departs from prior pixel- or token-level attribution by operating on semantically coherent units. The key conceptual departure is the streaming architecture: rather than computing attribution post-hoc over a completed trace, the method decouples attribution computation from generation via a producer-consumer pattern, enabling per-step visual grounding inspection during reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 784-parameter linear estimator trained in ~4.5 hours on a single GPU achieves faithfulness comparable to gradient- and perturbation-based methods while enabling real-time streaming (117× speedup), making interactive debugging of reasoning VLMs feasible for the first time. The trajectory dynamics analysis opens a new diagnostic dimension—distinguishing hallucination (fixation) from reasoning error (wandering) via attribution geometry, with early failure detection at 30% of reasoning—suggesting attribution streams could serve as runtime safety monitors. Open questions include whether the linear conditional-mean assumption holds for larger or architecturally different models, whether cross-model transfer is possible without retraining, and whether the method extends to video or multi-image inputs where temporal attention patterns add complexity.
