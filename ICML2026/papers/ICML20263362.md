## Problem

Standard dataset distillation aligns synthetic data to a single aggregate representation computed over all samples, which is dominated by majority demographic groups when subgroup representations differ. The paper shows that the resulting fairness gap is not caused by group imbalance or representational separation alone, but by their interaction, and that simply correcting group size (e.g., uniform sampling) does not eliminate the disparity because the aggregate target still pulls toward majority-group patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263362/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large RGB image dataset with class labels and per-sample sensitive-attribute (group) annotations, along with a user-chosen distillation backbone (gradient matching, distribution matching, feature alignment, or trajectory matching). It outputs a small synthetic image set at a fixed images-per-class (IPC) budget whose labels match the original classes; group labels are used only during the distillation optimization and are not required at downstream training or inference time. The distilled set is architecture-agnostic: a model trained from scratch on it should generalize across different network designs.

## Method

COBRA (Cross-grOup BaRycenter Alignment) replaces the standard class-conditional distillation target — a proportion-weighted mixture of subgroup representations — with a uniform-weight barycenter of those subgroup representations, computed per class. Concretely, for each class y it first computes subgroup-specific statistics Φ_{T_{a|y}} (gradients, embeddings, or features depending on the backbone), then solves m_y* = argmin_m Σ_a d(Φ_{T_{a|y}}, m) with equal weights to eliminate dependence on group proportions, and finally aligns the synthetic representation Φ_{S_y} to m_y* via the original backbone's distance function. Under a squared Q-norm discrepancy the barycenter admits the closed-form uniform mean of subgroup statistics, making the modification a lightweight drop-in. The paper proves (Theorem 4.1) that, under a geometric condition on the worst-case residual direction, COBRA does not increase the worst-case subgroup residual relative to vanilla distillation, thereby contracting the representation-space mismatch that drives equalized-odds disparity.

## Results

COBRA consistently achieves the lowest Equalized Odds Difference (EOD↓) across all seven biased benchmarks and four distillation backbones (DC, DM, CAFE, IDC) at IPC ∈ {10, 50, 100}, while often improving accuracy over vanilla distillation. Headline examples include C-MNIST (BG) with DM at IPC=50, where EOD drops from 100.0 to 7.46 and accuracy rises from 48.8% to 96.8%; CIFAR10-S with DM at IPC=100, where EOD falls from 82.87 to 9.37 and accuracy increases from 45.4% to 62.4%; and BFFHQ with DM at IPC=100, where EOD decreases from 63.47 to 7.87 while accuracy improves from 65.8% to 74.2%. The FULL (no-distillation) baseline achieves the best overall accuracy but still exhibits non-trivial EOD (e.g., 48.96 on CIFAR10-S, 64.00 on BFFHQ), confirming that distillation amplifies bias beyond what exists in the original data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla distillation backbones (DC, DM, CAFE, IDC), COBRA reduces EOD by 50–90+ points on the synthetic color-correlation datasets (e.g., from 100.0 to 7.46 on C-MNIST BG with DM) and by 30–60 points on real-world datasets (e.g., from 60.27 to 15.13 on BFFHQ with DM), while simultaneously matching or exceeding vanilla accuracy. Compared to FairDD (Zhou et al., 2025), the only prior method explicitly targeting subgroup fairness in distillation, COBRA achieves consistently lower EOD across all datasets and IPC levels; the gap widens with increasing group imbalance (e.g., at SKEW=0.85 on CIFAR10-S with DM, COBRA attains 13.27 EOD vs. 24.84 for FairDD). COBRA also outperforms FairDD on accuracy in most settings, suggesting that barycentric target alignment is more effective than per-group loss averaging. In the trajectory-matching setting (MTT), COBRA-MTT reduces EOD from 32.96% to 9.12% on BFFHQ at IPC=10, though at a modest accuracy cost (61.64% → 51.86%), whereas on CIFAR10-S it improves both metrics (EOD 39.40→30.38, Acc 26.36→36.32). Cross-architecture evaluation (Table 3) confirms that COBRA's fairness gains transfer stably to AlexNet, VGG11, and ResNet18, with mean EOD of 14.25 vs. 70.84 for vanilla DM on CIFAR10-S.

## Relation to Prior Work

COBRA sits at the intersection of dataset distillation (Wang et al. 2018; Zhao et al. 2021; Zhao & Bilen 2023) and group fairness via barycenter/optimal-transport methods (Gordaliza et al. 2019; Charpentier et al. 2023). The closest prior work, FairDD (Zhou et al. 2025), addresses subgroup fairness in distillation by averaging per-group losses, but COBRA departs by identifying that bias amplification arises from the *interaction* between group imbalance and representational separation rather than from imbalance alone, and by replacing the aggregate distillation target with a group-size-agnostic barycenter computed before the loss is formed. This makes COBRA a drop-in modification of the target statistic in any existing DD backbone (DC, DM, CAFE, IDC, MTT) rather than a reweighting of already-computed per-group losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As distilled datasets are deployed in healthcare, finance, and other high-stakes settings, the finding that vanilla DD systematically amplifies subgroup disparities (and that correcting group size alone does not fix this) is practically important for anyone using distillation in fairness-sensitive pipelines. COBRA's simplicity—replacing one aggregate target with a uniform-weight barycenter—makes it easy to adopt, and its compatibility with multiple distillation paradigms (gradient, distribution, feature, trajectory matching) broadens its applicability. Open questions include extending to multiple overlapping protected attributes, handling missing or noisy group labels during distillation, and whether the theoretical guarantee (Condition 9) holds broadly in high-dimensional representation spaces. The method also requires group annotations at distillation time, which may be unavailable in some deployment scenarios.
