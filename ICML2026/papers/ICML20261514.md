## Problem

LLMs perform poorly in multi-agent games because single-agent reasoning paradigms are inherently non-strategic and fail to model opponents' cognitive states. Existing multi-agent RL extensions of GRPO (self-play, timestep-aligned baselines) lack explicit opponent reasoning modeling, provide no fine-grained signal for intermediate reasoning steps, and suffer from severe baseline variance in long-horizon trajectories, making credit assignment across multiple reasoning turns unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261514/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives the full game state as a dialogue history (sequence of prior moves) plus the opponent's most recent action, and must produce a free-form thinking trace followed by a structured reasoning block containing four bracketed fields—OpponentIntent, OpponentPrediction, MyIntent, and MyPrediction—along with the chosen game action. The structured block enforces a 'Past-Present-Future' cognitive loop: decoding the opponent's past intent, inferring the opponent's prediction of the ego's current move, stating the ego's own intent and action, and predicting the opponent's next move. Invalid formatting or overlong responses trigger immediate episode termination with a heavy penalty.

## Method

Strat-Reasoner introduces three synergistic modules. First, a Recursive Reasoning module (inspired by Cognitive Hierarchy theory) structures each agent's CoT as a four-field block that explicitly models the opponent's beliefs and predictions, creating natural mapping relations between agents' reasoning. Second, a Centralized CoT Comparison module (following the CTDE paradigm) scores each turn by comparing the ego agent's three prediction fields against the opponent's actual realized reasoning and actions via an LLM-as-a-judge, yielding a turn-level CoT score. Third, a Hybrid Advantage Estimation module generates M micro-rollouts per turn to compute a GRPO-normalized CoT advantage, then combines it with a batch-centered Monte Carlo return advantage (A_hybrid = A_return + ω·A_cot) to balance dense local reasoning feedback against unbiased long-term game returns. Training uses LoRA on Qwen3-4B with strict on-policy role-specific updates (two separate agents trained independently on their own trajectories), built on the ROLL framework with vLLM for parallel inference.

## Results

Strat-Reasoner-4B achieves a 22.1% average performance improvement over the base Qwen3-4B model across three multi-agent game environments. On Kuhn Poker vs. Nash Equilibrium bot, it reaches 94.04/90.47 (first/second mover), surpassing both GPT-5-mini (77.72/86.56) and Gemini-2.5-flash (92.44/87.79). On Tic-Tac-Toe vs. MCTS (100 sims), it scores 90.77/81.84, and on MiniHanabi it achieves a collective score of 80.19, outperforming all open-source baselines including the 32B Qwen3 model and specialized MARL methods MARSHAL and SPIRAL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Strat-Reasoner-4B decisively outperforms all open-source baselines: it surpasses the base Qwen3-4B by 22.1% on average, beats the much larger Qwen3-32B by 10–24 points across games, and exceeds the specialized MARL baselines MARSHAL-4B (73.47/81.55 on TTT, 75.05/73.94 on Kuhn) and SPIRAL-4B (70.45/69.77 on TTT, 58.01/69.38 on Kuhn) by wide margins. Against closed-source models, Strat-Reasoner-4B is competitive: it beats GPT-5-mini on Kuhn Poker (94.04 vs 77.72 first-mover) and Tic-Tac-Toe vs MCTS-100 (90.77 vs 88.84 first-mover), and edges out Gemini-2.5-flash on MiniHanabi (80.19 vs 79.63), though it trails both on Tic-Tac-Toe vs MCTS-1000 second-mover (73.12 vs 87.10/82.53). The paper also reports OOD generalization (Table 2) where Strat-Reasoner-4B trained on Tic-Tac-Toe/Kuhn/MiniHanabi transfers to Connect Four, Leduc Holdem, and Simple Hanabi, consistently outperforming same-size open-source models and even Qwen3-32B on several OOD tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) from single-agent to multi-agent strategic reasoning by introducing a recursive reasoning paradigm grounded in Cognitive Hierarchy theory (Camerer et al., 2004), a centralized CoT comparison module inspired by the CTDE paradigm (Lowe et al., 2017), and a hybrid advantage that combines dense CoT signals with the Monte Carlo return-based advantage from MARSHAL (Yuan et al., 2025). It departs from the self-play paradigm used by SPIRAL and MARSHAL, which share parameters between agents, by training role-specific policies that preserve opponent non-stationarity. The LLM-as-a-judge mechanism (Zheng et al., 2023) is repurposed not for trajectory-level preference scoring but for fine-grained semantic alignment between structured reasoning fields across agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a 4B-parameter model can rival GPT-5-mini and Gemini-2.5-flash in multi-agent strategic games through structured recursive reasoning and fine-grained credit assignment, suggesting that strategic depth can be incentivized without massive scale. However, the evaluation is confined to relatively simple two-player alternating games (Tic-Tac-Toe, Kuhn Poker, MiniHanabi), and the N-agent extension, asynchronous action settings, and transfer to genuinely complex domains remain open. The reliance on the base model as its own CoT evaluator, while validated for robustness, raises questions about whether the signal degrades as the policy improves and the reasoning becomes more sophisticated.
