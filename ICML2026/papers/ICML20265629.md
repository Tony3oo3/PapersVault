## Problem

Existing token-level reweighting methods for supervised fine-tuning rely on a single signal—either ground-truth probability (prob-dominant) or predictive entropy (entropy-dominant)—and treat them in isolation. This one-dimensional view misidentifies noisy or easily replaceable tokens as learning-critical: entropy-dominant schemes up-weight high-uncertainty filler words, while prob-dominant schemes over-penalize naturally ambiguous synonyms. The paper asks how to calibrate downstream alignment by intrinsic uncertainty so that updates focus on genuinely under-learned critical tokens without amplifying noise or distorting linguistic flexibility.

## Input / Output

Given a prompt-response pair (x, y) and the model's per-token logit vectors z_t over the vocabulary, the method computes four scalar quantities per token in a single forward pass: the ground-truth probability p_t, the rank R_t of y_t among all vocabulary tokens, the expected rank E[R_t] (the guessing cost of the prediction distribution), and the token entropy H_t. The output is a token-wise Relative Scale S_t that multiplicatively modulates the standard NLL loss, producing a reweighted objective L = (1/T) Σ w_t · S_t · ℓ_t. All per-token operations are O(|V|) and fully vectorized, adding no asymptotic overhead beyond standard SFT.

## Method

RankTuner introduces the Relative Rank Indicator I_t = 2^{f(R_t) − f(E[R_t])} with f(x) = 1/log₂(x+1), which compares the realized rank of the ground-truth token against its expected rank under the model's prediction distribution, yielding a neutral value of 1 when R_t = E[R_t]. Tight theoretical bounds—R_t ≤ 1/p_t (rank-probability) and E[R_t] ≥ s(H_t) (expected-rank-entropy, derived via maximum-entropy/geometric distribution arguments)—make the rank-based quantities commensurate proxies for probability and entropy. Applying the Cauchy Mean Value Theorem to f yields a power-law form I_t ≈ (E[R_t]/R_t)^{K(ξ_t)}, which motivates the practical surrogate S_t = (p_t · s(H_t))^{−K(ξ_t)} where K(ξ_t) = [log₂(ξ_t+1)]^{−2} and ξ_t = max(R_t, s(H_t)). The final fine-tuning loss replaces the uniform weight with w_t · S_t, where w_t = p_t for math-reasoning tasks (variance-stable importance-sampling initialization) and w_t = 1 for general tasks, so that tokens the model underperforms relative to their entropy-induced difficulty receive amplified gradients while noisy or replaceable high-entropy positions are down-weighted.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B, RANKTUNER achieves the best Pass@1 on 4 of 5 math benchmarks: MATH-OAI (72.38), OlympiadBench (36.25), AIME24 (10.21), and AMC23 (46.56), with the largest margin over TALR on AIME24 (+2.92) and AMC23 (+2.81). On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, RANKTUNER leads on AIME24 Pass@1 (7.08 vs. 6.67 for TALR) and AMC23 Pass@1 (44.53 vs. 43.44), while trailing TALR slightly on MATH-OAI (68.60 vs. 68.83) and Minerva Math (33.30 vs. 35.68). Across both backbones, RANKTUNER consistently improves Pass@16 on OlympiadBench and AIME24, indicating gains in multi-sample coverage rather than just single-sample accuracy. Out-of-distribution, RANKTUNER achieves the best Pass@1 on both ARC-C (53.58) and GPQA (29.64) with Qwen2.5-Math-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest prob-dominant baseline TALR, RANKTUNER wins Pass@1 on AIME24 (+2.92 on Qwen3-8B, +0.41 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B) and AMC23 (+2.81, +1.09), and matches or slightly trails on MATH-OAI and Minerva Math; on Pass@16, RANKTUNER leads TALR on OlympiadBench (+4.00/+4.29) and AIME24 (+0.00/+3.33). Against DFT (the other strong prob-dominant method), RANKTUNER wins Pass@1 on all five benchmarks for Qwen3-8B (e.g., +1.46 on MATH-OAI, +1.46 on AIME24) and on four of five for Qwen2.5-Math-7B, with the sole loss on MATH-OAI (−0.55). The entropy-dominant EAFT and the gated OverTone are consistently outperformed by 10–30+ points on Pass@1 across all benchmarks, confirming that neither pure entropy weighting nor probability-gated smoothing captures the joint probability-entropy signal. On out-of-distribution ARC-C and GPQA, RANKTUNER edges out TALR by +1.04 and +0.35 respectively, while DFT underperforms the original model on ARC-C (26.50 vs. 13.46 original but below RANKTUNER's 53.58), suggesting prob-dominant over-sharpening hurts transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token-level reweighting literature for supervised fine-tuning (DFT, EAFT, TALR, OverTone) by arguing that probability-dominant and entropy-dominant schemes are each one-dimensional and can misidentify noisy or replaceable tokens as learning-critical. It departs from prior work by grounding the reweighting signal in the classical guessing problem (Massey, 1994), using the Cauchy Mean Value Theorem to derive a unified Relative Rank Indicator that calibrates ground-truth probability against entropy-induced expected rank. Conceptually, the paper reframes token importance as a conditional-competence ratio (alignment given prior support) rather than a function of either signal alone, and provides tight rank-probability and rank-entropy bounds that make the two dimensions commensurate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is practically attractive: it adds no asymptotic overhead beyond standard SFT, requires only a single forward pass, and the noise-sensitivity diagnostic (9% vs. 77% sequence-level noise hit) provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why one-dimensional weighting fails. However, the main performance gains over the strongest baselines (TALR, DFT) are modest (typically 1–2 points on Pass@1), and the evaluation is limited to math reasoning and code generation with a small training set (10k samples). Open questions include whether the calibration principle transfers to RL-style post-training (PPO/GRPO), multimodal fine-tuning, and whether the specific (f, g) function choices or the K(ξ) approximation materially affect results at scale.
