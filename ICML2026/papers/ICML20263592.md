## Problem

Max-min MORL promotes fairness across multiple objectives but is formulated as an unconstrained optimization, making it inapplicable to real-world problems with hard constraints (e.g., power budgets, emission caps). Existing constrained RL methods handle only single-objective rewards and cannot address the non-differentiability of the min scalarization when K ≥ 2 objectives must be balanced fairly under constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes a state observation (continuous or discrete, depending on the environment) and designer-specified constraint thresholds C^(l) for L constrained objectives. The reward is a (K+L)-dimensional vector: K unconstrained objectives optimized under the max-min criterion and L constrained objectives that must exceed their thresholds. The output is a softmax policy π(a|s) = exp(Q(s,a)/β) / Σ exp(Q(s,a')/β) over the action space, where Q is the learned action-value function.

## Method

The paper reformulates the constrained max-min MORL problem as a convex optimization over occupancy measures, then derives a dual convex program whose objective L(u,w) = Σ_s μ_0(s) v*_{u,w}(s) − Σ_l u_l C^(l) is minimized over (u,w) ∈ R_+^L × Δ^K. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.3) shows that the gradient of v*_{u,w}(s) with respect to (u,w) equals the value functions evaluated under the entropy-regularized optimal policy π*_{u,w}, enabling a unified gradient computation for both fairness weights w and constraint multipliers u. Algorithm 1 alternates between (i) a soft Bellman Q-update (value iteration for the entropy-regularized fixed point) and (ii) projected gradient descent on (u,w) with projection onto the simplex Δ^K and non-negative orthant R_+^L. A convergence guarantee (Theorem 3.6) establishes geometric convergence at rate (1−λ/α)^m up to O(ε) approximation error, with the smoothness constant α explicitly bounded in terms of reward magnitudes, discount factor, and entropy coefficient β.

## Results

Across three application domains, the proposed constrained max-min MORL method consistently satisfies the constraint threshold while achieving the highest worst-group (minimum) return among all constraint-satisfying methods. In the building thermal control environment (C_th=180), the method achieves a cost sum of 178.7 and a minimum return of 639.8, outperforming the only other constraint-satisfying baseline MA-SAC-L (cost 171.4, min return 620.9). In MoAnt-v5 (C_th=50), it achieves cost 28.3 and min return 92.2 versus MA-SAC-L's cost 47.8 and min return 83.0. In the 16-lane traffic signal control (C_th=70,000), it achieves cost 69,147 and min return −25,229 versus MA-CPGO's cost 67,887 and min return −27,830.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: MA-SAC (unconstrained max-average SAC), MA-SAC-L (max-average SAC with Lagrangian constraint handling), Max-min GS (unconstrained max-min via Gaussian smoothing, Park et al. 2024), and ARAM (unconstrained max-min via two-player zero-sum game, Byeon et al. 2025). The proposed method wins on the joint objective of constraint satisfaction plus max-min fairness: it is the only method that simultaneously satisfies the constraint threshold and achieves a higher minimum return than MA-SAC-L across all three environments (e.g., 639.8 vs. 620.9 in Building, 92.2 vs. 83.0 in MoAnt, −25,229 vs. −27,830 in Traffic). MA-SAC-L satisfies constraints but sacrifices worst-group performance because it optimizes max-average rather than max-min. Max-min GS and ARAM achieve higher minimum returns (653.6 and 664.3 in Building) but violate the constraint (cost sums 202.1 and 276.9 vs. threshold 180), confirming that unconstrained max-min methods cannot handle operational limits. In MoAnt, the gap is even more pronounced: ARAM's cost sum of 620.7 exceeds the threshold of 50 by over 12×, while the proposed method stays at 28.3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: unconstrained max-min MORL (Park et al. 2024, Byeon et al. 2025) and constrained single-objective RL via Lagrangian methods (Achiam et al. 2017, Tessler et al. 2018, Ha et al. 2020). The key methodological departure is reformulating the non-differentiable max-min objective as a convex program over occupancy measures (Puterman, 1994), then deriving a dual problem whose gradient structure—via the implicit function theorem—reduces to standard value functions under an entropy-regularized policy. This avoids the inexact gradient estimates of Gaussian smoothing (Park et al. 2024) and the proxy-objective approximations of prior max-min MORL (Fan et al. 2023, Peng et al. 2025), while extending beyond the single-objective scope of existing constrained RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first convergence-guaranteed algorithm for constrained max-min MORL, filling a gap where fairness and operational constraints (energy, emissions) must be jointly satisfied. The geometric convergence rate (Theorem 3.6) and the clean gradient characterization (Theorem 3.3) make the approach practically implementable, as demonstrated across building thermal control, locomotion, and 16-lane traffic management. Limitations include reliance on the action-value nondegeneracy assumption for positive definiteness, approximate Q-updates in continuous spaces, and the need for a designer-chosen feasible constraint set. Open questions include scalability to very large state spaces, handling of non-convex or stochastic constraints, and whether the entropy-regularized formulation can be replaced by lighter alternatives without losing the convexity guarantees.
