## Problem

Existing pipeline-level acceleration methods for diffusion models (e.g., StreamDiffusion) cannot be directly transferred to Rectified Flow models due to fundamental mismatches in representation (velocity fields vs. noise), scheduling semantics (variable-length time steps vs. fixed noise levels), and execution structure (dynamic computational graphs that break static TensorRT compilation). This paper addresses the gap by designing a comprehensive acceleration pipeline that is theoretically grounded in flow-model dynamics and practically compatible with existing flow-based architectures.

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts a text prompt (and optionally a reference image for img2img mode) and produces 512×512 RGB images using a Rectified Flow backbone (PeRFlow) with 4-step inference by default. Multiple images are generated concurrently through a batched pipeline where each UNet forward pass processes N samples at different denoising stages simultaneously, achieving continuous throughput after N−1 warmup iterations. The system also supports variable inference steps (up to 8) and scales to 1024×1024 resolution.

## Method

StreamFlow introduces three coupled innovations: (1) Velocity Field Batching (VFB), which vectorizes the PeRFlow time-windows mechanism to compute window parameters (γ, λ, η) for a batch of heterogeneous timesteps in parallel, decoupling step size from the velocity field so that a single UNet call processes samples at different denoising stages; (2) a heterogeneous timestep pipeline with asynchronous queue management, where a latent buffer holds intermediate states at N different stages and a shift operation after each forward pass advances all samples one step, yielding one complete image per UNet call once the pipeline is full; (3) a dynamic TensorRT compilation strategy that performs runtime homogeneity detection—invoking a single compiled engine for uniform timestep batches and decomposing into per-sample TensorRT calls for heterogeneous batches—achieving ~3× per-call speedup while maintaining plug-and-play compatibility with any TensorRT-compiled UNet. Together these components yield up to 611% speedup over the vanilla PeRFlow baseline at 512×512 with less than 1% quality degradation in CLIP Score and FID.

## Results

StreamFlow achieves up to 611% speedup over the vanilla PeRFlow baseline (1.87 FPS → 10.83 FPS for xformer+taesd+VFB+1-step at 512×512), with the TensorRT configuration reaching 10.05 FPS while consuming only 110W (47% less power than the 205W baseline). Peak memory is reduced from 3788MB to 2666MB (30% savings). Generation quality is preserved with less than 1% variation in CLIP Score (96.25–97.26 vs. 97.27–97.28 for baselines) and FID within ~2 points (30.75–31.82 vs. 29.44–30.08).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: the Hugging Face Diffusers pipeline (2.22 FPS) and the vanilla PeRFlow implementation (1.87 FPS). StreamFlow's best configuration (xformer+taesd+VFB+1-step) achieves 10.83 FPS, a 4.9× speedup over HF and 5.8× over vanilla; the TensorRT variant reaches 10.05 FPS (4.5× over HF). In img2img mode, the speedup is comparable (10.01 FPS for TensorRT), confirming the acceleration is not text-encoding-dependent. On quality, all StreamFlow variants stay within 1% CLIP Score and ~2 FID points of the baselines, confirming no meaningful quality degradation. The authors explicitly position StreamFlow as complementary to (not competing with) other acceleration techniques like distillation, Reflow, and few-step methods, noting it can be stacked on top of them for additional gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the pipeline-parallel queue architecture of StreamDiffusion (Kodaira et al., 2025) to the fundamentally different inference semantics of Rectified Flow and Flow Matching models, where the model predicts a velocity field with heterogeneous step sizes rather than a noise estimate at a shared timestep. It builds on PeRFlow's (Yan et al., 2024) piecewise time-window mechanism as the base acceleration layer and on TensorRT/Xformer for compilation-level optimization, but identifies three structural mismatches (velocity-field batching, heterogeneous timestep scheduling, dynamic compilation) that prevent direct transfer of diffusion-oriented accelerators. The key conceptual departure is decoupling step size from the velocity field computation, enabling vectorized batch processing of non-uniform timesteps that no prior diffusion accelerator handles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 611% speedup for 512×512 generation with <1% quality degradation makes real-time rectified flow deployment practical, and the plug-and-play design means existing flow models (InstaFlow, PeRFlow, SD3-style) can adopt the pipeline without retraining. However, the evaluation is limited to a single UNet-based model at 512×512 (with scalability checks to 1024px), and the 611% figure is measured against a vanilla 4-step baseline rather than the strongest available flow-model accelerators; the paper acknowledges existing public methods achieve ~18% but does not directly benchmark against them. Open questions include whether the heterogeneous-timestep pipeline generalizes to DiT-based flow backbones, video generation, or multi-GPU distributed settings, and whether the TensorRT decomposition overhead for heterogeneous batches becomes prohibitive at larger batch sizes or higher resolutions.
