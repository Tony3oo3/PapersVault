## Problem

As LLMs increasingly train on synthetic data, parametric representations lose their implicit grounding in verifiable sources, creating an epistemic failure mode where fluent outputs carry no recoverable provenance lineage. Standard RAG treats retrieval as optional augmentation with parametric fallback, but this paper argues that in high-stakes factual settings, unrestricted parametric factual generation is a safety liability that must be architecturally eliminated rather than merely augmented.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266386/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text query and retrieves evidence passages from a versioned, provenance-tagged knowledge base (with document IDs, span offsets, and timestamps). In factual mode, the output is structured at the atomic claim level: each factual proposition must carry inline evidence identifiers that pass NLI entailment verification, or the system emits an explicit abstention signal. Non-factual spans (definitions, planning text) remain unconstrained, and the active mode and its associated guarantees are transparent to the user.

## Method

The paper proposes a three-tier 'separation-of-powers' architecture that decouples parametric generation from factual authorization: a frozen reasoning core handles linguistic competence, a dynamic non-parametric knowledge layer provides versioned evidence with provenance metadata, and a mandatory attribution interface gates every atomic factual claim behind evidence identifiers verified by an NLI entailment model (e.g., DeBERTa-v3). Training uses a Predict-or-Retrieve (PoR) objective that conditions generation on retrieved evidence and explicitly rewards calibrated abstention when evidence is insufficient, inverting standard RLHF incentives that favor confident completion. The paper introduces three diagnostic metrics—Attribution Density (claim-level citation coverage), Parametric Leakage Ratio (accuracy persistence when retrieval is disabled, with degradation controls such as shuffled or adversarially irrelevant passages), and Abstention Accuracy—to operationalize referenceability as a measurable system property rather than an aspiration.

## Results

This is a position paper that does not report its own experimental results or benchmark numbers. It proposes three evaluation metrics—Attribution Density (AD), Parametric Leakage Ratio (PLR), and Abstention Accuracy—and supports its architectural claims with cited external evidence, most notably the AIS attribution gap of 87.9% (architectural evidence access) versus 25.2% (no evidence access) from Rashkin et al. (2023), and the 8–18% benchmark contamination rates reported by Yang et al. (2023). The paper's contribution is normative (a system invariant and architecture) rather than empirical.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper performs no direct quantitative baseline comparison; it is a position paper that contrasts its proposed evidence-gated 'Separation of Powers' architecture qualitatively against two alternatives: (1) standard RAG, which permits parametric factual fallback when retrieval is weak, and (2) purely parametric fine-tuned models, which lack any audit trail. The key empirical anchor is the AIS attribution study (Rashkin et al., 2023), where systems with architectural access to evidence score 87.9% versus 25.2% for post-hoc attribution—a 62.7-point gap the authors interpret as evidence that causal ordering (retrieve-then-generate) is structurally necessary, not merely an optimization detail. The paper also cites model-collapse results (Shumailov et al., 2024; Dohmatob et al., 2024) and knowledge-editing degradation studies (Hsueh et al., 2024; Li & Chu, 2024) to argue that parametric update paths are operationally fragile compared to index-based retrieval updates. No head-to-head accuracy or latency numbers are reported for the proposed architecture itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper synthesizes and repositions several existing threads—RAG (Lewis et al., 2020), attributed question answering (Bohnet et al., 2023; Rashkin et al., 2023), model collapse under recursive synthetic training (Shumailov et al., 2024), and external-memory architectures (RETRO, Memorizing Transformer)—into a unified architectural argument. Its core departure is reframing retrieval from an optional augmentation to a mandatory gating constraint: in high-stakes factual mode, parametric fallback is treated as a safety liability rather than a graceful degradation path. The proposed 'separation of powers' architecture and the Parametric Leakage Ratio diagnostic are novel formulations, but they repurpose well-known components (NLI verifiers, ColBERTv2-style retrieval, SQuAD 2.0-style abstention evaluation) under a stricter epistemic contract.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a genuine and growing gap: as synthetic data proliferates in training corpora, the implicit assumption that parametric weights encode grounded, traceable knowledge erodes, yet current RAG systems still permit unsourced parametric fallback. The practical urgency is sharpened by compliance pressures (GDPR erasure, EU AI Act transparency, FDA CDS reviewability) that make parametric unlearning intractable while retrieval-based deletion is straightforward. Key open questions the paper itself flags include multi-hop joint entailment verification, handling contested or temporally evolving evidence, verifier calibration, and the coverage-versus-abstention trade-off when evidence is sparse. As a position paper with no empirical validation of the proposed architecture or metrics, its impact depends on whether the community adopts the PLR/AD/abstention reporting bundle and the evidence-gated invariant as deployment standards.
