## Problem

Existing robust dataset distillation methods suffer from a poor accuracy-robustness trade-off because they (i) treat all adversarially perturbed examples uniformly, despite robust risk being dominated by the smallest robust margins, and (ii) rely on class-mean feature alignment that fails to explicitly widen inter-class separation near decision boundaries where adversarial attacks concentrate. The paper asks whether a distillation objective can simultaneously target the smallest-margin adversaries and enforce boundary-level class separation while keeping computation practical.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a real training set of RGB images with class labels (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 128×128 for ImageNet-1K subsets) and produces a compact synthetic dataset of N = IPC × C images with corresponding labels, where IPC ∈ {1, 5, 10, 30, 50}. During distillation, each synthetic sample is paired with an adversarial counterpart generated under an ℓ∞ budget (default ε = 2/255), and the synthetic set is iteratively updated via bilevel optimization so that a model trained on it matches full-data performance under both clean and adversarial evaluation.

## Method

C²R couples two components within a standard bilevel distillation loop. First, an Attack-Aware Curriculum (AAC) computes a per-sample perturbation score s(x) = [1 − m̂_rob(x;θ)]₊ using a warm-started line-search PGD (LS-PGD) attacker that replaces multi-step backpropagation with a single gradient direction plus forward-only line search over a geometric step-size shortlist, then sorts adversarial examples from hardest (smallest margin) to easiest and forms curriculum-ordered mini-batches. Second, a Contrastive Robustness Loss (CRL) replaces class-mean embedding alignment with a supervised contrastive objective that pulls each sample toward its adversarial counterpart and same-class neighbors (positives) while pushing it away from nearest competing-class embeddings (negatives), with a class-balanced memory queue and random-projection retrieval reducing per-step cost from O(M²) to O(Mk). The total loss is (1−η)L_perf + η·L_CRL, optimized on the curriculum-ordered batches so that the contrastive pressure is concentrated on the low-margin tail that governs robust risk.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (IPC=10, ε=2/255), C²R achieves 29.12% PGD robust accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline ROME (24.01%) by 5.11 points and D⁴M (20.14%) by 8.98 points. Across all datasets, attacks, and IPC budgets, C²R delivers an average 2.8% improvement in robust accuracy over prior robust DD methods. The drop rate (clean-to-robust accuracy degradation) under PGD stays below 66.8% on average, the lowest among all evaluated DD methods, and remains flatter as IPC scales from 1 to 50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C²R outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and attack evaluated. Against ROME (Zhou et al., ICML 2025), the closest robust DD competitor, C²R gains +5.11% on CIFAR-10 PGD (29.12 vs 24.01), +2.96% on CIFAR-100 PGD (11.38 vs 8.42), and +2.47% on Tiny-ImageNet PGD (3.83 vs 1.36) at IPC=10, with the gap widening at IPC=30 (e.g., +5.63% on CIFAR-10). Against D⁴M (Su et al., CVPR 2024), C²R leads by +8.98% on CIFAR-10 PGD and +7.13% on CIFAR-100 PGD at IPC=10, though D⁴M retains a slight clean-accuracy edge on CIFAR-100 (40.12 vs 37.12). SRe²L (Yin et al., NeurIPS 2023) is the weakest baseline, trailing C²R by over 16 points on CIFAR-10 PGD. On ImageNet-1K subsets (Table 2), C²R beats MTT (Cazenavette et al., CVPR 2022) by +2.5–3.3% under FGSM/PGD across all six subsets at IPC=10. Finally, under distributional shift (Table 10), C²R surpasses RDD (Vahidian et al., ICLR 2025) on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 48.5 vs 46.7 under cluster-min corruption) and ImageNet-10 (48.9 vs 47.1), confirming that the margin-oriented design generalizes beyond the adversarial threat model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust dataset distillation line initiated by Tsilivis et al. (2022) and most directly builds on ROME (Zhou et al., 2025), which optimizes a weighted sum of classification loss and class-mean robustness alignment under an information-bottleneck view. C2R departs from ROME in two structural ways: it replaces the class-mean embedding alignment with an instance-level contrastive robustness loss (CRL) that explicitly separates competing classes near decision boundaries, and it introduces an attack-aware curriculum (AAC) that reorders optimization to prioritize the smallest-margin adversarial examples, grounded in the robust-margin theory of Schmidt et al. (2018) and Tsipras et al. (2019). The LS-PGD attacker and class-balanced memory queue are efficiency contributions that distinguish the method from prior robust DD pipelines that naively run full multi-step PGD for every sample.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, margin-centric justification for why uniform robustness alignment in distilled datasets underperforms, and offers a practical recipe (curriculum + contrastive loss + efficient attacker) that consistently improves the accuracy-robustness trade-off across six attacks and multiple datasets. The 2.8% average robust-accuracy gain and the sub-66.8% drop rate under PGD are meaningful for deployment scenarios where distilled models must resist adversarial perturbations. However, the evaluation is confined to moderate-resolution classification benchmarks (CIFAR, Tiny-ImageNet, 10-class ImageNet subsets) with standard ConvNet backbones, leaving open whether the margin-centric curriculum and contrastive objective transfer to higher-resolution settings, dense prediction, or more diverse architectures. The slight robust-accuracy dip from IPC=10 to IPC=50 also hints at an unresolved tension between synthetic data density and boundary overlap that future work should address.
