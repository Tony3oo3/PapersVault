## Problem

Optimising loss functions whose gradients are expectations under parameter-dependent distributions with intractable normalising constants requires expensive inner MCMC sampling loops at every optimiser step, which are slow to converge and computationally prohibitive. Existing particle-based alternatives (e.g., persistent contrastive divergence, implicit diffusion) use unweighted or time-averaged particles that become biased when the particle population lags the evolving target distribution. The paper asks whether sequential Monte Carlo samplers can replace these inner loops with a principled, sequentially reweighted particle scheme that is both more accurate and more efficient.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a parameterised potential U_θ defining the intractable target π_θ ∝ exp(−U_θ), a (potentially non-differentiable) reward function R, a frozen reference distribution π_0, initial parameters θ_0, and a particle budget N. It produces optimised parameters θ_K after K outer iterations, along with a weighted particle cloud {X_K^(i), w_K^(i)} that approximates π_θ_K and self-normalised gradient estimates g_k = Σ w_k^(i) H_θ_k(X_k^(i)). A key practical detail is that particles and weights are treated as stop-gradient quantities during backpropagation, and the forward/backward kernel choice (ULA, MALA, RWM, HMC, or Moreau-Yosida ULA) determines the explicit form of the incremental weight G_k.

## Method

SOSMC constructs a Feynman-Kac flow over the product space E^{k+1} whose x_k-marginal is the current target π_θ_k, yielding a recursion for the intractable gradient ∇_θ ℓ(θ_k) = π_θ_k(H_θ_k). At each optimiser step, N weighted particles are propagated by a forward kernel K_k (e.g., one ULA step) and reweighted by the incremental weight G_k = Π_θ_k(x_k) L_{k-1}(x_k, x_{k-1}) / [Π_θ_{k-1}(x_{k-1}) K_k(x_{k-1}, x_k)], where L_{k-1} is a backward kernel (often the time-reversal of K_k). Resampling is triggered when the effective sample size drops below a threshold τN, and an optional adaptive rule scales the step-size γ_k multiplicatively based on ESS to maintain a stable discrepancy regime. The framework unifies prior methods as special cases: choosing ULA kernels recovers JALA-EM for MMLE and the SMC sampler of Carbone et al. for EBM training, while reversible kernels (MALA, RWM, HMC) simplify the weight to exp(−U_θ_k(x_{k-1}) + U_θ_{k-1}(x_{k-1})). Under PL and L-smoothness assumptions, the idealised (exact-expectation) scheme achieves linear convergence at rate (1−γμ)^k, and the χ²-divergence between consecutive targets is shown to scale as γ²∇ℓ^⊤ I_θ ∇ℓ + o(γ²), guiding step-size selection.

## Results

The paper presents results primarily through convergence plots and visual comparisons rather than a single numerical table. Across all reward tuning experiments (Langevin processes, 2D EBMs, MNIST), SOSMC variants converge to high reward significantly faster than IMPDIFF, with Metropolis-corrected kernels (MALA, RWM, HMC) showing robust performance where SOUL exhibits a failure mode in tight-reward settings. For 2D EBM tuning, SOSMC-ULA achieves better objective contours than IMPDIFF when β_KL is small, and its weighted particle rewards closely track E_π[R(X)] throughout tuning, requiring substantially shorter evaluation chains. For image deblurring (MMLE), SOSMC-MYULA outperforms the MYPGD baseline in both MSE and SSIM across multiple setups, producing sharper reconstructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IMPDIFF (Marion et al., 2025), SOSMC consistently wins on convergence speed and gradient quality across all three reward tuning settings. IMPDIFF uses unweighted particle averages after a single Langevin step, which are biased when particles lag the evolving target; SOSMC's importance-weighted particles correct this bias, yielding faster reward convergence and more accurate tracking of E_π[R(X)]. Against SOUL (De Bortoli et al., 2021), SOSMC-ULA achieves comparable convergence speed but is more robust: SOUL relies on a single long ULA chain and exhibits greater run-to-run variability, plus a documented failure mode in tight-reward settings where the chain cannot transition between modes, whereas SOSMC's multi-particle weighted scheme avoids this. Against MYPGD (Encinar et al., 2025) on image deblurring, SOSMC-MYULA achieves lower MSE and higher SSIM across all tested setups (varying ground-truth images and blurring operators), while both methods share the same proximal image dynamics and differ only in how the evolving posterior is approximated (weighted vs. unweighted particles). Overall, SOSMC dominates or matches all baselines on every metric reported, with the clearest advantages in convergence speed (vs. IMPDIFF) and robustness (vs. SOUL).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and generalises several existing SMC-based optimisation methods—JALA-EM (Cuin et al., 2025) for MMLE and the SMC sampler of Carbone et al. (2023) for EBM training—under a single Feynman-Kac flow framework where the target distributions evolve with the parameter iterates. The core departure from prior work is the explicit treatment of the parameter-dependent target sequence (π_θ_k) as the object of sequential sampling, replacing the expensive independent MCMC inner loops used by SOUL (De Bortoli et al., 2021) and the unweighted single-step estimates of IMPDIFF (Marion et al., 2025) with weighted SMC particle approximations that reuse information across iterations. The framework is flexible in kernel choice (ULA, MALA, RWM, HMC, Moreau-Yosida), subsuming these prior methods as special cases while allowing principled combinations not previously explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for any setting where gradient estimation requires sampling from a parameter-dependent distribution: the SMC approach avoids the O(N) MCMC burn-in per outer iteration, yielding concrete wall-clock speedups demonstrated across reward tuning, EBM training, and Bayesian image deblurring. However, the convergence theory is limited to the idealised (infinite-particle) regime; bounding the bias and MSE of the finite-N particle approximation in the self-referential setting where targets depend on previous particle estimates remains an open problem requiring interacting-particle/mean-field tools. The ESS analysis revealing exponential sensitivity to step-size and gradient norm provides useful practical guidance but also highlights a fundamental tension: aggressive optimiser steps that accelerate parameter convergence simultaneously degrade particle quality, a trade-off that adaptive schemes only partially address.
