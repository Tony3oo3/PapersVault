## Problem

Integrating auxiliary data from multiple sites to improve quantile estimation is hindered by two obstacles: (1) under local differential privacy, raw data is inaccessible, making standard plug-in variance estimators infeasible, and (2) existing data-integration methods assume either exact parameter homogeneity or shifts bounded away from zero, leaving the critical intermediate regime (bias of order N^{-1/2}) unaddressed for valid inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260364/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-site data where each site k provides either raw observations (non-private) or locally randomized binary responses via a randomized-response mechanism with site-specific truthful response rate r_k ∈ (0,1]. The quantile level τ, learning-rate schedule, number of parallel chains M_k, and tuning parameter λ are user-specified. The output is a single weighted point estimate of the target-site quantile θ_0, a (1−α) confidence interval constructed from a consistent asymptotic variance estimator, and the estimated site-specific weights that reveal which sources contributed most.

## Method

The core mechanism is a Parallel Stochastic Gradient Descent (PSGD) algorithm that replaces the raw binary indicator 1(X ≤ θ̂) in the quantile gradient with a locally randomized response (truthful with probability r_k, random otherwise), then applies a bias-correction factor to recover an unbiased gradient. Each site's data is partitioned into M_k independent SGD chains; the between-chain variance of the averaged iterates yields a consistent estimator of the site-specific asymptotic variance without ever accessing raw data. Site-specific quantile estimates are then combined via adaptive weights that minimize an objective balancing asymptotic variance against an ℓ₂ penalty on parameter shifts (b_k = θ_k − θ_0), with the penalty strength λ controlling the bias-variance trade-off. A conservative variant further inflates each estimated bias by a variance-dependent term C·√(σ̂²_k/n_k + σ̂²_0/n_0), ensuring robust inference even when source biases are of the same order as the standard error.

## Results

On the Government Salary Dataset (2018 ACS, ~200K records), ADP(decay) produces the shortest 95% confidence intervals in most settings: 1,992 (τ=0.25, r_k=0.7) and 1,041 (τ=0.5, r_k=0.7) versus 3,014 and 1,950 for the Target-only estimator, representing roughly 34–47% CI-length reduction. Under heterogeneous response rates (r_k ∈ [0.7, 1.0]), ADP(decay) further narrows intervals to 1,753 and 1,009. In simulations across nine heterogeneity levels (vanishing to distinguishable bias), all ADP variants achieve uniformly lower log-MSE and narrower CIs than the Target baseline while maintaining ≥95% empirical coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines: (1) Target (Liu et al., 2023), an ASGD-based single-site estimator under LDP with self-normalized inference, and (2) DPSGD (Song et al., 2013), which adds Laplace noise directly to stochastic gradients. ADP methods (adaptive and conservative weighted estimators) consistently outperform Target across all nine heterogeneity levels in simulations, achieving lower log-MSE and narrower confidence intervals while maintaining ≥95% empirical coverage; the advantage grows with source-site response rate. On the real Government Salary Dataset, ADP(decay) reduces CI length by ~34% (τ=0.25) and ~47% (τ=0.5) relative to Target. DPSGD produces larger MSE and wider CIs than both ADP variants and Target, confirming that randomized-response-based LDP is more efficient than gradient-noise DP for quantile problems. The conservative variant ADP(cons) additionally remains robust under continuously varying bias (μ from e^{-5} to 1) where ADP(1) and ADP(decay) suffer undercoverage, at the cost of slightly wider intervals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parallel stochastic gradient descent (PSGD) framework of Zhu et al. (2024) from smooth loss functions to the non-smooth check loss of quantile estimation, and adapts the LDP quantile inference procedure of Liu et al. (2023) from single-site to multi-site data integration. It departs from existing data-integration methods (Li et al., 2022a; Cai et al., 2024b; Han et al., 2025) in two key ways: (i) it handles the non-smooth quantile objective where classical plug-in variance estimators fail under LDP, and (ii) it introduces an ℓ₂-penalized adaptive weighting scheme that accommodates parameter shifts of arbitrary magnitude, including the intermediate N^{-1/2} regime where prior methods break down. The conservative weighted estimator is a novel addition that has no direct counterpart in the existing federated inference literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, privacy-respecting recipe for improving quantile inference by pooling heterogeneous multi-site data, with rigorous guarantees that the weighted estimator is strictly more efficient than target-only estimation whenever any source has vanishing bias. The conservative variant fills a practical gap: in real multi-site deployments, parameter shifts are rarely exactly zero or clearly large, and the intermediate regime is where naive adaptive weighting silently produces invalid confidence intervals. Key limitations include the requirement that the number of PSGD chains diverges (no fixed-chain or non-asymptotic guarantees), the assumption of a fixed number of sites K, and the fact that the real-data application is a single dataset used primarily for methodological validation rather than domain-specific insight.
