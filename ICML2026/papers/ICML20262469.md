## Problem

Existing video-based visible-infrared person re-identification (VVI-ReID) methods perform both temporal aggregation and cross-modality alignment in flat Euclidean space, which distorts the intrinsic hierarchical structure of video tracklets (e.g., branching variations from occlusion, pose, and viewpoint) and forces modality-specific manifolds into a shared flat geometry. This leads to cue crowding, structural collapse of identity representations, and ambiguous prototype learning when visible and infrared tracklets form disjoint regions. The paper argues that hyperbolic geometry, with its exponential volume growth, is theoretically optimal for preserving such tree-like cue hierarchies while enabling non-linear, structure-preserving modality alignment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video tracklets of 6 or 10 frames each from visible and infrared cameras, with input images resized to 288×144 and augmented via random horizontal flipping, padding, cropping, channel erasure, and channel swapping. A pretrained CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder (fully fine-tuned, text encoder unused) processes each frame, and the framework produces a single video-level identity embedding per tracklet via temporal average pooling over per-frame representations. Retrieval is evaluated under both Infrared-to-Visible and Visible-to-Infrared protocols using CMC (R@1/5/10) and mAP.

## Method

HHA unifies spatio-temporal modeling and cross-modality alignment on the Poincaré ball via two core modules. The Hyperbolic Hierarchical Spatio-Temporal Aggregator (HHSA) first applies Hyperbolic Geometry Interaction (HGI) at interleaved ViT layers, mapping frame-level CLS tokens to the Poincaré ball and performing hyperbolic attention (Möbius-weighted aggregation with hyperbolic distance-based softmax) to organize diverse temporal cues into hierarchical memory; Dual-Geometry Fusion (DGF) then reads out this memory via parallel Euclidean attention (for appearance consistency) and hyperbolic attention (for structural hierarchy), fusing both readouts into a discriminative per-frame feature. The Geometry-Aware Modality Alignment (GMA) module enforces cross-modality consistency by minimizing hyperbolic distance between per-identity modality centroids (HMA) and anchors the coupled centroids to learnable hyperbolic identity prototypes via a combined hyperbolic cross-entropy and prototype-pulling loss (HPA). Training optimizes a joint objective combining baseline ViT identity losses, HHSA-level triplet and cross-entropy losses, and the GMA alignment terms, with curvature fixed at c=1 and both alignment weights set to 0.05.

## Results

On HITSZ-VCM (SeqLen=6), HHA achieves 76.0% R@1 and 63.2% mAP (I2V) and 77.5% R@1 and 60.8% mAP (V2I), outperforming the previous best VLD by +1.7/+3.0 (I2V) and +2.9/+2.2 (V2I) in R@1/mAP. Increasing sequence length to 10 further boosts performance to 77.5%/64.7% (I2V) and 78.1%/62.8% (V2I). On BUPTCampus (SeqLen=6), HHA reaches 67.6%/64.6% (I2V) and 68.6%/64.2% (V2I), surpassing VLD by +2.3/+1.1 and +2.8/+1.2 respectively. The model adds only 15.8M parameters and 0.28G FLOPs over the baseline while maintaining ~1090 FPS per-frame throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HHA outperforms all 21 compared baselines on HITSZ-VCM under both I2V and V2I protocols. Against the strongest recent competitors, HHA (SeqLen=6) surpasses VLD (TIFS'25) by +1.7% R@1 and +3.0% mAP on I2V, and by +2.9% R@1 and +2.2% mAP on V2I; it also beats X-ReID (AAAI'26, SeqLen=10) by +2.6% R@1 and +2.7% mAP on I2V despite using a shorter sequence. On BUPTCampus, HHA (SeqLen=6) leads VLD by +2.3% R@1 and +1.1% mAP (I2V) and +2.8% R@1 and +1.2% mAP (V2I), while X-ReID (SeqLen=10) retains a narrow mAP edge on I2V (68.5 vs. 66.7) though HHA wins R@1 (69.7 vs. 68.2). Compared with the dual-space visible-only method DS-VReID adapted to VVI-ReID, HHA gains +5.6% R@1 and +6.1% mAP on I2V, confirming that unified hyperbolic temporal aggregation plus geometry-aware modality alignment is more effective than simply transplanting a dual-space design. Older baselines (MITML, SEFL, SAADG, CST, STHF) trail HHA by 6–12% in R@1 and 8–13% in mAP, highlighting the substantial benefit of hyperbolic hierarchical modeling over purely Euclidean spatio-temporal aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing hyperbolic geometry into video-based visible-infrared person re-identification, a task where all prior methods (MITML, CST, IBAN, SAADG, VLD, X-ReID) operate exclusively in Euclidean space. Conceptually, it extends the dual-space representation learning validated by DS-VReID (Leng et al., 2025) for visible-only video ReID to the cross-modality setting, and repurposes the Poincaré ball machinery (Möbius operations, hyperbolic attention, Fréchet means) from NLP and graph embedding literature for spatio-temporal cue aggregation. The dual-geometry fusion design draws on recent Euclidean-hyperbolic complementarity studies, while the CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone follows the current VVI-ReID paradigm established by VLD and X-ReID.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean geometric argument—supported by δ-hyperbolicity measurements showing 33–55% reduction in tree-likeness distortion—that Euclidean aggregation inherently collapses the branching structure of video tracklets, and that hyperbolic space offers a principled fix. This opens a direction for applying non-Euclidean geometry to other hierarchical or cross-modal representation learning tasks beyond ReID. However, the performance gains over strong baselines (VLD, X-ReID) are modest (1–3% R@1), the curvature is fixed at c=1 without learning, and the framework's added complexity (multiple exp/log mappings, Möbius operations) may limit adoption outside this specific task. Whether hyperbolic modeling provides similar benefits for image-based ReID or other cross-modal retrieval settings remains an open question.
