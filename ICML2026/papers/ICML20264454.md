## Problem

Existing data-adaptive two-sample tests rely on train-test splitting to decouple representation learning from hypothesis testing, but this paradigm breaks down under severe sample-size asymmetry where abundant reference data coexist with only a handful of query samples. Splitting an already tiny query set yields unstable learned representations and noisy null calibration, and the bottleneck cannot be mitigated by collecting more reference data. The paper asks how to retain the adaptivity of learning-based testing while avoiding any learning from scarce query data.

## Input / Output

The method takes an abundant reference sample X ~ P (large n, split internally into training, calibration, and held-out subsets) and a small query batch Y ~ Q (small m, used exclusively for testing and never for training). It produces a binary reject/not-reject decision at a user-specified significance level α, driven by an aggregated test statistic T(Y) that sums squared mean standardized score shifts across multiple reference-dependent representations, compared against a threshold obtained from pooled permutation over the held-out reference and query points.

## Method

LOTTERY learns a family of complementary reference-dependent representations (RDRs) exclusively from reference data: a consistent ME-RDR using kernel similarities to test locations, a Mahalanobis-RDR for global mean/covariance shifts, a kNN-RDR for local scale geometry, and a LOF-RDR for local relative density. Each RDR maps any input to a scalar compatibility score, which is standardized on a calibration set; evidence for each RDR is the squared mean standardized shift of query scores, and these are aggregated into a single statistic. An uncertainty-guided weighting scheme adaptively downweights unstable RDRs (high resampling variance) and uninformative ones (low sensitivity to controlled perturbations) by assigning each a weight proportional to its sensitivity-to-variance ratio. Testing uses a pooled permutation procedure: the held-out reference and query are combined, and B random size-m subsets are drawn to approximate the null distribution of the statistic, yielding exact finite-sample type I error control without ever splitting the query data.

## Results

On BLOB (N=4000 reference), LOTTERY achieves test power of 0.524 at M=20, rising to 0.999 at M=200 and 1.000 at M=300, outperforming the second-best method (MMD-FUSE) by margins of +0.406 to +0.080. On CIFAR-10-ResNet18 (N=100 reference), LOTTERY reaches 0.810 power with only M=2 query samples and 1.000 by M=12, exceeding the next-best baseline (MMD-M) by +0.664 at M=2 and +0.009 at M=14. In the fixed-M, varying-N setting (Table 1), LOTTERY overtakes all baselines on BLOB from N≥300 onward (e.g., 0.579 vs. 0.193 for RL-TST at N=500, M=50) and dominates CIFAR-10-ResNet18 across all N (0.789–0.932 vs. ≤0.531 for MMD-M). Type I error remains at or below α=0.05 across all configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOTTERY outperforms all six baselines across nearly every configuration, with the largest margins in the extreme few-shot regime. Against kernel-based methods (MMD-FUSE, MMDAgg, MMD-M), LOTTERY leads by 0.4–0.7 in power on CIFAR-10-ResNet18 at M=2–4 (e.g., 0.810 vs. 0.146 for MMD-M at M=2) and by 0.4–0.6 on BLOB at M=20–50. Against learning-based methods that require train-test splitting (MMD-Deep, RL-TST, C2ST-L), LOTTERY's advantage is even more pronounced: C2ST-L and MMD-Deep remain near chance (≤0.13) on BLOB at M=20–50, while LOTTERY reaches 0.524–0.824. The gap narrows as M grows (e.g., on BLOB at M=300, MMD-FUSE also reaches 1.000), confirming that LOTTERY's primary benefit is exploiting abundant reference data when query samples are scarce. On the Higgs dataset (Table 5), several learning-based baselines saturate to power 1.0 at all M, matching LOTTERY, but on high-dimensional CIFAR-10-WRN28, LOTTERY still leads by 0.5+ at M=4–8.

## Relation to Prior Work

LOTTERY departs from the standard learning-based two-sample testing paradigm (C2ST, MMD-Deep, RL-TST), which requires a train-test split of both samples to learn a discrepancy between P and Q. Instead, it repurposes the one-class classification / anomaly detection insight—that a single distribution's structure can be characterized without negative examples—into a two-sample testing framework where all representation learning is done from the reference sample alone. The individual RDRs draw on classical tools (kernel mean embeddings, Mahalanobis distance, kNN, LOF), while the aggregation mechanism adapts uncertainty-weighting ideas from multi-task learning (Kendall et al., 2018) to the hypothesis-testing context. The validity guarantee inherits from the pooled permutation testing tradition (Gretton et al., 2012; Lehmann & Romano, 2005), but applied to a one-sided statistic rather than a symmetric discrepancy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important and under-served regime: monitoring and detection pipelines where abundant historical reference data exist but only a handful of new observations arrive per test (e.g., adversarial detection, membership-inference auditing, rare-event monitoring). The key open question is how far the reference-only paradigm extends—whether it can handle sequential/online settings, non-i.i.d. query batches, or very high-dimensional embeddings where the classical RDRs (kNN, LOF, Mahalanobis) may degrade. The consistency guarantee requires at least one consistent RDR in the family, which is a mild but non-trivial assumption in practice; characterizing when the ME-RDR family is sufficient versus when additional representations are needed remains an open direction.
