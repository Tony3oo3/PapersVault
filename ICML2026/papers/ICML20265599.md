## Problem

Existing equivariant neural networks for Hamiltonian matrix prediction rely on SO(3) Clebsch-Gordan tensor products with O(L_max^6) computational complexity, which becomes prohibitive when high angular momentum degrees (L_max ≥ 4) are required to capture d-orbitals and beyond. While eSCN showed that a single SO(2) linear operation can replace one SO(3) TP, no prior work has demonstrated that a full set of powerful SO(2)-equivariant operations—including nonlinear tensor products and feed-forward networks—can be applied within local frames to eliminate all SO(3) TPs while preserving global SO(3) equivariance.

## Input / Output

The model takes 3D atomic coordinates and atomic types as input, operating on the STO-3G atomic orbital basis where each orbital carries angular momentum quantum numbers (ℓ, m). The output is the full Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian matrix in this basis, structured as blocks indexed by atom pairs and orbital pairs; each block must transform under the corresponding pair of Wigner-D matrices to satisfy block-wise SO(3) equivariance. From the predicted Hamiltonian, eigen energies and wavefunction coefficients (molecular orbitals) are derived, and the model is evaluated on MAE of H, ε, and cosine similarity of occupied orbital coefficients.

## Method

QHNetV2 constructs SO(2) local frames via minimal frame averaging (extending eSCN's edge-based frames to nodes and node pairs), then performs all off-diagonal feature updates and message passing entirely within these frames, reducing the dominant cost from O(L_max^6) to O(L_max^3). The architecture introduces four new SO(2)-equivariant building blocks—SO(2) Linear, SO(2) Gate, SO(2) LayerNorm, and a continuous SO(2) Tensor Product that fuses v sets of features via (v−1) consecutive complex multiplications under the selection rule m_o = s₁m₁ + … + s_vm—mimicking MACE's symmetric contraction module. Off-diagonal features are kept in the SO(2) local frame across all layers via SO(2) FFNs, exploiting the one-to-one correspondence between each local frame and its Hamiltonian block. Node features are updated by rotating into a per-node SO(2) frame (defined by the nearest-neighbor direction), applying the continuous SO(2) TP followed by SO(2) Linear, then rotating back to the global frame. The final Hamiltonian blocks are reconstructed via an expansion module that maps SO(3) irrep features to orbital-pair matrix elements using transformed Clebsch-Gordan coefficients.

## Results

On the QH9 benchmark, QHNetV2 achieves the best Hamiltonian MAE across all four sub-datasets: 31.50×10⁻⁶ Eₕ (stable-id), 22.97×10⁻⁶ Eₕ (stable-ood), 35.60×10⁻⁶ Eₕ (dynamic-300k-geo), and 49.01×10⁻⁶ Eₕ (dynamic-300k-mol), with corresponding eigen-energy MAEs of 417.89, 165.89, 270.02, and 629.64×10⁻⁶ Eₕ. On MD17, QHNetV2 achieves the best H MAE on ethanol (12.05), malondialdehyde (10.85), and uracil (10.38)×10⁻⁶ Eₕ, though QHNet remains stronger on the small water dataset (10.36 vs. 22.55). The model is also 4.34× faster than QHNet in inference throughput (83.33 vs. 19.20 samples/sec) while using less GPU memory (0.32 vs. 0.70 GB/sample).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QHNet (the direct predecessor), QHNetV2 reduces Hamiltonian MAE by 58.7% on QH9-stable-id (31.50 vs. 76.31) and by 59.0% on QH9-dynamic-300k-mol (49.01 vs. 119.66), with eigen-energy MAE reduced by 47.6–71.1% across sub-datasets. Compared to SPHNet, which uses sparse gating to prune tensor-product paths, QHNetV2 achieves a 33.7% lower H MAE on QH9-stable-id (31.50 vs. 45.48) and 46.9% lower on QH9-stable-ood (22.97 vs. 43.33), while also being faster (83.33 vs. 76.80 samples/sec). Against WANet, QHNetV2 substantially outperforms on QH9-stable-id (H: 31.50 vs. 79.99; ε: 417.89 vs. 833.61). On MD17, QHNetV2 beats PhiSNet, QHNet, and SPHNet on H MAE for ethanol (12.05), malondialdehyde (10.85), and uracil (10.38), but QHNet retains an advantage on the small water trajectory (10.36 vs. 22.55), which the authors attribute to water's limited 500-geometry training set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the eSCN insight (Passaro & Zitnick, 2023) that SO(2) linear operations within local frames can replace SO(3) Clebsch-Gordan tensor products, by introducing a broader family of SO(2)-equivariant operations—including SO(2) tensor products, gates, and layer normalization—that can be applied within minimal-frame-averaging local frames (Lin et al., 2024). It departs from eSCN in two key ways: (1) local frames are placed not only on edges but also on nodes and node pairs, and (2) a continuous SO(2) tensor product mimics MACE's symmetric contraction module to capture many-body interactions without SO(3) TPs. The model is a direct successor to QHNet (Yu et al., 2023b), replacing its SO(3) TP-based message passing and self-interaction with the new SO(2) local-frame operations while retaining the same expansion module for matrix construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating O(L_max^6) SO(3) tensor products in favor of O(L_max^3) SO(2) operations, the approach makes high-degree equivariant features (L_max ≥ 4, needed for d-orbitals) substantially more tractable, yielding a 4.34× speedup over QHNet and 33–47% MAE reductions on the Hamiltonian matrix. However, the authors explicitly note that SO(2) TP and SO(3) TP are not equivalent operations, so the performance ceiling of the SO(2) approach relative to full SO(3) TPs remains an open question. Practical limitations include potential frame discontinuities from nearest-neighbor reference vectors, weaker results on the small Water trajectory (500 training geometries), and the absence of experiments on the larger PubChemQH dataset. The SO(2) local-frame paradigm is a promising building block for scalable equivariant learning beyond Hamiltonian prediction, but its expressiveness relative to full SO(3) operations at higher degrees is not yet settled.
