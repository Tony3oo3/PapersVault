## Problem

VLM-based GUI agents frequently behave unfaithfully, relying on memorized shortcuts rather than conditioning on the current screen evidence and instruction, leading to ungrounded actions and reasoning-action mismatches. A key technical barrier to training faithful behavior via reinforcement fine-tuning is that discrete abstainment actions (e.g., SYSTEM_BUTTON, TERMINATE) produce near-binary rewards, causing standard GRPO's within-group normalization to collapse advantages to zero in low-variance rollout groups—precisely the states where evidence-grounded behavior matters most.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261630/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives a screenshot of the mobile UI (normalized to a 999×999 coordinate space), a text instruction, and the action history, and produces a natural-language reasoning trace (THOUGHT) followed by a structured tool-call ACTION from a unified schema covering CLICK, SWIPE, TYPE, SYSTEM_BUTTON, and TERMINATE. The output is parsed and validated against the tool-call schema; ill-formed outputs are discarded during training data construction.

## Method

Faithful-Agent uses a two-stage pipeline: Stage I performs SFT on faithfulness-oriented data (including controlled Trap perturbations such as occlusion and instruction-UI mismatch) to instill abstainment behaviors, and Stage II applies GRPO-based RFT with a combined action-match and thought-action consistency reward. The core novelty is the Guided Advantage Estimator (GuAE), which augments each rollout group's reward set with anchor points {0,1} to guarantee a strictly positive normalization scale and non-zero directional signal in fully collapsed groups, then applies Variance-Adaptive Tempering (VAT) that uses a sigmoid-gated exponent to sharpen advantages in low-variance groups and dampen them in high-variance groups. This prevents advantage collapse under sparse, near-binary GUI rewards without altering the GRPO objective, enabling the policy to refine faithful abstainment actions while preserving general instruction-following performance.

## Results

Faithful-Agent (Stage II) achieves an overall success rate of 71.67% and a Trap success rate of 80.21%, compared to 41.08% and 13.88% for the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone, respectively. On the General split, it reaches 65.62% SR (vs. 60.38% for the backbone), confirming that faithfulness gains do not come at the cost of general instruction-following. The Trap Type accuracy jumps from 36.63% to 91.29%, and Overall Type accuracy rises from 63.64% to 88.04%, indicating substantially better action-type prediction under evidence perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Faithful-Agent (Stage II) outperforms all five zero-shot baselines on every metric across both Trap and General splits. The most dramatic gap is on Trap SR: Faithful-Agent reaches 80.21% versus 13.88% for the Qwen3-VL-8B backbone, 26.88% for UI-TARS-1.5-7B, and a mere 3.99% for OS-Atlas-Pro-7B, which excels on General (64.36% SR) but is nearly useless under evidence perturbations. On General SR, Faithful-Agent (65.62%) edges out OS-Atlas-Pro-7B (64.36%) and the backbone (60.38%), while GUI-Owl-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B trail at 41.82% and 39.10% respectively. Stage I alone already lifts Trap SR to 71.20%, showing that SFT-based abstainment instillation captures most of the faithfulness gain, while Stage II (GRPO+GuAE) adds a further ~9-point Trap SR improvement through stabilized advantage estimation under sparse rewards. Cross-dataset transfer (AC↔AITZ) confirms that Stage II consistently improves over Stage I on both splits, indicating the gains are not dataset-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) as its policy-optimization backbone, but departs from prior GUI-agent RFT work (OS-Atlas, UI-TARS, GUI-Owl, ARPO, UI-R1) that optimizes task completion performance by reframing the objective as step-wise faithfulness—evidence groundedness plus internal consistency with abstainment under missing or conflicting evidence. It also extends the broader faithfulness-in-VLMs literature (which has focused on detecting or eliciting unfaithfulness) by proposing a training pipeline that actively instills faithful behavior. The key technical departure is GuAE, an anchor-based, variance-adaptive advantage tempering mechanism that addresses a specific collapse mode in GRPO arising from near-binary rewards on discrete enumerated actions, rather than treating stabilization as a general-purpose training improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important failure mode—GUI agents that rely on memorized shortcuts rather than conditioning on current screen evidence—which is a real deployment risk for mobile automation. The two-stage SFT+GRPO recipe with GuAE is a reusable training pipeline for anyone applying group-based RL to sparse-reward, near-binary action spaces. However, the evaluation relies on a self-constructed dataset with controlled perturbations (occlusion, content tampering, goal shifts) rather than open-ended real-world GUI environments, and the rule-based thought-action consistency reward may not scale to more complex reasoning traces. Open questions include whether faithfulness-first training transfers to truly adversarial or long-horizon settings, and whether the advantage-collapse diagnosis generalizes beyond the specific action structure studied here.
