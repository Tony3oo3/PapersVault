## Problem

Distributionally robust off-policy evaluation and learning (OPE/L) for continuous treatments remains underdeveloped: existing DRO methods are restricted to discrete actions, and the closest continuous-treatment work (Leung et al., 2025) relies on IPW estimators whose performance is tightly coupled to the convergence rate of nonparametric propensity density estimates. Additionally, pricing outcomes are inherently discontinuous due to threshold-based purchase decisions, violating the smooth outcome assumptions required by prior continuous-treatment DRO frameworks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265284/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes logged observational data as triplets (context vector x_t ∈ R^d, continuous price p_t ∈ [p_min, 1], binary purchase indicator y_t), along with a target policy or policy class Π, a KL-divergence uncertainty radius δ, and a kernel bandwidth h. For OPE, it outputs a 4-dimensional parameter vector [α̂, Ŵ_0, Ŵ_1, R̂_δ] where R̂_δ is the distributionally robust policy value; for OPL, it outputs a pricing policy π̂: X → P that maximizes the empirical robust value. The OPE estimator requires only three nuisance regressions (one propensity, two outcome), while OPL requires a continuum of outcome regressions estimated via data-driven forest weights.

## Method

The paper proposes two algorithms: LDR²O²PE-CP for evaluation and CDR²O²PL-CP for learning, both built on a KL-divergence DRO dual formulation involving moment generating functions. For OPE, a localization technique fits nuisance regressions only at a preliminary α_init estimate rather than over the full continuum α ∈ (0, 1/δ], reducing the problem to solving a 4-dimensional moment equation via Newton-Raphson while achieving √(Th)-consistency and semiparametric efficiency under a product rate condition. For OPL, generalized random forests construct data-driven leaf-membership weights that evaluate the continuum of outcome regressions m_0(x,p;α) = Σ_t ω_t(x,p)·exp(−p_t y_t/α) at any α without retraining, and an alternating optimization scheme iterates between updating α and searching over the policy class. The key theoretical insight is that s-smoothness of the demand noise CDF (s=1,2) suffices to handle the discontinuous revenue function, yielding a finite-sample regret bound of Õ_p(T^{−s/(2s+1)}) that strictly improves upon the O_p(T^{−1/4}) rate of prior DRO-IPW methods for continuous treatments.

## Results

On the Expedia Personalized Sort Competition dataset (T=500 training, 2500 test samples, δ_train=0.1, δ_test=0.2), the proposed CDR²O²PL-CP achieves a worst-case robust revenue of 6.350 ± 0.598, outperforming DRO-IPW (6.254 ± 0.873) and non-robust DR (6.201 ± 0.683). In simulation, LDR²O²PE-CP achieves lower MSE than DRO-IPW for the distributionally robust policy value estimation once T ≥ 1000, with a faster convergence rate across all bandwidth choices and test radii δ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3}. Theoretically, the paper establishes a finite-sample regret bound of Õ_p(T^{-s/(2s+1)}) for s=1,2, improving upon the O_p(T^{-1/4}) rate of prior DRO-based continuous-treatment methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: DRO-IPW (Leung et al., 2025) and non-robust DR (Ai et al., 2026). On the Expedia empirical study, CDR²O²PL-CP wins on both mean robust revenue (6.350 vs. 6.254 for DRO-IPW and 6.201 for non-robust DR) and standard error (0.598 vs. 0.873 and 0.683), indicating both higher worst-case performance and greater stability. In the simulation OPE task, LDR²O²PE-CP achieves lower MSE than DRO-IPW for T ≥ 1000 across all bandwidth and δ settings, though DRO-IPW is more stable in the small-sample regime (T < 1000). In the simulation OPL task, CDR²O²PL-CP achieves the highest distributionally robust policy value with tighter confidence intervals than DRO-IPW for large T, while the non-robust DR baseline exhibits substantially wider confidence intervals, reflecting its brittleness under distributional shifts. Theoretically, the proposed Õ_p(T^{-s/(2s+1)}) regret bound strictly improves upon the O_p(T^{-1/4}) rate of Leung et al. (2025) for both s=1 (rate T^{-1/3}) and s=2 (rate T^{-2/5}).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the localized debiased machine learning framework of Kallus et al. (2022, 2024) from discrete to continuous treatment settings, combining it with kernel-smoothed doubly robust estimation to handle the non-trivial coupling between the DRO dual parameter α and the outcome regressions. The closest prior work, Leung et al. (2025), formulates DRO OPE/L for continuous treatments using kernel-smoothed IPW, but its performance is tied to propensity density convergence rates; this paper's DR approach achieves semiparametric efficiency under milder product-rate conditions. The pricing-specific treatment of discontinuous revenue outcomes (via smoothness of the demand noise CDF) departs from the general continuous-treatment literature, which typically assumes smooth outcome functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a gap between the O_p(T^{-1/4}) regret rate of prior DRO methods for continuous treatments and the Õ_p(T^{-s/(2s+1)}) rate achievable in non-robust settings, making distributionally robust pricing theoretically competitive with its non-robust counterpart. Practically, the OPE algorithm requires only three nuisance regressions (comparable to standard non-robust DR), avoiding the intractable continuum of regressions that the DRO-DR coupling would otherwise demand. Open questions include whether the KL-divergence uncertainty set is the most appropriate geometry for pricing applications (Wasserstein alternatives are mentioned but not explored), whether the alternating optimization in the OPL algorithm can be made globally convergent, and how the framework scales to high-dimensional context spaces.
