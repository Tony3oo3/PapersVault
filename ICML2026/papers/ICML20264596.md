## Problem

Latent world models for planning produce highly curved latent trajectories, causing Euclidean distance in embedding space to misrepresent geodesic distance and making gradient-based planning objectives highly non-convex. Pretrained visual encoders like DINOv2 provide strong semantic features but are not tailored to planning dynamics, and prior work has not studied how straightening latent trajectories affects world modeling and planning performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264596/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB observations (current state and goal image) plus optional proprioceptive states as input, and produces optimized action sequences for goal-reaching. The encoder outputs spatial features (14×14×8) or global vectors (1×384), and the ViT predictor rolls out latent dynamics over a horizon of 5 steps (frameskip 5, 25 environment steps). Planning uses gradient descent through the learned predictor to minimize MSE between the predicted terminal latent state and the goal embedding, with a weighted intermediate-state loss for maze environments.

## Method

The paper introduces temporal straightening: a curvature regularizer that penalizes the angle between consecutive latent velocity vectors (v_t = z_{t+1} - z_t) via a cosine-similarity loss, applied jointly with a JEPA prediction objective (MSE with stop-gradient on the target branch). The world model consists of a sensory encoder (frozen DINOv2 + trainable CNN projector, or ResNet from scratch), an action encoder, and a ViT predictor with temporal causal attention. For spatial features, a learnable aggregation head maps patch features to a global vector before computing the curvature loss, allowing the straightening to act on global trajectory geometry while preserving local spatial detail. Theoretically, the paper proves that ε-straight transitions (‖A−I‖₂ ≤ ε) bound the effective condition number of the planning Hessian by κ(B)²e^{6εK}, showing that reduced curvature directly improves gradient-based optimization convergence. Empirically, straightening yields 20–60% open-loop and 20–30% MPC success rate improvements over DINO-WM baselines across Wall, PointMaze, and PushT environments.

## Results

On four 2D goal-reaching environments (Wall, PointMaze-UMaze, PointMaze-Medium, PushT), temporal straightening with a DINOv2+projector encoder (14×14×8 spatial features) achieves 90.7% open-loop and 100% MPC success on Wall, 94.0% open-loop and 100% MPC on UMaze, 82.7% open-loop and 98.7% MPC on Medium, and 77.3% open-loop and 85.3% MPC on PushT. Compared to the frozen DINOv2 CLS baseline (DINO-WM), open-loop success improves by 20–60% and MPC by 20–30% across environments. A ResNet trained from scratch with straightening also reaches 84.7–100% MPC success on Wall/UMaze/Medium and 91.3% MPC on PushT, demonstrating that the gains are not specific to a particular encoder architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DINO-WM (Zhou et al., 2025), which plans directly in frozen DINOv2 feature space using CEM. The proposed method consistently outperforms DINO-WM across all four environments and both open-loop and MPC settings. For example, on Wall, DINOv2 CLS achieves only 28.7% open-loop success while the straightened DINOv2+projector reaches 90.7% (+62 pp); on PushT, the gap is 19.3% → 77.3% (+58 pp) for open-loop. Even without explicit curvature regularization, simply training a lightweight projector on frozen DINOv2 patches (or a ResNet from scratch) already improves over the frozen DINOv2 CLS baseline, which the authors attribute to implicit straightening induced by the JEPA prediction objective. Adding the explicit curvature regularizer (λ=0.1) provides a further 10+ pp boost in most settings. The paper also compares GD vs. CEM planners: CEM achieves slightly higher absolute success rates but is ~10× slower in wall-clock time; straightening narrows the GD–CEM gap substantially, making gradient-based planning a practical and competitive alternative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the JEPA world-model framework (LeCun, 2022) and the DINO-WM baseline (Zhou et al., 2025) by adding a curvature regularizer that straightens latent trajectories during encoder-predictor training. The idea is inspired by the perceptual straightening hypothesis in human vision (Henaff et al., 2019) and prior observations of implicit straightening in pretrained encoders (Harrington et al., 2023; Niu et al., 2024), but is the first to explicitly study its effect on world-model planning. The theoretical analysis draws on classical controllability Gramians from linear systems theory, while the method is positioned as orthogonal to and complementary with temporal contrastive learning, smoothness penalties, and Koopman-based linearization approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that representation geometry—not just prediction accuracy—is a first-order factor for latent planning, and that a simple cosine-similarity regularizer can make gradient-based planning competitive with (and much faster than) search-based planners like CEM. The clean theoretical bound linking trajectory curvature to the planning Hessian's condition number provides a principled explanation for the empirical gains. Key limitations include the restriction to 2D goal-reaching tasks, the linear-dynamics assumption in the theory, and the use of a symmetric Euclidean cost that may be suboptimal under irreversible dynamics; extending the analysis to nonlinear predictors and asymmetric (quasimetric) costs are natural next steps.
