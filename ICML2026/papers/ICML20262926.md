## Problem

Relational Deep Learning faces a fundamental tension: Transformers' quadratic complexity in sequence length makes it intractable to process the millions of rows in real-world databases, while GNNs sacrifice global context for efficiency. Compounding this, existing models fail to generalize to unseen schemas at test time, because robust cross-schema generalization requires processing extensive contextual information that quadratic architectures cannot handle. The paper argues that linear-time processing is not merely a computational convenience but a prerequisite for generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multi-table relational database and a target row/attribute, constructing a context subgraph via width-limited BFS with foreign-key prioritization and strict causality constraints. Cells are embedded by fusing a frozen sentence-transformer schema embedding (all-MiniLM-L12-v2, 384-dim) with type-specific value encoders (piecewise-linear for numerics, learned embeddings for categoricals, sinusoidal for timestamps), then serialized column-by-column into a single 1D sequence with learnable separator tokens. Output is a class probability vector (softmax over vocabulary) for categorical targets or a scalar (MLP with column mean/std rescaling) for numerical targets, trained with a unified Huber + cross-entropy objective over masked cells.

## Method

RAMBA introduces two complementary mechanisms atop a bidirectional Mamba backbone. First, Topology-Aware Linearization decouples distributional modeling from structural reasoning: global columnar serialization concatenates all subgraph cells column-by-column (preserving attribute-wise statistics in O(L) time), while Sparse Structural Attention recovers relational topology via two binary masks—an intra-entity mask aggregating attributes within the same row and a relational mask propagating information along foreign-key edges—keeping attention cost linear since per-cell neighbor count is bounded by sampling degree. Second, Schema Dynamic Gating modulates the SSM discretization step Δ_t by a semantic relevance gate computed from frozen LM embeddings of the current column's schema metadata versus the target attribute's description; when the gate approaches zero (semantically irrelevant column), the discretized transition matrix approaches identity and the input matrix approaches zero, effectively freezing the hidden state to preserve relevant memory while bypassing noise from distant foreign tables. A theoretical result (Theorem 5.1) shows the signal-to-noise ratio scales as Ω((1−ε)·n_R / (δ·L)), depending on the fraction of relevant data rather than total context size, and that this filtering generalizes to unseen schemas because gates derive from schema semantics rather than value distributions.

## Results

On RelBench zero-shot cross-database classification, RAMBA achieves 72.5% average AUROC, outperforming Relational Transformer (RT, 69.3%) and Griffin (64.0%). For zero-shot regression, RAMBA reaches 25.2% average R² versus 21.9% (RT) and 8.9% (Griffin). After fine-tuning, RAMBA attains 75.3% AUROC (classification) and 38.3% R² (regression), a 43% relative improvement over the strongest fine-tuned baseline (RT, 26.8% R²). On the SALT ERP benchmark (RelBench v2), RAMBA achieves 0.77 average MRR, surpassing RT (0.75), HGT (0.69), and GraphSAGE (0.67).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAMBA (12M params) outperforms the strongest zero-shot baseline, Relational Transformer (RT, 22M params), by 3.2 points in average AUROC (72.5 vs. 69.3) and by 3.3 points in average R² (25.2 vs. 21.9) in the zero-shot setting. The gap widens substantially after fine-tuning: RAMBA achieves 38.3% R² versus RT's 26.8% (43% relative improvement) on regression. Against Griffin (22M params), RAMBA leads by 8.5 points in zero-shot AUROC and 16.3 points in zero-shot R². Notably, RAMBA surpasses the 3B-parameter RelLLM (63.2% AUROC) by 9.3 points despite having ~250× fewer parameters, demonstrating that architectural inductive biases for relational structure matter more than raw model scale. On the SALT ERP benchmark with industrial naming conventions, RAMBA edges out RT (0.77 vs. 0.75 MRR) and clearly beats HGT (0.69) and GraphSAGE (0.67), confirming that Schema Dynamic Gating generalizes beyond clean natural-language column names. RAMBA does not win every individual task (e.g., RT leads on rel-f1/driver-top3 zero-shot at 89.1 vs. 87.4, and rel-avito/user-visits at 61.4 vs. 61.2), but its average advantage is consistent and statistically significant on most tasks (p < 0.001 for 7 of 10 classification tasks).

## Relation to Prior Work

RAMBA adapts the selective state-space model (Mamba) to relational deep learning, departing from both the dominant GNN paradigm (limited receptive fields) and the emerging Transformer-based relational foundation models (RT, Griffin) that inherit quadratic attention cost. Its two core mechanisms—Topology-Aware Linearization (global columnar serialization with sparse structural attention) and Schema Dynamic Gating (modulating SSM discretization via frozen LM embeddings of column names)—are novel combinations: the serialization strategy repurposes the 'sequential reading + associative jumping' cognitive metaphor into a concrete two-stage encoding, while the gating mechanism is the first to use schema semantics rather than value distributions to control information flow in a relational model. The paper also builds on the RelBench benchmark and the leave-one-database-out evaluation protocol established by Robinson et al. (2024) and Ranjan et al. (2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 12M-parameter SSM-based model can match or exceed 22M-parameter Transformer baselines (and substantially outperform a 3B LLM) on relational prediction while scaling linearly in context length, RAMBA opens a viable architectural path for processing real-world databases with millions of rows that are intractable for quadratic-attention models. The finding that shuffled schema names perform worse than no gating at all validates that the mechanism genuinely exploits semantic structure rather than memorizing spurious correlations, strengthening the case for schema-aware inductive biases in relational foundation models. Open questions include whether fixed column ordering can be replaced by learned or adaptive orderings, how the approach scales to databases with truly massive fan-out (the 1.27M in-degree hubs in rel-avito), and whether SSMs can match Transformers on very deep multi-hop relational reasoning beyond the 7-hop sampling depth tested here.
