## Problem

Sentence-level text watermarking supports black-box deployment and robustness to paraphrasing, but existing methods suffer from low and unstable Watermark Success Rate (WSR). The paper identifies the root cause as a geometric mismatch: sentence encoders produce anisotropic, concentrated embedding distributions, so randomly selected watermark regions capture insufficient probability mass, causing injection to fail before detection even begins.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263847/cropped_page3_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

At injection, the method takes a text prompt (first 50 tokens), a secret key k, and the LLM's stochastic generation process; it encodes each candidate sentence via a fine-tuned BGE-m3 encoder (1024-dim, L2-normalized), computes the anchor-relative residual between consecutive sentence embeddings, and applies rejection sampling (up to T=100 attempts) until the quantized signature falls in the key-derived accept region. At verification, given a text of S sentences and the same secret key, it reconstructs per-sentence accept regions and outputs a z-score (threshold Z_r=4) indicating watermark presence, requiring as few as 7 sentences for reliable detection.

## Method

X-Guard improves injection success from two complementary angles. First, it fine-tunes the BGE-m3 encoder with an Alignment-Uniformity (A&U) contrastive loss that decouples semantic alignment (pulling paraphrase pairs together) from distributional uniformity (spreading embeddings over the unit hypersphere), yielding a more spherical posterior embedding distribution. Second, it introduces A²PQ (Anchor Angular Product Quantization), which partitions the semantic space using anchor-relative residuals (z_t − z_{t−1}) rather than raw embeddings, splits each residual into M=3 subvectors, and clusters each subspace with k-means under cosine distance so that hyperplane boundaries intersect at the posterior centroid and approximately equalize probability mass across K^M=8 cells. The accept region is derived deterministically via a cryptographic hash of the secret key and the previous sentence's quantized signature, making it recoverable under meaning-preserving edits. A formal analysis of injection success probability (p_is) shows that equalizing cell-wise mass μ_j nearly doubles p_is from 44.9% to 89.9% in a toy setting, and the z-test-based verifier aggregates evidence across sentences to control false positives.

## Results

On LLaMA3.2-3B with high-resource languages, X-Guard achieves 99.65% WSR and 99.80% AUC under no attack, outperforming the best token-level baseline SynthID (99.45%/99.90%) and the best sentence-level baseline SemStamp (86.50%/96.90%). Under the strongest optimized adaptive attack, X-Guard retains 47.15% WSR and 73.38% AUC, compared to 33.00%/56.80% for SemStamp and <0.2% WSR for all token-level methods. On low-resource languages, X-Guard achieves 95.58% WSR (no attack) and 35.97% WSR (adaptive attack), exceeding SemStamp by +30.78 and +10.67 points respectively. Across four downstream tasks (ELI5, FinQA, MultiNews, QMSum), X-Guard introduces the smallest mean quality drop (0.7% high-res / 1.75% low-res) while maintaining >99% WSR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against token-level baselines (KGW, Uni-WM, SynthID), X-Guard is competitive or superior under no attack (99.65% vs. 99.45% WSR for SynthID) but pulls dramatically ahead under semantic attacks: under mono-lingual paraphrase, all token-level methods collapse to <1% WSR while X-Guard retains 49.61%, and under the optimized adaptive attack the gap widens to 47.15% vs. <0.2%. Against the closest sentence-level baseline SemStamp, X-Guard improves no-attack WSR by +13.15 points (99.65% vs. 86.50%) and adaptive-attack WSR by +14.15 points (47.15% vs. 33.00%), with AUC gains of +10.65 and +16.58 respectively. SimMark, the other sentence-level baseline, is consistently weaker than SemStamp across all settings (e.g., 78.95% vs. 86.50% no-attack WSR), and X-Guard outperforms it by +20.70 WSR points under no attack. On low-resource languages the advantage over SemStamp is even larger: +30.78 WSR points under no attack (95.58% vs. 64.80%), confirming that the geometry-aware design generalizes better to under-resourced settings. In terms of efficiency, X-Guard reduces generation overhead to ×5.5 relative to unwatermarked, compared to ×18.0 for SemStamp, while requiring only 7 sentences for reliable detection versus 76 for SemStamp at T=10.

## Relation to Prior Work

X-Guard extends the sentence-level negative-sampling watermarking paradigm established by SemStamp (Hou et al., 2024a) and its k-means variant k-SemStamp, but reframes the core bottleneck as a geometric mismatch between posterior embedding distributions and accept-region selection rather than a detection-sensitivity problem. The partitioning mechanism A²PQ is a direct modification of Product Quantization (Jégou et al., 2011), replacing Euclidean k-means with cosine-distance k-means on anchor-relative residuals so that hyperplane boundaries intersect at the posterior centroid. The encoder training objective is the Alignment-Uniformity contrastive loss from Wang & Isola (2020), repurposed here to promote isotropic embedding geometry specifically to equalize cell-wise probability mass for watermarking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric diagnosis—that injection success probability, not detection sensitivity, is the primary WSR bottleneck—provides a principled lens for future sentence-level watermarking design and explains why prior methods (random LSH, standard k-means) underperform. Practically, the 3× efficiency gain over SemStamp (5.5× vs 18× slowdown) and the requirement of only 7 sentences for detection (vs 76) make black-box sentence-level watermarking more deployable. However, WSR still drops from ~95% to ~40–50% under paraphrasing and translation attacks, leaving a significant robustness gap; the paper does not fully resolve the tension between semantic invariance and region-specificity that is inherent to any embedding-space partitioning approach.
