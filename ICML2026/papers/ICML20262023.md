## Problem

Existing data attribution estimators like TracIn yield a single scalar influence score that aggregates over all loop iterations in looped transformers, obscuring when during the recurrent computation a training example matters. This prevents practitioners from localising influence to specific latent reasoning steps, detecting signal cancellation across steps, or calibrating test-time compute budgets based on where training data actually impacts the model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262023/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a set of training checkpoints, a training example z, a query example z', and loop horizon τ, the method produces a length-τ influence trajectory where each entry quantifies the influence of z on a specific loop step of z'. Rather than materialising full per-example gradients of dimension |w_body| (≈100M in their GPT-2 experiment), the method outputs sketched vectors of dimension m per parameter tensor (m=2048 in practice), concatenated across all parameter tensors, reducing memory by ~1000×. The trajectory losslessly sums to the standard scalar TracIn score, preserving compatibility with existing influence-based analyses.

## Method

SDI unrolls the recurrent computation graph via backpropagation through time, decomposing the total body gradient into per-step terms φ_t (each a sum of outer products of backpropagated signals and forward activations over tokens), then defines step-localised influence as the dot product of the full training gradient with a single φ_t. To make this practical at transformer scale, the method applies TensorSketch (FFT-based circular convolution of two CountSketches) to matrix-parameter outer products and CountSketch to vector parameters directly during backpropagation via forward hooks, never instantiating full per-example gradients. The authors derive a tighter variance bound of (4/m² + 6/m)‖g‖²‖p‖² for the sketched dot product, strictly improving on the 8/m bound in prior TensorSketch work, and prove the bound is tight. The conservation identity guarantees that summing the SDI trajectory exactly recovers standard TracIn, bridging static influence scores with dynamic latent computation.

## Results

On a 135.1M-parameter looped GPT (τ=32), the TensorSketch SDI implementation matches the full stepwise-gradient baseline with relative errors of 0.0388 ± 0.0030 (SDI) and 0.0220 ± 0.0052 (TracIn) at sketch dimension m=2048, while enabling a 10× increase in feasible batch size (4→40) and ~1000× less memory. The counterfactual validation on Sudoku shows that removing the top 30% of late-step SDI-influential training examples drops hard-puzzle accuracy from 95.8±2.7% to 91.7±1.8% (p=0.003) while barely affecting easy puzzles (98.7→98.0%). On the 328M-parameter Nanochat model, SDI reveals that the last loop step alone contributes ≥50% of total influence (0.604±0.026 at τ=8 in SFT), and a linear probe decodes the loop step from latent activations with 98.56% accuracy (vs. 25% chance).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark SDI against competing data-attribution methods (e.g., Influence Functions, TracIn variants) in a head-to-head accuracy comparison; instead, it validates SDI against the full-gradient TracIn baseline it generalises. The sketched SDI estimator reproduces scalar TracIn to numerical precision (~10⁻⁷) and matches full per-example gradient computation with only 3.9% relative error at m=2048, confirming the sketching introduces negligible distortion. The key differentiator from scalar TracIn is revealed in the signal-cancellation experiment: a training/test pair with TracIn ≈ 0.001 (near-irrelevant) actually has Σ|SDI_t| = 17.24, exposing large opposing positive (+8.62) and negative (−8.62) stepwise effects that the scalar score collapses. The counterfactual removal experiment further demonstrates that SDI enables targeted interventions (removing specifically late-step influential data) that scalar TracIn cannot formulate, as scalar TracIn can only rank by total influence without distinguishing which loop steps drive it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TracIn gradient-similarity influence estimator (Pruthi et al., 2020) from a scalar score to a step-resolved trajectory by exploiting the weight-tied recurrent structure of looped transformers, where the total body gradient decomposes losslessly into per-step terms via BPTT. For efficient computation, it adapts TensorSketch (Pham & Pagh, 2025) and CountSketch (Charikar et al., 2002) into a streaming sketch-during-backprop pipeline that avoids materialising per-example gradients, deriving variance bounds strictly tighter than prior TensorSketch analysis. Conceptually, it departs from prior stepwise attribution in recurrent models (e.g., REAT, TimeSHAP), which attribute importance to input timesteps, by instead decomposing training-data influence across the model's internal loop iterations of a shared parameter block.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDI enables practitioners to localise *when* training data matters during latent reasoning, supporting test-time compute calibration, depth-targeted data curation, and detection of signal cancellation invisible to scalar influence scores. The discovery that a 330M-parameter looped LLM implicitly encodes a 'loop step counter' in its latent state—concentrating influence geometrically in the final few iterations without explicit step embeddings—is a notable conceptual finding, though validated on a single model. Open questions include extending SDI to optimizer-aware inner products, scaling full-horizon attribution to very long recurrences, and applying step-resolved influence to RLHF/alignment pipelines where late-step failures are most consequential.
