## Problem

In long-context Transformers, softmax normalisation causes individual attention probabilities to shrink with context length, raising the question of whether tokens remain distinguishable in relative terms. While temperature scaling is a common remedy, no formal characterisation exists for how token distinguishability (the log-ratios between attention probabilities) depends on context length and embedding dimension, nor for the precise relationship between inverse-temperature scaling and this distinguishability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261569/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes attention logits produced by the dot product of Gaussian query and key vectors (dimension d), a context length L, and an optional inverse-temperature scaling function T(L). It produces the exact PDF of the Aitchison distance to uniform (a K-distribution family involving modified Bessel functions), the normalised Aitchison distance (equivalent to the L2 norm of centred attention logits divided by √L, avoiding the numerical issue of taking logs of near-zero probabilities), and a necessary lower bound Ω(√log L) on T(L) for maintaining a constant expected log maximum probability. The normalisation by √L removes the dimensional growth so that distances are comparable across different context lengths.

## Method

The paper introduces Aitchison distance from Compositional Data Analysis as a metric on the probability simplex, showing that the centred log-ratio (clr) transform of attention probabilities equals the centred attention logits, so the Aitchison distance to uniform reduces to the L2 norm of the centred logits. Under the standard assumption of independent Gaussian query and key vectors, the attention logits follow a Gaussian distribution with Gamma-distributed variance (a K-distribution family), from which the authors derive the exact PDF, MGF, and moments of the Aitchison distance. They prove that the normalised Aitchison distance converges to a finite non-zero constant as L→∞, establishing that relative token distinguishability does not vanish. Exploiting the linear scaling of Aitchison distance with inverse-temperature, they derive a necessary Ω(√log L) lower bound on T(L) for the expected log maximum probability to remain bounded below by a constant. Empirically, they validate the measure by training Pythia-1B models with different temperature scalings and by comparing OpenLLaMA-3B with its length-extrapolated LongLLaMA-3B, showing Aitchison distance captures hierarchical reorganisation of small probability components that KL divergence misses.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is that the expectation of the normalised Aitchison distance to uniform converges to a finite, non-zero limit of √(2c)·Γ((d+1)/2)/Γ(d/2) as context length L→∞, proving that token distinguishability does not vanish with context length. The second key result is a necessary lower bound of Ω(√log L) on the inverse-temperature scaling function T(L) required to keep the expected log maximum probability constant (Proposition 4.1). Empirically, the lognormal proxy shape parameter σ₁²→1 as embedding dimension d→∞, confirming the tightness of the distributional approximation. In the temperature scaling experiments (Figure 3), T(L)=(log L)² and T(L)=log L both saturate KL divergence after ~15,000 iterations while Aitchison distance continues growing, revealing that smaller probability components keep shrinking beyond what KL captures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KL divergence as a measure of attention flatness, Aitchison distance is shown to be strictly more informative: in the OpenLLaMA-3B vs LongLLaMA-3B comparison (Figure 5, Layer 6), Aitchison distance indicates LongLLaMA has *higher* token distinguishability (less uniform) while normalised KL divergence indicates *lower* distinguishability (more uniform) — the two measures disagree on the direction of the distributional shift, with Aitchison correctly capturing the hierarchical reorganisation of smaller probability components in LongLLaMA's memory layers. Against the concurrent √log L scaling of Giorlandino & Goldt (2026), this paper arrives at the same order but via a different route: independent Gaussian query/key vectors yielding an exact PDF (K-distribution) rather than a correlated-Gaussian/REM mapping, and the bound is derived as a *necessary* condition for constant expected log max probability rather than a phase-transition threshold. Against the log L scaling of Chen et al. (2026) and Jianlin (2021), Figure 10 shows that T(L)=0.75·log L causes the maximum probability to *increase* with context length (over-sharpening), while T(L)=2·√log L keeps it approximately constant, suggesting log L is a sufficient but not tight bound and √log L is the correct necessary order.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Aitchison geometry from Compositional Data Analysis (Aitchison, 2003) as a new lens for analyzing softmax attention, departing from the Euclidean-distance and entropy-based frameworks used in prior vanishing-attention work (Mudarisov et al., 2025; Veličković et al., 2025). For temperature scaling, it sits alongside concurrent theoretical efforts (Giorlandino & Goldt, 2026; Chen et al., 2026) but derives its Ω(√log L) lower bound from the full distribution of Aitchison distance under a Gaussian query-key model, rather than from phase-transition or entropy-invariance arguments. The lognormal proxy for attention probabilities follows Nahshan et al. (2024), and the empirical monitoring experiments build on the Pythia and OpenLLaMA/LongLLaMA model families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean formalization showing that token distinguishability in relative (log-ratio) terms is non-vanishing with context length, reframing 'vanishing attention' as an absolute-magnitude phenomenon rather than a loss of relative structure. The Ω(√log L) lower bound gives practitioners a theoretically grounded minimum for temperature scaling, and Aitchison distance offers a practical monitoring tool that captures small-probability-component shifts invisible to KL divergence. Limitations include the reliance on Gaussian query-key assumptions at initialization (not post-training dynamics), the bound being necessary rather than sufficient, and empirical validation on relatively small models (1B–3B). Open questions include extending the analysis to trained (non-Gaussian) attention distributions and multi-layer stacking effects.
