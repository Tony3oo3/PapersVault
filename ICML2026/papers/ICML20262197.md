## Problem

Multi-turn RL for user-centric agents suffers from a long-horizon credit assignment problem: trajectory-level rewards in GRPO-based methods are sparse and delayed, yielding near-zero group-relative advantages when rollout groups produce similar outcomes. This is especially acute in user-centric settings where a small number of clarification turns determine downstream feasibility, yet standard methods aggregate all signals into a single trajectory score, providing no fine-grained supervision for which interaction turns were actually informative.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multi-turn interaction transcript (system prompt, alternating user/assistant messages, and environment observations) formatted in the base model's chat template, with a maximum of 16–50 turns and 1024 tokens per turn depending on the benchmark. It produces a unified token-level advantage signal that drives PPO-style policy updates, where each token's advantage is a weighted combination of the trajectory-level outcome advantage and a turn-level information-gain advantage broadcast across the corresponding action segment. The counterfactual evaluation requires only teacher-forced forward passes on already-collected tokens (no extra environment rollouts), with a wall-clock overhead of roughly 1.63× on average.

## Method

InfoPO introduces a turn-level counterfactual information-gain reward that measures how much the received observation shifts the policy's log-probability of the realized next action compared to a masked-feedback counterfactual (replacing the observation with a 'No information found' placeholder), averaged over the next action's tokens. This reward is group-normalized and fused with the standard GRPO outcome advantage via an adaptive variance gate: a sigmoid function of the within-group standard deviation of external rewards amplifies the info-gain signal when outcomes are non-discriminative and suppresses it as task success becomes the dominant signal. The final objective is a PPO-clipped policy gradient with KL regularization against a reference policy. Theoretically, the paper proves the per-turn reward equals the conditional mutual information between feedback and the next action, the cumulative reward equals directed information from observations to decisions, and a minimum cumulative information gain is a strict lower bound on the information required to achieve a given task success probability (via Fano's inequality and the directed data processing inequality).

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, InfoPO achieves an overall average of 0.455 across UserGym, ColBench, and τ²-Bench, outperforming the strongest RL baseline RAGEN (0.410) by ~11% and UserRL (0.395) by ~15%. On ColBench, InfoPO reaches 0.534 Pass and 0.426 Success, slightly exceeding GPT-4.1 (0.529/0.403). On the Qwen3-4B backbone, InfoPO achieves 0.481 overall, surpassing UserRL (0.416) by ~16%. The paper reports consistent 14–16% improvements over GRPO-based methods across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoPO outperforms all three RL baselines (UserRL, RAGEN, Search-R1) on every benchmark and both model backbones. On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, the largest gains appear in cross-purpose generalization settings within UserGym (e.g., IntentionGym: 1.892 vs. 1.826 for UserRL; TelepathyGym: 0.488 vs. 0.424) and on ColBench where InfoPO (0.534 Pass) exceeds even GPT-4.1 (0.529). Against prompting baselines ReAct and Reflexion, InfoPO improves the overall average by roughly 58% (0.455 vs. 0.288/0.283), confirming the necessity of policy optimization over inference-time strategies. On the long-horizon τ²-Bench, InfoPO matches or slightly leads the best open-source baselines across all three domains (Telecom 0.181, Retail 0.188, Airline 0.163), despite the extreme interaction length (>30 turns) and severe data scarcity (178 tasks). Ablations show that removing the extrinsic reward (w/o R_ext) drops the average to 0.312, while removing the variance gate (w/o Gate) primarily hurts training stability rather than peak performance, indicating both components serve distinct roles.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfoPO builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its optimization backbone and addresses the credit-assignment weakness that plagues trajectory-level reward aggregation in multi-turn agent RL (UserRL, RAGEN, Search-R1). Conceptually, it repurposes the information-theoretic reward-shaping tradition (curiosity bonuses, empowerment objectives) by deriving a task-agnostic turn-level signal from a counterfactual masking comparison rather than from prediction error or novelty heuristics. The variance-gated fusion mechanism departs from fixed-weight multi-reward schemes (GDPO, ARIA) by making the intrinsic/extrinsic balance a function of within-group outcome discriminativeness, and the theoretical section grounds the signal in conditional mutual information and directed information (Massey, 1990; Kim, 2008), a connection not previously exploited for LLM agent training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InfoPO offers a principled, task-agnostic dense reward for multi-turn agent RL that avoids the need for process reward models, task-specific heuristics, or additional environment interactions, making it broadly applicable across interaction paradigms. The theoretical lower bound (Theorem 2) linking cumulative information gain to task success provides a rare formal guarantee in the LLM-agent RL literature. Open questions include whether the counterfactual signal remains informative when the policy is near-optimal (where distributional shifts shrink), how the method scales to even longer horizons or multi-agent settings, and whether the ~1.63× compute overhead becomes prohibitive at larger model scales. The reliance on LLM-simulated users for training also leaves open the question of transfer to genuinely heterogeneous human populations.
