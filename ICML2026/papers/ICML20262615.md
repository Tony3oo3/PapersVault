## Problem

Monolithic end-to-end neural simulators treat physics as an indivisible black box, leaving no access to intermediate quantities (forces, stress) and no mechanism to enforce known physical constraints, which undermines interpretability and long-horizon stability. Existing hybrid approaches that replace only a single module (e.g., constitutive law) sacrifice the scenario flexibility of data-driven methods for partial physical correctness, leaving open whether a fully modular neural simulator can maintain both flexibility and physical soundness across the entire elastic simulation pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-vertex positions and velocities on tetrahedral meshes (ranging from ~850 to ~10,648 vertices) along with boundary condition and collision specifications, and autoregressively produces next-step vertex positions and velocities. Crucially, the modular design also exposes intermediate physical quantities at module interfaces—per-element deformation gradients, first Piola-Kirchhoff stress, and assembled internal forces—which are used for direct supervision during training and enable analytical physical constraints (e.g., local volume preservation) at inference. The constitutive module operates per-element on the deformation gradient F, while the integration module operates per-vertex on (x, v, f_int) to predict a velocity increment Δv.

## Method

NMP decomposes the classical FEM computation flow into two learnable neural modules: a rotation-equivariant constitutive module (SVD-based MLP mapping F→P, following Ma et al. 2023) and an integration module (T-Net canonicalization + MLP predicting Δv from x, v, f_int), connected through the physically meaningful interface of internal forces. A two-stage training strategy prevents module collapse: each module is first pretrained independently with direct supervision on intermediate quantities (forces, velocities) from a differentiable numerical simulator, then jointly fine-tuned with an additional local volume-preservation hinge loss on det(F). This design enables plug-and-play interchange between neural and numerical sub-modules (e.g., swapping the learned integrator for a semi-implicit one, or the learned constitutive law for a St. Venant-Kirchhoff material) without retraining, and supports sim-to-real transfer by pretraining on simulated data and fine-tuning on position-only observations.

## Results

On 1,000-step rollouts, NMP achieves RMSE of 0.535 (CUBE), 0.440 (CUBEXL), 0.449 (SPOT), and 0.579 (BOB), representing over 60% error reduction compared to the best monolithic neural baseline (MeshGraphNet: 1.554, 1.963, 2.364, 2.104 respectively). NMP is also ~40× faster than the traditional physics simulator (2,250 ms vs. 90,275 ms for a 1,000-step BOB rollout) and ~5× faster than Transolver (11,796 ms). In the physics validation metric (tetrahedra exceeding 5% volume change over 1,000 steps on CUBE), NMP records 231,094 violations versus 479,566 (MGN), 1,271,827 (Transolver), and 1,595,317 (EGNO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NMP outperforms all three monolithic neural simulators (Transolver, EGNO, MeshGraphNet) on every environment and rollout horizon, with the gap widening dramatically at 1,000 steps where monolithic baselines diverge (e.g., Transolver reaches RMSE 3.200 on CUBE vs. NMP's 0.535). Even when baselines are enhanced to predict 100 steps per inference (reducing Transolver's 1,000-step RMSE from 1.782 to 0.75 on BOB), NMP's one-step autoregressive prediction (0.58) still wins. Against the hybrid neural-physics method NCLaw, NMP is ~43× faster in inference (2,250 ms vs. 97,121 ms) and uniquely handles the FRICTION environment with unknown contact dynamics, where NCLaw and the pure physics simulator both fail to model frictional effects. On the physics validation metric (volume preservation), NMP reduces violations by ~52% over MGN, ~82% over Transolver, and ~86% over EGNO, confirming superior physical consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant monolithic paradigm of neural simulators (MeshGraphNet, EGNO, Transolver) that treat the entire dynamics as a single end-to-end function, and from hybrid approaches (NCLaw, Bar-Sinai et al.) that replace only one component (e.g., the constitutive law) while retaining the rest of the classical pipeline. Drawing on the modular network literature (Mittal et al., 2022; Jarvis et al., 2023) and the natural decomposition of FEM into constitutive and integration sub-problems, NMP replaces the entire computation flow with successive neural modules connected through physically meaningful interfaces (deformation gradient, stress, internal force). The rotation-equivariant constitutive parameterization is inherited from Ma et al. (2023), but the paper's core contribution is the thorough modularization of the full pipeline plus a two-stage training strategy to prevent the 'collapse' of module specialization identified in the modular network literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NMP offers a practical path to combining the scenario flexibility of data-driven simulators (learning unknown friction, transferring to new materials) with the physical interpretability and constraint-enforcement of classical FEM, a combination that neither pure neural nor pure hybrid approaches achieve. The ability to expose intermediate quantities enables direct physical constraints (e.g., local volume preservation) and plug-and-play interchange between learned and analytic modules, which are genuinely new capabilities absent from prior neural simulators. However, the framework is currently limited to elastic simulation; extending it to fluid dynamics or other PDE families would require re-deriving the modular decomposition, and the two-stage training still requires access to intermediate physics during pretraining, limiting direct sim-to-real deployment without a simulated pretraining phase.
