## Problem

Foundation policies pre-trained on diverse data can handle many tasks zero-shot but fail on complex long-horizon problems that require composing multiple policies in sequence. The core technical obstacle is that compounding errors in long-horizon predictions make it difficult to accurately estimate the state visitation distribution induced by chaining policies, rendering compositional planning unreliable. This paper addresses this gap by learning multi-step 'jumpy' world models that capture policy-induced state occupancies across a continuum of timescales, enabling reliable evaluation of arbitrary policy sequences.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a current state s, a goal state g (parameterizing goal-conditioned policies), a reward function r, a repertoire of pre-trained policies {π_z} indexed by z ∈ S, and a learned geometric horizon model (GHM) conditioned on both policy encoding z and discount factor γ. It produces an optimal first action a₁*, a sequence of policy indices (z₁*, …, zₙ*) to execute, and Monte Carlo Q-value estimates for candidate geometric switching policies. The GHM operates in continuous d-dimensional state spaces and is trained entirely offline; planning is performed at test time via random shooting over m candidate sequences sampled from the GHM itself as a proposal distribution.

## Method

The core contribution is a policy- and horizon-conditioned jumpy world model trained with a novel Temporal Difference Horizon Consistency (TD-HC) loss that extends Temporal Difference Flows by enforcing coherence across timescales. Specifically, TD-HC exploits a Bellman-like decomposition of the successor measure at discount γ into three terms weighted by (1−γ), γ(1−γ)/(1−β), and γ(γ−β)/(1−β), where β < γ, thereby bootstrapping longer-horizon predictions from shorter-horizon ones to reduce compounding error. The model is a U-Net-style flow-matching network with FiLM conditioning on state, action, policy embedding z, and discount factor γ (embedded via sinusoidal + MLP with −log(1−γ) for horizon sensitivity). Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 1) that the successor measure of a Geometric Switching Policy decomposes as a weighted mixture of constituent policy successor measures, yielding an unbiased single-sample Monte Carlo estimator for the composite Q-value (Lemma 1). Planning (COMPPLAN) then reduces to random shooting: sample m candidate subgoal sequences from the GHM, evaluate each via the Lemma 1 estimator, and select the highest-value sequence, with distinct switching probabilities αᵢ per transition unifying GPI, GGPI, and action-level MPC as special cases.

## Results

On OGBench, COMPPLAN consistently improves zero-shot success rates across all five policy families and seven domains, with gains ranging from modest (e.g., HFBC on ANTMAZE-MEDIUM: 94%→94%) to dramatic (e.g., GC-BC on CUBE-2: 15%→97%). On long-horizon tasks, COMPPLAN achieves a 201% relative improvement over action-level planning (ACTIONPLAN) and an 89% relative improvement over Generalized Policy Improvement (GPI), averaged across policies and domains. Against state-of-the-art hierarchical baselines, COMPPLAN (with HFBC policies) reaches 79% on ANTMAZE-GIANT and 67% on CUBE-4, compared to 65%/0% for HIQL and 56%/9% for SHARSA on the same tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMPPLAN outperforms all five zero-shot base policies (CRL, GC-1S, GC-BC, GC-TD3, HFBC) across every domain, with the largest gains on long-horizon tasks (e.g., GC-BC on CUBE-2: 15%→97%; HFBC on ANTMAZE-GIANT: 42%→79%). Against action-level planning (ACTIONPLAN) with a dedicated one-step world model, COMPPLAN achieves a 201% relative improvement on long-horizon domains, confirming that temporal abstraction via jumpy predictions is critical. Against GPI (single-policy selection), COMPPLAN gains 89% relative improvement, showing that sequencing multiple policies at varying timescales is strictly better than committing to one. Against the two hierarchical baselines, COMPPLAN (using HFBC) wins on all seven domains: it reaches 79% vs. 65% (HIQL) and 56% (SHARSA) on ANTMAZE-GIANT, and 67% vs. 0% (HIQL) and 9% (SHARSA) on CUBE-4, demonstrating that test-time composition over pre-trained policies can surpass task-specific hierarchical training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Temporal Difference Flows (Farebrother et al., 2025) by introducing a horizon consistency objective (TD-HC) that enforces coherence between successor measure predictions at different discount factors, and generalizes Geometric Generalized Policy Improvement (Thakoor et al., 2022) from fixed switching probabilities to arbitrary per-policy switching, yielding a unified framework (COMPPLAN) that subsumes action-level MPC, GPI, and GGPI as special cases. The theoretical backbone builds on the successor measure Bellman equation (Janner et al., 2020) and flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023), while the planning procedure departs from hierarchical RL by performing composition entirely at test time over pre-trained policies without task-specific training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 200% relative improvement over action-level planning on long-horizon tasks, demonstrated across five policy classes and multiple maze/manipulation domains, provides strong evidence that temporal abstraction via policy composition is a practical and necessary ingredient for long-horizon decision-making with foundation policies. A nuanced finding is that the consistency loss substantially improves generative accuracy at long horizons (28% EMD reduction) yet yields only ~5% planning gains, revealing that the moderate horizons (50–100 steps) used in practice are already well-captured by base TD-FLOW — a useful calibration for practitioners. Open questions include state-dependent switching probabilities, joint learning of policies and predictive models, and whether jumpy world models in learned latent spaces can further reduce the compounding-error bottleneck.
