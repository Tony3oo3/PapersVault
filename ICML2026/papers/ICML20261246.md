## Problem

Category-level 6D pose estimation methods train all categories jointly with fully shared parameters, but geometric heterogeneity across categories causes gradient conflicts and negative transfer in shared modules. Existing work focuses on improving representations or geometric constraints within categories, leaving the cross-category optimization dynamics largely unaddressed. This paper identifies the correspondence module as the dominant source of contention and proposes a targeted architectural intervention to decouple incompatible optimization signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261246/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a cropped 224×224 RGB image, a 1024-point camera-frame point cloud, and a category label as input. Image features are extracted by a frozen DINOv2 encoder and projected onto points via camera intrinsics, while geometric features come from a trainable PointNet++ encoder; the two are concatenated per point into a shared 256-d canonical representation. Each active correspondence branch predicts 96 keypoints with associated NOCS coordinates, which are fed to a shared pose head of three parallel MLPs that regress the full 9DoF pose (rotation, translation, scale).

## Method

DecomPose selectively decouples only the correspondence module into G group-specific branches while keeping the backbone and pose head fully shared, based on gradient diagnostics showing the correspondence module has the highest category-specific heterogeneity ratio. Categories are routed offline via a difficulty-aware grouping function that uses a reference estimator's confidence scores to assign categories to quantile-based difficulty groups, with a lightweight boundary-refinement step for marginal categories. Branch capacity is allocated asymmetrically: high-capacity keypoint-centric branches (with cross-attention extraction, local geometry aggregation, and global context fusion) are assigned to easier categories as stable optimization anchors, while lightweight geometry-biased attention branches constrain harder categories to suppress noisy updates. Training is end-to-end with a combined objective of the main 9DoF pose loss and a routed branch-specific geometric loss (one-sided Chamfer, diversity margin, point-cloud reconstruction, and Smooth-L1 NOCS), where gradients from the branch loss back-propagate only through the active branch and shared backbone, isolating heterogeneous optimization dynamics.

## Results

DecomPose achieves state-of-the-art or near-best results across all three benchmarks. On REAL275, it attains 67.9 mAP at 5°5cm, 79.2 at 10°2cm, and 87.5 at 10°5cm, surpassing the strongest competitor CleanPose by 0.3–1.2 points. On CAMERA25, it leads all six metrics (e.g., 80.8 at 5°2cm, 92.8 at 10°5cm). On the more challenging HouseCat6D, it achieves 25.4 at 5°2cm and 58.9 at 10°5cm, outperforming GCE-Pose by 0.6 and 0.5 respectively, though it trails GCE-Pose on IoU75 (56.3 vs. 60.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest recent competitor CleanPose (ICCV'25), DecomPose wins on REAL275 at 5°5cm (+0.3), 10°2cm (+0.9), and 10°5cm (+1.2), but narrowly loses at 5°2cm (61.2 vs. 61.7). On CAMERA25, DecomPose beats CleanPose on every reported metric (e.g., +0.5 at 5°2cm, +0.2 at IoU75). On HouseCat6D, DecomPose outperforms CleanPose by 3.0 at 5°2cm and 2.4 at 10°5cm, though CleanPose retains a small edge on IoU50 (79.8 vs. 79.4). Compared to the AG-Pose baseline (CVPR'24), DecomPose gains 4.1–4.2 points at 5°2cm on REAL275 and 4.1 points on HouseCat6D, confirming that the decomposition strategy yields substantial improvements over the fully shared architecture. Against GCE-Pose (CVPR'25), DecomPose leads on pose accuracy metrics (5°2cm, 5°5cm, 10°5cm) on both REAL275 and HouseCat6D, while GCE-Pose retains a notable advantage on IoU75 for HouseCat6D (60.6 vs. 56.3). MK-Pose (IROS'25) and SecondPose (CVPR'24) are consistently outperformed across all datasets and metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DecomPose builds directly on the AG-Pose keypoint-centric correspondence architecture and the NOCS canonical coordinate framework, but shifts the design focus from representation capacity to optimization dynamics. Rather than improving feature extraction or geometric constraints as prior correspondence-based methods (SPD, SGPA, RBP-Pose, CleanPose) do, it draws on multi-task gradient-interaction analyses (gradient surgery, AdaShare) to diagnose and mitigate cross-category contention in shared parameter spaces. The key departure is treating the correspondence module as the primary locus of interference and selectively decoupling only that component, while keeping the backbone and pose head fully shared—a minimal intervention that contrasts with the fully-shared or fully-per-category alternatives in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient-based diagnostic framework provides a reusable tool for localizing optimization contention in any multi-category joint training setup, and the finding that contention concentrates in the correspondence module (not the backbone) offers a concrete design principle for future architectures. The counterintuitive result that lighter branches for harder categories outperform heavier ones suggests that capacity is not the bottleneck for difficult categories—stability is—opening a broader question about how to allocate model resources under heterogeneous learning difficulty. A key limitation is that the offline difficulty proxy may degrade as the category set grows substantially, and the paper itself identifies adaptive online routing as an important future direction.
