## Problem

Classical geometry optimization and inversion pipelines require costly forward PDE solves, non-differentiable geometry edits, and remeshing at every iteration, creating computational bottlenecks and heavy expert dependence. While neural surrogates accelerate the forward pass, they do not close the optimization loop because gradients from objectives to geometry are typically unavailable. Existing differentiable approaches either restrict design to low-dimensional parametric spaces or perform unstable latent-space optimization driven by scalar objectives, limiting interpretability, field-level control, and part-wise constraints.

## Input / Output

GANO consumes surface point clouds or irregular mesh coordinates (ranging from ~50k points for 2D airfoils to ~500k for 3D vehicles) along with a 256-dimensional latent code z, and optionally boundary observations for inversion tasks. It produces full physical field predictions (pressure, velocity, scattered field) at boundary-consistent query points, an optimized latent code z*, and the corresponding updated geometry. A key operational detail is that query points are maintained on the evolving surface via iterative SDF-based projection (a few Gauss-Newton steps) rather than explicit remeshing, and the forward surrogate operates directly on irregular point sets without requiring a regular grid.

## Method

GANO couples three components in a single differentiable loop: (1) STABLESDF, an auto-decoder SDF that maps a 256-dim latent code to a signed distance function, trained with Gaussian noise injection on latent codes that provably induces an implicit Jacobian-norm regularizer, reducing decoder sensitivity and yielding controlled surface deformations; (2) GI-TRANSOLVER, a transformer-based field predictor that injects the latent code into its physics-attention slice-token space via a gated residual mechanism, creating an explicit differentiable gradient path from predicted fields back to z; and (3) a latent-space optimization loop that backpropagates task objectives through the surrogate, optionally applies null-space projection to freeze specified geometric components (e.g., wheels, mirrors), updates z via Adam, and reprojects query points onto the updated surface using a remeshing-free SDF Gauss-Newton step. The theoretical contribution shows that the denoising mechanism bounds the latent-to-surface Hausdorff displacement per iteration and that detaching the projection pathway introduces only a controlled gradient bias proportional to the same sensitivity that denoising reduces.

## Results

GANO achieves the best forward prediction accuracy across all three benchmarks: Rel. L2 of 0.0170 on 2D Helmholtz, 0.0022 on 2D Airfoil, and 0.1782 on 3D Vehicle (Table 1). For 2D airfoil optimization under a soft drag constraint (C_D < 0.02), GANO achieves a lift-to-drag ratio of 83.4, a +55.9% improvement over the initial design (53.6), surpassing U-Net (75.0, +40.0%) and DeepONet (44.3, −17.3%). On 3D vehicle drag minimization, GANO reduces C_D by 6.64% (estateback: 0.346→0.323) and 7.02% (fastback: 0.299→0.278), validated by high-fidelity OpenFOAM simulations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On forward prediction, GANO outperforms all transformer-based surrogates (TRANSOLVER, TRANSOLVER++) and the graph-transformer AEROGTO across all three benchmarks, with the largest margin on 2D Airfoil (Rel. L2 0.0022 vs. 0.0057 for AEROGTO, a 61% relative reduction). GANO also substantially outperforms generic point-set and operator baselines (POINTNET++, FNO, U-NET, DEEPONET) on 2D Helmholtz, where the best non-GANO method (AEROGTO) still trails by ~37% in Rel. L2. For airfoil shape optimization, GANO achieves C_L/C_D = 83.4, beating U-Net (75.0) by 11% and DeepONet (44.3) by 88%, while keeping drag within the soft constraint. On 3D vehicle optimization, GANO achieves comparable or better drag reduction than PHYSGEN (You et al., 2025) while preserving standardized components (e.g., side mirrors) via null-space projection, whereas PHYSGEN often deforms or shrinks these parts. For 2D Helmholtz inversion, GANO produces finer-scale shape details and smaller reconstruction errors than CORAL (Serrano et al., 2023) and other differentiable inversion baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

GANO sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: implicit neural representations for geometry (building directly on DeepSDF) and transformer-based neural PDE solvers on irregular geometries (building on Transolver). The key conceptual departure is from prior latent-space optimization pipelines (PhysGen, 3DID, AGML, TripOptimizer), which either entangle geometry and physics in a single latent code, rely on voxel/occupancy representations that sacrifice fidelity, or require diffusion/flow-matching sampling with two-stage post-processing. GANO instead decouples a dedicated SDF auto-decoder (STABLESDF) from a geometry-informed field predictor (GI-TRANSOLVER), creating an explicit differentiable gradient path from field-level objectives to the geometry latent without generative sampling, and adds a denoising mechanism whose theoretical effect (implicit Jacobian regularization) is formally characterized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a long-standing gap in neural surrogate-based design: prior work accelerated forward PDE solves but left geometry updates non-differentiable, requiring remeshing and expert intervention. By unifying representation, prediction, and optimization in one latent-space loop with remeshing-free projection and part-wise null-space control, GANO offers a practically deployable pipeline validated against OpenFOAM and COMSOL. The main limitation is restriction to steady-state PDEs; extending to time-dependent or dynamic shape optimization is identified as future work. An open question is how the framework scales when the number of protected components or the dimensionality of the latent space grows, and whether the surrogate-based optimization can compete with adjoint-based methods on high-fidelity 3D aerodynamics.
