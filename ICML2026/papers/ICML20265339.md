## Problem

Connectionist and symbolic models embody divergent paradigms for tabular learning: neural networks excel at differentiable representation learning but lack transparency, while decision trees provide explicit rule-based reasoning but resist gradient-based optimization. Prior neural-symbolic approaches soften discrete boundaries or impose post-hoc explanations without establishing a principled, invertible correspondence between discrete symbolic structures and continuous neural representations, leaving a fundamental conceptual gap unbridged.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a tabular feature vector X ∈ R^t (e.g., 100 engineered features in TabCredit) with categorical variables one-hot encoded and missing values imputed by per-feature minimum. For classification it outputs a risk probability via sigmoid over the summed ArborCell logits; for regression it outputs a continuous value (summed logits plus a 0.5 bias). Uniquely, the trained network can also be decompiled back into an interpretable if-else decision tree whose ranking behavior matches the soft neural inference to 4–5 decimal places in KS and AUC.

## Method

ArbNN is a wide, shallow ensemble of ArborCells, each emulating one decision tree from a pretrained GBDT (e.g., XGBoost) via a one-shot matrix formulation: a sparse weight matrix W encodes feature-to-node assignments, a bias vector f holds split thresholds, a tree-structure matrix P encodes hierarchical subspace reachability with a (1/2)^(depth−1) decay, and v stores leaf values. The forward pass computes hyperplane projections H = WX − f, applies a scaled tanh to obtain a soft decision vector d, multiplies by P^T to get subspace affinities, normalizes via a topology-informed softmax, and aggregates leaf values — all in a single differentiable pass without recursive gating. The depth-aware 1/2 decay in P and the topology-informed softmax together enable one-shot full-path gradient propagation, yielding well-conditioned optimization that converges faster than prior neuralized-tree models (NRF, SDT). The paper proves ArborCell is structurally homomorphic to a single-query self-attention head (Q = d^T, K = P^T, V = v), unifying symbolic branching and neural routing under one conditional-computation primitive. Training freezes the topology (W, P) inherited from the GBDT and fine-tunes only f and v via AdamW, preserving full interpretability while enabling global co-optimization of all split thresholds and leaf values.

## Results

On 11 public tabular benchmarks (7 classification, 4 regression), ArbNN achieves the best or near-best result on nearly all datasets, e.g., AUC 0.974 on Magic Gamma Telescope (vs. 0.947 for XGBoost) and RMSE 0.832 on YProp 4.1 (vs. 0.857 for XGBoost). On the large-scale TabCredit credit-risk dataset (2M samples, 100 features), ArbNN attains the highest KS across all MOB horizons (57.14% at MOB2 vs. 56.52% for XGBoost) and a 92% reduction in Expected Calibration Error (0.0011 vs. 0.0138) while matching or slightly exceeding XGBoost on AUC-ROC (0.8194 vs. 0.8188) and Lift (1.8442 vs. 1.8440).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArbNN outperforms all 14 baselines on the majority of the 11 public benchmarks. Against the strongest tree ensembles, ArbNN leads XGBoost by +0.027 AUC on Magic Gamma Telescope (0.974 vs. 0.947) and by 0.025 RMSE on YProp 4.1 (0.832 vs. 0.857), and beats LightGBM by +0.036 AUC on H16 (0.987 vs. 0.951); the sole exception is California Housing where LightGBM's RMSE of 0.395 edges out ArbNN's 0.406. Among neural baselines, ArbNN surpasses FT-Transformer by up to +0.045 AUC (H16: 0.987 vs. 0.950), TabTransformer by +0.056 AUC (MA: 0.974 vs. 0.918), and TabNet by +0.037 AUC (MA: 0.974 vs. 0.937). Against recent hybrid methods, ArbNN leads Trompt by +0.041 AUC on JA (0.923 vs. 0.882), GRANDE by +0.051 AUC on JA (0.923 vs. 0.871), ExcelFormer by +0.017 AUC on CA (0.986 vs. 0.969), and SwitchTab by +0.035 AUC on MA (0.974 vs. 0.939). On the industrial TabCredit dataset, ArbNN achieves the highest KS at every MOB horizon (e.g., 57.14% at MOB2 vs. 56.52% for XGBoost and 56.31% for LightGBM) and a 92% ECE reduction over XGBoost (0.0011 vs. 0.0138), while TabPFNv2—designed for small-data zero-shot inference—underperforms substantially on this 2M-sample dataset (KS ≈ 28–31% vs. 57% for ArbNN at MOB2).

## Relation to Prior Work

ArbNN departs from prior neuralized-tree models (Neural Decision Forests, Soft Decision Trees, Neural Random Forests, GRANDE) that rely on recursive soft gating by replacing it with a one-shot matrix formulation combining depth-aware routing and topology-informed softmax, enabling full-path gradient propagation in a single forward pass. It also distinguishes itself from general-purpose neural tabular models (FT-Transformer, TabNet, TabM) by inheriting a fixed GBDT topology as a structural prior rather than learning representations from scratch, and from hybrid architectures (Adaptive Neural Trees, TEL, Net-DNF) by guaranteeing lossless decompilation back into an interpretable symbolic tree. The paper's central conceptual move is proving a structural homomorphism between a decision tree and a single-query self-attention head, positioning tree branching and attention routing as two realizations of the same conditional computation primitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Bidirectional Fidelity property—where the neural module can be compiled from and decompiled back into a symbolic tree with ranking-equivalent behavior (matching to 4–5 decimal places)—offers a principled path to audit-grade interpretability in high-stakes tabular domains without sacrificing end-to-end trainability. The 92% ECE reduction over XGBoost on TabCredit is practically significant for credit-risk calibration, and the demonstrated production deployment (<100 ms latency, <20 MB memory) shows the approach is deployable at scale. However, the paper acknowledges that learning the tree-structure matrix P from scratch via gradient descent causes structural collapse into dense, uninterpretable attention, leaving fully differentiable structure learning as an open challenge. The modest AUC/Lift gains on TabCredit (~0.07% AUC) suggest the primary value lies in interpretability and calibration rather than raw discriminative power.
