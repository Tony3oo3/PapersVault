## Problem

Existing proxy-based hashing methods treat learnable proxies as independent semantic anchors, so when a multi-label sample is associated with multiple proxies, their responses are accumulated rather than explicitly contrasted. This leads to overlapping decision regions and weakly discriminative boundaries in the Hamming space, particularly when semantic categories partially overlap. The paper identifies that the limitation lies not in how semantic relations are defined but in the absence of explicit competitive interaction among proxies during optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired RGB images and text captions from multi-label datasets (24–80 categories), processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone followed by a linear hash layer with tanh activation. It outputs K-bit binary codes (16, 32, 64, or 128 bits) in a shared Hamming space, where retrieval is performed via Hamming distance between image and text codes. Each category is represented by N learnable proxies (default N=3) to capture intra-class diversity, and a ratio r controls how many of these proxies participate in each sample's subgraph.

## Method

DDSPH constructs a sample-specific directed subgraph for each training sample by selecting the top-k most similar positive proxies (via cosine similarity with a hard positive label mask), forming a sparse adjacency matrix that encodes relational roles among proxies. Proxy responses are then aggregated over this subgraph to produce a label-aware prediction vector, where the structural selectivity implicitly contrasts positive and negative proxy contributions rather than uniformly accumulating them. Training jointly optimizes three losses—sample-proxy alignment (binary cross-entropy on aggregated predictions), proxy regularization (intra-category compactness and inter-category separation), and instance-level triplet preservation—all dynamically normalized by their detached magnitudes to avoid manual weighting. The key mechanism is that the subgraph structure forces proxies to compete for a sample's representation, sharpening decision boundaries without requiring a large number of proxies.

## Results

On MIRFLICKR-25K, DDSPH achieves 84.86% mAP (I2T) and 83.22% mAP (T2I) at 16 bits, reaching 87.53% and 85.52% at 128 bits, outperforming the strongest baselines DDBH (84.50%/82.45%) and DDWSH (84.05%/82.10%). On NUS-WIDE, DDSPH attains 70.25% (I2T) and 71.31% (T2I) at 16 bits, improving to 73.54% and 74.31% at 128 bits. On MS COCO, DDSPH reaches 78.97% (I2T) and 78.98% (T2I) at 128 bits, though at 16 bits it trails DDBH and DDWSH due to the higher semantic complexity of 80 categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDSPH outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of dataset–bit-length–direction settings. Against the two strongest recent competitors, DDBH (TCSVT'25) and DDWSH (TMM'26), DDSPH leads by 0.36–1.03 mAP on MIRFLICKR-25K and by 0.52–1.25 mAP on NUS-WIDE across all bit lengths in both directions. The exception is MS COCO at 16 bits, where DDBH (71.65% I2T, 71.67% T2I) and DDWSH (72.08% I2T) surpass DDSPH (69.57% I2T, 69.82% T2I), which the authors attribute to the 80-category semantic complexity exceeding the capacity of a 16-bit code. Against earlier proxy-based methods (DSPH, DNPH, DHaPH), DDSPH gains 1.9–3.6 mAP on MIRFLICKR-25K and 0.7–1.7 mAP on NUS-WIDE, confirming that the structural competition mechanism adds discriminative power beyond what independent proxy alignment or hierarchy/neighborhood constraints provide. On the FHD metric (Table 2), DDSPH achieves the highest Fisher ratio on NUS-WIDE in most settings, indicating that the structured proxy competition is particularly effective when semantic categories partially overlap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proxy-based hashing paradigm (DSPH, DNPH, DHaPH) by introducing sample-specific relational structures among proxies, departing from the standard treatment of proxies as independent semantic attractors. It draws on proxy-based metric learning (ProxyNCA, ProxyAnchor, SoftTriple) and graph-based relational modeling (ProxyGML), but uniquely organizes proxies into per-sample competitive subgraphs where positive and negative responses are explicitly contrasted during optimization. The framework builds on CLIP ViT-B/32 as a frozen backbone with lightweight hash layers, consistent with recent cross-modal hashing practice, and incorporates a triplet-based preservation loss alongside the novel structure-guided alignment and proxy regularization objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured proxy competition mechanism offers a general principle for sharpening decision boundaries in multi-label retrieval settings where semantics partially overlap, potentially transferable beyond hashing to other proxy-based classification or retrieval tasks. However, the method's reliance on label masks for subgraph construction limits its applicability under noisy or incomplete annotations, and performance gains narrow on high-complexity datasets (MS COCO, 80 categories) at short code lengths. Open questions include whether adaptive, label-free subgraph construction can recover the discriminative benefit without clean supervision, and whether the competitive structure generalizes to open-vocabulary or few-proxy regimes.
