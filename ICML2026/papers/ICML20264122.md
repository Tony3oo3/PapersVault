## Problem

Diffusion language models (DLMs) enable parallel decoding but produce lower-quality text than autoregressive (AR) models due to conditional independence assumptions. Existing Monte Carlo correction approaches (rejection sampling or importance sampling) that use a DLM as proposal and an AR model as target fail in practice because the large distribution mismatch causes frequent rejections or unstable importance weights, collapsing decoding back to near-sequential generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264122/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt, a DLM (Dream-7B-Instruct) that generates candidate continuations in parallel chunks, and an AR expert (Qwen2.5-Math-7B or Qwen2.5-Coder-7B) that scores those continuations via teacher-forced likelihood, the method outputs a single generated continuation that faithfully approximates the AR target distribution. The DLM is queried with the current prefix plus 256 trailing mask tokens per forward pass, and the AR model evaluates the proposed tokens in parallel across positions, making the verification step highly parallelizable on a single GPU.

## Method

PoE-Bridge introduces a token-level Product-of-Experts bridge distribution p_PoE(x_i|x_<i) ∝ p̃_D(x_i|x_<i)^w · p_AR(x_i|x_<i)^(1−w) that interpolates between the DLM proposal and AR target, splitting the difficult DLM-to-AR correction into two easier stages. First, speculative rejection sampling accepts DLM-drafted tokens with probability min(1, p_PoE/p̃_D) and corrects at the rejection point via a residual draw, moving candidates toward p_PoE with high acceptance rates. Second, importance sampling resamples among K PoE-aligned candidates using weights proportional to p_AR/p_PoE to refine toward the AR target. Two practical techniques complete the system: mixed-temperature sampling draws K candidates from linearly spaced tempered PoE distributions (τ_low=0.2 to τ_high=0.7) to prevent mode collapse and enable effective scaling with K, and an elastic rejection window limits verification to V positions (default 32) to avoid wasting compute on tokens that would be rejected.

## Results

On GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, and MBPP, PoE-Bridge recovers at least 95% of the target AR model's accuracy (e.g., 95.30% vs. 95.53% on GSM8K, 74.42% vs. 76.28% on MATH) while achieving approximately 2× the AR throughput (e.g., 100.71 vs. 49.26 tokens/s on GSM8K, 94.94 vs. 46.50 on MATH). Compared to the best standard DLM decoding (Dream 7B Entropy, 2 token/step), PoE-Bridge delivers up to 5× speedup (100.71 vs. 26.25 tokens/s on GSM8K) while substantially improving accuracy (95.30% vs. 72.00%). The importance sampling stage contributes a small but consistent accuracy gain over the rejection-only variant (PoE-Bridge w/o IS), with only a marginal throughput cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Dream 7B DLM baselines, PoE-Bridge achieves a dramatic accuracy improvement across all four benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K: 95.30% vs. 72.00% for the best DLM setting; MATH: 74.42% vs. 27.91%; HumanEval: 79.47% vs. 47.56%; MBPP: 73.20% vs. 55.93%) while simultaneously delivering 2–5× higher throughput. Against the Qwen2.5 7B autoregressive target, PoE-Bridge recovers 95–99% of AR accuracy (GSM8K: 95.30 vs. 95.53; MATH: 74.42 vs. 76.28; HumanEval: 79.47 vs. 83.54; MBPP: 73.20 vs. 75.87) while achieving roughly 2× the AR throughput (e.g., 100.71 vs. 49.26 tokens/s on GSM8K, 72.10 vs. 47.09 on MBPP). The small accuracy gap to AR is most pronounced on HumanEval (−4.07 points) and MATH (−1.86 points), while GSM8K is nearly matched (−0.23 points). The ablation variant without importance sampling (K=1) shows that the PoE rejection step alone already captures most of the AR quality, with the IS stage providing a final 0.5–1.5 point accuracy refinement at negligible throughput cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoE-Bridge synthesizes three established ideas—speculative decoding's token-by-token rejection verification (Leviathan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023), the Product-of-Experts interpolation (Hinton, 1999), and multiple importance sampling (Veach & Guibas, 1995)—into a two-stage correction pipeline that prior DLM acceleration work has not combined. Prior efforts either apply direct DLM-to-AR rejection (collapsing into near-sequential decoding due to large distribution mismatch) or direct DLM-to-AR importance sampling (requiring impractically large candidate budgets); the PoE bridge distribution is the key departure, making both the rejection and resampling stages operate on a smaller proposal-target gap. The practical additions of mixed-temperature candidate drawing and the elastic rejection window are novel engineering contributions tailored to realistic LLM inference constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PoE-Bridge demonstrates that AR-level generation quality (≥95% accuracy recovery) can be achieved at 5× the throughput of standard DLM decoding and 2× the throughput of AR decoding, breaking the conventional quality-speed tradeoff for diffusion language models. However, the evidence is limited to 7B-parameter models on a single GPU with batch size 1, a shared tokenizer between DLM and AR, and both models resident in memory—constraints that significantly limit near-term deployment. Open questions include whether the PoE bridge generalizes to mismatched tokenizers, multi-GPU serving with large batch sizes, and whether the finite-K importance sampling bias becomes problematic at larger model scales or longer generation horizons.
