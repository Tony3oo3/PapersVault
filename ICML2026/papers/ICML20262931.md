## Problem

Existing conformal prediction methods for LLM factuality (e.g., Conformal Factuality) guarantee coverage only under exchangeability, which is violated in online deployment where user queries and factual knowledge drift over time. Prior adaptive conformal methods (ACI, DtACI) adjust the target error rate purely reactively from past coverage failures, causing delayed adaptation and prolonged under- or over-coverage during distribution shifts. The paper asks whether input-level semantic signals can be leveraged proactively to accelerate adaptation before errors accumulate.

## Input / Output

At each time step the method receives a text prompt X_t, a fixed calibration set D_cal, and the LLM's raw response decomposed into atomic claims (for generation) or candidate labels (for QA). It produces a filtered prediction set—retained claims whose non-conformity scores fall below a dynamically updated threshold λ_t, or a subset of candidate labels—along with an updated adaptive conformal parameter α_{t+1}. Unusually, the method also consumes the prompt's feature embedding to compute a Relative Mahalanobis Distance against both the calibration distribution and a generic background corpus, yielding a proactive shift score that modulates the update magnitude without requiring any additional model inference.

## Method

PACE instantiates a generic adaptive conformal update rule α_{t+1} = α_t + γ_t(α − err_t) with a dynamic step size γ_t = c₁·S_error + c₂·S_shift, where S_error is the sliding-window deviation of realized error from the target α (a proxy for instantaneous parameter error Φ_t) and S_shift is a sigmoid-normalized Relative Mahalanobis Distance measuring how far the current prompt embedding deviates from the calibration distribution relative to a background corpus (a proxy for the latent distribution shift Δ_t). The theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.5) derives the optimal unconstrained step size under an L-Lipschitz miscoverage assumption, showing it should scale linearly with both Φ_t and a signed drift-feedback term E[(α−err_t)Δ_t], which motivates the dual-signal design. Unlike reactive baselines that wait for coverage violations to accumulate before increasing the step size, PACE's proactive signal detects semantic shifts in the incoming query and spikes γ_t toward γ_max immediately, enabling near-instantaneous re-calibration. The method is validated on synthetic streams with abrupt and smooth shifts, MMLU multi-choice QA (QWEN-72B), and WikiData open-ended biography generation (GPT-5.2), achieving up to 60% lower tracking RMSE and 2.5× faster recovery than the strongest reactive baseline DtACI while maintaining the highest claim retention among valid adaptive methods.

## Results

On MMLU (QA, target α=0.1), PACE achieves a tracking RMSE of 0.0068, a ~60% reduction over the strongest adaptive baseline DtACI (0.0175), while maintaining 89.85% factuality and a recovery time of only 3.0 steps (vs. 24.6 for DtACI). On WikiData (open-ended generation, target α=0.2), PACE achieves the highest Claim Retention rate (72.27%) among all valid adaptive methods and a recovery time of 39.57 steps, compared to 89.98 for conservative ACI(γ=0.01). On synthetic abrupt-shift streams, PACE accelerates coverage recovery by 2.6× over DtACI (46.06 vs. 120.66 steps) and reduces tracking RMSE by 24% (0.0349 vs. 0.0461).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PACE outperforms all five baselines on the primary tracking metric (RMSE) across both real-world tasks. Against SCP (Shafer & Vovk, 2008), PACE improves RMSE by ~81% on MMLU (0.0068 vs. 0.0360) and meets the 90% coverage target that SCP misses (88.07%). Against ACI (Gibbs & Candès, 2021), PACE dominates: the small-γ variant (0.01) lags with 24.9-step recovery, while the large-γ variant (0.1) achieves fast recovery (1.8 steps) but inflates set size to 1.27; PACE achieves 3.0-step recovery with a more moderate set size of 1.17. Against DtACI (Gibbs & Candès, 2024), PACE reduces MMLU RMSE by 61% (0.0068 vs. 0.0175) and cuts recovery time by 8× (3.0 vs. 24.6 steps), confirming the benefit of proactive shift detection over purely reactive expert aggregation. Against CF (Mohri & Hashimoto, 2024) on WikiData, PACE meets the 80% factuality target (79.96%) while CF violates it (78.15%), and PACE retains more claims (72.27% vs. 78.82% for CF, though CF's higher retention comes at the cost of invalid coverage). Against SAOCP (Bhatnagar et al., 2023) on synthetic streams, PACE achieves smaller worst-case violations (0.2507 vs. 0.4556 under abrupt shifts) and lower RMSE under smooth shifts (0.0233 vs. 0.0267), though SAOCP recovers slightly faster in the abrupt setting (21.11 vs. 54.18 steps).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI) framework of Gibbs & Candès (2021) by replacing the fixed step size with a dynamic one driven by two complementary signals: a proactive shift detector (Relative Mahalanobis Distance on input embeddings) and a reactive error tracker (sliding-window coverage gap). It builds on the Conformal Factuality (CF) scoring pipeline of Mohri & Hashimoto (2024) for open-ended generation but replaces its static exchangeability-based calibration with an online adaptive procedure. The key conceptual departure from prior adaptive methods (DtACI, SAOCP, AgACI) is that PACE incorporates semantic information from the current query to anticipate distribution shifts before errors accumulate, rather than relying solely on historical miscoverage feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in deploying LLMs in production: static conformal guarantees break under the non-stationary query streams observed in real chat logs, and purely reactive adaptive methods lag by hundreds of steps after abrupt topic shifts. PACE's proactive signal offers a lightweight (0.07 ms overhead) mechanism to close this gap, which is practically important for safety-critical deployments. However, the coverage guarantee remains asymptotic, the factuality evaluation assumes claim-level independence (limiting applicability to multi-step reasoning), and the method is restricted to text-only settings. Deriving finite-sample guarantees under arbitrary shifts and extending to multimodal or compositional factuality remain open.
