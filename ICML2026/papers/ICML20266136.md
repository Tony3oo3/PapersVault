## Problem

2D video generation models lack 3D structure awareness, causing failures in occlusion handling, object permanence, and physical plausibility, while existing 3D particle-based simulators rely on privileged signals (depth sensors, object tracks, physics priors) or hand-crafted kNN graph constructions that limit scalability. The paper asks whether a fully end-to-end, transformer-only 3D particle simulator can be learned directly from raw multi-view RGB video, dispensing with graph inductive biases in favor of scalable learned spatiotemporal embeddings.

## Input / Output

The model consumes T past multi-view RGB frames (4 uniformly sampled views from 12 synchronized cameras, at 12–42 FPS depending on dataset) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics; for real-world HO-Cap experiments it additionally ingests sparse 3D hand keypoints as action conditioning. It outputs novel-view rendered images (5 target cameras) over a 12-step future rollout, with the internal state being a compact per-particle representation of position plus a 64-dimensional visuo-physical latent feature split into invariant and dynamic components. The Gaussian splat parameters (position, rotation, spherical harmonics, scale, opacity) are materialized only at the final rendering stage for supervision.

## Method

3DGSim is a fully differentiable pipeline with three jointly trained modules: an MVSplat-based inverse renderer that uses FiLM conditioning on depth, pixel shift, density, and Plücker ray geometry to unproject pixel-aligned features into view-independent 3D particle latents; a PTv3-based transformer dynamics engine that serializes particles via space-filling curves (replacing kNN graphs) and introduces Temporal Encoding and Merging (TEM) to enable patch-wise attention across timesteps through a one-bit right shift on temporal serialization codes; and a Gaussian Splatting head that maps particle states to splat parameters for differentiable rendering. The key architectural insight is that temporal merging allows a single set of attention blocks to be reused across all timesteps (unlike prior work requiring a separate model per step), while the invariant/dynamic feature decomposition lets the dynamics model update only the dynamic component. Training uses a single weighted image reconstruction loss (L2 + LPIPS) computed over both past and future rendered frames with a temporal decay factor, requiring no physics priors, object masks, or depth supervision.

## Results

On the Elastic dataset, 3DGSim (4-step input, 12-step rollout, latent representation) achieves 33.15±3.51 future PSNR, 0.97 SSIM, 0.02 LPIPS, and 91.3 FVD. On Cloth, it reaches 26.46±2.62 future PSNR with 0.88 SSIM, and on Rigid Body, 28.35±2.70 future PSNR with 0.91 SSIM. The model achieves near real-time inference at ~16–20 FPS on a single H100 GPU, and generalizes to out-of-distribution scenarios (ground removal, multi-object interactions) without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CosmosFT (a LoRA-finetuned 2D video generation model), 3DGSim outperforms on all three datasets: +6.2 dB PSNR on Elastic (33.15 vs 26.97), +3.3 dB on Cloth (26.46 vs 23.12), and +1.0 dB on Rigid (28.35 vs 27.31), while also achieving substantially lower LPIPS (0.02 vs 0.07 on Elastic) and FVD (91.3 vs 156.4 on Elastic). Against Spring-Gaus (a per-scene spring-mass 3DGS simulator), 3DGSim leads by +9.0 dB PSNR on Elastic full-scene (33.15 vs 24.13) and +4.5 dB on Rigid full-scene (28.35 vs 23.85); even in the object-only rendering setting where Spring-Gaus avoids appearance artifacts, 3DGSim still wins by +6.0 dB on Elastic and +1.0 dB on Rigid. Spring-Gaus cannot handle cloth dynamics at all due to its limited collision model. Qualitatively, 3DGSim also generalizes to out-of-distribution edits (ground removal, multi-object scenes) where CosmosFT produces levitation and object-merging artifacts, highlighting the advantage of explicit 3D spatial causality over 2D image-space generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of GNN-based 3D particle simulators (Whitney et al. 2023/2024, Sanchez-Gonzalez et al. 2020) by replacing kNN graph construction entirely with a transformer-only dynamics engine built on PTv3's space-filling curve serialization, augmented with a novel Temporal Encoding and Merging (TEM) module for cross-timestep attention. For the reconstruction and rendering pipeline, it builds on MVSplat for feed-forward multi-view 3D Gaussian estimation and uses 3D Gaussian Splatting (rather than NeRF) as the differentiable rendering head, enabling end-to-end training on image reconstruction loss. The key conceptual departure from prior PBS work is the elimination of all privileged signals (depth, object tracks, physics priors) in favor of raw multi-view RGB, and the removal of hand-crafted graph biases in favor of learned spatiotemporal embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

3DGSim demonstrates that a fully differentiable 3D particle simulator can learn diverse physical behaviors (rigid, elastic, cloth) from raw multi-view video alone, and—critically—generalizes to out-of-distribution scenarios (ground removal, multi-object collisions) where 2D video generation models like Cosmos fail with physically impossible hallucinations. This validates the hypothesis that explicit 3D spatial causality provides robustness that pixel-space generative models lack. Key limitations include the multi-view input requirement (mitigated but not solved by the preliminary HO-Cap results), autoregressive error accumulation over long horizons, and reliance on simulated training data. Open questions include monocular extension, scalable action conditioning, and whether the transformer-only approach can match GNNs on highly structured rigid-body dynamics at larger scales.
