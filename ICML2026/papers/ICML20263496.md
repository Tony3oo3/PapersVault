## Problem

Existing VLMs can recognize visual content but fail to ground it in a user's accumulated visual-textual history, such as identifying that a person in a query image is the user's brother mentioned in prior interactions. This paper formalizes the challenge as contextualized visual personalization, requiring VLMs to jointly perform visual recognition and retrieval of personalized episodic experiences when interpreting new images, a capability that current open-source and proprietary models lack.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263496/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a query image containing 1–4 visual concepts, a free-form user prompt, and an interleaved image-text context of up to 10 dialogues (each pairing an image with a multi-turn conversation), where both positive (relevant) and visually similar negative (irrelevant) concept images are included to enforce fine-grained discrimination. The primary output is a personalized caption that must incorporate dialogue-grounded details for positive concepts while omitting negative ones; at inference, this caption is optionally reused as a conditioning signal (CAG) to produce downstream personalized responses such as last-seen locations, recalled actions, or proactive keyword triggers.

## Method

CoViP decomposes the VLM into a shared contextual visual encoder h_θ and a task-specific generator g_θ, then trains h_θ via personalized image captioning as a proxy task that externalizes the latent personalization state. Post-training uses GSPO (Group Sequence Policy Optimization) with two verifiable rewards: a set-level F1 score over predicted concept indices (r_vis) for fine-grained visual recognition, and an MCQA-based contrastive reward (r_caps) that scores whether the generated caption alone suffices to answer positive-context questions while correctly abstaining on negative-context questions, with a degeneration filter penalizing repetitive or overly long outputs. At inference, Caption-Augmented Generation (CAG) first elicits a caption and then conditions the downstream response on it, amplifying personalization gains without additional training. The benchmark comprises 2.8K training and 1.3K test samples with synthesized multi-concept query images, CLIP-retrieved negative distractors, and LLM-generated factual dialogues, evaluated via the CapEval-QAs MCQA probing protocol.

## Results

On the personalized image captioning benchmark (CapEval-QAs), CoViP (Qwen3-VL-8B + RL post-training) achieves Positive Accuracy of 77.4% (1-concept), 68.4% (2-concepts), 65.2% (3-concepts), and 59.7% (4-concepts), representing an average ~40% absolute improvement over the Qwen3-VL-8B baseline (39.0%, 25.6%, 23.3%, 18.6%). On downstream diagnostic tasks, CoViP with CAG achieves 58.2% recall on LSD, 49.2% on LAR, and 42.8% on ITR, consistently outperforming the base VLM by +9.4, +29.6, and +36.0 points respectively. CoViP also improves general multi-image benchmarks (MM-NIAH +1.3%, MMNeedle +4.0%, MuirBench +2.0%, MMIU +1.5%) over the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoViP substantially outperforms both post-training baselines: RAP (Hao et al., CVPR 2025) actually degrades below the zero-shot baseline (20.5% vs. 39.0% Acc⁺ on 1-concept), while RePIC (Oh et al., 2025) achieves only 44.0% vs. CoViP's 77.4%. Against proprietary VLMs, CoViP surpasses GPT-5 (48.3%) and Gemini-3.0 Pro (58.1%) on Acc⁺ for 1-concept captioning, but Gemini-3.0 Pro retains a large advantage on the LSD diagnostic task (76.2% vs. 37.2%) and ITR (89.4% vs. 28.0%), while GPT-5 leads on LAR (50.8% vs. 34.8%). The Qwen3-VL-8B base model serves as the primary open-source reference; CoViP's ~40% absolute gain over it demonstrates that the RL post-training, not model scale, drives the improvement. Notably, CAG amplifies CoViP's advantage on LAR (+29.6 over base) and ITR (+36.0), whereas for proprietary models CAG causes ITR recall to drop below 20%, highlighting that reliable captioning is a prerequisite for effective caption-augmented inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of VLM personalization work (MyVLM, Yo'LLaVA, RAP, RePIC) by shifting from explicit, surface-level personalization (e.g., name recall for a single concept) to implicit, experience-level personalization grounded in interleaved image-text contexts. It builds on GSPO as the RL optimization backbone and repurposes the LLM contextualized-personalization paradigm (where user history is provided in-context) to the visual domain. The key conceptual departure is treating personalized image captioning as a shared proxy task that captures the recognition-and-retrieval process common across all downstream personalization tasks, rather than training task-specific objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic formalization and diagnostic evaluation of contextualized visual personalization, revealing that even frontier proprietary VLMs (GPT-5, Gemini-3.0 Pro) exhibit unstable and task-dependent behavior on implicit personalization. The finding that retrieval—not recognition—is the primary bottleneck reframes where post-training effort should be directed. Key open questions include whether the synthetic benchmark generalizes to noisy, heterogeneous real-world user histories, whether the approach scales beyond 8B backbones, and how to handle conflicting or ambiguous contextual memories without introducing shortcut learning.
