## Problem

Existing visual token reduction (VTR) methods for MLLMs overlook two critical distortions introduced when reducing the visual token sequence: (1) softmax normalization redistributes attention mass from pruned tokens to remaining ones, disproportionately amplifying text tokens that are closer to the query, and (2) retaining original position indices causes RoPE's long-term positional decay to heavily penalize sparse visual tokens. Together, these distortions cause the model to neglect visual context and rely on textual information, leading to weakened visual grounding and hallucinations.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a training-free, plug-and-play module between the visual encoder/projector and the LLM. It takes the full visual token sequence (e.g., 576 tokens for LLaVA-1.5-7B, 2880 for LLaVA-NeXT-7B) and a text prompt, reduces the visual tokens to a target budget (e.g., 192, 128, or 64 tokens) via a hybrid pruning-and-merging strategy, and feeds the reduced sequence into the LLM to generate a text response. The only additional computation is a pre-computed N×N calibration matrix and a pairwise correlation matrix for anchor selection, both of which are negligible relative to LLM inference FLOPs.

## Method

RESTORE introduces two complementary mechanisms to rectify distortions in VTR. First, an attention calibration term augments each attention logit with log(s_n)·(c − D(|m−n|)), where D is the analytically derived RoPE decay function, s_n is the merged group size, and c is a constant; this counteracts the positional bias that suppresses distant visual tokens while preserving original position indices to maintain spatial relationships. Second, a distinctive anchor selection strategy for token merging, inspired by density peak clustering, scores each candidate token by the product of its representativeness (sum of pairwise cosine correlations with all visual tokens) and its discriminativeness (1 minus its maximum correlation with any higher-representativeness token), then selects the top-K anchors to minimize information loss during feature averaging. The framework is model-agnostic, generalizes from 1D RoPE to multi-dimensional M-RoPE (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL), and can be integrated with any existing pruning or merging-based VTR method without retraining.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B evaluated across eight benchmarks (GQA, MMBench, MME, POPE, SQA, VQAv2, TextVQA, SEED-Bench), RESTORE consistently improves all text-agnostic VTR baselines. At the most aggressive retention of 64 tokens (11.1%), HoloV+RESTORE achieves 96.5% average accuracy, a 4.3-point gain over HoloV alone (92.2%), while VisPruner+RESTORE reaches 96.0% vs. 92.8% for VisPruner. At 192 tokens (33.3%), the best configurations (VisPruner+RESTORE, HoloV+RESTORE) achieve 98.8% average, nearly matching the full 576-token model. The framework also generalizes to LLaVA-NeXT-7B (up to 92.0% at 320 tokens) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B with M-RoPE (up to 97.4% at 33.3% retention).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RESTORE is integrated as a plug-and-play module on top of existing VTR baselines and consistently outperforms all of them. Against text-agnostic methods at 64 tokens, the gains range from +1.8% (VisionZip: 93.0→94.8%) to +4.3% (HoloV: 92.2→96.5%), with DivPrune improving by +2.1% and VisPruner by +3.2%. Against text-aware methods (FastV, PDrop, SparseVLM), improvements are even larger under aggressive reduction: FastV gains +14.7% (75.2→89.9%) and PDrop gains +10.8% (80.9→91.7%) at 64 tokens, while SparseVLM improves by +3.9% (90.6→94.5%). Against the layer-wise adaptive method FlowCut, RESTORE adds +1.6% to +2.0% at 64 tokens depending on the pruning ratio γ. Notably, RESTORE+HoloV at 64 tokens (96.5%) surpasses the full-token HoloV at 192 tokens (96.5%), meaning the method recovers nearly all accuracy lost by reducing tokens by 89%.

## Relation to Prior Work

RESTORE builds directly on the visual token reduction (VTR) literature for MLLMs, incorporating the token merging paradigm from ToMe (Bolya et al., 2023) and the hybrid prune-then-merge strategy from VisionZip. It departs from prior VTR methods (FastV, VisionZip, DivPrune, VisPruner, HoloV) by identifying an overlooked issue: the positional and attentional distortions introduced by token reduction under RoPE, which cause visual attention weights to be systematically redistributed toward text tokens. The distinctive anchor selection draws on density peak clustering (Rodriguez & Laio, 2014) but adapts it to the specific problem of minimizing information loss during feature averaging in MLLM token merging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play calibration mechanism addresses a fundamental distortion that affects all existing VTR methods, and the consistent 2–5% accuracy gains across three MLLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen2.5-VL) and eight benchmarks make it practically valuable for efficient deployment. A notable limitation is the regression on fine-grained text recognition (TextVQA) caused by feature averaging during merging, which the authors acknowledge and leave as future work for adaptive merging suppression. The framework's compatibility with layer-wise adaptive methods like FlowCut suggests broad applicability, but the calibration is specifically tuned for the retain-original-indices strategy and does not generalize to reindexing, limiting its universality.
