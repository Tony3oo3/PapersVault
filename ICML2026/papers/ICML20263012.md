## Problem

Diffusion planners select actions by scoring sampled candidate trajectories with a learned value critic, but this can favour plans that score highly yet are locally inconsistent with feasible dynamics, causing brittle execution under replanning. Prior feasibility-oriented approaches either modify the diffusion generator (denoising guidance, manifold projection) or require additional training objectives, increasing complexity. The paper argues that local feasibility should be treated as a signal distinct from value and addressed at the selection stage without altering the generator or critic.

## Input / Output

At each decision step, SAGE receives C candidate state trajectories (horizon H=32) sampled unguided from a diffusion planner, the current state s_t, and per-candidate value scores J from the DV critic. It computes a scalar latent consistency energy over the first K=10 transitions of each candidate using a frozen JEPA encoder and a frozen action-conditioned predictor, then outputs a re-ranked candidate index whose first action is executed. If the base planner generates states only, actions for the prefix are inferred via a diffusion inverse-dynamics model; the entire gating adds only ~6.8% wall-clock overhead over the base MCSS pipeline.

## Method

SAGE is a two-stage, purely offline training pipeline that produces an inference-time feasibility gate. Stage I trains a JEPA encoder (MLP, d_z=256) on masked state windows with an EMA teacher and a Transformer predictor, using VICReg variance/covariance regularisation to prevent collapse; the frozen EMA encoder then defines the latent space. Stage II trains a block-causal Transformer action-conditioned predictor in that frozen latent space with three losses: teacher-forced one-step prediction, a short-horizon (H_ro=4) rollout consistency term, and an in-batch permuted-action hinge that penalises action-insensitivity. At inference, the latent consistency energy E(τ) is the mean L1 prediction error over the first K transitions of each candidate; SAGE retains the lowest-energy P=0.8 fraction and selects via argmax_i (J(τ_i) − λE(τ_i)) with λ=0.1, requiring no environment rollouts, no retraining of the diffusion generator or critic, and no reward signal.

## Results

SAGE improves the overall D4RL average from 95.59 (DV*) to 97.69 (+2.10, p=1.1×10⁻⁹) across 18 tasks, with the largest gains in Kitchen manipulation (81.8→85.6, +3.75) and AntMaze navigation (81.6→84.5, +2.90). On MuJoCo locomotion, SAGE raises the average from 82.9 to 84.4 (+1.5, p=4.8×10⁻¹³). Gains on Maze2D (161.6→163.1) are smaller and not statistically significant (p=0.152) due to ceiling effects on Medium and Umaze. SAGE adds only 6.8% wall-clock inference overhead relative to the base MCSS planner.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE is evaluated as a drop-in selector-side augmentation on top of the DV* (MCSS) generate-and-rank planner, so the most direct comparison is SAGE vs. DV*: SAGE wins on all 18 tasks with a statistically significant overall improvement of +2.10 (p=1.1×10⁻⁹), with the largest per-domain gains in Kitchen (+3.75, p=1.1×10⁻⁴²) and AntMaze (+2.90, p=0.020). Against other diffusion planners, SAGE outperforms Diffuser* by a wide margin (e.g., 96.6 vs. 55.7 on Kitchen-Partial; 200.6 vs. 123.0 on Maze2D-Large), and also exceeds RGG, LoMAP, and LDCQ across all domains where they report results. Against non-diffusion offline RL baselines (BCQ, CQL, IQL) and diffusion policies (DQL, IDQL), SAGE achieves the highest scores in every domain, e.g., 96.6 vs. 66.7 (IDQL) on Kitchen-Partial and 200.6 vs. 186.8 (DQL) on Maze2D-Large. The only domain where SAGE does not reach statistical significance over DV* is Maze2D (p=0.152), attributed to ceiling effects on Medium and Umaze where DV* already scores near-optimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGE positions itself as a selector-side complement to existing diffusion planners (particularly the MCSS-style generate-and-rank approach of DV), rather than modifying the generator or its training. It repurposes the JEPA self-supervised predictive architecture—originally developed for visual representation learning—as a feasibility signal for action-conditioned transition consistency, a use distinct from prior work that applies self-supervision to improve critics or value functions. Among feasibility-oriented diffusion planners, SAGE departs from RGG (denoising-time guidance), LoMAP (geometric manifold projection), and LDCQ (latent-constrained selection) by introducing a reward-free, inference-time energy that gates candidate selection without touching the diffusion process or requiring environment rollouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of local feasibility from long-horizon value is a useful conceptual framing for diffusion planning, and the plug-and-play, no-retraining design makes SAGE immediately applicable to existing pipelines. However, gains over the strong DV baseline are modest (+1.5 to +3.75 D4RL-normalised points), Maze2D improvements are not statistically significant, and all experiments are confined to low-dimensional D4RL environments, leaving open whether the JEPA latent consistency signal retains discriminative power in high-dimensional or visually rich robotic settings. A key open question is whether the self-supervised representation quality (and thus the energy's informativeness) degrades as state/action dimensionality grows, and whether the fixed hyperparameter transfer (K=10, P=0.8, λ=0.1) holds across domains without per-task tuning.
