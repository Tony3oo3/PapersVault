## Problem

It remains poorly understood whether LLMs acquire generalized, geometrically organized representations of algorithmic primitives (e.g., nearest-neighbor search, distance computation) and whether multi-step reasoning is implemented by composing such primitives in activation space. In particular, it is unclear how reasoning-finetuned models like Phi-4-Reasoning differ from their base counterparts in the primitives they deploy and how they compose them across tasks.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes text prompts for four multi-step reasoning benchmarks (TSP, 3-SAT, AIME, Graph Navigation) and collects token-by-token residual stream activations from layer 17 of Phi-4-Base across 100+ responses per task. It produces k-means clusters (k=50) annotated as algorithmic primitives, spectral meta-clusters capturing hierarchical reasoning stages, primitive vectors extracted from the top 35 attention heads (ranked by average indirect effect), steered reasoning traces via vector injection at chosen layers and magnitudes, and algorithmic fingerprints (cluster frequency profiles) compared via signed χ² distance across models and tasks.

## Method

AlgoTrace first applies k-means clustering to latent activations over entire self-generated reasoning traces to discover candidate algorithmic primitives, then uses an automated GPT-4o pipeline to label each cluster by sampling 50 tokens with 15-token context windows and prompting for their algorithmic role. Spectral clustering of the inter-cluster transition probability matrix yields meta-clusters that reveal stereotyped sequential motifs in the reasoning trace. Primitive vectors are extracted by averaging attention head activations (projected back into the residual stream) over tokens belonging to each cluster, restricted to the top 35 heads by average indirect effect, following the function vector methodology of Todd et al. (2024). Causal validation is performed by injecting these vectors into the residual stream at various layers and magnitudes and measuring predefined behavioral hallmarks (e.g., proportion of nearest-neighbor paths, number of verification tokens). The key differentiator is the compositional geometry analysis: primitive vectors are combined via addition, subtraction, and scalar multiplication to test whether arithmetic composition in activation space induces composite algorithmic behaviors, and cross-task transfer is evaluated by injecting primitives extracted from one domain (e.g., AIME) into another (e.g., TSP).

## Results

Injecting the nearest_neighbor primitive vector into Phi-4 increases the proportion of nearest-neighbor paths by +56.1% and total unique paths by +72.0%, while the generate_path vector increases unique paths by +143.9%. The compare_verify vector produces the strongest effects on verification (+655.4%) and comparison (+1103.7%) behavioral hallmarks, and compute_distance makes the model perform distance calculations 86.5% earlier in the trace. For compositional induction, adding the TNR and RC primitive vectors in Llama-3-8B nearly matches its few-shot performance on the composite task in a zero-shot setting, and cross-task injection of AIME-derived primitives into TSP responses significantly increases nearest-neighbor path generation (p < 10⁻⁶ for selective node mention effects).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing interpretability or steering methods; instead, it compares algorithmic behavior across model variants and validates primitive vectors against each other. Phi-4-Reasoning responses are highly stereotyped in their primitive usage, while Phi-4 splits into two distinct subgroups (brute-force search vs. random guessing) on TSP, with Phi-4-Reasoning showing significantly more verification, comparison, and nearest-neighbor heuristic primitives. On 3-SAT, Phi-4-Reasoning uniquely employs an if_clause_true strategy cluster rarely seen in Phi-4. For compositional induction, the sum of TNR and RC primitive vectors in Llama-3-8B zero-shot nearly matches its few-shot performance on the composite task, whereas single-primitive injection only partially improves performance. Cross-task transfer is validated by injecting AIME-derived spatial_reasoning and plan_final_answer vectors into TSP responses, which significantly increases path generation and nearest-neighbor path proportion, mirroring the hallmark of Phi-4-Reasoning behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the function vector methodology of Todd et al. (2024) from simple input-output mappings (antonym, capitalize) to multi-step algorithmic primitives in complex reasoning traces, using k-means clustering of residual stream activations to identify candidate primitives before extracting their vectors. It departs from prior steering vector work (persona vectors, truthfulness vectors) by targeting fine-grained computational operations rather than coarse behavioral traits, and from chain-of-thought analysis by grounding interpretability in internal activations rather than output text. The compositional geometry perspective draws on neuroscience findings about neural representational geometries supporting generalization (Fascianelli et al., 2024; Bernardi et al., 2020), adapting those ideas to the LLM setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, multi-level lens for understanding how LLMs decompose reasoning into reusable algorithmic building blocks, and how reasoning finetuning reshapes their composition. The cross-task transfer of primitives (AIME vectors affecting TSP behavior) and the compositional induction via vector addition suggest that LLMs may implement a shared algebraic geometry of reasoning operations, opening the door to algorithmic finetuning objectives (e.g., next-primitive prediction). Key limitations include the restriction to linear compositions, the fact that compositional induction does not work uniformly across all models, and the observation that identified primitives do not always map cleanly to known algorithms. Open questions include whether non-linear or manifold-based compositions capture richer structure, and whether a universal primitive ontology exists across architectures.
