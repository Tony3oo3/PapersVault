## Problem

Foundation models with Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layers rely on deterministic Top-K routing that is brittle to input perturbations, poorly calibrated, and opaque to uncertainty. While Bayesian methods offer principled uncertainty quantification, existing approaches target weight-space posteriors (experts or attention) and incur prohibitive computational overhead at foundation-model scale, leaving the routing decision itself unmodelled as a stochastic process.

## Input / Output

The method consumes token hidden-state representations (D-dimensional vectors) at selected MoE layers and produces a discrete K-expert selection mask, continuous routing probabilities, and a method-specific uncertainty signal (trace of the inferred logit covariance for VGLR-FC, or the predicted scalar temperature for VTSR). The variational inference heads are applied selectively to only the 10 most perturbation-sensitive layers (identified via a preliminary brittleness sweep), while all other layers retain standard deterministic routing. At inference, VGLR draws S=35 Monte Carlo samples to average routing probabilities before Top-K selection, whereas VTSR performs a single exact categorical Sample-K draw with zero additional sampling overhead.

## Method

VMoER reformulates MoE routing as a latent variable model (logits → softmax → Top-K) and applies amortised variational inference directly to the routing decision variables rather than global weights. Two complementary variants are proposed: (1) VGLR (Logit-Space) learns a residual mean shift and full covariance (parameterised via Cholesky factor) over the logits using a centred Gaussian prior on the deterministic solution, so the KL term reduces to the distance of the residual from zero, preserving pre-trained routing; (2) VTSR (Selection-Space) restricts the variational family to a 1D temperature-scaled simplex, learning an input-dependent temperature via a lightweight MLP with a uniform categorical prior, where minimising KL is equivalent to maximising routing entropy. Training uses the reparameterisation trick (VGLR) or Gumbel-Softmax relaxation (VTSR) with a two-stage protocol: first LoRA fine-tuning to establish a deterministic baseline, then optimising only the variational heads on the selected layers. The full-covariance VGLR-FC captures inter-expert correlations that mean-field or dropout baselines miss, while VTSR achieves comparable calibration gains with strictly O(1) sampling cost independent of the number of Monte Carlo samples.

## Results

On OpenBookQA, VGLR-FC achieves the lowest calibration error across all three MoE architectures, reducing ECE by up to 94% on Granite-MoE (0.252 → 0.015) while maintaining competitive accuracy (0.740 vs. 0.746 MAP). VTSR achieves the best accuracy on Qwen-MoE (0.818) and the best NLL on DeepSeek-MoE (0.431). For OoD detection, VGLR-FC's internal logit variance signal improves average AUROC by 9 points over gating entropy (0.659 → 0.749), and routing stability under perturbation (σ=0.010) improves by 38% (Jaccard 0.532 → 0.612). All methods incur less than 1.2% additional FLOPs and activation memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGLR-FC outperforms both weight-space baselines (MCDropout and SWAG) on ECE across all three architectures: on Granite-MoE, VGLR-FC achieves ECE 0.015 vs. 0.037 (MCDR) and 0.041 (SWAG); on Qwen-MoE, 0.014 vs. 0.064 and 0.098; on DeepSeek-MoE, 0.054 vs. 0.103 and 0.140. In terms of accuracy, VGLR-FC matches or slightly trails MAP (0.740 vs. 0.746 on Granite) but beats both MCDR (0.734) and SWAG (0.736). VTSR achieves the highest accuracy on Qwen-MoE (0.818) and the lowest NLL on DeepSeek-MoE (0.431), surpassing all baselines including the heuristic Temp-Scale (0.817 ACC, 0.870 NLL). However, VTSR's raw temperature signal is a poor OoD detector (AUROC 0.509), whereas its temperature-modulated gating entropy achieves the best OoD AUROC (0.743), outperforming VGLR-FC's logit variance (0.749) only marginally. Both weight-space baselines also impose ~2.6% activation memory overhead (scaling linearly with MC samples S), compared to <1.2% for VGLR-FC, which uses a fixed lightweight inference head.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant weight-space Bayesian approach to LLM uncertainty (MCDropout, SWAG, Deep Ensembles) by relocating the site of inference from expert/attention parameters to the router's decision variables, treating Top-K routing as a latent variable model. It builds directly on the heuristic stochastic routing introduced by Fedus et al. (2022) and Zoph et al. (2022), but reframes these ad-hoc stabilisation tricks as implicit Bayesian priors within a principled ELBO objective. The two proposed inference strategies—amortised variational inference over routing logits (VGLR) and learned temperature scaling (VTSR)—are positioned as complementary to, rather than replacements for, prior variational work on attention (Chen & Li, 2023) or expert weights (Dialameh et al., 2025), targeting a different and computationally cheaper decision manifold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-1% FLOPs overhead for calibrated Bayesian routing makes uncertainty quantification practically viable in production MoE deployments, a regime where weight-space posteriors remain intractable. The finding that internal routing variance (trace of the inferred covariance) substantially outperforms standard gating entropy for OoD detection suggests a previously underexploited uncertainty signal in MoE architectures. However, the evaluation is confined to MCQA benchmarks on 3B–16B models, VTSR exhibits temperature collapse without careful initialisation, and the framework has not yet been validated on open-ended generation or 70B+ architectures—key open questions for broader adoption.
