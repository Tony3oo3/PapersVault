## Problem

Prior work on interpretable, linearly controllable concept vectors for fair and safe text-to-image generation relies entirely on the U-Net bottleneck (h-space), which has no architectural analogue in modern ViT-based diffusion models such as FLUX, PixArt, and SD3.5. No existing method identifies where in a ViT backbone semantic concepts are encoded with the same near-linear, disentangled structure, leaving a gap in both theory and practice for responsible generation across current state-of-the-art architectures.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a standard text prompt and a set of pre-learned external concept vectors (one per selected attention head, each of dimension d_head) that are additively injected into the frozen model's linear semantic locus; no model parameters are modified. For fairness, concept vectors for each group member (e.g., male/female) are sampled with equal probability per generation; for safety, anti-content vectors are applied unconditionally. The output is a single generated image at the model's native resolution, with the only computational overhead being a vector addition at the LSL per diffusion step.

## Method

The core contribution is an architecture-agnostic framework that identifies the multi-modal attention heads in ViT-based DMs (and the bottleneck in U-Net DMs) as a linear semantic locus (LSL) where concept vectors can be injected. Theoretically, Theorem 1 proves exact linearity at the attention block output (injected vectors combine via simple addition through fixed output projections with no cross-head terms), and Theorem 2 shows near-linearity at the model output via a Taylor expansion, with deviations bounded by second-order terms. Training uses a novel concept-alignment loss: three frozen copies of the DM are employed, where M1 generates a reference image under a target-included prompt Φ, M2 conditions on a target-removed prompt Γ with the concept vector H added to the LSL, and M3 conditions on Φ unmodified; minimizing the L2 distance between M2's and M3's noise/velocity predictions forces H to encode exactly the semantic contribution of the removed attribute while suppressing spurious correlations. A Top-K head selection mechanism ranks attention heads by gradient norm during training to identify the most influential intervention points, and a mean-ablation study determines which transformer layers to target, reducing complexity while preserving fidelity.

## Results

On the WinoBias benchmark (36 occupations, 150 images each), the method reduces the deviation ratio Δ from 0.78–0.84 (original models) to 0.05–0.10 across PixArt, FLUX, SD3.5, and SDXL for both gender and race, substantially outperforming all baselines. On COCO-30k (1k captions), the method preserves visual fidelity with FID within ~1 point of the original model (e.g., 71.51 vs. 71.44 for SDXL gender) while maintaining CLIP scores at or above baseline levels (e.g., 31.00 vs. 30.84). On the I2P safety benchmark, unsafe image percentages drop from 56% (SDXL) to 14% and from 64% (FLUX) to 20%, outperforming H-Guide (26%) and SelfDisc (24%) on SDXL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four baselines on the primary fairness metric (deviation ratio Δ) across every model and attribute tested. Against the ViT-based baselines, it achieves Δ=0.05 vs. WG's 0.12 and EMBEDIT's 0.11 on PixArt gender (a 2.4× and 2.2× improvement respectively), and Δ=0.08 vs. WG's 0.13 and EMBEDIT's 0.14 on FLUX gender. Against the U-Net-based baselines on SDXL, it reduces Δ from 0.16 (H-Guide) and 0.15 (SelfDisc) to 0.07 for gender, and from 0.24 (H-Guide) and 0.20 (SelfDisc) to 0.10 for race. Crucially, unlike WG and EMBEDIT which degrade CLIP scores substantially (e.g., PixArt gender CLIP drops from 34.2 to 26.1 under WG), the proposed method maintains or slightly exceeds the original model's CLIP score (34.4), indicating that fairness gains come without sacrificing text-image alignment. On the I2P safety benchmark, the method reduces average unsafe content from 56% to 14% on SDXL (vs. 26% for H-Guide and 24% for SelfDisc) and from 64% to 20% on FLUX, confirming consistent safety improvements across both architecture families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the h-space line of work (Kwon et al., 2023; Haas et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024b; Shokrolahi et al., 2026), which established that U-Net bottleneck representations admit interpretable, near-linear concept vectors, to the ViT-based diffusion models (FLUX, PixArt, SD3.5) that now dominate the T2I landscape. The key conceptual departure is identifying multi-modal attention heads as the ViT analogue of the U-Net bottleneck, supported by two theorems proving exact linearity at the attention block and near-linearity (to leading order) at the model output. The proposed concept-alignment loss departs from the reconstruction-based objectives in prior h-space methods by explicitly isolating the target attribute's semantic contribution through a contrastive formulation between target-included and target-removed prompts, reducing spurious attribute entanglement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a significant architectural gap: prior interpretable concept-vector methods were fundamentally tied to U-Net bottlenecks and could not be applied to the ViT-based models (FLUX, PixArt, SD3.5) that are now state-of-the-art, leaving a blind spot in bias mitigation for the most widely deployed T2I systems. The frozen-model-plus-external-vector design is practically attractive because it requires no retraining, is modular and auditable, and introduces negligible inference overhead (~0.1–0.2 s). Limitations include degradation of linearity under extreme scaling (saturation beyond |α| > 40) and minor interference between highly correlated attributes (e.g., 'male' and 'mustache'), leaving open the question of how to handle strongly entangled concept spaces. A broader open question is whether the near-linear structure identified here persists under fine-tuning or adapter-based modifications of the base model.
