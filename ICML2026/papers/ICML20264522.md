## Problem

Large reasoning models underperform humans by 30–40% on spatial reasoning tasks, and existing remedies treat this as a factual knowledge deficit requiring supervised fine-tuning on externally labeled data. This paper argues that the necessary spatial capabilities are already latent in pre-trained models but lack internal logical coherence, and asks whether enforcing consistency under known geometric and semantic transformations can unlock those capabilities without any ground-truth labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264522/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image with highlighted objects (marked by colored dots or bounding boxes) paired with a True/False spatial question (e.g., 'Is object 1 left of object 2?') or a numeric question (counting, absolute distance in meters). At training time, each prompt is augmented on-the-fly with independently sampled image transforms (horizontal flip, object-preserving crop, color jitter) and text transforms (relation swap, object-order swap, template resampling), producing a paired prompt whose correct answer is related to the original by a known invariance or equivariance mapping. Outputs are structured as chain-of-thought reasoning in <think> tags followed by a final answer in <answer> tags.

## Method

The paper introduces consistency verifiers—label-free reward functions that check whether two model completions (one from the original prompt, one from its augmented version) satisfy the expected relationship induced by the transformation (matching for invariant transforms, disagreement for equivariant ones). To optimize these pairwise rewards with group-based policy optimization, the authors propose OT-GRPO, which replaces standard per-completion rewards with a minimal-consistency matching: given K completions from each prompt, it solves a linear assignment problem (via the network-simplex solver) to find the permutation that minimizes total consistency, then assigns the resulting pairwise scores as shared advantages to both completions in each matched pair. This adversarial pairing is shown to be equivalent to minimizing a Wasserstein distance between the model's output distributions under an inconsistency cost, and under a random-guessing baseline yields expected reward O(1/√K) versus the constant 1/2 of random or one-to-all pairing, making the signal robust to reward hacking. The full training loop fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL (3B/7B) with LoRA using GRPO-style clipped surrogate updates on both original and augmented completions, with a format reward enforcing the think/answer structure.

## Results

On SUN RGB-D (7B model), consistency training (no labels) achieves 76.8% average accuracy across four spatial tasks, trailing accuracy training (79.6%) by only 2.8pp and beating the pre-training baseline (63.4%) by 13.4pp. In the joint four-task setting, consistency reaches 83.8% versus 86.5% for accuracy training (2.7pp gap). On numeric tasks, consistency trails accuracy by just 0.5pp on counting (68.3% vs 68.8%) and 2.3pp on absolute distance (39.1% vs 41.4%). Cross-task and cross-domain transfer gaps between consistency and accuracy remain under 1pp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's consistency method (83.8%) outperforms the strongest Visual Jigsaw variant (Wu et al., 2025) by +3.6pp and all four SSL4RL variants (Guo et al., 2025b) by +13–24pp on the joint four-task SUN RGB-D benchmark; several SSL4RL rotation/jigsaw variants fall below the 60.6% pre-training baseline. Against the accuracy-trained upper bound (86.5%, which requires ground-truth labels), consistency training trails by only 2.7pp. In cross-task transfer, consistency gains +9.2pp over baseline versus +10.1pp for accuracy (0.8pp gap); in cross-domain transfer (SUN RGB-D → KITTI), the gap shrinks to 0.5pp. Under label corruption, consistency overtakes accuracy from 20% noise onward, reaching a 7.4pp advantage at 40% corruption, demonstrating that the label-free signal is more robust to annotation errors than supervised training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) as its RL backbone, but departs from the standard scalar-reward formulation by introducing pairwise consistency verifiers and the OT-GRPO optimal-transport matching scheme to handle them. Conceptually, it repurposes the equivariance/invariance framework from computer vision (cycle-consistency, equivariant similarity) as a self-supervised training signal, in contrast to the dominant prior approach of treating spatial reasoning as a knowledge deficit solved by SFT on labeled data from synthetic engines or vision-model cascades. It also extends the self-consistency and prompt-sensitivity literature (Wang et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2024) from inference-time heuristics to a formal RL objective, and connects the minimal-consistency pairing to Wasserstein distance minimization via the Birkhoff-von Neumann theorem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that label-free consistency training closes within ~2–3 pp of ground-truth supervised training across four spatial tasks and two domains challenges the assumption that spatial reasoning requires ingesting labeled 3D data, with direct practical implications given the cost and error-proneness of spatial annotation pipelines. The robustness result—consistency overtaking accuracy at 20% label corruption—is particularly relevant for real-world pipelines that chain depth estimators, detectors, and LLMs. Key limitations include the restriction to binary (and limited numeric) answer spaces, two model sizes, and pairwise (not compositional) consistency checks; extending to chains of three or more transformations, richer relational structures, and non-spatial modalities are natural open directions.
