## Problem

Replicating the in-context generation paradigm from image editing to video editing is nontrivial due to temporal complexities. Without pre-specified editing masks, instruction-based video editing suffers from two coupled failures: inaccurate localization of the editing region and token interference between source and target content across regions. The paper asks how to model region-wise relationships on both video latents and attention maps to achieve precise, mask-free instructional video editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265863/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (81 frames at 480×832, 16 fps, ~5 s) and a natural-language editing instruction as input, and produces an edited video of identical format. At inference, no editing mask is required; the model implicitly grounds the editing region through learned attention patterns. During training, binary latent-space masks (derived from SAM 2 segmentation with 5–10 px random padding) are used solely to compute the regional constraint losses and are not fed to the network as conditioning.

## Method

ReCo reformulates video editing as in-context generation by width-wise concatenating the source and target video latents into a single in-context latent that is jointly denoised by a flow-matching DiT (Wan-T2V-1.3B, fine-tuned with LoRA rank 128), supplemented by an auxiliary video-condition branch. Two regional-constraint regularization terms are added to the standard flow-matching MSE loss: (1) a latent-space term that, on one-step backward denoised latents, maximizes the absolute discrepancy between source and target within the editing mask while minimizing it outside, thereby amplifying intended modifications and suppressing background drift; and (2) an attention-space term that suppresses the attention of target-editing-region query tokens toward the corresponding source-editing-region key tokens (edit loss) and toward the entire source video (global loss), while simultaneously encouraging attention to the target video's own background keys for coherent integration. The overall objective is L = L_ic + λ₁·L_latent + λ₂·L_attn with λ₁ = λ₂ = 1×10⁻³, and the model is trained on the newly constructed ReCo-Data (500K instruction-video pairs) covering object add, remove, replace, and global style transfer.

## Results

ReCo achieves the best overall score S across all four instruction-based video editing tasks on a VLLM-based benchmark (480 video-instruction pairs): 8.23 on Add, 8.74 on Replace, 7.00 on Remove, and 9.17 on Style transfer. The evaluation uses nine sub-dimensions grouped into Edit Accuracy (S_EA), Video Naturalness (S_VN), and Video Quality (S_VQ), with S being the arithmetic mean of the three category scores. ReCo leads on S_EA for local editing tasks (e.g., 9.43 on Replace vs. 4.56 for Ditto), demonstrating superior instruction following and region localization, while also maintaining competitive S_VN and S_VQ scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCo outperforms all baselines on the overall score S across every task it supports. Against Ditto (the strongest competitor), ReCo leads by 0.67 on Add (8.23 vs. 7.56), 2.16 on Replace (8.74 vs. 6.58), and 0.16 on Style (9.17 vs. 9.01); Ditto does not support the Remove task. The gap is most pronounced on Replace, where ReCo's S_EA of 9.43 far exceeds Ditto's 4.56, indicating Ditto's ControlNet-based conditioning struggles with semantic understanding and precise region localization. Against Lucy-Edit, ReCo wins by 1.92 on Add, 2.02 on Replace, and 4.34 on Style, with Lucy-Edit also lacking Remove capability. InsViE is consistently the weakest baseline (S ≈ 3.0–3.2 on local tasks), while VACE (used only for Remove) scores 5.19 versus ReCo's 7.00. Notably, ReCo uses only 2.1B parameters and 26.9 GB peak inference memory, compared to Ditto's 17.3B parameters and 64.3 GB, making it substantially more efficient while delivering superior editing accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReCo adapts the in-context generation paradigm—recently successful in image editing (ICEdit, Flux-Kontext, HiDream-E1)—to the video domain by width-wise concatenating source and target videos for joint denoising within a flow-matching DiT (Wan-T2V-1.3B). Its two regularization terms (latent-space and attention-space regional constraints) draw conceptually from region-aware contrastive learning (Hu et al., ICCV 2021), repurposing the region-wise discrepancy formulation to guide diffusion training without requiring explicit masks at inference. This departs from prior training-based video editors (Ditto's ControlNet conditioning, Lucy-Edit's latent-concatenation conditioning) by treating the source video as a co-generated context rather than a static condition, and from mask-based methods (VACE, VideoPainter) by learning implicit region grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 500K ReCo-Data dataset (91.6% high-quality samples vs. 17.9–29.2% for prior datasets) and the VLLM-based 9-dimension evaluation benchmark address two critical infrastructure gaps in instructional video editing, making the field more reproducible and comparable. The finding that in-context joint denoising yields substantially better semantic understanding than ControlNet-style conditioning (e.g., 8.74 vs. 8.07 on Replace) suggests a broader principle for video editing architectures. Open questions include whether the regional constraints generalize to more than four task types, how the approach scales to longer or higher-resolution videos, and whether the shadow/reflection limitations can be resolved without task-specific pipelines.
