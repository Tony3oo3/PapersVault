## Problem

GRPO training in tool-integrated reinforcement learning (e.g., Search-R1) consistently suffers from abrupt reward drops and catastrophic collapse, yet the underlying mechanism remained unexplained. Prior attributions to training-inference mismatch or low-likelihood incorrect responses failed to account for why structurally similar algorithms like PPO remain stable, nor did they identify the structural origin of the instability. This paper identifies Lazy Likelihood Displacement (LLD)—a systematic, self-reinforcing reduction in the likelihood of correct response trajectories—as the core failure mode driving collapse.

## Input / Output

The model receives a natural-language question and generates a multi-turn trajectory alternating between search queries (tool calls to an E5 dense retriever over 2018 Wikipedia) and reasoning/answer tokens, with up to 3–4 turns. Retrieved passages are injected as out-of-distribution context between turns; they are masked from the GRPO loss but remain in the conditioning prefix for subsequent token predictions. Training uses a merged NQ+HotpotQA corpus with outcome-level exact-match rewards, and evaluation spans seven open-domain and multi-hop QA benchmarks (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA, HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, Musique, Bamboogle).

## Method

The paper proposes LLDS (LLD Suppression), a likelihood-preserving regularizer added to the GRPO objective that activates only when a response action's overall log-likelihood decreases (action-level gating) and penalizes only the individual tokens responsible for that decrease (token-level selectivity), thereby minimally interfering with beneficial updates. A variant, LLDS-MA, further reduces the regularization weight on final-answer tokens to discourage premature single-turn answering and encourage multi-step tool use. Theoretically, the paper proves that in tool-integrated GRPO, low-likelihood incorrect responses sharing high embedding similarity with correct ones produce disproportionately large negative gradients that suppress correct-trajectory likelihood, creating a self-reinforcing LLD Death Spiral (likelihood decay → low confidence → amplified negative gradients → entropy explosion → gradient collapse). Empirically, LLDS stabilizes training across Qwen2.5-3B/7B (Base and Instruct) and Llama3.2-3B, yielding relative improvements of +45.2% (3B-Base) and +37.1% (7B-Base) over vanilla GRPO while outperforming dense-reward baselines like TreeGRPO by 4–10% using only outcome rewards.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-3B-Base, LLDS-MA improves the average Exact Match from 0.312 (vanilla GRPO) to 0.440, a +45.2% relative gain, while on Qwen2.5-7B-Base the gain is +37.1% (0.350 → 0.480). For the Instruct variants, LLDS achieves 0.441 on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (+31.3%) and 0.483 on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (+22.0%). LLDS also stabilizes training where vanilla GRPO collapses within ~300 steps, and outperforms the strongest dense-reward baseline Tree-GRPO by 4% (3B) and 10% (7B) in overall average EM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLDS-MA on Qwen2.5-3B-Base (0.440 avg) outperforms all baselines: it beats Tree-GRPO (0.424), the strongest dense-reward method, by 4%, and exceeds ZeroSearch-Base (0.345), StepSearch (0.311), and vanilla Search-R1-GRPO (0.312) by 27%, 42%, and 41% respectively. On Qwen2.5-7B-Base, LLDS (0.480) surpasses Tree-GRPO (0.439) by 10% and ZeroSearch-Base (0.409) by 17%. Inference-only baselines (Search-o1 at 0.255, RAG at 0.270, R1 at 0.229, Rejection Sampling at 0.265) are all substantially below LLDS, confirming that RL-based training with likelihood preservation is necessary. CriticSearch (0.343 MH-Avg) and StepSearch (0.263 MH-Avg) rely on dense turn-level rewards from ground-truth documents or an external LLM judge, yet LLDS using only outcome rewards achieves 0.372–0.426 MH-Avg. LLDS also integrates with GSPO, where GSPO+LLDS (0.451) outperforms vanilla GSPO (0.331) by 36.3%, demonstrating generality across group-relative objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lazy Likelihood Displacement (LLD) analysis from single-turn GRPO (Deng et al., 2025) and DPO (Razin et al., 2024) to the multi-turn, tool-integrated setting, identifying it as the previously unexplained root cause of GRPO collapse in Search-R1-style pipelines. It departs from concurrent work that attributes instability to training-inference mismatch (Liu et al., 2025) or low-likelihood incorrect responses (Xue et al., 2025) by providing a unified mechanistic account that also explains PPO's relative robustness. Methodologically, it contrasts with dense-reward approaches (StepSearch, CriticSearch, TreeGRPO) by demonstrating that outcome rewards alone suffice once the likelihood-preserving LLDS regularizer stabilizes optimization, eliminating the need for turn-level ground-truth documents, external LLM judges, or tree-based rollouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training collapse in GRPO-based tool-integrated RL is a major practical bottleneck that has blocked scalable training of search-augmented and agentic LLMs; identifying LLD as the structural mechanism and providing a lightweight fix (LLDS) offers a concrete path forward without the supervision overhead of dense-reward methods. The LLD death spiral framework—where likelihood decay self-amplifies through low-confidence trajectories and embedding similarity—provides a diagnostic lens applicable beyond search to other tool-integrated settings (code execution, API calls). Open questions include whether the mechanism and fix generalize to non-search tools, larger model families, and whether the action-level gating granularity is optimal for longer multi-turn trajectories. The evaluation is limited to QA benchmarks and two model families (Qwen2.5, Llama3.2), so broader validation is needed.
