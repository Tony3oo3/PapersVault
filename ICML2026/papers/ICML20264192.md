## Problem

Diffusion models have excelled for continuous and categorical data, but count-based data on the natural numbers remain underdeveloped. Relaxing counts to reals wastes capacity on an unbounded continuous space before quantizing, while treating each count value as a separate category ignores natural ordering and scales poorly with maximum count. Blackout Diffusion (Santos et al., 2023) provides a theoretical foundation for count diffusion but lacks the flexible design space—continuous-time training, guidance, loss weighting, and non-monotone reverse dynamics—that modern diffusion frameworks rely on.

## Input / Output

The model takes count vectors over N₀ (e.g., 32×32×3 pixel values for CIFAR-10, or gene expression matrices for scRNA-seq) and produces full count vectors as output. For imputation, a binary mask specifies which entries are observed; observed entries are reset to their noised ground-truth values at each reverse step (RePaint-style), while only masked entries are resampled. An optional class label condition is incorporated via classifier-free guidance, where the network is trained with the label randomly dropped at rate p_uncond=0.1.

## Method

CountsDiff defines a forward process as an inhomogeneous pure-death process where each count element independently survives with probability p(t), yielding binomial marginals q(x_t|x₀) = Bin(x₀, p(t)). The key reparameterization expresses the process through a survival probability schedule p(t) whose signal-to-noise ratio exactly matches that of Gaussian diffusion noise schedules, enabling direct transfer of schedules like the cosine schedule. Training minimizes a weighted Poisson-type loss E[w(t)(ŷ_t − y_t log ŷ_t)] where the network predicts y_t = x₀ − x_t (the number of lost elements), with w(t) = (π/2)sin(πt) for the cosine schedule. The reverse process generalizes the pure-birth process to a birth-death process with an attrition parameter σ_{t,s} (analogous to churn/remasking), allowing non-monotone trajectories that correct overshooting errors. Classifier-free guidance is adapted by exponentiating the predicted y values, and a randomized rounding scheme (floor + Bernoulli) prevents the mode-collapse-at-zero failure mode of naive integer rounding.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the best CountsDiff configuration (cosine p-schedule, guidance γ=1.0, attrition η_rescale=0.01) achieves FID 5.26 and IS 9.85, improving over the Blackout-equivalent baseline (FID 5.73, IS 9.12). On the fetal cell atlas 50% MCAR imputation task, CountsDiff (1-sample) achieves the best distributional metrics among all methods: log(scFID) −9.18, log(MMD) −11.94, and ED 0.01, outperforming the closest competitor ReMDM (log(scFID) −8.98, log(MMD) −11.69) and its predecessor Blackout (log(scFID) −7.37, log(MMD) −10.78). CountsDiff uses roughly half as many parameters as ReMDM, whose output layer scales with the maximum count value.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CountsDiff outperforms its direct predecessor Blackout on all distributional metrics in the fetal MCAR task (log(scFID) −9.18 vs −7.37; log(MMD) −11.94 vs −10.78), with the gap attributable to the cosine p-schedule, guidance, and attrition. Against ReMDM, the strongest discrete-diffusion competitor, CountsDiff wins on log(scFID) (−9.18 vs −8.98) and log(MMD) (−11.94 vs −11.69) but trails slightly on SWD (0.08 vs 0.12 for 1-sample; 0.08 vs 0.08 for 5-sample), and the authors note ReMDM's higher RMSE standard error suggests outlier over-sampling. Non-generative methods (xTrimoGene, scGPT, HI-VAE, Conditional Mean) achieve better pointwise metrics (RMSE, Spearman) because they predict conditional means rather than sampling from a distribution, but they are substantially worse on distributional metrics (e.g., xTrimoGene log(scFID) −6.71 vs CountsDiff −9.18). Forest-Diffusion (Gaussian diffusion adapted via RePaint) catastrophically fails with RMSE 27.18, confirming the paper's argument that continuous relaxation is ill-suited to sparse count data. In the toy experiment, masked diffusion (MDLM) overfits outliers with 3–10× inflated per-dimension variance, while Gaussian diffusion in log-space suffers complete mode collapse; CountsDiff avoids both failure modes. In the MNAR setting, scGPT and xTrimoGene appear strong but the authors attribute this to fortuitous negative bias aligning with the low-biased missingness pattern, not genuine robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

CountsDiff is a direct generalization of Blackout Diffusion (Santos et al., 2023), reparameterizing its forward process in terms of a survival probability schedule p(t) and unlocking a design space with direct analogues to modern Gaussian and categorical diffusion: continuous-time training (Campbell et al., 2022), classifier-free guidance (Nisonoff et al., 2025), and churn/remasking (Wang et al., 2025a). The key conceptual departure from prior count-based approaches is rejecting both the relaxation-to-reals (Gaussian diffusion) and the treat-each-value-as-a-category (masked diffusion) strategies, instead modeling the natural numbers natively via a pure-death (forward) and birth-death (reverse) process that preserves ordinal structure. The RePaint algorithm (Lugmayr et al., 2022) is repurposed for imputation without retraining, following its prior adaptation in Forest-Diffusion (Jolicoeur-Martineau et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's main practical contribution is a unified design space for count-based diffusion where each hyperparameter (noise schedule, loss weighting, attrition, guidance) has a direct, interpretable counterpart in Gaussian or categorical diffusion, enabling systematic transfer of design choices that have been empirically validated in those domains. This matters for biology because scRNA-seq, ATAC-seq, and metagenomic data are inherently count-valued, and the framework matches a state-of-the-art discrete generative model (ReMDM) on imputation with roughly half the parameters while avoiding the outlier-over-sampling failure mode. Open questions include extending fast-sampling techniques (consistency models, mean flows) to the count domain, further optimizing the underexplored attrition schedule, and adapting the framework to pre-trained single-cell foundation models and perturbation prediction.
