## Problem

Frozen self-supervised representations transfer well in few-shot settings, but the geometric reason is unclear: classical within-class variance metrics (CDNV) are too coarse because SSL embeddings are anisotropic, with substantial variance persisting in task-irrelevant directions while variance along class-separating axes is small. The paper asks what geometric quantity governs few-shot error and multitask interference, and whether sharp, non-vacuous certificates can be derived from it at practical shot sizes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen SSL encoder (ResNet-18/50 with width multiplier 2, or ViT-B/16) operating on 224×224 RGB images, plus a support set of m labeled examples per class (m ranging from 1 to 500). It produces two outputs: (i) a class prediction via nearest-class-centroid or linear probing, and (ii) a non-asymptotic error certificate that decomposes into a decision-axis variance term, finite-shot centroid-estimation corrections decaying at m^{-1/2} and m^{-1}, and a fourth-moment tail term at m^{-3}. The certificate is evaluated on binary (C=2) and ternary (C=3) tasks formed by randomly selecting classes from mini-ImageNet, CelebA, or CUB-200.

## Method

The core contribution is a sharp non-asymptotic multiclass bound for NCC and linear-probing error whose leading term is the directional CDNV — the within-class variance projected onto the one-dimensional class-separating axis — rather than the classical CDNV that aggregates variance over all directions. The bound isolates three effects: (i) a decision-axis term with an optimal leading constant of 4 (proven tight via Cantelli's inequality and a two-point extremizer), (ii) finite-shot centroid-estimation leakage controlled by global CDNV at rates m^{-1/2} and m^{-1}, and (iii) a separate fourth-moment correction for heavy tails at m^{-3}. A second theoretical result (Prop. 4.2) shows that for two independent balanced labelings, simultaneously small directional CDNV forces the induced decision axes to be nearly orthogonal, explaining how a single representation supports many tasks with low interference. Empirically, across seven SSL paradigms (SimCLR, VICReg, MAE, DINO-v2, I-JEPA, CLIP, SigLIP) and multiple backbones, directional CDNV collapses by 2–4 orders of magnitude during pretraining while classical CDNV remains large, and the derived bounds closely track observed few-shot error at practical shot sizes where prior directional bounds are vacuous.

## Results

Across four SSL paradigms (MAE, SimCLR, DINO-v2, VICReg) trained on mini-ImageNet, directional CDNV drops from roughly 2⁻¹–2¹ to 2⁻³–2⁻⁵ over 1000 epochs, while classical CDNV decreases only modestly or even increases transiently. The proposed finite-m NCC bound (Theorem 4.1) is non-vacuous at practical shot counts (m ∈ [1, 500]), dropping below the 0.5 chance threshold, whereas the prior directional bound of Luthra et al. (2025b) remains above 0.5 and is therefore vacuous in the same regime. On synthetic multitask data with four independent visual factors, median absolute cosine similarity between decision axes of different labelings decays from substantial alignment at initialization to near zero, consistent with the near-orthogonality guarantee of Proposition 4.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its finite-m directional CDNV bound against two prior generalization bounds. Against Luthra et al. (2025b), which introduced directional CDNV but with a looser coefficient structure, the new bound (Theorem 4.1) is non-vacuous at practical shot sizes (m ∈ [1, 500]) on mini-ImageNet binary tasks, while the prior bound remains above the 0.5 chance level and thus provides no useful certificate. Against Galanti et al. (2023b), which bounds few-shot error via classical (global) CDNV, the proposed directional CDNV bound is substantially tighter in the anisotropic SSL regime where total within-class variance remains large but decision-axis variance is small. The leading constant 4 in the decision-axis term is proven minimax-optimal under second-moment information via Cantelli's inequality, meaning no distribution-free bound can improve this factor without additional tail assumptions. Empirically, the bound closely tracks observed NCC and LP error across seven pretrained encoders (MAE, CLIP, I-JEPA, SigLIP, SimCLR, VICReg, DINO-v2) on mini-ImageNet, and extends to C=3 tasks in the appendix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the directional CDNV framework introduced by Luthra et al. (2025b), which measures within-class variance only along class-separating axes, by deriving sharper non-asymptotic multiclass bounds with explicit finite-shot centroid-estimation corrections, a separate fourth-moment tail term, and a provably optimal leading constant (4) via Cantelli's inequality. It also builds on the CDNV-based generalization bounds of Galanti et al. (2022b; 2023b) by replacing the global variance aggregation with the anisotropy-aware directional quantity, and on the neural collapse literature (Papyan et al., 2020; Han et al., 2022) by showing that SSL induces a 'directional' analogue of collapse rather than full within-class collapse. A novel contribution is the multitask geometry result: small directional CDNV across independent balanced labelings forces near-orthogonality of decision axes, connecting few-shot transfer to efficient multi-task packing in a shared representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, method-agnostic geometric explanation for why frozen SSL representations support strong few-shot transfer: variability along class-separating directions collapses during pretraining even when total within-class variance remains large, and this directional collapse is what governs downstream error. The multitask orthogonality result (Prop. 4.2) offers a structural account of how a single representation can support many tasks with low interference, which is relevant to the design and evaluation of foundation models. Open questions include whether the orthogonality guarantee extends to correlated or imbalanced labelings, whether the bounds can be tightened under sub-Gaussian or other tail assumptions, and whether the directional-collapse perspective can inform the design of SSL objectives that explicitly target decision-axis variance rather than global uniformity.
