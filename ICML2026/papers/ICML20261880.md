## Problem

The Open X-Embodiment (OXE) dataset, the primary resource for training generalist robot policies, is severely imbalanced with over 85% of real trajectories from just four robot types, causing policies to overfit to specific robot-scene combinations and fail to transfer to new embodiments without costly re-collection and fine-tuning. This paper asks whether existing demonstrations can be synthetically augmented to appear as if performed by different robots, and how scaling the number of augmented embodiments affects robustness, transfer, and generalization.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes source robot RGB frames (640×480, 50-frame clips), corresponding 6D gripper poses, and the target robot's URDF model. It outputs augmented video frames with the source robot replaced by the target embodiment, along with kinematically aligned 6D poses and Cartesian actions for each frame. The resulting OXE-AugE dataset provides 4.4M trajectories across 16 source datasets and 9 robot embodiments; downstream policies (OpenVLA, π₀) are fine-tuned on 256×256 third-person RGB observations conditioned on language instructions.

## Method

AugE-Toolkit extends the cross-painting framework with three key components: (1) a mask fusion strategy that aligns a fine-tuned SAM2 segmentation with a simulation-rendered mask via grid-search translation, FoV search, pruning, and morphological closing, enabling accurate robot segmentation without precise camera calibration; (2) an automatic base-position search that iteratively adjusts the target robot's base along x, y, z axes until operational-space-control tracking error drops below 1 cm, accommodating robots with different reach and scale; and (3) a learning-free simulation rendering stage on MuJoCo Playground that replays the source trajectory with the target robot and composites it into the inpainted background (via E²FGVI), making the pipeline embarrassingly parallel across robots. The paper then systematically scales the number of augmented embodiments from 1 to N and shows that increasing embodiment diversity consistently improves robustness to visual perturbations on the source robot, transfer to augmented robots, and generalization to unseen robots, with embodiment diversity proving more effective than additional trajectory count in low-data regimes.

## Results

In physical experiments, fine-tuning OpenVLA-OFT and π₀ on OXE-AugE (N× augmentation) improves average success by 24% and 45% respectively over no-augmentation baselines across four Bridge manipulation tasks on two Franka embodiments. On the novel Robotiq++ gripper (unseen embodiment), policies reach 75% (OpenVLA-OFT) and 82% (π₀) average success. In simulation, AugE-Toolkit outperforms the diffusion-based RoVi-Aug by 48–77% and beats Shadow by 25% on average for transfer to augmented robots, while matching Shadow on generalization to unseen robots. The OXE-AugE dataset itself contains over 4.4M trajectories (3× the original OXE) spanning 9 robot embodiments across 16 source datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **Shadow** (Lepert et al., 2024), which masks robot pixels and performs test-time image editing, AugE-Toolkit matches performance on generalization to unseen robots but outperforms it by 25% on average for transfer to augmented robots, with the gap widening as the number of augmented embodiments scales. The authors attribute Shadow's transfer weakness to its union-of-masks approach progressively removing task-relevant visual cues as more robots are added. Against **RoVi-Aug** (Chen et al., 2024b), which uses diffusion-based (ControlNet) image-space robot transformation, AugE-Toolkit's simulation-based cross-painting outperforms it by 48–77% in policy success on physical Bridge tasks, with qualitative analysis showing RoVi-Aug produces misaligned grippers and geometric inconsistencies. AugE-Toolkit also substantially outperforms standard visual augmentations (color jitter, brightness/contrast) for robustness under lighting shifts and occlusions, and outperforms wrist-camera-only policies for cross-embodiment transfer, which fail due to sensitivity to gripper geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-painting framework introduced by Mirage (Chen et al., 2024a) for test-time zero-shot transfer and RoVi-Aug (Chen et al., 2024b) for diffusion-based robot appearance augmentation. The key departure is shifting from pairwise (one-source-to-one-target) augmentation to scalable multi-robot augmentation, where a single source trajectory is simultaneously transformed into N target embodiments. AugE-Toolkit replaces RoVi-Aug's diffusion-based inpainting with simulation-based rendering fused with learned SAM2 masks, trading generative flexibility for kinematic consistency and scalability. The paper also positions itself against Shadow (Lepert et al., 2024), which masks robot pixels entirely, by preserving physically consistent robot geometry and appearance in the augmented data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OXE-AugE (4.4M trajectories, 9 robots, 16 datasets) is a major open-source resource that triples the OXE dataset and covers 58% of the Octo pretraining mixture, directly addressing the severe embodiment imbalance (top 4 robots = 85% of data) that limits generalist policy training. The finding that scaling embodiment diversity can outperform scaling trajectory count alone (25% source data + more robots > 100% source data + fewer robots) provides a concrete, actionable insight for data-efficient robot learning. However, the method is limited to 2D image-space augmentation with similar gripper geometries (2- or 3-jaw), quasi-static tasks, and third-person views; open questions include wrist-view augmentation, substantially different gripper morphologies, dynamic and bimanual settings, and whether the gains transfer to more complex manipulation tasks beyond pick-and-place.
