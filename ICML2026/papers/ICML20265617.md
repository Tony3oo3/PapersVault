## Problem

OT-based alignment methods are highly sensitive to the quantity and quality of supervision, yet obtaining high-quality labels is costly in practice. Existing active alignment methods are not tailored to OT (ignoring its cost function and marginal constraints), lack a principled way to quantify how new supervision propagates through the OT formulation, and are task-specific rather than generalizable across alignment problems.

## Input / Output

The method takes two object sets (e.g., nodes from two networks, or images and text phrases), their marginal distributions μ and ν, a cost matrix C, an initial binary supervision matrix H (zero for unsupervised, partially filled for weakly supervised), a total query budget k, and an oracle that answers 'which target y_j aligns with source x_i?' queries. It outputs a set of k queried source objects and the final sparse transport plan T (over 99% zero entries due to small entropic regularization ε), which encodes the soft one-to-one correspondence between the two sets. The method operates in batches of size n_b, re-solving OT after each batch of queries.

## Method

AVATAR quantifies the informativeness of querying a source object by computing the gradient of a utility function f(T) (e.g., L2 norm, entropy, or graph consistency) with respect to all possible supervision entries for that object, propagated through the entropy-regularized OT formulation via the chain rule. The key technical challenge—differentiating through the implicitly defined transport plan T—is solved by the adjoint-state method, which reformulates the gradient computation into a singular linear system of size (n+m) solvable by conjugate gradient with linear time complexity O(K(n+m)) and guaranteed linear convergence. Pairwise query impacts are then aggregated into object-level posterior impacts using the transport plan row T_{i,:} as posterior alignment probabilities. The algorithm iteratively selects the candidate with the largest posterior impact, queries the oracle, updates H, and re-solves OT, making it a task-agnostic plug-in for any OT-based alignment method.

## Results

On network alignment (MRR), AVATAR-L2/CONSIST achieve state-of-the-art performance across all four datasets, outperforming the best baseline (ENTROPY) by up to 2.8% MRR (e.g., 0.981 vs 0.972 on ACM-DBLP-A with JOENA at 10 query rounds). On cross-domain alignment (Recall@1), AVATAR-ENTROPY/L2 consistently lead on both image-text retrieval and grounding, achieving up to 2.6% improvement over the ENTROPY baseline (e.g., 0.509 vs 0.489 on ImageNet-C with GOT-W at 10 rounds). AVATAR also achieves up to 25× speed-up over TOPMATCHINGS while delivering 8.1% higher MRR than GIBBSMATCHINGS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVATAR consistently outperforms all four baseline families across every dataset and task. Against the ENTROPY strategy from Ren et al. (2021), AVATAR-L2 achieves up to 2.8% higher MRR on NA (0.981 vs 0.972 on ACM-DBLP-A) and up to 2.6% higher Recall@1 on CDA (0.509 vs 0.489 on ImageNet-C). Against MARGIN, DENSITY, and DIVERSITY from Li et al. (2024), AVATAR leads by 1–4% MRR on NA and 1–3% Recall@1 on CDA, with DENSITY being the weakest baseline overall. Against BETWEENNESS from Macskassy (2009), AVATAR achieves substantially larger gains (up to 5.5% MRR on ACM-DBLP-A), confirming that graph centrality is a poor proxy for query informativeness in OT-based alignment. Against GIBBSMATCHINGS and TOPMATCHINGS from Malmi et al. (2017), which are specifically designed for active NA, AVATAR wins by 3–13% MRR; the paper notes these matching-based methods fail on OT because the deterministic transport plan makes their uncertainty sampling ineffective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the active alignment literature (Malmi et al. 2017, Ren et al. 2019, Zhou et al. 2021), which was confined to task-specific network alignment methods, into a general framework for entropy-regularized OT-based alignment applicable to network alignment, image-text retrieval, and image-text grounding. The key technical departure is the use of the adjoint-state method to differentiate through the constrained OT formulation, reducing gradient computation to a linear system solvable by conjugate gradients with linear complexity. Unlike prior active alignment methods that design query strategies around specific alignment objectives (e.g., consistency principles), AVATAR decouples the query strategy from the alignment method by encoding global alignment quality through pluggable utility functions over the transport plan.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AVATAR provides the first principled, task-agnostic active learning framework for OT-based alignment, which is increasingly adopted across network alignment, multimodal learning, and point cloud registration. The adjoint-state reformulation is a reusable technique for differentiating through any entropy-regularized OT problem, potentially applicable beyond active learning (e.g., sensitivity analysis, hyperparameter tuning). Open questions include whether the framework extends to unregularized or multi-marginal OT, how it scales when the transport plan becomes dense (large ε), and whether the utility function choice or the gradient propagation mechanism is the dominant source of the observed gains. The 2–3% improvements over strong baselines, while consistent, are modest, and the per-round OT re-solve remains a bottleneck for very large-scale problems.
