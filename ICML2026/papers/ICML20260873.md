## Problem

LLM agents frequently encounter underspecified user instructions where latent uncertainty over intent leads to erroneous tool actions, yet existing training pipelines provide no explicit signal for whether a clarification question actually reduces goal uncertainty. Current approaches either leave clarification behavior implicit or rely on surface-level quality judges that reward fluency over informational value, causing models to default to generic, low-utility questions rather than targeted information-seeking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260873/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The clarifier receives the step-level interaction context x_t (accumulated dialogue history, tool actions, observations, and prior clarification exchanges) and produces either a single concise clarification question or an abstention token ('No need to ask'). The ground-truth goal G* is used exclusively for reward computation via teacher-forced log-likelihood scoring and is never exposed to the clarifier at inference time. During training, a strict user simulator answers sampled candidate questions, revealing task-specific information only when the question is sufficiently targeted, preventing reward hacking through generic queries.

## Method

The paper formulates clarification as an amortized Bayesian Experimental Design problem and defines an Information Gain Reward R_t as the difference between length-normalized teacher-forced log-likelihoods of the ground-truth goal G* with and without the clarification exchange (posterior minus prior belief). This reward is mathematically equivalent to the log-importance weight (pointwise mutual information) between the user's response and the true goal, and naturally yields zero for abstention. Training uses DAPO (Decoupled Advantage Policy Optimization) with group-relative advantages: the policy samples K candidate questions per context, each is answered by a strict user simulator, and the resulting rewards are normalized within the group to compute advantages that drive the decoupled-clipping policy update. A strict user simulator (Qwen3-14B) is employed during training to prevent the degenerate policy of issuing vague questions that elicit goal information from a cooperative simulator, ensuring high rewards are assigned only to genuinely targeted clarifications.

## Results

Across five heterogeneous agent backbones (Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-32B, DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeek-V3.2, GLM-4-32B), the information-gain-trained 1.7B clarifier achieves an average success rate of 27.3%, improving +3.7% over the no-clarification baseline (23.6%) and +4.3% over the untrained Qwen3-8B clarifier (23.0%). The method adds only 1.2 clarification turns and 0.3 total interaction steps on average, while the Qwen3-8B clarifier adds 2.6 turns. On the out-of-distribution Airline domain, the improvement reaches +5.4% over baseline. The 1.7B trained clarifier also approaches the best large-model clarifier (DeepSeek-V3.1, 18.1%) with a mere 0.3% gap in the single-agent setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three clarification strategies across five agent backbones: no clarification (None), an untrained Qwen3-8B model used as a clarifier, and the proposed 1.7B clarifier trained with information-gain-driven DAPO. The proposed method wins on every agent backbone in average success rate, achieving +3.7% over None and +4.3% over Qwen3-8B on average. The Qwen3-8B clarifier is inconsistent: it helps DeepSeek-V3.2 (+6.3%) but hurts Qwen3-32B (−2.6%) and GLM-4-32B (−1.5%), whereas Ours delivers stable positive gains across all five agents. In the single-agent setting (Table 2), the 1.7B trained clarifier (17.8% avg) nearly matches the 671B DeepSeek-V3.1 (18.1%) and surpasses Qwen3-30B (17.4%) and DeepSeek-R1 (16.9%), while using only 1.3 clarification turns versus 3.8–5.1 for the larger models. The ablation (Table 1) further confirms that the information-gain reward is essential: removing it (w/o Information Gain) drops airline success from 17.3% to 10.0%, and replacing it with an LLM-as-a-judge signal collapses airline performance to 8.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DAPO reinforcement learning framework (Yu et al., 2025) and the τ-Bench tool-agent-user interaction environment (Yao et al., 2024), but its core conceptual contribution is importing the Bayesian Experimental Design (BED) framework (Chaloner & Verdinelli, 1995) into LLM clarification. Prior clarification work in dialogue and search (Clark, 1996; Zou et al., 2023; Aliannejadi et al., 2019) either relies on static question selection, known-answer targets, or LLM-as-judge quality scores; this paper departs by formulating clarification as an amortized policy that is trained end-to-end with a pointwise information-gain reward (the log-importance weight between the observed answer and the ground-truth goal), eliminating the need for subjective supervision. The strict user simulator is a practical innovation that prevents the degenerate reward-hacking behavior observed when cooperative simulators respond to generic questions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a principled information-theoretic reward can train a small (1.7B) clarifier to match or exceed much larger models (up to 671B) in resolving task ambiguity, while asking far fewer questions (1.3 vs. 3.9–5.1 turns). The finding that interactive clarification can outperform static full-intent provision in complex domains (airline: 17.3% vs. 16%) challenges the common assumption that dumping all constraints upfront is optimal. Key limitations include reliance on ground-truth goals for reward computation (unavailable in real deployment), a fixed base agent during training, and evaluation confined to τ-Bench's two domains. Open questions include whether the framework extends to noisy multi-turn user feedback, whether joint agent-clarifier optimization yields further gains, and whether the information-gain signal generalizes beyond the retail/airline task structures.
