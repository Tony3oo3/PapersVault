## Problem

Vision-Language-Action models process dense 256-token visual grids, creating a quadratic computational bottleneck that hinders real-time robotic deployment. Existing token pruning methods rely on discrete selection over a fixed patch grid, which introduces quantization errors that discard geometrically critical but semantically subtle regions (e.g., contact points between patches), forcing a fundamental trade-off between compression rate and manipulation accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261121/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes two RGB camera views (a front third-person view at ~45° and a wrist-mounted egocentric view) at 224×224 or 512×512 resolution, along with a natural-language instruction. The vision encoder (DINOv2 or SigLIP) produces a dense 16×16×C feature grid; GridS compresses this to K continuous-coordinate-sampled tokens (K=4, 16, or 32) with injected position embeddings before concatenation with language and action tokens. The output is a robot action chunk (10 or 50 steps) predicted via flow-matching (π₀, π₀.₅) or autoregressive decoding (SmolVLA).

## Method

GridS is a plug-and-play, differentiable module inserted between the frozen vision encoder and the downstream transformer. Stage 1 (Global Coordinate Prediction) applies global average pooling to the dense feature map and passes the resulting context vector through a lightweight MLP with a sigmoid output to predict K continuous normalized coordinates in [0,1]^(K×2). Stage 2 (Grid Sampling with Geometry Injection) extracts features at these sub-pixel locations via differentiable bilinear interpolation over the four nearest grid neighbors, then adds coordinate-derived position embeddings to restore spatial awareness. Because bilinear interpolation is fully differentiable with respect to the predicted coordinates, gradients from the task loss flow back through the interpolation weights to the coordinate-predictor MLP, enabling end-to-end joint optimization with the VLA policy using only the primary action-prediction loss—no auxiliary supervision or ground-truth attention maps are required. This continuous resampling eliminates the quantization error inherent in discrete patch-dropping and allows the model to sample precisely at task-relevant geometries (e.g., a grasp point lying on a patch boundary), achieving 76% FLOPs reduction with fewer than 10% of original visual tokens while matching or exceeding baseline success rates.

## Results

On LIBERO, GridS compresses π0 from 256 to 16 visual tokens (93.75% reduction), cutting FLOPs by 76% (216.01G → 51.65G) and inference time by 2.1s (8.17s → 6.05s), while *improving* average success rate by +1.6% (94.4% → 96.0%). At the extreme of 4 tokens, GridS still achieves 95.5% (+1.1% over baseline) with 80% FLOPs reduction. On the stronger π0.5 backbone, GridS_16 reaches 97.7% (+1.0%) and GridS_4 matches the baseline at 96.7%. In real-world Stack Cubes, GridS (K=16) achieves 61.9% success rate vs. 9.5% for the 64-token baseline, and 38.1% OOD success vs. 0.0% for the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three direct pruning competitors on the same π0 backbone, GridS (16 tokens) decisively wins: it outperforms FastV by +3.1% (96.0% vs. 92.9%) and SparseVLM by +6.2% (96.0% vs. 89.8%), both of which use 100 tokens and are training-free, while also beating VLA-Cache by +2.2% (96.0% vs. 93.8%) despite VLA-Cache retaining all 256 tokens. GridS also surpasses the unpruned π0 baseline itself by +1.6%, demonstrating that aggressive compression acts as a beneficial spatial regularizer rather than a lossy operation. On the stronger π0.5 backbone, GridS₁₆ extends the lead to +1.0% (97.7% vs. 96.7%), and even at 4 tokens it matches the baseline exactly (96.7%). In the real-world SO100 evaluation, GridS (K=16) achieves 61.9% success on Stack Cubes versus 9.5% for the 64-token SmolVLA baseline, and 38.1% OOD success versus 0.0% for the baseline, confirming that the advantage generalizes beyond simulation. The paper also notes that discrete pruning methods (FastV, SparseVLM) suffer catastrophic >20% drops on the harder LIBERO-PLUS benchmark, whereas GridS maintains near-lossless overall performance (−0.4% to −3.0%) while compressing 87.5% of tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

GridS departs from the dominant paradigm of discrete, training-free token pruning (SparseVLM, FastV, VLA-Cache) by reformulating visual token compression as a differentiable, jointly-optimized continuous resampling problem. It repurposes the continuous-sampling ideas from deformable attention (DAT, Deformable DETR) and implicit image representations (LIIF, NeRF) but applies them as a plug-and-play bottleneck between the vision encoder and the downstream Transformer in VLA architectures (π0, π0.5, SmolVLA). Unlike prior pruning that passively selects patches from a fixed grid, GridS actively predicts sub-patch coordinates via a lightweight MLP and extracts features through bilinear interpolation, making the entire compression differentiable and task-driven.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 76% FLOPs reduction with no in-domain degradation (and a modest +1.6% gain) makes VLA deployment on consumer-grade hardware substantially more feasible, while the 3.4× training speedup lowers the barrier to fine-tuning. The counter-intuitive finding that aggressive compression improves OOD generalization (+28.6% on real-world tasks) suggests an information-bottleneck mechanism that could reshape how practitioners think about visual redundancy in embodied AI. Open questions include whether the non-linear oscillating behavior observed in wrist-only inference (Table 7) reflects a fundamental optimization pathology, whether the bottleneck benefit persists in truly open-world settings beyond the tested benchmarks, and how the method handles tasks requiring full-body kinematic awareness (where GridS shows consistent −8% to −14% drops under robot initial state perturbations).
