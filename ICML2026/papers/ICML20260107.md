## Problem

While induction heads (IHs) are known to underpin in-context learning in transformers, the precise conditions governing their emergence during natural language pretraining remain unclear. Prior work reports widely varying emergence points (64M–3B tokens) and lacks a unified account of how training configuration and data statistics jointly determine when IHs form. This paper asks: can we predict the IH emergence point from training hyperparameters alone, and which data properties are necessary and sufficient for IH formation?

## Input / Output

The predictive law takes two scalar training hyperparameters—batch size B and context size C—and outputs the number of pretraining tokens N_PT at which IHs emerge, via N_PT = T · B^0.63 · C^0.38 with a single model-agnostic constant T ≈ e^13.26. For data-property analysis, the method takes a 2D configuration of bigram repetition frequency P(A,B,…,A) and reliability P(B|A,B,…,A) (each in [0,1]) and outputs a binary IH-formation outcome measured by prefix-matching score (PS) at the end of training. All experiments use a 50M-parameter 2-layer GPT-2 (8 heads, hidden dim 768) trained for 1B tokens on CC100 with a 50,257-token vocabulary, or a 10,000-token vocabulary for synthetic data.

## Method

The paper derives a predictive law by fitting a log-linear regression on 35 models spanning 50M–7B parameters, showing that the number of token-weighted updates (TWUs = U · B^0.37 · C^0.62) at which IHs emerge is a constant across model sizes (r = .986, p < .001). To characterize data properties, the authors define two metrics—frequency (proportion of tokens completing an ⟨A,B,…,A⟩ pattern) and reliability (conditional probability of B following such a pattern)—and fit a logistic decision boundary σ(k(α·log P_A + β·log P_B − τ)) with reliability more than twice as influential as frequency (β/α ≈ 2.6). For synthetic control, they optimize 2nd-order Markov transition matrices via Adam to satisfy specified local dependency, categoriality, and marginal distribution shape (Uniform, Gaussian, Zipfian), then train LMs on the generated sequences. The key finding is that local dependency plus high frequency and reliability is sufficient for IH formation, while categoriality and Zipfian marginal shape only modulate formation near the decision boundary.

## Results

The paper derives a predictive law for IH emergence: U_PT = T / (B^0.37 · C^0.62) with T = e^13.26 ≈ 574,000, achieving a correlation of r = .986 (p < .001) between predicted and observed emergence points across 3 orders of magnitude in model size (50M–7B) and 5 orders of magnitude in tokens per update (500–2M). A frequency–reliability decision boundary for IH formation is characterized by the fitted logistic model σ(k(α·log P_A + β·log P_B − τ)) with k=4.299, α=0.472, β=1.251, τ=−2.322 (MSE=0.019), yielding the functional form P_B = 0.156 × P_A^(−0.378); PS is more than twice as sensitive to reliability (β) as to frequency (α). In synthetic data experiments, local dependency coupled with high bigram repetition frequency and reliability is sufficient for IH formation, while categoriality and marginal distribution shape (Zipfian vs. uniform vs. Gaussian) modulate formation only near the decision boundary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical characterization study rather than a method-comparison paper, so no direct baseline methods are benchmarked against each other. The paper's findings extend and refine prior work in several ways: (1) it confirms and generalizes Aoyama & Wilcox (2025)'s observation that batch size shifts the phase transition point by adding context size as a second predictor and showing the combined effect is model-size-agnostic; (2) it contrasts with Zucchet et al. (2025), who find later emergence with larger context sizes in an associative-recall task, by arguing the next-token-prediction setup is dominated by increased bigram repetition rather than retrieval difficulty; (3) it provides a more precise, continuous characterization of what Chan et al. (2022) called 'burstiness' by decomposing it into frequency P(A,B,…,A) and reliability P(B|A,B,…,A), revealing that reliability is the dominant factor (β ≈ 2.6× α). The predictive law's r = .986 correlation and 5-fold cross-validation R²_test values of 0.76–0.93 (Table 5) demonstrate robustness across 3 random seeds and 35 model configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the induction head (IH) literature initiated by Olsson et al. (2022), which defined IHs and introduced the prefix-matching score and logit attribution metrics, by asking *when* IHs emerge during natural language pretraining rather than *what* they do. It builds directly on Aoyama & Wilcox (2025), who showed batch size shifts phase transition points, by adding context size and deriving a closed-form predictive law (token-weighted updates) that is agnostic to model size. The paper also departs from synthetic-task studies of ICL (Edelman et al., 2024; Zucchet et al., 2025) by grounding its analysis in next-token prediction on natural and semi-natural text, and it borrows the OLS-in-log-space fitting methodology from Kaplan et al. (2020) scaling-law work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The predictive law (N_PT = T·B^0.63·C^0.38, r=.986) gives practitioners a concrete, model-size-agnostic formula for anticipating IH emergence from training configuration alone, which has direct implications for dataset selection and training efficiency. The V-shaped relationship between emergence timing and final validation loss (reminiscent of the critical batch size hypothesis) suggests that naively accelerating IH formation does not improve outcomes, a non-obvious practical constraint. Open questions include whether the trend holds beyond 7B parameters, whether subword vs. orthographic tokenization shifts the frequency-reliability boundary, and whether the TWU-dependent emergence generalizes to other behavioral phase transitions (e.g., syntactic attention).
