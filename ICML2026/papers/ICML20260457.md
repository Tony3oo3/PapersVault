## Problem

Lyapunov-based neural control methods cannot certify stability in the presence of unstable equilibria or saddle points, and different controller/Lyapunov pairs cannot be smoothly blended to enlarge the region of attraction. While density functions offer a convex, blendable alternative stability certificate, no neural network parameterization existed that satisfies their intractable integrability constraint, and the effect of density-based blending on certified regions of attraction was formally uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The method takes a control-affine dynamical system (f, g) defined on a bounded state space S, a set of initial states X for sampling, and optionally a safety constraint h(x) defining the safe set C. It outputs a stabilizing controller u(x) = c(x)/a(x) paired with a density function ρ(x) = a(x)/e^(||x||²+||b(x)||²), verified by an SMT solver (dReal) to satisfy the divergence and integrability conditions. For safe-stable synthesis, the output additionally satisfies a CBF invariance condition, and for blending, the output is a weighted combination of multiple (ρᵢ, uᵢ) pairs whose certified RoA provably contains the union of individual RoAs.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel exponential parameterization ρ(x) = a(x)/e^(||x||²+||b(x)||²), ψ(x) = c(x)/e^(||x||²+||b(x)||²) using three neural networks (a with sigmoid activations for positivity, b with tanh and zero bias, c with tanh/linear), which provably satisfies the integrability condition required by Rantzer's density criterion (Proposition 5.1). Training follows a CEGIS loop: the networks minimize a density risk loss (penalizing negative ρ and non-positive divergence) via gradient descent, while a dReal SMT solver periodically searches for counterexamples violating the divergence or safety constraints, which are added to the training set until verification returns UNSAT. For blending, the paper proves (Theorem 4.4) that the a.e. RoA of the blended controller u* = Σ(ρᵢuᵢ)/Σρᵢ contains the union of all constituent RoAs, providing the first formal RoA guarantee for density-based blending. Safe-stable synthesis (Theorem 6.1) augments the divergence condition with a CBF inequality ρ(L_fh + α(h)) + L_gh·ψ ≥ 0, enabling joint certification of convergence and forward invariance of the safe set.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative results are region-of-attraction (RoA) certifications and learning times rather than standard accuracy metrics. On the inverted pendulum, NCDFs certify a.e. stability within ‖x‖₂ ≤ 6, and on path tracking within ‖xᵢ‖ ≤ 3. The key theoretical result (Theorem 4.4) proves that the a.e. RoA of a blended controller contains the union of the constituent controllers' RoAs, with m(Q⋆) ≥ m(∪ᵢ Qᵢ). Learning times range from 3m11s (path following, 74 parameters) to 21m24s (Caltech ducted fan, ~7,300 parameters), with the falsifier called every 100 iterations by default.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Yang et al. (2024, ICML), NCDFs yield strictly larger certified RoAs on the inverted pendulum and comparable RoAs on the path tracking task (Figure 1a–b). Against Chang et al. (2019, NeurIPS), NCDFs succeed in certifying a.e. stability on systems with multiple equilibria (e.g., the four-equilibrium system in Eq. 24 and the two-equilibrium system in Eq. 32) where Neural Lyapunov Functions provably fail (Figures 2, 7c–d). Against Prajna et al. (2004, IEEE TAC), the paper blends learned NCDFs with the published SoS-based controllers and densities, and Theorem 4.4 guarantees the blended RoA contains the union of both individual RoAs; empirically, Figure 8 confirms the blended controller achieves a strictly larger certified region than either the NCDF alone or the SoS solution alone. The paper does not report a single numerical metric where one method uniformly dominates; the advantage is in the *type* of certificate (a.e. stability vs. pointwise Lyapunov) and the ability to blend, which Lyapunov-based methods fundamentally cannot do.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CEGIS-based neural control synthesis framework (Chang et al., 2019) by replacing Lyapunov functions with density functions (Rantzer, 2001), which certify almost-everywhere stability and handle systems with multiple equilibria. The exponential parameterization (Eq. 18) is inspired by the SOS-based parametrization of (ρ,ψ) in Prajna et al. (2004), but is adapted to neural networks with a provable integrability guarantee (Proposition 5.1). The blending mechanism follows Rantzer & Ceragioli (2001), but the paper provides the first formal RoA guarantee for blended controllers (Theorem 4.4) and extends the framework to safe-stable synthesis by combining density functions with CBFs (Ames et al., 2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that density functions overcome two fundamental limitations of Lyapunov-based neural control: they can certify stability in the presence of unstable equilibria (where NLC provably fails), and they enable smooth blending of controllers with a guaranteed RoA superset. The exponential parameterization resolves the integrability constraint that previously made neural density functions intractable, making the approach practical. Limitations include the a.e. (not full) stability guarantee, the need to jointly optimize three networks, and the computational cost of SMT-based falsification. Open questions include scalability to higher-dimensional systems, handling the measure-zero exceptional set in practice, and whether the blending RoA guarantee tightens under additional structural assumptions.
