## Problem

Conventional wisdom in model-based RL holds that model accuracy and compounding errors are the primary obstacles to effective search. This paper challenges that view, showing that naively adding MPC to a state-of-the-art algorithm (MR.Q) actually degrades performance despite comparable model accuracy to TD-MPC2, which benefits from search. The real bottleneck is overestimation bias introduced by the distribution shift between search-selected actions and the policy used to train the value function.

## Input / Output

The agent receives raw environment state vectors (e.g., 17-dim for HalfCheetah, 376-dim for Humanoid) and outputs a single continuous action per time step, bounded to [-1,1] via tanh. Action selection is performed online via MPPI: 512 candidate action sequences of length 3 are sampled (24 from the policy network, 488 random), rolled forward through the learned dynamics model, scored using predicted rewards plus a terminal value estimate, and the first action of the value-weighted best trajectory is executed. The state is encoded into a 512-dim Simplicial Embedding (SEM) vector that feeds both the dynamics model and the policy network.

## Method

MRS.Q builds on MR.Q's state-action embedding architecture (which uses a model-based objective purely for representation learning) and adds three targeted modifications to enable effective search. First, it selects actions via MPPI with a 3-step horizon, following TD-MPC2's procedure. Second, and most critically, it replaces the standard minimum over 2 value functions with a minimum over an ensemble of 10 value functions, applied both in Bellman value targets and during MPC trajectory evaluation (Equation 3), producing pessimistic estimates that counteract the overestimation bias search introduces. Third, it removes exploration noise (since MPC already induces action variation), applies SEM to stabilize multi-step rollouts, and increases the terminal loss weight. The key insight is that aggressive ensemble minimization directly addresses the distribution shift between the MPC policy and the value function's training distribution, making search effective without requiring the policy network to imitate rapidly-shifting search-selected actions.

## Results

MRS.Q achieves state-of-the-art aggregate performance on 3 of 4 benchmarks at 1M environment steps: Gym (1.54, normalized to TD3), HumanoidBench No-Hand (0.59), and HumanoidBench Hand (0.58), outperforming all baselines. On DMC, MRS.Q scores 0.81, slightly behind BMPC (0.86) and MR.Q/SimbaV2 (0.84), but still competitive. Notably, naively adding MPC to MR.Q (MR.Q+MPC) degrades Gym performance from 1.46 to 0.67, while MRS.Q's ensemble-minimum strategy recovers and exceeds the original MR.Q score, demonstrating that overestimation mitigation—not model accuracy—is the key to unlocking search benefits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRS.Q outperforms all six baselines on Gym (1.54 vs. MR.Q 1.46, SimbaV2 1.44, TD-MPC2 0.41, TD-M(PC)² 0.62, BMPC 0.54, BOOM 0.61), with the largest margins over search-based methods that suffer from overestimation. On HumanoidBench (Hand), MRS.Q achieves 0.58, substantially exceeding TD-M(PC)² (0.44), BMPC (0.38), BOOM (0.23), TD-MPC2 (0.22), and SimbaV2 (0.18), confirming that aggressive ensemble minimization is critical in high-dimensional tasks with early termination. On HumanoidBench (No Hand), MRS.Q (0.59) edges out TD-MPC2 (0.58) and clearly beats MR.Q (0.48) and SimbaV2 (0.38). On DMC, MRS.Q (0.81) trails BMPC (0.86), MR.Q (0.84), and SimbaV2 (0.84) by a small margin, which the authors attribute to DMC's dense rewards and lack of early termination making overestimation less severe. The key contrast is that MR.Q+MPC (naive search) collapses to 0.67 on Gym, while MRS.Q's targeted overestimation fix not only recovers but surpasses the search-free MR.Q baseline, validating the paper's central thesis that mitigating overestimation bias—not improving model accuracy—is what enables effective search.

## Relation to Prior Work

MRS.Q builds directly on MR.Q (Fujimoto et al., 2025), a model-free algorithm that uses a model-based objective solely for representation learning, and grafts onto it the MPPI search procedure from TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2024). The key departure is from recent work (Lin et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025; Zhan et al., 2025) that addresses the distribution shift between search and value learning by constraining the policy network to imitate search-selected actions; instead, MRS.Q leaves the policy untouched and directly mitigates overestimation by taking the minimum over an ensemble of 10 value functions, extending the double-Q idea from TD3 (Fujimoto et al., 2018) to a much larger ensemble. The theoretical N-chain analysis and the empirical comparison of MR.Q vs. TD-MPC2 with/without MPC serve to isolate and challenge the field's dominant assumption that model accuracy is the primary determinant of search effectiveness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that search can actively degrade performance even with highly accurate dynamics models, the paper reframes the central bottleneck in MBRL from 'build a better model' to 'manage the distribution shift between search and value learning.' This has practical implications: practitioners can now enable search in existing model-free pipelines (like MR.Q) with minimal architectural changes, and the ensemble-minimization recipe is broadly applicable to other MPC-based methods (as shown by the TD-MPC2 ablation). Limitations include the substantial computational cost of 10 value functions plus MPC at every step, the inherited short 3-step planning horizon, and evaluation restricted to continuous control benchmarks; extending the analysis to discrete action spaces, longer horizons, and real-robot settings remains open.
