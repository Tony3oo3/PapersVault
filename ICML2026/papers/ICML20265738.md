## Problem

Existing ocean drifter placement campaigns rely on space-filling designs or ad-hoc expert heuristics, and no principled active learning method has been proposed for this setting. Standard active learning is fundamentally inadequate for Lagrangian observers because it evaluates utility only at the initial placement location, ignoring that drifters are continuously advected by the vector field and accumulate observations at many downstream locations and times.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265738/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes non-gridded Lagrangian velocity observations (position, time, 2D velocity vector) collected by previously deployed drifters, a regular spatial grid of candidate placement locations, and a temporal Helmholtz GP surrogate with its hyperparameters. It outputs a sequence of optimal 2D placement coordinates, one per decision time, with the constraint that only observations within the spatial domain are retained and observers are terminated upon leaving it. The key structural challenge is that observations are non-gridded while the surrogate must be sampled on a full spatio-temporal grid (typically 10^5+ points), creating a mismatch that standard GP or SPDE-GP inference handles poorly.

## Method

BALLAST amends the expected information gain acquisition by replacing the single hypothetical observation with the full projected trajectory of a placed drifter: it draws J posterior samples of the vector field, simulates each candidate's future path (and existing observers' paths) under each sample using an ODE solver, and averages the log-determinant utility over the aggregated observation sets. The computational bottleneck—posterior sampling over the full spatio-temporal grid—is solved by VaSE (Vanilla SPDE Exchange), which performs standard GP regression at the current time to obtain the posterior predictive over spatial test points, then propagates the sampled initial condition forward in time using the SPDE state-space model (exact Kalman filter transitions with Kronecker-product structure). This hybrid avoids the cubic-in-space cost of SPDE-GP (which must include non-gridded observation locations in its spatial Gram matrix) and the cubic-in-total-points cost of standard GP sampling, reducing per-sample cost from ~10^17 to ~10^8 operations. The reformulated EIG via mutual information symmetry further reduces per-candidate utility evaluation to O(N_obs^3) log-determinant computations.

## Results

On the synthetic temporal Helmholtz ground truth (20 drifters deployed over [0,10]), BALLAST-true and BALLAST-opt consistently achieve the best average policy rank across all deployment iterations, saving approximately 3 drifters (~16% deployment cost) relative to the uniform benchmark. On the high-fidelity SUNTANS dataset (10 drifters over [0,5]), both BALLAST variants save around 2 drifters (~22% cost saving) against the uniform baseline. Notably, standard EIG performs *worse* than the uniform policy in both settings, confirming the inadequacy of ignoring Lagrangian trajectory effects. The VaSE sampling method draws one posterior sample in under 4 seconds versus ~4.5 minutes for SPDE-GP (~65× speedup) on a consumer laptop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Sobol space-filling policy (representing Tukan et al., 2024), BALLAST-true consistently outperforms across all deployment iterations on both synthetic and SUNTANS settings, while BALLAST-opt is roughly comparable to Sobol on the synthetic setting but still superior on SUNTANS. Against the DIST-SEP heuristic (adapted from Chen et al., 2024b), BALLAST achieves a clear and consistent margin; DIST-SEP in fact underperforms even the standard EIG policy, which the authors attribute to its hand-crafted criteria being a poor proxy for information gain under a GP surrogate. The most striking comparison is that standard EIG (which ignores future Lagrangian trajectories) is consistently *worse* than the uniform random placement in both experiments, validating the paper's Proposition 1 that standard utility construction is suboptimal for Lagrangian observers. The advantage of BALLAST over all baselines grows as more observers are deployed, suggesting compounding benefits of trajectory-aware placement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the temporal Helmholtz GP surrogate of Ponte et al. (2024) and Berlinghieri et al. (2023) by coupling it with a principled active learning loop for sequential Lagrangian observer placement, a task previously addressed only by space-filling designs or hand-crafted heuristics (Chen et al., 2024b; Tukan et al., 2024). The key methodological departure is the introduction of trajectory look-ahead (BALLAST) to correct the well-known inadequacy of standard expected information gain for advected observers, and the development of VaSE, which hybridises vanilla GP regression with the SPDE state-space propagation of Sarkka et al. (2013) to sidestep the cubic-in-space cost that makes SPDE-GP impractical for non-gridded Lagrangian observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BALLAST provides the first information-theoretic framework for drifter deployment that accounts for the full future trajectory of placed observers, yielding 16–22% deployment cost savings over uniform baselines on both synthetic and SUNTANS high-fidelity fields. VaSE is of independent interest for any spatio-temporal GP problem with non-gridded observations and gridded prediction targets, offering orders-of-magnitude speedups over both standard and SPDE-GP sampling. Open questions include extending the framework to other surrogate models (variational GPs, deep adaptive designs), relaxing the pre-determined decision-time assumption, and validating the approach on real oceanographic deployment campaigns.
