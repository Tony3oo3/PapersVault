## Problem

Latent recursive reasoning in Tiny Recursive Models (TRMs) and Hierarchical Reasoning Models (HRMs) is typically explained as increasing effective depth, yet many recursive steps contribute no meaningful improvement—so-called 'dead compute.' The paper asks what algorithm is actually executing in the latent space during each recursion step and how to make every step a directed, non-redundant improvement rather than an unconstrained recurrent update.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260132/cropped_page9_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

For ARC-AGI, the model receives a sequence of 2–3 input-output demonstration grids plus 1–2 test grids (max 30×30, tokenized as discrete color IDs) and outputs the solved test grid; at test time it runs 1000 geometric/color augmentations with majority voting. For N-Queens, the input is an 8×8 board with Q pre-placed queens (sequence length 64, vocabulary size 3) and the output is a valid full placement. For Sudoku, the input is an 81-token sequence over digits 0–9 (0 = empty cell) and the output is the completed grid. The model uses a 2-layer Transformer backbone (D=512 medium, D=256 compact at 0.8M parameters) with N_sup=6 supervision steps and T=1, n=2 internal updates per step.

## Method

The paper formalizes a single TRM recursion step as a policy improvement operator: the pre-reasoning decode of z_H^t is the reference policy π̂_t, the post-reasoning decode of z_H^{t+1} is an optimality-conditioned policy π_t^+, and their log-ratio yields an implicit advantage signal A_t(s,a) = log π_t^+(a|s) − log π̂_t(a|s). Building on this, Deep Improvement Supervision (DIS) replaces TRM's single terminal cross-entropy with stepwise intermediate targets {y_s†} generated by a monotonically decreasing discrete corruption schedule (token masking/replacement) of the ground truth, so each supervision step s trains the post-reasoning logits toward y_s† via CE(ℓ_c^s, y_s†). This inductively anchors the pre-reasoning policy near the previous target and forces the log-ratio advantage to prefer the next correct refinement, converting long-horizon credit assignment into a sequence of supervised sub-goals. The architecture reuses TRM's single parameter-shared 2-layer Transformer (RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE) but eliminates the halting/ACT head and reduces the recursion budget from T=3, n=6 (336 total steps) to T=1, n=2 (18 total steps), cutting forward passes by 18× while achieving 41.3% pass@2 on ARC-AGI-1 and 24.0% with only 0.8M parameters.

## Results

On ARC-AGI-1 (pass@2), DIS-compact (0.8M params) achieves 24.0% accuracy, doubling TRM-compact's 12.0%, while DIS-medium (7M params) reaches 40.0% versus TRM-medium's 27.1%. The full DIS model (7M params, T=1, n=2, N_sup=6) attains 41.3% on ARC-AGI-1 and 6.0% on ARC-AGI-2, outperforming the original TRM (40.4% / 3.3%) while using 18× fewer forward passes (18 vs. 336 total steps) and no halting head. On N-Queens, DIS matches TRM's 0.69 accuracy with far fewer inference steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole direct baseline is the Tiny Recursive Model (TRM) in three configurations. At the compact scale (0.8M params), DIS-compact more than doubles TRM-compact on ARC-AGI-1 (24.0% vs. 12.0%), demonstrating that explicit stepwise supervision is critical when the model is too small to discover its own improvement curriculum. At the medium scale (7M params), DIS-medium achieves 40.0% on ARC-AGI-1 versus TRM-medium's 27.1%, a 12.9-point gap, while also being the first to score non-zero on ARC-AGI-2 (2.5% vs. 0.0%). The full DIS model (T=1, n=2, N_sup=6) edges out the original TRM (T=3, n=6, N_sup=16) on both benchmarks (41.3% vs. 40.4% on ARC-1; 6.0% vs. 3.3% on ARC-2) while reducing total forward passes from 336 to 18 (18×) and eliminating the halting/ACT module entirely. On N-Queens, both methods reach identical 0.69 accuracy, confirming that DIS's gains come from supervision structure rather than additional compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Tiny Recursive Models (TRM) and Hierarchical Reasoning Models (HRM), which reuse a small parameter-shared network across recursive latent steps to emulate deep computation. It departs from the dominant 'effective depth' narrative for looped transformers by reframing each latent-reasoning step as a policy improvement operator, drawing on the recent connection between classifier-free guidance and RL policy improvement (Frans et al., 2025). The proposed Deep Improvement Supervision (DIS) training method borrows the discrete diffusion corruption schedule from Ho & Salimans (2022) to generate stepwise intermediate targets, converting the terminal-supervision problem into a sequence of supervised sub-goals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The policy-improvement formalization provides a concrete, testable criterion (the Advantage Margin condition) for diagnosing when a latent-reasoning step is productive versus 'dead compute,' which has been a persistent ambiguity in looped-transformer research. Practically, DIS reduces total forward passes by 18× and eliminates the halting/ACT module, simplifying training while matching or exceeding TRM on ARC-AGI-1. However, the Sudoku experiment reveals that DIS does not universally outperform TRM, and ARC-AGI-2 scores remain very low (≤6%), suggesting the approach may be sensitive to task structure and that adaptive step allocation or task-specific target generators remain important open questions.
