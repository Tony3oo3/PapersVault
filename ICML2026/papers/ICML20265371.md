## Problem

Existing dehazing methods fail to jointly restore both scene radiance and polarization in hazy environments: single-image methods treat polarization channels independently and ignore inter-channel physical constraints, while polarization-based methods rely on the restrictive assumption that airlight and scene AoLP are identical, making true scene AoLP recovery theoretically impossible and propagating errors into DoLP and radiance estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265371/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 4-channel DoFP polarimetric image (intensities at 0°, 45°, 90°, 135°) and appends two derived rotation-invariant channels (I_max and I_min computed from the Stokes vector), forming a 6-channel input. It produces a haze-free radiance image (S0), a DoLP map, and an AoLP map, with the polarimetric outputs represented as a complex phasor C = P·e^(j2θ) that is decomposed into real and imaginary parts for loss computation. All processing operates at full resolution on 512×512 patches during training.

## Method

DuRP embeds two generalized physical models—the Polarization Reconstruction Model (PRM) and Radiance Reconstruction Model (RRM)—as differentiable operators within a dual-stage U-Net architecture with self-attention. The key insight is representing the DoLP/AoLP coupling as a complex phasor (C = P·e^(j2θ)), which linearizes the non-linear scattering-induced coupling into a simple vector addition (I·C_I = A·C_A + D·C_D), relaxing the traditional invariant-AoLP assumption. In the P-Stage, PT-Net and AP-Net estimate the polarization transmission K and airlight phasor C_A, which are fed through the differentiable PRM to recover scene polarization C_D, refined by SPR-Net; in the R-Stage, AL-Net estimates atmospheric light A_∞ and the RRM restores radiance R, refined by SRR-Net. Training follows a three-stage protocol: independent P-Stage and R-Stage pretraining (400 epochs each, Adam, cosine annealing), followed by end-to-end joint fine-tuning at a reduced learning rate to resolve cross-branch coupling errors.

## Results

On the SRPG dataset, DuRP achieves the best PSNR across all three signal types: 33.70 dB for radiance (S₀), 28.80 dB for DoLP, and 19.43 dB for AoLP. The most striking gains are in polarimetric recovery, where DuRP outperforms the next-best method (Selfpromer) by ~3.3 dB in DoLP PSNR and ~2.8 dB in AoLP PSNR. On radiance SSIM, DuRP (0.9480) is marginally behind SGDN (0.9510) but leads on all other metrics. The method also achieves 52.6 FPS with FP16+torch.compile acceleration on an RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DuRP outperforms all eight baselines on every PSNR metric (S₀, DoLP, AoLP) and on all DoLP/AoLP SSIM metrics. The largest margins are in polarimetric recovery: DuRP leads Selfpromer (the strongest single-image dehazing baseline) by 3.29 dB in DoLP PSNR and 2.80 dB in AoLP PSNR, and leads PD-Net (the strongest polarization-specific baseline) by 3.69 dB in DoLP PSNR and 6.89 dB in AoLP PSNR. On radiance restoration, DuRP's 33.70 dB PSNR exceeds SGDN (32.20 dB) and Deformer (32.18 dB) by ~1.5 dB, though SGDN edges out DuRP on SSIM(S₀) by 0.003. The polarization-based baselines (PD-Net, PLC) suffer severe AoLP degradation (PSNR ≤ 13.7 dB) due to their assumption of invariant AoLP between scene and airlight, while single-image methods (SLP, LFP) fail to maintain cross-channel physical consistency, yielding DoLP PSNR below 19 dB. The ~3 dB gap in DoLP and ~2.5–3 dB gap in AoLP over the best non-physics baselines demonstrate that the generalized phasor model and dual-stage architecture are critical for physically consistent joint restoration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the polarization-based dehazing line initiated by Zhou et al. (PD-Net, NeurIPS 2021) and Yang et al. (IEEE TMM 2025), both of which assume the airlight AoLP is identical to the scene AoLP. DuRP departs from this by deriving a generalized phasor model (Eq. 16) that relaxes the invariant-AoLP assumption, reformulating the non-linear Stokes coupling as a linear complex-domain mixture. Architecturally, it introduces a dual-stage cascade (polarization reconstruction followed by radiance reconstruction) with differentiable physics operators embedded as inductive biases, rather than treating polarization as an auxiliary cue for radiance enhancement as prior polarization-based methods do. The SRPG dataset is also introduced to address the unrealistic pseudo-polarization labels in existing benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Accurate joint recovery of radiance and polarization is critical for downstream polarization-based vision tasks (vehicle detection, shape-from-polarization) in scattering environments, and this paper demonstrates that physics-embedded restoration yields a ~3 dB advantage in polarimetric fidelity over both single-image and prior polarization-based methods. However, the approach is fundamentally tied to the atmospheric scattering model and degrades under extreme fog where scene polarization signals vanish. Open questions include whether the phasor formulation generalizes to other scattering media (underwater, indoor particulate), whether real-time deployment on edge hardware is achievable, and whether the SRPG synthesis pipeline can be validated against large-scale real-world paired data.
