## Problem

Auto-regressive video diffusion models accumulate a KV-cache that grows linearly with generation length, quickly dominating GPU memory (e.g., ~34 GB for a 5-second 480p clip on LongCat-Video) and blocking deployment on consumer hardware. Beyond the efficiency bottleneck, memory-bounded KV budgets force small context windows that directly degrade long-horizon consistency in identity, layout, and motion. Existing LLM-oriented KV-cache quantization methods fail on video models because video KV-cache exhibits far more heterogeneous numeric distributions across both token and channel dimensions.

## Input / Output

QVG operates on the BF16 KV-cache tensor of an auto-regressive video diffusion model, processing it in fixed-size chunks (e.g., 37,440 tokens per chunk for 24 frames at 480p). The output is a quantized INT2 or INT4 representation augmented with per-chunk metadata: k-means centroids (stored in BF16), uint8 assignment vectors, and FP8 E4M3 per-group scaling factors. During inference, a fused dequantization kernel reconstructs the KV-cache on-the-fly for attention computation, so the model still receives a full-precision tensor while only the compressed form resides in GPU memory.

## Method

QVG is a training-free, two-stage KV-cache quantization framework. First, Semantic-Aware Smoothing applies k-means clustering (K=256) on KV-cache tokens along the sequence dimension to group spatiotemporally similar tokens, then subtracts each group's centroid to produce low-magnitude residuals that are far more amenable to low-bit quantization (reducing key-cache quantization error by ~6.9×). Second, Progressive Residual Quantization iteratively re-applies Semantic-Aware Smoothing to the residual tensor across T stages (T=4 for QVG-Pro), progressively capturing information from coarse semantic structure to fine-grained variation in a manner analogous to multi-scale video codecs. The system uses pre-RoPE key caching, streaming chunk-wise compression to avoid re-compression drift, and a fused CUDA/Triton dequantization kernel that replays all stages in registers, yielding up to 7.0× KV-cache compression with <4% end-to-end latency overhead.

## Results

On LongCat-Video-13B (INT2, 480p), QVG achieves 28.72 PSNR at 6.94× KV-cache compression, while QVG-Pro reaches 30.38 PSNR at 4.97× compression. On HY-WorldPlay-8B (INT2, 480p), QVG attains 29.17 PSNR at 7.05× compression and QVG-Pro reaches 31.56 PSNR at 5.20× compression. End-to-end latency overhead is minimal: 2.1% on LongCat, 1.5% on HY-World, and 4.3% on Self-Forcing. QVG also enables running HY-WorldPlay-8B on a single RTX 4090 for the first time, achieving PSNR > 29 relative to the BF16 reference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QVG substantially outperforms both KIVI and QuaRot across all models and bit-widths. Under the most challenging INT2 setting on LongCat-Video-13B, QVG achieves 28.72 PSNR versus 20.32 (KIVI) and 21.57 (QuaRot) at the same 6.40× compression ratio — a gap of 7.1–8.4 dB. On HY-WorldPlay-8B (INT2), QVG reaches 29.17 PSNR at 7.05× compression, surpassing KIVI (24.27) by 4.9 dB and QuaRot (25.21) by 4.0 dB. The advantage is even more pronounced on VBench perceptual metrics: QVG's Subject Consistency (94.11) and Background Consistency (95.06) on LongCat-Video INT2 are within 1 point of the BF16 reference (95.51, 96.22), while KIVI and QuaRot drop by 15–25 points. Under INT4, all methods perform closer together, but QVG-Pro still leads with 37.10 PSNR vs. QuaRot's 33.74 on LongCat-Video. The paper also notes that QVG enables running HY-WorldPlay-8B on a single RTX 4090 — previously infeasible due to memory constraints — while maintaining PSNR > 29.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied KV-cache quantization literature from LLMs (KIVI, KVQuant, QuaRot, RotateKV) to auto-regressive video diffusion models, where the authors show that LLM-oriented assumptions break down due to substantially more heterogeneous activation statistics across both token and channel dimensions. The key methodological departure is exploiting video-specific spatiotemporal redundancy via k-means-based Semantic-Aware Smoothing and a Progressive Residual Quantization scheme inspired by multi-scale video codecs, rather than the rotation or outlier-handling strategies that dominate LLM KV quantization. The work also builds on the auto-regressive video generation paradigm (CausVid, Self-Forcing, LongCat-Video, HY-WorldPlay) and prior observations of spatiotemporal token similarity (Sparse VideoGen, Sparse VideoGen2), but reframes this redundancy as a quantization enabler rather than an attention-sparsity signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV-cache memory is simultaneously an efficiency bottleneck (blocking deployment on consumer GPUs) and a capability bottleneck (forcing short context windows that degrade long-horizon consistency), making this a system-algorithm co-design problem rather than a pure compression exercise. The practical impact is significant: QVG enables HY-WorldPlay-8B to run on a single RTX 4090 for the first time and allows substantially longer effective context that can surpass BF16 quality under the original memory budget. Open questions include whether the k-means clustering overhead remains negligible at much larger resolutions or longer horizons, whether training-based alternatives (e.g., learned codebooks) could push bit-widths even lower, and whether the approach transfers to other autoregressive modalities (audio, 3D) with similar spatiotemporal structure.
