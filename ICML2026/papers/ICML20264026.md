## Problem

The standard paradigm in geometric deep learning designs equivariant networks layer by layer, but it remains unclear whether end-to-end equivariance (the overall function respecting group symmetries) can be achieved without layerwise equivariant constraints. This paper addresses the fundamental question of whether any end-to-end equivariant deep network must necessarily be layerwise equivariant, and provides a mathematical explanation for the empirically observed emergence of equivariant structures in the weights of networks trained on symmetric data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264026/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Theoretically, the framework takes a deep model (MLP or multi-head attention) with a group G acting on the input and output spaces, along with a parameter θ that is weakly identifiable, and produces explicit group actions of G on each intermediate latent space V_i that make every layer equivariant. Empirically, the method consumes 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10 images (resized to 14×14) and a symmetry (left-right mirroring), and outputs per-layer equivariance error metrics (relative L2 error between f_i(mirror[x]) and A_i·f_i(x) estimated via least squares on 100k noise samples) plus qualitative visualizations of first-layer filters and attention head patterns.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 4.1) proves that for a weakly identifiable parameter θ of a G-equivariant model, there exist group actions of G on all latent spaces V_i (i=1,…,L−1) such that every layer f_i is G-equivariant at θ_i. The proof constructs a modified parameter θ′ by applying the group action g to the first and last layer parameters (via the adjunction property), then uses identifiability to show the unique sequence of latent-space symmetries relating θ and θ′ forms a group homomorphism ρ_i: G → K_i for each layer, thereby inducing a G-action on V_i. The theory is formulated in an architecture-agnostic abstract language (models as sequential compositions of parametric maps, symmetries as group actions, submodels via commutative diagrams), making it applicable to MLPs, attention networks, and other architectures. Empirically, small MLPs (depth 4, widths 64→256→256→256→10) and a multi-head attention autoencoder are trained on CIFAR-10 with an added equivariance loss, confirming that Tanh networks exhibit signed-permutation equivariance in their first layers while GELU networks show degenerate behavior due to the identity σ(x)−σ(−x)=x.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, proving that weakly identifiable equivariant networks must be layerwise equivariant. The illustrative experiments on CIFAR-10 report relative equivariance errors for learned layers: Tanh-activated MLPs achieve very low left-right mirroring equivariance errors (0.029 for f₁ autoencoder, 0.022 for f₂ autoencoder), while GELU-activated MLPs show substantially higher errors (0.40 for f₁, 0.11 for f₂), consistent with the theory's prediction that GELU's identity σ(x)−σ(−x)=x breaks identifiability. Up-down mirroring errors are uniformly higher (0.13–0.56) since networks are not trained for that symmetry. A multi-head attention autoencoder qualitatively shows that mirror equivariance emerges as a permutation over attention heads.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no formal method baselines; the experiments serve as illustrative validation of the main theorem rather than a comparison of competing approaches. The closest comparison is between Tanh and GELU activations, which proxy for the identifiability assumption: Tanh networks (whose intertwiner group is signed permutations) achieve equivariance errors roughly 4–14× lower than GELU networks on left-right mirroring, directly supporting the prediction that identifiability enforces layerwise equivariance. The GELU autoencoder's high f₁ error (0.40) but lower f₂ error (0.11) is explained by the identity σ(x)−σ(−x)=x requiring two linear layers to bypass the nonlinearity. No head-to-head comparison with prior equivariant network architectures (e.g., CNNs, equivariant transformers) is performed, as the paper's contribution is a structural theorem rather than a new architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes and unifies prior results on equivariant structures in specific network architectures (Agrawal & Ostrowski, 2022, for shallow ReLU MLPs; Marchetti et al., 2024, for the first layer of models with neuron-wise scaling symmetries) into a single architecture-agnostic theorem. The proof strategy reduces the equivariance question to parameter identifiability, drawing on the neuroalgebraic geometry and categorical deep learning literatures for the abstract formalism. Empirically, it connects to the body of work showing that trained networks approximately encode symmetries in their latent spaces (Lenc & Vedaldi, 2015; Gruver et al., 2023; Bökman & Kahl, 2023), providing the first general theoretical explanation for this phenomenon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result answers a major open question in geometric deep learning: end-to-end equivariance cannot be achieved without layerwise equivariance (under identifiability), meaning that designing equivariant layers is the only viable path to constructing equivariant networks. This provides a mathematical foundation for the empirical observation that equivariant structures emerge in trained networks, and clarifies the relationship between equivariance and symmetry-based data augmentation. Key open questions remain: identifiability for ReLU networks is unresolved, skip connections are not covered, the result is existential (it does not explain how equivariant parametrizations emerge during optimization), and it does not indicate whether a priori equivariant constraints outperform learning equivariance from data.
