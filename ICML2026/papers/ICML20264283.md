## Problem

The 'intention expression gap' — the difficulty of conveying complex, high-dimensional mental models to AI — forces users into inefficient trial-and-error loops when collaborating with passive, instruction-following LLMs. Existing clarification approaches address disambiguation of what the user is asking but not the broader problem of proactively discovering what has been left out in a divergent, creative construction task. This paper reframes the problem from passive instruction following to active Socratic collaboration, where the AI strategically asks questions to resolve its own uncertainty about user intent.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a generic initial request (e.g., 'I want to create a scientific diagram') and then engages in multi-turn dialogue, posing clarifying questions and receiving natural language responses from the user or a simulated Oracle. The output is a consolidated natural language specification covering N discrete attributes (layout, color palette, component types and counts, interconnections), which is then passed to a text-to-image model (4o-image-1, nano-banana) or the VisPainter vector-drawing framework to produce the final diagram. The specification space is a high-dimensional discrete state space with over 35,000 possible combinations, and the agent maintains a factorized probability distribution (belief state) over these attributes throughout the dialogue.

## Method

The core contribution is an information-theoretic training framework where the per-turn reward is the reduction in Shannon entropy of the agent's belief state over a structured diagram specification space, providing an intrinsic, computationally tractable signal that eliminates the need for human preference annotations or external reward models. Under a conditional independence assumption over N attributes, the reward simplifies to the sum of prior entropies of newly resolved attributes, and a few-shot prompted LLM serves as a semantic parser mapping user answers to hard constraints for reliable Bayesian belief updates. Training uses an offline adaptation of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO): an automated simulation pipeline with an Oracle agent holding ground-truth specifications generates large-scale preference-ranked inquiry data, and the policy (Qwen3-8B, full-parameter fine-tuning) is optimized via a PPO-style clipped surrogate objective with KL regularization against a reference policy. This offline design avoids the computational overhead of online sampling while maintaining stable convergence, requiring only 28.5 GPU-hours compared to 93 hours for the online variant.

## Results

On the scientific diagram generation task, Nous (OfG) completes dialogues in 20.3±2.60 turns while achieving the highest cumulative information gain of 120.5±20.11 bits, outperforming all prompt-based baselines (GPT-few: 93.5, Qwen-few: 90.5) by 27–30% in total IG. In subjective pairwise evaluations (400 judgments per pair), Nous (OfG) achieves a 0.68 win rate against human judges using 4o-image-1 rendering, compared to 0.45 for GPT-few and 0.23 for Qwen-zero. On the VisPainter objective benchmark, Nous (OfG) scores 0.76 overall (Precision 0.83, Recall 0.84), versus 0.65 for GPT-few and 0.57 for GPT-zero. Training cost is 28.5 GPU-hours for OfG versus 93 hours for online GRPO, confirming the efficiency of the offline approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Socratic-prompted LLMs (Chang, 2023), Nous (OfG) achieves 27–30% higher total information gain (120.5 vs. 93.5 for GPT-few, 90.5 for Qwen-few) while requiring fewer turns than GPT-based baselines (20.3 vs. 22.6/26.5). In subjective win-rate evaluations, Nous (OfG) wins 68% of pairwise comparisons against human judges (4o-image-1), compared to 45% for GPT-few and 23% for Qwen-zero, a gap of 23–45 percentage points. Against UoT (Hu et al., NeurIPS 2024), Nous (OfG) requires roughly half the dialogue turns (17.3 vs. 33.6) and achieves 3.3× higher total information gain (126.44 vs. 38.38) in the zero-start setting. Against CQ-Gen (Lee et al., EMNLP 2023), Nous (OfG) achieves 13× higher total IG (126.44 vs. 9.72) despite using more turns (17.3 vs. 7.1), revealing that CQ-Gen's brevity comes at the cost of collecting almost no structural information. On the VisPainter objective benchmark, Nous (OfG) leads all baselines in Precision (0.83 vs. 0.63–0.67) and Recall (0.84 vs. 0.73–0.78), confirming that the information-theoretic reward produces more complete and accurate diagram specifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Socratic prompting paradigm (Chang, 2023) from a template-based heuristic into a trainable policy, and adapts the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) objective from DeepSeekMath to an offline setting for dialogue. Conceptually, it departs from traditional task-oriented dialogue's 'convergent retrieval' (slot-filling against a fixed database) toward 'divergent construction' in a high-dimensional creative specification space, and from external reward models or human preference annotations toward an intrinsic reward derived from Shannon entropy reduction over a structured attribute space. The information-theoretic reward connects to active learning and optimal experiment design literature (entropy reduction, mutual information as question quality), but the key departure is using it as a real-time training signal for a generative LLM policy rather than a post-hoc selection heuristic over a fixed candidate set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, annotation-free training signal for proactive dialogue agents, which could reduce the cost barrier for deploying clarification-based AI in structured creative tasks. However, the attribute independence assumption limits fidelity in domains with strong inter-attribute correlations, and the framework requires manual specification of the attribute space, limiting immediate generalization. Open questions include whether the task space can be learned dynamically from interaction, how to support mixed-initiative dialogue where users proactively contribute information, and whether the qualitative leap observed in scientific diagrams (where LLM priors are weak) translates to domains where LLMs already have strong generative priors (where gains are incremental).
