## Problem

Advanced activation functions like SiLU, GELU, and tanh rely on exponentiation and division, which lack hardware fast-paths on edge devices, making them 13–26× slower than ReLU on embedded platforms. This forces edge ML to default to ReLU, sacrificing model accuracy. Existing mitigations either require custom silicon (e.g., Flex-SFU adds 5.9% chip area) or are manually crafted per-activation, limiting generality and hindering exploration of new AF designs.

## Input / Output

FFCC accepts an activation function expressed in a domain-specific language using basic floating-point operators (add, mul, div, exp, log, etc.) and produces a hardware-efficient approximation as a PyTorch nn.Module, NumPy expression, or LLVM-IR. The generated code replaces expensive transcendental calls with a single multiply-accumulate followed by a float-to-int bit-cast and an int-to-float reinterpretation, plus a final division, requiring no special hardware support. Constants in the generated code are annotated as tunable and are optimized before emission.

## Method

FFCC is a hardware-aware compiler that automatically lowers exact activation expressions into fast bit-cast approximations through a four-stage pipeline: (1) normalization rewrites the expression into base-2 log/exp form via algebraic identities and aggressive constant folding; (2) peephole rewrites replace 2^A with fp(L(B−ε+A)) and log₂(A) with int(A)/L−B+ε, exploiting the fact that IEEE 754 bit patterns reinterpreted as integers approximate log₂ and the reverse approximates 2^x; (3) a gradient-descent-based constant tuning phase (using PyTorch autograd with the piecewise-affine derivatives of the bit-cast operators) minimizes mean-squared error holistically across all tunable constants in the full expression; (4) lowering emits target-specific code. The key insight is that the float↔int reinterpretation provides a free piecewise-linear approximation of log₂ and 2^x at the cost of one multiply-accumulate and one type-cast, eliminating the need for hardware exponentiation or division fast-paths while maintaining accuracy within 0.019 absolute error of the exact function.

## Results

FFCC-generated approximations achieve 13–26× speedups over exact activation implementations on Arm M7, Aarch64, and Intel platforms, reducing SiLU/GELU/tanh overhead from ~130 cycles to <24 cycles on Cortex-M7 (15–16% of original cost). In post-training accuracy tests across 8 benchmarks, FastSiLU preserves model accuracy in 7 of 8 cases with no observable drop versus exact SiLU, while Hardswish consistently underperforms by 0.5–5.6 pp. On ConvNeXt, replacing GELU with FastGELU yields only 0.1–0.2 pp Top-1 accuracy loss while delivering 1.2×–1.37× end-to-end speedup on the Femto model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Hardswish (H-Swish) from MobileNetV3 (Howard et al., 2019), the standard lightweight activation used in edge-deployed models. FastSiLU matches or exceeds exact SiLU accuracy in 7 of 8 benchmarks (e.g., 74.0% vs 74.0% on DenseNet-121, 69.5% vs 69.1% on MobileNetV2), while Hardswish trails by 0.5–5.6 pp across the same models (e.g., 72.1% vs 74.0% on DenseNet-121, 78.24% vs 83.84% on MLPerf Image Classification). In terms of speed, FFCC's approximations are 13–26× faster than exact SiLU/GELU/tanh and 23× faster than the hand-tuned inline-assembly SiLU shipped in llama.cpp's ggml library. The sole failure case is the MLPerf Tiny Anomaly Detection auto-encoder, where FastSiLU produces NaN due to activations exceeding the numerically safe range (|x| > 74), a limitation the authors note can be mitigated with range guards. Overall, FFCC dominates Hardswish on both accuracy and speed, and matches exact SiLU accuracy while being an order of magnitude faster on edge hardware.

## Relation to Prior Work

FFCC extends the long-known bit-casting trick from graphics programming (Quake III's fast inverse square root, Lomont 2003; Kadlec 2010) into the machine-learning domain, where prior work has applied it only manually and in isolation (Schraudolph 1999 for exp, Qiumei et al. 2019 for ELU). The key departure is the compiler-based automation: rather than hand-designing each approximation, FFCC normalizes arbitrary activation expressions into base-2 log/exp form, applies peephole rewrites, and performs holistic constant tuning (following Kadlec's finding that isolated tuning underperforms by ~2.7×). This positions FFCC between the rough Hardswish approximation (Howard et al., 2019) and hardware-specific accelerators like Flex-SFU (Reggiani et al., 2025), offering a software-only, architecture-agnostic middle ground.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing SiLU/GELU/tanh to a single multiply-accumulate plus a bit-cast, FFCC removes the primary barrier to deploying modern activations on resource-constrained MCUs and mobile SoCs without custom silicon, directly addressing the accuracy-efficiency dilemma that has kept edge models stuck on ReLU. The demonstrated ≤0.2 pp accuracy loss on ConvNeXt and near-zero loss on 7/8 post-training benchmarks makes this a practical drop-in for existing trained models. Open questions include range-safe code generation (the Anomaly Detection model's nan failure outside ±74), systematic handling of activations with wider numerical ranges, and whether the approach generalizes to quantized or mixed-precision inference where bit-layout assumptions break down.
