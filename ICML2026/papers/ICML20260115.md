## Problem

There is no established methodology for reliably attributing goals to agentic systems, and purely behavioural evaluation faces fundamental limitations: capability confounders can mask goal-directedness, and misaligned internal objectives can produce aligned surface behaviour. This paper proposes a white-box framework that integrates behavioural assessment with interpretability-based probing of internal representations to holistically evaluate goal-directedness as an interaction of beliefs, planning, and action selection.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a fully observable grid world serialized as text with exactly one token per cell (to avoid tokenisation artefacts), and the framework extracts residual-stream activations from GPT-OSS-20B at layers 7, 15, and 23 at specific chat-template token positions (pre- and post-reasoning boundaries). Outputs include decoded cognitive maps (per-cell type classification across the grid), one-shot multi-step action plans (up to 10 steps decoded simultaneously, non-autoregressively), and behavioural metrics (per-action accuracy, JSD from the A*-derived optimal policy, goal success rate) computed across grid sizes 7–15 and obstacle densities 0.0–1.0.

## Method

The framework has two complementary arms. Behaviourally, the agent's policy is compared against an A* optimal policy across varying grid sizes and densities, then stress-tested with iso-difficulty transformations (reflection, rotation, start-goal swap, transposition) and multi-goal environments (instrumental key-door, implicit key distractors) to measure robustness and bias. Representationally, MLP and linear probes trained on augmented activations (concatenated with cell coordinates) decode cognitive maps, while a one-shot Transformer decoder (1–4 layers, 8 heads, shared 1024-dim bottleneck) decodes full 10-step action plans from three fixed token activations without autoregressive conditioning—ensuring that recovered plan structure must originate in the model's representations rather than probe-side computation. A key finding is that reasoning reorganises representations: pre-reasoning activations preserve coarse spatial maps and longer-horizon plans, while post-reasoning activations sharpen focus on next-action selection, with a measurable crossover in plan decodability at prefix length N=5.

## Results

Behaviourally, GPT-OSS-20B achieves 100% success rate on instrumental-goal grids (KeyDoorEnv) and 98.9% on implicit-goal grids (KeyNoDoorEnv), with per-action accuracy of 98.7% and 97.2% respectively. Representationally, an MLP probe decodes the agent's cognitive map at ~70% overall accuracy (peak 75.7% on 11×11 grids), substantially outperforming a linear probe (39.1%), confirming non-linear encoding of spatial information. When evaluating the agent's actions against the optimal policy derived from its own decoded cognitive map, accuracy averages 82.5% and the recovery rate (actions suboptimal in ground truth but optimal in the decoded map) averages 57.9%, indicating that a large fraction of behavioural failures stem from imprecise internal world representations rather than absent goal-directedness. For plan decoding, the 2-layer Transformer probe achieves 66.49% one-step accuracy on post-reasoning activations (vs. 41.5% pre-reasoning), with pre-reasoning activations retaining stronger longer-horizon structure (7.3% at N=5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an evaluation and analysis paper rather than a method-comparison study, so it does not benchmark against external baselines. The key internal comparisons are: (1) MLP vs. linear probes for cognitive map decoding, where the MLP achieves 75.7% accuracy versus 39.1% for the linear probe on 11×11 grids, establishing that environment information is encoded non-linearly. (2) Pre-reasoning vs. post-reasoning activations: post-reasoning improves one-step plan decoding (66.49% vs. 41.5%) but degrades cognitive map accuracy (60% vs. 75%), revealing a representational reorganisation during reasoning. (3) Top-1 vs. top-k cognitive map decoding: accounting for representational uncertainty via top-k (k∈{3,5,10}) consistently raises action accuracy above both the top-1 decoded grid and the ground-truth grid, showing that the agent plans under a distribution over plausible states. (4) Decoder capacity ablation (1-, 2-, 4-layer Transformers): performance is non-monotonic in capacity, with the 2-layer probe outperforming the 4-layer probe post-reasoning, arguing against the decoder itself solving the navigation task. (5) Iso-difficulty transformations (reflection, rotation, start-goal swap, transposition) show no statistically significant behavioural differences (all p-values > 0.17, Wilcoxon signed-rank test), confirming robustness to goal-irrelevant environmental variations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of goal-directedness measurement (Everitt et al. 2025; MacDermott et al. 2024; Xu & Rivera 2024), which has been almost exclusively behavioural, and the LLM interpretability/probing literature (Li et al. 2023; Nanda et al. 2023; Gurnee & Tegmark 2024; Pal et al. 2023), which has decoded spatial and planning representations but not in the context of agentic goal attribution. The key departure is the explicit integration of both: using activation probes to recover internal cognitive maps and multi-step plans, then evaluating whether the agent's actions are coherent with those decoded beliefs rather than only with ground-truth optimality. This reframes goal-directedness as a property of the interaction between internal representations and action selection, rather than a purely behavioural statistic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a recognised gap: behavioural evaluation alone cannot distinguish capability limitations from genuine absence of goal-directedness, and a misaligned agent could pass behavioural tests while harbouring different internal objectives. The finding that goal-like but task-irrelevant artefacts (keys) systematically bias navigation even when functionally useless has direct implications for AI safety monitoring. However, the study is limited to a single 20B model in a simple fully-observable grid world, activation patching failed to establish causal links, and partial observability proved intractable for current LLMs—leaving open whether these representational signatures generalise across architectures, scales, and more complex environments.
