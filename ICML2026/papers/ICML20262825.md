## Problem

Existing MLLMs that incorporate 3D geometry either ignore it entirely or rigidly fuse geometric features into every input regardless of task demands. This forced activation degrades general reasoning (e.g., OCR, math) while the model lacks any internal mechanism to recognize when its 2D perception is insufficient for spatial tasks — a gap the authors term the 'perception information gap.'
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262825/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a standard RGB image and a text query, processed through Qwen2.5-VL-3B's 2D visual encoder. Internally, the model decides whether to emit a <vggt> trigger token; if triggered, VGGT-1B extracts 3D geometric features from the same image, which are projected via a learnable linear layer and inserted as an independent token segment (delimited by boundary tokens) for a second inference pass. The final output is a text answer, optionally preceded by a Chain-of-Thought that explicitly states the need for geometric integration before the trigger fires.

## Method

GeoSense introduces an independent geometry input channel (rather than element-wise addition to 2D features) so that 3D tokens serve as an on-demand modality without polluting the 2D visual stream. Training proceeds in two stages: first, geometry alignment projects frozen VGGT and 2D encoder features into a shared embedding space via dedicated MLP projectors while training the LLM backbone and boundary tokens; second, spatial-aware SFT teaches the model to autonomously emit or suppress the <vggt> trigger based on task necessity. The training data is curated via dual-condition inference (with/without geometry) on ~700k samples, partitioned into correctness quadrants: T-F samples are reformulated into two-turn dialogues ending with the trigger (Strategy A), while F-T samples train the model to suppress geometry (Strategy B), yielding 117k task-aware samples. At inference, geometry is activated for only ~36% of spatial samples and ~3% of general samples, amortizing the 3D encoder cost and avoiding the cross-modal interference observed in always-on fusion baselines.

## Results

GeoSense achieves a spatial reasoning average of 56.6, outperforming all baselines including the larger Qwen2.5-VL-7B (50.5) and specialized spatial models like SpatialLadder-3B (48.5) and VG-LLM (49.7). On the overall average (spatial + general), GeoSense scores 55.9, ranking first among all compared models. Notably, on BLINK it reaches 68.5 (vs. 33.1 for the Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline) and on VSI-Bench it scores 54.9 (vs. 27.0 baseline), while maintaining competitive general visual performance (MMBench 75.9, WeMath 41.2). The adaptive geometry trigger activates for only ~36% of spatial samples and ~3% of general samples, yielding an amortized inference latency of 895 ms/sample versus 950 ms for always-on rigid fusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoSense outperforms all spatial-specialized baselines on the spatial average: it leads VG-LLM (56.6 vs. 49.7) by 6.9 points, SpatialLadder-3B (48.5) by 8.1 points, and Cambrian-S-3B (49.6) by 7.0 points. Against the same-scale Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline, GeoSense gains +13.2 on spatial average and +1.9 on general average. Critically, when compared under identical 940k training data (marked *), GeoSense beats VG-LLM* by 7.8 spatial points (56.6 vs. 48.8) and 7.0 general points (55.2 vs. 48.2), confirming the gains come from the adaptive mechanism rather than data volume. On general benchmarks, GeoSense (55.2) trails the larger Qwen2.5-VL-7B (57.8) and SpaceR-sft-7B (58.9) but surpasses all 3B-scale competitors, including the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B* (51.0). On the embodied Quantiphy benchmark, GeoSense-4B achieves the best overall score (37.36) among all compared models, edging out SpatialLadder-3B (36.86) and Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct (36.79).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Qwen2.5-VL as the base MLLM and VGGT as the 3D geometry encoder, but departs fundamentally from prior geometry-integration approaches (Spatial-MLLM, VG-LLM) that treat 3D features as a mandatory input fused via element-wise addition. Instead, GeoSense introduces an independent geometry input channel with a learned trigger mechanism, positioning geometry as an on-demand modality rather than a universal prerequisite. The model-adaptive data curation pipeline—using the model's own dual-condition inference discrepancies to generate training signals—repurposes the empirical-study paradigm into a training strategy, distinguishing it from hand-crafted rule-based gating or uniform geometry injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that rigid geometry injection actively degrades general reasoning (POPE drops from 86.9 to 74.2 when scaling VG-LLM training data) challenges the prevailing assumption that more geometric signal is always beneficial, and provides empirical justification for adaptive modality gating in MLLMs. The approach is particularly relevant for edge-deployed small models (3B) where the computational cost of always-on 3D extraction is prohibitive. Open questions remain around the quality ceiling imposed by the external VGGT encoder (dense scenes, wide-angle multi-view), the two-pass inference latency when triggered, and whether the trigger mechanism generalizes beyond the specific VGGT feature space to other 3D representations like point clouds or depth maps.
