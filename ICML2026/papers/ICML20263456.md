## Problem

In high-content screening, extracting image embeddings that group phenotypically similar perturbations while distinguishing them from controls is a central challenge. The standard weakly supervised learning (WSL) approach trains a treatment classifier whose objective inherently separates all classes, potentially splitting treatments that share the same mechanism of action or learning spurious correlations. The paper asks whether contrastive objectives with metadata-informed class prototypes can produce embedding spaces that better reflect biological similarity, particularly in data-limited settings where large pretrained models are impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263456/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-channel fluorescence microscopy tiles (128×128×4 for HaCaT screens, 256×256 for RxRx3-core, 3-channel crops for BBBC021) paired with a treatment or gene-KO label per well. It produces a 384-dimensional embedding per cell image (ResNet18) or 768-dimensional embedding (ViT), which are mean-averaged into well-level or treatment-level profiles. At evaluation, a batch-normalization domain adaptation step refits BN statistics using control wells to mitigate plate-to-plate variation, and profiles are compared via cosine distance for 1-NN, mAP, biological recall, and drug-target interaction metrics.

## Method

SPC (Spherical Phenotype Clustering) modifies the NTXent contrastive loss by replacing augmentation-based positives with learned class-vector prototypes and then decoupling the update of those prototypes from the encoder. The encoder is trained with the full NTXent objective (attractive pull toward the correct class vector plus repulsive push away from other class vectors and instance embeddings), while class vectors are updated using only the attractive cosine-similarity term via a no-momentum SGD step followed by re-normalization to the unit sphere. A key theoretical result shows the tangent to the shortest geodesic between a class vector and an embedding is a negative multiple of the cosine-loss gradient, justifying the spherical update path. By omitting repulsive forces from prototype updates, phenotypically similar classes are free to overlap in embedding space, while the encoder's repulsive terms prevent trivial collapse. The approach uses a small ResNet18 (11M parameters) or fine-tunes DINOv2 ViTs with LoRA, and achieves state-of-the-art NSC/NSCB on BBBC021, strong 1-NN/mAP on uncurated HaCaT screens, and biological recall and drug-target interaction scores competitive with models 25× larger on RxRx3-core.

## Results

On BBBC021, SPC achieves 98.0% NSC and 96.7% NSCB, surpassing the prior best (WSL at 97%/86%) by +1% and +10.2% respectively. On the uncurated HaCaT CRISPR screen, SPC reaches 0.714 1-NN and 0.450 mAP on unseen cells, outperforming WSL (0.626/0.326) and CellProfiler (0.550/0.197). On RxRx3-core, SPC with only 11M parameters (ResNet18) achieves 0.428 CORUM, 0.498 HuMAP, and 0.204 Reactome biological recall, exceeding Phenom-1 (307M params, trained on ~40× more data) on all four biological recall databases and the drug-target interaction AP score (0.298 vs 0.290).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPC outperforms WSL (Caicedo et al., 2018) on every metric across all three datasets: +1% NSC and +10.2% NSCB on BBBC021, +0.088 mAP on HaCaT JUMP unseen cells, and +0.124 mAP on HaCaT CRISPR unseen cells. Against SimCLR (Perakis et al., 2021), SPC gains +1.7% NSCB on BBBC021 and substantially larger margins on the uncurated HaCaT screens (e.g., 0.450 vs 0.098 mAP on CRISPR unseen cells). On RxRx3-core, SPC (11M params) surpasses Phenom-1 (307M params, trained on ~40× more data) on all four biological recall databases and the drug-target interaction AP, while remaining competitive with the 1.86B-parameter Phenom-2. SPC also beats the recent contrastive baselines CWA-MSN (22M) and CellCLIP (1.48B) on biological recall and drug-target interaction, and outperforms WS-DINO (Cross-Zamirski et al., 2022) by +6% NSC and +6.7% NSCB on BBBC021. The only metric where SPC trails is the Z-score on RxRx3-core (4.31 vs 6.04 for Phenom-2), though it matches Phenom-1's Z-score (4.35) with 28× fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the NTXent contrastive loss (SimCLR) and SupContrast by replacing augmentation-based positive pairs with learned class vectors (treatment prototypes) inherited from the weakly supervised learning (WSL) framework of Caicedo et al. The key departure is SPC, which removes repulsive terms from class vector updates and confines them to the unit sphere via geodesic (SLERP-inspired) steps, a geometric connection proven in Appendix A. The work positions itself against the prevailing trend of scaling model and dataset size (OpenPhenom, Phenom-1/2), demonstrating that a small ResNet18 with metadata-injected contrastive objectives can match or exceed much larger masked autoencoder and DINO-based models on standard HCS benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For drug discovery and biological research organizations operating with limited data or compute, this provides a practical, easy-to-implement method that outperforms both handcrafted features (CellProfiler) and large pretrained models across MoA inference, drug-target interaction, and biological recall metrics. The finding that removing repulsive terms from class updates (while retaining them for the encoder) improves phenotypic clustering is a useful design principle for any contrastive method with class structure. Open questions include whether the spherical constraint generalizes to other imaging modalities or 3D data, whether the approach benefits from larger encoder backbones beyond ViT/L, and how it performs on screens with very few replicates per treatment.
