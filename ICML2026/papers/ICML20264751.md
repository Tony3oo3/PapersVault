## Problem

Token-pruning accelerates Vision-Language Models by removing redundant visual tokens, but its safety implications under multimodal jailbreak attacks remain unexplored. This paper reveals that most pruning strategies significantly degrade safety (e.g., ~10% ASR increase) while query-based compression unexpectedly improves it, and identifies the underlying mechanism—Pruning-Induced Malicious Amplification—where background token removal forces attention to collapse onto retained malicious foreground anchors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264751/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SAP operates as a plug-and-play, training-free inference-time module atop any existing token-pruning method (e.g., TRIM, HiPrune) on LLaVA-1.5-7B. It consumes the pruned visual token sequence and the text instruction, using a pre-computed safety-aligned vector v_safe (extracted once from the VLM's hidden states) and per-head cross-attention weights at each decoding step. The output is the standard text response, but with a modified post-softmax attention distribution over visual tokens that dilutes malicious-anchor dominance; the method adds negligible latency (~1.3 ms over TRIM) and identical FLOPs.

## Method

SAP counteracts Pruning-Induced Malicious Amplification through three stages: (1) Malicious Anchor Identification scores each retained visual token by multiplying its average attention from text instruction tokens (Signal Magnitude) with its cosine deviation from a safety-aligned semantic direction (Risk Direction), selecting the top-m tokens as malicious anchors; (2) Benign Token Restoration uniformly samples k pruned background tokens and swaps them in place of the k lowest-scoring kept tokens, reintroducing a semantic buffer without changing sequence length; (3) Attention Reallocation down-weights malicious-anchor attention by a factor (1−λ) and uniformly redistributes the removed mass to the restored benign tokens, applied per-head across all layers before value aggregation. The theoretical grounding formalizes safety as Malicious Semantic Density ρ = S/(S+N), showing that pruning drives N→0 and ρ→1, while SAP's mixture reconstruction guarantees an entropy lower bound via Jensen's inequality that bounds the KL divergence from the safety-aligned output distribution.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B with 75% token pruning, SAP reduces average jailbreak ASR by 18.8–27.6% across five pruning methods (e.g., TRIM: 62.33→50.19%, PruMerge: 61.88→44.78%, FasterVLM: 63.22→47.60%) while preserving utility within ±2.1% (e.g., TRIM MMBench: 63.84→63.45%). When extended to textual jailbreak instructions (SAP†), the defense achieves near-immunity, reducing FasterVLM's MM-Safety ASR by 62% (72.02→10.32%). SAP also outperforms the unpruned Original model (54.01% ASR) on safety while retaining 75% of the original FLOPs reduction (2.22T vs. 8.79T) with only 1.29 ms additional latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAP consistently outperforms all five token-pruning baselines on safety: it reduces average ASR by 19.5% (TRIM, 62.33→50.19), 18.8% (HiPrune, 59.83→48.58), 27.6% (PruMerge, 61.88→44.78), 24.7% (FasterVLM, 63.22→47.60), and 23.1% (FitPrune, 63.14→48.55), while utility shifts by at most 2.1%. Notably, SAP+TRIM (50.19% ASR) surpasses even the unpruned Original model (54.01%) and Random Pruning (52.73%) on safety, demonstrating that the defense can make a pruned model safer than the full model. Against the external guardrail LLaVAGuard, SAP achieves comparable safety (52.78 vs. 51.59 on MM-Safety) at 3× lower latency (57.19 ms vs. 160.09 ms), and cascading both yields the strongest safety (41.07% MM-Safety). Query-based compression methods (LLaVA-Mini at 31.98%, LLaVA-MQT at 46.84% ASR) inherently improve safety without SAP, but at extreme compression ratios (99.3–99.8%) that sacrifice more utility (57.45% for MQT) compared to SAP's 75% pruning with near-identical utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of VLM token-pruning for efficiency (TRIM, HiPrune, PruMerge, FasterVLM, FitPrune, SparseVLM) and multimodal jailbreak defense (MM-SafetyBench, FigStep, JailBreakV-28K). It extends the parameter-pruning safety literature (Wei et al., 2024; Awal et al., 2025), which showed that weight pruning amplifies LLM vulnerability under jailbreak, to the token-pruning setting in VLMs. The closest prior method is SafePTR (Chen et al., 2025a), which identifies harmful tokens via representation deviation alone; SAP departs by jointly leveraging attention magnitude and semantic direction, and by adding benign token restoration and attention reallocation as explicit countermeasures. The observation that query-based compression (LLaVA-Mini, LLaVA-MQT) *improves* safety at extreme pruning ratios provides the empirical anchor for the paper's attention-equilibrium theory, distinguishing it from prior work that treated compression as uniformly safety-degrading.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Token-pruning is now a standard deployment technique for VLMs, and the finding that it silently degrades safety by 8–10% ASR (without any utility signal) is a critical gap for practitioners who assume efficiency and safety are orthogonal. The mechanism-level explanation (Pruning-Induced Malicious Amplification via attention collapse) is actionable and generalizes beyond the specific pruning methods tested. Key limitations include evaluation on a single architecture (LLaVA-1.5-7B), a white-box access requirement that restricts applicability to open-source models, and the fact that even with SAP the absolute ASR remains around 50%, far from robust. Open questions include robustness under adaptive attacks that specifically target the BTR/MAI pipeline, generalization to video or multi-image settings, and whether the attention-equilibrium principle transfers to non-attention-based compression (e.g., quantization, distillation).
