## Problem

Hierarchical RL for Sequential Stochastic Combinatorial Optimization (SSCO) places the high-level policy in a Semi-Markov Decision Process where each subgoal runs for a variable, agent-chosen number of primitive steps. Existing model-based HRL methods either impose a fixed temporal stride (Director, MTS-WM) or communicate only a scalar budget without semantic subgoal guidance (WS-option), making it difficult to perform model-based lookahead over variable-duration abstract actions under stochastic, path-dependent dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph-structured environment state (node features, edge topology, node statuses) and the remaining resource budget as input, encoded by a GNN into a fixed-size latent vector on the unit sphere. At each high-level decision it outputs a learned subgoal embedding (drawn from a finite dictionary of M subgoals) and a discrete budget allocation from {0, …, B_k}; the low-level DQN then translates this into a sequence of primitive combinatorial actions (e.g., individual node or city selections) executed for up to b_k steps before the next high-level decision.

## Method

LMTA couples three components: a MuZero-style latent-space MCTS planner that branches over subgoals, a Multi-Timescale SMDP (MTS-SMDP) world model, and a subgoal-conditioned budget policy. The MTS-SMDP world model is the core novelty: a single latent space is shaped by three complementary losses (a per-step cap on micro-displacement, a learned margin push on macro-displacement, and a budget-aware pairwise ranking loss) so that the chordal magnitude of a macro transition g_θ(h, z) → h' correlates with the effective duration of subgoal z, without an explicit duration predictor. The dynamics function collapses a variable-duration subgoal execution into one model evaluation, keeping the MCTS branching factor at |Z| rather than |Z|×|B| by marginalizing the budget through the learned budget policy. The low level is a subgoal-conditioned DQN with a GNN that concatenates the subgoal embedding to each node representation, and the entire system is trained via self-play with MuZero-style search-backed losses plus the MTS geometric constraints and an actor-critic budget objective.

## Results

On AIM (N=500), LMTA achieves 324.15 ± 2.38 average reward at (T=10, K=70), outperforming WS-option (301.53 ± 9.10) and the best domain heuristic average-score (313.74 ± 3.02). On SOP (N=500), the gap widens dramatically: LMTA reaches 31.60 ± 1.94 at (T=10, K=70) versus WS-option's 12.99 ± 1.25 and Greedy's 23.58 ± 0.16, a >2× improvement over the closest SSCO baseline. LMTA also improves over WS-option with p ≤ 0.05 (Welch's test) across all reported configurations in both domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMTA outperforms WS-option (Feng et al., 2025), the closest SSCO-specific baseline, on every configuration in both AIM and SOP, with the gap widening as budget K increases (e.g., +22.6 reward on AIM at K=70; +18.6 on SOP at K=70). Against Director (Hafner et al., 2022), a fixed-stride hierarchical model-based method, LMTA achieves higher final performance and greater sample efficiency in training curves (Fig. 2), with Director's fixed high-level stride limiting its ability to handle variable-duration budgeted decisions. Flat MuZero (Schrittwieser et al., 2020), which plans directly over primitive actions without temporal abstraction, is outperformed by LMTA, confirming the value of hierarchical subgoal-level planning in combinatorial action spaces. On SOP specifically, WS-option degrades markedly (12.99 vs. LMTA's 31.60 at K=70), exposing the limitation of budget-only signals that convey how long to act but not where to go, whereas LMTA's learned subgoals capture spatial structure (e.g., target regions) that guides the low-level routing policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of hierarchical RL (options, goal-conditioned subgoals), model-based planning (MuZero-style latent-space MCTS), and multi-timescale world models (Director, MTS-WM). It departs from fixed-stride hierarchical world models by formulating the high level as a true SMDP with agent-chosen variable durations, and from the closest SSCO baseline WS-option by coupling a learned subgoal embedding with a subgoal-conditioned budget policy rather than communicating only a scalar budget. The key methodological novelty is encoding temporal scale in latent geometry via a unified pull-push-order objective, so that macro-transition magnitudes correlate with subgoal durations without an explicit duration predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSCO problems (adaptive routing, influence maximization, resource-constrained scheduling) are practically important yet remain difficult for RL due to combinatorial action spaces, stochastic path-dependent dynamics, and intertemporal budget constraints. The paper's central insight—that generic model-based planning is insufficient for SMDPs and that explicit multi-timescale latent structure is the critical enabler—is supported by a clean ablation (variant C→D) and a regret bound connecting model quality to planning performance. Open questions include whether the fixed-size learned subgoal dictionary scales to open-ended task spaces, whether the decision-time discount approximation (Proposition A.8) remains tight under high duration variance, and whether the geometry-duration coupling generalizes beyond the two graph-structured benchmarks tested.
