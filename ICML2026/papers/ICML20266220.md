## Problem

Standard AdamW optimizers maintain FP32 first and second moment buffers (8 bytes per parameter), creating a critical memory bottleneck in distributed LLM training that limits batch size and forces scaling to more devices. Existing memory-efficient alternatives (Lion, Adam-mini, AdamS, Muon) either diverge under transferred AdamW hyperparameters, require substantial re-tuning, or introduce high computational and communication overhead from matrix factorization. The paper addresses the gap of an optimizer that matches AdamW performance while reducing optimizer-state memory to ~12.5% and directly inheriting AdamW hyperparameters without re-tuning.

## Input / Output

BAS consumes batch gradients and model parameters partitioned into N disjoint blocks (default: one block per Torch parameter tensor, with row-wise partitioning for embedding/output layers), along with standard AdamW hyperparameters (η, β₁, β₂, ε, λ). It produces updated model parameters and a single scalar adaptive scale per block. Unusually, the first moment is stored in FP8 (e5m2, 1 byte/param) rather than FP32, and the second moment is collapsed to one scalar per block rather than element-wise, yielding a total optimizer-state footprint of ~1 byte per parameter versus AdamW's 8 bytes.

## Method

BAS partitions parameters into blocks and computes a single adaptive scale αᵢᵗ per block as the ratio of the block's first-moment Frobenius norm to the square root of the EMA of squared gradient norms — an Adam-style signal-to-noise estimate at block granularity. The update then applies this shared block magnitude multiplied by the element-wise sign of the first moment (Sign(mᵢᵗ)), creating a continuum between Adam (element-wise blocks) and Signum (single global block). This sign-times-scale structure preserves Adam's optimization geometry, enabling direct transfer of AdamW hyperparameters, while the sign-based direction makes the update inherently robust to FP8 quantization of the first moment. A distributed variant, BAS-MV, replaces BF16 gradient all-reduce with 1-bit majority voting on momentum signs plus scalar averaging of block scales, achieving up to 16× communication reduction. The paper also provides an O(1/√T) convergence guarantee under bounded-gradient and bounded-variance assumptions.

## Results

On the Olmo3-7B SFT evaluation across 19 benchmarks, BAS (59.2 MATH, 50.3 BigBenchHard, 66.2 MMLU, 73.4 SimpleQA) matches or slightly exceeds AdamW (58.6, 49.5, 66.0, 71.0) on several key benchmarks while using only 12.5% of AdamW's optimizer-state memory. At 32B scale, BAS tracks AdamW within ~1 point on most benchmarks (e.g., 69.1 vs 69.2 MATH, 75.1 vs 74.3 HumanEvalPlus). In pre-training, BAS achieves a final loss effectively identical to AdamW on Qwen2.5-1.5B (100B tokens), while Adam-mini and AdamS diverge under transferred AdamW hyperparameters and Lion converges more slowly. BAS-MV achieves up to 16× communication reduction vs. BF16 DDP, with a 7.3× per-iteration speedup on 4-GPU PCIe-only hardware.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAS closely matches AdamW across the 7B SFT benchmarks, exceeding it on MATH (+0.6), BigBenchHard (+0.8), IFBench (+1.2), AlpacaEval 2 LC (+1.1), and SimpleQA (+2.4), while trailing slightly on HumanEvalPlus (−1.5) and IFEval (−0.8). Against the memory-efficient baselines, BAS consistently outperforms Lion, AdamS, and Adam-mini: for example, on MATH BAS scores 59.2 vs. Lion 56.5, AdamS 57.8, and Adam-mini 57.2; on HumanEvalPlus BAS scores 63.7 vs. Lion 60.8, AdamS 62.9, and Adam-mini 63.2. In pre-training, Adam-mini and AdamS diverge when given AdamW's hyperparameters, while BAS tracks AdamW's loss curve throughout 100B tokens of training; Lion remains stable but converges more slowly in token efficiency. At 32B scale, BAS stays within ~1 point of AdamW on most benchmarks (e.g., 69.1 vs. 69.2 MATH, 75.1 vs. 74.3 HumanEvalPlus), confirming that the block-wise variance approximation remains robust at scale. The key differentiator is that BAS achieves this with 12.5% of AdamW's optimizer-state memory (1 byte/param via FP8 first moment + per-block scalar second moment) and directly transfers AdamW hyperparameters without re-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

BAS sits at the intersection of two well-studied optimizer families: Adam's element-wise variance adaptivity and SignSGD's memory-efficient sign-based updates. It departs from Adam-mini (which also uses block-wise second moments) by preserving the sign-times-scale update geometry rather than retaining coordinate-dependent magnitudes, a distinction the paper argues is critical for AdamW hyperparameter transferability. It also differentiates from Muon by avoiding Newton-Schulz iterations and from Lion by retaining a true variance estimate at block granularity, addressing Lion's known hyperparameter sensitivity. The FP8 first-moment storage exploits the inherent quantization robustness of sign-based directions, a property not leveraged by prior memory-efficient optimizers that rely on precise momentum magnitudes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.5% optimizer-state footprint (8× less than AdamW, 4× less than Lion/Adam-mini) combined with direct AdamW hyperparameter transferability makes BAS a practical drop-in for existing LLM training pipelines, particularly for continuing or fine-tuning Adam-pretrained checkpoints where optimizer mismatch is a known concern. Open questions include reconciling BAS-MV with ZeRO-style state sharding, extending theoretical guarantees beyond bounded-gradient assumptions, and whether sub-8-bit moment storage can be made stable with specialized scaling. The communication-efficient variant (16× reduction) targets bandwidth-constrained environments but currently degrades gradually at high data-parallel sizes, limiting its applicability to very large clusters.
