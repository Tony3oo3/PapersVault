## Problem

Neural collapse (NC) is the structured geometry observed in trained classifiers, but whether it remains the unique optimal configuration in deep architectures trained without explicit regularization has been unclear. Prior work attributed the suboptimality of deep NC to L2 regularization, leaving open whether depth alone—through the implicit biases of gradient flow—suffices to break the benign landscape and favor alternative low-rank structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261251/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical model (deep UFM) takes unconstrained feature vectors h_ic ∈ R^d as input and produces a logit matrix Z ∈ R^{K×nK} whose singular values and effective rank are the primary objects of analysis. For empirical validation, the method consumes MNIST (28×28 grayscale) and CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB) images through a ResNet-20 backbone with a fully connected ReLU head of varying depth and width, producing class predictions and the mean logit matrix whose effective rank is tracked as a proxy for the degree of neural collapse versus low-rank structure.

## Method

The paper analyzes gradient flow on the deep unconstrained features model (deep UFM) with cross-entropy loss and no explicit regularization, isolating the implicit bias induced purely by depth and optimization dynamics. Asymptotically, it shows the non-convex max-margin landscape admits multiple stable local minima where DNC is strictly suboptimal, with the mechanism being that low-rank matrices propagate Frobenius norm more efficiently through successive multiplications, achieving larger logits for a fixed parameter norm. In the large-depth limit, the global optimum corresponds to the d=2 softmax code (class means as vertices of a regular K-gon), connecting depth-induced bias to explicit width bottlenecks. At finite time, under Hadamard initialization, the paper proves NC becomes unstable near the origin via a 'rich-get-richer' effect on singular values, while random initialization combined with large width counteracts this through concentration-of-measure effects that bias early dynamics toward the simplex ETF direction.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is that depth induces an exponential low-rank bias: the logit norm achievable under DNC is suppressed by a factor of exp(−L/2 · log((K−1)/r)) relative to a rank-r alternative, making DNC strictly suboptimal for L=2, K≥6 or L≥3, K≥4 (Theorem 3.1). In the large-depth limit, the global optimum is the d=2 softmax code (Theorem 3.2). Empirically, on MNIST and CIFAR-10 with ResNet-20, the effective rank of the mean logit matrix decreases systematically with head depth (from ≈K−1 at L=1 to substantially lower values at L=5), while increasing width raises the effective rank. The instability threshold for DNC under the linearized dynamics is ||a||₁ > (L−1)/(L+1)·(K−1), with the unstable region growing monotonically in L.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with the softmax codes of Jiang et al. (2023), which arise in width-bottlenecked single-layer UFMs (d < K−1). Figure 3 (right) shows that the normalized margins achieved by a depth-L deep UFM that converges to a rank-r logit matrix closely match those of a single-layer network with width d=r, confirming that depth acts as an implicit rank constraint equivalent to an explicit width bottleneck. In the L→∞ limit, the deep UFM selects precisely the d=2 softmax code (regular K-gon arrangement of class means), even when no width constraint is present. The paper also contrasts its unregularized analysis with prior regularized deep UFM results (Sukenik et al., 2024; Garrod & Keating, 2026), showing that depth alone—without L₂ regularization—suffices to make DNC suboptimal, correcting the prior interpretation that regularization was the cause. On the empirical side, the L=1 case (all effective ranks ≈K−1, corresponding to NC) serves as the baseline against which deeper heads show reduced rank, and the generalization scatter plot (Figure 6, right) shows that lower effective rank correlates with poorer test accuracy, consistent with the theoretical prediction that low-rank solutions trade discrimination quality for confidence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unconstrained features model (UFM) from the shallow, regularized setting studied by Mixon et al. (2020) and Garrod et al. (2025) to the deep, unregularized regime, isolating the implicit bias induced by depth and gradient flow alone. It builds directly on the Hadamard initialization framework of Garrod et al. (2025) for tractable CE dynamics and the max-margin landscape analysis of Lyu & Li (2019), generalizing both from single-layer to multi-layer networks. A key departure from prior deep-NC work (Sukenik et al., 2024; Garrod & Keating, 2026) is the removal of L2 regularization, which the authors show was confounding the attribution of low-rank bias to regularization rather than to depth itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous characterization of implicit bias in deep UFMs trained with unregularized cross-entropy loss, resolving an open question about whether NC's suboptimality at depth is an artifact of regularization or an intrinsic property of deep optimization. The identification of softmax codes as the infinite-depth limit of the low-rank optimum connects two previously separate phenomena (depth-induced rank reduction and width-bottlenecked geometry), while the width/initialization counteracting result explains why NC remains prevalent in practice. Open questions include extending the softmax-code characterization to finite depth, clarifying the role of skip connections in suppressing low-rank alternatives, and establishing a formal link between effective rank and generalization.
