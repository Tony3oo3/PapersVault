## Problem

Standard sparse autoencoders reconstruct activations as linear combinations of independent features, an assumption that cannot capture compositional structure: a linear decoder cannot distinguish whether 'Starbucks' arises from the composition of 'star' and 'coffee' features or merely their co-occurrence. This forces SAEs to allocate monolithic, context-specific features for compound concepts rather than decomposing them into interpretable atomic constituents, obscuring the mechanisms by which networks build complex representations from simpler parts.

## Input / Output

PolySAE consumes residual-stream activations from a single transformer layer of a pretrained LLM (e.g., layer 8 of GPT-2 Small, layer 19 of Gemma-2-2B) and produces reconstructed activations plus a sparse latent code of 16,384 features with K=64 active per token. Unlike standard SAEs where each feature has a fixed dictionary atom, PolySAE's effective feature contribution becomes context-dependent: the reconstruction direction for feature i shifts depending on which other features are simultaneously active, yielding learned interaction directions (d_i + d_j) that can be injected into the residual stream as steering vectors for causal intervention.

## Method

PolySAE preserves the standard linear SAE encoder (ReLU + TopK/BatchTopK/Matryoshka sparsification) and extends only the decoder with quadratic and cubic polynomial terms in the sparse code z. The key architectural choice is a shared orthonormal projection U ∈ R^{d_sae × R1} applied to z before forming interactions: y2 = ((zU_{:,1:R2}) * (zU_{:,1:R2})) C^(2)T and y3 = ((zU_{:,1:R3}) * (zU_{:,1:R3}) * (zU_{:,1:R3})) C^(3)T, where * denotes element-wise product and ranks follow the nested constraint R1 ≥ R2 ≥ R3 (e.g., 768/64/64 for GPT-2). Orthonormality of U is enforced via positive QR retraction after each gradient step, ensuring geometrically distinct interaction directions and removing rotational ambiguity. Setting the learnable scalar coefficients λ2 = λ3 = 0 recovers vanilla SAE exactly, making PolySAE a strict generalization applicable to any existing SAE variant. The low-rank factorization adds only ~3% parameter overhead (≈2.5–3% of the full SAE for GPT-2 Small) while supporting interaction-driven structure across C(d_sae,2)·R2 + C(d_sae,3)·R3 feature pairs and triples through a small number of reusable interaction modes.

## Results

Across four LLMs (GPT-2 Small, Pythia-410M/1.4B, Gemma-2-2B) and three sparsification strategies, PolySAE achieves a mean probing F1 improvement of ~8% over vanilla SAE (up to +12.3 pp on GPT-2 Small with BatchTopK: 65.7→78.0), while maintaining comparable reconstruction MSE (e.g., 0.52→0.55 on GPT-2 TopK) and cross-entropy recovery within 0.003. PolySAE also produces 2–10× larger 1-Wasserstein distances between class-conditional feature distributions, confirming genuinely better-separated semantic geometry rather than merely favorable decision boundaries. The parameter overhead is only ~3% for GPT-2 Small (R₂=R₃=64, R₁=768).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolySAE is compared against three standard SAE sparsification strategies—TopK (Gao et al., 2025), BatchTopK (Bussmann et al., 2024), and Matryoshka (Bussmann et al., 2025)—across four LLMs. On Mean Probing F1, PolySAE wins in all 12 model×sparsifier configurations: gains range from +0.7 pp (Gemma-2-2B TopK: 67.7→68.4) to +12.3 pp (GPT-2 Small BatchTopK: 65.7→78.0), averaging ~8% across models. Reconstruction MSE is comparable or marginally higher (e.g., GPT-2 TopK: 0.52→0.55; Pythia-1.4B TopK: 0.23→0.23), and cross-entropy recovery stays within 0.003 of vanilla SAE in every configuration, confirming no functional degradation. The largest absolute gains appear on GPT-2 Small and Pythia-410M, where the smaller models benefit most from explicit interaction modeling; Gemma-2-2B shows smaller F1 gains but still 2–10× larger Wasserstein distances. On the distributional separation metric, PolySAE consistently achieves 2–10× larger 1-Wasserstein distances than the corresponding vanilla SAE (e.g., GPT-2 TopK Europarl: 19.0→35.2; GPT-2 BatchTopK Europarl: 19.0→39.9), indicating the probing gains reflect genuinely more separated class-conditional geometry rather than threshold artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolySAE extends the sparse autoencoder framework for mechanistic interpretability (Huben et al., 2024; Bricken et al., 2023) by replacing the linear decoder with a polynomial one that explicitly models pairwise and triple feature interactions. The closest prior work is the Bilinear Autoencoder (Dooms & Gauderis, 2025), which introduces interaction terms for interpretability but operates on input neurons rather than learned sparse features; PolySAE instead models interactions directly between SAE latents, preserving their linear interpretability. The low-rank tensor factorization on a shared orthonormal projection subspace draws on compact bilinear pooling (Gao et al., 2016) and factorized interaction models (Rendle, 2010), while the conceptual motivation traces to Smolensky's (1990) tensor product variable binding as a solution to the compositionality problem in neural representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PolySAE addresses a fundamental expressivity gap in SAEs: the linear reconstruction assumption forces the model to allocate monolithic features for compositional concepts rather than decomposing them into interpretable constituents. The finding that learned interaction weights show negligible correlation with co-occurrence frequency (r=0.06 vs. r=0.82 for SAE covariance) is the paper's strongest conceptual contribution, suggesting polynomial terms capture compositional structure largely independent of surface statistics. However, all experiments are limited to models up to 2B parameters and forced-sparsity SAE variants, leaving open whether the approach scales to frontier models or integrates with other SAE improvements (gated, JumpReLU). The modest 8% average F1 gain and the fact that reconstruction error is essentially unchanged temper the practical impact, though the 2–10× Wasserstein separation and causal steering results indicate the learned interactions are semantically meaningful rather than mere fitting artifacts.
