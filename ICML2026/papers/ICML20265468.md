## Problem

Conditioned Sequence Models (CSMs) like Decision Transformer treat return-to-go (RTG) tokens as simple numerical inputs, but empirical studies show they exhibit significant insensitivity to RTG and fail to align realized returns with target RTG values. The core gap is the absence of a mechanism to enforce the partial ordering between RTG targets and policy behavior, so that higher RTGs consistently map to trajectories with higher expected cumulative returns.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a k-step context window of interleaved RTG, state, and action tokens (τ_t), where the RTG is a scalar conditioning signal scaled by 1/1000 for D4RL Gym environments. At inference, the user specifies a target RTG to select a policy from the learned family; the model outputs predicted actions via a causal Transformer with a lightweight 1D convolution (window size 6) applied to attention projections. A co-trained double Q-function (3-layer MLP, hidden dim 256) provides value estimates used both for the alignment loss during training and for Bellman-consistent critic updates.

## Method

Q-ALIGN DT introduces an alignment loss that enforces monotonicity between input RTG and the Q-value of predicted actions using a zeroth-order gradient estimate: for a perturbed RTG (RTG + δ, δ ~ N(0, σ_e²)), an asymmetric indicator penalizes only when the Q-value change has the wrong sign relative to δ, leaving already-ordered pairs untouched. The Q-function is co-trained with a fixed positive RTG offset ΔRTG in the Bellman target, so the critic evaluates the policy under a higher-return conditioning, creating a positive actor-critic feedback loop that prevents the critic from collapsing to the behavior policy's suboptimal returns. The overall objective combines standard supervised regression (state and action prediction) with the alignment loss weighted by λ_e. Theoretically, the monotonicity constraint reduces the policy class complexity from O(|S||G| log|A|) to Õ(|S||G|), and in the high-RTG limit the alignment objective becomes equivalent to Q-value maximization, recovering near-optimal in-distribution policies.

## Results

On the D4RL Gym benchmark (9 datasets), Q-ALIGN DT achieves a total normalized score of 856.9, surpassing the next-best CSM baselines QCS (812.3) and QT (808.6) by 44.6 and 48.3 points respectively. On the alignment metric (RMSE between target and realized RTG), Q-ALIGN DT achieves a mean of 9.01 across all Gym tasks, roughly halving the error of the best prior method DC (17.86) and reducing QT's error (34.22) by 74%. On the zero-shot HalfCheetah-vel transfer task, Q-ALIGN DT attains an average episode return of −142.3, dramatically outperforming all cross-domain CSMs (best: RADT at −754.7) and approaching in-domain Meta-RL methods like MACAW (−121.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-ALIGN DT outperforms all 15 baselines on the Gym sum score (856.9), with the largest margins over value-based methods IQL (688.8) and TD3+BC (677.4), and a 44.6-point lead over the strongest CSM competitor QCS (812.3). Among CSM baselines, QT (808.6) and DM (764.3) are the closest on peak performance, but both suffer substantially worse alignment (RMSE 34.22 and not reported, vs. Q-ALIGN DT's 9.01), indicating they sacrifice RTG sensitivity for return maximization. On alignment specifically, Q-ALIGN DT dominates: it achieves the lowest RMSE on 7 of 9 Gym tasks, with particularly large gaps on walker2d-medium (6.25 vs. DC's 16.72) and halfcheetah-medium-expert (1.24 vs. RADT's 17.55). On AntMaze, Q-ALIGN DT leads on umaze (96.4) and medium-play (85.6) but is slightly behind QCS on medium-diverse (76.2 vs. 79.5). On the zero-shot HalfCheetah-vel transfer, Q-ALIGN DT (−142.3) surpasses all cross-domain CSMs by over 600 points (next best: RADT at −754.7) and even exceeds in-domain Meta-RL methods MACAW (−121.6) and PEARL (−273.6), though it trails Prompt-DT with expert prompts (−38.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Decision Transformer (Chen et al., 2021) and broader CSM family by addressing a specific failure mode identified by Kim et al. (2024a) and Tanaka et al. (2025): RTG tokens are under-utilized, leading to poor alignment between target returns and realized performance. Unlike Q-gradient methods (QT, QCS, TD3-ODT) that push the policy toward maximum Q-value regardless of the RTG target, or RADT which adds architectural overhead, Q-ALIGN DT introduces a monotonicity-enforcing alignment loss and a co-training mechanism where RTG perturbation of the policy provides structured targets for Q-function learning. The theoretical analysis builds on the finite-sample bounds of Brandfonbrener et al. (2022), showing that the monotonicity constraint reduces the effective policy class complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental controllability gap in CSMs that limits their practical utility for tasks requiring graded behavioral responses (e.g., velocity tracking). The zero-shot transfer to HalfCheetah-vel suggests the learned RTG-conditioned policy family captures semantically meaningful structure beyond mere reward matching, though this is demonstrated on a single task. Key limitations include reliance on Q-function pretraining for sparse-reward environments (AntMaze), theoretical results restricted to discrete/simplified settings, and evaluation confined to D4RL Gym and AntMaze benchmarks. Extending to visual control, robotic manipulation, and more general theoretical settings remains open.
