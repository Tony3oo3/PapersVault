## Problem

Latent diffusion models achieve strong generation quality but sacrifice end-to-end modeling by discarding information through lossy tokenization and requiring a separately trained decoder, while pixel-space diffusion is end-to-end but locked into a frequency-domain generation order that predicts low-frequency details before semantics. The paper identifies that the *order* in which information is revealed during the diffusion trajectory is a critical, underexplored design axis, and asks whether the convergence benefits of latent diffusion can be recovered in a lossless, pixel-space setting by explicitly controlling this order.

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (or no class for unconditional generation) and Gaussian noise as input, and jointly denoises two modalities: 256 pixel tokens (16×16 patches of 768-dim RGB values, normalized to [-1,1]) and 256 latent tokens (16×16 spatial grid of DINOv2 or Data2Vec2 features, rescaled to match pixel variance). Each modality has its own time variable, and the generated latents are simply discarded at the end of sampling, yielding a 256×256 RGB image as the sole output. The architecture maintains the same token count as a standard pixel DiT, so training and inference speed are nearly identical to JiT.

## Method

Latent Forcing trains a single diffusion transformer to jointly denoise pixels and self-supervised encoder latents (e.g., DINOv2) using separate per-modality time variables, with a cascaded schedule that fully denoises latents before beginning pixel denoising. Architecturally, the approach requires minimal changes to a standard DiT: a second time-embedding MLP is added for adaLN conditioning, latent and pixel patch embeddings are combined by addition (preserving token count), and optionally the last four transformer layers are split into two output experts. Training uses x-prediction with v-loss weighting (following JiT), logit-normal time sampling per modality, and a small amount of noise (β=25%) applied to latents during pixel steps to prevent overfitting to high-frequency latent details. The key insight is that the denoised latents act as a semantic 'scratchpad' that conditions early pixel generation, making the trajectory easier than pure pixel diffusion, while the latents are discarded at the end so the model remains lossless and directly optimizes the pixel likelihood. The paper shows this ordering benefit is distinct from REPA-style distillation, achieving 1.9× lower unguided FID-50K over JiT+REPA at matched compute.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, LF-DiT-L (465M params, 200 epochs) achieves FID-50K unguided 7.2 and guided 2.48, establishing a new state-of-the-art for pixel-space diffusion transformers. For conditional generation at 80 epochs, LF-DiT with DINOv2 latents reaches FID-50K unguided 9.76 and guided 4.18, a 1.9× improvement over JiT+REPA (18.60/4.57) and 2.5× over JiT alone (25.18/5.64). For unconditional generation, LF-DiT achieves FID-50K unguided 20.44 and guided 13.36, outperforming JiT (53.26/44.80) and JiT+REPA (35.04/24.40) by large margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LF-DiT-L decisively outperforms the closest pixel-space ViT baseline JiT-L (FID-50K unguided 7.2 vs. 16.21, guided 2.48 vs. 2.79) and JiT+REPA (9.76 vs. 18.60 unguided at 80 epochs), demonstrating that ordering latents before pixels provides gains beyond what REPA-style distillation offers. Against latent diffusion methods, LF-DiT-L trails the strongest approaches—RAE (1.51/1.13), REPA-E (1.69/1.12), LightningDiT (2.17/1.35), and MAR+Detok (1.86/1.35)—which all use lossy tokenizers (PSNR 18–26) and substantially more training epochs (800–1480). However, LF-DiT-L surpasses DiT-XL/2+SD-VAE (9.62/3.04), SiT-XL/2+SD-VAE (8.3/2.62), and ADM (10.94/3.94) on both unguided and guided FID while maintaining lossless reconstruction (PSNR ∞). Against the high-capacity pixel-space SiD UViT/2 (2.77/2.44), LF-DiT-L is competitive on guided FID (2.48 vs. 2.44) but trails on unguided FID, though SiD uses a 2B-parameter U-Net architecture rather than a ViT. The paper's key claim is validated: among ViT-based pixel diffusion models at matched compute, reordering the trajectory with latent forcing yields the best results, narrowing the gap to latent diffusion without sacrificing end-to-end fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on JiT's pixel-space diffusion transformer (x-prediction with v-loss, 128-dim bottleneck) and DiT's architecture, while adapting the multi-time-variable formulation from Diffusion Forcing to a non-autoregressive, joint-generation setting. It departs from the dominant latent diffusion paradigm by eliminating the separate decoder and lossy tokenizer, and from REPA-style distillation by showing that the benefit of pretrained representations comes from *ordering* the denoising trajectory (latents before pixels) rather than from alignment losses. The paper also distinguishes itself from ReDi, which attributes gains to modeling a joint P(X,Y) distribution, by arguing that for deterministic latents the process reduces purely to P(Pixels) and the latent serves only as a conditioning scratchpad.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that generation order, not distillation, is the primary driver of REPA's gains—challenges a large and active line of research and suggests a simpler, lossless, end-to-end alternative to the tokenizer-decoder pipeline. Practically, the method requires only ~0.5% additional parameters and identical FLOPs to standard DiT, making it a drop-in upgrade for existing pixel-space training pipelines. Open questions include whether the ordering benefit transfers to text-to-image generation, higher resolutions, and non-DiT architectures, and whether the specific choice of self-supervised encoder (DINOv2 vs. Data2Vec2) matters as much as the ordering itself. The paper is limited to ImageNet 256×256, so scaling behavior remains untested.
