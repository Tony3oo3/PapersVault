## Problem

Extending Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) beyond 1D sequences lacks a unified theoretical formulation. Existing high-dimensional variants either decompose positions into independent axis-wise rotations or rely on learnable frequency mixing, both of which fragment coherent spatial displacements, introduce directional bias, and offer no principled guarantee of isotropic frequency coverage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262614/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes n-dimensional spatial coordinates (2D image patch indices, 3D point-cloud positions, or spatiotemporal video token coordinates) together with standard query and key content vectors from a Transformer. It outputs positionally-modulated query/key vectors via element-wise multiplication with complex rotation factors e^{jω^T x}, implemented as real-valued cosine-sine pairs. The per-head embedding dimension must be a multiple of 2(d+1) where d is the spatial dimensionality, and the construction is parameter-free beyond the frequency base θ and the number of scales S.

## Method

nD-RoPE derives its form from a translation-invariant attention kernel in continuous Hilbert space, showing that the relative-position phase must take the coupled n-dimensional inner product ω^T x rather than a sum of independent 1D phases. For wave-vector selection, it imposes two geometric criteria—non-degenerate spatial coverage (rank n) and maximal symmetry—and shows the centered regular simplex with n+1 vertices is the minimal-redundancy configuration satisfying both, yielding an isotropic second-order response ∑ω_iω_i^T ∝ I_n. The final embedding stacks S geometrically-spaced scales of these simplex wave vectors, each optionally rotated independently per attention head, producing a multi-scale, directionally balanced frequency set. Computationally, each phase e^{jω^T x} is realized as a standard (cos, sin) pair, so nD-RoPE drops into existing RoPE implementations and extrapolation techniques (e.g., YaRN) without modifying the attention mechanism.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (ViT-S, trained at 224×224), nD-RoPE achieves 81.07% top-1 accuracy in-domain, outperforming RoPE-Mixed (80.90%) and RoPE-Axial (80.89%). Under resolution extrapolation with YaRN, nD-RoPE+YaRN reaches 68.46% at 1024×1024, substantially exceeding RoPE-Mixed+APE+YaRN (49.40%) and RoPE-Axial+YaRN (48.02%). On ModelNet40 point cloud segmentation (2048 points), nD-RoPE with vector attention attains 85.97% mIoU, surpassing the best baseline (Learnable Rel. PE at 82.58%). Zero-shot rotational robustness at 30° shows nD-RoPE retaining 78.51% accuracy versus 71.34% for RoPE-Mixed, confirming isotropic geometric encoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

nD-RoPE consistently outperforms all RoPE variants in-domain and under extrapolation. On ImageNet-1K at 224×224, nD-RoPE (81.07%) edges out RoPE-Mixed (80.90%) and RoPE-Axial (80.89%); the gap widens dramatically at 1024×1024 with YaRN, where nD-RoPE+YaRN (68.46%) exceeds RoPE-Mixed+APE+YaRN (49.40%) by 19 points. On Kinetics-400 video, nD-RoPE achieves 75.85% in-domain (vs. 75.61% Learnable PE) and 59.23% at 1024 with YaRN (vs. 58.38% for RoPE-Axial+APE+YaRN). On ModelNet40, nD-RoPE with vector attention (85.97% mIoU) beats Learnable Rel. PE (82.58%) by 3.4 points and 3D RoPE-Axial+APE (81.92%) by 4.1 points. On SemanticKITTI, nD-RoPE (71.91%) marginally leads Learnable Rel. PE (71.70%) but trails it at the coarsest grid (0.15: 53.53% vs. 50.20%), while still outperforming all RoPE variants. The rotational robustness test (Table 5) shows nD-RoPE retaining 78.51% at 30° rotation versus 71.34% for RoPE-Mixed and 70.61% for RoPE-Axial, a 7–8 point advantage that confirms the isotropic simplex design eliminates axis-aligned directional bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends RoPE (Su et al., 2024) from 1D sequences to arbitrary n-dimensional spaces by deriving a unified formulation from translation-invariant attention in continuous Hilbert space, rather than the axis-wise decomposition used by Axial RoPE variants or the learnable frequency mixing of RoPE-Mixed (Heo et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is treating positions and wave vectors as coupled n-dimensional entities whose interaction is a single inner product ωᵀx, with wave vectors placed at the vertices of a regular simplex to guarantee isotropic, non-axis-aligned frequency coverage. This connects to the broader Fourier feature literature (Tancik et al., 2020; Rahimi & Recht, 2007) but replaces random Gaussian sampling with a deterministic, geometrically symmetric construction, and differs from FoPE (Hua et al., 2025) by addressing multi-dimensional isotropy rather than 1D spectral correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simplex-based wave-vector design is parameter-free and deterministic, making it a drop-in replacement for RoPE's frequency construction that requires no additional training or tuning beyond the standard RoPE hyperparameters. The consistent gains across images, videos, and point clouds—particularly the large extrapolation margins with YaRN (e.g., 68.5% vs. 43.5% at 1024×1024 on ImageNet)—suggest that directional bias in frequency selection is a more significant bottleneck for generalization than previously appreciated. Open questions include whether the fixed simplex geometry remains optimal for highly anisotropic data (the paper notes axial RoPE slightly wins on coarse SemanticKITTI grids), whether the construction extends naturally to non-Euclidean or curved domains, and whether the frequency base bound (Eq. 37) holds under different architectural choices beyond the tested configurations.
