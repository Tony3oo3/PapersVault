## Problem

LLM-generated optimization code can execute without errors yet contain subtle semantic errors—misspecified constraints, incorrect objective aggregation, or mishandled indices—that are extremely difficult for non-expert users to detect. Standard verification approaches (unit tests, behavioral test cases) are infeasible for optimization formulations because correctness depends on global interactions across high-dimensional variable spaces, making reliable test generation itself a hard optimization problem.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language problem description and associated structured data (JSON or CSV) as input, and produces a verified solver program (Gurobi/CPLEX/SCIP API code), the solver's optimal solution (status and valuation over all decision variables), and a self-verification score indicating confidence in the formulation. Up to K=10 candidate programs are generated via temperature sampling with error-based refinement, and the verification score is computed per candidate through constraint-mutation feasibility checks and variable-masking valuation queries, with the highest-ranked program selected for final execution.

## Method

VERISIMPL inverts the conventional verification workflow: instead of the LLM proposing test scenarios for the solver to check, the solver constructs simplified diagnostic queries from the candidate program that are tractable for LLM reasoning. Constraint-based verification mutates each constraint into three semantic variants (strict satisfaction e(V)≤−δ, boundary e(V)=0, violation e(V)≥+δ), uses the solver to find witness valuations for each mutation, and asks the LLM to judge feasibility against the natural language description. Variable-based verification obtains the solver-optimal valuation, then masks subsets of variables (singletons or the full set) and asks the LLM to predict the masked values given all others fixed, probing objective and interaction logic. A lightweight type-verification step checks variable domain consistency. The three semantic scores are aggregated with equal weighting, and type verification serves as a lexicographic tiebreaker, prioritizing semantic correctness over syntactic consistency.

## Results

VERISIMPL achieves the highest average end-to-end accuracy across four optimization benchmarks: 65.5% with GPT-4o and 72.8% with DeepSeek-R1 as the base model, outperforming all prior methods on every dataset. The self-verification mechanism provides a high-precision confidence signal, reaching 91.5% precision (GPT-4o) and 78.5% precision (R1) on fully verified cases, though coverage is limited to 34.2% and 23.0% respectively. Dataset-size-weighted averages further confirm the advantage, with VERISIMPL scoring 73.4% (GPT-4o) and 79.5% (R1) versus the next-best SELFDEBUG at 64.8% and 74.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VERISIMPL outperforms all three baselines on every dataset where they overlap. Against OPTIMUS (Ahmaditeshnizi et al., ICML 2024), VERISIMPL gains are largest on NLP4LP (+14.5 pp) and IndOR (+28.1 pp), where OPTIMUS's multi-agent pipeline degrades substantially (37.1% and 17.7% respectively). Against SELFDEBUG (Chen et al., ICLR 2024), VERISIMPL leads by 11.6 pp on NL4Opt, 4.8 pp on NLP4LP, 5.9 pp on CompOR, and 3.1 pp on IndOR, with the average gap of 6.4 pp (65.5% vs. 59.1%). Against Chain-of-Experts (Xiao et al., ICLR 2024), VERISIMPL achieves 88.1% vs. 58.9% on NL4Opt and 76.5% vs. 25.9% on CompOR, though COE was not evaluated on NLP4LP or IndOR. With R1 as the base model, VERISIMPL extends its lead to 72.8% average, surpassing SELFDEBUG (67.9%) by 4.9 pp and OPTIMUS (55.0%) by 17.8 pp, confirming that the gains are not model-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LLM-based optimization code generation (NL4Opt, OPTIMUS, Chain-of-Experts) and LLM code verification (SelfDebug, Reflexion). Its key conceptual departure is inverting the standard verification workflow: rather than having the LLM propose test cases that the solver executes, VERISIMPL uses the solver to generate simplified diagnostic queries (constraint mutations and variable-masked valuation queries) that the LLM then reasons about. This is conceptually closest to mutation testing in software engineering, but with the solver constructing semantically targeted simplifications rather than random mutations, and the LLM serving as the oracle for natural-language-grounded feasibility and optimality reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-verification signal (precision >90% for GPT-4o) offers a practical mechanism to reduce manual inspection burden in NL-to-optimization pipelines, which is valuable for non-expert users who cannot easily detect subtle semantic errors. However, coverage remains limited (~20–30%), and the paper identifies a fundamental limitation: when both the LLM's reasoning and the solver's formulation share the same misinterpretation of decision variables or problem semantics, verification passes on incorrect models. Open questions include extending verification to validate variable definitions themselves and detecting aspects of the natural-language description that are entirely omitted from the formulation rather than incorrectly specified.
