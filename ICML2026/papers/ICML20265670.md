## Problem

Pre-propagation GNNs (PP-GNNs) decouple graph diffusion from per-node transformation for scalable dense training, but exhibit a significant accuracy gap (up to 11%) relative to message-passing GNNs on heterophilic graphs. This gap stems from two design limitations: standard diffusion operators (normalized adjacency, random walk) act as low-pass filters with poorly conditioned monomial hop bases, and the one-shot propagation is never recoupled with evolving hidden representations during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph adjacency matrix and a node feature matrix, precomputes a fixed multi-hop diffusion bank (K ≤ 15 hops) as a one-time preprocessing step, and trains a dense per-node predictor (MLP, attention, or RNN) on the resulting features to output softmax class probabilities. The hop budget is fixed before training and the diffusion bank is reused across all epochs and HRP stages, so no sparse matrix-matrix multiplications occur during the dense optimization loop. An optional RNN-based hop aggregator (GARNN) replaces multi-head attention over the hop axis, reducing end-to-end training time by roughly 2× compared to HOGA at comparable accuracy.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, robust diffusion operators replace the standard monomial hop bank with better-conditioned bases: a Jacobi-polynomial basis calibrated once via stochastic Chebyshev moments to match the graph's spectral density, and a channel-specific Lanczos/Krylov basis that decomposes each feature channel into orthogonal Ritz components within a low-rank subspace. Second, few-shot hidden-state re-propagation (HRP) splits training into S ≤ 7 stages; at each stage boundary, the best hidden representation is detached, re-diffused through the graph using the same operator family, and blended hop-wise with the previous-stage input to form the next stage's backbone input. This lightweight recoupling injects task-adapted relational signals without reverting to fully iterative message passing, and empirically outperforms label-based alternatives (label-as-input, label propagation) by 1.5–3.5 points on heterophilic benchmarks.

## Results

Across 12 node-classification benchmarks (6 heterophilic, 6 homophilic), the proposed robust diffusion operators plus hidden-state re-propagation (HRP) improve PP-GNN test accuracy by +2.18 points on average on heterophilic graphs (max +6.3) and +1.96 on homophilic graphs (max +7.5). On the large heterophilic pokec graph, GARNN+ reaches 84.63% test accuracy, surpassing the best MP-GNN (GAT, 81.52%) by over 3 points. The enhanced PP-GNNs outperform MP-GNN baselines on 7 out of 12 datasets while retaining dense-dominated training efficiency (HRP adds only ~2% wall-clock overhead).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The enhanced PP-GNNs (with robust diffusion + HRP) outperform all MP-GNN baselines on tolokers (GAMLP+ 85.5 vs GAT-sep 83.8), questions (HOGA+ 79.0 vs FSGNN 78.9), amazon-ratings (SIGN+ 54.4 vs SAGE 53.6), pokec (GARNN+ 84.63 vs GAT 81.52), ogbn-arxiv (GAMLP+ 72.62 vs GAT 72.01), and amazon-computer (GARNN+ 92.1 vs O-GNN 92.0). On roman-empire, the best enhanced PP-GNN (GARNN+/HOGA+ at 85.5%) still trails DIR-GNN (91.2%) by ~5.7 points, though the original gap was 11.2 points. On minesweeper, SAGE (93.5) and GAT-sep (93.9) remain ahead of the best PP-GNN (GARNN+ 92.3) by 1.2–1.6 points. Among PP-GNN baselines, the robust diffusion operators alone contribute +2.09% average validation accuracy and HRP adds a further +1.54%, with the two components providing complementary gains. The RNN-based GARNN backbone matches or exceeds the attention-based HOGA backbone in accuracy while training ~1.9× faster on pokec (2290 s vs 4293 s end-to-end).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pre-propagation GNN (PP-GNN) paradigm, where graph diffusion is performed once as preprocessing and training reduces to dense per-node transformations, by addressing two identified limitations: the conditioning of the diffusion basis and the one-shot nature of propagation. It adapts Jacobi-polynomial and channel-specific Lanczos/Krylov bases—drawing on ChebNet's polynomial spectral filtering and LanczosNet's Krylov approximation—to the PP-GNN preprocessing regime, departing from LanczosNet by constructing per-channel rather than shared Krylov subspaces. The hidden-state re-propagation (HRP) mechanism positions itself between one-shot PP-GNNs and fully iterative message-passing GNNs, inspired by ChebNet's observation that depth increases expressivity even with large per-layer receptive fields, while the RNN-based hop aggregator (GARNN) offers a lower-cost alternative to the multi-head attention used in HOGA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for closing the accuracy gap between PP-GNNs and MP-GNNs on heterophilic graphs (reducing up to an 11% gap) while preserving the dense-compute, mini-batch training efficiency that makes PP-GNNs attractive for large-scale deployment. The finding that re-propagating intermediate hidden states substantially outperforms label propagation suggests that task-adapted representations encode relational information beyond class probabilities, an insight that could inform other decoupled-training architectures. Open questions include whether the spectral calibration and HRP schedule generalize to dynamic or streaming graphs, whether the approach extends to link prediction or graph-level tasks, and whether the modest 2× training-time overhead from HRP remains acceptable at extreme scale (billions of nodes).
