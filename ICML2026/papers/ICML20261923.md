## Problem

Existing safety alignment pipelines lack a reward model that is simultaneously configurable at inference time to new safety specifications, fast (no multi-step deliberation), and calibrated to provide a dense, gradient-compatible reward signal for RL policy optimization. Static reward models require costly retrain-and-deploy cycles when policies change, while configurable judges (e.g., LlamaGuard, DynaGuard) produce sparse or poorly shaped outputs that cause over-refusal and fail as inner-loop RL rewards.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a tuple (x, r, p): a multi-turn conversation history x, the last agent response r to be scored, and a natural-language safety configuration p consisting of named categories each with a free-text guideline description that can be added, removed, or modified at inference time. For long configurations exceeding the 8K context window (e.g., DynaBench), BM25 retrieval selects the top-20 most relevant policy entries. The output is a verbalized safety label (safe/unsafe) plus a scalar reward in [0,1] derived from the log-odds ratio of summed safe-token versus unsafe-token probabilities, yielding a dense, calibrated signal rather than a binary decision.

## Method

CSRM fine-tunes an instruction-tuned LLM (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) with a joint classification-reward objective: a cross-entropy loss over verbalized safe/unsafe tokens for classification, and a Bradley-Terry pairwise loss comparing the model's log-odds under a strict versus a lenient safety configuration with a margin parameter m. Two targeted data augmentations drive generalization and calibration without additional human annotation: (1) Configurable Safety Configuration Augmentation uses a reasoning model to propose conversation-specific positive and negative categories, creating two-sided augmented configurations that flip safe/unsafe labels; (2) Strictness Augmentation discovers subcategories per safety category, orders them by severity via iterative LLM selection, rewrites guideline descriptions to selectively allow/disallow severity prefixes, and retains only pairs whose dominance is confirmed by a one-sided Clopper-Pearson lower bound exceeding 0.95. Calibration is induced implicitly through these statistically validated severity pairs rather than via a separate calibration loss, and the resulting reward geometry supports both offline (Reward Distillation) and online (REINFORCE++) policy optimization.

## Results

CSRM achieves state-of-the-art F1 on both configurable safety benchmarks: 95.3% on CoSApien and 75.8% on DynaBench, outperforming all baselines on DynaBench (next best: Llama-3.1-8B-Inst at 73.4%). CSRM also attains the best WildGuardMix F1 (94.6%) and the lowest smECE on BeaverTails (0.017), indicating superior calibration. In downstream RL alignment (Table 5), CSRM achieves the highest CoSA score in 4 out of 5 domains under both Reward Distillation and REINFORCE++, with the largest gains in helpfulness (e.g., 4.750 vs. 3.800 on Arab Publisher under REINFORCE++), confirming a better safety-helpfulness tradeoff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSRM outperforms all six baselines on DynaBench F1 (0.758), with the closest competitor being Llama-3.1-8B-Inst at 0.734 and DynaGuard-8B-CoT at 0.731; LlamaGuard-3-8B is dramatically weaker here (0.131), reflecting its inability to adapt to novel configurations. On CoSApien, CSRM ties OSS-Safeguard-20B-High at 0.953 F1 but achieves substantially better calibration (smECE 0.047 vs. 0.076), while ShieldGemma-9B (0.951) and LlamaGuard-3-8B (0.950) trail slightly. CSRM dominates on WildGuardMix with the highest F1 (0.946) and AUPRC (0.987), surpassing ShieldGemma-9B (0.869 F1) and LlamaGuard-3-8B (0.840 F1). On BeaverTails, Qwen3-30B-Thinking achieves a marginally higher F1 (0.931 vs. 0.911), but CSRM's smECE of 0.017 is an order of magnitude better than the next-best (0.049 from CSRM without severity augmentation), confirming that the calibration gains are the distinguishing advantage. In downstream RL alignment, CSRM achieves the best CoSA in 4/5 domains under both optimization methods, with the clearest wins over Llama-3.1-8B-Inst (which over-refuses, dropping helpfulness to 3.044–3.800) and LlamaGuard-3-8B (which under-explores, yielding lower helpfulness across all domains).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the configurable safety guardrail paradigm established by LlamaGuard (Inan et al., 2023) and ShieldGemma (Zeng et al., 2024), adopting their category-guideline configuration structure and extending LlamaGuard's random category-dropping augmentation into a two-sided, conversation-specific procedure. It departs from both the static reward models of standard RLHF (which freeze safety knowledge in parameters) and the configurable judges of DynaGuard/MetaSC (which produce binary or reasoning-based outputs ill-suited for policy optimization) by unifying inference-time configurability with a dense, calibrated scalar reward within a single generative framework. The joint classification-reward training objective with Bradley-Terry-style severity pairs, filtered via Clopper-Pearson confidence bounds, is the key methodological departure from prior guardrail training recipes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSRM addresses a concrete operational gap: safety policies evolve faster than retrain-and-deploy cycles, and existing configurable judges cannot serve as inner-loop RL rewards due to sparse or poorly calibrated outputs. The clean ablation decomposition—configuration augmentation driving generalization to unseen policies while severity augmentation driving calibration and pairwise reward quality—gives practitioners a principled recipe for building configurable RMs without additional human annotation. Open questions include whether the LLM-generated augmentation pipeline inherits systematic biases (acknowledged in the LOO/Allow probes), whether configurable rewards are vulnerable to strategic exploitation under long-horizon online optimization (no reward-hacking study performed), and whether the approach generalizes beyond the single 8B backbone and English-language settings evaluated here.
