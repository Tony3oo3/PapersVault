## Problem

Conventional uniform frame sampling in long-video understanding often misses query-relevant visual evidence, degrading accuracy and increasing hallucinations. While recent agentic thinking-with-videos methods adopt a localize-clip-answer pipeline, they suffer from workflow complexity (multiple specialized models), imprecise grounding with no refinement mechanism, and rigid single-crop pipelines that waste computation on short videos while lacking iterative correction for long ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262920/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a video (uniformly sampled at up to 512 frames, 224×224 pixels, max 2 FPS) and a text question. It first skims the video at a low rate, then iteratively produces reasoning traces followed by either a temporal interval (triggering a dense-sampling crop of up to 64 frames at higher rate) or a final answer, with up to 3 conversational turns. The output is a structured multi-turn trajectory containing thinking tokens, tool-call parameters specifying [start, end] timestamps, and a final answer in a designated format, supporting both VideoQA and pure temporal grounding tasks.

## Method

VideoTemp-o3 unifies temporal grounding and VideoQA within a single Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone trained in two stages. The cold-start SFT stage introduces a unified masking strategy that supervises only the penultimate turn (correct temporal interval) and final turn (answer) of multi-turn trajectories, masking earlier exploratory turns to prevent noise from imprecise initial groundings from corrupting gradients. The subsequent RL stage uses GRPO with a three-component reward: binary accuracy, format compliance, and a penalty-aware IoU reward that subtracts a penalty λ when predicted interval IoU falls below threshold σ, specifically designed to prevent the model from hacking the reward by localizing arbitrarily. A dedicated data pipeline constructs high-quality multi-turn trajectories using Gemini-2.5-Pro for grounding with closed-loop verification (the cropped segment must be sufficient to answer the question, and the full-context reasoning must remain consistent), applied only to videos longer than 3 minutes to ensure iterative refinement is genuinely necessary.

## Results

VideoTemp-o3-7B-RL achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source 7B models on long video understanding, scoring 54.2 on MLVU, 53.2 on VideoMMMU, 64.5 on VideoMME (w/o subtitle), and 43.0 on LVBench. On temporal grounding, it reaches 57.8 mIoU on Charades-STA and 45.3 mIoU on ActivityNet-MR, surpassing dedicated grounding models like TimeMaker-8B on Charades-STA. On video GQA, it attains 33.4 mIoU / 76.4% Acc on NextGQA and 29.5 mIoU / 74.4% Acc on ReXTime, substantially outperforming prior RL-based video models on grounding precision (mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-source 7B baselines, VideoTemp-o3-7B-RL consistently outperforms all competitors: it gains +9.0 over VideoChat-R1-7B (45.2→54.2) and +6.2 over Video-R1-7B (48.0→54.2) on MLVU, and +2.4 / +3.1 over VideoMME and LVBench respectively. Against the stronger closed-source references, VideoTemp-o3-7B-RL surpasses GPT-4o on LVBench (43.0 vs 30.8) and Gemini-1.5-Pro on LVBench (43.0 vs 33.1), but trails both on VideoMME (64.5 vs 71.9 / 75.0) and VideoMMMU (53.2 vs 61.2 / 53.8). On temporal grounding (Table 2), VideoTemp-o3-7B-RL beats VideoChat-R1-7B on Charades-STA mIoU (57.8 vs 54.9) and ActivityNet-MR (45.3 vs 36.8 mIoU), and surpasses TimeMaker-8B on Charades-STA mIoU (57.8 vs 48.4), though TimeMaker retains an edge on ActivityNet-MR R@0.7 (33.0 vs 26.7). On video GQA (Table 3), the mIoU gains are most dramatic: VideoTemp-o3-7B-RL achieves 29.5 mIoU on ReXTime versus 8.7 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 10.3 for VideoChat-R1-7B, while also improving answer accuracy to 74.4%. The SFT-to-RL improvement is consistent across all benchmarks (≈4–5 points), confirming that the penalty-aware IoU reward and unified masking strategy contribute meaningfully beyond cold-start supervision alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'thinking-with-videos' paradigm (itself a temporal analogue of OpenAI-o3's thinking-with-images) by unifying temporal grounding and video QA within a single agentic model, departing from prior multi-agent or multi-stage pipelines like VideoExplorer, VITAL, REVISOR, and LongVT that rely on separate specialized modules. The training methodology builds on the SFT-RL hybrid paradigm established by VideoChat-R1 and Video-R1, but introduces two key departures: a unified masking strategy for multi-turn tool-call supervision (masking all but the final two turns to avoid noise from imprecise early groundings) and a penalty-aware IoU reward that explicitly discourages the reward hacking behavior observed with naive IoU rewards. On the data side, the paper's multi-turn trajectory construction pipeline using Gemini-2.5-Pro as a teacher with closed-loop verification is a practical contribution not present in prior agentic video works.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete bottleneck in long-video understanding—uniform frame sampling misses query-relevant evidence—and provides a practical, single-model solution that reduces the inference overhead of multi-agent systems. The VideoTemp-Bench benchmark (1,200 samples across four duration tiers) fills a gap in systematic evaluation of grounded QA as video length increases, revealing that grounding performance collapses dramatically beyond 10 minutes for most models. Key open questions remain: whether the approach scales to hour-long videos where initial skimming is inherently sparse, whether the sequential localize-clip-answer loop can be parallelized, and whether the reliance on strong teacher models (Gemini-2.5-Pro) for data construction limits reproducibility. The penalty-aware reward design is a practical contribution likely to be adopted by other RL-for-grounding efforts.
