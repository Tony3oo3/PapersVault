## Problem

State-space models underperform Transformers on in-context retrieval, and prior hybrid architectures bridge this gap by retaining attention heads at fixed layer-level patterns (e.g., every 4th layer), incurring 25–50% attention overhead. However, retrieval functionality is concentrated in a small subset of Gather-and-Aggregate heads, meaning most retained attention is functionally redundant and wastes KV-cache memory. The paper asks whether a targeted, retrieval-aware selection of heads can recover teacher performance with a fraction of the attention budget.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained Transformer (Llama-3.2-1B or Qwen-2.5-1.5B) and a synthetic KV-retrieval probe used solely for head ranking, then distills into a hybrid student trained on a 12B-token mixture of FineWeb-Edu and FineMath-4Plus. The output is a heterogeneous Transformer-SSM hybrid where only the top-k retrieval-critical attention heads (as few as 10 out of 512) are preserved and all remaining heads are replaced by DiscreteMamba2 recurrent components, yielding layers that are either pure SSM or mixed attention+SSM. A parameter-free normalization adapter aligns attention and SSM output statistics before the shared output projection, and the SSM state dimension can be independently reduced (e.g., 64→8) without significant performance loss.

## Method

Retrieval-aware distillation first scores every attention head in the teacher by individually ablating it on a synthetic KV-retrieval task and ranking heads by the resulting accuracy drop, identifying the Gather-and-Aggregate heads responsible for in-context retrieval. The student architecture retains only the top-k heads (k≈10, i.e., 2% of 512) unchanged and replaces all other heads with DiscreteMamba2 modules of matching head dimension, producing a sparse, non-uniform attention placement that clusters in specific layers (e.g., layers 4, 8, 9, 10 for Llama-3.2-1B). Training uses a modified MOHAWK pipeline: because the retained heads already encode the teacher's feature-space alignment, the matrix-orientation stage is skipped, and the student proceeds directly to hidden-state alignment (matching SSM block outputs to teacher block outputs) followed by end-to-end logit distillation. A zero-parameter adapter applies a static LayerNorm to attention outputs, rescaling them to the mean and variance of the parallel SSM branch before concatenation and the shared output projection. The key architectural insight is that once retrieval is offloaded to the few preserved attention heads, the SSM backbone no longer needs large recurrent states for memorization, allowing an 8× reduction in state dimension (64→8) with minimal degradation, yielding 5–6× total memory savings over heuristic hybrids.

## Results

Retaining just 10 retrieval-critical attention heads (2% of 512 in Llama-3.2-1B) recovers 95.0% of the teacher's retrieval-heavy coverage and 101.3% on knowledge-focused tasks; for Qwen-2.5-1.5B, 10 heads yield 96.4% retrieval coverage. Performance plateaus sharply after 10–20 heads, with diminishing returns beyond that. On memory, the retrieval-aware hybrid (d_state=8, 10 attn heads) uses 492 + 2.5L KB, versus 3,072 + 8L KB for a 25%-attention layer-wise baseline, yielding 5–6× savings at 4K sequence length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MOHAWK's fixed interleaving (Bick et al., 2024), which requires 128 heads (25% of layers) to reach 98.3% retrieval coverage, retrieval-aware distillation achieves 95.0% with only 10 heads—a 12.8× reduction in attention budget. Against Mamba-in-the-Llama's annealed interleaving (Wang et al., 2025b), the 25% variant (128 heads) reaches only 92.6% retrieval coverage, below the retrieval-aware 10-head model's 95.0%; even the 50% variant (256 heads, 99.7%) uses 25.6× more heads. Against the SSM-only Llamba baseline (Bick et al., 2025a, 0 attention heads), which scores only 49.2% retrieval coverage (SWDE 27.7%, KV-Ret 13.2%), adding just 10 G&A heads jumps coverage to 95.0% (SWDE 71.1%, KV-Ret 99.0%). On memory, the retrieval-aware hybrid uses 5.1× less than MOHAWK and 6.3× less than Mamba-in-the-Llama at L=4096, while matching or exceeding their retrieval accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the Gather-and-Aggregate (G&A) head identification from Bick et al. (2025b) into a concrete architecture-selection step for hybrid distillation, building on the MOHAWK distillation pipeline (Bick et al., 2024). It departs from prior hybrid distillation methods (Mamba-in-the-Llama, Zamba, Hybba) that place attention via fixed layer-level heuristics (uniform interleaving, annealed schedules) by instead selecting individual heads based on empirical retrieval sensitivity, shifting the focus from 'how to distill' to 'what to distill into.' The DiscreteMamba2 SSM replacement and the parameter-free adapter for distributional alignment are direct extensions of the MOHAWK toolkit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: retaining only 2% of attention heads (10 of 512) recovers >95% of teacher retrieval performance while enabling an 8× reduction in SSM state dimension, yielding 5–6× memory savings over comparable hybrids. This gives hybrid-model builders a clear, data-driven recipe for where to place attention rather than relying on fixed ratios. Key open questions include whether the same small head set suffices at scale (>3B), whether the synthetic KV-retrieval probe captures all retrieval sub-skills (e.g., multi-hop), and how to further compress the KV cache via cross-layer sharing during distillation.
