## Problem

Mueller Matrix Polar Decomposition (MMPD) recovers physically interpretable parameters (diattenuation, retardance, depolarization) from 4×4 Mueller matrix observations, but conventional iterative numerical inversion is computationally prohibitive for high-resolution polarimetric imaging. No standardized benchmark exists to evaluate deep surrogate models on this task across fidelity, physical consistency, robustness, and efficiency, and the problem has not been formally connected to the multimodal learning paradigm of modality fission.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261544/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a spatially-resolved 4×4 Mueller matrix image (16 channels at 256×256 pixels) acquired via wide-field transmissive polarimetry at 617 nm from bone tissue sections. The output consists of six per-pixel physical parameter maps: diattenuation magnitude D, depolarisation power Δ, linear retardance η, fast-axis orientation θ, optical rotation ψ, and total retardance R, each with known physical bounds (e.g., D, Δ ∈ [0,1]; θ, ψ ∈ [−π/2, π/2)). The benchmark includes 21,412 paired training samples plus specialized waveplate and multi-wavelength (610/650/690 nm) external test sets for cross-sample and spectral generalisation.

## Method

MMPD-Bench reframes Mueller matrix decomposition as a modality fission problem—disentangling a single entangled 4×4 observation into physically distinct subspaces—and benchmarks eight architectures across four families: state-space models (SwinUMamba, SegMamba), vision transformers (SwinUnet, FactFormer), conditional diffusion models (DDPM, DDIM with classifier-free guidance), and neural operators (FNO, UNO), each in small and base configurations. All models are trained with supervised learning (MAE loss for deterministic surrogates, MSE for diffusion forward noise) over 100 epochs on an A100 GPU. The evaluation protocol jointly assesses pixel-level fidelity (PSNR, SSIM), global statistical alignment (1D Wasserstein distance), physical consistency via retardance residuals (R² = η² + ψ²), robustness under injected Gaussian noise (σ = 0.1σ_pixel), and computational efficiency (runtime scaling, FLOPs, peak memory). A key finding is that deterministic surrogates (FactFormer, FNO, SwinUMamba) dominate quantitative metrology and physical consistency, while diffusion models exhibit superior robustness under noise but fail to preserve inter-channel algebraic constraints and angular phase structures.

## Results

On the MMPD-Bench benchmark (21,412 real-world Mueller matrix patches), deterministic surrogates dominate over generative models across all six polarimetric modalities. FactFormer-b achieves the highest PSNR for diattenuation (47.7 dB), depolarisation (57.7 dB), and optical rotation (52.5 dB), while UNO-b leads on fast-axis orientation (27.3 dB) and FNO-b on total retardance (47.9 dB). Diffusion models (DDPM/DDIM) trail by 20–30 dB in PSNR on most modalities, with DDIM-s scoring as low as 17.9 dB on diattenuation versus 48.8 dB for the best SSM. Under 10% Gaussian noise, smaller models degrade less (e.g., Transformer SU-s loses only 1.0% PSNR on D vs. 11.5% for SU-b), and diffusion models paradoxically improve (DDIM-s gains 82.1% PSNR on D) due to noise smoothing the peaked input distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among SSMs, SwinUMamba-b outperforms SegMamba-b on diattenuation (48.8 vs. 44.7 dB) and fast-axis orientation (24.8 vs. 15.6 dB), while SegMamba-b edges ahead on total retardance (47.2 vs. 47.8 dB is actually SU-b winning; Seg-b is 47.2). Among ViTs, FactFormer-b dominates SwinUnet-b across nearly all modalities (e.g., 57.7 vs. 49.6 dB on Δ, 52.5 vs. 50.5 dB on ψ), confirming that factorized attention better captures inter-channel physical coupling. Neural Operators (FNO-b, UNO-b) are the strongest on angular phase recovery: UNO-b achieves 27.3 dB on θ, surpassing all other architectures by ≥2.4 dB, while FNO-b reaches 25.9 dB. Diffusion models (DDPM-b, DDIM-b) are the clear losers in quantitative fidelity, scoring 20–30 dB below the best deterministic surrogates on most modalities (e.g., DDIM-b: 24.1 dB on D vs. 48.8 dB for SU-b), though DDPM-b recovers competitive ψ (51.1 dB) due to the weak signal in that channel. Under noise perturbation, FactFormer-b and SwinUnet-b show the most stable degradation (≤8.4% PSNR loss on D), while U-shaped Mamba and NO models degrade up to 49.8% on θ, and diffusion models paradoxically improve (negative degradation) on non-angular modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the long-established Mueller Matrix Polar Decomposition (Lu & Chipman, 1996) as a 'modality fission' problem in the multimodal learning paradigm, drawing on the fusion/fission formalism from representation learning (Vidal, 2011; Liang et al., 2024). While prior deep learning works have applied CNNs, ViTs, and diffusion models to individual polarimetric sub-tasks (Si et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2022; Moriconi et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2025), no unified benchmark with standardized evaluation existed. The paper's methodological lineage spans four architecture families (SSMs, ViTs, DMs, NOs) borrowed from general vision and scientific computing, with the notable departure of introducing Neural Operators (FNO, UNO) to MMPD for the first time, motivated by the analogy between polarimetric field transformations and operator learning between function spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first large-scale real-world dataset (21,412 Mueller matrix–decomposition pairs from bone tissue) and a multi-faceted evaluation protocol that goes beyond standard vision metrics to include physical consistency checks (retardance consistency, Wasserstein distance), filling a critical gap for trustworthy AI surrogates in computational optics. Key open questions include whether physics-informed constraints or data-efficient diffusion methods can close the physical-consistency gap that generative models exhibit, and whether the findings generalize beyond the single biological specimen type used here. The observation that the primary bottleneck for deployment is physical admissibility rather than computational throughput shifts the research agenda from pure speed to parameter-level correctness across coupled modalities.
