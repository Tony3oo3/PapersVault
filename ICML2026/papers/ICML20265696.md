## Problem

Existing learning-based PDE control policies parameterize actions with fixed-dimensional representations tied to a specific number of sensors, actuators, or agents, so any change in configuration requires complete retraining. This limitation is especially severe in multi-agent PDE control, where policies cannot scale across swarm sizes or degrade gracefully under partial agent failure. The paper asks whether a single policy can be trained on a small swarm and deployed zero-shot to arbitrarily larger or smaller populations.

## Input / Output

Each agent queries a shared DeepONet policy with a local spatial patch of the PDE error field (current state minus target, plus spatial derivatives computed on the mesh) restricted to its field of view, along with its 2D position coordinates. The policy outputs a scalar control intensity per agent, and for kinetic actuators additionally a velocity vector. Because the branch network compresses the local patch into a fixed-dimensional embedding and the trunk network encodes position, the input dimensionality is constant regardless of swarm size M, which is the architectural key to cardinality invariance.

## Method

CINOC parameterizes the decentralized control policy as a shared DeepONet neural operator (branch net on local field, trunk net on position, fusion net to action) and trains it end-to-end by backpropagating a tracking or moment-matching loss through a differentiable PDE solver (Discretize-then-Optimize). All M agents query the same parameter set, so coordination emerges implicitly through the PDE-governed environment (stigmergic coupling) rather than explicit communication. A key empirical discovery is that the effort-regularization term in the loss induces an emergent self-normalization: individual agent intensities scale as O(1/M), keeping aggregate forcing bounded as the population grows. The paper grounds this with a mean-field theorem showing that discrete policy gradients converge to the continuous operator gradient as M→∞, and validates cardinality invariance (up to 64× scaling in 2D density transport) across linear, nonlinear, chaotic, and turbulent PDEs.

## Results

CINOC achieves the best or near-best performance across all eight PDE control benchmarks. On the most challenging task (2D Kuramoto-Sivashinsky stabilization), CINOC drives system energy to 0.37±0.19, outperforming DPC (9.74±2.32) by a factor of ~26× and the best RL baseline MAPPO (0.50±0.24) by 26%. On trajectory tracking tasks, CINOC achieves MSE values of 1.2e-4 to 4.6e-5, surpassing the best RL baselines (MAPPO) by 1–2 orders of magnitude (e.g., 1.2e-4 vs. 0.004 on Heat 2D Obs). On density transport, CINOC matches DPC (MML 0.002) while outperforming all RL baselines (best: PPO at 0.005).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPC (Holl et al., 2020), CINOC achieves a dramatic 26× improvement on KS 2D stabilization (0.37 vs. 9.74 energy), a 2.2× improvement on Heat 2D with obstacles (1.2e-4 vs. 2.7e-4 MSE), and a marginal edge on Turbulence 2D (3.23 vs. 3.28 enstrophy); however, DPC retains a slight advantage on FKPP 1D (1.4e-4 vs. 4.6e-5) and Heat 1D (7.9e-5 vs. 2.9e-4) tracking, and the two methods tie on Density Control (both 0.002 MML). Against the RL baselines, CINOC dominates by 1–2 orders of magnitude on all tracking tasks (e.g., 1.2e-4 vs. MAPPO's 0.004 on Heat 2D Obs; 4.6e-5 vs. MAPPO's 0.004 on FKPP 1D) and on density transport (0.002 vs. PPO's 0.005). On stabilization, CINOC beats MAPPO on KS 2D (0.37 vs. 0.50) and MATD3 on Turbulence 2D (3.23 vs. 3.71), while tying MAPPO on KS 1D (both 0.13). The consistent pattern is that CINOC's operator-based formulation excels most on tasks requiring precise spatiotemporal coordination (tracking, chaotic stabilization), where fixed-dimensional RL representations struggle to generalize across agent configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of neural operator learning (DeepONet, FNO) and differentiable physics-based control (Holl et al., 2020), but departs from both in a key way: rather than using neural operators as dynamics surrogates or backstepping kernel approximators, it places the operator at the core of policy synthesis, mapping local state fields directly to continuous control functions. It explicitly contrasts with RL-based PDE control (Farahmand, Rabault, Pan et al.) and MARL (MAPPO, MATD3), which are constrained by fixed-dimensional state-action spaces and cannot transfer across swarm sizes without retraining. The mean-field gradient consistency theorem (Theorem 5.1) is a new theoretical contribution that connects discrete multi-agent policy gradients to a continuous operator limit, grounding the empirical cardinality invariance in functional-analytic convergence (Aubin-Lions, weak-* measure convergence).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cardinality invariance property—training on small swarms and deploying zero-shot on populations up to 64× larger—addresses a fundamental scalability bottleneck in PDE control that both classical methods and RL-based approaches cannot overcome. The emergent self-normalization mechanism (agents automatically scaling output as O(1/M) via effort regularization and stigmergic coupling through the PDE field, without explicit communication) is a conceptually important finding for multi-agent learning more broadly. Key limitations include the model-based requirement (a fully differentiable solver and known PDE are needed for training) and theoretical guarantees restricted to linear dissipative systems; extending the analysis to nonlinear/chaotic PDEs and scaling to 3D industrial flows are the most pressing open questions.
