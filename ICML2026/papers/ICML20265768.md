## Problem

While benign overfitting under adversarial training has been theoretically characterized for linear models and two-layer networks, no prior analysis exists for Vision Transformers, whose attention mechanism introduces fundamentally different training dynamics under perturbation. This paper asks whether adversarially trained ViTs can simultaneously achieve near-zero robust training loss and small robust test error, and under what conditions on signal-to-noise ratio, sample size, and perturbation budget this occurs.

## Input / Output

Each input sample consists of M token (patch) embeddings in R^d, where one token carries a class-relevant signal vector μ and the remaining M−1 tokens are Gaussian noise orthogonal to μ. Adversarial perturbations are applied per-token with an ℓ_p-norm budget τ (p ≥ 2), so the threat model constrains each patch independently rather than the full image globally. The model outputs a scalar logit for binary classification (±1 labels in theory, multi-class in experiments), and the theoretical contribution provides explicit upper bounds on both clean and robust test error as functions of d·SNR² and τ/‖μ‖₂.

## Method

The paper analyzes a simplified two-layer ViT (single self-attention head with Q, K, V projection matrices followed by a fixed linear readout) trained via full-batch gradient descent with PGD-generated adversarial examples. The key technical device is decomposing W_V into scalarized projections and W_Q, W_K into vectorized projections along fixed reference perturbation directions, enabling uniform tracking of signal and noise component growth across the entire perturbation manifold. This reveals three distinct regimes governed by the perturbation radius τ relative to ‖μ‖₂/log d_h: (1) small τ preserves attention structure and yields clean-like convergence, (2) moderate τ forces attention weights to remain near-uniform so the ViT degenerates into a linear model requiring M× more iterations and higher SNR, and (3) τ ≥ ‖μ‖₂ makes nontrivial robust accuracy impossible for any classifier. The benign overfitting condition N·SNR² = Ω(1) (or Ω(1/ε) in the degenerate regime) is validated empirically on synthetic data and real datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, Tiny-ImageNet) using both simplified and full ViT-Base architectures under PGD and APGD attacks.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) shows that under N·SNR² = Ω(1) and perturbation budget τ ≤ O(‖μ‖₂/log d_h), the adversarially trained two-layer ViT achieves robust training loss ≤ ε, clean test error ≤ exp(−C·d·SNR²), and robust test error ≤ exp(−C·d·SNR²(1−τ/‖μ‖₂)²). A matching lower bound (Theorem 4.3) establishes that when τ ≥ ‖μ‖₂, no classifier can achieve robust test error below 0.25. Empirically, a clear phase transition at N·SNR² = Ω(1) separating high-accuracy from low-accuracy regions is observed on synthetic data, MNIST, CIFAR-10, and Tiny-ImageNet under both PGD and APGD attacks, with robust accuracy consistently improving as both N and SNR increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a traditional method-vs-method comparison table; instead, the key comparison is between the ViT with functional attention (Case 1: small τ) and its degenerated linear-model equivalent (Case 2: moderate τ). The ViT with working attention requires only N·SNR² = Ω(1) for benign overfitting, whereas the degenerated linear model requires the stricter N·SNR² = Ω(1/ε), and converges M times slower (T scales by an extra factor of M). Compared to prior adversarial benign-overfitting results, the convergence time here is T ~ O(N·max{SNR², SNR⁻⁴}), significantly smaller than the T ~ O(d²/(‖μ‖₂²ε²)) reported by Wang et al. (2024) for CNNs in the over-parameterized regime. The analysis also avoids the assumption ‖μ‖₂ = Θ(d^r) for r ∈ (1/4, 1/2] required by Chen et al. (2023) for linear regression. Empirically, the phase-transition boundary predicted by theory is confirmed across MNIST, CIFAR-10, and Tiny-ImageNet under single-norm (ℓ₂) and multi-norm (ℓ₁, ℓ₂, ℓ∞) attacks, as well as with a realistic ViT-Base architecture, showing the results are not an artifact of the simplified two-layer model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to provide a theoretical analysis of benign overfitting under adversarial training for Vision Transformers, extending the line of work that established this phenomenon for linear regression (Chen et al., 2023) and two-layer neural networks (Wang et al., 2024) to the more complex attention-based architecture. It builds directly on Jiang et al. (2024), who analyzed benign overfitting in ViTs under clean training, by incorporating adversarial perturbations and revealing how the softmax attention mechanism responds differently to perturbation magnitudes than linear or activation-plus-linear layers. A key departure from prior work is that the analysis does not require the large signal norm assumption (‖μ‖ = Θ(d^r)) used by Chen et al., and it identifies a convergence time significantly smaller than that reported for CNN-based adversarial training by Wang et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of when adversarially trained ViTs can simultaneously interpolate training data and generalize robustly, yielding concrete practical guidelines (perturbation budget τ ≤ O(‖μ‖/log d_h), sufficient N·SNR²) that are validated across MNIST, CIFAR-10, and Tiny-ImageNet with multiple attack types. The discovery of three distinct perturbation regimes—particularly that moderate perturbations cause the attention mechanism to collapse into a uniform distribution, effectively reducing the ViT to a linear model—reveals a non-obvious failure mode that could inform architecture design and training strategies. However, the theoretical model is a simplified two-layer ViT with a specific multi-patch data distribution and small token count (M = Θ(1)), leaving open whether the three-regime structure and the derived bounds hold for deeper, multi-head ViTs with realistic patch counts and more complex data distributions.
