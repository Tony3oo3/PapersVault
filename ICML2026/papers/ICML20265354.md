## Problem

Directly training an LLM to model P(hypothesis|background) for scientific discovery is mathematically intractable: generating a valid hypothesis requires implicitly retrieving and composing k inspirations from a literature space of size N≈10⁷, yielding a combinatorial search space of O(N^k) (e.g., ~10²¹ for k=3). Prior work either focuses on inference-time methods or trains on external feedback signals, leaving the core generative reasoning process unaddressed. This paper provides the first theoretical analysis of this intractability barrier and the first training recipe that makes P(h|b) tractably and scalably learnable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a research background (a specific research question plus a survey of prior methods) and a global scientific literature corpus (~10⁷ papers, embedded via SPECTER2 and organized into a hierarchical K-means tree with branching factor 15) as the inspiration search space. It produces a structured hypothesis as a sequence of incremental 'Delta Hypotheses' (Δh), each mapped one-to-one to a retrieved inspiration and internally structured into three levels: Motivation (why this direction), Mechanism (why it can work), and Methodology (how it is implemented). At inference, the system also outputs the ranked list of retrieved inspiration papers with their retrieval ranks.

## Method

MOOSE-Star operationalizes a probabilistic decomposition of P(h|b) into a tri-stage Hierarchical MDP per step: Motivation Planning (O(1) directional guide), Inspiration Retrieval (hierarchical best-first search over a SPECTER2-embedded K-means tree, O(log N/M) in the best case), and Hypothesis Composition (generating Δh conditioned on the retrieved inspiration, O(M) within a bounded semantic tolerance radius). Four progressive innovations reduce complexity from O(N^k) to O(k·[1 + log(N_m/M) + M]): decomposed sequential training breaks the Cartesian product into k linear steps; bounded composition trains the HC module on noisy proxy inspirations within a similarity-stratified tolerance space to handle retrieval imperfections; hierarchical search replaces flat scanning with probability-guided top-down navigation using geometric-mean path scoring; and motivation planning prunes the effective search space from N to a motivation-aligned subspace N_m. Training uses teacher-based Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning (R1-Distilled-Qwen-32B as teacher) to fine-tune R1-Distilled-Qwen-7B on 152k retrieval and 97k composition examples from the TOMATO-Star dataset (108,717 decomposed papers, 38,400 GPU hours).

## Results

On inspiration retrieval (IR), MS-IR-7B achieves 54.37% accuracy, matching Gemini-3-Pro (54.89%) and exceeding GPT-4o (48.37%) and DeepSeek-R1 (45.11%), while the untrained base R1-Distilled-Qwen-7B scores only 28.42%. On hypothesis composition (HC) with ground-truth inspirations, the unified MS-7B model reaches a total M3 score of 5.02/12, substantially improving over the base model (4.05) but remaining below frontier models such as GPT-5.4 (7.82) and Claude-Sonnet-4.6 (7.28). In test-time scaling, MOOSE-Star completes all 109 test papers within ~6,000 inference calls, whereas brute-force sampling saturates at ~38.5% coverage after ~9,500 samples, with the gap widening sharply for multi-inspiration (k≥2) problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On inspiration retrieval, MS-IR-7B (54.37%) decisively outperforms the base R1-Distilled-Qwen-7B (28.42%) by +25.95 points, and also exceeds GPT-4o (48.37%), DeepSeek-R1 (45.11%), and Gemini-3-Flash (51.44%), reaching near-parity with Gemini-3-Pro (54.89%). On hypothesis composition, MS-7B (5.02/12) improves over the base model (4.05) by +0.97 but remains clearly below all frontier LLMs: GPT-5.4 (7.82), Claude-Sonnet-4.6 (7.28), Gemini-3-Pro (6.70), and DeepSeek-R1 (6.42), indicating that composition quality is still bottlenecked by the 7B base model's generative capacity. The paper also compares against brute-force end-to-end sampling: at k=3 inspirations, brute-force per-attempt pass rate collapses to 0.23% versus 25.75% for the decomposed HC subtask, and in cumulative test-time scaling, brute force saturates at ~38.5% paper coverage while MOOSE-Star achieves full coverage, with the gap most severe at k=3 (8% vs. near-complete). The unified MS-7B model preserves IR accuracy (54.34% vs. 54.37% for the dedicated MS-IR-7B) while gaining +0.34 M3 points on HC over the single-task MS-HC-7B, confirming that multi-task training provides a beneficial inductive bias without degrading retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the probabilistic decomposition of P(h|b) introduced in MOOSE-Chem (Yang et al., 2025b) for training rather than inference, and adapts the hierarchical search principle from MOOSE-Chem2 (Yang et al., 2025a) from the hypothesis space to the inspiration retrieval space. It departs from the dominant paradigm in LLM4SR training—where prior work (Weng et al., 2024; Behzadifar et al., 2025; Goel et al., 2025) relies on external feedback or reward signals—by directly modeling the generative reasoning process P(hypothesis|background) and providing the first theoretical analysis of why this is combinatorially intractable (O(N^k)). The dataset construction pipeline adapts ResearchBench (Liu et al., 2025b) but introduces stricter quality controls and a novel incremental-delta hypothesis representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clear theoretical and empirical case that end-to-end training of P(h|b) is ill-posed, and provides a concrete recipe (decomposed subtasks + hierarchical search + bounded composition + motivation planning) that reduces complexity from exponential to logarithmic in the best case. The release of TOMATO-Star (108,717 decomposed papers, ~38,400 GPU hours) is a major resource that lowers the barrier for the community to explore training-side discovery. Key limitations remain: hypothesis composition quality still lags frontier LLMs substantially (5.02 vs. 7.82 M3), retrieval is the primary bottleneck on temporally OOD data (only 20–29% top-100 success), and the framework requires a finite, pre-constructed inspiration corpus. Open questions include whether the learned 'logic of discovery' generalizes to truly novel cross-domain associations and whether the fixed-order and uniqueness assumptions hold for complex multi-inspiration discoveries.
