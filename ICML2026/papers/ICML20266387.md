## Problem

Current XAI evaluation practice reports explanation stability under a single attribution method and implicitly generalizes the finding to the model as a whole, despite well-documented disagreement across attribution paradigms. No prior work systematically quantifies how stability rankings across architectures reverse when the attribution method changes, leaving regulatory and deployment decisions resting on method-contingent rather than model-intrinsic properties.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 224×224 chest X-ray images (five classes: Normal, Pneumonia, Tuberculosis, COVID-19, Lung Opacity; 3,354 test samples with 4.2–35.8% class imbalance) and produces, for each image, a class label plus saliency maps from two attribution operators (LayerCAM and Grad-CAM++) applied to penultimate convolutional layers. The primary analytical output is a set of four semantic-drift metrics—overlap IoU, spatial displacement, pattern correlation, and concentration change—computed between transfer-learning (epoch 8) and fine-tuning (epoch 19) attribution maps, restricted to 2,430 true-positive-filtered samples and aggregated with inverse-frequency class weights.

## Method

Three ImageNet-pretrained CNNs (DenseNet201, ResNet50V2, InceptionV3) are trained in two phases—frozen-backbone transfer learning (epochs 1–10) followed by full fine-tuning (epochs 11–20)—on a five-class chest X-ray dataset. Attribution maps are generated at the transfer-learning plateau (epoch 8) and fine-tuning convergence (epoch 19) using two gradient-based methods with distinct mathematical objectives: LayerCAM (pixel-wise first-order gradients preserving spatial detail) and Grad-CAM++ (globally pooled higher-order adaptive weighting). A true-positive filter retains only samples correctly classified by all three architectures in both phases, isolating explanation evolution from prediction corrections. The core finding is a complete ranking reversal: LayerCAM places InceptionV3 first (IoU 0.777) while Grad-CAM++ places DenseNet201 first (IoU 0.690), with InceptionV3 suffering a 17.3% cross-method degradation and ResNet50V2 a 26.2% collapse, whereas DenseNet201 maintains only 1.3% variation—establishing explanation stability as an emergent property of the model-method pair rather than an intrinsic model trait.

## Results

All three architectures (DenseNet201, ResNet50V2, InceptionV3) achieve >99% AUC after fine-tuning (0.995–0.998), yet exhibit completely reversed explanation stability rankings depending on the attribution method. Under LayerCAM, InceptionV3 leads with IoU 0.777, followed by DenseNet201 (0.699) and ResNet50V2 (0.519); under Grad-CAM++, DenseNet201 leads (0.690), InceptionV3 drops to 0.643 (17.3% degradation), and ResNet50V2 collapses to 0.383 (26.2% degradation). DenseNet201 shows only 1.3% cross-method IoU variation, establishing it as the most method-robust architecture, while InceptionV3's 17.3% and ResNet50V2's 26.2% cross-method changes demonstrate severe method-dependency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three CNN architectures (DenseNet201, ResNet50V2, InceptionV3) under two attribution methods (LayerCAM and Grad-CAM++) on a five-class chest X-ray task. Under LayerCAM, InceptionV3 outperforms DenseNet201 by 11.2% IoU (0.777 vs. 0.699) and ResNet50V2 by 49.7% (0.777 vs. 0.519). Under Grad-CAM++, the ranking reverses: DenseNet201 now leads InceptionV3 (0.690 vs. 0.643, +7.3%), and ResNet50V2 suffers a catastrophic 26.2% IoU drop from its LayerCAM score. DenseNet201 is the only architecture maintaining cross-method consistency (1.3% IoU change), while InceptionV3's 17.3% degradation and ResNet50V2's 26.2% collapse demonstrate that single-method evaluation produces misleading architecture rankings. All pairwise architecture comparisons reach p < 0.001 significance, with InceptionV3's cross-method difference showing Cohen's d = 0.73 (medium-large effect) and ResNet50V2's showing d = 0.90 (large effect).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes the XAI disagreement literature (Krishna et al., 2024; Duan et al., 2024; Klein et al., 2024) and the no-free-lunch results for explanations (Han et al., 2022) into a concrete empirical argument: that single-method stability evaluation is scientifically invalid. It departs from aggregation-based solutions (Pirie et al., 2023; Schwarzschild et al., 2023; Kazmierczak et al., 2025) that treat method disagreement as a problem to resolve post-hoc, instead arguing that cross-method validation must be a prerequisite for any stability claim. The true-positive filtering protocol and the temporal comparison (transfer learning vs. fine-tuning) are methodological contributions not present in prior attribution stability studies, which typically evaluate a single training checkpoint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that architecture stability rankings completely reverse between LayerCAM and Grad-CAM++ despite equivalent >99% AUC has direct regulatory implications: FDA-cleared medical AI devices specifying one attribution method may produce fundamentally different clinical rationales if deployed with another. The identification of DenseNet201 as method-robust (1.3% cross-method variation) versus InceptionV3's 17.3% degradation offers a concrete, actionable criterion for architecture selection in explanation-critical deployment. Key limitations include evaluation of only two gradient-based methods and three CNN architectures on a single chest X-ray dataset; whether the ranking reversal generalizes to perturbation-based methods, Vision Transformers, or foundation models remains open. The paper also does not validate whether the observed stability differences correspond to clinically meaningful differences in explanation quality, as no radiologist ground-truth annotations are used.
