## Problem

LLM time series reasoning remains in its infancy due to three intertwined gaps: the absence of time series CoT training data with process-verifiable annotations, the lack of principled data scheduling for efficient RL training, and the reliance of existing RL methods on outcome-based rewards that ignore the validity of intermediate reasoning steps. Without structured, verifiable reasoning trajectories tailored to time series properties, models cannot learn to explicitly comprehend multi-scale temporal correlations before forming conclusions.

## Input / Output

The model receives a numeric time series sequence (ranging from ~256 to 720 time points depending on the task) embedded in a text prompt that specifies the task type and question. The output is a structured six-step reasoning trajectory—task intent identification, key attribute extraction, critical segment analysis, preliminary answer, backtracking/self-reflection, and summary—wrapped in <THINK> tags, followed by a final answer in <ANSWER> tags. Process-verifiable labels are extracted at specific steps (target entity, task-specific attribute set, intermediate and final answers) to provide fine-grained supervision signals for RL.

## Method

VeriTime comprises three components: (1) TSRgen, an automated data synthesis pipeline that uses DeepSeek-R1 as an expert model to generate TS-tailored six-step CoT trajectories, with a rule-based extractor deriving process-verifiable labels and cross-LLM validation (Qwen3-235B, GPT-5, Gemini-2.5) ensuring quality; (2) a difficulty-hierarchy data scheduling mechanism where knowledge-based tasks are designated hard, scenario-based tasks are split by a warm-up model's inference accuracy into normal (SFT) and hard (RL) subsets, enabling selective rollout that allocates RL compute only to genuinely challenging samples; and (3) a two-stage RFT training procedure—SFT on normal-difficulty samples followed by GRPO on hard samples—using a multi-objective reward that combines structural rewards (format compliance, length), a hard reward (±5 for final answer correctness), and four process-level rewards (task comprehension, critical pattern identification, answer alignment, and answer verification) aggregated uniformly to supervise both the validity of intermediate steps and the final prediction.

## Results

On TSRBench scenario-based tasks, VeriTime with Qwen3-4B-Instruct achieves 86.55% overall accuracy, surpassing GPT-5 (83.49%) and Gemini-3-Pro (78.91%), while the smaller Qwen2.5-3B variant reaches 82.86%—competitive with GPT-5. On knowledge-based tasks, VeriTime (Qwen3-4B) outperforms GPT-5 across all four domains (e.g., CTU: 67.50% vs. 50.00%; RCW: 63.59% vs. 53.64%). The framework also reduces average inference token count by ~71% compared to the base model while improving accuracy, and achieves an average improvement of over 35% across all tasks relative to base models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VeriTime (Qwen3-4B) outperforms all time-series-specific LLM baselines by a wide margin: TimeOmni-1 (67.66% overall), Time-MQA (≤53.66%), and both Time-R1 variants (≤51.73%) on scenario-based tasks, with the largest gaps on inferential calculation (77.14% vs. 70.48% for TimeOmni-1). Against general LLMs, VeriTime (Qwen2.5-3B) matches GPT-5 on anomaly detection (90.56% vs. 90.56%) and exceeds it on scenario attribution (83.52% vs. 78.98%), while VeriTime (Qwen3-4B) surpasses GPT-5 on overall accuracy (86.55% vs. 83.49%). On knowledge-based tasks, VeriTime (Qwen3-4B) beats GPT-5 on all four domains (CTU 67.50 vs. 50.00, ECG 30.30 vs. 25.00, EMG 65.31 vs. 58.33, RCW 63.59 vs. 53.64) and also outperforms the best classical TS models (e.g., TimesNet at 73.33% on EMG is the only case where a classical model edges out VeriTime). Compared to ChatTS SFT (78.31% overall on Qwen2.5), VeriTime adds +4.55% overall and a substantial +14.29% on inferential calculation, demonstrating the value of process-verifiable CoT over concise Q&A pairs. In the architecture-agnostic study (Table 10), VeriTime improves OpenTSLM from 28.12% to 46.22% and ITFormer-3B from 36.43% to 44.03% on TimeSeriesExam, confirming gains are not tied to a specific backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the emerging line of work on LLM-based time series reasoning (ChatTS, Time-MQA, Time-R1, TimeOmni) by introducing process-level supervision that prior methods lack. While those approaches rely on supervised fine-tuning or outcome-only RL rewards, VeriTime departs by synthesizing TS-tailored Chain-of-Thought trajectories with verifiable intermediate-step annotations (via DeepSeek-R1 as a teacher) and designing multi-objective GRPO rewards that explicitly score task comprehension, pattern identification, answer alignment, and self-verification. The data scheduling mechanism—routing samples to SFT or RL based on a warm-up model's difficulty assessment—draws on selective-rollout ideas from general LLM RL but is the first to apply such difficulty-hierarchy-based allocation specifically to time series reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The process-verifiable annotation paradigm and the finding that selective SFT-RL routing outperforms full RL on medium-difficulty TS tasks offer a practical recipe for efficient post-training of small TS-reasoning models, with the 71% token reduction showing that structured CoT is both more accurate and more efficient than unstructured generation. TSRBench, as the first TS reasoning dataset with multi-step verifiable process labels, provides a reusable resource for the community. Open questions include whether the process-reward design generalizes to tasks beyond the seven covered (e.g., long-horizon forecasting, multivariate causal inference), whether the approach scales to larger model families, and how well the synthesized CoT trajectories transfer to genuinely novel or adversarial time series patterns not seen during training.
