## Problem

Existing multimodal foundation model benchmarks predominantly evaluate environment-centric spatial reasoning (object-object relations, distance estimation) from a detached, third-person perspective, while largely overlooking observer-centric situated awareness — the ability to reason about one's own position, orientation, and motion relative to the surrounding environment. This gap leaves a foundational layer of human spatial cognition untested, despite its critical role in robotics, AR/VR, and embodied AI.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance consists of a sequence of egocentric video frames (sampled at 2 fps from Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 smart glasses recordings, with audio excluded) and a multiple-choice question. Models must produce a letter answer plus a reasoning trace in a structured JSON-like format; no bird's-eye view, global scene map, or external spatial representation is provided. For Spatial Memory tasks, two short clips recorded before and after a controlled environmental modification are concatenated into a single video.

## Method

SAW-BENCH is constructed around 46 pre-defined camera trajectories recorded by human participants wearing Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 glasses across 15 real-world scenes (10 outdoor, 5 indoor), yielding 786 videos and 2,071 QA pairs spanning six situated awareness tasks: Self-Localization, Relative Direction, Route Shape, Reverse Route Plan, Spatial Memory, and Spatial Affordance. Ground-truth answers are derived deterministically from the recording protocol (trajectory shape, starting/ending viewpoints) rather than open-ended video interpretation, with inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's κ = 0.987 for Relative Direction, 0.952 for Reverse Route Plan) ensuring label reliability. The evaluation protocol is zero-shot: 23 MFMs (17 open-source, 6 proprietary) are prompted with a system instruction framing the camera as the model's own body, and accuracy is extracted via regex with GPT-4o-mini fallback. The paper's empirical contribution is a systematic error analysis revealing four failure modes: conflation of egocentric camera rotation with translational movement, accuracy degradation as trajectory complexity increases, inability to maintain persistent object representations beyond the current field of view, and the finding that environment openness is not a reliable proxy for spatial reasoning difficulty.

## Results

On SAW-BENCH, the best-performing model, Gemini 3 Flash, achieves 53.89% overall accuracy, leaving a 37.66% gap compared to human performance (91.55%). The hardest task for models is Relative Direction (41.13% for Gemini 3 Flash vs. 89.39% for humans), while Route Shape is the most accessible (64.84% vs. 97.62%). All 23 evaluated MFMs fall well below human level, with the best open-source model (Qwen3-VL 235B-A22B) reaching only 41.40% overall. Both the Blind LLM and Socratic Model baselines (GPT-5.2) score 31.34%, barely above the random-chance level of 27.49%, confirming that visual access is essential for situated awareness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Proprietary MFMs consistently outperform open-source MFMs across all six tasks, with the largest gap on Reverse Route Plan (Gemini 3 Flash: 61.57% vs. Qwen3-VL 235B: 30.13%), a task requiring sustained tracking of intermediate movements. Gemini 3 Flash leads all models at 53.89% overall, followed by Gemini 2.5 Pro (50.80%) and Gemini 3 Pro (45.97%), while the best open-source model, Qwen3-VL 235B-A22B, reaches 41.40%. The Blind LLM and Socratic Model baselines (both GPT-5.2) tie at 31.34% overall, barely exceeding random chance (27.49%), demonstrating that high-level semantic captions are insufficient for situated awareness and that direct visual access is necessary. Notably, Qwen2.5-VL 32B outperforms the much larger Qwen3-VL 235B on Self-Localization (53.00% vs. 43.50%), suggesting that model scale does not monotonically improve observer-centric reasoning. The Socratic model shows a slight edge over the Blind LLM on Route Shape (41.58% vs. 36.63%), indicating that video captions can convey coarse trajectory information, but this advantage does not generalize to other tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing spatial reasoning from an environment-centric (allocentric) to an observer-centric (egocentric) perspective. Prior spatial reasoning benchmarks (VSI-Bench, MindCube, SpaCE-10) and 3D grounding datasets (SQA3D, ReferIt3D, EmbodiedScan) evaluate models as detached spectators reasoning about object-object relations or static poses in reconstructed scenes. SAW-BENCH departs from both by using raw egocentric video from Ray-Ban Meta glasses and defining 'situation' dynamically through the observer's ongoing trajectory rather than a fixed pose, thereby probing path integration, self-localization, and action feasibility as continuous, embodied processes rather than discrete scene-level queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 37.66% human-model gap and the systematic failure to distinguish camera rotation from translation reveal that current MFMs lack a stable observer-centric coordinate frame — a prerequisite for robotics, AR/VR, and any embodied agent. The finding that accuracy collapses with trajectory complexity (e.g., Qwen3-VL 235B drops from 90% to 8.6% with a single turn) mirrors known path-integration error accumulation in cognitive science, suggesting that scaling model size alone may not resolve this. Open questions include whether explicit pose estimation or world-model pretraining can close the gap, and whether the benchmark's constrained 15-scene, predefined-trajectory design generalizes to truly open-ended navigation.
