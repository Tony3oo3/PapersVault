## Problem

Current emotion-oriented OmniMLLMs lack reliable omni-modal perception: they underutilize multimodal cues in their reasoning trajectories (capturing only ~50% of human-annotated evidence) and exhibit unfaithful behavior by hallucinating modality-specific statements from other modalities (e.g., asserting a visual frown based solely on an angry audio tone). Existing RL methods for MLLMs reinforce text reasoning but do not explicitly optimize whether the model's reasoning is grounded in the correct sensory modality.

## Input / Output

The model receives a multimodal input X = {V, A, T} consisting of a video clip, an audio stream, and a text/subtitle transcript. The output is a structured generation constrained to a reasoning chain wrapped in <think>... tags followed by an open-vocabulary emotion prediction in <answer>...</answer> tags, where the prediction is a free-form set of emotion words evaluated via an Emotion Wheel–based set-level F1 metric rather than exact string matching.

## Method

OPPO augments a GRPO-style RL pipeline (after a 5k-sample SFT warm-start on MER-Caption+) with two perception-specific components. First, the Omni-Perception Reward decomposes ground-truth reasoning into fine-grained visual, acoustic, and emotion cues via GPT-5 extraction, segments the model's generated thinking into clauses, and computes an Evidence-Routing Matrix of cosine similarities (using Qwen3-Embedding) to reward the fraction of annotated cues semantically covered by at least one clause. Second, the Omni-Perception Loss constructs unimodally masked counterfactual inputs (zeroing 70% of one modality), identifies modality-specific evidence tokens via the same cue-matching mechanism, and applies a KL-divergence penalty only to those tokens—forcing the model to retract modality-specific claims when the source modality is absent, unlike PAPO's indiscriminate all-token KL constraint. An information-theoretic analysis shows the loss expands the conditional mutual information channel I(T_m; m | X_m̃) that upper-bounds the reward, explaining their synergistic optimization: a sufficient loss weight (β ≥ 8e-3) raises the information ceiling, enabling the sparse reward to drive cue retrieval effectively.

## Results

OPPO achieves a state-of-the-art mean score of 81.05% on MER-UniBench (aggregating 9 datasets across sentiment analysis, basic emotion recognition, and fine-grained emotion recognition), outperforming the reproduced AffectGPT-R1 baseline (75.60%) by 5.45 points. On MME-Emotion, OPPO attains the best overall CoT score of 49.5% (vs. 45.3% for the internal baseline and 38.6% for AffectGPT), with a mean reasoning score of 68.1%. On the newly introduced MEP-Bench, OPPO boosts multimodal cue recall from 58.00% (baseline) to 70.44% and improves unimodal-masking faithfulness accuracy to 76.40% (video) and 50.60% (audio), substantially closing the gap to the oracle reference (80.17% recall).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPPO outperforms all 13 baselines on the MER-UniBench mean score. Against the strongest prior method, AffectGPT-R1, OPPO gains +5.45 points over the reproduced version (81.05 vs. 75.60) and +1.07 over the originally reported numbers (81.05 vs. 79.98), despite the latter benefiting from per-dataset checkpoint selection. On MME-Emotion, OPPO improves the mean CoT score from 45.3% (internal baseline) to 49.5%, and from 38.6% (AffectGPT) to 49.5%, with the largest relative gain in reasoning (62.7% → 68.1%). On MEP-Bench, OPPO surpasses all baselines on both utilization and faithfulness: cue recall rises from 54.85% (AffectGPT-R1) and 53.00% (Qwen2.5-Omni) to 70.44%, while video-masking accuracy jumps from 54.20% to 76.40% and audio-masking accuracy from 30.00% to 50.60%. Even the oracle-level HumanOmni-V2 (80.17% recall) is outperformed on faithfulness (48.40%/37.60% vs. 76.40%/50.60%), indicating that high recall alone does not guarantee grounded perception. Among non-emotion-specific MLLMs (SALMONN, mPLUG-Owl, Emotion-LLaMA, R1-Omni), OPPO leads by 16–33 points on the MER-UniBench mean, confirming that task-specific RL with perception-aware optimization is critical for multimodal emotion reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based reinforcement learning pipeline for Multimodal Emotion Reasoning (established by AffectGPT-R1) by introducing two targeted modifications: an embedding-based Omni-Perception Reward that measures fine-grained cue coverage in the reasoning chain, and an Omni-Perception Loss that applies KL divergence only to modality-specific evidence tokens identified via cue matching, rather than uniformly to all tokens as in PAPO. The key conceptual departure from PAPO is the shift from indiscriminate global masking penalties to targeted supervision on tokens that semantically correspond to the masked modality, which the authors argue prevents optimization instability and allows the model to satisfy the constraint by globally perturbing unrelated tokens. The paper also introduces MEP-Bench, a POPE-style diagnostic benchmark for quantifying utilization and faithfulness, and provides an information-theoretic analysis showing the loss is a variational upper bound on conditional mutual information between evidence tokens and the source modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that current emotion Omni-MLLMs recall only ~50% of human-annotated multimodal cues and achieve 26–54% accuracy on unimodal-masking faithfulness probes quantifies a previously underappreciated failure mode in affective computing. The information-theoretic framing—that the Omni-Perception Loss expands the faithful information channel that upper-bounds the reward—provides a principled explanation for why the two components must be jointly optimized and why the loss weight β must exceed a threshold before the reward becomes effective. However, faithfulness scores remain moderate (50.60% on audio probes), the approach is validated only on the Qwen2.5-Omni backbone for emotion tasks, and it is unclear whether the embedding-based cue-matching mechanism generalizes to other multimodal reasoning domains or to modalities beyond video and audio.
