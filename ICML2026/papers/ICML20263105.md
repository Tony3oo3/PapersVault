## Problem

Iterative generative policies (diffusion/flow matching) offer superior expressivity for continuous control but are incompatible with standard Maximum Entropy RL, which requires the policy log-density log π(a|s) to regulate stochasticity. For these implicit policies, the action distribution is defined only through a multi-step generation procedure, making log π(a|s) intractable to compute. Existing work patches this gap with auxiliary density-estimation networks or tractable distributional proxies, adding complexity and often yielding suboptimal exploration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous environment state vector s ∈ R^{d_s} (e.g., 223-dim for DMC Dog, 51-dim for HumanoidBench H1) and produces a continuous action vector a ∈ R^{d_a} (e.g., 38-dim or 19-dim). The action is not output directly but generated by integrating a learned velocity field u_θ(s, τ, X_τ) over τ ∈ [0,1] via a numerical solver (Midpoint Euler, NFE=2), transporting a base noise sample X_0 ~ N(0,I) to the terminal action a = X_1. The policy's stochasticity is regulated entirely through the kinetic energy of the velocity field, requiring no explicit density evaluation at any point during training or inference.

## Method

FLAC reframes entropy-regularized policy optimization as a One-Ended Generalized Schrödinger Bridge problem: find a path measure that minimizes divergence from a high-entropy reference process (Brownian motion) plus a terminal potential encoding negative Q-values, subject only to a fixed initial distribution. The key theoretical insight is that the kinetic energy of the velocity field (expected ∫½‖u_θ‖² dτ) equals the path-space KL divergence from the reference (via Girsanov's theorem) and upper-bounds the terminal action distribution's divergence from the prior (via the Data Processing Inequality), making it a sufficient, computable proxy for entropy regularization without density estimation. Practically, FLAC derives an energy-regularized Bellman operator (subtracting α·E_π from the backup) and an off-policy actor-critic algorithm where the actor minimizes α·Ê_θ(s) − Q_ψ(s,a) via pathwise gradients through the differentiable solver, while the critic learns the regularized value function. A Lagrangian dual mechanism automatically tunes the penalty α against a target energy budget E_tgt = C·dim(A), dynamically relaxing constraints early in training and tightening them later to prevent mode collapse.

## Results

FLAC is evaluated on high-dimensional continuous control tasks from DMControl (Dog: s∈ℝ²²³, a∈ℝ³⁸; Humanoid: s∈ℝ⁶⁷, a∈ℝ²⁴) and HumanoidBench (Unitree H1: obs∈ℝ⁵¹⁻⁶⁴, a∈ℝ¹⁹). The paper reports learning curves (Figure 2) rather than a single numerical summary table, showing that FLAC consistently matches or exceeds all model-free baselines across all 19 tasks. Notably, FLAC achieves these results using only N=2 function evaluations (NFE) per action, compared to N=16 for DIME and N=4 for SAC-Flow, while also forgoing the cross Q-learning enhancement that DIME relies on. Against the model-based reference TD-MPC2, FLAC attains comparable asymptotic returns within a purely model-free framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLAC is compared against six baselines: DIME, FlowRL, SAC-FLOW, SAC, TD7, and TD-MPC2. Against the strongest diffusion/flow-based competitors, FLAC achieves superior or comparable asymptotic performance to DIME (Celik et al., 2025) and SAC-Flow (Zhang et al., 2025) while using substantially fewer solver steps (N=2 vs. N=16 and N=4, respectively) and without the cross Q-learning trick that DIME employs. Against standard Gaussian-policy methods (SAC, TD7), FLAC matches or exceeds performance, demonstrating that the kinetic-energy-regularized flow policy is at least as effective as well-tuned unimodal actors. Against the model-based TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2023), FLAC reaches comparable asymptotic returns, though the paper notes model-based and model-free methods are not directly comparable due to different assumptions. The ablation in Figure 3 further shows that FLAC's automatic Lagrangian tuning of the energy penalty outperforms fixed regularization coefficients, and the method is robust to the target energy budget E_tgt across a wide range (C ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 2.5}).

## Relation to Prior Work

FLAC reframes maximum-entropy RL with iterative generative policies as a Generalized Schrödinger Bridge (GSB) problem (Liu et al., 2024), where the kinetic energy of the velocity field (from the flow-matching framework of Lipman et al., 2022) serves as a likelihood-free proxy for policy entropy. This departs from prior approaches like DIME (Celik et al., 2025), SAC-FLOW (Zhang et al., 2025), and FlowRL (Lv et al., 2025), which require auxiliary density estimation or variational surrogates to regulate stochasticity in diffusion/flow policies. The actor-critic structure and automatic Lagrangian tuning mechanism are directly inspired by SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018), but the entropy term is replaced by a path-space kinetic energy cost grounded in the Benamou-Brenier optimal transport formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GSB perspective provides a theoretically clean justification for why penalizing kinetic energy is sufficient (not merely heuristic) to bound the divergence of the terminal action distribution from a high-entropy reference, eliminating the need for explicit log-density computation that has been a practical bottleneck for flow/diffusion policies in online RL. The automatic Lagrangian tuning and the NFE=2 efficiency (vs. NFE=16 for DIME) make the approach practical for high-dimensional control. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the isotropic energy regularization across all action dimensions, leaving open the question of anisotropic or state-dependent constraints for tasks requiring heterogeneous stochasticity across control channels. Whether the kinetic-energy proxy fully captures the information-theoretic entropy in the deterministic (ODE) limit remains an open theoretical question, as the paper itself notes the W2 bound is a geometric rather than strict entropy guarantee.
