## Problem

MLLMs suffer significant performance degradation under real-world visual corruptions (noise, compression, adverse weather), yet existing robustness methods are limited: black-box feature alignment lacks interpretability and cannot model the corruption process, while white-box text-based reasoning cannot restore lost pixel-level details. The paper asks whether MLLMs can explicitly recover corrupted visual content by themselves, establishing a more intrinsic form of robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264369/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a corrupted RGB image and a text query as input. During inference, it first generates a recovered clean image via a 50-step rectified-flow denoising loop in the latent space, then performs multimodal reasoning over an interleaved sequence of both the corrupted and recovered images followed by the query. The output is a text answer with an explicit reasoning chain that references both visual inputs, and the recovered image is also produced as a byproduct.

## Method

Robust-U1 builds on the unified MLLM BAGEL and trains it through three stages. Stage I performs supervised fine-tuning with a rectified-flow denoising objective in latent space, conditioning on the corrupted latent and a recovery prompt to learn the approximate inverse of the corruption mapping. Stage II applies Flow-GRPO reinforcement learning with a dual reward: a pixel-level SSIM structural reward and a semantic CLIP cosine-similarity reward, jointly optimizing for both structural fidelity and semantic correctness of the recovered image. Stage III trains the model to perform multimodal reasoning by jointly attending to both the corrupted and recovered images, learning to use the recovery for primary content understanding while consulting the corrupted image to resolve ambiguities. The key distinction from prior work is that the model actively reconstructs lost pixel-level visual information rather than merely describing corruption effects in text, and the dual-reward RL ensures the recovery is task-aligned for downstream reasoning rather than merely perceptually clean.

## Results

On R-Bench (real-world corruption), Robust-U1 achieves an overall score of 0.7398, substantially outperforming the strongest general MLLM baseline BAGEL (0.5770) and the prior robust SOTA Robust-R1 (0.5017). The advantage grows with corruption severity: at the highest degradation level, Robust-U1 scores 0.6768 (MCQ), 0.6934 (VQA), and 0.7640 (CAP) versus 0.5793/0.6150/0.4288 for BAGEL. On adversarial corruptions applied to standard VQA benchmarks, Robust-U1 scores 83.18 on MMMB at 100% corruption (vs. 78.48 for BAGEL and 75.35 for Robust-R1), with only a 1.57-point drop from clean to 100% corruption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Robust-U1 outperforms all 12 baselines across every task and corruption level on R-Bench. Against the strongest general MLLM (BAGEL, 0.5770 overall), Robust-U1 gains +0.1628 overall, with the largest gap on CAP at high corruption (0.7640 vs. 0.4288, a +0.3352 margin). Against the prior robust SOTA Robust-R1 (0.5017), the overall gain is +0.2381, and the advantage widens at higher corruption severities (e.g., VQA high: 0.6934 vs. 0.4980). On adversarial corruptions (Table 3), Robust-U1 leads BAGEL by +4.70 points on MMMB at 100% corruption (83.18 vs. 78.48) and by +14.34 points on MMStar (63.87 vs. 49.53 for Robust-R1). External restoration modules (DFPIR, EVSSM, MambaIRv2, BiLaLoRA) paired with Qwen2.5-VL-7B all underperform Robust-U1 by 0.19–0.28 overall on R-Bench, confirming that task-aligned internal recovery outperforms generic preprocessing pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established paradigms for MLLM corruption robustness: black-box feature alignment in the visual encoder (TeCoA, Robust CLIP, Robust LLaVA) and white-box textual reasoning chains that describe corruption semantically (Robust-R1). Instead, it introduces explicit visual self-recovery as the robustness mechanism, building on the unified understanding-and-generation architecture of BAGEL and the rectified-flow generative formulation, then refining recovery quality via Flow-GRPO reinforcement learning with dual pixel-level (SSIM) and semantic-level (CLIP) rewards. The key conceptual departure is that the model actively reconstructs lost pixel content and reasons jointly over both corrupted and recovered images, rather than compensating for corruption through feature-space alignment or text-only description.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that task-aligned visual self-recovery is a fundamentally different and more effective robustness mechanism than generic image restoration: an all-in-one restoration model paired with a strong 7B MLLM scores only 0.5511 on R-Bench versus 0.7398 for Robust-U1, and the PSNR-reasoning decoupling (SFT adds +6.5 dB PSNR but only +0.02 R-Bench, while RL adds ≤0.6 dB but ~+0.13 R-Bench) shows that recovery must preserve the semantic cues downstream reasoning depends on, not merely look cleaner. Key limitations include high inference latency (55 s vs. 1.8 s for a standard MLLM), dependence on paired corrupted-clean training data, and recovery quality bounded by the underlying generative model. Open questions include extending the paradigm to video, incorporating corruption-specific physical priors, and developing efficient recovery architectures that reduce the denoising step count.
