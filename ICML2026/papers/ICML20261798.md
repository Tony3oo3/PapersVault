## Problem

Test-time latent optimization with one-step generative models suffers from two practical failures: reward hacking, where the latent drifts away from white Gaussian noise and produces unrealistic artifacts, and slow convergence requiring hundreds of gradient steps per sample. Existing regularization-based fixes are soft penalties that offer no hard guarantee of noise-like properties, introduce a tuning hyperparameter λ, and can be gamed by the optimizer, leaving both efficiency and reliability unresolved.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a differentiable reward model (Aesthetic Score, PickScore, HPSv2, or ImageReward) as input, and produces a single RGB image from a one-step generative model (FLUX-schnell at 1024×1024, SDXL-Turbo, SANA-Sprint, or SD-Turbo). Optimization starts from a white Gaussian noise latent of dimension N=65,536 (FLUX) or N=16,384 (SDXL-Turbo) and runs for 200 or 50 Adam iterations respectively, with the reward evaluated via a single forward pass of the one-step model at each step. Held-out reward models serve as quality checks to confirm that gains in the target reward do not come at the cost of realism or prompt fidelity.

## Method

The core idea is to replace soft regularization with hard gradient preconditioning: at each step, the reward gradient is projected onto a white Gaussian noise feasible set before the Adam update, steering every step into a noise-aligned direction without any additional hyperparameter. The feasible set is constructed by first mapping the real latent x∈R^N to a compact complex spectral domain y∈C^{N/2} via a bijective map F that removes Hermitian redundancy in the DFT, then imposing blockwise ℓ1 and ℓ2 norm constraints on y that match the theoretical expectations under CN(0,1) (i.e., ‖y^(p)‖₁=√π/2·B and ‖y^(p)‖₂²=B for each block of size B=16). A closed-form projection onto this set is derived via KKT conditions on the intersection of ℓ1 and ℓ2 spheres per block, yielding an O(N log N) algorithm dominated by FFT and per-block sorting. In practice, the method also projects the latent vector itself onto the feasible set after each update, and the combined procedure adds only 0.04% overhead to the FLUX forward pass, enabling 30% fewer iterations to match state-of-the-art regularization baselines while substantially outperforming them in target reward.

## Results

On FLUX-schnell with Aesthetic Score as the target reward, the proposed gradient preconditioning reaches an Aesthetic Score of 8.908 after 200 iterations, substantially outperforming the best regularization-based baseline MPGR (7.133) while maintaining held-out PickScore at 0.220 (vs. 0.220 for MPGR). Crucially, with only 60 iterations (30% of the budget), the method already attains 7.120 in 69.7 seconds, matching the 200-iteration performance of all baselines (7.024–7.133) which require 232–255 seconds. Across all four reward models (Aesthetic Score, PickScore, HPSv2, ImageReward), the method forms the rightmost Pareto frontier in target-vs-held-out reward trade-offs, and the projection overhead accounts for only 0.04% of total runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five regularization-based baselines: ReNO (ℓ₂-norm penalty), PRNO (mean/covariance structure-aware penalty), MPGR (blockwise ℓ₁ spectral penalty), KL divergence, and Kurtosis regularization. The proposed gradient preconditioning decisively outperforms all baselines on the target reward: on Aesthetic Score it reaches 8.91 vs. 7.02–7.13 for the best baselines, a gap of roughly 1.8 points that no choice of regularization coefficient λ (swept over {0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0}) can close. On held-out metrics, the method matches or slightly exceeds baselines (e.g., HPSv2 0.2986 vs. MPGR's 0.2922; ImageReward 0.8548 vs. MPGR's 0.7538), confirming that the gains are not achieved by sacrificing realism. The efficiency advantage is the most striking: at 60 iterations the method matches MPGR's 200-iteration Aesthetic Score (7.12 vs. 7.13) in 69.7 s versus 235.5 s, a 3.4× speedup. The method also generalizes across architectures (SDXL-Turbo, SANA-Sprint, SD-Turbo) and at scale (1,000-prompt T2I-CompBench++ with PickScore: 0.2660 vs. MPGR's 0.2438), and does not suffer mode collapse (IS 21.10, Vendi 6.97, within variance of unoptimized baselines).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MPGR (Hwang et al., 2025), which proposed blockwise ℓ₁ spectral regularization (L_power) to preserve white Gaussian noise during test-time latent optimization, by converting that soft penalty into a hard feasible set with a closed-form projection operator. The projection algorithm itself draws on Liu et al. (2020) for projecting onto the intersection of ℓ₁ and ℓ₂ spheres, adapted to the compact spectral domain defined by a bijective mapping that removes Hermitian redundancy. Conceptually, the paper departs from the entire regularization-based lineage (ReNO, PRNO, MPGR, KL, Kurtosis) by replacing 'penalize deviation from noise' with 'project each gradient step onto a noise-compatible direction,' eliminating the regularization coefficient λ entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30% wall-clock time reduction (60 iterations matching 200-iteration baselines) and the elimination of hyperparameter tuning make test-time reward-guided generation substantially more practical for one-step models, which are increasingly the default for fast text-to-image inference. The hard-constraint formulation provides a principled guarantee against reward hacking that soft penalties cannot offer, and the O(N log N) projection adds negligible overhead (0.04% of runtime). Open questions include whether the blockwise spectral structure generalizes to multi-step diffusion/flow models where intermediate reward evaluation is unreliable, whether the approach extends to other modalities (video, 3D), and whether the fixed block size B=16 remains optimal as latent dimensions grow with next-generation architectures.
