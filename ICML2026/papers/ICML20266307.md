## Problem

Existing diffusion-based solvers for linear inverse problems either represent the prior implicitly (score-based, making density evaluation expensive) or rely on intractable likelihood approximations that introduce sampling biases. Regression-based conditional models avoid these issues but sacrifice flexibility, requiring retraining for each degradation operator. No prior method provides a single, normalized, explicit density model that handles the correlated (anisotropic) noise inherent to linear inverse problems while remaining flexible across degradation types.

## Input / Output

The model takes a noisy measurement y and a noise covariance Σ (derived from the degradation operator H and noise level σ via Σ = σ²H⁻¹(H⁻¹)ᵀ), restricted to diagonal matrices in either the spatial or frequency domain to keep the parameterization tractable. It produces posterior samples via anisotropic denoising, one-shot posterior means via an anisotropic Tweedie identity, explicitly normalized posterior densities p(y|Σ), and—uniquely—can estimate unknown degradation parameters (box size, noise level) by maximizing the learned log-likelihood. Experiments use 64×64 RGB images (CelebA, ImageNet) and 192×192 (AFHQ-Cat), with up to 1,000 NFEs at inference.

## Method

The core contribution is Anisotropic Covariance Score Matching (A-CSM), which extends the dual score-matching framework to anisotropic (colored) noise by jointly matching the energy gradient with respect to the noisy input y (data score, via anisotropic denoising score matching) and with respect to the covariance Σ (covariance score, via a novel regularizer derived from the Fokker-Planck equation). The key insight is the equivalence between linear inverse problems and denoising under correlated additive noise, which allows a single model trained over a family of covariances to solve diverse inverse problems without retraining. The architecture is a UNet with a novel anisotropic noise embedding that injects spatially-varying or frequency-domain covariance information through gain modulation at each layer, preserving the inductive biases of standard score networks. The A-CSM objective enforces mass conservation across the covariance space, guaranteeing that the learned energy U_θ(y,Σ) ≈ −log p(y|Σ) is properly normalized up to a constant, which is then fixed by matching to a Gaussian at large Σ. This explicit normalized density unlocks capabilities unavailable to score-based models: energy-guided adaptive covariance scheduling (via Bregman mirror descent on Σ), unbiased Metropolis-Hastings (MALA) corrector steps, and blind estimation of unknown degradation operators by maximizing the learned log-likelihood.

## Results

On CelebA 64×64 inpainting, the proposed anisotropic energy model achieves the best LPIPS (0.093), FID (34.57), and DISTS (0.14) among all baselines, while RED-Diff leads on PSNR (17.96 vs. 17.70). For CelebA deblurring, the method attains the lowest LPIPS (0.002), FID (41.87), and DISTS (0.04), again trailing RED-Diff on PSNR (32.27 vs. 34.53). On ImageNet 64×64, the energy model achieves the best FID for both inpainting (47.54) and deblurring (44.82), and the best DISTS for deblurring (0.07), while RED-Diff maintains a PSNR advantage in both tasks. Overall, the method consistently outperforms all baselines on perceptual metrics (LPIPS, FID, DISTS) while being competitive on PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed anisotropic energy model is compared against four baselines: DPS (guidance-based Bayesian), RED-Diff (variational inference), DAPS (decoupled noise annealing), and Palette (regression-based conditional diffusion). On CelebA, the energy model dominates on all perceptual metrics (LPIPS, FID, DISTS) for both inpainting and deblurring, with FID improvements of 2–11 points over the next-best method; however, RED-Diff consistently achieves 0.3–2.3 dB higher PSNR, reflecting its smoother (MAP-like) reconstructions. On ImageNet, the energy model achieves the best FID for both tasks (47.54 inpainting, 44.82 deblurring) and the best DISTS for deblurring (0.07), while DAPS edges it out on inpainting LPIPS (0.048 vs. 0.052) and RED-Diff retains a PSNR lead. Palette, the regression-based baseline, is substantially worse on all metrics (e.g., FID 75.22 vs. 34.57 on CelebA inpainting), confirming the flexibility advantage of the unified energy formulation. The paper also demonstrates that a single trained model handles both spatial (inpainting) and spectral (deblurring) degradations without retraining, a capability none of the baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the isotropic dual score matching framework of Guth et al. (2025) to handle anisotropic (colored) noise, which is the natural noise structure arising from linear inverse problems. While prior energy-based diffusion models (Du et al., 2023; Thornton et al., 2025) and recent normalization proposals (Yu et al., 2025; Plainer et al., 2025) were restricted to isotropic noise, the key departure here is the Anisotropic Covariance Score Matching (A-CSM) regularizer, which enforces Fokker-Planck consistency across a family of covariance matrices. The architecture builds on the UNet/EDM score network with a novel spatially-varying gain-modulation embedding for covariance conditioning, and the overall formulation unifies the Bayesian and regression perspectives on inverse problems by learning a single normalized posterior density over a shared prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The normalized energy formulation unlocks capabilities unavailable to score-based solvers: blind estimation of unknown degradation operators, unbiased MALA corrector steps, and energy-guided adaptive sampling schedules. However, the practical impact is tempered by the restriction to diagonal covariances (spatial or spectral), which covers standard tasks but limits the flexibility of energy-guided paths (as evidenced by the underperformance on CelebA inpainting). Open questions include extending to general covariance spaces, reducing the training cost of dual backpropagation (potentially via sliced score matching), and whether the framework scales to higher-resolution or video settings. The paper positions itself as a principled middle ground between flexible Bayesian methods and efficient regression approaches, and the competitive (not dominant) results suggest the field still has room for improvement in both directions.
