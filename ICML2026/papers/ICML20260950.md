## Problem

Diffusion and flow-based policies in online RL can model multi-modal action distributions that Gaussian policies cannot, but their iterative generative process requires 10–20 sampling steps, making training and inference substantially slower than one-step policies. The paper asks whether a few-step generative policy can retain the expressivity needed for effective exploration while closing the efficiency gap with conventional policies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous state vector from the environment (e.g., joint positions and velocities in MuJoCo/DM Control) and outputs a continuous action vector of matching dimensionality. Actions are generated by integrating a learned average-velocity ODE over only 2 discrete steps starting from a standard Gaussian prior. During evaluation, 10 candidate actions are generated and the one with the highest Q-value is selected. The policy also produces a log-likelihood estimate for each generated action via the average divergence network, which is consumed by the soft Bellman target and the importance-weight computation.

## Method

MFPO represents the policy as a MeanFlow model that learns the average velocity field (time-integrated instantaneous velocity divided by the interval) rather than the instantaneous velocity, so that a 2-step Euler integration of the average velocity approximates the full ODE trajectory with negligible discretization error. Two key technical components enable integration into soft policy iteration: (1) an Average Divergence Network (ADN) trained with the MeanFlow identity applied to the divergence operator, using the Skilling-Hutchinson trace estimator for unbiased divergence computation, which yields an efficient few-step action-likelihood estimate for the soft Bellman target; and (2) an Adaptive Instantaneous Velocity Estimation (AIVE) scheme that constructs the policy-improvement training target via self-normalized importance sampling from two complementary proposals—the current policy distribution and a Gaussian conditional—combined with ESS-weighted averaging to minimize estimation variance across all ODE times. The full algorithm alternates soft policy evaluation (distributional Q-learning with the ADN-based likelihood) and soft policy improvement (MeanFlow loss with the AIVE target) under automatic temperature tuning.

## Results

MFPO achieves comparable or superior performance to diffusion-based baselines across 5 MuJoCo locomotion tasks, 6 DeepMind Control Suite tasks, and 3 HumanoidBench tasks, while using only 2 sampling steps (vs. 16–20 for diffusion baselines). Inference latency is 0.46 ms per action, roughly 2–4× faster than DIME (0.97 ms), MaxEntDP (1.56 ms), and QVPO (1.68 ms). Training time is reduced by approximately 50% compared to diffusion-based policies, substantially narrowing the efficiency gap with one-step policies like SAC (0.15 ms) and TD3 (0.14 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFPO matches or exceeds the performance of all six diffusion/flow-based baselines (DIME, FlowRL, SAC-Flow, MaxEntDP, DACER, QVPO) on most MuJoCo, DMC, and HumanoidBench tasks while requiring only 2 sampling steps versus 16–20 for the diffusion methods. Against the classical one-step baselines SAC and TD3, MFPO achieves comparable or better returns on multi-modal tasks (e.g., HalfCheetah, Hopper) where Gaussian/deterministic policies struggle, at the cost of slightly higher inference latency (0.46 ms vs. 0.14–0.15 ms). FlowRL uses only 1 sampling step and has the lowest inference time (0.42 ms) among generative baselines, but MFPO achieves stronger performance with just 2 steps. In the supplementary comparison (Fig. 9), MFPO also outperforms MEow (Chao et al., 2024), an energy-based normalizing flow MaxEnt method, on MuJoCo benchmarks. Overall, MFPO's key advantage is achieving diffusion-level expressivity at near-one-step efficiency, reducing training time by ~50% relative to 16–20-step diffusion policies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper integrates MeanFlow models (Geng et al., 2025), a few-step flow-based generative architecture that models average velocity rather than instantaneous velocity, into the Maximum Entropy RL framework via soft policy iteration. It builds directly on the line of diffusion/flow-based policy optimization for online RL (DIME, MaxEntDP, SDAC, DACER, QVPO, FlowRL, SAC-Flow), but departs from them by replacing the 10–20 step iterative generation with a 2-step MeanFlow policy. Two novel components address challenges specific to this integration: an average divergence network (mirroring the MeanFlow identity for the divergence integral in the change-of-variables formula) for tractable action likelihood, and an ESS-weighted combination of two self-normalized importance sampling estimators for policy improvement when the Boltzmann target is intractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MFPO narrows the efficiency gap between expressive multi-modal policies and conventional one-step policies by roughly 50% in training time while matching or exceeding diffusion-based baselines on MuJoCo, DeepMind Control Suite, and HumanoidBench. The average divergence network and adaptive SNIS estimator are reusable components for any flow-based policy requiring likelihood evaluation under MaxEnt RL. The main limitation is that two sampling steps are still required for strong performance, and the authors note that reducing this to one step remains open. Broader impact extends to Vision-Language-Action systems for robotics, where the reduced inference latency (0.46 ms vs. 0.97–1.68 ms for diffusion baselines) enables faster and more responsive control.
