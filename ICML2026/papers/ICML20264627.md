## Problem

LIME's local explanations assume a single linear coefficient per feature captures local behavior, which fails when feature effects are heterogeneous or nonlinear within the neighborhood, and its LASSO-based feature selection is misaligned with the local effect estimation objective. These two coupled limitations jointly produce unfaithful and unstable explanations, particularly as local nonlinearity increases.

## Input / Output

Given a black-box model and a tabular instance to explain, PL-LIME generates locally perturbed samples (truncated Gaussian around the instance) and queries the black-box for predictions. It outputs a sparse set of feature-level piecewise linear shape functions (each with a single knot anchored at the explained instance, yielding two linear segments), nonnegative shrinkage coefficients that act as binary feature selectors, and a variance-based feature importance ranking. The explanation budget is fixed by the number of retained features k, selected along the group nonnegative garrote solution path.

## Method

PL-LIME refines locality by replacing LIME's single linear coefficient per feature with an instance-anchored piecewise linear function (one knot at the explained instance, two linear segments), adding one degree of freedom per feature as a minimal extension. Sparsity is enforced through a decoupled two-stage procedure: Stage 1 estimates all feature-level local effects via weighted least squares without sparsity constraints, and Stage 2 applies nonnegative group garrote shrinkage to scaling coefficients θ_i that multiplicatively scale the first-stage estimates, ensuring feature selection and effect estimation share the same surrogate objective. A sparse variance-based importance measure then quantifies relative feature contributions. The paper proves that under piecewise-linear true effects, LIME's coefficient is a kernel-weighted average of interval slopes (Proposition 4.1), and establishes monotonicity of recovered effect strength under nested surrogate spaces (Theorem 4.6), showing piecewise linear surrogates strictly dominate linear ones in capturing local effect structure.

## Results

On six UCI datasets with Random Forest black-box models, PL-LIME consistently achieves higher local fidelity (R_w²) than LIME across all feature counts and sparsity levels. Under low sparsity on the Adult dataset, feature ranking stability improves from 0.331 (LIME) to 0.500 (PL-LIME), a +51% gain; under medium sparsity on Breast Cancer, ranking stability rises from 0.458 to 0.553 (+20.7%). On the synthetic benchmark, as the nonlinearity parameter p_nonlinear increases, LIME's true feature recall and ranking consistency degrade substantially while PL-LIME remains robust. In the nonlinear surrogate comparison (Table 5), PL-LIME achieves 0.736 local fidelity vs. LIME's 0.560 on Breast Cancer/RF, with runtime of 10.3 min vs. MARS-LIME's 76.9 min.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LIME (the primary baseline), PL-LIME wins on local fidelity across all six datasets and both RF/XGBoost black-box models, and achieves consistently higher feature ranking stability, with the largest gains under low and medium sparsity (e.g., +51% on Adult low-sparsity, +20.7% on Breast Cancer medium-sparsity). Feature selection stability is comparable, with PL-LIME slightly ahead on most datasets. Against MARS-LIME (BMB-LIME without bootstrap), PL-LIME achieves slightly lower fidelity (0.736 vs. 0.760 on Breast Cancer/RF) but at a fraction of the runtime (10.3 vs. 76.9 min); under matched sparsity (10 features, Table 6), PL-LIME achieves 0.696 fidelity with perfect FS stability (1.000) in 22.1 s, versus MARS-LIME (deg=1) at 0.755 fidelity, 0.967 FS stability, and 46.1 s. Against TreeSHAP/KernelSHAP (auxiliary comparison on Bike Sharing), PL-LIME has lower rank stability than TreeSHAP (0.779 vs. 1.000) but competitive normalized Keep/Drop AUC, and runs substantially faster than KernelSHAP under neural-network black-box models.

## Relation to Prior Work

PL-LIME extends LIME (Ribeiro et al., 2016) by replacing its single linear surrogate per feature with an instance-anchored piecewise linear function (one knot at the explained instance), drawing on the truncated power basis spline representation. For sparsity, it departs from LIME's decoupled LASSO-path selection and from coupled approaches like GLIME (Li et al., 2023) by adopting a two-stage nonnegative garrote (Yuan & Lin, 2006) that shrinks already-estimated local effects, ensuring objective alignment between estimation and selection. The theoretical analysis connects to the L²-projection framework for surrogate fidelity and builds on prior identifications of LIME's instability (Zhang et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2021; Garreau & Luxburg, 2020), but reframes the root cause as a locality-definition problem rather than a perturbation or weighting issue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a minimal, interpretable upgrade to the most widely used local explanation method, with the piecewise linear shape functions offering qualitatively new information (directional changes, threshold effects) that linear surrogates structurally cannot capture. The decoupled objective-consistent sparsification design is a clean contribution applicable beyond this specific surrogate. Key limitations include the single-knot default (restricting expressiveness in highly nonlinear regions), moderate-dimensionality scope, and the observation that saturated neural-network predictions degrade all LIME-style methods. Open questions include adaptive knot placement, scaling to high-dimensional feature spaces, and whether the framework extends naturally to interaction terms or non-additive local structures.
