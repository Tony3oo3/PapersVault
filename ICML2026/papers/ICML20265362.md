## Problem

Existing fairness-aware classification methods rely on model constraints, data reweighting, or global interventions that limit interpretability, distort data distributions, or scale poorly to large datasets. Granular-ball computing (GBC) offers a structured, interpretable partitioning framework, but its potential for enforcing group fairness at the granular level remained unexplored. This paper asks whether the objectives of maximizing granule purity and satisfying local demographic fairness can be jointly optimized through structural constraints within a recursive GB generation process.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular datasets with numerical and categorical feature vectors, binary class labels, and a designated sensitive attribute (e.g., Gender, Sex, Race) whose group membership is known for every sample. Before processing, the input is augmented via Neighborhood Mixup to equalize sensitive-group sizes while preserving each group's conditional label distribution. The output is a set of fair granular balls, each characterized by a center, radius, majority label, purity, and sensitive-group balance statistics, from which test samples receive class labels via a prioritized coverage-based decision rule that weights balls by purity and structural balance.

## Method

FairGBFC operates in two stages. First, FairGBG applies Neighborhood Mixup to balance sensitive groups, then recursively partitions the augmented data using the proposed Group Aware Fair Clustering (GAFC) algorithm, which minimizes a joint objective of geometric compactness and a pairwise-squared demographic imbalance penalty via an EM scheme; an algebraic identity transforms the fairness term into per-group gradient potentials (V_{j,u} = m·n_{j,u} − |C_j|), enabling O(N·K·d) per-iteration complexity. The recursion splits impure balls (purity < τ) with an adaptively calibrated penalty λ_j proportional to the ball's geometric magnitude and demographic imbalance, terminating when all balls meet the purity threshold. Second, FairGBFC performs inference by assigning each test sample the label of the covering GB with the highest P_j·exp(−ε_j) score, or the nearest GB if uncovered. Theoretically, Theorem 3.2 proves that ΔLDP(GB_j) ≤ m_j(1−P_j) + ε_j/(2N_min,j), showing that increasing purity directly suppresses the local unfairness bound, while Theorem 3.3 derives a global ΔDP bound in terms of aggregated structural imbalances across all GBs.

## Results

On six benchmark datasets, FairGBFC achieves the best or near-best fairness (ΔDP, ΔEO) across all settings while maintaining competitive or superior accuracy. On the German dataset, FairGBFC reaches 71.6% ACC and 82.7% F1 while reducing ΔDP to 0.007 (94.2% lower than the runner-up) and ΔEO to 0.015 (92.2% lower). On the large-scale Adult dataset, it attains 81.2% ACC with ΔDP of 0.089 and ΔEO of 0.083, outperforming the next-best method by 52.2% and 44.7% on fairness respectively. On the Law dataset, FairGBFC achieves perfect fairness (ΔDP = ΔEO = 0.000) alongside the highest utility (94.9% ACC, 97.4% F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GB-based baselines (GBKNN, ACC-GBKNN, GB-KNN+), FairGBFC wins on every dataset for both utility and fairness; e.g., on German it improves ACC by 3.6% over GBKNN (0.716 vs. 0.691) while cutting ΔDP from 0.186 to 0.007. Against fairness-aware baselines, FairGBFC achieves the lowest ΔDP and ΔEO on 5 of 6 binary-attribute datasets (all except Por, where f-FERM has slightly higher ACC at 0.878 vs. 0.858 but much worse ΔEO at 0.193 vs. 0.016). On the multi-valued Race attribute (Adult), FairGBFC reduces ΔDP by 34.6% and ΔEO by 56.2% relative to the runner-up (Unbias), while also achieving the highest ACC (0.805) and F1 (0.489). CFA and GDebias degenerate into trivial classifiers on imbalanced datasets (Por, Adult) where they predict a single class, rendering their fairness metrics undefined. The Neighborhood Mixup augmentation ablation confirms that removing it increases ΔDP and ΔEO substantially, particularly on multi-group datasets like Law and Adult, though the GAFC-based partitioning alone still outperforms most fairness-aware baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Granular-Ball Computing (GBC) by embedding group fairness directly into the data partitioning process, rather than treating fairness as a model-level constraint (in-processing) or a data/prediction adjustment (pre/post-processing). It builds on the GBC framework of Xia et al. (2019) for recursive top-down splitting and on the Neighborhood Mixup strategy of Liu et al. (2024b) for demographic balancing, but introduces the novel GAFC algorithm that jointly optimizes geometric compactness and demographic equilibrium via an EM scheme. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant fairness literature by reframing fairness as a structural property of the data representation (balanced granular balls) rather than a constraint on the classifier, and provides a theoretical bridge (Theorem 3.2) showing that maximizing granule purity inherently suppresses local unfairness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers an interpretable, model-agnostic path to fair classification that avoids the opacity of fairness-constrained neural networks and the distributional distortion of reweighting-based pre-processing. However, its scope is limited to tabular data with binary labels and a small number of sensitive groups; the authors themselves note that extension to ultra-high-dimensional settings and integration of individual fairness remain open. The reliance on data augmentation to enforce global group balance also raises questions about how well the method generalizes when the original demographic imbalance reflects genuine population structure rather than sampling bias. For practitioners working on high-stakes tabular classification (credit, hiring, criminal justice), the method provides a practical and transparent alternative, but its applicability to deep learning pipelines or multi-class settings is unexplored.
