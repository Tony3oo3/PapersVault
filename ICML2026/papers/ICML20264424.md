## Problem

Existing probabilistic error bounds for kernel regression are largely restricted to (conditionally) independent sub-Gaussian noise and conflate exploration uncertainty with noise-induced uncertainty, leading to unnecessary conservatism. This paper addresses the gap by deriving non-asymptotic probabilistic uniform error bounds that are tailored to a broad hierarchy of noise distributions (sub-Gaussian, bounded, sub-exponential, variance-bounded) and extend to correlated noise, while explicitly separating the two sources of uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The method consumes t noisy input-output pairs (x_i, y_i) drawn from a compact domain X ⊂ R^{n_x}, a known positive definite kernel k, a known RKHS-norm bound B on the true function, noise distribution parameters (e.g., variance proxy σ_M², bound m̄, sub-exponential parameters ν_M and α_M), and a regularization parameter ϱ > 0. It outputs a kernel ridge regression point estimate μ_t(x) and a probabilistic uniform error bound η_t(x) = B·σ̃_t(x) + η_t^M(x) that holds simultaneously for all x ∈ X and all t ∈ N with probability at least 1−δ. The noise term η_t^M(x) is distribution-class-specific and depends on data-dependent quantities ‖h_t(x)‖_2 and ‖h_t(x)‖_∞ rather than solely on the GP posterior variance, which is the key structural difference from prior bounds.

## Method

The core framework (Lemma 3.1) decomposes the regression error |f(x) − μ_t(x)| into an exploration term B·σ̃_t(x) (derived via RKHS reproducing properties and Cauchy-Schwarz) and a noise term |M_t^T h_t(x)|, then applies distribution-specific concentration inequalities directly to the decision-dependent noise term rather than using Cauchy-Schwarz to decouple noise from h_t(x). To lift the resulting non-uniform bound to hold over the uncountable domain X, the paper employs a discretization argument: it covers X with a finite grid of width ζ_t, applies union bounds over grid points and time steps, and uses Hölder continuity of the kernel (Assumption 2.2) to bound the interpolation error between grid points, yielding the covering-number-dependent scaling factors β_t and discretization terms Δ_t in Table 1. This produces four main bounds (Theorem 3.2): a sub-Gaussian bound with logarithmic dependence on the covering number, a bounded-noise bound offering a minimum of Hoeffding-type and Bernstein-type alternatives, a sub-exponential bound with a max of linear and square-root terms, and a variance-bounded (Chebyshev) bound. Extensions include correlated sub-Gaussian noise via a matrix variance proxy C_t (Proposition 3.3) and heavy-tailed noise via a truncation approach requiring only moment bounds on the output (Proposition 3.5).

## Results

Under sub-exponential (chi-squared) noise, the proposed bound from Theorem 3.2(c) reduces the uncertainty region size by over two orders of magnitude compared to Chowdhury & Gopalan (2019) and by over one order of magnitude compared to the truncation-based Proposition 3.5. In the safe control benchmark under sub-exponential noise, Theorem 3.2(c) achieves a 71.6% success rate, while Theorem 3.2(d) and Chowdhury & Gopalan achieve 0%, and Proposition 3.5 drops to 0% for t > 50. Under bounded/sub-Gaussian noise, the proposed bounds outperform Abbasi-Yadkori (2013) in all scenarios, with the relative improvement increasing with input dimension; Fiedler et al. (2021) slightly outperforms the proposed bounds by up to 7.5% in the low-data regime but deteriorates rapidly due to its √t scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Abbasi-Yadkori (2013), the proposed bounds consistently produce smaller uncertainty regions across all input dimensions (n_x ∈ {1,2,3}) and noise levels, and yield a substantially higher safe-control success rate; Abbasi-Yadkori's bound, which relies solely on the GP posterior variance, is overly conservative in unexplored regions. Against Fiedler et al. (2021), the proposed bounds are slightly worse in the low-data regime (up to 7.5% larger uncertainty, <1.1% lower success rate) but quickly surpass Fiedler's bound as data accumulates, because Fiedler's scaling factor grows as √t while the proposed bounds grow only logarithmically in the covering number. Against Chowdhury & Gopalan (2019) under heavy-tailed (chi-squared) noise, the proposed sub-exponential bound (Theorem 3.2(c)) dominates by over two orders of magnitude in uncertainty size and is the only method that maintains a non-zero safe-control success rate (71.6%) across all learning steps, whereas Chowdhury & Gopalan and the variance-bounded bound (Theorem 3.2(d)) both fail entirely (0% success rate) and the truncation-based Proposition 3.5 collapses to 0% after t > 50.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discretization-based uniform error bound framework of Lederer et al. (2019) from GPR to kernel ridge regression, and generalizes the truncation approach of Chowdhury & Gopalan (2019) for heavy-tailed noise. The key technical departure from prior bounds (Abbasi-Yadkori 2013; Fiedler et al. 2021) is avoiding the Cauchy-Schwarz separation of the decision-dependent noise term, instead applying concentration inequalities directly to |M_t^T h_t(x)| and then using Hölder continuity of the kernel to lift non-uniform bounds to uniform ones via a covering-number argument. This yields bounds that decompose uncertainty into an exploration term (B·σ̃_t(x)) and a noise term (η_t^M(x)) tailored to the specific distribution class, rather than collapsing both into a single scaled posterior variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is most relevant for safety-critical applications (safe Bayesian optimization, safe control) where overly conservative error bounds render problems infeasible; the numerical experiments show the proposed bounds achieve substantially higher success rates and lower inferred costs than baselines, particularly as data accumulates. A key limitation is that the variance-bounded bound (Theorem 3.2(d)) grows with √Z(ζ_t, X) rather than log Z(ζ_t, X) due to the Chebyshev inequality, causing blow-up for large t and limiting its practical utility. Open questions include extending the framework to more general KRR formulations (e.g., medians-of-means estimators for robustness under heavy tails), finite-dimensional parameter estimation bounds, and whether the discretization-based approach can be replaced by more refined uniform concentration techniques that avoid the covering-number dependence entirely.
