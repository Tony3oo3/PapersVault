## Problem

Existing 4D VAEs rely on 2D pointmap projections that eliminate native spatio-temporal coordinates, causing projection-induced distortions (motion blur, stretching, temporal flickering) and irreversible token dislocation. These methods also encode only incomplete, view-dependent observations and cannot represent complete 4D scenes with object appearance/disappearance, limiting their use as a holistic 4D compression backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260921/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The VAE accepts heterogeneous 4D sources (depth videos, RGB-D videos, multi-view point clouds, dynamic meshes) unified into dynamic colored voxel sequences at 1024³ spatial resolution with 1–32 frames, where each voxel carries 6 attributes (3D coordinates + RGB). The encoder compresses to latent tokens with temporal ratio λ∈[1,4] and spatial ratio γ=16; the decoder reconstructs full-resolution voxel sequences. For the generative model, an input video conditions a DiT that denoises 64³ 4D latent tokens (patchified to 32³) to produce complete 4D hand-object reconstructions.

## Method

The core contribution is a VAE that operates entirely in native 4D voxel space, preserving explicit (x,y,z,t) coordinates on every token. Since 4D convolution is unsupported in standard frameworks, the paper introduces Spatio-Temporal Downsampling/Upsampling (STD/STU) blocks that partition the 4D domain into local windows and perform cross-scale attention: coarse tokens query the union of fine and coarse tokens within each window, enabling flexible temporal compression without decoupling spatial and temporal processing. A depth-to-mesh preprocessing step eliminates spatial discontinuities before voxelization, and Plücker embeddings inject camera information only in the encoder so the latent space remains camera-free for generative use. Training combines BCE, DICE, RGB MSE, and KL losses with a novel differentiable voxel rendering loss built on batched sparse voxel rasterization, which significantly improves color fidelity over direct voxel-space supervision alone.

## Results

On the 4D-Dyn benchmark (140 sequences), the full model achieves Pre@0.001 of 0.9876, Recall@0.001 of 0.9918, CD-L1 of 0.0003, and PSNR of 29.63 dB, substantially outperforming the PointMapVAE baseline (Pre@0.001 = 0.0387, CD-L1 = 0.024). On the DyMesh dataset, the method reaches Pre@0.0005 of 0.8895 and CD-L1 of 0.0001, compared to DyMesh VAE's 0.7972 and 0.0008 respectively. The ablation rows confirm that the spatio-temporal attention module, voxel rendering loss, and Plücker embedding each contribute meaningfully to the final performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the PointMapVAE baseline (Wan et al., 2025, used as a proxy for 2D pointmap-based VAEs), the proposed native 4D VAE achieves a dramatic improvement: Pre@0.001 jumps from 0.0387 to 0.9876 and CD-L1 drops from 0.024 to 0.0003, a roughly 80× reduction in Chamfer distance. The paper attributes this gap to the pointmap representation's inability to preserve native 4D positional relationships, causing projection-induced distortions and irreversible token dislocation. Against the flow-based DyMesh VAE (Wu et al., 2025), the method wins on every metric on the DyMesh dataset, with the largest gains at the tighter threshold (Pre@0.0005: 0.8895 vs. 0.7972; Recall@0.0005: 0.9729 vs. 0.9254) and a 8× lower CD-L1 (0.0001 vs. 0.0008). The paper also notes that DyMesh VAE cannot encode color into its latent space, whereas the proposed VAE naturally fuses geometry and RGB. In the backbone analysis (Table 3), even a strengthened PointMapVAE with a VGGT transformer backbone (Pre@0.001 = 0.7425) remains far below the proposed method (0.9981), confirming that the advantage stems from the native 4D representation rather than architectural choices alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of 4D VAEs that encode dynamic 3D content through 2D-projected pointmaps (Geo4D, GeometryCrafter, 4DNeX) or decomposed static-geometry-plus-flow representations (DyMeshVAE), arguing that both introduce projection-induced distortions and irreversible token dislocation. It builds on sparse voxel rasterization (Sun et al., 2025) for a differentiable rendering loss adapted to feed-forward training, borrows the 4D positional encoding and temporal compression conventions from the Wan video VAE (Wan et al., 2025), and leverages the WAN-VACE DiT architecture (Jiang et al., 2025a) for the downstream video-conditioned diffusion model. The core architectural contribution is a spatio-temporal window attention module (STD/STU) that performs native 4D compression without decomposing into separate 3D spatial and 1D temporal convolutions, a design necessitated by the absence of native 4D convolution operators in standard frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By operating directly in native 4D voxel space, this VAE provides a unified representation that handles both partial (single-view) and complete 4D content without view-dependent artifacts, making it a more natural backbone for 4D generative modeling than 2D-projected alternatives. The demonstrated video-conditioned 4D diffusion model on Hot3D shows the latent space is expressive enough for end-to-end generation, positioning this as a potential standard encoder for future 4D content creation pipelines. Key limitations include high GPU memory consumption from dense 4D token sequences, degradation in large-scale scenes due to insufficient voxel resolution, and the mesh preprocessing step that can filter out fine-grained geometry (grass, small rocks). Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer temporal sequences and whether the window attention design can be made more efficient for real-time applications.
