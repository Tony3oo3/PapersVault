## Problem

LLM-augmented relational database analytics are dominated by the costly prefill phase, yet the default row-wise, column-wise prompt layout fragments shared prefixes and wastes prefix KV-cache reuse opportunities. Existing layout optimizers (OPHR, GGR) rely on exponential search or heuristic recursion with no theoretical guarantees, making them impractical at scale. The paper identifies the missing principled link between the structural repetition in relational tables and the prefix-cache topology that serving engines exploit.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a relational table of N tuples over M attributes together with a fixed query prefix (prompt template) and produces a pair of permutations: a column permutation π_c that reorders attribute serialization within each prompt, and a row permutation π_r that reorders the execution sequence of tuple-level LLM calls. The reordering is purely logical (applied at scheduling time) and does not alter the physical table layout; the output is a drop-in layout plan integrated into the LOTUS semantic operator pipeline. Planning runs in O(M²N + MN log N) expected time with O(MN) space, producing the full layout in seconds for 100K-tuple workloads.

## Method

SOLO establishes an isomorphism between prefix-cache reuse and path sharing on a radix tree induced by the column ordering: each tuple becomes a root-to-leaf path, and the LCP between two tuples equals the depth of their LCA. This decomposition separates the joint optimization into (i) row ordering, where lexicographical sorting under any fixed column order is provably optimal (Theorem 3.2, minimum cost equals tree size), and (ii) column ordering, which is NP-hard (Theorem 3.3) but admits a greedy tree-shaping algorithm that selects at each depth the column minimizing newly created distinct prefixes, achieving a tight 1/(M−1) worst-case approximation (Theorem 3.4) and exact optimality under a product-uniform independence model (Proposition 3.5). The implementation maintains a rolling equivalence-class vector F and uses hash-based COUNTUNIQUE to score candidates in expected O(N) per evaluation, avoiding repeated sorts and yielding near-linear planning overhead.

## Results

SOLO improves prefill throughput by up to 90.3% across four real-world datasets (OpenPayments, DMV, Food, GoogleAnalytics) under a fixed KV-cache budget. In terms of prefix overlap, SOLO achieves 75.93 on OpenPayments (vs. 10.49 default), 154.91 on Food (vs. 0.00 default), 44.68 on GoogleAnalytics (vs. 1.16 default), and 12.72 on DMV (vs. 0.80 default), with corresponding prefix-cache hit rates of 76.3%, 71.1%, 62.1%, and 35.2%. Planning overhead is reduced by up to 242.81× compared to GGR, with total planning time of only 3.7s (DMV) and 17.5s (OpenPayments) at the 100K-tuple scale, while preserving output quality (ΔACC within 0.6 points, ≥97.0% unchanged correctness).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOLO outperforms GGR (Liu et al., 2025b) on all four datasets in prefix overlap (e.g., 75.93 vs. 62.72 on OpenPayments; 154.91 vs. 99.58 on Food) and reduces planning overhead by 242.81×, while GGR has no theoretical guarantee. Against NDV (Lemire & Kaser, 2011), SOLO achieves higher overlap on every dataset (75.93 vs. 72.47 on OpenPayments; 154.91 vs. 140.30 on Food) with comparable planning time (1.04× faster). SOLO dramatically outperforms the meta-heuristic baselines ACLR (Bertsimas, 1993) and GCLR (Mitchell, 1998) in planning speed (13× and 389.56× faster respectively) while also achieving higher overlap (75.93 vs. 54.97 and 36.85 at matched 187.5s budget on OpenPayments). Compared to compression-oriented orderings Vortex (Lemire et al., 2012), Refgc (Richards, 1986), and Zgray (Faloutsos, 1986), SOLO is 2.32×, 0.96×, and 2.94× faster in planning respectively, with the Refgc comparison being the only case where SOLO is marginally slower in planning time. On the Flight functional-dependency benchmark, SOLO maintains 73.06 overlap versus 57.48 for NDV under natural dependencies, confirming robustness to correlated schemas.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical database reordering-for-locality principle (Lemire, Stonebraker) into the LLM serving context, where 'locality' becomes prefix-KV-cache reuse rather than I/O or compression locality. It departs from the closest prior work (OPHR and GGR in Liu et al., MLSys 2025), which treat prompt-layout optimization as a generic combinatorial search problem, by establishing a structural isomorphism between prefix-cache reuse and radix-tree topology. This isomorphism yields two clean reductions: lexicographic row sorting is provably optimal for any fixed column order, and the remaining column-ordering problem admits a greedy algorithm with a tight 1/(M−1) approximation guarantee and average-case optimality under a product-uniform independence model—neither of which prior heuristic or exhaustive-search methods provided.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying LLM-augmented database engines (LOTUS, Azure semantic operators, Databricks AI Functions), SOLO offers a drop-in layout planner that nearly doubles prefill throughput with negligible planning overhead, making prefix-cache reuse practical at 100K+ tuple scale. The NP-hardness proof and tight approximation bound set a clear ceiling on what any polynomial-time column-ordering heuristic can guarantee, guiding future work toward hybrid DP-greedy or sketch-based approaches for very wide schemas. Open questions include how the method behaves under strong functional dependencies (where the 1/(M−1) bound degrades), whether the conservative adjacent-prefix assumption (Assumption 3.1) remains tight under aggressive cache eviction, and whether the product-uniform optimality result extends to correlated real-world schemas beyond the stylized model.
