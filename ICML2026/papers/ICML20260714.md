## Problem

Transformers used for evidence-grounded binary adjudication (support/refute, yes/no, verifier-backed pass/fail) can produce different predictions under permutations of the same exchangeable evidence, making answers unreliable when chunk order is a nuisance variable. While next-token training minimizes expected conditional description length over orderings—yielding near Bayes-optimal average performance—a fixed ordering remains position-sensitive, creating an expectation-realization gap that lacks both a formal dispersion bound and a deployment-facing abstention rule.

## Input / Output

The method takes a question with n evidence chunks (48-token capped), a binary predicate g with a reference probability p_ref (sourced from gold labels, deterministic tools, or calibrated verifiers), and a target hallucination rate h*. Input is consumed through m permuted forward passes (m = 3–16 banded permutations), each yielding a renormalized two-label Bernoulli probability q_π. The output is a binary answer/abstain decision gated by the Information Sufficiency Ratio (ISR = Δ̄/B2T ≥ 1), where Δ̄ is the mean clipped Bernoulli KL budget across permutations and B2T is the minimum budget to certify the target reliability p* = 1 − h*.

## Method

The paper contributes two theoretical results and a fixed deployment rule. The Quantified Martingale Violation (QMV) bound controls permutation-induced dispersion by the cumulative logit sensitivity to adjacent rank changes, yielding O(log n) growth under harmonic positional decay (α = 1) and O(1) for α > 1. The Expectation-level Decompression Law (EDFL) specializes a KL-convexity plus data-processing bound to Bernoulli predicates, lower-bounding the expected information budget needed to shift an event's probability from prior mass q̄ to posterior mass p, with a rare-event scaling of log(1/q̄). From EDFL the paper derives closed-form planners—Bits-to-Trust (B2T = KL(Ber(p*)‖Ber(q_lo))), Risk-of-Hallucination, and ISR = Δ̄/B2T—and a fixed ISR = 1 answer/abstain threshold that is theory-determined rather than tuned. Empirically, uniform permutation mixtures achieve near-optimal cross-entropy (mixture-optimality gap < 10⁻⁴ nats/token), dispersion follows a + b log n across 58 depth values, and a pre-specified held-out audit on 528 items confirms the ISR = 1 gate attains 0.0–0.7% hallucination with 20.6–27.9% abstention (95% CIs).

## Results

On 3,059 evidence-grounded items (n ∈ [3,60] chunks), permutation-induced dispersion follows a + b·log n scaling: Qwen2-7B has slope b = 0.377 (95% CI [0.319, 0.435], R² = 0.742) and Llama-3.1-8B has b = 0.147 (95% CI [0.109, 0.184], R² = 0.515), consistent with the harmonic-regime QMV bound. Uniform permutation mixtures yield positive Jensen gaps (0.1041 nats/token for Qwen2-7B; 0.00982 for Llama-3.1-8B) with near-zero optimality gaps (<10⁻⁴ and ≤5.3×10⁻⁵ nats/token respectively). In the pre-specified held-out audit (528 items, Gemma-2-9B-FT, m=6), the fixed ISR=1 gate achieves 96.2% [94.3, 97.5] boundary alignment, 0.0–0.7% hallucination, 24.1% [20.6, 27.9] abstention, and 80.5% [76.8, 83.8] accuracy on attempts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against external baseline methods; its empirical comparisons are internal validations of theoretical predictions. The key contrasts are: (1) single-permutation vs. uniform-mixture cross-entropy, where the uniform mixture strictly dominates (positive Jensen gap at every depth n) and is within <10⁻⁴ nats/token of a globally optimized convex mixture, confirming near-MDL optimality within the permutation family; (2) Qwen2-7B vs. Llama-3.1-8B, where Qwen2-7B exhibits roughly 2.6× stronger positional sensitivity (b = 0.377 vs. 0.147) and a 10.6× larger Jensen gap, reflecting architectural differences in positional encoding; (3) the ISR=1 gate vs. no gating, where the gate converts 24.1% of items into abstentions to achieve near-zero hallucination (0.0–0.7%) at the cost of reduced coverage, with boundary alignment of 96.2% confirming the theory-determined threshold generalizes out-of-sample without tuning. Auxiliary model-breadth diagnostics (Llama-3.3-70B, gpt-4.1-mini, DeepSeek-V3.2 MoE) show probability-space slopes shrink for larger models while logit-space slopes remain positive, suggesting sigmoid compression rather than vanishing positional sensitivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three threads: (1) empirical observations of order sensitivity in transformers (Liu et al. 2024 'lost-in-the-middle'; Falck et al. 2024 martingale tests of exchangeability), (2) Bayesian/MDL characterizations of in-context learning (Xie et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2024), and (3) selective prediction with reject options (Chow 1970; Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017). The key departure is formalizing the expectation-realization gap over evidence orderings as a measurable quantity (QMV bound) and deriving a Chow-style reject rule whose threshold (ISR=1) is fixed analytically from Bernoulli KL geometry rather than fit on held-out confidence scores. Unlike self-consistency or perturbation ensembling (Wang et al. 2023), permutation mixtures here marginalize a nuisance variable to estimate evidence sufficiency, not to improve point accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theory-derived abstention interface for evidence-grounded QA that does not require post-hoc threshold tuning, which is practically valuable for deployment where hallucination cost is asymmetric. The O(log n) dispersion law and near-optimality of uniform permutation mixtures (gap < 10⁻⁴ nats/token) give practitioners a clear picture of when and why order matters. However, the held-out audit covers a single fine-tuned Gemma-2-9B model on one 528-item set, the framework requires a deterministic or calibrated verification primitive (excluding open-ended generation), and broader validation across model families and unrestricted tasks remains open. Whether the log-scaling regime persists under different chunking strategies, retrieval pipelines, or longer contexts is an important unresolved question.
