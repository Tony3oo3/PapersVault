## Problem

Deep Transformer architectures face a fundamental trade-off: PreNorm ensures training stability through clean residual paths but suffers from representation collapse where deep layers degenerate into identity functions, while PostNorm yields stronger performance but exhibits severe gradient vanishing that prevents convergence beyond a few dozen layers. No existing normalization scheme successfully combines the stability of PreNorm with the representational capacity of PostNorm at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260450/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard text token sequences for autoregressive language modeling, producing next-token distributions as output. The architectural modification is internal to the Transformer block and is agnostic to the attention variant (MHA, GQA, MLA) or feedforward type (dense FFN or MoE), making it a drop-in replacement that requires no changes to input tokenization, sequence length, or output head design.

## Method

SpanNorm modifies the second residual connection in a PostNorm block: instead of adding the intermediate attention output Y_l to the FFN output, it adds the original block input X_l', creating a residual path that spans the entire block. This single structural change preserves a clean, unnormalized skip connection (PreNorm's stability benefit) while still applying LayerNorm to the aggregated sum of the FFN output and the block input (PostNorm's performance benefit). Theoretical analysis shows this topology reduces per-block gradient attenuation from σ^{-2} (PostNorm) to σ^{-1} (SpanNorm), converting quadratic decay into linear decay, while the per-block normalization resets variance to Θ(1), preventing the Jacobian collapse that causes PreNorm's representation degradation. A complementary Scale Init strategy scales the FFN output matrix W_2 and attention output matrix W_O by 1/√L to guarantee bounded signal variance as depth grows, enabling stable training up to 512 layers with fixed hyperparameters.

## Results

On a 5B dense model trained on 200B tokens, SpanNorm achieves a WikiText perplexity of 11.8 and LAMBADA perplexity of 5.8, with an average downstream accuracy of 66.6%, outperforming the PreNorm baseline (64.1%) by 2.5 points and the strongest competitor HybridNorm (65.4%) by 1.2 points. At the ultra-deep 128-layer 6.5B scale (400B tokens), SpanNorm reaches 68.4% average accuracy, a 4.0-point margin over PreNorm (64.4%), while PostNorm and HybridNorm both fail to converge. SpanNorm also improves over PreNorm by 1.0–2.5 points across dense scales (740M to 5B) and by 1.2 points in the MoE setting (A2.4B-16B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpanNorm consistently outperforms all eight baselines. Against the standard PreNorm baseline (Touvron et al., 2023), SpanNorm gains 1.0–2.5 points in average accuracy across dense scales and 1.2 points in the MoE setting. PostNorm (Vaswani et al., 2017) fails to converge entirely at both 5B and 6.5B scales. Among the advanced variants, HybridNorm (Zhuo et al., 2025) is the closest competitor at 5B (65.4% vs. 66.6%) but suffers catastrophic divergence at 128 layers, while SpanNorm maintains stable training and achieves 68.4%. Mix-LN (Li et al., 2024) trails SpanNorm by 2.0 points at 5B and 2.4 points at 128 layers. LayerNorm Scaling (Sun et al., 2025) performs worse than the PreNorm baseline (60.2% vs. 64.1%), which the authors attribute to its depth-dependent scaling factor over-suppressing shallow-layer gradients. Peri-LN (Kim et al., 2025) is outperformed by 2.3 points. In the topological ablation (Table 4), SpanNorm beats Parallel Transformer (Chowdhery et al., 2023) by 1.6 points and ResiDual (Xie et al., 2023) by 3.2 points in average accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpanNorm sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines of work: stabilizing PostNorm for deep networks (DeepNorm, Zhang et al. 2019, Huang et al. 2020) and mitigating PreNorm's representation collapse (Normformer, LayerNorm Scaling, Peri-LN). It also engages with hybrid approaches like Mix-LN (inter-layer PostNorm→PreNorm transition) and HybridNorm (intra-layer QKV-Norm + PostNorm), but departs from all of them by making a single minimal structural change—routing the block input X_l' directly into the FFN residual instead of the intermediate Y_l—rather than adding auxiliary normalization layers or switching paradigms across depth. The theoretical analysis builds on signal-propagation theory (Kedia et al., 2024) and the Jacobian-based stability criteria from the DeepNorm line, but reframes the problem around a 'span' topology that unifies the clean-path benefit of PreNorm with the aggregate-normalization benefit of PostNorm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpanNorm offers a zero-overhead, plug-and-drop normalization that resolves the long-standing stability-vs-performance trade-off, enabling stable training at 128 layers (6.5B) where PostNorm and HybridNorm diverge, and demonstrating a monotonic 'deeper is better' trend up to 512 layers with fixed hyperparameters. Its practical impact is significant for industrial-scale LLM training, where the ability to reliably scale depth without re-tuning learning rates or initialization is economically critical. Open questions include whether the benefits persist at 100B+ parameter scales, whether the Scale Init strategy is strictly necessary or merely helpful (the paper applies it to all baselines for fairness, muddying the ablation), and how SpanNorm interacts with emerging architectural changes like new attention variants or speculative decoding pipelines.
