## Problem

Multi-agent debate for LLM reasoning has conflicting theoretical predictions: Bayesian latent-concept models show interaction amplifies correct seeds, while Dirichlet-multinomial analyses prove symmetric debate is a martingale with zero expected drift. Existing frameworks apply debate uniformly across all instances, incurring substantial token overhead even when interaction is theoretically neutral, without a principled mechanism to distinguish corrective from neutral regimes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text problem as input and internally monitors the leader model's token-level log-probabilities and entropies over the extracted answer span to compute a binary uncertainty gate. Output is a single extracted discrete answer (via a deterministic extractor on the leader's final response) accompanied by the full reasoning trajectory. The staged escalation produces at most three sequential peer interactions (adversarial, supportive, expert), each conditioned on the leader's current response, rather than parallel all-to-all exchanges.

## Method

LASE (Leader-Adaptive Structured Engagement) is a confidence-gated, leader-centric multi-agent framework that breaks the symmetry underlying martingale neutrality in two ways: (1) a directed star topology where only the leader incorporates peer messages, and (2) selective engagement triggered only when the leader's min log-prob falls below a threshold or max entropy exceeds a threshold. When the gate fires, LASE performs staged one-peer-at-a-time escalation—an adversarial Devil's Advocate check, a supportive hint-giver, and an expert consultant—each followed by a leader revision, with early stopping if confidence recovers. Theoretically, this asymmetric, conditioned update rule invalidates the averaging condition required for martingale neutrality and concentrates interaction budget on instances where a correct seed is likely to exist, yielding a provable accuracy lower bound of P(U=1)·ρδ over the solo baseline while reducing expected token cost by a factor of P(U=1) relative to always-on debate.

## Results

On MATH-500 with Gemini-2.0-Flash, LASE achieves 87.2% accuracy (vs. 86.4% for MV and 74.8% for MAD) while consuming only 1.1k average tokens per sample—roughly 10× fewer than MAD (11.8k) and 5× fewer than MV (5.2k). On GPQA-Diamond, LASE reaches 65.2% accuracy, surpassing both MV (62.7%) and MAD (62.1%). Across all six benchmarks and both models (Gemini-2.0-Flash, GPT-4.1-mini), LASE consistently matches or exceeds multi-agent baselines in accuracy while maintaining near single-agent token cost (e.g., 0.4k tokens on GSM-Symbolic vs. 5.6k for MAD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Multi-Agent Debate (Du et al., 2023), LASE wins decisively on both accuracy and efficiency: on MATH-500 (Gemini-2.0-Flash), LASE scores 87.2% vs. MAD's 74.8% (+12.4 pp) while using 1.1k tokens vs. MAD's 11.8k (~11× reduction); on GPQA-Diamond, LASE reaches 65.2% vs. MAD's 62.1%. Against Majority Voting (Wang et al., 2022, N=7), LASE achieves higher accuracy on most benchmarks (e.g., 87.2% vs. 86.4% on MATH-500, 93.4% vs. 91.2% on GSM-Symbolic, 81.3% vs. 79.3% on MMLU-Pro for Gemini) while consuming roughly 5× fewer tokens. Against Mixture-of-Agents (Wang et al., 2025), LASE outperforms MoA by +2.2 pp accuracy (74.2% vs. 72.0% on MATH-500, GPT-4.1-mini) while using only ~25% of the token budget (0.9M vs. 3.6M total tokens). Notably, on the harder MATH-lv5 subset with GPT-4.1-mini, LASE (86.6%) slightly trails MAD (86.4%) by a negligible margin, suggesting the advantage is most pronounced on tasks where uncertainty gating can selectively target difficult instances.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reconciles two seemingly contradictory theoretical accounts of multi-agent debate: Hu et al. (2025) showing that debate amplifies correct seeds via Bayesian posterior updating, and Choi et al. (2025) proving that symmetric debate dynamics form a martingale with zero expected drift. LASE departs from the standard all-to-all multi-agent debate paradigm (Du et al., 2023) by introducing a directed leader-supporter star topology and a confidence-gated trigger mechanism, so that interaction is invoked only when the corrective regime is likely. It also positions itself as orthogonal to and composable with majority voting (Wang et al., 2022) and more efficient than static multi-agent routing frameworks like Mixture-of-Agents (Wang et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10×+ token reduction while matching or exceeding multi-agent accuracy is practically significant for deployment cost, and the confidence-gated leader-supporter design is simple enough to be widely adopted as a drop-in test-time strategy. However, the framework's performance ceiling is bounded by the base model's intrinsic reasoning capacity (as shown by the 'execution failure post-critique' mode), and the current 1-on-1 homogeneous topology may not fully exploit collective intelligence on extremely hard problems. Open questions include whether heterogeneous model ensembles, hierarchical supporter sub-debates, or learned gating policies can push the Pareto frontier further, and whether the martingale-neutrality breakdown generalizes to truly asymmetric agent populations.
