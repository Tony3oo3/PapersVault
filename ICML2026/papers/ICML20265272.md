## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) exhibit 'Lazy Reasoning' in complex multi-turn tool use: they generate lengthy reasoning dominated by repetitive reflection while bypassing substantive task decomposition, causing RL optimization to stall and performance to lag behind specialized SFT models. The paper identifies that this failure stems from an inability to decompose multi-intent queries into executable subtasks, a capability that existing RL-only training (e.g., GRPO) neither incentivizes nor restores.

## Input / Output

The model receives a structured context comprising a system policy, a set of tool API definitions (name, description, parameters), conversation history, and a user query. It outputs a decomposed reasoning trajectory: an explicit numbered subtask list followed by per-subtask reasoning blocks (delimited by think tags) interleaved with concrete tool calls and their execution results. For tool-irrelevant queries, the output is a single reasoning block explaining why no tool invocation is needed. The trajectory format enforces sequential or parallel subtask execution depending on dependency structure.

## Method

D-CORE is a two-stage training framework. Stage 1 (self-distillation) prompts the LRM itself to reverse-engineer subtask decompositions from reference trajectories and few-shot examples, generates per-subtask reasoning and tool calls, then composes them into complete trajectories for SFT—eliminating the need for a stronger teacher model. Stage 2 (DA-GRPO) addresses the critical failure mode where self-distillation collapses reward variance to near-zero, causing GRPO's normalized advantage to vanish and gradients to stop. DA-GRPO introduces a piecewise advantage: when the standard GRPO advantage falls below a threshold ζ, it is replaced by a detached entropy term ψ(H_{i,t}) = min(α·H_{i,t}^{detach}, δ) that scales each token's gradient update proportionally to its vocabulary-level entropy. This selectively amplifies high-entropy (reflective) tokens while bounding the entropy contribution, restoring reasoning diversity without breaking tool-call syntax. The paper provides two theorems proving that DA-GRPO prevents learning stagnation and preferentially reduces entropy of high-uncertainty tokens.

## Results

D-CORE-8B achieves 77.7% average accuracy on BFCLv3 and 47.6% on τ-bench, surpassing the best 8B baseline (xLAM2-8B at 72.0% / 42.4%) by 5.7% and 5.2% respectively. D-CORE-14B sets a new state-of-the-art at 79.3% BFCLv3 and 51.3% τ-bench, outperforming the previous best 70B model (xLAM2-70B at 78.4% / 48.3%) with 5× fewer parameters. The most dramatic gain is on BFCLv3 Multi-Turn, where D-CORE improves from 33.0% (Qwen3-8B) to 63.8% (+30.8%), directly addressing the identified Lazy Reasoning failure mode.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Qwen3 backbone baselines, D-CORE-8B gains +11.4% on BFCLv3 and +18.6% on τ-bench, while D-CORE-14B gains +13.4% and +17.7% respectively. Compared to the specialized tool-use model xLAM2-8B, D-CORE-8B wins by 5.7% on BFCLv3 overall and 5.2% on τ-bench, with the largest margin on Multi-Turn (63.8% vs. 69.1% — actually xLAM2-8B leads on Multi-Turn at 69.1% vs. 63.8%, but D-CORE wins decisively on τ-bench Airline at 44.4% vs. 34.0%). D-CORE-14B surpasses the much larger xLAM2-70B (78.4% BFCLv3, 48.3% τ-bench) on both benchmarks with 5× fewer parameters. Against the RL-only baseline ToolRL-Qwen3-8B, D-CORE-8B improves BFCLv3 by 11.8% and τ-bench by 16.1%, demonstrating that self-distillation plus DA-GRPO substantially outperforms GRPO alone. On out-of-distribution benchmarks (τ²-Bench, ACEBench, BFCLv4-Agentic), D-CORE-14B achieves 44.2% on τ²-Bench (vs. 43.4% for xLAM2-70B) and 76.9% on ACEBench (vs. 72.2% for Qwen3-32B), confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (Shao et al., 2024) by introducing an entropy-based advantage term (DA-GRPO) to address gradient collapse in post-distillation RL, and builds on the ToolRL reward formulation for tool-use tasks. Conceptually, it departs from the pure-RL paradigm of ToolRL and Nemotron-N1 by showing that RL alone is insufficient (even harmful) for multi-turn tool use, and instead inserts a self-distillation stage that injects task decomposition structure before RL. The self-distillation mechanism draws on decomposed prompting (Khot et al., 2022) and least-to-most prompting (Zhou et al., 2022), but automates the process without requiring a stronger teacher model. The entropy-aware advantage design is informed by recent observations that high-entropy tokens correlate with reflective reasoning (Wang et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025), and is positioned against concurrent RL variants like RL-ZVP, DAPO, and Seed-GRPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'Lazy Reasoning' as a specific, quantifiable failure mode in LRMs for multi-turn tool use provides a useful diagnostic framework for the community, and the finding that pure GRPO degrades multi-turn performance (26.8% vs. 33.0% baseline) is a practically important negative result. The DA-GRPO formulation offers a targeted, theoretically grounded fix for advantage collapse that could generalize beyond tool use to other RL settings where reward variance is low. However, the 4× computational overhead of the two-stage pipeline, the restriction to ≤14B models, and the tool-use-specific evaluation limit broader applicability; whether the decomposition-then-diversify paradigm scales to 70B+ models or transfers to non-tool reasoning tasks remains open.
