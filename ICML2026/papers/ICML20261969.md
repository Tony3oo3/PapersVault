## Problem

Existing automatic prompt optimization (APO) methods are ill-suited for auto-routing in LLM-based systems: iterative methods (APE, OPRO) incur prohibitive multi-round inference latency, while training-based methods (RLPrompt) are model-specific and optimize only response quality. No prior approach jointly handles model heterogeneity (architecture, parameter scale, quantization), multi-objective trade-offs (quality, latency, token usage), and offline deployment constraints simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a structured conditioning string encoding task metadata (name, type, description, difficulty), model metadata (family, parameter scale, quantization precision), and a user-specified preference vector w ∈ R³ over quality/latency/tokens, and produces a single optimized text prompt in one forward pass. The prompt is a free-form instruction string (e.g., zero-shot, few-shot, CoT, or role-playing style) that is directly prepended to the target LLM's input, with no task-specific post-processing required. The preference vector is sampled from a Dirichlet distribution during training to cover the full Pareto frontier, but can be fixed at inference to select a specific operating point.

## Method

TAMPO is a three-stage offline pipeline: (1) a DeBERTa-v3-Large reward model is trained with a combined MSE + Gaussian NLL loss to predict quality, latency, and token count along with aleatoric uncertainty; (2) a Phi-3.5-mini-instruct policy (LoRA-adapted) is initialized via SFT with curriculum learning on high-reward prompts filtered from a 328K-record heterogeneity-aware dataset; (3) multi-objective RL using GRPO with Dirichlet-sampled preference vectors explores the Pareto frontier. The key anti-reward-hacking mechanism is Monte Carlo Dropout (M=5 passes) on the frozen reward model during RL, which yields an epistemic uncertainty σ_k that is subtracted from the predicted mean in the scalarized reward R = Σ w_k(μ_k − λσ_k), establishing a pessimistic lower bound (Theorem 3.1) that suppresses over-optimistic scores on out-of-distribution prompts. Proposition 3.2 guarantees that maximizing the positive-weighted scalarized objective yields properly Pareto-optimal prompts, and the Dirichlet sampling over w ensures diverse frontier coverage during training.

## Results

Across 86 tasks spanning reasoning, world knowledge, and NLU, TAMPO³ (quality-focused) achieves the highest average accuracy of 48.4% on reasoning and 57.8% on world knowledge, with a worst-case accuracy of 39.7% and 41.3% respectively. The token-efficient variant TAMPO¹ reduces token consumption to 21 (reasoning) and 19 (world knowledge) while maintaining 47.8% and 57.4% average accuracy. The latency-focused variant TAMPO² achieves inference times of 285 ms (reasoning) and 268 ms (world knowledge), compared to 2098 ms for APE and 9236 ms for OPRO. On NLU tasks, TAMPO³ reaches BERTScore F1 of 31.0 and ROUGE-L of 27.8, the best among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAMPO³ outperforms all three baseline categories on both reasoning and world knowledge accuracy: it beats APE (48.4% vs. 47.4% reasoning, 57.8% vs. 55.7% world knowledge), OPRO (48.4% vs. 46.9%, 57.8% vs. 53.6%), and RLPrompt (48.4% vs. 46.6%, 57.8% vs. 52.1%). The latency advantage is dramatic: TAMPO³ requires 858 ms end-to-end latency on reasoning versus 2098 ms for APE, 9236 ms for OPRO, and 1400 ms for RLPrompt. On worst-case robustness, TAMPO³ sustains 39.7% reasoning accuracy across all model configurations, whereas RLPrompt drops to 37.4% and APE to 36.9%, confirming that model-aware conditioning prevents cross-model performance collapse. The token-efficient variant TAMPO¹ achieves the lowest token consumption (21 tokens for reasoning, 19 for world knowledge) while remaining within 0.6% of TAMPO³'s accuracy, demonstrating that the preference vector enables meaningful trade-off control without retraining. On NLU tasks, TAMPO³ leads in both ROUGE-L (27.8) and BERTScore F1 (31.0), with a Δb of 0.0 on both metrics, while RLPrompt is closest behind at 27.6/29.8.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAMPO extends the conditional-generation formulation for prompt optimization introduced by RLPrompt (Deng et al., 2022) by explicitly conditioning on model metadata (architecture, parameter scale, quantization) rather than task alone, and by replacing the single-objective reward with a multi-objective scalarized objective over quality, latency, and token consumption. The RL stage adopts GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for stable policy updates, while the uncertainty-aware reward model draws on Gaussian NLL regression (Kendall & Gal, 2017) for aleatoric uncertainty and Monte Carlo Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016) for epistemic uncertainty, combining both to construct a conservative reward that resists reward hacking in the offline setting. Conceptually, the paper departs from iterative APO methods (APE, OPRO) that require repeated LLM calls and from prior multi-objective prompt optimizers (MORL-Prompt, ParetoPrompt, Prompt-OIRL) that either lack cross-model awareness or rely on expensive online search, positioning itself as an offline, model-aware, multi-objective alternative tailored to auto-routing pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM-based systems increasingly adopt auto-routing across heterogeneous model pools, the ability to generate task- and model-specific prompts in a single forward pass—without iterative API calls—addresses a real deployment bottleneck in latency and cost. The uncertainty-aware reward model and its theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) provide a principled mechanism to prevent the policy from exploiting out-of-distribution prompts, a failure mode that plagues offline RL in general. Limitations include evaluation on relatively small models (0.5B–8B), a fixed set of four model families, and an upfront dataset-construction cost (~$660 + ~90 GPU-hours) that may not amortize well for very small deployments. Open questions include whether the framework scales to frontier-scale models, whether online feedback loops can refine the policy without retraining, and how to extend the objective space to safety and fairness constraints.
