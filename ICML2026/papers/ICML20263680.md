## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has driven major gains in LLM reasoning, but progress is bottlenecked by the scarcity of high-quality QA pairs, especially in physics where internet-derived corpora are sparse and unevenly distributed. Unlike mathematics, which benefits from abundant structured problems, physics lacks large-scale datasets with automatically verifiable answers, leaving LLMs poorly equipped for multi-step physical reasoning on benchmarks like the International Physics Olympiad.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263680/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes natural-language physics problems as input (text-only, no diagrams) and produces a reasoning chain ending in a numeric or symbolic answer. During training, the synthetic QA pairs come in three formats: numeric (forward state queries like 'velocity at time t'), reverse (inverse parameter inference with one quantity masked), and symbolic (all parameters replaced by variables). The reward signal is binary: the model's final answer must fall within 5% relative error of the simulator-recorded ground truth, accounting for numerical integration approximations in MuJoCo.

## Method

Sim2Reason procedurally generates diverse classical-mechanics scenes using a three-level Domain-Specific Language (scene → entity → body) that isolates physically meaningful randomization axes, compiles them into MuJoCo XML, and simulates the dynamics to record time-series traces of forces, velocities, energies, and tensions. From these traces, the pipeline automatically instantifies verified QA pairs and applies a shortcut-solution filter that ablates entities and joints to discard questions whose answers are invariant under oversimplified approximations (~15% removed). The LLM is then post-trained with RLVR using Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) with DAPO-style dynamic sampling, optimizing a clipped sequence-level policy-gradient objective with group-relative advantages. Crucially, the simulator acts as a data generator rather than an external tool: the LLM never writes simulation code, and the procedural DSL enables millions of unique, physically grounded training samples without human annotation, yielding zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to IPhO, JEEBench, and other real-world physics benchmarks.

## Results

Training solely on simulator-generated synthetic data via RLVR yields consistent zero-shot sim-to-real gains on IPhO Mechanics: +7.5% for Qwen2.5-3B (5.68→13.15%), +5.4% for Qwen2.5-32B (19.8→25.2%), and +4.4% for Qwen3-30B (35.6→40.0%). On Qwen2.5-32B, the largest gain appears on JEEBench (+17.9%, 34.38→52.28%), with additional improvements on PHYSICS (+3.67%), OlympiadBench (+3.12%), and out-of-domain MATH500 (+4.4%). The method also improves on HCV (Concepts of Physics) by 1.8–7.6% across model sizes, confirming gains are not limited to a single benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sim2Reason (Qwen3-30B) achieves 40.0% on IPhO, outperforming Prime P1 30B (Chen et al., 2025; trained on 5,000+ curated real-world physics QA pairs) by 1.4 points (38.6%). Against DAPO-32B (Yu et al., 2025; trained on real-world math QA), Sim2Reason on Qwen2.5-32B edges ahead at 25.2% vs. 24.7% on IPhO. However, LIMO-32B (Ye et al., 2025) slightly outperforms Sim2Reason on IPhO (25.5% vs. 25.2%), though the gap is within noise. In a matched RL setup on Qwen2.5-3B, Sim2Reason's synthetic data (13.15% IPhO) substantially outperforms DAPO-17K real-world math data (9.98%), and combining both yields only a marginal additional gain (10.35%), indicating domain-aligned simulator supervision is the primary driver of physics reasoning improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR paradigm (DeepSeek-R1, DAPO, LIMO) from domains with abundant internet QA pairs to physics, where such data is scarce. It departs from the 'simulator as external tool' line of work (Toolformer, Sarch et al.) by instead using physics simulators as scalable QA generators: a domain-specific language procedurally constructs scenes, MuJoCo simulates them, and the resulting traces are converted into verifiable question-answer pairs. The synthetic-data-generation approach draws conceptual parallels to AlphaGeometry's procedural geometry generation and MetaMath's self-generated math problems, but targets continuous physical dynamics rather than discrete symbolic structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a scalable, human-annotation-free pipeline for generating physics reasoning training data, demonstrated to yield zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on IPhO, JEEBench, and other benchmarks. The finding that purely synthetic simulator data can match or slightly exceed carefully curated real-world physics QA (Prime P1) challenges the assumption that domain-specific real data is necessary for physics reasoning. However, the approach is currently limited to classical mechanics in MuJoCo, and extending it to E&M, thermodynamics, or other sciences remains open. The DSL abstraction and the shortcut-filtering strategy are reusable components that could generalize to other simulator-based data generation settings.
