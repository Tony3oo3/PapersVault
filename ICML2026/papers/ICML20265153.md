## Problem

Clebsch-Gordan tensor products (CGTP) in E(3)-equivariant neural networks scale as O(L^6), and while the Gaunt tensor product (GTP) was identified as the only prior operation with true asymptotic gains, it is incomplete: it cannot compute odd-sum interactions such as cross products. No existing tensor product operation simultaneously provides genuine asymptotic speedup over CGTP and full expressivity (i.e., the ability to simulate all CGTP interactions).

## Input / Output

The method takes pairs of irrep-valued feature vectors, where each input irrep of type j is interpreted as coefficients of tensor spherical harmonics Y_{j,m}^{ℓ,s} on the sphere for valid ℓ satisfying |j−s|≤ℓ≤j+s. The output is a set of irrep-valued vectors in TSH coefficient space, labeled by (j₃, ℓ₃). In the Tetris classification experiment, the input is a randomly oriented 3D point cloud and the output is one of 8 class labels; the network uses VSTP in place of GTP within a NequIP-style architecture.

## Method

The paper first shows that GTP naturally arises from generalizing FFT-based convolution to compact Lie groups via the Peter-Weyl theorem, then quotienting SO(3) by its maximal torus SO(2) to obtain the sphere. To fix GTP's antisymmetry problem (which excludes odd-sum interactions), the authors generalize from scalar spherical harmonics to tensor spherical harmonics (TSHs), where signals are irrep-valued maps S²→ℝ^{2s+1}. They prove a generalized Gaunt formula for TSH products involving Wigner 9j symbols, Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, and a C_{ℓ₁,0,ℓ₂,0}^{ℓ₃,0} factor. The key result is that only vector signals (s=1) are needed for completeness: the (1,1,1) irrep signal tensor product (VSTP) is equivalent to a pointwise cross product of vector spherical signals, and its selection rules permit all CGTP paths except trivial scalar multiplication. A constant number (≤9) of VSTP calls per irrep pair suffices to simulate full CGTP, yielding O(L⁴ log² L) total runtime with fast spherical harmonic transforms, close to the O(L⁴) lower bound.

## Results

The paper's primary result is asymptotic: VSTP simulates full CGTP in O(L⁴ log² L) time, improving over the naive O(L⁶) and sparse O(L⁵) CGTP, approaching the O(L⁴) lower bound. On the 3D Tetris classification task, VSTP achieves 100% accuracy (distinguishing chiral pieces), while GTP is limited to 80% due to its inability to compute odd-parity interactions like cross products. In microbenchmarks, VSTP runs at a constant factor slower than GTP but faster than CGTP for ℓ ≥ 3, confirming the theoretical prediction that VSTP shares GTP's asymptotic scaling while adding completeness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GTP (Luo et al., 2024), VSTP matches the asymptotic runtime exactly (O(L² log² L) per call with fast SH transforms) but is strictly more capable: GTP cannot compute any interaction where ℓ₁+ℓ₂+ℓ₃ is odd (e.g., cross products), while VSTP handles all non-scalar interactions. This is demonstrated empirically on the 3D Tetris task where GTP plateaus at 80% accuracy (failing to distinguish chiral pairs) while VSTP reaches 100%. Against standard CGTP (Passaro & Zitnick, 2023), VSTP provides a true asymptotic speedup from O(L⁶) to O(L⁴ log² L) for full simulation, though it requires a constant number (≤9) of VSTP calls per irrep pair to recover full expressivity. In wall-clock microbenchmarks, VSTP is a constant factor slower than GTP (as expected from the extra vector-valued signal overhead) but faster than CGTP for ℓ ≥ 3, confirming the theoretical scaling advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on two recent works: Luo et al. (2024) introduced the Gaunt Tensor Product (GTP) as an asymptotically faster alternative to Clebsch-Gordan tensor products, and Xie et al. (2025) established a formal framework showing that most prior TPO speedups stem from reduced expressivity rather than true algorithmic gains. The authors show that GTP naturally emerges from generalizing FFT-based convolution to compact Lie groups via the Peter-Weyl theorem, then remedy GTP's incompleteness (it cannot compute antisymmetric interactions like cross products) by generalizing from scalar spherical harmonics to tensor spherical harmonics. The key insight is that vector spherical harmonics (s=1) suffice to recover all CGTP interactions, yielding the Vector Signal Tensor Product (VSTP) with O(L⁴log²L) runtime for full CGTP simulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first complete tensor product operation with a true asymptotic speedup over CGTP, closing a gap identified by Xie et al. (2025) where all prior 'fast' TPOs either lost expressivity or were incomplete. The generalized Gaunt formula for tensor spherical harmonics (involving Wigner 9j symbols) is a novel mathematical result with potential applications beyond equivariant ML. However, practical impact is currently limited: the O(L²log²L) fast SH transforms only outperform O(L³) versions at L~1000, far exceeding L values used in current E(3)NNs. Open questions include whether VSTP with standard O(L³) transforms is viable in practice (it would be a constant-factor overhead over GTP with full completeness), and whether spin-weighted spherical harmonics offer an alternative path.
