## Problem

Unified multimodal models for image generation and editing are bottlenecked by training data that lacks systematic structure and coverage of challenging real-world scenarios. Existing datasets address basic tasks like style transfer and simple object manipulation but omit specialized categories such as scientific imagery, multi-instruction editing, spatial reasoning, and in-image text rendering, leaving models underprepared for practical applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260470/cropped_page1_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model receives structured text prompts encoding specific constraints (e.g., 'render a physics diagram showing electromagnetic induction in Ghibli style with the label "Faraday's Law" in serif font') and produces a single RGB image. For editing, the model takes a source image plus one or more natural-language editing instructions (ranging from single operations like 'remove the hat' to multi-turn sequences of up to four rounds or complex instructions combining 2–4 sub-operations) and outputs the edited image. The dataset comprises 80k instruction-image pairs; source images for editing are drawn from SEED-Data-Edit, ImgEdit, OmniEdit, GPT-4o-generated images, and curated high-resolution images (>1024 px), while all target images are regenerated via the gpt-image-1 API to ensure quality and consistency.

## Method

The core contribution is a hierarchical taxonomy decomposing image generation into five modules (Style Control, Complex Instruction Following, In-Image Text Rendering, Spatial Reasoning, Scientific Imagery) and image editing into six categories with 21 subtasks, totaling 51 fine-grained sub-capabilities across 11 domains. An automated pipeline generates instructions at scale by populating diverse syntactic templates with components from structured resource pools (object, relation/action, and qualifier pools), then uses GPT-4o's gpt-image-1 API to produce the corresponding images. A key quality-control insight is the 'no invalid images, only invalid text' principle: when GPT-4o's output deviates from the prompt (e.g., generating three objects instead of four), the instruction is recalibrated to match the actual image rather than discarding the sample, maximizing data utilization. For subject-driven editing, a novel reverse-construction strategy uses inpainting instructions and edited outputs to synthesize the corresponding source image. Fine-tuning experiments on four architecturally diverse models (UniWorld-V1, Harmon, OmniGen2, MagicBrush) across four benchmarks demonstrate consistent gains of up to 18% on editing and 13% on generation, with ablations confirming that targeted modules (e.g., 8k Spatial Reasoning samples) drive specific capability improvements.

## Results

Fine-tuning UniWorld-V1 on OpenGPT-4o-Image yields an 18.4% relative improvement on ImgEdit-Bench (3.26 → 3.86) and a 12.0% gain on GEdit-Bench (4.85 → 5.43). On the generation side, Harmon achieves a 13.2% improvement on GenEval (0.76 → 0.86) and a 5.3% gain on DPG-Bench (81.27 → 85.60), surpassing the proprietary GPT-4o score of 0.84 on GenEval. MagicBrush shows the largest relative editing gain at 21.1% on ImgEdit-Bench (1.90 → 2.30) and 21.7% on GEdit-Bench (4.19 → 5.10). Data scaling experiments confirm consistent performance growth from 20K to 40K samples, with the 40K subset selected as the optimal trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImgEdit-Bench, fine-tuned UniWorld-V1 (3.86) surpasses FluxKontext dev (3.52), OmniGen2 (3.44), and BAGEL (3.20), approaching Ovis-U1 (4.00) and GPT-4o (4.20). Fine-tuned OmniGen2 (3.82) exceeds its base (3.39) and FluxKontext dev (3.52), while fine-tuned MagicBrush (2.30) still trails all modern baselines, reflecting its older architecture. On GenEval, fine-tuned Harmon (0.86) surpasses GPT-4o (0.84) and BAGEL (0.82), while fine-tuned UniWorld-V1 (0.83) edges out BAGEL and OmniGen2 (0.82). On DPG-Bench, fine-tuned Harmon (85.60) outperforms BAGEL (85.07) and T2I-R1 (84.76), establishing a new open-source state of the art. Against the directly competing dataset ShareGPT-4o-Image, OpenGPT-4o-Image achieves consistent gains of 3.2% on ImgEdit-Bench, 1.7% on GEdit-Bench, 1.2% on GenEval, and 1.1% on DPG-Bench under identical UniWorld-V1 fine-tuning, attributed to its finer-grained taxonomy and richer instruction diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the emerging line of GPT-4o-based synthetic dataset construction (ShareGPT-4o-Image, Echo-4o-Image) by introducing a hierarchical taxonomy with 51 fine-grained subtasks across 11 domains, rather than the coarser categorization used in prior work. For image editing, it builds on the task coverage of MagicBrush, SEED-Data-Edit, UltraEdit, and OmniEdit while adding previously absent categories such as multi-turn editing, complex multi-operation instruction editing, and reference-image editing. For image generation, it departs from large-scale unstructured corpora (LAION, Megalith, Public Domain 12M) by organizing data around targeted capabilities—scientific imagery, spatial reasoning, in-image text rendering—that were not systematically addressed in prior instruction-following datasets. The automated pipeline combining structured resource pools with template-based prompt generation is a methodological refinement over the more ad-hoc prompting strategies in earlier GPT-4o distillation efforts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset provides a structured, reproducible training resource that enables targeted capability improvement in unified multimodal models, with the hierarchical taxonomy supporting fine-grained ablation and evaluation of specific sub-skills. The consistent improvements across four architecturally diverse models (diffusion-based, autoregressive, unified) validate that the gains stem from data quality and coverage rather than model-specific artifacts. Key limitations include the dependence on GPT-4o as a generation oracle (introducing potential model-specific biases and limiting long-term utility as the landscape shifts) and evaluation restricted to existing benchmarks that may not capture all real-world deployment scenarios. Open questions include whether the taxonomy generalizes to emerging capabilities (e.g., video, 3D) and whether the 'recalibrate the text, keep the image' quality-control strategy scales to more complex compositional tasks.
