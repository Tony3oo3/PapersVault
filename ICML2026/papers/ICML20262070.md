## Problem

People with limb differences are largely absent from existing large-scale vision-language datasets, and state-of-the-art VLMs consistently fail to detect residual limbs, identify prosthetic devices, or reason about functional capabilities in limb-deficiency contexts. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates VLMs on limb-deficiency-aware multimodal understanding grounded in medical-functional classification frameworks (WHO ICF, IPC WPA).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262070/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image from real-world scenarios (daily living, rehabilitation, or Paralympic sports) paired with a text question as input. Outputs are task-specific: residual-limb bounding boxes in [xmin, ymin, xmax, ymax] pixel coordinates, categorical labels over fine-grained taxonomies (8 deficiency types, 68 functional classes, 96 action categories), numeric counts, or free-text captions and rehabilitation guides. Under the BSI training scheme, the model is required to emit a structured sequence where bounding-box predictions precede the textual answer, enforcing spatial grounding before semantic generation.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a Body-centric Structure-aware Initialization (BSI) two-stage fine-tuning strategy for VLMs. Stage 1 freezes the vision encoder and LLM backbone, updating only the multimodal projector via LoRA (r=16) with cross-entropy loss on residual-limb bounding-box coordinates to establish spatial-structural priors. Stage 2 performs full-parameter fine-tuning on IVQA-LD with a unified objective that jointly supervises limb localization and disability-specific Q-A pairs, requiring the model to predict bounding boxes before generating answers. This attend-then-reason mechanism forces the model to ground its reasoning in limb-deficiency-relevant image regions, mitigating language-only shortcut learning and superficial question-answer correlations. The dataset itself comprises 80K expert-annotated VQA pairs across 8 tasks at 4 cognitive levels (perception, classification, reasoning, generation), with ~90% generated by trained annotators under structured guidelines and the remainder authored or fully verified by rehabilitation and biomechanics experts.

## Results

On the IVQA-LD benchmark, Qwen2.5-VL-7B fine-tuned with the proposed BSI strategy achieves the best performance across all eight task categories, reaching 52.60% on Deficiency Type classification, 26.34% Acc@0.5 on Visual Grounding, 90.24% on Prosthesis Counting, 75.40% on ICF-based Functioning and Disability Classification, and 79.20 CIDEr on Descriptive Caption Generation. These results substantially surpass the strongest closed-source baseline GPT-5 (e.g., 7.86% on ICF, 17.07% on VG, 19.55 CIDEr on DCG) and all open-source models, which remain near floor on most limb-aware tasks. The BSI strategy also improves over plain SFT fine-tuning of the same backbone, confirming the benefit of structure-aware initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The BSI-finetuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B outperforms all eight baselines on every task. Against the strongest closed-source model GPT-5, BSI gains +21.3 points on Deficiency Type (52.60 vs 31.30), +9.3 on Visual Grounding Acc@0.5 (26.34 vs 17.07), +19.1 on Prosthesis Counting (90.24 vs 71.18), +67.5 on ICF classification (75.40 vs 7.86), +30.6 on WPA classification (72.40 vs 41.83), and +59.7 CIDEr on caption generation (79.20 vs 19.55). Gemini-2.5-Pro, the second-best closed-source model, is surpassed by BSI on all metrics, with the largest gaps in ICF (+69.3) and CIDEr (+61.0). All open-source baselines (LLaVA-OneVision, LLaVA-NeXT, Llama-3.2-Vision, InternVL3.5, and zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL) remain far below BSI, with several scoring near 0–3% on ICF/WPA classification and under 8% on Visual Grounding, confirming a pronounced domain gap that general-purpose training does not close. Even plain SFT fine-tuning of Qwen2.5-VL-7B (without BSI) already surpasses all closed-source models on most tasks, but BSI adds further consistent gains, most notably on Deficiency Type (+15.0), Prosthesis-Human Interaction (+21.0), and CIDEr (+13.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of inclusive AI and multimodal VQA. It builds directly on the LDPose dataset (Ying et al., 2025) as its image source, extending a pose-estimation resource into a vision-language benchmark with expert clinical annotations. Conceptually, it departs from prior assistive VQA work (VizWiz for visual impairment, VQA-RAD/SLAKE for medical imaging) by targeting external, real-world body-centric scenarios with limb-deficiency-specific semantics grounded in WHO ICF and IPC WPA classification systems. The BSI fine-tuning strategy repurposes the curriculum-learning intuition—warm up on spatial grounding before semantic QA—within the standard VLM adaptation pipeline (LoRA + full SFT), rather than proposing a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IVQA-LD is the first large-scale expert-annotated VQA benchmark for limb-deficiency contexts, filling a clear gap where hundreds of millions of individuals are absent from mainstream vision-language resources. The finding that even GPT-5 scores below 8% on ICF classification and ~17% on residual-limb grounding underscores a systematic representational failure in frontier VLMs that general-purpose scaling does not resolve. Key limitations include fine-tuning experiments restricted to a single 7B base model (Qwen2.5-VL), a moderate 14K-image corpus, and reliance on LLM-as-a-judge for open-ended generation tasks. Open questions include whether the BSI strategy generalizes to other underrepresented populations or domain-specific VQA settings, and whether the 8:2 image-level split adequately prevents leakage given the 96 action and 68 functional class taxonomies.
