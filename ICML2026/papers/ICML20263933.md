## Problem

Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) offer parallel generation but suffer from two critical limitations: they lack KV caching (making inference far slower than autoregressive models) and exhibit a noticeable perplexity gap versus AR models. The prior hybrid approach BD3-LMs partially addresses speed via block-level caching but degrades severely at low sampling steps and still cannot cache within blocks. The paper asks whether a single transformer can fuse AR and MDM paradigms to interpolate smoothly between their perplexities while enabling exact KV caching throughout the entire generation process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263933/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence length L and a hyperparameter α₀ (expected fraction of tokens generated by diffusion) as configuration, starting from a fully masked sequence of length L. Generation proceeds in two phases: a diffusion phase that denoises a random subset of tokens in parallel over T steps, followed by a sequential AR phase that fills remaining mask tokens left-to-right. The output is a complete text sequence of length L. The unified denoising schedule S pre-computes which tokens are denoised at each step, and the total NFEs equals |S|, allowing flexible speed-quality trade-offs at inference time by varying α₀^eval and T independently of the training-time α₀^train.

## Method

Eso-LMs replaces the standard bidirectional-attention MDM denoiser with a decoder-only transformer using causal attention, exploiting the equivalence between MDMs and Any-Order autoregressive models. Training uses a hybrid loss: half the batch optimizes an MDM objective (with clean tokens shuffled before mask tokens and causal attention enforced via a permutation-dependent attention bias), and the other half optimizes an AR objective (using a concatenated z₀⊕x input with a specialized 2L×2L sparse attention mask that lets each mask token attend to its clean left context). At inference, the causal structure enables exact KV caching: previously denoised tokens' keys and values are computed once and reused, while the forward pass at each step is restricted to only the newly denoised and current mask tokens. This yields the first (asymptotically) exact likelihood formula for MDMs via an importance-weighted bound over random permutations, and establishes a new state-of-the-art on the speed-quality Pareto frontier for unconditional generation.

## Results

On LM1B (L=128), Eso-LMs smoothly interpolates between AR (21.86 PPL at α₀=0) and MDM (31.65 exact PPL at α₀=1), achieving 23.02 exact PPL at α₀=0.125 — beating MDLM (26.82 exact) and all BD3-LMs variants (28.23–30.60 NELBO). On OWT (L=1024), Eso-LMs at α₀=0.125 reaches 20.53 exact PPL, outperforming BD3-LMs (best 20.96 NELBO) and approaching AR (17.78). At long context (L=8192), Eso-LMs is ~65× faster than MDLM and ~3.8× faster than BD3-LMs (L'=4) while maintaining competitive sample quality on the speed–quality Pareto frontier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MDLM (Sahoo et al., 2024a), Eso-LMs at α₀=1 has worse exact PPL (31.65 vs. 26.82 on LM1B; 29.31 vs. ~25.19 NELBO on OWT) due to causal vs. bidirectional attention, but at α₀=0.125 Eso-LMs substantially outperforms MDLM (23.02 vs. 26.82 exact on LM1B; 20.53 vs. 25.19 NELBO on OWT). Against BD3-LMs (Arriola et al., 2025), Eso-LMs at α₀=0.125 beats the best BD3-LM variant (L'=4) on both datasets: 23.02 exact vs. 28.23 NELBO on LM1B and 20.53 exact vs. 20.96 NELBO on OWT, while also avoiding BD3-LMs' severe quality degradation at low NFEs. Against the AR baseline, Eso-LMs at α₀=0 exactly matches AR PPL (21.86 LM1B, 17.78 OWT), confirming seamless interpolation. Against older diffusion baselines (D3PM, Diffusion-LM, DiffusionBert, SEDD), Eso-LMs dominates by large margins (e.g., 23.02 vs. 76.90 for D3PM Absorb on LM1B). On inference speed at long context (L=8192), Eso-LMs is ~65× faster than MDLM and ~3.8× faster than BD3-LMs (L'=4), establishing a new state of the art on the speed–quality Pareto frontier for unconditional generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Eso-LMs builds on the MDLM framework (Sahoo et al., 2024a) and the Any-Order AR equivalence for MDMs (Ou et al., 2025), but departs from both by replacing bidirectional attention with causal attention over a random permutation, enabling KV caching for the first time in MDMs. It also extends the AR-MDM interpolation idea from BD3-LMs (Arriola et al., 2025), which interpolates via block size, to a fundamentally different mechanism: varying the fraction α₀ of tokens generated by diffusion over the full sequence. The foundational discrete diffusion process follows D3PM (Austin et al., 2021), and the specialized attention masks leverage FlexAttention (Dong et al., 2024) for efficient implementation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to perform exact KV caching during diffusion while preserving parallel generation is a significant practical advance, yielding 14–65× speedups over MDMs and 3–4× over BD3-LMs at long contexts, making diffusion LMs viable for real-time applications. The first (asymptotically) exact likelihood formula for MDMs also unlocks RL-based finetuning, which was previously intractable. Key limitations include a ~1.37× training slowdown from doubled sequence length in the sequential phase, a perplexity gap versus MDLM at α₀=1 (mitigated by the fact that Eso-LMs produces higher-quality samples at every finite NFE), and a one-step KV lag that makes it slightly slower than pure AR at the same NFE. Open questions include scaling to multi-billion-parameter models, downstream task performance, and whether the α₀=1 perplexity gap can be closed without sacrificing KV caching.
