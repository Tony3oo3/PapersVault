## Problem

The Shampoo optimizer, despite its strong empirical performance in language model pretraining, incurs substantial computational overhead per optimizer step due to expensive inverse matrix root computations. Existing implementations process preconditioner blocks sequentially and rely on suboptimal matrix scaling (Frobenius norm), creating a trade-off between runtime speed and optimization quality that limits Shampoo's practical adoption.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-layer gradient matrices G ∈ R^{m×n} and maintains left/right preconditioner states L ∈ R^{m×m} and R ∈ R^{n×n} as exponential moving averages. These are split into B×B blocks (B ∈ {1024, 2048}) and stacked into 3D tensors of shape (N, B, B) for batched GPU processing, where N is the total number of blocks across all layers assigned to a worker. The output is updated model parameters θ_{t+1} = θ_t − η_t · s_t · L^{-1/4} G R^{-1/4}, with the inverse roots computed per stacked batch in FP16 or FP32 precision.

## Method

DASH introduces two complementary techniques to accelerate Shampoo's inverse root computation. First, it replaces sequential per-block processing with a stacking strategy that concatenates all B×B preconditioner blocks (from L, R, and their inverse roots) into a single 3D tensor, enabling one batched matrix-multiplication call per iteration and full tensor-core utilization. Second, it introduces the Newton-Denman-Beavers (NDB) iteration as a faster inverse-root solver and replaces Frobenius-norm scaling with multi-Power-Iteration, which estimates the spectral radius via a pool of 16–32 parallel starting vectors to push the eigenvalue spectrum toward 1 where NDB converges in fewer steps. The combination of batched execution, NDB, and Power-Iteration scaling achieves up to 5.6× faster optimizer steps versus Distributed Shampoo while attaining the lowest validation perplexity among all tested inverse-root methods.

## Results

On Llama-953M pretrained on C4 (9089 steps), DASH matches Distributed Shampoo (DIST) in validation perplexity across nearly all configurations (differences ≤ 0.01), while reducing optimizer step time by up to 5.6× (DIST-CN-FP32 at 666 ms vs. DASH-CN-FP16 GPU at 119 ms for B=1024). The Newton-Denman-Beavers (NDB) solver with Power-Iteration scaling achieves the lowest validation perplexity of 11.68 (B=1024) and 11.72 (B=2048), outperforming both EVD (11.80/11.72) and CN (11.87). For the fastest runtime, DASH-CN-FP16 with Frobenius scaling in GPU mode reaches 119 ms per optimizer step, a 5.6× speedup over the prior best DIST-CN-FP32 at 666 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Distributed Shampoo (DIST, Shi et al. 2023), the well-optimized reference implementation. DASH matches DIST in validation perplexity for EVD and CN across all block sizes and frequencies (differences ≤ 0.01), confirming that the architectural changes (3D block stacking, batched matmuls) do not degrade optimization quality. On runtime, DASH achieves 1.08–1.26× speedup for EVD, 1.44–5.6× for CN, and 1.25–4.18× for NDB, with the largest gains coming from the combination of block stacking, FP16 matmuls, and GPU-level cross-layer batching. Notably, NDB with Power-Iteration scaling (a DASH contribution) achieves lower perplexity than DIST's EVD (11.68 vs. 11.80 at B=1024) and CN (11.68 vs. 11.87), while also being faster than DIST-EVD by 3.8–4.2×. DIST's NDB with Frobenius scaling fails entirely at B=2048 (numerical instability), whereas DASH's Power-Iteration scaling remains stable, further validating the scaling analysis in Section 3.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

DASH is a systems-level acceleration of Distributed Shampoo (Shi et al., 2023), which itself implements the Shampoo optimizer (Gupta et al., 2018; Anil et al., 2020) with block preconditioning. The paper departs from prior work in two conceptual directions: (1) it introduces the Newton-Denman-Beavers iteration (Higham, 2008) and Chebyshev polynomial approximation as alternative inverse-root solvers beyond the EVD and Coupled-Newton methods already present in Distributed Shampoo, and (2) it provides a convergence analysis showing that the Frobenius-norm scaling used in prior implementations is suboptimal, motivating a multi-Power-Iteration approach for spectral-radius estimation. The architectural contribution—stacking preconditioner blocks into 3D tensors for batched GPU execution—is a repurposing of standard tensor-core batching applied to the specific structure of block preconditioners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Shampoo has emerged as a leading second-order optimizer (AlgoPerf winner, improved quantization robustness, lower activation outliers), but its computational overhead has limited widespread adoption; DASH's up-to-5.6× reduction in optimizer-step time makes high-quality preconditioning practical for routine use. The finding that Frobenius-norm scaling degrades convergence (requiring 3× more iterations for small eigenvalues) and that Power-Iteration scaling yields measurably lower validation perplexity (11.68 vs. 11.76) is a practical insight that corrects a default heuristic in the existing codebase. Open questions include stabilizing NDB in FP16/BF16, dynamic per-block solver selection based on condition number, and validation at larger model and data scale with tensor-parallel training.
