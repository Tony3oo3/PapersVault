## Problem

Existing VAE-based methods for disentangling shared and condition-specific representations suffer from three recurring failures: they leave condition-specific information in the invariant representation without an explicit enforcement mechanism, allow the invariant representation to collapse into an uninformative state, or impose independence assumptions (e.g., unconditional z ⊥ w) that misalign with the true conditional generative structure. No prior method simultaneously addresses all three while maintaining a principled probabilistic objective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of input–label pairs (x, y) where x ∈ ℝ^d and y ∈ {1,…,K} denotes the experimental condition. It produces two d-dimensional latent codes (z invariant, w condition-specific) parameterized as diagonal Gaussians, along with two reconstructions: x̂ from z alone and x̃ from (z, w). The adversarial classifier g(y|z) is applied to the z-reconstruction x̂ rather than to z directly, which reduces sampling variance and acts as a regularizer. The prior mean for w is computed as the class-wise average of inferred z values, coupling the two latents only at the population level.

## Method

DisCoVR formulates disentanglement as a max-min variational game over q_{z|x}, q_{w|x,y}, and an adversarial classifier g_{y|z}, with a proven unique saddle point under convexity conditions. The objective combines two ELBO terms (one for the marginal posterior p_{z|x} and one for the joint posterior p_{z,w|x,y}) with a negative cross-entropy adversarial term that penalizes predictability of y from the z-reconstruction. A key structural choice is the prior p(w|y=k) = N(μ_k, I) where μ_k is the class-wise mean of z, which anchors w's prior in the learned invariant space without conditioning on individual z samples, thereby preventing z from absorbing condition-specific leakage while forcing z to remain informative. Two separate decoder paths—one reconstructing x from z alone and another from (z, w)—further compel both representations to carry complementary information. The classifier is trained adversarially (minimized) while the encoders and decoders are trained to maximize the combined objective, implemented with the reparameterization trick and single-sample Monte Carlo estimates.

## Results

On the Noisy Swiss Roll benchmark, DisCoVR achieves the lowest deviation from the optimal Bayes classifier (Δ-Bayes = 1.14 ± 0.21) and near-zero mutual information between latent factors (I(z;w) = 0.005 ± 0.002), while maintaining reconstruction quality (NLL = 3.302). On the parametric model, DisCoVR reduces the KL divergence of the invariant posterior from 1.77 (CCVAE) to 0.17 and Δ-Bayes from 3.57 to 0.1. On CelebA-Glasses, DisCoVR achieves the lowest I(z;w) = 0.030 ± 0.011 among all methods. On the scRNA-seq dataset, DisCoVR attains the highest z-Cell Type NMI (0.716) while keeping z-Stimulation NMI at 0.002, confirming clean separation of cell-type identity from stimulation effects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisCoVR outperforms all four baselines on the primary disentanglement metrics. Against CSVAE (Klys et al., 2018), DisCoVR reduces Δ-Bayes from 17.99 to 1.14 and I(z;w) from 0.031 to 0.005 on Noisy Swiss Roll, while CSVAE's reconstruction-only-from-w design leaves z uninformative. Against HCSVAE (Beker et al., 2024), DisCoVR dramatically improves Δ-Bayes from 30.00 to 1.14, showing that the hierarchical extension still fails to separate label information from the shared representation. Against DIVA (Ilse et al., 2020), DisCoVR reduces I(z;w) from 0.336 to 0.005 and Δ-Bayes from 1.88 to 1.14, confirming that DIVA's unconditional independence assumption z ⊥ w allows substantial label leakage into z. Against CCVAE (Joy et al., 2020), DisCoVR reduces I(z;w) from 0.502 to 0.005 and Δ-Bayes from 2.21 to 1.14, demonstrating that CCVAE's prior p(z|y) which permits z to depend on y undermines disentanglement. On CelebA-Glasses, DisCoVR achieves the lowest I(z;w) (0.030) but with a slightly higher NLL (135.68) than CCVAE (131.76) and HCSVAE-No Adv. (131.81), reflecting a modest reconstruction trade-off for stronger disentanglement.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisCoVR extends the VAE-based disentangled representation learning line initiated by CSVAE (Klys et al., 2018), DIVA (Ilse et al., 2020), and CCVAE (Joy et al., 2020), which each addressed partial aspects of separating condition-invariant and condition-specific factors. It departs from these methods by (i) formulating a probabilistic objective with the correct conditional independencies (z ⊥ w | y, x ⊥ y | w) rather than unconditional independence, (ii) coupling the prior on w to the class-wise mean of z to keep z informative while preventing label leakage, and (iii) using two distinct reconstruction paths to enforce informativeness of both representations. The adversarial classifier is framed as posterior regularization (Ganchev et al., 2010), and the weighting scheme follows β-VAE (Higgins et al., 2017), but the key novelty is the structured prior and the theoretical guarantee of a unique saddle point for the max-min game.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DisCoVR provides the first variational disentanglement framework with a provable unique equilibrium, which is valuable for practitioners who need convergence guarantees in adversarial training settings. The clean separation demonstrated on single-cell RNA-seq data (cell type in z, stimulation effect in w) has direct practical implications for biological interpretability and cross-condition generalization. Limitations include the higher computational cost of the adversarial classifier (noted on CelebA), reliance on single-sample Monte Carlo estimates, and the fact that the class-wise mean coupling in the prior requires access to all class members during training, which may be problematic in streaming or few-shot settings. An open question is whether the two-reconstruction architecture can be simplified or whether the adversarial term can be replaced by a tighter mutual information bound without the associated training instability.
