## Problem

Existing dynamic data selection methods rely on task-specific handcrafted metrics (e.g., gradient norms, clustering statistics) that are difficult to generalize across learning paradigms, and they typically use static or snapshot-based scores that fail to capture how sample utility evolves throughout training. The paper asks whether an agent can adaptively select data on the fly in a training-aware, plug-and-play manner that scales across tasks and architectures without task-specific redesign.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265419/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each training step, the agent consumes the backbone's feature embeddings (e.g., pre-FC outputs for classification, detection loss for YOLOv8) as the state, along with per-sample loss values and softmax prediction probabilities computed from standard forward passes. The policy outputs a continuous selection weight in [0,1] per sample; the top-k weighted samples form the training subset for the next epoch. Unlike discrete select/discard schemes, the continuous action space avoids combinatorial complexity and enables differentiable policy optimization in a non-stationary environment.

## Method

Data Agent formulates dynamic data selection as a Markov decision process solved with PPO, where the state is the evolving backbone feature embedding, the action is a per-sample continuous selection weight, and the reward is a composite of a loss-based difficulty signal (R_diff) and a confidence-based predictive-entropy signal (R_conf). Two propositions provide theoretical grounding: R_diff is monotonic in gradient magnitude (optimization impact), while R_conf is proportional to Fisher information (expected information gain), making the two signals complementary. A tuning-free adaptive weighting coefficient r = Var(R_diff)/(Var(R_diff)+Var(R_conf)+ε) automatically shifts emphasis from difficulty early in training to uncertainty later, creating a self-adjusting curriculum. The actor and critic are lightweight three-layer MLPs optimized with PPO's clipped objective and GAE advantage estimation, and the entire agent co-evolves with the target model in a closed-loop pipeline with only ~3% wall-clock overhead.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18, Data Agent achieves 78.9% top-1 accuracy at 50% selection ratio, surpassing the full-dataset baseline of 78.2%. On Tiny-ImageNet with ResNet-50, it reaches 47.0% at 50% selection (vs. 45.0% full dataset), and on ImageNet-1k with ResNet-50 at 60% selection, it achieves 76.8% accuracy (vs. 76.4% full dataset) while saving over 55 GPU hours. For LLM instruction tuning with LLaMA-7B on MMLU at 50% data, it improves from 34.9% to 36.9%, and on MS-COCO object detection with YOLOv8 at 70% data, it matches the full-dataset mAP of 39.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Data Agent consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and selection ratio. Among static methods, the strongest competitors are CLIP-Sel (94.5% on CIFAR-10 at 50%) and Moderate (73.4% on CIFAR-100 at 50%), both of which trail Data Agent by 0.8% and 5.5% respectively; EL2N and GraNd are further behind at 95.0% and 94.6% on CIFAR-10. Among dynamic methods, InfoBatch is the closest competitor, achieving 95.1% on CIFAR-10 and 78.1% on CIFAR-100 at 50% selection, but Data Agent still leads by 0.2% and 0.8% respectively, with a much larger gap on Tiny-ImageNet (47.0% vs. 43.2%, a 3.8% margin). UCB and ε-Greedy (Hong et al., 2024) are competitive on CIFAR-10 (94.7% and 94.9%) but are not reported on Tiny-ImageNet, and both fall short of Data Agent's 95.3%. On ImageNet-1k at 60% selection, Data Agent (76.8%) surpasses InfoBatch (76.5%), UCB (75.8%), and all static methods (≤73.4%), while also reducing total GPU hours from 140 (full dataset) to 85. On noisy Tiny-ImageNet with 20% label noise, Data Agent achieves 38.1% accuracy at 20% selection, outperforming CLIP-Sel (26.1%) and Random* (32.5%) by over 8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic data selection line (InfoBatch, UCB, ε-Greedy, GREATS) by reframing sample selection as a sequential decision-making problem solved with a lightweight PPO agent, rather than relying on handcrafted loss-based or uncertainty-based heuristics. It repurposes the difficulty and uncertainty signals already present in static methods (DUAL, InfoBatch) as reward components within an RL loop, adding a variance-based adaptive weighting mechanism to balance them without manual tuning. The modular reward design also absorbs the cross-modality semantic alignment idea from CLIP-Sel for noisy-data robustness, positioning the framework as a unifying wrapper around existing signal designs rather than a fundamentally new scoring criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play generalization across image classification, object detection, semantic segmentation, and LLM instruction tuning with a single 3-layer MLP agent is practically significant, offering 50%+ training cost reduction with lossless or improved performance and minimal overhead (~0.125 h on ImageNet). Open questions include whether a pretrained generic agent can be rapidly adapted to new tasks (reducing amortized cost), whether the co-evolutionary non-stationarity remains well-controlled at much larger scales, and whether the same agentic formulation extends to curriculum pacing or data generation. The paper's main limitation is that the RL agent must be trained jointly with the target model, precluding a fully offline selection strategy.
