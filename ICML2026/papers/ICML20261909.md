## Problem

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has become the dominant method for aligning language models with human preferences, yet its theoretical behavior in distributed settings—where preference data are fragmented across heterogeneous users subject to privacy constraints and communication limits—remains entirely unexplored. Standard distributed nonconvex optimization theory does not apply because DPO's trajectory-level log-ratio objective induces sigmoid-weighted gradients whose variance depends on the current policy, and preference heterogeneity across clients amplifies drift in ways absent from federated SGD analysis.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local dataset of offline preference pairs (trajectory comparisons) drawn from its own user-specific distribution, along with initial policy parameters and a frozen reference policy. The policy is parameterized as a log-linear softmax over a finite action space, and the DPO loss operates on full trajectory log-probability differences rather than per-token scores. The output is optimized policy parameters accompanied by explicit convergence rate guarantees whose constants depend on the RL horizon H, inverse temperature β, trajectory mixing rate, and distributed parameters (heterogeneity κ, local steps E, participation S, spectral gap ρ).

## Method

The paper derives closed-form expressions for the DPO smoothness constant L = (β²C_mix + 2β)ζ_φ²H and gradient variance ζ_g² = 4β²C_mixζ_φ²H, making all optimization constants explicit in terms of horizon, temperature, and trajectory mixing. FedDPO integrates DPO with FedAvg under full and partial client participation, yielding convergence rates that quantify the interplay between client drift (η²Eκ²), sampling variance (ζ_g²/S), and communication frequency, with extensions to asynchronous staleness and formal lower bounds proving these dependencies are intrinsic. DecDPO extends to fully decentralized peer-to-peer learning over general graphs, where the spectral gap of the mixing matrix governs consensus speed and the algorithm achieves the same O(R^{-1/2}) rate as classical decentralized SGD despite the nonlinear interaction between consensus error and sigmoid-weighted preference gradients.

## Results

On the SHP dataset with 5 agents (N=5, 120 preference pairs each) and DistilGPT-2 (~82M params), all four methods (Centralized DPO, FedDPO full S=5, FedDPO partial S=3, DecDPO ring ρ=0.54) show monotonically decreasing gradient norms and DPO loss over 80 rounds. DecDPO ring achieves the steepest descent and lowest final gradient norm, while Centralized DPO converges slowest due to its smaller effective batch size per round. Ablation studies confirm theoretical predictions: local steps E=6 yields a final stationary gap of 0.039 vs. E=3 (0.0956) and E=1 (~0.87); participation S=5 reduces the gap to ~0.10 vs. S=1 (~0.56); and spectral gap effects span an order of magnitude across topologies (path ~0.35, ring ~0.04, complete ~0.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole baseline is Centralized DPO (oracle with pooled data), serving as the upper-bound reference. DecDPO ring outperforms Centralized DPO in both gradient norm descent speed and final gradient norm, attributed to its larger effective local computation (5 local steps vs. E=3 for FedDPO) and the absence of a communication bottleneck through a central server. FedDPO full (S=5) also outperforms Centralized DPO by benefiting from parallel gradient aggregation across all clients. FedDPO partial (S=3) sits between the two, incurring a participation-variance penalty (ζ_g²/S term) that slows convergence relative to full participation. Notably, DecDPO achieves the best final gradient norm despite having no central coordinator, validating the theoretical claim that decentralization incurs no asymptotic penalty relative to centralized DPO, with only mixing-matrix constants influencing convergence speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) from its original centralized formulation to federated and decentralized learning settings, building on the FedAvg framework (Li et al., 2019b) and decentralized gradient descent protocols (Jiang et al., 2017). It departs from standard distributed nonconvex optimization theory because DPO's trajectory-level log-ratio objective induces sigmoid-weighted gradients whose variance depends on the current policy state, and preference heterogeneity across clients creates drift patterns not captured by classical FedAvg or decentralized SGD analyses. Unlike prior DPO variants (ODPO, uncertainty-penalized DPO, token-level DPO) that focus on algorithmic refinements, this work provides the first convergence and time-complexity guarantees for DPO under communication constraints and non-IID preference distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous convergence theory for distributed DPO, providing explicit constants (smoothness L, variance ζ_g²) in terms of RL-specific quantities (horizon H, inverse temperature β, trajectory mixing rate) and distributed parameters (heterogeneity κ, local steps E, participation S, spectral gap ρ), along with lower bounds confirming these dependencies are intrinsic. Key limitations include the restriction to log-linear softmax policies (extension to transformers requires controlling Fisher information spectra), the exponential mixing assumption that may yield loose constants in long-horizon settings, and the absence of adaptive optimizers, communication compression, or Byzantine robustness. The empirical validation, while confirming theoretical predictions, uses a small model (DistilGPT-2, 82M params) with only 5 agents and 120 preference pairs, leaving open whether the qualitative conclusions hold at LLM scale. Open questions include polynomial mixing extensions, LoRA-based parameter-efficient distributed DPO, and whether the O(1/H) step-size scaling observed here persists in practice for long-context models.
