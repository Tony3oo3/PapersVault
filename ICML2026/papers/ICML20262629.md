## Problem

Existing DPO-based hallucination mitigation for MLLMs relies on external evaluators (e.g., GPT-4V) to score or rewrite responses, which introduces two critical failures: discretization loss that collapses fine-grained preference differences into a few labels, and an off-policy distributional mismatch that renders external feedback unlearnable under DPO's KL-divergence constraint. The paper asks whether the model's own implicit reward signal—defined in continuous log-probability space—can replace external evaluators to identify and correct internal modal conflicts that cause hallucinations.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed by CLIP ViT-L/14) and a free-form text instruction as input, and produces a grounded natural-language response. During training, the model samples K=5 candidate responses per prompt from its own current policy at temperature 0.7; these candidates are scored using length-normalized log-likelihood ratios between consecutive policies, and the highest- and lowest-scoring candidates form a preference pair. The SFT warm-up uses 5,700 image-instruction-response triples from RLHF-V, and the entire preference alignment stage operates without any external human or AI feedback.

## Method

IRIS replaces external discrete evaluators with DPO's implicit reward (the log-likelihood ratio between the current policy and a frozen reference) as a continuous scoring signal in the model's native probability space. A key mechanism is Rectified Visual Guidance (RVG), which computes a composite score S = r_image − γ·max(0, r_text − r_image) by subtracting a penalty when a response is more strongly supported under text-only context than under the image, thereby down-weighting candidates driven by language priors rather than visual evidence. After an SFT warm-up that calibrates the implicit reward landscape, IRIS iteratively samples K candidates from the current policy, sifts them into on-policy preference pairs via RVG scoring, and optimizes a composite objective combining standard DPO (conditional textual preference), a conditional visual preference term that penalizes high reward under perturbed images, and an anchored regularization that prevents the absolute likelihood of preferred responses from drifting downward. The iterative design uses separate references for scoring (two consecutive policies) and for DPO optimization (frozen copy of the previous round's policy), enabling progressive expansion of the KL-feasible region without external feedback.

## Results

On the 7B LLaVA-1.5 backbone, IRIS-R2 (2 rounds, 5.7K samples, no external feedback) achieves CHAIR 2.4, HalRate 11.3, and Cog 1.1 on AMBER, and CHAIRs 8.66 / CHAIRi 4.56 on Object HalBench, reducing object hallucination by 84% relative to the base model (CHAIRs 54.70). On the 13B backbone, IRIS-R2 reaches CHAIR 3.5, HalRate 18.0 on AMBER and CHAIRs 10.0 / CHAIRi 5.49 on Object HalBench. The method also preserves general capability, scoring 56.4% overall on LLaVA-Bench (in-the-Wild) versus 55.7% for the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IRIS-R2 (7B, 5.7K samples, no external feedback) outperforms most baselines on object-level hallucination metrics: it reduces CHAIRs to 8.66 vs. 35.70 (mDPO), 41.40 (HALVA), 20.4 (SymMPO), and 24.3 (LPOI), while also improving AMBER HalRate (11.3 vs. 24.5, 32.2, 25.4, 26.4 respectively). Against the closest competitor OPA-DPO (4.8K, GPT-4V), IRIS wins on Cover (51.1 vs. 47.9), HalRate (11.3 vs. 11.6), and CHAIRs (8.66 vs. 13.00), but trails slightly on CHAIR (2.4 vs. 2.2), Cog (1.1 vs. 0.9), and CHAIRi (4.56 vs. 4.25). Against RLAIF-V (16K, LLaVA-Next), IRIS achieves lower CHAIR (2.4 vs. 3.0), lower HalRate (11.3 vs. 16.2), and substantially lower CHAIRs (8.66 vs. 16.00), though RLAIF-V edges ahead on CHAIRi (3.70 vs. 4.56). On the 13B backbone, IRIS-R2 similarly dominates: CHAIRs 10.0 vs. 33.3 (mDPO), 45.40 (HALVA), 18.3 (SymMPO), and 24.3 (LPOI). Notably, IRIS achieves these results with only 5.7K samples and zero external evaluator calls during alignment, whereas most baselines require 10K–122K samples scored by GPT-4V or other proprietary models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO's implicit reward formulation (Rafailov et al., 2023), repurposing the log-likelihood ratio between consecutive policies not as a training signal but as a data-curation signal for sifting self-generated candidates into preference pairs. It departs from the dominant external-evaluator paradigm (RLAIF-V, OPA-DPO, SymMPO, HALVA) that scores or rewrites responses with GPT-4V, human annotators, or large open-source labelers, and instead introduces Rectified Visual Guidance (RVG) to penalize candidates whose likelihood is inflated by language priors rather than visual evidence. The on-policy iterative refinement loop follows the general strategy suggested by Tajwar et al. (2024) and the SFT-calibrates-implicit-reward connection from Wang et al. (2025a), but applies it specifically to multimodal hallucination mitigation where no prior work had used the model's own probability space for preference construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a ~11.5× reduction in preference-data curation cost and removal of dependency on proprietary or large open-source evaluator models, which is meaningful for labs without GPT-4V API access or for iterative alignment loops where re-curation at each round would otherwise be expensive. However, the method is validated only on LLaVA-1.5 (7B/13B) with a preliminary one-round Qwen2.5-VL-3B check, and gains on compositional hallucination (MMHal-Bench) are modest compared to object-level metrics, leaving open whether the signal is sufficient for more complex reasoning failures. A broader open question is whether implicit-reward sifting generalizes to audio- or video-conditioned models where the 'visual' prior is replaced by other modality priors.
