## Problem

Multi-concept personalized generation in diffusion models suffers from a fundamental trilemma: enforcing identity fidelity disrupts prompt-consistent layout, rigid layout control suppresses fine-grained identity details, and cross-concept interference amplifies both failures. Existing approaches treat customization as a uniform control problem across the entire denoising trajectory, ignoring the coarse-to-fine spectral hierarchy of diffusion where low-frequency structure emerges early and high-frequency texture resolves late, leading to systematic zero-sum trade-offs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing a multi-concept scene (e.g., 'a rabbit wearing a black hat playing the guitar at home') and n reference concept images as input, all processed at 768×768 resolution on an SDXL backbone. Reference images are spatially aligned to a design blueprint via bounding-box-guided warping before feature extraction. The output is a single coherent RGB image containing all referenced concepts with preserved identity, prompt-consistent spatial arrangement, and semantic disentanglement, generated entirely training-free.

## Method

OTComp decomposes training-free multi-concept composition into three stages aligned with diffusion's spectral dynamics: (1) Layout Planning samples a design blueprint from the base T2I model and extracts per-concept regions via SAM3; (2) Structure Sketching applies Barycentric Soft-Transport (BST) during early denoising (t∈[5,20]), where each generated feature token is projected onto the convex hull of its top-k nearest reference tokens via a temperature-scaled softmax, minimizing a barycentric divergence as a gradient guidance step on the latent; (3) Texture Rendering applies Geometry-Guided Transport (GGT) in later timesteps, computing a Sinkhorn-Knopp transport plan on low-frequency structural costs (cosine similarity plus a Gaussian spatial penalty) to inject high-frequency texture residuals while enforcing mass conservation. A Concept-wise Masked Multi-Reference Attention (CWSA) mechanism imposes a union-window connectivity constraint on the attention graph, allowing reference information to flow only within concept-specific spatial neighborhoods and blocking cross-concept semantic leakage.

## Results

On MagicBench (the paper's proposed benchmark), OTComp achieves state-of-the-art performance across all concept counts without any fine-tuning. For 4-concept composition, it reaches CLIP-T 0.3828, CLIP-I 0.7627, and DINO 0.6844, outperforming the strongest baseline (OminiControl) by +0.022 CLIP-T, +0.140 CLIP-I, and +0.178 DINO. The performance gap widens as concept count increases: on 2-concept tasks the DINO margin over Diptych Prompting is +0.107, growing to +0.178 on 4-concept tasks. On FreeBench, OTComp similarly leads with CLIP-I 0.7821 and DINO 0.6747, surpassing the next-best method (Diptych Prompting) by +0.056 and +0.094 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTComp outperforms all 8 baselines on every metric (CLIP-T, CLIP-I, DINO) across both MagicBench and FreeBench, and is the only training-free method to do so. The closest competitor is Diptych Prompting (FLUX.1, training-free), which leads on CLIP-T for 2-concept tasks (0.3503 vs. 0.3528 — a narrow margin) but trails OTComp by 0.052 on CLIP-I and 0.107 on DINO for 2 concepts, with the DINO gap widening to 0.167 at 4 concepts. Among tuning-based methods, OminiControl (FLUX.1) is the strongest on 4-concept CLIP-T (0.3606) but still lags OTComp by 0.022, and falls far behind on identity fidelity (DINO 0.5068 vs. 0.6844). DreamBooth, despite requiring per-concept fine-tuning, achieves only DINO 0.4415 on 4-concept MagicBench — 0.243 below OTComp. FreeCustom, the primary training-free baseline for multi-concept composition, degrades most severely with concept count (DINO drops from 0.5367 to 0.3311), while OTComp maintains DINO above 0.68 even at 4 concepts. In the human preference study, OTComp achieves 74.51% top-1 preference for identity fidelity, 69.61% for prompt alignment, and 65.20% for semantic disentanglement, compared to at most 16.18% for any competing method on any axis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of training-free multi-concept composition methods (FreeCustom, Diptych Prompting) by replacing their uniform, stage-agnostic attention or guidance strategies with a dual optimal-transport formulation that respects diffusion's coarse-to-fine spectral dynamics. It builds on the OT-based concept alignment of Lv et al. (2024) and the backward-guidance mechanism of Bansal et al. (2023), but departs from both by decomposing the transport into two spectrally-separated stages: a barycentric soft-transport for early structural alignment and a geometry-gated Sinkhorn plan for late texture injection. The paper explicitly contrasts with parameter-space merging (Mix-of-Show, LoRA interpolation) and hard spatial-masking approaches (Attend-and-Excite, Spatex), arguing that both fail because they impose a single uniform constraint across the entire denoising trajectory rather than respecting the frequency hierarchy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spectral decoupling principle—imposing structural constraints early and texture constraints late—offers a general design guideline for any diffusion-based personalization or composition task, potentially transferable beyond multi-concept settings. The training-free nature (no per-concept fine-tuning) makes the approach scalable to arbitrary numbers of reference concepts, and the introduced MagicBench provides a more realistic evaluation protocol than prior single-concept or template-based benchmarks. Key limitations remain: the method depends on the quality of the initial layout from the base model and SAM3 segmentation, and cannot recover from upstream failures such as omitted concepts or heavily occluded objects. Open questions include whether iterative layout verification or segmentation-aware feedback loops could close this gap, and whether the spectral stage boundaries generalize across architectures (e.g., DiT-based models like FLUX).
