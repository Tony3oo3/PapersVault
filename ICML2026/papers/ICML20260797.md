## Problem

Prior high-dimensional analyses of pool-based active learning with linear classifiers relied on oracle or sample-splitting assumptions, treating the base classifier as either perfect or trained on a separate held-out dataset at zero labeling cost. More broadly, iterated empirical risk minimization (ERM) procedures—where the predictions of a first estimator enter the loss of a second trained on the same data—lacked a rigorous asymptotic theory in the high-dimensional regime n ≍ d, due to the intricate statistical dependencies induced by data reuse and prediction-dependent losses.

## Input / Output

The framework takes n unlabeled d-dimensional covariates, a total labeling budget γn, a budget allocation ψn for the base classifier, convex loss functions, and regularization strengths λ, λ₀. The output is a finite system of self-consistent scalar equations (involving order parameters q, t, θ, m_c, q_0, θ_0, m_{0,c}) whose solution, combined with a (3+|V|)-dimensional Gaussian random vector, yields an explicit asymptotic prediction for any Lipschitz test metric of the final estimator. The key structural feature is that the second-stage proximal map argument includes a correction term proportional to the first-stage loss derivative, encoding the data-reuse correlations that distinguish this setting from single-stage ERM.

## Method

The main technical result (Theorem 1.1) establishes a sharp asymptotic characterization of the second-stage ERM estimator's test error via two nested applications of the leave-one-out technique, extending the single-stage framework of Karoui (2013) and El Karoui (2018) to the iterated setting. The second-stage estimator's statistics are governed by a proximal map whose argument is no longer purely Gaussian but includes an additional correction term χ∂_u ℓ₀(u, g₃, c, ε) that compactly captures the correlations between each single-sample loss and the entire dataset through the first estimator. For the active learning application, the framework models margin-based selection where a base classifier trained on ψn samples identifies the (γ−ψ)n samples closest to its decision boundary, and the final classifier is trained on the union of both sets. This reveals a non-monotonic budget-allocation tradeoff (an optimal ψ* minimizing test error) and a selection-driven double descent at fixed model size and sample count—phenomena absent from prior oracle-based analyses and validated on a chest X-ray pneumonia diagnosis dataset.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is a sharp asymptotic characterization (Theorem 1.1) of the test error for two-stage iterated ERM in the high-dimensional regime n ≍ d, expressed through a finite set of scalar order parameters satisfying self-consistent equations. In the active learning instantiation (Fig. 1, α=8, λ=0.01, quadratic loss, d=2000), the test error exhibits a U-shaped dependence on the budget allocation ψ, with an optimal ψ* minimizing error for each total budget γ, and a selection-driven double-descent peak near ψ ≈ 1/α. On the real chest X-ray pneumonia dataset (Kermany et al., 2018; scattering transform, logistic loss, α=5.41, d=2000), the same U-shaped tradeoff is clearly observed (Fig. 2), confirming the theory beyond synthetic Gaussian settings. Theoretical predictions match numerical experiments to within one standard deviation across all tested configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing active learning algorithms in a standard tabular format; instead, it compares its budget-constrained, data-reuse framework against the oracle/sample-splitting assumptions prevalent in prior high-dimensional active learning analyses (Sorscher et al., 2022; Kolossov et al., 2023; Feng et al., 2024; Dohmatob et al., 2025; Askari-Hemmat et al., 2025). Key comparisons include: (1) Active selection (ψ < γ) consistently outperforms passive random selection (ψ = γ), with the gap widening as γ decreases; (2) Reusing the base training set S₀ in the final classifier (solid lines, Fig. 1) yields significantly lower test error than discarding it (dashed inset), demonstrating that prior works' sample-splitting assumption wastes labeled data; (3) Small-margin selection (π(u) = 1{|u| < κ⁻}) outperforms large-margin selection (π(u) = 1{|u| > κ⁺}) in the fully budget-constrained setting (Fig. 3, α=1), reversing the oracle-case finding of Sorscher et al. (2022) where large-margin selection is preferable when a good base estimator is available at no budget cost; (4) A mixed policy retaining both small- and large-margin samples (Fig. 4, α=8, γ=0.3, ψ=0.1, d=3000) can outperform either pure strategy, echoing observations in Kolossov et al. (2023) and Hacohen & Weinshall (2023).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-stage high-dimensional ERM asymptotics of Karoui (2013), El Karoui (2018), and Thrampoulidis et al. (2018) to a two-stage iterated ERM where the first estimator's predictions enter the second loss, using a novel nested leave-one-out proof technique. It departs from prior active learning analyses (Sorscher et al., 2022; Kolossov et al., 2023; Dohmatob et al., 2025) that assumed an oracle base classifier or a free held-out dataset, and from the state-evolution framework of Celentano et al. (2025), which does not yield explicit scalar order-parameter characterizations. The paper also contrasts with non-rigorous statistical-physics treatments (Saglietti & Zdeborova, 2022; Takahashi, 2022) and the square-loss-only analysis of Garg et al. (2025), providing the first fully asymptotic, loss-general characterization of iterated ERM on the same data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing oracle and sample-splitting assumptions, the paper delivers the first principled treatment of the fully budget-constrained pool-based active learning problem, revealing a non-trivial U-shaped tradeoff in how the labeling budget should be split between training the base classifier and selecting informative samples. The discovery of a selection-driven double descent—occurring at fixed model dimension and fixed total sample size, driven purely by training-set composition—is a new phenomenon with no analogue in the classical double-descent literature. Open questions include optimizing the selection policy π in closed form, extending the analysis to multi-stage iterated ERMs and non-Gaussian data, and understanding whether the budget-allocation tradeoff persists for nonlinear models.
