## Problem

Existing Graph Masked Auto-Encoders focus on reconstruction outcomes but give limited specification to how neighborhood context is weighted during reconstruction. The paper shows through reconstruction-matched diagnostics that similar reconstruction losses can correspond to substantially different attention-based context-weighting patterns, which in turn produce downstream performance shifts. Additionally, uniform masking tends to be dominated by regions with strong local support, leaving weakly-supported or role-mixed regions undertrained.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph with node features, its adjacency matrix, and precomputed truncated Laplacian eigenvectors (computed once offline and reused across all epochs). It operates a dual-branch encoder: a Feature branch that takes masked features and amplified positional distances to reconstruct node features, and a PE branch that takes clean features and noised amplified positional distances to predict edge-wise positional distances. The frozen encoder outputs node representations for linear-probe node classification, or pooled graph representations for graph-level regression/classification.

## Method

C2-GMAE regularizes the reconstruction process through three coupled mechanisms. First, relative positional distances derived from Laplacian eigenvectors are injected as a head-wise bias into pre-softmax attention scores, anchoring edge-level context weighting to a fixed structural reference. Second, density-partitioned masking sorts nodes by 1-hop/2-hop feature similarity, splits them into B equal-size partitions, and samples the same number of masked nodes from each partition, ensuring every local-support level receives equal training budget. Third, heterophilic edges (identified unsupervised via low feature-similarity quantiles) are amplified at four sites: scaling positional noise around masked nodes with many heterophilic neighbors, scaling PE distances on heterophilic edges, adding a gate to pre-softmax attention scores, and upweighting heterophilic edges in the positional regression loss. The final objective combines a Scaled Cosine Error feature reconstruction loss (on masked nodes plus a residual target on unmasked nodes) with a Smooth-L1 positional regression loss computed only on edges incident to masked nodes.

## Results

On node classification (Table 1), C2-GMAE achieves the best mean accuracy on all 8 datasets, with particularly large margins on heterophilic graphs: 42.59% on Actor (+4.04 over GraphPAE), 82.42% on Chameleon (+1.91), and 74.80% on Squirrel (+2.75). On the two large-scale benchmarks, it reaches 42.16% on arXiv-year and 74.17% on Penn94, outperforming the next-best GraphPAE by 0.31 and 2.38 points respectively. On graph prediction (Table 2), C2-GMAE attains the lowest RMSE on all three regression tasks (e.g., 0.996 on molesol vs. 1.015 for GraphPAE) and the highest ROC-AUC on all three classification tasks (e.g., 81.97 on molbace vs. 81.11 for GraphPAE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C2-GMAE outperforms all 13 node-classification baselines on every dataset, with the closest competitor being GraphPAE (Liu et al., 2025), the most recent positional-encoding GMAE. The largest absolute gains appear on heterophilic graphs: +4.04 points on Actor (42.59 vs. 38.55 for GraphPAE), +2.75 on Squirrel, and +1.91 on Chameleon, while on homophilic Facebook and WikiCS the margins over GraphPAE are modest (+0.11 and +0.27). Among contrastive baselines, Sp²GCL and BGRL are the strongest, yet C2-GMAE still leads by 7–15 points on heterophilic sets. On the six OGB graph-prediction tasks, C2-GMAE again beats all 10 baselines; GraphPAE is the nearest rival (e.g., 0.996 vs. 1.015 RMSE on molesol; 81.97 vs. 81.11 ROC-AUC on molbace), while contrastive methods such as Sp²GCL and JOAO trail by 1–3 RMSE points on regression. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that both density-partitioned masking and heterophilic-edge amplification contribute: removing either drops Actor accuracy by ~1–2 points and Chameleon by ~1.5–1.8 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

C2-GMAE extends the GraphMAE corruption-reconstruction framework by shifting focus from reconstruction targets and masking heuristics to the reconstruction *process*—specifically, how neighborhood context edges are weighted during decoding. It borrows relative positional encoding from GraphPAE (Liu et al., 2025) and GRPE (Park et al., 2022) as a structural anchor for edge scoring, and adopts the Scaled Cosine Error and residual-target formulation from Zheng et al. (2025). The key departure is the introduction of density-partitioned masking for coverage and a four-site heterophilic-edge amplification mechanism, neither of which appears in prior GMAE variants that primarily refine decoders, targets, or uniform masking schedules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reconstruction-matched diagnostic showing that similar reconstruction losses can correspond to different attention-based context-weighting patterns and downstream accuracy shifts provides a useful lens for evaluating GMAE methods beyond reconstruction loss alone. The method's consistent gains on heterophilic benchmarks (Actor, Chameleon, Squirrel) suggest that explicitly preserving cross-type relational evidence during masked reconstruction is a practical lever for self-supervised graph learning. However, the reliance on precomputed Laplacian eigenvectors limits scalability to very large graphs, the four-site amplification introduces dataset-sensitive hyperparameters (especially the attention gate γ), and the gains on strongly homophilic graphs are modest, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to extreme heterophily or billion-node-scale settings.
