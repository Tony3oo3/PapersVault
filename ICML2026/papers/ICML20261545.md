## Problem

Autoregressive image generation models treat visual tokens as a flat, unstructured vocabulary, discarding the geometric relationships in the codebook where proximity reflects semantic similarity. This forces the model to solve a massive N-way classification at every step, wasting capacity re-learning semantic structure and hindering convergence. Existing codebook priors based on k-means are inadequate because Euclidean distance and arithmetic-mean centroids are poor proxies for semantic relationships on the curved token manifold.

## Input / Output

The system takes a class label as input and generates a 256×256 RGB image. MASC operates as a one-time offline preprocessing step on the pre-trained tokenizer's codebook (N=16,384 tokens in d-dimensional space), producing a mapping from fine-grained token indices to k coarse cluster indices (default k=8,192). During inference, the AR model outputs coarse cluster indices which are decoded back to fine tokens via uniform random sampling within the predicted cluster (or optionally a small refinement network for hierarchical decoding).

## Method

MASC constructs a hierarchical semantic tree from the codebook using two core components: (1) a centroid-free, manifold-aligned distance metric (average-linkage/UPGMA) that computes inter-cluster dissimilarity as the mean of all pairwise Euclidean distances between tokens in two clusters, avoiding off-manifold centroids; and (2) a density-driven agglomerative construction that iteratively merges the closest pair of clusters, naturally prioritizing dense, semantically coherent regions. The resulting tree is cut at level k to yield a mapping M:{1,...,N}→{1,...,k}, and the AR Transformer is trained to predict coarse cluster indices rather than fine token indices, transforming the flat N-way classification into a structured k-way task. This plug-and-play module accelerates training by up to 71%, improves FID (e.g., LlamaGen-XL from 2.87 to 2.49), and enables convergence for complex architectures like RAR that otherwise diverge on large vocabularies.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, MASC consistently improves FID across all LlamaGen scales: 4.81 (−13.5%) on LlamaGen-B, 2.92 (−13.6%) on LlamaGen-L, and 2.58 (−10.1%) on LlamaGen-XL, while simultaneously accelerating training by +42%, +55%, and +57% respectively. As a plug-and-play module, MASC further reduces FID on VAR-d24 from 2.09 to 1.98 (−5.26%) and on RandAR-XL from 2.25 to 2.02 (−10.22%). Notably, MASC enables convergence for the RAR framework with 16k-token tokenizers, where the unstructured baseline diverges entirely, achieving FID 1.57–1.67 with MASC(4k).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LlamaGen baseline (Sun et al., 2024), MASC wins decisively on all three scales, reducing FID by 10–13.5% while also improving IS and Recall; the k-means prior, by contrast, degrades FID by 3–13% and slows training. On VAR-d24 (Tian et al., 2024), MASC(2k) improves FID from 2.09 to 1.98 (−5.26%) with +28% training acceleration, whereas k-means(2k) worsens FID to 2.56 (+22%). For RandAR-XL (Pang et al., 2025), MASC(8k) achieves FID 2.02 (−10.22%) versus the 2.25 baseline, while k-means(8k) degrades to 2.62 (+16%). On IAR (Hu et al., 2025), MASC(8k) improves FID by 3.5–6.8% across B/L/XL scales with +21–32% acceleration. CTF-LlamaGen-L (Guo et al., 2025) sees the largest relative gain: FID drops from 2.97 to 2.51 (−15.49%) with +29% speedup. GigaTok-L (Xiong et al., 2025) shows a modest FID improvement (3.39→3.35) at k=8,192, confirming MASC's benefit even with a strong tokenizer. Finally, RAR (Yu et al., 2024) diverges without MASC on 16k-token codebooks but converges to FID 1.57–1.67 with MASC(4k), establishing MASC as a convergence enabler rather than merely a quality booster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the codebook-prior line of work (Hu et al. 2025; Guo et al. 2025) that re-introduces structural information into autoregressive image generation, but departs fundamentally from their k-means-based clustering. The authors argue k-means is geometrically ill-suited to the token embedding manifold because Euclidean distance misrepresents semantic similarity and arithmetic-mean centroids fall off-manifold. MASC replaces this with a centroid-free average-linkage distance within a density-driven agglomerative hierarchical construction (Lukasova 1979; Ward 1963), drawing on manifold learning principles (Tenenbaum et al. 2000) to build a principled semantic tree. The result is positioned as a plug-and-play preprocessing module applicable to any discrete-token AR framework (LlamaGen, VAR, RandAR, IAR, CTF, GigaTok).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most impactful finding is that MASC acts as a convergence enabler for random-permutation architectures (RAR) that otherwise diverge entirely on 16k-token vocabularies, suggesting that structuring the prediction space is a prerequisite for scaling certain AR paradigms rather than merely an optimization trick. The 71% training acceleration and consistent FID improvements across five distinct architectures underscore the practical value of this one-time offline preprocessing step. Open questions include whether the benefits persist for text-to-image generation, whether dynamic or context-aware priors could further exploit the hierarchy, and how the approach scales to the 3B+ parameter tokenizers now emerging. A limitation is that all validation is on ImageNet class-conditional generation at 256×256, leaving open questions about transfer to open-ended or higher-resolution settings.
