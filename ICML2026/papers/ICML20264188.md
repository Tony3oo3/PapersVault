## Problem

Generative models systematically produce samples whose distributional statistics—class proportions, secondary structure frequencies, demographic representations—deviate from desired targets, yet no general fine-tuning procedure exists that imposes arbitrary expectation constraints while staying close to the base model. Prior approaches either apply to a single model class, handle only low-dimensional constraints, or enforce constraints during training rather than as a post-hoc calibration step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264188/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained base generative model with tractable likelihoods (diffusion, normalizing flow, or autoregressive LM), a possibly high-dimensional constraint function h(x) (e.g., a 99-dimensional indicator vector for protein secondary structure CDF cutoffs, or a per-profession gender indicator for LLMs), and a target expectation h*. It outputs a fine-tuned model whose parameters are updated via stochastic gradient descent; the constraint h need not be differentiable, and the method supports up to 10^3 simultaneous constraints. For LLMs, calibration is applied via a single shared LoRA adapter across multiple profession-conditional prompts rather than separate fine-tunes.

## Method

The paper frames calibration as finding the model closest in KL divergence to the base model subject to E_{p_θ}[h(x)] = h*, then introduces two surrogate objectives that remove the hard constraint. CGM-relax replaces the constraint with a squared ℓ₂ violation penalty plus a λ-weighted KL term, optimized via an importance-sampling gradient estimator with leave-one-out baselines. CGM-reward instead connects the problem to the maximum entropy principle: it first estimates the optimal tilt parameters α* by solving a finite-sample convex dual (Eq. 7), then fine-tunes by minimizing KL divergence to the resulting exponential tilt, which is equivalent to reward fine-tuning with r_α(x) = αᵀh(x). Both algorithms require only sampling from p_θ, computing p_θ(x), and ∇_θ log p_θ(x), and use the score-function gradient estimator with stop-gradient importance weights for unbiased, low-variance updates. CGM-relax scales to 10³ constraints while CGM-reward becomes infeasible beyond ~30, making the relax loss the more robust choice for high-dimensional calibration.

## Results

Across three applications, CGM-relax consistently reduces miscalibration: for protein design, it reduces the symmetrized KL distance to CATH secondary-structure distributions by >4× for Genie2 and >2× for ESM3-open while keeping design-failure rates at 14% (vs. 74% from simply increasing sampling noise). For conditional image generation on AFHQ wildlife, CGM-relax (λ=10⁻⁴) reduces the total variation distance of animal proportions to uniform from 0.306 to 0.101. For the 9B-parameter Gemma-2-9B-IT LLM, CGM-relax reduces gender-profession imbalance by more than 3× while maintaining symmetric KL < 1.7 from the base model. In simulation, CGM-relax scales to 10³ simultaneous constraints, whereas CGM-reward becomes infeasible beyond ~30 constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior calibration methods. Against Khalifa et al. (2021) on the Gemma-2-9B-IT gender-bias task, CGM-relax reduces constraint violation by up to 94% versus Khalifa's ~81%, and CGM-reward achieves a small but statistically significant (two-sided t-test) improvement over Khalifa at the same distance to the base model. Against Khalafi et al. (2026) (augmented Lagrangian / primal-dual method) in the 1D GMM simulation, CGM-relax outperforms even when CGM-reward is given the oracle α*, and CGM-relax scales to 10³ constraints while the primal-dual approach is validated only on ≤4 constraints. A third prior method, Shen et al. (2024), is noted as not directly comparable because it requires a pre-trained probabilistic classifier and relies on optimal transport, limiting it to diffusion models with known class labels. The paper also shows that simply increasing sampling noise (Genie2 σ: 0.5→1) or temperature (ESM3 τ: 0.7→1) achieves some diversity gain but at prohibitive failure rates (74% and 97% respectively), whereas CGM-relax maintains a 14% failure rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads—maximum-entropy reweighting (Kullback, 1959; Wainwright & Jordan, 2008) and reward fine-tuning (Rafailov et al., 2023; Williams, 1992 score-function estimator)—into a single calibration framework for generative models. Prior distributional-constraint methods (Khalifa et al., 2021 for autoregressive LMs; Shen et al., 2024 for diffusion via optimal transport; Khalafi et al., 2026 for diffusion via primal-dual) each target a single model class and are validated only on low-dimensional (≤4) constraints. CGM departs from these by requiring only tractable likelihoods (no specific architecture) and by handling up to 10³ simultaneous constraints, with the key conceptual bridge being that the calibration constraint itself generates the reward r_α(x) = αᵀh(x) rather than requiring a user-specified one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, model-agnostic recipe for fixing miscalibration in any generative model with a computable likelihood, demonstrated across protein design (Genie2, ESM3), image generation (TarFlow), and language modeling (Gemma-2-9B) with up to hundreds of simultaneous constraints. Two important limitations remain: (1) the method is inapplicable to implicit models like GANs where likelihoods are intractable, and (2) rare-event calibration (base probability < 10⁻³) still leaves substantial residual error, which is precisely the regime most relevant to protein ensemble modeling. The finding that CGM-relax outperforms CGM-reward even with oracle α* suggests the reward-loss formulation has intrinsic limitations beyond estimation error, pointing to open questions about the geometry of the parametric model class relative to the maximum-entropy solution.
