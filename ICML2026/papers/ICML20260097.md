## Problem

While the Edge of Stability (EOS) for full-batch gradient descent and the Edge of Stochastic Stability (EoSS) for vanilla mini-batch SGD are well characterized, it remained unclear whether momentum-based optimizers (SGDM, SGDN) self-organize at an analogous instability boundary in the stochastic regime, and if so, which curvature threshold they saturate. The paper reveals a surprising batch-size-dependent reversal: in small batches, momentum *tightens* the operative curvature constraint relative to vanilla SGD, biasing training toward flatter regions, while in large batches it recovers the classical stabilizing role that permits sharper curvature.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on neural network parameters θ ∈ ℝ^d, consuming mini-batch gradients g_B(θ) and mini-batch Hessians H_B(θ) sampled from the mini-batch distribution P_b to compute the Batch Sharpness statistic BS(θ) = E_B[g_B^T H_B g_B / ‖g_B‖²]. The primary outputs are (i) the batch-size-dependent plateau level of Batch Sharpness as a function of η, β, and b, and (ii) intervention responses: destabilizing perturbations (η↑, β↑, b↓) trigger catapult dynamics (loss spikes + curvature excursions) followed by restabilization, while stabilizing perturbations reopen progressive sharpening. Experiments use MLPs, CNNs, ResNet-18, and a small ViT on CIFAR-10, SVHN, and SST-2 with MSE loss.

## Method

The paper combines empirical characterization, intervention-based certification, and linear mean-square stability analysis. Empirically, Batch Sharpness is tracked across training and shown to plateau at 2(1−β)/η in the noise-dominated small-batch regime and at 2(1+β)/η (SGDM) or 2(1+β)/(η(1+2β)) (SGDN) in the large-batch regime, with a broad monotone transition in between. To certify that these plateaus reflect a genuine instability boundary rather than a descriptive artifact, the authors perform mid-training checkpoint interventions: small destabilizing changes to η, β, or b that push Batch Sharpness above its plateau trigger catapults, while stabilizing changes reopen progressive sharpening, and these transitions are governed by Batch Sharpness rather than full-batch λ_max. Theoretically, a linearized mean-square stability analysis of the augmented state (x_t, v_t) near an interpolating minimizer shows that in the noise-dominated regime the dominant slow-mode operator reduces to I_{d²} − η_eff K + η_eff² G with η_eff = η/(1−β), matching the vanilla SGD stability operator of Ma & Ying (2021) at the effective step size, thereby explaining the small-batch threshold 2(1−β)/η. A 1D closed-form interpolation formula 1/η_max = a/(2(1+β)) + σ_b²/(2a(1−β)) explicitly bridges the deterministic and stochastic limits.

## Results

The paper's headline quantitative finding is that Batch Sharpness under SGDM/SGDN plateaus at 2(1−β)/η in the small-batch (noise-dominated) regime and at 2(1+β)/η (SGDM) or 2(1+β)/(η(1+2β)) (SGDN) in the large-batch regime, with a broad interpolation in between. In the noise-dominated limit, the linear mean-square stability analysis (Theorem 4.1) reduces SGDM(η,β) to vanilla SGD with effective step size η_eff = η/(1−β), yielding the small-batch threshold 2/η_eff = 2(1−β)/η. Intervention experiments confirm that destabilizing perturbations (increasing η, increasing β in small-batch, or decreasing b) trigger catapult dynamics precisely when Batch Sharpness is pushed above its operating plateau, while stabilizing perturbations reopen progressive sharpening.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to vanilla SGD EOSS (Andreyev & Beneventano, 2024), the paper shows a qualitative reversal: in small batches, momentum *tightens* the curvature constraint (plateau 2(1−β)/η < 2/η), biasing training toward flatter regions, whereas in large batches it recovers the classical stabilizing role. Against full-batch GD with momentum (Cohen et al., 2021), the paper demonstrates that the monotonic increase of the stability threshold in β (2(1+β)/η) holds only in the large-batch limit; in small batches the trend reverses, with higher β yielding a *lower* plateau. The SGDM run with η=0.001, β=0.9, b=4 matches the λ_max stabilization level of vanilla SGD with η=0.01, b=4 (Figure 5), confirming the η_eff reduction, though the two trajectories do not track each other in parameter space (Appendix G). For Adam (Cohen et al., 2022), the paper shows that the EMA normalization cancels the stochastic (1−β₁) factor, making the small-batch preconditioned sharpness nearly independent of β₁, in contrast to the full-batch threshold 2/η·(1+β₁)/(1−β₁) which increases with momentum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Edge of Stochastic Stability (EOSS) framework of Andreyev & Beneventano (2024) from vanilla SGD to momentum-based optimizers (SGDM and SGDN), and connects it to the deterministic Edge of Stability thresholds for momentum established by Cohen et al. (2021). The theoretical analysis builds on the mean-square stability machinery of Wu et al. (2018) and Ma & Ying (2021), showing that in the noise-dominated regime SGDM reduces to SGD with an effective step size η/(1−β). The key departure from prior work is the discovery that the stability threshold is not a single momentum-corrected value but splits into two batch-size-dependent regimes, with a qualitative reversal of momentum's role in the small-batch limit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that small-batch momentum *tightens* the operative curvature constraint (2(1−β)/η < 2/η) rather than relaxing it challenges the standard intuition that momentum is uniformly stabilizing, with direct implications for hyperparameter coupling (keeping η/(1−β) fixed when varying β) and for understanding why small-batch momentum training selects flatter regions. Key open questions include: a direct momentum-specific instability criterion (Batch Sharpness is currently justified only indirectly via reduction to SGD), the implications for generalization and solution quality, and whether the two-regime structure persists at larger model scales. The experiments are limited to models up to ResNet-18 and a small ViT, and the theoretical bridge from the operator stability condition to Batch Sharpness remains incomplete outside the small-batch reduction.
