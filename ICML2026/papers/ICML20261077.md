## Problem

AI-generated video detectors trained on the widely-used GenVideo benchmark exhibit a hidden quality shortcut: when real training data (e.g., Kinetics-400) is substantially lower quality than generated data (e.g., Pika), models learn to classify by visual fidelity rather than forgery artifacts, causing severe performance collapse on real videos with quality comparable to or exceeding generated samples. Existing benchmarks mask this deficiency because their evaluation real data shares the same narrow, low-quality profile as training data, creating an illusion of generalization.

## Input / Output

The method operates on video clips and produces a real/fake detection score, evaluated via AUC with 1:1 real-fake pairing against 9 generative models. For image-level backbones (DINOv2, DINOv3, NPR, SAFE, AIDE), 8 frames are sampled per video and their prediction scores are averaged to yield a video-level score. The approach is backbone-agnostic, validated across both image-level and video-level architectures (TimeSformer, VideoMAEv2, DeMamba), requiring no architectural modification—only a change in training data composition.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage data-driven training strategy grounded in video quality assessment via DOVER scores. First, quality-aware data alignment replaces the biased Kinetics-400 + Pika training pair with InternVid-AES + Pika, where the DOVER quality distributions of real and generated videos closely overlap, eliminating the quality gap that enables shortcut learning. Second, quality-aware data expansion augments the aligned baseline with LQ data (Kinetics-400 + OpenSora) and UHQ data (OpenVidHD + DynamiCrafter), each selected to minimize quality mismatch with its paired generator, thereby covering the full quality spectrum and forcing the model to learn quality-agnostic forgery features. The strategy is validated across seven diverse backbones, yielding an average 6.06% Overall Mean AUC improvement over the biased baseline, and is further supported by a revised evaluation protocol spanning six real datasets across three quality tiers.

## Results

Across seven detector backbones (image-level: SAFE, AIDE, DINOv2, DINOv3; video-level: DeMamba, TimeSformer, VideoMAEv2), the quality-aware Aligned training setting yields an average +3.18% Video-level AUC over the Biased baseline, while the full Expanded configuration adds a further +6.06% on average. The best individual result is DINOv2 with Expanded training, reaching 94.83 Overall Mean AUC across six real datasets spanning low, high, and ultra-high quality tiers. On the GenVidBench cross-dataset evaluation, Expanded training also consistently achieves the best performance (e.g., DINOv2: 91.17 vs. 84.32 Biased).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's only direct method-level comparison is against DDA (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2025), which performs active pixel-level alignment via 2D frequency-domain processing and image-based VAE reconstruction. Using DINOv2 as the backbone, DDA achieves an Overall Mean AUC of 66.65 on Kinetics-400 (Biased) and 63.27 on InternVid-AES (Aligned), substantially below the paper's own Biased baseline (84.07) and Aligned setting (89.81). The gap is most pronounced on ultra-high-quality real data (RealVSR: 48.14/77.68 for DDA vs. 74.98/93.29 for the paper's approach; UltraVideo: 36.57/67.94 vs. 63.53/83.46). The authors attribute DDA's inferiority to its image-level design being ill-suited for video, where modern generators employ video-level VAEs and temporal modules that introduce temporal artifacts invisible to 2D pixel manipulation. The paper's selection-based quality matching preserves content authenticity while effectively closing the quality shortcut, yielding a 17–27 point AUC advantage over DDA on the hardest test sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within the AI-generated video detection literature by identifying a previously overlooked data curation issue: the quality distribution mismatch between real and synthetic training videos induces shortcut learning. While prior work (GenVideo, GenVidBench, GenBuster-200K, GenBuster++) has focused on generalization to unseen generators, this paper shows that quality homogeneity in real data is a confounding factor that masks true generalization. The closest conceptual relative is DDA (Chen et al., 2025), which aligns real and fake image distributions via active pixel manipulation; here, the authors instead achieve alignment through intelligent data selection, arguing this preserves temporal authenticity critical for video. The paper also repurposes the VQA model DOVER as a diagnostic tool for stratifying datasets by quality, a use not previously applied to AIGV detection benchmark design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work exposes a systemic blind spot across all major AIGV detection benchmarks: their real data is confined to narrow quality bands, creating an illusion of generalization that collapses on higher-quality real videos. The practical implication is significant—detectors deployed in the wild will encounter diverse quality profiles, and the revised six-dataset protocol provides a more honest evaluation. Open questions include how quality dynamics will shift as both real-world video quality and generative model fidelity continue to escalate, and whether active augmentation (e.g., quality degradation/enhancement) could complement the passive selection strategy proposed here. The authors acknowledge their approach is 'relatively passive' compared to active augmentation, leaving proactive methods as future work.
