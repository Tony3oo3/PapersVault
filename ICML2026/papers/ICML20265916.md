## Problem

Non-monotonic sequence generation methods like masked diffusion models operate on a fixed-length canvas and marginalize over all possible update schedules, making them order-agnostic and unable to natively support variable-length generation. Existing learning-order approaches address the order-agnosticism issue but are confined to fixed-canvas settings where global positions are static; extending them to variable-length insertion is non-trivial because insertion locations are relative and evolve with the partial sequence, tightly coupling generation order to intermediate states.

## Input / Output

The model starts from an empty sequence and, at each step, selects one of i+1 actions (i insertion slots or a TERM signal) and a token to insert, growing the sequence until termination. For conditional generation, the input is a partial sequence (e.g., subgoal tokens for maze planning, a partial SMILES fragment with restricted insertion slots for molecular design), and the output is a variable-length completion whose length is not prescribed a priori. The number of available insertion slots grows linearly with the current sequence length, and in constrained tasks only a subset of slots may be permitted.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution is a bijection between insertion trajectories and permutations of the target sequence indices, which converts trajectory-dependent relative slot indices into a fixed latent space over permutations and yields an exact permutation-marginalized likelihood. The Insertion Process (IP) is an encoder-decoder Transformer: the generative decoder scores candidate insertion slots (location head) and predicts token content (content head) conditioned on the chosen slot, while the inference encoder is a bidirectional Transformer that outputs Plackett-Luce scores over the full observed sequence. Training maximizes a permutation ELBO where the inner expectation over the next permutation element is computed in closed form for variance reduction, and the outer expectation is estimated via Gumbel-Top-k sampling with a REINFORCE Leave-One-Out (RLOO) gradient estimator. Unlike fixed-canvas masked diffusion, IP natively supports variable-length generation through an explicit termination mechanism (policy-based or classifier-based) and learns data-driven preferences over insertion orders rather than marginalizing over all of them.

## Results

On Maze Planning, IP achieves near-perfect accuracy across all maze families and difficulty levels (99.9–100.0% on BRAIDED, 95.3–100.0% on IMPERFECT, 97.9–99.4% on PERFECT), dramatically outperforming the best baseline FO-ARM which degrades to 27.2% on PERFECT-HARD. On Star Graph Planning, IP reaches 83.0% sequence accuracy and 85.7% token accuracy, versus 30.1% and 72.5% for the best baseline LO-ARM. On unconditional GuacaMol SMILES generation, IP (classifier-based termination) achieves 97.4% validity, 95.6% novelty, 97.2% KL score, and 89.2% FCD, combining high structural validity with the highest novelty among all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IP dominates all baselines on both planning benchmarks. On PERFECT mazes (the most constrained setting), IP scores 97.9% on HARD while FO-ARM drops to 27.2%, LO-ARM to 25.2%, and FlexMDM/MDM/AO-ARM collapse to 0.0–0.1%. On Star Graph Planning, IP's 83.0% sequence accuracy nearly triples the best baseline LO-ARM (30.1%) and reduces Levenshtein distance from 4.16 to 2.44. On GuacaMol, IP (classifier-based) achieves the highest novelty (95.6%) and validity (97.4%), while FO-ARM/Transformer slightly leads on distributional metrics (KL 99.4 vs 97.2, FCD 90.3 vs 89.2), indicating a flexibility–fidelity trade-off. The random-order ablation AO-IP confirms that learning the insertion policy is critical: it drops validity from 97.4% to 88.2% on GuacaMol and from 99.8% to 40.9% on fragment decoration. Compared to the LSTM/VAE/AAE baselines from Brown et al. (2019), IP substantially outperforms on novelty (95.6% vs 87.5%/84.7%/82.1%) while maintaining comparable validity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: insertion-based variable-length generation (Insertion Transformer, Stern et al. 2019; Gu et al. 2019a; Patel et al. 2025) and learning-order autoregressive models (LO-ARM, Wang et al. 2025b). Prior learning-order methods operate on fixed-length canvases where token positions are pre-assigned, while insertion models are order-agnostic and marginalize over all possible insertion schedules. The key conceptual departure is the permutation-trajectory bijection (Lemma 2.1), which converts the trajectory-dependent relative slot indices into a fixed latent space over global permutations, enabling an exact ELBO for variable-length insertion — a combination that the authors argue is non-trivial because insertion locations evolve with the partial sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, likelihood-based way to learn data-driven generation orders in settings without a canonical left-to-right structure, with the permutation reparameterization yielding an unbiased ELBO estimator. The interpretable SMILES generation order (skeleton/ring closures first, then atoms, then termination) suggests the model internalizes structural grammar without explicit rules, which is promising for molecular design. However, all empirical validation is on synthetic or small-scale benchmarks (maze grids ≤41×41, star graphs of degree 5, GuacaMol SMILES), and the factorial permutation space limits sequence length in practice. Open questions include scaling to longer sequences, application to protein or code generation, and whether the learned orders transfer across related tasks.
