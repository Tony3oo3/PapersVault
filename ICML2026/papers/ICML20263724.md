## Problem

Standard score matching for diffusion models minimizes an upper bound on the negative log-likelihood rather than the data likelihood itself, which can lead to severe loss of statistical efficiency compared to maximum likelihood estimation. This paper asks whether one can directly maximize the data likelihood in a diffusion model training framework, and if so, how to make it computationally tractable given that the exact reverse transition densities are intractable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263724/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of i.i.d. samples from the target distribution (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR-10, 64×64 CelebA, 224×224 ImageNet) and a standard Gaussian noise vector as the starting point for generation. During training, N intermediate time points are randomly sampled per iteration from the simplex (0,T), and the forward SDE is evaluated at those points to construct the quasi-likelihood. The stochastic sampler (12) produces a generated sample by iterating N reverse steps, each using both the learned score and Hessian to define a Gaussian transition with data-dependent mean and covariance, rather than the fixed-covariance transitions of standard DDPM.

## Method

The key insight is that the expected log-likelihood of the data distribution equals (up to a constant) the sum of log reverse transition densities along a forward path (Proposition 3.1), enabling a direct likelihood-based training objective. Since the true reverse transition densities are intractable, the paper applies Quasi-Maximum Likelihood Estimation: each transition is approximated by a Gaussian whose mean depends on the score ∇log q_t and whose covariance depends on both the score and the Hessian ∇²log q_t (Proposition 4.1). Two separate U-Net networks parameterize the score s_t and Hessian H_t, with the Hessian using a diagonal-plus-low-rank (rank r) structure for computational efficiency, and the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury formula applied to compute the Gaussian log-likelihood in O(d·r) time. The resulting Likelihood Matching (LM) objective unifies score matching and covariance matching with an implicit likelihood weighting, reducing to standard score matching when the Hessian is zero. The paper provides non-asymptotic TV-distance convergence guarantees for the sampler and proves consistency of the QMLE under an oracle parametric model.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Likelihood Matching (LM) with Hessian rank r=30 achieves FID 3.03 and NLL 3.11 bpd, outperforming the Score Matching (SM) baseline (FID 3.15, NLL 3.28 bpd). On CelebA 64×64, LM(r=30) reaches FID 2.62 versus 2.71 for SM. Even the simplest diagonal Hessian variant (r=0) already improves FID on both datasets (3.12 vs. 3.15 on CIFAR-10; 2.69 vs. 2.71 on CelebA), and performance peaks around r=20–30 before diminishing returns set in at higher ranks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline throughout the paper is Score Matching (SM) from Song et al. (2021c), the standard training objective for score-based diffusion models. LM consistently outperforms SM on every reported metric and dataset: on CIFAR-10, LM(r=30) reduces FID from 3.15 to 3.03 and NLL from 3.28 to 3.11 bpd; on CelebA, FID drops from 2.71 to 2.62. On the 1D Gaussian and t-distribution mixture experiments, LM achieves lower MMD than SM across all transition-step settings (N=2, 3, 8), with the gap widening as N increases. On the Swiss-roll distribution, LM(N=2) yields consistently lower log-MMD than SM at every sampling step, confirming the benefit of Hessian-informed covariance on curved manifolds. In the MNIST speed-quality trade-off (Figure 4), LM reaches a given FID with fewer reverse sampling steps than SM, and after only 20 reverse iterations LM produces clearly recognizable digits while SM samples remain noticeably blurred. The only metric where the two methods are essentially tied is Inception Score on CIFAR-10 (9.45–9.48 range), suggesting the gains are concentrated in sample fidelity (FID) and likelihood (NLL) rather than class diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard score-matching training paradigm for diffusion models (Song et al., 2021c; Ho et al., 2020), which minimizes an upper bound on the negative log-likelihood, by instead directly targeting the data likelihood through a quasi-maximum likelihood approximation of the reverse diffusion path. It builds on the reverse-time SDE framework of Anderson (1982) and Haussmann & Pardoux (1986) to establish an exact equivalence between the data likelihood and the reverse path likelihood, then applies the QMLE framework of Wedderburn (1974) to make this tractable. The method also incorporates the low-rank Hessian parameterization of Meng et al. (2021) for computational efficiency, and positions itself against both likelihood-oriented ODE training (Song et al., 2021b; Lu et al., 2022) and discrete-time covariance-tuning strategies (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021; Bao et al., 2022), arguing that its continuous-time QML formulation provides a distinct likelihood-based training paradigm rather than a surrogate or ELBO-based objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled MLE-based training objective for continuous-time diffusion models with non-asymptotic convergence guarantees and a consistency proof, filling a gap where prior methods only optimized surrogate bounds. Practically, even a diagonal Hessian (r=0) consistently outperforms score matching on FID and NLL, and the Hessian-informed sampler reduces the number of reverse steps needed for a given quality. However, the 3–4× training time and 2–3× memory overhead on ImageNet, combined with modest FID gains (~0.1–0.15 on CIFAR-10), limit immediate large-scale adoption. Open questions include whether the Hessian modeling cost can be further reduced, whether the approach scales to high-resolution or video generation, and how it interacts with modern architectures like rectified flow transformers.
