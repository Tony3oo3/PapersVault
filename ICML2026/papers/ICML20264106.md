## Problem

Existing label shift estimators (moment-matching and likelihood-based) are fragile in two ways: they depend on well-calibrated source classifiers that degrade in data-scarce regimes, and they operate on raw inputs where strict conditional invariance is easily broken by domain-specific nuisances such as sensor artifacts or style variations. This paper asks whether shifting the estimation to a discriminative latent subspace can simultaneously reduce sensitivity to input-level perturbations and improve numerical stability of the moment-matching linear system.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled source set and an unlabeled target set of RGB images, along with a frozen pre-trained backbone (ResNet-18 for MNIST/CIFAR-10, ViT-B-16 for CIFAR-100) used purely as a fixed feature extractor with no gradient updates during estimation. It outputs a vector of K importance weights (the ratio of target to source class priors) and a re-weighted classifier trained on the source data. Unlike calibration-dependent baselines that require a held-out validation split, RDALS uses the full source dataset for LDA fitting and moment estimation, making it calibration-free and more data-efficient.

## Method

RDALS operates in three stages within the latent space of a frozen backbone. First, it fits multiclass LDA on source features to obtain K−1 discriminative projection directions, which are proven (Theorem 4.1) to maximize the minimum singular value of the moment matrix and thus yield the best-conditioned linear system for recovering the label distribution. Second, it constructs a moment-matching linear system b = Aq using these LDA projections as summary functions, where A encodes source class-conditional means and b encodes target marginal means. Third, rather than inverting the (potentially ill-conditioned) matrix, it solves a regularized second-order cone program (SOCP) over the probability simplex with an L2 penalty, guaranteeing non-negative, numerically stable weight estimates even under severe shift or limited samples. The paper further provides finite-sample error bounds for the estimated weights and a generalization bound for the downstream re-weighted classifier, and demonstrates that the first-order moment matching is inherently robust to zero-mean feature noise by the law of large numbers.

## Results

Under Dirichlet shift (α=1, N=10,000), RDALS achieves the lowest weight-estimation MSE across all three benchmarks: 2.34e-2 on MNIST, 5.18e-2 on CIFAR-10, and 2.68 on CIFAR-100. For downstream classification, RDALS reaches 97.00% accuracy on MNIST, 85.06% on CIFAR-10, and 58.03% on CIFAR-100, with the largest margin on CIFAR-100 where it outperforms the best baseline (RLLS, 51.81%) by over 6 percentage points. The method also reduces CIFAR-100 MSE by nearly 50% relative to the strongest baseline (CPMCN, 5.25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDALS outperforms all four baselines (BBSL, RLLS, MLLS, CPMCN) on every dataset and every metric reported. The most dramatic gains appear on CIFAR-100 (K=100), where RDALS achieves 58.03% accuracy versus 51.81% (RLLS) and 51.59% (CPMCN), a 6+ point improvement, while cutting the weight-estimation MSE from 5.25–5.34 to 2.68. On CIFAR-10, RDALS edges out CPMCN (85.06% vs. 85.02% ACC) and MLLS (85.01%) with a substantially lower MSE (5.18e-2 vs. 8.22e-2 for MLLS). On MNIST the absolute accuracy gap is small (97.00% vs. 96.99% for CPMCN) but the MSE advantage is an order of magnitude (2.34e-2 vs. 7.19e-2). MLLS, the strongest likelihood-based baseline, degrades most severely on CIFAR-100 (MSE 9.42, ACC 40.23%), highlighting its sensitivity to miscalibration in high-cardity settings, whereas RDALS remains calibration-free and stable.

## Relation to Prior Work

RDALS extends the moment-matching framework for label shift (BBSE, RLLS) by relocating the estimating equations from raw input space or classifier-output space into a discriminative latent subspace defined by LDA on pre-trained deep features. It departs from likelihood-based methods (MLLS, CPMCN) by being entirely calibration-free, and from direct-inversion solvers by replacing matrix inversion with a regularized second-order cone program. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) that the classical Fisher LDA criterion is provably optimal for maximizing the minimum singular value of the moment matrix, thereby linking a 90-year-old discriminant analysis result to the numerical stability of label-shift estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical fragility: standard label-shift estimators degrade sharply under even mild input-level perturbations (sensor noise, style variation), as shown by the CIFAR-10-C and PACS experiments. The calibration-free, training-free estimation phase (frozen backbone + LDA + SOCP) makes the method attractive for resource-constrained deployment where validation splits for calibration are unavailable. Open questions include whether the latent conditional invariance assumption holds for non-visual modalities, how the method behaves under covariate shift (not just label shift), and whether the LDA step can be replaced by learned projections in a fully end-to-end test-time adaptation setting, which the authors flag as future work.
