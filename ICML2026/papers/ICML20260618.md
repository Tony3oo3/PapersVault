## Problem

Current real-time causal video generation methods suffer from a fundamental student-teacher mismatch: a memoryless short-context teacher (e.g., 5s window) supervises a long-context student, preventing the student from learning global temporal dependencies. This creates the Forgetting-Drifting Dilemma—short context windows cause identity loss over long rollouts, while longer contexts accumulate uncorrected errors and drift off the data manifold, limiting effective context to 1.5–9.2 seconds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260618/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single text prompt as input and autoregressively generates a long video (up to 60+ seconds) at 17 FPS using the Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B base architecture. Generation proceeds chunk-by-chunk with a KV cache of 21 latent frames organized into a three-part memory (3 sink + 12 slow + 6 fast), enabling an effective context span exceeding 20 seconds. The student model is a few-step distilled causal diffusion model, so each chunk requires only a small number of denoising steps rather than the full diffusion schedule.

## Method

Context Forcing resolves the student-teacher mismatch via a two-stage distillation curriculum: Stage 1 performs standard DMD on short windows (1–5s) to learn local dynamics, and Stage 2 introduces Contextual Distribution Matching Distillation (CDMD), where a long-context teacher—trained with Error-Recycling Fine-Tuning to recover from accumulated drift—supervises the student on its own long rollouts using the same KV cache context. The key architectural innovation is a Slow-Fast Memory KV cache system: an Attention Sink retains initial tokens, a Slow Memory buffer stores high-surprisal keyframes (selected by key-vector similarity thresholding), and a Fast Memory rolling queue captures immediate local context. Bounded Positional Encoding constrains all RoPE indices to a fixed range regardless of generation step, preventing the distribution shift that unbounded positional indices cause over long sequences. A dynamic rollout-length curriculum progressively extends the context horizon during training to avoid early distribution shift.

## Results

On 60-second video generation (VBench), Context Forcing achieves a total score of 82.45, outperforming LongLive (83.64 total but lower on semantic/consistency sub-metrics), Infinity-RoPE (79.99), Rolling Forcing (79.31), and Self Forcing (71.86). On fine-grained consistency metrics at 60s, the student model scores 95.34 (background) and 94.88 (subject) consistency, surpassing LongLive (94.62/93.88) and Infinity-RoPE (94.21/93.05). On 5-second generation, the method is competitive (83.44 total) with the highest background (97.38) and subject (96.84) consistency among all autoregressive baselines. The model operates at 17.0 FPS with a 1.3B parameter budget and supports 20+ seconds of effective context, a 2–10× improvement over prior SOTA (1.5–9.2s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest long-context baseline LongLive, Context Forcing achieves higher semantic (76.10 vs 74.97), background consistency (95.34 vs 94.62), and subject consistency (94.88 vs 93.88) at 60s, though LongLive edges ahead on total VBench score (83.64 vs 82.45) and 5s total (84.87 vs 83.44). Against Infinity-RoPE, the method wins decisively on all 60s sub-metrics (total 82.45 vs 79.99; semantic 76.10 vs 74.30; subject consistency 94.88 vs 93.05) and on DINOv2 structural identity at 60s (87.89 vs 83.72). Against Rolling Forcing, gains are consistent across all 60s metrics (total +3.14, semantic +8.41, subject consistency +1.78). Against standard autoregressive baselines (Self Forcing, CausVid, SkyReels-V2, MAGI-1, NOVA), the improvement is substantial—total 60s scores jump from 65–72 to 82.45, with semantic scores improving by 25–31 points. On 5s generation, the method is competitive but not dominant on total score (83.44 vs LongLive 84.87, Self Forcing 84.31), yet it achieves the best background (97.38) and subject (96.84) consistency among all autoregressive models. In the user study, Context Forcing achieves a 77.3% preference rate over Infinity-RoPE and 69.3% over LongLive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal autoregressive video generation lineage established by CausVid (block-wise causal attention) and Self-Forcing (self-rollout training with KV caching), and builds directly on Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) as the core distillation mechanism. Its key departure is from LongLive and Self-Forcing++, which train a long-context student under supervision from a memoryless 5-second teacher; Context Forcing identifies this as a fundamental student-teacher mismatch and resolves it by distilling from a long-context teacher that retains full generation history. The Slow-Fast Memory KV-cache architecture and Bounded Positional Encoding are novel engineering contributions that draw on dual-process memory theory and StreamingLLM-style attention sinks, while Error-Recycling Fine-Tuning is adapted from Stable Video Infinity to make the context teacher robust to the student's accumulated drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Forgetting-Drifting Dilemma is a genuine bottleneck for real-time long video generation, and the 2–10× effective context improvement (20s+ vs. 1.5–9.2s) is practically significant for applications like interactive world models and long-form storytelling. The Slow-Fast Memory with surprisal-based consolidation offers a reusable pattern for managing growing KV caches in any autoregressive generative model. However, residual drifting persists in complex scenes, fine-grained textures can be lost, and the approach relies on a 1.3B-parameter base model; whether the framework scales to larger architectures or truly minute-scale generation without further compression remains open.
