## Problem

Conventional Mixture-of-Experts frameworks for continual learning rely on a small inter-task shared parameter space for expert communication, which becomes a bottleneck as tasks accumulate and reintroduces catastrophic forgetting via uncontrolled propagation of shortcut features. Fully independent experts avoid this but require explicit task-ID routers, adding complexity and fragility. The paper asks whether expert communication can be achieved without any shared parameters or dedicated router, while still preventing overconfident OOD errors from the shared backbone.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input, processed through a frozen CLIP visual encoder, with class names fed through a prompt template into the frozen text encoder to produce joint-space embeddings. At inference in the class-incremental setting (no task ID provided), the system outputs a class label by combining per-expert predictions weighted by sample-specific Mahalanobis distances to each expert's learned multivariate Gaussian over visual embeddings. No reference dataset, replay buffer, or external task-ID signal is required; the task identification is implicit in the MD-based weighting.

## Method

The architecture uses a frozen CLIP backbone with lightweight LoRA adapters as task-specific experts, strictly isolating each expert by freezing all other adapters during training. The key training mechanism is a Temperature-Guided Expert Mixture: previous experts' top-k confidence predictions on the current task's training set identify shortcut-activated samples, which receive a lower temperature (sharper logits) while remaining samples receive a higher temperature (flatter logits), effectively injecting an epistemic prior from past experts into the current expert's loss. The paper proves this is equivalent to Generalized Bayesian Inference with a Boltzmann-form epistemic prior (Proposition 3.1) and that it guarantees a lower-bounded confidence gain over shortcut-activated regions (Theorem 3.2). At inference, each expert's visual embeddings are modeled by a single multivariate Gaussian, and inverse Mahalanobis distances converted via a sharp softmax (ζ=0.01) yield sample-specific expert weights that replace any router, enabling both accurate task identification and OOD rejection without additional parameters.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (10-step CIL), the proposed method achieves 87.28% average and 80.6% last-task accuracy, outperforming the strongest baselines MoE-Adapters (85.21%/77.52%) and RAIL (84.03%/76.09%) by +2.07% and +3.25% average accuracy respectively. On TinyImageNet (10-step), it reaches 82.13%/78.31%, surpassing MoE-Adapters (80.23%/76.35%) and RAIL (75.90%/72.09%). On the sub-population shift BREEDS Entity-13 benchmark (5 steps), the method achieves 87.08% overall accuracy, a +7.43% gain over the second-best RAIL (81.06%), while on Entity-30 (4 steps) it reaches 84.88%, a +7.95% gain over RAIL (78.63%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 12 baselines across every benchmark and step configuration. The closest competitors are MoE-Adapters (CVPR 2024) and RAIL (NeurIPS 2024): on CIFAR-100 10-step CIL, the proposed method gains +2.07% average and +3.08% last accuracy over MoE-Adapters, and +3.25%/+4.51% over RAIL. On TinyImageNet 10-step, the gap widens to +1.90% over MoE-Adapters and +6.23% over RAIL, with RAIL suffering significant degradation in longer sequences (75.85% at 20 steps vs. 81.64% for the proposed method). On the sub-population shift BREEDS benchmarks, the advantage is most pronounced: +7.43% over RAIL on Entity-13 and +7.95% on Entity-30, where MoE-Adapters and ZSCL show substantial seen/unseen imbalance. Among the additional CLIP-based baselines (Table 3), PROOF (TPAMI 2025) is the nearest at 86.70%/79.05% on CIFAR-100 B0 Inc10, still trailing by +0.58%/+1.55%, while CLAP4CLIP and CIL-CLIP both require replay buffers that the proposed method avoids entirely. Traditional non-CLIP baselines (UCIR, DyTox, LwF, iCARL, LwF-VR) are outperformed by 7–29% in average accuracy, confirming the benefit of the multimodal CLIP backbone combined with the proposed expert collaboration mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MoE-Adapters line of work (Yu et al., 2024) by removing the shared parameter space and the explicit task-ID router, replacing them with a confidence-aware temperature scaling mechanism during training and a Mahalanobis-distance-based weighting during inference. It also builds on ZSCL's (Zheng et al., 2023) use of CLIP for continual learning but departs from its reliance on a reference dataset and parameter regularization. The key conceptual departure is framing inter-expert communication as an implicit epistemic prior under Generalized Bayesian Inference, providing a theoretical justification (Proposition 3.1, Theorem 3.2) that prior MoE-CL methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The memory-free, reference-dataset-free design makes this approach more deployable in real-world continual learning where storing past data or maintaining a reference set is impractical. The 1500× speedup of the MD weighting over a learned router (0.0016s vs 0.21s) is a practical efficiency win for inference. Open questions include scalability to very large task sequences (beyond 50 steps), whether the binary temperature (two fixed values) can be made fully continuous, and whether the approach transfers to non-CLIP backbones or non-vision-language modalities. The per-task (rather than per-class) Gaussian simplification, while effective, may limit fine-grained OOD detection in highly imbalanced class distributions.
