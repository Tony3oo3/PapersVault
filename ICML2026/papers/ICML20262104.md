## Problem

Training super-resolution models for mesh-based simulations via fully supervised learning demands large quantities of computationally expensive high-resolution (HR) data, making data collection a major bottleneck. Existing alternatives—unsupervised methods like PhySRNet or MAgNet—either restrict applicability to regular grids or produce substantially higher prediction errors. Semi-supervised learning had never been applied to mesh-based super-resolution, and the role of inductive biases in MPNNs for this task remained unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262104/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a low-resolution (LR) solution field defined on an irregular LR mesh (node positions and edge connectivity) and produces a high-resolution (HR) solution field on a finer HR mesh. Because different PDE parameter values can yield different mesh geometries, LR and HR samples may be defined on non-matching node positions, requiring kNN interpolation to project values between meshes for both training targets and the auxiliary model's difference prediction. The framework operates on graph-structured data natively, avoiding the grid interpolation overhead of CNN-based approaches.

## Method

SuperMeshNet introduces complementary learning, a semi-supervised scheme that jointly trains two MPNN models with distinct roles: a primary model F_θ maps a single LR input to an HR prediction, while an auxiliary model G_φ predicts the difference between two HR solutions from two LR inputs. During unsupervised steps, each model's prediction generates a pseudo-ground truth that supervises the other, exploiting the fact that their structurally different tasks (inter-resolution vs. intra-resolution mapping) produce less correlated errors than conventional co-training with identical models. The architecture is built on SRGNN with a shared feature extractor between F_θ and G_φ to reduce training cost, and incorporates two MPNN-agnostic inductive biases—node-level centering (subtracting the global mean of node embeddings) and message-level centering (subtracting the global mean of aggregated messages)—that smooth the loss landscape and consistently reduce RMSE across six MPNN architectures. With only 10% of the HR data (N_h=20 vs. N=200), SuperMeshNet achieves lower RMSE than a fully supervised baseline trained on 100% HR data without inductive biases.

## Results

On the three FEM datasets, SuperMeshNet (MGN backbone, N_h=20, N=200) achieves RMSE of 0.0226 (Dataset 1), 0.0507 (Dataset 2), and 0.0245 (Dataset 3), matching or surpassing the fully supervised baseline trained with 10× more HR data (N_h=N=200: 0.0228, 0.0461, 0.0243 respectively). The improvement is consistent across all six MPNN architectures tested (GCN, SAGE, GAT, GTR, GIN, MGN), with the largest gains on GIN (e.g., 0.0277 vs 0.0381 on Dataset 1). On the real-world geometry dataset, SuperMeshNet (N_h=40, N=200) predicts the drag coefficient with 1.4% relative error versus 1.9% for full supervision (N_h=N=200), while on time-dependent PDE dataset 2, SuperMeshNet successfully predicts HR vorticity where full supervision fails entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuperMeshNet (MGN, N_h=20, N=200) achieves RMSE 0.0226 on Dataset 1, outperforming the fully supervised baseline with 10× more HR data (N_h=N=200, RMSE 0.0228) and dramatically beating the fully supervised baseline with only 20 HR samples (RMSE 0.0655). Against benchmark semi-supervised regression methods, SuperMeshNet achieves the lowest RMSE (0.0226) and shortest training time (421 s) among all methods: Mean-Teacher (0.0325, 694 s), TNNR (0.0624, 477 s), and UCVME (0.0293, 1123 s) all underperform on both metrics. The zero-shot MAgNet baseline (no HR data) is approximately 4× worse in RMSE (0.0979 vs 0.0226 on Dataset 1), confirming that even a small amount of HR supervision is critical. On the real-world geometry dataset, SuperMeshNet (N_h=40) achieves a lower drag coefficient relative error (1.4%) than full supervision (N_h=200, 1.9%), though it shows a slightly higher lift coefficient relative error (17.7% vs 3.3%) due to the small absolute magnitude of the lift value. On time-dependent PDE dataset 2, where LR and HR vorticity fields differ substantially, SuperMeshNet produces visually accurate HR predictions while the fully supervised baseline fails, attributed to the auxiliary model G_φ capturing intra-resolution relationships that pure LR→HR mapping cannot learn.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuperMeshNet builds directly on SRGNN (Barwey et al., 2024) for its model architecture, extending it with a semi-supervised complementary learning framework and MPNN-agnostic inductive biases. It departs from the dominant CNN-based super-resolution approaches (Li & McComb, 2022; Yonekura et al., 2023; Obiols-Sales et al., 2024) by operating natively on irregular meshes via message passing, and from unsupervised methods (PhySRNet, MAgNet) by exploiting a small amount of paired HR supervision. The complementary learning mechanism is conceptually inspired by semi-supervised regression methods (Mean-Teacher, UCVME, TNNR) but restructured around two models with distinct input-output configurations—inter-resolution mapping versus intra-resolution difference modeling—rather than two identical models predicting the same target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 90% reduction in required HR data while matching or exceeding full-supervision accuracy addresses a critical practical bottleneck in simulation-based workflows, where generating fine-mesh solutions is the dominant computational cost. The MPNN-agnostic inductive biases (node-level and message-level centering) offer a simple, broadly applicable recipe that consistently improves super-resolution across six architectures, though their benefit is task-dependent (they hurt norm prediction). Open questions include a rigorous theoretical characterization of when complementary learning remains stable without mutual error amplification, principled HR data selection strategies, and whether the framework extends to regimes with strong nonlinearities or bifurcations where the auxiliary model's parameter-sensitivity learning may break down.
