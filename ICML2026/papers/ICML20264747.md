## Problem

LLM-GFlowNets trained with Trajectory Balance suffer from two coupled failure modes: prefix collapse (early-token entropy drops sharply, causing distinct terminals to share near-identical prefixes) and length bias (systematic over- or under-shooting of sequence length). The paper attributes these to (i) high-variance terminal-only credit assignment that provides ambiguous feedback to intermediate prefixes, and (ii) reward-prioritized replay that concentrates training on a narrow subset of the search space, inducing a shifted and non-representative training distribution.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a terminable prefix tree induced by an autoregressive LLM (e.g., Llama-3.2-1B fine-tuned with LoRA), where each prefix state has an explicit EOS termination edge. Input is a fixed prompt or molecular scaffold; output is a variable-length token sequence (SMILES string, arithmetic expression, or natural-language sentence) whose terminal distribution is proportional to a mixed reward combining a frozen reference LM prior and a task-specific score (e.g., QED drug-likeness, exact arithmetic correctness, or BLEU). The key structural constraint is that the state space forms a tree with a unique parent per prefix, making the backward kernel deterministic.

## Method

RapTB augments standard Trajectory Balance with a rooted-prefix auxiliary loss: instead of enforcing consistency on all O(N²) subtrajectory windows (as SubTB does, which creates conflicting termination boundary conditions), it restricts dense supervision to O(N) rooted residuals anchored at the root, eliminating the global normalizer and avoiding heterogeneous window boundaries. To reduce variance, it introduces absorbed suffix rewards that propagate terminal task rewards backward to each prefix via a mixture of max and soft (log-sum-exp with distance penalty) backups, providing a lower-variance partial-credit target; the TB term remains the sole exact balance constraint while the auxiliary term acts as a variance-reducing regularizer whose deviation from the TB optimum vanishes as its weight η→0. Orthogonally, SubM refreshes a fixed-size replay buffer by greedily maximizing a monotone submodular objective that jointly balances per-sample quality, facility-location diversity coverage (via Tanimoto or Jaccard similarity), and concave-over-counts length-bin coverage, with a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee and negligible per-step overhead (~10 ms).

## Results

On scaffold-conditioned SMILES generation (L_max=10), RapTB+SubM achieves the best quality-diversity trade-off: Score 0.844, Entropy 2.726, and FPDiv 0.898 while maintaining high validity (Acc 0.988). On the longer-horizon stress test (L_max=15), RapTB+SubM maximizes long-horizon coverage (Frac[11+] = 0.701) and yields the lowest prefix concentration (Top1 = 0.071). On Expr24 with submodular replay, RapTB+SubM doubles the normalized solution coverage of the strongest baseline (NormCov 0.209 vs. 0.100 for TB+SubM) while maintaining near-perfect accuracy (0.994). On CommonGen, RapTB+SubM achieves BLEU-4 of 33.23 at natural length (11.83), whereas SubTB saturates length at 20.00 with catastrophic termination drift (Δlog p_term ≈ −28.32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TB (Malkin et al., 2022a), RapTB+SubM improves Score by 17.6% (0.844 vs. 0.717), Entropy by 9% (2.726 vs. 2.503), and FPDiv by 11% (0.898 vs. 0.807), while also correcting TB's severe length bias (Len 7.435 vs. 3.065); TB retains a marginal validity edge (Acc 0.998 vs. 0.988). Against SubTB (Madan et al., 2023), RapTB+SubM wins decisively on validity (Acc 0.988 vs. 0.328) and diversity (Entropy 2.726 vs. 2.127, FPDiv 0.898 vs. 0.836), confirming that SubTB's overlapping window constraints cause termination drift that degrades chemical validity. Against PRT (Shen et al., 2023, reward-prioritized replay training), the comparison is most visible on Expr24: under PRT, TB collapses to only ~5 unique solutions (NormCov 0.001), whereas RapTB+SubM reaches NormCov 0.209 with Acc 0.994, demonstrating that submodular replay is essential for coverage when reward-prioritized selection is not available. On CommonGen, SubTB's termination drift (Δlog p_term = −28.32, Len 20.00) is catastrophic compared to RapTB+SubM (Δlog p_term = +4.89, Len 11.83, BLEU 33.23), showing the failure is structural rather than task-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Trajectory Balance (TB) objective for GFlowNets by inserting a rooted-prefix auxiliary loss that propagates terminal rewards to intermediate prefixes via absorbed suffix backups, positioning itself between TB's sparse high-variance supervision and Subtrajectory Balance's dense but over-constrained windowed consistency. It departs from SubTB (Madan et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2024) by restricting dense supervision to prefixes anchored at the root and explicitly detaching termination gradients, thereby avoiding the conflicting boundary conditions that cause termination drift in terminable prefix trees. Orthogonally, the submodular replay (SubM) mechanism draws on facility-location and concave histogram coverage from the submodular data-selection literature (Killamsetty et al., 2021; Kothawade et al., 2022) to replace reward-prioritized replay, which prior work (Shen et al., 2023) has shown induces rich-get-richer collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnosis that SubTB's arbitrary-start windows create heterogeneous termination boundary conditions—resolvable by a global shift in log q(⊤|s) rather than improved token transitions—is a structural finding that persists across model scales (1B–32B) and task domains, and it cautions against naively densifying GFlowNet objectives in autoregressive settings. The complementarity between within-trajectory credit assignment (RapTB) and external replay coverage (SubM) suggests that future GFlowNet training should treat these as separable, jointly-tunable axes rather than conflating them. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned subtrajectory selection can further reduce the number of auxiliary constraints, whether the absorbed-reward aggregation (max/soft mixture) generalizes to non-stationary or multi-objective rewards, and whether the submodular refresh strategy remains effective at much larger buffer sizes or in fully asynchronous training regimes (e.g., TBA-style pipelines).
