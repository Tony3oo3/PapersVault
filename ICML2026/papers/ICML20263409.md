## Problem

Existing generative planners for long-horizon robotic manipulation either apply a single generative paradigm (typically diffusion) at all hierarchy levels, incurring prohibitive iterative-denoising costs at the low-level control stage, or lack an explicit mechanism to assess the long-term viability of generated subgoal sequences in sparse-reward settings. The paper asks whether a single generative modeling paradigm is optimal for all levels of a planning hierarchy and shows empirically that it is not: high-level planning demands exploration, while low-level planning demands speed and deterministic execution.

## Input / Output

The world model encoder consumes two RGBD views (front and wrist cameras, each resized to 320×240 then center-cropped to 224×224) concatenated with an 11-dimensional proprioceptive vector (3D EE position, 4D quaternion orientation, 3D linear velocity, 3D angular velocity, 1D gripper width), producing a 2048-dimensional latent state (32 stochastic + 1024 deterministic RSSM dimensions, combined). The goal is a fixed latent vector obtained by encoding the fully assembled state. At deployment, the system outputs 8-dimensional delta end-effector actions (3D position, 4D quaternion orientation, 1D gripper) bounded in [−1, +1], executed via an inverse dynamics model that maps consecutive latent pairs to actions within an MPC loop.

## Method

HDFlow is a two-stage hierarchical planner. Stage 1 trains an RSSM world model with a DINOv2 RGB encoder, augmented by a contrastive InfoNCE loss that pulls intermediate latent states from successful trajectories toward the goal representation while pushing them away from failed-trajectory states, plus an inverse dynamics loss for action-aware structure. Stage 2 freezes the world model and trains a high-level DiT-based diffusion planner to generate K sparse latent subgoals conditioned on (z₀, z_G), guided at inference by a contrastively-trained EBM whose gradient steers generation away from failing plans; a two-step manifold-aware projection (Tweedie denoising → local rank-r PCA on k nearest successful subgoals) prevents the guided samples from drifting off the feasible latent manifold. The low-level planner is a rectified flow transformer that generates dense H-step latent trajectories between consecutive subgoals by solving a learned ODE with a Dormand-Prince integrator in 20 steps, exploiting the straight-line transport property for fast, deterministic trajectory synthesis.

## Results

On FurnitureBench (simulation), HDFlow achieves 92% success on one_leg (Low), 68% on lamp (Low), 61% on round_table (Low), and 55% on cabinet (Low), outperforming the best baseline SHD by 21, 25, 20, and 34 percentage points respectively. On RLBench, HDFlow reaches 100% on Drag Stick and Push Buttons, 93.3% on Insert Peg (vs. 65.6% for 3D Actor), and 73.3% on Sort Shape (vs. 44.0% for 3D Actor). On OGBench, HDFlow achieves 72% on antmaze-medium-v0 and 48% on antmaze-giant-v0, surpassing the next-best method (Diffusion Veteran) by 11 and 13 points. In real-world experiments, HDFlow achieves 8/10 on one_leg (Low) versus 3/10 for IQL and 0/10 for BC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HDFlow dominates all 14 baselines across every benchmark. On FurnitureBench, it outperforms the strongest hierarchical diffusion baseline SHD by 21 pp (one_leg Low: 92 vs. 71) and 34 pp (cabinet Low: 55 vs. 21), while non-hierarchical planners like Diffuser (56%) and Decision Diffuser (60%) trail by 36 and 32 pp on one_leg. Imitation learning methods (BC, DP, JUICER) are far behind, with JUICER reaching only 68% on one_leg Low versus HDFlow's 92%. On RLBench, HDFlow matches or exceeds 3D Actor and RVT-2 on most tasks, with the largest gaps on Insert Peg (93.3% vs. 65.6%) and Sort Shape (73.3% vs. 44.0%). On OGBench, HDFlow consistently leads all diffusion-based planners (Diffuser, DD, AD, DL, DV, HDMI, SHD), with the margin widening on harder tasks: on puzzle-4x6-v0, all baselines score 0% while HDFlow achieves 14%. In real-world FurnitureBench, HDFlow (8/10 one_leg Low) substantially outperforms IQL (3/10) and BC (0/10).

## Relation to Prior Work

HDFlow extends the hierarchical diffusion planning line (SHD, HDMI) by replacing the low-level diffusion planner with a rectified flow model, motivated by the observation that high-level strategic planning benefits from diffusion's stochastic exploration while low-level trajectory generation benefits from ODE-based flow's deterministic speed. The world model backbone follows the RSSM lineage (Dreamer, Mastering Diverse Domains) augmented with a DINOv2 visual encoder and a contrastive objective to structure the latent space for planning. The manifold-aware EBM guidance extends the two-step projection framework of He et al. (2024) and Lee & Choi (2025) to the goal-conditioned, energy-guided planning setting, providing a theoretical lower bound on guidance gap scaling with latent dimensionality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in diffusion-based robotic planning: iterative denoising is too slow for real-time low-level control, and applying diffusion at all hierarchical levels inherits this cost. The hybrid diffusion-flow architecture offers a principled solution that is empirically validated across three benchmarks and real-world deployment. Key limitations include the dependence on both successful and failed demonstrations (resource-intensive collection) and the need to tune the temporal abstraction (number of subgoals K) per task. Open questions include whether the EBM can be learned online or from reward signals alone, and whether the manifold projection generalizes to higher-dimensional or multi-task latent spaces.
