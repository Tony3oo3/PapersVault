## Problem

Safety alignment in LLMs and MLLMs is commonly assumed to operate as a near-binary threshold separating refusal from compliance, yet empirical evidence shows stochastic, context-dependent behavior near boundaries. No prior work provides a unified diagnostic framework that jointly characterizes output uncertainty and internal safety signals to identify these unstable regimes, nor a principled jailbreak that exploits this instability without model-specific optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265075/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Furina takes a single harmful query as input and never exposes it directly to the target model. Instead, an auxiliary model decomposes it into K safety-neutral semantic probes (ordered by decomposition phase) and a short metaphorical scene description; for multimodal targets, the scene is additionally rendered as a typographic image or a Stable Diffusion XL–generated scene image. The target model is queried independently on each probe and (for MLLMs) the visual scene, and an auxiliary model synthesizes all collected probe answers and the scene interpretation into a single final response, which is the only artifact submitted to the safety judge.

## Method

The paper first establishes a multi-metric diagnostic framework combining token-level entropy, semantic entropy, attack success rate, and two internal safety signals (HiddenDetect hidden-state projection and refusal-direction alignment), revealing a characteristic decoupling signature: inputs in unstable regimes exhibit elevated output uncertainty yet decreased internal safety activation. Building on this, Furina operates in three stages: (1) an auxiliary model decomposes the harmful query into a structured threat-analytic representation and generates K safety-neutral probes with controlled semantic drift plus a metaphorical scene description; (2) for MLLMs, the scene is realized as either a typographic rendering or a diffusion-generated image; (3) the target model answers each probe and interprets the visual scene, and an auxiliary model synthesizes the distributed evidence into a final response. The key design principle is that harmful intent is distributed across individually benign fragments anchored by a shared scene, pushing the model into the instability band without gradient-based optimization or model-specific tuning, which also makes the attack resistant to input-side defenses that inspect probes in isolation.

## Results

On HarmBench (first 200 queries), Furina achieves query-level ASR of 92.5% on LLaMA-3-8B, 94.0% on GPT-4o-mini, 90.5% on GPT-4o, 93.5% on Gemini-2.5-Flash, and 83.5% on Claude-Haiku-4.5, outperforming the strongest multi-turn baseline ActorBreaker by 4.5–18.5 percentage points across all five LLM targets. On MM-SafetyBench (1,680 image-text pairs), Furina (Typo) reaches 93.93% on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 93.81% on GPT-4o-mini, 93.51% on GPT-4o, 92.79% on Gemini-2.5-Flash, and 77.20% on Claude-Haiku-4.5, matching or exceeding the best multimodal baseline MML on four of five targets. The diagnostic framework further reveals that unstable-regime inputs exhibit elevated token entropy (H_tok) and ASR coupled with decreased internal safety signals (HD_max, RD_max), a decoupling signature shared across diverse jailbreak methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Furina outperforms all 14 baselines on the LLM setting (Table 4). Among single-turn attacks, AutoDAN-Turbo is the strongest (83.0–87.0% on GPT-4o-mini/GPT-4o) yet still trails Furina by 7–11 points; CodeAttack (69.0–71.0%) and CipherChat (0.0–36.0%) are substantially weaker, with CipherChat and AmpleGCG failing almost entirely on closed-source models. Among multi-turn attacks, ActorBreaker is the closest competitor (79.0–86.0%), but Furina extends its lead to 92.5–94.0% on LLaMA-3-8B and GPT-4o-mini, and by a wider 18.5-point margin on Claude-Haiku-4.5 (83.5% vs. 65.0%). Crescendo (60.0–63.5%) and CoA (18.0–27.0%) are clearly outperformed. On the multimodal MM-SafetyBench (Table 5), Furina (Typo) beats MML on GPT-4o-mini (93.81% vs. 92.14%), Gemini-2.5-Flash (92.79% vs. 91.96%), and Claude-Haiku-4.5 (77.20% vs. 40.76%), while slightly trailing MML on GPT-4o (93.51% vs. 93.75%) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (93.93% vs. 95.42%). All other MLLM baselines (JailBound, UMK, VAJM, FigStep, and the official baseline) are outperformed by 10–60+ points across targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the well-established jailbreak task through a new diagnostic lens: rather than treating safety alignment as a binary threshold, it proposes an 'instability band' where small perturbations induce stochastic refusal decisions. Conceptually, it repurposes uncertainty quantification tools (token entropy, semantic entropy) and interpretability probes (HiddenDetect, refusal-direction projections) as joint safety diagnostics, a combination not previously explored in the jailbreak literature. The attack method (Furina) sits in the multi-turn/contextual jailbreak family alongside Crescendo and ActorBreaker but departs from escalation-based designs by using fragmented, safety-neutral probes anchored in a shared metaphorical scene, with an auxiliary model performing cross-fragment synthesis. It does not build on a single prior method but synthesizes ideas from uncertainty estimation, activation-space interpretability, and multi-turn jailbreaking into a unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling phenomenon—elevated output uncertainty co-occurring with weakened internal safety activation—provides a mechanistic explanation for why detection-based defenses (LlamaGuard, perplexity filters) fail against distributed-risk attacks, and suggests that future defenses must aggregate evidence across turns rather than inspect isolated inputs. The instability-band formalization, while currently diagnostic rather than calibrated, offers a principled vocabulary for characterizing boundary-adjacent safety behavior that could inform more robust alignment training. Key limitations include the heavy reliance on auxiliary models (GPT-4o-mini, o4-mini) for probe construction, which complicates the 'black-box' framing, and the absence of a calibrated method for assigning individual inputs to stability regions. Open questions include whether instability signals can be repurposed as explicit defense objectives and whether the framework extends to agentic or tool-use settings.
