## Problem

Existing parallel diffusion sampling methods (ParaDiGMS, ParaTAA, ParaSolver) reformulate sequential denoising as a nonlinear residual solve but rely on fixed or heuristically structured sparse Jacobian approximations. These fixed-band approximations become ill-conditioned in locally stiff regions where score curvature is high, causing unreliable convergence and trajectory drift in high-dimensional image and video generation. The paper asks whether dynamically controlling Jacobian sparsity can achieve a better stability-efficiency trade-off for scalable parallel sampling.

## Input / Output

ROPA is a training-free inference-time accelerator that takes a text prompt and standard Gaussian noise as input and produces a generated image (1024×1024 for SD-3.5-Large, 1360×768 for FLUX) or video (960×544, 61 frames for HunyuanVideo and CogVideoX1.5-5B). It operates on a discrete grid of N=50 diffusion steps and is executed across up to 8 H200 GPU cores in parallel, with a short 3-step sequential warm-up for initialization. The method requires no retraining or architectural modification to the underlying diffusion model.

## Method

ROPA reformulates the sequential denoising trajectory as a block-banded nonlinear residual system and solves it with an inexact Newton iteration whose Jacobian sparsity is adapted on the fly. Three mechanisms work together: (1) adaptive bandwidth control widens the forward-looking window w_i at grid indices where the local residual norm exceeds α·ē (indicating truncation or linearization failure) and prunes it where residuals are small, reducing the off-band truncation error τ_b only where needed; (2) Levenberg-Marquardt damping (JᵀJ + λI)Δx = −JᵀR provides a descent safeguard that limits update amplification in ill-conditioned directions; and (3) a rank-one score-aligned preconditioner reduces update gain along the score direction when the residual-score alignment exceeds a threshold γ, using only the score vector already computed by the model. The theoretical analysis identifies the combined quantity hL_t + τ_b (drift stiffness times step size plus truncation error) as the governing conditioning parameter, explaining why fixed sparse Jacobians fail in high-curvature regions and motivating the residual-driven adaptive control.

## Results

On video diffusion models (HunyuanVideo, Wan2.1, CogVideoX1.5) evaluated with VBench, ROPA achieves 2.5×–2.9× speedup at 8 cores, with the best case on HunyuanVideo reducing generation time from 378.6 s to 131.8 s (2.9×) while maintaining VBench Quality at 73.6% (vs. 73.8% sequential) and Latent RMSE of 0.055. On image diffusion models (SD-3.5-Large, FLUX) evaluated on COCO2017, ROPA reaches up to 2.3× speedup at 8 cores on FLUX (4.8 s vs. 11.2 s sequential) with CLIP score unchanged at 37.4 and the lowest Latent RMSE (0.120) among all baselines. ROPA consistently converges in 8–12 outer Newton iterations, reducing total NFE compared to baselines that require additional refinement or fail to converge in stiff regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROPA outperforms all four parallel baselines on speedup across every model and core count. On HunyuanVideo at 8 cores, ROPA achieves 2.9× speedup versus 2.4× for CHORDS and ParaTAA and 2.3× for ParaSolver, while ParaDIGMS trails at only 1.4×. On quality, ROPA matches or slightly exceeds baselines: its VBench Quality of 73.6% is within 0.1–0.2% of the sequential reference (73.8%) and ties ParaTAA, while ParaSolver drops to 73.5%. On Latent RMSE, ROPA (0.055) is competitive with ParaTAA (0.055) and ParaSolver (0.052) but dramatically better than CHORDS (0.185) and ParaDIGMS (0.189), indicating that the latter two suffer from persistent trajectory deviation. On FLUX image generation at 8 cores, ROPA achieves 2.3× speedup and RMSE of 0.120, beating ParaSolver (2.0×, RMSE 0.150) and ParaDIGMS (1.5×, RMSE 0.313) on both speed and fidelity while maintaining identical CLIP score (37.4). The key differentiator is that ROPA's adaptive bandwidth and damping keep the effective stiffness hL_t well below the instability threshold, whereas non-adaptive baselines exhibit late-step stiffness spikes (L ≈ 40–50 vs. ROPA's L ≈ 5) that degrade convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROPA extends the parallel diffusion sampling line initiated by ParaDiGMS (Picard/fixed-point iteration), ParaTAA (triangular Anderson acceleration), and ParaSolver (fixed banded Jacobian), all of which approximate the Newton-update Jacobian with a static sparsity pattern. The key departure is making the Jacobian bandwidth *adaptive*—driven by per-step residual diagnostics rather than a fixed window—combined with Levenberg-Marquardt damping and a score-aligned rank-one preconditioner. The theoretical contribution formalizes why fixed sparsity fails: a score-curvature-to-conditioning cascade (Theorem 2.3) shows that locally stiff regions and finite-band truncation error jointly push the residual Jacobian toward singularity, a mechanism absent from prior parallel-sampling analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By keeping the effective Jacobian sparse in easy regions and widening it only where residuals indicate truncation failure, ROPA makes parallel sampling viable for large video diffusion models (HunyuanVideo, CogVideoX, Wan2.1) where prior methods degrade or fail, achieving up to 2.9× speedup with negligible quality loss. The method is training-free and orthogonal to attention-level accelerations (SpargeAttention, ToCa), so it composes multiplicatively with existing inference stacks. Limitations include reduced benefit for few-step distilled samplers and VRAM constraints on very long high-resolution video; open directions include combining ROPA with model-level distillation and tensor parallelism for long-horizon generation.
