## Problem

Existing federated causal discovery methods for time series assume that each client's causal mechanisms are time-invariant and that all clients share a common sampling frequency. In practice, local intervention policies drift over time (creating transient 'hotspot' correlations that can be mistaken for stable causal edges), and clients sample at heterogeneous rates (making lag structures incompatible across clients). These two violations jointly undermine reliable aggregation of local causal graphs into a coherent global structure.

## Input / Output

Each client provides a multivariate time series over a shared variable set V of size d, along with its own sampling interval Δ_m and effective sample size T_m; raw data never leaves the client. The method outputs a global causal graph G^glob capturing edges consistently supported across clients, personalized local graphs G_local^(m) that retain client-specific deviations, and per-edge sharing probabilities Q_e that quantify the breadth and directional agreement of cross-client support. The only quantities transmitted to the server are compact edge-level signatures (noisy Fisher z-statistics plus credibility scores), not raw observations or full adjacency matrices.

## Method

Fed-CAW operates in three stages. On the client side, a sliding-window SVAR model is fitted with cross-window Frobenius-norm smoothness penalties (coupling adjacent windows) to suppress transient intervention-induced edges and recover a temporally stable local backbone; window-wise Fisher z-statistics are then aggregated into a single client-level evidence value per edge. For cross-client comparability, each client's lag-augmented companion matrix is mapped to a shared reference time scale via the principal matrix logarithm (Ĝ = Δ_m^{-1} log Φ), so that edges estimated at different sampling intervals are expressed on a common continuous-time grid. The server then performs credibility-weighted meta-analysis: each edge's aggregation weight is the product of a within-client temporal stability score κ (geometric mean of support frequency and magnitude stability across windows) and a cross-client temporal consistency score ρ (cosine similarity of lag-profile distributions on the unified scale), applied to Gaussian-noise-perturbed z-statistics under a calibrated (ε,δ)-differential privacy guarantee.

## Results

On the fMRI benchmark, Fed-CAW achieves an F1-score of 0.802, SHD of 4.30, AUPRC of 0.685, and AUROC of 0.787, approaching the centralized All-dyno oracle (F1=0.832, SHD=4.20) while operating under differential privacy. On the Dream3 gene-regulatory benchmark, Fed-CAW attains an average AUROC of 0.572 across five networks, outperforming all federated baselines. Across three synthetic suites (temporal drift, client scaling, heterogeneous sampling), Fed-CAW consistently achieves the lowest SHD and highest F1-score among federated methods, with the gap over FDBNL/PFDBNL widening as non-stationarity or sampling heterogeneity increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fed-CAW outperforms both federated DBN baselines (FDBNL and PFDBNL) on every metric in both real-world benchmarks. On fMRI, Fed-CAW improves F1-score by 0.049 over FDBNL (0.802 vs. 0.753) and by 0.094 over PFDBNL (0.802 vs. 0.708), while reducing SHD from 5.80/6.30 to 4.30. On Dream3, Fed-CAW's average AUROC of 0.572 exceeds FDBNL (0.556) by 0.016 and PFDBNL (0.521) by 0.051. Against the centralized All-dyno oracle, Fed-CAW trails by a modest margin (F1: 0.802 vs. 0.832 on fMRI; AUROC: 0.572 vs. 0.586 on Dream3), indicating that credibility-aware aggregation recovers most of the information available from pooled data while preserving client-level privacy. Fed-CAW also consistently surpasses Local-dyno (per-client averaging), confirming that cross-client aggregation adds value beyond independent local learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated dynamic Bayesian network learning framework of Chen et al. (2026, FDBNL/PFDBNL) and the Dynotears SVAR formulation (Pamfil et al., 2020) by addressing two practical limitations they leave unhandled: within-client temporal non-stationarity caused by short-term interventions, and cross-client heterogeneity in sampling frequencies. The key methodological departures are (i) sliding-window temporal smoothing with cross-window Frobenius-norm penalties to extract stable local backbones, (ii) continuous-time reparameterization via principal matrix logarithms of lag-augmented companion matrices to project heterogeneous sampling intervals onto a unified reference scale, and (iii) edge-level credibility scores (temporal stability κ and lag-profile consistency ρ) that modulate a credibility-weighted meta-analysis aggregation under differential privacy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled recipe for federated causal discovery in settings where raw data cannot be pooled and where both temporal drift and sampling heterogeneity are present—conditions common in healthcare, industrial monitoring, and multi-institutional research. The matrix-logarithm alignment is theoretically clean (exact recovery under the continuous-time model, Lipschitz-bounded deviation under mild heterogeneity), and the credibility weighting gives a transparent mechanism for downweighting fragile or inconsistent edges. Open questions include extending beyond linear SVAR to non-parametric or non-linear mechanisms, handling latent confounders and missing variables, and incorporating uncertainty quantification into the edge evidence rather than relying on point Fisher-z statistics. The moderate utility cost of differential privacy and the assumption of causal sufficiency limit immediate applicability to the most adversarial privacy regimes.
