## Problem

Merging multiple task-specific LoRAs into a single diffusion model causes destructive parameter interference in the shared weight space, degrading individual task fidelity as the number of merged adapters grows. Existing training-free methods (Task Arithmetic, TIES, DARE) either blindly superimpose updates or heuristically prune conflicts, failing to scale beyond a handful of tasks, while dynamic gating approaches break the re-parameterization property and incur inference latency.

## Input / Output

The method takes K pre-trained LoRA modules (each a pair of down/up-projection matrices A_k ∈ R^{r×d}, B_k ∈ R^{d×r}) and a single calibration text prompt per task (no ground-truth images required). It produces a merged LoRA module (A_comb, B̃_comb) structurally identical to a standard rank-Kr LoRA, which can be either kept as a standalone adapter or fully absorbed into the backbone weights for zero additional inference latency. Calibration requires only one forward pass per task (one diffusion timestep), with the streaming algorithm reducing memory to O((Kr)²) regardless of feature volume.

## Method

SSR reframes LoRA merging as signal routing within a unified low-rank subspace rather than parameter-space arithmetic. It first concatenates all K LoRAs along the rank dimension to form a unified projection space of dimension Kr, then constructs a training-free router R = QG⁻¹ from second-order statistics: G (the correlation matrix of projected features) acts as a whitening filter to decorrelate mixed intermediate signals, while Q (the directional guide capturing cross-covariance with task-specific targets) steers purified signals into their respective up-projection bases. The paper proves R is analytically equivalent to the projection of the OLS estimator, guaranteeing it strictly minimizes the feature reconstruction error across all tasks. A streaming algorithm grounded in the additivity of sufficient statistics enables on-the-fly accumulation of G and Q without caching raw features, and structural re-parameterization absorbs R into the up-projection (B̃_comb = B_comb R), yielding a module indistinguishable from standard LoRA with no inference overhead.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev single-task preservation (Table 1), SSR recovers 90.2% DINOv2 and 92.9% CLIP of the single-LoRA oracle at K=9, outperforming the strongest baseline IterIS by 0.0473 DINO and 0.0330 CLIP. In multi-concept composition (Table 3), SSR achieves 0.5704 DINOv2, 0.7357 CLIP, and a 91% Success Rate, surpassing DARE by 6.54% DINOv2 and 29 percentage points in Success Rate. On the FFHQ facial editing benchmark (Table 4), SSR attains 0.9610 ArcFace and 0.9625 CLIP, exceeding DARE by 1.39% in identity preservation. Merging time at K=9 is 34.26 s, roughly 2.6× faster than TIES (88.93 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSR outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the arithmetic baselines (Linear Average, Task Arithmetic), SSR gains 0.2974 DINO at K=9; the paper further proves Task Arithmetic is a degenerate special case of SSR with an identity router (R=I), explaining its susceptibility to interference. Among sparsification methods, SSR beats DARE by 0.0876 DINO and 0.0474 CLIP at K=9, and TIES by 0.1990 DINO, with the gap widening as K grows (TIES drops from 0.6264 to 0.4723 DINO while SSR only drops from 0.7342 to 0.6713). Against the two strongest recent baselines, SSR exceeds RobustMerge by 0.1233 DINO and IterIS by 0.0473 DINO / 0.0330 CLIP at K=9. On the GLUE generalization benchmark, SSR achieves 80.9 average, surpassing DOGE TA (79.9) and Concrete TA (78.0). In multi-concept composition, SSR's 91% Success Rate dwarfs DARE's 62% and TIES's 69%, confirming that sparsification-based methods sacrifice task recall to reduce interference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA's low-rank decomposition (Hu et al., 2022) but departs fundamentally from prior model-merging work (Task Arithmetic, TIES, DARE) that performs arithmetic or sparsification in the full parameter space. Instead, SSR reframes merging as signal routing within a unified rank-concatenated subspace, where a statistics-derived router (proven equivalent to the OLS projection) decorrelates and steers task-specific signals. The paper elegantly shows that Task Arithmetic is a degenerate special case (identity router) of its framework, and contrasts with dynamic gating approaches (LoRA-Flow, Mixture of LoRA Experts) that sacrifice re-parameterizability and introduce inference latency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSR offers a training-free, zero-inference-overhead merging solution with a provable optimality guarantee (OLS minimization of reconstruction error), making it immediately deployable in the existing LoRA ecosystem without architectural changes. The one-shot calibration requirement (a single forward pass per task) is practically significant, reducing merging cost to ~34 seconds for 9 LoRAs on FLUX.1-dev. However, the method optimizes a local linear objective that does not guarantee global optimality in the full nonlinear diffusion process, and performance degrades under severe domain conflicts or high semantic overlap—open questions for future work include whether the routing principle extends to non-LoRA PEFT variants or to merging across different base models.
