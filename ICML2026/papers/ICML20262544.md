## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) suffer from 'overthinking,' producing excessively long and redundant chain-of-thought traces that increase inference cost and can degrade accuracy. Existing mitigations are limited: training-based approaches are expensive, prompt-based methods lack task generalization, and output-based methods like DEER rely solely on token-level confidence, which is unreliable due to overconfidence on hard problems and underconfidence on easy ones. The paper asks whether internal attention dynamics can provide a more principled, multi-dimensional signal for determining when reasoning has truly converged.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a text prompt (the reasoning question) and consumes the LRM's internal attention weight matrices from the last four layers at Action Transition Points (ATPs, e.g., 'wait' tokens). At each ATP, a probing prompt ('\n\nFinal Answer\n\n\boxed') is appended to extract an intermediate answer, from which both token-level confidence and normalized Shannon attention entropy are computed. The output is a final answer in boxed format preceded by a shortened reasoning trace; the method also emits control signals (early exit, logits modification, or jump prompt insertion) that alter the generation trajectory in real time.

## Method

ASAG is a training-free, plug-and-play framework that adaptively selects among four generation strategies at each ATP by jointly monitoring model confidence C (mean token probability of the probed intermediate answer) and attention entropy variation rate ΔH (relative change in aggregated entropy across all heads in the last 4 layers compared to the first ATP). When C > λ and ΔH < α, the model has converged and ASAG triggers an early exit; when C < λ but ΔH < α (converged attention but low token confidence), ASAG injects 5% of the intermediate answer's normalized logits into subsequent generation to boost commitment; when ΔH > α and the global attention matrix shows the model is fixated on prior reasoning (a 'thinking trap'), ASAG inserts a jump prompt to redirect the trajectory. A theoretical lemma bounds the attention Jacobian's spectral norm by 2α_max(1−α_max)B_J·B_V, showing that entropy reduction forces contraction of the update map and thus guarantees convergence to a stable fixed point. The framework requires no retraining and integrates into any LRM by modifying only the decoding loop.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B, ASAG achieves an average accuracy of 75.1% across five benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH-500, AIME 2024, OlympiadBench, GPQA Diamond), improving over the vanilla baseline (72.5%) by 3.2% while reducing generated tokens by ~36% (compression ratio 64.2%). On Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-14B, ASAG yields absolute accuracy gains of 2.9% and 2.6% respectively. On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, the largest gain is observed: +4.1% accuracy with a 64.7% compression ratio. The method also generalizes to code reasoning (HumanEval, LiveCodeBench) and sampling-based decoding (top-p=0.95, T=0.6), maintaining consistent token reductions of ~37% with accuracy improvements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASAG consistently outperforms all four baselines on average accuracy across every model scale tested. Against the closest competitor DEER (confidence-based early exit at ATPs), ASAG gains +1.7% average accuracy on Qwen3-8B (75.1% vs 73.4%) with a comparable compression ratio (64.2% vs 63.5%), and +1.8% on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (62.3% vs 60.5%). Against Dynasor (answer-consistency-based exit), ASAG improves accuracy by +2.3% on Qwen3-8B while also achieving a lower CR (64.2% vs 69.7%), indicating Dynasor's fixed-interval probing leads to premature stopping. Against TALE (token-budget prompting), ASAG gains +4.6% accuracy on Qwen3-8B with a better CR (64.2% vs 68.9%). NoThinking (skipping reasoning entirely) achieves the lowest CR (~40%) but suffers severe accuracy drops (−12.7% on Qwen3-8B), confirming that some reasoning is necessary for challenging problems. The gap between ASAG and DEER is most pronounced on hard benchmarks (AIME 2024: 66.7% vs 63.3% on Qwen3-8B), where DEER's confidence-only signal triggers premature exits due to model overconfidence.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASAG directly extends DEER (Yang et al., 2025b), which probes model confidence at Action Transition Points (ATPs) to trigger early exit, by adding attention entropy as a second, complementary signal for assessing reasoning convergence. The entropy-based perspective draws on KV-cache eviction literature (SnapKV, KVZip) where attention matrices serve as information filters, repurposing them here as a reasoning-state indicator rather than a compression tool. The logits injection and jump prompt mechanisms are novel lightweight interventions that go beyond DEER's binary exit/continue decision, addressing the two specific failure modes (overconfidence on hard problems, insufficient confidence on easy ones) that purely confidence-based methods cannot resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ASAG offers a practical, training-free solution to the overthinking problem that is immediately deployable on existing LRMs, with the 40% token reduction carrying direct cost and latency implications for production inference. The decoupling analysis (Appendix G) showing that confidence and attention entropy progressively diverge as task difficulty increases provides a useful diagnostic for understanding when confidence signals become unreliable. Open questions include whether attention dynamics can be integrated as process-level rewards during RL training (as the authors suggest), whether the approach extends to search-based inference (MCTS, ToT), and whether the entropy threshold α generalizes across model families without re-tuning.
