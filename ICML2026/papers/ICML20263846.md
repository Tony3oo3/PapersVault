## Problem

Most existing Generative Reward Models (GRMs) generate evaluation principles after reading the actor's response (Q+R→P), creating a conditional dependence that allows response-level artifacts to leak into the rubric. The paper identifies this as 'Principle Drift': when an actor attempts reward hacking via verbosity, self-aggrandizement, or hallucinated self-justifications, the coupled GRM shifts its criteria to rationalize the response, yielding inflated scores that reinforce hacking during RL. This structural leakage channel is especially severe for open-ended, subjective tasks like creative writing where no deterministic checker exists.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user question Q and a candidate response R as input. Stage 1 consumes only Q to produce a set of weighted evaluation principles P (5–7 criteria with percentage weights summing to 100%). Stage 2 consumes (Q, P, R) and produces a per-criterion analysis with individual scores (X.X/10.0), a weighted-average calculation, and a final scalar reward r (one decimal place, 1.0–10.0) parsed from a designated \boxed{} field. During GRPO training, the Principle Cache reuses a single P⁰ for all G responses in a group, so the effective input to Stage 2 is (Q, P⁰, Rᵢ) for each i, ensuring intra-group consistency.

## Method

IP-GRM enforces conditional independence I(P;R|Q)=0 by factorizing the joint distribution into P(P|Q) × P(J|Q,P,R), so principles are generated before observing any response and remain invariant to response-level artifacts. The SFT corpus is constructed with a decoupled labeling procedure: a teacher model (Claude-3.5-Sonnet) first produces unbiased principles from Q alone, then separately produces principle-grounded judgments conditioned on (Q,P,R), and the student (DeepSeek-GRM-16B) is fine-tuned on two single-turn tasks with a decomposed loss L_principle + L_judgment. At inference, the Principle Cache generates P⁰ once per question (O(1)) and broadcasts it to all G group members, reducing principle-generation cost from O(G) to O(1) and yielding a 23.66% end-to-end latency speedup over the coupled baseline. The decoupled reward signal is then used in GRPO to train actor models (Qwen3-8B / Qwen3-8B-base), where the fixed rubric blocks the feedback loop that enables reward hacking while the cache ensures within-group reward differences reflect true quality rather than rubric variation.

## Results

On RewardBench (pointwise), IP-GRM achieves 68.22% overall accuracy, outperforming the same-size GRM-16B (61.65%, +6.57 pp), the larger GRM-27B (67.97%), and the scalar RM Skywork-Reward-V2 (66.32%). In downstream GRPO training with Qwen3-8B as the actor, IP-GRM reaches 87.6 on WritingBench (WB-all) and 77.8 on Creative Writing V3 (CW-v3), setting a new SOTA among open-source models and surpassing closed-source systems like Gemini-2.5-pro (85.6/77.0) and Qwen3-max (87.1/77.5). The Principle Cache strategy further reduces end-to-end reward computation latency by 23.66% relative to the coupled GRM baseline while maintaining intra-group evaluation consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the coupled GRM-16B (DeepSeek-GRM), IP-GRM wins on every metric: +6.57 pp on RewardBench overall (68.22 vs 61.65), +2.4 on WB-all (87.6 vs 85.2), and +3.6 on CW-v3 (77.8 vs 74.2) with Qwen3-8B; the gap widens to +2.8/+5.8 on the base model. IP-GRM also surpasses the larger GRM-27B on RewardBench overall (68.22 vs 67.97) and the scalar RM Skywork-Reward-V2 (68.22 vs 66.32). Among open-source actor models, IP-GRM-trained Qwen3-8B outperforms QwQ-32B (87.6 vs 84.7 WB-all) and Qwen3-235B-a22B-Thinking (87.6 vs 85.3), and beats closed-source Gemini-2.5-Pro (87.6 vs 85.6) and Qwen3-Max (87.6 vs 87.1). However, IP-GRM still trails the top-tier closed-source systems: Claude-Opus-4.5 (91.9/81.4), GPT-5.2 (90.1/79.2), DeepSeek-V3.2 (88.3/78.4), and Claude-Sonnet-4 (87.9/78.1) on WB-all/CW-v3, indicating a remaining gap of roughly 2–4 points to the strongest proprietary models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DeepSeek-GRM (Liu et al., 2025d), a generative reward model that produces explicit evaluation principles and rationales for open-ended tasks. The key departure is a factorization change: existing dynamic GRMs (including DeepSeek-GRM and WritingZero) generate principles conditioned on both the question and the response (Q+R→P), while IP-GRM enforces conditional independence by generating principles solely from the question (Q→P) before observing any response. The paper also positions itself against the broader reward-hacking and Goodhart's Law literature, arguing that the coupled factorization creates a structural leakage channel that algorithmic mitigations (regularization, adversarial training) do not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of Principle Drift as a named, measurable failure mode gives the GRM community a concrete diagnostic and a minimal architectural fix (decoupling) that is easy to adopt. The Principle Cache strategy makes the approach practically viable for large-scale GRPO training by cutting inference latency by ~24% while enforcing intra-group consistency. Key open questions remain: the Stage 2 judge still reads the response, so persuasive self-justifications may still bias per-criterion scoring; the primary RL experiments target creative writing with Qwen3 actors, and cross-domain validation is limited to UltraFeedback; and whether the decoupling fully eliminates hacking over very long training horizons (beyond 300 steps) is untested.
