## Problem

Counterfactual Credit Attribution (CCA), a relaxation of differential privacy that requires crediting any input on which an output significantly depends, was recently formalized for PAC learning but its applicability to generative models remained unexplored. This paper initiates the study of CCA for autoregressive language models and uncovers two fundamental barriers: CCA does not compose under autoregressive generation (unlike DP), and retrofitting credit onto an existing model is computationally infeasible in the black-box setting.

## Input / Output

The CCA generative model takes a deployment-time dataset S (e.g., a RAG database or in-context learning examples) and a text prompt x as input, and produces a generated token sequence y along with a credit set C ⊆ S indicating which documents the output depends on. The CCA condition requires that for every document s_i ∈ S, the output distribution conditioned on s_i not being in C is (ε,δ)-close to the counterfactual distribution where s_i is removed from S entirely. The credit set size |C| is a key cost parameter: smaller C means stricter CCA guarantees, while C = S trivially satisfies CCA and C = ∅ collapses it to full DP.

## Method

The paper proves two negative results. First, for non-composition, they construct a (0,0)-CCA next-token predictor whose autoregressive rollout is not (ε,δ)-CCA for any ε ≥ 0 and δ < 1, and derive a general lower bound showing the rollout's CCA parameter ε′ is at least max(f(x,y,S,s_i) − |y|·ε), counterintuitively worsening as the per-step ε shrinks. Second, for retrofitting infeasibility, they construct a family of models M_ℓ indexed by binary strings z ∈ {0,1}^ℓ where the only non-uniform behavior occurs at the single prompt x = z, then use Yao's Minimax Lemma to show that finding z requires Ω(2^ℓ) queries to the base model, while an α-approximation of the optimal CCA augmentation reveals z in only O(ℓ log ℓ) queries. The optimal CCA augmentation is characterized via a linear program, showing that even when a document's impact on the model is vanishingly small (TV ≤ 2^{-ℓ}), CCA still requires crediting it with constant probability γ > 0.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical barriers. First (Theorem 4.2), CCA does not compose autoregressively: for all ε≥0 and 0≤δ<1, there exists a (0,0)-CCA next-token predictor whose rollout is not (ε,δ)-CCA. Second (Theorem 5.5), CCA retrofitting is computationally infeasible: any black-box algorithm solving the α-approximate CCA-RETROFIT problem (for α<1/2, δ=0) requires Ω(2^ℓ/ℓ log ℓ) oracle queries to the base model in the worst case, where ℓ is the output length. Theorem 4.3 further provides a general lower bound showing that the rollout's CCA parameter ε′ is at least max(f(x,y,S,sᵢ) − |y|·ε), counterintuitively growing as the next-token predictor's ε shrinks toward 0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The key theoretical contrast is with differential privacy (DP): unlike DP, which composes under sequential/autoregressive generation, CCA does not compose autoregressively (Theorem 4.2). The paper also contrasts CCA with data attribution methods based on Shapley values (Ghorbani & Zou, 2019), noting that CCA is coarser (binary credit) and formulated differently, focusing on deployment-time datasets rather than training data. The retrofitting hardness result (Theorem 5.5) is proven via Yao's Minimax Lemma, showing that even approximating the optimal crediting probability within ±1/2 is infeasible with black-box access, in contrast to the non-black-box setting where the optimal augmentation is fully characterized (Lemma 5.6) for the constructed model family.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Counterfactual Credit Attribution (CCA) framework introduced by Livni et al. (2024) from the PAC learning setting to autoregressive generative models, explicitly addressing a direction they left as future work. It draws methodological inspiration from the standard DP LLM recipe of composing a private next-token predictor to obtain end-to-end privacy guarantees (Majmudar et al., 2022; Amin et al., 2024), but shows this approach fundamentally breaks for CCA. The paper also positions itself relative to the broader data attribution literature (e.g., Data Shapley, Ghorbani & Zou, 2019), noting that CCA is coarser (binary credit) and targets deployment-time datasets rather than training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies two fundamental barriers to building CCA generative models: CCA does not compose autoregressively (unlike DP), and black-box retrofitting requires exponentially many queries. These negative results redirect the design space for credit-attributing generative models and raise the question of whether CCA is the right formalization, since the optimal augmentation can require crediting documents with vanishing impact on the output. Open questions include whether allowing δ>0, relaxing the augmentation requirement, or non-black-box access can overcome these barriers, and whether simple sufficient conditions on the next-token predictor can guarantee CCA of its rollout.
