## Problem

Existing graph-based multimodal document retrieval relies on a uniform, hop-agnostic similarity metric and rigid pre-defined traversal plans, so once the retriever follows a spurious edge or hits a dead end, errors propagate across hops with no mechanism for recovery. There is also no principled way to decide when expensive LLM reasoning is warranted versus when lightweight vector matching suffices, leading to either under-reasoning on ambiguous hops or over-spending computation across a trajectory.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language query and a corpus of multimodal documents (paragraphs, tables, images) pre-parsed into a three-layered component graph: Layer 0 (document nodes), Layer 1 (component nodes), and Layer 2 (subcomponent nodes such as sentences, table rows, visual objects), connected by hierarchical 'contains' edges and navigational edges derived from hyperlinks and cross-references. It outputs a ranked list of top-k relevant components, which can be fed into a multimodal LLM for downstream QA. The retrieval is explicitly multi-hop, navigating both within a document (drilling down the hierarchy) and across documents (following navigational links), with the initial hop performed as a corpus-wide dense retrieval at subcomponent granularity.

## Method

FIF formulates open-domain multimodal retrieval as a finite-horizon information-state MDP where the state is a structured memory recording the original query, a subquery list, and an action history with explicit success/failure outcomes for each hop. An LLM-driven Orchestrator selects among three actions—TRAVERSE (executing a retrieval hop with a configurable strategy tuple controlling hop scope, vector-scoring granularity, and LLM-reasoning mode), PLAN (revising or expanding subqueries), and STOP (final reranking)—to balance accuracy and cost. The two core innovations are: (i) history-aware backtracking, where upon ≥2 consecutive failures the Orchestrator performs a joint three-axis update that re-anchors the search to a prior successful context, revises the subquery with constraints extracted from the failure trace, and escalates the strategy tuple one step along a predefined cost ladder; and (ii) cost-aware strategy escalation, which starts from low-cost vector-only matching and only escalates to LLM-based reranking when a hop is ambiguous or a recorded failure justifies the additional compute, avoiding both over-reasoning and under-reasoning.

## Results

FIF achieves state-of-the-art retrieval across all three benchmarks, with an average Recall@10 of 80.26 and MRR@10 of 83.16. On the end-to-end QA task, FIF attains an average EM/F1 of 59.63/68.45, outperforming the strongest agentic baseline IRCoT by +8.71% EM and +7.14% F1. The largest retrieval gains appear on WEBQA^Doc at higher cutoffs (R@10: 80.79 vs. 73.72 for IRCoT), while the most pronounced QA improvements occur on MMCoQA^Doc (EM: 51.11 vs. 47.21 for IRCoT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIF outperforms all six baselines on every (dataset, metric) cell in the retrieval table. Against the closest graph-based competitor LILAC, FIF gains +22.03% relative Recall@10 and +18.60% MRR@10 on average, with the largest absolute gap on WEBQA^Doc (R@10: 80.79 vs. 64.23). Versus the strongest agentic baselines, FIF improves over IRCoT by +12.88% R@10 and +9.31% MRR@10 on average, and over MoLoRAG by +11.39% R@10 and +8.92% MRR@10; the gap over IRCoT is most pronounced on the multi-hop benchmarks MMQA^Doc and MMCoQA^Doc where structural navigation matters. Single-shot embedding retrievers (NV-EMBED-v2, VisRAG, ColPali) trail FIF by 15–30+ points in R@10, confirming that multihop agentic traversal is essential for this task. In downstream QA, FIF leads IRCoT by +8.71% EM and +7.14% F1 on average, and LILAC by +16.37% EM, validating that better evidence retrieval translates directly into more accurate grounded generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIF builds directly on LILAC's layered component graph for multimodal document retrieval, extending it from a two-layer (component/subcomponent) structure to a three-layer hierarchy with an explicit Document Layer for early pruning. Conceptually, it departs from the rigid, similarity-driven beam search of LILAC and the flat-index agentic loops of IRCoT/MoLoRAG by formulating traversal as a sequential decision process (MDP) over a structured information state, where an LLM orchestrator dynamically chooses actions (traverse, plan, stop) and escalates strategy cost only when failure justifies it. The history-aware backtracking mechanism is the key architectural departure: rather than treating dead ends as terminal or simply reverting state, FIF piggybacks on failure traces to re-anchor, revise subqueries, and escalate strategy in a single joint update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical bottleneck for multimodal RAG over webpages and PDFs: existing retrievers either discard structural navigation (VisRAG, ColPali) or follow rigid plans that cannot recover from spurious hops (LILAC, MoLoRAG). The cost-aware strategy escalation and history-aware backtracking offer a principled way to balance retrieval accuracy against LLM inference cost, which is essential for deployment. However, the method remains 5–7× slower and ~10× more expensive per query than LILAC, and it assumes clean component parsing and reliable navigational signals—limitations that constrain immediate open-web deployment. Open questions include whether the orchestrator policy can be learned end-to-end (the paper explicitly leaves reward-driven fine-tuning as future work) and how the approach generalizes to noisier, dynamically structured web content.
