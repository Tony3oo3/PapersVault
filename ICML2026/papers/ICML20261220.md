## Problem

Existing robot world models are confined to in-distribution, discrete-control settings and fail to generalize to unseen objects, novel environments, or counterfactual continuous actions. This limitation stems from the narrow coverage of robot datasets (limited by hardware divergence and teleoperation cost) and the scarcity of fine-grained action labels needed for action-conditioned learning. The paper asks whether large-scale unlabeled human egocentric videos can be leveraged to pretrain a generalist world model that simulates dexterous, contact-rich manipulation with precise continuous action controllability.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single initial video frame at 640×480 resolution and a continuous action sequence as input, producing future video frames autoregressively in the WAN2.1 latent space (temporal compression ratio 4, so each latent frame encodes 4 pixel frames). During pretraining, actions are 32-dimensional continuous latent vectors extracted self-supervisedly from consecutive frame pairs; during post-training, they are relative robot joint actions (rebaselined per latent frame) chunked into groups of 4 to match the tokenizer's temporal compression. The distilled student model generates at 10.93 FPS in real time with only 4 denoising steps, supporting arbitrary-horizon autoregressive rollouts.

## Method

DreamDojo builds on Cosmos-Predict2.5 (a DiT-based latent video diffusion model trained with flow matching) and introduces three key modifications: (1) continuous latent actions extracted by a 700M spatiotemporal Transformer VAE with an information bottleneck serve as unified proxy labels across all 44k hours of egocentric human video, enabling self-supervised action conditioning without manual annotation; (2) relative actions rebaselined per latent frame and chunked injection (4 actions per latent frame) enforce causal structure and reduce modeling complexity; (3) a temporal consistency loss (matching predicted velocity differences between consecutive latent frames to ground-truth differences) is added to the flow matching objective to improve object dynamics and action following. After pretraining, the action MLP is reinitialized and the model is finetuned on small-scale target robot data. A Self Forcing–based distillation pipeline then converts the bidirectional 50-step teacher into a causal 4-step autoregressive student, achieving real-time inference while preserving generalization.

## Results

DreamDojo-2B achieves PSNR of 21.114 / 20.411 / 18.813 / 20.907 on In-lab, EgoDex, DreamDojo-HV, and Counterfactual Eval respectively, while DreamDojo-14B reaches 21.413 / 20.525 / 18.924 / 21.087. The distilled student model runs at 10.93 FPS (vs. 2.72 FPS for the teacher) with only modest quality degradation (PSNR 13.146 vs. 14.086 on GR-1 Long Eval). For policy evaluation, DreamDojo achieves Pearson r = 0.995 and MMRV = 0.003 against real-world success rates, and for model-based planning it improves success rate by 5.33% over the best policy checkpoint and ~13% over uniform sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cosmos-Predict2.5 (the base video diffusion model without human-video pretraining), DreamDojo-2B improves PSNR by 0.54 dB on In-lab Eval (21.114 vs. 20.576), 0.46 dB on EgoDex Eval (20.411 vs. 19.952), 0.54 dB on DreamDojo-HV Eval (18.813 vs. 18.274), and 0.44 dB on Counterfactual Eval (20.907 vs. 20.472), with consistent SSIM gains as well. Against Ctrl-World (Guo et al., 2025b), the prior SOTA dedicated robot world model, DreamDojo-2B wins by a large margin on every metric and every evaluation set: e.g., +1.04 dB PSNR and +0.055 SSIM on In-lab Eval, +0.77 dB PSNR on EgoDex Eval, +0.89 dB PSNR on DreamDojo-HV Eval, and +1.52 dB PSNR on Counterfactual Eval. The gap is largest on Counterfactual Eval, indicating that the human-video pretraining with latent actions is particularly effective for simulating actions not present in robot training data. In human preference studies on novel scenarios (EgoDex-novel and DreamDojo-HV-novel Eval), DreamDojo-2B is preferred over Cosmos-Predict2.5 62.5% of the time for physics correctness and 63.45% for action following, while DreamDojo-14B extends these to 73.5% and 72.55% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamDojo builds on the Cosmos-Predict2.5 latent video diffusion architecture (Ali et al., 2025) and the Self Forcing distillation paradigm (Huang et al., 2025b), but departs fundamentally from prior robot world models (Ctrl-World, IRASim, UniSim) that are trained exclusively on limited teleoperation data and remain confined to in-distribution settings. The key conceptual departure is using 44k hours of egocentric human videos as a pretraining corpus with continuous latent actions as unified proxy labels, drawing on the latent action literature (AdaWorld, Genie, LAPT) but scaling it to open-world dexterous manipulation for the first time. Architecturally, the paper introduces relative action transformation, chunked action injection aligned to the tokenizer's temporal compression, and a temporal consistency loss that directly supervises frame-to-frame velocity differences rather than individual frame predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a world model pretrained on diverse human videos can generalize zero-shot to unseen objects and novel environments after minimal post-training on a target robot, addressing the core data-coverage bottleneck that has limited prior robot world models. The practical impact is significant: the distilled model enables real-time (10.93 FPS) live teleoperation, policy evaluation with near-perfect rank correlation (MMRV=0.003) to real-world outcomes, and model-based planning that improves success rates by ~13% over uniform sampling. Key limitations include the absence of multi-view support, difficulty simulating uncommon actions (slapping, fast waving), and a tendency to overestimate success rates in simulation. Open questions include how to best preserve pretrained knowledge during embodiment-specific fine-tuning, how to cover broader failure distributions, and whether the approach extends to multi-camera or 3D-consistent world simulation.
