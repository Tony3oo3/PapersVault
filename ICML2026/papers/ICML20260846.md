## Problem

In dynamic graphs, information must propagate across both spatial neighbors and long temporal horizons, but existing temporal GNNs suffer from uncontrolled noise accumulation (when dynamics are purely conservative) or premature memory collapse (when dissipation is uncontrolled). While over-smoothing and over-squashing have been extensively studied for static graphs, the temporal counterpart—how to selectively retain or filter information over long spatio-temporal ranges—remains largely unexplored, directly limiting a model's ability to reason about distant interactions.

## Input / Output

LAMP consumes a sequence of discrete-time dynamic graph snapshots, each providing a node set, an evolving edge set, node feature vectors, and edge feature vectors. Between snapshots, the hidden state evolves continuously via a neural ODE solved with L forward-Euler steps; at each snapshot boundary, a discrete impulse injects the new node features into the latent state. The model outputs per-node hidden representations that are read out for future node-value forecasting (MSE) or link-prediction scores (F1), with all weights shared across both discretization steps and time steps.

## Method

LAMP formulates the latent state evolution as a neural impulsive ODE: between snapshots, a continuous flow governed by an antisymmetric parameterization (skew-symmetric channel mixing W_sk, symmetric-normalized diffusion V_sk, and antisymmetric random-walk diffusion Z_sym) ensures purely imaginary Jacobian eigenvalues and thus non-dissipative, lossless propagation. The key novelty is a learned per-channel diagonal damping matrix Γ = diag(sigmoid(γ)) subtracted from the channel-mixing term, which shifts the Jacobian spectrum into the left half-plane by a task-dependent amount, enabling the model to adaptively balance memory retention against noise filtering on a per-dimension basis. At each snapshot, a linear impulse operator with normalization ξ fuses the incoming node features into the hidden state. The paper proves three theoretical results: (i) a contraction-theory bound showing that strict contraction is guaranteed whenever dissipation exceeds the activation-induced expansion rate, (ii) an input-to-state-stability condition (ξ > e^{ωεL}) guaranteeing global boundedness over infinite sequences, and (iii) a sensitivity bound demonstrating that long-range propagation is limited only by topology and sequence length, not by a rigid architectural decay.

## Results

On the real-world node-level prediction benchmarks, LAMP achieves 0.444 MSE on Wikipedia Math (vs. 0.542 for the best baseline HMM4G), 0.748 MSE on ChickenPox Hungary (vs. 0.907 for DynGESN), and 0.304 MSE on Twitter Tennis (essentially tied with DynGESN at 0.300). On the novel ColoredLeafCounting long-range benchmark (three-color, T=200), LAMP achieves 0.057 MSE, dramatically outperforming the best baseline (GCRN-LSTM at 1.248). On link prediction, LAMP reaches 96.20 F1 on AS-733 (vs. 91.22 for GC-LSTM) and 93.71 F1 on Bitcoin-α (vs. 91.33 for LRGCN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAMP clearly outperforms all baselines on Wikipedia Math (0.444 vs. 0.542 for HMM4G, a ~18% relative improvement) and ChickenPox Hungary (0.748 vs. 0.907 for DynGESN, ~18% improvement). On Twitter Tennis, LAMP (0.304) is essentially tied with DynGESN (0.300), the strongest baseline. On the ColoredLeafCounting long-range benchmark, LAMP dominates by a wide margin (e.g., 0.057 vs. 1.248 MSE for GCRN-LSTM at T=200 three-color), while all baselines degrade significantly as sequence length increases. On link prediction, LAMP achieves 96.20 F1 on AS-733, surpassing GC-LSTM (91.22) by over 5 points, and 93.71 F1 on Bitcoin-α, beating LRGCN (91.33). On traffic forecasting (Metr-LA, PeMS-Bay), LAMP is competitive but not the best: RGDAN and STD-MAE achieve slightly lower MAE/RMSE, while LAMP outperforms classical GNNs like DCRNN and Graph WaveNet and is comparable to attention-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAMP sits at the intersection of three research threads: non-dissipative neural ODEs with antisymmetric parameterization (Chang et al., 2019; Gravina et al., 2023), impulsive differential equations for handling discrete events (Lakshmikantham et al., 1989), and temporal graph ODEs for dynamic graphs (Gravina et al., 2024b). The key departure from prior DE-GNN work is the introduction of a learned, channel-wise diagonal damping matrix Γ that relaxes the strictly conservative dynamics, allowing the model to adaptively trade off information retention against noise filtering. Unlike prior approaches that impose static architectural constraints (orthogonal or antisymmetric weights) to ensure stability, LAMP makes dissipation a learnable, data-driven quantity while retaining the non-dissipative backbone for long-range propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized gap: while over-smoothing and over-squashing in static GNNs are well-studied, the temporal dimension's role in information propagation has been far less explored, and existing methods struggle with long-range spatio-temporal dependencies. The ColoredLeafCounting benchmark fills a practical need for stress-testing joint spatial-temporal propagation, and the theoretical guarantees (contraction bounds, ISS-based global boundedness) provide principled stability without requiring weight normalization or gradient clipping. Open questions include whether the adaptive mechanism's benefit over fixed or zero dissipation generalizes beyond the tested settings (the ablation shows differences within standard deviation in several configurations), and whether the framework extends to irregularly sampled graphs or higher-order graph operators.
