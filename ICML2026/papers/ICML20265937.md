## Problem

Point cloud self-supervised learning remains domain-fragmented: prior methods like Sonata and Concerto are trained within a single domain (indoor, outdoor, or object-centric) and transfer poorly across domains due to extreme shifts in spatial extent, density, sampling patterns, and modality availability. Simply merging datasets for joint training is unstable and degrades performance, as the encoder becomes dominated by domain-specific cues rather than learning transferable geometry.

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a unified input of point cloud coordinates concatenated with optional color and normal channels (missing modalities filled with zeros), and produces per-point feature vectors. A key design detail is that coordinates are rescaled to a shared perceptual granularity before positional encoding, so the same architectural operator covers comparable metric extents regardless of whether the input is a city-scale LiDAR scan or a small object CAD model. At inference, the rescaling factor can be adjusted to match the desired granularity of the target scene.

## Method

Utonia pretrains a single Point Transformer V3 encoder via teacher-student self-distillation across 250k cross-domain point clouds plus 1M CAD assets, introducing three domain-agnostic designs to resolve cross-domain mismatches. First, Causal Modality Blinding randomly drops entire color or normal channels at both per-sample and per-point levels during pretraining, preventing the encoder from treating modality availability as a shortcut. Second, Perceptual Granularity Rescale maps all point clouds into a shared coordinate space by rescaling to a standard observing granularity, with stronger scale and SO(3) rotation augmentations applied to object-centric data to erase gravity-axis bias. Third, 3D Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) is applied to the granularity-aligned (and further jittered/scaled) coordinates in every attention layer, providing a continuous relative-geometry positional cue that makes attention robust to density non-uniformity and cross-domain coordinate shifts without altering neighborhood construction.

## Results

Utonia achieves SOTA or near-SOTA performance across all three domain categories. On indoor segmentation (full fine-tuning), it reaches 81.1% mIoU on ScanNet and 78.1% on S3DIS, surpassing Concerto (80.7% and 77.4%). On outdoor segmentation, it attains 82.2% mIoU on NuScenes, 71.4% on Waymo, and 72.0% on SemanticKITTI, outperforming Concerto on all three. On object tasks, Utonia scores 92.4% mAcc on ModelNet40, 95.0% on ScanObjectNN, and 62.7% mIoU on PartNetE (full fine-tuning). A standout result is modality robustness: under linear probing without color, Utonia retains 77.0% mIoU on ScanNet versus Concerto's 36.8%, a 40-point gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Concerto (the most recent SOTA), Utonia wins on nearly every benchmark: +0.4 mIoU on ScanNet (81.1 vs 80.7), +0.7 on S3DIS (78.1 vs 77.4), +0.2 on NuScenes (82.2 vs 82.0), +2.2 on Waymo (71.4 vs 69.2), +0.8 on SemanticKITTI (72.0 vs 71.2), +2.7 on ScanObjectNN (95.0 vs 92.3), and +1.9 on PartNetE (62.7 vs 60.8) under full fine-tuning. Against Sonata, Utonia consistently outperforms by 1–3 mIoU on indoor/outdoor segmentation and by 2–3 points on object classification. The most dramatic advantage is under modality-missing conditions (Table 7): Concerto's linear-probing mIoU on ScanNet collapses from 77.3 (with color) to 36.8 (without color), while Utonia drops only to 77.0, demonstrating that Causal Modality Blinding eliminates the over-reliance on auxiliary channels. On the finer-grained ScanNet200 and ScanNet++ benchmarks, Utonia trails Concerto slightly under linear probing (36.4 vs 37.4; 44.7 vs 45.6) but closes or reverses the gap with decoder probing (38.0 vs 37.8; 48.5 vs 48.3), suggesting Utonia's representations encode richer spatial structure that benefits from non-linear readout. Against the supervised PPT baseline, Utonia (self-supervised) surpasses it on ScanNet (81.1 vs 78.6) and S3DIS (78.1 vs 74.3) under full fine-tuning, confirming that cross-domain SSL can match or exceed supervised pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

Utonia builds directly on the Point Transformer V3 backbone and the teacher-student self-distillation recipe established by Sonata and Concerto, which each demonstrated stable large-scale point cloud SSL within individual domains (indoor/outdoor for Sonata, plus video-lifted clouds for Concerto). The key departure is that prior methods remain domain-siloed: they train on a single sensing geometry and transfer poorly across domains, as shown by the toy-car-to-real-car retrieval failure. Utonia identifies three concrete mismatches (inconsistent modality availability, granularity shifts, gravity-convention bias) and addresses them with minimal domain-agnostic designs—Causal Modality Blinding, Perceptual Granularity Rescale, and RoPE on granularity-aligned coordinates—rather than introducing domain-specific modules or mixture-of-experts routing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a single point transformer encoder can be stably pretrained across indoor, outdoor, object, remote-sensing, and video-lifted point clouds while achieving competitive or SOTA performance on each domain's standard benchmarks, suggesting that cross-domain fragmentation in point cloud SSL is a design artifact rather than a fundamental barrier. The emergent behaviors—mutual benefit across domains, gravity-prior erasure for objects while retaining scene alignment, and transfer to VLM spatial reasoning and VLA robotic manipulation—point toward sparse point clouds as a viable substrate for unified spatial cognition. Open questions include whether the current sparse-convolution backbone can scale further without memory bottlenecks, whether query-based decoders can close the linear-probing gap on part segmentation, and how to extend from static 3D to 4D spatiotemporal pretraining.
