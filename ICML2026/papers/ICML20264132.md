## Problem

High-performance agentic systems (multi-agent frameworks, proprietary assistants) operate as opaque black boxes whose internal workflows—agent roles, tool invocations, coordination topology—are inaccessible to users, limiting interpretability, controllability, and deployment in safety-critical domains. The paper introduces Agentic Workflow Reconstruction (AWR), a new task that asks whether an explicit, editable white-box surrogate workflow can be synthesized from input-output observations alone, without access to model parameters or internal architecture.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of input-output pairs (task descriptions and corresponding outputs) collected from a strict black-box system, where only the I/O interface is accessible—no internal prompts, agent topology, or model weights are available. It produces a linear sequence of agentic primitives (each a tuple of role, base model, thought pattern, and attached toolset) of length at most L_max, forming an executable and editable workflow. Fidelity is measured via Static Functional Equivalence (SFE), a structure-aware AST-based code similarity metric combining interface (20%), logic (50%), and semantic (30%) components, with a TF-IDF cosine fallback for unparseable outputs.

## Method

AgentXRay formulates AWR as combinatorial optimization over a unified primitive space Ω where agents and tools are treated as atomic search units, and searches for the optimal linear workflow using Monte Carlo Tree Search over workflow prefixes. The key mechanism is a dynamic Red-Black Pruning scheme: each tree node receives a composite score combining Quality (Q/N), normalized Depth, and Width (children/branching cap), and nodes above a β-quantile threshold are colored RED (select among existing children via UCB for depth refinement) while lower-scoring nodes are BLACK (create new children for width expansion). This color-guided selection concentrates the limited iteration budget on high-potential workflow backbones, preventing MCTS from stagnating at shallow depth (L=2 without pruning vs. L=6 with pruning). The paper provides theoretical bounds showing exponential search-space contraction under the pruning rule, and empirically demonstrates 8–22% token reduction and higher reconstruction fidelity (avg. SFE 0.426) compared to the unpruned AFlow baseline across five diverse domains.

## Results

AgentXRay (All Tools) achieves the highest average Static Functional Equivalence (SFE) score of 0.426 across five domains, outperforming the unpruned MCTS baseline AFlow (0.339), Claude Opus 4.5 + ReAct (0.299), Claude alone (0.292), and SFT (0.196). The largest gains appear on MetaGPT (0.557 vs. 0.280 for AFlow) and ChatDev (0.425 vs. 0.403), while the smallest margin is on Scientific Computing (0.395 vs. 0.373). Red-Black Pruning also reduces token consumption by 8–22% relative to unpruned search, with the largest saving on ChatDev (22.3%, from 17.16M to 13.33M tokens).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgentXRay (All Tools) wins on all five domains against every baseline. Against AFlow (the closest competitor, an MCTS-based workflow search without pruning), AgentXRay improves average SFE from 0.339 to 0.426 (+25.7%), with the most dramatic gain on MetaGPT (+0.277) and a more modest one on Sci (+0.022). Against the strong single-model baselines, Claude Opus 4.5 alone scores 0.292 and adding ReAct-style tool use lifts it only to 0.299, both roughly 0.13 below AgentXRay, indicating that raw model capacity and tool augmentation cannot substitute for structured workflow search. SFT (behavior cloning on I/O pairs) performs worst at 0.196 average, collapsing to 0.091 on 3D Modeling and 0.124 on Education, confirming that input-output supervision alone cannot recover the procedural structure of agentic workflows. Notably, removing Red-Black Pruning from AgentXRay causes it to underperform AFlow on ChatDev (0.286 vs. 0.403), underscoring that the pruning mechanism is essential for the unified primitive space's larger branching factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

AgentXRay extends the MCTS-based workflow search paradigm introduced by AFlow (Zhang et al., 2025a) by adding a Red-Black Pruning mechanism that dynamically allocates search budget between depth refinement and width expansion, addressing the combinatorial explosion that arises when agents and tools are unified into a single primitive space. Conceptually, the paper carves out a distinct problem from both automated workflow generation (which optimizes from scratch with white-box access) and model distillation (which transfers behavior into parameters), positioning AWR as a black-box-to-white-box reconstruction task analogous in spirit to model extraction via prediction APIs but yielding editable workflows rather than parametric replicas. The linearity hypothesis, drawing on execution-order observations from MacNet and orchestrator-based coordination, deliberately restricts the search to chain-structured workflows, departing from the graph-structured topologies used in frameworks like ChatDev and MetaGPT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: deployed agentic systems are often opaque black boxes, and AWR offers a path to recover interpretable, editable surrogates from I/O observations alone, which is relevant for debugging, adaptation, and safety-critical deployment. However, the linearity assumption excludes true concurrency and asynchronous coordination, and the evaluation is code-centric (SFE metric), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to natural-language or multi-threaded agentic settings. The modest 8–22% token savings under practical budgets (N=20) fall well short of the exponential asymptotic bounds, suggesting that the pruning's real value is budget allocation rather than asymptotic speedup. Establishing theoretical bounds on reconstructibility and extending to graph-structured workflows remain important open problems.
