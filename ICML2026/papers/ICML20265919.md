## Problem

Most LLM-based sentence embedding methods rely on final-layer hidden states, which are optimized for next-token prediction and thus often fail to capture global, sentence-level semantics. This paper asks whether alternative internal features of LLMs—specifically attention value vectors—provide a stronger basis for representing sentence semantics, and whether this can be exploited in a training-free setting to match or surpass prompt-based ensemble methods like MetaEOL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265919/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw text sentence as input and produces a fixed-dimensional sentence embedding vector via a single standard forward pass with no additional prompts or computation. For models with grouped-query attention (e.g., Qwen-3), the value vectors naturally have one-fourth the dimensionality of hidden states (1024 vs. 4096), making downstream similarity computation substantially cheaper. The primary VA and AlignedWVA variants are entirely training-free, though a LoRA-based fine-tuning variant (Finetune-VA) is also explored.

## Method

Value Aggregation (VA) pools token-level value vectors v_n^{l,h} (concatenated across heads) via mean pooling over tokens and a selected set of deep layers, requiring only a standard forward pass to extract values without any extra prompt. The theoretical motivation draws on truth-conditional semantics: a sentence's meaning is approximated by its influence on continuations, and value vectors—being the content that attention weights and aggregates—encode this influence more directly than hidden states, which are shaped by next-token discrimination. The method extends to Aligned Weighted VA (AlignedWVA), where the last token's attention scores serve as token weights and the output projection matrix W_O maps the weighted value aggregation back into the residual-stream space, aligning it with the representation space used by EOL-style baselines. Layer selection is model-specific (e.g., layers 20–27 for LLaMA-2-7B; layers 26, 27, 29, 30, 31 for Qwen-3-8B), chosen empirically to balance task performance across retrieval, STS, clustering, and classification. AlignedWVA with a FutureEOL prompt achieves state-of-the-art training-free performance, surpassing the 8-prompt MetaEOL ensemble by over 4 points on average while requiring only a single forward pass.

## Results

On a 14-task MTEB subset, training-free VA (Llama-2-7B) achieves an average score of 52.25, outperforming all prompt-free baselines (best: WMP at 40.46) and most prompt-based methods (best: MetaEOL at 50.83). The enhanced AlignedWVA(FutureEOL) with Qwen-3-8B reaches 56.84 average, surpassing the 8-prompt ensemble MetaEOL (52.72) by over 4 points while requiring only a single forward pass. On retrieval (SciFact), VA (Llama-2) scores 54.58 vs. MetaEOL's 40.59; on STS17, AlignedWVA(FutureEOL) (Qwen-3) reaches 84.31 vs. MetaEOL's 80.72. VA with Qwen-3 operates at only 1024 dimensions (vs. 4096 for hidden-state methods) due to grouped-query attention, yet remains highly competitive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prompt-free hidden-state baselines (LT, WMP, HS variants from Muennighoff 2022), VA achieves a substantial 11.8-point average improvement over WMP (52.25 vs. 40.46 on Llama-2) and ~19 points over LT (52.25 vs. 33.05), with the largest gains on retrieval (SciFact: 54.58 vs. 38.89 for WMP). Against Echo Embedding (Springer et al., 2025), VA wins by 5.7 points on average (52.25 vs. 46.54 on Llama-2) despite EE's self-prompting strategy. Against PromptEOL and MetaEOL (Lei et al., 2024), VA (Llama-2) surpasses PromptEOL by 8.7 points and MetaEOL by 1.4 points on average; AlignedWVA(FutureEOL) extends this lead to +4.1 points over MetaEOL on Qwen-3 (56.84 vs. 52.72) while using only 1 forward pass instead of 8. Against PromptEOL+CP (Cheng et al., 2025), VA wins by 7.7 points on Llama-2 (52.25 vs. 44.60). In the fine-tuning comparison (Table 6), Finetune-VA (62.14 avg) slightly outperforms Finetune-MP (61.28) and approaches LLM2Vec (66.11), while Finetune-VA (Attention Only) matches Finetune-MP performance using less than 1/4 of the trainable parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in LLM-based sentence embedding, where all prior methods (LT, WMP, HS, Echo Embedding, PromptEOL, MetaEOL, CP) pool hidden states in various ways. It builds on the EOL prompting framework (Lei et al., 2024) for the AlignedWVA variant and on LLM2Vec (BehnamGhader et al., 2024) for the fine-tuning experiments, but its core contribution is a shift in *which* internal signal to aggregate: attention value vectors rather than hidden states. The truth-conditional semantics framing (Davidson, 1967) provides a conceptual justification for why value vectors—being the content that attention actually mixes—should better encode a sentence's influence on its continuation than the next-token-optimized hidden state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: VA is training-free, requires a single forward pass (vs. 8× for MetaEOL), and for GQA models like Qwen-3 produces embeddings at 1/4 the dimensionality while matching or exceeding ensemble-based baselines. The finding that a simple mean-pool of value vectors rivals expensive prompt-ensemble methods suggests that the field has been overlooking a rich representation source. Open questions include whether the truth-conditional framing is genuinely explanatory or post-hoc, whether the layer-selection heuristic generalizes to new architectures, and whether the gap to fully-trained embedding models (e.g., Qwen3-Embedding-8B) can be closed through more targeted fine-tuning of the value space.
