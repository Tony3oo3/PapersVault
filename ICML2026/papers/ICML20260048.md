## Problem

Existing discrete-time dynamic graph (DTDG) methods adopt an offline learning setting where models are trained once on historical snapshots and then frozen for all future predictions, ignoring the structural distribution shift between training and test snapshots. This shift causes pattern-blurred embeddings that fail to distinguish positive from negative node pairs, degrading link prediction accuracy. The paper asks whether a lightweight, label-free adaptation mechanism can bridge this gap at inference time without disturbing the temporal dynamics encoded in the pre-trained backbone.

## Input / Output

At each inference time step t, the method consumes the test graph adjacency matrix A_t^test, the node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×D}, and the historical temporal embedding H_{t-1} from the frozen backbone. It produces a learnable prompt P_t ∈ R^{N×D} (or a compact global vector via low-rank factorization) that is added to X before passing through the backbone, yielding adapted node embeddings H̃_t. The final output is a similarity score s_uv = h̃_u h̃_v^T for each candidate node pair, used to rank positive edges among 1000 sampled negatives for MRR evaluation. No ground-truth labels are required during adaptation.

## Method

PromptDyG freezes a pre-trained DTDG backbone (e.g., Roland-GRU) and injects a lightweight learnable prompt P_t into the input feature space, so that H̃_t = f_θ^t(A_t^test, X + P_t, H_{t-1}). The prompt is optimized by minimizing the average Shannon entropy over the softmax-normalized node embeddings (feature-wise, label-free entropy), which requires no labels and no backbone parameter updates. Theoretically, the paper shows that the final linear layer defines K structural evolutionary prototypes, and entropy minimization drives each node's embedding toward its dominant prototype, expanding the similarity margin γ = s_uv − s_uw between true and non-existent links. The approach is model-agnostic and plug-and-play: it can be attached to any DTDG backbone (EvolveGCN, GCRN, TGCN, SFDyG) with only two hyperparameters (learning rate and number of adaptation steps), achieving O(eSK) complexity per snapshot versus O(NK) for Tent or O(N²K) for GTrans.

## Results

PromptDyG achieves the best MRR on all six DTDG benchmarks, with an average MRR of 0.2794 and an average rank of 1.00. The largest improvement over the Roland-GRU backbone (0.2627 avg. MRR) is on Reddit-body (+2.84%), while the smallest is on AS-733 (+0.67%). In terms of efficiency, PromptDyG's average adaptation time per epoch ranges from 3.20 ms (Bitcoin-Alpha) to 33.90 ms (Reddit-title), substantially lower than GTrans (15.46–149.83 ms) and competitive with Tent (5.45–39.72 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PromptDyG outperforms all ten DTDG baselines and three TTA baselines on every dataset. Against the strongest DTDG backbone Roland-GRU (0.2627 avg. MRR), PromptDyG gains +0.67% on AS-733, +2.13% on Reddit-title, +2.84% on Reddit-body, +17.0% on UCI, +3.7% on Bitcoin-OTC, and +12.0% on Bitcoin-Alpha. Among TTA methods, Tent and Matcha actually degrade the backbone on temporally-dependent datasets (e.g., Tent drops from 0.3381 to 0.3135 on AS-733 and from 0.4212 to 0.3628 on Reddit-title), while GTrans improves the backbone but at 4–44× higher adaptation time than PromptDyG (e.g., 149.83 ms vs. 33.90 ms on Reddit-title). PromptDyG also surpasses the recent SFDyG (0.2569 avg. MRR) by +2.25% on average, and when applied as a plug-and-play module to weaker backbones like EvolveGCN-H, it yields up to +3.37% MRR improvement (Reddit-title). Overall, PromptDyG achieves the best average rank (1.00) and highest average MRR (0.2794) while maintaining the lowest or near-lowest adaptation latency among all TTA methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: discrete-time dynamic graph (DTDG) models (Roland, EvolveGCN, GCRN, SFDyG) that capture spatio-temporal evolution but operate in an offline, train-once-evaluate-many setting, and test-time adaptation (TTA) methods (Tent, GTrans, Matcha) that handle distribution shift but are designed for static graphs. The key departure is from both: rather than updating backbone parameters (as Tent/Matcha do) or augmenting graph data (as GTrans does), PromptDyG introduces a lightweight learnable prompt on a frozen DTDG backbone, optimized via feature-wise, label-free entropy minimization. The theoretical framing—interpreting the final linear layer's columns as 'evolutionary prototypes' and showing entropy minimization drives embeddings toward dominant prototypes to expand the positive-negative similarity margin—provides a principled justification absent from prior TTA-for-graphs work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete gap: existing DTDG models ignore structural distribution shifts between training and test snapshots, which is particularly problematic under the live-update online setting that better reflects real-world deployment. The plug-and-play design (consistent 1–4% MRR gains across five diverse backbones) and low computational overhead (linear in sampled nodes, no backbone backpropagation) make it practically attractive for practitioners deploying dynamic graph models. However, the scope is limited to link prediction on DTDGs; the authors explicitly note that extension to CTDGs, node classification, anomaly detection, and other graph tasks remains open. Whether the entropy-minimization objective generalizes beyond the specific prototype-interpretation assumed in the theory (orthogonal W columns) is an open question.
