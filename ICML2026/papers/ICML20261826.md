## Problem

The Edge of Stability (EoS) phenomenon—where Hessian sharpness hovers near the 2/η threshold during gradient descent—has been characterized for vanilla GD and preconditioned GD, but no unified sharpness measure or theoretical framework exists for the broader family of non-Euclidean gradient methods such as ℓ∞-descent, Block CD, Spectral GD, and their normalized variants. This paper asks whether EoS generalizes to arbitrary norm geometries and, if so, what the correct sharpness diagnostic is.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a neural network's parameter vector (or matrix blocks), the loss Hessian (accessed via Hessian-vector products), a norm specification defining the update geometry, and a step size η. It produces a generalized sharpness estimate S^{‖·‖}(w) = max_{‖d‖=1} dᵀ∇²L(w)d, which is computed in closed form for ℓ2, preconditioned ℓ2, and block ℓ1,2 norms but requires multi-restart Frank-Wolfe approximation for ℓ∞ and spectral norms where the maximization over the nonconvex unit sphere is NP-hard. The directional smoothness D^{‖·‖}(w_t, w_{t+1}) is computed exactly from the loss change along each update step, and together these quantities yield a geometry-aware EoS diagnostic that flags when the algorithm is at, above, or below the stability threshold 2/η.

## Method

The paper's key insight is that directional smoothness (Mishkin et al., 2024)—the average curvature between consecutive iterates—provides an exact loss-change identity: for non-Euclidean GD, ΔL_t = −η(1 − (η/2)D^{‖·‖})‖∇L‖_*², so the loss decreases if and only if D^{‖·‖} ≤ 2/η. This motivates defining a generalized sharpness S^{‖·‖}(w) = max_{‖d‖=1} dᵀ∇²L(w)d that upper-bounds directional smoothness and recovers the standard λ_max(∇²L) for ℓ2 and the preconditioned-Hessian sharpness for Mahalanobis norms. For norms where the maximization is NP-hard (ℓ∞, spectral), the paper uses Frank-Wolfe with multiple random restarts and a final projection onto the unit sphere. Theoretical results on quadratic objectives establish linear convergence below 2/η and a divergence construction above 2/η via an invariant direction lemma. Empirically, across MLPs, CNNs, and Transformers trained with ℓ∞-descent, Block CD, Spectral GD, SignGD, and normalized Spectral GD, the generalized sharpness exhibits progressive sharpening followed by oscillations at or slightly above 2/η, while the standard ℓ2 sharpness often remains far below the threshold—confirming that EoS is a geometry-dependent phenomenon.

## Results

Across MLP, CNN, and Transformer architectures trained on CIFAR-10 and Tiny Shakespeare, the proposed generalized sharpness S^{‖·‖} exhibits progressive sharpening followed by oscillations around (or slightly above) the stability threshold 2/η for all tested non-Euclidean GD variants: ℓ∞-descent, Block CD, Spectral GD, and their normalized counterparts (SignGD, normalized Spectral GD). On larger models (ResNet20, VGG11), the generalized sharpness stabilizes at 2/η while the standard ℓ2 sharpness λmax(∇²L(w_t)) remains well below the threshold, confirming that EoS is a geometry-dependent phenomenon. The quadratic Taylor approximation experiments (Figures 6, 17) confirm that non-Euclidean GD is stable before EoS but divergent on the local quadratic model once the generalized sharpness reaches 2/η.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Cohen et al. (2021), who established EoS for vanilla GD using the standard ℓ2 sharpness λmax(∇²L(w_t)), this paper shows that the ℓ2 sharpness fails to capture EoS behavior for non-Euclidean GD: on ResNet20 and VGG11 trained with ℓ∞-descent (Figure 12) and Spectral GD (Figure 18), the ℓ2 sharpness remains significantly below 2/η throughout training, while the proposed generalized sharpness stabilizes at the threshold. Compared to Cohen et al. (2022), who showed that adaptive methods like AdaGrad and RMSprop exhibit EoS via the top eigenvalue of the preconditioned Hessian stabilizing at 2/η, this paper demonstrates (Figure 19) that this adaptive EoS characterization breaks down when β2 is small (as in SignGD, the β2→0 limit of RMSprop): the preconditioned Hessian eigenvalues do not stabilize at 2/η but instead exceed it by a substantial margin, whereas the generalized sharpness framework correctly identifies the EoS threshold in this regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Edge of Stability (EoS) literature, which has so far characterized the phenomenon for vanilla GD (Cohen et al., 2021), preconditioned/adaptive methods (Cohen et al., 2022), and SAM (Long & Bartlett, 2024), to an arbitrary non-Euclidean norm. The key conceptual bridge is directional smoothness (Mishkin et al., 2024), which the authors show yields an exact loss-change identity that naturally generalizes beyond the Euclidean setting. By defining a generalized sharpness S^{‖·‖} as the maximum Hessian quadratic form over the unit ball of the chosen norm, the paper recovers the standard λmax(∇²L) for ℓ2-GD and the preconditioned-Hessian sharpness for Mahalanobis-norm GD as special cases, while extending the EoS diagnostic to previously unstudied methods such as ℓ∞-descent, Block CD, Spectral GD (underlying Muon), and their normalized variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a geometry-aware spectral diagnostic that practitioners can apply to a broad class of non-Euclidean optimizers increasingly used in practice (Muon, Scion, SignGD), and demonstrates that the standard ℓ2 sharpness can fail to capture EoS behavior for these methods. Two open questions are highlighted: (1) the quadratic analysis proves divergence only for a specific initialization rather than generically, and (2) a novel pre-EoS oscillatory regime—where directional smoothness climbs toward 2/η before generalized sharpness does—has no Euclidean analogue and its mechanism is unexplained. Extending the framework to stochastic, momentum-based, and adaptive optimizers would clarify how geometry-aware EoS diagnostics should be used in large-scale training.
