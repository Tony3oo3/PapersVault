## Problem

The Graph Label Selection Problem (GLS) seeks k vertices whose labels best predict the remaining vertices, formalized by maximizing the minimum sparsity over all unlabeled subsets. Prior algorithms either relied on resource augmentation (using substantially more than k labeled vertices) or were heuristics without provable guarantees. This paper resolves the open problem posed by Cohen-Addad et al. (2025) by presenting the first Õ(log^1.5 n)-approximation algorithm under the standard budget constraint.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a weighted undirected graph G=(V,E,w) with n vertices and edge weights in [1, poly(n)], plus a budget k ∈ ℕ, and returns a set L' of at most k vertices maximizing Ψ(L') = min_{C⊆V\L'} w(C,V\C)/|C|. The objective ensures no large cluster of unlabeled vertices with few connections to the rest of the graph exists. The algorithm runs in polynomial time, with total runtime O(T_sparsifier + n·k²·log(n·W)) where W is the edge-weight aspect ratio.

## Method

The algorithm performs three successive reductions. First, it constructs a tree cut sparsifier (Räcke & Shah, 2014) with Õ(log^1.5 n) approximation, reducing GLS on general graphs to a Leaf Label Selection problem on a binary tree. Second, it formulates a flow gadget T_{L,τ} where the s-t mincut equals n·τ if and only if the label selection quality meets threshold τ, converting the problem into a 'sink selection' subproblem. Third—and most critically—it solves the sink selection problem via dynamic programming on the binary tree rather than a general max-flow solver, with DP state DP[v][k] tracking the maximum injectable flow at vertex v given k allowed sinks in its subtree. This DP-based approach captures global interactions between labeled points that greedy one-at-a-time selection cannot, and a binary search over τ (justified by monotonicity of the optimal |L| in τ) yields the final solution in O(n·k²·log(n·W)) time.

## Results

The paper presents the first Õ(log^1.5 n)-approximation algorithm for Graph Label Selection under the standard budget constraint (no resource augmentation). In experiments on ca-GrQc, the proposed FIEDLER-based algorithm runs in ~22 seconds across all tested budgets (k=10, 50, 100), compared to 4,967–22,956 seconds for Cohen-Addad et al. (2025) and 144–1,835 seconds for Guillory & Bilmes (2009). On the large com-dblp graph (317K vertices), the METIS variant achieves objective values of 0.030, 0.048, and 0.083 for k=50, 500, and 5000 respectively, while prior methods cannot scale to this size. The authors report that solution quality essentially matches the state-of-the-art Cohen-Addad et al. (2025) algorithm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cohen-Addad et al. (2025), the closest prior work with theoretical guarantees, the proposed method achieves essentially the same solution quality (as shown in Figures 6 and 7) while being roughly 225× faster at k=10 on ca-GrQc (22 s vs. 4,967 s) and over 1,000× faster at k=100 (22 s vs. 22,956 s). Against Guillory & Bilmes (2009), the original heuristic, the proposed method is 6–85× faster and matches or slightly exceeds quality. Against Cesa-Bianchi et al. (2010), which is restricted to trees, the proposed method is slightly slower (22 s vs. 14 s) but applies to general graphs and achieves comparable quality. The key practical advantage is scalability: the proposed algorithm handles the 317K-vertex com-dblp graph within hours, whereas Cohen-Addad et al. and Guillory & Bilmes cannot complete even a single k-value on the much smaller ca-GrQc graph (4K vertices) within reasonable time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper resolves an explicit open problem posed by Cohen-Addad et al. (2025), who showed GLS is NP-hard and provided only a resource-augmented algorithm (using O(log n)·k labeled vertices). The key technical departure is a two-stage reduction: first to a binary tree via a tree cut sparsifier (Räcke & Shah, 2014), then to a flow problem solvable by dynamic programming on the tree structure, bypassing the need for general max-flow. This contrasts with prior greedy or heuristic approaches (Guillory & Bilmes, 2009; Cesa-Bianchi et al., 2010) that lacked guarantees under the standard budget constraint, and with Cohen-Addad et al.'s flow gadget that required solving max-flow on a general graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first true approximation algorithm (no resource augmentation) for graph label selection on general graphs, with a Õ(log^1.5 n) guarantee, and it is optimal on weighted trees. The extension to vertex-importance-weighted objectives (e.g., conductance-like metrics) broadens the framework's applicability to diverse sampling and active learning settings. The main practical limitation is the O(n·k²) dynamic program, which the authors identify as the bottleneck for scaling to massive graphs; an approximate DP running in linear time is flagged as a key open direction. Empirically, replacing the exact tree cut sparsifier with simple METIS/Fiedler heuristics retains quality while enabling runs on 300K-vertex graphs where prior methods struggle.
