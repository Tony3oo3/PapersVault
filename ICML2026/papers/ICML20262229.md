## Problem

Standard deep neural network controllers rely on dense multiply-accumulate operations, making them impractical for resource-constrained embedded platforms like UAVs and mobile robots that demand low latency and minimal energy per control action. While quantization and pruning reduce bit-width or sparsity, they still retain weight-based matrix multiplications. This paper challenges whether continuous control policies can be learned without any weights at all, replacing arithmetic entirely with sparse boolean logic that maps directly to FPGA lookup tables.

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous observation vectors (e.g., 376-dim for Humanoid) which are normalized via running statistics, clipped to [-10, 10], and discretized into 63-bit thermometer codes per dimension using stretched-Gaussian quantile thresholds. Outputs are continuous control actions: the final binary features are partitioned into per-action-dimension groups, popcounted, and mapped through a learnable per-dimension affine transform (plus tanh for SAC) to produce real-valued actions. At deployment, the entire pipeline reduces to integer comparisons, LUT evaluations, popcounts, and a single SRAM lookup per action dimension.

## Method

DWCs replace dense linear layers with two layers of k=6-input boolean lookup tables (1024 LUTs per layer), where each LUT is a 64-entry binary table addressed by a sparse selection of input bits. Real-valued inputs are converted via adaptive thermometer encoding with quantile-based thresholds, and binary outputs are aggregated by group popcount and decoded to continuous actions through a differentiable affine head. Training uses standard RL algorithms (SAC, DDPG, PPO) with extended finite difference (EFD) surrogate gradients for LUT entries and straight-through estimation for the learnable interconnect between layers, making the entire architecture end-to-end differentiable despite its discrete forward pass. The resulting circuits compile to FPGA LUTs with zero DSP and zero BRAM usage, achieving 1–3 clock-cycle latency and ~1–2 nJ energy per action on an Artix-7 at 100 MHz, while matching full-precision policy returns on four of five MuJoCo benchmarks.

## Results

Across five MuJoCo continuous-control benchmarks, DWCs (1024 LUTs per layer, k=6) achieve median returns of 5.7k (Ant), 7.5k (HalfCheetah), 3.1k (Hopper), 6.1k (Humanoid), and 5.0k (Walker2d), matching or exceeding full-precision SAC baselines on four of five tasks. The sole exception is HalfCheetah, where DWCs reach 7.5k versus 11.5k for FP (though a 16k-LUT variant closes the gap to 10.3k). On the Artix-7 FPGA at 100 MHz, DWCs achieve 2–3 clock-cycle latency and ~2 nJ energy per action, compared to 21–24,000+ cycles and 2.8×10⁻⁵–6.5×10⁻⁸ J for the quantized baseline — a 100×–10,000× reduction in latency and energy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DWCs against two baselines from Kresse & Lampert (2026): full-precision (FP) SAC policies and 2–3-bit quantization-aware-trained (Quant) policies. On return, DWCs match or slightly exceed FP on Ant (5.7k vs 5.6k), Hopper (3.1k vs 2.8k), Humanoid (6.1k vs 6.2k), and Walker2d (5.0k vs 5.0k), but fall short on HalfCheetah (7.5k vs 11.5k), a task the authors identify as capacity-limited. Against the Quant baseline, DWCs are at least as good on four of five tasks and substantially better on Hopper (3.1k vs 1.9k). The most striking advantage is in hardware efficiency: on the same Artix-7 XC7A15T FPGA, DWCs require only 2–3 clock cycles and ~2 nJ per action with zero BRAM/DSP usage, whereas the Quant baseline requires 21–24,000+ cycles, 2.8×10⁻⁵–6.5×10⁻⁸ J per action, and non-trivial BRAM and DSP resources — representing orders-of-magnitude improvements in both latency and energy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Differentiable Weightless Networks (DWNs; Bacellar et al., 2024), which were designed for discrete classification, to the continuous reinforcement learning domain by introducing adaptive thermometer input encoding with running normalization and a popcount-plus-affine continuous output head. It departs from the broader quantized-RL line of work (Kresse & Lampert, 2026; Ivanov et al., 2025) by eliminating all weights entirely—replacing dense multiply-accumulate operations with sparse boolean lookup tables—rather than reducing bit-width. The architecture also generalizes Logic Gate Networks (Petersen et al., 2022) from k=2 gates to k-input LUTs with learnable interconnects at all layers, and positions itself against neuro-symbolic RL approaches (decision-tree distillation, symbolic policy projection) by learning the boolean structure directly via gradient descent rather than post-hoc extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial for embedded robotics: compiling DWCs to an Artix-7 FPGA yields 1–3 clock-cycle latency and ~1–2 nJ per action, versus 21–24,000 cycles and 28 µJ–80 µJ for the best quantized baselines, with zero DSP or BRAM usage in the core. The sparse binary connectivity also offers a degree of interpretability (which sensor dimensions and thresholds drive decisions) that dense networks lack. Key limitations include a significant training-time overhead (up to 7.7× for Humanoid) that currently precludes on-device learning, and a persistent capacity gap on HalfCheetah that only closes at 16k LUTs per layer. Open questions include whether the approach scales to vision-based or high-frequency control tasks, and whether the surrogate-gradient training can be made more efficient to close the train/deploy cost asymmetry.
