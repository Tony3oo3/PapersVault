## Problem

Standard antibody language models treat all residues equivalently, inheriting a germline bias that systematically down-weights the rare non-germline (NGL) substitutions from somatic hypermutation as statistical noise. This causes generated sequences to be structurally plausible but functionally conservative, and all compared ALMs anti-correlate with experimental binding affinity on deep mutational scanning landscapes, failing to capture the very mutations that drive antigen specificity.

## Input / Output

PRISM consumes antibody variable domain sequences (heavy and/or light chains, max 320 tokens) annotated with IMGT region labels and germline V/J gene assignments, tokenized into a 53-token vocabulary where uppercase letters denote germline-conserved residues and lowercase letters denote non-germline variants. Outputs include per-residue probability distributions over the factorized vocabulary, NGL-biased or GL-biased generated sequences (10 mutations per variant), zero-shot binding affinity scores computed as summed NGL log-probabilities, and developability scores computed as GL-manifold perplexity. For physics-based scoring, a WT PDB structure is additionally required to compute Rosetta interface and stability ΔΔG.

## Method

PRISM replaces the standard masked-LM head of an ESM-2 35M backbone (SwiGLU, RoPE, trained from scratch) with an Origin-Conditioned LM Head comprising three components: an Origin Head predicting per-position GL/NGL probability, an AA Head predicting amino acid identity, and an Alpha Head learning a position-specific gate on the evolutionary prior. The final 53-token logit is constructed via multiplicative gating in log-space, combining the biochemical prior from the AA Head with a gated evolutionary constraint from the Origin Head, with a stop-gradient operator preventing the AA Head from delegating NGL prediction to the Origin Head. Training uses a weighted multi-head objective (focal loss with per-token NGL reweighting α=3.0) and region-aware masking (50% CDR, 30% framework, 15% background) on ~58M unpaired OAS sequences for pretraining followed by 620K paired sequences for fine-tuning. The dual-vocabulary design enables property-specific controllable generation: restricting sampling to NGL tokens favors binding affinity, while restricting to GL tokens preserves stability and solubility, without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

PRISM is the only model with positive Spearman ρ on all three DMS binding-affinity landscapes: CR9114 (ρ=+0.391, 4.6× top-10% enrichment), Trastuzumab (ρ=+0.327, 2.6×), and G6.31 (ρ=+0.158), while every baseline is negative on CR9114 (ρ∈[−0.428, −0.326]) and G6.31 (ρ∈[−0.202, −0.018]). On hypervariable CDR3-NGL residues, PRISM achieves a marginalized PPL of 6.15 (heavy chain) versus 24.08 (AntiBERTy), 9.26 (AbLang2), and 25.12 (IgLM). For controllable generation, PRISM-NGL sampling produces the strongest Rosetta interface ΔΔG improvements across all three antibodies, while PRISM-GL sampling dominates on Rosetta stability and CamSol solubility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ESM2-35M/650M (general pLMs), PRISM wins decisively on binding affinity: ESM2-35M yields ρ=−0.428 on CR9114 and −0.202 on G6.31, while PRISM is positive on both; ESM2-650M is the closest competitor on FLAb2 breadth (19.5% win rate vs. PRISM's 41.5%) but still trails on mean rank (4.00+ vs. 3.59). Against AbLang2 (masked ALM with focal-loss reweighting), PRISM outperforms on CDR3-NGL PPL (6.15 vs. 9.26) and is the only model positive on CR9114 (AbLang2 ρ=−0.326); AbLang2 also shows negative Self-Interaction correlation (ρ=−0.124) where PRISM is positive. Against AntiBERTy, PRISM edges out on Trastuzumab (ρ=+0.327 vs. +0.297) and dominates on CDR3-NGL PPL (6.15 vs. 24.08), though AntiBERTy is competitive on developability (Immunogenicity ρ=0.316 vs. PRISM 0.310). Against Sapiens, PRISM wins on G6.31 (ρ=+0.158 vs. −0.018) and all PPL metrics. Against IgLM (autoregressive, explicitly optimized for antibody generation), PRISM achieves 4× lower CDR3-NGL PPL (6.15 vs. 25.12) and is the only positive model on G6.31 (IgLM ρ=−0.065); IgLM's generative focus does not translate to binding-affinity prediction. The ablation (PRISM-less: ESM2-35M fine-tuned with simple head) confirms that architectural factorization—not scale or initialization—drives the affinity-prediction gap (PRISM-less ρ=−0.409 on CR9114 vs. PRISM Full +0.393).

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM builds directly on the ESM-2 transformer backbone (Lin et al., 2023) and adopts AbLang2's (Olsen et al., 2024) focal-loss reweighting strategy for handling germline imbalance, but departs fundamentally at the representational level: rather than treating germline bias as an optimization problem solvable by loss reweighting, PRISM encodes evolutionary origin (GL vs. NGL) as a structural axis in a factorized 53-token vocabulary with an Origin-Conditioned LM Head. This positions it beyond the masked-ALM lineage (AbLang2, AntiBERTy, Sapiens) and the autoregressive IgLM, which all operate over a flat 20-amino-acid vocabulary and thus cannot separate conservation from functional variation in their output distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that every compared ALM—including antibody-specialized models—systematically anti-correlates with experimental binding affinity, while PRISM is the sole positive correlate across all three DMS landscapes, reframes germline bias from a minor calibration issue to a fundamental representational failure. The dual-channel controllable generation (NGL sampling for affinity, GL sampling for developability) opens a practical design workflow previously unattainable with entangled models. Key open questions include whether the factorization principle generalizes to other hierarchically generated sequences (e.g., TCRs, catalytic enzymes), whether zero-shot developability can be improved via the planned RL approach, and whether the modest absolute correlations (ρ ≈ 0.16–0.39) are sufficient for real-world screening at scale.
