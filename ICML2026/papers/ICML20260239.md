## Problem

Standard transfer learning assumes that final-layer representations are optimal for downstream tasks, but empirical evidence shows task-discriminative information is distributed non-monotonically across encoder depth, with intermediate layers frequently outperforming the final layer by up to 10%. No principled method existed to identify which specific layers encode complementary task-relevant structure and how their geometric properties (isotropy, separability) affect downstream performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full hierarchy of L layer-wise embeddings from a pretrained encoder (vision, language, or speech) along with a small calibration set (20% of training data, as few as 64 samples) with task labels. It outputs a fused representation from k=3–4 selected layers, passed through lightweight adapters (LN + linear + GELU, projected to 256-dim each) and a task-specific head, yielding class labels, regression values, or pixel-level segmentation masks. The selection itself is a one-time closed-form computation independent of training epochs.

## Method

LOES formulates layer selection as a greedy spectral-ridge optimization: at each step, candidate layers are orthogonalized against the span of already-selected layers, then scored by a composite objective combining marginal residual loss (ridge regression on the residual), an isotropy penalty (ratio of mean eigenvalue to eigenvalue variance of the layer covariance), a redundancy term (normalized Frobenius inner product with selected layers), and a class-geometry term (expected triangle area among class centroids on orthogonalized features). The selected layers are fused via lightweight adapters and a linear probe. GeoReg complements this with an auxiliary fine-tuning loss that penalizes spectral imbalance (variance of covariance eigenvalues) and low centroid-volume configurations in the fused representation, preventing the representation collapse that degrades accuracy when the encoder is trainable. The scoring uses orthogonalized features against the current residual, while the final refit uses raw features against original targets, ensuring the downstream probe operates on unmodified encoder outputs at inference.

## Results

On vision benchmarks (Table 3), LOES with k=3 consistently outperforms last-layer transfer across all six encoder architectures, with the largest gains on MAE-B/16 (e.g., Stanford Cars: 7.42% → 15.45%, CUB-200: 9.78% → 20.39%) and DINOv2-S (Stanford Cars: 48.61% → 60.86%). On language benchmarks (Table 5), LOES-4 on ModernBERT-B achieves 94.48% on MTOP (vs. 78.07% last-layer) and 78.51% on AM-Scenario (vs. 62.84%), while on BERT-L it reaches 97.81% on MTOP. For speech, Wav2Vec 2.0 with LOES improves ASVspoof from 90.89% to 98.80% and CREMA-D from 37.84% to 69.06%. Gains scale with model depth: ModernBERT (22L) benefits far more (+17.7% avg.) than BERT-base (12L, +2.5% avg.).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOES-4 outperforms the Entropy-4 and Curvature-4 baselines from Skean et al. (2025) on all seven MTEB datasets for ModernBERT-B, with the largest margin on AM-Scenario (78.51% vs. 74.55% and 61.43%) and MTOP (94.48% vs. 91.91% and 76.68%). Against the Intrinsic-4 baseline from Cheng et al. (2025), LOES-4 wins on 6 of 7 datasets for ModernBERT-B (e.g., MTOP: 94.48% vs. 90.36%) but is narrowly outperformed on BERT-L MTOP (97.81% vs. 98.29%) and AM-Scenario (87.46% vs. 85.27% — actually LOES wins here; Intrinsic-4 wins only on BERT-L MTOP). For the VICReg comparison (Bardes et al., 2022), GeoReg achieves 31.04% on TweetEval-Emoji with trainable BERT-base, edging out the VICReg-adapted baseline (30.69%) and the last-layer baseline (30.15%), while also showing stronger resistance to late-training representation collapse. LOES also outperforms a stronger learned-fusion baseline that applies per-layer linear projections across all 22 ModernBERT layers, reaching 94.19 on MTOP with 1.7× fewer parameters and FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the growing body of evidence that intermediate layers in deep encoders often outperform final-layer representations (Alain & Bengio, 2017; Skean et al., 2025; Park et al., 2025), but departs from prior layer-selection approaches that rely on learnable scalar weights, fixed concatenation, or information-theoretic metrics (entropy, curvature, intrinsic dimensionality). LOES introduces a constructive spectral framework—combining closed-form ridge regression, orthogonalization, and geometric diagnostics (isotropy, redundancy, centroid simplex volume)—to select task-discriminative subspaces, while GeoReg adapts VICReg-style variance-covariance regularization from the pretraining stage to the fine-tuning of fused multi-layer embeddings. The work also connects to the 'compression valley' literature in autoregressive LLMs and the observation that pretraining data diversity shapes representational geometry across depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As foundation models scale and their pretraining corpora grow more diverse, the default assumption that the final layer is task-optimal becomes increasingly unreliable, making principled layer selection a practically important problem. The cross-lingual finding that underrepresented languages (Hindi, Arabic, Urdu) benefit disproportionately from LOES suggests a concrete pathway for improving low-resource transfer without additional data. Open questions include whether the greedy selection strategy can be made globally optimal for very deep encoders, how the method behaves under strong distribution shift between calibration and deployment, and whether the isotropy objective remains beneficial for tasks with strong structured priors where anisotropic directions carry task signal.
