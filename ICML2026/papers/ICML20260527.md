## Problem

Reasoning LLMs (R-LLMs) trained with RL for math and coding hallucinate substantially more than their non-reasoning counterparts on long-form factuality benchmarks, yet extending online RL to the long-form factuality setting is blocked by the absence of reliable, fast verification methods. Naively using automatic evaluators like FActScore or VeriScore as the RL reward leads to multiple reward-hacking modes: the model produces shorter, less detailed responses to inflate precision, or generates factually correct but entirely irrelevant content.

## Input / Output

The model receives a fact-seeking question (e.g., 'Tell me a bio of [PERSON]') and produces a structured output consisting of a Long Chain-of-Thought reasoning trace enclosed in <think> tags followed by a final factual response in <answer> tags. Training prompts are 7k synthetic fact-seeking questions generated by Llama 4, grounded on diverse WildChat prompts and LongFact examples to ensure real-world diversity while requiring factual knowledge. The base model is Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and evaluation uses the original (non-scalable) VeriScore for consistency with prior literature.

## Method

The paper proposes a three-component reward function for online RL via GRPO that simultaneously optimizes factual precision (F/(T+1) from a scalable VeriScore implementation), response detail level (log(1+F) to reward more factual claims without unbounded growth), and answer relevance (a binary LLM-as-a-judge signal comparing the rollout against the base model's own response). Each component targets a specific reward-hacking mode: precision alone incentivizes shorter answers, adding detail without relevance allows tangential but factual content, and the relevance guardrail prevents the model from reciting irrelevant Wikipedia articles. The scalable VeriScore implementation achieves a 30× speedup (from ~2 minutes to <5 seconds per response) by parallelizing claim extraction and verification via batched Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct workers on a Matrix cluster, plus async Google Search API calls. The full training pipeline is SFT (to instill the Long CoT format) followed by online GRPO with 4 rollouts per prompt, and ablations show that the fact+relevance combination (λ=0, μ=0.1) achieves 23.1-point precision gains with >50% AlpacaEval win rate, while adding the detail term (λ=0.01) trades ~1 point of precision for 13% more factual claims.

## Results

On six long-form factuality benchmarks, the proposed SFT+GRPO model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct base) achieves an average factual precision of 68.1%, a 23.1-point improvement over the base model (45.0%), while increasing the detail level (number of supported claims) by 23% (29.0 vs. 23.5). The model maintains a 54.4% AlpacaEval win rate over the base model, indicating no degradation in response helpfulness. In contrast, existing reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1, QwQ-32B) show 10–13 percentage points *lower* precision than their non-reasoning counterparts on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base model Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (45.0% precision, 23.5 detail), SFT+GRPO improves precision by 23.1 points and detail by 23%, while maintaining a >50% win rate (54.4%). Compared to the offline DPO baseline (SFT+DPO: 67.8% precision, 25.7 detail, 37.8% WR), the online GRPO method matches precision but substantially outperforms on detail (+3.3) and win rate (+16.6 points), showing that on-policy optimization avoids the quality degradation seen with offline preference learning. Against existing reasoning models, SFT+GRPO (68.1% precision) surpasses DeepSeek-R1 (44.7%) by 23.4 points and QwQ-32B (38.3%) by 29.8 points in precision, while also exceeding both in detail level. Notably, both DeepSeek-R1 and QwQ-32B exhibit *lower* precision than their non-reasoning counterparts (DeepSeek-V3: 55.0%, Qwen 2.5-32B: 51.4%), confirming that current R-LLMs exacerbate hallucination in long-form settings. The SFT-only baseline (55.9% precision) improves over the base model but at the cost of a 25% reduction in detail level, highlighting that precision gains alone are insufficient without the multi-component reward.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the factuality alignment literature (Tian et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024) from offline RL (DPO with FActScore-curated preference pairs) to online RL (GRPO) for long-form factuality, which is the first such recipe. It builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and the Dr. GRPO modified objective (Liu et al., 2025) as the optimization backbone, and adapts VeriScore (Song et al., 2024) into a 30x faster scalable implementation suitable for real-time reward computation during rollouts. The key conceptual departure from prior R-LLM training (DeepSeek-R1, QwQ) is that those pipelines optimize verifiable rewards for math/code and overlook factuality entirely, whereas this paper designs a multi-component reward (precision + detail + relevance) specifically to counter the reward hacking modes unique to long-form factual generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that popular R-LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, QwQ-32B) hallucinate 10–13 percentage points more than their non-reasoning counterparts on long-form benchmarks is a practical warning as these models are deployed for higher-stakes tasks. The paper's characterization of reward hacking modes (shorter responses, irrelevant-but-factual content) and the ablation showing that the relevance reward is the critical guardrail (not the detail reward) provide actionable design guidance for future factuality RL. Limitations include evaluation on a single 8B base model, reliance on automatic evaluators without human validation, and the acknowledged risk of overfitting to VeriScore's specific verification stack. The authors identify agentic factual reasoning (model with search tool access) as a natural next step where the online RL framework could unlock new reasoning strategies.
