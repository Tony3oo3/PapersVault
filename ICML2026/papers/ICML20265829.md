## Problem

Existing time series representations face a dual limitation: discrete-time models cannot handle irregular sampling or avoid the fidelity-efficiency tradeoff imposed by the Nyquist-Shannon theorem, while implicit neural representations (INRs) suffer from spectral bias and frequency entanglement that cause smooth trends and transient events to interfere within a single shared field. No prior method explicitly separates these two temporal phenomena in a continuous-time framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265829/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a multivariate time series X = {x(t_i)} with D variables at N timestamps (normalized to [0,1]), which may be irregularly sampled. It learns a continuous generative function x̂(t) = Φ_CTF(t) + Ψ_DGF(t) that can be queried at arbitrary timestamps t* without requiring ground-truth values at query time, using only a stored context representation h̄ from training observations. The output is explicitly decomposed into a smooth trend component and a sparse event component, each individually interpretable (e.g., each active Gabor atom reports its temporal location, duration, and oscillation frequency).

## Method

DualTimesField decomposes the signal into two parallel implicit neural fields trained jointly. The Continuous Time Field (CTF) uses bandwidth-limited learnable Fourier features (f_k = σ(b_k)·f_max) to enforce an explicit spectral cutoff, combined with a frequency-domain low-pass filter and first-order smoothness regularization, ensuring it captures only low-frequency trends. The Discrete Geometric Field (DGF) models the residual as a sparse superposition of M learnable Gabor atoms (modulated Gaussian wavelets), with sample-specific amplitudes predicted from a context vector and binary-like gating (temperature-sharpened sigmoid) for selective atom activation. Training employs coarse-to-fine scale annealing (cosine schedule progressively narrowing atom widths over 30% warmup) to stabilize optimization of localized basis functions, along with a sparsity penalty on mean gate activation and a DGF residual loss that prevents the two fields from competing for the same signal components.

## Results

On nine long-horizon reconstruction benchmarks, DualTimesField achieves the best MSE on all nine datasets and the best MAE on eight, reducing average MSE by 51.2% and MAE by 31.7% over TimesNet (the strongest deep-learning baseline). The largest gains appear on ETTm2 (96.9% MSE reduction, 0.0075 vs. 0.2424), Weather (75.0%, 0.0110 vs. 0.0440), and ETTh2 (59.5%, 0.0395 vs. 0.0975). On six irregularly sampled interpolation benchmarks, DualTimesField attains the lowest MSE on five of six, with margins exceeding 90% over the second-best method on EPA-Air (0.15×10⁻³ vs. 2.17×10⁻³) and Human Activity (0.19×10⁻³ vs. 4.24×10⁻³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualTimesField outperforms all three categories of reconstruction baselines: INR methods (SIREN, WIRE, Fourier features) cluster at MSE 0.84–1.07 due to spectral bias; deep time-series models (N-BEATS, PatchTST, iTransformer, TimesNet) achieve moderate improvements but still trail DualTimesField by 11–97% in MSE; and PCA, while competitive on some datasets (e.g., ETTm1 at 0.3115), cannot match the dual-field approach on any benchmark. The sole exception is Exchange MAE, where iTransformer (0.1325) narrowly beats DualTimesField (0.1415), though DualTimesField retains the best MSE on that dataset. For irregular-sampling interpolation, DualTimesField dominates five of six benchmarks, beating the strongest baselines LS4 and CRU by 52.4% on PhysioNet (0.30 vs. 0.63 ×10⁻³) and by over 90% on EPA-Air and Human Activity. The one loss is USHCN, where LS4 (0.06×10⁻³) and CRU (0.16×10⁻³) outperform DualTimesField (0.17×10⁻³), which the authors attribute to USHCN's smooth seasonal patterns offering little benefit from explicit event modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

DualTimesField extends the implicit neural representation (INR) paradigm for time series—pioneered by HyperTime (Fons et al., 2022) and built on Fourier feature encodings (Tancik et al., 2020)—by replacing the single holistic field with two structurally separated sub-fields: a bandwidth-limited Continuous Time Field for smooth trends and a sparse Discrete Geometric Field using learnable Gabor atoms (Gabor, 1946) for transient events. This departs from prior INR approaches (SIREN, WIRE, Fourier features) that attempt to capture all frequency content within shared parameters, and from classical decomposition methods (N-BEATS, Autoformer) that operate only on discrete sequences. The key conceptual move is making the trend-event separation an architectural constraint (explicit spectral cutoffs, gated sparsity) rather than an emergent property of training, drawing on time-frequency analysis and sparse coding as inductive biases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 51.2% average MSE reduction over the strongest deep baseline (TimesNet) and near-order-of-magnitude gains over INR baselines demonstrate that explicit frequency separation is a substantially more effective inductive bias than letting a single network implicitly disentangle components. The ECG5000 interpretability analysis—where unsupervised decomposition recovers clinically meaningful event patterns per arrhythmia class—suggests the framework could serve as a diagnostic tool beyond pure reconstruction. However, the USHCN result (where smooth state-space models LS4/CRU outperform DualTimesField) honestly delineates the method's sweet spot: signals with rich event structure on smooth baselines. Open questions include whether the per-sample optimization required for interpolation scales to streaming settings, whether hierarchical multi-scale extensions can capture nested dynamics, and how learned decompositions transfer to downstream tasks like forecasting or anomaly detection.
