## Problem

Existing beyond-worst-case analyses of k-means rely on assumptions about the optimal clustering (separability, approximation stability) that are computationally intractable to verify on real data. This paper asks whether the manifold hypothesis—a geometric regularity assumption on the input distribution that is empirically well-supported and easily checkable—can serve as a practical structural model, and whether the predictable scaling laws it induces can be exploited to design faster seeding algorithms with provable guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a dataset of n points in R^D, a target number of clusters k, an LSH approximation parameter ρ < 1, and an iteration cap m ∈ N. After a one-time O(nD log k) preprocessing step (JL dimensionality reduction, centering, and construction of a binary-tree sampler for the ℓ²-norm distribution), each seeding run produces k cluster centers in Õ(ρ⁻¹k^{1+γ}) time where γ = ε + ρ and ε = 2/d is the quantization exponent. The output centers achieve an O(ρ⁻² log k) approximation to the optimal k-means cost, with an additional exponentially decaying additive term in opt₁(X) that becomes negligible for m = O(β_k(X)).

## Method

Qkmeans simulates the D² sampling step of k-means++ via rejection sampling from a lightweight ℓ²-norm-based proposal distribution κ(·|C) = (‖x‖² + ‖c₁‖²)/(‖X‖²_F + n‖c₁‖²), whose max Rényi divergence from the true D² distribution is bounded by log β_k(X) = O(log k / d) under the manifold hypothesis. To avoid the O(k²D) cost of exact nearest-center queries during rejection, the algorithm maintains an LSH-based approximate nearest neighbor data structure that returns a center within a 1/√ρ factor of the true nearest in O(D log η (t log η)^ρ) time. The finite iteration cap m and LSH approximation introduce a perturbation modeled as a (ρ,δ)-perturbed D² distribution, for which the paper proves a potential-based analysis showing the approximation guarantee degrades gracefully to O(ρ⁻² log k)·opt_k(X) + δ·O(k + ρ⁻² log k)·opt₁(X). Under the manifold hypothesis, the data-dependent parameters β_k and η follow predictable power-law scaling (β_k ~ k^ε, log η ~ ε log k), yielding the final runtime of Õ(ρ⁻¹k^{1+γ}) with γ = ε + ρ, which empirically gives 5–10× speedups over prior methods on high-dimensional datasets.

## Results

Under the manifold hypothesis, Qkmeans achieves an O(ϱ⁻² log k) approximation guarantee with a one-time preprocessing cost of O(nD log k) and per-seeding time Õ(ϱ⁻¹ k^(1+γ)), where γ = ε + ϱ and ε = 2/d is the quantization exponent. Empirically, Qkmeans delivers consistent 5–10× speedups over AFKMC² and PRONECoreset, and up to two orders of magnitude (≈100×) speedups over RejectionSampling, while maintaining comparable seeding cost. The predicted scaling laws for β_k(X) and η(X) are validated across 12 datasets with linear fits in log-log coordinates achieving R² ≥ 0.95, and the estimated quantization exponent ε̂ is inversely proportional to independently estimated intrinsic dimension d_MLE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qkmeans outperforms all six baselines on both runtime and (in several cases) approximation quality. Against AFKMC² (Bachem et al., 2016a) and FastKMeans (Draganov et al., 2024), which both have Ω(k²) dependence, Qkmeans achieves a near-linear k^(1+γ) dependence (γ ≈ 0.1–0.2 + ϱ in practice), yielding 5–10× speedups as k grows. Against RejectionSampling (Cohen-Addad et al., 2020a), Qkmeans improves the approximation factor from O(ϱ⁻³ log k) to O(ϱ⁻² log k), reduces the ambient dimension dependence from O(D³) to O(D), and eliminates the need for multi-tree metric embeddings, resulting in up to 100× faster seeding. Against PRONECoreset (Charikar et al., 2023), which has O(k⁵) dependence in its coreset variant, Qkmeans is substantially faster while maintaining the same O(log k) approximation order. Against Shah & Jaiswal (2025), Qkmeans removes the Δ² (aspect-ratio-squared) factor that can be unbounded, replacing it with the data-dependent β_k(X) which scales as k^ε under the manifold hypothesis. The only tradeoff is that Qkmeans introduces a ϱ⁻² constant factor in the approximation guarantee (vs. a constant 1 in some baselines), but this is a tunable parameter that can be set close to 1 for near-optimal quality at modest runtime cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on k-means++ (Arthur & Vassilvitskii, 2007) as the base seeding procedure, the LSH-based ANNS data structure of Cohen-Addad et al. (2020) for approximate nearest-center queries, and the ℓ²-norm proposal distribution from quantum-inspired d²-sampling (Shah & Jaiswal, 2025; Cornelissen et al., 2025). Its key conceptual departure is adopting the manifold hypothesis—already the implicit structural model in deep learning—as the beyond-worst-case assumption for k-means, replacing the harder-to-verify separability and approximation-stability conditions used in prior work. By importing Zador's and Gruber's optimum quantization theory, the paper derives finite-sample scaling laws for data-dependent parameters (β_k, η) that are empirically checkable, then exploits these regularities to design a rejection-sampling seeding algorithm with O(k^{1+γ}) runtime where γ = ε + ρ is small in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The manifold hypothesis provides a structurally verifiable assumption for beyond-worst-case k-means analysis, unlike stability or separability conditions that constrain the (computationally intractable) optimal solution. The empirical validation across 12 diverse datasets (images, CLIP/MiniLM embeddings, tabular sensor data) showing R² ≥ 0.95 fits to the predicted power-law scaling is a strong bridge between quantization theory and practice. Open questions include how the framework handles noisy or non-smooth manifolds, whether the approach extends to other clustering objectives (k-median, k-median), and whether the intrinsic dimension estimates remain stable under distribution shift. For practitioners, the 5–10× seeding speedup over prior methods is meaningful for large-k regimes but k-means seeding is rarely the dominant cost in end-to-end pipelines.
