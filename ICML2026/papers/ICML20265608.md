## Problem

Width expansion during mid-stage LLM pre-training is essential for practical compute savings but remains largely unexplored due to severe training instabilities. The paper identifies two core obstacles: naive initialization disrupts activation RMS statistics causing loss spikes, while copy-based initialization (the natural choice for function preservation) induces a backward gradient symmetry that locks duplicated parameters into identical evolution, rendering the expanded capacity functionally redundant.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially-trained small model at a mid-training checkpoint (e.g., 100B of 200B tokens), a width expansion specification identifying which dimensions to grow (expert intermediate dim, hidden dim, or both) and by what factor (e.g., 2×), and continues training under the same token budget. It produces an expanded wider model whose downstream performance is evaluated on 12 standard benchmarks. A key structural detail is that width expansion always involves paired fan-out/fan-in transformations across consecutive layers (e.g., up/gate projections expand output dim while the down projection expands input dim), requiring joint rescaling of both sides.

## Method

SPARKLING balances two complementary principles. For signal preservation, it derives closed-form RMS-preserving rescaling factors for fan-out, fan-in (with separate formulas for random/one-side-copied vs. both-sides-copied regimes), and RMSNorm weight expansion, ensuring activation statistics remain stable at the expansion moment. For symmetry breaking, it applies two targeted backward-dynamics interventions that leave the copied forward function intact: (i) an asymmetric optimizer state reset that zeroes momentum only for newly introduced parameters while retaining the original states, and (ii) an asymmetric learning-rate re-warmup that briefly boosts the new parameters' LR to ρ·η_e (ρ≈1.3) over ~250 steps before resuming cosine decay, while original parameters continue on the unchanged baseline schedule. The paper proves the symmetry lock persists under both AdamW and Muon (including Newton-Schulz orthogonalization), and shows that the two principles yield additive, complementary gains across dense and MoE architectures, multiple width axes, and optimizer families, saving up to 35% FLOPs under 2× expansion.

## Results

Under 2× mid-stage width expansion at 100B tokens (OLMoE, 200B total tokens), SPARKLING achieves the best downstream average across all three expansion axes: 60.30 (Inner 2×), 61.13 (Hidden 2×), and 62.55 (Hidden 2× & Inner 2×), outperforming the from-scratch expanded baseline (59.64, 60.46, 60.89) despite a slightly higher pre-training loss. The largest gains appear on reasoning-heavy tasks: Arithmetic improves from 55.67 to 60.50 (Inner 2×) and 55.03 to 66.00 (joint), while MMLU rises from 32.67 to 34.05 and Winogrande from 64.80 to 65.04. In terms of compute efficiency, SPARKLING saves 20–35% training FLOPs relative to from-scratch training and achieves up to 1.49× wall-clock speed-up under the joint 2× expansion setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPARKLING outperforms all eight compared baselines on both final pre-training loss and downstream average under the 2× expert-inner expansion setting (Table 7). Against the uneven-splitting heuristics from Net2Net (Chen 2016), bert2BERT (Chen 2022), and Stacking Your Transformers (Du 2024), SPARKLING achieves loss 2.3153 vs. 2.3180 (Uneven-1:2) and 2.3234 (Uneven-r:(1-r)), with downstream averages of 60.30 vs. 59.75 and 58.34 respectively. The symmetric ±Perturbation strategy from Splitting Steepest Descent (Wu 2019) and Firefly (Wu 2020) yields loss 2.3256 and average 58.93, trailing SPARKLING by 0.01 in loss and 1.37 points in average. Among dynamics-based baselines, LEMON's accelerated-decay schedule (Wang 2024) performs worst (loss 2.3516, avg 58.00 at 50% faster decay), while Staged Training's optimizer-state scaling + LR remapping (Shen 2022) achieves loss 2.3426 and avg 59.06, and Yuan et al.'s (2023) weight-norm-adapted LR reaches loss 2.3253 and avg 59.42. SPARKLING's advantage is most pronounced on reasoning tasks (Arithmetic: 60.50 vs. 57.17 for Uneven-1:2; MMLU: 34.05 vs. 33.69), consistent with its symmetry-breaking mechanism enabling the expanded capacity to learn diverse features rather than redundant copies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the progressive learning (PL) literature for LLMs, which has been dominated by depth-expansion strategies (layer stacking, block insertion) and early-stage width expansion (Net2Net, BERT2BERT, AKI variants). The key departure is twofold: (1) it targets mid-stage width expansion (at 50% of training tokens), a regime prior work largely avoided due to instability, and (2) it reframes the expansion problem as balancing RMS-scale signal preservation with backward symmetry breaking, rather than the previously dominant function-preservation (loss-continuity) criterion. Prior symmetry-breaking heuristics—uneven splitting and ±perturbation—address the forward pass only, while this paper introduces targeted backward interventions (asymmetric optimizer state reset and asymmetric LR re-warmup) that leave the copied forward function intact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20–35% FLOPs reduction with matching or exceeding downstream accuracy under 2× width expansion makes mid-stage width expansion practically viable for pre-training budgets, and the clean two-principle framework (RMS preservation + symmetry breaking) gives practitioners a systematic recipe rather than ad-hoc heuristics. The symmetry-lock analysis showing that even Muon's Newton-Schulz orthogonalization cannot spontaneously break copy-induced coupling is a useful theoretical result for anyone designing optimizers for growing architectures. Open questions include whether the framework extends to simultaneous width-and-depth expansion, whether RMS preservation satisfies the μP transferability condition, and whether the gains persist at much larger scales (10B+ active parameters) where the tested 0.5–1.5B models may not be representative.
