## Problem

Real-world RF fingerprint identification must handle continuously arriving unregistered devices while privacy constraints forbid storing historical signal data, yet existing methods either assume a closed set of known transmitters, require source-data replay to prevent catastrophic forgetting, or rely on active supervision to distinguish among new classes. This paper formalizes the Source-Free Open-World (SF-OW) RFFI setting, where a pre-trained model must adapt to an unlabeled mixed stream of old and new devices without any source data, exposing a severe stability-plasticity dilemma: similarity-driven self-organization causes new-class confusion while unsupervised adaptation drifts old-class representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw RF IQ segments (lengths 256–8192 samples depending on protocol) converted to channel-invariant log-power spectrograms via STFT with adjacent-frame ratioing, yielding 3-channel inputs of 32×32 (WiSig, Oracle) or 102×62 (LoRa). During the target adaptation stage, only unlabeled signals from a mixed old/new stream are available; the number of new classes K_new is assumed known but no labels or source samples are provided. The output is a unified class prediction over Y_total = Y_old ∪ Y_new produced by a fixed classifier head M = [M_old, M_new], where new-class identities are discovered via Hungarian-matched cluster assignment.

## Method

The core insight is shifting anti-forgetting from parameter-space regularization to output-space geometry design: a fixed classifier head simultaneously governs worst-case decision-boundary separability and constrains gradient entry directions during back-propagation. The IO-ETF framework freezes old anchors and constructs new anchors via a repulsive-potential optimization on the unit hypersphere that enforces intra-block low coherence (maximizing new-class margins) and inter-block orthogonality (routing new-task gradients into the null space of old anchors, creating a structural 'gradient firewall'). To stably map unlabeled samples onto this rigid geometric skeleton, TLGA operates at three levels: (1) Balanced Entropic Optimal Transport replaces greedy pseudo-labeling with capacity-constrained global matching to prevent assignment collapse; (2) Progressive Manifold Anchoring blends local contrastive cohesion with global cross-entropy traction to IO-ETF slots via a soft-to-hard evolutionary schedule; and (3) Reliability-Aware Pseudo-Replay mines high-confidence old-class samples from the frozen teacher to anchor old subspaces against self-drift. The backbone is a ResNet-18 (d=512) trained with Adam, cosine LR decay, and an EMA teacher for stable pseudo-label generation.

## Results

The proposed IO-ETF + TLGA framework achieves 99.20% old-class accuracy and 95.15% new-class accuracy overall, yielding the highest H-score of 97.05% (+6.27% over the best baseline, +7.72% over the mean of all baselines). On Oracle, performance is near-perfect (100.00% old, 99.69% new, 99.84% H-score, 99.97% AUROC). On the most challenging LoRa dataset (25 classes, 20:5 split), the method achieves 97.62% old, 85.85% new, and 91.36% H-score, outperforming all baselines on the stability-plasticity trade-off metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper outperforms all nine baselines on the overall H-score (97.05% vs. best baseline 90.78% for ST+LwF), with the largest margin on Oracle where it achieves 99.84% H-score versus 90.28% for the closest competitor SF-OpenLDN (+9.56%). CL baselines (LwF, EWC, MAS, GPM) suffer severe catastrophic forgetting in the source-free regime, with Oracle old accuracy collapsing to ~72% (vs. 100% for the proposed method), because their parameter-regularization mechanisms require source data or accurate old-class inputs that are unavailable. Among OW baselines, SF-ORCA achieves the highest new-class accuracy on LoRa (87.75% vs. 85.85%) but at the cost of lower old accuracy (94.07% vs. 97.62%), yielding a lower H-score (90.80% vs. 91.36%); SF-OpenLDN leads on Oracle new accuracy (82.30%) but the proposed method nearly doubles this to 99.69%. SHOT and SF-GCD show the weakest overall performance (H-scores 87.00% and 89.33%), with SHOT particularly failing on new-class discovery (74.00% on LoRa) due to new samples being absorbed into old-class regions. The proposed method's consistent advantage stems from its geometric gradient isolation (preventing forgetting) combined with maximal-separation anchor design (preventing new-class confusion), achieving near-perfect old accuracy (≥97.62%) while maintaining strong new-class discovery across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes neural collapse (Papyan et al., 2020) from a passive training byproduct into an active output-geometric prior, constructing an Incremental Orthogonal ETF (IO-ETF) classifier head that simultaneously maximizes worst-case decision margins and enforces inter-block gradient isolation. The IO-ETF construction draws on Generalized Neural Collapse (Liu et al., 2023b) for its repulsive potential energy formulation on the unit hypersphere, while the Triple-Level Geometric Alignment (TLGA) mechanism borrows capacity-constrained slot activation from SwAV (Caron et al., 2020) and Fini et al. (2021) via Balanced Entropic Optimal Transport. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant source-free anti-forgetting paradigm (parameter regularization à la EWC/MAS, gradient projection à la GPM) by shifting the control point from input-side covariance statistics to a prescribed output-space geometry, eliminating dependency on source data entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formalization of SF-OW RFFI as a distinct problem (source-free + multi-new discovery + physical-layer authentication) addresses a practical gap in wireless security where privacy constraints preclude storing RF fingerprints. The geometric gradient isolation result (Prop. 3.3) provides a clean, data-free mechanism for anti-forgetting that could potentially transfer to other source-free open-world tasks beyond RFFI. Key limitations include the assumption that K_new is known a priori, reliance on a single ResNet-18 backbone, and the NC-consistent non-mixing assumption (A.1) for layer-wise propagation which is not guaranteed under unsupervised adaptation. Open questions include scalability to very large class counts, generalization to other signal modalities, and whether the geometric prior can be learned rather than analytically constructed.
