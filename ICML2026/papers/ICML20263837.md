## Problem

State-of-the-art scaling-based OOD detection methods (ASH, SCALE, LTS) compute sample-specific scaling factors but apply a static percentile threshold uniformly across all inputs, regardless of whether a sample is likely ID or OOD. This rigidity limits optimal ID-OOD separability because the scaling mechanism cannot differentiate between samples that need stronger or weaker scaling. The paper asks whether making the percentile threshold adaptive to each sample's estimated OODness can yield more separable energy scores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263837/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a small set of ID validation samples (as few as 10) used to construct an empirical CDF for calibrating the OODness estimate. At inference, it requires two forward passes (original and a minimally perturbed input where only o% of low-attribution pixels are shifted by ε=0.5) to compute activation shifts. The output is a scalar energy score whose magnitude indicates ID-likelihood; a threshold τ then yields a binary ID/OOD decision. No retraining or architectural modification is needed, and ID classification accuracy is preserved.

## Method

AdaSCALE's key observation is that OOD samples exhibit significantly larger shifts in their top-k activations under minor pixel perturbation compared to ID samples. It quantifies this via Q (sum of absolute activation shifts at the top-k₁ highest activations) and a balancing correction term C_o (sum of top-k₂ perturbed activations), combined as Q′ = λ·Q + C_o to prevent overconfident scaling. An empirical CDF built from Q′ values on a few ID validation samples converts Q′ into a normalized OOD probability, which linearly maps to an adaptive percentile threshold p ∈ [p_min, p_max]. This adaptive p then determines the scaling factor r = Σa_j / Σ(a_j > P_p(a)), which is applied either to activations (AdaSCALE-A: a·exp(r)) or logits (AdaSCALE-L: z·r²) before computing the energy score. The approach is entirely post-hoc, requires only one tunable hyperparameter (p_max) for strong generalization across architectures, and outperforms OptFS by 14.94%/21.28% in FPR@95 on near/far-OOD across eight ImageNet-1k architectures.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k across 8 architectures, AdaSCALE-A achieves an average FPR@95/AUROC of 61.17/80.50 (near-OOD) and 30.11/92.73 (far-OOD), surpassing the best-generalizing method OptFS by 14.94% and 21.28% in FPR@95 respectively. On ResNet-50 specifically, AdaSCALE-A reaches 58.97/78.98 (near-OOD) and 17.84/96.14 (far-OOD), outperforming the best-performing method SCALE by 12.96%/6.44% in FPR@95/AUROC for near-OOD. On CIFAR benchmarks (WRN-28-10, DenseNet-101), AdaSCALE-A achieves the best average AUROC across all four near/far-OOD settings, with FPR@95 of 43.07 (CIFAR-10 near-OOD) and 33.11 (CIFAR-10 far-OOD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaSCALE-A decisively outperforms OptFS (the best-generalizing method) by 14.94% FPR@95 on near-OOD and 21.28% on far-OOD averaged across 8 ImageNet-1k architectures, and also beats the best-performing method SCALE by 12.96% FPR@95 on ResNet-50 near-OOD. Against logit-based baselines (MSP, MLS, EBO), AdaSCALE-A improves FPR@95 by 13–18 points on near-OOD and 24–37 points on far-OOD (ResNet-50). Among activation-shaping methods, AdaSCALE-A surpasses ASH by 10.49% FPR@95 (near-OOD, ResNet-50) and ReAct by 13.63%, while also outperforming BFAct and LTS by 13.37% and 9.03% respectively. On CIFAR benchmarks, AdaSCALE-A achieves the best AUROC in all four settings but MSP remains competitive on FPR@95 for CIFAR-10 near-OOD (43.18 vs. 43.07). In full-spectrum OOD detection, AdaSCALE-A outperforms OptFS by 4.49 and 3.45 FPR@95 points on near- and far-OOD respectively, and significantly outperforms distance-based methods (LINE, NNGuide, MDS) by 10–20 FPR@95 points on ImageNet-1k.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaSCALE extends the ASH → SCALE → LTS lineage of activation-shaping post-hoc OOD detection, which computes a sample-specific scaling factor from the ratio of total activation sum to the sum of top-percentile activations. The key departure is replacing the static percentile threshold (fixed across all samples) with a dynamic, sample-adaptive one driven by an estimated OODness score. This OODness is computed from activation shifts at top-k activations under minor input perturbation, a signal the authors connect to but distinguish from ODIN's full-image perturbation for confidence boosting. The method also draws on ReAct's observation of abnormally high OOD activations, extending it to show that the *positions* of such activations are unstable under perturbation for OOD inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdaSCALE delivers a substantial and well-evidenced SOTA across 8 architectures on ImageNet-1k (14.94%/21.28% FPR@95 gains over OptFS for near/far-OOD) while requiring as few as 10 ID validation samples for its eCDF, making it practical in large-scale settings where training data is inaccessible. The main practical limitation is the 2.91× latency overhead from the additional forward pass and gradient computation (reducible to 1.56× with random perturbation), which may constrain real-time deployment. An open question is whether the activation-shift signal generalizes to non-visual modalities or to settings where the energy-score framework itself is less appropriate, and whether the adaptive percentile can be further simplified to eliminate the need for even the small eCDF.
