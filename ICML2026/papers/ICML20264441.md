## Problem

Existing mode-connectivity methods (FGE, AutoNEB) do not reliably connect truly independently trained modes and have been validated only on a narrow set of dated architectures (basic CNNs, VGG, ResNet). Whether low-loss paths persist between independently trained solutions in more recent architectures (MobileNet, EfficientNet, CCT, DLA, etc.) and whether a reproducible algorithm exists for constructing such paths remained open.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264441/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two independently trained model parameter vectors (obtained with different random seeds but identical hyperparameters) and a training dataset, and produces a discrete sequence of parameter points forming a continuous low-loss path between them. Unusually, the path is constructed layer-by-layer rather than in the full D-dimensional parameter space: at each step only a subset of layers is moved, and the output includes per-layer L2 distance trajectories, training/test loss and accuracy at every intermediate point, and a variance-correction projection that keeps each layer's parameter distribution on its target variance sphere.

## Method

LLPF comprises two complementary algorithms. LLPF_M2M connects two modes on the same variance sphere by iteratively: (1) moving the current point a small step toward the destination, (2) projecting back onto the variance sphere via a VarianceCorrection step that rescales layer parameters to preserve their variance (counteracting the vanishing-variance problem), (3) performing r SGD training steps to reduce loss, and (4) re-projecting to the sphere. LLPF_M2O extends this across different variance spheres by moving toward the origin with an AngleConformal step that adaptively scales the learning rate as variance decreases. The critical design choice is the Follow Data Flow (FDF) layer ordering: layers are processed sequentially from shallow to deep (with parallel branches handled individually), which is essential for complex architectures where simultaneous all-layer movement fails. This layer-wise decomposition is what enables LLPF to generalize to architectures where full-parameter-space linear interpolation breaks down.

## Results

LLPF achieves a worst-case (maximum) training loss of 0.006 along the connection path for ResNet18@CIFAR-10 with targeted hyperparameter tuning (Figure 6), and approximately 0.04 under standard settings (Figure 2). The algorithm consistently produces low-loss paths across 10+ architectures (ResNet, DLA, CCT, MobileNet, ShuffleNet, EfficientNet, RegNet, VGG, DenseNet, LeNet) on MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-10, with narrow standard-deviation bands across repeated runs indicating high reproducibility. LLPF also successfully connects modes trained with different hyperparameters (different variance spheres), a setting not evaluated by prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLPF substantially outperforms AutoNEB in both path quality and reliability. In the paper's four independent runs of AutoNEB on ResNet20@CIFAR-10, the maximum training loss along the path ranges from 0.519 to 4.200 (Table 9), whereas LLPF achieves 0.04 under standard settings and 0.006 with tuned hyperparameters on ResNet18@CIFAR-10. AutoNEB also fails entirely to connect certain CCT mode pairs (Appendix J, Figure 18), while LLPF succeeds with a maximum training loss below 0.3. FGE is shown to be non-comparable: the paper demonstrates (Appendix C) that FGE's released training script uses a fixed seed, producing modes with per-layer cosine similarity of 0.45–0.98 (nearly identical) rather than the ~0.01 expected for truly independent modes. When FGE is applied to genuinely independent modes, the maximum training loss exceeds 1.15 on VGG16@CIFAR-10, confirming it cannot connect truly independently trained solutions. LLPF is the only method that simultaneously supports the broadest architecture range, consistent reproducibility across random seeds, and cross-hyperparameter (different variance sphere) connectivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the layer-wise mode connectivity principle from Adilova et al. (ICLR 2024) into a practical algorithm (LLPF) that constructs low-loss paths between independently trained modes, building on the foundational mode-connectivity framework of Garipov et al. (2018) and Draxler et al. (2018). It departs from prior methods by introducing a variance-sphere geometric framework and a VarianceCorrection step to address the vanishing variance problem identified in Tian et al. (2024), enabling reliable layer-by-layer traversal. The paper also demonstrates that the widely-cited FGE results were compromised by a fixed-seed bug in the original training script, effectively connecting near-identical rather than truly independent modes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LLPF provides the first reliable, reproducible tool for exploring the low-loss region across modern architectures (MobileNet, EfficientNet, CCT, DLA, RegNet), and uniquely supports connecting modes trained under different hyperparameters (different variance spheres), a setting absent from prior work. The empirical evidence for highly consistent path trajectories across random seeds raises the open conjecture that all SGD-trained modes lie within a single path-connected low-loss manifold, though no theoretical framework currently supports this. Limitations include the lack of theoretical guarantees, the computational cost of path construction (tens of hours on modern GPUs), and the observation that low training loss along the path does not guarantee low test loss.
