## Problem

Bisimulation-based visual RL methods learn representations by matching a latent dissimilarity to a behavioral fixed-point target, but the latent distance is typically fixed a priori (e.g., ℓ_p norms or cosine distance), which is either too rigid to capture context-dependent anisotropy or, if made fully learnable, admits degenerate solutions where the distance head absorbs supervision without shaping the encoder. The paper asks whether a structured yet adaptive latent dissimilarity can improve embeddability while preserving meaningful learning pressure on the representation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pixel-based image observations from DeepMind Control Suite environments, encoded by a shared 4-layer CNN into d-dimensional latents (d=50 by default). The PAMD module takes a pair of latent vectors (z, z' ∈ R^d) via their concatenation [z; z'] ∈ R^{2d} and produces a single scalar pairwise distance through a pair-conditioned positive-definite quadratic form on the displacement Δ = z − z'. This distance serves as a drop-in replacement for the latent comparator inside existing bisimulation objectives (DBC, MICo, SimSR), which in turn train the encoder jointly with a SAC actor-critic to produce a control policy.

## Method

PAMD parameterizes the latent distance as d_θ(z,z') = √(Δ^T G̃_θ(z,z') Δ + ε), where G̃_θ is a pair-conditioned positive-definite matrix constructed via a lightweight MLP (MetricNet: R^{2d}→128→128→d(d+1)/2) that outputs the lower-triangular entries of a Cholesky factor L_θ, yielding G_θ = L_θ L_θ^⊤ (PD by construction). Symmetry is enforced by averaging G_θ(z,z') and G_θ(z',z), a small ridge λI is added for numerical stability, and per-pair trace normalization fixes the global scale degree of freedom. A small auxiliary loss weakly encourages the normalized metric to deviate from isotropy, promoting pair-dependent directional structure. The key design insight is that tying the distance to the displacement Δ through a PD quadratic form prevents the distance head from freely regressing the fixed-point target without encoder adaptation—unlike an unconstrained pairwise MLP, which can drive the Bellman residual to near-zero under a frozen encoder—thereby preserving representational learning pressure while remaining expressive enough to capture context-dependent anisotropy.

## Results

On the DBC family (Table 1), DBC-Det+PAMD substantially outperforms DBC-Det across all four pixel-based DMC tasks: Cartpole Swingup (818 vs. 710), Cheetah Run (524 vs. 350), Finger Spin (860 vs. 630), and Hopper Hop (181 vs. 33), with the largest relative gain on Hopper Hop (~5.5×). Under the independently-coupled operator family, the PAMD-equipped variant achieves the best mean return on Cheetah Run (878±8) and Walker Run (652±24), and is competitive with TACO on Acrobot Swingup (237 vs. 241) and Hopper Hop (250 vs. 261). The method adds only ~1.43% FLOPs relative to the pixel encoder, confirming that the gains come from the distance structure rather than additional model capacity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DBC (Zhang et al., 2021), PAMD yields large and consistent gains: DBC-Det+PAMD improves over DBC-Det by 108 points on Cartpole Swingup, 174 on Cheetah Run, 230 on Finger Spin, and 148 on Hopper Hop, while probabilistic DBC and DBC-Det are broadly comparable to each other, confirming the gain is attributable to the distance replacement. Against MICo (Castro et al., 2021), the independently-coupled PAMD variant dominates on all overlapping tasks, e.g., 878 vs. 347 on Cheetah Run and 250 vs. 78 on Hopper Hop. Against SimSR (Zang et al., 2022), PAMD wins on Cheetah Run (878 vs. 778), Hopper Hop (250 vs. 220), and Walker Run (652 vs. 613), and is close on Acrobot Swingup (237 vs. 141, a large margin). Against CURL (Laskin et al., 2020), the PAMD variants achieve substantially higher returns on all four overlapping tasks (e.g., 878 vs. 657 on Cheetah Run, 250 vs. 152 on Hopper Hop), though CURL uses a different training recipe (contrastive augmentation) rather than bisimulation objectives, so this comparison is contextual rather than a controlled head-to-head.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits squarely within the bisimulation-based representation learning lineage (DBC, MICo, SimSR), where encoders are trained to match a latent dissimilarity to a Bellman fixed-point behavioral distance. Its departure is in the latent comparator: rather than a fixed ℓ_p norm or cosine distance, PAMD introduces a pair-conditioned positive-definite quadratic form (a local Mahalanobis geometry) parameterized via Cholesky decomposition, occupying a middle ground between rigid global norms and unconstrained pairwise MLPs. The geometric motivation draws on Riemannian metric fields as a local surrogate, and the metric-embedding perspective from Castro et al. (2023) motivates why the choice of δ affects embeddability. The method is explicitly a drop-in plug-in that leaves the target behavioral operator and all other pipeline components unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a concrete failure mode in bisimulation-based RL: an unconstrained distance head can absorb the fixed-point supervision without shaping the encoder, degrading downstream control. The residual-fitting diagnostic (frozen vs. trainable encoder) provides a clean conceptual tool for diagnosing this, and the finding that off-diagonal structure (not just adaptive axis-wise scaling) is necessary for the gains is a useful inductive-bias insight. Limitations include the dense p×p matrix scaling unfavorably with latent dimension, evaluation restricted to pixel-based DMC tasks, and the DBC comparison requiring a deterministic-transition variant rather than the original probabilistic formulation. Open questions include whether structured adaptive distances transfer to other similarity targets (value-based, advantage-based) and to broader manipulation benchmarks like Meta-World.
