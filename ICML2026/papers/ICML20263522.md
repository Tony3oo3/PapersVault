## Problem

Existing red-teaming methods for LLMs focus on eliciting unsafe text generation from standalone models or RAG systems, overlooking a distinct threat surface introduced by web search: the retrieval and citation of harmful external webpages. Because search-enabled LLMs aggregate content from uncontrolled, heterogeneous web sources, they can expose users to misinformation and toxic material through citations even when the generated response itself appears benign — a risk class that conventional response-level evaluation cannot detect.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263522/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a harmful content category (harassment, hate, self-harm, sexual, violence) and an attack strategy (keyword injection, exaggeration, role play) as inputs, and produces adversarial search queries that appear superficially benign but steer the target LLM's retrieval toward unsafe webpages. Each query is executed against a black-box commercial LLM with web search; the system then extracts citation URLs from the response, renders the cited pages via a headless browser, strips non-visible noise, and runs two independent moderation tools (OpenAI Moderation API and Llama Guard) to classify risks into response, citation, or combined categories. The final output is a structured risk detection report with per-query risk labels and aggregate statistics.

## Method

CREST-Search combines three retrieval-targeted query generation strategies — keyword injection (embedding sensitive terms to bias search results), exaggeration (amplifying query elements toward fringe content), and role play (reframing search intent via persona contexts) — with a three-stage iterative pipeline: (1) a red-teaming model generates candidate queries, (2) queries are executed on the target LLM and cited webpages are evaluated for harm, and (3) a judgment model uses in-context learning to produce refinement guidance that revises unsuccessful queries while preserving the attack scenario. To reduce per-query cost, the authors construct the WebSearch-Harm dataset via a two-stage few-shot expansion (expert-written seeds → validated exemplars) and fine-tune Gemini-2.5-flash via supervised fine-tuning, enabling single-shot generation of high-quality adversarial queries. The fine-tuned model achieves an 80.5% risk detection rate at $1.50 per 100 queries and 49.6 s per query, substantially outperforming zero-shot (31.1%) and few-shot (49.3%) baselines while maintaining controlled query toxicity (23.6%) and diversity (self-BLEU 0.59).

## Results

CREST-Search achieves a risk detection rate (RDR) of 80.5% across four commercial search-enabled LLMs (GPT-4o-search, GPT-4o-mini-search, Gemini-2.0-flash-search, Gemini-2.5-flash-search), substantially outperforming the best baseline SafeSearch (32.4%). Among the detected risks, 89.3% are citation risks, and 74.7% involve only citation risk while the generated response itself remains benign—making them invisible to conventional red-teaming. CREST-Search maintains a query toxicity of 23.6% (second lowest) and a self-BLEU diversity score of 0.59 (third highest), balancing attack effectiveness with query naturalness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CREST-Search dominates all five main baselines on risk detection rate, achieving 80.5% versus SafeSearch's 32.4% (the closest competitor), HarmBench's 11.6%, DangerousQA's 10.5%, RED-EVAL's 5.7%, and UAT's 5.2%. The gap is most dramatic against UAT and RED-EVAL, which were designed for standalone LLM jailbreaking and fail to exploit the retrieval-citation pipeline (RDR < 6%). SafeSearch, the only baseline targeting search-enabled LLMs, achieves the lowest toxicity (5.9%) but operates under an offline setting that manipulates retrieved pages, making it less representative of real-world black-box auditing; CREST-Search's 23.6% toxicity is still well below UAT (87.9%) and DangerousQA (65.5%). On diversity, CREST-Search (0.59) outperforms SafeSearch (0.27) and HarmBench (0.48) but trails UAT (0.77) and RED-EVAL (0.76), reflecting a deliberate trade-off toward more targeted, effective queries. A persona-based baseline (Deshpande et al., 2023) tested in the appendix achieves 43.4% overall risk but only 10.3% citation risk with 92.3% toxicity, confirming that generation-stage attacks are poorly suited to the search-citation threat surface.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the red-teaming paradigm for LLMs from the generation stage to the retrieval-citation pipeline, identifying citation risk as a distinct and previously underexplored attack surface in web-search-augmented LLMs. While prior red-teaming work (Ganguli et al., Perez et al., Ge et al.) and the closest baseline SafeSearch (Dong et al., 2025) target unsafe generation or manipulate retrieved content offline, CREST-Search operates purely at the query level under black-box constraints, repurposing search-engine manipulation concepts (keyword injection, exaggeration, role-play) to steer retrieval toward harmful sources. The WebSearch-Harm dataset and the fine-tuned red-teaming model represent a new contribution that bridges adversarial prompt optimization with supervised fine-tuning for search-specific safety evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 74.7% of detected risks involve only harmful citations while the LLM response itself remains benign is practically critical: it means conventional response-level safety evaluation would miss the majority of real-world threats in search-augmented systems, and users would encounter harmful content through seemingly authoritative citations. This underscores an urgent need for citation-level defense mechanisms (URL filtering, source credibility ranking) that go beyond generation-stage safety alignment. Open questions include whether the three fixed strategies generalize to future retrieval architectures, whether adaptive strategy scheduling can outperform single-strategy optimization, and how to balance citation safety filtering against response latency in production deployments.
