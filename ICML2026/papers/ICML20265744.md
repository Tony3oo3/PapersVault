## Problem

RNA 3D structure modeling is hampered by extreme data scarcity (fewer than 6,000 resolved structures versus 200,000+ for proteins), the limitations of continuous latent spaces that suffer posterior collapse or produce biophysically implausible conformations, and the lack of interpretability in learned representations that fail to correspond to the recurring structural motifs (hairpin loops, internal loops, junctions) that compose RNA architecture. No existing method simultaneously addresses all three challenges while capturing the modular, motif-based organization of RNA folds.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RNA 3D atomic coordinates in one of three granularities (single C4' atom, 10-atom backbone, or 11-atom backbone-plus-base-anchoring), mean-centered and augmented with random SO(3) rotations. The primary output is a sequence of discrete structural tokens produced by FSQ quantization (codebook sizes of 240, 1000, or 4375 via per-channel level configurations like (8,6,5) or (7,5,5,5,5)), which condition a flow-matching decoder to reconstruct full atomic coordinates. For downstream tasks, the frozen discrete tokens and continuous encoder embeddings are transferred to an autoregressive GVP decoder for inverse folding or fused with 1D sequence and 2D secondary-structure embeddings for RNA-ligand binding prediction.

## Method

RiboSphere combines a shallow (2-layer) geometric transformer encoder with a deep (8-layer) flow-matching decoder, separated by a Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) bottleneck that discretizes continuous latent codes into a Cartesian-product codebook without auxiliary losses. The encoder injects pairwise distance-bucket embeddings and relative positional encodings as geometric biases into multi-head self-attention with a sliding-window mechanism, while the decoder learns a time-dependent vector field conditioned on the discrete codes and uses classifier-free guidance plus SDE-based sampling for high-fidelity reconstruction. The key architectural insight is the asymmetric encoder-decoder design: a compact encoder forces key geometric features into the discrete latent space, preventing the encoder from bypassing the quantization bottleneck, while the deep decoder handles the complex continuous reconstruction. The learned code indices are shown to be statistically enriched for specific RNA motifs (three-way junctions show KL divergence of 0.70 from background), confirming that the discrete vocabulary captures biologically meaningful structural primitives rather than arbitrary compression.

## Results

On RNA structure reconstruction, RiboSphere achieves RMSD 1.25 Å, TM-score 0.84, and lDDT 0.83 with its best configuration (2-layer encoder, 8-layer decoder, 4,375-token FSQ codebook, single-atom C4' representation). For inverse folding, RiboSphere attains 63.0% sequence recovery and 0.63 scMCC, substantially outperforming gRNAde (52.9% recovery) and RIDiffusion (53.3%). On RNA-ligand binding prediction, RiboSphere achieves the best AUROC on 5 of 8 evaluation splits, reaching 0.7534 on the most challenging Biosensor homology-fingerprint split (vs. 0.7279 for GerNA-Bind).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On inverse folding (Table 2), RiboSphere achieves 63.0% sequence recovery, outperforming gRNAde (52.9%), RIDiffusion (53.3%), RiFold (41.6%), and RDesign (41.5%) by 10+ points over the strongest baselines; it also leads on 2D self-consistency (scMCC 0.63 vs. 0.60 for gRNAde) but trails RiFold on 3-mer diversity (0.82 vs. 0.89), reflecting a deliberate fidelity-over-diversity trade-off. On RNA-ligand binding (Table 3), RiboSphere wins 5 of 8 splits: it surpasses GerNA-Bind on Biosensor RNA-homology (0.9031 vs. 0.9014) and the hardest homology-fingerprint split (0.7534 vs. 0.7279, +2.6%), and on Robin RNA-homology (0.6335 vs. 0.6188), ligand-fingerprint (0.6950 vs. 0.6814), and homology-fingerprint (0.6349 vs. 0.6176). RiboSphere concedes to GerNA-Bind on the easier random splits (Biosensor 0.9524 vs. 0.9755; Robin 0.6945 vs. 0.7094) and on Biosensor ligand-fingerprint (0.7406 vs. 0.7723), suggesting the discrete bottleneck's denoising advantage is most pronounced under distributional shift. All other baselines (RSAPred, DeepDTIs, DeepConv-DTI, GraphDTA) are consistently outperformed by both RiboSphere and GerNA-Bind across every split, confirming that the gap is primarily between the two most recent RNA-specific methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

RiboSphere sits at the intersection of three established lines: (1) geometric deep learning for RNA (ARES, gRNAde, EquiRNA), which provides the E(3)-equivariant encoder backbone; (2) discrete representation learning via VQ/FSQ, as applied to proteins by FoldToken, GCP-VQVAE, and ESM3; and (3) flow matching as a generative decoder, following Lipman et al. and ProteinFlow-style inference. The key departure is from continuous-latent RNA generative models (Dfold, diffusion-based approaches) that the authors argue suffer from posterior collapse and biophysical implausibility under RNA's data scarcity. By inserting an FSQ bottleneck between a shallow geometric transformer encoder and a deep flow-matching decoder, RiboSphere adapts the protein-domain VQ paradigm to RNA's specific challenges—high backbone flexibility, non-canonical interactions, and fewer than 6,000 resolved structures—while retaining the interpretability that continuous embeddings lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discrete codebook provides a rare form of interpretability in RNA structure modeling: the learned tokens statistically correspond to biologically defined motifs (hairpin loops, internal loops, three-way junctions), offering a potential 'structural alphabet' for RNA that could inform design and drug discovery. The transfer to inverse folding (63% recovery, ~10 points over gRNAde) and OOD binding prediction demonstrates practical utility beyond reconstruction. However, the model is limited to single-chain RNA, lacks experimental validation, and the pLDDT in inverse folding (0.55) trails gRNAde (0.62), suggesting the discrete bottleneck may lose information relevant to full 3D self-consistency. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-chain complexes, dynamic conformational ensembles, and whether the motif-level token structure can be leveraged for zero-shot function annotation.
