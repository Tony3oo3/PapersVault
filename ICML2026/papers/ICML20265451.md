## Problem

Existing diffusion-model alignment methods like Diffusion-DPO suffer from two fundamental issues: training instability driven by high gradient variance at early timesteps and extreme sensitivity to the KL penalty β, and off-policy bias arising because preference data is collected from a fixed reference model rather than the evolving policy. The paper identifies that early timesteps (t < ~60) carry low importance weights that inject noisy gradients, while distributional drift between training data and the current policy causes reward hacking and performance collapse over extended training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts paired with preference-annotated sample pairs (a preferred and a dispreferred image or video) drawn from datasets such as Pick-a-Pic (images) or human-annotated video pairs (10k pairs for video). It outputs a fine-tuned diffusion model (SD1.5, SDXL, CogVideoX-2B/5B, Wan2.1-1.3B, or FLUX-dev) that generates images or videos better aligned with human preferences. The preference pairs are typically off-policy, meaning they were generated by a different or stronger model than the one being optimized, which is precisely the distributional gap SIPO is designed to correct.

## Method

SIPO introduces two complementary mechanisms on top of the Diffusion-DPO objective. First, DPO-C&M computes a per-timestep importance weight w(t) as the ratio of the learned reverse transition p_θ(x_{t-1}|x_t) to the forward posterior q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0), clips it to [1−ε, 1+ε], and uses it as a multiplicative soft mask that suppresses gradients from low-weight early timesteps where forward and reverse paths diverge. Second, the full SIPO objective reinterprets the RLHF reward-maximization problem via importance sampling over off-policy data, reformulating it as a KL divergence to a shaped target distribution p* ∝ p_ref · exp(w_θ·r/β); the clipped inverse importance weight 1/w_θ then adaptively scales the pairwise log-ratio preference signal, reinforcing under-represented preferred samples, imposing a finite peak that prevents reward hacking, and reducing sensitivity to β. The final loss is a sigmoid-weighted pairwise logistic loss where the preference margin is multiplied by the shared clipped inverse importance weight, yielding stable, timestep-aware updates throughout the denoising trajectory.

## Results

On text-to-image generation (SD1.5, Pick-a-Pic test set), SIPO achieves the best PickScore (21.68), HPSv2 (0.2783), and Aesthetics (5.811), outperforming Diffusion-DPO by +0.71 PickScore and +0.0127 HPSv2. On text-to-video (VBench), SIPO reaches 81.68 Total on CogVideoX-2B@1000Steps where Diffusion-DPO catastrophically collapses to 67.28, and achieves 84.78 on WanX-1.3B. On the GenEval benchmark (FLUX.1-dev), SIPO attains 0.85 Overall, surpassing Diffusion-DPO (0.82) and D3PO (0.81), with the largest gains on Position (0.73 vs. 0.67) and Color Attribute (0.69 vs. 0.66).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIPO consistently outperforms Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., CVPR 2024) across all evaluated settings: on SD1.5 it improves PickScore from 20.97 to 21.68 and HPSv2 from 0.2656 to 0.2783, while on CogVideoX-2B@1000Steps it prevents the catastrophic collapse (67.28 → 81.68 VBench Total). Against D3PO (Yang et al., CVPR 2024) on GenEval, SIPO achieves 0.85 Overall vs. 0.81, with the largest margin on Position (0.73 vs. 0.66) and Counting (0.79 vs. 0.76). Compared to SPIN-Diffusion (Yuan et al., 2024) under the best-of-5 evaluation protocol, SIPO edges out with a higher average score (7.4150 vs. 7.4126) and better PickScore (22.0130 vs. 22.0024), though SPIN-Diffusion slightly leads on Aesthetics (6.2481 vs. 6.2451). Versus SPO (Swamy et al., 2024), SIPO wins on PickScore (21.68 vs. 21.46), HPSv2 (0.2783 vs. 0.2671), and Aesthetics (5.811 vs. 5.702), while SPO's ImageReward is lower (0.2321 vs. 0.3051). The only metric where SIPO does not lead is ImageReward in Table 2, where Diffusion-KTO scores 0.6156, though SIPO still provides more balanced performance across the remaining preference and aesthetic metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIPO extends Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024) by incorporating importance-sampling-based reweighting and timestep-aware clipping/masking, drawing the clipping mechanism from PPO/GRPO and the off-policy correction justification from TIS-DPO (Liu et al., 2024). The per-timestep importance weights are computed following DDPO's (Black et al., 2023) Gaussian transition density framework, while the overall pairwise logistic loss structure remains rooted in the original DPO formulation (Rafailov et al., 2023). Conceptually, the paper's key departure is a systematic timestep analysis that identifies early denoising steps as the primary source of gradient instability, reframing the alignment problem as one of selective, reliability-weighted updates rather than uniform optimization across all timesteps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making preference optimization for diffusion models substantially more robust: SIPO's insensitivity to β (working at 0.02 where Diffusion-DPO collapses) and its resistance to training collapse at extended step counts (1000+ steps) address two of the most common failure modes practitioners encounter. The timestep analysis provides actionable guidance for the broader community on where preference signals are most informative. However, the method remains offline and the importance weight computation adds per-step overhead; open questions include whether the approach extends naturally to fully on-policy or iterative settings without the distributional drift that motivates the reweighting, and whether the modest absolute gains (e.g., ~0.4 VBench points) scale to larger preference datasets or more diverse evaluation protocols.
