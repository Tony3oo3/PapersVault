## Problem

Reverse Chain-of-Thought Generation (RCG) synthesizes reasoning traces from query-answer pairs, but the visible answer acts as a cognitive anchor that drives post-hoc rationalization rather than genuine derivation. The paper identifies two gaps: (1) no comprehensive measurement framework captures the multi-level anchoring effect beyond surface lexical overlap, and (2) the intuitive mitigation—semantic suppression—paradoxically increases deeper entropic and probabilistic anchoring while only masking surface artifacts, degrading downstream reasoning quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query Q and a pre-committed response A as input and produces a two-part output: a structural skeleton S (a sequence of functional tags from a closed 8-tag vocabulary—PLAN, RETR, INFR, EVAL, SUMM, BTRK, RFLX, BRCH—each paired with a ≤20-token content summary describing the step's intent without revealing values) followed by a full reasoning trace R. The skeleton is generated first as a content-neutral structural target, then used to condition the reasoning generation, enforcing a strict two-phase ordering where the skeleton must be complete before reasoning begins.

## Method

SSR replaces answer suppression with structural decoupling via two-phase generation: first synthesizing a response-abstracted functional skeleton S ~ P(S|Q,A) that encodes reasoning topology (e.g., PLAN→BRCH→EVAL) while discouraging direct answer encoding, then generating the full trace R ~ P(R|S,Q,A) guided by this skeleton. The key insight is that a positive structural target redirects generative focus away from monitoring forbidden answer content, breaking the ironic-process cycle that makes suppression counterproductive. The paper provides an information-theoretic bound (Proposition C.2) showing the skeleton channel transmits at most n·log₂|F| + Σεᵢ bits about A, and formalizes a three-level anchoring hierarchy (lexical, entropic, probabilistic) to measure residual dependence. SSR-D extends this to a distillation setting where a fine-tuned SSR teacher generates (S*, R*) pairs and the student is trained with a combined objective L = L_S + L_R, internalizing the skeleton-first generation pattern for more reliable format adherence and stronger downstream gains.

## Results

SSR-D achieves the highest scores across all four in-distribution benchmarks and both model scales tested. On ArenaHard-v2.0, SSR-D reaches 59.5 (Qwen3-8B) and 50.8 (Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507), improving over the NEU baseline by +8.7 and +9.1 points respectively. On MultiChallenge, SSR-D gains up to +3.1 points over NEU, while suppression baselines (SUP, AUG-SUP) actually degrade multi-turn performance by 2.7–5.2 points. On OOD benchmarks, SSR-D recovers ~30% of the AIME 2025 gap for Qwen3-8B (44.2 vs. 33.3 for NEU) and exceeds the untrained GPQA-Diamond score for Qwen3-32B (65.2 vs. 64.1 untrained).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SSR/SSR-D against two suppression-based baselines: SUP (Wang et al., 2025a; 'do not reveal the answer until the end') and AUG-SUP (Xu et al., 2025; intensified suppression with explicit prohibition and punishment framing). Both suppression methods reduce lexical anchoring (A_lex drops from 48.5 to 45.3/42.9) but paradoxically increase entropic anchoring (55.9→57.4/58.4) and probabilistic anchoring (37.0→43.6/44.4), confirming the paper's ironic-process hypothesis. On downstream tasks, SUP and AUG-SUP yield only marginal single-turn gains on ArenaHard (+0.7/+2.0 for Qwen3-8B) but consistently degrade MultiChallenge performance (−2.7/−5.2 points), whereas SSR-D improves both. On OOD AIME 2025, AUG-SUP actually worsens Qwen3-32B by 7.5 points relative to NEU, while SSR-D recovers 44.2 vs. 33.3 for NEU on Qwen3-8B. The paper attributes suppression's failure to active monitoring of the forbidden answer, which deepens latent dependence rather than eliminating it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the structural skeleton concept from Skeleton-of-Thought (Ning et al., 2024), originally designed for efficient parallel generation, as a mechanism to decouple reasoning topology from answer-specific content in the reverse CoT setting. The entropic anchoring metric is directly derived from Gwak et al. (2025)'s information-density analysis of LLM reasoning traces, extended here to diagnose post-hoc rationalization through global uniformity and local non-uniformity. The paper departs from the standard RCG literature (Zelikman et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2025a) by formalizing the anchoring problem with a three-level hierarchy (lexical, entropic, probabilistic) and by demonstrating that the widely-adopted semantic suppression baseline is counterproductive, connecting its failure to Ironic Process Theory from cognitive psychology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that semantic suppression—the intuitive and widely-deployed fix for post-hoc rationalization—paradoxically deepens latent anchoring while masking surface overlap has direct practical implications for anyone building reasoning distillation pipelines on LMArena-style data. The three-level anchoring hierarchy provides a more diagnostic evaluation toolkit than surface-level faithfulness checks, and the SSR/SSR-D method offers a concrete, implementable alternative with consistent gains across model scales and families. Open questions include whether the ironic-process interpretation is mechanistically correct (the paper explicitly defers attention/probing verification to future work), whether the skeleton-mediated information bound (Proposition C.2) can be tightened to cover the residual reasoning-generation channel, and whether the approach generalizes to settings where the answer is not pre-committed but emerges from multi-agent or iterative refinement.
