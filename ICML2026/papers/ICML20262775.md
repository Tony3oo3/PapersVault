## Problem

Continuous monitoring of randomized experiments with delayed outcomes is fundamentally harder than the standard setting because treatment can change which units have realized outcomes by any given time, making the observation sequence itself treatment-dependent. No prior work provides design-based, anytime-valid confidence sequences that simultaneously handle staggered entry, differentially delayed potential outcomes, and environmental nonstationarity without imposing a superpopulation or proportional-hazards model.

## Input / Output

The method operates on right-censored experimental data where, at any calendar time t, some units' outcomes remain unobserved and the treatment itself governs this censoring. The target estimand is the sample cumulative reward—the total reward that would have been realized by time t had all entered units been assigned to a given arm—which is a fixed quantity under the design-based perspective rather than a population parameter. Outputs are calendar-time-indexed confidence sequences for each arm's cumulative reward and for the treatment effect, plus a sequential p-value, all valid for continuous monitoring without a pre-specified stopping time.

## Method

The central technical contribution is a filtration analysis proving that the treatment-effect estimation error is not a martingale under any filtration, a negative result arising from cross-arm dependence induced by randomized assignment combined with temporal misalignment from differentially delayed outcomes. Each arm-specific IPW estimation error, however, is a martingale under a carefully constructed single-arm event-time filtration that reveals unit i's assignment exactly at its arm-w potential event time. Confidence sequences are built arm-by-arm via Strassen's strong approximation to a time-changed Brownian motion and the normal-mixture boundary of Howard et al. (2021), then combined through a union bound to yield a valid confidence sequence for the treatment effect. The paper further shows this union-bound boundary is asymptotically no wider—and often narrower—than the classical variance-upper-bound benchmark when arm-level variance clocks are imbalanced, and characterizes the class of AIPW augmentations (event-time activations that are zero before and constant after the potential event time) that preserve the martingale property.

## Results

In the simulation (Dataset 5.1, N=500, 200 resamples), the IPW confidence sequences achieve empirical time-uniform coverage of 96.0% for r_t(0), 97.0% for r_t(1), and 99.0% for Δ_t, against nominal levels of 97.5% per arm and 95% for the treatment effect. Classical pointwise confidence intervals (equation 31) exhibit clear coverage violations at multiple monitoring times, while the anytime-valid sequences cover the estimand at all times. Proposition 4.12 shows the union-bound boundary is asymptotically no wider than the variance-upper-bound benchmark, with relative width R_π → 2√(π(1−π)) in the symmetric case and R_π → √(1−π) or √(π) under arm-level variance imbalance, both ≤ 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its design-based IPW confidence sequences against the Poisson-process confidence sequences of Lindon & Kallus (2025) on Netflix OOM error telemetry data (Appendix C, Figures 6–7). Both methods yield identical point estimates when the Poisson estimates are rescaled by 1/π(w), so neither 'wins' on estimation accuracy. The key distinction is in uncertainty quantification: the Poisson approach requires a time-inhomogeneous Poisson (proportional hazards) modeling assumption to establish its martingale property, while the design-based approach requires no distributional assumptions on event times or rewards, relying solely on randomization and SUTVA. The design-based method also naturally accommodates unit-varying assignment probabilities π_i(w), which the Poisson approach does not, making it more flexible for adaptive experimental designs. Additionally, the paper demonstrates that its union-bound sequential boundary can be asymptotically narrower than the classical variance-upper-bound benchmark when arm-level variance clocks are imbalanced (Proposition 4.12), a property not available to the fixed-horizon variance upper bound used in standard design-based practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the design-based confidence sequence framework of Ham et al. (2022) — which handles randomized experiments with immediate outcomes — to the substantially harder setting of delayed outcomes with staggered entry. The technical machinery draws on the normal-mixture boundary of Howard et al. (2021) and the strong-approximation + mixture-martingale pipeline of Waudby-Smith et al. (2024), but the central contribution is a new filtration analysis: the paper proves that the treatment-effect estimation error is not a martingale under *any* filtration, a negative result that has no analogue in the fixed-sample or no-delay settings. The resolution via a union bound over arm-specific martingales is the sequential counterpart of the classical variance upper bound, and the paper shows it can actually be tighter than that bound when arm-level variance clocks are imbalanced.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides distribution-free, anytime-valid inference for a setting that is extremely common in industry (conversion experiments, canary deployments, marketing A/B tests) but has previously required either proportional-hazards modeling assumptions or ad-hoc zero-imputation without valid error control. The key limitation is that only IPW is directly practical: the martingale-preserving AIPW augmentations require oracle knowledge of potential event times, so variance reduction through covariate adjustment remains an open problem. The negative result (no common martingale filtration for the treatment effect) is a clean impossibility that clarifies a fundamental structural constraint in sequential design-based inference, and the union-bound construction is a natural and provably non-worse resolution that practitioners can implement immediately.
