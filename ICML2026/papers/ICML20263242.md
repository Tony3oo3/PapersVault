## Problem

Token-ranking heuristics for LLM prefill acceleration are currently evaluated only through end-to-end benchmarks, making it impossible to isolate ranking quality from architectural choices, and existing methods exhibit sharp, model-specific accuracy collapses when the scoring layer coincides with a performance trough. The paper addresses both gaps by introducing an Answer-Informed Reference framework for direct ranking evaluation and a cross-layer aggregation method that eliminates layer-dependent instability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263242/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long-context text prompt (up to 64K tokens) and a query, operating as a training-free single forward pass through the target model. At each layer it scores all prompt tokens using post-softmax attention from an observation window of W=8 most recent prompt tokens, then at a designated pruning layer retains only the top-k% tokens (10%, 20%, or 40%) for the remaining layers. The output is a generated answer produced from the pruned sequence, with a per-layer compressed KV cache (0.3 GB at 10% keep rate vs. 0.1 GB for the reference) available for subsequent decode steps.

## Method

CLAA defers all KV cache compression for the first m=4 layers so the model can build high-fidelity semantic representations before any pruning occurs. At each subsequent layer, it computes a per-token importance score by summing post-softmax attention from an observation window of W=8 recent prompt tokens across all heads, then buffers these scores in a sliding window of n=4 consecutive layers. At the pruning layer l_p, CLAA averages the buffered scores across all n layers to produce a single stable ranking, which is used both to prune the hidden state to the top-k% tokens and to compress the KV cache. This multi-layer mean aggregation smooths out layer-specific noise, preventing the 11–12 point accuracy collapses that single-layer methods like FastKV exhibit at model-specific trough layers (e.g., layer 11 for Llama-3.2-3B, layer 12 for Llama-3.1-8B). The paper additionally introduces an Answer-Informed Reference that measures token importance by aggregating attention from the generated (or ground-truth) answer back to the prompt, providing a task-specific upper bound and a Spearman-correlation metric for evaluating any heuristic's ranking quality independent of its architectural design.

## Results

On LongBench with Llama-3.1-8B at 10% token keep rate, CLAA achieves 47.13% average accuracy, closely approaching the Model-Ref upper bound of 47.83% and the GT-Ref upper bound of 49.29% (which matches FullKV's 49.32%). On RULER (64K context, 40% keep rate), CLAA leads in retrieval (89.85%) and multi-hop reasoning (87.72%), outperforming FastKV (87.69%, 87.16%). CLAA reduces TTFT by 39% compared to FullKV at 10% keep rate while maintaining accuracy within 2.2 points of the full-context baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLAA outperforms all three token-ranking heuristics on LongBench average: 47.13% vs. FastKV (46.81%), Speculative Prefill (41.99%), and GemFilter (37.59%). Against FastKV, CLAA's primary advantage is layer stability rather than raw accuracy at a fixed layer—FastKV collapses by 11–12 points when the pruning layer coincides with a correlation trough (layer 11 for 3B, layer 12 for 8B), while CLAA remains stable across all layers. On Needle-in-a-Haystack, GemFilter fails at intermediate document positions (22–44% depth) and Speculative Prefill misses needles in the latter half, whereas CLAA maintains 0.909 average accuracy across all positions. Against semantic compression baselines, CLAA dominates on both speed and quality: Selective Context is actually slower than FullKV (0.32× speedup) while degrading scores (e.g., 0.186 vs. 0.487 on Qasper), and LLMLingua achieves at most 1.37× speedup with substantially lower scores (0.236 vs. 0.310 on GovReport), compared to CLAA's 1.34×–1.45× speedup with scores within 4% of FullKV.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLAA is a direct architectural extension of FastKV, reusing its observation-window post-softmax attention scoring (Eq. 5 mirrors Eq. 2) but replacing the single-layer TSP decision with a cross-layer averaging window to mitigate the layer instability that FastKV itself partially acknowledges. The paper's more distinctive contribution is the Answer-Informed Reference evaluation framework, which is novel in this literature: prior token-ranking heuristics (GemFilter, FastKV, Speculative Prefill) have only been compared via end-to-end task accuracy, making it impossible to isolate ranking quality from architectural choices. By grounding token importance in attention from the generated (or ground-truth) answer, the framework decouples scoring quality from the pruning/compression architecture, a separation the prior methods never attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that 10% of prompt tokens can match full-context LongBench performance across three models is a strong efficiency signal for long-context deployment, and the layer-instability diagnosis (FastKV collapsing 11–12 points at model-specific layers) gives practitioners a concrete reason to avoid single-layer pruning without a sweep. However, a meaningful gap persists between the best heuristic (CLAA, 47.13%) and the GT-Ref upper bound (49.29%), and the paper itself notes that summarization tasks show flat rank correlations where static prefill pruning may be fundamentally limited. Open questions include whether a lightweight learned predictor trained on GT-Ref rankings can close the information gap, and how these methods extend to multi-turn conversations where importance shifts across turns.
