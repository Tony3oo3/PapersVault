## Problem

Existing medical large vision-language models (Med-LVLMs) perform high-level visual comprehension but cannot ground their reasoning at the pixel level, which is essential for clinically verifiable localization of pathological regions. Unified models that add segmentation (e.g., MedPLIB) rely on patch-level CLIP features that discard fine-grained spatial details and use a single special token to represent all segmentation targets, severely limiting the model's ability to distinguish diverse anatomical or pathological structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260442/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a medical image (across CT, MRI, X-ray, ultrasound, endoscopy, dermatoscopy, OCT, and pathology) and a free-form text prompt. The image is encoded by UniMed-CLIP (ViT-L/14) into patch-level embeddings, which are then processed by the Region Perceiver to yield 20 region query tokens; both patch and region embeddings are projected into the LLM's space. The LLM generates a diagnostic text response that may embed discrete region code tokens (e.g., [C2-16]) from an expanded vocabulary of 18 modalities × 32 codes; these tokens are decoded through the Region Perceiver's segmentation head to reconstruct pixel-level masks, and bounding boxes can be derived from the masks.

## Method

MEDSIGHT introduces two complementary modules integrated into a Qwen3-8B LLM via a progressive four-stage training pipeline. The Region Perceiver uses L=3 iterative layers of dual cross-attention (region-to-image and image-to-region) with a convolutional upsampling adapter, allowing 20 learnable region queries to capture both global semantics and fine-grained spatial detail; it is pre-trained with DETR-style segmentation (BCE + Dice + cross-entropy with Hungarian matching) on BiomedParse. A modality-aware region codebook discretizes the continuous region embeddings via vector quantization into 18 modality-specific sets of 32 codes (dimension 64), which are appended to the LLM vocabulary as learnable token embeddings initialized through the vision-to-text projector. Training proceeds as: (1) pre-train the Region Perceiver, (2) align visual features to language via a vision-to-text projector on 647K PubMedVision pairs, (3) learn the codebook and align text-to-vision grounding on 60K samples, and (4) jointly fine-tune the LLM, codebook, and both projectors on 72K instruction-tuning pairs (60K comprehension + 12K grounding), optimizing language modeling loss plus segmentation loss end-to-end.

## Results

On the proposed DiagSeg benchmark for grounded diagnostic segmentation, MEDSIGHT achieves an average diagnostic Recall of 58.9 and mean Dice of 69.9 across eight imaging modalities, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline MedPLIB (13.1 Recall, 31.8 Dice). On medical visual comprehension, MEDSIGHT attains a 62.3 average across VQA-RAD, SLAKE, PathVQA, MMMU-Med, and OmniMedVQA, surpassing the best comprehension-only baseline HuatuoGPT-Vision (58.3) while using only 72K instruction-tuning samples versus 647K. On text-prompted segmentation (MeCoVQA-G), MEDSIGHT achieves 42.8 mean Dice, edging out MedPLIB (40.1) and BiomedParse (40.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the novel DiagSeg benchmark, MEDSIGHT dominates all baselines by a wide margin: it achieves 58.9 diagnostic Recall versus MedPLIB's 13.1 and OMG-LLaVA's 18.3, and 69.9 segmentation Dice versus MedPLIB's 31.8 and LISA's 31.8. Among unified comprehension-segmentation models (MedPLIB, OMG-LLaVA, MIMO), MEDSIGHT is the only one that jointly performs strong diagnostic reasoning and pixel-level grounding; segmentation-oriented models like LISA and GLaMM produce only segmentation tokens without meaningful diagnostic text. On visual comprehension (Table 2), MEDSIGHT (62.3 avg) outperforms all unified models (MedPLIB 37.3, OMG-LLaVA 41.0) and most comprehension-only models, though it trails Lingshu-7B (69.5) and HealthGPT-L14 (64.7), the latter of which was partially trained on the evaluation benchmarks. On MeCoVQA-G text-prompted segmentation, MEDSIGHT (42.8) narrowly beats MedPLIB (40.1) and BiomedParse (40.3), demonstrating cross-modality generalization despite several out-of-distribution modalities. Under LoRA fine-tuning (Appendix F.4), MEDSIGHT also outperforms LLaVA-Med, HuatuoGPT-Vision, and ExGra-Med on SLAKE, VQA-RAD, and PathVQA, confirming the architectural advantage persists under downstream adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEDSIGHT extends the DETR/Mask2Former lineage of region-query-based segmentation by embedding a dual cross-attention Region Perceiver directly into a medical LVLM, replacing the patch-level CLIP features that prior medical VQA models (LLaVA-Med, HuatuoGPT-Vision, HealthGPT) rely on. On the output side, it departs from the single-[SEG]-token paradigm used by LISA, GLaMM, OMG-LLaVA, and MedPLIB (which trigger an external SAM-Med2D module) by introducing a modality-aware vector-quantized codebook whose discrete tokens are native to the LLM vocabulary, enabling the model to generate multiple semantically distinct region codes in a single response. The progressive multi-stage training pipeline (Region Perceiver pre-training → codebook learning → bidirectional alignment → unified instruction tuning) is a novel integration strategy that addresses the representational-space mismatch between these components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying diagnostic reasoning and pixel-level segmentation in a single generative framework without an external segmentation module, MEDSIGHT addresses a clinically critical gap: models must identify what the abnormality is before localizing it, a workflow that prior benchmarks (MeCoVQA-G, BiomedParse) bypass by explicitly naming the segmentation target. The DiagSeg benchmark and the modality-aware codebook design are reusable contributions for the medical multimodal community. Open questions include codebook generalization to truly unseen modalities (the authors note template/void codes as future work), the ultrasound performance gap attributed to training-data overlap, and whether the 72K-sample data efficiency holds under broader evaluation. The reliance on heavily pre-trained backbones (UniMed-CLIP, Qwen3-8B) also limits the degree to which architectural gains can be isolated from backbone quality.
