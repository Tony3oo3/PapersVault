## Problem

Discrete protein sequence generative models typically initialize from uniform or masked-token noise, which erases the strong position-specific evolutionary constraints that define family membership. This forces the denoiser to reconstruct conserved residues from heavily corrupted states, resulting in poor family control (near-zero profile-HMM accuracy) and low structural plausibility. The paper asks whether encoding phylogenetic context directly into the generative prior can turn 'from-scratch synthesis' into structured mutation from an evolved scaffold.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a family-specific MSA and inferred phylogenetic tree to compute site-wise Dirichlet concentration parameters (α^(h,l) ∈ R^>0^K) via ancestral sequence reconstruction, which serve as the generative prior; an optional fitness score J(X) is used only during the rerouting phase. Output is a protein amino-acid sequence of variable length (gaps removed), produced by integrating a simplex-valued ODE from t=0 to t=1 and decoding each position by argmax over K=20 residues. Gap positions are fixed throughout generation via a per-family Bernoulli mask sampled from empirical gap rates, and the simplex state at each site is a 20-dimensional probability vector.

## Method

LineageFlow replaces the uniform/mask prior in Dirichlet flow matching with an ASR-derived ancestral prior: for each family, a maximum-likelihood tree (IQ-TREE, LG+Γ₄) is rooted via MAD, and marginal root posteriors from PAML are mapped to Dirichlet concentrations (α = ε + λ·p_root, λ=10). A shared ESM2-650M transformer encoder is trained as a classifier p̂_θ(X₁|X_t, t) predicting terminal residues, from which the lineage-specific drift field v^(h,l) = Σᵢ u_t^(h,l)(x|eᵢ)·p̂_θ(eᵢ) is reconstructed, where the analytic speed c_h^(l) depends on the family-specific α and target residue via the regularized incomplete beta function. At inference, a single intermediate-time 'rerouting' step (t_int=0.5) applies R rounds of mutate→select→amplify (token-Dirichlet mutation biased toward high-entropy sites, exponential reweighting by ESM2 masked pseudo-likelihood, systematic resampling), formalized as KL-regularized selection that maximizes expected fitness with minimal deviation from the proposal law. The paper proves the lineage-specific field satisfies the continuity equation on the simplex with zero boundary flux, and that rerouting yields the unique maximizer of the KL-regularized objective.

## Results

On Pfam-A RP35 (8,886 families, ~8.9M sequences), LineageFlow achieves 95.3% top-1 family validity (Acc_fam) and 99.0% Hit_any, approaching the 96.6%/100.0% of held-out natural sequences, while improving mean OmegaFold pLDDT to 76.6±13.9 (vs. 70.8 for the ASR-prior-only baseline and 69.6 without rerouting). Among foldable samples (pLDDT≥70), 86.2% have nearest-neighbor identity <0.8 and 48.9% <0.6, with 587 MMseqs2 clusters at 80% identity. Uniform- and mask-initialized baselines (DFM, EvoDiff) and the MSA-prompt baseline (PoET) all fail to produce family-recognizable sequences (Acc_fam=0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DFM (Stark et al., 2024) and EvoDiff (Alamdari et al., 2023), both of which use uniform or masked-token priors, LineageFlow wins decisively on every metric: family validity jumps from 0% to 95.3% Acc_fam, pLDDT improves from ~46 to 76.6, and scPPL drops from ~12.6 to 6.67, indicating that generic noise priors force the denoiser to reconstruct conserved residues from scratch and fail to produce family-recognizable sequences. Against PoET (Truong Jr & Bepler, 2023), which conditions on an MSA prompt, LineageFlow again dominates: PoET achieves 0% Acc_fam and 52.0 pLDDT versus LineageFlow's 95.3% and 76.6, suggesting that in-context MSA prompting does not translate into accurate family recognition under strict profile-HMM evaluation. Against ProtBFN (Atkinson et al., 2025), a large pretrained Bayesian flow network on ~8× more data (UniProtCC, 71M sequences), LineageFlow achieves higher pLDDT (76.6 vs. 71.9) and lower scPPL (6.67 vs. 5.91—actually ProtBFN is slightly better on scPPL), though ProtBFN cannot be family-conditioned so family validity is not comparable. The ASR-PSSM i.i.d. ablation (sampling directly from the ancestral prior without any learned flow) already reaches 92.8% Acc_fam, confirming that the lineage prior carries most of the family signal, while the learned flow plus rerouting adds ~7 pLDDT points and substantial novelty/diversity gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Dirichlet Flow Matching (Stark et al., 2024) and the general flow-matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023) to protein sequence generation by replacing the uniform simplex prior with phylogeny-informed ancestral priors derived from ancestral sequence reconstruction (ASR). The key conceptual departure is reframing family-conditioned generation as structured mutation from an evolved scaffold rather than from-scratch denoising under family-agnostic noise, and introducing 'rerouting'—a single intermediate-time mutate-select-amplify operator inspired by directed evolution—as an inference-time steering mechanism that avoids per-step predictor guidance. The paper positions itself against protein language models, discrete diffusion (EvoDiff), Bayesian flow networks (ProtBFN), and MSA-prompted generation (PoET), arguing that the choice of initialization prior, not the denoiser architecture or conditioning interface, is the dominant factor in family-conditional generation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ASR-derived priors alone (without any learned flow) already achieve near-natural family validity, while uniform/mask-initialized baselines fail completely even with explicit family labels, provides a clear diagnostic for why prior approaches struggle with family control and suggests that evolutionary structure should be injected at the prior level rather than learned by the denoiser. The zero-shot enzyme generation case study demonstrates practical utility for protein engineering, though all evaluation relies on computational proxies (OmegaFold pLDDT, ESM-IF perplexity) without wet-lab validation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to de novo design beyond family-conditioned generation, how it handles indels and variable-length evolution (currently masked out), and whether the rerouting mechanism generalizes to more complex multi-objective optimization beyond the single ESM2 plausibility score used here.
