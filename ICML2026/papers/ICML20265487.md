## Problem

Cross-task generalization in multi-agent reinforcement learning is hindered by a gap between existing inductive biases: entity-level permutation invariance preserves individual policy flexibility but discards inter-agent collaborative structure, while task-level sub-task decomposition captures coordination but fails when novel sub-tasks appear in unseen scenarios. The paper identifies this as a missing intermediate abstraction and proposes a role-level inductive bias that simultaneously encodes functional specialization and composable team coordination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265487/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a variable-length local observation decomposed into its own ego-features and a set of other-entity features (whose cardinality changes with team size), concatenated with its previous action. The method outputs a per-agent action, a discrete role label from K GMM components, and a continuous role embedding in R^16. Critically, the observation dimension is not fixed across tasks (N_src ≠ N_tgt), so the architecture must process arbitrary entity counts without retraining, and the role representations must remain valid when the atomic action space changes between source and target tasks.

## Method

GTR operates in three phases. First, a permutation-invariant attention encoder compresses variable-length observations into a fixed-size embedding, which is projected onto a GMM latent space (K learnable Gaussian components with a uniform categorical prior) via a factorized variational inference network using the reparameterization trick; a Utility-Weighted Determinantal Point Process (UW-DPP) then enforces diverse role assignment by maximizing the log-determinant of a kernel matrix weighted by per-agent role confidence, creating a self-regulating trade-off between exploration (early orthogonality) and cooperation (late redundancy). Second, a dual-stream regularization decouples role embeddings from environment-specific details: an Intention Decoder predicts coarse action categories (Idle, Self-Directed, Interaction) to discard task-specific execution, while an Influence Decoder predicts next-step role dynamics (via KL consistency with a stop-gradient target) and global reward (via mean-pooled team context) to ground roles in causal efficacy. Third, roles are injected into the policy as a perceptual attention filter and RNN conditioning, and into a permutation-invariant mixing network that replaces the fixed-dimension global state for credit assignment, enabling the CTDE framework to transfer across team scales.

## Results

On the SMAC zero-shot generalization benchmark (Table 1), GTR achieves the best or near-best win rate across 16 tasks with varying team scales and compositions. The advantage is most pronounced on unseen heterogeneous tasks: GTR reaches 0.91 on 8m_vs_9m (vs. 0.56 for RPG, 0.15 for HPN), 0.92 on 4s_vs_7z (vs. 0.58 for RPG, 0.10 for DT2GS), and 0.47 on the hardest task 4s5z_vs_4s7z (vs. 0.18 for RPG, 0.17 for DT2GS). On source tasks, GTR is competitive but occasionally slightly behind specialized baselines (e.g., 0.92 vs. 0.95 for HPN on 5m_vs_6m), confirming that the role-level bias trades a small in-distribution cost for substantially improved out-of-distribution transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTR is compared against four entity-level baselines (ASN, UPDeT, HPN, RPG) and one task-level baseline (DT2GS) on zero-shot transfer. RPG, the strongest entity-level method, is the closest competitor but is consistently outperformed by GTR on unseen tasks: the gap widens from ~2% on 3s_vs_3z (0.99 vs. 0.99) to 35% on 8m_vs_9m (0.91 vs. 0.56) and 29% on 4s_vs_7z (0.92 vs. 0.58). DT2GS, the task-level decomposition method, is competitive on some homogeneous tasks (e.g., 0.98 on 2s7z) but collapses on heterogeneous unseen tasks (0.10 on 4s_vs_7z, 0.17 on 4s5z_vs_4s7z), where GTR achieves 0.92 and 0.47 respectively. ASN and UPDeT are weakest overall, frequently scoring near zero on larger-scale unseen tasks. On source (training) tasks, GTR is occasionally 1–3% behind HPN or RPG (e.g., 0.92 vs. 0.95 on 5m_vs_6m), but this small in-distribution cost is more than offset by the large out-of-distribution gains. In fixed-task SMAC/SMACv2 experiments (Figure 4), GTR also outperforms role-based methods (RODE, ROMA, SIRD, ACORM, R3DM) and task-decomposition methods (LDSA, LSVQ) on high-difficulty maps, with the largest margins on stochastic SMACv2 scenarios where baselines overfit fixed trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions its contribution as a role-level inductive bias that bridges two existing lines of MARL generalization work: entity-level methods (UPDeT, HPN, RPG) that enforce permutation invariance but miss collaborative structure, and task-level methods (DT2GS) that decompose tasks into rigid sub-task modules with limited coverage. It builds on QMIX's monotonic value decomposition as the base RL framework and repurposes variational inference (GMM prior, reparameterization trick) and Determinantal Point Processes from other domains to construct a structured, transferable role space. The key departure from prior role-based MARL methods (ROMA, RODE, SIRD, ACORM, R3DM) is the explicit decoupling of role embeddings from environment-specific details via intention/influence regularization, combined with the UW-DPP mechanism that avoids the 'blind repulsion' problem of standard DPPs in cooperative settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-task generalization in MARL is a critical bottleneck for deploying multi-agent systems in dynamic real-world settings, and the role-level abstraction offers a principled intermediate representation that is both more flexible than fixed sub-task decompositions and more semantically rich than pure permutation invariance. The plug-and-play validation on VDN and QMIX suggests broad applicability beyond the specific architecture. However, the method's sensitivity to agent types (roles cannot transfer to unseen unit types) is a significant practical limitation, and the evaluation is confined to StarCraft II benchmarks, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to continuous-action or communication-based multi-agent domains. Future work on extensible role spaces and catastrophic forgetting during role-space expansion is needed before real-world deployment.
