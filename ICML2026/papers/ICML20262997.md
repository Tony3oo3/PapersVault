## Problem

Current multimodal speech-text models process all tokens through identical dense transformer parameters, ignoring the fundamentally different statistical properties of continuous speech features versus discrete text tokens. Standard Mixture-of-Experts routing is modality-agnostic, leaving untapped the potential to dedicate specialized expert capacity based on known input modality. No prior speech-text LLM combines a MoE architecture with explicit modality-aware routing while relying exclusively on open-source training data.

## Input / Output

MoST accepts interleaved sequences of continuous audio waveforms (encoded via frozen HuBERT at 25 Hz into 2048-dim embeddings) and text tokens, along with speaker embeddings for synthesis. It produces text transcriptions for ASR, synthesized waveforms via a HiFi-GAN vocoder conditioned on predicted HuBERT tokens and speaker embeddings for TTS, and text answers for spoken question answering. Unlike discrete-tokenization approaches (AudioLM, SpiritLM), MoST operates on continuous acoustic representations, preserving richer spectral detail.

## Method

The core contribution is the Modality-Aware Mixture of Experts (MAMoE) layer, which partitions N routed experts into disjoint text and audio groups and adds a parallel shared expert that processes every token. A modality-aware router computes standard softmax gating scores, then applies a binary mask based on the token's modality indicator (text=0, audio=1) to zero out scores for the wrong modality's experts before top-K selection, ensuring text tokens only activate text+shared experts and audio tokens only activate audio+shared experts. The shared expert output is added to the routed output, providing a cross-modal information bridge. Training follows a two-stage pipeline from DeepSeek-V2 Lite: 500k steps of cross-modal post-training on ASR/TTS data (LibriHeavy, Common Voice, VoxPopuli) to specialize the modality-specific experts, followed by 10k steps of mixed instruction fine-tuning on synthesized interrupted dialogues and TTS-converted text instructions, with ASR/TTS data mixed in to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

MoST achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance across all evaluated speech-text benchmarks. On ASR, MoST reaches 2.0% WER on LibriSpeech-clean and 6.2% on VoxPopuli (SOTA, ↓0.1 over previous best); on TTS, it achieves 10.1% WER on VoxPopuli (SOTA, ↓0.2). For audio language modeling, MoST attains 75.28 on sWUGGY (↑2.1), 83.64 on sTopic-StoryCloze (↑0.3), and 71.94 average (↑2.9), outperforming all dense baselines. On spoken question answering, MoST leads on WebQ (58.2 S→T, 44.7 S→S) and Trivial QA (32.1 S→S), and on text benchmarks it scores 66.9 on GSM8K and 58.2 on HumanEval, substantially exceeding other speech-native models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoST outperforms all nine baselines on the audio language modeling average (71.94 vs. 69.00 for Phi-4 Multimodal, 68.39 for LLaMA-Omni2, 65.19 for MinMo) and sets new SOTA on sWUGGY (75.28 vs. 73.21 for LLaMA-Omni2) and sTopic-StoryCloze (83.64 vs. 83.32 for SpiritLM). On ASR, MoST achieves the best VoxPopuli WER (6.2%) among all models that support both S→S and S→T, beating MinMo (6.7%) and SeamlessM4T-v2 (7.5%), though Qwen2-Audio and Phi-4 Multimodal report slightly lower LibriSpeech-clean WER (1.8%, 2.1%) but do not support speech-to-speech output. For TTS, MoST leads on VoxPopuli (10.1% vs. 10.3% for SeamlessM4T-v2) and LibriSpeech-clean (6.0% vs. 6.3% for SeamlessM4T-v2). On spoken question answering, MoST achieves the best WebQ scores (58.2 S→T, 44.7 S→S) and the highest Trivial QA S→S score (32.1), while on text benchmarks it surpasses all speech-native models on GSM8K (66.9 vs. 58.1 for MinMo) and HumanEval (58.2 vs. 42.3 for MinMo), though MinMo retains a slight MMLU edge (58.5 vs. 55.4). Overall, MoST is the only MoE-based model in the comparison and consistently matches or exceeds the strongest dense baselines across all task categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoST builds directly on the DeepSeek-V2 Lite MoE LLM as its backbone, adapting its sparse expert architecture for multimodal speech-text processing. It departs from the dominant paradigm in speech-text modeling (SpiritLM, Moshi, Qwen2-Audio) where all modalities are processed through identical dense transformer parameters, instead introducing modality-aware routing that partitions experts into text-specific and audio-specific groups with a shared expert for cross-modal interaction. While prior work has explored modality-aware expert grouping for image-text (MoMA, MoME), MoST is the first to apply this principle specifically to the speech-text domain, and the shared-expert-as-bridge design is novel in this context. The training pipeline—targeted ASR/TTS post-training followed by mixed instruction fine-tuning—follows the general LLM-adaptation recipe but is distinguished by its exclusive reliance on open-source data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoST provides the first fully open-source, reproducible pipeline for building a speech-text LLM on a MoE backbone, lowering the barrier for the community compared to models like SpiritLM and Moshi that depend on proprietary data. The controlled ablation (identical Llama3.2 3B initialization) cleanly isolates the architectural contribution of modality-aware routing from initialization quality, which is methodologically valuable. Open questions remain: whether the simple 50% index-based expert partition is near-optimal versus clustering-based or adaptive allocation strategies, how MAMoE scales to additional modalities (vision), and whether the shared-expert mechanism generalizes beyond the two-modality setting. The paper also acknowledges the societal risk of voice spoofing enabled by the TTS capability.
