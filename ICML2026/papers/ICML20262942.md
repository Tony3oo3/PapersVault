## Problem

Existing hallucination mitigation methods for LVLMs rely on external supervision (ground-truth captions, preference labels, human feedback) or post-hoc decoding interventions, leaving the model's internal tendency to favor language priors over visual evidence unaddressed during training. Recent analyses reveal that hallucinated tokens exhibit a characteristic layer-wise pattern—weak support in intermediate layers followed by amplification in final layers—but this internal signal has not been exploited as a training-time optimization objective. The paper asks whether this anchor-final probability discrepancy can serve as a self-supervised signal to directly reshape the model's generation dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with a short text instruction (e.g., "Describe the image.") and produces a free-form text caption or a binary yes/no answer for discriminative object-existence probes. Training uses 38,400 caption-free images from MSCOCO 2014, so no ground-truth captions or reference texts are required at any stage. The reward signal is computed entirely from the model's own intermediate-layer and final-layer probability distributions during GRPO rollouts, with no external evaluator, reward model, or annotation pipeline involved.

## Method

AFS constructs a token-level reward from three coupled components: (i) visual-selective gating that restricts optimization to a predefined set of visually descriptive tokens (identified offline via an LLM classifier), (ii) hallucination-aware classification that flags tokens whose maximum anchor-layer confidence falls in a moderate range (ε₁ < p_max < ε₂) while their final-layer probability exceeds it (negative anchor-final gap Δ_t), and (iii) a Jensen-Shannon divergence–based consistency score measuring alignment between the most confident anchor layer's full vocabulary distribution and the final layer's distribution. Hallucinated tokens receive a negative exponential reward scaled by their probability gap, while grounded tokens receive a positive reward, and the sequence-level reward (mean over gated tokens) is optimized via GRPO with group-relative advantages over 12 sampled completions. The key insight is that the layer-wise probability dynamics already encode hallucination signals internally, so the model can be trained to suppress late-stage language-prior amplification without any external supervision, ground-truth captions, or preference labels.

## Results

On MSCOCO caption generation, AFS reduces CHAIR_S from 28.8 (vanilla) to 24.6 and CHAIR_I from 9.1 to 7.7, while slightly improving recall from 64.3% to 64.6%. On AMBER-Generative, AFS lowers CHAIR from 5.2 to 4.5 and hallucination rate (Hal) from 28.2 to 24.5. On discriminative benchmarks, AFS achieves the best MME total score of 2341.44 and AMBER discriminative F1 of 88.2%, demonstrating transfer beyond captioning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFS outperforms all six baselines on CHAIR_S, with the closest competitor being MOCHa (25.6 vs. 24.6) and HA-DPO (25.8 vs. 24.6). On CHAIR_I, AFS ties HA-DPO at 7.7 and beats MOCHa (8.3) and all decoding-time methods (8.5–9.8). Among decoding-time baselines, DeCo is the strongest (CHAIR_I 8.5) but still trails AFS by 1.0; VCD, ICD, and DoLa all perform worse than vanilla on CHAIR_S, indicating that post-hoc interventions can inadvertently increase hallucination. On AMBER-Generative, AFS achieves the lowest CHAIR (4.5) and Hal (24.5), beating the best training-time baseline MOCHa (4.7 / 25.1) and HA-DPO (5.1 / 27.4). On discriminative tasks (Table 2), AFS attains the best MME total (2341.44) and AMBER discriminative F1 (88.2%), though Standard GRPO edges it slightly on POPE accuracy (87.81 vs. 87.31), suggesting AFS's gains are most pronounced on perception and reasoning rather than simple object-existence probing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes DeCo's decoding-time layer-contrast observation (hallucinated tokens show weak intermediate-layer support but final-layer amplification) as a training-time self-supervised reward signal, and couples it with GRPO as the optimization backbone. It departs from prior training-time hallucination mitigation (MOCHa, HA-DPO, RLHF-V) by eliminating the need for ground-truth captions, preference pairs, or external reward models, and from decoding-time methods (DeCo, VCD, ICD, DoLa) by internalizing the correction into model weights rather than applying it post-hoc. The visual-selective gating and Jensen-Shannon distributional consistency reward are novel components that make the self-supervised signal token-aware and layer-aware simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-supervised formulation removes the annotation bottleneck that limits scalability of prior hallucination-mitigation methods, and the demonstrated cross-task (captioning → VQA → reasoning) and cross-dataset generalization suggest the learned grounding behavior is not overfit to a specific format. However, the method requires access to intermediate-layer probabilities, restricting it to open-source architectures, and the self-supervised signal may inherit or amplify systematic misperceptions when the model's own visual encoding is consistently wrong. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to much larger models (70B+), whether the anchor-layer selection generalizes across architectures with different depth, and whether the visual token set V_vis remains stable as tokenizers evolve.
