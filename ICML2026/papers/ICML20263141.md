## Problem

Autoregressive LLMs trained on forward factual relations (e.g., 'Alice's husband is Bob') consistently fail to answer the reversed query ('Bob's wife is?'), a failure mode called the reversal curse. Prior work characterized this as a fundamental, inherent limitation of causal autoregressive architectures, implying that reversal knowledge must be explicitly included in training data. This paper challenges that view by showing the curse can be broken without any reversal training data, architectural changes, or loss-function modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263141/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a two-token prompt [entity, relation] (e.g., 'Bob', 'wife') and produces a single entity token as the next-token prediction (e.g., 'Alice'). Training data consists of forward relation pairs, identity bridge pairs (entity mapped to itself), and—critically in the OCR form—the identity bridge for entity a is rephrased as the composition of forward and reversal relations (e.g., 'The wife of Alice's husband is Alice' rather than 'The name of Alice is Alice'). The forward relation data is duplicated k times (k=6 in main experiments) to ensure the primary learning gradient comes from informative data while the identity bridge acts as regularization.

## Method

The paper augments the forward-relation training set with an 'Identity Bridge' dataset of the form A→A, but in a specific OCR-equivalent format where the identity relation for entity a is expressed as the composition of the forward and reversal relations (e.g., 'The wife of Alice's husband is Alice'). Theoretically, they prove via the implicit bias of gradient descent on a one-layer transformer that the nuclear-norm-minimizing SVM solution under this regularized dataset encodes positive weights in the upper-right block of W_OV, yielding a strictly positive margin on reversal queries—whereas the forward-only dataset yields a zero margin (the reversal curse). They further prove the regularized reversal task is formally equivalent to an out-of-context reasoning (OCR) problem, providing mechanistic insight into why the identity bridge works. Empirically, fine-tuning Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct with this recipe (OCR-6 format plus weight decay 0.30) achieves ~50% reversal accuracy on Husband-Wife and Parent-Child tasks, compared to near-zero for forward-only training, without any reversal examples in the training set.

## Results

On real-world reversal tasks (Husband-Wife and Parent-Child) with Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, the proposed identity bridge (OCR) method achieves approximately 50% reversal test accuracy, in stark contrast to the forward-only (FWD) baseline which stays at near-zero accuracy. In the one-layer transformer setting (N=10 pairs), the reversal MRR rises from the initialization level to full generalization when the identity bridge is added. The weight decay ablation identifies an optimal band around 0.30, yielding a peak reversal accuracy of 55%. Entity token length significantly affects performance: one-token number names reach ~100% reversal accuracy, while three-token names drop to ~10%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the standard forward-only training (FWD: training solely on 'The husband of Alice is Bob') and the proposed identity bridge regularized training (OCR: adding identity bridge data in the OCR-reformulated format with k=6 repetitions of the forward relation). FWD achieves 0% reversal test accuracy on both Husband-Wife and Parent-Child tasks, confirming the reversal curse, while the OCR method reaches ~50% reversal accuracy on both tasks. The paper further contrasts the OCR format against the direct identity (IDN) format ('The name of Alice is Alice'), showing that IDN fails to break the reversal curse (reversal accuracy remains near zero) while the OCR reformulation is essential for generalization. This highlights that the semantic reformulation of the identity bridge into its OCR-equivalent form is critical, not merely the addition of identity data. No other external baselines (e.g., reverse training, permutation training) are directly compared in the experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Identity Bridge data recipe from Lin et al. (2025), originally designed for two-hop reasoning, and reframes the reversal curse through the out-of-context reasoning (OCR) lens established by Cohen et al. (2024) and Huang et al. (2025b). It departs sharply from the dominant narrative in the reversal curse literature (Berglund et al. 2023; Zhu et al. 2024; Wang & Sun 2025), which treated the forward-reverse asymmetry as an inherent limitation of autoregressive causal models requiring architectural or objective-level changes. By proving via the implicit bias of gradient descent (nuclear norm minimization) that even a one-layer transformer can break the reversal curse with identity bridge regularization, the paper positions the problem as one of optimization geometry rather than model capacity, and shows the OCR equivalence is the critical mechanism enabling generalization in real LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result challenges a widely held assumption in the LLM community that the reversal curse is a fundamental, unfixable limitation of autoregressive training, offering instead a minimal, architecture-agnostic data recipe that requires no loss function changes or explicit reversal examples. However, the 50% accuracy ceiling (vs. ideal 100%) is attributed to an unavoidable shortcut-learning dynamic where the model first learns to copy the input entity name, and this gap has not been closed. Open questions include why one-token entities generalize near-perfectly while multi-token entities drop to ~10%, whether the effect scales beyond 1B parameters, and whether the OCR-form identity bridge can be integrated into large-scale pretraining pipelines without quality trade-offs.
