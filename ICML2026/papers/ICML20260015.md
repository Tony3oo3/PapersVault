## Problem

In RLHF and RLVR post-training, policies can exploit inaccuracies in proxy rewards (trained reward models, rule-based rewards, or LLM judges), leading to reward hacking where the proxy reward increases while true performance degrades. The standard mitigation is a KL penalty toward the initial policy, but this slows training, caps learning capacity, and is increasingly abandoned in favor of no regularization. The paper asks whether policy updates can instead be biased toward regions where the proxy reward is inherently more accurate, without constraining the policy to remain near its initialization.

## Input / Output

The method takes an autoregressive language model policy, a proxy reward function (trained RM, rule-based correctness/formatting reward, or LLM-as-a-judge), and a set of training prompts. For explicit GR, the method requires a second forward-backward pass at perturbed parameters (φ + ε∇J), reusing the same sampled actions from the original policy to avoid extra generation cost, with perturbations applied only to transformer blocks (not embeddings or output head). The output is a trained policy that achieves higher true reward while avoiding sharp reward maxima, effectively replacing the KL penalty with a gradient-norm regularization term.

## Method

The paper derives a theoretical chain linking three quantities: flatness of the policy optimum in parameter space, pairwise robustness in action space (low probability of δ-close action pairs with reward gap exceeding K), and excess Bradley-Terry loss of the proxy reward, showing that flat optima correspond to regions where the proxy reward is accurate. This motivates regularizing the policy gradient norm to bias optimization toward such flat optima. Two implementations are proposed: implicit GR via Reference Resets (iteratively resetting the KL reference to the current policy every R steps, which implicitly regularizes the gradient during later training phases), and explicit GR using a finite-difference estimate Δ_φ‖∇_φ J‖² = (∇_φ J(φ + ε∇_φ J) − ∇_φ J(φ))/ε with gradient clipping. Empirically, explicit GR outperforms KL penalty, Reference Resets, and no regularization across RLHF (TL;DR, AlpacaFarm) and RLVR (GSM8K, MATH) tasks, preventing format hacking, LLM-judge hacking, and over-focus on easy questions.

## Results

On the AlpacaFarm RLHF benchmark (Qwen 2.5), explicit GR achieves 18.5% (0.5B), 29.2% (1.5B), and 59.2% (3B) GPT-4.1-Nano-judged win-rate, consistently outperforming KL penalty (16.9%, 27.6%, 52.8%) and Reference Resets (17.4%, 27.1%, 49.2%). On TL;DR summarization, GRPO+Reference Resets reaches 78.1% win-rate with Pythia 1B, surpassing standard GRPO (62.2%) by 16 points. On GSM8K with an LLM-as-a-Judge reward, GR achieves 41.0% (0.5B) and 67.8% (1.5B) test accuracy versus 20.7% and 1.9% without regularization, demonstrating that GR prevents the judge-hacking that collapses true accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GR outperforms the standard KL penalty (Stiennon et al., 2020) across all model sizes on AlpacaFarm, with the largest gap at 3B (59.2% vs 52.8%, +6.4 points) and a consistent 1.6–2.1 point advantage at smaller scales. Against Reference Resets (Liu et al., 2025a), GR wins on all three sizes (e.g., 59.2% vs 49.2% at 3B), and the paper shows GR is more robust when the reward model is weaker. On TL;DR, GRPO+Reference Resets beats DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by 32.2 points (78.1% vs 45.9% on Pythia 1B) and TR-DPO (Gorbatovski et al., 2025) by 32.6 points, while also surpassing standard GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by 15.9 points on Pythia 1B, though it ties GRPO on Qwen 2.5 1.5B (82.0% vs 82.5%). In the LLM-as-a-Judge GSM8K setting, GR dramatically outperforms no regularization (41.0% vs 20.7% at 0.5B) and KL penalty (41.0% vs 24.2%), confirming that GR is the only method that both prevents judge-hacking and maintains high true accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes gradient regularization (GR) from the supervised-learning generalization literature (Karakida et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2022) and applies it to RL post-training of language models, specifically within the GRPO/Dr.GRPO policy-gradient framework (Shao et al., 2024). It provides a novel theoretical bridge connecting parameter-space flatness to proxy-reward accuracy via the Bradley-Terry loss, and reinterprets Reference Resets (Liu et al., 2025a) as an instance of implicit GR rather than a mechanism to prevent KL dominance. Conceptually, the paper departs from the standard KL-penalty framing (Stiennon et al., 2020) by arguing the goal should be to keep the policy in regions where the PR is accurate, rather than simply constraining divergence from the initial policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GR offers a principled, tunable alternative to the KL penalty that does not cap how far the policy can move from the base model, which is important as recent large-scale RLVR efforts (GLM-4.5, OLMo 3) have abandoned KL entirely and risk reward hacking with RMs and LLM judges. The theoretical link between gradient norm and BT loss gives practitioners a monitorable signal during training. Key open questions remain: the theory assumes continuous action spaces and a Lipschitz true reward, neither of which cleanly holds for discrete-token LMs; and GR could in principle favor flat but incorrect PR maxima, a failure mode the authors acknowledge but do not fully address. Whether GR scales to much larger models and more diverse task distributions beyond math and summarization is untested.
