## Problem

While self-attention dynamics have been studied as interacting particle systems, the geometric structure of the zero-temperature (hardmax) limit and its quantitative relationship to finite-temperature behavior remained uncharacterized. In particular, the discrete-time hardmax update had not been connected to a known optimization scheme, and it was unclear how long finite-β dynamics approximate the hardmax regime before eventual collapse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260065/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a finite set of token embeddings in R^d as initial configuration, together with a symmetric key-query matrix B^t (of fixed sign) and a scalar value matrix V^t = h^t I_d, and produces a complete dynamical characterization: an O(t^{-1}) energy convergence rate in the negative-semidefinite case, an explicit Voronoi cell partition with exponential convergence to vertices in the positive-definite case, and a metastability window of order exp(cβ) steps for the finite-β Markov chain. The outputs are structural (cell geometry, convergence rates, trapping times) rather than predictive, and the ODE well-posedness result provides a continuous-time counterpart to the discrete dynamics.

## Method

The central insight is recognizing the hardmax self-attention update as a Frank-Wolfe (conditional gradient) step for a quadratic objective over the convex hull of current token embeddings, with the value matrix acting as a preconditioner. When B is negative semidefinite, this recovers standard Frank-Wolfe convergence with O(t^{-1}) rate; when B is positive definite, the scheme operates on a concave objective and the authors prove that extending the argmax to the convex hull induces a B-Voronoi tessellation in which vertices are stationary and interior particles converge along straight lines to their cell's vertex at exponential rate. For finite β, the authors reformulate self-attention as a Markov chain via a Gumbel-trick-inspired construction and prove dynamic metastability: particles reach near-vertex configurations in a constant number of steps and remain trapped with high probability for times exponential in β, before eventual collapse to a single point in the initial convex hull.

## Results

For the negative semidefinite key-query case, the hardmax dynamics converge to a single cluster at the origin with the standard Frank-Wolfe sublinear rate O(t⁻¹) on the quadratic energy (Theorem 3.1). For the positive definite case, interior particles converge to their Voronoi cell vertices along straight lines with exponential rate ∏(1−γˢ), which is exponentially fast when inf γˢ > 0 (Theorem 4.2). At finite β, the Markov-chain formulation exhibits dynamic metastability: particles reach near-vertex configurations in O(1/γ) steps (Theorem 5.2) and remain trapped for times scaling as t ≲ γ⁻¹·e^{cβ} with high probability (Theorem 5.4), before eventual collapse to a single point in the initial convex hull (Proposition 5.1). The associated singular ODE is shown to be well-posed under the same geometric assumptions (Theorem 4.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without standard benchmark comparisons. The main comparison is against prior self-attention dynamics results: the O(t⁻¹) rate recovers and extends standard Frank-Wolfe convergence theory (Frank & Wolfe 1956; Jaggi 2013; Bach 2024) to the time-dependent, shrinking-convex-set setting of self-attention. The positive-definite case goes beyond existing Frank-Wolfe theory for concave objectives (Yurtsever & Sra 2022), which only provides coarse duality-gap bounds, by establishing an explicit Voronoi cell structure with exponential convergence. The metastability result extends prior work on the unit sphere (Geshkovski et al. 2024a; Bruno et al. 2025) to a non-spherical setting with a different normalization mechanism, and the ODE well-posedness result partially resolves an open question from Geshkovski et al. (2025). Numerical experiments (Section 6) confirm that the qualitative picture—vertex clustering, metastability, eventual collapse—persists under relaxed assumptions (non-diagonal value matrices, non-symmetric key-query matrices, added ReLU feedforward layers), though the cell geometry deforms and metastability shortens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of self-attention dynamics (Geshkovski et al., 2023; Sander et al., 2022) and convex optimization, identifying the hardmax (β→∞) self-attention update as a Frank-Wolfe step for a quadratic objective over the token convex hull. It extends the dynamic metastability results of Geshkovski et al. (2024a) from the unit-sphere setting to a non-spherical normalization, and partially resolves an open well-posedness question from Geshkovski et al. (2025) on the singular ODE limit. The Gumbel-trick Markov chain formulation provides a new probabilistic lens on the finite-β process, connecting attention scores to categorical sampling in a way that makes the metastability argument tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean geometric dictionary for attention: tokens move toward convex-hull vertices, the positive-definite regime induces a Voronoi partition with stationary vertices, and the hardmax dynamics faithfully approximate finite-β behavior for exponentially long horizons. This gives practitioners a principled justification for why attention concentrates on a small set of 'extreme' tokens, and suggests algorithmic shortcuts (e.g., tracking only hull vertices) for the O(n²) attention bottleneck. Key limitations include the restriction to single-head, symmetric, fixed-sign key-query matrices and diagonal value matrices; extending to multi-head (low-rank) settings and time-dependent parameters remains open, as does connecting the Frank-Wolfe perspective to training dynamics rather than inference.
