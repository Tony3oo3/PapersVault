## Problem

Existing neural symbolic regression methods linearize expression trees into token sequences, which obscures ancestor-descendant hierarchy and makes it difficult to enforce global structure-dependent constraints (e.g., forbidding specific operator compositions along root-to-leaf paths) during batched decoding. Additionally, hybrid neural-GP frameworks that directly imitate GP-refined elites as training targets suffer from distribution mismatch, since those elites are produced by an evolutionary procedure rather than sampled from the current neural policy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of input-output pairs {(x_n, y_n)} and a fixed symbol library (operators, variables, constants), and produces a symbolic mathematical expression as a preorder token sequence. Expressions are generated as node-attribute assignments on a fixed-topology Symbolic Perfect Binary Tree of depth D=5 (up to 31 tokens), with <empty> placeholders removed post-hoc to recover the valid expression. Continuous constants in the expression are treated as free parameters and optimized via BFGS after the structure is sampled, without backpropagating through the generator.

## Method

GCN-SR embeds variable-topology expression trees into a fixed Symbolic Perfect Binary Tree (SPBT) scaffold with <empty> placeholders, reformulating symbolic generation as node-attribute prediction on a shared graph. A 2-layer GCN with self-loops (SPBT-SL) autoregressively decodes symbols in preorder, using precomputed ancestor tables to enforce structural, syntactic, and semantic constraints via batched masking at each step. For training, Similarity-Weighted Policy Gradient (SWPG) runs GP refinement from the on-policy batch, selects top-K elites from the combined pool, and assigns each on-policy sample a reward weighted by LCS-based structural similarity to those elites—keeping the policy gradient strictly on-policy while letting GP discoveries shape credit assignment. Theoretical analysis shows the resulting gradient estimator is bounded with variance O(1/B), and ablations confirm that GP refinement, global semantic constraints, and similarity-weighted reward shaping each contribute substantially to exact recovery.

## Results

GCN-SR achieves the highest exact symbolic recovery rate across all three standard benchmarks and all data scales (N ∈ {20, 100, 1000}): 93.3%±23.1% on Nguyen, 90.0–91.8% on Livermore, and 100% on R*. On the 100 Feynman equations (0.1% noise), GCN-SR recovers 61/100, outperforming PySR (53/100), uDSR (40/100), and AI Feynman (38/100). Ablations confirm that GP refinement contributes the largest single gain (removing GP drops Livermore recovery from 89.5% to 29.5%), while SWPG's similarity-weighted reward shaping adds ~9–13 points over raw-reward or PQT alternatives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCN-SR outperforms all sequence-based neural baselines (DSR, DGSR) by a wide margin on Livermore (90–92% vs. 29–30% for DSR; 77–79% for DGSR), where structural complexity and global constraints matter most. Against the closest hybrid competitor NGGP (91.7% on Nguyen, 76–80% on Livermore), GCN-SR gains 1.6 points on Nguyen and 11–15 points on Livermore, attributed to constraint-aware SPBT decoding and SWPG's on-policy reward shaping versus NGGP's Priority Queue Training. On R*, GCN-SR achieves 100% recovery at all data scales, matching RSRM (which also reaches 100% at N≥100) but surpassing it at N=20 (100% vs. 96.7%). On the 100 Feynman equations, GCN-SR (61/100) leads PySR (53/100) by 8 equations and uDSR (40/100) by 21, while standard GP (18/100) and GP-GOMEA (23/100) trail substantially. The ablation replacing SWPG with PQT (Model 5) drops Livermore recovery from 89.5% to 82.3%, confirming that the similarity-weighted, strictly on-policy update is a meaningful advantage over direct elite imitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid neural-evolutionary symbolic regression line initiated by DSR (Petersen et al., 2021) and NGGP (Mundhenk et al., 2021), which both use policy-gradient-trained sequence generators coupled with GP refinement. It departs from DSR's RNN/Transformer sequence interface by replacing it with a GCN that decodes node attributes on a fixed-topology Symbolic Perfect Binary Tree (SPBT) scaffold, and from NGGP's Priority Queue Training by introducing SWPG, which uses GP-refined elites only to construct similarity-weighted rewards for on-policy samples rather than as direct imitation targets. The constraint-aware decoding enabled by the explicit tree representation is a practical departure from prior sequence-based SR methods, which can only enforce local (parent-conditioned) masking during autoregressive generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SPBT scaffold provides a clean, batch-compatible solution to the long-standing problem of vectorizing variable-topology tree generation, and the global semantic constraints it enables (e.g., forbidding trigonometric nesting or exp-log cancellation along ancestor paths) address a real gap in prior neural SR methods. The SWPG formulation offers a principled way to exploit GP discoveries without the distribution-mismatch instability of elite-matching objectives. Key limitations include the exponential growth of SPBT size with depth (capping expressivity at moderate tree depths) and the reliance on a fixed symbol library; the authors note that future work could use LLMs to extract domain-specific constraints from scientific literature, and adaptive or sparse tree representations could improve scalability.
