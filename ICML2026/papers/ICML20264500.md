## Problem

Existing mechanistic interpretability methods for protein language models, such as sparse autoencoders, decompose hidden states into interpretable features but treat each layer independently, failing to capture the cross-layer computation that drives pLM predictions. This limitation prevents them from serving as faithful replacement models or recovering the full computational circuits responsible for protein structure and function predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264500/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes protein sequences (up to 1022 residues) processed through ESM2, extracting residual stream activations at each transformer layer. It produces sparse latent vectors (TopK with k=16 per layer, d_latent=3200 for ESM2-8M) that reconstruct MLP outputs, a compressed circuit (a minimal subset of latents using <1% of the total latent space that recovers ≥70% of original model performance), steered protein variants constrained to ≤5 mutations from wildtype, and sparse graph visualizations of inter-layer latent dependencies.

## Method

ProtoMech builds cross-layer transcoders (CLTs) where each layer's MLP output is reconstructed as a weighted sum of sparse latent vectors from ALL preceding layers, rather than only the current layer's latents as in per-layer transcoders. The encoder applies TopK sparsity to residual stream activations, while the decoder uses matrices W_dec^{l'→l} mapping latents from every prior layer l' to the current layer's output. Training minimizes MSE reconstruction error plus an auxiliary loss that forces dead latents to activate, using 5M UniRef50 sequences. Circuit discovery employs iterative greedy search over gradient-based attribution scores to identify the minimal latent subset recovering task performance, and steering clamps selected circuit latents to high activations within the CLT replacement model (with attention outputs held at ground-truth values) to generate high-fitness variants.

## Results

On protein family classification (Swiss-Prot/InterPro), ProtoMech recovers 89% of ESM2's performance (F1 0.82 vs. 0.92), while compressed circuits using only ~0.8% of the latent space retain 79% of model accuracy. On function prediction (12 ProteinGym DMS assays), ProtoMech recovers 82% of ESM2's Spearman correlation (0.41 vs. 0.50), with circuits using ~0.9% of latents retaining 74% of performance. In steering experiments across 7 DMS assays, ProtoMech generates the top-fitness variant in 71% of cases, outperforming PLT, CAA, and random mutation baselines on all reported metrics (mean, max, top-10%, top-20% fitness scores).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProtoMech outperforms the CAA baseline (Huang et al., 2025) across all seven DMS assays and all four fitness metrics; for example, on GFP_AEQVI_Sarkisyan_2016, ProtoMech achieves a top-10% score of 4.56 vs. CAA's 3.16, and on A4_HUMAN_Seuma_2022, ProtoMech reaches 0.76 vs. CAA's −1.87. The authors attribute CAA's weakness to data scarcity (as few as 1,000 high-fitness sequences) and mutation-locality, causing the concept vector to overfit to a narrow region around the wildtype. Against the black-box optimizers PEX (Ren et al., 2022) and GGS (Kirjner et al., 2024), ProtoMech circuit steering outperforms both on the SPG1 and HIS7 DMS assays across all reported metrics, despite ProtoMech not being designed as an optimizer. ProtoMech also beats the PLT (per-layer transcoder) baseline in 71% of steering cases, including generating the single highest-fitness variant, though PLT occasionally matches or slightly exceeds ProtoMech on individual assays (e.g., PLT achieves a higher max score of 5.15 vs. 4.63 on GFP). Overall, circuit-based methods (both ProtoMech and PLT) consistently dominate CAA and random mutation, confirming that sparse, biologically disentangled latent representations enable more targeted and effective protein design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the circuit tracing framework from Ameisen et al. (2025) and the transcoder formulation from Dunefsky et al. (2024) from natural language models to protein language models, specifically ESM2. It departs from the dominant SAE-based interpretability approach for pLMs (Adams et al., 2025; Simon & Zou, 2025), which decomposes single activation states into features but cannot capture cross-layer computation or serve as a replacement model. The key methodological adaptation is anchoring circuit discovery to supervised biological tasks (protein family classification, DMS fitness prediction) rather than next-token prediction, which the authors argue is more appropriate for masked pLMs encoding global structural and functional properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProtoMech provides the first principled framework for tracing computational circuits in pLMs, demonstrating that <1% of the latent space suffices to recover ~79% of model performance while revealing known biological motifs (HRD catalytic loop, Rossmann fold, GB1 hydrophobic core). The denoising observation—that sparse circuits occasionally outperform the full model in poor-performance regimes—suggests potential utility as a regularizer beyond interpretability. Key limitations include reliance on manual biological annotation, validation only on ESM2 (a masked LM), O(L²) parameter scaling for CLTs, and the open challenge of extending to autoregressive or diffusion-based pLM architectures.
