## Problem

As the pool of available LLMs grows, users face the challenge of selecting a single model for sustained deployment when their true preferences are latent, multi-objective, and difficult to articulate upfront. Existing LLM routing methods operate at the per-query level with externally specified utility functions and do not model persistent, user-specific latent preferences that must be inferred through limited interactive comparisons under explicit cost and time budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263431/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CUPID consumes sequential pairwise preference feedback (which of two LLM responses the user preferred on the same prompt), optional free-form natural language cues (e.g., 'I need a cheaper model'), and provider-supplied LLM metadata (cost, capabilities, context window). The user specifies a total monetary budget B and an interaction budget T rounds upfront. The system outputs a single selected LLM for sustained deployment, a posterior belief vector over K latent objectives, and per-LLM HEART-UCB utility scores. Notably, the user never explicitly states their objectives; they are inferred entirely from comparative feedback, and the language feedback is optional and unstructured.

## Method

CUPID's core algorithm, HEART-UCB, places a Gaussian process prior over a latent utility function f(z,a) that is conditioned on an unobserved multi-objective state z, and models pairwise preferences via a probit likelihood. A belief vector over K latent objectives is maintained on the probability simplex and updated via Bayes' rule after each comparison; the UCB score for each LLM is obtained by marginalizing the GP posterior mean and variance over all latent objectives (belief-aware UCB). An auxiliary LLM converts optional natural language feedback into arm-level binary indicators that are added as a scaled bias term, with the scaling factor adapted to the current spread of UCB scores. A time-varying resource penalty, driven by a knapsack-inspired update rule, penalizes expensive model pairs when spending exceeds a target rate, and a cooldown term discourages repeatedly selecting the same LLM. The intractable GP posterior under the probit likelihood is handled via Laplace approximation, yielding closed-form posterior predictive probabilities for any pair of LLMs.

## Results

On the primary multi-objective LLM selection benchmark (25 OpenAI models, 4 user-type setups), CUPID achieves 100% convergence percentage (CP) across all setups while attaining the lowest or near-lowest cost: $0.056 (A1, K=5), $0.035 (A2, K=7), $0.070 (A3, K=9), and $0.063 (A4, K=5), with convergence in 1–2.4 rounds. In the cross-provider experiment (17 models from diverse providers), CUPID reaches 100% CP at $0.031 cost versus 20% CP at $0.045 for LMArena. On the AIME 2025 robustness test with out-of-context objectives, CUPID achieves a perfect 100.0 normalized score at $0.0279 cost, an 86.5% cost reduction relative to LMArena.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CUPID is compared against two main baselines: ε-greedy dueling bandits (Yue et al., 2012) and LMArena (Chiang et al., 2024). Against LMArena, CUPID dominates on convergence reliability (100% CP vs. 20–100% for LMA) and cost (e.g., $0.056 vs. $0.115 in Setup A1; $0.070 vs. $0.125 in Setup A3), while matching or exceeding quality in human studies. Against ε-greedy, CUPID achieves strictly higher convergence (100% vs. 80% in A1, A3, A4) at comparable or slightly higher cost (e.g., $0.056 vs. $0.055 in A1), and in the cross-provider experiment CUPID reaches 100% CP at $0.031 versus ε-greedy's 80% CP at $0.026. In the noisy-feedback robustness test, CUPID outperforms ε-greedy by +7.24 score while being 22.3% cheaper. In the out-of-context objective shift experiment, CUPID achieves a perfect 100.0 AIME score at $0.0279, improving over ε-greedy by +28.2 points at 87.8% lower cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

CUPID extends the dueling bandits framework (Yue et al., 2012) by introducing a latent multi-objective belief vector over user preferences, combined with a GP-probit preference model (Chu & Ghahramani, 2005) and Laplace-approximated posteriors (Williams & Barber, 1998). The exploration strategy builds on GP-UCB (Srinivas et al., 2010), while the budget enforcement mechanism reformulates knapsack-bandit penalties (Badanidiyuru et al., 2018; Immorlica et al., 2022) for the dueling setting. The key departure from prior work is twofold: (i) user objectives are treated as latent and inferred online rather than assumed known, and (ii) natural language feedback is integrated as a principled, bounded UCB bias rather than used for sentiment or context summarization. The paper explicitly positions itself against per-query LLM routing (FrugalGPT, RouteLLM, Hybrid LLM) as a complementary user-level, sustained-deployment matching problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the LLM pool grows and agentic deployments become more expensive, efficient user-level model selection under budget constraints becomes practically critical, and CUPID provides a theoretically grounded, interaction-efficient framework for this. The consistent 100% convergence across all experimental setups (vs. 0–33% for baselines) and the robustness to noisy language feedback and out-of-context objectives suggest the GP-belief architecture is genuinely informative rather than merely budget-aware. Key limitations include the GP's inability to analyze response content (only pairwise preferences), the reliance on LLM-as-a-judge for most automated evaluation, and the cold-start problem where the first round is driven entirely by language bias and cost penalty. Open questions include whether content-aware kernel design, informative priors from evolving benchmarks, and scaling to larger online participant pools would further improve convergence and generalization.
