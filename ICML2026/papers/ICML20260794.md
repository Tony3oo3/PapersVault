## Problem

No budget-feasible mechanism with provable approximation guarantees existed for submodular welfare maximization in procurement auctions, where a buyer with a finite budget must procure items from strategic sellers with private costs. The welfare objective v(S) − c(S) is substantially harder than valuation maximization because it can be negative and cannot be directly evaluated since costs are private, rendering existing budget-feasible mechanisms inapplicable. The only prior mechanism with approximation guarantees for welfare maximization (Deng et al., 2025) sacrificed budget feasibility, limiting its practical use.

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes a budget B, access to a submodular valuation oracle v(·), and mechanism parameters (α, β, ε, ℓ), and interacts with sellers exclusively through descending price offers in a clock auction format—sellers' private costs are never directly observed but are revealed only through accept/reject responses. It outputs a winning seller set S* and a per-seller payment p(u) for each winner, guaranteed to satisfy p(S*) ≤ B, v(S*) − p(S*) ≥ 0, and the weaker approximation guarantee v(S*) − c(S*) ≥ γ_w · v(O) − c(O) − ε/4. The running time is O(n log(OPT/ε)) for BFM-SWM and O(n log n) for the BFM-VM variant.

## Method

BFM-SWM is a multi-round descending clock auction that maintains ℓ = 2 disjoint candidate seller sequences and a singleton candidate u*. A geometrically increasing threshold ρ_t (starting at ε/α and multiplying by α each round) serves a dual role: it scales the price offers (p(u) = v(u|S)/ (β + ρ_t/B)) and acts as a welfare benchmark via a break condition that triggers when a candidate set's surplus exceeds ρ_t, ensuring budget feasibility. A control parameter β enforces a value-to-payment ratio of at least β, guaranteeing non-negative auctioneer surplus, while an owner index keeps the candidate sequences disjoint and a temporal protection on u* prevents premature exit of high-value singletons. The by-product variant BFM-VM (for valuation maximization) removes the welfare-specific components, sets β = 0, and initializes ρ_1 to max_u v(u), achieving a deterministic approximation ratio of 1/(12 + 4√3) ≈ 0.0528—substantially improving the prior best deterministic ratio of 1/64—while reducing running time from O(n² log n) to O(n log n).

## Results

On the influence maximization (welfare maximization) application across Slashdot, Email, and Epinions, BFM-SWM achieves social welfare v(S)−c(S) ranging from 1.22× to 26.41× that of the best-performing baseline at each budget level, with an overall average improvement factor of 4.49×. BFM-SWM also demonstrates superior computational efficiency, requiring fewer oracle queries to the valuation function than all baselines. On the crowdsourcing (valuation maximization) application using CIFAR-10, the by-product variant BFM-VM achieves an average 21.85% improvement in valuation over SIP and 5.27% over TripleEagleNm, while running significantly faster than SIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BFM-SWM consistently outperforms all three welfare-maximization baselines (Deng-Distorted, Deng-ROI, Deng-CostScaled) across all three social network datasets (Slashdot, Email, Epinions) and all budget levels, with an average welfare improvement of 4.49× over the best baseline. The baselines lack budget-feasibility guarantees, and even after truncating their solutions to satisfy the budget, BFM-SWM's provable approximation under budget constraints yields substantially higher welfare. For valuation maximization on CIFAR-10, BFM-VM surpasses SIP (Balkanski et al., 2022) by an average of 21.85% in objective value and also significantly outperforms it in oracle query count and wall-clock time, confirming the theoretical improvement from 1/64 to 1/(12+4√3)≈0.0528 deterministic approximation ratio. BFM-VM also edges out TripleEagleNm (Han et al., 2025), the best-known randomized mechanism, by 5.27% on average, demonstrating that the deterministic clock-auction design is competitive with or superior to randomized approaches in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: budget-feasible mechanism design for submodular valuation maximization (Singer 2010; Balkanski et al. 2022; Han et al. 2025) and regularized submodular maximization for welfare/profit maximization with public costs (Harshaw et al. 2019; Nikolakaki et al. 2021; Sviridenko et al. 2017). The key departure is that Deng et al. (2025) recently designed mechanisms for welfare maximization in procurement auctions but explicitly sacrificed budget feasibility, while all prior budget-feasible mechanisms relied on the non-negativity and direct observability of the objective—properties that fail for the welfare objective v(·)−c(·) with private costs. BFM-SWM bridges this gap by using a geometrically increasing threshold and a descending clock auction format (following Milgrom & Segal 2020) to circumvent the need to directly evaluate the welfare of candidate sets, while a by-product variant BFM-VM improves the deterministic valuation-maximization ratio from 1/64 to 1/(12+4√3) with reduced running time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first budget-feasible mechanism with provable approximation guarantees for submodular welfare maximization in procurement auctions, filling a gap that limits the practical applicability of prior welfare-maximizing mechanisms in real-world settings where buyer budgets are inherently finite. The welfare objective is more economically meaningful than pure valuation maximization as it promotes cost-efficiency and allocative efficiency, which is critical for sustainable procurement in AI-driven marketplaces (data acquisition, crowdsourcing). Open questions include extending the framework to Nash welfare objectives, XOS/subadditive valuations, and establishing lower bounds for budget-feasible welfare mechanisms with general submodular functions. The approximation ratios (0.0328 general, 0.0877 monotone) remain modest, and the empirical baselines for welfare maximization are limited to converted non-budget-feasible algorithms, leaving room for stronger comparisons.
