## Problem

Existing visual token pruning methods for VLMs rely on point-wise Top-K selection over spiky attention scores, which under high compression produces spatially fragmented token subsets (creating holes within objects) and systematically discards contextual background needed for spatial-relation and scene-level reasoning. The paper argues that pruning should instead respect the collective spatial structure of visual tokens, ensuring importance varies smoothly across coherent regions while maintaining explicit spatial coverage.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the visual token embeddings (Z ∈ R^{N×d}) and CLS self-attention from a CLIP-style ViT encoder (e.g., 576 tokens from a 336×336 image in LLaVA-1.5, up to 2880 tokens from 672×672 in LLaVA-NeXT) together with a text prompt. It outputs a condensed visual token sequence of K retained tokens plus two lightweight sink tokens (a mean and a residual anchor), which replaces the full N-token sequence in the LLM decoder's prefill and decoding stages. The entire pipeline is training-free, plug-and-play, and compatible with FlashAttention.

## Method

SpecFlow performs conservative token condensation in three stages. First, it seeds a restart-diffusion (personalized PageRank) process with CLS attention scores and propagates energy over a kNN graph built from token feature cosine similarity (not raw patch-to-patch attention), yielding a region-coherent importance field via spectral heat flow. Second, it reshapes the diffused energy into a 2D map and applies adaptive quadtree partitioning—splitting crops only where energy variance exceeds a threshold—then allocates per-region token budgets proportionally to energy mass (shown to be the unique proportional-fairness optimum). Third, rather than discarding pruned tokens, it compresses them into two coreset sink tokens (a mean and a max-residual anchor) that are appended to the retained sequence, preserving summary statistics and diversity at negligible overhead.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, SpecFlow achieves the best average relative performance across all three pruning ratios: 98.7% at 66.7% pruning (192 tokens), 97.8% at 77.8% (128 tokens), and 95.6% at 88.9% (64 tokens), outperforming all 10 baselines. At the most aggressive 88.9% pruning, SpecFlow retains 95.6% of the unpruned 576-token baseline while reducing end-to-end latency from 0.397 s to 0.229 s (1.73× speedup) and peak GPU memory from 19.2 GB to 14.8 GB. On LLaVA-NeXT (2880→320 tokens, 88.9% pruning), SpecFlow reaches 95.8% relative accuracy, and on video QA (MSVD-QA/MSRVTT-QA at 455 tokens) it achieves 63.4% average accuracy vs. 62.8% for HoloV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpecFlow outperforms all 10 baselines on average relative accuracy at every pruning ratio tested. At 66.7% pruning, the margin is narrow: SpecFlow (98.7%) edges out DART (98.5%) and HoloV (98.2%) by 0.2–0.5 points, while VisionZip (98.1%) trails by 0.6 points. The gap widens substantially under aggressive compression: at 88.9% pruning, SpecFlow (95.6%) beats HoloV (94.9%) by 0.7 points, VisionZip (94.5%) by 1.1 points, and DART (93.9%) by 1.7 points, while older methods like FastV (76.7%) and ToMe (70.1%) degrade severely. On LLaVA-NeXT (Table 2), SpecFlow (95.8%) surpasses HoloV (95.6%) and DART (93.9%), with a larger 2.5-point margin over FasterVLM (91.1%). On video QA (Table 3), SpecFlow achieves 63.4% average accuracy vs. HoloV (62.8%), SparseVLM (62.1%), and FastV (61.2%). On Qwen2.5-VL (Table 4), SpecFlow consistently beats HoloV across all three ratios, with the advantage growing from 1.3 points at 66.7% to 2.1 points at 88.9% pruning, confirming architectural generality beyond the LLaVA family.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpecFlow repurposes spectral heat diffusion (Coifman & Lafon, 2006) as a training-free importance propagation mechanism on a kNN token graph, departing from the dominant Top-K attention-ranking paradigm used by FastV, SparseVLM, VisionZip, and HoloV. It also draws on coreset theory (Feldman & Langberg, 2011) to justify its mean + residual sink tokens that aggregate pruned information rather than discarding it. The key conceptual departure is reframing token pruning as 'conservative condensation' with explicit spatial coverage guarantees via adaptive quadtree partitioning, rather than point-wise discrete selection on spiky attention scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpecFlow offers a practical, plug-and-play, FlashAttention-compatible recipe that retains 95.6% accuracy at 88.9% token reduction on LLaVA-1.5, making it immediately deployable for latency-sensitive VLM applications. However, the improvements over the strongest baselines (HoloV, VisionZip, DART) are modest (0.4–0.7% average), and the sink-token mechanism (one mean + one residual vector) is a very compact summary that may fail on fine-grained tasks like dense OCR or small-object detection. Open questions include extending the 2D quadtree to spatiotemporal partitioning for video, adapting to black-box VLM APIs that do not expose CLS attention, and whether adaptive sink counts or stronger coreset constructions can close the remaining gap under extreme compression.
