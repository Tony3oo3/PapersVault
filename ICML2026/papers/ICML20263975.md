## Problem

Structure-based and ligand-based drug design rely on disjoint data sources and modeling assumptions: SBDD requires predefined binding pockets and experimentally resolved structures, while LBDD leverages large-scale bioactivity data but cannot make pocket-specific predictions. No prior method efficiently unifies both data types in a single model that enables pocket-level predictions without relying on predefined pockets or computationally expensive docking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263975/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein 3D structure (geometric graph of Cα coordinates with ESM-2 residue embeddings, edges to 10 nearest neighbors within 10 Å) and a small molecule (2048-bit ECFP fingerprint concatenated with 210 RDKit descriptors, yielding a 2258-dim input vector). It produces a global protein embedding, K predicted pocket centers with confidence scores, K pocket embeddings, and a 2D-dimensional ligand embedding split into a protein-matching half and a pocket-matching half. The dual-component ligand embedding is the key structural choice: the protein-matching component scores virtual screening and target fishing, while the pocket-matching component enables ligand-conditioned pocket ranking via cosine similarity to individual pocket embeddings.

## Method

ConGLUDe extends VN-EGNN by adding a non-geometric virtual protein node that aggregates whole-protein information through two additional message-passing steps (residue→protein and protein→residue), yielding both a global protein embedding and N virtual pocket nodes whose coordinates and features are clustered via DBSCAN into K final pockets. A lightweight 2-layer MLP encodes ligands into a joint 2D embedding. Training alternates between structure-based batches (minimizing a geometric loss for pocket localization plus a three-way InfoNCE loss aligning concatenated protein-pocket representations with ligands, ligand protein-matching components with protein embeddings, and ligand pocket-matching components with the closest predicted pocket) and ligand-based batches (minimizing a sigmoid contrastive loss between the frozen protein embedding and ligand protein-matching components using active/inactive labels). The three-axis contrastive formulation—extending CLIP-style InfoNCE along a pocket dimension—is the core novelty, enabling the novel ligand-conditioned pocket selection task without explicit docking or co-folding.

## Results

On LIT-PCBA virtual screening, ConGLUDe achieves AUROC 64.06±3.25, BEDROC 12.24±2.06, and EF1% 11.03±1.81, matching the pocket-agnostic SPRINT (BEDROC 12.30, EF0.5% 15.90) while substantially outperforming all pocket-based contrastive methods (best: HypSeek AUROC 62.10, BEDROC 11.96). On the challenging out-of-domain Kinobeads target fishing task, ConGLUDe achieves AUROC 65.6 and EF1% 9.9, significantly outperforming the strongest baseline DiffDock (AUROC 58.9, EF1% 5.3; Wilcoxon p≈10⁻²⁴). For ligand-conditioned pocket selection, ConGLUDe attains top-1 DCC@4Å of 0.54 (PDBbind Time), 0.27 (PoseBusters), and 0.35 (ASD), setting state-of-the-art on all three benchmarks. Binding site prediction performance matches the original VN-EGNN (e.g., 0.689 vs 0.669 DCC on PDBbind2020).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConGLUDe substantially outperforms all pocket-based contrastive baselines (DrugCLIP, DrugHash, S²Drug, LigUnity, HypSeek) on LIT-PCBA virtual screening, achieving BEDROC 12.24 vs. their range of 6.23–11.96, and EF1% 11.03 vs. 5.51–6.81. Against the pocket-agnostic SPRINT, ConGLUDe matches on BEDROC (12.24 vs. 12.30) and EF0.5% (15.87 vs. 15.90) and slightly exceeds it on EF1% (11.03 vs. 10.78), though SPRINT achieves a notably higher AUROC (73.40 vs. 64.06); however, ConGLUDe additionally provides pocket-level predictions and target fishing capabilities that SPRINT lacks. On target fishing (Kinobeads), ConGLUDe dominates all baselines: AUROC 65.6 vs. DiffDock 58.9, DrugCLIP+VN-EGNN 54.2, DrugCLIP+P2Rank 51.2, and SPRINT 42.5, with the gap over DiffDock being highly significant (p≈10⁻²⁴). For ligand-conditioned pocket selection, ConGLUDe (0.54/0.27/0.35 DCC@4Å) beats the best two-step baseline Vina+VN-EGNN (0.52/0.25/0.31) on all three datasets, with the largest margin on the allosteric ASD benchmark (0.35 vs. 0.31). On binding site prediction, ConGLUDe essentially ties VN-EGNN (0.602 vs. 0.605 on COACH420; 0.689 vs. 0.669 on PDBbind2020), confirming that the architectural extensions for contrastive learning do not degrade the geometric pocket-prediction capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConGLUDe extends VN-EGNN (Sestak et al., 2025) by adding a non-geometric virtual protein node and coupling the geometric encoder with a CLIP-style three-way contrastive loss that simultaneously aligns ligands with whole-protein, pocket, and concatenated protein-pocket representations. It departs from prior contrastive drug design methods (DrugCLIP, DrugHash, S2Drug, LigUnity, HypSeek) that require predefined binding pockets as input, and from pocket-agnostic methods (SPRINT) that lack pocket-level specificity, by unifying structure-based and ligand-based training in a single end-to-end model that predicts pockets implicitly. The three-axis InfoNCE formulation generalizes the standard two-way CLIP loss to a third axis over multiple candidate pockets on the same protein, enabling the novel ligand-conditioned pocket selection task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ConGLUDe demonstrates that a single contrastive geometric model can serve as a general-purpose tool across virtual screening, target fishing, binding site prediction, and ligand-conditioned pocket selection while being orders of magnitude faster than docking or co-folding, positioning it as a practical step toward foundation models for drug discovery. Key limitations include the inability to generate explicit docked conformations, uncertain performance on AlphaFold-predicted structures versus experimentally resolved ones, and the assumption of uniquely associated protein targets in ligand-based data. Open questions include whether the framework extends to phenotypic or target-agnostic assays, whether generative models can be integrated for joint ligand-pocket design, and whether the unified training paradigm generalizes to multi-target or multi-state proteins.
