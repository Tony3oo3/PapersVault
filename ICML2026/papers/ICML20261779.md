## Problem

Existing preference optimization frameworks (DPO, SimPO, IPO) treat all preference pairs with equal confidence, regardless of whether annotators unanimously agree or heavily disagree. This uniform treatment forces models to overfit to inconsistent supervision signals from ambiguous comparisons, degrading alignment quality. The paper addresses how to explicitly model annotator reliability and annotation consistency to produce more robust preference optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261779/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-annotator preference comparisons where each sample has M annotators providing scores or direct preference labels, projected to a 3-scale vote space {-1, 0, +1} (prefer A, tie, prefer B). It also requires a base LLM (Llama-3-8B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) fine-tuned via LoRA. Beyond the aligned policy, the framework outputs per-annotator reliability scores in [0,1], MAP-estimated latent preference labels per comparison, and per-sample tanh-scaled consistency weights that modulate the training gradient. Tie-labeled comparisons are filtered from training since preference optimization requires a strict direction.

## Method

RGPO consists of two modules. First, Iterative Latent Reliability Estimation (ILRE) applies the Dawid-Skene EM algorithm to jointly estimate per-annotator confusion matrices (3×3, capturing systematic biases like tie-aversion) and latent true preference labels, yielding a scalar reliability score q_j per annotator as the mean diagonal of their confusion matrix. Second, Reliability-Aware Consistency Optimization (RACO) computes a per-sample consistency weight by taking the binary entropy of reliability-weighted directional votes (excluding ties), then maps it through a tanh scaling to [0,1]. This weight multiplies the standard DPO loss as a sample-specific gradient scaler, so high-consensus pairs receive full optimization strength while contentious pairs are down-weighted. The key distinction from uniform ensemble or confidence filtering is that reliability is inferred statistically from the annotation pattern itself rather than assumed or self-reported, and the weighting operates at the gradient level rather than merely filtering samples.

## Results

On MultiPref with Llama-3-8B-Instruct, RGPO-enhanced DPO achieves 38.30% LC Win Rate on AlpacaEval 2 (vs. 35.55% for standard DPO) and 46.60% on Arena-Hard (vs. 43.70%). The largest gains appear with SimPO on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, where RGPO lifts AlpacaEval 2 LC Win Rate from 73.09% to 80.05% and Arena-Hard to 85.20%. On the more challenging HelpSteer2-Disagreement dataset, RGPO stabilizes SimPO dramatically, recovering Llama-3's Raw Win Rate from 12.31% to 25.60% and LC Win Rate from 28.62% to 40.32%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGPO consistently outperforms all three baselines (DPO, SimPO, IPO) across both datasets and both base models. Against DPO, RGPO yields the most consistent moderate gains: +2.75 pp LC Win Rate and +2.90 pp Arena-Hard on MultiPref (Llama-3), with similar improvements on HelpSteer2. Against SimPO, the gains are largest and most dramatic—on MultiPref with Qwen2.5-7B, RGPO adds +6.96 pp LC Win Rate (73.09→80.05%) and +2.40 pp Arena-Hard (82.80→85.20%); on HelpSteer2 with Llama-3, it recovers a collapsed model from 12.31% to 25.60% Raw Win Rate (+13.29 pp) and from 28.62% to 40.32% LC (+11.70 pp). Against IPO, improvements are smaller but still positive: +1.20 pp Arena-Hard on MultiPref (51.90→53.10%) and +1.80 pp on HelpSteer2 (40.00→41.80%). Notably, RGPO's advantage is most pronounced on the noisier HelpSteer2-Disagreement dataset, where standard SimPO catastrophically underperforms the base model, confirming that reliability-aware weighting is most critical when annotation inconsistency is highest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Dawid-Skene EM framework from the crowdsourcing literature (Dawid & Skene, 1979; Raykar et al., 2010) to the LLM preference-optimization setting, where prior work (DPO, SimPO, IPO) treats all preference pairs as equally reliable regardless of annotator disagreement. It departs from the standard uniform-ensemble aggregation by jointly estimating latent ground-truth labels and per-annotator confusion matrices, then uses the resulting reliability scores to compute a per-sample consistency weight that modulates the DPO/SimPO/IPO loss. The method is explicitly positioned as a drop-in enhancement to existing preference-optimization objectives rather than a new optimization paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Annotation inconsistency is a well-acknowledged but under-addressed source of noise in RLHF pipelines, and RGPO provides a principled, plug-and-play mechanism to down-weight unreliable supervision without requiring new model architectures or additional annotation. The finding that SimPO's catastrophic instability on noisy data (12.31% win rate) is largely resolved by consistency weighting (25.60%) suggests that much of the perceived fragility of reference-free objectives may stem from data quality rather than the loss function itself. Key open questions include whether the EM-based reliability estimation scales meaningfully to larger models (only 7–8B LoRA experiments are reported), whether gains persist on datasets with true annotator metadata at scale, and whether the ~3× increase in identified tie samples (12%→29% on MultiPref) reflects genuine ambiguity or over-conservative filtering that discards learnable signal.
