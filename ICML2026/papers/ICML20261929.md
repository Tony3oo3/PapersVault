## Problem

Existing 3D assembly methods formulate the task as pure pose estimation, rearranging observed fragments via rigid transformations but unable to synthesize missing geometry. The paper asks whether 3D assembly and whole-shape generation can be unified into a single coupled process where each provides priors the other lacks: assembly supplies part-level structural evidence for generation, while generation injects holistic shape context that resolves pose ambiguities, especially under missing or eroded fragments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261929/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N variable-size fragment point clouds (each normalized to [-0.5, 0.5]³, with points sampled proportional to surface area and downsampled by a factor of 4) and an optional reference image encoded by a frozen DINOv2. It simultaneously outputs an SE(3) pose (rotation via SO(3) geodesic and translation) for every input fragment and a complete 3D shape decoded from the shared VAE latent via SDF evaluation, effectively hallucinating geometry for any missing parts. The number of fragments ranges from 2 to 20 in experiments.

## Method

CRAG employs a joint flow-matching framework with a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture: an Assembly Branch (21-layer DiT, 768-dim) predicts per-fragment SE(3) velocity fields, and a Generation Branch (21-layer TripoSG DiT, 2048-dim) predicts latent-space velocity for the whole shape. Both branches share the TripoSG VAE as a common embedding space, with fragments encoded via the VAE encoder and the decoded latent z̃ used as the fragment feature to align the two branches. A Joint Adapter at every layer performs symmetric bidirectional cross-attention (generation queries fragments, fragments query generation) with zero-initialized output projections to preserve pretrained priors at the start of training. Training is two-stage: 100k steps of assembly-only warm-up with uniform timestep sampling, followed by 150k steps of joint fine-tuning with the generation branch activated and classifier-free guidance dropout increased from 10% to 50% to strengthen reliance on part-level structural evidence.

## Results

On PartNeXt (Complete), CRAG achieves 65.89% Part Accuracy and 2.40 Chamfer Distance, outperforming GARF (58.19% PA, 3.10 CD), RPF (46.17% PA, 10.46 CD), and Assembler (44.18% PA, 27.93 CD). On Breaking Bad (Complete), CRAG reaches 94.68% PA with 0.21 CD, surpassing GARF (93.56% PA, 0.42 CD) and RPF (80.23% PA, 1.01 CD). Under the challenging Missing-part setting, CRAG maintains 71.81% PA on PartNeXt and 92.07% PA on Breaking Bad, while Assembler degrades sharply to 49.86% and 45.93% respectively. CRAG also eliminates the 25% VAE-decoding failure rate of image-only TripoSG, achieving 0% failure while improving generation quality (F-Score@1% from 14.63% to 20.49%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRAG outperforms all baselines across every metric and setting. Against GARF (the strongest pose-estimation baseline), CRAG w/o img improves PartNeXt Complete PA from 58.19% to 61.67% and CD from 3.10 to 3.31 (slightly worse CD but better PA), while full CRAG pushes PA to 65.89% and CD to 2.40. Against RPF, CRAG achieves substantially lower translation error (9.13 vs. 29.54 on PartNeXt) and higher PA (65.89% vs. 46.17%). The most dramatic gap is against Assembler on PartNeXt: CRAG increases PA from 44.18% to 65.89% and reduces CD from 27.93 to 2.40 (a 91.4% relative reduction), with the gap widening further under missing parts (PA 71.81% vs. 49.86%). On Breaking Bad, CRAG w/o img already matches or exceeds GARF (PA 94.64% vs. 93.56%), and full CRAG further tightens CD to 0.21. In the shape-completion comparison (Table 2), CRAG outperforms both SDFusion and MGPC even when those baselines receive oracle pre-aligned fragments, confirming that joint assembly-generation is more informative than a sequential pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRAG departs from the dominant formulation of 3D assembly as pure SE(3) pose estimation (GARF, RPF, Assembler) by coupling it with whole-shape generation in a single flow-matching loop. It builds directly on TripoSG's VAE and pretrained generative weights as a shared latent space, and on GARF's SE(3) flow-matching formulation for the assembly branch, but introduces a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture with a Joint Adapter that enables bidirectional information exchange between the two branches at every layer. This contrasts with sequential approaches like Jigsaw++ that apply shape completion as a downstream step, and with Assembler which conditions on images but still treats assembly and generation as decoupled sub-problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRAG is the first method to simultaneously assemble observed fragments and synthesize missing geometry, which is critical for real-world applications in archaeology, paleoanthropology, and medicine where specimens are routinely incomplete. The paper's comparison showing that joint assembly-and-generation outperforms a sequential pipeline even when the latter receives oracle assembly suggests that the coupling itself provides structural information beyond what either task contributes independently. Key limitations include distribution bias in PartNeXt, metrics that miss semantic correctness under symmetry (e.g., swapping identical legs), and reliance on image conditioning rather than richer multimodal controls like sketches or language, all of which point to important open questions for the field.
