## Problem

Black-box adversarial attacks on long-term time series forecasting (LTSF) models remain largely unexplored, as existing classification-oriented attack paradigms fail to address three fundamental impediments: temporal redundancy that suppresses local perturbations, the continuous regression manifold that resists discrete boundary-crossing strategies, and the ambiguity of quantifying attack success in point-forecasting settings. No systematic query-efficient framework existed for exploiting spectral vulnerabilities in deployed LTSF APIs under strict query budgets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{T×F} (T=96 input length, F channels), a small domain-relevant calibration set (N_cal=50 samples) for one-time offline sensitivity profiling, and interacts with the target model solely through input-output queries (200-query online budget). It produces an L∞-bounded adversarial perturbation δ (‖δ‖∞ ≤ ε, ε=0.1) reconstructed via inverse DFT from frequency-domain optimization variables, yielding a perturbed input X' = X + δ. Attack efficacy is measured by DTW and SME between the clean prediction f(X) and perturbed prediction f(X'), capturing geometric deformation and directional trend reversal respectively.

## Method

TSFAdv operates in the frequency domain via DFT parameterization, representing the perturbation as a 2TF-dimensional vector of real and imaginary spectral components. A one-time offline profiling phase perturbs amplitude and phase independently at each frequency bin (exploiting conjugate symmetry to halve the search space), measuring model response to build architecture-specific sensitivity maps S_amp and S_phase. These maps are combined into a complex sensitivity modulus |S_c| that serves as importance weights in a modified Natural Evolution Strategy: the NES search direction u_k is element-wise multiplied by the normalized sensitivity weight vector w̃ before gradient estimation, biasing stochastic sampling toward spectrally vulnerable components. This sensitivity-guided weighting is the key differentiator from vanilla NES, as ablation shows it contributes +37% DTW improvement over uniform-weighted frequency-domain NES, while the frequency-domain parameterization alone provides only the structural foundation without direct attack gains.

## Results

Under a 200-query budget with ε=0.1 (L∞), TSFAdv achieves median DTW improvements of 38.78% and median SME improvements of 26.47% over the best baseline (NES) across seven LTSF architectures and five datasets. Per-model average DTW improvements range from 21.91% (TimeXer) to 85.00% (DLinear), while SME improvements range from 15.04% (Autoformer) to 61.97% (DLinear). The largest single-dataset gain is 179.92% DTW improvement on DLinear/Weather, and TSFAdv maintains consistent advantages across all model–dataset combinations with only minor negative deviations on DLinear for ETTm1/ETTm2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSFAdv is compared against three baselines: random Gaussian noise, ZOO (coordinate-wise finite-difference gradient estimation), and NES (stochastic gradient estimation via isotropic Gaussian sampling). TSFAdv consistently outperforms all three baselines across every model–dataset combination on both DTW and SME. Against NES (the strongest baseline), TSFAdv achieves 21.91–85.00% average DTW improvement and 15.04–61.97% average SME improvement, with the largest gains on DLinear (85.00% DTW, 61.97% SME) and TimeMixer (50.45% DTW). Against ZOO, the gap is even wider, as ZOO's coordinate-wise approach is particularly inefficient in the high-dimensional frequency domain (e.g., ZOO achieves only 4.46 DTW on TimeMixer/Weather vs. TSFAdv's 93.58). The ablation confirms that the sensitivity-weighted weighting (not merely frequency-domain parameterization) drives the gains: removing sensitivity weights (V1) yields 11.5% lower DTW than full NES, while random weights (V2) perform worse than even uniform weights, validating that structured spectral priors are essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Natural Evolution Strategies (NES) from Ilyas et al. (2018) — originally designed for black-box image classification — to the fundamentally different setting of deterministic long-term time series forecasting, where the regression manifold's continuity and temporal redundancy make direct transfer infeasible. It departs from prior frequency-domain analyses in vision (Yin et al., 2019) and time-series backdoor attacks (Huang et al., 2025) by operating entirely in a black-box inference setting with no training-time access, using offline sensitivity profiling to guide gradient estimation. The paper also introduces trajectory-level evaluation metrics (DTW, SME) to address the evaluation ambiguity that makes point-wise regression metrics (MSE, MAE) ill-suited for measuring adversarial degradation in forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard defenses (randomized smoothing, magnitude pruning, adversarial training) are not merely ineffective but can actively amplify frequency-domain attacks (e.g., +38.2% SME under smoothing) reveals a critical gap in the security of deployed LTSF systems used in smart grids and energy infrastructure. The demonstration that spectral perturbations exploit architecture-specific vulnerabilities invisible in the time domain suggests that robustness evaluations relying solely on temporal noise are fundamentally incomplete. Open questions include developing frequency-aware defense mechanisms, extending attacks to longer horizons and multivariate cross-channel interactions, and understanding whether sensitivity profiles transfer across model families or domains beyond the ETT family tested here.
