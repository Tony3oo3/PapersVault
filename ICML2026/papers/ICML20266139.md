## Problem

Self-improvement training for large reasoning models (LRMs) often fails on complex reasoning tasks and can cause model collapse. The paper identifies two root causes: data imbalance (most self-generated training samples are simple while crucial hard samples are scarce) and overthinking (redundant, repetitive reasoning steps in self-generated solutions degrade both accuracy and efficiency).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266139/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base LLM, a small seed dataset (~1K samples) with reasoning trajectories, and a larger answer-labeled but reasoning-trace-free dataset (M >> N). It iteratively self-generates K=10 candidate solutions per query, then produces a filtered set of accurate, diverse, and concise training trajectories. The final output is an improved reasoning model that achieves higher accuracy while generating up to 42.4% fewer output tokens at inference time, evaluated zero-shot on multiple-choice and free-form reasoning benchmarks.

## Method

HSIR introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, VeriExit is a trajectory-recycling sampling strategy: for each failed solution, it scans intermediate reasoning steps for the ground-truth answer, truncates the trajectory at that point, inserts an exit prompt, and resamples only the final answer J times — salvaging partially-correct reasoning without costly full resampling. Second, InDiv (Intrinsic Diversity score) quantifies overthinking by computing an attention-weighted log-determinant of the centered cross-covariance matrix of hidden representations at the model's middle layer; solutions with low InDiv scores (indicating highly correlated, repetitive reasoning steps) are filtered below a threshold τ. These two components are applied within iterative SFT, DPO, and RLVR (H-GRPO) training loops, where H-GRPO additionally uses the normalized InDiv score as an extra reward term (weight ω=0.2) alongside accuracy and format rewards to encourage concise, diverse reasoning during reinforcement learning.

## Results

On the Qwen2.5 family (1.5B/3B/7B) evaluated on MedQA and GSM8K, HSIR-DPO (3 iterations) achieves 73.35% overall accuracy (+10.90% over SFT-Initial) while reducing output tokens by 42.4% (885 vs. 1,536). HSIR-SFT (3 iterations) reaches 71.33% overall accuracy (+8.88%) with 41.7% fewer tokens, surpassing even the SFT-Oracle upper bound (70.49%). Across four additional LLMs (Qwen3-1.7B, Phi-3.5-mini, Mistral-7B, LLaMA3-8B), HSIR-SFT brings +5.62% average accuracy gains and 22.4% token reduction in a single iteration. In the RLVR setting, H-GRPO improves over vanilla GRPO by +1.54% accuracy while cutting inference tokens by 27.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSIR-DPO (Iter 3) outperforms IRPO (Iter 3) by +5.35% overall accuracy (73.35% vs. 68.00%) and reduces tokens by 25.2% (885 vs. 1,271), confirming that data quality filtering is critical for preference learning. In the SFT setting, HSIR-SFT (Iter 3) beats ReST^EM (Iter 3) by +1.60% (71.33% vs. 69.73%) and STaR (Iter 3) by +6.51% (71.33% vs. 64.82%), while also surpassing the SFT-Oracle upper bound by +0.84%. Against RFT and ReGenesis, HSIR-SFT gains +3.91% and +5.74% respectively. In the RLVR setting, H-GRPO improves over vanilla GRPO by +1.54% accuracy (70.71% vs. 69.17%) with 27.1% fewer tokens; it also beats the length-oriented baselines Long2Short (+1.23% accuracy) and CosFn (+2.23% accuracy, 26.5% fewer tokens), demonstrating that InDiv-based reward avoids the accuracy degradation caused by pure length penalties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative self-improvement paradigm established by STaR and ReST^EM, and the preference-learning framework of IRPO, by diagnosing two specific failure modes—data imbalance and overthinking—that cause self-improvement to stall or collapse on complex reasoning tasks. Rather than following the common remedy of allocating more sampling trials to hard queries (DART, Socratic-guided sampling) or penalizing solution length (Long2Short, CosFn), HSIR recycles partially-correct reasoning from previously failed trajectories (VeriExit) and measures reasoning redundancy via an attention-aware eigenvalue analysis of the model's own hidden states (InDiv). The H-GRPO variant further departs from length-oriented GRPO rewards by injecting InDiv as a semantic-diversity reward, positioning it as a more principled alternative to the length-penalty approaches in Kimi K1.5 and Demystifying Long CoT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Self-improvement is a critical pathway for reducing the cost of obtaining high-quality reasoning trajectories, and this paper provides a concrete, multi-paradigm recipe (SFT, DPO, RLVR) that simultaneously improves accuracy and reduces inference tokens across seven model families. The InDiv metric is notable for being entirely intrinsic—requiring no external embedding model or length heuristic—making it cheap to compute and easy to integrate into existing training loops. Open questions remain: InDiv may misclassify semantically similar but logically necessary steps (e.g., in multi-step math derivations), VeriExit cannot rescue queries beyond the model's capability boundary, and the combination of HSIR-SFT with H-GRPO incurs additional compute that the authors flag as a future efficiency target.
