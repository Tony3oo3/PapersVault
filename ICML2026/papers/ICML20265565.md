## Problem

Existing change detection methods assume fixed or mildly varying viewpoints, near-identical camera trajectories, or only a few changed objects, making them inadequate for long-term autonomy where a mobile agent traverses the same environment at different times under unconstrained motion. This paper introduces Video-based Scene Change Detection (VSCD), which must detect pixel-wise object-level changes between two temporally unsynchronized RGB videos with severe cross-view misalignment, occlusions, and many simultaneous object appearances/disappearances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265565/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB videos of the same indoor space (reference and query) recorded at different times with no temporal synchronization, geometric calibration, or shared trajectory; each video is uniformly sampled to 32 keyframes at 1024×1024 resolution. The output is a per-pixel binary change mask for every query frame, where a pixel is marked changed if its corresponding physical location is occupied by an object instance present at exactly one of the two time points (appearances, disappearances, or relocations), while appearance-only variations (illumination, shadows) are treated as nuisance factors.

## Method

VSCDNet is a query-centric multi-reference model with three stages operating on frozen SAM-ViT patch tokens. Stage 1 performs frame-level alignment by computing a cosine similarity grid between learned frame descriptors (alignment token + mean pooling), refining it with a shallow residual conv head, and constructing matched segment proposals to select up to 6 reference candidates per query keyframe. Stage 2 computes patch-level correspondence via dot-product correlation in a 5×5 local window, derives an expected displacement field, warps reference features by bilinear sampling, and produces per-candidate change features with a lightweight conv head. Stage 3 fuses per-candidate features using a product of frame-level confidence (soft matching probability) and patch-level confidence (peakiness minus normalized entropy of the patch matching distribution), then decodes the fused feature once per query frame through progressive upsampling with query RGB injection at two resolutions. The model is trained end-to-end with BCE-with-logits and soft Dice losses on pixel-accurate change masks, keeping the ViT backbone frozen.

## Results

On the VSCD-Syn benchmark, VSCDNet achieves a frame-wise F1 of 36.6%, outperforming the strongest image-based SCD baseline GeSCF (29.5%) by 7.1 points and the strongest video-based AOD baseline PBCD-MC (26.8%) by 9.8 points. On the real-world VSCD-Real test set, VSCDNet reaches 25.4% F1 versus 17.3% for GeSCF and 16.1% for PBCD-MC. The advantage is most pronounced under high object-change density (39.0% vs. 30.6% for GeSCF) and persists across all video-length and graphics-level strata.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSCDNet outperforms all seven baselines on both synthetic and real-world splits. Among SCD methods, GeSCF (Kim & Kim, CVPR 2025) is the closest competitor at 29.5% synthetic / 17.3% real F1, yet VSCDNet still leads by 7.1 and 8.1 points respectively; C-3PO (Wang et al., 2023) trails further at 24.1% / 11.7%, while DR-TANet (Chen et al., 2021) and CSCDNet (Sakurada et al., 2020) score around 20–21% synthetic. The zero-shot method ZSSCD (Cho et al., AAAI 2025) is essentially non-functional under unconstrained motion, recording only 0.3% synthetic F1. Among AOD methods, PBCD-MC (Tavares et al., 2025) reaches 26.8% / 16.1% but degrades sharply under the high-misalignment regime that defines VSCD, and TCF-LMO (Padilla et al., 2023) scores 19.7% / 10.3%. The consistent margin across all stratification bins (video length, graphics level, number of changed objects) indicates that the gains stem from sequence-level alignment and multi-reference fusion rather than exploitation of a particular easy subset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new problem formulation by combining and extending two existing lines: image-pair scene change detection (SCD), which assumes static or mildly misaligned viewpoints, and video-pair abandoned object detection (AOD), which assumes nearly identical camera trajectories and few changed objects. The key conceptual departure is shifting from independent frame-wise comparisons to sequence-level alignment that jointly exploits temporal continuity and multi-view geometry without any pose or trajectory supervision. Methodologically, it repurposes frame-wise similarity maps from video copy localization and a frozen SAM-ViT backbone, wrapping them in a three-stage pipeline (frame-level soft matching, differentiable patch-level warping, confidence-weighted multi-reference fusion) that has no direct precedent in either SCD or AOD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.1M-frame benchmark across 218 environments with pixel-accurate masks, plus a real-world test set, provides the first systematic evaluation platform for change detection under unconstrained motion—a setting that is arguably the one that matters most for long-term robot autonomy but has been absent from prior work. However, absolute performance remains modest (36.6% F1 synthetic, 25.4% real-world), and the real-world set is limited to only 8 pairs, so sim-to-real generalization is still an open question. The paper identifies that the sim-to-real gap is concentrated in small/medium changes and boundary precision rather than a failure of sequence-level alignment, pointing toward future work on domain adaptation, outdoor scenes, and multimodal inputs. The robot deployment (surveillance, incremental learning) is promising but preliminary, with a 62% hit rate and 2-of-4 object classification success.
