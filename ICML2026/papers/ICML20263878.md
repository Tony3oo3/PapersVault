## Problem

Popular visual benchmarks for large multimodal models (LMMs) are rapidly losing headroom as frontier models achieve near-ceiling scores, reducing their ability to differentiate capabilities and track progress. This creates a pressing need for benchmarks that remain challenging over longer timespans. ZeroBench addresses this by being designed to be completely impossible for all contemporary frontier LMMs at release, with 0% pass@1 and pass^5 across 20 evaluated models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263878/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question pairs one or more images (93 single-image, 7 multi-image; 70 natural, 30 synthetic) with a text prompt requiring multi-step visual reasoning and a precise, non-guessable answer (typically numeric or a specific string). The model must enclose its final answer in curly braces for automatic exact-match evaluation; formatting failures are scored as incorrect. Beyond the 100 main questions, 334 subquestions (averaging 3.3 per question) decompose the reasoning chain into intermediate steps, enabling finer-grained model differentiation when main-question scores are uniformly zero.

## Method

ZeroBench is constructed through a three-part adversarial curation pipeline: (1) 140 candidate questions are created by 23 human annotators under deliberately broad instructions emphasizing visual difficulty and multi-step reasoning; (2) iterative review by non-creators refines difficulty, answer uniqueness, and format; (3) adversarial filtering removes any question correctly answered by any of 20 LMM baselines under greedy decoding, anchoring the question distribution to the frontier of current model capabilities. The resulting 100 questions undergo community red teaming, yielding v2 with 23% of questions modified. Evaluation employs both greedy decoding (pass@1) and stochastic decoding with 5 samples (pass@5, pass^5) to capture both peak and consistent performance. A quantitative error analysis using GPT-5.4 assigns each incorrect response to one of 9 mechanism-based categories (Counting, Recognition, Grounding, Measurement, Rules, Simulation, Calculation, Compliance, Ungrounded inference), revealing that visual interpretation errors dominate over reasoning errors.

## Results

At release (Jan 2025), all 20 frontier LMMs scored 0% pass@1 and 0% pass^5 on the 100 main ZeroBench questions, confirming the benchmark was 'impossible' for contemporary models. The highest pass@5 score at release was 5% (Gemini 2 Flash Thinking), while on the 334 subquestions, Claude 3.5 Sonnet v2 led with 25.50% pass@1 (82/334 correct). Over the following year, SotA improved to 19% pass@5 (Gemini 3 Pro) and 6% pass^5 (GPT-5.2), still well below the human baseline of 29.5 ± 27.1%, indicating substantial remaining headroom after 12 months.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 20 pre-release baselines (spanning OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, xAI, Meta, Mistral, Qwen, NVIDIA, and Reka) score identically at 0% pass@1 and 0% pass^5 on main questions, making the benchmark a true ceiling test. On the easier subquestions, Claude 3.5 Sonnet v2 leads at 25.50%, narrowly ahead of Gemini 2 Flash (23.24%) and o1 pro (22.40%), while the weakest model (Reka Edge) scores only 3.74%. Reasoning models (o1, QVQ) generate 3–7k completion tokens per question versus a few hundred for non-reasoning models, yet show no clear subquestion advantage. Post-release, Gemini 3 Pro achieves the highest pass@5 (19%) and GPT-5.2 the highest pass^5 (6%), both still below the 29.5% human baseline. Open-weight models (QVQ, Qwen2-VL, Llama 3.2, NVLM, Pixtral) trail the best proprietary models by roughly 5 percentage points on subquestions, and no model consistently answers the same questions correctly across all 5 samplings (pass^5 remains near zero for most).

## Relation to Prior Work

ZeroBench sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) visual benchmarks for LMMs (BLINK, SPACE, ReMI, SEED-Bench), which typically evaluate single-step or multiple-choice visual tasks; (2) deliberately difficult evals (ARC-AGI, GPQA, VibeEval, Humanity's Last Exam), which target the frontier of model capability but are not specifically visual; and (3) the subquestion decomposition approach from TheAgentCompany (Xu et al., 2024) for finer-grained model differentiation. The key methodological departure is the adversarial filtering pipeline—questions are iteratively removed if any evaluated model answers them correctly—combined with community red teaming, which anchors difficulty to current model capabilities rather than relying on expert intuition alone. The lightweight 100-question scale is motivated by the rising cost of test-time compute scaling in reasoning models, making large benchmarks impractical to evaluate with multiple samplings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZeroBench addresses a concrete and growing problem: popular visual benchmarks are rapidly losing headroom as frontier models saturate them, reducing their ability to differentiate capabilities. The 0%-at-release design and the observed 0%→19% pass@5 trajectory over 12 months (with 47% of questions still unsolved) suggest meaningful longevity, positioning it as a potential long-term yardstick for visual reasoning progress. The error analysis quantifies that the dominant remaining gap is in visual perception (counting, recognition, grounding) rather than logical reasoning or arithmetic, reinforcing that LMMs' visual encoder remains the bottleneck. Open questions include whether the non-automated curation process can be repeated as the difficulty frontier shifts, and whether the 100-question scale is sufficient to reduce sampling variance for fine-grained model comparisons.
