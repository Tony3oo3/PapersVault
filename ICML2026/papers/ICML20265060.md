## Problem

Time-series decomposition is a foundational primitive across domains, yet its quality is typically assessed informally and no unified, public benchmark exists for quantitatively comparing component-recovery methods under controlled generative mechanisms. The community lacks a standardized protocol to characterize failure modes—such as phase degradation under frequency drift or trend collapse under regime switching—and to support evidence-based method selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265060/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds each method a synthetic series of length N=512 drawn from a controlled generative scenario (known trend, cycle, noise, and event mechanisms) along with the true base period for period-requiring methods (STL/MSTL/RobustSTL); for multi-component methods (SSA, EMD, VMD), the period serves only as a grouping hint for frequency-based alignment. Each method's heterogeneous native outputs (multiple IMFs, modes, wavelet levels, or seasonal indices) are deterministically mapped to a canonical (T̂, Ŝ, R̂) triplet, which is then scored against ground truth using complementary time-domain (Trend R², DTW, Seasonal R²) and frequency-domain (spectral correlation, max-lag correlation) metrics. The downstream symbolic-regression track additionally takes the decomposed components as input and outputs a recovered symbolic expression (e.g., sin(2πt)) with its fit R².

## Method

The paper formalizes decomposition as a component-recovery problem and introduces a diagnostic evaluation framework built on three pillars: (i) a synthetic scenario taxonomy spanning six trend mechanisms (linear, polynomial, logistic, exponential, piecewise, random-walk) and eight cycle mechanisms (single sinusoid, multi-harmonic, multi-seasonal, sawtooth, square, amplitude-modulated, frequency-drifting chirp, regime-switching) with four noise models and event types; (ii) a method-agnostic alignment protocol that maps six decomposition families—Moving Average, STL/MSTL (LOESS), SSA (subspace/SVD), EMD/CEEMDAN (sifting), VMD (variational spectral), and Wavelet (multi-scale filter bank)—into a common (T̂, Ŝ, R̂) representation using dominant-frequency matching anchored to the injected period; and (iii) complementary metrics that isolate distinct error modes: scale-sensitive R², shape-sensitive DTW, phase-robust max-lag correlation, and spectral-energy correlation. The key diagnostic insight is that each family's inductive prior (fixed-period, low-rank Hankel, narrow-band, local symmetry, multi-scale locality) matches or mismatches specific generative mechanisms, producing regime-dependent capability maps rather than a single leaderboard ranking. A downstream 'decompose-then-regress' pipeline is further introduced, showing that isolating components before symbolic regression (PySR, ND2) materially improves physical-law recoverability and reduces expression complexity compared to direct regression on raw series.

## Results

On stationary regimes (Tiers 1–2), STL achieves the best trend recovery (Trend R²=0.965) and seasonal spectral correlation (Sea. ρ_Spec=0.980), with SSA (0.929/0.876) and EMD (0.936/0.867) close behind. Under non-stationary regimes (Tier 3), all methods suffer severe trend degradation (STL drops to 0.247, SSA to 0.195, VMD to −1.488), yet seasonal spectral correlation remains high for subspace and time-frequency methods (SSA 0.987, Wavelet 0.983, STL 0.973). On the Mauna Loa CO₂ symbolic-regression track, decompose-then-regress recovers the annual sinusoidal law (Seasonal R²=0.897–0.989) where Direct PySR completely fails (Seasonal R²=0.000).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STL/MSTL (Cleveland 1990; Bandara 2025) dominate stationary regimes with the highest Trend R² (0.965) and Seasonal ρ_Spec (0.980), but degrade to 0.247 trend R² under non-stationary drift, confirming their fixed-period prior as the limiting factor. SSA (Hassani 2010) is the most robust across both regimes, retaining the best non-stationary seasonal spectral correlation (0.987) and competitive trend recovery (0.195), though it still suffers under regime switching (Trend R²≈−0.39). EMD (Huang 1998) tracks SSA closely on stationary tasks (Trend R²=0.936) and maintains high phase-robust seasonal consistency under frequency drift (max-lag 0.924), but its non-stationary trend recovery is the weakest among the top three (0.119). VMD (Dragomiretskiy 2014) fails catastrophically under default settings on both stationary (Trend R²=0.077) and non-stationary (−1.488) regimes, indicating strong sensitivity to K, α, and mode-selection choices; bounded tuning in Appendix C improves but does not overturn this ranking. Wavelet methods (Nason 1999) provide the best non-stationary seasonal fidelity after SSA (0.983) but show the weakest trend recovery among non-VMD methods (−0.183). The Moving Average baseline (Hyndman 2018) is consistently the weakest on trend (0.571 stationary, −0.607 non-stationary) while retaining moderate seasonal spectral correlation (0.938/0.779), confirming its local-constancy prior as insufficient for structured trends.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes well-established decomposition methods (STL/MSTL, SSA, EMD/CEEMDAN, VMD, wavelets, moving averages) as evaluation subjects rather than contributions, framing decomposition as a standalone component-recovery task with controlled generative ground truth. It departs from the dominant time-series literature that treats decomposition as an informal preprocessing step for forecasting or anomaly detection, instead introducing a diagnostic benchmark with explicit trend/cycle/noise/event taxonomies and complementary time- and frequency-domain metrics. The downstream symbolic-regression track (PySR, ND2) connects decomposition to scientific law discovery in a way not previously formalized as a benchmarked pipeline, and the non-stationary periodicity scenarios (frequency drift, regime switching) introduce a mechanism-level stress axis absent from prior decomposition comparisons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first unified, reproducible protocol for comparing decomposition quality under controlled mechanisms, yielding actionable capability maps that link method priors to specific failure modes (e.g., fixed-period priors break under drift, global low-rank priors break under regime shifts). The decompose-then-regress pipeline demonstrates a concrete scientific-discovery benefit—recovering the annual CO₂ cycle that direct symbolic regression completely misses—but the main leaderboard is limited to six methods, a fixed true-period protocol, and synthetic scenarios, leaving unknown-period estimation, change-point localization, and real-panel benchmarking as open extensions. The public Hugging Face leaderboard and one-command runner lower the barrier for future method developers to validate against a shared diagnostic suite.
