## Problem

Existing asymmetric actor-critic methods for partially observable RL assume the critic has access to the full environment state during training, which is often unrealistic in practice. This paper asks whether arbitrary state-dependent privileged signals (e.g., partial state features, expert actions, auxiliary observations) can replace full-state access while preserving unbiased policy gradients, and how to systematically select the most informative signals among many candidates.

## Input / Output

The actor consumes observation-action histories h_t encoded by a recurrent network into a fixed-length representation, and outputs action probabilities π(a|h_t). The critic additionally receives a privileged signal i_t drawn from a state-conditioned distribution I(i_t|s_t) during training, producing value estimates V(h_t, i_t) via TD learning. The privileged signal is never available at deployment, so the actor's input remains unchanged; the signal can be any state-dependent quantity (partial state, expert policy output, auxiliary observation) without requiring the Markov property or full-state access.

## Method

The Informed Asymmetric Actor-Critic (IAAC) framework builds on the informed POMDP formalism, where an information variable i_t ~ I(i_t|s_t) is conditionally independent of the observation given the state. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) proves that for any such state-conditioned signal, the policy gradient using the informed history Q-function Q^π(h_t, i_t, a_t) is exactly equal to the standard policy gradient, generalizing the history-state critic of Baisero & Amato (2022) as the special case i_t = s_t. To address signal selection, the paper introduces two informativeness criteria: an α-residual-informativeness test (applicable pre-training) that removes predictable components of returns and signals via cross-fitted regression on RNN-encoded histories, then tests unconditional residual independence using a dependency measure (e.g., HSIC) with episode-level permutation; and an (ε,δ)-prediction-informativeness test (applicable post-hoc) that compares squared-error gains between symmetric and informed critics via bootstrap or t-test. The critic is trained with temporal-difference learning on the informed Bellman equation, while the actor uses the resulting low-variance advantage estimates for policy gradient updates.

## Results

On 12 POMDP benchmarks (6 navigation + 6 POPGym), informed-asym-A2C consistently improves sample efficiency and training stability over symmetric A2C, and matches or outperforms full-state asymmetric critics (asym-A2C-hs, asym-A2C-s) while using strictly less state information. In Car-Flag, IAAC outperforms all baselines in both convergence speed and asymptotic return; in Memory-Four-Rooms (7×7 and 9×9), it outperforms both asymmetric variants. In the synthetic informativeness validation (Table 1), selecting the reward-relevant subset i_t=[s¹,s²,s⁴,s⁵] achieves an AUC of 1.23×10⁵, surpassing the symmetric A2C baseline (1.06×10⁵) and even the full-state signal (1.19×10⁵), confirming that targeted privileged signals can outperform full-state access.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares informed-asym-A2C against three baselines: symmetric A2C (history-based critic), asym-A2C-hs (history-state critic from Baisero & Amato 2022), and asym-A2C-s (state-based critic, navigation tasks only). Against A2C, IAAC consistently improves sample efficiency and stability across all 12 benchmarks, with the most dramatic gains in Position Cart Pole where A2C fails to converge within 2M steps while both asymmetric variants succeed. Against asym-A2C-hs (the closest competitor requiring full state), IAAC converges faster in Shopping-5, outperforms in both Memory-Four-Rooms tasks, and matches in Cleaner, though it is slightly outperformed in Heaven-Hell-3 and marginally in Cleaner. Against asym-A2C-s, IAAC clearly wins in Car-Flag and Heaven-Hell-3. In POPGym, IAAC achieves the highest final returns in most environments, though A2C wins on Repeat First and all methods tie on Higher Lower. The key finding is that IAAC uses strictly less state information than the full-state baselines yet matches or exceeds their performance, validating that signal selection matters more than signal completeness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the asymmetric actor-critic framework of Baisero & Amato (2022), which required the critic to condition on the full environment state, by showing that any state-conditioned privileged signal suffices for unbiased policy gradients. It builds on the Informed POMDP formalism of Lambrechts et al. (2024) to formalize arbitrary training-time signals without altering execution-time dynamics. The key conceptual departure is shifting the admissible critic input from the full state to an arbitrary subset of state-dependent features, which in turn introduces a previously unaddressed signal-selection problem that the paper tackles with two statistical informativeness criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that practitioners no longer need full-state access (often unavailable in robotics, simulation-to-real transfer, or foundation-model-augmented settings) to benefit from asymmetric critics; carefully selected partial signals can match or outperform full-state critics. The finding that adding irrelevant state features can degrade value estimation challenges the implicit assumption that more state information is always beneficial for the critic. Open questions include whether the informativeness criteria scale to high-dimensional continuous signals, whether signal selection can be integrated end-to-end into the learning loop, and how to account for signal complexity or direct policy effects in the selection criterion.
