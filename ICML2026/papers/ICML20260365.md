## Problem

Existing high-order relation inference methods learn a single static embedding per entity and assume symmetric relation scoring, which fails when an entity's functional role is context-dependent (e.g., a drug's effect conditioned on a specific gene variant) and when the inference direction matters (predicting an ADR given drug-gene vs. predicting a gene given drug-ADR). This semantic entanglement and symmetry assumption are particularly problematic in sparse, combinatorial biomedical settings where the same entity participates in many distinct relational compositions.

## Input / Output

The method takes base entity embeddings (derived from SMILES strings, gene expression profiles, or taxonomy/MeSH descriptors) and a query tuple of m−1 observed entities from an m-ary relation. It produces query-conditioned entity representations for all candidates and a ranked list for the unobserved position. The framework is plug-and-play: it wraps around any backbone (MLP, GCN, HGNN) by replacing or augmenting the original loss, and supports inference over any entity position in the tuple by cycling through all m query configurations.

## Method

CONTEXTOR formulates high-order relation inference as a dynamic query-response process. Given a query vector z_q (an MLP over concatenated observed-entity embeddings), an Asymmetric Conditional Modulation (ACM) module generates query-dependent affine parameters (γ_q, β_q) that apply feature-wise scaling and shifting to each candidate's base embedding, stacked over L layers with residual connections before projection into query space. Training uses InfoNCE contrastive loss across all m query configurations (each entity in turn treated as unobserved), with in-batch negatives, so the model learns directional, context-sensitive alignment. Theoretically, the paper proves that static embeddings face a geometric bottleneck (triangle-inequality lower bound on alignment error under semantic polysemy) that ACM resolves by mapping a single base point to arbitrarily distant target regions, and shows the contrastive objective maximizes a variational lower bound on mutual information between queries and conditioned representations.

## Results

On the DMD dataset, CONTEXTOR achieves Hits@1 of 93.30% and NDCG@5 of 95.05%, surpassing the strongest baseline MCHNN (90.04% Hits@1, 92.91% NDCG@5) by +3.26% and +2.14% respectively. On Drug Synergy prediction, CONTEXTOR attains AUROC of 0.8581 (ALMANAC random) and 0.9293 (O'Neil random), outperforming HGSynergy (0.8530 and 0.9230) and DeepSynergy (0.8350 and 0.9060). On the newly introduced DGA dataset, CONTEXTOR-augmented HGNN reaches MRR of 0.3180 (Drug Discovery), 0.2466 (Gene Identification), and 0.4092 (ADR Prediction), improving over vanilla HGNN by 16.0%, 15.0%, and 11.1% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONTEXTOR consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and evaluation metric. On DMD, it surpasses the closest competitor MCHNN (Liu et al., 2023) by +3.26% Hits@1 and +2.14% NDCG@5, and leads HyperSAGNN (Zhang et al., 2019) and HGSynergy (Liu et al., 2022) by 6.26% and 4.62% Hits@1 respectively; non-graph baselines (GCN, DHNE, CP, Tucker) trail by 20–50+ points. On Drug Synergy, CONTEXTOR beats HGSynergy by +0.005 AUROC on ALMANAC random and +0.012 on O'Neil drug-pair stratified evaluation, while DeepSynergy (Preuer et al., 2018), DTF (Sun et al., 2020), ComboFM (Julkunen et al., 2020), Celebi's method, and Li's method all fall 1.5–12 points behind in AUROC. On the DGA dataset, CONTEXTOR-augmented HGNN (Feng et al., 2019) improves MRR by 16.0% (Drug Discovery), 15.0% (Gene Identification), and 11.1% (ADR Prediction) over vanilla HGNN, and also outperforms MLP and GCN backbones by 20–30% in MRR. When integrated with the semantic-aware Natural-HNN (Lee et al., 2026), CONTEXTOR further boosts ADR Prediction MRR from 0.4082 to 0.5129 (+25.6%), confirming orthogonality to existing disentanglement mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

CONTEXTOR builds on the InfoNCE contrastive objective (Oord et al., 2018) and the hypergraph neural network lineage (HGNN, MCHNN, HGSynergy) for high-order relation inference, but departs from both in a key way: rather than learning a single static embedding per entity and scoring triplets via symmetric similarity, it formulates inference as an asymmetric query-response process where candidate entity representations are dynamically modulated by the query context through an Asymmetric Conditional Modulation (ACM) module. This positions the work at the intersection of contrastive representation learning and contextual/conditional representation learning, explicitly addressing the semantic polysemy problem that static-embedding hypergraph methods cannot resolve. The plug-and-play design means CONTEXTOR is not a new backbone but a loss-and-modulation module that can be grafted onto MLP, GCN, or HGNN architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical argument (triangle-inequality impossibility) for why static embeddings fundamentally fail under semantic polysemy, and shows that query-conditioned affine modulation resolves this geometric bottleneck — a conceptual contribution that generalizes beyond the biomedical setting. The 85.3% relative MRR improvement on tail entities and the label-efficiency gap (40% data matching 60% baseline) suggest practical value in data-sparse biomedical discovery where most entities are poorly characterized. However, evaluations are limited to third-order relations due to the absence of large-scale higher-order benchmarks, and the absolute performance gains over the strongest baselines (e.g., +3.26% Hits@1 on DMD) are solid but incremental, leaving open whether the approach scales to genuinely higher-order (4+) relations or to settings with richer entity features.
