## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors suffer severe performance drops under domain shift (new generators, new natural sources), yet UMAP analysis reveals that intermediate features remain partially separable on unseen datasets. This indicates the classification head overfits to training-domain artifacts rather than the representation itself collapsing, pointing to a need for more transferable, source-aware feature geometry and a more stable decision criterion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265797/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 224×224 RGB image and outputs a binary natural/synthetic score. At inference the backbone (DINOv3 ViT-L/16 by default) is frozen and a lightweight plug-and-play head—k-NN, prototypical classifier, linear probe, or RBF-kernel SVM—is instantiated from a small support set (as few as 10 labeled samples per class) or from the full training feature bank, requiring no backbone updates. The same frozen encoder can be swapped for other pretrained backbones (CLIP ViT-L/14, DINOv2-Large, DFN2B-CLIP) with the same hierarchical contrastive training recipe.

## Method

FiSeR fine-tunes a vision transformer with a two-level supervised contrastive objective: a coarse loss that pulls natural and synthetic embeddings apart, and a fine loss that, restricted to synthetic samples, pulls same-generator pairs together while pushing different-generator pairs apart. A key implementation detail is that all positives within a batch are aggregated into a single averaged positive logit before exponentiation, reducing softmax competition and improving training stability. The overall loss is L_y + λ·𝕀[synthetic]·L_src with λ=1, optimized end-to-end with AdamW and cosine LR decay over 20 epochs. At test time the backbone is frozen and a lightweight head (k-NN, SVM, linear, or prototypical) is fit on a small target-domain support set, decoupling representation quality from decision-boundary overfitting. The paper also introduces a k-NN graph homophily score (normalized by a random-mixing null baseline) as a low-variance proxy for representation separability, showing a Pearson r of 0.942 correlation with few-shot AUROC across 12 detectors.

## Results

Trained on WildFake and evaluated without any target-domain data, FiSeR achieves an average AUROC of 98.23 and average TPR@FPR=5% of 93.43 across five benchmarks (WildFake, Community Forensics, AIGIBench, Chameleon, GenImage), outperforming the strongest baseline DIRE by +10.22 AUROC and +31.78 TPR5%. On the two hardest OOD benchmarks, FiSeR reaches 98.00 AUROC on AIGIBench and 96.35 AUROC on Chameleon, where most baselines drop below 75. With only 10 labeled samples per class from the target domain, a lightweight SVM head further improves AUROC to 99.23 on AIGIBench and 98.68 on Chameleon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FiSeR outperforms all 15 baselines on every dataset and both metrics. The largest absolute gaps appear on AIGIBench and Chameleon, the two most challenging OOD benchmarks: FiSeR gains +26.89 AUROC over DIRE (98.00 vs. 71.11) on AIGIBench and +15.00 over DIRE (96.35 vs. 81.35) on Chameleon. Among the strongest baselines, C2P-CLIP (86.83 avg AUROC) and Effort (86.42) trail FiSeR by roughly 11–12 points on average, with particularly large deficits on AIGIBench (85.85/87.91 vs. 98.00) and Chameleon (64.18/61.69 vs. 96.35). CLIP-based detectors (UniFD 82.85, CLIPDetection 85.15, LASTED 75.24) show moderate in-domain performance but degrade sharply cross-domain, especially on Chameleon where CLIPDetection drops to 60.81. CNN-based baselines (ResNet-50 81.63, Gram-Net 80.99, LGrad 84.98) and frequency/artifact-based methods (FreqNet 70.00, NPR 60.41, LOTA 62.29) are consistently weaker, with NPR and LOTA falling below 65 average AUROC. The few-shot SVM refitting experiment (Table 4) further confirms FiSeR's advantage: with 10-shot target labels on AIGIBench, FiSeR reaches 99.23 AUROC versus 91.71 for DIRE and 90.42 for UniFD, and the improvement is consistent across k-NN, linear, SVM, and prototypical heads.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends supervised contrastive learning (Khosla et al., 2020) by introducing a hierarchical two-level structure: a coarse natural-vs-synthetic objective combined with a fine-grained generator-identity objective among synthetic samples. It departs from the dominant paradigm in AI-generated image detection—where methods either exploit spatial/frequency artifacts (Gram-Net, FreqNet, SPAI) or build on foundation-model features with a fixed classification head (UniFD, DIRE, AIDE)—by reframing the cross-domain failure as a head-overfitting problem rather than a representation-collapse problem. The plug-and-play inference head (k-NN, SVM, linear probe) and the k-NN graph homophily metric are novel contributions that decouple representation quality from the specific classifier used at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that intermediate representations remain separable under domain shift while the classification head fails has immediate practical value: existing detectors can be substantially improved by freezing the backbone and fitting a lightweight head on 10 target-domain samples, without retraining. The homophily metric offers a cheap, stable diagnostic for representation quality that correlates strongly (r=0.94) with few-shot AUROC, potentially guiding backbone selection without expensive evaluation. Open questions include whether the fine-grained generator structure generalizes to truly novel generation paradigms (e.g., autoregressive or video models), whether the approach extends to other modalities, and how performance scales when the number of generators in the training set grows to thousands as in Community Forensics.
