## Problem

Existing AI-generated music detectors are trained on specific generators' outputs and degrade sharply when evaluated on unseen generators, making them impractical as generative models proliferate. This paper formulates a zero-shot detection setting where the detector is trained exclusively on real music with no access to any generated samples, enabling generator-agnostic identification of AI-produced music.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261459/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw music waveform, resampled to 16 kHz mono and standardized to 4 seconds, then computes an STFT (n_fft=512, hop_length=160) and extracts spectral energy features via convolutional layers. It outputs a log-likelihood score under the learned real-music distribution rather than a calibrated probability; a manually selected threshold (e.g., −20) converts this score into a binary real/AI-generated decision. The class-conditional variant additionally learns a separate latent prior for AI-generated music but still scores test samples only under the real-music prior at inference.

## Method

MusicDET is a one-class, frequency-guided normalizing-flow framework that models the distribution of real music in the time-frequency energy domain. Spectral features are first decomposed along the frequency axis into sub-bands (e.g., low and high), each processed by an independent Glow-style band-wise flow (K steps of ActNorm, invertible 1×1 convolution, and affine coupling) to capture band-specific statistical regularities. The resulting band-wise latent codes are concatenated and passed through a global normalizing flow trained against a Gaussian prior, yielding an exact log-likelihood via the change-of-variables formula. Training minimizes the negative log-likelihood over real music only (zero-shot) or over class-conditional likelihoods when generated samples are available (class-conditional variant), and detection flags samples whose likelihood falls below a threshold as AI-generated.

## Results

On FakeMusicCaps (5 generators, cross-generator EER↓), zero-shot MusicDET achieves 4.51% average EER, outperforming the best non-zero-shot baseline W2V2-AASIST† (11.46%) by 7 points and the best spectrogram baseline SpecTTTra-α (17.63%) by 13 points. The class-conditional variant further reduces EER to 0.89%. On SONICS (5 generators), zero-shot MusicDET achieves 2.89% average EER, while the class-conditional variant reaches 0.00% EER, perfectly separating all AI-generated music from real music.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MusicDET (zero-shot, 4.51% avg EER) outperforms all 11 baselines on FakeMusicCaps by a wide margin: it beats AASIST (32.73%) by 28.2 points, MERT-AASIST (23.27%) by 18.8 points, W2V2-AASIST (25.80%) by 21.3 points, WPT-W2V2-AASIST (17.61%) by 13.1 points, Spec-ViT (22.65%) by 18.1 points, Spec-ConvNeXt (21.05%) by 16.5 points, and the SpecTTTra family (17.63–19.00%) by 13–14.5 points. Even the strongest fine-tuned baseline, W2V2-AASIST† (11.46%), is surpassed by 7 points, and notably MusicDET achieves this without any generated training data. On SONICS, zero-shot MusicDET (2.89%) is competitive with Spec-ViT (1.23%) and SpecTTTra-α (2.66%), while the class-conditional variant achieves 0.00% EER, surpassing all baselines. On ASVspoof 2019 LA, W2V2-MusicDET (0.86%) outperforms W2V2-AASIST (1.28%), WavLM-AASIST (2.49%), W2V2-ResNet (2.07%), and W2V2-LCNN (3.63%). On CtrSVDD, W2V2-MusicDET (6.74%) beats AASIST (11.54%), W2V2-AASIST (8.23%), W2V2-ResNet (11.72%), W2V2-LCNN (12.78%), and Spec-ResNet (22.59%), but is narrowly outperformed by the task-specific SingGraph (6.23%) by 0.51 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of discriminative AI-generated music detectors (AASIST, W2V2-AASIST, MERT-AASIST, SpecTTTra) that require generated samples during training and overfit to generator-specific artifacts. It repurposes normalizing flows—previously applied to anomaly detection in computer vision (Rudolph et al., 2021; Hirschorn & Avidan, 2023)—as a one-class density estimator over real-music energy spectrograms, with a Glow-style architecture (Kingma & Dhariwal, 2018) adapted via frequency-wise decomposition to handle music's heterogeneous multi-band statistics. The class-conditional extension introduces a supervised variant that shares flow parameters across classes and differentiates them only through latent Gaussian priors, a design choice distinct from standard multi-class flow formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot formulation is practically critical because generative music models proliferate rapidly, making per-generator detector training infeasible; MusicDET's generator-agnostic design addresses this directly. However, the robustness results reveal a significant practical limitation: common post-processing (pitch shifting, additive noise, codec re-encoding) degrades EER to near-random levels (~44%), suggesting the learned spectral-energy distribution is fragile to realistic distribution shifts. The class-conditional variant's 0.00% EER on SONICS, while impressive, warrants scrutiny regarding evaluation protocol strictness. Open questions include whether the approach can be made robust to adversarial post-processing, whether it scales to longer-form music beyond 4-second clips, and whether the frequency-band decomposition generalizes to non-musical audio without task-specific tuning.
