## Problem

Existing ML charge density models for periodic materials either probe a dense real-space grid (incurring high inference cost that can negate DFT SCF savings) or rely on atom-centered orbital expansions that struggle with the non-local, long-range features of crystalline charge densities. A model that is simultaneously periodic, plane-wave-compatible, captures long-range structure, and fast enough to deliver net end-to-end DFT wall-time reductions was missing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261024/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a periodic atomic structure (element types, Cartesian positions, and lattice vectors defining the unit cell) and produces the valence charge density on a real-space grid matching the VASP CHGCAR resolution, equivalently represented as plane-wave Fourier coefficients over the reciprocal lattice. The density is normalized by construction to integrate to the correct number of valence electrons, and the number of Gaussian components scales linearly with total valence electron count (M Gaussians per electron, M=100–120 depending on dataset).

## Method

ELECTRAFI represents the valence density as a mixture of anisotropic Gaussians whose parameters (signed weights, displacement centers, SPD covariances) are predicted per-atom by a modified EScAIP attention backbone with custom dyadic neighbor aggregation layers producing scalar, vector, and tensor channels. The key insight is that the Fourier transform of each Gaussian is closed-form, so plane-wave coefficients are obtained analytically via the Poisson summation formula without explicit periodic image summation or real-space grid evaluation; a single inverse FFT then reconstructs the full periodic density. Covariances are parametrized through a Gram factorization (Σ = γAAᵀ) to guarantee positive-definiteness, and weights use a tanh-bounded MLP for magnitude stability. Training is supervised with normalized mean absolute error (NMAE) on the full real-space density grid, and the model is trained on the full Materials Project distribution (117,876 structures) with PBC-aware graph construction.

## Results

On the MP-Full test set, ELECTRAFI achieves 0.58% NMAE with a 0.17 s average inference time, compared to ChargE3Net's 0.54% NMAE at 78.73 s (a 463× speedup). On the ECD-HSE06 benchmark, ELECTRAFI attains state-of-the-art accuracy at 1.35% NMAE, outperforming ChargE3Net (1.53%) while delivering a 633× inference speedup (0.05 s vs. 31.65 s). On MP-Mixed and Cubic, ELECTRAFI achieves 1.24% and 1.37% NMAE respectively, outperforming all previously published models (next-best: SCDP at 2.59% on Cubic). In end-to-end DFT acceleration experiments, ELECTRAFI reduces total compute time by ~17–20%, whereas ChargE3Net's high inference cost negates its DFT savings and results in a net slowdown.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ChargE3Net (Koker et al., 2024), the strongest prior grid-based model, ELECTRAFI trades a small accuracy gap on MP-Full (0.58% vs. 0.54% NMAE) and GNoME (0.93% vs. 0.69%) for a 300–463× reduction in inference time; on ECD-HSE06, ELECTRAFI actually surpasses ChargE3Net in both accuracy (1.35% vs. 1.53%) and speed (633×). Against DeepDFT (Koker et al., 2024), ELECTRAFI improves NMAE by 0.22 pp on MP-Full (0.58% vs. 0.80%) and by over 9 pp on MP-Mixed (1.24% vs. 11.50%) and Cubic (1.37% vs. 10.37%). On the Cubic benchmark, ELECTRAFI outperforms SCDP (Fu et al., 2024) by 1.22 pp (1.37% vs. 2.59%), and on MP-Mixed it beats GPWNO (Kim & Ahn, 2024) by 3.59 pp (1.24% vs. 4.83%) and InfGCN by 3.87 pp (1.24% vs. 5.11%). In the DFT acceleration experiments (Table 2), ELECTRAFI achieves a net ~17–20% total time reduction relative to the default SAD initialization, while ChargE3Net's 72 s inference time causes a net 5% slowdown on MP and 1% slowdown on GNoME, making ELECTRAFI the first model to deliver consistent end-to-end DFT speedups for periodic materials.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELECTRAFI extends the floating-Gaussian charge density representation established for molecules in ELECTRA (Elsborg et al., 2026) to periodic crystalline systems by exploiting the closed-form Fourier transform of Gaussians and the Poisson summation formula, thereby constructing plane-wave coefficients analytically rather than probing a real-space grid. The backbone is a modified EScAIP attention architecture (Qu & Krishnapriyan, 2024) adapted for periodic boundary conditions, with custom dyadic aggregation layers producing per-atom scalar, vector, and tensor channels. This positions ELECTRAFI as a third paradigm distinct from both orbital models (atom-centered basis expansions) and probe models (grid-point GNN queries like ChargE3Net), delegating all non-local and periodic behavior to analytic reciprocal-space transforms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that inference cost, not just density accuracy, is the binding constraint for end-to-end DFT acceleration: ChargE3Net's superior NMAE is negated by its ~79 s inference time, yielding a net slowdown, while ELECTRAFI's 0.17 s inference enables consistent ~18–21% total wall-time reductions. This reframes how the community should evaluate density-prediction models and motivates co-design with DFT codes. Open questions include extending to magnetic/spin-polarized systems, jointly predicting PAW augmentation occupancies, and building hybrid models that combine ELECTRAFI's efficient global Fourier representation with ChargE3Net's superior local real-space corrections for covalent and transition-metal bonding.
