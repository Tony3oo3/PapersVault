## Problem

Distribution shift in supervised learning is traditionally viewed as a negative phenomenon that degrades performance, and the literature focuses on mitigating it. However, in practice much of the innovation in modern ML comes from finding good training distributions rather than new algorithms. This paper formalizes the complementary perspective of Positive Distribution Shift (PDS), where a deliberately chosen training distribution D' ≠ D makes computationally hard learning problems tractable, and provides the first rigorous framework and results for this phenomenon.

## Input / Output

The learner receives m i.i.d. labeled samples (x, y) drawn from a shifted training distribution D' over the Boolean hypercube {±1}^d, where labels are generated by the true target f with optional random classification noise η. The training distribution D' is constructed as a mixture (e.g., ½ Rad(1−1/d)^{⊗d} + ½ Unif({±1}^d) for parities) that depends on the target distribution D and hypothesis class H but not on f itself. The output is a binary predictor h: X → {0,1} whose performance is measured by 0-1 error on the target distribution D, with the goal of achieving error at most L_{D,f}(H) + ε.

## Method

The paper introduces a hierarchy of PDS learning frameworks—f-PDS (training distribution may depend on f), D-DS-PAC (deterministic, D' depends only on D and H), and R-DS-PAC (randomized, D' drawn from a meta-distribution)—and proves that computationally hard classes like noisy parities and k-juntas become tractably learnable under these frameworks. For parities, the key mechanism is that a biased product distribution creates a correlation gap between in-support and out-of-support coordinates, enabling polynomial-time support recovery and making the target a 'staircase' in the Fourier basis of D', which standard SGD on a two-layer ReLU network can then learn. For juntas, they provide a Correlational Statistical Query algorithm and a layerwise GD procedure with covariance loss on a two-layer network. The paper further establishes a tight equivalence between D-DS-PAC and non-adaptive membership queries (NA-MQ), and between NA-MQ and R-DS-PAC, connecting the PDS framework to classical query-based learning models.

## Results

The paper establishes that noisy parity functions are D-DS-PAC learnable with O(d² log(2d/ε) / (1-2η)²) samples and O(d³ log(2d/ε) / (1-2η)²) runtime (Theorem 4.3), and that k-sparse noisy juntas are D-DS-PAC learnable with O(dk + 2^k) correlational statistical queries (Theorem 4.6). Empirically, training a degree-25 parity over 50 bits with a two-layer ReLU network (1024 hidden units) under PDS (D′ = ½D₀.₉₆ + ½D₀) converges to Bayes error substantially faster than standard training on the uniform distribution D₀, and the benefit grows with input dimension for juntas (Figure 2). The key finding is that the mixture training distribution is essential: training purely on the biased distribution D₀.₉₆ fails to generalize to the uniform target D₀, while the 50/50 mixture achieves PDS generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between standard PAC learning (training and testing on the same distribution D) and PDS learning (training on a shifted D′, testing on D). Under standard PAC, k-sparse parities and log(d)-juntas are believed to be computationally hard to learn (requiring d^Ω(k) or 2^Ω(log d) time), yet PDS renders them tractably learnable in polynomial time. Empirically, for a degree-25 parity over 50 bits with label noise η ∈ {0, 0.02, 0.05}, PDS training on D′ = ½D₀.₉₆ + ½D₀ reaches Bayes error orders of magnitude faster than standard training on D₀ (Figure 1). For k-juntas (k ∈ {7, 9}), the PDS advantage increases with input dimension d, with the sample complexity gap widening as d grows (Figure 2). The paper also distinguishes its framework from prior work: unlike Malach et al. (2021) which changes both train and test distributions, and Cornacchia & Mossel (2023) which uses a curriculum (sequential distributions), PDS uses a single fixed shifted training distribution while keeping the test distribution unchanged.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the distribution shift literature by arguing that a well-chosen training distribution can be *beneficial* rather than harmful, formalizing this as Positive Distribution Shift (PDS) with three nested definitions (f-PDS, D-DS-PAC, R-DS-PAC). It builds on curriculum learning results (Cornacchia & Mossel, 2023; Abbe et al., 2023c) and smoothed analysis (Kalai & Teng, 2008; Kalai et al., 2009) but departs by focusing on a single shifted training distribution rather than a sequence, and by emphasizing computational (rather than statistical) benefits. The connection to non-adaptive membership queries (NA-MQ) is a new formal bridge, and the use of Abbe & Sandon (2020) / Abbe et al. (2021) simulation theorems to show f-PDS learnability of circuits via GD on non-standard networks extends prior universality results into the distribution-shift setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal framework for studying when and why a shifted training distribution makes computationally hard learning problems tractable, directly relevant to the practical 'dataset selection' problem in modern ML. Key open questions include whether standard SGD on standard networks can learn juntas under D-DS-PAC (only R-DS-PAC is proven), whether sparse Fourier functions are DS-PAC learnable, and whether adaptivity in membership queries is truly necessary. The f-PDS result (Theorem 3.2) is acknowledged as 'cheating' since it encodes the target in the distribution, so the more meaningful contributions are the DS-PAC results for parities and juntas and the NA-MQ equivalences.
