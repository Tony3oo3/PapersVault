## Problem

Existing multimodal visual tracking methods either train task-specific architectures for each modality combination, rely on multi-stage fine-tuning of pretrained RGB trackers, or naively concatenate heterogeneous tokens, leading to feature conflicts, suboptimal convergence, and vulnerability to missing modalities. No prior framework achieves a single unified architecture with shared parameters trained end-to-end in one step across all RGB and RGB+X tracking tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261587/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes template and search regions from any combination of RGB, depth, thermal, event, or text modalities at resolutions of 224×224 or 384×384 (search) and 112×112 or 192×192 (template), with CLIP-L encoding text descriptions for language-guided tasks. For pure RGB tracking, the same RGB frame substitutes the auxiliary modality slot, so the architecture remains identical across all tasks. The output is a single bounding box per frame, predicted via classification and regression heads with a Hanning window penalty and template update every 25 frames.

## Method

OneTrackerV2 introduces two key modules on a shared HiViT backbone. First, the Meta Merger projects RGB and auxiliary features through spatial-channel attention, then uses a learnable meta embedding as a global mediator that absorbs and redistributes cross-modal information via lightweight convolutions, yielding a compact modality-agnostic representation that remains stable under missing modalities. Second, the Dual Mixture-of-Experts (DMoE) splits the transformer's FFN into a shared expert, a T-MoE for spatio-temporal matching, and an M-MoE for multimodal fusion, each with top-k sparse routing over 8 low-rank experts. Two auxiliary losses enforce functional separation: an expert decoupling loss penalizes cosine similarity between T-MoE and M-MoE outputs, and a router clustering loss encourages consistent expert selection within the same modality while discriminating across modalities. Training is single-step and supervised, with stochastic modality perturbation (random replacement and masking) to further improve robustness.

## Results

OneTrackerV2-L384 achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 5 tracking tasks and 12 benchmarks with a single unified training run. On RGB tracking, it reaches 76.1 AUC on LaSOT, 88.6 AUC on TrackingNet, and 81.3 AO on GOT-10k, surpassing RGB-only trackers like SPMTrack-L384 (76.8 AUC on LaSOT) and ARPTrack-L384 (74.2 AUC). On RGB+X tracking, it attains 67.5 F-Score on DepthTrack, 63.7 AUC on LasHeR, and 76.0 AUC on VisEvent, outperforming the closest unified baseline SUTrack-L384 (66.4/61.9/72.1 respectively). On the language-guided TNL2K benchmark, OneTrackerV2 achieves 69.5 AUC, and on the unseen NIR modality (CMOTB), it reaches 65.1 SR without any task-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneTrackerV2 outperforms the closest unified multimodal baseline SUTrack (Chen et al., 2025) across all RGB+X benchmarks: +1.1 F-Score on DepthTrack (67.5 vs 66.4), +1.8 AUC on LasHeR (63.7 vs 61.9), and +3.9 AUC on VisEvent (76.0 vs 72.1) for the L384 variant, while also leading on RGB benchmarks (76.1 vs 75.2 AUC on LaSOT). Against RGB-only trackers, OneTrackerV2-L384 surpasses SPMTrack-L384 on TrackingNet (88.6 vs 86.9 AUC) and GOT-10k (81.3 vs 80.0 AO), though SPMTrack edges it slightly on LaSOT (76.8 vs 76.1 AUC). Compared to the predecessor OneTracker (Hong et al., 2024b), OneTrackerV2 gains +5.6 AUC on LaSOT and +9.8 AUC on LasHeR, demonstrating the benefit of unified end-to-end training over multi-stage fine-tuning. On the unseen NIR modality (CMOTB), OneTrackerV2 achieves 65.1 SR, exceeding both ProtoTrack (59.7 SR) and MAFNet (56.1 SR) which were explicitly trained on that dataset. Under missing-modality stress tests (Table 3), OneTrackerV2-B224 leads all baselines by a wide margin (e.g., 56.9 F-Score on DepthTrack-miss vs 49.9 for STTrack and 49.5 for SUTrack), confirming the robustness advantage of the Meta Merger design.

## Relation to Prior Work

OneTrackerV2 is a direct successor to OneTracker (Hong et al., 2024b), which first unified visual object tracking under a foundation-model paradigm but still required multi-stage fine-tuning and task-dependent weights for RGB+X tasks. It also departs from SUTrack (Chen et al., 2025), the closest prior unified multimodal tracker, by replacing naive token concatenation with a Meta Merger (learnable meta embedding as a cross-modal bridge) and introducing Dual Mixture-of-Experts that explicitly decouples spatio-temporal matching from multimodal fusion via orthogonality and router-clustering losses. The MoE design draws on the broader conditional-computation literature (Fedus et al., 2022; Dai et al., 2024) but repurposes it for the specific heterogeneous-objective conflict in tracking, where motion cues and modality-specific patterns would otherwise compete within a shared parameter space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A single model trained once that handles five tracking tasks across twelve benchmarks with shared parameters is practically significant for deployment in autonomous systems and search-and-rescue robots where sensor configurations vary and retraining is infeasible. The demonstrated robustness under missing modalities and the 2.2× compression speedup with only ~2% accuracy loss make the approach viable for edge deployment. Open questions include whether the Meta Merger's generalization to truly unseen modalities (shown on NIR/CMOTB) extends to fundamentally different sensor types, whether the DMoE decoupling transfers to other video understanding tasks, and how the framework scales as the number of supported modalities grows beyond the current five.
