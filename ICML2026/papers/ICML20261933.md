## Problem

Existing LLM reasoning evaluation methods either depend on external supervised verifiers that lack scalability at inference time, or reduce multi-step reasoning chains to static scalar statistics (e.g., last-token probability, perplexity), discarding the temporal dynamics of thought. No prior framework integrates the geometric structure of hidden-state trajectories—where correct reasoning and hallucination exhibit distinct topological signatures—with a practical, label-free quality assessment mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261933/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the per-token final-layer hidden states h_t ∈ R^d of a generated chain-of-thought (up to 16,384 tokens for reasoning models) together with the model's unembedding matrix W_U to define a semantic metric. It produces a binary correctness label via MAP classification and a continuous posterior probability P(y=1|x) over the 2D geometric feature vector [M_n, K_n]. The framework requires only ~400 reference trajectories for calibration and operates without any task-specific fine-tuning, making it deployable as a plug-in reward signal (e.g., inside GRPO) or a standalone evaluator.

## Method

TRACED decomposes reasoning quality into two geometric kinematic signatures computed in a contrastive quality subspace. First, hidden states are whitened by the semantic metric G^{1/2} (derived from W_U^T W_U) to filter non-semantic noise, then projected onto a basis B extracted from the top-k eigenvectors of S = C⁺ − λC⁻, where C⁺ and C⁻ are kinematic covariance matrices of correct and incorrect trajectories respectively. In this subspace, Progress is measured as Normalized Net Displacement M_n = (1/T)‖z_T − z_0‖₂, and Stability as Average Trajectory Curvature K_n computed from discrete velocity and acceleration vectors. A Gaussian Discriminant Analysis model then performs MAP classification on the [M_n, K_n] feature pair, with the prior set to uniform. Theoretically, the paper grounds these signatures in an Itô SDE framework: correct reasoning (high signal-to-noise ratio) yields linear displacement scaling D(T) ∝ T with vanishing curvature, while hallucination (noise-dominated) degenerates to a random walk with sub-linear scaling D(T) ∝ √T and maximal curvature, explaining the observed topological divergence.

## Results

On DeepSeek-R1-Llama-8B, TRACED achieves the best AUROC on 5 of 6 benchmarks: GPQA (0.830), GSM8K (0.806), MATH (0.749), Social IQA (0.754), and TheoremQA (0.873), with Fables at 0.719 (nearly tied with LR Probe at 0.718). Across all four models, TRACED consistently outperforms scalar probability baselines (MSP, Perplexity, Entropy) by 5–25 AUROC points, and surpasses trajectory-based methods (CoE, CoT-Kinetics) on most benchmarks. On Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, TRACED reaches 0.850 AUROC on MATH and 0.783 on GSM8K, while on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct it leads in AUPR on Fables (0.738), GPQA (0.758), MATH (0.786), and Social IQA (0.737).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACED decisively outperforms all three scalar probability baselines (MSP, Perplexity, LN-Entropy) across every model and benchmark, with AUROC gaps of 5–25 points (e.g., on DeepSeek-R1-Llama-8B/GPQA, TRACED scores 0.830 vs. MSP's 0.384 and Perplexity's 0.383). Against supervised hidden-state probes, TRACED is highly competitive: it matches or beats LR Probe on 5/6 benchmarks for DeepSeek-R1 (e.g., GPQA 0.830 vs. 0.759) and SAPLAMA on most tasks, though SAPLAMA edges ahead on MATH for Qwen3-4B (0.844 vs. 0.850 is actually TRACED winning; SAPLAMA leads on TheoremQA for DeepSeek-R1 at 0.852 vs. 0.873 — TRACED still wins). TRACED surpasses both trajectory-based baselines CoE and CoT-Kinetics on nearly all configurations, with the largest margins on GPQA (0.830 vs. 0.500 for CoE) and MATH on Qwen3-4B (0.850 vs. 0.422 for CoT-Kinetics), confirming that temporal geometric features (displacement + curvature) capture reasoning dynamics more effectively than layer-wise embedding paths or kinetic-energy formulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRACED extends the geometric analysis of LLM hidden states initiated by Manson (2025), who showed that semantic concerns induce measurable curvature in metric-aligned spaces, and Zhou et al. (2025), who established reasoning as a 'geometric flow' in representation space. It departs from prior trajectory-based methods (CoE, CoT-Kinetics) by operating in a contrastive quality subspace derived from kinematic covariance differences rather than layer-wise or force-field models, and from scalar probability methods by preserving full temporal dynamics. The SDE framework in Appendix E provides a theoretical bridge that prior empirical works lacked, formalizing why correct reasoning should exhibit linear displacement scaling while hallucinations degenerate into random-walk dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a label-free, interpretable diagnostic for reasoning quality that avoids the scalability limitations of external verifiers and the information loss of scalar aggregation, with the SDE grounding providing a principled explanation rather than a post-hoc fit. The GRPO extension demonstrates practical utility as a reward signal that mitigates zero-variance collapse in high-difficulty RL training. However, the approach still requires a small calibration set (~400 samples) for subspace construction, the cognitive-state extraction relies on hand-curated vocabulary lists that may not generalize across languages or domains, and the SDE assumptions (Lipschitz continuity of the logical velocity field, isotropic noise) are idealizations whose validity in actual transformer dynamics remains unverified. Open questions include whether the geometric signatures persist in non-CoT or multimodal reasoning settings and whether the contrastive subspace can be constructed in a fully unsupervised manner.
