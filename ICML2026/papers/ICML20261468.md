## Problem

Theoretical work in statistical physics predicts that diffusion models undergo sharp symmetry-breaking transitions (speciation) where semantic structure commits from ambiguity to class-specific identity, but no operational quantity exists that can be directly estimated in trained models to detect and localize these windows. This paper bridges that gap by identifying class-conditional entropy as a measurable signature of speciation and demonstrating it can be tracked along the denoising trajectory of practical models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained diffusion model (EDM2-XS or Stable Diffusion 1.5), a conditioning signal (ImageNet class label or text prompt), and the noisy latent state at each denoising timestep. It produces a class-conditional entropy profile H(Z|X_t) and its temporal derivative (entropy production) over the full denoising trajectory, with a partitioned variant that isolates specific binary semantic decisions (e.g., one class vs. its complement, or two prompt variants differing by a single attribute). In practice, posteriors are estimated via iterative log-likelihood-ratio updates between conditional and unconditional denoisers, with balanced priors (yielding a Jensen-Shannon divergence formulation) to counteract the extreme class imbalance in one-versus-complement settings.

## Method

The core insight is that the temporal derivative of class-conditional entropy decomposes into pairwise terms each governed by a single control parameter λ_rs(t) that simultaneously controls posterior competition (via log-odds) and score-field separation. Entropy production is non-negligible only in the intermediate regime λ_rs(t) = O(1), where posterior mass is no longer exponentially suppressed but score fields have not yet collapsed — precisely the speciation window predicted by high-dimensional statistical physics. For VP processes this window sharpens to an O(1) interval around t_s = ½ log d, while for VE-EDM it broadens to O(√d). The partitioned variant restricts the entropy to a binary subset of classes, turning it into a probe of a specific semantic distinction at a chosen level of abstraction. In trained models, the entropy is estimated by propagating class posteriors backward along the denoising trajectory using local likelihood increments between successive noise levels, computed from the model's noise or noiseless predictions.

## Results

The paper's central theoretical result is that class-conditional entropy production concentrates at the speciation time: for VP processes, t_s(d) = (1/2)log(d) + O(1), with the transition sharpening into an O(1) window around the rescaled time u=1 as d→∞; for VE-EDM processes, t_s(d) ∝ √d, but the transition broadens to an O(√d) window on the physical time axis. Empirically on EDM2-XS (ImageNet 512), entropy production is confined to intermediate noise stages, with class-specific deviations (e.g., tiger shark peaks at higher noise, paintbrush at lower noise). On Stable Diffusion 1.5, low-frequency semantic decisions (global color, B&W) resolve at higher noise levels while high-frequency attributes (cat, window) resolve later, confirming hierarchical speciation. Guidance applied in the FD_DINOv2-optimal interval shifts entropy production toward higher noise and suppresses it at lower noise, accelerating overall speciation for most classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical-analysis and diagnostic paper rather than a method-comparison study, so it does not benchmark against competing generative methods or report standard metrics like FID or precision/recall against baselines. Instead, the paper validates its entropy-production framework against two prior theoretical predictions: (i) the speciation time t_s(d) = (1/2)log(d) from Biroli et al. (2024), which the entropy production reproduces exactly for VP processes, and (ii) the more general speciation times from Achilli et al. (2026), which the entropy production also captures, including cases where classes are separated only by covariance structure rather than first moments. The empirical validation on EDM2-XS and Stable Diffusion 1.5 confirms that the entropy-production peaks align with the noise regimes where semantic structure is empirically observed to emerge (consistent with Sclocchi et al., 2025; Li & Chen, 2024). The guidance analysis complements and extends the empirical findings of Kynkäänniemi et al. (2024) on limited-interval guidance by providing an information-theoretic explanation for why guidance is most effective in specific noise windows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: the statistical-physics analysis of diffusion sampling (Biroli et al. 2024; Achilli et al. 2026), which identified sharp speciation transitions but lacked an operational, estimable quantity, and the information-theoretic study of diffusion (Kong et al. 2023; Premkumar 2025; Stančević & Ambrogioni 2026), which tracked mutual information and trajectory-level entropy but not class-conditional uncertainty as a speciation detector. The key departure is showing that the temporal derivative of class-conditional entropy peaks precisely at the speciation window predicted by high-dimensional analysis, and that this signal can be estimated in trained models via iterative posterior updates (building on Koulischer et al. 2025a). The partitioned entropy construction further extends the full class-entropy into a probe of specific semantic decisions at different abstraction levels, going beyond the aggregate picture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, time-localized diagnostic for when and where semantic structure forms during denoising, connecting the symmetry-breaking picture from statistical physics to an information-theoretic observable that can be computed in practice. The guidance analysis offers a new explanation for the empirically observed limited effectiveness of classifier-free guidance: guidance redistributes entropy production toward higher noise levels, accelerating coarse semantic commitment while leaving fine-grained structure largely unaffected. Open questions include whether the VE-EDM broadening of the transition window has practical implications for inference or training, how the results extend to stronger models (SDXL, SD3), and a formal characterization of how guidance controls the magnitude and timing of entropy redistribution.
