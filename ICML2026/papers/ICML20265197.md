## Problem

Existing robot co-design methods converge to a single static morphology, which fails on long-horizon tasks spanning heterogeneous physical domains (e.g., walking, flying, swimming) because no single configuration can satisfy conflicting requirements like traction and aerodynamics simultaneously. While reconfigurable robots can in principle adapt their structure, prior work relies on human-engineered configurations and does not automate the discovery of a configuration library together with a switching policy. The automated co-optimization of dynamic reconfiguration strategies—jointly designing multiple configurations and learning when to switch among them—remains an unexplored frontier.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a raw robot embodiment with J hinge joints and a heterogeneous terrain map (composed of sequential sub-terrains such as flat ground, stairs, tunnels, mountains, and water) as input, along with per-step observations including terrain height fields and proprioceptive state (position, velocity). It produces a library of K discrete configurations, each defined by a binary lock mask (which joints are rigidly fixed) and a continuous lock-angle vector (the fixed angles), together with a per-configuration control policy and a high-level scheduler that selects the active configuration every M simulation steps based on global task progress. The multi-physics MuJoCo environment models rigid-body contact plus simplified fluid forces (drag, lift, buoyancy) with per-link submersion detection to handle water-air interfaces.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-level hierarchical pipeline. At the low level, a multi-tail policy architecture disentangles configuration design from control into three specialized heads—lock mask (Bernoulli), lock angle (Gaussian), and motor control (Gaussian)—sharing a common feature extractor, so that design and control gradients do not conflict. Each primitive is trained per sub-task with PPO using a staged procedure (mask first, then angles with a warm-up freeze, then control) and an ε-greedy exploration strategy on the design tails to prevent premature convergence to local optima; design policies use a single-step temporal credit assignment formulation (episodic return as reward) borrowed from BodyGen. At the high level, all primitives are frozen and a lightweight MLP scheduler is trained with PPO to select among the K configurations every M steps, with a shared PD transition controller smoothing the kinematic gap between configurations and a reconfiguration penalty discouraging excessive switching. This temporal abstraction decouples long-horizon planning from high-frequency local control, which the paper shows is critical: end-to-end joint optimization of scheduler and primitives fails due to conflicting gradient landscapes.

## Results

The paper evaluates on 7 heterogeneous multi-physics locomotion tasks (fly-walk, car-leg, wheel-leg, wheel-fly) using normalized traversal progress S (distance traveled / map length). The proposed hierarchical co-optimization pipeline achieves 5.97× average progress over PPO, 10.70× over TD-MPC2, and 1.19× over human-designed domain-expert configurations. Against morphology co-design baselines, it achieves 10.52× over BodyGen and 9.10× over GLSO on average, with scores frequently exceeding 1.0 (full map traversal) while baselines hover around 0.3–0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) applied to the raw robot without reconfiguration, the proposed method wins on all 7 tasks by a large margin (e.g., 1.978 vs. 0.403 on Fly-Walk Mount; 9.148 vs. 0.210 on Wheel-Leg), with a 5.97× average improvement. TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2023) is even weaker, achieving 10.70× lower average progress, as both reconfiguration-free policies overfit to a single sub-task and fail at terrain transitions. Against BodyGen (Lu et al., 2025), the method achieves 10.52× average progress (e.g., 1.978 vs. 0.498 on Fly-Walk Mount; 9.148 vs. FAIL on Wheel-Leg), as BodyGen converges to a single compromise morphology that cannot handle heterogeneous physics. GLSO (Hu et al., 2023) is similarly outperformed by 9.10× on average (e.g., 2.618 vs. 0.536 on Car-Leg Narrow), confirming that static co-design cannot match dynamic reconfiguration for multi-physics traversal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of reconfigurable robotics (M-TRAN, SuperBot, SMORES-EP) and robot co-design (BodyGen, GLSO, Transform2Act), but departs from both in a key way: prior reconfigurable systems rely on human-engineered configuration libraries, while prior co-design methods converge to a single static morphology. The multi-tail architecture borrows the temporal credit assignment mechanism from BodyGen for one-shot design decisions, and the hierarchical structure draws on hierarchical RL for reconfiguration planning (Pathak et al., Whitman et al.), but the first automated joint optimization of a *library* of configurations plus a learned switching scheduler is the novel contribution. The multi-physics MuJoCo environment extends standard locomotion benchmarks by unifying terrestrial, aerial, and aquatic dynamics in a single episode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong evidence that dynamic reconfiguration is not merely beneficial but a *prerequisite* for heterogeneous multi-physics locomotion, with order-of-magnitude gaps over both single-configuration control and single-morphology co-design. However, the work is entirely simulation-based with simplified fluid dynamics, uses pre-defined robot embodiments (not co-designed from scratch), and ignores energy efficiency and manufacturing constraints. Open questions include whether the discovered configurations transfer to physical hardware, whether the pipeline can jointly optimize the base embodiment alongside configurations, and whether the hierarchical decomposition generalizes to even more diverse physical regimes or longer task horizons.
