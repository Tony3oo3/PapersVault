## Problem

Fair allocation of indivisible goods has been extensively studied under the assumption that agent valuations are known exactly or can be queried without noise. This paper introduces the fundamentally different problem of finding an envy-free allocation when valuations can only be accessed through noisy (additive Gaussian) queries, a setting motivated by real-world scenarios where utilities must be estimated via stochastic simulations or averaged over group members. The central question is the query complexity: how many noisy observations are needed to guarantee an envy-free allocation, and how does this depend on the number of items and the gap from the optimal allocation to being envy-free?

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives m indivisible items with additive utilities in [0,1] for two agents, and a query budget q. Each query selects an item and returns a pair of independent Gaussian observations (one per agent) with known variance σ². The algorithm must output a partition of all m items between the two agents that is envy-free (neither agent values the other's bundle more than their own). A key structural constraint is the gap parameter Δ, which lower-bounds the optimal negative envy; the algorithm's success probability and query complexity depend critically on Δ, and the results hold for non-adaptive queries with polynomial-time computation.

## Method

The upper bound uses a simple item-by-item thresholding rule: assign item i to agent a if c·v_i^a > v_i^b for a balancing parameter c chosen from a discrete grid, where v_i^ν are sample-mean estimates. The key analytical insight is decomposing envy into three components—total negative envy captured by the thresholding (bounded via Gaussian tail probabilities and Cauchy-Schwarz), estimation error between true and observed valuations (shown to be Gaussian with zero mean via a covariance calculation), and a balance term that can be made small by an appropriate choice of c (established via a smoothness argument over the discrete grid). The lower bound constructs a randomized hard instance with four item types (slightly/strongly favored or disfavored by each agent) and applies Assouad's lemma with KL divergence chain-rule arguments, showing that the strongly-favored items create random envy fluctuations of order √m that the queried items must overcome. Together these yield matching Õ(m^2.5/Δ²) query complexity bounds when Δ ≫ m^{1/4}·log²m.

## Results

The paper establishes that the optimal number of noisy queries to find an envy-free allocation of m items between two agents is Θ̃(m^2.5/Δ²) when Δ ≫ m^(1/4) log²m, where Δ is the optimal negative envy gap. The upper bound (Theorem 2) is achieved by a non-adaptive, polynomial-time thresholding algorithm, while the lower bound (Theorem 3) holds even for adaptive queries with arbitrary computation. A naive confidence-interval approach (Theorem 1) requires Õ(m³/Δ²) queries, which is suboptimal by a factor of √m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines. The key comparison is between the naive repeated-sampling algorithm (Algorithm 1), which requires Õ(m³/Δ²) queries via a brute-force search over all allocations, and the improved thresholding-based algorithm, which achieves Õ(m^2.5/Δ²) queries with polynomial-time computation. The upper and lower bounds match to within logarithmic factors, establishing tightness. The paper also notes that the m^2.5/Δ² scaling can be naturally interpreted as √m/(Δ/m)², where Δ/m is a normalized gap, connecting the result to the quadratic gap dependence ubiquitous in pure-exploration multi-armed bandit problems. No direct comparison with prior noisy-query allocation work (e.g., Li et al. 2025 on round-robin) is made, as those papers study fundamentally different query models (comparison-based or value-based with constant-probability exact observations) and different algorithmic goals (implementing a specific algorithm vs. finding any envy-free allocation).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a genuinely new problem: envy-free allocation of indivisible goods where valuations can only be accessed through noisy (additive Gaussian) queries on individual items. It departs from the fair-division query-complexity literature (Oh et al. 2021; Bu et al. 2024), which assumes noiseless bundle queries, and from the noisy round-robin work of Li et al. (2025), which uses discrete noise models where repeated queries recover exact answers. The technical toolkit draws on multi-armed bandit pure-exploration methods (concentration inequalities, Assouad's lemma for lower bounds), but the allocation-specific structure—particularly the gap parameter Δ and the item-by-item thresholding rule—repurposes these tools in a way not seen in prior bandit or fair-division work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first tight (up to log factors) query-complexity bounds for a natural and practically motivated problem, with the non-trivial m^{2.5}/Δ² scaling that can be reinterpreted as √m/(Δ/m)², connecting it to the quadratic gap dependence ubiquitous in pure exploration. Key open questions include the regime Δ ≤ O(m^{1/4}) (where the item-by-item thresholding approach may fundamentally fail and bundle-based algorithms may be needed), extension to n > 2 agents (where the single threshold parameter c becomes a combinatorial problem over C(n,2) ratios), and generalization beyond additive valuations and Gaussian noise. The equivalence between envy-freeness and proportionality for two agents means the results immediately transfer to proportionality, broadening the practical relevance.
