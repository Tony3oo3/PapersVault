## Problem

In classical planning, standard RL exploration via real-time search is ineffective for sparse-reward domains, and existing learning-based methods either rely on expert demonstrations, hindsight relabeling, or supervised heuristic learning that cannot self-improve. The paper asks whether best-first search (WA*) can replace real-time search as the exploration mechanism in a self-improving RL loop, enabling a single learned Q-function to generalize across states, goals, and problem sizes within a planning domain.

## Input / Output

The method takes a PDDL planning instance (object set, initial state as a set of ground atoms, and goal as a conjunction of literals) and encodes it as a relational graph where objects are nodes, ground atoms from the state and goal are edges, and each applicable grounded action introduces a dedicated action object with its own atom. The relational GNN produces a scalar Q-value per applicable action; at test time this yields either a greedy policy (argmax over actions) or a heuristic function for guiding WA* search over the full state space.

## Method

GSP (Generalized Search for Planning) creates a self-improving loop: a relational GNN parameterizes Q(s,a), WA* search (w=2) explores sampled instances using f(s,a)=g(s)+w·Q(s,a), and the resulting search data updates Q via Q-learning from a replay buffer. Three supervision signals are combined: standard one-step Bellman bootstrapping for non-terminal transitions, a fixed penalty R⊥ for dead-end states, and search-derived lower bounds on return for goal-path pairs (taking max with the bootstrap target to prevent regression). The GNN uses predicate-specific message passing with explicit action objects so that actions are scored in mutual context, and a shared MLP readout combines the action-object embedding with a pooled state summary. Instance selection dynamically prioritizes unsolved instances over solved ones, and the entire pipeline uses identical hyperparameters across all domains.

## Results

On the 2023 IPC Learning Track (10 domains, 90 test instances each), GSP as a greedy policy (GSP_π) achieves 100% coverage on Blocksworld, Miconic, and Spanner, and 87% on Ferry, outperforming all learning-based baselines on most domains. As a WA* heuristic (GSP_WA*), it improves Sokoban coverage from 14% to 32% and Floortile from 20% to 28%, but underperforms greedy execution on domains with very large branching factors. On the Sokoban puzzle (10×10, 4 boxes), GSP_WA* solves all 1000 test instances with only 207 average expansions, compared to 3298 for the WA* baseline and 1050 for DeepCubeA. On PushWorld Level 0, GSP_π solves 93/200 instances versus 20/200 (DQN) and 11/200 (PPO), and GSP_WA* solves all 200 with shorter plans than LAMA (18 vs. 24 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Lifted HER (Stahlberg & Geffner, 2026), GSP_π wins on Blocksworld (100% vs. 98%), Miconic (100% vs. 90%), and Spanner (100% vs. 97%), while Lifted HER wins on Ferry (100% vs. 87%) and Transport (96% vs. 73%). Against LAMA, GSP_π dominates on Blocksworld (100% vs. 61%), Spanner (100% vs. 33%), and Miconic (100% vs. 100% with shorter plans), but LAMA retains 100% on Satellite and 79% on Rovers due to known 1-WL expressivity limitations of GNNs. Against the supervised heuristic baselines WL-f (Chen, 2025) and Horčík et al. (2025), GSP_π is substantially stronger on Blocksworld (100% vs. 73%/59%) and Miconic (100% vs. 98%/68%), while they lead on Childsnack (52%/71% vs. 41%). On the Sokoban puzzle, GSP_WA* reduces expansions from 3298 (Orseau & Lelis WA*) and 1050 (DeepCubeA) to 207 while solving all 1000 instances, and GSP_π (681 solved) more than doubles Lifted HER's coverage (309). On PushWorld, GSP_π (93/200) outperforms DQN (20/200) and PPO (11/200) by a wide margin, and GSP_WA* matches LAMA's 200/200 coverage with ~4× fewer expansions (1.8K vs. 6.9K). The AlphaZero (MCTS) baseline fails to generalize, achieving 0% coverage on 7 of 10 IPC domains, confirming that best-first search is a more effective exploration mechanism than MCTS for this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the relational GNN architecture for Q-function representation from Stahlberg & Geffner (2026), but replaces their real-time search and hindsight experience replay with best-first WA* search, creating a self-improving loop where search generates training targets and the learned heuristic guides subsequent searches. It departs from the LRTA*/RTDP lineage (which uses stepwise real-time search) and from supervised heuristic-learning approaches (Stahlberg et al. 2022, Horčik et al. 2025, Bai et al. 2025) that require precomputed optimal values, by learning heuristics entirely from search-generated experience. The paper also positions itself against AlphaZero-style MCTS exploration, arguing that best-first search is more effective for single-goal pathfinding with sparse rewards when the transition model is known.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that best-first search is a superior exploration mechanism for generalized model-based RL in sparse-reward planning, yielding heuristics that generalize zero-shot to dramatically larger instances (e.g., <30 blocks to 488 blocks in Blocksworld) without any search at test time. A key open limitation is that the learned Q-function is stronger as a local action-ranking mechanism than as a globally calibrated value function, which degrades WA* performance on high-branching-factor test instances; combining the learned heuristic with classical heuristics like h_ff or with HER-style relabeling are natural next steps. The 1-WL expressivity ceiling on domains like Rovers and Satellite remains unresolved for all GNN-based methods, and the failure on 24-puzzle (where the model cannot find a single training solution within budget) highlights the sensitivity of the self-improving loop to initial heuristic quality.
