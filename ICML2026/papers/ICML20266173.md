## Problem

Current text-guided image inpainting models rely on supervised reconstruction of a single ground truth, which forces the model into 'probability averaging' when instructions admit multiple valid completions, producing over-smoothed, low-diversity outputs. No prior work has applied online reinforcement learning (GRPO or DPO) to the inpainting setting, where the small masked region makes global reward signals insensitive to local quality differences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266173/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (1024×1024 for Flux.1-fill, 512×512 for SD1.5-inpainting), a binary mask indicating pixels to regenerate, and a free-form text prompt describing the desired content in the masked region. During training, prompts are generated by multimodal LLMs and are deliberately unpaired with the masked image, decoupling the rigid one-to-one text-image correspondence of supervised training. The output is a full-resolution inpainted image that preserves unmasked pixels exactly while filling the masked region to match the prompt and blend seamlessly with surrounding context.

## Method

The paper formulates inpainting as a sequential decision process and applies GRPO (primary) and DPO (alternative) as online policy optimizers. Stochastic exploration is enabled by injecting Gaussian noise into the deterministic ODE/flow-matching sampling step, yielding a tractable Gaussian transition kernel for policy gradient computation. A key architectural choice is decoupling text-image pairing: the model samples K=12 candidates per prompt using unpaired prompts, and the group-relative advantage (Eq. 3) drives updates without a critic network. To address the 'background dominance' problem where global aesthetic scores barely differentiate small edits, the paper introduces a dynamic reward R = (1−λ)·R_boundary + λ·(R_align + R_quality), where λ is the mask coverage ratio and R_boundary is a Boundary Gradient Consistency Score (BGCS) computed from Sobel gradient magnitudes in morphologically extracted inner/outer boundary strips. This adaptively prioritizes seam-free blending for small occlusions and visual aesthetics for large-scale repainting, with all components normalized within each generation group before weighting.

## Results

On FluxBench, Flux.1(Ours) achieves HPSv3 of 4.461, CLIP of 25.61, and BGCS of −0.0163, substantially outperforming the base Flux.1 (HPSv3 3.099, BGCS −0.0369) and all adapter-based baselines. On EditBench, Flux.1(Ours) reaches HPSv3 of 2.779 and BGCS of −0.0042, the best boundary score among all methods. On BrushBench (512×512), SD1.5(Ours) attains BGCS of −0.1157, nearly doubling the boundary quality of BrushNetX (−0.2222) and PowerPaint v2 (−0.1364), while matching or exceeding them on HPSv2.1 and PickScore. In human evaluation, Flux.1(Ours) receives 410 preference votes versus 122 for BrushNetX and 98 for PowerPaint v2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flux.1(Ours) decisively outperforms all baselines on FluxBench and EditBench: it surpasses BrushNetX by +7.5 in HPSv3 (4.461 vs 3.885) and by +0.0135 in BGCS (−0.0163 vs −0.0298) on FluxBench, and leads PowerPaint v2 by +0.74 in HPSv3 (2.779 vs 2.038) on EditBench. Against PrefPaint (PPO-based), the gap is even larger—Flux.1(Ours) scores 4.461 vs −1.651 in HPSv3 on FluxBench, indicating PrefPaint's PPO training destabilizes generation. On BrushBench (512×512, SD1.5 series), SD1.5(Ours) trails BrushNetX and PowerPaint v2 slightly on HPSv3 (0.962 vs 1.099/1.446) but dominates on BGCS (−0.1157 vs −0.2222/−0.1364), confirming that the dynamic boundary reward is the key differentiator for small-mask inpainting. The SFT variants of both backbones show minimal or negative gains over the base models, suggesting the dataset is too small for supervised fine-tuning to be effective, whereas the RL approach consistently improves quality without additional inference parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online preference-alignment line (DDPO, PrefPaint, DanceGRPO, Flow-GRPO) from text-to-image generation to text-guided image inpainting, which had previously been addressed only by supervised reconstruction or PPO-based methods (PrefPaint). It departs from the dominant supervised inpainting paradigm (BrushNet, PowerPaint, FLUX.1 Fill) by decoupling the rigid one-to-one text-image pairing and instead using unpaired prompts with online exploration. The inpainting-specific dynamic reward mechanism (BGCS + mask-ratio-adaptive weighting) is a novel contribution not present in prior T2I RL work, addressing the 'background dominance' problem unique to local editing tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, parameter-free post-training recipe that improves both SD1.5 and FLUX.1 inpainting without architectural modifications, making it broadly applicable to existing production models. The finding that SFT with the same 24K-image dataset underperforms the base model while RL yields consistent gains is a useful signal that the supervised paradigm is fundamentally limited for diverse inpainting. Open questions remain around perceptually meaningful boundary metrics (BGCS is a proxy, not a true perceptual measure) and whether the approach scales to video inpainting or multi-region editing. The paper also raises the important safety concern of advanced inpainting enabling visual forgery.
