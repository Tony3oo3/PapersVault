## Problem

Theoretical guarantees for time-inhomogeneous Langevin diffusions (used in annealed Langevin sampling, geometric tempering, and diffusion-model-based samplers) are fragmented across papers, each specialized to a particular annealing path, and most control the backward-KL divergence rather than the forward-KL divergence that naturally arises as the Lyapunov functional of the Fokker-Planck equation. Additionally, existing analyses implicitly assume strong solutions of the Fokker-Planck equation, whereas only weak solutions are guaranteed in general. This paper provides a single unified non-asymptotic convergence theorem in forward-KL for both the continuous-time diffusion and its Euler-Maruyama discretization under abstract path assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method requires a potential U (or its gradient ∇U), an annealing schedule τ(t) that decreases to zero, and an initial distribution; the step sizes are set to the theoretical maximum h_k = a_{τ(t_k)}/L_{τ(t_k)}², where a and L are the dissipativity and Lipschitz constants of the time-dependent potential. The output is a sequence of samples whose distribution converges to the target π, accompanied by explicit forward-KL convergence bounds that decompose into a tracking error (proportional to τ(t)), an exponential mixing term governed by the log-Sobolev constant, and a schedule-speed term. In the image denoising experiment, the input is a noisy 256×256 CelebA image and a learned prior (DiffUNet), and the output is a denoised posterior mean estimate.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a unified convergence analysis under five abstract assumptions on the path of potentials (quadratic growth of ∂_τ U_τ, uniform Lipschitz continuity, uniform dissipativity, and a uniform log-Sobolev inequality). The key lemma (Lemma 4.2) derives the time-derivative of the forward-KL divergence as −∫q_t|∇log(q_t/p_{τ(t)})|² − q_t ∂_t log p_{τ(t)} dx, proven rigorously via mollification techniques that handle the fact that the Fokker-Planck equation is only satisfied in the weak sense. The resulting bound (Lemma 4.3) shows KL(μ_t|π) ≤ c·τ(t) + exp(−∫4/C_LSI ds)·KL(μ_0|π_{τ(0)}) + c∫exp(−∫4/C_LSI)·|τ̇| ds, yielding convergence as τ(t)→0. The paper then verifies that geometric tempering, dilation, the Moreau-envelope (DAZ) path, and the convolutional path all satisfy the abstract assumptions, and for the Euler-Maruyama discretization proves bias-free convergence under square-summable (but not summable) step sizes.

## Results

The paper establishes forward-KL convergence of the time-inhomogeneous Langevin diffusion (Theorem 4.4) and its Euler-Maruyama discretization (Theorem 4.7) under abstract path assumptions, with worst-case complexity t = O(t₁ + log(ε⁻¹)) in continuous time and k = O(T₁ε⁻¹ + log(ε⁻¹)h⁻¹) for constant step size. Numerically, across 1D, 2D, and 10D Gaussian mixture targets, the convolutional path and DAZ (Moreau envelope) consistently achieve the lowest forward-KL divergence, followed by geometric tempering, dilation, and ULA. In the CelebA image denoising experiment (256×256, σ=0.5), annealing schemes produce clean denoised images while ULA retains visible noise artifacts after 1700 iterations, and the convolutional path shows the steepest MSE convergence slope due to favorable log-Sobolev constants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five sampling schemes: ULA (no annealing), geometric tempering, dilation, the convolutional path (annealed Langevin sampling), and DAZ (Moreau envelope path). DAZ and the convolutional path dominate across all dimensions (1D, 2D, 10D GMM) and the image denoising task, achieving the lowest KL divergence and best mode coverage; DAZ in particular performs best in the 10D setting where the gap to other methods is most pronounced. Geometric tempering is a clear middle performer, improving over ULA but failing to match the top two, especially at fast annealing speeds (small T). Dilation is the second-worst method because it increases the Lipschitz constant (L_τ = L₀/(1−τ)), forcing the smallest allowed step sizes and causing the sampler to stagnate far from the origin. ULA is the weakest baseline in every setting, with the highest KL divergence and, in image denoising, visible noise artifacts persisting well beyond 1700 iterations. A notable exception is that with a fixed (non-adaptive) step size and very slow annealing (T=10), geometric tempering narrows the gap to the top methods, suggesting its benefit is partly step-size-driven.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the fragmented convergence analyses of time-inhomogeneous Langevin diffusions, which previously existed only for specific paths: geometric tempering (Chehab et al., 2025), convolutional paths (Cordero-Encinar et al., 2025), and Moreau-envelope paths (Habring et al., 2026). It departs from prior work in three key ways: (1) it proves convergence in forward-KL rather than the backward-KL used by most existing analyses, which is the natural Lyapunov functional of the Fokker-Planck equation; (2) it rigorously handles the case where the density is only a weak solution of the Fokker-Planck equation via mollification, rather than assuming classical smoothness; and (3) it establishes bias-free convergence for vanishing step sizes that are square-summable but not summable. The discrete-time analysis builds directly on the KL-descent lemma of Vempala & Wibisono (2019), extended to the time-inhomogeneous setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides practitioners with a single set of abstract conditions under which a wide family of annealing schemes (geometric tempering, dilation, DAZ, convolutional paths, and Bayesian posteriors) are guaranteed to converge, along with concrete path-design guidelines (small LSI constants, fast-decreasing τ, small Lipschitz constants). The main limitation is that the analysis requires smooth potentials and does not provide explicit LSI constants, making optimal schedule design an open problem. Future work on deriving explicit LSI bounds for specific paths would enable data-driven choice of annealing schedules. The numerical experiments confirm that well-conditioned paths (convolutional, DAZ) yield both faster convergence and better mode coverage, with the practical implication that path choice matters more than step-size tuning.
