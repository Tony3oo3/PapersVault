## Problem

Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) has shown promise for improving LLM correctness, but in materials generation the goal is not a single correct answer but a diverse set of candidates satisfying physical constraints. Naive preference optimization on coordinate-based crystal representations causes mode collapse, and prior generative models either lack explicit crystallographic symmetry encoding or suffer from high inference costs, limiting their ability to produce stable, unique, and novel structures at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265218/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language prompt as input, optionally augmented with a target space group number for conditional generation (e.g., 'Generate a crystal structure with space group 194'). The output is a crystal structure encoded in a compact Wyckoff-based text format that specifies the chemical formula, space group number, lattice parameters, and per-atom Wyckoff site labels with fractional coordinates and site multiplicities. This encoding averages 185.5 tokens per crystal versus 214.7 for coordinate-based strings, and scales efficiently because additional atoms at the same Wyckoff site require only a multiplicity update rather than new coordinate lines.

## Method

PLaID++ fine-tunes Qwen-2.5 7B (via LoRA) on Wyckoff text representations of MP-20 crystals, then applies iterative Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) using a framework called RLIP (Reinforcement Learning from Interatomic Potentials). The key insight is that encoding symmetry constraints directly into the text representation prevents the mode collapse observed with 3D coordinate encodings, because local coordinate changes propagate through symmetry operations to produce globally distinct structures. Preference pairs are constructed in a tiered fashion (stable ≻ metastable ≻ unstable) using eqV2 MLIP energy-above-hull predictions, with dynamic temperature increases across DPO iterations acting as an entropy regularizer to preserve diversity. The model is jointly trained on unconditional and space-group-conditioned preference data, and the pipeline generalizes to arbitrary property targets (e.g., bulk modulus, band gap) by simply adding new preference pair tiers under the same iterative DPO procedure.

## Results

On the MP-20 unconditional generation benchmark, PLaID++ achieves 22.27% stability and 7.74% S.U.N. rate, outperforming the best prior method (ADiT at 15.4% stability and 5.3% S.U.N.) by ~45% and ~46% respectively. On the stricter LeMat-GenBench (using ~5M-structure LeMat-Bulk as reference), PLaID++ achieves 12.4% Stable% and 1.0% S.U.N.%, more than 3× the next-best model (WyFormer-DFT at 3.7% Stable%) and 5× the next-best S.U.N. rate (MatterGen at 0.2%). PLaID++ also achieves the highest compositional validity (97.34%) and generates 10,000 crystals in ~23 minutes on a single H100 GPU, 5× faster than FlowLLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLaID++ outperforms all nine baselines on the two primary metrics—stability and S.U.N. rate—on MP-20. Against the strongest prior method ADiT (15.4% stability, 5.3% S.U.N.), PLaID++ improves stability by 6.9 percentage points and S.U.N. by 2.4 percentage points (~46% relative gain). FlowLLM (13.9% stability, 4.7% S.U.N.) and MatterGen-MP (13.0% stability) are the next-closest competitors but trail by 8+ points on stability. On the external LeMat-GenBench, PLaID++ (12.4% Stable%, 1.0% S.U.N.) dominates all evaluated models, exceeding WyFormer-DFT (3.7%, 0.4%) by over 3× on stability and MatterGen (2.0%, 0.2%) by 5× on S.U.N. PLaID++ matches or exceeds all baselines on compositional validity (97.34% vs. CrystalLLM-70B's 95.40%) while remaining competitive on structural validity (99.75% vs. CDVAE/DiffCSP's 100%). In generation throughput, PLaID++ produces 27.17 S.U.N. crystals per minute on a single H100, 5× faster than FlowLLM's 5.25 per minute on an A100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CrystalLLM line of work (Gruver et al., 2024), which demonstrated that fine-tuned LLMs can generate stable inorganic crystals from text, by introducing a preference-optimization stage (RLIP) that uses MLIP-predicted energies as reward signals. It departs from prior symmetry-aware crystal generators (WyFormer, CrystFormer, CrystalICL) that rely on explicit space-group conditioning and template search, instead encoding Wyckoff positions directly into the text representation so the model learns symmetry implicitly. The iterative DPO framework with dynamic temperature scheduling is adapted from NLP preference optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023) but repurposed here with tiered stability/novelty/symmetry preference pairs, and the key methodological departure is showing that coordinate-based representations cause mode collapse under DPO while the Wyckoff encoding prevents it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PLaID++ achieves state-of-the-art stability (22.27%) and S.U.N. rate (7.74%) on MP-20, with external validation on LeMat-GenBench confirming the gains under a stricter protocol, and a 5× throughput advantage over FlowLLM that is practically important for high-throughput screening pipelines. The finding that joint training across conditional and unconditional tasks transfers benefit in data-sparse regimes (e.g., space groups with <400 training samples) suggests a general recipe for multi-task RL in scientific domains. Open questions include whether the approach scales to million-structure datasets (Alexandria, LeMaterial), whether alternative RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO) yield different stability-diversity trade-offs, and whether the low absolute S.U.N. rates (~1% under LeMat-GenBench) are sufficient for real-world discovery or whether the reward design needs further refinement.
