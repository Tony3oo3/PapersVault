## Problem

Current LLM agents rely on fixed cognitive patterns—non-thinking models produce reflexive actions while thinking models engage in deep reasoning uniformly—creating a critical inefficiency in long-horizon agentic tasks where cognitive demands vary dramatically from step to step. Standard RL methods like GRPO exacerbate this by collapsing to uniform deep thinking (L4), wasting computational resources on routine steps that require only instinctive responses. The paper asks: how can agents be trained to adaptively allocate cognitive depth across multiple levels at each step, balancing task performance with token efficiency?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265454/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step, the agent receives a task instruction, the current environment observation, and the full interaction history (sequence of prior observation-action pairs). It produces a structured three-part output: a cognitive level tag (<level>1</level> through <level>4</level>), a thinking process in <think> tags whose structure scales with the selected level (L1 uses a fixed placeholder string; L2 adds current-state and available-actions assessment; L3 adds goal and reflection; L4 adds multi-candidate evaluation), and an executable action in <action> tags. The four levels form a strict hierarchy where each higher level's thinking format is a superset of the one below, grounding the design in ACT-R's procedural-to-declarative spectrum.

## Method

COGROUTER uses a two-stage pipeline. First, COSFT performs supervised fine-tuning on expert trajectories (GPT-4o) where each step is annotated with a randomly sampled cognitive level, enforcing a balanced ~25% distribution across all four levels to prevent the model from inheriting a single reasoning bias. Second, COPO is a novel RL algorithm that extends GRPO with step-level credit assignment via confidence-aware advantage reweighting: for each successful trajectory, the model regenerates its thinking process under all four cognitive levels while keeping the observation and action fixed, forming a 'cognitive group.' The action prediction confidence (average log-probability of the action tokens) under each level is normalized and passed through a temperature-scaled softmax to produce relative weights that redistribute the trajectory-level advantage across levels. This mechanism rewards the cognitive depth that most confidently produces the correct action at each step, preventing the collapse to uniform L4 thinking observed in GRPO and GiGPO while maintaining a balanced, contextually adaptive cognitive profile.

## Results

Using Qwen2.5-7B as the base model, COGROUTER (CoPO) achieves a 92.5% success rate on ALFWorld and 72.0% on ScienceWorld (82.3% average SR), outperforming GPT-4o (+40.3%), OpenAI-o3 (+18.3%), and GRPO (+14.0%) while consuming 62% fewer tokens than GRPO (1,641 vs. 4,367 average tokens per task). With Llama3.1-8B, the method reaches 81.0% average SR with only 887.7 tokens, a 79% token reduction over GRPO (4,849.4 tokens). CoPO also converges faster, reaching 90% ALFWorld success within 100 RL iterations versus GRPO's 83.5% at 150 iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoPO decisively outperforms all RL baselines: over GRPO it gains +9.0% ALFWorld SR and +19.0% ScienceWorld SR while using 62% fewer tokens; over GiGPO it gains +4.5% and +25.0% SR respectively. Against the adaptive-thinking baseline AdaptThink, CoPO improves average SR by 19.3% (82.3% vs. 63.0%) with only 22% more tokens. Among frontier models, CoPO surpasses GPT-4o by +40.3% average SR, OpenAI-o3 by +18.3%, and DeepSeek-R1 by +25.8%, while consuming far fewer tokens than the thinking models (1,641 vs. 4,737 for o3 and 11,367 for R1). Prompt-based methods (ReAct, Reflexion) and offline training methods (SFT, ETO) are substantially outperformed, with CoPO achieving 82.3% vs. 26.5% (ReAct) and 45.5% (ETO) average SR. The Llama3.1-8B results confirm generalizability: CoPO reaches 81.0% average SR with 887.7 tokens, beating GRPO (67.3%, 4,849.4 tokens) and GiGPO (76.8%, 5,381.7 tokens) on both accuracy and efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO/GiGPO line of group-based RL for LLM agents by introducing step-level, multi-granularity credit assignment grounded in ACT-R cognitive theory. It departs from prior adaptive-thinking work (AdaptThink, AdaCoT) that operates at the task level with binary think/no-think switching, instead formulating a four-level hierarchical cognitive depth that is selected independently at each step. The confidence-aware advantage reweighting in COPO is a novel mechanism that uses the model's own action-prediction confidence as a proxy for cognitive-level appropriateness, rather than relying on external reward signals or state-similarity grouping as in GiGPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard trajectory-level RL (GRPO, GiGPO) rapidly collapses to uniform deep thinking despite balanced initialization is a practical warning for anyone training adaptive agents: without step-level credit signals, RL will default to the 'safest' (deepest) reasoning mode. The 62% token reduction alongside a 14-point success-rate gain over GRPO makes this directly relevant to deployment cost. Open questions include whether the four-level ACT-R taxonomy is optimal, whether the approach scales to larger models or more complex multi-agent environments, and whether the confidence metric generalizes beyond the two text-based benchmarks tested here.
