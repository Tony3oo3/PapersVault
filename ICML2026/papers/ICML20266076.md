## Problem

Existing vericoding benchmarks either test a single verification language or aggregate unaligned tasks across tools, making cross-paradigm performance comparisons meaningless. Moreover, they predominantly target simple input-output functions where correctness follows from local reasoning, leaving open whether LLMs can verify classical algorithms whose correctness depends on global invariants (e.g., red-black tree balance, graph reachability, flow conservation). ALGOVERI addresses both gaps by providing the first multi-language suite with strictly aligned specifications for 77 textbook algorithms requiring global reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model receives a natural-language algorithm description, a formal specification (preconditions, postconditions, helper predicates), and an incomplete code skeleton in Dafny, Verus, or Lean. It must produce a complete implementation plus all proof artifacts (loop invariants, ghost assertions, or tactic scripts) that the verifier accepts. Evaluation is multi-turn with up to 15 repair rounds driven by compiler/verifier error messages, and a final LLM semantic filter rejects solutions that pass verification but implement the wrong algorithm or use degenerate tricks (e.g., `assume false`, `sorry`).

## Method

ALGOVERI formalizes 77 classical algorithms (heaps, segment trees, red-black trees, Bellman-Ford, Edmonds-Karp, Gaussian elimination, DP/greedy problems) into semantically identical specifications across Dafny, Verus, and Lean, ensuring that a 'success' in one system corresponds to the same logical difficulty in another. The evaluation pipeline prompts the LLM to generate implementation and proof artifacts, then iteratively feeds back compiler/verifier errors for up to 15 repair rounds; success requires the verifier to accept the proof under the given specification. An LLM judge then performs a semantic check to confirm the code implements the intended algorithm rather than a simpler variant. Beyond aggregate metrics, the paper conducts an iso-compute analysis comparing iterative repair (depth) against parallel sampling (width) and a fine-grained error decomposition (syntax, type, verification, hallucination) that reveals how language design shapes the repair trajectory across the three systems.

## Results

On ALGOVERI's 77 classical algorithm problems, the best frontier model (Gemini-3 Flash, 15 repair rounds) achieves 55.84% compiler-verified and 40.26% full-mark accuracy in Dafny, 25.97%/24.68% in Verus, and 9.09%/7.79% in Lean. GPT-5.3 Codex (8 rounds) reaches 49.35%/42.86% in Dafny but drops to 14.29%/11.69% in Verus and 23.38%/11.69% in Lean. Open-weight models (GPT-OSS-120B, Qwen3-235B, Devstral-2-123B) achieve at most 21–25% in Dafny and under 10% in Verus/Lean with a single 15-round pass, though 10× parallel sampling roughly doubles their Dafny scores (e.g., GPT-OSS-120B: 21.04% → 44.16%). The steep Dafny→Verus→Lean gradient (40.3% → 24.7% → 7.8% for Gemini-3) reveals that vericoding of global-property algorithms remains far from solved, in stark contrast to prior benchmarks reporting 80%+ on Dafny.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALGOVERI is a benchmark rather than a method paper, so the primary comparisons are across verification systems and model tiers. Across all models, Dafny consistently outperforms Verus by 2–3× and Lean by 5–7×, confirming that SMT automation (Dafny) is currently the most tractable paradigm for LLMs, while Verus's Rust-macro syntax barrier and Lean's tactic-search space impose fundamentally different failure modes. Compared to prior multi-lingual benchmark VeriCoding (Bursuc et al., 2025), which reported >80% on Dafny and ~40% on Verus, ALGOVERI's aligned, global-property specifications yield dramatically lower scores (40.3% Dafny, 24.7% Verus, 7.8% Lean for the best model), demonstrating that prior high numbers were inflated by rudimentary tasks. Among frontier models, Gemini-3 Flash leads in Dafny (55.84%) and Verus (25.97%), while GPT-5.3 Codex is competitive in Dafny (49.35%) and surprisingly stronger in Lean (23.38% vs. 9.09%). For open-weight models, the iso-compute analysis shows that iterative repair (depth) is inefficient: GPT-OSS-120B saturates by round 3–4, and 15-round repair chains fail to beat the pure parallel-sampling baseline, meaning width (10×15) is the more effective compute allocation strategy for current open models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM code generation and formal verification by introducing the first benchmark with strictly aligned specifications across three verification paradigms (Dafny, Verus, Lean). Prior benchmarks either target a single language (CLEVER, DafnyBench, VeruSAGE) or aggregate unaligned tasks across languages (VeriCoding), making cross-system comparisons confounded by specification difficulty. ALGOVERI departs from these by enforcing identical functional contracts and targeting textbook algorithms requiring global invariant reasoning (e.g., red-black tree balance, graph reachability), rather than the basic input-output functions that dominate existing suites. The iterative repair evaluation protocol draws on the broader test-time compute literature (Wu et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2024) but applies it specifically to the neuro-symbolic verification loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that vericoding of complex algorithms is far from solved, with even frontier models achieving only ~40% in Dafny and under 8% in Lean, challenging the optimistic picture painted by prior work reporting 80%+ success rates on easier tasks. The finding that language design fundamentally shapes the repair trajectory—Dafny enables logic-focused refinement while Verus and Lean trap models in syntax/search barriers—has direct implications for how verification toolchains should be designed for LLM use. Open questions include whether the ITP gap will close as models improve, whether the SMT trust-base limitation becomes a practical concern at scale, and whether the 'algorithmic fidelity' problem (models gaming specifications) can be addressed without weakening formal guarantees. The small benchmark size (77 problems) and limited model coverage (one frontier, one open model in the repair dynamics analysis) leave room for broader validation.
