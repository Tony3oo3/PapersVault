## Problem

Current VLA models operate as direct input-to-action mappings that cannot monitor execution status, reason dynamically, or recover from runtime errors. Existing reasoning methods impose rigid 'reason-at-every-step' overhead, while error-handling approaches rely on external LLM/VLM monitors that are architecturally cumbersome and bottlenecked by the VLA's limited ability to follow complex external corrections.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262561/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the model consumes a single RGB image observation and a natural language task instruction, and produces a continuous robot action (via flow matching), a discrete status label from {Initial, Normal, New-subtask, Error}, and an updated thought memory holding the task plan, current subtask, and error reflections. In the dominant 'Normal' state no new thoughts are generated—the model reuses the existing thought memory and directly outputs an action, keeping real-world inference at 13 ms per step on an RTX 4090.

## Method

Sentinel-VLA introduces a three-expert architecture (VLM, action, and a novel Status Monitor) built on PI0, where the Status Monitor processes a learnable [MONITOR] query token that probes the VLM's KV cache via cross-attention to trigger on-demand reasoning only when the status is Initial, New-subtask, or Error. Training is fully end-to-end with a unified loss combining flow matching for actions, cross-entropy for thought generation, and cross-entropy for status classification. The EC-Gen pipeline automatically synthesizes 2.6M+ transitions across 44 RLBench tasks by injecting three error modalities (gripper interaction, spatial pose, semantic target) into expert trajectories with template-based CoT annotations, masking the action loss on erroneous waypoints. For continual learning, SECL identifies boundary settings where success rates fall in [20%, 80%], collects successful rollouts, and trains online LoRA adapters constrained by an orthogonality penalty (‖B_offline × B_online‖²) to prevent catastrophic forgetting, followed by EMA fusion into the offline weight base.

## Results

Sentinel-VLA achieves 63.5% average success rate on RLBench Seen tasks, 51.3% on RLBench Unseen tasks, 90.7% on LIBERO-LONG, and 60.0% on real-world tasks, outperforming the SOTA baseline PI0 (57.8%, 42.0%, 85.2%, 46.0% respectively). Under high disturbance (5% random perturbation per step), Sentinel-VLA maintains 54.7% success rate versus PI0's 46.0% and OpenVLA's 25.6%. Inference latency is 13 ms/action on an RTX 4090, comparable to non-reasoning PI0 (8.5 ms) and dramatically faster than CoT-based ECoT (1528 ms) or AHA+OpenVLA (547 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sentinel-VLA outperforms all five baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest baseline PI0, it gains +5.7 pp on RLBench Seen, +9.3 pp on RLBench Unseen, +5.5 pp on LIBERO-LONG, and +14 pp on real-world tasks. Compared to the CoT-reasoning method ECoT, Sentinel-VLA improves by 11–25 pp on success rate while reducing inference time from 1528 ms to 13 ms per action (≈117× speedup). Against the external-monitor approach AHA+OpenVLA, Sentinel-VLA leads by 10–24 pp and is 42× faster (13 ms vs 547 ms). Versus OneTwoVLA, which also attempts selective reasoning, Sentinel-VLA wins by 6.6 pp on RLBench Seen, 7.3 pp on Unseen, 2.9 pp on LIBERO-LONG, and 8 pp on real-world, while being 2.8× faster (13 ms vs 37 ms). OpenVLA, the weakest baseline, trails Sentinel-VLA by 28–29 pp on average across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sentinel-VLA builds directly on the PI0 flow-matching VLA architecture, inheriting its PaliGemma VLM and Gemma action expert, while introducing a dedicated Status Monitor expert that departs from both static chain-of-thought methods (ECoT, CoT-VLA) that reason at every step and external-monitor approaches (AHA, Phoenix, Racer) that rely on separate LLM/VLM modules for error detection. The on-demand reasoning paradigm—triggering deep cognition only at planning, subtask transitions, or detected errors—represents a conceptual shift from the 'always think' or 'never think' extremes in prior VLA work. The SECL algorithm with its Orthogonal Continual Adapter extends LoRA-based continual learning by explicitly constraining new adapter updates to be orthogonal to previously learned ones, a mechanism not present in prior VLA fine-tuning pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying VLA models: their inability to detect and recover from execution errors without external supervision, which limits reliability in real-world manipulation. The 13ms inference latency (vs. 1528ms for ECoT) demonstrates that metacognitive reasoning need not sacrifice real-time control, making the approach viable for high-frequency robotic applications. However, evaluation is limited to RLBench simulation and a single Agilex Piper arm across only 3 real-world tasks, leaving open questions about scalability to multi-robot settings, more diverse failure modes beyond the three injected error types, and whether the status monitor generalizes to fundamentally different embodiments or task domains.
