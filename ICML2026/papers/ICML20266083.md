## Problem

Spurious correlations cause models to rely on biased features rather than true causal signals, leading to poor generalization under distribution shift. Existing robustness methods either require expensive human bias annotations or depend on pretrained foundation models limited to physically interpretable biases, leaving a gap for handling unknown or abstract biases without any bias supervision.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (e.g., 32×32 for cMNIST/cCIFAR10, 224×224 for Waterbirds/CelebA) or text (CivilComments) and outputs class labels. Unusually, the pipeline operates in three stages: a biased feature extractor is first trained on a γ-fraction subset to produce latent representations dominated by spurious factors, then class-specific sample weights are optimized in this latent space, and finally a classifier is trained via weighted random sampling. The intermediate output of Stage 2 is a per-sample weight vector per class, not a model prediction.

## Method

CCDB reframes spurious correlations as mismatches between class-conditional and marginal distributions of spurious features, and addresses them by maximizing the conditional entropy H(y|z_s) through class-specific sample reweighting. The key mechanism minimizes the 2-Wasserstein distance (under a Gaussian approximation) between each reweighted class-conditional distribution p(z|y=k, w_k) and the frozen marginal p(z), with weights constrained to a per-class simplex. This single objective simultaneously reduces distribution imbalance (the KL/mutual-information term) and class imbalance (the entropy term, handled implicitly by the WeightedRandomSampler enforcing uniform class priors). Stage 1 trains a biased feature extractor on a data subset with an intra-class compactness regularizer to amplify spurious-factor dominance, and Stage 3 trains the final classifier using the learned weights via weighted random sampling, requiring no bias annotations at any stage.

## Results

On the multi-class synthetic benchmarks, CCDB achieves 83.20% worst-group accuracy on cMNIST (0.5% bias-conflicting ratio) and 55.07% on cCIFAR10 (0.5%), substantially outperforming the next-best bias-agnostic method GERNE (77.25% and 39.90% respectively). On real-world datasets, CCDB attains 90.48% worst-group accuracy on Waterbirds, 85.27% on CelebA, and 75.00% on CivilComments, matching or exceeding bias-supervised GroupDRO (91.40%, 88.90%, 73.70%) without requiring any bias annotations during training or validation. The method also achieves competitive i.i.d. accuracy (92.59%, 90.08%, 83.60%), demonstrating that robustness gains do not come at a large cost to standard performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCDB outperforms all bias-agnostic baselines on worst-group accuracy across most settings: it beats GERNE by 0.6% on Waterbirds WGA (90.48 vs 89.88), by 11.0% on CelebA WGA (85.27 vs 74.24), and by 11.9% on CivilComments WGA (75.00 vs 63.10). Against uLA (which relies on expensive MoCoV2+ pretraining), CCDB wins on Waterbirds WGA (90.48 vs 86.10) but trails slightly on CelebA WGA (85.27 vs 86.50) and i.i.d. accuracy (90.08 vs 93.90). Compared to XRM, CCDB leads on Waterbirds WGA (90.48 vs 86.10) and CivilComments WGA (75.00 vs 70.10) but is slightly behind on CelebA WGA (85.27 vs 88.50). Against the bias-supervised GroupDRO, CCDB is within 0.9% on Waterbirds WGA (90.48 vs 91.40) and 3.6% on CelebA WGA (85.27 vs 88.90) while surpassing it on CivilComments WGA (75.00 vs 73.70), all without any bias annotations. On the multi-class cMNIST benchmark at the most extreme 0.5% bias-conflicting ratio, CCDB achieves 83.20% WGA, outperforming GERNE (77.25%), uLA (75.13%), and even the bias-supervised GroupDRO (63.12%), demonstrating a clear advantage in challenging multi-class settings where bias-supervised methods degrade significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stable learning line of work (Zhang et al., 2021; Yu et al., 2023) that uses sample reweighting to decorrelate features, but departs fundamentally by replacing global decorrelation with class-specific reweighting that maximizes the conditional entropy of labels given spurious factors. Rather than treating spurious correlations as a group-imbalance problem requiring bias annotations (GroupDRO, DFR) or bias predictions (LFF, JTT, XRM), CCDB reframes them as class-conditional distribution mismatches, connecting the objective to the covariate balance principle in causal inference. The method also diverges from prior bias-agnostic approaches (uLA, GERNE, MASKTUNE) by avoiding the need for pretrained foundation models or explicit feature decorrelation, instead relying on a three-stage pipeline with a deliberately biased feature extractor and 2-Wasserstein distribution matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CCDB offers a practical path to group-robust classification without the costly bias annotations or large pretrained models that limit prior methods' real-world applicability, and it handles both interpretable (background, gender) and non-interpretable (noise patterns) biases uniformly. Key limitations include the Gaussian moment-matching approximation (potentially insufficient for highly multimodal latent spaces), dependence on Stage 1 quality for identifying spurious factors, and sensitivity to hyperparameters (γ, T, λ) that currently require heuristic selection. Open questions include extending to mixture-of-Gaussians matching, handling highly entangled core-spurious features, and whether the IID-WGA trade-off can be controlled more adaptively without validation-set group information.
