## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning of reasoning models is highly sensitive to data quality, yet existing curation pipelines rely on expensive LLM-based filtering (e.g., prompting GPT-4o-mini or DeepSeek-R1 to estimate difficulty and categorize diversity), which is costly and often yields suboptimal selections. The paper asks whether the difficulty and diversity of reasoning traces can be inferred from only the initial portion of the chain-of-thought, eliminating the need for full-trace processing or auxiliary LLM judges.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a data mixture of question–CoT reasoning traces (up to 91k tokens each), a pretrained checkpoint θ₀, a fine-tuned checkpoint θ_f (to define the fine-tuning direction v = θ_f − θ₀), and a total selection budget N. It outputs a curated SFT subset of N diverse, difficult reasoning traces. Critically, loss is evaluated only on the first 100 tokens (difficulty filtering) and first 1k tokens (diversity clustering) of each trace, rather than the full response, yielding a 91% reduction in token processing compared to baselines that require full-trace evaluation.

## Method

TEMP (Token-Efficient Model Perturbation) operates in two stages. First, for difficulty filtering, it evaluates the loss of the first 100 CoT tokens at a randomly perturbed checkpoint θ_rnd = θ₀ + λξ (ξ ~ N(0,I), scaled so the loss ratio to θ₀ falls in [2,3]); examples with higher perturbed loss are retained as difficult, because weight noise disrupts memorization and exposes the true structural complexity of the problem. Second, for diversity sampling, it generates n perturbed checkpoints extrapolating along the fine-tuning direction v = θ_f − θ₀ with small Gaussian noise, computes the loss of the first 1k reasoning tokens at each checkpoint to form a per-example loss vector, clusters examples by these vectors, and samples the highest-brittle-difficulty examples from each cluster. A key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) proves that pairwise differences in perturbed loss vectors upper-bound pairwise gradient differences during fine-tuning, justifying loss-vector clustering as a proxy for gradient diversity without computing gradients directly.

## Results

On the M23k medical reasoning dataset (1k selected examples, 10-benchmark average accuracy), TEMP achieves 57.7% with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, outperforming the next-best baseline (m1k at 56.6%) by 1.1% and random selection (55.1%) by 2.6%. On Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, TEMP outperforms the best baseline (S2L at 55.8%) by up to 1.7%. TEMP is also 91% more token-efficient than baselines, requiring only ~1k tokens of loss evaluation per example versus up to 91k full reasoning traces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEMP outperforms all five baselines on both Qwen2.5-7B and Llama3.1-8B across the M23k medical reasoning benchmarks. Against the LLM-filtering baseline m1k (Huang et al., 2025), TEMP gains +1.1% average accuracy (57.7% vs. 56.6%) while being 91% more token-efficient, since m1k requires full-trace LLM scoring. Among loss-based methods, S2L (Yang et al., 2024) is the strongest on Llama3.1 (55.8%) but drops to only 55.3% on Qwen2.5, showing poor cross-architecture generalizability; TEMP maintains top performance on both. Learnability (Zhou et al., 2025) and Middle Perplexity (Marion et al., 2023) underperform random selection on Qwen2.5 (53.7% and 55.4% vs. 55.1%), confirming that full-trace loss signals are dominated by verbosity noise in long CoT settings. Embedding Diversity (Bhatt et al., 2024) achieves 55.7%, still 2.0% below TEMP, indicating that hidden-state clustering fails to capture gradient-level reasoning diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-selection-for-instruction-tuning literature (LIMA, ALPAGASUS, S2L, DAVIR) to the specific challenge of curating SFT data for reasoning models, where extremely long chain-of-thought traces (up to 91k tokens) render standard loss-, embedding-, and gradient-based signals dominated by verbosity and stylistic noise. It departs from the dominant LLM-as-judge paradigm for difficulty and diversity filtering (used in S1, M1, OpenThoughts) by showing that both properties can be inferred from only the initial 100–1k CoT tokens evaluated at strategically perturbed model checkpoints. The directional perturbation along the fine-tuning trajectory, combined with a theoretical guarantee bounding gradient differences via perturbed loss vectors, is a novel mechanism not present in prior data-selection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 91% token-efficiency gain makes large-scale reasoning data curation substantially cheaper, which is practically important as reasoning-model training pipelines grow. The theoretical connection between perturbed loss vectors and gradient similarity (Theorem 3.1) provides a principled justification for the clustering step that prior heuristic methods lack. Limitations include reliance on a fine-tuned checkpoint θ_f (though ablations show robustness to suboptimal θ_f), evaluation only on 7B–8B models, and testing in just two domains (medical and mathematical reasoning). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to RL-based reasoning training, scales to 70B+ models, and generalizes to non-CoT reasoning formats.
