## Problem

Real-world graphs support multiple predictive tasks (node classification and link prediction) over the same structure, yet existing evaluations rely on incompatible splits, observed-graph assumptions, and negative sampling rules that make conclusions about same-graph cross-task transfer unreliable. This paper formalizes the setting and asks: when can supervision from one task genuinely improve the other, and is the direction of transfer predictable from simple graph diagnostics?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260061/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an undirected graph with node features, node labels for NC supervision, and observed edges for LP supervision, but critically restricts message passing to a protocol adjacency A_obs built only from training edges, excluding all validation and test edges to prevent leakage. LP evaluation uses fixed negative sets (1:1 positive-to-negative ratio) generated once and reused across all methods. Outputs include per-task scores (NC accuracy, LP AUC) and the CoTask Score (CTS), a dimensionless average percent gain over a fixed GCN reference that summarizes joint NC+LP utility for a shared encoder.

## Method

The paper introduces a leakage-free evaluation protocol that fixes node and edge splits, enforces a shared message-passing graph excluding evaluated edges, and uses fixed LP negatives, then studies five lightweight transfer mechanisms across GCN, GraphSAGE, and GPS backbones: Warm Start (parameter initialization from source task), Embedding Transfer Replace/Concat (frozen source embeddings injected as input features), Multi-View (two task-specific encoders aligned via symmetric InfoNCE), and Joint training (single shared encoder with weighted multi-task loss). The key empirical finding is a strong directional asymmetry: NC→LP transfer is reliably beneficial on homophilic graphs (predicted by homophily correlations up to r=0.90), while LP→NC is fragile and only consistently positive in a structure-dominant regime where LP is highly learnable but NC remains unsaturated. A cosine-similarity gap analysis provides geometric evidence that NC training amplifies linked-vs-unlinked pair separability by ~2× more on homophilic than heterophilic graphs, explaining the asymmetry.

## Results

On homophilic graphs (CORA, CITESEER, PUBMED), NC→LP transfer yields consistent AUC gains of 4–14 pp across all three backbones (GCN, GraphSAGE, GPS), with Joint and MV achieving the largest improvements (e.g., +12.3 pp on CORA/GCN, +13.4 pp on CITESEER/GraphSAGE). In contrast, LP→NC transfer is fragile: ET Rep causes severe negative transfer on homophilic datasets (e.g., −21.4 pp on PUBMED/GCN, −28.2 pp on ROMAN/GCN), while gains concentrate in structure-dominant regimes (USA, EUROPE, BRAZIL) where ET Concat and Joint deliver up to +24 pp accuracy (GPS ET Rep on USA). The CoTask Score (CTS) confirms that coupled training (MV, Joint) provides the most stable combined NC+LP utility, with regime-averaged CTS of +7.1/+7.3 (MV/Joint) on homophilic graphs versus +24.7/+23.3 (GraphSAGE MV/Joint) on heterophilic graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods; instead, it benchmarks five cross-task transfer mechanisms (Warm Start, ET-Rep, ET-Concat, Multi-View, Joint) against single-task baselines (NC-only and LP-only models trained under the same leakage-free protocol). On homophilic graphs, all five mechanisms improve LP AUC over the LP base, with Joint and MV consistently outperforming post-hoc reuse (e.g., on CORA/GCN: Joint +12.3 pp vs. WS +9.4 pp vs. ET-Rep +10.7 pp). On heterophilic graphs, ET-Rep is the most unstable, ranging from +6.6 pp (TEXAS/GCN LP→NC) to −34.5 pp (ROMAN/GPS LP→NC), while MV and Joint remain the most reliable (e.g., ROMAN/GPS Joint achieves +27.9 pp NC→LP AUC, the single largest gain in the paper). In structure-dominant regimes (USA, EUROPE, BRAZIL), ET-Concat and Joint deliver the strongest LP→NC accuracy gains (up to +24.2 pp on USA/GPS ET-Rep, +22.6 pp on BRAZIL/GPS ET-Concat), confirming that LP acts as structural pretraining when NC has headroom. The CoTask Score analysis further shows that coupled training (MV, Joint) dominates post-hoc reuse in combined utility, particularly on heterophilic graphs where ET-Rep is consistently negative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of multi-task graph learning and evaluation protocol design. Prior MTL-on-graphs work (PCGrad, GradNorm, VGAE, GPT-GNN) either mitigates gradient interference for joint training or studies pretraining for a single generative objective, but does not isolate bidirectional NC↔LP transfer on the same graph under a unified protocol. The paper repurposes standard transfer mechanisms (warm start, embedding transfer, multi-view alignment, joint training) and applies them under a leakage-free protocol that fixes splits, observed graphs, and LP negatives—addressing a gap the authors identify in existing benchmarks like OGB, which standardize within-task evaluation but not cross-task comparisons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a reproducible protocol and actionable heuristics (homophily for NC→LP, structure-dominant regime for LP→NC) for deciding when a shared encoder is worthwhile, reducing the risk of protocol artifacts masquerading as transfer gains. The CoTask Score offers a compact multi-objective metric for model selection when one encoder must serve both tasks. Open questions include whether the directional asymmetry persists under cross-graph distribution shift, whether newer architectures (state-space graph models) alter the regime boundaries, and how the protocol extends to other task pairs (community detection, graph classification) where leakage and split compatibility are substantially harder to control.
