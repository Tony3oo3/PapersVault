## Problem

Prior convergence analyses for Shampoo-type optimizers were confined to convex settings or addressed only the one-sided preconditioning variant, leaving the more complex and practically dominant two-sided preconditioning without nonconvex guarantees. This paper fills that gap by establishing the first nonconvex convergence rate for the full AdamW-style Shampoo (with momentum, decoupled weight decay, and tunable two-sided exponents) that won the AlgoPerf competition, while unifying one-sided and two-sided preconditioning under a single framework.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on m×n matrix parameters (e.g., weight matrices in transformer layers) and consumes stochastic gradients at each iteration, along with hyperparameters η (step size), θ (momentum), β (second-moment decay), λ (weight decay), ε (regularization), and exponents p, q satisfying 1/p + 1/q = 1. The convergence guarantee is expressed in nuclear norm (sum of singular values) rather than the more common Frobenius norm, yielding a rate of O(√(m+n)·C/K^{1/4}) that matches SGD's optimal rate in the ideal case where the nuclear norm is Θ(√min{m,n}) times the Frobenius norm. The analysis also requires the spectral norm of iterates to remain bounded by 1/λ, which is ensured by a restricted weight decay parameter.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a two-pronged bounding strategy for the two-sided preconditioned update L^{-1/(2p)} M R^{-1/(2q)}. First, a Schatten-p Hölder inequality (Lemma 3.3) decomposes the nuclear norm of the gradient into a product involving tr(L^{1/2}) + tr(R^{1/2}) and the Frobenius norm of the preconditioned gradient, reducing the problem to bounding the trace of the square roots of the second-moment matrices. Second, a matrix Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (Lemma 3.6) shows that the spectral norm of the update is uniformly bounded by 2 under the condition θ ≤ β ≤ √θ, which controls the interaction between momentum and second-moment decay. The proof then follows the AdamW ℓ₁-norm analysis framework, building a Lyapunov-style recursion that couples the function decrease with the momentum error and the trace terms, ultimately yielding the K^{-1/4} rate. Conceptually, the paper shows that AdamW-style Shampoo is a signal-to-total-energy-ratio (STR) preconditioned variant of Muon, where the left and right preconditioners capture row-wise and column-wise correlations respectively, and the √(m+n) factor in the rate is conjectured to be unavoidable by analogy with AdaGrad's lower bound.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2.1) establishes that AdamW-style Shampoo achieves a convergence rate of O(√(m+n)·C/K^{1/4}) measured by nuclear norm for nonconvex stochastic optimization, where C matches the constant in SGD's optimal rate. In the ideal case where ‖∇f(X)‖_* = Θ(√(min{m,n}))‖∇f(X)‖_F and m ≈ n, this rate is analogous to SGD's optimal O(C/K^{1/4}) Frobenius-norm rate. Empirically, the paper verifies on GPT-2 training that the nuclear-to-Frobenius norm ratio is indeed Θ(√(min{m,n})), supporting the tightness of the bound. The AlgoPerf competition result (Kasimbeg et al., 2025) demonstrates a 28% average speedup of the Shampoo-based implementation over the NAdamW baseline across eight deep learning workloads.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to SGD, AdamW-style Shampoo introduces a √(m+n) factor and measures convergence in nuclear norm rather than Frobenius norm; however, since ‖∇f‖_* ≤ √(min{m,n})‖∇f‖_F, the rate matches SGD's optimal O(C/K^{1/4}) in the ideal balanced case, which is empirically confirmed on GPT-2. Compared to AdamW (Li et al., 2025a), the Shampoo variant is a direct matrix analogue: nuclear norm replaces ℓ₁ norm and spectral norm replaces ℓ∞ norm, operating in the space of singular values rather than individual coordinates. Relative to Xie et al. (2026), this paper extends the analysis from one-sided to two-sided preconditioning, incorporates momentum and decoupled weight decay, and uses a weaker stochastic expectation-based assumption rather than a deterministic bound. The paper also positions AdamW-style Shampoo as a signal-to-total-energy (STR) preconditioned variant of Muon, analogous to how AdamW relates to SignSGD. In the AlgoPerf competition, the distributed Shampoo implementation achieved a 28% average speedup over NAdamW across eight workloads, providing practical evidence of the theoretical advantage of non-diagonal preconditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of adaptive optimizers from the diagonal (AdamW) and one-sided (Xie et al., 2026) settings to the full two-sided preconditioning of Shampoo, incorporating momentum and decoupled weight decay. The proof framework adapts the ℓ₁-norm analysis of AdamW (Li et al., 2025a) to the matrix nuclear norm via the Schatten-p Hölder inequality, while the matrix Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (Lemma 3.5) handles the spectral norm of the two-sided update. Conceptually, the paper positions AdamW-style Shampoo as a 'signal-to-total-energy ratio' preconditioned variant of Muon, drawing an analogy to the AdamW–SignSGD relationship, and unifies one-sided (p=1 or q=∞) and two-sided (p,q<∞) preconditioning under a single (p,q) parameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first nonconvex convergence guarantee for the two-sided Shampoo with all practical components (momentum, decoupled weight decay, tunable exponents), closing a gap that prior analyses (Gupta et al. 2018, Xie et al. 2025, An et al. 2025) left open by restricting to convex settings or one-sided variants. The rate O(√(m+n)/K^{1/4}) in nuclear norm is argued to be near-optimal via a conjecture linking it to AdaGrad's √d lower bound, and is empirically validated on GPT-2 training. Open questions include whether the √(m+n) factor is truly unavoidable, whether the β≤√θ constraint (excluding the common (0.9, 0.999) setting) can be tightened, and whether the gap between the theoretical ε=O(σ²/(m+n)) and the practical ε=10⁻¹² can be bridged without the auxiliary condition (4).
