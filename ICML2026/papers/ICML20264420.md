## Problem

Hybrid systems exhibit discontinuous state resets that are ill-posed for differentiable optimization. While prior work (CHyLL, Simic et al.) showed that the reset map induces a continuous latent manifold (hybrifold), the vector field on that manifold was not guaranteed to be continuous, leaving a gap in making gradient-based learning well-posed. This paper asks whether a provably continuous extrinsic vector field representation exists and, if so, how to learn it from data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete time-series state observations {(t_k, x_k)} from a hybrid system's flow, where the state dimension is n. It produces a three-component latent ODE model: an encoder f_θ: R^n → R^m, a latent vector field V_θ (MLP) operating on the m-dimensional embedded image, and a decoder f_θ^{-1}: R^m → R^n. The latent dimension m must satisfy m > 2n (the Whitney bound), and training uses a rollout curriculum that progressively extends the prediction horizon from short to long segments (e.g., 32→200 steps).

## Method

The core theoretical result (Theorem 6) proves via parametric transversality that for m > 2n, a generic smooth embedding f: M → R^m simultaneously satisfies value compatibility f(x) = f(r(x)) and velocity compatibility Df(x)V(x) = Df(r(x))V(r(x)) at all reset surfaces, yielding a C^0 latent vector field. The proof constructs f on collar coordinates near the guard surface S and its reset image r(S), then extends globally via relative perturbation, showing the bad set has negative dimension. Algorithmically, CHyLL++ trains an encoder, latent MLP vector field, and decoder jointly with a composite loss: state-space MSE (L_x), latent-space MSE (L_z), gluing loss (L_g) enforcing (C-1) at detected reset indices, velocity compatibility loss (L_v) enforcing (C-2), and a covariance regularization (L_c) to prevent latent collapse. Ablations reveal that L_z (latent consistency) is the dominant factor for accuracy, while L_v is redundant because the finite Lipschitz continuity of the MLP vector field implicitly enforces velocity matching.

## Results

The proposed CHyLL++ method achieves the lowest reconstruction MSE across all five hybrid system benchmarks. On the 2D Torus and Klein Bottle (complex topologies), it reaches MSE 0.00367±0.00166 and 0.00587±0.00461 respectively, outperforming the closest baseline CHyLL by 4.5× and 3.7×. On the 6D Three-Link Walker, CHyLL++ achieves 0.0952±0.00424 versus 0.234±0.0140 for CHyLL. All other baselines (Neural ODE, Latent ODE with RNN, Deep Koopman, Neural Event ODE) either diverge, produce incorrect patterns, or are ill-conditioned on at least four of the five test cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHyLL++ outperforms its most direct predecessor CHyLL (Teng et al., 2025) on all five benchmarks, with the largest relative gains on the topologically complex Torus (4.5× lower MSE) and Klein Bottle (3.7× lower MSE), and a 2.5× improvement on the Three-Link Walker. The classical Neural ODE (Chen et al., 2018) fails qualitatively on the 2D systems (large penetration on Bouncing Ball, incorrect patterns on Torus/Klein) and achieves only 0.275 MSE on the Walker, roughly 3× worse than CHyLL++. The Latent ODE with RNN encoder (Rubanova et al., 2019) diverges on four of five cases and produces incorrect patterns on the remaining two. Deep Koopman (Lusch et al., 2018) diverges on all but the Walker, where it still underperforms CHyLL++ by 3×. The Neural Event ODE (Chen et al., 2020) is ill-conditioned across all five systems due to the difficulty of differentiating through randomly initialized event functions, confirming the paper's motivation for a continuous latent representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometric theory of hybrid systems initiated by Simic et al. (2005), which showed that reset maps induce an equivalence relation gluing the state space into a continuous 'hybrifold' manifold, and the subsequent CHyLL algorithm (Teng et al., 2025) that leveraged the Whitney Embedding Theorem to learn a latent representation of this manifold. The key departure is that prior work established continuity of the latent *manifold* but left open whether the induced latent *vector field* is continuous; this paper closes that gap by proving via transversality theory that for m > 2n, a generic embedding makes both the manifold and its vector field C⁰. Algorithmically, CHyLL++ adds a velocity compatibility loss to CHyLL's gluing and consistency losses, though ablations reveal this addition is redundant in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that m > 2n suffices for a provably continuous latent vector field makes differentiable optimization well-posed for hybrid system learning without requiring event-based simulation or combinatorial mode selection, which are the two main failure modes of prior approaches. Open questions include whether the dimension bound can be tightened to m = 2n (where transversality leaves finitely many singular points), how to handle piecewise-smooth guard surfaces with corners, and how to make the decoder truly discontinuous (e.g., via atlas learning). The practical impact is currently limited to a small set of synthetic and low-dimensional physical benchmarks, and the method's reliance on a rollout curriculum and careful loss weighting limits immediate broad adoption.
