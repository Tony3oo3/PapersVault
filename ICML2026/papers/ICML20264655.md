## Problem

Logit-based LLM watermarking involves a fundamental trade-off between detectability and semantic distortion, yet existing analyses provide no principled guidance for selecting the watermark hyperparameters (γ, δ), leaving practical deployments reliant on heuristic grid search without reliable performance guarantees. This paper addresses the gap by establishing explicit quantitative relationships between watermark parameters, statistical detection power, and KL-divergence distortion, transforming watermark design from trial-and-error into a guided optimization problem.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the base LLM's next-token logits, a prompt (first 50 tokens), and a secret key that drives the pseudo-random green-list partition at each generation step. It outputs watermarked text generated under the calibrated logit bias, the optimized parameter pair (γ*, δ*) computed once before generation and applied uniformly across the sequence, and a binary detection decision via a one-sided z-score test on the aggregate green-token count. The key structural detail is that the two-dimensional hyperparameter search over (γ, δ) is reduced to a one-dimensional optimization over γ alone, because for fixed γ the KL divergence is strictly monotone in δ, establishing a one-to-one mapping between logit shift and distortion budget.

## Method

The framework formalizes KGW watermark detection as a sequence-level hypothesis test (H0: green-token probability = γ vs. H1: green-token probability = γ′), then derives a closed-form expression for γ′ under a uniform Dirichlet prior on next-token probability vectors, yielding γ′ = e^δγ / (1 + γ(e^δ − 1)) in the large-vocabulary limit. Under an information-decay assumption on the autoregressive indicator sequence, a central limit theorem establishes the asymptotic normal distribution of the green-token count under the alternative, producing an explicit power function π*(γ, δ) that depends on the gap γ′ − γ, the variance inflation constant c, and sequence length n. The KL divergence between watermarked and unwatermarked distributions admits a closed-form plug-in expression that is strictly increasing in δ for fixed γ, enabling the distortion budget to parameterize δ implicitly. The resulting constrained optimization max_γ π*(γ, δ(γ, K₀)) is initialized near the theoretical upper bound γ* = n/(n + z²_{1−α}) and solved numerically, with the larger root γ_h of the distortion equation shown to dominate for all practically relevant power targets above 0.8.

## Results

The proposed power-calibrated framework consistently identifies Pareto-optimal watermark configurations across GPT-2, OPT-125M, Pythia-160M, and Gemma-2 9B on C4, LFQA, and Wikipedia. In post-attack robustness (WinMax-C, 10% signal removal, C4), the method maintains ≥0.95 TPR at -log(D_KL) = 2.70 (GPT-2), 2.53 (OPT-125M), and 2.70 (Pythia-160M), compared to 1.97/1.39/1.34 for DP and 2.15/1.86/2.22 for grid search. Theoretical green-token rate predictions match empirical measurements with R² ≥ 0.98 across all model-dataset combinations, and KL divergence predictions achieve R² ≥ 0.99.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: DP (Cai et al., 2024, KL-divergence/difference-in-probability optimization), OPT-style (Wouters, 2024, log-perplexity/green-token optimization), and a dense grid-search heuristic. The proposed method dominates all baselines on the detectability-distortion Pareto frontier across all three datasets and four model families, maintaining near-saturated TPR at substantially lower KL divergence. In the post-attack robustness setting (Table 4), the method achieves 0.5–1.3 higher -log(D_KL) than DP and 0.4–0.7 higher than grid search while sustaining 0.95 TPR, with the gap most pronounced on OPT-125M (e.g., 2.53 vs. 1.39 for DP at 10% attack). The OPT-style baseline performs closest to the proposed method on Pythia-160M but still lags by ~0.5 in -log(D_KL). The advantage is consistent across BLEU, ROUGE, and BERTScore quality metrics, indicating the gain is not tied to a specific similarity notion but reflects more efficient use of the distortion budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the KGW logit-based watermarking framework (Kirchenbauer et al., 2024a), which introduces a constant bias to green-list token logits. It departs from prior calibration approaches—Wouters (2024) using log-perplexity shifts and Cai et al. (2024) using plug-in KL estimates—by reframing detectability as statistical power in a formal hypothesis-testing setting and deriving closed-form mappings between the watermark parameters (γ, δ), detection power, and KL distortion. The key conceptual shift is replacing heuristic proxies (green-token count differences, perplexity shifts) with the power function of a z-score test, which reduces the two-dimensional hyperparameter search to a one-dimensional optimization under a distortion budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides practitioners with a principled, one-shot calibration procedure for watermark strength that guarantees Pareto-optimal operating points, replacing ad hoc grid search with a theoretically grounded optimization. However, the analysis relies on a non-informative Dirichlet prior for next-token distributions and sets the variance inflation constant c=1 in practice, both of which may limit accuracy for highly structured or domain-specific generation. Open questions include how the calibration behaves under long-form generation where signal accumulation changes the effective power landscape, and whether the framework extends naturally to more complex watermarking schemes beyond single-bias KGW.
