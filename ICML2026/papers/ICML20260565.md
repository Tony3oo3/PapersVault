## Problem

Existing protein representation methods either rely on sequence-pretrained language models that conflate evolutionary signal with physical interactions, or use backbone-only graphs that discard side-chain geometry and chemical detail. No general-purpose pretraining framework exists that extracts, compresses, and transfers knowledge of all-atom structure across proteins, leaving a gap between atomistic physics and scalable, transferable protein embeddings.

## Input / Output

SLAE takes a single all-atom protein structure as input, represented as a directed graph over heavy atoms with edges within an 8 Å cutoff; node features are one-hot atom types and edge features encode interatomic distances (Bessel radial basis) and directions (spherical harmonics), with no sequence, residue index, or torsion-angle information. The encoder produces 128-dimensional residue-level latent embeddings, which the decoder expands to reconstruct up to 37 heavy/side-chain atom coordinates per residue (Atom37 format), a 20-class amino acid identity per position, and a pairwise n×n×3 tensor of Rosetta-derived inter-residue energy terms (hydrogen bonding, electrostatics, solvation).

## Method

The encoder is a two-layer SE(3)-equivariant graph network (inspired by Musaelian et al., 2023) that maintains coupled scalar and tensor latent spaces per directed edge, updating them via an equivariant tensor product that projects the atomic density around each center atom onto the center-neighbor pair; atom-level features are then mean-pooled to residues, creating a strict local-to-global information bottleneck. The decoder is an 8-layer pre-norm Transformer with Rotary Positional Embeddings and global self-attention over the residue tokens, feeding three parallel MLP heads for coordinate, sequence, and energy prediction. Pretraining jointly optimizes a multi-task loss combining SmoothLDDT and FAPE for all-atom structure recovery, cross-entropy for sequence recovery, and MSE on Rosetta inter-residue energy scores, which together act as a multi-view regularizer that shapes the latent space to be geometrically continuous, chemically informative, and energetically grounded without explicit regularizers. The 8 Å local encoder radius and full all-atom input are validated as essential through ablations showing substantial degradation with smaller radii or backbone-only inputs.

## Results

SLAE achieves 99.9% sequence recovery and all-atom RMSD of 1.12 Å (<128 residues) / 1.92 Å (<512 residues) on the CATH test set, with only mild degradation on 2000 diverse RCSB PDB structures (1.2 Å / 2.3 Å). On the StructTokenBench, the continuous latent representation reaches 75.20% average AUROC for functional-site prediction (+3.8% over AminoAseed) and 48.19% average Spearman's ρ for structural flexibility (+26.6%), while the decoder hidden representation further improves to 79.54% and 57.05%. Across four downstream tasks, SLAE attains 55.1% fold classification accuracy, 1.86 kcal/mol RMSE for binding affinity, 0.68 kcal/mol RMSE for thermostability, and 1.88 ppm RMSE for NMR chemical shift prediction, matching or exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLAE outperforms all structure-informed PLM baselines (ESM3, SaProt, ISM) across all four downstream tasks: on fold classification it reaches 55.1% Fold accuracy vs. 41.2% (SaProt) and 34.9% (ESM3); on binding affinity it achieves 1.86 kcal/mol RMSE vs. 2.03 (SaProt) and 2.06 (ESM3); on thermostability it reaches 0.68 RMSE vs. 0.69 (SaProt) and 0.71 (ESM3); and on chemical shift prediction it attains 1.88 ppm RMSE vs. 2.12 (SaProt) and 2.08 (ESM3). Against the MACE force-field encoder, SLAE dominates on fold classification (55.1% vs. 5.7%) and chemical shift (1.88 vs. 2.37 ppm), while MACE is slightly better on thermostability (0.76 vs. 0.68 RMSE — actually SLAE wins here too at 0.68). On the StructTokenBench, SLAE's continuous latent surpasses FoldSeek (75.20% vs. 51.90% AUROC for functional sites; 48.19% vs. 7.80% ρ for flexibility) and AminoAseed (75.20% vs. 72.43%; 48.19% vs. 38.08%), with the decoder hidden representation extending the gap to 79.54% and 57.05%. For fold classification specifically, SLAE (55.1% Fold, 77.1% Superfamily) slightly trails ProNet-Schull on the Fold split (56.1%) and Family split (99.4% vs. 99.1%) but clearly leads on Superfamily (77.1% vs. 74.6%), and substantially outperforms GVP-GNN, IEConv, and GearNet. Notably, SLAE achieves these results using only all-atom structural input with no evolutionary or sequence-pretraining signal, whereas most baselines rely on PLM embeddings or sequence-structure co-embedding.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLAE builds directly on the SE(3)-equivariant local neighborhood embedding architecture of Musaelian et al. (2023), originally developed for small-molecule interatomic potentials, and extends it to protein-scale graphs with a residue-level pooling bottleneck. It departs from the dominant protein representation paradigms—sequence-pretrained language models (ESM, ESM3) and backbone-only structure tokenizers (FoldSeek, SaProt, ISM)—by pretraining exclusively on all-atom coordinates and interatomic geometry without any evolutionary or sequence-level signal. The multi-task autoencoder objective (coordinate reconstruction + sequence recovery + Rosetta energy regression) is novel in combining physics-based energy supervision with geometric reconstruction, drawing on FAPE/LDDT losses from AlphaFold and Rosetta scoring from computational structural biology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SLAE provides the first general-purpose, all-atom, structure-only protein representation that achieves SOTA or competitive performance across fold classification, binding affinity, thermostability, and NMR chemical shift prediction without any evolutionary information, suggesting that atomistic geometry alone carries sufficient signal for diverse downstream tasks. The interpretable latent space—organized by side-chain chemistry, solvent exposure, and secondary structure—and its ability to produce physically coherent intermediates via simple linear interpolation open new avenues for conformational analysis and generative model evaluation at all-atom resolution. Key limitations include the requirement for input structures (experimental or predicted), training only on single folded chains (disordered regions and complexes are out-of-distribution), and the 8Å cutoff that may miss longer-range packing effects. Open questions include whether the framework extends to protein-ligand complexes, whether the local encoder can be made more expressive for very large conformational changes, and whether the energy regression objective can be replaced by learned potentials as they improve.
