## Problem

Existing data attribution methods for diffusion models require access to target-model gradients or repeated retraining, making them inapplicable to proprietary models or settings where training a separate model solely for attribution is infeasible. The few nonparametric alternatives that do exist (e.g., CLIP similarity) perform poorly because they ignore the internal dynamics of the generative process, leaving effective attribution in restricted-access settings as an open challenge.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single generated RGB image and a training dataset of images, applying a chosen forward diffusion noise schedule (no target-model architecture or parameters needed) to produce noisy versions at selected timesteps T={100,…,500}. It outputs a scalar attribution score per training image (aggregated over timesteps and spatial locations) along with per-patch influence scores that yield spatially interpretable attribution maps. The method operates at resolutions from 32×32 (CIFAR) to 256×256 (ArtBench-2), using zero-padding for boundary patches and stride-2 subsampling at higher resolutions to control compute.

## Method

NDA is grounded in the analytical form of the optimal score function under locality and equivariance inductive biases (Kamb & Ganguli, 2025). For each timestep t, it extracts P×P patches from the noisy generated image and all training images, then computes patch-wise influence scores as softmax-weighted quadratic distances between the noisy patch and √(ᾱ_t)-scaled training patches (Eq. 11), which directly mirrors the posterior weighting in the score function. Image-level attribution is obtained by selecting the top-k most influential patches per training image at each spatial location and aggregating across locations and timesteps. A multiscale extension combines original-resolution and 2×2-pooled low-resolution patch distances with a timestep-dependent weighting γ_t, and patch size P is chosen per timestep (smaller for low noise, larger for high noise). A convolution-based acceleration reformulates the all-pairs patch distance computation as a single convolution pass, reducing peak memory from O(NL²CP²) to O(BNL²) and enabling scalable attribution on large datasets.

## Results

NDA achieves LDS scores of 24.88/15.91 (validation/generation) on CIFAR-2, 11.81/7.41 on CIFAR-10, and 16.89/13.92 on CelebA, substantially outperforming the strongest nonparametric baseline (CLIP cosine similarity: 8.54/4.01, 3.39/1.69, 10.92/3.03 respectively). On the 256×256 ArtBench-2 benchmark, NDA reaches 14.35/12.98 LDS, surpassing CLIP (8.62/8.66) and TRAK (12.26/7.78). In counterfactual evaluation, NDA achieves median ℓ₂ distances of 8.43 (CIFAR-2) and 14.38 (CelebA) after removing top-1000 attributed samples, outperforming both CLIP and D-TRAK. NDA also generalizes across architectures and objectives, matching or exceeding D-TRAK under UViT-Flow Matching on CIFAR-2 (15.61 vs. 15.20 LDS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NDA decisively outperforms all nonparametric baselines: over CLIP cosine similarity (the strongest nonparametric method), NDA gains +16.34 LDS on CIFAR-2 validation, +8.42 on CIFAR-10 validation, and +5.97 on CelebA validation, with similar margins on generation sets. Against gradient-based methods without kernels (Gradient, TracInCP, GAS), NDA achieves 3–20× higher LDS scores, indicating that raw gradient similarity is a poor proxy for diffusion-model attribution. Compared to kernelized gradient methods, NDA trails D-TRAK by 5.94/2.92 LDS on CelebA (val/gen) but narrows the gap dramatically relative to CLIP (which trails D-TRAK by 11.91/13.81), while requiring no model parameters or gradients. On ArtBench-2 at 256×256, NDA (14.35/12.98) surpasses TRAK (12.26/7.78) and CLIP (8.62/8.66) but remains below D-TRAK (27.61/24.16). In counterfactual removal experiments, NDA produces larger output changes (higher ℓ₂ distance) than both CLIP and D-TRAK, confirming its practical effectiveness in identifying truly influential training samples. Under UViT-Flow Matching on CIFAR-2, NDA (15.61) slightly exceeds D-TRAK (15.20), suggesting the nonparametric signal can be competitive even when the target model uses a different training paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges the analytical theory of optimal score functions in diffusion models (Song et al., 2021; Kamb & Ganguli, 2025) with the practical problem of data attribution. While prior nonparametric attribution methods (CLIP similarity, raw pixel comparison) measure image-level similarity without regard to the generative process, and gradient-based methods (TRAK, D-TRAK, TracInCP) require full model parameter access, NDA repurposes the posterior weighting term from the equivariant local score machine as a patch-level influence signal. This positions the work as a theoretically grounded departure from both similarity-based and gradient-based attribution, operating entirely in data space under a chosen forward diffusion process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NDA addresses a practically important gap: attribution for proprietary or restricted-access diffusion models where gradients and parameters are unavailable, with applications to copyright auditing, data valuation, and dataset curation. The method's spatial interpretability (patch-level correspondences between training and generated images) offers a form of explainability absent in most gradient-based approaches. However, the attribution is model-agnostic—identical for all models trained on the same data—which the authors acknowledge as a limitation, and the significant performance gap to D-TRAK (especially at 256×256) suggests that parametric information still carries substantial attribution signal. Open questions include whether multiscale extensions with more resolutions, or integration with learned feature spaces, could close this gap.
