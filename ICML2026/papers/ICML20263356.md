## Problem

IDM-based semi-supervised imitation learning methods (VM-IDM, IDM labeling) empirically outperform behavior cloning with the same labeled data, but prior work only offered partial, ad-hoc explanations for this sample-efficiency gap. This paper unifies these methods under a single 'IDM-based policy' concept and provides a more complete statistical learning theory explanation for why IDM learning is more sample-efficient than BC.

## Input / Output

The methods consume a small set of action-labeled transitions (s, a, s') and a much larger set of unlabeled transitions (s, s'), where states are either 4-frame RGB stacks (Push-T, LIBERO-10), single RGB frames (Procgen), or 2D position coordinates (maze experiments). Actions range from 4 discrete directions (mazes) to discrete game actions (Procgen) to 16-dimensional continuous action chunks (manipulation). The IDM takes the pair (s, s') and predicts a; the video model takes s and predicts s'; the final policy maps s to a. In the maze experiments, the ground-truth video model v* is used directly (infinite unlabeled data regime), while in Procgen and manipulation experiments, all 2.6M unlabeled transitions serve as D_U.

## Method

The paper first proves that VM-IDM and IDM labeling converge to the same 'IDM-based policy' π_{v*,ĥ} in the limit of infinite unlabeled data, then attributes IDM's sample-efficiency advantage to two factors: (i) the ground-truth IDM h*(a|s,s') often belongs to a lower-complexity hypothesis class than the expert policy π*(a|s) (e.g., linear in mazes where the policy is not), and (ii) h* is often less stochastic than π* since observing (s,s') disambiguates the action. Motivated by these insights, the paper proposes LAPO+, which inverts stages 2 and 3 of LAPO: it first decodes the latent IDM to a true-action IDM via a small D_L, then performs IDM labeling on all unlabeled data, leveraging the superior sample efficiency of IDM learning for the decoding step. Additionally, the paper shows that sampling a unified video-action model (UVA) as a VM-IDM (sample next-state from the video head, then action from the IDM head) outperforms sampling it as a standard policy on Push-T and LIBERO-10.

## Results

On the 16-environment Procgen benchmark, IDM labeling outperforms BC by a few orders of magnitude in 9 environments, while LAPO+ consistently outperforms LAPO across all environments in low-data regimes. On Push-T, IDM-based policies (IDM labeling and VM-IDM) outperform BC by an especially large margin, attributed to the particularly simple nature of the ground-truth IDM (the action is essentially the agent's location). On LIBERO-10, IDM-based policies outperform BC in most settings, and sampling the UVA architecture as a VM-IDM (VM-IDM (UVA)) yields equal or improved performance over standard UVA policy sampling (Policy (UVA)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BC (Pomerleau, 1988), all IDM-based methods (IDM labeling, VM-IDM, LAPO+) consistently win, with the gap being largest in low-data regimes and in environments with simple dynamics (e.g., Push-T, where the IDM is nearly deterministic). Against LAPO (Schmidt & Jiang, 2024), the proposed LAPO+ wins consistently on Procgen by inverting the decoding and labeling stages—decoding the LIDM directly with a small D_L (leveraging IDM sample efficiency) before performing IDM labeling—yielding substantially higher normalized returns in low-data settings. Against standard UVA policy sampling (Li et al., 2025), the proposed VM-IDM (UVA) sampling scheme achieves equal or improved success rates on both Push-T and LIBERO-10, suggesting that the unified architecture learns capable IDMs from relatively few labeled samples. IDM labeling (Baker et al., 2022) serves as both a studied method and a baseline; it outperforms BC but trails VM-IDM by a small margin on manipulation tasks, which the authors attribute to the finite (rather than infinite) unlabeled dataset and the VM's generalization ability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate IDM-based semi-supervised imitation learning approaches—VM-IDM (Du et al., 2023) and IDM labeling (Baker et al., 2022/VPT)—by proving they recover the same 'IDM-based policy' in the infinite unlabeled-data limit. It then provides a statistical learning theory explanation for the empirically observed sample-efficiency advantage of IDM learning over behavior cloning, attributing it to the ground-truth IDM being both lower-complexity and less stochastic than the expert policy. Practically, it builds on LAPO (Schmidt & Jiang, 2024) to propose LAPO+, which inverts the decoding and labeling stages, and explores VM-IDM sampling within the UVA unified video-action architecture (Li et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The complexity-and-stochasticity framework gives practitioners a principled way to predict when IDM-based methods will outperform BC and by how much, which is valuable for method selection in SSIL settings. However, the analysis assumes a curated unlabeled dataset drawn from the same expert/task as the labeled data, and the paper does not study the finite-D_U regime where VM-IDM and IDM labeling may diverge. Open questions include whether the sample-efficiency gap persists with non-curated or domain-shifted unlabeled data, and whether the simplicity-bias explanation for high-capacity IDM generalization can be made more rigorous.
