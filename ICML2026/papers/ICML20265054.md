## Problem

Existing methods for enforcing marginal constraints on generative models (importance reweighting, conditional sampling) are derived for deterministic transformations Y = f(X) and provably fail when the transformation is stochastic, as in the common case of a noisy classifier mapping images to class labels. This paper identifies this gap, provides a spectral-gap lower bound on the reconstruction error of deterministic-constraint methods under stochastic p_{Y|X}, and develops a principled alternative that handles the general probabilistic-constraint setting.

## Input / Output

The method requires three inputs: (1) a pretrained score-based model providing the score ∇_x log p_X^0(x) (e.g., a diffusion model's time-dependent score network), (2) a known stochastic transformation density p_{Y|X}(y|x) such as a trained attribute classifier, and (3) a target marginal distribution p_Y^1(y) over the lower-dimensional variable Y. The output is a lightweight neural network φ_θ(y) that defines an additive score correction; at inference time, this correction is plugged directly into SGLD updates or the reverse diffusion process to generate samples from the constrained distribution p_X^*, without any modification or retraining of the base model. For diffusion models, the correction is time-dependent (parameterized as φ_θ(y,t)) and applied at every reverse-time step.

## Method

DUALSCORE formulates the constrained generation problem as a KL-minimization: find p_X^* closest to the reference p_X^0 subject to the induced marginal p_{Y|X} ∘ p_X^* matching p_Y^1 within an L² tolerance ε. Applying the Fenchel dual of Altun & Smola (2006), the infinite-dimensional dual variable φ ∈ L²(Y) is parameterized by a neural network, reducing the problem to standard SGD training. The dual solution yields an additive score correction ∇_x log w_θ(x) = E_{y~p_{Y|X}(·|x)}[φ_θ(y) ∇_x log p_{Y|X}(y|x)], which is added to the base score and sampled from via a persistent SGLD chain (PCD-style) or reverse diffusion. This score-based sampling is critical for handling density chasms where the reference and target distributions have low overlap, a regime where importance-resampling baselines fail due to vanishing effective sample size. For diffusion models, the dual is extended to a time-weighted objective L(θ) = E_{t~U[0,T]}[λ(t)L(θ,t)] that applies the marginal constraint at all diffusion timesteps, following the smooth-score-correction principle of Kim et al. (2024).

## Results

On the balanced class-frequency task, DUALSCORE achieves the lowest marginal constraint violation (‖p̂_Y* − p_Y¹‖₂) across all noise levels: on CIFAR-10 (LT/5%), it reaches 0.004 at ρ=0.75 versus 0.037 for both IRS and CONDSMPL, and on CelebA it reaches 0.003 at ρ=0.75 versus 0.054 for baselines. On the more challenging reversed-distribution task, the gap widens dramatically: on CIFAR-10 (LT/5%) at ρ=0.75, DUALSCORE achieves L2=0.041 while IRS and CONDSMPL both reach 0.176. The corrected model incurs a higher FID (e.g., 4.02 vs 1.42 on CIFAR-10 LT/5% balanced at ρ=0.75), reflecting the greater distributional shift required to satisfy the stochastic marginal constraint, but this is the expected trade-off for a method that enforces the constraint rather than approximating it via reweighting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DUALSCORE against two baselines derived for deterministic transformations: Importance Resampling (IRS) and Conditional Sampling (CONDSMPL), both of which are standard solutions when Y is a deterministic function of X. At ρ=0 (noise-free classifier), the baselines sometimes achieve slightly lower L2 constraint violation (e.g., on CIFAR-10 LT/5% balanced: IRS 0.009, CONDSMPL 0.007 vs. DUALSCORE 0.014), but neither consistently outperforms DUALSCORE. As classifier noise ρ increases, the baselines degrade sharply—on CIFAR-10 LT/5% balanced, their L2 rises from ~0.008 to 0.037 at ρ=0.75—while DUALSCORE maintains L2 ≤ 0.014 throughout. On the reversed-distribution task, the advantage is more pronounced even at moderate noise: at ρ=0.5 on CIFAR-10 LT/5%, DUALSCORE achieves L2=0.038 versus 0.168–0.171 for both baselines. The FID cost of DUALSCORE is higher (reflecting the larger distributional shift needed for stochastic constraints), but the paper argues this is the correct trade-off since the baselines fail to actually satisfy the marginal constraint under stochastic p_{Y|X}.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on marginal constraints for generative models (Kessler et al., 2015; Tang, 2023; Choi et al., 2020; Kim et al., 2024), which has been restricted to deterministic transformations X→Y, to the more general setting of stochastic transformations p_{Y|X}. The core mathematical machinery is a Fenchel dual formulation from Altun & Smola (2006), adapted to yield an additive score correction parameterized by a neural network. For sampling, the method leverages SGLD (Welling & Teh, 2011) and reverse diffusion (Song et al., 2021; Karras et al., 2022), with the time-dependent extension following Kim et al. (2024). The training algorithm's persistent Markov chain design draws on Persistent Contrastive Divergence (Tieleman, 2008) and two-timescale stochastic approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, plug-in mechanism for enforcing distributional constraints (fairness, domain knowledge, measurement consistency) on pretrained score-based models without retraining, and handles the density-chasm regime where importance sampling provably fails. Key open questions include extending to multiple potentially inconsistent constraints, discrete or infinite-dimensional sample spaces, and establishing rigorous convergence guarantees for the full pipeline (neural approximation + Monte Carlo + SGD + SGLD mixing). The empirical results in the 'Reversed' task with high classifier noise show FID blowup (61–127), suggesting the method's robustness to adversarial or highly stochastic transformations remains an active area.
