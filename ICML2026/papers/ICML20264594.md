## Problem

Self-supervised multi-view 3D human pose estimation remains limited by reliance on synthetic 3D pose catalogues that introduce domain bias and poor generalization to real-world scenes. The inherently discrete multi-view person-assignment problem is either solved with non-differentiable combinatorial solvers or approximated within fixed voxel grids that anchor representations to specific camera configurations, hindering generalization to novel viewpoints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264594/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated multi-view RGB images (5–7 views typical) processed through a ResNet-50 backbone into per-joint heatmaps, from which 2D root candidates are extracted via soft-argmax to form hypergraph nodes. Training additionally requires off-the-shelf 2D pose detections (ViTPose) and higher-order correspondence pseudo-labels from COMPOSE, but no 3D ground-truth annotations. The output is a set of K full-body 3D poses (J×3 joint coordinates per person), produced in two stages: first 3D hip-joint roots via triangulation, then refined full skeletons.

## Method

DisPOSE formulates multi-view person association as a projected diffusion process over the space of polystochastic tensors: the forward process corrupts log-score potentials with Gaussian noise and projects back onto the feasible set via multi-marginal Sinkhorn normalization, while a hypergraph neural network denoiser predicts clean scores under DDIM sampling, with Sinkhorn projection enforced at every reverse step to maintain marginal feasibility. After convergence, greedy rounding extracts discrete hyperedge matches and algebraic triangulation yields 3D root positions. A second stage refines full-body poses through T=4 layers of a hypergraph convolutional decoder that alternates between multi-view feature sampling (projective attention), 2D offset prediction with confidence-weighted differentiable triangulation, and a person-part hypergraph enforcing anatomical consistency. The entire pipeline is trained self-supervised with 2D pseudo-labels and correspondence pseudo-labels, avoiding any 3D ground-truth supervision or synthetic pose catalogues.

## Results

On CMU Panoptic, DisPOSE achieves an MPJPE of 18.91 mm and AP25 of 82.68, establishing a new self-supervised state-of-the-art that surpasses the previous best (DSP) by 25 points in AP25 and 18% in MPJPE. On the proposed MM-OR Pose surgical benchmark, DisPOSE achieves 97.04% Recall@500 and 56.91 mm MPJPE, outperforming SelfPose3D by 2.7 points in recall and 13.8 mm in MPJPE. On Shelf and Campus, DisPOSE attains 97.1% and 95.6% average PCP respectively, beating DSP (96.7%, 92.8%) and SelfPose3D (95.1%, 87.9%). In generalization experiments, DisPOSE maintains >69% root mAP across unseen camera arrangements while SelfPose3D drops to ~30%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among self-supervised methods, DisPOSE decisively outperforms both SelfPose3D (AP25: 82.68 vs. 55.13; MPJPE: 18.91 vs. 24.47 mm) and DSP (AP25: 82.68 vs. 57.60; MPJPE: 18.91 vs. 23.10 mm), with the largest gap at the strict 25 mm threshold. Against optimization-based methods, DisPOSE improves AP25 by 28 points over MvPose (82.68 vs. 37.63) and 28 points over COMPOSE (82.68 vs. 54.66), while also reducing MPJPE from 26.46/23.62 mm to 18.91 mm. Compared to fully-supervised methods, DisPOSE narrows the gap substantially: it approaches VoxelPose (AP25: 82.68 vs. 83.59) and Faster VoxelPose (82.68 vs. 85.22) within 1–3 points at AP25, and matches or exceeds them at AP50 (98.71 vs. 98.33/98.08), though it remains behind the best fully-supervised methods (Voxel.+3DSA at 94.20 AP25, TEMPO at 14.68 MPJPE). On the new MM-OR Pose surgical benchmark, DisPOSE doubles SelfPose3D's AP50 (47.06 vs. 23.38) and reduces MPJPE by 19.5%. In camera generalization, DisPOSE maintains 69–79% root mAP across all unseen arrangements, whereas SelfPose3D collapses to 30–32%, demonstrating that the voxel-grid-free hypergraph formulation avoids the systematic bias of fixed camera configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisPOSE builds directly on COMPOSE's (Wang et al., 2026) hypergraph formulation of multi-view person assignment, adopting its higher-order correspondence structure and using its predicted correspondences as pseudo-labels. The key departure is replacing COMPOSE's non-differentiable discrete solver with a learned projected diffusion process over polystochastic tensors, where the multi-marginal Sinkhorn operator (Lin et al., 2022) enforces marginal feasibility at every denoising step. For pose regression, the paper follows the iterative 2D-offset-then-triangulate strategy of MVGFormer (Liao et al., 2024) but replaces its transformer decoder with attention-based hypergraph convolutions (Bai et al., 2021) that explicitly model intra-person anatomical and inter-view relational structure. The projected diffusion framework itself draws on constrained generation work (Lou & Ermon, 2023; Fishman et al., 2024), but its application to the discrete assignment problem via log-score parameterization and Sinkhorn projection is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for 3D ground-truth supervision, DisPOSE addresses a critical bottleneck for deploying multi-view pose estimation in safety-critical settings (surgery, human-robot interaction) where 3D annotation is impractical. The newly introduced MM-OR Pose benchmark fills a gap by providing a challenging surgical-environment evaluation with severe occlusions and out-of-distribution poses, which existing datasets lack. Open questions include whether the method can incorporate temporal information (currently unused), how it scales to single-view-only individuals (which cannot be triangulated), and whether the pseudo-label preprocessing pipeline (requiring COMPOSE or MvPose) can be further simplified. The strong camera-arrangement generalization (89% vs. 59% mAP) suggests the polystochastic formulation is genuinely more geometry-agnostic than voxel-grid approaches, but this remains to be validated on truly novel scene types beyond CMU Panoptic.
