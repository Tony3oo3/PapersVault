## Problem

Existing treatment responder classification frameworks require a deterministic decision for every individual, which leads to significant errors on high-uncertainty samples. No prior work addresses causal decision-making when abstention is optional, and existing abstention methods either lack causal context or do not explicitly control the rejection rate. This paper fills this gap by formulating treatment responder classification with an explicit constraint on the proportion of abstained samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264919/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes individual covariates X, binary treatment assignment T ∈ {-1,1}, and binary outcome Y ∈ {-1,1} from observational data. It produces two outputs: a predictor f: X → {-1,1} assigning responder/non-responder labels to retained samples, and a rejector r: X → {0,1} flagging samples for abstention. The abstention rate is explicitly constrained to at most δ proportion of the population, making the rejection budget directly interpretable rather than relying on an implicit cost parameter.

## Method

The core insight is revealing that the causal misclassification risk under abstention equals the Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) of the conditional risk V_f(x), which reformulates the loss as a supremum over a threshold β that directly serves as the abstention boundary. TRECA constructs a doubly robust estimator for this CVaR loss using Neyman-orthogonal augmentation with propensity score π and outcome regression μ, ensuring consistent estimation even when one nuisance model is misspecified. Training proceeds as a min-max problem: gradient ascent on β while gradient descent updates the predictor f, CATE estimator τ, and nuisance parameters iteratively. Extensions handle violations of monotonicity (by minimizing a tight upper bound under partial identification of ρ(x)) and unconfoundedness (via sensitivity bounds on CATE), with theoretical guarantees on convergence rates and abstention rule accuracy.

## Results

On Twins_mono (abstention rate δ=30%), TRECA achieves f-regret-r of 0.001±0.002 (within-sample) and 0.003±0.003 (out-of-sample) on retained samples, substantially lower than the next-best baseline CTRL (0.004/0.006) and CFRNet (0.018/0.016). On Jobs_mono, TRECA achieves f-regret-r of 0.017±0.031 (within) and 0.025±0.038 (out-of-sample), outperforming CTRL (0.036/0.038), CFRNet (0.039/0.042), and DragonNet (0.038/0.042). The overall f-regret-o on Jobs_mono is 0.033±0.069 for TRECA versus 0.047±0.061 for CFRNet, confirming that the abstention strategy improves end-to-end classification accuracy despite higher regret on abstained samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRECA consistently achieves the lowest f-regret-r (regret on retained samples) across both datasets, which is the primary optimization target. On Twins_mono, TRECA (0.001) outperforms the closest competitor CTRL (0.004) by 4× and CFRNet (0.018) by 18×; on Jobs_mono, TRECA (0.017) beats CTRL (0.036) by 2× and CFRNet (0.039) by 2.3×. The trade-off is visible in f-regret-a: TRECA's abstained-sample regret is higher (0.352 on Twins_mono, 0.167 on Jobs_mono) than baselines like CFRNet (0.063, 0.082), reflecting that the method deliberately routes uncertain samples to abstention. However, the overall f-regret-o remains competitive or superior: on Jobs_mono, TRECA (0.033) beats all baselines including CFRNet (0.047) and DragonNet (0.044), while on Twins_mono it is slightly higher than CFRNet (0.039 vs 0.023) but far below meta-learners and CEVAE. The robustness variants (w/o mono., w/o uncf.) maintain lower f-regret-r than all baselines even under assumption violations, with w/o uncf. achieving 0.004 on Twins_mono and 0.039 on Jobs_mono, still outperforming CFRNet and DragonNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends treatment responder classification (Kallus, 2019; Wu et al., 2025) by introducing a constraint-based abstention mechanism, and connects the resulting causal misclassification risk to Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR), building on the CVaR optimization framework of Rockafellar et al. (2000) and the treatment effect risk analysis of Kallus (2023). The doubly robust estimation strategy follows the Neyman orthogonality paradigm from Bang & Robins (2005) and Chernozhukov et al. (2018), adapted to the CVaR-structured loss. Unlike prior abstention learning work (Chow, 1957; Cortes et al., 2016) that relies on cost-based or confidence-threshold rejection, this paper formulates abstention as an explicit rate constraint, and unlike prior causal decision-making methods, it handles partial identification when monotonicity or unconfoundedness may be violated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to formalize causal decision-making with abstention, providing a theoretically grounded and interpretable mechanism for practitioners to withhold uncertain treatment-responder classifications (e.g., in surgery decisions or public policy). The CVaR connection and doubly robust guarantees under loose nuisance convergence offer practical robustness, while the partial identification extensions broaden applicability beyond idealized assumptions. Open questions include extending to multi-valued treatments and continuous outcomes, the dual formulation (minimizing abstention under a loss constraint), and whether the constraint-based abstention rate δ can be learned end-to-end rather than set a priori. The evaluation is limited to two tabular datasets with binary outcomes, leaving open questions about scalability to high-dimensional or continuous-outcome settings.
