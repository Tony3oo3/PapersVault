## Problem

Existing Meta-Black-Box Optimization methods focus on *how* to search (controlling optimizer internals) but largely overlook *where* to search, a critical distinction under tight evaluation budgets where ineffective exploration rapidly depletes resources. For expensive constrained multi-objective optimization, no prior MetaBBO framework provides a compact, problem-agnostic region abstraction with a well-defined search-priority ranking, nor an ELA representation tailored to region-level guidance under sparse evaluations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes M black-box objective functions and J black-box constraint functions over a bounded d-dimensional search space, with a fixed evaluation budget FE_max (300 in experiments, starting from 100 LHS samples). The meta-policy's state is an attention-pooled embedding of normalized objective values paired with MM-CCI scalar levels (in [0,1]) plus a normalized budget-consumption scalar; its action is one of 10 discrete MM-CCI level intervals. The output is a set of feasible Pareto-optimal solutions, with IGD on the feasible front as the evaluation metric.

## Method

MetaSG-SAEA is a bi-level MetaBBO framework where a Double DQN meta-policy selects a target MM-CCI region and a surrogate-assisted NSGA-II (with GP surrogate and ND-A infill) performs the actual search. The key region abstraction, MM-CCI, maps each solution's constraint evaluations to an ordered scalar in [0,1] via per-constraint Max-CCI (solving λ^α g_j(x) ≤ C(1−λ)^β for the maximal feasible λ) aggregated by a worst-constraint min rule, with hyperparameters adaptively fitted per constraint from initial LHS quantiles. Region-level guidance is realized by training a denoising diffusion model on evaluated solutions within the selected MM-CCI interval and sampling an informed initial population to warm-start the SAEA, preserving its intrinsic evolutionary dynamics. The ELA module uses two Transformer attention blocks over (normalized objective, MM-CCI level) pairs, making the state representation scalable across varying dimensions, population sizes, and numbers of objectives/constraints without including decision variables. Training uses parallel environment sampling with centralized Double DQN updates, co-training the ELA encoder end-to-end with the policy.

## Results

On the zero-shot cross-suite and cross-dimensional transfer setting (trained at d=8, tested at d=10, 300 evaluations), MetaSG-SAEA achieves the lowest IGD on 21/23 problems vs. KTS, 17/23 vs. EIC-MSSAEA, 23/23 vs. DRLOS, and 18/23 vs. DRL-SAEA (Wilcoxon rank-sum test, p<0.05). MetaSG-SAEA is the only method that finds feasible solutions on MW5 and MW10, where all four baselines return NaN. Representative IGD values include 0.083 on MW3, 0.061 on MW7, 0.111 on DAS-CMOP2, and 0.268 on DAS-CMOP9, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline by 20–60% relative IGD reduction on most problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaSG-SAEA dominates DRLOS across all 23 problems (23/0/0), with DRLOS returning NaN on 10 of 23 problems due to failure to find feasible solutions. Against KTS, MetaSG-SAEA wins 21 of 23 problems, losing only on MW11 (IGD 0.807 vs. 0.144) and DAS-CMOP6 (both NaN); it is the sole method to find feasible solutions on MW5 and MW10. Versus EIC-MSSAEA, MetaSG-SAEA wins 17 problems, ties 2 (MW4, DAS-CMOP7), and loses 4 (MW11, DAS-CMOP4, DAS-CMOP5, DAS-CMOP6), where EIC-MSSAEA's staged constraint-relaxation strategy handles the shared difficult constraint geometry more effectively. Against DRL-SAEA, MetaSG-SAEA wins 18 problems, ties 1 (MW3), and loses 4 (MW11, DAS-CMOP4, DAS-CMOP5, DAS-CMOP6). The three DAS-CMOP failures (4–6) share a common constraint structure that causes boundary stagnation, a limitation the authors attribute to the lack of a staged constraint-relaxation curriculum in the current design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MetaBBO bi-level paradigm (Ma et al., 2023) by shifting the meta-policy's control target from 'how to search' (algorithm selection, configuration, solution manipulation, algorithm generation) to 'where to search' via region-level guidance. It departs from prior MetaBBO methods that operate in the decision space or hyperparameter space by introducing MM-CCI, a theoretically grounded constraint-calibration mechanism that maps heterogeneous constraint evaluations to an ordered scalar, and a diffusion-model-based warm-start that translates region-level decisions into solution-level priors without directly manipulating candidates. The attention-based ELA also differs from prior neural ELA approaches by operating on objective values paired with MM-CCI levels rather than embedding decision variables, enabling dimension-agnostic transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in MetaBBO for evaluation-limited settings: most prior work assumes abundant evaluations and focuses on search strategy rather than search location, yet in expensive optimization, identifying promising regions is critical to avoiding budget depletion. The MM-CCI's theoretical properties (monotonicity, convexity, diminishing decrease) provide a principled foundation for region stratification, and the zero-shot cross-suite/cross-dimensional transfer demonstrates practical generalizability. However, the method fails on DAS-CMOP4–6 due to boundary-induced stagnation, and the framework is currently limited to multi-objective settings with a fixed NSGA-II backbone; integrating internal optimizer control (operator/infill selection) with external region guidance, and extending beyond multi-objective problems, remain open.
