## Problem

Spatial reasoning from egocentric videos is inherently constrained by the recorded camera trajectory, leaving 3D layouts and object relations underdetermined. Existing MLLM approaches perform single-turn inference, forcing models to resolve geometric ambiguity through semantic priors rather than verifiable cross-view evidence, which produces plausible but incorrect answers that cannot be detected or corrected. The paper argues that spatial reasoning should be revisitable: initial hypotheses should remain open to revision when complementary viewpoints become available.

## Input / Output

The method takes an egocentric video (8 uniformly sampled frames for the Reason Phase; a 1 fpsampled video fed into VGGT for 3D reconstruction in the Re-reason Phase) and a natural-language spatial query. It produces a structured two-part output: a thinking trace enclosed in  tags articulating observations and spatial inferences, followed by a provisional or final answer in <answer> tags (a letter for multiple-choice or a numeric value for regression). The synthesized allocentric video V_exo is rendered as 8 frames to match the original frame budget, and inference uses low-temperature decoding (T=0.1, top-p=0.001).

## Method

ReRe is a training-free, two-phase inference framework that decomposes spatial reasoning into hypothesis formation and cross-view verification. In the Reason Phase, a frozen MLLM produces a provisional hypothesis (thinking trace + answer) from the egocentric video. In the Re-reason Phase, the model observes a synthesized allocentric video and explicitly compares it against its prior reasoning to confirm or revise the answer. The key technical component is a Geometry-to-Video pipeline: VGGT predicts a 3D point cloud from the egocentric video, an Oblique Sweep trajectory (a diagonal path through the scene center at 45° elevation) is planned to maximize occlusion reduction and spatial coverage, and point-based rasterization with Z-buffer depth ordering, confidence filtering, and per-frame median filtering renders temporally coherent video frames. This design treats 3D geometry as observable visual evidence rather than latent features, enabling explicit self-correction without any architectural modifications or fine-tuning.

## Results

On VSI-Bench, ReRe yields consistent average-score gains across all tested open-source MLLMs, with the largest improvement of +8.5% for Qwen3-VL-2B (22.5→31.0) and +5.8% for Qwen3-VL-4B (30.7→36.5). On STI-Bench (Static subset), Qwen3-VL-2B improves by +8.0% (22.2→30.2) with a notable +18.5% gain in Spatial Relation reasoning. The inference-time overhead is modest: the full pipeline adds ~10 s per sample on a single A100 GPU, dominated by the VGGT 3D reconstruction step (~9 s), while rendering and the second MLLM call are negligible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VSI-Bench, Qwen3-VL-4B with ReRe (36.5) surpasses GPT-4o (34.0) by 2.5 points in average score, effectively closing the open-source-to-proprietary gap. However, Gemini-1.5 Pro (45.4) and Gemini-1.5 Flash (42.1) remain substantially ahead of all ReRe-augmented open-source models, with a gap of roughly 9–14 points. On STI-Bench, InternVL2.5-8B with ReRe (34.8) exceeds GPT-4o (31.0) by 3.8 points, while Gemini-2.5-Pro (37.1) and Gemini-2.0-Flash (36.9) still lead. The gains are most pronounced on smaller models (Qwen3-VL-2B: +8.5% on VSI-Bench, +8.0% on STI-Bench), suggesting ReRe is particularly effective at compensating for weaker base spatial priors. Sample-level flip analysis confirms that 71.6% of changed answers are corrections (positive flips) versus 28.4% regressions, a 2.52:1 ratio, indicating the revisiting mechanism reliably improves rather than degrades predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant lines of work on video spatial reasoning. First, it contrasts with training-based approaches (Video-R1, SpaceR, SpatialLadder) that inject spatial awareness via fine-tuning, instead operating entirely at inference time with frozen MLLMs. Second, and more fundamentally, it diverges from prior geometry-augmented methods (Spatial-MLLM, Video-3D LLM) that use VGGT's 3D representations implicitly as latent features aligned to the MLLM's embedding space. ReRe repurposes VGGT's output as explicit, observable visual evidence—a rendered novel-view video—enabling a structured two-phase hypothesis-then-verification protocol rather than a single-turn inference pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The revisitable reasoning paradigm offers a general, training-free recipe that can be dropped onto any frozen MLLM without architectural changes, making it immediately practical for deploying spatial reasoning in embodied agents and robotics. The ablation showing that re-reasoning on the same video actually degrades performance (amplifying hallucinations) is a useful negative result clarifying that the bottleneck is partial observability, not insufficient deliberation. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to non-spatial multimodal tasks, how it interacts with faster or more accurate 3D priors as they emerge, and whether the ~10 s per-sample VGGT cost becomes a bottleneck in real-time deployment despite the paper's caching suggestion.
