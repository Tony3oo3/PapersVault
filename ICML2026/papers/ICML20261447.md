## Problem

RL policies trained in simulation suffer performance degradation and safety violations when deployed in the real world due to the Sim2Real gap. Existing mitigations—domain randomization, robust RL, and pessimistic safety bounds—either sacrifice task performance for conservatism or lack explicit mechanisms to regulate the safety-performance trade-off at deployment time when encountering unmodeled dynamics. This paper asks: can a policy trained entirely in simulation be deployed safely while adapting its risk level dynamically as it gathers limited real-world experience?

## Input / Output

The agent receives a state vector (e.g., ego/lead/follow vehicle kinematics for driving, or 2D position for POINTGOAL2) and, during deployment, a small stream of online context transitions (s, a, s′, r, c) used to refine the latent context estimate. It outputs a continuous control action (throttle/brake in [−1,1] or 2D velocity) generated by a risk-sensitive policy whose conservatism parameter η is a function of the number of collected real contexts, transitioning from highly conservative to risk-neutral as the latent estimate converges. No network parameters are updated at deployment; only the latent z and η are adapted.

## Method

The framework formulates a family of CMDPs indexed by an environment parameter ξ and introduces a probabilistic latent context variable z inferred by a permutation-invariant encoder (product of Gaussian factors over context transitions) trained with a variational objective combining reward/cost critic losses and a KL information bottleneck. Distributional RL via IQN models full return distributions for both reward and cost, enabling risk-sensitive deployment: at inference time, the cost critic is evaluated using upper-tail quantiles U([η,1]) rather than U([0,1]), and a lightweight projected-gradient action refinement enforces the upper-tail cost constraint without updating network weights. The risk parameter η is dynamically scheduled—starting high (conservative) when few real contexts are available and decreasing toward zero as the latent estimate ẑ converges to z*—with the safe η schedule determined by offline simulation calibration of the estimation error ε(N), Lipschitz constant L_c, and cost-reduction Δ_c(η). Theoretical results establish that conditioning on z preserves the γ-contraction of the distributional Bellman operator, that the alternating frozen training scheme is stable, that non-contextual policies incur irreducible regret under task aliasing, and that the deployed cost is bounded by d + L_c·ε(N_real), with the risk-sensitive policy guaranteeing safety whenever Δ_c(η,z) ≥ L_c·ε(N_real).

## Results

In the POINTGOAL2 deployment, the proposed method achieves a cost of 8.86±0.85 (below the threshold of 10) while maintaining a reward of 49.59±8.52, outperforming SPiDR (cost 11.20, reward 43.44) on both metrics. In the Autonomous Driving deployment, the method achieves the lowest cost (16.80±3.33, below threshold 20), lowest jerk (2.86±1.90), and lowest fuel consumption (0.314±0.026) among all baselines, while attaining a reward of 364.85±160.74 that exceeds SPiDR (279.88) and Nominal (284.44). All baseline methods (Nominal, Domain Randomization, Robust RL) violate the cost threshold in deployment, with costs ranging from 21.58 to 35.92 across tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates SPiDR (As et al., 2025) on both reward and cost in deployment: in POINTGOAL2 it reduces cost from 11.20 to 8.86 while increasing reward from 43.44 to 49.59; in Autonomous Driving it lowers cost from 18.53 to 16.80 and raises reward from 279.88 to 364.85. Against Nominal and Domain Randomization, the method trades some reward for a decisive safety advantage—deployment cost drops from 29.62/35.92 (POINTGOAL2) and 30.98/21.58 (AD) to 8.86 and 16.80 respectively, keeping the agent below the safety threshold. Robust RL (Liu et al., 2023) also violates the cost constraint in deployment (24.07 in POINTGOAL2, 27.80 in AD) and exhibits the worst jerk (4.51) and fuel (0.327) in the driving task, whereas the proposed method achieves the best jerk (2.86) and fuel (0.314). In the ablation (Table 5), removing the risk-adaptation module (Ours w/o R.A.) raises deployment cost from 8.86 to 10.12, confirming that the dynamic risk-sensitive policy is the key component that pushes cost below the threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: probabilistic latent context variables from meta-RL (PEARL, Rakelly et al. 2019) and distributional RL via implicit quantile networks (IQN, Dabney et al. 2018) within a constrained MDP formulation. While PEARL-style encoders have been used for task adaptation and distributional RL has been applied to safety-constrained settings (Yang et al., 2023), no prior work combines them to enable *inference-time* risk-level adjustment conditioned on the accuracy of the inferred environment context. The paper departs from distributionally robust Sim2Real methods (SPiDR, As et al. 2025) by replacing worst-case pessimism with a dynamic, context-dependent risk parameter that relaxes as real-world data accumulates, and from standard safe RL by making the risk preference a deployment-time variable rather than a fixed training-time hyperparameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, theoretically grounded path to safe Sim2Real deployment: a provable cost upper-bound (Theorem 5.2), a conditional safety guarantee under risk-sensitive deployment (Theorem 5.3), and an existence result for a safe quantile (Theorem 5.4) collectively give practitioners a mechanism to start conservatively and relax over time. Key limitations include the assumption that the real environment lies within the training randomization distribution (Assumption 5.1), evaluation on only two tasks, and the assumption of a fixed environment during deployment. Open questions raised by the authors include handling continuously changing environments (e.g., dry-to-icy pavement transitions), extending to offline RL with limited real-world trajectories, and validating the framework on a broader range of safety-critical tasks.
