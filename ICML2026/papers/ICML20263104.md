## Problem

Existing data augmentation methods suffer from two forms of semantic infidelity: visual artifacts and manifold intrusion from pixel-level mixing, and label misalignment because the pixel-area ratio is assumed to equal the label-semantic ratio. Diffusion-based approaches improve visual quality but still assign labels based on imprecise assumptions about denoising-step-to-semantic-change correspondence, leaving a fundamental gap between what the image actually shows and what the label claims.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an input image, a reference image from a different class, and a set of four semantic region text prompts (e.g., 'head', 'wing', 'tail', 'breast' for birds) defined per super-class. It produces a synthesized image where only the single most salient region is replaced via diffusion inpainting (generated at 512×512, then resized to 448×448 for FGVC or 32×32/64×64 for general datasets), along with a soft label vector whose mixing ratio ω* is determined by the Grad-CAM saliency proportion of that specific region rather than by pixel area.

## Method

SCalDA constructs a three-domain semantic calibration pipeline. First, Grounding DINO and SAM segment four predefined semantic regions per super-class, and Grad-CAM saliency within each region yields a per-region weight ω_k; the maximum ω* selects both the region to edit and the exact soft-label mixing ratio. Second, SDXL inpainting with IP-Adapter (weight 0.8, 25 steps, guidance 7.5) replaces only that region with content guided by the reference image and a text prompt, preserving the rest of the input structure to avoid manifold intrusion. Third, a metric-learning loss on a normalized hypersphere drives feature similarity to match the saliency-calibrated label similarity, with an exponential gating term (γ=8) that suppresses supervision when two synthetic labels are too dissimilar, preventing misleading geometric constraints. The overall training loss combines the standard classification loss with this metric loss (λ=0.6, τ=0.05).

## Results

SCalDA achieves the best Top-1 accuracy across all six evaluation datasets. On fine-grained benchmarks, it reaches 88.17% on CUB-200-2011, 91.45% on FGVC-Aircraft, 96.37% on Stanford Cars, and 88.42% on Stanford Dogs, yielding an FGVC average of 91.10% (vs. 90.28% for the next-best SGD-Mix). On general datasets, it attains 83.18% on CIFAR-100 and 65.89% on Tiny-ImageNet-200. In the few-shot setting (CUB), SCalDA improves over the strongest baseline DiffMix by 4.02 points at 2-shot (40.21% vs. 36.19%) and by 3.20 points at 20-shot (84.16% vs. 80.96%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCalDA outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset where they are directly comparable. Against traditional mixing methods, the gains are most pronounced: over CutMix (87.23%) and Mixup (86.68%) on CUB, SCalDA reaches 88.17%, and over PuzzleMix (87.06%) it gains +1.11 points. Among diffusion-based baselines, DiffMix (87.16% on CUB) and DiffuseMix (86.19%) are the closest competitors, yet SCalDA still leads by 1.01 and 1.98 points respectively; on FGVC-Aircraft the margin over DiffMix widens to +1.20 points (91.45% vs. 90.25%). The strongest fine-grained baselines, SaSPA (90.59% on Aircraft, 95.34% on Cars) and SGD-Mix (87.66% on CUB, 88.01% on Dogs), are surpassed by 0.86–1.03 points on FGVC datasets. On general datasets (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet), only DiffuseMix is a direct competitor, and SCalDA edges it out by 0.68 and 0.12 points respectively, with the gap being smaller due to lower-resolution inputs and more complex backgrounds that reduce IP-Adapter feature distinctiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCalDA carves new ground at the intersection of saliency-guided mixing (PuzzleMix, SaliencyMix) and diffusion-based augmentation (DiffMix, DiffuseMix, SaSPA, SGD-Mix), departing from both by rejecting the implicit assumption that pixel-area ratio equals label-semantic ratio. Rather than performing full image generation or pixel-level splicing, it repurposes SDXL inpainting with IP-Adapter conditioning to replace a single saliency-selected region, and introduces a metric-learning objective (building on SupCon and i-Mix) that explicitly transfers continuous soft-label geometry into the feature domain. The three-domain alignment framework (label → image → feature) is the paper's unifying conceptual contribution, positioning it as a principled alternative to ad-hoc mixing strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a foundational assumption in mixing-based augmentation—that spatial proportion of pixels determines semantic proportion—and provides a saliency-calibrated alternative with measurable alignment error (SAE). However, the method is computationally heavy (requiring Grounding DINO, SAM, Grad-CAM, and SDXL inpainting per sample), requires hand-defined semantic region templates per super-class, and yields only modest gains on general datasets (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet) where backgrounds are complex and reference features less distinct. Open questions include whether the template-based approach generalizes to open-vocabulary or long-tail settings, and whether the metric-learning component can be distilled into a lighter training-time overhead.
