## Problem

Analytic class-incremental learning (CIL) with pre-trained models offers theoretically zero forgetting and extreme efficiency via closed-form RLS updates, but suffers from a critical bottleneck termed representation rigidity: the frozen or narrowly-adapted feature subspace becomes rank-deficient for future task distributions, causing accumulated approximation errors and catastrophic collapse in long sequences. The paper formalizes this as a projection-residual lower bound on task error and shows empirically that no single-branch architecture can simultaneously satisfy stability and plasticity demands.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a class label as output. At training time, the ViT-Adapter branch is optimized via SGD on only the first task, after which all subsequent tasks are handled exclusively by closed-form RLS matrix updates with no backpropagation. At inference, the analytic classifier generates primary logits, which are then refined by matching the CLIP visual embedding against precomputed text prototypes for only the top-K candidate classes (K=5 by default), yielding a fused score vector over all seen classes.

## Method

VILA is an asymmetric dual-branch framework that constructs a hypothesis space S_total = S_spec ⊕ S_uni to break the representation rigidity bottleneck. The specialized branch (ViT-B/16 + Adapters, trained on Task 1 then frozen) captures task-specific discriminability, while a frozen CLIP-ViT-B/16 provides a universal semantic anchor. At the feature level, the Unified Geometric Calibration (UGC) module l2-normalizes both branches' features onto a common hyperspherical manifold before concatenation and random-projection buffering, preventing the high-magnitude supervised features from dominating the RLS update. At the decision level, the Candidate Semantic Enhancement (CSE) module uses the analytic prediction as a proposal generator (top-K filtering) and rectifies ambiguous boundaries via sparse cosine matching against CLIP text prototypes built from a 19-template prompt ensemble. A practical deployment variant (VILA-OSC) offloads the text encoder after caching class prototypes, reducing active inference FLOPs to 33.98G with 174.27M parameters.

## Results

VILA achieves an average last-task accuracy (A_T) of 85.06% and average incremental accuracy (Ā) of 89.98% across 8 benchmarks, outperforming the strongest analytic baseline LoRanPAC by +5.42% in A_T and +4.75% in Ā. Gains are most pronounced on fine-grained datasets (e.g., +20.11% A_T over LoRanPAC on Aircraft T=10, +2.05% on Cars T=20) and long-sequence settings (e.g., +3.44% A_T on SUN T=50). Under strict 1-epoch online learning, VILA still leads by +4.99% A_T on ImageNet-R (T=10) over the best baseline, while requiring only ~7 min training and ~7.5 min inference on a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VILA outperforms all 10 baselines on the average A_T metric (85.06% vs. 79.64% for the closest competitor LoRanPAC). Against the strongest analytic baselines, VILA gains +5.42% A_T over LoRanPAC and +7.45% over RanPAC, with the largest margins on fine-grained tasks (Aircraft T=10: 68.02% vs. 59.38% for LoRanPAC; Cars T=20: 90.28% vs. 70.17%). Versus the best iterative PEFT method TUNA (75.27% avg A_T), VILA leads by +9.79%, particularly on ImageNet-R T=20 (85.28% vs. 77.33%) and SUN T=50 (83.55% vs. 75.30%). Against the VLM-based ENGINE (77.42% avg A_T), VILA wins on 10 of 12 dataset settings, with the clearest advantage on ImageNet-R T=20 (+4.81%) and Aircraft T=10 (+9.33%), though ENGINE retains a narrow edge on Cars T=20 (89.49% vs. 90.28% is actually VILA winning; ENGINE leads on Aircraft T=20 at 55.51% vs. 65.56% — VILA wins here too). Prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) and LoRA-based methods (SD-LoRA, CL-LoRA) are consistently outperformed by 10–25+ points on fine-grained benchmarks, confirming that VILA's dual-branch calibration addresses a fundamental limitation of single-branch iterative adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analytic CIL line (RanPAC, LoRanPAC) by identifying 'representation rigidity' as the fundamental bottleneck when frozen features are paired with recursive least-squares updates, and departs from iterative PTM-based CIL (prompt, adapter, LoRA methods) by eliminating backpropagation after the first task. It also builds on VLM-based CIL (e.g., ENGINE) but reframes the role of CLIP: rather than fine-tuning the vision-language model, VILA uses frozen CLIP as a universal geometric anchor and text-prior source, combined with a task-adapted ViT-Adapter branch. The key conceptual departure is the two-level calibration (geometric alignment at the feature level via ℓ₂-normalization, semantic rectification at the decision level via sparse text matching) that makes heterogeneous subspaces compatible with the RLS solver's Euclidean assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VILA offers a practically compelling recipe for online CIL: a single gradient-based training pass on Task 1 followed by closed-form recursive updates, yielding ~7-minute total training across 20 tasks on a single GPU. The theoretical lower bound (projection residual onto the frozen subspace, linked to Grassmann distance) gives a clear diagnostic for when single-branch analytic methods will fail, and the matched-budget experiments confirm gains are architectural rather than capacity-driven. Open questions include the quadratic memory growth of the RLS solver for very high-dimensional buffers, the assumption of a consistent domain after Task 1, and whether the dual-branch overhead can be distilled for edge deployment.
