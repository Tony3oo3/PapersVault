## Problem

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) in flow-matching models suffers from severe instability at high guidance scales, producing visual artifacts, color over-saturation, and mode collapse. The paper identifies the root cause as a distributional shift in the predicted velocity field that decouples into a linear barycentric drift (shifting the global distribution center off-manifold) and a quadratic energetic instability (injecting surplus kinetic energy that violates optimal transport dynamics). No prior method addresses both failure modes simultaneously under a unified geometric framework.

## Input / Output

MIST is a training-free, plug-and-play sampling-time intervention that takes a text prompt and operates within the ODE solver loop of any flow-matching model. For text-to-image, it generates 1024×1024 images using models like SD3.5 medium (28 denoising steps) or Flux-dev; for text-to-video, it produces clips at resolutions such as 121×704×1280 (Wan2.2 5B) or 81×480×832 (Wan2.2 A14B). The method requires no additional model evaluations or denoising steps beyond the base model's standard pipeline, and its only hyperparameters are a clipping factor γ (default 1.5) and a temporal skip threshold T_clip (default 1).

## Method

MIST applies a hierarchical two-stage transform to the CFG velocity field v_t^CFG = v_t(x) + w·δv_t. The first stage, Stability Thresholding (ST), acts locally: Temporal Decay clips the guidance magnitude to enforce monotonic energy decay across consecutive timesteps (preventing ODE stiffness), and Spatial Suppression adaptively attenuates guidance at spatial locations where the local guidance-to-structure ratio exceeds a threshold (preventing numerical singularities in high-curvature regions). The second stage, Invariant Alignment (IA), acts globally: Drift Correction projects δv_t onto the zero-mean subspace to eliminate the linear barycentric drift, and Energy Matching renormalizes the velocity variance to match the unconditional prior's kinetic energy profile, preventing the quadratic variance explosion. The final update is v_t^MIST = IA(v_t(x) + w·ST(δv_t)), following a locally-regulated, globally-aligned paradigm that confines the sampling trajectory to the learned data manifold across guidance scales w ∈ [2, 20].

## Results

On the HPD v2 benchmark (SD3.5 medium, 28 steps), MIST consistently outperforms all baselines across guidance scales 5, 10, and 15. At the critical high-guidance setting (G.S.=15), MIST achieves PickScore 22.98, Aesthetic 6.067, CLIP 37.31, HPS 31.60, ImageReward 1.2482, and UnifiedReward 3.4812, while standard CFG collapses to PickScore 21.43, Aesthetic 5.507, and ImageReward 0.4922. On DPG (G.S.=15), MIST reaches an overall score of 86.40 versus 79.93 for CFG and 84.31 for APG. On GenEval (G.S.=15), MIST achieves 68.43 overall versus 59.17 for CFG. For text-to-video (Wan2.2 A14B, VBench), MIST at G.S.=9 improves Total Score from 82.24 (CFG) to 82.61, with gains in Aesthetic Quality (62.82 vs 62.64) and Quality Score (84.07 vs 83.63).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIST outperforms all four baselines (CFG, CFG++, APG, CFG-Zero) on every metric across all three guidance scales on HPD v2, DPG, and GenEval. The most dramatic gap is against standard CFG at G.S.=15, where CFG's ImageReward collapses to 0.4922 (from 1.0818 at G.S.=5) while MIST actually improves to 1.2482, and Aesthetic drops from 5.984 to 5.507 for CFG but rises to 6.067 for MIST. Against CFG-Zero (the closest competitor, also designed for flow models), MIST leads by +0.30 PickScore, +0.081 Aesthetic, and +0.186 ImageReward at G.S.=10, and by +0.73 PickScore and +0.243 Aesthetic at G.S.=15 where CFG-Zero degrades. CFG++ and APG, designed for SDE-based diffusion rather than flow matching, show the largest deficits—CFG++ scores only 20.93 PickScore and −0.0159 ImageReward at G.S.=5, far below MIST's 22.95 and 1.1126. On the video benchmark (VBench, Wan2.2 A14B), MIST at G.S.=9 improves Total Score by +0.37 over CFG at the same scale and +0.31 over CFG at the lower G.S.=4, confirming the method's transferability to video generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIST extends the classifier-free guidance (CFG) framework of Ho & Salimans (2022) within the flow-matching velocity-prediction paradigm (Lipman et al., 2022), providing a principled moment-decomposition analysis of why high guidance scales destabilize the transport dynamics. It departs from prior CFG corrections—APG (parallel/orthogonal decomposition), CFG++ (manifold-constrained inverse problem), and CFG-Zero (velocity projection with zero-initialization)—by unifying the failure modes into two geometrically distinct components (linear barycentric drift and quadratic energetic instability) and addressing them through a hierarchical, training-free transform. The theoretical grounding draws on optimal transport cost (Benamou & Brenier) and information geometry (Fisher Information Matrix), offering a more structured narrative than the ad-hoc heuristics in earlier corrections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIST is practically valuable as a training-free, plug-and-play module that extends the usable guidance range (w ∈ [2, 20]) across multiple production flow models (SD3, SD3.5, Flux-dev, Lumina-Next, Wan2.2) without additional model evaluations or architectural changes. However, the authors acknowledge that the moment-decomposition analysis is 'empirical and intuitive' rather than formally proven, and the method is restricted to velocity-prediction (flow-matching) frameworks, leaving SDE-based models (DDPM, EDM) unaddressed. A key open question is whether the linear/quadratic decomposition generalizes to higher-order moments or to stochastic samplers, and whether the information-geometric interpretation via the Fisher Information Matrix can be made operational rather than purely motivational.
