## Problem

Sequence-level temperature scaling in discrete sequence models is fundamentally difficult: the partition function is intractable, naive token-wise scaling introduces bias and sequence degeneration, and stochastic-order models (e.g., discrete diffusion) further complicate matters because the same sequence can be generated via many different unmasking trajectories, leading to high-variance likelihood estimates. No existing method provides a unified, partition-free way to both measure and enforce sequence-level temperature across the diverse formalisms of autoregressive, masked diffusion, and block diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained sequence model (e.g., GPT2, BD3LM, Phi-3.5-mini-instruct, Qwen2.5-7B), a scalar target temperature T > 0, and a corpus of training sequences (OpenWebText or MATH). It produces a fine-tuned model whose marginalized sequence distribution approximates p^{1/T}/Z_{p_T}. A key practical detail is that the ratio-based objectives (L_p, L_k) are fully offline—requiring only samples from the base model p or the data distribution—so no on-policy sampling from the evolving q_T is needed during fine-tuning. For block diffusion models, exact sequence likelihoods are computed via an O(L·2^B) dynamic program over partial block states, avoiding the combinatorial explosion over full unmasking schedules.

## Method

The paper introduces a unifying Stochastic Sequence Unmasking Model (SSUM) formalism in which all sequence models (AR, MDM, block diffusion) are characterized solely by their distribution over unmasking schedules Π, enabling a single likelihood definition p(x) = E_Π[p(x;Π)]. Building on this, the authors derive partition-free metrics—Kendall's τ for rank preservation and an effective temperature T_eff via least-squares regression on log-likelihood ratios—that cancel the unknown partition function. For fine-tuning, three objectives are proposed: (1) an importance-sampling SSTS loss extending LHTS to stochastic schedules, (2) an entropy variance regularizer L_p that minimizes Var_p[h(x)] where h(x) = log p(x) − T log q_T(x), and (3) a Monte-Carlo variant L_k that replaces exact log p(x) with a k-sample estimator for scalability. The key insight is that likelihood ratios between sequences cancel Z_{p_T}, making both the metrics and the variance-based objectives tractable without the partition function; the variance objectives are shown to be strictly proper (minimizing KL(p_T‖q_T) at their optimum) while being far more stable in practice than importance-sampling weights.

## Results

On BD3LM, the proposed L_p objective achieves an effective temperature range of 0.143–2.36 with NLL 4.24 and schedule variance 72.3, compared to myopic token-wise scaling which is capped at T_eff ≤ 1.04 with NLL 5.28 and variance 624.3. On GPT2, L_p reaches T_eff up to 2.50 with NLL 3.33, versus myopic scaling's max of 1.10 and NLL 5.50. The Monte-Carlo variant L_k (k=4) achieves the lowest NLL of 3.39 on BD3LM while maintaining a wide temperature range (0.162–2.24) and variance of 118.0. On downstream reasoning, L_p fine-tuning at T=0.25 improves Phi-3.5-mini-instruct MATH500 accuracy from 0.330 (base) to 0.452 and HumanEval from 0.066 to 0.095.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is myopic (token-wise) temperature scaling, which the proposed sequence-level objectives consistently outperform. On BD3LM, myopic TS is unable to flatten the distribution beyond T_eff ≈ 1.04, while L_p and L_k reach T_eff up to 2.25–2.36, nearly doubling the achievable range. NLL drops from 5.28 (myopic) to 3.39–4.24 (proposed), and schedule variance is reduced by a factor of 4–9× (624 → 72–135). The KL-based LHTS baseline (Shih et al., 2023), evaluated on GPT2 at target T=0.8, achieves only a minor effective temperature shift (T_eff = 0.89) with NLL 3.10, whereas L_p with the SSUM objective reaches T_eff = 0.90 with comparable NLL (3.11) but better rank preservation (τ = 0.936 vs. 0.809). On reasoning tasks, the comparison is model-dependent: Phi-3.5-mini-instruct benefits more from L_p fine-tuning than myopic TS on both MATH500 (0.452 vs. 0.446) and HumanEval (0.095 vs. 0.035), while Qwen2.5-7B shows the opposite pattern, with myopic TS achieving higher HumanEval accuracy (0.539 vs. 0.370).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Long Horizon Temperature Scaling (LHTS, Shih et al., 2023) from autoregressive models to the broader class of stochastic-order sequence models (SSUMs), which includes masked diffusion and block diffusion language models. The variance-based regularizer draws directly on the log-variance loss from VarGrad (Richter et al., 2020), repurposing it as a partition-free sequence-level objective. The unified unmasking-schedule formalism (SSUM) subsumes prior unified treatments (Fathi et al., 2025; Hoogeboom et al., 2022) while being time-independent, and the partition-free metrics (Kendall's τ, effective temperature via likelihood ratios) depart from prior work that required access to the intractable partition function or was restricted to autoregressive factorizations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sequence-level temperature scaling is a fundamental control knob for generation quality and diversity, yet the intractable partition function and stochastic generation ordering in diffusion models have made it largely intractable in practice. The partition-free metrics and variance-based objectives offer a practical path forward, and the finding that myopic token-wise TS cannot flatten distributions (T_eff > 1) reveals a blind spot in a widely-used technique. Open questions include extending the exact likelihood computation to parallel unmasking settings, understanding when the biased k-sample estimator suffices, and whether the approach transfers to other generative domains such as molecular generation. The mixed reasoning results (model-dependent gains) suggest the interaction between temperature scaling and downstream task performance is not yet well understood.
