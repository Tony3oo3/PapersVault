## Problem

Data cleaning and AutoML are typically executed as decoupled sequential stages, ignoring their mutual influence and leading to inefficient use of limited computational budgets. Under resource constraints, this separation causes either insufficient cleaning or shallow model optimization, and neither stage can adapt to intermediate improvements from the other. The paper asks how to jointly schedule and optimize both processes under a fixed time budget to maximize downstream model performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260912/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes dirty tabular data (with injected errors such as Gaussian noise, random/systematic missing values, and outliers at 10–50% corruption), a total time budget δ, and a clean validation set for model evaluation. It produces a trained differentiable model (from a family of 8 models including SVM, MLP, GaussianNB, LDA, QDA, ARD, and Gaussian Process) that outputs class labels or regression predictions, alongside a progressively cleaned subset of the training data. The cleaning is selective and incremental—only top-k high-gradient samples are repaired per slice—rather than exhaustive full-dataset repair.

## Method

DMCO partitions the total budget into TS time slices and uses a discounted UCB multi-armed bandit to dynamically allocate each slice to either data cleaning or AutoML, with a cost-aware reward (Δmetric/cost) guiding the policy. For cleaning, it computes a cross-model aggregated gradient score G_i = Mean(g_i^(1..M)) + α·Var(g_i^(1..M)) over M candidate models from AutoML, then cleans the top-k samples by this score; a theoretical result (Theorem C.1) shows that sampling proportional to gradient norm minimizes gradient estimation variance under unbiasedness constraints. For AutoML, it applies loss-driven Gumbel top-k sampling to select representative high-loss subsets and a progressive fitting strategy that prunes the hyperparameter search space to the neighborhood of top-K models from the previous slice, reducing per-slice cost from O(R·Train(n)) to O(R'·Train(k)). The closed feedback loop—gradients from AutoML models guide cleaning priorities, and cleaned data improves subsequent AutoML search—enables DMCO to reach 82.19% of full-cleaning-plus-exhaustive-AutoML performance under limited budgets.

## Results

On six real-world tabular datasets, DMCO is evaluated over 10 time slices against four baselines. On classification tasks (F1, higher is better), DMCO reaches 0.9592 on Cancer, 0.9495 on Skin, and 0.9488 on Smartfactory at slice 10, matching or exceeding the full-cleaning + full-AutoML upper bound (CA) on Cancer and Smartfactory. On regression tasks (MSE, lower is better), DMCO achieves 0.1038 on Soilmoisture (beating CA's 0.1246) and 7.6229 on Nasa (vs. CA's 1.8630). The paper reports that under limited budgets, DMCO achieves up to 82.19% of the performance of full data cleaning with exhaustive AutoML.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMCO is compared against four baselines: DR (no cleaning, no AutoML), DA (no cleaning, full AutoML), CR (partial cleaning, no AutoML), and CA (full cleaning + full AutoML, serving as the ideal upper bound). On classification, DMCO consistently outperforms DR and CR across all four datasets, and surpasses DA on Cancer (0.9592 vs. 0.9474) and Smartfactory (0.9488 vs. 0.7427). Most notably, DMCO matches or exceeds the CA upper bound on Cancer (0.9592 vs. 0.9495) and Smartfactory (0.9488 vs. 0.9417), indicating that the time-sliced co-optimization can rival full sequential cleaning + exhaustive search. On regression, DMCO beats CA on Soilmoisture (0.1038 vs. 0.1246 MSE) but lags significantly on Nasa (7.6229 vs. 1.8630), where the larger dataset and higher noise make the budget constraint more binding. Ablation studies confirm that removing gradient-based sampling (No grad) or loss-driven sampling (No loss) degrades performance, and replacing the time-slice strategy with a two-stage pipeline (Two stage) slows convergence due to the severed feedback loop.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of task-aware data cleaning (ActiveClean, BoostClean, CPClean, DemandClean) and AutoML (Auto-sklearn, Auto-Gluon, FLAML), departing from the dominant two-stage pipeline where cleaning precedes model search. Prior joint-optimization attempts (Neutatz et al., 2022; Auto-DP, 2025; cleanTS, 2022) treat cleaning as searchable operators within an AutoML pipeline but remain at operator-level integration with heuristic scheduling. DMCO repurposes gradient-based sample selection (Aljundi et al., 2019) for cleaning prioritization and introduces a multi-armed bandit scheduler with time-sliced interleaving, shifting the problem from one-shot sequential execution to a budget-constrained sequential decision process with cross-model gradient aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically critical gap: under real-world compute budgets, neither exhaustive cleaning nor exhaustive AutoML is feasible, yet the two are mutually reinforcing. The 82.19% recovery of full-optimization performance under limited budgets, combined with robustness across three AutoML backends and six error types, suggests the time-sliced MAB paradigm could generalize to other resource-constrained data+model co-design problems. Key limitations include restriction to differentiable models on tabular data, a simple two-arm MAB that may not scale to richer action spaces, and relatively small datasets (≤245K samples). Open questions include whether the gradient-aggregation strategy transfers to non-differentiable or foundation-model settings, and whether the UCB scheduling remains optimal as the number of cleaning operations or model families grows.
