## Problem

Existing bilinear bandit algorithms assume full observability of both user and item features, which is unrealistic when reward-relevant latent factors (preferences, compatibility) remain unobserved. The natural workaround—vectorizing two-sided features via Kronecker products and reducing to a partially observable linear bandit—destroys the bilinear structure, inflates dimensionality to MN, and forfeits algorithmic techniques designed for bilinear models. This paper asks whether one can handle two-sided partial observability while preserving the bilinear form and its computational advantages.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner receives observed user descriptors {x_i} ∈ R^{d_x} and item descriptors {y_j} ∈ R^{d_y} (with latent components u_i, v_j never revealed) and selects one pair from a fixed [M]×[N] action pool, observing only the scalar reward of that pair. The algorithm outputs the selected pair and maintains an M×N matrix-valued effective parameter Φ̂_t, whose rows and columns are indexed by observed row-space coordinates and orthogonal-complement basis directions rather than by the original feature coordinates. The augmented feature vectors are M-dimensional and N-dimensional (not d_x* or d_y*), so the parameter lives in R^{M×N} and the per-round decision is a bilinear form x̃_i^⊤ Φ̂_t ỹ_j over these augmented vectors.

## Method

BiRoLF augments the observed feature spaces on both sides with orthonormal bases of Row(X)⊥ and Row(Y)⊥, yielding an M×N effective parameter Φ_* whose four blocks (o,o), (o,h), (h,o), (h,h) separate observed-observed, observed-orthogonal, orthogonal-observed, and orthogonal-orthogonal reward components. To handle the bandit-feedback constraint (only one of MN rewards observed per round), the algorithm constructs doubly robust pseudo-rewards for every pair using a coupling distribution with constant match probability p, resampling pseudo-actions until a match occurs to keep inverse-propensity weights bounded. The parameter is estimated by a bilinear DR-Lasso with entrywise ℓ₁ regularization that promotes sparsity in the orthogonal-complement blocks, where the effective dimensions d_hx and d_hy enter the regret bound. A key computational contribution is an exact blockwise solver: because the augmented Gram matrices are block-diagonal, the full DR-Lasso objective decomposes into four independent subproblems (a matrix Lasso, two sets of vector Lassos, and an entrywise soft-thresholding step), reducing per-iteration cost from O((MN)²) under Kronecker linearization to O(Nd_x² + Md_y²) while preserving the same global minimizers.

## Results

On synthetic bilinear bandit instances (M=N=20, d_x*=d_y*=15, d_x=d_y=7), BiRoLF achieves cumulative regret comparable to RoLF (the strongest partially observable baseline) across all four partial-observability cases, while reducing main-estimator optimization time per trial. The blockwise DR-Lasso update reduces per-iteration gradient cost from O((MN)²) under naive Kronecker linearization to O(Nd_x² + Md_y²), and empirically outperforms the non-blockwise variant (BiRoLF w/o Blockwise) in wall-clock time. The theoretical regret guarantee is Õ(√((d_x+d_hx)(d_y+d_hy)T)), where d_hx and d_hy are effective orthogonal-complement dimensions that vanish when latent row spaces are absorbed by observed row spaces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiRoLF matches RoLF (Kim et al., 2025) on cumulative regret across all four cases but is consistently faster in optimization time, since RoLF applies the partially observable linear-bandit method after Kronecker-product linearization, inflating the ambient dimension to MN. Against DRLasso (Kim & Paik, 2019) and UCB (Lattimore & Szepesvári, 2020), BiRoLF achieves lower regret because these baselines either lack the orthogonal-augmentation mechanism for latent features or are feature-agnostic multi-armed bandit methods that cannot exploit the bilinear structure. Compared to fully-observable bilinear baselines ESTR+LowOFUL (Jun et al., 2019) and rO-UCB / ε-FALB (Jang et al., 2021), BiRoLF maintains competitive regret despite operating under two-sided partial observability, whereas these methods are fundamentally misspecified when latent features influence rewards and can incur linear regret. The ablation BiRoLF w/o Blockwise confirms that the exact blockwise decomposition (Theorem 5.1) provides a genuine computational speedup without changing the estimator, while BiRoLF-NoXAug and BiRoLF-Imputation show that removing user-side orthogonal augmentation or the DR correction, respectively, degrades regret performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the partially observable linear bandit framework of RoLF (Kim et al., 2025) to the bilinear setting, where rewards depend on interactions between two-sided features. Rather than reducing the bilinear problem to a linear one via Kronecker-product vectorization (which inflates dimensionality and destroys bilinear structure), BiRoLF preserves the matrix-valued parameter through two-sided orthogonal-complement augmentation. The doubly robust estimation and forced-exploration machinery are inherited from the linear RoLF framework, while the bilinear DR-Lasso estimator and the exact blockwise Gram decomposition are new contributions specific to the two-sided structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first structure-preserving regret guarantee for bilinear bandits under two-sided partial observability, with the leading term depending on effective orthogonal-complement dimensions rather than raw latent dimensions. The exact blockwise decomposition of the DR-Lasso objective (Theorem 5.1) is a clean structural result that yields genuine computational savings without sacrificing optimality, making the approach practical for large user-item pools. Open questions include whether the sparsity assumption on orthogonal-complement blocks holds in real recommendation data, how the method scales when M and N grow substantially, and whether the framework extends to non-stationary or adversarial settings.
