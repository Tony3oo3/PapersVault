## Problem

Test-time scaling for VLMs via 'thinking with images' entangles perception and reasoning in long, unstructured multi-turn contexts, causing small perceptual errors to cascade into wrong answers, requiring expensive RL with hand-crafted rewards, and offering no mechanism to allocate compute asymmetrically between the two processes. The paper asks whether explicitly decoupling visual search from logical deduction can yield more robust, efficient, and independently optimizable test-time scaling.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes an RGB image (evaluated at 256px, 512px, or full resolution) and a multiple-choice text question. Stage 1 outputs spatial coordinates in a strict format—JSON bounding boxes for Qwen3-VL or point coordinates for Molmo2—identifying question-relevant regions without answering. Stage 2 receives the original global image plus one or more high-resolution crops extracted from the full-size source image (regardless of the global input resolution) and produces a single-letter answer. The crops are the only high-fidelity visual tokens the reasoning step processes, keeping the context compact.

## Method

SPARC implements a two-stage prompting protocol inspired by the brain's what/where visual pathways converging in prefrontal cortex: Stage 1 (Implicit Relevance Detection) prompts the VLM to output only coordinates of question-relevant regions, and Stage 2 (Reasoning) re-prompts with the global image plus extracted high-res crops to generate the final answer. For asymmetric test-time scaling, the perception branch runs N independent rollouts (e.g., N=8 at T=0.7) whose bounding boxes are fused via Weighted Box Fusion, so the expensive reasoning backbone processes only one refined visual context. The perception circuit can be fine-tuned independently via a lightweight LoRA adapter trained with standard SFT on synthetic IRD annotations (generated by a larger teacher model with rejection sampling), without degrading the reasoning backbone. Counter-intuitively, training the LoRA at reduced resolution (256px) outperforms full-resolution training, acting as a regularizer that prevents shallow distillation and yields more robust perceptual features.

## Results

On the in-domain average (V*, HRBench-4K, HRBench-8K), SPARC with Qwen3-VL 4B achieves 74.8% at full resolution, outperforming the native baseline (72.6%) and the native 'thinking with images' mode (73.1%). At the more challenging 256px resolution, SPARC reaches 51.0% versus 41.7% native and 36.8% for 'thinking with images', a 9.3-point gain over the latter. On the out-of-distribution XLRS remote sensing benchmark, SPARC (Qwen3-VL 4B) scores 54.8% at full resolution versus 53.5% for 'thinking with images', while at 256px it achieves 48.7% versus 46.2%, processing roughly 0.1% of the visual tokens of a naive full-resolution pass. With Weighted Box Fusion over 8 rollouts, Qwen3-VL 4B further improves to 82.0% at full resolution, and the fully optimized SPARC w/SFT pipeline reaches 91.2% on V*, narrowly surpassing the 235B-parameter Qwen3-VL model (91.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPARC consistently outperforms the native 'thinking with images' paradigm of Qwen3-VL across all resolutions, with the largest gains at low resolution (e.g., +14.3 points at 256px on V* for Qwen3-VL 4B: 54.6 vs. 40.3 native, 54.6 vs. 32.1 TwI). On the V* benchmark, the fully optimized SPARC w/SFT (Qwen3-VL 8B) reaches 91.2%, surpassing DeepEyes (90.1%), ViGoRL-7B (86.4%), Pixel-Reasoner (84.3%), Reinforcing VLMs (80.1%), and ChatGPT-o1 (69.7%), and narrowly exceeding the 30× larger Qwen3-VL 235B-A22B (91.1%), while remaining below ChatGPT-o3 (95.7%). For Molmo2, SPARC improves over native performance by 2.1–4.2 points at full resolution and 3.8–5.5 points at 256px, with the OOD XLRS gap widening to 3.3 points at 256px (40.8 vs. 38.1). The 'thinking with images' baseline is notably unstable: the authors report cases where it underperforms even the native model, whereas SPARC provides consistent gains. The key efficiency advantage is that SPARC at 256px resolution with crops outperforms 'thinking with images' at full resolution while being over 200× faster in time-to-first-token and 50× faster end-to-end.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPARC departs from the dominant 'thinking with images' paradigm (OpenAI o3, DeepEyes, ViGoRL, Pixel Reasoner) that entangles perception and reasoning within a single multi-turn chain-of-thought requiring expensive RL training. Instead, it repurposes the visual grounding capabilities already present in VLMs like Qwen3-VL and Molmo2, framing localization as a standalone 'Implicit Relevance Detection' step that feeds into a separate reasoning prompt. The asymmetric self-consistency mechanism (applying WBF aggregation only to the cheap coordinate-output stage) draws on classical object detection fusion techniques and the self-consistency literature, but applies them in a novel decoupled architecture inspired by the brain's what/where pathway separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPARC offers a training-free, architecture-agnostic prompting strategy that consistently outperforms the native 'thinking with images' baseline across multiple model families and grounding modalities, while enabling asymmetric compute allocation that is impossible in monolithic CoT pipelines. The counter-intuitive finding that low-resolution (256px) training of the perception module outperforms full-resolution training suggests that task difficulty acts as a regularizer against shallow distillation. Key limitations include the requirement for VLMs with native spatial grounding (coordinate or point outputs), and the framework has not yet been extended to iterative zooming, video, or super-resolution augmentation of crops—open directions the authors identify for future work.
