## Problem

Existing spherical vision transformers, such as SphereUFormer, achieve only partial SO(3) equivariance (e.g., horizontal/yaw rotations only) and rely on location-dependent local positional embeddings (LPEs) that introduce memory overhead and degrade robustness to camera tilt or object reorientation. This paper addresses the gap of achieving full SO(3) equivariance in local spherical attention without any LPE mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263993/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates natively on an icosphere grid (rank 7, sampled from 256×512 equirectangular panoramas) rather than on planar projections. For 360° depth estimation and semantic segmentation, it produces per-direction depth values or class labels on the same icosphere; for PDE forecasting, it maps spherical state fields (geopotential height and velocity at 15-minute intervals) to a 1-hour-ahead forecast; for classification, it maps spherical digit images to a single class label. The transformer blocks operate on ranks {6,5,4,3} after initial downsampling, with a neighborhood size of M=19 nearest neighbors per token.

## Method

SoLA achieves full SO(3) equivariance through a distance-preserving positional modulation: each query is lifted via outer product with [b, w·u_i^T] and each key with [1, u_j^T], where u_i, u_j ∈ S² are the unit direction vectors of the respective tokens. The resulting Frobenius dot product factors into a content term ⟨q_i, k_j⟩ and a geometric term (b + w·cos δ_ij), where δ_ij is the great-circle distance, making the similarity invariant to global rotations without any lookup-table LPE. Input-dependent channel-wise gating (Eq. 3) adds per-token, per-channel modulation of b and w while preserving equivariance. A linear (softmax-free) variant reorders computation to aggregate key-value products first (KV-first), avoiding per-query neighbor materialization and scaling linearly in the number of tokens. The full architecture is a U-shaped spherical transformer following SphereUFormer, with SoLA blocks replacing Spherical Local Self-Attention at each encoder/decoder stage.

## Results

On canonical Stanford2D3D depth estimation, SoLA achieves MAE 0.166, MRE 0.071, and δ₁ 94.0%, matching SphereUFormer (MAE 0.165, δ₁ 94.0%). The critical advantage appears under zero-shot 3D rotation: SoLA retains δ₁ 93.3% on rotated Stanford2D3D and 96.1% on rotated Structured3D, while SphereUFormer collapses to δ₁ 25.5–28.9%. For semantic segmentation, SoLA maintains mIoU 72.2% on rotated Stanford2D3D (vs. 37.4% for SphereUFormer) and 53.1% on rotated Structured3D (vs. 6.94%). On the shallow-water PDE forecasting task, SoLA reduces test L2 loss from 0.0972 (SLSA) to 0.0389 under random spin-axis rotation, and on spherical digit classification it achieves 94.3% rotated accuracy versus 15.3% for the GPE baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoLA matches SphereUFormer on canonical inputs (δ₁ 94.0% vs. 94.0% on Stanford2D3D depth; mIoU 72.2% vs. 72.2% on segmentation) but dramatically outperforms it under zero-shot 3D rotation: SoLA retains δ₁ 93.3% and mIoU 72.2% on rotated Stanford2D3D, while SphereUFormer drops to δ₁ 28.9% and mIoU 15.3%, a gap of over 60 points. Against projection-based methods (PanoFormer, EGFormer, Elite360D), SoLA achieves comparable or slightly better canonical depth accuracy (MAE 0.166 vs. 0.169–0.174) and segmentation mIoU (72.2% vs. 60.6–71.4%), but none of these baselines report rotated-input results, making direct rotation-robustness comparison impossible. On the PDE forecasting task, SoLA reduces test L2 loss by 60% over SLSA (0.0389 vs. 0.0972) under random spin-axis rotation, confirming the benefit of full SO(3) equivariance for dynamics. On spherical digit classification with full (global) attention, Linear-SoLA achieves 94.3% rotated accuracy versus 15.3% for the GPE baseline, while using fewer FLOPS (4.1G vs. 5.9G) and lower latency (1.66 ms vs. 2.05 ms). SoLA's FLOPS (19.4G) are higher than SphereUFormer (14.9G) due to the 4D lifting, but actual latency is slightly lower (94.2 ms vs. 95.0 ms) because SoLA avoids the irregular memory access of SphereUFormer's location-dependent LPE lookup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SphereUFormer (Benny & Wolf, 2025), which introduced spherical local self-attention (SLSA) within a U-shaped transformer for 360° perception, but departs from it in two key ways: achieving full SO(3) equivariance (rather than only horizontal/yaw equivariance) and eliminating location-dependent local positional embeddings (LPEs) in favor of a distance-preserving outer-product modulation. The broader lineage includes projection-based methods (PanoFormer, EGFormer, Elite360D), spherical CNNs (Cohen et al., Esteves et al., DISCO), and other spherical transformers (HEAL-SWIN, SpRePE, Attention in the Sphere), all of which either sacrifice full rotation equivariance, incur high computational cost, or rely on expensive integration operations. SoLA's conceptual contribution is showing that a simple 4D outer-product coupling of query/key features with unit direction vectors induces great-circle distance in the attention score while preserving SO(3) equivariance, without any lookup-table or integration overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LPE-free full SO(3)-equivariant local attention mechanism addresses a practical gap: prior spherical transformers either lack full rotation equivariance (limiting robustness to camera tilt and object reorientation) or achieve it at significant computational cost through irregular memory access or expensive integration. The zero-shot rotation robustness demonstrated across depth estimation, segmentation, PDE forecasting, and classification suggests the technique could reduce reliance on rotation augmentation in omnidirectional perception pipelines. Open questions include how to selectively incorporate gravity cues for applications where 'up' is informative (the paper sketches a restricted-equivariance extension), whether the linear formulation can operate directly on ERP/fisheye grids without icosphere resampling, and whether task-specific architectural tuning could close the small canonical-accuracy gap versus SphereUFormer.
