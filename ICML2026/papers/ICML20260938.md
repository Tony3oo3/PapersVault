## Problem

Existing constraint-based causal discovery methods for latent variables (PC, FOFC, RLCD) rely on equality constraints over unconditional covariance matrices, which provably fail under nonstationarity. The paper addresses the gap of characterizing which distributional constraints survive when edge coefficients and noise terms vary across domains, and developing valid finite-sample statistical tests that exploit all domains simultaneously rather than conditioning on a single one.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260938/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. tabular samples of observed variables X paired with a categorical domain index T (e.g., country, time period, intervention type), with no assumption on the number of domains or their sample sizes. It outputs the full causal DAG G over both observed and latent variables (not just a PAG over observed variables), plus an augmented graph G^aug with an extra node T whose outgoing edges mark exactly which variables have causal mechanisms that shift across domains. Phase 2 requires at least two pure measurements per latent variable (one-factor model structure) to resolve scaling indeterminacies when comparing parameters across domains.

## Method

The key theoretical insight (Theorem 1) is that the set of conditional covariance matrices under nonstationary POLCMs is exactly equal to the set of unconditional covariance matrices under homogeneous POLCMs, meaning all equality constraints (tetrads, rank constraints, vanishing partial correlations) transfer verbatim when one conditions on T. Building on this, Corollary 3 extends the rank–t-separation characterization to the nonstationary setting, and two novel statistical tests are proposed: a likelihood-ratio test (Theorem 4) that aggregates canonical correlations across all domains under a Gaussianity assumption, and a permutation-based test (Theorem 5) that requires no distributional assumption by exploiting asymptotic exchangeability of trailing canonical coordinates. The LCD-NOD algorithm has two phases: Phase 1 is a general plug-in upgrade of any equality-constraint-based method (PC, FOFC, RLCD) by replacing its covariance rank tests with the proposed conditional rank tests; Phase 2 identifies nonstationary variables by recovering model parameters (latent covariances, measurement coefficients, noise variances) in each domain up to trivial scaling indeterminacies, then detecting which parameters change across domains under an invariant-anchor and faithfulness assumption.

## Results

For Phase 1 (structure G identification), LCD-NOD consistently outperforms PC, FOFC, RLCD, their naive conditioned-data variants, and CD-NOD on both F1 score and SHD across all three structural settings (no latent variables, one-factor model, and general latent-variable models), with performance improving as sample size increases while the original methods plateau or degrade. For Phase 2 (augmented structure G^aug), the end-to-end method achieves 0.83 F1 and 6.5 SHD at 2000 samples per domain, improving to 0.85 F1 and 5.7 SHD at 5000 samples per domain. The proposed conditional rank tests control Type-I error at the nominal α=0.05 level while achieving substantially lower Type-II error than the classical Bartlett rank test, which exhibits Type-I error approaching 1 in the nonstationary setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LCD-NOD (Phase 1) outperforms the original PC, FOFC, and RLCD algorithms on both F1 and SHD across all sample sizes and structural settings; critically, LCD-NOD's performance improves with larger samples while the unmodified methods do not, confirming that nonstationarity breaks their assumptions. Against the naive 'conditioned data' upgrade (conditioning on a single domain value), LCD-NOD also wins, validating the need for the proposed multi-domain conditional rank tests. CD-NOD, which handles nonstationarity but cannot accommodate latent variables, is outperformed by LCD-NOD in the no-latent-variable setting (Figures 2–3). In the end-to-end Phase 2 comparison, LCD-NOD outperforms both LIT (Yang et al., 2024) and UT-IGSP (Squires et al., 2020) even under the restricted setting favorable to those methods (changes only in exogenous noise, edges only from T to X). For the statistical tests, the classical Bartlett CCA-based rank test applied to unconditional covariance has Type-I error near 1 (i.e., it almost always falsely rejects), while both proposed conditional rank tests maintain Type-I at ~0.05 and achieve lower Type-II error than the standard conditional rank test on a single domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: constraint-based causal discovery with latent variables (PC, FCI, FOFC, RLCD) and causal discovery from heterogeneous/nonstationary data (CD-NOD, LIT, UT-IGSP). Prior latent-variable methods assume stationarity, while prior nonstationary methods typically exclude latent variables or restrict where changes can occur (e.g., only exogenous noise). The key conceptual departure is Theorem 1, which proves that the conditional covariance set under nonstationarity is exactly equal to the homogeneous covariance set, thereby providing a principled bridge that lets any existing equality-constraint-based method be upgraded to handle nonstationarity by simply replacing covariance tests with conditional rank tests. The rank-t-separation characterization (Corollary 3) extends Sullivant et al.'s (2010) result to the nonstationary setting, and the two novel statistical tests (likelihood-ratio and permutation-based) address the practical challenge of testing conditional covariance rank with finite, multi-domain data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The general upgrade framework (Phase 1) is broadly applicable: any existing covariance-based latent-variable causal discovery method can be made nonstationary-robust with minimal modification, which is a clean and reusable contribution. Phase 2, which identifies which specific variables undergo distributional shifts, has no direct prior counterpart that simultaneously handles latent variables and flexible variable relationships. Key limitations include Phase 2 being restricted to one-factor models (parameter identifiability up to orthogonal transformations in multi-factor settings remains open), the need for rank faithfulness, and the non-trivial invariant-anchor assumption (A1). Extending change detection beyond one-factor models and handling more complex nonstationarity patterns (e.g., continuous time, correlated domain effects) are natural open questions.
