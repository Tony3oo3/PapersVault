## Problem

E(3)-equivariant diffusion models for 3D molecular generation replicate biases from semi-empirical training data rather than capturing the true Boltzmann equilibrium distribution, and existing physics-based guidance methods incur prohibitive run-time costs from repeated DFT or force-field evaluations at every denoising step. The paper addresses both gaps by amortizing quantum-chemical cost into a pretrained MLFF and amortizing run-time physical steering into a one-time RL post-training phase.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262460/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a molecular graph specifying atom types and connectivity, and generates a 3D conformation as atomic coordinates in a center-of-mass-free subspace (so the output is defined up to global rotation and translation). The diffusion state at each step is a pair (positions ∈ R^{N×3}, invariant features ∈ R^{N×d_h}), and the score network is E(3)-equivariant with a zero-CoM projection applied to coordinate updates at every denoising step. At inference, no external energy or force oracle is queried—the post-trained model samples at the same speed as the unguided base model.

## Method

Elign formulates reverse diffusion as a finite-horizon MDP and fine-tunes the denoising policy with a critic-free Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) scheme that uses a pretrained foundation MLFF (UMA) as a frozen reward oracle. The reward is disentangled into two channels: a dense, step-wise energy signal obtained via potential-based reward shaping on MLFF energies of reconstructed clean geometries (z_{0|t}), and a sparse, trajectory-level force signal from the terminal RMS force norm. Shared-prefix grouping (rolling out a common noisy prefix then branching into K stochastic continuations) reduces variance, and the two channels are z-score normalized separately before being combined into a single advantage. A theoretical result shows the aligned terminal law is a Gibbs tilt of the pretrained distribution by the MLFF energy, and a uniform energy-approximation bound translates MLFF error into a bounded total-variation distance on the terminal law. The framework is portable across generator backbones (EDM, GeoLDM, flow matching), reward models (UMA, MACE-POLAR), and sampler families, acting as a general post-training alignment layer.

## Results

On QM9, Elign (EDM + UMA-1p1-M) achieves 99.33% atom stability, 93.70% molecule stability, and 95.31% Validity×Uniqueness (V×U), the best V×U among all compared methods and approaching the ground-truth data value of 97.70%. On GEOM-Drugs (larger drug-like molecules up to 181 atoms), Elign reaches 87.94% atom stability and 99.40% RDKit validity, surpassing the RLPF (EDM+xTB PPO) baseline (87.52% / 99.20%). On the alanine dipeptide finite-temperature benchmark, Elign reduces (φ,ψ)-JSD from 0.6432 to 0.5078 and energy W₁ from 0.831 to 0.254 (3.3× smaller), outperforming run-time energy guidance. Crucially, inference speed matches unguided EDM sampling (no oracle calls at generation time), making post-trained sampling 8–16× faster than run-time-guided methods across 60–200 atoms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Elign outperforms the unguided EDM baseline on every QM9 metric, most notably lifting molecule stability from 82.00% to 93.70% and V×U from 90.70% to 95.31%. Against the DFT-based RL baseline RLPF (EDM+DFT PPO, Zhou et al. 2025), Elign matches atom stability (99.33% vs 99.08%) and exceeds V×U (95.31% vs 92.87%) without any DFT queries during training or inference. Elign also surpasses run-time guidance methods: compared to EDM+UMA-1p1-M Guidance (which calls the MLFF at every denoising step), Elign improves molecule stability from 87.25% to 93.70% and V×U from 92.98% to 95.31%, while eliminating all inference-time oracle calls. On GEOM-Drugs, Elign (87.94% atom stability) edges out RLPF with xTB rewards (87.52%) and substantially exceeds the EDM baseline (81.3%). On the alanine dipeptide finite-temperature task, Elign reduces (φ,ψ)-JSD to 0.5078 versus 0.6231 for run-time energy guidance, and cuts energy W₁ by 3.3× (0.254 vs 0.782), confirming that the post-training alignment generalizes beyond minimum-energy conformer benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

Elign sits at the intersection of three established lines: E(3)-equivariant diffusion models for 3D molecular generation (EDM, GeoLDM), RL-based fine-tuning of diffusion samplers (DDPO, Flow-GRPO), and machine-learning force fields as DFT surrogates (UMA, MACE). The key conceptual departure is a two-level amortization framework: MLFFs replace expensive DFT queries (first amortization), and GRPO post-training compiles the resulting physics rewards into the denoising policy so that no oracle calls are needed at inference (second amortization). This is explicitly framed by analogy to LLM post-training (pretraining → SFT → RLHF), a framing not previously applied to molecular generation. The disentangled energy/force advantage formulation and the shared-prefix grouping strategy are novel adaptations of GRPO to the diffusion-as-MDP setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Elign matches or exceeds DFT-guided RL in stability metrics while being 8–16× faster at inference, removing the run-time oracle bottleneck that has limited physics-aware molecular generation. The transfer experiments across backbones (EDM→GeoLDM), reward models (UMA→MACE-POLAR), and sampler families (diffusion→flow matching) demonstrate that the alignment layer is portable rather than a one-off recipe. However, Theorem 1 makes clear the aligned law is a Gibbs tilt of the pretrained distribution, not exact Boltzmann sampling; closing this residual gap would require correcting the pretrained model's implicit prior energy. Open questions include whether single-objective (energy-only) alignment suffices, whether smaller preference models can match UMA's quality, and how the framework extends to systems with explicit solvent or periodic boundary conditions.
