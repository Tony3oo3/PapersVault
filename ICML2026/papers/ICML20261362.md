## Problem

Existing CAD sequence generation methods either add visual inputs without any geometric supervision during training, or apply sequence-level rewards (e.g., Chamfer Distance on rendered programs) that are sparse and non-differentiable. Neither approach provides token-wise geometric feedback linking individual argument tokens to their 3D geometric effects, and prior works also fail to exploit the deterministic structure of CAD grammar, inflating the prediction space and producing invalid programs.

## Input / Output

The method accepts heterogeneous inputs—text prompts, SVG vector sketches, point clouds, or CAD sequences—and autoregressively generates a sketch-extrude CAD program composed of quantized 8-bit argument tokens (2D coordinates, extrusion distances, translation, rotation, scale, and boolean operation type). The key structural detail is that the output sequence interleaves sketch primitives (line, arc, circle with their coordinate arguments) and extrusion operations (10 parameters each), and the method specifically targets the numeric argument tokens for geometric supervision while treating structural tokens (end markers, primitive type tags) as grammar-determined.

## Method

The core contribution is the Argument-induced 3D Point Loss (A3PL), which uses a fully differentiable Point Lifting Module (PLM) to map predicted 2D sketch coordinates and extrusion parameters into 3D space via scale, rotation, and translation, then computes L2 loss between predicted and ground-truth 3D points. The sketch loss lifts predicted 2D points using ground-truth extrusion context, while the extrusion loss evaluates each of the 9 extrusion arguments individually by substituting one predicted token at a time into the ground-truth extrusion sequence. Dequantization uses the expectation over predicted token probabilities rather than hard argmax, enabling gradient flow. A Grammar-constrained Operator (GCO) masks deterministic structural tokens during training and fills them via four grammar constraints (line, arc, circle, extrusion) at inference, reducing the effective prediction space. Extrusion Sequence Augmentation (ESAug) randomly samples valid extrusion configurations with task-specific probability to prevent overfitting to a fixed transformation context and improve generalization.

## Results

Across five CAD generation tasks, the proposed A3PL+GCO method consistently improves geometric accuracy and sequence validity over all baselines. On Drawing2CAD, mean Chamfer Distance drops from 108.80 to 21.59 (≈5× reduction) and Invalidity Ratio from 20.31% to 1.52%. On Text2CAD, mean CD decreases by ~25% (30.50→22.78) while Arc F1 improves by 25% (44.86→55.91). On DeepCAD-LFA, Arc F1 doubles (9.78→19.37) and IR drops from 2.14% to 0.86%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six baselines on every task. Against DeepCAD (Wu et al., 2021), it reduces median CD from 0.78 to 0.18 and IR from 2.72% to 1.39%. Against Text2CAD (Khan et al., 2024), it achieves a 25% relative gain in Arc F1 (44.86→55.91) and cuts mean CD by 25% (30.50→22.78). Against CADFusion (Wang et al., 2025), it improves Arc F1 from 78.92 to 82.43 and reduces IR from 6.02% to 1.92%. Against Drawing2CAD (Qin et al., 2025), the gains are most dramatic: mean CD drops from 108.80 to 21.59 and IR from 20.31% to 1.52%. Against DeepCAD-LFA (Khan et al., 2024), Arc F1 more than doubles (9.78→19.37) and IR falls from 2.14% to 0.86%. On the GeoCAD benchmark (Zhang et al., 2026), the method improves CD Mean from 29.5 to 23.8, COV from 57.6 to 62.4, and IR from 11.2% to 4.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the sketch-extrude CAD sequence representation established by DeepCAD and refined by Text2CAD, and departs from two existing strategies for incorporating visual information: (1) augmenting inputs with additional visual modalities (images, point clouds) that provide no per-token geometric feedback, and (2) RL-based rewards computed on rendered final objects that are sparse and sequence-level. The key conceptual departure is the Point Lifting Module, which differentiably maps 2D sketch argument tokens into 3D space using extrusion parameters, enabling dense token-wise geometric supervision during autoregressive training. The Grammar-constrained Operator draws on structured sequence generation techniques (grammar-aligned decoding, type-constrained decoding) but instantiates them specifically for CAD grammar, masking structural tokens during training and deterministically recovering them at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Token-wise geometric supervision is a natural but previously unexploited signal for CAD sequence generation, and the modular A3PL+GCO framework can be plugged into existing CAD generation pipelines across diverse input modalities (text, SVG, point clouds, CAD sequences). The paper's own limitations are significant: A3PL only supervises numeric argument tokens, leaving Boolean operations and primitive types unsupervised, which leads to errors in solid combination; minor primitives are also frequently omitted. Open questions include extending geometric supervision to non-numeric tokens, improving compositional generalization for highly complex shapes, and adapting the approach to other CAD representations (e.g., Python-based CAD scripts) beyond sketch-extrude.
