## Problem

Converting pretrained dense LLMs into sparse MoE architectures is expensive when done from scratch, and existing dense-to-MoE methods either break the model's intrinsic neuron-level activation patterns (necessitating costly router retraining) or apply uniform expert configurations across all layers, ignoring systematic layer-wise differences in neuron specialization. No prior method exploits the GLU gating mechanism as a structural signal to guide expert construction in a training-free, layer-adaptive manner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full weight matrices (W_gate, W_up, W_down) of each SwiGLU FFN layer from a pretrained dense LLM, plus a multi-task calibration set (210 samples from 42 Flan-v2 tasks) used only to record per-neuron, per-task gate activations. It produces a restructured MoE model where each FFN layer's d_ffn neuron slices are partitioned into N_se always-active shared experts and N_re routed experts of fixed capacity d_expert = d_ffn/N_e, along with a training-free router matrix W_router ∈ R^{d_model × N_e} built from averaged gating vectors. In downcycling mode, the output MoE is further refined via 200B-token continued pretraining with a softmax router and load-balancing loss.

## Method

ExpertWeaver is a three-stage, training-free framework that converts dense GLU-based FFNs into structured MoE layers. First, it records token-averaged Swish(xW_gate) activations for every neuron across a multi-task calibration set, yielding an activation matrix A ∈ R^{d_ffn × M}. Second, it computes the Coefficient of Variation (CV) of each neuron's activation profile to quantify specialization, then maps each layer's fraction of high-CV neurons to a layer-specific shared-expert ratio α_ℓ via a linear formula, producing a U-shaped allocation where boundary layers receive more shared experts and middle layers more routed experts. Third, it selects the top-activated universal neurons to form shared experts and applies balanced K-Means clustering on the remaining specialized neurons' activation vectors to form routed experts of equal size; the MoE router is constructed training-free by averaging each cluster's original gating vectors. In the pruning mode, the router performs hard top-k selection (no weighted combination), preserving original neuron-level GLU computations; in the downcycling mode, a standard softmax router with load-balancing auxiliary loss is introduced during continued pretraining.

## Results

As a training-free dynamic structural pruner at 25% sparsity, ExpertWeaver achieves an average score of 60.4 on LLaMA3-8B and 67.0 on Qwen2.5-7B, outperforming the strongest MoE baseline CMoE (57.2) by 5.6% relative improvement and static pruners LLM-Pruner (48.2/65.0) and FLAP (45.8/58.7) by large margins. As a downcycling strategy, ExpertWeaver(Qwen2.5-7B) with 200B tokens of continued pretraining reaches 63.5 average across 8 benchmarks, surpassing OLMoE-1B-7B* (58.9, trained on 500B tokens) by 4.6 points while activating only 3.5B of 7B parameters. The OLMo-7B downcycled variant retains 96.9% of the original dense model's performance (58.9 vs 60.8) after just 200B tokens of CPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExpertWeaver consistently outperforms all training-free structural pruning baselines at 25% sparsity. Against CMoE (the closest MoE-based competitor), ExpertWeaver gains 3.2 points on LLaMA3-8B (60.4 vs 57.2) and 1.8 points on Qwen2.5-7B (67.0 vs 65.0), attributed to its GLU-informed expert construction, multi-task calibration, and layer-adaptive configuration. Against static pruners LLM-Pruner and FLAP, the advantage is dramatic (12.2 and 14.6 points on LLaMA3-8B; 2.0 and 8.3 points on Qwen2.5-7B), confirming that dynamic MoE activation preserves far more knowledge than permanent weight removal. In the downcycling setting, ExpertWeaver(Qwen2.5-7B) at 63.5 average surpasses OLMoE-1B-7B* (58.9) by 4.6 points despite using half the training tokens (200B vs 500B), and outperforms LLaMA-MoE-v2 (54.4) by 9.1 points. The OLMo-7B variant (58.9) matches OLMoE-1B-7B* exactly while retaining 96.9% of the source dense model, and in a controlled downcycling-vs-upcycling-vs-scratch comparison, ExpertWeaver downcycling maintains its lead throughout training whereas upcycling regresses toward the from-scratch baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dense-to-MoE conversion literature by identifying GLU gating activation patterns as a natural blueprint for expert construction, departing from prior methods (Moefication, LLaMA-MoE, CMoE) that either break intrinsic activation patterns or use fixed layer configurations. It builds directly on the SwiGLU mechanism (Shazeer, 2020), repurposing the gating signal not just as a computational component but as a structural indicator of neuron specialization. The layer-aware allocation strategy using Coefficient of Variation distinguishes it from CMoE's uniform configuration, and the training-free router construction from gating centroids avoids the router training overhead in Moefication and ToMoE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ExpertWeaver provides a practical, training-free pathway to convert existing dense LLMs into structured MoEs, reducing inference cost while preserving more of the original model's knowledge than static pruning. However, the training-free pruning scenario still shows substantial performance degradation (e.g., 72.8→60.4 average on LLaMA3-8B at 25% sparsity), and the downcycling scenario requires 200B tokens of continued pretraining, limiting its accessibility. Open questions include whether the GLU-based approach generalizes to non-GLU architectures (e.g., ReLU-based models), whether the multi-task calibration requirement can be further reduced, and whether the layer-aware allocation principle holds across model scales and training regimes.
