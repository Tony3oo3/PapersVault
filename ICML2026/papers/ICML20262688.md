## Problem

Existing deep learning models for multispectral/hyperspectral image fusion are hard-coded to fixed spectral band counts and integer upscaling factors, forcing separate per-sensor training or costly fine-tuning. This architectural rigidity also prevents pooling heterogeneous datasets for large-scale joint training, exacerbating overfitting on small HSI benchmarks. The paper asks whether a single model can simultaneously handle arbitrary ordered band counts and arbitrary (including non-integer) spatial scales.

## Input / Output

The model takes a low-resolution hyperspectral cube (h×w×C) and a high-resolution multispectral image (H×W×c, c≪C) as input, where C can range from 31 to 191 bands (bounded by C_max=194) and the spatial scale factor s can be any positive real number. The output is a high-resolution hyperspectral cube at the queried resolution, produced as a residual added to the bicubic-interpolated LR-HSI. Unlike fixed-scale methods that require a discrete upsampling layer, the INR decoder is queried at normalized coordinates in [-1,1]², so the same trained weights generate outputs at ×3.2, ×4, ×5.7, ×8, ×16, or ×32 without architectural modification.

## Method

SSA combines two mechanisms: (1) Matryoshka Kernel Layers (MKL), which store a single nested convolution weight tensor W_nested ∈ R^{D×C_max×k×k} and generate a valid kernel by slicing the first C_in channels (input) or first C_out channels (output), enabling one set of parameters to process any band count up to C_max; and (2) an Implicit Neural Representation backbone where EDSR-based encoders produce spectral and spatial latent codes, and a shared MLP decoder predicts the per-pixel spectral residual from query coordinates, sampled latent features, and relative offsets, with LIIF-style 4-neighbor softmax fusion for spatial continuity. Training uses bucket-based mini-batch sampling across seven heterogeneous datasets (31–191 bands) with a unified L1+SSIM loss, so the same parameters are optimized on structurally different inputs each step. The UMAP visualization of learned MK weights reveals a smooth spectral manifold, indicating the nested kernel captures inter-band correlation that supports cross-sensor generalization.

## Results

On the CAVE dataset at the in-distribution ×4 scale, SSA achieves 45.96 dB PSNR, 2.55° SAM, 1.72 ERGAS, and 0.994 SSIM, outperforming all six individually-trained baselines. At out-of-distribution scales (×8, ×16, ×32), SSA maintains the best or second-best results across all four metrics on both CAVE and PaviaC, e.g., 38.80 dB PSNR at ×8 and 28.06 dB at ×32 on CAVE. Critically, these results come from a single model jointly trained on seven heterogeneous datasets (31–191 bands), whereas all baselines are trained independently per dataset. On unseen fractional scales (×3.2, ×5.7) and unseen-band datasets (Houston 48 bands, Loukia 176 bands), SSA also achieves the best PSNR after only 500 adaptation steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSA (a single jointly-trained model) outperforms all six individually-trained baselines on nearly every metric at every scale. At the in-distribution ×4 scale, SSA leads by ~0.15 dB PSNR over DCFormer (45.96 vs. 45.81) on CAVE and by ~0.79 dB over LRTN (45.92 vs. 45.13) on PaviaC, while also achieving the best SAM and ERGAS. The gap widens dramatically at out-of-distribution scales: at ×8 on CAVE, SSA gains ~2.7 dB PSNR over the next-best DCFormer (38.80 vs. 41.46 is actually DCFormer higher — correction: DCFormer achieves 41.46 at ×8 on CAVE, but SSA's 38.80 is lower; however SSA wins on SAM/ERGAS/SSIM). At ×16 and ×32, DCFormer cannot produce outputs at all (marked '–'), and SSA leads the remaining methods by 2–4 dB PSNR. DCINN is competitive at ×4 but degrades sharply at higher scales and is ~10× slower at inference (861 ms vs. 83 ms). PSTUN and LRTN are the strongest fixed-scale competitors at ×4 but lose 3–6 dB PSNR to SSA at ×16/×32. MIMO-SST and DSPNet are consistently the weakest baselines across all scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate ideas into a single MS/HS fusion framework: the nested dim-slicing principle from Matryoshka Representation Learning (Kusupati et al., NeurIPS 2022), repurposed from embedding dimensions to convolution kernel weights to handle variable spectral band counts, and the continuous coordinate-based decoding from LIIF (Chen et al., CVPR 2021) for arbitrary-scale reconstruction. Prior INR-based fusion methods (e.g., DCINN, OTIAS) solved spatial rigidity but required re-engineering for different band counts, while spectral-flexible approaches (e.g., PSTUN's mixed training) did not natively support non-integer scales. SSA departs from the dominant 'one dataset, one model' paradigm by demonstrating that a single jointly-trained network with Matryoshka Kernels can match or exceed per-sensor specialized models across 31–191 bands.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: a single model replacing per-sensor retraining reduces engineering overhead in multi-sensor remote sensing and medical imaging pipelines. However, the paper is candid about limitations—the model is not permutation-invariant (relies on wavelength ordering), INR decoding cost grows with output resolution (701 ms at ×32 on a 32×32 patch), and cross-sensor transfer still benefits from ~500 adaptation steps rather than being fully unconditional. Open questions include whether MK generalizes to extreme band counts beyond C_max=194 without retraining, performance under real-world sensor noise and spectral response mismatch, and whether the approach extends to other fusion tasks (e.g., panchromatic sharpening) with similar heterogeneity.
