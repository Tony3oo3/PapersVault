## Problem

Geographic location encoders based on spherical harmonics distribute representational capacity uniformly across the globe, making them ill-suited for localized tasks that demand high spatial resolution within a specific region. Raising the global bandlimit to capture fine local detail is blocked by numerical instability in spherical harmonic computation (divergence beyond L≈40 in 32-bit precision) and yields prohibitively dense embeddings. The paper asks whether a basis can concentrate high-frequency representational capacity inside a chosen region-of-interest while remaining computationally feasible and pole-safe.

## Input / Output

The encoder takes geographic coordinates (λ, φ) on the unit sphere and, for spatio-temporal tasks, a normalized time coordinate t∈[-1,1]; for geo-aware image tasks, it also accepts frozen image embeddings (e.g., AlphaEarth 63-dim or Galileo features) that are concatenated or element-wise multiplied with the location encoding. The output is a fixed-dimensional positional encoding vector whose size is governed by the regional Shannon number K≈(area(R)/4π)(L_r+1)² for the Slepian component plus (L_g+1)² for the global SH component, yielding embeddings of only 86–785 dimensions for regional tasks versus 1600+ for equivalent global SH. For temporal encoding, the DPSS component produces K_t≈2N_tW features from a discrete sequence of N_t timesteps, interpolated to continuous time via cubic splines.

## Method

The core mechanism solves the spherical Slepian concentration problem: given a region R and high-resolution bandlimit L_r, compute eigenvectors of the D_{L_r}×D_{L_r} concentration matrix K whose entries are integrals of spherical harmonic products over R, then retain the first K=⌈N(R,L_r)⌉ eigenfunctions (ordered by descending eigenvalue μ_n) as the regional positional basis. For computational tractability at high L_r, the paper restricts R to spherical caps, where the concentration matrix block-diagonalizes by harmonic order m, reducing the eigenproblem to independent blocks of size at most L_r×L_r and enabling precomputation at a reference cap followed by rotation to the target center. The hybrid encoder concatenates these well-concentrated Slepian modes with a coarse global SH basis at bandlimit L_g≪L_r, bridging local detail and global context while preserving pole-safety and spherical surface-distance geometry inherited from the SH structure. The temporal extension replaces Fourier or polynomial time encodings with Discrete Prolate Spheroidal Sequences (DPSS), the 1D analogue that concentrates energy in both time and frequency, combined with global SH via concatenation to form a joint space-time encoder.

## Results

On the three supervised geographic tasks, the Hybrid Slepian encoder (Lr=120) achieves R²=0.71 on California Housing, 92% accuracy on Japan Prefectures, and R²=0.98 on Arctic MSS interpolation, outperforming the strongest global SH baseline (L=40) by 0.07 R², 4 percentage points, and 0.01 R² respectively, while using only 186–785 embedding dimensions versus 1,681 for SH. On species distribution modeling (SINR), the hybrid encoder with two Slepian caps achieves 0.704 mAP on S&T Birds and 0.479 mAP on IUCN with a linear classifier, a 40% relative improvement over SH(L=10) on IUCN. For spatio-temporal climate prediction on ACE, DPSS temporal encodings (NW=15) reduce mean RMSE to 1.344, a 9% improvement over Fourier temporal encodings (1.477).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Hybrid Slepian encoder (Lr=120) outperforms all eight baseline families across all three supervised tasks. Against the strongest global baseline, Spherical Harmonics (Rußwurm et al., 2024), Slepian wins by 0.07 R² on California Housing (0.71 vs 0.64) and 4 pp on Japan Prefectures (0.92 vs 0.88), while SH diverges numerically at L≥40 on Arctic MSS where Slepian achieves R²=0.98. The DFS-based Sphere2Vec variants (Mai et al., 2023) peak at R²=0.53 on California Housing, well below Slepian's 0.71, and the multi-scale Grid/Theory encodings (Mai et al., 2020) reach only 0.45 R² and 84% accuracy. Random Fourier Feature baselines underperform substantially: Planar RFF (Tancik et al., 2020) achieves just 0.42 R² despite 2000 dimensions, and Deep RFF (Chen et al., 2025) reaches 0.57 R² with only 64 dimensions. Spherical wavelets (Cai & Balestriero, 2025) are the weakest frequency-based baseline at 0.34 R² and diverge on Arctic MSS. Simple geometric encodings (Cartesian3D from Tseng et al., 2023; Wrap from Mac Aodha et al., 2019) are competitive only at very low resolution (R²≈0.37, Acc≈0.71) and are clearly outperformed by Slepian at all scales. Notably, Slepian achieves these gains with 1/10th to 4/10th the embedding dimensionality of high-bandlimit SH, and is 3× faster at matched performance (189s vs 558s training time).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes spherical Slepian functions—long established in geophysics for signal concentration and inversion (Simons et al., 2006; Bates et al., 2017)—as a positional encoding basis for geographic machine learning, a role they had not previously served. It extends the global spherical harmonic (SH) encoder of Rußwurm et al. (2024) by replacing the uniform global basis with a spatio-spectrally concentrated regional basis, and introduces a hybrid Slepian-SH architecture that concatenates high-resolution regional modes with a coarse global backbone. The temporal extension via Discrete Prolate Spheroidal Sequences (DPSS) follows Slepian's 1978 discrete-sequence formulation and mirrors the joint space-time encoder framework of Mickisch et al. (2025), substituting their Fourier temporal component with a spectrally concentrated one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that *where* representational capacity is allocated on the sphere matters more than *how much* is allocated, offering a principled solution to the local-global tradeoff that has limited geospatial ML at fine resolutions. Practical limitations remain: the region of interest, cap radius, and bandlimits must be manually specified, very small regions cap out in Shannon number, and dense local training data is required. Open directions include adaptive region selection, contrastive pretraining with Slepian heads (analogous to SatCLIP), and extending to geometrically complex non-cap-shaped regions such as archipelagos or administrative boundaries where spherical caps are poorly suited.
