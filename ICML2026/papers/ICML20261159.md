## Problem

Existing multimodal knowledge updating research focuses exclusively on injecting previously unknown knowledge, overlooking the distinct challenge of correcting knowledge the model has already internalized but that has since changed in the real world. Moreover, prior evaluations are confined to same-modality settings, leaving cross-modal consistency of updated knowledge entirely unexamined. MMKU-Bench addresses both gaps by providing a unified benchmark that distinguishes updated from unknown knowledge and systematically evaluates cross-modal transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261159/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark sample pairs an image (selected via CLIP cosine-similarity filtering to ensure semantic relatedness but visual distinctness) with a natural-language question and a concise 1–4 word ground-truth answer. Knowledge injection can be performed in multimodal form (image + text) or text-only form, and evaluation can be conducted in either modality, yielding three cross-modal transfer paradigms (M→M, M→T, T→M). For updated-knowledge tasks, the model output is scored against both the new (Correct) and old (Outdated) answers using Cover Exact Match and F1, enabling measurement of whether the model has actually overwritten prior knowledge rather than merely adding new information.

## Method

The benchmark is constructed through a four-step pipeline: (1) collecting updated knowledge via GPT-4o counterfactual editing of EVQA pairs and unknown knowledge from Wikipedia snapshot diffs (Feb 2025 vs. Nov 2025); (2) generating and paraphrasing QA pairs with CLIP-based image selection; (3) constructing cross-modal variants by replacing entity hypernyms with explicit names and removing images; and (4) multi-round human quality control. On this benchmark, the paper evaluates seven injection methods—RAG with Golden Context, SFT (Full-FT, LoRA), RLHF (CPO, ORPO, SimPO), and knowledge editing (GRACE, WISE)—on InternVL2.5-8B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B across three complementary axes: knowledge injection accuracy, general capability preservation (11 benchmarks across 6 dimensions), and cross-modal/decision consistency. Key findings reveal that SFT and RLHF suffer severe catastrophic forgetting (especially on updated knowledge), knowledge editing preserves general capabilities but fails at scale, and cross-modal transfer of injected knowledge remains structurally limited.

## Results

On MMKU-Bench, RAG with Golden Context achieves the highest performance, reaching 67.02% CEM on Updated knowledge and 76.03% CEM on Unknown knowledge for InternVL2.5-8B (69.44% and 79.36% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B). Among parameter-modifying methods, SFT Full-FT and RLHF ORPO lead with ~35% CEM on Updated and ~17–18% CEM on Unknown for InternVL2.5-8B, while knowledge editing methods (GRACE, WISE) achieve near-zero performance (≤6.54% CEM). On general capability preservation, WISE maintains scores within 1–5 points of the vanilla model across all 11 benchmarks, whereas Full-FT on Updated knowledge causes MME to drop from 1688.2 to 499.1 (InternVL) and from 1691.6 to 393.9 (Qwen2.5-VL), indicating severe catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAG with Golden Context substantially outperforms all parameter-modifying methods, achieving 67.02% CEM on Updated knowledge versus 35.19% for the best SFT/RLHF methods (a ~32-point gap), confirming that models struggle to internalize conflicting knowledge even when the correct answer is explicitly provided. Among SFT variants, Full-FT (35.19% CEM Updated) outperforms LoRA (29.54%) by ~6 points, but LoRA causes less general capability degradation (MME 984.7 vs. 499.1 for Full-FT on Updated). Among RLHF variants, ORPO and CPO achieve comparable Updated CEM (~33–35%) while reducing Outdated-answer rates to 4.0–4.85% (vs. 5.53% for Full-FT), indicating slightly better suppression of stale knowledge. Knowledge editing methods (GRACE, WISE) are dramatically inferior on injection accuracy (≤6.54% CEM) but uniquely preserve general capabilities: WISE scores within 1–5 points of vanilla on all 11 general benchmarks, whereas Full-FT on Updated drops MME by 70% (InternVL) or 77% (Qwen2.5-VL). Overall, no single method dominates: SFT/RLHF win on injection accuracy but suffer catastrophic forgetting, while KE preserves capabilities but fails at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal knowledge editing line (VLKEB, MMKE-Bench, MMEVOKE) by introducing a critical distinction that prior benchmarks conflate: updating knowledge the model already knows versus injecting entirely new knowledge. While text-domain benchmarks (ZSRE, CounterFact, MQuAKE) study single-fact or multi-hop editing, and multimodal counterparts focus on isolated fact replacement, none systematically evaluate the harder scenario of overwriting existing parametric memories or assess cross-modal transfer consistency (M→T, T→M). The paper repurposes the EVQA dataset and Wikipedia snapshots to construct counterfactual update pairs, positioning itself as the first benchmark to jointly measure injection effectiveness, general capability preservation, and cross-modal alignment under a unified protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that updating existing knowledge is more destructive to general capabilities than injecting unknown knowledge (e.g., Full-FT drops MME from 1688→499 for updated vs. 1688→1090 for unknown on InternVL2.5), and that cross-modal transfer of injected knowledge remains severely limited—findings that should reshape how the community designs continual learning pipelines for LMMs. A key open question is whether the counterfactual editing construction (using GPT-4o to generate new answers) faithfully captures real-world knowledge drift, since the authors acknowledge distribution shift as a limitation. The benchmark also highlights that no current method (SFT, RLHF, or KE) simultaneously achieves strong knowledge updating, minimal forgetting, and cross-modal consistency, leaving a clear target for future algorithmic work.
