## Problem

Reward models in RLHF are prone to reward hacking, where policies exploit spurious correlations (response length, formatting, stylistic artifacts) encoded in dense, opaque reward functions rather than capturing true human intent. Existing mitigations—model ensembles, information-theoretic bottlenecks, and supervised debiasing—are either computationally expensive, rely on implicit relevance notions that fail to explicitly separate semantic intent from nuisance factors, or generalize poorly beyond narrowly defined bias settings. The paper asks whether a principled probabilistic structure can simultaneously model uncertainty, enforce disentanglement, and suppress spurious correlations within a single reward model.

## Input / Output

Given a user prompt x and a candidate response y, the LLM backbone produces a dense hidden representation z ∈ R^{d_model}, which is then mapped through a lightweight variational inference network to a sparse non-negative latent vector θ ∈ R_+^K. The scalar reward is computed as r(x,y) = θ^T Φ, where Φ ∈ R_+^K is a shared global reward dictionary, and the preference probability follows a Bradley-Terry likelihood over reward differences. Unlike standard BT models that project z through a single dense linear head, BNRM outputs a factorized, non-negative reward decomposition that is both interpretable and uncertainty-aware.

## Method

BNRM reformulates reward learning as a hierarchical Bayesian generative process over sparse, non-negative latent factors, extending the Bradley-Terry model with two complementary levels of structure: instance-specific θ (local sparsity for disentanglement) and a global dictionary Φ (global sparsity for debiasing), both governed by Gamma priors. The pretrained LLM backbone is repurposed as an amortized inference network that parameterizes a reparameterizable Weibull variational posterior over θ, enabling end-to-end training via standard backpropagation. Training maximizes the ELBO, where the reconstruction term captures the BT preference likelihood and KL divergence terms regularize the posteriors toward sparse Gamma priors, with a single trade-off hyperparameter η. The key mechanism is a 'disentanglement-then-debiasing' pipeline: local sparsity in θ activates only a small subset of semantic factors per instance, while global sparsity in Φ drives population-level weights toward zero for factors that encode systematic but non-causal biases, yielding a reward function that is simultaneously interpretable and robust to over-optimization.

## Results

On the primary reward modeling benchmark (40K Unified Feedback, Gemma-2B-it, LoRA), BT-BNRM achieves 74.2% on UF, 83.6% on HHH-Alignment, 75.2% on MT-Bench, and 72.5% on RewardBench (average), improving over the BT baseline by +5.4, +13.3, +6.1, and +8.0 points respectively. Under full fine-tuning on Skywork-Preference-v0.2, BNBT-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B reaches 93.6% average on RewardBench (95.3% Chat, 89.7% Chat-Hard, 92.6% Safety, 96.9% Reasoning), surpassing the Skywork-Reward baseline by +0.5 points overall. In downstream PPO-based RLHF, BNRM-aligned Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct achieves 74.98% average accuracy across 8 benchmarks, outperforming the best baseline (ALBM at 63.92%) by over 11 points. On RM-Bench Hard (length/format bias), BNRM reduces the Pearson correlation between response length and reward score from 0.488 (BT) to 0.123, while improving Hard-subset accuracy from 33.6% to 36.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard BT baseline, BT-BNRM wins on every reported metric with 40K data: +5.4% on UF, +13.3% on HHH, +6.1% on MT-Bench, and +8.0% on RewardBench average; the gap narrows but persists at 400K (+4.9% UF, +13.0% HHH, +4.9% MT, +5.0% RewardBench). Compared to BT-Margin, BT-LabelSmooth, and BT-Ensemble, BNRM consistently outperforms all three variants across ID and OOD benchmarks, with the largest margins on HHH-Alignment (e.g., 83.6% vs. 69.8% for BT-Margin at 40K). Against the GRM family (GRM-DPO, GRM-DPO-Noref, GRM-SFT), BT-BNRM leads on UF, HHH, and MT-Bench, while GRM-DPO edges out BNRM on RewardBench Chat (97.8% vs. 95.6%) at 40K; however, GRM-BNRM (the BNRM variant built on GRM-SFT) recovers and surpasses GRM-SFT on most metrics. The strongest uncertainty-aware baseline, InfoRM, is the closest competitor: at 40K, InfoRM slightly leads on HHH (83.9% vs. 83.6%) and RewardBench Chat (95.8% vs. 95.6%), but BT-BNRM wins on UF (+2.6%), MT-Bench (+3.8%), and RewardBench average (+1.3%); at 400K, BT-BNRM leads InfoRM on all four primary benchmarks. In the full-parameter RewardBench comparison (Table 2), BNBT-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B (93.6% avg) narrowly beats Skywork-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B (93.1%) and all other discriminative baselines, with the largest gain on Chat-Hard (+1.3 points). In the RLHF downstream evaluation (Table 3), BNRM-fine-tuned policies outperform all baselines including SK, PoE, LP, ALBM, and InfoRM by 5–12 points in average accuracy on both Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and OpenRLHF-Llama3-8B-SFT.

## Relation to Prior Work

BNRM synthesizes two previously separate research threads: classical non-negative factor analysis (NFA/Poisson factor analysis) for sparse, parts-based representations, and the Bradley-Terry preference model that underpins modern RLHF reward modeling. It departs from prior reward-hacking mitigations—ensembles (Coste et al., 2024), information-bottleneck objectives (InfoRM, Miao et al., 2024), and supervised debiasing (Chen et al., 2024)—by reshaping the reward representation itself rather than post-hoc penalizing uncertainty or correcting specific biases. The amortized variational inference with Weibull-parameterized posteriors follows the scalable inference recipe of Zhang et al. (2020) for non-negative latent variable models, repurposed here for preference learning on top of a frozen or LoRA-tuned LLM backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BNRM offers a principled, interpretable alternative to opaque scalar reward heads: the sparse non-negative factorization r = θᵀΦ yields mechanistic explanations (signal amplification vs. error rectification) that are unattainable in standard BT models, and the 'plug-and-play' enhancement of an already-strong 8B reward model (Skywork 93.1→93.6) suggests broad adoptability. Key limitations include a single method-specific hyperparameter η, evaluation capped at 8B-scale models, and the absence of multi-turn, tool-use, or long-horizon RLHF settings where more adaptive reward hacking may emerge. Open questions include whether the sparsity-induced debiasing generalizes to non-text modalities and whether the factor semantics remain stable under adversarial policy optimization at scale.
