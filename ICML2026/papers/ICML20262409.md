## Problem

Existing heterogeneous multi-modal remote sensing detectors (e.g., SM3Det) adopt a 'late alignment' strategy that entangles cross-modal feature alignment with detection optimization during fine-tuning, causing gradient conflicts, training instability (NaNs, gradient explosions under AMP), and poor generalization—especially as backbone capacity grows. Compounding this, prior pretraining frameworks for multi-modal remote sensing require spatially aligned image pairs (e.g., co-registered optical-SAR), which are infeasible to collect at scale for complex RGB/SAR/infrared configurations.

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the model consumes a single remote sensing image from any modality (RGB, SAR, or infrared) paired with a natural-language instruction and expected text response, producing a generated text sequence via a frozen LLM—no paired multi-modal images are required. During fine-tuning, the same shared ViT-Large backbone (wrapped in a ViT-adapter) takes a single-modality image and outputs object bounding boxes (both horizontal and oriented) with class labels, evaluated on the SOI-Det benchmark spanning SARDet-100K, DOTA, and DroneVehicle.

## Method

BabelRS decouples modality alignment from task learning via a two-component language-pivoted pretraining stage. First, Concept-Shared Instruction Aligning (CSIA) projects visual tokens from a shared ViT-Large encoder into a frozen Qwen2 LLM's embedding space and trains with a causal language modeling loss over instruction-response pairs, implicitly aligning heterogeneous modalities through shared linguistic concepts without requiring spatially paired data. Second, Layerwise Visual-Semantic Annealing (LVSA) bridges the granularity gap between global language semantics and dense detection by progressively fusing intermediate ViT layers (3rd, 9th, 18th, last) into the language-aligned representation using a linearly annealed coefficient α(t)=min(t/τ,1), avoiding abrupt distribution shifts. After pretraining, fine-tuning uses a simple joint training strategy with modality-specific detection heads and no auxiliary alignment losses, yielding stable optimization and SOTA performance on SOI-Det.

## Results

On the SOI-Det benchmark (combining SARDet-100K, DOTA, and DroneVehicle), BabelRS achieves 81.32 AP@50, 51.57 mAP, and 53.02 H-mAP overall, outperforming the strongest fine-tuning-stage baseline SM3Det (80.68 / 50.20 / 51.31) by +0.64 / +1.37 / +1.71 respectively. Gains are most pronounced on SAR (63.30 vs. 60.64 mAP) and infrared (51.32 vs. 48.87 mAP), where general-purpose pretraining typically struggles. Under AMP training, BabelRS remains stable at 79.13 AP@50 while four late-alignment baselines (MAE, BEiTv2, DINOv2, ScaleMAE) diverge to NaN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BabelRS outperforms all 12 baselines across every metric. Against fine-tuning-stage methods, it leads SM3Det (the strongest prior unified detector) by +1.37 mAP and +1.71 H-mAP, and beats the simpler baselines (Simple Joint Training, DA, UniDet, Uncertainty loss) by 2.8–4.5 mAP. Against pretraining-stage baselines, the margins are substantially larger: BabelRS surpasses BEiTv2 by +6.90 mAP, DINOv2 by +6.55 mAP, and MAE by +8.73 mAP, while CLIP-style methods (CLIP, RemoteCLIP) lag by over 14 mAP due to their reliance on final-layer global alignment. Under AMP training, four late-alignment pretraining methods (MAE, BEiTv2, DINOv2, ScaleMAE) produce NaN losses and fail entirely, whereas BabelRS retains 79.13 AP@50, confirming that early language-pivoted alignment yields a fundamentally better-conditioned optimization landscape for heterogeneous multi-modal detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

BabelRS builds on the InternVL-2.5 vision-language model architecture and the Visual Instruction Tuning paradigm, repurposing them for remote sensing cross-modal alignment. It departs fundamentally from the 'late alignment' strategy used by SM3Det and SkySense, where modality alignment is entangled with detection fine-tuning, by shifting alignment to a dedicated pretraining stage that uses language as a semantic pivot. Unlike LanguageBind or ImageBind, which align modalities to a frozen LLM via contrastive or binding objectives, BabelRS enforces alignment implicitly through causal language modeling on instruction-following data, and introduces Layerwise Visual-Semantic Annealing to resolve the granularity mismatch between global language semantics and multi-scale detection features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically demonstrates a concrete optimization pathology (gradient explosion, NaN failures under AMP) that plagues late-alignment multi-modal detectors, and shows that early language-pivoted pretraining resolves it with substantially lower compute (20 GPU-hours vs. 250–400 for prior RS pretraining). The proposed H-mAP metric is a useful diagnostic for detecting modality-specific failures hidden by category imbalance. Open questions include whether language supervision is sufficient for fine-grained or physics-specific SAR/IR patterns that lack clear linguistic counterparts, and whether the framework extends to temporal or video-based remote sensing tasks.
