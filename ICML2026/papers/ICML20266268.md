## Problem

Existing amortized Bayesian inference methods (PFNs, TabPFNs, ICL-based predictors) bake the prior distribution into model weights during meta-training, leaving no mechanism to adapt to new priors at test time. This makes them brittle under prior shifts across users, domains, or environments, with unclear out-of-distribution behavior. The paper asks whether in-context learning can be made explicitly Bayesian with a controllable prior that is swappable at inference without retraining.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single variable-length sequence of (x, y) tuples organized as K prior datasets (each of M pairs, separated by special ⟨prior⟩ tokens) followed by a target context and a final query input x marked by ⟨target⟩. At the query position, the model outputs the full posterior predictive distribution — a Gaussian (mean, variance) for regression or a Bernoulli probability for classification — rather than a point estimate. The prior datasets are the key control knob: swapping them at test time changes the induced prior and steers the predictive distribution without any parameter updates.

## Method

The core mechanism is to represent prior information as a prefix of in-context datasets: K auxiliary datasets drawn from distinct tasks sharing a common prior p(w|λ) are prepended to the target task context, separated by special tokens, forming a multi-task episode. A decoder-only GPT-2 with Rotary Position Embeddings is trained from scratch on hierarchically generated episodes (sample λ from a meta-distribution, then K+1 task parameters, then datasets), minimizing NLL only on target-task positions. This trains the transformer to implicitly perform hierarchical Bayesian inference — inferring the episode-level prior parameter λ from the prefix datasets and computing the target PPD conditioned on both. The key architectural insight is that the prior is exposed as a test-time interface: changing the prefix datasets modifies the induced prior and correspondingly shifts the posterior predictive distribution, enabling adaptation to out-of-meta-distribution priors, heavy-tailed Student-t priors, and high-dimensional flow-based priors without retraining, while matching oracle MCMC-HIER accuracy at orders-of-magnitude lower inference cost.

## Results

On synthetic linear and logistic regression tasks, multi-task ICL achieves near-zero KL divergence to the oracle PPD under in-meta-distribution (IMD) priors, matching MCMC-HIER across all context lengths. Under out-of-meta-distribution (OoMD) Student's t priors with heavy tails (log ν ≤ 0), multi-task ICL exhibits a threshold generalization pattern aligned with MCMC-HIER, degrading only when the test prior exceeds the training support. On flow-based priors with high-dimensional latents, multi-task ICL achieves KL of 0.262 versus 0.264 for MCMC-HIER (K=50) while being ~200,000× faster (6.24 ms vs. 1.30 s wall-clock). On the ERA5 spatiotemporal benchmark, MT with K=2 prior datasets improves NLL from −1.72 to −2.29 (validation) and from −2.02 to −2.33 (test) over K=0, and generalizes to 2020 (NLL −2.31).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MCMC-HIER (the asymptotically exact hierarchical Bayesian reference), multi-task ICL matches or slightly exceeds oracle KL under IMD priors for both linear and logistic regression, and shows superior robustness in low-data OoMD regimes where MCMC-HIER is constrained by its mis-specified prior. Against SVI-HIER, multi-task ICL is substantially better, especially under heavy-tailed Student's t priors: at μ=4 in logistic regression, SVI-HIER achieves KL of 126.57×10⁻³ while multi-task ICL achieves 1.81×10⁻³, a ~70× gap. On the flow-based prior benchmark, multi-task ICL (KL 0.262, 6.24 ms) matches MCMC-HIER (KL 0.264, 1.30 s) in accuracy while being ~200,000× faster, and outperforms SVI-HIER (KL 0.788) by a factor of ~3 in KL. ICL without the prior prefix consistently underperforms multi-task ICL, particularly under OoMD priors, confirming that the explicit prior prefix is the critical mechanism for test-time adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Prior-Data Fitted Network (PFN/TabPFN) line of work, which demonstrated that transformers can approximate Bayesian posterior predictive distributions but with the prior implicitly fixed in model weights. The key departure is introducing a multi-task in-context learning framework where prior information is explicitly represented as in-context dataset prefixes, transforming amortized inference from a single-prior mechanism into a hierarchical Bayesian inference engine with a direct test-time interface for prior adaptation. The approach also builds on the broader in-context learning paradigm (Brown et al., 2020) and amortized/meta-learning inference (Edwards & Storkey, 2017; Garnelo et al., 2018), but repositions the prior as a first-class, swappable input rather than a baked-in hyperparameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, retraining-free mechanism for adapting Bayesian predictors to new priors at test time, which is critical in settings where prior beliefs vary across users, domains, or environments. The demonstrated orders-of-magnitude speedup over MCMC while matching oracle accuracy makes hierarchical Bayesian inference feasible in latency-sensitive applications. However, limitations remain: the quadratic attention cost grows with the number of prior datasets, the ERA5 OOD results show degradation under severe seasonal shift, and length extrapolation beyond training K degrades performance. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models and more complex real-world prior structures, and whether explicit permutation invariance would further improve robustness.
