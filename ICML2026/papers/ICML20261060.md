## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models suffer from the 'Hubris of Semantics': their dense retrieval bias forces virtually any visual input into a known category with high confidence, making them fundamentally unsafe for open-world deployment. Existing OOD detection methods (MSP, Energy Scores, activation shaping) struggle in the high-dimensional latent spaces of foundation models due to the concentration of measure phenomenon, while pixel-space generative approaches face combinatorial explosion at ImageNet scale. The paper asks how to proactively define the boundary of the unknown in a way that is both computationally tractable and semantically informed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image, encodes it through a frozen CLIP visual encoder (ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14) into a unit-norm embedding, then passes it through a lightweight residual MLP adapter (d→d/4→d, ~0.5M parameters) that warps the local geometry. The output is a per-class acceptance probability derived from the Differential Immunity Score (DIS), which subtracts weighted antibody reactivity from prototype proximity, thresholded per-class via a fitted Weibull extreme-value model. Rejection is binary: the sample is either assigned to the predicted class or flagged as open-world (⊥).

## Method

Immuno-VLM is a three-phase bio-inspired pipeline that operationalizes Immunological Negative Selection in the semantic manifold of a frozen VLM. Phase 1 defines each class's 'Self' as a Von Mises-Fisher distribution on the hypersphere, with the prototype estimated via a geodesic constraint that interpolates between the visual mean and the text embedding (Theorem 3.4). Phase 2 uses an LLM (GPT-4) as a 'Computational Thymus' to hallucinate Semantic Antibodies—textual descriptions of hard near-OOD look-alikes and contextual anomalies—then applies a band-pass cosine-similarity filter to retain only antibodies that are neither synonyms nor irrelevant. Phase 3 trains the Rejection Adapter with a Push-Pull Vaccination Loss: a vMF log-likelihood Pull term preserves in-distribution clustering, while a margin-based Hinge Push term forces visual embeddings away from all retained antibody directions. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.5) proves that isotropic random negatives are asymptotically orthogonal in d≥512, justifying the shift from geometric to semantic sampling; Theorem 3.7 then bounds the true open-space risk by the antibody covering density and adapter complexity.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with a CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone, Immuno-VLM achieves 78.5% in-distribution accuracy while reaching 83.4% AUROC and 28.5% FPR95 on the adversarial ImageNet-O benchmark, substantially outperforming the best zero-shot baseline (MCM: 74.5% AUROC, 46.2% FPR95). Scaling to ViT-L/14 further improves performance to 88.7% AUROC and 19.2% FPR95 on ImageNet-O, with 94.3% AUROC on far-OOD Texture data. The method also improves in-distribution accuracy by 0.3% over the frozen CLIP baseline (78.2% → 78.5%), demonstrating that the vaccination process does not degrade closed-set recognition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Immuno-VLM outperforms all six baselines across every OOD benchmark and metric. On the most challenging Near-OOD benchmark (ImageNet-O), it achieves 83.4% AUROC, surpassing the strongest baseline MCM (74.5%) by 8.9 points and the simplest MSP (66.8%) by 16.6 points; its FPR95 of 28.5% is more than halved relative to MSP's 61.3%. On fine-grained iNaturalist, Immuno-VLM reaches 84.2% AUROC vs. MCM's 79.1%, and on far-OOD Texture it achieves 89.5% AUROC vs. MCM's 83.4%. Notably, the training-based baselines CSI (76.5% ID-ACC) and Neg-Label/Standard AIS (77.5% ID-ACC) both degrade in-distribution accuracy, whereas Immuno-VLM slightly improves it to 78.5%, confirming that the Rejection Adapter preserves closed-set performance. The gap is largest on adversarial Near-OOD samples, validating the paper's claim that semantic antibodies specifically target the 'Hubris of Semantics' failure mode that passive density-based methods (MSP, Energy) cannot address.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Open Set Recognition formulation of Scheirer et al. (2012) to the high-dimensional latent space of Vision-Language Models, and reinterprets the Artificial Immune System's Negative Selection principle (Forrest et al., 1994) in a semantic rather than pixel-space domain. It departs from passive OOD scoring methods (MSP, Energy, ReAct, ASH-B) and reactive text-modality approaches (MCM) by introducing a proactive 'vaccination' paradigm: an LLM generates near-distribution textual outliers ('Semantic Antibodies') that are projected into the shared embedding space to explicitly carve the decision boundary. The key conceptual shift is replacing isotropic random negative generation—which Theorem 3.5 proves is asymptotically useless in d≥512 spaces—with structured, LLM-hallucinated negatives that respect the semantic manifold geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine deployment safety gap: LVLMs confidently misclassify novel anomalies as known classes, a failure mode that is critical in autonomous driving and medical imaging. The practical footprint is attractive (0.5M adapter parameters, 3.5 GPU-hours training, <0.5% inference overhead), making it a viable drop-in for existing CLIP pipelines. Open questions include how the system's safety ceiling is bounded by the LLM's 'imagination' quality (Theorem 3.3 ties FPR to Wasserstein alignment of the generator), whether the approach transfers to non-ImageNet domains with sparser semantic structure, and whether the EVT-based per-class thresholding generalizes to open-vocabulary settings where the known-class set itself is dynamic.
