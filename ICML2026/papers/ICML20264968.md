## Problem

Existing auto-bidding algorithms in search advertising treat the value of an ad slot as the revenue from winning (e.g., click or conversion), ignoring that the advertiser may still earn revenue through organic search results even when losing the auction. The true value is the marginal gain—the treatment effect between winning and losing—which is never directly observed. This paper extends the treatment-effect bidding framework from first-price auctions to second-price (Vickrey) auctions, where the payment rule reveals the highest other bid (HOB) only upon winning, creating an asymmetric information structure that fundamentally improves the regret rate from T^{2/3} to √(dT) under binary feedback.

## Input / Output

At each auction round t, the bidder observes a context vector x_t ∈ R^d (‖x_t‖₂ ≤ 1) and submits a bid b_t from a discretized grid of √T levels in [0,1]. The feedback is asymmetric: if the bidder wins (b_t ≥ m_t), she observes the winning outcome v_{t,1} and the HOB m_t via the second-price payment; if she loses, she only observes the baseline outcome v_{t,0} and the win/loss indicator. The algorithm also requires known HOB distribution parameters (ω, λ) characterizing a locally-bounded CDF, and produces both the bid b_t and an estimated treatment effect θ̂_t^T x_t at each round.

## Method

The method models the ad value as a linear treatment effect E[Δv_t] = θ_*^T x_t and estimates it via inverse-propensity-weighting (IPW) using the estimated HOB CDF as the propensity score. A key insight is that the IPW estimation error is generally unbounded (no overlap condition is required), but this is neutralized by a 'better of two UCBs' decision step: two reward formulations with different dependence on the estimated value are constructed, and the one with the smaller confidence width is selected, ensuring the regret scales with min{Ĝ_t(b), 1−Ĝ_t(b)}^{-1} which vanishes precisely when the value estimation is worst. The HOB distribution is learned via interval splitting that exploits the one-sided feedback from the second-price payment (higher bids reveal information about lower bids), combined with a hierarchical level-based algorithm to maintain conditional independence. The resulting regret is Õ(√(dT)) under binary feedback, matching a minimax lower bound and strictly improving over the Õ_d(T^{2/3}) rate for first-price auctions.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2.5) establishes that under binary feedback in repeated SPAs, the proposed algorithm achieves regret R(π) = O(√(dT) log³T + d log⁵T), which is tight up to poly-logarithmic factors by the matching lower bound R* = Ω(√(dT)) (Theorem 2.6) that holds even when the HOB CDF G is perfectly known. This yields a complete minimax characterization of Õ(√(dT)) regret for both full-information and binary feedback (Table 1). Empirically, on a synthetic environment with nonzero periodic baseline outcomes, Algorithm 1† converges at the desired O(d√T) rate, while the LinUCB baseline suffers linear regret due to consistent overbidding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary empirical baseline is LinUCB (Li et al., 2010), the standard linear contextual bandit algorithm that regresses only on winning outcomes as done in current industry practice. In the synthetic experiment with nonzero periodic baseline outcomes v_{t,0}, LinUCB consistently overbids and suffers linear regret, while Algorithm 1† successfully identifies the causal (treatment) ad value and converges at the desired O(d√T) rate. The key theoretical comparison is with first-price auctions (Wen et al., 2025a), where the optimal regret under binary feedback scales as Õ_d(T^{2/3}); the SPA setting achieves the fundamentally better Õ(√(dT)) rate by exploiting the asymmetric information revealed by the second-price payment rule (the winner observes the HOB). This improvement from T^{2/3} to √T under binary feedback is the central contribution distinguishing SPAs from FPAs in this causal bidding framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the joint treatment-effect estimation and bidding framework of Wen et al. (2025a) from first-price auctions (FPAs) to second-price auctions (SPAs), and builds on the interval-splitting HOB estimation technique of Han et al. (2025). The key conceptual departure is exploiting the asymmetric information in SPAs—where the winner observes the HOB via the payment rule—which the authors show fundamentally improves the regret rate under binary feedback from T^{2/3} (FPAs) to √(dT). They also generalize the HOB estimation condition to accommodate arbitrary error bounds rather than requiring the Bernstein-type structure needed in the FPA analysis, and relax the propensity score estimation to allow arbitrary estimation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a complete minimax-optimal regret characterization for SPAs under both full-information and binary feedback, clarifying when and why online causal inference is intrinsically easier in SPAs than in FPAs. The practical algorithm (Algorithm 1†) demonstrates that standard LinUCB-style methods that ignore baseline (organic) outcomes systematically overbid, highlighting a real gap in current industry practice for search ads. Open questions include extending to non-i.i.d. (contextual) HOBs, whether more delicate IPW designs can eliminate the need for random exploration, and whether the asymmetric-information advantage generalizes to other auction formats or multi-slot settings.
