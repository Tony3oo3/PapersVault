## Problem

Existing sub-model extraction methods for heterogeneous federated learning rely on stochastic or rule-based neuron selection, which causes parameter conflicts under non-IID data and insufficient optimization across devices with varying hardware resources. The key gap is the absence of a principled mechanism that simultaneously preserves global model generalization (through shared parameters) and alleviates gradient conflicts from data heterogeneity (through data-aware neuron selection).

## Input / Output

Each client provides local RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and EMNIST, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) along with its hardware resource profile (determining whether it can train 30%, 60%, or 100% of the global model) and uploads per-neuron activation statistics from the previous round. The server produces a device-specific sub-model per round (backbone + selected dynamic neurons) and, after T rounds, a single trained global model whose parameters are aggregated via a presence-aware weighted average that only averages neurons actually present in each contributing sub-model.

## Method

SpineFL decomposes every layer of the global model into a mandatory backbone (shared by all sub-models, with a higher retention ratio in shallow layers based on the observation that pruning deep layers causes less degradation) and a dynamic portion subject to pruning. The backbone size is assigned per device according to its hardware resources, with smaller backbones nested within larger ones. For the dynamic portion, SpineFL computes a per-neuron importance score as the mean absolute activation over spatial positions from the previous round, then selects neurons probabilistically via a softmax over scores with a temperature parameter T, so that highly activated neurons are more likely to be retained while preserving selection diversity. This activation-guided probabilistic selection avoids the over-specialization of deterministic top-K or device-specific assignment strategies. A convergence analysis under standard smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions shows the expected gradient norm decreases at rate O(1/T) with an additive term proportional to (1−r), where r is the neuron retention ratio.

## Results

SpineFL achieves the highest test accuracy across all 36 experimental configurations (3 architectures × 4 datasets × 3 data distributions). On the most challenging Non-IID setting (α=0.3) with ResNet-34, SpineFL reaches 78.30% on CIFAR-10, 43.46% on CIFAR-100, 98.14% on EMNIST, and 28.59% on TinyImageNet, outperforming the strongest baseline FedDSE by 1.66%, 2.83%, 2.08%, and 3.09% respectively. Under IID settings, SpineFL achieves 86.17% (CIFAR-10), 50.31% (CIFAR-100), 99.51% (EMNIST), and 36.51% (TinyImageNet) with ResNet-34. The paper reports up to 3.28% accuracy improvement over state-of-the-art heterogeneous FL methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpineFL outperforms all 8 baselines across every dataset, distribution, and architecture tested. The closest competitor is FedDSE (distribution-aware sub-model extraction), which SpineFL surpasses by 1.3–3.1% under Non-IID α=0.3 with ResNet-34 (e.g., 78.30% vs. 76.64% on CIFAR-10; 43.46% vs. 40.63% on CIFAR-100). FIASRE and FlexFL are the next strongest baselines, trailing SpineFL by roughly 1–2% in most settings. Classical methods like HeteroFL and Fjord lag significantly behind, with gaps exceeding 10% on CIFAR-100 (α=0.3) and TinyImageNet. The advantage of SpineFL is most pronounced under high data heterogeneity (α=0.3), where gradient conflicts are most severe, confirming that the backbone-sharing and activation-guided selection mechanisms specifically address the non-IID challenge. Results are consistent across ResNet-34, CNN, and VGG-16 architectures, with VGG-16 showing the largest absolute gains (e.g., 84.34% vs. 78.69% for FedDSE on CIFAR-10, α=0.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

SpineFL extends the sub-model extraction paradigm for heterogeneous FL (HeteroFL, FedRolex, FedDSE, ScaleFL, FlexFL) by introducing two complementary mechanisms: a layer-wise backbone-sharing strategy that ensures all sub-models retain a common parameter subset for generalization, and an activation-guided probabilistic neuron selection that replaces the stochastic or rotation-based selection used by prior methods. The backbone design draws on the observation (Li et al., 2017; Jia et al., 2024) that pruning deep layers is less harmful than pruning shallow ones, while the activation-guided selection directly addresses the gradient-conflict problem under non-IID data that plagues existing extraction strategies. Unlike completely heterogeneous methods (FedMD, FedProto) that require public proxy datasets, SpineFL operates within the parameter-compatible sub-model framework, making it more deployable in privacy-sensitive AIoT settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpineFL offers a practical, drop-in improvement for heterogeneous FL deployments in AIoT systems where devices vary in compute capacity and data distributions are non-IID. The consistent 1–3% accuracy gains across four datasets, three architectures, and multiple Dirichlet α settings suggest the backbone-sharing + activation-guided combination is robust rather than dataset-specific. However, the method assumes all clients share the same global model architecture (e.g., all CNNs), limiting applicability to truly heterogeneous model families (CNN vs. Transformer), and the reliance on historical activation statistics may underperform in highly dynamic environments where activation patterns shift rapidly. Open questions include whether the approach extends to depth-wise or mixed width-depth pruning, and whether the probabilistic selection can be made adaptive to per-round data drift without additional communication overhead.
