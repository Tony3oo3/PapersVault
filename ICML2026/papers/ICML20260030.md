## Problem

Online influence maximization under the independent cascade model with node-level feedback (where the learner observes only which nodes activate, not which edges caused activation) has previously been solved only via a 'pair oracle' that jointly optimizes over seed sets and feasible edge probabilities — an abstraction with no known efficient implementation. This paper asks whether sublinear regret can be achieved using only a standard offline influence maximization oracle, and answers affirmatively.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a directed graph G=(V,E), a seed budget k, and per-round node-level cascade feedback (the sequence of active node sets S_{t,0}, S_{t,1}, …, S_{t,n-1} revealing which nodes activate at which diffusion steps but not the triggering edges). It produces a seed set of size at most k each round, along with per-node parameter vectors θ_v and optimistic set-level activation probabilities P̂_{S,v} computed on-the-fly via the relation 1−exp(−1_S^T θ_v), avoiding explicit enumeration of the exponentially many neighborhoods. The offline oracle is invoked with these implicit parameters rather than a full probability table.

## Method

NODE-GLB overcomes the partial identifiability of edge-level probabilities under node-level feedback by estimating set-level aggregate parameters θ_v per node, where the activation probability given active neighborhood S is P_{S,v}=1−exp(−1_S^T θ_v), inducing a generalized linear model with link function μ(z)=1−e^{−z}. Parameters are estimated via regularized quasi-likelihood minimization over the convex set Θ_v, and separate upper confidence bounds are constructed for each set-level probability using the self-normalized martingale concentration bound on the Gram matrix M_{v,t−1}. A standard α-approximate offline oracle (e.g., greedy) then selects optimistic seed sets from these UCBs. The key analytical contribution is a pay-per-use stability lemma that bounds the influence-spread gap by n·Σ_v Σ_S Q_{S,v}(S_0)·ε_{S,v}, where Q_{S,v} is the probability that neighborhood S actually arises during diffusion, enabling a pathwise elliptical-potential argument that yields Õ(γ^{−1} n√T Σ_v d_v^{3/2}) regret without any burn-in phase.

## Results

NODE-GLB achieves consistently low average cumulative regret R(t)/t across all four networks (Epinions, Flixter, Barabási-Albert, Decoy Hub), with regret decreasing steadily after an initial exploration phase—consistent with the theoretical Õ(√T) bound. Observed γ values are substantially larger than worst-case lower bounds (e.g., 6.48×10⁻³ vs. 1.1×10⁻⁵ on Epinions; 1.46×10⁻¹ vs. 1.88×10⁻³ on BA), indicating the worst-case analysis is conservative in practice. Per-round wall-clock time ranges from 0.19 s (Decoy Hub, BA) to 2.52 s (Epinions) on a single Apple M3 core, confirming polynomial-time feasibility. On the Decoy Hub network, NODE-GLB initially suffers from large UCBs that over-weight high-degree decoy nodes, but regret improves significantly once confidence intervals shrink.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NODE-GLB outperforms all four baselines (RANDOM, HIGH-DEGREE, PE-NLF, MSE3) on every dataset. Against RANDOM, the gap is large and persistent: RANDOM's average regret remains flat and high throughout, while NODE-GLB's regret decreases monotonically after the initial exploration phase. Against HIGH-DEGREE, NODE-GLB wins on all networks; the gap is most pronounced on Decoy Hub graphs, where HIGH-DEGREE is misled by high-degree decoy nodes with limited actual influence and its regret curve stays essentially flat (it is non-adaptive). Against PE-NLF (the exploitation-only baseline from Vaswani et al., 2015), NODE-GLB consistently achieves lower regret; the paper attributes PE-NLF's underperformance to its lack of principled exploration and 'random credit assignment,' which causes premature commitment to suboptimal seed sets. Against MSE3 (the sum-max submodular bandit algorithm of Pasteris et al., 2024 applied via live-edge reduction), NODE-GLB substantially outperforms: MSE3's theoretically prescribed learning rate η = Θ(1/√T) is on the order of 10⁻⁴ for the (n, k, T) settings used, so its simplex distribution barely moves from uniform within T rounds and it behaves essentially like RANDOM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online influence maximization (OIM) line of work under the independent cascade model with node-level feedback, most directly building on Zhang et al. (2022), who achieved Õ(√T) regret but required a powerful pair oracle that jointly optimizes over seed sets and feasible edge-probability parameters. The key departure is replacing this pair oracle with a standard α-approximate offline oracle (e.g., greedy), made possible by estimating set-level activation probabilities via generalized linear bandits (Filippi et al., 2010) and constructing separate UCBs per neighborhood rather than a joint confidence ball. The pay-per-use stability lemma is adapted from Agarwal et al. (2024) on semi-bandit learning for monotone stochastic optimization, and regularization is introduced to eliminate the costly burn-in phase required by prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a concrete open question in the OIM literature: whether sublinear regret is achievable under node-level feedback with only a standard offline oracle, which is far more practical than the pair oracle whose efficient implementation was unknown. The improved dependence on network size (n·Σd_v^{3/2} vs. n^{5/2}D) and the elimination of the burn-in phase make the guarantee more applicable to dense real-world networks. Open questions include scaling experiments to networks with millions of nodes, extending to weaker feedback models (e.g., observing only the final activated set), and generalizing beyond the independent cascade model to linear threshold or other diffusion processes.
