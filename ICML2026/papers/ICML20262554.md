## Problem

CLIP-based NR-IQA methods treat pretrained embeddings as monolithic feature pools, but CLIP's contrastive pretraining explicitly encourages semantic invariance, causing subtle distortion-induced perceptual signals to be suppressed—a phenomenon the authors term 'perceptual submergence.' Standard preprocessing (grid cropping, bicubic interpolation) further exacerbates this by indiscriminately discarding high-frequency quality cues. The paper asks whether perceptual quality signals can be explicitly disentangled from the dominant semantic manifold and whether input fidelity can be preserved during mandatory resolution adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image at its original (arbitrary) resolution and two quality text prompts ('Low quality image', 'High quality image') as input. The RPD preprocessor adaptively downsamples the image to 224×224 (CLIP ViT-B/32's fixed input) using a random crop ratio of ~0.6 relative to the shortest edge, with patches smaller than 224 up-interpolated. The output is a scalar quality score computed as the cosine similarity between the perception-aligned visual global embedding and the textual quality anchors. Only 1.8M parameters are trainable (<2% of the 151.5M CLIP backbone), making it a parameter-efficient fine-tuning approach.

## Method

The framework comprises two synergistic modules inserted into a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone. The Cross-modal Perception Alignment Adapter (CMPA) is applied layer-wise across transformer blocks: its Perception-Sensitive Feature Extractor (PFE) projects both visual and textual features into a compact low-dimensional bottleneck (d=48 optimal) via a shared MLP, filtering out semantic redundancies to expose distortion-induced off-manifold deviations; its Cross-Modal Perception Alignment Injector (PAI) then performs dual-path multi-head cross-attention in this subspace—visual features query text anchors and vice versa—before re-injecting the aligned residuals back into the original D-dimensional space via a residual connection. The Residual-enhanced Perceptual Downscaling (RPD) preprocessor decomposes the input into low- and high-frequency components via Gaussian filtering, extracts sampling residuals through a down-up cycle (RED operator), and adaptively re-injects them using a JND-guided gain map derived from luminance adaptation and visual masking, with injection strength modulated by a Weber-Fechner logarithmic term that scales sub-linearly with the downsampling ratio. Training uses PLCC loss with AdamW (lr=1e-3, weight decay 0.01, batch 128) on a single RTX 4090.

## Results

The full framework (Ours with RPD) achieves SRCC of 0.983 on LIVE, 0.938 on KADID-10k, 0.938 on KonIQ-10k, and 0.929 on SPAQ, consistently ranking first or second across all eight benchmark datasets. The method uses only 1.8M trainable parameters (less than 2% of CLIP ViT-B/32's 151.5M total), far fewer than competing CLIP-based methods like LIQE (151M) or TReS (152M). On the challenging in-the-wild FLIVE dataset, the method achieves SRCC 0.587 / PLCC 0.689, though GRMP-IQA remains slightly ahead (0.616/0.704). Cross-dataset generalization (Table 2) shows the method outperforms LoDa by 5–6 SRCC points (e.g., 0.825 vs. 0.763 on KonIQ→FLIVE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (1.8M params) outperforms all CLIP-based competitors—LIQE (151M), GRMP-IQA, and LoDa (9M)—on 7 out of 8 datasets in SRCC, using an order of magnitude fewer trainable parameters. Against the strongest non-CLIP baselines, it surpasses DEIQT (24M) by 4–5 SRCC points on KADID-10k (0.938 vs. 0.889) and KonIQ-10k (0.938 vs. 0.921), and exceeds QMamba (50M) on KADID-10k (0.938 vs. 0.923) and LIVEC (0.903 vs. 0.863). The sole notable exception is FLIVE, where GRMP-IQA leads by ~3 SRCC points (0.616 vs. 0.587), likely due to FLIVE's extreme diversity in content and aspect ratios. On TID2013, QMamba edges out the proposed method on PLCC (0.913 vs. 0.911) while the proposed method wins on SRCC (0.899 vs. 0.896). In cross-dataset transfer (Table 2), the method beats LoDa by 5–6 SRCC points (e.g., 0.825 vs. 0.763 KonIQ→FLIVE), and in data-efficient settings (Table 3), it surpasses LoDa by 1–3 SRCC points even at 20% training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CLIP-based NR-IQA by introducing the concept of 'perceptual submergence'—the idea that CLIP's contrastive pretraining suppresses distortion signals in favor of semantic invariance—and addresses it through a manifold-aware adapter (CMPA) that projects features into a compact low-dimensional perceptual subspace before cross-modal alignment. It departs from generic PEFT methods (LoRA, Adapter-CLIP) that use low-rank subspaces purely as optimization tools, instead treating the subspace as a perceptual filter that isolates quality-relevant deviations. The RPD preprocessor repurposes classical JND theory (Chou & Li, 1995; Yang et al., 2005) and the Weber-Fechner law into a residual-enhanced downsampling strategy, building on the observation from LoDa (Xu et al., 2024) that IQA performance peaks at remarkably low intrinsic dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.8M-parameter adapter achieving SOTA across 8 benchmarks offers a practical, parameter-efficient path for adapting VLMs to fine-grained perceptual tasks, and the plug-and-play RPD module can benefit any IQA pipeline without retraining. The RSA evidence (0.033 → 0.562 correlation with human perception) and the ALIGN negative result (weaker gains on noisy-pretraining backbones) provide meaningful support for the manifold hypothesis framing. Open questions include whether the optimal subspace dimension is truly universal or dataset-dependent, whether RPD generalizes to deep-learning-based resizers, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D quality assessment where temporal perceptual submergence may differ.
