## Problem

Existing dataset distillation theories are confined to paradigm-specific assumptions (first-order gradient matching, kernel statistics, or heuristic trajectory approximations), leaving it unexplained why gradient, distribution, and trajectory matching all achieve near full-data utility. No quantitative boundary characterizes how distilled data degrade under configuration shifts (optimizer, architecture, augmentation), and the empirically observed IPC saturation lacks a principled explanation.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a real training dataset D_τ of n samples and a family of target configurations A (each specifying optimizer, architecture depth/width/activation/normalization, and augmentation) as input, and produces a compact synthetic dataset D_s of k prototypes (k ≪ n) along with provable generalization error bounds. The key output is a utility boundary: the minimum k required to maintain a target error ε₀ across the configuration family, which scales as Θ(H_cov(A,r)) where H_cov is the log covering number under an update-induced configuration distance. In experiments, configurations are instantiated over a ConvNet design space with depth ∈ {1,2,3,4}, width ∈ {32,64,128,256}, four activations, three normalizations, SGD/Adam, and DSA on/off.

## Method

The paper introduces a Configuration-Dynamics-Error (CDE) framework that treats each training configuration as an update operator Φ_a(θ;μ) = θ − ηP_a(θ)E_μ[g_a(θ;z)] and measures distillation quality by the test-risk gap between models trained on distilled versus full data. A matching discrepancy Δ_a(μ,ν) = sup_θ ‖P_a(θ)(E_μ g_a − E_ν g_a)‖ unifies GM, DM, and TM as exchangeable surrogates (Lemma 6.1 shows their bounds are equivalent up to constants). Under a trajectory-local contraction assumption (weaker than global PL), the single-configuration bound yields a scaling law: the risk gap decays as O(1/√k) before saturating at an irreducible floor ηL_R C_{2,a}^{traj} Δ_a^⋆, explaining IPC saturation. Extending to a configuration family via a covering-number argument produces an order-tight coverage law: the required k scales as Θ(H_cov(A,r)), with matching upper and lower bounds (Theorems 5.2–5.3). The practical coverage entropy is estimated from one-step update vectors using a farthest-first greedy r-cover, enabling data-driven selection of distilled set size and source configurations.

## Results

The paper validates two theoretical laws across six datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, ImageNette, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet-1K) and four distillation families. For the single-configuration scaling law (Δ vs. 1/√k), DC/DSA achieve near-perfect linearity (R² up to 0.97 on CIFAR-10), while DM and MTT show more deviation on CIFAR-100 (R² ≈ 0.84 and 0.73 respectively). For the cross-configuration coverage law (Δ vs. √(H_cov/k)), the linear fit reaches R² up to 0.99 on MNIST and 0.90 on CIFAR-10, degrading to R² ≈ 0.3 on CIFAR-100 for some methods. Out-of-sample predictive validation yields a scaling-law MAE of 0.0214, with coverage predictions systematically conservative (risk-aware lower bound).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new distillation method but validates its theoretical scaling and coverage laws across nine baselines (DC, DSA, DM, MTT, MGD³, TESLA, NCFM, DATM, SRe2L). DC and DSA (gradient matching) consistently show the strongest conformity to both laws: near-linear R² ≈ 0.97 on CIFAR-10 for scaling and R² ≳ 0.7 under coverage shifts on CIFAR-10/100. DM (distribution matching) is intermediate, with R² ≈ 0.84 on CIFAR-100 scaling but weaker coverage fits on harder datasets. MTT (trajectory matching) is steeper but less stable (R² ≈ 0.73 on CIFAR-100 scaling), reflecting variance from trajectory unrolling. For the sparse-grid baselines (TESLA, NCFM, DATM, SRe2L), extracted IPC points remain strictly monotone, confirming the 1/√k law is not tied to a specific matching mechanism. MGD³ (diffusion-based) extends validation to ImageNette, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet-1K with R² ≈ 0.87–0.92 across ConvNet/ResNet backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new theoretical ground for dataset distillation by introducing a configuration-dynamics-error (CDE) framework that unifies gradient matching (DC, DSA), distribution matching (DM, MMD-based), and trajectory matching (MTT) as instances of a single matching-discrepancy minimization under a bi-level optimization template. It departs from prior paradigm-specific analyses (first-order GM theory, kernel-based DM theory, heuristic TM guarantees) by treating training configurations as update operators and measuring distillation quality through the induced optimization dynamics rather than any single surrogate. The covering-number and Rademacher-complexity machinery is drawn from standard generalization theory (Bartlett & Mendelson, Hardt et al., Russo & Zou) but is repurposed to quantify a new object: the configuration diversity that a distilled dataset must cover to remain useful across deployment settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two predictive laws—scaling (O(1/√k) + irreducible floor) and coverage (k ∝ H_cov)—provide the first formal utility boundary for dataset distillation, explaining the long-observed IPC saturation and giving practitioners a principled rule for how many synthetic samples are needed under configuration shift. The exchangeability lemma showing DM/GM/TM surrogates bound the same alignment discrepancy up to constants reframes method selection as a compute-vs-robustness tradeoff rather than a paradigm choice. Open questions include extending the ConvNet-based configuration family to transformers and generative-prior methods, systematically comparing coverage proxies, and tightening the information-theoretic penalty when distilled data depend on real data.
