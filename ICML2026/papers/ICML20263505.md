## Problem

Prior work on online inverse linear optimization (contextual recommendation) established regret bounds of O(d log T) or a finite but exponentially large exp(O(d log d)), leaving open whether a finite regret bound polynomial in the dimension d is achievable. This paper resolves the question partially by showing that when the agent's feasible sets are M-convex—a broad class subsuming matroids—a finite O(d log d) regret bound is possible, and extends the result to adversarially corrupted feedback.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the learner receives the M-convex feasible set X_t ⊆ ℤ^d and observes the agent's action x_t ∈ X_t (which is optimal for the hidden objective w* unless corrupted). The learner must output a recommended action x̂_t ∈ X_t computed from an estimated objective vector ŵ_t derived from all past (X_s, x_s) pairs. The key structural constraint is that X_t is M-convex, which restricts the feasible sets to combinatorial structures with an exchange property (e.g., matroid bases, m-sets, integer-lattice extensions), and the regret guarantee is finite and independent of the time horizon T.

## Method

The algorithm maintains a directed graph ([d], A_t) whose arcs encode pairwise orderings w*(i) > w*(j) implied by observed optimal actions via the M-convex optimality characterization (Proposition 2.4: x is optimal for w iff no feasible local exchange improves the objective). The estimate ŵ_t is chosen as the center of gravity of the polytope P_t = {w ∈ [0,1]^d : w(i) ≥ w(j) for all (i,j) ∈ A_t}, computed approximately via hit-and-run sampling. A volume argument using Grünbaum's theorem shows that each round where x_t ≠ x̂_t shrinks Vol(P_t) by a constant factor (1 − 1/e), and since P_T must contain at least one order simplex of volume 1/d!, the total number of mistakes is O(d log d). For corruption robustness, the algorithm monitors the acyclicity of ([d], A_t): a directed cycle certifies at least one corrupted round (Lemma 5.1), triggering a restart that resets A_t to empty, yielding O((C+1) d log d) regret without knowing C in advance.

## Results

The paper establishes a finite regret bound of O(d log d) for online inverse linear optimization under M-convex action sets, improving upon the prior O(d log T) bound, and extends this to O((C+1) d log d) under up to C adversarial corruptions without prior knowledge of C. A matching Ω(d) lower bound is shown for the M-convex case, making the upper bound tight up to a log d factor. In a small empirical validation on time-varying uniform matroids (Appendix C, Table 2), the O(d log d) algorithm with N=10⁴ hit-and-run iterations achieves regret 1.201±0.382, substantially outperforming both baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines on time-varying uniform matroids: ONS (Sakaue et al., 2025b) and OGD (Bärmann et al., 2020). All variants of the proposed Algorithm 1 achieve lower regret than both baselines. The O(d log d) version with N=10⁴ hit-and-run iterations attains regret 1.201±0.382, roughly 2.9× lower than ONS (3.510±0.776) and 4.0× lower than OGD (4.848±1.105). The simpler O(d²) version (topological sort) also beats both baselines (1.640±0.339) while running in just 11 μs per round, compared to 70 μs for ONS and 4 μs for OGD. The O(d log d) version with fewer iterations (N=10²) is competitive with the O(d²) version in regret but slightly slower, suggesting that the center-of-gravity approximation quality matters for the volume argument to fully materialize in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on online inverse linear optimization (contextual recommendation) initiated by Bärmann et al. (2017, 2020) and advanced by Besbes et al. (2021, 2025), Gollapudi et al. (2021), and Sakaue et al. (2025b), which established O(d log T) regret bounds but left open whether a finite bound polynomial in d is achievable. The key departure is restricting action sets to M-convex sets (a broad class subsuming matroids from discrete convex analysis) and combining the structural characterization of optimal solutions on such sets (Murota's exchange property) with a Grünbaum volume argument borrowed from the contextual search/pricing literature. For corruption robustness, the paper departs from the parallel-copies approach of Gupta et al. (2025) by instead monitoring directed graph acyclicity to detect corruptions and restarting, yielding a similar O(C) multiplicative degradation through a different mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(d log d) finite regret bound (independent of T) partially resolves a central open question in the field and is tight up to a log d factor given the Ω(d) lower bound, making it a clean theoretical milestone for combinatorial action sets with M-convex structure. The corruption-robust extension without prior knowledge of C is practically relevant for settings where agent feedback may be adversarially perturbed. Open questions include closing the remaining log d gap, extending to the more general cumulative-suboptimality corruption framework of Sakaue et al. (2025b), and connecting the M-convexity-based approach to inverse bandit and inverse reinforcement learning settings where observed actions may be generated by online learners rather than being exact optimizers.
