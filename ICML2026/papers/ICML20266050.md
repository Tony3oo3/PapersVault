## Problem

RL-finetuned vision-language models show strong gains on visual reasoning benchmarks, yet their practical robustness to simple textual perturbations—misleading captions or conflicting chain-of-thought prefixes—remains uncharacterized. This paper asks whether these gains reflect genuine visual grounding or over-reliance on language priors, and whether the RL finetuning process itself introduces a systematic accuracy-faithfulness trade-off that standard evaluations miss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266050/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample pairs an RGB image with a multiple-choice spatial reasoning question, optionally augmented with a procedurally generated misleading caption (Wrong-Caption) or a seeded incorrect CoT prefix (Wrong-Think), plus variants with corrective disclaimers. The model produces a chain-of-thought trace in <think> tags followed by a letter answer in <answer> tags; performance is scored on both top-1 accuracy and faithfulness, where faithfulness is an LLM-judged (Qwen3-32B) binary consistency check between the final reasoning conclusion and the emitted answer. Entropy-based diagnostics (letter entropy and P(correct letter)) are additionally computed over the answer-token distribution to probe calibration.

## Method

The paper introduces a controlled stress-testing protocol with three perturbation families—Stop-Think (suppressing CoT via an uninformative tag), Wrong-Think (seeding a misleading reasoning prefix), and Wrong-Caption (prepending a false descriptive caption)—applied to eight spatial and general VQA benchmarks. Open-source RL-finetuned models (SpaceR, Video-R1, Vision-R1, VLAA-Thinker, ViGoRL-Spatial, all from Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) are compared against closed-source models (o3, o4-mini, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Gemini-3.1-Pro) under identical perturbations. The core experimental contribution is a series of controlled GRPO finetuning runs on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct with three data compositions (SAT2+Pixmo-Count, +Geometry3K, +adversarial caption/thinking augmentation at 10% each), tracking accuracy and faithfulness across intermediate checkpoints to expose the accuracy-faithfulness trade-off. A faithfulness-aware reward variant is also tested, revealing that combining it with augmentation causes training to collapse onto shortcut strategies (e.g., always trusting correct captions) rather than learning to discriminate valid from invalid contextual signals.

## Results

Across five spatial-reasoning benchmarks (3DSRBench, CV-Bench, Spatial-MM Obj, Spatial-MM Multihop, WhatsUp), RL-finetuned open-source models (SpaceR, Video-R1, Vision-R1, VLAA-Thinker, ViGoRL-Spatial) achieve base accuracies ranging from ~50% to ~97%, with ViGoRL-Spatial leading on CV-Bench (82.29%) and WhatsUp (94.43%). Under Wrong-Caption and Wrong-Think perturbations, all open-source models suffer substantial accuracy drops (e.g., Vision-R1 drops from 54.22% to 53.67% on 3DSRBench under Stop, and further under misleading text), while closed-source models (o3, o4-mini, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Gemini-3.1-Pro) show markedly smaller degradation. Faithfulness analysis reveals that even when open-source models answer correctly under misleading context, a large fraction of their CoT traces are inconsistent with the final answer, whereas closed models maintain substantially higher reasoning-answer consistency. RL finetuning experiments further show that accuracy improves over training steps while faithfulness systematically declines, exposing an accuracy–faithfulness trade-off that data augmentation alone cannot resolve.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new model but systematically compares six open-source VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL base, SpaceR, Video-R1, Vision-R1, VLAA-Thinker, ViGoRL-Spatial) against four closed-source models (o3, o4-mini, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Gemini-3.1-Pro) under controlled textual perturbations. Among open-source models, ViGoRL-Spatial and SpaceR are the most robust to misleading captions and thinking prefixes, with ViGoRL-Spatial achieving the highest CV-Bench accuracy (82.29% Base, 83.81% Stop) and minimal faithfulness degradation; Video-R1 and Vision-R1 show the largest sensitivity to Stop-Think prompting (entropy increases of +2.79 to +4.50 nats) and the sharpest accuracy drops under Wrong-Think. VLAA-Thinker, despite being explicitly trained with structured reasoning, exhibits minimal Stop-Think sensitivity but the highest proportion of unfaithful generations even under clean Base conditions. Closed-source models, particularly Gemini-3.1-Pro, demonstrate substantially greater robustness (smaller accuracy drops under Wrong-Caption/Wrong-Think) and markedly higher CoT faithfulness, often explicitly detecting and acknowledging misleading context before self-correcting. The key finding is that the open–closed gap in robustness and faithfulness reflects a shortcoming of current RL finetuning methods rather than an inherent task limitation, since the same base model (Qwen2.5-VL) underlies all open-source variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active threads: (1) RL-based post-training for VLMs (GRPO/RLVR applied to spatial and visual reasoning, as in SpaceR, Video-R1, Vision-R1, VLAA-Thinker, and ViGoRL-Spatial) and (2) the growing body of work questioning CoT faithfulness in LLMs (Turpin et al., Lanham et al., Chen et al. 2025c). It departs from prior robustness analyses of VLMs (Liu et al. 2026a, PerturboLLaVA) by focusing on subtle, targeted textual perturbations—misleading captions and conflicting CoT prefixes—rather than visual distortions, and by explicitly tracing how these vulnerabilities emerge and evolve during RL finetuning. The paper positions itself as orthogonal to new RL recipes: rather than proposing another training method, it uses controlled perturbations and entropy-based diagnostics to reveal side effects of standard verifiable-reward RL that accuracy-only benchmarks obscure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that RL finetuning raises benchmark accuracy while simultaneously eroding CoT faithfulness and robustness to simple textual context—challenges the assumption that verifiable-reward RL is a safe, monotonic improvement for multimodal reasoning. The negative result that combining faithfulness-as-reward with adversarial augmentation collapses onto shortcut strategies (models learn to follow correct captions rather than genuinely discriminate) is a concrete warning for practitioners designing multi-objective RL pipelines. Open questions include whether stronger base-model perceptual grounding, richer reward signals beyond correctness, or uncertainty-aware training can break the accuracy-faithfulness tradeoff, and whether CoT is the right representational vehicle for RL-optimized visual reasoning at all. The perturbation protocol itself (Wrong-Caption, Wrong-Think, Stop-Think) is a lightweight, reproducible stress test that could be adopted as a standard robustness check alongside clean-benchmark accuracy.
