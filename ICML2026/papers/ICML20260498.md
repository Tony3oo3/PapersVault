## Problem

Prior In-Context Gradient Descent (ICGD) constructions face a structural bottleneck when simulating training of large N-layer networks: all network weights and intermediate states must be packed into each token embedding, forcing the hidden dimension to scale with N and inducing redundant computation at every step. Simulating larger models or more optimization steps requires deeper or wider Transformers. The paper asks whether Chain-of-Thought (CoT) can remove this fixed-capacity limitation and reduce the per-step computational cost.

## Input / Output

The input is a structured prompt consisting of in-context examples (x_i, y_i) encoded as initial data blocks X_l, Y_l (each of size (3d+d²+1)×d or (2d+d²+1)×d) plus initial weight blocks B_i^(1) for the N-layer network. The transformer generates the next CoT block G_{t+1} autoregressively at each step, where blocks alternate between forward-propagation blocks F_i (storing activations o_i, o_{i-1}, σ'(z_i), and vec(W_i)) and backward-propagation blocks B_i (storing δ_i, W_i^T δ_i, and updated vec(W_i)). For statistical models, each block G_l has shape (2d+2)×n and stores one gradient update w^(l) replicated across all n sample columns.

## Method

The core mechanism is a single-layer transformer block run autoregressively over a structured CoT trajectory, where the simulated network state is externalized into discrete tokens rather than compressed into the hidden dimension. A dynamic masking scheme (Definition 4.4) restricts attention at each step to only the two relevant prior blocks (e.g., F_{i-1} and B_i for forward propagation, or B_{i+1} and F_i for backpropagation), eliminating the O(N) cost of processing all weight matrices simultaneously. The forward propagation is implemented by d attention heads computing the pre-activation z_i = W_i o_{i-1} coordinate-by-coordinate (via Lemma A.5), followed by a token-wise FFN applying σ and σ'. Backpropagation uses a multi-head attention to compute δ_i = W_{i+1}^T δ_{i+1} ⊙ σ'(z_i) and the weight gradient vec(δ_i o_{i-1}^T), with a linear subtraction yielding the updated weight. A universal one-step update block (Proposition 4.3) automatically routes to the correct computation (forward, backward, x-init, or y-init) based on input type, so a single fixed transformer block handles the entire 2LN-step trajectory. This yields an O(N) FLOP improvement over Wu et al. (2025) while maintaining constant transformer depth.

## Results

The paper's main result is a provable O(N) computational improvement over the prior best in-context gradient descent construction (Wu et al., 2025): a single-layer transformer with CoT and dynamic masking simulates L steps of gradient descent for an N-layer neural network while processing only 2 weight matrices per step (O(1) per step) instead of all N (O(N) per step). For statistical models (GLM, ridge, lasso), the approximation error after L CoT steps is bounded by Lε + exp(−L/2κ)·B_w/2, where ε is the per-step attention approximation error and κ = β/α is the condition number. The construction requires only constant transformer depth (one block reused autoregressively) regardless of N or L, removing the fixed-depth/fixed-width bottleneck of prior ICGD results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Wu et al. (2025), which encodes all N layer weights vec(W_{1:N}) into every token embedding, forcing each transformer layer to process the full O(N)-dimensional weight stack at every step. This paper's dynamic masking retrieves only the two relevant blocks (F_{i-1}, B_i) for forward propagation or (B_{i+1}, F_i) for backward propagation, reducing per-step computation from O(N) to O(1) and yielding an O(N) total FLOP saving. Compared to Bai et al. (2023), the CoT formulation improves memory consumption by O(L) (storing one parameter block per step in the sequence rather than in the hidden dimension) and replaces the unrealistic ReLU attention with standard softmax attention. The paper does not report head-to-head numerical benchmarks against these baselines; the comparison is entirely in terms of asymptotic computational complexity and architectural requirements (constant depth vs. O(N) depth or width).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context gradient descent (ICGD) line of work initiated by Akyurek et al. (2023), Von Oswald et al. (2023), and Bai et al. (2023), and builds directly on Hu et al. (2025) for the single-step softmax-attention approximation of gradient updates. Its key departure is from Wu et al. (2025) and Wang et al. (2024), which simulate N-layer network training by packing all weights into every token and requiring O(N) transformer depth; here, CoT externalizes the state into an expanding token trajectory and dynamic masking restricts each step to only the two relevant blocks, yielding a constant-depth construction with O(N) fewer FLOPs. The paper also connects to the CoT expressiveness literature (Merrill & Sabharwal 2024; Li et al. 2024) by providing an optimization-theoretic (rather than circuit-complexity) justification for why CoT expands transformer capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result gives the first provable, constructive guarantee that a single frozen transformer block, run autoregressively over a CoT trace, can simulate L steps of backpropagation on an N-layer network with controlled approximation error—something prior deep-transformer ICGD constructions could only achieve with depth scaling in N. This provides a theoretical foundation for why long CoT traces are useful beyond empirical observation, and the dynamic masking mechanism offers a principled alternative to token-deletion approaches (Yang et al., 2025) for managing quadratic attention cost. However, the guarantees rely on idealized assumptions (cost-free omniscient masking, bounded inputs, ReLU activations, convex statistical objectives), and the O(N) FLOP saving is asymptotic with unquantified constant factors from prompt growth, leaving open whether the efficiency gain materializes in practical settings. Extending the framework to convolutional, residual, or attention-based architectures and to adaptive/second-order optimizers is explicitly left for future work.
