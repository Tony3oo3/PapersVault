## Problem

Automated protein model building from cryo-EM density maps must simultaneously enforce strict stereochemical constraints, handle noisy and non-uniform resolution, and resolve conformational heterogeneity—challenges that existing solvers address only partially. Most learning-based methods are limited to homogeneous single-structure reconstructions, and those that attempt heterogeneity (e.g., CryoBoltz) condition on pre-built structural priors rather than learning density-to-atom correspondences directly, limiting robustness under noisy or ambiguous maps.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple sequence alignments (MSAs) and 3D cryo-EM density maps (volumetric grids, typically 2–4 Å resolution) as joint inputs, and produces full all-atom 3D coordinates, per-residue Q-scores, and a predicted local resolution map. For heterogeneous datasets, it generates distinct atomic models for each conformational state sampled from a CryoDRGN latent space, with the local resolution map serving as a spatially varying prior that modulates the trade-off between sequence-driven prediction and density-map guidance across the volume.

## Method

CryoACE builds on the Boltz-1 sequence encoder (MSA module + Pairformer) and a 3D ResUNet density encoder, fused via cross-attention, with an atom-transformer diffusion decoder generating coordinates. Its central innovation is an atom-centric density sampling strategy: instead of expensive voxel convolutions, a profiling module trilinearly interpolates the density map at predicted atomic coordinates, and these local descriptors are iteratively recycled across self-refinement cycles so that improved coordinates yield progressively more accurate density readouts. A training-free staged guidance mechanism drives the reverse diffusion—Sinkhorn-divergence-based global alignment on a k-means point cloud in early timesteps, then Q-score-based local refinement in late timesteps—without additional training. Training uses a multi-task objective combining diffusion loss, a map-fitting MSE between simulated and experimental density, and FAPE/stereochemical auxiliary losses, with conditional dropout on atomic profiles to ensure the model remains functional when local density cues are absent.

## Results

On the Cryo2StructData homogeneous benchmark, CryoACE achieves 100% structural completeness, 98.3% backbone accuracy, 87.5% all-atom accuracy, 2.1 Å backbone RMSD, 3.0 Å all-atom RMSD, and a Q-score of 0.524, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On the heterogeneous EMPIAR-10345 (αVβ8 Integrin) dataset, CryoACE attains the lowest clash rate (0.446%), best MolProbity score (1.198), 100% completeness, WCC of 0.642, and PSC of 0.526±0.051. On the CryoBoltz heterogeneous benchmark (12 conformational states across 5 proteins), CryoACE achieves a mean Cα RMSD of 1.15 Å and mean TM-score of 0.989, surpassing CryoBoltz (1.48 Å / 0.984) and Boltz-1 (5.15 Å / 0.852).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CryoACE outperforms all three homogeneous baselines across every reported metric. Against E3-CryoFold, the gap is largest in completeness (100% vs. 78.21%) and Q-score (0.524 vs. 0.433), with E3-CryoFold unable to produce valid all-atom results after its own post-processing. Against CryoAtom (the closest competitor), CryoACE gains +6.8 pp completeness, +3.7 pp backbone accuracy, +5.4 pp all-atom accuracy, and a 0.003 Q-score improvement. Against ModelAngelo, the advantages are +10.8 pp completeness, +4.6 pp backbone accuracy, and +3.2 pp all-atom accuracy. On the heterogeneous EMPIAR-10345 task, CryoACE edges out CryoBoltz (the only other method achieving 100% completeness) with a lower clash rate (0.446% vs. 2.363%), better MolProbity (1.198 vs. 1.365), and slightly higher WCC (0.642 vs. 0.641) and PSC (0.526 vs. 0.520). On the CryoBoltz 12-state benchmark, CryoACE achieves a mean Cα RMSD of 1.15 Å versus 1.48 Å for CryoBoltz and 5.15 Å for Boltz-1 alone, with the largest relative gains in low-resolution states (e.g., CYP102A1 open at 6.5 Å: 2.80 Å vs. 3.95 Å vs. 8.44 Å).

## Relation to Prior Work

CryoACE builds directly on the Boltz-1 architecture (Wohlwend et al., 2025) for its sequence encoder and the AlphaFold 3 (Abramson et al., 2024) diffusion-based atom-transformer denoiser, extending both with a learned density modality. The key conceptual departure from prior neural model-building methods (ModelAngelo, E3-CryoFold, CryoAtom) is the atom-centric sampling paradigm: instead of processing the full volumetric grid with 3D convolutions, density features are queried at predicted atomic coordinates and iteratively recycled, establishing a physically grounded atom-density correspondence learned during training rather than applied as a post-hoc refinement signal. The training-free guided diffusion strategy (global Sinkhorn guidance + Q-score guidance) follows CryoBoltz (Raghu et al., 2025), but CryoACE differs fundamentally in that its density encoder is trained end-to-end on maps spanning 2–4 Å, rather than deriving gradients from raw density at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CryoACE is the first framework to produce complete, physically valid atomic ensembles from heterogeneous cryo-EM reconstructions without requiring pre-built static structures, addressing a gap where prior methods either fail in flexible regions or produce fragmented models. The released 10,915-entry curated dataset with dual-stage alignment and quality filtering addresses a well-known data-quality bottleneck in cryo-EM multi-modal learning. However, significant limitations remain: no nucleic acid support, GPU memory constraints for sequences beyond ~2000 residues, and performance degradation below 8 Å resolution. Open questions include whether the atom-centric sampling strategy generalizes to very large assemblies (ribosomes, viruses) and whether the local resolution head can be extended to predict per-atom B-factors for more nuanced refinement.
