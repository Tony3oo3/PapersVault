## Problem

IoT sensing signals lack natural semantic units analogous to words in NLP or patches in vision, creating a tokenization gap that forces existing methods to rely on uniform, content-agnostic windowing. This fixed patching misaligns with irregular physical event boundaries, wastes computation on redundant stationary intervals, inflates input length, and requires extensive per-domain hyperparameter tuning of patch size and stride.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262167/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Dywave takes a raw multichannel time-series segment X ∈ R^{C×L} (e.g., 3-channel IMU at 100 Hz over 2–30 s, single-channel ECG/EMG at 700 Hz, or audio at 8 kHz) and produces a variable-length sequence of compact token embeddings E ∈ R^{C×|A|×d}, where |A| is the number of detected temporal anchors and adapts per-sample to semantic complexity rather than raw duration. The tokens are fed into an unmodified backbone encoder (Transformer or Mamba2) for downstream classification, with the tokenization module operating as a plug-and-play front-end that requires no architectural changes to the encoder.

## Method

Dywave applies MODWT to decompose the signal into multi-resolution coefficients, then encodes high-frequency details via 1D convolutions and low-frequency context via an hourglass transformer, fusing both into temporally aligned hierarchical embeddings. It estimates event saliency as cosine dissimilarity between adjacent embeddings, applies temporal NMS and TopK selection to extract a compact set of anchors at semantic transitions, and aggregates neighboring timesteps into anchor-aligned tokens via saliency-weighted pooling. A reconstruction loss in the wavelet coefficient domain regularizes the compressed tokens to preserve both transients and trends. The entire pipeline is backbone-agnostic and eliminates the need for fixed patch-size/stride hyperparameters, reducing input token counts by up to 75% while improving accuracy by up to 12% over state-of-the-art baselines.

## Results

Dywave achieves the best accuracy across all five short-context sensing configurations (MOD seismic, PAMAP2 acc/gyro, RWHAR acc/gyro) with both Transformer and Mamba2 backbones, with headline gains of up to 12% accuracy on RWHAR (0.8517 vs. 0.7340 for PatchTST with Mamba2). On long-context signals (Ego4D 30s, MOD audio 16 kHz, WESAD ECG/EMG), Dywave produces 55 tokens on average for MOD audio—nearly 4× fewer than PatchTST—while maintaining superior accuracy. Under head-only finetuning for cross-domain generalization, Dywave retains 0.7565 accuracy on MOD seismic versus PatchTST's 0.3414, demonstrating substantially more transferable representations. Token counts are reduced by up to 75% relative to fixed-patch baselines without sacrificing accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dywave outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and backbone configuration. Against PatchTST (the strongest fixed-patch baseline), Dywave gains 1.7–11.8% accuracy (e.g., 0.8517 vs. 0.7340 on RWHAR acc with Mamba2) while using far fewer tokens. MedFormer's multi-granularity patching is competitive on PAMAP2 acc (0.7709 vs. 0.7977) but degrades sharply on RWHAR gyro (0.5239 vs. 0.7294 with Transformer). DropPatch and WaveToken perform poorly on gyroscope signals (often below 0.50 accuracy), indicating that random token dropping and wavelet quantization disrupt fine-grained temporal coherence. Multi-Patch is the closest competitor on Mamba2 (within 1–3% on several datasets) but still trails Dywave on all configurations. Against the dynamic patching baselines LightGTS and Ruptures (Table 2), Dywave achieves 0.9094 vs. 0.8586 (LightGTS) and 0.7761 (Ruptures) on RWHAR with Transformer, while using fewer tokens (29.61 vs. 14.66 and 57.60 respectively), confirming that representation-driven event-aligned tokenization surpasses both learned per-sequence patching and statistical change-point detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dywave carves new ground at the intersection of wavelet signal analysis and adaptive token selection, repurposing ideas from distinct lineages without directly extending any single prior method. It draws on MODWT for multi-scale decomposition (as in WaveToken and classical time-frequency analysis), borrows the hourglass transformer pattern for long-range context encoding, and applies attention-style key-query similarity for boundary detection in a way analogous to DynamicViT/TokenLearner in vision—but transplanted to continuous sensing waveforms where no natural patch boundaries exist. The key conceptual departure from prior work is operating in a learned embedding space for saliency estimation rather than on raw signal statistics (Ruptures) or fixed periodicity (LightGTS), and maintaining continuous token embeddings rather than discretizing into token IDs (WaveToken).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine representational gap: sensing signals lack the natural atomic units (words, image patches) that make tokenization straightforward in NLP and vision, and uniform patching is demonstrably misaligned with event structure. The practical impact is significant for edge IoT deployment—reducing token count by up to 75% while improving accuracy directly translates to lower latency and memory on constrained devices like Raspberry Pi. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly streaming/online settings, whether it can serve as a front-end for sensing foundation models (the authors hint at this but do not demonstrate it), and whether the task-supervised saliency learning transfers well to zero-shot or few-shot scenarios. The micro-activity decomposition capability, while promising, remains qualitative and unvalidated against ground-truth fine-grained annotations.
