## Problem

Existing self-explainable temporal GNNs predominantly attribute predictions to previously seen (stability) interactions while under-modeling newly emerging first-time (transition) interactions, leading to biased and unfaithful explanations. This paper identifies two concrete challenges—stability bias and transition under-modeling—and shows empirically that seven prevalent TGNNs exhibit a consistent performance gap between seen and unseen interactions, motivating a disentangled explanation framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a temporal interaction sequence and, for each target event (u, v, t), constructs a one-hop ego-temporal subgraph of the L most recent historical interactions incident to u or v, enriched with node features, edge features, periodic time encodings, and relative-activity encodings. It outputs a binary link-prediction probability, a compact explanatory subgraph defined by per-edge inclusion probabilities (sampled via Gumbel-Softmax relaxation), and two disentangled pattern representations (stability h_S and transition h_T) obtained by frequency-guided soft masking of the explanatory subgraph.

## Method

ST-TGExplainer is a self-explainable TGNN built on an MLP-Mixer encoder that processes the ego-temporal subgraph and produces per-edge inclusion probabilities through a Gumbel-Softmax bottleneck, yielding a stochastic explanatory subgraph G_E. A frequency-guided disentangler then splits G_E into stability (G_S) and transition (G_T) subgraphs via a soft Hadamard-product mask derived from interaction recurrence counts, and a shared encoder with mean pooling produces pattern-specific representations. Training optimizes a disentangled information bottleneck objective with three terms: a prediction loss (BCE on the fused representation), a compression loss (KL divergence between the learned Bernoulli edge-selection distribution and a fixed-rate prior), and an adversarial disentanglement loss (JS-divergence-based discriminator enforcing conditional independence between h_S and h_T given the label Y). The min-max optimization alternates between maximizing the discriminator and minimizing the combined encoder-predictor loss, ensuring the two pattern branches capture complementary, non-redundant evidence.

## Results

On link prediction (AP), ST-TGExplainer achieves the best score on 5 of 6 datasets: Reddit (99.43%), UCI (97.28%), Enron (94.87%), USLegis (94.47%), and Can.Parl. (90.25%), while remaining competitive on Wikipedia (99.21% vs. TGIB's 99.37%). On MRR, it leads on Reddit (88.96%) and UCI (81.68%), with a particularly large margin on UCI over DyGFormer (76.61%). For explanation faithfulness (ACC-AUC), ST-TGExplainer outperforms all seven explanation baselines on every dataset, with gains up to 9% on low-repeat-ratio datasets such as UCI (96.04% vs. TempME's 87.06%) and Can.Parl. (96.88% vs. TempME's 95.98%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest TGNN baselines, ST-TGExplainer surpasses FreeDyG on UCI (+1.00 AP), Enron (+4.58), USLegis (+24.81), and Can.Parl. (+18.03), and edges out DyGFormer on Enron (+2.40), USLegis (+23.36), and Can.Parl. (+13.95). The only AP loss is on Wikipedia, where TGIB leads by 0.16 points (99.37 vs. 99.21), though TGIB does not report Reddit results. On MRR, ST-TGExplainer beats DyGFormer on UCI by 5.07 points (81.68 vs. 76.61) and FreeDyG by 8.08 points, but trails FreeDyG on Wikipedia by 2.32 points (86.68 vs. 89.00). For explanation faithfulness (ACC-AUC), ST-TGExplainer dominates all seven explanation baselines on all six datasets; the largest margins are on UCI (+8.98 over TempME) and Can.Parl. (+7.16 over TGIB), confirming that the stability–transition disentanglement is most beneficial on datasets with lower interaction repeat ratios. Grad-CAM underperforms even the Random baseline on several datasets, highlighting the unreliability of gradient-based post-hoc methods for temporal graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Graph Information Bottleneck (Wu et al., 2020) framework to temporal graphs by decomposing the single explanatory subgraph used in prior self-explainable TGNNs (notably TGIB, Seo et al., 2024) into two complementary branches: a stability pattern for recurring interactions and a transition pattern for newly emerging ones. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a conditional mutual information disentanglement term I(G_S; G_T | Y), approximated via adversarial JS-divergence, which has no direct analogue in the static GIB or TGIB objectives. For the stochastic edge-selection mechanism, the paper adopts Gumbel-Softmax relaxation (Jang et al., 2017) as in TGIB, and uses an MLP-Mixer encoder following the GraphMixer architecture, positioning the method within the efficient non-attention-based TGNN family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated gap: existing TGNNs and their explainers are biased toward frequent, recurring interactions and under-model newly emerging ones, as evidenced by the consistent seen/unseen MRR gap across seven baselines. The disentangled IB formulation provides a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism (with variational bounds and adversarial surrogates) for separating these two evidence types, yielding more faithful explanations especially on low-repeat-ratio datasets. Limitations include reliance on one-hop histories and heuristic frequency-based soft assignment for the stability/transition split, leaving open questions about multi-hop extension and more principled, dataset-agnostic disentanglement signals.
