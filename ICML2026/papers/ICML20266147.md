## Problem

Reparameterization Policy Gradient (RPG) achieves high sample efficiency by backpropagating through differentiable dynamics, but has been restricted to Gaussian policies, limiting action distribution expressiveness. Flow policies offer superior expressiveness for robotics, yet existing RL methods for them either require intractable log-likelihood approximations (on-policy) or rely on Q-function backpropagation (off-policy). The paper identifies that flow policies naturally align with the RPG framework but shows that naively combining them fails due to training instability and insufficient exploration.

## Input / Output

The policy receives either a state vector (e.g., R^37 for Ant, R^49 for ANYmal) or a point-cloud pixel observation (e.g., [R^{250×3}, R^3, R^3] for manipulation tasks) plus a Gaussian noise sample, and outputs a continuous action vector whose dimensionality ranges from R^3 (Transport, Rolling Pin) to R^222 (Soft Jumper). Actions are bounded via a tanh squashing function applied to the flow ODE output, with pre-tanh values clamped to a maximum absolute value of 1 (or 5 for Hand Flip and Rolling Pin). The flow ODE is discretized with 4 Euler steps by default, and an action-chunking variant predicts C future actions jointly from a single observation.

## Method

RFO exploits the key insight that a flow policy's Euler-integrated ODE defines a differentiable mapping F_θ(ε; s) from source noise to actions, making it directly compatible with RPG: policy gradients are computed by backpropagating reward through both the K-step ODE integration and the differentiable environment dynamics (via BPTT over a truncated horizon H=32, following the SHAC paradigm). Two CFM-based regularization terms are critical: (1) Past Data CFM, which uses actions from the current and previous rollout iterations as targets to stabilize the vector field and prevent it from drifting away from recently generated actions, and (2) Uniform Exploration CFM, which uses uniformly sampled actions over the bounded action space as targets to explicitly encourage exploration without approximating policy entropy. The total policy loss combines the negative RPG objective with weighted past-data and uniform CFM losses, while a dual-critic value network (TD-λ targets) provides the terminal value estimate for the truncated horizon.

## Results

RFO achieves a SHAC-normalized average score of 1.60 across seven locomotion and manipulation tasks, outperforming SAPO (1.21), DrAC (1.01), SHAC (1.00), and FlowRL (0.91). The headline result is on the Soft Jumper task (soft-body quadruped, action space R^222), where RFO reaches a normalized score of 2.63, nearly 2× the best baseline SAPO (1.39) and dramatically surpassing DrAC (0.07) and FlowRL (−0.07). On rigid-body tasks, RFO gains ~21% over SAPO on Hand Reorient (1.48 vs 1.22) and ~17% on Ant (1.81 vs 1.55). RFO also matches or exceeds SOTA on Transport (1.87) and Rolling Pin (1.06), while trailing DrAC slightly on Hand Flip (1.26 vs 1.37).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RFO is compared against four baselines: SHAC (Gaussian RPG), SAPO (SOTA Gaussian RPG with max-entropy), DrAC (SOTA diffusion-policy RL), and FlowRL (SOTA flow-policy RL). RFO decisively outperforms all four baselines on Soft Jumper (2.63× SHAC), Ant (1.81× SHAC), Hand Reorient (1.48× SHAC), and ANYmal (1.07× SHAC), with the largest margin on the high-dimensional soft-body task where DrAC and FlowRL essentially fail (scores near 0). On Transport, RFO (1.87) is competitive with but slightly below DrAC (1.90) and FlowRL (1.93), while on Hand Flip RFO (1.26) trails DrAC (1.37) and SAPO (1.27) by a small margin. On Rolling Pin, all methods are within ~3% of each other. Overall, RFO's advantage is most pronounced on tasks with high-dimensional or soft-body dynamics, where the expressiveness of flow policies combined with RPG's low-variance gradients provides a clear edge over both Gaussian RPG and off-policy flow/diffusion RL methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected research threads: Reparameterization Policy Gradient (RPG), which has been applied almost exclusively to Gaussian policies in differentiable simulators (SHAC, AHAC, SAPO), and flow/diffusion policies for RL, which have been trained either off-policy via Q-function backpropagation (DrAC, FlowRL) or on-policy through intractable log-likelihood approximations (DPPO, FPO, GenPO). The key conceptual contribution is recognizing that flow policies, which generate actions via differentiable ODE integration from a source noise distribution, are structurally identical to the reparameterization mapping that RPG requires, enabling end-to-end backpropagation through both the flow generation and environment dynamics without any likelihood computation. The algorithm inherits the truncated-horizon BPTT paradigm from SHAC/SAPO and the CFM objective from flow matching, but introduces two novel regularization terms (Past Data CFM and Uniform Exploration CFM) that are specific to stabilizing RPG-driven flow optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RFO offers a practical path to combining the expressiveness of flow policies (multimodal action distributions, fast inference with few integration steps) with the sample efficiency of RPG, which is critical for differentiable-simulation-based robot learning where environment rollouts are expensive. The ~2× reward improvement on the high-dimensional soft-body Soft Jumper task (R^222 action space) suggests the approach is particularly beneficial when action distributions are complex and Gaussian policies are insufficient. Open questions include whether the regularization terms generalize beyond differentiable simulators (e.g., to learned world models or real-robot settings), how RFO scales to longer-horizon tasks, and whether the action-chunking variant can be improved via offline-to-online pretraining as the authors suggest. The method's dependence on differentiable dynamics limits its applicability to non-differentiable or stochastic environments.
