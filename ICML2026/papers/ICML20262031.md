## Problem

Goal-conditioned value functions in offline GCRL suffer from overgeneralization: they propagate value estimates by Euclidean proximity rather than temporal distance, causing large estimation errors in long-horizon tasks. While quasimetric architectures (IQE, MRN) partially mitigate this, they exhibit inconsistent performance across domains—collapsing in robotic manipulation while excelling in maze navigation—and can induce catastrophic training instability. No existing method achieves both reliable value generalization and consistent performance across diverse task types.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state vectors and goal vectors (state-reaching setting where G=S) from fixed offline datasets, with state dimensions ranging from 2D (PointMaze) to 59D (HumanoidMaze) and 6-DoF robot arm configurations for manipulation. It produces a two-level hierarchical policy: a high-level policy that maps (state, goal) to a 10-dimensional subgoal representation, and a low-level policy that maps (state, subgoal) to continuous actions. The value function itself is parameterized as the negative L2 distance between a 64-dimensional state embedding and a 64-dimensional goal embedding in a learned latent space.

## Method

LAVL introduces the Latent Alignment Network (LAN), which parameterizes the goal-conditioned value as V(s,g) = -||φ_S(s) - φ_G(g)||_2 using asymmetric state and goal encoders, breaking the strict metric constraint of prior quasimetric architectures while retaining enough structure to guide accurate generalization. The value is trained with an IVL expectile-regression TD objective augmented by a continuity regularization term that penalizes value variation between dataset-adjacent states exceeding a threshold δ, stabilizing the local value landscape without requiring gradient computation. For policy extraction, LAVL adopts HIQL's hierarchical framework but uses a goal-only subgoal representation w=φ(g) that integrates naturally with LAN, trained via advantage-weighted regression with a single shared value function for both levels. The key empirical finding is that the asymmetric latent representation—not the quasimetric property—is what enables reliable value generalization, as LAN consistently outperforms IQE, MRN, and Hilbert parameterizations across both navigation and manipulation tasks.

## Results

LAVL achieves the highest success rate on 20 out of 22 OGBench datasets, with particularly strong results on long-horizon and stitching tasks. On giant mazes, LAVL maintains 85% average success rate with only a 9.6% relative drop from medium to giant, compared to 23.1% (OTA), 58.3% (CGCIVL), and 75.4% (HIQL) drops for baselines. On robotic manipulation, LAVL reaches 83% on Cube-single and 88% on Scene, while QRL collapses to 5% and HIQL to 15%/38% respectively. On trajectory stitching datasets, LAVL shows only a 1.1% relative performance drop from navigate to stitch, versus 18.6–36.9% for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAVL outperforms all eight baselines on 20 of 22 datasets. QRL (Wang et al., 2023) is competitive on medium/large mazes (e.g., 86% on Pointmaze-large-navigate) but collapses to near-zero on robotic manipulation (5% on Cube-single, 5% on Scene) and degrades severely on giant mazes (14% on Antmaze-giant-navigate vs. LAVL's 85%). HIQL (Park et al., 2023) is competitive on medium mazes (96% on Antmaze-medium-navigate) but suffers a 75.4% relative drop from medium to giant, while LAVL drops only 9.6%. OTA (Ahn et al., 2025) and CGCIVL (Ke et al., 2025) are the strongest baselines on long-horizon mazes, yet both degrade substantially on giant-stitch (37% and 8% respectively on Antmaze-giant-stitch vs. LAVL's 82%). On robotic manipulation, LAVL's advantage is most dramatic: 83% on Cube-single vs. 68% (GCIQL), 15% (HIQL), and 5% (QRL). CRL (Eysenbach et al., 2022) and GCBC (Ghosh et al., 2021) are consistently the weakest baselines, with CRL failing entirely on stitch datasets (0% on all Pointmaze-stitch) and GCBC rarely exceeding 45%. Eik-HIQL and Eik-HiQRL (Giammarino et al., 2025) are competitive on mazes under their own evaluation protocol but remain well below LAVL on manipulation (25%/52% on Cube-single vs. LAVL's 83%/88% on Scene).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the HIQL hierarchical policy extraction framework and the IVL temporal-difference objective, while departing from the quasimetric architecture line (IQE, MRN) that prior work (Wang et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2023) identified as essential for goal-conditioned value learning. The key conceptual departure is showing that the quasimetric property of the optimal value function is not what drives reliable generalization; instead, an asymmetric latent-space distance (φ_S ≠ φ_G) provides the critical inductive bias. The continuity regularization term is a new loss component that complements the TD objective by suppressing local value fluctuations, drawing inspiration from eikonal-based regularization (Giammarino et al. 2025) but implemented via finite differences rather than gradient computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that architectural inductive bias—not the learning objective or quasimetric constraint—is the primary determinant of value generalization quality reframes how practitioners should design value functions for offline GCRL, and the method's strong performance on OGBench (20/22 datasets) positions LAVL as a likely new standard baseline. Open questions include whether the approach extends to real-world robotic control, high-dimensional action spaces beyond 6-DoF manipulation, and whether the asymmetric latent alignment principle generalizes to other value-based RL settings beyond goal-conditioned tasks. The paper also acknowledges that policy extraction for long-horizon tasks with complex dynamics remains a separate challenge orthogonal to value learning.
