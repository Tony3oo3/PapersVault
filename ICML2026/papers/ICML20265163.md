## Problem

Existing optimizers for LLM pretraining fail to simultaneously satisfy the spectral μP condition (‖W‖₂ = Θ(√(d_out/d_in))) for both weights and updates. Muon, interpreted as steepest descent under the spectral norm, constrains only the update direction while leaving weights free to drift, causing activation instability that forces practitioners to rely on architectural patches like QK-Norm or logit softcapping. The paper asks whether a single optimizer can achieve both optimal convergence (steepest descent) and fundamental stability (strict spectral constraints on both W and ΔW).

## Input / Output

The method operates on individual 2D hidden-layer weight matrices (atomic modules such as per-head QKV projections or separate FFN gate/up matrices) and their corresponding gradients. It produces updated weights whose spectral norm is exactly equal to the target radius R = c·√(d_out/d_in), with the update magnitude scaled as η·R·Φ where Φ is a unit-spectral-norm direction. Unlike element-wise optimizers, SSO requires full gradient matrices per module (incompatible with flat-buffer ZeRO-1 sharding) and performs Power Iteration in BF16 alongside FP32 Newton-Schulz iterations for the matrix sign computation.

## Method

SSO formulates steepest descent constrained to a spectral sphere of radius R, where both the current weights and the post-update weights must satisfy ‖W‖₂ = R. The key insight is that the first-order tangent constraint ⟨Θ, Φ⟩ = 0 (where Θ = u₁v₁ᵀ is the outer product of the top singular vectors) reduces the problem to finding a Lagrange multiplier λ* such that h(λ*) = ⟨Θ, msign(M̂ + λ*Θ)⟩ = 0, solved via bracketing and bisection. The resulting update direction Φ = msign(M̂ + λ*Θ) is the unique steepest descent direction on the spectral sphere's tangent space, and a retraction step W ← W·R/σ projects weights back onto the manifold each iteration. This eliminates the need for weight decay on hidden 2D weights, as the spectral constraint inherently bounds the Frobenius norm. The implementation in Megatron-LM uses atomic module sharding, a ping-pong load-balancing strategy for variable solver depths, adaptive kernel dispatch (JIT for small matrices, Triton SYRK for large ones), multi-stream parallelism, and cached singular vectors to reduce per-step overhead to ~11% over Muon.

## Results

On Dense 1.7B (100B tokens), Spectral Sphere achieves the best average benchmark accuracy of 56.35%, outperforming Muon (55.26%) and AdamW (54.75%), while also delivering a 1.24× training speedup over AdamW. On MoE 8B-A1B, Spectral Sphere attains the lowest validation loss and the best router load balancing (lowest MaxVio) among all four optimizers. On the 200-layer DeepNet stress test, Spectral Sphere achieves both the lowest training loss and the highest stability, with no loss spikes observed, whereas AdamW exhibits occasional instability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdamW, Spectral Sphere wins decisively: it achieves 1.6 percentage points higher average accuracy (56.35% vs. 54.75%), a 1.24× training speedup, and strictly bounded activations (AdamW's attention AbsMax and FFN RMS reach ~100× larger magnitudes). Against Muon, Spectral Sphere improves average accuracy by 1.09 points (56.35% vs. 55.26%) and, critically, eliminates the activation drift and logit explosion that Muon still exhibits due to its unconstrained forward weights. On the MoE 8B-A1B model, Spectral Sphere further improves router load balancing (lower MaxVio) over Muon, which in turn yields a lower validation loss. The MuonSphere variant (spectral retraction without the Lagrange solver) is competitive—achieving the best LMB perplexity (4.87) and BoolQ accuracy (66.97%)—but trails Spectral Sphere on average accuracy (56.19% vs. 56.35%) and on individual tasks like CSQA and PIQA, confirming that the full tangent-space steepest descent direction provides a measurable edge over simple spectral normalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Muon optimizer (Jordan et al., 2024), which performs steepest descent under the spectral norm for weight updates, by adding a complementary constraint on the weights themselves. The theoretical foundation draws on the μP spectral condition (Yang et al., 2024) for width-invariant activations and the metrized deep learning framework (Bernstein & Newhouse, 2024) that unifies optimizers as norm-constrained steepest descent. The key conceptual departure is identifying the spectral sphere as the natural manifold where both the update direction and the weight state are simultaneously constrained, yielding a Lagrange-multiplier-based tangent-space solution rather than the heuristic projection approaches in Scion (Pethick et al., 2025) or the overly rigid Stiefel/Hyperball alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSO offers a principled replacement for the ad-hoc architectural patches (QK-Norm, logit softcapping, SandwichNorm) that practitioners currently rely on to stabilize Muon training, potentially simplifying model design. The practical Megatron implementation with atomic module sharding and adaptive kernel dispatch makes it deployable at scale, though the 11.45% per-step overhead over Muon remains a non-trivial cost that limits immediate adoption. Open questions include whether a GPU-native root solver can close this gap, whether the spectral constraint interacts favorably with low-precision (FP8/FP4) training, and whether the approach generalizes beyond 2D hidden-layer matrices to higher-order tensors or non-standard architectures.
