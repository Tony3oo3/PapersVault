## Problem

Existing VLN agents either apply explicit reasoning at every step (causing overthinking and prohibitive computational cost) or rely on rigid rule-based triggers that lack flexibility. Learning truly adaptive reasoning—deciding when and which mode to reason—typically demands large-scale supervised fine-tuning with task-specific reasoning annotations, but embodied interaction data is far from web-scale and prohibitively expensive to collect. The paper asks how to enable difficulty-aware, mode-adaptive reasoning in VLN without costly labeled reasoning trigger data.

## Input / Output

The agent receives sequential monocular RGB frames and a free-form natural language instruction, and outputs a low-level navigation action (turn_left, turn_right, move_forward, stop) at each step. Optionally, it also generates reasoning text in one of three modes—description, summary, or error correction—triggered adaptively by the UAR Block. Internally, the UAR Block consumes the policy's token-level action entropy (computed over the generated action tokens) as a scalar uncertainty signal, fused with the current observation and instruction embedding to produce a compact control vector over reasoning-mode logits. No depth, odometry, or topological map inputs are required.

## Method

AdaNav introduces a lightweight Uncertainty-Adaptive Reasoning Block (UAR) that computes action entropy H as an interpretable uncertainty indicator, fuses the historical entropy trend H_{≤t} with the current observation o_t and instruction I into a control vector p_mode^t = W₁H_{≤t} + W₂o_t + W₃I + b, and applies softmax to yield a reasoning-mode selection policy. Training follows a Heuristic-to-RL schedule: an uncertainty-based prior p_prior (a Gaussian-shaped distribution over modes centered at mode-specific entropy thresholds τ_m) is fused with the learned model prediction via a linearly annealed coefficient λ_t (from 1 to 0), so the agent initially reasons when entropy exceeds a threshold and progressively lets RL refine the trigger policy. The reward combines a distance-reduction extrinsic reward with a normalized reasoning-length penalty, encouraging the agent to navigate efficiently while minimizing unnecessary reasoning calls. With only 6K training episodes, AdaNav reduces average reasoning steps to 2.5 per trajectory (over ~55-step trajectories) while concentrating 71% of reasoning on hard episodes, outperforming closed-source models trained on million-scale data.

## Results

With only 6K training episodes, AdaNav achieves substantial gains over closed-source models trained on million-scale data. On R2R-CE Val-Unseen, NaVILA-AdaNav reaches 60.19% SR and 50.0% SPL, surpassing MonoDream (55.8% SR) and NaVid-4D (43.8% SR). On RxR-CE Val-Unseen, Uni-NaVid-AdaNav achieves 54.5% SR and 61.5% nDTW, outperforming all baselines. The average improvement over base models is ~20% SR on R2R and ~11.7% on RxR, while reducing average reasoning steps to just 2.5 per trajectory (vs. 55-step trajectories).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaNav consistently outperforms all baselines across both R2R-CE and RxR-CE Val-Unseen splits. Against the strongest open-source baseline NaVILA (54.0% SR on R2R), NaVILA-AdaNav improves SR by 6.19 points to 60.19% and SPL by 1.0 point to 50.0%. Against the strongest closed-source baseline MonoDream (55.8% SR on R2R, 49.4% SR on RxR), NaVILA-AdaNav leads by 4.39 points on R2R SR, while Uni-NaVid-AdaNav leads by 5.1 points on RxR SR (54.5% vs. 49.4%). On RxR-CE, Uni-NaVid-AdaNav (54.5% SR, 61.5% nDTW) surpasses NaVILA (44.0% SR, 58.8% nDTW) by 10.5 and 2.7 points respectively. In the cross-dataset setting (Table 2, trained only on R2R), NaVILA-AdaNav achieves 38.82% SR on RxR Val-Unseen, beating MonoDream (25.1%) by 13.7 points. All AdaNav variants use only 6K additional training episodes, compared to 550K–3600K for open-source baselines and 1420K–1840K for closed-source baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaNav builds on the VLN backbone lineage of NaVid, NaVILA, and Uni-NaVid, which scale VLM fine-tuning on large navigation datasets but still struggle with temporal grounding and perception-action mapping in long-horizon trajectories. It departs from prior explicit-reasoning approaches (NavGPT, MiC, DiscussNav, MCGPT) that apply rule-based or fixed-step reasoning modes, instead introducing a learnable, uncertainty-triggered reasoning policy via the UAR Block. The Heuristic-to-RL training mechanism is a key methodological departure: rather than requiring costly labeled reasoning-trigger data for supervised fine-tuning, it bootstraps with an interpretable action-entropy prior and progressively anneals it into a fully learned RL policy, addressing the data-scarcity bottleneck specific to embodied tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a lightweight 6K-sample plugin can outperform million-scale closed-source VLN models is practically significant for resource-constrained embodied AI deployment, and the UAR Block's negligible latency (<0.4% overhead) makes adaptive reasoning viable in real-time settings. Cross-dataset transfer (R2R-trained model improving RxR by 11.7% without RxR training) and validated Sim-to-Real deployment on a Jetson AGX Orin platform strengthen the case for generalization. Open questions include whether action entropy remains a sufficient uncertainty signal in substantially more complex or outdoor environments, whether the approach extends to multi-agent or cooperative navigation, and how the method scales as base VLMs themselves improve in native reasoning capability.
