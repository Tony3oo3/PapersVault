## Problem

Adversarial training degrades severely on worst-performing (tail) classes under long-tailed distributions, yet the underlying cause remains unexplained. The paper identifies a 'posterior mismatch': coarse-grained absolute (one-hot) labels collapse class posteriors into point estimates, inducing biased class-frequency estimation and an enlarged robust generalization gap that disproportionately harms tail-class robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265974/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR10-LT/CIFAR100-LT, 64×64 for TinyImageNet-LT) and outputs class labels, but the key distinction is in how supervision is structured: instead of one-hot targets, the model is trained against fine-grained probabilistic labels produced by a learned posterior surrogate, with class-frequency priors also estimated from these soft labels rather than from the dataset's empirical counts. Evaluation is reported separately for all classes, tail classes (bottom 80% by sample count), and worst classes (bottom 50% by accuracy), with robustness measured under l∞ PGD (ε=8/255) and other attacks.

## Method

PAT introduces a learnable posterior surrogate q (same architecture as the target model) trained on clean data with a LogSumExp-aggregated class-specific BSL objective that emphasizes worst-class performance, then frozen during adversarial training. During adversarial training of the target model h, PGD-generated adversarial examples are supervised with mixed soft labels that blend the surrogate's clean and adversarial posterior predictions (controlled by ratio r), and class-frequency priors are re-estimated from these soft labels to maintain distributional consistency. Adversarial weight perturbations (AWP) are incorporated to enforce a flatter weight loss landscape, which the theory shows stabilizes the robust generalization gap against posterior approximation errors. The theoretical analysis proves that accurate posterior approximation simultaneously tightens class-frequency estimation bounds and robust generalization bounds, while landscape flatness reduces the sharpness penalty and bounds the impact of residual approximation error.

## Results

On CIFAR10-LT (IR=50, ResNet-18), PAT achieves 73.41% clean accuracy and 36.71% robust accuracy across all classes, with worst-class robustness of 26.23% under PGD (ε=8/255). This represents a 3.4-point improvement over the strongest baseline AT-SD (22.79% worst-class robustness) and a 6.9-point gain over RoBal (19.36%). On CIFAR100-LT and TinyImageNet-LT, PAT similarly leads on all metrics, with worst-class robustness of 9.27% and 6.79% respectively, consistently outperforming all competitors by 1–4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAT outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest long-tailed adversarial training baselines, PAT improves worst-class robustness by 3.4 points over AT-SD (26.23% vs. 22.79% on CIFAR10-LT) and by 6.9 points over RoBal (26.23% vs. 19.36%). The gap is even larger against simpler methods: AT-BSL achieves only 15.59% worst-class robustness (a 10.6-point deficit), while standard AT and AWP fall below 9%. On CIFAR100-LT, PAT leads AT-SD by 3.1 points on worst-class robustness (9.27% vs. 6.15%) and RoBal by 5.2 points. The gains are most pronounced on worst-class and tail-class metrics, confirming that PAT specifically addresses the long-tail robustness degradation that other methods fail to resolve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the BSL-based long-tailed adversarial training lineage (RoBal, AT-BSL, TAET, AT-SD), which all share the assumption that absolute one-hot labels combined with class-frequency margins are sufficient for rebalancing. The key departure is a theoretical diagnosis: absolute labels induce a 'posterior mismatch' that biases class-frequency estimation and enlarges the robust generalization gap, effects that are amplified when imbalance and adversarial perturbations coexist. PAT introduces a learnable posterior surrogate (trained with an LSE objective on clean data) to provide fine-grained probabilistic supervision, and combines it with adversarial weight perturbation (AWP) for a flat loss landscape. The paper explicitly distinguishes its posterior surrogate from knowledge distillation and adversarial robustness distillation, arguing the surrogate's role is to correct label representation rather than transfer generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical framework connecting posterior approximation quality to both class-frequency estimation error and robust generalization bounds, offering principled guidelines (accuracy on worst-class + flat landscape) rather than ad-hoc heuristics. A notable practical finding is that AWP applied to imbalance-unaware adversarial training can actually worsen long-tailed robustness, underscoring that landscape flatness and label representation must be addressed jointly. Limitations include diminishing gains under extreme imbalance (IR=200) where posterior approximation error dominates, and the approach is validated only on small-scale benchmarks (CIFAR10/100, TinyImageNet) with CNN backbones. Open questions include whether the posterior surrogate framework extends to transformer architectures, larger datasets, or non-BSL rebalancing objectives.
