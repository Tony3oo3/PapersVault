## Problem

Existing dual-stream time-series forecasting models suffer from strong feature coupling between global and local branches, causing them to learn similar representations and reducing sensitivity to local abrupt variations. As input sequences grow longer, global trends dominate the prediction process and subtle local changes are smoothed out or absorbed by global representations, a problem that is especially critical in medical signals like blood glucose where local variations indicate pathological events.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264915/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a historical blood glucose window L ∈ R^{L×M} (look-back length L ranging from 1 hour to 6 days, M variates) and produces F future glucose values (horizons from 0.5h to 24h). A key design choice is the Dynamic Multi-Scale Patching (DMSP) algorithm, which adaptively selects different patch lengths and strides for the global and local streams based on the forecast length F, using a scaling factor β(F) = clip(F/F*, β_min, β_max) to control temporal granularity. This means the same model handles multi-scale forecasting without architectural changes, simply by adjusting patching parameters.

## Method

DSENet employs a dual-stream architecture where the global stream uses DFB-Mamba (a bidirectional Mamba with a forget gate and learnable dynamic convolution) to act as a low-pass filter capturing smooth trends, while the local stream combines a LoRE module (low-rank FFN + dynamic 1D convolution) with a windowed lightweight self-attention to act as a high-pass filter sensitive to abrupt changes. The core novelty is the Dual-Stream Enhancement Mechanism, which structurally enlarges the representational discrepancy between the two streams at the architectural level, reducing feature coupling as verified by CKA analysis. The two streams are fused via DSSAF, which generates sample-specific fusion weights from the original input sequence rather than using fixed concatenation. The entire model maintains O(L) computational complexity, with the global stream leveraging Mamba's linear recurrence and the local stream restricting attention to local windows.

## Results

On OhioT1DM, DSENet achieves the best average MSE of 0.465 (HL=1h) and MAE of 0.477, outperforming the nearest competitor SST (MSE 0.473, MAE 0.483) and DLinear (MSE 0.478, MAE 0.491). On DCLP5, DSENet achieves the best average MSE of 0.609 (HL=1h) and MAE of 0.561, beating SST (MSE 0.632, MAE 0.586) and DLinear (MSE 0.627, MAE 0.576). The advantage grows substantially at longer forecasting horizons: at FL=24h with HL=1h on OhioT1DM, DSENet reaches MSE 0.778 vs. SST's 0.786 and Crossformer's 0.786, while on DCLP5 at the same setting DSENet achieves MSE 0.618 vs. SST's 0.626. DSENet also achieves the highest Clarke Error Grid Region A percentage across all clinically meaningful horizons (0.5h, 1h, 2h) on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSENet outperforms all eight baselines on both datasets across the majority of historical-length settings. Against the closest competitors SST and DLinear, DSENet achieves 1.7–3.5% lower MSE on OhioT1DM (e.g., 0.465 vs. 0.473 for SST at HL=1h) and 3.4–4.8% lower MSE on DCLP5 (e.g., 0.609 vs. 0.632 for SST at HL=1h). The gap widens markedly at longer forecasting horizons: at FL=24h, DSENet's MSE advantage over SST grows to ~1% on OhioT1DM and ~1.3% on DCLP5, while over heavier baselines like iTransformer and PatchTST the MSE gap reaches 20–35%. Crossformer is competitive at short horizons (HL=4h on OhioT1DM: 0.468 vs. DSENet's 0.466) but degrades faster as historical length increases. NsT, TimeFilter, and xPatch consistently trail DSENet by 10–40% in MSE, confirming that single-paradigm or weakly-coupled dual-branch designs struggle with multi-scale non-stationary glucose dynamics. DSENet also achieves the highest CEG Region A percentage at all clinically relevant horizons (0.5h, 1h, 2h), indicating superior clinical safety.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual-stream architecture paradigm (originally from video understanding) to time-series forecasting, building specifically on Bi-Mamba+ for the global stream and lightweight windowed attention for the local stream. It departs from prior dual-branch approaches like xPatch and SST by identifying feature coupling between global and local streams as the core bottleneck, and proposes the Dual-Stream Enhancement Mechanism (DFB-Mamba for global, LoRE for local) to explicitly widen the representational gap between the two streams. The Dynamic Multi-Scale Patching (DMSP) and Dual-Stream State Adaptive Fusion (DSSAF) algorithms are novel contributions that tie the two streams together through forecast-length-aware patching and input-conditioned fusion weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-stream enhancement mechanism offers a general architectural principle—deliberately widening the functional gap between complementary modeling branches—that could transfer beyond blood glucose to other non-stationary biomedical or industrial time series. However, the evaluation is limited to two small clinical datasets (12 patients in OhioT1DM), the performance gains over strong baselines like SST and Crossformer are modest (often <0.01 MAE), and the low-pass/high-pass filter interpretation of Mamba vs. Transformer, while intuitive, is not a new theoretical insight. Open questions include whether the mechanism generalizes to longer-horizon or multivariate clinical settings, and whether the CKA-based decoupling evidence translates to downstream task improvements beyond glucose prediction.
