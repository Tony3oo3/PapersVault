## Problem

Standard PINNs enforce Navier-Stokes residuals pointwise, but this local supervision cannot propagate global conservation information through tortuous, topologically complex channels such as TPMS heat-exchanger passages, leading to unstable optimization, mass-conservation violations, and physically inconsistent solutions with relative errors exceeding 90% on challenging geometries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265940/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes 3D spatial coordinates as input, mapped through a deterministic linearly-spaced Fourier feature embedding (60-dimensional) into an MLP, and outputs a 4-vector of velocity components (u, v, w) and pressure at each point. The fluid domain geometry is consumed as a watertight triangle mesh for containment queries and boundary sampling (not as a volumetric mesh), and spherical control volumes are clipped against this mesh to define integration surfaces. Boundary conditions (prescribed inlet velocity, outlet pressure, no-slip walls) and the Reynolds number parameterize the problem, with ground truth generated by Ansys CFX finite-volume simulations on ~10⁷-element tetrahedral meshes.

## Method

MUSA-PINN augments a standard strong-form PINN with weak-form conservation constraints derived by integrating the Navier-Stokes equations over spherical control volumes and applying the divergence theorem, converting volumetric residuals into surface flux-balance integrals evaluated via Monte Carlo sampling on clipped sphere-geometry boundaries. A three-scale subdomain placement strategy positions these control volumes hierarchically: large-radius volumes sampled in the bounding box enforce long-range mass coupling, medium-radius volumes centered along a Mean Curvature Flow skeleton trace dominant transport pathways, and small-radius volumes provide local refinement. Training follows a two-stage schedule where Stage 1 (first 7000 of 9000 epochs) activates only the weak-form continuity constraint to suppress long-range mass imbalance, then Stage 2 enables the weak-form momentum flux-balance term to refine the flow field. The overall loss combines strong-form pointwise residuals, boundary-condition penalties, and multi-scale weak-form continuity and momentum losses with fixed per-scale weights, all optimized with the SOAP optimizer on a single A40 GPU.

## Results

On the four TPMS heat-exchanger channels (Primitive, Gyroid, Diamond, IWP), MUSA-PINN achieves relative ℓ₂ errors of 1.42%–12.46% for velocity magnitude and 1.33%–36.37% for pressure, outperforming all baselines on every geometry. The gains are most dramatic on the hardest cases: on Diamond, MUSA-PINN reaches 12.46% velocity error vs. 86.71% for the best baseline (RoPINN), and on IWP it reaches 6.12% vs. 93.02% (MDPINN-GD), corresponding to relative improvements of up to 99.57% in MSE. At Re=400 on Gyroid, MUSA-PINN maintains 21.08% velocity and 8.09% pressure error while the best baseline (MDPINN-GD) degrades to 69.33% and 61.51%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MUSA-PINN outperforms all four baselines (PINN-PE, hp-VPINN, RoPINN, CoPINN, MDPINN-GD) on every geometry and every metric. The strongest baseline is MDPINN-GD, which enforces global dynamics constraints across subdomains; on Gyroid it achieves 45.03% velocity error, but MUSA-PINN reduces this to 6.13% (an 86.4% relative improvement). On the most challenging Diamond and IWP channels, all point-wise baselines (PINN-PE, RoPINN, CoPINN) collapse to >86% velocity error, while MUSA-PINN stays at 12.46% and 6.12% respectively. The variational weak-form baseline hp-VPINN performs poorly across all geometries (39–96% velocity error), confirming that standard element-based variational formulations do not transfer well to tortuous 3D internal flows without meshing. At the higher Reynolds number (Re=400, Gyroid), MUSA-PINN's advantage widens further: 21.08% velocity error vs. 69.33% for MDPINN-GD, an 86.85% relative improvement in pressure error (8.09% vs. 61.51%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PINN framework for Navier-Stokes by combining strong-form pointwise residuals with weak-form integral conservation constraints, departing from prior weak-form PINNs (hp-VPINN, V-PINN, Deep Ritz) that rely on predefined mesh elements or domain decomposition. The key methodological departure is the use of clipped spherical control volumes with Monte Carlo surface sampling to enforce flux-balance residuals, avoiding volumetric meshing while providing globally informative supervision. The multi-scale placement strategy—particularly the skeleton-aware medium-scale volumes extracted via Mean Curvature Flow—distinguishes this work from existing multi-scale PINNs that target spectral bias or stiff coupling rather than geometric topology. The hybrid strong-weak formulation (retaining pointwise residuals alongside integral constraints) and the two-stage continuity-then-momentum training schedule are additional departures from both pure strong-form and pure weak-form baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: standard PINNs collapse on topologically complex 3D industrial geometries (heat exchangers, cooling plates) where CFD meshing is prohibitively expensive, and the demonstrated 93% error reduction with preserved mass conservation makes mesh-free surrogate simulation viable for parameterized design loops. However, the control-volume placement remains heuristic (fixed radii, skeleton extraction), no formal convergence guarantee is provided, and the additional training cost (~26 hours vs. ~10 hours for PINN-PE) limits immediate deployment. Open questions include adaptive/sparse constraint activation, extension to transient and multi-physics (conjugate heat transfer) regimes, and whether the skeleton-aware placement generalizes to geometries without clear channel topology.
