## Problem

In multi-agent LLM pipelines, each agent's output is embedded into the next agent's prompt, forcing the serving system to recompute KV caches from scratch during prefill despite the content having already been processed during the upstream agent's decoding phase. Existing KV cache reuse methods—prefix caching (requiring strict positional alignment) and pre-computed caching (requiring static, offline content)—fail in this dynamic decode-to-prefill setting where the same tokens appear under different preceding contexts, leaving full prefill as the only viable option and causing TTFT to grow quadratically with the number of interaction turns.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-RoPE decoding KV caches from the upstream agent, along with two auxiliary states accumulated during that agent's decoding: a single-layer hidden state at L_start and per-token scalar influence scores (cumulative attention weights). At the downstream agent's prefill stage, these are combined with the new prefix context (the downstream agent's system prompt and task instructions wrapping the upstream output). The output is a rectified KV cache in which the vast majority of layer-token entries are directly reused (after RoPE re-application for positional alignment), while only a sparse subset of tokens within a critical middle-layer range are recomputed, yielding generation quality comparable to full prefill.

## Method

RelayCaching is a training-free, two-component method. First, an offline layer-range profiler exploits a U-shaped value-cosine-similarity profile (discovered empirically) to identify a critical middle-layer range [L_start, L_end] where deviations concentrate, and a detection layer L_det where inter-layer Spearman rank correlation of token-wise deviations has stabilized. Second, at runtime, a token selector operating at L_det combines two criteria: deviation-based selection (tokens whose value-cosine deviation exceeds τ_dev × mean deviation) and influence-based selection (tokens receiving high cumulative attention from subsequent decoding steps, plus a fixed suffix of K_suf trailing tokens to handle boundary effects). The union of these two sets is recomputed only within [L_det, L_end], while layers in [L_start, L_det) are fully recomputed from cached hidden states to enable detection, and all layers outside [L_start, L_end] are reused directly. This selective rectification achieves over 80% KV cache reuse and up to 4.7× TTFT reduction with negligible accuracy loss across reasoning, coding, and knowledge tasks.

## Results

On GSM8K with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, RelayCaching achieves 85.17% accuracy with 4 agents (vs. 84.69% for full prefill), while maintaining ≥80% KV cache reuse. On HumanEval with Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct, it matches full prefill at 87.58% pass@1 with 4 agents. In controlled TTFT experiments on AIME24 with Qwen3-0.6B, RelayCaching achieves up to 4.71× speedup over full prefill at Agent 5 (104.8 ms vs. 493.9 ms), with sub-linear latency scaling (2.6× growth vs. 5.8× for full prefill) as context accumulates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RelayCaching outperforms all three KV-reuse baselines across all three benchmarks. Against CacheBlend, RelayCaching gains +10.44 pp on GSM8K (85.17% vs. 74.73%, 4 agents) and +1.28 pp on MMLU (67.97% vs. 66.67%), as CacheBlend's value-norm-based detection fails to capture the value-direction mismatch that governs decode-to-prefill deviations. Against EPIC, the gap is even larger: +26.75 pp on GSM8K (85.17% vs. 58.42%) and +24.18 pp on MMLU (67.97% vs. 43.79%), because EPIC's fixed 16-token prefix recomputation cannot adapt to content-sensitive deviations induced by prefix shifts. Against KVCOMM, the closest competitor, RelayCaching leads by +0.26 pp on GSM8K, +1.96 pp on MMLU, and +2.49 pp on HumanEval (4 agents, greedy), while also maintaining a more stable reuse rate (78–83% vs. KVCOMM's 53–70% under stochastic decoding). In TTFT, RelayCaching achieves 4.71× speedup at Agent 5 versus CacheBlend's 3.10×, and its auxiliary memory overhead is <50 MB compared to KVCOMM's 3.3–8.2 GB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant KV cache reuse paradigms—prefix caching (vLLM, SGLang) which requires strict positional alignment, and pre-computed caching (CacheBlend, EPIC, KVLink) which assumes static offline content—by introducing a third mechanism: direct decode-to-prefill KV transfer. While KVCOMM is the first to address cascading context redundancy in multi-agent pipelines, it relies on retrieval-based anchor matching with external samples; RelayCaching instead exploits the inherent similarity between an agent's own decoding KV and the downstream agent's prefill KV, requiring no external reference. The selective rectification strategy (layer-range profiling + dual-criterion token selection) is novel, drawing on the empirical observation that deviations are U-shaped across layers and sparse across tokens, but the underlying infrastructure (PagedAttention-style KV management, RoPE position correction) is standard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As multi-agent LLM pipelines become the dominant deployment paradigm for complex tasks, the quadratic prefill cost from cascading context redundancy is a practical bottleneck that this paper addresses with a 4.7× TTFT reduction and >80% KV reuse at negligible accuracy cost. The training-free, plug-and-play nature makes it immediately deployable in existing serving stacks. Key limitations include the homogeneous-model constraint (all agents must share architecture and tokenizer), the need for offline layer-range profiling, and diminishing returns on short reusable segments. Open questions include extending to heterogeneous multi-model workflows, online adaptation of layer/token selection criteria, and whether the sub-linear scaling advantage persists at extreme context lengths beyond 12K tokens.
