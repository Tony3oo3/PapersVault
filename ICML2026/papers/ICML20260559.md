## Problem

Render-supervised 3D VAEs like TripoSF supervise only the visible geometry per view, yet still decode and attend over all frustum-contained voxels, wasting computation on voxels that are behind the visible surface and never contribute to the render. This makes high-resolution (1024³) training memory-prohibitive and leaves many latent voxels under-constrained. The paper asks: what is the minimum set of voxels actually needed to reconstruct the visible surface from a given viewpoint?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260559/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The VAE takes a raw (possibly non-watertight) mesh, voxelizes it, and aggregates oriented point-cloud features per voxel via a frozen PointNet to form sparse input voxels. The decoder outputs a SparseFlex representation (per-corner SDF values, deformations, and Dual Marching Cubes interpolation weights) at 1024³ resolution from 512³ trunks, which is extracted into a mesh. For image-to-3D generation, a condition image provides DINO features to a rectified-flow model operating in the VAE's latent space.

## Method

The core mechanism is depth-driven voxel carving performed directly in the structured latent space before decoding: each latent voxel center is projected into the rendered depth map, and voxels whose projected depth exceeds the surface depth by a threshold r are discarded (with a 3×3×3 neighborhood retained for boundary robustness), so the decoder and attention layers process only view-consistent geometry. An adaptive zooming strategy then randomly selects a visibility ratio α, finds the αN closest voxels to a random seed in camera space, refines a bounding box via binary search, and rescales the camera intrinsics to zoom into that region—stabilizing VRAM across shapes and viewpoints while forcing the model to learn high-frequency local detail. The VAE uses a sparse transformer with shifted window attention (from TRELLIS) and self-pruning upsampling modules for 4× resolution increase, trained with render losses (depth/normal/mask L1, SSIM, LPIPS), a new sparse-voxel total variation loss to suppress holes, and a brief TSDF warmup loss for early convergence when render gradients are unavailable.

## Results

On the Dora Benchmark, Focusing outperforms TripoSF at both 512³ and 1024³ resolutions across all four detail levels in Chamfer Distance (CD) and F-score. At 1024³ resolution, Focusing achieves a mean CD of 1.294×10⁻⁵ (L1) and 2.264×10⁻⁵ (L4) versus TripoSF's 1.315 and 2.431, while F-score improves from 0.957 to 0.958 (L1) and 0.873 to 0.886 (L4). The method also reduces peak VRAM from 78 GB (carving only) to 49 GB (full pipeline with α_min=0.15) at 512³ resolution, and enables 1024³ training on a single 80 GB A100 GPU. In some high-detail cases, Focusing's 512³ output recovers geometric details more accurately than TripoSF's 1024³ result.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is TripoSF (He et al., ICCV 2025), the only other method supporting raw (possibly non-watertight) mesh input. Focusing wins on both CD and F-score at every detail level and both resolutions; the gap is most pronounced at L4 (finest detail), where CD drops from 3.107 to 2.806 at 512³ and from 2.431 to 2.264 at 1024³. In the appendix (Table 4), comparisons with TRELLIS, Dora, and Direct3D-S2 are grouped by input type: on raw meshes, all three methods fail catastrophically (CD > 200×10⁻⁵) because they require watertight inputs, while Focusing and TripoSF remain in the 1.3–3.1 range. On watertight meshes (processed via Dora's ε-level-set), Focusing₁₀₂₄ achieves the best CD at L4 (2.258 vs. Direct3D-S2's 2.308 and Dora's 3.217), though the advantage over Direct3D-S2 is modest at coarser levels. TRELLIS, which uses image features as input, is only comparable on visible regions and shows inflated CD at L3–L4 due to its dense-grid mesh extraction artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TripoSF/SparseFlex framework, which replaced lossy SDF-based supervision with rendering losses (depth, normal, mask) to handle non-watertight meshes, and the TRELLIS structured sparse-voxel latent representation with shifted window attention. The key departure is moving voxel pruning from the frustum/decoded-mesh level (as in TripoSF's Frustum-aware Sectional Voxel Training) into the structured latent space itself via depth-driven carving, combined with an adaptive zooming mechanism that adjusts camera intrinsics rather than near/far planes to control active voxel count. This reframes the efficiency problem: instead of asking which voxels are inside the frustum, the paper asks which voxels are actually visible at the rendered depth, a strictly tighter criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: enabling 1024³-resolution VAE training with 512³ trunks on 50 GB VRAM (vs. OOM without carving) lowers the hardware barrier for high-resolution 3D VAE research. The view-consistent sparsity principle is modular and could in principle be integrated into the downstream rectified-flow or diffusion stage, letting generative models allocate capacity to geometrically relevant regions. However, the method still depends on point normals and struggles with sub-voxel thin structures, and the quantitative gains over TripoSF (e.g., CD 1.294 vs. 1.315 at 1024³ L1) are modest, so the broader question of whether rendering-based supervision can match SDF supervision in fidelity remains open.
