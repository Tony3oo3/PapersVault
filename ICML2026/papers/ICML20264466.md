## Problem

Training multimodal models from scratch requires large-scale aligned paired data that is often impractical to collect, and existing fusion approaches entangle shared cross-modal information with modality-specific signals, limiting interpretability and robustness to missing modalities. While parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA and LoReFT address the data-efficiency gap for unimodal adaptation, no framework efficiently fuses pretrained unimodal encoders while explicitly and interpretablely decoupling shared from modality-specific information in a self-supervised manner.

## Input / Output

MultiLoReFT consumes frozen pretrained unimodal encoder outputs (e.g., DINO 768-d for images, BERT/LaBSE 768-d for text, Wav2Vec 2.0 768-d for audio, VideoMAE 768-d for video) and produces fine-tuned representations Φ₁, Φ₂ of the same dimensionality, each decomposable via learned low-rank projections into a shared component z_s and modality-specific components z_m1, z_m2. Training is fully unsupervised—no task labels are used—so the method can be applied to any paired multimodal corpus without annotation. For downstream use, the fine-tuned representations are simply concatenated and fed to a lightweight classifier, and under missing-modality inference the shared projection of the available modality substitutes for the absent one.

## Method

MultiLoReFT learns three low-rank projection matrices (R_s, R_m1, R_m2) that define orthogonal subspaces for shared and modality-specific information, along with lightweight nonlinear transforms f_si, f_mi that edit representations within each subspace. The fine-tuned representation is computed as Φ_i = h_i + R_s^T(f_si(h_i) − R_s h_i) + R_mi^T(f_mi(h_i) − R_mi h_i), so only low-rank edits are applied while the bulk of the pretrained embedding is preserved. Training is driven by a composite self-supervised objective combining (i) an HSIC-based independence loss enforcing statistical independence between shared and modality-specific projections, (ii) a Frobenius-norm orthogonality loss on the projection matrices to ensure geometric disjointness, and (iii) an InfoNCE mutual-information loss that preserves information from the original unimodal embeddings and enforces cross-modal consistency of the shared component. An adaptive SVD-based pruning mechanism dynamically reduces each subspace rank by removing low-energy directions (capped at 10% per step), and GradNorm automatically balances the three loss terms without manual weight tuning.

## Results

On multimodal prediction (Table 3), MultiLoReFT achieves 76.0% emotion accuracy on CREMA-D, 61.1% humor accuracy on UR-FUNNY, and 58.3% on UR-FUNNY raw video, outperforming all fusion and disentanglement baselines. On the decoupling evaluation (Tables 1–2), MultiLoReFT achieves the largest separation gap (Δ) between the correct and incorrect subspaces across all datasets, e.g., Δ=38.3 on Simulation II shared factor vs. 0.0 for both DRIM-U and APOLLO, and Δ=75.9 on CREMA-D Age vs. 0.4 (DRIM-U) and −8.5 (APOLLO). The method also matches or slightly exceeds SOTA fusion models: 76.0 vs. 75.13 (MMPareto) on CREMA-D and 61.1 vs. 60.5 (FactorCL) on UR-FUNNY.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiLoReFT outperforms both disentanglement baselines (DRIM-U and APOLLO) on every dataset for both decoupling quality and downstream prediction. On CREMA-D emotion, MultiLoReFT reaches 76.0% vs. 69.3% (DRIM-U) and 46.8% (APOLLO); on UR-FUNNY humor, 61.1% vs. 59.3% and 50.6% respectively. The decoupling advantage is most dramatic on the shared factor: on Simulation II, MultiLoReFT achieves a Δ of 38.3 while both DRIM-U and APOLLO collapse to Δ=0.0, indicating complete failure to isolate shared information. Against classical fusion baselines, MultiLoReFT beats Late Fusion by 1.1–7.6 points and Multiplicative Interactions by 0.2–5.2 points across datasets. It also marginally surpasses the strongest SOTA fusion models: MMPareto (75.13 on CREMA-D) and FactorCL (60.5 on UR-FUNNY), suggesting that explicit subspace decoupling provides a small but consistent edge over task-specific fusion architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiLoReFT extends LoReFT (Wu et al., 2024), which applies low-rank projection-based edits to intermediate representations of a single pretrained model, into the multimodal setting by learning separate shared and modality-specific low-rank subspaces across multiple frozen unimodal encoders. It departs from prior disentanglement frameworks (APOLLO, DRIM-U, FactorCL) that rely on autoencoders, adversarial training, or supervised contrastive objectives, instead using purely structural constraints—HSIC independence, subspace orthogonality, and InfoNCE mutual-information preservation—in a self-supervised regime. The adaptive SVD-based rank pruning mechanism is a novel addition not present in prior PEFT or disentanglement methods, dynamically adjusting subspace dimensionality during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MultiLoReFT offers a practical, parameter-efficient path to interpretable multimodal representations in data-scarce settings (e.g., healthcare), where collecting large paired multimodal datasets is infeasible and interpretability is essential for decision-making. The finding that simple concatenation of the fine-tuned representations matches or exceeds purpose-built SOTA fusion models (MMPareto, FactorCL) suggests that explicit disentanglement may be a more efficient route to multimodal performance than increasingly complex fusion architectures. Key limitations include dependence on pretrained encoder quality, restriction to bimodal evaluation in the main results, and unresolved interpretability questions for partially-shared factors in three or more modalities. Future work on integrating MultiLoReFT with joint pretraining and scaling to larger multimodal corpora could broaden its applicability.
