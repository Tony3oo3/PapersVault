## Problem

Wasserstein metrics are increasingly used as image similarity scores, particularly in high-noise domains like cryo-EM, where they appear empirically more robust than the Euclidean norm. However, no prior work had provided a theoretical characterization of how Wasserstein distances between discrete grid measures respond to pixel-wise additive noise, leaving the source of their apparent robustness unexplained.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262264/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n×n pixel images (treated as signed discrete measures on a toroidal grid with cyclic boundary conditions) corrupted by zero-sum Gaussian noise with per-pixel standard deviation σ, and produces finite-sample expectation bounds on the signed Wasserstein discrepancy between noisy and clean images. The zero-sum constraint on noise (sum of all noise pixels equals zero) is essential to avoid the mass-rescaling complications that arise with standard i.i.d. Gaussian noise, and the signed-measure formulation accommodates negative pixel values that appear after noise addition.

## Method

The core technique is a multiscale dyadic partition argument: the pixel grid is recursively split into quadrants, and a suboptimal transport coupling is constructed at each scale by matching mass imbalances across the four children of each cell. For W₁, the Kantorovich-Rubinstein dual is combined with the dyadic bound (Weed & Bach, 2019) to show the expected discrepancy scales as O(nσ log₂ n). For p > 1, the key result is that E[(W_p^±)ᵖ] ≤ O(nσ), yielding by Jensen's inequality a bound of O((nσ)^(1/p)) — a square-root dependence on σ for p=2, in contrast to the linear σ scaling of L². The signed Wasserstein discrepancy W_p^±(μ,ν) = W_p(μ₊+ν₋, ν₊+μ₋) (Mainini, 2012) is the object of study, and the absence of a triangle inequality for p>1 necessitates a separate proof strategy via suboptimal couplings relating W_p^± to W₁ of the clean measures.

## Results

The paper establishes that the signed 2-Wasserstein discrepancy between a clean image and its noisy counterpart scales as O((nσ)^(1/2)), i.e., with the square root of the noise standard deviation σ, compared to the linear O(n²σ²) scaling of the L² metric (Theorem 3.5). For the 1-Wasserstein case, the deviation between noisy and clean inter-image distances is bounded by O(σn log₂n) (Theorem 3.7). Empirically, on 32×32 DOTMark microscopy images, the W₂ distance ratio (noisy/clean) remains closest to 1 across noise levels from 10⁻⁷ to 10⁻¹, while L² diverges first. On cryo-EM projections of E. Coli hsp90, W₂ tolerates approximately 3.5× more additive noise than W₁ before confusing noise with a spatial shift, and preserves the global diagonal structure of pairwise distance matrices under heavy noise where L² degrades into a random pattern.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three distance metrics—L², W₁, and W₂—on the same image pairs under identical zero-sum Gaussian noise. W₂ consistently outperforms both L² and W₁ in noise robustness: its distance ratio stays closest to 1 over the widest range of noise levels on DOTMark images, and its empirical scaling exponent of ~0.5 matches the theoretical σ^(1/2) bound from Theorem 3.5. L² is the least robust, with its distance ratio sharply diverging from 1 at the lowest noise levels, consistent with its linear σ scaling. W₁ sits between the two, with its O(σn log₂n) bound confirmed empirically. In the cryo-EM case study, both W₁ and W₂ preserve the geometric structure of pairwise distance matrices under heavy noise, while L² completely loses it; however, W₂ is superior to W₁ in distinguishing genuine spatial shifts from additive noise, tolerating ~3.5× more noise before the two effects become confounded. The paper also identifies a peculiar non-monotonicity of W₂ (the 'decreasing distance phenomenon') where increasing noise can actually reduce the measured distance for sparse images, a consequence of the lack of triangle inequality for p>1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mainini's (2012) definition of the signed Wasserstein discrepancy W_p^± for signed measures and on the dyadic multiscale bound from Weed & Bach (2019), which serves as the core technical tool in all main theorem proofs. It positions itself as the first work to analyze the noise robustness of Wasserstein metrics when images are treated as discrete measures on a fixed pixel grid, filling a gap between the empirical observation that OT distances are more noise-robust than L2 (noted in cryo-EM applications by Riahi et al., 2023; Singer & Yang, 2024) and a rigorous theoretical explanation. The zero-sum Gaussian noise model (Assumption 3.2) is a deliberate simplification that avoids the rescaling complications of i.i.d. noise, and the authors note that unbalanced OT (Appendix B) could handle the more general case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean σ^(1/p) scaling for the signed p-Wasserstein discrepancy (vs. linear σ for L2) provides the first theoretical justification for the empirical preference of OT metrics in noisy imaging, with direct implications for cryo-EM where signal-to-noise ratios are extremely low. The authors acknowledge that their bounds are not tight and that the lack of a triangle inequality for p>1 signed Wasserstein complicates the analysis of E[W_p^±(μ_ε,ν_ε)] − E[W_p^±(μ,ν)], leaving a gap between upper and lower bounds. The counterintuitive 'decreasing distance' phenomenon—where added noise can reduce W_p between sparse images by providing transport 'stepping stones'—raises open questions about the reliability of W_p as a similarity measure in very noisy regimes. Future work should investigate whether higher p values (which the theory suggests are more robust) are practically beneficial and whether tighter bounds can be established.
