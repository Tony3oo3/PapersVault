## Problem

Existing AI assistance systems correct immediate behavioral errors through action-level steering (alerts, overrides, shared autonomy) but do not diagnose or correct the underlying misconceptions that cause systematic, repeated mistakes. This paper formalizes AI assistance as knowledge correction: inferring which components of a human's structured task knowledge are flawed and producing minimal, interpretable corrections so that post-guidance behavior aligns with an expert on future task instances, rather than merely fixing the current move.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three symbolic inputs: the expert's PDDL domain definition (decomposed into individual code blocks such as action definitions and object type assignments), the expert's symbolic plan, and the student's symbolic plan. Each PDDL code block is treated as an atomic knowledge component, and the system outputs a binary gap indicator per component plus, for each flagged component, a corrected PDDL block representing the student's erroneous variant. A predefined mapping then translates the corrected PDDL blocks into natural-language guidance for the user. All inputs are encoded by a frozen CodeT5+ encoder into fixed-dimensional embeddings before processing.

## Method

SENSEI is a two-stage pipeline that decouples localization from correction. Stage 1 (localization) uses a 3-layer MLP to predict, per PDDL component, whether the student's behavior is inconsistent with that component, taking as input the expert/student behavior embeddings, their difference, the component embedding, and a global knowledge embedding. Stage 2 (editing) uses a 2-layer MLP to predict a latent edit vector applied to the expert component embedding (gated by the localization output), and a finetuned CodeT5+ decoder generates the student's PDDL text from the edited embedding. Training jointly optimizes three objectives: a localization BCE loss augmented with a mixup generalization loss (linearly interpolating behavior embeddings from two single-misconception students to simulate multi-misconception interference), an alignment MSE loss on true-gap components plus an L2 no-op loss on non-gap components, and an autoregressive cross-entropy loss for the decoder. This structured diagnose-then-edit design constrains the editor to act only on predicted gaps, preventing hallucinated modifications to correct components, while the mixup loss enables zero-shot compositional generalization to multi-misconception trajectories despite training exclusively on single-misconception data.

## Results

SENSEI achieves the best system-level F1 (Sys_F1) across all three domains: 0.709 on Breakfast, 0.726 on Overcooked, and 0.561 on Rover, while maintaining near-perfect edit accuracy (Edit_acc = 0.97–1.0) once a gap is correctly localized. Localization F1 (Loc_F1) reaches 0.731/0.726/0.561 respectively, substantially outperforming random baselines (0.188–0.500) and the monolithic end-to-end decoder (0.033–0.465). In the user study with 20 participants, SENSEI corrected 89.5% of induced misconceptions (Sys_rec) and improved plan alignment (IoU) from 0.736 to 0.844 after guidance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SENSEI decisively outperforms all baselines on Sys_F1 across all three domains. Against the monolithic End-to-End decoder, SENSEI's modular design (separating localization from editing) is critical: the end-to-end model achieves near-zero localization recall on Overcooked (Loc_rec = 0.042) and always edits components regardless of necessity (Edit_no-op = 0.0), yielding Sys_F1 of only 0.028 vs. SENSEI's 0.726. Off-the-shelf LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-5.2, Llama-3.3-70B) struggle severely with producing valid PDDL corrections, achieving Sys_F1 of 0.0 on Rover and at most 0.126 on Breakfast, with high abstention rates (Edit_no-op up to 0.760). Random localization baselines achieve near-perfect recall by flagging almost all components as gaps, but their precision collapses (e.g., Random-Loc Loc_prec = 0.040 on Overcooked), producing Sys_false rates of 0.957–1.0. SENSEI approaches the Oracle-Loc upper bound on localization (Loc_F1 gap of ~0.27–0.44) and matches it on edit accuracy in Overcooked and Rover (Edit_acc = 1.0), with the remaining gap attributable to imperfect localization rather than correction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of intelligent tutoring (misconception diagnosis via Repair Theory), human-AI coordination (behavior steering, shared autonomy), and explainable AI planning (model reconciliation). Prior work in these areas either steers behavior without diagnosing the underlying misconception, assumes a known or finite set of human models for coordination, or explains robot behavior given assumed model differences. SENSEI is positioned as the first framework to solve the inverse diagnostic problem: inferring an unknown student's structured symbolic knowledge model from behavioral traces and synthesizing minimal, interpretable corrections to specific knowledge components. Methodologically, it repurposes a frozen CodeT5+ encoder for PDDL embedding, a lightweight MLP for component-level gap localization, and a latent-space editing + decoding pipeline for correction synthesis, with a mixup-based training trick to enable compositional generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper shifts the AI-assistance paradigm from trajectory-level steering ('fix the move') to structured knowledge-model correction ('fix the mind'), with the practical implication that corrections transfer to future task instances rather than only the current episode. The zero-shot compositional generalization (trained on single misconceptions, generalizing to 2–3 overlapping misconceptions) and the 90% real-human correction rate in the user study are encouraging signals for practical deployment. Key limitations include reliance on a normative expert reference (potentially penalizing valid alternative strategies), restriction to PDDL planning domains, and a precision-recall trade-off where high recall increases erroneous corrections. Open questions include value-aware gap weighting (prioritizing gaps by downstream behavioral impact), extension beyond symbolic planning to open-ended settings, and reducing the rate of hallucinated misconceptions in real-world deployment.
