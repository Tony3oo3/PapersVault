## Problem

Existing fairness measures for sequential decision-making, such as CATE and path-specific effects, either conflate all causal pathways (risking cancellation of opposing effects) or exclude spurious associations, failing to decompose total observed disparity into contributions from direct, indirect, and spurious discriminatory mechanisms. This paper formulates an online causally-fair learning problem where fine-grained structural counterfactual measures (Ctf-DE, Ctf-IE, Ctf-SE) serve as multiple a priori unknown roundwise constraints, and establishes sublinear regret and violation bounds without requiring Slater's condition.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner observes a factual context (sensitive attribute, confounders, mediators) and selects a decision from a finite set, receiving a binary reward. The fairness constraints, however, depend on expected rewards under both the factual and counterfactual contexts (e.g., had the sensitive attribute taken a different value), so the learner must accumulate estimates across contexts it has not directly observed. The method requires known conditional probabilities P(m|a,w) and P(w|a) from domain knowledge or offline data, user-defined fairness thresholds, and in the logistic setting a known bounded feature map; it outputs a probability distribution over decisions per round along with theoretical guarantees on cumulative regret and maximum constraint violation.

## Method

For non-parametric contextual bandits, the algorithm combines optimistic plug-in estimators of the three counterfactual effects (with Hoeffding-style bonuses) to construct an estimated feasible set, then performs one OMD step followed by Bregman projection onto that set and an epsilon-greedy exploration step to ensure coverage of counterfactual contexts. For parametric logistic bandits, the method uses OMD-based parameter estimation (achieving O(1) per-round complexity versus O(t) for MLE) to build a convex confidence ellipsoid, and introduces a policy-independent bonus ξ_t(d) that makes the relaxed fairness constraint linear in the policy while guaranteeing the true feasible set is contained in the estimated set with high probability. A key technical challenge is that the fairness of an action in the current context depends on its expected reward across many other (potentially counterfactual) contexts, necessitating an exploration phase (random play for T₀ rounds in the logistic setting, or epsilon-greedy in the non-parametric setting) to ensure the feature vectors collectively span the parameter space. The resulting regret bound for logistic bandits has leading term Õ(n√T), matching the unconstrained rate, and is independent of the number of constraints and Slater's margin.

## Results

In the non-parametric contextual bandits setting, Algorithm 1 achieves regret $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{\max\{1/2,\,1-\omega\}})$ and cumulative constraint violations $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{\max\{(1+\omega)/2,\,1-\omega\}}/\sqrt{\gamma_1})$ without Slater's condition. In the parametric logistic bandits setting, Algorithm 2 achieves regret $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(n\sqrt{T} + T_0)$ and violations $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(T_0 + T\sqrt{n\kappa/(\gamma_2 T_0)})$, where the leading regret term $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(n\sqrt{T})$ matches the best-known unconstrained logistic bandit rates, with $\mathcal{O}(1)$ per-round time complexity. Empirically on a synthetic SCM with 10 arms over $T=10{,}000$ rounds, the proposed method (DO-LogB) shows a large and growing gap in penalized cumulative regret versus all baselines, while attaining cumulative rewards on par with unconstrained methods and dominating the best-fair oracle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines on a synthetic SCM with 10 arms: GLM-UCB (unconstrained generalized linear bandits), GLB-OMD (unconstrained logistic bandits with $\mathcal{O}(1)$ per-round cost), and CCLB (causal logistic bandits with a single CATE constraint). On penalized cumulative regret (where unfair actions yield zero reward), the proposed DO-LogB method shows a large and growing advantage over all three baselines as the horizon increases; notably, CCLB exhibits even larger penalized regret than the unconstrained GLM-UCB and GLB-OMD, despite being designed for a fairness-constrained setting, because its CATE-based constraint is pathway-agnostic and susceptible to mediation inconsistency. On cumulative rewards, DO-LogB attains rewards on the same order as the unconstrained methods and consistently dominates the best-fair oracle. On running time, GLB-OMD is slightly faster than DO-LogB (which must additionally solve the constraint projection), while CCLB has the highest computational cost due to its $\mathcal{O}(t)$ per-round complexity versus $\mathcal{O}(1)$ for the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal fairness bandit line initiated by Huang et al. (2022b) and Chen et al. (2025) by replacing the pathway-agnostic CATE constraint with the fine-grained Ctf-DE/Ctf-IE/Ctf-SE decomposition from Plečko & Bareinboim (2024), and generalizing from a single constraint to multiple simultaneous constraints. For the parametric logistic setting, it adopts the OMD-based confidence set construction from Zhang et al. (2025) to achieve O(1) per-round complexity, in contrast to the O(t) cost of the MLE-based approach in Chen et al. (2025). The non-parametric setting builds on the epsilon-greedy OMD framework but introduces a key technical novelty: the fairness of one action in the current context depends on reward estimates across many counterfactual contexts, requiring a carefully designed optimistic bonus that couples factual and counterfactual estimation errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition into direct, indirect, and spurious effects aligns with legal frameworks (disparate impact doctrine in US/EU anti-discrimination law), making the constraints more interpretable and actionable than a single CATE threshold. The main open challenge is that the fairness of an action in one context depends on its performance across many other (counterfactual) contexts, forcing the learner to explore actions that may be suboptimal or even unfair in those other contexts. The paper leaves open whether the mandatory exploration phase can be eliminated for structured feature maps, and whether the framework extends to MDPs where the counterfactual context space grows with the horizon. Empirical validation is limited to a single synthetic SCM, so real-world applicability remains untested.
