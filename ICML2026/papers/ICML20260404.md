## Problem

Evaluation of Text-to-Audio-Video generation remains fragmented: existing benchmarks either isolate video quality (VBench, EvalCrafter) or audio fidelity (AudioCaps, TTA-Bench), and even emerging joint benchmarks (JavisBench, VABench, Verse-Bench) lack fine-grained coverage of cross-modal temporal synchronization, instruction following under compositional constraints, and physically grounded realism. No prior benchmark simultaneously diagnoses whether generated sounds correspond to visible events, whether multiple audio sources synchronize with complex visual interactions, and whether models faithfully follow detailed multi-constraint prompts while maintaining perceptual plausibility.

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds 500 dense text prompts (average ~154 tokens, ~10 constraint points each, spanning 4+ subjects and 3+ event units on average) to 15 T2AV systems, which produce synchronized video-audio pairs at 720p (480p for JavisDiT, 1080p for Kling-2.6). The evaluation framework then consumes each generated video-audio pair and emits a structured score vector across 13 objective metrics (video technical/aesthetic, audio aesthetic/speech quality, text-audio/video alignment, audio-video alignment, desync, lipsync) and 4 subjective MLLM-judged dimensions (IF Video, IF Audio, Video Realism, Audio Realism), each further decomposed into 17+5 sub-dimensions respectively. The IF Audio score incorporates a penalty term that reduces the speech sub-score when unwanted speech appears in contexts where it should not be present.

## Method

T2AV-Compass combines a taxonomy-driven prompt construction pipeline with a dual-level evaluation framework. Prompts are built via three stages: (1) multi-source aggregation (VidProM, Kling AI, LMArena, Shot2Story) with all-mpnet-base-v2 embedding deduplication (cosine ≥ 0.8) and square-root cluster sampling to suppress over-represented topics; (2) Gemini-2.5-Pro rewriting that injects visual, motion, acoustic, and cinematographic constraints, increasing average length from 54 to ~154 tokens; and (3) a video-inversion stream where 100 real YouTube clips (4–10 s, post-Oct 2024) are densely captioned by Gemini-2.5-Pro with human-in-the-loop verification to anchor prompts in physical plausibility. The objective level decomposes performance into video quality (DOVER++ for technical fidelity, Aesthetic Predictor V2.5 for aesthetics), audio quality (Audiobox PQ/CU mean for acoustic aesthetics, NISQA for speech MOS), and cross-modal alignment (CLAP for text-audio, VideoCLIP-XLV2 for text-video, ImageBind for audio-video semantic similarity, Synchformer DeSync for temporal offset, LatentSync for lip-sync). The subjective level employs Gemini-2.5-Pro as a reasoning-first judge that must articulate rationale before scoring on a 5-point scale, evaluating instruction following via per-prompt QA checklists spanning 7 primary dimensions (Attribute, Dynamics, Cinematography, Aesthetics, Relations, World Knowledge, Sound) and 17 sub-dimensions, and realism via five perceptual sub-scores (Motion Smoothness, Object Integrity, Temporal Coherence, Acoustic Artifacts, Material-Timbre Consistency). This checklist-based, reasoning-first protocol is validated against human ratings (L1 distance within ~10% of inter-human agreement on three of four dimensions) and via Bradley-Terry pairwise ranking that reproduces human-derived model ordering.

## Results

On T2AV-Compass (500 prompts), Veo-3.1 achieves the highest overall subjective average of 70.29, followed by Sora-2 (69.83), Kling-2.6 (68.16), and Wan-2.6 (67.68). Among open-source end-to-end models, LTX-2 leads with 63.72 and the best Video Realism (89.95), while Wan-2.6 dominates Instruction Following (IF Video 78.52, IF Audio 74.95). Audio Realism is a universal bottleneck: even the best model (Seedance-1.5) scores only 53.84, far below video realism scores (76–90), and the weakest model (JavisDiT) drops to 14.85. No single model dominates all dimensions, and failure rates on long-narrative prompts (4+ events) reach 63–80% across models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Veo-3.1 leads the overall leaderboard (70.29) but is outperformed by Sora-2 on IF Audio (67.90 vs. 72.86) and by Wan-2.6 on IF Video (76.15 vs. 78.52) and IF Audio (67.90 vs. 74.95), revealing that no closed-source model is uniformly best. Among open-source end-to-end models, LTX-2 (63.72) substantially outperforms Ovi-1.1 (51.14) and JavisDiT (24.41), with LTX-2 achieving the highest Video Realism (89.95) but the lowest IF Audio among top-6 models (64.74). Cascaded pipelines such as Wan-2.2+Hunyuan-Foley match or exceed end-to-end models on Video Realism (89.63 vs. 87.14 for Veo-3.1) but collapse on IF Audio (37.10 vs. 67.90), confirming that fragmented T2V→V2A optimization sacrifices cross-modal instruction adherence. On objective metrics, PixVerse-V5.5 achieves the best temporal synchronization (DS 0.6627) and speech quality (SQ 1.824), while Veo-3.1 leads in video technical fidelity (VT 13.39) and Seedance-1.5 in audio aesthetics (AA 7.403) and A-V alignment (0.2875). The AudioLDM2+MTV pipeline, which generates audio first then video, achieves the highest Text-Audio alignment (0.2698) but the worst video quality (VT 8.066, VA 3.458), illustrating the trade-off inherent in audio-first cascading.

## Relation to Prior Work

T2AV-Compass carves new ground at the intersection of unimodal video benchmarks (VBench, EvalCrafter), unimodal audio benchmarks (TTA-Bench, AudioLDM-Eval), and the small set of emerging joint audio-video benchmarks (JavisBench, Verse-Bench, Harmony-Bench, VABench). It departs from these by (1) targeting pure T2AV (not I2AV or TIA2V) with substantially longer, taxonomy-driven prompts (~154 tokens vs. 50–68 for competitors), (2) introducing a dual-level evaluation that pairs classical signal metrics (CLAP, VideoCLIP, ImageBind, Synchformer, DOVER++, NISQA) with an MLLM-as-a-Judge protocol for instruction following and realism—dimensions absent from all prior T2AV benchmarks—and (3) anchoring 100 prompts in real-world video inversion to enforce physical plausibility. The MLLM-judge component draws on the broader LLM-as-a-Judge literature but is the first to apply it systematically to joint audiovisual generation with a reasoning-first, checklist-based protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a stark and consistent 'Audio Realism Bottleneck' across all 15 evaluated systems (best audio realism ≈ 54 vs. best video realism ≈ 90), and reveals that long-narrative prompts with 4+ events drive failure rates to 63–80% even for frontier models like Veo-3.1 and Sora-2—identifying temporal coherence over multi-event horizons as a critical unsolved challenge. Open questions include whether native joint audio-visual modeling (vs. cascaded T2V→V2A pipelines) can close the audio realism gap, how to scale evaluation beyond 10-second clips, and whether distilled lightweight judges can reduce the computational cost of the MLLM-judge protocol for iterative model development. Limitations include dependence on closed-source Gemini variants for judging, a 500-prompt scale that may miss extreme long-tail interactions, and the inherent biases of MLLM evaluators on audio realism (where human verification remains necessary).
