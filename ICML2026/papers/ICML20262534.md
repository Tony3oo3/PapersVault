## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for sparse matrix reordering optimize surrogate fill-in objectives but provide no interpretable account of *why* particular fill-in entries appear. The paper asks whether the Fill-Path Theorem can be turned into a concrete, learnable causal structure—specifically, whether blocking a compact set of length-2 candidate triplets is provably sufficient to prevent all fill-in, and whether a neural model can be trained to identify and suppress those structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262534/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the sparsity pattern of a sparse (symmetric or symmetrized) matrix as an undirected graph G(A) = (V, E) and produces two outputs: (1) a per-vertex scalar score vector s, sorted in descending order to yield the elimination permutation π, and (2) binary causal-structure labels for every sampled candidate triplet (u, w, v) indicating whether w is eliminated before both endpoints. The graph is processed through a multi-grid pipeline (coarsening via Graclus clustering + average pooling, then refinement), so the network sees the graph at multiple resolutions before producing the final scores.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution is an inductive reduction of the Fill-Path Theorem: any fill-generating path of arbitrary length collapses to a length-2 candidate triplet (u, w, v) with π(w) < min(π(u), π(v)), formalized as a directed causal structure u ← w → v; Proposition 3.2 then proves that satisfying the inequality π(w) > min(π(u), π(v)) for *every* candidate triplet is necessary and sufficient for zero fill-in. Architecturally, MgKGAT initializes vertex features with an approximate Fiedler vector (via a GNN-based spectral embedding module), then applies L layers of KGAT (Graph Attention Network with Fourier-KAN activations) at each multi-grid level during both coarsening and refinement, with skip-connection fusion between levels. Training uses a causal triplet loss: the negative log-likelihood of the sigmoid-mapped margin z_{uwv} = max(s_u, s_v) − s_w over all sampled candidate triplets, which directly penalizes configurations where the middle vertex would be eliminated before both endpoints. The network is trained end-to-end with Adam on matrices of size 200–500 and evaluated on SuiteSparse matrices up to ~15 million nonzeros.

## Results

On the SuiteSparse benchmark (69 matrices >10,000 rows across 5 problem categories), CTS achieves the best overall fill-in ratio of 85.54, outperforming PFM (96.09), UDNO (91.60), ND (92.73), and Spectral (97.42). On the CPS subset, CTS reduces fill-in by 13× relative to PFM (160.43 vs. 173.87) and 6× relative to ND (160.43 vs. 166.26). In terms of LU factorization time, CTS achieves a 12× speedup over PFM on CFD (5.02 s vs. 7.52 s) and a 16× speedup over Spectral ordering on the same subset (5.02 s vs. 6.19 s), with an overall mean factorization time of 25.10 s versus 688.18 s for natural ordering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTS achieves the best fill-in ratio on 4 of 5 problem categories (2D3D, CPS, CFD, and overall), losing only on SP to UDNO (52.17 vs. 48.64) and on the 'Others' subset to ND (64.90 vs. 62.18). Against the strongest deep-learning baseline PFM, CTS wins on every category, with the largest gap on CPS (160.43 vs. 173.87, a 13× reduction in absolute fill-in count as reported in the paper). Against classical ND (METIS), CTS is superior on 2D3D, CPS, and CFD but slightly worse on 'Others'; against Spectral ordering, CTS dominates across all categories. In LU factorization time, CTS is fastest on CPS (54.24 s), CFD (5.02 s), and 'Others' (15.27 s), while Spectral ordering is marginally faster on 2D3D (9.44 s vs. 10.53 s) and UDNO leads on SP (42.49 s vs. 48.37 s). AMD is consistently the weakest baseline, with fill-in ratios 5–7× higher than CTS and factorization times an order of magnitude slower.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Fill-Path Theorem (Rose et al., 1976) and the spectral embedding module from Gatti et al. (2021), but departs from the broader deep-learning-for-reordering line (UDNO, PFM, AlphaElim) in a key conceptual way: rather than optimizing surrogate fill-in objectives (diagonal concentration, differentiable factorization proxies, or MCTS-based elimination), it encodes the fill-in generation mechanism itself as a set of causal triplet constraints derived from the theorem. The multi-grid GAT backbone with Fourier-KAN activations is a novel architectural combination, but each component (multi-grid coarsening, GAT, KAN) is individually established; the contribution is their integration with a theory-motivated loss that targets the structural cause of fill-in rather than a downstream proxy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Proposition 3.2 equivalence (blocking all length-2 causal triplets ⟺ global no-fill) is a clean theoretical result that gives the learning objective a direct mechanistic interpretation, which prior surrogate-objective methods lack. However, the practical gains are more modest in the mean than the headline 6×/12× figures suggest (e.g., overall fill-in ratio improves from 96.09 to 85.54, roughly 1.12×), and CTS does not outperform UDNO on banded structural matrices. Open questions include why performance varies across matrix families, whether the causal structure framework extends naturally to non-symmetric or parallel factorization settings, and whether the low IoU (2–3%) between predicted and actual fill-in locations limits the interpretability claim.
