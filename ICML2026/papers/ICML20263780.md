## Problem

Federated continual learning of LLMs on edge devices faces a communication-stability-plasticity trilemma: dense uplink of LoRA updates is costly and, as the paper reveals, can actually degrade continual performance by disproportionately transmitting high-conflict layers that overwrite previously consolidated behaviors. Existing PEFT-based FCL methods either isolate adapters per task (sacrificing plasticity) or share indiscriminately (sacrificing stability), without identifying which specific layers drive forgetting or enforcing a principled stability constraint on finite local updates.

## Input / Output

Each client consumes a non-stationary local task data stream and maintains LoRA adapter parameters (rank r=8) plus a compact diagonal Fisher summary (F̄, z̄) updated via exponential moving average at task boundaries—these summaries are never uploaded. The method outputs a sparse subset of LoRA layers selected for uplink under a fixed per-round traffic budget P, along with per-layer certified step sizes that cap updates on conflict-heavy layers; the server then aggregates only the transmitted layers into the global model. The key constraint is that the Fisher summaries and conflict scores are computed entirely client-side, keeping the uplink payload to just the selected LoRA tensors.

## Method

RieSelect decomposes into three layer-wise subroutines. First, it identifies conflict by computing a layer score S_ℓ = Σᵢ vᵢ F̄ᵢ(θᵢ − θ̄ᵢ) where v = F_curr⁻¹g is the Fisher-preconditioned natural-gradient direction and F̄ is the replay-free historical Fisher proxy; layers with S_ℓ < 0 are flagged as conflicting. Second, it adjusts updates on conflicting layers via a closed-form certified safe step size η_safe derived from Riemannian comparison theorems under bounded sectional curvature, guaranteeing the post-update iterate remains within a Fisher-metric safe basin B_ε(θ̄) of radius R_ε = √(2ε); non-conflicting layers receive a lighter quadratic-proxy cap. Third, it selects which layers to upload by solving a 0-1 knapsack that maximizes per-layer learning utility B_ℓ while penalizing persistent conflict frequency p_ℓ and endpoint forgetting Φ_ℓ, subject to the uplink budget P. The theoretical core is a geodesic-quadratic mismatch lemma showing the standard quadratic proxy misestimates intrinsic drift under finite steps, motivating the curvature-aware certificate rather than a simple norm constraint.

## Results

On LLaMA2-7B, RieSelect achieves the best Average Accuracy (AA) across all four task orders: 41.12% (Order 1), 44.46% (Order 2), 73.39% (Order 3), and 74.42% (Order 4), with positive Backward Transfer (BWT) of +5.08, +6.13, and +6.72 on Orders 2–4. Total uplink is only 0.07 GB, a 41–115× reduction compared to baselines (2.93–8.06 GB). On T5-Large, RieSelect improves AA by up to 28.14 points over the best baseline while reducing uplink by up to 53×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RieSelect outperforms all 10 baselines on AA across every task order. The largest margins appear on Long Sequence: on Order 3, RieSelect (73.39 AA) beats the strongest baseline A-GEM (60.38) by 13.01 points, and on Order 4 it leads A-GEM (55.43) by 18.99 points. On SuperNI, gains are smaller but consistent: +2.56 over Replay (38.56) on Order 1 and +3.54 over A-GEM (40.92) on Order 2. RieSelect is the only method achieving positive BWT on Orders 2–4 (+5.08, +6.13, +6.72), whereas all baselines show negative BWT on at least three of four orders. In terms of communication, RieSelect transmits only 0.07 GB total, a 41× reduction over the 2.93 GB used by LoRA/O-LoRA/N-LoRA/C-LoRA/Replay/A-GEM and a 115× reduction over HydraLoRA (8.06 GB). The FCL-specific baselines PILoRA and LoRM, which rely on auxiliary uplink payloads (prototypes, Gram statistics), are outperformed on both AA and BWT while using 4.69–5.86 GB of communication.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of federated continual learning (FCL), Fisher-regularized continual learning (EWC, A-GEM), and PEFT-based continual adaptation (O-LoRA, N-LoRA, C-LoRA, HydraLoRA). It departs from prior FCL methods that either isolate adapters per task or share indiscriminately, and from classical CL safeguards that reason about parameter importance or gradient direction globally, by pinpointing sparse high-conflict layers and treating the per-round uplink budget as a first-class design constraint. The Riemannian safe-basin formulation with a closed-form certified step size and the 0-1 knapsack selection are novel contributions that unify the stability and plasticity objectives under a single geometric framework, building on the Fisher-metric machinery of EWC but extending it to a curvature-aware, layer-granular, budget-constrained setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The less-is-more finding—that beyond a moderate upload ratio, stability loss offsets saturated plasticity gains—challenges a default assumption in federated systems that more communication monotonically improves performance, and the 53–115× uplink reduction with simultaneous accuracy gains is practically significant for bandwidth-constrained edge deployment of LLMs. Limitations include a fixed safe-basin radius across task transitions, reliance on local Fisher storage, and validation restricted to NLP benchmarks with transformer backbones (T5-Large, LLaMA2-7B, Qwen2.5-14B). Open questions include whether adaptive radius selection, non-diagonal Fisher approximations, or extension to multimodal and non-transformer architectures would preserve the conflict-concentration phenomenon and the knapsack-based selection gains.
