## Problem

While Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) improves generalization in supervised learning by seeking flatter minima, directly applying it to offline RL is destabilizing because bootstrapped value targets are non-stationary, causing SAM's flatness pursuit to exacerbate extrapolation errors and disrupt convergence. No prior work has addressed how to make sharpness-aware optimization effective under the unique dynamics of offline RL's bootstrapping mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard offline RL datasets (state, action, reward, next-state tuples) from D4RL MuJoCo and AntMaze benchmarks, and produces a deterministic policy mapping states to continuous actions along with Q-value estimates. Unusually, it requires training an additional parametric error model Δ_φ(s,a) alongside the standard Q-network to estimate cumulative Bellman error bounds per sample, and SAM optimization is applied exclusively to the policy network rather than the value function to avoid propagating Bellman errors.

## Method

Q-SAM resolves the SAM-bootstrapping conflict through a two-stage 'filter-then-optimize' cascade. First, Weighted Samples uses DisCor's recursive Q-value bound Δ_k to compute exponential importance weights w_k that downweight transitions with high cumulative Bellman error, directing the critic update toward samples with reliable target values. Second, Weighted Sharpness replaces SAM's equal-weight objective with a λ-scaled sharpness term λ/(1−λ)·g(φ) + L(π_φ), where λ < 0.5 biases updates toward loss minimization over flatness, preventing the policy from drifting into regions of poor value estimation. The actor update combines both the perturbed SAM gradient and the standard Adam gradient with complementary coefficients, and the two components operate orthogonally—sample weighting stabilizes the data distribution for value learning while sharpness weighting shapes the parameter landscape for policy optimization.

## Results

On D4RL MuJoCo-v2 (9 tasks), IQL+Q-SAM achieves a total normalized score of 772.1, outperforming the IQL baseline (734.6) by +37.5 and CQL+Q-SAM (745.0) by +27.1. On AntMaze-v2 (6 tasks), IQL+Q-SAM reaches 403.3 versus 343.2 for IQL (+60.1), with particularly large gains on antmaze-medium-play (80.8 vs 65.8) and antmaze-large-play (63.3 vs 42.0). Direct SAM application yields only marginal improvements (e.g., IQL+SAM: 751.8 vs IQL: 734.6 on MuJoCo), whereas Q-SAM consistently delivers substantially larger gains across nearly all tasks. In the small-sample (5% data) setting, TD3BC+Q-SAM maintains competitive performance where the baseline degrades significantly, confirming superior generalization under data scarcity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three base offline RL algorithms, Q-SAM consistently improves performance: IQL+Q-SAM (772.1 MuJoCo / 403.3 AntMaze) beats IQL (734.6 / 343.2), CQL+Q-SAM (745.0 / 335.0) beats CQL (704.5 / 304.5), and TD3BC+Q-SAM (732.7 / 163.4) beats TD3BC (688.0 / 116.2). The largest single-task gains appear on hopper-medium (IQL+Q-SAM: 75.3 vs IQL: 67.5, +7.8) and antmaze-large-play (IQL+Q-SAM: 63.3 vs IQL: 42.0, +21.3). Against dedicated generalization methods (Table 3), IQL+Q-SAM achieves a MuJoCo total of 772.1, surpassing POR (721.3), DOGE (728.8), and TSRL (697.1) by 50.8, 43.3, and 75.0 points respectively. However, Q-SAM does not dominate every individual task: DOGE outperforms IQL+Q-SAM on hopper-medium (98.6 vs 75.3) and hopper-medium-expert (102.7 vs 113.4 is actually Q-SAM winning), while POR leads on halfcheetah-medium (48.8 vs 49.2 is Q-SAM winning) — the key takeaway is that Q-SAM's advantage is most pronounced on harder AntMaze tasks and in aggregate, rather than uniformly across all 15 datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) from supervised deep learning for offline RL, adapting two specific components: the weighted sharpness formulation from WSAM (Yue et al., 2023) to control the flatness-vs-minima trade-off, and the Q-value error bounds from DisCor (Kumar et al., 2020a) to identify which samples are stable enough for sharpness-aware updates. It departs from the dominant offline RL literature—which focuses on policy constraints, value regularization, uncertainty estimation, and density ratio correction—by arguing that optimizer geometry (loss landscape flatness) is an underexplored and orthogonal axis for improving generalization. The key conceptual departure is identifying a fundamental conflict between SAM's stationary flatness objective and the non-stationary bootstrapped targets in offline RL, which prior work has not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes optimizer design as a viable and previously neglected direction for offline RL, offering a plug-and-play module (Q-SAM) that integrates into existing actor-critic frameworks without architectural changes. The Local Action Sensitivity (LAS) metric provides a useful diagnostic linking parameter-space flatness to policy smoothness, a connection that was previously only hypothesized. Key limitations include the restriction to the policy network (SAM on the value function destabilizes training), the need for an additional error-model network to compute Δ_k, and the fixed λ hyperparameter. Open questions include adaptive λ scheduling based on value convergence rates, specialized SAM variants for value functions, and whether the approach extends to model-based or distributional offline RL settings.
