## Problem

Replicable reinforcement learning in the episodic (exploration) setting had a large gap between the best upper bound of Õ(S⁷A⁷) and the generative-model lower bound of Õ(S²A²), raising the question of whether replicable exploration is inherently far more expensive than batch learning. This paper resolves the gap for low-horizon tabular MDPs, showing that exploration is not a significant barrier to replicability.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives episodic sample access to an unknown tabular MDP (S states, A actions, horizon H) and a shared random string ξ, interacting with the environment over multiple episodes rather than querying state-action pairs directly. It outputs an ε-optimal policy (a collection of functions π_h: S → A for h ∈ [H]) that is identical across two independent runs with probability at least 1−ρ, using Õ(S²A·poly(H)) samples in the low-horizon regime where H is subpolynomial in S.

## Method

The key insight is to separate exploration from decision-making by introducing 'ignorable state combinations'—a relaxed reachability partition that can be generated sample-efficiently. The exploration phase (REPEXPLORE) runs a non-replicable optimistic Q-learning agent (QEXPLORE) on a zero-reward MDP multiple times to compute fractional estimates of which states are under-explored, then replicably rounds this fractional solution to a discrete set of ignorable states using correlated sampling over the product distribution. The decision-making phase applies a replicable multi-instance best-arm algorithm (exponential mechanism + correlated sampling over the joint action space [A]^S) combined with backward induction over the non-ignorable states, with non-uniform sample budgets allocated according to each state's exploration likelihood to maintain the required TV-distance bound for replicability.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1.3) gives a ρ-replicable (ε,δ)-PAC policy estimator in the episodic setting with sample complexity Õ(S²A/(ρ²ε²)) · poly(H, log(1/δ)), bridging the large gap between the previous best episodic bound Õ(S⁷A⁷) (Eaton et al., 2023) and the generative-model bound Õ(S²A²) (Karbasi et al., 2023). A matching parallel-sampling lower bound of Õ(S²AH²/(ρ²ε²)) (Theorem 1.4) is established via a new optimal lower bound for replicable sign-one-way marginals, resolving an open question of Bun et al. (2023). An unconditional episodic lower bound of Õ(S²H²/(ρ²ε²)) (Theorem 1.5) shows near-optimality in the state space S and is the first unconditional result proving replicable RL is asymptotically harder than standard RL (which requires Õ(SAH³) samples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is against two prior replicable RL results. Against Eaton et al. (2023), which achieved Õ(S⁷A⁷) for replicable episodic RL via phased value iteration, this work improves the state-space dependence from S⁷ to S² and the action dependence from A⁷ to A, a dramatic reduction. Against Karbasi et al. (2023), which achieved Õ(S²A²) in the generative (no-exploration) setting, this work shows that exploration adds only a factor of A (rather than the previously feared S⁵A⁵ gap), essentially closing the gap between the two settings. The unconditional episodic lower bound of Õ(S²) (Theorem 1.5) is the first to prove replicable RL is asymptotically harder than standard non-replicable RL (Õ(SAH³)), a separation that Karbasi et al. (2023) could only establish conditionally on an unproven conjecture. The parallel-sampling lower bound (Theorem 1.4) also resolves an open question from Bun et al. (2023) on replicable sign-one-way marginals, improving their Õ(n) bound to the optimal Õ(nρ⁻²ε⁻²).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of replicability theory (Impagliazzo et al., 2022) and sample-efficient RL, directly addressing the gap between the generative-model upper bound of O(S²A²) (Karbasi et al., 2023) and the episodic upper bound of O(S⁷A⁷) (Eaton et al., 2023). The key technical departure from prior work is the introduction of 'ignorable state combinations' — a relaxed reachability partition that can be generated sample-efficiently and replicably rounded via correlated sampling over a fractional solution, drawing on reward-free RL ideas (Jin et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2020) and the exponential mechanism / correlated sampling paradigm (Ghazi et al., 2021; Holenstein, 2007). Unlike Eaton et al.'s phased value iteration (which estimates each state-action pair independently at O(S³A³) cost), this paper estimates only the state value function replicably via a multi-instance best-arm subroutine, saving a factor of S.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a central open question in replicability theory: replicable exploration is NOT inherently more expensive than batch learning, at least for low-horizon tabular MDPs. The near-tight bounds (O(S²A) upper, Ω(S²) lower) and the new sign-one-way marginals lower bound (resolving an open problem of Bun et al., 2023) provide a clean picture of the statistical cost of replicability in control settings. Key open questions remain: closing the A-dependence gap between upper and lower bounds (the lower bound only captures S², not S²A), improving the H-dependence, and making the algorithm computationally efficient (the current version requires exp(S log A) time). The techniques — particularly fractional rounding of non-replicable exploration outputs — may generalize to other interactive learning problems where replicability is desired.
