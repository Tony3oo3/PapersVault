## Problem

Vision-language models degrade significantly under real-world corruptions (blur, noise, compression, illumination shifts), yet existing remedies are limited: external image restoration pipelines introduce latency and can disrupt multimodal alignment, while naive parameter-efficient fine-tuning that minimizes feature MSE to clean references is not semantics-preserving and can even catastrophically damage downstream reasoning. The paper identifies this failure as the Semantic Misalignment Gap (SMG), where Euclidean alignment pulls features off the in-distribution manifold on which multimodal reasoning is calibrated.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single corrupted RGB image (processed at the standard CLIP ViT-L/14 resolution) and a text prompt, producing a free-form text response via the frozen LLM. At training time, paired clean-reference and corrupted images are required, but the reference branch is discarded at inference. The adapters add only ~17 ms of latency over the base model (230 ms vs. 213 ms end-to-end), compared to ~800 ms for external restoration pipelines, while keeping generation length comparable.

## Method

MAA inserts a three-branch residual adapter (window self-attention, token-wise MLP, and max pooling, all gated and zero-initialized) into each of the 24 transformer blocks of a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 vision encoder, adding ~25.2M trainable parameters (<10% of the encoder). Training couples two objectives: (1) paired feature self-distillation minimizing token-wise MSE between projected corrupted and clean features, and (2) a token-level adversarial manifold constraint where a discriminator is trained to distinguish clean from corrected features, forcing the corrected distribution to remain on the clean in-distribution manifold and suppressing off-manifold Euclidean shortcuts. A theoretical analysis proves that the MSE-optimal estimator lies in a vanishing-density region of the feature space, formally justifying the need for the adversarial constraint. At inference, the discriminator is discarded and only the lightweight adapters remain, yielding a single-stage robust VLM without external restoration.

## Results

On R-Bench-Dis (the primary corruption-robustness benchmark), MAA achieves 59.39, outperforming the zero-shot LLaVA-1.6 baseline (58.79) and all external restoration pipelines (best: MoCE-IR at 58.38). MAA also improves the clean-reference subset (R-Bench-Ref: 59.92 vs. 58.91) while maintaining or slightly improving general multimodal scores (MMBench 76.30, MMVet 42.61, HallusionBench 51.31). Inference overhead is minimal: MAA adds only 17 ms per image (230 ms total) versus 800–1,000 ms for two-stage external restoration, yielding a 0.93× speedup relative to the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAA outperforms all four external restoration baselines on R-Bench-Dis: MoCE-IR (58.38), RAM-PromptIR (57.37), Real-ESRGAN (57.17), and AirNet (56.97), with margins of 1.01, 2.02, 2.22, and 2.42 points respectively. The Naive Adaptation (MSE-only) baseline, which uses the same adapter architecture but without the adversarial manifold constraint, actually degrades below the zero-shot baseline (57.98 vs. 58.79 on Dis), confirming that Euclidean feature alignment alone is harmful. Among alternative PEFT mechanisms (Table 6), LoRA with adversarial constraint reaches 57.98 on Dis and Prefix Tuning with adversarial constraint reaches 58.99, both below MAA's 59.39. Distribution-aware feature objectives (Table 7) such as KL (59.39 Dis) and MMD (59.19 Dis) approach but do not exceed MAA's combined MSE+Adv objective (59.39 Dis, 59.92 Ref), while InfoNCE collapses entirely (0.00 Dis) and CORAL matches the baseline (58.79 Dis). Cross-architecture validation on InternVL3.5-30B-A3B confirms MAA's gains transfer (+1.42 on Dis) without model-specific tuning, whereas Naive Adaptation degrades that model (−2.62 on Dis).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the vanilla adapter architecture (Houlsby et al., 2019) and window self-attention (Liu et al., 2021) to construct a layer-wise correction module, but fundamentally repurposes the PEFT paradigm: rather than adapting to a new task, the adapters serve as an internal feature-repair mechanism for corruption-induced drift. It departs sharply from the dominant two-stage restoration-then-VLM pipeline (Real-ESRGAN, AirNet, MoCE-IR) by arguing that pixel-fidelity objectives are misaligned with multimodal semantics, and from naive PEFT (LoRA, MSE-only adapters) by demonstrating that Euclidean feature alignment is not semantics-preserving. The adversarial manifold constraint, inspired by GAN-based distribution matching, is the key conceptual departure that distinguishes MAA from prior feature-distillation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled diagnosis (the Semantic Misalignment Gap) for why the most intuitive robustness strategy—minimizing feature distance to clean references—backfires, supported by both a formal density-decay proof and empirical distributional metrics (MMD, CKA). Practically, MAA offers a single-stage, near-zero-overhead alternative to two-stage restoration pipelines (~230 ms vs. ~1000 ms), which is significant for latency-sensitive deployments. However, gains are modest (≈0.6 points on R-Bench-Dis), cross-architecture validation is limited to one additional model (InternVL3.5-30B), and the method has not been tested on video or on corruption types beyond the synthetic pipeline. Open questions include whether the adversarial constraint generalizes to weaker supervision regimes and whether the SMG formalization extends to non-Gaussian or anisotropic feature manifolds.
