## Problem

Perceptual image compression at low bitrates requires balancing fidelity and realism, but existing solutions either optimize rate-distortion alone (losing naturalness) or rely on heavy generative/diffusion decoders (incurring orders-of-magnitude higher decoding cost than commercial codecs). Implicit neural representation codecs offer low decoding complexity, yet their perceptual potential remains largely unexplored, and the Wasserstein distortion they adopt for perceptual optimization relies on restrictive independence assumptions and saliency-only weighting that neglects regional structure and frequency-dependent visual characteristics.

## Input / Output

The codec takes a single RGB image as input and produces a compressed bitstream containing quantized per-region latent vectors, quantized LPN/GPM/ARM network parameters, and chain-coded region contours. Decoding is extremely lightweight: the bitstream is decompressed and fed into small 4-layer convolutional networks (one per salient region) plus a shared global modulator, yielding a reconstructed RGB image at a fraction of the cost of generative codecs. The system is an overfitted codec, meaning each image is individually optimized into its own parameter set, making it best suited for encode-once-decode-many scenarios such as streaming.

## Method

Re²IC partitions each image into 1–2 salient regions via EML-Net saliency maps, compresses region boundaries with adaptive chain coding, and reconstructs each region with a dedicated 4-layer local perceptual network (LPN) modulated by a shared global perceptual modulator (GPM) that injects multi-scale context from quantized latent vectors. The key perceptual loss is Wavelet-Wasserstein Distortion (WA-WD), which applies a Haar DWT to VGG feature maps before computing Wasserstein distortion in each subband (LL, LH, HL, HH), thereby absorbing intra-block correlations into subband-specific variances and making the diagonal-Gaussian approximation statistically tighter. This decomposition explicitly disentangles fidelity (LL) from realism (LH/HL/HH), enabling saliency-guided, frequency-aware bit allocation. Training is per-image overfitting with a two-stage quantization-aware schedule (soft rounding with Kumaraswamy noise for 80k steps, then hard rounding for 8k steps), optimizing WA-WD plus an autoregressive entropy-coded rate term, with common randomness (Gaussian matrices) shared between encoder and decoder at no coding cost.

## Results

On CLIC2020 at ~0.15 bpp, Re²IC achieves LPIPS 0.0754, DISTS 0.0381, FID 11.82, KID 1.41, and WD 0.5498, outperforming C3/WDs (LPIPS 0.0767, DISTS 0.0459, FID 13.71, KID 2.63, WD 0.6487) and HiFiC (FID 18.81, KID 2.33, WD 1.0509) on most perceptual metrics while also improving PSNR by up to 0.82 dB over C3/WDs. In the user study on Kodak, Re²IC surpasses HiFiC in human preference (Elo score) while requiring less than 1% of HiFiC's decoding complexity. As a standalone perceptual metric, WA-WD achieves PCC 94.6% and SRCC 92.3% alignment with human ratings, outperforming standard WD (PCC 94.2%, SRCC 91.3%) and all other tested IQA metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VTM and MLIC+/MLIC++, Re²IC achieves substantially better perceptual quality (e.g., FID 11.82 vs. 85.65–89.83 at medium bpp) at the cost of lower PSNR (~28.5 vs. ~33 dB), reflecting the fundamental rate-distortion-perception trade-off. Against C3/WDs (the closest overfitted-codec baseline), Re²IC wins on nearly all perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DISTS, FID, KID, NIQE, WD) and also improves PSNR by up to 0.82 dB, demonstrating that region-wise wavelet-WD optimization is strictly more effective than global saliency-weighted WD. Against HiFiC, the comparison is mixed on individual metrics: HiFiC achieves lower LPIPS (0.0589 vs. 0.0754 at medium bpp) and lower NIQE (3.30 vs. 4.40), but Re²IC wins decisively on FID (11.82 vs. 18.81), KID (1.41 vs. 2.33), DISTS at high bpp (0.0187 vs. 0.0492), and WD (0.5498 vs. 1.0509), and critically wins the human preference study while using less than 1% of HiFiC's decoding cost. Diffusion-based baselines (CDC, TACO) and WeConvene are reported only in appendix tables, where Re²IC similarly leads on perceptual metrics at comparable or lower bitrates. Overall, Re²IC establishes a new state-of-the-art among overfitted codecs and matches or exceeds the generative HiFiC in perceived quality at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit neural representation (INR) codec lineage (COIN, COOL-CHIC, C3, LotteryCodec) by introducing saliency-guided region partitioning with dedicated local perceptual networks and a shared global perceptual modulator, departing from the single-network-overfitting paradigm. It builds directly on Wasserstein distortion (Qiu et al., 2024; Ballé et al., 2025) for perceptual optimization but departs from it by decomposing WD into orthonormal wavelet subbands (WA-WD), which the authors prove yields tighter diagonal-Gaussian approximation bounds and explicit fidelity-realism separation across frequencies. The region-wise design with chain-coded contours and common randomness draws on shape-adaptive coding ideas from classical video coding but repurposes them for INR-based compression, where arbitrary shapes are naturally supported.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in closing the gap between overfitted implicit codecs and generative codecs (HiFiC) on perceptual quality while reducing decoding cost by two orders of magnitude, making perception-driven compression viable on resource-constrained devices. WA-WD's strong alignment with human ratings (PCC 94.6%, SRCC 92.3%) positions it as a potentially widely adopted standalone perceptual metric beyond this codec. Key limitations include the VGG backbone inflating encoding complexity (suitable only for one-time-encode/massive-decode scenarios like streaming) and the single-level DWT used in practice, leaving open whether deeper decompositions or efficient perception-tailored backbones can further improve the trade-off. Extension to video via motion-guided dynamic region partitioning is identified as a natural next step.
