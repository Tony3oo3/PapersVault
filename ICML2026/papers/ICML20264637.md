## Problem

State-of-the-art credal predictors quantify epistemic uncertainty (EU) as disagreement among ensemble members trained with different random initializations, which mainly reflects sensitivity to optimization randomness rather than uncertainty from more substantive sources such as potential train-test distribution shifts. This paper addresses the gap by formulating EU as disagreement among models trained under different degrees of relaxation of the i.i.d. assumption between training and test distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264637/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet and Camelyon17 histopathology tiles) and produces a box credal set: per-class probability intervals [p_k, p̄_k] formed by taking the min and max across M ensemble softmax outputs, constrained to sum to 1. EU is then computed as the difference between upper and lower Shannon entropy over this box credal set via constrained optimization. In the binary medical classification setting, the credal set reduces to a single probability interval and EU is measured by its width.

## Method

CreDRO trains an ensemble of M standard neural networks (no architectural modification) where each member i is trained with a distinct CVaR level δ_i, uniformly interpolated over [δ_G, 1], simulating different hypothesized degrees of train-test distribution divergence. Training uses adversarially reweighted learning: within each batch, only the top-δ_i fraction of highest-loss samples are selected for backpropagation, providing a batch-wise approximation of the full CVaR DRO objective. At inference, the M softmax probability vectors are converted into per-class min/max intervals forming a box credal set, and EU is quantified as the gap between the maximum and minimum Shannon entropy achievable within that set. This design ensures ensemble disagreement reflects informative differences in robustness profiles rather than mere initialization randomness, and the approach is robust to the choice of δ_G and the interpolation strategy.

## Results

On the primary OOD detection benchmark (CIFAR-10 as ID, M=20, δ_G=0.5), CreDRO achieves the best AUROC across all five OOD datasets: 97.4% (SVHN), 92.7% (Places365), 92.5% (CIFAR-100), 96.4% (FMNIST), and 91.1% (ImageNet). CreDRO also improves point-prediction quality over the deep ensemble baseline, reaching 96.37% test accuracy and 0.0038 ECE on CIFAR-10 (vs. 95.69% and 0.0051 for DE). In the medical selective classification task on Camelyon17 under realistic domain shift, CreDRO achieves the highest accuracy-rejection AUC, while CreDE's EU estimates are unreliable (accuracy decreases with rejection).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CreDRO consistently outperforms all six baselines on every OOD detection benchmark. Against the standard deep ensemble (DE, Lakshminarayanan et al.), CreDRO gains 2.6–3.5 AUROC points (e.g., 97.4 vs. 94.8 on SVHN; 91.1 vs. 88.9 on ImageNet), and also improves test accuracy (96.37% vs. 95.69%) and ECE (0.0038 vs. 0.0051). Versus the credal wrapper (CreWra, Wang et al. 2025b), CreDRO leads by 1.7–2.4 points (e.g., 97.4 vs. 95.7 on SVHN; 91.1 vs. 89.0 on ImageNet). Against credal relative likelihood (CreRL, Lohr et al. 2025), the margin is 2.6–2.2 points (97.4 vs. 94.8 on SVHN; 91.1 vs. 88.9 on ImageNet). Compared to credal ensembling (CreEns, Nguyen et al. 2025), CreDRO wins by 1.9–2.3 points across all datasets. Against the closest DRO-based competitor, credal deep ensembles (CreDE, Wang et al. 2024), CreDRO leads by 3.1–2.7 points (97.4 vs. 94.3 on SVHN; 91.1 vs. 88.4 on ImageNet). The largest gap is against credal Bayesian deep learning (CreBNN, Caprio et al. 2024), where CreDRO outperforms by 6.7–5.2 points, reflecting CreBNN's reliance on random initialization for its credal prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

CreDRO sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: deep ensembles for epistemic uncertainty (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) and distributionally robust optimization via adversarially reweighted learning (Sagawa et al., 2019; Levy et al., 2020). It departs from prior credal ensemble methods (CreWra, CreEns, CreRL) that source EU solely from random-initialization disagreement, and from CreDE (Wang et al., 2024) which uses a single fixed DRO hyperparameter and requires architectural modification. The key conceptual shift is assigning each ensemble member a distinct CVaR level δ to simulate varying degrees of train-test distributional relaxation, so that ensemble disagreement reflects not just optimization randomness but also informative disagreement about potential domain shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: most SOTA credal predictors conflate EU with sensitivity to random initialization, which is a weak proxy for the distributional uncertainty that matters at deployment. By grounding ensemble diversity in DRO, CreDRO yields consistently better OOD detection and selective classification, including under realistic medical domain shift (Camelyon17) where competing methods fail. Open questions remain around principled extraction of a single predictive distribution from credal sets, theoretical guarantees, and extension to regression. The robustness to the δ_G hyperparameter and interpolation scheme is practically encouraging, reducing the method's sensitivity to subjective design choices.
