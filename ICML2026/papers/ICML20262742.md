## Problem

Current activation quantization methods for LLM training are limited to 8-bit formats and still cause accuracy loss, while gradients remain stored in FP32 even when quantized for communication, occupying the same memory as model parameters. Naively pushing activations to 4-bit or gradients to 8-bit causes severe convergence degradation or overflow during All-Reduce, making these aggressive compression levels impractical for production LLM training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262742/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system operates within Megatron-LM's TP/PP/DP infrastructure, consuming standard Transformer LLM architectures (LLaMA2-7B/13B, LLaMA3-8B, CodeLLaMA-34B) with training mini-batches at sequence lengths from 2K to 80K. It produces trained model parameters with up to 52% peak GPU memory reduction and up to 1.34× training speedup, while maintaining convergence loss and downstream accuracy comparable to full-precision BF16 baselines. The bit-width assignment is dynamic per layer type and PP stage rather than uniform across the model.

## Method

AGoQ combines two key techniques: (1) Layer-Aware Activation Quantization (LAAQ), which uses first-order perturbation analysis to show that attention gradient errors scale as O(ηL√d_k) versus O(η) for RMSNorm, justifying 4-bit block-wise FP4 (blocksize 128) for RMSNorm, SiLU, and FFN input activations while keeping attention at BF16, plus a Dynamic Bit-width Compensation strategy (DBCA-PP) that assigns higher bit-widths to PP stages storing fewer activation mini-batches in Interleaved 1F1B schedules; and (2) Precision-Preserved Gradient Quantization (QuanGrad), which stores gradients in 8-bit FP8 with a dequantize-accumulate-quantize loop for local mini-batch accumulation, and decomposes All-Reduce into All-to-All + local FP32 reduce + All-Gather to prevent FP8 overflow during inter-GPU communication. Quantization/dequantization is fused into adjacent GEMM kernels to eliminate extra CUDA-core overhead, and the entire system is compatible with 8-bit Adam optimizer states.

## Results

On LLaMA2-13B (64 GPUs, PP=4, TP=8), AGoQ achieves an average 1.22× speedup over Megatron-LM and 1.21× over ZeRO-1, with the gap widening to 1.34× at 80K sequence length (111,422 ms vs. 149,667 ms for Megatron-LM). Across 372 experiments spanning LLaMA3-8B, LLaMA2-13B, and CodeLLaMA-34B, AGoQ delivers a 1.23× average speedup over Megatron-LM and 1.19× over ZeRO-1, while reducing peak GPU memory by up to 52%. Against COAT on OLMo-1B, AGoQ cuts memory by 31% at 24K and achieves a 1.1× end-to-end speedup at 32K. Training loss closely tracks the BF16 baseline on both LLaMA2-7B and LLaMA3-8B pretraining, and zero-shot accuracy across six benchmarks shows no meaningful degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGoQ consistently outperforms Megatron-LM (BF16) across all sequence lengths, with the speedup growing from 1.03× at 32K to 1.34× at 80K, primarily because AGoQ eliminates the need for activation recomputation (R=0 vs. R=3–10). Against ZeRO-1, AGoQ achieves a similar 1.21× average speedup, and the paper notes that ZeRO-2 and ZeRO-3 perform even worse than ZeRO-1 due to additional communication overhead, making AGoQ's advantage more pronounced. Compared to COAT (the closest prior work using FP8 activations), AGoQ reduces memory by 31% at 24K and achieves a 1.1× speedup at 32K where COAT requires recomputation to avoid OOM. On convergence, AGoQ closely tracks the BF16 baseline loss on both LLaMA2-7B and LLaMA3-8B, whereas Microsoft's FP8-AllReduce method exhibits significantly higher loss, validating the precision-preserved All-Reduce design. For communication, AGoQ's decomposed All-to-All + All-Gather approach achieves a 3.4× speedup over standard All-Reduce at 200 Gbps (39.17 ms vs. 131.23 ms for 32 MB messages), widening to 3.7× at 10 Gbps, confirming the benefit on bandwidth-constrained clusters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation quantization line (Jetfire, COAT) from 8-bit to near-4-bit by introducing a layer-aware strategy (LAAQ) that selectively quantizes only those layers whose gradient errors remain bounded under low-bit storage, while leaving attention activations in full precision. For gradients, it departs from prior work that stored gradients in FP32 and quantized only at communication time, instead storing and accumulating gradients in FP8 locally and replacing the standard All-Reduce with a decomposed All-to-All + local reduce + All-Gather to avoid FP8 overflow. The system is built atop Megatron-LM's TP/PP/DP infrastructure and is compatible with 8-bit block-wise optimizers (Dettmers et al., 2022), positioning AGoQ as a holistic quantization stack rather than a single-component improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 52% peak-memory reduction and up to 1.34× training speedup make AGoQ practically relevant for groups constrained by GPU memory or interconnect bandwidth, and the theoretical error analysis provides a reusable framework for deciding which layers tolerate which bit-widths. Limitations include evaluation primarily on A6000/Pro-6000 GPUs (not H100/A100 datacenter hardware), testing on LLaMA-family architectures only, and the 'near 4-bit' activation storage still requiring 5U for attention, which caps the total compression ratio. Open questions include whether the layer-aware bit allocation generalizes to non-Transformer architectures (e.g., Mamba, hybrid models), whether the DBCA-PP compensation strategy remains effective at very high PP degrees (>16), and whether the FP8 All-Reduce decomposition introduces latency penalties on high-bandwidth interconnects (e.g., NVLink) where standard All-Reduce is already fast.
