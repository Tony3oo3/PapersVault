## Problem

Existing reward-free alignment methods (e.g., DPO and its multi-objective extensions) rely on linear aggregation of objective-specific preference losses, which is ill-suited when objectives induce opposing gradient directions—no single update can simultaneously improve all objectives. Prior multi-objective alignment approaches either require explicit reward models or use heuristic scalarization without theoretical guarantees, leaving a gap in principled, reward-free handling of gradient conflicts during LLM fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes, for each objective i, a set of pairwise preference tuples (x, y_i^+, y_i^-) where the preferred/dispreferred responses may differ across objectives (e.g., the quality winner is the conciseness loser), along with user-specified objective weights w ∈ Δ_m. It produces a fine-tuned LLM policy π_θ that, at inference time, generates text responses reflecting the requested trade-off. Training is fully offline (no online sampling or reward model), and the user can sweep weights to trace out a Pareto frontier of aligned policies.

## Method

RACO adapts Conflict-Averse Gradient Descent (CAGrad) to multi-objective DPO-style preference alignment. At each step, it computes objective-specific DPO gradients g_i, forms a user-weighted anchor g_0 = Σ w_i g_i, then solves a small (dimension-m) subproblem to find correction weights p that maximize the worst-case local improvement across objectives. The key novelty is a clipping step: each correction coefficient p_i is capped at its corresponding user weight w_i, preventing the correction from over-shifting toward less-preferred objectives in the high-dimensional, noisy LLM parameter space. The final update is g_0 + c‖g_0‖·(clipped correction direction)/‖clipped correction direction‖. The paper proves convergence to a Pareto-critical point of the weighted loss (Theorem 3.1) and shows that, in the two-objective case, clipping strictly improves the one-step descent rate over unclipped CAGrad whenever the correction is active (Theorem 3.2).

## Results

On the BeaverTails safety alignment task, RACO achieves LLM-as-a-judge win rates of 63.9–78.9% against DPO LW and 43.4–76.5% against AMoPO across four model configurations (Qwen3-4B, Gemma3-4B, each in Instruct and Base variants) and three helpfulness weights (0.2, 0.5, 0.8). On the Reddit Summary quality-conciseness task, RACO consistently attains the outermost Pareto frontier across all input weights w_qual ∈ {0.8, 0.65, 0.5, 0.35, 0.2} for both Qwen3-4B and Llama3-8B, while baselines sacrifice the less-weighted objective. The quality-faithfulness task shows the same pattern, with RACO dominating the Pareto curve endpoints under highly imbalanced weights (w_qual ∈ {0.8, 0.2}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RACO is compared against two fully offline, reward-free baselines: AMoPO (Liu et al., 2025) and DPO LW / MODPO (Zhou et al., 2024). Against AMoPO, RACO wins in 10 out of 12 model-weight configurations on BeaverTails, with the largest margins on Gemma3-4B-Base (66.3–76.5% WR) and Qwen3-4B-Instruct (59.0–78.9% WR); the two losses occur on Qwen3-4B-Base at w_help=0.2 (43.4%) and Gemma3-4B-Instruct at w_help=0.2/0.5 (45.8%/47.0%), where the judge's harmlessness-priority scoring slightly favors AMoPO's more conservative harmlessness. Against DPO LW, RACO wins in 11 out of 12 configurations, with the sole near-tie at Qwen3-4B-Instruct w_help=0.5 (50.6% WR). On the Reddit Summary Pareto frontiers (quality-conciseness and quality-faithfulness), RACO achieves the outermost frontier at every tested weight for both Qwen3-4B and Llama3-8B, whereas both baselines systematically trade off the less-weighted objective, with the gap most pronounced at extreme weights (0.8/0.2) where gradient conflicts are strongest.

## Relation to Prior Work

RACO adapts conflict-averse gradient descent (CAGrad, Liu et al. 2021) from multi-task learning to reward-free preference alignment, combining it with DPO-style objective-specific losses. The closest prior work is AMoPO (Liu et al. 2025), which is also fully reward-free and offline but relies on heuristic reweighting without explicit gradient-conflict resolution or convergence guarantees. The key departure is the introduction of a clipping mechanism on CAGrad's correction weights to prevent over-correction in high-dimensional LLM parameter spaces, yielding a method that provably converges to Pareto-critical points respecting user-specified weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first reward-free multi-objective alignment method with explicit gradient-conflict handling and convergence guarantees to weight-respecting Pareto-critical points, addressing a structural limitation of weighted-sum approaches that cannot simultaneously improve all objectives under conflicting gradients. The counterintuitive acceleration result (clipping strictly improves one-step descent in the two-objective case) offers a principled justification for a simple practical trick. However, the analysis is deterministic (no stochastic-gradient theorem), provides no sample complexity or generalization bounds, and the empirical gains over AMoPO, while consistent across model families, are moderate rather than transformative.
