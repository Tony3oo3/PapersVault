## Problem

Existing adversarial robustness certification methods typically rely on deterministic training abstractions, restrictive model-family assumptions, or known attack strategies, and often provide only point-wise guarantees. They largely overlook the inherent stochasticity of modern training pipelines (random initialization, minibatch sampling, data augmentation), which undermines practical reliability. This paper asks whether one can formally certify, with probability at least ρ, an ℓp-bounded perturbation radius within which a stochastically trained model's test accuracy is guaranteed to remain above a threshold α, without requiring knowledge of the attack mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a parameterized ML model (MLP, CNN, or ResNet) trained on RGB images, along with stochastic training trajectories generated under a grid of ℓp perturbation budgets and a held-out test set. It produces a certified robust radius δ_cert* (the largest admissible perturbation norm), a trained neural network barrier certificate (NNBC) with associated constants (b1, b2, b3, κ, εM), and explicit PAC bounds: a confidence level 1−β and a violation probability v. The certified radius is the maximum perturbation level for which the model is (α,ρ)-certified accurate, and the guarantee holds distributionally over the stochastic training process rather than for a single trained instance.

## Method

The framework models gradient-based training as a discrete-time stochastic dynamical system (dt-SDS) and reformulates adversarial robustness as a probabilistic safety verification problem. A barrier certificate (BC) is parameterized as a neural network (NNBC) and trained via a composite ReLU loss that enforces initial-bound, terminal-separation, non-negativity, expected-contraction, and scalar-feasibility constraints on sampled trajectories. For scalability, a lightweight surrogate transition model (fitted via PCA-reduced latent-space regression) generates additional synthesis trajectories, but the final certificate is always validated on fresh i.i.d. scenarios from the true dynamics. Verification proceeds by relaxing an intractable robust convex problem (RCP) through a chance-constrained program (CCP) to a tractable scenario convex problem (SCP), with Chebyshev's inequality controlling the empirical-mean approximation error and scenario-approach theory (Calafiore & Campi) bounding the finite-sample generalization gap. The resulting PAC guarantee certifies that, with confidence 1−β, the NNBC is valid and the model is (α,ρ)-accurate for all perturbations within δ_cert*, up to violation probability v.

## Results

The framework certifies non-trivial robust radii across MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10 under ℓ∞ and ℓ2 threat models with 99.9% confidence. For example, on SVHN with MLP/SGD under PGD (ℓ2, τ=0.9), the certified radius reaches δ_cert*=2.34 at α=0.75, ρ=0.90, while on CIFAR-10 with CNN/Adam under BPA (ℓ∞, τ=0.1), δ_cert*=0.45 is certified at α=0.70. In the direct comparison with RAB on test-time BDA attacks, the proposed method certifies larger radii in all settings where RAB provides a value (e.g., 0.52 vs. 0.28 on SVHN at α=0.40) and additionally certifies in regimes where RAB returns 'NA' (e.g., MNIST at α=0.90, SVHN at α=0.80, CIFAR-10 at α=0.50 and 0.40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against RAB (Weber et al., 2023), a randomized-smoothing-based certification method, on test-time BDA attacks across MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10. The proposed method strictly outperforms RAB in every setting where RAB provides a certified value: on MNIST (ℓ∞), the certified radius is 0.11 vs. 0.10 at α=0.80 and 0.16 vs. 0.14 at α=0.60; on SVHN (ℓ2), it reaches 0.28 vs. 0.17 at α=0.60 and 0.52 vs. 0.28 at α=0.40; on CIFAR-10 (ℓ∞), it achieves 0.09 vs. 0.05 at α=0.30. Critically, the proposed method also certifies non-zero radii in four settings where RAB returns 'NA' (MNIST α=0.90, SVHN α=0.80, CIFAR-10 α=0.50 and α=0.40), demonstrating that modeling training as a stochastic dynamical system enables certification in regimes where instance-conditional smoothing-based methods fail. The authors note that RAB certifies a single trained model instance without accounting for training-time randomness, whereas the proposed framework provides distribution-level PAC guarantees over the stochastic training process.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the control-theoretic barrier certificate framework (Prajna et al., 2007; Ames et al., 2014; Steinhardt & Tedrake, 2012) to the stochastic ML training setting, building directly on the authors' own prior deterministic barrier certificate work (Taheri et al., 2026) and their neural barrier certificate synthesis method (Taheri & Zamani, 2025). The key conceptual departure is modeling gradient-based training as a discrete-time stochastic dynamical system rather than a deterministic map, which necessitates the scenario convex problem (SCP) from robust optimization (Calafiore & Campi, 2006; Campi & Garatti, 2008) to obtain PAC-style generalization guarantees beyond sampled trajectories. Unlike prior certification approaches—ensemble voting, randomized smoothing, privacy-inspired bounds, or architecture-specific analyses—that typically provide pointwise or instance-conditional guarantees under restrictive assumptions, this framework unifies train-time poisoning and test-time evasion under a single probabilistic safety specification that is model- and attack-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first unified, model-agnostic formal certification of robustness that explicitly accounts for the stochasticity inherent in modern training pipelines (random initialization, minibatch sampling, optimizer noise), addressing a gap the authors identify as largely unaddressed in prior literature. The consistent gap between empirical and certified radii across all experiments quantifies the price of moving from sample-dependent estimates to distribution-level PAC guarantees, offering practitioners a principled way to bound worst-case performance degradation. Limitations include the conservatism introduced by stochastic modeling (certified radii are systematically smaller than deterministic counterparts), the restriction to input-space perturbations (no label or mixed corruptions), and computational costs that may limit scalability to very large models. Open questions include whether the framework extends to label-flipping or mixed attacks, how it behaves under adaptive adversaries who know the certification procedure, and whether the surrogate-assisted synthesis can be made tighter to reduce the conservatism gap.
