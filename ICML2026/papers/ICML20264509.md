## Problem

Deep GNNs suffer from oversmoothing, where node representations collapse to a homogeneous, non-informative state as depth increases, limiting most architectures to 2–4 layers. Existing theoretical analyses treat this as a spectral or contractivity issue but largely ignore the role of the activation function, and when activations are considered, the analysis is restricted to ReLU. This paper asks whether the activation function itself can be chosen to fundamentally alter the stability landscape of the GNN's iterative dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph adjacency matrix and a node feature matrix, with node labels used only during supervised training. Beyond standard node class predictions, the architecture produces two additional structured outputs: a bifurcation-aware initialization (a closed-form weight variance v_c ≈ 1/(dλ_k²α²) computed from the graph's dominant eigenvalue) that positions the network at the critical point, and learnable polynomial spectral filter coefficients {c_k} that reshape the adjacency spectrum to select which topological eigenmode the emergent pattern aligns with. The node representations themselves are guaranteed to be non-homogeneous (aligned with a specific graph eigenmode) once the system is supercritical.

## Method

The paper reframes a GNN layer as a discrete dynamical system x^(ℓ+1) = φ(wAx^(ℓ)) and applies Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction to prove that replacing ReLU with an odd C³ activation possessing a stabilizing cubic term (e.g., sine or tanh, where φ'(0)=α>0 and φ'''(0)=−γ<0) induces a supercritical pitchfork bifurcation at the critical coupling αwλ_k=1, destabilizing the homogeneous fixed point and creating two stable non-homogeneous patterns aligned with the dominant graph eigenvector. A closed-form bifurcation-aware initialization derived from random matrix theory (Ginibre circular law) sets the weight variance to place the network exactly at this critical point. A second, independent lever—learnable polynomial spectral filtering P(A)=Σc_k A^k—controls which eigenmode bifurcates first, enabling task-specific topological prior selection (low-pass for homophilic, high-pass for heterophilic graphs). An NTK analysis further shows that near criticality the kernel becomes asymptotically rank-one along the bifurcating mode, so gradient descent is strongly biased toward learning that specific graph pattern, providing a principled inductive bias beyond mere anti-collapse.

## Results

On node classification across 12 benchmarks spanning homophily levels from 0.11 (Texas) to 0.83 (Photo, ogbn-ArXiv), Sine-Poly(A)-sh achieves the best mean rank of 3.00±2.13, with Sine-Poly(A)-pl close behind at 3.18±3.31. On the most heterophilic dataset Texas, Sine-Poly(A)-pl reaches 93.69% accuracy, outperforming the best baseline SGOS-Expn (88.65%) by over 5 points. In the critical deep-regime test (64 layers on CORA), bifurcating activations (sin, tanh) maintain stable accuracy while ReLU-based networks collapse, with a phase transition observed near the theoretically predicted critical parameter δ=0. The paper emphasizes that shallow-benchmark results demonstrate that the proposed activations do not compromise expressivity, while the deep-regime results validate the anti-oversmoothing theory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sine-Poly(A) variants dominate on heterophilic graphs: on Texas (homophily 0.11), Sine-Poly(A)-pl achieves 93.69%, beating SGOS-Expn (88.65%), GREAD (84.59%), and GraphSAGE (82.43%) by 5–11 points; on Squirrel and Chameleon, Sine-Poly(A)-pl outperforms GREAD by 3.2 and 0.9 points respectively. On homophilic graphs, Sine-Poly(A)-sh leads on CoauthorCS (95.84% vs. SGOS-Expn's 93.71%) and Photo (95.69% vs. GCN's 91.2%), while GREAD edges out on PubMed (90.01% vs. 89.71%) and GAT slightly leads on ogbn-ArXiv (73.65% vs. 73.29%). Compared to standard GCN, the proposed methods gain 7–38 points depending on the dataset, with the largest margins on heterophilic WebKB and Wikipedia graphs. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that Sine-Poly(A) ranks first (mean rank 3.00) versus ReLU-Poly(A) (rank 6.6–6.9) and Sine-GCN without polynomial filtering (rank 10.73), validating both the activation choice and the spectral filter as necessary components. The paper positions these shallow-benchmark results as demonstrating that bifurcating activations do not sacrifice expressivity, with the primary theoretical validation occurring in the 64-layer deep regime where ReLU collapses but Sine/tanh maintain performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant oversmoothing literature (spectral filtering, residual connections, normalization, continuous-time reaction-diffusion formulations) by reframing the problem through bifurcation theory and Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction. While prior dynamical-systems analyses (Arroyo et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025) characterize oversmoothing as contractive convergence, and continuous-time GNNs (Choi et al., 2023; Chamberlain et al., 2021) mitigate it via learnable reaction terms, this work shows that a simple activation substitution in standard discrete message-passing—replacing ReLU with odd functions possessing a stabilizing cubic term—induces a supercritical pitchfork bifurcation that destabilizes the homogeneous fixed point. The NTK analysis (building on Jacot et al., 2018) and random-matrix-theory initialization (extending Li et al., 2023) are novel applications that connect the bifurcation mechanism to learning dynamics and practical initialization, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, closed-form explanation for why certain activations resist oversmoothing while others do not, and yields a simple initialization recipe (v_c ≈ 1/(dλ_k²α²)) that positions networks at the bifurcation threshold. The NTK rank-one result reveals that bifurcation does more than prevent collapse—it creates a topological learning prior that biases gradient descent toward specific graph eigenmodes, offering a new lens on inductive bias in GNNs. Key limitations include the local nature of the analysis (valid near the bifurcation point), reliance on fixed graph topologies, and the fact that practical benchmarks use shallow networks where oversmoothing is not the binding constraint; extending the framework to learnable architectures (e.g., attention) is identified as an important open direction.
