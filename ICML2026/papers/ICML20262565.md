## Problem

Existing multi-DNN inference schedulers on edge GPUs (e.g., Pantheon) execute real-time tasks serially, causing subsequent tasks to miss deadlines under concurrent bursts and forcing aggressive early exits that degrade accuracy by up to 30.6%. Enabling concurrent RT execution introduces coupled challenges—GPU memory exhaustion, fragmentation from urgency-based scheduling, and host-to-GPU transfer latency during variant switching—that no prior system addresses jointly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262565/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system ingests multiple concurrent periodic DNN streams with heterogeneous inputs: 1080p RGB frames for YOLOv8-L object detection, 1024×1024 images for ViT-L scene recognition, 640×640/512×512 images for YOLOv8n/MobileViT-S, and bounded text prompts for GPT-2 Small command generation. Each stream carries a strict deadline (150–540 ms) and period, and the system outputs per-task inference results (bounding boxes, scene labels, generated text) while jointly deciding which pruned/early-exit variant to run, its memory placement on the GPU address plane, and whether to trigger an early exit to meet the deadline.

## Method

RTInfer combines three components. Offline, an accuracy-calibrated lightweight variant co-optimization jointly prunes the backbone and early-exit heads via a multi-step freezing strategy (pruning chunk-by-chunk while freezing preceding chunks to preserve intermediate tensor interfaces), then solves a mixed-variable Genetic Algorithm over pruning ratios and exit depths to retain a sparse Pareto set under accuracy-cap and memory-budget constraints. Online, a memory-layout-aware scheduler formulates buffer placement as an ILP over a 2D time×address plane, solved by a heuristic-driven search that prioritizes blocks by area, size, and lifetime, with minor/major backtracking under extreme memory pressure. A Delta-Graph load-aware pipeline represents all variants as a shared trunk plus sparse content-addressed chunks, so variant switching transfers only missing blocks via zero-copy indirection or materialize-once merge, bounding host-to-GPU latency before first-use.

## Results

RTInfer achieves 0% average deadline miss ratio (DMR) across all three edge applications (Smart Traffic, UAV Ground Station, Service Robot), compared to 66.6% for classical scheduling (RMS-P, DMS-P) and 32.8% for the SOTA Pantheon. Under tight deadlines (κ=0.6–0.8), RTInfer maintains 0% DMR with 94.2% average relative accuracy, while Pantheon suffers 27.6% DMR and RMS-P/DMS-P reach 61.2%/77.6% DMR. At default deadlines (κ=1.0), RTInfer sustains 0% DMR with 95.8% average accuracy. Scheduling overhead is minimal at ~34.6 μs per invocation, and power consumption is only 0.76 W higher than Pantheon (9.56 W vs. 8.80 W).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTInfer decisively outperforms all three baselines on both DMR and accuracy. Against the two classical real-time schedulers (RMS-P and DMS-P), RTInfer reduces average DMR from 66.6% to 0% and improves accuracy by 2.2–6.3×, as these methods lack DNN-aware preemption and memory management. Against the SOTA Pantheon (Han et al., 2024), RTInfer cuts DMR from 32.8% to 0% and boosts accuracy by 34.5–56.5%, primarily because Pantheon's serial RT execution and heavy reliance on early exits cause up to 30.6% accuracy degradation under task bursts. The gap is largest under tight deadlines (κ=0.6–0.8), where Pantheon still misses 27.6% of deadlines while RTInfer maintains 0%; under loose deadlines (κ=1.2–1.4) the advantage narrows as Pantheon's serial queue becomes less of a bottleneck. RTInfer's concurrent execution with memory-layout-aware placement and Delta-Graph-based variant switching is the key differentiator, enabling parallel RT inference that Pantheon's single-queue design cannot match.

## Relation to Prior Work

RTInfer extends the preemptible multi-DNN inference lineage established by REEF (kernel-level preemption), Miriam (elastic kernels for concurrent streams), and Pantheon (urgency-based RT preemption with early exits). Its key departure from Pantheon is replacing serial RT execution with concurrent RT inference, enabled by three coupled mechanisms: accuracy-calibrated pruning/early-exit variant co-optimization (building on DepGraph-style structural pruning and Shallow-Deep early exits), a memory-layout-aware ILP-based scheduler that jointly optimizes task priority and spatial buffer placement, and a Delta-Graph load-aware pipeline that deduplicates shared weight chunks across variants to bound host-to-GPU transfer latency. The paper positions these as necessary because prior systems either underutilize GPU resources through serial queuing or sacrifice excessive accuracy through aggressive early exits during task bursts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result of achieving 0% deadline miss rate while simultaneously improving accuracy by 34–56% over SOTA challenges the common assumption that meeting tight real-time deadlines on memory-constrained edge GPUs necessarily requires sacrificing inference quality. This is practically significant for safety-critical edge deployments (autonomous vehicles, UAVs, service robots) where both timeliness and accuracy are non-negotiable. Limitations include evaluation on a single hardware platform (Jetson Xavier NX), no cross-task layer sharing, and diminishing returns under loose deadlines or SM-saturated workloads. Open questions include whether the memory-layout-aware scheduling generalizes to multi-GPU or heterogeneous accelerator settings, and whether SM-aware admission control can extend gains to compute-bound (rather than memory-bound) regimes.
