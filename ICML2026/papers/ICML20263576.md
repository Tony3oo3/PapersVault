## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is a standard post-training recipe for autoregressive language models, but its application to diffusion language models (DLMs) is poorly understood and can even degrade performance relative to pretraining. The paper identifies that vanilla SFT for DLMs overlooks a critical interaction: rare tokens become effectively unlearnable when most of the input is masked (late diffusion times), while frequent tokens become trivial to predict when most of the input is unmasked (early diffusion times). No prior work had systematically analyzed or addressed this joint 'what and when' learnability problem during DLM fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

During training, LIFT takes a clean text sequence (math problem with chain-of-thought solution) and progressively masks tokens according to a diffusion schedule, producing a corrupted input x_t where each token is masked with probability t. The model must predict the original tokens at masked positions, but LIFT selectively supervises only a learnability-informed subset of those positions rather than all masked tokens. At inference, the model takes a text prompt and generates a full reasoning trace and answer by iteratively unmasking tokens over multiple diffusion steps (2 tokens per step), with generation lengths of 128–512 tokens.

## Method

LIFT introduces a learnability-informed masking strategy that adaptively selects which masked tokens to supervise at each diffusion timestep. It first samples a secondary masking ratio ρ to create a more corrupted input x_{t+ρ}, computes per-token confidence scores p_θ(x_0^k | x_{t+ρ}), then partitions the diffusion time axis into three regimes controlled by a hyperparameter H: for low t (abundant context), it trains on the Bottom-K hardest tokens; for moderate t, it uses standard vanilla SFT; and for high t (scarce context), it trains on the Top-K easiest tokens. Non-selected masked positions are filled with ground-truth tokens to form the actual training input x_t, and the NELBO loss is restricted to the selected subset. A lightweight variant LIFT-A avoids the second forward pass by evaluating the loss directly at t+ρ with a gating mask, trading a biased objective for roughly 2× compute savings. This design ensures each training step targets tokens that are both learnable given the available context and non-trivial enough to provide useful gradient signal.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, LIFT₃ achieves 38.4% on MATH, 26.4% on Countdown, 17.4% on Sudoku, 16.7% pass@16 on AIME'24, and 6.7% pass@16 on AIME'25, representing up to a 5× relative gain over the base model on AIME'24 and a 2× gain on AIME'25. On LLaDA-1.5, LIFT₃ reaches 38.8% MATH, 31.2% Countdown, and 18.2% Sudoku. Remarkably, LIFT achieves performance close to the RLVR oracle d1 (Zhao et al., 2025) while using only 1.8 H100 GPU hours versus d1's 2,303 hours—a roughly 1,000× compute reduction—establishing a new compute-efficient Pareto frontier for DLM post-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIFT₃ consistently outperforms CART (Ye et al., 2025) across all six benchmarks on LLaDA-8B-Instruct, with the largest margins on AIME'24 (16.7% vs 10.0% pass@16) and MATH (38.4% vs 35.5%), confirming that accounting for token identity (what) in addition to context availability (when) is critical. Against GIFT (Xu et al., 2026), LIFT₃ wins on MATH (38.4% vs 34.2%), Countdown (26.4% vs 21.7%), and AIME'25 (6.7% vs 0.0%), while tying on AIME'24 (both 16.7%); GIFT's time-independent uncertainty-based masking fails to capture the diffusion-time interaction that LIFT exploits. Compared to the RLVR oracle d1 (Zhao et al., 2025), LIFT₃ is slightly behind on GSM8K (79.4% vs 81.9%), MATH (38.4% vs 39.2%), Countdown (26.4% vs 37.1%), and Sudoku (17.4% vs 18.4%), but achieves these results with only 1.8 H100 hours versus d1's 2,303 hours—a ~1,000× compute reduction—making LIFT the most compute-efficient method to reach near-RL performance on DLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked diffusion language model (MDLM) framework of Sahoo et al. (2024) and the LLaDA architecture of Nie et al. (2025) by modifying the NELBO training objective for supervised fine-tuning. Prior DLM post-training work has addressed either token difficulty (GIFT, Xu et al. 2026) or context availability (CART, Ye et al. 2025) in isolation; LIFT is the first to jointly model the interaction between what tokens are learned and when they are learned in the diffusion process. The method also draws conceptual parallels to curriculum learning but departs from it by adaptively scheduling token selection based on the model's current confidence rather than a fixed difficulty ordering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that vanilla SFT can degrade DLM performance—and that the root cause is a mismatch between token learnability and available context at each diffusion timestep—provides a principled explanation for a previously observed but unexplained failure mode. The 500× compute reduction relative to the RLVR baseline d1 while matching its performance establishes a practical path for DLM post-training that does not require the intractable sequence-level likelihoods that make RL for DLMs difficult. Open questions include whether the learnability-informed scheduling generalizes to pre-training, to non-mathematical reasoning domains, and to larger model scales beyond 8B, and whether the same what×when interaction principle applies to other discrete diffusion formulations.
