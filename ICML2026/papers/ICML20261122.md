## Problem

Multi-model training for universal adversarial suffixes has been largely abandoned because standard optimizers yield only marginal gains (+0.17%) when aggregating gradients from heterogeneous source models. The paper argues the root cause is not the multi-model paradigm itself but the instability of implicit SGD in discrete token space, which converges to model-specific minima that fail to transfer to unseen targets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of harmful queries (20 behaviors from HarmBench for training, 200 for evaluation) and produces a single universal adversarial prefix of length L=100 ASCII tokens. The prefix is optimized simultaneously against an ensemble of source models (Llama-3.1-8B and Vicuna-7B-v1.5) using a progressive prompt-addition schedule, and is applied at inference as [Prefix] + [Query] with a system-enforced response template. A Maximally Distant Template (MDT) step selects the target model's instruction format with maximum embedding divergence from the source template to amplify the adversarial perturbation.

## Method

Lookahead-GCG replaces the implicit SGD of GCG with Stochastic Nesterov Accelerated Gradient (SNAG), which evaluates gradients at an extrapolated lookahead position (e_t + βv_t) rather than the current iterate, reducing the cumulative weight of each individual gradient and preventing any single model's signal from dominating the update. To make Nesterov momentum operable in discrete token space, the method accumulates velocity in a continuous embedding space (R^{L×d}) and periodically projects the momentum vector back to the vocabulary via top-k scoring against the embedding matrix. A Maximally Distant Template (MDT) strategy selects the instruction format with the largest cosine-distance embedding shift from the source model's native template, exploiting SNAG's tighter stability bound to preserve attack efficacy under format perturbation. The theoretical analysis proves that SNAG's projected stability Δ_T^proj is strictly smaller than SGD's for all T≥1, directly translating to better cross-model generalization while maintaining the same O(1/√t) convergence rate.

## Results

On open-source LLMs (HarmBench 200-behavior test set), Lookahead-GCG with multi-model training (M=2) achieves 50.37% average Attack Success Rate (ASR) without Maximally Distant Template (MDT) and 54.78% with MDT, outperforming TransferAttack (39.00%/36.37%) and Momentum-GCG (43.06%/45.67%). On closed-source commercial APIs, Lookahead-GCG reaches 23.85% average ASR (multi-model), including 58.83% on Gemini-1.5-Pro and 27.25% on Grok-3-mini. The key multi-model gain is +11.78% for Lookahead-GCG versus only +0.49% for TransferAttack, confirming that SNAG's stability is essential for multi-model optimization to be effective.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lookahead-GCG outperforms all three baselines across every target model in the multi-model setting. Against GCG (Zou et al., 2023), the gain is +21.67% average ASR (50.37% vs. 28.70%), with the largest per-model improvement on Llama-3 (+26.38 pp). Against TransferAttack (Yang et al., 2025), Lookahead-GCG leads by +11.37% on average (50.37% vs. 39.00%), and critically, multi-model training adds +11.78% for Lookahead-GCG versus only +0.49% for TransferAttack—demonstrating that the SNAG optimizer, not the multi-model paradigm itself, is the bottleneck. Against SlotGCG (Jeong et al., 2026), the advantage is +34.30% (50.37% vs. 16.07%), with SlotGCG showing inconsistent multi-model gains (negative on Llama-2 and Qwen). The ablation Momentum-GCG (heavy-ball momentum without lookahead) achieves 43.06%, confirming that the Nesterov lookahead mechanism specifically—not just momentum—drives the transferability improvement (+7.31% over Momentum-GCG). On closed-source APIs, Lookahead-GCG leads TransferAttack by +8.77% (23.85% vs. 15.08%) and is the only method to achieve non-zero ASR on GPT-5-mini (2.00%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GCG lineage of gradient-based universal jailbreaking (Zou et al., 2023; TransferAttack/Yang et al., 2025; SlotGCG/Jeong et al., 2026) by identifying the optimizer as the bottleneck in multi-model training, which prior works had largely abandoned after observing marginal gains. It departs from the discrete token-space gradient descent implicit in all prior GCG variants by reformulating optimization in continuous embedding space with Stochastic Nesterov Accelerated Gradient (SNAG), enabling true directional momentum and a lookahead mechanism. The theoretical contribution adapts standard algorithmic stability analysis (Hardt et al., 2016; Yan et al., 2018) to the discrete projection setting, showing SNAG's tighter stability bound translates to better cross-model transfer. The maximally distant template selection builds on ChatBug's template manipulation but inverts the strategy: rather than exploiting malformed templates, it selects the most divergent valid template to amplify adversarial impact under SNAG's stability guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that multi-model training's failure was an optimizer problem rather than a paradigm problem—reframes a direction the community had written off, and the +11.78% multi-model gain (vs. +0.49% for TransferAttack) provides empirical support. However, the practical scope is narrow: the method requires loading multiple 7B+ models simultaneously, and the gains are most pronounced on open-source targets with weaker safety alignment. Open questions include whether the stability advantage persists at larger model ensembles (M>3), whether adaptive defenses (e.g., gradient masking, input filtering) neutralize the lookahead mechanism, and whether the embedding-space momentum formulation generalizes to non-ASCII or multilingual adversarial suffixes. The theoretical analysis, while correct, relies on standard L-smoothness and bounded-gradient assumptions that may not tightly characterize real LLM loss landscapes.
