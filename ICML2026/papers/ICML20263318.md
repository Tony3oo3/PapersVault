## Problem

Hierarchical knowledge structures (product taxonomies, medical ontologies, label trees) require representing asymmetric parent-child relations, but existing embedding methods either impose symmetric Euclidean distances that mismatch hierarchy, couple semantic similarity with structural depth in a single geometry (as in hyperbolic or cone embeddings), or rely on unstable angular parameterizations that introduce discontinuities. Prior polar embedding approaches, which naturally decouple direction from radius, remain underused because ad hoc stabilizers (modulus wrapping, sector partitions, sigmoid rescaling) break manifold continuity and amplify noise under weak supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pretrained encoder features (BERT text embeddings or CLIP image embeddings) for each concept node, along with the seed hierarchy's structural statistics (node depth and subtree size) used to compute a deterministic orbital potential in [0,1]. At inference, given a query node, Polaris produces a ranked list of candidate parents (or class labels in the multimodal setting) by first applying a structure-guided radial gate that filters candidates whose orbital potential deviates too far from the query's, then re-ranking survivors by cosine similarity on the hypersphere. The output is a permutation over all seed nodes, from which top-K parents or the single best label are extracted.

## Method

Polaris maps each concept onto the unit hypersphere S^{d-1} by projecting its latent vector to the tangent space at the North pole, applying the Riemannian exponential map, and processing through spherical linear layers (unit-norm rows, no bias, output re-projection) so all transformations remain closed on the manifold. Training combines four objectives: (i) a rotation-invariant geodesic triplet loss using a bounded Welsch M-estimator on intrinsic angular distance for robust parent-child learning, (ii) an anisotropic spherical SVGD regularizer with a vMF kernel and a pole-favoring structural score that prevents high-dimensional equatorial collapse while preserving uniform longitudinal spread, (iii) an asymmetric vMF-KL probabilistic objective that encodes directional containment by forcing parent distributions to be lower-concentration than children, and (iv) Riemannian Adam with parallel-transported momentum for manifold-consistent optimization. At inference, a coupled orbital retrieval step computes a parabolic decision boundary S_cos(z_q, z_c) > 1 − γ(Δr)² that tightens the angular threshold for candidates at similar orbital potential and loosens it for larger radial gaps, enabling efficient level-wise pruning before final angular re-ranking.

## Results

On single-parent taxonomy expansion, Polaris leads all metrics across Science, WordNet, and Environment, improving R@1 by ~11 points (46.18 vs. 34.8 for STEAM) and reducing Mean Rank by ~49% (16.19 vs. 31.7) on Science, and delivering the largest absolute gains on Environment (R@1 47.60, R@5 82.69, MR 6.48, a ~4× reduction over STEAM). On multi-parent DAGs (MeSH, Verb, Food), Polaris achieves H@1 of 33.56 and H@5 of 62.67 on MeSH (+15.4/+24.5 over STEAM) and the lowest MR (74.43, ~2× lower) on Food. On the multimodal CUB-200-2011 benchmark, Polaris reaches Precision@1 of 78.91 and MRR of 86.94, outperforming Gumbel Box (73.93/84.87) and CLIP-based baselines by over 45 points in Precision@1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Polaris outperforms all 13 baselines on every metric across the three single-parent benchmarks (Science, WordNet, Environment), with the strongest competitor being STEAM. The largest single-dataset gain is on Environment, where Polaris improves R@5 by +27.1 points (82.69 vs. 55.6) and cuts Mean Rank from 27.1 to 6.48 (~76% reduction) over STEAM. On multi-parent DAGs, Polaris dominates MeSH (H@1 33.56 vs. STEAM's 18.2) and Verb, but on Food, STEAM slightly edges Polaris on R@1 (39.1 vs. 35.83) and MRR (53.8 vs. 46.28), while Polaris still wins on H@5, R@5, and MR. Containment-based methods (Box, Gumbel Box) are the closest structured baselines on single-parent tasks but trail Polaris by 10–20 points in R@1; flat-space KGE methods (TransE, RotatE) and BERT+MLP are far behind, confirming that Euclidean dot-product scoring is poorly suited to asymmetric hierarchy. On the multimodal CUB-200-2011 setting, Polaris beats Gumbel Box by ~5 points in Precision@1 (78.91 vs. 73.93) and surpasses CLIP-based similarity baselines (CLIP-1, CLIP-2) by over 65 points, demonstrating that the coupled orbital geometry captures fine-grained visual hierarchy that direct image-text similarity cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

Polaris builds on the polar embedding line (Iwamoto et al., 2021) that decouples semantic direction from radial depth, but departs from prior polar methods that rely on ad hoc stabilizers (modulus wrapping, sector partitions, sigmoid rescaling) by using manifold-consistent spherical encoding via tangent-space projection and the exponential map. It also draws on hyperbolic and cone-based hierarchical embeddings (Nickel & Kiela, 2017; Ganea et al., 2018) and containment-based box embeddings (Jiang et al., 2023; Vilnis et al., 2018), but replaces their coupled geometry with a separation where direction encodes semantics and a structure-derived orbital potential encodes depth. The anisotropic SVGD regularizer with a vMF kernel and pole-favoring score is a novel application of Stein variational methods to prevent the equatorial collapse predicted by concentration-of-measure theorems, and the asymmetric vMF-KL objective extends probabilistic box embeddings (Dasgupta et al., 2020) to a directional distributional setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded explanation (concentration of measure, Theorems 2.3–2.4) for why naive spherical embeddings collapse to the equator in high dimensions, and offers a principled fix via anisotropic SVGD that is broadly applicable to any hyperspherical representation learning task. The consistent gains across single-parent trees, multi-parent DAGs, and multimodal image-to-label hierarchies suggest the geometric decoupling is representation-agnostic. Key open questions include whether the deterministic radial coordinate can be learned end-to-end under noisy or incomplete hierarchies, whether anisotropic directional distributions (Kent, matrix Bingham) can capture multi-faceted concepts, and whether the orbital gate can be relaxed for sparsely connected regions without sacrificing efficiency.
