## Problem

In heterogeneous federated learning, existing methods force personalization and generalization to compete within the same parameter space, creating a zero-sum trade-off where improving one degrades the other. Knowledge transfer mechanisms (logits, prototypes, averaged parameters) further act as a bottleneck that cannot satisfy diverse personalization needs across clients with different architectures and data distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260967/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client processes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) through a heterogeneous CNN backbone (~11.2 MB, ResNet-18-scale) with a distinct MoE configuration—varying numbers of personalized experts, expert architectures, and top-K routing. The output is a standard class label. Unusually, only the sparse subset of activated shared-expert sub-networks (not the full model) is uploaded to the server per round, and the server dynamically restructures the shared expert's internal expert count via SVD-based diagnosis every 20 rounds.

## Method

FedEMoE physically decouples personalization from generalization by splitting each client model into a Personalized Expert Group (PEG) of architecturally diverse parallel feature extractors capturing multi-scale client-specific patterns, and an Elastic Shared Expert (SE) that serves as a dynamic, pattern-diverse knowledge repository. The SE employs a MoE structure whose sub-experts can expand (single expert → MoE of N sub-experts) or shrink (MoE → weighted-merged single expert) based on weight-spectrum analysis: SVD of each layer's weight matrix yields an effective rank and tail energy that diagnose under-fitting (rapid spectral decay) versus over-fitting (heavy tail), with an EMA-adaptive threshold preventing runaway expansion. A bidirectional KL-divergence knowledge exchange continuously flows personalized knowledge into the SE and generalized knowledge back into PEG experts. On the server, sparse aggregation updates each sub-expert only over the clients that activated it, avoiding over-averaging while reducing communication cost.

## Results

Under the primary heterogeneous-model setting (K=50 clients, Dirichlet β=0.1), FedEMoE achieves 79.83% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, 47.69% on CIFAR-100, and 29.91% on Tiny-ImageNet. The gains over the strongest baselines are most pronounced on harder datasets: +32.43% on CIFAR-100 (vs. FedSA at 33.27%) and +40.62% on Tiny-ImageNet (vs. FedSA at 21.27%). In the homogeneous-model setting, FedEMoE still leads by +22.78% on CIFAR-100 and +26.33% on Tiny-ImageNet, confirming that the decoupling architecture benefits both heterogeneous and homogeneous scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedEMoE outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset and setting. In the heterogeneous-model setting (Table 1), the closest competitor is FedMRL (78.37% on CIFAR-10), which FedEMoE surpasses by 1.46%; on CIFAR-100 the gap widens dramatically to 11.68% over FedSA (33.27%), and on Tiny-ImageNet FedEMoE leads FedSA by 8.64%. In the homogeneous-model setting (Table 2), FedEMoE beats FedGC (38.71%) by 8.82% on CIFAR-100 and FedAvg (23.68%) by 6.30% on Tiny-ImageNet, while the CIFAR-10 margin is modest (+0.21% over FedALA). Against the four decoupling-based PFL methods (Table 17), FedEMoE (46.47%) edges out the strongest, FedCAC (45.93%), by 0.54% and leads DualFed (42.32%) by 4.15%. The largest absolute gains appear on Tiny-ImageNet at K=100, where FedEMoE reaches 25.82% versus FedSA's 17.43% (+48.05%), suggesting the elastic shared expert scales particularly well under severe data fragmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedEMoE carves new ground at the intersection of centralized Mixture-of-Experts architectures (Switch Transformers, ST-MoE, BASE layers) and model-based federated learning methods (FedGC, FedRep, FedBABU) that split models into shared and personalized components. While prior model-based FL methods use fixed-capacity shared branches, this paper repurposes the MoE sparsity mechanism as a dynamic, elastic shared expert whose sub-expert count can expand or shrink via weight-spectrum (SVD) diagnosis—a diagnostic tool borrowed from random-matrix-theory literature on spectral dynamics of weights. The personalized expert group (PEG) with multi-scale feature extraction extends the idea of heterogeneous classifiers in FedRep/FedAlt into a richer ensemble, and the bidirectional KL-divergence knowledge exchange generalizes standard distillation-based FL (FedKD, FedDF) into a two-way flow between personalized and shared components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of personalization and generalization at the architectural level (rather than merely the parameter level) offers a principled escape from the zero-sum trade-off that plagues existing HtFL methods, and the data-free SVD-based expert diagnosis is a practical, communication-efficient mechanism for adaptive capacity control in federated settings. However, all experiments are confined to image classification on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet with models of roughly ResNet-18 scale (~11 MB) and at most 100 communication rounds, leaving open whether the elastic mechanism scales to larger backbones, NLP, or tabular tasks. The absolute accuracies remain modest (e.g., ~25% on Tiny-ImageNet with 100 clients), and the server-side SVD cost, while offloaded, grows with expert width and may become non-trivial at larger model scales. A key open question is whether the elastic expansion/shrinkage strategy remains stable under highly non-stationary client participation or when the number of clients changes mid-training.
