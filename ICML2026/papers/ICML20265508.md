## Problem

Exact LAP solvers like Jonker-Volgenant guarantee optimality but suffer from O(N³) worst-case complexity, while neural approximations lack exactness and hit O(N²) memory walls beyond N≈2,000. Prior learned-dual warm-starting (Dinitz et al., 2021) relied on expensive iterative projections to repair infeasible predictions and did not scale to industrial sizes. This paper closes the gap by introducing a constructive feasibility mechanism and a row-independent architecture that enables neural warm-starting up to N=16,384 with strict optimality preserved.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense cost matrix C ∈ R^{N×N} and compresses it into 21 row-centric features per row (distributional statistics, entropy, rank-based competitiveness, and sinusoidal positional encodings), reducing the input dimension from O(N²) to O(N·21). It outputs the exact optimal permutation matrix X* from the seeded LAPJV solver, along with the feasible dual pair (û, v̂) used to initialize the search. A fallback check on the equality-subgraph average degree ρ ensures that if the neural prediction is poor, the system reverts to standard cold-start heuristics with no asymptotic degradation.

## Method

RowDualNet is a row-independent residual MLP (3 blocks, H=192) that predicts continuous row potentials û from per-row features, followed by a sparse k-NN refinement step that aggregates the K=16 columns with the lowest pseudo-reduced costs to inject competitive context without O(N²) memory. The Min-Trick then constructively sets v̂_j = min_i(C_ij − û_i), guaranteeing dual feasibility (û_i + v̂_j ≤ C_ij ∀i,j) by definition and eliminating iterative projection. The feasible dual pair is injected directly into the LAPJV solver's memory, seeding a dense equality subgraph that resolves ~76% of assignments in the greedy phase (vs. ~26% for cold-start), cutting augmenting-path searches by ~68%. Training is supervised with MAE loss on ground-truth duals from LAPJV plus a complementary-slackness regularizer on optimal edges, using multi-scale training across N ∈ {512, 1536, 2048, 3072} to encourage size-invariant representations.

## Results

On the Dense synthetic model at N=16,384, the method achieves a mean end-to-end speedup of ≈2.0× over SciPy and ≈2.5× over the LAP C++ solver. On the Block-Structured model at the same scale, speedups reach ≈2.25× vs SciPy and ≈4.0× vs LAP. Zero-shot generalization to real-world data yields ≈1.4–1.6× speedup on OpenStreetMap transportation networks (N=10,000) and ≈1.25× on the Large-Scale MOT tracking dataset (N≥8,000) over the fastest baseline (LAP), with ≈2× over SciPy. The optimality gap remains exactly 0% across all evaluated instances, and the neural warm-start reduces the solver's augmenting-path search effort by ≈68% (greedy match rate rises from ≈26% to ≈76%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks against three baselines: SciPy's linear_sum_assignment (modified Jonker-Volgenant with heuristic initialization), the optimized C++ LAP library (Kazmar, 2021), and the static 'Learned Median' warm-start from Dinitz et al. (2021). Against SciPy, the proposed method consistently wins across all four datasets, with speedups ranging from ≈1.4× (OpenStreetMap) to ≈2.25× (Block-Structured). Against the faster LAP C++ solver, the method still achieves clear wins: ≈2.5× on Dense, ≈4.0× on Block-Structured, ≈1.3–1.8× on OpenStreetMap, and ≈1.25× on MOT. Against Dinitz et al.'s Learned Median, the comparison is stark: the static median baseline drops below 1.0× (i.e., slower than cold-start) on the Block-Structured model and provides only negligible gains on Dense, while the proposed RowDualNet maintains robust 2.0×–4.0× speedups, confirming that instance-specific neural prediction is essential over distribution-level statistics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'learned duals' framework of Dinitz et al. (2021), which introduced the theoretical idea of using predicted dual variables to warm-start the Hungarian algorithm but relied on iterative projection for feasibility and simple statistical heuristics (coordinate-wise medians) that fail on unstructured data. It departs from the broader neural-solver line (GLAN, MAGNET, HybridGNN) that attempts to replace the combinatorial algorithm entirely with GNN-based link prediction, which suffers from O(N²) memory and lacks optimality guarantees. The key architectural departure is the constructive Min-Trick for feasibility (eliminating iterative projection) and the row-independent RowDualNet that reduces memory from O(N²) to O(N·D), enabling scale to N=16,384 where prior GNN-based approaches exhaust GPU memory around N≈2,000.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first learning-augmented LAP solver to scale to industrial problem sizes (N=16,384) while preserving strict optimality and worst-case safety, with zero-shot generalization to real-world transportation and tracking data without fine-tuning. The constructive feasibility mechanism (Min-Trick) is a clean, general-purpose trick that could transfer to other dual-based combinatorial solvers. Open questions include extending the paradigm to Min-Cost Flow and other network flow problems, and developing semi-supervised training regimes where optimal duals are unavailable. A limitation is that speedups diminish on sparse matrices (e.g., MOT tracking) where classical heuristics already restrict the search space, and the method requires a GPU for the neural component, adding deployment complexity.
