## Problem

Recovering editable, parametric CAD construction sequences from raw geometric data remains an open inverse problem: a single shape admits many valid construction sequences, and the search space is highly non-convex and combinatorial. Existing AI-based CAD reconstruction methods are largely restricted to simple sketch-and-extrude pipelines trained on low-complexity shapes, struggle with multi-operation designs involving Boolean logic and finishing features (fillets, chamfers), and often produce invalid or non-executable programs on complex inputs.

## Input / Output

CADFit takes a watertight 3D mesh as primary input (or an RGB image, from which a surface mesh is first reconstructed via Hunyuan3D and post-processed with Taubin smoothing and decimation to enforce watertightness). The output is an ordered, executable CadQuery/OpenCascade program composed of parametric operations (EXTRUDE, REVOLVE, FILLET, CHAMFER, UNION, CUT) with continuous parameters (heights, angles, radii) and geometric references (sketch profiles, edges, existing solids). The program is validated at every step by the CAD kernel: any operation that fails to execute is discarded, guaranteeing zero invalid outputs.

## Method

CADFit formulates mesh-to-CAD reconstruction as an explicit IoU-driven optimization over executable CAD programs rather than amortized sequence prediction. The single-pass pipeline extracts candidate sketch profiles from planar face clusters and axis-aligned slicing planes, filters them with a learned DINOv2+MLP sketch prior (trained on optimization-derived labels to rank useful profiles), generates discrete EXTRUDE/REVOLVE candidates via one-sided Chamfer parameter sweeps with stable-interval selection, and assembles a compact program through IoU-guided forward greedy selection followed by backward marginal pruning. An outer iterative residual loop then refines the program by reconstructing under-reconstructed regions (added via UNION) and over-reconstructed regions (removed via CUT) until residual volumes fall below a threshold. This decoupled design—where perception models supply geometry and CADFit performs structured parametric optimization—enables the same pipeline to operate on meshes from CAD exports, point clouds, or image-based reconstruction without retraining.

## Results

On mesh-to-CAD reconstruction, CADFit achieves IoU of 0.964 on DeepCAD, 0.921 on Fusion360, and 0.662 on the hardest ABC subset (151–1500 STEP faces), while maintaining zero invalid outputs (IR = 0.000) across all benchmarks. The largest gains appear on complex geometry: on ABC Hard, CADFit more than doubles the IoU of the strongest prior method (0.662 vs. 0.438 for Cadrille) and reduces Chamfer Distance by 20%. In the end-to-end image-to-CAD setting, CADFit reaches IoU 0.669 (Easy), 0.545 (Medium), and 0.380 (Hard), outperforming all baselines by 39–60% in IoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CADFit outperforms all four baselines across every benchmark and complexity level. Against GenCAD-3D, the gap is largest: CADFit achieves 0.964 vs. 0.255 IoU on DeepCAD and 0.662 vs. 0.135 on ABC Hard, reflecting GenCAD-3D's limited generalization beyond its training distribution. Against CAD-Recode (the strongest prior method on complex shapes), CADFit improves IoU by 17.7% on DeepCAD (0.964 vs. 0.818) and by 98.8% on ABC Hard (0.662 vs. 0.333), while also reducing IR from 0.010 to 0.000. Against Cadrille, the closest competitor, CADFit gains 10.6% IoU on DeepCAD (0.964 vs. 0.872) and 51.1% on ABC Hard (0.662 vs. 0.438), with a consistent 18–21% reduction in Chamfer Distance. In the image-to-CAD setting (Table 2), CADFit also beats CAD-Coder (a direct vision-language model baseline) by 26.4% IoU on ABC Easy (0.669 vs. 0.405) and 12.3% on ABC Hard (0.380 vs. 0.171), while maintaining zero invalid outputs where CAD-Coder reports 5.3% IR on Hard.

## Relation to Prior Work

CADFit departs from the dominant learning-based paradigm for CAD reconstruction (DeepCAD, GenCAD-3D, CAD-Recode, Cadrille), which relies on amortized autoregressive sequence prediction over sketch-and-extrude programs, and instead formulates reconstruction as explicit IoU-driven optimization over executable CadQuery/OpenCascade programs. Conceptually, it extends the inverse-CSG optimization framework (Du et al., 2018; Kania et al., 2020) from simple primitive boolean trees to parametric sketch-based operations (extrude, revolve, fillet, chamfer) with kernel-validated execution. The learned DINOv2+MLP sketch prior is a novel auxiliary component that repurposes a visual encoder as a stochastic candidate filter within the optimization loop, rather than as a generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CADFit addresses a practical bottleneck: the inability to accurately reconstruct complex, multi-operation CAD programs has constrained the diversity and quality of datasets available for training learning-based CAD models. By producing compact, valid construction sequences (often shorter than ground-truth programs) with zero invalid outputs, it offers a path to generating higher-quality supervision at scale. Key limitations remain: the method is slow (minutes per shape vs. seconds for feed-forward baselines), the operation set excludes lofts, sweeps, and intersections, and smooth high-curvature geometries without stable planar faces are a persistent failure mode. Open questions include whether the optimization loop can be accelerated, whether the operator set can be extended, and whether the compact programs can serve as structured policies for RL-based CAD agents.
