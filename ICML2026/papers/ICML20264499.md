## Problem

Existing adaptive rounding algorithms for LLM quantization (GPTQ/LDLQ) minimize a local layerwise activation error as a proxy for the end-to-end KL divergence, ignoring the effect of quantization on all downstream layers. This makes the proxy a poor surrogate, and no prior quantization algorithm comes with theoretical end-to-end error bounds. The paper asks what structural properties of Hessian approximations admit both tractable rounding and provably good quantized models.

## Input / Output

YAQA takes a pre-trained LLM's weight matrices, a calibration dataset (RedPajama v1, 20M tokens for Sketch A or 64K sequences for Sketch B), and an arbitrary quantizer specification (scalar, vector, or lattice-based). It produces quantized weights in the same representable set as the chosen quantizer, adding zero inference overhead. The method also outputs Kronecker-factored Hessian sketches (H_O ∈ R^{m×m} and H_I ∈ R^{n×n}) computed via power iteration on the real Fisher, which require O(n²) + O(m²) storage per layer—roughly double LDLQ's single H_I.

## Method

YAQA replaces LDLQ's input-only Hessian H_1 with a Kronecker-factored approximation H_O ⊗ H_I, introducing symmetric output-side feedback (L_O^T Δ and L_O^T Δ L_I) alongside the existing input-side feedback. The paper introduces the structural nilpotence degree (SND) to characterize convergence: a Kronecker-factored Hessian yields SND ≤ m+n−1, making the fixed-point iteration tractable in m+n parallelizable steps. The first end-to-end error bound shows the true second-order error is controlled by the cosine similarity between H_O ⊗ H_I and the true Hessian ∇²L(W*), motivating near-optimal sketches via power iteration. Two practical sketches are provided: Sketch A assumes token independence within a layer (biased but low-variance, ~20 GPU-hours for 10B models), and Sketch B performs one round of power iteration on the real Fisher over full sequences (unbiased, ~30 GPU-hours). Empirically, YAQA reduces KL by ~30% over LDLQ, outperforms GuidedQuant and DiscQuant, and achieves lower KL than Google's Gemma 3 QAT recipe while adding no inference cost.

## Results

On Llama 3.1 70B Instruct quantized to 2 bits with the QTIP quantizer, YAQA-B achieves a KL divergence of 0.335 (Wikitext2), reducing the KL by ~33% over LDLQ (0.497) and ~13% over GuidedQuant (0.383), while also improving 0-shot accuracy from 65.45% to 66.19%. On Llama 3.1 8B Instruct at 2 bits, YAQA-B achieves KL 0.241 vs. LDLQ's 0.356 (a 32% reduction). On Gemma 3 12B Instruct at 4 bits, YAQA-B achieves a KL of 0.056, lower than Google's QAT model (0.089) despite requiring 100× less data, while maintaining competitive 0-shot accuracy (69.90% vs. 70.83%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

YAQA consistently outperforms all five baselines across every model and bit-width tested. Against LDLQ (equivalent to GPTQ), YAQA-B reduces KL by approximately 30–33% (e.g., 0.335 vs. 0.497 on 70B 2-bit; 0.241 vs. 0.356 on 8B 2-bit) while also improving perplexity and 0-shot accuracy. Against GuidedQuant, which uses a block-diagonal empirical Fisher approximation, YAQA-B achieves lower KL across all settings (e.g., 0.335 vs. 0.383 on 70B 2-bit), and the paper shows GuidedQuant's performance plateaus after just 4 blocks. Against DiscQuant (gradient-descent-based), YAQA-A achieves KL 0.028 vs. DiscQuant's 0.061 on Llama 3.1 8B INT4, demonstrating that second-order adaptive rounding can outperform descent methods. Against Google's Gemma 3 QAT recipe, YAQA-B achieves a lower KL (0.056 vs. 0.089) despite using 100× less data, though QAT retains a slight edge in 0-shot accuracy (70.83% vs. 69.90%), suggesting QAT produces a somewhat different model. Against CBQ on Llama 2 7B, YAQA-B achieves lower perplexity at all bit-widths (e.g., 9.22 vs. 11.30 at INT2 on C4).

## Relation to Prior Work

YAQA generalizes the LDLQ/GPTQ line of local layerwise adaptive rounding (which uses the Hessian of immediate activation error H₁ as a proxy) to a Kronecker-factored Hessian H_O ⊗ H_I that captures both input- and output-side curvature, drawing on the Kronecker-factored Fisher approximations from KFAC and Shampoo in the optimization literature. It departs from prior 'end-to-end' methods like GuidedQuant and SqueezeLLM, which use ad-hoc block-diagonal approximations of the empirical Fisher, by instead power-iterating on the real Fisher (which equals the true Hessian ∇²L(W*)) to obtain near-optimal Kronecker factors. The incoherence processing via the Random Hadamard Transform is inherited from QuIP#/QuIP, and the structural nilpotence degree (SND) framework provides a new theoretical lens connecting Hessian structure to rounding tractability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide end-to-end error bounds for any quantization algorithm, with the bound governed by the cosine similarity between the Hessian sketch and the true Hessian — a clean, actionable criterion for sketch design. Practically, YAQA is a drop-in replacement for LDLQ in existing quantization pipelines (QuIP#, QTIP, etc.) that reduces KL by ~30% and, notably, achieves lower KL than Google's Gemma 3 QAT recipe despite using 100× less data, suggesting that second-order PTQ with the right Hessian can match or exceed expensive training-based approaches. Open questions include whether the bounds are tight, whether the approach extends to weight-activation quantization, and whether further power iteration rounds or alternative sketch constructions could close the remaining gap to the true Hessian.
