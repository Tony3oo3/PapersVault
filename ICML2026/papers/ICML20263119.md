## Problem

While SFT with teacher forcing has been shown to provably learn parity via CoT (Kim & Suzuki, 2025), the theoretical understanding of RL fine-tuning with verifiable process rewards for CoT learning was entirely absent, and how RL and SFT differ in their learning dynamics remained unexplored. This paper addresses both gaps by providing a unified theoretical framework for analyzing the learning dynamics of RL and SFT on a broad class of k-sparse Boolean functions that are recursively decomposable into fixed 2-sparse sub-tasks.

## Input / Output

The input is a d-bit binary sequence x ∈ {-1,+1}^d and a k-sparse Boolean function Φ_k determined by a random subset B ⊆ [d] of size k = 2^T. The function is recursively decomposed into T intermediate steps via a complete binary tree, where each node computes a fixed 2-sparse Boolean function φ_2 over its two children. The one-layer transformer iteratively generates the CoT chain y^(1), …, y^(T) autoregressively, with each step y^(t) containing d_t = k/2^t tokens, and the final answer is y_1^(T) = Φ_k(x).

## Method

The paper analyzes a one-layer transformer (positional encoding + single-head self-attention + feedforward activation) equipped with a 'pretrained' mask that enforces the binary-tree dependency structure, so each token attends only to its two child nodes in the previous step. The key technical contribution is identifying a critical gradient component γ that captures whether the gradient signal can distinguish relevant positions (child nodes) from irrelevant ones; separation of γ between relevant and irrelevant positions is shown to be a sufficient condition for learnability. For RL with verifiable process rewards, the transformer learns the entire CoT chain simultaneously in a single sign-policy-gradient update (Thm. 3.1), while for SFT without teacher forcing the same separation condition guarantees learnability but the transformer exhibits intrinsic step-by-step learning, requiring one gradient update per CoT step (Thm. 3.3). The separation condition is verified for k-PARITY, k-AND, and k-OR, and a complementary negative result (Prop. 3.2) shows that RL with only a final reward is provably hard for parity, highlighting the necessity of process-level supervision.

## Results

The paper establishes that a one-layer transformer with CoT provably learns k-sparse Boolean functions (k-PARITY, k-AND, k-OR) under a separation condition on a critical gradient component γ. For RL with verifiable process rewards, the transformer learns the entire T-step CoT chain in a single sign-gradient update (Thm. 3.1), with attention scores converging to the optimal 0.5/0 pattern at relevant/irrelevant positions. For SFT without teacher forcing, the transformer learns the chain step-by-step, requiring exactly T sign-gradient updates (one per CoT step) (Thm. 3.3). For k-PARITY specifically, Thm. 4.1 gives the exact attention score trajectory: σ(s) = [1/2]·[1/(1 + ((d_{t-1}-2)/2)·exp(-2ηs))] at relevant positions, confirming convergence with learning rate η = Ω(ln(d/ε)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without traditional empirical baselines; the key 'comparison' is between learning paradigms. RL with verifiable process rewards achieves simultaneous learning of all T CoT steps in one update, matching the efficiency of SFT with teacher forcing (Kim & Suzuki, 2025), despite fundamentally different mechanisms. In contrast, SFT without teacher forcing (the paper's main SFT setting) exhibits stepwise learning requiring T updates, which the paper shows is an intrinsic property of autoregressive generation rather than an artifact of missing data augmentation or curriculum learning. RL with only a final (outcome-based) reward is shown to be provably hard for k-PARITY (Prop. 3.2), with policy gradient variance exponentially small in d, in stark contrast to the learnability under process rewards. The paper argues that empirical RL-vs-SFT comparisons (e.g., 'SFT memorizes, RL generalizes') conflate the optimization method with supervision design (process vs. outcome reward, teacher forcing vs. self-generated prefixes), and that controlling for these factors is essential for fair comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on provable CoT learning in transformers initiated by Kim & Suzuki (2025), who showed a one-layer transformer learns k-PARITY in one SFT update with teacher forcing, and by Wies et al. (2023), who introduced the recursive binary-tree decomposition of sparse Boolean functions into 2-sparse sub-tasks. The key departure is twofold: (1) it introduces RL with verifiable process rewards (inspired by DeepSeekMath, Shao et al. 2024) as a training paradigm for CoT, providing the first theoretical characterization of RL fine-tuning dynamics for this class of problems; and (2) it removes teacher forcing and data augmentation from SFT, revealing that the resulting step-by-step learning is an intrinsic property of autoregressive generation rather than an artifact of curriculum design. A unifying 'critical gradient component' is identified as the sufficient condition for learnability across both paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central conceptual contribution is reframing the RL-vs-SFT comparison: learning behavior (simultaneous vs. step-by-step) is determined not by the optimization method per se but by whether supervision remains informative for the states the model actually trains on (process rewards on sampled trajectories vs. fixed labels under self-generated prefixes). This has direct implications for how practitioners should design and interpret post-training experiments. However, the analysis is confined to a one-layer transformer with special attention masks and three specific Boolean functions, so extending to multi-layer architectures, realistic reasoning tasks, and finite-sample regimes remains open. The paper also shows RL with only final reward is provably hard for parity (exponentially small gradient signal), reinforcing the practical importance of process-level supervision.
