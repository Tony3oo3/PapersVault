## Problem

Current 7B GUI grounding models achieve only ~50% accuracy on high-resolution professional software screens (ScreenSpot-Pro), making pixel-level actions like precise clicking unreliable. Fixed coarse-to-fine cropping heuristics offer limited gains, and existing RL approaches for multi-turn tool-use grounding suffer from training collapse due to sparse, volatile rewards when the model generates malformed tool calls. The paper addresses both the architectural limitation (static single-pass grounding) and the training instability that prevents effective RL for iterative, tool-coordinated visual localization.

## Input / Output

The model receives a natural-language instruction (e.g., 'Click the Send button') and a high-resolution GUI screenshot (up to 3840×2160 / 4K). During inference it may iteratively invoke three tools—crop (rectangular region by coordinates), extract (quadrant selection by position), and find_color (CIE-Lab color matching)—each producing a cropped sub-image and a top-left offset that are appended to the dialogue history. The final output is an absolute (x, y) click coordinate on the original image, computed by adding the tracked offset of the image where the target was located to the relative coordinate returned by the model. Over 95% of test cases require only a single tool call, with an average of ~1.05 calls.

## Method

GUI-Spotlight is a think-with-image agentic model that iteratively narrows its visual focus like a spotlight by invoking specialized tools (crop, extract, find_color) before committing to a final coordinate, maintaining a registry of all cropped images and their offsets relative to the original. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) SFT warm-up on 2,561 multi-turn tool-invocation trajectories generated by Qwen2.5-VL-72B to teach tool composition; (2) RL on 12K samples using a modified GSPO objective augmented with an auxiliary cross-entropy loss computed only on format-valid and result-correct samples, which prevents the training collapse observed with vanilla GRPO/GSPO in multi-turn tool-use settings; (3) refined RL on 4K high-resolution samples with bucketed uniform sampling across tool types to balance exploration. The five-component reward combines a sparse answer reward (0.3), dense IoU-based crop reward (0.25), sparse extract and color rewards (0.05, 0.2), and a format-validity reward (0.2). With only 18.5K total training samples, the model achieves 52.8% on ScreenSpot-Pro, surpassing models trained on 1.5M–10M samples.

## Results

On ScreenSpot-Pro (4K professional GUI screenshots), GUI-Spotlight initialized from UI-TARS-1.5-7B achieves 52.8% overall accuracy using only 18.5K training samples, surpassing V2P-7B (50.6%, 9.6M samples), UI-Venus-7B (50.8%, 107K samples), and GTA-1-7B (50.1%, 1.56M samples). On UI-Vision (desktop GUI), the same model reaches 23.4% average, outperforming all other 7B open-source models and approaching UI-TARS-72B (25.5%). On OSWorld-G, it attains 62.7% average, competitive with 72B-scale models. The Qwen2.5-VL-7B-initialized variant gains +11.9 points over its raw baseline (26.8% → 38.7%) on ScreenSpot-Pro, demonstrating transfer beyond UI-specialized backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GUI-Spotlight (UI-TARS init.) achieves 52.8% on ScreenSpot-Pro, outperforming its backbone UI-TARS-1.5-7B (38.7%) by +14.1 points, UI-Venus-7B (50.8%) by +2.0, V2P-7B (50.6%) by +2.2, and GTA-1-7B (50.1%) by +2.7, all while using 18.5K samples versus 107K–9.6M for those baselines. Against UGround-V1-7B (31.1%, 10M samples), the gap is +21.7 points; against Aguvis-7B (22.9%, 4.2M) and See-Click-7B (1.1%, 1M), the margins are +29.9 and +51.7 respectively. On UI-Vision, GUI-Spotlight (23.4%) surpasses UI-TARS-1.5-7B (18.1%) by +5.3 and UI-Venus-Ground-7B (26.5%) is the only 7B model that edges it out by 3.1 points. On OSWorld-G, GUI-Spotlight (62.7%) beats UI-TARS-1.5-7B (61.9%) by +0.8 and approaches UI-Venus-Ground-72B (70.4%). The Qwen2.5-VL-7B-initialized variant (38.7%) improves over the raw Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline (26.8%) by +11.9 points, confirming that the RL training transfers to non-UI-specific backbones. In the downstream agent evaluation on OSWorld, GUI-Spotlight as a grounding module yields 60.90% versus 58.17% for UI-TARS-1.5-7B, a +2.73 point gain in end-to-end task completion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GUI visual grounding task (established by SeeClick, UGround, UI-TARS, and others) by introducing a 'think-with-image' iterative tool-use framework where a multimodal LLM dynamically invokes crop, extract, and find-color tools to progressively narrow focus. Methodologically, it builds directly on GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025) but introduces a critical stabilization modification—tool-filtered positive examples with an auxiliary cross-entropy loss—that prevents the RL collapse observed in vanilla GRPO/GSPO for multi-turn tool-use. The approach also draws on coarse-to-fine localization strategies (Li et al., 2025) and attention-inspired focus mechanisms (Shu et al., 2022), but departs from fixed-heuristic cropping by making the cropping decisions learned via RL rather than rule-based.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is the data efficiency: 18.5K curated samples suffice to surpass models trained on 9.6M–10M samples, suggesting that structured RL with tool-augmented feedback can substitute for raw data scale in visual grounding. The GSPO stabilization recipe (tool-filtered positives + CE loss) is a reusable technique for anyone training multi-turn tool-use policies via RL. However, the inference-time benefit is modest—over 95% of cases require only a single tool call—raising the question of whether the iterative framework's value is primarily a training signal rather than a genuine multi-step reasoning capability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly multi-step GUI tasks (beyond single-click grounding), whether the three-tool set is optimal, and whether the gains persist at larger model scales.
