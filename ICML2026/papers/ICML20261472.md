## Problem

Existing ViT autoencoders degrade outside their training resolution due to fixed positional embeddings, and their reliance on adversarial losses makes scaling decoders beyond ~350M parameters impractical. This prevents exploring the full reconstruction-generation trade-off mediated by compression ratio r, since both autoencoder and generator capacity must scale jointly to advance the Pareto frontier.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261472/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at arbitrary resolutions and aspect ratios, which are gray-padded to dimensions divisible by the patch size (16 or 32) and patchified into non-overlapping tokens. It produces a continuous latent tensor of shape h×w×c (where spatial dimensions are reduced by factor f and c is the number of latent channels), which the decoder maps back to a reconstructed image. At inference, 2D sliding window attention (radius 8) can replace full attention to reduce complexity from O(L²) to O(L·r²), enabling 8K image processing in seconds where CNN baselines run out of memory.

## Method

ViTok-v2 uses an asymmetric ViT architecture with a shallow 4-layer encoder (~10% of total parameters) and a deep decoder scaled up to 4.5B parameters (3072 width, 40 depth), connected by a linear bottleneck with LayerNorm regularization. Native resolution generalization is achieved through NaFlex-style training (90% at 256-token budget, 10% at 1024-token budget) combined with 2D RoPE positional encoding, eliminating grid artifacts at non-training resolutions. A novel DINOv3 perceptual tile loss—sampling random 224×224 tiles, extracting multi-block features from a frozen DINOv3-S encoder, L2-normalizing per token, and computing MSE—replaces both LPIPS and GAN objectives, enabling stable training at 5B parameters without adversarial instability. The full loss is Charbonnier + 0.1·SSIM + λ_p·DINOv3, and the model is trained on ~2B images with FSDP in bfloat16/float8 on 128 H200 GPUs at ~90% MFU.

## Results

ViTok-v2 5B achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all compression ratios at 256p and 512p: at r=12 on ImageNet 256p, it reaches 34.2 dB PSNR (+3.1 dB over FLUX.2) and 0.924 SSIM, while at COCO 512p it reaches 35.9 dB PSNR and 0.938 SSIM. On high-resolution DIV8K 2048p, ViTok-v2 achieves rFID 0.06 (87% better than FLUX.2's 0.48) with 34.9 dB PSNR. At 8192p, ViTok-v2 is the only method that completes reconstruction (all CNN baselines OOM), processing 4K images in 0.4–1.2 s via sliding window attention versus >60 s or OOM for CNN methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViTok-v2 5B dominates on pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM) across every baseline and compression ratio: at r=12 on ImageNet 256p it leads FLUX.2 by +3.1 dB PSNR (34.2 vs 31.1) and +0.037 SSIM (0.924 vs 0.887), and leads VA-VAE by +3.5 dB PSNR. On perceptual metrics (rFID), FLUX.2 (0.15) and VA-VAE (0.14) retain an edge at 256p due to adversarial training, but ViTok-v2's high-DINO variant (‡) narrows this to 0.30, and at 512p and 2048p ViTok-v2 wins outright (rFID 0.41 at COCO 512p vs FLUX.2's 0.45; rFID 0.06 at DIV8K 2048p vs FLUX.2's 0.48). Against SD-VAE (r=48), ViTok-v2 gains +2.8 dB PSNR and +0.091 SSIM; against RAE (r=48), the gap is even larger at +9.7 dB PSNR. At high resolutions (4K–8K), all CNN baselines (FLUX.2, SD-VAE, DC-AE) either time out (>60 s) or run out of memory, while ViTok-v2 with sliding window attention processes 4K in 0.4–1.2 s and 8K in 1.3–5.7 s, making it the only viable tokenizer at 8192p.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViTok-v2 is the direct successor to ViTok (Hansen-Estruch et al., 2025), which established the compression-ratio axis and asymmetric encoder-decoder design for ViT autoencoders, but was limited to 350M parameters, fixed 256×256 training, and adversarial losses. The paper integrates NaFlex-style native aspect-ratio training (Dehghani et al., 2023; Tschannen et al., 2025) with 2D RoPE to overcome the resolution generalization failure that plagues prior ViT-AEs, and introduces a DINOv3-based perceptual tile loss that replaces both LPIPS and GAN objectives—a departure from the standard VAE training recipe (Eq. 1) used by SD-VAE, FLUX, and prior ViT tokenizers. The joint AE-flow scaling study extends the reconstruction-generation trade-off analysis from ViTok's single 450M generator to a 2×2 grid of AE sizes (350M, 5B) and flow model sizes (450M, 1.2B), providing a more complete picture of how both components interact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DINOv3 perceptual loss is a practically important simplification: it eliminates the training instability of adversarial objectives while achieving 58% better rFID than LPIPS, making billion-scale autoencoder training feasible without GAN mode-collapse risks. The ability to process 8K images (where all CNN-based tokenizers OOM) via sliding-window attention opens high-resolution generation and editing workflows previously intractable with learned tokenizers. The joint scaling finding—that larger generators flip the optimal compression ratio preference—provides a concrete design principle for future latent diffusion pipelines. Open questions include whether the DINOv3 loss generalizes to video or 3D tokenization, whether the reconstruction-generation trade-off can be further improved beyond the current Pareto frontier, and whether the 5B decoder's benefits persist when paired with even larger (e.g., 5B+) flow models.
