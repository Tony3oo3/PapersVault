## Problem

Existing geometric characterizations of memorization in diffusion models (LID, sharpness) are inherently global and cannot reveal where within a generated image memorization occurs, while the prior spatial method Bright Ending relies on model-specific cross-attention signals that produce false positives. Additionally, the widely used score-difference detection metric lacks a principled explanation for why the gap between conditional and unconditional scores is the decisive signal and why the unconditional model is the appropriate reference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262130/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt, a trained diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion v1.4 or v2.1), and a baseline model (either the unconditional version of the same model or a less-trained checkpoint such as SD v1.1 for SD v1.4). It produces a 256×256 spatial localization map (upsampled from the 64×64 latent resolution) where high values indicate memorized regions, computed at the final DDIM sampling step (t≈0) with 50 inference steps and CFG scale 7.5. Spatially averaging the map yields a scalar detection score; the curvature-based variants require K Hutchinson random vectors (K=16 in main experiments) for Hessian-vector product estimation.

## Method

The paper characterizes local memorization as coordinate-wise variance collapse, which manifests geometrically as high diagonal Hessian curvature of the log-density. To isolate overfitting-driven memorization (OD-Mem) from intrinsic data constraints (DD-Mem), it subtracts the curvature of an underfitted baseline—either the unconditional model or a less-trained checkpoint—yielding the curvature-difference metrics Δh_∅ and Δh_θ̃, computed efficiently via Hutchinson estimators. A key theoretical contribution is a Fisher-information-type identity (Proposition 4.2) that shows the squared score difference (s_θ(x_t,c) − s_θ(x_t))² is an unbiased proxy for the curvature difference, providing a geometric justification for Wen et al.'s score-difference metric as a spatially aggregated curvature-difference principle. This reframes the unconditional model not merely as a conditioning reference but as an underfitted baseline capturing intrinsic data-manifold curvature, explaining why subtracting it isolates overfitting-driven memorization.

## Results

On memorization localization (Table 1), the proposed curvature-difference metric Δh_θ̃ achieves the best IoU across most settings: 0.921 on SD v1.4 TV-only, 0.947 on SD v2.1 TV-only, and 0.953 for Δh_∅ on SD v1.4 All (vs. 0.564 for Bright Ending). On memorization detection (Table 2), the score-difference surrogate Δs_θ̃ reaches AUC 0.998 / TPR@1%FPR 0.988 on SD v1.4 and 0.993 / 0.968 on SD v2.1, substantially outperforming BE-attention (0.886 / 0.390 and 0.945 / 0.877 respectively). The raw curvature baseline diag(−H_θ(x_t,c)) performs near chance on detection (AUC 0.861 on SD v1.4, 0.775 on SD v2.1), confirming that subtracting an underfitted baseline is essential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is Bright Ending (BE) from Chen et al. (2025), which localizes memorization via cross-attention at the final denoising step. On SD v1.4, the proposed methods decisively outperform BE: Δh_θ̃ achieves IoU 0.921 vs. BE's 0.751 on TV-only, and Δh_∅ reaches IoU 0.953 vs. BE's 0.564 on the All setting. On SD v2.1 TV-only, the gap narrows (Δh_θ̃ IoU 0.947 vs. BE 0.933), and on SD v2.1 TV+Non-mem, BE actually achieves higher IoU (0.956 vs. 0.828 for Δh_θ̃), which the authors attribute to dataset bias—85% of SD v2.1 memorized prompts produce visually simple floor images where attention-based methods are naturally effective. For detection (Table 2), all four proposed difference-based metrics substantially outperform BE-attention on both models (e.g., AUC 0.998 vs. 0.886 on SD v1.4), while the raw curvature baseline without subtraction performs near chance, confirming that the underfitted-baseline subtraction is the critical design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometric memorization framework of Ross et al. (2025, MMH/LID) and Jeon et al. (2025, sharpness) from global, scalar characterizations to a coordinate-wise, spatially resolved analysis, arguing that where variance collapses matters as much as how much. It introduces a curvature-difference principle—subtracting the Hessian diagonal of an underfitted baseline (unconditional or less-trained model)—to isolate overfitting-driven memorization from intrinsic data-driven curvature, and derives via a Fisher information identity that the widely used score-difference metric of Wen et al. (2024) is a spatially aggregated proxy for this curvature difference. The primary empirical comparison is against Bright Ending (Chen et al., 2025), a cross-attention-based localization method that is model-specific and prone to false positives in complex regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The curvature-difference framework offers a model-agnostic, geometrically principled alternative to attention-based localization, and the Fisher information connection provides the first unified explanation for why score-difference detection works (it filters intrinsic data curvature, not merely measures conditioning strength). However, the method is explicitly scoped to verbatim/template memorization and does not capture concept-level memorization (e.g., celebrity faces, artistic styles), which the authors acknowledge as an important open direction. Practical limitations include the computational cost of Hessian-vector products for the curvature-based variants (though score-difference surrogates mitigate this) and the dependence on access to a suitable less-trained baseline checkpoint.
