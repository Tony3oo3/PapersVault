## Problem

Multimodal forecasting models that fuse time series with textual context frequently fail to outperform unimodal baselines, a gap the authors attribute to unverified and low-quality context in existing CAF datasets. Without provably useful context, it is impossible to determine whether a model's underperformance stems from architectural inability to exploit text or from the text simply lacking complementary predictive signal. The paper tackles this by constructing a large-scale dataset where context usefulness is empirically verified through a forecasting-based filtering procedure.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a univariate numerical history window z_{1:P} (length sampled between the prediction horizon Q and 512) and a free-form natural language context string (150–250 words of scenario-style text describing plausible events, background, or constraints). It outputs a probabilistic forecast distribution over Q future steps (Q ranges from 5 to 64 depending on data frequency), produced via ancestral sampling with Monte Carlo trajectories from the decoder. When the prediction horizon exceeds the model's native 64-step length, forecasting proceeds autoregressively in 64-step chunks, appending per-step sample medians to the numerical context between calls.

## Method

The paper introduces a generate-then-verify data augmentation pipeline and a dual-modality forecasting architecture. For data construction, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct generates scenario-style contexts conditioned on both the historical and future portions of each window, then a Direct Prompt forecaster (GPT-5.2) verifies that including the context reduces CRPS relative to a no-context baseline; only passing windows are retained, yielding CAF-7M (7.4M training windows, 904 verified test windows across 11 domains). For modeling, DoubleCast augments the Chronos T5 decoder with DualT5 blocks: each decoder layer gains a secondary cross-attention sublayer that attends to frozen Qwen3-14B text embeddings (extracted from the second-to-last hidden layer) via learned key/value adapter projections, placed after the original Chronos encoder-decoder cross-attention. Training is parameter-efficient: the entire Chronos backbone and text encoder are frozen, and only the text cross-attention parameters, their layer norms, and the FFN within dual blocks are updated (with a 0.01× learning-rate multiplier on FFN parameters), enabling the model to learn context conditioning without disrupting pretrained numerical forecasting capabilities.

## Results

On the CAF-7M verified test set (ALL split), DoubleCast achieves a normalized CRPS of 0.231 and a 71% win rate against Chronos, outperforming the unimodal Chronos baseline (CRPS 0.278) and the state-of-the-art ChatTime model (CRPS 0.385, 25% win rate). Context ablation confirms active utilization: removing context increases CRPS by 18.3% and swapping it increases CRPS by 26.5%, while TimeLLM trained on the same data shows no such degradation. On the real-world CGTSF benchmark, DoubleCast fine-tuned on CGTSF after CAF-7M pretraining outperforms both Chronos and ChatTime on 11 of 12 dataset-prediction-length pairs, demonstrating simulated-to-real transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoubleCast (CRPS 0.231, Win Rate 0.71) decisively outperforms the unimodal Chronos backbone (0.278), the state-of-the-art multimodal ChatTime (0.385, Win Rate 0.25), and all TimeLLM variants (0.501–3.309) on the CAF-7M test set. It also beats the Direct Prompt method with Qwen3-14B (0.296) and all additional numerical baselines including Chronos-2 (0.262), FlowState (0.296), TimeMixer++, Multi-Patch Prediction, and Aurora (0.589). The only method that outperforms DoubleCast is DP GPT-5.2 (0.217), which is expected since GPT-5.2 was the verifier used to filter the test set. On the real-world CGTSF benchmark, finetuned DoubleCast achieves the best CRPS on 11 of 12 settings (e.g., 0.1234 vs. Chronos 0.1297 and ChatTime 0.1478 on PTF hist=120), while zero-shot DoubleCast beats ChatTime on all 12 settings but lags Chronos on MSPG due to a prediction-length mismatch (96 steps vs. 64-step training maximum). On Time-MMD, finetuned DoubleCast improves over zero-shot on all 9 domains and beats Chronos on 7 of 9, though it remains behind strong numerical backbones like iTransformer and PatchTST.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Chronos time series foundation model (Ansari et al., 2024), augmenting its T5 decoder with a lightweight DualT5 cross-attention mechanism to condition forecasts on textual context. It positions itself within the context-aided forecasting (CAF) literature, which includes alignment-based methods (TimeLLM, ChatTime, UniTime) and prompt-based methods (Direct Prompt, LLM Processes), but departs from prior data construction approaches that rely on web-scraped news, rigid templates, or LLM-generated descriptions conditioned only on historical values. The key methodological departure is the generate-then-verify pipeline: contexts are generated by an LLM conditioned on both history and future, then filtered by a strong forecaster (GPT-5.2 via Direct Prompt) to retain only windows where context measurably improves CRPS. The closest concurrent work is Aurora (Wu et al., 2026), which also performs large-scale multimodal pretraining, but CAF-7M adds the verification step and provides both training and evaluation data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete bottleneck in CAF: the absence of large-scale training data where context is provably complementary to numerical histories, which has likely contributed to the observed 'modality gap' where multimodal models fail to beat unimodal baselines. The 7M-window dataset with a rigorously verified 904-instance test set provides the field with a much-needed benchmark that can distinguish between 'the model can't use context' and 'the context isn't useful.' Key open questions include whether the verifier-based filtering introduces bias toward context patterns exploitable by a specific LLM, whether synthetic forward-looking contexts generalize to the messier, less structured context encountered in deployment, and whether the architecture-dependent finding (DoubleCast exploits context while TimeLLM does not, on the same data) extends to other model families. The paper also leaves the training corpus unfiltered for cost reasons, so the full benefit of verified training data remains unquantified at scale.
