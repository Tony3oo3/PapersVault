## Problem

The Mountain Car problem, a canonical RL benchmark introduced in 1990, has no known optimal solution, making it impossible to quantify how close state-of-the-art RL agents are to optimality. This paper closes that gap by analytically deriving the optimal control, revealing that the solution is surprisingly simple (proportional to velocity) while current SOTA agents exhibit a surprisingly high regret of 2.7–5.5 return points. Motivated by this analysis, the paper seeks a policy representation that is both universal and efficient for low-dimensional control tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a normalized state vector s ∈ [-1,1]^n (e.g., position and velocity for Mountain Car, angular position/velocity for Pendulum, pitch angle/velocity/target for Aero 2) and produces a stochastic scalar action drawn from a Gaussian N(μ(s), σ(s)), where both μ and σ are multi-variate Chebyshev polynomials of max-degree d. The trainable parameters are the Chebyshev coefficients (θ^(μ) and θ^(σ)), totaling (d+1)^n per polynomial, compared to thousands of weights in a typical [64,64] MLP. For PPO, a third Chebyshev polynomial models the critic v_π(s).

## Method

The paper first analytically solves Mountain Car by reformulating the dynamics as a spatial ODE with a potential U(x), applying a Cauchy-Schwarz argument in L² to show the optimal action is α(t) = C·ẋ(t) (velocity-proportional), then reestablishing discrete constraints to identify a two-phase optimal policy. Building on this insight, the authors introduce multi-variate Chebyshev polynomials T_{d₁,…,dₙ}(x) = ∏ᵢ T_{dᵢ}(xᵢ) as a universal (dense) orthonormal basis for the space of continuous policies, parameterizing both the policy mean and standard deviation (and the PPO critic) as linear combinations of these basis functions. These Chebyshev policies serve as drop-in replacements for MLPs in PPO, ARS, and REINFORCE, reducing regret by a factor of 6.18 with 277× fewer parameters. Evaluation uses a multi-variate Horner scheme requiring only (d+1)^n − 1 multiplications and additions per forward pass, enabling real-time deployment on embedded hardware without neural accelerators.

## Results

On MountainCarContinuous-v0, the best Chebyshev policy (CH-3-ARS) achieves a mean return of 98.95 with a regret of 0.44, reducing the regret of the best MLP baseline (ARS at 96.67, regret 2.72) by a factor of 6.18 while using 277× fewer trainable parameters. Even the weakest Chebyshev policy (CH-3-PPO, regret 1.29) outperforms the best MLP policy by a factor of 2.1. On the real-world Quanser Aero 2 testbed, CH-3-PPO achieves a pitch deviation of 0.0279 rad versus 0.0910 rad for MLP-PPO (3.3× improvement), and retains simulation performance with only 13.4% degradation versus 115% for MLP-PPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MLP-ARS (Mania et al., 2018), the best neural baseline at 96.67 mean return, CH-3-ARS achieves 98.95, reducing regret from 2.72 to 0.44 (6.18× improvement) with 277× fewer parameters. Against MLP-PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) at 93.91, CH-3-PPO reaches 98.10, a 4.24× regret reduction. Against MLP-SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) at 94.61, all three Chebyshev policies (98.10–98.95) outperform it by 3.5–6.2× in regret. Against MLP-TRPO (Schulman et al., 2015) at 92.49, every Chebyshev policy wins by a wide margin. Notably, even classical REINFORCE with Chebyshev policies (CH-3-REI, regret 0.77) outperforms all MLP baselines trained with modern algorithms, while REINFORCE with MLPs fails entirely to solve the task across 960 training attempts. On the real-world Aero 2 testbed, CH-3-PPO outperforms MLP-PPO by 3.3× in pitch deviation and shows far superior sim-to-real transfer (13.4% vs. 115% degradation), while CH-3-ARS dramatically outperforms MLP-ARS which essentially fails to track the target signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the univariate Chebyshev polynomial approximation framework of Waclawek & Huber (2024) to a multi-variate setting and, for the first time, deploys it as a parametrized stochastic policy in reinforcement learning. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of using multilayer perceptrons as universal function approximators in RL by proposing an orthogonal polynomial basis that forms a dense subset of the continuous policy space with far fewer parameters. The analytical solution to Mountain Car builds on classical tools from nonlinear oscillator theory (Theorem 2.1, elliptic integrals) and Cauchy-Schwarz minimization in L² spaces, but no prior work had closed the 36-year gap on the optimal control for this canonical benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a 36-year gap by providing the first analytical optimal solution to Mountain Car, revealing that SOTA RL agents carry a regret of 2.7–5.5 on a benchmark they are supposed to have 'solved.' The Chebyshev policy representation offers a lightweight, interpretable, and real-time-capable alternative to neural networks for low-dimensional control, addressing five of the nine key challenges identified by Dulac-Arnold et al. (2021). However, the combinatorial growth of (d+1)ⁿ basis functions limits applicability to low-dimensional state spaces, and the uniform convergence property is a weakness when the policy needs heterogeneous behavior across state-space regions. Open questions include hybrid MLP–Chebyshev architectures, scaling to higher-dimensional tasks (humanoid robotics), and whether the simplicity of the optimal Mountain Car control generalizes to other benchmarks.
