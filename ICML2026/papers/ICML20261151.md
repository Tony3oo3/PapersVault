## Problem

Dirichlet process slice samplers have unbounded per-iteration computational cost because the number of stick-breaking components that must be instantiated depends on the minimum slice variable, which can be arbitrarily close to zero. No deterministic worst-case bound exists, and prior formal complexity analyses have been largely absent, leaving the practical scalability of slice-based posterior inference theoretically uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The analysis applies to any DP-based model of the form Y|η,z_n ~ L(z_n,η), z_i|G iid~ G, G ~ DP(α,P₀), requiring only the concentration parameter α and a non-atomic base measure P₀ as hyperparameters, with no likelihood-specific assumptions. The key output is a high-probability bound on the dynamic truncation level K_n (the number of stick-breaking components instantiated per iteration), showing K_n − H_n = O_P(log n) uniformly over all posterior partitions ρ_n, where H_n is the number of occupied clusters. The bound is explicit in α and holds a posteriori for any fixed observed dataset, not just in expectation under the generative model.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 3.3) establishes that for any δ∈(0,1), P(K_n − H_n ≤ C_δ log n | ρ_n) ≥ 1−δ uniformly over all partitions, with C_δ = B_α^(1) + B_α^(2) log(1/δ). The proof proceeds in three steps: (i) a structural proposition showing that merging clusters monotonically increases the survival probability of the minimum slice variable u_min, identifying the singleton partition as the worst case; (ii) a Poisson characterization of the stick-breaking weights beyond H_n (via the fact that −log(1−V_i) ~ Exp(α) i.i.d.), combined with a Chernoff-type concentration inequality to bound the number of additional components needed; and (iii) a union bound over n slice variables under the singleton partition, exploiting the Dirichlet(1,…,1,α) posterior weight structure to control P(u_min ≤ x). Corollaries extend the result to exponential tail bounds, uniformly bounded moments of all orders, and an almost-sure Borel-Cantelli statement, while a tightness proposition shows no o(log n) rate can hold uniformly.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.3) establishes that the slice-sampler truncation overhead K_n − H_n = O_P(log n) uniformly over all posterior partitions, with explicit constants C_δ = B_α^(1) + B_α^(2) log(1/δ) where B_α^(1), B_α^(2) ≍ α. In the worst-case cluster-growth regime H_n = O(n), per-iteration cost is O_P(n²); for H_n = O(log n) or O(1), it drops to O_P(n log n). Numerically, on three equally-balanced Normal clusters (α=1), the slice sampler completes 1000 iterations in 191.9 s at n=12000 (vs. 106.4 s for BGS L=10, which targets the wrong posterior), while CRP becomes infeasible beyond n=3000. On the challenging perturbed-Zipf setting, the slice sampler achieves ARI 0.646 vs. 0.352 for BGS at n=3000, with non-zero ESS for the number of clusters (0.996 vs. 0.000).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the slice sampler against two families of baselines: marginal CRP samplers (Neal, 2000) and blocked Gibbs samplers with fixed truncation (Ishwaran & James, 2001). Against CRP, the slice sampler wins decisively on scalability: CRP's O(n × H_n) per-iteration cost with expensive predictive-likelihood integrals makes it infeasible beyond n≈3000 (74.4 s per 1000 iterations), while the slice sampler remains tractable to n=12000 (191.9 s). Against BGS with L=10, the slice sampler is slightly slower per iteration (191.9 s vs. 106.4 s at n=12000) but targets the exact posterior, whereas BGS-L introduces a non-vanishing hard-threshold bias that assigns zero probability to partitions with more than L clusters. In the perturbed-Zipf experiment with growing cluster counts, BGS L=10 completely fails to explore the cluster-count dimension (ESS for H = 0.000 at all n) and achieves ARI ≈ 0.35, while the slice sampler achieves ARI 0.646 and RI 0.976 at n=3000. BGS with L=n avoids the bias but incurs Θ(n²) cost in all regimes, becoming infeasible beyond n=600. Overall, the slice sampler uniquely combines exact posterior targeting, no bookkeeping, joint updates, and favorable high-probability scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the slice-sampling framework for Dirichlet process models introduced by Walker (2007) and refined by Ge et al. (2015), which itself extends Neal's (2003) general slice-sampling construction to discrete random measures. The key prior theoretical input is the Poisson characterization of the truncation level from Muliere & Tardella (1998), but that result applies only under prior sampling with a fixed slice level. The paper departs from the truncated-stick-breaking literature (Ishwaran & James, 2001; 2002), which provides average-case error bounds under the generative model, by instead delivering a posteriori, data-agnostic, high-probability complexity guarantees that hold uniformly over all partitions visited by the chain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

These are the first formal complexity guarantees for DP slice samplers, resolving a long-standing gap that had made it difficult to theoretically justify the practical scalability of slice-based inference. The logarithmic overhead bound is shown to be tight (no o(log n) rate holds uniformly), and the explicit dependence on the concentration parameter α opens the door to adaptive and data-driven DP formulations. Open questions include extending the analysis to Pitman-Yor processes, general species sampling processes, and hierarchical or temporally evolving DP models, where the conditional structure may yield even stronger guarantees. The results are specific to the DP; whether analogous bounds hold for processes with heavier-tailed stick-breaking weights remains to be established.
