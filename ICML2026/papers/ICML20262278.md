## Problem

Existing foundation models for time series anomaly detection (TSAD) predominantly rely on reconstruction-error scoring at inference, which misses subtle contextual anomalies that are well-reconstructed and falsely flags complex but normal patterns in unseen domains. The paper identifies a fundamental 'representation conflict': normality is not an intrinsic property of a pattern's morphology but a context-relative concept, so a fixed global normality assumption breaks down in zero-shot settings where the same shape may be normal in one domain and anomalous in another.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate d-channel time series (or univariate series) and tokenizes it into non-overlapping variate-window patches that are flattened into a single sequence, enabling any-variate self-attention over both intra- and inter-variate dependencies. At inference, a fixed context window of 5000 timesteps is used in strict zero-shot mode (no target-domain fine-tuning), and the reconstruction head is discarded entirely; only the anomaly head produces a per-timestep anomaly score (softmax probability of the anomalous class) that is thresholded to yield binary labels.

## Method

TimeRCD replaces reconstruction-error inference with a dedicated anomaly head trained under the Relative Context Discrepancy (RCD) paradigm, where the model learns to judge a token's normality by comparing it against its surrounding context rather than against a memorized global distribution. The architecture is a standard encoder-only Transformer with variate-window tokenization and any-variate attention, requiring no structural modifications. Training uses a joint loss combining masked reconstruction (15% patched masking) as a dense representation-learning scaffold and binary cross-entropy on token-level anomaly labels; the reconstruction branch is discarded at test time. To provide supervised RCD signals, the authors build a 2.5B-point synthetic corpus via a four-stage pipeline: (1) randomized univariate baselines (trend + seasonality + noise), (2) multivariate causal coupling via a sampled DAG and discretized ARX dynamics, (3) context-violating anomaly injection (structural/seasonal perturbations plus endogenous causal propagation through the DAG), and (4) causally-aware token-level label generation. The synthetic design deliberately assigns identical morphological patterns different normality labels across contexts, breaking the semantic coupling between shape and anomaly status that plagues real-data augmentation.

## Results

In strict zero-shot evaluation across 16 datasets and 4 metrics (Affiliation-F1, F1-T, Standard-F1, VUS-PR), TimeRCD ranks first in 38 and second in 14 of 64 cases, outperforming all other zero-shot foundation models (DADA, MOMENT, TimesFM, Chronos, Time MOE) and even the simple MovingVar statistical baseline. Against full-shot, dataset-specific baselines (TranAD, USAD, OmniAnomaly, Sub-PCA, DCdetector, TFMAE, LOF, IForest), TimeRCD remains highly competitive, ranking first in 35 and second in 3 of 64 cases. On a specialized contextual-anomaly benchmark, TimeRCD achieves a Standard-F1 of 0.827, while competing zero-shot models degrade substantially, confirming the advantage of long-context comparative modeling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-shot foundation models, TimeRCD dominates: it outperforms DADA, MOMENT, TimesFM, Chronos, and Time MOE on the majority of dataset-metric combinations, with particularly large margins on VUS-PR (e.g., 44.65 vs. 24.73 for DADA on NAB) and on contextual-anomaly-heavy datasets like SMAP and SMD. The simple MovingVar baseline is competitive on variance-shift-aligned datasets (e.g., 89.43 vs. 84.66 on MSL Affiliation-F1) but is clearly outperformed overall (2 first-place vs. 38 for TimeRCD). Against full-shot baselines, TimeRCD (still zero-shot) matches or exceeds most methods: it beats TranAD, USAD, OmniAnomaly, DCdetector, LOF, and IForest on most datasets, while TFMAE is the strongest full-shot competitor (5 first-place in Affiliation-F1) and Sub-PCA is competitive on multivariate datasets. On the specialized contextual-anomaly split, TimeRCD achieves Standard-F1 of 0.827 while all zero-shot competitors degrade substantially, confirming that long-context RCD is the key differentiator. DADA and MOMENT results are partially excluded (marked *) due to potential data leakage in the zero-shot protocol, which further strengthens TimeRCD's relative standing.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeRCD builds on Moirai's variate-window tokenization and any-variate attention for multivariate time series processing, but departs fundamentally from the reconstruction-error inference paradigm that dominates existing TSAD foundation models (DADA, MOMENT, TimesFM, Chronos, Time MOE). The key conceptual departure is the Relative Context Discrepancy (RCD) paradigm, which reframes anomaly detection as a context-relative comparison task rather than a deviation-from-global-normality task, addressing what the authors term 'representation conflict' in zero-shot settings. The synthetic data generation pipeline, which uses causal DAGs with ARX dynamics to create context-dependent anomalies with cross-variate propagation, extends prior augmentation approaches (DADA, DAcAD) by decoupling pattern morphology from its normality label through structured causal violation rather than simple perturbation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RCD paradigm offers a principled alternative to reconstruction-error scoring that could generalize beyond TSAD to other tasks where 'normality' is context-dependent rather than absolute. The controlled ablation (Table 3) showing reconstruction error as an inference rule (VUS-PR 4.08) is dramatically worse than the anomaly head (33.57) under identical training, providing strong evidence that the inference rule—not just the training objective—matters critically. Key limitations remain: the fixed 5000 context window is a practical default rather than an optimal choice, the method can degrade when many anomalies co-occur within a single window (e.g., Stock dataset), and the standard Transformer backbone leaves open the question of whether RCD-specific architectures could improve efficiency. The scaling results (350M → 2.5B points) show positive but modest gains, suggesting data quality and curriculum design may matter more than raw scale for this task.
