## Problem

LoRA confines weight updates to a fixed low-rank subspace, which degrades effectiveness and slows convergence relative to full fine-tuning, especially at low ranks. While accumulating high-rank updates from sequential low-rank increments is a natural remedy, the theoretically optimal per-step adapters require a truncated SVD of the gradient (prohibitive cost) and switching optimization variables forces costly optimizer restarts that break convergence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen pretrained LLM weights (e.g., LLaMA-2-7B, Gemma-3-12B), a task-specific fine-tuning dataset, and a differentiable loss function. It produces fine-tuned model weights whose cumulative update W_T − W_0 is high-rank (empirically reaching rank 3000+ in 7B models), while each individual step still operates on low-rank adapter matrices of rank r (typically 4–8). The merged frozen matrix W̃_t^pt is updated in place each scaling step, so the full m×n weight matrix must be stored on disk rather than kept entirely in GPU memory.

## Method

ScaLoRA merges the current low-rank component A_t B_t^T into the frozen weights and re-parameterizes with new adapters Ã_t B̃_t^T, breaking the telescoping sum that confines LoRA to a fixed subspace. The theoretically optimal adapters (Theorem 3.2) require a rank-2r SVD of the gradient, which is infeasible per step; instead, ScaLoRA restricts the new adapters to column-wise scalings Ã_t = A_t diag(α_t), B̃_t = B_t diag(β_t), for which the global minimizer of the quadratic loss upper bound is obtained by solving a 2r×2r linear system in O((m+n+r)r²) time. Crucially, this column-wise structure allows AdamW's first and second moment estimators to be updated equivariantly from the previous step's moments in O((m+n)r) time, eliminating the need to reset the optimizer or re-warm-up the learning rate. When the linear system lacks a non-negative solution (~20% of layers), ScaLoRA falls back to a scalar scaling variant with a closed-form solution. An intermittent variant ScaLoRA-I performs the scaling every I iterations, amortizing the per-step overhead to O((mnr+(m+n+r)r²)/I) while preserving performance.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (DeBERTaV3-base, r=4), ScaLoRA achieves 88.98 average score, outperforming LoRA (88.13), MoRA (88.35), HiRA (88.46), and even full fine-tuning (88.25). On commonsense reasoning with LLaMA2-7B (r=8), ScaLoRA-I reaches 74.75 average, surpassing LoRA with r=32 (74.52) while using less compute. On mathematical problem solving with Gemma-3-12B, ScaLoRA-I achieves 82.11 on GSM8K and 37.96 on MATH, improving over LoRA (81.20 and 37.20 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScaLoRA(-I) consistently outperforms all five baselines across both LLaMA2-7B and LLaMA3-8B on commonsense reasoning. Against LoRA (r=8), ScaLoRA-I gains +1.12% (LLaMA2-7B) and +0.74% (LLaMA3-8B) in average score; notably, ScaLoRA-I with r=8 matches or exceeds LoRA with r=32 (74.75 vs 74.52 on LLaMA2-7B; 77.57 vs 77.54 on LLaMA3-8B) at lower computational cost. Against ReLoRA, ScaLoRA-I leads by +0.35% and +0.31% respectively, while against LoRA-GA the margin is +0.41% and +0.35%. MoRA and HiRA trail ScaLoRA by approximately 0.7–0.9% on LLaMA2-7B and 0.4–0.6% on LLaMA3-8B. On the GLUE benchmark (Table 1), ScaLoRA achieves the best score in 7 of 8 tasks and a 0.5%+ average gain over LoRA, also surpassing full fine-tuning (88.98 vs 88.25). On mathematical problem solving (Gemma-3-12B), ScaLoRA-I improves GSM8K by +0.91% and MATH by +0.76% over LoRA, with the column-wise scaling variant outperforming the scalar-only ablation by +0.84% on GSM8K.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScaLoRA extends LoRA by replacing its fixed low-rank subspace with a dynamic, per-iteration optimal adapter selection that accumulates into a high-rank weight update. It is positioned within the high-rank-update-via-low-rank-adapters family alongside ReLoRA (cascading adapters with optimization restarts), MoRA (nonlinear compress/decompress mappings), and HiRA (Hadamard product with pretrained weights), but departs from all of them by restricting the optimal adapter to column-wise scalings of the current adapters. This restriction is the key design choice: it makes the optimal scaling analytically solvable (via a 2r×2r linear system) and, critically, allows gradient moment estimators in AdamW to be updated in O((m+n)r) time without resetting the optimizer, unlike ReLoRA's restart-based approach. The paper also identifies LoRA-GA's initialization as a special case of its Theorem 3.2, unifying prior gradient-approximation work under the same SVD-based optimality condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ScaLoRA addresses a well-recognized tension in PEFT: LoRA's low-rank constraint degrades effectiveness and slows convergence, and simply increasing rank r is expensive. The method's practical value lies in achieving high-rank updates with negligible extra memory (0.01 GB vs. 7.83 GB for HiRA) and no optimizer restarts, making it a drop-in upgrade for existing LoRA pipelines. However, the requirement to store the full merged weight matrix W_t (rather than just the small adapters) is a notable storage limitation that constrains scalability to the largest models, and the intermittent variant (ScaLoRA-I) trades some convergence speed for amortized compute. Open questions include whether the column-wise scaling restriction is truly near-optimal for very high-rank regimes, and whether the storage overhead can be mitigated through periodic re-merging into the frozen backbone.
