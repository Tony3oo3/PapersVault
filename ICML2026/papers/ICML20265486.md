## Problem

Existing multi-agent system (MAS) optimization approaches treat each agent as a monolithic unit, failing to exploit internal LLM mixtures that can enhance individual role capabilities through propose-synthesis collaboration. When jointly optimizing intra-node LLM configurations and inter-node communication topology, credit assignment becomes substantially harder: final task rewards mask individual node errors, and per-edge optimization suffers from entangled attribution. No prior work unifies these two forms of collaboration (intra-node mixtures and inter-node topology) within a single optimizable framework.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text query (e.g., a math problem, code task, or multiple-choice question) and produces a final task answer. The query is encoded alongside role descriptions via a frozen Sentence-BERT encoder (384-dim) to drive LLM selection, while a GCN-based classifier scores pre-generated DAG topologies. The output is the synthesizer's aggregated response from the final decision node, with the system adaptively determining which LLMs to assign, which roles to activate (via skip tokens), and which communication graph to use per query.

## Method

HieraMAS introduces supernodes—each comprising W proposer LLMs and one synthesizer LLM in a propose-synthesis structure—replacing monolithic agents. A two-stage training algorithm addresses the resulting credit assignment challenges: Stage 1 optimizes LLM selection within supernodes using REINFORCE with multi-level rewards (α·R_node + (1−α)·R_final) and a cost-sensitive reward function, while randomly sampling topologies to decouple learning; Stage 2 freezes the LLM selector and trains a GCN-based graph classifier to score a pool of K=200 random DAGs, reformulating topology selection as holistic graph classification rather than intractable per-edge optimization. A unified skip token mechanism enables adaptive pruning of proposers and entire roles without separate modules. Theoretical analysis proves that multi-level rewards guarantee correct gradient direction for failing nodes (Corollary C.1) and that holistic graph scoring reduces per-edge credit assignment error from an irreducible Ω((1−ρ)pq) to a vanishing O(1/√N_samples) estimation error (Theorem C.3).

## Results

HieraMAS achieves the best average accuracy of 94.61% across HumanEval++, MATH, and MMLU-Redux, outperforming the strongest baselines (AFlow at 92.69% and Full-Graph at 92.78%) by roughly 2 percentage points. On MATH with GPT-5-Mini, HieraMAS reaches 96.67% accuracy, and on MMLU-Redux with GPT-5-Mini it achieves 95.20%, both leading all compared methods. In terms of cost, HieraMAS costs $1.29 on MMLU-Redux (GPT-5-Mini) versus $4.23 for Full-Graph (3.27× cheaper), and its training cost is 18.41× lower than AFlow on HumanEval++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HieraMAS wins on average (94.61%) over all seven baselines, but the picture is nuanced per benchmark. On HumanEval++, both AFlow (95.31%/98.44%) and MASRouter (96.88%/98.44%) outperform HieraMAS (93.75%/96.88%) under both LLM settings, suggesting code generation benefits from AFlow's MCTS-based workflow search and MASRouter's topology routing. On MATH, HieraMAS leads with GPT-5-Mini (96.67% vs. Full-Graph's 95.56% and AFlow's 95.56%), though Full-Graph edges ahead under Qwen3-80B (96.67% vs. 95.56%). On MMLU-Redux, HieraMAS clearly dominates with GPT-5-Mini (95.20% vs. next-best Self-Consistency at 94.40%), but under Qwen3-80B it trails Self-Consistency+CoT (89.60% vs. 92.80%). Among learning-based baselines, GDesigner (90.68% avg.) and MASRouter (90.89% avg.) both underperform HieraMAS by 3.7–4.0 points on average, with MASRouter showing particularly weak MMLU-Redux results (88.33%/81.67%) due to its constrained search over only 4 predefined topologies. The cost-efficiency advantage is substantial: HieraMAS costs $1.29 on MMLU-Redux versus $3.66 for GDesigner and $4.23 for Full-Graph, while its training cost ($1.85 on MMLU-Redux) is 7.5× cheaper than AFlow ($13.94).

## Relation to Prior Work

HieraMAS unifies two previously separate lines of MAS research: the Mixture-of-Agents paradigm (Wang et al., 2024), where multiple LLMs collaborate via propose-synthesis within a single role, and topology/role optimization methods (GDesigner, AFlow, MASRouter), which treat each agent as a monolithic single-LLM unit. The key departure is introducing 'supernodes' that embed heterogeneous LLM mixtures inside each functional role, then jointly optimizing these intra-node configurations with inter-node communication topology—a joint formulation that prior work has not addressed. The credit assignment solutions (multi-level rewards for per-node attribution, holistic graph classification for topology) draw on classical RL credit assignment theory (Sutton, 1984) but apply it to the novel setting of coupled intra-node and inter-node decisions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a practical recipe for building cost-efficient MAS that adaptively select both which LLMs to use per role and how those roles should communicate, rather than relying on fixed topologies or uniform model assignment. The finding that 2 proposers outperform 6 or 8 (Table 6) and that the system prefers sparse topologies over role pruning are useful design insights for practitioners. However, generalization to unseen domains remains a weakness (Table 3 shows HieraMAS underperforming simpler methods like AFlow on held-out MMLU categories), and the finite graph pool (K=200) limits scalability to larger role sets. Open questions include whether the two-stage decoupling is truly necessary versus end-to-end joint optimization, and how the framework scales when the LLM pool or role set grows substantially.
