## Problem

Unified Foundation Models (UFMs) are designed to couple multimodal understanding and generation, yet it remains unclear whether the visual and textual cues they produce during reasoning function as grounded, causally necessary evidence or merely as auxiliary outputs that the model ignores. Existing benchmarks evaluate generation and understanding as isolated capabilities and do not test their functional dependence within a single inference trace, leaving the question of evidential coupling—whether intermediate cues are state-consistent and indispensable for the final prediction—entirely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

Each UFO instance provides one or more input images (64.9% involve multiple images) plus a question about a future state, and requires the model to first generate an intermediate textual cue (1–2 sentences) and an intermediate visual cue (a generated image) before producing a final answer. The benchmark contains 3,366 questions split roughly evenly between multiple-choice (49.8%) and free-form (50.2%) formats, with average question length of ~39 tokens and average answer length of ~3 tokens. Evaluation is decomposed into four inference pathways—Direct (no cues), Textual-only, Visual-only, and Joint—so that the causal contribution of each modality can be isolated against a no-cue baseline.

## Method

UFO organises 3,366 questions across three state-transition regimes—State Determination (future state fully specified by inputs), State Reconstruction (future state partially occluded or corrupted), and State Augmentation (future state present but requiring implicit constraints to be made explicit)—spanning 10 task types such as hybridisation, inpainting, multi-table reasoning, and physics. The core protocol is a two-step procedure: the model first generates intermediate textual and visual cues describing the future state, then answers the question conditioned on those cues, enabling direct measurement of whether the cues are state-consistent and causally sufficient. Cue quality is assessed along five dimensions (Relevance, Faithfulness, Causal Utility, Specificity, Compactness) using a calibrated VLM-as-judge (Qwen3-VL-32B with binary scoring for open-source; Gemini-3-Flash with chain-of-thought for proprietary), validated against human annotations. To guard against spurious gains, the benchmark enforces a dual-layer adversarial filter: instances solvable by external validators without intermediate steps are discarded, and a blind-modality audit removes any example answerable without visual input, ensuring that correct answers require genuine cross-modal grounding rather than parametric memorisation or textual shortcuts.

## Results

On UFO's multiple-choice tasks, Gemini-3.0-pro with Reasoning achieves the highest average Joint accuracy of 66.13%, followed by GPT-5.2 at 52.12%; among open-source models, OmniGen2 leads at 45.88% Joint accuracy. A consistent performance hierarchy emerges across all models: State Reconstruction > State Augmentation > State Determination, validating the decreasing information-gain design. Critically, the Joint schedule (combining textual and visual cues) does not consistently outperform the best single-modality baseline—e.g., UniCoT scores 39.65% Textual vs. only 37.19% Joint in State Augmentation—revealing weakened evidential coupling. In open-ended generation (Table 4), performance collapses dramatically (e.g., UniPic1 drops from 25.91% MCQ to 0.96% open-ended in State Determination), exposing a severe discriminative-generative gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-3.0-pro with Reasoning dominates all regimes, reaching 85.31% Joint in State Reconstruction and 66.13% average, while GPT-5.2 trails by ~14 points (52.12% average). Among open-source models, OmniGen2 (3B+4B) is the strongest, achieving 45.88% average Joint and the best State Reconstruction Joint score (61.18%), outperforming larger models like Bagel (41.70%) and UniWorld-V1 (40.73%). UniPic2-Metaquery (9B) is competitive at 41.15% average Joint, benefiting from its dedicated generator. Notably, the Joint schedule fails to consistently beat the best single-modality baseline: UniCoT's Textual (39.65%) exceeds its Joint (37.19%) in State Augmentation, and Bagel's Visual (44.18%) exceeds its Joint (41.70%) in the average, indicating that combining cues introduces interference rather than synergy. Smaller models (UniPic1 1.5B, Janus-Pro 1B, EMU3 8.5B) cluster near 23–31% average, with EMU3 showing the weakest performance (23.41%), suggesting that pure autoregressive unified tokenization remains the least effective paradigm for evidential coupling.

## Relation to Prior Work

UFO departs from prior multimodal benchmarks (ReasonPix2Pix, ReasonEdit, EditWorld, KRIS-Bench, RISEBench, WorldGenBench, Unified-Bench, MetaQuery) that evaluate generation and understanding as isolated capabilities or assess only endpoint accuracy. Rather than building on a specific prior method, the paper carves new ground by introducing a two-step protocol that enforces evidential coupling—requiring intermediate multimodal cues to be both state-consistent and causally sufficient for the final answer—and by organising tasks along a state-transition taxonomy (determination, reconstruction, augmentation) rather than by subject domain. The multi-dimensional cue evaluation (relevance, faithfulness, causal utility, specificity, compactness) and the contamination/filtering safeguards further distinguish UFO from observational benchmarks like ROVER and Uni-MMMU that record intermediate artifacts without controlled interventions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a fundamental gap in the UFM narrative: across twelve models, evidential coupling does not consistently improve performance, and the Joint schedule frequently trails the best single-modality baseline, suggesting that shared latent spaces do not guarantee functional cross-modal synergy. The severe textual-visual fidelity gap (e.g., Janus-Pro 1B: 22.13% vs. 2.34% cue accuracy) and the discriminative-generative collapse in open-ended settings indicate that current UFMs rely on surface-level discrimination rather than constructing grounded world models. Limitations include restriction to vision-language modalities, question-driven state transitions with explicitly defined future states, and a relatively modest 3,366-question scale; extending to video, audio, open-ended reasoning, and longer interaction horizons remains open.
