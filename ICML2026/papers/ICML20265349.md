## Problem

Continual learning research is dominated by single-view (exocentric) recognition tasks, while embodied agents inherently perceive multi-view video (ego, exo, or both) with simultaneous task and viewpoint shifts. Existing CL protocols abstract away viewpoint bias and heterogeneous semantic objectives, making it impossible to disentangle genuine cross-view generalization from view-specific overfitting or to assess whether CL methods truly improve continual video understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265349/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are egocentric and/or exocentric video clips standardized to 25 fps at 224×224 resolution, drawn from the EgoExoLearn dataset (120 hours of paired ego/exo procedural videos). Outputs vary across four benchmarks: pairwise skill rankings (score difference), per-frame action labels, cross-view matching clips selected from 20 candidates, next-action verb/noun categories, and fixed-length future step sequences. A key protocol detail is that evaluation explicitly separates within-view retention from zero-shot cross-view transfer, and three training regimes (Ego-only, Exo-only, Ego+Exo co-training) are supported to isolate the contribution of multi-view evidence.

## Method

VISTA stores task knowledge in lightweight residual bottleneck adapters (bottleneck dimension r≪C) applied per time step on top of a frozen pretrained video encoder, with each new adapter warm-started from the previous task's parameters. At inference, a training-free subspace residual router extracts a compact routing vector via temporal mean pooling, then compares it against per-task whitened subspaces built from accumulated second-order statistics (top-q PCA eigenvectors plus a whitened mean direction, orthonormalized via QR). Routing scores are computed as the complement of explained energy in each task's whitened subspace, converted to a softmax posterior over tasks, and the top-K adapters are mixed with the resulting weights. The shared prediction head is frozen after the first task to preserve calibration, and the entire routing mechanism requires no learned classifier, making it robust to the subtle task boundaries and viewpoint shifts characteristic of multi-view video streams.

## Results

On the CE⁴L benchmark (EgoExoLearn, Ego+Exo co-training), VISTA achieves near-joint-training performance across all four tasks: 80.38% ranking accuracy on skill assessment (vs. 80.42% joint) with near-zero forgetting (0.12%), 45.35% frame-wise accuracy on action segmentation (vs. 45.93% joint), 38.26% Top-1 on cross-view association (vs. 40.07% joint), and 35.23% Top-5 recall on action anticipation (vs. 35.50% joint). VISTA consistently outperforms all replay-free CL baselines (L2P+, S-Prompt+, EWC, LwF) by 3–7 points on anticipation and by 5–10 points on skill assessment, while also surpassing replay-based methods (ER, DER++) that require large memory buffers. In terms of efficiency, VISTA uses only 0.01M storage for routing statistics versus 1,696M for ER, with per-batch compute (793 ms) comparable to S-Prompt+ (730 ms) and well below EWC (1,517 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISTA outperforms all six CL baselines on the action anticipation benchmark (Ego+Exo): it leads ER (34.49) and DER++ (33.70) by 0.7–1.5 points on Ego-V, and surpasses EWC (31.29), LwF (30.57), L2P+ (28.51), and S-Prompt+ (31.83) by 3.4–6.7 points. The gap is most pronounced against ensemble-based methods L2P+ and S-Prompt+, whose learned routing signals fail to capture task structure under viewpoint shifts, degrading especially on noun prediction (16.19% and 16.49% vs. VISTA's 25.23%). On skill assessment, VISTA reaches 80.38% with 0.12% forgetting, matching joint training (80.42%) while ER and DER++ plateau around 77–79% with 10–14% forgetting. On cross-view association, VISTA (38.26%) edges out ER (36.47%) and DER++ (35.57%) in the Ego→Exo direction, though all methods remain well below joint training (40.07%), highlighting that multi-view evidence is the primary bottleneck. On action planning, the gap to joint is smallest (VISTA 82.81 vs. joint 83.14 ED@8), and VISTA matches or slightly beats replay baselines, suggesting planning is less dominated by catastrophic forgetting. Overall, VISTA is the only replay-free method that consistently approaches joint-training accuracy across all four CE⁴L tasks while incurring negligible storage overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

VISTA's adapter architecture is directly derived from residual bottleneck adapters (Rebuffi et al., 2017) and the adapter-mixture inference mechanism follows the MoE-adapter line of work for CL (Yu et al., 2024, 2025). The key conceptual departure from prior pretraining-based CL methods (L2P, S-Prompt) is replacing learned task routers with a training-free whitened-subspace residual router, motivated by the observation that learned routers themselves suffer from nonstationarity in multi-view video streams. More broadly, the paper extends video CL from the single-view, homogeneous-recognition setting (Park et al., 2021; Villa et al., 2022) to a multi-view (ego/exo/ego-exo) regime with heterogeneous semantic objectives, a coupling that prior benchmarks have not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CE4L is the first benchmark to explicitly disentangle within-view retention from zero-shot cross-view generalization in continual video learning, revealing that viewpoint-conditioned mismatch can persist even when within-view forgetting is minimized—a failure mode invisible to standard single-view CL protocols. The finding that cross-view transfer remains low even under joint training for action segmentation suggests the gap is partly representational rather than purely a CL interference problem, pointing to a need for multi-view-aware pretraining. Limitations include instantiation on a single ego-exo corpus (EgoExoLearn), fixed task boundaries, and no 3D spatial reasoning; open questions include whether VISTA's per-task adapter storage scales to very long streams and whether the subspace routing generalizes to additional sensor modalities.
