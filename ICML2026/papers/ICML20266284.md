## Problem

Learned octree-based LiDAR point cloud compression faces two critical bottlenecks: fully-causal multi-stage context modeling forces a heavyweight backbone to be re-executed for every prediction stage, causing decoding latency to scale linearly with the number of stages, and fixed-stage designs cannot adapt to varying performance-latency constraints in real applications. These limitations stem from the tight coupling between the context aggregation backbone and the probability prediction head in existing frameworks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a LiDAR point cloud in Cartesian coordinates and, when available, sensor intrinsics (laser pitch angles and per-beam vertical offsets) to produce a hybrid cylindrical-beam representation (ρ, θ, b) before octree construction; when intrinsics are unavailable, a standard spherical-coordinate preprocessing is used instead. The output is an arithmetic-coded bitstream of level-wise octree occupancy sequences, with the number of prediction stages S transmitted to the decoder for consistent stage decomposition. Octree depth L is configurable (e.g., L=16 for SemanticKITTI/nuScenes, 1 mm voxelization for Ford/QNX) to span different bitrate operating points.

## Method

PACE reformulates the entropy modeling pipeline into a post-causal architecture: a non-causal backbone (window-partitioned, using graph-based positional encoding with k-NN EdgeConv and unmasked multi-head attention) aggregates inter-level ancestor context exactly once per window, while a lightweight stage-scalable predictor confines all intra-level causality via an elastic causal embedding that conditionally incorporates sibling occupancy only when decoded in a preceding stage. The predictor uses a Mamba (selective SSM) block for efficient causal sequence modeling followed by an MLP-Softmax head to produce per-node occupancy distributions. Because the heavy backbone is decoupled from the causal predictor, the number of prediction stages S can be set to any value at inference time (from S=1 fully parallel to S=W fully autoregressive) without retraining or reloading parameters, enabling on-the-fly reconfiguration. Training employs a mixed-stage strategy where S is sampled from a small subset (e.g., {1, 2, 4, W}), and the single shared predictor generalizes to unseen stage configurations at test time.

## Results

PACE achieves state-of-the-art BD-BR savings against the G-PCC (Octree) anchor across four datasets: 39.01%–50.82% in fully parallel (one-stage) mode and 45.14%–55.18% in autoregressive mode. In autoregressive mode, PACE reduces decoding latency by over 90% compared to TopNet (16.36 s vs. 290.26 s at octree level 14) while simultaneously improving BD-BR by more than 20 percentage points on SemanticKITTI (−45.14% vs. −22.58%). The one-stage mode (Ours-FP) delivers the best speed–performance trade-off, encoding in 1.72 s and decoding in 1.55 s with BD-BR savings up to −55.02% on Ford.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PACE (Ours-AR) outperforms all learning-based octree baselines by wide margins: it surpasses TopNet by 22.56 pp on SemanticKITTI D1 (−45.14% vs. −22.58%), SCP by 14.88 pp on Ford D1 (−55.07% vs. −40.26%), and ASRL by 22.64 pp on Ford D1 (−55.07% vs. −32.43%). Against rules-based predtree codecs, PACE wins on Ford (Ours-AR: −55.07% D1 vs. G-PCC Predgeom −45.24% and L3C2 −45.67%) but trails on the extremely sparse QNX dataset (Ours-AR: −55.18% D1 vs. L3C2 −57.76% and G-PCC Predgeom −53.21%), which the authors attribute to predtree methods' advantage under very low point density. PACE also outperforms the learned predtree method LPCM on both SemanticKITTI (−45.14% vs. −28.91% D1) and Ford (−55.07% vs. −48.30% D1). Among sparse-tensor baselines, PACE dominates Unicorn (−45.14% vs. −22.26% on KITTI D1) and NBP (−55.07% vs. −39.71% on Ford D1). Notably, PACE's one-stage mode (Ours-FP) already exceeds all prior methods on every dataset where they report results, while the autoregressive mode adds 5–10 pp further BD-BR improvement at the cost of ~10× decoding time.

## Relation to Prior Work

PACE departs from the fully-causal paradigm dominant in octree-based learned LPCC (OctAttention, TopNet, EHEM, ASRL, SCP), where a heavyweight backbone is re-executed for every prediction stage due to intra-level causality. The key conceptual reformulation is to make the inter-level (ancestor) context aggregation non-causal—executing the backbone once per window—and confine causality to a lightweight, stage-scalable predictor that uses elastic causal embedding to condition on previously decoded siblings. This draws on graph-based positional encoding (EdgeConv over k-NN graphs) for 3D structural priors and Mamba (selective SSM) for efficient causal sequence modeling, repurposing both in a compression-specific context where they had not previously been combined.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >90% decoding latency reduction in autoregressive mode (16.36 s vs. ~290 s for TopNet) makes learned octree-based LPCC viable for near-real-time autonomous driving pipelines, where prior autoregressive methods were prohibitively slow. The stage-scalable design (one model, any number of stages at inference) addresses a practical deployment need—archiving vs. interactive streaming—that fixed-stage models cannot serve. Open questions include: whether the encoding-side parallelism bottleneck in AR mode can be resolved via probability quantization, whether the density-dependent performance gap versus predtree codecs on very sparse scans (e.g., QNX, ~30k points) can be closed, and whether the post-causal decomposition generalizes beyond octree structures to other hierarchical representations.
