## Problem

Existing HDR novel view synthesis methods predominantly rely on color regression as the sole supervision signal, ignoring how exposure variations affect geometric reliability. This single-modal paradigm fails to capture structural variations induced by exposure disparities and produces hallucinated geometry and ghosting artifacts near luminance transitions (e.g., shadow boundaries, overexposed highlights). No prior framework jointly models exposure-aware geometry and radiance in a unified, interpretable manner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse multi-exposure LDR images (35 viewpoints × 5 exposure levels from the HDR-NeRF dataset) along with known camera poses, where each training view is paired with a randomly sampled LDR exposure from a subset. A learnable tone-mapping network operating in the log domain converts the internal HDR radiance representation to LDR for supervision, and the system outputs both HDR and LDR novel views at arbitrary viewpoints, a 3D Gaussian scene representation, and an implicit geometric surface encoded as a pseudo-SDF. The framework is evaluated under three configurations: LDR with observed exposures (LDR-OE), LDR with unseen exposures (LDR-NE), and full HDR reconstruction.

## Method

Expo-GS decomposes HDR-NVS into three disentangled-then-joint training stages: (1) irradiance field training that learns anisotropic Gaussian primitives with SH-based view-dependent radiance and a shared learnable tone mapper via soft front-to-back rasterization; (2) geometry field training that constructs an exposure-aware signed distance function (Expo-SDF) by normalizing Gaussian density with per-pixel exposure reliability estimates, thereby suppressing noisy gradients from saturated or underexposed regions while reinforcing supervision in well-exposed areas; and (3) interactive joint training that couples Expo-SDF-guided density control (growth near the zero-level surface, pruning in uncertain regions) with radiance refinement through a unified loss combining photometric, SDF, normal-alignment, and disk-regularization terms. The key insight is that the negative log of the exposure-normalized Gaussian density induces a Mahalanobis distance that approximates a true SDF in well-exposed regions, providing a smooth, differentiable geometric surrogate even where exposure is unreliable. This biologically inspired decoupling of color and geometry channels, followed by their joint integration, mirrors the dual-stream processing of the human visual system.

## Results

On the HDR-NeRF synthetic dataset, Expo-GS achieves a peak PSNR of 39.06 dB under full HDR evaluation, surpassing the strongest prior method HDR-GS by 1.08 dB and HDR-NeRF by 2.94 dB. Under the LDR-OE setting (exposures t1, t3, t5), it reaches 41.38 dB PSNR with SSIM 0.989 and LPIPS 0.010, the best values across all three evaluation configurations. On real-world scenes, Expo-GS attains 35.59 dB PSNR (LDR-OE) and 32.17 dB PSNR (LDR-NE), consistently outperforming all baselines in SSIM and LPIPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Expo-GS outperforms all 16 compared baselines across nearly every metric and setting. Against the closest HDR-GS competitor, it gains +1.08 dB PSNR in HDR (39.06 vs 37.98), +0.32 dB in LDR-OE (41.38 vs 41.06), and +1.11 dB in LDR-NE (37.47 vs 36.36), while also achieving lower LPIPS in all three configurations. The NeRF-based baseline HDR-NeRF trails by a wider margin: +2.94 dB in HDR and +2.89 dB in LDR-OE. Among SDF-based methods (SuGaR, GSDF, PulledGS), Expo-GS leads by 2.6–8.4 dB PSNR in HDR, confirming that exposure-aware SDF supervision is critical under multi-exposure conditions. Relightable methods (NeRFactor, ReLight-NeRF, GaSLight) degrade sharply under unseen exposures (LDR-NE), dropping to 18–30 dB PSNR, whereas Expo-GS maintains 37.47 dB, demonstrating superior cross-exposure generalization. On real-world scenes, Expo-GS leads HDR-GS by +0.65 dB (LDR-OE) and +0.93 dB (LDR-NE) in PSNR, with substantially better SSIM (0.981 vs 0.962) and LPIPS (0.020 vs 0.031).

## Relation to Prior Work

Expo-GS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) for its anisotropic Gaussian representation and differentiable rasterization, and on HDR-GS (Cai et al., 2024) for the learnable tone-mapping network and dual dynamic-range Gaussian framework. It departs from prior HDR-NVS methods (HDR-NeRF, HDR-GS, GaussHDR) that rely exclusively on color regression by introducing an exposure-aware signed distance function (Expo-SDF) that reweights geometric supervision based on per-pixel radiometric reliability, drawing conceptual inspiration from the human visual system's dual-stream processing of color and structure. The three-stage decoupled-then-joint training paradigm, with SDF-guided density control for Gaussian growth and pruning, is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior SDF-GS hybrids (SuGaR, GSDF, PulledGS), which assume fixed-exposure LDR inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored gap in HDR-NVS: the lack of exposure-aware geometric supervision, which causes hallucinated structures and ghosting at exposure transitions. The Expo-SDF mechanism is interpretable and modular, and the HVS-inspired decomposition provides a principled rationale for why color-only optimization fails under extreme dynamic range. However, the method is evaluated only on the HDR-NeRF dataset (8 synthetic + 4 real scenes), requires strictly static multi-exposure inputs, and adds ~3 minutes of training overhead from the geometry stage. Open questions include scalability to large-scale or dynamic scenes, whether the pseudo-SDF can be replaced by learned depth priors to reduce dependence on ground-truth meshes, and how the method generalizes to real-world captures with imperfect exposure metadata.
