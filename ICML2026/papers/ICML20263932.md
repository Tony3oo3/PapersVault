## Problem

Prior scaling-law studies of discrete diffusion language models focused almost exclusively on Masked Diffusion (MDLM), leaving Uniform-state and interpolating diffusion families unexplored. More fundamentally, perplexity—the canonical metric for comparing language models—is misleading across diffusion families because different forward/reverse processes induce incommensurable likelihood bounds, so the family with the best perplexity may not be the most effective in practice. The paper asks whether Masked diffusion is categorically the future of diffusion language modeling or merely the current front-runner in a larger design space.

## Input / Output

All models consume text sequences tokenized with the Llama-2 tokenizer (vocab 32,001 for the scaling study, 128,000 for the 1.7B models) at a fixed context length of 2048 tokens (256 for GSM8K SFT). Generation proceeds by iteratively refining an entire sequence in parallel over T denoising steps (diffusion) or token-by-token (AR), producing a full text sequence as output. For evaluation, 1000 unconditional samples are scored with Generative Perplexity under a frozen Llama2-7B evaluator, and throughput is measured in tokens/sec on a single 80GB H100 at the largest power-of-two batch size that fits.

## Method

The paper conducts the first systematic IsoFLOP scaling study across three diffusion families—MDLM (masked), Duo (uniform-state), and Eso-LM (interpolating)—plus an AR baseline, training compute-matched DiT-architecture models across five FLOP budgets (6×10¹⁸ to 1×10²⁰) and fitting power-law scaling curves for compute-optimal validation loss and model size. A key finding is that training MDLM with a simple low-variance cross-entropy objective (replacing the divergent α′/(1−α) weight with −1) improves compute efficiency by ~12% and shifts compute-optimal checkpoints toward smaller models. Because perplexity alone cannot capture inference-time advantages, the authors construct speed-quality Pareto frontiers by fitting Gen PPL and throughput as functions of sampling steps T, revealing that Duo dominates in throughput ranges [200,400]∪[600,∞) and Eso-LM in [400,600] despite worse likelihood scaling. At 1.7B parameters trained on 2.1T Nemotron tokens, Duo outperforms AR, MDLM, and Eso-LM on GSM8K after SFT (65.8% vs. 62.9%, 58.8%, 33.4%) despite having the worst validation perplexity among the four methods.

## Results

On zero-shot likelihood benchmarks (Table 1), AR-1.7B achieves the best overall performance (e.g., ARC-e 72.7, PIQA 78.1), while among diffusion models MDLM leads on ARC-e (50.5) and BoolQ (62.8) and Duo leads on OBQA (33.0), PIQA (62.7), and RACE (35.0). On GSM8K after SFT (Table 2), Duo-1.7B achieves 65.8% accuracy, outperforming AR-1.7B (62.9%), MDLM-1.7B (58.8%), and Eso-LM-1.7B (33.4%), despite having the worst validation perplexity among the four methods. Throughput is comparable across methods at batch size 1 (24.6–25.6 toks/sec), confirming that the accuracy gap is not due to a speed advantage. On the speed-quality Pareto frontier, Duo dominates in throughput ranges [200,400] ∪ [600,∞), Eso-LM dominates in [400,600], and AR dominates below 200 toks/sec.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior-work masked diffusion baselines, Duo-1.7B (65.8% GSM8K) substantially outperforms SMDM-1B (58.5%) by 7.3 points, though the larger LLaDa-8B (70.7%) still leads by 4.9 points at 4.7× the parameter count. Within the paper's own 1.7B comparison, Duo beats AR-1.7B by 2.9 points on GSM8K (65.8 vs. 62.9) and MDLM-1.7B by 7.0 points (65.8 vs. 58.8), while AR retains the edge on all six zero-shot likelihood benchmarks (e.g., ARC-e 72.7 vs. 53.4 for Duo). MDLM-1.7B outperforms Duo on ARC-e (50.5 vs. 53.4 is actually Duo winning; correction: MDLM 50.5 vs. Duo 53.4 — Duo wins), BoolQ (62.8 vs. 59.6), and SIQA (39.2 vs. 39.0), confirming that MDLM's stronger likelihood scaling translates to better zero-shot commonsense performance. Eso-LM-1.7B trails all other methods on both GSM8K (33.4%) and likelihood benchmarks (e.g., ARC-e 46.0), consistent with its use of causal attention reducing modeling capacity in full-diffusion mode. The key takeaway is that perplexity ranking (AR > MDLM > Duo > Eso-LM) does not predict downstream task performance, where Duo inverts the ordering on math reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compute-optimal scaling law framework of Kaplan et al. (2020) and Hoffmann et al. (2022) from autoregressive models to three discrete diffusion families: Masked diffusion (MDLM, Sahoo et al. 2024a; Nie et al. 2025a), Uniform-state diffusion (Duo, Sahoo et al. 2025a), and interpolating diffusion (Eso-LM, Sahoo et al. 2025b). Prior scaling work on diffusion LLMs focused almost exclusively on MDLMs (Nie et al. 2025a), leaving the other families underexplored. The paper departs from the standard perplexity-only evaluation by introducing a speed-quality Pareto frontier that accounts for sampling efficiency (few-step generation, KV caching), and builds on the low-variance training objective identified by Sahoo et al. (2025b) to show a ~12% compute-efficiency gain for MDLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that perplexity is informative within a diffusion family but misleading across families—challenges a default assumption in the field and motivates evaluation protocols that jointly consider likelihood, sampling efficiency, and downstream task performance. Practically, the Pareto frontier shows that no single diffusion family dominates across all throughput regimes, giving practitioners a concrete decision framework. Open questions include whether these trade-offs persist at much larger scales (tens of billions of parameters), whether improved samplers will further shift the frontier, and whether the distinct generative mechanisms of each family yield consistent real-world advantages beyond the 1.7B scale studied here.
