## Problem

Existing multi-view outlier detection methods suffer from two fundamental issues: outlier propagation, where anomalous samples are repeatedly reinforced as neighbors and corrupt cross-view consensus learning, and inter-view scale discrepancy, where view-specific latent representations have different numerical magnitudes that distort neighborhood geometry. Prior approaches implicitly assume all samples and neighbors are reliable and apply alignment uniformly, which violates both conditions and degrades detection performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes V aligned multi-view feature matrices X^v ∈ R^{d_v × N} (e.g., 2–6 views with dimensions ranging from 8 to 34,279) of N unlabeled samples. It outputs a single per-sample outlier score S_i that is a z-scored combination of an attribute score (sum of reconstruction errors across views) and a class score (mean of angular and distance disagreements between each view's reliable neighborhood and its leave-one-out consensus), thereby characterizing all three outlier types (attribute, class, attribute-class) in one ranking.

## Method

RNAMOD trains view-specific autoencoders and, at each iteration, computes two reliability scores per sample: a sample reliability φ_i (sigmoid of negative reconstruction error) that down-weights attribute outliers, and a consistency reliability ψ_i (cross-view neighborhood overlap raised to power p<1) that down-weights class outliers. These scores modulate a reliability-weighted KNN construction so that outliers are excluded from neighbor sets, directly preventing outlier propagation. For cross-view alignment, the method defines a leave-one-out directional consensus: for each reliable edge (i,j), it compares the view-specific edge direction d_ij^v against the average direction from all other views using a cosine-based angular loss, which is invariant to view-specific scaling. A dynamic view weight π_v (softmax of negative total angular deviation) further suppresses low-quality views. Training alternates between updating reliability scores, view weights, neighborhood structures, and network parameters, with the total loss combining weighted reconstruction and reliability-gated directional consensus.

## Results

RNAMOD is evaluated on six real-world multi-view datasets (100Leaves, Caltech101, NUSWIDEOBJ, BBCSport, Reuters, CiteSeer) using AUC as the metric, with six different outlier ratio configurations per dataset. Across all 36 dataset–ratio settings, RNAMOD achieves the best AUC in 32 cases and is second-best in the remaining 4. On Caltech101, BBCSport, Reuters, and CiteSeer, RNAMOD attains the top AUC in every configuration (e.g., 0.909 on Caltech101 ratio 6, 0.952 on BBCSport ratios 5–6, 0.964 on CiteSeer ratio 5). The closest competitor, RCPMOD, is second-best in most of the settings where RNAMOD does not lead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RNAMOD outperforms all seven baselines in the majority of the 36 dataset–ratio settings, winning 32 of 36. The strongest competitor is RCPMOD (Wang et al., 2024), which is second-best in most settings where RNAMOD does not lead (e.g., 100Leaves ratios 1–2, NUSWIDEOBJ ratio 4), with gaps typically under 0.02 AUC. APOD (Marcos Alvarez et al., 2013), the clustering-based baseline, consistently achieves the lowest AUC across nearly all datasets, confirming the paper's argument that clustering assumptions are too restrictive for neighborhood-based outlier detection. MODGD (Hu et al., 2024) and LRTDM (Lin et al., 2025) are competitive on NUSWIDEOBJ (e.g., MODGD 0.959 vs. RNAMOD 0.971 at ratio 5; LRTDM 0.964 vs. 0.973 at ratio 3) but trail RNAMOD by 0.01–0.04 AUC on the other datasets. SRLSP (Wang et al., 2023) achieves the second-best result on NUSWIDEOBJ ratio 1 (0.888 vs. 0.857) but is generally outperformed by RNAMOD by 0.03–0.08 AUC on the remaining settings. On the text-heavy datasets (BBCSport, Reuters, CiteSeer), RNAMOD's advantage over the next-best method is most pronounced, reaching up to 0.05 AUC (e.g., 0.952 vs. 0.908 on BBCSport ratio 5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neighborhood-based multi-view outlier detection line (MODDIS, NCMOD, SRLSP, MODGD, RCPMOD) by challenging a shared implicit assumption: that all samples and their neighbors are reliable during graph construction and cross-view alignment. Rather than building on a single prior method, RNAMOD repurposes autoencoder reconstruction error as a reliability signal and introduces a leave-one-out directional consensus mechanism that departs from prior distance- or representation-level alignment strategies. The reliability-driven neighborhood filtering and direction-based (rather than magnitude-based) cross-view alignment are the key conceptual departures from the existing MVOD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reliability framework offers a general principle—dynamically down-weighting unreliable samples during neighborhood construction—that could transfer to other graph-based or kNN-based anomaly detection settings beyond multi-view data. However, the paper is limited to six relatively small-to-medium datasets (largest: 30K samples), does not address incomplete multi-view scenarios, and provides no theoretical guarantees on when reliability estimation converges to true outlier status. Open questions include whether the alternating optimization scales efficiently to very large or streaming settings, and whether the directional alignment generalizes to non-Euclidean latent spaces.
