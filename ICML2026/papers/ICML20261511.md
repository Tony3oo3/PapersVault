## Problem

Existing PD-disaggregated LLM serving systems are designed for single-round inference and fail to handle the interleaved prefill-decode workload pattern inherent in multi-round workflows (autonomous agents, iterative RAG). Specifically, they lack adaptive mechanisms to route incremental prefill tasks between decode and prefill workers under real-time load, and they determine model deployment configurations using only single-round input/output length statistics, ignoring the unique multi-round workload structure.

## Input / Output

At runtime, the system consumes multi-round inference sessions (each comprising an initial prefill, multiple decode steps, and incremental prefills triggered by environmental interactions) along with real-time windowed TTFT/ITL statistics and per-worker queue metadata stored in distributed shared memory (Redis). The offline planner takes workload traces (round counts, prefill/decode lengths), hardware specifications (GPU count, interconnect bandwidth), and a piecewise α-β performance model as inputs, producing an integer deployment configuration specifying data-parallelism replica counts and model-parallelism (tensor-parallelism) degrees for both prefill and decode worker pools. The end-to-end output is the generated token stream for each request, with SLO attainment (TTFT and ITL thresholds) as the primary quality metric.

## Method

AMPD combines an online adaptive scheduler with an offline deployment planner. The online scheduler has two components: (1) an adaptive routing mechanism that, for each incremental prefill task, estimates local execution cost (on the bound decode worker) versus remote execution cost (on each prefill worker, including KV-cache transfer via RDMA/NIXL and queuing delay) using a piecewise α-β performance model, then routes to the option with minimum estimated cost while respecting TTFT/ITL slack thresholds; and (2) a TTFT-aware prefill reordering policy that peeks a small window (w≤5) at the head of each prefill queue and enumerates feasible orderings to maximize the count of tasks meeting the TTFT SLO, with a starvation guard limiting postponements. The offline planner formulates the joint selection of data-parallelism replica counts and model-parallelism degrees for both prefill and decode workers as an Integer Linear Program minimizing the worst-case P95 latency subject to a global GPU capacity constraint, solved via a standard MILP solver (SCIP) in under one minute for 256-GPU clusters.

## Results

AMPD achieves the highest SLO attainment across all experiments (3 models × 4 traces × multiple arrival rates). Compared to the disaggregated baseline Dynamo, AMPD improves SLO attainment by up to 967.54% (67.29% on average); compared to the co-located baseline vLLM, the improvement reaches up to 3435.1% (339.74% on average). The adaptive routing mechanism alone routes 13.9%–31.7% of prefill tasks locally on decode workers, boosting SLO attainment by 27.37%–350%, while the prefill reordering policy adds a further 13.42%–14.81% gain. The runtime overhead of both scheduling techniques is negligible (0.39 ms for routing, 0.45 ms for reordering, both on CPU and overlapping with GPU computation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMPD is compared against three baselines: Dynamo (NVIDIA's disaggregated serving system that always routes all prefill to dedicated prefill workers), vLLM (the standard co-located serving system), and vLLM-Continuum (a vLLM variant with session-aware queuing for multi-round workflows). AMPD dominates all three baselines on SLO attainment across every model (Qwen3-32B, Llama3.1-70B, Mixtral-8x7B) and trace (ToolBench, GAIA, HotpotQA, DuReader). Against Dynamo, AMPD's advantage comes from adaptive routing that offloads a meaningful fraction of incremental prefill tasks back to decode workers, reducing prefill-worker congestion and KV-transfer overhead; against vLLM, AMPD avoids the PD interference that inflates ITL in co-located serving. vLLM-Continuum, which prioritizes same-session tasks to shorten TTFT via KV reuse, is often comparable to or only marginally better than plain vLLM, and is consistently outperformed by AMPD. The paper notes that on GAIA (the longest multi-round trace with ~11 rounds), co-located serving becomes more competitive than Dynamo because its TTFT advantage offsets a moderate ITL penalty, yet AMPD still leads both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: prefill-decode (PD) disaggregation (DistServe, Splitwise, Dynamo) and multi-round LLM inference optimization (vLLM-Continuum, InferCept, KVFlow). Prior PD disaggregation systems assume a single prefill followed by a single decode per request, while prior multi-round optimizations (e.g., KV cache TTL, prefix sharing) are designed exclusively for co-located serving. AMPD departs from both by recognizing that multi-round workflows produce an interleaved prefill-decode pattern that invalidates the 'always route prefill to dedicated workers' assumption, and by formulating the deployment planning as an ILP that accounts for this interleaved structure rather than treating prefill and decode lengths independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As agentic and iterative-RAG workloads become the dominant deployment pattern for LLMs, the serving infrastructure must handle repeated incremental prefills between decode steps, a regime where naive disaggregation incurs substantial KV transfer overhead and prefill-worker congestion. The paper's adaptive routing insight—that a meaningful fraction (14–32%) of incremental prefills should execute locally on decode workers—challenges the rigid phase-separation assumption and offers a practical, low-overhead mechanism (sub-millisecond CPU cost) that practitioners can adopt atop existing disaggregated stacks. Open questions include integration with chunked prefill to further reduce decode blocking, handling workload drift and elastic scaling without full replanning, and whether the ILP-based planner generalizes to heterogeneous GPU clusters or multi-tenant settings.
