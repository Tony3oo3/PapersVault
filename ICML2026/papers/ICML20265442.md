## Problem

Label flip attacks on offline RLHF are practically plausible but lack theoretical analysis, unlike data injection attacks which have been studied. The core difficulty is that an attacker can only flip labels on existing comparisons (not inject new ones), and the effect of a single flip on the learned policy is hard to predict, making it unclear which labels to target and how many are needed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-collected pairwise preference dataset D (with known feature mapping ψ), a target policy parameter θ† chosen by the attacker, a flip budget K, and a lattice penalty M. It produces a binary flip pattern x ∈ {0,1}^n identifying which preference labels to invert, yielding an attacked dataset D̃(x). The attack matrix V is constructed from per-sample parameter-independent gradient shifts (columns v_i = o_i β Δψ_i), and the problem reduces to finding a sparse binary combination of V's columns that approximates the target gradient -g† within tolerance ε.

## Method

The key structural insight is that in log-linear DPO, flipping one preference label changes the DPO gradient by a fixed vector o_i β Δψ_i independent of the policy parameter θ, converting the targeted attack into a binary sparse approximation problem min 1ᵀx s.t. ‖Vx + g†‖₂ ≤ ε. Two algorithms solve this: BAL-A embeds the binary selection into a (d+n)-dimensional lattice with penalty M on non-binary coefficients, applies LLL basis reduction and Babai's nearest-plane decoding, and provides sufficient conditions (large M enforces binary coefficients; a separation condition on M recovers the minimum-flip solution). BMP-A adapts Binary Matching Pursuit to non-normalized gradient columns using normalized correlation scores, yielding coherence-based recovery guarantees and spectral-norm/coherence impossibility certificates showing when no K-flip attack can succeed. A strong-convexity lemma connects the gradient residual bound to policy-space closeness, justifying the gradient-space formulation as a sufficient surrogate for attack success.

## Results

On synthetic Gaussian dictionaries (64×20, K*=5), BAL-A achieves near-perfect support TPR for M below the separation threshold M_all_sep ≈ 0.68, with recall dropping sharply above it; the theoretical binary-coefficient bound M_0 ≈ 1.69 is overly conservative. On a low-coherence synthetic dictionary (200×200, μ(V) ≈ 0.197), BMP-A achieves perfect support recovery within the guaranteed regime (K* ≤ 3) and degrades gracefully beyond it. On the Stanford Human Preferences dataset (n=50, K*=7), both BAL-A and BMP-A recover the exact ground-truth flip pattern (TP=7, FP=0, FN=0), yielding an attack-vs-groundtruth policy distance of |π_θ† − π_θ̂| ≈ 0.012 and a clean-vs-attacked policy distance of ‖π_clean − π_θ̂‖₁ ≈ 0.224 (~19× larger), confirming the attack misleads downstream DPO. Runtime on the matched SHP case: BAL-A takes 0.69 s vs. BMP-A at 1.37×10⁻⁴ s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against other attack methods; it is the first theoretical analysis of label-flip attacks on offline DPO, so no prior attack baselines exist for direct comparison. The two proposed methods (BAL-A and BMP-A) target different sub-problems—BAL-A solves the unconstrained minimum-flip problem via lattice reduction, while BMP-A solves the budgeted K-flip sparse recovery problem via greedy pursuit—and are not intended as interchangeable solvers. In the one matched SHP diagnostic (n=50, K*=7), both methods recover the identical flip pattern exactly, so their downstream policy effects coincide. The key empirical validation is against the paper's own theoretical predictions: the BAL-A separation threshold and the BMP-A coherence bound (K_coh=3) are confirmed as conservative but correct, with empirical performance exceeding the guaranteed regime in both cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two classical tools—lattice reduction (LLL + Babai's nearest-plane algorithm) and Binary Matching Pursuit (Wen & Li, 2021)—for the novel application of targeted label-flip attacks on DPO. It builds directly on the log-linear DPO framework from Rafailov et al. (2023) and the RLHF theory of Nika et al. (2025), who provided the first comprehensive analysis of data-injection attacks but left label-flip attacks without theoretical treatment. The key departure from prior RLHF security work (Rando & Tramer, 2024; Baumgartner et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2025a) is the shift from empirical demonstrations to a structured binary sparse approximation formulation enabled by the parameter-independent gradient shift of log-linear DPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical framework for label-flip attacks on offline RLHF, providing both constructive attack algorithms (BAL-A, BMP-A) and impossibility certificates that quantify when DPO is robust to K-flip poisoning. The parameter-independent gradient shift (Theorem 3.1) is a clean structural result that could inform defense design, and the coherence-based impossibility conditions give practitioners a concrete robustness diagnostic. However, the analysis is restricted to log-linear policies with white-box feature access, and the experiments are small-scale (n ≤ 401); extending to neural policies, black-box settings, and designing provable defenses remain open.
