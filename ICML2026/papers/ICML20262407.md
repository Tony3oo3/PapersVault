## Problem

Existing unified object detectors that use Mixture-of-Experts to handle heterogeneous multi-dataset training suffer from imbalanced expert activation: dominant tasks and head classes over-activate experts while auxiliary modalities and tail classes are under-activated, impeding balanced feature learning. Additionally, task-specific drone detectors lack the flexibility to handle diverse input formats (RGB, IR, RGB-IR) within a single model, creating severe model redundancy and deployment challenges for all-weather aerial perception.

## Input / Output

The model accepts three input configurations: single RGB images (VisDrone), single IR images (DroneVehicle, RGBTDronePerson), and aligned RGB-IR image pairs (DroneVehicle, RGBTDronePerson). Single-modal inputs pass through modality-specific patch embedding functions, while dual-modal inputs are fused via simple averaging of the two patch embeddings before entering the shared transformer stream. Outputs are horizontal bounding boxes with confidence scores and category labels, produced by three dataset-specific detection heads (one per source dataset) rather than a single unified head.

## Method

DroneDINO extends the DINO framework with a Heterogeneous Routed Mixture of Experts (HR-MoE) module that structurally decouples the feature space into three expert groups with distinct activation principles: a shared expert activated for all inputs to capture task-invariant patterns, task-specific experts activated exclusively for their matching task to align heterogeneous distributions, and a dynamic expert group using standard top-k noisy routing for intra-task variability. The dynamic experts employ a Logarithmic Ensemble aggregation strategy and a dual load-balancing loss (monitoring both gating probability variance and token assignment count variance) to prevent expert collapse. A task-recognition auxiliary head, active only during training, classifies encoded features into five task categories via cross-entropy loss, penalizing representations that lack task discriminability. Training uses task-specialized batch sampling where each iteration processes a single task, and parameter updates selectively freeze detection heads for non-matching tasks to prevent inter-task interference.

## Results

On the Drone-UOD benchmark unifying five subtasks across three datasets, DroneDINO achieves 30.2 mAP / 50.4 mAP50 on VisDrone (Task 1), 63.2 mAP / 85.5 mAP50 on DroneVehicle IR (Task 2), 64.5 mAP / 86.4 mAP50 on DroneVehicle RGB-IR (Task 3), 55.8 mAP50 on RGBTDronePerson IR (Task 4), and 52.2 AP50 on RGBTDronePerson RGB-IR (Task 5). Against the strongest unified baseline CerberusDet, DroneDINO leads by 6.9 mAP on VisDrone and 15.4 mAP50 on RGBTDronePerson. Against task-specific detectors, DroneDINO surpasses RTMDet by +11.3 mAP on DroneVehicle RGB-IR and PPSDet by +8.6 AP50 on RGBTDronePerson RGB-IR, while trailing only UAV-DETR by 1.3 AP on VisDrone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against four unified detectors (UniDet, CerberusDet, PlainDet, SM3Det), DroneDINO wins on every reported subtask, with the largest margins over CerberusDet (+6.9 mAP on VisDrone, +15.4 mAP50 on RGBTDronePerson IR) and over SM3Det (+13.8 mAP on VisDrone). Among task-specific VisDrone detectors, DroneDINO (30.2 AP) beats all YOLO variants (25.2–26.3 AP), QueryDet (28.3 AP), HIC-YOLOv5 (26.0 AP), and all transformer-based baselines (DETR 24.1, Deformable-DETR 27.1, Sparse-DETR 27.3, RT-DETR 28.4 AP), but trails UAV-DETR (31.5 AP) by 1.3 AP. On DroneVehicle, DroneDINO outperforms Faster R-CNN, DDQ-DETR, and RTMDet in both IR and RGB-IR settings, with the most striking gain of +11.3 mAP over RTMDet in the RGB-IR fusion task. On RGBTDronePerson, DroneDINO surpasses all IR baselines (Faster R-CNN 35.2, RTMDet 31.1, DDQ-DETR 53.5 AP50) and all RGB-IR fusion methods (HRFuser 22.2, TINet 28.3, ICAFusion 28.6, C2Former 37.7, QFDet 42.1, PPSDet 43.6 AP50), achieving 55.8 and 52.2 AP50 respectively. Overall, DroneDINO is the only method evaluated on all five subtasks simultaneously and wins 16 of 17 head-to-head comparisons, losing only to UAV-DETR on the single-modality VisDrone RGB task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DINO detection framework by integrating a Mixture-of-Experts module into the backbone, following the line of work from V-MoE, DAMEX, and SM3Det that applies sparse MoE to multi-dataset or multi-modal detection. Its key architectural departure is the Heterogeneous Routed MoE (HR-MoE), which replaces the conventional homogeneous top-k routing with a tripartite expert structure (shared, task-specific, dynamic) to address expert over-activation under imbalanced multi-task data distributions. The task-recognition auxiliary loss, which penalizes features with low task-discriminability, is a novel training strategy not seen in prior unified detectors like UniDet, PlainDet, or CerberusDet, which rely solely on detection losses without explicit task-alignment supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DroneDINO provides a practical single-model solution for all-weather drone perception by unifying RGB, IR, and RGB-IR detection across five subtasks, reducing deployment redundancy compared to maintaining separate task-specific models. The HR-MoE design offers a generalizable recipe for balancing expert activation in multi-task MoE settings beyond drone detection. However, the framework is limited to horizontal bounding boxes, retains task-specific detection heads rather than a truly unified output interface, and is validated on only three datasets with 12-epoch training, leaving open whether the gains persist with oriented boxes, open-vocabulary labels, additional modalities (SAR, hyperspectral), or longer training schedules.
