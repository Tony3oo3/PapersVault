## Problem

Asynchronous RL accelerates LLM training through parallel data collection but introduces policy lag—experiences collected under stale weights destabilize learning, particularly on hard tasks where the loss landscape is sharp. Existing staleness control applies uniform thresholds that ignore task structure, while curriculum learning methods assume synchronous updates that bottleneck throughput. The paper identifies the underlying mechanism (gradient variance scales exponentially with task difficulty due to narrow solution spaces and large Hessian eigenvalues) and derives principled, difficulty-aware staleness budgets from a constrained optimization formulation.

## Input / Output

The method takes code prompts paired with a controlled fraction of a canonical solution (90% revealed at the easiest level down to 0% at the hardest) along with test cases for execution-based reward; the policy generates a code completion scored by test-case pass rate. Difficulty is an ordinal index that can be set structurally via solution revelation (as in the code experiments) or estimated online from loss and empirical success rate (as in the GSM8K transfer), making the framework applicable to domains without reference solutions.

## Method

GAS is a drop-in recipe for asynchronous GRPO training built from three coordinated components, each targeting a distinct failure mode of naive async training. ACB selects task difficulty each iteration via a UCB bandit that balances success rate against gradient magnitude, steering the policy toward the zone of proximal development where signal-to-noise ratio is highest. EAAS scales each task's staleness budget inversely with its predicted execution time so that slow tasks receive fresher weights and do not bottleneck rollout. CSC implements the paper's central theoretical result: the maximum staleness budget decays exponentially with difficulty as η_max(d) = η_base · exp(−λd) with λ = α/2 (half the measured Hessian growth rate), so easy tasks tolerate more staleness while hard tasks are gated to near-fresh gradients; experiences exceeding their difficulty-specific threshold are discarded or down-weighted before the GRPO update with group-relative advantages, clipped surrogate loss, and KL regularization.

## Results

On code generation (HumanEval + Synthetic, 214 tasks), GAS achieves 60.1% Pass@1, a +20.4-point improvement over synchronous GRPO (39.7%), while training at 2.3× the throughput (22.4 vs. 9.7 samples/s). The gains transfer to a 6× larger Qwen3.5-9B hybrid model (Gated DeltaNet + MoE) with +54% training reward over Sync and to GSM8K mathematical reasoning with +83% training reward and 2.3× faster convergence, all without retuning hyperparameters (η_base=8, λ=0.5, ω=0.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAS outperforms all four baselines on Pass@1 while maintaining near-async throughput. Against Sync-GRPO (39.7%), GAS gains +20.4 points at 2.3× the speed. Against Sync-GRPO+CCCS (51.5%), which adds a fixed curriculum but remains synchronous, GAS gains +8.6 points and 2.5× throughput, showing that adaptive curriculum plus difficulty-aware staleness coupling recovers curriculum benefits in the async setting. Against naive Async-GRPO (31.8%), GAS gains +28.3 points, confirming that uncontrolled staleness severely degrades hard-task learning. Against Async-GRPO+Staleness (40.3%), which applies a uniform staleness threshold, GAS gains +19.8 points, demonstrating that difficulty-aware (exponential-decay) staleness budgets are essential—uniform thresholds cannot protect the sharp-loss-landscape hard tasks. The only tradeoff is a modest 4.7% throughput reduction versus plain Async-GRPO (22.4 vs. 24.3 smp/s) due to CSC discarding stale hard-task samples, which the paper argues is a favorable exchange for the 18-point quality gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two largely separate literatures: asynchronous RL (IMPALA, AReaL) which addresses throughput via parallel data collection but applies uniform staleness thresholds, and curriculum learning for code RL (StepCoder/CCCS, Graves et al.) which sequences task difficulty but assumes synchronous updates. The key departure is a theoretical coupling: by showing that Hessian eigenvalues grow exponentially with task difficulty (Theorem 4.2), the authors derive that the optimal staleness budget must decay exponentially with difficulty (Theorem 4.4), unifying the two threads into a single principled recipe (GAS). The GRPO objective from DeepSeekMath provides the training backbone, while the UCB bandit from Graves et al. is repurposed for adaptive difficulty selection, and the execution-time-aware scheduling extends AReaL's uniform staleness control with code-specific latency prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners running asynchronous RL for code agents, GAS offers a concrete, drop-in recipe that recovers curriculum-quality sample efficiency at 2.3× synchronous throughput—a practically significant efficiency gain. The theoretical framing (staleness tolerance ∝ e^{−αd/2} from Hessian curvature) gives a principled, non-heuristic way to set per-difficulty staleness budgets, and the transfer to GSM8K with unchanged hyperparameters suggests the coupling is not code-specific. However, evidence is limited to the Qwen model family, n=3 seeds, and a self-generated synthetic benchmark; the execution-time predictor ignores generation latency (the dominant async bottleneck), and the proof's Step 5→5b reconciliation introduces modeling assumptions (M1, M2) that are asserted rather than derived. Open questions include online estimation of α, continuous difficulty, and whether the exponential law holds under different reward structures or larger-scale training.
