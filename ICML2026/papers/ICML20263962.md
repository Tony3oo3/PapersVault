## Problem

Vision-Language Models exhibit systematic failures on multi-object visual tasks—hallucinating non-existent objects, confusing which color belongs to which shape—yet the internal representational mechanisms driving these errors remain poorly understood. While behavioral documentation exists for proprietary models, no prior work has characterized the geometric structure of visual concept representations in open-weight VLMs or demonstrated that this geometry causally determines model behavior.

## Input / Output

The method consumes synthetic 448×448 images containing 2–40 colored shapes (from 6 colors × 6 shapes) and natural images (COCO/VQA), paired with text prompts, and produces normalized concept vectors in the LLM's residual stream (3584-d for Qwen, 4096-d for InternVL, 3840-d for Gemma). Downstream, these vectors yield cosine similarity scores that predict error rates on visual search and color similarity tasks, and steering interventions that redirect model perception (e.g., forcing a red rose to be described as blue) with 85–96% accuracy on natural images.

## Method

The paper proposes a three-part framework: (1) concept vector extraction via two complementary methods—supervised attention-based linear probes (with a PCA-regularized variant that projects probe directions onto the 2N−2 dimensional subspace spanned by the factorial color×shape structure) and centroid-based distillation (subtracting the global activation mean from per-concept mean activations and normalizing); (2) causal validation through a projection-based steering intervention that, for each token embedding, subtracts its component along the source concept vector and re-injects it along the target concept vector, preserving original feature intensity rather than using a fixed scaling factor; (3) geometric analysis showing that the color manifold forms a continuous 1-D structure with categorical-perception warping (within-hue compression, between-hue separation), and that cosine similarity between concept vectors monotonically predicts accuracy on multi-object visual search (r = −0.69 to −0.97) and logit separation on color similarity tasks (r = 0.78–0.84), establishing geometric interference as a quantitative signature of VLM binding failures.

## Results

Centroid-based concept vectors achieve 84.7–95.7% steering accuracy on natural images across Qwen, InternVL, and Gemma, substantially outperforming raw probes (3.7–61.1%) and PCA-regularized probes (16.3–92.0%). In the visual search task, cosine similarity between target and distractor concept vectors yields strong negative correlations with model accuracy (r = −0.90 to −0.97 for target-present; r = −0.69 to −0.88 for target-absent), confirming that geometric interference predicts binding failures. On the color similarity task, concept-vector similarity predicts model logit separation (confidence) with r = 0.78–0.84, versus only r = 0.60–0.66 for HSV perceptual distance, while both metrics achieve similar answer-prediction accuracy (70–75%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods but instead benchmarks three internal concept-vector extraction approaches: raw attention probes, PCA-regularized probes, and centroid-based distillation. For color steering on natural images, the centroid method dominates on Qwen (84.7% vs. 3.7% for probes) and Gemma (95.7% vs. 3.7%), while PCA-regularized probes achieve the best result on InternVL (92.0% vs. 88.4% for centroids). For compositional color-shape steering on synthetic multi-object images, centroids again lead on Qwen (78.1%) and Gemma (75.3%), but PCA probes outperform centroids on InternVL (46.8% vs. 35.9%). Raw probes fail almost entirely across all settings (0–61%), confirming that unconstrained discriminative objectives learn shortcut directions that do not align with the model's causal representational structure. The PCA regularization consistently improves over raw probes but remains inconsistent across architectures, whereas centroids provide the most stable performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept vector framework from Kim et al. (2018) and the probing methodology of Tenney et al. (2019) to the visual domain of VLMs, introducing a centroid-based extraction method and PCA structural regularization that depart from standard linear-probe practice. It builds on activation steering (Turner et al., 2024) but replaces the fixed global scaling factor with an activation-modulated intervention that preserves feature intensity. Conceptually, it operationalizes the 'Curse of Generalization' hypothesis (Frankland et al., 2026) and Treisman's feature integration theory (1980) by showing that the geometric overlap of concept vectors in a shared latent space quantitatively predicts the binding failures previously documented only at the behavioral level (Campbell et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first quantitative, geometry-based account of why VLMs fail on multi-object tasks: cosine similarity between concept vectors predicts both error rates (|r| > 0.83) and model confidence (r > 0.77) across three architecturally diverse models, offering a principled early-warning signal for hallucinations and illusory conjunctions. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic stimuli with simple categorical attributes (6 colors, 6 shapes), analysis of only the vision encoder output, and characterization of what representations look like without tracing how they are computed. Open questions include whether the observed categorical-perception-like 'accordion effect' is induced by the LLM's discrete token space or arises intrinsically, and whether the framework scales to abstract or compositional attributes beyond basic color-shape conjunctions.
