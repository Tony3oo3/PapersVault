## Problem

Q-based imitation learning methods such as IQ-Learn were widely believed to mitigate compounding errors by leveraging online environment interactions, yet their theoretical foundations remained largely unexplored. This paper proves that IQ-Learn actually reduces to behavioral cloning and inherits its quadratic compounding errors, then asks the fundamental question: which algorithmic mechanisms are necessary for Q-based IL to provably generalize beyond demonstrations and eliminate compounding errors?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265269/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in episodic MDPs with layered state spaces, consuming N expert demonstration trajectories of horizon H along with online environment interactions. Online interactions serve two distinct roles: estimating the initial-state log-sum-exp term and, critically, evaluating Bellman constraints over the online replay buffer to enable value propagation. The output is a bounded Q-function from which a softmax policy is derived, with the key structural property that Q-values at unvisited states are non-uniform due to value flow through transition dynamics, unlike IQ-Learn which assigns uniform Q-values at unseen states.

## Method

Dual Q-DM is derived through a primal-dual framework: starting from primal distribution matching over occupancy measures, Lagrangian duality yields a dual V-function formulation, and a change-of-variables produces the final single-maximization Q-function objective. The critical distinguishing feature from IQ-Learn is the Bellman constraints (0 ≤ Q(s,a) − E_{s'}[LSE(Q)(s')] ≤ 1), which drive value flow—Q-values propagate from demonstrated successor states to unvisited current states through environment dynamics. The paper proves Dual Q-DM is equivalent to AIL (Theorem 2) and can identify expert actions at uncovered states in transition-discriminative MDPs (Proposition 1), making it the first non-adversarial IL method provably free of compounding errors. Practically, Bellman constraints are enforced via quadratic ReLU penalties evaluated over the online replay buffer, enabling value flow to reach states far from the demonstration support.

## Results

On the hard tabular instance (Corollary 1), BC, IQ-Learn (both TV and χ² divergences), and ValueDICE exhibit imitation gaps growing quadratically with horizon H, confirming compounding errors, while Dual Q-DM and AIL maintain consistently small imitation gaps as H increases. On 5 MuJoCo tasks, Dual Q-DM achieves the highest final performance among all Q-based methods across all trajectory counts (1, 5, 10, 20, 50), and demonstrates faster convergence and improved training stability compared to AIL baselines (DAC, HyPE) due to its non-adversarial optimization. The paper does not report a single consolidated numerical results table; quantitative comparisons are presented via learning curves (Figure 2), final performance plots (Figure 3), and imitation gap plots (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dual Q-DM outperforms all Q-based baselines on MuJoCo: IQ-Learn and ValueDICE perform comparably to BC (confirming Theorem 1's prediction that IQ-Learn reduces to BC), while LS-IQ and ReCOIL improve over IQ-Learn via regularization but still fall short of Dual Q-DM. On the hard tabular instance, Dual Q-DM matches AIL in maintaining small imitation gaps across horizons, whereas BC, IQ-Learn, and ValueDICE all show quadratic growth. Against AIL methods (DAC and HyPE), Dual Q-DM achieves comparable final performance but with faster convergence and greater training stability, attributed to its single-objective non-adversarial optimization. The paper also notes that the regularization terms in LS-IQ and ReCOIL share structural similarities with Dual Q-DM's Bellman constraints, providing a theoretical explanation for their partial effectiveness from a value flow perspective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the adversarial imitation learning (AIL) framework of Ho & Ermon (2016), which achieves linear-horizon imitation gaps through state-action distribution matching, and on the Q-based IL line initiated by ValueDICE (Kostrikov et al., 2020) and IQ-Learn (Garg et al., 2021). It departs sharply from the prevailing view that Q-based IL methods inherit AIL's compounding-error mitigation: the paper proves IQ-Learn reduces to BC via a re-parameterization argument, identifying an omitted constraint in IQ-Learn's change-of-variables step. The proposed Dual Q-DM method is derived through a rigorous primal-dual framework (Lagrangian duality over occupancy measures) that recovers the Bellman constraints as the missing ingredient, establishing formal equivalence with AIL while remaining non-adversarial.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that IQ-Learn — a widely adopted Q-based method — is provably equivalent to BC overturns a core assumption in the Q-based IL literature and explains why online interactions alone are insufficient without a value-propagation mechanism. The identification of Bellman constraints as the key to value flow provides a principled design criterion for future Q-based IL methods and retroactively justifies regularization terms in LS-IQ and RIZE. Open questions include whether the TD MDP assumption (transition discriminability) is necessary or merely sufficient, whether the results extend to function approximation and continuous action spaces, and whether the quadratic penalty implementation of Bellman constraints scales to high-dimensional environments.
