## Problem

Computational RNA inverse design—finding sequences that fold into a target secondary structure—suffers from an exponentially large search space (4^ℓ), sparse rewards, and numerous local optima that cause existing RL and stochastic methods to stall. Prior RL approaches treat the task as a single-agent sequential decision problem, making value estimation unreliable and exploration inefficient. This paper is the first to frame biological sequence design as a cooperative multi-agent decision-making problem, decomposing the design task to reduce per-agent complexity and improve collective exploration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target RNA secondary structure in dot-bracket notation (length up to 450 nt in the primary implementation; longer sequences handled via sliding window) and produces a nucleotide sequence (A/U/G/C) whose MFE-predicted fold matches the target exactly (Hamming distance = 0). Each agent observes only a local 61-nucleotide window of sequence and structure centered on its assigned position, while the centralized Critic receives the full 900-dimensional global state (450 sequence + 450 structure). A matrix-based variant replaces dot-bracket input with a three-channel (Structure, Sequence, Position) tensor processed by a CNN, enabling pseudoknot design.

## Method

COCORNA formulates RNA design as a Dec-POMDP solved with MAPPO under the CTDE paradigm: N agents (default n=4) each control a segment of the sequence via an MLP Actor operating on local observations, while a shared CNN-based Critic evaluates the global sequence-structure state to compute advantages via GAE. The key architectural insight is problem decomposition—either position-based (each agent owns a contiguous segment) or structure-type-based (each agent handles a structural motif)—which reduces each agent's state and policy space dimensionality. Base-pair compatibility is enforced as a hard constraint through environment-side validity masks that restrict admissible nucleotides at paired positions. A search-augmented exploration (SAE) heuristic performs brief local greedy searches during the first 30% of training to seed the replay buffer with higher-quality experiences, mitigating cold-start without altering the optimization objective. The reward combines a per-step normalized Hamming-distance reduction with a large terminal bonus C upon exact structural match, and multi-objective variants add GC-content or MFE penalties only after structural success.

## Results

On the Rfam test set (650 structures), COCORNA achieves a 97.75% success rate under a strict 30-second time limit, surpassing the best same-budget baseline (SAMFEO-30s, 70.50%) by 27.25 percentage points. Even when the strongest baseline (libLEARNA-600s) is given 20× more compute (600 s), COCORNA still leads by 7.96 points (97.75% vs. 89.79%). Inference is extremely fast: COCORNA solves a structure in 3.24 s on average, over 70× faster than Meta-LEARNA-Adapt (234.2 s). COCORNA also achieves the highest solution diversity (0.405 normalized pairwise Hamming distance) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COCORNA decisively outperforms all four baseline families. Against LEARNA (Runge et al., 2019), the gap is stark: LEARNA-600s reaches only 58.80% success versus COCORNA's 97.75% at 30 s, a 38.95-point deficit even with 20× more time. libLEARNA (Runge et al., 2024) is the closest competitor at 600 s (89.79%), yet still trails COCORNA by 7.96 points while requiring 20× the compute. OmniGenome (Yang et al., 2025), a 186M-parameter foundation model, achieves 81.91% at 1200 s—15.84 points below COCORNA—and does not surpass classical stochastic optimizers. antaRNA (Kleinkauf et al., 2015) performs worst overall, reaching only 26.37% even at 1200 s, frequently trapped in local optima. In terms of speed, COCORNA's 3.24 s average solution time is over 70× faster than Meta-LEARNA-Adapt and roughly 370× faster than antaRNA at its best setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

COCORNA builds directly on MAPPO (Yu et al., 2022) as its base algorithm and adopts the CTDE paradigm from the MARL literature, but its key conceptual departure is framing RNA inverse design—a task previously tackled by single-agent RL (Eastman et al., 2018; Runge et al., 2019) or stochastic optimization (RNA-SSD, INFORNA, antaRNA, SAMFEO)—as a cooperative multi-agent decision-making problem over a Dec-POMDP. The problem decomposition into position-based or structure-type-based subtasks, combined with a centralized CNN Critic for global structural awareness, distinguishes it from both prior single-agent RL approaches and from RNA-SSD's independent substructure optimization, which lacks global coordination. The search-augmented exploration heuristic is a practical addition that does not alter the underlying optimization objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COCORNA's 97.75% success rate at 30 seconds (vs. 70.50% for the best baseline at the same budget) and 70× speed advantage make RNA inverse design substantially more practical for experimental workflows, and its zero-shot generalization to sequences ~2× longer than training and to a different folding oracle (RibonanzaNet) suggests the learned policies capture transferable structural principles rather than overfitting to a specific oracle. Open questions include whether adaptive or hierarchical decomposition can further improve scalability, how to handle credit assignment as agent counts grow, and whether more accurate (e.g., AlphaFold-3-based) reward models can push success rates further. The framework's demonstrated applicability to other biological sequence design tasks (TF binding, drug molecules, UTR design) hints at broader utility beyond RNA.
