## Problem

Existing Federated Continual Learning (FCL) methods assume all clients receive and begin new tasks simultaneously, an unrealistic constraint that forces faster clients into costly idle waiting. In practice, asynchronous task arrival means the server receives a mixture of updates from clients at different time steps, inducing a severe 'task drift' that collapses the global model between conflicting tasks and prevents effective learning of any single task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262598/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client trains a ResNet-18 classifier on local RGB images (CIFAR-100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, Office-31, Office-Caltech-10) partitioned into sequential tasks with 10–20 classes per task or one domain per task. Rather than exchanging raw data, clients upload parameter updates (differences from the downloaded global model) to the server, which must integrate these updates asynchronously as they arrive from clients potentially working on different tasks. The final output is a unified global model that predicts class labels across all learned tasks.

## Method

C²-AFCL introduces a two-stage orthogonal calibration before aggregation. First, intra-client calibration maintains a task-aware cache per client on the server; each incoming raw update is differenced against the last cached update for the same task, isolating the client's immediate learning progress and canceling slowly-varying components attributable to fixed data distributions and system delays. Second, inter-task orthogonal calibration dynamically constructs an interference subspace from the SVD of historical global task-update vectors; each calibrated update is decomposed into a component within this subspace (identified as potentially harmful to prior knowledge) and an orthogonal preserving component, and only the latter is aggregated into the global model. This explicitly decouples new task knowledge from conflicts at the fine-grained update level, and the paper proves the resulting algorithm achieves the same O(1/√R) convergence rate as standard federated optimization under bounded staleness.

## Results

C²-AFCL achieves the best average accuracy (AC) and lowest forgetting score (FS) across all four datasets and both incremental settings. On CIFAR-100 (class-incremental), it reaches 42.57% AC with 27.36 FS, surpassing the strongest baseline FedSpace (38.61% AC, 30.99 FS) by approximately 4 percentage points in accuracy. On Office-Caltech-10 (domain-incremental), it attains 52.66% AC with 20.32 FS. The paper reports up to 13.07% improvement in average test accuracy over state-of-the-art baselines across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C²-AFCL outperforms all nine baselines on both AC and FS across all four datasets. Among asynchronous FL methods, FedAsync and FedBuff are the weakest (e.g., 30.66% and 29.50% AC on CIFAR-100), while CA²FL improves to 33.08% but still trails C²-AFCL by over 9 points. FCL baselines (GLFC, FedCIL, Re-Fed) reduce forgetting via replay or regularization but remain fragile under asynchrony, achieving only 31–32% AC on CIFAR-100. The strongest FCL baselines, FOT (37.13% AC, 28.76 FS) and FedSSI (33.75% AC, 30.36 FS), are surpassed by C²-AFCL by 5.4 and 8.8 points in AC respectively on CIFAR-100. FedSpace, the only prior AFCL method, is the closest competitor (38.61% AC, 30.99 FS on CIFAR-100; 48.32% AC, 23.72 FS on Office-Caltech-10), yet C²-AFCL still leads by 3.96 and 4.34 points in AC and by 3.63 and 3.40 points in FS on those datasets, respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate lines of work—cached update calibration for asynchronous FL (CA²FL, ICLR 2024) and orthogonal subspace projection for federated continual learning (FOT, 2023)—into a unified two-stage framework tailored to the asynchronous FCL setting. The asynchronous aggregation protocol follows FedAsync's immediate-integration scheme, while the inter-task interference subspace construction generalizes FOT's static orthogonal projection to a dynamic, online SVD-based estimation. The key conceptual departure is reframing update calibration from a purely statistical variance-reduction operation to a semantic knowledge-management operation, where the orthogonal projection explicitly decouples new-task learning from prior-task preservation at the update-vector level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying task drift as a distinct and more severe failure mode than client drift in asynchronous settings, the paper provides a principled decomposition that future AFCL methods can build upon. The convergence guarantee matching standard FL rates (O(1/√R)) under bounded staleness and periodic subspace updates gives practitioners confidence that the calibration overhead does not degrade optimization. Open questions include whether the SVD-based subspace estimation scales to very large model dimensions or long task sequences, whether the eigen-gap assumption (Lemma 5.5) holds in practice for high-dimensional neural network updates, and whether the approach extends beyond classification to tasks like segmentation or generation where update geometry differs.
