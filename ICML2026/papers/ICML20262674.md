## Problem

Extending diffusion-based policies to online multi-agent reinforcement learning is blocked by three mechanism mismatches: intractable likelihoods prevent entropy-based exploration, monolithic architectures conflict with the CTDE paradigm's decentralized execution requirement, and iterative denoising complicates step-wise inter-agent coordination. Prior diffusion RL work was confined to offline settings with expert data or single-agent online settings, leaving efficient from-scratch multi-agent coordination with generative policies largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

Each agent's diffusion policy takes its local observation s and, during the reverse denoising process, the current noisy action a_h^i concatenated with a 256-dimensional Fourier timestep embedding, producing a continuous action vector a_0^i after H=8 Euler-Maruyama steps initialized from N(0, η²I). The centralized distributional critic consumes the concatenated joint state-action pair (s, a_0) through Batch Normalization and outputs a 101-atom probability distribution over the return support [−V_max, V_max]. The joint action a_0 = (a_0^1, …, a_0^N) is the terminal output of synchronized per-agent denoising trajectories.

## Method

OMAD factorizes the joint policy as π_θ(a|s)=∏_i π_θ_i(a^i|s), where each agent's policy is a continuous-time SDE-based diffusion process parameterized by a score network f_θ_i. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.1) derives a tractable ELBO lower bound for the intractable joint entropy by exploiting the factorized structure, enabling a relaxed maximum-entropy objective that drives exploration without requiring exact likelihoods. A centralized Joint Distributional Critic (CrossQ architecture with Batch Normalization and stop-gradient, no target Q-network) models the full return distribution and provides unified value guidance. All agents' diffusion policies are optimized simultaneously via a single synchronized KL-divergence loss over latent denoising trajectories (Eq. 14), aligning each agent's reverse process with the forward noising process whose terminal distribution is exp(Q_φ/α)/Z(s). A dual-constrained auto-tuning mechanism dynamically adjusts the temperature α to maintain the aggregate ELBO at a target threshold, eliminating manual hyperparameter search.

## Results

OMAD achieves state-of-the-art maximum average episode return across all 10 MPE and MAMuJoCo tasks, trained for only 3×10⁶ steps (vs. 1×10⁷ for baselines), yielding a 2.5×–5× sample efficiency gain. On the most challenging HalfCheetah 2×3 task, OMAD reaches 14,368.5 ± 1,166.0, surpassing the best baseline (MASDAC, 10,540.7) by over 36%. On Ant 2×4, OMAD attains 7,517.0 ± 279.2, outperforming the strongest baseline (MASDAC, 7,266.2) with notably lower variance. State coverage analysis on Ant 2×4 shows OMAD explores 68.3% of the discretized state space vs. 48.4% (HATD3) and 55.0% (HASAC), confirming superior exploration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMAD outperforms all four baselines on every one of the 10 evaluated tasks. Against the value-based Gaussian-policy baselines HATD3 and HASAC, OMAD achieves substantially higher returns (e.g., +1,255 on HalfCheetah 2×3 over HASAC's 8,835.7) while requiring only 30% of the training steps. Against the diffusion-based baselines MADPMD and MASDAC (naive multi-agent extensions of single-agent diffusion RL), OMAD still wins across the board, with the largest margin on HalfCheetah 2×3 (+3,828 over MASDAC) and Ant 2×4d (+513 over MASDAC). Notably, MADPMD and MASDAC employ centralized training *and* centralized execution, yet their lack of a distributional critic and effective entropy regularization leads to slower convergence and inferior asymptotic performance. The gap is particularly pronounced in high-dimensional locomotion tasks (Ant, HalfCheetah), where the multimodal coordination demands exceed what unimodal Gaussian policies can express, while in lower-dimensional MPE tasks the advantage is more moderate but still consistent.

## Relation to Prior Work

OMAD extends the single-agent diffusion-based maximum entropy RL framework of DIME (Celik et al., 2025) to the multi-agent online off-policy setting by deriving a factorized entropy lower bound (Theorem 4.1) that makes the intractable joint diffusion policy entropy computable. The centralized critic combines CrossQ's Batch Normalization + stop-gradient mechanism (Bhatt et al., 2024) with distributional value estimation (Bellemare et al., 2017), while the policy side adopts the SDE-based diffusion formulation from Ma et al. (2025). The key departure from prior work is the synchronized CTDE update: rather than decoupled per-agent losses (as in HARL/HASAC), all agents are jointly optimized under a single global distributional critic, and the entropy regularization is applied via the variational ELBO rather than exact likelihoods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to make online off-policy diffusion MARL tractable, resolving the fundamental intractability barrier that has kept diffusion policies confined to offline or single-agent online settings. The 2.5×–5× sample efficiency gains and the state-coverage analysis (68.3% vs. 48.4%/55.0%) provide concrete evidence that expressive generative policies, when properly coupled with centralized value guidance, outperform both unimodal Gaussian policies and naive multi-agent diffusion extensions. Open questions include scalability to large agent counts (N≫10), extension to discrete action spaces, tighter ELBO bounds to reduce approximation bias, and reducing the per-step computational overhead of iterative denoising.
