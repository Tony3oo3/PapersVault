## Problem

Existing KV cache eviction methods (H2O, SnapKV, AdaKV, HeadKV) identify critical cache entries solely by accumulated attention weights, an assumption that lacks formal justification. This paper asks two foundational questions: what criteria actually determine critical KV cache entries, and whether attention weights alone are sufficient. The answer is no — the value states projected through the pretrained output matrix W^O are equally essential, and ignoring them makes prior selection strategies inherently suboptimal.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes the current query state q, the full KV cache (K, V ∈ R^{n×d_h}), and the output projection matrix W^O ∈ R^{d_h×d}, along with a budget b specifying how many entries to retain. It outputs b selected critical KV entries. A key practical detail is that the metric requires computing the L1 norms of projected value states V·W^O, a tensor absent from standard attention computation graphs; the authors implement a custom fused Triton kernel with block-wise online L1 accumulation to avoid materializing this ~7.8 GB intermediate per layer. The algorithm is designed as a drop-in replacement for the final Top-K selection step in existing eviction pipelines, leaving their upstream accumulation and budget-allocation logic untouched.

## Method

The paper formalizes critical entry selection as minimizing the L1 distance between full-cache and evicted-cache attention outputs, then derives a worst-case upper bound θ = C − (2 − 1/ΣN_iA_i)·ΣN_iA_i‖V_iW^O‖_1 that couples attention weights with projected value-state norms. Guided by this bound, a two-stage greedy algorithm is proposed: Stage 1 reserves α·b entries (α=0.5) for the highest-attention-weight entries as a safeguard ensuring cumulative attention mass exceeds 0.5 (Assumption 3.4, validated in >99% of heads); Stage 2 allocates the remaining budget to entries maximizing A_i·‖V_iW^O‖_1, which Theorem 3.5 proves equivalent to minimizing a tighter perturbation upper bound than the attention-only relaxation used by prior methods. The approach is model-agnostic and training-free, requiring only a single forward pass to compute the projected value norms. Integrated into SnapKV, AdaKV, and HeadKV, it reduces average compression loss by more than half across 29 Ruler and LongBench datasets on Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, and Qwen-2.5-32B, with negligible TTFT overhead (~0.06 s at 32K context).

## Results

On the Ruler benchmark at 40% cache size, the perturbation-constrained selection algorithm reduces the average compression loss by more than half across all three base methods and three LLMs (Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-32B), with an average score improvement of 14.6 points. The most dramatic gain is on Qwen2.5-32B with HeadKV, where the loss relative to full cache drops from 13.7% to just 3.4% (score 81.04 → 90.69 vs. full cache 93.91). On LongBench (16 datasets, 6 domains), the method improves 88 out of 90 test cases (97.8% success rate) across three models, three eviction methods, and two cache sizes (20% and 40%). The algorithm introduces negligible overhead: only 0.06s additional TTFT at 32K context (batch size 1) due to the linear-complexity computation of ‖VW^O‖₁.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method is a plug-and-play enhancement integrated into three SOTA eviction baselines (SnapKV, AdaKV, HeadKV), and it consistently outperforms each base method across all 29 datasets. Against PyramidKV on LongBench (40% cache, Llama-3.1-8B), AdaKV+Ours achieves 48.00 vs. PyramidKV's 44.20, and HeadKV+Ours reaches 47.79. Against DuoAttention, the advantage is most pronounced under aggressive compression: on Mistral-7B at 20% cache, HeadKV+Ours scores 43.46 while DuoAttention drops to 31.13 (a 12.3-point gap), and AdaKV+Ours (42.85) leads by 11.7 points. Against OBCache (which also uses an output-perturbation perspective but via second-order Taylor expansion without the W^O matrix), the paper's method achieves higher scores on both LongBench and Ruler at both 20% and 40% budgets—for example, on Llama-3.1-8B at 40% cache, SnapKV+Ours scores 76.89 on Ruler vs. SnapKV+OBCache's 71.53. The method also outperforms the H2O baseline (simulated via last-256-token attention) in all settings, though H2O is included only for reference due to FlashAttention-2 incompatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of KV cache eviction methods (H2O, Scissorhands, SnapKV, AdaKV, HeadKV) that identify critical entries via accumulated attention weights, but departs from them by formalizing the selection problem through output perturbation analysis. Conceptually, it draws on perturbation-based techniques from neural network interpretability (Catformer, Admin) and pruning (Wanda, SparseGPT), applying this lens to KV cache eviction for the first time. The key departure is showing that the pretrained output projection matrix W^O and value state norms—previously ignored by all attention-weight-based methods—must be jointly considered to bound worst-case output perturbation, yielding a two-stage greedy selection that replaces the final Top-K step in existing pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first formal grounding for what makes a KV cache entry 'critical,' moving the field beyond empirical heuristics and showing that attention weights alone are provably insufficient. As a universal plug-and-play module with negligible overhead (linear-time W^O projection), it is immediately deployable alongside any existing eviction method, which broadens its practical impact for efficient long-context inference. Open questions include extending the framework to attention-free eviction (e.g., StreamingLLM), exploring finer-grained per-head α tuning, investigating more sophisticated distance metrics beyond L1/L2, and adapting the analysis to multimodal settings where visual redundancy may alter the perturbation landscape.
