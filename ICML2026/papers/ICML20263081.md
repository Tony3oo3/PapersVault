## Problem

Online change point detection for multivariate inhomogeneous Poisson point process (PPP) time series remains largely unexplored: existing point-process change-point methods are either parametric (e.g., Hawkes-network CUSUM) or offline, and none provide a general-purpose nonparametric procedure with finite-sample guarantees under realistic temporal dependence. The core difficulty is that each observation is an unordered random set with random cardinality, the signal is an infinite-dimensional intensity-function change, and consecutive samples exhibit geometric β-mixing dependence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of PPP realizations X^(i) ⊂ X ⊂ R^d (d ≥ 2), where each X^(i) is a random set of points generated over a fixed observation window (e.g., one day of events). A training phase of N_train pre-change samples calibrates the basis size, rank, coordinate partition, and detection threshold via block-permutation. The output is a single binary alarm raised at the first time the restricted-SVD CUSUM statistic exceeds a data-driven threshold, yielding a detection delay measured in number of post-change windows.

## Method

Each PPP sample is mapped to a finite-dimensional intensity matrix via a tensor-product RKHS basis expansion, with the d coordinates split into two groups (sizes p and q) to form an M^p × M^q matrix. A CUSUM statistic is computed as the difference between pre- and post-change matrix averages over a sliding window, and a Restricted SVD (RSVD) step adaptively trims the basis to a size proportional to the effective sample size before applying a rank-r SVD projection, exploiting the approximate low-rank structure of the intensity difference. Dynamic programming maintains running prefix and suffix sums so that the per-observation cost is O(rW^{1+d/(2γ+p∨q)})—constant in the elapsed series length—making the algorithm single-pass. The theoretical analysis rests on a new Matrix Bernstein inequality for geometrically β-mixing PPP windows (Theorem C.9), which yields non-asymptotic bounds simultaneously controlling basis-truncation bias and stochastic variance, and establishes that the detection delay scales as O(κ^{-2-(p∨q)/γ}) where κ is the L2 change size.

## Results

In the 3D simulation (100 replications, change point at b=1200), the proposed Matrix detector achieves an average detection delay (ADD) of 9.19 (SD 3.80), substantially outperforming the MMD detector (ADD 40.39, SD 29.79) and the KIE detector (ADD 43.98, SD 34.63) while maintaining a comparable false alarm rate (6% vs. 5% and 6%). In the 4D setting, the Matrix detector again leads with ADD 13.44 (SD 5.21) versus 20.83 (MMD) and 31.50 (KIE), with the highest correct detection rate (97%) and zero missed alarms. On the real Oklahoma earthquake dataset, the Matrix detector correctly identifies the change in June 2009 (consistent with USGS reports of increased seismicity linked to wastewater injection), whereas the KIE detector alarms prematurely in July 2007 and the MMD detector fails to alarm by the end of the data in December 2018.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: the MMD detector (Li et al., 2015), which uses kernel mean embeddings and a blockwise maximum mean discrepancy statistic, and the KIE detector (Madrid Padilla et al., 2023), which estimates pre- and post-change intensities via a kernel intensity estimator and forms a CUSUM statistic in L2-norm. The proposed Matrix detector decisively outperforms both on detection delay: in the 3D setting it achieves ADD 9.19 versus 40.39 (MMD) and 43.98 (KIE), a roughly 4–5× speedup, while in the 4D setting it achieves ADD 13.44 versus 20.83 (MMD) and 31.50 (KIE). False alarm rates are comparable across all three methods (3–6%), so the delay advantage comes without sacrificing specificity. The Matrix detector also achieves the highest correct detection rate (94% in 3D, 97% in 4D) and the only zero missed-alarm rate. On the real Oklahoma earthquake data, the Matrix detector's June 2009 alarm aligns with the documented onset of induced seismicity, while the KIE detector alarms two years too early (July 2007) and the MMD detector never alarms within the observation window.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online change point detection literature (kernel CUSUM, MMD-based, and e-value methods) to the specific and previously unaddressed setting of multivariate inhomogeneous Poisson point process intensities under geometric β-mixing temporal dependence. It builds directly on the Matrix Bernstein inequality for dependent random matrices of Banna et al. (2016) and the low-rank tensor decomposition framework for Poisson intensity estimation of Xu et al. (2025), combining them into a new concentration inequality (Theorem C.9) tailored to β-mixing PPP windows. The method departs from prior point-process change detection work (Wang et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2023, Zhao et al. 2024), which is either parametric (Hawkes networks), offline, or assumes temporal independence, by providing the first nonparametric, online, multivariate PPP-intensity detector with finite-sample guarantees under realistic dependence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first general-purpose method with provable false-alarm and detection-delay guarantees for nonparametric changes in multivariate PPP intensities under temporal dependence, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify in the literature. The detection delay rate O(κ^{-2-(p∨q)/γ}) is strictly better than the offline density change point rate O(κ^{-2-d/γ}) because p∨q < d, a conceptual result showing that the matrix structure of the intensity representation is advantageous. Open questions include extending to multiple change points, adapting the coordinate partition in a fully data-driven way without the correlation heuristic, and understanding whether the low-rank assumption on intensity differences holds broadly in practice. The Oklahoma earthquake application demonstrates practical relevance but is a single case study.
