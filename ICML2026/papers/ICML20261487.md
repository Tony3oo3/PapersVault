## Problem

Advanced network topologies like exponential and 1-peer exponential graphs enable communication-efficient decentralized learning via large spectral gaps, but they have only been evaluated under homogeneous communication delays. In real geographically distributed networks, delays are heterogeneous, and the bottleneck communication delay (BCD) dominates per-round runtime. Node assignment optimization for BCD minimization on these advanced topologies remained unexplored, as the problem is NP-hard and prior solvers covered only ring or tree graphs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a number of nodes n, an n×n symmetric time-invariant communication delay matrix d (e.g., derived from Euclidean distances plus a fixed overhead), and the edge set of a circulant digraph specified by edge generators S^(l) over τ communication rounds. It outputs a node assignment permutation π* that is a 2·min_c g(σ_c S)-approximate solution to the BCD minimization, where g is the maximum skip-sum term. The assignment is computed once before training begins, so the O(n² log n) runtime is negligible relative to total training cost.

## Method

The core insight is that any circulant digraph can be decomposed into a union of directed ring graphs, allowing the BCD to be upper-bounded by a product of two tractable terms: the BTSP objective B(π) (max edge delay on the ring) and the MSR term g(S) (average of max skip widths per round). BTSP-MSR is a two-stage solver: Stage 1 computes a 2-approximate BTSP solution in O(n² log n), and Stage 2 refines it by searching over multiplicative permutations σ_c (coprime to n) in O(n) to minimize the MSR term, exploiting the fact that permuting virtual node labels changes which skip widths appear without altering the underlying topology. Theorem 1 formalizes the bound φ(π∘σ_c; E) ≤ B(π)·g(σ_c S), and Theorem 3 tightens the approximation ratio to 2·min_c √(g(σ_c S)+β) under practical assumptions of Euclidean-distance-based delays and bounded edge-direction correlation along the BTSP tour.

## Results

BTSP-MSR consistently reduces Bottleneck Communication Delay (BCD) across all four circulant digraph topologies (ring, exponential, 1-peer exponential, sparse exponential) for n ∈ [8, 128], with the performance gap over baselines widening as n increases. For the exponential graph with n=47 nodes, BTSP-MSR (1st+2nd) reduces BCD by approximately 20% compared to the greedy solver. On the real-world Ebone NW (n=87), BTSP-MSR achieves the lowest BCD across all circulant digraphs, and when paired with exponential or 1-peer exponential topologies, yields the highest test accuracy on both CIFAR-10 (LeNet-5) and CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18) in decentralized learning, outperforming MATCHA+ which achieves lower BCD but limited spectral gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BTSP-MSR outperforms all four baselines in nearly all tested instances. Against the Random baseline, BTSP-MSR achieves substantially lower BCD across all topologies and node counts. Against the Greedy heuristic, BTSP-MSR (1st+2nd) wins in nearly all cases, with a notable ~20% BCD reduction on the exponential graph at n=47; the greedy solver is particularly susceptible to local optima as n grows. Against SCIP (exact solver with 30-min time limit), BTSP-MSR matches or exceeds solution quality for small n where SCIP finds optima, and decisively outperforms SCIP for larger n where SCIP fails to return any solution within the time limit. Against MATCHA+ (only in the Ebone experiment T2), BTSP-MSR with exponential/1-peer exponential graphs achieves higher final test accuracy on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 despite MATCHA+ having lower raw BCD, because the large spectral gap of these topologies compensates for the slightly higher per-round delay. In the supplementary comparison with Simulated Annealing (Appendix E.2, n ∈ {64,…,128}), BTSP-MSR outperforms SA in most cases, especially at larger n, confirming that exploiting the circulant structure is more effective than generic local-search heuristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the node assignment optimization problem from ring and tree graphs (Marfoq et al., 2020) to the broader class of circulant digraphs, including the exponential and 1-peer exponential graphs that have been shown to offer superior spectral gaps for decentralized learning (Assran et al., 2019; Ying et al., 2021). The key methodological departure is exploiting the structural property that any circulant digraph decomposes into a union of directed ring graphs, enabling a BCD upper bound expressed as a product of a BTSP term (solvable via the classical 2-approximation of Parker & Rardin, 1984) and a novel MSR term minimized through multiplicative permutations. Prior work on advanced topologies for decentralized learning assumed homogeneous communication delays, leaving the heterogeneous-delay node assignment problem for these topologies unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As decentralized learning scales to geographically distributed data centers, heterogeneous communication delays become the dominant bottleneck, and this paper provides the first practical solver (O(n² log n)) for minimizing BCD on the advanced topologies that offer the best spectral-gap-to-degree tradeoff. The combination of BTSP-MSR's BCD reduction with the large spectral gaps of exponential/1-peer exponential graphs enables communication-efficient decentralized learning in realistic settings, as validated on the Ebone network. Key limitations include the assumption of static, symmetric delays, restriction to circulant digraphs, and the use of only multiplicative permutations (which may be suboptimal for non-square-free n). Open questions include joint optimization of topology and assignment (a nonlinear combinatorial problem), handling asymmetric or time-varying delays, and extending to broader classes of structure-preserving permutations.
