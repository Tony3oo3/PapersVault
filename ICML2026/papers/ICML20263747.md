## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for Vision-Language Models treat Mixture-of-Experts architectures as monolithic dense structures, ignoring two critical forms of heterogeneity: the large sensitivity gap between vision and text tokens (text gradients are ~22× larger) and the non-uniform activation of experts (a small subset of 'hot' experts dominates inference). This uniform treatment causes severe accuracy degradation under aggressive low-bit quantization, particularly on reasoning-intensive multimodal tasks.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on MoE VLMs (Kimi-VL-Instruct, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct) that accept an RGB image paired with a text prompt and generate a text response. During calibration, the method consumes a small set of image-text pairs (e.g., 128 COCO samples) to compute per-expert routing frequencies, router affinity logits, and gradient magnitudes for both modalities. The output is a weight-only quantized model (W3A16 or W4A16) that preserves the original inference interface while reducing memory footprint.

## Method

VEQ introduces two complementary mechanisms tailored to MoE VLMs. First, VEQ-ME (Modality-Expert-Aware Quantization) computes a per-expert importance score S_i = γ·N_i^text + β·N_i^vis, where β normalizes the vision-to-text token count ratio and γ scales by the gradient magnitude ratio, then reweights the AWQ-style grid-search reconstruction loss so that quantization error is preferentially minimized in high-impact experts. Second, VEQ-MA (Modality-Affinity-Aware Quantization) modifies GPTQ's Hessian computation by scaling each token's contribution by its router affinity p_j and a modality indicator α_j (γ for text, 1 for vision), yielding an enhanced Hessian H̃ = XCX^T that directs the inverse-Hessian weight update to preserve accuracy on high-affinity, information-dense text tokens. Together, these components ensure that both the expert-level optimization target and the token-level second-order calibration adapt to the structural sparsity and cross-modal sensitivity of MoE VLMs.

## Results

Under the W3A16 setting, VEQ-MA achieves an average accuracy of 65.41% on Kimi-VL-Instruct (+2.04% over the best baseline AWQ at 63.37%) and 67.14% on Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct (+3.09% over the best baseline GPTQ at 64.16%). Under W4A16, VEQ-MA reaches 73.57% on Kimi-VL (+0.61% over GPTQ) and 74.36% on Qwen3-VL (+1.16% over GPTQ), recovering approximately 98.4% and 94.2% of the respective BF16 averages. The gains are most pronounced on reasoning-intensive benchmarks (MMMU, ScienceQA) and OCR tasks (TextVQA, InfoVQA) under 3-bit compression, where uniform quantization assumptions break down most severely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VEQ-MA outperforms all three baselines (AWQ, GPTQ, MBQ) across both models and both bit-widths. Under W3 on Kimi-VL, VEQ-MA (65.41% avg) beats AWQ (63.37%) by 2.04%, GPTQ (62.33%) by 3.08%, and MBQ (62.93%) by 2.48%. The advantage is even more dramatic on Qwen3-VL under W3, where VEQ-MA (67.14%) surpasses GPTQ (64.16%) by 3.09%, AWQ (62.22%) by 4.92%, and MBQ (60.97%) by 6.17%. Under W4, the margins narrow but remain consistent: VEQ-MA leads GPTQ by 0.61% on Kimi-VL and 1.16% on Qwen3-VL, while beating AWQ and MBQ by 0.94–2.29% and 1.11–1.89% respectively. The largest per-benchmark gains appear on TextVQA (e.g., 78.30% vs. 74.04% for AWQ on Kimi-VL W3) and ScienceQA (84.85% vs. 81.84% for AWQ), confirming that modality-aware and expert-aware weighting is most beneficial when bit-budget is tight and reasoning/OCR demands are high.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: VLM quantization (MBQ, VLMQ, Q-VLM) that addresses cross-modal distribution heterogeneity, and MoE LLM quantization (MoEQuant, MxMoE) that addresses expert sparsity and load imbalance. Prior VLM quantization methods treat the model as a dense structure and ignore expert-level routing, while MoE quantization methods for LLMs do not account for the distinct statistical properties of vision versus text tokens. VEQ departs from both by jointly incorporating expert activation frequency (via a weighted reconstruction loss, extending AWQ's grid search) and token-expert affinity with modality sensitivity (via an enhanced Hessian, extending GPTQ's second-order calibration), making it the first framework to simultaneously model both forms of heterogeneity in MoE VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE architectures become the default for frontier VLMs (Kimi-VL, Qwen3-VL, DeepSeek-VL2, ERNIE-4.5-VL), effective compression of these models is critical for practical deployment on resource-constrained hardware. The 2–3% average accuracy gains at W3 over prior SOTA are practically meaningful for low-bit inference, and the dual-awareness principle (weighting quantization error by expert importance and modality sensitivity) is a generalizable design insight applicable to future MoE multimodal models. Open questions include whether the gradient-based sensitivity metric (γ) remains stable across different calibration datasets and model scales, whether the approach extends to activation quantization (WxAy), and how it performs on MoE VLMs with different routing mechanisms (e.g., top-k vs. top-p).
