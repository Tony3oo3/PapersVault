## Problem

In online bilateral trade, the Global Budget Balance (GBB) benchmark—requiring only non-negative cumulative profit over the horizon—can outperform the best Weak Budget Balance (WBB) fixed-price mechanism by a factor of two, meaning all existing WBB-focused algorithms suffer linear regret against the GBB optimum. While Bernasconi et al. (2024) achieved Õ(T^{3/4}) regret against the best SBB fixed price using GBB strategies, no algorithm was known to achieve sublinear regret against the stronger GBB distribution benchmark in stochastic environments under one-bit feedback.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the learner posts a price pair (p_t, q_t) ∈ [0,1]² to the seller and buyer respectively, and receives only a single bit indicating whether the trade occurred (i.e., whether s_t ≤ p_t and b_t ≥ q_t simultaneously). The algorithm is parameterized by the time horizon T and a confidence parameter δ, and must guarantee that the cumulative profit ∑(q_t − p_t)·I(trade) ≥ 0 over all T rounds. The valuations (s_t, b_t) are i.i.d. draws from a fixed joint distribution P on [0,1]² with bounded density σ, and the algorithm's output is a sequence of price pairs whose distribution converges to an optimal GBB-feasible distribution over the K×K grid.

## Method

The algorithm operates in three phases over a uniform K×K price grid G_K. Phase 1 (Profit-Max) runs Exp3 over an additive-multiplicative grid F_K to accumulate a profit buffer β = Õ(T^{3/4}), ensuring later phases can temporarily violate per-round budget balance. Phase 2 (Simultaneous-Exploration) exploits the decomposition GFT(p,q) = L(p,q) + R(p,q) + PRO(p,q) to estimate the two non-bandit components L and R for all K² grid points using only 2K price pairs: by posting (U_t, q) with U_t uniform, a single observed trade bit yields unbiased estimates of L(·,q) for all p simultaneously via the indicator I(U_t ≤ p). Phase 3 (Explore-Exploit) formulates the remaining problem as a constrained bandit optimization: at each round it solves a linear program over Δ(G_K) maximizing the optimistic biased reward r_t(p,q) = R̄(p,q) + L̄(p,q) + PRŌ_t(p,q) subject to the optimistic GBB constraint ∑γ(p,q)·PRŌ_t(p,q) ≥ 0, where PRŌ_t uses a UCB confidence bonus. This yields an Õ(T^{3/4}) regret bound with K = Θ(T^{1/4}) and N = Θ(T^{1/2}) exploration samples, matching the known Ω(T^{3/4}) lower bound for the more restrictive SBB class.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 4.1) shows that under bounded density of the joint valuation distribution, Algorithm 1 achieves $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{3/4})$ regret against the best GBB fixed distribution over price pairs, using only one-bit feedback. This rate is tight, matching the known $\Omega(T^{3/4})$ lower bound for the more restrictive SBB benchmark (Chen et al., 2025). A complementary lower bound (Theorem 3.1) establishes that without the bounded density assumption, any algorithm must suffer linear regret $\mathbb{E}[R_T] \geq \Omega(T)$, even under two-bit feedback. The algorithm's three-phase structure (profit collection, simultaneous exploration, constrained bandit optimization) requires $K = \Theta(T^{1/4})$ grid points and $N = \Theta(T^{1/2})$ exploration samples per grid row.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is against the GBB benchmark introduced by Bernasconi et al. (2024), which can outperform the best WBB/SBB fixed-price mechanism by a multiplicative factor of 2. All existing WBB-focused algorithms (including those of Cesa-Bianchi et al., 2021; Azar et al., 2022; Bernasconi et al., 2024) suffer linear regret when evaluated against this stronger GBB optimum. Bernasconi et al. (2024) achieve $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{3/4})$ regret, but only against the weaker SBB fixed-price benchmark, effectively reducing the problem to one dimension. This paper is the first to achieve sublinear regret against the full two-dimensional GBB distribution benchmark, while maintaining the same $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{3/4})$ rate. The key technical distinction is that the exploration phase estimates GFT over the full $K \times K$ grid using only $2K$ price pairs (rather than $K^2$), and the exploitation phase handles unknown feasibility constraints via optimistic estimation, both of which are absent in the prior SBB-focused work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GBB framework introduced by Bernasconi et al. (2024), who used global budget balance as a smoothing device to achieve Õ(T^{3/4}) regret against the best SBB fixed-price mechanism in adversarial settings. The key departure is that Bernasconi et al. reduce the problem to a one-dimensional set of nearly budget-balanced prices, whereas this paper must compete against the inherently two-dimensional benchmark of the best distribution over all K×K price pairs. The algorithm reuses Bernasconi et al.'s additive-multiplicative grid and profit-maximization phase, but introduces a novel simultaneous-exploration procedure that exploits a GFT decomposition to estimate K² quantities using only 2K samples, and a UCB-based constrained bandit optimizer for the final exploitation phase.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that the 1D SBB problem and the 2D GBB problem achieve the same Õ(T^{3/4}) regret rate—resolves a natural question about whether the richer GBB benchmark fundamentally increases learning difficulty. The bounded-density requirement (absent in SBB) is a new and necessary condition, revealing that the non-continuity of profit for non-budget-balanced price pairs creates a genuine hardness barrier. Open questions include whether sublinear regret against GBB is achievable in adversarial environments (shown impossible by Bernasconi et al.), whether bounded density can be replaced by weaker regularity assumptions, and how the approach extends to multiple buyers or contextual settings.
