## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) relies on verifiers that are often imperfect in practice—unit tests are incomplete, checkers are flaky, and LLM judges are biased. While it is known that noisy feedback can compound across policy-gradient updates, no clear criterion existed for distinguishing when such noise merely slows convergence from when it reverses the learning direction entirely, leading to systematic anti-learning and mode collapse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263797/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a binary verifier characterized by its true-positive rate (TPR) and false-positive rate (FPR), and produces a trained LLM policy represented as a probability distribution over coarse-grained reasoning modes (solution templates). The policy is updated via GRPO-style group normalization over G rollouts per prompt, with the structural constraint that verifier noise is block-symmetric: all correct modes share the same TPR and all incorrect modes share the same FPR. The output dynamics are tracked on the probability simplex, where the scalar bad-mass p(t) and within-block compositions y, z capture the full mode-probability evolution beyond aggregate accuracy.

## Method

The paper models GRPO-style RLVR as a multi-armed bandit over recurring reasoning modes and derives mean-field ODEs for the probability simplex dynamics. The key result is that the bad-mass evolution decouples from within-block dynamics and obeys ṗ = −Jp²(1−p)²ψ(p) where J = TPR − FPR (Youden's index) and ψ > 0 on (0,1), establishing a sharp phase transition at J = 0: positive J drives incorrect modes to extinction, zero J yields neutral drift, and negative J causes anti-learning collapse. The multi-mode extension reveals that GRPO induces a replicator/natural-gradient flow under the Shahshahani/Fisher metric, with within-good modes concentrating (diversity collapse) while within-bad modes spread toward uniformity. Empirically, the J = 0 boundary is reproduced on Python code generation (Qwen2.5-3B, GRPO, OpenR1) and confirmed on mathematical reasoning (PPO, DeepSeek-LLM-7B, GSM8K), showing the transition is robust across algorithms and task domains.

## Results

On OpenR1 code generation with Qwen2.5-3B (GRPO, G=8, 2 epochs), the predicted J=0 phase transition is clearly reproduced: at J=1.0 (noise-free), pass@1 reaches 20.8% (+8.0% over base); at J=0.0, performance is nearly flat at 13.4% (+0.6%); and at J=−0.1, pass@1 collapses to 0.16% (−12.6%). At fixed J=0.3, high-FPR noise (FPR=0.70, δFN=0) yields 14.6% versus 16.0% for high-δFN noise (FPR=0.00, δFN=0.70), confirming the asymmetric-variance prediction. PPO on GSM8K with DeepSeek-LLM-7B-chat shows the same qualitative transition: positive-J runs rise from ~24% to ~63–64%, near-zero-J stays flat at ~21–22%, and negative-J collapses to ~1–3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing RLVR methods; instead, its empirical 'comparisons' are across noise regimes (different J values) to validate the theoretical phase transition. The key finding is that the J=0 boundary is sharp: all J>0 settings improve over the base model (by 1.8–8.0 percentage points), J≈0 is neutral (+0.6%), and J<0 causes catastrophic degradation (−12.6%). Within the J>0 regime, larger J consistently yields faster and larger gains, supporting the 'rate, not fate' claim. The asymmetric-noise comparison at fixed J=0.3 (high-FPR vs. high-δFN) shows that false positives are more damaging than false negatives at equal J, matching the variance-geometry prediction. Finally, the PPO-on-GSM8K experiment serves as a cross-algorithm comparison: despite PPO's token-level credit assignment (unlike GRPO's per-completion abstraction), the same sign-of-J qualitative behavior emerges, indicating the phase transition is a robust property of RLVR rather than an artifact of GRPO's group normalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and REINFORCE-style policy gradients (Williams, 1992) by recasting per-prompt RLVR updates as a multi-armed bandit over coarse-grained reasoning modes, then deriving mean-field ODEs on the probability simplex. It departs from prior noisy-reward analyses in LLM alignment (Chen et al., 2025; Cai et al., 2025), which characterized degradation qualitatively, by identifying a single scalar—Youden's index J = TPR − FPR—as the exact phase-transition threshold separating learning from anti-learning under group normalization. The geometric connection to replicator dynamics and Shahshahani/Fisher natural-gradient flow (Shahshahani, 1979; Amari et al., 2019) is borrowed from evolutionary game theory and information geometry, but its application to diagnose RLVR stability is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The J = 0 threshold gives practitioners a concrete, estimable diagnostic (compute TPR and FPR on a small audited split) to detect when a verifier is too noisy to support RLVR, and the 'rate, not fate' result reassures that any J > 0 verifier will eventually learn, just more slowly. Key limitations include the per-prompt mean-field approximation (ignoring parameter sharing across prompts), the binary-reward assumption, synthetic noise injection, and short training horizons; whether the threshold holds under mode-dependent verifiers, graded LLM judges, and longer non-stationary training remains open. The framework also opens a design path: by viewing GRPO as a controllable vector field on the simplex, one can engineer advantage-shaping or regularization terms to prevent within-good diversity collapse while preserving bad-mass decay.
