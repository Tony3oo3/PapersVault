## Problem

Current neural full-reference image quality assessment (FR IQA) models face two critical limitations: slow inference that precludes real-time deployment, and high vulnerability to imperceptible adversarial perturbations that can manipulate quality scores in safety-critical applications. No existing FR IQA metric simultaneously achieves state-of-the-art accuracy, real-time throughput, and strong adversarial resilience.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261418/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BiRQA takes a reference–distorted RGB image pair at original resolution (no cropping) and computes four lightweight analytic feature maps—SSIM, Informational Map, YCbCr Color Difference, and Local Binary Patterns—organized into a four-level pyramid (each level downsampled by 2×). The output is a single scalar quality score produced by a Reliability-Aware Head that forms a softmax-weighted convex combination of per-scale contributions, making each scale's influence explicitly interpretable.

## Method

BiRQA injects the four analytic features into a bidirectional multiscale pyramid where a Cross-Scale Residual Attention Module (CSRAM) lifts fine-scale cues upward via uncertainty-aware gated residuals (separately learned strength α and confidence ρ), while a Spatial Cross-Gating Block (SCGB) routes coarse semantic context back downward, creating a two-way information highway absent in prior top-down-only designs. A Reliability-Aware Head pools each scale with learnable GeM and aggregates via softmax-normalized confidence weights. For robustness, the paper introduces Anchored Adversarial Training (AAT): within each mini-batch, a subset of clean samples serves as anchors, and a max-near-anchored ranking loss penalizes the worst per-sample ranking violations relative to those anchors, yielding a provable pointwise error bound E ≤ ε + η + Rδ (Theorem 1) without requiring label rescaling or dataset-specific calibration.

## Results

On five standard FR-IQA benchmarks, BiRQA achieves SROCC of 0.988 (LIVE), 0.979 (CSIQ), 0.959 (TID2013), 0.845 (PieAPP), and 0.822 (PIPAL), matching or surpassing the previous SOTA (TOPIQ) on all five. The robust variant AAT-BiRQA retains SROCC of 0.978/0.974/0.951/0.831/0.809 on the same datasets, sacrificing at most ~0.01 SROCC. Under four unseen white-box attacks on KADID-10k, AAT-BiRQA lifts SROCC from 0.30–0.57 (base) to 0.60–0.84, and improves IR-Score by up to 12% over prior defenses. BiRQA runs at ~15 FPS on 1920×1080 images, approximately 3× faster than TOPIQ, with only 5.5M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiRQA outperforms the previous SOTA TOPIQ on all five benchmarks in SROCC (e.g., +0.004 on LIVE, +0.001 on CSIQ, +0.005 on TID2013, +0.004 on PieAPP, +0.009 on PIPAL), with the largest gap on PIPAL where the 23k-image scale amplifies small per-sample differences. Against AHIQ, BiRQA wins on four of five datasets but loses narrowly on TID2013 (0.959 vs. 0.962 SROCC), which the authors attribute to underrepresented distortion types (radial geometry, chromatic aberration). Compared to LPIPS and DISTS, BiRQA leads by 0.016–0.227 SROCC depending on dataset, with the largest margins on PIPAL (+0.227 over LPIPS). The robust AAT-BiRQA variant drops only 0.007 SROCC on PIPAL relative to vanilla BiRQA (confirmed by paired bootstrap, 95% CI excludes zero), while under FGSM/C&W/AutoAttack/FACPA attacks it improves SROCC by 0.26–0.31 over the undefended model and by 0.02–0.06 over the strongest prior defense (AT with label smoothing). In cross-dataset transfer (trained on KADID-10k or PIPAL, tested on LIVE/CSIQ/TID2013), BiRQA exceeds TOPIQ in 9 of 12 experiments, confirming strong generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiRQA extends the multiscale IQA paradigm established by TOPIQ (top-down cross-self attention) and IQT (coarse-to-fine flow) by introducing genuinely bidirectional cross-scale communication: a bottom-up CSRAM with uncertainty-aware gating and a top-down SCGB, a combination not previously explored in FR IQA. For adversarial robustness, it builds on Chistyakova et al. (2024) adversarial training for IQA but departs by replacing direct label penalization—which suffers from label-shift under perturbation—with an anchored ranking loss that ties perturbed samples to clean in-batch anchors, yielding a provable pointwise error bound. Architecturally, it positions itself between classical analytic metrics (SSIM, LBP, VIF) and heavy transformer backbones by injecting four lightweight perceptual feature maps into a compact CNN-attention hybrid, avoiding the raw-image inputs that dominate LPIPS, DISTS, and TOPIQ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical trilemma—accuracy, real-time throughput, and adversarial resilience—that prior FR IQA methods have only solved pairwise, making BiRQA relevant for safety-critical deployments (medical imaging, autonomous driving, content authentication) where quality scores must remain trustworthy under attack. The theoretical pointwise-error bound (Theorem 1) is a rare formal guarantee in the IQA literature and provides a principled stopping criterion for adversarial fine-tuning. Limitations include weaker performance on legacy distortion types (radial geometry, chromatic aberration in TID2013), the additional training time for AAT (~2.5× vanilla), and the fact that the bound's tightness depends on batch construction heuristics rather than dataset-specific calibration. Open questions include whether the anchored ranking loss transfers to NR-IQA or video IQA, and whether the analytic-feature-plus-lightweight-CNN paradigm can scale to more diverse distortion taxonomies without the accuracy ceiling observed on PieAPP.
