## Problem

Existing joint audio-video generation models treat human-centric tasks—reference-based generation (R2AV), video editing (RV2AV), and audio-driven animation (RA2V)—as isolated objectives with separate architectures, and no unified framework exists in the audio-video domain. Moreover, multi-person scenarios suffer from pervasive identity-timbre binding failures and speaker confusion that remain unresolved in prior work.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt, a set of reference identity images, and corresponding reference voice timbres as core inputs, with an optional source video (for editing) or driving audio (for animation) that toggles the operating mode. It outputs a synchronized video-audio pair up to 10 seconds in length. A key structural detail is that the text prompt is processed through an MLLM into a Structured Caption using anchor tokens (e.g., <sub_k>) that explicitly bind each reference identity to its visual attributes, motion, and speech content across separate video, audio, and joint caption fields.

## Method

DreamID-Omni builds on a dual-stream DiT (initialized from Ovi) where video and audio backbones interact via bidirectional cross-attention. The Symmetric Conditional DiT injects reference identity/timbre features by concatenation to the noisy latents (identity channel) and structural conditions (source video or driving audio) by element-wise addition (structural channel), so nullifying the additive term seamlessly switches between R2AV, RV2AV, and RA2V without architectural changes. To resolve multi-person confusion, a Dual-Level Disentanglement strategy operates at two levels: Syn-RoPE assigns each identity a non-overlapping temporal positional segment in the RoPE attention space (with a margin M=150) to rigidly bind visual and acoustic features of the same person while suppressing cross-identity attention, and Structured Captions provide explicit semantic grounding via anchor tokens. Training follows a three-stage progressive curriculum—In-pair Reconstruction, Cross-pair Disentanglement, then Omni-Task Fine-tuning—where the weakly-constrained R2AV task is mastered first to build a robust generative prior before strongly-constrained editing and animation tasks are introduced, preventing overfitting to the easier conditional tasks.

## Results

On the proposed IDBench-Omni benchmark (200 samples), DreamID-Omni achieves the best scores on 8 out of 10 metrics for the R2AV task: ID-Sim. 0.674/0.603 (single/multi-person), ViCLIP 13.911, CLAP 0.278, WER 0.052, T-Sim. 0.493/0.402, Sync-C 6.226, Sync-D 7.791, and Spk-Conf. 0.080. The most striking margin is on Speaker Confusion, where DreamID-Omni scores 0.080 versus 0.380 for Wan2.6 and 0.340 for Qwen-Image+LTX-2, a 4–5× reduction. On RV2AV, it leads on AES (0.584), ViCLIP (14.832), and ID-Sim. (0.635) over VACE and HunyuanCustom; on RA2V, it achieves the best AES (0.591), ViCLIP (16.618), ID-Sim. (0.623), and Sync-C (6.325).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest full audio-video baseline Wan2.6 (closed-source), DreamID-Omni wins decisively on identity preservation (ID-Sim. 0.674/0.603 vs. 0.523/0.455), timbre similarity (T-Sim. 0.493/0.402 vs. 0.391/0.217), speech accuracy (WER 0.052 vs. 0.534), and speaker confusion (0.080 vs. 0.380), while trailing slightly on video aesthetics (AES 0.618 vs. 0.632) and audio production quality (PQ 6.290 vs. 6.391). The two cascaded pipelines (Qwen-Image + LTX-2, Qwen-Image + Ovi) are substantially outperformed on every shared metric, with ViCLIP gaps of 5.4 and 4.9 points respectively, reflecting their inability to jointly model audio-video identity. Video-only baselines (Phantom, VACE, HunyuanCustom) are competitive on ID-Sim. in single-person settings but lack any audio capability and degrade sharply in multi-person scenarios (e.g., VACE drops to 0.395 multi-person ID-Sim.). On the RA2V task, DreamID-Omni surpasses Humo on AES (0.591 vs. 0.550), ViCLIP (16.618 vs. 14.859), and Sync-C (6.325 vs. 6.114), while HunyuanCustom is outperformed on all five reported metrics. The user study (30 professional evaluators, 7 dimensions) confirms these quantitative findings, with DreamID-Omni leading on every dimension including Lip-sync (4.50 vs. 3.32 for the best baseline) and Timbre-Sim. (3.50 vs. 2.95).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Ovi dual-stream DiT architecture for joint audio-video generation and extends the DiT backbone with a Symmetric Conditional injection scheme that unifies heterogeneous conditioning signals (reference images, voice timbres, source videos, driving audio) into a shared latent space. It departs from the prevailing task-specific paradigm—where Phantom, VACE, HunyuanCustom, and Humo each handle one of reference-based generation, editing, or animation in isolation—by formulating all three as a single conditional distribution and training them jointly. The Dual-Level Disentanglement strategy (Syn-RoPE for signal-level identity-timbre binding, Structured Captions for semantic-level subject-attribute mapping) is a novel combination that has no direct precedent in prior audio-video or video-only controllable generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DreamID-Omni is the first framework to unify human-centric audio-video generation, editing, and animation under one model, and the accompanying IDBench-Omni benchmark (200 instances across three task splits) provides the community with a standardized evaluation platform that did not previously exist. The demonstrated ability to outperform closed-source commercial models (Wan2.6) on multi-person speaker-confusion and timbre-similarity metrics suggests that explicit identity-timbre binding mechanisms are more critical than previously assumed for practical deployment. Key limitations include the 10-second video cap (constrained by the Syn-RoPE margin) and the three-forward-pass CFG inference cost, leaving long-form generation and efficient inference as open problems for future work.
