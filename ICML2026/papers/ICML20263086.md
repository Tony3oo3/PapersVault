## Problem

Causal matrix completion under multiple discrete treatment levels with MNAR missingness remains largely unexplored, particularly when treatment assignments are highly imbalanced. Existing anchor-based methods like SNN require fully observed anchor submatrices exclusively from the target treatment level, which fails under data-scarce treatments. The paper identifies that under shared latent row factors, imputation coefficients are treatment-invariant, enabling cross-treatment data integration that was previously unexploited.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially observed outcome matrix (m×n) where each entry is observed only under its assigned treatment level, plus a treatment assignment matrix D of the same shape indicating which of l discrete levels (or 0 for unobserved) each entry belongs to. It produces entry-wise estimates of the latent expected potential outcome A_ij^(d) for any target unit-item-treatment triple, accompanied by finite-sample error bounds of order f(d)·K^{-1/2} and asymptotic normality. The estimation is feasibility-gated: an entry is only estimated if a valid mixed anchor submatrix of size at least μ×c can be found, and the feasible rate (fraction of estimable entries) is a key practical metric.

## Method

MSNN relaxes SNN's same-treatment anchor requirement by introducing Mixed Anchor Rows (MAR) and Mixed Anchor Columns (MAC), where the anchor block S_w may pool observations from different treatment levels as long as each column's treatment matches the corresponding row-i observation. The key identification result (Lemma 3.6) shows that under shared latent row factors, the linear imputation coefficient β is invariant across treatments, so it can be estimated from a mixed-treatment block while the target vector x(d) still comes from the target treatment. Scale heterogeneity across treatments is handled via column-wise weights w(b, d(b)) = 1/f(d(b)) that normalize the anchor block before SVD-based coefficient estimation. The theoretical contribution proves an exponential sample-efficiency gain: under MCAR, E[K_MSNN]/E[K_SNN] = [Σ_{d'}(p_{d'}/p_d)^{r+1}]^c, reducing the dependence on the sparse treatment's probability from order rc to order r, with analogous results under MNAR via a multi-variant softmax missingness model.

## Results

On MCAR data with data-scarce treatment levels (p=0.01), MSNN achieves a feasible rate (FR) of 4.69% and mean relative error (MRE) of 3.91×10⁻², compared to SNN's 0.02% FR and 0.806 MRE. At the medium sparsity level (p=0.025), MSNN reaches 63.73% FR with MRE of 1.18×10⁻³, while SNN manages only 1.20% FR and 0.577 MRE. Under MNAR (λ=0.02), MSNN achieves 26.96% FR and 0.129 MRE at the low proportion, versus SNN's 9.57% FR and 0.366 MRE. The theoretical exponential sample-efficiency gain is confirmed empirically: MSNN's effective sample size scales as [Σ(p_d'/p_d)^(r+1)]^c relative to SNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSNN dominates SNN on both feasible rate and estimation error across all sparsity levels: at the lowest MCAR probability (p=0.01), MSNN achieves 4.69% FR versus SNN's 0.02% (a ~235× improvement), and reduces MRE from 0.806 to 0.039 (a ~20× reduction). Against CY (Choi & Yuan, 2024), which reports 100% FR by design (it produces an estimate for every entry via nuclear-norm regularization), MSNN achieves dramatically lower MRE (e.g., 0.039 vs. 0.499 at low sparsity), indicating that CY's estimates, while always produced, are far less accurate. Against TS-NN (Sadhukhan et al., 2025), MSNN achieves higher FR at low and medium sparsity (4.69% vs. 0.92%; 63.73% vs. 19.83%) and substantially lower MRE (0.039 vs. 0.353; 0.0012 vs. 0.508). Under MNAR settings (λ=0.05), SNN and CY both fail or produce high-error estimates, while MSNN maintains 1.6–2.5% FR with MRE around 0.15, and TS-NN produces numerically unstable results (MRE on the order of 10⁷–10⁹). The key takeaway is that MSNN is the only method that simultaneously achieves high feasibility and low error in the data-scarce multi-treatment regime that motivates the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Synthetic Nearest Neighbors (SNN) framework of Agarwal et al. (2023b) from binary to multiple discrete treatment levels, introducing Mixed Anchor Rows and Columns that relax the same-treatment requirement for the anchor block while preserving the target row's treatment. It departs from the Synthetic Interventions framework (Agarwal et al., 2020), which also handles multi-treatment settings but targets time-average effects in panel data rather than entry-wise counterfactual estimation in a general matrix completion setting. Unlike other multi-treatment MNAR methods (CY, TS-NN, KernelNN, DRNN), MSNN exploits the cross-treatment invariance of imputation coefficients under a shared latent row factor assumption, a structural property those methods do not leverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exponential sample efficiency gain for data-scarce treatment levels challenges the conventional wisdom that estimating effects for a rare treatment necessarily requires proportionally more data from that specific treatment. The practical implication is significant for settings like policy evaluation or advertising where some treatment arms are inherently sparse. Key limitations include the reliance on Assumption 3.5 (shared latent row factors across treatments), which, while standard in low-rank causal models, may fail in settings where treatment fundamentally alters unit-level latent structure. Open questions include computational scalability for large anchor sets (the biclique search becomes intractable beyond size ~10), extension to continuous or combinatorial treatments, and whether the shared-row-factor assumption can be relaxed or tested empirically.
