## Problem

Most post-training LLM compression methods require calibration data and degrade severely at extreme (≈3-bit) compression. The paper asks whether a calibration-free weight compression method can maintain acceptable accuracy at such aggressive bit-widths, and whether the reconstruction can be made hardware-efficient by avoiding the floating-point matrix multiplications that prior seed-based methods (e.g., SeedLM) require.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264917/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM's FP16 weight tensors as input and requires no calibration data. For each fixed-size weight block it outputs a single LFSR seed (S bits), an adaptive number of scalar coefficients (k values, quantized to int8 with a shared FP16 scaling factor per group of G blocks), yielding an effective bitwidth of S + 8k + 16/G bits per block. At inference time, the compact seeds and coefficients are loaded into a hardware weight generator that reconstructs full-precision weights on the fly via a single-cycle LFSR lookup followed by multiplier-adder operations, feeding a systolic array for MAC computation.

## Method

S-Quant partitions each weight tensor into fixed-size blocks and approximates each block as a linear combination of K basis vectors deterministically generated by a Linear Feedback Shift Register (LFSR) initialized with a per-block seed. An adaptive basis-selection algorithm (Algorithm 1) iterates over candidate seeds and increments the basis count k until the explained energy ratio R_k exceeds a target threshold R_th, ensuring every block meets the reconstruction-quality target without over-allocating bases to easy blocks. Coefficients are then grouped and quantized to int8 with a shared scaling factor. The four-dimensional configuration space (block size B, seed length S, group size G, threshold R_th) is explored via multi-objective Bayesian optimization using a Gaussian Process surrogate and Expected Incremental Predictive Volume acquisition to find Pareto-optimal accuracy–bitwidth trade-offs. Unlike SeedLM, reconstruction relies only on shift/XOR (LFSR) and scalar multiply-add operations rather than dense floating-point matrix multiplication, enabling a compact ASIC implementation that achieves ~4× latency reduction for memory-bound LLM inference at 7 nm.

## Results

On Llama 2 7B, S-Quant achieves a mean zero-shot accuracy of 67.65 at ~4-bit (3.8 bits) and 64.56 at ~3-bit (2.7 bits), with WikiText-2 perplexity of 5.7 and 6.5 respectively (baseline: 5.5). On Llama 3 70B, S-Quant reaches 78.11 (4-bit) and 74.74 (3-bit) mean accuracy, while QuIP# runs out of memory entirely at both bit widths. The dedicated ASIC accelerator achieves a 4× latency reduction over the uncompressed baseline for memory-bound LLM inference at 7 nm FinFET.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SeedLM (the closest prior seed-based method), S-Quant achieves marginally better mean accuracy at both bit widths (67.65 vs. 67.57 at 4-bit; 64.56 vs. 64.48 at 3-bit on Llama 2 7B), with the gap widening slightly on larger models (78.11 vs. 78.06 at 4-bit on Llama 3 70B). Against AWQ, S-Quant shows a substantial advantage at 3-bit: 64.56 vs. 52.46 on Llama 2 7B and 74.74 vs. 61.35 on Llama 3 70B, while at 4-bit the gap is smaller (67.65 vs. 67.30). QuIP# is outperformed at 3-bit (64.56 vs. 55.85 on Llama 2 7B) and fails entirely (OOM) on 70B models at both bit widths. OmniQuant degrades catastrophically at 3-bit (38.46 on Llama 2 7B; 32.95 on Llama 3 70B), far below S-Quant's 64.56 and 74.74. A critical practical distinction is that S-Quant is calibration-free, whereas AWQ, OmniQuant, and QuIP# all require layer-wise calibration data, making S-Quant directly applicable to pretrained checkpoints without any held-out dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

S-Quant builds directly on SeedLM (Shafipour et al., 2024), which first demonstrated that LLM weight blocks can be reconstructed from pseudo-random generator seeds, but departs from it by replacing floating-point matrix multiplications with an LFSR-based generator that relies only on shift and XOR operations, making reconstruction hardware-friendly. The broader lineage includes weight-only quantization methods (GPTQ, AWQ, QuIP#, OmniQuant) and seed/basis-based compression (PRANC, LoRA, NOLA), but S-Quant is positioned as a calibration-free alternative that avoids the accuracy degradation those methods suffer at extreme compression. The LFSR primitive itself is borrowed from communications and cryptography, repurposed here as a deterministic, pipelined weight generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the combination of calibration-free operation at ~3-bit effective precision with a concrete ASIC design that claims 4× speedup for memory-bound inference, addressing a real deployment bottleneck for multi-chip LLM serving. However, the performance margins over SeedLM are consistently small (often <0.1 points on zero-shot tasks), and the hardware results are simulation-based (7 nm FinFET standard-cell synthesis) rather than silicon-validated, leaving open questions about real-world power and timing behavior. The Bayesian optimization step for hyperparameter search adds pipeline complexity that may limit adoption, and the method has not been extended to activation quantization or fine-tuning scenarios.
