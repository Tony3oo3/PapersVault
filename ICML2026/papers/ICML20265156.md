## Problem

Existing Graph Chain-of-Thought methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: they employ disjoint architectures that isolate LLM semantic reasoning from GNN structural propagation, preventing step-by-step semantic-topological interaction, and they treat CoT as an uninterpretable black box with no geometric grounding for how natural language reasoning drives node representation optimization. This paper asks whether CoT-based graph learning can be reframed through a principled clustering mechanism that simultaneously provides interpretability and tightens the semantic-structural coupling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text-attributed graph where each node carries raw text (title + abstract) rather than numerical features, along with a fixed number of reasoning steps M (default 2) and a neighbor count K (default 5). At each iteration, the LLM (Vicuna-7B) receives a prompt containing the target node's text plus texts of two neighbor subsets—structural neighbors sampled from 1- and 2-hop topology and semantic neighbors retrieved via KNN on the current GNN embeddings—and outputs a single dense paragraph. The final output is the node representation after M modulation steps, fed to a task-specific head producing either a class probability vector (node classification) or an edge-existence probability (link prediction).

## Method

The key theoretical insight (Proposition 4.1) establishes that a Transformer self-attention layer with W_Q = 2I, W_K = I, and a key-dependent bias b_k = −‖μ_k‖² exactly reproduces soft k-means assignment weights, making each attention head an ε-approximation (ε = 0 under this construction) of a clustering step. Guided by this, the Semantic Discriminating Prompt explicitly instructs the LLM to perform k-means Assignment (identify shared aspects and discard low-similarity neighbors) and Update (compress selected neighbors into a single concise paragraph serving as a semantic centroid). A Condition-Net (MLP hypernetwork) takes the concatenation of BERT-encoded structural thoughts and thought-induced thoughts to produce a reasoning matrix P^(t) ∈ R^{|V|×d}, which modulates node features via element-wise product before re-encoding by a frozen 2-layer GCN in the next iteration. Theorem 4.4 proves that this iterative process contracts a label-free semantic-structural misalignment score Δ_t geometrically (Δ_{t+1} ≤ ρΔ_t + ε), providing a formal guarantee that CoT aligns LLM semantics with graph topology across steps.

## Results

Under the Single Focus protocol, KCoT achieves the best accuracy across all four datasets and both tasks: 79.25% on OGBN-Arxiv, 86.39% on OGBN-Products, 95.87% on Pubmed, and 90.63% on Cora for node classification; and 95.14%, 96.70%, 93.08%, and 88.45% for link prediction, respectively. All improvements over the strongest baseline (LLaGA-HO) are statistically significant with p-values ranging from 8.33e-3 to 7.96e-8. The gains are most pronounced on Arxiv (+2.59% NC over LLaGA-HO) and Pubmed link prediction (+3.90% over LLaGA-HO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KCoT outperforms all eight baseline families across every dataset and task under the Single Focus protocol. Against the strongest LLM-based baseline LLaGA-HO, KCoT gains +2.59% on Arxiv NC, +1.72% on Products NC, +0.84% on Pubmed NC, and +1.41% on Cora NC, with link prediction improvements of +0.99% to +3.90%. Compared to GraphGPT (the other CoT-based method), KCoT leads by 4.14% on Arxiv NC and 10.58% on Pubmed LP, demonstrating the advantage of the k-means-grounded prompt design over generic step-by-step reasoning. Traditional GNNs (GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT, SGC, SAGN) trail KCoT by 3–8% on node classification, while the standalone LLM Vicuna-7B performs poorly on classification (49–60%) due to its lack of structural awareness, though it remains competitive on link prediction. NodeFormer, a graph transformer, is outperformed by KCoT on all metrics, with the largest gap on Pubmed LP (93.08% vs. 77.69%). All reported improvements are statistically significant (p < 0.01) via t-tests.

## Relation to Prior Work

KCoT departs from prior Graph CoT methods (GraphCoT, GraphGPT, LLaGA) that employ disjoint architectures separating LLM semantic processing from GNN structural propagation, by unifying both into a single iterative loop where CoT reasoning directly modulates graph representations. Theoretically, it builds on the Condition-Net module from Yu et al. (ICLR 2025) for thought-conditioned feature modulation, but introduces a novel k-means interpretation of Transformer self-attention as the mathematical grounding for why iterative CoT steps refine node embeddings. The Semantic Discriminating Prompt explicitly reformulates k-means assignment and centroid-update steps as LLM reasoning instructions, a design principle absent from prior prompting-based graph methods that treat CoT as an unstructured 'think step by step' black box.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The k-means interpretation provides the first principled geometric explanation for why iterative CoT improves graph representations, moving beyond post-hoc rationalization to a formal contraction theorem on semantic-structural misalignment. However, the theoretical result is an existence claim (a parameterization exists that makes attention equal k-means) rather than evidence that trained LLMs naturally implement this dynamics, and the assumptions (A.6, A.7) are supported primarily by t-SNE visualization rather than rigorous distributional guarantees. Practical adoption is constrained by the per-node LLM inference cost (O(|V|·C_LLM) per iteration), limiting scalability to very large graphs like Products (2.4M nodes). Open questions include whether the k-means framing generalizes to other clustering objectives (e.g., spectral, DBSCAN) and whether the frozen-encoder assumption can be relaxed without breaking the contraction guarantee.
