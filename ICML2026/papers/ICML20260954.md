## Problem

Flow-based policies offer high expressivity for capturing multimodal action distributions in offline RL, but reparameterized gradient-based optimization requires backpropagating through ODE solvers (BPTT), which is computationally expensive and optimization-unstable. Existing work resolves this by distilling the flow into a one-step student policy, which restores stability but fundamentally sacrifices the representational capacity that motivated flow-based policies in the first place. The paper asks whether stable gradient-based policy optimization is possible while retaining the full multi-step flow model.

## Input / Output

The policy takes a continuous environment state s and produces a continuous action a by integrating a state-conditioned vector field v_θ(x, τ, s) from Gaussian noise x_0 over τ ∈ [0,1] using a fixed-step Euler solver (10 steps in main experiments). The intermediate value network V_ω^π additionally takes the flow timestep τ as input (via Fourier embedding) to evaluate latent states x_τ along the generative trajectory. The outer critic Q_φ operates only on terminal (state, action) pairs, while the inner value function operates on (state, intermediate latent, flow time) triples.

## Method

Q-Flow interprets the flow sampler as an inner deterministic MDP whose terminal reward is the outer critic Q(s, a), yielding a flow-consistent intermediate value V^π(s, x_τ, τ) = Q(s, Ψ^π_{1,τ}(x_τ, s)) that is invariant along policy-induced trajectories. This value is learned by regressing V_ω^π at dataset-path intermediate states x_τ = τx_1 + (1−τ)x_0 against the target outer critic Q_{φ̄}(s, x̂_1) evaluated at the terminal state reached by rolling the policy forward from x_τ. Policy optimization then avoids BPTT entirely: the vector field v_θ is trained to match a target v_target = (x_1 − x_0) + (1/λ)∇_{x_τ} V_ω^π(s, x_τ, τ), where the CFM term anchors the policy to dataset support and the value-gradient term steers it toward higher-value actions. The slowly updating target critic Q_{φ̄} stabilizes the nonstationary intermediate value regression, and the guidance coefficient λ trades off value exploitation against behavior adherence.

## Results

On the OGBench suite under the standard setting, Q-Flow achieves an average score of 54.4, outperforming the strongest flow-based baseline FQL (43.8) by 10.6 percentage points and the best Gaussian baseline ReBRAC (31.0) by 23.4 points. The largest gains appear on long-horizon navigation: +31 points over FQL on antmaze-giant (40 vs. 9) and +25 points on humanoidmaze-medium (83 vs. 58). Under the advanced setting (Li & Levine, 2026), Q-Flow achieves 47.5 average, surpassing the strongest baseline QAM-E (44.8) by 2.7 points, though it underperforms on puzzle-4x4-sparse and cube-double where action chunking introduces a noisy latent space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-Flow outperforms all flow-based baselines on average: FQL (43.8), IFQL (30.2), FBRAC (28.6), and FAWAC (16.0), with the most dramatic margin over FBRAC, whose BPTT-based optimization collapses on long-horizon tasks (e.g., 4 vs. 40 on antmaze-giant). Against Gaussian-policy methods (IQL 23.4, ReBRAC 31.0) and diffusion-policy methods (IDQL 23.0, CAC 25.3), Q-Flow leads by 19–31 points on average, confirming that the flow-based parameterization combined with stable gradient matching yields substantial gains over unimodal or diffusion alternatives. In the advanced setting, Q-Flow (47.5) edges out QAM-E (44.8) and QAM (43.1), the two most recent adjoint-matching baselines, but trails IFQL (100) on puzzle-3x3-sparse and QAM-E (36) on puzzle-4x4-sparse, where action chunking inflates the latent space and degrades intermediate value estimation. On D4RL Antmaze, Q-Flow (80.4 average) surpasses FQL (76.2), FBRAC (56.8), and FAWAC (44.6), though it remains below the diffusion-based QGPO (78.3) on some individual tasks. In offline-to-online RL, Q-Flow achieves a +23-point average improvement over FQL during online fine-tuning, while FBRAC fails entirely on complex tasks like puzzle-4x4 due to gradient instability.

## Relation to Prior Work

Q-Flow builds directly on the Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) framework for policy parameterization (Lipman et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2018) and the flow-based actor-critic formulation established by Flow Q-Learning (Park et al., 2025), where CFM serves as a behavior regularizer. It departs from prior flow-based RL methods that either backpropagate through the ODE solver (FBRAC, DQL) causing instability, distill to a one-step policy (FQL) sacrificing expressivity, or query the outer critic at OOD intermediate states (QSM, DAC). The key conceptual departure is the 'flow-consistent value': by exploiting the deterministic nature of flow dynamics, the paper shows that terminal utility is invariant along the policy-induced trajectory, enabling a principled intermediate value function learned via simple regression rather than the contrastive learning required by the closest analogue, CEP (Lu et al., 2023), which operates on stochastic diffusion policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Q-Flow resolves a concrete and well-identified dilemma in flow-based RL—optimization stability versus model expressivity—with a clean theoretical grounding (value invariance along deterministic flows) and strong empirical validation (10.6% average gain over FQL on OGBench, +31% on antmaze-giant). The framework is broadly applicable, plugging into multiple offline RL techniques and extending to offline-to-online adaptation without architectural changes. However, the paper honestly reports that Q-Flow struggles under action chunking in complex manipulation tasks (puzzle-4x4-sparse, cube-double), where the expanding noisy latent space makes accurate intermediate value assignment difficult. The moving-target problem from nonstationary flow dynamics during training remains an open challenge, with the authors suggesting UTD ratio control or flow-aware architectures as future directions.
