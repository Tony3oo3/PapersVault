## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on Video Diffusion Models for Image-to-Video generation fail to suppress temporal motion: image-based methods only introduce superficial artifacts, and video-specific approaches like Vid-Freeze merely reduce attention energy without addressing the underlying spectral structure of motion. The paper identifies that static video corresponds to a rank-1 degeneracy (temporal rank collapse) in the temporal attention matrix, and asks how to reliably induce this collapse through adversarial optimization without falling into vanishing-gradient traps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265640/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (the I2V conditioning input, resized to match each target model's resolution: 576×1024 for SVD, 256×256 for DynamiCrafter and LTX-Video) and produces an adversarial perturbation δ bounded by ε=16/255 in the ℓ∞ norm. The perturbation is optimized via PGD over 50–100 iterations, targeting a specific temporal attention block at a high-noise diffusion timestep (t* = ⌊0.8T⌋), and the resulting adversarial image, when fed to the VDM, yields a near-static video sequence.

## Method

MEDUSA exploits the observation that temporal dynamics in VDMs are governed by the spectral structure of the temporal attention matrix: rich motion requires high-rank attention, while stasis corresponds to rank-1 degeneracy (temporal rank collapse). The attack minimizes the nuclear norm of the temporal attention matrix, which suppresses trailing singular values and drives the matrix toward rank-1, thereby eliminating temporal variation in the generated video. This spectral objective circumvents the vanishing-gradient problem inherent in Hard-Target approaches (which force the attention matrix to match a static template through softmax), because the nuclear norm's subgradient maintains constant magnitude via the sign function even as singular values approach zero. The optimization is performed via PGD on the reference image, targeting intermediate temporal attention blocks (Block 5 for U-Net, Block 15 for DiT) where motion semantics are most densely encoded.

## Results

MEDUSA achieves state-of-the-art motion suppression across three VDM architectures (SVD, DynamiCrafter, LTX-Video). On SVD, it reduces Optical Flow from 2.78 (clean) to 1.22 and Dynamic Degree from 43.20% to 13.72%, while raising Temporal SSIM to 91.47%. On LTX-Video, Dynamic Degree drops to 7.98% and Temporal SSIM reaches 97.33%. Subject Consistency remains competitive (95.27% on SVD, 91.32% on DynamiCrafter, 86.93% on LTX-Video), confirming that motion is suppressed without degrading identity fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEDUSA outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three architectures. The closest competitor, Vid-Freeze (Frobenius-norm minimization), is consistently beaten: on SVD, MEDUSA reduces Dynamic Degree to 13.72% versus Vid-Freeze's 27.36%, and on LTX-Video to 7.98% versus 12.13%. Image-based attacks (PhotoGuard, MFA) fail to suppress motion meaningfully—on DynamiCrafter they actually increase Optical Flow above the clean baseline (4.04 and 1.79 vs. 2.17 clean for PhotoGuard and MFA respectively)—confirming that spatial-only perturbations cannot disrupt temporal priors. I2VGuard, designed for quality degradation rather than motion freezing, also fails to reduce motion (Dynamic Degree 40.19% on SVD vs. 43.20% clean). The ablation in Table 5 further shows that a Hard-Target MSE baseline (directly matching a static attention template) performs nearly identically to the clean baseline (OF 2.33 vs. 2.80 clean), validating the paper's claim that rigid constraints suffer from vanishing gradients through the softmax, whereas the nuclear norm objective avoids this bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEDUSA extends the line of adversarial attacks on video diffusion models initiated by I2VGuard (distribution divergence) and Vid-Freeze (Frobenius norm minimization), but departs fundamentally in its objective: rather than suppressing attention energy or maximizing distributional distance, it targets the spectral structure of the temporal attention matrix via nuclear norm minimization. The theoretical framing draws on classical matrix analysis (Eckart-Young-Mirsky, Perron-Frobenius, nuclear norm as convex rank surrogate) and repurposes these tools for adversarial optimization. The paper also builds on the broader adversarial image-generation literature (PhotoGuard, MFA) to highlight why spatial-only perturbations fail to arrest temporal dynamics in VDMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of temporal rank collapse as the mechanistic cause of video stasis provides a principled lens for understanding motion in VDMs, potentially informing both attack and defense design. However, the practical impact is currently narrow: the attack is evaluated on only three models, targets a single timestep, and shows partial motion recovery under standard input purification defenses (JPEG, Gaussian noise, bit-depth reduction). Open questions include whether multi-timestep or multi-layer targeting improves robustness, whether the spectral insight generalizes to T2V or longer video sequences, and whether defenders can design spectral-aware purification that specifically restores the attention rank.
