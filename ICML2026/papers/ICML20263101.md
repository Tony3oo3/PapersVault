## Problem

Existing samplers for masked diffusion language models select tokens to unmask based on per-token confidence or entropy scores, ignoring spatial interactions among positions revealed in parallel. This causes confidence-based planners to cluster nearby tokens in the same step, effectively collapsing to slow autoregressive behavior and incurring a large entropy gap between the product-of-marginals approximation and the true joint distribution. No prior method provides a deterministic, model-agnostic schedule that explicitly controls token spacing to minimize this gap while reducing denoiser calls from O(B) to O(log B) per block.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially masked text sequence (unmasked prompt followed by a contiguous block of B masked positions), a frozen denoiser model, and a block size B (typically 8–64). It produces a fixed, position-based unmasking schedule computed solely from B and a dilation base a (default a=2), specifying which indices to reveal at each of R = ⌈log_a B⌉ iterations, and the final unmasked text. The schedule is entirely deterministic and independent of model outputs, requiring no additional parameters, planner modules, or retraining. A variant applies the same spacing principle as a post-filter on adaptive samplers (EB/CB), where the gap adapts as the block fills: gap = max(1, ⌊M_rem · g₀ / B⌋).

## Method

DUS partitions each block of B positions into R = ⌈log_a B⌉ iterations with step sizes s_t = ⌊B/a^t⌋, unmasking at iteration t all remaining positions k where (k−1) mod s_t = 0. This creates a coarse-to-fine dilated pattern: early iterations reveal a sparse set of widely spaced tokens, and later iterations fill in finer gaps. Under a fast-mixing variable-length Markov chain assumption, the paper proves that pairwise mutual information between simultaneously revealed tokens decays geometrically with their separation (Lemma 3.6), so the dilated spacing makes the entropy gap Δ(I_t; S_t) between the tractable sum-of-marginals surrogate and the intractable joint conditional entropy arbitrarily small (Lemma 3.5). This justifies parallel (independent) sampling within each iteration as a faithful proxy for the joint objective. The method is purely inference-time and model-agnostic, and the dilated-spacing principle also transfers as a drop-in post-filter that enforces minimum inter-token distance on the candidate sets selected by adaptive entropy- or confidence-bounded samplers, improving their accuracy at matched or modestly higher NFE.

## Results

DUS reduces denoiser calls per block from O(B) to O(log B), yielding up to 5.8× wall-clock speedup over token-by-token MDLM decoding. At matched block size B=16, DUS improves GSM8K accuracy from 51.23% (self-confidence) to 59.51% for LLaDA-Base, and MATH500 from 16.6% to 21.4%; on HumanEval the gain is from 15.85% to 25.61%. At B=32 (6.4× speedup), DUS reaches 49.36% on GSM8K versus 29.04% for self-confidence, a 20-point gap. The dilated-spacing post-filter applied on top of EB-Sampler further boosts HumanEval accuracy by up to +13.4% (24.4→37.8 for LLaDA-Instruct).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the self-confidence planner (the primary MDLM baseline), DUS wins at every block size and on every benchmark, with gains of up to 27% on math (GSM8K B=32: 49.36 vs. 29.04) and 20% on code (HumanEval B=16: 25.61 vs. 15.85 for LLaDA-Base). Against the adaptive EB-Sampler and CB-Sampler, DUS operates at lower NFE (faster) but slightly lower peak accuracy; however, applying DUS's dilated-spacing as a post-filter on EB/CB selections improves their accuracy by up to +13.4% (EB, HumanEval) and +12.2% (CB, HumanEval) at modestly higher NFE, confirming the spacing principle is complementary to score-based selection. Against the AR baseline LLaMA-3-8B, DUS at B=16 on GSM8K (59.51%) actually exceeds LLaMA-3-8B (49.81%) while running 5.6× faster in wall-clock time; on MATH500 and code, DUS remains below the AR baseline. Against Qwen-3-8B (88.55% GSM8K), DUS is substantially below, reflecting the overall maturity gap between diffusion and AR models at this scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the masked diffusion language model (MDLM) framework of Sahoo et al. (2024) and the semi-autoregressive block-wise inference paradigm of LLaDA (Nie et al., 2025b), where a sequence is partitioned into contiguous blocks and each block is denoised over multiple iterations. It departs from the dominant line of denoiser-guided self-planners (confidence, entropy, or margin-based token selection) and from adaptive bounded samplers (EB, CB) by proposing a fully deterministic, model-agnostic position-based schedule: the Dilated Unmasking Scheduler (DUS) fixes in advance which indices to reveal at each step using a logarithmic dilation pattern, independent of any per-token score. The theoretical framing—minimizing an entropy gap between the tractable sum-of-marginals surrogate and the intractable joint conditional entropy under a fast-mixing VLMC assumption—is a new analytical lens for planner design that prior confidence-based or path-planning (P2) methods do not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DUS offers a deterministic, predictable speedup (set by block size B) that is orthogonal to and composable with adaptive samplers and KV-caching, making it a practical drop-in for the growing MDLM ecosystem. The dilated-spacing post-filter, which improves EB and CB samplers by up to ~13% accuracy at modestly higher NFE, suggests the spacing principle is a general design axis for parallel unmasking beyond DUS itself. Key open questions include whether the fast-mixing VLMC assumption is sufficient for domains with strong long-range dependencies (code, constrained generation), whether the approach transfers to continuous or score-matching diffusion LMs, and how DUS interacts with emerging training-time planning objectives (Liu et al., 2025) that co-optimize the denoiser and the schedule.
