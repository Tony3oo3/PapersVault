## Problem

Educational social dynamics (e.g., bullying, peer cooperation) are difficult to study causally because observational methods yield only correlational evidence and controlled experiments are ethically constrained in school settings. Existing LLM-based multi-agent simulations address scalability but suffer from weak psychological grounding and lack mechanisms to quantify latent psychological states (self-esteem, peer pressure) that drive behavior but remain invisible in observable actions.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a theory-grounded scenario package (grounding theory, core constructs, role assignments, validated psychometric scales), agent profiles specifying personality traits, goals, formative memories, and a target SVO orientation (Altruistic/Prosocial/Individualistic/Competitive), and optional user interventions (scenario branching or single-step behavior override). Outputs include natural-language interaction traces, per-step psychological need scores across 13 dimensions on a 0–10 Likert scale, a continuous SVO angle θ ∈ [0, π/2] computed as arctan(S_other/S_self), post-hoc behavioral classifications (cooperation, competition, quasi-variants), parallel simulation branches for counterfactual comparison, and log-to-comic visualizations.

## Method

EduMirror is built on the Concordia simulation backbone with an LLM-driven Game Master that narrates events, enforces rules, and advances time. Each agent uses a value-driven cognitive architecture comprising two coupled systems: an Individual Value System tracking 13 psychological need dimensions (from Maslow's hierarchy and the PERMA model) as 0–10 Likert values with expected baselines, and a Social Value System that computes a continuous SVO orientation angle via arctan of the ratio of clipped other-satisfaction to self-satisfaction signals, regularized toward a profile-specific reference interval. A Value-driven Planner generates candidate actions conditioned on interaction history, unmet-need gaps Δ_t(d), and the SVO angle θ_t, then selects the argmax under a prompt-conditioned LLM comparative evaluation that weighs need-gap reduction, effects on others, and SVO consistency. A dual-track measurement protocol separates observable-behavior scoring (LLM Rater on completed traces) from latent-state probing (LLM Surveyor administering psychometric questionnaires post-hoc to logged internal states), enabling quantitative access to constructs beyond the built-in value variables. The platform supports 20 pre-built scenarios across four themes in eight virtual environments, with an intervention engine for generating parallel branches under matched initial conditions.

## Results

Across 17 educational scenarios, EduMirror achieves the highest average win rate in pairwise LLM-based evaluations, maintaining a consistent advantage over all five baselines. In the kindergarten scalability experiment (5→30 agents), EduMirror scores 4.80, 4.18, and 4.03 (average of Naturalness, Coherence, Plausibility, Developmental Typicality), outperforming the best baseline (BabyAGI at 3.86 with 30 agents) by 0.17 points. In cross-setting generalization (university, family, classroom), EduMirror reaches 4.63–4.71 Naturalness and 4.64–4.82 Human-likeness, exceeding the next-best method (JAG-Concordia at 4.04–4.25 / 4.17–4.25) by roughly 0.5–0.6 points. In a human evaluation of bullying simulations, participants' accuracy in distinguishing real from simulated cases dropped below 30% in six of seven groups, with over 10% selecting 'difficult to distinguish' across all groups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EduMirror outperforms all five baselines across every reported setting and metric. ReAct (iterative reasoning) is consistently the weakest, scoring 2.35–2.93 in the kindergarten scalability test and 3.33–3.95 in cross-setting generalization, trailing EduMirror by 1.1–2.4 points. BabyAGI (task-driven autonomous agent) is second-weakest, with its best score (3.86 at 30 agents) still 0.17 below EduMirror. D2A (desire-driven autonomy), the closest architectural baseline, is outperformed by EduMirror in all settings (e.g., 3.12 vs. 4.03 at 30 agents in kindergarten; 3.80–3.87 vs. 4.52–4.71 in generalization), confirming that the added SVO and structured psychological-need architecture provides a measurable advantage over desire-only modeling. LLMob (context-conditioned) is the most competitive baseline in the 30-agent kindergarten setting (3.83 vs. 4.03) and in classroom Human-likeness (4.333 vs. 4.824), but remains below EduMirror in every cell. JAG-Concordia, evaluated only in the generalization table, achieves the highest baseline scores (4.04–4.25 N, 4.17–4.25 H) yet is still surpassed by EduMirror by 0.4–0.6 points on both metrics across all three scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

EduMirror builds on the Concordia simulation backbone (Vezhnevets et al., 2023) and the D2A desire-driven autonomy framework (Wang et al., 2025b), extending them with an education-specific cognitive architecture grounded in Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs, the PERMA model, and Social Value Orientation theory. It departs from traditional ABM's rigid rule-based agents and from general-purpose LLM agent frameworks (Park et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2024) by explicitly constraining generation within a psychologically informed value system and introducing a dual-track measurement protocol (LLM Rater + LLM Surveyor) that prior social simulation platforms lack. The paper positions itself at the intersection of generative social science (Epstein, 2006) and LLM-based agent simulation, arguing that neither hand-crafted ABM nor unconstrained LLM agents adequately capture the fidelity and measurability required for educational research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EduMirror provides a practical computational laboratory for ethically studying sensitive educational phenomena (bullying, peer conflict, home-school dynamics) and testing counterfactual interventions that would be infeasible or unethical in real classrooms. The dual-track measurement protocol and intervention branching mechanism address a genuine gap in computational social science: the ability to quantify latent psychological states and perform controlled causal comparisons in silico. Key limitations include the small group sizes tested (up to 30 agents), single cultural context, snapshot rather than longitudinal design, and the fact that individual and social value systems remain parallel rather than coupled. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes across cultures, LLMs, and age groups, and whether longitudinal simulations can capture cumulative developmental effects.
