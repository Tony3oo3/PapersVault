## Problem

While first-order VC bounds (e.g., Theorem 1.1) give the best known uniform convergence guarantees for any hypothesis class of VC-dimension d, it was unclear whether these bounds are actually tight for halfspaces. The paper resolves this by showing the answer depends critically on dimension and structure: inhomogeneous halfspaces in d ≥ 2 achieve the full VC first-order rate, but homogeneous halfspaces in R² exhibit a fundamentally different, log-free per-band behavior that the generic VC bound cannot capture.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a distribution D over R^d (realizable) or R^2 × {−1,1} (agnostic), a halfspace hypothesis class, and n i.i.d. labeled samples, and produces a high-probability bound on sup_h |er_D(h) − er_S(h)|. The output is structured as a bandwise guarantee: for homogeneous halfspaces in R², the deviation bound on each dyadic risk band (2^{−i}, 2^{−i+1}] is log-free in the error level, and unioning over bands incurs only a ln ln n additive overhead. Matching lower bounds are provided for both the inhomogeneous (d ≥ 2) and homogeneous (d = 2) settings, establishing tightness.

## Method

For inhomogeneous halfspaces in d ≥ 2, the lower bound constructs a finite support X_{d/2,k} of points arranged on unit circles in orthogonal 2D coordinate subspaces, so that any labeling with at most one −1 per circle is realizable by a halfspace (Lemma 3.1). Anti-concentration via Klein–Young and Paley–Zygmund then shows a constant fraction of circles have a missing (or under-sampled) point, yielding a consistent hypothesis with error Θ(d ln(n/d)/n) or a deviation of Θ(√(er_S · d ln(e/er_S)/n)). For homogeneous halfspaces in R², the key insight is a critical-wedge localization argument: after reducing to angles on the unit circle, all hypotheses in a given dyadic band must agree with a reference halfspace outside a single wedge of measure O(2^{−i}), reducing the uniform deviation to a constant-VC-dimension problem inside the wedge plus a single-hypothesis Bernstein bound outside. This yields a log-free per-band deviation, and a matching lower bound (using nested semicircle shifts on a uniform grid) proves the ln ln n union-bound overhead is unavoidable.

## Results

For inhomogeneous halfspaces in R^d (d ≥ 2), the paper proves the standard first-order VC bound is tight: consistent hypotheses can incur population error Θ(d ln(n/d))/n (Theorem 1.2), and in the agnostic regime the deviation scales as √(τ ln(1/τ)) at true error τ (Theorem 1.3). In sharp contrast, homogeneous halfspaces in R² exhibit a log-free improvement: in the realizable case every consistent hypothesis has error O(ln(2/δ)/n) (Theorem 1.4), and in the agnostic case a bandwise deviation bound of O(√(er_S(h)·ln(1/δ)/n) + ln(1/δ)/n) holds per dyadic risk band (Theorem 1.5). Unioning over dyadic bands incurs only a ln ln n overhead (Corollary 1.6), and a matching lower bound (Theorem 1.7) shows this ln ln n factor is unavoidable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the general first-order VC bound of Li et al. (2001) (Theorem 1.1), which applies to any hypothesis class of VC-dimension d. For inhomogeneous halfspaces in d ≥ 2 dimensions, the paper shows this general bound is exactly tight — no improvement is possible (Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 match Theorem 1.1 term-by-term). For homogeneous halfspaces in R², the paper demonstrates a strict improvement: the realizable-case bound improves from O(d ln(n/d)/n) = O(ln n / n) to O(ln(2/δ)/n), removing the ln n factor entirely. In the agnostic case, the bandwise bound removes the √(ln(e/er_S(h))) factor present in the general VC bound, at the cost of a ln ln n overhead when unioning over all risk levels. The paper further shows (Theorem 1.7) that this ln ln n overhead is provably necessary, establishing that a simultaneous guarantee over all risk levels is strictly more expensive than a single-band guarantee — a phenomenon not present in the general VC setting where the ln(2^i) term absorbs the union-bound cost for free.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the first-order VC uniform convergence framework of Li et al. (2001) and the recent tightness results of Hanneke et al. (2024), which established that general VC-based first-order bounds are achievable for some hypothesis sets. It departs from that line by showing the bounds are indeed tight for inhomogeneous halfspaces in d≥2, while simultaneously revealing that homogeneous halfspaces in R² escape the general VC prediction entirely due to the geometric structure of semicircles on a circle. The critical-wedge localization argument is a new geometric tool specific to this 2D homogeneous setting, and the lower-bound constructions draw on the finite-support design of Zhivotovskiy & Hanneke (2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers a nearly complete fine-grained picture of uniform convergence for halfspaces, exposing sharp dimensional and structural thresholds that pure VC-dimension analysis cannot capture. The first demonstration that a simultaneous guarantee over all risk levels is provably more expensive (by an ln ln n factor) than a single-level guarantee opens a new axis of analysis for uniform convergence theory. Open questions include whether analogous geometric simplifications exist for other hypothesis classes or in higher dimensions, and whether the critical-wedge localization technique generalizes beyond the 2D homogeneous setting.
