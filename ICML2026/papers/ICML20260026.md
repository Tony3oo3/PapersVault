## Problem

The Bad Triangle Transversal (BTT) problem asks for the minimum set of edges to remove from a signed graph so that no triangle with exactly one negative edge remains. Prior work relied on either a slow iterative LP re-solving procedure (Krivelevich, 1995) or standard 3-approximations via maximal matching, leaving open whether simpler 2-approximations exist and how tight the hardness bounds are, especially on complete graphs where BTT is closely related to Correlation Clustering.

## Input / Output

The BTT algorithms take a signed graph G = (V, E⁺, E⁻) together with the optimal fractional solution {x_e} of the covering LP LP_Δ (one constraint per bad triangle) and output a set of edges whose removal eliminates all bad triangles. Algorithm 4 additionally takes a feasible bad triangle cover F ⊆ E and outputs a vertex partition (clustering) with at most (3/2)|F| disagreements. On complete graphs, the LP_Δ can be (1+ε)-approximated in Õ(ε⁻⁷ m^{3/2}) time where m is the number of positive edges, making the full pipeline nearly as fast as the standard 3-approximation.

## Method

The paper contributes two simple 2-approximations for BTT that round the LP_Δ solution in a single pass: a deterministic rule (Algorithm 2) that selects all negative edges with x_e > 0 and positive edges with x_e ≥ 1/2, and a randomized rule (Algorithm 3) that draws r ~ Uniform[0,1] and includes positive edges with x_e ≥ r/2 and negative edges with x_e > 1−r, inspired by Lovász's hypergraph vertex cover algorithm. For hardness, a gap-preserving reduction from Minimum 2CNF Deletion using hexagram (12-node) gadgets and clause edges yields NP-hardness of approximation within 2137/2136 on complete graphs, which also applies to CC, MinSTC, and Cluster Deletion. Finally, a modified pivot procedure (Algorithm 4) transforms any feasible BTT cover F into a clustering with at most (3/2)|F| mistakes in expectation, improving Veldt's 2|F| bound by using inclusion probabilities of 1/4 (positive edges in F) and 3/4 (negative edges in F) instead of the standard 1 or 0, with the analysis charging disagreements to per-edge budgets rather than LP values.

## Results

The paper provides two novel 2-approximations for the Bad Triangle Transversal (BTT) problem (Algorithms 2 and 3), both simpler and faster than the prior Krivelevich (1995) iterative LP approach. On complete graphs, combining a (1+ε)-approximate LP_Δ solver with the randomized rounding yields a (2+ε)-approximation in Õ(ε^{-7} m^{3/2}) time (Theorem 1.1). On the hardness side, BTT is shown NP-hard to approximate within a factor better than 2137/2136 on complete graphs (Theorem 1.2), a gap-preserving reduction from Minimum 2CNF Deletion. Finally, the paper proves OPT_CC ≤ (3/2)·OPT_Δ on complete graphs (Theorem 1.4), improving the prior bound of 2·OPT_Δ by Veldt (2022).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's 2-approximations (Algorithms 2 and 3) match the approximation ratio of the Krivelevich (1995) baseline but are significantly simpler: they require solving LP_Δ only once and use a single rounding step, versus Krivelevich's O(m) sequential LP re-solves. Compared to the standard 3-approximation (maximal edge-disjoint bad triangles) and the 2.4-approximation of Cao et al. (2024b), the new algorithms improve the ratio to 2 (or 2+ε on complete graphs) while running in comparable time. For transforming covers into clusters, Algorithm 4 improves the Veldt (2022) MatchFlipPivot bound from 2|F| to (3/2)|F| mistakes, yielding a (3+ε)-approximation for CC versus the prior 6-approximation. The hardness result of 2137/2136 is new for BTT and also applies to CC, MinSTC, and Cluster Deletion; it is slightly weaker than the 24/23 ratio of Cao et al. (2024a) for CC but uses a deterministic reduction rather than randomized ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions BTT as a bridge between the triangle-covering literature (Krivelevich 1995, Lovász 1975) and the correlation clustering / strong triadic closure literature (Ailon et al. 2008, Sintos & Tsaparas 2014, Veldt 2022). The 2-approximations depart from prior work by directly rounding a single LP_Δ solution rather than iteratively re-solving LPs (Krivelevich) or relying on maximal matching for a 3-approximation. The hardness reduction from Min 2CNF Deletion via hexagram gadgets borrows structural ideas from Charikar et al. (2005) but achieves a far tighter gap (2137/2136 vs. an unspecified APX constant) and simultaneously covers CC, MinSTC, and CD. The 3/2 bound on OPT_CC / OPT_Δ improves Veldt's (2022) factor-of-2 result by refining the pivot inclusion probabilities used to convert covers into clusterings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2137/2136 inapproximability on complete graphs is a strikingly tight hardness result, implying that no algorithm can beat a factor of ~1.0005 unless P=NP, yet the best known upper bound remains 2 — leaving a wide open gap and suggesting a substantially better algorithm likely exists for complete graphs. The open question of whether OPT_CC = OPT_Δ on complete graphs, if resolved, would unify two major clustering formulations and could unlock new approximation strategies for CC via BTT. For practitioners, the simpler 2-approximations (solving one LP and rounding) are more scalable than prior iterative or matching-based approaches, and the (3+ε)-approximation for CC via BTT covers offers a practical alternative to expensive LP_CC solvers.
