## Problem

Representation steering methods can modify LLM behavior by manipulating internal activations, but prior work evaluates them on single target behaviors without measuring how interventions on one safety perspective cascade into unintended changes on others. This paper identifies and quantifies this cross-perspective interference—termed entanglement—across nine distinct safety dimensions, revealing that strong steering on one axis routinely degrades or unpredictably shifts performance on others.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264184/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes text prompts from 18 curated datasets spanning nine safety perspectives (refusal, implicit/explicit bias, intrinsic/extrinsic hallucination, social behaviors, reasoning, epistemic integrity, and normative judgment) and applies steering direction vectors to LLM activations at selected layers and token positions. Outputs are steered model responses scored by LLM judges or substring matching, aggregated into two metrics: Effectiveness (headroom-normalized improvement on the target perspective) and Entanglement (RMSE of absolute drift across all out-of-distribution perspectives, deliberately unnormalized to preserve practical interpretability).

## Method

SteeringSafety provides a modularized framework decomposing steering into three interchangeable components—direction generation (DiffInMeans, PCA, or LAT), direction selection (grid search over layer index and steering coefficient with a KL-divergence filter on Alpaca), and direction application (directional ablation, affine ablation, or activation addition at specified token positions and layers). Five methods (DIM, ACE, CAA, PCA, LAT) are constructed as compositions of these blocks and evaluated on Gemma-2-2B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Qwen-2.5-7B across five steered perspectives (refusal suppression, intrinsic/extrinsic hallucination reduction, implicit/explicit bias reduction). Three direction-selection settings are compared: Standard (KL-filtered), NoKL (maximum raw effectiveness), and Conditional (CAST-style cosine-similarity gating that activates the intervention only when prompt activations exceed a learned threshold). The key empirical finding is that effectiveness and entanglement are jointly determined by the (method, model, perspective) triple, with social behaviors most vulnerable (up to 76% degradation) and reasoning most robust (<2% drift).

## Results

Across Gemma-2-2B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Qwen-2.5-7B, DIM and ACE achieve the strongest refusal-steering effectiveness, exceeding 50% headroom closure on every model except Gemma-2-2B, while bias steering remains below 20% effectiveness for all methods. The effectiveness/entanglement ratio (Table 2) ranges from negative values (indicating net harm) up to 9.68 (ACE on Qwen refusal), with PCA reaching 1.76 on Llama hallucination and LAT reaching 8.37 on Qwen bias. Social behaviors are the most entangled perspective (degradation up to 76%), while reasoning stays robust with entanglement below 2% in every configuration. Conditional steering (CAST gating) consistently reduces entanglement at comparable effectiveness, delivering Pareto improvements on refusal and hallucination but underperforming on bias due to over-triggering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

No single steering method dominates uniformly across all perspectives and models, which is the paper's central empirical finding. On the refusal perspective, ACE and DIM clearly outperform CAA, PCA, and LAT, with ACE achieving the best ratios (5.96–9.68) across all three models, while CAA and PCA yield near-zero or negative ratios. For hallucination steering, PCA is the standout on Llama-3.1-8B (ratio 1.76), and CAA is competitive (0.79), whereas DIM and ACE produce negative ratios on Gemma-2-2B, indicating that mean-difference methods are poorly suited to this perspective. On bias, LAT achieves the highest ratios on Gemma-2-2B (7.05) and Qwen-2.5-7B (8.37), while CAA leads on Llama (4.26), showing that variance-based and token-localized methods各有优势 depending on the model. The paper also shows that PCA and CAA, despite targeting the same extrinsic-hallucination direction in Llama-3.1-8B, entangle with different downstream perspectives (PCA reduces intrinsic hallucination while CAA degrades it), demonstrating that method choice matters beyond raw effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the representation steering literature (RepE/PCA/LAT from Zou et al., 2023; CAA from Panickssery et al., 2023; DIM from Arditi et al., 2024; ACE from Marshall et al., 2024) and the conditional steering mechanism from CAST (Lee et al., 2024), but reframes the evaluation question from single-target effectiveness to multi-perspective entanglement measurement. Prior benchmarks like AxBench (Wu et al., 2025) and EasyEdit2 (Xu et al., 2025) assess steering on isolated behaviors, while this work systematizes cross-behavior interference across nine safety axes using a modular pipeline inspired by the taxonomy in Wehner et al. (2025). The key conceptual departure is treating entanglement as a first-order metric rather than a side effect to be noted anecdotally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that entanglement is determined by the (method, model, perspective) triple rather than any single factor has direct practical implications: practitioners cannot assume a steering direction that works for refusal will have predictable effects on bias or hallucination. The counterintuitive results—jailbreaking not uniformly increasing toxicity, hallucination steering producing opposing political shifts across models—challenge the assumption that safety behaviors are monolithically coupled. Open questions include whether the feature-superposition hypothesis can be causally validated with sparse autoencoders, whether findings generalize to larger or agentic models, and whether adaptive (non-static) steering methods reduce entanglement. The benchmark's scope is limited to English, instruction-tuned models with static vectors, and the analysis is explicitly descriptive rather than mechanistic.
