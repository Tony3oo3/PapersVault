## Problem

Autoregressive next-pixel prediction is a simple, end-to-end, unsupervised framework for unified vision, yet it has been largely abandoned in favor of multi-stage or patch-based approaches without a rigorous understanding of its scaling limits. This paper addresses the unanswered question: how far are we from scaling up raw pixel-level modeling, and is the bottleneck compute or data?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265334/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images as flat pixel sequences in raster order at 16×16, 32×32, or 64×64 resolutions, with each pixel modeled as a categorical variable of size K. For generation-based completion, the top half of the image is provided as a spatial prompt and the model autoregressively predicts the bottom half one pixel at a time; for classification, frozen Transformer layer activations feed a linear 1000-way head evaluated on ImageNet. Training uses JFT-300M with Inception-style random cropping and horizontal flips.

## Method

The paper trains a family of modern Transformers (RMSNorm, 1D RoPE, GEGLU) at five scales (10M–449M parameters) using autoregressive next-pixel prediction with a categorical distribution per pixel, following the IsoFLOP profiles methodology to identify compute-optimal model and data sizes across five FLOP budgets per resolution. Three key empirical findings emerge: (1) raw pixels require 10–20× higher token-to-parameter ratios than language tokens, reflecting the low semantic content per pixel; (2) optimal scaling is task-dependent, with generation-based completion demanding 3–5× faster data growth than classification at fixed 32×32 resolution; and (3) as resolution increases from 16×16 to 64×64, the optimal strategy shifts toward faster model-size growth over data growth, since higher-resolution sequences inherently contain lower-resolution sub-sequences. The authors project that 10²⁴ FLOPs would yield >80% ImageNet accuracy and sub-100 Fréchet Distance, arguing the primary bottleneck is compute rather than data availability, making raw pixel modeling feasible within five years at current 4–5× annual compute growth.

## Results

At 32×32 resolution, raw next-pixel prediction requires 10–20× higher optimal token-to-parameter ratio than language modeling (ratio >400 vs. ~20), with scaling exponents N_opt ∝ C^0.55 and D_opt ∝ C^0.45 for the prediction loss. The scaling predictions are verified with high precision: a model trained at 3.5×10²⁰ FLOPs achieves 46.41% ImageNet top-1 accuracy (projected: 46.39%) and 240 Fréchet Distance for completion (projected: 244). Extrapolating to 10²⁴ FLOPs, the paper projects 84.9% ImageNet accuracy and 92.9 Fréchet Distance at 64×64 resolution, with the primary bottleneck identified as compute rather than data availability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against language-model scaling laws (Hoffmann et al., 2022): at 32×32, raw pixels require 10–20× more tokens per parameter to reach compute-optimal balance compared to text tokens, even beyond 10²¹ FLOPs, confirming that individual pixels carry far less semantic content than words. Against the iGPT architecture (Chen et al., 2020), the authors use comparable 77M and 449M parameter models but with modern additions (RoPE, GEGLU), and find that the optimal parameter scaling exponent is slower than what Henighan et al. (2020) reported for pixel prediction, correcting for the overestimation of parameter scaling. In terms of practical competitiveness, the paper acknowledges that established methods like MAE (4.6×10²⁰ FLOPs) and DINO (2.1×10²⁰ FLOPs) achieve comparable ImageNet performance with significantly less compute than the 10²⁴ FLOPs projected here, making raw pixel modeling currently impractical but feasible within ~5 years given 4–5× annual compute growth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Chinchilla-style IsoFLOP scaling methodology (Hoffmann et al., 2022) to the autoregressive next-pixel prediction paradigm established by iGPT (Chen et al., 2020), updating the architecture with modern components (RoPE, GEGLU, RMSNorm). It deliberately revisits a direction that has been largely abandoned in favor of image tokenization (VQ-VAE, VQGAN) and multi-stage cascaded generation, asking whether raw pixel-level modeling can scale competitively. Unlike prior pixel scaling work (Henighan et al., 2020), which suffered from methodological issues in estimating optimal compute allocation, this study uses proper IsoFLOP profiles and extends the analysis across multiple downstream tasks and resolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous, multi-task scaling law characterization for raw next-pixel prediction, showing that the primary bottleneck is compute rather than data availability—a finding that reframes a direction many consider impractical. The task-dependent optimal scaling (generation requiring 3–5× faster data growth than classification) highlights a concrete tension for unified vision models. However, the analysis is bounded to 16×16–64×64 resolutions and two downstream tasks (ImageNet classification, completion-based Fréchet Distance), and the headline projection to 10²⁴ FLOPs represents a four-order-of-magnitude extrapolation beyond the empirically verified regime (3.5×10²⁰ FLOPs), leaving open whether power-law exponents remain stable at multi-billion-parameter scale and higher resolutions.
