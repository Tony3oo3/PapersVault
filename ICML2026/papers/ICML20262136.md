## Problem

Path-based attribution methods like Integrated Gradients and Guided IG can accumulate spurious gradients when the integration path traverses off-manifold regions of input space. While GIG reduces noise by adaptively updating low-gradient features, its axis-aligned input-space updates are geometrically misaligned with the data manifold's tangent structure, causing intermediate path samples to drift into implausible regions. No prior method simultaneously controls manifold alignment and suppresses gradient noise along the attribution path.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 256×256 RGB image, a zero baseline, a pre-trained classifier (VGG16, ResNet18, or InceptionV1), and a pre-trained VAE encoder-decoder pair (from MAR, Stable Diffusion v1/v2, or Kandinsky 2.1). It outputs a per-pixel attribution map of the same spatial resolution as the input, computed as a discrete path integral over K=200 decoded intermediate samples with endpoint correction to the original input and baseline. The VAE is used only at inference time for path construction; no classifier or VAE parameters are trained or fine-tuned for attribution.

## Method

MA-GIG constructs the GIG-style guided integration path in the VAE latent space rather than input space. At each step, the current latent state is decoded to input space, latent gradients are computed via the chain rule through the decoder Jacobian (∇_z f = J_D^T ∇_x f), and only the lowest-q-quantile latent dimensions are updated toward the target latent code. The key geometric insight is that axis-aligned updates in latent space, when mapped through the decoder Jacobian, produce correlated input-space directions that lie in the manifold's tangent space (under the smooth immersion assumption), unlike axis-aligned input-space updates that generically violate tangent-space constraints. The final attribution is computed as a discrete Riemann sum over the decoded intermediate path with endpoint correction to the true input and baseline, preserving the completeness axiom. This design combines GIG's noise-robust gradient guidance with manifold-aligned path geometry, and the paper provides a formal off-manifold drift proposition (Proposition 3.1) showing why input-space GIG updates necessarily leave the manifold.

## Results

MA-GIG achieves the best DiffID scores across all three benchmarks and all three classifiers (ResNet18, VGG16, InceptionV1). On Oxford-IIIT Pet with ResNet18, MA-GIG reaches a DiffID of 0.4637 and Insertion AUC of 0.5886, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline GIG (DiffID 0.3634, Ins 0.5093). On ImageNet with ResNet18, MA-GIG attains DiffID 0.2707 and Insertion AUC 0.3409, again leading all baselines. The method also achieves the lowest Deletion AUC on ImageNet (0.0702) and Oxford 102 Flower (0.0944) with ResNet18, though GIG occasionally achieves lower Deletion on Oxford Pet with VGG16 (0.0333 vs. 0.0817).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MA-GIG outperforms all seven baselines on DiffID (the primary faithfulness metric) across every dataset–classifier combination. The largest margin is against GIG, the closest competitor: on Oxford-IIIT Pet/ResNet18, MA-GIG improves DiffID from 0.3634 to 0.4637 (+27.6%), and on Oxford 102 Flower/ResNet18 from 0.1891 to 0.2389 (+26.3%). Against the standard IG baseline, gains are even more pronounced (e.g., 0.4637 vs. 0.3790 on Pet, 0.2707 vs. 0.1358 on ImageNet). Latent-space baselines EIG and MIG, which also operate in VAE latent space but use linear or geodesic paths without gradient-guided selection, are consistently outperformed by MA-GIG on both DiffID and Insertion, confirming that the adaptive low-gradient selection in latent space is the key differentiator. The one setting where a baseline edges out MA-GIG is Deletion AUC on Oxford Pet/VGG16, where GIG achieves 0.0333 versus MA-GIG's 0.0817; however, MA-GIG still leads on DiffID and Insertion in that setting. IG² and AGI, which optimize for steeper or adversarial paths in input space, show particularly poor performance on Oxford 102 Flower (DiffID 0.0193 and 0.0136 respectively), highlighting their vulnerability to fine-grained classification tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

MA-GIG extends Guided Integrated Gradients (GIG), which adaptively selects low-gradient features in input space to avoid noisy regions, by relocating this greedy guidance into the latent space of a pre-trained VAE. This departs from prior latent-space attribution methods (EIG, MIG) that use fixed linear or geodesic interpolation and remain agnostic to the classifier's logit surface, and from input-space path methods (IG, IG², AGI) that suffer from manifold deviation. The key conceptual move is showing that axis-aligned updates in a low-dimensional latent space, when decoded through the VAE, induce correlated input-space directions that locally follow the data manifold's tangent structure—something axis-aligned input-space updates cannot achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean geometric diagnosis (Proposition 3.1) of why input-space greedy guidance inevitably drifts off-manifold, and a principled fix that preserves IG's axiomatic guarantees while reducing off-manifold noise. However, the method's effectiveness is tightly coupled to the quality and domain alignment of the underlying VAE—reconstruction fidelity alone does not predict attribution quality, and a VAE trained specifically on ImageNet (MAR) can outperform one trained on LAION-5B (SD2). Open questions include when path-integrated attribution coincides with the desired semantic attribution, how to develop decoder-fidelity diagnostics, and whether the approach extends to non-image modalities or settings where no suitable generative prior exists.
