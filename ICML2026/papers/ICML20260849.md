## Problem

Reward-guided fine-tuning of pre-trained flow models and flow merging (integrating multiple pre-trained models into one) have been addressed via fundamentally distinct formulations and methods. No unified framework existed that could simultaneously adapt flows to maximize downstream rewards while controllably combining information from multiple prior models, nor did principled theoretical guarantees exist for either task in the multi-model setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260849/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes n pre-trained flow models (each defined by a velocity field), a scalar reward function f(x), model-specific divergence weights α_i, and a choice of divergence D_i per prior, and produces a single merged flow model π* whose terminal density p_1* balances reward maximization against divergence constraints to all priors. A key structural constraint is p_0^π = p_0^pre, preserving the source distribution. For the intersection operator, the score diverges at t=1, so the method lifts optimization from the terminal marginal to the entire flow process {p_t^π}_{t∈[0,1]}, using a weight schedule λ_t to control score scale near the endpoint.

## Method

The core contribution is a probability-space optimization problem (Eq. 5) that unifies reward-guided fine-tuning (n=1, forward KL) and pure flow merging (constant reward) as limit cases, with arbitrary divergences yielding intersection (forward KL → product density), union (reverse KL → mixture density), and interpolation (Wasserstein → barycenter) operators. Reward-Guided Flow Merging (RFM) solves this via a mirror descent scheme: at each of K iterations, it computes the first variation δG of the objective at the current density, uses its gradient ∇_x δG as a surrogate reward, and fine-tunes the flow via a standard entropy-regularized control solver (Adjoint Matching). For intersection, a stability-enhancing extension lifts the problem to the space of joint flow processes, replacing the divergent terminal score with weighted score estimates along the entire noised trajectory. The paper proves that stochastic optimal control fine-tuning exactly recovers score information (Theorem 6.1), enabling the mirror-descent interpretation, and establishes almost-sure convergence to a stationary point of G under inexact updates with Robbins-Monro step sizes (Theorem 6.2).

## Results

On the GEOM-Drugs molecular design task, RFM's balanced intersection (RFM-B) achieves a mean total energy of −10.95 ± 0.28 Ha with 74.34% validity, correctly placing density in the overlap region between PRE-1 (−8.09 Ha) and PRE-2 (−14.76 Ha). Reward-guided merging (RFM-RG) further reduces energy to −12.85 ± 0.16 Ha while maintaining 74.02% validity, and unbalanced merging (RFM-UB, α₁=1, α₂=2) shifts to −13.69 ± 0.28 Ha. On conformer generation (ETFlow), RFM-B achieves intermediate energy MAE of 0.3356 kcal/mol between PRE-1 (0.3385) and PRE-2 (0.3175), while RFM-RG reaches 0.3193 kcal/mol, improving upon both priors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline is weight-space averaging (following Model Soups [Wortsman et al., 2022] and Diffusion Soup [Biggs et al., 2024]), evaluated in the illustrative 2D setting (Fig. 9a). Weight-space averaging fails to implement any controlled density-level operation: the averaged model produces a degenerate density that neither approximates the intended intersection nor the union of the two base distributions, whereas RFM correctly recovers both operators. Beyond this direct comparison, RFM is benchmarked against the individual pre-trained models (PRE-1, PRE-2) to verify that merging produces the expected intermediate or improved behavior: RFM-B's energy (−10.95 Ha) falls between the two priors as predicted by the closed-form intersection solution, and RFM-RG surpasses both in energy while retaining comparable validity. The paper also demonstrates that RFM can merge models with different architectures (FlowMol + SemlaFlow), which weight-space averaging cannot do at all since it requires parameter alignment. On conformer generation, RFM-UNION achieves the lowest dipole moment MAE (0.1467 debye) and minimum energy MAE (0.2761 kcal/mol) among all variants, while RFM-RG achieves the best energy MAE (0.3193 kcal/mol), confirming that reward-guided merging improves upon both individual priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously distinct research threads—reward-guided fine-tuning of flow/diffusion models via entropy-regularized optimal control (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2024; Uehara et al., 2024b) and flow model merging (Song et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2025)—under a single probability-space optimization framework using density operators. The algorithmic core, Reward-Guided Flow Merging (RFM), is a mirror descent scheme over the space of probability measures, building on the mirror-flow theory of Hsieh et al. (2019) and Karimi et al. (2024), and on the distributional fine-tuning framework of De Santi et al. (2025a). The key departure from prior work is that both reward-guided adaptation (n=1) and pure merging (constant reward) emerge as limit cases of a single objective, and the first variation of the objective automatically determines the surrogate reward for each fine-tuning step, making the approach operator-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first-of-its-kind convergence guarantees for (reward-guided) flow merging provide a theoretical foundation that was previously absent, and the score-retention theorem (Thm. 6.1) rigorously justifies a structural assumption underlying several prior control-based fine-tuning analyses. Practically, the framework enables task-aware merging of heterogeneous pre-trained models (e.g., combining safety priors with drug-discovery objectives) and supports complex logical compositions via generative circuits. Open questions include scalability to very large models (experiments here use small molecules and 2D settings), whether the convergence guarantees hold tightly in practice with highly approximate solvers, and how to incorporate additional practical constraints like molecular synthesizability into the reward functional.
