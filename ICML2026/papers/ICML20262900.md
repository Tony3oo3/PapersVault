## Problem

Standard RLVR for Large Reasoning Models assigns a uniform advantage to every token in an outcome-correct trajectory, indiscriminately reinforcing both essential deductions and redundant explorations (self-repetition, off-target attempts), which causes the 'overthinking' phenomenon. Existing length-penalty methods operate only at the trajectory level and cannot perform step-level credit assignment to identify which specific reasoning segments are redundant within an otherwise correct chain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262900/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (math or science problem) and produces a concise chain-of-thought reasoning enclosed in  tags followed by a final answer. The reasoning chain is decomposed into discrete steps using predefined delimiters (e.g., double newlines), and during training the model introspectively prunes segments from its own correct trajectories, appending an end-of-thought token to force immediate answer generation from the shortened context. Verification uses K=4 parallel rollouts with a 0.75 correctness threshold to accept pruned candidates.

## Method

ThoughtFold integrates outcome-based GRPO with a fine-grained masked preference objective. An introspective two-phase binary search (tail truncation, then internal folding guided by attention-based step importance scores) identifies redundant segments in correct trajectories, yielding a spectrum of candidate sub-trajectories that form preference pairs. A dynamic mask strategy assigns step-level binary masks per pair: 'Fold Anchors' (steps immediately following pruned segments) are activated in the winner to encourage direct logical bridging, while shared correct steps are masked out to prevent conflicting gradients. The joint objective maximizes J_GRPO + λ·J_MDP, where the masked DPO term (Eq. 9) precisely penalizes redundant explorations and rewards efficient connections between essential reasoning steps, reducing token usage by ~56% while improving accuracy over all baselines.

## Results

Across four reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B/14B, Qwen3-8B/14B) and five benchmarks, ThoughtFold improves overall accuracy by 0.98–2.82% over vanilla models while reducing average token consumption by 39.4–56.1%. The headline result is on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, where ThoughtFold achieves 67.38% overall accuracy (+2.82% over vanilla) with only 4,496 tokens (−56.1%). On Qwen3-8B, it reaches 79.00% overall accuracy with 5,874 tokens, outperforming all baselines on both metrics simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ThoughtFold outperforms all four baselines on both accuracy and token efficiency across all four models. Against standard GRPO, it gains 1.04–2.24% accuracy while reducing tokens by 40.2–56.2% (e.g., Qwen3-8B: 79.00% vs. 77.08% acc, 5,874 vs. 9,932 tokens). Compared to length-penalty methods (RL+Length Penalty, Short-RL), ThoughtFold achieves 1.0–3.3% higher accuracy with fewer tokens, indicating that trajectory-level length penalties sacrifice correctness on hard problems (AIME drops of 3–5% for Short-RL). Against the strongest baseline S-GRPO, ThoughtFold wins on accuracy across all reported benchmarks (e.g., Qwen3-8B AIME 2024: 78.1% vs. 77.3%; MATH-500: 97.4% vs. 95.2%) and on tokens for most benchmarks, though S-GRPO uses slightly fewer tokens on AIME 2024 (8,810 vs. 9,099 for Qwen3-8B). Notably, ThoughtFold also generalizes better out-of-domain, achieving the highest GPQA accuracy across all models (e.g., 57.9% on Qwen3-8B vs. 57.7% for S-GRPO and 55.8% for GRPO).

## Relation to Prior Work

ThoughtFold sits at the intersection of two lines of work: RLVR-based reasoning training (GRPO, DeepSeek-R1) and preference optimization (DPO, Mask-DPO). It departs from prior efficient-reasoning methods (Short-RL, RL+Length Penalty, S-GRPO) that apply trajectory-level length penalties, arguing that such coarse signals cannot perform step-level credit assignment within a correct trajectory. The key conceptual move is an introspective prune-and-verify mechanism that identifies which specific reasoning steps are redundant, combined with a fold-anchor-based dynamic masking strategy that converts this identification into fine-grained preference pairs. This positions the work as a bridge between outcome-based RL (which preserves accuracy) and step-level preference learning (which targets redundancy), rather than a replacement for either.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 56% token reduction while simultaneously improving accuracy across four models and five benchmarks addresses a critical deployment bottleneck for reasoning models, where inference cost scales linearly with CoT length. The introspective prune-and-verify mechanism is attractive because it requires no external reward model, no Monte Carlo rollouts, and only a single attention computation per correct sample, making it scalable. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-verifiable domains (the training data is exclusively mathematical), whether the attention-based importance proxy remains reliable as models grow larger, and whether the joint GRPO+MDPO objective can be further simplified. The geometric analysis (core expansion + tail compression) suggests the method reshapes the reasoning distribution structurally rather than merely truncating it, but this remains to be validated beyond math benchmarks.
