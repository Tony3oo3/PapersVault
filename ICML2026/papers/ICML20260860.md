## Problem

Pretrained Transformers perform in-context learning (ICL) from a few demonstrations, but this ability degrades sharply when the test distribution differs from pretraining. While the attention temperature τ is a known inference-time knob that controls softmax selectivity, its role in ICL under distribution shift has never been systematically analyzed—no prior work derives how τ should be set to recover robustness when inputs, task vectors, or noise levels shift.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of l−1 in-context input-label pairs (x_i, y_i) with x_i ∈ ℝ^d and a query x_l, and produces a scalar prediction ŷ_l. The key additional input is the attention temperature τ, which is not learned but set at inference time; the paper's main output is a closed-form optimal τ_opt expressed in terms of moments of the pre-softmax attention scores (specifically, the ratio of the second moment E[(z_i^T M z_j)²] to the first moment E[z_i^T M z_i]). The analysis operates in the high-dimensional regime where both context length l and input dimension d diverge jointly.

## Method

The paper analyzes a single-layer Transformer with 'approximate softmax attention'—a first-order Taylor expansion of softmax around zero that preserves row-wise normalization and temperature-dependent selectivity while remaining analytically tractable (unlike linear attention, which loses both properties). Using Isserlis' theorem to compute fourth-order Gaussian moments, they derive a closed-form generalization error (Theorem 4.2) that is quadratic in 1/τ, and minimize it to obtain an explicit optimal temperature τ_opt (Theorem 4.3) that depends on the test-time input covariance, task covariance, and noise variance. In a simplified isotropic shift setting, τ_opt reduces to a clean expression proportional to the input variance shift a, and in the large-l limit it equals the ratio of the second to first moments of pre-softmax scores—a 'moment-ratio heuristic' that extends to general softmax settings. Pretrained parameters are configured to emulate the Bayes-optimal ridge estimator, and the theory is validated on synthetic linear regression, GPT-2, and Llama2-7B on SCIQ with noisy in-context demonstrations.

## Results

On synthetic linear regression tasks, applying the theoretically derived optimal temperature (Theorem 4.3) restores the Transformer's ICL performance to near Bayes-optimal levels under input covariance shift (Σ_test = 2Σ_train) and noise shift, whereas the default temperature (τ=1) shows significant degradation. On SCIQ with Llama2-7B under noisy in-context demonstrations, the optimal attention temperature increases monotonically with the noisy ratio — from 0.9 (nearly clean, ratio 0.01) to 1.2 (high noise, ratio 0.99) — and consistently outperforms the default temperature across all tested noise levels and context lengths. The moment-ratio heuristic (Eq. 25), derived from the closed-form optimal temperature, provides a practical inference-time rule that tracks the empirically best temperature without requiring full distributional knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the default attention temperature (τ=1, as used during pretraining) and the theoretically optimal temperature (τ_opt from Theorem 4.3) under various distribution shifts. Under input covariance shift (Σ_test = 2Σ_train), the default-temperature model diverges from the Bayes-optimal ridge estimator, while the optimal-temperature model recovers near-Bayes-optimal generalization error (Figure 2b). Under increasing label noise (σ_test from 0.1 to 10) at fixed l/d=1, the default-temperature model's error grows substantially, but the optimal temperature closes the gap to the Bayes-optimal benchmark (Figure 3b). On Llama2-7B with SCIQ, the optimal temperature (identified via the moment-ratio heuristic) consistently yields higher exact-match accuracy than the default τ=1 across all noisy ratios and context lengths (Figure 4). The paper also contrasts approximate softmax attention with linear attention, showing that linear attention with temperature scaling fails to capture the correct temperature-dependent selectivity (Figure 1), motivating the approximate softmax framework used throughout.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ICL-under-distribution-shift analysis of Zhang et al. (2024), who showed that linear-attention Transformers approximate Bayes-optimal ridge regression, by replacing linear attention with an approximate softmax formulation (from Han et al., 2024) that preserves temperature-dependent selectivity while remaining analytically tractable. The key departure is isolating the attention temperature as the sole inference-time control variable, which linear attention cannot capture. Empirically, the paper connects to a scattered body of work showing that tuning attention temperature helps NLP and CV benchmarks (Lin et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2022; Peng et al., 2024), but none of those studies examine ICL under distribution shift or derive a principled optimal value.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form optimal temperature and the resulting moment-ratio heuristic (optimal τ ≈ E[(z_i^T M z_j)²] / E[z_i^T M z_i]) give practitioners a lightweight, retraining-free inference-time knob to improve ICL robustness when test distributions shift from pretraining. Limitations are notable: the theory assumes a single-layer, MLP-free Transformer with approximate softmax, and the LLM validation uses only SCIQ with a specific label-noise model, so per-layer/per-head temperature selection and broader task coverage remain open. The practical takeaway—that a single scalar adjustment can recover near Bayes-optimal performance under covariance or noise shift—is potentially broadly applicable to deployed LLMs facing distribution drift.
