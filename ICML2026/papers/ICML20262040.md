## Problem

Existing deepfake detectors suffer from a Semantic Masking Effect: dominant high-level semantic features overwhelm subtle low-level artifact cues within entangled representations, causing overconfident yet brittle predictions that fail on unseen manipulation techniques. Even multi-view approaches that combine semantic and artifact cues typically rely on implicit soft constraints or deterministic fusion, lacking geometric rigor to truly decorrelate the views and failing to quantify epistemic uncertainty when the two views produce contradictory evidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262040/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB face images (video frames) as input, processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone with LoRA-adapted attention layers. Rather than outputting a single probability, the model produces a structured evidential report: two subjective opinions (one per view) each decomposed into belief masses for Real/Fake and an epistemic uncertainty scalar, which are then fused via Dempster-Shafer combination into a final belief-uncertainty pair. The fused uncertainty score serves as a calibrated confidence indicator enabling risk-aware rejection of ambiguous samples.

## Method

The framework follows a Divide-and-Conquer strategy. In the Divide phase, a LoRA-tuned CLIP encoder extracts semantic features f_s, which a β-VAE manifold projector reconstructs into f_c; the residual f_r = f_s − f_c is then projected onto the orthogonal complement of f_s (via vector projection theorem) to yield purified artifact features f_a satisfying ⟨f_a, f_s⟩ = 0, explicitly suppressing semantic leakage by geometric construction. In the Conquer phase, each view is mapped through a lightweight evidential head (MLP + Softplus) to Dirichlet concentration parameters, producing Subjective Logic opinions with belief and uncertainty components. The two opinions are fused via the Dempster-Shafer orthogonal sum, which explicitly models inter-view conflict: when the Semantic View and Artifact View disagree, the conflict coefficient elevates epistemic uncertainty rather than forcing a deterministic decision. Training uses a composite loss combining evidential cross-entropy with annealed KL regularization for the Dirichlet heads, plus a cosine-alignment loss and KL divergence term for the β-VAE manifold projector, all optimized with AdamW on FF++ (c23).

## Results

Trained on FaceForensics++ (c23), DiCoME achieves a cross-dataset average AUC of 0.939 and a cross-manipulation (DF40) average AUC of 0.976, outperforming all 12 compared methods on every individual benchmark. The closest competitor, GenD (WACV'26), reaches 0.923 / 0.958, while Effort (ICML'25) reaches 0.907 / 0.940. The method also achieves substantially better calibration (ECE 0.07 vs. 0.16 for the deterministic baseline) and maintains >99% AUC under JPEG compression, block-wise distortion, and contrast changes up to severity level 5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiCoME outperforms all 12 main-table baselines on every individual benchmark under both cross-dataset and cross-manipulation protocols. Against the two most recent SOTA methods, the gains are consistent but moderate: over GenD (WACV'26) the cross-dataset average improves from 0.923 to 0.939 (+1.6 pp) and the DF40 average from 0.958 to 0.976 (+1.8 pp); over Effort (ICML'25) the improvements are larger, +3.2 pp and +3.6 pp respectively. Classic frequency- and reconstruction-based methods (F3Net, SPSL, SRM, CORE, RECCE, SBI) trail by 14–22 pp on the cross-dataset average, confirming that single-view holistic encoders remain vulnerable to the Semantic Masking Effect. Among recent generalization-focused methods, ProDet (NeurIPS'24) and LSDA (CVPR'24) achieve 0.821 and 0.792 cross-dataset averages, still 12–18 pp below DiCoME. In the robustness evaluation (Figure 7), DiCoME also surpasses Face X-ray, PatchForensics, SBI, FWA, LipForensics, and FTCN under block-wise distortion, contrast change, and JPEG compression, maintaining >99% AUC where pixel-dependent methods drop sharply at higher severity levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) CLIP-based deepfake detection (e.g., Effort, GenD, Forensics Adapter), which the authors argue suffers from a 'Semantic Masking Effect' where dominant identity features overwhelm subtle forgery traces; (2) Evidential Deep Learning and Subjective Logic (Sensoy et al., 2018; Jøsang, 2018), which provide the Dirichlet-parameterized belief/uncertainty decomposition used in the 'Conquer' phase; and (3) Dempster-Shafer evidence fusion (Dempster, 1968), repurposed here to handle inter-view 'epistemic conflict' rather than sensor disagreement. The key departure from the closest prior work (Effort, ICML 2025) is replacing its global SVD rank-reduction with a per-sample geometric projection onto the orthogonal complement of the CLIP semantic direction, and replacing deterministic Softmax fusion with DS-based evidential aggregation that explicitly elevates uncertainty when views contradict.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in deployment-ready deepfake detectors: most SOTA methods produce overconfident point predictions that fail silently on unseen attacks, whereas DiCoME provides calibrated epistemic uncertainty (ECE 0.07 vs. 0.16 for the deterministic baseline) enabling risk-aware rejection. The geometric orthogonality constraint is a clean, interpretable inductive bias that could transfer to other forensic or anomaly-detection settings where a dominant signal masks a subtle one. Open questions include whether the global (per-sample, single-direction) orthogonality assumption holds for region-specific forgeries, whether CLIP remains the optimal semantic anchor as foundation models evolve, and whether the framework extends naturally to temporal video-level or cross-modal (audio-visual) settings, which the authors acknowledge as future work.
