## Problem

Neural network-based HTE estimators struggle with uninformative features prevalent in real-world tabular data, while existing tree-based causal methods (e.g., Causal Forests) rely on greedy, non-differentiable optimization that cannot be integrated into end-to-end deep learning pipelines. This paper bridges the gap by introducing a fully differentiable tree ensemble architecture that retains the sparse feature selection and interaction-capturing strengths of trees while supporting gradient-based training within modern ML frameworks.

## Input / Output

GRACE consumes a pre-treatment feature vector (tabular, potentially high-dimensional up to ~4,000 features) and a treatment assignment that can be binary, multi-valued, continuous, bundle, or multi-dimensional. For binary treatment, it outputs two potential outcome estimates via a TARNet-like structure (shared layer followed by treatment-specific layers); for non-binary settings, it concatenates feature and treatment embeddings before a final output layer. The architecture is modular: a single GRACE layer is a drop-in replacement for any fully-connected layer in existing HTE architectures, and multiple GRACE layers can be stacked with skip connections.

## Method

GRACE is an ensemble of fully-grown decision trees with multi-way (b-way), oblique, and soft splits, all fully parameterized and trained end-to-end via backpropagation. Each internal node uses an oblique decision boundary where entmax enforces sparse feature selection, and a sigmoid-based soft split function assigns continuous (non-normalized) branch weights rather than hard routing. Theorem 5.1 formalizes a GRACE layer as an extension of a FCNN layer that adds three components: ensemble averaging, sparse feature selection, and tree routing that encodes multiplicative feature interactions through products of nonlinear activations. A relaxed variant, GRACE-R, introduces oblivious trees (shared split parameters per depth) and decomposable leaf scores to reduce complexity from O(b^d) to O(bd), enabling practical use at greater depths and branching factors.

## Results

On the toy dataset (binary treatment, 5,000 training samples), GRACE achieves RMSE of 0.024 at k=5, 0.026 at k=20, and 0.024 at k=100 features, compared to Causal Forest (0.041, 0.046, 0.051) and TARNet (0.034, 0.057, 0.135). Across synthetic and semi-synthetic benchmarks (IHDP, ACIC 2016, TCGA, News, Glucose) spanning binary, multi-valued, continuous, and bundle treatment settings, GRACE consistently achieves the lowest RMSE across all training sample sizes (100–10,000), with the largest margins in high-order interaction scenarios. The relaxed variant GRACE-R reduces training complexity from O(b^d) to O(bd) while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRACE outperforms all 15+ baselines (BART, TransTEE, Causal Forest, BCF, T-RF, TARNet, CFRNet, DragonNet, SNet, DIGNet, ESCFR, S-RF, S-NN, NCoRE, RMNet, VSR, SCP, VCNet) across nearly every dataset and training size in both binary and non-binary treatment settings. The margin is most pronounced in interaction-heavy DGPs, where tree-based baselines like Causal Forest and BART struggle with smooth surfaces and neural baselines like TARNet and CFRNet degrade sharply with uninformative features (e.g., TARNet RMSE jumps from 0.034 to 0.135 as k goes from 5 to 100, while GRACE stays at 0.024). On high-dimensional semi-synthetic data (TCGA with 4,000 features, News with 2,858 features), GRACE maintains its advantage over both tree-based and neural baselines. In the ablation study, the ensemble (E) and sparse feature selection (S) components are essential across all settings, while tree routing (T) provides additional gains specifically in interaction-heavy scenarios, confirming that GRACE's advantage stems from the combination of all three architectural elements rather than any single one.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRACE extends the line of differentiable tree architectures for tabular data (Deep Neural Decision Trees, NODE, GRANDE/GradTree) into the causal inference domain, generalizing GRANDE's dense representation to support multi-way oblique splits and introducing a relaxed formulation (GRACE-R) that achieves linear complexity via oblivious structure and decomposable leaf scores. It also draws on the HTE estimation literature (TARNet, CFRNet, Causal Forest, BART) by positioning the GRACE layer as a drop-in replacement for fully-connected layers within existing representation-learning architectures, and provides a formal theorem (Theorem 5.1) decomposing GRACE into an FCNN plus ensemble, sparse feature selection, and tree-routing components. The Entmax-based sparse feature selection mechanism is borrowed from NLP to address the well-documented difficulty of neural networks with uninformative features in tabular settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRACE offers a principled, modular architecture that brings the known advantages of tree-based models (sparse feature selection, natural interaction capture) into differentiable deep learning pipelines for HTE estimation, with a clean ablation isolating the contribution of each component. The consistent and substantial performance gains across binary, multi-valued, continuous, and bundle treatment settings—including high-dimensional semi-synthetic data (TCGA, News)—suggest practical utility for healthcare and policy applications. Key limitations include higher computational cost than neural networks even under GRACE-R, the absence of explicit covariate-imbalance regularization, and restriction to static (non-time-series) settings, all of which are identified as open directions.
