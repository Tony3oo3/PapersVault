## Problem

Learning junta distributions had a quadratic gap between the best known upper bound O(2^{2k}log(n)/ε⁴) and the lower bound Ω(2^k/ε²), and quantum junta states had never been studied. Additionally, prior work on QAC⁰ circuits only exploited low-degree Pauli concentration, missing the stronger structural property that their Choi states are close to juntas, which yields exponentially better sample complexity.

## Input / Output

For classical junta distributions, the algorithm consumes T samples drawn from p and outputs a proper probability distribution p′ within ε in total variation distance. For quantum junta states and QAC⁰ Choi states, the algorithm consumes single copies of the state and performs only Pauli measurements (no joint or adaptive measurements), outputting a learned state within ε in trace distance (or 2ⁿ-Frobenius norm for QAC⁰). The testing variant takes copies of an unknown n-qubit state and outputs a binary decision on whether it is ε-close or 7ε-far from being a k-junta.

## Method

The core insight is that junta objects are simultaneously low-degree and sparse in their Fourier/Pauli spectrum: a k-junta has at most 2^k (classical) or 4^k (quantum) non-zero coefficients, all of degree ≤ k. The algorithm refines the Linial–Mansour–Nisan low-degree procedure by adding a sparsity-aware rounding step: after estimating all low-degree coefficients via empirical means (classical) or Classical Shadows with Pauli measurements (quantum), coefficients below a threshold are zeroed out, which provably eliminates false positives while preserving true coefficients. For QAC⁰ circuits, the paper proves a new structural result (Theorem 4.1) that the Choi state is (ε/2^{2n})-concentrated on a junta of size O((log(s²2^a/ε))^d), then applies the quantum junta learning algorithm directly. This yields a copy complexity of 2^{O((log(s²2^a/ε))^d)} log(n), exponentially improving the prior n^{O((log(s²2^a/ε))^d)} bound.

## Results

For learning k-junta distributions, the paper achieves O(2^k log(n)/ε²) samples in total variation distance, quadratically improving the previous O(2^{2k} log(n)/ε⁴) bound and matching the known lower bound in every parameter. For quantum junta states (a new problem), the paper shows O(12^k log(n)/ε²) copies suffice for trace-distance learning, with a matching lower bound of Ω((4^k + log(n))/ε²). For QAC⁰ circuits, the paper reduces the copy complexity from n^{O((log(s²2^a/ε))^d)} to 2^{O((log(s²2^a/ε))^d)} log(n), an exponential improvement for constant depth and size. Additionally, Õ(2^n/ε²) copies are shown necessary and sufficient to test whether a state is ε-close or 7ε-far from being a k-junta for constant k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Aliakbarpour, Blais, and Rubinfeld (COLT'16), the paper quadratically improves the junta distribution learning upper bound from O(2^{2k} log(n)/ε⁴) to O(2^k log(n)/ε²), closing the gap to their lower bound Ω(2^k/ε² + k log(n)/ε) in every parameter. The time complexity also improves from O(n^k 2^{2k}/ε⁴) to O(n^k 2^k/ε²). Against Nadimpalli et al. (STOC'24), the paper exponentially improves the QAC⁰ circuit learning bound from n^{O((log(s²2^a/ε))^d)} copies to 2^{O((log(s²2^a/ε))^d)} log(n) copies, which is a dramatic gain when s and d are constants (polylog vs. quasi-poly in n). The key technical insight enabling both improvements is that the relevant objects are not merely low-degree but also sparse (close to juntas), allowing a rounding step in the Linial–Mansour–Nisan algorithm that exploits sparsity. For quantum junta states, no prior work exists, so the paper establishes both the first upper and lower bounds, with the lower bound Ω(4^k/ε²) inherited from k-qubit state tomography and Ω(log(n)/ε²) from a new indistinguishability argument.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper refines the Linial–Mansour–Nisan low-degree learning algorithm by exploiting both low-degree and sparsity of the Fourier/Pauli spectrum, a technique previously used for Boolean functions (Eskenazis et al., 2023) but not for distributions or quantum objects. For classical junta distributions, it quadratically improves the sample complexity of Aliakbarpour et al. (COLT'16) from O(2^{2k} log(n)/ε⁴) to O(2^k log(n)/ε²), matching their lower bound. For QAC⁰ circuits, it builds on Nadimpalli et al. (STOC'24), who showed low-degree Pauli concentration of Choi states, by proving the stronger property that these states are close to quantum juntas, and uses Classical Shadows (Huang et al., 2020) as the measurement primitive with a novel Pauli-analytic proof of its guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a clear gap by initiating the study of quantum junta states, an object that had no prior learning or testing theory despite junta unitaries and channels being recently studied. The key structural insight—that QAC⁰ Choi states are close to juntas, not merely low-degree—enables exponentially better learning bounds for constant-depth quantum circuits and yields a stronger lower bound for the address function. The open question of whether QAC⁰ circuits can compute parity remains unresolved, and the near-optimal copy complexity for testing junta-ness (Θ̃(2^n/ε²) for constant k) suggests that the quantum junta framework may be a natural lens for understanding the computational limits of shallow quantum circuits.
