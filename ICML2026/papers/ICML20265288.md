## Problem

Task-level metrics such as accuracy, perplexity, and MIA AUC are the standard way to evaluate LLM unlearning, but they can be misleading: models can show degraded performance on forget sets while their internal representations remain intact, allowing supposedly erased knowledge to be rapidly restored through minimal fine-tuning. The representational dynamics that distinguish genuine erasure from reversible suppression have not been systematically characterized, leaving a critical gap in whether unlearning achieves true deletion or merely obscures information.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a trained LLM, a forget set, a retain set, and a probe set of inputs, from which hidden activations are extracted at every Transformer layer for the original, unlearned, and relearned models. It produces per-layer PCA similarity and shift coordinates, CKA alignment scores, FIM spectra, and a layer-averaged mean PCA distance, which jointly classify the forgetting into one of four regimes (reversible/irreversible × catastrophic/non-catastrophic). The relearning probe is constrained to a budget matched to the forget set size, with data drawn from the forget set itself, a domain-aligned retain subset, or unrelated out-of-distribution samples.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified representation-level diagnostic toolkit comprising four complementary tools—PCA similarity (cosine alignment of dominant activation directions), PCA shift (translational drift in the top-2 PCA plane), centered kernel alignment (activation-subspace preservation), and Fisher information (local loss-landscape sensitivity)—supplemented by a new summary metric, the mean PCA distance, that captures overall representational drift in a single scalar. To test reversibility, they define a constrained relearning protocol where the unlearned model is briefly fine-tuned on a dataset size-matched to the forget set, and demonstrate that alternative recovery strategies (prompt attacks, jailbreaking, quantization, in-context demonstrations) all fail once the model enters reversible catastrophic forgetting, making relearning the only reliable probe. A perturbation-based theoretical analysis models unlearning as layer-wise weight perturbations, using the Davis-Kahan theorem to bound PCA similarity and showing that small perturbations concentrated near the logits can produce large task-level metric drops while leaving deeper representations intact. The framework is applied across six unlearning methods (GA family, NPO family, RLabel), multiple LLMs (Yi-6B, Qwen-2.5-7B, Llama-3-8B, Qwen3-8B/14B), and both single and continual unlearning settings, revealing that irreversible non-catastrophic forgetting—the ideal regime—remains exceptionally challenging to achieve.

## Results

On Yi-6B (simple task, continual unlearning, N=100), GA at LR=3×10⁻⁵ drives F.Acc and R.Acc to 0.0% with MIA dropping to 26.1%, and relearning recovers F.Acc to only 2.1% (irreversible catastrophic forgetting). In contrast, NPO+KL at LR=3×10⁻⁶ maintains F.Acc at 78.8% and R.Acc at 65.5% post-unlearning, with relearning restoring F.Acc to 83.8% (reversible non-catastrophic). Mean PCA distance confirms the distinction: at LR=3×10⁻⁵ it remains 104.6 after relearning (vs. 121.5 unlearned), while at LR=3×10⁻⁶ it drops from 9.6 to 2.1, indicating full representational recovery. Single unlearning across all six methods yields only 2–5% R.Acc degradation and near-complete F.Acc recovery after one relearning pass, confirming the reversible non-catastrophic regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates six primary unlearning methods (GA, GA+GD, GA+KL, NPO, NPO+KL, RLabel) plus four additional methods (RMU, UnDIAL, AltPO, PDU) across Yi-6B, Qwen-2.5-7B, Llama-3-8B, and Qwen3-14B. GA and RLabel are the most aggressive: at LR=3×10⁻⁵, N=100 they collapse F.Acc to 0.0% and, critically, relearning recovers only 2–4%, confirming irreversible catastrophic forgetting. NPO+KL is the most conservative, maintaining F.Acc ≥ 77.6% and R.Acc ≥ 64.3% even at LR=5×10⁻⁶, N=100, and achieving the best post-relearning F.Acc of 83.8% (+4.9 over original). GA+GD at LR=3×10⁻⁶, N=100 is the only method that shows virtually no degradation (F.Acc 78.8%, R.Acc 65.5%) yet still exhibits irreversible representational drift in some settings. The key finding is that no method achieves the ideal irreversible non-catastrophic regime under standard settings; only GA+GD+WAGLE with constrained relearning (50% retain set or unrelated data) approaches it, showing F.Acc stuck at 31–47% with PCA distance remaining 9–25 on the forget set while retain-set drift stays modest (5–8). Relearning on the forget set is consistently the most sample-efficient recovery path (10% of forget set restores 67% F.Acc), while retain-set or unrelated data require 100% to achieve comparable recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLM unlearning literature (Yao et al., 2024; Jia et al., 2024; Wuerkaixi et al., 2025) and prior observations that unlearned models can be 'jogged' back into remembering via benign fine-tuning (Lo et al., 2024; Lynch et al., 2024) or quantization (Zhang et al., 2025). However, those studies treated reversibility as a behavioral phenomenon assessed through task-level metrics. This work departs by introducing the first systematic representation-level diagnostic toolkit (PCA similarity/shift, CKA, Fisher information, mean PCA distance) and a four-regime taxonomy (reversible/irreversible × catastrophic/non-catastrophic) that explains *why* task-level collapse does not imply genuine erasure, complemented by a perturbation-based theoretical analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical evaluation gap: standard metrics (accuracy, perplexity, MIA AUC) can certify 'successful' unlearning while internal representations remain nearly intact, meaning the knowledge is merely suppressed and trivially recoverable. The four-regime taxonomy and mean PCA distance provide a practical protocol for distinguishing reversible obscuration from durable erasure, which is essential for compliance with regulations like the EU Right to be Forgotten. A key open question is how to achieve the ideal regime—irreversible, non-catastrophic forgetting—since the only observed instance required a highly constrained setup (GA+GD+WAGLE with limited relearning data), and stronger updates inevitably cause representational collapse. Limitations include evaluation on models up to 14B parameters and a handful of tasks, leaving open whether the taxonomy holds at production scale.
