## Problem

Unified hyperspectral image (HSI) restoration must handle diverse, mixed, and unseen degradations within a single model, yet existing prompt-based approaches either require explicit degradation labels (predefined types or text prompts) that are impractical to obtain in real-world settings, or rely on opaque black-box CNN embeddings that overfit to training distributions and lack explicit spectral modeling. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether interpretable, physically grounded spatial-spectral metrics can serve as a universal degradation descriptor that generalizes to unseen corruptions without any external supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded HSI patch (128×128×31 for natural scenes or 128×128×100 for remote sensing) suffering from an arbitrary combination of Gaussian noise, Gaussian/motion blur, bicubic downsampling, random pixel masking, or spectral band dropout, and produces the corresponding clean HSI at the same spatial resolution. Crucially, no degradation type label, kernel, or noise parameter is provided at inference time; the model computes six interpretable spatial-spectral metrics (HFER, STU, SCM, SCSD, GSD, SCC) directly from the degraded input to form its Degradation Prompt, making it fully autonomous and zero-shot capable on unseen degradation types such as Poisson noise or motion blur.

## Method

DAMP is a hierarchical U-shaped Transformer (4 encoder levels, 3 decoder levels, base channel 64) whose decoder integrates a Degradation-Adaptive Mixture-of-Experts (DAMoE) at every level. The Degradation Prompt (DP) is a 6-dimensional vector of interpretable spatial-spectral metrics computed from the degraded input; it is concatenated with globally-pooled features, projected through learnable matrices, and used as a top-k gating router that selects specialized experts based on the physical degradation profile rather than abstract visual features. Each expert is a Spatial-Spectral Adaptive Module (SSAM) with parallel branches—Window-based Multi-head Self-Attention for spatial structure and 1D convolutions for spectral correlation—whose fusion weights (λ_s, λ_c) are expert-specific learnable scalars constrained to sum to 1, forcing experts to specialize as spatial-heavy or spectral-heavy during training. The selected experts' outputs are aggregated via weighted summation and fused with shared degradation-agnostic features through a channel-wise convolution, enabling the model to dynamically compose spatial and spectral recovery strategies tailored to the input's degradation severity.

## Results

On the ARAD dataset (natural HSI), DAMP achieves an average PSNR of 51.43 dB and SSIM of 0.989 across five restoration tasks (Gaussian denoising, deblurring, super-resolution, inpainting, and band completion), outperforming the best unified baseline MP-HSIR (50.69 dB) by 0.74 dB. On remote sensing data, DAMP reaches 39.42 dB PSNR, surpassing MP-HSIR (38.33 dB) and MoCE-IR (36.78 dB). In zero-shot evaluation on unseen degradations (CAVE dataset), DAMP attains 31.05 dB PSNR for motion deblurring and 24.08 dB for Poisson denoising, exceeding the strongest baseline PromptIR by 0.52 dB and 2.10 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAMP consistently outperforms all five unified restoration baselines (InstructIR, PromptIR, DFPIR, MoCE-IR, MP-HSIR) across all five restoration tasks and both natural and remote sensing domains. On the ARAD average, DAMP leads MP-HSIR by 0.74 dB PSNR and MoCE-IR by 2.71 dB, with the largest gap over InstructIR at 8.13 dB. Against task-specific SOTA methods, DAMP also achieves superior results in most settings (e.g., 52.84 dB on ARAD deblurring vs. Loformer's 49.91 dB; 44.01 dB on ARAD super-resolution vs. VolFormer's 41.83 dB). In zero-shot generalization, DAMP surpasses PromptIR (the strongest unified baseline) by 0.52 dB on motion deblurring and 2.10 dB on Poisson denoising, while MP-HSIR degrades severely (23.63 dB) on unseen tasks. The efficiency comparison shows DAMP requires only 313.8G FLOPs and 14.91M parameters, substantially fewer than PromptIR (573.5G, 26.13M) and MP-HSIR (894.6G, 13.88M), while delivering the best restoration quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of unified image restoration (UIR) and hyperspectral image (HSI) restoration, departing from two dominant paradigms in prior work: explicit degradation priors (text prompts in InstructIR, predefined degradation labels in PromptHSI and MP-HSIR) and implicit black-box representations (CNN-learned embeddings in PromptIR, MoCE-IR). Rather than learning an opaque latent prompt or requiring external degradation annotations, DAMP extracts six interpretable spatial-spectral metrics (HFER, STU, SCM, SCSD, GSD, SCC) directly from the degraded input to form a Degradation Prompt that serves as a physical-prior-guided router for a Mixture-of-Experts decoder. The MoE design draws on recent MoE-for-restoration work (MoCE-IR, MoERL) but replaces visual-feature-based or frequency-based routing with metric-driven routing, and introduces SSAM experts whose learnable spatial/spectral fusion coefficients (λs, λc) force specialization into 'spatial' and 'spectral' experts without explicit supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the need for degradation labels or kernel parameters at inference time, which is critical for real-world HSI acquisition where the exact corruption is unknown. The zero-shot generalization to motion deblurring and Poisson denoising (unseen during training) demonstrates that interpretable metric-based conditioning can capture degradation severity in a way that transfers to novel corruption types. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include the requirement for separate natural-scene and remote-sensing models due to domain gaps, and unvalidated performance on extremely rare or physically distinct degradations. Open questions include whether dynamic metric weighting or domain-adaptive techniques can unify cross-domain restoration in a single model, and whether the 6-metric DP is sufficient for the full diversity of real-world HSI corruptions.
