## Problem

Feldman (2016) left open whether there exists a stochastic convex optimization instance in linear dimension where the Empirical Risk Minimizer is unique and fails to generalize. This paper resolves that question and further shows that even approximate ERMs with error O(1/(m√m)) must overfit, and derives a new polynomial generalization lower bound for Gradient Descent that exponentially narrows the gap to the best known upper bound.

## Input / Output

The construction takes an i.i.d. sample S = (z_1, …, z_m) drawn from a uniform distribution over [k] with k = 2m, and outputs a parameter vector w_S in the unit ball W_d ⊂ R^d with d = 6m. The parameter is decomposed as w = (w^c, w^m) ∈ R^{2k} × R^k, where w^c is a 'code term' and w^m is a 'message term.' The loss f(w, i) is 1-Lipschitz and λ-strongly convex (with λ = m^{-3/2} in the main theorem), and the instance space Z is finite with |Z| = k.

## Method

The construction combines Feldman's function (built on asymptotically good codes) with a convex 'link function' p(w) = max_{S'} {γ^m ⟨v_{S'}, w^m⟩ − γ^c ⟨G(v_{S'}^s), w^c⟩} that couples the message and code components. The message component w^m is incentivized by a linear noise term to align with the sample-specific vector v_S, while the link function p(w) then penalizes this alignment and simultaneously drives the code component w^c toward G(v_S^s)/||G(v_S^s)||, which is a known bad ERM for Feldman's function. Strong convexity (via quadratic regularization terms) ensures the ERM is unique, and the parameter regime is chosen so that v_S is the unique maximizer of p at the optimum. For the Gradient Descent lower bound, the same construction is analyzed along the GD trajectory, showing that the code component converges geometrically to the bad direction at rate (1−ηλ^c)^t while the message component tracks v_S, yielding a generalization error of Ω(√(ηT/m^{3/2})) for the suffix-averaged output.

## Results

The paper proves that in dimension d = 6m, there exists a 1-Lipschitz, m^{-3/2}-strongly convex loss where every Θ(m^{-3/2})-ERM solution incurs constant generalization error Ω(1) (Theorem 3.1), resolving an open question by Feldman (2016). A fine-grained extension (Theorem 3.2) shows that for any λ ∈ [m^{-3/2}, m^{-1/2}], the ERM generalization error is Ω(1/(λm^{3/2})). For Gradient Descent, the paper establishes a new lower bound of Ω(√(ηT/m^{3/2})) on generalization error (Theorem 3.4), which is the first polynomial lower bound capturing overfitting as a function of training horizon and sample size, and exponentially narrows the gap to the best known upper bound O(η√T + ηT/m) of Bassily et al. (2020).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines. The key comparison is between the new lower bounds and existing upper/lower bounds for Gradient Descent generalization. The prior best lower bound from Amir et al. (2021a) required exponential dimension and only showed overfitting for exponentially long training (T = Ω(2^m)), while Livni (2024) gave a linear-dimension lower bound of Ω(η√T + 1/(ηT)) that did not capture the ηT/m overfitting term. The new bound Ω(√(ηT/m^{3/2})) is polynomial in all parameters and applies in linear dimension d = 6m, closing the gap to the Bassily et al. (2020) upper bound O(η√T + ηT/m) from exponential to polynomial. For ERM sample complexity, the result improves upon Shalev-Shwartz et al. (2009), which required exponential dimension for a unique overfitting ERM, by achieving the same in linear dimension and extending to approximate ERMs with error Θ(m^{-3/2}).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Feldman's (2016) construction of bad ERMs using asymptotically good codes, extending it from a setting where only *some* ERMs fail to one where *all* ERMs (including approximate ones with error O(1/(m√m))) fail, and crucially in linear dimension rather than exponential. It also builds on Livni's (2024) lower bounds for Gradient Descent in SCO, using the same code-based construction to derive a new polynomial lower bound of Ω(√(ηT/m^1.5)) that exponentially narrows the gap to the best known upper bound. The key technical departure from prior work is the introduction of a convex 'link function' p(w) that identifies the sample from noise and forces the unique minimizer to be a bad ERM, overcoming the obstacle that a simple noisy linear term cannot correlate with all possible bad ERMs simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a concrete open question posed by Feldman (2016) and establishes that convexity alone does not guarantee that generic empirical risk minimizers will generalize, even in the simplest possible loss landscape. The practical implication is that algorithmic properties (implicit bias, regularization, stability) are what enable learning in SCO, not any intrinsic property of the empirical minimizer. Open questions remain: whether the gap between the new GD lower bound and the Bassily et al. (2020) upper bound can be fully closed, whether the results extend to smooth objectives, and whether there exists a problem where every ε-ERM with m^{-3/2} < ε < m^{-1/2} fails.
