## Problem

Adapting GNN force field foundation models to new chemical systems requires expensive joint retraining on combined datasets, and existing model-merging techniques (weight averaging, Fisher, TIES, EMR) designed for discrete vision/language tasks fail catastrophically on continuous force-field regression. No principled framework existed for merging independently fine-tuned GNN force fields into a single model without full retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264550/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes n independently fine-tuned GNN force field models (e.g., M3GNet or Orb variants) along with atomistic graphs (atomic coordinates, species features) from each source domain to compute intermediate node embeddings. It produces a single merged force field that predicts total potential energy and per-atom forces for any input graph in the union of source domains. The closed-form merging step requires only the linear weight matrices and the source models' intermediate embeddings; the subsequent targeted fine-tuning updates only the last 3 interaction blocks and the force/energy head, leaving the majority of parameters frozen.

## Method

GFFMERGE exploits the linear structure of GNN message-passing layers to formulate merging as a convex embedding-alignment problem: minimize the squared discrepancy between the merged model's node embeddings and each source model's embeddings at every interaction layer. By relaxing inter-layer dependencies (feeding source-model embeddings directly into each layer rather than the merged model's own outputs), the objective decomposes into independent least-squares problems per linear parameter block, yielding a closed-form solution via the normal equations (Eq. 11). A brief targeted fine-tuning of only the late interaction layers and force/energy heads then corrects residual nonlinear discrepancies. This two-stage procedure—analytical merging followed by sparse fine-tuning—achieves 5–27× training speedups over joint fine-tuning while recovering near gold-standard accuracy across molecular, supramolecular, and solid-state benchmarks.

## Results

On M3GNet force fields, GFFMERGE achieves Force MAE of 0.72–1.83 kcal/mol/Å across MD17 and MD22 scenarios, within 5–15% of the Joint Fine-Tuning gold standard (0.65–1.48), while all baselines (Weight Averaging, Fisher, TIES, EMR) degrade by 2–5×. On the solid-state LiPS20 benchmark, baselines suffer catastrophic failures (Force MAE up to 117 eV/Å) whereas GFFMERGE maintains 0.42–1.21 eV/Å. GFFMERGE delivers 5×–27× training speedups over Joint FT (e.g., 27.81× on MD22 3-task mix with M3GNet). For generic GNNs, GNNMERGE achieves 88.94% average node-classification accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (SURGERY, 78.01%) by 10.93% and GIT-REBASIN (53.39%) by 35.53%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four force-field merging baselines (Weight Averaging, Fisher, TIES, EMR) are designed for discrete prediction tasks and fail catastrophically on continuous force-field regression: on MD17/MD22 they incur 2–5× higher Force MAE than Joint FT, and on LiPS20 (M3GNet) they produce errors of 4–117 eV/Å versus GFFMERGE's 0.42–1.21 eV/Å. TIES is consistently the weakest baseline (e.g., 5.38 kcal/mol/Å on MD22 Ac-Ala3+AT-AT vs. GFFMERGE's 1.10), while EMR and Weight Averaging are marginally better but still 3–4× worse than GFFMERGE. For generic GNN merging (GNNMERGE), the method outperforms SURGERY by 10.93%, ZIPIT! by 13.78%, PERMUTE by 14.67%, GIT-REBASIN by 35.53%, and Weight Averaging by 23.57% in average node-classification accuracy. Notably, GFFMERGE's closed-form solution alone (before any fine-tuning) already outperforms all baselines, and the targeted fine-tuning stage closes the remaining gap to within 5–15% of Joint FT while achieving 5×–27× speedups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the general model-merging paradigm (task arithmetic, Fisher-weighted averaging, TIES, EMR, weight averaging) from vision and language into the GNN force-field setting, but departs fundamentally from all prior merging formulations by exploiting the linear structure of message-passing layers to derive a convex, closed-form embedding-alignment objective rather than relying on heuristic weight-space operations. The authors explicitly position GNN force-field merging as an open problem, noting that existing methods were designed for discrete prediction tasks and have never been extended to continuous regression on graph-structured data. The method also generalizes to generic GNNs (GCN, GAT, GIN, GraphSAGE, NodeFormer), where it subsumes and outperforms permutation-alignment baselines (GIT-ReBasin, Permute, ZIPIT!, SURGERY) that were previously the state of the art for merging independently trained networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GFFMERGE provides the first systematic benchmark showing that all existing model-merging methods catastrophically fail on GNN force fields (force MAEs 2–360× worse than joint training), while the proposed closed-form solution alone already outperforms every baseline before any fine-tuning. The 5–27× training speedups (and up to 1000× for large-scale graph benchmarks) make modular composition of specialized force fields practical for resource-constrained settings, reducing both cost and carbon footprint relative to retraining. Open questions include robustness under distribution shift, dynamic/incremental merging for lifelong learning, and extension to equivariant GNN architectures; a practical limitation is that the method requires forward passes through source models to collect intermediate embeddings, so it is not fully data-free in the strictest sense.
