## Problem

Structure-preserving dynamics models (Hamiltonian and GENERIC neural networks) enforce conservation laws by construction but must be retrained from scratch for each parametric configuration, making them impractical for many-query applications. Existing optimization-based meta-learning remedies (MAML, Reptile, ANIL) require expensive gradient updates over high-dimensional parameters and exhibit limited generalizability, while modulation-based meta-learning—despite its success in implicit neural representations—has not been systematically studied for structure-preserving dynamics.

## Input / Output

The model consumes canonical state variables (q, p) for Hamiltonian systems or (q, p, S) for GENERIC systems, conditioned on a low-dimensional latent code z^(k) that implicitly encodes the unknown system parameters μ without requiring explicit parameterization. At test time, a small set of trajectories (n-shot) from the target system is used to adapt the latent code via auto-decoding gradient steps, while all base network weights remain frozen. The output is the predicted velocity field (dq/dt, dp/dt) or the full GENERIC vector field, evaluated either on held-out trajectories or on a uniform spatial mesh for out-of-distribution assessment.

## Method

The paper proposes two novel modulation techniques—latent multi-rank (MR) and latent SVD-like modulation—that decompose each network layer's parameters into shared base weights and instance-specific low-rank corrections generated by a hypernetwork from a latent code. In SVD-like modulation, the basis vectors U and V are shared base parameters trained with meta-gradients, while per-instance singular values d_i are produced by the hypernetwork; a soft orthogonality penalty (‖UᵀU−I‖_F, ‖VᵀV−I‖_F) and ReLU activation on the hypernetwork enforce SVD-like structure without explicit factorization. Training follows a two-loop meta-learning algorithm where latent codes evolve continuously across epochs (rather than resetting to zero), and test-time adaptation initializes new latent codes with the Euclidean mean of all training codes before n-shot auto-decoding. A locality regularization (penalizing ‖z‖₂ and ‖φ‖₂) constrains instance-specific deviations from the shared base, and a theoretical proposition shows that the local parametric dimensionality of the dynamics is bounded by the rank r, justifying the low-rank parameterization.

## Results

On three Hamiltonian benchmark systems (Duffing, Mass Spring, Pendulum), the proposed SVD(5) modulation achieves the lowest trajectory errors across all systems: 2.303×10⁻² (Duffing), 1.116×10⁻² (Mass Spring), and 5.336×10⁻² (Pendulum). Modulation-based methods collectively improve performance by approximately 65% over the best optimization-based baselines (Reptile, ANIL). On the dissipative DNO system, SVD(3) achieves the best trajectory error of 1.423×10⁻¹, while MAML, Reptile, and ANIL all fail (NaN or divergence).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed SVD(5) modulation outperforms all optimization-based baselines (MAML, Reptile, ANIL) by a wide margin on trajectory error across all three Hamiltonian systems — e.g., on Pendulum, SVD(5) achieves ε_traj = 5.336×10⁻² versus 75.73×10⁻² for Reptile and 37.33×10⁻² for ANIL. Among modulation-based baselines, SVD(5) dominates on trajectory error for all systems (e.g., 2.303 vs. 2.784 for FW on Duffing; 5.336 vs. 10.65 for FW on Pendulum) and on field error for Pendulum (4.624 vs. 8.231 for FW). The Shift baseline is competitive on Duffing field error (9.031 vs. 10.03) but lags substantially on trajectory error (5.952 vs. 2.303) and on Mass Spring (16.66 vs. 1.509 field error). MAML is the least stable baseline, producing field errors exceeding 300×10⁻² on Mass Spring and 92.90×10⁻² on Duffing, far worse than per-instance Scratch training. On the dissipative DNO, optimization-based methods (MAML, Reptile, ANIL) all fail with NaN or divergence, while SVD(3) achieves the best trajectory error (1.423×10⁻¹) with the lowest variance across seeds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of structure-preserving neural networks (HNNs, GENERIC/metriplectic networks) and modulation-based meta-learning developed for implicit neural representations (Dupont et al., 2022; Kirchmeyer et al., 2022/CODA). While prior meta-learning for Hamiltonian dynamics (Lee et al., 2021b; Song & Jeong, 2024) relied on optimization-based strategies like MAML and ANIL, this work repurposes latent modulation—previously studied only in INRs and one dynamics paper (CODA)—into the structure-preserving setting, and introduces two novel low-rank modulation variants (multi-rank and SVD-like) inspired by LoRA. It further extends the scope from energy-conserving to dissipative GENERIC systems, where no prior structure-preserving meta-learning work exists.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical bottleneck of per-configuration retraining in structure-preserving methods is directly addressed, and the ~65% performance gap between modulation-based and optimization-based meta-learning provides a clear signal that the field should shift toward modulation for many-query dynamics. The complete failure of Reptile and ANIL (NaN/divergence) on the dissipative DNO system highlights a real limitation of optimization-based meta-learning in thermodynamically constrained settings. Open questions include whether the low-rank modulation structure persists for higher-dimensional or multi-body systems, how these methods scale to continuous parameter spaces rather than discrete instances, and whether the SVD-like basis alignment with dominant singular modes generalizes beyond the small MLP architectures tested here.
