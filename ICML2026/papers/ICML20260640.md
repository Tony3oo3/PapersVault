## Problem

While classifier-free guidance (CFG) and auto-guidance (AG) improve diffusion model generation by creating quality differences between denoising results, guidance methods tailored to the unique generative dynamics of bridge models—specifically their exploitation of clean prior information—remain largely unexplored. Bridge models perform data-to-data generation with a U-shaped SNR profile, yet no existing guidance method leverages this prior exploitation mechanism to improve generation quality in a training-free manner.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained bridge models (DDBM, DBIM) without any retraining, taking a clean prior representation at the boundary t=T (edge maps at 64×64 for Edges→Handbags, depth maps at 256×256 for DIODE, or a masked 256×256 image plus a class label for ImageNet inpainting) and producing the corresponding target image. At each sampling step, the method constructs a degraded copy of the current hidden representation via a simple corruption operator (Gaussian noise, blur, or JPEG compression), requiring only one additional forward pass per step beyond the standard denoiser call.

## Method

Prior Guidance (PG) constructs a weak prior by applying a degradation operator H (e.g., adding Gaussian noise) to the clean prior at each sampling step, then extrapolates the high-quality denoising result D_θ(x_t, t, x_T) against the degraded one D_θ(H(x_t), t, x_T) with a scaling factor w_PG, explicitly amplifying prior exploitation. Frequency-Modulated Prior Guidance (FMPG) extends PG by decomposing the guidance residual into low-frequency (LF) and high-frequency (HF) bands via FFT, applying an inverted U-shaped scale to LF (strengthening structural anchoring at intermediate low-SNR steps) and a U-shaped scale to HF (relaxing detail guidance where the bridge SNR is lowest), aligning the guidance schedule with the U-shaped SNR inherent to data-to-data bridge dynamics. For inpainting where the prior is weak, a cascaded CFG-FMPG framework first uses CFG at early steps to establish coarse semantic structure, then switches to FMPG for prior-exploitation-driven refinement, achieving complementary gains without increasing NFE.

## Results

On Edges→Handbags (64×64), DBIM+FMPG at 20 NFE achieves FID 1.07, IS 3.69, LPIPS 0.093, and MSE 0.005, substantially outperforming the unguided DBIM baseline (FID 1.74) at the same budget and even surpassing DBIM at 100 NFE (FID 0.91) with only 20% of the sampling steps. On DIODE-Outdoor (256×256), DBIM+FMPG at 20 NFE reaches FID 3.20 versus 4.99 for DBIM and 4.12 for ECSI at the same NFE. On ImageNet 256×256 inpainting, the cascaded CFG-FMPG strategy achieves FID 3.86 and IS 154.4 at 10 NFE, outperforming DBIM+CFG (FID 4.34, IS 149.4) and the unguided DBIM (FID 4.54, IS 137.6) at the same budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DBIM+FMPG consistently outperforms the unguided DBIM baseline across all NFEs: at 20 NFE it reduces FID from 1.74 to 1.07 on Edges→Handbags (38% improvement) and from 4.99 to 3.20 on DIODE (36% improvement). Against the fast bridge sampler ECSI, DBIM+FMPG achieves lower FID at every matched NFE (e.g., 1.07 vs. 1.54 on Edges→Handbags at 20 NFE; 3.20 vs. 4.12 on DIODE at 20 NFE). Compared to the slower DDBM sampler, DBIM+FMPG at 20 NFE (FID 1.07) surpasses DDBM at 118 NFE (FID 1.83) and approaches DDBM at 200 NFE (FID 0.88) with only 10% of the sampling steps. On the older baselines, the gap is dramatic: I2SB (FID 7.43), SDEdit (FID 26.5), Pix2Pix (FID 74.8), and DDIB (FID 186.84) on Edges→Handbags are all far outperformed. On ImageNet inpainting, the cascaded CFG-FMPG strategy beats standalone DBIM+CFG by 0.48 FID at 10 NFE (3.86 vs. 4.34) and by 0.68 FID at 20 NFE (3.53 vs. 3.69), while also improving IS from 149.4 to 154.4 and CA from 74.5% to 75.1% at 10 NFE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the guidance paradigm established by CFG (Ho & Salimans, 2021) and Auto-Guidance (Karras et al., 2024) to the bridge model setting, where the key generative advantage is prior exploitation rather than condition alignment or score accuracy. While CFG and AG create quality differences via conditioning or model capacity, PG creates the difference by degrading the informative prior itself—a dimension unique to data-to-data bridge processes (DDBM, DBIM). The frequency-modulated variant (FMPG) further departs from prior guidance work by aligning the guidance schedule with the U-shaped SNR trajectory inherent to bridge sampling, a structural property absent in standard noise-to-data diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of PG/FMPG makes it immediately applicable to any pre-trained bridge model without retraining, which is practically significant as bridge models are gaining traction for image-to-video, audio super-resolution, and signal restoration. The U-shaped SNR analysis provides a principled explanation for why frequency-specific guidance outperforms uniform scaling, offering a design principle that could inform future bridge-specific methods. Open questions include whether the frequency modulation principle generalizes to non-Gaussian transition kernels, whether the approach extends to video or 3D bridge models, and whether the cascaded CFG-FMPG framework can be further optimized for very low NFE regimes where both semantic establishment and detail refinement must occur in fewer than 5 steps.
