## Problem

Scaling actor-critic networks in continuous-control RL hits a hard plateau: simply widening monolithic networks destabilizes training and saturates performance, as shown by SimBa and BRC. While Mixture-of-Experts has proven effective for scaling in NLP and discrete-action RL, no prior work has systematically investigated joint MoE scaling of both actor and critic networks for continuous control, leaving the scaling behavior of conditional-computation architectures in this regime unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes low-dimensional proprioceptive state vectors (and a task embedding in multi-task settings) as input to the gating network and expert towers. The actor produces a Gaussian policy parameterized by a weighted mixture of expert means and variances, while the critic outputs a convex combination of expert Q-values; notably, the actor's gating is conditioned solely on state, whereas the critic's gating is conditioned on the state-action pair to properly route based on the specific action being evaluated. In multi-task mode, the task identifier is concatenated with state features as gating input.

## Method

ScaleMoE replaces a single actor and critic with N expert networks of each, connected by a learned top-K gating mechanism that selects and combines a sparse subset of experts per input. Two integration variants are provided: output-level gating (late fusion) that blends Gaussian policy parameters and Q-values across selected experts, and feature-level gating (early fusion) that mixes penultimate-layer representations before a shared output head. Training uses standard actor-critic losses on the fused outputs, augmented with a load-balancing loss (negative entropy of batch-averaged expert usage) and an importance loss (mean squared gating probability) to prevent expert collapse. The architecture is algorithm-agnostic and instantiated as a drop-in module for both SimBa (single-task DDPG) and BRC (multi-task SAC with categorical value functions), preserving each backbone's original training procedure. The key insight is that sparse conditional computation decouples total model capacity from per-sample FLOPs, enabling monotonic performance scaling with expert count (up to 64) where equivalent-width monolithic networks plateau or degrade.

## Results

On multi-task DMC, ScaleMoE-BRC with 8 experts (critic width 512) achieves IQM 849 on DOGS and 730 on HUMANOIDS, surpassing the monolithic BRC (critic width 4096) at 835 and 709 respectively, while using only ~20M parameters vs. ~136M (15%). On HumanoidBench (20 tasks), ScaleMoE with 16 experts reaches a normalized IQM of 0.62 versus BRC-4096's 0.49 (+27% relative). On MetaWorld (50 tasks), ScaleMoE with 16+ experts achieves IQM ≥ 0.9, outperforming BRC-4096 at 0.88. On single-task DMC hard tasks, scaling to 8 experts yields up to 60% IQM improvement over the single-expert SimBa baseline on humanoid-run, whereas naively widening SimBa to matching parameter count (critic width 1472) plateaus or degrades.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SimBa (single-task DDPG backbone), ScaleMoE shows consistent monotonic improvement as experts increase from 1 to 8, with 32% IQM gains on dog-run (4 experts) and 60% on humanoid-run (8 experts), while naively scaling SimBa's critic width to 1472 (matching the 8-expert parameter budget) yields no improvement or degradation. Against BRC (multi-task SAC backbone, critic width 4096), ScaleMoE-BRC with 8 experts of width 512 outperforms on both DMC DOGS (849 vs. 835) and DMC HUMANOIDS (730 vs. 709) using only 15% of the parameters. On HumanoidBench, ScaleMoE with 16 experts achieves 0.62 normalized IQM versus BRC-4096's 0.49, a 27% relative improvement. On MetaWorld, ScaleMoE with 16+ experts (IQM ≥ 0.9) surpasses BRC-4096 (0.88). The paper also shows that ScaleMoE outperforms both naive width scaling of monolithic networks and ensemble-of-BRC baselines, with the latter suffering performance degradation as ensemble size increases while ScaleMoE improves monotonically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) monolithic scaling in continuous-control RL, where SimBa (Lee et al., 2024) and BRC (Nauman et al., 2025) showed that inductive biases (normalization, residual connections, regularization) enable positive scaling but plateau beyond a certain width; and (2) MoE in RL, where prior efforts (Obando-Ceron et al., 2024; Willi et al., 2024; Akrour et al., 2021; Hendawy et al., 2023) applied expert routing to discrete control, multi-task policy specialization, or orthogonal-constrained diversity without studying joint actor-critic scaling. ScaleMoE departs from both by introducing top-K gated MoE into both the actor and critic simultaneously in continuous control, treating the number of experts as a systematic scaling axis rather than a fixed architectural choice, and providing two integration variants (output-level vs. feature-level fusion) tailored to Gaussian policy parameterization and Q-value aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical finding is that 8 experts of width 512 (~20M parameters) outperform a single monolithic BRC network of width 4096 (~136M parameters) across DMC, MetaWorld, and HumanoidBench, suggesting conditional computation via sparse routing is a more effective scaling axis than width expansion in continuous-control RL. Open questions include whether these gains extend to pixel-based observations (the paper only evaluates low-dimensional proprioceptive states), whether scaling continues beyond 64 experts, and whether the MoE policy can be distilled into a compact network for real-time deployment. The added hyperparameter surface (N, K, gating network size, regularization weights) and the fact that JAX/XLA does not realize top-K compute savings (all experts are evaluated under JIT) are practical caveats for adoption.
