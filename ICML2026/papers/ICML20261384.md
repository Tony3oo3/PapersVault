## Problem

Existing federated test-time adaptation methods rely on local feature statistics (normalization parameters, prediction entropy, pseudo-labels) that become unreliable under severe and heterogeneous distribution shifts in open federated environments, where unseen clients have no access to source data or cross-client feedback. The paper identifies that while feature distributions vary substantially across clients, the pairwise relational structure among class prototypes remains remarkably stable, and exploits this invariant structure as a global prior for robust adaptation.

## Input / Output

At test time, an unseen target client receives the pre-trained global model, a C×C global Prototype Affinity Topology matrix, and C global class prototypes (all transmitted once during initialization with no further communication). The client processes unlabeled test images in streaming batches, updating only Batch Normalization affine/statistical parameters and the linear classifier head via entropy minimization on the fused posterior. The output is a per-sample class posterior distribution combining topology-aware non-parametric inference and parametric classifier predictions, from which the final class label is extracted.

## Method

FedPAT constructs a global Prototype Affinity Topology (PAT) by aggregating ℓ2-normalized class prototypes from all source clients via sample-weighted averaging, then computing pairwise inner products to form a C×C affinity matrix encoding consensus inter-class relationships. During a lightweight validation phase, source clients refine BN parameters using a topology consistency alignment loss (Smooth L1 between batch-level affinities and the global PAT) plus an intra-class compactness loss, and the server aggregates updated BN statistics. At test time, a topology-aware non-parametric inference module computes prototype-based top-k posteriors and refines them via single-step graph diffusion over the global PAT (p_TNI = (1−α)p_proto + α·D⁻¹A_G·p_proto), which is theoretically a first-order Neumann-series approximation to the graph-regularized solution min_p ‖p−p_proto‖² + αpᵀL_rw p. This non-parametric prediction is fused with the parametric classifier output through a confidence-guided sigmoid gate, and entropy minimization on the fused posterior drives minimal parameter updates for robust adaptation.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-C (severity=3), FedPAT achieves 46.50% average accuracy across feature shift, label shift, and hybrid shift, outperforming the strongest federated baseline ATP (45.92%) and the best general TTA method MEMO (45.77%). Under random corruption severity, FedPAT reaches 75.63% on CIFAR-10-C and 45.41% on CIFAR-100-C, yielding consistent improvements of 0.40% and 1.73% over ATP respectively. On Tiny-ImageNet-C (200 classes), FedPAT achieves an average improvement of approximately 4% over baselines. Under the extreme DomainNet Real→Quickdraw domain gap, FedPAT achieves 62.96% accuracy, a +5.31% gain over the unadapted model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedPAT consistently outperforms all three federated TTA baselines: ATP (45.92%), FedSPL (42.99%), and FedCTTA (41.95%) on CIFAR-100-C severity=3, with the largest margin over FedSPL (+3.51%) which degrades severely under hybrid shift (32.36% vs FedPAT's 40.08%). Among general TTA methods, FedPAT surpasses MEMO (45.77%), T3A (45.07%), and BN-Adapt (45.06%), while SHOT (32.11%) and BBSE (26.03%) perform poorly under feature and hybrid shifts. Under random corruption severity on CIFAR-10-C, FedPAT (75.63%) edges out ATP (75.23%) and MEMO (73.09%), with a particularly strong advantage in label shift (81.81% vs 79.95% for ATP). On the more challenging CIFAR-100-C random severity setting, FedPAT (45.41%) leads ATP (43.68%) by 1.73%, while FedCTTA (40.24%) and FedSPL (40.60%) lag significantly, suggesting their noise-aggregation and contrastive mechanisms become unreliable at scale. The ablation confirms that removing the PAT component reduces performance to BN-Adapt level (64.22% vs 70.56% on CIFAR-10-C severity=5), validating that the topology prior is the primary source of the gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedPAT extends the federated test-time adaptation line initiated by ATP, FedSPL, and FedCTTA, which all rely on local instance-level statistics (normalization parameters, pseudo-labels, contrastive representations) for adaptation. Its key departure is identifying the Prototype Affinity Topology—a C×C matrix of inter-class cosine similarities—as a stable cross-client structural prior, shifting the adaptation signal from instance-level features to class-level relational geometry. The topology-aware diffusion mechanism is grounded in the graph-regularized learning framework of Zhou et al. (2003) and Zhu et al. (2003), recast as a first-order Neumann approximation to a graph-smoothed posterior. Prototype aggregation follows the federated prototype learning tradition (Tan et al., 2022a; Guo et al., 2024b), but repurposes prototypes not for classification per se but as nodes in a relational graph that guides test-time inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled alternative to local-statistics-driven TTA in federated settings, where unseen clients lack cross-client feedback to validate their adaptation signals. The empirical demonstration that PAT consistency (0.875) exceeds feature-distribution consistency (0.336) by 2.6× under severe corruption offers a concrete, reusable structural prior for any federated system with a shared label space. Limitations include modest absolute gains (1–4% over ATP), dependence on labeled source data for prototype computation, and the assumption of a fixed shared label space that may not hold in truly open-world deployments. Open questions include whether PAT can be estimated without labeled source data (e.g., via clustering) and whether the approach extends to open-set or incremental label scenarios.
