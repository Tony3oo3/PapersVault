## Problem

Prior PAC-Bayesian generalization guarantees for symmetric models required both compact symmetry groups and data distributions invariant under the group action—assumptions rarely met in practice (e.g., translations are non-compact, and real-world data are seldom invariant). This paper removes both restrictions, providing the first PAC-Bayesian generalization analysis that accommodates non-compact symmetries and non-invariant data distributions.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on diverse input modalities—2D images (MNIST, CIFAR-10/100), 3D volumetric shapes (ModelNet), particle jet data (Top Tagging), and 2D points (toy example)—each transformed by a corresponding group action (SO(2), SE(2), SO(3), R₊, SO⁺(1,3)) to construct symmetric data variants. Outputs are class labels or real-valued predictions. A key structural requirement is that the loss function must be G-invariant (e.g., squared loss under orthogonal groups), and the PAC-Bayes bound is evaluated on orbit representatives rather than the full data, making explicit data augmentation theoretically redundant in the equivariant setting.

## Method

The core mechanism is an averaging operator Q that projects arbitrary hypothesis functions onto the equivariant subspace by integrating over group orbits with respect to a conditional distribution derived from the Disintegration Theorem, replacing the Haar measure required for compact groups. A KL decomposition lemma then shows that pushing prior and posterior measures through Q strictly reduces their KL divergence (a data-processing inequality), tightening the complexity term in McAllester's bound. The main theorem (Theorem 4.3) further establishes that when the data-generating process is G-equivariant and the loss is G-invariant, symmetrization cannot increase true risk and both true and empirical risks are computable entirely on orbit representatives. The resulting bound simultaneously improves the KL term, the empirical risk evaluation, and the true risk, and the construction extends to other PAC-Bayes bounds (Catoni, Maurer, Tolstikhin–Seldin). Empirically, equivariant architectures (e2cnn, e3nn, Lorentz-equivariant GNNs) are trained by minimizing the PAC-Bayes bound with a cross-entropy surrogate, then evaluated with 0-1 loss, showing consistent KL reductions of 12–89% and tighter bounds across all tested datasets.

## Results

Across all seven dataset–group combinations, the equivariant (symmetric) posterior consistently reduces the KL divergence by 12.5%–89.2% and the McAllester PAC-Bayesian bound by 10.4%–34.2% relative to the non-equivariant baseline. Test risk is matched or improved in every setting, with the largest gains on ModelNet with the non-compact scaling group R₊ (41.5% risk reduction) and the toy example under SO(2) (34.8% risk reduction). The most pronounced KL reduction occurs on ModelNet with SO(3) (89.2%), while the tightest absolute bound is achieved on the toy example (0.463). These results are the first PAC-Bayesian guarantees established for non-compact groups (SE(2), R₊, SO⁺(1,3)) and non-invariant data distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between an equivariant (symmetry-respecting) posterior and a standard non-equivariant baseline posterior, both trained to minimize McAllester's PAC-Bayesian bound. The equivariant model wins on the PAC-Bayesian bound in every one of the seven settings, with reductions ranging from 10.4% (CIFAR-10, SO(2)) to 34.2% (ModelNet, R₊). On test risk, the equivariant model matches or beats the baseline in all cases; the largest risk improvements appear on ModelNet with the non-compact scaling group R₊ (41.5% reduction) and the toy example (34.8%), while the smallest gap is on CIFAR-100 with SE(2) (1.5%), where the authors note the baseline may already learn approximate symmetries from data. The KL divergence reduction—the primary driver of the tighter bound—reaches 89.2% on ModelNet with SO(3), confirming the theoretical prediction that projecting onto the equivariant subspace strictly reduces the complexity term. No prior PAC-Bayesian analysis could establish these advantages for the non-compact groups (SE(2), R₊, SO⁺(1,3)) or for non-invariant data distributions used here, making this the first such comparison in that regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC-Bayesian generalization analysis of equivariant models initiated by Lyle et al. (2020) and Elesedy & Zaidi (2021), which were restricted to compact symmetry groups and data distributions invariant under the group action. The key technical departure is the use of the Disintegration Theorem to replace the Haar measure (available only for compact groups) with a conditional probability kernel over the group, yielding a generalized averaging operator that works for non-compact groups such as translations and scalings. The KL decomposition lemma from Lyle et al. (2020) is applied to this new averaging operator, and the resulting tighter complexity term is plugged into McAllester's bound, with the authors noting the argument extends to other PAC-Bayes formulations (Catoni, Maurer, Tolstikhin & Seldin).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first PAC-Bayesian generalization guarantees for equivariant models under non-compact symmetries and non-invariant data distributions, closing a gap that left the most practically important symmetry (translation, underlying all CNNs) without theoretical backing. The free-action assumption (Assumption 3.1) and the requirement that the hypothesis class exactly match the data's equivariance structure remain limitations that may not hold in all practical settings. Open questions include whether the results extend to non-free group actions (e.g., rotations with fixed points), whether tighter bounds from Catoni or compression-based PAC-Bayes yield even more informative guarantees in the equivariant setting, and whether the theoretical advantage translates to measurable sample-efficiency gains in large-scale training.
