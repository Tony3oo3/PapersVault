## Problem

Conditional diffusion models appear capable of compositional generalization—generating convincing samples for out-of-distribution combinations of conditioners—but the mechanisms underlying this ability remain unclear. Prior work on local score functions (Kamb & Ganguli, 2024; Niedoba et al., 2024) only addressed unconditional and class-conditional settings, leaving open whether local mechanisms are relevant to the flexible, variable-length conditioners central to compositional generalization. This paper asks: what structural property of the score function is both necessary and sufficient for a single conditional model to learn the compositional structure needed to generalize to novel conditioner combinations?

## Input / Output

In the CLEVR experiments, the model takes 128×128 noisy images plus variable-length conditioners—either a 16×16 integer grid counting objects per cell (grid-style) or a padded array of embedded xy-locations (list-style)—and must generate images at test-time with more conditioned locations than seen in training (e.g., trained on 1–3 objects, tested on 9). For SDXL, the analysis hooks hidden-state activations (post-FFN outputs of BasicTransformerBlocks) from the U-Net given text prompts. Beyond generated images, the paper produces quantitative locality metrics: pixel Jacobian heatmaps, per-conditioner finite-difference gradient magnitudes, and cosine similarity matrices measuring feature-space disentanglement across semantic categories.

## Method

The core contribution is a proven equivalence (Lemma 3.3) between Conditional Projective Composition (CPC)—a conditional distribution that factorizes into independent marginals over disjoint pixel subsets, each depending on exactly one condition—and Local Conditional Scores (LCS), where the score at each pixel depends only on a local pixel neighborhood and a sparse subset of relevant conditioners. This equivalence holds at all noise levels and is extended to feature-space (F-CPC/F-LCS), where a necessary-but-not-sufficient orthogonality heuristic (Lemma 3.5) based on cosine similarity of mean conditional-unconditional score differences enables empirical testing. Empirically, CLEVR models that length-generalize exhibit strong conditional locality at high noise while failing models do not, and a direct causal intervention—modifying the architecture to process images as independent patches each conditioned only on the subset of locations within that patch—restores length generalization in a previously failing model. In SDXL, pixel-space shows spatial locality but largely lacks conditional locality, whereas feature-space hidden states near the U-Net bottleneck exhibit quantitative F-LCS structure with disentanglement ratios of 1.4–1.6× (within-category cosine similarities 0.41–0.47 vs. between-category 0.27–0.33).

## Results

In location-conditioned CLEVR models trained on 1–3 objects, the grid-style conditioner labeling all objects (Exp. 1) length-generalizes to K_max = 7, while the single-object grid conditioner (Exp. 2) reaches only K_max = 2 and the list-style conditioner (Exp. 3) reaches K_max = 3. A causal intervention enforcing a local patch-based architecture (Exp. 2L) restores length generalization to K_max = 9 under the same single-object conditioning that previously failed. In SDXL, the F-LCS disentanglement heuristic (Lemma 3.5) yields within-category cosine similarities of 0.41–0.47 versus between-category similarities of 0.27–0.33, giving disentanglement ratios of 1.4–1.6× in feature-space, compared to 1.42× in pixel-space and 1.31× in latent/VAE-space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external baselines; instead it contrasts three conditioning architectures under identical EDM2 U-Net training to isolate the effect of conditioner design on compositional generalization. Exp. 1 (grid-style, all objects) succeeds at length generalization (K_max = 7) while Exp. 2 (grid-style, single object) and Exp. 3 (list-style, all objects) both fail (K_max = 2 and 3 respectively), showing that the same underlying data can yield compositional or non-compositional behavior depending on how conditioners are structured. The key causal comparison is Exp. 2 vs. Exp. 2L: both use identical training data and single-object conditioning, but Exp. 2L's explicitly local patch-based architecture achieves K_max = 9 versus Exp. 2's K_max = 2, directly demonstrating that enforcing local conditional scores causes compositional generalization. Similarly, Exp. 3L (local intervention on the list-style conditioner) improves from K_max = 3 to 9. In SDXL, feature-space representations are more disentangled than both pixel-space (ratio 1.42) and latent/VAE-space (ratio 1.31), with the per-layer disentanglement profile peaking near the U-Net bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the local score framework of Kamb & Ganguli (2024) and Niedoba et al. (2024)—which explained creativity in unconditional and class-conditional diffusion—to flexible compositional conditioners, and specializes the projective composition formalism of Bradley et al. (2025) from multi-model score combination to a single conditional distribution. The key conceptual departure is proving an exact equivalence between conditional projective composition (a compositional factorization of the data distribution) and local conditional scores (sparse pixel- and conditioner-dependencies in the score function), a connection that was only implicit in the object-centric representation learning literature (Brady et al., 2023; Wiedemer et al., 2023). The paper further extends this equivalence to feature-space (F-CPC/F-LCS), linking it to disentanglement, and provides the first causal test of local scores as a mechanism for compositional generalization via an architectural intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CPC↔LCS equivalence gives a precise, testable criterion for when a conditional diffusion model has learned compositional structure, and the causal intervention (Exp. 2L) provides direct evidence that local conditional scores are not merely correlated with but can cause length generalization. However, the evidence is confined to a synthetic CLEVR setting with simple location conditioners; the SDXL analysis is explicitly preliminary, showing feature-space disentanglement but no direct compositional generalization test. Major open questions include whether the framework explains compositional generalization in real-world text-to-image models, how to practically exploit F-LCS structure for interventions (requiring learning time-dependent feature-space transforms), and whether the equivalence holds under the approximations inherent in finite-capacity networks.
