## Problem

Existing jailbreak methods operate within a prompt-optimization paradigm that retains detectable malicious semantic signals, or within a static decomposition paradigm that requires the attacker to possess domain expertise a priori and fails entirely if any single sub-query is blocked. The paper identifies a deeper, overlooked vulnerability: the highly interconnected structure of an LLM's internal knowledge allows harmful objectives to be reconstructed from sequences of individually benign sub-queries, a threat that current guardrails are not designed to detect.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language harmful objective and a black-box target LLM API as input, and produces a synthesized harmful response as output. Unusually, the harmful objective is never directly queried; instead, the attack agent (Qwen3-32B-abliterated) generates a sequence of locally innocuous sub-queries whose responses are treated as revealed internal knowledge fragments. The final output is assembled by a synthesizer module that aggregates these fragments along the explored tree path, rather than being elicited in a single prompt.

## Method

CKA-Agent reformulates jailbreaking as an adaptive, tree-structured exploration of the target LLM's latent knowledge DAG, where each node is a query-response pair representing a correlated knowledge fragment. At each iteration, a UCT policy selects the most promising leaf node, then a depth-first expansion loop generates adaptive branches of innocuous sub-queries conditioned on prior responses; a hybrid evaluator (combining introspection and target-feedback scores) assigns immediate rewards to new nodes. A synthesizer aggregates the trajectory into a candidate answer, and an online judge verifies success; on failure, a negative penalty is backpropagated to discourage revisiting unproductive branches. This simulation-free, feedback-driven design eliminates reliance on attacker domain expertise (the target model serves as a knowledge oracle) and enables multi-path rerouting when specific edges are blocked, achieving >95% success rates against strongly aligned commercial LLMs.

## Results

CKA-Agent achieves 96–98% Full Success (FS) on both HarmBench and StrongREJECT across all four target models (Gemini-2.5-Flash/Pro, GPT-oss-120B, Claude-Haiku-4.5). On the most heavily guarded model, Claude-Haiku-4.5, prompt-optimization methods collapse dramatically (PAIR drops from 90.5% to 3.2% FS; Vanilla from 15.1% to 0.8%), while CKA-Agent maintains 96.0% FS on HarmBench and 96.9% on StrongREJECT. CKA-Agent outperforms the strongest decomposition baseline, Multi-Agent Jailbreak, by 15–21 percentage points in FS across all models and datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CKA-Agent is compared against 11 baselines spanning prompt-optimization attacks (POAs) and decomposition attacks (DAs). Among POAs, PAIR and TAP are the strongest on less-secure models (81–87% FS on Gemini-2.5-Flash) but collapse to 3–12% FS on Claude-Haiku-4.5, while CKA-Agent holds steady at 96% FS. AutoDAN reaches 92.1% FS on Gemini-2.5-Pro but drops to 0.8% on Claude-Haiku-4.5; X-Teaming and ActorBreaker (multi-turn agentic methods) similarly degrade to 0–8% FS on the strongest guardrails. The closest competitor is Multi-Agent Jailbreak (static decomposition), which sustains 76–82% FS across all models but is outperformed by CKA-Agent by 15–21 percentage points, confirming that adaptive, feedback-driven decomposition is critical. In the appendix, CKA-Agent also outperforms DAMON (dialogue-aware MCTS, 65.4% FS) and guardrail-evasion methods (FlipAttack, JAM, JAIL-CON) on StrongREJECT with GPT-oss-120B. Standard input-level defenses (Llama Guard-3, mutation-based rephrasing) and representation-level defenses (Circuit Breaker) reduce POA success substantially but remain largely ineffective against CKA-Agent, which maintains >92% FS even under the strongest tested defense.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both dominant jailbreak paradigms: prompt-optimization attacks (GCG, AutoDAN, PAIR, TAP) that encode malicious intent in a single prompt, and static decomposition attacks (Multi-Agent Jailbreak, segmented prompting) that rely on the attacker's a priori domain expertise to pre-plan a fixed query sequence. CKA-Agent instead formulates jailbreaking as an adaptive, simulation-free tree search over the target model's internal correlated knowledge, borrowing the UCT selection policy from Monte Carlo Tree Search (Kocsis & Szepesvári, 2006) but replacing random rollouts with direct target-model queries scored by a hybrid evaluator. The key conceptual departure from prior decomposition work is that the search tree is constructed dynamically from the target's responses rather than from the attacker's priors, enabling multi-path exploration and recovery when individual sub-queries are refused.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a structural vulnerability in current alignment: even when the model has full conversation history (single-session setting), it cannot aggregate individually benign sub-queries into a coherent harmful intent, maintaining 92%+ attack success. This suggests that alignment training optimizes for atomic prompt-level detection rather than long-horizon intent reasoning, a gap that no current input-level defense (Llama Guard, mutation-based filters) or representation-level defense (Circuit Breaker) adequately addresses. Open questions include whether this limitation is architectural (inherent to transformer attention over long contexts) or a training-data artifact, and whether session-level intent-tracking mechanisms can be made robust enough to close the gap without degrading legitimate multi-turn use.
