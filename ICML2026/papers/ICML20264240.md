## Problem

Conventional test-time adaptation (TTA) evaluations assume unbounded processing time, reporting only mean accuracy while ignoring the accuracy-latency trade-off that determines whether a prediction is actually useful in latency-sensitive deployments. Prior temporal evaluation (Alfarra et al., 2024) addressed only a single discrete scenario using model-relative speed proxies, leaving practitioners without a systematic way to assess which TTA method fits their specific temporal constraints. This paper asks: how do method rankings change under realistic, physically-grounded temporal pressure, and why?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264240/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes pre-trained image classifiers (ResNet-50, ViT-B/16, ResNet-18) and streams of unlabelled RGB image batches (size 64) from ImageNet-C corruptions at severity 5, with batch arrival times governed by a chosen inter-arrival interval γ. It produces decomposed time-contingent utility scores—availability × served accuracy (discrete), accuracy × hyperbolic responsiveness (continuous), and adapted/frozen accuracy mix (amortised)—along with per-method rankings across 16 temporal scenarios per corruption. All timing is measured in wall-clock milliseconds on a specified hardware platform (Nvidia RTX 4080 or Raspberry Pi 5), with a 6σ provision on baseline latency λ to target five-nines availability.

## Method

Tempora defines three time-contingent utility metrics, each decomposable to isolate distinct failure modes. Discrete utility (U = α · ā_served) models asynchronous streams with hard deadlines via a buffered discrete-event simulation where batches arriving while the pipeline is occupied are evicted, making availability α an emergent outcome rather than a predetermined penalty. Continuous utility (U = ā · κ̄ + Cov(a,κ)) models interactive settings with a greedy user, applying hyperbolic decay κ_i = (1 + d_i/(T−λ))⁻¹ to discount accuracy by effective response delay d_i relative to an HCI threshold T. Amortised utility (U = β · ā_adapt + (1−β) · ā_frozen) models budget-constrained deployments where cumulative overhead c_i = δ_i − λ exhausts a budget B, after which the model freezes at state θ_m. The key design choice is grounding all temporal parameters in physical units (ms) rather than model-relative proxies, enabling consistent simulation of exogenous constraints (e.g., a 50 ms deadline) across methods and hardware. Applied to 11 TTA methods across 750+ evaluations, the decompositions reveal three failure modes—availability collapse, accuracy erosion, and harmful adaptation—and identify three necessary properties for deployable adaptation: corruption-conditioned compute allocation, time-aware scaling, and anytime performance.

## Results

Across 240 temporal evaluations (9 TTA methods × 15 ImageNet-C corruptions × 16 scenarios), rank instability is pervasive: CMF, the majority offline winner (10/15 corruptions), achieves first rank in only 29/240 evaluations (12.1%) and underperforms standard inference in 79 cases (32.9%). Under the strictest discrete pressure (ρ=100%), AdaBN achieves 30.8% utility (97.2% availability × 31.7% served accuracy) versus CMF's 11.5% (25.1% availability × 46.0% served accuracy), a 12.1-point gap driven entirely by availability. ETA, which never wins any offline corruption, becomes the majority temporal winner with 103/240 evaluations (42.9%), mainly in mildly relaxed settings (35%≤ρ≤70%, T≥100 ms, 8≤B≤16 s). Spearman correlation with offline rankings ranges from r_s=−0.75 (brightness, T=50 ms) to r_s=0.97 (ρ=25%), confirming that temporal pressure—not corruption type—drives the instability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 11 Fully TTA methods (AdaBN, LAME, NEO, Tent, ETA, SHOT-IM, SAR, DeYO, CMF, SPA, ZeroSIAM) plus standard inference as a non-adaptive baseline. Offline, CMF and DeYO dominate (CMF wins 10/15 corruptions, DeYO the remaining 5), with mean accuracies of 49.1% and 48.8% respectively versus 18.2% for standard inference. Under temporal pressure, the ranking inverts: gradient-free methods AdaBN (30.8%) and NEO (22.1%) outperform all gradient-based methods at ρ=100%, while ETA (48.4% accuracy) is discounted to 7.2% utility at T=50 ms due to 85% responsiveness loss. Under amortised evaluation at B=1 s, most gradient-based methods collapse to 0.1% frozen accuracy (utility <1%), except SHOT-IM which retains 32.2% frozen accuracy due to its source running-statistics retention, yielding the highest gradient-based utility (32.2%). The key finding is that no single method dominates across all temporal scenarios—AdaBN/NEO win under strict pressure, ETA under moderate pressure, and CMF/DeYO only when pressure is relaxed—making method selection inherently deployment-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Alfarra et al. (2024), which first penalised slow TTA methods via a discrete sample-starvation protocol, by grounding temporal constraints in physical units (ms) rather than model-relative proxies and by introducing two additional protocols (continuous and amortised) alongside a revised discrete one with buffering. It also draws on Ghunaim et al. (2023)'s analogous sample-starvation penalty in online continual learning, but departs from both by treating the arrival rate as an exogenous deployment parameter (e.g., a camera's frame rate) rather than a function of the model's own speed. The evaluation targets the standard Fully TTA methods (Tent, ETA, CMF, etc.) that the field has benchmarked exclusively under offline, unbounded-time assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the majority offline winner (CMF) loses 87.9% of temporal evaluations and that a method winning zero offline corruptions (ETA) becomes the majority temporal winner fundamentally challenges how the field selects and designs TTA methods. The three identified failure modes (availability collapse, accuracy erosion, harmful adaptation) and the corresponding design properties (corruption-conditioned compute allocation, time-aware scaling, anytime performance) provide concrete targets that current methods lack. Limitations include single-seed evaluation, episodic i.i.d. sampling that disadvantages methods designed for non-stationary streams (e.g., LAME), and scope to image classification; open questions include whether corruption-conditioned overhead estimation is feasible without supervision and how to combine the three metrics for richer deployment dynamics.
