## Problem

Existing multi-subject video generation methods that concatenate reference subject tokens with noisy video latents suffer from two coupled failures: cross-frame identity drift and spatial identity entanglement, because dense self-attention indiscriminately mixes all subject features into every spatial region. Additionally, the quadratic attention cost grows with the number of reference subjects, making multi-subject generation computationally prohibitive as subject count increases.

## Input / Output

The method takes N reference images (one per subject, encoded via the same 3D VAE as the video) and a free-form text prompt describing the scene and inter-subject interactions. It produces an 81-frame video at 480×832 resolution where each subject maintains consistent identity across all frames. A key design constraint is that the number of reference subjects is variable at inference time (up to M=10), yet the computational cost remains nearly constant regardless of N, unlike baselines whose runtime scales with subject count.

## Method

DiasR builds on Wan 2.1-14B-I2V-480P (flow-matching DiT) and introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, Dual-Modal Identity-Anchored Alignment uses learnable identity queries (up to 10) projected into both visual and textual spaces to create per-subject anchors; during training, the visual similarity scores (from self-attention) and textual attention maps (from cross-attention, blocks 5–25) are each supervised against ground-truth subject masks via an MSE loss (L_mask), enforcing subject-specific cross-modal semantic alignment. Second, a Sparse Routing Strategy pre-estimates a per-token subject relevance score using mean-pooled key descriptors, applies Gumbel-Softmax top-1 routing to assign each video token to exactly one subject, and computes attention within subject-specific buckets (query tokens attend to video keys plus only their assigned subject's keys), eliminating (N−1)·h·w redundant tokens per query and converting variable-length conditioning into a constant-length representation. The model is trained in two stages (10K + 2K steps on 64 A800s) on the newly constructed MuSA-2M dataset of 2M video-text-subject-mask quadruples with pixel-level subject annotations.

## Results

On the OpenS2V-Eval benchmark (180 test cases), DiasR achieves the best TotalScore of 57.79%, surpassing the strongest open-source baseline HuMo-17B (56.22%) and the closed-source Kling 1.6 (56.32%). DiasR leads on the two identity-consistency metrics—FaceSim (60.66%) and NexusScore (47.40%)—by a clear margin over all baselines. In the human preference study (30 participants, 40 cases), DiasR is selected as most preferred for Identity Consistency (72.75%) and Interaction Naturalness (52.42%). Computationally, DiasR maintains nearly constant one-step inference time as the number of reference subjects grows, unlike baselines whose runtime and FLOPs scale rapidly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiasR outperforms all open-source baselines on TotalScore, with the largest gaps over Phantom-14B (+2.41%) and Kaleido-14B (+1.96%). On the two identity-consistency metrics that are central to multi-subject generation, DiasR leads decisively: FaceSim reaches 60.66% versus 55.37% (HuMo-17B) and 47.97% (Kaleido-14B), and NexusScore reaches 47.40% versus 44.08% (VACE-14B) and 41.29% (HuMo-17B). Against the strongest closed-source system Kling 1.6, DiasR wins TotalScore (57.79% vs 56.32%) and both identity metrics, but trails on NaturalScore (74.60% vs 82.52%), which the authors attribute to Kling's training on large-scale real-world data. On text fidelity (GmeScore), DiasR (66.60%) is slightly below VACE-1.3B (71.26%) and Phantom-14B (70.65%), indicating a modest trade-off. In the human preference study, DiasR is the top choice for Identity Consistency (72.75%) and Interaction Naturalness (52.42%), while ranking second on Video Quality (42.58%) and Text Fidelity (29.25%). Computationally, DiasR's sparse routing yields nearly constant inference time as the number of reference subjects increases, whereas HuMo, Phantom, VACE, and Kaleido all show rapid runtime and FLOP growth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context conditioning paradigm for DiT-based subject-driven video generation (FullDiT, SkyReels-A2, HuMo, Phantom, VACE, Kaleido), where reference image latents are concatenated with video latents and identity is injected via self-attention. It departs from this line in two key ways: (1) introducing learnable identity queries with dual-modal (visual + textual) mask supervision to explicitly anchor per-subject identity rather than relying on implicit in-context learning, and (2) replacing dense full-sequence attention with a subject-level Gumbel-Softmax routing and bucketed attention mechanism, drawing on sparse-attention ideas from SVG, HiStream, and MoC but applying them at the subject granularity rather than token or frame level. The MuSA-2M dataset with subject-level masks also fills a gap left by existing benchmarks like OpenS2V-5M and HOIGen-1M, which lack per-subject paired annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-constant inference time as the number of reference subjects grows is a practically significant efficiency gain for multi-subject generation pipelines, and the MuSA-2M dataset (2M annotated quadruples) provides a much-needed training resource for the field. However, the method is currently limited to 2–3 subjects in training, and the authors acknowledge that 4+ subject scenarios remain unmodeled due to data scarcity. Open questions include whether the top-1 routing generalizes to highly overlapping subjects (e.g., crowds), whether the mask supervision loss transfers to out-of-domain styles, and whether the sparse routing can be combined with other DiT acceleration techniques (quantization, caching) without compounding quality loss.
