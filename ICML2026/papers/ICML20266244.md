## Problem

Existing voice deepfake detectors rely on general-purpose speech foundation models (Wav2vec, WavLM, HuBERT) whose representations are not optimized for capturing generative artifacts, and downstream finetuning has reached diminishing returns. The paper demonstrates that the standard masked token prediction pretraining objective, which uses discrete quantized targets, discards the fine-grained acoustic information needed to distinguish real from synthetic speech, as confirmed by mutual information analysis showing near-zero correlation between quantized targets and deepfake labels.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes raw 16 kHz audio waveforms, which during pretraining are corrupted by a 10% learnable mask at the CNN output and an additional 15% 2D (time × feature) zero-mask applied independently at every transformer layer. The primary output is a layer-averaged bottleneck embedding vector that serves as a universal representation; downstream, this is fed to a 2-layer MLP for utterance-level real/fake scores, applied per time step (20 ms resolution) for frame-level localization, or used directly for source-category embedding separability. The generative branch additionally outputs a complex velocity field over a 512-point FFT spectrogram (hop 160, window 400) conditioned on the bottleneck latent.

## Method

Alethia is pretrained with a dual-branch self-supervised recipe. In the predictive branch, a student encoder (24- or 48-layer Transformer with a 7-layer CNN front-end) processes a masked waveform and produces a layer-averaged bottleneck embedding, which is projected to predict six evenly-spaced raw (non-quantized) teacher layers from a frozen WavLM-Large or Wav2vec-XLSR-1B using combined L1 and cosine losses computed over all time steps rather than only masked positions. In the generative branch, an Optimal Transport Conditional Flow Matching (OT-CFM) decoder, conditioned on the same bottleneck embedding, learns to predict the velocity field between a noisy spectrogram and the clean target, with noise-to-data pairing determined by the Hungarian algorithm to enforce straight-line probability paths. The key architectural insight is the bottleneck design: by averaging all student layers before projecting into the multi-layer teacher space, the model reconstructs diverse levels of acoustic and semantic information without being bound to the teacher's per-layer capacity. Ablations confirm that the continuous (non-discrete) targets are essential for preserving deepfake traces, and that the flow-matching branch is specifically critical for detecting artifacts from modern diffusion- and flow-matching-based synthesizers, where it yields a 5.6-point EER improvement over the predictive-only variant.

## Results

On the SDD-Eval-50 benchmark (50 speech deepfake detection datasets), Alethia-Large achieves an average EER of 5.2% and accuracy of 93.3%, outperforming the strongest baseline W2V-1B (EER 6.0%, Acc 91.9%). On the challenging subset (datasets where W2V-1B underperforms the mean), Alethia-Large reduces EER to 11.5% and raises accuracy to 81.2%, compared to W2V-1B's 13.2% EER and 78.2% accuracy. In zero-shot singing voice deepfake detection (SVDD), Alethia-Large achieves 10.8% EER, surpassing the in-domain finetuned CtrSVDD baseline (13.8% EER) without any singing-voice training data. For source tracing on ASVspoof5-ST, Alethia-Base reaches 98.8% classification accuracy with frozen embeddings, far exceeding WavLM-Large (78.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Alethia-Large outperforms all four SFM baselines on the primary SDD task. Against the closest competitor W2V-1B (same 1B parameter scale), Alethia-Large reduces EER by 0.8 pp overall and by 1.7 pp on the challenging subset, while gaining 3.0 pp accuracy on challenging datasets. The gap widens substantially against smaller models: Alethia-Large beats WavLM-Large by 2.8 pp EER and 7.4 pp accuracy, and HuBERT-Large by 6.2 pp EER and 9.3 pp accuracy. On zero-shot SVDD, Alethia-Large (10.8% EER) surpasses W2V-1B (13.2%) and even the in-domain finetuned CtrSVDD baseline (13.8%), demonstrating that the pretraining recipe captures generation artifacts invariant to vocal type. For partially fake speech localization, Alethia's zero-shot frame-level EER (19.0% average) far exceeds in-domain finetuned baselines that degrade to near-random on unseen datasets (35.5–36.3% average). In source tracing, Alethia-Base achieves 98.8% accuracy with a frozen encoder, compared to 78.9% for WavLM-Large and 51.0% for HuBERT-Large, indicating markedly richer source-discriminative embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant masked token prediction paradigm (Wav2vec2, HuBERT, WavLM) by replacing discrete quantized targets with continuous multi-layer teacher embeddings predicted through a bottleneck architecture, drawing on the self-distillation lineage of Data2vec2 and JEPA. It further introduces a flow-matching (OT-CFM) spectrogram reconstruction branch as a complementary generative objective, addressing the well-documented failure of prior predictive+generative combinations (e.g., UniWav) to maintain discriminative performance. The CNN feature extractor + Transformer encoder architecture and learnable masking follow the Wav2vec2 design, while the specific combination of bottleneck embedding prediction with conditional flow matching for deepfake-specific pretraining is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first foundational encoder purpose-built for voice deepfake tasks, and its 56-dataset, 5-task benchmark (SDD-Eval-50) is the largest to date, providing a critical reference point for the community. The MI analysis demonstrating that quantized targets carry negligible deepfake-relevant information (≤0.212 vs ≥0.563 for phonetic labels) offers a clear mechanistic explanation for why standard SFMs struggle with generalization, and the finding that the generative branch specifically benefits detection of modern diffusion/flow-matching deepfakes (5.6% EER) while leaving traditional methods unchanged suggests a path toward future-proofing detectors. Open questions include whether the 19k-hour pretraining corpus is sufficient, whether the approach extends to other modalities (e.g., music deepfakes), and whether the bottleneck+flow-matching recipe can be further scaled or simplified.
