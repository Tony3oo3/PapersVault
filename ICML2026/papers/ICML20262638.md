## Problem

LLM-based social agents rely on static personas that produce rigid, stereotypical behaviors, while existing optimization methods (offline RL, external planners, prompt optimizers) assume stationary reward distributions that break down in multi-agent social simulation where opponents co-adapt and induce persistent reward shifts. The paper addresses the absence of a principled online framework for dynamically selecting interaction strategies under this inherent non-stationarity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262638/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each dialogue turn, the method consumes the running dialogue history (encoded via a frozen Qwen3-Embedding-8B model into a 4096-dim context vector), the agent's fixed base persona, and a pool of 12 predefined strategy instructions (each pre-embedded as an augmented-persona vector). It outputs a single selected strategy instruction that is textually concatenated to the persona to form the prompt context for the frozen LLM, which then generates the agent's next utterance. The per-turn scalar reward (normalized average over 7 Sotopia dimensions) serves as the bandit feedback signal.

## Method

ALSO formulates per-turn strategy selection as an adversarial multi-armed bandit problem, where each arm is a strategy instruction and the EXP3-inspired exponential-weights distribution (Eq. 12) provides randomized selection robust to shifting opponent behaviors. A lightweight 2-layer MLP surrogate (hidden size 512, GELU) predicts expected rewards for all arms by operating on the concatenation of precomputed augmented-persona embeddings and the current dialogue context embedding, enabling dense score estimates and generalization across semantically related strategies from sparse per-turn feedback. Score smoothing via exponential decay (λ=0.9) ensures recent interactions dominate, tracking non-stationarity without assuming reward stationarity. The underlying LLM remains entirely frozen; only the surrogate is updated online via MSE loss on a replay buffer, making the approach sample-efficient and requiring no LLM fine-tuning or external optimizer calls.

## Results

On Sotopia-Hard, ALSO achieves an Overall score of 3.53, improving +16.60% over the Vanilla baseline (3.02) and +2.92% over the strongest baseline INSTINCT (3.43). The largest gain is on the Relationship dimension, where ALSO scores 2.43 versus 1.32 for Vanilla (+83.79%) and 2.16 for INSTINCT (+12.59%). On Sotopia-All, ALSO ranks first in Overall (3.89 vs. 3.85 for INSTINCT, +0.98%) and Knowledge (6.14 vs. 6.09, +0.73%). The method requires no LLM fine-tuning or external optimizer calls, relying solely on a lightweight online surrogate updated per turn.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALSO outperforms all three online prompt-optimization baselines (OPRO, EvoPrompt, INSTINCT) on Overall score in both Sotopia-All and Sotopia-Hard. Against INSTINCT (the strongest online baseline), ALSO gains +0.98% Overall on Sotopia-All (3.89 vs. 3.85) and +2.92% on Sotopia-Hard (3.53 vs. 3.43), with the largest margin on Relationship (+12.59% on Hard). Against OPRO and EvoPrompt, the gaps are wider: ALSO leads by +5.42% and +2.67% Overall on Sotopia-Hard respectively. In the supplementary offline comparison (Table 15, Qwen-7B self-play), ALSO also surpasses Sotopia-Ω (DSI, Overall 3.51) and Think-on-Your-Feet/AMPO (Overall 3.54) with an Overall of 3.58, despite requiring no offline training data. The gains are most pronounced in challenging, high-conflict scenarios (Sotopia-Hard), where static personas and offline-optimized strategies struggle most with non-stationary opponent adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALSO builds directly on the EXP3 exponential-weights bandit algorithm (Lattimore & Szepesvári, 2020) for randomized arm selection, but departs from standard bandit formulations by explicitly rejecting stationarity assumptions and instead adopting an adversarial online learning perspective motivated by co-evolving multi-agent dynamics. It also draws on the neural surrogate idea from INSTINCT (Lin et al., 2024b) for generalizing sparse feedback across semantically related arms, but repurposes it within an adversarial setting with score decay to track non-stationarity. The paper positions itself against two prior paradigms for social intelligence: offline optimization (Sotopia-π, Sotopia-RL, SDPO) and offline-trained external planners (Sotopia-Ω, DAT, EPO), arguing that both are ill-suited to the non-stationary, strategically adaptive nature of multi-turn social interaction where optimal strategies shift as opponents co-adapt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ALSO addresses a genuine gap in LLM-based social simulation: static personas produce homogeneous, rigid behaviors, and existing optimization methods assume stationary reward distributions that break down when opponents adapt. The framework's practical appeal lies in requiring no LLM fine-tuning or external optimizer calls—only a lightweight 2-layer MLP trained online—making it a low-overhead drop-in for existing social simulation pipelines. However, several open questions remain: the paper explicitly avoids claiming formal regret guarantees, the strategy space is limited to 12 hand-designed instructions (with performance degrading at 24+ arms), and the two-agent setting leaves multi-agent (N>2) dynamics unexplored. Whether the adversarial framing provides genuine robustness advantages over simpler non-stationary bandit approaches (e.g., sliding-window or discounted UCB) is not isolated in the ablations.
