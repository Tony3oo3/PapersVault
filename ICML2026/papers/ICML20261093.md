## Problem

Existing bottleneck identification methods for hierarchical RL rely on topological analysis of explicit state-transition graphs (e.g., betweenness centrality, cut-based measures), which limits scalability to high-dimensional or continuous state spaces and prevents integration with function approximation. This paper asks whether bottleneck structure can be captured directly through value functions, enabling a graph-free, scalable bottleneck metric that also serves as a principled basis for option discovery.

## Input / Output

The method consumes transition tuples (s, a, s') collected under a random-walk or RND exploration policy, along with a set of random reward signals generated either via QR-orthogonalized Gaussian vectors (tabular) or Random Fourier Features on a learned encoder (continuous/high-dimensional). It produces per-state VPS bottleneck scores and a set of 2k dual options (k toward-bottleneck and k away-from-bottleneck), each defined by an initiation set (full state space), a greedy intra-option policy, and a termination condition that fires when the option's Q-value drops to zero or below. In the Atari setting, the output is an aggregated VPS heatmap over 84×84 grayscale frames evaluated on human-gameplay trajectories, with no option policies trained or executed.

## Method

The core contribution is Value Power Strength (VPS), defined as the expected squared value difference between a state and its policy-induced successors, which the paper shows is a degree-normalized analogue of electrical node power via a Bellman–Kirchhoff correspondence (value ↔ potential, weighted reward ↔ external current, Bellman operator ↔ grounded Laplacian). Random rewards are generated by QR-orthogonalizing Gaussian samples (tabular) or by random linear combinations of RFF features on a shared encoder (continuous), and a spectral decomposition (Theorem 1) shows that averaging VPS over isotropic random rewards amplifies low-frequency Laplacian eigendirections, naturally highlighting coarse bottlenecks. Options are then constructed from dual potential-difference intrinsic rewards r_int = ξ(φ(s′) − φ(s)) with ξ ∈ {+1, −1}, trained via Q-learning with a zero-continuation-value mechanism so that each option terminates automatically when continuation has non-positive value. For function approximation, a shared encoder feeds a value head (trained with TD targets) and a VPS head (trained to regress the squared value gap from the target network), and the online estimator's almost-sure convergence is proven under standard Robbins–Monro conditions.

## Results

The paper's results are presented primarily as learning curves and heatmaps rather than a single numeric table. In MiniGrid-KeyLock, VPS options consistently improve state coverage and Q-learning convergence speed over Primitive, Random Option, and Eigenoption baselines (Figures 4b–c). In PointMaze-FourRooms, SAC with VPS-assisted exploration achieves higher mean evaluation reward than both vanilla SAC and SAC with DIAYN-assisted exploration on the sequential waypoint task (Figure 8b). In Atari 2600 (Venture, PrivateEye, MontezumaRevenge, Freeway), aggregated VPS peaks across eight estimators reliably align with interpretable bottleneck frames such as doorways and traffic-crossing states (Figures 9, 18–20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Primitive baseline (no options), VPS options improve state coverage and accelerate Q-learning in MiniGrid-KeyLock, and increase random-walk task completions in Taxi-v3. Compared to Random Options (Gaussian-potential targets), VPS options produce more semantically meaningful behaviors anchored to bottleneck states rather than arbitrary extrema. Against Eigenoptions (Machado et al., 2017), VPS maintains bottleneck-focused semantics even as the number of options grows, whereas eigenoptions become heterogeneous (mixing room centers, edges, and passages) at higher k; VPS outperforms eigenoptions in MiniGrid-KeyLock coverage and Taxi-v3 random-walk completions, though the paper notes VPS does not always improve final Q-learning performance in Taxi-v3. In the PointMaze-FourRooms downstream evaluation, VPS options outperform DIAYN reward-free skills (Eysenbach et al., 2019) in mean evaluation reward, indicating that bottleneck-oriented temporal abstraction is more useful than diversity-oriented skills for this sequential waypoint task. In Atari, no option policies are trained or executed; the comparison is qualitative, validating that VPS identifies the same bottleneck frames a human player navigates through.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Laplacian/spectral option-discovery lineage (Eigenoptions, Machado et al. 2017; proto-value functions, Mahadevan 2005) by replacing explicit eigenvector computation with a value-function-based bottleneck metric (VPS) derived from the Bellman-Kirchhoff current-law analogy. It departs from graph-topological bottleneck methods (betweenness centrality, cut-based measures) by operating entirely through value estimation, making it graph-free and compatible with function approximation. The use of QR-orthogonalized and RFF random rewards is borrowed from auxiliary-task representation learning (Farebrother et al., 2023; Lyle et al., 2021), but here the random rewards serve a diagnostic role—probing the value landscape to construct a scalar bottleneck score—rather than providing learned features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VPS offers a principled, interpretable route to option discovery that scales beyond tabular domains without explicit graph construction, and the Bellman-Kirchhoff correspondence provides a clean theoretical bridge between circuit theory and MDP value functions. However, the computational cost of many reward probes limits practicality, and the paper itself notes that VPS options do not always improve final performance (Taxi-v3), leaving open whether bottleneck-oriented temporal abstraction consistently accelerates learning in complex tasks. A natural next step identified by the authors is combining VPS with successor representations to amortize computation across reward probes, which could substantially improve scalability.
