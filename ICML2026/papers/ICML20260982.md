## Problem

In large-scale platform settings with structured treatments, certain covariate-treatment combinations are structurally impossible under the production policy, creating a support mismatch that no amount of reweighting or sample-size increase can repair. The paper shows that under this treatment-induced structural non-overlap, standard weighted-loss fusion of RCT and observational data provably fails to satisfy randomized identifying moment conditions, and that the infeasibility is irreducible in the original covariate space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260982/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two data streams—large-scale observational logs (X, T, Y) and smaller RCT samples (X, T, Y)—along with known RCT assignment probabilities p_k, where T may be multi-valued or combinatorial (hundreds of features in the ride-hailing application). It jointly outputs a learned representation φ(X) that coarsens the covariate space to restore approximate overlap, and a predictor m_θ(φ(X), T) that yields the conditional response function μ_t(x). The representation is not a generic embedding but is optimized endogenously to balance overlap recovery (IPM discrepancy ε_ov) against outcome-relevant information preservation (ε_info), with both quantities appearing explicitly in the risk bound.

## Method

The core framework formulates RCT-OBS fusion as a constrained program: minimize observational risk subject to exact satisfaction of randomization-induced moment conditions E_{P_r}[(D_k − p_k)(Y − m(X,T))] = 0, which constrain only treatment-specific causal increments while remaining agnostic about baseline outcome structure. Under conditional structural non-overlap, the paper proves a strictly positive feasibility gap in the original covariate space (Theorem 3.6), motivating joint learning of a representation φ that coarsens X to restore approximate moment feasibility (Corollary 3.7). Training proceeds via an augmented Lagrangian primal-dual scheme that alternates gradient descent on (θ, φ) with gradient ascent on the dual variable ν, with a Wasserstein-based overlap regularization term. The paper establishes an oracle inequality decomposing excess risk into a quadratic feasibility term (moment violation + residual overlap), a statistical term, and optimization suboptimality, and proves a separation result showing weighted-loss fusion cannot enter the feasible region for any non-degenerate tuning parameter α.

## Results

On synthetic data with n=5,000 samples, the proposed method (Dual Loss + Wasserstein) achieves the highest Qini coefficient across all three overlap regimes: 0.5284 (large), 0.5499 (moderate), and 0.5137 (minimal), with the lowest standard deviation as overlap deteriorates. On the large-scale ride-hailing application (n=10,000,000 per dataset), the method attains the best Qini on all four datasets (e.g., 0.66075 on Dataset 3) and the best MAPE on three of four (e.g., 0.02883 on Dataset 2), outperforming the production baseline, OBS-only, and RCT-only models. The method's Qini advantage over the strongest baseline (T-Learner) grows from ~0.04 in the large-overlap setting to ~0.42 in the minimal-overlap setting, confirming robustness under severe conditional structural non-overlap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates all seven baselines on Qini (treatment-effect ranking) across every overlap setting. Against the strongest competitor, T-Learner, the Qini gap widens from 0.037 (large overlap) to 0.419 (minimal overlap), demonstrating that the advantage is most pronounced precisely where structural non-overlap is most severe. Representation-learning baselines (TARNet, CFRNet, DragonNet) all suffer dramatic Qini collapse under minimal overlap (dropping to 0.02–0.04), while the proposed method retains 0.51. Experimental Grounding (Kallus et al., 2018) and Integrative R-Learner (Wu & Yang, 2022) exhibit catastrophic MSE inflation (6–19 and 2–6 respectively) under non-overlap, confirming the paper's theoretical claim that extrapolation-based and orthogonal-loss methods fail when support is structurally absent. Yang2022 Elastic Integrative achieves the lowest MSE in the minimal-overlap setting (0.4254) but at the cost of a much lower Qini (0.3414 vs. 0.5137), illustrating the trade-off between predictive fit and causal validity that the constrained formulation resolves. On the real-world ride-hailing data, the method outperforms the production baseline by 0.01–0.03 Qini and reduces MAPE by up to 80% (0.02883 vs. 0.14563 on Dataset 2), matching or exceeding RCT-only performance while using far fewer experimental samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant RCT-OBS fusion paradigm (weighted-loss combination, orthogonal learning, experimental grounding) by reframing randomization as hard moment constraints rather than a soft loss term, and by introducing representation learning as an endogenous feasibility-recovery mechanism rather than a standalone balancing objective. It builds conceptually on the representation-learning-for-causal-inference line (TARNet, CFRNet, DragonNet) but explicitly shows that those methods, trained without encoding experimental identification, provably fail under structural non-overlap. The constrained primal-dual formulation with augmented Lagrangian draws on classical constrained optimization, while the feasibility-gap and oracle-inequality analysis is novel to this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable separation between weighted-loss fusion and the constrained estimator (Theorem 3.4) identifies a fundamental limitation of the most common practical approach to RCT-OBS integration, with direct implications for platform teams who currently tune a single mixing weight. The real-world ride-hailing validation (10M samples, matching RCT-only performance with far fewer experimental samples) suggests substantial cost savings in experimental design. Key limitations include the reliance on conditional (not marginal) non-overlap, strong regularity assumptions (quadratic growth, Lipschitz moments), and validation on a single platform; open questions include scalability to truly combinatorial treatment spaces and whether the feasibility-information trade-off (Theorem B.15) can be tightened or bypassed with richer representation classes.
