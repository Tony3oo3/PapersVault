## Problem

Existing 4D LiDAR generation methods are either computationally prohibitive (dense 3D/4D voxel or Gaussian splatting representations) or lack explicit temporal modeling (rangeview-based 2D approaches), making it difficult to produce large-scale, temporally coherent outdoor scenes. No prior work provides a compact, hierarchical representation that simultaneously disentangles static geometry from dynamic motion while remaining efficient enough for scalable generative modeling over unbounded environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265084/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw sparse LiDAR point cloud sequences (e.g., 64-beam Velodyne HDL-64E scans) that are first decomposed into static background and dynamic foreground via annotations or heuristic clustering, then encoded into multi-resolution volumetric scaffold grids (L=3 levels per node). For controllable generation, it additionally accepts scene-graph layouts or text prompts as conditioning. The output is a temporally coherent 4D LiDAR point cloud sequence rendered via raycasting from the reconstructed UDF surface, along with dense 3D point clouds and optionally auto-labeled semantic categories (vehicle/pedestrian) for downstream training.

## Method

HieraScaffold operates in three stages. First, a 4D dynamic scaffold factorizes the scene into five volumetric feature cubes (four spatio-temporal D^{1-4} for dynamic objects and one purely spatial D^{5} for static background), each learned via a transformer-based neural field that predicts unsigned distance values and their gradients; static geometry is self-supervised through a Chamfer-based surface-movement consistency loss, while dynamic geometry is supervised by retrieved object meshes. Second, a Neural Contourlet Representation (NCR) compresses each scaffold through a learnable Laplacian pyramid (redundancy minimization across K levels) followed by a directional filter bank at the final level (core disentangling), yielding a compact latent pair (C_final, F_final) that reduces representation complexity from 4N³ to N³/128. Third, a Hierarchical Recoupling Generation (HRG) pipeline uses a diffusion transformer to first generate the coarse volume, then a detail predictor refines fine coefficients conditioned on the coarse output; static and dynamic scaffolds are recoupled progressively via a cross-attention mapping (dynamic queries attend to static keys/values), and large-scale scenes are assembled through parallel 2×2 block-chunk generation with adaptive stopping based on boundary UDF ratios.

## Results

On KITTI-360, HieraScaffold achieves FRD of 979.3, MMD_BEV of 1.98×10⁻⁵, and JSD_BEV of 0.037, outperforming the closest competitors LiDMs (1012.5 / 2.64×10⁻⁵ / 0.049) and RangeLDM (1074.9 / 3.07×10⁻⁵ / 0.045) by 3.3%, 25.0%, and 17.8% respectively. On Waymo Open, the gains are larger: FRD 1087.4 vs. LiDMs 1251.3, and JSD_BEV 0.059 vs. LiDMs 0.129 (a 54.3% reduction). For temporal coherence, HieraScaffold reduces average ICP energy to 0.009 m and outlier percentage to 5.38%, improving over LidarDM (0.014 m / 7.12%) by 35.7% and 24.4% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HieraScaffold outperforms all eight baselines across both datasets on every reported metric. Against the closest generation competitors LiDMs (CVPR'24) and RangeLDM (ECCV'24), it achieves 3.3–8.9% lower FRD, 25.0–35.5% lower MMD_BEV, and 17.8–24.5% lower JSD_BEV on KITTI-360, with even larger margins on Waymo (54.3% JSD reduction over LiDMs). The rectified-flow method R2Flow (ICRA'25) is the second-closest on KITTI (FRD 1047.3 vs. 979.3) but still trails by 6.5% in FRD and 10% in JSD. LidarDM (ICRA'25), which separates static and dynamic branches, shows substantially worse generation quality (FRD 1423.2 on KITTI) and higher temporal ICP energy (0.014 m vs. 0.009 m), confirming the benefit of the unified hierarchical scaffold. For controllable generation on nuScenes (Table 4), HieraScaffold (Ours-sg) beats LiDARCrafter (AAAI'26) on both JSD (2.87 vs. 3.11) and MMD (0.39 vs. 0.42) while also being faster (3.1 s vs. 3.6 s per frame). In reconstruction (Table 1), HieraScaffold surpasses K-Planes (CVPR'23) by 24.0% in Chamfer Distance on KITTI-360 and 21.7% on Waymo, and outperforms NSKR/UltraLiDAR (CVPR'23) by a wide margin, validating the 4D scaffold over static 3D or 2D-plane representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

HieraScaffold builds on the K-Planes/HexPlane lineage of 4D spatiotemporal decomposition but replaces 2D latent planes with volumetric 3D scaffold nodes, arguing that 2D projections cause feature entanglement for sparse LiDAR geometry. It incorporates unsigned distance field learning (DCUDF, CAP-UDF) for surface reasoning and adapts the contourlet transform (Do & Vetterli, 2005) into a learnable Neural Contourlet Representation for frequency-domain compression, drawing on WaveGen's finding that most wavelet coefficients can be discarded. The hierarchical recoupling generation pipeline departs from prior diffusion-based LiDAR generators (LiDMs, RangeLDM, LidarDM) by explicitly separating static and dynamic components and generating them in a conditioned, coarse-to-fine order rather than as a single dense tensor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in autonomous driving: generating large-scale, temporally coherent 4D LiDAR scenes without the computational cost of dense 4D voxel grids. The NCR compression (reducing 4.7M parameters to 37.8k with negligible quality loss) and block-chunk generation strategy make scalable outdoor scene synthesis feasible, and the data augmentation result (Sim10k+1k outperforming Real10k on segmentation and detection) demonstrates direct utility for downstream perception tasks. Key limitations include dependence on existing LiDAR datasets for training, boundary discontinuities from unscanned regions, and the need for dynamic object annotations (though a GT-free pipeline is outlined). Open questions include whether the scaffold representation generalizes to indoor or non-vehicle-centric environments and how the method scales to even larger, multi-city scenes.
