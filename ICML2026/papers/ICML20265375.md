## Problem

Modern time series forecasting models achieve low observation-space error yet learn latent representations that are temporally disordered and lack continuity—a phenomenon the authors term Latent Chaos. This arises because the dominant observation-space paradigm minimizes pointwise errors on noisy, partially observed data, encouraging shortcut fitting of low-order statistical patterns rather than recovery of underlying system dynamics. The paper asks whether a training paradigm can explicitly guide models toward temporally coherent latent dynamics instead of merely optimizing observation-space accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time series window X ∈ R^{C×L} (C variables, L lookback steps) and produces a future forecast Ŷ ∈ R^{C×T} over horizon T. Unusually, the backbone never directly sees or predicts raw observations: each time step is first mapped through a frozen point-wise AutoEncoder into a latent state z_t ∈ R^D, the backbone predicts the future latent sequence Ẑ_Y ∈ R^{D×T}, and a frozen decoder maps Ẑ_Y back to observation space. The latent dimension D can differ from C (e.g., 321→512 on Electricity), and the AutoEncoder is pre-trained with MAE reconstruction before being frozen for the entire forecasting training phase.

## Method

LatentTSF is a two-stage paradigm that shifts TSF from observation regression to latent state prediction. Stage 1 pre-trains a point-wise AutoEncoder (2-layer MLP encoder, 1-layer decoder) to project each observation vector into an expanded latent state space, then freezes both E and D. Stage 2 trains a standard TSF backbone (iTransformer, DLinear, PatchTST, etc.) to map historical latent states Z_X to future latent states Ẑ_Y, supervised entirely in latent space by a joint objective: L_Pred = ‖Z_Y − Ẑ_Y‖²_F (magnitude accuracy) plus L_Align = 1 − cos(Z_Y, Ẑ_Y) (directional consistency). Theoretically, L_Pred is derived as a variational surrogate for maximizing I(Z_Y; Ẑ_Y) under a Gaussian noise assumption, while L_Align is motivated as a non-contrastive surrogate for maximizing I(Y; Ẑ_Y) inspired by InfoNCE. Because the target encoder is frozen, the alignment loss regresses toward a stationary, high-rank reference, structurally excluding representation collapse without negative samples, stop-gradient, or EMA—unlike SimSiam or BYOL.

## Results

LatentTSF is a training paradigm applied to six existing TSF backbones (CMoS, DLinear, PatchTST, TimeBase, TimeXer, iTransformer) across six benchmarks (ETTm1, ETTm2, ETTh1, ETTh2, Traffic, Electricity), reporting averaged MSE and MAE over horizons {96, 192, 336, 720}. The paradigm yields consistent improvements: for example, iTransformer on Electricity drops from MSE 0.268/MAE 0.375 to 0.194/0.299, PatchTST on Traffic improves from MSE 0.982 to 0.719, and TimeXer on ETTh1 improves from MSE 0.485 to 0.432. Gains are largest on high-dimensional datasets (Electricity: 321 vars, Traffic: 862 vars) and at longer horizons (T=720), while improvements on low-dimensional ETT datasets (7 vars) are smaller but still positive. One notable exception is DLinear on Traffic, where MSE slightly worsens (0.525→0.552).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LatentTSF is not a new architecture but a training paradigm applied to each backbone, so the comparison is each model's standard observation-space training versus the same model trained with LatentTSF. **iTransformer** benefits consistently across all six datasets (e.g., Electricity MSE 0.268→0.194, ETTh1 0.485→0.441), with the largest relative gain on ETTh1 (−9.1% MSE). **DLinear** shows the most dramatic single improvement on ETTh2 (MSE 0.500→0.363, −27.4%) and strong gains on Electricity (0.201→0.182), but is the one backbone that slightly degrades on Traffic (0.525→0.552). **PatchTST** sees the largest absolute MSE reduction on Traffic (0.982→0.719, −26.8%) and Electricity (0.389→0.207, −46.8%), suggesting it was most affected by Latent Chaos in its original form. **TimeXer** improves substantially on ETTh1 (0.485→0.432) and Traffic (1.270→0.636), indicating its exogenous-variable attention mechanism benefits from a cleaner latent target. **CMoS** and **TimeBase** show moderate but consistent gains across all datasets, with CMoS achieving the best absolute MSE on Electricity after LatentTSF (0.187). Overall, 35 out of 36 dataset–backbone combinations improve in MSE, confirming the paradigm's broad applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant observation-space forecasting paradigm in TSF, where all major backbones (iTransformer, PatchTST, DLinear, TimeXer, CMoS, TimeBase) directly regress future observations using point-wise losses. It draws on information-theoretic representation learning (InfoNCE, mutual information maximization) and non-contrastive alignment (SimSiam, BYOL) to motivate a latent-space training objective, but fundamentally repositions the forecasting target from observation-space values to learned latent states via a pre-trained AutoEncoder. Unlike prior work that treats latent representations as auxiliary regularizers (e.g., TimeAlign, Glocal-IB) or adds structured losses in observation space (patchwise losses), LatentTSF shifts the entire prediction and supervision into the latent space, making the latent dynamics the primary learning objective rather than a byproduct.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of Latent Chaos—where accurate predictors exhibit temporally disordered internal representations—raises a legitimate concern about generalization and robustness that the field has not systematically addressed. The practical value is that LatentTSF is a backbone-agnostic, drop-in training paradigm requiring no architectural changes, which broadens its applicability. However, gains are modest on low-dimensional data (ETT family, ~7 variables) where the observation space is already close to the true state space, and the theoretical justification for the alignment loss is explicitly acknowledged as a heuristic surrogate rather than a strict variational bound. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to other time series tasks (classification, anomaly detection, imputation), whether the AE pre-training can be made end-to-end without destabilizing the latent manifold, and whether the benefits persist under distribution shift or non-stationary regimes beyond the tested benchmarks.
