## Problem

Existing LiDAR anomaly detection methods learn exclusively from visible measurements and treat occlusion as a fixed, uninformative byproduct of sensing. The paper identifies a fundamental identifiability gap: under single-view observations, anomaly-induced structural missingness is inseparably entangled with normal occlusion patterns, so the model cannot distinguish whether unobserved regions arise from benign geometry or from abnormal perturbations, leading to overly permissive notions of normality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265400/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single LiDAR point cloud (N points in R⁴ with 3D coordinates and intensity) as input and produces per-point anomaly scores plus object-level panoptic segmentation masks (Recall, SQ, RQ, UQ, PQ). During training, each scene is augmented with physics-conformed synthetic anomalies to generate a paired factual/counterfactual observation set that differs only in occlusion-induced missing regions; at inference, only the factual scan is processed with no counterfactual construction required. Reconstruction supervision operates in the range-view domain (a dense angular-range grid aligned with LiDAR scanning geometry) rather than in sparse 3D space.

## Method

COVAL introduces Physics-Conformed Anomaly Construction (PCAC), which injects perturbed objects (scale, orientation, geometry, intensity) into normal scenes while respecting LiDAR line-of-sight and ground-contact constraints, then constructs a factual view (occlusion enforced) and a counterfactual view (occluded points retained) that share identical scene geometry and abnormal cause. Two complementary losses are applied on a shared backbone (MinkNet or Mask4Former3D): Visibility-Variant Counterfactual Reconstruction (VVCR) trains multi-scale reconstruction heads to predict counterfactual features at occluded locations from factual features in the range-view domain, explicitly modeling what is missing due to anomaly-induced occlusion; Visibility-Invariant Counterfactual Consistency (VICC) enforces L2 feature agreement at commonly visible points across the two views, encoding scene-intrinsic properties independent of visibility. The total objective combines a base segmentation loss with weighted VVCR and VICC terms, and anomaly scoring at test time uses a learned anomaly-class logit (AnomLogit) rather than max-logit confidence.

## Results

On the STU benchmark with MinkNet, COVAL (AnomLogit scoring) achieves AUROC 99.92, FPR@95 0.13, and AP 89.00 at the point level, and PQ 57.95, RQ 70.94, and UQ 55.48 at the object level. With Mask4Former3D, the method reaches AUROC 97.70, FPR@95 7.33, AP 62.16, and object-level PQ 22.47. The in-distribution mIoU on SemanticKITTI is maintained at 63.75, matching or exceeding all baselines, confirming no degradation of closed-set segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COVAL dominates all seven baselines on every reported metric. The closest competitor, REL (Li et al., 2025), achieves only AP 7.26 and FPR@95 40.96, whereas COVAL reaches AP 89.00 and FPR@95 0.13 — a roughly 12× improvement in AP and a 315× reduction in false positives. REAL (Cen et al., 2022) and Void Classifier (Blum et al., 2021) achieve moderate AUROC (94.02 and 93.55) but remain severely limited in precision (AP 4.18 and 1.04) and object-level recall (2.01 and 28.44 vs. 67.92 for COVAL). Deep Ensemble and Max Logits, which rely purely on uncertainty or confidence scoring without explicit anomaly supervision, plateau at AUROC ~92–93 with FPR@95 above 22, confirming that passive single-view learning cannot resolve the occlusion-entanglement problem. RbA (Nayal et al., 2023) is effectively non-functional on this 3D benchmark (AUROC 7.49, all object-level metrics at 0). MC Dropout (Srivastava et al., 2014), evaluated only with Mask4Former3D, is the weakest baseline overall (AUROC 65.76, AP 0.17). Notably, COVAL also matches or exceeds the best in-distribution mIoU (63.75), showing that the counterfactual training does not sacrifice closed-set segmentation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior LiDAR anomaly detection methods (REAL, REL, LiON, APF) that treat occlusion as a fixed, incidental property of the sensing process and learn exclusively from visible measurements. It repurposes the synthetic anomaly injection paradigm from LiON/REL but elevates it by introducing a counterfactual visibility intervention: for the same injected anomaly, paired factual and counterfactual observations are constructed that differ only in occlusion-induced missing regions. The two training objectives—visibility-variant reconstruction (drawing on range-view feature projection) and visibility-invariant consistency (drawing on invariant representation learning à la Arjovsky/Krueger)—are novel combinations applied to this specific identifiability problem, rather than extensions of a single prior method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete identifiability limitation in LiDAR anomaly detection—single-view observations entangle anomaly-induced structural effects with normal occlusion patterns—and provides a principled training-time intervention to disentangle them. However, the practical gains from the counterfactual visibility component specifically are modest (roughly 1–2 AP points over PCAC+AnomLogit alone), and the dramatic FPR@95 reduction is largely attributable to the AnomLogit scoring strategy rather than the visibility intervention per se. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the STU benchmark, whether physics-conformed construction is strictly necessary at scale, and how the method behaves for small or far-range anomalies where occlusion cues are weak (as acknowledged in the failure analysis).
