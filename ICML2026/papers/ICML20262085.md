## Problem

Amortized neural posterior estimators (NPEs) in simulation-based inference are brittle under model misspecification because they are trained under the prior-predictive distribution and degrade when observations deviate from it. Existing robust SBI methods address this by modifying the NPE training objective, incorporating observed data during training, or augmenting the simulator with error models, which couples robustness to the inference network and undermines the amortization and modularity that make NPEs attractive for repeated downstream queries.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a test dataset of N i.i.d. observations (time-series trajectories, 32×32 cryo-EM images, or tabular samples), a frozen pretrained NPE q_ψ(θ|s), and a pretrained amortized decoder mean embedding μ_ω(s) (a small fully-connected network mapping summary statistics to 512-dimensional random Fourier feature embeddings). It produces an adapted summary statistic s* via a deterministic L-BFGS optimization over the summary space, which is then passed to the unchanged NPE to yield a robust posterior q_ψ(θ|s*). A calibrated MMD threshold gates the adaptation so it only activates when misspecification is detected, preserving performance in the well-specified regime.

## Method

The core contribution is minimum-distance summaries (MDS): at test time, the summary statistic query to the NPE is adapted by minimizing the MMD between the summary-conditional data distribution P_{x|s} and the empirical distribution of observations, without touching the NPE itself. To avoid expensive density estimation, the MMD is approximated via random Fourier features, reducing the objective to a Euclidean distance between finite-dimensional mean embeddings: ||μ_ω(s) − (1/N)Σz(x̃_n)||². The decoder mean embedding μ_ω(s) is trained offline by MSE regression on the same training samples used for the NPE, making the entire pipeline decoupled from the inference network. At test time, L-BFGS optimizes this deterministic objective starting from the observed summary, yielding a lightweight adaptation step. The robustness guarantee (Theorem 4.1) follows from the bounded-kernel MMD's stability under Huber contamination, combined with posterior stability results from Sprungk (2020), showing that infinitesimal contamination induces only proportionally small KL changes in the resulting posterior.

## Results

Across all four evaluation tasks (bivariate Gaussian, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, SIR model, and cryo-EM), MDS consistently reduces posterior RMSE and MMD against uncontaminated observations compared to the standard NPE baseline under Huber contamination (ε up to 0.5). On the Gaussian task, MDS outperforms all robust baselines (NNPE, NPE-OR, NPE-RS) for moderate contamination (ε ≤ 0.3), though performance degrades at ε = 0.5. On the high-dimensional cryo-EM task (1024-d images), MDS substantially improves robustness despite the severe contamination of replacing images with pure Gaussian noise. The adapted MDS summary remains close to the oracle (uncontaminated) summary even when the objective is minimized in data-space, confirming that the alignment recovers the correct summary rather than overfitting to outliers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard NPE (Papamakarios & Murray, 2016), MDS wins on every task and every contamination level in terms of posterior RMSE and MMD, with the largest gains at moderate contamination (ε = 0.2–0.3). Against the Noisy NPE (NNPE) of Ward et al. (2022), which uses a spike-and-slab error model, MDS achieves comparable or better RMSE on the Gaussian and OUP tasks but underperforms NNPE on the SIR model, where the structural weekend-reporting misspecification is better captured by the explicit error model. Against the OC-SVM outlier-removal baseline (NPE-OR, Schölkopf et al., 2001), MDS consistently outperforms across all tasks, as the hard-thresholding approach is less effective than the soft MMD-based adaptation. Against NPE-RS (Huang et al., 2023), which regularizes the NPE training with an MMD term on observed data, MDS achieves lower posterior RMSE on the Gaussian task while preserving full amortization (no observed data needed at training time). A notable caveat is that the misspecification-detection gate can produce false negatives for small outlier shifts, causing a temporary performance dip in the Gaussian task before adaptation is triggered.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a post-hoc, test-time robustness method for pretrained neural posterior estimators (NPEs), departing fundamentally from existing robust SBI approaches (Ward et al. 2022, Huang et al. 2023, Elsemüller et al. 2025) that couple robustness to the training procedure via error models, modified objectives, or observed-data augmentation. Conceptually, it repurposes the minimum distance estimation framework (Wolfowitz, 1957) and MMD-based inference (Briol et al., 2019) to adapt the summary statistic query at test time, leveraging the bounded-kernel robustness of MMD (Chérief-Abdellatif & Alquier, 2022) and random Fourier features (Rahimi & Recht, 2007) for computational efficiency. The theoretical analysis builds on Sprungk (2020) for posterior stability and Briol et al. (2019) for MDS influence function bounds, but the key architectural insight—decoupling robustness entirely from the amortized inference network—is novel within the SBI literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular, post-hoc design preserves the central benefit of amortized SBI (reusing a single pretrained NPE across many queries) while adding robustness, which is practically important for scientific workflows where inference is repeated across diverse datasets. Theoretical guarantees (finite posterior influence under Huber contamination, consistency under correct specification) provide a principled foundation that many competing robust SBI methods lack. Key limitations include applicability only to summary-statistic-based NPEs (not full-data methods), sensitivity to MMD kernel choice in high-dimensional settings, and degradation under severe contamination (ε > 0.3). Open questions include extending MDS to other divergences (Stein, Fisher), other amortized SBI paradigms (neural likelihood estimation), and sequential NPE settings.
