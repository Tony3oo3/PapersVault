## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods designed for autoregressive LLMs suffer severe accuracy degradation (e.g., >16% drop with AWQ at W4A4) when applied to diffusion-based LLMs (DLLMs). This stems from three unaddressed conflicts: temporal distribution shifts across iterative decoding steps are not captured by standard calibration, quantization errors accumulate geometrically over denoising iterations, and the coexistence of fixed unmasked tokens with probabilistic masked tokens creates non-uniform feature distributions that uniform quantization strategies cannot handle.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained DLLM weights (LLaDA-8B, LLaDA-1.5-8B, DREAM-7B) and 128 calibration text segments from WinoGrande, which are processed through the model's iterative denoising pipeline to collect activation statistics across multiple time steps and masking ratios. It produces 4-bit quantized weights (per-channel symmetric) and activations (per-token asymmetric) in a plug-and-play format that can be layered on top of existing PTQ backends (AWQ, QuaRot, GPTQ, SmoothQuant, OSTQuant) without modifying their core algorithms.

## Method

DLLMQuant introduces three complementary techniques. Temporal-Mask Adaptive Sampling (TMAS) collects calibration data by proportionally sampling across time steps and four mask-ratio intervals (proportions [0.3, 0.2, 0.2, 0.3]), ensuring the calibration set represents the full temporal distribution shift inherent in iterative decoding. Interaction-Aware Activation Quantization (IA-AQ) identifies that the matmul between softmax output and the attention value matrix is the primary source of error accumulation across iterations; it redesigns the quantization error metric for the value matrix by using the quantized softmax output as a spatial weighting term, then searches over scaling factors (α from 1.0 to 0.8 in steps of 0.02) to minimize this weighted error. Certainty-Guided Quantization (CGQ) modifies the Hessian matrix used in GPTQ-style error compensation by element-wise multiplying activations with a mask-aware indicator (weight 1 for masked, 0.7 for unmasked) plus the square root of each token's final confidence score, thereby directing quantization error correction toward high-confidence masked tokens whose outputs propagate to subsequent iterations.

## Results

Under 4-bit weight and activation quantization (W4A4), DLLMQuant++ (QuaRot-based) achieves 54.29 average accuracy on LLADA-8B, 64.31 on LLADA-1.5-8B, and 61.90 on DREAM-7B, outperforming the strongest baseline QuaRot by 3.26, 3.25, and 3.05 points respectively. The gains are most pronounced on reasoning tasks: on GSM8K for LLADA-8B, DLLMQuant++ scores 56.25 versus QuaRot's 44.57 (+11.68) and AWQ's 36.88 (+19.37), and on HumanEval it reaches 28.92 versus QuaRot's 25.33. Inference speedup averages 1.6× with over 3.2× memory savings compared to FP16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DLLMQuant consistently outperforms all baselines across the three DLLMs. Against AWQ, DLLMQuant+ improves average accuracy by ~1.2–1.5 points (e.g., 49.26 vs 48.09 on LLADA), with the largest gap on GSM8K (+3.78) and HumanEval (+2.03). Against QuaRot, DLLMQuant++ yields a more substantial ~3.0–3.3 point average gain, driven primarily by reasoning tasks: GSM8K improves by 11.7 points on LLADA (56.25 vs 44.57) and 8.5 points on LLADA-1.5 (74.33 vs 65.86), while HumanEval gains 3.6 and 7.3 points respectively. Against GPTQ and SmoothQuant (Table 7), DLLMQuant variants add ~1.5–2.0 average points, with the advantage concentrated on GSM8K and HumanEval. Against OSTQuant, the strongest non-DLLMQuant baseline, DLLMQuant*+ still leads by ~0.8–1.0 average points. On the calibration sampling comparison (Table 6), TMAS-based calibration (RTN+TMAS) outperforms LLM-QAT's self-generated calibration by 2.69 points on GSM8K (18.12 vs 15.43) and 3.22 points on HumanEval (16.56 vs 13.34), confirming that temporal-mask-aware sampling is critical for DLLM quantization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-training quantization (PTQ) lineage—GPTQ's Hessian-based error compensation, AWQ's activation-aware smoothing, QuaRot's Hadamard rotation, and SmoothQuant's activation migration—from autoregressive LLMs to diffusion-based LLMs (DLLMs). The key departure is identifying three structural conflicts between DLLM mechanisms (iterative denoising with dynamic masking, bidirectional attention, and fixed-length sequence decoding) and assumptions baked into standard PTQ calibration and error-compensation strategies. Rather than proposing a standalone quantizer, DLLMQuant introduces three plug-and-play modules (TMAS, IA-AQ, CGQ) that layer on top of any existing PTQ base, positioning the work as an architectural adapter rather than a new quantization paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DLLMs (LLaDA, DREAM, DiffuLLaMA) are gaining traction for parallel decoding and reversal-curse mitigation, but their large FFN sizes and iterative inference make them harder to deploy than autoregressive counterparts; this paper's 3× memory reduction and 1.6× speedup at 4-bit with minimal accuracy loss directly addresses that bottleneck. The identification of the post-softmax attention matmul as the primary error-propagation chokepoint is a useful diagnostic that could inform quantization of other iterative or masked-generation architectures. Open questions include whether the techniques generalize to 2-bit or mixed-precision regimes, to multimodal DLLMs (LaViDa, LLaDA-V), and to DLLMs with different block/step configurations; the evaluation is also limited to three models of comparable scale, leaving open whether the findings hold for much larger or smaller diffusion LLMs.
