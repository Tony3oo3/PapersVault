## Problem

Most curriculum learning strategies for domain-specific LLM fine-tuning rely on static data ordering (e.g., easy-to-hard by perplexity or answer length) that is fixed before training and cannot adapt to the model's evolving knowledge. This rigidity is especially costly in data-scarce domains where every training sample must be maximally informative, and in RLFT where rapid entropy collapse further limits exploration. No prior work dynamically reorders training samples based on the model's instantaneous inference uncertainty during fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

EDCO takes a preprocessed domain-specific QA dataset (e.g., 12,000 synthesized question-answer pairs for communication domains, or public benchmarks like MedQA and JEC-QA) and a pre-trained LLM (Qwen3-4B or Llama3.2-3B) as input. At each curriculum update interval, the model performs forward passes using only the first 128 output tokens under a quick-answer prompt to estimate per-sample entropy, rather than generating full-length responses. The output is the fine-tuned model parameters after multiple cycles of entropy-based sample selection and optimization, compatible with both SFT (cross-entropy loss) and RLFT (GRPO with rule-based or LLM-verified rewards).

## Method

EDCO implements a dynamic reverse curriculum driven by inference entropy: at each training interval k, it computes a prefix-token entropy estimate for every sample in the dataset using Quick-Answer Prompting (which forces the model to begin its substantive answer within the prefix window) and selects the top-N highest-entropy samples to form the next training batch. The prefix entropy approximation reduces per-sample computation from O(T) to O(L) (L=128), achieving an 83.5% speedup while maintaining a Spearman rank correlation of 0.73 with full-sequence entropy. This creates a continuously adapting curriculum that prioritizes samples at the model's current capability frontier, preventing the entropy collapse that causes performance saturation in RLFT. The framework is agnostic to the fine-tuning algorithm and has been validated under both SFT and GRPO-based RLFT across communication, medical, legal, mathematical reasoning, and summarization tasks.

## Results

On the Datacom domain (Qwen3-4B, RLFT), EDCO achieves 46.96% accuracy, outperforming Random Sampling (40.43%) and Perplexity-based curriculum (44.78%). On MedQA (Llama3.2-3B, RLFT), EDCO reaches 36.7% accuracy versus 32.9% for RS and 24.6% for PPL. On JEC-QA, EDCO scores 17.4% compared to 16.2% for RS. The prefix-based entropy estimation reduces per-sample computation from 2.24s to 0.37s (83.5% reduction) while maintaining a Spearman rank correlation of 0.7333 with full-sequence entropy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDCO consistently outperforms all compared baselines across domains. Against the Perplexity-based curriculum (Hu et al., 2024), EDCO gains 2.2 points on Datacom RLFT (46.96% vs 44.78%) and a substantial 12.1 points on MedQA (36.7% vs 24.6%), indicating that static perplexity ordering is particularly harmful in specialized domains. Against DEITA (Liu et al., 2024), a strong static data-selection method, EDCO leads by 1.8 points on MedQA (36.7% vs 34.9%). Against DOTS (Sun et al., 2025), a recent dynamic difficulty-targeted method, EDCO achieves a 1.7-point margin on MedQA (36.7% vs 35.0%). Against SEC (Chen et al., 2025), a bandit-based learnable curriculum, EDCO outperforms by 12.2 points on Datacom (47.0% vs 34.78%), with SEC suffering from training instability during its policy learning phase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the inference-entropy signal from RL exploration literature (Cui et al., 2025) as a dynamic curriculum selection criterion for domain-specific LLM fine-tuning, departing from the static easy-to-hard ordering paradigm established by Kim & Lee (2024) and PPL-based methods. It extends the reverse-curriculum idea from RL (Florensa et al., 2017) to the fine-tuning setting and contrasts with SEC (Chen et al., 2025), which uses a bandit-based learnable policy but suffers from training instability. The efficient prefix-token entropy estimation and quick-answer prompting are novel engineering contributions that make the dynamic re-evaluation loop computationally feasible, building on the observation that initial-token uncertainty correlates with full-sequence uncertainty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners fine-tuning LLMs on scarce, expensive domain data, EDCO offers a drop-in curriculum strategy compatible with both SFT and RLFT that consistently outperforms static heuristics by 2–5 points across communication, medical, legal, math, and summarization tasks. The 83.5% reduction in entropy-estimation cost makes periodic curriculum updates practical at scale. Key open questions include whether the fixed update interval can be replaced by adaptive triggering, whether the approach extends to code generation and long-context tasks, and whether the entropy signal remains informative as base models grow larger and more capable.
