## Problem

Despite rapid diversification of proactive defense methods against deepfakes, the field lacks a unified evaluation protocol, with prior studies relying on incompatible metric choices, limited generator sets, and idealized settings that ignore real-world degradations like JPEG compression. This fragmentation masks critical vulnerabilities, including the overestimation of cross-model transferability and the conflation of generator-induced artifacts with genuine defensive efficacy.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 256×256 RGB facial images from CelebA-HQ (primary), FFHQ, and VGGFace2-HQ (validation), with face swap tasks additionally requiring source-target image pairs. Outputs are structured as a multi-dimensional scorecard per defense-generator pair spanning four metric categories (pixel fidelity via MSE, perceptual fidelity via LPIPS, identity disruption via the proposed CIDR, and visual quality via BRISQUE), supplemented by a relative robustness score (ROB) averaged over JPEG/blur/noise transformations and a transfer efficiency score (TE) averaged over all source-target generator pairs, with an overall Average Rank aggregating across all criteria.

## Method

The benchmark systematically evaluates seven proactive defenses (PGD, Disrupting, DF-RAP, Anti-Forgery, Latent Attack, SCOL, NullSwap) spanning white-box optimization, white-box model-based, and black-box paradigms against eight deepfake generators covering GAN, autoencoder, and diffusion architectures for both attribute manipulation and face swap. A key technical contribution is the Calibrated Identity Ratio (CIDR), which normalizes identity disruption by the generator's own baseline synthesis error L_ID(x, G(x)), thereby decoupling defensive efficacy from generator-induced domain shift. Robustness is quantified via a relative ratio comparing post-transformation disruption to clean disruption, while transferability is measured by the ratio of transferred disruption to native white-box disruption on the target generator. The paper further introduces Average Rank as a scale-free aggregation that avoids pitfalls of combining metrics with incompatible units or directions, and empirically demonstrates that fidelity and identity metrics capture orthogonal failure axes, that diffusion-based iterative denoising progressively attenuates pixel-level perturbations, and that peak white-box scores often signal overfitting rather than generalizable protection.

## Results

Across 8 deepfake generators (3 attribute manipulation, 5 face swap), no single defense dominates: PGD achieves the best AvgRank on GAN-based models (1.50 on StyleCLIP, 1.75 on StarGAN/SimSwap) but drops to 5.13 on DiffSwap and 4.50 on DiffFace. Black-box identity methods (SCOL, NullSwap) reverse this trend, achieving AvgRank 2.25–2.50 on diffusion-based generators where optimization-based methods fail. Robustness (ROB) is highest for methods with explicit robustness constraints (SCOL: 0.99, NullSwap: 0.99, Disrupting: 0.85–0.99), while PGD suffers the sharpest degradation (ROB 0.44–0.75). Transfer efficiency (TE) is moderate across all methods (0.29–0.69), with ensemble strategies (CMUA, LEAT) showing the smallest white-box-to-black-box performance gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGD (Madry et al., 2017) achieves the strongest disruption on GAN-based generators (AvgRank 1.50–2.00 on StarGAN, StyleCLIP, SimSwap, pSp-mix) but collapses on diffusion-based models (5.13 on DiffSwap, 4.50 on DiffFace), confirming that pixel-level optimization is attenuated by iterative denoising. Anti-Forgery (Wang et al., 2022) is the best performer on StarGAN (AvgRank 1.75, MSE 1.6180) yet is the worst on DiffAE (AvgRank 7.00, MSE 0.0020), revealing heavy dependence on specific generative mechanisms. Latent Attack (Kos et al., 2018) uniquely excels on diffusion-based face swap (AvgRank 2.25 on DiffSwap, 2.50 on DiffFace) by operating in latent space rather than pixel space. Black-box methods SCOL (Lee et al., 2025) and NullSwap (Wang et al., 2025) maintain competitive disruption on diffusion models (AvgRank 2.25–3.38) without generator access, and achieve the highest robustness (ROB ≥ 0.93), though they trail on GAN-based attribute manipulation. Ensemble strategies CMUA (Huang et al., 2022) and LEAT (Shim & Yoon, 2025) sacrifice 5–10% white-box MSE/CIDR relative to their single-source counterparts (PGD, Latent Attack) but substantially reduce the black-box transfer gap, e.g., CMUA improves CIDR by +0.05 on StyleCLIP and +0.04 on pSp-mix versus single-source PGD in the black-box setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first unified benchmark for proactive defense against deepfake generation, filling a gap analogous to what DeepfakeBench and similar efforts provided for deepfake detection. It synthesizes and standardizes the evaluation of a diverse set of prior defense methods (PGD, Disrupting, DF-RAP, Anti-Forgery, Latent Attack, SCOL, NullSwap) that were previously assessed under incompatible metric choices, limited generator sets, and idealized settings. The paper departs from prior work by introducing CIDR to correct generator-induced identity bias and by systematically coupling disruption, robustness, and transferability into a single evaluation protocol rather than treating them as separate concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes that no single defense method dominates across all generators and metrics, and that the field's reliance on isolated white-box disruption scores has masked severe performance collapse under post-processing and cross-architecture transfer. The identification of fidelity and identity metrics as orthogonal axes, and the demonstration that peak white-box performance signals overfitting, provide a concrete corrective for how the community should interpret and report defense effectiveness. Open questions remain about whether any defense can simultaneously achieve strong disruption, robustness, and transferability, and how the landscape will shift as generative architectures continue to evolve beyond the GAN and diffusion models evaluated here.
