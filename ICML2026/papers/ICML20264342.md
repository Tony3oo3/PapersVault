## Problem

Training a family of LLMs requires separate, expensive training runs for each size, and existing compression methods still need hundreds of billions of tokens per variant. Moreover, no prior framework supports elastic nesting for hybrid Mamba-Attention-MoE architectures, and static budget control forces the same model capacity across reasoning and answering phases despite their asymmetric computational demands.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained parent hybrid Mamba-Transformer-MoE reasoning LLM and a budget specification (target active parameter count or total parameter count, encoded as a one-hot vector) as input. It produces multiple nested submodel checkpoints (e.g., 30B/23B/12B or 12B/9B/6B) extractable zero-shot from a single elastic checkpoint, and at inference time generates reasoning text followed by a final answer, with the router dynamically selecting which nested submodel handles the thinking phase versus the answering phase based on the budget.

## Method

Star Elastic is a three-stage post-training pipeline: (1) importance estimation ranks embedding channels, attention/SSM heads, MoE experts (via REAP saliency), and FFN channels using activation-based scores, with iterative depth scoring via normalized MSE; (2) an elastic formulation defines nested weight-sharing along width axes (embedding dim, head counts, expert counts, FFN intermediate dims, with per-layer heterogeneity) and depth (binary layer-retention vector), where smaller submodels always retain the top-ranked contiguous components of larger ones; (3) elastic training uses a learnable two-layer router with Gumbel-Softmax that maps budget inputs to per-axis configuration choices, trained end-to-end via KL-divergence knowledge distillation from the frozen parent model plus a resource-matching router loss, under a two-stage curriculum (8K-context uniform budget sampling followed by 49K-context non-uniform sampling skewed toward larger budgets). The key distinction from prior compression is that architecture selection is optimized jointly with model accuracy through the training signal rather than determined post-hoc, and at inference the nested structure enables elastic budget control where a smaller model handles the high-volume thinking phase and a larger model handles the high-fidelity answering phase, advancing the accuracy-latency Pareto frontier.

## Results

On Nemotron Nano v3 (30B/3.6A), the full-size Elastic-30B variant matches or exceeds the parent model on most benchmarks (e.g., AIME-2025: 88.54 vs. 87.92, LiveCodeBench v5: 72.70 vs. 71.75, IFBench-instruct: 73.96 vs. 73.19), while the 23B (2.8A) and 12B (2.0A) nested variants remain competitive with Qwen3-30B-A3B. Elastic budget control (using a smaller model for thinking and a larger model for answering) advances the accuracy–latency Pareto frontier by up to 16% in accuracy and 1.9× in latency reduction. Training cost is reduced by 360× versus pretraining from scratch (110B vs. 40T tokens for Nano v2) and 7× versus Minitron-SSM (110B vs. 750B tokens), with up to 2.4× throughput speedup on H100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen3-30B-A3B (3.3A), Nano v3 Elastic-30B wins decisively on reasoning and instruction-following benchmarks (AIME-2025: 88.54 vs. 80.00, IFBench-prompt: 70.58 vs. 43.28, IFBench-instruct: 73.96 vs. 46.57) and on Tau-Retail/Tau-Telecom, but trails on MMLU-Pro (78.63 vs. 81.11) and Tau-Airline (43.33 vs. 52.67). Compared to the parent NanoV3-30B teacher, Elastic-30B matches or slightly exceeds on AIME-2025 (+0.62), LiveCodeBench (+0.95), IFBench-instruct (+0.77), Tau-Retail (+5.85), and Tau-Telecom (+2.34), while being marginally below on GPQA (−1.01) and MMLU-Pro (−0.23). For Nano v2, Elastic-9B outperforms the independently trained NanoV2-9B on AIME-2025 (75.42 vs. 71.43) but is slightly below on GPQA (62.50 vs. 63.01) and MMLU-Pro (73.45 vs. 73.61); Elastic-12B beats NanoV2-12B on AIME-2024 (83.44 vs. 82.90) and AIME-2025 (75.83 vs. 72.50) but lags on GPQA (63.25 vs. 65.28) and MMLU-Pro (76.20 vs. 78.47). Against Minitron-SSM, Star Elastic achieves a 7× reduction in total training tokens (110B vs. 750B) by eliminating the exploratory pruning phase entirely, while maintaining comparable or superior accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

Star Elastic extends the nested weight-sharing paradigm of MatFormer and Flextron (which targeted standard Transformers) to hybrid Mamba-Attention-MoE architectures, and builds on the pruning-and-distillation pipeline of Minitron/Minitron-SSM by replacing their two-stage explore-then-distill procedure with a single end-to-end router-guided training run. The learnable router with Gumbel-Softmax relaxation departs from prior methods that decouple architecture selection from training objectives, instead optimizing sub-network configurations directly from the distillation signal. The elastic budget control mechanism for reasoning phases (thinking vs. answering) is a novel application of the nested model family, with no prior work supporting dynamic per-phase model allocation in hybrid SSM-Transformer-MoE settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7× token reduction over prior compression (and 360× over pretraining) with constant deployment memory makes training and serving model families substantially cheaper, which is practically critical as hybrid Mamba-Attention-MoE architectures become the dominant efficient-LLM paradigm. The elastic budget control finding that a smaller model for thinking and a larger model for answering (M_S→M_L) optimally trades off token-volume cost against synthesis fidelity advances the accuracy-latency Pareto frontier by up to 16%/1.9×. Open questions include whether the approach scales to extreme compression ratios (beyond the current ~3×), whether task-specific elastic routing (auto-selecting configurations per input domain) can further improve efficiency, and whether cache reuse across nested models can be fully exploited once inference frameworks support it.
