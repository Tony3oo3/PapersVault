## Problem

Stability and almost sure convergence of two-timescale stochastic approximation (SA) were previously established only under i.i.d. noise, while realistic RL algorithms like TDC(λ) and actor-critic methods operate under Markovian noise. Prior attempts to extend to Markovian noise required restrictive assumptions—uncoupled parameters, compact noise spaces, or projection operators—that prevented application to representative RL algorithms. This paper closes the gap by establishing stability and convergence under Markovian noise without projections or compactness requirements.

## Input / Output

The framework takes two-timescale SA iterates (x_n ∈ R^{d1}, y_n ∈ R^{d2}) driven by a Markovian noise sequence {W_n} in a general (possibly unbounded, uncountable) space, with learning rates satisfying β(n)/α(n) → 0. It produces an almost sure boundedness guarantee (stability) and convergence of x_n to the equilibrium λ(y_n) and of the full iterate z_n to (λ(y*), y*). For the TDC(λ) application, the input is a finite-state MDP with a behavior policy, a target policy, and linear function approximation parameters (θ, ν), and the output is an almost sure convergent value function estimate without requiring projection of eligibility traces.

## Method

The core innovation is Lemma 3.1, which bounds the fast timescale iterate x_n by the running maximum of the slow timescale iterate y_n^max rather than by the current y_n, as all prior two-timescale SA works require. This weaker coupling condition is sufficient to combine the monotonic rescaling technique of Lakshminarayanan & Bhatnagar (2017) with the Markovian noise averaging of Liu et al. (2025b), since the rescaling factor r_n already uses the maximal scaling seen thus far. The proof proceeds in three stages: (1) fast timescale analysis via the ODE∞ method shows x_n ≤ K(1 + ‖y_n^max‖) a.s.; (2) slow timescale analysis uses this bound to control the fast iterates and establish full boundedness of z_n via a contradiction argument on the rescaling sequence; (3) convergence follows by showing the discretization error between the discrete iterates and the associated ODE trajectories vanishes, so the iterates converge to the invariant set of the limiting ODEs. The rescaling factor r_n is defined monotonically (r_n = max{1, r_{n-1}, ‖z(T_n)‖}), which is critical for upgrading subsequence convergence to full-sequence convergence via the sub-subsequence lemma.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical benchmarks. The headline results are: (1) Theorem 3.2 establishes almost sure stability (boundedness) of two-timescale SA under Markovian noise without any projection operator; (2) Theorem 3.3 establishes almost sure convergence of the iterates to the unique equilibrium, with the Markovian noise allowed to evolve in a possibly unbounded and uncountable space; (3) Theorem 7.2 provides the first almost sure convergence guarantee for TDC(λ) (temporal difference learning with gradient correction and eligibility traces) under off-policy learning with linear function approximation. The key methodological contribution is Lemma 3.1, which bounds the fast-timescale iterate by the running maximum of the slow-timescale iterate, enabling the combination of prior techniques that were previously incompatible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper is the only work that simultaneously handles all seven properties in Table 1: two-timescale dynamics, coupled fast-slow parameters, no projection, Markovian noise, and noncompact noise space. Borkar (2009) and Lakshminarayanan & Bhatnagar (2017) handle two-timescale SA but only under i.i.d. noise. Karmakar & Bhatnagar (2021) extends to Markovian noise but requires the two parameter sets to be uncoupled and the noise to evolve in a compact space. Liu et al. (2025b) handles Markovian noise with noncompact space but only for single-timescale SA. Panda & Bhatnagar (2025) handles two-timescale with Markovian and noncompact noise but requires additional projections to enforce boundedness. Consequently, none of the prior works applies to TDC(λ), which has coupled dynamics, Markovian noise, and unbounded eligibility traces; this paper is the first to establish its almost sure convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a synthesis of two prior lines of work: Lakshminarayanan & Bhatnagar (2017), which established stability for two-timescale SA under i.i.d. noise, and Liu et al. (2025b), which extended the ODE method to single-timescale SA under Markovian noise. The key methodological departure is the 'running max' technique (Lemma 3.1), which bounds the fast-timescale iterate by the maximum slow-timescale iterate seen so far rather than by the current slow iterate — a weaker condition that prior two-timescale analyses (Kushner & Yin, Mokkadem & Pelletier, Dalal et al., Panda & Bhatnagar) did not need but which becomes essential when combining the two prior frameworks. The paper also identifies a specific error in Chandak et al. (2025), whose almost sure convergence claim relies on the false implication that boundedness in expectation implies almost sure boundedness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first almost sure convergence guarantee for TDC(λ) under off-policy learning with linear function approximation, removing the need for projection operators or truncation that prior analyses (Yu 2017, Zhang & Whiteson 2022) required to handle unbounded eligibility traces. The result closes a long-standing gap in the theoretical understanding of two-timescale RL algorithms and provides a template applicable to actor-critic methods, average-reward gradient TD, and TD with target networks. Open questions include extending the analysis to convergence rates, applying the running-max technique to the functional central limit theorems of Borkar et al. (2025), and formal verification of the two-timescale ODE method in proof assistants.
