## Problem

Standard probabilistic scaling of LLMs assumes that larger models will eventually learn to generate valid quantum circuits, but this paper argues the premise is structurally wrong: the valid subset of n-qubit circuit designs decays exponentially (e^{-γn}) within the Hilbert space, so post-hoc filtering is computationally intractable and training on unverified code introduces a bias that makes larger models more confidently wrong (inverse scaling). The paper formalizes why the syntax-semantics gap in quantum programming is fundamentally wider than in classical code and proves that no amount of parameter scaling can close the coupled exponential cost of structural and functional verification.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a target unitary specification U_target ∈ SU(2^n), hardware constraints (coupling graph and gate set for NISQ; patch-layout adjacency, boundary-type matching, magic-state factory throughput, and code-distance budgets for FTQC), and algorithmic parameters (qubit count n, error tolerance ε, success probability targets). Output is a verified quantum circuit in a fixed intermediate representation, structured across three abstraction levels (module-level program, gate-level decomposition, elementary-operation sequence), accompanied by a rewrite trace that records which property-preserving rewrites were applied, why, and which alternatives were rejected — providing an audit surface for human specification authors rather than a bare circuit artifact.

## Method

The paper proposes a verifier-centric agent that formulates iterative circuit design as a Markov Decision Process over a discrete syntactic space, where each state is a design snapshot (circuit in IR, formal parameters, evaluation metrics, bookkeeping) and actions are tool invocations (EDIT with a property-preserving rewrite schema, EVAL, FINISH). The key mechanism is a validity filter V(a,s) — a composition of polynomial-time checks (e.g., no-overlap and no-obstacle for parallelization) — that restricts the admissible action set at each state, yielding a constrained policy π_θ(a|s) ∝ π_θ^(0)(a|s)·V(a,s) that multiplies the unconstrained LLM proposal by the mask. This is layered over a three-level abstraction hierarchy (module composition → gate decomposition → elementary rewrites) so that verification obligations are localized to small footprints rather than applied to the full unitary monolithically. The theoretical contribution proves that the expected cost of any generate-then-filter pipeline scales as O(e^{(ln2+γ)n}) for NISQ and O(e^{(max(ln2,β)+γ)n}) for FTQC, establishing that constructive verification during generation is not merely preferable but necessary. Training data is specified as trace-based corpora contrasting optimal solutions against valid-but-suboptimal candidates, encoding the optimization manifold rather than a binary correctness label.

## Results

This is a position paper with no empirical benchmark table; its primary quantitative contributions are theoretical bounds. The paper proves that the expected cost of a generate-then-filter pipeline scales as E[C]^NISQ = O(e^{(ln 2 + γ_NISQ)·n}) and E[C]^FTQC = O(e^{(max(ln 2, β) + γ_FTQC)·n}), where γ_NISQ = Ω(d·log(1/ε)) and γ_FTQC = Ω(d·log n), establishing that two (NISQ) or three (FTQC) independent exponential factors compound. The functional pass rate is bounded by P ≤ C_θ · e^{−γn}, and the FTQC structural pass rate is at most e^{−Ω(n)} unless NP ⊆ BPP (Proposition B.1). The only empirical number cited is from the authors' prior work: LLMs trained without data verification plateau at 79% accuracy on quantum circuit optimization tasks with no further gains from scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not run head-to-head experiments against named baselines; the `compares_to` list is empty. Instead, it positions its verifier-centric paradigm against three conceptual alternatives: (1) the probabilistic scaling hypothesis (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022), which the paper argues is structurally mismatched to quantum circuit synthesis due to the exponential sparsity of valid outputs; (2) generate-then-filter pipelines (Li et al., 2022; Shin et al., 2021), which the paper shows incur coupled exponential cost (Eq. 2) that no amount of model scaling amortizes; and (3) human-in-the-loop verification, which becomes infeasible beyond ~40–50 qubits (Dalzell et al., 2020; Arute et al., 2019). The paper contrasts its approach with existing systems—Qiskit Code Assistant (Dupuis et al., 2024) and AlphaTensor-Quantum (Ruiz et al., 2025)—noting that their success derives from domain-specific verification loops or curated datasets rather than raw parameter scaling, and argues that outside these 'verified islands' the scaling hypothesis faces a hard mathematical wall. The proposed verifier-centric agent (Section 4) is presented as a constructive alternative but is not benchmarked against these systems in this paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant probabilistic-scaling paradigm for code generation (Kaplan et al., 2020; Li et al., 2022) by arguing it is structurally misaligned with quantum circuit synthesis, where valid outputs occupy an exponentially sparse subset of the circuit space. It builds on their own earlier empirical work (Song et al., 2026) showing LLMs trained without data verification plateau at 79% accuracy, and adapts constrained-decoding principles from semantic parsing (Shin et al., 2021; Scholak et al., 2021) and LLM-Modulo architectures (Kambhampati et al., 2024) into a multi-level, verifier-centric generation framework. The formal complexity analysis draws on quantum information theory (Haar-measure concentration, Solovay-Kitaev, Gottesman-Knill) and classical complexity (NP-hardness of qubit allocation, QMA-completeness of non-identity testing) to establish that post-hoc filtering inherits a coupled exponential cost no amount of model scaling can amortize.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the paper's central claim holds—that probabilistic scaling is not merely insufficient but actively counterproductive (inverse scaling) for quantum program generation—it reframes the design space for AI4Science tools in any domain with hard mathematical constraints (protein design, theorem proving, chip layout). The proposed verifier-centric paradigm with hierarchical abstraction (module → gate → rewrite) is concrete but remains largely unvalidated empirically beyond the Cuccaro adder example; open questions include whether the multi-level constraint system scales to genuinely novel algorithms, how to audit the verifier stack itself, and whether trace-based training data can be constructed at sufficient volume. The paper is a position paper, so its strongest contribution is the formal complexity lower bounds rather than a demonstrated system.
