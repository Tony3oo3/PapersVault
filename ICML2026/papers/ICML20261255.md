## Problem

Individual features in learned neural network representations often lack interpretable meaning: a single neuron may activate on scattered, geometrically unrelated inputs. Sparsity regularization (L1) constrains how many samples a feature activates on but not which ones, so a sparse feature can still fire on disconnected regions of the data manifold. The paper asks whether one can formalize the locality principle observed in biological neural codes (grid cells, head direction cells) and impose it as a geometric constraint on artificial representations.

## Input / Output

The method operates on non-negative matrices M ∈ R^{B×L} produced by autoencoder bottlenecks (grayscale MNIST digits rotated at 72 angles, or synthetic point clouds on circles/spheres/tori embedded in R^512) or BERT token embeddings (word-level tokens from Wikitext-2, vocabulary capped at 2000, sequence length 128, Softplus(β=20) activation). It produces a regularized latent space where both the sample geometry (rows) and feature geometry (columns) share compatible Vietoris-Rips topology, yielding features that tile the data manifold into contiguous, human-readable regions rather than scattered activation patterns.

## Method

The paper defines ε-coherence for a non-negative matrix: it is ε-local if the Fréchet variance of each row's weighted attention over columns (and vice versa) is bounded by ε, and ε-covered if each row lies within ε of some column's barycentric image (and vice versa). The key theoretical result proves that ε-coherent matrices with 1-Lipschitz barycentric maps induce an ε^{1/2}-interleaving between the Vietoris-Rips filtrations of samples and features, guaranteeing topological compatibility. The practical COH loss computes barycentric maps via squared-L1-normalized weights, penalizes the top-k worst variance and covering terms exceeding a threshold τ, and is addable to any architecture with non-negative activations. In the BERT setting, the loss is applied via a combination of one global and five local sub-matrix iterations to prevent degenerate cluster separation and to bias features toward fitting token geometry rather than the reverse.

## Results

On the single-digit rotated MNIST autoencoder, COH achieves 100% of features with angular tuning (MRL > 0.5) and MRL of 0.88, compared to 63% and 0.58 for L1-sparsity, at negligible MSE cost (0.011 vs 0.010). On the more challenging double-digit setting, COH+L1 achieves 99% tuned features, 91.9% component purity, and MRL of 0.85, while maintaining coherence scores (Loc/Cov ≈ 0.15) an order of magnitude lower than L1 (≈ 3.67/2.84). In the BERT token embedding experiment, COH yields 87.6/256 features judged interpretable by an LLM (Claude Opus 4.7) and 77.4/256 with >50% token-neighborhood overlap, versus 0/256 and 1.0/256 for the non-negative (Softplus) baseline alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines: a vanilla autoencoder (no regularization), L1 sparsity regularization, and the proposed COH coherence objective (sometimes combined with L1). On the single-digit experiment, COH decisively outperforms L1 on angular tuning (100% vs 63% of features with MRL > 0.5) and coherence (Loc/Cov ≈ 0.15 vs ≈ 3.67/2.84), while L1 achieves slightly lower MSE (0.010 vs 0.011) and higher sparsity (19.5% vs 33.2% active features). On the double-digit experiment, COH alone shows high variance across seeds due to two qualitatively different coherent solutions (separated vs merged circles), but COH+L1 resolves this ambiguity, achieving 99% tuned features and 91.9% purity with stable coherence, at the cost of higher MSE (0.014 vs 0.008 for L1). In the BERT token embedding setting, the non-negative Softplus baseline (without COH) yields essentially zero interpretable features (0/256 by LLM judgment, 1.0/256 by overlap), while COH produces 87.6/256 interpretable features and 77.4/256 with >50% overlap, demonstrating that non-negativity alone is insufficient and the geometric coherence constraint is what drives interpretability. The key trade-off is that COH+L1 sacrifices reconstruction quality (MSE increases ~75% over L1) to gain reliable, disentangled, and topologically coherent features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a geometric analogue of Dowker Duality (Dowker, 1952), which establishes homology equivalence between row and column complexes of a binary matrix; here, the authors must explicitly define a class of matrices (coherent matrices) for which the Vietoris-Rips filtrations of rows and columns are interleaved, rather than receiving the duality for free. It departs from sparse autoencoders (Bricken et al., 2023; Cunningham et al., 2024) by requiring not just that features activate rarely, but that their active samples form geometrically contiguous regions, and from topological autoencoders (Moor et al., 2020; Hofer et al., 2019) by mirroring topology between latent samples and latent features rather than preserving input topology or imposing a target topology. The biological motivation from grid cells and head direction cells provides the design principle of locality, while Sengupta et al. (2018) on similarity-preserving receptive fields is the closest prior work, though it lacks the bidirectional topological guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical guarantee (bounded interleaving between sample and feature VR filtrations) that is rare in interpretability work, and the resulting COH loss is simple, differentiable, and requires only a non-negative activation function, making it broadly applicable. Key limitations include the Lipschitz assumption on barycentric maps being verified empirically rather than enforced, the inability to control which coherent solution is found (as seen in the double-digit experiment's two qualitatively different solutions), and the small scale of experiments (256-dim BERT with 2000-token vocabulary, rotated MNIST). Open questions include scaling to larger models and datasets, applying coherence to supervised settings where within-class structure matters, multi-layer application, and whether the topological guarantee holds under the distribution shifts encountered in production systems.
