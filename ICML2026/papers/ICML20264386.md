## Problem

Existing RL-based perception policy models for MLLMs rely on natural-language reasoning chains that operate in an unstructured semantic space, leading to poor spatial grounding and limited generalization across perception tasks. Prior remedies—removing thinking entirely or designing task-specific no-think pipelines—mask the deeper issue: the form of reasoning, not its presence, is misaligned with the spatial and object-centric nature of visual perception.

## Input / Output

The model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) receives an RGB image plus a text query for grounding/counting tasks, or an image alone for detection. Intermediate reasoning is emitted as a JSON list of {"label": ..., "bbox": [x1, y1, x2, y2]} pairs inside  tags, followed by a final answer in <answer> tags containing either a single bounding box (grounding), a list of (label, bbox) pairs (detection), or a numeric count (counting). All coordinates are in pixel space following Qwen2.5-VL's convention, and the structured format is enforced by a format reward that penalizes malformed outputs.

## Method

Artemis introduces structured visual reasoning in which each intermediate thinking step is a (label, bounding-box) pair capturing a verifiable visual state, replacing free-form linguistic rationales. Training uses GRPO with a redesigned composite reward: a Structured Visual Reasoning Reward that matches predicted (label, bbox) pairs to ground-truth annotations via the Hungarian algorithm and scores them with IoU and ROUGE thresholds (assigning the highest weight η=0.8 to the key target object and distributing the remainder to contextual objects), plus standard format and answer rewards (GIoU-based for grounding, Hungarian-matched GIoU+label+F1 with quantity and missing penalties for detection). The model is cold-started on 80k COCO detection samples, then trained for one epoch on the 77k-sample Artemis-RFT dataset (grounding from RefCOCO/+/g and detection from COCO) under a unified policy, with no task-specific architectural modifications. This spatially grounded, object-centric chain rule enables a single 3B model to generalize zero-shot from natural-image grounding to counting, mathematical diagram perception, and reasoning grounding without task-specific retraining.

## Results

On visual grounding (RefCOCO/+/g), Artemis (3B) achieves 71.7/74.2/65.6 valAvg/testA-Avg/testB-Avg on RefCOCO, outperforming VLM-R1 (69.3/71.8/62.4), Vision-R1 (67.7/71.5/62.0), and Perception-R1 (67.9/71.2/61.9), with the largest gains at higher IoU thresholds (e.g., +5.7 over VLM-R1 on RefCOCO testB@95). On COCO2017 object detection, Artemis reaches mAP 31.0, AP50 48.0, and AR100 46.6, surpassing the Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline (mAP 15.4) by more than 2× and achieving the highest AP50 and AR100 among all RL-based MLLMs. Zero-shot on Pixmo-Count, Artemis scores 81.4/76.9 (val/test) without any counting-specific training, exceeding Perception-R1 (78.1/75.6) and VisionReasoner (70.1/69.5). On MATHGLANCE (zero-shot diagram perception), Artemis achieves the best overall score of 49.3, and on LISA reasoning grounding it reaches 69.8, outperforming all prior RL-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Artemis consistently outperforms all RL-based MLLM baselines on visual grounding, with the most pronounced advantage at stricter IoU thresholds: on RefCOCO testB@95 it scores 33.4 vs. 27.7 (VLM-R1), 28.8 (Perception-R1), and 28.6 (Vision-R1), indicating substantially tighter localization. On COCO detection, Artemis achieves the highest AP50 (48.0) and AR100 (46.6) among all compared methods, though Perception-R1 edges it slightly on mAP (31.9 vs. 31.0) and AP75 (33.4 vs. 31.9), likely because Perception-R1 is optimized specifically for COCO detection while Artemis uses a unified policy. On zero-shot visual counting (Pixmo-Count), Artemis (81.4 val) surpasses Perception-R1 (78.1) and VisionReasoner (70.1) despite being trained without any counting data, whereas both baselines use a detect-then-count paradigm with post-hoc box aggregation. On LISA reasoning grounding, Artemis (69.8) outperforms VLM-R1 (63.1), No-Thinking-RL (61.8), and UniVG-R1 (59.7) by 6.7–10.1 points. On the MATHGLANCE diagram-perception benchmark, Artemis achieves the best zero-shot score (49.3), substantially ahead of general MLLMs and R1-based models, demonstrating cross-domain transfer from natural images to geometric diagrams. On general MLLM benchmarks (Table 4), Artemis marginally improves over its Qwen2.5-VL-3B base (e.g., MMBench 79.3 vs. 79.1, POPE 88.6 vs. 85.9) while VLM-R1 degrades significantly (MMBench 70.7, POPE 79.3), suggesting that structured visual reasoning preserves and slightly enhances general multimodal capability rather than sacrificing it for perception gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

Artemis builds on the GRPO framework from DeepSeek-R1 for rule-based RL in MLLMs, but departs fundamentally from prior perception-policy work (VLM-R1, Perception-R1, Vision-R1) in how intermediate reasoning is represented. Where those methods either induce free-form linguistic 'thinking' or skip reasoning entirely, Artemis represents each reasoning step as a (label, bounding-box) pair, grounding the policy's search in spatial and object-centric space rather than semantic linguistic space. This design is motivated by cognitive-science accounts of human visual search (Torralba et al., Wolfe & Horowitz) and enables deterministic process supervision over intermediate perceptual states via Hungarian-matched IoU/ROUGE rewards, a form of supervision absent in prior grounded-reasoning methods that rely on region revisiting or re-encoding at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that the *form* of intermediate reasoning, not its mere presence, determines perceptual generalization—challenges the prevailing 'add thinking tokens' paradigm in MLLM post-training and offers a concrete, task-agnostic alternative. A single 3B policy trained on grounding and detection transfers zero-shot to counting, reasoning grounding (LISA), and mathematical diagram perception (MATHGLANCE), suggesting that object-centric spatial reasoning is a more general perceptual primitive than task-specific linguistic rationales. Key limitations include the restriction to static 2D images and a simple label-bbox representation; extending to video, 3D scenes, or richer structured evidence (e.g., keypoints, segmentation masks) remains open. Whether the approach scales to larger models or benefits from multi-domain training data beyond COCO is also untested.
