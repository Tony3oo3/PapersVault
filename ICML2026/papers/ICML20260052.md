## Problem

Current atmospheric AI models are fragmented: each task (forecasting, downscaling, sparse reconstruction, physics enforcement) requires a separately trained or fine-tuned network. Existing atmospheric foundation models still rely on task-specific fine-tuning to adapt to new downstream applications, limiting their versatility. WIND addresses this by learning a single task-agnostic generative prior of the atmosphere and solving all downstream problems purely at inference time as inverse problems, without any retraining.

## Input / Output

The model operates on spatiotemporal tensors of 70 atmospheric variables (surface and 13 pressure levels) at 1.5° resolution (240×121 grid) with T=5 frames at 6-hourly stride. At inference, the frozen backbone always outputs the full atmospheric state at native resolution regardless of the observation dimensionality: for forecasting, one clean context frame is provided and future frames are denoised from pure noise; for inverse problems (downscaling, sparse reconstruction, physics constraints), all frames initialize as noise and are steered by a task-specific forward operator A applied to the predicted clean state. The only thing that changes between tasks is the operator A and the target observation Y, while the model weights, DDIM schedule, and sampling loop remain identical.

## Method

WIND pre-trains a ~458M-parameter UViT backbone using diffusion forcing: each frame in a T=5 sequence is independently corrupted at a random noise level k^t ∈ [0,1], and the network is trained via weighted MSE to reconstruct the clean sequence without being conditioned on the noise levels, forcing it to infer per-frame uncertainty from the input alone. This design treats diffusion noise as a continuous masking mechanism, enabling the model to accept arbitrary mixtures of clean and noisy frames without distribution shift, which supports stable arbitrarily long autoregressive rollouts. At inference, every downstream task is framed as an inverse problem A(X) = Y, and the generation is guided by Moment Matching Posterior Sampling (MMPS): the posterior score decomposes into the prior score from the frozen denoiser plus a likelihood score computed via Tweedie's covariance formula and a conjugate-gradient solve through the Jacobian of A. Because A can be any differentiable operator (identity for forecasting, AvgPool for spatial downscaling, temporal averaging, binary masking, or the non-linear dry-air-mass integral), the same model handles all tasks zero-shot, with guidance strength naturally modulated by the noise level—strong at low noise when predictions are reliable, weak at high noise where the prior dominates.

## Results

On WeatherBench2 (2020), WIND achieves CRPS of 0.286 (T₂ₘ, 24h), 38.81 (MSLP, 24h), and 0.300 (TP₆ₕ, 24h), remaining competitive with specialized SOTA models despite training at a coarser 1.5° resolution. For spatial downscaling (s=4), WIND achieves RMSE of 0.63 (Temperature) and 42.68 (MSLP), outperforming the FNO baseline (0.66 and 48.53) without any task-specific training. In the warmer-climate ablation (Storm Bernd, +2K), the guided ensemble yields a mean peak precipitation intensification of +13.9%, closely matching the theoretical Clausius-Clapeyron expectation of ~14%, while the unconstrained warm free run retains only 50.3% of this signal (+7.0%). WIND maintains global dry air mass conservation over a 4-year rollout where the unconstrained run drifts after ~200 days.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the FNO baseline (Li et al., 2021) on spatial downscaling, WIND achieves lower RMSE on 7 of 10 variables (e.g., Temperature 0.63 vs. 0.66, MSLP 42.68 vs. 48.53, U-Wind 1.89 vs. 2.08) without any task-specific training, while FNO is marginally better on precipitation (1.55 vs. 1.77). Against GenCast (Price et al., 2023) on WeatherBench2, WIND shows higher CRPS (e.g., T₂ₘ 120h: 0.624 vs. 0.482; MSLP 24h: 38.81 vs. 24.56), which the authors attribute to WIND's deliberately coarser 1.5° training resolution prioritizing a generalizable prior over single-task optimization. Against Kriging (Cressie, 1990) on sparse reconstruction, WIND substantially outperforms the minimum-variance estimator, preserving high-frequency power spectral content and generating realistic textures in unobserved regions where Kriging produces overly smooth fields. Internally, WIND outperforms its autoregressive AR-UViT baseline in CRPS after the first few days and avoids the SSR overshoot (SSR > 1) that destabilizes the AR baseline for moisture variables. On sparse reconstruction, WIND also beats a specialized UViT baseline, demonstrating that a strong generative prior can outperform task-specific training in data-scarce (1% observation) regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

WIND builds on diffusion forcing (Chen et al., 2024) for stable long-horizon generation with independent per-frame noise levels, and on Moment Matching Posterior Sampling (Rozet et al., 2024) for solving inverse problems via posterior guidance, replacing the point-estimate assumption of standard diffusion posterior sampling (Chung et al., 2022). It departs from prior atmospheric foundation models (CLIMAX, AtmoRep, the Earth foundation model) that require task-specific fine-tuning by framing all downstream tasks—forecasting, downscaling, sparse reconstruction, physics constraints—as zero-shot inverse problems solved purely at inference. The UViT backbone and DDIM sampling follow recent video diffusion practice, while the key architectural choice of omitting explicit noise-level conditioning (motivated by Sun et al., 2025a) encourages the model to infer per-frame uncertainty from its inputs alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single self-supervised generative prior can replace an entire zoo of specialized atmospheric models, with the only task-specific component being a differentiable forward operator A applied at inference. This is practically significant for climate impact modeling, where downscaling, sparse data assimilation, and physics-consistent long rollouts are all needed but currently require separate pipelines. Key limitations include the coarse 1.5° resolution (limiting extreme-event resolution), high inference cost from iterative MMPS gradient computation (5–10× overhead), and the inability to enforce local conservation laws. Open questions include whether the framework scales to 0.25° resolution, whether distillation can reduce inference cost, and whether the OOD climate scenario results generalize beyond single-event case studies.
