## Problem

Active learning for measure-valued trajectories is an open problem because probability measures reside in the infinite-dimensional Wasserstein space, where standard Euclidean interpolation and uncertainty quantification are ill-defined. Existing distribution interpolation methods (flow matching, Schrödinger bridges) are deterministic and do not provide the epistemic uncertainty over plausible probability paths that an acquisition policy requires to decide which time point to measure next.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261790/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes empirical measures (weighted point clouds of cell embeddings or other feature vectors) at a growing set of discrete time points, a candidate pool of available measurement times, and a fixed acquisition budget. At each iteration it outputs the next optimal measurement time to query; after the budget is exhausted, it produces a continuous probability path reconstruction over [0,1] along with a full epistemic uncertainty field. The input measures are arbitrary point clouds of varying cardinality per time point, and the output path is represented as a point cloud supported on the reference landmarks with the same weights as the Wasserstein barycenter reference.

## Method

The framework linearizes the Wasserstein space via Linearized Optimal Transport: each empirical snapshot is mapped to a displacement field in the tangent space at a Wasserstein barycenter reference, computed through discrete OT couplings and barycentric projection. These tangent vectors are reweighted by the reference measure, flattened, and compressed to K dimensions via PCA. A multi-output Gaussian Process with a Matérn-5/2 kernel is then placed over the latent coefficients, with physical time reparameterized by an intrinsic arc-length warping (cumulative pairwise W₂ distances fit with a monotone cubic spline) to handle non-stationary dynamics. The GP posterior covariance provides a tractable epistemic uncertainty, which drives an acquisition function (trace of predictive covariance or expected integrated risk reduction) to select the next measurement time. The reference barycenter is refit at each iteration to minimize linearization distortion.

## Results

On the synthetic oscillatory branching dataset, the active strategy consistently outperforms both Uniform and Random baselines across all acquisition budgets, with the gap widening after budget 5. At the most localized branching duration (l=0.05), the active method achieves a 23.1% relative improvement over Uniform and 34.2% over Random on mean W₂ error, and 35.7% and 45.4% respectively on the velocity-weighted metric. On the Schiebinger single-cell reprogramming dataset, the active strategy achieves the lowest reconstruction error in the low-to-moderate budget regime (B ≤ 12), where uniform grids are most likely to miss rapid reprogramming phases. The velocity-weighted metric (w-W₂) confirms that the acquisition function specifically targets high-velocity branching events.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Uniform baseline (equidistant grid), the active method wins decisively when dynamics are highly non-stationary: at branching duration l=0.05 it reduces mean W₂ error by 23.1% and velocity-weighted W₂ by 35.7%. The advantage diminishes as branching windows widen to l=0.20, where the mean W₂ gap becomes negative (−17.2%) but the velocity-weighted metric remains positive (+7.1%), confirming the method still targets high-velocity regions. Against the Random baseline, the active method consistently wins across all configurations, with the largest gains (45.4% on w-W₂) at the most localized branching. Against MMFM (Rohbeck et al., 2025) as a reconstruction method given the same acquired snapshots, the LOT+GP surrogate substantially outperforms: at 12 snapshots on the synthetic dataset, mean W₂ is 0.031 (Ours) vs. 1.746 (MMFM), and at 20 snapshots on the single-cell dataset, 2.906 vs. 4.967. The adapted spline-uncertainty acquisition from Singh et al. (2005) also underperforms the proposed GP-based uncertainty acquisition in the supplementary comparisons (Figure 7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends active timepoint selection (previously studied for Euclidean-valued gene-expression curves by Singh et al., 2005) to the fundamentally different setting where outputs are probability measures in Wasserstein space. It repurposes Linearized Optimal Transport (Wang et al., 2013; Kolouri et al., 2016), previously used for classification and barycenter estimation on measures, as a geometric linearization step that makes Gaussian Process regression applicable. The paper explicitly departs from distributional interpolation methods (flow matching, Schrödinger bridges, MMFM) by arguing they are deterministic reconstruction tools lacking the epistemic uncertainty quantification that active acquisition requires, and positions its GP-in-tangent-space surrogate as the missing probabilistic component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically important bottleneck in single-cell biology (and analogous expensive-sequencing domains) where thousands of dollars per timepoint preclude dense sampling, and provides the first tractable probabilistic surrogate for epistemic uncertainty over probability paths. Key limitations include reliance on the tangent-space approximation (large distributional shifts still induce linearization error, motivating future atlas/multi-chart constructions) and OT computational cost that constrains scalability to million-point snapshots. The sensitivity analysis revealing that the method's advantage vanishes or reverses for broad (non-localized) dynamic windows is an honest and useful characterization of when active selection helps versus when uniform grids suffice.
