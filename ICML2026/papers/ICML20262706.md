## Problem

Zeroth-order optimization faces a fundamental trade-off: coordinate-wise finite differences are sample-inefficient (O(d) queries per step), while randomized estimators (SPSA, Evolution Strategies) reduce per-step cost but introduce high variance that slows wall-clock convergence. The paper challenges two prevailing assumptions: that stale gradients are harmful noise to be discarded, and that the finite-difference interval ε must be minimized for accuracy. It shows that temporal coherence in optimization trajectories makes stale gradients useful 'warm starts,' and that larger ε can implicitly smooth the landscape by reducing the effective smoothness constant.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only scalar function evaluations (no gradient or Hessian access) and the current model parameters, which may be structured as heterogeneous tensors (4D convolution kernels, 2D weight matrices). A user-defined compute budget B controls the number of finite-difference queries per step, and a memory budget m (typically equal to total parameter count n) sizes the gradient buffer. The output is updated model parameters, applied in-place via virtualized flattened views of the buffer, requiring exactly one extra copy of the parameters in memory—no additional moment tensors or projection matrices.

## Method

CoCD maintains a dense gradient buffer and cyclically traverses coordinates in a fixed order. Each optimization step has two phases: a temporal decay phase that multiplies the entire buffer by a momentum factor γ ∈ [0,1], and a refresh phase that overwrites B coordinates with fresh central finite-difference estimates. The hybrid buffer (fresh entries for updated coordinates, decayed stale entries for the rest) is then used as the descent direction. Theoretically, CoCD is proven equivalent to Block Cyclic Coordinate Descent with stale-gradient warm starts, yielding linear convergence under the PL condition. The momentum parameter γ interpolates between full memory reuse (γ=1, standard CoCD) and classical BCCD (γ=0), while a larger finite-difference interval ε reduces the effective coordinate-wise smoothness constant L_ε, enabling larger learning rates and longer gradient histories without sacrificing stability. An efficient implementation uses a circular FIFO buffer with integer state pointers and in-place tensor views to avoid any reshaping or copying overhead.

## Results

On SARCOS (regression), CoCD achieves a final loss of 31.18, dramatically outperforming BCCD (188.73) while approaching the SGD oracle (5.38). On MNIST, CoCD reaches 95.48% accuracy versus BCCD's 27.03% (SGD oracle: 99.21%). On CIFAR-10, CoCD attains 45.08% accuracy compared to BCCD's 10.13% (SGD oracle: 62.51%). Training time per epoch is essentially identical between CoCD and BCCD across all three datasets (e.g., 6.12s vs. 6.23s on SARCOS), confirming that gradient reuse adds negligible overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BCCD (γ=0, the no-momentum ablation), CoCD wins decisively on all three tasks: 6× lower loss on SARCOS (31.18 vs. 188.73), 3.5× higher accuracy on MNIST (95.48% vs. 27.03%), and 4.4× higher accuracy on CIFAR-10 (45.08% vs. 10.13%), all at matched wall-clock time. Against the randomized ZO baselines under a matched compute budget of 64 queries per step (Figure 4), CoCD matches ZO-SGD and SPSA on classification accuracy but clearly outperforms both on the SARCOS regression task, where SPSA diverges mid-training while CoCD stabilizes. CoCD is also approximately 2× faster in wall-clock time than ZO-SGD (8.1s vs. 15.7s per episode) because it avoids the dense Gaussian sampling overhead. The SGD oracle remains the upper bound, but CoCD closes the gap substantially (e.g., 95.48% vs. 99.21% on MNIST) using only function evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoCD extends the Block Cyclic Coordinate Descent (BCCD) framework of Beck & Tetruashvili (2013) and the non-convex convergence guarantees of Cai et al. (2023) into the zeroth-order setting, where gradients are approximated via finite differences rather than computed exactly. It departs from the dominant randomized ZO paradigm (Evolution Strategies, SPSA, DeepZero) by replacing stochastic gradient estimation with a deterministic cyclic traversal and a FIFO buffer of stale gradient estimates, drawing conceptual inspiration from the Web of Affine Spaces (WASP) framework but at a much lighter computational weight. The key conceptual departure from prior work is twofold: treating stale gradients as informative warm starts rather than noise to be bounded, and showing that larger finite-difference intervals can be beneficial through an implicit landscape-smoothing mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoCD offers a practical, memory-efficient alternative to randomized ZO methods for edge and embedded deployment where backpropagation is infeasible, with a strict one-copy memory footprint and O(1) query complexity per coordinate. The implicit smoothing insight—that larger epsilon reduces the effective Lipschitz constant and stabilizes descent—challenges the standard heuristic of minimizing epsilon and could inform hyperparameter choices in any finite-difference-based optimizer. However, the method is currently limited to small-to-medium models (≤270k parameters), and open questions include scaling to larger architectures, adaptive selection of epsilon and gamma, extending convergence guarantees beyond the PL condition, and whether the gradient-reuse strategy transfers to first-order optimizers like AdamW or Muon.
