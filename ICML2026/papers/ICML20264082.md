## Problem

Chain-of-Thought reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) generates verbose textual reasoning chains that incur substantial inference costs, yet prior latent-reasoning work (e.g., Hao et al., 2024) was limited to small-scale GPT-2 on text-only math tasks. This paper addresses the gap of compressing CoTs into compact latent representations for large-scale MLLMs handling general multimodal reasoning, while providing a theoretical guarantee that reasoning capability is preserved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264082/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Heima takes an RGB image and a text question as input and produces a sequence of one special thinking token per CoT stage (e.g., <Thinking_of_Summary>, <Thinking_of_Caption>, <Thinking_of_Reasoning>) followed by the final text answer, reducing intermediate tokens from ~150–230 to roughly 13–17. Separately, adaptive interpreters (pure text LLMs with no visual access) consume the last hidden states of these thinking tokens along with the text question and explanatory prompts, and reconstruct variable-length textual CoT sequences for each stage, enabling post-hoc inspection of the compressed reasoning.

## Method

Heima distills each CoT stage into a single special vocabulary token via progressive distillation: CoT stages are replaced by thinking tokens one at a time (rather than all at once), followed by a final recovering stage that optimizes interactions among all thinking tokens jointly. Training uses LoRA on LLaVA-CoT (Llama-3.2-11B-Vision) with the image encoder frozen. An information-theoretic analysis grounded in the data-processing inequality proves that the compressed thinking tokens form a Markov chain Y–(X, CoTs)–<CoTs>, guaranteeing that non-trivial mutual information I(Y; <CoTs> | X) > 0 is retained and quantifying the compression-induced gap as I(Y; CoTs | X, <CoTs>). To empirically validate this gap, separate interpreters initialized from Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct are fine-tuned with next-token prediction to decode the last hidden state of each thinking token (substituted after the embedding layer) back into the original textual CoT, guided by explanatory prompts that anchor reconstruction to the question.

## Results

Heima achieves 58.0% average zero-shot accuracy across six multimodal reasoning benchmarks while generating only ~13 tokens per sample, compared to ~178 tokens for the LLaVA-CoT baseline (a ~93% token reduction). On MMBench, Heima actually surpasses LLaVA-CoT (72.8% vs. 70.7%), and on AI2D it nearly matches (77.5% vs. 77.6%). The largest accuracy gaps versus LLaVA-CoT appear on MathVista (43.6% vs. 50.9%) and MMStar (49.9% vs. 54.0%), while HallusionBench shows a smaller drop (60.6% vs. 63.8%). In the LLaVA-Next-Vicuna-7B family, Heima reaches 53.8% average accuracy with ~13 tokens, closely matching the CoT variant's 55.0% while using only ~6% of the generated tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base Llama3.2-11B-Vision model (no CoT), Heima improves average accuracy from 52.1% to 58.0%, with particularly large gains on MMBench (+14.6 pts) and HallusionBench (+23.4 pts), while generating far fewer tokens. Compared to the primary CoT baseline LLaVA-CoT, Heima trades a 3.1-point average accuracy drop (58.0% vs. 61.1%) for a ~93% reduction in generated tokens (~13 vs. ~178 tokens). Heima wins on MMBench (72.8% vs. 70.7%) and essentially ties on AI2D (77.5% vs. 77.6%), but loses notably on MathVista (43.6% vs. 50.9%) and MMStar (49.9% vs. 54.0%). In the LLaVA-Next-Vicuna-7B family (Table 2), Heima (53.8% avg) closely matches the LoRA-fine-tuned CoT variant (55.0% avg) while using only ~6% of the tokens, and substantially outperforms the non-CoT base model (44.4% avg). The ablation variants (w/o progressive, w/o recover) confirm that both progressive distillation and the recovering stage are necessary to close the gap with full CoT performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends latent reasoning from small text-only models (Hao et al., 2024; Deng et al., 2024b) to large-scale MLLMs, departing from prior work that either compressed CoTs for GPT-2 on narrow math tasks or used visual decoders (LISA, DetGPT, ViSA) to extract spatial information from latent tokens rather than compressing reasoning itself. It also contrasts with continuous-embedding CoT compression (Cheng & Van Durme, 2024), which showed significant accuracy degradation, by using discrete special tokens with progressive distillation. The information-theoretic framing via the data-processing inequality and the adaptive interpreter design for gap quantification are novel contributions not present in prior latent-reasoning or context-compression literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 94% token reduction while retaining most zero-shot accuracy offers a practical path toward scalable latent reasoning in deployed MLLMs, where inference cost is a primary bottleneck. However, the accuracy gap versus full CoT remains non-trivial (61.1→58.0 average; MMVet drops 49.8→43.3), and the evaluation is limited to two model families (11B, 7B) and a single training dataset with only three CoT stages. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer reasoning chains, more complex multimodal tasks, and larger models, and whether the information-theoretic guarantee (which is essentially the well-known data-processing inequality) can be tightened to predict *how much* information is lost in practice.
