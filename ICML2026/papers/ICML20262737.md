## Problem

Existing 3D Visual Question Answering methods produce free-form generative outputs that introduce decision ambiguity and are not optimized for logical discrimination, making them unsuitable for downstream planning systems that require deterministic binary preconditions (e.g., verifying whether a path is clear before a robot proceeds). No prior benchmark or method directly targets the task of verifying natural language assertions against 3D scenes with strict Yes/No judgments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262737/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D scene represented as per-object point clouds (sampled to 1,024 xyz+rgb points per object, processed by PointNet++ and a 4-layer spatial Transformer) and a natural language assertion (up to 50 tokens, encoded by the first 4 layers of BERT-base at 768 dimensions). The output is a single binary label (Yes/No) produced by a 512→768→2 MLP head, with no free-form text generation. Assertions span six semantic types (spatial, negation, attribute, quantity, existence, verification) and the benchmark enforces deterministic binary outputs with per-type diagnostic labels.

## Method

DualLPSS introduces dual-stage subspace routing on top of the 3D-VisTA backbone. Early LPSS uses the question representation as a routing signal through a two-layer MLP with Gumbel-Softmax and Soft Top-2 selection over K=6 learnable subspaces, producing a T×T attention bias matrix (combining query/key position biases and cross-modal/intra-text/intra-object structural strengths) that is injected pre-Softmax at the 0-th Transformer layer to adapt how text and scene features fuse. Late LPSS uses the pooled scene representation to route over 6 learnable bias vectors of length L, injecting a bias into the text aggregation attention scores to focus on scene-relevant question regions. Training uses Focal Loss with label smoothing under a staged schedule (first 25 epochs with balanced loss weights, last 15 epochs with 10× answer loss emphasis), plus auxiliary orthogonality, entropy, and load-balancing losses to maintain subspace diversity. The design achieves 82.5% accuracy on 3DSAV with only 131.8M parameters and 22s inference, outperforming the strongest LLM baseline (Chat-Scene, 6.7B params, 193s) by 2 points.

## Results

On the 3DSAV test set (1,899 samples), DualLPSS achieves 82.5% accuracy, 79.2% precision, 84.3% recall, and 81.7% Macro-F1, outperforming all nine baselines. The largest per-type gains are on Negation (77.2%, +3.6 pp over the best baseline Video3D-LLM at 73.6%) and Spatial (76.9%, +3.0 pp over Chat-Scene at 73.9%). On cross-dataset generalization (color/quantity subsets of ScanQA and SQA3D), DualLPSS reaches 82.1% and 81.9%, exceeding the next-best Chat-Scene (70.9%, 70.7%) by over 11 points. Inference costs 22 s and 3.8 GB GPU memory per test set, comparable to specialized models and far cheaper than LLM baselines (e.g., Chat-Scene: 193 s, 28.6 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualLPSS outperforms all nine baselines on overall accuracy (82.5%) and Macro-F1 (81.7%). Among LLM baselines, it beats Chat-Scene (80.5% Acc, 78.6% F1) by 2.0 pp in accuracy and 3.1 pp in F1, with the largest per-type margins on Negation (+7.4 pp) and Spatial (+3.0 pp); however, Chat-Scene retains a 5.1 pp advantage on Quantity (95.3% vs. 90.2%). Against specialized models, DualLPSS leads the strongest SM baseline 3D-VisTA (74.6% Acc) by 7.9 pp, and surpasses BUTD-DETR (74.5%), FE-3DGQA (73.3%), BridgeQA (73.4%), and ScanQA (71.5%) by 8.0–11.0 pp. On the 2D-VLM comparison (Table A12), DualLPSS exceeds GPT-5.2 (76.0%), Qwen3-VL-32B (74.1%), and Qwen3-VL-Plus (74.1%) by 6.5–8.4 pp overall, with the largest gaps on Quantity (+6.8 pp over GPT-5.2) and Existence (+15.5 pp over Qwen3-VL-32B). On zero-shot outdoor transfer (Mip-NeRF 360), DualLPSS achieves 65.1% versus Chat-Scene's 57.5% and Qwen3-VL-Plus's 54.2%. The only per-type loss is Quantity, where Chat-Scene's generative counting capability gives it a narrow edge; all other five semantic types are won by DualLPSS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 3D-VQA paradigm (generative free-form answers) by reframing scene understanding as deterministic binary verification, drawing a parallel to the QA-vs-NLI distinction in NLP. Architecturally, it builds on the 3D-VisTA backbone (BERT text encoder + PointNet++ scene encoder + cross-modal Transformer) but introduces a dual-stage subspace routing mechanism (Early LPSS for type-aware fusion direction, Late LPSS for scene-guided text focus) that has no direct precedent in prior 3D scene understanding work. The routing design borrows concepts from Mixture-of-Experts (Gumbel-Softmax routing, load balancing, orthogonality constraints) and applies them to attention bias generation rather than expert selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: embodied agents and AR systems need deterministic yes/no judgments (e.g., 'is the path clear?') rather than ambiguous descriptions, and no prior benchmark or method was designed for this. The 3DSAV benchmark (22.5k samples, 6 semantic types, diagnostic per-type evaluation) provides the first structured evaluation of logical reasoning in 3D scenes, and the efficiency results (131.8M params outperforming 6.7B Chat-Scene) suggest specialized architectures can match or exceed LLMs on narrow verification tasks. Remaining challenges—spatial negation errors (65% of negation failures involve spatial descriptions) and fine-grained attribute states—point toward the need for interactive view acquisition and better geometric grounding as open directions.
