## Problem

Prior jailbreak research has focused exclusively on single-model attacks, overlooking the practical 'wide-net-casting' scenario where an adversary simultaneously queries a group of large models from different families and succeeds if any one model is breached. This paper identifies this unexplored scenario, demonstrates that it substantially amplifies safety risks (e.g., GCG's success rate jumps from 46.2% to 75.0% when adapted to four models), and develops a tailored attack that achieves 100% jailbreak success under original safety alignments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265119/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a harmful intent (text prompt) and a group of M target large models (LLMs or MLLMs) as input, and produces M adversarial samples—text suffixes for LLMs or adversarial images (via a fine-tuned PixArt-α diffusion model) for MLLMs—each targeting a specific model in the group. At inference, all M adversarial samples are fed to their respective models in parallel, and a judge LLM (Beaver-Dam-7B) selects the highest-jailbreak-quality response as the final output. Evaluation uses the proposed WASR metric (fraction of intents that jailbreak at least one model in the group) and a W-Toxicity Score measuring the severity of the selected harmful response.

## Method

The core contribution is a joint training procedure that pairs each target model with a dedicated adversarial sample generator ('jailbreak expert') and optimizes all M generators simultaneously with an exploration-to-exploitation schedule. At each training step, the update weight vector η*_t is computed as a Boltzmann distribution over the M generators' losses, with temperature β_t uniquely determined by a monotonically decreasing entropy constraint H(η_t) ≥ H̄_t = log(M)·(T−t)/T. This formulation, derived via Lagrangian/KKT conditions, simultaneously maximizes exploitation (concentrating updates on low-loss generators to sharpen specialization) while maintaining sustainable exploration (preventing any generator from being starved of updates due to noisy intermediate losses). The result is that each generator progressively specializes on its target model's distinct vulnerabilities, increasing the probability that at least one model in the group is successfully jailbroken.

## Results

The proposed method achieves a 100% Wide-net-casting Attack Success Rate (WASR) on AdvBench when targeting LLMs (Gemma-2-9b, Vicuna-7b-v1.5, Llama-3.1-8b, Mistral-7b) with only their original safety alignment, compared to 92.3% for the ReMiss baseline. With additional safeguards, it reaches 76.7% WASR (SmoothLLM) and 72.8% (RobustKV), versus 61.5% and 56.1% for the baseline. For MLLMs, the method similarly achieves 100% WASR with original safety alignment on both AdvBench and MM-SafetyBench, and 50.8%–53.5% with VLGuard, outperforming the MLAI+PixArt-α baseline (37.5%–40.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four baselines: GCG (Zou et al., 2023), ReMiss (Xie et al., 2024), MLAI (Hao et al., 2025), and MLAI+PixArt-α (Chen et al., 2024). GCG, when straightforwardly adapted to the wide-net-casting scenario, achieves only 75.0% WASR on AdvBench for LLMs, substantially below the proposed method's 100%. ReMiss, the strongest single-model model-based baseline, reaches 92.3% WASR on LLMs and 93.7% on MLLMs (MM-SafetyBench), still trailing the proposed method by 7.7 and 6.3 percentage points respectively. MLAI (instance-based) achieves 89.1% WASR on MLLMs, while the model-based MLAI+PixArt-α combination improves this to 93.7%, but both remain below the proposed method's 100%. Under additional safeguards (e.g., SmoothLLM, RobustKV, VLGuard, ASTRA), the proposed method consistently outperforms all baselines by 10–15 percentage points in WASR, demonstrating that the exploration-exploitation joint training strategy provides a meaningful advantage over both straightforward adaptation and simpler specialization heuristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the optimization-based jailbreak literature (GCG, ReMiss for LLMs; MLAI, HADES for MLLMs) and the model-based adversarial generator paradigm (ReMiss, AdvPrompter), but departs fundamentally by introducing a multi-target threat model: the 'wide-net-casting' scenario where an adversary queries a group of models and succeeds if any single model is breached. The joint training strategy draws on the exploration-to-exploitation transition framework from annealed multiple choice learning (Perera et al., 2024), adapting its Lagrangian/KKT derivation to produce a Boltzmann-distributed update schedule over M specialized generators. Unlike prior work that treats each model in isolation, this paper formalizes the OR-aggregation success criterion and introduces corresponding metrics (WASR, W-Toxicity Score) to quantify the amplified risk.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a realistic and previously unexamined threat model—users (and adversaries) routinely switch between multiple LLMs/MLLMs—and shows that this simple behavioral pattern amplifies jailbreak risk from ~46% to 75% (GCG) or to 100% (proposed method) when models rely only on original safety alignment. Critically, even with state-of-the-art additional defenses (SmoothLLM, RobustKV, VLGuard, IMMUNE, ASTRA), the wide-net-casting success rate remains substantially elevated, suggesting that per-model 'acceptable' ASR thresholds are insufficient. Open questions include whether cross-provider collaborative defenses can mitigate this, how to integrate wide-net-casting-aware adversarial training into safety alignment pipelines, and whether the findings generalize to larger model groups or closed-source-only settings.
