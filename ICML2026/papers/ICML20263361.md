## Problem

Existing KV cache eviction methods for LLMs rely on hand-crafted heuristics—recency, past attention scores, or key-vector statistics—that serve only as indirect, backward-looking proxies for a token's future utility. These heuristics assume that what was important before remains important, leading to suboptimal eviction decisions and irrecoverable loss of critical information, while attention-based variants introduce computational overhead and are incompatible with efficient kernels like FlashAttention. No prior method directly learns to predict a token's future utility for eviction in a query-independent, inference-free manner.

## Input / Output

Each per-head agent takes as input the concatenation of a token's key vector, value vector, and original position index—no queries, attention scores, or future tokens are required. The agent outputs a scalar score per token, which induces a full permutation (ranking) of all cached tokens from most to least valuable. Because the ranking is budget-agnostic, eviction at any cache size b is simply keeping the top-b entries, making the policy applicable to arbitrary and varying memory budgets without re-scoring.

## Method

KVP trains a lightweight 2-layer MLP (~650K parameters) as a separate RL agent for each KV head (112 agents for Qwen2.5-7B-Chat), parameterizing a Plackett-Luce stochastic ranking policy over cached tokens. Permutations are sampled efficiently in a single parallel step via Gumbel-Sort rather than sequential autoregressive sampling. The reward is the negated normalized area-under-curve of eviction cost across all budgets, computed as a rank-weighted sum of future attention from evicted tokens—this makes the optimum tractable by construction (unique and nested) and requires no LLM inference during training. Agents are trained offline on pre-computed KV traces using REINFORCE with a Leave-One-Out baseline, and at inference the learned scores are applied as a drop-in post-hoc module that ranks and evicts trailing entries, adding only ~1% FLOPs overhead to prefill.

## Results

On RULER-4k, KVP achieves the highest accuracy across all cache budgets, outperforming both attention-based (TOVA, SnapKV) and attention-free (StreamingLLM, LagKV, KeyDiff, K-Norm) baselines. On OASST2-4k, KVP attains the lowest perplexity at nearly all cache sizes. Critically, KVP trained on ~4K-token sequences extrapolates to RULER-128K (32× longer context) and still dominates every baseline at every budget. The inference overhead is negligible: 14.00→14.15 GFLOPs/token (1% increase), with compression latency of 0.71 ms per layer at 10K context versus 404 ms for full-model prefill (570× faster).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KVP outperforms all seven baselines on the primary in-distribution benchmarks (RULER-4k accuracy, OASST2-4k perplexity) and the 32× length-extrapolation test (RULER-128K). Against attention-based methods TOVA and SnapKV, KVP matches or exceeds their accuracy despite not using query-specific attention scores at inference, confirming that the offline RL policy successfully distills attention-based ranking into a static, query-independent scorer. Against attention-free heuristics, KVP's advantage is most pronounced at tight budgets: StreamingLLM shows a sharp perplexity increase as cache shrinks, KeyDiff exhibits hard failure modes on certain heads, and LagKV is competitive only at the very smallest budgets on OASST2. Against the concurrent learned baseline JudgeQ (which requires full LLM forward passes during training and couples scoring to prefill at inference), KVP consistently outperforms on RULER-128K, LongBench passage retrieval (both English and Chinese), and BoolQ, while being trained entirely offline on pre-collected (K, V) traces. In zero-shot generalization, KVP^S (OASST2-trained) leads on BoolQ and GovReport, while KVP^R (RULER-trained) leads on LongBench passage retrieval, demonstrating domain-appropriate specialization without any fine-tuning on the target tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of KV cache eviction, which relies on backward-looking heuristics (past attention scores, recency, key-vector norms) as proxies for token importance. It also distinguishes itself from concurrent learned approaches like JudgeQ and LOCRET, which require full LLM forward passes during training or couple eviction to the inference-time query. Conceptually, KVP builds on the Plackett-Luce model for stochastic ranking and Gumbel-Sort for efficient permutation sampling, but repurposes them in a novel setting: a per-head RL agent trained entirely offline on pre-computed (K, V) traces, with a budget-agnostic AUC reward that evaluates eviction quality across all cache sizes simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache memory is the primary bottleneck for long-context LLM deployment, and this work shows that a compact, head-specific policy (~650K params, trained in under 30 minutes) can match or exceed attention-based eviction methods without requiring query access or attention recomputation, adding only ~1% prefill overhead. The 32× length extrapolation (trained at 4K, evaluated at 128K) and zero-shot generalization across diverse benchmarks suggest the learned ranking captures intrinsic, length-agnostic token-utility structure. Open questions include adaptive non-uniform budget allocation across heads and layers, modeling cross-head dependencies, and replacing the future-attention proxy with direct task-level reward signals.
