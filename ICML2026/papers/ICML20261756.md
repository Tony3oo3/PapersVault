## Problem

Post-training quantization of diffusion LLMs is brittle because dLLM decoding commits tokens irreversibly at a write frontier, and quantization perturbations can flip borderline decisions that are then permanently locked in and amplified across subsequent refinement steps. Standard PTQ methods designed for autoregressive LLMs do not account for this diffusion-specific mechanism of irreversible commits and the resulting 'stability lag' where committed positions remain fragile.

## Input / Output

The method takes a full-precision diffusion LLM (LLaDA or Dream family), a probing corpus (512 GSM8K questions with CoT prompts for Stage I teacher rollouts over T=256 diffusion steps with random commits), and a calibration corpus (128 WikiText2 sequences at length 1024 for Stage II). It produces a W4A4 quantized model with per-channel/per-token symmetric quantization parameters and FlatQuant-style affine transforms, calibrated via layer-wise teacher-forcing on fully observed tokens (no masks), avoiding expensive end-to-end diffusion rollouts during calibration.

## Method

FAIR-Calib is a two-stage PTQ framework. Stage I probes the FP teacher with random commits to build a static position prior that additively combines (i) a frontier-hit indicator with an early-boost polynomial time schedule λ₀(t) emphasizing earlier commits, and (ii) a masked-stage teacher-reliability gate (row-wise normalized token probability) that reduces finite-sample noise and improves cross-corpus transferability. Stage II performs off-policy, layer-wise teacher-forcing calibration minimizing a position-weighted hidden-state MSE, where the fixed prior upweights fragile frontier positions. The paper theoretically justifies this weighted MSE as a KL-consistent surrogate by showing output KL ≤ trajectory KL → per-step KL over committed positions → token KL ≤ ¼‖Δlogit‖² ≤ (L²/4)‖Δhidden‖², yielding an additive time×position upper bound that the calibration objective directly minimizes.

## Results

On W4A4 quantization, FAIR-Calib achieves 72.40% average accuracy on LLaDA-Instruct (vs. 71.38% for FlatQuant and 71.34% for QuaRot) and 66.66% on Dream-Instruct (vs. 63.98% for FlatQuant and 61.14% for QuaRot), consistently outperforming all baselines across 10 benchmarks. The gains are most pronounced on Dream, where the heavier stability-lag tail makes commit decisions more fragile: FAIR-Calib improves Dream-Base average from 62.08% (FlatQuant) to 64.64%, a +2.56-point gain. FAIR-Calib also uses a shorter calibration sequence length (1024) compared to baselines (2048–4096), and reduces teacher-forced commit-step flips from 2.9±1.4 to 1.9±0.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAIR-Calib consistently outperforms both QuaRot and FlatQuant across all five model variants (LLaDA-Base/Instruct/1.5, Dream-Base/Instruct) at W4A4. On LLaDA-Instruct, FAIR-Calib achieves 72.40% average, beating FlatQuant (71.38%) by +1.02 points and QuaRot (71.34%) by +1.06 points; the gap widens substantially on Dream-Instruct, where FAIR-Calib reaches 66.66% versus FlatQuant's 63.98% (+2.68) and QuaRot's 61.14% (+5.52), reflecting Dream's heavier stability-lag tail. RTN is the weakest baseline, losing 10–28 points to FAIR-Calib depending on the model. Notably, FAIR-Calib uses a shorter calibration sequence (1024 tokens) than QuaRot and FlatQuant (2048–4096 tokens), yet still achieves superior accuracy, and it also outperforms FlatQuant under the more challenging W3A4 setting (70.41% vs. 69.58% on LLaDA-Instruct).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard layer-wise PTQ pipeline (GPTQ-style reconstruction, FlatQuant's affine flattening transforms) to the diffusion LLM setting, where the key departure is recognizing that irreversible token commits create a 'stability lag' absent in autoregressive decoding. While Lin et al. (2025) demonstrated that naive transfer of autoregressive PTQ to dLLMs degrades on reasoning tasks, this work identifies the specific mechanism—frontier decision flips at fragile commit states—and formulates a position-weighted calibration objective grounded in a KL-divergence upper bound over the Markov decoding chain. The theoretical analysis (additive time×position decomposition, softmax-KL-to-hidden-MSE bridge) provides a principled justification for the weighting scheme that prior dLLM quantization work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As diffusion LLMs (LLaDA, Dream) gain traction as alternatives to autoregressive generation, efficient low-bit deployment becomes critical, and this paper provides the first calibration method explicitly designed around the irreversible-commit failure mode. The practical advantages—no end-to-end diffusion rollouts during calibration, shorter sequences (1024 vs. 2048/4096), and ~2–2.7× lower peak GPU memory—make it accessible on consumer hardware. Open questions include whether the random-commit probing assumption generalizes to dLLM variants with non-monotone decoding, whether the approach extends to continuous-token or latent-space diffusion LLMs, and how the gains scale to much larger models (70B+) where the stability-lag tail may be even heavier.
