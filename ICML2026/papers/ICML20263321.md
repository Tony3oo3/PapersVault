## Problem

The average run length (ARL) and average detection delay (ADD) are the standard optimality criteria for quickest changepoint detection (QCD) models, but their estimation on real-world datasets is hindered by limited and irregular sequence lengths. Conventional estimators (e.g., LB-ARL, LB-ADD) discard sequences in which the detector never raises an alarm before the horizon, introducing substantial negative bias and high variance that make reliable model comparison and selection impractical.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of N time-series sequences, each with a (possibly irregular) length T_i, a changepoint label ν_i (or ∞ for no change), and a predicted detection point τ_i from an online QCD model. It produces two scalar estimates: KM-ARL, the area under the Kaplan-Meier survival curve of detection points up to the maximum last-observed time, and KM-ADD, the analogous area under the survival curve of detection delays (τ_i − ν_i) for sequences with ν_i < ∞ and τ_i ≥ ν_i. No parametric assumptions on the underlying pre- or post-change distributions are required.

## Method

The core idea is a structural analogy between QCD and survival analysis: detection points (or detection delays) play the role of event times, while sequence truncation—min{ν_i, T_i} for ARL and T_i − ν_i for ADD—plays the role of right-censoring times. The Kaplan-Meier estimator is adapted to estimate the survival function of detection points/delays under this censoring, and the ARL/ADD is recovered as the restricted mean survival time (the integral of the survival curve). The paper decomposes the total estimation bias into a finite-sample bias (shown to decay exponentially with dataset size N) and a truncation bias (proven to be no larger in magnitude than that of the conventional LB-ARL/LB-ADD), establishing asymptotic unbiasedness whenever the detection support does not exceed the censoring support. The independent censoring assumption is justified for online QCD models that do not look ahead in the input sequence.

## Results

On simulated Gaussian process datasets (1000–10000 sequences), KM-ARL tracks the true ARL closely across GSR thresholds even under heavy censoring (90% of sequences containing a changepoint, irregular lengths in [30, 300]), whereas LB-ARL and Naive ARL exhibit substantial negative bias and high variance. On the WISDM Actitracker real-world dataset (51,326 sequences with lengths ranging from 1 to 54,401), LB-ARL and LB-ADD become unstable with large error bars at high QCD thresholds, while KM-ARL and KM-ADD remain stable because they compute from a constant number of sequences regardless of threshold. The ARL-ADD tradeoff curves (Fig. 4) are more accurately estimated by KM-ARL/KM-ADD than by the conventional estimators under both light and heavy censoring scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares KM-ARL/KM-ADD against three baselines: LB-ARL (adapted from Qiu, 2013), Naive ARL, and LB-ADD. KM-ARL dominates LB-ARL on truncation bias: Theorem 4.3 proves that B_TR(KM-ARL) ≥ B_TR(LB-ARL) ≥ 0, meaning KM-ARL's truncation bias is always closer to zero. Empirically (Fig. 2), under heavy censoring (90% changepoints, irregular lengths [30,300]), LB-ARL shows large negative bias while KM-ARL remains close to the true ARL. The Naive ARL has a provably non-vanishing bias because E[τ | τ < ν, τ < T] ≠ E[τ | ν = ∞], and it also discards fewer sequences than LB-ARL but still underestimates. For ADD, KM-ADD similarly outperforms LB-ADD (Theorem 4.4), remaining robust when changepoints are shifted to later timestamps (Fig. 3b–c) where LB-ADD degrades due to fewer observable detection delays. On the WISDM Actitracker, the key practical advantage is variance reduction: KM-ARL/KM-ADD use all sequences in the risk set at each time point, yielding stable estimates across thresholds, while LB-ARL/LB-ADD discard sequences that fail to alarm within the horizon, causing the number of contributing sequences to shrink and variance to explode at high thresholds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges quickest changepoint detection (QCD) and survival analysis by adapting the Kaplan-Meier estimator (Kaplan & Meier, 1958) to model detection probabilities under sequence truncation, with bias bounds built on Stute (1994) and Stute & Wang (1993). Prior QCD evaluation work either assumed infinite sequences (theory), fixed-length truncation with parametric exponential assumptions (Sahki et al., 2020), or semi-parametric Cox models (Bradley et al., 2023); the conventional LB-ARL/LB-ADD estimators (Qiu, 2013) simply discard sequences where no alarm fires. The key departure is a fully non-parametric treatment that handles irregular sequence lengths without distributional assumptions, accompanied by the first formal bias decomposition (finite-sample + truncation) and asymptotic unbiasedness proofs for these estimators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARL and ADD are the standard optimality criteria in QCD theory, yet their estimation on real-world data with limited, irregular-length sequences has been ad hoc and biased; this paper provides the first principled, ready-to-use non-parametric estimators with theoretical guarantees, directly enabling reliable model selection in applications like process control, health monitoring, and seismic sensing. Open questions remain for multiple changepoints, changepoint isolation (classification), probability of false alarms under censoring, and dependent censoring in offline detectors. The extrapolation region (ARL > T_max) remains fundamentally unresolvable without additional parametric assumptions, limiting applicability when sequences are very short relative to the true ARL.
