## Problem

Existing analyses of LLM watermarking rely on detector-specific empirical statistics (z-scores, black-box tests) that cannot compare different watermark families in a common theoretical currency or explain how post-generation edits erode the detection signal. No unified framework simultaneously characterizes detectability across token-level, semantic-level, and distribution-preserving schemes, quantifies robustness under editing, and provides a principled design rule for selecting among families given an anticipated editing regime and stealth constraint.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on autoregressive token sequences of length T (or sentence sequences of length T_s) where at each step the model produces a conditional distribution p_t over the vocabulary, which the watermark modifies to q_t via a keyed rule. The hybrid selection rule consumes the anticipated edit rate ε, the induced semantic flip rate ε_s(ε), text length T, and stealth parameters (M, τ) to output a family selection (distribution-preserving, token-level, or semantic) together with the minimal per-unit information budget D_0 that meets a target verification power 1−β at false-alarm level α. Verification outputs are standard hypothesis-test quantities: AUROC, TPR at 1% FPR for keyed robustness, and a keyless z-score for detectability.

## Method

The core contribution is treating the accumulated KL divergence between the watermarked and baseline sequence distributions as a single 'usable information budget' that simultaneously upper-bounds stealth via Pinsker's inequality (TV ≤ √(KL/2)) and lower-bounds verification power via Neyman-Pearson bounds (requiring C(ε) ≳ log₂(1/β) bits). Under a substitution edit channel, this budget contracts quadratically—as (1−ε)² for token-level schemes and (1−2ε_s)² for semantic schemes—exposing an unavoidable trilemma among robustness, stealth, and reliable verification for fixed-vocabulary LLMs. The hybrid selection rule (Theorem 4.2) picks the family whose required per-unit budget D_req is smallest while staying within the Pinsker-derived stealth cap D_stealth, with the token-versus-semantic comparison reducing to whether (1−2ε_s)²/(1−ε)² > T/T_s. A second stage then selects a concrete scheme within the chosen family using a composite score that rewards post-edit keyed AUROC and penalizes keyless z-score detectability.

## Results

On Llama-2-7B with 500 LFQA prompts, the Hybrid scheme achieves AUROC 0.94–0.96 under moderate paraphrasing (DIPPER ε≈0.25, OPT-2.7B ε≈0.15), matching or exceeding the best single-family baselines (PMark 0.94–0.95, Kuditipudi 0.93–0.94). Under heavy back-translation (ε≈0.42), the Hybrid maintains AUROC 0.86 with z-score 1.8, outperforming all token-level schemes (AUROC 0.55–0.65) and matching PMark (0.85). Under summarization (ε≈0.55), the Hybrid reaches AUROC 0.61 with z-score 0.6, while all token-level schemes collapse to near-random (0.50–0.53). Keyless detectability (z-score) stays below 4.4 for the Hybrid across all conditions, versus 30.1 for KGW and 105 for HCW on clean text.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against biased token-level baselines (KGW, Unigram), the Hybrid wins on both robustness and detectability under every attack: e.g., AUROC 0.94 vs 0.86–0.88 and z-score 4.2 vs 8.8–9.6 under DIPPER. Against bias-free token-level baselines (HCW, Kuditipudi, HeavyWater, SimplexWater), the Hybrid matches or slightly exceeds AUROC under moderate attacks (0.94 vs 0.91–0.94 on DIPPER) but pulls decisively ahead under heavy back-translation (0.86 vs 0.55–0.65) and summarization (0.61 vs 0.52–0.62). Against semantic baselines (PMark, SemStamp, SimMark), the Hybrid ties PMark under moderate paraphrasing (0.94 vs 0.94 on DIPPER, 0.96 vs 0.95 on OPT-2.7B) and edges it out under back-translation (0.86 vs 0.85), while maintaining comparable or lower z-scores. Against the distribution-preserving baseline CGW, the Hybrid trades a small detectability cost (z=4.2 vs −5.8 on clean text) for a large robustness gain under any editing (0.94 vs 0.50 on DIPPER). Against the training-time baseline GaussMark, the Hybrid wins on robustness across all attacks (0.94 vs 0.82 on DIPPER, 0.86 vs 0.62 on back-translation) and on detectability (z=4.2 vs 8.6). Against the co-designed DAWA baseline, the Hybrid dominates on robustness (0.94 vs 0.54 on DIPPER, 0.86 vs 0.75 on back-translation) while DAWA retains a marginal detectability edge in some conditions (z=1.6 vs 4.2 on DIPPER).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not build on a single prior method but rather provides a unifying information-theoretic lens over the entire LLM watermarking landscape, which prior work has analyzed in a fragmented, detector-specific manner. It repurposes standard tools—KL divergence, Pinsker's inequality, and Neyman-Pearson hypothesis testing—as a common currency to place biased token-level (KGW, Unigram), bias-free (HCW, Kuditipudi, HeavyWater), semantic (SemStamp, PMark, SimMark), and distribution-preserving (CGW) schemes on equal footing. The key departure from prior work is the derivation of an impossibility region (Corollary 3.5) and a principled hybrid family-selection rule (Theorem 4.2), neither of which had a theoretical counterpart in the existing literature, where robustness was assessed only through empirical z-scores before and after paraphrasing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework gives practitioners a concrete feasibility boundary: for typical text lengths and watermark strengths, reliable verification becomes information-theoretically impossible beyond roughly 18–32% edit rate depending on the family, which no prior analysis had formalized. The hybrid selection rule offers a deployment-ready decision procedure, but its practical value depends on accurate offline estimation of the edit rate and semantic flip rate, which the paper acknowledges is an open problem under adaptive, watermark-aware adversaries. Extending the analysis to training-time watermarks (GaussMark) and to correlated, non-i.i.d. edit processes beyond the substitution channel model are natural next steps that the paper leaves for future work.
