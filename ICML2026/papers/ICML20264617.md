## Problem

Explicit excitatory-inhibitory (E-I) lateral connections are ubiquitous in cortical circuits and play a central role in sensory processing, yet they are rarely incorporated into task-performing neural network models, and when included, their functional role is often left unjustified or treated as a biological embellishment. This paper asks whether explicit E-I lateral connections in a recurrent network can improve visual encoding capabilities under varying input conditions, and whether they give rise to adaptive coding strategies that explain why cortical activity is neither uniformly sparse nor dense but state-dependent.

## Input / Output

The network receives whitened 9×9 natural image patches (small-scale, 180 E + 180 I neurons) or whitened 32×32 grayscale CIFAR-10 images (large-scale, 14,400 E + 14,400 I neurons) through feedforward weights, with each neuron in the large model receiving input via a 10×10 receptive field arranged on a 144-location cortical sheet with 8-pixel overlap. Grating stimuli (18 orientations, 16 spatial frequencies, 16 phases) are used for tuning characterization. The output is the steady-state population firing rate vector after recurrent dynamics converge (200 inference steps), from which Gabor-like receptive fields, orientation tuning curves, and class labels (via a linear probe) are extracted.

## Method

The core architecture is a Stabilized Supralinear Network (SSN) with explicit E-I lateral connections, supralinear power-law transfer functions (α=2), and strong recurrent excitation stabilized by feedback inhibition in an inhibition-stabilized regime. Training uses only local, biologically plausible plasticity rules: the BCM rule for feedforward connections (driving receptive field formation) and a correlation-measuring (CM) rule for all recurrent connections (E-E, E-I, I-E, I-I), with no global loss function or backpropagation. Weight normalization is applied after each update to maintain constant total incoming synaptic strength per neuron, preventing runaway excitation. The key finding is that the trained network spontaneously transitions between population coding (recruiting many neurons for weak/degraded inputs) and sparse coding (few highly selective neurons for strong inputs) as a function of input contrast, without any explicit sparsity constraint or external supervision. Ablation experiments confirm that E→E connections are necessary for population coding under low contrast, while I→E connections are necessary for sparse coding and dynamical stability.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (14,400-neuron SSN), the trained network consistently outperforms all six baselines under low-contrast conditions, where its population-coding strategy recruits more neurons for robust feature detection. Under increasing Gaussian noise, the SSN shows a slower decline in classification accuracy than all other models, indicating greater representational stability. Ablation experiments confirm that removing E→E lateral connections specifically impairs population coding and degrades low-contrast performance, while removing I→E connections prevents sparse representations and causes unstable activity across contrast levels. The network also spontaneously exhibits contrast-dependent sharpening of orientation tuning and Gabor-like receptive fields matching V1 experimental observations, without any explicit sparsity constraint in the loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the trained SSN against six unsupervised representation-learning baselines (ICA, sparse coding, P-VAE, PredNet, predictive coding, and SimCLR) on CIFAR-10 classification under varying contrast and noise perturbations, with model size matched to ~14,400 latent units. Under low-contrast conditions, the SSN consistently achieves the highest classification accuracy across all six baselines, attributed to its population-coding regime that recruits a larger neural ensemble for weak signals. Under increasing noise, the SSN degrades more gracefully than ICA, sparse coding, P-VAE, predictive coding, and SimCLR, and performs comparably to PredNet. A secondary orientation-classification experiment (180 neurons, Gabor gratings) confirms the same trend: SSN outperforms all baselines under contrast distortion and matches PredNet under noise. Ablation further shows that SSN without E→E connections loses its population-coding advantage at low contrast, while SSN without I→E connections fails on both tasks due to unstable activity, confirming that both excitatory and inhibitory lateral connections are functionally necessary for the adaptive coding strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Stabilized Supralinear Network (SSN) framework (Ahmadian et al., 2013; Rubin et al., 2015), which had previously been limited to hand-crafted configurations or heavily constrained architectures, by demonstrating that SSNs with strong E-I recurrence can be trained end-to-end using only local plasticity rules (BCM for feedforward, CM for recurrent connections). It departs from the classical ICA and sparse coding paradigms (Bell & Sejnowski, 1997; Olshausen & Field, 1996) by arguing that explicit excitatory lateral connections—ubiquitous in cortex but largely absent from encoding models—are not biological embellishments but a functional necessity for input-adaptive coding. The paper also positions itself against the broader DL trend of relying on implicit intralayer interactions (attention, normalization) by showing that explicit E-I recurrence provides a principled mechanism for balancing performance and metabolic cost across input conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete functional justification for explicit E-I lateral connections in cortical circuits, showing they enable a spontaneous, input-dependent switch between population coding (robust, high-cost) and sparse coding (efficient, low-cost) without any explicit sparsity constraint or global regularization. This reframes cortical activity as neither uniformly sparse nor dense but state-dependent, offering a bridge between efficient coding theory and circuit dynamics. Key limitations include the absence of short-term plasticity and spike-frequency adaptation (affecting metabolic cost estimates), evaluation only on CIFAR-10 and small 9×9 patches, and no testing on occlusion, colored noise, or naturalistic video. Whether these adaptive coding strategies translate to practical advantages in neuromorphic hardware or scale to more complex visual tasks remains an open question.
