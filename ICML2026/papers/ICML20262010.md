## Problem

In scene graph generation, predicate representations remain static after training—whether as single prototypes, multi-prototype decompositions, or retrieved exemplars—so they cannot reorganize semantics to reflect image-specific relational evidence. This causes systematic confusions for polysemous predicates (e.g., 'standing on' vs. 'riding') whose meaning shifts depending on contextual cues such as motion, intent, or surrounding scene structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262010/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AlignG takes a single RGB image and a frozen Faster R-CNN (ResNeXt-101-FPN) detector that produces object proposals with bounding boxes and categories. From these, subject-object pairs form relation candidates that are encoded into d-dimensional relation embeddings. The model outputs a scene graph with predicate labels assigned to each edge, evaluated under PredCls (GT boxes and categories given), SGCls (GT boxes only), and SGDet (raw image) settings on VG-150 (50 predicates) and GQA-200 (100 predicates).

## Method

AlignG introduces a two-stage prototype feedback loop. In the first stage, a compatibility-weighted cross-attention aggregates all P relation embeddings for each of R static prototypes, and a GRU cell gated by the normalized static prototype produces an image-conditioned prototype that selectively integrates contextual evidence without overwriting global semantics. In the second stage, a reverse cross-attention propagates the adapted prototypes back to each relation embedding, which is recalibrated by concatenating the raw embedding with the prototype-informed guidance vector and projecting through a learned network. Training combines cross-entropy classification, a prototype regularization loss (similarity penalty plus diversity margin), and a contrastive alignment loss computed against the static prototypes rather than the adapted ones, anchoring the classifier to image-agnostic semantic centers and preventing trivial co-adaptation. The GRU's gating mechanism is critical: ablations show that simpler linear or EMA updates inflate R@100 toward head predicates while collapsing mR@100, whereas the gated update boosts mR@100 by over +5 points at the cost of a small recall drop, achieving the best F@100.

## Results

On VG-150, AlignG† achieves F@100 of 50.3 (PredCls), 30.2 (SGCls), and 23.8 (SGDet), with mR@100 of 42.6, 26.1, and 19.7 respectively. On GQA-200, it reaches F@100 of 43.4 (PredCls), 20.4 (SGCls), and 19.5 (SGDet). The headline gains are +1.4 F@100 over MCL† on VG-150 SGDet and +2.7 F@100 over RA-SGG† on GQA-200 SGDet, with mR@100 improvements of +8.8, +7.2, and +5.2 over the PE-Net backbone across the three VG-150 settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlignG† achieves the best F@100 across all three settings on both benchmarks. On VG-150, it matches MCL† on PredCls F@100 (50.3) but surpasses it by +1.3 (SGCls) and +1.4 (SGDet), while outperforming RA-SGG† by +1.7, +1.6, and +1.9 F@100 respectively. Relative to the PE-Net backbone, gains are +8.8, +7.2, and +5.2 in mR@100, confirming the improvement stems from prototype feedback rather than architectural scale. On GQA-200, AlignG† leads RA-SGG† by +1.0, +1.6, and +2.7 F@100 and DPL† by +2.3, +1.9, and +4.0 F@100 across PredCls, SGCls, and SGDet. Notably, AlignG† trades some R@100 (e.g., 61.3 vs. PE-Net's 67.2 in PredCls) for substantially higher mR@100, indicating better long-tail predicate coverage. Among earlier baselines (KERN, BGNN, Motifs, TDE, NICE, IETrans, CFA, ST-SGG, SSC-SGG, CooK, SQUAT, HetSGG), AlignG† leads on F@100 in every setting, with the largest margins over KERN (+19.1 F@100 SGDet) and TDE (+12.6 F@100 SGDet).

## Relation to Prior Work

AlignG extends the prototype-based SGG lineage initiated by PE-Net, which introduced static predicate prototypes as fixed semantic anchors. While subsequent work (CSGG, MCL, RA-SGG) expanded prototype capacity through multi-concept decomposition or retrieval-augmented memory, all kept prototypes frozen at inference. AlignG departs from this static-prototype paradigm by treating prototypes as context-conditioned variables that adapt per-image via a two-stage cross-attention + GRU feedback loop, then feeds the adapted semantics back to recalibrate relation embeddings. The design is architecturally minimal relative to PE-Net, adding only +7G FLOPs, and explicitly contrasts with retrieval-based and LLM-augmented approaches by deriving all contextual evidence from the image's own relational candidates rather than external sources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that semantic coherence and contextual flexibility can coexist within a unified prototype structure, offering a principled alternative to recall-sacrificing reweighting strategies for long-tail SGG. However, the absolute gains are moderate (+1.4 to +2.7 F@100), and the approach remains validated only on two image-based benchmarks with a frozen detector, leaving open whether context-conditioned adaptation generalizes to video, 3D scenes, or settings with noisier detection. The authors themselves flag that per-image adaptation can be misled by contradictory cues or noisy proposals, and the failure cases in Appendix E (e.g., over-adapting to visual posture over action context) suggest that confidence-aware gating and action-affordance priors are needed before the principle can be broadly deployed.
