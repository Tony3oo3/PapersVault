## Problem

While MoE architectures achieve tensor-level sparsity during pre-training through finer-grained expert configurations, this sparsity remains significantly under-exploited during post-training and inference, where deployment conditions and workloads differ substantially from pre-training scenarios. Static expert configurations established during pre-training fail to capture additional sparsity opportunities at both the tensor and neuron levels that become available in these subsequent stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260368/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained MoE model weights (Mixtral-8×7B, OLMoE, DeepSeek-V2-Lite) and requires only a lightweight calibration pass—a single inference over a small dataset such as MMLU—for neuron importance profiling, with no parameter updates needed for the inference-only optimizations. The complete expert partition transformation is mathematically equivalent to the original model, so the fine-tuned output preserves the same architecture while operating on finer-grained experts (e.g., 8→32). Inference produces standard generated text but with 20–27% of token-expert computations selectively dropped, translating directly into proportional MoE speedup (up to 1.41×).

## Method

The paper proposes three complementary techniques targeting different stages. For post-training, complete expert partition converts a pre-trained MoE model into P× finer-grained experts by replicating gating weights, evenly partitioning neurons, and scaling down-projection weights by P, maintaining mathematical equivalence while amplifying accuracy gains during supervised fine-tuning. For inference, 1T-Drop filters computation noise by dropping token-expert pairs whose normalized gating scores fall below a low threshold (~0.05), which surprisingly improves accuracy by removing low-signal contributions. The key contribution, 2T-Drop, combines partial expert partition with neuron-level reconstruction: each expert is statically split into major and minor sub-experts based on neuron importance profiling (e.g., accumulated absolute gate-up values), and dual thresholds (lower for major, higher for minor) create a tiered computation mode where experts with marginal gating scores compute only their high-importance neurons. Load-aware thresholding further adapts dropping rates per device in expert-parallel deployment, applying higher thresholds to overloaded devices and lower thresholds to underloaded ones to balance accuracy and throughput.

## Results

On Mixtral-8×7B, complete expert partition (P=4) combined with 1T-Drop (T=0.05) improves average accuracy across nine benchmarks from 70.18% to 71.43% (+1.25%), with a notable +4% gain on GSM8K. For the accuracy-efficiency trade-off, 2T-Drop with neuron-level reconstruction achieves a 1.19× MoE speedup at 24% drop rate while maintaining 71.04% average accuracy (0.5% above the fine-tuned baseline). In inference-only settings, 2T-Drop at ~25% drop rate incurs only 0.08%–0.28% average accuracy loss across OLMoE-Instruct and DeepSeek-V2-Lite-Chat, and with load-aware thresholding under EP=8, achieves a 1.41× MoE speedup with 0.5% accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper directly compares against EES (Efficient Expert Skipping) and EEP (Efficient Expert Pruning) from Lu et al. (ACL 2024) on Mixtral-8×7B-Instruct. On GSM8K accuracy, 1T-Drop (accuracy-oriented) achieves +2.5% while EES degrades by −2.4%, and 2T-Drop (Reconstruct) achieves +1.1% versus EES's −2.4%. On speedup, 2T-Drop reaches 1.08× MoE speedup compared to EES's 1.05×, while EEP(r=6) achieves a higher 1.20× speedup but at the cost of −8.0% GSM8K accuracy and −24% memory. The combined EEP+EES pipeline reaches 1.28× speedup but suffers a severe −14.9% GSM8K drop. Overall, the proposed methods consistently outperform both baselines on accuracy at comparable or better speedup, demonstrating that dynamic tensor-level dropping preserves more information than static expert pruning or skipping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the pre-training insight that finer-grained expert configurations (more experts, smaller intermediate sizes) reduce loss (Dai et al. 2024; He 2024; Ludziejewski et al. 2024), but repurposes it as a post-training transformation (complete expert partition) rather than a pre-training design choice. For inference, it departs from static expert pruning (EEP) and dynamic expert skipping (EES) (Lu et al. 2024) by introducing a dual-threshold mechanism that coordinates tensor-level dropping with neuron-level sub-expert reconstruction, addressing the accuracy sensitivity and hardware inefficiency challenges that limit prior weight/activation sparsity methods in SwiGLU-based MoE models. The Soft Expert-Tensor Parallelism (S-ETP) contribution extends hybrid parallelism strategies (Singh et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2025) by achieving tensor-level expert partitioning algorithmically rather than through system-level TP, simplifying the communication pattern to a single AlltoAll.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in providing a training-free, drop-in inference optimization (2T-Drop) that yields 1.19×–1.41× MoE speedup with <0.5% accuracy loss across multiple production MoE models, directly applicable to existing serving frameworks like SGLang. The load-aware thresholding for expert-parallel deployment addresses a real and underexplored bottleneck in distributed MoE inference. Open questions include whether per-layer thresholding and automatic profiling-method selection can further tighten the accuracy-efficiency frontier, whether the noise-reduction interpretation generalizes to next-generation MoE architectures with different gating mechanisms, and whether the modest speedups (1.07×–1.12× end-to-end) remain meaningful as MoE models grow to hundreds of experts where the absolute FLOP savings become larger.
