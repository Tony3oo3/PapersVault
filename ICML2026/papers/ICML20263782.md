## Problem

The Sinkhorn algorithm, the de facto standard for entropic-regularized optimal transport, is GPU-friendly but converges slowly on challenging problems. Quasi-Newton methods like SPLR offer much faster convergence, but their GPU efficiency is severely limited by the serial CPU-bound symbolic analysis required for sparse Cholesky decomposition and irregular memory access patterns, leaving the GPU idle during critical phases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263782/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The solver takes a cost matrix M ∈ R^{n×m} (normalized by its maximum entry), source and target probability vectors a ∈ R^n and b ∈ R^m, and a regularization parameter η (fixed at 0.001 in main experiments). It produces the transport plan T via the dual variables (α, β) as T_ij = exp((α_i + β_j − M_ij)/η), with convergence monitored through the marginal error ‖T1_m − a‖₁ + ‖T^T1_n − b‖₁. Problem sizes range from 1600×1200 to 6400×4800 for synthetic benchmarks and 5000×5000 for CIFAR-10 image-transport instances built from ResNet-18 features.

## Method

cuRegOT builds on the SPLR sparse-plus-low-rank quasi-Newton method and introduces three GPU-oriented designs to eliminate the CPU bottleneck. First, amortized symbolic analysis reuses the sparsity pattern of the Hessian across S iterations (default S=10), reducing expensive CPU-side reordering to once per S steps while a theorem guarantees the condition number remains bounded. Second, a collaborative CPU-GPU mechanism launches asynchronous Sinkhorn iterations on a separate CUDA stream during the CPU symbolic analysis phase, then selects the better of the quasi-Newton and Sinkhorn candidates by objective value, masking CPU latency without extra wall-clock time. Third, a fused CUDA kernel computes the gradient in a single pass over the cost matrix using warp-shuffle intrinsics for row reduction and shared-memory atomics for column reduction, eliminating redundant global memory reads. Theoretical analysis proves that both the pattern reuse and the candidate-selection step preserve global convergence and at least a linear convergence rate of the original SPLR algorithm.

## Results

The paper evaluates solvers using marginal error (‖T·1_m − a‖₁ + ‖Tᵀ·1_n − b‖₁) plotted on a logarithmic scale against wall-clock time, with no single numerical summary table. Across synthetic benchmarks (sizes up to 6400×4800) and CIFAR-10 (5000×5000, η=0.001), cuRegOT achieves significantly faster convergence than all Sinkhorn-based baselines, with the gap widening at higher precision targets and larger problem sizes. At smaller regularization (η=0.0001), where Sinkhorn variants deteriorate rapidly due to ill-conditioning, cuRegOT maintains fast convergence, demonstrating the practical importance of second-order curvature information. The ablation study confirms both the amortized symbolic analysis and the asynchronous Sinkhorn candidate generation contribute to the overall speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

cuRegOT outperforms all three GPU-based Sinkhorn baselines across every tested problem (synthetic I iid/diff, synthetic II, and CIFAR-10 class pairs) on the marginal-error-vs-wall-time metric. POT Sinkhorn and OTT-JAX Sinkhorn produce nearly identical convergence curves, confirming their comparable GPU efficiency; OTT-JAX with Anderson acceleration (memory=5) provides a visible speedup over plain Sinkhorn on Synthetic I and CIFAR-10 but still converges slowly at high precision, and can even be slower than plain Sinkhorn on the multi-modal Synthetic II problem. AccSinkhorn (the CuPy implementation of Lin et al.'s accelerated Sinkhorn) also trails cuRegOT, particularly at larger problem sizes where cuRegOT's advantage grows. Greenkhorn (POT) is excluded from all plots due to near-flat convergence curves. The paper does not report explicit speedup ratios, but the convergence plots consistently show cuRegOT reaching a given error threshold in substantially less wall time, with the gap most pronounced at η=0.0001 and at the 6400×4800 problem size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SPLR sparse-plus-low-rank quasi-Newton method (Wang & Qiu, 2025) for entropic-regularized OT, which itself extends earlier sparse Newton approaches (SNS, SSNS) by incorporating the specific Hessian structure of the OT dual. The key departure is in GPU system design: while prior Newton-type OT solvers were confined to CPUs or suffered severe GPU under-utilization due to serial sparse symbolic analysis, cuRegOT introduces amortized pattern reuse, asynchronous CPU-GPU overlap with Sinkhorn candidate generation, and fused CUDA kernels to make the quasi-Newton method practical on modern accelerators. It positions itself against the dominant GPU baselines—POT Sinkhorn, OTT-JAX (with Anderson acceleration), and accelerated Sinkhorn (Lin et al., 2022)—which all rely on first-order iterations and converge slowly at high precision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As OT becomes a standard component in domain adaptation, generative modeling, and single-cell biology, the ability to solve large-scale entropic-regularized OT quickly on GPUs is a practical bottleneck that this paper addresses with both algorithmic and systems-level contributions. The theoretical guarantee that amortized sparsity-pattern reuse and candidate-iterate selection preserve global convergence and linear rate is important for confidence in the engineering modifications. Open questions include whether the sparsity-pattern stability assumption holds for all problem structures, whether the approach extends to unregularized or non-entropic OT formulations, and whether further elimination of the remaining CPU-side symbolic step (e.g., via iterative or mixed-precision sparse solvers) can close the last gap in GPU utilization.
