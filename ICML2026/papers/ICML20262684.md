## Problem

Current VLA models trained on goal-driven robotic datasets suffer from Information Collapse: because language instructions are nearly deterministic given visual observations (H(ℓ|v)≈0), the conditional mutual information between instructions and actions vanishes, causing policies to degenerate into vision-only shortcuts that ignore language. This leads to catastrophic failures in ambiguous scenes where multiple tasks share the same visual context, and in out-of-distribution environments where spurious visual correlations no longer hold.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB visual observation and a natural language instruction, producing a continuous action trajectory via a Diffusion Transformer. Rather than feeding all VLM token hidden states to the action head (as in π₀ or GR00T), only the hidden states of 64 learnable Latent Action Query tokens are forwarded, creating a fixed-size bottleneck that decouples DiT conditioning complexity from input sequence length. At inference, only the Posteriori Branch is executed, incurring zero additional computational overhead over standard VLA baselines.

## Method

LangForce introduces a dual-branch architecture with shared VLM weights that instantiates a Bayesian decomposition of the policy. The Priori Branch arranges tokens as [v, Q, ℓ] so that causal masking restricts the Latent Action Queries to attend only to vision, learning the vision-only prior p(a|v); the Posteriori Branch arranges them as [v, ℓ, Q] so queries attend to both modalities, learning the full policy π(a|v,ℓ). Both branches feed their query hidden states into a shared DiT action decoder trained with Rectified Flow Matching. The key training signal is a Log-Likelihood Ratio (LLR) objective that maximizes the conditional Pointwise Mutual Information between actions and instructions, computed as the difference in language-token log-probabilities between the two branches with a stop-gradient on the baseline term. This explicitly penalizes the vision shortcut by rewarding only actions that make the instruction more probable than vision alone would predict, forcing genuine language grounding without requiring new data.

## Results

On SimplerEnv (OOD evaluation), LangForce achieves a state-of-the-art average success rate of 66.5%, delivering an absolute +11.3% improvement over the direct QwenGR00T baseline (55.2%) built on the same StarVLA framework. On RoboCasa (24 tasks), LangForce reaches 52.6% average success, surpassing QwenOFT (48.8%), Isaac-GR00T N1.5 (48.2%), and QwenGR00T (47.8%). On LIBERO, LangForce achieves 98.4% average, with the largest gain on the ambiguous Goal suite (99.4% vs. 97.4% baseline), confirming that the method specifically targets the vision shortcut in ambiguous scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LangForce wins across all 13 baselines on SimplerEnv, with the most meaningful comparison being the +11.3% gain over QwenGR00T (55.2%→66.5%), the same-architecture baseline that isolates the contribution of the Bayesian decomposition. It also surpasses the two strongest recent competitors—π₀.₅ (57.1%) and Isaac-GR00T-N1.6 (57.1%)—by 9.4 points, and beats VideoVLA (53.1%) and π₀ (53.1%) by 13.4 points. On RoboCasa, LangForce (52.6%) outperforms QwenOFT (48.8%), Isaac-GR00T N1.5 (48.2%), QwenGR00T (47.8%), and Isaac-GR00T N1.6 (47.6%), with the largest per-task gain on 'PnP Novel From Placemat To Bowl' (62.0% vs. 44.0% QwenGR00T). On LIBERO, LangForce (98.4%) edges out OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%), π₀.₅ (96.9%), and Qwen3-VL-GR00T (96.5%), with the decisive +2.0% margin on the Goal suite (99.4% vs. 97.4%) where visual ambiguity is highest. The VisionOnly ablation (44.7% on RoboCasa, 9.8% on LIBERO Goal) confirms that standard VLA models largely ignore language, and LangForce's gains are concentrated precisely in the ambiguous/OOD settings where this shortcut fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

LangForce builds on the StarVLA/GR00T dual-system architecture (VLM backbone + Diffusion Transformer action head) and the Rectified Flow Matching training objective, but departs from them in two key ways: it replaces the standard full-sequence conditioning with a compact set of Latent Action Queries as a bottleneck, and it introduces a dual-branch Bayesian training objective that contrasts a vision-only prior against a language-conditioned posterior. The closest prior work is BayesVLA (Xu et al., 2025), which also performs a Bayesian factorization of the VLA policy, but requires a two-stage pipeline with a frozen vision prior; LangForce achieves the same decomposition in a single end-to-end training stage. The information-theoretic framing (maximizing conditional PMI via a Log-Likelihood Ratio) is the paper's central conceptual departure from standard imitation-learning objectives used across the VLA literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a pervasive and previously under-appreciated pathology—information collapse due to goal-driven data collection—that likely affects most existing VLA models, and provides a practical, drop-in fix that requires no new data and adds no inference overhead. The 11.3% OOD improvement on SimplerEnv and the preservation of general VLM conversational abilities (vs. catastrophic forgetting in baselines) suggest the approach could be widely adopted as a post-training regularizer. Open questions include whether the method extends to contact-rich or dexterous manipulation (current real-world experiments are limited to pick-and-place), whether the dual-branch overhead becomes prohibitive at larger model scales, and whether the information-collapse diagnosis holds for non-goal-driven data collection paradigms such as human video.
