## Problem

Existing multimodal time-series forecasting methods directly fuse raw textual embeddings with numerical observations, but a fundamental modality gap prevents effective translation: text expresses temporal impacts implicitly and qualitatively, while forecasters require explicit quantitative signals. Controlled semi-synthetic experiments reveal that current fusion mechanisms systematically over-attend to redundant tokens and, even after removing redundancy, still fail to decode qualitative textual semantics into predictive numerical gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262603/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a historical numerical window of length L (multivariate, d-dimensional) and temporally aligned free-form text (news articles, announcements) as input, and produces a numerical forecast of length H. The text is consumed offline by a frozen LLM (Qwen3-8B) that outputs discrete primitive labels; the online forecaster then operates solely on compact gated semantic vectors plus the numerical sequence, decoupling expensive LLM inference from the training loop. All four primitives have finite, small value domains (5–6 categories each), making the semantic representation a low-dimensional discrete code rather than a continuous embedding.

## Method

TESS introduces a Temporal Evolution Semantic Space as an intermediate bottleneck between text and numerical series, consisting of four interpretable primitives—distribution shift (mean and volatility), evolution shape (inter-patch trend pattern), and lag-and-decay (onset timing and persistence)—each with a small discrete value domain. A frozen LLM extracts primitive labels via structured prompting, and a learnable confidence-aware gating network (trained with binary cross-entropy against ground-truth primitive values computed from the forecast window) suppresses unreliable extractions by scaling each semantic embedding by its estimated correctness probability. The gated semantic vectors are prepended as prefix tokens to PatchTST patch embeddings, allowing a standard Transformer encoder to fuse semantic conditions with temporal dynamics through self-attention. Training is end-to-end (LLM frozen) with a combined MSE forecasting loss and gating supervision, and the paper provides information-theoretic generalization bounds showing that restricting the encoder to the semantic space preserves predictive information while tightening the expected generalization gap.

## Results

TESS achieves up to 29.1% MSE reduction on Bitcoin (2.2726 vs. 3.2036 for the best baseline NewsForecasting) and 20.0% MSE reduction on FNSPID (0.0012 vs. 0.0015 for TimesNet), demonstrating strong gains on event-driven non-stationary financial data. On general datasets, TESS attains the best MSE on Electricity (0.0230, a 5.0% improvement over TimesNet) and runner-up on Environment (0.3534, within 1.8% of Nonstationary Transformer). Across all four datasets, TESS outperforms every unimodal and multimodal baseline on at least two of the three metrics (MAE, MSE, RMSE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the two financial datasets with pronounced non-stationarity, TESS dominates all baselines: on Bitcoin it beats the strongest multimodal baseline NewsForecasting by 18.2% MAE, 29.1% MSE, and 15.8% RMSE, and on FNSPID it surpasses the best unimodal model TimesNet by 3.3% MAE and 20.0% MSE. Among unimodal baselines, PatchTST and TimesNet are the strongest on Bitcoin and FNSPID respectively, while Nonstationary Transformer leads on Environment; TESS outperforms all of them on the three financial/electricity datasets. Among multimodal baselines, NewsForecasting is the closest competitor on Bitcoin (MSE 3.2036 vs. TESS 2.2726), TimeLLM is strongest on FNSPID (MSE 0.0017 vs. 0.0012), and ChatTime is consistently the weakest multimodal method across all four datasets. On the Environment dataset, TESS is a narrow runner-up to Nonstationary Transformer (MSE 0.3534 vs. 0.3472, a 1.8% gap), indicating that the semantic-space approach provides the largest gains precisely where event-driven non-stationarity is most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

TESS departs from the dominant multimodal time-series forecasting paradigm (TimeLLM, ChatTime, NewsForecasting) that directly fuses raw text embeddings with numerical features via cross-attention or concatenation. Instead, it introduces an intermediate Temporal Evolution Semantic Space—a structured bottleneck of four discrete temporal primitives (distribution shift, volatility, shape, lag)—extracted by a frozen LLM via structured prompting, then injected as prefix tokens into a PatchTST backbone. The confidence-aware gating mechanism, supervised by numerically verifiable ground-truth labels from the forecast window, is a novel addition that has no direct analogue in prior multimodal fusion work. The theoretical grounding draws on the Xu & Raginsky information-theoretic generalization framework and standard Lipschitz stability arguments, applied to the specific setting of semantic compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semi-synthetic diagnostic revealing that even with all redundant tokens removed, textual signals still underperform equivalent numerical features is a useful conceptual finding: it shifts the problem from 'filter noise' to 'bridge a representational gap,' motivating the intermediate semantic space design. However, the semantic sufficiency assumption (that four fixed primitives capture all task-relevant text content) is explicitly scoped and may fail for seasonality, periodicity, or fine-grained causal effects. Open questions include whether primitives can be learned data-driven rather than hand-designed, whether the approach generalizes to longer horizons or higher-frequency data, and whether the modest gains on general (non-financial) datasets indicate diminishing returns when non-stationarity is mild.
