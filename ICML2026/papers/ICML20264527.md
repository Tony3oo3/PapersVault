## Problem

Existing bias-auditing methods for text-to-image diffusion models either target predefined fairness categories (closed-set) or identify majority attributes that dominate outputs (open-set). Neither approach systematically discovers minority attributes that are already encoded in the model's internal representations but consistently underexpressed during generation, leaving a critical gap in understanding what the model suppresses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264527/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt and a target diffusion model, RAIGen extracts 1280-dimensional bottleneck vectors from each spatial location (8×8 grid) at the final denoising timestep across 5,000 generated images, then trains an MSAE on these vectors. It outputs a ranked set of coarse-level MSAE neurons identified as encoding minority attributes, each accompanied by a language annotation (via GPT-5.2), top-activating images with spatial heatmaps, and optionally a revised prompt that injects the discovered attribute descriptors for targeted amplification.

## Method

RAIGen trains a Matryoshka Sparse Autoencoder on flattened bottleneck representations from the diffusion model, using the coarsest sparsity level (k₁=2048) to obtain semantically coherent, interpretable features. The core contribution is the Minority Score s(zᵢ) = dᵢ · (1 − νᵢ), a product of activation frequency (fraction of samples where the neuron fires) and semantic distinctiveness (cosine distance between the neuron's CLIP-activation-weighted centroid and the dataset-wide centroid), which jointly filters out rare-but-noisy neurons and coherent-but-frequent concepts. Redundancy is removed by greedily selecting neurons in descending score order while pruning those within a fixed cosine distance of an already-retained centroid. The pipeline is label-free—requiring no predefined minority categories—and is validated across SD v1.4, SDXL, and FLUX.1-schnell, with a controlled toy experiment confirming that low activation frequency reliably tracks true feature rarity under near-ideal alignment.

## Results

On WinoBias and COCO, RAIGen-discovered minority attributes achieve Attribute Presence of 0.194–0.220 across SD v1.4 and SDXL, confirming they are genuinely underrepresented in default generations. In contrast, OpenBias majority attributes appear in 93–94% of samples, validating that RAIGen isolates a complementary set of suppressed features. A user study with 25 participants confirms that RAIGen attributes appear in fewer than 3 out of 10 images on average (e.g., 0.70/10 for CEO, 1.18/10 for Doctor). RAIGen-guided prompt revision reduces attribute-presence deviation from ~0.50 to ~0.22–0.25 while preserving prompt alignment (CLIP score drops <1 point).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAIGen is compared against OpenBias (D'Incà et al., 2024), which targets majority/overrepresented attributes. The two methods are complementary rather than directly competing: OpenBias attributes appear in 93–94% of generated images, while RAIGen attributes appear in only 19–22%, confirming that RAIGen surfaces a distinct set of suppressed features that OpenBias does not capture. Against Minority Guidance (Um et al., 2024), a statistical-rarity sampling method, RAIGen serves an upstream discovery role: Minority Guidance amplifies any low-density attribute indiscriminately (e.g., boosting 'animals' from 0.150 to 0.382 for the prompt 'a photo of an assistant'), which can be contextually irrelevant, whereas RAIGen identifies which specific attributes are encoded but suppressed, leaving the amplification decision to the practitioner. In a direct comparison with CLIP k-means clustering (Appendix G.6), RAIGen achieves substantially lower Attribute Presence across all six WinoBias professions (average 0.18 vs. 0.37), demonstrating that neuron-level MSAE decomposition isolates more precisely suppressed attributes than image-level clustering.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAIGen departs from two established lines of bias work in text-to-image diffusion: closed-set mitigation methods (Chuang et al., Friedrich et al., Li et al.) that assume fairness categories are known a priori, and open-set detection methods (D'Incà et al., OpenBias) that identify majority/dominant attributes via LLM-suggested probes. The key conceptual shift is from identifying what models overproduce to discovering what they suppress: attributes already encoded in internal representations but systematically underexpressed during generation. For the interpretability mechanism, the paper builds on Sparse Autoencoders for diffusion models (Kim et al., Cywiński & Deja, Surkov et al.) and specifically adopts Matryoshka SAEs (Bussmann et al., Pach et al.) for their hierarchical coarse-to-fine decomposition, arguing that the coarsest level yields more stable, semantically coherent attribute hypotheses than finer levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAIGen provides the first label-free, representation-grounded mechanism for auditing which attributes a diffusion model has learned but fails to express, filling a gap that neither predefined fairness categories nor majority-attribute detection can address. The cross-model analysis (SD v1.4 → v2.1 → SDXL) revealing that scaling shifts the *type* of underrepresentation (demographic gaps narrow while stylistic and contextual diversity collapses) is practically important for model developers. Key limitations include: only attributes already encoded in the representation space can be discovered (entirely absent groups remain invisible), the pipeline is prompt-specific and computationally non-trivial (~72 min per prompt on A100), and transformer-based architectures (FLUX) yield less spatially grounded features. The paper explicitly positions RAIGen as complementary to LLM-based tools, motivating future hybrid frameworks that combine internal representation discovery with external semantic priors.
