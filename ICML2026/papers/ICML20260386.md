## Problem

Standard greedy decoding in Diffusion Language Models commits to the highest-confidence positions at each denoising step, but this local choice can lock the model into a suboptimal unmasking order, particularly on reasoning-heavy prompts. While exploring alternative unmasking sequences (position-space search) improves quality, it scales linearly with beam width and roughly doubles inference latency. The paper asks how to retain the quality gains from position-space search without incurring prohibitive decoding cost.

## Input / Output

SOAR takes a text prompt and a pre-trained mask-based DLM (DREAM-7B or LLADA-8B) as input, starting from a fully masked sequence of fixed maximum length (256 or 512 tokens). It produces a generated text sequence through iterative denoising, with a model-dependent confidence threshold (τ=0.95 for LLADA-8B, τ=0.90 for DREAM-7B) controlling the search/acceleration switch. The method is entirely training-free and operates at inference time without modifying model weights or architecture.

## Method

SOAR is a training-free confidence-switched position beam search that dynamically alternates between two decoding modes at each denoising step. When at least one masked position exceeds the confidence threshold τ, SOAR enters parallel decoding mode and unmask all such positions simultaneously; when no position exceeds τ, it falls back to position beam search (PBS), exploring the top-K most confident positions one token at a time with a cumulative average-confidence score. A dynamic candidate-size rule then collapses the beam to 1 if the best new candidate originated from parallel decoding (high confidence), or resets it to K if the best came from PBS (uncertainty), concentrating compute only where needed. The scoring function uses the mean confidence of selected positions rather than the sum, preventing sequences with more remaining masked tokens from being unfairly penalized. This design yields O(T·N̄) complexity where N̄ stays well below K in practice because most tokens exceed the threshold, achieving a quality-speed trade-off that dominates both pure greedy decoding and full position beam search.

## Results

On DREAM-7B-Base, SOAR improves average accuracy from 60.1 (greedy) to 62.4 (+2.3) across HumanEval, MBPP, and GSM8K, with the largest single-benchmark gain of +5.5 on HumanEval (256). On LLADA-8B-Base, SOAR lifts average accuracy from 47.8 to 49.2 (+1.4). Crucially, SOAR maintains a speedup of ×1.43 (DREAM-7B) and ×1.62 (LLADA-8B) over greedy decoding, whereas full Position Beam Search (single-token) achieves higher accuracy (63.0 / 48.9) but at roughly half the speed (×0.54 / ×0.48).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Prophet (Li et al., 2025), an early-termination method, SOAR+Prophet improves HumanEval from 37.5 to 40.2 and GSM8K from 75.4 to 77.2 on LLADA-8B-Instruct, while also increasing speedup (×2.27 vs ×1.80 on HumanEval). Against SlowFast Sampling (Wei et al., 2026) on GSM8K (LLADA-8B-Instruct, Block Diffusion, max length 256), SOAR achieves 78.1 accuracy vs 75.1 for SlowFast under lm-evaluation-harness, but SlowFast is substantially faster (×2.93 vs ×2.04 speedup). Against WINO (Hong et al., 2025), SOAR wins on accuracy in both evaluation frameworks (82.3 vs 81.2 on the WINO codebase; 78.1 vs 76.7 on lm-eval-harness), though WINO delivers a larger speedup (×3.67 vs ×2.04). In summary, SOAR consistently outperforms all three baselines on generation quality, trading some of the extreme speedups that pure acceleration methods achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the classic beam search paradigm (Freitag & Al-Onaizan, 2017) from the token/vocabulary space of autoregressive decoding to a new dimension specific to diffusion language models: the position space, i.e., the order in which masked positions are unmasked. Its parallel decoding mode is directly inspired by Fast-dLLM's confidence-based parallel unmasking (Wu et al., 2025), but SOAR departs by adding a beam-search fallback when confidence drops below a threshold, rather than simply decoding fewer tokens. The method is positioned as complementary to concurrent search-based work like Order-Token Search (Shen et al., 2026), which explores both position and token dimensions at higher cost, and to acceleration methods like SlowFast Sampling and WINO, which prioritize throughput over quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOAR offers a practical, training-free mechanism for DLM practitioners to improve generation quality while retaining competitive inference speed, addressing a core tension in DLM decoding where position-space search helps quality but linearly increases latency. Key limitations remain: token-level confidence is an imperfect proxy for sequence-level correctness (failure cases show locally confident but globally wrong branches), overconfident models may under-activate the search mode, and the confidence threshold is model-dependent. Open questions include whether span-level or verifier-based scoring can replace average token confidence, and whether the observed correlation between lower AR-ness and higher accuracy reflects a causal mechanism in unmasking order.
