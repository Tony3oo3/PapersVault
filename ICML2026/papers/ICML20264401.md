## Problem

Existing dense associative memories operate exclusively on vector representations in Euclidean space, yet modern representation learning (Gaussian word/sentence embeddings, VAE latent codes, Bayesian posteriors) naturally produces probability distributions as the fundamental data object. This paper asks whether associative memory can be generalized to store and retrieve probability distributions directly, preserving the geometric structure of the space of measures rather than flattening it into a vector.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264401/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query Gaussian N(m, Ω) and a bank of N stored Gaussians {N(μ_i, Σ_i)} whose covariance eigenvalues lie in [λ_min, λ_max], and iteratively produces a retrieved Gaussian via the Φ operator until convergence to a fixed point. In experiments, queries are generated by perturbing a stored Gaussian by W_2 distance √λ_min (affecting both mean and covariance), and retrieval is deemed successful when the nearest stored pattern in W_2 distance matches the original. The per-iteration cost is O(Nd³) due to eigendecompositions of d×d matrices for each stored pattern.

## Method

The core contribution is a log-sum-exp energy E(ξ) = -(1/β) log Σ_i exp(-β W_2²(X_i, ξ)) defined on the Bures-Wasserstein space, whose Wasserstein gradient aggregates optimal transport maps T_i from the query to each stored Gaussian with Gibbs weights w_i(ξ). Retrieval is performed by iterating the fixed-point map Φ(ξ) = (Σ_i w_i(ξ) T_i)_# ξ, which for Gaussians reduces to a closed-form update of the mean (weighted average of stored means) and covariance (weighted transport coefficient applied to the query covariance). Stationary points are self-consistent Wasserstein barycenters, generalizing classical DAM fixed points. The paper proves exponential storage capacity N = ⌊√p · e^{αd}⌋ with basin radius r = √λ_min independent of N (unlike Euclidean DAM where basins shrink as 1/√(βN)), geometric convergence at rate L < 1, and one-step retrieval error decaying as O(√d · e^{-γd}).

## Results

On CelebA and CIFAR-10 image retrieval (20% pixel masking), BW-DAM substantially outperforms both Eu-DAM and Pixel-DAM across all inverse temperature values β, achieving near-perfect retrieval accuracy for β ≥ 1 on CIFAR-10 while the baselines plateau at significantly lower levels. On the text8 word-embedding task (N = 11,815 words, 50-dim Gaussians), BW-DAM exhibits a sharp phase transition: retrieval accuracy is low for small β but approaches 100% for large β. On synthetic Gaussian spheres (N = 1000, d = 10 and d = 20), BW-DAM converges to the correct stored Gaussian in a single iteration at β = 2, while at β = 0.1 retrieval fails entirely with monotonically increasing W₂ distance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares BW-DAM against two baselines derived from the Euclidean dense associative memory of Ramsauer et al. (2020): Eu-DAM, which vectorizes Gaussian parameters (μ, Σ) into ℝ^{d+d(d+1)/2} and applies the standard softmax-weighted update, and Pixel-DAM, which operates directly on flattened pixel vectors. BW-DAM wins decisively on all four datasets (CelebA, CIFAR-10, text8, NLI). On CIFAR-10, BW-DAM reaches near-100% retrieval accuracy for β ≥ 1, while Eu-DAM and Pixel-DAM plateau well below this level; qualitatively, Eu-DAM converges to incorrect attractors in all five illustrated cases. The paper attributes Eu-DAM's failure to the mismatch between the Frobenius norm on stacked covariance entries and the Bures metric, and Pixel-DAM's failure to the absence of semantic structure in pixel-space Euclidean distance. On the synthetic 2D experiment (Figure 1), Eu-DAM fails on 2 of 5 retrievals while BW-DAM succeeds on all 5 from identical initializations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dense associative memory lineage from Hopfield (1982) through Krotov & Hopfield's LSE energy (2016) and Ramsauer et al.'s exponential-capacity proof (2020) by replacing the Euclidean vector space with the Bures-Wasserstein manifold of Gaussian measures. The key conceptual departure is working directly with optimal transport maps and Wasserstein barycenters rather than vectorizing Gaussian parameters (mean and covariance) into a flat vector, which the paper shows leads to retrieval failures. The geometric machinery draws on the Bures-Wasserstein structure developed by Asuka (2011) and Lambert et al. (2022), while the empirical evaluation connects to Gaussian embedding methods in NLP (Word2Gauss, GaussCSE) and VAE-based image encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing that associative memory can operate on probability distributions with exponential storage capacity and basin radii independent of the number of stored patterns (unlike the 1/√(βN) shrinkage in Euclidean DAM), this work opens a principled path toward distributional memory architectures for uncertainty-aware generative modeling and probabilistic reasoning. Key limitations include the restriction to Gaussian families, the need for a separation condition that is not verified on real data, and the O(Nd³) per-iteration cost. Open questions include extending to non-Gaussian distributions (e.g., empirical measures for point clouds), particle-based retrieval, and whether the barycentric fixed-point structure can be integrated into end-to-end trainable generative pipelines.
