## Problem

Existing generalist graph anomaly detection methods align heterogeneous features across domains using PCA or SVD, which only matches feature dimensionality while ignoring semantic discrepancies. This leads to widespread negative transfer, where source-domain pre-training actually degrades performance on unseen target graphs. The paper asks how to construct truly transferable, semantics-aware features that bridge heterogeneous graph domains for generalist anomaly detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262874/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an attributed graph (node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and adjacency matrix A) along with a few-shot support set of k normal and k anomalous labeled nodes from the target domain. Raw features of arbitrary dimensionality are first converted into a fixed 5-dimensional relational fingerprint (REFI) per node via rank-based percentile transformation, ensuring a unified input space regardless of the original feature dimension. The model outputs a per-node anomaly score in [0,1] computed via softmax-weighted cosine similarity against the support set, with no parameter updates during target-domain inference.

## Method

REFI-GAD replaces raw feature alignment with a Relational Fingerprint (REFI): a 5-dimensional vector per node encoding neighborhood positional consistency (via similarity-aware graph convolution that re-weights edges by feature similarity to avoid masking anomalies), neighborhood directional consistency (cosine similarity with neighbors), global directional consistency (alignment with the graph's dominant manifold), degree, and local clustering coefficient. All five attributes undergo rank-based percentile transformation to unify scales across domains. A fingerprint-grounded model then processes REFI through an MLP projector and a context-aware Transformer with a structured attention mask that isolates support nodes from query nodes, enabling domain-conditioned encoding. An SNR-guided refinement module computes dimension-wise signal-to-noise ratios between normal and anomalous centers to adaptively re-weight representation dimensions for the target domain. The model is trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy in an episodic few-shot manner on source graphs, and performs training-free inference on unseen target graphs.

## Results

REFI-GAD achieves the best average AUROC rank of 1.14 across 14 datasets spanning citation, social, e-commerce, and behavioral domains, evaluated under a strict cross-domain protocol (train on one group, test on the other). It yields an average AUROC improvement of 7.39% over the strongest generalist baseline (IA-GGAD) and 15.57% over the best traditional GAD method (SmoothGNN). Notably, REFI-GAD achieves positive transfer on nearly all datasets, whereas existing generalist methods (ARC, AnomalyGFM, UNPrompt) suffer negative average improvements after source-domain training. On the fully cross-domain citation transfer setting, REFI-GAD still leads IA-GGAD by 4.76% average AUROC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REFI-GAD outperforms all 14 baselines on 13 out of 14 datasets in AUROC, with the sole exception being Amazon (71.21 vs. IA-GGAD's 82.49), where anomalies are primarily feature-driven rather than structure-driven. Against the strongest generalist baseline IA-GGAD, REFI-GAD gains 7.39% average AUROC, with particularly large margins on Flickr (+21.75%), YelpChi (+30.95%), and Facebook (+14.91%). Compared to the best traditional GAD method SmoothGNN, the improvement is 15.57% on average, though SmoothGNN remains competitive on Weibo (85.96 vs. 92.66) and Cora (88.39 vs. 96.14). Among other generalist methods, ARC (rank 3.50) and UNPrompt (rank 5.93) are clearly surpassed, while AnomalyGFM (rank 12.21) performs near-random on most datasets. In the fully cross-domain citation transfer setting (Table 4), REFI-GAD leads IA-GGAD by 4.76% average AUROC and achieves positive transfer (+4.15%) where all other generalist methods show negative average gains from source-domain training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant feature-alignment strategy in generalist GAD (ARC, AnomalyGFM, UNPrompt, IA-GGAD), which relies on PCA/SVD to unify heterogeneous feature dimensions across domains. The authors argue this only matches dimensionality while preserving semantic discrepancies, and instead replace raw features entirely with a 5-dimensional Relational Fingerprint (REFI) encoding neighborhood positional/directional consistency, global directional consistency, degree, and clustering coefficient. The model architecture draws on transformer-based in-context learning (inherited from ARC) and introduces an SNR-guided dimension-weighting module for domain-adaptive refinement, while the similarity-aware graph convolution and rank-based transformation are novel design choices specific to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a fundamental weakness in the generalist GAD paradigm: source-domain pre-training often causes negative transfer rather than positive transfer, suggesting that existing methods' generalization stems from architectural inductive biases rather than learned cross-domain knowledge. The relational fingerprint approach offers a principled alternative for cross-domain feature alignment that could inform other heterogeneous-data transfer settings. Key limitations include the requirement of few-shot contextual samples at inference and the manual design of the five REFI dimensions; the authors identify zero-shot generalist GAD and automated fingerprint learning as natural next steps. The scaling-law experiments (data size × parameter count) hint at foundation-model potential but remain preliminary.
