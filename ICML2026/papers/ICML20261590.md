## Problem

Recent work shows that high-dimensional, randomly initialized networks can already exhibit substantial alignment with primate visual cortex, suggesting brain-relevant representations are discovered rather than fully learned through task optimization. However, it remains unclear how such latent, overcomplete representations are selectively stabilized, refined, and retained during biological development. This paper addresses that gap by proposing activity-dependent synaptic pruning as a computational mechanism for distilling informative, brain-aligned representations from an initially overcomplete spiking substrate.

## Input / Output

The model takes natural RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32 or Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64) and applies stochastic augmentations (random crops, color jitter, horizontal flipping, grayscale) to generate positive pairs for contrastive learning. The spiking encoder (ResNet-18/34 with LIF neurons simulated over T=4 timesteps) produces temporal spike-train embeddings via membrane-potential averaging, which are then projected into a contrastive space. Downstream, the frozen encoder representations are evaluated for neural predictivity (SVCCA, RSA, TSVDR) against mouse and macaque cortical recordings and for linear-probe classification accuracy under few-shot (20–80% labels) and noisy (Gaussian, salt-and-pepper) conditions.

## Method

The core contribution is a deep unsupervised spiking neural network that integrates a SimCLR-style temporal contrastive objective with activity-dependent synaptic pruning. The contrastive loss operates over T=4 timesteps of LIF spike trains, maximizing cosine similarity between augmented views of the same image while minimizing similarity to other images in the batch, with a lightweight projection head discarded after training. For pruning, the paper introduces a temporal cosine similarity metric that measures directional alignment between original and pruned synaptic weight vectors across all timesteps; synapses with consistently low temporal alignment are removed at a target ratio to emulate developmental refinement. Starting from an overcomplete spiking architecture, the network self-organizes through sensory-driven activity and progressively eliminates weak or redundant connections, yielding compact hierarchical representations that align with neural responses across mouse (VISp, VISl, VISrl, VISal, VISpm, VISam) and macaque (V1, V4, IT, AM face patch) visual cortex without any category labels.

## Results

On the Allen Brain Mouse dataset (CIFAR-10 pretraining, ResNet-18), Pr. UL SNN* achieves the highest neural predictivity with SVCCA 0.6308 and RSA 0.4608, substantially exceeding the best supervised baseline (SL ANN: SVCCA 0.6075, RSA 0.1852) and the supervised SNN baseline SEW (SVCCA 0.6065, RSA 0.2603). On the Macaque Face dataset, Pr. UL SNN* reaches SVCCA 0.4687 and TSVDR 0.4636 (ResNet-18, CIFAR-10), outperforming all other models. For downstream few-shot transfer (20% labels, Tiny-ImageNet→CIFAR-10), Pr. UL SNN achieves 63.11% Top-1 accuracy, and under 20% Gaussian noise it reaches 59.94%, both leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SimCLR-based UL ANN baseline (Zhuang et al., 2021), the proposed UL SNN and Pr. UL SNN achieve dramatically higher RSA on Allen Brain Mouse (0.4279/0.4608 vs. 0.1849/0.1868 for ResNet-18/34) and higher TSVDR on Macaque Face (0.4533/0.4636 vs. 0.4455/0.4449), indicating that spiking dynamics plus unsupervised learning capture representational geometry far better than rate-based contrastive ANNs. Against the supervised SEW-ResNet (Fang et al., 2021), UL SNN wins on RSA by a wide margin (0.4279 vs. 0.2603 on Allen Brain Mouse) and on TSVDR for Macaque Face (0.4533 vs. 0.4439), while SEW remains competitive on SVCCA (0.6065 vs. 0.6294). In the cross-architecture comparison (Table 8, sourced from Huang et al., 2023), Pr. UL SNN achieves the highest RSA (0.4608) among all tested model families, surpassing the best CNN (SqueezeNet-1.1 at 0.4360) and all Transformer-like architectures (ConvNeXt-Base at 0.2664). On downstream few-shot and noise-robustness tasks, Pr. UL SNN consistently leads: 63.11% vs. 61.49% (SL SNN) and 58.59% (UL ANN) at 20% labels, and 59.94% vs. 58.11% (UL ANN) under 20% Gaussian noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: (1) SimCLR-style contrastive learning adapted to spiking networks (building on Chen et al., 2020 and Zhuang et al., 2021), (2) the finding that high-dimensional architectures exhibit brain alignment even without extensive task training (Kazemian et al., 2025), and (3) the observation that SNNs outperform architecture-matched CNNs in neural predictivity (Huang et al., 2023). The key departure is introducing activity-dependent synaptic pruning as a developmental refinement mechanism, positioning pruning not as a compression heuristic but as a principled process that distills latent brain-aligned structure from overcomplete spiking representations. The SEW-ResNet (Fang et al., 2021) serves as the primary supervised SNN baseline, but the paper's unsupervised + pruned formulation is novel in combining all three elements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a computational bridge between the recent 'brain alignment emerges in random networks' finding and biological developmental neuroscience, offering a mechanistic account of how overcomplete representations are refined into stable cortical circuits. A key open question is whether the pruning is truly activity-dependent in a biological sense, since it is applied statically post-training using temporal cosine similarity rather than through an online, experience-driven process. Additionally, the approach is evaluated only on ResNet-18/34 backbones with T=4 timesteps, leaving open whether the pruning benefit persists at larger scale or with more complex spiking dynamics. For the computational neuroscience community, this work strengthens the case that unsupervised sensory experience plus structural plasticity may be sufficient for cortical-level representation without task supervision.
