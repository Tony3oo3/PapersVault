## Problem

No publicly available dataset previously combined co-registered EEG and eye-tracking with span-level mind-wandering (MW) onset/offset annotations during naturalistic multi-page reading. Existing MW detection relied on coarse temporal windows (e.g., 2–5 s before thought probes), and the only simultaneous EEG+ET reading datasets (ZuCo 1.0/2.0) used tightly controlled sentence-level paradigms without attention-state labels, leaving a gap for studying how attention fluctuations affect both behavioral signals and neural language representations in ecologically valid settings.

## Input / Output

For MW detection, the pipeline consumes 2-second non-overlapping windows of 64-channel EEG (resampled to 256 Hz, 0.5–50 Hz band-pass filtered) and eye-tracking signals (1000 Hz EyeLink, downsampled to 256 Hz), producing a binary MW/non-MW label per window. ET features include fixation count, mean fixation duration, pupil size, saccade count/duration/amplitude/peak velocity; EEG features are power spectral density values across 8 frequency bands (theta1–gamma2) for all 64 channels, yielding 512 EEG features per window. For EEG-to-text decoding, word-level PSD features (8 bands × 64 channels) are extracted during each fixation, averaged across multiple fixations on the same word, grouped into sentence-level sequences, and fed to a BART-based decoder that outputs the corresponding text.

## Method

The core contribution is the ROAMM dataset itself, collected via the ReMind retrospective self-report paradigm in which participants press a key upon noticing MW and then select the words marking perceived onset and offset, yielding 1,001 span-level MW episodes (~45.5% of 2,200 pages) with precise temporal boundaries. For the MW detection benchmark, features are standardized and reduced via PCA within a scikit-learn pipeline (to prevent leakage), then classified by seven supervised models (logistic regression, linear/RBF SVC, random forest, gradient boosting, kNN, MLP) under a leave-one-subject-out protocol that mitigates both temporal autocorrelation and subject-identity leakage; the best cross-subject model (random forest on hybrid ET+EEG features) achieves 0.609 AUROC. For EEG-to-text decoding, the paper adopts the Wang & Ji (2022) architecture—an EEG encoder producing embeddings that fine-tune a pretrained BART model—trained and evaluated separately on MW, non-MW, and all segments, demonstrating that decoding performance drops substantially (BLEU-1: 0.057 vs. 0.189) when MW-labeled segments are included, confirming MW as a critical confound for neural language decoding.

## Results

Under leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) evaluation, the best cross-subject mind-wandering classifier (Random Forest with combined EEG + eye-tracking features) achieves 0.609 AUROC, while within-subject performance reaches a mean AUROC of 0.72 (best single subject: 0.92). For EEG-to-text decoding, Non-MW segments yield BLEU-1 of 0.189 and ROUGE-1 F1 of 0.258, whereas MW segments drop sharply to BLEU-1 of 0.057 and ROUGE-1 F1 of 0.094, confirming that attention state critically shapes neural language representations. Combining EEG and ET features improves MW detection over either modality alone for most classifiers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For EEG-to-text decoding, the paper directly compares against the Wang & Ji (2022) BART-based decoder evaluated on ZuCo (BLEU-1 = 0.401, BLEU-2 = 0.231, ROUGE-1 F1 = 0.301). ROAMM's Non-MW subset achieves BLEU-1 of 0.189 and ROUGE-1 F1 of 0.258, roughly half the ZuCo performance, which the authors attribute to the more naturalistic multi-page reading setting versus ZuCo's controlled sentence-level paradigm. The key finding is the MW effect: decoding on MW-only segments collapses to BLEU-1 of 0.057 (a ~70% drop from Non-MW), demonstrating that mind-wandering is a major confound for neural language decoding. For MW detection, ROAMM's cross-subject AUROC of 0.609 is comparable to prior controlled-paradigm studies (Jin et al. 2019: ~0.60; Dong et al. 2021: 0.613; Tang et al. 2025: 0.56), while the within-subject mean of 0.72 matches Dong et al.'s 0.715, suggesting the naturalistic setting does not substantially degrade detectability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ZuCo dataset line (Hollenstein et al., 2018; 2020) from controlled sentence-level reading to naturalistic multi-page free-viewing, while adding simultaneous eye-tracking and span-level mind-wandering annotations via the ReMind retrospective self-report paradigm (Sun et al., 2026). For EEG-to-text decoding, it directly adopts the Wang & Ji (2022) BART-based architecture as its baseline, and for MW detection it follows the feature-engineering and supervised-classification pipeline established by Jin et al. (2019) and Dong et al. (2021), but introduces a leave-one-subject-out protocol to mitigate the temporal and subject-identity leakage that plagues arbitrary train/test splits on time-series EEG. The key conceptual departure from prior simultaneous EEG+ET datasets (EEGEyeNet, CG-EEGET, EEGET-RSOD) is the combination of co-registered neural and ocular signals with time-resolved MW onset/offset labels in an ecologically valid reading task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ROAMM fills a concrete gap: no prior public dataset provides span-level MW annotations alongside co-registered EEG and ET during naturalistic reading, making it the first resource for attention-aware multimodal decoding benchmarks. The demonstration that EEG-to-text decoding degrades substantially on MW-labeled segments (BLEU-1 drops from 0.189 to 0.057) provides empirical motivation for attention-aware BCI architectures, though the absolute decoding scores remain well below ZuCo baselines, highlighting that naturalistic reading introduces challenges (variable reading strategies, longer contexts) that current architectures do not yet handle. Open questions include whether deeper temporal models (LSTM, EEGNet) can close the within-subject-to-cross-subject gap (0.72 vs. 0.609 AUROC), whether MW content/subtype annotations would enable finer-grained detection, and whether the modest EEG-over-ET gain reflects true complementary information or shared physiological variance.
