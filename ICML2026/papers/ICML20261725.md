## Problem

Existing multimodal brain network fusion methods (e.g., RH-BrainFS, NeuroPath) model SC-FC coupling at a topological or architectural level—using detour masks or fusion bottlenecks—but lack a principled formulation grounded in neural communication dynamics. This limits their ability to quantify how much information is actually routed along each structural pathway and to explain why SC and FC exhibit dynamic states of both coupling and regional heterogeneity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261725/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of weighted undirected graphs per subject—a structural connectivity graph (from DTI streamline counts) and a functional connectivity graph (from fMRI temporal correlations)—parcellated into N=90–400 ROIs via standard atlases (HCP-MMP1.0, FreeSurfer, AAL), along with node feature vectors. Beyond the standard disease class label, AFR-Net produces an edge-level information flow intensity map (Φ_ij) computed in closed form via the inverse of a regularized graph Laplacian, which is differentiable and jointly optimized with the classifier, yielding interpretable routing patterns as a byproduct of training.

## Method

AFR-Net reconceptualizes the structural connectome as an electrical circuit with learnable edge conductances and the functional connectome as the demand matrix driving current through that circuit. Three integrated phases implement this: (1) a structure-aware Transformer encoder that replaces shortest-path positional encoding with effective resistance distance (ERD) and learns edge capacities via a gating MLP; (2) a differentiable flow solver that computes the global equilibrium Φ_ij = 2·c_ij·(e_i−e_j)ᵀ L_flow⁻¹ L_fc L_flow⁻¹ (e_i−e_j) using Cholesky factorization for the forward pass and implicit differentiation (adjoint method) for backpropagation; and (3) a pattern-guided aggregation layer that converts the flow map into a soft attention mask (Log-MinMax + Sigmoid) to bias downstream message passing. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss, with only a small set of parameters (edge gate, ERD encoder, mask scaling, classifier) learned while the circuit equations impose a global physical constraint.

## Results

AFR-Net achieves the best F1 and AUC on 7 out of 8 metric-dataset combinations across four binary classification tasks. On the PPMI (Parkinson's) dataset, it reaches 83.70% F1 and 90.38% AUC, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline NeuroPath (74.56% F1, 71.88% AUC). On the three ABCD tasks (OCD, ADHD, Anxiety), AFR-Net leads in F1 by 1.4–6.6 points over NeuroPath; the sole exception is ABCD-ADHD AUC, where NeuroPath edges ahead by 1.8 points (64.19 vs. 62.38) while AFR-Net still attains the best F1 (65.50 vs. 62.36). Ablation confirms the flow-routing module is the dominant contributor, with its removal dropping Anxiety F1 from 62.15% to 49.70%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFR-Net outperforms all 14 baselines on 7 of 8 metric-dataset pairs; the single loss is ABCD-ADHD AUC, where NeuroPath leads by 1.8 points (64.19 vs. 62.38) within one standard deviation, while AFR-Net retains the best F1 on that task (65.50 vs. 62.36). The largest margins are on PPMI, where AFR-Net surpasses NeuroPath by 9.1 F1 points and 18.5 AUC points, and on ABCD-Anx, where it beats NeuroPath by 6.6 F1 points. Among specialized brain-network baselines, RH-BrainFS and NeuroPath are the strongest competitors, confirming that addressing regional heterogeneity and topological detours helps, but AFR-Net's physics-informed flow routing provides a further 4–9 point F1 gain over them. General GNN backbones (GCN, GIN, GraphSAGE) perform near or below chance on several tasks, and even the surprisingly competitive MLP baseline (60.48 F1 on OCD) is outperformed by AFR-Net by 10+ points, underscoring that naive feature-level fusion cannot capture the SC–FC coupling that the flow-routing mechanism explicitly models.

## Relation to Prior Work

AFR-Net builds directly on Graphormer's Transformer-for-graphs architecture, replacing its shortest-path positional encoding with effective resistance distance (ERD) to capture parallel-path robustness. It departs from the dominant line of multimodal SC-FC fusion work (RH-BrainFS, NeuroPath, Cross-GNN) by shifting the level of abstraction from topological (which structural pathways support a functional link) to communication-dynamics (how much information is routed along each pathway), grounding the fusion in Kirchhoff circuit theory and a closed-form Laplacian solve rather than learned attention or heuristic detour masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The physics-informed formulation provides a mechanistic, interpretable account of SC-FC coupling that prior black-box fusion methods lack, and the discovered routing patterns (e.g., left hippocampus as a pathological hub in anxiety) align with established clinical neuroscience, offering testable hypotheses. Key open questions include scaling beyond region-level atlases (O(N³) Cholesky bottleneck), extending to dynamic/directed FC, and prospective multi-site clinical validation. The framework's constraint that only a small set of parameters is learned while the circuit equations impose global physical structure may generalize to other physics-informed graph learning problems.
