## Problem

Pre-trained VLM representations used in Vision-Language-Action models are dominated by visual appearance (scene layout, background objects) and lack sensitivity to control-relevant robotic signals such as proprioceptive states and low-level actions. This misalignment causes VLA policies to produce suboptimal actions, particularly on tasks requiring precise positioning like pick-and-place, and existing remedies (curated robotics datasets, multi-stage training, discretized action pre-training) add complexity and data requirements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262128/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images (2–3 views: exterior and wrist cameras), a natural-language task instruction, and the robot's proprioceptive state (end-effector position with 6D rotation and gripper width, or absolute joint positions; min-max normalized to [-1,1] per dimension). It outputs a continuous action chunk of horizon H (e.g., 16 steps) generated by a DiT-based flow-matching decoder via iterative denoising from Gaussian noise. The VLM backbone is frozen; only a lightweight adapter, the contrastive projection head, and the action decoder are trained.

## Method

RS-CL is a soft-weighted InfoNCE contrastive loss that reshapes VLM conditioning representations to align with proprioceptive states. A learnable summarization token is appended to the VLM output and projected to a compact embedding z; pairwise soft weights w_ij are computed from the Euclidean distance between proprioceptive states q_i and q_j (via an exponential kernel with temperature β), so that samples with similar robot states are pulled closer in representation space. To form contrastive pairs without extra VLM forward passes, the paper introduces view cutoff: a representation-level augmentation that masks the feature slice corresponding to one randomly selected camera view, so only the lightweight adapter and projector process the augmented variant. The total training objective is L_FM + λ·L_RS-CL, jointly optimizing the action decoder, adapter, and projector in a single end-to-end stage, with λ cosine-decayed from 1.0 to 0 to emphasize representation refinement early and action accuracy later.

## Results

On the RoboCasa-Kitchen benchmark (300 demos), RS-CL achieves a state-of-the-art 69.7% average success rate, surpassing the strongest prior methods (FLARE at 66.4%, GR00T N1.5+HAMLET at 66.4%) by 3.3 percentage points. On LIBERO, RS-CL improves the GR00T N1.5 baseline from 95.7% to 96.4% average success rate. In real-robot experiments on a Franka Research 3 arm, RS-CL boosts success rates from 45.0% to 58.3% (+13.3%) on challenging pick-and-place and close-lid tasks, with the largest gains on pick-and-place (30.3% → 41.5%, +11.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RS-CL (69.7%) outperforms all 9 baselines on RoboCasa-Kitchen, with the closest competitors being FLARE (66.4%) and GR00T N1.5+HAMLET (66.4%), both trailing by 3.3 points. The direct baseline GR00T N1.5 (65.7%) is improved by 4.0 points, while the flow-matching π₀ (62.5%) and autoregressive π₀-FAST (63.6%) are surpassed by 7.2 and 6.1 points respectively. On LIBERO, RS-CL improves GR00T N1.5 from 95.7% to 96.4%, and also exceeds π₀ (94.1%) and π₀-FAST (85.5%). In from-scratch training, RS-CL consistently outperforms further-trained VLM backbones (RoboBrain 2.0, VeBrain, Cosmos-Reason1, NORA) across Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B and SigLIP2, e.g., achieving 17.3% vs. 9.1% on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 14.1% vs. 4.0% on unfrozen SigLIP2. RS-CL also generalizes to the autoregressive π₀-FAST architecture (65.2% vs. 63.6%) and to higher-dimensional embodiments on DexMimicGen (bimanual Panda: 57.3% vs. 52.7%; GR-1 humanoid: 73.1% vs. 68.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard VLA training paradigm (GR00T N1.5, π₀) where a frozen VLM provides conditioning representations to a flow-matching or autoregressive action decoder, and on the InfoNCE/supervised contrastive learning literature (Oord et al., 2018; Khosla et al., 2020; Suresh & Ong, 2021) for structuring embedding spaces. It departs from prior VLA training strategies—direct gradient backprop through the VLM, auxiliary objectives on curated robotics datasets, or multi-stage embodied-reasoning pretraining—by introducing a self-supervised contrastive regularizer (RS-CL) that uses proprioceptive state distances as soft supervision, requiring no additional datasets or training stages. The view-cutoff augmentation, inspired by token cutoff (Shen et al., 2020), is a novel representation-level operation that avoids extra VLM forward passes, distinguishing it from data-level augmentations used in prior contrastive robotics work (TCN, CURL, R3M).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, lightweight mechanism (+1.25% wall-clock overhead) to close the gap between general-purpose VLM representations and control-relevant robotic signals, achieving SOTA on RoboCasa-Kitchen (69.7%) and meaningful real-robot gains (45%→58.3%) without architectural changes or additional data. The finding that proprioceptive state distance outperforms next-action distance as soft supervision is a useful design insight: action sequences are multimodal and serve as the prediction target, making them unreliable alignment signals, whereas the robot's physical state provides a stable, unimodal cue. Open questions include whether RS-CL extends to richer sensor modalities (object pose, tactile, contact force) and whether the approach scales to high-dimensional proprioceptive spaces in humanoid or dexterous-hand settings, which the authors identify as promising but unexplored directions.
