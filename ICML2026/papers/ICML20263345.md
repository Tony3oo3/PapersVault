## Problem

Chemical reaction networks compiled via the Napp-Adams construction to implement belief propagation grow prohibitively large as inference problems become more complex, yet classical CRN reduction techniques (stoichiometric, Laplacian-based) fail because the message-passing semantics are carried by catalytic dependencies and bundle-normalized concentration ratios rather than by reaction stoichiometry alone. This paper asks whether factor-graph-level reductions can be transported to their chemical implementations while preserving the BP fixed points on surviving variables.

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts either a mass-action CRN satisfying recognition conditions (W1)–(W6) and recycling condition (R1), or a factor graph to be compiled. From a CRN, the method reconstructs the underlying factor graph (variable classes, factor classes, factor tables) purely from stoichiometric block structure and rate-polynomial coefficients. The output is a reduced CRN obtained by deleting entire message bundles (zero species plus all coordinate species) and their associated reactions, while rescaling only the rate parameters on surviving sum-bundle production reactions; BP fixed points on surviving variables are preserved up to bundle-normalized concentration ratios.

## Method

The core mechanism is a three-stage pipeline: (1) recognize the Napp-Adams bundle structure via six local conditions (W1–W6) on stoichiometry and rate polynomials, which identify sum bundles (factor-to-variable messages), product bundles (variable-to-factor messages), and factor classes with clamped tables; (2) apply SP-B deformation retractions to the associated finite poset of the factor graph, deleting linear and colinear points and updating neighboring factor tables via explicit marginalization formulas; (3) functorially transport each retraction to the CRN by deleting the corresponding message bundles and their reactions while updating only the production rate constants on surviving sum bundles according to the SP-B table update. The key theoretical result is that the Napp-Adams compilation defines a functor from the category of factor graphs with SP-B retractions to the category of CRNs with bundle-deletion morphisms, so that reducing-then-compiling equals compiling-then-reducing, and positive steady states of the reduced CRN correspond bijectively to BP fixed points on the reduced factor graph (Theorem 5.4, Proposition 6.2, Lemma 6.3). Empirically, this yields 95–97% species reduction on tree/chain graphs (with ~270×–886× simulation speedups) and 73–79% on loopy-core graphs with retractable tendrils, while grid graphs (irreducible cores) show no reduction, confirming the method targets exactly the acyclic structure outside the loopy core.

## Results

Across the structured benchmark suite, SP-B retractions yield dramatic CRN compression: chains and trees collapse to a small core with ~95% fewer variables and ~97% fewer chemical species, producing median simulation speedups of 270× (chains) and 886× (trees). Loopy-core instances with reducible tendrils achieve 79% variable and 73% species reduction with a 22× speedup, while grid graphs (essentially irreducible) show 0% reduction, serving as a control. On Erdős-Rényi G(n,p) graphs, species reduction exceeds 60% in the subcritical regime (peaking near ~69%) and vanishes in the dense regime, with median simulation speedups peaking around 16×. BP marginal agreement on surviving variables is preserved to a median discrepancy of 4×10⁻⁸ across all 440 ER runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing CRN reduction methods in a head-to-head comparison; instead, it establishes that classical stoichiometric and Laplacian-based reductions (Hirono et al. 2021, Rao et al. 2013, Gasparyan et al. 2025) do not apply to Napp-Adams compiled CRNs because the BP message-passing structure is carried by catalytic dependencies invisible to the stoichiometric matrix. The primary 'baseline' is the unreduced compiled CRN (compile-first), and the method's gain is measured as the ratio of species counts and wall-clock ODE integration times before versus after the SP-B retraction. Grid graphs serve as a negative control, confirming that the method correctly halts when no linear or colinear points exist (0% reduction, 1.0× speedup). Random planted-core graphs show only modest reduction (20% variables, 10% species, 1.5× speedup), consistent with fewer retractable tendrils. Correctness is validated by comparing BP marginals on surviving variables between original and reduced networks, with the median discrepancy at 4×10⁻⁸ on ER instances, confirming that the speedups reflect genuine computational savings rather than altered inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: the Napp-Adams compilation of belief propagation into mass-action CRNs (Napp & Adams, 2013) and the SP-B deformation retraction of factor graphs that preserves BP fixed points (Sergeant-Perthuis & Boitel, 2025). It departs from classical CRN reduction methods (Hirono et al., 2021; Rao et al., 2013; Gasparyan et al., 2025), which operate on stoichiometric or Laplacian structure, by showing that BP-compiled CRNs carry their inference semantics in catalytic dependencies and bundle-normalized concentration ratios invisible to stoichiometry alone. The key conceptual move is to reduce at the factor-graph level and then functorially transport the reduction to the CRN, rather than attempting a direct chemical reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first semantics-preserving reduction for BP-compiled CRNs, providing a principled path to smaller DNA strand-displacement implementations of probabilistic inference. The scope is intentionally narrow (the Napp-Adams compiled subclass characterized by W1–W6 and R1), and the paper explicitly notes that general biochemical CRNs or HMM-learning networks fall outside the framework. A longer-horizon open question is whether gene regulatory networks performing approximate inference can be cast in Napp-Adams form, which would make them amenable to the same pipeline. The empirical results confirm that reduction tracks the graph's irreducible loopy core rather than depending on planted structure, but the practical impact remains limited to a specific compilation scheme.
