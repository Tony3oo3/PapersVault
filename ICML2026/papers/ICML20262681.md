## Problem

Existing error-based membership inference attacks on diffusion models overlook the model's inherent 'high-frequency deficiency' — the fact that diffusion models reconstruct high-frequency components with substantially more variation than low-frequency ones. This deficiency inflates the standard deviation of membership scores, causing member images rich in high-frequency detail to be misclassified as hold-out and vice versa, thereby reducing the attack's discriminative power. The paper formalizes these attacks into a unified paradigm and identifies this frequency-domain gap as the key bottleneck limiting attack performance.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a grey-box setting where the attacker accesses intermediate denoising outputs at specified timesteps but not internal model parameters; for text-to-image models (Stable Diffusion), a text prompt is optionally available, while unconditional DDIM models require only the image. Images range from 32×32 (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, STL10-U) to 512×512 (MS-COCO, Flickr, Pokémon). The output is a binary membership decision (member/hold-out) determined by comparing the L2 distance between the high-frequency-filtered model prediction and the filtered target against a threshold τ, with that distance serving as the continuous membership score.

## Method

The paper first unifies existing error-based attacks (Naive, SecMI, PIA) into a general paradigm that computes the norm of the difference between a model's predicted image and a target image at a given timestep. The core contribution is a plug-and-play high-frequency filter: each image is transformed to the frequency domain via FFT, a circular mask attenuates components beyond radius r_t (default 5) by a factor s (default 0.2) while preserving lower frequencies, and the result is transformed back via IFFT before computing the distance. Proposition 4.2 theoretically proves that this filtering increases the σ_H/σ_M ratio (hold-out to member score standard deviations), directly amplifying the membership advantage. The module requires no retraining, adds only ~5–10 seconds of overhead per dataset, and integrates seamlessly into any attack conforming to the general paradigm, yielding average AUC gains of 4.5–9% and TPR@1%FPR gains up to 51% across baselines.

## Results

The proposed high-frequency filter (denoted +F) consistently improves all three error-based baselines across both DDIM and fine-tuned Stable Diffusion settings. On fine-tuned Stable Diffusion (Table 3), average gains are +8.12–10.01% ASR, +7.47–9.01% AUC, and +19.73–28.26% TPR@1%FPR across Pokémon, MS-COCO, and Flickr. On DDIM (Table 2), average gains are +5.38–7.25% ASR, +4.49–5.59% AUC, and +4.48–11.80% TPR@1%FPR across STL10-U, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet. The most striking single improvement is Naive on Flickr, where TPR@1%FPR jumps from 16.59 to 67.60 (+51.01%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three error-based MIA baselines—Naive (Matsumoto et al., 2023), SecMI (Duan et al., 2023), and PIA (Kong et al., 2023)—each with and without the proposed high-frequency filter. The filter wins across every dataset, model, and metric: on MS-COCO, Naive+F reaches 93.60% ASR vs. 80.29% (Naive), 91.00% (SecMI), and 76.00% (PIA+F); on Flickr, Naive+F achieves 67.60% TPR@1%FPR, far exceeding SecMI+F (21.20%) and PIA+F (19.60%). Among the unfiltered baselines, SecMI generally outperforms Naive and PIA on DDIM, while PIA is weakest overall. The filter's gains are largest for the weakest baseline (PIA) and on higher-resolution text-to-image models, where high-frequency deficiency is most pronounced. A fourth method, CLID (Zhai et al., 2024), also benefits from the filter under weak-overfitting conditions (e.g., +2.09 AUC on Pokémon) but shows only marginal gains under typical settings where it already achieves near-saturation (99.39% AUC). Under defense mechanisms (data augmentation, SS_ei memory mitigation), the filter still delivers consistent 4–7% AUC improvements over the corresponding unfiltered baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a family of error-based membership inference attacks on diffusion models (Naive, SecMI, PIA) into a single general paradigm based on reconstruction distance, then identifies a previously overlooked systematic bias: diffusion models' well-documented low-to-high frequency generation pattern (Rissanen et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2023; Falck et al., 2025) introduces variance in membership scores that is correlated with image content rather than membership status. Rather than proposing a new attack, the paper repurposes a standard Fourier-domain low-pass filter as a plug-and-play module that can be inserted into any attack within the paradigm, supported by a theoretical proposition (Prop. 4.2) linking the filter to an increase in the σ_H/σ_M ratio that governs membership advantage (Yeom et al., 2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means practitioners can immediately improve any existing error-based MIA on diffusion models with negligible computational overhead, which is practically valuable for privacy auditing of deployed generative models. However, the method shows limited efficacy in the pre-training setting where baselines already perform near-random, and the theoretical analysis relies on a Gaussian approximation of score distributions (though normality tests and experiments suggest robustness to this assumption). Open questions include whether the frequency-domain perspective generalizes to video diffusion, whether distribution-free theoretical guarantees are possible, and how the approach interacts with emerging adaptive defenses that target low-frequency memorization specifically.
