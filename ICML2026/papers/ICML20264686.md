## Problem

Token-level evidence in multimodal sentiment analysis is often semantically inconsistent—redundant within a modality and conflicting across modalities (e.g., sarcasm)—which injects noise into fusion and wastes computation on uninformative tokens. Existing approaches treat intra-modality structure and inter-modality alignment as separate objectives, and prior token-pruning methods score tokens in raw feature space rather than in a decision-aligned semantic space, risking removal of sentiment-critical but low-saliency tokens.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw audio waveforms, text sequences, and optionally video frames, encoded by Data2Vec-Audio (768-dim, 50 tokens), RoBERTa (1024-dim, 96 tokens), and BERT respectively. It produces a continuous sentiment score via L1 regression (scale −3 to +3 on MOSI/MOSEI, −1 to +1 on CH-SIMS). Under hard selection, the output is further reduced to a Top-K subset of tokens (as few as 10% of the original sequence) before fusion, yielding a 2×+ inference speedup without significant accuracy loss.

## Method

ConsMSA projects each modality's token features into a shared latent sentiment distribution space via a two-layer semantic projector followed by Softmax, then computes an Intra- and Inter-modality Consistency Score (I²CS) using Jensen-Shannon divergence between token-induced distributions within and across modalities. This consistency score is coupled with predictive relevance (the max probability of each token's distribution) to form a unified importance signal that simultaneously drives three mechanisms: a relevance-weighted consistency loss for training regularization, a sigmoid-gated soft mask for differentiable token reweighting, and a TopK hard selection for explicit token pruning. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end with a joint objective combining L1 task loss, the I²CS consistency loss, and modality-specific auxiliary losses, using AdamW with early stopping. The key distinction from prior work is that consistency is measured in the prediction distribution space (decision-aligned) rather than in raw feature space, making the pruning criterion directly tied to sentiment-relevant semantic agreement.

## Results

On CMU-MOSI (RoBERTa, T+A), ConsMSA achieves 51.46% Acc-7, 90.09% Acc-2, 90.08% F1, and 0.557 MAE, outperforming the prior SOTA MMML (50.34% Acc-7, 89.69% Acc-2). On CMU-MOSEI (RoBERTa, T+A), it reaches 56.00% Acc-7, 88.41% Acc-2, 88.39% F1, and 0.485 MAE. In the BERT-based (T+A+V) setting, ConsMSA attains 54.92% Acc-7 and 86.67% Acc-2 on MOSEI, matching or exceeding all prior methods. On the Chinese CH-SIMS benchmark, ConsMSA improves over MMML by +1.31% Acc-3 and +1.39% Acc-5 while also reducing MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the RoBERTa-based (T+A) setting, ConsMSA outperforms the strongest prior method MMML on both benchmarks: +1.12% Acc-7 and +0.40% Acc-2 on MOSI, and +0.26% Acc-7 and +0.39% Acc-2 on MOSEI, while also reducing MAE (0.557 vs. 0.580 on MOSI; 0.485 vs. 0.492 on MOSEI). In the BERT-based (T+A+V) setting, ConsMSA achieves the best Acc-2 and F1 on both MOSI (86.06%) and MOSEI (86.67%), though MDF retains a marginal MAE advantage on MOSI (0.692 vs. 0.698). Against earlier SOTA methods like DMD, MMIM, and ULMD, ConsMSA consistently leads on Acc-2 and F1 by 0.2–1.5 percentage points. On the Chinese CH-SIMS dataset, ConsMSA surpasses MMML across all five metrics (Acc-2, Acc-3, Acc-5, F1, MAE), with the largest gains in fine-grained Acc-3 (+1.31%) and Acc-5 (+1.39%). The paper also demonstrates that under aggressive token compression (retaining only 10% of tokens), ConsMSA preserves 89.18% Acc-2 on MOSI, compared to 90.09% at full sequence length, confirming robustness that no baseline reports.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConsMSA departs from two established lines of MSA research: (1) disentangled/aligned representation methods (MISA, FDMER, DMD, DLF, MDF) that operate in feature space to reduce distribution mismatch but never explicitly quantify whether individual tokens are semantically consistent with the downstream sentiment decision, and (2) token compression approaches that score tokens via attention weights or entropy in feature space, which can be misaligned with the prediction boundary. The paper's key conceptual move is to shift the consistency measurement into the *semantic distribution (prediction) space*, using JS divergence to compute intra- and inter-modality agreement, then coupling this with predictive relevance to produce a unified importance signal that simultaneously drives regularization, soft reweighting, and hard pruning. This unifies what prior work treats as separate objectives (representation alignment and token selection) under a single decision-aligned criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that consistency-driven token pruning acts as a 'semantic purifier': hard selection at 80% retention actually *outperforms* full-sequence soft gating on MOSI, and retaining only 10% of tokens preserves most accuracy while yielding ~2× inference speedup. This suggests a large fraction of tokens in multimodal sequences are semantically redundant or conflicting, and that explicitly modeling prediction-level agreement is a more principled compression criterion than saliency or entropy. Open questions include whether the consistency principle generalizes to modality-missing settings (acknowledged by the authors), whether it transfers to other multimodal tasks beyond sentiment, and how to handle the long-tail class imbalance (HN/HP) that still limits fine-grained accuracy. The method's backbone-agnostic design (BERT and RoBERTa) and cross-lingual validation (English, Chinese) strengthen its practical relevance.
