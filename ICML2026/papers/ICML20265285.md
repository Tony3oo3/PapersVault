## Problem

Training reasoning VLMs for rare domains like geospatial is fundamentally constrained by supervision scarcity: raw imagery is abundant but task-direct annotations (detection boxes, segmentation masks, QA pairs) are not scalable. The paper asks whether indirect, verifiable signals derived from metadata (GPS coordinates, timestamps) are sufficient to induce sophisticated, generalizable geospatial reasoning without any task-specific expert labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265285/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During RL training, the model receives one ground-level panorama image alongside k satellite image candidates (one correct match plus k−1 hard negatives from the same spatiotemporal neighborhood) and must output a free-form reasoning chain followed by a single letter choice in a structured <answer> tag. For zero-shot evaluation, the model takes a single street-view or aerial image (448×448 for NAIP aerial) and produces latitude/longitude coordinates to 4 decimal places, city/country names, or scene classification labels, all in the same think-then-answer format.

## Method

Geo-R1 is a two-stage post-training framework on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Stage 1 (Scaffolding) uses 12.6k synthetic chain-of-thought trajectories from CV-Cities to instill a unified geospatial reasoning paradigm (visual cue identification → knowledge association → evidence corroboration → conclusion) via minimal SFT, deliberately limiting task diversity to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Stage 2 (Elevating) applies GRPO reinforcement learning on a Cross-View Pairing proxy task where the reward is a binary verifiable signal (correct/incorrect satellite match), augmented with format, cosine-length, and repetition penalties. The key theoretical insight is that by sampling hard negatives from the same spatiotemporal neighborhood, nuisance factors become statistically non-discriminative (I(Y;N)=0), so maximizing mutual information between the reasoning chain and the target is provably equivalent to maximizing mutual information with view-invariant geometric features Φ, forcing the model to internalize universal projection and correspondence laws rather than superficial shortcuts. This indirect reward signal, requiring no task-specific annotations, yields zero-shot transfer across 25+ geospatial tasks and matches or surpasses task-directly supervised specialists like GLOBE-7B on MP16-Reason.

## Results

On the in-distribution cross-view pairing task, Geo-R1 achieves 82.4% accuracy (vs. 19.0% for the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base and 23.1% for Geo-SFT), with a completion length of only 378.8 tokens. On MP16-Reason, Geo-R1 trained solely with indirect cross-view rewards nearly matches GLOBE-7B (trained with task-direct strong rewards) at all distance thresholds: 17.98% vs. 17.99% at 1 km, 61.02% vs. 62.85% at 25 km, and 93.56% vs. 92.52% at 2500 km. On the OOD IMAGEO-GSS benchmark, Geo-R1 achieves 32.7% city accuracy and 69.4 km median distance, outperforming all open-source VLMs (including Llama-3.2-90B and InternVL3-8B) and trailing only the closed-source o3 model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Geo-R1's central comparison is against GLOBE-7B, the only prior model trained with RL on geolocation using task-direct strong rewards; Geo-R1 (indirect rewards only) matches or slightly exceeds GLOBE-7B at every distance threshold on MP16-Reason (e.g., 93.56% vs. 92.52% at 2500 km), demonstrating that indirect cross-view rewards are sufficient to induce equivalent reasoning. Against the non-LLM specialist GeoCLIP, Geo-R1 more than triples city accuracy on IMAGEO-GSS (32.7% vs. 10.9%) and reduces median distance error from 266.9 km to 69.4 km. Geo-R1 substantially outperforms GeoReasoner-7B (17.98% vs. 10.06% at 1 km on MP16-Reason) and all general-purpose VLM baselines (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL3-8B, Llama-3.2-90B) on both ground-view and aerial-view geolocation. On GeoBench-VLM aerial interpretation, Geo-R1 surpasses domain specialists GeoChat and EarthDial in zero-shot settings. The only model that consistently outperforms Geo-R1 is the closed-source o3, which benefits from vastly greater parameter scale and RL compute; Geo-R1 also preserves general VLM capabilities (MEGA-Bench, MMMU, GPQA) with only minor degradation attributable to the SFT stage rather than RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two distinct lineages: (1) cross-view alignment work (Mall et al., 2024) that showed ground-level and aerial imagery from the same location can be matched without annotations, and (2) GRPO-based RLVR (Shao et al., 2024; DeepSeek-R1) for inducing reasoning in LLMs/VLMs. The key departure from prior geospatial RL work (e.g., GLOBE, Li et al. 2025a) is the use of an *indirect* proxy reward—binary cross-view matching driven by GPS metadata—rather than task-direct supervision on reasoning correctness. The paper also contrasts with SFT-based geospatial models, arguing that supervised fine-tuning on narrow task distributions produces brittle, non-generalizing behavior, and positions its two-stage scaffold-then-elevate pipeline as a solution to the fundamental data asymmetry (abundant imagery, scarce task labels) in the geospatial domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim—that a single binary verifiable reward from metadata (cross-view pairing) is sufficient to induce zero-shot geospatial reasoning across 25+ heterogeneous tasks—offers a scalable pathway for rare domains where task-specific annotation is prohibitively expensive. If the indirect-reward paradigm generalizes beyond geospatial to other data-rich but label-scarce domains (medical imaging, industrial inspection, etc.), it could substantially reduce the annotation bottleneck in post-training. Open questions include: whether the approach scales to larger models or more complex multi-step reasoning, whether the hard-negative construction is the critical ingredient or whether any verifiable proxy suffices, and whether the zero-shot gains persist as the proxy task becomes easier (reducing the 'reasoning margin'). The paper's reliance on a single 7B backbone (with one 8B confirmation) and the persistent gap to o3 on fine-grained geolocation leave room for further validation.
