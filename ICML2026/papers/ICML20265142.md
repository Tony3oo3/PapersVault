## Problem

Dynamic Sparse Training (DST) applied to LLM pre-training suffers from severe loss spikes after topology updates, caused by a 'cold-start' effect where newly regrown parameters lack accumulated Adam optimizer states and receive updates ~3.16× the nominal learning rate. Additionally, conventional DST implementations allocate full-sized optimizer states over the entire parameter space, negating the memory benefits of sparsity for large-scale models.

## Input / Output

SMET consumes standard text token sequences (C4, OpenWebText) for self-supervised pre-training of LLaMA and Qwen transformer architectures (60M–1B parameters, sequence length 256, batch size 512). It produces a sparse language model in which only a fraction d of parameters remain active throughout the entire training lifecycle, supporting both unstructured and block-wise (size B) sparsity patterns. Unlike LoRA or GaLore, the final model retains its sparse structure, enabling potential inference-time acceleration via block-sparse kernels.

## Method

SMET stabilizes DST through two complementary mechanisms: (1) an optimizer-state warm-up that resets the local timestep t_i for each regrown parameter (reactivating Adam's bias correction so the first-step update is bounded by α rather than 3.16α) combined with an explicit linear learning-rate ramp over W=10 steps; and (2) a density-aware learning-rate scaling α_s = α_0/√d motivated by Maximal Update Parameterization, compensating for the reduced effective fan-in of sparse layers. For memory efficiency, SMET stores gradients and Adam moment estimates only for active parameters via an index-based representation, with per-parameter timesteps and indices amortized across blocks of size B to O(d·n_out·n_in/B) overhead. A theoretical analysis under L-smoothness shows that bounding the regrowth update norm by C_t (which grows smoothly during warm-up) controls both the first-order and quadratic terms in the local loss variation, suppressing post-regrowth spikes.

## Results

On the C4 dataset, SMET achieves validation perplexity of 33.38 (60M), 25.21 (130M), 18.90 (350M), and 15.61 (1B), consistently outperforming all memory-efficient baselines and approaching dense training (15.56 at 1B). At the 1B scale, SMET narrows the gap to dense training to just 0.05 perplexity points while maintaining 40% sparsity. Under block-wise sparsity at density 0.1, SMET remains stable across block sizes up to B=32, with only ~1 perplexity point degradation versus unstructured sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMET outperforms all four memory-efficient baselines across every model size. Against LoRA, SMET improves perplexity by 1.61 points at 60M (33.38 vs 34.99) and by 3.60 points at 1B (15.61 vs 19.21). Against ReLoRA, the gap is even larger, with SMET achieving 2.83 points lower perplexity at 1B (15.61 vs 18.33). Against GaLore, the closest competitor, SMET edges ahead by 0.03 perplexity at 1B (15.61 vs 15.64) and by 0.05 at 350M (18.90 vs 18.95). In the DST-specific comparison (Table 2, LLaMA-350M), SMET beats SET by 1.10–3.11 perplexity points across densities 0.5–0.1, while SMET_grad beats RigL by 3.23–3.40 points, confirming that the stabilization mechanism generalizes across both random and gradient-based regrowth strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Dynamic Sparse Training (DST) lineage established by SET and RigL from vision tasks to LLM pre-training, where it identifies a previously unaddressed failure mode: the Adam cold-start effect causes newly regrown parameters to receive updates ~3.16× the nominal learning rate, triggering loss spikes that degrade convergence. The stabilization mechanism draws on Maximal Update Parameterization (μP) for density-aware learning-rate scaling, while the memory-efficient sparse optimizer storage departs from prior memory-saving methods (LoRA, GaLore, ReLoRA) that compress optimizer states but ultimately produce dense models. SMET thus positions itself at the intersection of sparse training stability and practical memory reduction, preserving sparsity throughout the training lifecycle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By diagnosing and fixing the cold-start instability, SMET makes DST a viable option for LLM pre-training at scales up to 1B parameters, achieving near-dense perplexity while reducing gradient and optimizer-state memory by a factor proportional to density. The finding that vanilla DST methods underperform even static sparse training at lower densities is a useful caution for the sparse-training community. Key open questions remain: whether the cold-start diagnosis generalizes beyond Adam-family optimizers, whether activation and KV-cache memory can be similarly addressed, and whether the modest inference speedups (1.3×–1.6×) will improve as sparse kernels mature. The approach is currently limited to pre-training; extension to fine-tuning and RLHF is left for future work.
