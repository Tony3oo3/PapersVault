## Problem

Prior grokking research has focused almost exclusively on group operations, which enforce a rigid bundle of algebraic properties (associativity, unitality, commutativity) and make it impossible to isolate which specific algebraic traits drive the grokking phenomenon. This paper extends the analysis to general finite-dimensional algebras (FDA), including non-associative, non-commutative, and non-unital structures, to understand how algebraic and structural properties govern the emergence and timing of grokking.

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequence of four tokens (u, ×, v, =) where u and v are discrete vocabulary symbols indexing elements of an n-dimensional algebra over a finite field F_p (with q = p^n total elements plus two special tokens). The output is a q-way classification predicting the algebra product u·v. Unlike continuous-valued regression, the discrete finite-field setting forces the model to learn explicit embeddings of algebra elements, making representation learning intrinsic and grokking phenomena most salient.

## Method

The paper establishes a tensorial framework in which any tensor C ∈ F^{n×n×n} defines an n-dimensional algebra, with associativity, commutativity, and unitality characterized as explicit tensor identities (Proposition 2.2), and group learning shown to be a special case (Proposition 3.1). Models (MLP, LSTM, Transformer) are trained via supervised cross-entropy classification over the finite field, with a linear classifier head. The key analytical contribution is connecting FDA learning over R to matrix factorization with implicit low-rank bias, while over finite fields grokking emerges when model embeddings align with the algebra's structure tensor. Generalization is verified via linear probing: a bilinear tensor W is fitted to check whether model features satisfy the algebra's multiplication law, and the resulting representation quality A_rep is shown to correlate with test accuracy. Experiments systematically vary algebraic properties (associative/commutative/unital categories) and structural tensor features (sparsity, mode-3 rank) to measure their effects on grokking delay and sample complexity.

## Results

For (n,p)=(2,7), the paper enumerates all 7⁸=5,764,801 structure tensors and finds that 97.96% are non-associative, non-commutative, non-unital (āc̄ū), while only 0.017% are associative-commutative-unital (acu). Among the learnable categories, acū (associative, commutative, non-unital) algebras consistently exhibit the shortest grokking delay and lowest post-grokking test loss, followed by āc̄ū, with remaining categories showing nearly indistinguishable behavior. Test loss and grokking step t₄ both increase monotonically with the mode-3 unfolding rank r⁽³⁾ and the sparsity s of the structure tensor C*. Representation quality A_rep remains low during memorization and sharply increases at the grokking transition, with A_test increasing with A_rep in the low-data regime (r≤0.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external baseline methods; instead, it performs a controlled study of how algebraic and structural properties of the FDA influence grokking. The key comparison is across the 2³ Boolean combinations of associativity, commutativity, and unitality. The central finding is that removing the unit constraint (going from u to ū) is the single most impactful factor: acū algebras grok significantly earlier and achieve lower test loss than their unital counterparts, because the absence of the identity constraint ∑ᵢλᵢRᵢ=Iₘ enlarges the feasible representation space and admits simpler solutions (zero representation, left regular representation). Commutativity provides a secondary benefit by halving the number of distinct products the classifier must separate (n² → n(n+1)/2 equations in the representation constraint). Non-associative and non-unital categories (āc̄ū, the vast majority at 97.96%) show intermediate difficulty, while the rare unital categories are consistently harder. These effects persist across all training data fractions r∈{0.2,…,0.9} and are robust across 10 sampled tensors per category.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the grokking literature, which has focused almost exclusively on group operations (Power et al. 2022; Nanda et al. 2023; Chughtai et al. 2023), to the much broader class of finite-dimensional algebras (FDAs) that can be non-associative, non-commutative, and non-unital. The key conceptual move is a tensorial reformulation: any n-dimensional algebra over a field F is identified with its structure tensor C ∈ F^{n×n×n}, and algebraic properties (associativity, commutativity, unitality) become explicit tensor identities. This framework formally subsumes prior group-grokking results as a special case (Proposition 3.1) while opening the door to Lie brackets, chemical reaction prediction, and semantic composition as grokking testbeds. The real-field analysis additionally connects FDA learning to matrix factorization with implicit low-rank bias, drawing on the compressed-sensing and implicit-regularization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that individual algebraic properties—associativity, commutativity, unitality—each exert distinct, measurable effects on grokking delay and generalization, rather than grokking being a monolithic phenomenon. The counterintuitive finding that unitality *delays* grokking (by adding representation constraints without adding degrees of freedom) challenges the common intuition that more algebraic structure should make a task easier. However, the experimental scope is narrow (primarily n=2 over F_7, 10 tensors per category), and the authors acknowledge that scaling to larger algebras and connecting to real-world tasks with latent algebraic structure remain open. A tighter theoretical link between tensor properties (rank, coherence) and sample complexity over finite fields is also identified as needed.
