## Problem

The hippocampus encodes space through a continuous gradient of place field sizes along its dorsal-ventral axis, yet it remains unclear how this smooth gradient emerges from discretely scaled grid cell modules, what functional advantage the multiscale organization confers for learning, and how this biological principle can inform the design of efficient artificial neural networks.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard text token sequences for language modeling (WikiText-103, C4) and synthetic key-value pair sequences for retrieval tasks, producing next-token predictions, retrieved values, and sentiment classification scores on SST-2. The key structural detail is that positional information is encoded via frequency-dependent spectral scaling applied to RoPE's rotary frequency subspaces, where the scaling factor S(ω) = λ·√(ω·exp(−σ_p²ω²/2)) shapes the effective positional kernel width without altering the dot-product attention structure or requiring additional attention bias matrices.

## Method

HIPE (Hippocampus-Inspired Positional Embedding) translates the analytically derived grid-to-place projection principle into a spectral scaling transformation on RoPE: the frequency-dependent exponential decay of projection weights (|W_i(k)| ∝ exp[−σ_p²(2πk/L)²/2]) becomes a Gaussian spectral filter S(ω)² applied to each rotary frequency subspace, with a √ω Jacobian term compensating for RoPE's log-spaced frequency density. The bipartite deployment assigns standard RoPE (σ_p→0, sharp-field limit) to shallow layers for fine positional discrimination and nonzero-σ_p HIPE to deeper layers for broad spatial integration, mirroring the dorsal-to-ventral hippocampal gradient. The theoretical analysis shows that the optimal σ_p scales proportionally with the task's characteristic spatial scale D (σ_p^opt ≈ L/(2√2πn) ~ D), and that a multi-scale code (mixture of σ_p values) achieves robust few-shot performance across environments of varying scale without prior knowledge of the task structure. Experiments on 20M–300M parameter OLMo-based Transformers confirm that HIPE improves data efficiency in restricted-budget pretraining and low-resource fine-tuning while remaining compatible with FlashAttention.

## Results

Under a restricted 0.1B-token pretraining budget, HIPE (bipartite) consistently outperforms RoPE on both WikiText-103 and C4 at 20M and 60M scales, e.g., achieving 47.98 vs. 48.85 PPL on WikiText-103 (L=512, 60M) and 130.47 vs. 141.65 PPL on C4 (L=1024, 60M). At the 300M scale (1B C4 tokens), learnable-σ_p HIPE nearly matches RoPE (28.97 vs. 28.93 PPL at L=512) while fixed-σ_p HIPE outperforms XPos by ~1 PPL. In low-resource SST-2 fine-tuning with LoRA, HIPE improves over RoPE by 1.2–1.1 points from 100 to 2k labeled examples (e.g., 77.4% vs. 76.3% at 1k), while RoPE regains a slight edge (82.03% vs. 81.77%) on the full 67k-example set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RoPE, HIPE achieves consistent perplexity improvements in the data-limited 20M/60M regime (e.g., −1.1 PPL on C4 L=1024 at 60M) and a clear accuracy advantage in low-resource SST-2 fine-tuning (up to +1.1 points at 1k samples), while the two methods are essentially tied at the 300M pretraining scale (28.97 vs. 28.93 PPL). Against ALiBi, HIPE wins on WikiText-103 at both scales and avoids the OOM failures that ALiBi suffers at L=2048 (20M) and L=2048 (60M C4), since HIPE's element-wise rescaling remains compatible with FlashAttention without materializing an L×L bias matrix. Against XPos at 300M scale, both fixed-σ_p and learnable-σ_p HIPE outperform XPos by approximately 0.9–1.1 PPL on C4 (29.04/28.97 vs. 29.97 at L=512). The one setting where RoPE edges out HIPE is full-data SST-2 (82.03% vs. 81.77%), consistent with the paper's theoretical prediction that the sharp-field limit becomes favorable in data-rich regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: (1) the neuroscience of discrete grid cell modules producing continuous place field gradients (extending Solstad et al. 2006 and Sorscher et al. 2023, which used continuous or abstract grid scales, to a discrete-module formulation consistent with Stensola et al. 2012), (2) the population coding / kernel regression framework of Bordelon & Pehlevan (2022) for analyzing how code structure shapes few-shot generalization, and (3) rotary positional embeddings in Transformers (Su et al. 2024). Unlike prior high-level hippocampus-Transformer analogies (Whittington et al. 2022; Gershman et al. 2025), the paper derives an explicit mathematical mapping from grid-to-place projection weights to a spectral scaling factor S(ω) applied to RoPE frequencies. It also departs from length-extrapolation methods like XPos and ALiBi, which modify RoPE to extend context windows, by instead using frequency-dependent decay to optimize sample efficiency under data-limited regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, biologically grounded inductive bias (HIPE) that improves data efficiency in small-scale language modeling and low-resource fine-tuning, with the practical advantage of remaining compatible with FlashAttention and requiring only element-wise rescaling. However, the empirical evidence is limited to 20M–300M parameter models, and at the 300M scale HIPE is essentially on par with RoPE rather than clearly superior, leaving open whether the advantage persists at frontier model scales. Key limitations include the linear, static, 1D theoretical model (no non-linearities, temporal dynamics, or environmental boundaries), and the question of how spatial principles generalize to non-spatial relational domains remains unresolved. The multi-scale code's robustness across unknown task scales is a compelling design principle, but whether continuous gradients (more biologically faithful) can outperform the discrete bipartite schedule at larger scales is an open question.
