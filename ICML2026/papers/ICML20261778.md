## Problem

Deep learning approaches require prohibitive amounts of data (e.g., >100k samples for Sudoku) to implicitly learn known structural constraints, while existing neurosymbolic methods face training infeasibility and scalability bottlenecks when constraints are learned or neural-parameterized. HONet addresses this by asking: for tasks where the constraint topology is already known, can we decouple neural perception from symbolic feasibility so that the network only needs to learn objective-shaping rather than rule discovery?

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse set of instance-specific clues (e.g., 17–36 Givens in a 9×9 Sudoku grid, or MNIST handwritten digit images for Visual Sudoku) as input, which are projected into variable-node embeddings on a hypergraph whose hyperedges encode the known constraint scopes. The output is a complete discrete assignment vector z ∈ {0,1}^N (flattened indicator matrix over n variables × d labels), produced by solving a strongly convex QP over the fixed relaxed polytope P = {z | Az = b, z ≥ 0} and then discretizing the relaxed solution. The constraint matrix A and vector b are immutable constants derived from the problem definition, so only the objective coefficients p are instance-specific.

## Method

HONet integrates a Deep Residual Hypergraph Neural Network encoder with a differentiable fixed-constraint QP decoder under a 'Fixed Polytope, Dynamic Objective' paradigm. The HGNN encoder performs iterative variable-to-factor and factor-to-variable message passing along the native variable-constraint incidence structure (preserving high-order hyperedge identity that pairwise clique expansion would discard), producing label-wise potentials that are temperature-scaled into objective coefficients p. The QP decoder (implemented via CvxpyLayers) solves min_{z∈P}(−pᵀz + λ/2‖z‖²) and backpropagates through the KKT conditions. The key training signal is the Geometric Consistency Loss (GCL), which combines coordinate-level MSE, a regularized energy-gap term (ReLU of E_λ(z*;p) − E_λ(ẑ;p)) that explicitly reshapes p so the target assignment becomes energetically favorable within the polytope, and an auxiliary cross-entropy warm-start term for sparse-clue regimes. Theoretically, GCL is motivated by the normal-cone optimality condition p − λz* ∈ N_P(z*), which characterizes the geometric region of coefficients under which the target is the unique QP minimizer.

## Results

On the 9×9 Sudoku benchmark, HONet achieves 100% whole-board accuracy across all difficulty levels (≈36.2, 26–28, 23–25, and 20–22 Givens) using only 9k training samples, converging in just 1–7 epochs. In the near-minimal-clue regime (17–19 Givens), HONet reaches 91.80% accuracy with 9k samples, approaching baselines trained on 180k samples. On Visual Sudoku, HONet attains 98.40% accuracy in 24 epochs, substantially outperforming SATNet (63.20% in 99 epochs) and SMTLayer (79.10%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SATNet (Wang et al., 2019), HONet wins decisively: 100% vs. 0–18.4% accuracy on 9k samples across all difficulty levels, and 98.40% vs. 63.20% on Visual Sudoku with 4× faster convergence (24 vs. 99 epochs). Against RRN (Palm et al., 2018), HONet (9k) outperforms RRN (9k) by 43–66 percentage points on Medium/Hard Sudoku (e.g., 100% vs. 33.90% at 20–22 Givens) and by 13.6 points in the 17–19 Givens regime (91.80% vs. 0.00%). Against the CNN baselines (Park, 2018), HONet matches or exceeds 1M-sample accuracy (100% vs. 98.60–99.10%) while using 111× fewer samples and converging in 4–7 epochs versus 85–148. Against the Recurrent Transformer (Yang et al., 2023), HONet (9k) achieves 91.80% vs. 78.20% on 17–19 Givens, approaching the 180k-sample Transformer (96.70%). Against SMTLayer (Wang et al., 2023), HONet leads by 19.3 points on Visual Sudoku (98.40% vs. 79.10%). Against symbolic solvers (Backtracking, Z3), HONet ties on accuracy (100%) but is a learned, end-to-end model that generalizes zero-shot to unseen sparsity levels, whereas solvers require no training but scale exponentially in worst case.

## Relation to Prior Work

HONet sits at the intersection of differentiable optimization layers (OptNet, CvxpyLayers) and neurosymbolic reasoning (SATNet, SMTLayer), but departs from them by fixing the constraint structure as immutable constants rather than learning or relaxing it. The encoder draws on hypergraph neural networks (Feng et al., 2019) and factor-graph message passing to preserve high-order variable-constraint incidence, in contrast to the pairwise clique-expanded graphs used by SATNet and standard GNNs. The Geometric Consistency Loss is motivated by KKT optimality conditions and the normal-cone characterization of the QP, positioning the neural network as an 'objective landscape shaper' rather than a constraint learner—a conceptual reframing within the decision-focused learning family (SPO+, Mandi et al.) that targets exact feasibility under known symbolic constraints rather than decision quality under uncertain objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a clean division of labor—neural perception shapes objective coefficients while a fixed QP enforces feasibility—that yields striking data efficiency (100% Sudoku accuracy at 9k samples, ~90% at 100 samples) and zero-shot OOD generalization across sparsity levels. However, the fixed-polytope paradigm is inherently limited to tasks where all defining constraints are shared and equality-based; the Futoshiki experiment concretely demonstrates the 'geometric mismatch' when instance-varying inequality constraints fall outside the solver. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger instances (16×16 Sudoku), how to integrate instance-specific inequality constraints into the QP without losing differentiability, and whether the data-efficiency gains transfer to other exact-cover families beyond Sudoku.
