## Problem

Current VLM post-training paradigms emphasize long chain-of-thought reasoning via RL but largely neglect visual perception, yet the paper shows that 86.9% of incorrect answers from Qwen3-VL-8B on visual math tasks stem from perceptual errors that longer reasoning cannot fix. No prior work systematically studies how to decouple perception from reasoning as separate training capabilities or determines the optimal order in which they should be learned.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265006/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text question as input and produces a structured output consisting of a reasoning trace enclosed in <thinking> tags followed by a final answer in \boxed{} format. For training, the perception dataset is synthesized from 15K DOCCI image-caption pairs by prompting Qwen2.5-72B to generate multiple-choice questions targeting spatial relations or fine visual details, then filtered via a dual-model criterion (Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-32B) that retains only samples where the model fails with the image but succeeds given the caption, isolating perception-specific failures from reasoning failures.

## Method

The paper proposes a staged post-training framework that decomposes VLM capabilities into three sequential stages—visual perception, textual reasoning, and visual reasoning—each optimized with GRPO (RLVR) on dedicated datasets. The perception stage uses a scalable data pipeline that transforms DOCCI captions into structured QA pairs and applies a perception-sensitive filter to retain only samples reflecting genuine visual perception deficiencies. The key empirical findings are: (a) perception requires targeted optimization with specialized data rather than being a pre-training byproduct; (b) staged training consistently outperforms merged training, with the order mattering—perception must precede visual reasoning; and (c) RLVR provides a stronger training signal for perception than caption-based SFT, which can degrade performance through off-policy token-level supervision. The framework is further shown to be orthogonal to difficulty-based curricula, and combining capability-based staging with difficulty-based ordering yields additive gains of 4.43% over merged training.

## Results

The staged-training Qwen3-VL-8B achieves 65.77% overall average across visual math and perception benchmarks, surpassing OneThinker-8B (64.87%) and the base model (62.19%). On visual math, it reaches 56.10% on WeMath, 75.90% on MathVista, and 51.10% average; on perception, it attains 74.51% on RealWorldQA and 73.07% on MMStar. Notably, staged training produces 20.8% shorter reasoning traces (445 vs. 562 tokens) while achieving 1.5% higher math reasoning accuracy compared to merged training. The Qwen2.5-VL-7B staged model reaches 59.75% overall, outperforming GThinker-7B (59.27%), OpenVLThinker-7B (58.48%), and MMR1-7B (57.50%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OneThinker-8B (the strongest 8B baseline), the staged Qwen3-VL-8B wins on WeMath (56.10% vs. 54.57%), MathVista (75.90% vs. 75.10%), MMStar (73.07% vs. 70.20%), and RealWorldQA (74.51% vs. 71.50%), but loses on MathVision (28.62% vs. 33.22%), yielding a 0.9% overall advantage. In the 7B category, the staged Qwen2.5-VL-7B outperforms GThinker-7B (59.75% vs. 59.27% overall), OpenVLThinker-7B (59.75% vs. 58.48%), MMR1-7B (59.75% vs. 57.50%), and R1-OneVision-RL-7B (59.75% vs. 53.24%), though WeThink-7B edges it slightly on overall average (60.16% vs. 59.75%) primarily due to a higher WeMath score (46.57% vs. 38.29%). The staged model also produces 20.8% shorter reasoning traces than merged training while achieving higher accuracy, and the advantage generalizes to InternVL3-8B (+3.77%) and InternVL3.5-8B (+0.95%) over their merged counterparts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RLVR-based post-training paradigm for VLMs (following DeepSeek-R1's adaptation to multimodal settings as in Vision-R1, LMM-R1, OneThinker, and GThinker) and on difficulty-based curriculum learning for VLMs (Curr-ReFT, PC-GRPO). It departs from prior diagnostic studies (VisOnlyQA, NoReGeo, 'More thinking, less seeing?') that identified perception as a bottleneck by proposing a concrete training methodology: a capability-dimension curriculum that explicitly decouples visual perception, textual reasoning, and visual reasoning into sequential stages. The key conceptual departure is treating perception as an independent trainable capability requiring its own dedicated stage and RLVR signal, rather than a pre-training byproduct or a component to be jointly optimized with reasoning in a single merged phase.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a directly actionable post-training recipe (perception → textual reasoning → visual reasoning, each via GRPO/RLVR) that practitioners can adopt to improve VLM performance without architectural changes. The finding that staged training yields 20.8% shorter reasoning traces while improving accuracy suggests a practical efficiency gain beyond raw benchmark scores. Key limitations include evaluation only at 7–8B scale (32B+ validation is future work), dependence on fine-grained captions (DOCCI) for perception data synthesis, and the open question of whether three stages represent the optimal granularity of capability decomposition. The orthogonality between capability-based and difficulty-based curricula opens a promising direction for multi-dimensional training trajectories.
