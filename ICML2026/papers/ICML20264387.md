## Problem

Standard neural networks rely on fixed activation functions (ReLU, GELU, SiLU, etc.) whose expressivity is limited by their inability to efficiently represent non-smooth features or nearby complex singularities. While trainable rational activations have shown empirical promise, no rigorous approximation-theoretic separation had been established to explain why they should be fundamentally more expressive. This paper proves that rational-activation networks are exponentially more parameter-efficient than smooth-activation networks in the worst case, closing the gap between empirical observations and theoretical understanding.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard inputs—32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10), 64×64 RGB images (Tiny ImageNet), and continuous state vectors (MuJoCo locomotion)—and produces class labels or continuous actions, respectively. The key structural difference is that each scalar activation is a trainable rational function r(x) = P(x)/Q(x) with numerator degree 5 and denominator degree 4, whose 10 coefficients are learned end-to-end alongside the affine weights. The denominator is enforced strictly positive via absolute values to exclude real poles by construction, and the rational degrees remain uniformly bounded across all layers regardless of depth or width.

## Method

The paper replaces fixed scalar activations in standard feedforward, CNN, and transformer architectures with trainable low-degree rational functions, then proves a sharp approximation-theoretic separation: any GELU/SiLU/Swish network can be uniformly approximated by a constant-width rational network of size O(log³log(1/ε)), while worst-case rational targets require Ω(log(1/ε)) parameters from bounded-parameter smooth-activation networks. The constructive upper bound decomposes GELU into square roots, logarithms (via the Sasaki–Kanada AGM-theta identity), and tanh (via Halley iteration), each realized by constant-width rational subnetworks with doubly exponential convergence. The lower bound exploits curvature constraints: rational targets with nearby complex poles force second derivatives of order ε⁻¹, which bounded-parameter smooth networks cannot match without logarithmic size growth. The separation extends to gated activations and transformer MLP blocks, and the paper further shows that standard normalization layers (BatchNorm, LayerNorm, GroupNorm) create non-identifiability and conditioning pathologies specific to adaptive rationals, motivating normalization-free variants.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with plain VGG, rational activations achieve 81.96% top-1 accuracy (VGG4) and 84.89% (VGG8), outperforming the best fixed activation (Swish at 80.16% for VGG4, LeakyReLU at 82.19% for VGG8) by 1.8 and 2.7 points respectively. On offline MuJoCo RL (IQL), rational activations yield 94.71 normalized score on HalfCheetah-medium (vs. 85.78 for ReLU, the best fixed baseline) and 72.28 on Walker2d-medium (vs. 61.25 for ReLU). On Tiny ImageNet, rational activations match or slightly exceed GELU across ViT-S, CaiT-S24, and Swin-T, and are the only activation that trains stably in a normalization-free ViT variant (61.24% vs. NaN for GELU/ReLU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rational activations consistently outperform all four fixed-activation baselines (GELU, ReLU, Swish/SiLU, LeakyReLU) on CIFAR-10: on VGG4, Rational reaches 81.96% vs. Swish (80.16%), GELU (79.93%), LeakyReLU (79.19%), and ReLU (79.03%); on VGG8, Rational reaches 84.89% vs. LeakyReLU (82.19%), ReLU (82.00%), GELU (80.48%), and Swish (78.71%). In offline RL (IQL), Rational dominates on HalfCheetah (94.71 vs. 85.78 ReLU) and Walker2d (72.28 vs. 61.25 ReLU), and edges out GELU on Hopper (91.58 vs. 90.75); under TD3+BC, Rational leads on Walker2d (84.65 vs. 69.02 ReLU) and Hopper (93.05 vs. 92.56 ReLU) but is essentially tied with GELU on HalfCheetah (62.44 vs. 62.61). On Tiny ImageNet, Rational matches or slightly beats GELU on CaiT-S24 (56.15 vs. 55.30) and ViT-S (58.35 vs. 58.15), is marginally behind GELU on Swin-T (60.66 vs. 60.76), and is the only activation that converges in the no-LayerNorm ViT variant (61.24% vs. NaN for all fixed activations). The paper notes that with GroupNorm, rational activations suffer a larger accuracy drop than fixed activations, consistent with the theoretical analysis of normalization interference, yet still retain top or second-best performance within each model block.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rational-vs-ReLU network separation established by Boullé et al. (2020) from piecewise-linear activations to the full family of modern smooth activations (GELU, SiLU, Swish, Mish, ELU, etc.), providing constructive proofs via AGM-theta logarithm blocks, Halley iteration, and Chebyshev/Clenshaw evaluation. It also builds on the practical rational-activation implementation from Delfosse et al. (2024), which enforces a strictly positive denominator to avoid real poles during training. A notable departure from prior work is the explicit analysis of how standard normalization layers (BatchNorm, LayerNorm, GroupNorm) interact adversely with adaptive rational coefficients, connecting to the recent normalization-free transformer line (Zhu et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exponential parameter-efficiency gap (log-log vs. log size) provides a principled explanation for why rational activations consistently outperform fixed ones on problems with sharp transitions or multiscale structure, and suggests they could form a more efficient foundation for deep architectures. The finding that normalization layers are disproportionately harmful for adaptive rationals—and that removing LayerNorm can rescue training where fixed activations diverge to NaN—offers a concrete architectural guideline. However, all empirical validation is confined to small-to-mid scale models (VGG, ViT-S, Swin-T) on a single RTX 4070, leaving open whether the expressivity advantage and normalization interaction persist at LLM scale or in large-scale RL, and whether a native fused kernel can close the modest runtime overhead.
