## Problem

Existing GRPO-based reinforcement learning methods for role-playing agents optimize a problem-centric objective that rewards context-specific logical correctness, causing the model to sacrifice character fidelity and collapse into generic assistant-style responses. This manifests as ambiguous reward signals (intra-group normalization rewards de-characterized but logically correct outputs), rigid character-agnostic KL constraints that fail to adapt to heterogeneous role difficulty, and late-stage style degradation due to the absence of explicit negative contrasts.

## Input / Output

The model receives a character profile, multi-turn dialogue context, and a user query, and produces a structured response y = y_CoT ⊕ y_ans where the chain-of-thought segment contains explicit <focus> and <focus_attr> tags (e.g., <focus>Memory</focus><focus_attr>I don't have any holidays.</focus_attr>) that serve as verifiable cognitive checkpoints, followed by the final character response enclosed in \boxed{}. Training uses only 650 samples from CharacterBench with two verifiable reward types: a Cognitive Focus Reward checking that the CoT explicitly states the relevant focus dimension, and a Reference-Guided Reward measuring overlap with a target reference style.

## Method

CRPO restructures GRPO's optimization around three synergistic mechanisms. First, Dual-Stream Advantage Estimation decomposes the total reward into a task stream (normalized intra-group relative to the sampling batch) and a style stream (normalized globally against the exponential moving average of that character's historical reward statistics), then combines them as A_i = λA_i^task + (1−λ)A_i^style to decouple logical and stylistic gradient directions. Second, Entropy-Aware Adaptive Exploitation operates at two levels: an instance-level gate that multiplies the advantage by (1 − γH_id) where H_id is the binary entropy of the model's confidence in distinguishing character vs. generic responses (suppressing gradients on ambiguous samples), and a model-level adaptive KL target d_targ^c = d_targ^base · clamp(r_H, δ_min, δ_max) with a proportional-integral controller that dynamically adjusts the penalty coefficient β per character based on its intrinsic fitting difficulty. Third, Contrastive Anchor Sampling injects one generic response y_anchor (generated with character instructions removed) into each sampling group, lowering the style-reward baseline so that intra-group normalization amplifies the advantage of genuinely character-specific outputs and prevents collapse toward the pre-trained generic distribution.

## Results

On CharacterBench (Qwen3-8B backbone), CRPO achieves an average score of 4.043, outperforming the strongest RL baseline PPO (3.901) by +0.142 and the best GRPO variant GSPO (3.894) by +0.149. CRPO leads on 8 of 13 individual metrics, including Memory Consistency (4.525), Boundary Consistency (4.308), Attribute Consistency–Bot (4.994), and Emotional Self-regulation (3.819). On SocialBench (zero-shot generalization), CRPO attains the highest average (0.802) with a decisive lead in Situational Understanding (0.350 vs. 0.280 for all other methods) and Long-Term Memory (0.970). Notably, CRPO is trained on only 650 samples, yet surpasses SFT baselines trained on 100k+ examples and proprietary 70B+ foundation models (GPT-4o: 3.722, Claude-4-opus: 3.866) on CharacterBench average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRPO outperforms all 10 general RL baselines (PPO, GRPO, GSPO, DAPO, GDPO, OAR, Dr.GRPO, REINFORCE++, RLOO, ReMax) on CharacterBench average, with the largest margins over GRPO (+0.214) and REINFORCE++ (+0.260). Against the closest RL competitor PPO (3.901), CRPO gains +0.142 on average, driven primarily by superior Persona (ACb: 4.994 vs. 4.844) and Emotion (ES: 3.819 vs. 3.575) scores. CRPO also surpasses all specialized role-playing SFT models (CharacterGLM-6B: 3.213, Haruhi-Zero-7B: 3.654, CoSER: 3.667, RoleMRC: 3.641) by 0.17–0.83 points despite using only 650 training samples versus their 100k–270k corpora. On SocialBench (zero-shot), CRPO leads in Situational Understanding (0.350 vs. 0.280 for OAR/PPO/GSPO) and Long-Term Memory (0.970 vs. 0.941 for OAR), while trailing PPO slightly on Humor/Sarcasm Detection (0.790 vs. 0.860). Among proprietary foundation models, CRPO (4.043) exceeds GPT-4o (3.722), Gemini-3-pro (3.787), and Claude-4-opus (3.866) on CharacterBench average, though Claude-4-opus retains an edge on Morality Robustness (4.735 vs. 4.659) and Human-likeness (3.151 vs. 3.300 is actually CRPO winning; Claude leads on MR only).

## Relation to Prior Work

CRPO directly extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by reconceptualizing its optimization objective from problem-centric to character-centric, building on the verifiable reward design introduced in Character-R1 (Tang et al., 2026). While a rich family of GRPO variants (DAPO, GSPO, GDPO, OAR, Dr.GRPO) have refined credit assignment, signal enhancement, and stability for mathematical/code reasoning, they all share the assumption that a single scalar reward per sample suffices. CRPO departs from this by arguing that role-playing requires fundamentally different optimization dynamics—decoupled task/style reward streams, character-adaptive KL constraints, and explicit negative contrastive anchors—none of which appear in the existing GRPO literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 650 RL training samples can outperform SFT models trained on 100k+ character-specific examples, suggesting RL with the right objective can be far more data-efficient for persona alignment than imitation. However, the evaluation is limited to two small backbones (3B and 8B) and two benchmarks, leaving open whether the entropy-aware and contrastive mechanisms scale to larger models or more diverse character distributions. The theoretical justification (Appendix A.1) consists of proof sketches rather than full theorems, and the practical impact of the dual-stream normalization on other multi-objective RL settings (beyond role-playing) remains unexplored.
