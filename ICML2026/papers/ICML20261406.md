## Problem

Reconstruction-based multimodal anomaly detection relies on deterministic crossmodal regression between RGB and 3D features, but the mapping is inherently one-to-many: a single 3D geometry can correspond to multiple valid RGB appearances. Under this ambiguity, deterministic regression collapses multiple valid targets into an averaged, over-smoothed reconstruction, producing spurious anomaly responses in normal but visually complex regions—especially under few-shot settings where the model has insufficient data to resolve the ambiguity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pixel-registered RGB image (resized to 224×224) and a 3D point cloud as input, extracting features via frozen DINO ViT-B/8 (224×224×768) and PointMAE (224×224×1152) backbones. It produces a pixel-level anomaly score map computed as the element-wise product of 2D and 3D reconstruction error maps (Gaussian-smoothed), and an image-level anomaly score taken as the maximum value in that map. Training uses only K∈{1,2,4} normal samples per class under a fixed random seed.

## Method

CFR operates in three stages. First, Crossmodal Feature Learning trains bidirectional GeGLU mapping networks (M_2D→3D and M_3D→2D) with a cycle-consistency loss (cosine distance over two mapping rounds) to obtain coarse crossmodal reconstructions while simultaneously building clustered memory banks via minimax facility-location coreset selection. Second, Crossmodal Feature Retrieval constructs a key-value crossmodal bank (3D features as keys, 2D features as values, projected via Johnson-Lindenstrauss) and trains a lightweight scaled dot-product attention module that aggregates top-K retrieved 2D candidates into a confidence-weighted reconstruction. Third, at inference, Feature Replacement filters reconstructed 2D features by their cosine distance to 2D prototypes; any feature exceeding a fixed threshold θ is selectively replaced with the attention-weighted retrieved feature, correcting ambiguity-induced collapse without resorting to wholesale retrieval. The final anomaly map is the element-wise product of 2D and 3D reconstruction discrepancies, suppressing modality-specific false positives.

## Results

On MVTec 3D-AD, CFR achieves the best mean I-AUROC and 30% AUPRO across all few-shot settings: 74.0/92.3 (1-shot), 77.1/93.1 (2-shot), and 80.5/94.2 (4-shot). On Eyecandies, CFR similarly leads with 75.9/82.7 (1-shot), 75.8/84.5 (2-shot), and 77.9/84.7 (4-shot), outperforming the second-best method by 11.8% I-AUROC and 2.8% AUPRO in the 1-shot setting. The gains are most pronounced on categories with strong one-to-many crossmodal ambiguity (e.g., Chocolate Praline, Licorice Sandwich), where CFR improves I-AUROC by up to 25.5% over the next-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFR outperforms all five baselines on both MVTec 3D-AD and Eyecandies across every few-shot setting. The closest competitor is CFM (Costanzino et al., 2024), the direct predecessor that also uses crossmodal feature mapping; CFR improves I-AUROC by 6.5 points (74.0 vs. 67.5) in the 1-shot MVTec 3D-AD setting while also gaining 0.9 in AUPRO. M3DM (Wang et al., 2023) is competitive on MVTec 3D-AD (73.9 I-AUROC in 1-shot) but lags CFR by 1.1 I-AUROC and 2.1 AUPRO; on Eyecandies, M3DM trails CFR by 11.8 I-AUROC in 1-shot. ShapeGuided (Chu et al., 2023) achieves strong AUPRO (87.4 in 1-shot) but weak I-AUROC (65.0), while EasyNet (Chen et al., 2023) and AST (Rudolph et al., 2023) are consistently the weakest baselines, with EasyNet's AUPRO dropping to 49.3 in the 2-shot MVTec setting. The largest relative gains for CFR appear on Eyecandies categories with pronounced one-to-many ambiguity (Chocolate Praline: +13.6 I-AUROC; Licorice Sandwich: +25.5 I-AUROC over the next-best method), confirming that the retrieval-based replacement mechanism specifically targets the failure mode of deterministic crossmodal regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

CFR builds directly on CFM (Costanzino et al., 2024), which learns deterministic crossmodal feature mappings between RGB and 3D representations for anomaly detection, but departs from it by identifying the one-to-many mapping ambiguity as a fundamental failure mode and introducing selective retrieval-based correction at inference time. Conceptually, CFR bridges two previously separate paradigms—reconstruction-based methods (which learn generative crossmodal mappings) and memory-bank methods (which store and retrieve normal prototypes)—by using reconstruction as a coarse predictor and retrieval as a targeted fallback only where reconstruction becomes unreliable. The cyclic consistency loss and coreset-based memory bank construction draw on standard techniques from feature-space reconstruction and PatchCore-style retrieval, respectively, but their combination into a coarse-to-fine pipeline is the paper's architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-to-many crossmodal mapping problem is a genuine and underappreciated limitation of deterministic regression-based multimodal methods, and CFR's selective replacement mechanism offers a practical, lightweight fix that is particularly valuable in data-scarce industrial settings where collecting many normal samples is infeasible. The counterintuitive finding that increasing training shots for the crossmodal mapping can degrade image-level detection (Hazelnut Truffle) serves as an important cautionary note for the field: naively scaling normal data may amplify mapping ambiguity rather than resolve it. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more than two modalities, how it behaves under spatially varying (rather than global) misalignment, and whether the fixed replacement threshold can be made adaptive without per-class tuning.
