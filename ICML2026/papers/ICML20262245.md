## Problem

Existing SDG-as-a-service platforms provide only output privacy (differential privacy on released synthetic data) while implicitly granting the service provider full plaintext access to raw training data. Input privacy—protecting the raw data from the provider—remains unsolved for the single-data-holder, single-provider setting, since MPC requires multiple non-colluding servers and federated learning requires multiple data holders. No prior work trains a synthetic data generator directly on fully homomorphically encrypted data.

## Input / Output

The data holder one-hot encodes their tabular dataset, encrypts it column-wise under CKKS FHE, and additionally uploads pre-generated encrypted unit Gaussian and Gumbel noise samples sufficient for the full AIM execution. Due to the column-wise SIMD packing strategy, the upload size scales with the total domain size Σωᵢ rather than the number of records N, and the data holder goes fully offline after this one-time upload. The system outputs a differentially private synthetic tabular dataset; the service provider never observes raw data, unperturbed marginals, or the DP noise itself, as all noise is injected within the encrypted domain before any decryption occurs.

## Method

FHAIM adapts the AIM marginal-based SDG algorithm to the FHE setting via three novel protocols: π_COMP computes 1- and 2-way marginals on encrypted one-hot encoded data using column-wise SIMD packing (multiplicative depth depends only on marginal degree k, not dataset size N); π_SELECT computes a squared-L2-norm quality score with Gumbel-Max noise entirely in the encrypted domain, replacing the L1 norm to avoid unstable polynomial approximations of the absolute value function; and π_MEASURE adds encrypted Gaussian noise to the selected marginal before decryption. A trusted Crypto-Service Entity (CSE) within the data holder's trust boundary decrypts only the DP-perturbed scores and marginals, performing the argmax selection in the clear. The generate step (probabilistic graphical model update) runs in plaintext since it is data-independent and operates solely on already-privatized statistics, preserving the (ε,δ)-DP guarantee via the post-processing property.

## Results

At ε=∞ (no DP noise), FHAIM-L2 achieves near-identical utility to the plaintext AIM-L2 baseline: workload error Δ=0.058 (Cancer), 0.014 (COMPAS), 0.298 (Diabetes) and logistic-regression accuracy of 0.684, 0.625, and 0.701 respectively, matching AIM-L2 to within 0.002. Under strict ε=1 privacy, FHAIM-L2 remains competitive (e.g., COMPAS accuracy 0.575 vs. AIM-L2's 0.580; COMPAS F1 0.620 vs. 0.597), while FHAIM-L1 degrades substantially due to polynomial approximation errors in the L1 norm (COMPAS accuracy drops to 0.532 vs. AIM-L1's 0.625). Total runtime is ~11–30 minutes on the three main datasets (peak memory <32 MB), and ~5.5 hours on the larger Adult dataset (vs. 19 min for plaintext AIM-L1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the plaintext AIM algorithm in both its L1-norm (AIM-L1) and L2-norm (AIM-L2) variants. FHAIM-L2 essentially matches AIM-L2 at ε=∞ across all three datasets (e.g., identical Δ=0.058 and Acc=0.684 on Cancer; identical Δ=0.014 and Acc=0.625 on COMPAS), confirming that the FHE overhead introduces negligible utility loss when using the exact squared-L2 norm. At ε=1, FHAIM-L2 is within 0.5% accuracy of AIM-L2 on COMPAS (0.575 vs. 0.580) and actually achieves higher F1 (0.620 vs. 0.597), while on Cancer it slightly outperforms AIM-L2 in F1 (0.495 vs. 0.307). In stark contrast, FHAIM-L1 suffers severe degradation relative to AIM-L1 due to the degree-10 polynomial approximation of |x| in FHE: COMPAS accuracy drops from 0.625 (AIM-L1) to 0.532 (FHAIM-L1) at ε=1, and F1 collapses from 0.605 to 0.139 at ε=∞. This motivates the paper's central design choice of replacing the L1 quality score with a squared-L2 norm, which is natively computable in CKKS with a single multiplication level and avoids all approximation instability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on AIM (McKenna et al., 2022), a marginal-based differentially private synthetic data generator, by adapting its select-measure-generate loop to operate entirely within the FHE domain. It departs from prior input-privacy approaches for SDG—MPC-based CAPS (Pentyala et al., 2024) and federated FLAIM (Maddock et al., 2024)—by eliminating the multi-party non-collusion requirement, enabling a single-provider, single-data-holder deployment. The work also extends a line of research on combining FHE with DP for individual statistics (Roy Chowdhury et al., 2020; Ushiyama et al., 2021, 2022; Bakas et al., 2022) to the full iterative training pipeline of a synthetic data generator, introducing the 'DP-in-FHE' paradigm where noise is injected before decryption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FHAIM establishes that input-private SDG is practically feasible in the most common outsourced setting (one data holder, one service provider), a gap that MPC and FL cannot fill without multiple non-colluding parties. Key limitations include the substantial computational overhead (~5.5 hours vs. 19 minutes for plaintext AIM on the Adult dataset), the reliance on approximate CKKS arithmetic, and the IND-CPA-D security setting where the CSE acts as a restricted decryption oracle—vulnerabilities acknowledged but not fully resolved. Open questions include extending the approach to other SDG algorithms (MST, RAP), scaling to larger and higher-cardinality datasets, and addressing CKKS-specific key-recovery risks in decryption-feedback deployments.
