## Problem

Heterogeneous federated learning (HFL) must aggregate models of different architectures across edge devices, but existing approaches either exchange only superficial knowledge (distillation, shared heads) or are confined to neuron-level pruning within a rigid predefined supernet. Neither paradigm can freely exploit the structural diversity of heterogeneous models to produce truly personalized architectures for individual clients.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262384/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a heterogeneous model zoo (e.g., 4 lightweight CNNs or a mix of CNNs and VGG11/13/16 across 50 clients), a small server-side public dataset (10% of training data) used for CKA-based block clustering and alignment-layer optimization, and each client's private local dataset. The output is a set of personalized heterogeneous models that retain each client's original architecture while having absorbed global feature representations, ultimately producing class labels on standard image benchmarks (MNIST, CIFAR-10, SVHN).

## Method

LEGO-FL decomposes heterogeneous models into neural blocks, groups them into Equivalence Sets via CKA similarity and Affinity Propagation clustering, then assembles diverse candidate architectures using a Center-Anchored Tree Search (CTSearch) governed by non-strict ordered constraints and a dynamic pool-block completion fallback. A training-free consensus selection (TFreeC) picks the optimal global architecture by scoring stitched candidates (with lightweight linear adapters bridging channel mismatches) via the NASWOT linear-region-complexity metric, avoiding any iterative training. Finally, Personalized Grafting (PG) constructs a hybrid teacher by docking the global feature extractor onto each client's local classifier (with a single optimized alignment layer), and the client model is updated via a combined cross-entropy and KL-divergence distillation loss, preserving local decision boundaries while injecting global representations.

## Results

On the most heterogeneous setting (Model Zoo 4, 7 architectures), LEGO-FL achieves 97.81% on MNIST (IID), 80.15% on CIFAR-10 (IID), and 87.52% on SVHN (IID), consistently leading all baselines by 2–16 percentage points. Under the challenging Non-IID 2-class label-shift setting on CIFAR-10 (Zoo 4), LEGO-FL reaches 79.88%, surpassing the strongest reassembly baseline pFedClub (71.46%) by 8.42 points and the distillation baseline FedMD (61.90%) by 17.98 points. The advantage is most pronounced as architectural heterogeneity increases: on SVHN Non-IID (Zoo 4), LEGO-FL outperforms pFedClub by 5.93 points (86.48% vs. 80.55%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEGO-FL outperforms all five heterogeneous FL baselines (FedCache, FedMD, FedGH, pFedHR, pFedClub) across every dataset, model zoo, and data partitioning setting. The largest margin is against FedGH, which shares only classification heads: on CIFAR-10 Non-IID (Zoo 4), LEGO-FL gains +16.26 points (79.88% vs. 63.62%), demonstrating that head-sharing is insufficient for deep heterogeneous networks. Against the strongest reassembly baseline pFedClub, LEGO-FL leads by 3.4–8.4 points depending on dataset and setting, with the gap widening under Non-IID conditions (e.g., +5.93 on SVHN, +8.42 on CIFAR-10). Distillation-based methods (FedMD, FedCache) degrade noticeably as heterogeneity increases from Zoo 1 to Zoo 4, while LEGO-FL remains stable, confirming that its training-free consensus (TFreeC) constructs a more effective global representation than soft-label alignment. In homogeneous settings (Table 3), LEGO-FL also surpasses FedAvg, FedProx, FLAYER, and DisUE, indicating the reassembly mechanism itself adds value even without architectural diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEGO-FL extends the Deep Model Reassembly (MR) paradigm (Yang et al., 2022) and its first FL application (pFedHR, Wang et al., 2023b) by shifting from neuron-level pruning within a fixed supernet to block-level reassembly across freely decomposed heterogeneous architectures. It departs from prior reassembly methods (pFedClub) by replacing strict sequential constraints and random anchors with non-strict ordered constraints and a fixed shallowest-block anchor, and introduces a training-free consensus selection via NASWOT (Mellor et al., 2021) in place of iterative training. The personalized grafting stage draws on model-stitching techniques to bridge global and local knowledge, positioning the work as a synthesis of NAS, knowledge distillation, and modular architecture search within the HFL setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free consensus selection (TfreeC) is a practical efficiency win that removes the most expensive iterative-training bottleneck in reassembly-based FL, making the paradigm more deployable on resource-constrained edge systems. However, the framework still relies on a single global consensus architecture, which may discard localized structural information, and ultimately depends on distillation as the knowledge-transfer mechanism—both acknowledged as limitations. Open questions include whether multi-consensus or augmented consensus strategies can capture richer local diversity, and whether a distillation-free transfer paradigm can achieve pure model reassembly. The counterintuitive finding that higher client participation degrades performance (due to increased architectural variance) is a useful practical insight for system design.
