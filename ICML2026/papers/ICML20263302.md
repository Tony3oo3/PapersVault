## Problem

Existing generative PDE solvers based on diffusion models (e.g., DiffusionPDE) rely on deterministic ODE-based guided sampling and do not exploit particle-based methods for physics-informed regularization. While SMC-based guidance has been applied to conditional generation in computer vision, no adaptation to physics-informed PDE solving exists, and the statistical degeneracy of SMC (collapsing effective sample size) in high-dimensional diffusion settings has been poorly understood. The paper asks whether SMC can be made practical for PDE solving and what actually drives its empirical success when theoretical consistency breaks down.

## Input / Output

The method operates on 128×128 spatial grids where only ~3% of pixels (500 per field) are observed for both the solution u and coefficient a fields, with the PDE residual evaluated over all m grid points. The diffusion model is pretrained on concatenated tuples x = (a, u) ∈ A × U, so the sampler jointly recovers both fields. For multiphysics systems (2- and 3-species reaction-diffusion), the input extends to multiple interacting scalar fields and their respective diffusion coefficients, and observation noise σ_O can be added to test robustness under imperfect measurements.

## Method

The core contribution is a Sequential Monte Carlo framework where N particles are propagated through the reverse-time diffusion SDE using a novel Second-Order Stochastic Guided (SOSaG) proposal that combines Karras et al.'s 2nd-order stochastic jittering with PDE-residual and observation-based guidance gradients. Because the SOSaG proposal is not point-evaluable as a density, the authors introduce a pseudo-bootstrap (pBS) Feynman-Kac formulation that modifies the target to p̄_θ(x₀|y)·p̃_θ(y|x₀)^ρ, effectively tempering the likelihood to calibrate the relative influence of the diffusion prior versus the PDE residual. The paper argues that SMC's empirical success in this setting stems not from maintaining high effective sample size (which degenerates toward a single particle) but from its inherent multiple-try evolutionary structure, where the proposal acts as mutation, the PDE-residual weight as fitness, and resampling as selection. This pBS formulation with ρ > 1 empirically outperforms the theoretically consistent TDS target across all benchmark PDEs and multiphysics systems.

## Results

On five benchmark PDEs (Darcy, Poisson, Helmholtz, Navier-Stokes non-bounded, Navier-Stokes bounded), the proposed SOSaG-pBS method achieves the lowest L₂ relative error on Darcy Forward (3.96% vs. 5.58% for DiffPDE), Darcy Inverse (5.28% vs. 8.31%), and Bounded NS Inverse (2.17% vs. 3.43%). Across the 2- and 3-species reaction-diffusion multiphysics systems, SOSaG-pBS and GEM-pBS consistently outperform the DiffPDE baseline, with the advantage growing as observation noise increases (e.g., at σ_O=0.02 on 2SRD, SOSaG-pBS achieves 8.42% D-field error vs. 8.14% for DiffPDE, while GEM-TDS degrades to 13.82%). The pBS weighting scheme generally outperforms the TDS variant across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is DiffPDE (Huang et al., 2024), an ODE-based guided diffusion method that the authors note already outperformed PINNs, DeepONets, PINOs, and FNOs in its original paper. SOSaG-pBS beats DiffPDE on Darcy Forward (3.96% vs. 5.58%), Darcy Inverse (5.28% vs. 8.31%), and Bounded NS Inverse (2.17% vs. 3.43%), while DiffPDE retains an edge on Poisson Inverse (19.88% vs. 24.61%) and NS Inverse (9.34% vs. 11.24%). On the multiphysics reaction-diffusion systems, the stochastic methods (SOSaG-pBS, GEM-pBS) consistently outperform DiffPDE, with the gap widening under observation noise: at σ_O=0.02 on 2SRD, SOSaG-pBS achieves 8.42% D-field error versus 8.14% for DiffPDE, while GEM-TDS degrades to 13.82%. The paper also shows that at equal computational cost (single particle, no SMC), the stochastic proposals (GEM, SOSaG) still beat DiffPDE on most benchmarks, indicating the gain is not solely from multi-particle SMC but from the stochastic integrator itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DiffusionPDE framework (Huang et al., 2024), which first incorporated PDE residuals as guidance during diffusion sampling, by embedding the sampling procedure within a Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) framework originally developed for conditional diffusion sampling (Wu et al., 2023; Karras et al., 2022). The key methodological departure is the SOSaG proposal, which combines Karras's second-order stochastic integrator with PDE guidance, and the pseudo-bootstrap (pBS) weighting scheme that deliberately trades the statistical consistency of the standard SMC target for empirically better solutions. Conceptually, the paper reframes SMC-based guidance for diffusion models as an evolutionary algorithm, arguing that the methods' effectiveness stems from their 'multiple try' sampling nature rather than from maintaining high effective sample size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical, scalable generative PDE solver that handles both forward and inverse problems as well as multiphysics (multi-species reaction-diffusion) systems, outperforming the ODE-based DiffPDE baseline across all tested PDEs and noise levels. The pBS framework and its tempering parameter ρ offer a principled way to calibrate the relative influence of the diffusion prior versus the PDE residual likelihood, bridging the gap between Bayesian sampling and PINN-style maximum likelihood estimation. Open questions include the problem-specific tuning of ρ (which shows non-monotonic behavior), the computational cost of evaluating PDE residual gradients at each SMC step for large systems, and whether the evolutionary-algorithm interpretation generalizes beyond PDE solving to other guided diffusion tasks.
