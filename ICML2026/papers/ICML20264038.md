## Problem

Most long-context inference methods (streaming, sparse, or windowed attention) impose restricted execution only at inference time while training with full-context attention, creating a mismatch in both execution semantics and cross-segment state evolution. This mismatch causes the model to depend on information available during training that is absent under the constrained inference regime, undermining stability and generalization at long context lengths.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long-context token sequence partitioned into non-overlapping segments of fixed length S=4096. Each segment is processed with two cross-segment inputs: a carried KV tail of length M=512 (the only differentiable state from the previous segment) and an optional forward-only retrieved prefix of length R=512 drawn from a past-only KV pool. The model outputs standard next-token predictions, and the entire pipeline is fine-tuned on SlimPajama with a standard language modeling objective for 1,000 steps.

## Method

The paper proposes a training-inference consistent segment-level generation framework in which both training and inference follow an identical segment-level forward operator, with cross-segment information flow decomposed into a local continuity channel (a fixed-size carried KV tail, the sole differentiable state) and a forward-only long-range retrieval channel (a top-k retrieved prefix from a past-only KV pool that carries no gradient). Training uses truncated backpropagation through time (TBPTT) with depth K=1, which the paper proves computes the exact gradient of the inference-consistent objective rather than an approximation, since the stop-gradient boundary at the truncation point eliminates all gradient paths beyond K state transitions. Architecturally, long-range retrieval is sparsified across both heads and layers: only a prior-based subset of heads (16 out of 32) in a small set of layers (4 out of 24) consume the retrieved prefix, while the majority of heads perform local, state-carrying computation over the carried KV tail and within-segment tokens. RoPE positions are re-indexed to treat both the carried tail and retrieved prefix as preceding context, preserving standard attention mechanics.

## Results

On LongBench-E, the proposed method achieves the highest average score under both LLaMA2-7B-32K (23.24) and LLaMA2-7B-80K (24.17) settings, outperforming full self-attention (23.13 / 23.38) while using substantially less peak prefill memory (18.56 GB vs. 23.61 GB at 32K; 19.06 GB vs. 34.67 GB at 80K). On RULER length-scaling (4K–64K), the method attains the best average accuracy (CWE 46.39 / FWE 43.88) and is the only method that retains non-zero accuracy at 64K context, where all baselines collapse to zero. The paper also reports approximately 6× lower peak prefill memory at 128K compared to full-context attention with FlashAttention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against full self-attention (the unconstrained reference), the proposed method matches or slightly exceeds average LongBench-E scores (23.24 vs. 23.13 at 32K; 24.17 vs. 23.38 at 80K) while reducing peak prefill memory by ~21% (32K) and ~45% (80K). Compared to CCA-attention, the method wins by a large margin on average (23.24 vs. 21.12 at 32K; 24.17 vs. 21.98 at 80K) and uses less memory (18.56 vs. 28.08 GB at 32K; 19.06 vs. 43.64 GB at 80K). Against StreamingLLM, the method achieves higher average scores (23.24 vs. 21.90 at 32K; 24.17 vs. 21.56 at 80K) with comparable or lower memory. Versus DuoAttention, the method edges out the average (23.24 vs. 23.00 at 32K; 24.17 vs. 22.94 at 80K) with slightly higher memory (18.56 vs. 18.15 GB at 32K) but lower memory at 80K (19.06 vs. 23.66 GB). Against MInference, the method achieves a higher average (23.24 vs. 23.08 at 32K; 24.17 vs. 23.35 at 80K) with lower TTFT (1.70 vs. 2.84 s at 32K) and lower memory (18.56 vs. 22.19 GB at 32K; 19.06 vs. 31.77 GB at 80K). On RULER, the method substantially outperforms all baselines in average accuracy and is the only method that does not fully collapse at 64K context.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by treating segmented execution as a shared modeling assumption between training and inference, rather than an inference-time optimization layered onto a full-attention-trained model. It draws conceptually on Transformer-XL's segment-level recurrence with gradient truncation and on DuoAttention's head-level separation of retrieval vs. streaming heads, but departs from both by (i) proving that TBPTT computes the *exact* gradient of an inference-consistent objective (not an approximation) when the carried KV tail is the sole differentiable cross-segment state, and (ii) introducing a forward-only retrieval prefix that provides long-range conditioning without creating additional gradient paths. Unlike prior training-inference alignment work (Longformer, CCA, Sliding Window Attention Training) that enforces identical attention connectivity, this paper aligns the *state-transition semantics* and *credit-assignment horizon* across training and inference under a strictly bounded recursion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~6× peak prefill memory reduction at 128K context and the clean theoretical guarantee that TBPTT with K=1 yields the exact gradient of the inference-consistent objective make this a practically relevant recipe for scaling long-context LLMs under bounded hardware budgets. The dramatic misalignment penalty (perplexity jumping from ~7 to ~111 at 64K) provides strong empirical evidence that training-inference consistency is not merely a nice-to-have but a necessity under constrained execution. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 7B/8B backbones, whether the unbounded retrieval pool can be made capacity-limited without quality loss, and how the method interacts with other efficiency techniques (quantization, speculative decoding) in production serving stacks.
