## Problem

Linear-complexity backbones (e.g., VRWKV, Mamba) offer scalable global receptive fields for 4D light field segmentation but rely on naive 1D tokenization that severs epipolar geometry, causing a phenomenon the authors term Structural Collapse—severe degradation in texture-less or occluded regions where depth cues are encoded as EPI slopes. No existing linear backbone explicitly embeds EPI geometric priors into its scanning or fusion process, leaving a persistent accuracy gap versus quadratic-complexity Transformers on complex urban scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265807/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 4D light field tensor I ∈ R^(U×V×H×W×C) (angular × spatial × channel), randomly cropped to 640×480, and decouples it into a Central View Stream (H×W×C) preserving high-frequency texture and an EPI-Geometry Stream ((UV)×H×W×C) encoding angular disparity. It outputs a pixel-level semantic segmentation map over 14 urban classes (Bike, Building, Fence, Others, Person, Pole, Road, Sidewalk, T.Sign, Veg., Vehicle, Bridge, Rider, Sky) via a softmax classification head applied to concatenated multi-scale features.

## Method

E²I-VRWKV builds a hierarchical Dual-Modal Stream architecture on a MiT-B4 Vision-RWKV linear backbone, where the core LF-ECMA block synergizes two branches: (1) an EPI Geometric Prior Generator that applies MHSA exclusively on horizontal EPIs to compute a slope-coherence affinity map, aggregated into a learnable EPI Bias tensor that encodes disparity-sensitive geometric constraints; and (2) a GC-Gate mechanism that functions as a geometrically modulated aperture, dynamically generating a gating tensor Γ conditioned on spatial context, aligned angular features, and the EPI Bias to calibrate when and how angular information is injected. A Dual-Modal Stream module performs bidirectional residual rectification between the spatial and angular streams at each stage, while a UniFusion Head aggregates four multi-scale feature maps (strides 4–32) into the final prediction. The design ensures O(N) linear complexity in the semantic backbone while confining the only quadratic-cost operation (MHSA) to the lightweight EPI prior branch, achieving 86.55% mIoU on UrbanLF-Real with 103M parameters and 44.1 ms inference.

## Results

E²I-VRWKV achieves 86.46% mIoU on UrbanLF-Syn and 86.55% mIoU on UrbanLF-Real, setting new SOTA on both benchmarks. On UrbanLF-Real, it surpasses the next-best method (EPI-Mamba with VMamba-B, 84.13% mIoU) by 2.42% and the leading Transformer-based method OAFuser (82.21%) by 4.34%. The model uses 103.0M parameters and 221.0 GFLOPs, achieving the fastest inference latency (44.1 ms) among all compared methods while delivering the highest accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E²I-VRWKV outperforms all 15 compared methods on both UrbanLF-Syn and UrbanLF-Real across every metric (Acc, mAcc, mIoU). The closest competitor is EPI-Mamba (VMamba-B) at 84.99% mIoU on Syn and 84.13% on Real; E²I-VRWKV leads by 1.47% and 2.42% respectively. Among Transformer-based methods, OAFuser (81.93% Syn / 82.21% Real) and MMSFormer (81.79% / 83.07%) trail by 4.5% and 3.5–4.7% mIoU, respectively. On the disparity-magnitude splits, E²I-VRWKV achieves 84.33% mIoU on UrbanLF-Syn-Small (beating SA-Gate by 6.13%) and 85.16% on UrbanLF-Syn-Big (beating LF-IENet++ by 5.28%), confirming robustness across both subtle and large displacement regimes. In the structural-collapse analysis (BT mIoU on thin/geometry-reliant classes), E²I-VRWKV reaches 83.01% versus 79.68% for OAFuser and 66.52% for a naive flattened-linear baseline, highlighting the benefit of explicit EPI priors for boundary integrity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Vision-RWKV (Duan et al., 2025) as its linear-complexity backbone, but departs fundamentally from both the Transformer-based light field segmentation line (OAFuser, SA-Gate) and the naive linearization approach used by SSM-based methods like EPI-Mamba. The key conceptual departure is elevating EPI geometry from an implicit feature to an explicit inductive bias: the EPI Geometric Prior Generator computes a disparity-sensitive attention map via restricted MHSA, and the GC-Gate uses this as a modulated aperture for cross-modal fusion. This positions the work at the intersection of linear sequence modeling and geometry-aware representation learning, addressing what the authors term 'Structural Collapse'—the degradation that occurs when 4D epipolar structure is flattened into 1D sequences without geometric anchoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled recipe for injecting explicit geometric priors into linear-complexity backbones, a strategy that could generalize beyond light fields to any 4D or multi-view task where naive serialization destroys structural information. The BT mIoU metric and the λ evolution analysis offer useful diagnostic tools for quantifying how well a model preserves geometric structure under linearization. However, evaluation is confined to the UrbanLF dataset family, and the EPI Prior Generator still relies on quadratic-complexity MHSA (albeit restricted to a small EPI tensor), leaving open whether fully linear geometric reasoning is achievable. Broader validation on other 4D modalities (e.g., multi-view stereo, volumetric data) would strengthen the generalizability claim.
