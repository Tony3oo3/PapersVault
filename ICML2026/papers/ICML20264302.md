## Problem

Existing GNN-based SAT models represent formulas as bipartite graphs or DAGs, limiting their ability to capture higher-order multi-literal clause interactions and inter-clause dependencies. Additionally, these approaches treat positive and negative literals as independent nodes without explicitly enforcing the complementary polarity relationship between them, weakening the modeling of intrinsic SAT structure for tasks like unsat-core prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264302/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a CNF formula as input, encoded as a clause-literal hypergraph (literals as nodes, clauses as hyperedges) augmented with a clause incidence graph where clauses are connected if they share literals, weighted by Jaccard similarity. It produces a per-variable score vector indicating each variable's likelihood of appearing in an unsatisfiable core, trained exclusively on UNSAT instances. For solver integration, the model runs once before solving and the precomputed scores periodically overwrite the CDCL solver's variable activity scores, avoiding repeated GPU inference.

## Method

PASAT models each variable's representation as a 2d vector decomposed into a polarity-invariant component (shared by both literals, encoding structural properties like unsat-core membership) and a polarity-equivariant component (sign-flipped under negation), with positive and negative literal embeddings constructed as inv+eq and inv−eq respectively. Message passing proceeds over the hypergraph via a degree-normalized incidence operator, then over the clause incidence graph to capture direct clause-clause interactions, before updated literal embeddings are recombined to recover variable-level components. Training combines a KL-divergence unsat-core prediction loss with two polarity-inversion consistency regularizers: an output-level L2 loss enforcing that predictions from a formula and its polarity-flipped counterpart are similar, and a decomposition-level loss enforcing that invariant components match while equivariant components are negated across the two views.

## Results

On the three synthetic SAT benchmarks (SR, CA, PS), PASAT achieves the best average Precision across all difficulty splits: 0.917 on SR, 0.408 on CA, and 0.797 on PS, outperforming the strongest baseline NeuroCore (0.895, 0.238, 0.774) by 2.5%, 71.4%, and 3.0% respectively. PASAT also leads on PR-AUC (0.963 / 0.511 / 0.857) and ROC-AUC (0.872 / 0.736 / 0.862) across the three datasets. When integrated with the Glucose CDCL solver, PASAT-guided solving resolves 15, 7, and 6 more instances than default Glucose on SATCOMP 2023, 2024, and 2025 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASAT outperforms all three prediction baselines (GCN, NeuroCore, SATFormer) on every metric and every dataset. The largest margin is on the CA dataset, where PASAT's average Precision of 0.408 exceeds NeuroCore's 0.238 by 71.4% and GCN's 0.171 by 138%, reflecting the benefit of hypergraph-based higher-order clause modeling on community-structured instances. On the simpler SR and PS datasets, gains over NeuroCore are more modest (2.5% and 3.0% in Precision) but consistent across all difficulty levels. SATFormer is consistently the weakest of the three baselines on CA (Precision 0.185) and competitive on SR/PS, yet still trails PASAT on all metrics. In the solver-integration comparison, Glucose augmented with PASAT scores solves 15 more instances than default Glucose on SATCOMP 2023 (148 vs. 133), 7 more on 2024 (118 vs. 111), and 6 more on 2025 (141 vs. 135), with improvements on both SAT and UNSAT instances.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NeuroCore (Selsam & Bjørner, 2019) paradigm of predicting unsat-core variables to guide CDCL solvers, but replaces the bipartite literal-clause graph with a clause-literal hypergraph augmented by a clause incidence graph, drawing on hypergraph convolution (Bai et al., 2021) for message passing. The key conceptual departure is the invariant-equivariant variable decomposition, which explicitly separates polarity-invariant from polarity-equivariant components of each variable's representation, and the polarity-inversion consistency regularization that leverages sign-flipped formulas as an auxiliary training view. Unlike SATFormer (Shi et al., 2023) which uses a transformer over a bipartite graph, PASAT models higher-order clause interactions natively through hyperedges and clause-clause edges, and unlike NeuroBack (Wang et al., 2024) which exploits polarity only at the label level, PASAT enforces polarity structure in both the architecture and the training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 71.4% relative precision gain on the CA dataset (from 0.238 to 0.408) is the most striking result, suggesting that hypergraph-based higher-order modeling and explicit polarity constraints are particularly valuable for structurally complex SAT instances with community-attachment topology. The one-shot scoring strategy (running the neural model once before solving rather than repeatedly during solving, as in NeuroCore) reduces GPU overhead while maintaining or improving solver performance, making the approach more practical for deployment. However, absolute performance on CA remains modest (precision ~0.41), and the framework has only been validated on unsat-core prediction; whether the polarity-aware decomposition and hypergraph representation transfer to other solver-guidance tasks (backbone prediction, phase selection, clause deletion) remains an open question. The clause incidence graph adds construction overhead (top-50 edge pruning is needed for large instances), and it is unclear whether the gains over a pure hypergraph without CIG would be sufficient in practice.
