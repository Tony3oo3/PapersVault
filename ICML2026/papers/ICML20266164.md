## Problem

Modern high-fidelity generators concentrate their generative capacity on the main semantic subject, displacing subtle discriminative artifacts to background regions. Existing detectors rely on global representations biased toward high-energy foreground content, causing these localized traces to be overshadowed and leading to severe performance degradation on commercial forgeries. Additionally, prior benchmarks predominantly use open-source generators and fail to capture proprietary post-processing pipelines of commercial platforms.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (center-cropped to 224×224) or a video frame (sampled every 30–60 frames, center-cropped to 320×320 for CommGen15 standardization). Internally, it derives two parallel inputs: a YCbCr quantization residual map and the original RGB image. The output is a binary real/fake decision with a calibrated probability score, computed as the sigmoid of an additive logit that fuses a global classification head with a local peak-aggregation bias.

## Method

PGC employs a dual-stream encoder: a lightweight CNN extracts spatial features from the YCbCr quantization residual (capturing low-level imaging-physics violations), while a DINOv2-Large backbone with LoRA (r=8) extracts semantic features from the RGB image. The Peak-Guided Calibration Module (PGCM) partitions each feature map into local patches, scores them via stream-specific heads, and aggregates scores using a temperature-scaled log-sum-exp (τ=0.5) that approximates a max operator, forcing the network to focus on the highest-scoring 'peak' patches rather than averaging uniformly. The resulting local bias Z_local (residual stream as anchor, RGB stream modulated by a learnable scalar λ) is additively injected into the global logit, enabling decisive correction when the global representation is misled by realistic foreground content. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy loss, jointly updating the backbone, stream heads, and calibration parameters.

## Results

PGC achieves 98.3% mean Acc and 100.0% mean AP on GenImage (8 generators), surpassing the previous best CoD by +2.1% in mAcc. On the newly introduced CommGen15 benchmark (15 commercial models), PGC reaches 97.9% mean Acc and 99.0% mean AP, outperforming the strongest baseline DDA by +12.3% in Acc. On AIGI (25 models), PGC attains 86.6% mean Acc and 95.0% mean AP (+3.5% over DDA), and on UniversalFakeDetect it sets a new record of 98.0% mean AP (+2.2% over AIDE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGC outperforms all 14 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On GenImage, the closest competitor is CoD (CVPR 2025) at 96.2% mAcc, which PGC surpasses by +2.1%; SAFE (KDD 2025) is second at 95.5%. On the new CommGen15 benchmark, the gap is dramatically larger: DDA (NeurIPS 2025) achieves only 85.6% mAcc versus PGC's 97.9% (+12.3%), while Effort (ICML 2025) reaches 82.3% and AIDE (ICLR 2025) just 68.7%, exposing a severe real/fake accuracy imbalance in existing detectors. On AIGI, PGC leads DDA by +3.5% in Acc (86.6% vs. 83.1%) and +5.1% in AP (95.0% vs. 89.9%). On UniversalFakeDetect, PGC's 98.0% mean AP exceeds AIDE (95.8%) by +2.2% and B-Free (95.0%) by +3.0%. Notably, PGC is the only method that simultaneously maintains >99% Real Accuracy and >95% Fake Accuracy on CommGen15, whereas baselines like NPR collapse to 2.8% R.Acc and AIDE to 37.5% F.Acc.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two established lines in AI-generated image detection—residual-based forensic features (NPR, LGrad) and foundation-model adaptation (UnivFD, FatFormer, Effort)—but departs from both by introducing a peak-focusing aggregation (temperature-scaled log-sum-exp) that explicitly counters the 'semantic dominance' problem where high-fidelity foreground suppresses subtle artifacts. Rather than treating all spatial patches uniformly as prior patch-level methods (PatchCraft, SAFE, AIDE) do, PGC treats the most discriminative local regions as a calibration bias additively injected into the global logit. The CommGen15 benchmark extends existing evaluation suites (GenImage, AIGI, UniversalFakeDetect) from open-source generators to 15 commercial platforms, addressing a gap the authors identify in prior data-centric forensics work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CommGen15 benchmark is practically valuable because it captures proprietary post-processing, web compression, and watermarking absent from raw local inference, and the stark failure of existing detectors on it (NPR at 2.8% R.Acc, AIDE at ~35% F.Acc) highlights a real deployment gap. The SHAP-based attribution showing backgrounds carry primary fake-detection evidence while foregrounds act as counter-evidence offers a useful diagnostic for future detector design. Open questions include whether the YCbCr quantization residual remains informative under aggressive re-compression or format conversion, whether the near-saturated GenImage scores (100% on 7/8 subsets) indicate benchmark exhaustion, and whether the peak-guided mechanism transfers to video-level (temporal) detection beyond frame-by-frame evaluation.
