## Problem

Standard real-valued networks (ReLU FNNs, SIREN, N-BEATS) require large width to resolve sharp rational-like spikes or near-singular targets, and degrade under partially observed inputs, making them unsuitable for edge deployment and data-scarce settings. The paper asks whether the analytic structure of such targets—specifically their pole locations—can be directly parameterized by a compact architecture derived from Cauchy's integral formula, yielding both a universal approximation guarantee and a practical parameter-count advantage.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real-valued input vector x ∈ ℝ^N (1D or 2D in experiments) and embeds it into the complex plane as z = x + i0 before applying per-unit complex bias shifts. The output is a complex scalar o = (1/h)C^T h; the real part Re(o) is the prediction, while the imaginary part Im(o) is optionally penalized via a λ|e|² term (set to zero in the final fixed-pole gap-filling configuration). For gap-filling, training samples are drawn away from turning-point neighborhoods and the model must reconstruct values within distance 0.15 of those neighborhoods, with the 1/h normalization keeping outputs bounded as width grows.

## Method

CauchyNet is a single-hidden-layer complex-valued network whose k-th hidden unit computes the Cauchy activation X(z) = ∏_{i=1}^N (H_{k,i} + ε)^{-1}, a product of shifted reciprocals that directly mirrors the multivariate Cauchy kernel K(ξ, x) = ∏(ξ^i − x_i)^{-1}. Crucially, there is no input weight matrix W: the Cauchy kernel's translation covariance (1/(wx+b) = (1/w)·1/(x−(−b/w))) makes any pre-activation scaling absorbable into a rescaled bias and output coefficient, so only complex biases B ∈ ℂ^{h×N} and output coefficients C ∈ ℂ^h are learned, giving 2h(N+1) real parameters. Training uses Wirtinger derivatives to backpropagate through the holomorphic activation, with a loss L = (y − y_true)² + λ|e|² that optionally penalizes the imaginary output. The paper proves that finite linear combinations of multivariate Cauchy kernels are dense in C(M) on compact M ⊂ ℝ^N (Theorem 1) and that CauchyNet realizes these kernel sums via its bias and coefficient parameters (Theorem 2), establishing universal approximation with a bias-only parameterization. A conjugate-symmetric pole variant pairs hidden units as (k, k') with B_{k',i} = B̄_{k,i} and C_{k'} = C̄_k, guaranteeing purely real output by construction and eliminating the λ hyperparameter.

## Results

On the 1D gap-filling benchmark (held-out turning-point neighborhoods, 200 train / 100 test samples), CauchyNet achieves a mean MAE of 0.0202 with only 128 trainable real parameters, compared to 0.0872 (SIREN), 0.1940 (N-BEATS), and 0.2032 (ReLU FNN) — a 4.3×, 9.6×, and 10.1× improvement respectively at 34% fewer parameters. On the mixed 1D rational-spike target with fixed poles and ridge-fitted coefficients, CauchyNet reduces MSE by 14.8×–81.7× over a parameter-matched ReLU FNN as n_train grows from 20 to 50. On UCI tabular data (n=100, 5-fold CV), refined CauchyNet beats the matched FNN on Diabetes (MSE 5007 vs 5920) and Wine (0.679 vs 0.731) but loses on California Housing (0.672 vs 0.578).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SIREN (Sitzmann et al., 2020), CauchyNet achieves 4.3× lower mean MAE (0.0202 vs 0.0872) on the gap-filling benchmark; SIREN's sinusoidal activations produce Gibbs-style ringing near the sharp rational spikes that the reciprocal Cauchy atoms capture directly. Against N-BEATS (Oreshkin et al., 2020), CauchyNet is 9.6× lower in MAE (0.0202 vs 0.1940) while using 34% fewer parameters (128 vs 204), since N-BEATS's multi-block ReLU architecture is poorly matched to near-singular structure. Against a width-matched ReLU FNN, CauchyNet wins by 10.1× on the gap-filling task and by 14.8×–81.7× on the fixed-pole mixed 1D target at small n, but the advantage is not universal: ReLU wins by 1.6× on the piecewise-affine step-ramp target, and the trainable-pole Adam run on the mixed target is effectively tied (MSE 0.041 vs 0.040). On UCI tabular data, standalone CauchyNet beats the matched FNN on 2 of 3 datasets (Diabetes, Wine) but loses on California Housing, where the smooth low-curvature structure favors standard real activations.

## Relation to Prior Work

CauchyNet directly extends the Cauchy activation function introduced by Li et al. (2025) into a full single-hidden-layer architecture, but its key conceptual departure is from rational-network constructions (Broomhead & Lowe, 1988; Boullé et al., NeurIPS 2020): rather than applying element-wise rational activations P(x)/Q(x), it multiplies reciprocal terms across all input dimensions jointly in a single unit, derived directly from the multivariate Cauchy integral formula. The bias-only parameterization (no input weight matrix) is a notable structural departure from both standard MLPs and prior complex-valued networks (Hirose, 1992), justified by the translation covariance of the Cauchy kernel. The universal approximation theorem connects to Stone-Weierstrass but establishes density specifically for finite Cauchy-kernel sums, with quantitative rates via tensor-product quadrature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fixed-pole ridge variant (128 learned scalars) achieving 14–81× lower MSE than a parameter-matched ReLU FNN on a rational-spike target at n_train=20–50 is a practically useful recipe for data-scarce, near-singular regression where edge deployment matters. However, the paper honestly delineates a narrow regime: trainable-pole optimization is fragile at small n, the advantage collapses when poles are extremely close to the real axis (δ≤0.02), and ReLU wins on piecewise-affine targets. Open questions include whether the holomorphic inductive bias extends to higher-dimensional inputs, deeper/residual compositions, and integration with neural operators or PDE solvers for localized singularities; a CNP comparison for the functional-imputation claim is explicitly deferred.
