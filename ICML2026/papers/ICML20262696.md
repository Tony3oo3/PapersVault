## Problem

Existing multimodal low-light video enhancement methods rely on auxiliary sensors (event cameras, infrared) as mandatory inputs at inference, but these require additional hardware, calibration, and synchronization that are often unavailable in real-world deployment. When such modalities are missing or corrupted, these models suffer substantial performance degradation, limiting their practical deployability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262696/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-light RGB video (processed as 128×128 crops in clips of 8 frames) and optionally an event stream (voxel grid at H×W×B) and/or infrared image (H×W×1). It produces an enhanced normal-light video by predicting a residual map added to the input. Critically, any subset of the auxiliary modalities may be absent at inference without architectural changes, and the model is trained to handle all combinations uniformly.

## Method

AMNet builds on a RetinexFormer backbone with ConvLSTM temporal modeling and SNR-guided multimodal fusion, but its key contribution is the Spatial-Spectral Dual-Gated (S2DG) Translator that synthesizes implicit auxiliary representations from degraded RGB features when real modalities are unavailable. The S2DG Translator operates in two stages: an Illumination-Aware Detail Selector (IADS) decomposes RGB features into low/high-frequency components and uses the illumination map to spatially gate out noise-dominated regions, while a Frequency-Band Selector (FBS) applies channel-wise 2D FFT and predicts spectral gating and scaling maps to selectively preserve informative frequency bands. Training proceeds in two phases: large-scale multimodal pretraining on ~1.7M frames of synthetic pseudo-multimodal data (events via v2e, infrared via ThermalGen) to learn cross-modal correspondence, followed by supervised fine-tuning with a composite loss combining reconstruction (L1+SSIM) under all modality-availability combinations and a feature-level distillation term that aligns generated implicit representations with real modality features.

## Results

Under the RGB-only setting (no auxiliary modalities at inference), AMNet achieves 31.57 dB PSNR / 0.95 SSIM on DID, 29.03 / 0.92 on SDSD-Indoor, and 26.37 / 0.84 on SDSD-Outdoor, outperforming the previous best method STCD by 1.47 dB on DID and 0.10–0.05 dB on SDSD. On the multimodal SDE dataset, AMNet with RGB-only inference (23.04 / 0.816 indoor, 23.75 / 0.775 outdoor) surpasses all event-based baselines that require event streams at test time. In zero-shot evaluation without fine-tuning, AMNet reaches 25.07 / 0.93 on DID, exceeding the best foundation model (DarkIR at 19.62 / 0.82) by over 5 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMNet outperforms all 18 baselines across every dataset and metric. Among RGB-only methods (Table 1), the closest competitor STCD (30.10 dB on DID) is surpassed by 1.47 dB in PSNR and 0.02 in SSIM; earlier methods like RetinexFormer (25.40 dB) and BLLRVE (23.25 dB) trail by 6+ dB. On the multimodal SDE benchmark (Table 2), AMNet with RGB-only inference (23.04 dB indoor) beats the strongest event-based method EvLight++ (22.67 dB, which requires event streams at test time) by 0.37 dB, and the gap widens to 0.41 dB outdoors; all other event-based baselines (eSL-Net, ELIE, EvLowLight, LLVE-SEG, EvLight) fall 1.3–3.1 dB behind. In the zero-shot setting (Table 3), AMNet (25.07 dB on DID) exceeds the best foundation model DarkIR (19.62 dB) by 5.45 dB, with FoundIR (13.28 dB) and MobileIE (16.03 dB) lagging even further, confirming that large-scale multimodal pretraining yields strong cross-dataset generalization without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

AMNet builds directly on RetinexFormer's illumination-guided encoder-decoder as its RGB backbone and adopts EvLight++'s SNR-guided fusion strategy for combining RGB and auxiliary latent features. It departs from prior multimodal LLVE methods (e.g., EvLight++, LLVE-SEG) that treat event streams or infrared images as mandatory inference inputs, instead reframing them as optional cues whose absence is handled by an implicit representation generator (the S2DG Translator). The large-scale pretraining with synthetic modalities (via v2e and ThermalGen) draws on the broader missing-modality learning literature but is the first to apply this recipe at scale specifically for low-light video enhancement, where the source RGB is itself severely degraded.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: real-world deployment of multimodal perception systems cannot guarantee that every sensor (event camera, thermal imager) is operational, calibrated, or synchronized, yet prior LLVE methods degrade sharply when any modality is missing. AMNet's modality-agnostic design addresses this gap, and the zero-shot generalization results suggest the learned cross-modal priors transfer to unseen scenes without fine-tuning. Key open questions include whether the synthetic-to-real gap (v2e/ThermalGen vs. real sensors) limits gains in truly extreme low-light, how the approach scales to additional modalities (e.g., LiDAR, depth), and whether the pretraining data scale can be further reduced without sacrificing the implicit representation quality.
