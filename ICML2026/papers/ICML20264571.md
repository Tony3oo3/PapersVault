## Problem

Standard RL pipelines require policy-grounded supervision (rewards, action labels, or annotated demonstrations) to train reusable policies, yet many practical tasks only need a single feasible solution recovered from large collections of unannotated task-execution videos. No existing mechanism directly exploits the progression structure in such videos to recover solutions without policy training, leaving a gap between the abundant passive video data and actionable task completion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264571/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 1200 unannotated egocentric video clips (10 fps, 20–60 s each) from the OpenAI Contractor Dataset plus a 50-clip warm-start set with coarse stage labels to initialize the VQVAE, DBSCAN clustering, and backward planner. At test time, it takes an initial state image and a goal state image, encodes them into flow categories, and outputs a concrete solution trajectory (a sequence of environment actions) of roughly 500 steps, along with the intermediate task-flow plan (an ordered sequence of ~5 discrete categories). In the PFR-guided RL variant, the same task-flow abstraction produces a trained PPO policy under a 10⁶-step interaction budget.

## Method

PFR-Search first learns a discrete task-flow abstraction by encoding video frames with a VQVAE (128-dim latent, 128-entry codebook, 10-frame windows) and clustering latents with DBSCAN, where the encoder is shaped by two novel consistency losses: an in-flow loss that aligns each local latent transition with the global flow vector (z_T − z_1) via cosine similarity, and a cross-flow loss that penalizes deviations from an autoregressive backward planner's predicted predecessor-category distribution across demonstrations. Backward planning then uses the trained 2-layer MLP backward planner to iteratively sample predecessor categories from the goal category until the initial category is reached, yielding a high-level task-flow plan. Forward search executes this plan with purely random actions, performing one-flow searches (200-step budget per transition) and using anchor-based replay—re-executing previously discovered action segments from the environment initial state—to monotonically accumulate progress without environment rollback. The PFR-guided RL extension repurposes the learned task flow as a shaped intrinsic reward (+1 for reaching the next planned category, −1 for off-flow deviation, −0.1 per-step penalty) to train a standard PPO policy, providing an evaluation interface that compares video-derived task structure against video-pretrained and reward-shaped RL baselines.

## Results

On five long-horizon Minecraft tasks, PFR-Search achieves a 52.0% average backward planning success rate and recovers feasible solutions in 16.0% of end-to-end forward search trials, compared to only 1.8% for unguided random exploration. With oracle task flows, success rises to 27.8%, indicating the remaining gap is due to abstraction and planning errors rather than execution limitations. In the PFR-guided RL evaluation interface, the method achieves competitive or superior performance against video-pretrained and reward-shaped RL baselines under a shared 10⁶-step interaction budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the PFR-guided RL evaluation (Figure 7), PFR-guided RL is compared against four baselines under a shared 10⁶-step budget: VPT (Baker et al., 2022), STEVE-1 (Lifshitz et al., 2023), PTGM (Yuan et al., 2024), and Director (PPO/MineCLIP) (Fan et al., 2022). The paper reports that PFR-guided RL achieves competitive or superior performance across all five Minecraft tasks, leveraging task-flow-aligned intrinsic rewards extracted from unannotated videos. The comparison is not fully equalized in pretraining resources: VPT and STEVE-1 rely on large-scale video pretraining, PTGM uses large unlabeled gameplay corpora, and Director(MineCLIP) uses a pretrained vision-language progress model, whereas PFR-guided RL uses only 1200 unannotated clips plus 50 coarse-label warm-start clips. The core policy-free PFR-Search results (Table 3) show an 8.9× improvement over random exploration (16.0% vs 1.8% average success), with the oracle-flow gap (27.8% vs 16.0%) attributed primarily to deviation in backward planning (60.4% of failures) rather than execution limitations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of using unannotated videos as auxiliary supervision for policy training (VPT, STEVE-1, PTGM, Director) by instead treating videos as the sole source of task structure for direct solution recovery without any policy learning. It repurposes standard representation-learning tools (VQVAE, DBSCAN) to induce a discrete task-flow graph, then combines backward planning (conceptually related to Recall Traces and backward curriculum RL) with forward random-action search guided by anchor-based replay. The key conceptual departure is that no dynamics model, reward function, or executable policy is ever learned; the task-flow abstraction itself serves as the planning substrate, which prior video-driven RL and procedural-discovery works do not attempt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a viable alternative to policy training for settings where a single feasible solution suffices, potentially reducing the annotation and interaction costs that bottleneck standard RL pipelines. However, the approach is currently limited to discrete-action Minecraft tasks with directed (non-cyclic) progress, and end-to-end success rates remain modest (16% average), with the oracle-flow gap (27.8%) indicating that task-flow abstraction quality is the primary bottleneck. Open questions include whether the discrete task-flow formulation extends to continuous action spaces, cyclic or repeated behaviors, and larger-scale environments, and whether the 50-clip warm-start requirement can be eliminated entirely.
