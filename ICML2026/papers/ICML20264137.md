## Problem

Existing adaptive discretization and zooming methods for stochastic Lipschitz optimization implicitly avoid suboptimal regions through tree-based refinement, but they do not expose explicit certificates identifying which regions are provably suboptimal at any time t, nor do they provide a measurable progress indicator showing how much of the domain remains plausibly optimal. This gap is critical in 'precious call' settings (hyperparameter tuning, simulation-based optimization) where each evaluation is expensive and practitioners need principled, anytime-valid stopping criteria rather than fixed-budget heuristics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential noisy point evaluations (sub-Gaussian noise with known or adaptively estimated σ) over the domain [0,1]^d, along with a Lipschitz constant L (or an initial estimate for the adaptive variant), a total budget T, and a confidence parameter δ. Beyond the optimal point x̂_T, the primary output is the active set A_T defined by the exact membership rule U_t(x) ≥ ℓ_t (computable in O(N_t) time per query), which certifies that every point outside A_T is suboptimal with probability ≥ 1−δ. A secondary output is the computable gap proxy ε_t = 2(β_t + Lη_t) + γ_t, which serves as an always-valid stopping signal; volume Vol(A_t) provides an additional progress diagnostic whose validity as a stopping rule requires an extra lower-regularity assumption (Assumption 4.12).

## Method

CGP constructs a Lipschitz UCB envelope U_t(x) = min_i {UCB_i(t) + L·d(x,x_i)} that upper-bounds f(x) everywhere with high probability, and a global lower certificate ℓ_t = max_i LCB_i(t) that lower-bounds f(x*). The active set A_t = {x : U_t(x) ≥ ℓ_t} is the set of points not yet certifiably suboptimal; queries are selected by maximizing a score combining UCB (exploitation) and a negative distance-to-nearest-sample penalty (coverage), with a replication rule that allocates extra samples to active points whose confidence radii exceed a target schedule β_sched(t) = O(σ√(log(T/δ))/t). Under a margin condition parameterized by the near-optimality dimension α, the paper proves Vol(A_t) ≤ C·(2(β_t + Lη_t) + γ_t)^{d−α}, yielding sample complexity Õ(ε^{-(2+α)}) that is minimax-optimal up to logarithmic factors. Three extensions broaden applicability: CGP-Adaptive learns L online via a doubling scheme with O(log(L*/L̂_0)) overhead and provably valid certificates after the final doubling; CGP-TR maintains local certificates within adaptively sized trust regions with certified restarts (a region is restarted only when its UCB maximum falls below the global lower certificate), enabling d > 50; and CGP-Hybrid estimates a local smoothness ratio ρ = L_local/L_global and switches to GP-UCB refinement within A_t when ρ < 0.5, while preserving the Phase-1 certificate regardless of the Phase-2 optimizer.

## Results

On 7 low/medium-dimensional benchmarks (d ∈ [2,10]), CGP-Hybrid achieves the lowest simple regret (×10⁻²) at T=200 across all tasks: 1.1 on Needle-2D, 1.4 on Branin, 2.7 on Hartmann-6, 7.8 on Ackley-10, 3.5 on Levy-5, 3.4 on Rosenbrock-4, and 2.6 on SVM-RBF-6. On high-dimensional benchmarks (d ∈ [36,100]), CGP-TR outperforms TuRBO by 9–12% (e.g., 11.2 vs 12.4 on Rover-60, 10.4 vs 11.2 on NAS-36, 17.1 vs 18.7 on MuJoCo-Ant-100) while additionally providing local optimality certificates. CGP-Adaptive is robust to 100× underestimation of the initial Lipschitz constant, degrading regret by only 12% (3.2 vs 2.9 on Hartmann-6) with 7 doublings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGP-Hybrid outperforms all 9 baselines on every low/medium-dimensional benchmark. Against GP-UCB, CGP-Hybrid wins by 1.0 on Needle-2D (1.1 vs 2.1) and 0.4 on Branin (1.4 vs 1.8), with the gap widening on non-smooth functions where GP's kernel assumption is violated. Against TuRBO, CGP-Hybrid leads by 0.7 on Needle (1.1 vs 1.8) and 0.7 on Branin (1.4 vs 2.1); on high-dimensional Rover-60, CGP-TR beats TuRBO by 1.2 (11.2 vs 12.4). Against HEBO, the closest competitor, CGP-Hybrid wins by 0.8 on Needle (1.1 vs 1.9), 0.2 on Branin (1.4 vs 1.6), and 0.6 on Hartmann-6 (2.7 vs 3.3). Against the Lipschitz-specific baselines, CGP dominates HOO (1.2 vs 3.4 on Needle) and StoSOO (1.2 vs 4.1 on Needle), while CGP-Adaptive's doubling scheme beats LIPO's adaptive L estimation by 2.1× on Hartmann-6 (2.9 vs 6.2 regret). The key differentiator is that CGP variants provide computable optimality certificates and principled stopping rules that none of the baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

CGP sits at the intersection of zooming-based Lipschitz bandits (Kleinberg et al., 2008; Bubeck et al., 2011) and confidence-bound optimization (Auer et al., 2002), but its core departure is making the pruning mechanism that zooming algorithms maintain only as an analysis artifact into an explicit, computable active set A_t with measurable volume and a closed-form gap proxy. The trust-region variant (CGP-TR) borrows the expand/contract design from TuRBO (Eriksson et al., 2019) while adding certified restarts that TuRBO lacks, and the adaptive-L variant (CGP-Adaptive) extends LIPO's (Malherbe & Vayatis, 2017) doubling scheme with the first provable certificate-validity guarantee. The hybrid variant composes CGP's Lipschitz certificates with GP-UCB (Srinivas et al., 2010) refinement, preserving certificate validity regardless of the Phase-2 optimizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners running expensive black-box evaluations (NAS, simulation-based design, safe robotics), CGP provides the first Lipschitz-optimization method with anytime-valid optimality certificates and a computable stopping signal, eliminating the need to commit to a fixed budget in advance. The sample complexity O(ε^-(2+α)) is minimax-optimal and strictly improves on the worst-case O(ε^-(2+d)) whenever the optimum is sharp (α < d), which holds for most practical objectives. Key limitations include the requirement of Lipschitz continuity (excluding functions with discontinuities), dimension caps (d ≤ 15 for vanilla CGP, d ≤ 100 for CGP-TR), and the margin condition that may fail for flat-plateau objectives. Open directions flagged by the authors include using A_t for safety certificates in constrained optimization and combining CGP-TR with random embeddings to recover global certificates in very high dimensions.
