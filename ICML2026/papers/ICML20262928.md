## Problem

Most spatio-temporal forecasting models are optimized for a single training distribution and degrade sharply under non-stationary environments such as cross-city transfer, seasonal shifts, or evolving road networks. Existing OOD generalization methods cast spatio-temporal evolution as discrete graph inference, making it difficult to disentangle universal physical dynamics from environment-specific heterogeneity, and they often force a trade-off between exploiting environmental cues for in-distribution accuracy and suppressing them for generalization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a historical window of T=12 five-minute steps of node features on a spatio-temporal graph (X ∈ R^{T×N×C}, with N up to 1,728 nodes) and produces T'=12 future steps in a single forward pass. Rather than autoregressive recurrence, STPDE adopts a 'time-as-features' principle: the entire history is compressed into a D-dimensional latent field via a linear projection, evolved by a one-shot PDE solver, and decoded back to the output space, enabling parallel inference without step-wise unrolling.

## Method

STPDE reformulates spatio-temporal dynamics as the evolution of an inhomogeneous PDE ∂u/∂τ = α(Φ,ξ)∇²u + S(Φ,ξ), explicitly decoupling a shared, domain-invariant Laplacian (universal transport/conservation) from environment-conditioned diffusivity and source terms. The Invariant Diffusion Operator approximates the Green's function (heat kernel) of the Laplacian via a separable-kernel linear attention mechanism, reducing global propagation from O(N²) to O(ND²) through an aggregate-distribute factorization. The Environment Basis Manifold parameterizes spatially varying coefficients using K shared bases with sparse Top-k routing and AdaLN modulation, while stochastic perturbations (real-average-misaligned sampling) on the bases emulate unobserved environmental changes to improve OOD robustness. The full pipeline is an end-to-end trainable Encode-Solve-Decode architecture: linear projection to latent field → single-pass PDE solver with environment modulation → SwiGLU FFN residual → linear decode, with a load-balancing auxiliary loss to prevent router collapse.

## Results

On the LargeST benchmark (4 sub-regions: CA-D3, SD, Orange, LA), STPDE achieves the best average MAE across all four evaluation settings, reducing average MAE by 1.5% (ID), 4.0% (OOD), 7.2% (few-shot cross-city transfer), and 27.2% (continual learning) over the strongest runner-up. Under OOD (seasonal shift from Jan to Aug 2019), STPDE attains MAE of 16.34 on CA-D3, 19.89 on SD, 21.52 on Orange, and 23.71 on LA, outperforming all baselines in every cell. On the PEMS-Stream continual learning benchmark, STPDE achieves MAE 11.40 / RMSE 18.95 / MAPE 16.01%, dramatically surpassing the next-best method STRAP (MAE 15.67).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STPDE achieves the best average MAE across all four LargeST sub-regions in both ID and OOD settings, though it does not win every individual cell (e.g., HimNet edges out STPDE on SD ID MAE by 0.42, and D²STGNN leads on Orange ID MAE by 0.28). Under OOD, STPDE dominates all four datasets, beating the strongest OOD-specific baselines CauSTG and STOP by 0.66–1.29 MAE points. In few-shot cross-city transfer (Table 2), STPDE (MAE 16.25) outperforms the best pre-training baseline CrossST (17.51) by 7.2%, confirming that freezing the Invariant Diffusion Operator and only reconstructing the Environment Basis Manifold enables data-efficient adaptation. On continual learning (Table 3), STPDE (MAE 11.40) surpasses STRAP (15.67) by 27.2% and EAC (no reported value in the main table), demonstrating that the freeze-and-expand strategy for the basis manifold effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting under growing topologies. Notably, several OOD-tailored methods (STONE, STOP) underperform even standard STGNNs like GWNet in certain scenarios, which the authors attribute to underfitting and overly rigid spatial-temporal decoupling assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the diffusion-to-attention theory of Wu et al. (2025a), which establishes the mathematical equivalence between PDE-based kernel propagation and attention mechanisms, and repurposes it as the computational backbone for spatio-temporal forecasting. It departs from the dominant STGNN paradigm (GWNet, DCRNN, STGCN) and from OOD-specific methods (CauSTG, CaST, STONE, STOP) that treat spatio-temporal evolution as discrete graph inference, instead reformulating the problem as the numerical solution of an inhomogeneous PDE where a shared Laplacian captures universal dynamics and a sparse Environment Basis Manifold encodes local heterogeneity. The linear-complexity Green's-function approximation via kernel factorization (Theorem 4.2) is a practical bridge that makes the PDE formulation scalable, while the stochastic perturbation and load-balancing loss draw from data augmentation and Mixture-of-Experts literature respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit decoupling of invariant physics from environment-specific modulation offers a principled, unified mechanism for handling distribution shift across ID, OOD, few-shot transfer, and continual learning settings without task-specific re-architecting, which is a meaningful step toward deployable spatio-temporal forecasters. The 27.2% MAE reduction in continual learning and the observation that purpose-built OOD methods can underperform strong ID baselines (attributed to underfitting and spurious spatial-temporal decoupling) are practically important diagnostics for the field. Open questions include whether the PDE formulation generalizes beyond traffic to other spatio-temporal domains (weather, epidemiology), whether the linear-attention approximation of the Green's function captures genuine physical structure or merely a strong inductive bias, and whether the multi-component architecture (PDE solver + basis manifold + AdaLN + stochastic perturbation) can be simplified without losing the robustness gains.
