## Problem

Existing personalized FedLoRA (pFedLoRA) methods rely on a dual-LoRA architecture—one module for global knowledge and another for client-specific adaptation—which doubles computational overhead and introduces structural redundancy for each client. The paper asks whether effective personalization can be achieved within a single LoRA module by exploiting the asymmetric roles of its A and B matrices, without auxiliary routing networks or expert mechanisms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261509/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client fine-tunes a frozen foundation model (RoBERTa-Large for text, ViT-B/16 for images) using a single LoRA pair (A ∈ R^{r×d}, B ∈ R^{l×r}) on local private data. Clients upload both A_k and B_k to the server each round; the server returns a decorrelated aggregated B_agg to all clients while A matrices remain local. The final output is task-specific predictions (sentiment, entailment, paraphrase, or image class) from the personalized single-LoRA model at each client.

## Method

FedPissa rethinks single-LoRA federated personalization through two modules. First, Federated Matrix Selection (FMS) selectively aggregates only the B matrices across clients via weighted averaging while keeping A matrices local, motivated by the empirical observation that B converges faster and exhibits greater cross-client variance, making it the better carrier of shared directional updates. Second, Personalized Matrix-level Adaptation (PMA) constructs a decorrelated projection matrix P_k for each client from the tail eigenspace (r smallest singular vectors) of the aggregated covariance C_k = Σ_{i≠k} α_i A_i A_i^T of all other clients' A matrices. The server then computes B_agg = B_avg + λ Σ_k p_k (B_k − B_avg) P_k, so each client's personalized residual is projected into a subspace nearly orthogonal to other clients' A subspaces, reducing cross-client interference. A theoretical proof via the Ky Fan / Rayleigh-Ritz theorem shows that the tail-rank projection minimizes the cross-client interference objective Σ_{i≠k}‖(B_k − B_avg) A_i‖_F² under an isotropy surrogate, providing formal justification for the design.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (RoBERTa-Large) under feature non-IID, FedPissa achieves a mean accuracy of 83.88%, outperforming the best single-LoRA baseline FedSA (82.05%) and the best dual-LoRA baseline FedDPA (77.98%). Under the more challenging feature-and-label non-IID setting (β=0.1), FedPissa reaches 89.09% mean accuracy, a 26.63-point gain over FedIT (62.46%). On DomainNet (ViT-Base) under feature non-IID, FedPissa achieves 51.80% mean accuracy, surpassing FedLEASE (49.23%) and FedSA (47.43%). The paper also reports up to 35% lower communication and computation cost compared to dual-LoRA methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedPissa outperforms all five baselines across both GLUE and DomainNet under every non-IID setting tested. On GLUE (feature non-IID), it leads FedSA (the closest single-LoRA competitor) by 1.83 points in mean accuracy and surpasses the dual-LoRA methods FedDPA and FedLEASE by 5.90 and 10.15 points respectively, while also cutting their computational overhead by roughly 35%. On DomainNet (feature non-IID), FedPissa achieves 51.80% mean accuracy versus 49.23% for FedLEASE and 47.43% for FedSA, with the largest margin over FFA-LoRA (25.17%), which suffers from its fixed-A design. Under the most heterogeneous label setting (β=0.1), FedPissa's advantage over FedIT widens to 26.63 points, indicating that the decorrelated subspace projection is particularly effective when label distributions diverge sharply. The only task where FedPissa trails a baseline is SST-2 under feature non-IID (91.40 vs. FFA-LoRA's 94.70), but this is offset by large gains on QNLI, QQP, RTE, and MNLI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the pFedLoRA line of work (FedIT, FedSA, FFA-LoRA, FedDPA, FedLEASE), which combines LoRA-based PEFT with federated learning to enable privacy-preserving collaborative fine-tuning of foundation models. The key departure is architectural: all existing pFedLoRA methods adopt a dual-LoRA design (one branch for global knowledge, one for client-specific adaptation), whereas FedPissa demonstrates that a single LoRA module suffices if one selectively aggregates only the B matrices and applies a tail-rank decorrelated subspace projection to the residuals. This inverts the sharing convention of FedSA (which shares A and keeps B local), motivated by an empirical observation that B converges faster and exhibits greater cross-client variance, making it the more suitable component for global coordination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 35% reduction in computation and communication cost from eliminating the dual-LoRA redundancy is practically significant for resource-constrained federated deployments, and the up-to-8% accuracy gain over strong baselines (including dual-LoRA methods) validates that the efficiency loss was not necessary. However, the method's reliance on tail-rank projection introduces a sensitivity to LoRA rank (degrading for very small r) and a potential performance drop under near-homogeneous data distributions, both of which limit its universality. Open questions include whether the asymmetric A/B sharing principle generalizes to other PEFT variants (e.g., DoRA, VeRA), whether the decorrelation objective can be made differentiable for end-to-end optimization, and how the approach scales to hundreds of clients or much larger foundation models where the covariance construction becomes costly.
