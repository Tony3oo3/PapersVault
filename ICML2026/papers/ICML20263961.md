## Problem

Existing LLM-driven symbolic regression methods guide equation search with a single scalar metric (e.g., global MSE), which indicates whether a proposed equation is accurate but fails to identify which specific terms drive or degrade performance. Without granular credit assignment, the discovery process degenerates into inefficient trial-and-error that relies heavily on the LLM's generative priors rather than data-driven reasoning, making it difficult to navigate the combinatorial space of possible equations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a numerical dataset (features and target) together with a free-text task description and domain context that grounds the LLM's proposals. It produces a sparse closed-form equation f(x) = Σ w_j ψ_j(x) where the ψ_j are LLM-proposed nonlinear basis functions (arbitrarily complex expressions over the input features) and w_j are weights from an OLS fit, preserving global interpretability. The per-term influence scores Δ_j are computed as the increase in validation MSE when a term's weight is zeroed while all other coefficients are held fixed, providing a structured vector of credit-assignment signals rather than a single scalar.

## Method

IGSR decouples generative creativity from data-driven selection via a propose-and-prune cycle: an LLM agent proposes ~5 candidate basis functions conditioned on task context and a history of previously kept/dropped terms; an OLS linear model is fit to obtain weights; per-term influence scores Δ_j are computed on a validation set (no-refit: set w_j→0, hold other weights fixed); and a deterministic pruner retains the top-K terms by Δ_j. This cycle is embedded in a Monte Carlo Tree Search where each node represents an equation state, children are generated by repeated stochastic LLM runs from the post-pruning state, and the UCT criterion (with negative validation MSE as reward) balances exploration of novel functional branches against exploitation of high-influence components. The key architectural insight is that the influence score vector provides granular, structure-aware feedback that tells the LLM exactly which terms were retained or discarded and why, enabling iterative refinement that converges on the underlying mechanism rather than merely minimizing a global loss.

## Results

On six biological/clinical benchmarks, IGSR achieves the lowest test MSE among all interpretable (white-box) methods in 5/6 datasets, with an average rank of 1.17 (vs. 3.83 for the next-best white-box method, ICL). On the Lung Cancer (Chemo. & Radio.) dataset, IGSR reaches 0.0141 MSE versus 6.1 for ICSR and 0.0557 for ICL. On LLM-SRBench (128 discovery problems), IGSR attains the best average rank in both in-distribution NMSE (1.35) and out-of-distribution NMSE (1.77), as well as the best Acc₀.₁ ranks (1.12 ID, 1.21 OOD), outperforming all LLM-based baselines including LLM-SR (3.71/3.92 NMSE) and ICSR (4.21/4.22 NMSE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGSR outperforms all white-box non-LLM baselines (SINDy, GPLearn, PySR) by orders of magnitude on the PKPD and Warfarin datasets, where these methods struggle with high-dimensional or nonlinear feature spaces. Among LLM-based white-box methods, IGSR dominates: on Lung Cancer (Chemo. & Radio.), IGSR achieves 0.0141 MSE versus 6.1 for ICSR, 63.3 for ICL, and 253 for D3; on LLM-SRBench, IGSR's average NMSE rank of 1.35 (ID) beats LLM-SR (3.71), ICSR (4.21), LaSR (5.53), and D3 (6.89). The only white-box method that beats IGSR on a single dataset is ICSR on Warfarin (0.497 vs. 0.565 MSE), a narrow margin. Against black-box baselines, IGSR is competitive: it matches or exceeds XGBoost and Transformer on the PKPD tasks while remaining fully interpretable, though RNN and Transformer achieve slightly lower MSE on the high-dimensional RNA Pol dataset (7.24e-3 vs. 0.0111). On LLM-SRBench symbolic recovery metrics, IGSR also leads with Term Recall rank 1.99 and Symbolic Accuracy rank 1.17, confirming that the influence-guided search recovers correct equation structure, not just predictive accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

IGSR extends the LLM-driven symbolic regression line (D3, ICSR, LLM-SR, LaSR) by replacing their coarse scalar-loss feedback with per-term influence scores (Δ_j), a concept adapted from classical influence functions (Cook & Weisberg, 1980) but applied to model structure rather than data points. The search strategy builds on MCTS/UCT (Kocsis & Szepesvári, 2006) to navigate the combinatorial equation space, departing from the linear iterative refinement used by most prior LLM-SR methods. Unlike traditional SR (GP, SINDy, PySR) that operate over predefined operator libraries, IGSR delegates basis-function generation to an LLM while retaining a deterministic, data-driven selection mechanism, effectively decoupling generative creativity from statistical validation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most compelling contribution is the demonstration of a complete discovery loop: IGSR generated a novel hypothesis (intragenic DNA methylation suppresses Pol II pausing) that was subsequently confirmed via targeted wet-lab experimentation (5-azacytidine treatment + eNET-seq), a rare validation path for AI-generated scientific hypotheses. Key limitations include the assumption of a linear-in-terms structure (f(x) = Σ w_j ψ_j(x)), which precludes fully nested nonlinear dynamics, and dependence on the LLM's generative priors for proposing candidate terms. Open questions include whether explicit parsimony penalties could better navigate the accuracy–interpretability Pareto frontier, and whether the influence-guided search generalizes to multi-output or stochastic dynamical systems beyond the current benchmarks.
