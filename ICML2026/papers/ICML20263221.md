## Problem

Existing red-teaming methods for Vision-Language Models treat the text-to-image generation process as a black box, returning only terminal toxicity scores without revealing when or how toxic semantics emerge during multi-step diffusion synthesis. This temporal opacity limits both diagnostic depth and attack effectiveness, as sparse global rewards provide little guidance for attributing toxicity to specific generation phases. The paper asks whether the denoising trajectory itself can be exploited as an attack surface and whether adversarial optimization imposes a predictable temporal structure on toxicity formation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt (sampled from RealToxicityPrompts or PolygloToxicityPrompts) and produces an adversarial image generated by a fine-tuned rectified-flow T2I model (Stable Diffusion 3.5-Medium, 20 training / 40 inference denoising steps) paired with an edited prompt. When this image-prompt pair is fed to a query-only black-box VLM (LLaVA-v1.6-mistral-7b), it elicits a toxic text continuation scored by Detoxify across six dimensions. A secondary output is a T×D temporal alignment heatmap (40 timesteps × 6 toxicity dimensions) quantifying per-step sensitivity via Multi-Level Monte Carlo estimation, which serves as a diagnostic artifact for phase-specific vulnerability analysis.

## Method

STARE is a hierarchical reinforcement learning framework that decomposes the attack into a high-level prompt editor and a low-level T2I fine-tuner, both optimized with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). The high-level policy uses a 6-layer encoder-decoder Transformer to generate K stochastic embedding-space edits (clipped to an ℓ₂-ball of radius 0.8) on the prompt embedding, decoded into text via vec2text, thereby instantiating toxic semantic subgoals. The low-level policy fine-tunes the rectified-flow velocity field via LoRA (rank 16) using a marginal-preserving stochastic SDE for exploration, with a terminal reward combining Detoxify toxicity and CLIPScore alignment. The key empirical finding is Optimization-Induced Phase Alignment: adversarial optimization concentrates conceptual harms (Identity, Threat) into early denoising steps (t≈1–15) and detail-oriented harms (Obscene, Insult) into late steps (t≈21–40), a structure validated by causal window-zeroing interventions that produce a double dissociation across toxicity categories. This temporal structure is absent in vanilla models and in flat RL baselines (DDPO), confirming it emerges from the synergy of hierarchical prompt editing and velocity-field fine-tuning.

## Results

On the RTP dataset with LLaVA as the target VLM, STARE (w_align=0.2) achieves a peak Attack Success Rate of 31.36% (Any dimension), a 68% improvement over the strongest Tier-2 baseline ART (18.62%). The cleanest controlled comparison against STARE w/ DDPO (identical white-box T2I budget, same RL framework, same backbone) shows a +3.52 pp gain in Any-ASR, isolating the contribution of the hierarchical temporal design. On the OOD PTP dataset, STARE reaches 30.83% Any-ASR, and transfers to Gemini (15.96%), Qwen (21.47%), and GPT-5.4 (13.64%) without retraining. Image-prompt alignment (CLIPScore) is maintained at 0.78, confirming that toxicity gains do not come at the cost of visual coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STARE outperforms all three Tier-2 baselines by a wide margin on the primary RTP/LLaVA benchmark: it exceeds ART (18.62% Any-ASR) by 12.7 pp, DiffZOO (17.20%) by 14.2 pp, and PGJ (14.86%) by 16.5 pp. The most informative comparison is against the Tier-1 controlled baseline STARE w/ DDPO (27.84%), which shares the same white-box T2I access, RL framework, and SD 3.5-Medium backbone; STARE's +3.52 pp gain isolates the value of the hierarchical prompt-editing + LoRA design over flat RL. On the OOD PTP dataset, STARE (30.83%) similarly dominates ART (22.01%), DiffZOO (21.70%), and PGJ (19.66%). Transferability is consistent: on Gemini, Qwen, and GPT-5.4, STARE retains the top position over all baselines (e.g., 15.96% vs. 12.81% for ART on Gemini; 21.47% vs. 13.27% on Qwen). Ablations confirm that removing either the prompt editor or the LoRA module degrades ASR by 5–9 pp, validating that both hierarchical components contribute independently.

## Relation to Prior Work

STARE extends the T2I red-teaming literature (PGJ, DiffZOO, ART) by moving beyond prompt-only optimization to directly fine-tune the denoiser's velocity field via hierarchical RL, building on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025; Xue et al., 2025) and the rectified-flow formulation of Stable Diffusion 3.5 (Esser et al., 2024). The high-level prompt editor repurposes vec2text (Morris et al., 2023) for adversarial subgoal generation, while the low-level policy exploits the marginal-preserving stochastic flow (MPS) sampling strategy from Flow-GRPO/DanceGRPO for RL exploration. The key conceptual departure from prior work is treating the denoising trajectory itself as the attack surface and diagnostic object, rather than a black-box oracle that returns only a terminal toxicity score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Optimization-Induced Phase Alignment finding—that adversarial optimization binds conceptual harms to early denoising steps and detail-oriented harms to late steps, with a clean double dissociation under causal window-zeroing—reframes diffusion-model safety from continuous monitoring to targeted, phase-specific intervention. This provides a concrete blueprint for phase-aware defense mechanisms, though the paper does not build or evaluate such a defense. Key limitations include the requirement for white-box T2I access (restricting real-world applicability), English-only evaluation, and causal-intervention evidence from a single VLM (LLaVA) and single T2I backbone (SD 3.5-Med); whether the temporal structure generalizes to other architectures, languages, or harm modalities remains open.
