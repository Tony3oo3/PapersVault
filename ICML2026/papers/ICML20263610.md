## Problem

Fine-tuning generative control policies (GCPs) such as diffusion- and flow-based policies via RL faces a fundamental tradeoff: off-policy methods (noise steering, residual learning, behavior cloning) are sample-efficient but cannot expressively reshape the full action distribution, while on-policy policy-gradient methods (DPPO) achieve strong final performance at the cost of ~7× more environment interactions. No existing method simultaneously achieves off-policy data reuse and full-policy expressive updates for GCPs, and prior approaches struggle with mixed data quality, high-precision tasks, and long horizons without extensive task-specific tuning.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes robot proprioceptive states and/or RGB images (plus language instructions for the LIBERO benchmark) and outputs an action chunk of length h executed in open-loop. Actions are generated through an iterative denoising chain of K steps (typically 4–10 for flow policies, 50–100 for diffusion), starting from Gaussian noise and refining to the final action. All baselines share the same BC-pretrained flow GCP checkpoint clipped to ≤50% success, with no access to the original offline dataset during online RL.

## Method

OGPO decouples the bi-level MDP of GCP optimization by truncating it at the end of each denoising trajectory: an off-policy ensemble of Q-functions is trained via TD-learning on expensive environment transitions, while policy extraction uses on-policy PPO applied purely within the cheap, computationally-generated denoising MDP, with Q-values serving as a terminal reward. The PPO importance-sampling ratio is an Annealed Importance Sampling product over all K denoising steps, and multiple denoising trajectories are sampled in parallel per state (GRPO-style) to estimate the value baseline without a separate critic. OGPO+ adds Best-of-N inference using the Q-function as a verifier at deployment, plus a BC regularization term on a success buffer to prevent the policy from over-exploiting imperfect Q-estimates. OGPO+CA further introduces conservative advantages that only update the policy when all ensemble members agree on the sign of the advantage, mitigating the offline-to-online performance dip.

## Results

OGPO+ achieves ~7× sample efficiency improvement over the on-policy DPPO baseline on RoboMimic tasks, reaching equivalent final success rates in a fraction of the environment steps. Across 11 tasks spanning Franka Kitchen, RoboMimic, and Adroit Hand, OGPO is the only method that converges with minimal hyperparameter tuning on mixed-data-quality, high-precision, partial-demonstration, and long-horizon settings simultaneously. Notably, OGPO+ can fine-tune a BC policy clipped to 50% success up to near-full task success using zero expert data in the online replay buffer. On dense-reward dexterous Adroit tasks, EXPO remains competitive, but OGPO still matches or exceeds it with fewer task-specific hyperparameter adjustments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPPO (on-policy bi-level PPO), OGPO+ reaches the same final success rates in roughly 1/7th the environment steps on RoboMimic Square and ToolHang, confirming the benefit of off-policy critic reuse. Against QC (Best-of-N + behavior cloning extraction), OGPO consistently achieves higher asymptotic success and better sample efficiency; QC plateaus because supervised fine-tuning cannot expand the GCP's action-distribution support needed for exploration. Against DSRL (noise steering), OGPO wins decisively on high-precision and long-horizon tasks where steering's limited expressivity (only modifying initial noise) is insufficient; DSRL also exhibits training instability on flow-based policies. Against EXPO (residual correction), OGPO outperforms on Kitchen and most RoboMimic tasks where the base policy is weak, while EXPO remains competitive on dense-reward Adroit tasks where only minor corrections are needed. The combined Steering+Residual (S/R) baseline improves over either alone on Square but still trails OGPO and shows instability on ToolHang.

## Relation to Prior Work

OGPO builds on the bi-level MDP formulation introduced by DPPO (Ren et al., 2024) and Black et al. (2023), which embeds the GCP denoising chain as an inner MDP within the environment dynamics. The key departure is decoupling this bi-level structure: OGPO performs off-policy TD-learning for the environment MDP (expensive samples) while applying on-policy PPO only to the denoising MDP (cheap, purely computational samples), using the learned Q-function as a terminal reward. This contrasts with DPPO/ReinFlow (on-policy through the full bi-level MDP), DSRL/EXPO (off-policy critics but partial finetuning via steering or residuals), and QC (off-policy critics with BC-based policy extraction). The ODE-to-SDE noise correction from Albergo et al. (2023) and the multi-trajectory sampling inspired by GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) are adapted to the GCP setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OGPO+ is the first method to fine-tune poorly-initialized BC policies to near full task success with zero expert data in the online replay buffer, pointing toward deployment scenarios where task-specific human data is unavailable. The decoupled optimization framework cleanly separates sample-efficient critic learning from expressive policy updates, and the empirical study (across 11+ tasks spanning precision, horizon, and dexterity) provides a systematic map of when full-policy finetuning succeeds versus when steering/residual methods suffice. A key limitation is that parallel denoising rollouts for Q-value estimation become prohibitively expensive for large VLA models, and the method's reliance on an accurate Q-function as terminal reward means performance is bounded by critic quality in contact-rich or high-dimensional settings.
