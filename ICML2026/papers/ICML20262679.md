## Problem

Existing illumination-editing methods cannot simultaneously provide customized, image-guided lighting control and preserve content integrity. Visual-guided approaches (background replacement, HDR compositing) compromise the original image content, while text-guided methods lack the precision to specify geometric-structured light effects such as Tyndall beams, lens flares, or iridescent halos. The paper tackles the specific challenge of transferring visually perceptible, geometrically structured light effects from a reference image to a target portrait while keeping the target's content unchanged.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262679/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target RGB image, a reference RGB image containing prominent light effects, and optional 2D control parameters (horizontal/vertical translation, flip/rotation, multiplicative intensity coefficient) as input. At inference, the reference image is first processed by a dedicated light-extraction diffusion model to produce a standalone light-effect image on a pure black background; this extracted image is then spatially transformed per user parameters before being fed as conditioning. The output is a single edited RGB image where the target's content and background are preserved while the reference's light effect is naturally integrated.

## Method

TransLight introduces Generative Decoupling: two diffusion models (light removal and light extraction) fine-tuned from IC-Light's U-Net weights, trained on synthesized composites (I_S = aI + bL) and relighted images to learn separating content from geometric-structured light effects without content leakage. Over one million image-content-light triplets are constructed via a VLM-guided selection, decoupling, and DINOv2-similarity filtering pipeline. TransLight itself is trained in two stages on the SD1.5 version of IC-Light: Stage 1 applies LoRA fine-tuning (rank 128, 10K iterations) to teach the frozen backbone to preserve background content when given a content-plus-background-light composite; Stage 2 trains a ControlNet (40K iterations) that takes the extracted light-effect image as its conditioning input while the U-Net receives only the light-removed content. At inference, the light-extraction model isolates the effect from the reference, the user applies 2D transforms, the background portion of the light is additively composited onto the content, and the combined latent is denoised through the LoRA+ControlNet-augmented generator.

## Results

On the primary Light-FID metric (distributional distance between transferred and real light-effect images over 12,000 held-out triplets), TransLight achieves 6.02, substantially outperforming IC-Light's background-conditioned model (10.05) and even the unfiltered-data baseline (6.49). On per-image similarity (1,000 held-out triplets), TransLight scores PSNR 19.58, SSIM 0.7931, and LPIPS 0.1982 versus IC-Light's 15.34, 0.6784, and 0.2756. In a 15-participant user study, TransLight attains 84% light-effect consistency and 73.8% content consistency, far exceeding the next-best method (Nano Banana at 39.5% and 61.3%). On the MIIW indoor relighting benchmark (zero-shot), TransLight reports RMSE 0.212 and SSIM 0.627, competitive with but slightly below the best indoor-specific methods (LumiNet: 0.180/0.647; DIR: SSIM 0.797).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IC-Light (Zhang et al., 2025), the primary open-source baseline, TransLight wins decisively on every metric: Light-FID 6.02 vs 10.05, PSNR 19.58 vs 15.34, and user-study light consistency 84% vs 8.4% (bg) / 4.0% (prompt). Among closed-source editors, Nano Banana (Google DeepMind) is the closest competitor in the user study (39.5% light, 61.3% content) but still trails TransLight by ~45 and ~12 percentage points respectively; Seedream 4.0 (32.4%/31.1%) and SeedEdit 3.0 (qualitative only, fails to capture light type/direction) perform worse. GPT-Image-1 (OpenAI) is noted qualitatively to alter facial identity, making it unsuitable for portrait relighting. On the MIIW indoor benchmark, TransLight (RMSE 0.212, SSIM 0.627) is competitive with but does not surpass LumiNet (0.180/0.647) or DIR (SSIM 0.797), which are purpose-built for indoor state transfer; however, the paper argues these methods solve a fundamentally different task (illumination state change vs. geometric light-effect transfer) and cannot replicate TransLight's ability to add structured effects like Tyndall beams or lens flares to portraits.

## Relation to Prior Work

TransLight builds directly on IC-Light (Zhang et al., 2025) as its frozen generative backbone and on ControlNet (Zhang et al., 2023a) as the conditioning mechanism for injecting extracted light effects, but departs from both the style-transfer and portrait-relighting literatures in a key way: rather than using text prompts, HDR maps, or background images as illumination conditions, it treats the reference image's light effect as a standalone, decoupled visual signal. The generative decoupling strategy—fine-tuning two diffusion models (light removal and light extraction) to separate content from geometric-structured lighting—repurposes the diffusion denoising paradigm for an inverse problem (content-light disentanglement) that prior style-transfer methods (InstantStyle, FreeTuner, Swapping self-attention) addressed only for abstract stylistic attributes, not for spatially structured light phenomena like Tyndall beams or lens flares.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and previously unformulated need in portrait photography: transferring a specific, visually identifiable light effect from a reference image while preserving the target's content, with user control over position, direction, and intensity. The 1M+ image-content-light triplet dataset and the generative decoupling pipeline provide reusable infrastructure for future illumination-editing research. Key limitations include dependence on the light extraction model's quality (subtle or ambiguous light effects degrade performance) and the method's focus on geometric-structured effects, leaving open whether the framework generalizes to diffuse, non-geometric illumination changes. The large margin over IC-Light (Light-FID 6.02 vs. 10.05; user-study light consistency 84% vs. 8.4%) suggests the decoupling approach is substantially more effective than background-conditioned relighting for this specific task.
