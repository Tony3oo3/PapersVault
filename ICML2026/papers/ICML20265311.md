## Problem

Existing learning-based whole-body controllers for humanoid robots are embodiment-specific, requiring costly retraining for each new platform due to differences in degrees of freedom, joint ordering, and physical properties. Unlike quadrupeds, humanoids exhibit substantial morphological heterogeneity that breaks the shared-dynamics and aligned-state assumptions underlying prior cross-embodiment work. The paper asks whether a single policy can achieve robust zero-shot whole-body control across a wide range of humanoid designs with one-time training.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a five-step history of proprioception (base angular velocity, gravity direction, joint positions and velocities, previous action) projected into a fixed 32-dimensional canonical joint space via semantic alignment, a whole-body command vector (target velocity, base height, pelvis angle, waist rotations, gait phase parameters), and a binary joint-controllability indicator. The output is a per-joint position command vector in the same 32-dim canonical space, which is inverse-mapped to the robot's physical joints (12–32 active DoF depending on embodiment). A learned state estimator reconstructs privileged quantities (base linear velocity, base height) from the encoded features to close the sim-to-real observability gap.

## Method

XHugWBC combines three key components. First, physics-consistent morphological randomization parameterizes each rigid body's inertia via the Cholesky factor of its pseudo-inertia matrix, then perturbs it through a 10-dimensional interpretable vector (affine deformation + density scaling) that guarantees positive-definiteness and thus physical feasibility; joint randomization covers hip-axis permutation, position offsets, and actuation type (revolute vs. fixed), yielding embodiments from 12 to 32 active DoF. Second, a universal representation maps all robots into a canonical 32-joint space and constructs a kinematic-tree adjacency matrix encoding each embodiment's topology. Third, the policy uses a Transformer encoder with hybrid-mask attention (first layer masked by the adjacency matrix to respect local kinematic structure, subsequent layers unmasked for global coordination) or a GCN encoder, followed by a per-node action decoder that fuses node features with global context and estimated privileged state. Training is standard PPO over a family of randomized embodiments, with the critic receiving additional privileged observations (torso velocities, collision pairs, morphological parameters) for stable value estimation.

## Results

Across 12 unseen simulated humanoids, the XHugWBC generalist policy achieves 100% survival rate and reaches approximately 85% of the specialist (HugWBC) tracking performance zero-shot, with average forward velocity tracking error of 0.084 m/s vs. 0.060 m/s for specialists. Fine-tuning the generalist on a single robot yields ~10% improvement in return over the specialist trained from scratch, with substantially faster convergence. In real-world deployment, the policy transfers zero-shot to 7 diverse humanoid platforms (Booster T1, Fourier N1, Unitree G1 23/29 DoF, Agibot X2, Dobot Atom, Unitree H1-2) with 100% task success rate, though sim-to-real tracking error gaps range from 27% (Unitree G1 23 DoF) to 108% (Dobot Atom).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against per-robot specialist controllers (HugWBC, Xue et al. 2025), the XHugWBC generalist achieves 100% survival on all 12 robots but with ~30–40% higher tracking errors on velocity commands (e.g., 0.084 vs. 0.060 m/s for E_vx); however, fine-tuning the generalist on a single robot surpasses the specialist by up to 10% in return and converges significantly faster. Against MetaMorph (Gupta et al. 2022), which explicitly feeds link inertial properties and joint ranges as input, XHugWBC substantially outperforms on zero-shot survival: MetaMorph struggles to learn generalizable representations from the high-dimensional, heterogeneous morphological descriptors of humanoids. MorAL (Luo et al. 2024) performs worst among all baselines, with a substantial survival drop on most robots due to its simple policy architecture and lack of informative morphological features in the proprioceptive input. Naive Random (Feng et al. 2023; Ai et al. 2025), which approximates rigid bodies as box primitives, generalizes only to a small subset of embodiments with similar mass distributions and kinematic features, exhibiting significant degradation on the remaining robots. In fine-tuning comparisons, both MetaMorph and MorAL benefit from improved initialization but converge slower than Generalist-FT and peak at performance comparable to (but consistently below) the fine-tuned generalist.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends cross-embodiment learning from quadrupeds (MetaMorph, MorAL, GenLoco) to the substantially more complex humanoid setting, where morphological diversity in DoF count, joint ordering, and whole-body dynamics breaks the aligned state-action assumptions of prior work. It builds directly on HugWBC's unified command space for expressive whole-body behaviors and H-Zero's hardware-agnostic joint mapping, while introducing a key technical departure: physics-consistent morphological randomization via Cholesky-level parameterization of the pseudo-inertia matrix, replacing the naive box-primitive scaling used in prior quadruped transfer work. The hybrid-mask Transformer encoder draws on Body Transformer's topology-aware attention, adapted here to a kinematic tree graph over a canonical 32-joint space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Demonstrating zero-shot whole-body control across seven physically distinct real-world humanoids (12–66 kg, 12–32 DoF) with 100% survival is a practical milestone for scalable humanoid deployment, reducing the per-robot training cost that has limited the field. The physics-consistent randomization via pseudo-inertia Cholesky factorization provides a principled, smooth parameterization that avoids the non-convex feasibility constraints of direct inertial perturbation—a technique likely reusable beyond this specific task. A key limitation is the reliance on a unified command interface, which precludes morphology-aware motion tracking; extending cross-embodiment learning to expressive, embodiment-specific motion representations remains an open direction.
