## Problem

Federated LoRA fine-tuning of LLMs forces all clients to use a uniform LoRA rank, creating a mismatch with heterogeneous client resources: constrained clients cannot afford high-rank computation, while powerful clients are underutilized. Existing heterogeneous LoRA methods (HeteroLoRA, FlexLoRA, FLoRA) either lack convergence guarantees or introduce extra overhead such as truncated SVD or model merging, leaving a gap for an efficient, theoretically-grounded solution that adapts LoRA rank per client.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen base LLM, per-client local text data, and a per-client sketching ratio k_i/r (e.g., 0.125–1.0) that determines how many of the r global LoRA columns/rows each client updates. The server maintains full-rank global LoRA modules B∈R^{m×r} and A∈R^{r×n}; clients only compute and upload the k_i non-zero columns of ΔB and k_i non-zero rows of ΔA, so the uplink is (k_i/r)·q rather than q. The output is the updated global LoRA pair [B; A] on the server, which can be applied to the frozen base model for inference.

## Method

FSLoRA introduces a Random-k diagonal sketching matrix S_i (with k_i non-zero diagonal entries scaled by r/k_i) into the LoRA loss, so that B·S_i·A activates only k_i columns of B and k_i rows of A at client i. Because E[S_i]=I_r, the sketched product is an unbiased estimate of BA, and the resulting gradients are structurally sparse, reducing both local computation and uplink communication proportionally to k_i/r. The server samples per-client sketches each round, broadcasts the global LoRA modules, collects sparse submatrix updates, zero-pads them to full size, and aggregates via FedAvg-style averaging. A convergence theorem shows the sketching ratios enter the rate through scaled smoothness constants (r/k_i)·L and (r²/k_i²)·L, and the bound recovers the standard FedAvg rate as k_i/r→1, establishing convergence under arbitrary heterogeneous sketching ratios.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa (125M), FSLoRA achieves 83.3% average accuracy, outperforming the best baseline HeteroLoRA (79.6%) by 3.7 points while using comparable GPU hours (10.9h vs. 10.7h). On the more challenging commonsense reasoning benchmark with LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct, FSLoRA reaches 79.4% average accuracy versus 77.4% for FlexLoRA and 74.6% for HeteroLoRA/FLoRA, again with the lowest GPU hours (44.3h) among competitive methods. FSLoRA also scales to LLaMA-7B, achieving 33.93% average MMLU score across Wizard, Dolly-15k, and Alpaca datasets, surpassing FlexLoRA (31.42%) and FLoRA (28.65%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSLoRA consistently outperforms all five baselines across both main benchmarks. On GLUE (RoBERTa), FSLoRA leads HeteroLoRA by 3.7 points (83.3 vs. 79.6), FlexLoRA and FLoRA by 4.4 points (83.3 vs. 78.9), and RAVAN by 6.7 points (83.3 vs. 76.6). On commonsense reasoning (LLaMA-3.2-3B), FSLoRA beats FlexLoRA (the strongest baseline) by 2.0 points (79.4 vs. 77.4), HeteroLoRA and FLoRA by 4.8 points, and RAVAN by 4.7 points. In terms of efficiency, FSLoRA uses fewer GPU hours than FlexLoRA (44.3h vs. 68.3h) and FLoRA (44.3h vs. 49.8h) while matching HeteroLoRA's cost (44.3h vs. 43.7h). In the homogeneous-rank comparison with FFA-LoRA (Table 14), FSLoRA achieves 77.64% average versus FFA-LoRA's 69.92%, confirming that freezing one LoRA factor limits expressiveness. Under heavier heterogeneity (50–100 clients, heavy-tail rank distributions), FSLoRA's advantage persists, with the smallest performance drop when shifting from normal to heavy-tail client capability distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

FSLoRA extends the federated LoRA framework (Zhang et al., 2024) by introducing a random-k sketching mechanism that allows each client to update only a subset of columns/rows of the global LoRA modules, thereby adapting to heterogeneous client resources. It departs from prior heterogeneous LoRA methods—HeteroLoRA (zero-padding/truncation), FlexLoRA (SVD-based aggregation), and FLoRA (model merging)—by avoiding their respective theoretical gaps, extra computational overhead, or loss of LoRA flexibility. The sketching idea itself is borrowed from distributed optimization literature (Sarlos, 2006; Gower & Richtárik, 2015; Shrivastava et al., 2024), but its application to structured low-rank adaptation modules in a federated setting is new, and the paper provides the first convergence analysis characterizing how sketching ratios scale the smoothness constants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FSLoRA fills a notable gap by providing the first theoretically-grounded, low-overhead solution for heterogeneous federated LoRA fine-tuning, with a convergence guarantee that recovers FedAvg in the limiting case. Practically, it enables resource-constrained clients to participate in collaborative LLM fine-tuning while benefiting from higher global ranks, which is important for on-device and edge deployment scenarios. Key limitations include the focus on synchronous full/partial participation (no stragglers, async, or network instability), the reliance on random-k sketching rather than adaptive importance-based selection (which remains an open problem due to the lack of a principled importance metric for LoRA components), and the fact that the convergence analysis, while rigorous, involves constants that may be loose in practice. Future work on adaptive sketching, asynchronous settings, and integration with differential privacy could extend the framework's applicability.
