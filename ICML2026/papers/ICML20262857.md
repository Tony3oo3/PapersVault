## Problem

Existing Multimodal Graph Foundation Models (MGFMs) such as UniGraph2 fail to explicitly model modality interaction, missing intricate cross-modal semantics beyond simple aggregation, and exhibit sub-optimal modality alignment that inadequately bridges the significant semantic disparity between distinct modal spaces. The paper identifies these as two fundamental, previously unaddressed limitations through systematic empirical ablation studies on the MGFM architecture.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Multimodal Attributed Graph where each node carries text and image features encoded by frozen encoders (Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct by default, or BERT/ViT as alternatives) into fixed-dimension vectors (3,584-d), along with graph edges. During pre-training, modality masking is applied to the encoded features. The output is a unified node embedding formed by concatenating modality-specific and cross-modal representations, which supports downstream tasks including node classification, link prediction, few-shot learning, graph-to-text generation, and graph-to-image generation.

## Method

PLANET employs a Divide-and-Conquer strategy that decouples modality interaction and alignment at two distinct granularities. At the embedding level, the EDG module uses a Mixture-of-Experts mechanism to extract diverse cross-modal semantic patterns from neighboring nodes' complementary modalities, followed by a topology-aware Graph Transformer attention that queries these expert-distilled signals, enabling each node to dynamically absorb cross-modal context during layer-wise propagation. At the node level, the NDR module projects final embeddings into a Discretized Semantic Representation Space (DSRS) of 20,480 learnable tokens via nearest-neighbor quantization, then enforces alignment through a text-anchored General Knowledge Loss (symmetric InfoNCE) and a VQ-VAE commitment loss. Training is self-supervised, combining dual feature reconstruction (self-modal and cross-modal), structural link reconstruction, the general knowledge loss, VQ loss, and MoE load balancing. Theoretical analysis via Information Bottleneck proves EDG preserves synergistic features discarded by vanilla encoders, and via Wasserstein distance shows NDR accelerates alignment convergence from O(N^{-1/d}) to O(C/√N).

## Results

PLANET achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six node classification datasets, with the largest gains over the strongest MGFM baseline UniGraph2 on Movies (+5.92% Acc, 57.06 vs 53.02) and RedditS (+3.17% Acc, 96.62 vs 93.65). On the large-scale Goodreads-NC dataset, PLANET reaches 84.16% accuracy and 74.43% F1-Macro, outperforming UniGraph2 by 5.10 and 5.69 points respectively. In few-shot node classification (Table 3), PLANET achieves relative improvements of 15.65%–22.35% over UniGraph2, e.g., 81.93% vs 66.27% on Grocery-5way-10-shot. For link prediction (Table 2), PLANET leads on Goodreads-LP with 27.62 MRR vs 19.44 for UniGraph2 (+10.87%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLANET consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and task. Against the closest MGFM competitor UniGraph2, PLANET wins on all six node classification datasets (by 0.26%–5.92% in accuracy) and on all link prediction benchmarks (by 1.55%–10.87% in MRR), with the most dramatic gap on Goodreads-LP (27.62 vs 19.44 MRR). Among GFM baselines, RiemannGFM and GFT are the strongest on Ele-fashion (87.07 and 87.14 Acc respectively), but PLANET still edges ahead at 87.37. Traditional multimodal GNNs (MMGCN, MGAT) and self-supervised methods (GRACE, GraphMAE2) trail PLANET by 3–10+ points on most datasets, confirming that the divide-and-conquer modality interaction and alignment design provides substantial gains over simple aggregation or contrastive pre-training. In few-shot settings, PLANET's advantage over UniGraph2 widens considerably (e.g., 81.93 vs 66.27 on Grocery-5way-10-shot), suggesting the learned representations transfer more effectively under data scarcity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Multimodal Graph Foundation Model (MGFM) paradigm established by UniGraph2 (He et al., WWW 2025), which it identifies as lacking explicit modality interaction and effective modality alignment. It repurposes the Graph Transformer (Dwivedi & Bresson, 2020) as a topology-aware attention backbone within a Mixture-of-Experts module (EDG) for embedding-level cross-modal enrichment, and adapts VQ-VAE (Van Den Oord et al., NeurIPS 2017) into a Discretized Semantic Representation Space (NDR) for node-level alignment. The modality masking strategy follows GraphMAE (Hou et al., KDD 2022), and the overall pre-training objective combines self-supervised reconstruction with contrastive alignment, positioning PLANET as a methodological refinement rather than a new architectural family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formally decomposing MGFM design into two distinct sub-problems (interaction at embedding granularity, alignment at node granularity) and providing theoretical guarantees for each (Information Bottleneck synergy preservation, Wasserstein convergence acceleration), this paper offers a principled design template for future multimodal graph models. The large few-shot gains (up to 22% relative) and strong OOD transfer suggest the divide-and-conquer paradigm yields more transferable representations than monolithic fusion. Open questions include scalability to additional modalities (audio, video), whether the DSRS size and discretization granularity can be adapted per-domain, and how the approach behaves when modalities are partially missing rather than fully present.
