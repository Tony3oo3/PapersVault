## Problem

Existing incremental learning methods for open-ended image-to-text generation assume disjoint image categories or background scenes when splitting tasks, which fails to capture realistic distribution shifts where dominant visual topics overlap and evolve over time. Moreover, prior approaches rely on full-scale fine-tuning of fusion and language components with stored raw exemplars, introducing privacy concerns, inefficiency for large pre-trained models, and no explicit handling of semantic overlap across tasks. This paper introduces the notion of continual alignment—incrementally adapting only the cross-modal alignment module in pre-trained VLMs without exemplars—to address these gaps.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at 364×364 or 490×490 resolution along with a text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of' for captioning, a question for VQA, or OCR tokens for TextCaps/TextVQA). The frozen ViT-g/14 visual encoder produces patch embeddings that interact with learnable query tokens in the Q-Former alignment module, and the frozen OPT-2.7B LLM generates the output sentence or short answer. Notably, no task identifier is provided at inference time; the model must handle all topics from the sequential task stream with a single forward pass.

## Method

ECA operates exclusively at the alignment module (Q-Former) of BLIP-2 with frozen visual encoder and LLM, using only ~12M trainable parameters. Three mechanisms work together: (1) Mixture of Query (MoQ) learns per-task query tokens and keys, then uses per-sample attention over the average image embedding to dynamically compose them with the pre-trained query tokens, enforced by an orthogonality loss to prevent cross-task interference; (2) Fisher Dynamic Expansion (FeDEx) computes a normalized FIM-based conflict metric S(ω_t) via second-order Taylor expansion and selectively expands a new parallel adapter only when S(ω_t) > 0.5, freezing prior adapters and averaging their outputs; (3) Dictionary Replay (DR) maintains an over-complete embedding dictionary (m = 5×d_v atoms) updated via sparse Lasso coding (solved with FISTA) after each task, then replays it during subsequent training through a knowledge-distillation loss that matches the Q-Former's output on dictionary atoms between the frozen teacher and current student. The total training objective combines cross-entropy, MoQ losses (orthogonality + key-alignment), and the DR distillation loss weighted by λ=0.1.

## Results

On ToS-COCO Caption, ECA achieves 43.42 BLEU-4, 125.56 CIDEr, and 23.80 SPICE (Avg), with only 12.29M trainable parameters—matching the single-PA baseline budget while closing 38–50% of the gap to the joint-training upper bound. On ToS-VQAv2, ECA reaches 68.05 VQA accuracy, just 0.13 below the upper bound (68.18). On the harder ToS-TextCaps and ToS-TextVQA benchmarks (outside BLIP-2's pre-training), ECA attains 30.05 BLEU-4 and 38.13 VQA accuracy respectively, improving over the best uni-modal exemplar-free baseline (LwF) by 2.16 and 5.21 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECA outperforms all six baselines on Avg, BWT, and FWT across all four benchmarks while using the same 12.29M trainable parameters as single-PA methods. On ToS-VQAv2, ECA (68.05) surpasses CODA-Prompt (65.64) by 2.41 and LwF (64.92) by 3.13 in VQA accuracy, and dramatically outperforms EWC (59.63) and MoE-LoRA (61.02). On the harder ToS-TextCaps/TextVQA (outside pre-training), ECA leads LwF—the strongest uni-modal exemplar-free baseline—by 2.16 BLEU-4 and 5.21 VQA accuracy, while prompt-pool methods (CODA-Prompt, Dual-Prompt) and MoE-LoRA (98.84M params) trail by 5+ points. Notably, EWC underperforms even Vanilla (PA) on TextCaps/TextVQA because its disjoint-task assumption conflicts with the semantic overlap in the main-topic setting. ECA also achieves the best BWT (−0.64 BLEU-4 on COCO; +1.81 VQA on VQAv2) and FWT (7.39 BLEU-4; 16.38 VQA), indicating it both retains prior knowledge and transfers positively to new tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of exemplar-free incremental learning for open-ended image-to-text generation (RATT, VQA-CL, Symbolic Replay) by shifting the focus from full-model fine-tuning with exemplar buffers to targeted adaptation of the alignment module (Q-Former or projector layers) in pre-trained VLMs. It departs from prior IL benchmarks that assume disjoint object categories or background scenes by introducing a 'main topic' split that preserves realistic semantic overlap across tasks. The method draws on parameter-efficient fine-tuning (Parallel Adapters), Fisher Information Matrix-based regularization (EWC lineage), sparse dictionary learning, and attention-based query composition, but combines them into a unified framework specifically designed to preserve cross-modal alignment without raw data access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The four new ToS benchmarks (ToS-COCO Caption, ToS-VQAv2, ToS-TextCaps, ToS-TextVQA) fill a gap by providing IL evaluation settings that reflect real-world distribution shifts with overlapping semantics, and are likely to become standard references for multimodal continual learning. The FIM-based expansion criterion (S(ω_t) threshold) offers a theoretically grounded, data-driven decision rule for when to allocate new capacity, which could inform broader PEFT-continual-learning design. Key open questions include whether the fixed-size embedding dictionary scales to very long task sequences, whether the approach transfers to weaker or less pre-trained backbones, and how it behaves under truly non-stationary (non-sequential) task arrivals.
