## Problem

Controlling the likelihood-diversity trade-off (temperature sampling) at inference for pre-trained diffusion and flow matching models remains an open challenge. Existing approaches either require fine-tuning or distillation, incur significant additional inference-time computation (e.g., MCMC corrector steps), or lack a probabilistic interpretation as true temperature scaling (e.g., classifier-free guidance, constant noise scaling). The paper asks whether one can analytically relate the learned score functions of noisy data distributions to those of a temperature-scaled distribution, enabling a training-free, zero-overhead rescaling mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained score model (denoising diffusion or flow matching) and two user-defined hyperparameters: k (variance scaling factor, where k>1 sharpens and k<1 flattens the distribution) and σ (controls how early in the sampling process rescaling takes effect). It produces samples from a locally temperature-scaled distribution without altering the global mode structure. The rescaling is applied as a simple multiplicative factor on the model's score prediction at each denoising step, making it compatible with any parameterization (noise ε, velocity v, x₀-prediction) and any sampler (DDPM, DDIM, Euler-ODE, Euler-SDE) with zero additional score evaluations.

## Method

The key insight is that for a single isotropic Gaussian data distribution, the score of the locally temperature-scaled noisy distribution is a time-dependent scalar multiple of the original score, with rescaling factor r_t(k,σ) = (η_t σ² + 1)/(η_t σ²/k + 1), where η_t = α_t²/σ_t² is the signal-to-noise ratio. This relationship extends as a bounded approximation to mixtures of well-separated Gaussians, with the error vanishing as mode separation increases. In practice, TSR simply multiplies the model's noise prediction (for diffusion) or applies a linear transformation to the velocity prediction (for flow matching) by r_t at each sampling step, requiring no architectural changes, no additional training, and no extra function evaluations. The approach is provably exact for single Gaussians, empirically validated on toy 2D distributions, and shown to preserve multimodal structure (unlike CNS or CFG) while reducing local variance around each mode.

## Results

On text-to-image generation (Stable Diffusion 3), TSR reduces FID from 24.77 to 22.81 and increases CLIP score from 32.82 to 33.05, improving beyond the CFG Pareto frontier. For depth estimation (Marigold on ETH3D), TSR lowers AbsRel from 7.1 (DDIM) to 6.68 and raises δ₁ from 90.4% to 95.7%. On pose prediction (SYMSOL), TSR reduces mean angular error from 0.444° to 0.356° and improves Acc@0.5° from 68.3% to 84.0%. For robotic manipulation (Pi-0 on LIBERO-10), TSR raises average success rate from 81.7% to 82.8% across 10 tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSR outperforms Constant Noise Scaling (CNS) on depth estimation (AbsRel 6.68 vs 6.82 on ETH3D), image generation (FID 19.57 vs 19.87 on SD2 with DDPM), and toy distributions where CNS exhibits mode collapse while TSR preserves all modes. On pose prediction, CNS achieves a marginally lower error (0.350° vs 0.356°) but requires a much larger k (1600 vs 7.0) and is incompatible with deterministic samplers and flow matching models. TSR is orthogonal to Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), improving FID and CLIP beyond the CFG Pareto frontier on SD3. Compared to DDIM and DDPM baselines, TSR consistently improves depth estimation accuracy and image generation quality without additional training. Against MCMC correctors (Du et al.) and Constant Score Scaling (Skreta et al.), TSR preserves uniform mode weights on the 2D checkerboard while MCMC still biases toward central modes and CSS drops peripheral modes. On robotic manipulation, TSR improves 6 of 10 LIBERO tasks over the Pi-0 baseline, with the two regressions occurring on tasks where the base policy already has low success rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the stochastic interpolant framework (Albergo et al., 2023) that unifies denoising diffusion and flow matching, and derives a time-dependent score rescaling factor by analytically relating the score of a variance-scaled Gaussian to the original score. It departs from prior temperature-sampling approaches in two key ways: unlike likelihood-weighted finetuning (Shih et al., 2023) or MCMC correctors (Song et al., 2021b; Du et al., 2023), TSR requires no additional training or inference-time computation; and unlike Constant Noise Scaling (CNS), which the paper proves is only a 'pseudo-temperature' method that drops modes, TSR uses a time-dependent schedule that preserves the global multimodal structure while scaling local variance. The velocity-to-score conversion from Ma et al. (2024) enables direct application to flow matching models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TSR offers a genuinely plug-and-play, zero-overhead mechanism to control sampling diversity for any pre-trained diffusion or flow model, which is practically significant given the ubiquity of such models across image generation, scientific discovery, and robotics. The paper's proof that CNS (the de facto standard for variance control in protein generation and other domains) does not correspond to true temperature scaling and provably drops modes is a useful correction to a widely-used heuristic. Key limitations include that TSR only performs 'local' temperature scaling (preserving mode weights, only altering local variance), and theoretical guarantees are restricted to well-separated isotropic Gaussians, leaving open whether better rescaling schedules exist for other distribution families. The modest but consistent gains across five disparate tasks suggest the technique will be broadly adopted, though per-task hyperparameter tuning (k, σ) remains necessary.
