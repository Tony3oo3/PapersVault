## Problem

Evaluating long-context language models on natural language conflates architectural capacity to capture long-range dependencies with semantic knowledge and vocabulary statistics, making it impossible to diagnose whether failures stem from fundamental structural limits or insufficient domain training. This ambiguity prevents practitioners from testing whether efficient architectures (SSMs, sparse attention) will maintain long-context capability before committing to expensive real-data training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260484/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes continuous-valued hierarchical Gaussian sequences (not tokenized text) of length L = n^D, with models adapted via continuous input adapters and Gaussian output heads predicting (μ̂_t, σ̂_t) instead of discrete token logits. Evaluation compares a model trained on context+target pairs against one trained on target alone, producing a utilization score U(X;Y) that can be negative (indicating conditioning worsens prediction), a KL residual Δ_KL quantifying architectural mismatch, and a maximum utilized context length defined as the longest context achieving relative residual ρ < 1%. Ground-truth conditionals p(w_t | w_{1:t-1}) are available in closed form as Gaussians, enabling exact conditional KL divergence rather than perplexity-based proxies.

## Method

L-CUBE generates sequences through a multi-scale hierarchical process inspired by MERA tensor networks: at each of D scales, m channels are reused from the coarser scale while n−m fresh Gaussian innovations are injected, mixed by a block correlator M^(d) ∈ R^{n×n}, and flattened to create branching structure. The reuse parameter m directly controls bipartite mutual information scaling—m=1 yields constant I^BP, 1<m<n yields sub-volume power-law L^{log m/log n}, and m=n yields volume-law—with the default n=4, m=3 configuration matching natural language's β≈0.79 exponent. The utilization metric U(X;Y) = H(p_Y, q_y) − H(p_{Y|X}, q_{xy}) decomposes as G(X;Y) − Δ_KL, where G is the true mutual information and Δ_KL is the KL residual from model mismatch. An optional local correlation operation smooths block-boundary discontinuities, and the entire construction is linear-Gaussian, yielding O(L) generation and conditional computation for m<n. Experiments train transformers, SSMs, LLA, and windowed attention on unbounded streams of fresh L-CUBE samples, isolating architectural effects from token-budget confounds.

## Results

On L-CUBE (sub-volume configuration, β≈0.79), full-attention transformers (GPT-2, GPT-NeoX, Qwen3) track the ground-truth bipartite mutual information I^BP across all tested sequence lengths (256–16,384), while state space models (Mamba, Mamba2, RWKV-7) peak and decline as length increases. A 124M GPT-2 achieves maximum utilized context length of 12,288 (ρ<1%), whereas a 1.4B Mamba peaks around 3,072, a ~4× gap in parameter efficiency. When plotted against theoretical FLOPs rather than parameters, all architectural families exhibit similar slopes in utilized context length vs. compute, suggesting a potential universal compute-scaling law. In the pretraining augmentation experiment, Pythia-410M trained with 1% L-CUBE synthetic data achieves lower loss at 4096-token evaluation than the same model trained without augmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Full-attention transformers (GPT-2, GPT-NeoX, Qwen3) consistently outperform all other architectures, tracking ground-truth I^BP across all tested lengths up to 16,384 tokens, confirming L²M theory's prediction of sufficient history state capacity. Among SSMs, RWKV-7 (163M) maintains the best utilization, followed by Mamba2 (130M) and Mamba (129M), all of which peak and decline as sequence length grows—exhibiting the fixed-capacity failure mode predicted by L²M theory. Log-linear attention (LLA) outperforms SSMs due to its logarithmic history state growth but still fails to match ground truth at longer lengths, confirming that logarithmic scaling is insufficient for sub-volume power-law I^BP growth. Mistral with sliding-window attention tracks ground truth within its context window but exhibits sharp degradation once sequence length exceeds the window size. In the pretraining augmentation setting, Pythia-410M with L-CUBE synthetic data achieves lower 4096-token loss than the same model trained on PG19 alone with 1% long real data, demonstrating that L-CUBE can serve as a data augmentation tool when natural long-context data is scarce.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the L²M capacity theory (Chen et al., 2025), which proves a necessary condition linking bipartite mutual information growth to required history-state dimension, by constructing a synthetic benchmark where that information scaling is exactly controllable. The hierarchical generator draws structural inspiration from MERA tensor networks (Vidal, 2007; 2008), adapting the multi-scale renormalization idea to produce Gaussian sequences with tunable long-range dependence. Unlike existing long-context benchmarks (SCROLLS, LongBench, RULER, needle-in-haystack) that evaluate end-to-end performance on natural text and thus conflate architectural capacity with semantic knowledge, L-CUBE removes these confounds by construction, providing ground-truth conditionals for unconfounded evaluation via conditional KL divergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

L-CUBE gives practitioners a principled, low-cost way to test whether a proposed architecture will maintain long-context capability at target sequence lengths before committing to expensive real-data training, directly addressing a gap where current evaluation cannot distinguish capacity failures from knowledge failures. The preliminary finding that augmenting pretraining with L-CUBE sequences improves length generalization on PG19 suggests the synthetic benchmark may serve a dual role as both diagnostic and training data. Open questions include whether the observed compute-scaling regularity (similar utilized-context-length slopes across architectures when plotted against FLOPs) holds beyond the tested model sizes and architectures, and whether the synthetic-to-real transfer generalizes to domains with non-stationary or non-Gaussian dependency structures.
