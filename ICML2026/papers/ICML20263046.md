## Problem

In replay-heavy PPO training, stale experience generates heavy-tailed importance ratios that cause standard hard clipping to zero out gradient contributions for a large fraction of replayed samples—a phenomenon the authors term 'utilization collapse.' Existing surrogate-side fixes (e.g., SAPO's smooth advantage-weighted decay) lack a symmetric, theoretically grounded mechanism that simultaneously preserves non-zero gradients in the tails and provides explicit bias-variance control under finite-sample estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes replay-buffer entries of the form (s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1}, μ(a_t|s_t)) where the stored behavior-policy probability μ may be many versions behind the current learner π_θ. For continuous-control benchmarks (Meta-World, MuJoCo) the state is a low-dimensional vector; for LIBERO the backbone is a 7B OpenVLA-OFT VLA model ingesting RGB images and natural-language instructions. The output is a stochastic action distribution π_θ(a|s) from which actions are sampled; the per-sample importance ratio ρ_t = π_θ(a_t|s_t)/μ(a_t|s_t) is computed online and clamped to [ρ_min, ρ_max] before log-space weighting.

## Method

GIPO replaces PPO's piecewise hard clipping with a smooth Gaussian trust weight applied in log-ratio space: ω(ρ̄_t; σ) = exp(−½(log ρ̄_t / σ)²), where ρ̄_t is the stop-gradient importance ratio and σ is a tunable damping scale. The surrogate objective becomes L_GIPO = −E[ω(ρ̄_t;σ)·ρ_t·A_t], yielding an effective per-sample multiplier m_t = ω(ρ̄_t;σ)·ρ_t that is provably bounded by exp(σ²/2), ensuring Hoeffding-style concentration bounds on the finite-sample surrogate estimate. The design is strictly symmetric in log-space (ω(ρ)=ω(1/ρ)), smooth for all ρ>0, and interpolates between a conservative near-on-policy estimator (σ→0) and the unbiased but high-variance importance-sampling gradient (σ→∞). The theoretical analysis derives a monotonic-improvement lower bound on J(π′) parameterized by a tunable threshold τ, with the optimal τ given by the Lambert-W function of σ². An Advantage-Aware variant applies separate scales σ_pos and σ_neg for positive and negative advantages while preserving the core log-space symmetry.

## Results

On the Meta-World Stale regime (10 tasks, 5 seeds, IQM), GIPO(1.0, 1.0) achieves 0.730, outperforming SAPO (0.412) by 77% and PPO (0.180) by 306%. On 10 MuJoCo tasks (IQM), GIPO(0.5, 0.5) reaches 0.606 versus SAPO (0.413) and PPO (0.119). On Classic Control (IQM), GIPO(0.2) scores 0.8095, edging out PPO (0.7908) and SAPO (0.7495). On the LIBERO benchmark with a 7B OpenVLA-OFT backbone (>10,000 H200 GPU-hours), GIPO consistently reaches near-optimal success rates earlier than both baselines across all four suites.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIPO dominates both baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against PPO, the advantage is most dramatic under heavy policy lag: on Meta-World Stale, GIPO(1.0,1.0) scores 0.730 versus PPO's 0.180 (a 4× gap), and on MuJoCo the gap widens to 0.606 vs. 0.119 (5×), reflecting PPO's utilization collapse where hard clipping zeroes out stale-sample gradients. Against SAPO, GIPO still wins consistently but by a smaller margin: 0.730 vs. 0.412 on Meta-World Stale, 0.606 vs. 0.413 on MuJoCo, and 0.8095 vs. 0.7495 on Classic Control. The gap narrows in the Fresh regime where policy lag is minimal, as expected. On the analytically tractable 2×2 GridWorld, GIPO establishes a dominant Pareto frontier in bias-variance space across all three lag levels (Cases A–C), while PPO's apparent zero variance in high-lag cases is an artifact of full gradient clipping rather than genuine efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

GIPO sits squarely in the PPO-family conservative policy optimization lineage, building on the clipped surrogate of Schulman et al. (2017) and the off-policy TRPO framework of Meng et al. (2022). It departs from target-side corrections (V-trace, Retrace) that fix value estimation under policy lag, instead targeting the actor-side surrogate where hard clipping causes 'utilization collapse' of stale replay. The closest prior work is SAPO (Gao et al., 2025), which also replaces hard clipping with smooth weighting, but GIPO introduces a symmetric Gaussian kernel in log-ratio space with a tunable σ that provides an explicit bias-variance interpolation, plus formal monotonic-improvement and concentration guarantees absent in SAPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners running replay-heavy actor-critic training in cost-sensitive domains (robotics, healthcare, industrial automation), GIPO offers a drop-in replacement for PPO's clipping that recovers gradient signal from stale data without sacrificing stability, validated at scale (10,000+ H200 GPU-hours, 7B VLA backbone on LIBERO). The clean theoretical guarantees (monotonic improvement lower bound, Hoeffding-style concentration) give practitioners confidence in the method's behavior under finite samples. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to fully off-policy actor-critic methods (SAC-style), whether the σ schedule should be adaptive during training, and whether the gains persist in physical robot post-training where data collection is even more constrained.
