## Problem

Autoregressive text-to-image models, despite matching diffusion-model quality on global semantics, fail to achieve precise control over fine-grained visual details when prompts differ only in subtle word-level distinctions. This paper identifies and quantifies this limitation through a new paired-prompt benchmark and proposes a training method that explicitly teaches models to map small textual variations to specific visual token changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263546/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural-language text prompt as input and produces a discrete sequence of visual tokens (encoded via SigLIP) that are decoded into an RGB image by a diffusion decoder. During training and evaluation, the key structural detail is that prompts are always presented in pairs sharing high semantic overlap but differing in one fine-grained attribute (color, count, position, style, text, or appearance), and the model must generate distinct images for each prompt that reflect only the localized difference.

## Method

FocusDiff combines a purpose-built dataset with a modified RL algorithm. FocusDiff-Data (~500K pairs) is constructed via an image-first pipeline: before-and-after image-editing pairs are collected, screened for quality, and captioned by InternVL3.5-38B to produce style-matched prompt pairs that differ in only a few words, with post-verification ensuring cross-alignment is prohibited. Pair-GRPO extends GRPO in two ways: (1) the group for advantage computation is expanded from G samples of a single prompt to 2G samples from a pair of similar prompts, forcing the model to contrast outputs under subtly different conditions; and (2) a dynamic probability p (annealed from 1.0 to 0.0) controls whether ground-truth images from FocusDiff-Data are injected into the group, providing exploitation guidance early in training and transitioning to fully autonomous exploration later. The backbone X-Omni is first supervised-fine-tuned on FocusDiff-Data, then optimized with Pair-GRPO using a QA-based VLM reward (InternVL3.5-38B) that scores both global alignment and fine-grained detail consistency.

## Results

On DeltaBench (the paper's new fine-grained alignment benchmark), X-Omni-FocusDiff achieves an average arithmetic mean score of 76.1 and geometric mean score of 71.8, improving over the X-Omni backbone by +14.3 and +18.6 points respectively. On GenEval, it reaches 0.90 overall (vs. 0.83 for X-Omni and 0.89 for GLM-Image), and on DPG-Bench it scores 88.35 (vs. 87.65 for X-Omni). The s_a − s_g stability gap shrinks from 8.6 points (X-Omni) to 4.3 points, matching the stability of top-tier diffusion models like FLUX.2-dev (4.9) and Qwen-Image (4.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-Omni-FocusDiff outperforms the best AR-based baseline GLM-Image (72.9/66.7) by +3.2/+5.1 points on DeltaBench average s_a/s_g, and surpasses the X-Omni backbone by a large margin (+14.3/+18.6). It slightly trails the strongest diffusion model FLUX.2-dev (81.6/77.7) by 5.5/5.9 points and is within 1.2 points of Qwen-Image (77.3/73.0), despite using fewer total parameters (19B vs. 56B and 27B respectively). On GenEval, X-Omni-FocusDiff (0.90) edges out GLM-Image (0.89) and GPT-4o (0.85) but falls just short of Qwen-Image (0.91). On DPG-Bench it achieves 88.35, marginally beating Qwen-Image (88.32) and Z-Image (88.14). On LongText-Bench it scores 0.935, below GPT-4o (0.956) and GLM-Image (0.952) but well above the X-Omni backbone (0.900). The most dramatic gains appear in Counting (+25.6 s_a over backbone) and Position (+17.3 s_a), confirming that the paired RL training specifically targets fine-grained semantic control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on X-Omni (Geng et al., 2025) as its autoregressive text-to-image backbone and extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) from LLM reasoning to visual generation via a paired-sample formulation (Pair-GRPO). It departs from prior compositional benchmarks like Winoground-T2I and EvoGen, which target core semantic shifts (e.g., subject-object swaps), by focusing on fine-grained, easily-overlooked details (e.g., button vs. zipper, EXP 2026 vs. EXP 2028) using an image-first construction pipeline from editing datasets. The approach also contrasts with contrastive-learning-based alignment methods (Yin et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024) that the authors argue undermine the autoregressive paradigm, instead preserving the next-token prediction framework while injecting differential signals through paired training data and group-relative RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeltaBench provides a concrete, reproducible metric (geometric vs. arithmetic mean gap) for measuring fine-grained generation stability that prior single-prompt benchmarks cannot capture, and the finding that most AR-based models show 5+ point stability gaps highlights a real, under-addressed weakness. The paired RL paradigm (Pair-GRPO) offers a general recipe for injecting fine-grained contrastive signals into any AR generation pipeline without architectural changes, and the ~500K paired dataset is a reusable resource. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to video generation or non-AR backbones, whether the VLM-based evaluation (Qwen3-VL-30B) introduces systematic biases that inflate gains, and whether the image-editing-derived pairs adequately cover the full distribution of fine-grained semantic variations a user might request.
