## Problem

Structure-Based Drug Design fails when 3D target structures are unavailable or when disease phenotypes emerge from multi-pathway dysregulation rather than a single actionable target. Existing transcriptomics-driven ML methods only solve the forward problem (predicting cellular responses to known compounds), leaving the inverse design problem—generating molecules that induce a desired transcriptomic state transition—unaddressed. This paper formalizes Transcriptome-based Drug Design (TBDD) as a generative inverse problem and identifies its inherent ill-posedness, cross-modal domain gap, and sparse-signal challenges.

## Input / Output

The method accepts paired transcriptome profiles (T_pre, T_post) at either bulk resolution (978-gene L1000 vectors) or single-cell resolution (populations of n cells × 60,000+ genes from Tahoe-100M), plus optional cellular context. For single-cell input, the population is projected via SCimilarity into a 128-dim latent manifold and then cycle-stratified into N=128 representative vectors rather than collapsed to a single mean. The output is a distribution over molecular graphs (atom and bond type assignments) sampled via a discrete graph diffusion process, reflecting the ill-posed nature of the inverse mapping where many structures can produce similar transcriptomic signatures.

## Method

CURE comprises a Transcriptome Perturbation Functional Feature Extractor (TFE) and a Perturbation-guided Molecular graph Diffusion model (PMD). The TFE has three sub-modules: TFE-H performs Cycle-Stratified Structured Aggregation (partitioning cells by G1/S/G2M phases, local pooling, proportional sampling) to preserve sub-population heterogeneity while denoising; TFE-I uses 3 stacked blocks of symmetrical cross-attention between T_pre and T_post streams to distill a function-oriented perturbation embedding; TFE-A projects this embedding into two complementary chemical domains—global topology (aligned to a pretrained HierVAE latent space via ELBO + alignment loss) and local bioactivity (aligned to 2048-dim Morgan fingerprints via masked InfoNCE + sparse regression). The PMD is a Graph Diffusion Transformer that performs discrete Markov-chain diffusion over atom/bond types, injecting the TFE embedding via AdaLN with Classifier-Free Guidance (dynamic feature dropping at probability p plus isotropic noise). TFE is trained for ~30k steps then frozen before PMD training (~40k steps, 500 diffusion steps, batch 400).

## Results

On the L1000 bulk in-distribution benchmark, CURE achieves 100% heavy atom coverage, a Fréchet ChemNet Distance of 6.79, Morgan fingerprint similarity of 0.823, and a PRnet functional MSE of 0.233 — substantially outperforming all baselines on every metric. Under the rigorous OOD unseen-drugs scaffold split, CURE retains 90.9% coverage and 0.772 Morgan similarity while baselines drop to 45–64% coverage and 0.51–0.59 Morgan similarity. On the Tahoe-100M single-cell dataset, CURE achieves 0.611 Morgan similarity and 0.483 PRnet MSE, whereas pseudo-bulk-adapted baselines fall to 0.20–0.29 Morgan similarity and 4.1–7.7 PRnet MSE, confirming that heterogeneity-aware aggregation is critical for noisy single-cell inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CURE dominates all three baselines (GexMolGen, Gx2Mol, TRIOMPHE) across every reported metric and every evaluation split. The most striking gap is in functional fidelity: CURE's PRnet MSE of 0.23 (bulk in-distribution) is roughly 11× lower than Gx2Mol (2.60) and 32× lower than TRIOMPHE (7.46), indicating generated molecules induce transcriptomic responses far closer to the target. On structural similarity, CURE achieves 0.823 Morgan similarity versus 0.54–0.69 for baselines, and its Fréchet ChemNet Distance (6.79) is 2.6–7× lower than competitors. Under OOD conditions the advantage widens: baselines lose 10–28 percentage points in coverage and see Morgan similarity drop by 0.08–0.19, while CURE degrades only modestly (e.g., Morgan Sim. 0.82→0.77 on unseen drugs). On the single-cell Tahoe-100M dataset, pseudo-bulk-adapted baselines suffer severe signal dilution (Morgan Sim. 0.20–0.29, PRnet MSE 4.1–7.7), whereas CURE's TFE-H module preserves sub-population structure to achieve 0.611 Morgan Sim. and 0.483 PRnet MSE — a 2–3× improvement over the best baseline. In the zero-shot gene-inhibitor task, CURE also achieves the highest Morgan similarity (0.75–0.88) and strongest binding affinities (−8.3 to −9.2 kcal/mol) across all 10 targets, outperforming GexMolGen (−7.1 to −7.6) and TRIOMPHE (−5.7 to −6.5) by 1–3 kcal/mol.

## Relation to Prior Work

CURE builds on the Diffusion Transformer backbone (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and HierVAE (Jin et al., 2020) for molecular generation and topological constraint, respectively, while leveraging SCimilarity (Heimberg et al., 2025) as a frozen single-cell manifold projector. Conceptually, it inverts the well-studied forward perturbation-prediction problem (drug → transcriptomic response) into a generative inverse problem (transcriptomic transition → drug molecule), departing from prior TBDD methods (TRIOMPHE, GexMolGen, Gx2Mol) that rely on explicit gene-expression statistics or simpler conditioning without addressing the cross-modal domain gap or single-cell heterogeneity. The dual-view alignment strategy (graph topology + Morgan fingerprint) is a novel architectural choice for grounding transcriptomic features in complementary chemical priors, and the cycle-stratified aggregation is the first to natively preserve sub-population structure in single-cell perturbation data for molecular generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CURE addresses a practically critical gap: designing molecules when 3D target structures are unavailable or when disease phenotypes arise from multi-pathway dysregulation rather than a single actionable target. The zero-shot gene-inhibitor demonstration—achieving docking scores exceeding known inhibitors without ever conditioning on protein structure—suggests transcriptomic signatures may implicitly encode target structural requirements, opening a function-first pathway complementary to SBDD. However, all functional validation relies on in silico proxies (PRnet MSE, AutoDock Vina) with no wet-lab confirmation, and the single-cell baselines are adapted via pseudo-bulk rather than being native single-cell methods, leaving open whether the observed heterogeneity advantage generalizes to truly novel cellular contexts. Scalability to larger perturbation atlases and integration with experimental feedback loops remain important next steps.
