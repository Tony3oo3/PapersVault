## Problem

Diffusion-based data assimilation methods degrade under sparse observations because unobserved regions lack spatial constraints, causing severe error accumulation and physical inconsistency. Existing physics-guided strategies rely on dense observational data and lose constraining power in sparse settings, leaving a gap in how to simultaneously enforce global structural continuity and local dynamical consistency when only a small fraction of grid points are observed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263628/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse point observations (1–10% of a 128×256 global grid at 1.40625° resolution) spanning 69 atmospheric variables (5 upper-air fields at 13 pressure levels plus 4 surface fields), a 48-hour background forecast from the FengWu model, and a binary observation mask. Before conditioning the diffusion model, the sparse observations are first reconstructed into a dense global field via tensor completion, which then serves as the conditioning signal. The output is a fully resolved, physically consistent assimilated state field at the same spatial resolution and variable set as the input.

## Method

LoPhyDA introduces a dual-guidance mechanism into latent diffusion sampling. First, a low-rank tensor prior reconstructs sparse observations into a dense field by applying t-SVD with soft-thresholding in the Fourier domain over a 3-mode tensor (longitude × latitude × variable), exploiting matrix-level low-rankness for spatial continuity and tubal-rank for cross-variable dynamical correlations; the reconstruction is solved via ADMM and acts as a global conditioning signal for the diffusion model. Second, at each reverse-denoising step, the model estimates the clean latent via Tweedie's formula, decodes it to physical space, computes the geostrophic balance residual, backpropagates the PDE gradient to the latent, and injects it into the noise prediction (ε′ = ε + ρ√(1−α_t)∇L_physics), thereby rectifying the sampling trajectory in real time without retraining. The two signals are fused at each step using a Gaspari-Cohn localization function that blends the noisy reconstructed observation with the predicted state, ensuring high-confidence constraints near observation sites while allowing the physics gradient to govern unobserved regions.

## Results

Under the 1% sparse observation setting on ERA5, LoPhyDA achieves MAE 0.1183 and MSE 0.0336, outperforming the best baseline PhyDA (MAE 0.1225, MSE 0.0347) by 3.5% and 3.3% respectively. The WRMSE for z500 drops to 58.84, a 17.9% reduction over PhyDA (71.67) and a 14.1% reduction over AlignDA (68.67). On real-world GDAS prepbufr observations, LoPhyDA further reduces MSE by 6.78% relative to the second-best method AlignDA, confirming robustness beyond simulated sparse grids.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoPhyDA outperforms all four baselines across every reported metric under both simulated (1% grid) and real-world (GDAS prepbufr) observation settings. Against SDA (Rozet & Louppe, 2023), which uses observations as guidance signals during reverse diffusion, LoPhyDA reduces MSE from 0.0401 to 0.0336 (16.2% improvement), showing that score-based guidance alone is insufficient under extreme sparsity. Against DiffDA (Huang et al., 2024), which repaints observations into a pre-trained conditional diffusion model, LoPhyDA cuts z500 WRMSE from 75.53 to 58.84, demonstrating that repainting fails to constrain unobserved regions. Against AlignDA (Sun et al., 2025), which aligns the diffusion posterior via preference optimization, LoPhyDA achieves lower MSE (0.0336 vs. 0.0353) and substantially lower z500 WRMSE (58.84 vs. 68.67), indicating that reward-based alignment cannot match explicit low-rank reconstruction plus PDE gradient correction. Against PhyDA (Wang et al., 2025), which bakes physical constraints into training via regularization, LoPhyDA wins on all metrics (e.g., z500 WRMSE 58.84 vs. 71.67) while remaining training-free at inference, confirming that inference-time gradient injection is more effective than parameter-level physics regularization under sparse observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoPhyDA sits at the intersection of two research threads: score-based diffusion for data assimilation (Rozet & Louppe, 2023; Huang et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2025) and low-rank tensor completion for missing-data recovery (Lu et al., 2018; Qin et al., 2022). It departs from prior physics-guided DA methods (PhyDA, Bastek et al.) by moving physical constraints from the training objective to the inference-time sampling trajectory, following the diffusion posterior sampling paradigm of Chung et al. (2023). The key conceptual departure is treating sparse observation reconstruction as a separate, training-free tensor-completion step that produces a dense global prior, rather than forcing the diffusion model to implicitly learn variable couplings from sparse inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: existing diffusion-based DA methods degrade sharply under sparse observations because unobserved regions lack spatial constraints. The training-free, inference-time physics gradient injection is attractive for operational settings where retraining for different observation densities is infeasible. However, the current physical constraint is limited to geostrophic balance (invalid near the equator), the 15-second-per-sample inference cost is high for real-time assimilation, and the single-constraint setup leaves open the question of how to compose multiple PDE residuals. The authors identify flow matching as a path toward faster sampling and region-adaptive physics as a necessary extension.
