## Problem

Classical blind source separation and ICA methods require latent components to be independent or at least uncorrelated, yet real-world signals (e.g., fMRI brain networks) exhibit strong inter-component dependencies. Existing approaches that allow dependence impose restrictive assumptions—parametric dependency forms, paired multi-view data, interventions, or sparsity—leaving the fundamental question open: can dependent components be identified from linear mixtures using only multi-domain observations with no independence or distributional assumptions?

## Input / Output

The method takes T unpaired datasets (domains), each containing N samples of d-dimensional observations, where domains differ only through unknown diagonal rescalings of the latent variables—no cross-domain pairing is required. It outputs the shared mixing matrix A, per-domain diagonal scaling matrices D^τ, the latent covariance matrix G (arbitrary positive definite), and the recovered latent variables Z up to permutation, scaling, and domain-specific sign. The latent dimension n is determined from the rank of the sample covariance, and only second-order statistics are used, making the approach applicable even to Gaussian latents.

## Method

The key insight is that sufficient variability in domain-specific diagonal scalings (formalized via a full-rank degree-4 monomial matrix condition requiring m = C(n+3,4) domains) makes the mixing matrix identifiable from covariance structure alone, even when the latent covariance G is fully dense. ## Results

On simulated data (n=10, d=n, T=2n, N=5000), MuDo-CoM achieves near-perfect d-MCC of 0.999±0.001 across all dependency levels (k=0–3) and noise types (Gaussian, Exponential, Gumbel, nonlinear SCM). The only exception is the independent-component case (k=0), where FastICA and QNDIAG also reach 1.000. On the real CamCAN resting-state fMRI dataset (264 ROIs, 565 subjects, n=5 latents), MuDo-CoM recovers components with bootstrap d-MCC of 0.88±0.11 and identifies strongly correlated latent components (up to r=0.96) that all baselines fail to capture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuDo-CoM outperforms all five baselines in every setting with dependent components (k≥1), with the gap widening as dependency increases: at k=3, MuDo-CoM (0.999) leads QNDIAG (0.791) by ~0.21 and PARAFAC (0.474) by ~0.53 in d-MCC. FastICA and QNDIAG, which assume independent or uncorrelated sources respectively, degrade monotonically from k=0 to k=3, confirming that their independence assumptions are the bottleneck. PARAFAC consistently performs worst among baselines (0.47–0.58 at k=2–3), as its CP decomposition structure cannot model cross-component covariance. MHA, which does not assume independence but requires an orthonormal and non-negative mixing matrix, shows the most variable performance (0.51–0.78) and is the only baseline that occasionally approaches MuDo-CoM at higher k. The sole case where MuDo-CoM does not win is k=0 (truly independent components), where FastICA and QNDIAG achieve perfect 1.000 d-MCC, as expected since their independence assumption is exactly satisfied.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes classical joint diagonalization (Pham & Cardoso, 2001; Ablin et al., 2019) and PARAFAC-based covariance decomposition (Harshman et al., 1970), both of which require the latent covariance to be diagonal (i.e., uncorrelated components), to the fully dependent case where the latent covariance G is an arbitrary positive-definite matrix. It also departs from causal representation learning approaches (Lippe et al., 2022; von Kügelgen et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2024) that achieve identifiability of dependent components through interventions, temporal structure, or sparsity, by showing that domain-specific diagonal scaling variability alone suffices. The key conceptual move is recognizing that the multi-domain scaling structure provides enough algebraic constraints on the mixing matrix to force it to be monomial, even when the latent covariance has arbitrary off-diagonal entries, reducing the identifiability problem to a full-rank condition on degree-4 monomials of the scaling vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a long-standing gap in blind source separation: for the first time, dependent (correlated) latent components are element-wise identifiable from second-order statistics alone in a linear multi-domain model, without independence, distributional, or interventional assumptions. The practical impact is significant for fMRI, genomics, and climate science, where components are known to be strongly correlated (e.g., inter-regional brain connectivity) yet standard ICA and joint diagonalization methods silently discard this structure. Key limitations include the worst-case domain requirement of C(n+3,4) domains, the restriction to linear mixing, and the absence of measurement noise in the theoretical guarantees; extending to nonlinear mixing, noisy observations, and more statistically efficient objectives are identified as important open problems.
