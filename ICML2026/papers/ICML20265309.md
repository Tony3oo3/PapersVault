## Problem

Applying latent world models to multi-agent tasks creates spurious correlations between agents' latent states, since modeling joint dynamics requires integrating information across agents. Existing model-based MARL methods either operate in observation space (forfeiting history compression and sample efficiency) or rely on inter-agent communication to maintain latent consistency, violating fully decentralized execution. The paper asks how to learn disentangled latent representations that capture multi-agent interactions while remaining deployable without any communication at execution time.

## Input / Output

Each agent independently consumes its local observation (vector features in SMAC/SMACv2; 44×44 RGB images downsampled from 88×88 in Melting Pot), an available-action mask, and a continuation flag. The agent module encodes these into a factorized latent state (deterministic h^i via a recurrent model, stochastic z^i via a representation model using 32 one-hot vectors of 32 classes each). At execution, each agent outputs an action conditioned solely on its own latent state; during centralized training, a transformer-based critic produces per-agent value estimates from the joint deterministic state, and the environment module decodes rewards, continuation flags, and action masks from the joint state.

## Method

DMAWM decomposes the world model into independent per-agent RSSM modules and a shared environment module containing a transformer-based interaction predictor. The key mechanism is that during imagination, the interaction predictor takes the joint deterministic state h^{1:n} as input to sample the joint stochastic state z^{1:n}, modeling inter-agent dependencies without observations; however, the joint prior p_φ(ẑ^{1:n}|h^{1:n}) is aligned to the factorized posterior ∏_i q_ψ(z^i|h^i,o^i) via a KL-based dynamics loss and a stop-gradient representation loss, explicitly preventing information leakage between agents' latent states. The world model is trained end-to-end with BPTT and the straight-through estimator on an ELBO objective combining dynamics, representation, and decoder losses. Policies are trained with PPO on 4-step imagined rollouts (1024 parallel), using a transformer-based centralized critic that combines an on-policy GAE loss on imaginary trajectories with an off-policy loss grounded in real trajectories via truncated importance sampling, narrowing the gap between imagined and actual returns.

## Results

On SMAC, DMAWM achieves the highest win rates on 4 of 5 maps, with particularly large margins on hard maps: 86.5% on 2c_vs_64zg (vs. 31.8% for the best baseline QMIX) and 71.5% on corridor (vs. 18.6% for MAMBA). On SMACv2, DMAWM leads all three maps by 25+ points over the next-best method MAMBA (e.g., 77.0% vs. 51.4% on protoss_5_vs_5). On vision-based Melting Pot, DMAWM achieves 405.7 return on Coop Mining, roughly 2.3× the best baseline (MAMBA at 175.9 on Commons Harvest; 175.9 vs. 170.6 for MAPPO). DMAWM also matches or outperforms MARIE on 10 of 13 SMAC maps under MARIE's own protocol, with a 50.7-point gap on 2c_vs_64zg (76.6% vs. 25.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against model-free baselines, DMAWM dominates: it outperforms QMIX, MAPPO, and MAT on 9 of 10 maps, with the sole exception being 2s_vs_1sc where MAPPO and MAT reach 100% win rate versus DMAWM's 98.3%. Against the closest model-based baseline MAMBA (DreamerV2-based with inter-agent communication), DMAWM wins on 9 of 10 maps, with the largest gaps on hard SMAC maps (2c_vs_64zg: 86.5% vs. 22.8%; corridor: 71.5% vs. 18.6%) and all three SMACv2 maps (25–23 point margins). MBVD, which operates in observation space, is consistently outperformed by DMAWM on every map. Against MARIE (a transformer-based multi-agent world model), DMAWM matches or exceeds it on 10 of 13 SMAC maps under MARIE's own protocol, with a decisive 50.7-point advantage on 2c_vs_64zg (76.6% vs. 25.9%) and 10.7 points on 3s_vs_5z (89.1% vs. 78.4%). Notably, DMAWM achieves these results with fully decentralized execution, whereas MAMBA and MARIE rely on inter-agent communication or centralized latent-state broadcasting during execution.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMAWM extends the DreamerV2 RSSM framework (Hafner et al., 2019, 2021) from single-agent to multi-agent settings by decomposing the latent state into independent per-agent modules and a shared transformer-based environment module. The key departure from prior model-based MARL methods (MAMBA, MAG, CoDreamer, MAZero) is that those approaches either broadcast latent states across agents or rely on inter-agent communication to maintain consistency between imagination and execution, thereby violating the CTDE paradigm. DMAWM instead enforces a factorized posterior structure and aligns the joint prior to it via KL divergence, ensuring that imagined rollouts respect the information constraints of decentralized execution without any communication channel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves a fundamental tension in model-based MARL: latent imagination requires integrating multi-agent information, yet decentralized execution demands that each agent operate solely on local observations. The probing experiment (85% vs 42.3% action-prediction accuracy for unobservable teammates) provides a concrete diagnostic for latent-state information leakage that the field lacked. Open questions include whether the 4-step imagination horizon plateau can be overcome in multi-agent settings, whether the approach extends to mixed-motive (non-cooperative) tasks, and how the quadratic-complexity environment module scales beyond 20 agents. The parameter-sharing assumption also limits applicability to heterogeneous teams.
