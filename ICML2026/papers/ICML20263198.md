## Problem

Natural-language explanations for ML models are typically trained on human-written rationales, producing fluent but behaviorally unfaithful narratives that neither reproduce nor predict the target model's actual decision function. No prior method directly optimizes explanations for behavioral faithfulness—i.e., the ability to serve as a functional surrogate for the classifier's input-output mapping—without relying on human annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263198/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

OPEX takes N=25 labeled input-label pairs from a target classifier (structured tabular or text-based) linearized into a fixed prompt template, and produces a single task-level natural-language paragraph describing the classifier's decision logic in terms of explicit feature conditions and value ranges. The explanation is intended to be sufficient for predicting the target on new, unseen samples, and the prompt explicitly forbids referencing the model, using external knowledge, or employing relative/qualitative terms. At inference, the same model is applied to a held-out (task, classifier) pair with no additional fine-tuning; the specific classifier to explain is specified entirely by the in-context examples.

## Method

OPEX initializes from LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct and is fine-tuned with LoRA adapters (rank 16) using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which eliminates the need for a learned critic by computing group-normalized advantages from multiple sampled explanations per prompt. The key departure from prior work is the reward signal: instead of supervised loss against human-written rationales, a User-proxy LLM (LLaMA-3.3-70B-Instruct) evaluates each candidate explanation by predicting the classifier's label on inducing examples (recoverability) or held-out examples (simulatability), with scores scaled to [-1,1] as the RL reward. Training is multi-task across 16 classification tasks (14 structured, 2 text-based) with ~8,000 prompt instances, and synthetic tasks with unknown labeling rules are included to prevent the evaluator from exploiting pretraining knowledge. Two variants are trained—OPEX-R (recoverability reward) and OPEX-S (simulatability reward)—and a combined variant optimizes both objectives simultaneously.

## Results

Across six structured and text-based classification tasks, OPEX achieves an average simulatability of 0.86 and recoverability of 0.99, substantially outperforming all LLM baselines (GPT-4o: 0.68/0.73, LLaMA-70B: 0.68/0.73, LLaMA-8B: 0.63/0.66) and MaNtLE (0.52/0.53). On the Finance and YouTube Spam tasks, OPEX reaches near-perfect scores (simulatability 1.00 and 0.95, recoverability 1.00), while the largest gap over baselines appears on Car Evaluation (simulatability 0.93 vs. 0.75 for GPT-4o) and Balance Scale (0.75 vs. 0.61). A 3B-parameter variant (OPEX-SMALL) retains 0.85 simulatability and 0.99 recoverability, and a user study shows OPEX-R explanations improve human classification accuracy by 15 percentage points (0.63→0.78) versus negligible gains from LLaMA baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MaNtLE (Menon et al., 2023), OPEX wins decisively on both metrics: average simulatability of 0.86 vs. 0.52 and recoverability of 0.99 vs. 0.53, with the largest per-task gaps on Balance Scale (0.75 vs. 0.35 simulatability) and YouTube Spam (0.94 vs. 0.43). Against Chain-of-Thought baselines (Wei et al., 2023; instantiated via DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B), OPEX achieves 0.99 recoverability and 0.84 simulatability versus 0.73/0.66 for zero-shot CoT and 0.76/0.65 for CoT+few-shot, a gap of roughly 23–25 points in recoverability and 18–19 points in simulatability. OPEX also outperforms GPT-4o (0.68 simulatability, 0.73 recoverability) and LLaMA-70B (0.68/0.73) by 18 and 26 percentage points respectively on average, despite using an 8B backbone. The advantage is consistent across structured (tabular) and text-based tasks, and holds when the evaluator is switched from LLaMA-70B to GPT-4o (simulatability drops only from 0.86 to 0.84), confirming the gains are not evaluator-specific artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of training explanation generators on human-written rationales (MaNtLE, WT5, CAGE) by instead using reinforcement learning (GRPO) to directly optimize explanations against behavioral faithfulness metrics—recoverability (Menon et al., 2023) and simulatability (Hase & Bansal, 2020)—as reward signals. It borrows the GRPO optimization framework from DeepSeekMath and the LLM-as-user-proxy evaluation protocol from Poché et al. (2025) and Bona et al. (2024), but applies them to a novel objective: making explanations function as testable surrogates for a classifier's input-output mapping rather than plausible narratives. The multi-task training across heterogeneous structured and text-based datasets extends prior single-task explanation work, and the use of synthetic tasks (CLUES) to guard against evaluator knowledge leakage is a methodological addition not present in prior explanation evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the dependency on human-written rationales, OPEX addresses a key scalability bottleneck in explainability and demonstrates that a compact 8B model, when optimized for the right behavioral objective, can outperform 70B+ models and GPT-4o on explanation faithfulness. The 15% improvement in human classification accuracy from a user study provides concrete evidence of practical utility. Open questions include whether the framework extends to open-ended generation, multimodal, or sequential decision-making tasks; whether the LLM-proxy evaluation fully captures human judgment; and whether the 25-sample sweet spot generalizes across domains. The reliance on a single dataset for the user study and the absence of mechanistic faithfulness analysis limit the breadth of claims.
