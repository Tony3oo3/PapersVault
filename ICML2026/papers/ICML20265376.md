## Problem

Existing diffusion-based SBDD methods perturb and reconstruct ligand atomic coordinates in a fully coupled manner, forcing the model to simultaneously solve global binding positioning and internal molecular structure design within a single high-dimensional joint space. This entanglement leads to inefficient exploration and suboptimal generation quality in both aspects, despite the two factors having distinct biophysical roles (a ligand is a pre-formed entity whose placement and internal geometry are governed separately).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265376/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein pocket as a set of 3D atomic coordinates and one-hot atom-type vectors (point cloud), centered so the protein's center of mass is at the origin. It outputs a 3D ligand molecule as atomic coordinates and atom types, where the number of atoms is sampled from an empirical distribution conditioned on pocket size and molecular bonds are inferred as a post-processing step. Internally, the ligand coordinates are decomposed into a centroid position vector (binding position) and centroid-centered coordinates (conformation), which are diffused independently.

## Method

DeCoDe decouples the forward diffusion into two independent processes: one over the ligand centroid (binding position, diffused in the original complex frame with noise N(0, 1/N_M · I_3)) and one over centroid-centered atom coordinates (conformation, diffused in a translation-invariant subspace). A time-dependent asynchronous noise schedule is applied so that early forward steps primarily perturb conformation while later steps accelerate position noise, inducing the reverse process to first coarsely localize the ligand in the pocket before refining its detailed geometry. The SE(3)-equivariant GNN is modified to allow the entire protein to translate rigidly during message passing (equivalent to translating the ligand oppositely), which offloads global positioning from ligand atoms and lets them focus on internal structure while preserving equivariance. The framework is architecture-agnostic and can be plugged into existing models (TargetDiff, IRDiff) without changing network parameters, reducing average JSD of bond-distance distributions by 18%.

## Results

On the CrossDocked2020 benchmark, IRDeCo (DeCoDe applied to IRDiff) achieves the best structural fidelity across nearly all bond types, with a C-C bond JSD of 0.305 and an all-atom distance JSD of 0.137, reducing the average JSD by 18% over its base model IRDiff. For binding affinity, IRDeCo attains a Vina Score of −6.69 (avg) and 69.5% high-affinity rate, competitive with IPDiff (−6.42, 69.5%) but trailing AliDiff (−7.07, 73.4%). When combined with soft-DPO fine-tuning, IRDeCo-DPO surpasses AliDiff on Vina Score (−7.20 vs −7.07) and high-affinity rate (75.3% vs 73.4%). IRDeCo also achieves the best synthetic accessibility (SA = 0.62) and PoseBusters validity rate (73.2%) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IRDeCo consistently outperforms its base model IRDiff on structural fidelity (reducing C-C JSD from 0.439 to 0.305, a 48% reduction for mid-range C-C bonds) and on binding affinity (Vina Score −6.69 vs −6.03; Vina Min −7.49 vs −7.27), while matching QED (0.52 vs 0.53) and improving SA (0.62 vs 0.59). Against IPDiff, IRDeCo wins on structural fidelity and Vina Score (−6.69 vs −6.42) but trails slightly on Vina Dock (−8.58 vs −8.57) and ties on high-affinity rate (69.5%). Against AliDiff (the strongest affinity baseline using soft-DPO), IRDeCo without DPO loses on binding affinity (Vina Score −6.69 vs −7.07; High Affinity 69.5% vs 73.4%) but wins on SA (0.62 vs 0.57); with DPO fine-tuning, IRDeCo-DPO surpasses AliDiff on Vina Score (−7.20 vs −7.07) and high-affinity rate (75.3% vs 73.4%). DeCoDe (applied to TargetDiff) improves over TargetDiff on Vina Score (−6.19 vs −5.47) and structural JSD (C-C: 0.164 vs 0.225) but slightly underperforms on high-affinity rate (56.9% vs 58.1%). Overall, the decoupled framework delivers the largest gains in structural realism (JSD, PB-Valid) while maintaining competitive affinity, with the caveat that affinity-focused baselines like AliDiff still lead on raw Vina scores unless DeCoDe is also paired with DPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeCoDe builds directly on the SE(3)-equivariant DDPM framework established by TargetDiff (Guan et al., 2023a), DiffSBDD (Schneuing et al., 2023), and DiffBP (Lin et al., 2022), which jointly diffuse ligand coordinates and atom types in a single entangled process. The key conceptual departure is from DecompDiff (Guan et al., 2023b), which decomposes molecular substructures within externally-selected pocket regions, whereas DeCoDe decomposes the ligand's own geometry into global binding position (centroid) and internal conformation (centroid-relative coordinates) with asynchronous noise schedules. The parameterization decoupling—allowing the protein to translate rigidly during message passing—repurposes the equivariant GNN architecture from prior work to separate the learning of placement from structure, and the soft-DPO fine-tuning connection to AliDiff (Gu et al., 2024) demonstrates composability with affinity-oriented post-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling framework is architecture-agnostic and plug-and-play, demonstrated by improving both TargetDiff and IRDiff without modifying their network parameters, making it immediately adoptable by the SBDD community. The large PB-Valid jump (41.4% → 73.2% for IRDiff → IRDeCo) suggests that structural realism and physical plausibility are more tightly coupled than previously appreciated, with implications for downstream docking and virtual screening pipelines. However, the modest binding-affinity gains relative to structural gains highlight an open question: whether decoupled generation alone suffices for high-affinity design or must be combined with explicit affinity optimization (as shown with DPO fine-tuning). Whether the coarse-to-fine scheduling principle generalizes beyond SBDD to other 3D molecular generation tasks (e.g., protein design, crystal structure prediction) remains unexplored.
