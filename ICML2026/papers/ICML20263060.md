## Problem

Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for dexterous manipulation are bottlenecked by data scarcity: they rely on small-scale teleoperated demonstrations or synthetic data with persistent sim-to-real gaps, orders of magnitude smaller than the internet-scale data that enables LMM success. Prior attempts to leverage human videos use implicit learning (contrastive, masked autoencoding, latent actions) that yields unclear mechanisms and limited transfer, failing to achieve the dramatic gains seen in visual instruction tuning for LMMs. The paper asks whether a dexterous VLA can be pretrained from large-scale human videos via explicit hand-motion modeling and then adapted to robot manipulators.

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the model consumes a 448×448 RGB scene image, a text instruction, and (for prediction tasks) a short motion context, and autoregressively generates discrete motion tokens (128 per hand per second at 15 FPS) that decode to MANO-D162 parameters (joint angles, wrist rotation, translation, shape). During post-training, the same visual-text backbone additionally receives the robot's proprioceptive state and a chunk of learnable action queries, producing continuous robot action chunks via an MLP or flow-matching head. The motion tokenizer uses a part-level design with separate codebooks for wrist (global pose) and finger (fine-grained) parameters, each with 8-layer grouped residual quantization (group size 2, codebook 4096, code dim 512).

## Method

The core contribution is Physical Instruction Tuning, a three-stage paradigm that extends visual instruction tuning to the physical domain. (1) Pretraining: a unified autoregressive LMM (InternVL3 backbone) is extended with motion tokens via a part-level GRQ tokenizer that quantizes MANO-D162 hand motion into discrete codes, treating hand movement as a 'foreign language' with explicit <MOT>/</MOT> delimiters; a wrist-specific reconstruction loss and dual-level masking (vocabulary logit masking at 50% probability, token-level loss filtering at 15th–95th percentile) stabilize training. (2) Perspective spatial alignment: all heterogeneous video sources are mapped to a unified weak-perspective camera space via intrinsics re-projection, and view-invariant motion distribution balancing (depth scaling + in-plane rotation) augments underrepresented viewpoints while preserving 3D consistency. (3) Post-training: a non-autoregressive adapter with learnable action queries attends to the pretrained backbone's hidden states and regresses to robot actions via a lightweight MLP (or flow-matching head), requiring only the frozen VLA backbone plus two MLP projectors. The UniHand dataset (150M+ instruction-following samples from 11 sources spanning mocap, VR, and pseudo-annotated RGB videos) supports this pipeline, with a 2.5M balanced subset used for training.

## Results

On LIBERO, Being-H achieves a 90.3% average success rate across four task suites (Spatial 92.6%, Object 96.8%, Goal 94.4%, Long 77.4%), with the flow-matching variant (Being-H FM) reaching 94.5%. On RoboCasa, Being-H outperforms GR00T N1.5 (23.8% vs. 21.0% average) despite GR00T being pretrained on extensive robot data. For hand motion generation, Being-H-14B achieves MPJPE of 6.87 mm (head) and 8.11 mm (tail), substantially outperforming GR00T N1.5 (9.82/15.35 mm), with the gap widening on the long-tail split. On real-world mobile stereo-view tasks, Being-H achieves 50% and 45% success on Bimanual-Handover and Arrange-Flower respectively, versus 35% and 15% for π0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Being-H outperforms all eight baselines on LIBERO, achieving 90.3% average vs. GR00T N1.5 (86.5%), MolmoAct (86.6%), π0-FAST (85.5%), OpenVLA (76.5%), Octo (75.1%), and Diffusion Policy (72.4%); the flow-matching variant extends this lead to 94.5%. On RoboCasa, Being-H surpasses GR00T N1.5 (23.8% vs. 21.0%) and the unpretrained InternVL3 backbone (18.2%), with the largest gains in high-precision tasks like Insert (30.7% vs. 26.7%) and Button (17.0% vs. 6.0%). Against VITRA, the closest human-video pretraining baseline, Being-H achieves a 13.6-point improvement on LIBERO (90.3% vs. 76.7%), demonstrating that explicit hand-motion tokenization is more effective than visual-dynamics-only pretraining. On hand motion generation, Being-H-14B reduces MPJPE by 30% on the tail split compared to GR00T N1.5 (8.11 vs. 15.35 mm), indicating substantially better generalization to diverse motion distributions. On real-world dexterous tasks, Being-H consistently outperforms GR00T N1.5 (e.g., Pour-Cup 100% vs. 90%, Unfold-Clothes 75% vs. 60%) and the InternVL3 baseline, with the advantage most pronounced on unseen objects and cluttered scenes. On the challenging mobile stereo-view setting, Being-H nearly doubles π0.5's success on Arrange-Flower (45% vs. 15%) and exceeds it by 15 points on Bimanual-Handover (50% vs. 35%).

## Relation to Prior Work

Being-H extends the visual instruction tuning paradigm from LMMs into the physical domain via 'Physical Instruction Tuning,' treating hand motion as a discrete foreign language (analogous to how LMMs treat text) rather than relying on implicit representations like contrastive features, masked autoencoders, or latent actions used by prior human-video VLA methods (VITRA, Phantom, EgoMimic). The motion tokenizer architecture is adapted from GRQ (Yang et al., 2023, originally for audio codec), and the backbone is InternVL3, but the key conceptual departure is using explicit part-level hand motion tokens as structured physical supervision rather than task-specific latent codes. For downstream adaptation, the paper departs from autoregressive action generation (as in OpenVLA, π0) by mapping pretrained motion-informed latents to robot actions via learnable queries and a lightweight MLP or flow-matching head, positioning the human hand as a universal manipulator template rather than a source of retargeted demonstrations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a critical bottleneck in dexterous manipulation: the scarcity of high-quality teleoperated data for multi-fingered hands. By demonstrating that explicit hand-motion pretraining from 150M+ human video samples transfers effectively to robot control with strong data efficiency (25% data matching 100% baseline on several tasks), it offers a practical pathway to reduce reliance on costly robot data collection. The UniHand dataset and the Physical Instruction Tuning recipe are likely to become foundational resources for the dexterous VLA community. Open questions include whether the weak-perspective alignment assumption holds under aggressive ego-motion, how pseudo-labeled RGB data quality limits ceiling performance, and whether the explicit-tokenization advantage over implicit latent actions persists at substantially larger scales or with more complex multi-object interactions.
