## Problem

Traditional sparse attention for LLM decoding assumes the attention operator is memory-bound, so it budgets by fetched tokens and computes all Q-heads for every retrieved key. However, MLA and MTP architectures push arithmetic intensity well beyond the hardware ridge, making decoding compute-bound; in this regime, K-only sparsity wastes significant FLOPs on Q-heads that contribute negligible attention mass to specific tokens, and no prior method exploits the within-group Q-head sparsity that MLA exposes.

## Input / Output

At each decode step the method consumes the current query vector, pre-computed per-page KV digests (compact max/min vectors cached at page-fill time), and a tier configuration specifying page counts and head counts per tier. It outputs the standard attention output vector, but only the selected (Q-head, key-page) pairs are actually computed; unselected pairs are implicitly zero. A hardware-aware constraint enforces that each active page uses either zero heads or at least τ_hw·m_kv/2 heads to avoid falling back into the memory-bound regime where pruning heads saves no latency.

## Method

TileSparse introduces QK 2D Sparsity, which extends the standard K-only (1D) page-selection pipeline (inherited from ArkVale's cuboid-mean digest estimation and top-k page selection) with a novel Q-head selection stage that generates a per-page binary mask identifying the top-contributing query heads, so the compute budget is spent on meaningful (head, page) pairs rather than uniformly across all heads. To preserve accuracy under aggressive sparsity, Tiered QK 2D Sparsity partitions selected pages into a Critical Tier (full 128 heads) and an Ambient Tier (reduced head count h ≥ τ_hw/2), matching the contour-like importance distribution observed in MLA attention maps. An online AutoTuner periodically evaluates candidate tier configurations by estimating attention-mass coverage over the H×P score matrix and selects the scheme with the highest coverage, adapting to varying semantic contexts without retraining. The entire framework is training-free and operates at page granularity to maintain memory-coalesced access, with the cost metric defined as total active compute entries rather than fetched tokens.

## Results

Under a 256-token equivalent compute budget, TileSparse improves accuracy by 36.4% on RULER and 7.7% on LongBench over the state-of-the-art K-only baseline ArkVale, while simultaneously reducing computational overhead by 18.3% and 40.7% respectively. At the 99% full-attention accuracy threshold, TileSparse requires 18.3% less budget on RULER and 40.8% less on LongBench compared to K-only sparsity. End-to-end, the TileSparse attention operator achieves 4.13× speedup on RULER and 3.99× on LongBench over Full Attention (vs. ArkVale's 3.86× and 3.33×), with core attention latency reduced by 12.2% and 34.7% respectively relative to ArkVale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ArkVale (the primary K-only dynamic sparsity baseline), TileSparse wins on every reported axis: higher accuracy at the same compute budget (36.4% on RULER, 7.7% on LongBench at 256 tokens), lower budget to reach the same accuracy target (18.3%/40.8% savings at 99% full-attention accuracy), and lower core attention latency (12.2%/34.7% reduction). Against StreamingLLM (static fixed-window sparsity), TileSparse consistently outperforms across the entire budget range on both RULER and LongBench, as StreamingLLM's query-agnostic window misses critical long-range semantic anchors. Against Quest (dynamic K-sparsity with cuboid-max digest), TileSparse also wins on both benchmarks; the paper attributes the gap partly to Quest's cuboid-max digest being less faithful at capturing aggregate page-level importance than the cuboid-mean digest shared by ArkVale and TileSparse. In all three comparisons, TileSparse's advantage stems from its 2D Q-head pruning, which reclaims wasted compute to retrieve additional semantically important pages under the same arithmetic budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

TileSparse builds directly on ArkVale's cuboid-mean digest and top-k page selection pipeline (stages 1–2 of its algorithm) but fundamentally departs from the K-only (1D) sparsity paradigm that dominates the field. While prior head-wise sparsity designs (DuoAttention, LycheeDecode, MoH, Polar Sparsity) prune at the group level in memory-bound MHA/GQA settings where computing all Q-heads is effectively free, TileSparse introduces a finer pruning unit—a single Q-head within a group—motivated by the compute-bound regime of MLA architectures. The paper also repositions the cost metric from fetched tokens (memory-centric) to active compute entries (compute-centric), establishing a Roofline-aware formulation that prior sparse attention work did not consider.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MLA and MTP become standard in frontier models (DeepSeek-V3, Kimi K2, LongCat-Flash), the compute-bound regime is no longer an edge case but the default operating condition, making this reframing broadly relevant. The paper's identification of within-group Q-head sparsity opens a new axis of optimization that was invisible under the memory-bound assumption, and the 40% compute reduction at 99% accuracy has direct practical value for serving cost. However, the benefit is hardware-dependent: on newer GPUs with higher ridge points (H200, B200), the feasible range for the ambient-tier head count shrinks, diminishing the method's leverage unless MTP is also deployed. Open questions include whether variable-size or work-stealing kernels can fully exploit the 2D sparsity pattern on heterogeneous hardware, and whether the two-tier approximation can be replaced by a continuous allocation without prohibitive kernel-launch overhead.
