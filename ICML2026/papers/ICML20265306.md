## Problem

Existing score-based diffusion models for speech enhancement treat spectrograms as generic 2D images with spatially uniform operations, ignoring their intrinsic anisotropy and non-uniform information density (dense low-frequency harmonics vs. sparse high-frequency transients). This fundamental oversight results in inefficient spectral representation and prohibitive computational complexity, making lightweight deployment impractical despite the quality gains of generative approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265306/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a degraded 16 kHz speech waveform, converted to a complex STFT spectrogram (512-point Hann window, 128-point hop) with power-law compression to handle heavy-tailed amplitudes. The predictive branch receives the full 3-channel degraded spectrogram (real, imaginary, magnitude), while the diffusion branch operates on the noise-perturbed magnitude. Output is an enhanced waveform reconstructed by fusing predictive and diffusion magnitude estimates (weighted by α=0.4) with the predictive branch's estimated phase, avoiding the need for degraded-phase inheritance.

## Method

DVPD is a parallel predictive-diffusion architecture that exploits the dual nature of spectrograms as both visual textures and physical frequency representations. The FANC encoder applies band-specific non-uniform compression (preserving 0–2 kHz, moderately compressing 2–4 kHz, heavily compressing >4 kHz) with heterogeneous dilated kernels (3×3, 3×5, 3×7) to create anisotropic receptive fields targeting horizontal harmonics and vertical transients. A 3-layer U-Net backbone integrates the LISA module (dynamic stripe convolution with multi-scale dilations) and Omni-Directional Attention for hierarchical feature extraction, while a Frequency-aware Interaction module adaptively gates cross-branch feature exchange using dual-path sigmoid gating. The diffusion branch uses the BBED SDE formulation (linear mean evolution reaching the degraded signal at T=1) with only 3 reverse Euler-Maruyama steps from a reduced starting time T_rs=0.12. At inference, the TLB strategy modulates backbone and skip-connection features with frequency-band-specific scaling factors (s, b) without any additional training, providing the largest gains on severely degraded samples.

## Results

On the WSJ0-UNI universal speech enhancement benchmark, DVPD (with TLB) achieves PESQ 3.15, ESTOI 0.92, CSIG 4.21, CBAK 3.01, and WV-MOS 4.27, outperforming the previous SOTA lightweight model PGUSE (PESQ 2.95, ESTOI 0.91, CSIG 4.01, WV-MOS 3.44) while using only 35% of its parameters (1.9M vs. 5.1M) and 40% of its inference MACs (10.2G vs. 26.3G). The predictive-only branch DVPD-P (0.61M params, 2.41G MACs) matches MP-SENet's PESQ (2.70 vs. 2.71) with one-third the parameters and an order-of-magnitude fewer MACs. On VBDMD denoising, DVPD reaches PESQ 3.35 and CSIG 4.73, approaching MP-SENet (PESQ 3.50) while surpassing all generative baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest lightweight competitor PGUSE (5.1M params, 26.3G MACs), DVPD (1.9M, 10.2G) wins on every metric except COVL (3.51 vs. 3.53, a negligible 0.02 gap), with the largest gains in CBAK (+0.40) and WV-MOS (+0.83). Compared to the heavier D+P hybrids StoRM (55.1M, 15.8T MACs) and UNIVERSE++ (42.9M, 42.8G MACs), DVPD achieves substantially better PESQ (3.15 vs. 2.75/2.66) and CBAK (3.01 vs. 2.66/2.60) with 29× and 4.2× fewer MACs respectively. Against pure diffusion models (CDiffuSE, SGMSE+, DOSE+), DVPD dominates across all metrics, e.g., PESQ 3.15 vs. 1.97/2.61/2.84, confirming the advantage of the predictive-diffusion hybrid over standalone diffusion. On the single-distortion VBDMD benchmark, DVPD (PESQ 3.35) trails the SOTA predictive model MP-SENet (PESQ 3.50) by 0.15, consistent with the paper's observation that predictive methods retain an edge when information loss is mild; however, DVPD still surpasses all generative baselines (PGUSE 3.11, FlowSE 3.12, UNIVERSE++ 3.03). On VBDMD-SR (bandwidth extension), DVPD leads PGUSE on PESQ (4.15 vs. 4.09) and ESTOI (0.96 vs. 0.94) while matching on CSIG (4.44 vs. 4.41), demonstrating strong generative capacity for ill-posed high-frequency reconstruction. The TLB transferability analysis (Table 6) further shows that applying TLB to PGUSE's U-Net variant boosts its PESQ from 2.82 to 3.02, confirming TLB as a general-purpose inference-time booster beyond DVPD itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

DVPD extends the predictive-diffusion hybrid paradigm established by PGUSE, StoRM, and UNIVERSE++, which combine a deterministic regression branch with a score-based diffusion branch to reduce sampling steps and suppress hallucinations. Its core departure is the 'dual-view' reframing of spectrograms: rather than treating them as spatially homogeneous 2D images (as in prior U-Net-based diffusion SE), DVPD explicitly exploits their anisotropic physical structure via the FANC encoder (non-uniform frequency-band compression) and the LISA module (anisotropic dynamic convolution for harmonic/transient features). The BBED SDE formulation from Lay et al. (2023) is adopted to resolve the prior mismatch of OUVE, and the training-free TLB inference strategy draws on the U-Net frequency-band dynamics observed in image denoising (Si et al., 2024) but applies it in a spectrogram-specific, band-partitioned manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 35% parameter and 40% MAC reduction relative to PGUSE while matching or exceeding its quality is practically significant for deploying generative SE on resource-constrained devices, and the TLB strategy's demonstrated transferability to StoRM and PGUSE suggests a broadly applicable inference-time technique for U-Net diffusion models. However, the TLB parameters require per-quality-tier tuning (even if selected via reference-free DNSMOS), and the efficiency gains are measured against a single lightweight baseline; whether the dual-view principle generalizes to non-U-Net backbones or to tasks beyond SE (e.g., separation, synthesis) remains open. The paper also acknowledges that in single-distortion denoising, predictive models still hold an edge, suggesting the generative branch's value is most pronounced under severe or multi-type degradation.
