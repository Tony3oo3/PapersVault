## Problem

Traditional multi-fidelity learning methods in AI4Chemistry rely on paired samples across fidelity levels, simple identifiable biases, or overlapping input distributions—assumptions that rarely hold in real-world scientific data. When these assumptions fail, directly using low-fidelity labels as approximate proxies for high-fidelity targets introduces misleading systematic constraints. The paper asks how to selectively extract trustworthy supervisory information from heterogeneous multi-fidelity data without requiring label comparability, sample pairing, or explicit bias models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unpaired multi-fidelity datasets where each sample carries a molecular/material descriptor (132-dim magpie features for bandgap tasks, or a molecular graph with Gaussian-expanded edge features for QM7b) plus a fidelity label indicating the computational method (e.g., HSE, GGA, PBE, SCAN, CCSD, MP2, HF). It outputs a predicted high-fidelity property value along with full uncertainty parameters (mean, variance, and degrees of freedom from a Student-t predictive distribution), enabling both point prediction and calibrated uncertainty quantification. No cross-fidelity sample pairing is required; the test set contains only highest-fidelity samples to evaluate prediction accuracy.

## Method

The core contribution is decomposing multi-fidelity supervisory signals into three complementary credible subsets—absolute value, interval, and ranking—each learned through a dedicated mechanism within a unified end-to-end framework. A fidelity-agnostic VAE extracts shared latent representations from inputs alone (independent of fidelity labels), preventing low-fidelity noise from leaking into the feature space. An evidential regressor (Student-t NLL) models the absolute credible subset with sample-weighted loss, while a separate interval encoder predicts a fidelity-dependent width τ that adaptively modulates the predictive uncertainty parameters (v and β), relaxing constraints for low-fidelity and tightening them for high-fidelity data. A rank-sensitive encoder with fidelity-aware attention produces a representation trained via pairwise ranking loss restricted to same-fidelity pairs, capturing relative ordering that remains reliable even under systematic bias. All three components are jointly optimized through a weighted multi-objective loss, with theoretical analysis showing that the ranking constraint provably reduces effective hypothesis class complexity and that the variational representation reduces bias propagation relative to direct learning.

## Results

On the TF-Bandgap (2-fidelity) dataset, the proposed Credible Subset Decomposition method achieves MAE 0.57±0.012, RMSE 0.78±0.018, and Kendall's τ_b 0.68±0.027, outperforming the best multi-fidelity baseline MFSE by 11.7% in MAE and 19.5% in RMSE. On the FF-Bandgap (5-fidelity) dataset, the method reaches MAE 0.50±0.007, RMSE 0.71±0.010, and τ_b 0.70±0.033, surpassing the second-best MFGNN (MAE 0.56, RMSE 0.83, τ_b 0.68). The method also generalizes to molecular property prediction on QM7b (3-fidelity), achieving MAE 10.63±0.61 and RMSE 14.86±0.75, and to drug-target affinity on Davis/CASF-2016 (MAE 1.42±0.13, RMSE 1.73±0.16, τ_b 0.78±0.008).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all baselines across every dataset and fidelity setting. On TF-Bandgap (2-fi), it beats the strongest multi-fidelity baseline MFSE by 11.7% in MAE (0.57 vs. 0.64) and 19.5% in RMSE (0.78 vs. 0.97), while also surpassing MFGNN (MAE 0.68) and DMMT (MAE 0.71). On FF-Bandgap (5-fi), the gap narrows but remains clear: MAE 0.50 vs. MFGNN's 0.56 and MFSE's 0.58, with a notable RMSE advantage (0.71 vs. 0.83 and 0.84 respectively). Against single-fidelity baselines (RFR, MLP, GIN), the multi-fidelity method achieves substantially lower errors, confirming that the credible subset decomposition effectively leverages additional fidelity levels. On QM7b (3-fi), the method achieves MAE 10.63 vs. MFGNN's 11.32 and MFSE's 11.45, and on Davis/CASF-2016 it reaches MAE 1.42 vs. MFSE's 1.55 and MFGNN's 1.60. Statistical significance testing (paired t-tests) confirms all improvements over the second-best baseline are significant (p < 10⁻³).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant multi-fidelity learning paradigm in AI4Chemistry—Δ-learning and its deep-learning descendants (MFSE, MFGNN, DMMT)—which either require paired cross-fidelity samples or treat low-fidelity labels as noisy proxies needing bias correction. Instead, it reframes the problem as selective extraction of trustworthy supervisory signals, decomposing multi-fidelity labels into three semantically distinct credible subsets (absolute, interval, ranking) learned through evidential regression, adaptive uncertainty modulation, and pairwise ranking loss respectively. The fidelity-agnostic VAE feature extractor draws on standard variational inference, while the evidential regression head and ranking loss are adapted from Amini et al. (2020) and Burges et al. (2005) into a unified multi-objective training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap: real scientific datasets often lack paired cross-fidelity samples and exhibit complex, source-dependent systematic biases that Δ-learning and feature-enhancement methods cannot handle. The credible-subset decomposition offers a principled way to exploit low-fidelity data without assuming label comparability, and the demonstrated robustness to fidelity-label mismatches (Table 12) suggests practical utility in noisy experimental pipelines. Open questions include sensitivity to large distributional shifts across fidelity sources (e.g., GLLB-SC in FF-Bandgap), the need for task-specific hyperparameter tuning, and whether the three-subset decomposition captures higher-order cross-fidelity correlations in strongly nonlinear systems.
