## Problem

Existing point-cloud registration methods face a trilemma: correspondence-based methods like ICP are fast but brittle to noise, outliers, and non-uniform sampling, and are non-differentiable due to discrete nearest-neighbor assignments; probabilistic methods like CPD and FilterReg improve robustness but incur higher per-iteration complexity. No prior approach simultaneously achieves linear scaling in point count, correspondence-free robustness, and differentiability suitable for embedding in end-to-end trainable pipelines.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source point cloud X of m points and a target point cloud Y of n points in R³, with no requirement for pre-established correspondences or uniform sampling density. It outputs a rigid transformation T ∈ SE(3) (rotation and translation) aligning X to Y. In the Neural MMD-Reg variant, the network additionally predicts an adaptive kernel length-scale ℓ and, under partial overlap, per-point sigmoid overlap weights for both clouds; the random Fourier feature dimension D (e.g., 32–16384) controls the accuracy–speed trade-off of the MMD approximation.

## Method

MMD-Reg formulates registration as minimization of a random-Fourier-feature surrogate of the Maximum Mean Discrepancy between the source and target point distributions, reducing the standard O(m²+n²) kernel computation to O(m+n) for fixed feature dimension D. The resulting objective is a smooth nonlinear least-squares problem over SE(3) parameters, solved with Levenberg-Marquardt, and is differentiable with respect to the input point clouds via the implicit function theorem (implemented through JAXopt). Neural MMD-Reg wraps this optimization layer inside a set transformer with alternating cross-attention blocks that predict an initial transformation, an adaptive kernel scale, and (in partial-overlap mode) pointwise overlap weights; the network is trained in supervised or self-supervised fashion with gradients backpropagated through the MMD-Reg layer. A coarse-to-fine strategy applies the solver sequentially with decreasing kernel scales ℓ to handle larger pose offsets.

## Results

On PCPNet (50k points per cloud), MMD-Reg with D=32 random Fourier features achieves RRE of 0.72–0.89° and RTE of 0.006–0.007 across high-noise, gradient-density, and striped-density conditions, reducing rotation error by 87–97% and translation error by 83–96% relative to the best non-MMD baselines (ICP Pt2Pl, GICP, FilterReg, GMMReg, SVR) while also running 18–90% faster on CPU and 90–97% faster on GPU. On Wild Places outdoor LiDAR, MMD-Reg reduces RRE by 61–85% and RTE by 40–77% over multi-scale ICP. On ModelNet40 clean registration, Neural MMD-Reg achieves RRE of 0.006–0.008° and RTE < 0.0001, outperforming all learning-based baselines including FGR (0.022°) and RPMNet++ (0.030°). On KITTI driving sequences, MMD-Reg is competitive but does not surpass point-to-plane ICP (RRE 0.049–0.066° vs. 0.073–0.099°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMD-Reg decisively outperforms all correspondence-based geometric baselines (ICP Pt2Pt, ICP Pt2Pl, GICP) across all three PCPNet conditions, with the best baseline (ICP Pt2Pl) achieving RRE of 6.2–15.5° versus MMD-Reg's 0.7–0.9°. Among probabilistic methods, FilterReg (5.8–13.0° RRE) and GMMReg (18.9–20.1°) are also substantially outperformed, while SVR (15.1–16.6°) and CPD (reported only for runtime, scaling poorly beyond a few thousand points) are similarly dominated. On Wild Places outdoor LiDAR, MMD-Reg reduces RRE by 61–85% over multi-scale ICP (e.g., 0.41° vs. 1.23° on sequence V-03), but on KITTI driving sequences it is slightly worse than point-to-plane ICP (0.073–0.099° vs. 0.049–0.066° RRE), suggesting the method's advantage is most pronounced in sparse, unstructured geometry. For the Neural MMD-Reg formulation on ModelNet40, the method achieves the best RRE in both clean (0.006–0.008°) and partial-overlap (1.292°) settings, beating FGR (0.022°), RPMNet++ (0.030°), GeoTransformer (0.634°/2.160°), and DMS (1.319°), with the refinement layer contributing a consistent 0.2–0.3° RRE improvement over the set-transformer prediction alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMD-Reg sits at the intersection of three established threads: the Maximum Mean Discrepancy as a non-parametric distributional distance (Gretton et al., 2012), random Fourier features for linear-scaling kernel approximation (Rahimi & Recht, 2007), and differentiable optimization layers via implicit differentiation (Blondel et al., 2022). It departs from correspondence-based methods (ICP, GICP) by eliminating nearest-neighbor matching entirely, and from probabilistic GMM-based methods (CPD, GMMReg, FilterReg) by avoiding any parametric density model, instead estimating the discrepancy directly from samples. The Neural MMD-Reg variant extends the set transformer architecture (Lee et al., 2019) with a novel cross-attention block for paired point clouds, positioning the MMD-Reg solver as a differentiable refinement layer rather than a standalone algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear-in-points scaling combined with differentiability makes MMD-Reg a practical building block for large-scale, end-to-end trainable registration pipelines (e.g., SLAM), where correspondence-based methods suffer from irregular memory access and non-differentiability. However, the method is explicitly local and non-convex, requiring either a coarse-to-fine scale schedule or a learned initializer for challenging pose offsets, and the KITTI results show it does not outperform point-to-plane ICP on structured driving scenes. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-rigid or partial-overlap registration without the supervised overlap-weighting mechanism, and whether the random-feature stochasticity can be controlled more tightly for safety-critical applications.
