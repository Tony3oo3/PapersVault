## Problem

GFlowNet-based LLM recommenders have been applied to improve diversity and mitigate popularity bias, but existing objectives like Sub-Trajectory Balance (SubTB) are derived under online-style assumptions with full transition support. In offline recommendation, the logged dataset only covers a subset of token-prefix transitions, making SubTB non-identifiable and allowing probability mass to leak to unsupported regions. This paper is the first to systematically formalize this offline non-identifiability and propose a principled fix.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user's textualized interaction history (sequence of previously interacted items with ratings) as prompt context and autoregressively generates the next item as a sequence of tokens from the LLM vocabulary. Critically, the dataset induces a token-prefix DAG where only transitions observed in logged data are considered supported; the forward policy is constrained to assign zero probability to unsupported token transitions at each prefix state, and training trajectories are sampled from this constrained subgraph rather than the full vocabulary space.

## Method

CFlower introduces a Conservative SubTB (CSubTB) objective that augments the standard SubTB loss with an explicit penalty on forward flow mass allocated to transitions absent from the logged dataset (Eq. 9), shrinking the null space of SubTB by making the optimization support-aware. The paper proves three independent invariance mechanisms—state-flow scaling ambiguity, local transition redistribution, and backward compensation (gauge symmetry)—that together establish SubTB's non-identifiability under partial support (Theorem 1). For training, CFlower employs constrained on-policy learning: a renormalized forward policy that zeros out unsupported transitions is used to dynamically sample trajectories from the dataset-induced DAG, replacing passive uniform off-policy replay and improving convergence while respecting offline constraints.

## Results

On Video Games and Movies and TV, CFlower achieves the best NDCG@5/HR@5 among all methods (0.0646/0.0841 and 0.1036/0.1254, respectively), outperforming the closest GFlowNet baseline Flower by ~18% and ~8% in NDCG. On CDs and Vinyl, CFlower (NDCG 0.0737, HR 0.0923) trails the accuracy-focused D3 baseline (0.0810/0.0996) but achieves substantially better distributional balance (DGU 0.071 vs. 0.353, H 8.132 vs. 7.641). Across all three datasets, CFlower consistently improves over Flower in both accuracy and exposure diversity (higher entropy H and tail coverage TTR), demonstrating a strictly better accuracy–exposure trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFlower outperforms Flower (the closest GFlowNet baseline) on all three datasets in both NDCG and HR: +18% NDCG on Video Games (0.0646 vs. 0.0546), +8% on Movies and TV (0.1036 vs. 0.0956), and +5% on CDs and Vinyl (0.0737 vs. 0.0702), while simultaneously achieving lower DGU and higher exposure entropy. Against the accuracy-focused D³ baseline, CFlower trades a small accuracy gap on CDs and Vinyl (NDCG 0.0737 vs. 0.0810) for dramatically better fairness (DGU 0.071 vs. 0.353, a 5× reduction in group-utility imbalance). CFlower dominates BIGRec and Temp across all datasets and metrics, with the largest margins on Video Games where BIGRec achieves only 0.0328 NDCG versus CFlower's 0.0646. Compared to the traditional SASRec backbone, CFlower matches or exceeds its accuracy on Video Games and Movies while delivering far superior distributional balance (e.g., DGU 0.106 vs. 0.169 on Video Games). In the RL fine-tuning setting (Table 3), CFlower as a reference policy yields the best S-DPO results on all three datasets (e.g., NDCG 0.0805 on CDs vs. 0.0776 for Flower-referenced), confirming its role as a more reliable starting point for downstream optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet-based LLM recommender line initiated by Flower (Gao et al., 2025) and the SubTB training objective (Madan et al., 2023) into the offline learning setting, where only logged transitions are available. It draws on the conservative flow regularization idea from COFlownet (Zhang et al., 2025) but applies it specifically to the token-prefix DAG structure of autoregressive item generation, where the tree topology eliminates the need for backward-policy regularization. The key departure from prior GFlowNet recommenders (e.g., GFlowGR) is a formal demonstration that SubTB is non-identifiable under partial support, motivated by the observation that most real-world recommender training is offline rather than simulator-based.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-proposition non-identifiability analysis (flow overestimation, forward mass leakage, backward compensation) provides a clean theoretical diagnosis of why naively porting online GFlowNet objectives to offline logs fails, which is practically important since offline is the dominant real-world training regime for recommenders. The CSubTB fix is lightweight and composable with existing RL fine-tuning pipelines, as shown by its role as a reference policy for S-DPO and PPO. Open questions include whether the conservative penalty generalizes to non-tree state spaces (e.g., listwise or set-based generation), how it behaves under severe catalog drift, and whether richer reward designs beyond item frequency can further improve the accuracy-exposure Pareto frontier.
