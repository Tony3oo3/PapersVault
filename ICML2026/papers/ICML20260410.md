## Problem

Standard masked autoregressive (MAR) models flatten 2D latent grids into 1D sequences with generic transformer attention, weakening spatial topology and making strict instance-level layout alignment unreliable—especially under dense, overlapping, or small-object layouts. Existing layout-conditioned generation methods treat bounding boxes as soft context (via concatenation or generic cross-attention), so the intended token-to-instance correspondence is diluted across layers rather than explicitly enforced during attention computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260410/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (encoded by a CLIP text encoder into token-level embeddings) and instance-level bounding boxes in normalized [0,1]² coordinates with category or text-tag labels (up to 16 instances per image) as input, and produces a 256×256 RGB image as output. Bounding boxes are mapped to the 16×16 VAE latent grid (using a frozen KL-VAE with 16× downsampling) to determine per-token box membership for the attention bias. On COCO-MIG, each instance additionally carries a color attribute (e.g., 'red cat') that is jointly evaluated for correctness.

## Method

StructMAR operates on a joint sequence [L; X] of layout tokens and continuous latent tokens within a MAR backbone, introducing three structural inductive biases: (1) 2D Rotary Positional Embeddings that encode relative horizontal and vertical offsets in query-key interactions to recover spatial topology lost by flattening; (2) a Layout-Guided Attention Bias implemented as block-wise logit injection into the X→L attention block, where a binary mask (0 for box-compatible pairs, −∞ otherwise) explicitly restricts which layout tokens each latent token can attend to, turning layout alignment into a hard structural constraint within attention rather than a soft learned correlation; and (3) a gated cross-attention branch for text semantics, initialized with gate ≈ 0 so that structural layout guidance dominates early training. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised MAR denoising, followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) that treats masked decoding trajectories as a stochastic policy and optimizes detector-based geometric rewards (IoU, confidence, centroid distance from a frozen Faster R-CNN) via critic-free group-relative advantages, aligning the generation policy with evaluation-time layout metrics.

## Results

On COCO-Position, StructMAR achieves state-of-the-art layout alignment with 57.20 AP and 79.40 mIoU, surpassing the strongest diffusion baseline MIGC (54.69 AP, 77.38 mIoU) by +2.51 AP and +2.02 mIoU while maintaining competitive image quality (FID-6K 24.70 vs. 24.52). On COCO-MIG, StructMAR achieves 61.70 average Instance Success Rate and 56.90 mIoU, outperforming MIGC (58.43 ISR, 51.48 mIoU) with a particularly large gap at the densest L6 setting (62.80% vs. 56.88% ISR). StructMAR also delivers a 4.05× inference speedup over MIGC (3.85s vs. 15.61s per image) due to lower per-step latency of masked autoregressive decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline MIGC, StructMAR wins on all spatial alignment metrics: +2.51 AP, +1.43 AP₅₀, and +2.02 mIoU on COCO-Position, while image quality is nearly matched (FID-6K 24.70 vs. 24.52; CLIP 24.60 vs. 24.66). On COCO-MIG, StructMAR surpasses MIGC in average ISR (+3.27) and mIoU (+5.42), with the gap widening at higher instance counts (L6: +5.92 ISR), and runs 4.05× faster (3.85s vs. 15.61s). Against GLIGEN, the margin is much larger: +16.52 AP and +7.79 mIoU on COCO-Position, and +29.31 average ISR on COCO-MIG. All earlier baselines (Stable Diffusion, TFLCG, Box-Diffusion, Multi Diffusion, Layout Diffusion) are outperformed by wide margins on every alignment metric, with Layout Diffusion being the closest among them at 23.45 AP versus StructMAR's 57.20. The only metric where StructMAR is marginally behind MIGC is CLIP score (24.60 vs. 24.66), a negligible 0.06-point difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

StructMAR builds on the continuous-latent MAR backbone (Li et al., 2024) and adapts 2D RoPE (Heo et al., 2024) to recover spatial topology lost by 1D flattening, while borrowing GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for critic-free metric-aligned fine-tuning. Its key conceptual departure is from the dominant diffusion-based layout-control paradigm (GLIGEN, MIGC, BoxDiff), where layout is injected as soft feature-level conditioning or generic cross-attention; instead, StructMAR enforces layout alignment as a hard structural constraint directly in attention logits via a binary X→L block mask. This positions the work at the intersection of masked autoregressive generation and explicit geometric inductive biases, rather than as another diffusion-based layout controller.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.05× inference speedup over MIGC makes strict layout alignment practical for iterative design workflows (UI, posters, multi-object compositing) where diffusion-based methods are too slow for rapid iteration. The finding that a simple binary attention mask in a single block provides the largest single ablation gain suggests the field may have over-invested in elaborate soft-conditioning mechanisms when hard structural constraints are more effective for geometric fidelity. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond axis-aligned bounding boxes to masks or free-form layouts, whether detector-based GRPO rewards introduce systematic biases toward geometrically regular placements, and whether the performance crossover (MIGC better on sparse layouts, StructMAR better on dense ones) reflects a fundamental trade-off between generative prior strength and structural rigidity.
