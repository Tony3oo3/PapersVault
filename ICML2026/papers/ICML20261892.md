## Problem

No single EEG foundation model (EFM) consistently dominates all downstream BCI tasks, yet fine-tuning every candidate model incurs prohibitive computational cost. Moreover, even task-level inferior models retain sample-level strengths due to diverse architectural inductive biases, but existing parameter-level and output-level fusion methods are incompatible with the structural heterogeneity among EFMs. The paper asks whether collective knowledge from heterogeneous EFMs can be fused at the representation level with sample-aware adaptivity and an optimal carrier selection mechanism.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes raw EEG signals resampled to 200 Hz and segmented into non-overlapping patches, which are simultaneously fed through M frozen source EFMs (LaBraM, CBraMod, CodeBrain) for knowledge extraction and through one trainable carrier for joint optimization. Per-channel physiological meta-features (10 values: mean, std, ZCR, five-band log power, two band-power ratios) are extracted from the raw signal to drive adaptive weighting. The output is a class label for the target BCI task (4-class motor imagery, 5-class emotion/sleep/speech, 2-class seizure/stress/attention, etc.), with the carrier model producing predictions via a standard task-specific head.

## Method

EmBrace formulates knowledge fusion as a representation-centric problem with three stages. First, multi-scale intermediate representations (patch-level embeddings and attention maps) are externalized from each frozen EFM and projected into a shared manifold via per-model MLP bridges (for embeddings) and a block-diagonal aligner (for attention), circumventing architectural heterogeneity. Second, a meta-guided fusor computes sample-dependent weights over the model pool by projecting 10-dimensional physiological meta-features through a learnable matrix and applying temperature-scaled softmax, then synthesizes a weighted knowledge reference per scale. Third, the optimal carrier is selected via Spearman rank correlation between the model's latent feature geometry (lt-RDM) and the label space on a stratified probe set, after which the carrier is jointly optimized with a combined objective: supervised task loss plus MSE fusion losses aligning the carrier's own externalized representations to the fused reference at both embedding and attention scales.

## Results

Across 12 EEG datasets covering 8 BCI paradigms, EmBrace achieves the best performance among all foundation models on 21 out of 24 reported metrics. On FACED (9-class emotion recognition), EmBrace reaches 0.5608 Kappa, surpassing the strongest EFM CodeBrain (0.5290) by 3.18 points. On BCIC2020-3 (imagined speech), EmBrace attains 0.5867 ACC-B versus CodeBrain's 0.5659, and on MentalArithmetic (stress detection) it achieves 0.8813 AUROC versus CodeBrain's 0.8487. Inference latency is reduced by up to 82.4% compared to deploying all three source EFMs independently.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmBrace outperforms all six foundation-model baselines (LaBraM, CBraMod, CodeBrain, BIOT) on the majority of metrics across all 8 paradigms, with the largest gains on FACED (+3.18 Kappa over CodeBrain) and MentalArithmetic (+3.26 AUROC over CodeBrain). Among task-specific models, EEGNet remains competitive on BCIC-IV-2A (0.5668 ACC-B vs. EmBrace 0.5047) and ATTENTION (0.8271 AUROC vs. 0.8031), while EEG-Deformer is close on Mumtaz2016 (0.9705 vs. 0.9798 AUROC). The remaining task-specific baselines (SERA, SPaRCNet, ContraWR, CNN-Transformer, FFCL, ST-Transformer) are consistently outperformed by EmBrace across all evaluated datasets, with gaps often exceeding 10–20 points in Kappa or AUROC. Notably, on CHB-MIT and Mumtaz2016, EmBrace does not always achieve the highest ACC-B among EFMs but attains the best probabilistic discrimination (AUC-PR and AUROC), indicating superior calibration for clinical binary tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of EEG foundation model research and knowledge fusion, departing from both parameter-level fusion (Model Soups, Wortsman et al. 2022) and output-level alignment (knowledge distillation, Moslemi et al. 2024) which are incompatible with architecturally heterogeneous EFMs. It builds directly on the lt-RDM feature-label compatibility criterion from Bolya et al. (2021) for carrier selection, and repurposes the intermediate representations of existing EFMs (LaBraM, CBraMod, CodeBrain) as frozen knowledge sources rather than independently fine-tuned backbones. The key conceptual departure is formulating fusion as a representation-centric problem in a unified manifold, guided by physiological meta-features (temporal-spectral-rhythmic priors) rather than purely data-driven weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in EFM deployment: the computational cost of fine-tuning all candidate models to identify the task-optimal one, while the observation that no single EFM dominates all tasks is well-supported across 12 datasets. However, the performance gains over the best individual EFM are modest (typically 2–3 percentage points in Kappa or ACC-B), and the model pool is limited to three architectures, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse pools. The efficiency argument (single deployable carrier vs. parallel deployment of all models) is compelling for practical BCI systems, but the 47–82% latency reduction is somewhat expected given that only one model is active at inference. An open question is whether the physiological meta-feature weighting provides genuine advantage over simpler learned weighting schemes, as the ablation evidence for this specific component is limited to visualization rather than controlled comparison.
