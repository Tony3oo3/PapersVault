## Problem

Diffusion LLMs offer structural advantages for global reasoning planning, yet verifying whether a generated reasoning trace is logically valid remains an open challenge. Existing verification methods (Process Reward Models, self-consistency) were designed for the autoregressive paradigm, treat the model as a black box, and fail to exploit the unique bidirectional noise-injection/denoising dynamics inherent to diffusion generation. The paper asks whether the dLLM's own generative process contains intrinsic geometric signals that correlate with solution correctness, eliminating the need for external annotators or expensive sampling ensembles.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fully generated reasoning sequence x_0 (typically 128–512 tokens) and a task query, then perturbs x_0 by replacing a fraction γ=0.9 of tokens with [MASK] and performs a truncated K=16-step backward reconstruction (versus T=1024 full generation steps). The output is a composite scalar BMC score computed from six weighted sub-metrics (token accuracy, semantic cosine similarity, number retention, final-answer match, character-level Levenshtein ratio, and intrinsic denoising confidence), which downstream serves as a validity discriminator (AUROC/AUPR), an adaptive acceptance threshold for resampling (MGRS), or a dense reward term in RL training.

## Method

BMC operationalizes the hypothesis that valid solutions are stable attractors on the high-density manifold of the learned distribution while erroneous paths exhibit off-manifold drift. Concretely, it performs a forward-masking perturbation (x_0 → x̃_t) followed by a truncated K-step denoising reconstruction (x̃_t → x̂_0), then measures the reconstruction discrepancy via a composite of six metrics spanning lexical, semantic, structural, and confidence levels. Theoretically, the paper proves BMC is equivalent to a reweighted ELBO estimator (Prop. 3.2), that f-divergence-based token metrics maximize the marginal ELBO over decision-critical positions (Prop. 3.3), that embedding-space similarity bounds the likelihood ratio under local Lipschitz continuity (Prop. 3.4), and that the reconstruction residual upper-bounds the true manifold distance via a contraction-mapping argument (Prop. 3.5). For inference, Manifold-Guided Rejection Sampling (MGRS) uses the BMC score as an adaptive early-exit criterion, concentrating compute on hard queries; for alignment, a gated reward r(x_0) = I(y_pred=y*)·[r_base + α_t·S_BMC] injects dense geometric guidance into RL (via the SPG framework) while preventing reinforcement of confident-but-wrong chains.

## Results

On error diagnosis, BMC achieves 0.893 AUROC on GSM8K and 0.678 AUROC on GPQA with LLaDA-8B, outperforming Self-Consistency by up to 13.9% on expert-level tasks. For adaptive self-correction, MGRS reaches 79.5% accuracy on GSM8K with a sample efficiency of 3.98 (vs. 1.86 for Self-Consistency), using only ~3.3 samples on average. In geometric alignment, the BMC-augmented RL reward yields 85.8% on GSM8K and 41.6% on MATH at 512 tokens, surpassing Outcome RL by +4.4% on MATH and matching strong AR baselines like Qwen2.5-7B (85.4% GSM8K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BMC consistently outperforms all three error-diagnosis baselines across all four benchmarks. Against Model Confidence (Kadavath et al.), BMC gains +19.6 AUROC on GPQA (0.678 vs 0.482) for LLaDA, where confidence-based scoring degrades to near-random. Against Self-Consistency (Wang et al.), BMC's advantage widens with task difficulty: +2.1% on GSM8K growing to +13.9% on GPQA, confirming that population-level agreement is unreliable when correct solutions are sparse. Against Self-Evaluation (Madaan et al.), BMC achieves substantially higher AUROC (0.893 vs 0.549 on GSM8K), showing that prompted self-assessment is far less discriminative than geometric stability. In the alignment setting, Geometric Alignment (BMC as dense reward) beats both SFT (+5.4% GSM8K, +6.8% MATH at 512 tokens) and Outcome RL (+2.3% GSM8K, +4.4% MATH), demonstrating that the dense geometric signal provides finer-grained guidance than sparse outcome-only rewards. On GPQA, the method reaches 34.4%, approaching Qwen2.5-7B (36.4%) while remaining a 7B dLLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the masked discrete diffusion framework of Austin et al. (2021), repurposing the forward-masking and backward-reconstruction cycle not for generation but as a verification probe. It departs from the dominant verification paradigm (Process Reward Models, self-consistency, execution-based checkers) by treating the dLLM's own bidirectional dynamics as an intrinsic validity signal, formalized through a geometric 'manifold attractor' lens. For the RL alignment component, it adopts the Sandwiched Policy Gradient (SPG) framework of Wang et al. (2025a) to handle intractable dLLM likelihoods, but augments the sparse outcome reward with a dense BMC-derived geometric signal. The paper positions itself as the first to systematically exploit dLLM-specific dynamics for self-verification, distinguishing itself from prior dLLM refinement methods (RemeDi, CDLM) that use diffusion dynamics for generative correction rather than explicit verification scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BMC offers a training-free, low-overhead verification mechanism (K=16 steps vs. T=1024 for full generation) that could become a standard diagnostic tool for dLLM deployments, particularly where ground-truth answers are unavailable. The geometric framing—validity as attractor stability on a learned manifold—provides a principled alternative to ad-hoc confidence scoring and opens a path toward self-improving dLLMs via dense geometric rewards. However, the contraction-mapping assumption (Proposition 3.5) is strong and unverified empirically; the method is tested only on 7–8B models across four benchmarks, and performance on Dream-7B is notably weaker than LLaDA-8B, raising questions about model-dependence. Open questions include whether the manifold hypothesis generalizes to non-mathematical open-ended reasoning, how sensitive BMC is to the choice of sentence-embedding model, and whether the approach scales to larger dLLMs where the learned distribution's geometry may be more complex.
