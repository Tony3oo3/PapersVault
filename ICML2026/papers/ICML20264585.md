## Problem

Existing diffusion-based inverse problem solvers enforce measurement or physics consistency through heuristic step-wise guidance, intermittent projections, or soft penalties whose strengths and schedules are tuned empirically, offering no guarantee that the final output satisfies the forward model. This is because standard score-based diffusion is an initial-value sampling process (noise → data), while physics-constrained inference is naturally a terminal-value problem where the feasibility requirement is specified at the data end. The paper identifies this mathematical mismatch as the root cause of physically inconsistent yet visually plausible hallucinations in high-stakes applications like medical imaging.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse CT sinogram y (30 views over 180° with Gaussian noise variance 10⁻⁵), a frozen pretrained latent diffusion prior (unconditional, never retrained on task data), and a terminal specification operator Ψ that encodes measurement feasibility. It produces a reconstructed CT image guaranteed to satisfy ‖A(x̂) − y‖ ≤ ε‖y‖ by construction, a recovered latent prior state Y₀ at a chosen noise level τ, and a family of conditional neighborhood samples (Y₀ + λ·ε) propagated forward through the frozen prior for uncertainty quantification. Critically, the measurement y never enters the SDE coefficients or score evaluation — it is consumed exclusively through the terminal boundary condition.

## Method

The core contribution is an 'associated BSDE' construction: given any frozen Score-SDE prior dX_t = b_θ(X_t,t)dt + σ(t)dW_t and a terminal specification ξ = Ψ(y), one defines a backward SDE Y_t = ξ + ∫_t^τ f_θ(s,X_s,Y_s,Z_s)ds − ∫_t^τ Z_s dW_s whose unique adapted solution (Y_t, Z_t) maps the terminal requirement to a prior anchor Y₀ at noise level τ. The key distinction from reverse-time SDE sampling is that the BSDE is posed as a terminal-value problem where Z_t is an unknown martingale integrand solved from the boundary condition, rather than a prescribed diffusion coefficient. Well-posedness (existence and uniqueness) is established under standard Lipschitz regularity of the score-induced driver via classical Pardoux–Peng theory. For practical solving, a neural BSDE solver parameterizes Z_t with a network Φ_β and optimizes (α, β) by minimizing a terminal measurement loss ‖A(D(X̃₀)) − y₀‖² through the discretized Euler–Maruyama recursion, keeping the prior entirely frozen. The recovered Y₀ then enables neighborhood sampling (Y₀ + λ·ε) propagated forward through the pretrained denoising recursion to generate diverse, distributionally consistent feasible outputs.

## Results

On the sparse-view CT (SVCT) reconstruction benchmark, the proposed BSDE method achieves the best MAE (3.57), SSIM (0.868), NSSIM (0.869), NMSE (0.0101), and NCC (0.989) among all compared methods, while remaining competitive in PSNR (30.99) and NPSNR (30.85). On star lightcurve conditional generation, BSDE Diffusion attains the lowest Jensen-Shannon divergence across all three stellar classes (Cepheid 0.3323, Eclipsing Binary 0.4223, RR Lyrae 0.3510) and the highest average cosine similarity for Cepheid (0.7095) and RR Lyrae (0.6838). The ablation confirms that the hard terminal constraint is essential: removing it drops PSNR from 35.36 to 17.38 and increases the measurement residual by two orders of magnitude.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SVCT reconstruction, BSDE outperforms all non-CT-specific-prior baselines across every reported metric: it reduces MAE from 4.56 (TVDIP) and 4.81 (TV) to 3.57, improves SSIM from 0.816 (TVDIP) to 0.868, and lowers NMSE from 0.0197 (TVDIP) to 0.0101. Against the diffusion-based baselines, BSDE substantially outperforms PnPDM (MAE 23.38→3.57, SSIM 0.551→0.868), DPS (MAE 9.84→3.57, SSIM 0.494→0.868), and SiTCoM (MAE 5.83→3.57, SSIM 0.695→0.868). The only method that edges out BSDE on pixel-wise distortion is DDS (PSNR 31.10 vs. 30.99, NPSNR 30.93 vs. 30.85), but the authors note DDS uses a CT-pretrained prior and is therefore not a fair comparison under the general-prior setting; BSDE still leads DDS on MAE, SSIM, NSSIM, NMSE, and NCC. On star lightcurve conditional generation, BSDE Diffusion achieves the best JSD for all three classes (Cepheid 0.3323, Eclipsing Binary 0.4223, RR Lyrae 0.3510) versus SDE Diffusion, GAN Inversion, and LSTMVAE, and the highest cosine similarity for Cepheid (0.7095) and RR Lyrae (0.6838), though it trails SDE Diffusion on Eclipsing Binary cosine similarity (0.4321 vs. 0.4648).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes physics-constrained inverse problem solving with diffusion priors as a backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE) terminal-value problem, departing from the dominant paradigm of step-wise guidance, intermittent projections, or soft penalties injected into the reverse-time SDE (as in DPS, PnPDM, SiTCoM). It builds on the Score-SDE formulation of diffusion models (Song et al., 2021) as the base prior and on classical BSDE well-posedness theory (Pardoux & Peng, 1990) to guarantee existence and uniqueness of the adapted solution, while adapting the deep BSDE solver of E et al. (2017) for neural terminal matching. Conceptually, it is closest in spirit to Schrödinger bridge and diffusion bridge methods that also condition on path properties, but differs by conditioning on a terminal feasibility event rather than a full path measure, and by keeping the pretrained prior entirely frozen and unconditional.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The terminal-consistency-by-construction guarantee is a meaningful theoretical advance over heuristic guidance schedules, and the framework is modular: any frozen score prior can be composed with an arbitrary terminal specification operator without conditional retraining. However, practical limitations temper the impact: the method is validated only on 2D sparse-view CT and toy datasets, the per-reconstruction optimization cost (~41 s) is non-trivial, the BSDE driver choice remains somewhat ad hoc, and the strongest quantitative comparison (vs. DDS) is acknowledged as not like-for-like due to a domain-matched prior. Open questions include scaling to 3D volumetric reconstruction, reducing solver cost, and whether the well-posedness assumptions hold for large-scale latent diffusion models in practice.
