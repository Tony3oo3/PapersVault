## Problem

While RLVR algorithm design has received substantial attention, the data-side decisions in competitive code generation—how to curate SFT data and how to schedule rollouts during RL—remain largely underexplored. Existing approaches either train on uniform difficulty distributions or apply simple filtration, failing to push the model's frontier on the most challenging problems. This paper systematically investigates data scheduling as the key lever for improving RLVR performance on competitive programming.

## Input / Output

The model takes competitive programming problem prompts (natural language descriptions with input/output specifications) and produces executable code solutions that are verified by running unit tests in a sandbox. A critical constraint is the max sequence length: the pipeline deliberately varies it between 24k (Stage 1 RL) and 32k (SFT and Stage 2 RL), and solutions must be both logically correct and computationally efficient within strict time limits while handling multiple edge cases, unlike single-instance math problems.

## Method

The pipeline consists of hardness-prioritized SFT followed by two-stage RL. For SFT, arena learning iteratively filters 1.27M distilled samples down to 470k hard prompts, and a 7B classifier then doubles the training weight of samples labeled 'hard.' In Stage 1 RL (entropy expansion), the model trains on 9k mixed-difficulty problems with max sequence length reduced to 24k and only 8 rollouts per prompt, which empirically expands token-selection entropy and suppresses repetitive generation patterns. In Stage 2 RL (progressive curriculum), the model trains exclusively on the hardest 175 LiveCode V6 samples with max sequence length restored to 32k and 80 rollouts per prompt, using a three-phase curriculum that progressively narrows to the top 72, 50, and 25 hardest samples over 128 total gradient steps. The key insight is that the shorter sequence length in Stage 1 acts as an entropy-expansion mechanism that prevents mode collapse, enabling the high-rollout exploration in Stage 2 to effectively improve performance on the hardest instances.

## Results

On the full pipeline (RL Stage 1 + Stage 2, 32k max sequence length), the model achieves Pass@1 of 0.699 on LiveCode 08-11, 0.697 on LiveCode V5, 0.703 on LiveCode V6, 0.653 on LeetCode Weekly, and 0.182 on Codeforces Weekly. Relative to the SFT-only model, the two-stage RL yields improvements of +16.1% to +58.3% across benchmarks, with the largest gain on the hardest benchmark (Codeforces Weekly, +58.3%). The method achieves state-of-the-art performance among 32B-parameter models on competitive code generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two external 32B baselines (QwQ-32B and OpenReasoning-Nemotron-32B) and two internal checkpoints (SFT-only and RL Stage 1 only). The full pipeline wins on all five benchmarks against both external baselines: it outperforms QwQ-32B by 12.1–58.3 percentage points (e.g., 0.699 vs 0.578 on LiveCode 08-11, 0.182 vs 0.124 on Codeforces Weekly) and OpenReasoning-Nemotron-32B by 7.6–50.0 percentage points (e.g., 0.703 vs 0.600 on LiveCode V6, 0.182 vs 0.132 on Codeforces Weekly). Against its own SFT model, the two-stage RL adds 16.1–58.3% relative improvement, with the hardest benchmark (Codeforces Weekly) benefiting most. RL Stage 1 alone improves on LiveCode benchmarks but slightly degrades on Codeforces Weekly (0.112 vs 0.115 SFT), confirming that the entropy-expansion stage alone is insufficient for hard problems and that Stage 2's large-rollout curriculum is essential. When scaled to a 406B MoE model (Table 5), the full pipeline reaches 0.737 on LiveCode V6 and 0.194 on Codeforces Weekly, approaching Seed1.6-0715 (0.770 / 0.188) and DeepSeek V3.1 (0.693 / 0.161) with far fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself explicitly against the dominant RLVR literature that has focused on algorithmic innovations (GRPO variants, PPO improvements, importance sampling corrections) while leaving data scheduling largely unaddressed. It adapts arena learning (Luo et al., 2024) for SFT data curation and draws on entropy-related analyses (Cheng et al., 2025; Cui et al., 2025) to motivate a practical entropy-expansion technique, but departs from prior data-selection work for RLHF (Gao et al., 2025; Shen et al., 2025) by applying these ideas specifically to RLVR for competitive programming. The two-stage RL design with progressive curriculum on hard samples is novel in its combination, though each component (curriculum learning, rollout scheduling) has precedent in adjacent settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, well-ablated recipe showing that data scheduling decisions (difficulty-prioritized SFT, entropy expansion via sequence-length truncation, large rollout counts on hard problems) can close much of the gap between 32B and 10×-larger models on competitive programming, suggesting that compute is better spent on data strategy than on raw scale. The finding that mixed-difficulty RL training causes performance collapse while hard-only training with positive transfer works is a useful caution for practitioners. Limitations include the high compute cost of 64–80 rollouts per prompt, the MoE results being under-converged, and evaluation limited to code generation; open questions include whether adaptive curriculum and efficient rollout techniques can reduce the compute overhead while preserving gains.
