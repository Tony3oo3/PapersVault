## Problem

Energy-based Transformers like ET guarantee convergence via monotone energy descent, but their trajectories are fully dictated by local energy geometry, making them vulnerable to spurious local minima under noisy or heterogeneous relational interactions. Classical Continuous Attractor Neural Networks offer biologically plausible excitation-inhibition dynamics that stabilize activity on manifolds, yet their continuous-attractor structure is incompatible with the discrete, topology-heterogeneous couplings present in real graph data. The paper asks whether one can inject CANN-inspired modulation into modern Hopfield-type attention to reshape attractor basins and expose a principled control interface beyond naive gradient descent.

## Input / Output

CDAT consumes a graph as an N×D token state matrix (node features) together with its relational topology, which defines a geodesic distance d(i,j) over feature dimensions to condition the coupling kernel. Inference proceeds by iterating a T-step Euler rollout of the continuous-time dynamics, after which a decoder maps the refined token matrix to either a graph-level class label (classification) or per-node anomaly scores (detection). The graph topology is not used as a message-passing adjacency but rather as a structural prior that shapes the feature-space coupling matrix W, distinguishing CDAT from standard GNN message passing.

## Method

CDAT formulates token refinement as a continuous-time ODE with four drift terms: passive decay, topology-conditioned mutual excitation (Wx, where W is a Gaussian heat-kernel prior plus a learnable residual, parameterized as symmetric low-rank W = P^T Q P for efficiency), global self-inhibition (−ωx modeling spike-frequency adaptation), and the negative gradient of a composite semantic energy. The energy decomposes into a Mo-vMF attention term (proven equivalent to negative log-likelihood of a spherical mixture model, driving coarse directional alignment) and a Hopfield refinement term (ReLU-based prototype retrieval for late-stage sharpening). A trajectory-wise storage functional certifies monotonic dissipation (dV/dt ≤ 0) under the LayerNorm Jacobian's PSD property, guaranteeing convergence to a stable invariant set. Euler discretization of the excitation term is shown to be isomorphic to a residual convolution on the feature manifold, unifying the update with modern deep architectures while the inhibition term acts as a pointwise stabilization that repels states from shallow traps.

## Results

On graph classification (Table 1), CDAT achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on 5 of 7 TUDataset benchmarks, with the largest gains over the closest energy-based baseline ET: +3.4% on NCI1 (93.5% vs. 90.1%), +3.8% on NCI109 (94.3% vs. 90.5%), +2.6% on DD (98.5% vs. 95.9%), and +2.2% on MUTAG (98.8% vs. 96.6%). On graph anomaly detection (Table 2), CDAT leads on most splits, notably surpassing UniGAD by 5.6% AUC on Yelp (70% split: 91.9% vs. 86.23%) and outperforming ET by 2.6–3.7% AUC in the challenging 1% labeled regime on Amazon and T-Finance. CDAT trails ET only on PROTEINS (87.1% vs. 90.3%) and ENZYMES (99.1% vs. 99.8%), where structural heterogeneity is lower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDAT outperforms the strongest energy-based baseline ET on 5 of 7 graph classification datasets (NCI1 +3.4%, NCI109 +3.8%, DD +2.6%, MUTAG +2.2%, MUTAGENICITY +0.1%) but trails ET on PROTEINS (87.1% vs. 90.3%) and ENZYMES (99.1% vs. 99.8%). Against classical pooling and GNN baselines, CDAT dominates: on DD it exceeds HGP-SL by >13% and ASAP by >21%; on NCI1 it surpasses GIN+GRANOLA by 9.5% and WKPI by 6.0%. In anomaly detection, CDAT beats ET by 2.6% AUC on Amazon (1% split) and 3.7% on T-Finance (1% split), and outperforms UniGAD by 5.6% AUC on Yelp (70% split). Compared to fraud-detection-specific baselines (CAREGNN, PC-GNN, BWGNN), CDAT consistently leads on MF1 and AUC across all three datasets and all training ratios, with the largest margin over BWGNN on T-Finance (1%: 95.9% vs. 91.1% AUC). The only notable loss is on Amazon 70% AUC where CDAT (93.0±5.0) falls below ET (97.1±2.1) and UniGAD (97.84), suggesting the iterative dynamics may be less beneficial when label supervision is abundant.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDAT extends the Energy Transformer (Hoover et al., 2023) paradigm of iterative energy-based inference by augmenting pure gradient descent with CANN-inspired excitation-inhibition dynamics (Amari, 1977) and a two-energy decomposition: a Mo-vMF attention energy for coarse directional alignment (building on the attention-as-Hopfield equivalence of Ramsauer et al., 2021) and a Hopfield refinement energy for prototype retrieval. The key departure from prior energy-based Transformers is the introduction of a topology-conditioned mutual-excitation operator with symmetric low-rank (LoRA-like) parameterization, which reshapes attractor basins rather than merely shaping the energy landscape. The dissipation certificate (Theorem 3.3) provides a formal convergence guarantee that prior iterative attention models lacked, while the Mo-vMF interpretation operationalizes the probabilistic reading of attention established by Schaeffer et al. (2023) into an explicit energy term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled control interface for attractor dynamics in energy-based Transformers, showing that excitation-inhibition modulation can escape spurious local minima that trap naive energy descent—a practical benefit validated across graph classification and anomaly detection benchmarks. The dissipation analysis and the finding that symmetric low-rank coupling (r=4) is critical (full-rank W collapses to 82.9% on NCI1) provide actionable guidance for designing stable iterative inference. Limitations include the iterative rollout cost (though comparable to ET), evaluation restricted to graph-structured data, and the fixed damping parameter ω; future work on state-dependent schedules, richer energy families, and deployment within larger hierarchical architectures could broaden applicability beyond the graph domain.
