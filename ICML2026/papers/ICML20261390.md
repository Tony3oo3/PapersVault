## Problem

Existing adaptive post-hoc calibration methods rely on indirectly learned proxies of sample difficulty (feature-space proximity, semantic grouping), and their standard NLL training objective can paradoxically worsen calibration. The paper identifies the logit margin as a direct, principled hardness indicator that provably constrains the feasible temperature range, and proves a fundamental mismatch between NLL minimization and calibration improvement.

## Input / Output

SMART consumes only the top-two logit margin (a single scalar per sample) from a frozen classifier's logits, requiring no access to intermediate features or full logit vectors. A 2-layer MLP with 49 trainable parameters maps this margin to a positive per-sample temperature, which rescales all logits before softmax to yield calibrated class probabilities. Because the temperature is a positive scalar, logit ordering is preserved and top-1 predictions remain unchanged, incurring only ~23 s total runtime on ImageNet/ResNet-50.

## Method

SMART learns a direct margin-to-temperature mapping via a lightweight 2-layer MLP (hidden dim 16, softplus output) trained on a validation set. The key theoretical contribution is proving that the logit margin m = z_max − z_2nd tightly bounds the feasible temperature interval for any target confidence, unlike the maximum logit which leaves T unbounded. Training uses Charbonnier-SoftECE, a smooth calibration objective that replaces the absolute-value gap in SoftECE with the Charbonnier function √(r²+δ²), provably upper-bounding the smooth calibration error (smCE) up to a kernel approximation term O(κ/√λ). This objective directly targets calibration rather than likelihood, resolving the demonstrated NLL-calibration mismatch where NLL can decrease while ECE increases. The method requires only 49 parameters regardless of class count, compared to K for CTS or 13K for spline calibration.

## Results

SMART achieves state-of-the-art ECE (%, 15 bins) across all six benchmarks and nine architectures. On ImageNet-1K, SMART reaches 0.46–0.71% ECE (e.g., 0.46% on Swin-B, 0.48% on ViT-B/16), substantially below the best baselines (ProCal 0.88–1.00%, Spline 0.62–0.91%). On the most challenging distribution-shift setting (ImageNet-Sketch), SMART attains 0.59–1.26% ECE while Feature Clipping degrades to 1.56–22.67% and ProCal to 4.44–12.93%. The method requires only 49 trainable parameters and adds ~23 s of calibration time on ImageNet (vs. 2.4 s for TS and 5,458 s for CTS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMART outperforms all eight post-hoc baselines (TS, PTS, CTS, Spline, GC, ProCal, FC, and Vanilla) on every dataset–model combination in Table 1. The largest margins appear under distribution shift: on ImageNet-Sketch/DenseNet-121, SMART achieves 0.59% ECE versus 22.67% for Feature Clipping and 12.93% for ProCal; on ImageNet-Sketch/ResNet-50, SMART (0.92%) beats the next-best CTS (1.48%) by 0.56 points. On standard ImageNet-1K, SMART consistently leads by 0.3–0.5 ECE points over the strongest baselines (e.g., 0.46% vs. ProCal 1.00% on Swin-B; 0.48% vs. ProCal 0.97% on ViT-B/16). On CIFAR-100, where Spline is the second-best method (3.48–3.76%), SMART (1.37–1.80%) nearly halves the ECE. Among training-time baselines (Table 3), SMART (0.48–0.98% on ImageNet/Sketch) also beats UWG, DFL, and MDCA without requiring classifier retraining, and its Charbonnier-SoftECE objective (Table 4) yields lower ECE than NLL, MSE, Brier, or plain SoftECE training objectives across both CNN and ViT backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMART extends Temperature Scaling (Guo et al., 2017) from a single global scalar to a sample-wise margin-to-temperature mapping, and builds on SoftECE (Karandikar et al., 2021) by adding Charbonnier smoothing to obtain a provable smCE upper bound (Blasiok et al., 2023). Unlike prior adaptive methods that rely on indirectly learned difficulty proxies—feature-space proximity (ProCal), semantic grouping (GC), or feature sparsity (LTS)—SMART uses the logit margin as a direct, closed-form hardness indicator with a formal temperature-feasibility bound. The paper also departs from the standard practice of optimizing NLL for post-hoc calibration by proving a fundamental NLL-calibration mismatch and replacing it with a calibration-centric objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proof that NLL minimization can actively worsen calibration (Proposition A.7) challenges a widely held assumption in the calibration literature and provides a principled justification for calibration-specific objectives over proper scoring rules in the post-hoc setting. SMART's 49-parameter architecture achieves state-of-the-art ECE across CNNs and ViTs on standard, long-tailed, and distribution-shifted benchmarks with minimal inference overhead, making it practical for safety-critical deployment. Open questions include whether the top-two margin remains sufficient when ambiguity is dispersed across many non-top classes, and whether the Charbonnier-SoftECE framework extends naturally to multi-label or structured prediction settings.
