## Problem

Faithful pruning methods for tree ensembles guarantee prediction equivalence over the entire input space, but this requirement forces preservation of fine-grained decision boundaries in out-of-distribution regions that are rarely observed, limiting compression. Accuracy-oriented pruning methods achieve higher compression but can change predictions even on in-distribution inputs, undermining decision consistency in high-stakes settings. The paper asks whether one can obtain a tunable, probabilistic prediction-equivalence guarantee restricted to a calibrated in-distribution region to improve the fidelity-compression trade-off.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained tree ensemble (XGBoost or Random Forest, M trees with fixed structure) along with its original non-negative weights, a fit set for estimating the Chow-Liu score, a calibration set for conformal threshold selection, and a user-specified miscoverage level α. It outputs a sparse weight vector over the same M trees (trees with zero weight are removed), preserving tree structures unchanged. The guarantee is probabilistic: with probability at least 1−α over a future input drawn from the same distribution, the pruned ensemble's prediction matches the original, rather than a universal guarantee over all inputs.

## Method

PINE builds on the FIPE Pruner-Oracle framework but restricts the Oracle's counterexample search to an in-distribution region X_ID(α) = {x : s(x) ≤ τ(α)}. The plausible score s(x) is the negative log-likelihood under a Chow-Liu tree fitted on the training data, which captures pairwise feature dependencies via a maximum spanning tree over mutual information. The threshold τ(α) is calibrated via split conformal prediction on a held-out calibration set, providing a distribution-free guarantee that P[X_new ∈ X_ID(α)] ≥ 1−α under exchangeability. The Chow-Liu score decomposes additively into a root-marginal term and per-edge conditional terms, enabling a compact MILP encoding with O(pB) bin-indicator variables and O(pB²) bin-pair variables (where B is the number of discretization bins per feature), far smaller than the exponential O(B^p) full space. A theoretical result shows the number of discrete states the Oracle must explore is upper-bounded by e^{τ(α)}, so increasing α monotonically shrinks the search space and enables higher compression while the conformal guarantee controls the fidelity loss.

## Results

Across 12 public tabular datasets, PINE increases mean pruning rate from 44.6% (α=0.05) to 67.8% (α=0.8) while mean fidelity remains between 99.96% and 99.15%, compared to FIPE's fixed 100% fidelity at lower compression. On Pima-Diabetes, PINE at α=0.8 achieves 55.3% pruning rate with 98.3% fidelity versus FIPE's 17.3% pruning rate at 100% fidelity. PINE attains 99.57% mean fidelity across all datasets, outperforming the best non-PINE baseline (cluster_centroids at 96.74%) by 2.83 percentage points. Empirical test coverage closely tracks the conformal target 1−α, validating the in-distribution region calibration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FIPE (the faithful pruning baseline guaranteeing equivalence over the entire input space), PINE consistently achieves higher pruning rates—up to 30% relative improvement—while maintaining fidelity within 1–2 percentage points; for example, on Pima-Diabetes PINE at α=0.8 prunes 55.3% of trees at 98.3% fidelity versus FIPE's 17.3% at 100%. Against the three accuracy-oriented tree-level baselines (IC, DREP, MDEP from PyPruning), PINE achieves substantially higher fidelity: PINE's mean fidelity is 99.57% versus 90.84% (IC), 95.41% (DREP), and 94.41% (MDEP), while these methods can still produce prediction disagreements even within PINE's calibrated in-distribution region. Against ForestPrune (leaf-level depth-cut pruning under an accuracy constraint), PINE achieves both higher leaf pruning rate (63.64% vs. 58.05%) and higher overall fidelity (99.42% vs. 96.65%) at depth 2, and maintains 100% in-distribution fidelity in all dataset-level comparisons. PINE also outperforms all 15 PyPruning baselines and three simple Top-K heuristics in mean fidelity, with the gap to the strongest non-PINE method (cluster_centroids) being 2.83 percentage points.

## Relation to Prior Work

PINE builds directly on the FIPE framework for faithful pruning of tree ensembles (Emine et al., 2025), which certifies prediction equivalence over the entire input space via an iterative Pruner-Oracle MILP loop. The key departure is restricting the equivalence guarantee to a calibrated in-distribution region defined by a Chow-Liu tree score and a split-conformal threshold, rather than the full input space. This positions PINE between two existing extremes: accuracy-oriented pruning (IC, DREP, MDEP) that tolerates prediction changes, and faithful pruning (FIPE, Born-Again Tree Ensembles) that forbids them everywhere. The conformal calibration mechanism is borrowed from the split-conformal prediction literature (Vovk et al., 2005; Angelopoulos & Bates, 2023) and applied here to a novel pruning context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PINE provides practitioners with a single interpretable parameter (α) to trade off compression against a probabilistic prediction-equivalence guarantee, which is valuable in high-stakes deployment where model outputs trigger downstream workflows, fairness audits, or recourse suggestions. The case study revealing that FIPE's compression is limited by logically infeasible feature combinations (e.g., 'Preschool' with 13.5 years of education) offers a concrete explanation for why the in-distribution relaxation helps. Key limitations include reliance on exchangeability (distribution shift weakens the guarantee), MILP scalability that degrades sharply with tree depth (runtime grows from ~4s at D=2 to ~933s at D=5), and the fact that the guarantee is only as strong as the solver's certified optimality. Open questions include adaptive recalibration under distribution shift and more efficient Oracle formulations for deeper or larger ensembles.
