## Problem

Warping-based Gaussian Splatting methods (e.g., IBGS) rely on uncertain 3DGS geometry to project source-view pixels into the target view, causing spatial misalignments that corrupt residual learning. Existing mitigations—hard visibility thresholding and naive mean aggregation—indiscriminately discard valid visual cues and dilute multi-view evidence, fundamentally capping the recovery of high-frequency details on thin structures and complex textures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262729/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unstructured source RGB images, a trained set of 3D Gaussian primitives (positions, opacities, SH coefficients, covariance matrices), and a target camera pose. It first renders a base 3DGS color C_base, then constructs warped images by projecting proxy surface intersections back into each source view. The final output is a novel-view RGB image computed as C_base plus a learned residual ΔC, where the residual is predicted by a lightweight CNN decoder conditioned on the target ray direction and base color. Deformable offsets are bounded to a local search radius of σ_max=7 pixels on the target-view plane, and the recurrent refinement runs for K=5 iterations.

## Method

GADA reframes geometric misalignment in warped images not as discardable noise but as locally preserved cues displaced by a bounded amount, enabling active retrieval via iterative deformable offsets. A shared encoder fuses the photometric residual (warped image minus base color) with a 4-D relative-pose vector into a state vector; a lightweight MLP then predicts incremental target-plane offsets (clamped by tanh and projected to stay within σ_max), which are accumulated over K=5 recurrent steps to resample the initial warped image at corrected coordinates. After refinement, a confidence network assigns softmax-normalized weights to each source view's feature, replacing naive mean aggregation with geometry-verified weighted fusion that suppresses occluded or misaligned evidence. An elastic regularization term (λ₂=0.01) penalizes large accumulated displacements to anchor the deformation to the proxy geometry, while the multi-view photometric consistency loss supervises the offset field to sample physically meaningful locations. By eliminating explicit depth-based visibility checks, GADA avoids on-the-fly depth rasterization, achieving 47 FPS (2.13× faster than IBGS) with lower peak VRAM.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, GADA achieves the best PSNR (28.62 dB), SSIM (0.840), and LPIPS (0.179) among all compared methods. On Tanks & Temples, it leads with PSNR 24.92 and LPIPS 0.144; on Deep Blending, it attains the best LPIPS (0.235) and SSIM (0.911) though Octree-GS edges it on PSNR (30.41 vs. 30.22). In terms of efficiency, GADA renders at 47 FPS on Mip-NeRF 360, more than 2× faster than the closest warping-based baseline IBGS (22 FPS), while using less peak VRAM (4.47 GB vs. 6.12 GB at inference).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary warping-based baseline IBGS, GADA consistently wins on all three metrics across all datasets: +0.33 dB PSNR and −0.012 LPIPS on Mip-NeRF 360, +0.17 dB PSNR and −0.010 LPIPS on Tanks & Temples, and +0.28 dB PSNR and −0.002 LPIPS on Deep Blending, while also rendering 2.1× faster (47 vs. 22 FPS). Compared to the strongest non-warping baseline 3DGS-MCMC, GADA gains +0.64 dB PSNR and −0.045 LPIPS on Mip-NeRF 360, and +0.63 dB PSNR on Tanks & Temples. On Deep Blending, Octree-GS narrowly leads in PSNR (30.41 vs. 30.22) and SSIM (0.913 vs. 0.911), but GADA achieves the best LPIPS (0.235 vs. 0.238), indicating superior perceptual fidelity. On the Shiny specular dataset, GADA outperforms both IBGS and Spec-Gaussian in PSNR (e.g., 35.93 vs. 35.78 vs. 30.62 on Guitars) and LPIPS, confirming robustness to view-dependent effects. GADA also surpasses all other 3DGS variants (2DGS, PGSR, Taming 3DGS, Mip-Splatting, Scaffold-GS, AbsGS) on PSNR and LPIPS across every benchmark, with the largest margins over 2DGS (+1.81 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360) and Taming 3DGS (+1.41 dB PSNR).

## Relation to Prior Work

GADA extends the warping-based Gaussian Splatting paradigm introduced by IBGS (Nguyen et al., 2026), which augments standard 3DGS rendering with image-space residuals derived from warped source views. The key conceptual departure is reinterpreting geometrically misaligned pixels—previously discarded by heuristic visibility thresholding—as locally displaced cues recoverable through iterative deformable offsets, rather than as irrecoverable outliers. For multi-view fusion, GADA replaces IBGS's naive mean aggregation with a confidence-weighted mechanism learned from post-correction features, and borrows the normal consistency loss from 2DGS (Huang et al., 2024) for geometric regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in real-time novel-view synthesis: warping-based methods lose high-frequency detail because geometric uncertainty in 3DGS causes spatial misalignments that existing pipelines handle by discarding pixels. GADA's 2.13× speedup over IBGS while simultaneously improving PSNR and LPIPS makes it a compelling option for VR/AR and digital-twin applications where both fidelity and frame rate matter. A clear limitation is dependence on sufficient view density—under sparse-view conditions (e.g., LLFF Horn), the warped evidence contains no valid cues and the method degrades gracefully but cannot recover detail. The authors suggest diffusion-prior-assisted supervision as a path forward for evidence-scarce regions, an open question that could broaden the method's applicability.
