## Problem

3D molecular generative models achieve high geometric fidelity but lack an explicit, fixed-dimensional representation space that supports both faithful reconstruction and semantically meaningful manipulation. MolFLAE introduced a fixed-dimensional E(3)-equivariant latent space enabling atom-count editing and interpolation, but its latent manifold was optimized solely for geometric reconstruction, leaving it semantically shallow and inadequate for property prediction or structure–property interpolation. The paper asks whether anchoring such equivariant latents with pretrained molecular semantics can yield a unified space that is simultaneously geometrically precise, semantically dense, and manipulable.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a centered 3D molecular structure (N_M atomic coordinates in R^{N_M×3} and one-hot species vectors in {0,1}^{N_M×K}) and produces a fixed-size latent of N_Z=8 virtual nodes, each carrying a 3D equivariant coordinate and a (D_Z + D_u')-dimensional invariant feature vector, independent of atom count. The BFN decoder maps this latent back to variable-sized atomic coordinates and discrete atom types. For unconditional generation, the latent diffusion model starts from Gaussian noise in the same fixed-dimensional space and iteratively denoises to recover anchored latents before BFN decoding. A lightweight task head on the anchored latent supports downstream property prediction without architectural changes.

## Method

MolAlign3D extends MolFLAE's virtual-node autoencoder by inserting a semantic anchoring module between the E(3)-equivariant encoder and the Bayesian Flow Network decoder. The encoder's invariant feature latents Z_h are affine moment-matched to the scale of a frozen pretrained molecular embedding (Uni-Mol), then concatenated with per-node segments of that embedding to form enriched features Ẑ_h, while the equivariant coordinate branch Z_x remains untouched. Training combines an asymmetric variational KL on the coordinate branch (with clamped posterior variance and a high-variance prior to encourage broad latent occupancy), a quadratic norm regularizer on Z_h, and a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss across multiple conformers of the same molecule to enforce conformation-invariant semantics. The BFN decoder is trained with per-step KL divergence between sender and receiver distributions over both coordinates and Gaussian-embedded atom types. For generation, a DDPM latent diffusion model with cosine schedule and continuous-time shift is trained on the anchored latent space, preserving E(3)-equivariance through an equivariant denoising network.

## Results

On GEOM-Drugs reconstruction, MolAlign3D achieves 80.79% MCS-IoU and 64.91% Shape Tanimoto, substantially outperforming MolFLAE (46.11% / 49.84%) and the Uni-Mol decoding baseline (34.35% / 26.86%). For unconditional generation, MolAlign3D attains 87.2% Atom Stability and drug-likeness metrics closely matching the data distribution (QED 0.64 vs. 0.64, LogP 2.96 vs. 2.99), surpassing EDM, GeoLDM, and UniGEM. On MoleculeNet property prediction, MolAlign3D matches or exceeds Uni-Mol across all eight tasks (e.g., 93.7% ROC-AUC on ClinTox vs. 91.9% for Uni-Mol) while consistently outperforming MolFLAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MolAlign3D decisively outperforms its direct predecessor MolFLAE across every evaluation axis: reconstruction (MCS-IoU 80.79% vs. 46.11%), unconditional generation (Atom Stability 87.2% vs. 86.6%, QED 0.64 vs. 0.60, LogP 2.96 vs. 1.38), property prediction (e.g., ClinTox 93.7% vs. 75.2% ROC-AUC), and zero-shot editing (MCS-IoU at +1 atom: 78.45% vs. 74.28%). Against the Uni-Mol decoding baseline, MolAlign3D achieves a 46.4-point MCS-IoU advantage (80.79% vs. 34.35%), demonstrating that pretrained semantics alone without a geometry-aware equivariant latent are insufficient for 3D reconstruction. In unconditional generation, MolAlign3D surpasses EDM (Atom Stability 87.2% vs. 81.4%, QED 0.64 vs. 0.36), GeoLDM (87.2% vs. 84.5%, QED 0.64 vs. 0.40), and UniGEM (87.2% vs. 85.0%, QED 0.64 vs. 0.36), while matching GeoBFN on Atom Stability (87.2% vs. 86.2%) though GeoBFN does not report drug-likeness metrics. On MoleculeNet, MolAlign3D is competitive with Uni-Mol (e.g., BBBP 72.7% vs. 72.9%, BACE 85.6% vs. 85.7%) and clearly beats all supervised and pretraining baselines including D-MPNN, AttentiveFP, PretrainGNN, GraphMVP, MolCLR, and GEM on most tasks, with the largest margins on ClinTox (93.7% vs. 91.2% for MolCLR) and FreeSolv (RMSE 1.445 vs. 1.480 for Uni-Mol).

## Relation to Prior Work

MolAlign3D directly extends MolFLAE (Chen et al., 2025b), which first mapped variable-sized molecules into a fixed-dimensional E(3)-equivariant latent space using virtual nodes and a Bayesian Flow Network decoder. The key departure is anchoring MolFLAE's reconstruction-oriented feature latents with global embeddings from a frozen pretrained molecular encoder (Uni-Mol) via affine moment matching and per-node concatenation, drawing a conceptual parallel to RAE and SVG in computer vision that replace or regularize VAE latents with self-supervised features. The paper also introduces multi-conformer contrastive regularization to enforce semantic consistency across 3D realizations of the same molecule, a component absent from both MolFLAE and the vision analogues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying geometric fidelity and semantic depth in a single fixed-dimensional equivariant latent space, MolAlign3D offers a practical substrate for both generative and discriminative molecular modeling without requiring separate architectures. The substantial reconstruction gain (MCS-IoU 46→81%) over MolFLAE demonstrates that even a simple moment-matched concatenation of pretrained features can dramatically regularize an equivariant latent manifold. However, editing experiments remain zero-shot and unconditioned, the disentanglement gains between geometric and semantic branches are modest, and the approach inherits its semantic ceiling from the frozen pretrained encoder—extending to pocket-aware or property-conditioned design and achieving cleaner geometry-semantics separation are open directions.
