## Problem

Current LLM safety alignment remains vulnerable to persona-based jailbreak attacks, where wrapping harmful intent in a role-playing persona bypasses safety guardrails that successfully refuse the same intent in direct form. Existing defenses lack mechanistic constraints specifically targeting persona-induced distributional shifts, and prior persona-based attack research focuses on attack iterations without systemic defense-side analysis. The paper formalizes the structural separation hypothesis: for a robustly aligned model, safety-critical decisions should be structurally independent of persona context.

## Input / Output

The attack module (PLE) consumes harmful instruction sets (JBB-Behaviors, PKU-SafeRLHF-unsafe) and evolves persona prompts via LLM-based mutation and crossover (Qwen3-Max), producing elite adversarial personas evaluated against target models with a WildGuard safety judge. The defense module (PICL) ingests the resulting persona-based jailbreak samples to construct 10k persona-based DPO pairs, 10k standard DPO pairs, and 15k SFT samples, yielding a QLoRA-finetuned policy. At inference, the input template structurally separates persona and query as 'persona: {persona_text} question: {question_text}', and the output is a safe text response that should be invariant to the persona context.

## Method

PIA is an adversarial self-play framework with two co-evolving modules over S rounds. On the attack side, PLE models persona search as a directed acyclic graph and introduces lineage-based credit propagation—propagating ASR success counts from descendant nodes back to ancestors with exponential decay (γ=0.8)—combined with a UCB exploration bonus to overcome the 'lineage amnesia' of flat genetic algorithms. On the defense side, PICL jointly optimizes DPO, SFT, and a novel Persona-Invariant Consistency (PIC) loss that applies forward KL divergence to align the persona-conditioned output distribution toward a stop-gradient persona-free teacher distribution. The theoretical grounding is a variational upper bound on the conditional mutual information I(Y;P|q), showing that minimizing this KL constraint reduces the dependence of safety decisions on persona context. Forward KL (rather than reverse) is deliberately chosen to ensure persona-based safe behavior covers the full semantic support of the unperturbed distribution, preventing mode collapse onto a single refusal pattern.

## Results

On the defense side, PICL reduces the average ASR against OOD persona-based jailbreak attacks to 0.054 on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (from 0.601 base) and 0.052 on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (from 0.302 base), substantially outperforming all baselines. On the attack side, PLE achieves near-saturation ASR of 0.928 on SafeRLHF-unsafe and 0.990 on MaliciousInstruct for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, consistently exceeding Persona-GA (0.821 and 0.900 respectively). PICL preserves general capability with negligible degradation (e.g., MMLU 0.743 vs. 0.742 base on Qwen2.5) while keeping over-refusal rates close to the base model (e.g., 0.105 vs. 0.055 on TrustLLM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Persona-GA (Zhang et al., 2025b), PLE achieves higher ASR across all OOD benchmarks on both models (e.g., 0.928 vs. 0.821 on SafeRLHF-unsafe, 0.990 vs. 0.900 on MaliciousInstruct for Qwen2.5), with faster convergence (near-0.9 ASR by generation 10 vs. >25 for Persona-GA). On the defense side, PICL dominates all five baselines: it reduces average ASR from 0.447 (DPO) and 0.340 (Self-RedTeam) to 0.054 on Qwen2.5, and from 0.152 (DPO) and 0.207 (Self-RedTeam) to 0.052 on Llama-3.1. SFT achieves the second-best average ASR (0.122 on Qwen2.5, 0.075 on Llama-3.1) but induces severe over-refusal (e.g., 0.430 RtA on JBB-Benign vs. 0.160 for PICL). SmoothLLM and LLM self-eval provide only moderate gains (avg. ASR 0.336 and 0.217 on Qwen2.5), remaining far above PICL's 0.054. PICL's advantage is most pronounced on StrongREJECT (0.026 vs. 0.556 for DPO) and MaliciousInstruct (0.010 vs. 0.730 for DPO), confirming that persona-invariant consistency specifically targets the structural vulnerability exploited by persona-based attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the persona-based jailbreak attack line initiated by Zhang et al. (2025b), who used a flat genetic algorithm to evolve adversarial personas, by introducing lineage-based credit propagation and UCB-based exploration to overcome what the authors term 'evolutionary amnesia.' On the defense side, it builds on the adversarial self-play paradigm (SEAS, Self-RedTeam, STAIR, MAGIC) but departs from those methods by targeting the specific distributional shift induced by persona context rather than instruction-level perturbations. The theoretical contribution—the structural separation hypothesis and its information-theoretic grounding via conditional mutual information—is novel, providing a principled variational upper bound that motivates the forward-KL consistency loss (PICL) as a complement to standard DPO and SFT objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structural separation hypothesis offers a clean, testable theoretical target for safety alignment: safety decisions should be invariant to persona perturbations. The near-zero ASR (5.4% average) achieved by PICL while preserving general capability and role-playing ability is practically significant for deploying LLMs in high-stakes settings where persona-based social engineering is a realistic threat. Open questions include whether the structural separation principle generalizes to other contextual perturbations (multi-turn manipulation, multi-lingual shifts, agent-based attacks), whether the defense degrades under adaptive attacks that co-evolve with the defender, and whether the forward-KL consistency constraint can be extended to multi-turn or multi-agent settings where persona context accumulates over dialogue.
