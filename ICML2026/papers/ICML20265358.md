## Problem

Representation intervention methods for LLM safety alignment (e.g., ReFT, LoFiT) assume harmful concepts reside in linear subspaces of the activation space, but the paper proves that when harmful and benign concepts are non-linearly entangled, perfect concept erasure is fundamentally impossible without distorting benign information (Theorem 2.2). This faithfulness gap explains why existing interventions fail against out-of-distribution jailbreak attacks despite exhaustive tuning of intervention positions across layers and token locations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes mixed safety training data (10K illegal instructions from Beavertails and 60K benign instructions from Evol-Instruct at a 6:1 ratio) and a teacher model (GPT-4o or the base model itself) to generate structured COCA-annotated responses. At inference, the fine-tuned model produces a multi-stage structured trace—reasoning in <think>, concept listing in <concept>, safety gating in <check>, erasure decision in <erase unsafe concepts>, and final output in <response>—followed by either a firm refusal or a benign completion, while a frozen ReFT or LoFiT adapter edits hidden states on every forward pass.

## Method

COCA is a data-level linearization strategy that refactors safety training examples with explicit concept-reasoning annotations, forcing the model to identify and label harmful concepts before generating a response. The key theoretical result (Corollary 2.3) shows that at a stationary point of the COCA training objective, the covariance between the concentrated representation and the harmful label is proportional to a single direction vector, effectively projecting non-linearly entangled concepts into a linear subspace. This geometric simplification circumvents the impossibility of Theorem 2.2, after which standard parameter-efficient interventions (ReFT or LoFiT adapters trained only on the COCA-structured data) achieve near-perfect linear erasure with minimal distortion to benign capabilities. The approach is complementary to prior reasoning-based safety methods: rather than using chain-of-thought for free-form introspection, COCA uses structured reasoning as a geometric regularizer that reshapes the representation space to make downstream intervention faithful.

## Results

On LLaMA-3.1-8B, COCA-enhanced LoFiT reduces average OOD jailbreak attack success rate from 44.9% (vanilla LoFiT) to 10.4%, achieving 0.0% on in-distribution attacks (HarmBench, WildChat). On Qwen-2.5-7B, the same improvement brings average jailbreak ASR from 40.0% to 16.9%. Helpfulness is preserved or slightly improved: enhanced LoFiT scores 44.3% average on math/coding benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, MATHQA, HumanEval, MBPP) versus 43.6% for vanilla LoFiT, while training-free ACE collapses to 7.3% average utility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COCA+LoFiT (10.4% avg jailbreak ASR on LLaMA-3.1-8B) substantially outperforms vanilla LoFiT (44.9%) and vanilla ReFT (44.3%), and also beats reasoning-based safety methods SRG (17.1%) and STAIR (11.5%). Against the strongest baseline, CircuitBreaker/RR (7.8% avg), COCA+LoFiT is slightly higher in ASR but avoids RR's critical failure mode: RR produces 39–100% abnormal outputs (repeated tokens, formatting collapse) across attack datasets, whereas COCA+LoFiT preserves coherent refusals. Training-free methods CAST (40.5%) and ACE (9.3%) are both inferior: CAST is barely better than no intervention, and ACE, while achieving low ASR, catastrophically degrades utility to 7.3% average on math/coding benchmarks versus 44.3% for COCA+LoFiT. Compared to proprietary models, COCA-enhanced LLaMA-3.1-8B (10.4% avg) matches Claude-3.7-Sonnet (11.2%) and outperforms GPT-4o (16.4%), Gemini-1.5-Pro (24.6%), and DeepSeek-R1 (30.4%). A self-generated variant (no GPT-4o teacher) still achieves 13.2% avg, confirming the method does not require a strong external annotator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the linear concept erasure framework of LEACE (Belrose et al., 2023) and the representation intervention methods ReFT (Wu et al., 2024) and LoFiT (Yin et al., 2024), which assume harmful concepts reside in linearly separable subspaces of activation space. It departs from this lineage by proving (Theorem 2.2) that perfect erasure is fundamentally infeasible when harmful and benign concepts are non-linearly entangled, and by proposing a data-level solution (COCA) that reshapes the representation geometry before intervention rather than searching for better intervention positions. Unlike prior reasoning-based safety approaches (SRG, STAIR) that elicit free-form chain-of-thought for introspection, COCA uses structured reasoning tags as a geometric regularizer specifically designed to concentrate harmful concepts into a linear subspace, making standard ReFT/LoFiT adapters effective again.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility theorem provides a clean theoretical explanation for why representation intervention methods fail against OOD jailbreaks despite exhaustive position tuning, shifting the community's focus from 'where to intervene' to 'how to simplify the space before intervening.' The practical impact is significant: COCA-LoFiT on LLaMA-3.1-8B achieves 10.4% average OOD jailbreak ASR, comparable to GPT-4o (16.4%) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (11.2%), while preserving or improving math/coding utility. Key limitations include residual vulnerability to Code Attack (where harmful intent is obfuscated inside program structures), reliance on structured annotation (though a self-generated variant narrows the gap to 2.8%), and evaluation only up to 14B models. Open questions include robustness to adaptive attackers who forge benign <concept> content, scaling to 70B+ models, and whether the linearization benefit persists under distribution shift in the safety taxonomy itself.
