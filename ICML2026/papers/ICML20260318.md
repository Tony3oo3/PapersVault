## Problem

Prior work has shown that chain-of-thought (CoT) traces can be unfaithful to a model's internal reasoning, but it remains unclear *when* and *why* this unfaithfulness arises. This paper asks whether reasoning models can be strongly confident in their final answer well before that confidence is verbalized in the CoT, and whether this 'performative reasoning' is a uniform phenomenon or depends on task difficulty and model size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260318/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes residual-stream activations from every transformer layer at every token position during CoT generation, paired with a four-way multiple-choice question (A–D). At inference, the probe is evaluated at every prefix position, yielding a probability distribution over the four answer choices that evolves throughout the trace. This signal is compared against two external baselines: forced-answering logits (obtained by injecting a completion prompt mid-CoT) and a CoT monitor (Gemini-2.5-Flash) that reads the emitted text and outputs either a predicted choice or 'N/A'. The combined outputs support three downstream uses: tracking belief evolution, classifying reasoning as performative vs. genuine, and triggering confidence-threshold early exit.

## Method

The core tool is an attention probe (Kantamneni et al., 2025) that applies a learned attention-weighted pooling over a single transformer layer's hidden states, followed by a linear 4-class head predicting the final answer. During training, random prefixes of the full CoT sequence are sampled so the probe learns to decode the answer independent of sequence length; one probe is trained per layer and the best-performing layer is selected. This attention-based pooling is critical: linear probes on individual token activations perform near chance because the belief state updates dynamically at specific tokens, whereas attention pooling lets the probe weight relevant tokens across the sequence. The paper then compares probe accuracy against forced answering and a CoT monitor across sequence position on MMLU-Redux (easier, recall-heavy) and GPQA-Diamond (harder, multi-hop), quantifying the 'performativity rate' as the difference in accuracy slopes between internal and external signals. Probes trained on MMLU transfer directly to GPQA-Diamond without fine-tuning, and their well-calibrated confidence scores enable early exit that saves up to 80% of tokens on MMLU and 30% on GPQA-Diamond with comparable accuracy.

## Results

The paper quantifies performative CoT via a performativity rate (difference in accuracy slope between internal probes and a CoT monitor). On MMLU-Redux, DeepSeek-R1 shows a performativity rate of 0.417 (probe) and 0.505 (forced answer), while GPQA-Diamond drops to 0.012 and 0.010, indicating near-genuine reasoning on harder tasks. Attention probes achieve 87.98% macro accuracy on DeepSeek-R1 MMLU (vs. 31.85% for linear probes), and are well-calibrated enough to enable early exit that saves 68% of tokens on MMLU and 50% on GPQA-Diamond while retaining 97% accuracy. Inflection points (backtracking, 'aha' moments) occur 2.25× more frequently in low-confidence responses (0.045/step) than high-confidence ones (0.020/step), confirming they track genuine uncertainty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three early-decoding methods: attention probes (proposed), forced answering (Lanham et al., 2023), and a CoT monitor (Korbak et al., 2025, using Gemini-2.5-Flash). On MMLU-Redux, both attention probes and forced answering reach high accuracy far earlier in the CoT than the CoT monitor, with performativity gaps of 0.417–0.505 (DeepSeek-R1) and 0.334–0.435 (GPT-OSS), demonstrating that the model internally knows its answer before verbalizing it. On GPQA-Diamond, all three methods rise at similar rates (gaps of only 0.010–0.012 for DeepSeek-R1), indicating the CoT is genuinely necessary and the monitor keeps pace with internal belief. Attention probes substantially outperform linear probes (87.98% vs. 31.85% macro accuracy on DeepSeek-R1 MMLU), confirming that sequence-level pooling is essential for tracking dynamic belief states. For early exit, the calibrated attention probe achieves 97% accuracy at 95% confidence threshold while saving 68% of tokens on MMLU, a practical advantage over the CoT monitor which cannot signal exit without reading the full trace.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention probe methodology of Kantamneni et al. (2025) from single-token or layer-wise probing to sequence-level belief tracking during chain-of-thought generation, enabling continuous monitoring of when a model internally commits to an answer. It builds on the CoT faithfulness literature (Turpin et al. 2023; Lanham et al. 2023; Arcuschin et al. 2025) and the performative CoT framing of Palod et al. (2025) by introducing a difficulty- and model-size-dependent quantification of the gap between internal belief and verbalized reasoning. The Gricean cooperative communication lens is a novel conceptual framing that positions CoT monitors as 'cooperative listeners' facing non-cooperative speakers, connecting faithfulness failures to the model's RL training objective rather than treating them as purely representational issues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical early-exit result (80% token savings on MMLU, 30% on GPQA-D with comparable accuracy) offers a concrete efficiency tool for deploying reasoning models, while the safety implication—that CoT monitors systematically lag behind internal belief on easier tasks—argues for activation-based monitoring as a complement to text-based approaches. Key open questions remain: the inconsistent relationship between verbalized inflection points and internal belief shifts (mixed across models, datasets, and window sizes) suggests the mapping from internal computation to verbalized reasoning is more complex than local co-occurrence captures. The Gricean framing, while illuminating, is underdeveloped and would benefit from formalization or training-objective interventions to test whether incentivizing cooperative communication improves faithfulness.
