## Problem

Existing safety mechanisms for text-to-image diffusion models exhibit significant cross-seed instability: a single malicious prompt can produce harmful outputs under some random seeds but not others, and current defenses fail to consistently suppress all harmful variants. The paper further identifies two fundamental algorithmic flaws in directly extending Noise Contrastive Alignment (NCA) to diffusion models—a gradient reversal pathology in the positive-sample term that paradoxically penalizes safe generation, and a uniform suppression strategy that ignores severity differences among harmful samples.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a text prompt and produces a generated image (512×512 for SD-v1.5/v2.1, 1024×1024 for SDXL) that is safe across all random seeds. During training, the method consumes five-tuples of (harmful prompt, one safe image, three harmful images) where the safe image is produced via safety-aware inpainting of a harmful seed-4 output, and the three harmful images are generated from the same prompt with seeds 1–3. The NCD-10K dataset (10K entries across 7 harmful categories) is constructed from DiffusionDB prompts filtered by GPT-4 and images generated with SDXL.

## Method

NCD (Noise Contrastive Diffusion) adapts NCA to diffusion models with two targeted modifications. First, it eliminates the positive-sample contrastive term (1/N log σ(−R(x_t^w))) from the NCA objective, which Theorem 3.1 proves causes gradient reversal when the safe-sample reward exceeds N/(N+1), thereby ensuring consistent gradient direction for safe content throughout training. Second, it introduces a pairwise preference loss L_pair that computes individualized comparisons between the safe sample and each harmful sample via log σ(R(x_t^w) − R(x_t^{l_i})), enabling adaptive suppression intensity proportional to relative harmfulness rather than uniform 1/N weighting. The final objective L_NCD = L_mod + λ·L_pair is optimized step-wise over diffusion timesteps using the DDPM noise-prediction parameterization, with the implicit reward R_θ(c, x_t) = K·(L_diff(ε_ref) − L_diff(ε_θ)). Training is performed via LoRA fine-tuning (rank 32) on SD-v1.5, SD-v2.1, and SDXL, and the framework generalizes to transformer-based architectures (SD3, FLUX) with rank-8 LoRA.

## Results

On SD-v1.5, NCD achieves 6.2% SSR-10 and 0.8% ASR on I2P-Sexual, reducing the previous best (RECE: 11.1%/2.0%) by nearly half. On adversarial jailbreak benchmarks, NCD reaches 5.0% SSR-10 on Sneaky-Prompt (vs. AlignGuard's 10.5%) and 20.0% SSR-10 on MMA-Diffusion (vs. AlignGuard's 25.0%). Generation quality is preserved or improved: NCD attains CLIP-Score 26.39 and FID 19.85 on COCO-30K, outperforming AlignGuard (25.84/22.90) and matching training-free SLD-MAX (25.78/21.28) while providing far stronger safety.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCD outperforms all 12 baselines across every safety metric on SD-v1.5. Against concept-erasure methods (ESD-u, CA, UCE), NCD reduces SSR-10 on I2P-Sexual from 24.5–25.8% to 6.2%, a 4× improvement, while also matching or exceeding their generation quality. Against model-editing methods (RECE, UCE), NCD nearly halves SSR-10 on I2P-Sexual (6.2% vs. 11.1% for RECE) and dramatically improves on MMA-Diffusion (20.0% vs. 64.8%). Against training-free methods (SLD-MAX, Safree), NCD achieves lower SSR-10 on all four safety benchmarks while maintaining comparable CLIP/FID. Against the strongest alignment-based baseline AlignGuard, NCD wins on every metric: SSR-10 drops from 24.8→6.2 (I2P-Sexual), 21.4→14.8 (NSFW-56K), 10.5→5.0 (Sneaky-Prompt), 25.0→20.0 (MMA-Diffusion), and generation quality improves (CLIP 25.84→26.39, FID 22.90→19.85). The filter-based Safety Checker is the weakest baseline, with SSR-10 still above 36% on I2P-Sexual, confirming its vulnerability to seed variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Noise Contrastive Alignment (NCA) from language models to diffusion-based T2I safety alignment, building on the step-wise reward formulation from Diffusion-DPO. Its key departure is identifying two fundamental flaws in the direct NCA-to-diffusion extension: a gradient reversal pathology in the positive-sample contrastive term (formalized as Theorem 3.1) and a uniform suppression strategy that ignores severity variation among harmful samples. The proposed NCD framework removes the problematic positive term and introduces a pairwise preference loss, positioning itself as a principled correction rather than a new alignment paradigm. It contrasts with prior parameter-modification methods (ESD, UCE, RECE) and preference-alignment approaches (AlignGuard, DUO) by explicitly targeting the one-to-many structure of cross-seed harmful outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of SSR-N as an evaluation metric exposes a previously concealed vulnerability: existing defenses that appear strong under single-seed evaluation degrade substantially when multiple seeds are considered, with SSR-50 reaching 49–70% for several SOTA methods. This reframes T2I safety from a point-estimation problem to a distributional coverage problem, with implications for how safety is audited in production. Limitations include the dependence on multi-candidate sampling at training time and on the quality of the safety scorer used for preference labeling; open questions include whether the approach scales to video generation, whether the gradient reversal phenomenon generalizes to other contrastive alignment objectives, and how NCD interacts with emerging transformer-based architectures (SD3, FLUX) beyond the LoRA fine-tuning regime tested here.
