## Problem

Policy optimization methods are among the most practical techniques in reinforcement learning, yet no rigorously-proved efficient algorithm leveraging PO for regret minimization in stochastic contextual bandits with offline function approximation existed. Existing optimal-regret algorithms (UCCB, IGW-based methods) rely on deterministic optimistic policies or complex low-switching mechanisms, leaving a gap between the practical appeal of PO's smooth stochastic updates and the theoretical guarantees available for CMAB.

## Input / Output

The agent observes a fresh context c_k each round from an unknown distribution over a potentially huge context space, and interacts with a finite realizable loss function class F through an offline least-squares regression oracle that returns the ERM minimizer over all past (context, action, loss) triples. Unusually, to compute the exploration bonus for the current context, the agent must counterfactually evaluate all past policies π_1, …, π_{k−1} at the current context c_k, requiring O(K²) total computation. The output is a stochastic policy π_k(c_k, ·) over a finite action set A, from which an action is sampled and a stochastic loss in [0,1] is observed.

## Method

OPO-CMAB generalizes the counterfactual confidence bounds of Xu and Zeevi (2020) from deterministic to stochastic policies, defining an exploration bonus b_k^β(c,a) = min{1, β/2 / (1 + Σ_{i<k} π_i(c,a))} that quantifies how well-explored each arm is for the current context under past stochastic policies. The algorithm constructs an optimistic loss estimate ℓ̂_k(c,a) = max{0, f̂_k(c,a) − b_k^β(c,a)} and updates the policy via exponential improvement (equivalently, Online Mirror Descent with KL divergence), yielding π_{k+1}(c,a) ∝ π_k(c,a) exp(−ηℓ̂_k(c,a)). The regret analysis decomposes into approximation error for the played policy, sub-optimality on estimated losses, and approximation error for the optimal policy, bounding each via AM-GM inequalities, uniform convergence of the regression oracle, and the OMD fundamental inequality. This yields the first high-probability optimal regret bound Õ(√(K|A|log|F|)) for a PO-based algorithm in this setting, without requiring additional structural assumptions such as Eluder dimension.

## Results

The primary theoretical contribution is a high-probability optimal regret bound of $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{K|\mathcal{A}|\log|\mathcal{F}|})$ for OPO-CMAB, matching the state-of-the-art rate for stochastic CMAB with offline function approximation. Empirically, OPO-CMAB is evaluated on 18 OpenML multiclass classification datasets using Progressive Validation (PV) loss as the metric. The paper reports that all algorithms (OPO-CMAB, FastCB, SquareCB, AdaCB, RegCB, Supervised) exhibit similar performance with no significant evidence that any one consistently outperforms the others, positioning OPO-CMAB as competitive with SOTA baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares OPO-CMAB against four regression-oracle-based CMAB algorithms (FastCB, SquareCB, AdaCB, RegCB) and a supervised learning baseline on 18 OpenML datasets. On dataset 1084 (3-armed), OPO-CMAB ranks second behind Supervised, ahead of RegCB, FastCB, SquareCB, and AdaCB. On dataset 1062 (2-armed), OPO-CMAB ranks third behind Supervised and FastCB, with AdaCB, RegCB, and SquareCB trailing. On dataset 1015 (2-armed), OPO-CMAB shows slightly higher PV-loss than AdaCB, FastCB, Supervised, and RegCB, though the gap is small. Overall, the authors conclude there is no significant evidence that any algorithm consistently outperforms the others, and OPO-CMAB's main contribution is theoretical (first provably optimal PO-based regret bound for stochastic CMAB) rather than empirical superiority. The $O(K^2)$ runtime of OPO-CMAB (due to computing all past counterfactual policies) is noted as a practical limitation compared to the $O(K)$ baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: the counterfactual confidence bounds of Xu and Zeevi (2020) for deterministic UCB-based policies in stochastic CMAB, and the optimistic policy optimization (OPO) framework of Shani et al. (2020) for tabular MDPs. The key technical contribution is generalizing the counterfactual exploration bonus from deterministic to stochastic policies, enabling it to plug into an exponential-weighting (KL-regularized OMD) policy update. Prior stochastic CMAB algorithms achieving the same optimal regret rate relied on either deterministic UCB (UCCB) or inverse-gap-weighting with complex low-switching mechanisms (Simchi-Levi & Xu 2022; Foster et al. 2021b), but no rigorously proved PO-based method existed in this setting. The paper also positions itself as the first application of pure policy optimization beyond tabular and linear MDPs in the RL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a specific theoretical gap by showing that the widely used PO update rule (exponential weighting with KL regularization) can provably achieve optimal high-probability regret in stochastic CMAB with general offline function approximation, without additional structural assumptions like Eluder dimension. However, the O(K²) runtime from evaluating all past predictors on each new context limits practical adoption, and the empirical evaluation on 18 small OpenML datasets shows OPO-CMAB is competitive but not clearly superior to FastCB or SquareCB. Open questions include extending the analysis to contextual RL and larger state spaces, and developing scalable heuristics (noisy batching, heuristic look-back) that preserve the theoretical guarantees while reducing computational cost.
