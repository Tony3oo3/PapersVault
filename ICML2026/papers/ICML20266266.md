## Problem

Data-driven scientific modeling faces a functional dichotomy: neural operators exhibit spectral bias that blurs high-frequency structures like shock waves, while physics-constrained paradigms suffer from optimization pathologies where gradient conflicts between data fitting and physical residuals cause training instability. No existing architecture simultaneously achieves high predictive accuracy, computational efficiency, and strict adherence to conservation laws for nonlinear dissipative systems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single 3D spatiotemporal field snapshot U_t (e.g., a 3D compressible turbulence volume from PDEBench, a 2D urban traffic density grid, or atmospheric reanalysis fields like Z500/T850) and produces the predicted next-time-step state U_{t+1}. The physics branch internally computes spatial derivatives via fixed finite-difference stencils (Sobel kernels for first-order gradients, 7-point templates for Laplacians, cascaded Laplacians for fourth-order hyper-diffusion), while the data backbone operates on the raw input to capture high-frequency residuals the operator cannot model. Evaluation uses multi-dimensional metrics including relative L2, H1 (gradient-aware), vorticity error, and mass conservation residual for CFD, and RMSE/MAE/MAPE for traffic and weather tasks.

## Method

APIC is a dual-stream neuro-symbolic architecture that runs a Physics Stream (a reconfigurable G-KSCH operator basis derived from the Reynolds Transport Theorem, combining advection, Fickian diffusion, Cahn-Hilliard phase separation, hyper-diffusion, and reaction terms with learnable adaptive coefficients λ) in parallel with a Data Stream (3D SE-ResNet backbone). The critical mechanism is Gradient Isolation: a detach operation is applied to the physics prediction before it enters the data branch, severing the backward gradient path so that λ is optimized solely by physical conservation constraints while the backbone learns only the residual the operator cannot capture. The two streams are fused via channel concatenation followed by a nonlinear transform (stacked fusion) rather than linear gating, expanding the hypothesis space to capture non-linear cross-modal dependencies. A Physics Adapter projects both streams into a shared latent manifold to resolve spatiotemporal semantic misalignment. The G-KSCH operator is structurally reconfigurable per domain—e.g., the phase-separation term becomes a congestion potential for traffic, and the Laplacian decomposes into anisotropic horizontal/vertical components with a Coriolis rotation term for meteorology.

## Results

On the 3D compressible turbulence benchmark (PDEBench), APIC achieves the best Relative L2 error across all data splits: 0.033 (80%), 0.042 (20%), 0.051 (10%), and 0.059 (5%), while also leading on H1 (0.072–0.125) and Vorticity (0.106–0.182) metrics. In the extreme 5% data-scarce regime, APIC (L2=0.059) outperforms 3D-FNO (0.145) by 2.5× and matches ResNet-3D trained on 4× more data (0.058 at 20%). On Traffic (TaxiBJ), APIC achieves RMSE 17.89 with only 0.46M parameters, beating SimVP_v2 (18.36, 5.45M params). On Weather (WeatherBench), APIC reaches RMSE 16.88 m, trailing only PredRNN (14.89 m) but using just 6% of its parameter count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APIC dominates all 11 CFD baselines on L2, H1, and Vorticity metrics across every data split. The largest margins appear against neural operators: at 5% data, APIC (0.059) beats 3D-FNO (0.145) by 2.5×, UNO (0.146) by 2.5×, and Transolver_Patched (0.158) by 2.7×; the unpatched Transolver (0.297) suffers severe mean collapse with the lowest Mass error (0.79) but worst L2/H1. Among physics-informed baselines, APIC outperforms PeRCNN (0.167) and PhyDNet (0.112) at 5% data, confirming that gradient isolation avoids the optimization pathologies of hard-constraint and implicit-constraint approaches. On Traffic, APIC (RMSE 17.89, 0.46M params) beats all five baselines including SimVP_v2 (18.36, 5.45M) and PredRNN (25.48, 13.76M). On Weather, APIC (16.88 m) wins against ConvLSTM (29.26), FourCastNet (22.61), PhyDNet (40.04), and SimVP_v2 (31.96), but trails PredRNN (14.89 m) by ~1.4 m while using only 6% of its parameters (1.35M vs 22.18M).

## Relation to Prior Work

APIC carves new ground at the intersection of three existing lines of work: physics-informed neural networks (PINNs), neural operator learning (FNO, Transolver), and hard-constraint architectures (PeRCNN). Rather than treating physics as a soft regularization term (PINNs) or a rigid weight constraint (PeRCNN), it embeds a reconfigurable symbolic operator basis (G-KSCH, derived from the Reynolds Transport Theorem) as a first-class network component and uses a gradient-isolation (detach) mechanism to decouple physics parameter identification from data-driven residual correction. The non-linear stacked fusion departs from the linear gating strategies common in prior hybrid methods, and the adaptive coefficient design with dominant/perturbation modes generalizes the fixed-coefficient stencils of PeRCNN into a learnable hypothesis space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient isolation mechanism addresses a well-known optimization pathology in hybrid physics-data models (gradient conflicts between residual fitting and physical constraint satisfaction), and the G-KSCH unification provides a principled, RTT-grounded template for reconfiguring physical priors across domains (fluids, traffic, weather) without architectural redesign. However, the framework is currently limited to Euclidean grids and dissipative systems; extending it to non-Euclidean meshes, non-dissipative (Hamiltonian/Lagrangian) dynamics, and ODE/SDE settings remains open. The paper also candidly notes that a fundamentally mismatched operator basis can mislead the model, and that benchmark performance should not be over-interpreted as deployment reliability in safety-critical settings.
