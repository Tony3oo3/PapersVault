## Problem

In Pre-LN Transformers, residual-stream variance grows sub-exponentially with depth, pushing deeper layers toward identity mappings and causing the 'curse of depth' where most layers contribute minimally to performance. While prior work proposed explicit variance-control mechanisms (scaled initialization, LayerNorm rescaling), the impact of the widespread sparsity mechanisms already present in modern LLMs—weight decay, long-context attention, GQA, and MoE—on variance propagation remained unexplored. This paper asks whether sparsity, implicit or explicit, serves as an intrinsic variance regulator that mitigates the curse of depth.

## Input / Output

The experiments take Pre-LN Transformer architectures (1.2B–7B parameters) and systematically vary one sparsity dimension at a time—weight decay (λ ∈ {0, 0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 3.0}), training sequence length (T = 256–8192), GQA group size (G ∈ {1, 4, 16}), or MoE routing (top-4/32 and top-8/64 experts)—while holding all other hyperparameters and total training FLOPs constant. Outputs include per-layer and last-layer variance trajectories tracked throughout training, three complementary layer-effectiveness scores (Causal, Permutation, Usefulness), validation perplexity, and average accuracy on ARC-C and HellaSwag. The Usefulness Score in particular measures the fraction of layers whose removal causes >10% loss increase via linear-approximation ablation, providing a direct quantification of effective nonlinear depth.

## Method

The paper provides a theoretical bound (Theorem 1) showing that in a residual recursion r_{ℓ+1} = r_ℓ + W_ℓ(D_ℓ r_ℓ), the per-layer variance gain is (1+√(α_ℓρ_ℓ))², so smaller mask density ρ_ℓ directly slows variance accumulation across depth; Theorem 2 extends this to full Transformer blocks with MHA and FFN sublayers. Empirically, the authors categorize sparsity into two paradigms—implicit (weight decay driving parameters toward zero, and long sequences inducing peaked attention via RoPE distance decay and softmax concentration) and explicit (GQA/MQA sharing KV heads to reduce independent attention paths, and Top-k MoE routing to average over k experts)—and validate each through controlled depth-scaling experiments from L=12 to L=32. They introduce three layer-effectiveness metrics (Causal Score measuring downstream disruption, Permutation Score measuring interchangeability, and Usefulness Score measuring nonlinear contribution) and demonstrate a consistent pattern: increased sparsity correlates with reduced output variance, Jacobians further from identity, and higher layer differentiation. Finally, they distill findings into a practical recipe combining complementary sparsity mechanisms (long context + moderate weight decay + GQA + MoE) that yields a 4.6-point accuracy gain over a naively trained 32-layer baseline while restoring layer utilization from 0.53 to 0.75.

## Results

The paper demonstrates that sparsity mechanisms consistently reduce last-layer output variance and improve layer effectiveness across all tested configurations. MoE architectures dampen variance by approximately 6× (400M active params) and 3× (1B active params) compared to dense counterparts, while improving usefulness scores from 0.87→0.94 and 0.81→0.94 respectively. Combining complementary sparsity mechanisms (GQA with G=2 and MoE) on a 32-layer, 1.2B model yields a 4.6-point average accuracy gain over the L=16 baseline on ARC-C and HellaSwag, with usefulness recovering from 0.53 (naive L=32) to 0.75. Weight decay at λ=0.1 improves PPL from 15.63 to 14.83 and usefulness from 0.75 to 0.81, while MQA (G=16) achieves 2× lower variance than MHA with a 7% usefulness increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between MoE (explicit sparsity) and dense baselines matched by active parameter count. MoE-7BA1B outperforms Dense-1B by 0.7 PPL (13.82 vs 14.52) and improves usefulness from 0.81 to 0.94; MoE-2BA400M outperforms Dense-400M by 1.67 PPL (15.89 vs 17.56) with usefulness rising from 0.87 to 0.94. For implicit sparsity, moderate weight decay (λ=0.1) beats no decay (λ=0) on PPL (14.83 vs 15.63) and all three effectiveness scores, while excessive decay (λ≥1.0) degrades performance despite further variance reduction. Longer training sequences (T=2048) improve PPL by 4 points over T=256 (14.51 vs 18.51) and raise usefulness from 0.69 to 0.81, though T=8192 shows diminishing returns. GQA/MQA (G=16) achieves marginally better PPL (14.47 vs 14.52) and higher usefulness (0.87 vs 0.81) than MHA (G=1) under equal FLOPs. The combined sparsity recipe (GQA + MoE) at L=32 achieves 44.1 average accuracy, surpassing the L=16 baseline by 4+ points while maintaining 0.75 usefulness versus 0.53 for the naive L=32 model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the curse-of-depth (CoD) framework established by Sun et al. (2025), which identified exponential variance growth in Pre-LN Transformers as the mechanism driving deeper layers toward identity mappings. While prior work has addressed CoD through explicit variance-control interventions (scaled initialization, LayerNorm rescaling, Mix-LN, hyper-connections), this paper posits a complementary and previously unexplored axis: sparsity as an intrinsic variance regulator. It draws on the 'signal collapse' observation in sparse networks (Dey et al., 2024; 2025) to formalize how reduced interaction density attenuates per-layer variance gain, then empirically validates this across four distinct sparsity sources (weight decay, long-context attention, GQA/MQA, and MoE routing) that were previously justified solely on efficiency grounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes sparsity from a compute-saving tool into an optimization mechanism that directly governs depth utilization, offering practitioners a concrete recipe (combine GQA + MoE + moderate weight decay + longer context) that yields a 4.6-point accuracy gain when scaling from 16 to 32 layers at fixed 1.2B parameters. Key limitations include the idealized independence assumptions in the theory (weights decoupled from sparsity masks), all empirical validation at ≤1.2B scale, and the observation that over-dampening (excessive weight decay or sequence length) degrades performance, suggesting a narrow optimal sparsity regime. Open questions include whether the sparsity-variance link persists at 70B+ scale, how it interacts with the shortcut-path variance contribution identified in the appendix, and whether the practical recipe transfers to architectures without Pre-LN.
