## Problem

Prior work on consistent submodular maximization only addressed insertion-only streams, leaving the more realistic fully-dynamic setting (with both insertions and deletions) unexplored. In the fully-dynamic case, a single deletion can drastically reduce the optimal value, requiring O(k) compensating changes, and the optimal value no longer evolves monotonically, breaking standard analysis techniques. This paper provides the first constant-factor approximation algorithms with sublinear consistency for fully-dynamic submodular maximization under both cardinality and matroid constraints.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a stream of n operations (each an insertion or deletion of an element), a monotone submodular function f accessed via value oracle, a matroid (or cardinality) constraint accessed via a feasibility oracle, and a global precision parameter ε ∈ (0,1). At each time step t it outputs a feasible subset ALG_t ⊆ X_t (where X_t is the set of currently active elements) satisfying E[f(ALG_t)] ≥ α·f(OPT_t) in expectation, while enforcing |ALG_t △ ALG_{t-1}| ≤ C realization-wise. The consistency is measured via symmetric difference rather than insertions-only counting, which is the natural metric when deletions occur.

## Method

The framework combines three modular components: (1) a RANDOM-SCHEDULING routine that recursively partitions the stream into hierarchical transition windows at O(log k) robustness levels d_i = ⌈d_0/2^i⌉, with a random cyclic shift to avoid adversarial alignment; (2) a robust submodular routine (ROBUST-SWAP for matroids, ROBUST-GREEDY for cardinality) that builds deletion-robust coresets by sampling elements with probability inversely proportional to their marginal contribution, stopping when the candidate set drops below d/ε; and (3) a non-robust routine (SWAP) that processes remaining candidates greedily between transitions. During transition windows, the algorithm gradually swaps elements between the old and new solutions in equal-cardinality chunks to maintain feasibility via the matroid exchange property. For matroids, the O(log k) levels handle unknown deletion counts; for cardinality, a single level d = ε²k suffices, yielding a 1/2 − O(ε) approximation with O(1/ε²) consistency versus 1/4 − O(ε) with O(log k/ε²) consistency for matroids.

## Results

For cardinality constraints, the paper achieves a 1/2 − O(ε) approximation of the dynamic optimum with O(1/ε²) per-step consistency. For rank-k matroid constraints, it achieves a 1/4 − O(ε) approximation with O(log k / ε²) consistency. These are the first constant-factor approximation algorithms in the fully dynamic setting (with both insertions and deletions) that exhibit sublinear-in-k consistency. The cardinality result nearly matches the state-of-the-art ≈0.51 poly-time guarantee for the insertion-only case, while the matroid result matches the 1/4 streaming guarantee of Chakrabarti & Kale (2015) in the significantly harder fully dynamic setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's cardinality result (1/2 − O(ε) with O(1/ε²) consistency) nearly matches the best known poly-time insertion-only guarantee of ≈0.51 (Dutting et al., 2025a), while extending to the much harder fully dynamic setting with deletions. The information-theoretic upper bound for insertion-only is 2/3 for randomized strategies, and no deterministic algorithm can exceed 1/2. For matroid constraints, the paper's 1/4 − O(ε) approximation matches the streaming algorithm of Chakrabarti & Kale (2015), which also achieves 1/4 with constant (one-element) consistency, but does so in the significantly more challenging fully dynamic setting with both insertions and deletions and only O(log k/ε²) consistency. The paper notes that the gap between cardinality (1/2) and matroid (1/4) approximations mirrors a well-known gap in the offline and semi-streaming submodular maximization literature, where the 1−1/e bound for cardinality took over three decades to extend to matroids.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consistent submodular maximization framework of Dutting et al. (2024, 2025a), which was restricted to insertion-only streams and cardinality constraints, to the fully dynamic setting (insertions and deletions) and to general matroid constraints. The technical core draws on deletion-robust submodular maximization (Zhang et al., 2022; Mirzasoleiman et al., 2017) for the robust routines and on the SWAPPING algorithm of Chakrabarti & Kale (2015) for the matroid case, but the key novelty is the general framework combining randomized scheduling, hierarchical robustness levels, and a transition-window mechanism that ensures sublinear per-step changes. The paper positions itself at the intersection of three previously separate lines: consistent algorithms, deletion robustness, and fully-dynamic optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

These are the first constant-factor approximation algorithms for fully dynamic submodular maximization with sublinear consistency, addressing a practical need for stable solutions over evolving datasets (e.g., data summarization, recommendation systems). The near-matching of the insertion-only state-of-the-art (~0.51) with a 1/2 guarantee in the much harder fully dynamic setting is a notable theoretical achievement. Open questions include whether constant consistency is achievable for matroids, whether the 1/2 barrier for cardinality can be broken, and whether the framework extends to non-monotone objectives or other constraint families like knapsack.
