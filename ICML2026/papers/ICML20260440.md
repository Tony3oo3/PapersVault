## Problem

Scientific data annotation is bottlenecked by the 'last mile' problem: even with strong automation, human verification and correction remain expensive, yet standard ML approaches treat annotation as a direct data-to-label mapping, discarding the rich process supervision in how experts navigate, click, verify, and correct. No standardized benchmarks exist for long-horizon annotation sessions, and the practical barriers of collecting behavioral data have prevented systematic study of how well models can learn annotation workflows, what challenges persist with scale, and what makes some tasks harder than others.

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved sequences of up to 20 GUI screenshots (resized to 524×436 pixels for multi-task training) and predicts the next click as independent categorical distributions over 524 x-bins and 436 y-bins. The action space is a single canvas click whose type (button press, marker placement, undo, done) is determined entirely by the GUI simulator's hit-testing of the predicted coordinates, so the model never explicitly specifies action types. Each screenshot is encoded into 108 spatially pooled patch tokens (12×9 grid) by a DINOv2 encoder, preserving spatial structure needed for pixel-precise prediction.

## Method

The paper introduces a three-component framework separating task instances (procedurally generated data with ground truth), a virtual human annotation model (simulating navigation, placement, verification, and 10–30% mistake-then-undo sequences), and an HTML/JavaScript GUI renderer (Playwright + headless Chromium) that produces interleaved screenshot-action training data and supports closed-loop autoregressive evaluation. The model architecture pairs an unfrozen DINOv2 vision encoder with a transformer head using block-causal attention (bidirectional within each frame's 108 patches, causal across frames) and RoPE positional encoding, trained with cross-entropy on discretized x and y coordinates. Four model sizes (25M–320M parameters) are trained jointly on 9 synthetic annotation tasks to study scaling, transfer, and interpretability. Key findings include hierarchical skill emergence (GUI mechanics before task-critical decisions), a shared cross-task mistake representation (0.87 pooled ROC AUC, 0.71 mean leave-one-task-out transfer), and the critical result that multi-task pretraining enables fine-tuning to a held-out task at 76.6% accuracy with 500 sequences while training from scratch fails entirely at 0% even with 7,800 sequences.

## Results

The 95M-parameter BC model achieves 97.4% teacher-forced placement accuracy @5px on COLORED DOT TRACKING, outperforming Gemini 3 Flash Preview (80.0%) and Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct (25.0%); in autoregressive evaluation, Gemini succeeds on only 3/9 tasks and Qwen on 0/9. For downstream adaptation to the held-out SHAPE MATCHING task, fine-tuning the multi-task pretrained model on just 500 sequences reaches 76.6% accuracy, while training the same architecture from scratch on 7,800 sequences (15× more data) achieves 0%. On real connectomics data, the fine-tuned model reaches 95.1% skeleton accuracy on H01 human cortex and 89.4% on C. elegans nerve ring, with 100% task completion. Scaling analysis shows 10× more parameters yields roughly 3× better sample efficiency within the 25M–320M range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gemini 3 Flash Preview and Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct (both evaluated with a fully descriptive text scaffold plus 3 most recent screenshots), the 95M BC model wins decisively on all 5 placement tasks in teacher-forced evaluation (e.g., 97.4% vs. 80.0% and 25.0% on COLORED DOT TRACKING @5px) and in autoregressive closed-loop evaluation (9/9 tasks vs. 3/9 and 0/9). Against the DAgger baseline (β-DAgger, Ross et al. 2011), the paper reports that DAgger also fails to bootstrap performance on the two hardest tasks (animal_behavioral_tracking and animal_limb_tracking), indicating the difficulty is inherent to the task rather than a compounding-error artifact of standard BC. For the downstream adaptation comparison, multi-task pretraining followed by fine-tuning on 500 sequences (76.6%) dramatically outperforms training from scratch on 7,800 sequences (0%), while in-context learning (zero-shot and few-shot) achieves negligible accuracy (<2%), establishing that pretraining provides essential inductive biases that cannot be recovered by extended task-specific training alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends behavioral cloning (Pomerleau, 1988; Bain & Sammut, 1995) from its established domains (autonomous driving, game-playing, robotics) to scientific data annotation, where the full interactive workflow—navigation, verification, mistake correction—has been largely ignored in favor of direct data-to-label prediction. The closest methodological analog is Video PreTraining (Baker et al., 2022), which applied BC to Minecraft gameplay; here the authors adapt that recipe to annotation tasks but use synthetic data and virtual annotators to overcome the absence of large-scale behavioral datasets. Unlike GUI agents (WebArena, SeeClick) that target general web navigation, this work focuses on the distinct challenges of long-horizon scientific annotation: rare critical actions, order constraints, and the 'last mile' verification problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first systematic benchmark for studying BC on scientific annotation, providing baselines and realistic expectations that could resolve the collective action problem discouraging both ML and domain scientists from investing in this direction. Key open questions remain: whether the 25M–320M scaling findings hold at frontier scales, whether reward-based RL can address the underweighting of rare critical actions (undo, done), and why in-context learning fails entirely despite multi-task pretraining building transferable representations. The practical path of synthetic pretraining followed by fine-tuning on limited real data (demonstrated on real EM connectomics) is promising but untested at the scale and diversity of actual annotation workflows.
