## Problem

Conventional RL fine-tuning of visual generative models relies on sample-wise reward functions, which are prone to reward hacking that collapses diversity and introduces visual artifacts. Distribution-wise metrics like FID better capture holistic distributional fidelity, but directly using them as RL rewards is infeasible due to the ~50K samples needed for stable estimation and the inability to assign per-trajectory reward signals. Additionally, SDE-based RL training exhibits a train-inference inconsistency where gains do not transfer to ODE-based deterministic inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes class labels from ImageNet (256×256 for SiT, 512×512 for EDM2) and random noise vectors as input, producing generated images or, in the post-hoc merging variant, a vector of N_c=8 weighting coefficients for combining sequential training checkpoints. The subset-replace strategy operates on a class-balanced reference set of 5,000 generated images, replacing a small subset of 50 images per rollout batch to compute the distribution-wise FID reward. Training uses 50 denoising steps (denoising reduction) while evaluation uses 250 steps on 50K images.

## Method

The core contribution is a subset-replace strategy that converts the expensive FID metric into a dense, per-batch RL reward: a reference set of N generated images is maintained, a small subset of n images is replaced with newly generated samples of the same classes, and the negative FID of the partially updated set serves as the reward for those n trajectories. This reward drives policy gradient optimization (GRPO-style with batch-level advantage normalization and top-25% sample selection) to fine-tune the full diffusion model. To resolve the SDE-to-ODE train-inference gap, the paper additionally applies the same distribution-wise reward to optimize post-hoc model merging coefficients via a lightweight 3-layer MLP policy that outputs Gaussian parameters for each checkpoint weight, enabling ODE-based rollouts throughout training. The reference set is refreshed every 10 training steps to track the evolving model distribution.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, the proposed distribution-wise RL fine-tuning reduces SiT-XL/2's FID-50K from 8.30 to 5.77 (RL, 450 steps) and to 6.98 (rejection sampling, 120 steps), while also reducing FD_DINOv2 from 230.39 to 164.88. On ImageNet 512×512, post-hoc RL-EMA optimization of model merging coefficients improves EDM2-XS FID from 3.74 to 3.52 and EDM2-S from 2.57 to 2.52. Cross-metric evaluation confirms broad distributional gains: KID drops 53.5%, MMD drops 48.3%, Precision rises 4.3%, and Density rises 12.0%, while Recall decreases only 3.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method is applied as a post-training refinement on top of existing pretrained models rather than competing from scratch. On SiT-XL/2 (FID 8.30), the distribution-wise RL fine-tuning achieves FID 5.77, substantially outperforming ADM-U (7.49) and DiT-XL/2 (9.60) with far fewer training steps (450 vs. 1.98M–7M). On EDM2-XS (FID 3.74), the RL-EMA merging optimization improves to 3.52, surpassing DiT-XL/2 (12.03) and ADM-U (9.96) on ImageNet 512×512. The method also improves EDM2-S from 2.57 to 2.52, maintaining its position as the strongest model in the comparison. Notably, the sample-wise RL baseline (shown in Figure 1) suffers severe reward hacking with FID degrading to 34.26, whereas the distribution-wise approach avoids this failure mode entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-for-diffusion-models line initiated by Black et al. (2023) and Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025), which cast denoising as an MDP and optimize with sample-wise reward models (CLIP Score, HPS). The key departure is replacing per-sample rewards with a distribution-wise signal (FID), made tractable via a novel subset-replace strategy that swaps a small fraction of a reference set with new samples. For the post-hoc merging component, the paper builds directly on EDM2's (Karras et al., 2024) retrospective EMA formulation, substituting grid search with RL-guided coefficient optimization and eliminating the SDE-based rollouts that prior RL methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically confirms a train-inference inconsistency in SDE-based RL for diffusion models: gains measured under SDE evaluation do not transfer to the ODE solvers used at inference, a gap that has been underappreciated in the literature. The subset-replace strategy offers a practical, low-cost way to use distribution-wise metrics as RL rewards, and the post-hoc merging approach provides a lightweight plug-and-play improvement path for pretrained models. Limitations include evaluation only on class-conditional ImageNet (256×256 and 512×512), modest absolute gains on EDM2 (3.74→3.52), and the open question of whether the approach generalizes to text-to-image generation or other distribution metrics beyond FID.
