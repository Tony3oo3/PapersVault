## Problem

Most long-horizon time-series forecasters are trained end-to-end with backpropagation, incurring high computational cost and producing opaque internal representations that are difficult to trace. Classical forward self-organizing polynomial networks (GMDH/PNN) avoid backpropagation but lack inductive bias for periodicity and suffer from severe multicollinearity and redundancy as depth increases. No prior framework unifies forward-grown polynomial construction, frequency-aware spectral modeling, and rule-level interpretability in a single forecasting architecture.

## Input / Output

FIPN takes a multivariate time-series window of fixed look-back length L=96 across R channels (e.g., 7 for ETT, 862 for Traffic) and produces a multi-step forecast of horizon H ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720} for all R channels simultaneously via a multi-output regression head. The model is evaluated under a standard sliding-window protocol with chronological splits (6:2:2 for ETT, 7:1:2 for others). Notably, the entire model contains only ~0.89K parameters and requires 9.12M MACs per forward pass, orders of magnitude smaller than Transformer baselines.

## Method

FIPN grows its architecture layer by layer through a generate–fit–validate–select loop without backpropagation. Each neuron couples a fuzzy C-means antecedent (softly partitioning the input space into C local regimes with normalized rule gates β_nq) with a Fourier-enhanced polynomial consequent that builds rule-specific harmonic cosine bases centered at fuzzy prototypes, using inverse-variance-scaled angular frequencies to adapt spectral resolution per dimension. Candidate neurons are fitted independently via ridge-regularized closed-form least squares, then ranked by a validation score combining prediction error and an ℓ₂ coefficient penalty; only the top-K candidates are retained per layer. Redundancy and multicollinearity are suppressed through column dropout on the design matrix during fitting and persistent concatenation of raw inputs at every layer, ensuring deeper layers retain access to original features rather than relying solely on increasingly correlated intermediate nodes.

## Results

On eight long-horizon forecasting benchmarks (horizon-averaged over H ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720} with L=96), FIPN achieves the best MSE on 5 of 8 datasets: ETTh1 (0.428), ETTh2 (0.361), ETTm2 (0.267), Traffic (0.441), and Electricity (0.168). It is second-best on ETTm1 (0.385 vs. 0.381 for TimeStacker), Weather (0.242 vs. 0.240 for TimeMixer), and Exchange (0.356 vs. 0.336 for TimeStacker). FIPN uses only 0.89K parameters and 9.12M MACs, compared to 1.04M–17.40M parameters for DLinear through Crossformer, while matching or exceeding their accuracy on most datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIPN outperforms all Transformer-based baselines (iTransformer, PatchTST, Crossformer) and the linear DLinear baseline on all eight datasets in MSE. Against recent efficient forecasters, FIPN wins on five datasets (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm2, Traffic, Electricity) but trails TimeStacker on ETTm1 (0.385 vs. 0.381) and Exchange (0.356 vs. 0.336), and TimeMixer on Weather (0.242 vs. 0.240). The largest margins appear on Traffic (FIPN 0.441 vs. FITS 0.627, a 30% MSE reduction) and Electricity (FIPN 0.168 vs. Crossformer 0.244, a 31% reduction), where periodic and multi-scale structures align with FIPN's Fourier-enhanced design. On Exchange, where dynamics are weakly predictable and event-driven, the gap between FIPN and the best baseline (TimeStacker) narrows to 0.020 MSE. In the non-gradient comparison (Appendix B.7), FIPN also substantially outperforms LCESN and PFPNN across all eight datasets, e.g., 0.428 vs. 0.818 MSE on ETTh1 and 0.441 vs. 1.968 on Traffic.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIPN extends the GMDH/Polynomial Neural Network lineage (Ivakhnenko, 1971; Chrysos et al., 2022) by embedding two previously separate ideas into the forward-grown polynomial framework: fuzzy c-means rule gating for local regime interpretation, and Fourier-enhanced harmonic bases for periodic temporal modeling. It departs from prior PNNs (including PFPNN, Wang et al. 2023) by adding frequency inductive bias and redundancy-controlled node selection (column dropout + regularized scoring) to stabilize deeper forward growth. It also departs from frequency-aware forecasters (FITS, FilterTS, TimeStacker) by eliminating backpropagation entirely, relying instead on closed-form ridge regression per candidate neuron.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FIPN demonstrates that a forward-grown, closed-form polynomial network with only ~1K parameters can match or beat Transformer-based and frequency-domain forecasters with millions of parameters on standard long-horizon benchmarks, while offering rule-level and structural interpretability that post-hoc methods cannot provide. This is particularly relevant for resource-constrained or high-stakes deployment where both efficiency and traceable reasoning are required. Key limitations include the greedy (non-optimal) forward growth, fixed Fourier frequency bands that may be suboptimal under non-stationarity, and the restriction to deterministic point forecasting. Open directions include adaptive spectral representations, online/streaming variants, and extension to probabilistic forecasting.
