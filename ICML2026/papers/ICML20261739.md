## Problem

Standard JEPA formulations predict future latent representations via deterministic regression, providing no explicit uncertainty over future states and offering limited probabilistic semantics for planning and control. Additionally, the conditions under which a learned JEPA embedding can serve as a sufficient information state for optimal control remain formally unexplored, and reconstruction-based world-model baselines are known to waste capacity on high-variance nuisance variation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261739/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a context observation (image patches, video clips, or time-series history x_C) along with optional action sequences u_t and structural side information ξ_T (mask position, temporal offset, or action), and produces a predictive latent distribution p_φ(Z_T | Z_C, ξ_T) parameterized as a diagonal Gaussian over future latent states. For BJEPA, the output is a Product-of-Experts posterior combining a dynamics expert and a swappable prior expert (goal embedding or energy-based constraint). In the control setting, the predictive distribution is rolled out via Monte Carlo sampling to yield an optimal action sequence through VJEPA-MPC, with no pixel-level reconstruction at any stage.

## Method

VJEPA replaces JEPA's deterministic point prediction with an explicit predictive distribution p_φ(Z_T | Z_C, ξ_T) (diagonal Gaussian with learned mean and variance), while the target encoder also becomes stochastic via q_θ̄(Z_T | x_T). Training minimizes a latent negative log-likelihood plus a target-side KL regularizer, entirely in representation space without any observation-space likelihood. The paper proves this objective maximizes a Barber-Agakov lower bound on predictive mutual information, formally explaining why the model can discard high-variance nuisance variation that reconstruction objectives are forced to encode. BJEPA extends VJEPA with a Product-of-Experts factorization into a learned dynamics expert and a modular prior expert, enabling constraint-aware prediction and zero-shot prior swapping without retraining. A predictive-sufficiency theorem establishes that VJEPA latents can serve as sufficient information states for optimal control, and VJEPA-MPC performs sampling-based MPC entirely in latent space.

## Results

On the multi-seed (N=5) Noisy-TV benchmark, predictive JEPA-family objectives substantially outperform reconstruction-based baselines under high-variance nuisance distractors. At the hardest noise scale (σ=8, SNR −2.2 dB), deterministic JEPA achieves signal R² = 0.749 ± 0.084 and noise R² = 0.203 ± 0.052, while VJEPA reaches 0.566 ± 0.224 signal R²; in contrast, the VAE collapses to −0.226 ± 0.846 signal R² and the AR model to 0.118 ± 0.352. At moderate noise (σ=4), VJEPA attains the best mean signal recovery (0.796 ± 0.187) with the lowest noise leakage (0.129 ± 0.151). On DMC Cheetah-run, VJEPA-MPC achieves a mean episode return of 9.5 versus 7.8 for JEPA-MPC and ≈2.3 for Dreamer-lite.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the VAE baseline (Kingma & Welling), VJEPA and JEPA dominate at high distractor strength: at σ=8 the VAE's signal R² collapses to −0.226 ± 0.846 (worse than random) while JEPA retains 0.749 ± 0.084, a gap of roughly 1.0 in R². The autoregressive (AR) baseline (van den Oord) shows a similar degradation pattern, reaching only 0.118 ± 0.352 signal R² at σ=8 versus 0.566–0.749 for the JEPA family. The RSSM/PlaNet-style baseline (Hafner et al., 2019) exhibits trajectory-specific overfitting, producing negative held-out signal R² at every noise scale, confirming that reconstruction ELBOs fit nuisance-specific structure that does not generalize. On DMC Cheetah-run, VJEPA-MPC (9.5 mean return) outperforms both JEPA-MPC (7.8) and Dreamer-lite (≈2.3, Hafner et al., 2020), with Dreamer-lite's reward-model loss stuck near 0.998, consistent with generative collapse under pixel-level ELBO training. Notably, deterministic JEPA is the most stable model at the extreme σ=8 regime (lowest variance across seeds), while VJEPA's advantage is most pronounced at moderate noise (σ=4) and when calibrated uncertainty estimates are required for planning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the JEPA family (I-JEPA, V-JEPA) from deterministic latent regression to a variational probabilistic framework, while explicitly connecting it to classical control-theoretic constructs: Predictive State Representations, Kalman/particle filtering, and sufficient statistics for POMDP control. It departs from reconstruction-based world models (Dreamer, PlaNet, RSSM) by eliminating the observation likelihood entirely, and from MuZero by requiring no reward or policy supervision during representation learning. The BJEPA Product-of-Experts factorization into a dynamics expert and a swappable prior expert is a novel modular extension not present in prior JEPA or world-model work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean information-theoretic justification (Barber-Agakov lower bound on predictive mutual information) for why latent prediction can discard nuisance variation that reconstruction must model, and empirically confirms this across linear, nonlinear, image, ViT, and control benchmarks. However, the advantage over deterministic JEPA is not uniform (JEPA is more stable at extreme noise), the Gaussian head is unimodal, BJEPA underperforms in some finite-capacity regimes, and several key experiments (ViT/STL-10, DMC control) are single-seed. Open questions include whether richer predictive families (GMMs, diffusion heads) recover the multi-modal futures the theory promises, and whether the framework scales to high-dimensional continuous control with multi-seed validation.
