## Problem

Cross-domain time-series generation suffers from periodicity misalignment because existing attention mechanisms operate on either individual points (point-wise) or fixed equidistant segments (patch-wise), both of which fail to adaptively capture complex periodic patterns that vary across domains. Point-wise attention lacks the representational capacity for long-range and high-order interactions, while patch-wise slicing rigidly breaks evolving trends and periodic structures. The paper asks whether the fundamental processing unit of attention can be extended from discrete points or patches to continuous sliding windows that naturally encapsulate domain-specific periodic cycles.

## Input / Output

The model consumes noisy univariate time-series sequences of fixed length 168 (reformatted from multivariate sources into non-overlapping slices) along with a composite domain condition comprising a domain prompt embedding, learned prototype embeddings, and the current diffusion step index. The condition is injected via adaptive Layer Normalization (DiT-style) rather than attention masking, avoiding interference with periodicity extraction. The output is a denoised synthetic sequence at each of 200 diffusion steps, ultimately yielding a generated time-series sample of the same 168-length format.

## Method

Winformer's core insight is a theoretical equivalence: window-wise similarity computed via discrete Fourier transforms over sliding windows is reducible to a learnable 2D convolution applied directly on the standard QK^T attention score matrix, derived through the convolution theorem and Parseval's theorem. This yields the Ample Attention mechanism, which replaces the pairwise dot-product with a Conv2d layer (kernel size 25, initialized with a DCT basis to avoid complex arithmetic) that slides over the score map to perform window-to-window comparison before softmax. The architecture is a DiT-based diffusion model with a hybrid encoder that concatenates outputs from vanilla attention heads (preserving point-wise similarity) and Ample attention heads (capturing window-wise periodic alignment), with the convolutional kernel remaining learnable to adapt beyond the initial DCT basis. Domain conditioning via adaptive Layer Norm enables cross-domain generation, and the model is trained with L1 loss over 200 diffusion steps on mixed multi-domain data.

## Results

On 12 real-world time-series datasets across four domains (energy, weather, transportation, economic), Winformer achieves the best MMD on 10 out of 12 datasets and the best K-L divergence on 9 out of 12, winning 19 of 24 total metric-dataset combinations. The headline result is an average 10.67% MMD reduction over the SOTA cross-domain baseline TimeDP. Winformer's advantage is most pronounced on strongly periodic datasets (e.g., Solar: 0.035 vs. 0.041 MMD; Traffic: 0.071 vs. 0.083 MMD) while it trails slightly on trend-dominated data (Wind: 0.034 vs. 0.025 MMD; Temperature: 0.230 vs. 0.219 MMD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Winformer is compared against six baselines spanning three model families: diffusion-based (TimeDP, Diffusion-TS), GAN-based (TimeGAN, GT-GAN), and VAE-based (TimeVAE, TimeVQVAE). Against the strongest baseline TimeDP (the current SOTA for cross-domain generation), Winformer wins 10/12 on MMD and 8/12 on K-L, with the largest gaps on periodic datasets like Solar (0.035 vs. 0.041 MMD) and Rain (0.036 vs. 0.057 MMD), while losing narrowly on trend-dominated Wind (0.034 vs. 0.025 MMD) and Temperature (0.230 vs. 0.219 MMD). Against Diffusion-TS, Winformer dominates across all 24 metric-dataset combinations, with MMD gaps ranging from 2× (Electricity: 0.001 vs. 0.003) to over 40× (Temperature: 0.230 vs. 0.241, though the absolute values are close; the K-L gap is more dramatic at 0.176 vs. 0.342). The GAN-based baselines (TimeGAN, GT-GAN) and VAE-based baselines (TimeVAE, TimeVQVAE) are consistently outperformed by large margins — e.g., on Traffic MMD, Winformer achieves 0.071 versus 0.567 (TimeGAN), 0.538 (GT-GAN), 0.218 (TimeVAE), and 0.213 (TimeVQVAE). GT-GAN is the only baseline that wins any single metric-dataset combination (K-L on Wind: 0.107 vs. Winformer's 0.202), reflecting its relative strength on weakly periodic, trend-heavy signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

Winformer extends the Transformer-based diffusion model lineage for time-series generation (DiT, TimeDP, Diffusion-TS) by rethinking the fundamental processing unit of attention from pairwise point similarity to sliding window comparison. It departs from both point-wise attention (Informer, Fedformer) and fixed patch-wise attention (PatchTST, ViT) by introducing a continuous sliding window that adaptively captures periodicity across domains. The key theoretical contribution bridges frequency-domain window similarity with learnable convolutions on the original attention score map via the convolution theorem and Parseval's identity, enabling an efficient 'Ample Attention' mechanism initialized with DCT bases rather than explicit Fourier transforms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The window-wise attention paradigm offers a principled way to handle periodicity misalignment in cross-domain time-series generation, a problem that normalization techniques alone cannot resolve. The clean equivalence between window-wise spectral similarity and convolutional attention provides a reusable theoretical tool for future frequency-aware attention designs. However, the model degrades on trend-dominated data (wind, temperature), the multivariate extension remains unexplored, and the hyper-parameter (stride/kernel size) selection for long seasonal cycles is non-trivial, limiting immediate practical adoption. The 10.67% MMD gain over TimeDP is meaningful but the evaluation is confined to 12 univariate datasets of fixed length 168, leaving open whether the advantage persists at scale or with more diverse periodic structures.
