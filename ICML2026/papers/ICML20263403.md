## Problem

Genomic language models (gLMs) achieve strong performance on downstream tasks but their internal biological representations remain opaque, and sparse autoencoders (SAEs)—a proven interpretability tool in vision and NLP—had not been systematically applied to gLMs. The paper addresses this gap by introducing a comprehensive benchmark of 55 human genomic concepts, genome-tailored evaluation metrics, and a feature representation method to quantify and organize SAE-derived features in genomic models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263403/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 10kb DNA sequence windows (matching Evo2's single-base-pair token resolution) from human chromosome 22 as held-out test sequences, alongside 55 genomic annotation categories sourced from GENCODE, MANE, ENCODE, JASPAR, RepeatMasker, and other databases. For the feature atlas, a 500kb representative sequence is used to capture diverse activation patterns. Outputs include per-feature activation signals scored against annotations via three complementary metrics (nucleotide-precision/domain-recall F1, univariate sparse probing AUPRC, and Gaussian-windowed enrichment for rare events), a low-dimensional node2vec embedding of the SAE dictionary, and per-variant perturbation scores with associated motif logos for disease analysis.

## Method

The core pipeline trains BatchTopK sparse autoencoders (16384 features, 4× expansion, Top-K=64) post-hoc on intermediate-layer embeddings extracted from Evo2 (Hyena architecture, 1M-token context) and Nucleotide Transformer v3. The key finding is that intermediate layers (e.g., layer 19–26 of Evo2) yield SAE features that outperform raw embeddings for 76% of genomic concepts, with 26 concepts achieving F1 > 0.7. For organizing the SAE dictionary, the authors propose a Tanimoto/Node2Vec algorithm that computes activation-pattern similarity over a 500kb sequence (smoothing → downsampling → ANN → Tanimoto affinity graph → node2vec embedding), which significantly outperforms the decoder-weight similarity baseline from InterPLM in kNN precision. A disease application links 15 SLE-associated variants to localized perturbations in regulatory-element features via log-ratio variant scores over 100bp windows, followed by motif extraction from activation peaks.

## Results

Using Evo2 as the primary gLM, SAE features outperform raw model embeddings for 42 out of 55 genomic concepts (76%), with 26 concepts achieving a modified F1 score above 0.7. For Nucleotide Transformer v3, SAEs win on 30/55 (55%) of concepts. Intermediate layers (e.g., layers 19–26 in Evo2) consistently yield the most interpretable SAE representations, while early and late layers favor raw embeddings. The proposed Tanimoto+Node2Vec feature atlas method achieves kNN precision of 0.420–0.626 across tested concepts, substantially outperforming the SAE decoder-weights baseline (0.130–0.581).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline is InterPLM (Simon & Zou, 2025), which represents SAE features using decoder weight similarity. The proposed Tanimoto+Node2Vec method outperforms this baseline across all four tested genomic concepts and all three k values (10, 25, 50), with the largest gains on Exons (0.420 vs. 0.130 at k=10, a 3.2× improvement) and Retroposon Repeats (0.421 vs. 0.232 at k=10, a 1.8× improvement). The second major comparison is SAE features versus raw gLM embeddings: for Evo2, SAEs achieve higher sparse-probing AUPRC on 42/55 concepts (76%), while embeddings win on only 13/55; for Nucleotide Transformer, the split is 30/55 vs. 25/55. Among the 13 concepts where embeddings win in Evo2, the median performance gap is small (0.031 AUPRC), whereas the median SAE gain on the 42 winning concepts is 0.084 AUPRC, indicating that SAEs provide a consistent and meaningful interpretability advantage over raw representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAE-based interpretability paradigm established for natural language models (Cunningham et al., 2023) and protein language models (InterPLM, Simon & Zou, 2025) to genomic language models, a domain where prior work was limited to a single-layer, small-feature-set exploration by Goodfire AI on Evo2. It adopts the BatchTopK SAE architecture (Bussmann et al., 2024) and adapts sparse probing (Gurnee et al., 2023) with a one-feature-at-a-time formulation suited to the monosemanticity goal. The key departure from InterPLM is the introduction of genome-specific evaluation metrics (nucleotide-level F1, sparse event activation) and a 55-concept benchmark drawn from diverse annotation sources, addressing the challenges of long sequences and sparse functional annotations that make genomic interpretability harder than its protein counterpart.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first systematic, multi-layer, multi-concept framework for interpreting genomic LMs, establishing a reusable benchmark and evaluation suite that the field can adopt as gLMs become more central to biomedical research. The demonstration that SAE features can link disease-associated variants (SLE) to interpretable regulatory perturbations opens a path toward clinically actionable model interpretability. Key open questions include whether findings generalize beyond Evo2 and Nucleotide Transformer, how to handle cell-type- and developmental-stage-specific annotations that limit ground-truth quality, and whether the computational cost of SAE training and feature atlas construction scales to the next generation of larger gLMs.
