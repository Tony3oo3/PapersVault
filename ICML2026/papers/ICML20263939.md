## Problem

Post-processing fair classifiers provide post-hoc controllability of the fairness-accuracy trade-off but suffer from suboptimal trade-off efficiency, while in-processing methods achieve efficient trade-offs but require costly retraining for each new trade-off parameter. This paper asks whether one can learn feature representations during training such that subsequent post-processing at any target fairness level yields trade-off efficiency comparable to in-processing methods, without any retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263939/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a feature vector (RGB face images for CelebA/UTKFace or tabular features for Adult/COMPAS) and a binary sensitive attribute, and produces a binary class prediction. During training, group-specific transformation networks g_a and logit predictors z_a are learned jointly. At inference, a single trained model is evaluated at multiple fairness tolerances δ via an O(n log n) binary search over group-specific thresholds, so adjusting the trade-off requires no retraining—only a threshold recomputation on the test set.

## Method

GFB (Guidance to Fairest-Boundary) is motivated by a theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) showing that the δ-dependent accuracy loss of a post-processed classifier is governed by how concentrated the conditional class-probability distributions are near the most fair threshold τ_a^η(0). The method learns a parametrized transformation g_a that reshapes the feature space so that induced logits concentrate near this threshold, thereby increasing the δ-dependent accuracy term. Training is formulated as a bi-level optimization: the inner problem minimizes a prediction loss L_pred for the logit predictor z_a, while the outer problem minimizes a combined objective L_gen = (1−λ)L_dist + λL_pred for the transformation g_a, where L_dist penalizes the distance of logits from the most fair threshold within the relevant interval. The bi-level problem is solved with MA-SOBA, using smooth sigmoid surrogates to handle the non-differentiable distance metric and an implicit-function-theorem-based gradient for the threshold parameter t̂₀. At inference, GFB applies the identical FairBayes post-processing (group-specific threshold search), preserving post-hoc controllability while benefiting from the improved representation learned during training.

## Results

GFB achieves the highest standard hypervolume (HV) on 3 of 4 datasets: CelebA (0.820), UTKFace (0.858), and Adult (0.801), while EPO slightly edges it on COMPAS (0.819 vs. 0.816). More importantly, GFB attains the lowest inverted HV (a measure of poorly performing trade-off points) across all four datasets, e.g., 0.227 on CelebA vs. 0.262 for FairBayes and 0.456 for EPO, and 0.101 on UTKFace vs. 0.156 for FairBayes. This indicates GFB not only covers favorable trade-off regions well but also avoids degenerate solutions that other methods produce at certain trade-off parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the post-processing baseline FairBayes, GFB achieves higher HV on all four datasets (e.g., 0.820 vs. 0.726 on CelebA, 0.816 vs. 0.760 on COMPAS) and lower inverted HV everywhere, confirming that the learned representation meaningfully improves the post-processing trade-off curve. Against YOTO, which also provides post-hoc controllability but requires ~2× model width, GFB dominates on both HV and inverted HV across all datasets (e.g., 0.858 vs. 0.748 on UTKFace). Against the in-processing method EPO, GFB wins on HV for CelebA, UTKFace, and Adult but trails by a narrow margin on COMPAS (0.816 vs. 0.819); however, GFB achieves lower inverted HV on all four datasets, indicating more stable trade-off behavior. Against FairBiNN, GFB wins on HV for CelebA (0.820 vs. 0.692) and Adult (0.801 vs. 0.755) but loses on UTKFace (0.858 vs. 0.888) and COMPAS (0.816 vs. 0.841); again, GFB's inverted HV is strictly lower on every dataset, suggesting FairBiNN's higher HV is driven by a few favorable points at the cost of poor solutions elsewhere. The key practical advantage is that GFB matches or exceeds in-processing methods while retaining post-hoc controllability—adjusting the fairness-accuracy trade-off at inference requires only an O(n log n) threshold search, not full retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FairBayes post-processing framework (Zeng et al., 2024), which provides post-hoc controllability of the fairness-accuracy trade-off via group-specific thresholding but suffers from suboptimal trade-off efficiency. The key departure is a theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.1) showing that post-processing efficiency is governed by how concentrated the learned feature distributions are near the most fair decision boundary, motivating a representation-learning objective (GFB) that reshapes logits during training. For optimization, the paper adapts the MA-SOBA bilevel optimizer (Chen et al., 2024) to handle the non-smooth distance metric and implicit threshold gradient, positioning the method between in-processing approaches (efficient but requiring retraining) and pure post-processing (controllable but inefficient).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Post-hoc controllability is practically critical in deployment scenarios where regulatory or policy requirements shift after model release (e.g., LLM compliance updates), making retraining infeasible. GFB's ability to match in-processing trade-off efficiency without retraining addresses a genuine gap, and the theoretical characterization of when post-processing is efficient provides a reusable design principle. However, the framework is currently restricted to binary sensitive attributes, binary labels, and primarily demographic parity; extending to equalized odds, multiclass settings, and intersectional groups remains open and would require substantially different threshold structures.
