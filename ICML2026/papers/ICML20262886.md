## Problem

Existing generative visuomotor policies (Diffusion Policy, DP3, FlowPolicy) treat observations solely as high-level conditioning signals to the denoising network, forcing the reverse sampling process to begin from uninformative random Gaussian noise. This underutilization weakens the coupling between perception and control, often yielding suboptimal precision in robotic manipulation. The paper asks whether observations can be embedded directly into the stochastic dynamics of the diffusion process itself so that sampling starts from an informative, observation-derived prior.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (downsampled to 512 or 1024 points in simulation, 2048 in real-world via farthest point sampling from a ZED-2i depth camera) and a proprioceptive robot state vector as input. These heterogeneous modalities are encoded by separate MLPs and fused via cross-attention into a single latent vector whose shape matches the action chunk. The output is a short action chunk (4 action steps over an 8-step horizon) that is executed directly by the robot, with no intermediate decoding step required.

## Method

BridgePolicy reformulates policy learning as a diffusion bridge problem under the UniDB stochastic optimal control framework, where the forward SDE connects the clean action (a₀) to the observation representation (a_T) rather than to Gaussian noise. A semantic aligner resolves the modality and shape mismatch: separate MLPs encode the robot state and point cloud, and a cross-attention fusion module produces a unified observation vector matching the action chunk dimensionality. A CLIP-style contrastive loss (weighted by α) is jointly trained with the L1 reconstruction loss to align the observation and action distributions in the shared latent space. At inference, the UniDB++ closed-form solver iteratively transforms the observation-derived starting point into an action in 10 steps, and Theorem 3.1 provides a linear error bound showing that small perturbations in the observation encoder propagate only weakly (empirically C ~ 10⁻²–10⁻³) to the generated action.

## Results

On 52 simulation tasks across three benchmarks (MetaWorld, Adroit, DexArt), BridgePolicy achieves an average success rate of 0.74, outperforming FlowPolicy (0.68), VITA (0.64), DP3 (0.60), and DP (0.37). The advantage is most pronounced on MetaWorld Medium (0.75 vs. 0.67 for FlowPolicy) and MetaWorld Very Hard (0.79 vs. 0.76), as well as on Adroit (0.81 vs. 0.70). On 5 real-world Franka Emika Panda tasks, BridgePolicy achieves a 0.90 average success rate, substantially exceeding DP3 (0.76) and FlowPolicy (0.56), with perfect scores on Oven-Closing and Oven-Opening.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BridgePolicy consistently outperforms all four baselines on the overall simulation average (0.74 vs. 0.68 FlowPolicy, 0.64 VITA, 0.60 DP3, 0.37 DP), with the largest margins on MetaWorld Medium (+8 pts over FlowPolicy) and Adroit (+11 pts over FlowPolicy). The only near-tie is MetaWorld Hard, where FlowPolicy edges ahead by 1 point (0.59 vs. 0.58). On real-world tasks, the gap widens dramatically: BridgePolicy scores 0.90 average versus 0.76 for DP3 and 0.56 for FlowPolicy, with FlowPolicy collapsing to 0.1 on the Pour task due to poor location generalization. VITA, which operates in a joint image-action latent space requiring a decoder, underperforms DP3 on most simulation tasks, suggesting the decoder introduces generalization error. The ablation in Table 5 further confirms that the diffusion bridge formulation itself (vs. standard diffusion or regression heads) contributes the largest single gain, e.g., 0.84 vs. 0.68 on Adroit Door.

## Relation to Prior Work

BridgePolicy builds directly on the UniDB/UniDB++ unified diffusion bridge framework (formulated via stochastic optimal control) and repurposes it for visuomotor policy learning, where prior generative policies (Diffusion Policy, DP3, FlowPolicy, VITA) treat observations solely as external conditions to the denoising network. The key conceptual departure is embedding observations into the forward SDE trajectory itself—setting the terminal state a_T to the observation representation rather than random Gaussian noise—so that sampling starts from an informative prior. To make this feasible with heterogeneous robot data (point clouds, proprioceptive states, actions of different dimensions), the paper introduces a semantic aligner combining cross-attention fusion and a CLIP-style contrastive loss, a component absent from prior diffusion-bridge work in image restoration or translation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The observation-embedded SDE provides a mathematically principled coupling between perception and control that prior conditional-diffusion policies lack, and Theorem 3.1 gives a linear error bound showing that encoder imperfections do not catastrophically propagate into generated actions. The consistent SOTA across 52 simulation tasks and 5 real-world tasks (with a notable 0.90 vs. 0.76 real-world gap over DP3) validates the approach, and the sample-efficiency results suggest the informative prior is especially valuable under data scarcity. A stated limitation is that the SDE formulation precludes distillation to one-step generation, leaving a gap versus flow-matching or consistency-based methods in inference speed; whether bridge-based policies can be distilled or combined with fast solvers beyond UniDB++ remains an open question.
