## Problem

Prior identifiability results for contrastive learning (e.g., Zimmermann et al., 2021) assume full-support von Mises-Fisher conditionals on the latent sphere, but real-world augmentation pipelines fix certain latent dimensions while perturbing others. This paper asks what happens to latent geometry recovery when this implicit diversity condition is violated, and how architectural inductive bias compensates for the resulting information deficit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265692/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder f: X → Z maps observations x ∈ R^n and their augmented views x̃ = τ(x) to L2-normalized embeddings in a k-dimensional latent space modeled as the unit hypersphere S^{k-1} (k < n). Positive pairs are drawn from a conditional law P_{Z̃|z} that may be restricted to a lower-dimensional submanifold K(z) ⊂ S^{k-1} (fixing m of k latent coordinates), while negatives are sampled either from the full sphere or, in the adapted variant, from K(z) itself. The output is a spherical embedding whose pairwise inner products encode the recovered latent geometry.

## Method

The paper formalizes a measure-theoretic diversity condition (P_Z ≪ P_{Z̃|z} a.s.) and proves it is necessary for isometric latent recovery. Under full-support vMF conditionals, the diversity condition holds automatically, and a probabilistic Mazur-Ulam argument shows that global minimizers of the asymptotic InfoNCE loss are orthogonal maps (no differentiability required). When diversity is violated by constraining positive-pair sampling to a submanifold K(z), the paper constructs an explicit non-orthogonal map h_λ that attains strictly lower asymptotic contrastive loss than any orthogonal map, proving that the standard objective actively disincentivizes geometry-preserving solutions. To address this, a support-corrected InfoNCE variant restricts the model conditional's normalization to K(z), making orthogonal solutions achievable (Theorem 4.6) but not unique. Experiments on five synthetic generative processes and CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, ViT, and MLP encoders confirm that inductive bias becomes increasingly critical as sampling diversity decreases.

## Results

On synthetic spherical latent spaces (S²), when the diversity condition holds, a standard MLP encoder achieves near-perfect linear identifiability (R² ≥ 0.99) across all five generative processes, confirming the theoretical prediction of orthogonal recovery. When diversity is violated (one latent dimension fixed during positive-pair sampling), standard InfoNCE collapses to R² ∈ [0.05, 0.72], while the paper's support-corrected (adapted) InfoNCE recovers to R² ≈ 0.60–0.65, and inverse-encoder inductive bias restores R² ≥ 0.88. On CIFAR-10 with SimCLR, linear probe accuracy follows the predicted hierarchy ResNet-18 > ViT > MLP across all three augmentation regimes, and the inter-architecture gap widens substantially when cropping is removed (reducing sampling diversity).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against standard InfoNCE (van den Oord et al., 2019) as implemented in SimCLR. Under the full-support vMF setting where the diversity condition holds, standard InfoNCE already achieves R² ≈ 1.00, so no method gains an advantage. The critical comparison emerges under violated diversity: standard InfoNCE drops to R² = 0.05–0.25 for most generative processes (Identity, Linear, Invertible MLP, Patches), while the paper's adapted InfoNCE (support-corrected negatives drawn from the constrained submanifold K(z)) recovers to R² ≈ 0.60–0.65, and inverse-encoder inductive bias achieves R² ≥ 0.88. Thus, the adapted loss partially closes the gap (roughly 3× improvement over standard InfoNCE) but does not match inductive bias, confirming the theoretical prediction that support correction makes isometric solutions achievable but not uniquely selected. On CIFAR-10, the qualitative comparison across architectures (ResNet-18, ViT, MLP) and augmentation regimes (All, Crop Only, All w/o crop) shows that the performance gap between high- and low-inductive-bias encoders widens as sampling diversity decreases, with the MLP showing nearly identical accuracy under 'All' and 'Crop Only' regimes—indicating it cannot exploit additional augmentations without appropriate architectural priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiability framework of Zimmermann et al. (2021), which proved that InfoNCE recovers latent factors up to orthogonal transformation under full-support von Mises-Fisher conditionals, by introducing a measure-theoretic 'diversity condition' that is necessary for isometric recovery and showing it is violated by most practical augmentation pipelines. It departs from the invariance/content-style explanation of contrastive learning (von Kügelgen et al., 2021) in favor of a recovery explanation, arguing that the sampling mechanism's support structure—not just which factors are invariant—determines whether latent geometry is preserved. The proof technique draws on a probabilistic Mazur-Ulam theorem (Zaliaduonis & Gatidis, 2026) to extend almost-everywhere distance preservation to global orthogonal maps without differentiability assumptions, and the support-corrected InfoNCE variant modifies the model conditional to match the constrained sampling support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that violated diversity makes non-orthogonal maps attain strictly lower asymptotic contrastive loss than any orthogonal map—reframes a common practical failure mode as an active misincentive of the objective rather than a mere optimization difficulty, with implications for how augmentation pipelines and encoder architectures should be co-designed. However, the adapted InfoNCE correction is explicitly a theoretical diagnostic requiring knowledge of the invariant component, and the analysis is restricted to spherical latent spaces with vMF conditionals, leaving open how to estimate and correct for diversity violation from data in general settings. The CIFAR-10 experiments provide only qualitative consistency checks rather than direct identifiability tests, and the practical gap between the theoretical fix and deployable training procedures remains substantial.
