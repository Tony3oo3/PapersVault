## Problem

Standard long-context fine-tuning of LLMs produces models whose accuracy depends strongly on the absolute position of relevant evidence within the input, exhibiting high positional variance even when task format and difficulty are held constant. This positional brittleness is undesirable in practical pipelines like RAG and multi-document reasoning, where the same evidence span can appear at widely different locations across queries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single long-context token sequence (64K tokens for Llama-3-8B, 256K for Qwen-3-4B) and produces next-token distributions under a causal mask. During training, the same token sequence is processed in two forward passes that differ only in their RoPE index assignment: a standard pass with sequential indices and a perturbed pass with a contiguous suffix shifted by a sampled offset. At inference, only the standard (unperturbed) indexing is used, so no architectural or serving-time changes are required.

## Method

RoPE-Perturbed Self-Distillation creates an alternative 'view' of each training sequence by sampling a split point s and skip length y, then adding offset y to the RoPE indices of all tokens in the suffix [s, L-1] while leaving the prefix [0, s-1] unchanged. The model is trained with two forward passes: the standard view is optimized with the usual causal LM loss, while the perturbed view is regularized to match the standard view's next-token distribution via a reverse KL divergence with stop-gradient on the standard path, so gradients flow only through the perturbed branch. The distillation loss is applied only to suffix positions (i ≥ s), where the two views actually differ. The total objective is L_CLM + λ·L_KL with λ=1, adding one extra forward pass per step. The skip-based perturbation is order-preserving, which ablations show is critical: structure-disrupting alternatives like NoPE or chunked permutation degrade performance, while the skip shift preserves local monotonic token order and yields the strongest gains.

## Results

On Llama-3-8B-Instruct at 64K context, the skip-based variant achieves 69.29 on RULER (vs. 57.25 for Standard, a 12.04 pp gain) and 47.88 on HELMET (vs. 44.30), while the cyclic-shift variant reaches 71.30 on RULER-64K. On Qwen-3-4B at 256K, the combined Ours(skip)+LongCE variant attains 68.95 on RULER (vs. 67.01 Standard) and 53.59 on HELMET (vs. 51.80). After instruction SFT, the combined variant achieves 67.15 on RULER-256K (a 2.71 pp gain over Standard's 64.44) and 32.22 on LongBench-v2 (vs. 27.63). Short-context performance (MMLU, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, OpenBookQA) remains within ~0.5 pp of the baseline across both model families.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: LongCE (Fang et al., ICLR 2025), which reweights CLM tokens by likelihood ratio, and PoSE (Zhu et al., 2023), which trains CLM on a perturbed RoPE view without a consistency loss. On Llama-3-8B at 64K, the proposed skip variant (69.29 RULER) outperforms both LongCE (52.51) and PoSE (67.47) by 16.78 and 1.82 pp respectively, while the cyclic variant (71.30) leads by an even wider margin. On Qwen-3-4B at 128K, the cyclic variant (78.37 RULER) beats LongCE (76.87) and PoSE (75.47), and at 256K the combined Ours(skip)+LongCE (68.95) edges out LongCE (68.01) and PoSE (65.48). On HELMET, the skip variant consistently achieves the best single-technique average (47.88 on Llama, 52.99 on Qwen) over both baselines. The combined Ours(skip)+LongCE variant, which is orthogonal in motivation, achieves the strongest overall scores across all benchmarks, suggesting the two techniques are complementary rather than redundant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the positional-perturbation idea from PoSE (Zhu et al., 2023), which trains CLM on shifted RoPE indices, by wrapping it in a self-distillation consistency objective: instead of treating the perturbed view as additional training data, the model is trained to match its standard-view predictions under shifted indices via a reverse-KL regularizer. It is positioned as orthogonal to RoPE extension methods (YaRN, ABF, LongRoPE) that modify the frequency schedule, and complementary to LongCE (Fang et al., 2025), which reweights the CLM signal; the two are shown to combine additively. The broader conceptual lineage draws on consistency regularization across augmented views (analogous to semi-supervised or data-augmentation regularizers), but the specific instantiation—perturbing only RoPE indices while keeping tokens and causal mask fixed—is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Positional brittleness is a practical bottleneck for RAG pipelines, multi-document QA, and codebase-level reasoning where evidence placement varies across queries; a simple training-time regularizer that reduces this sensitivity without architectural changes is directly deployable in existing long-context continued-pretraining recipes. The gains persisting after instruction SFT and under YaRN-based length extrapolation suggest the method improves an underlying capability rather than a benchmark-specific artifact. Open questions include whether the principle extends to non-RoPE positional encodings, whether task-aware perturbation distributions (e.g., biased toward mid-context) yield further gains, and how the method scales to much larger models or ultra-long (>1M) contexts where the 1.6× forward-pass overhead becomes more costly.
