## Problem

Existing logic benchmarks predominantly test deduction from given specifications, leaving open whether language and reasoning models can perform the complementary task of induction: discovering a compact first-order formula that explains extensional labels across multiple finite relational worlds. The paper asks whether prompted models can synthesize intensional definitions that generalize to new structures rather than overfitting the observed finite cases, and whether formula compactness serves as a measurable proxy for genuine concept recovery.

## Input / Output

Each instance provides k finite Σ-structures (domain sizes 5–12) over a fixed signature of two unary predicates (P, Q) and two binary predicates (R, S), together with a target extension T_W labeling which objects belong to an unknown unary concept. The system must output a single first-order formula φ(x) in Lisp-style S-expression syntax with exactly one free variable, evaluated under standard finite-model semantics. Three regimes alter the validity condition: FULLOBS requires exact extensional match in every world, CI requires matching YES worlds while not exactly matching NO worlds, and EC requires existence of a world-local completion of masked atoms under which the formula matches the target. Correctness is checked mechanically via grounding and Z3, and formula complexity is tracked through AST node count and quantifier depth.

## Method

INDUCTION is a solver-verifiable benchmark built around a controlled generation pipeline: gold formulas are drawn from a bounded pool of ~200 structurally tagged templates, and finite worlds are constructed with kill-tracking so that each accepted world eliminates at least one surviving hypothesis from a ~1,500-candidate distractor pool, with rejection filters removing instances solvable by atomic, subformula, or quantifier-free shortcuts. CI instances use trap-based contrastive construction that preserves plausible shortcuts through YES worlds and then builds NO worlds to kill those specific survivors, while EC instances mask 20% of binary atoms and check validity via world-local existential completion in Z3. Evaluation pairs unbounded task success with budgeted metrics (Acc@gold+Δ) and bloat rate, and held-out generalization tests (5 new worlds per FullObs instance, 3 YES + 2 NO per CI instance) to separate compact concept recovery from case-splitting overfit. Symbolic baselines include a unified Z3 prenex formula synthesizer, a FullObs-specific Z3 portfolio with schema encodings, and a restricted ILASP answer-set-programming comparator, all under a 30-minute per-instance budget.

## Results

No single prompted model dominates all three INDUCTION regimes. Grok4 leads FullObs with 50.7% accuracy (46.7% Acc@gold+25) but only 89.3% coverage; GPT-5.2 retains the best raw CI accuracy (82.5%) while Gemini 3.5 Flash achieves the best budgeted CI score (78.0% Acc@gold+25); and GPT-5.4 clearly leads EC with 93.5% validity and 64.0% Acc@gold+25. The central empirical finding is that compactness predicts generalization: among instance-correct FullObs formulas, near-gold solutions (AST ≤ gold+1) generalize at 77.3–97.6% on held-out worlds, whereas above-gold solutions drop to 6.7–73.3%, with the gap reaching +72.8 points for GPT-5.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 14 prompted language/reasoning models alongside three symbolic synthesis baselines (z3-prenex+rescue, z3-ad-mix, ilasp-frag) under identical exact-semantics evaluation. The unified SMT baseline z3-prenex+rescue is competitive on CI (82.0%, matching GPT-5.2's 82.5%) and achieves 100% on EC, but is substantially weaker on FullObs (48.8%); the more engineered z3-ad-mix portfolio closes much of that FullObs gap at 65.9%, surpassing all prompted models. ilasp-frag, a restricted ILP/ASP comparator, is far weaker (15.7% FullObs, 45.5% CI), confirming that the benchmark is not solved by a single generic symbolic approach. Among prompted models, no single system dominates: Grok4 leads FullObs (50.7% vs. 43.5% for GPT-5.4) but trails on EC (53.0% vs. 93.5%); GPT-5.4 improves over GPT-5.2 in parsimony (bloat drops from 24.3% to 11.5% on FullObs, 9.5% to 3.0% on CI) while trading a small amount of raw accuracy for better budgeted scores. The held-out generalization analysis further shows that even among instance-correct solutions, compact near-gold formulas outperform bloated ones by 36–87 points on FullObs, a gap that persists (though smaller, 2–15 points) on CI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of inductive logic programming (Muggleton 1991; Quinlan 1990), constraint-based program synthesis (Solar-Lezama 2008; Alur et al. 2013), and LLM reasoning evaluation (FOLIO, ProofWriter). It repurposes ILP's core idea—learning a symbolic relational definition from extensional examples—and program synthesis's counterexample-guided refinement (constructing worlds to kill shortcut hypotheses) into a solver-verifiable benchmark that evaluates both prompted language models and SMT/ASP baselines under identical exact semantics. Unlike prior logical-reasoning benchmarks that test deduction from given rules, INDUCTION tests inductive hypothesis discovery, and unlike standard ILP systems it does not propose a new learning algorithm but instead provides a controlled diagnostic testbed with bloat-aware metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that instance validity and concept recovery are measurably distinct, with compact near-gold formulas generalizing at 77–97% on held-out worlds versus 7–73% for bloated ones—provides a concrete, solver-checkable argument that parsimony is not aesthetic preference but a signal of whether a formula captures a stable concept. The structural error analysis (universal-to-existential collapse) gives an interpretable failure taxonomy for future work on LLM symbolic reasoning. Limitations include the deliberately small signature (two unary, two binary predicates), template-bounded gold formulas, and non-unique explanations on finite worlds; open questions include whether the bloat phenomenon persists under richer vocabularies, larger domains, or with explicit compactness prompting, and whether the three-regime decomposition transfers to other logical formalisms.
