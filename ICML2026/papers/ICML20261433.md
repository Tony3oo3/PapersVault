## Problem

State-of-the-art neural surrogates for aerodynamic simulations (e.g., Transolver, AB-UPT) use the CFD-optimized simulation mesh as an active input feature that shapes their learned representations, but generating such a mesh for a new geometry can take up to 30 minutes and, for adaptive meshing, requires running a full simulation first. Existing mesh-free alternatives avoid this cost but suffer from substantially higher prediction errors. SMART addresses this trade-off by achieving mesh-dependent-level accuracy while requiring only a cheap point-cloud geometry at inference.

## Input / Output

The model takes a geometry point cloud of N points (extracted from a CAD/STL mesh, not a CFD-optimized mesh), simulation parameters ξ (e.g., yaw angle, Mach number, angle of attack), and an arbitrary set of M query positions, and outputs N_c physical quantities (surface pressure, 3D velocity) at each query location. Queries are treated independently—no self-attention among them—so the full field of millions of points can be evaluated sequentially in subregions to keep GPU memory constant. The geometry resolution N and query count M are decoupled via a fixed-size latent representation, allowing independent scaling.

## Method

SMART is an encoder-decoder Transformer with L blocks. The geometry encoder compresses the N-point input into K-point latent geometries (N ≫ K) by iteratively cross-attending to randomly subsampled geometry points, with FiLM-style modulation injecting simulation parameters at each block. The physics decoder maps query embeddings to physical quantities via cross-attention to the encoder's intermediate latent geometries. The key architectural insight is the cross-layer geometry-physics update: each decoder block attends to the corresponding encoder block's intermediate latent geometry (not just the final output), enabling joint refinement of geometric and physical representations across all depths and multi-scale geometric grounding. A modulated positional encoding with learned per-frequency scaling adapts the sinusoidal embedding to the local spectral content of each coordinate. Training uses random subsampling of query points per batch to make millions-of-point domains tractable on a single GPU.

## Results

On the primary subsampled evaluation (16k surface + 16k volume points), SMART achieves the lowest relative L2 error across all four datasets: 6.77/5.02 (ShapeNetCar), 3.12/5.15 (AhmedML), 0.0175/6.26 (SHIFT-SUV), and 0.0557/14.66 (SHIFT-Wing) for surface/volume respectively. On full-resolution industry-scale evaluation (millions of points), SMART maintains its lead, while GNOT and Transolver run out of memory on an A100 80GB GPU. When the CFD-optimized simulation mesh is replaced with a CAD surface mesh and uniform volume grid (SHIFT-SUV Uniform), mesh-dependent models degrade sharply (e.g., MD AB-UPT volume error jumps to 141×10⁻²) while SMART remains robust at 5.95×10⁻².

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMART outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of surface and volume metrics across all four datasets. The closest competitors are the two AB-UPT variants: MD AB-UPT (mesh-dependent) is within 1–11% on most metrics but is beaten by SMART on every dataset except AhmedML volume (5.18 vs. 5.15, a 1% gap), while MF AB-UPT (mesh-free) is competitive on automotive datasets but catastrophically fails on SHIFT-Wing volume (39.47 vs. SMART's 14.66, a 169% increase), making SMART the only mesh-free model that accurately predicts the full flow field on the aerospace dataset. GNOT and Transolver are competitive on SHIFT-SUV (within 5–9%) but degrade significantly on SHIFT-Wing and suffer out-of-memory failures at full resolution due to self-attention over queries. GP-UPT is competitive on ShapeNetCar and AhmedML (within 3–11%) but lags substantially on SHIFT-Wing (176% higher surface error, 172% higher volume error). GINO, OFormer, and LNO are consistently the weakest baselines, with errors 36–7680% above SMART depending on the dataset and metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMART builds directly on the Universal Physics Transformer (UPT) and AB-UPT lineage (Alkin et al., 2024; 2025a), adopting the anchor-attention mechanism and the encode-process-decode paradigm for large-scale aerodynamic surrogates. Its key architectural departure is replacing the simulation-mesh-dependent anchor set with an iterative geometry encoder that cross-attends to subsampled points from the raw point cloud, combined with a cross-layer decoder that attends to intermediate encoder representations rather than only the final output. The modulated positional encoding and FiLM-style parameter conditioning are borrowed from prior work (Parmar et al., 2018; Perez et al., 2018), while the overall problem framing—predicting PDE solutions from geometry via neural operators—follows the GINO, Transolver, and GNOT line of research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for iterative automotive and aerospace design: eliminating the 30-minute (or longer, for adaptive meshes) simulation-mesh generation step removes a major bottleneck in the design loop, and the mesh-free property is essential for datasets like SHIFT-Wing where the adaptive mesh only exists after a full simulation. Key limitations remain: the model handles only time-independent flows, does not enforce conservation laws, and relies on random query subsampling that may underrepresent rare localized phenomena. Open questions include extending to transient CFD, other engineering domains (turbomachinery, biomedical), and whether physics-informed training objectives can further close the gap to full solver accuracy.
