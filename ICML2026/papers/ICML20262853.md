## Problem

Prior continual learning research in robotics has focused almost exclusively on small behavior cloning policies trained from scratch, where catastrophic forgetting is pervasive and demands large replay buffers or complex regularization. Whether large pretrained Vision-Language-Action models exhibit fundamentally different continual learning dynamics—particularly regarding forgetting, transfer, and knowledge retention—remains unexplored. This paper asks whether pretrained VLAs behave differently in continual learning and, if so, in what ways.

## Input / Output

The VLA policy consumes multi-view RGB images, a natural language instruction, and proprioceptive state at each timestep, and outputs an action chunk (a sequence of future actions) predicted by an action head (flow-matching or GMM) conditioned on the VLM backbone's latent representation. In the continual learning protocol, a single task-conditioned policy π(·|s,T) sequentially learns K tasks, each specified by a unique initial-state distribution and goal predicate while sharing state, action, and transition spaces; performance is measured via per-task success rate and negative backward transfer (NBT) across four LIBERO suites and a real-robot Mutex suite.

## Method

This is a comprehensive empirical study rather than a new algorithm: the authors evaluate pretrained VLAs (Pi0, GR00T N1.5) against non-pretrained small policies (BC-Transformer, BC-ViT, BC-Diffusion Policy) under sequential task learning with Experience Replay on four LIBERO benchmarks, finding that simple ER with as little as 2% replay data yields near-zero or even positive backward transfer on VLAs while non-pretrained models require >20% replay for comparable performance. To isolate pretraining's contribution, they compare three Pi0 variants (full VL+Action pretraining, VLM-only initialization from PaliGemma, and from-scratch training) across replay buffer sizes of 10, 100, and 1000 samples, showing that pretraining reduces forgetting most sharply in low-data regimes while simultaneously maintaining high forward transfer. They further decompose where knowledge is lost via a component-swapping protocol that pairs the VL backbone and action head from different training stages, revealing that the VL backbone is the dominant source of forgetting while the action head retains relatively consistent action-relevant information. Finally, they demonstrate that seemingly forgotten task knowledge is still encoded in the VLA's internal representations: finetuning the VL backbone on a prior task recovers peak performance in under 10% of the original training steps, whereas non-pretrained BC-Transformer requires comparable or greater steps, indicating genuine knowledge retention rather than mere parameter proximity.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmarks (sample size = 1000, ~20% of dataset per task), pretrained VLA models achieve near-zero or negative Negative Backward Transfer (NBT) while maintaining high success rates: GR00T N1.5 reaches 91.9% average SR with NBT = 0.027, and Pi0 reaches 76.8% average SR with NBT = −0.016 (indicating positive backward transfer). In contrast, non-pretrained BC baselines suffer substantial forgetting: BC-Transformer achieves only 58.5% SR with NBT = 0.245, and BC-ViT achieves 50.8% SR with NBT = 0.193. A real-robot pilot study (3 tasks, Pi0 + ER) confirms the trend with average SR = 35.4% and NBT = −0.003.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LIBERO BC baselines (BC-Transformer, BC-ViT, BC-Diffusion Policy from Liu et al. 2023), pretrained VLAs dominate on both success rate and forgetting: GR00T achieves 91.9% average SR vs. 50.8–69.6% for BC models, while its NBT (0.027) is 4–9× lower than the BC baselines (0.127–0.245). Pi0 similarly shows near-zero NBT (−0.016) compared to 0.127–0.245 for BC models. Against EWC (Kirkpatrick et al. 2017), the advantage is even more stark: on LIBERO-Object, ER with Pi0 yields NBT = −0.007 versus EWC's 0.608 and Sequential's 0.696; for GR00T, ER gives NBT = 0.004 versus EWC's 0.766. This demonstrates that simple experience replay with pretrained VLAs is far more effective than classical regularization-based continual learning methods, which were designed for smaller models trained from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the standard continual learning evaluation framework (LIBERO benchmark, NBT metric, experience replay) from the small-model regime to study large pretrained VLA models (Pi0, GR00T N1.5). Prior work on continual learning in robotics has focused almost exclusively on small behavior-cloning policies trained from scratch, where catastrophic forgetting is pervasive and requires large replay buffers or explicit regularization (EWC, distillation). The paper positions pretrained VLAs as a distinct continual learning regime, drawing on the broader observation from NLP that pretraining alters interference patterns, but provides the first systematic empirical characterization of this effect in robotic manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that simple experience replay with as little as 2% of training data achieves near-zero forgetting on pretrained VLAs has direct practical implications: lifelong robot learning pipelines may not need increasingly complex continual learning algorithms, but rather strong pretraining and modest replay. The knowledge-retention result (rapid recovery via minimal finetuning) opens a new axis for mitigating forgetting—reusing retained representations rather than relying solely on larger buffers. Key limitations include evaluation primarily in simulation on a single embodiment (Franka), a small real-robot pilot (K=3 tasks, single run), and mechanistic analyses focused mainly on Pi0, leaving open whether these dynamics generalize across embodiments, simulators, and VLA architectures.
