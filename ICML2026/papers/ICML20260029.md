## Problem

RANDOMSHUFFLE achieves O(1/T²) convergence for strongly convex objectives, outperforming SGD's O(1/T), but generating a uniformly random permutation requires Ω(min(N, (N/B)·log_{M/B}(N/B))) I/Os in the external-memory model, far exceeding the O(N/B) cost of a linear scan. The paper asks whether a shuffle with optimal linear I/O cost can still recover the convergence benefits of full random reshuffling.

## Input / Output

The algorithms operate on a sequence of N atomic elements stored in n = ⌈N/B⌉ blocks on slow (disk) memory, with a fast memory of M words, under the tall-cache assumption M = Ω(B²). GEN-2-WISE-IND-PERM requires N to be prime and produces an exactly 2-wise independent permutation via an affine map over the finite field. IO SHUFFLE produces an almost k-wise independent permutation with total ℓ₁ error O(k²B²/N), valid for k ≤ √N/B, and reads and writes each element exactly twice.

## Method

GEN-2-WISE-IND-PERM implements the Carter–Wegman affine permutation f_{a,b}(i) = ai + b mod N by organizing reads and writes into overlapping 'squares' of size ~B² centered at each element; a combinatorial lemma (Lemma 3.2) guarantees each element belongs to at most 4 such squares, yielding O(N/B) total I/Os. IO SHUFFLE performs two identical rounds: in each round it randomly permutes the block list, loads B blocks at a time, shuffles elements within each block, forms a B×B matrix, transposes it, shuffles the resulting rows, and writes them out; the transposition is the key mechanism that breaks within-block correlations that local shuffling alone cannot eliminate. The convergence analysis shows that 2-wise independence of the per-epoch permutation is sufficient to replicate all guarantees from Gürbüzbalaban et al. (2021) and Haochen & Sra (2019), because those proofs only require pairwise uniformity of index pairs and the strong law of large numbers under pairwise independence (Etemadi, 1981).

## Results

On N=16,000,057 elements with block size B=400 (40,000 repetitions), the expected number of preserved block-pairs under a uniform random permutation is ≈79,600.5. IO Shuffle (2 rounds) achieves a median of 79,598 (IQR 79,335–79,867), matching the Fisher-Yates baseline (median 79,600) almost exactly. In stark contrast, CorgiPile (1 round) retains a median of 8,017,871 preserved pairs—roughly 100× the expected uniform value—indicating severe residual clustering. Gen-2-Wise-Ind-Perm yields a median of 0 preserved pairs (mean 74,305), reflecting its exact element-level 2-wise independence but a highly skewed block-level distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its shuffling algorithms against two baselines: CorgiPile (Xu et al., 2024), the prior I/O-optimal shuffling method, and Fisher-Yates, the gold-standard uniform random permutation. IO Shuffle (2 rounds) matches Fisher-Yates almost exactly on the block-level preserved-pair metric (median 79,598 vs. 79,600; mean 79,603 vs. 79,601), confirming that two rounds of the proposed algorithm produce a permutation statistically indistinguishable from a fully uniform shuffle at the block granularity. CorgiPile, by contrast, retains a median of 8,017,871 preserved pairs—approximately 100× the expected uniform value—demonstrating that its single-round cache-level shuffle fails to break block-level clustering. IO Shuffle with only one round (median 60,591) falls below the expected uniform value, indicating it is not yet fully mixed; the second round is essential to reach uniformity. Gen-2-Wise-Ind-Perm achieves exact 2-wise independence at the element level (median 0 preserved pairs) but exhibits a highly skewed block-level distribution (mean 74,305), making IO Shuffle (2 rounds) the more practically uniform option for SGD convergence guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied threads: the convergence theory of RANDOMSHUFFLE over SGD (Gürbüzbalaban et al. 2021, Haochen & Sra 2019, Nagaraj et al. 2019) and the I/O model analysis of data movement (Aggarwal & Vitter 1988). It departs from CorgiPile (Xu et al. 2024), which achieves O(N/B) I/Os by locally shuffling cache blocks but provides no formal guarantees on the global randomness of the resulting permutation, by introducing a matrix-transposition-based two-round shuffle (IO SHUFFLE) and an affine-mapping shuffle (GEN-2-WISE-IND-PERM) that provably produce k-wise or almost k-wise independent permutations. The key conceptual departure from the full-uniformity assumption in prior convergence analyses is the observation that only 2-wise independence of the permutation is needed to replicate all existing RANDOMSHUFFLE guarantees, drawing on the k-wise independence literature (Alon & Lovett 2012) and the Etemadi (1981) extension of the strong law of large numbers to pairwise-independent sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that 2-wise independent permutations suffice for all known RANDOMSHUFFLE convergence guarantees is a clean theoretical simplification that, combined with the I/O-optimal generation algorithms, shows that the O(N/B) I/O cost of a linear scan is compatible with O(1/T²) convergence under strong convexity. However, the paper is explicitly 'strictly theoretical': it provides no experiments on real training tasks, is limited to the strongly convex regime, and requires the tall-cache assumption M = Ω(B²) (which the authors argue is satisfied in practice but which rules out some memory hierarchies). Open questions include extending the analysis to non-strongly convex and non-convex objectives, deriving cache-oblivious versions independent of B and M, and understanding how the small approximation error in almost k-wise permutations (O(k²B²/N)) affects high-probability convergence bounds.
