## Problem

Extending pretrained Video LLMs to new modalities (audio, 3D, high-frame-rate, egocentric) typically requires large modality-specific encoders, complex fusion modules, and paired cross-modal supervision, making the process costly and poorly scalable. This paper asks whether the internalized visual pathway in a Video LLM can be directly repurposed for novel sensory inputs without architectural overhaul or paired data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263590/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

All heterogeneous inputs are transformed into image-like tensors consumable by the frozen SigLIP vision encoder: audio becomes normalized log-mel spectrograms, depth maps become 3-channel disparity images via LUT, high-frame-rate video uses a 4-frame spatial stacking strategy (downsampled 0.5× and tiled), and egocentric video is fed directly. The model outputs free-form text answers via autoregressive decoding from the Qwen-2 LLM, conditioned on both video tokens and new-modality tokens projected through the shared 2-layer MLP projector.

## Method

V-LynX exploits the discovery that Video LLMs carve out a continuous 'token interface' manifold where visual tokens form a compact, near-orthogonal geometric region distinct from vocabulary embeddings. A lightweight LoRA adapter (Δψ, rank 64) is inserted into the frozen vision encoder's self-attention layers to route new-modality inputs through the same architectural path as video. Training uses only unpaired unimodal data with two objectives: (1) attention response alignment, which minimizes L1 distance between the new modality's attention outputs and a reference computed by substituting video-derived mean Key/Value statistics, and (2) distribution regularization, which matches the mean and variance of projected tokens to video token statistics. A second LoRA (Δφ) in the LLM is then trained via standard instruction-tuning loss on paired QA data, requiring no modality-specific encoder or cross-modal pairs at any stage.

## Results

V-LynX achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four modality expansion tasks. On audio-visual QA, V-LynX-7B reaches 163.0 CIDEr on AVSD, 94.2% accuracy on AVQA, and 83.0% overall accuracy on MUSIC-AVQA, while V-LynX-0.5B achieves 145.7 CIDEr / 93.1% / 81.1% with only 68.7M additional parameters. On 3D reasoning, V-LynX-7B attains 107.4 CIDEr and 29.7 EM@1 on ScanQA and 60.5 EM@1 on SQA3D. For high-frame-rate video understanding, V-LynX-7B scores 62.7 on VideoMME, 61.2 on MVBench, and 68.4 on MLVU, and for multi-view DPE it reaches 46.9% accuracy—all with 195.0M extra parameters versus 256.7M–500.5M for PAVE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PAVE (Liu et al., 2025), the closest prior method, V-LynX wins on every benchmark at both model sizes while using substantially fewer parameters: at 0.5B, V-LynX adds 68.7M vs. PAVE's 127.6M (audio) or 345.9M (3D); at 7B, 195.0M vs. 256.7M (audio) or 475.0M (3D). V-LynX-7B improves over PAVE-7B by +10.1 CIDEr on AVSD, +0.4% on AVQA, +0.7% on MUSIC-AVQA overall, +4.0 CIDEr on ScanQA, +1.5 EM@1 on SQA3D, +2.8 on VideoMME, +3.2 on MVBench, +1.4 on MLVU, and +2.7% on DPE. Against LLaVA-OV-FT (instruction tuning without modality data), V-LynX gains +28.1 CIDEr on AVSD and +13.5% on MUSIC-AVQA overall at 0.5B, confirming that interface alignment is essential. V-LynX-7B also surpasses task-specific 3D models (3D-LLM-7B, LEO-7B, Scene-LLM-7B, LLaVA-3D-7B) on ScanQA CIDEr (107.4 vs. 74.5–101.4) and SQA3D EM@1 (60.5 vs. 49.8–55.6), and beats CAT-7B-FT on AVQA (94.2 vs. 92.0). The only marginal loss is MVBench fine-grained pose (−0.5% vs. PAVE-7B), attributed to resolution loss from frame-stacking downsampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LLaVA-OV as its Video LLM backbone but departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm of modality extension (PAVE, Video-LLaMA, VideoLLaMA2) that requires dedicated modality-specific encoders and paired cross-modal supervision. Instead, it identifies an emergent 'token interface'—a continuous geometric manifold where visual tokens operate as standalone entities distinct from vocabulary embeddings—and repurposes the frozen vision encoder via LoRA to map new modalities into this existing space. The training objectives (attention response alignment and distribution regularization on unpaired unimodal data) draw on domain adaptation ideas (Deep CORAL, MMD) but apply them at the encoder-attention level rather than on raw features, which is a novel positioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

V-LynX offers a practically important recipe for scaling Video LLMs to new modalities with 40–80% fewer additional parameters than PAVE and no paired cross-modal data, lowering the barrier for data-scarce domains and resource-constrained deployments. The key open question is the inherent ceiling: because all new modalities are forced through the visual token interface, purely non-visual reasoning (e.g., identifying an instrument from background music alone) remains fundamentally limited. Future work could explore whether the interface can be expanded or whether hybrid routing (visual interface + modality-native pathway) can overcome this representational bottleneck.
