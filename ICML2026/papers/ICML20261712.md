## Problem

I-JEPA's masked feature prediction task suffers from high uncertainty at masked positions, where multiple plausible predictions exist (e.g., a bookshelf or a clean wall), making the method sensitive to masking strategy and prone to representation collapse. This paper addresses the gap by showing that image captions can explicitly reduce this prediction uncertainty, turning I-JEPA into a text-conditional representation learner that produces more semantically meaningful patch features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261712/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the method consumes 224×224 RGB images paired with N=8 randomly sampled text captions (synthesized via ShareGPT4V, averaging 8.3–8.7 sentences per image on ImageNet, or raw+synthetic captions on CC12M/YFCC15M). Captions are mapped to word embedding sequences via a frozen T5 encoder rather than CLIP's text encoder, to better preserve compositional and ordering information. At inference time, only the ViT encoder (f_θ) is retained to produce patch-level visual representations; the predictor and text conditioner are discarded, so no text is needed at test time.

## Method

TC-JEPA conditions the I-JEPA predictor g_φ on text by inserting lightweight cross-attention layers at every predictor depth, where patch features (queries) attend to word embedding sequences (keys/values), enabling patch-specific, fine-grained text modulation rather than holistic caption-level conditioning. When multiple captions are available, each independently conditions the predictor and the resulting patch features are fused via max-pooling at each layer. Two regularization terms—L1 sparsity on rectified patch-word cosine similarities and cross-layer consistency—steer the self-supervised learning of patch-word correspondences toward sparse, stable, and semantically meaningful alignments without any grounding annotations. The entire text conditioner is jointly optimized with the predictor under the standard L2 feature prediction loss, so the only supervision signal is accurate target-patch feature reconstruction, making the approach a non-contrastive, prediction-based vision-language pretraining paradigm.

## Results

TC-JEPA (ViT-H/14) achieves 80.4% top-1 accuracy on IN-1k linear probing, outperforming I-JEPA (79.3%) and StoP (79.6%) while approaching iBOT (81.0%) without hand-crafted augmentations. On dense prediction, TC-JEPA (ViT-H/14) reaches 39.5% mIoU on ADE20k (linear) and 55.2 AP^b on COCO detection, surpassing I-JEPA (36.9/53.7) and invariance-based DINO (27.5/50.1) by large margins. Scaled to CC27M (YFCC15M+CC12M), TC-JEPA (ViT-L/16) sets a new SOTA of 42.1% mIoU on ADE20k, exceeding DINOv2 distilled on LVD-142M (41.8) with 5× less data, and outperforming SigLIP2 (24.6) by +17.5% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct JEPA baseline I-JEPA, TC-JEPA wins on every task: +1.1% top-1 on IN-1k (ViT-H/14), +2.6% mIoU on ADE20k, and +1.5 AP^b on COCO detection. TC-JEPA also consistently outperforms StoP (stochastic positional embeddings) by 0.9–1.3% top-1 across model scales. On dense prediction, TC-JEPA substantially surpasses invariance-based methods: +12.0% mIoU over DINO and +7.2% over iBOT on ADE20k (linear), confirming that invariance learning sacrifices localization. Compared to contrastive vision-language models, TC-JEPA achieves 42.1% mIoU on ADE20k (CC27M) versus 24.6% for SigLIP2 and 20.5% for SigLIP, while remaining competitive on classification (81.4% vs 82.5% for SigLIP2). Among fine-grained contrastive methods, TC-JEPA outperforms SPARC (55.6 vs 52.6 AP^b on COCO) and DreamLIP (55.6 vs 50.7 AP^b) on detection, and beats both on segmentation (56.8 vs 54.0/52.4 mIoU on ADE20k). On vision-language tasks, TC-JEPA achieves 111.6 CIDEr on COCO captioning and 57.8% accuracy on VQAv2, surpassing CLIP (108.3/55.2) and SPARC (109.7/56.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends I-JEPA's masked feature prediction framework by conditioning the predictor on image captions via sparse cross-attention over word tokens, directly addressing the prediction uncertainty that plagues latent MIM at masked positions. It departs from prior JEPA remedies (stochastic positional embeddings in StoP, position-conditional encoders) that reduce uncertainty without adding new information, and from contrastive vision-language methods (CLIP, SigLIP, SPARC, DreamLIP) that learn image-text alignment through contrastive or consistency losses. The key conceptual departure is learning fine-grained patch-word correspondences in a self-supervised, non-contrastive manner purely to facilitate feature prediction, rather than to align global image and text embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TC-JEPA demonstrates that a non-contrastive, feature-prediction-based vision-language pretraining paradigm can substantially outperform contrastive methods on fine-grained tasks (segmentation, detection) while remaining competitive on classification, challenging the assumption that contrastive alignment is necessary for strong multimodal representations. The method's marginal compute overhead (text conditions the lightweight predictor, not the encoder) and strong scaling behavior make it a practical alternative for representation learning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video JEPA, whether it benefits from stronger or more diverse caption sources, and whether the learned patch-word correspondences can be directly exploited for downstream grounding or retrieval without additional fine-tuning.
