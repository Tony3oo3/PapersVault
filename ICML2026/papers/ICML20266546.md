## Problem

GenAI systems trained on copyrighted content currently compensate data providers either not at all or through coarse, one-time licensing deals that are opaque, non-auditable, and misaligned with the ongoing inference-time value those data actually generate. No scalable mechanism exists to attribute model outputs back to specific training sources and distribute revenue proportionally, leaving creators uncompensated while developers capture compounding economic returns. This position paper argues that a fundamental shift to contribution-aware, inference-time revenue sharing is necessary for sustainable GenAI ecosystems.

## Input / Output

The system consumes training data provenance records, user inference queries, and revenue indicators (per-query fees, subscription payments, or ad revenue) alongside offline-computed influence scores derived from gradient-based attribution methods. Influence scores are converted into standardized 'influence tokens' that abstract away model-specific details, serving as a model-agnostic interface between the attribution layer and the compensation layer. Outputs include proportional revenue payouts to individual data providers, updated influence token balances, and immutable, auditable transaction records on a permissioned blockchain.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-layer architecture: (1) an influence tracking and tokenization layer that extends gradient-based influence estimation techniques (TracIn, DataInf, UnTrack) to GenAI scale, computing per-sample influence scores offline and asynchronously, then converting them into standardized influence tokens associated with revenue indicators; and (2) a decentralized smart contract layer on permissioned blockchains that automatically triggers on revenue-generating inference events, computes proportional payouts from influence tokens, and maintains auditable state for all participants. The tokenization step deliberately decouples attribution logic from compensation logic, making the framework method-agnostic so that more efficient influence estimation techniques can be swapped in without redesigning the payment system. Three deployment models are envisioned—centralized, semi-decentralized, and fully decentralized—progressing toward permissioned blockchain-based smart contracts that enable public or delegated auditing without requiring full disclosure of proprietary model internals. The entire compensation pipeline operates post-inference, so blockchain operations and payout settlement do not block or interfere with production serving workloads.

## Results

This is a position paper and does not report empirical results, quantitative metrics, or experimental evaluations. The paper's contributions are normative and architectural: it articulates four positions (inference-time proportional compensation, revenue-proportional scaling, auditability, and community/legal support) and proposes a two-layer system framework combining influence tracking/tokenization with decentralized smart contracts. The only quantitative illustration is a worked example (Section 4.3) showing how a data provider with 20% and 80% influence on two ad-revenue events ($10 and $5) would receive $2 and $4 respectively, demonstrating the revenue-proportional payout logic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This position paper does not compare against empirical baselines or run experiments. Instead, it engages with two alternative normative positions: (1) the fair-use argument that training on public data requires no compensation, and (2) the one-time licensing model (fixed upfront payments). The paper argues both are insufficient: fair use is legally unstable across jurisdictions (citing Bartz v. Anthropic and Kadrey v. Meta as case-specific, not generalizable rulings) and fails to address future market-harm displacement; one-time licensing is misaligned with compounding inference-time value, favors large rightsholders, and lacks transparency. The paper positions its contribution-aware, revenue-proportional, auditable framework as a complement to (not a replacement for) fair use, but provides no head-to-head quantitative comparison since no competing system has been implemented at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper synthesizes three largely separate research threads—gradient-based influence estimation (TracIn, DataInf, UnTrack), data valuation (Data Shapley, EcoVal), and permissioned blockchain infrastructure (Hyperledger Fabric)—into a unified system architecture for inference-time, revenue-proportional compensation of data providers. It departs from prior data-valuation work, which typically assigns a static score to a dataset, by tying compensation to realized downstream revenue at inference time and enforcing payouts through programmable smart contracts. The paper also explicitly positions itself against the fair-use and one-time-licensing paradigms that dominate current legal and commercial discourse, arguing that both are structurally insufficient for GenAI-scale ecosystems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete governance gap: no existing mechanism can attribute inference-time model behavior to specific training sources, account for usage at scale, and distribute revenue in a verifiable manner. Key open questions include whether influence-estimation methods can scale to frontier-model sizes with acceptable accuracy, how to handle multi-stage training pipelines (pretraining → fine-tuning → RLHF) where contribution propagates across intermediate models, and how to achieve cross-jurisdictional legal enforceability. The framework's practical viability hinges on unresolved technical challenges (attribution fidelity vs. computational cost) and regulatory adoption, making it a research agenda as much as a deployment blueprint.
