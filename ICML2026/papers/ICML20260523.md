## Problem

Mean-field Langevin dynamics (MFLD) minimize entropy-regularized functionals over probability distributions via N interacting particles, but the pairwise interaction terms impose an Ω(N²) per-iteration cost that severely limits scalability for high-dimensional or multimodal targets. Existing subsampling-based accelerations (random thinning, random batch methods) either degrade convergence rates or lack applicable theoretical guarantees in the MFLD setting. The paper asks whether a structured thinning procedure can reduce the per-iteration cost to O(N^{3/2}) while preserving the same convergence guarantees as full MFLD up to logarithmic factors.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial set of N particles sampled i.i.d. from a distribution μ₀, a bounded thinning kernel k (whose RKHS must contain the interaction functions q₁(u,·) and ∇₁q₂(x,·)), and the parameters of the entropy-regularized functional (nonlinear functional F₀, L₂-regularization strength ζ, entropy coefficient σ, step size γ, and time horizon T). At each iteration, all N particles are updated, but the interaction vector field is computed using only a thinned coreset of M=⌈√N⌉ particles. The output is the full set of N particles at time T, which asymptotically behave as i.i.d. samples from the target minimizer π of the entropy-regularized functional.

## Method

KT-MFLD replaces the full empirical average over N particles in the MFLD vector field with an average over a coreset of M=⌈√N⌉ particles selected via KT-SPLIT-Compress (or KT-Compress), a near-linear-time kernel thinning algorithm. The key insight is that the thinning kernel is chosen so that the interaction functions belong to the associated RKHS, enabling KT-SPLIT-Compress to provably control the integration error of the thinned vector field at O(N^{-1/2}) per coordinate. Theorem 3.3 establishes that the additional KL divergence error from thinning scales as O(N^{-1}(log N)³), matching the original MFLD particle approximation error up to logarithmic factors, whereas random subsampling would degrade the error to Ω(N^{-1/2}). The per-iteration cost drops from Ω(N²) to O(N^{3/2}) since each of the N particles interacts with only M=O(√N) coreset particles, and the thinning step itself costs only Õ(N). The approach is validated on mean-field neural network training, MMD quantization, PrO posterior computation, and mean-field game simulation.

## Results

KT-MFLD reduces the per-iteration computational cost of mean-field Langevin dynamics from O(N²) to O(N^{3/2}) while maintaining a convergence guarantee of O(N^{-1}(log N)^3) in KL divergence, matching the original MFLD rate up to logarithmic factors. Across all four experimental tasks (student-teacher neural networks, MMD quantization, PrO posteriors, and mean-field games), both KT-MFLD variants (using KT-SPLIT-Compress and KT-Compress) consistently achieve lower loss/MMD/KGD/risk than Random-MFLD and RBM-MFLD under equal computational cost. When compared against the original MFLD at equal total cost (allowing KT-MFLD to use more particles), KT-MFLD² outperforms MFLD in MMD quantization and PrO posteriors, and both variants outperform MFLD in the neural network task for all oversampling parameters g ∈ {0, 1, 2}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Random-MFLD (uniform random subsampling of M=⌈√N⌉ particles per iteration), KT-MFLD consistently achieves lower test loss, MMD, KGD, and risk across all four experimental tasks, confirming the theoretical prediction that random subsampling degrades the approximation error to Ω(N^{-1/2}) versus the O(N^{-1}(log N)^3) achieved by kernel thinning. Against RBM-MFLD (random batch method with batch size p=⌈√N⌉), KT-MFLD also wins on every task; the paper notes that the convergence guarantee of RBM-MFLD [44] relies on a strong contraction assumption not satisfied in the MFLD setting, so its theoretical batch-size prescriptions do not apply. Against the original MFLD (no thinning, O(N²) per iteration), the comparison is made at equal total computational cost, meaning KT-MFLD can afford more particles: both KT-MFLD variants beat MFLD in the student-teacher network and PrO posterior tasks, while in MMD quantization only KT-MFLD² (using KT-Compress) consistently outperforms MFLD, attributed to KT-Compress's stronger MMD-specific guarantee. In the mean-field game experiment, the random batch method is not applicable, and KT-MFG variants outperform Random-MFG under equal wall-clock time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of mean-field Langevin dynamics (MFLD) for entropy-regularized variational inference and kernel thinning for distribution compression. It builds directly on the convergence analysis framework of Nitanda et al. [68] for MFLD and the near-linear-time KT-SPLIT-Compress algorithm of Shetty et al. [76], combining them to replace full N-particle empirical averages with structured M=O(√N) coreset averages. The key departure from prior acceleration approaches (random subsampling, random batch methods) is the use of kernel thinning, which provably achieves O(N^{-1/2}) integration error in the RKHS rather than the O(N^{-1/4}) error of uniform subsampling, translating into a tighter overall convergence bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(N^{3/2}) per-iteration cost (vs. O(N²)) is practically significant for particle-based variational inference, where representing multimodal or high-dimensional targets requires large N. However, the thinning-induced error term carries a curse of dimensionality through the log-Sobolev constant, and the experiments are limited to kernel-based particle flows at modest scale (N up to ~4096). Open questions include whether the approach extends cleanly to SVGD (where the vector field may not lie in the relevant RKHS), whether it composes with momentum-based acceleration, and whether the theoretical guarantees hold at the larger scales where the quadratic cost is most painful.
