## Problem

Multi-objective RL must support preferences that change online or are specified only after data collection, but standard reductions are protocol-unsafe: re-scalarizing past stochastic rewards with future weights breaks the martingale structure needed for self-normalized confidence bounds, and hypervolume evaluation ignores that episode-start randomization makes the deployable return set a convex hull. The paper provides near-minimax guarantees for both predictable adversarial preferences and reward-free preference-free exploration in linear MDPs, with protocol-aligned semantics that prior MORL shortcuts violate.

## Input / Output

The online protocol (M1) consumes known state-action features φ_h(s,a) ∈ R^d, a predictable preference weight w_k ∈ Δ_m revealed before each episode, and full m-dimensional reward vectors r_{k,h} ∈ [0,1]^m plus next-state transitions; the PFE protocol (M3) consumes only transitions during exploration and later receives the mean scalar reward ⟨w, r̄_h⟩ at query time. Outputs include per-episode policies, the set of return vectors v(π), a deployable hypervolume certificate on the convex hull HV(conv(·)), and scalarized value estimates. A key structural constraint is that preferences enter only at query/planning time—never during the learning of reward coordinates or transition structure—making the interface inherently preference-agnostic during data collection.

## Method

The core mechanism is a protocol-safe reward interface that estimates each reward coordinate independently via coordinate-wise ridge regression (θ̂_{k,h,i} = Λ_{k,h}^{-1} b_{k,h,i}) and performs scalarization only at query time by linearly combining parameters: θ̂_{k,h}(w_k) = Σ_i w_{k,i} θ̂_{k,h,i}, with the scalar confidence radius β^r = Σ_i w_{k,i} β^r_i. This avoids post-hoc re-scalarization of past noise, preserving the martingale difference structure needed for time-uniform self-normalized concentration bounds, and yields only a log m overhead without discretizing Δ_m. The resulting scalarized reward oracle (r̂^w, β^r) is fed into any episodic optimistic linear-MDP planner (e.g., LSVI-UCB), which recomputes optimistic value functions each episode. For PFE, reward-free exploration is treated as a black box producing transition data sufficient for planning under any subsequently revealed scalar reward, yielding a near-minimax query-answering rate Õ(d²U_ret²/ε²) that is information-theoretically separated from explicit base-kernel recovery Θ(d(|S|−1)/ε_P²). A stability chain from uniform support-function error to Hausdorff-∞ distance to hypervolume error (|HV(A)−HV(B)| ≤ mU^{m−1} d_{H,∞}(A,B)) converts per-weight planning errors into deployable HV(conv) certificates.

## Results

The paper establishes near-minimax online regret of Õ(d√(H³K)) for multi-objective RL under predictable adversarial preferences in linear MDPs (Corollary 6.3), matching a single-objective reduction lower bound of Ω(d√(H³K)) (Theorem 6.4), with dependence on the number of objectives m entering only logarithmically. For reward-free preference-free exploration, the decision-optimal query-answering sample complexity is Õ(d²U_ret²/ε²) (Theorem 6.10), while explicit base-kernel recovery requires Θ(d(|S|−1)/ε_P²) episodes (Theorems 6.13–6.14), yielding an information-theoretic separation. The w-net curse is quantified: achieving covering radius η on Δ_m requires L = Θ(η^{−(m−1)}) weights, exponential in m (Theorem 6.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its protocol-safe coordinate-wise wrapper (Algorithm 1) against two baselines in the online setting (E1): a w-net discretization baseline that solves scalarized problems on a finite simplex grid, and a feature-aware scalar baseline that learns only the scalarized reward ⟨w_k, r⟩ online with the same features and optimistic planner. The protocol-safe method consistently reduces cumulative regret across i.i.d., cyclic, and predictable-adversary preference schedules, with the gap widening under adversarial preferences (Figure 4). The w-net baseline becomes throughput-limited as m grows: at m=20, q=10 the lattice has 20 million points and is infeasible, while at m=100 even q=2 requires 5,050 grid points with 2.62× slowdown (Table 1). In the PFE setting (E3), the decision-optimal approach follows the predicted N^{−1/2} scaling without |S| dependence, while explicit-model recovery exhibits the unavoidable linear |S| factor, confirming the theoretical separation. The paper also notes (Proposition 6.9) that zero regret does not imply deployable hypervolume coverage, a distinction absent from prior MORL evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'picky customers' MORL framework of Wu et al. (2021) from known to unknown reward models in linear MDPs, and builds on near-minimax optimistic planners (LSVI-UCB, LSVI-UCB++) as a modular base. It also extends reward-free exploration (Jin et al., 2020a; Wagenmaker et al., 2022) to a multi-objective preference-free protocol where scalar rewards are revealed only post-exploration. The key conceptual departure is a 'protocol-safety' lens: the paper identifies that standard MORL shortcuts—post-hoc re-scalarization of past stochastic rewards with future weights, and hypervolume evaluation on non-convex policy sets—are statistically or semantically invalid under the stated interaction protocols, and provides corrected formulations (coordinate-wise regression with query-time scalarization, HV(conv) evaluation) that preserve martingale structure and deployment consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The filtration-safety result (Theorem 6.1) identifies a subtle but real correctness bug in how MORL practitioners combine adaptive preferences with confidence bounds, and the HV(conv) semantics (Proposition 4.4, Theorem 6.7) shows that the standard hypervolume metric can misrank methods when deployment permits episode-start randomization—both are practically relevant for anyone deploying multi-objective agents. The information-theoretic separation between decision-optimal PFE (|S|-free) and explicit-model recovery (|S|-dependent) clarifies what must actually be learned for preference queries. Limitations include the assumption of vector feedback for the online protocol, known feature maps, and linear MDP structure; extending filtration-safe learning to scalar-only feedback or anchor-free transition recovery remains open.
