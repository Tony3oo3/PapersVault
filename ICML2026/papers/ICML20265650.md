## Problem

When foundation models are fine-tuned on data with latent confounders (e.g., data source correlated with sentiment label), they exploit spurious shortcuts that break when the confounding relationship shifts at deployment. Existing domain-generalization methods assume invariant features are the only useful cross-environment signal but do not explicitly decompose representations into causal and spurious components under hidden confounding, leaving a gap in principled fine-tuning under latent confounded shift.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text sentence and a pre-trained BERT model (bert-base-uncased) as input. During training, the sentence is processed by both a frozen copy of the pre-trained model (yielding R0) and the fine-tuned model (yielding R1), while local features Φ are extracted from the embedding layer by splitting the token sequence into 10 non-overlapping patches, mean-pooling each patch, and passing through a learned MLP. At inference, the frozen model is discarded; the fine-tuned model produces C and Φ, and the final binary sentiment label is obtained by Monte-Carlo marginalization over K=20 within-batch shuffles of Φ to approximate the front-door adjustment p(y|do(x)).

## Method

CFT uses a structural causal model as an identification scaffold to motivate a three-component fine-tuning objective: (1) standard supervised fine-tuning to learn the tuned representation R1; (2) an invariance-plus-entropy loss that aligns p(c|R0) and p(c|R1) across the frozen and tuned views to recover the stable causal component C, grounded in Theorem 4.4 of Von Kügelgen et al. (2021); and (3) a learnable MLP that extracts shift-sensitive local features Φ from embedding-layer activations. The key identification result (Theorem 4.6) shows that p(y|do(x)) can be expressed as E_{x'}E_{Φ'|x'}[p(y|Φ',C)], a front-door-style adjustment that blocks the active collider path between the regime variable σ and Y. At inference, this is approximated by shuffling Φ within each mini-batch K times and averaging the resulting predictions, effectively marginalizing over the spurious component while retaining the causal signal in C. The frozen pre-trained model acts as an implicit second environment, enabling the two-view separation without requiring multiple labeled domains.

## Results

On the Amazon review benchmark (Experiment 1), CFT achieves F1 scores of 92.37 (ID, 90% spurious), 83.16 (70%), 74.25 (50%), 65.24 (30%), and 56.40 (10%) across increasing OOD shift severity. The advantage over standard fine-tuning (SFT) grows with shift magnitude, reaching +7.07 F1 points at 10% spurious correlation. On the Yelp benchmark, CFT similarly outperforms SFT by up to +9.16 F1 points at 10% spurious. Under the data-source injection attack (Experiment 2), CFT achieves 49.22 F1 at 10% spurious versus 37.78 for SFT and 47.41 for SWA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFT consistently outperforms SFT (standard supervised fine-tuning) across all OOD settings on both Amazon and Yelp, with margins growing from +1.55 F1 at 70% spurious to +7.07 F1 at 10% spurious on Amazon. Against SWA (stochastic weight averaging), CFT is competitive under mild shift (90% and 70% spurious, where SWA edges ahead by ~0.8–1.5 F1) but clearly surpasses SWA under stronger shifts, gaining +2.4 F1 at 30% and +3.3 F1 at 10% spurious on Amazon. WISE (weight interpolation between pre-trained and fine-tuned models) is the weakest baseline, with CFT outperforming it by 4.4–11.4 F1 points across OOD settings on Amazon. The paper notes that SWA provides strong generic regularization, while CFT's structured causal decomposition becomes increasingly advantageous as the shift magnitude grows, suggesting the two methods address robustness through fundamentally different mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the invariant causal representation learning line (Von Kügelgen et al., 2021) for identifying a stable component C from two views, and on front-door adjustment methods for visual recognition (Mao et al., 2022) for handling hidden confounders between input and output. It departs from prior domain generalization work (IRM, invariant features) by explicitly modeling both causal and spurious components rather than assuming only invariant features are useful, and from prior causal representation learning by using a pre-trained foundation model as an implicit second environment, eliminating the need for multiple annotated domains. The key conceptual contribution is an identification scaffold (a structural causal model with regime variables) that motivates a fine-tuning objective decomposing representations into stable C and shift-sensitive Φ, then applies a front-door-style adjustment at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and increasingly common failure mode: foundation models fine-tuned on data with latent confounders (platform identifiers, annotation artifacts) latch onto spurious shortcuts that invert at deployment. The method is practical—it fits within standard BERT fine-tuning pipelines and requires no domain annotations or multiple environments. However, evaluation is limited to controlled spurious-correlation injection attacks on binary sentiment classification with BERT-base, and the authors acknowledge this isolates one mechanism rather than covering real-world shifts. Open questions include extension to multi-modal settings, whether the identification assumptions hold under more complex confounding, and whether the approach scales to tasks where the causal/spurious decomposition is less clean.
