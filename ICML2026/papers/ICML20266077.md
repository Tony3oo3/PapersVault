## Problem

Tree-structured rollouts in test-time reinforcement learning suffer from two failure modes: (1) high-entropy branching causes rollout collapse, where the branching budget over-concentrates on a few trajectories with consecutive high-entropy segments, degenerating the search tree into near-chain rollouts; and (2) early noisy majority-vote pseudo-labels induce self-reinforcing overfitting, causing the policy to sharpen prematurely and suppress exploration.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt (for mathematical reasoning) or an image-plus-text prompt (for visual math reasoning) and generates multiple candidate reasoning trajectories via entropy-confidence guided tree search, from which a majority-voted final answer is extracted. During rollout, 64 candidate responses are generated per prompt (max 3,072 tokens each), of which 32 are downsampled for policy updates. The tree shares reasoning prefixes and branches at adaptively selected nodes, with online pruning terminating degenerate branches mid-generation to reallocate the fixed inference budget.

## Method

ECHO introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, an entropy-confidence hybrid tree search jointly normalizes local token entropy and group-level confidence to compute an adaptive branch width (shrinking in high-confidence regions, expanding in high-entropy/low-confidence regions), and applies three online pruning criteria—sliding-window minimum confidence, consecutive tail-confidence decline, and consecutive entropy-spike—to terminate persistently low-quality branches early. Second, confidence-adaptive clipping modulates the PPO trust-region radius per trajectory via a sigmoid of tail-smoothed confidence, tightening updates for overconfident (potentially spurious) trajectories and loosening them for uncertain ones. Third, entropy-confidence hybrid advantage shaping multiplies the group-normalized GRPO advantage by a per-token signal combining masked-whitened entropy and inverse confidence, reallocating gradient mass toward informative yet uncertain decisions. The full objective combines the clipped surrogate with a KL regularizer against the reference policy.

## Results

On text-only reasoning (pass@16), ECHO achieves 55.3% average on Qwen2.5-7B (trained on AIME2024) and 76.1% on Qwen3-8B, outperforming the strongest baseline INTUITOR by 3.7 and 2.2 points respectively. On multimodal reasoning (pass@1), ECHO improves average accuracy by 1.2–2.4 points over MM-UPT across Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B, with up to +3.1 on LogicVista. Under strict IID evaluation (Table 4), ECHO delivers 2.4–3.4 point average gains over the best baseline across four model scales (1.5B to 8B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECHO consistently outperforms all five label-free baselines (TTRL, ETMR, EVOL-RL, INTUITOR, MM-UPT) across every benchmark and model scale tested. The strongest label-free competitor is INTUITOR, which ECHO surpasses by 2.2–3.7 points in average pass@16 on text reasoning and by 1.2–2.4 points in average pass@1 on multimodal reasoning. ETMR, the closest tree-search baseline, is beaten by 2.2–4.7 points on average, with the largest gaps on AIME2025 (up to +3.3) and GPQA (up to +3.3). Against supervised GRPO (RLVR with ground-truth labels), ECHO is within 1.9 points on AIME2024 and actually exceeds it by 2.0 points on MATH-500, while trailing by 0.2–2.1 points on vision benchmarks, indicating that label-free ECHO nearly matches supervised optimization on text tasks but still lags on vision-heavy reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECHO extends the test-time reinforcement learning (TTRL) paradigm initiated by Zuo et al. (2025), which uses majority-vote pseudo-labels for label-free policy optimization, and the entropy-fork tree rollout mechanism of ETMR (Liu et al., 2025), which branches at high-entropy nodes to improve sampling efficiency. The key departure is the introduction of a joint entropy-confidence signal that governs both branch width scheduling and online pruning during rollout, and a confidence-adaptive clipping mechanism with hybrid advantage shaping during the GRPO-style policy update. Conceptually, ECHO positions itself as a fix for two diagnosed failure modes of entropy-only tree search—rollout collapse and early pseudo-label overfitting—rather than proposing an entirely new optimization framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, label-free recipe for improving test-time RL that is directly applicable to current LLM/VLM reasoning pipelines, and the rollout-collapse diagnosis (budget concentration on a few high-entropy trajectories) is a useful empirical finding for anyone using tree-structured rollouts. However, the method's sensitivity to pruning hyperparameters (τ_prune, W_T) and its dependence on the backbone having sufficient task-specific priors limit its generality; on distribution-mismatched training data (ReClor, ThinkLite-11K), gains vanish or reverse. An open question is whether the entropy-confidence hybrid can be made fully adaptive (e.g., learned thresholds) to reduce the manual tuning burden, and whether the approach extends to longer-horizon or multi-turn reasoning settings where confidence signals are less informative mid-trajectory.
