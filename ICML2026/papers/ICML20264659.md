## Problem

Existing discrete diffusion protein language models rely on masked diffusion, which is a poor proxy for biological evolution: natural proteins evolve through substitutions, insertions, and deletions that jointly modify sequence identity and length, yet masking-only frameworks lack native indel actions and enforce fixed-length generation. This limits both mutation effect prediction (no explicit pretraining objective for indels) and practical protein engineering workflows that require variable-length post-editing and directed evolution.

## Input / Output

For variant effect prediction, DPLM-Evo takes a wild-type protein sequence and directly reads out substitution probabilities at mutated sites without introducing an artificial mask token (unlike MLM-based scoring); indel variants are scored via binary deletion/insertion logits. For generation, the model takes an initial length L_init and produces variable-length protein sequences through iterative edit trajectories. For motif scaffolding, it additionally consumes 3D coordinates of motif residues via a structure encoder while editing only the scaffold region. The output is always a concrete amino acid sequence plus, during sampling, an explicit sequence of substitution/insertion/deletion operations.

## Method

DPLM-Evo decouples a variable-length observed sequence space from an upsampled-length latent alignment space (N=2L) with interleaved gap slots, so that indels are represented as gap↔residue transitions in the latent forward process while the neural network operates on the collapsed observed sequence. Three independent prediction heads—categorical substitution, binary deletion, and binary insertion—explicitly model evolutionary edit operations during denoising, with a decomposed loss that balances the class imbalance between frequent substitutions and rare indels. A contextualized evolutionary noising kernel replaces uniform corruption by sampling substitution noise from the model's own on-policy masked predictions (averaged over an auxiliary partially-masked context), yielding biologically plausible, sequence-context-dependent mutations that improve learning efficiency. Sampling follows a route-and-denoise factorization under a canonical alignment approximation: at each step the model deletes low-confidence tokens, inserts mask placeholders, substitutes noisy positions, and renoises the remaining low-confidence set with the biologically grounded kernel. The framework subsumes masked diffusion, uniform diffusion, and mixed kernels as special cases of its transition matrix, enabling initialization from pretrained DPLM weights.

## Results

On the ProteinGym substitution benchmark (217 DMS assays), DPLM-Evo achieves 0.420 average Spearman correlation in the single-sequence setting, the best among all sequence-only foundation models, and 0.459 after explicit GEMME alignment. On the ProteinGym indel benchmark, DPLM-Evo reaches 0.495 Spearman, the strongest single-sequence result. For unconditional generation, DPLM-Evo achieves 83.6 pLDDT (competitive with DPLM at 84.0 and DiMA at 83.3), and in GFP directed evolution it improves pTM from 0.263 to 0.857 (with indels) over 20 iterations, surpassing ESM-2 (0.737) and random mutation (0.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among single-sequence methods, DPLM-Evo (0.420 Spearman) clearly outperforms ESM-2, ESM-C, ESM1v, DPLM, ProGen2, RITA, and Tranception on ProteinGym substitution, and with GEMME alignment (0.459) it surpasses structure-using SaProt and MSA-using TranceptEVE L. On the indel benchmark, DPLM-Evo (0.495) beats the strongest single-sequence baseline ProGen2 M (0.464) by 0.031 but remains below MSA-based PoET (0.517) and ProFam ensemble (0.530). For unconditional generation, DPLM-Evo (83.6 pLDDT) is on par with DPLM (84.0) and DiMA (83.3) while substantially exceeding prior generative models (EvoDiff-OADM 37.1, D3PM 36.7, proteinGAN 30.4). In GFP directed evolution, DPLM-Evo with indels reaches pTM 0.857 after 20 iterations, outperforming ESM-2 (0.737) and random mutation (<0.6) under the same beam-search protocol. In motif scaffolding, DPLM-Evo achieves a 0.23 overall success rate, solving more motifs than both EvoDiff and DPLM-Mask in the zero-shot setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPLM-Evo extends the DPLM family of masked discrete diffusion protein language models (Wang et al., 2024b) by replacing the absorbing-state mask kernel with a unified evolutionary noising process that explicitly models substitutions, insertions, and deletions. The key architectural departure is the decoupling of a variable-length observed sequence space from an upsampled-length latent alignment space (inspired by CTC-style alignment from Graves et al., 2006 and EditFlow from Havasi et al., 2025), which makes indel-aware diffusion tractable within a fixed-dimensional computation. The contextualized evolutionary noising kernel, which uses the model's own masked predictions to generate biologically plausible corruption, is a novel training-time mechanism not present in prior discrete diffusion PLMs. The sampling procedure reuses the route-and-denoise factorization of Zheng et al. (2023a) but extends it with binary indel decision heads and a renoising step drawn from the evolutionary kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion protein language model to natively support all three evolutionary edit operations, unlocking variable-length generation, indel-aware variant effect prediction, and directed-evolution-style optimization of existing proteins—capabilities that fixed-length masked diffusion fundamentally cannot express. The practical impact is significant: the framework enables iterative post-editing of natural scaffolds (e.g., GFP optimization) and motif scaffolding with dynamic length adjustment, moving protein design closer to how engineers actually work. Key limitations include the canonical alignment restricting to at most one indel per position per step (requiring multi-residue indels to compose across steps), the +24% per-step training overhead from the contextualized kernel, and the reliance on practical approximations (binary classification for indels, fixed canonical alignment) rather than exact marginalization. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multimodal (structure-conditioned) evolutionary diffusion and whether tighter variational bounds can replace the current approximations.
