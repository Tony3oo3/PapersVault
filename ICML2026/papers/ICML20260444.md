## Problem

Cross-layer KV-Cache compression is limited by two prior approaches: architecture-sharing methods (CLA, YOCO) that require expensive pretraining, and token-merging methods (MiniCache) that rely on per-token cosine similarity which is empirically too weak across layers. The paper identifies that while individual token embeddings diverge across layers, their dominant singular vectors remain highly aligned (verified via CKA), revealing a structural redundancy that neither pretraining-based nor token-merging approaches exploit.

## Input / Output

During prefill, the method consumes the full KV-Cache tensors (pre-RoPE keys and values) of W contiguous layers (default W=4) as a horizontally concatenated matrix of shape L×(W·d), and produces a shared token basis A_k ∈ R^{L×r} plus per-layer reconstruction matrices B_ell ∈ R^{r×d} (decomposed into per-head blocks B_{ell,g} ∈ R^{r×d_h}). During decode, it additionally takes the current query vector to select a small subset of token indices S (|S|≪L) via landmark-guided chunk scoring, reconstructing only those rows. The compressed storage scales as O(L·r + W·r·d) versus O(W·L·d) for the uncompressed cache, with r≪d.

## Method

xKV applies Cross-Layer Factorization (CLF): it horizontally concatenates the KV-Caches of W adjacent layers and performs a rank-r randomized SVD, yielding a shared left-singular-vector basis A_k = U_k Σ_k reused across all layers in the window and layer-specific right factors B_ell. The key empirical insight, established via Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) analysis, is that the dominant left singular vectors of KV-Caches are strongly aligned across layers even when token-wise cosine similarity is low, justifying a single shared basis. At decode time, Selective Reconstruction (SR) avoids the O(L) cost of dense reconstruction by using landmark-guided chunk selection (adapted from ShadowKV) to identify query-relevant tokens and reconstructing only those rows, making per-step cost O(|S|·r·d_h) independent of sequence length. The entire pipeline is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no architectural modification or fine-tuning, and can be combined with quantization for further compression (up to 32× total).

## Results

On RULER at 64K context, xKV achieves 88.50% average accuracy at 8.03× compression on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (vs. 91.89% full attention), 87.05% on Qwen3-4B (vs. 92.19%), and 90.19% on Qwen2.5-14B at 6.21× (vs. 93.36%), representing only ~2–3% accuracy loss. With Selective Reconstruction (xKV-SR), the method achieves a 4.23× end-to-end generation throughput speedup over Full Attention (FlashAttention-2) at 122K context on an A100 GPU, while maintaining 89.69% RULER accuracy at 5.35× KV-Cache compression. The on-the-fly SVD overhead during prefill is negligible, accounting for only 1.24% of prefill time at 256K context length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior inter-layer method MiniCache, xKV is dramatically superior: MiniCache collapses to 45.04% (Llama), 7.32% (Qwen3), and 13.78% (Qwen2.5) even at a modest 1.3× compression, while xKV maintains 88.50%, 87.05%, and 90.19% at 8× compression, confirming that token-wise cosine similarity is insufficient for cross-layer merging. Against the intra-layer low-rank baseline Single SVD, xKV improves average accuracy by 42.8 points on Llama-3.1-8B (88.50% vs. 45.71%) at comparable compression, demonstrating that cross-layer alignment preserves far more information than per-layer SVD. Against token-eviction baselines SnapKV and PyramidKV, xKV is slightly lower (88.50% vs. 89.68%/89.94% on Llama) but operates as a true inter-layer method without permanently discarding tokens, and it remains robust in multi-turn settings where SnapKV and PyramidKV degrade sharply after the first turn. Against the quantization baseline KIVI-2, xKV achieves higher accuracy at a higher compression rate (88.50% at 8.03× vs. 86.87% at 7.10× on Llama). In the selective-reconstruction regime (Table 2), xKV-SR at 5.35× compression achieves 89.69% accuracy, outperforming ShadowKV* (70.94% at 5.51×) by ~19 points, and xK-SR beats ShadowKV by ~2.5% at matched GPU memory budgets, while also surpassing Quest (84.87%) by ~5 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends cross-layer KV-Cache compression beyond the two existing paradigms: architecture-level sharing (CLA, YOCO) that requires expensive retraining, and token-wise merging (MiniCache) that relies on per-token cosine similarity. The key conceptual departure is the CKA-based observation that while individual token embeddings show low cosine similarity across layers, their dominant singular vectors remain highly aligned—enabling a shared low-rank basis via cross-layer SVD that neither prior approach exploits. The Selective Reconstruction component adapts ShadowKV's sparse token-loading paradigm but applies it to the cross-layer compressed cache, upgrading the compression substrate from single-layer SVD to the shared cross-layer factorization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

xKV provides a plug-and-play, training-free method that achieves 8× KV-Cache compression with only ~3% accuracy loss and 4.23× end-to-end throughput improvement, making it immediately deployable for long-context inference on existing pretrained models. The CKA diagnostic framework is a reusable tool for understanding cross-layer geometric structure in any Transformer. The main limitation is that the method addresses long-prefill compression only; extending the cross-layer alignment insight to long-generation scenarios where the KV-Cache grows during decoding remains open. Compatibility with emerging architectures (MLA, MoE, hybrid attention) is demonstrated but the method's behavior under test-time scaling or very long autoregressive generation is unexplored.
