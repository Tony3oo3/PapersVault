## Problem

Existing iEEG foundation models tokenize neural signals either at the individual channel level or as a single brain-wide aggregate, both of which fail to capture the brain's inherent functional modularity. No prior method autonomously discovers data-driven functional clusters from neural activity to serve as the unit of representation, limiting efficiency, generalization, and interpretability of downstream decoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266117/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-channel iEEG signals (X ∈ R^{C×T}) that are band-pass filtered (0.5–200 Hz), notch-filtered, resampled to 400 Hz, median-referenced, and z-score normalized per channel. Signals are segmented into temporal patches of length L (e.g., 200 ms) per channel. The output is a set of K=8 region-level prototype embeddings per timestamp, which are concatenated across prototypes (and optionally across time steps) and passed through a linear head to produce class labels for seizure detection, speech perception, or speech production tasks.

## Method

NeuroCLUS employs a two-stage self-supervised pre-training framework. In Stage 1 (Functional Context Prediction), a temporal CNN patch tokenizer feeds a temporal transformer followed by a spatial transformer; the model is trained with an I-JEPA-style contrastive objective (EMA target network) to discriminate channels whose activity has been replaced by unrelated time points, yielding a per-subject sparse functional connectivity graph P ∈ R^{C×C} from averaged spatial attention. In Stage 2 (Functional Clustering), K=8 learnable prototype tokens are concatenated with channel patch tokens and processed by a joint spatial-temporal transformer; a Gumbel-softmax reparameterization produces soft cluster membership matrices, and the training loss combines MSE signal reconstruction (preserving fine-grained information) with a trace-based clustering loss that maximizes within-cluster connectivity alignment and enforces between-cluster separation using the Stage-1 graph as a self-supervision signal. This cluster-aware tokenization lets the transformer backbone model interactions between coherent functional units rather than raw channels.

## Results

NeuroCLUS achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five iEEG datasets and six downstream tasks. On the challenging Du-IN speech production task, it reaches 65.92% balanced accuracy, surpassing the specialized Du-IN baseline (62.70%) and all general-purpose foundation models (PopT: 42.57%, Brant: 12.42%). On seizure detection, NeuroCLUS attains the highest Cohen's Kappa (0.61 on SWEC) and F1 scores (0.59 SWEC, 0.40 MAYO, 0.51 FNUSA). On Brain Treebank speech perception, it achieves ROC-AUC of 0.99 on speech/non-speech discrimination and 0.96 on sentence onset detection, the most cognitively demanding sub-tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroCLUS outperforms all seven baselines on every task and dataset. The most dramatic margin is on Du-IN speech production, where NeuroCLUS (65.92% BAC) surpasses the task-specific Du-IN model (62.70%) by 3.2 points and the next-best general foundation model PopT (42.57%) by over 23 points, highlighting that channel-level and brain-level tokenization fundamentally fail to capture the region-level structure needed for speech production decoding. On seizure detection, NeuroCLUS leads MVPFormer (the strongest seizure baseline) by 0.04 Kappa on SWEC and 0.05 F1 on FNUSA. On Brain Treebank, NeuroCLUS achieves the highest ROC-AUC on all four sub-tasks, with the largest gains on the harder onset (0.96 vs. 0.91 for BaRISTA) and speech/non-speech (0.99 vs. 0.94 for LaBraM) tasks. Notably, H2DiLR, which also uses region-level tokens but via discrete code sequences, drops to 23.92% on Du-IN, confirming that continuous prototype-based clustering is critical for preserving fine-grained neural information.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuroCLUS extends the iEEG foundation model lineage from BrainBERT/Brant (channel-level tokens) and PopT (single brain-level [CLS] token) by introducing a data-driven functional clustering tokenization strategy. It departs from BaRISTA's fixed anatomical grouping and MVPFormer's uniform multi-variate attention by learning an inter-channel dependency graph via a JEPA-style contrastive objective (functional context prediction), then using that graph to guide soft assignment of channels into K learnable prototype tokens. The two-stage design—first extracting functional connectivity, then performing connectivity-guided clustering with a dedicated trace-based loss—is the core architectural departure from prior self-supervised iEEG models that rely on reconstruction alone or static spatial priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that data-driven functional clustering is not merely beneficial but essential for decoding complex generative processes like speech production, where channel-level and brain-level tokenization fail dramatically (12–43% BAC vs. 66% for NeuroCLUS on Du-IN). This reframes the tokenization question for iEEG foundation models: the optimal unit of representation is neither the individual electrode nor the whole brain, but a small set of functionally coherent, spatially distributed clusters. Key open questions include whether the ~10k-hour pre-training corpus is sufficient (scaling curves suggest diminishing returns only after ~2.5k hours, implying large headroom), whether the approach generalizes beyond auditory and seizure paradigms to visual, motor, or affective decoding, and whether the discovered clusters remain stable across tasks or are task-specific.
