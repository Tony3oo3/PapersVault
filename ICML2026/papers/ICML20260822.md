## Problem

Existing Reasoning Video Object Segmentation (RVOS) methods are restricted to single-pass inference over a fixed set of sampled frames, lacking the agency to actively seek supplementary visual evidence when initial context is insufficient for resolving complex references. Additionally, in the implicit [SEG] token paradigm, standard GRPO optimizes only the text-level probability of the [SEG] token while the mask decoder remains differentiably detached from the policy gradient, creating a structural disconnect between token likelihood and actual segmentation quality.

## Input / Output

The model ingests a text query (referring expression) alongside resolution-adaptive video frames: up to 20 low-resolution temporal frames (32×28×28 max pixels) for global motion context, up to 5 moderate-resolution spatial frames (128×28×28) for environmental detail, and a single high-resolution keyframe (512×28×28) per reasoning turn for spatial precision. During multi-turn interaction (up to 3 turns), the model outputs <select> tokens specifying temporal intervals and keyframe indices to request refined visual details, and terminates with an <answer> token that triggers the [SEG] token to produce a pixel-level segmentation mask via the SAM2 decoder, along with a structured chain-of-thought trace organized into temporal, spatial, and linguistic reasoning stages.

## Method

VideoSEG-O3 formulates RVOS as a Markov Decision Process where the policy iteratively selects temporal intervals and keyframes via a <select> token, dynamically augmenting its visual observation with low-resolution temporal segments and high-resolution keyframes until it determines sufficient evidence has been gathered. The core architectural innovation is SEG-aware Logit Calibration, which injects the spatial average of pixel-wise mask log-likelihoods directly into the [SEG] token's policy probability during GRPO, decomposing the importance ratio into a text contribution and a segmentation contribution so that the mask decoder receives gradient signal from the reward. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: (1) SFT with LoRA to align the [SEG] token with SAM2 and establish frame-level temporal index understanding, (2) CoT cold-start on the constructed VTS-CoT dataset (6K samples) to elicit structured multi-turn reasoning with a Decoupled Thinking Trace, and (3) full-parameter GRPO with a composite reward (format, temporal, segmentation quality, and progressive improvement) plus an auxiliary joint segmentation loss (λ_seg=0.2) to stabilize pixel-level decoding during exploration.

## Results

VideoSEG-O3-4B achieves state-of-the-art J&F scores across all five RefVOS benchmarks: 60.0 on MeViS, 74.1 on Ref-Youtube-VOS, 79.4 on Ref-DAVIS17, 65.5 on Ref-SAV, and 57.4 on Long-RVOS. On the reasoning benchmark ReVOS, it reaches 67.7 overall J&F, surpassing the strongest RL competitor Veason-R1-7B (61.3) by +6.4. In zero-shot evaluation, VideoSEG-O3-4B outperforms MoRA-13B on GroundMoRe by +8.8 (31.9 vs. 23.1) and achieves 62.9 on ReasonVOS. Notably, even the compact 2B variant matches or exceeds the previous SOTA UniPixel-7B on MeViS (55.6 vs. 55.8) and Ref-DAVIS17 (80.0 vs. 76.4), demonstrating strong parameter efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoSEG-O3-4B outperforms the previous SOTA UniPixel-7B (Liu et al., 2025a) by +4.2 on MeViS (60.0 vs. 55.8), +3.1 on Ref-Youtube-VOS (74.1 vs. 71.0), and +8.3 on Ref-DAVIS17 (79.4 vs. 76.4), while also being the first method to report competitive results on Ref-SAV (65.5 vs. Sa2VA-8B's 50.0, +15.5) and Long-RVOS (57.4 vs. ReferMo's 51.3, +6.1). On the reasoning benchmark ReVOS, VideoSEG-O3-4B surpasses the strongest RL-based competitor Veason-R1-7B (Gong et al., 2025a) by +6.4 overall J&F (67.7 vs. 61.3), with a particularly large +6.1 gain in the Reasoning sub-category. In zero-shot transfer, VideoSEG-O3-4B outperforms MoRA-13B (Deng et al., 2025) on GroundMoRe by +8.8 (31.9 vs. 23.1) and exceeds Veason-R1-7B on ReasonVOS by +3.0 (62.9 vs. 59.9). The 2B variant is especially notable: it matches UniPixel-7B on MeViS (55.6 vs. 55.8) and surpasses it on Ref-DAVIS17 (80.0 vs. 76.4) despite using roughly one-third the parameters. On image segmentation (Table 3), VideoSEG-O3-4B achieves 80.9 cIoU on RefCOCO val, competitive with specialist methods like PropVG (82.0) and surpassing most MLLM-based baselines, confirming that video-centric RL training does not degrade static image capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning paradigm (from DeepSeek-R1) to video object segmentation by introducing SEG-aware Logit Calibration, which injects pixel-wise mask confidence into the [SEG] token's policy gradient to resolve the gradient disconnect between text-level optimization and mask quality. It departs from the dominant explicit-prompt RL approaches (SegZero, Veason-R1) that generate bounding boxes or points, instead adopting the implicit [SEG] embedding paradigm from Sa2VA/VideoLISA while adding a multi-turn temporal-spatial Chain-of-Thought that enables active frame selection via a <select> token. The Decoupled Thinking Trace and VTS-CoT cold-start dataset further distinguish it from prior single-pass CoT methods by hierarchically separating temporal, spatial, and linguistic reasoning with resolution-adaptive visual inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SEG-aware Logit Calibration addresses a structural gap that likely affects any RL-based approach to tasks with non-textual output heads (segmentation, detection), making it a potentially transferable technique beyond RVOS. The finding that standard GRPO *degrades* segmentation performance without this calibration (59.43% → 57.92% on MeViS) is a useful cautionary result for practitioners applying text-level RL to multimodal generation tasks. Open questions include whether the 3-turn exploration limit generalizes to much longer videos, the computational overhead of multi-turn inference at scale, and whether the approach extends to other dense prediction tasks (e.g., depth, pose) where similar text-to-pixel gradient disconnects exist.
