## Problem

Training-free diffusion guidance methods approximate the diffusion posterior via Tweedie's formula, which is inherently biased and produces unreliable guidance in low-density regions where high-value samples (e.g., novel drug-like molecules) reside. Stochastic optimal control (SOC) provides a principled posterior sampling framework but requires backpropagating through entire neural-SDE trajectories, making it computationally prohibitive. The paper asks whether one can retain the theoretical guarantees of SOC while achieving the efficiency of training-free guidance, particularly for sampling beyond high-density data regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained score network s_θ, an off-the-shelf differentiable classifier or reward function r(x_T), and a batch of N noisy particles on the current diffusion manifold M_t. For molecular tasks, inputs are node and edge feature matrices (X, E) modeled by coupled VPSDE/VESDE systems with permutation-equivariant score networks; outputs are molecular graphs evaluated against four hit criteria (Tanimoto similarity < 0.4, SA < 5, QED > 0.5, docking score below median of known actives). For image tasks, outputs are guided RGB samples evaluated via FID, LPIPS, accuracy, or CLIP/style scores. The method requires no classifier training across noise levels and no model fine-tuning.

## Method

SDG formulates low-density diffusion guidance as a surrogate stochastic optimal control problem with a novel cost functional that includes a Dirac-delta state cost α(t)log p_t(x_t) for density annealing and a terminal reward −β(t)r(x_T). A variational bound on the SOC value function (Proposition 3.2) reveals that Tweedie-based posterior approximation neglects a KL divergence term between the proposal and true posteriors, making standard training-free guidance suboptimal. To correct this, SDG introduces a back-and-forth Stein correction: particles are mapped backward from M_t to M_T via Tweedie's formula, updated on M_T along the kernelized Stein discrepancy direction (using the posterior score approximation s_θ(x_T) − η(t)s_θ(x_t) from Lemma 3.3), then mapped forward back to M_t. This avoids expensive Jacobian-vector products by performing the SVGD update directly on the data manifold. The resulting optimal control decomposes into a low-density reward-guidance term on M_t and a Stein correction term on M_T, and in the limit ε(t)→0 it recovers the standard Langevin correction as a special case.

## Results

On the primary molecular docking task (novel hit ratio %), SDG achieves 1.156% (Fa7), 22.690% (5ht1b), 9.167% (Jak2), and 8.780% (Parp1), substantially outperforming the best diffusion baseline MOOD (0.733%, 18.673%, 9.200%, 7.017%) on three of four targets. Removing the Stein correction (SDG♣) causes a catastrophic drop to 0.299%, 0.033%, 0.000%, and 0.671%, confirming that Tweedie-based posteriors alone are unreliable in low-density regions. On image guidance tasks, SDG♥ (with Stein correction, α=0) achieves 54.0% label-guidance accuracy and 68.90 FID on super-resolution, beating all baselines including UGD (45.9% acc, 75.9 FID) and MPGD (38.0% acc, 82.0 FID).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDG outperforms the strongest diffusion-based baseline MOOD (Lee et al., 2023) on three of four protein targets: Fa7 (1.156% vs 0.733%), 5ht1b (22.690% vs 18.673%), and Parp1 (8.780% vs 7.017%), while essentially tying on Jak2 (9.167% vs 9.200%). Against the non-diffusion baselines FREED, GDSS, HierVAE, and MORLD, SDG achieves the highest novel hit ratio on all four targets, with the largest margin over FREED on 5ht1b (22.690% vs 10.187%) and Jak2 (9.167% vs 4.520%). On image guidance tasks (Table 1), SDG♥ beats DPS (Chung et al., 2023) and LGD (Song et al., 2023) on label-guidance accuracy (54.0% vs 50.1% and 32.2%) and super-resolution FID (68.90 vs 109.0 and 96.7), and outperforms the more advanced UGD (Bansal et al., 2023) and MPGD (He et al., 2024) on three of four tasks. The ablation SDG♣ (without Stein correction) collapses to near-zero hit ratios on 5ht1b and Jak2, demonstrating that the Stein correction is not a marginal improvement but a critical component for reliable low-density guidance. The Langevin-only variant SDG◇ (ε=0) is consistently outperformed by full SDG, confirming that inter-particle repulsive forces from the kernelized Stein update provide non-trivial guidance benefits beyond simple score-based correction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: training-free diffusion guidance (DPS, LGD, UGD, MPGD), which relies on Tweedie's formula for one-step posterior estimation; stochastic optimal control (SOC) for diffusion models (Uehara et al., 2024; Domingo-Enrich et al., 2024), which provides a principled but computationally prohibitive posterior sampling framework; and Stein variational gradient descent (Liu & Wang, 2016), a nonparametric particle transport method. The key departure is a variational bound on the SOC value function (Proposition 3.2) that formally decomposes the optimal control into a standard guidance term plus a KL-correction term, showing that all existing training-free methods implicitly discard the latter. The back-and-forth Stein correction then provides a memory-efficient, training-free way to approximate that missing KL term without backpropagating through full SDE trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable suboptimality of Tweedie-based posteriors (via the neglected KL term) gives a principled explanation for why training-free guidance degrades in low-density regions, and the Stein correction offers a plug-and-play fix that requires no retraining or distillation. The orders-of-magnitude improvement on protein-docking hit rates (e.g., 0.000% → 9.167% on Jak2) suggests practical value for drug discovery, where high-value ligands are hypothesized to reside in low-density chemical space. Open questions include the instability of kernel-based SVGD updates in high dimensions (particle-size saturation observed empirically), the 36–85% runtime overhead, and whether geometry-informed or preconditioned SVGD variants can stabilize the correction at scale. Combining SDG with few-step consistency models is identified as a promising direction for fast inference.
