## Problem

Multi-agent LLM teams often underperform single strong models, and the paper identifies a structural cause in sequential fine-tuning: each agent update shifts the shared context distribution, so evaluating later agents on cached stage-start rollouts introduces a compounding occupancy mismatch. The authors formalize this as the compounding occupancy shift and prove that stale-occupancy evaluation incurs a certificate penalty scaling as O(n²√δ̄) with team size n, whereas intermediate-occupancy evaluation reduces it to O(n√δ̄).

## Input / Output

The method operates on a team of n LLM agents (1.7B–8B parameters, e.g., Qwen2.5/3 or LLaMA-3.3) sharing a single textual context (prompt plus accumulated messages) under a fixed round-robin router that activates one agent per decision step. Training inputs are text prompts from reasoning datasets (DeepScaleR, DAPO, AR-Bench, PlanBench) with group-normalized sequence-level rewards; outputs are updated per-agent policies that generate autoregressive token sequences (reasoning chains and answers). The trust-region radius δᵢ and KL penalty coefficient β are tuned per update step, and the monitored token-level KL is estimated from on-policy rollouts via sampled log-probability differences rather than full-vocabulary sums.

## Method

TeamTR is a stage-wise sequential fine-tuning framework that, at each within-stage step, resamples fresh rollouts under the partially updated intermediate team before optimizing the next agent, thereby eliminating the stale-occupancy penalty. Under the turn-taking protocol, a team-level KL trust region reduces to a single-agent token-decomposable reverse KL (via the autoregressive chain rule), which is enforced by an adaptive KL penalty on a PPO-style clipped surrogate with GRPO group-normalized advantages. The key theoretical contribution is a per-update and per-stage improvement lower bound (Theorems 3.4–3.6) showing that intermediate-occupancy evaluation with per-agent trust regions yields a certificate penalty of O(n√δ̄) versus O(n²√δ̄) for naive cached-rollout sequential training. A plug-and-play extension allows replacing any agent mid-training: the new component is aligned on a probe set to satisfy the trust-region constraint (Stage-0 alignment), after which subsequent TeamTR updates retain the same improvement guarantees.

## Results

TeamTR (3×Qwen3-8B) achieves 88.1% on AIME24, 78.3% on AIME25, 99.3% on MATH-500, 94.7% on ZebraLogic, and 90.5% on AutoLogi, outperforming all single-agent fine-tuning baselines (GRPO, DAPO) by 47+ points on AIME24 and 8+ points on MATH-500. The heterogeneous configuration (1.7B+8B+14B) further improves to 89.7% on AIME24 and 96.3% on ZebraLogic. TeamTR also surpasses larger open-source thinking models (Qwen3-32B, QwQ-32B, DeepSeek-R1-70B) on AIME24 by 6.7–18.1 points while using only 24B total parameters. The paper reports a 7.1% average improvement over single-agent and sequential multi-agent baselines across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TeamTR (3×8B) dominates all single-agent fine-tuning baselines: it achieves 88.1% on AIME24 versus 39.1% (GRPO) and 41.3% (DAPO), a gap of ~47 points, and 99.3% on MATH-500 versus 90.7%/91.5%. Against multi-agent fine-tuning baselines at the same 3×8B budget, TeamTR outperforms Debate (68.7%), Debate+Judge (71.1%), and Role-play (69.9%) on AIME24 by 17–19 points, and leads on ZebraLogic by 8.4–10.6 points. TeamTR also surpasses substantially larger open-source thinking models: it beats Qwen3-32B (81.4%), QwQ-32B (79.5%), and DeepSeek-R1-70B (70.0%) on AIME24 by 6.7–18.1 points while using only 24B total parameters. On AR-Bench and PlanBench, TeamTR leads all baselines including o4-mini (46.7 vs 46.7 on DC; 44.1 vs 41.2 on PlanBench). The heterogeneous 1.7B+8B+14B configuration further improves AIME24 to 89.7% and ZebraLogic to 96.3%, showing that mixing model sizes benefits on harder tasks while the homogeneous 3×8B setup is stronger on simpler benchmarks like MATH-500 and AutoLogi.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trust-region policy optimization lineage (TRPO, PPO) and its multi-agent generalizations (HATRPO/HAPPO) from low-dimensional continuous control to the autoregressive, token-level message space of LLMs. It builds on the observation that sequential updates are more stable than joint updates in multi-agent settings, but identifies a previously unformalized failure mode—compounding occupancy shift—where cached rollouts become increasingly stale as earlier agents are updated. The token-decomposable KL trust region and intermediate-occupancy resampling mechanism are novel adaptations that bridge the gap between classical MARL trust-region theory and the practical constraints of LLM fine-tuning (discrete tokens, variable-length messages, shared textual context).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical explanation for why multi-agent LLM teams often underperform single-model baselines: the training process itself introduces a quadratic-in-team-size occupancy mismatch that degrades coordination even when each individual update appears locally beneficial. The plug-and-play certified replacement result has practical implications for modular LLM system evolution, enabling component upgrades without full retraining. Key limitations include the restriction to turn-taking protocols (no concurrent generation or branching), reliance on per-update resampling (which adds ~11% compute overhead), and the fact that the empirical certificate is a training diagnostic rather than a literal per-stage guarantee. Open questions include extending the analysis to concurrent multi-agent generation, tool-mediated state changes, and whether the trust-region framework can be relaxed for longer-horizon settings without the resampling cost.
