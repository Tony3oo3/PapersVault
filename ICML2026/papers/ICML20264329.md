## Problem

Single-cell foundation models like scGPT and GEARS consistently underperform simple regression baselines on perturbation-response prediction. The authors attribute this gap to two design choices: predicting absolute expression on dropout-prone scRNA-seq data (making post-hoc fold-changes unstable) and pretraining on static co-expression reconstruction rather than on how genes co-regulate under expression changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264329/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At fine-tuning, the model receives a perturbation indicator vector (±10.0 for the target gene, 0 elsewhere—deliberately separated from the pretraining logFC range), a basal scVI-denoised expression vector over up to 5,000 HVGs, and per-gene functional embeddings (768-dim BioBERT + 7,411-dim MSigDB pathway membership). It outputs a predicted logFC vector across all genes in the evaluation set. During pretraining, inputs are DE profiles of up to 1,024 genes with observed logFC and basal expression, and the model reconstructs only the 15% masked logFC positions.

## Method

scDEBART is a BART encoder-decoder (8 encoder / 4 decoder Transformer layers, d=768, 12 heads) pretrained via masked denoising on 6.28 million differential-expression profiles derived from 66.6 million cells across 617 CELLxGENE datasets. The key design shift is that the pretraining objective reconstructs logFC values conditioned on basal expression and gene priors, rather than reconstructing absolute expression—teaching the model how gene sets co-vary across expression-change contexts. To make logFC supervision robust under technical sparsity, all fold-changes are computed from scVI posterior-sampling denoised expression, and the corpus is restricted to genes with non-zero proportion ≥ 0.3, Proba-DE ≥ 0.8, and |FC| ≥ 1.5. Fine-tuning replaces the logFC input with a large-magnitude perturbation indicator (±10.0) and optimizes a composite loss combining weighted MSE (emphasizing high-fold-change genes) and a differentiable Pearson-correlation term over both global and top-20% genes, with loss weights learned jointly via sigmoid-parameterized scalars.

## Results

Across five Perturb-seq benchmarks (Norman K562, Replogle K562/RPE1, Nadig HepG2/Jurkat), scDEBART achieves a mean enrichment factor (EF) of 11.96 at top-50 DEGs, 4–7× higher than scGPT (mean EF 1.74) and GEARS (mean EF 2.99), with per-dataset EF ranging 7.73–16.14 versus 0.69–4.29 for the best baselines. RMSE is consistently lowest (0.53–1.01 vs. 0.70–1.06 for next-best), and F1 for direction classification reaches 0.35–0.61 versus 0.03–0.36 for all baselines. On reverse perturbation identification (Norman K562, 7 test samples), scDEBART achieves 71.4% top-1 exact-match accuracy and 100% top-1 partial-match accuracy, compared to 0% for both scGPT and GEARS on exact match. In cross-modal transfer to SCIPLEX drug perturbations, scDEBART shows dose-dependent cosine similarity improvement (0.04→0.30) and above-random DEG enrichment (EF 2.91–4.32), while scVI-scGPT remains near or below random (EF 0.36–0.95).

## Baselines & Comparisons

scDEBART decisively outperforms both scGPT and GEARS on the primary DEG-recovery metric (enrichment factor): mean EF 11.96 vs. 1.74 (scGPT) and 2.99 (GEARS), a 4–7× margin that holds across all five datasets (per-dataset advantage of 2.3–26.3× over the best baseline). On cosine similarity at top-50, scDEBART is competitive but not uniformly dominant—it ranks first on Norman K562 (0.777 vs. 0.721 for counts-LR) and Replogle K562 (0.737 vs. 0.685 for scVI-scGPT), but trails scVI-scGPT by 2–5% on Replogle RPE1, Nadig HepG2, and Nadig Jurkat. The gap widens dramatically on RMSE (scDEBART 0.53–1.01 vs. 0.70–1.06) and F1 direction classification (0.35–0.61 vs. 0.03–0.36), indicating that while baselines capture broad directional trends, they fail to rank true DEGs highly. On reverse perturbation identification, scDEBART achieves 71.4% top-1 exact-match accuracy versus 0% for both scGPT and GEARS, a categorical separation. In cross-modal drug transfer (SCIPLEX), scDEBART is the only model to achieve above-random DEG enrichment (EF 2.91–4.32) and positive dose-dependent cosine similarity, whereas scVI-scGPT shows near-zero to negative alignment (cos −0.05 to −0.27) and near-random EF (0.36–0.95). Notably, among count-based (non-denoised) inputs, simpler baselines (MLP, LR) outperform the complex pretrained models (scGPT, GEARS), consistent with prior findings that dropout-prone raw counts favor low-capacity regressors.

## Relation to Prior Work

scDEBART departs from the dominant paradigm in single-cell foundation models (scGPT, GeneFormer) that pretrain by reconstructing masked absolute expression within individual cells, instead pretraining a BART encoder-decoder to predict log fold-changes conditioned on basal expression across population-level comparisons. It also diverges from prior perturbation predictors (GEARS, scGPT) in its supervision: rather than predicting absolute expression and deriving fold-changes post hoc from dropout-prone point estimates, it trains on scVI-denoised logFC with stringent detection and confidence filtering. The architecture itself is a direct adaptation of BART (Lewis et al., 2019) to the gene-expression domain, with gene priors from BioBERT and MSigDB replacing token embeddings, and the pretraining corpus is assembled from 6.28M cluster-pair DE profiles mined from CELLxGENE via SECUER clustering and scVI posterior-sampling DE analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why prior deep perturbation models underperform simple baselines—dropout-induced bias toward low-detection genes—and shows that corpus-level filtering during pretraining can mitigate this, yielding 4–7× higher DEG enrichment than scGPT and GEARS. The finding that scVI denoising benefits pretrained models but degrades non-pretrained ones (scVI-MLP cosine drops from 0.63 to −0.27) is a useful diagnostic for the field. Key open questions include whether the scVI-based supervision introduces its own inductive biases, how to extend coverage to lowly expressed genes without reintroducing dropout artifacts, and whether the modest cross-modal transfer to drug perturbations (cosine 0.30 at highest dose) can be improved with pharmacology-aware training data.
