## Problem

Existing learning-to-optimize and deep unfolding methods parameterize single-point, gradient-based updates that become ineffective in complex, multimodal, high-dimensional landscapes. There is no principled, learnable framework that simultaneously integrates gradient-driven exploitation with sampling-based exploration at the population level, and most generative-model approaches to optimization require ground-truth solutions or one-shot mappings that cannot capture iterative dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a population of P=20 candidate solutions x_j ∈ R^n along with per-member context (objective values f(x_j) and gradients ∇f(x_j) when available) and global population statistics (empirical mean, covariance, population-average objective/gradient, and weighted covariance Cov(X, f(X))). After K=10 unfolded iterations, it outputs the updated population and returns the best solution found. The dimension-agnostic variant replaces per-dimension embeddings with pooled statistics (mean, std, max) so the same model handles arbitrary n without retraining, enabling transfer from 20D to 200D problems.

## Method

FlowOptimizer represents each optimization iteration as a continuous-time velocity field v_θ(z_t, t, c^k) that acts jointly on the entire population z_t ∈ R^{P×n}, parameterized by a permutation-equivariant self-attention architecture to capture inter-member interactions while respecting set symmetry. Training is two-phase: first, a simulation-free flow matching pre-training matches displacements from source populations to improved target populations (obtained via CMA-ES) without requiring ground-truth solutions; second, the K iterations are unrolled as sequential layers in a deep network and fine-tuned end-to-end by directly minimizing a weighted sum of per-iteration losses (a SoftMin-best and mean objective combination) via Euler integration of the velocity ODE. An optional Gaussian base sampler can be inserted before the flow to stabilize exploration, and the final K_final iterations share parameters to enable extended inference. The key distinction from prior L2O work is that the learned update operates on the full population simultaneously rather than on individual points, enabling the model to coordinate exploration and exploitation through learned population-level transport dynamics.

## Results

On standard 20D non-convex benchmarks (Ackley, Rastrigin, Levy), FlowOptimizer achieves final normalized errors orders of magnitude below all baselines within 10 iterations, despite baselines being granted ×5 more wall-clock time. On real-world problems (Table 1), FlowOptimizer attains Best(f(X^K))/f(μ⁰) ratios of 0.003–0.013 across robotic arm (10D/30D), power grid (20D/50D), and supply chain (40D/80D) tasks, versus 0.005–0.68 for the best baselines. The dimension-agnostic variant trained on 20D problems generalizes to 200D instances while all baselines degrade severely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowOptimizer outperforms all eight baselines across every problem class and dimension tested. Random Search and CEM are the weakest, stagnating near initial error on all benchmarks due to poor scalability in high-dimensional multimodal landscapes. CMA-ES shows moderate progress on Rastrigin but fails to consistently improve on Ackley and Levy; on real-world problems it achieves ratios of 0.008–0.11, still 2–10× worse than FlowOptimizer. PSO is the strongest sampling-based baseline (0.005 on 10D robotics, 0.011 on 30D), but degrades noticeably as dimension increases. Among gradient-based methods, MS-Adam is the strongest (0.007 on 20D power grid, 0.02 on 80D supply chain), yet still 14× worse than FlowOptimizer on power grid and 1.5× worse on supply chain. The L2O-GD baseline (Sambharya & Stellato, 2024), which learns per-iteration step sizes for gradient descent, achieves 0.012–0.027 across real-world tasks but consistently trails FlowOptimizer, highlighting that learning individual GD steps is insufficient without explicit population-level coordination and the richer expressiveness of flow-based transport dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowOptimizer sits at the intersection of three research threads: flow matching for generative modeling (Lipman et al., 2023), deep unfolding of iterative algorithms into neural network layers (Monga et al., 2021), and population-based optimization (CMA-ES, PSO, CEM). The key departure from prior L2O work is the shift from single-point, gradient-based update rules to a population-level velocity field parameterized by a permutation-equivariant attention network, trained first via simulation-free flow matching and then fine-tuned end-to-end through unfolding. This repurposes the flow matching objective—originally designed to learn transport maps between distributions—as a mechanism for learning optimization dynamics, and recovers classical methods (CEM, PSO, GD) as special cases of the learned flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, self-supervised way to learn optimizers that integrate gradient exploitation and sampling-based exploration without hand-crafted algorithmic choices, with the practical advantage of dimension-agnostic generalization (20D→200D) that most L2O methods lack. Key limitations include the current restriction to unconstrained optimization, the computational overhead of ODE integration during fine-tuning (long computational graphs), and the dependence on training within a problem class. Open questions include extending to constrained settings via projection or implicit layers, distilling the integrated dynamics into shallower surrogates for efficiency, and developing more sophisticated pre-training sampling schemes beyond the current CMA-ES-based improvement operator.
