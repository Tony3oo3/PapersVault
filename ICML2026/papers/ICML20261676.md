## Problem

Dao and Gu (2024) established that a scalar-times-identity state-space model is exactly equivalent to a 1-semiseparable masked attention, but the natural extension to general diagonal state matrices remained only a conjecture without a formal proof. This paper fills that gap by providing a self-contained proof for the diagonal case, characterizing the exact class of SSMs that admit a 1-semiseparable masked-attention dual, and showing that the duality fundamentally breaks down for standard softmax attention.

## Input / Output

The method takes a time-varying linear SSM specified by per-step state transition matrices A^t ∈ R^{N×N}, input vectors b_t ∈ R^N, and output vectors c_t ∈ R^N, applied to an input sequence X ∈ R^{T×d}. It produces the equivalent sequence-to-sequence map Y = MX ∈ R^{T×d}, where M is a T×T lower-triangular kernel matrix. The key structural constraint is that M must be N-semiseparable (every lower-triangular submatrix has rank ≤ N), and the attention dual requires M to factor as L ⊙ (CB^⊤) where L is a 1-semiseparable causal mask and C, B ∈ R^{T×N}.

## Method

The paper extends SSD from scalar-identity to general diagonal SSMs by showing the induced kernel M decomposes as a sum of N one-semiseparable masked-attention heads, each corresponding to one diagonal state mode. When all diagonal state matrices are full-rank, these N heads collapse into a single 1-SS masked attention with rescaled query/key vectors. The authors prove the diagonal SSD algorithm (Algorithm 1) achieves the same O(NTd) FLOP complexity as the scalar case, matching the training complexity lower bound. The central theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) gives a necessary and sufficient condition for a general SSM to have a 1-SS masked-attention dual: the kernel must decompose into contiguous principal blocks where each block has at most N new columns (independent memory directions). Finally, the paper shows the duality cannot extend to softmax attention, since Softmax(QK^⊤) generically explodes to full rank T regardless of the rank of QK^⊤.

## Results

On WikiText-2 next-token prediction (L=128, d_model=64, d_state=16, 2 layers, averaged over 6 seeds), the proposed SSD-Mamba variant achieves a best validation loss of 9.7 and best validation accuracy of 11.1%, outperforming the baseline Mamba (selective SSM) which reaches 10.5 and 9.5% respectively. However, SSD-Mamba fits the training set less tightly (train loss 5.7 vs. 4.5; train accuracy 17% vs. 33%), indicating reduced overfitting. Numerical verification experiments confirm exact equivalence between diagonal SSMs and sums of 1-SS masked attention heads to within 10⁻¹⁴ absolute error, and validate that the semiseparable rank of the induced kernel equals the number of distinct decay modes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed SSD-Mamba (diagonal SSD-style SSM block) against the standard Mamba selective SSM baseline (Ma, 2023) on WikiText-2 next-token prediction. SSD-Mamba wins on both generalization metrics: it achieves a lower best validation loss (9.7 vs. 10.5) and higher best validation accuracy (11.1% vs. 9.5%), while also maintaining a lower end-of-training validation loss (9.8 vs. 11.7). In contrast, baseline Mamba fits the training data more aggressively (train loss 4.5 vs. 5.7; train accuracy 33% vs. 17%) but exhibits clear overfitting, with validation loss degrading after an initial improvement. The authors attribute SSD-Mamba's superior generalization to the structural constraints of the diagonal parameterization, which provides N independent decay modes at the same O(NTd) computational cost as the scalar-identity case. The comparison is limited to a single small-scale language modeling task (5,000 training segments, 2 layers), so the generalization advantage should be interpreted cautiously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Structured State-Space Duality (SSD) introduced by Dao and Gu (2024), which established a one-to-one equivalence between scalar-identity SSMs and 1-semiseparable masked attention. The authors formalize the diagonal SSM generalization that Dao and Gu conjectured but left unproven, and they draw on the structured-matrix literature (semiseparable and quasiseparable matrices of Vandebril et al., Eidelman et al.) to provide a self-contained constructive proof of the N-SS / N-SSS equivalence. The empirical validation builds on the Mamba architecture (Gu and Dao, 2023) and its minimal PyTorch implementation (Ma, 2023), positioning the diagonal SSD block as a drop-in replacement for the selective SSM core.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean necessary-and-sufficient condition (Theorem 3.1) for when an SSM admits a 1-SS masked-attention dual, which sharpens the theoretical boundary between recurrent and attention-based sequence models and clarifies why softmax attention resists SSM duality (rank explosion). However, the empirical validation is limited to small-scale settings (d_model=64, 2 layers, 5K training segments on WikiText-2; synthetic time-series regression), and no optimized diagonal-SSD kernel exists yet, so the practical impact on large-scale sequence modeling remains to be demonstrated. Open questions include whether the structural insights can be translated into hardware-efficient algorithms and whether the diagonal parameterization's mild validation advantage over Mamba persists at scale.
