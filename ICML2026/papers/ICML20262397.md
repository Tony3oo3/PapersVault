## Problem

Existing theory predicts that DDPM requires iteration complexity scaling linearly with data dimension d, yet in practice DDPM achieves high-quality samples in far fewer steps for structured data. This paper asks whether diffusion models can provably achieve dimension-free sampling when the target distribution is well-approximated by a Gaussian Mixture Model, and provides the first non-asymptotic guarantee showing Õ(1/ε) iterations suffice, independent of both d and the number of components K.

## Input / Output

The sampler starts from Y_T ~ N(0, I_d) and a sequence of score estimates {s_t}_{t=1}^T obtained via score matching (minimizing expected squared error against the true score). The estimates are truncated via a clip operator with threshold proportional to √(d log(dT)/(1−ᾱ_t)) for technical stability. The output is a single sample Y_1 whose distribution is guaranteed to be ε-close to the target in total variation distance, with the guarantee depending on the GMM approximation error ε_apprx, the averaged score estimation error ε_score, and the number of iterations T.

## Method

The core technical contribution is proving that the trace of the Jacobian of the GMM score function satisfies tr(I_d + J_t(X_t^GMM)) ≤ C_1 log(KT) with high probability, which is the key mechanism enabling dimension-free iteration complexity. The proof constructs three auxiliary reverse processes (true-score, discretization-error, and estimated-score) and decomposes the TV error into discretization error, score estimation error, and GMM approximation error. The discretization error is bounded inductively using the Jacobian trace bound, yielding Õ(log²(KT)·log²T / T). The score estimation error is controlled via KL divergence between Gaussian transition kernels under true vs. estimated scores, giving ε_score·√(log T). The GMM approximation error contributes √(d·ε_apprx)·log^{3/2}(dT), completing the bound in Theorem 1.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes that DDPM achieves TV distance ≲ log²(KT)log²(T)/T + ε_score·√(log T) + √(d·ε_apprx)·log^(3/2)(dT) between the generated sample and the target distribution. For exact GMM targets (Corollary 1), this simplifies to ≲ log²(KT)log²(T)/T + ε_score·√(log T), yielding an iteration complexity of Õ(1/ε) that is independent of both ambient dimension d and number of components K. Empirically (Figure 1), with T=200 fixed iterations, the TV distance remains stable as d increases from 10 to 200 (with K=50) and exhibits only mild logarithmic growth as K increases from 5 to 200 (with d=50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper's Õ(1/ε) iteration complexity for GMM-approximable distributions represents a fundamental improvement over all prior theoretical bounds, which scale at least linearly with dimension d. Compared to Liang et al. (2024b), which established Õ(d/ε²) for GMMs by analyzing Lipschitz properties and second moments, this paper removes both the d-dependence and improves the ε-dependence from ε⁻² to ε⁻¹. Compared to the general-distribution bound of Õ(d/ε) from Li & Yan (2024b), the GMM structure enables a dimension-free guarantee. The key mechanism is a Jacobian trace bound (tr(I_d + J_t) ≤ C₁ log(KT)) that holds specifically for isotropic GMMs, which does not generalize to arbitrary distributions. The paper also notes that end-to-end results from Chen et al. (2024) and Gatmiry et al. (2024) still require diffusion steps scaling linearly with d, whereas this work isolates the sampling-phase complexity that is dimension-free.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the divide-and-conquer framework for diffusion model analysis pioneered by Chen et al. (2022), adopting the TV-distance analysis toolkit from Li & Yan (2024b) and the learning rate schedule from Li & Cai (2024). The most direct prior work is Liang et al. (2024b), which established Õ(d/ε²) iteration complexity for GMMs by analyzing Lipschitz constants and second moments, but did not reveal any dimension-free adaptation. The key departure here is the identification of a GMM-specific structural property—the Jacobian trace bound tr(I_d + J_t) ≤ C log(KT)—that enables a dimension-free analysis, in contrast to the general O(d/ε) bounds that apply to arbitrary distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result provides the first theoretical justification for the well-known empirical observation that DDPM requires far fewer steps than worst-case theory predicts in high-dimensional settings, by showing that GMM structure (a universal approximation family) is sufficient to eliminate dimension from the complexity. The main limitations are the restriction to isotropic Gaussians and the requirement that the target be well-approximated by a GMM; extending to general well-conditioned covariances is identified as a key open problem due to the loss of isotropic cancellation in the Jacobian. An end-to-end theory integrating score estimation efficiency with the sampling phase, and a matching lower bound on the 1/ε dependence, remain important future directions.
