## Problem

Existing continual learning methods optimize for in-domain performance on previously seen training domains, making them prone to shortcut learning—relying on spurious, domain-specific cues that fail on unseen domains after deployment. No prior CL method explicitly learns domain-invariant representations, and naive sequential extensions of domain-invariant representation learning (DIRL) methods provide only limited benefits because static invariance priors cannot faithfully reproduce multi-domain invariance objectives under sequential access.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261845/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives labeled samples from a sequence of source domains (28×28 grayscale digits, 32×32 or 64×64 RGB images, 48×48 wafer maps, 96×96 histopathology patches, or 50-dimensional tabular features) and outputs a class label from a fixed label space shared across all domains. A key operational detail is the domain-partitioned memory buffer storing (x, y, z) triples, where z encodes method-specific information (logits or features) captured at insertion time and later used for invariance alignment. Evaluation follows a deployment-oriented protocol: sequential training on source domains followed by testing on a single, completely unseen target domain whose characteristics are unknown at training time.

## Method

The paper proposes a class of five CL algorithms (★-CL-VREX, ★-CL-Fishr, ★-CL-CORAL, ★-CL-MMD, ★-CL-ANDMask) that combine replay-based training with two key mechanisms: (i) multi-domain invariance computation via replay, where per-domain statistics (risk, gradient variance, feature moments, kernel mean embeddings, or gradient signs) are computed on replayed minibatches from all previously seen domains within each update, enabling genuine cross-domain invariance penalties rather than comparisons against static priors; and (ii) sequential invariance alignment via knowledge distillation, which anchors replayed-domain statistics to their original domain references to prevent drift as the model evolves. The overall objective is L_ERM^replay + λ·P_s^replay + β·L_align, where the invariance penalty and alignment loss are method-specific. This design overcomes the fundamental limitation that standard DIRL methods require joint access to all source domains, which is unavailable in sequential CL settings.

## Results

Across six datasets (RotatedMNIST, CIFAR10C, TinyImageNetC, WM811K, Covertype, Camelyon17), the best proposed method ★-CL-CORAL achieves 64.7% average target-domain accuracy/F1, followed by ★-CL-VREX (63.4%) and ★-CL-MMD (63.1%). These outperform the strongest replay-based baselines (ER-ACE at 62.8%, STAR at 62.1%) by 1.9–2.6 pp, optimization-based methods (AGEM at 58.5%) by ~6 pp, and regularization-based methods (EWC at 52.0%, SI at 52.9%) by ~10–13 pp. The offline upper bound URM (joint access to all domains) reaches 73.3%, indicating a remaining gap of ~9 pp for the sequential setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed ★-CL methods consistently outperform all 13 CL baselines on average target-domain performance. Against optimization-based methods, ★-CL-CORAL (64.7%) beats AGEM (58.5%) by 6.2 pp and UPGD (48.2%) by 16.5 pp. Against regularization-based methods, the gap is even larger: EWC (52.0%), SI (52.9%), and SNR (52.8%) are all surpassed by 12–13 pp. Among replay-based baselines, the closest competitors are ER-ACE (62.8%) and STAR (62.1%), which are outperformed by 1.9 and 2.6 pp respectively; FDR (61.2%), LODE (54.9%), CoPE (56.2%), EFC (59.2%), and SARL (54.0%) trail by 3.5–10.7 pp. The offline upper bound URM (73.3%), which has joint access to all source domains, remains ~9 pp above the best sequential method, confirming the inherent difficulty of the sequential setting. Against CDA/CTTA baselines (TENT, SHOT++, CoTTA) that are allowed unsupervised updates on the target domain, the proposed methods still win by up to 10 pp, demonstrating that the invariance learned during sequential training is more effective than post-hoc adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established but largely independent research threads: continual learning (CL), which prevents forgetting across sequential domains, and domain-invariant representation learning (DIRL), which enforces cross-domain invariance to improve out-of-domain generalization. The authors adapt five specific DIRL objectives—VREX (risk variance), Fishr (gradient-variance matching), CORAL (feature moment alignment), MMD (kernel mean embedding), and ANDMask (gradient sign agreement)—to the sequential CL setting by combining replay-based multi-domain invariance computation with a knowledge-distillation-style sequential alignment to stored domain priors. A key departure from prior work is the demonstration that naive sequential extensions of DIRL (storing static invariance statistics and matching the current domain to them) fail to reproduce the multi-domain invariance objective, motivating the tailored replay-plus-alignment architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The deployment-oriented evaluation protocol (sequential training → deployment → testing on a single unseen target domain) addresses a critical gap in CL evaluation, where most methods are assessed only on held-out splits of previously seen domains. The consistent 6–10 pp out-of-domain improvements across six diverse datasets (vision, medicine, manufacturing, ecology) and the robustness to reduced buffer sizes and compute budgets make the findings practically relevant for domains where post-deployment generalization is essential but data cannot be freely shared or fully stored. Open questions include whether the approach extends to class-incremental or task-incremental settings, how it behaves when domain labels for buffer partitioning are unavailable, and whether the invariance alignment component generalizes to invariance notions that cannot be computed on a per-batch basis.
