## Problem

Even state-of-the-art text-guided image editing models (e.g., Nano Banana Pro) frequently fail to preserve fine-grained geometric structure during texture editing, instead regenerating the edited subject with altered shape or layout. Existing benchmarks compound this problem by relying on synthetic single-object Blender scenes and MLLM-based scoring that cannot reliably detect subtle unintended structural changes, leaving the field without both effective training signals and adequate evaluation for structure-preserving texture editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266042/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (1024×1024) and a natural language prompt specifying a texture modification (e.g., 'replace the table with polished walnut wood') and outputs a single edited image where the target object's material appearance changes while its geometry, spatial position, and surrounding scene remain invariant. Editing targets can span individual objects or semantically grouped sets of components (e.g., a full chair including legs and backrest), and the two sub-tasks—attribute-level adjustment (roughness, metallicity, transparency) and global texture replacement—differ in the magnitude of visual change expected.

## Method

TexEditor employs a two-stage training pipeline on top of the Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 diffusion transformer backbone. Stage 1 (SFT) uses TexBlender, a 5,000-pair synthetic dataset rendered in Blender from 3D-Front scenes where paired images share identical 3D geometry and differ only in target texture (via Principled BSDF parameter changes or MatSynth PBR texture swaps), providing clean structural supervision at multiple editing granularities. Stage 2 (StructureNFT) applies reinforcement learning on 1,500 real COCO-based queries using DiffusionNFT as the RL framework, with a composite reward combining an MLLM instruction-adherence score (Gemini 3 Flash) and a structure-preservation score computed as SSIM between SAUGE wireframe extractions of the original and edited images, with task-specific empirical normalization thresholds to handle the compressed SSIM score range. The key design insight is that structural invariance must be enforced jointly from the data side (geometry-locked synthetic pairs) and the optimization side (explicit low-level structural loss in RL), since neither alone suffices to generalize structure preservation to real-world scenes.

## Results

On TexBench, TexEditor achieves a TexEval score of 0.886 on texture replacement and 0.760 on attribute editing, outperforming the strongest baseline Nano Banana Pro (0.824 and 0.708 respectively). On the Blender-based benchmark, TexEditor reaches 0.972 TexEval on both sub-tasks, surpassing Nano Banana Pro (0.855 and 0.872). On the general-purpose ImgEdit benchmark, TexEditor improves over its base model Qwen-2509 on texture-related sub-tasks (e.g., Background: 4.13 vs. 3.87; Adjust: 4.13 vs. 4.05) while slightly underperforming on less texture-related tasks (Action: 2.79 vs. 3.33).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TexEditor outperforms all three baseline groups across every metric on both TexBench and the Blender-based benchmark. Against the base model Qwen-2509, TexEditor gains +0.169 TexEval on texture replacement (0.886 vs. 0.717) and +0.246 on attribute editing (0.760 vs. 0.514), with the largest improvement in structural consistency (+0.287 on texture, +0.408 on attribute). Against Alchemist, the prior texture-editing method, TexEditor leads by +0.145 TexEval on texture and +0.177 on attribute, showing that scene-level Blender data and StructureNFT RL substantially improve over single-object synthetic training. Against the strongest commercial baseline Nano Banana Pro, TexEditor achieves +0.062 TexEval on texture (0.886 vs. 0.824) and +0.052 on attribute (0.760 vs. 0.708), with a particularly large structural advantage (+0.128 on texture, +0.119 on attribute). On the Blender benchmark, the gap widens further: TexEditor scores 0.972 on both sub-tasks versus Nano Banana Pro's 0.855/0.872. On ImgEdit, TexEditor improves over Qwen-2509 on texture-adjacent tasks (Background, Adjust, Compose) but lags on Action and Add, which are less related to texture editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

TexEditor builds on the Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 diffusion backbone and the DiffusionNFT RL framework, extending them with a structure-preserving auxiliary loss (StructureNFT) that uses SAUGE edge maps and SSIM to enforce geometric invariance during texture editing. On the data side, it departs from prior texture editing datasets (Alchemist, TexSliders) that focus on isolated single-object Blender scenes by constructing TexBlender from 3D-Front indoor layouts with grouped multi-object editing, providing scene-level structural supervision. The evaluation side introduces TexBench (COCO-based, real-world) and TexEval (combining MLLM instruction scores with low-level structural metrics), addressing the gap that existing benchmarks are synthetic and MLLM-only scoring misses structural degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structure preservation during texture editing is a non-trivial failure mode even for SOTA commercial systems, and that a two-stage recipe (synthetic SFT cold-start + RL with explicit structural loss) is a practical and effective solution without architectural changes. The finding that the structural loss cannot be compensated by simply scaling RL data or compute (No-Struct-80 still lags Base-40) underscores that explicit geometric supervision is necessary rather than emergent. Open questions include multi-object texture editing (a documented failure mode), generalization beyond texture-related sub-tasks, and whether the empirical SSIM thresholds transfer across domains.
