## Problem

Deploying LLMs across heterogeneous memory budgets (from 80 GB server GPUs to <16 GB edge devices) requires flexible control of model capacity, but existing sparse-and-low-rank (SLR) compression methods either apply post-hoc decomposition to trained weights (which fails due to structured correlations violating RPCA assumptions) or impose heuristic, layer-agnostic structure schedules during training that ignore spectral heterogeneity across blocks. A principled, training-compatible framework that generalizes across architectures and enables continuous, deployment-time capacity control without retraining or architectural modification remains lacking.

## Input / Output

SALAAD takes a standard LLaMA-based Transformer architecture and pretrains it on text (C4 dataset) using self-supervised next-token prediction. The output is a single checkpoint containing both the dense weights X and structured surrogates L+S per block; at deployment, a user-specified memory budget C and allocation ratio κ are fed into the HPA strategy to produce a compressed model with a continuously adjustable parameter count, enabling elastic deployment from near-full-rank down to heavily compressed variants without retraining.

## Method

SALAAD formulates SLR weight learning as an augmented Lagrangian problem (min ℓ(X) + α|L|_* + β|S|_1 s.t. X=L+S) and solves it via a two-stage ADMM reformulation: a first stage performs standard gradient-based X-updates on a coupled loss ℓ_c = ℓ + (ρ/2)|X−L−S+Y/ρ|²_F, while a second stage applies closed-form proximal operators (SVD soft-thresholding for L, element-wise soft-thresholding for S) to recover the structured surrogate. An I-controller adaptively adjusts block-wise thresholds α and β at each iteration based on the gap between observed effective rank ratio (under energy coverage γ) and target density versus user-specified targets, reducing structural hyperparameters to a single global penalty coefficient ρ that follows the scaling law ρ ∝ 1/(N√(nm)). At deployment, the Homomorphic Parameter Allocation (HPA) strategy splits the parameter reduction budget C between low-rank and sparse components via a mixing coefficient κ, then applies uniform proportional truncation (removing the smallest-magnitude singular values and sparse entries) across all blocks, yielding a continuous spectrum of model capacities from a single checkpoint.

## Results

On C4 pretraining, SALAAD's structured surrogate (L+S) achieves perplexity of 31.26 (60M, 50M params), 22.65 (130M, 126M params), 18.04 (350M, 276M params), and 14.72 (1B, 905M params), outperforming all baselines at every scale. The HPA-compressed variant (L̃+S̃) further reduces parameters to 44M/97M/194M/646M while maintaining PPL of 31.97/23.90/19.70/15.07, still beating or matching most baselines at comparable budgets. On zero-shot downstream tasks (MMLU, ARC-C, COPA, HellaSwag, BoolQ, PIQA), the 1B HPA-compressed model matches the vanilla 1B model on 4 of 6 benchmarks with at most 2-point drops on the others.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SALAAD (L+S) achieves the lowest perplexity across all four model scales, beating the strongest baseline LOST by 1.0 PPL at 60M (31.26 vs 32.25), 1.4 at 130M (22.65 vs 24.05), 0.91 at 350M (18.04 vs 18.95), and 0.30 at 1B (14.72 vs 15.02). The HPA-compressed variant (L̃+S̃) at matched parameter budgets (~43–44M at 60M, ~94–97M at 130M, ~185–194M at 350M, ~609–646M at 1B) outperforms LORO, CoLA, SLTrain, and LOST at every scale, e.g., 31.97 vs 33.87 (LORO) and 32.25 (LOST) at 60M. Against full-rank pretraining, SALAAD (L+S) improves PPL by 2.8–3.1 points while using 14–33% fewer parameters. LoRA and ReLoRA, which do not reduce inference-time parameters, show substantially worse PPL (up to 5.7 points worse at 60M), confirming that post-hoc low-rank adaptation is insufficient for pretraining. GaLore, which only reduces training memory but yields dense inference models, is outperformed by SALAAD at all scales. The paper notes that LOST introduces additional nonlinear layers altering the architecture, whereas SALAAD preserves the original architecture without modification.

## Relation to Prior Work

SALAAD builds directly on the RPCA/augmented Lagrangian framework (Candès et al., 2011; Lin et al., 2010) and nonconvex ADMM convergence theory (Hong et al., 2015), repurposing them from matrix recovery to structured LLM pretraining. It departs from the closest training-time SLR methods—SLTrain and LOST—by replacing their fixed, layer-agnostic rank and sparsity schedules with an adaptive I-controller that regulates structure per block, and from elastic inference frameworks (Flextron, MatFormer) by operating entirely in weight space rather than requiring nested or routed architectures. The HPA post-hoc truncation strategy further distinguishes SALAAD by enabling continuous capacity scaling from a single checkpoint, whereas prior methods support only discrete submodel selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to obtain a continuous spectrum of model capacities from a single pretraining run addresses a practical gap in LLM deployment: practitioners currently must choose from a fixed set of model sizes or apply lossy post-hoc compression. The empirical finding that embedding layers are inherently SLR-inducible while the LM head is not reveals a previously uncharacterized structural asymmetry in LLM components, with implications for how future compression methods should treat different submodules. Key limitations include evaluation only up to 1B parameters on C4 with LLaMA-based architectures, reliance on float32 training for headline results, and the HPA strategy's assumption of structural homomorphism across blocks that may not hold for heterogeneous architectures. Open questions include whether the scaling law for ρ holds at 70B+ scale, whether the embedding-layer finding generalizes beyond LLaMA, and how to handle the LM head without performance loss.
