## Problem

Existing vision-based pressure estimation methods are limited to planar surfaces or fingertip-only contacts, failing to capture full-hand pressure distributions on complex 3D objects. In the egocentric setting, severe self-occlusion makes pixel-to-pressure alignment ill-posed, and visually identical objects can have vastly different physical attributes (weight, fill state), rendering deterministic regression insufficient. No benchmark provides synchronized full-hand pressure supervision for diverse 3D object grasps under controlled conditions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 16-frame egocentric RGB clip (256×256, sampled at 5 fps from a 15 Hz synchronized timeline) along with optional conditioning: per-frame binary hand masks extracted via GroundedSAM, a structured text prompt encoding object weight/material and subject demographics, and a uniform-pressure prototype heatmap (100 N across the hand region). Output is a per-sensor pressure sequence of M=162 values (normalized to [0,1] with a 5 N noise floor and 200 N ceiling) or an equivalent 256×256 single-channel hand heatmap; the two are bidirectionally convertible via a fixed Gaussian-diffusion rendering operator A and its Ridge-regression inverse.

## Method

EgoPressureDiff formulates pressure estimation as conditional latent diffusion by adapting a pre-trained Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) backbone: both the RGB clip and the target pressure heatmap are encoded into the same VAE latent space, and the noisy pressure latent is concatenated channel-wise with the RGB latent as input to the 3D U-Net denoiser. Three complementary conditioning signals guide denoising—spatial hint masks added via a lightweight convolutional Mask Encoder, and text/prototype features injected through the proposed Physically-Informed Feature Rectification (PIFR) layer. PIFR performs cross-attention from the denoiser's intermediate features to CLIP-encoded prototype and text tokens, then applies a zero-initialized affine modulation (scale γ, shift β) to calibrate the anatomical topology prior with physical magnitude constraints before feeding the rectified feature to subsequent blocks. Training uses a masked MSE noise-prediction loss restricted to the valid hand region over 40k steps with AdamW (lr 1e-5), and latent consistency distillation compresses inference from 25 to 4–8 steps while retaining most accuracy. A complementary discriminative baseline, EgoPressureFormer, uses TimeSformer with learnable sensor-query cross-attention and a frame-level contact gate to directly predict per-sensor pressure bins without pixel alignment.

## Results

On the EgoTactile Gloved-hand benchmark (Object-Held-Out), EgoPressureDiff achieves 96.4% Temporal Accuracy, 56.3% Contact IoU, 38.9% Volumetric IoU, 3.4 N MAE, and 3.1 CoP Error, substantially outperforming the discriminative EgoPressureFormer baseline (84.5% Temp Acc., 36.8% C-IoU, 26.5% V-IoU, 6.2 N MAE, 7.5 CoP) and PressureVision (65.2% Temp Acc., 24.5% C-IoU, 16.8% V-IoU, 9.2 N MAE, 12.5 CoP). Under the Subject-Held-Out protocol, EgoPressureDiff maintains 92.5% Temp Acc. and 51.2% C-IoU, while EgoPressureFormer drops to 80.2% and 32.5% respectively. In the Bare-hand transfer setting (fine-tuned), EgoPressureDiff reaches 57.8% C-IoU and 40.2% V-IoU, surpassing even its own gloved-setting performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoPressureDiff outperforms both baselines across all five metrics under both evaluation protocols. Against PressureVision (a CNN encoder-decoder designed for planar surfaces), EgoPressureDiff improves C-IoU by +31.8 points (24.5→56.3) and reduces CoP Error by 75% (12.5→3.1) on Object-Held-Out, reflecting the advantage of full-hand 3D modeling over pixel-aligned planar regression. Against the stronger EgoPressureFormer (a TimeSformer-based query decoder), EgoPressureDiff gains +19.5 points in C-IoU (36.8→56.3), +12.4 points in V-IoU (26.5→38.9), and halves the CoP Error (7.5→3.1), which the authors attribute to the diffusion model's ability to infer plausible contact patterns under severe self-occlusion where deterministic regression yields conservative, blurred outputs. Under Subject-Held-Out, EgoPressureDiff's advantage narrows slightly (C-IoU 51.2 vs. 32.5) but remains substantial, suggesting the generative prior and subject-specific text conditioning aid cross-subject generalization. In the Bare-hand zero-shot transfer (Setting A), EgoPressureDiff maintains 47.2% C-IoU—surpassing even the fine-tuned EgoPressureFormer (36.2%)—demonstrating that the diffusion backbone learns appearance-invariant physical priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual pressure estimation line (PressureVision, EgoPressure, PressureVision++) from planar surfaces and fingertip-only contacts to full-hand 3D object grasping in egocentric video. Methodologically, it departs from the deterministic regression paradigm by adapting Stable Video Diffusion as a generative backbone, motivated by the ill-posed nature of pressure inference under self-occlusion. The PIFR layer is a novel lightweight module that injects text-encoded physical attributes (weight, material) into the diffusion denoiser via affine modulation of a CLIP-encoded prototype, building on the cross-attention conditioning pattern of SVD but repurposing it for physical constraint injection rather than image-to-video guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoTactile is the first benchmark pairing egocentric video with synchronized full-hand pressure on 3D objects, filling a clear gap between planar-surface pressure datasets and in-the-wild tactile datasets like OpenTouch. The finding that a generative diffusion model substantially outperforms deterministic regression under occlusion (V-IoU +12.4%, CoP halved) suggests a broader principle for ill-posed visual-to-physical inference tasks. Key limitations include the controlled green-screen collection, the weakly-paired nature of the bare-hand subset (~105 ms timing gaps), and the still-slower inference speed (2.8 FPS vs 6.5 FPS for the transformer baseline), though latent consistency distillation partially addresses the latter. Open questions include whether explicit 6D pose conditioning would further close the gap on rare interaction patterns (palm support, hook lift) and whether the approach transfers to robot hands for imitation learning.
