## Problem

Multimodal agents fine-tuned on Android interaction trajectories achieve strong in-distribution performance but generalize poorly to unseen applications, post-training interface changes, and multi-app workflows because they lack explicit task-level procedural knowledge. Existing remedies—unconditional external search or fixed pre-execution planning—either break temporal coherence during closed-loop execution or fail to adapt when the agent's internal knowledge is outdated. The paper asks whether an agent can explicitly assess the sufficiency of its own procedural knowledge and selectively acquire external information only when a deficiency is detected.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the grounded actor receives the natural-language goal, the current UI screenshot (rendered as up to four 448x448 image tiles), the full action history, and the current task-level procedure (a complete ordered list of natural-language steps, not just remaining steps). The meta-reasoner additionally consumes the two most recent screenshots and the prior procedure to emit a structured reasoning chain ending in a binary CONTINUE or SEARCH decision. When SEARCH is triggered, app-specific web pages are retrieved as screenshots, processed into textual procedural snippets, and fed back to the procedural generator to produce an updated complete procedure before the next action is taken.

## Method

PAMAR decouples high-level procedural reasoning from low-level grounded execution via three components: a procedural generator (GPT-4o) that produces and online-updates a complete natural-language procedure, a meta-reasoning module that evaluates whether the current procedure remains sufficient given observed interaction context, and a grounded actor (PLM-8B) trained via SFT on procedure-augmented trajectories to condition actions on that procedure. Procedural supervision is synthesized from expert demonstrations through transition-grounded step extraction, redundancy merging, condition-guided abstraction (replacing fixed-count actions with state-dependent stopping criteria), and LLM-generated paraphrases to expose the actor to heterogeneous procedure phrasings. The meta-reasoner is implemented either as a prompt-based heuristic that checks plan position, action-outcome consistency, and next-step reachability, or as a trained classifier (Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E) supervised by the log-probability margin between the reference expert procedure and the internally generated procedure under the grounded actor. External retrieval is triggered only when the meta-reasoner detects a procedural deficiency, reducing search usage from 100% to ~45% while maintaining comparable success rates.

## Results

On DigiData-Bench-Auto, PAMAR-MR-Dynamic (prompt-based meta-reasoner) achieves 54.20% overall success rate, improving over the direct PLM-8B baseline (41.47%) by 12.7 points, while invoking external search in only 45.14% of episodes. On the cross-application split, PAMAR-MR-Dynamic reaches 47.06% versus 31.39% for the baseline. On DigiData-AppUpdate, the training-based meta-reasoner achieves 63.93% success (vs. 29.50% without procedures), and on DigiData-MultiApp, PAMAR-MR-Dynamic reaches 15.0% versus 0.0% for the action-only baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the DigiData-trained PLM-8B agent (Sun et al., 2025a), fine-tuned via SFT on DigiData, AitW, AndroidControl, and Cauldron. PAMAR-MR-Dynamic (prompt) outperforms this baseline by 12.7 points overall (54.20% vs. 41.47%) and by 15.7 points on cross-app generalization (47.06% vs. 31.39%), while using external search in only 45% of episodes. GPT-4o used as a direct action generator performs far worse (9.00% overall), confirming that grounded fine-tuning is essential for low-level UI control. The always-search variant achieves a slightly higher overall score (54.90%) but at 100% search usage, whereas PAMAR-MR-Dynamic matches or exceeds it on cross-app (47.06% vs. 41.17%) with less than half the retrieval calls. On DigiData-AppUpdate, PAMAR-MR-Dynamic (train) reaches 63.93% versus 29.50% for the no-procedure baseline, and on DigiData-MultiApp, PAMAR-MR-Dynamic achieves 15.0% where the baseline scores 0.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of fine-tuned multimodal mobile agents (DigiData, Android in the Wild) and hierarchical LLM planning, but departs from both in a key way: rather than treating high-level plans as fixed execution scaffolds or invoking external search unconditionally, PAMAR introduces 'procedural sufficiency' as an explicit meta-reasoning signal that gates retrieval. Prior hierarchical agents (Plan-and-Act, ADAPT) replan on execution failure, while search-augmented agents (AppAgent, O²-Searcher) retrieve as a fixed pipeline component; PAMAR instead trains the agent to assess whether its current procedure explains ongoing execution and only then triggers acquisition. The grounded executor builds directly on the PLM-8B + SFT recipe from DigiData, with the novelty lying in procedure-augmented training data and the meta-reasoning loop rather than in the base model or training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The selective-retrieval mechanism addresses a practical deployment concern: always-search policies introduce latency and can override correct internal knowledge with noisy external content, while no-search policies fail on distribution shifts. The introduced benchmarks (DigiData-AppUpdate, DigiData-MultiApp) target realistic failure modes—version drift and cross-app composition—that prior Android benchmarks underrepresented, though their small sizes (61 and 20 tasks) limit statistical confidence. Key open questions include whether the training-based meta-reasoner's bias toward conservative retrieval (79.86% search rate) can be corrected, whether procedural sufficiency signals transfer to truly novel tasks with no prior procedure, and whether the 15% multi-app success rate can be meaningfully improved. The framework's reliance on GPT-4o as the procedural generator also raises cost and latency questions for real-time deployment.
