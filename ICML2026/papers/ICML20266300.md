## Problem

Prior perturbation-based studies of LLMs focus on high-level semantic perturbations or treat character-level perturbations merely as adversarial attack tools, leaving a gap in understanding how LLMs actually process and recover from character-level noise at the tokenization and internal-representation level. This paper asks: how well do contemporary LLMs handle different types of character-level perturbations, and what mechanisms underlie their surprising robustness when text becomes nearly unreadable to humans?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266300/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are GSM8K math word problems or SQuAD reading-comprehension passages with controlled character-level perturbations applied: Typo-Method (sequential deletion, substitution, swapping, insertion on a configurable word ratio of 30–100%), Shuffle-Method (random character permutation within words at the same ratio levels), and UCC-Inj (injection of 1–3 invisible Unicode Variation Selectors after every visible character). Outputs include the model's final answer (integer for GSM8K, span for SQuAD), accuracy scores, a three-way behavioral categorization (A: correct explicit rewrite, B: erroneous explicit rewrite, C: no explicit rewrite), per-layer linear-probing accuracy on a synthetic sentence-completion task, and attention-head heatmaps over ASCII vs. noise tokens.

## Method

The paper introduces three character-level perturbation probes of increasing severity and systematically evaluates 15+ model variants (DeepSeek, Qwen3, Llama families, 0.6B–235B) on GSM8K and SQuAD. The mechanistic analysis decomposes robustness into two components: (i) robustness to abnormal tokenization, tested by forcing pure character-level tokenization and randomized mid-word segmentation on clean text, and (ii) denoising ability, probed by categorizing CoT outputs into explicit-rewrite vs. implicit-handling types and by training per-layer linear probes on a synthetic 20-word sentence dataset to track when hidden states recover target-word semantics under perturbation. Attention visualizations reveal specific heads that selectively attend to clean characters over injected noise, while cosine-similarity tracking of hidden states across layers shows that denoising is implicit and degrades with longer noisy prefixes rather than following a stable rule-based procedure. The key finding is a dual-layer resilience mechanism: a high-level explicit CoT rewriting step that correlates with and improves accuracy when prompted, and a low-level implicit denoising process operating through attention and positional-embedding continuity that recovers word meaning directly from corrupted token sequences without rule-based reconstruction.

## Results

On GSM8K, under the most severe UCC-Inj perturbation (3 invisible Unicode characters per visible character), DeepSeek-Reasoner retains 70.6% accuracy (vs. 94.4% baseline), while Llama-3.1-70B collapses to 1.1%. Under 100-Shuffle (all words character-shuffled), DeepSeek-Reasoner maintains 90.4% accuracy, and Qwen3-235B(A22B) Thinking retains 58.0%. Under character-level tokenization of clean text, Qwen3-30B(A3B) achieves 91.4% accuracy versus 94.1% under normal tokenization, demonstrating that LLMs tolerate fragmented tokenization with less than 3% degradation. On SQuAD, Qwen3-30B(A3B) drops from 63.2% EM (baseline) to 14.0% EM under 100-Shuffle but still retains 27.2% F1, confirming robustness beyond mathematical reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three model families—DeepSeek, Qwen3, and Llama—under identical character-level perturbations. DeepSeek models are the most robust overall: DeepSeek-Reasoner retains 90.4% under 100-Shuffle and 70.6% under 3-Inj, far exceeding Qwen3-235B(A22B) Thinking (58.0% and 36.6%) and Llama-3.1-70B (35.8% and 1.1%). Within the Qwen3 family, larger models are consistently more robust; Qwen3-30B(A3B) achieves 43.4% under 1-Inj while the smaller Qwen3-14B drops to 11.7%, and Qwen3-32B (53.6%) outperforms Qwen3-30B(A3B) (43.4%) under the same condition. Llama models are the least robust to UCC-Inj specifically: Llama-3.1-70B (10.0% under 1-Inj) is dramatically outperformed by the smaller Qwen3-30B(A3B) (43.4%), a 4.3× gap. For Shuffle-Method, the gap narrows considerably—DeepSeek-Reasoner (90.4%) and Qwen3-235B(A22B) (71.6%) both remain strong, while Llama-3.1-70B (35.8%) still lags. The paper also shows that explicit denoising instructions or 1-shot examples substantially improve UCC-Inj performance (e.g., Qwen3-30B(A3B) Thinking rises from 57.5% to 78.8% under 1-Inj with a 1-shot example), indicating that the robustness is partly elicitable through prompting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of character-level adversarial perturbation studies (Rocamora et al., 2024; Yi et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2023; Tao et al., 2024), which primarily proposed attack strategies and reported performance drops for specific application scenarios. It departs from that lineage by treating perturbations as controlled probes for mechanism analysis rather than as attacks to be optimized, and by systematically separating two distinct capabilities: robustness to abnormal tokenization (building on Zheng et al., 2026) and the ability to denoise character-level noise. The Typo-Method operations are directly inherited from TextBugger (Li et al., 2018), while the UCC-Inj perturbation builds on the observation by Butler (2025) that LLMs process invisible Unicode Variation Selectors while text remains visually intact to humans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a previously underexplored dual-layer resilience mechanism in LLMs: an explicit high-level CoT rewriting step that correlates with accuracy, and an implicit low-level denoising process (evidenced by layer-wise linear probing and attention visualization) that operates without rule-based reconstruction. This has practical implications for safety-critical filtering, OCR error correction, anti-cheating via invisible noise injection, and low-resource language processing. Key open questions remain: whether the implicit denoising mechanism generalizes to long-context or multimodal settings, how it emerges during training (instruction-tuning vs. pretraining), and whether the robustness to shuffle is fundamentally tied to positional encoding properties like RoPE.
