## Problem

Latent Chain-of-Thought internalizes reasoning into continuous hidden states, but outcome supervision alone fails to induce meaningful latent dynamics due to a dual collapse: gradient attenuation along the optimization path and representational drift in the latent space. The field lacks a principled framework for understanding how different supervision signals govern the geometry and information content of the latent reasoning manifold.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (e.g., a GSM8K math word problem) and produces a final answer, with intermediate reasoning internalized as a sequence of continuous latent blocks (each block containing g vectors of dimension d=768 for GPT-2) that replace explicit reasoning steps. During training, a progressive schedule gradually converts explicit reasoning steps into latent tokens, while an auxiliary decoder (for generative reconstruction) or a frozen embedding table (for geometric compression) provides space-level alignment signals on the latent states.

## Method

The paper formalizes Latent CoT supervision through an information-theoretic lens, decomposing process supervision into trajectory supervision (dense stepwise signals that maximize local mutual information I(L≤k; S_{k+1}) by training latent states to predict future reasoning suffixes) and space supervision (semantic anchoring via either geometric compression with MSE to step-embedding centroids or generative reconstruction with a trainable decoder). The key empirical finding is that geometric compression acts as a destructive prior that collapses the reasoning manifold, while generative reconstruction serves as a flexible semantic tether preserving intrinsic dimensionality. To quantify these effects, the authors introduce the Unified Latent Probe (ULP), a variational reconstruction-based diagnostic that trains a shared GPT-2 conditional decoder on frozen latent states from all models to recover explicit reasoning steps, providing a principled mutual information estimate. This reveals a strict information-performance binding: reasoning accuracy is upper-bounded by the reconstructible information in the latent chain, and autoregressive generation inherently suffers spatiotemporal information decay that generative reconstruction periodically calibrates.

## Results

On GSM8K-Aug with GPT-2 (124M), the best method PS-GR (g=2) achieves 39.1% max accuracy, approaching the Explicit CoT upper bound of 43.1%, while the prior-work baseline PS-LATENT (Hao et al.) reaches 31.2% and all outcome-supervised variants plateau at ≤18.7%. On ProntoQA, PS-LATENT substantially outperforms both OS-LATENT (78.60%) and Explicit CoT (80.80%), reaching 97.40%. A scale-up to LLaMA-3.2-1B-Instruct confirms the optimization barrier: OS-LATENT (33.81%) barely exceeds OS-No-COT (32.98%) while Explicit CoT reaches 60.20%. The Unified Latent Probe (ULP) reveals a strict information–performance binding: PS-GR achieves the lowest reconstruction loss (0.375 with a 2L-4H Transformer probe), while OS-GC is worst (1.049).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is against PS-LATENT from Hao et al. (2025), the progressive latent CoT method that introduced trajectory supervision. PS-GR (g=2) outperforms PS-LATENT by 7.9 percentage points (39.1% vs. 31.2%) on GSM8K-Aug, demonstrating that adding generative reconstruction as space supervision closes most of the gap to the Explicit CoT upper bound (43.1%). All outcome-supervised baselines (OS-LATENT, OS-GC, OS-GR) fail to exceed the trivial OS-No-COT baseline (18.7%), confirming the dual collapse of gradient attenuation and manifold drift. Geometric compression (PS-GC) actively degrades performance relative to PS-LATENT (23.2% vs. 31.2%), validating the paper's claim that rigid alignment acts as a destructive prior. On ProntoQA, PS-LATENT (97.40%) surpasses both OS-LATENT (78.60%) and Explicit CoT (80.80%), suggesting that latent reasoning can exceed explicit CoT when the task structure is well-suited to continuous representation. The scale-up to LLaMA-3.2-1B reproduces the same qualitative pattern, ruling out backbone capacity as the explanation for the OS failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Hao et al. (2025), which introduced the progressive latent CoT training schedule and the PS-LATENT baseline, and extends it by providing an information-theoretic decomposition of process supervision into trajectory supervision (sequential information injection) and space supervision (semantic anchoring). It positions existing latent CoT regularization methods along a new axis—geometric compression (analogous to JEPA-style latent prediction) versus generative reconstruction (analogous to MAE-style decoding)—and argues that the distinction between these two paradigms is not merely architectural but information-theoretic. The Unified Latent Probe (ULP) is a new diagnostic instrument that operationalizes mutual information estimation for frozen latent states, filling a gap left by prior work that lacked a principled verification tool for latent reasoning quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for why outcome supervision fails in latent CoT (gradient attenuation plus manifold drift) and why space supervision alone cannot rescue it, offering practitioners a clear design principle: trajectory scaffolding is a prerequisite for any alignment objective to be effective. The finding that rigid geometric compression acts as a 'destructive prior' that collapses the reasoning manifold, while generative reconstruction preserves intrinsic dimensionality, has direct implications for how future latent reasoning systems should be regularized. Key limitations include the small model scale (GPT-2 primarily, with one LLaMA-3.2-1B check), two datasets, and reliance on explicit process annotations that restrict scalability; open questions include whether the information-performance binding holds at frontier model scale and whether latent CoT can ultimately surpass explicit CoT rather than merely approaching it.
