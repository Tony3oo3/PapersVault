## Problem

Despite transformers scaling effectively in supervised learning, naively scaling transformer-based Q-functions in RL leads to training instability and worse performance rather than improvement. The paper identifies the root cause: as model capacity increases, attention scores collapse onto a handful of tokens, producing non-smooth value landscapes and preventing larger models from learning useful attention patterns during value bootstrapping.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260941/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Q-function takes a state-action pair (s ∈ R^{n_s}, a ∈ R^{n_a}) where each scalar dimension is treated as an individual token, projected into a shared hidden space, and concatenated with a prepended [VALUE] token to form a sequence of length 1 + n_s + n_a. The [VALUE] token's final representation is passed through an ensemble of K lightweight MLP heads to produce the Q-value estimate. For policy extraction, a one-step flow policy outputs continuous actions, behavior-constrained via distillation toward a BC flow policy.

## Method

TQL prevents attention entropy collapse by introducing learnable per-layer temperature parameters α^ℓ (with a separate α_{[VALUE]}^ℓ for the [VALUE] token) that are optimized to steer the entropy of each layer's attention distribution toward a target value H̄. The critic loss combines the standard TD bootstrapping loss with an attention entropy loss (maximizing entropy weighted by α) and a temperature loss (penalizing deviation from target entropy), so the network is encouraged to distribute attention across all tokens while the temperature adaptively regulates the level. Learnable modality embeddings are added to state and action tokens to help larger models differentiate information sources. Policy extraction uses a one-step flow policy that maximizes Q-values while being distillation-constrained toward a BC flow policy, making the method orthogonal to the specific policy parameterization.

## Results

On the OGBench benchmark (25 tasks across 5 domains), TQL achieves the best average success rate of 40±7%, outperforming the next-best method floq (34±7%) and FQL (27±4%). TQL wins on 4 out of 5 domains, with particularly large margins on cube-double (67% vs. 29% for FQL) and scene (61% vs. 59% for floq). In scaling experiments from 0.4M to 26M parameters, TQL achieves a 43% relative performance improvement, while all prior methods (FQL, floq, PAC, and a vanilla transformer baseline) suffer an average 10.6% performance degradation at the largest scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TQL outperforms all 8 baselines on the overall 25-task average (40% vs. 34% for floq, 27% for FQL, 24% for PAC, 18% for ReBRAC and IFQL, 17% for FBRAC, 10% for IQL, and 2% for Q-Transformer). The closest competitor is floq (34±7%), which actually edges out TQL on the puzzle-3x3 domain (35% vs. 32%), but TQL dominates on the remaining four domains. Among transformer-based value function baselines, Q-Transformer (2%) and PAC (24%) both suffer severe performance degradation, with Q-T being particularly poor due to attention collapse even without TQL's entropy guidance. In the scaling analysis (Figure 4), TQL is the only method that improves with increased capacity: while FQL, floq, PAC, and the vanilla transformer baseline all lose 10.6% on average going from 0.4M to 26M parameters, TQL gains 43%, confirming that attention entropy collapse is the primary bottleneck for all prior approaches at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

TQL builds directly on FQL's flow-based policy extraction but introduces a novel transformer value function design with attention entropy control as its core contribution. It departs from prior RL scaling approaches (periodic resets, MoE architectures, normalization tricks) by requiring no architectural modifications, and it adapts the NLP insight that attention entropy collapse destabilizes large transformers (Zhai et al., 2023) to the specific setting of bootstrapped value function learning. Unlike Q-Transformer and PAC, which use transformers for value functions but do not diagnose or address the scaling failure mode, TQL identifies attention collapse as the critical bottleneck and provides a minimal, layer-wise entropy guidance mechanism inspired by maximum entropy RL (SAC).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic (attention entropy collapse) and a simple, architecture-agnostic fix that enables transformer value functions to scale from 0.4M to 26M parameters with a 43% performance gain, whereas all prior methods degrade. This is practically important because it allows the use of pretrained transformer backbones without costly retraining or structural changes. Limitations include evaluation only on OGBench continuous control tasks, a single target-entropy hyperparameter that requires per-environment tuning, and a modest scaling range (up to 26M). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to online RL, scales beyond 26M parameters, or applies to other generative value function architectures like flow-matching critics.
