## Problem

Existing video benchmarks have been saturated by state-of-the-art models (85%+ on VideoMME and Neptune), and even recent scientific video benchmarks (MMVU, Video-MMMU, Video-MMLU) remain at college-level, often permitting answers through memorized textbook knowledge or without watching the video at all. This paper addresses the absence of a benchmark that demands research-level scientific video reasoning, where each question requires the synergistic integration of precise spatiotemporal visual perception, expert domain knowledge, and multi-step logical reasoning grounded in real, cutting-edge experimental settings.

## Input / Output

Each question is paired with a peer-reviewed JoVE experimental video (average 484 seconds) and includes explicit timestamp references (e.g., 'shown at 02:31') that anchor the question to specific visual moments, making the video indispensable for answering. The 1,000 multiple-choice questions each carry 10 options (A–J) and are partitioned into three reasoning types—conceptual (370), hypothetical (385), and quantitative (245)—spanning four disciplines and over 25 subjects. For quantitative questions, all numeric values must be extracted from the video itself, and evaluation reports accuracy broken down by both question type and discipline.

## Method

The benchmark is constructed through a semi-automatic multi-agent annotation pipeline that jointly leverages video, aligned audio transcripts, and paired peer-reviewed papers. Four human experts first author exemplar QA pairs, which are expanded via GPT-4o into 12 high-quality examples per reasoning type to prompt a multi-agent system in which distinct Gemini 2.5 Pro agents assume specialized roles: QA Generator produces candidate questions, Evaluator answers them with rationale, Visual Comparer verifies grounding and assigns timestamps, and Refiner strengthens visual dependence and distractor quality. A GPT-4o filtering stage then runs two complementary audits—an option-only test for answer-pattern leakage and a visual-blind test to detect questions solvable without any video input—flagging items that pass either check for revision or removal. Human verifiers perform final review, confirming timestamps, ensuring answerability from visual evidence, and overlaying transcript-only values onto relevant frames when needed; over 3,000 preliminary questions were eliminated in this process, yielding the final 1,000 verified items.

## Results

On SCIVIDEOBENCH (1,000 research-level scientific video QA items), the strongest proprietary model, Gemini-2.5-Pro, achieves only 64.30% overall accuracy, while the best open-source model (InternVL-3-38B) reaches 38.30% and graduate-student humans score just 17.40%. Quantitative reasoning is the hardest sub-task: even Gemini-2.5-Pro drops to 50.61%, and GPT-4o falls to 11.84% (near the 10% random-guess floor). Chain-of-thought prompting yields the largest gains for proprietary models (Gemini-1.5-Pro: +21.10% overall, +25.31% on Quantitative) but often degrades conceptual/hypothetical scores for open-source models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCIVIDEOBENCH is a benchmark, so the 'comparisons' are among the 38 evaluated LMMs. Proprietary models dominate: Gemini-2.5-Pro (64.30%) leads overall, followed by GPT-5 (55.70%) and Gemini-2.5-Flash (46.40%), while the best open-source model, InternVL-3-38B (38.30% direct / 37.90% CoT), trails the top proprietary by roughly 26 percentage points. The gap is most severe on Quantitative reasoning, where GPT-5 (55.10%) more than doubles InternVL-2-Llama3-76B (25.31%), and several small open-source models (e.g., InternVL-3-1B at 7.35%, LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B at 5.71%) fall below the 10% random-guess baseline. Vision-blind baselines confirm the benchmark's visual grounding: GPT-4o without video scores only 15.80%, and Qwen2.5-72B (text-only) reaches 18.90%, far below their multimodal counterparts. Chain-of-thought prompting narrows but does not close the proprietary–open-source gap: Gemini-1.5-Pro jumps from 27.50% to 48.60% (+21.10%), surpassing Gemini-2.5-Flash, while open-source models like Qwen2.5-VL-32B see only a 0.7% overall drop but a +57% relative gain on Quantitative. Notably, even the strongest model (Gemini-2.5-Pro) remains well below the 85%+ saturation seen on general video benchmarks like VideoMME, confirming SCIVIDEOBENCH's substantially higher difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of scientific video benchmarks (MMVU, Video-MMMU, Video-MMLU) by pushing from college-level textbook reasoning to research-level reasoning grounded in real peer-reviewed experimental videos from JoVE. It departs from general-domain video QA benchmarks (Video-MME, MVBench, LongVideoBench) that have become saturated at 85%+ accuracy, arguing that those benchmarks test perception/recognition rather than genuine multimodal reasoning. The annotation methodology draws on multi-agent LLM pipelines (QA Generator, Evaluator, Visual Comparer, Refiner) with human-in-the-loop verification, a design pattern increasingly common in data construction but here applied specifically to enforce visual grounding and eliminate text-only solvability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides a much-needed stress test for LMMs in scientific video understanding, with even the strongest model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) reaching only 64.30% and graduate-level domain experts scoring just 17.40%, confirming genuine research-level difficulty. The finding that 70.7% of quantitative reasoning errors stem from visual perception failures rather than arithmetic mistakes offers a concrete diagnostic for where to invest improvement effort. Open questions include whether the 1,000-question scale and 4-discipline coverage are sufficient for robust cross-domain generalization, whether the multiple-choice format adequately captures open-ended scientific reasoning, and whether the benchmark will remain discriminative as models improve rapidly.
