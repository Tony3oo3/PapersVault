## Problem

Generating high-fidelity hand motions in human image animation remains a persistent challenge due to the hands' high degrees of freedom and motion complexity. While Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) offers a principled path for preference alignment, constructing strict good-bad preference pairs for dynamic hand regions is prohibitively expensive and often impractical, since frame-wise hand inconsistencies make valid pairings rare (only ~7.5% of curated good samples could be paired). The paper asks whether effective preference alignment for hand regions is possible without requiring paired negative examples.

## Input / Output

The method takes a static human reference image and a target pose sequence (extracted via DWPose) as conditioning signals, and produces an 81-frame animated video at 832×480 resolution. As a post-training framework, it operates on the VACE-14B DiT-based flow matching model using LoRA (rank 128 on QKV projections), requiring only 93 self-generated high-quality samples—no negative samples—curated from 6,000 candidate videos generated across 1,500 pose-image pairs. The hand mask for local optimization is derived directly from hand keypoint coordinates within the pose sequence, requiring no additional segmentation model.

## Method

IPA eliminates the need for bad samples by formulating the alignment objective as maximizing the KL-divergence gap Δ(p_ref, p_θ) = D_KL(q‖p_ref) − D_KL(q‖p_θ) between the preference distribution and the model, regularized by a coefficient β to prevent overfitting. The paper proves this objective is equivalent to maximizing an implicit reward function derived from the KL-constrained RLHF formulation, with the loss expressed as −log σ(βΔ). In the flow matching setting, Δ is made tractable via velocity-field MSE differences: the loss becomes −log σ(β/2 · (1−t)² · (‖v−v_ref‖² − ‖v−v_θ‖²)), where v is the target velocity. A Hand-Aware Local Optimization (HALO) mechanism injects a spatial weight matrix W = 1 + λ·M (M being the binary hand mask) into both velocity terms, explicitly steering the alignment toward hand regions. The entire framework is trained for 1,000 steps with β=600 and λ=10 on 8 H20 GPUs.

## Results

On the standard TikTok benchmark, IPA achieves FID-VID of 5.9, FVD of 255, SSIM of 0.841, and PSNR of 23.8, outperforming the strongest baseline Wan-Animate (FID-VID 8.6, FVD 316, SSIM 0.799, PSNR 20.5) by a wide margin across all four metrics. On the authors' more challenging hand-dynamics benchmark, the advantage is even more pronounced: FID-VID drops to 6.3 (vs. 10.6 for the best baseline UniAnimate-DiT) and PSNR rises to 21.5 (vs. 17.4). On hand-region-specific metrics, IPA reaches SSIM-Hand 0.606 and PSNR-Hand 18.9, surpassing the next-best Wan-Animate (0.544 / 14.1) by 11% and 34% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPA wins on every metric against all ten baselines on both the TikTok and the authors' hand-dynamics benchmarks. Against the strongest video-generation baseline Wan-Animate, IPA reduces FID-VID from 8.6 to 5.9 (−31%), FVD from 316 to 255 (−19%), and lifts PSNR from 20.5 to 23.8 (+16%). The gap widens substantially on the more challenging hand-dynamics benchmark, where the best competitor UniAnimate-DiT scores FID-VID 10.6 versus IPA's 6.3, and PSNR 17.4 versus 21.5. Among image-based methods (MagicAnimate, MagicPose, Moore-AnimateAnyone, MuseV), all are dominated by a large margin (FID-VID ≥ 12.4, FVD ≥ 728), reflecting their architectural limitations in temporal consistency. On hand-region-specific metrics (SSIM-Hand, PSNR-Hand), IPA leads Wan-Animate by 0.062 SSIM and 4.8 dB PSNR, and VACE by 0.105 SSIM and 3.6 dB PSNR. The KTO baseline (which still requires unpaired bad samples) also trails IPA by 4.6 FID-VID and 2.4 dB PSNR on the hand-dynamics benchmark, confirming that eliminating bad samples entirely does not sacrifice alignment quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the flow-matching generative paradigm (Liu et al., 2023) and the DiT-based VACE architecture (Jiang et al., 2025) as its base model, then departs from standard Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023) by eliminating the requirement for paired preference data. The theoretical derivation draws on the trajectory-based KL approximation from Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024) and the flow-matching-specific KL decomposition from Flow-DPO (Liu et al., 2025) and Kingma & Gao (2023), but reframes the objective as minimizing a KL-divergence gap between the preference distribution and the model under a prior constraint, rather than borrowing the Bradley-Terry formulation. The Hand-Aware Local Optimization mechanism is a new spatial weighting strategy not present in prior preference-alignment work for video generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that only 7.5% of curated good samples could be paired with valid bad counterparts quantifies a real bottleneck for DPO in dynamic, fine-grained generation tasks, making the data-efficiency argument concrete. The SFT ablation (where naive fine-tuning on the same good samples degrades SSIM from 0.777 to 0.715) provides strong evidence that the KL-constrained objective is not merely a convenience but essential to prevent mode collapse. Open questions include whether IPA generalizes beyond hand regions to other fine-grained anatomical structures, whether the structural equivalence to a 'positive-only DPO' limits its theoretical novelty, and whether the approach scales to longer videos or higher resolutions where hand artifacts compound over time.
