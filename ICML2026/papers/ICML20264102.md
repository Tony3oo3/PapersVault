## Problem

Existing medical vision-language pre-training for 3D CT suffers from two coupled limitations: visual backbones are either computationally prohibitive (ViT volumetric patchification) or architecturally incompatible with pre-trained cross-modal priors (pure CNN), and global image-report contrastive alignment conflates distinct pathologies within the same organ (e.g., atelectasis, emphysema, and lung nodules all mapped to a generic 'abnormal' label), obscuring disease-specific image-language associations.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D chest CT volumes resampled to 1 mm × 1 mm × 5 mm isotropic resolution with HU values clipped to [−1150, 350] and normalized to [0,1], randomly cropped to 112 × 256 × 384 patches during training, paired with free-text radiology reports parsed by an LLM into per-organ snippets. At inference it produces per-organ, per-disease diagnostic scores via cosine similarity between organ-level visual embeddings and aggregated disease prototypes; an auxiliary U-Net decoder simultaneously outputs soft organ segmentation masks at native CT resolution, and a BERT-based text decoder can be attached for radiology report generation.

## Method

CT-DiagVLM co-designs three components. First, a CNN-ViT hybrid encoder replaces ViT's native patch embedding with a 3D ResNet-18 backbone whose four-stage features are projected, trilinearly aligned, concatenated, and fused via a final 3D convolution (Multi-Scale Fusion Patch Embedding), then fed into a ViT initialized from SigLIP-2 weights—preserving global self-attention and cross-modal priors while injecting multi-scale local anatomy. Second, a disease-level contrastive learning mechanism introduces learnable condition query tokens per organ that extract disease-specific textual embeddings from full organ descriptions via cross-attention; a batch-wise InfoNCE-style loss then pulls each organ's visual embedding toward all same-disease textual embeddings across the batch while pushing it away from other conditions, disentangling co-occurring pathologies without explicit report decomposition. Third, a diagnosis-aware prompt strategy constructs inference-time textual prototypes by aggregating query-guided condition embeddings from ≥500 real clinical reports per condition-organ pair, replacing handcrafted binary templates and bridging the pre-training–inference gap. Training jointly optimizes a Dice loss for organ segmentation, an organ-level InfoNCE alignment loss, and the disease-level contrastive loss (λ₁ = λ₂ = 0.5), with disease labels sourced from ground-truth annotations or Qwen3-Max pseudo-labels.

## Results

On the CT-RATE internal test set, CT-DiagVLM achieves 84.4% AUC, 78.1% ACC, and 80.3% F1 for zero-shot diagnosis across 16 diseases, outperforming the strongest prior method (ViSD-Boost, 79.0% AUC) by 5.4 points. On the external Rad-ChestCT test set, it reaches 75.4% AUC (+5.4 over ViSD-Boost) and 73.4% F1. Under a more challenging 60-disease setting with LLM-generated pseudo-labels, the model attains 85.6% AUC, a +9.8-point gain over the base model. For radiology report generation, CT-DiagVLM achieves 45.5% F1 on CT-RATE and 37.3% F1 on Rad-ChestCT, roughly doubling the best prior method (CT-CHAT, 20.2% F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CT-DiagVLM outperforms all nine baselines on both CT-RATE and Rad-ChestCT across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor ViSD-Boost (Cao et al., 2025), it gains +5.4% AUC on CT-RATE (84.4 vs. 79.0) and +6.0% on Rad-ChestCT (75.4 vs. 69.4). Over fVLM (Shui et al., 2025), the primary organ-level VLP baseline, the improvement is +6.6% AUC internally and +7.4% externally. The gap widens substantially against earlier global-alignment methods: CT-CLIP (Hamamci et al., 2024) trails by 11.1% AUC, Merlin (Blankemeier et al., 2026) by 11.6%, and BIUD (Cao et al., 2024) by 13.1% on CT-RATE. On the 60-disease pseudo-label benchmark, CT-DiagVLM's 85.6% AUC exceeds the base model (75.8%) by 9.8 points, with no direct external comparison reported. For report generation, CT-DiagVLM's 45.5% F1 on CT-RATE more than doubles CT-CHAT (Hamamci et al., 2024a) at 20.2% and RadFM (Wu et al., 2025) at 6.1%, while also surpassing CT2Rep (Hamamci et al., 2024b) at 18.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the medical VLP lineage from CT-CLIP and Merlin (global image-report contrastive learning) through fVLM's organ-level alignment to a finer disease-level granularity, using learnable condition query tokens to disentangle co-occurring pathologies within the same organ. Architecturally, it departs from pure ViT or pure CNN backbones by replacing ViT's patch embedding with a 3D ResNet-18 multi-scale fusion module, preserving compatibility with pre-trained SigLIP-2 cross-modal priors while capturing local anatomical detail. The diagnosis-aware prompt strategy builds on the observation that handcrafted binary templates create a pre-training–inference gap, instead aggregating real clinical phrases into disease prototypes—a practical extension of prototype-based zero-shot classification to the radiology domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The disease-level contrastive learning mechanism addresses a concrete and previously unresolved limitation (semantic conflation of distinct pathologies within one organ) and yields consistent gains across internal, external, and scaled (60-disease) benchmarks, suggesting the approach generalizes beyond a single dataset. The honest reporting of a sensitivity–specificity trade-off under the 60-disease pseudo-label setting, along with the demonstration that the sensitivity loss is recoverable via template prompting without retraining, provides useful calibration guidance for clinical deployment. Open questions include whether the framework extends to other body regions or imaging modalities (MRI, ultrasound), whether the reliance on TotalSegmentator and LLM-generated pseudo-labels introduces systematic biases in under-resourced settings, and whether the disease-level alignment scales further as the condition taxonomy grows beyond 60 classes.
