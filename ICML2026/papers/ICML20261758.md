## Problem

Autoregressive video world models suffer from compounding error accumulation during long-horizon generation due to exposure bias: models trained on ground-truth frames must condition on their own imperfect predictions at inference. Existing mitigations—Teacher Forcing, Diffusion Forcing, and Self-Forcing—either create train-inference mismatch, fail to capture genuine rollout error distributions, or rely on expensive teacher distillation with sequence-level objectives that do not explicitly bound error propagation beyond the training horizon.

## Input / Output

LIVE takes p prompt video frames (initially p=T, progressively reduced to p_min) along with per-frame camera poses or 25-dimensional action vectors as conditioning, and autoregressively generates future frames using a 32-frame sliding context window with causal attention. The model operates at 256×256 (RealEstate10K), 352×640 (UE Engine), or 224×384 (Minecraft) resolution with a 16× spatial VAE downsampling, and is evaluated on rollouts of 64 to 400+ frames—far exceeding the 32-frame training window—using 18-step ODE sampling at inference.

## Method

LIVE enforces bounded error accumulation through a cycle-consistency objective that eliminates the need for teacher-based distillation. During training, the model first performs a forward rollout from p ground-truth prompt frames to generate T−p frames (gradients disabled, using the same causal attention mask as inference), then reverses the rollout temporally along with camera/action conditions, injects independent random noise per frame, and trains the model to recover the original p GT prompt frames from this noisy reversed context via standard frame-level diffusion loss. This creates an implicit error bound: the model must keep forward distortion within a recoverable range, preventing the monotonic quality degradation that plagues autoregressive inference. A progressive training curriculum gradually decreases p from T to p_min, starting with easy recovery from mostly-GT context and increasing error tolerance as more rollout frames enter the context. The framework unifies Teacher Forcing (p=T, clean context), Diffusion Forcing (p=T, noisy GT context), and LIVE (p<T, imperfect rollout context) under a single objective parameterized by the GT-to-rollout ratio.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, LIVE achieves PSNR 18.11 / LPIPS 0.2215 / SSIM 0.5810 at 0–64 frames and maintains PSNR 13.89 / LPIPS 0.4682 / SSIM 0.4400 at ≥256 frames, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline NFD-DF (PSNR 11.51 / LPIPS 0.5506 / SSIM 0.4397) at the longest horizon. On UE Engine Videos, LIVE reaches PSNR 12.96 at ≥400 frames versus 11.02 for NFD-DF. On Minecraft, LIVE achieves PSNR 14.02 / LPIPS 0.4299 at 0–200 frames, surpassing NFD-DF (PSNR 12.34 / LPIPS 0.5091) by a wide margin. FID curves confirm LIVE maintains stable quality (~FID 10) across 32–200 frames while all baselines degrade sharply beyond 64 frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIVE outperforms all four baselines (CameraCtrl, DFoT, Geometry Forcing, and NFD-TF/DF) across every metric and every rollout length on all three benchmarks. The gap widens dramatically with sequence length: at ≥256 frames on RealEstate10K, LIVE achieves PSNR 13.89 versus 11.51 for NFD-DF (the strongest baseline), a 2.4 dB improvement, while LPIPS drops from 0.5506 to 0.4682. On UE Engine at ≥400 frames, LIVE's PSNR of 12.96 exceeds NFD-DF's 11.02 by nearly 2 dB. On Minecraft at 0–200 frames, LIVE reaches PSNR 14.02 / LPIPS 0.4299 versus NFD-DF's 12.34 / 0.5091. Notably, LIVE shares the identical NFD architecture and inference pipeline with NFD-TF/DF, isolating the training strategy (cycle-consistency objective + progressive curriculum) as the sole source of improvement. The non-real-time baselines (CameraCtrl, DFoT, GF) are consistently weaker, with CameraCtrl showing the largest degradation at longer horizons (PSNR drops from 14.09 to 9.48).

## Relation to Prior Work

LIVE sits within the autoregressive video diffusion lineage (NFD, TF, DF) and directly departs from Self-Forcing (SF), which relies on sequence-level KL distillation from a pre-trained bidirectional teacher. The key conceptual departure is replacing distribution matching with a cycle-consistency objective: the model performs a forward rollout from GT frames, then reverse-generates back to the initial state, computing frame-level diffusion loss on the reconstruction. This unifies TF (p=T, perfect GT context), DF (p=T, noisy GT context), and LIVE (p<T, imperfect rollout context) under a single parameterized framework, with a progressive curriculum that gradually decreases p to build error tolerance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expensive pre-trained teacher models, LIVE makes long-horizon interactive video world modeling more accessible, particularly for domain-specific settings where training a bidirectional teacher is prohibitive. The bounded-error guarantee (stable FID across 64–200+ frames) is practically important for real-time interactive applications like gaming and robotics. Limitations include evaluation at modest resolution (256×256) and 774M parameters, no direct comparison with SF due to compute constraints, and the cycle-consistency objective's behavior under very long rollouts (>256 frames) remains less characterized. Open questions include whether the approach scales to billion-parameter models and whether the reverse-generation step can be made more efficient at inference time.
