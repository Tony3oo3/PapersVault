## Problem

Enhancing LLM reasoning via reinforcement fine-tuning is computationally expensive and produces model-specific adapters (e.g., LoRA) that cannot transfer across architectures or be reliably composed. The paper asks whether reasoning capability can be decoupled into a lightweight, architecture-agnostic module that plugs into any frozen LLM sharing a tokenizer, enabling both cross-scale transfer and multi-task composition without retraining the backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262093/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a text prompt and produces a generated response by sampling from a guided policy whose logits are the element-wise sum of the frozen backbone's logits and one or more trained reasoning modules' logits (optionally weighted). The backbone and reasoning module must share a tokenizer or use a cross-tokenizer alignment (e.g., EVA); no access to the backbone's internal layers or parameter dimensions is required. Multiple specialized modules can be combined via weighted logit summation at inference time, with weights adjustable without retraining.

## Method

UniR trains a small reasoning module π_r (e.g., 1.5B) via GRPO to maximize a verifiable trajectory-level reward r(x,y), while a larger backbone π_b (e.g., 3B) remains frozen. The key theoretical insight (Lemma A.1, Theorem 4.1) is that any trajectory-level reward can be equivalently represented as β·log π_r(y|x), which by the chain rule decomposes into a sum of token-level log-probabilities; at convergence, π_r's per-token log-probabilities recover the scaled optimal soft Q-function. At inference, the guided policy is formed by adding backbone and reasoning-module logits, yielding the closed-form solution to a KL-regularized reward maximization. This additive logit structure is not heuristic but the exact solution to multi-objective KL-regularized optimization, enabling principled composition of multiple task-specific modules via weighted summation. Training updates only π_r's parameters (no backbone gradients), reducing VRAM by ~4× versus full fine-tuning and enabling batch sizes up to 128 under 80 GB.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning (pass@1, T=0.6), UniR with a 1.5B reasoning module on a Qwen2.5-3B backbone achieves an average of 42.7 across GSM8K (84.5), MATH-500 (66.8), AIME24 (7.7), Minerva (26.3), and OlympiadBench (28.2), outperforming GRPO Full (40.3) and GRPO LoRA (38.5). With a Llama3.2-3B backbone and 1B module, UniR reaches 33.3 average versus 25.4 for GRPO Full. On machine translation (IWSLT 2017), UniR improves BLEU by +1.77 over GRPO Full for EN→DE (27.03 vs 25.26) and +1.69 for DE→EN (37.21 vs 35.52).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniR consistently outperforms GRPO Full and GRPO LoRA (Shao et al., 2024) across both Llama and Qwen backbones: with Qwen2.5-3B, UniR achieves 42.7 avg vs. 40.3 (GRPO Full) and 38.5 (GRPO LoRA); with Llama3.2-3B, the gap widens to 33.3 vs. 25.4 and 25.6 respectively. Against inference-time guidance methods (Table 11), UniR (42.7 avg) surpasses Reasoning Vectors (Zbeeb et al., 2025; 37.8) by 4.9 points and RAST (Ouyang et al., 2025; 36.3) by 6.4 points, with the largest margins on MATH-500 (66.8 vs. 55.7 and 58.1) and AIME24 (7.7 vs. 3.0 and 3.3). The 7B reference models from the Qwen2.5-Math report (Yang et al., 2024b) such as DeepSeekMath-7B-RL (avg ~45.1) remain slightly ahead, but UniR's 3B+1.5B configuration (42.7) approaches their performance while training only 1.5B parameters. UniR also demonstrates superior robustness under stochastic decoding: GRPO Full drops 4.5 points (Qwen) or 5.3 points (Llama) from T=0 to T=0.6, whereas UniR degrades by only 1.2 points (Qwen) or 2.7 points (Llama), indicating less mode collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniR builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its training algorithm and on GenARM (Xu et al., 2024b) for the theoretical result that any trajectory-level reward can be equivalently represented as the log-likelihood of some probability distribution. However, it departs sharply from both the standard RLVR paradigm (which updates the full backbone) and PEFT methods like LoRA (which are architecturally locked to a specific model) by training a fully decoupled, architecture-agnostic reasoning module whose only interface to the backbone is additive logit combination at inference time. This positions UniR between inference-time guidance methods (RAST, Reasoning Vectors) and full fine-tuning, but with a principled multi-objective optimization justification for logit summation rather than heuristic weight arithmetic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, composable design offers a practical path to enhancing frozen LLMs without retraining, with the added benefit that multiple task-specific modules can be mixed at inference via weighted logit addition. Key limitations include performance being bounded by the frozen backbone's latent capabilities, tokenizer mismatch across model families still degrading results, and potential negative interference when combining modules with conflicting reward signals. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large backbones (70B+), how to handle non-verifiable or subjective rewards, and whether the logit-addition composition remains stable as the number of combined modules grows.
