## Problem

Post-hoc vision-language alignment methods can connect independently pretrained image and text encoders with lightweight mappings, but they still require substantial paired image-text data to learn those mappings. Meanwhile, the classification head weights from large-scale supervised pretraining (e.g., ImageNet-21K) are typically discarded after training despite encoding rich semantic structure. The paper asks whether these discarded weights can serve as semantic prototypes that enable alignment without any paired data and that are compatible with real image-text pairs for augmentation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen image encoder (e.g., BEiT-B/16, ConvNeXt-B, TinyViT-21M) and a frozen text encoder (e.g., CLIP ViT-B/32, RoBERTa, MPNet, MiniLM), together with the 21,841 row vectors of the ImageNet-21K classification head matrix W (each of dimension d, e.g., 768) paired with their class-name text embeddings. In the augmented regime, a small set of real image-text representation pairs (as few as 817, one per downstream class) is added. The aligned model then takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input and produces a class label (zero/few-shot classification) or a retrieved text/image (cross-modal retrieval on Flickr30K or MS-COCO).

## Method

The core insight is that each row w_i of the supervised classification head W is a high-quality semantic prototype of its class, validated by near-linear-baseline cosine-classifier accuracy across five architectures and by mutual k-NN alignment scores that consistently exceed those of averaged image embeddings. In the weight-only regime, the alignment dataset D_weights = {(w_i, f_T(t_i))}_{i=1}^{C} is used to train lightweight alignment functions g and g̃ (a two-layer MLP with GELU mapping text to image space, or linear projections via CSA / text-to-concepts) with no image-text pairs at all. In the augmented regime, D_aug = D_imgtxt ∪ D_weights combines real image-text representation pairs with the weight vectors, and the same alignment functions are trained on the union. The entire alignment step requires under two GPU-minutes on a single GPU, and the approach is architecture-agnostic: any frozen image encoder with a linear classification head can be endowed with language capabilities by recycling its discarded weights.

## Results

On zero-shot classification across nine datasets, BEiT-B/16 aligned via recycled ImageNet-21K classification head weights plus one image-caption pair per class achieves 94.11% on CIFAR-10, 74.04% on CIFAR-100, 80.98% on OxfordPets, and 73.74% on Flowers102, outperforming CLIP ViT-B/16 on CIFAR-10 (+4.4 pp), CIFAR-100 (+6.0 pp), and Flowers102 (+7.8 pp) while trailing on Food101 (64.76 vs 86.04) and RESISC45 (32.37 vs 57.14). In few-shot classification (1-shot), the method reaches 93.34% on CIFAR-10 and 75.14% on CIFAR-100, substantially exceeding NCC/KNN baselines (76.03% and 49.98% respectively). Weight-only alignment (no image-text pairs) yields non-trivial Flickr30K retrieval (i2t mAP 0.4404, t2i P@1 0.3350 with MLP), far above random (0.0026, 0.0010).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP ViT-B/16 (the primary reference VLM trained on ~400M pairs), the weight-recycling method with one image-caption pair per class wins on CIFAR-10 (94.11 vs 89.67), CIFAR-100 (74.04 vs 68.05), and Flowers102 (73.74 vs 65.96), but loses on Food101 (64.76 vs 86.04), RESISC45 (32.37 vs 57.14), EuroSAT (30.85 vs 51.93), DTD (38.94 vs 43.99), and Places365 (34.78 vs 38.96), with OxfordPets slightly behind (80.98 vs 88.31). In few-shot classification, the method significantly outperforms both NCC and KNN baselines in most settings: at 1-shot, it achieves 93.34% vs 76.03% on CIFAR-10 and 75.14% vs 49.98% on CIFAR-100 (a 17–25 pp gap), and 35.51% vs 21.96% on Places365; at 4-shot, it reaches 94.81% vs 91.01% on CIFAR-10 and 62.04% vs 61.54% on DTD, with the gap narrowing as more shots are available. The method also matches CLIP ViT-B/32 on the specialized HAM10000 dermatology benchmark (18.32% vs 18.85% balanced accuracy), confirming comparable robustness in out-of-domain settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-hoc vision-language alignment literature (CSA, Text-to-Concepts, ASIF, SAIL) by identifying a previously discarded resource—supervised classification head weights—as a source of semantic prototypes for alignment. Conceptually, it departs from prior work that requires image-text pairs for alignment by showing that the row vectors of a linear classifier, trained jointly with the image encoder during supervised pretraining, already encode class-level semantics that align with text embeddings. The theoretical grounding draws on Neural Collapse (Papyan et al., 2020), which predicts self-duality between classifier weights and class centroids, though the authors acknowledge gaps between NC's assumptions and large-scale pretraining practice. The MLP alignment backbone traces back to DeViSE (Frome et al., 2013), while the modality gap analysis parallels Liang et al. (2022) but applied to the weight-vs-image separation rather than text-vs-image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for resource-constrained settings: endowing a frozen vision backbone with language capabilities requires only ~2 GPU-minutes and zero (or very few) image-text pairs, dramatically lowering the barrier to VLM deployment. However, absolute performance still trails CLIP by 10–30 points on many benchmarks, and the method inherits biases and coverage limits from the pretrained classifier. Open questions include whether more sophisticated gap-mitigation strategies can close the remaining performance gap, whether combining classifier heads from multiple datasets (when architecturally compatible) further improves coverage, and whether the aligned model supports richer VLM capabilities like prompt tuning, VQA, or captioning. The observed modality gap between weight vectors and image representations—statistically significant yet not performance-limiting—remains an unexplained geometric phenomenon worth further investigation.
