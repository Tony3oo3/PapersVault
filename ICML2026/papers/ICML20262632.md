## Problem

Despite widespread adoption of RNA foundation models for downstream tasks, the mechanisms underlying their transferability remain unclear. It is unknown whether pretrained representations provide reusable features, whether the hierarchical feature reuse paradigm from computer vision generalizes to RNA, or whether pretraining merely offers a better optimization initialization. This paper presents the first systematic dissection of these transfer learning mechanisms across diverse RNA structure and function prediction tasks.

## Input / Output

The study evaluates nine downstream tasks spanning token-level secondary structure prediction (Archive2, bpRNA; 151–512 nt), token-level structural score imputation (SSI; 100 nt), and sequence-level function prediction (ncRNA 13-class classification at 38–1182 nt, MRL regression at 25–100 nt, and deep mutational scanning fitness prediction at fixed 282 or 425 nt lengths). All four training settings (Onehot, Frozen, RI-T, PT-FT) share identical task heads and loss functions, differing only in backbone initialization and update policy, ensuring performance differences are attributable solely to transfer learning effects. Nine encoder-only transformer models ranging from 0.5M (RNA-BERT) to 239M (mRNA-FM) parameters are evaluated, all pretrained via masked language modeling.

## Method

The paper is an empirical study that tests three hypotheses about RNA FM transfer through a controlled experimental framework. Four training settings—Onehot (linear probe on one-hot encodings), Frozen (linear probe on frozen last-layer representations), RI-T (training from random initialization), and PT-FT (full fine-tuning from pretrained weights)—are compared across nine tasks and nine models. To characterize how knowledge transfers, the authors track block-wise ℓ₂ distance between each transformer block's parameters and their initial values at every training epoch, revealing three distinct patterns: aligned feature reuse (shallow layers stable, as in structure-aligned models), feature stagnation (shallow layers stable but performance poor, as in RNA-BERT), and global adaptation (widespread parameter updates across all layers, as in weakly aligned function tasks). To test whether pretraining acts as an optimization prior, the authors visualize 2D loss landscape projections with filter-wise normalization and compute SAM-style perturbation metrics around converged solutions, showing that PT-FT consistently reaches flatter minima than RI-T when pretraining and downstream objectives are aligned and model capacity is sufficient.

## Results

Across nine downstream RNA tasks, fine-tuning from pretrained weights (PT-FT) consistently outperforms training from random initialization (RI-T) and frozen linear probing. The best models (OmniGenome-52M/186M) achieve 0.9162 ACC on Archive2 (vs. SOTA 0.9050), 0.8320 ACC on bpRNA (vs. SOTA 0.6350), 0.5711 Pearson R² on SSI (vs. SOTA 0.3720), and 0.9634 ACC on ncRNA (vs. SOTA 0.8573). However, frozen last-layer representations are highly task-dependent: they substantially outperform one-hot baselines on structure prediction (e.g., 0.7284 vs. 0.3481 on Archive2) but underperform one-hot on ncRNA classification (0.5188 vs. 0.7807), revealing a misalignment between MLM pretraining and local sequence statistics critical for certain functional tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Ufold SOTA (Fu et al., 2022), OmniGenome-186M PT-FT surpasses the reported 0.9050 ACC on Archive2 (0.9162) and dramatically exceeds the 0.6350 ACC on bpRNA (0.8320), a gap of ~20 percentage points. On SSI, OmniGenome-186M PT-FT achieves 0.5711 Pearson R², substantially outperforming the Gong et al. (2021) SOTA of 0.3720. For ncRNA classification, RNA-FM PT-FT reaches 0.9634 ACC, well above the Rossi et al. (2019) SOTA of 0.8573. The one exception is MRL, where the best PT-FT result (0.7646 R², OmniGenome-52M) falls slightly short of the Sample et al. (2019) SOTA of 0.7800. Internally, PT-FT outperforms RI-T on 8 of 9 tasks for most models, while frozen representations beat one-hot on structure tasks but lose on ncRNA (0.5188 vs. 0.7807 for OmniGenome-186M), confirming that pretraining's benefit is task-alignment-dependent rather than universal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transfer learning analysis framework established in computer vision (Neyshabur et al., Raghu et al., Matsoukas et al.) and protein language models (Li et al., 2024) to RNA foundation models, adapting the loss landscape visualization of Li et al. (2018a) and the layer-wise ℓ₂ distance analysis of Matsoukas et al. (2022) by tracking the full per-epoch learning trajectory rather than only initial-versus-final weight comparisons. It departs from the prevailing assumption—borrowed from CV and protein science—that pretrained representations provide hierarchically reusable features, instead proposing that pretraining in RNA FMs functions primarily as an optimization prior whose value is governed by task alignment and model capacity. The work also engages with the recent genomic foundation model literature (Vishniakov et al., 2024) that questioned whether pretraining consistently helps, narrowing the scope to small-to-medium encoder-only transformer RNA models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that frozen RNA FM representations can underperform a trivial one-hot baseline on function prediction tasks challenges the field's implicit assumption that MLM pretraining yields universally useful features, and provides concrete guidance that task alignment and model capacity—not scale alone—determine transfer effectiveness. Limitations include the restriction to small-to-medium (≤239M parameter) encoder-only transformers with MLM pretraining, leaving open whether larger models, decoder-only architectures, or structure-aware pretraining objectives would alter the conclusions. Open questions include whether parameter-efficient adaptation strategies (e.g., LoRA, adapter tuning) can recover the benefits of full fine-tuning without the extensive parameter overwriting observed here, and whether the optimization-prior interpretation holds under different pretraining objectives that more explicitly encode functional signals.
