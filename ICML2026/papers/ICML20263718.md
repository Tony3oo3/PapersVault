## Problem

Existing federated bilevel optimization methods assume fixed client data distributions, but deployed model decisions can reshape client behavior and induce decision-dependent distribution shifts (performativity). While performative prediction has been studied in centralized bilevel settings and federated single-level settings, no prior work combines all three: federated communication constraints, nested UL/LL objectives, and client-specific decision-dependent shifts. This paper formalizes the resulting federated bilevel performative prediction problem and provides the first convergence guarantees under coupled performativity.

## Input / Output

Each client i holds private UL and LL datasets drawn from decision-dependent distributions C_i(y*(x)) and D_i(x), respectively, where the Wasserstein-1 sensitivity of each distribution map is bounded by client-specific constants ε_{i,c} and ε_{i,d}. The server receives only aggregated stochastic gradients (not raw data) from a randomly sampled subset of clients per round, with effective weights ẋp_i = (M/|C_r|)p_i. The method outputs the FBPS point x_s (stable UL parameters), the corresponding LL response y*(x_s), and the auxiliary IHVP variable v*, with the FBPS-FBPO gap bounded by O(ε̄_c(1+ε̄_d)) where ε̄_c, ε̄_d are weighted averages of client sensitivities.

## Method

The paper introduces two algorithms. FBi-RRM is a deterministic repeated-risk-minimization scheme where each client solves a local bilevel subproblem under its own decision-dependent distributions and the server aggregates the minimizers; the induced update operator R(·) is shown to be a contraction when C_d·ε_d + C_cd·ε_c·ε_d < γ_f, yielding a unique FBPS point and linear convergence. FBi-SGD is a communication-efficient stochastic variant that introduces an auxiliary variable v to decompose the non-decomposable global inverse-Hessian-vector-product into client-local quadratic subproblems, enabling federated hypergradient estimation via linear aggregation of per-client contributions. At each round, participating clients perform K local SGD steps on (x_i, y_i, v_i) using minibatch stochastic gradients, then the server aggregates and updates global variables with diminishing O(1/r) step sizes, achieving O(1/r) mean-square convergence to the FBPS point when performative sensitivities are sufficiently small. The key theoretical contribution is a contraction condition with a bilinear cross-term structure (linear in ε_d plus bilinear in ε_c·ε_d) that explicitly captures how coupled UL/LL distribution shifts interact to determine stability in the federated setting.

## Results

The paper provides theoretical convergence guarantees rather than a single numerical benchmark table: FBi-RRM achieves linear convergence (rate O(log(1/r))) to the unique FBPS point under a contraction condition, while FBi-SGD converges at rate O(1/r) under diminishing step sizes with sufficiently small performative sensitivities. The FBPO–FBPS gap is bounded by O(ε̄_c(1+ε̄_d)), where ε̄_c and ε̄_d are weighted-average client sensitivities. Empirically, performativity-aware (PP) training consistently improves meta-test accuracy over non-performative (noPP) baselines across all three experimental settings (strategic regression, meta strategic classification, and CNN-MNIST), and FBi-SGD slightly outperforms the centralized Bi-SGD baseline in both stationarity gap and meta-train/test accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the centralized Bi-SGD (Lu, 2023), FBi-SGD is competitive and slightly better in both stationarity gap and meta-train/test accuracy on the meta strategic classification task (Fig. 2) and CNN-MNIST (Fig. 4); the paper attributes this edge to FBi-SGD performing multiple local SGD steps per communication round versus one step in the centralized baseline. Against the single-level performative RRM/GD (Perdomo et al., 2020), FBi-RRM extends the analysis to the federated bilevel setting while preserving the same O(log(1/r)) convergence rate, but with a condition that accounts for coupled UL/LL sensitivities (ε̄_c ε̄_d + ε̄_d) rather than a single ε. Against P-FedAvg (Jin et al., 2024), FBi-SGD matches the O(1/r) rate but handles the more complex bilevel structure with an auxiliary IHVP variable, at the cost of a slightly more involved stability condition (ε̄_c + ε̄_c ε̄_d + ε̄_d vs. ε̄). Across all experiments, the key empirical finding is that performativity-aware training (PP) consistently outperforms non-performative (noPP) variants in meta-test accuracy, confirming that ignoring decision-dependent distribution shift leads to a mismatched objective and degraded generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: centralized bilevel performative prediction (Lu, 2023), which handles decision-dependent distributions but assumes a single server, and federated performative prediction (Jin et al., 2024), which handles distributed clients but only single-level objectives. The federated bilevel optimization machinery (auxiliary variable v for decomposable IHVP, local SGD with server aggregation) is borrowed from the FBO literature (FedBiOAcc, SimFBO, FedMBO), while the performative stability framework and Wasserstein-sensitivity assumptions follow Perdomo et al. (2020) and Lu (2023). The key departure is making both the UL and LL distributions client-specific and decision-dependent simultaneously, which introduces coupled heterogeneity (data heterogeneity × performative-response heterogeneity) absent from either prior line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first existence/uniqueness and convergence guarantees for federated bilevel performative prediction, with explicit contraction thresholds (C_d·ε_d + C_cd·ε_c·ε_d < γ_f) that practitioners can check before deployment. This matters for federated systems where deployed models reshape user behavior (recommenders, clinical decision support, adaptive interfaces), where treating data as static can cause persistent instability. Limitations include the reliance on strong convexity for all theoretical results (the CNN experiment is purely empirical), the assumption of a central server, and the use of simplified linear performative shift models in experiments. Open questions include extending guarantees to nonconvex LL objectives, fully decentralized (serverless) bilevel performative learning, and more realistic stateful or multi-agent performative dynamics.
