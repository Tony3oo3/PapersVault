## Problem

Existing DRO methods handle either geometric perturbations (via optimal transport) or statistical contamination (via divergence constraints) in isolation, and coupling them through a single constraint conflates geometric displacement with loss-based outlierness. This 'geometric conflation' causes valid high-leverage points in heavy-tailed or anisotropic regimes to be indistinguishable from adversarial outliers, leading robust methods to discard informative tail data. The paper asks whether geometric robustification and statistical cleaning can be structurally decoupled within a unified framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes supervised data samples z=(x,y) where x is a feature vector (d-dimensional, as in the synthetic regression experiments) or a 32×32 RGB image (CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18), and y is a class label or regression target. The output is a robust model parameter θ trained to minimize a mean-variance objective that simultaneously accounts for worst-case geometric perturbations (estimated via multi-step PGD) and statistical outlier suppression (via batch variance of the robust surrogate loss). The training procedure requires two hyperparameters: the Wasserstein radius ρ controlling geometric perturbation magnitude and the divergence radius ε controlling the aggressiveness of statistical cleaning.

## Method

The paper proposes a nested bilevel DRO formulation: an outer optimistic φ-divergence minimization (statistical cleaning) wraps an inner pessimistic Wasserstein maximization (geometric smoothing), so the model first selects a statistically cleaned anchor distribution and then applies worst-case geometric perturbations around it. A tractable strong dual (Theorem 3.1) reduces the infinite-dimensional measure search to a finite-dimensional optimization over scalars (λ, η, μ), with the inner maximization handled by multi-step PGD. For the χ²-divergence instantiation, the dual simplifies to a closed-form mean-variance objective (Theorem 3.3): minimize λρ + E[ψ] − √(2ε)·Var(ψ), where ψ is the robust surrogate loss, providing a direct statistical interpretation of the induced reweighting as variance-driven outlier suppression. The practical algorithm (Algorithm 1) alternates between PGD-based inner maximization and SGD updates on the mean-variance objective, with the variance term automatically downweighting high-loss outliers without relying on their geometric position.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 under an overlap-confounded corruption protocol (hard rotations + label noise), Nested DRO achieves 78.78% best clean test accuracy, outperforming OR-WDRO (70.11%) by 8.67 points and UOT-DRO (74.10%) by 4.68 points. Both geometry-coupled baselines actually underperform contaminated ERM (75.00%), confirming the geometric conflation failure mode. On the synthetic regression task, Nested DRO consistently achieves the lowest excess risk across sample sizes (500–1000) and dimensions (10–50), with the gap widening as dimensionality increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two geometry-coupled DRO baselines: OR-WDRO (Nietert et al., 2023), which combines total variation with Wasserstein distance, and UOT-DRO (Wang et al., 2024), which uses unbalanced optimal transport. On CIFAR-10, Nested DRO (78.78%) decisively outperforms both: OR-WDRO trails by 8.67 points (70.11%) and UOT-DRO by 4.68 points (74.10%). Notably, both baselines underperform even the unregularized contaminated ERM (75.00%), indicating that their coupled geometry-statistics formulations actively harm performance in the overlap-confounded regime by over-suppressing valid high-leverage samples. On the synthetic regression task, Nested DRO also consistently achieves the lowest excess risk across all tested sample sizes and dimensions, with the advantage growing as dimensionality increases from 10 to 50.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper nests two previously separate DRO paradigms: the inner layer recovers the Wasserstein DRO dual via Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality (Sinha et al., 2017), while the outer layer draws on φ-divergence DRO and its known equivalence to variance regularization (Duchi & Namkoong, 2019). The key departure from prior compound-uncertainty methods (OR-WDRO, UOT-DRO) is the bilevel ordering: by placing the optimistic φ-divergence minimization outside the pessimistic Wasserstein maximization, the framework structurally prevents the transport cost from conflating geometric distance with statistical abnormality. The authors position this as a principled composition of geometry-agnostic contamination handling (related to Huber's ε-contamination model) with local geometric smoothing, rather than a single coupled constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of outlier suppression (loss-based reweighting) from transport-induced regularization offers a theoretically grounded alternative to ad-hoc trimming heuristics, and the variance-regularization equivalence (Theorem 3.3) gives practitioners a simple mean-variance objective to implement. However, the empirical validation is limited to CIFAR-10 with a single corruption protocol and a synthetic regression task; the small-uncertainty regime assumption (ε → 0) restricts practical applicability, and the inner PGD approximation introduces additional hyperparameters. Open questions include whether the decoupling benefit persists at ImageNet scale, whether the variance term remains stable under high-loss volatility in deep networks, and how the framework extends to sequential or multi-task settings.
