## Problem

Existing structure-aware LLMs rely on fixed-length query connectors (e.g., Q-Former) that over-compress large structures and waste capacity on small ones, while sequence-only inputs lack geometric evidence, causing structural hallucinations. No unified model handles heterogeneous all-atom modalities (molecules, proteins, nucleic acids) with a token budget that adapts to structural complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261993/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an all-atom spatial graph (per-atom element features, 3D coordinates, and spatial edge relations) alongside a free-form text instruction. The number of structural tokens injected into the LLM is not fixed but determined at inference by an instruction-conditioned mass threshold, so a small ligand may yield fewer tokens than a full-length protein. Outputs span text descriptions, open-ended QA, molecular SMILES strings (evaluated by validity and RDKit fingerprint similarity), and closed-form classification (true/false, multiple-choice).

## Method

Cuttlefish encodes the all-atom graph with an SE(3)-equivariant EGNN, then applies two novel connector components. Scaling-Aware Patching computes per-atom instruction-relevance logits, selects a variable number of anchor atoms whose cumulative softmax mass exceeds a threshold ρ, and grows each anchor into a soft spatial patch via a distance-plus-logit assignment; in-patch weighted pooling yields a variable-size set of structural query tokens. Geometry Grounding Adapter then refines these queries through L_f cross-attention fusion blocks against the full EGNN embeddings, recovering fine-grained geometric detail, and projects the resulting modality tokens into the LLM's embedding space at a designated placeholder position. Training proceeds in three stages: masked-reconstruction pretraining of the EGNN (element type, pairwise distance, direction noise), connector-only alignment tuning on the new GEO-AT all-atom instruction corpus with encoder and LLM frozen, and final end-to-end LLM adaptation at a reduced learning rate.

## Results

On the GEO-AT benchmark across four all-atom modalities, Cuttlefish+Qwen3-8B achieves an average METEOR of 0.428 and BERTScore of 0.876, substantially outperforming the strongest general LLM baseline (Mistral-3-8B-Reasoning-2512 at 0.176 METEOR / 0.744 BERTScore). On the Mol-Instructions molecule benchmark, Cuttlefish leads on all 17 tasks, e.g., 0.766 ROUGE-L on captioning vs. Mol-LLama-3.1's 0.759, and 0.509 FTS on reagent prediction vs. 0.411. On protein tasks, Cuttlefish achieves 0.273 IE F1 and 0.551 ROUGE-L on catalytic activity, surpassing the best prior baseline (Mol-Instructions at 0.224 IE F1). On RNA-QA, Cuttlefish attains 0.512 ROUGE-L (AIS) and 0.471 ROUGE-L (D&C), exceeding RNA-GPT (0.475 and 0.441 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against modality-specific molecular LLMs, Cuttlefish outperforms Mol-LLama-3.1 on every Mol-Instructions task, with the largest margins on reagent prediction FTS (0.509 vs. 0.411) and interaction extraction F1 (0.270 vs. 0.200). On protein benchmarks, Cuttlefish dominates ProtChatGPT across all five tasks (e.g., IE F1 0.273 vs. 0.055; catalytic activity ROUGE-L 0.551 vs. 0.306) and also surpasses ProtLLM and ProteinGPT. On DNA-Chat, Cuttlefish achieves the top-1 average MCC across 18 tasks, slightly exceeding ChatNT, though gains are modest due to structural repetition in DNA. On RNA-QA, Cuttlefish beats RNA-GPT on both AIS (ROUGE-L 0.512 vs. 0.475) and D&C (0.471 vs. 0.441). In the matched-budget connector comparison (Table 7), Cuttlefish's adaptive token policy consistently outperforms fixed-length Q-Former at every budget level (256–2048 tokens), with the largest advantage on proteins (0.896 vs. 0.784 at 2048 tokens), confirming that adaptivity rather than raw token count drives the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cuttlefish departs from the dominant Q-Former-style fixed-length connector paradigm (BLIP-2, Mol-Llama, 3D-MoLM, ProtChatGPT) that compresses variable-size all-atom structures into a constant token budget, and from sequence-only baselines (MolT5, ProtST, ChatNT) that omit geometric evidence entirely. It repurposes the EGNN equivariant encoder and cross-attention adapter ideas into a unified all-atom framework, introducing instruction-conditioned anchor selection with mass-based thresholding (Scaling-Aware Patching) and a retrieval-based geometry injection stage (Geometry Grounding Adapter) that together replace the rigid fixed-capacity bottleneck. The theoretical analysis (compression distortion bounds, Bayes-risk reduction under log loss) provides a formal justification absent from prior empirical-only structure-LLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cuttlefish is the first unified LLM that reasons over heterogeneous all-atom modalities (molecules, proteins, nucleic acids) with explicit geometric grounding, and the released GEO-AT dataset fills a gap in the community by providing the first all-atom instruction corpus with coordinates. The finding that structure-aware training instills lasting geometric priors even when coordinates are absent at inference (Table 8) suggests a path toward robustness in settings where 3D structures are unavailable. Open questions include whether the simplified sequence-derived nucleic-acid structures (no higher-order folding) limit generalization, whether the EGNN encoder is optimal versus more expressive equivariant architectures, and how the approach scales to very large multi-component complexes beyond the tested entity sizes.
