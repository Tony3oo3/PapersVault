## Problem

Existing position-independent KV cache reuse methods for RAG (CacheBlend, EPIC, KVShare) select tokens for partial recomputation using global attention magnitudes or positional heuristics, which saturates the limited recomputation budget with globally salient but query-irrelevant tokens. This 'crowding-out effect' displaces the tokens truly essential for answering the user query, causing accuracy drops of up to 86% on representative benchmarks and limiting real-world viability of KV cache reuse in long-context RAG.

## Input / Output

The method consumes independently precomputed chunk-wise KV caches (position-independent, so chunks can be concatenated in any order) and the user query tokens, which must be placed at the end of the prompt under causal masking so that query tokens can attend to all preceding context. It outputs a reconstructed KV cache in which only a small fraction (e.g., 20%) of context tokens have been fully recomputed to restore cross-chunk attention, along with the user query's own KV cache, ready for the subsequent autoregressive decode stage. The per-layer scoring cost scales as O(|Q_s| × s) rather than O(s²), making the lightweight first stage nearly negligible in long-context settings.

## Method

ProphetKV is a training-free, dual-stage selective recomputation framework that replaces blind global-attention recovery with query-targeted attention repair. In Stage I, a lightweight forward pass computes only the attention weights from the short user query tokens to the long context at every Transformer layer, yielding a per-layer importance score α_l(t) equal to the average query-to-token attention weight; this signal is available at all layers without a full prefill, avoiding the computational deadlock that forces prior methods to approximate deep-layer importance from shallow layers. In Stage II, the per-layer scores are fused via uniform averaging across all L layers into a single global importance function, and the top-p fraction of tokens are selected for full KV recomputation across all layers. The core insight is that the terminal user query acts as a 'prophet': its attention distribution consistently predicts which context tokens will be critical during decoding (validated across model families), and because this signal is query-contingent rather than globally salient, it naturally prioritizes task-relevant tokens over attention-sink or positionally prominent but irrelevant ones.

## Results

Under a 20% recomputation budget, ProphetKV retains 96%–101% of full-prefill accuracy across three models (Llama3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-14B, Qwen3-14B) on both RULER (8K) and LongBench. On RULER, ProphetKV achieves average scores of 84.71 (Llama3.1-8B), 88.60 (Qwen2.5-14B), and 89.89 (Qwen3-14B), compared to 88.82, 90.28, and 91.79 for full recomputation. On LongBench, ProphetKV scores 50.80, 53.43, and 59.52 versus 50.19, 54.86, and 61.06 for full recomputation. In terms of latency, ProphetKV achieves up to 5× TTFT speedup over full recomputation at 16K context (e.g., 1.13 s vs. 5.23 s on Llama3.1-8B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProphetKV outperforms all three SOTA baselines (CacheBlend, EPIC, KVShare) on every model and both benchmarks. On RULER, ProphetKV improves over the best baseline (CacheBlend) by 7.44 points (Llama3.1-8B: 84.71 vs. 77.27), 10.49 points (Qwen2.5-14B: 88.60 vs. 78.11), and 17.90 points (Qwen3-14B: 89.89 vs. 71.99). On LongBench, the gains are even larger: 10.98 points over CacheBlend on Llama3.1-8B (50.80 vs. 39.82), 18.03 points on Qwen2.5-14B (53.43 vs. 35.40), and 9.35 points on Qwen3-14B (59.52 vs. 50.17). The gap is most pronounced on retrieval-intensive tasks (RULER-MV, RULER-MQ) and multi-hop QA (LongBench WQA, HQA), where cross-chunk attention is critical. In terms of efficiency, ProphetKV matches EPIC's TTFT (the fastest baseline) while delivering substantially higher accuracy, and is slightly slower than EPIC but faster than CacheBlend and KVShare due to its lightweight query-to-context scoring stage (Stage I costs only 1.0% of full prefill at 16K context).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the position-independent KV cache reuse line initiated by CacheBlend, EPIC, and KVShare, which all address the problem of reassembling independently precomputed chunk caches and selectively recomputing a subset of tokens to recover missing cross-attention. The key departure is a reframing of the selection objective: rather than approximating the full cross-attention map (which the authors argue is infeasible under tight budgets), ProphetKV uses the terminal user query's attention weights as a query-contingent importance signal, combined with a dual-stage layer-fusion pipeline that avoids the single-layer approximation deadlock identified in prior dynamic methods. The approach is training-free and plug-and-play, distinguishing it from fine-tuned predictors like CacheClip.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'crowding-out effect' provides a useful diagnostic for why prior partial-recomputation methods underperform: globally salient but task-irrelevant tokens consume the limited recompute budget, displacing query-critical tokens. The query-as-prophet mechanism is elegant in its simplicity (a lightweight query-to-context attention pass) and the 96–101% accuracy retention at 20% recomputation with ~5× TTFT speedup is practically significant for production RAG serving. Open questions include integration into optimized inference engines (vLLM, SGLang), robustness to noisy or adversarial retrieval, and whether the query-at-end layout requirement limits applicability to non-standard prompt templates.
