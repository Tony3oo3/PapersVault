## Problem

In heterogeneous human-robot collaboration, decentralized multi-agent policy updates create a rationality gap where individual gradient directions diverge from the team-optimal ascent direction, producing non-conservative vector fields with rotational dynamics and limit cycles. Existing stabilization techniques (symplectic gradient adjustment, extragradient, consensus optimization) assume low-dimensional games, centralized Jacobian access, or fully modeled opponents, leaving no formal stability certificate for decentralized policy learning in heterogeneous, partially observed, embodied settings.

## Input / Output

Each agent consumes a 210D ego-centric observation vector combining 5 look-ahead waypoints from a pre-planned global path, self- and partner-proprioception, 4-corner object geometry, binary contact signals, 3 temporal snapshots (58D each) for history without recurrence, and 36-ray synthetic proximity. The policy outputs an 11D command vector using a delta-over-base scheme: 3D absolute locomotion (vx, vy, yaw), 2D absolute postural setpoints (CoM height, torso pitch), and 6D relative wrist offsets superimposed on task-specific base poses, executed at 2 Hz by a 50 Hz whole-body controller via zero-order hold.

## Method

HALO defines the rationality gap V(θ) = ½‖u_ind − u_team‖² as a Lyapunov candidate measuring the L2 discrepancy between concatenated independent actor gradients and the true team-level gradient. It formulates a minimum-norm quadratic program projecting u_ind onto the stability half-space {d : ⟨∇V, d⟩ ≤ −σV}, whose KKT conditions yield a closed-form rectified update d* = u_ind − max(0, (⟨h, u_ind⟩ + σV)/(‖h‖² + ε))·h, where h = ∇V is the stability normal. The vector h is computed as a Hessian-vector product (H_ind − H_team)ᵀ(u_ind − u_team) via double backpropagation without materializing the full D×D Hessian, keeping memory overhead under 21.1%. Under L-smoothness of V, the update guarantees monotonic non-increase of V(θ) per step, and under Robbins-Monro learning rates, V(θ) → 0 asymptotically, collapsing decentralized preferences onto the team-optimal synergy manifold.

## Results

Across nine coordination scenarios spanning three embodied tasks (OSP, SCT, SLH), HALO achieves an overall success rate of 86.0% at the 2B-step steady state, outperforming HAPPO (77.1%), HATRPO (80.8%), and PCGrad (82.1%). The most striking metric is the rationality gap V(θ): HALO reduces it to 0.09 versus 4.89 (HAPPO), 1.53 (HATRPO), and 0.20 (PCGrad), while simultaneously achieving the highest gradient alignment (cos φ = 0.91) and lowest gradient conflict rate (4.2%). In real-world humanoid deployment, HALO reduces time-to-destination to 76.2 s (SCT) and post-halt drift to 1.22 cm/s (SLH), compared to 101.6 s and 100% drop rate for the robot-script baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HALO is compared against three baselines: HAPPO and HATRPO (sequential trust-region MARL methods) and PCGrad (HAPPO augmented with gradient surgery from Yu et al., 2020). HALO wins on every reported metric across all three task suites. Against PCGrad—the strongest baseline—HALO improves overall success rate by 3.9 percentage points (86.0% vs. 82.1%), reduces the rationality gap by 5.5× (0.09 vs. 0.20), and cuts the gradient conflict rate from 25.0% to 4.2%. Against HAPPO, the gains are even more pronounced: a 9.1-point SR improvement and a 54× reduction in the rationality gap (0.09 vs. 4.89). PCGrad narrows the gap relative to HAPPO/HATRPO but still leaves substantial residual conflict (GCR 25.0%), confirming that gradient surgery alone cannot fully eliminate the solenoidal (rotational) component of the non-conservative vector field that HALO's Lyapunov projection explicitly targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

HALO synthesizes three previously separate threads: (1) differentiable-game dynamics analysis showing that decentralized MARL gradients form non-conservative vector fields with rotational components (Balduzzi et al., 2018; Zhao et al., 2023), (2) Lyapunov stability theory from nonlinear control, which prior RL work has applied only to state-space safety constraints (Chow et al., 2018; Sikchi et al., 2021), and (3) constrained quadratic projection via KKT conditions for gradient rectification. The key departure is repurposing Lyapunov certification from trajectory-level safety to policy-parameter-space learning dynamics, and making it tractable in high-dimensional heterogeneous settings via Hessian-vector products rather than explicit Jacobian access. Unlike PCGrad, which performs local pairwise gradient surgery without global invariants, HALO enforces a monotonic contraction certificate over the entire learning trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal stability certificate for decentralized MARL fills a recognized gap: prior methods regularize update magnitudes (trust regions) or assume centralized Jacobian access, leaving heterogeneous embodied HRC without a principled convergence guarantee. The real-world Unitree G1 experiments demonstrate that parameter-space stability translates to measurable physical coordination quality (reduced tilt, post-halt drift), validating the sim-to-real pipeline. Open questions include scalability beyond two agents, whether the stationarity assumption in the CTDE critic remains a bottleneck under highly non-stationary human behavior, and whether the HVP overhead becomes prohibitive as policy dimensionality grows. The modest SR gains (~9 points over HAPPO) relative to the dramatic gap reduction (~54×) suggest the stability certificate may unlock further gains in longer-horizon or more adversarial interaction regimes not yet tested.
