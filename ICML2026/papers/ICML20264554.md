## Problem

Existing differentiable causal discovery methods (NOTEARS, DCDI, DCDFG) enforce acyclicity only through soft augmented-Lagrangian penalties, causing them to evaluate and optimize over cyclic graphs that lack well-defined joint likelihoods. This leads to numerical instability at scale, prohibitive runtimes, and the need for post-hoc thresholding to recover valid DAGs. The paper asks whether one can search the DAG space directly—guaranteeing acyclicity by construction—while still supporting interventional data and scaling to thousands of variables.

## Input / Output

PACER consumes observational and interventional data across R perturbation regimes (e.g., single-cell RNA-seq counts, flow-cytometry protein measurements) together with optional structural priors such as transcription-factor-only outgoing edges. It outputs a Bernoulli-Plackett-Luce distribution over DAGs, from which a single DAG (a member of the interventional Markov equivalence class) can be sampled or the expected adjacency matrix extracted. The framework is functionally agnostic to variable domains, supporting continuous, discrete, or mixed data via appropriate conditional likelihoods (Gaussian, negative binomial, or neural MLP parameterizations).

## Method

PACER parameterizes a distribution over DAGs as a product of a Plackett-Luce distribution over variable permutations (topological orderings) and independent Bernoulli edge masks, so every sampled graph is acyclic by construction—eliminating surrogate acyclicity penalties entirely. The objective is the expected interventional log-likelihood of non-intervened variables across all regimes, regularized by an L1 sparsity term on the expected adjacency matrix. For the general case, gradients are estimated via REINFORCE with a running-average baseline; for linear-Gaussian mechanisms, the paper derives a closed-form expression (Theorem 4.4) for the expected log-likelihood that depends only on pairwise and triple-wise Plackett-Luce probabilities, enabling exact analytic gradients without any Monte Carlo sampling. This yields O(n²) forward-pass complexity (versus O(n³) for competing permutation-based methods) and up to two orders of magnitude speedup over penalty-based approaches on large-scale perturbation benchmarks.

## Results

On the CausalBench benchmark (RPE1 and K562 Perturb-seq datasets), PACER achieves a Wasserstein score of 0.167 (RPE1) and 0.150 (K562) while completing training in 15.1 and 3.1 minutes respectively—over 300× faster than NOTEARS(MLP) (698 and 1005 min) and ~76× faster than DCDFG (159 and 236 min). On the Sachs interventional dataset, PACER attains the best F1 (0.49) and FDR (0.58) among all 17 methods tested, with SHD=19 and SID=31. On Perturb-CITE-seq (~1000 genes, 248 perturbations), PACER achieves the lowest interventional NLL and MAE across all three experimental conditions while running over 300× faster than DCDFG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PACER's primary advantage is computational: it is 30–300× faster than penalty-based differentiable methods (NOTEARS, DCDI, DCDFG) while matching or exceeding their Wasserstein scores on CausalBench. On RPE1, SCD and DCDFG achieve slightly higher Wasserstein (0.126 vs. 0.167), but PACER reverses this on K562 (0.150 vs. 0.186 for SCD, 0.139 for DCDFG) and is 59–76× faster. The analytic variant PACER(A) further reduces runtime to under 1 minute on K562 with competitive accuracy (W=0.153). On the Sachs interventional benchmark, PACER achieves the best F1 (0.49) and FDR (0.58), edging out SCD (F1=0.41, FDR=0.67) and CAM (F1=0.38, SID=20); IGSP achieves a marginally lower SHD (18 vs. 19). On Perturb-CITE-seq, PACER dominates both I-NLL and I-MAE across all three conditions, outperforming DCDFG, NOTEARS, and NOTEARS-LR. Sort&regress is the only method faster than PACER (2 min on RPE1) but trails on Wasserstein (0.157 vs. 0.167) and FOR (0.110 vs. 0.134), suggesting PACER's joint interventional modeling provides a meaningful accuracy gain over simple sort-and-regress heuristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

PACER extends the Plackett-Luce distribution (Plackett, 1975) into a Bernoulli-Plackett-Luce model that jointly parameterizes topological orderings and edge probabilities, guaranteeing acyclicity by construction. Its interventional likelihood objective is directly inherited from DCDI (Brouillard et al., 2020) and DCDFG (Lopez et al., 2022), but replaces their augmented Lagrangian acyclicity penalties with a by-design DAG search space. This positions PACER as a departure from the NOTEARS-style continuous relaxation paradigm and from discrete permutation-search methods (SP, CAM, GRaSP), offering a probabilistic middle ground that is both acyclicity-guaranteed and amenable to stochastic optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PACER demonstrates that exact acyclicity enforcement via permutation-based parameterization can match or exceed soft-penalty methods while achieving O(n²) complexity and 300× speedups, making transcriptome-wide causal discovery from Perturb-seq data computationally feasible for the first time. Key limitations include the assumption of perfect interventions and causal sufficiency (no latent confounders), and the Bernoulli-Plackett-Luce model's deliberate trade-off of expressivity for scalability (it cannot capture arbitrary edge dependencies beyond a shared ordering). Open questions include extending to imperfect/unknown-target interventions, broader mechanism classes beyond linear-Gaussian, and providing calibrated Bayesian uncertainty over the interventional Markov equivalence class.
