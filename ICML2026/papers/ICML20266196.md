## Problem

Continual Offline Reinforcement Learning (CORL) requires agents to sequentially learn from static datasets while retaining prior knowledge, but existing methods employ flat parameter sharing or isolation that cannot distinguish shared domain-level commonalities from task-specific characteristics. Combined with the inherent distribution shift in offline RL that makes parameters highly sensitive to updates, this flat structure intensifies the stability-plasticity trade-off and limits cross-task generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations, return-to-go values, and natural-language task descriptions (e.g., 'Push the puck to a goal') as input, with the textual description encoded by a frozen SentenceBERT model to drive hierarchical expert routing. The output is a continuous action vector predicted by a task-specific linear head. A distinctive aspect is the warmup evaluation step: before committing to new parameter creation, the system evaluates a temporary action head against a performance threshold θ=0.8 to decide whether existing frozen experts suffice or a new task expert must be instantiated.

## Method

HTAC extends Decision Transformer with a dual-level expert architecture where domain experts (inner dim 1024) store coarse-grained shared knowledge per domain and task experts (inner dim 512) encode fine-grained task-specific patterns, both using low-rank decomposition. Task descriptions are decomposed into domain-level and task-level embeddings via soft assignment to learnable domain prototypes, which drive a dual-level attention composition module that aggregates frozen historical expert outputs at intermediate Transformer hidden layers rather than at the policy output level. Experts are created on-demand: a warmup evaluation with all experts frozen determines whether the composed policy meets a threshold, and only if it fails is a new expert trained. Per-task low-rank adapters (initialized with zero scaling) are trained after expert creation to learn task-specific query transformations while keeping shared projection matrices frozen, preventing interference with historical routing weights. A composition pretraining phase on the first three tasks initializes the attention mechanism before it is frozen for subsequent tasks.

## Results

On OCW-10, HTAC achieves an average performance (P) of 0.72±0.04, a negative forgetting rate (F) of −0.02±0.04, and forward transfer (FWT) of 0.06±0.10, outperforming the best structure-based baseline Prune (P=0.69) by 4.3%. On the longer OCW-20 sequence, HTAC improves to P=0.77±0.01 with F=−0.04±0.02 and FWT=0.38±0.02, surpassing Prune (P=0.72, FWT=0.03) by 5.5% in performance and 12.7× in forward transfer. The negative forgetting values indicate that HTAC actually enhances rather than degrades historical task performance after learning new tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HTAC outperforms all 14 baselines on both OCW-10 and OCW-20 across all three metrics. Against the strongest structure-based competitor Prune (CompoFormer variant), HTAC gains +4.3% P on OCW-10 (0.72 vs 0.69) and +5.5% P on OCW-20 (0.77 vs 0.72), while achieving 12.7× higher FWT on OCW-20 (0.38 vs 0.03), indicating substantially better knowledge reuse on revisited tasks. Regularization-based methods (EWC, MAS, LwF, VCL) and rehearsal-based methods (PM, A-GEM) all suffer severe catastrophic forgetting (F > 0.4) and achieve P below 0.30, far behind HTAC. LoRA, while preventing forgetting (F≈0), plateaus at P=0.54 on both benchmarks due to its low-rank capacity bottleneck, and shows no improvement from OCW-10 to OCW-20. The multi-task learning upper bound (MTL) remains slightly ahead (P=0.75/0.80), confirming that HTAC approaches the joint-training ceiling while operating under the stricter sequential constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

HTAC builds directly on the Decision Transformer backbone (Chen et al., 2021) and extends the CORL line initiated by CompoFormer (Hu et al., 2024e) and MH-DT/LoRA-DT (Huang et al., 2024), which introduced task-specific adaptation and semantic-similarity-based policy composition for offline continual RL. The key departure is moving from flat, single-level parameter isolation or output-level policy composition to a hierarchical dual-level expert architecture (domain experts + task experts) with composition performed at Transformer hidden layers rather than the policy output. The design also draws on LoRA-style low-rank parameterization for experts and SentenceBERT for task semantic encoding, combining these into an on-demand expansion mechanism with warmup evaluation that prior CORL methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CORL is a practically important but still young subfield for building long-horizon autonomous agents from sequentially arriving static datasets, and HTAC demonstrates that hierarchical knowledge decomposition at intermediate representations can simultaneously achieve near-zero forgetting and strong forward transfer (FWT 0.39 on OCW20, with 0.70 knowledge activation on revisited tasks). However, evaluation is confined to the OCW/Meta-World benchmark family with textual task descriptions, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to multimodal task specifications, non-manipulation domains, or longer/more heterogeneous task sequences. The reliance on a fixed number of domain prototypes (D=4) and a hand-tuned warmup threshold also raises questions about automatic domain discovery and threshold adaptation.
