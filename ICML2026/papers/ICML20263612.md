## Problem

Hard primitives in AD frameworks (sorting, argmax, comparisons, indexing, rounding) produce zero or undefined gradients, blocking gradient-based optimization in applications like differentiable simulation, structured prediction, and combinatorial layers. While numerous soft relaxations have been proposed, their implementations are fragmented across disparate projects with inconsistent interfaces, making them difficult to combine, compare, or adopt in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263612/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The libraries accept standard JAX/PyTorch tensors as input, augmented by a softness parameter τ > 0, a smoothness mode (smooth for C∞, c0 for C⁰, c1 for C¹, c2 for C²), and a method selection (OT, SoftSort, NeuralSort, FastSoftSort, SmoothSort, or Sorting Network). Outputs replace discrete values with continuous surrogates: indices become probability distributions on the unit simplex (SoftIndex), Booleans become probabilities in [0,1] (SoftBool), and permutation matrices become bistochastic matrices. For value-returning operators like sort, the output is a convex combination of input elements; for index-returning operators like argsort, the output is a soft permutation matrix whose rows are simplex distributions.

## Method

The paper unifies soft differentiable programming under two core mechanisms: (1) soft surrogates derived from Heaviside step function relaxations (sigmoidal in smooth mode, piecewise polynomials in c0/c1/c2 modes) for elementwise operators, and (2) multiple algorithmic families for axiswise operators—regularized optimal transport (entropic/Euclidean/p-norm), unit-simplex projections (SoftSort, NeuralSort), permutahedron projections (FastSoftSort, and a novel SmoothSort variant with C∞ differentiability via log-sum-exp bounds), and differentiable bitonic sorting networks. All methods share a common interface where τ→0⁺ recovers the hard operation, and the softness parameter controls the faithfulness-informativeness trade-off. The paper also introduces a 'straight-through pitfall' showing that STE must be applied to composite functions rather than individual primitives to avoid gradient vanishing from multiplicative gating, and provides a unified st decorator to handle this correctly.

## Results

On the four-digit MNIST sorting task (smooth mode, τ=0.1), all four soft methods achieve comparable exact-match accuracy: at n=3, SoftSort reaches 93.0%, OT 92.7%, Sorting Network 92.6%, and NeuralSort 92.5%; at n=7, NeuralSort leads at 69.8% vs. SoftSort 69.5%, OT 68.9%, and Sorting Network 67.5%. For quantile regression (n=3), NeuralSort achieves the lowest MSE of 26.8×10⁻⁴ (Spearman ρ=97.7), followed by SoftSort (30.9) and Sorting Network (31.9). In runtime benchmarks on an RTX 3060 GPU, the sorting network is the fastest soft method (1.0 ms at n=4096, ~3.8× the hard baseline), followed by SoftSort (16 ms) and NeuralSort (37 ms), while FastSoftSort is the most memory-efficient (420 kB at n=4096) by avoiding materialization of the permutation matrix.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a single dominant method but unifies five algorithmic families (OT, SoftSort, NeuralSort, FastSoftSort/SmoothSort, Sorting Network) into one library, enabling direct comparison. On MNIST sorting, SoftSort and NeuralSort are the strongest performers, with NeuralSort slightly ahead at n=7 (69.8% vs. 69.5%) and n=15 (25.5% vs. 24.0%), while SoftSort maintains a lead in element-wise accuracy at larger n (62.1% vs. 37.1% at n=32). The Sorting Network (Petersen et al., 2021) degrades more sharply at n≥9 (48.5% at n=9 vs. ~58% for SoftSort/NeuralSort) but is the fastest in wall-clock time (1.0 ms vs. 16–37 ms at n=4096). OT-based sorting (Cuturi et al., 2019) is competitive in accuracy but the slowest in both time and memory among the tested methods. FastSoftSort (Blondel et al., 2020) is not included in the MNIST tables (it only supports sort/rank, not argsort) but is the most memory-efficient in benchmarks (420 kB at n=4096, scaling roughly linearly), making it the practical choice for large-n value-space sorting where argsort is not needed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper consolidates a fragmented landscape of soft relaxation techniques—optimal transport (Cuturi et al., 2019; Xie et al., 2020), unit-simplex projections (Prillo & Eisenschlos, 2020; Grover et al., 2019), permutahedron projections (Blondel et al., 2020; Sander et al., 2023), and differentiable sorting networks (Petersen et al., 2021)—into two unified open-source libraries with a consistent API parameterized by a softness τ and smoothness mode (smooth/c0/c1/c2). Conceptually, the paper's main departures are (i) identifying the 'STE pitfall' where applying straight-through estimation to individual factors in a product causes gradient vanishing, and (ii) systematically deriving new regularizer combinations (e.g., c1/c2 Bregman projections for SoftSort and NeuralSort, and the novel SmoothSort variant with log-sum-exp bounds) that were not previously available. The elementwise operators are all derived from a common Heaviside relaxation, following a long line of sigmoidal approximations (Funahashi, 1989; Hendrycks & Gimpel, 2016; Semenov, 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary impact is practical: by providing a single, well-tested, feature-complete library with drop-in replacements for hard JAX/PyTorch operations, the paper significantly lowers the barrier to using soft differentiable programming in robotics, structured prediction, and scientific simulation. The STE pitfall identification is a useful practical warning that could prevent subtle gradient failures in existing codebases. Limitations include the deliberate exclusion of stochastic relaxations (Gumbel-softmax, perturbed optimizers), the fact that some methods (SoftSort, NeuralSort) retain non-differentiabilities at ties, and the O(n²) cost of OT-based and simplex-projection methods for large n. An open question is whether the unified smoothness-mode framework can be extended to stochastic methods while preserving the same API consistency.
