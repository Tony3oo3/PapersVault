## Problem

Reinforcement learning has developed a wide range of behavioral structures—value functions, bisimulation relations, behavioral metrics, safety invariants—each with its own definition and proof of transfer under state abstraction, yet no general principle unifies them. Even mild variants of existing structures are re-analyzed from scratch following the same proof template, indicating missing shared definition and proof principles. The paper asks: what is a compositional, reusable framework that subsumes these structures and yields transfer guarantees uniformly?

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a state transition system specified as an F-coalgebra (a stochastic Moore machine for environments or a hidden Markov model for policy-conditioned dynamics), an abstraction map (encoder φ: S→Z), and a bundle specification consisting of an arity n, a codomain V (e.g., {⊤,⊥} for logical or [0,∞] for quantitative), and three operators (a combinator ⊕, an aggregator □, and a barycenter ⊙). It produces behavioral structures as post-fixed points of a derived closure operator, sound transfer guarantees (pullback reflects post-fixed points along surjective homomorphisms; pushforward preserves them along any homomorphism), and—under an algebraic compatibility condition—quantitative metrics constructed systematically from logical semantics. No data or training is involved; the outputs are theorems and construction recipes.

## Method

The framework models environments as F-coalgebras (stochastic Moore machines) and policy-conditioned dynamics as hidden Markov models, with state abstraction formalized as coalgebra homomorphisms that preserve one-step dynamics. Behavioral structures are defined compositionally: a bundle h_X: X^n → V is lifted to the one-step behavior space FX via a functor lifting that decomposes inductively over the functor's product, exponentiation, and probability components using the chosen operators, then pulled back along the coalgebra t_X to form a closure operator T_X; a behavioral structure is a post-fixed point of T_X. This single construction subsumes the Bellman operator (unary, real-valued, expectation + addition), bisimulation relations (binary, logical, almost-sure + conjunction), safety predicates (unary, logical, almost-sure + conjunction), and successor features (unary, vector-valued, expectation + addition). Transfer is guaranteed by two theorems: pullback along a surjective homomorphism reflects post-fixed points (safe verification from abstract to concrete), and pushforward along any homomorphism preserves them (safe construction from concrete to abstract). A further theorem bridges logical and quantitative semantics by requiring the zero predicate to be an algebra homomorphism between the operator families, so that a quantitative bundle vanishes iff the corresponding logical bundle holds.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The main results are three transfer theorems: (1) Theorem 3.12 (Safe Verification) shows that under a surjective homomorphism, post-fixed points on the abstract system can be safely pulled back to the concrete system; (2) Theorem 3.13 (Safe Construction) shows that pushforward along any homomorphism preserves post-fixed points from concrete to abstract; and (3) Theorem 3.14 establishes that quantitative metrics can be systematically constructed from logical behavioral semantics when the zero predicate is an algebra homomorphism between the operators. Additionally, Proposition 4.2 proves that model-irrelevance abstraction (Li et al., 2006) is exactly a coalgebra homomorphism, and Proposition 4.3 shows that bisimulation equivalences are precisely the post-fixed points of a closure operator derived from the homomorphism condition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not perform empirical comparisons against baselines; its contribution is a unifying theoretical framework rather than a new algorithm. Instead of benchmarking, the paper demonstrates that several previously separate notions in RL are special cases of its compositional construction: bisimulation relations and metrics (Dean & Givan, 1997; Ferns et al., 2004; 2011) are recovered as binary post-fixed points of the closure operator with conjunction/universal-quantifier/almost-sure operators; the Bellman operator and value functions (Sutton & Barto, 1998) are recovered as the closure operator from the reward-aggregation lifting with addition/expectation operators; model-irrelevance abstraction (Li et al., 2006) is shown to be exactly the coalgebra homomorphism condition; and successor features (Dayan, 1993; Barreto et al., 2017) emerge as vector-valued bundle liftings. The framework's value is that it replaces case-specific proofs (e.g., the recurring proof template in Ferns et al., 2004) with reusable transfer theorems that apply uniformly across all these structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on the coalgebraic theory of systems (Rutten 2000; Jacobs 2016) and functor lifting from coalgebraic modal logic (Kurz 2001; Pattinson 2003) to provide a unified compositional framework for behavioral structures in RL. It subsumes bisimulation relations and metrics (Ferns et al. 2004; 2011), model-irrelevance abstraction (Li et al. 2006), value equivalence, successor features, and safety predicates as instances of a single lifting-and-closure-operator construction, replacing the recurring pattern in RL theory where each mild variant of a behavioral structure is re-analyzed from scratch following the same proof template. The key departure from prior RL abstraction work is the explicit use of pullback and pushforward along coalgebra homomorphisms to obtain general transfer theorems, and the algebraic compatibility condition (Theorem 3.14) that systematically constructs quantitative metrics from logical semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a reusable definition-and-proof template for a broad class of behavioral structures, meaning that new structures (e.g., novel safety constraints or temporal logic specifications) can be specified as bundles and their transfer properties verified via the general theorems rather than ad hoc proofs. The logical-to-quantitative bridge (Theorem 3.14) generalizes the bisimulation-relation-to-metric connection to arbitrary behavioral structures, offering a principled construction path. However, the paper is limited to strict homomorphisms and exact structural preservation; the authors explicitly identify relaxed (lax) homomorphisms, quantitative approximation theory, and practical learning algorithms as important open directions. For practitioners, the immediate impact is indirect: the framework clarifies which abstraction objectives are jointly satisfiable and provides soundness guarantees for representation learning, but no algorithms or empirical validation are provided.
