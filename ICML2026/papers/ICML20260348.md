## Problem

Conventional RF ray tracing relies on hand-engineered interaction rules and cannot model spatially varying or internal material structures, while existing neural surrogates (e.g., NeRF², NeWRF) treat attenuation as a black-box and require roughly 200 channel samples per square foot. No prior framework simultaneously preserves the composability and interpretability of ray tracing, satisfies fundamental physical constraints such as reciprocity and reversibility, and achieves high fidelity from sparse RF measurements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260348/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes TX and RX 3D coordinates, a carrier frequency, and a small set of complex-valued RF measurements (as few as 0.175 samples/sq ft) for supervised training. It outputs a coherent superposition of all valid propagation paths, yielding either a complex-valued received signal, per-subcarrier CSI for MIMO arrays (up to 104 antennas), or a scalar RSSI in dB depending on the task. The same architecture operates across 2.4 GHz BLE, 60 GHz mmWave, and 5G OFDM bands without architectural changes, with the carrier frequency fed as an additional input to the material network.

## Method

SNRFT models RF propagation as a stochastic transport process in which the attenuation coefficient is the rate parameter of a continuous-time discrete-state Markov process on the object indicator function. Theorem 4.4 proves that exponential transport is equivalent to this Markov condition and derives a closed-form attenuation coefficient σ(x,ω) = (m(x)(1−v(x))+1)|ω·∇v(x)|/(1−v(x)) that inherently satisfies reciprocity and reversibility. Geometry is represented by an 8-layer coordinate MLP outputting an SDF-like scalar field and 128-dim local features; a second 8-layer MLP maps (x, f_c, h) to material coefficient m(x) and phase φ(x). Ray tracing uses sphere tracing for SDF intersections, Monte Carlo sampling weighted by directional attenuation for reflection-vs-penetration decisions, and differentiable ray marching for object-interior attenuation integration. The entire pipeline is end-to-end differentiable, optimized by minimizing the sim-to-real loss over sparse RF measurements with a discrete Laplacian regularizer, converging in ~20k iterations on a single GPU.

## Results

On the BLE RSSI task with very sparse data (0.175 samples/sq ft), SNRFT achieves a median RSSI error of 5.6 dB, approximately 4 dB lower than all baselines including NeRF², k-NN, and MRI. On the mmWave channel estimation task (0.23 samples/ft²), SNRFT consistently outperforms all baselines except RFcanvas (which leverages a visual prior), while conventional ray tracing exhibits a 7 dB median error. On the 5G CSI estimation task, SNRFT matches NeRF²'s synthesis quality while using only 12.5% of the training data (6× less), and outperforms both GSRF and FIRE in SNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNRFT outperforms NeRF² (Zhao et al., 2023) on the BLE RSSI task, especially under sparse data (0.175 samples/sq ft), where the gap is ~4 dB in median error; on the 5G CSI task, SNRFT achieves comparable SNR to NeRF² while using 6× less training data. Against MRI (Shin et al., 2014) and k-NN on BLE, SNRFT shows clear advantages, with the physics-informed formulation providing stronger generalization from few measurements. On mmWave, SNRFT beats conventional ray tracing (Ma et al., 2024) which suffers a 7 dB median error due to its inability to model internal propagation, and also outperforms linear interpolation. The only method that edges out SNRFT is RFcanvas (Chen et al., 2024) on mmWave, but RFcanvas relies on a visual prior of the scene, whereas SNRFT operates purely from RF measurements. On 5G CSI, SNRFT outperforms both GSRF (Yang et al., 2025) and FIRE (Liu et al., 2021) in reconstruction SNR, confirming that the stochastic transport formulation with reciprocity constraints provides a stronger inductive bias than Gaussian-splatting or FDD-reciprocity approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SNRFT extends the stochastic object theory of Miller et al. (2024) from optical rendering to RF propagation, adding material-dependent attenuation and object penetration (rather than opaque boundary termination) to capture the physics of longer-wavelength signals. It also builds on RFScape (Chen et al., 2025) for its Monte Carlo path selection and neural implicit geometry, but departs from the broader NeRF-based RF modeling line (NeRF², NeWRF, RFGS, WRF-GS) by replacing black-box ray-marching attenuation with a closed-form, physics-derived attenuation coefficient that provably satisfies reciprocity and reversibility. The result is a hybrid that retains the composability and interpretability of classical ray tracing while learning geometry and material properties from sparse measurements via neural implicit fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled path toward sample-efficient RF channel modeling that respects fundamental physical constraints (reciprocity, reversibility), which is critical for TDD-based 5G systems and for deploying channel models in environments where measurements are expensive. The theoretical derivation (Theorem 4.4) provides the first stochastic-object formulation that rigorously explains RF signal propagation through complex, material-heterogeneous environments, bridging a gap between optics-based rendering theory and RF engineering practice. Key open questions include whether the exponential transport assumption holds at all frequency bands and geometries, whether non-exponential models (e.g., first-passage times of Gaussian processes) yield meaningful gains, and how the approach scales to very large outdoor scenes or multi-frequency joint modeling.
