## Problem

Weak-to-strong generalization—training stronger LLMs on samples from aligned weaker models—suffers from poor robustness and limited generalization because weak-model outputs contain inherent noise and biases. The central question is how to extract higher-quality supervision signals from weak models without relying on explicit rewards such as human feedback or trained reward models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of text prompts along with three model checkpoints: a pre-alignment weak model (reference), a post-alignment weak model (aligned via DPO on UltraFeedback), and an initial strong model. Contrastive decoding is performed between the two weak-model states to generate chosen responses, which are then used to train the strong model in two sequential stages (SFT followed by DPO), producing a final aligned strong model. The contrastive coefficient α (typically 0.3–0.5) and vocabulary pruning threshold λ=0.1 are the key hyperparameters controlling generation quality.

## Method

The paper establishes the CD-Implicit Reward Correlation, proving that the log-likelihood-ratio structure of contrastive decoding is mathematically equivalent to the implicit reward formulation from DPO, so that CD-generated samples approximately maximize implicit reward. ConG operates in two stages: Stage I (ConG-S) performs contrastive decoding between the pre- and post-alignment weak models to produce high-reward SFT samples, which are used to fine-tune the strong model; Stage II (ConG) pairs each CD response (preferred) with a standard-decoding sample from the SFT-trained strong model (rejected) and applies DPO to further sharpen preferences. The theoretical justification for the preference ordering relies on showing that SFT projection attenuates the exponential tilting of the CD distribution, guaranteeing E[r̂(y_w)] > E[r̂(y_l)]. This design requires no external reward model or human annotations, and naturally reduces to self-alignment when weak and strong models are identical.

## Results

On the weak-to-strong alignment setting, ConG(w→s) achieves an average score of 52.7 on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and 41.6 on Llama3-8B-Instruct (AlpacaEval2 + Arena-Hard), representing gains of approximately 17.5 and 15.1 points over their respective base models. In the self-alignment setting, ConG(self) reaches 45.5 on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and 34.6 on Llama3.2-3B-Instruct, improving over the base by 22.7 and 12.8 points. Across all configurations, ConG yields an average gain of about 16.5% over the base model, while introducing negligible degradation on downstream tasks (within 1.0 point on MMLU, ARC, GSM8K, etc.).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the weak-to-strong setting, ConG(w→s) substantially outperforms all baselines: it beats the strongest baseline WeakTeacher (Tao & Li, 2025) by 9.2 points on Qwen2.5-7B (52.7 vs. 43.5) and 9.0 points on Llama3-8B (41.6 vs. 32.6). The gap over WSPO (Zhu et al., 2025) is even larger at 27.1 and 23.5 points, while Weak SFT (post) (Burns et al., 2024) trails by 16.8 and 14.4 points. Notably, Weak SFT (pre) and AuxConf actually degrade performance below the unaligned base model, confirming the noise problem that ConG addresses. In the self-alignment setting, ConG(self) outperforms SimPO (Meng et al., 2024) by 4.0 points on Qwen2.5-3B (45.5 vs. 41.5) and 1.1 points on Llama3.2-3B (34.6 vs. 33.5), and beats DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by 7.7 and 5.0 points respectively. ORPO (Hong et al., 2024) is the weakest self-alignment baseline, trailing ConG(self) by 15.1 and 8.8 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously independent threads: contrastive decoding (Li et al., 2023), a decoding-time strategy for reducing generation noise, and implicit rewards from DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), which parameterize preference as a log-likelihood ratio between aligned and reference policies. The central theoretical contribution—the CD-Implicit Reward Correlation—shows that contrastive decoding is mathematically equivalent to approximately maximizing implicit reward, thereby providing a principled denoising mechanism for weak-to-strong generalization (Burns et al., 2024). Unlike prior weak-to-strong methods that directly fine-tune on raw weak-model outputs, ConG exploits the pre/post-alignment contrast to filter out noise before transfer, and then applies DPO on the resulting preference pairs to further sharpen the strong model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ConG offers a practical, reward-model-free pathway to improve LLM alignment by ~16.5% over base models, with consistent gains across Qwen2.5 and Llama3 families and even cross-family transfers, addressing a key bottleneck in scalable alignment where human feedback becomes infeasible. The theoretical grounding (CD as implicit-reward maximization) gives practitioners a clear knob (α) to tune the trade-off between preference signal strength and distributional stability. Open questions include compatibility with fast inference (speculative decoding, KV caching), whether the approach extends to multimodal or agentic settings, and how it behaves at much larger scale where the weak model's noise profile may differ. The paper also notes that maintaining multiple alignment states of the weak model adds engineering overhead, and that ConG can still inherit biases from the weak model if deployed without careful evaluation.
