## Problem

Structured pruning of LLMs typically relies on heuristic one-shot importance scores that degrade sharply under aggressive sparsity, while learning sparsity via full weight updates is prohibitively expensive at LLM scale. Prior mask-learning approaches use stochastic hard-concrete relaxations that introduce a train-test mismatch (random masks during training vs. deterministic masks at deployment) and constrain masks to a bounded near-binary range, limiting both convergence speed and the quality of discovered sparsity patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261771/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes frozen pre-trained LLM weights (dense or MoE), a target sparsity ratio, and a small training token budget (~30M tokens from FineWeb-Edu) as input. It produces a structured pruned model in which attention heads and MLP channels (dense) or expert channels (MoE) are removed, and the remaining nonzero mask values are folded directly into the model weights for deployment. The trainable mask parameters are extremely small (~0.35M for LLaMA-7B), far fewer than LoRA adapters, and the entire optimization converges within ~40 minutes on 4 GPUs.

## Method

DDP formulates structured pruning as an ℓ0-constrained mask optimization problem solved via a deterministic augmented Lagrangian. In the forward pass, a simple ReLU(z) gate replaces stochastic hard-concrete sampling, expanding the mask range from [0,1] to [0,∞) so retained components can be rescaled. For the sparsity regularizer, a separate annealed sigmoid mapping projects logits to retention scores in [0,1], with a sharpness parameter μ that is annealed toward zero so the surrogate converges to exact ℓ0 counting; this decouples the forward mask from the regularization score. An augmented Lagrangian with gradient-ascent-updated multipliers enforces the target keep ratio, and an additional binarization loss pushes retention scores toward {0,1} to accelerate convergence. Knowledge distillation from the frozen dense model (as a parameter-free teacher) is integrated with minimal overhead, and a theoretical guarantee shows that staged annealing recovers the exact discrete ℓ0 budget at convergence.

## Results

On dense LLaMA models, DDP achieves the best mean zero-shot accuracy at every sparsity level: 64.13% on LLaMA-7B at 20% sparsity (vs. 62.41% for SlimLLM) and 62.14% on LLaMA-13B at 50% sparsity (vs. 58.20% for LoRAP), a >7-point gain under aggressive pruning. On MoE models, the advantage grows with sparsity: on DeepSeekMoE-16B at 60% sparsity, DDP reaches 58.18% mean accuracy and 12.65 C4 perplexity, outperforming the strongest baseline (Camera-P: 51.62% / 18.10) by +6.6 accuracy points. On Qwen3-30B-A3B at 60% sparsity, DDP achieves 63.35% mean accuracy versus 59.03% for Camera-P, while maintaining the lowest perplexity (13.54 vs. 24.48). End-to-end vLLM speedups reach 2.20× on LLaMA-7B (RTX 5090, 50% sparsity) and 1.51× on Qwen3-30B-A3B (B200, 60% sparsity).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDP consistently outperforms all one-shot structured pruning baselines (LoRAPrune, LoRAP, SlimLLM) on dense LLaMA models across both 20% and 50% sparsity, with the largest gap at 50% sparsity on LLaMA-13B where DDP gains +3.94 mean accuracy over LoRAP (62.14 vs. 58.20) and +7.39 over SlimLLM (62.14 vs. 54.75). On MoE models, DDP beats all four training-free baselines (NAEE, D2-MoE, Camera-P, HEAPr) at every sparsity level; the margin widens sharply at 60% sparsity on DeepSeekMoE-16B, where DDP achieves 58.18% mean accuracy versus 51.62% for Camera-P (the strongest baseline) and 45.28% for NAEE. Against Tyr-the-Pruner (Table 8), DDP wins on all four model families (LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-2-13B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, Mistral-Nemo) at all sparsity levels, with the gap reaching +4.76 mean accuracy on Mistral-7B at 50% sparsity (50.87 vs. 46.21). The paper also shows that DDP with 30M mask-optimization tokens outperforms LoRAP + 120M LoRA recovery tokens on LLaMA-2-7B (64.82 vs. 62.98 mean accuracy), indicating that directly optimizing the sparse structure is more effective than post-pruning fine-tuning. DDP uses only 0.35M trainable parameters (vs. 20M for LoRA) and achieves the lowest per-step training time (2.1s vs. 2.4s for LoRA) on LLaMA-7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the hard-concrete relaxation (Louizos et al., 2018) for ℓ0-regularized sparse network learning, but departs from it in two key ways: replacing stochastic sampling with a deterministic ReLU forward gate and an annealed soft surrogate for the sparsity constraint, and decoupling the forward mask values from the regularization scores to expand the effective search space beyond near-binary values. It positions itself as a lightweight middle ground between heuristic one-shot pruning (LLM-Pruner, LoRAP, SlimLLM) and full sparsity-aware training (ShearedLLaMA, Compresso), operating in a mask-only regime where only tens of millions of gating variables are optimized while all pretrained weights remain frozen. The augmented Lagrangian formulation for enforcing an exact keep-ratio budget follows standard constrained optimization practice, but the deterministic surrogate and binarization regularizer are novel contributions specific to the structured LLM pruning setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DDP achieves 20% structured sparsity with only ~1% downstream accuracy loss on flagship models (Qwen3-32B, Qwen3-30B-A3B) using just 30M training tokens (~40–60 minutes), and delivers 1.36×–2.20× end-to-end inference speedups via vLLM. The finding that mask-only optimization (0.35M trainable parameters) outperforms LoRA recovery (20M parameters) with 4× more tokens suggests the bottleneck in post-pruning fine-tuning is the quality of the initial sparse structure, not the capacity to adapt remaining weights. Open questions include whether continued training on the pruned model can close the remaining gap at 50–60% sparsity, whether the approach extends to other prunable units (entire layers, token-level sparsity), and how the method behaves under distribution shift or domain-specific fine-tuning after pruning.
