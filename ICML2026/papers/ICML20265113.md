## Problem

Existing stereo image compression methods face a fundamental trade-off: VAE-based approaches optimize pixel-level distortion and produce poor perceptual quality at low bitrates, while diffusion-based generative compression achieves high perceptual fidelity but fails to maintain object consistency across viewpoints, degrading downstream tasks like depth estimation. Additionally, diffusion-based methods incur prohibitive denoising latency (50+ steps), making them impractical for real-time stereo applications.

## Input / Output

The method takes a stereo image pair (main and auxiliary view) as input, with images at 2048×1024 (Cityscapes) or 1080×860 (InStereo2K) resolution. Encoding is distributed: each view is independently compressed by a shared-parameter codebook-based latent compressor operating in the latent space of a pre-trained LDM encoder, producing quantized codes plus a single estimated timestep per pair. Decoding is centralized, where the pre-trained LDM decoder reconstructs both views from the shared diffusion prior, yielding the final decoded stereo image pair.

## Method

SDiD employs a distributed architecture where a pre-trained LDM's denoising network generates a main-view prior from the compressed latent, which is then transferred to the auxiliary view via a Diffusion Prior Transfer (DPT) module using Look-Back Attention (LBA) that computes attention between the auxiliary compressed latent (query), main compressed latent (key), and the denoised prior (value). Two key innovations enable efficiency: (1) Diffusion Prior Alignment (DPA) maps the ε-prediction output back to z₀ at any timestep with a consistency loss, enabling 2-step sampling instead of 50; (2) Adaptive Noise Level Estimation (ANE) replaces Gaussian initialization by matching the SNR of the compressed representation to the forward diffusion schedule, so denoising starts directly from the compressed latent at its estimated noise level. Training jointly optimizes codebook compression loss, DPA consistency and z₀-prediction losses, and reconstruction losses (DINOv2 feature distance, perceptual loss, and GAN adversarial loss) across both views.

## Results

On InStereo2K, SDiD achieves BD-Rate savings of 80.19% (LPIPS) and 97.63% (RMSE) over the closest diffusion-based baseline RDEIC, and 783.01% (LPIPS) and 271.95% (RMSE) over the VAE-based DSC baseline LDMIC. On Cityscapes, the corresponding savings over RDEIC are 25.07% (LPIPS) and 27.99% (RMSE). At ultra-low bitrates, SDiD requires only 0.02 bpp versus 0.06 bpp for the SOTA baseline to reconstruct stereo pairs with comparable depth information (RMSE 3.99 vs. 4.11 at 0.016 bpp vs. 0.069 bpp). Decoding latency is 1.41 s/image with only 2 NFE, a 42.2% speedup over RDEIC (2.44 s, 5 NFE) and a 15× speedup over 50-step diffusion baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDiD outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the VAE-based DSC baseline LDMIC, SDiD achieves BD-Rate savings exceeding 780% in LPIPS and 270% in RMSE on InStereo2K, reflecting the severe perceptual and depth-estimation degradation of distortion-optimized VAEs at low bitrates. The GAN-based baselines HiFiC and MS-ILLM show intermediate perceptual quality but suffer catastrophic depth-estimation errors (RMSE BD-Rate of 555% and 277% on InStereo2K), confirming that GAN perceptual losses do not preserve geometric structure. Among diffusion-based methods, RDEIC is the closest competitor, with SDiD gaining 80.19% BD-Rate in LPIPS and 97.63% in RMSE on InStereo2K, while also decoding 42.2% faster (1.41 s vs. 2.44 s per image). DiffEIC and PerCo, which rely on 50-step and 30-step sampling respectively, are dominated by SDiD on both quality and speed (SDiD uses only 2 NFE). On Cityscapes, MS-ILLM narrows the LPIPS gap to 23.45% BD-Rate, but SDiD still leads by 109.32% in RMSE, underscoring its advantage in preserving depth-relevant structure for downstream tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDiD sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: distributed source coding (DSC) for stereo image compression and diffusion-based generative compression. It inherits the asymmetric encode-light/decode-heavy structure from DSC methods like LDMIC and the codebook-based latent compressor from RDEIC, but departs from both by replacing the VAE-style reconstruction with a shared pre-trained latent diffusion prior. The key architectural departure is the Diffusion Prior Alignment (DPA) module, which uses a consistency-model-style loss to map denoising outputs back to z₀, enabling two-step sampling, and the Diffusion Prior Transfer (DPT) module with Look-Back Attention, which explicitly transfers the main-view prior to the auxiliary view rather than independently denoising each view. This shared-prior design is the conceptual novelty: prior diffusion-based compression methods (PerCo, DiffEIC, RDEIC) treat each image independently, while prior stereo compression methods (CAMSIC, ECSIC) lack generative priors entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in ultra-low-bitrate stereo compression where VAE-based methods lose texture and diffusion-based methods lose inter-view consistency, both of which degrade downstream depth estimation critical for autonomous driving. The 50% decoding speedup over other few-step diffusion baselines and the 15× speedup over standard 50-step sampling make the approach more deployable. Limitations include reliance on a pre-trained LDM (Stable Diffusion) that introduces hallucination risk, testing on only two datasets (Cityscapes, InStereo2K), and the fixed two-view setting that does not generalize to multi-view or dynamic scenes. Open questions include whether the shared-prior strategy extends to more than two views, how it handles temporal sequences (video stereo), and whether the ANE-based timestep estimation remains robust under extreme compression ratios beyond those tested.
