## Problem

Existing vector-based semantic typography methods that rely on vanilla Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) with text-only prompts produce glyphs with coarse, unpolished semantic details and limited visual expressiveness. Additionally, these methods lack holistic structural constraints, leading to overfitting that compromises legibility, and they frequently generate intersecting Bezier curves that cause local artifacts—issues that are commonly overlooked in SVG generation for typography.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target word (text prompt) and a font file as input. The font file is processed via FreeType to extract character outlines, which are converted into SVG paths of cubic Bezier curves with updatable control points. The output is a set of deformed SVG vector glyphs (one per character in the word), where each glyph's Bezier control points have been optimized to visually embody the word's meaning while preserving the original character's topology. All rasterized intermediates are 512×512, and the method is applied independently to each character.

## Method

VecDesigner extends SDS into Visual-Guided Score Distillation Sampling (VGSDS), which periodically generates reference images from the pre-trained diffusion model and uses CLIP Image Encoder features to compute a cosine-similarity energy term between intermediate glyph renderings and the semantic center of those references, steering optimization toward richer visual details. For structural preservation, a vector-based Procrustes loss is applied at the path level between initial and current control points (with additional sampled points along outlines for denser constraints), enforcing global geometric coherence via optimal translation, rotation, and scaling alignment. To suppress Bezier curve intersections, Positional Relationship Constraints maintain the absolute ordering of control points and penalize non-adjacent points that become too close (detected via KDTree nearest-neighbor queries with a learnable threshold). The combined objective is optimized over 500 iterations using DiffVG as the differentiable rasterizer and Stable Diffusion 2-1-base as the diffusion backbone, with gradients backpropagated through the rasterizer to update control point coordinates.

## Results

On a 100-concept, 5-font benchmark, VecDesigner achieves 85.36% OCR accuracy, 1.3717 Chamfer Distance, 0.2733 CLIPScore, and 0.3535 BLIPScore, setting new state-of-the-art among vector-based methods. Compared to the strongest vector baseline Word-As-Image, VecDesigner improves OCR by 3.0%, reduces Chamfer Distance by 0.1205, and gains +0.0215 CLIPScore and +0.0297 BLIPScore, all statistically significant (p < 0.05, two-tailed t-test). Notably, VecDesigner also surpasses most raster-based approaches (DS-Fusion, MetaDesigner, DALL-E3) on both semantic and structural metrics, while remaining competitive with Nano Banana Pro on CLIPScore/BLIPScore while offering the additional advantage of editable vector output.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary vector baseline Word-As-Image (Iluz et al., 2023), VecDesigner wins on all four metrics: +3.0% OCR (85.36 vs 82.36), −0.1205 Chamfer Distance (1.3717 vs 1.4922), +0.0215 CLIPScore, and +0.0297 BLIPScore, with all differences statistically significant (p < 0.05). Against CLIPDraw (Frans et al., 2022), VecDesigner leads by a wide margin on every metric (e.g., +12.71% OCR, −0.3156 CD). Among raster-based methods, VecDesigner outperforms DS-Fusion, MetaDesigner, and DALL·E3 on both CLIPScore and BLIPScore, and matches or exceeds them on OCR. The closest competitor is Nano Banana Pro, which edges VecDesigner by a narrow margin on CLIPScore (0.2739 vs 0.2733) and BLIPScore (0.3554 vs 0.3535), but lacks the vector editability and structural consistency (CD) that VecDesigner provides. The paper also reports a user study (20 participants, 25 test cases) where VecDesigner achieves the highest character identification accuracy and is judged most semantically representative by a substantially larger proportion of participants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SDS-based vector typography pipeline established by Word-as-Image (Iluz et al., 2023), which itself builds on DreamFusion's Score Distillation Sampling and DiffVG's differentiable rasterization. The key departure is twofold: (1) augmenting text-only SDS with visual guidance from CLIP-encoded reference images (VGSDS), and (2) replacing the local angular constraints of prior work with a global vector-based Procrustes loss plus a KDTree-based positional relationship constraint to prevent Bezier curve intersections. Conceptually, the paper repurposes classical shape-matching (Procrustes analysis) and nearest-neighbor geometry into the differentiable SVG optimization loop, rather than introducing a new generative architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Vector semantic typography has direct practical value in poster design, education, and marketing, and this paper demonstrates that a relatively lightweight optimization-based approach (500 iterations, no training) can outperform both raster-based diffusion models and prior vector methods on both semantic fidelity and legibility. However, the method remains per-character and struggles when the target concept's shape is incompatible with the glyph's inherent topology (e.g., 'DEER' for D/R), and holistic whole-word deformation is left as future work. The reliance on a pre-trained diffusion model for reference image generation and CLIP for feature extraction also ties the method's quality to these upstream models' capabilities.
