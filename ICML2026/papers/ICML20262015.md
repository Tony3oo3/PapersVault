## Problem

Existing model merging methods treat non-interference (preventing tasks from degrading each other) as the final objective, which inherently caps performance at the pre-trained model's cross-task ceiling. The paper argues that the greater potential of merging lies in enabling positive task synergy—where tasks actively improve one another—and identifies cross-task functional compatibility between encoders and predictors as the key structural indicator of merge quality that prior work has overlooked.

## Input / Output

SyMerge takes K independently fine-tuned expert models (sharing a common pre-trained backbone), the pre-trained weights, and unlabeled test samples from each task. It produces a single merged multi-task model consisting of a shared encoder (parameterized by learned task-vector coefficients) and one adapted task-specific layer per task, requiring only 0.39M trainable parameters total. The framework is modality-agnostic: the same self-labeling objective applies cross-entropy for classification, L1 loss for dense prediction (segmentation, depth, surface normals on NYUv2), and token-level cross-entropy for NLP generation tasks.

## Method

SyMerge jointly optimizes two components on unlabeled test data: (1) per-task, per-layer merging coefficients that construct the shared encoder via task-vector interpolation, and (2) a single task-specific layer (e.g., the classifier head or one transformer block). The supervisory signal is expert-guided self-labeling: the merged model is trained to match the confident predictions of the individual fine-tuned experts, using cross-entropy (or task-appropriate loss) rather than the unstable entropy minimization used by AdaMerging. The paper provides a theoretical guarantee (Proposition 3.1) showing that under cross-task linearity and convex loss, synergistic task vectors tighten the merged model's loss bound below the non-interference case. A key empirical finding is that this joint co-adaptation—rather than either component alone—enables recovery even across disjoint optimization basins (different pre-trained initializations), where coefficient-only or adapter-only methods collapse entirely.

## Results

On 8 vision tasks with ViT-B/32, SyMerge achieves 90.1% average accuracy, matching the individual-model reference (90.5%) and surpassing the best test-time adaptive baseline WEMoE (89.4%) and ProbSurgery (87.4%). Scaling to 20 tasks, SyMerge maintains 88.6% while most competitors degrade sharply (e.g., Task Arithmetic drops to 60.8%, AdaMerging to 69.6%). On dense prediction (NYUv2), SyMerge reaches 49.8 mIoU / 73.1 pixel accuracy for segmentation, 45.3 absolute error for depth, and 26.2° mean angular error for normals—closest to individual models (52.0/74.2/41.5/24.2) among all baselines. On 8 GLUE NLP tasks, SyMerge attains 83.9% average, outperforming ProbSurgery w/TA (81.6%) and EMR-Merging (80.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyMerge outperforms all 15 compared baselines across every main results table. Against training-free methods, the gap is largest: Task Arithmetic (69.1%) and Ties-Merging (72.9%) on 8 vision tasks are surpassed by 21 and 17 percentage points respectively, with the gap widening to ~28 points at 20 tasks. Among test-time adaptive methods, SyMerge beats WEMoE (89.4%) by 0.7 points on 8 tasks but pulls ahead dramatically at 20 tasks (88.6% vs. 78.9%), indicating superior scalability. ProbSurgery (87.4%) and Surgery (87.5%) are the closest competitors on 8 tasks but trail by 2.6–2.7 points; on dense prediction, ProbSurgery's 43.6 mIoU lags SyMerge's 49.8 by 6.2 points, and on NLP, ProbSurgery w/TA (81.6% avg) is 2.3 points below SyMerge (83.9%). EMR-Merging (88.7% on 8 vision tasks) is the strongest training-free baseline but still trails SyMerge by 1.4 points and degrades more at 20 tasks (86.6% vs. 88.6%). On the disjoint-basin stress test (Table 5), all baselines collapse (Weight Averaging: 6.9%, Surgery: 25.6%) while SyMerge recovers to 79.1%, a 53.5-point margin over the best baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

SyMerge extends the test-time adaptive merging line initiated by AdaMerging (Yang et al., 2024b), which learns task-vector coefficients via entropy minimization on unlabeled data. The key departure is twofold: (1) it replaces the unstable entropy proxy with an expert-guided self-labeling objective (drawing on pseudo-labeling, Lee et al., 2013), and (2) it jointly optimizes a single task-specific layer alongside the coefficients, rather than tuning coefficients alone. Conceptually, the paper reframes the merging objective from the non-interference / weight-disentanglement criterion of Ortiz-Jimenez et al. (2023) to a stronger synergy condition, supported by a convexity-based proposition under cross-task linearity (Zhou et al., 2024). The task-vector formulation of Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) remains the underlying parameterization throughout.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that cross-task functional alignment (not merely non-interference) is the structural driver of merge quality, the paper gives the model-merging community a more actionable design principle: adapt a single layer to the merged encoder and let the coefficients co-adapt. The disjoint-basin result (79.1% vs. 5–25% for baselines) shows the approach can bridge parameter-space gaps that invalidate the linearity assumption, opening a path toward merging models from heterogeneous pre-training. Limitations include dependence on expert quality (a corrupted or backdoored expert still degrades performance, though less than baselines) and the need for unlabeled target inputs. Open questions include whether the optimal single layer can be identified without exhaustive search, and how the method scales to very large task counts or generative models beyond the 8-task GLUE setting tested.
