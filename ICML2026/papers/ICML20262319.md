## Problem

Inference-time steering of diffusion and flow models via ratio-of-densities compositions (e.g., classifier-free guidance, product-of-experts) implicitly assumes the intermediate time-indexed marginals remain normalizable. The paper identifies Marginal Path Collapse, a failure mode where composing heterogeneous experts with mismatched noise schedules and/or negative exponents causes the intermediate density to become non-integrable despite valid endpoints, rendering the score field mathematically undefined and the sampler to transport an unintended distribution. No prior criterion existed to diagnose this collapse, and no correction mechanism preserved endpoint targets while restoring path validity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pretrained expert score and velocity networks (e.g., EDM for de-novo molecules, GeoDiff for conformers, DiffSBDD for pocket-conditioned generation) along with their respective noise schedules and user-specified guidance exponents, which may be negative (ratios) and time-varying. Experts of differing dimensionalities are embedded into a common ambient space via projection and embedding maps. The output is a batch of 3D molecules (scaffold decoration, fragment linking) or compositional images, sampled via importance-weighted particles with sequential Monte Carlo resampling; a lightweight variant (ACE-lite) omits resampling to cut inference time by ~44% at the cost of higher variance.

## Method

The paper contributes two theoretical tools and a sampling algorithm. First, the Path Existence Criterion (PEC) provides a sharp sufficient condition C_k(t) = Σ_{i: k∈I_i} γ_i(t)/α_t^(i)² > 0 for every coordinate k and timestep t, certifying normalizability of the composed intermediate density; violation (C_k(t) < 0) guarantees Marginal Path Collapse. Second, Adaptive Path Correction with Exponents (ACE) constructs time-varying exponent schedules via bump functions (quadratic and linear kernels) applied to a covering subset of positive-exponent experts, preserving endpoint exponent values while enforcing C(t) ≥ δ > 0 throughout the trajectory. The sampling dynamics extend the Feynman-Kac weighted SDE to time-dependent exponents by tracking auxiliary log-density processes for each expert and augmenting the importance-weight update with a term Σ γ̇_i(t) log q̃_t^(i)(X_t), which corrects for the distributional shift induced by the changing schedule. This reduces to standard FKC when all exponents are constant, and the resampling step eliminates out-of-distribution trajectories that persist in no-resampling heuristics.

## Results

On the CrossDocked2020 scaffold decoration benchmark, ACE at guidance scale ω=1.4 achieves an Optimization Success Rate (OSR) of 0.75 and average Vina docking score of −7.10 kcal/mol, substantially outperforming FKC (OSR 0.40, Vina −6.24) and NR (OSR 0.47, Vina −6.35) which both violate the Path Existence Criterion. On the synthetic 2D Checker benchmark, ACE (B₁=10) reduces W₁ distance to 0.20±0.04 and MMD to 0.012±0.003, a ~4× improvement over NR (W₁ 0.89, MMD 0.092) and a dramatic recovery from FKC's catastrophic failure (W₁ 1.37±1.09, MMD 0.419±0.579). On COCO-MIG compositional image generation, ACE improves attribute success rates by +9.6 percentage points over constant-exponent baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FKC (Skreta et al., 2025a), ACE wins decisively on all metrics at every guidance scale: at ω=1.4, ACE achieves OSR 0.75 vs. FKC's 0.40, Vina −7.10 vs. −6.24 kcal/mol, and QED 0.53 vs. 0.44, with the gap widening as ω increases because FKC's constant exponents trigger Marginal Path Collapse. Against the specialized fixed-pose baselines AutoFragDiff (Ghorbani et al., 2023) and Delete (Chen et al., 2025), ACE achieves a higher OSR (0.75 vs. 0.36 and 0.47) and better average Vina scores (−7.10 vs. −6.01 and −5.07), though AutoFragDiff and Delete retain advantages on drug-likeness (QED 0.57/0.55 vs. 0.53; SA 0.71/0.66 vs. 0.56), reflecting ACE's focus on docking affinity over synthetic accessibility. On the synthetic 2D Checker benchmark, ACE (B₁=10) reduces W₁ from 0.89 (NR) and 1.37 (FKC) to 0.20, and MMD from 0.092/0.419 to 0.012, confirming that the correction is not merely a resampling artifact but a genuine path-validity fix. For the fragment-linking task, ACE (OSR ≥ 0.45) remains competitive with the specialized FFLOM model (Jin et al., 2023) while outperforming FKC and NR which degrade due to path collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Feynman-Kac corrector framework (Skreta et al., 2025a) from constant-exponent, homogeneous compositions to general heterogeneous ratio-of-densities settings where experts use different noise schedules and operate on different dimensions. The key conceptual departure is identifying Marginal Path Collapse—a previously unacknowledged failure mode where intermediate densities become non-normalizable despite valid endpoints—and providing both a sharp diagnostic (the Path Existence Criterion) and a constructive repair (adaptive bump-function exponents). Unlike empirical time-varying guidance schedules (Wang et al., 2024b) that target homogeneous CFG, ACE derives its exponent schedule from a theoretical validity condition, making it applicable to negative exponents and partial-support compositions that prior methods cannot handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 41–80% of heterogeneous schedule combinations produce path collapse (depending on guidance scale) is a significant warning for the growing practice of composing pretrained diffusion experts in scientific domains like drug design. The Path Existence Criterion offers a cheap, analytical pre-check that can prevent wasted computation on mathematically invalid paths. Limitations include linear scaling with expert count, restriction to Gaussian-based interpolants on compactly supported targets, and the fact that ACE corrects the transport path but cannot repair semantically misaligned experts. Open questions include extending the criterion to non-Gaussian priors, discrete diffusion, and whether the bump-function protocol generalizes to more complex multi-constraint compositions beyond the two-bump structure studied here.
