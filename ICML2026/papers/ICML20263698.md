## Problem

Post-training quantization methods face a rigid coupling between compression rate and representational bit-width: achieving 8× compression forces a 2-bit representation with only four distinct values, causing functional collapse in data-free methods. Data-dependent alternatives (calibration, recovery fine-tuning) mitigate this but require training data and substantial compute that are often unavailable for instruction-tuned or reasoning models. No existing data-free method maintains functional performance at effective 2-bit rates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the raw weight matrices in Float16 or BFloat16 precision (no calibration data, no forward passes on data). It produces, per transformer block, a single ANS-compressed bitstream, a set of channel-wise scale parameters stored in BFloat16, and ANS frequency-table metadata. The bitstream is decompressed on-the-fly into a per-block buffer during inference, so the stored representation is a variable-length code rather than a fixed-width integer or float per weight.

## Method

EntQuant decouples storage cost from representational precision by quantizing weights to a high-precision base format (Float8 or Int8) and then minimizing the entropy of the quantized values via a rate-distortion objective: min d(W, Ŵ) + λ‖W_q‖₁, where the ℓ₁-norm serves as a differentiable surrogate for entropy. Only channel-wise scale parameters are optimized (via L-BFGS with the straight-through estimator), which reshapes the weight distribution toward lower entropy while preserving the full 256-level dynamic range of the base format. The resulting low-entropy byte stream is then losslessly compressed with a GPU-parallel ANS coder (nvCOMP), achieving arbitrary effective bit-rates down to ~2 bits per parameter. At inference, a block-wise decompression pipeline (following DFloat11) feeds the decoded Float8 weights into the Marlin GEMM kernel, adding only 1.5–2× latency over BFloat16 while retaining the expressivity that prevents the functional collapse seen in fixed 2-bit quantization.

## Results

On LLaMA-2 70B at 3 effective bits, EntQuant achieves C4 perplexity of 5.74 and 71.7% average zero-shot accuracy (only −1.6% relative to the 16-bit base of 72.8%). At 2.1 effective bits, EntQuant retains 68.6% accuracy (−5.8%) and C4 perplexity of 6.47, while all data-free baselines (HQQ) suffer functional collapse with perplexity exceeding 1000. Against calibration/fine-tuning methods at 2 bits, EntQuant at 2.1 bits (68.6%) is within 0.3% of EfficientQAT (68.9%) and 2.3% of QuIP# (70.9%), while GPTQ drops to 34.4% and OmniQuant to 54.9%. Compression of a 70B model completes in under 10 minutes on a single H100, versus 2–50 hours for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against data-free baselines, EntQuant's advantage is most dramatic at 2 bits: HQQ's C4 perplexity explodes to 111–6000+ across model sizes, while EntQuant at 2.1 bits maintains perplexity of 6.5–10.6 and 57–68% zero-shot accuracy. At 3 bits, EntQuant (C4 5.74, LM Eval 71.7%) slightly outperforms HQQ (C4 6.02–6.48, LM Eval 69.5–70.4%) and matches NF4 at 4 bits. Against calibration-based methods, EntQuant at 2.1 bits (68.6%) nearly ties EfficientQAT at 2 bits (68.9%) and trails QuIP# (70.9%) by only 2.3 points, while GPTQ (34.4%) and OmniQuant (54.9%) collapse. The critical practical distinction is that EntQuant requires no calibration data or recovery training (<10 min on H100), whereas QuIP# needs ~50 h on 8×A100 and EfficientQAT ~41 h on a single A100. On instruction-tuned models (IFEval, GSM8K CoT, GPQA, MMLU), EntQuant at 2 bits retains functional performance where prior work shows calibration-based methods degrade alignment and reasoning capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

EntQuant draws on the classical signal-processing pipeline of quantize-then-entropy-code (as in JPEG) and the rate-distortion optimization framework of entropy-constrained quantization (Chou et al., 1989), but applies it to LLM weight matrices for the first time with GPU-parallel ANS decoding integrated into the inference loop. It builds directly on DFloat11 (Zhang et al., 2025) for the block-wise decompression architecture and on ANS (Duda, 2013) as the coding primitive, but departs from both by explicitly optimizing quantized weights for low entropy via an ℓ₁-regularized Lagrangian rather than relying on the natural low entropy of exponent bits. In the PTQ landscape, it positions itself as a Level-1 (data-free) method that breaks the rigid coupling between storage cost and representational bit-width that limits GPTQ, AWQ, HQQ, and NF4 below 4 bits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EntQuant is the first purely data-free method to achieve functional extreme compression (effective ~2 bits) on large LLMs, matching or approaching calibration- and fine-tuning-based methods (QuIP#, EfficientQAT) while requiring no training data, no recovery fine-tuning, and under 10 minutes of compute — a critical advantage for instruction-tuned and reasoning models whose training corpora are inaccessible or legally restricted. The practical impact is substantial: a 70B model fits in ~19 GiB of VRAM, enabling deployment on consumer hardware. Key open questions include whether fused decompression-GEMM kernels can close the current 1.5–2× inference overhead, how the method extends to mixture-of-experts architectures, and whether extreme compression interacts adversely with safety alignment in ways not captured by standard benchmarks.
