## Problem

Existing Image-to-CAD and Point Cloud-to-CAD pipelines operate under a reconstruction paradigm that presupposes the prior existence of a target CAD model, making them unsuitable for the conceptual design stage where design intent is expressed only through incomplete, unstructured modalities like hand-drawn sketches and free-form text. This paper addresses the underexplored problem of directly mapping multi-level human intents—ranging from a single sketch to combined sketch-and-text inputs—into executable parametric CAD code from scratch, without any pre-existing model as reference.

## Input / Output

The method accepts four input configurations: a 512×512 hand-drawn sketch alone, a complete textual description alone, or either sketch combined with a partial or complete textual description. Textual descriptions are generated by a VLM pipeline (Qwen3-VL-32B) from the CadQuery code, a rendered image, and geometric attributes, then partially corrupted by randomly removing sentences to simulate incomplete user input. The output is a CadQuery Python script that, when executed, produces a parametric B-Rep or 3D mesh CAD model; the script must be syntactically valid and geometrically consistent to receive a positive reward.

## Method

HiCAD is a two-stage framework built on Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct. Stage 1 (CMTA) performs full-parameter supervised fine-tuning with the visual encoder frozen, interleaving data from all four intent-to-CAD tasks to promote cross-modal latent alignment and positive transfer between tasks. Stage 2 (SARL) applies reinforcement learning using Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO), which computes length-normalized sequence-level importance ratios rather than token-level ones, yielding more stable updates for long CadQuery code generation. The spatial-aware reward combines a topological consistency term (inverse graph edit distance on B-Rep face adjacency graphs) with a geometric consistency term (3D volume IoU), plus a validity gate that assigns a fixed penalty for non-executable code. Unlike prior RL-based CAD methods that optimize a single task, SARL is conducted jointly across all four tasks, preserving multi-task generation capability while improving geometric and topological fidelity.

## Results

On the HiCAD dataset, HiCAD achieves the best IoU across all four intent-to-CAD tasks: 69.49% (HDS-to-CAD), 74.27% (CTD-to-CAD), 76.03% (HDS & PTD-to-CAD), and 80.90% (HDS & CTD-to-CAD). The invalid rate (IR) is reduced to 0.17%–0.42%, an order of magnitude lower than the strongest CAD-specific baseline Cadrille (6.82%–9.41%). Mean Chamfer Distance ranges from 3.04 to 6.91 (×10⁻³), consistently outperforming all baselines. On real human-drawn sketches (Table 3), HiCAD achieves 43.27% IoU on HDS-to-CAD and 73.37% on HDS & CTD-to-CAD, substantially outperforming Cadrille (17.74% and 59.20%) and all general-purpose VLMs (<10% IoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiCAD outperforms all seven baselines across every task and metric. Against the strongest CAD-specific baseline Cadrille, HiCAD gains +2.95 IoU on HDS-to-CAD (69.49 vs 66.54), +7.08 on CTD-to-CAD (74.27 vs 67.19), +9.17 on HDS & PTD-to-CAD (76.03 vs 66.86), and +8.34 on HDS & CTD-to-CAD (80.90 vs 72.56), while reducing the invalid rate by roughly 20× (e.g., 0.37% vs 9.41% on HDS-to-CAD). Against CAD-Coder, the margins are even larger (e.g., +20.6 IoU on HDS-to-CAD, +16.3 on HDS & CTD-to-CAD). General-purpose VLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-5-mini, Gemini-2.5, Claude-3.7, Qwen-VL-Max) lag far behind, achieving IoU below 55% on the best task and IR as high as 54%, indicating they lack the domain-specific geometric reasoning required for parametric CAD code generation. On real human-drawn sketches, HiCAD's advantage over Cadrille widens further (43.27 vs 17.74 IoU on HDS-to-CAD), while all general VLMs drop below 10% IoU, confirming that domain-specific training with spatial-aware RL is critical for robustness to imperfect human inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant reconstruction paradigm in CAD generation (image-to-CAD, point-cloud-to-CAD) by targeting the conceptual design phase where no target CAD model pre-exists. It builds on the CadQuery-based sequence modeling line (DeepCAD, CAD-Coder, Cadrille) and the emerging RL-for-CAD trend (RLCAD, ReCAD, CADCrafter), but introduces two key departures: (i) a multi-task cooperative alignment stage that jointly trains on heterogeneous sketch-text inputs rather than single-modality SFT, and (ii) a GSPO-based spatial-aware RL stage that replaces token-level importance sampling with sequence-level optimization and a composite topological-geometric reward. The HiCAD dataset itself extends DeepCAD by adding hand-drawn sketches and code-oriented textual descriptions, filling a gap no prior dataset addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HiCAD dataset is the first to jointly align hand-drawn sketches, multi-level textual descriptions, and executable CadQuery code, providing a practical benchmark for the underexplored conceptual-design-to-CAD pipeline. The framework's ability to handle varying information completeness (partial vs. complete text, sketch-only vs. sketch+text) mirrors real design workflows, and the order-of-magnitude reduction in invalid code rates (e.g., 9.41% → 0.37% IR) addresses a critical practical barrier. Open questions remain: performance degrades on highly complex structures, the synthetic-to-real sketch domain gap persists, and the paper acknowledges that the four introduced tasks are not exhaustive of how humans express design intent. Future directions include iterative conversational refinement and explicit cross-modal alignment strategies.
