## Problem

Vision-language models suffer from Contextual Variable Overestimation (CVE), a systematic failure mode where sparse but decision-critical variables (e.g., a single country name) are statistically drowned out by high-volume signals (dense image patches, verbose product descriptions) during attention-based fusion, causing the model to produce market-invariant predictions that ignore the very instruction it should follow. No prior reward model or VLM framework explicitly addresses this representational imbalance for cross-market preference tasks.

## Input / Output

The reward model consumes a pair of product images (A and B), a textual product description (title and attributes), and a target country name, and outputs a binary preference label indicating which image historically achieved higher click-through rate in that market. Pairs with relative CTR difference below 0.1 are excluded to avoid label ambiguity. In the generation pipeline, the DGM takes country, product attributes, and an original product image to produce a textual background design, which is then fed into SDXL with a Canny-edge ControlNet to synthesize a market-tailored advertising image while preserving the original product layout.

## Method

GeoReward builds on Qwen2-VL-7B and implements a triple-gated pipeline derived from a Bayesian hierarchical evidence-integration framework. The Retrieval Gate (MA-RAG) performs two-stage retrieval (text-then-image) over the training set with a similarity threshold of 0.7, aggregates CTR evidence via position-aware weighting, and injects the resulting augmented prior as an embedding into the classifier input. The Consolidation Gate (CGVM) applies a FiLM-style affine transformation (scale γ and shift β) to all visual patch features, where the parameters are generated by a small two-layer FFN conditioned on the mean-pooled country token embedding, enabling country-specific visual adaptation without distorting pre-trained knowledge. The Sensitivity Gate (SSL) computes L2-norm-based focus scores for country, product, and image tokens in the final hidden states and applies a penalty only on mispredicted samples, encouraging the model to strengthen attention to under-attended critical variables. The total loss combines binary cross-entropy with a weighted penalty term (λ=0.1). For generation, GeoReward serves as a frozen preference scorer in a DPO loop that fine-tunes the Design Generation Model, with β=0.05 regularization, to produce country-adapted background designs.

## Results

On the MACP dataset (180K test samples, 10 countries), GeoReward achieves 60.37% accuracy and 40.84% sensitivity, outperforming the standard Qwen2-VL-7B fine-tuned baseline (55.60% accuracy, 36.73% sensitivity) by 4.77% and 4.11% respectively, and the collapsed Qwen2-VL-7B (44.61% accuracy, 20.82% sensitivity) by 15.76% and 20.02%. On the cross-domain ImageGen-CoT-Reward-5K generalization task, GeoReward reaches 67.9% accuracy and 24.0% sensitivity versus the UnifiedReward baseline's 62.7% and 1.9%. An online A/B test with 6M+ impressions confirms a 1.1% average CTR improvement (range: 0.7%–1.4%) for GeoReward-optimized images across all 10 markets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard Qwen2-VL-7B fine-tuned with an FC head (the closest direct baseline), GeoReward wins by 4.77% in accuracy (60.37% vs. 55.60%) and 4.11% in sensitivity (40.84% vs. 36.73%) on MACP, with consistent per-country gains (e.g., +5.01% on BR, +4.97% on FR, +4.81% on JP). The paper also shows that the raw Qwen2-VL-7B without any architectural modification collapses to 44.61% accuracy due to CVE, a 15.76% gap that GeoReward closes. On the cross-domain generalization task (ImageGen-CoT-Reward-5K), GeoReward outperforms the UnifiedReward baseline [Wang et al., 2025b] by 5.2% in accuracy (67.9% vs. 62.7%) and by a dramatic 22.1 percentage points in sensitivity (24.0% vs. 1.9%), demonstrating that the CVE problem and its mitigation generalize beyond the advertising domain. Additional baselines from Appendix K (prompt engineering at 49.77%, focal loss at 58.10% with only 12.02% sensitivity, logistic regression at 52.28%, GBDT at 55.39%) all fall short of GeoReward, confirming that no single simpler mechanism suffices to resolve the representational imbalance.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoReward builds on the Qwen2-VL backbone and repurposes the FiLM conditioning paradigm (Perez et al., 2017) as its Context-Guided Visual Modulation module, while extending retrieval-augmented generation into a two-stage text-then-image pipeline that aggregates CTR signals from retrieved neighbors. It departs from the broader multimodal reward model literature (UnifiedReward, VideoScore, LLaVA-Critic) by explicitly targeting the failure mode where sparse critical variables (e.g., country names) are drowned out by high-volume features during attention-based fusion. The RL fine-tuning stage follows the CAIG (Chen et al., 2025) pipeline of using a CTR reward model to guide VLM-generated background designs into a T2I model, but addresses CAIG's noted limitation of overlooking niche market segments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CVE diagnosis identifies a systematic architectural bias in autoregressive VLMs that likely affects any instruction-sensitive task with sparse critical cues (age, season, user segment), not just cross-market advertising. The MACP dataset (823K/180K samples, 10 countries) and the sensitivity metric provide a concrete benchmark for this under-studied failure mode. However, the full model's sensitivity remains modest (40.84%), the evaluation is confined to a single 7B backbone and primarily one domain, and scaling to multiple simultaneous sparse variables is left open. The 6M-impression A/B test (1.1% average CTR lift) adds practical credibility but the generalization evidence (image quality assessment) is preliminary.
