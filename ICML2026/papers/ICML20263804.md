## Problem

Existing accuracy-first (ex-post) differential privacy definitions have been shown to lack post-processing immunity, a fundamental property ensuring adversaries cannot weaken privacy guarantees by processing the output. The only prior definition with this property, pure ex-post privacy, lacks practical tools such as a Gaussian-mechanism analogue or a way to check accuracy on a private validation set. This paper identifies which definitions have post-processing immunity and proposes a new RDP-based ex-post definition that both has the property and supports the Brownian mechanism for practical use.

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes a private dataset split into disjoint training (X_y) and validation (X_ε) subsets, a sensitive function f with known ℓ₂-sensitivity Δ, an RDP order α, and an accuracy threshold. It adaptively releases noisy estimates of f(X) via the Brownian mechanism, increasing the privacy budget from a fixed list of ε values until the accuracy threshold is met on the private validation set (checked under DP via a Gaussian or SVT stopping rule). The output is the sequence of noisy estimates, the number of iterations t, and a data-dependent total privacy bound ε that is part of the mechanism's output rather than a separate function of it.

## Method

The paper introduces a new notation where the privacy bound ε is an explicit component of the mechanism's output (M(X) = (M(X)_y, M(X)_ε)), enabling a clean definition of post-processing via a dedicated operator ⊙. It then defines α-ex-post RDP (Definition 4.1), which bounds the moment-generating function of the privacy loss with ε inside the expectation, and proves it has post-processing immunity via the data-processing inequality. The Brownian mechanism is shown to satisfy ex-post RDP by proving its equivalence to a 'sequential precision-weighted Gaussian mechanism' that composes Gaussian releases with increasing budgets and precision-weighted averaging. Composition theorems (Theorems 4.4–4.6) support adaptive sequences of mechanisms and a data-dependent stopping rule on a separate private validation set, enabling the practical workflow of iteratively releasing synthetic data or finetuning a classifier until an accuracy threshold is met while minimising the spent privacy budget.

## Results

On the Adult dataset, the Brownian mechanism with ex-post RDP (α=20) successfully minimises the privacy budget ε while meeting a minimum accuracy threshold on a private validation set, with ε values logarithmically spaced between 0.01 and 1. The plain Gaussian threshold check generally achieves higher validation accuracies, while the sparse vector technique (SVT) check yields smaller final ε values. In the image classifier finetuning experiment (Appendix E, CIFAR-10), the mechanism similarly adjusts the privacy bound until an accuracy threshold is met. The ex-post RDP-based privacy bound for the Brownian mechanism matches the linear bound of Whitehouse et al. (2022) within floating-point error when free parameters are optimised, confirming no utility loss from the added post-processing immunity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the probabilistic ex-post privacy framework of Whitehouse et al. (2022). Theoretically, the paper proves that Whitehouse et al.'s δ-probabilistic ex-post privacy (δ>0) does NOT have post-processing immunity, while the proposed α-ex-post RDP does — a critical distinction since without PPI, an adversary can weaken the privacy guarantee via post-processing. In terms of practical utility (Figure 1), the ex-post RDP-based bound for the Brownian mechanism is numerically identical to Whitehouse et al.'s linear bound within floating-point error when all free parameters are optimised per σ value, and very close to their mixture bound. When using Whitehouse et al.'s recommended fixed-σ calibration procedure, their bounds become suboptimal for other σ values, while the RDP bound remains tight. Thus, the paper wins decisively on the theoretical property of post-processing immunity while tying on empirical privacy-utility trade-offs, meaning the added guarantee comes at no cost to utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the accuracy-first differential privacy framework initiated by Ligett et al. (2017) and the Brownian mechanism of Whitehouse et al. (2022) by introducing a new notation that makes the privacy bound part of the mechanism's output, enabling a clean definition of post-processing immunity. It builds directly on Mironov's (2017) Rényi DP to propose an ex-post RDP definition, and on the data-processing inequality to prove composition and post-processing theorems. The key departure from prior work is the identification of post-processing immunity as a critical, previously unaddressed property: the paper shows that while pure ex-post privacy retains it, the more practical probabilistic ex-post privacy of Whitehouse et al. does not, motivating the RDP-based alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that probabilistic ex-post privacy lacks post-processing immunity is significant because it means the most widely used practical relaxation of ex-post privacy provides a weaker guarantee than advertised, undermining its use in applications where outputs are further processed. The proposed ex-post RDP definition fills this gap while retaining compatibility with the Brownian mechanism and providing composition theorems (including a data-dependent stopping rule on a private validation set) that enable practical ML workflows. Open questions include whether approximate ex-post privacy has post-processing immunity, whether ex-post GDP or f-DP can be developed for tighter conversions, and how to meaningfully compare mechanisms whose privacy bounds are random variables dependent on private data.
