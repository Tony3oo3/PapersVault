## Problem

While upper-bound complexity analyses for bilevel optimization are well-developed, nontrivial lower bounds under standard first-order oracles have been largely missing. Existing lower bounds either rely on second-order oracles, assume convexity of the hyper-objective, use non-standard y*-aware oracles that reduce the problem to single-level optimization, or fail to capture the dependence on the lower-level condition number κ. This paper establishes the first lower bounds for smooth nonconvex-strongly-convex bilevel optimization under standard (stochastic) first-order oracles that explicitly reflect the condition number.

## Input / Output

The analysis considers bilevel problems where the upper-level function f is L_f-smooth and nonconvex, the lower-level function g is L_g-smooth and μ-strongly convex in y (with condition number κ = L_g/μ), and algorithms have access only to (stochastic) first-order oracles for both f and g. The output is a lower bound on the number of oracle calls required by any zero-respecting first-order algorithm to find an ε-stationary point of the hyper-objective H(x) = f(x; y*(x)): Ω(κ^{3/2} ε^{-2}) in the deterministic case and Ω(κ^{5/2} ε^{-4}) in the stochastic case with bounded variance σ². The bounds hold uniformly over all first-order bilevel algorithms including single-loop, double-loop, penalty-based, and primal-dual methods.

## Method

The hard instance couples a tri-diagonal 1-D discrete Laplacian matrix A (from Nesterov's strongly-convex construction) in the lower-level function g with Ψ-Φ hardness functions (from Carmon et al.'s nonconvex construction) in the upper-level function f, arranged across T+1 blocks of n-dimensional y-variables. The bilevel coupling creates a sequential zero-chain: activating the n-th coordinate of y^{(i)} requires n iterations (due to A's tri-diagonal structure), and activating x_i requires y_n^{(i)} to be nonzero (since ∂g/∂x_i = -y_n^{(i)}), which in turn activates y_1^{(i+1)} through the Ψ-Φ coupling in f. This forces at least Tn iterations to reach the end of the chain, yielding the κ^{3/2} dependence since n = Θ(√κ) and T = Θ(κ^{1/2} ε^{-2}). For the stochastic setting, the same deterministic instance is restricted to bounded hypercubes to control gradient variance, and a probability-p zero-chain is constructed by perturbing only the next-to-activate coordinate with a Bernoulli(p) gate, giving the additional 1/p factor that produces the κ^{5/2} ε^{-4} bound.

## Results

In the deterministic setting, the paper proves that any first-order zero-respecting algorithm requires at least Ω(κ^{3/2} ε^{-2}) oracle calls to find an ε-accurate stationary point for smooth nonconvex-strongly-convex bilevel optimization. In the stochastic setting, at least Ω(κ^{5/2} ε^{-4}) stochastic oracle calls are necessary under bounded variance. These bounds improve upon the best known lower bounds for single-level nonconvex optimization (Ω(ε^{-2}) deterministic, Ω(ε^{-4}) stochastic) and for nonconvex-strongly-concave min-max optimization (Ω(√κ ε^{-2}) deterministic, Ω(κ^{1/3} ε^{-4}) stochastic) by factors of κ^{3/2} and κ^{5/2}, respectively. A gap of order κ^{2} remains between the deterministic lower bound and the best known upper bound of κ^{3.5} ε^{-2} (via Nesterov-accelerated penalty methods).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's deterministic lower bound of Ω(κ^{3/2} ε^{-2}) strictly dominates the Ω(ε^{-2}) bound for single-level nonconvex optimization (Carmon et al., 2020) by a factor of κ^{3/2}, and the Ω(√κ ε^{-2}) bound for nonconvex-strongly-concave min-max optimization (Li et al., 2021) by a factor of κ. In the stochastic setting, the Ω(κ^{5/2} ε^{-4}) bound improves upon the Ω(ε^{-4}) single-level bound by κ^{5/2} and the Ω(κ^{1/3} ε^{-4}) min-max bound by κ^{13/6}. Compared to Kwon et al. (2024), who establish Ω(ε^{-6}) under a y*-aware oracle, this work's bound applies to the more standard first-order oracle model and recovers Ω(ε^{-6}) only in the special regime L_g = O(ε). The paper also notes that its construction is simpler than the concurrent work of Chen & Zhang (2025), which introduces an additional auxiliary variable z, and that the stochastic construction uses bounded hypercubes rather than eliminating the coupling variable y.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-chain lower-bound construction paradigm from single-level nonconvex optimization (Carmon et al., 2020) and strongly-convex optimization (Nesterov et al., 2018) to the bilevel setting, combining the tri-diagonal Laplacian matrix for the strongly-convex lower level with the Ψ/Φ hardness functions for the nonconvex upper level. It departs from prior bilevel lower bounds (Ji & Liang, 2023; Kwon et al., 2024; Dagréou et al., 2024) by working with standard first-order oracles rather than second-order oracles, y*-aware oracles, or finite-sum structures, and by handling the nonconvex hyper-objective without convexity assumptions. The stochastic construction adapts the probability-p zero-chain framework of Arjevani et al. (2023) and Li et al. (2021), using bounded hypercubes to control gradient variance without modifying the deterministic instance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

These lower bounds establish that nonconvex-strongly-convex bilevel optimization is provably harder than both single-level nonconvex optimization and min-max optimization, with condition-number dependencies (κ^{3/2} deterministic, κ^{5/2} stochastic) that are substantially larger than in either comparison setting. However, large gaps remain between these lower bounds and the best known upper bounds (e.g., a κ^2 gap in the deterministic case), leaving the optimal complexity open. The authors suggest that studying the simpler quadratic lower-level case may be a productive intermediate step, and that an Ω(ε^{-6}) stochastic lower bound under standard smoothness constants remains unresolved.
