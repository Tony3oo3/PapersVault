## Problem

Current WSI analysis pipelines rely on patch-level feature extraction and aggregation, which dilutes cell-centric diagnostic cues such as subtle nuclear atypia and sparse micro-metastatic foci. Existing methods also lack a unified framework that simultaneously supports heterogeneous local tasks (segmentation, subtyping) and global tasks (diagnosis, prognosis) while providing faithful, decision-aligned evidence rather than untrustworthy attention heatmaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262993/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, Cello consumes a single H&E whole slide image tiled into patches; Cellpose-SAM segments cell instances within each patch, and a Transformer encoder produces per-cell tokens and a patch-level CLS token. During training, paired multi-channel mIF images from the same tissue section supply P-dimensional protein vectors per cell to supervise the H&E encoder via contrastive alignment. Outputs span three granularities: slide-level diagnosis/grade (softmax over gated-attention MIL), patch-level Gleason pattern labels via prototype matching, and cell-resolved evidence maps that localize micro-lesions within high-scoring patches.

## Method

Cello follows a cell-first route: Cellpose-SAM detects cell ROIs in each H&E patch, a Transformer encoder extracts cell tokens and a cross-attention CLS token, and bidirectional InfoNCE contrastive alignment with paired mIF protein signals (at both cell-token and CLS-token resolutions) injects molecularly grounded cues into H&E representations without requiring nucleus-type labels. A scalable prototype bank (K-means-initialized, R prototypes per class) is trained with a prototype-to-instance contrastive loss to make patch CLS tokens discriminable for fine-grained local semantics, enabling plug-and-play extension to new lesion types. Slide-level prediction uses gated-attention MIL (ABMIL-style) over the cell-aware patch tokens. A decision-route consistency objective then calibrates global attention by shifting logits with prototype-derived per-patch evidence scores and enforcing KL divergence between the evidence distribution and calibrated attention, while simultaneously back-propagating local contrastive gradients into the cell-wise encoder to sharpen micro-lesion sensitivity at the aggregation stage.

## Results

Cello achieves the highest AUC across all eight evaluation settings (4 datasets × diagnosis/grading), with headline values of 0.978/0.950 on PANDA, 0.941/0.900 on 4500PCas, 0.857/0.832 on CPTAC-CCRCC, and 0.937/0.916 on TCGA-CRC. Compared to the strongest pathology foundation model (PFM) baselines, Cello gains 0.6%–6.0% AUC, with the largest margins on grading tasks (e.g., +6.0% on 4500PCas grading, +4.3% on TCGA-CRC grading). Against the best non-PFM baselines (GNN/MIL), improvements range from 7.4% to 16.0% AUC. On micro-cancer localization (Hit@Top-10%), Cello reaches 72.5% on Stomach40 versus <30% for the best PFM baseline, and achieves 100% Hit@Top-50% on Liver52.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cello outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and task. Against GNN-based methods (Tea-Graph, Patch-GCN, TransGNN), Cello leads by 7.4–16.0% AUC, with the largest gap on 4500PCas grading (+16.0% over Patch-GCN's 0.776). Among MIL-based aggregators (CLAM, ABMIL, TransMIL, ZoomMIL), the best baseline is ZoomMIL (0.860 on PANDA diagnosis), still trailing Cello by 12.7% AUC; CLAM is weakest on CPTAC-CCRCC (0.579 diagnosis) where Cello reaches 0.857. Among PFM baselines, CHIEF is the strongest competitor on PANDA diagnosis (0.953) and TCGA-CRC diagnosis (0.896), but Cello surpasses it by 2.6% and 4.6% respectively; UNI leads on PANDA grading (0.944) yet Cello still gains 0.6%. The most striking margin is on 4500PCas grading, where Cello (0.900) exceeds the best PFM (CONCH, 0.849) by 6.0% AUC, reflecting the benefit of cell-level evidence for subtle morphological cues. On micro-cancer Hit@Top-10%, Cello (72.5% Stomach40, 83.7% Prostate104, 55.8% Liver52) surpasses the best PFM (CONCH at 54.8% Prostate104) by 18.9–60.0 percentage points, demonstrating that patch-level heatmaps from PFMs fundamentally cannot resolve intra-patch micro-lesions.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cello sits at the intersection of three established lines: (1) gated-attention MIL for WSI aggregation (ABMIL, CLAM, TransMIL), which it adopts as the global-level backbone; (2) pathology foundation models (UNI, CONCH, CHIEF, CTrans) that provide strong patch-level representations but remain patch-first in abstraction; and (3) cell/nucleus-centric and graph-based WSI methods that capture fine-grained morphology but require heavy supervision (nuclear masks, cell categories) or incur prohibitive graph-construction costs at gigapixel scale. The key departure is a 'cell-first' paradigm: cell representations are learned under paired H&E–mIF contrastive supervision (InfoNCE at token and cls levels) and aggregated into patch summaries via cross-attention, injecting molecularly grounded cellular cues without explicit nucleus-type labels or explicit graph edges. The prototype-based local module extends prior prototype-MIL work (Tai et al., 2025) into a scalable, extensible bank that supports heterogeneous local semantics, while the decision-route consistency objective is novel in explicitly aligning cell evidence, local prototype matching, and global attention into a single calibrated pathway.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and clinically important gap: patch-level abstraction dilutes micro-lesion evidence (sparse tumor budding, subtle nuclear atypia) that pathologists rely on, and existing interpretable MIL methods do not enforce cross-scale agreement among cell, local, and global evidence. The unified cell–local–global framework with a plug-and-play prototype bank offers a concrete path toward multi-granularity pathology AI that can serve both slide-level diagnosis and region-level localization without redesigning the backbone. Key limitations temper the impact: the cell-wise learning stage requires paired H&E–mIF data (even if only hundreds of pairs), which is not universally available; the micro-lesion detection results, while striking, are evaluated on very small private cohorts (40–104 WSIs); and the current 'cell-level' reasoning operates at the token level within Cellpose-SAM-detected regions rather than at true instance-adaptive geometry. Open questions include noise-aware prototype learning for subjective grading, extending to instance-adaptive cell embeddings, and prospective clinical validation.
