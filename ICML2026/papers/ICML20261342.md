## Problem

Mean-field Langevin dynamics (MFLD) minimize entropy-regularized functionals on the Wasserstein space but cannot handle constrained domains, since the global diffusion term causes mass to escape the constraint set. While mirror Langevin dynamics solve constrained sampling by imposing a non-flat geometry via a barrier function, no prior work extends this to the interacting (mean-field) particle setting. This paper fills that gap by proposing the first algorithm with global convergence guarantees for constrained distributional optimization over general convex subsets of R^d.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the space of probability measures P₂(X) over a convex set X ⊆ R^d, taking as input a linearly convex functional F, a thrice-differentiable strictly convex barrier function φ (whose Hessian defines the local geometry), a temperature parameter λ, and N initial particle positions. The output is a collection of N particles whose empirical distribution approximates the unique minimizer μ* ∝ exp(−(1/λ) δF(μ*)/δμ). Unlike standard MFLD which works on R^d, all operations (drift, diffusion, convergence analysis) are defined with respect to the local Hessian metric ∇²φ, and the constraint is enforced geometrically rather than via projection.

## Method

The Mirror Mean-Field Langevin Dynamics (MMFLD) is a McKean-Vlasov SDE defined in primal-dual form: the drift term uses the first variation of F evaluated at the current empirical measure μ_t, while the diffusion term uses the Hessian of the barrier function ∇²φ(X_t), ensuring particles remain in X without projection. The corresponding Fokker-Planck PDE is a nonlinear diffusion with Hessian-dependent covariance, and convergence is established via a uniform mirror log-Sobolev inequality yielding linear rates in the objective gap. For the finite-particle discretization, the paper uses the forward scheme of Ahn & Chewi (2021) (discretizing drift but not diffusion) and proves propagation-of-chaos bounds that are uniform in time and independent of the LSI constant, extending the sharpest known MFLD bounds to the constrained setting. The analysis further extends to stochastic gradients with an additional σ²/c₂ error term, where c₂ is the minimum eigenvalue of ∇²φ.

## Results

The paper provides linear convergence guarantees (rate e^{-2C_LSI·λt}) for the continuous-time MMFLD and non-asymptotic bounds for the discretized algorithm, with a particle approximation error of O(LR²/N) independent of the LSI constant. Empirically, across all three constrained optimization settings (simplex, spectraplex, and norm-constrained neural network), MMFLD consistently achieves significantly lower final loss than projected MFLD. On the XOR classification task (d=2, N=512, T=100), MMFLD continues decreasing loss after 100 epochs while projected MFLD stagnates after approximately 30–50 epochs. High-dimensional experiments (d=100 simplex, d=50 spectraplex, d=100 network) confirm the same qualitative gains, with the simplex experiment completing in under 10 minutes on a single GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is projected MFLD, which applies standard mean-field Langevin dynamics (Eq. 8) followed by projection onto the constraint set after each update. MMFLD wins decisively in all three experimental settings. On the unit simplex (d=3, 50K particles), projected MFLD exhibits characteristic boundary mass accumulation that leads to excess error, while MMFLD maintains a well-behaved interior distribution and reaches a significantly lower loss; adding a small barrier (β=10⁻⁴) further widens the gap. On the spectraplex (d=10, N=2048), projected MFLD makes very little progress because the eigendecomposition-based projection roughly cancels the gradient step, whereas MMFLD with the von Neumann mirror map converges smoothly. On the norm-constrained two-layer ReLU network for XOR classification (d=2, N=512), projected MFLD stagnates after 30–50 epochs and saturates the norm constraint, scattering neurons over a wide region, while MMFLD aligns neurons tightly in the XOR directions and continues reducing both training and test loss. These advantages persist in high-dimensional settings (Appendix D), confirming that the mirror geometry provides a structural advantage over naive projection for constrained distributional optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: mirror Langevin dynamics (Hsieh et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2020; Ahn & Chewi, 2021) for constrained sampling, and mean-field Langevin dynamics (Nitanda et al., 2022; Chizat, 2022) for unconstrained distributional optimization of interacting particle systems. The key technical contribution is showing that the entropy sandwich inequality, proximal Gibbs distribution framework, and recent LSI-constant-free propagation of chaos bounds (Nitanda, 2024; Chewi et al., 2024; Nitanda et al., 2025) all carry over to the constrained setting with only the replacement of Euclidean norms by mirror-map-dependent local norms. The discretization scheme follows the forward (drift-only) discretization of Ahn & Chewi (2021), and the self-concordance assumption is borrowed from interior-point and Riemannian geometry literature (Nesterov & Nemirovskii, 1994; Gatmiry & Vempala, 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm with global (non-asymptotic) convergence guarantees for entropy-regularized distributional optimization over a general convex domain, a setting that arises naturally in norm-constrained neural networks, trajectory inference, Wasserstein barycenter computation, and signal deconvolution. The paper identifies the mirror Poincaré inequality as the natural next step (replacing the mirror LSI), which would be more geometrically principled but requires developing mean-field Langevin convergence under Poincaré-type conditions. A practical limitation is that the analysis requires a self-concordant barrier with parameter O(d), and the empirical gains, while consistent, are demonstrated on relatively small-scale problems (d ≤ 100, N ≤ 10K particles).
