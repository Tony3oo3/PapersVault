## Problem

Autoregressive (AR) modeling has not been successfully applied to protein backbone generation due to two fundamental conflicts: extending AR to continuous 3D coordinates typically requires discretization that degrades structural fidelity, and the strong bidirectional spatial dependencies between residues (e.g., distant-in-sequence but close-in-space contacts) violate the unidirectional assumption of standard next-token prediction. Additionally, AR models suffer from exposure bias—training on ground-truth context but generating on self-predictions—which substantially degrades structure quality.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, PAR takes only a target sequence length L (32–256 residues) and produces a full Cα backbone as an L×3 coordinate matrix. For conditional generation, it accepts sparse structural prompts (e.g., 16 3D points specifying a coarse layout) or motif atom coordinates, which are teacher-forced at the corresponding scale during autoregressive upsampling. The generation proceeds through n discrete scales (default {64, 128, 256}), where each scale is an interpolated subset of the full backbone, and the final output is the full-resolution Cα structure.

## Method

PAR decomposes protein backbone generation into a coarse-to-fine autoregressive process via next-scale prediction: a non-equivariant AR transformer (60M) encodes all prior coarser scales (concatenated along the sequence dimension with interpolated position embeddings) to produce a scale-wise conditional embedding z^i, which conditions a shared flow-matching decoder (up to 400M) to generate Cα coordinates at scale i directly in continuous space. This design avoids both discretization (by using flow matching rather than VQ tokens) and unidirectional residue ordering (by modeling all residues within a scale jointly). To mitigate exposure bias, PAR employs noisy context learning (corrupting ground-truth prior-scale inputs with Gaussian noise during training) and scheduled sampling (replacing ground-truth context with the model's own predictions at 50% probability). At inference, the model iterates n autoregressive steps, and a multi-scale orchestration of SDE sampling at the coarsest scale followed by ODE sampling at finer scales yields a 2.5× inference speedup over single-scale baselines while preserving designability.

## Results

On the unconditional backbone generation benchmark, PAR (400M) with γ=0.45 achieves 88.0% designability, 1.28 Å sc-RMSD, and 231.5 FPSD vs. PDB / 211.8 FPSD vs. AFDB. After PDB fine-tuning, PAR_pdb reaches 96.6% designability and 161.0 FPSD vs. PDB, the best distributional fidelity among all compared methods. PAR also achieves 2.5× sampling speedup over the single-scale Proteina 400-step baseline by orchestrating SDE at the coarsest scale and ODE at finer scales (400/2/2 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAR (400M, γ=0.45) outperforms Proteina (400M) by 39.8 FPSD points vs. PDB (231.5 vs. 271.3) and 60.8 points vs. AFDB (211.8 vs. 272.6), while maintaining comparable designability (88.0% vs. 92.6%). Against Genie2 (16M), PAR improves FPSD vs. PDB by 118.5 points (231.5 vs. 350.0) and vs. AFDB by 102.0 points (211.8 vs. 313.8). RFDiffusion (60M) is beaten by 22.2 FPSD points vs. PDB (231.5 vs. 253.7). FrameDiff (17M) achieves a lower FPSD vs. PDB (194.2) but at the cost of substantially lower designability (65.4% vs. 88.0%), indicating it captures distributional shape poorly in terms of foldable structures. ESM3 (1.4B), a token-based protein language model, is far outperformed on all metrics (933.9 vs. 231.5 FPSD vs. PDB; 22.0% vs. 88.0% designability). After PDB fine-tuning, PAR_pdb achieves the best FPSD vs. PDB (161.0) and highest designability (96.6%) among all methods, surpassing even FrameDiff's distributional score while matching Proteina's designability.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAR combines three established ideas into a new protein-specific framework: the next-scale prediction ordering from VAR (Tian et al., 2024), the AR-transformer-conditions-continuous-decoder paradigm from MAR (Li et al., 2024), and flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) for the backbone decoder, all built on Proteina's (Geffner et al., 2025) non-equivariant transformer architecture. It departs from prior autoregressive protein work (Gaujac et al., 2024) by avoiding discrete structure tokens and from standard residue-wise AR by using multi-scale coarse-to-fine ordering that respects bidirectional spatial dependencies. The framework is deliberately general: at a single scale it reduces to a standard flow-based model, making it a strict superset of Proteina-style approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAR's multi-scale formulation unlocks zero-shot conditional generation (point-prompted layout, motif scaffolding) without the fine-tuning or auxiliary conditioning that diffusion/flow baselines require, which is a practical advantage for protein designers who want flexible control. The 2.5× sampling speedup via SDE/ODE orchestration across scales is a concrete efficiency win. Open questions include whether the framework extends to all-atom generation, conformational ensembles, and multi-chain systems, and whether the exposure-bias limitation on AR module scaling can be resolved with more data or better training strategies. Long-protein generation (500–700 residues) still degrades significantly, suggesting the current training data is insufficient for that regime.
