## Problem

Multi-agent path finding (MAPF) is combinatorial because assignment, path planning, and scheduling are coupled, and prior flow-network or ILP formulations do not explicitly characterize solution integrality as a polyhedral property of the underlying LP. Moreover, the natural multi-marginal optimal transport (MMOT) formulation over path spaces has an exponentially large state space (K^{T+1} variables), making it intractable for large-scale instances. This paper closes both gaps by showing that the Markovian structure of robot motion collapses the MMOT to a polynomial-size LP with total unimodularity, and then develops a Schrödinger-bridge-based entropic relaxation for scalability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite connected graph (typically a W×H grid with obstacles), N robot initial positions, N target positions, a time horizon T, and per-edge cost parameters (move cost, wait cost at targets vs. non-targets). It produces T transport matrices Π_t ∈ {0,1}^{K×K} encoding collision-free, non-overlapping robot trajectories, where each entry π_{ij,t}=1 means one robot moves from vertex i to j at time t. In the scalable pipeline, the output additionally includes a pruned subgraph retaining roughly 40–60% of the original edges, on which the final LP is solved to yield a near-optimal integral transport with <10% cost degradation.

## Method

The key insight is that robot motion is Markovian, so the joint path-space tensor P factorizes into T pairwise transport matrices Π_t, reducing the MMOT from K^{T+1} to K²T variables. The resulting LP (P1) enforces gluing (conservation), boundary, and vertex-capacity constraints; its constraint matrix is shown to be totally unimodular via a node-splitting construction on the time-expanded graph, guaranteeing integral {0,1} extreme points without explicit integer constraints. For scalability, MAPF is recast as a discrete Schrödinger bridge with a Gibbs-kernel reference distribution, which reduces to an entropic regularization (P2) solvable by multi-marginal Sinkhorn iterations in O(KT) per sweep. The fractional shadow transport from P2 identifies high-probability edges, enabling principled graph pruning; a modified LP (P3) with a linearized KL penalty toward the shadow is then solved on the pruned graph, preserving total unimodularity and recovering integral, near-optimal trajectories at 5–7× speedup.

## Results

The optimal P1 LP solves anonymous MAPF to exact integrality in polynomial time, with solve time scaling as O(K^1.68) across 162 runs on grids up to 22,500 vertices (3.4M LP variables). The scalable P2+P3 pipeline (Sinkhorn shadow + pruned LP) scales nearly linearly at O(K^1.15), achieving 3.6×–7.1× speedup over P1 with a cost gap consistently below 10% (median 6.4%) while retaining only 32–43% of edges. On the 30×30 grid benchmark, P1 solves 300 agents in 0.54s and P2+P3 in 0.49s at 0.63% gap, both at 100% success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against CBM (Conflict-Based Min-Cost-Flow) and an ILP-based solver from Ma & Koenig (2016) on 30×30 grids with 10% obstacles. The proposed P1 LP solves 300 agents (6× more than the baselines) in 0.54s at 100% success, versus CBM's 5.32s for only 50 agents and the ILP's 162s at just 4% success rate. The scalable P2+P3 pipeline further reduces solve time to 0.49s while maintaining 100% success and a negligible 0.63% cost gap. The baselines are multi-team TAPF formulations, so the comparison is not perfectly apples-to-apples (single-team anonymous MAPF is simpler), but the 10×+ speedup at 6× more agents demonstrates the structural advantage of the total-unimodularity-based LP over conflict-based search and integer programming approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: the multi-marginal optimal transport (MMOT) literature (Pass 2015; Lin et al. 2022; Haasler et al. 2023) and the MAPF literature (Stern 2019; Sharon et al. 2015; Yu & LaValle 2013). While prior flow-network formulations (Yu & LaValle 2013; Ma 2020) implicitly rely on network-flow integrality, this paper explicitly establishes total unimodularity of the MAPF LP from first principles via a node-splitting construction on a time-expanded graph, and frames the problem as a Markovian MMOT whose exponential tensor collapses to a polynomial-size LP. The Schrödinger bridge connection (Léonard 2014; Pavon & Ticozzi 2010; Haasler et al. 2021) is then used to derive an entropic relaxation solvable by Sinkhorn iterations (Cuturi 2013; Di Marino & Gerolin 2020), which prior MAPF work had not exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The total unimodularity result means anonymous MAPF can be solved as a plain LP in polynomial time with guaranteed integral, collision-free solutions—eliminating the need for integer programming or ad-hoc conflict resolution (CBS, SAT solvers). The Schrödinger-bridge-to-Sinkhorn pipeline provides a principled, probabilistic relaxation that achieves 5–7× speedup at under 10% cost degradation on instances up to 3.4M variables, making large-scale warehouse-style coordination tractable. Open questions include extending the TU guarantees to non-anonymous (labeled) MAPF, handling dynamic or online settings, and whether the shadow-transport pruning can be made adaptive to heterogeneous cost landscapes without the fixed-threshold infeasibility observed at extreme ε values.
