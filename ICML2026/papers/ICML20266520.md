## Problem

Generative AI can produce pixels, waveforms, and tokens on demand, yet the models that perform this generation are still constructed through repeated, costly optimization for every new task or user. This position paper argues that trained neural network checkpoints form a structured, learnable data modality and that the field lacks a standardized framework, unified taxonomy, and principled evaluation protocol for generative modeling in weight space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266520/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The proposed framework formulates weight generation as conditional sampling p(W|A,C,R), where A specifies the architecture graph and tensor schema, C encodes a task prompt, dataset descriptor, user context, or image identity embedding, and R captures training-recipe and checkpoint-lineage metadata. The generator's behavior shifts across three regimes: when A is fixed it models parameter values, when C varies it performs conditional adaptation (e.g., producing a LoRA adapter from a text prompt), and when A varies it must also map generated tensors to operator types, shapes, and connectivity. Outputs range from low-rank personalization adapters (tens of millions of parameters) to full model checkpoints (up to ~200M parameters for vision backbones, and 0.72B LoRA parameters for an 80B multimodal foundation model).

## Method

As a position and survey paper, the core contribution is a standardized five-stage pipeline—tokenization, embedding, generative predictor, training strategy, and evaluation—that organizes the rapidly growing weight-generation literature into a coherent research stack grounded in six geometric properties of weight space (mode connectivity, permutation symmetry, flatness and low intrinsic dimension, optimizer-induced implicit bias, modularity, and shared representation subspaces). The paper surveys predictor families (hypernetworks, graph-conditioned predictors, weight-space diffusion, normalizing flows, and hybrid recurrent-plus-diffusion stacks) and maps each to the regime where it is practically strongest, with diffusion-based and hybrid approaches currently leading for mid-scale full-weight generation. A three-axis evaluation framework (within-architecture generation, conditional adaptation, cross-architecture generation) is proposed to replace single-metric accuracy benchmarks with multi-axis reporting covering novelty beyond interpolation baselines, robustness, calibration, memorization, and safety. The paper identifies four structural barriers—representation alignment under symmetry, long-range cross-layer dependencies, checkpoint heterogeneity, and memorization/provenance risk—as the key obstacles preventing current adapter-scale successes from extending to unrestricted frontier-scale full checkpoint synthesis.

## Results

This is a position paper surveying weight-space generation methods rather than reporting a single experimental result. Key quantitative claims from surveyed systems include: RPG generates a ConvNeXt-L backbone matching fully trained ImageNet accuracy (within a few tenths of a percent) in under 90 seconds on a single GPU; GHN predicts all 24M weights of a ResNet-50 in one forward pass, achieving 50% ImageNet top-5 and 60% CIFAR-10 top-1 without any SGD; HyperDreamBooth produces subject-specific personalization in ~20 seconds (25× faster than DreamBooth); and D2NWG matches standard transfer-learning accuracy while improving few-shot performance by ~6%. At the frontier, HY-WU demonstrates conditional LoRA generation (0.72B params) for an 80B multimodal foundation-model backbone using an 8.11B generator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not propose a single new method to benchmark against baselines; instead it surveys and compares existing weight-generation approaches against traditional adaptation paradigms. The central comparison is weight generation vs. iterative fine-tuning/PEFT: Liang et al. (2025) show that generated LoRA adapters match or exceed standard fine-tuning on zero-shot tasks (reasoning puzzles, multimodal queries) while operating in seconds rather than hours. RPG-generated ConvNeXt-L matches conventional training accuracy on ImageNet, and D2NWG matches transfer-learning accuracy while improving few-shot by ~6%. However, the paper also flags a critical negative result: Zeng et al. (2026) report that several representative weight generators produce replicas or simple interpolations of training checkpoints and fail to outperform noise or ensemble baselines when novelty and performance are required jointly. Against non-generative baselines such as model soups (Wortsman et al., 2022) and aligned interpolation (Git Re-Basin, Ainsworth et al., 2023), the paper argues that current generators have not yet demonstrated consistent superiority, positioning the field as promising but not yet decisively ahead of simpler weight-averaging strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper synthesizes a scattered but rapidly growing literature—HyperNetworks (Ha et al., 2017), graph-conditioned weight predictors (Knyazev et al., 2021), weight-space diffusion (Wang et al., 2024; Soro et al., 2025), and prompt-to-LoRA generation (Liang et al., 2025)—into a unified conditional-sampling framework p(W|A,C,R) with a standardized five-stage pipeline. Rather than proposing a new generator, it repurposes optimization-theory results (mode connectivity, permutation symmetry, flatness, implicit bias) to explain *why* weight space is generatively tractable, and distinguishes this from the weaker claim of mere connectivity (Entezari et al., 2022). The paper carves new conceptual ground by elevating checkpoints from optimization artifacts to a first-class data modality, analogous to how pixels or tokens are treated in image/text generation, and by proposing a multi-axis evaluation protocol that prior individual method papers lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the field with a shared vocabulary, a regime map (adapter / mid-scale full-weight / cross-architecture), and an evaluation framework that should reduce the fragmentation currently hindering progress. Its most important open question is whether adapter-scale successes (e.g., HY-WU at 80B backbone, RPG at 200M) can extend to unrestricted frontier-scale full-checkpoint synthesis, where representation alignment, long-range dependencies, and checkpoint heterogeneity remain unsolved. The paper also flags a practical governance gap: provenance tracking, memorization auditing, and safety screening for generated weights are currently absent from the literature, yet become critical as checkpoint zoos grow. For practitioners, the immediate takeaway is that second-scale LoRA/adapter generation is already competitive with fine-tuning, shifting the default question from 'how do we fine-tune?' to 'what distribution should we sample from?'
