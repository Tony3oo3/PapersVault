## Problem

Existing diffusion-based time series methods require target observations at training locations for normalization, forward corruption, and loss computation, making them inapplicable when targets are entirely absent at test locations. ZeroDiff addresses this gap by formalizing zero-shot cross-location time series reconstruction: given only exogenous inputs at a new location, reconstruct the full target time series without any historical target measurements there.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261052/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-dimensional exogenous time series X ∈ R^{L×D} (e.g., 33 meteorological/catchment features for CAMELS, 48 for Solar) at a held-out location and produces a reconstructed target series Y ∈ R^L over the same temporal span (typically 365 days at daily resolution). Both training and inference use the aligned formulation where X and Y share the same time window, and the model must generalize spatially across locations rather than temporally. The VAE-estimated moments (μ̂, σ̂) are also provided as explicit conditioning features to the denoiser, enabling moment-parameterized correction.

## Method

ZeroDiff decomposes reconstruction into two stages connected by a shared informed prior. First, a conditional VAE performs cross-modal moment estimation, inferring location-specific target statistics (μ, σ) from exogenous summary statistics alone, while a shared dynamics model f_ω (LSTM, DLinear, Mamba, or TimesNet) learns the normalized input-target relationship in standardized space; the prior Ŷ = μ̂ + σ̂·f_ω(X) is constructed without any target signal. Second, a diffusion model calibrates this prior by diffusing toward Ŷ rather than N(0,I), yielding a three-term posterior mean that anchors each reverse step to the prior. A bidirectional denoiser with full self-attention conditions on both past and future timesteps, and moment-guided Gaussian weighting upweights training locations whose estimated moments are close to the target, enabling the calibration learned on observed locations to transfer to unobserved ones.

## Results

ZeroDiff achieves NSE of 0.596 (Streamflow), 0.846 (Solar), 0.868 (Temp), and 0.788 (Methane), outperforming all five diffusion baselines on every dataset. All baselines fail entirely (NSE < 0) on Streamflow and Methane without the dynamics model, and even when augmented with fω predictions, the best baseline (NsDiff+fω) reaches only 0.105 NSE on Streamflow versus ZeroDiff's 0.596. On Solar, ZeroDiff improves NSE from 0.515 (best baseline, NsDiff) to 0.846, and RMSE from 5.546 to 3.109. The deterministic prior Φψ,ω alone already achieves positive NSE on all four datasets (0.481–0.852), confirming that the informed prior carries most of the signal, while diffusion calibration adds a further 0.115–0.307 NSE gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All five diffusion baselines (CSDI, SSSD, CSBI, DiffusionTS, NsDiff) fail to transfer across locations on Streamflow and Methane (NSE < 0) when used in their standard form, because they require target observations for normalization and loss computation. Augmenting baselines with fω predictions (+fω rows) partially rescues performance on Solar and Temp but still fails on Methane for CSDI, SSSD, CSBI, DiffusionTS, and NsDiff. On Streamflow, ZeroDiff (NSE 0.596) outperforms the best augmented baseline NsDiff+fω (0.105) by 0.491 NSE and reduces RMSE from 2.488 to 1.423. On Solar, ZeroDiff (0.846) beats NsDiff+fω (0.473) by 0.373 NSE and cuts RMSE from 6.131 to 3.109. On Temp, the gap is smaller (0.868 vs 0.763 for NsDiff+fω) because cross-location heterogeneity is lower, making the moment-guided weighting less critical. The consistent pattern across all four datasets is that integrating the informed prior directly into the diffusion forward/reverse process (rather than using it only as pseudo-labels) is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZeroDiff extends the informed-prior diffusion framework (CARD, SDEdit, PriorGrad) to a regime where the prior itself must be constructed without any target observations at the reconstruction location. It adapts the non-stationary time series insight (RevIN, Non-stationary Transformers) that separating marginal statistics from temporal dynamics improves generalization, but replaces target-based normalization with cross-modal moment estimation from exogenous variables via a conditional VAE. Unlike prior diffusion-based time series methods (CSDI, SSSD, TimeGrad, Diffusion-TS) that all require ground-truth targets for forward corruption and loss computation, ZeroDiff reformulates diffusion as calibration of a structured prior rather than generation from noise, enabling the framework to operate where targets are entirely absent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap in environmental and energy monitoring (ungauged basins, sparse irradiance stations) where exogenous data are abundant but target measurements are unavailable, and where probabilistic uncertainty quantification is essential for downstream decision-making. A key open question is whether the assumption of shared input-target dynamics across locations holds in more heterogeneous or non-stationary settings, and whether the moment-guided weighting can be replaced by more principled spatial priors. The finding that joint optimization degrades zero-shot transfer when the X→Y coupling is weak (Methane) suggests that gradient isolation between prior construction and denoiser calibration may be a general principle for cross-domain transfer, but this remains underexplored.
