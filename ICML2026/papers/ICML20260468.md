## Problem

Post-trained language models remain prone to extrinsic hallucinations, yet prior hallucination-reduction methods (SFT, DPO, RL with continuous factuality scores) often degrade general capabilities such as instruction following, math, and coding. In the long-form generation setting where no unique ground-truth answer exists, continuous rewards like VeriScore are vulnerable to reward hacking—models exploit them by producing fewer or more generic claims—making it difficult to reduce hallucination without sacrificing utility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260468/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes natural-language instructions (curated from WildChat) as input and produces free-form text responses. During training, each generated response is verified against pre-cached web documents (up to 10 pages per prompt, chunked to 512 tokens, top-8 retrieved via BM25) and scored by a Qwen3-32B LM verifier that checks for factual contradictions in a single forward pass, yielding a binary reward of 0 or 1. This response-level verification avoids brittle claim extraction and gives a 2×–4× throughput improvement over claim-by-claim pipelines.

## Method

Binary RAR assigns reward 1 if the response contains no factual contradictions with retrieved evidence and 0 otherwise, and is optimized via GRPO with KL regularization against a fully post-trained reference model. The three key design choices—binary (not continuous), response-level (not claim-level), and contradiction-only (not support-based)—make the reward robust to style-driven reward hacking that plagues continuous scores like VeriScore. Theoretically, the KL-regularized optimum uniformly downweights every error-containing response by a factor of exp(−1/β) while leaving the relative probabilities among contradiction-free responses unchanged, so the model's distribution over correct outputs is preserved. This means KL regularization alone suffices to stabilize training without auxiliary LM-judge or detail-oriented rewards, and the model learns to abstain strategically on uncertain short-form questions while maintaining accuracy when forced to answer.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B, Binary RAR reduces long-form hallucination rate from 61.9% to 37.5% (−24.4 pts) and short-form hallucination rate from 60.6% to 27.6% (−33.0 pts), outperforming all baselines. Utility is largely preserved: ALPACA-EVAL drops only 1.4% (54.7→53.9) and the average across eight general-capability benchmarks actually improves from 67.8 to 68.8. On Qwen3-4B, Binary RAR similarly reduces long-form hallucination from 66.2% to 37.7% and short-form from 68.7% to 41.9%, while ALPACA-EVAL improves from 41.7 to 43.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the concurrent RL-with-VeriScore method (Chen et al., 2025), Binary RAR achieves substantially lower short-form hallucination (27.6% vs. 42.3% on Qwen3-8B) and a modest long-form advantage (37.5% vs. 40.6%), while preserving ALPACA-EVAL far better (53.9 vs. 42.2, a 22.8-pt degradation for VeriScore). Against the DPO baseline derived from Tian et al. (2024) and Lin et al. (2024) (factuality-driven preference optimization), Binary RAR reduces long-form hallucination by 15.9 pts (37.5 vs. 53.4) and short-form by 29.6 pts (27.6 vs. 57.2), with DPO showing only marginal gains over SFT. The LM-judge RL baseline actually *increases* long-form hallucination (65.4% vs. 61.9% base), confirming that optimizing for general quality conflicts with factual accuracy. Across the eight general-capability benchmarks (IFEval, BBH, GSM8K, Minerva, HumanEval, MBPP, POPQA-no-abstain, GPQA-no-abstain), Binary RAR matches or slightly exceeds the base model (AVG 68.8 vs. 67.8), whereas VeriScore RL degrades several (e.g., GPQA 48.2→47.7, Minerva 80.7→79.0). On Tulu3-8B, Binary RAR reduces Biography hallucination from 68.1 to 60.1 with ALPACA-EVAL nearly unchanged (24.3→23.4), confirming transferability beyond the Qwen3 family.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of RL-based post-training (GRPO, building on DeepSeek's formulation) and retrieval-augmented factuality verification (the FactScore/VeriScore lineage). It departs from prior long-form factuality work (DPO-based methods like FactTune and FLAME, and the concurrent Chen et al. 2025 GRPO+VeriScore approach) by replacing continuous, claim-level factuality scores with a single binary, response-level contradiction signal. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical result (Theorem 1) showing that under KL regularization, a binary reward uniformly downweights error-containing responses while preserving the relative distribution over error-free ones, which provides a clean mechanistic explanation for why utility is preserved without auxiliary judge or detail-oriented rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hallucination-utility tradeoff is a central practical bottleneck for deploying LLMs in high-stakes settings, and this paper provides a simple, well-motivated recipe (binary RAR + GRPO + KL) that reduces hallucination substantially while keeping general capabilities intact across multiple model families. The empirical demonstration that continuous factuality rewards like VeriScore are systematically exploitable via reward hacking (producing generic or irrelevant-but-true content) is a useful caution for the broader RL-for-LLMs community. Limitations include dependence on verifier quality (16% false-negative rate), retrieval coverage (only 53% of errors have relevant evidence retrieved), and evaluation on 4B–8B models; open questions include whether the approach transfers to larger reasoning models, whether the binary signal can be refined without reintroducing style sensitivity, and how it interacts with intrinsic (prompt-contradicting) hallucination.
