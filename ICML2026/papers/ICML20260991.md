## Problem

In tensor-parallel LLM inference on bandwidth-constrained hardware, inter-device collectives (AllReduce, AllGather) dominate end-to-end latency, forming a 'bandwidth wall' that render compute-only quantization ineffective. Existing approaches treat computation quantization and communication compression as orthogonal problems, producing fragmented precision flows with redundant quantize-dequantize overheads, compounding quantization error, and missed cross-operator fusion opportunities. The paper asks whether computation and communication quantization can be co-designed as a unified, hardware-aware optimization problem and solved automatically.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-quantized LLM (weights quantized via GPTQ with random Hadamard rotation), a small calibration set (128 sequences of length 4096 from WikiText-2), a target hardware description (GPU compute throughput, interconnect bandwidth, tensor-core efficiency), and a user-specified latency budget expressed as a fraction of the full-precision baseline (e.g., 0.4× or 0.6× T_FP). It produces a per-layer, per-module mixed-precision allocation (bit-width and group size for each of 8 modules per layer), a set of specialized fused CUDA kernels implementing that allocation, and the resulting quantized model ready for distributed serving in vLLM. Sweeping the latency budget yields an exact Pareto frontier of accuracy-latency trade-offs rather than a single configuration.

## Method

CoCoQuant has three tightly coupled components. First, a precision-aligned graph rewriting exploits the equivalence f(AllGather(x)) ≡ AllGather(f(x)) to push row-wise operations (RMSNorm, residual add, MoE gating) from the replicated post-AllGather phase into the sharded pre-AllGather phase, enforcing isomorphic precision constraints between each communication output and the subsequent GEMM input; this eliminates two of five Q/DQ steps per layer and insulates precision-sensitive operations from quantization noise. Second, a hardware-aware mixed-precision allocation formulates bit-width selection as an ε-constraint Integer Linear Program over 8 quantizable modules per layer, using relative Frobenius-norm sensitivity scores from calibration and a roofline-based latency cost model, with additional ILP constraints that encode the graph-rewriting precision alignment (mapping weight-activation configs to activation-only configs via φ). Third, a suite of C++ template-specialized fused CUDA kernels (DQ→Reduce→RMSNorm/Add/Route→Q) keeps all intermediates in registers/L1 cache and supports over 2000 mixed-precision input/output combinations, achieving up to 51.7× speedup over torch.compile-generated baselines.

## Results

On 8×RTX-4090 (PCIe Gen4, 32 GB/s), CoCoQuant achieves up to 2.92× end-to-end prefill speedup on Qwen3-MoE-235B (CoCoQuant¹) with only +0.14 PPL over FP16 (4.18 vs. 4.04). On Llama3.3-70B, CoCoQuant² delivers 2.09× speedup at PPL 3.66, effectively matching the full-precision baseline (3.63) while the conservative W8A8C8 baseline only reaches 1.82×. Compute-only quantization (W8A8C16, W4A4C16) plateaus at 1.1–1.3× speedup across all models, confirming the bandwidth wall. On MoE architectures, uniform W4A4C16 causes catastrophic PPL explosion (457.96 on Llama4-Scout-109B), whereas CoCoQuant¹ maintains PPL 10.49 at 2.55× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CoCoQuant against five baseline classes: full-precision vLLM, compute-only quantization (W8A8C16, W4A4C16), and uniform communication+computation quantization (W8A8C8, W4A4C4*). Compute-only quantization consistently hits the bandwidth wall, yielding only 1.1–1.3× speedup regardless of model scale, confirming that communication dominates on PCIe-connected GPUs. Against the uniform W4A4C4* baseline (the strongest speedup competitor), CoCoQuant¹ achieves comparable or higher speedup (e.g., 2.73× vs. 2.78× on Llama3-8B; 2.92× vs. 2.01× on Qwen3-MoE-235B) while dramatically reducing perplexity (7.87 vs. 9.21 on Llama3-8B; 4.18 vs. 5.31 on Qwen3-MoE-235B). Against the conservative W8A8C8 baseline, CoCoQuant² matches or exceeds accuracy (PPL 3.66 vs. 3.72 on Llama3.3-70B) while delivering substantially higher speedup (2.09× vs. 1.82×). On MoE models, the advantage is most pronounced: uniform W4A4C16 causes PPL to explode to 457.96 on Llama4-Scout-109B, while CoCoQuant¹ maintains PPL 10.49 at 2.55× speedup, demonstrating that the ILP-based mixed-precision allocation effectively protects sensitive expert-routing operations.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoCoQuant unifies two previously isolated research threads: computation-centric quantization (GPTQ, SmoothQuant, QuaRot) that exploits low-precision tensor cores, and communication-centric compression (FlashComm, Zero++) that reduces collective communication volume. It builds directly on FlashComm's A2A+Reduce+AG decomposition as its communication foundation but departs fundamentally by introducing precision-aligned graph rewriting that eliminates the opaque boundary between quantized communication and computation, transforming a fragmented precision flow into a unified low-precision dataflow. The hardware-aware mixed-precision allocation via ε-constraint ILP extends prior single-device mixed-precision work (HAQ, HAWQ) to the distributed setting where communication bit-widths and computation bit-widths must be jointly constrained.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bandwidth wall is a structural and growing bottleneck in distributed LLM inference, and this paper provides the first systematic framework to break it through co-design rather than treating computation and communication as orthogonal problems. The practical impact is significant: up to 2.92× speedup with negligible accuracy loss on bandwidth-constrained hardware (PCIe-connected GPUs) where compute-only quantization plateaus at ~1.3×. Key limitations include the current focus on prefill/TTFT (decode-heavy serving remains unaddressed), restriction to tensor parallelism (not expert parallelism), and the need for offline calibration. Open questions include whether tile-level compute-communication overlapping can further close the gap, and how the approach scales to cross-node deployments with even lower bandwidth.
