## Problem

Attention in transformers scales quadratically with sequence length, motivating fast approximations. However, a substantial gap exists between theory and practice: prior methods with correctness guarantees either require quadratic time for fast polynomial error decay or only achieve slow near-constant n^{-o(1)} error decay in near-linear time. No existing approach simultaneously delivers super-polynomial error decay, near-linear runtime, and a practical GPU implementation.

## Input / Output

WILDCAT takes query Q ∈ R^{m×d}, key K ∈ R^{n×d}, and value V ∈ R^{n×d} tensors along with a scale factor β (typically 1/√d) and a rank parameter r, and produces an approximate attention output matrix Ō ∈ R^{m×d} with entrywise error guarantees. For autoregressive models, COMPRESSKV additionally outputs a compressed KV cache consisting of r selected keys K_S, r weighted values V_S = WV (where all n values contribute), and a normalization vector w = W·1_n, reducing storage from Θ(nd) to O(rd). The coreset size r can be near-constant (n^{o(1)}) while still guaranteeing super-polynomial error decay under bounded-entry assumptions.

## Method

WILDCAT approximates the attention matrix A = exp(βQK^⊤) via a weighted Nyström coreset: it selects r keys using randomly pivoted Cholesky (RPNYS), which samples pivots from the diagonal of the residual kernel matrix, then computes optimal Nyström weights W = h(K_S, K_S)^{-1} h(K_S, K) to minimize reconstruction error. Two key invariances are exploited: attention is invariant under global key recentering, and the attention matrix is invariant under the asymmetric rescaling Q → τQ, K → τ^{-1}K, where the optimal temperature τ is derived in closed form via the Lambert-W function to balance low-rank approximability of the key kernel against query-based error inflation. The sequential RPNYS selection is parallelized across B bins for GPU throughput, reducing parallel runtime to O(nr²/B³ + nrd/B²). The resulting plug-in approximation Ō = clip(Ď^{-1}ÂV, v_min, v_max) achieves super-polynomial O(n^{-√(log(log n))}) entrywise error decay in near-linear O(n^{1+o(1)}) time, and the same guarantees extend to super-logarithmic entry or dimension growth via a refined entropy-based analysis.

## Results

On the BigGAN image generation benchmark, WILDCAT (r=96, B=8) achieves a 4.33× speed-up over exact attention with only 1.22% Inception Score degradation and 0% FID degradation, outperforming all five baselines on both speed and quality. On T2T-ViT ImageNet classification, WILDCAT attains 82.19% Top-1 accuracy (vs. 82.55% exact) with an 11.59× speed-up on the dominant first layer. For KV cache compression with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on 13 LongBench-E tasks, COMPRESSKV achieves the highest average score at every compression level (47.25 at 75%, 42.12 at 87.5%, 36.55 at 93.75%), surpassing the next-best method by 1.9–3.1 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WILDCAT dominates all five attention approximation baselines (Reformer, ScatterBrain, Performer, KDEformer, Thinformer) on both the BigGAN and T2T-ViT benchmarks, achieving the highest speed-up (4.33× and 11.59× respectively) while maintaining the smallest quality loss. On BigGAN, WILDCAT's 1.22% IS degradation is lower than Thinformer (1.79%) and KDEformer (2.02%), while its 4.33× speed-up exceeds Performer (3.46×) and Thinformer (2.32×); Reformer and ScatterBrain are both slower than exact attention or suffer severe quality drops (66.55% and 36.77% IS degradation). For KV cache compression, COMPRESSKV outperforms all five baselines (StreamingLLM, PyramidKV, BalanceKV, Uniform, SnapKV) on average across 13 LongBench-E tasks at every compression level, with the largest margin at 93.75% compression (36.55 vs. 33.44 for SnapKV/PyramidKV). Notably, COMPRESSKV also achieves the highest individual task scores on a plurality of the 13 tasks, while StreamingLLM and PyramidKV degrade most severely under high compression (e.g., 30.22 and 33.44 average at 93.75% vs. COMPRESSKV's 36.55).

## Relation to Prior Work

WILDCAT builds directly on the randomly pivoted Cholesky (RPC) algorithm of Chen et al. (2022a) for spectrally-accurate kernel matrix approximation and the operator-norm analysis framework of Epperly & Moreno (2023), repurposing both for the attention setting via a Nyström-based low-rank approximation of the softmax attention matrix. The paper positions itself as bridging a substantial theory-practice gap: prior practical approximations (Reformer, Performer, ScatterBrain) lack error guarantees, while prior theoretical results with guarantees (Alman & Song 2023; Thinformer, BalanceKV, KDEformer, HyperAttention) either require quadratic time for polynomial error decay or achieve only near-constant n^{-o(1)} decay in near-linear time. WILDCAT's key departure is combining RPC-based coreset selection with optimal Nyström reweighting and a temperature rescaling trick to achieve super-polynomial error decay in near-linear time, a regime no prior method reaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The super-polynomial error decay in near-linear time is a genuine theoretical advance that pushes beyond the computational hardness limits identified by Alman & Song (2023) and Keles et al. (2023), and the practical GPU implementation consistently outperforms five leading baselines across image generation, image classification, and KV cache compression. The COMPRESSKV component is particularly impactful for long-context LLM inference, reducing KV cache memory from Θ(nd) to near-constant O(rd) while maintaining the strongest compression quality across 13 LongBench-E tasks at all tested compression levels. Key limitations include the inability to handle causal masking/streaming generation, the sequential nature of RPNYS pivot selection (mitigated but not eliminated by binning), and the theoretical guarantees requiring bounded or sub-logarithmically growing input norms—though Corollary 2 extends this to super-logarithmic growth in favorable regimes.
