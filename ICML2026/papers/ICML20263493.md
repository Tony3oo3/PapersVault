## Problem

Existing equivariance methods for neural networks—group convolutions, averaging, and Lie algebra canonicalization—are restricted to compact, finite, or finite-dimensional Lie groups, making them inapplicable to the infinite-dimensional group of diffeomorphisms that naturally models realistic spatial deformations in imaging. This paper extends energy-based canonicalization (LieLAC) to the infinite-dimensional diffeomorphism group, enabling pre-trained networks to achieve approximate diffeomorphism equivariance or invariance without retraining or exhaustive group sampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a grayscale image (128×128 for chest X-rays, standard MNIST resolution for digits) and a pre-trained task network (U-Net for segmentation, CNN for classification). It produces a binary segmentation mask or a discrete class label (genus/hole count). The canonicalization step internally parameterizes the deforming diffeomorphism as a stationary velocity field (SVF) encoded by a SIREN network, and the reverse canonicalization applies the inverse diffeomorphism to the network output to recover equivariance (segmentation) or invariance (classification).

## Method

DiffeoNN wraps any pre-trained network in a three-step pipeline: (1) canonicalization, which finds a diffeomorphism g_x by gradient-based optimization (100 steps) minimizing a task-specific energy E_can = λ_VAE·E_VAE + λ_adv·E_adv + E_reg over the SVF-parameterized diffeomorphism group; (2) application of the frozen inner model f_θ to the canonicalized input; and (3) reverse canonicalization via g_x^{-1} on the output. The image similarity energy combines a VAE reconstruction loss (trained on the sparse training set) with a WGAN-GP adversarial discriminator (trained to distinguish training images from randomly deformed ones), while the regularizer penalizes non-smooth velocity fields and negative Jacobian determinants to enforce orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms. SVFs are parameterized by SIREN networks and exponentiated via scaling-and-squaring, borrowing the computational machinery of differentiable image registration. The key theoretical contribution is showing that regularization acts as a selection operator (via Tikhonov-style analysis) that preserves approximate equivariance despite breaking exact equivariance conditions, and that the finite SVF parameterization is sufficient because it only needs to be expressive enough for the optimization to reach the training distribution.

## Results

On the synthetic nested-squares segmentation task, DiffeoNN achieves IoU 0.9571 and Dice 0.9777, outperforming the naive U-Net (IoU 0.9276, Dice 0.9582) but remaining slightly below the augmented baseline (IoU 0.9770, Dice 0.9878). On real-world chest X-ray lung segmentation, DiffeoNN reaches IoU 0.9214 and Dice 0.9586 versus 0.9147/0.9547 for the naive model and 0.9251/0.9606 for the augmented model. For diffeomorphism-invariant homology classification on MNIST, DiffeoNN matches the augmented classifier at 82% accuracy while the naive CNN achieves only 68%. The equivariance error is small: comparing the inner network output to the full DiffeoNN output on training images yields a mean Dice of 0.9812 and pixel-wise accuracy of 0.9902.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DiffeoNN against two baselines: (1) the naive inner network (a U-Net or CNN trained only on the canonical training set without any augmentation) and (2) an augmented model trained on both canonical and diffeomorphically transformed data. DiffeoNN consistently outperforms the naive baseline across all three tasks: by +2.95 IoU points on synthetic squares, +0.67 IoU on chest X-rays, and +14 accuracy points on MNIST. Against the augmented gold-standard baseline, DiffeoNN is slightly below on segmentation (−1.99 IoU on synthetic, −0.37 IoU on chest X-rays) but matches it exactly on MNIST classification (82% vs. 82%). Importantly, DiffeoNN produces fewer and smaller outliers than the augmented model (visible in the Dice box plots), suggesting greater robustness. The key practical advantage is that DiffeoNN requires no retraining of the task model and no data augmentation at inference time, making it a drop-in module for any pre-trained network.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the energy-based canonicalization framework of Kaba et al. (2023) and LieLAC (Shumaylov et al., 2024) from finite-dimensional Lie groups to the infinite-dimensional group of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms. The key technical departures are: (1) replacing Lie algebra descent with gradient-based optimization over stationary velocity fields (SVFs) parameterized by SIREN networks, following the image registration literature (Dalca et al., 2019; Han et al., 2023; Bostelmann et al., 2026), and (2) designing a composite canonicalization energy that combines a VAE reconstruction loss, a WGAN-GP adversarial discriminator, and a deformation regularizer (gradient + Jacobian determinant) to ensure both data similarity and physical plausibility. The paper positions itself against data augmentation (heuristic, no formal guarantees), group-equivariant layers (require finite/compact groups with Haar measure), and averaging-based methods (infeasible without a measure on the diffeomorphism group), arguing that canonicalization is the only viable strategy for this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that approximate diffeomorphism equivariance can be injected into a pre-trained network via a lightweight canonicalization preprocessing step, without retraining or extensive data augmentation, achieving performance on par with the augmented baseline while producing fewer outliers. The practical implication for medical imaging is significant: a model trained on a small, canonical dataset can generalize to arbitrarily deformed inputs (e.g., different breathing phases in lung CT), reducing annotation burden. However, the paper itself identifies a critical gap: there are few theoretical results on the stability and generalizability of canonicalization, and the method only achieves approximate (not exact) equivariance due to finite optimization budgets, regularization, and SVF parameterization limitations. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D volumetric data (only preliminary results are shown), how sensitive it is to the choice of energy terms, and whether formal learning bounds can be established for approximate canonicalization.
