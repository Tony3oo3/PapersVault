## Problem

Large reasoning models (LRMs) exhibit superficial safety alignment (SSA), where unsafe or harmful content persists in internal reasoning traces even when the final response is a safe refusal. Existing defenses operate primarily at the output level or rely on SFT/DPO pipelines that do not explicitly align the internal reasoning process, leaving the latent geometry of reasoning traces unstructured and vulnerable to jailbreak attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263079/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (including adversarial jailbreak prompts) and the hidden representation of the final reasoning token, which is projected via a learnable head onto a normalized latent hypersphere of dimension k. Each reasoning trace is labeled as Safe, Unsafe, or Rethink (an intermediate transitional state). The output is a safety-aligned reasoning trace whose intermediate hidden states are steered toward the safety subspace, paired with a safe final response, ensuring consistency between internal reasoning and external output.

## Method

CRAFT integrates two components: (1) Latent Contrastive Learning for Reasoning (LCLR), which structures the latent space via a margin-based triplet loss separating safe/unsafe/rethink prototypes, an InfoNCE instance-invariance loss, and a calibration loss aligning latent geometry with probabilistic safety scores; and (2) Reinforcement over Reasoning Latents (R²L), which applies GRPO with three complementary rewards—a Latent Semantic Reward measuring cosine proximity to safety prototypes, a Textual Safety Reward from an external discriminator, and a Latent-Textual Consistency Reward penalizing the L1 gap between latent and textual safety scores. The key theoretical contribution shows that the consistency reward eliminates SSA by ruling out superficially aligned policies as local optima under GRPO, since any policy with a latent-textual mismatch can be locally perturbed to improve the total reward without changing the output distribution.

## Results

On safety benchmarks, CRAFT achieves an average StrongReject score of 0.062 on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B and 0.099 on Qwen3-4B-Thinking, representing an 89.6% reduction in final-response jailbreak success and an 82.1% improvement in reasoning-trace safety over the base models. On reasoning benchmarks, CRAFT improves average pass@1 by 8.0% over the base models (0.481 vs. 0.437 on DeepSeek; 0.602 vs. 0.569 on Qwen3), indicating that safety alignment does not degrade—and in fact slightly enhances—general reasoning capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRAFT outperforms all six baselines on average safety metrics across both backbone models. The strongest baseline, IPO (corrective intervention via preference optimization), achieves an average of 0.084 on DeepSeek and 0.130 on Qwen3, while CRAFT reaches 0.062 and 0.099 respectively—a 26.2% and 23.8% relative improvement. STAR and SafeKey are competitive on individual sub-metrics (e.g., STAR achieves 0.003 response score on DeepSeek JBB) but lag CRAFT on reasoning-trace safety, where CRAFT scores 0.051 vs. STAR's 0.080 on DeepSeek JBB. ReasoningShield, a guard-model approach, performs worst among baselines (avg 0.511/0.423), confirming that post-hoc detection is insufficient compared to proactive latent alignment. On reasoning benchmarks (Table 2), CRAFT also leads all baselines, with an 8.0% average improvement over the base model, while methods like ReasoningShield cause the largest reasoning degradation (−16.0% on Qwen3).

## Relation to Prior Work

CRAFT builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework introduced with DeepSeek-R1 and the contrastive learning paradigm (SimCLR-style InfoNCE) to create a new alignment objective over hidden representations. It departs from prior reasoning-defense methods (SafeChain, RealSafe-R1, STAR, SafeKey, IPO, ReasoningShield) that operate at the output level or rely on SFT-based trajectory curation, by explicitly optimizing latent-space geometry so that unsafe reasoning trajectories are discouraged during generation rather than merely filtered at the surface. The paper also extends the recently formalized notion of superficial safety alignment (SSA) by providing a theoretical guarantee (Proposition 5.1) that a latent-textual consistency reward rules out SSA policies as local optima under GRPO, a result not available in prior output-level defenses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRAFT addresses a practically important gap: reasoning models can produce safe final refusals while leaking harmful content in their internal reasoning traces, a failure mode that output-level defenses cannot catch. The 82.1% and 89.6% average improvements in reasoning-trace and final-response safety, combined with an 8.0% *gain* in reasoning performance (countering the well-documented 'safety tax'), make this a compelling practical contribution for deploying reasoning models. Key open questions include whether the latent safety geometry transfers across model families (the paper acknowledges it does not), whether the method holds under fully adaptive attacks targeting the latent safety mechanism itself, and whether the substantial GRPO compute cost can be reduced for broader adoption.
