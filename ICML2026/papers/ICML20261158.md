## Problem

Agent planning under dynamic heterogeneity—where observations, transition dynamics, and reward structures shift between training and deployment—remains poorly addressed by both model-free RL and world-model planning, which either compound errors step-by-step or drift in learned simulators calibrated to a fixed regime. No existing method explicitly models and controls the redistribution of probability mass over the agent's internal state representation as the environment changes, leaving generalizable planning as a core bottleneck for embodied and open-world agents.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes egocentric RGB observations (or proprioceptive state vectors in control benchmarks) along with a task identifier, and encodes the full observation-action history into a learned meta-state embedding s_t ∈ R^{d_s} that serves as an approximate sufficient statistic. The method outputs a discrete or continuous action at each environment step via the policy head, and simultaneously produces a task-conditioned planning-time velocity field v_θ(s, τ, l) that specifies how probability mass over meta-states should be advected from the current occupancy toward a value-shaped target distribution.

## Method

FlowMAP formulates agent planning as continuous-time flow matching over meta-state occupancy distributions: it learns a task-conditioned velocity field whose induced flow transports the current meta-distribution ρ_0^l toward a target ρ_⋆^l via the continuity equation. Value-Transport Flow Matching (VTFM) constructs the target as a soft-optimal distribution ρ_⋆^l(s) ∝ exp(βV̂(s,l)) and pairs source-target meta-states through a cost-regularized entropic coupling, yielding a scalable supervised regression loss for the velocity field that provides a distribution-level planning signal even under sparse, delayed rewards. Flow-Policy Co-Training (FPCT) shares a representation network between the flow head and the policy head, and adds a dynamics-consistency regularizer that aligns the planning-time transport direction κ·v_θ(s,τ,l) with the predicted one-step meta-state drift Δs_t, ensuring the learned transport remains realizable under environment-feasible transitions. The total objective jointly minimizes the actor-critic RL loss, the flow-matching loss, and the consistency loss, co-adapting the representation, transport field, and policy in a single training loop.

## Results

FlowMAP is evaluated across eight benchmark families (Minecraft, DMLab-30, ProcGen, Atari, Atari100k, Visual Control, Proprioceptive Control, and BSuite) and is reported as competitive with or outperforming strong baselines including DreamerV3, PPO, Rainbow, and IMPALA. The gains are most pronounced in environments with strong dynamic heterogeneity (ProcGen, Minecraft), where FlowMAP outperforms world-model and model-free baselines. On Minecraft, the full FlowMAP achieves a mean episodic return of 9.0±0.2 (5 seeds), substantially above the RL-only baseline (6.0±0.4); on BSuite, Full FlowMAP reaches 69.0 mean return versus 54.2 for RL-only. FlowMAP also converges faster than DreamerV3 on Minecraft Diamond, reaching comparable final returns with fewer effective interactions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowMAP is compared against nine baselines spanning world-model and model-free paradigms. Against DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., 2025), the strongest world-model baseline, FlowMAP is competitive overall but shows clearer advantages in high-heterogeneity settings like ProcGen and Minecraft, where DreamerV3's learned simulator drifts under regime shifts; on Minecraft Diamond, both reach similar final returns but FlowMAP converges substantially faster. Against model-free baselines (PPO, Rainbow, IMPALA), FlowMAP outperforms in long-horizon and sparse-reward settings, with the distribution-level transport signal providing more stable credit assignment than step-wise value updates. On the low-data Atari100k benchmark, FlowMAP is compared against SimPLe, EfficientMuZero, SPR, IRIS, and TWM; the paper reports improved robustness over these step-wise value-optimization methods, attributing the gain to VTFM's ability to convert noisy scalar rewards into a distribution-level planning objective that is less sensitive to value miscalibration. The ablation evidence (Minecraft: 9.0 vs. 6.0 for RL-only; BSuite: 69.0 vs. 54.2) confirms that the flow-matching and consistency components jointly contribute beyond what any single baseline paradigm provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowMAP repurposes the continuous-time flow-matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023) from generative modeling to agent planning, but departs from prior flow-based RL variants (GFlowNets, FlowRL, ReinFlow) that shape action or terminal-outcome distributions: here the velocity field transports the meta-state occupancy distribution rather than a policy or reward distribution. On the planning side, it contrasts with world-model agents (DreamerV3, RoboDreamer, Vista) that plan via latent rollouts and with model-free RL (PPO, Rainbow, IMPALA) that optimize step-wise; FlowMAP instead learns a planning-time transport over internal state representations, coupled to the policy via shared encoders (FPCT) and a value-shaped target (VTFM). The POMDP/belief-MDP formulation and the use of a learned meta-state as an approximate sufficient statistic follow standard practice, but the explicit distributional-transport objective over that meta-state is the paper's core methodological departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized gap—planning that generalizes under nonstationary observations, dynamics, and sparse rewards—by reframing the problem as distribution-level control over internal hypotheses rather than trajectory-level prediction. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines like DreamerV3 are modest and the practical implementation reduces to an actor-critic backbone plus two auxiliary losses, so the methodological overhead is non-trivial relative to the performance delta. Open questions include how the approach scales to truly high-dimensional meta-state spaces, whether the value-shaped target remains stable under severe critic miscalibration, and how it extends to multi-agent or language-grounded settings. The authors themselves flag mean-flow-style objectives as a natural next step for smoother velocity estimation.
