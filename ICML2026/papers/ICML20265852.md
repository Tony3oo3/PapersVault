## Problem

Existing encoder-based adversarial attacks on LVLMs optimize perturbations over the full visual token space, but deployed models often use visual token compression (pruning/merging) at inference, creating an optimization-inference mismatch that makes robustness estimates overly optimistic. The adversarial robustness of compressed LVLMs under this mismatch has not been systematically studied.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265852/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack takes an RGB image and white-box access to the vision encoder (e.g., CLIP ViT in LLaVA), with black-box access to the compression module and downstream LLM. It produces an ℓ∞-bounded perturbation δ (ε=2/255) applied to the input image, optimized via 100 PGD steps. The deployment token budget K_model and compression mechanism are treated as unknown, so the attack must be effective across a range of budgets (16–576 tokens) without knowing which will be used at inference.

## Method

CAGE (CompressionAliGnEd attack) couples two complementary objectives optimized jointly via PGD. First, Expected Feature Disruption (EFD) models the unknown deployment budget as a discrete random variable with a uniform prior over [K_min, K_max], computes each token's survival probability from its rank under Top-K selection, and weights per-token cosine-distance distortion by these probabilities to concentrate perturbation energy on likely survivors. Second, Rank Distortion Alignment (RDA) converts both the distortion vector and the attention-score vector into softmax distributions and maximizes the cross-entropy of the selection distribution against the (gradient-stopped) distortion target, explicitly steering the ranking so that the most perturbed tokens receive the highest selection scores. The joint objective L_total = L_EFD + λ·L_RDA (λ=0.005) is maximized under an ℓ∞ constraint, with EFD providing the adversarial payload on survivors and RDA ensuring those survivors are actually retained by the compression bottleneck.

## Results

Across five compression mechanisms (VisionZip, VisPruner, DivPrune, FlowCut, PruMerge) and three datasets (GQA, TextVQA, VQA-v2), CAGE consistently reduces robust accuracy by an average of ~11.4% relative to the VEAttack baseline at every token budget from 576 (full) down to 16 tokens. On VQA-v2 with LLaVA, CAGE drops robust accuracy from 55.8% to 49.4% at full tokens and from 50.3% to 42.9% at 16 tokens. The gain is most pronounced on TextVQA (e.g., 25.5% → 15.7% at 16 tokens), where sparse text evidence makes predictions highly sensitive to corruption of a few survivor tokens. CAGE also outperforms the baseline on Qwen2.5-VL (up to 19.1% relative decrease on TextVQA at K=36) and InternVL3-8B (up to 16.3% relative decrease on VQA-v2 at K=72).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAGE is compared against three encoder-based attack baselines. Against the primary baseline VEAttack (Mei et al., ICLR 2026), CAGE achieves lower robust accuracy in every single configuration tested (5 compression methods × 3 datasets × 6 budgets), with an average relative reduction of ~11.4% on VQA-v2; the gap is largest under moderate compression (e.g., 55.4% → 47.2% at K=192) and narrows slightly at extreme budgets where robust accuracy approaches the blind (no-visual) floor. Against AttackVLM-ii (Zhao et al., NeurIPS 2023), CAGE is substantially stronger: on VQA-v2 with VisionZip, CAGE achieves 49.4% robust accuracy at full tokens versus 62.6% for AttackVLM-ii, and 42.7% vs. 52.5% at 16 tokens. Against VT-Attack (Wang et al., ACM MM 2024), CAGE similarly dominates: 49.4% vs. 59.4% at full tokens and 42.7% vs. 51.1% at 16 tokens on VQA-v2. The advantage of CAGE over all baselines is most pronounced under compressed inference, confirming that compression-aware optimization (EFD + RDA) is the key differentiator rather than a stronger optimizer or larger perturbation budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the encoder-based adversarial attack paradigm established by VEAttack (Mei et al., 2026) and VT-Attack (Wang et al., 2024c) by identifying a critical optimization-inference mismatch: prior attacks optimize perturbations in the full-token space, while deployed LVLMs with visual token compression (VisionZip, VisPruner, DivPrune, FlowCut, PruMerge) operate on a reduced token subset. CAGE departs from this by introducing two compression-aware objectives—Expected Feature Disruption (EFD), which probabilistically reweights token distortion by survival probability under an unknown budget, and Rank Distortion Alignment (RDA), which differentially aligns selection scores with distortion strength—without requiring knowledge of the deployed compression mechanism or token budget. The work also situates itself within the broader LVLM efficiency literature, targeting the outer-LLM plug-and-play compression methods that are most widely adopted in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study showing that visual token compression, far from acting as a denoiser, functions as a 'distortion concentrator' that preferentially retains the most adversarially corrupted tokens, making compressed LVLMs more vulnerable than full-token models under properly aligned attacks. The finding that standard robustness evaluations using full-token attacks are overly optimistic has direct implications for safety-critical deployments (autonomous driving, mobile agents) where compression is essential for latency constraints. However, the explored defenses (robustness-aware selection, stochastic candidate pools, attention-based detection) provide only partial mitigation, leaving the design of effective compression-aware defenses as an open problem. The moderate but consistent ~11% relative robustness reduction across five compression mechanisms and three datasets suggests the gap is real but the practical severity depends on deployment context.
