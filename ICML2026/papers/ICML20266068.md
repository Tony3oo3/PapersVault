## Problem

On-policy distillation for LLM reasoning requires a separate, often larger teacher model and does not explicitly exploit ground-truth solutions available in reasoning datasets. This paper asks whether a single sufficiently capable LLM can serve as its own teacher by conditioning on privileged answer information, thereby eliminating the need for an external teacher while retaining dense token-level supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266068/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes problem-solution pairs (x, y*) from a reasoning dataset (OpenThoughts, up to 30K pairs with chain-of-thought). At training time, the student policy sees only the problem x and generates an on-policy rollout capped at 1024 tokens, while the teacher policy receives both x and the ground-truth solution y* as privileged context but performs only a single forward pass (no token generation). At inference, the model produces a full reasoning trace and final answer conditioned solely on the problem statement.

## Method

OPSD instantiates two conditional distributions from the same LLM parameters θ: a student policy p_S(·|x) and a teacher policy p_T(·|x, y*), differing only in conditioning context. The student samples its own trajectory ŷ, and at each token position n both policies produce next-token distributions over the full vocabulary; the training loss minimizes the per-token forward KL divergence D(p_T‖p_S) along the student's rollout, with gradients backpropagated only through the student's logits. A per-token pointwise KL clipping mechanism caps individual vocabulary-entry divergence contributions to prevent stylistic tokens from dominating the training signal over mathematically meaningful tokens. The teacher is fixed at the initial (pre-training) policy rather than the updating policy, which stabilizes training and acts as implicit regularization. The optimal configuration uses a thinking-mode-off student paired with a thinking-mode-on teacher, maximizing the KL signal on math tokens.

## Results

On competition-level math benchmarks (AIME 2024, AIME 2025, HMMT 2025), OPSD achieves Avg@12 accuracy of 64.8 on Qwen3-8B, 63.6 on Qwen3-4B, and 43.4 on Qwen3-1.7B, consistently outperforming both SFT and GRPO across all model scales. OPSD converges within 100 training steps using a single 1024-token rollout per problem, whereas GRPO requires 8 rollouts of 16k tokens each over 500 steps. Notably, SFT degrades performance below the base model at every scale (e.g., 59.8 vs. 61.8 on Qwen3-8B), attributed to the concise reasoning style of ground-truth solutions reducing test-time generation length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPSD is compared against two baselines trained on the same 30K OpenThoughts problem-solution pairs: SFT (off-policy distillation on expert trajectories) and GRPO (on-policy RL with binary outcome rewards). OPSD outperforms GRPO on average across all three model scales—64.8 vs. 64.0 (8B), 63.6 vs. 62.7 (4B), and 43.4 vs. 37.7 (1.7B)—while using roughly 150× fewer generated tokens (1×1024 vs. 8×16000 per problem). OPSD also decisively beats SFT, which actually degrades below the base model at every scale due to exposure bias and the concise style of ground-truth solutions. The largest gap over GRPO appears at the 1.7B scale (+5.7 avg), where GRPO suffers from reward diversity collapse (zero reward standard deviation in over half of batches within 100 steps), yielding no gradient signal. OPSD sidesteps this by providing dense per-token distillation loss that remains informative regardless of final-answer correctness.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPSD extends the on-policy distillation paradigm (GKD, Agarwal et al. 2024; Lu & Lab 2025) by eliminating the need for a separate teacher model: the same LLM serves as both teacher and student, with the teacher conditioned on privileged ground-truth solutions. Conceptually, it bridges context distillation (Snell et al., 2022), which uses the same model with privileged context but in an off-policy SFT setting, and on-policy imitation learning (DAgger, Ross et al., 2011), which requires an external expert. The key departure from STaR and ReST is that OPSD provides dense per-token distributional supervision rather than sequence-level binary rewards, and from standard on-policy distillation is that the 'teacher' is the model itself with answer access rather than a larger external model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OPSD offers a practical training recipe that matches or exceeds GRPO performance while using roughly 1/128th the generated tokens (one 1024-token rollout vs. eight 16k-token rollouts), making post-training substantially cheaper. The finding that SFT on concise reference solutions actively degrades reasoning performance (by shortening test-time generation) while OPSD converts the same data into a dense learning signal is a useful diagnostic for practitioners. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 8B parameters (the authors observe instability in later training steps), whether incorporating correctness verification would add stability, and whether curriculum learning over problem difficulty is necessary for harder tasks where the model's own rationalization ability is insufficient.
