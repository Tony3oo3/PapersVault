## Problem

State-of-the-art autoregressive language models achieve strong performance but their internal reasoning remains opaque, and existing interpretability tools (attention visualization, probing, sparse autoencoders) are post-hoc analyses constrained by the self-attention architecture they operate on. No prior architecture incorporates interpretability directly into its design while simultaneously maintaining competitive language-modeling performance and offering computational efficiency advantages over quadratic self-attention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265567/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes BPE-tokenized text sequences (16K vocabulary) and autoregressively predicts the next token, with context lengths ranging from 256 to 2048 tokens. Unlike standard transformers where each position attends to all prior positions, ProtoT routes information through R=32 prototype channels with per-channel time-discounted prefix means, yielding O(1) per-token inference cost and memory. The final output is a standard vocabulary distribution from the LM head after L=6–12 transformer blocks with SwiGLU FFNs and RMSNorm skip connections.

## Method

ProtoT replaces self-attention with a prototype-based mixer in which R learnable parameter vectors define R parallel communication channels. Each channel aggregates past context via a write gate (softmax over prototypes for channel-aligned, rather than sequence-aligned, gating) and a time-discounted prefix mean (exponential moving average with per-prototype decay β_k), then reads back through a read gate with a separate temperature parameter. The prefix mean enforces a strict autoregressive constraint by excluding the current position, removing the self-attention vertical shortcut and empirically inducing a predict-and-consolidate pattern where read-gate peaks precede write-gate activations. The R-channel bottleneck forces semantic specialization across prototypes, and the per-prototype decay parameters provide directly interpretable time-scale preferences (half-life). The remainder of the architecture is identical to LLaMA-3, and the overall computational cost scales linearly in sequence length rather than quadratically.

## Results

At large-scale training (h=512, L=12, ctx=512, 339k docs), ProtoT achieves 29.5 test perplexity, outperforming DeltaNet (31.5) but trailing Mamba (26.5) and LLaMA (25.8). On open-ended text generation (Elo), ProtoT scores 1021, beating LLaMA (975) and DeltaNet (962) while approaching Mamba (1042). On GLUE, ProtoT attains 67.6 average, competitive with Mamba (68.6) and clearly above DeltaNet (64.5), though below LLaMA (71.6). On interpretability, ProtoT's prototypes achieve 6.52 disentanglement and 3.86 average themes per feature, outperforming LLaMA attention heads (5.02 / 5.02) and SAE-extracted features (5.91 / 4.33).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaMA (quadratic self-attention), ProtoT loses on perplexity (29.5 vs 25.8) and GLUE (67.6 vs 71.6) but wins on text-generation Elo (1021 vs 975) and operates at linear rather than quadratic sequence-length cost, surpassing LLaMA in inference throughput beyond 32k tokens. Against Mamba (state-space model), ProtoT trails on perplexity (29.5 vs 26.5), GLUE (67.6 vs 68.6), and text generation (1021 vs 1042), but offers superior interpretability with directly inspectable prototype channels. Against DeltaNet (linear-attention), ProtoT consistently wins: lower perplexity (29.5 vs 31.5), higher text-generation Elo (1021 vs 962), and better GLUE (67.6 vs 64.5). On robustness to meaning-preserving perturbations, ProtoT outperforms LLaMA on synonyms, typos, spelling, and morphology slices, while Mamba retains the overall lowest Jensen-Shannon divergence. On meaning-altering interventions (gender, negation, number), ProtoT shows greater appropriate sensitivity (lower top-1 invariance and Spearman correlation) than both LLaMA and Mamba, indicating it adapts predictions more reliably when semantics change.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtoT repurposes the prototype-learning idea from vision (Chen et al., 2019; Rymarczyk et al., 2022) and NLP classification (ProtoAttend, ProtoryNet) by integrating learnable prototype vectors as per-layer communication channels rather than final-stage decision units. Architecturally, it sits between Perceiver (where latents interact via O(R²) self-attention) and Slot Attention (iterative refinement over static inputs): ProtoT prototypes never interact with each other, forming R independent parallel channels updated via strict autoregressive EMA, creating a semantic routing bottleneck. The interpretability framing departs from post-hoc methods (SAEs, causal tracing, attention visualization) by embedding interpretability into the mixer design itself, while the linear-complexity positioning places it alongside Mamba and DeltaNet as an alternative to quadratic self-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that interpretability-by-design is compatible with competitive language-modeling performance: prototypes automatically capture nameable concepts (gender, geographic entities, disease names) across abstraction levels, and targeted interventions on individual prototypes produce surgical behavioral edits with <1% perplexity degradation. However, ProtoT still trails LLaMA by ~3.7 perplexity at large scale, context scalability is bottlenecked by the R-channel prefix mean, and some prototypes remain polysemantic. Open questions include whether the concept-emergence and surgical-editing properties persist at much larger scales, whether prototypes can be explicitly aligned to stakeholder-relevant concepts, and whether the linear-compute advantage translates to practical long-context deployment where LLaMA's quadratic cost becomes prohibitive.
