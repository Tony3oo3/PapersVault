## Problem

Irregular multivariate time series forecasting is hindered by two coupled limitations: classical frequency-domain operators (DFT/FFT) require uniform sampling grids and cannot directly extract spectral representations from non-uniform timestamps, while patch-based local temporal approaches suffer from severe information density imbalance when observations are sparsely and unevenly distributed across time windows. No existing framework jointly addresses both global periodic modeling and robust local aggregation under irregular sampling.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an irregular multivariate time series in canonical pre-alignment form — a set of unique chronological timestamps T, a value matrix X with zero-fill for unobserved entries, and a binary mask M indicating which (time, variable) pairs are observed — together with a set of arbitrary future timestamp queries Q. It produces forecasted scalar values at each queried future timestamp for every variable, with the loss computed only over observed future points to accommodate irregular target sampling.

## Method

TFMixer processes the input through two parallel branches. The Global Frequency Module applies a learnable Non-Uniform Discrete Fourier Transform (NUDFT) that computes real and imaginary spectral coefficients directly from irregular timestamps via masked cosine/sine projections at learnable frequencies, followed by an MLP spectrum refinement stage and a projection into the latent space; the refined coefficients are later used for inverse NUDFT to generate an explicit seasonal bias at future timestamps. The Local Time Module partitions the series into transformable patches of fixed time-window size, encodes each patch with a Transformable Time-aware Convolution Network (TTCN), then compresses P variable-length patch embeddings into W fixed learnable query tokens via cross-attention to suppress noise from sparse patches, and finally applies L layers of Dual-Mixing Blocks that alternate MLP-based patch mixing (intra-variable temporal) and variable mixing (inter-variable correlation). The Output Module fuses the two branch representations with a learnable scalar weight, decodes base predictions via an MLP conditioned on future time embeddings, and adds the inverse-NUDFT seasonal bias before masked denormalization. Training combines a supervised MAE forecasting loss over observed future points with a self-supervised reconstruction loss that penalizes the MAE between original historical values and their NUDFT reconstruction, regularizing the spectral coefficients to capture authentic periodicities.

## Results

TFMixer achieves the best overall performance across four irregular multivariate time series benchmarks (HumanActivity, USHCN, PhysioNet, MIMIC), surpassing the second-best baseline APN by 3.0% in average MSE and 8.1% in average MAE. On USHCN, TFMixer reduces MSE to 0.1448 (vs. 0.1590 for APN) and MAE to 0.2052 (vs. 0.2611), representing the largest relative gains. On MIMIC, TFMixer achieves MSE 0.4182 and MAE 0.3782, outperforming all 14 baselines. In extended-horizon experiments (Table 4), TFMixer maintains its lead, e.g., achieving MSE 0.4444 on USHCN (vs. 0.4528 for Hi-Patch) and MAE 0.4095 on MIMIC (vs. 0.4415 for Hi-Patch).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFMixer outperforms all 14 baselines in aggregate (average MSE and MAE across the four datasets), with the closest competitor being APN (Liu et al., 2026b), which it beats by 3.0% MSE and 8.1% MAE on average. The largest individual gains are on USHCN, where TFMixer's MSE of 0.1448 improves over APN (0.1590) by 8.9% and over KAFNet (0.1698) by 14.7%, and its MAE of 0.2052 improves over APN (0.2611) by 21.4%. On MIMIC, TFMixer (MSE 0.4182, MAE 0.3782) leads APN (0.4292, 0.4016) and Hi-Patch (0.4279, 0.4033) on both metrics. On PhysioNet, TFMixer's MSE (0.3118) is marginally higher than Hi-Patch (0.3071) and GraFITi (0.3075), but it still achieves the best MAE (0.3576 vs. 0.3637 for GraFITi). Compared to older architectures—GRU-D, SeFT, Raindrop, and NeuralFlows—TFMixer delivers 2–10× lower MSE across all datasets, confirming that the joint time-frequency design substantially advances the state of the art for irregular multivariate forecasting.

## Relation to Prior Work

TFMixer builds directly on the transformable patching paradigm of tPatchGNN (Zhang et al., 2024) for local temporal encoding and on the Canonical Pre-Alignment representation widely used in IMTSF. Its key departure is from KAFNet (Zhou et al., 2026), which projects irregular observations onto a shared grid before performing spectral analysis; TFMixer instead introduces a learnable NUDFT that operates directly on raw irregular timestamps, eliminating the alignment step. The query-based patch mixing mechanism is a novel response to the information density imbalance problem identified in patch-based IMTSF methods (tPatchGNN, Hi-Patch), which the authors argue is under-addressed in prior work. The overall architecture positions itself as the first to jointly model global frequency structure and local temporal dynamics in parallel for irregular multivariate series.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The learnable NUDFT provides a principled way to bring frequency-domain reasoning to irregular time series without lossy interpolation, which could be adopted beyond IMTSF (e.g., irregular sensor networks, financial tick data). The query-based patch mixing offers a general-purpose remedy for information density imbalance that may transfer to other patch-based architectures. Open questions include whether the learnable frequency set generalizes across domains with very different periodicity structures, how the method scales to very long sequences or high-dimensional variable sets, and whether the reconstruction loss remains beneficial when the frequency module is made deeper. The paper does not explore probabilistic forecasting or uncertainty quantification, which are increasingly expected in clinical and environmental applications.
