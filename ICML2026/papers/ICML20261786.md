## Problem

VLA models trained on simulated robotic data suffer from a substantial visual domain gap and limited environmental diversity, causing catastrophic failure under minor perturbations in lighting, camera viewpoint, or object layout. While simulated data is cheap and parallelizable, its sterile, uniform appearance leads models to memorize fixed action sequences rather than learn robust instruction-to-action mappings. The paper addresses this by converting simulated VLA videos into visually diverse, realistic training videos while strictly preserving task semantics and action trajectories.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes simulated robotic videos at 960×720 resolution (93 frames per chunk), depth maps extracted via Depth Anything as geometric control signals, and text captions generated by VideoChat2 then rewritten by Qwen3-8B to alter only the table surface material (wood, stainless steel, marble) while preserving task intent. It produces temporally coherent realistic videos at the same resolution and a compact coreset of trajectories (e.g., 10–50% of the dataset) selected for augmentation, which are then mixed or substituted into the original training set for VLA policy fine-tuning.

## Method

The core pipeline extracts structured conditions (captions + depth) from simulation, rewrites captions via an LLM to diversify environmental context, and synthesizes realistic videos using Cosmos-Transfer 2.5 as a conditional flow-matching video diffusion model. Two efficiency mechanisms make large-scale augmentation practical: (1) a three-stage velocity caching strategy that partitions the N-step denoising into initial, stable, and adjustment phases, reusing cached velocity predictions every α=8 steps during the stable phase (detected via a relative smoothness threshold k=0.4), achieving 61.2% generation-time reduction with negligible quality loss; and (2) a trajectory-level coreset sampling algorithm extending D² Pruning to embodied video data, which scores each trajectory by RDT-1B policy loss (difficulty) and Cosmos-Embed1 visual embedding topology (diversity), then performs greedy selection with forward message passing to aggregate neighborhood difficulty and reverse message passing to suppress visually redundant neighbors.

## Results

On Robotwin 2.0 single-task learning with RDT-1B, the method achieves a 10% average improvement in the Hard setting (29.0% → 39.0%) and 6% in Easy (49.0% → 55.0%). On the more challenging LIBERO-Plus spatial suite, π₀ improves by 5.1% average (42.7% → 47.8%), with the largest gain of +22.0% on language instruction perturbations and +16.6% on object layout. In real-world experiments on an AgileX Piper arm, the method yields 13% and 15% absolute gains in average success rate for π₀.₅ (60% → 73%) and π₀ (60% → 75%) respectively. The velocity caching mechanism achieves a 61.2% reduction in video generation time with negligible quality loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against RoboTransfer (Liu et al., 2025b), a geometry-consistent video diffusion method for robotic visual policy transfer. The proposed method consistently outperforms RoboTransfer across all five evaluated tasks on both geometric consistency and semantic alignment metrics: RMSE drops from 0.30–0.49 to 0.12–0.28, Abs.Rel from 0.28–0.37 to 0.11–0.17, and Sq.Rel from 0.16–0.39 to 0.02–0.07. Prompt-video similarity (VideoCLIP-XL) also improves substantially, from 18.6–24.2 to 25.6–27.7. The trade-off is a modest increase in per-video generation time (e.g., 340s → 441s on 'adjust bottle'), though the velocity caching mechanism recovers most of this overhead. Against the original simulation data baseline (no augmentation), the method shows consistent gains across all benchmarks, with the largest improvements appearing under distribution-shift conditions (LIBERO-Plus perturbations, Robotwin Hard mode, and real-world OOD settings), confirming that the augmentation specifically targets the generalization gap rather than in-distribution performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: conditional video diffusion for sim-to-real transfer (building directly on Cosmos-Transfer 2.5 as the generation backbone, in the same lineage as RoboTransfer, Rebot, and EgoDemoGen), flow-matching-based diffusion acceleration (extending the velocity-prediction formulation from Lipman et al. with a three-stage caching strategy), and data-centric selection (adapting D² Pruning's diversity-difficulty message-passing framework from text to embodied video trajectories). Rather than proposing a new generative architecture, the paper's conceptual contribution is the systematic integration of caption rewriting, depth-conditioned video transfer, velocity caching, and coreset sampling into a single scalable pipeline, with the velocity caching and coreset components being the primary methodological departures from prior video-transfer-for-robotics work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making large-scale VLA data augmentation computationally feasible: the 61% generation speedup and the finding that augmenting only 10% of trajectories (via coreset) captures most of the benefit dramatically lower the barrier to deploying video transfer in real training pipelines. However, the method's augmentation is primarily appearance-level (background, texture, lighting) and does not address geometry- or viewpoint-dependent perturbations, as evidenced by the performance drops on camera viewpoint and robot initial state shifts in LIBERO-Plus. Open questions include whether the coreset strategy generalizes to substantially larger or more heterogeneous datasets, whether the velocity caching parameters transfer across different diffusion backbones, and whether the approach can be extended to augment action trajectories rather than only visual appearance.
