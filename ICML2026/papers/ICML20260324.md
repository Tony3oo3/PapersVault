## Problem

While quantization is the standard approach for reducing VLM inference cost, its impact on reliability metrics beyond top-1 accuracy—calibration, OOD detection, noise robustness, covariate-shift resilience, and spurious-correlation sensitivity—remains critically under-explored. The community has extensively characterized full-precision CLIP robustness but lacks evidence on whether these safety-critical properties survive aggressive lossy compression, creating a blind spot for real-time deployment.

## Input / Output

The evaluation pipeline takes pre-trained discriminative VLMs (10 architectures: CLIP ViT/ConvNeXt, SigLIP, ALIGN, CoCa) and applies 16 quantization strategies (8 PTQ, 8 QAT) at W8A8, W6A8, and W4A8 to both visual-only and joint visual-text scopes. For each quantized checkpoint, the system consumes RGB images paired with fixed text prompts (zero-shot class vocabularies) and produces class labels, confidence scores, OOD detection scores (AUROC/FPR95 via 6 scoring functions), ECE calibration metrics, relative accuracy changes under 8 shift/corruption benchmarks, and spurious-correlation gap metrics on Counter-Animal. The full suite yields over 700k evaluation runs across 8,640 unique model checkpoints.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a two-part analysis: (1) a large-scale empirical benchmark of 16 quantization methods across 10 VLMs on five reliability dimensions, and (2) a mechanistic explanation via SVD-based spectral analysis of penultimate-layer features. The spectral analysis reveals that quantization acts as a deterministic low-pass filter: because the fixed discretization step dominates components whose magnitude approaches the grid (high-rank, low-variance features), it collapses the Signal-to-Quantization-Noise Ratio at higher ranks, forcing the model to concentrate discriminative information in robust low-rank dimensions. This passive filtering explains the counterintuitive gains in calibration, OOD detection, and synthetic-noise robustness, while the active subspace concentration (higher accuracy in Ranks 0–8) clarifies decision boundaries. The same mechanism explains the costs: erasure of fine-grained high-rank detail degrades semantic-shift robustness and amplifies spurious correlations. Causal validation via SVD rank truncation on FP32 models confirms the spectral cutoff—not finite-precision arithmetic per se—drives the reliability shifts, and rotation-based methods (QuaRot+LSQ) mitigate the trade-off by aligning the activation distribution with the quantization grid, preserving intermediate-rank signal.

## Results

Across 700k+ evaluation runs on 10 VLMs, quantization does not uniformly degrade reliability: ~40% of W8A8 evaluations show improved calibration, and 7.8% improve both accuracy and calibration simultaneously. Synthetic-noise robustness improves universally by an average of +8.9% relative, while semantic robustness (ImageNet-A/R/Sketch) degrades consistently. In real TensorRT deployment, the INT8 (LSQ) engine achieves 66.30% CIFAR-100 accuracy (+1.83 pp over FP32's 64.47%, p<10⁻⁷) at 7.72× speedup and 118.4 MB size, and degrades 2.2–4.9 pp less than FP16 under Gaussian blur. Rotation+LSQ achieves a 0.0% failure rate (>5% accuracy drop) versus 62.0% for Simple PTQ and 25.0% for standard QAT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is quantized VLMs against their FP32 baselines across five reliability dimensions, with method-family comparisons (PTQ vs. QAT vs. Rotation+LSQ) as the secondary axis. Rotation+LSQ dominates: it is the only method that simultaneously improves calibration (↑↑), maintains OOD AUROC, improves synthetic-noise robustness, and does not amplify spurious correlations at W8A8/W6A8 (ΔRSG = −0.1 ± 0.1, n.s.), while achieving a 0.0% failure rate. Simple PTQ is the worst performer, degrading calibration (↓↓), amplifying spurious correlations by +2.7% at W8A8 and +12.5% at W4A8, and failing 62% of evaluations. Standard QAT (Contrastive) sits in between, halving the PTQ failure rate to 25% and reducing spurious amplification to +1.6%, but still degrades semantic robustness. On the deployment axis, the INT8 TensorRT engine beats both FP32 (PyTorch) and FP16 (TensorRT) on accuracy and blur-robustness while being 7.72× faster and 2.55× smaller than FP32. Pre-training data quality modulates outcomes: WIT/DFN models gain from quantization-induced regularization, while LAION models suffer +49% ECE and accuracy drops, particularly at the Base-32 scale where capacity is insufficient to buffer quantization noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two mature but previously disconnected research threads: model quantization for efficient inference (PTQ, QAT, LSQ, QuaRot) and VLM reliability benchmarking (OpenOOD, ImageNet-X, CounterAnimal). It extends the quantization-as-implicit-regularizer line of work (AskariHemmat et al., 2022; Javed et al., 2025), which had been studied only through the lens of top-1 accuracy and domain generalization, to a full reliability suite including calibration, OOD detection, covariate-shift robustness, and spurious-correlation sensitivity. The spectral-bias literature (Rahaman et al., 2019; Huh et al., 2023) provides the theoretical vocabulary for the paper's central mechanistic claim—that quantization acts as a deterministic low-pass spectral filter on the feature space—but no prior work had connected this mechanism to downstream reliability metrics in VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first large-scale (700k+ evaluation runs, 10 architectures, 16 quantization methods) evidence that quantization is not merely a lossy compression step but a spectral regularizer with non-trivial, sometimes beneficial, effects on model reliability. This reframes quantization as a design knob for reliability, not just efficiency, and the rotation-based (QuaRot+LSQ) recommendation offers a concrete, deployable path to faster and more trustworthy VLMs. Key open questions remain: whether the spectral filtering mechanism transfers to generative VLMs (LLaVA, Qwen-VL) where autoregressive decoding may interact differently with quantization noise, whether the findings hold under adversarial (rather than unintentional) distribution shifts, and whether a fully factorial architecture × pre-training-data design would reveal additional interactions. The paper also acknowledges that simulated quantization, while validated on TensorRT for one configuration, may not capture all hardware-specific effects across the full method suite.
