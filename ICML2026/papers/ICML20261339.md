## Problem

Cognitive structure—a student's construction of both individual concepts and inter-concept relations—remains largely unassessable in educational practice. Existing knowledge tracing and cognitive diagnosis methods reduce student states to per-concept vectors (R^L), ignoring inter-concept relations, and tightly couple representation learning with prediction objectives, limiting generalization, interpretability, and cross-task reuse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261339/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a student's sequential interaction history (question indices and binary responses) and a Q-matrix (question-to-concept weight matrix, normalized across concepts per question) as input. It produces an undirected cognitive structure graph with L concept nodes and L×L/2 relation edges, each in a discrete construction state space Δ^c (e.g., constructed vs. unconstructed); the graph is then compressed via edge-aware hard-clustering graph pooling into a fixed-length vector that is concatenated with the question embedding for downstream KT/CD prediction.

## Method

CSG casts cognitive structure generation as a discrete graph diffusion task using a Cognitive Structure Diffusion Probabilistic Model (CSDPM) parameterized by an 8-layer extended Graph Transformer (DiGress-style categorical transitions on nodes and upper-triangular edges). Training follows a two-stage paradigm: Stage I pretrains the CSDPM on rule-based simulated structures (weighted-accuracy proxies for concept and relation construction derived from the Q-matrix), and Stage II fine-tunes the reverse denoising policy via reinforcement learning, casting the T-step denoising chain as an MDP and optimizing with an eager policy gradient under a SOLO-taxonomy-inspired hierarchical reward that scores generated structures across five developmental levels (pre-structural → extended abstract). The key architectural insight is decoupling cognitive structure representation from downstream prediction: the generated graph serves as a task-agnostic, interpretable intermediate that can be plugged into any KT or CD model.

## Results

CSG-KT achieves the best AUC across all five datasets, reaching 0.8220 (Math1), 0.7772 (Math2), 0.8636 (FrcSub), 0.7413 (NIPS34), and 0.7963 (ASSISTment17), outperforming the strongest baseline PSI-KT by 0.010–0.015 AUC. CSG-CD similarly leads on all datasets with AUC of 0.8133, 0.8179, 0.8699, 0.8036, and 0.8003, surpassing the best CD baseline DisenGCD by 0.015–0.016 AUC. Gains are statistically significant (p < 0.05 to p < 0.001) on nearly all dataset–metric pairs, with the largest absolute improvements observed on FrcSub (e.g., +0.010 AUC for KT, +0.014 AUC for CD over the prior best).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSG-KT outperforms all 10 KT baselines on every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is PSI-KT (AUC 0.8118 on Math1, 0.7759 on Math2, 0.8533 on FrcSub, 0.7260 on NIPS, 0.7890 on ASSISTment17); CSG-KT exceeds it by 0.010, 0.001, 0.010, 0.015, and 0.007 AUC respectively, with the largest gap on NIPS34. Graph-based KT methods that model only concept construction (GKT, SKT, GRKT) trail CSG-KT by 0.02–0.03 AUC, confirming the benefit of explicitly modeling inter-concept relations. For CD, CSG-CD beats all 7 baselines on all five datasets; the strongest baseline DisenGCD (AUC 0.7983/0.8039/0.8559/0.7886/0.7842) is surpassed by 0.015, 0.014, 0.014, 0.015, and 0.016 AUC. Notably, the diffusion-based CD baseline DiffCog underperforms even classical IRT on Math1 (0.7892 vs. 0.7356 AUC) and ASSISTment17, while CSG-CD achieves 0.8133 and 0.8003, demonstrating that coupling diffusion with explicit cognitive-structure generation yields substantially better representations than diffusing latent ability vectors alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the discrete graph diffusion framework of DiGress (Vignac et al., 2023) and the Graph Transformer architecture (Dwivedi & Bresson, 2021) to instantiate a Cognitive Structure Diffusion Probabilistic Model (CSDPM), and adopts the eager policy gradient from GDPO (Liu et al., 2024c) for RL-based fine-tuning. It departs from prior KT/CD methods—including recent diffusion-based approaches like DiffuQKT, MSKT, and DiffCog—that model student states as latent vectors over individual concepts, by instead generating explicit learner-specific graphs whose edges encode inter-concept relations. The two-stage paradigm (pretraining on rule-based simulated structures, then RL fine-tuning with SOLO-based hierarchical rewards) is novel in this domain, and the use of edge-aware graph pooling (Zhu et al., 2023) to bridge generated graphs to simple downstream models (DKT, NCD) is a practical design choice that keeps the evaluation focused on the quality of the generated structures rather than downstream model complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling cognitive structure representation from downstream prediction, CSG produces interpretable, transferable graph representations that can be reused across KT, CD, and potentially other student-modeling tasks without retraining the generator. The interpretability gain is concrete: nodes and edges directly correspond to concept mastery and relation construction, enabling educators to inspect a student's knowledge state rather than relying on opaque latent vectors. Key limitations include dependence on the Q-matrix for the simulation stage (introducing potential misspecification bias), evaluation only on simple downstream models (DKT, NCD) rather than state-of-the-art architectures, and the rule-based simulation being a proxy rather than ground truth. Open questions include whether the approach scales to domains with hundreds of concepts and dense relation structures, whether the Q-matrix dependency can be eliminated, and whether the SOLO-based reward generalizes beyond the five-level taxonomy to more fine-grained or domain-specific developmental scales.
