## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models generate samples following their learned priors, which often mismatch a user's target distribution specified by only a few reference examples (e.g., 50–500 images). Existing inference-time guidance methods (classifier guidance, CFG, score distillation) optimize surrogate objectives like class likelihoods rather than directly minimizing distributional discrepancy, leaving no principled way to align generated samples to an arbitrary reference distribution without expensive retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of reference images (typically 50–500) from the target distribution, which are encoded once into the LDM's latent space via the VAE encoder; in prompt-aware mode, each reference image is paired with a text prompt. The output is a batch of generated images (at 512×512 or 1024×1024 depending on the base model) whose empirical distribution matches the reference set's distribution. All MMD computation and gradient updates occur in the low-dimensional latent space (e.g., 4×64×64 for SDXL) rather than pixel space, and the guidance is applied at every reverse-diffusion timestep with a schedule λ_t.

## Method

MMD Guidance augments the standard reverse-diffusion update by subtracting the gradient of the empirical squared MMD between the current batch of noisy latents and the encoded reference set: z_{t-1}^{(i)} = sampler(z_t^{(i)}, t, ε_θ) − λ_t ∇_{z_t^{(i)}} MMD². The gradient decomposes into an intra-batch repulsion term (promoting diversity) and a cross-term attraction toward reference samples (driving distribution matching), with the cross term providing a concentration-bounded estimate of the population gradient (Theorem 4.2). For prompt-aware conditional generation, a product kernel k_⊗([p,z],[p′,z′]) = k_p(p,p′)·k_z(z,z′) is used so that the prompt kernel acts as a semantic attention weight, weighting reference samples by prompt similarity. MMD is chosen over f-divergences and Wasserstein distances because it yields unbiased, low-variance estimates with sample complexity independent of ambient dimension and admits closed-form differentiable gradients. The entire procedure is training-free, requires no parameter modification, and operates efficiently in the LDM latent space with only a modest runtime overhead (~10% over unguided sampling).

## Results

On FFHQ (500 reference samples per user), MMD Guidance achieves the best fidelity and coverage across both user subsets: FD of 693.0 and KD of 3.25 for the sunglasses user, and FD of 574.3 and KD of 1.39 for the reading-glasses user, while also attaining the highest Density (1.131 and 0.871) and Coverage (0.791 and 0.846) scores. On the prompt-aware SDXL benchmark (100 reference samples), MMD Guidance reduces FD from 1953.8 to 1674.4 and increases Coverage from 11.34 to 34.20 (×10²). On synthetic 8-component GMMs, MMD Guidance achieves the lowest FD (0.33–0.64) and KD (14.46–17.02 ×10³) across all three users, outperforming fine-tuning and all training-free baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMD Guidance outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric in the main FFHQ comparison (Table 1). Against Domain Guidance (Zhong et al., 2025), the closest training-free competitor, MMD achieves lower FD (693.0 vs. 710.6 for sunglasses; 574.3 vs. 667.6 for reading-glasses) and lower KD (3.25 vs. 4.06; 1.39 vs. 2.08), while also improving Density and Coverage. Against Classifier Guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), MMD is substantially better: CG yields FD of 1195.9 and Coverage of only 0.273 for the sunglasses user, whereas MMD reaches FD 693.0 and Coverage 0.791, indicating CG fails to capture the target distribution's diversity. Against Classifier-Free Guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022), which is evaluated on the synthetic GMM benchmarks (Table 6), MMD achieves FD of 0.33–0.64 versus CFG's 0.97–2.20 and KD of 14.46–17.02×10³ versus CFG's 136–197×10³, confirming that CFG's amplification of conditioning does not substitute for explicit distributional alignment. Notably, MMD Guidance also surpasses training-based baselines (fine-tuning and user-trained DMs) while requiring zero parameter updates, and the runtime overhead is modest (~9% additional time for 500 samples on SDXL per Table 3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of diffusion model guidance (classifier guidance, classifier-free guidance) and kernel-based distributional statistics (MMD). It departs from prior guidance methods—classifier guidance, CFG, and score distillation—which optimize surrogate objectives (class likelihoods, conditioning amplification, external losses) rather than directly minimizing a distributional discrepancy between generated and reference samples. The concurrent work of Galashov et al. (2025) also leverages MMD but as a training loss for a learnable timestep-dependent guidance function, whereas this paper injects ∇MMD² directly into the reverse diffusion sampler at inference time with no parameter updates. The product-kernel extension for prompt-aware adaptation draws on tensor-product RKHS structure (Bamberger et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2025) but applies it in a new guidance context for conditional diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, inference-time nature of MMD Guidance makes it immediately deployable in privacy-constrained or compute-limited settings where retraining a diffusion model on a user's small reference set is infeasible. The concentration bounds (Theorems 4.2, 4.4) provide rare theoretical grounding for why the empirical MMD gradient is a reliable steering signal even with 50–100 reference samples, addressing a practical concern that has limited prior distribution-matching approaches. Open questions include: whether adaptive kernel bandwidth selection can further improve robustness across domain gaps, whether the method extends naturally to sequential modalities (video), and how it interacts with other divergences (Wasserstein, f-divergences) in a complementary ensemble. The authors note that extremely sparse reference sets (n < 10) remain challenging due to gradient variance.
