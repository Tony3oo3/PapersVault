## Problem

The Ledoux-Talagrand contraction lemma is a cornerstone of classical generalization theory, but it was unknown whether it extends to the sequential Rademacher complexity (governing online learning) or the offset Rademacher complexity (governing squared-loss regression). This paper resolves the question affirmatively up to polylogarithmic factors under symmetry and convexity assumptions, and proves these factors are tight, showing that contraction fundamentally fails without such structural conditions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a function class F (assumed C-bounded, symmetric, and in the offset case also convex and Donsker), a 1-Lipschitz contraction ℓ, a binary tree z of depth n (for the sequential setting), and an offset parameter η (for the offset setting). It produces explicit upper bounds of the form R_seq(ℓ∘F, z) ≤ O(C²d^{3/2} log^{5/2}(Cn)) · R_seq(F, z) and R^η(ℓ∘F) ≲ √log(n)·(1+1/η)·R^η(F), together with matching lower-bound constructions demonstrating that the polylogarithmic and dimension-dependent factors cannot be improved in general.

## Method

For the sequential case, the key insight is to relate the tree-dependent sequential Rademacher complexity to the predictable quadratic variation of the associated martingale, which satisfies contraction trivially since |ℓ(x)|≤|x| implies ⟨T_{ℓ∘f}⟩_n ≤ ⟨T_f⟩_n. Burkholder-Davis-Gundy-type inequalities (via Freedman's inequality and escape-time tail bounds) then show the quadratic variation approximates the running maximum of the tree process up to polylog(Cn|F|) factors, with symmetry of F ensuring the supremum of absolute values equals the supremum of signed values. For the offset case, the proof relates R^η(F) to the critical radius r̃_n(F) from Liang et al. (2015), shows r̃_n satisfies contraction via covering-number monotonicity, and uses Fano's inequality with Gaussian regression to establish the matching lower bound. The lower-bound constructions exploit random walks: a sticky reflecting barrier at zero yields the Ω(√n) failure without symmetry, while the longest constant-sign block in a random binary string yields the Ω(log n) lower bound even for symmetric classes.

## Results

For sequential Rademacher complexity, the paper proves that contraction holds up to a factor of O(C² d^{3/2} log^{5/2}(Cn)) for symmetric C-integral classes with sequential scale-sensitive dimension d (Theorem 2.1), and shows this is qualitatively tight: without symmetry the factor degrades to Ω(√n) (Theorem 2.2), and even with symmetry an Ω(d + log n) factor is unavoidable (Theorem 2.3). For offset Rademacher complexity, contraction holds up to √log(n)·(1+1/η) for convex, symmetric, Donsker classes with η ≤ (4√log n)^{-1} (Theorem 2.4), with a matching Ω(log n) lower bound (Theorem 2.6). Absent both symmetry and convexity, no contraction inequality is possible at all: the offset Rademacher complexity can be zero while the contracted version is strictly positive (Theorem 2.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a pure theory paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are to prior theoretical results. The classical Ledoux–Talagrand contraction (factor 1) does not extend to sequential or offset settings without additional structure. The prior worst-case sequential contraction of Rakhlin et al. (2015) achieves O(log^{3/2} n) but only for the supremum over all trees, not for a fixed tree z; the present paper's Theorem 2.1 is the first per-tree (tree-dependent) contraction, at the cost of a d^{3/2} log^{5/2}(Cn) factor. Block et al. (2021) improved the worst-case bound to be independent of n under combinatorial assumptions, but again only for the worst-case quantity. For offset Rademacher complexity, no prior contraction result existed; Theorem 2.4 provides the first, requiring convexity, symmetry, and the Donsker condition. The lower bounds (Theorems 2.2, 2.3, 2.5, 2.6) demonstrate that the polylogarithmic and dimension-dependent factors in the upper bounds are essentially necessary, and that removing symmetry or convexity causes contraction to fail entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Ledoux-Talagrand contraction lemma (1991) from classical Rademacher complexity to its sequential and offset variants, building directly on the sequential Rademacher complexity framework of Rakhlin et al. (2010, 2015) and the offset Rademacher complexity of Liang et al. (2015). Prior contraction results (Rakhlin et al. 2015; Block et al. 2021) applied only to worst-case (over all trees) sequential Rademacher complexity; this paper provides the first tree-dependent contraction inequalities, using quadratic variation and Burkholder-Davis-Gundy-type martingale estimates as the key technical bridge. For the offset case, the paper leverages the critical radius framework from Liang et al. (2015) and Fano's inequality lower bounds (Yang & Barron, 1999) to connect contraction to minimax regression rates, a route not previously explored for this purpose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The contraction lemma is the workhorse that allows uniform convergence machinery to be applied to composed function classes (e.g., neural networks with Lipschitz activations), so establishing its validity for sequential and offset complexities directly determines whether one can obtain tight sample complexity bounds for online learning and squared-loss regression over such classes. The paper's negative results—that symmetry is necessary (√n failure without it) and that convexity is necessary for offset contraction (complexity can be 0 while the contracted version is positive)—sharply delineate the boundary of what is possible. Open questions include extending contraction to distribution-dependent sequential complexity, sequential offset Rademacher complexity, and whether the polylogarithmic factors can be removed under stronger structural assumptions.
