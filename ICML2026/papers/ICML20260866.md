## Problem

Consistency models enable one-step super-resolution without expensive teacher distillation, but suffer from two critical failures: (1) consistency drift, where the transitive training mechanism accumulates errors along the PF-ODE trajectory, and (2) Geometric Decoupling, a phenomenon where pixel-wise alignment (e.g., via LPIPS-based DTM) achieves positional convergence yet fails to preserve local structural coherence, resulting in incoherent geometric structures and sub-optimal texture recovery.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image (64×64 for ×4 synthetic SR, 128×128 for real-world benchmarks) and produces a high-resolution RGB image (256×256 or 512×512) in one forward pass. The LR image is consumed as a residual prior within a ResShift-style forward diffusion process, where the noisy state is constructed as x_t = x_0 + α_t·(y_0 − x_0) + σ_t·ε, explicitly leveraging the LR-HR residual to condition the generative trajectory. Inference requires only 0.08 seconds on an RTX 4090 with 172M parameters.

## Method

GTASR employs a two-stage consistency training paradigm. Stage I combines standard Consistency Training (L_CT) with a Trajectory Alignment (L_TA) loss that re-projects the predicted clean sample back onto the noisy manifold via the forward operator Q at multiple discretized timesteps, enforcing frequency-band-specific consistency that rectifies the PF-ODE tangent vector field and mitigates drift. Stage II introduces Distribution Trajectory Matching (L_DTM) alongside a Dual-Reference Structural Rectification (DRSR) mechanism derived from a theoretical upper bound on Sobel-domain structural error: a Stability Loss (L_Stab) minimizes the consistency gap between fake and real trajectory endpoints in the gradient domain, while a Rectification Loss (L_Rect) anchors the online model's structural predictions to the ground-truth Sobel map. The key insight is that the noise schedule acts as an implicit dynamic filter—prioritizing global semantic structure at high noise levels and high-frequency texture at low noise levels—enabling targeted supervision across the full trajectory rather than only at the clean endpoint.

## Results

On ImageNet-Test, GTASR-1 achieves the best perceptual quality among all one-step methods: MANIQA 0.5826 (vs. CTMSR-1's 0.4857), TOPIQ 0.7423 (vs. 0.6793), and CLIPIQA 0.7475 (vs. 0.6912), while maintaining the same 0.08 s inference latency and 172 M parameters. On the real-world RealSR benchmark, GTASR-1 also leads in perceptual metrics (MUSIQ 66.50, MANIQA 0.4694, TOPIQ 0.6732) and achieves the highest PSNR (26.34 dB) among all compared methods. The method trades a small PSNR/SSIM drop on ImageNet-Test (24.01 dB / 0.6520 vs. CTMSR's 24.73 / 0.6660) for substantially richer texture, a trade-off the authors attribute to more aggressive high-frequency generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest one-step baseline CTMSR-1, GTASR wins on every perceptual metric on ImageNet-Test (MANIQA +0.097, TOPIQ +0.063, CLIPIQA +0.056, MUSIQ +4.92) at identical 0.08 s runtime and 172 M parameters, while sacrificing ~0.7 dB PSNR. On RealSR, GTASR also leads CTMSR in all perceptual scores (TOPIQ 0.6732 vs 0.5965, MANIQA 0.4694 vs 0.4154) and achieves the highest PSNR (26.34 vs 26.19 dB). Compared to multi-step diffusion methods (StableSR-200, ResShift-15/4, UPSR-5), GTASR is 14×–140× faster (0.08 s vs 0.35–11.21 s) while surpassing them on all no-reference quality metrics. Against GAN-based baselines (BSRGAN, Real-ESRGAN), GTASR dominates every perceptual score (e.g., TOPIQ 0.7423 vs 0.6078 on ImageNet-Test) but yields lower PSNR/SSIM, consistent with its generative texture emphasis. Versus T2I-distillation methods (OSEDiff-1, AddSR-4), GTASR uses only ~10% of the parameters (172 M vs 1775–2280 M) and runs 3×–9× faster, while matching or exceeding their perceptual quality on RealLQ250 and RealSet65 (e.g., TOPIQ 0.7047 vs 0.6075 for OSEDiff on RealLQ250). The only metric where GTASR trails is NIQE on ImageNet-Test (5.83 vs CTMSR's 5.66), a minor gap in a no-reference naturalness score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CTMSR (You et al., 2025), which first brought Consistency Training and Distribution Trajectory Matching to one-step super-resolution, by diagnosing and addressing two specific failure modes: consistency drift from transitive CT supervision and a newly identified 'Geometric Decoupling' where pixel-wise trajectory alignment fails to preserve local structural coherence. The forward diffusion formulation and noise schedule are inherited from ResShift (Yue et al., 2023), and the overall training paradigm rests on Consistency Models (Song et al., 2023). Conceptually, the paper departs from the T2I-distillation line (OSEDiff, AddSR) by achieving competitive or superior quality with a 172M-parameter model trained from scratch on ImageNet alone, rather than inheriting billion-scale generative priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of Geometric Decoupling—where low pixel-wise MAE coexists with high Sobel-domain structural error—challenges the implicit assumption in DTM-style losses that positional convergence guarantees structural fidelity, and the derived upper bound (Eq. 11) provides a principled decomposition into two tractable sub-objectives. Practically, GTASR's 0.08s / 172M-parameter footprint makes one-step real-world SR viable on edge hardware, a regime previously dominated by either multi-step diffusion or heavy T2I distillation. Open questions include whether the Sobel-based structural constraint generalizes to other edge/gradient operators or to video SR, whether the two-stage training (500K + 4K iterations) can be compressed, and whether the method's reliance on paired ImageNet training limits its robustness to truly unknown real-world degradations beyond those simulated by the Real-ESRGAN pipeline.
