## Problem

Large-scale notification dispatch requires jointly optimizing timing, presentation style, and volume under platform budgets and device-vendor per-user quotas, but existing methods face two gaps: (1) standard Lagrangian relaxation becomes computationally intractable when per-user quota constraints scale to billions of instances, and (2) uplift models treat multi-dimensional bundle treatments (time × style) and multiple outcomes independently, failing to capture cross-treatment and cross-outcome structural relationships that are critical for accurate small-effect estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 700 tabular features derived from each user's 30-day behavioral history (categorical features embedded to 5 dimensions, concatenated, layer-normalized, and projected to a 128-dim shared representation) along with the bundle treatment grid (H time slots × K presentation styles) and constraint parameters (platform budget C, global quota Q, per-user per-style vendor quotas q_{i,k}). It produces a binary dispatch decision z_{i,j} for every user–bundle pair (at most one notification per user per time slot), together with predicted incremental DAU (Δd_{i,j}) and incremental cost (Δc_{i,j}) for each bundle, which jointly drive the downstream assignment.

## Method

BUOPLR is a two-stage method. The uplift model uses 2K MMoE modules (one per outcome–style pair) to learn implicit user-specific temporal representations, then applies explicit relational structure: it predicts ROI and cost jointly (recovering DAU as ROI × cost) to enforce outcome coupling, and enforces style monotonicity by predicting non-negative adjacent uplifts and cumulating them. To handle small, noisy uplift signals under sparse counterfactual observation, a sliding-window reconstruction (window size 3, stride 2 over sorted styles) chains adjacent treatments for cross-treatment supervision, combined with a direct binary outcome prediction head in a multi-task loss. The billion-scale solver defines a composite score s_{ij} blending net return and same-slot style competitiveness, ranks bundles per user–style pair, and retains only the top q_{i,k} candidates, thereby satisfying all N×K vendor quota constraints by construction and reducing the Lagrangian dual to just two global multipliers (budget λ and quota μ) that converge in ~100–200 subgradient iterations, yielding an effectively O(N) per-iteration cost.

## Results

On the CRITEO dataset, BUOPLR achieves AUUC 0.0057 and QINI 0.1753, outperforming all baselines by 7–8% relative gain. On the more challenging Notification dataset (40 bundle treatments, 3.5M samples per treatment), BUOPLR reaches AUUC 0.0089 and QINI 0.0081, improving 60–80% over the best baseline. On the synthetic dataset with full counterfactuals, BUOPLR attains AUUC 0.0888 and QINI 0.1064, roughly 1.5× the next-best method (DFCL). In online A/B testing on Kuaishou, BUOPLR increases DAU by 0.5% (p<0.01) and CTR by 3.9% over the production TimeWheel baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BUOPLR outperforms all three baselines on every dataset and metric. Against S-learner (Kunzel et al., 2019), BUOPLR gains 76% AUUC on the Notification dataset (0.0089 vs 0.0054) and 76% on the synthetic set (0.0888 vs 0.0504). Against Causal Forest (Athey & Wager, 2019), the advantage is similar: 56% AUUC improvement on Notification (0.0089 vs 0.0057) and 81% on synthetic (0.0888 vs 0.0491). Against DFCL (Zhou et al., 2024), the closest competitor, BUOPLR still leads by 56% AUUC on Notification (0.0089 vs 0.0057) and 52% on synthetic (0.0888 vs 0.0584), while on CRITEO the gap narrows to ~7% (AUUC 0.0057 vs 0.0053; QINI 0.1753 vs 0.1625). On the AUCC metric for resource allocation (Table 2), BUOPLR scores 0.8030 versus DFCL's 0.7859, Causal Forest's 0.7440, and S-learner's 0.6490, confirming its advantage in cost-aware dispatch ranking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lagrangian dual relaxation framework for billion-scale knapsack problems (Zhang et al., 2020) by introducing a score-based domain pruning step that eliminates per-user vendor quota constraints from the dual, reducing the number of Lagrange multipliers from O(N·K) to a constant. On the modeling side, it departs from standard uplift approaches (S-learner, Causal Forest, DFCL) that treat treatments as single-dimensional, instead formulating a 'bundle treatment' space (time slot × presentation style) with explicit structural priors: ROI coupling between outcomes, monotonicity across styles, and implicit temporal representations via MMoE. The sliding-window reconstruction mechanism for cross-treatment supervision is a novel training strategy not present in prior uplift or multi-task learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The score-based pruning technique offers a general recipe for handling massive per-user constraints in any large-scale allocation problem, not just notifications, and the O(1/N) vanishing-gap guarantee (via Shapley-Folkman) provides theoretical justification for the approximation. The bundle treatment uplift architecture addresses a real gap: most uplift models assume a single treatment dimension, while industrial systems increasingly require joint optimization over multiple treatment axes. Key limitations include the assumption of temporal independence between notifications (no sequential modeling), the reliance on a monotonicity prior that may not hold for all style pairs, and evaluation against relatively weak baselines (S-learner, Causal Forest) that do not themselves handle bundle treatments. Open questions include whether the framework extends to real-time streaming decisions and whether the pruning approximation degrades under highly heterogeneous per-user quotas.
