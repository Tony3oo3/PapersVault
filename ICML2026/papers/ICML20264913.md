## Problem

Existing image dehazing methods—whether supervised (relying on synthetic hazy pairs) or unsupervised (requiring large real hazy datasets)—fundamentally depend on observing hazy image distributions during training, which limits generalization to unseen atmospheric conditions. This paper asks whether hazy images are truly necessary for learning to dehaze and proposes the first fully zero-shot framework trained exclusively on real clean images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264913/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, a single hazy RGB image at 512×512 resolution is processed by first extracting four domain-invariant representations (circular chromatic, standardized local residual, projected orientation field, and low-frequency illumination), which condition a diffusion model to generate the corresponding clean image. A post-processing step based on Fattal et al. (2002) refines output saturation. Training uses only ~20,000 clean images merged from OTS and Snow100K, with no hazy data at any stage.

## Method

The core insight is that four physically grounded representations—hue (encoded circularly via sin/cos), standardized local residual (pixel minus local mean, divided by local std), projected gradient orientation (arctan of Sobel gradient ratio), and low-frequency Gaussian-blurred illumination—remain statistically invariant between hazy and clean images under the Atmospheric Scattering Model, as proven analytically and validated via JS divergence, cosine similarity, and t-SNE. A Stable Diffusion v2-1 backbone is trained as a conditional generative model that maps these invariant representations to clean images, learning the clean-scene distribution without ever seeing hazy data. At inference, the same representations extracted from an unseen hazy image serve as the conditioning signal, guiding the diffusion process toward the clean image distribution. This decouples training entirely from degradation-specific data, enabling zero-shot generalization to previously unseen haze types and densities.

## Results

On the four real-world hazy datasets (URHI, RTTS, 500 Foggy, Fattal), the proposed zero-shot method achieves the best FADE (haze density, lower is better) on URHI (0.613), RTTS (0.695), and 500 Foggy (0.404), and the best MUSIQ and PAQ2PIQ (perceptual quality, higher is better) across all four datasets. For example, on URHI it reaches MUSIQ 62.92 and PAQ2PIQ 71.64, surpassing the strongest unsupervised baseline ZRID (56.77 / 69.70) by over 6 and 2 points respectively. On the Fattal dataset, it attains the highest MUSIQ (70.63) and PAQ2PIQ (75.86), outperforming all supervised and unsupervised competitors. The method is trained exclusively on ~20,000 clean images from OTS and Snow100K, with no hazy data at any stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed zero-shot method outperforms all six supervised baselines (DEA-Net, NDR, MB-TylorV2, SFMN, SSID) by large margins on every metric across all four datasets; for instance, on URHI it improves MUSIQ by 3.4–6.1 points and FADE by 0.9–1.1 over the best supervised method SSID. Against the three unsupervised baselines, it achieves the best MUSIQ and PAQ2PIQ on all datasets (e.g., +6.15 MUSIQ over ZRID on URHI, +3.92 over DNMGDT on RTTS) and the best FADE on URHI, RTTS, and 500 Foggy. The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is FADE on the Fattal dataset, where ZRID scores 0.294 versus 0.327 for the proposed method, though the gap is small. On the non-uniform illumination enhancement task (NPE dataset), the zero-shot model achieves MUSIQ 66.46, surpassing the dedicated Zero-DCE++ method (63.34) and all dehazing baselines, while matching Zero-DCE++ on PAQ2PIQ (73.14 vs. 73.40). Overall, the zero-shot paradigm consistently matches or exceeds haze-trained methods without ever observing degraded data during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Atmospheric Scattering Model (Narasimhan & Nayar, 2002) as a physical foundation, deriving four invariant representations (circular chromatic, standardized local residual, projected orientation field, low-frequency illumination) that remain statistically consistent across hazy and clean domains. It departs fundamentally from both supervised dehazing (which trains on synthetic hazy-clean pairs) and unsupervised dehazing (which still requires real hazy images during training) by formulating dehazing as a conditional generative problem on a diffusion backbone (Stable Diffusion v2-1), trained exclusively on clean images. The zero-shot learning paradigm from high-level vision (Lampert et al., 2014) is extended here to low-level restoration, where identifying domain-invariant representations is substantially harder due to the continuous, pixel-level nature of degradations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for any hazy training data, this framework addresses a core practical bottleneck in dehazing: the difficulty of collecting diverse real-world hazy images and the poor generalization of models trained on limited or synthetic haze distributions. The approach also generalizes to other degradations (non-uniform illumination) without retraining, suggesting the invariant-representation-plus-diffusion paradigm could transfer to other restoration tasks. Limitations include degraded performance in dense haze and nighttime scenarios, and the reliance on physically reasonable assumptions (uniform airlight, locally constant transmission) that may not hold in all real-world conditions. Open questions include whether the invariant representation framework can be generalized to more complex or compound degradations, and whether the diffusion backbone can be replaced with lighter architectures for real-time deployment.
