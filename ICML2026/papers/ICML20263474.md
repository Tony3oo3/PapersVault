## Problem

Scaling exact Gaussian processes beyond ~10,000 data points has been considered infeasible because standard kernels produce dense covariance matrices, forcing O(|D|³) computation. Existing scalable GP methods (inducing points, kernel interpolation, Vecchia, NNGP) all introduce approximations that degrade prediction accuracy and restrict kernel and noise-model flexibility. The paper identifies the root cause as a model misspecification: sparsity is not naturally absent from the data but is hidden by kernel designs that cannot return zero covariance for non-adjacent points.

## Input / Output

The method accepts labeled data (x, y) where x can reside in arbitrary input spaces—low-dimensional Euclidean, 8-dimensional tabular, 28×28 pixel arrays (MNIST), or 3D spatiotemporal coordinates—along with user-defined kernel hyperparameters and optional bump-function positions. It outputs posterior mean predictions, calibrated posterior standard deviations, and a sparse covariance matrix in CSR format. Unlike approximate methods that require user-specified inducing point counts or neighbor radii, gp2Scale requires no such structural choices; the sparsity pattern emerges from the kernel's compact support during training. The 10-million-point production run consumed 1024 A100 GPUs on NERSC's Perlmutter system with ~477 seconds per MCMC iteration.

## Method

gp2Scale introduces a class of compactly supported non-stationary kernels built from Wendland kernels, convolution-based non-stationary extensions (Paciorek-Schervish), bump functions for far-field interactions, and collapsed deltas for discrete input spaces. These kernels act as sparsity-discovering masks on arbitrary user-defined core kernels, so that the GP's covariance matrix becomes naturally sparse where the data supports it, rather than having sparsity imposed by approximation. Training proceeds via a customized block Metropolis-Hastings MCMC that samples hyperparameters in separate blocks for faster convergence and natural regularization, while a DASK-based distributed pipeline computes the full covariance matrix in parallel across GPU workers, assembling it in sparse COO/CSR format. The key distinction from all competing methods is that the GP remains exact—no inducing points, no kernel interpolation, no neighborhood truncation—so all standard GP customizations (arbitrary non-stationary kernels, heteroscedastic noise, custom mean functions) are preserved without modification.

## Results

On the 10-million-point US temperature dataset, gp2Scale achieves RMSE 2.8509, narrowly beating Vecchia (2.8602) and substantially outperforming SVGP (5.90) and VNNGP (5.21). On the 20,000-point US topography dataset, gp2Scale (kernel 6) achieves RMSE 136.3 and CRPS 63.8, outperforming Vecchia (150.0 / 78.4), SKI (206.3 / 117.3), VNNGP (236.0 / 175.5), and SVGP (266.5 / 148.0). On the 60,000-sample MNIST regression task, gp2Scale achieves a Brier score of 0.018, roughly half that of SVGP (0.033) and VNNGP (0.052). On the 8-dimensional California Housing dataset, gp2Scale achieves RMSE 0.49 and CRPS 0.27, beating all four baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

gp2Scale outperforms all four baselines on the topography dataset, achieving RMSE 136.3 vs. Vecchia's 150.0 (the closest competitor) and SVGP's 266.5, with a 9% improvement over Vecchia in RMSE and a 19% improvement in CRPS. On the 10-million-point temperature dataset, gp2Scale (RMSE 2.8509) edges out Vecchia (2.8602) by a narrow margin while SVGP and VNNGP are roughly 2× worse. On MNIST, gp2Scale's Brier score of 0.018 is nearly half that of SVGP (0.033) and VNNGP (0.052); SKI and Vecchia could not be applied to this high-dimensional input space. On the 8-dimensional California Housing dataset, gp2Scale (RMSE 0.49) beats SVGP (0.60), Vecchia (0.60), VNNGP (0.66), and SKI (0.71), though the authors note Vecchia was competitive here due to the high dimensionality limiting sparsity discovery. On the 1-D synthetic function, gp2Scale (RMSE 0.107) matches a standard base GP (0.109) while all four approximate methods oversmooth and score 0.19–0.20, confirming that the exact GP preserves local oscillations that approximations lose.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proof-of-concept from Noack et al. (2023), which demonstrated exact GPs on 5M climate points using a single kernel, into a full methodology with a class of compactly supported non-stationary kernels, a distributed-computing pipeline, and a tailored block-MCMC. The kernel constructions draw on the convolution-of-kernels result of Paciorek & Schervish (2006) and the product-kernel framework of Noack & Sethian (2022). Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant approximate-GP paradigm (SVGP, VNNGP, SKI, Vecchia) by arguing that covariance-matrix density is an artifact of kernel design rather than an inherent property of GPs, and that sparsity should be *discovered* through flexible kernel choice rather than *induced* via user-specified inducing points or neighbor sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that exact GPs with full kernel, noise, and mean-function customizability become feasible at 10M+ data points, removing the accuracy and flexibility compromises inherent in all major approximation families. However, the compute cost is substantial (1024 A100 GPUs, ~1 week wall-clock for a full 10M-point run), and the performance gains over the best approximations (Vecchia) are modest on most benchmarks, limiting the method to settings where exactness and customizability are scientifically non-negotiable. Open questions include whether the MCMC convergence can be accelerated to reduce the iteration count, whether the kernel class generalizes to truly high-dimensional (>10D) settings where sparsity discovery becomes harder, and whether the distributed pipeline can be made more memory-efficient for even larger datasets.
