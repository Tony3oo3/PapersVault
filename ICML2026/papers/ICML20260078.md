## Problem

The feed-forward perceptron block is largely absent from the variational mean-field picture of Transformer dynamics. While pure self-attention admits non-atomic stationary densities (the uniform measure is the global minimizer in the repulsive regime), practical evidence suggests perceptrons qualitatively reshape the landscape. This paper asks: what is the precise effect of the perceptron on the structure of stationary states in the mean-field limit, and can one prove that the coupled dynamics generically produce discrete, localized configurations?

## Input / Output

The method operates on a probability measure μ ∈ P(S^{d-1}) representing the distribution of token embeddings, an attention temperature β > 0, and perceptron parameters θ = (a_j, ω_j) with collinear input/output weights. Rather than producing a single prediction, the output is a structural characterization: proofs that stationary measures are purely atomic with finite support (for ReLU in d=2, or generically in d≥2), quantitative anti-concentration bounds showing no cluster at scale 1/√β can exceed mass ≈ 0.5742, and explicit descriptions of energy maximizers as solutions to finite quadratic programs. The analysis is conducted at the measure level (not individual particles), with the key constraint that the perceptron drift must be a gradient field (collinear weights) for the Wasserstein gradient flow structure to hold.

## Method

The paper casts the coupled attention-perceptron dynamics as a Wasserstein gradient flow for the combined energy E_{β,θ}[μ] = (1/2β)∬e^{βx·y}dμ(x)dμ(y) + (1/2)∫v_θ(x)dμ(x), where v_θ is a primitive of the perceptron drift. The atomicity proofs exploit the Funk-Hecke identity to show the attention kernel map μ ↦ ∫e^{βx·y}dμ(y) is injective and that its spherical-harmonic inversion converts polynomial structure in the first variation into polynomial densities. For ReLU in d=2, the non-analyticity of v_θ at the ReLU kink forces any infinite-support stationary measure to have a globally polynomial density, contradicting the piecewise-quadratic form of v_θ across adjacent active regions. For analytic activations (GeLU), the argument upgrades to strict SOPD Wasserstein critical points by constructing tangent perturbations along which the Hessian vanishes. In d≥2, parametric transversality (Lee, 2012) yields generic atomicity. The anti-concentration bound in the repulsive regime follows from the strict concavity of e^{β cos θ} on (−1/√β, 1/√β), which makes the Hessian negative along intra-cluster perturbations unless the cluster mass is bounded by a universal constant.

## Results

The main quantitative result is an anti-concentration bound (Theorem 3.5): in the repulsive (descent) regime on S¹, the total mass of any cluster at the interaction scale 1/√β is bounded by 0.5742 + O(e^{-β}) for sufficiently large β. Corollary 3.6 further shows the number of atoms with mass ≥ ε scales at most as (M/ε)(1 + 2L√β)(0.5742 + O(e^{-β})), i.e., linearly in √β. The central qualitative result (Theorems 3.1–3.3) is that adding a ReLU or GeLU perceptron to self-attention forces all stationary measures to be singular (and generically purely atomic with finite support), even in regimes where pure repulsive attention admits the uniform distribution as a global minimizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is pure self-attention dynamics (Geshkovski et al., 2025), shown side-by-side in Figures 1, 3, 5, and 9. In the attractive (ascent) regime, both pure attention and attention+perceptron collapse to a single Dirac mass, so the perceptron does not qualitatively change the outcome. In the repulsive (descent) regime, the comparison is stark: pure self-attention admits the uniform measure on the sphere as its global minimizer (a fully diffuse equilibrium), whereas the coupled attention+perceptron dynamics force all stationary states to be atomic and singular (Theorem 3.3). Simulations (Figure 9) confirm that the perceptron anchors clusters to seed-independent locations determined by the potential landscape, while pure attention yields seed-dependent cluster positions. The perceptron thus 'wins' by fundamentally reshaping the energy landscape from one with continuous equilibria to one with discrete, localized stationary states.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interacting-particle-system / Wasserstein gradient flow framework for pure self-attention established by Geshkovski et al. (2023, 2025) by incorporating the feed-forward perceptron block, which the authors note is 'still largely absent' from the prior variational mean-field picture. The analysis relies on the Funk-Hecke spectral decomposition of the isotropic attention kernel (Lemma A.1) and the Wasserstein Hessian structure from Otto (2001) and Jordan et al. (1998), but the key departure is showing that the perceptron-induced potential—non-analytic for ReLU, analytic for GeLU—fundamentally reshapes the stationary landscape from potentially diffuse equilibria to generically atomic measures. The anti-concentration bound (Theorem 3.5) builds on the local concavity structure of e^{β cos θ} quantified in Lemma A.3, a tool not previously exploited in the Transformer mean-field literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that perceptrons generically force atomicity provides a rigorous explanation for why real Transformers (which always include MLP blocks) produce discrete, clustered token representations rather than the smooth equilibria predicted by attention-only models. The anti-concentration bound (cluster mass ≤ 0.5742 + O(e^{-β})) is a quantitative guarantee that the repulsive regime cannot collapse to a single atom, with the number of heavy atoms scaling as √β. Open questions include extending the atomicity results to non-conservative (non-collinear-weight) drifts, to general anisotropic attention kernels, and to higher-dimensional anti-concentration estimates; the authors also note that the unique-continuation arguments rely heavily on the isotropic kernel's spectral properties and do not yet extend to general domains.
