## Problem

Diffusion-based video frame interpolation (VFI) handles large motion and occlusions better than feed-forward methods, but its deployment is impractically slow due to two structural inefficiencies: pairwise inference that redundantly re-encodes intermediate keyframes across N−1 separate calls, and multi-step iterative denoising that adds procedural latency. No prior work simultaneously eliminates both the pairwise redundancy and the iterative sampling cost while preserving the generative prior that makes diffusion VFI robust.

## Input / Output

The method takes N sparse keyframes (e.g., 17 frames at 832×448) as input, VAE-encodes them into condition latents, and concatenates them along the temporal axis with M noise latents where M = R(N−1)/C + 1 (R is the interpolation ratio, C is the VAE temporal compression ratio, typically 4). A single forward pass of the one-step generator produces the complete dense interpolated sequence (e.g., 65 frames), from which the conditional portion is discarded and the remaining latents are VAE-decoded into the final video. The formulation naturally supports any interpolation factor that is a multiple of C.

## Method

SpeedVFI recasts generative VFI as unified one-step sequence interpolation: all N keyframes are processed jointly in a single forward pass, and the multi-step denoising trajectory is collapsed into one step via Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) augmented with a flow-matching loss for motion continuity and a pixel-space loss (LPIPS + MSE) for structural fidelity. Two architectural components make this formulation effective: (1) temporal RoPE alignment maps both condition and noise latents onto a shared continuous temporal coordinate system so that positional embeddings reflect true physical spacing rather than token indices, preventing the model from treating interpolation as extrapolation; and (2) noise-centric partial attention restricts self- and cross-attention queries to noise latents only, reducing attention complexity from O((N+M)²) to O(M(N+M)) while preserving full access to conditional context. Condition-noise modulation assigns fixed timesteps (t=0 for condition, t=1 for noise) to give asymmetric DiT modulation, and training uses TTUR with the fake score network updated 5× more frequently than the generator for stability.

## Results

On DAVIS, SpeedVFI achieves PSNR 27.12, SSIM 0.87, LPIPS 0.123, FID 27.01, and FVD 205.69, matching or slightly exceeding the best learning-based method GIMM-VFI (PSNR 27.10, FVD 210.45) while outperforming all diffusion-based baselines by large margins (e.g., FVD 205.69 vs. 338.51 for Framer). On DiversityVideo, SpeedVFI reaches PSNR 34.71 and FVD 116.68, again competitive with GIMM-VFI (PSNR 34.86, FVD 132.63) and far ahead of diffusion baselines. In inference speed, SpeedVFI completes 17→65 frame interpolation in 9.98 s with a single NFE, a 15×–157× speedup over diffusion-based competitors (Framer 150.69 s, FCVG 1570.18 s) and slightly faster than the best learning-based method BiM-VFI (14.16 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three diffusion-based baselines, SpeedVFI wins decisively on every metric: on DAVIS it improves FVD from 338.51 (Framer, the strongest diffusion baseline) to 205.69 and PSNR from 22.79 to 27.12; on DiversityVideo it cuts FVD from 401.89 to 116.68. Against the best learning-based method GIMM-VFI, SpeedVFI is essentially tied on DAVIS (PSNR 27.12 vs. 27.10; FVD 205.69 vs. 210.45) and slightly behind on DiversityVideo PSNR (34.71 vs. 34.86) but ahead on FVD (116.68 vs. 132.63). Against BiM-VFI, SpeedVFI is marginally lower on DAVIS PSNR (27.12 vs. 26.90 — actually higher) and DiversityVideo PSNR (34.71 vs. 34.65 — higher), while being faster (9.98 s vs. 14.16 s). Against the flow-based references RIFE and MoMo (reported in Appendix D), SpeedVFI achieves substantially higher PSNR (27.12 vs. 23.75/24.14 on DAVIS) and FVD (205.69 vs. 554.81/379.16) at the cost of higher TFLOPs (660 vs. 4.5/77), reflecting the generative-model overhead. Overall, SpeedVFI is the only diffusion-based method that reaches the quality tier of the strongest learning-based interpolators while being 15×–157× faster than other diffusion-based VFI systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpeedVFI sits at the intersection of diffusion distillation (DMD/DMD2) and video frame interpolation, adapting the one-step distribution-matching recipe from image/video generation to the VFI setting where additional motion and reconstruction constraints are needed. It departs from the dominant pairwise formulation of diffusion-based VFI (ViBiDSampler, FCVG, Framer) by recasting interpolation as a unified single-pass sequence generation problem, and from generic distillation by introducing task-specific architectural modifications: temporal RoPE alignment for positional consistency between sparse conditions and dense targets, and noise-centric partial attention to exploit the informational asymmetry between fixed conditions and evolving noise. The flow matching loss serves as a VFI-specific bridge between the distribution-level DMD objective and the pixel-level reconstruction fidelity that interpolation demands.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the efficiency gap between generative and feed-forward VFI (9.98s vs. 10–14s for learning-based methods, versus 150–1570s for prior diffusion-based methods), SpeedVFI makes the robustness advantages of generative modeling—handling occlusions, large motion, and ambiguous boundaries—practically deployable for long-sequence densification. Key limitations include validation on a single backbone (Wan-TI2V-5B), pixel-space supervision restricted to the first two generated latents due to memory constraints, and a residual sharpness-smoothness trade-off in extreme cases. Open questions include whether the one-step formulation generalizes across different video diffusion backbones, whether it scales to higher resolutions and longer sequences without quality degradation, and whether the partial attention mechanism transfers to other conditional generation tasks beyond VFI.
