## Problem

While chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning is a major driver of LLM performance on hard tasks, there is no principled understanding of how many reasoning tokens are fundamentally required as input size grows. Existing empirical work on CoT compression reports constant-factor reductions on fixed benchmarks but does not address asymptotic scaling with problem size. This paper asks: what is the minimum reasoning budget (token complexity) required to reliably solve a task as a function of input size, and when is compression even information-theoretically possible?

## Input / Output

The BAPO-CoT model takes a token sequence (the problem instance) and autoregressively generates reasoning tokens, with each token-generation step constrained by constant prefix bandwidth a and attention bandwidth b, limiting how much information can cross any prefix/suffix split. The three canonical problems analyzed take binary strings of length n (MAJORITY), integer sequences in Z_m^n (MATCH3n), and directed graph edge lists with a source-target node pair (REACHABILITY), each producing a binary yes/no answer. The paper's output is the token complexity function c(n)—the minimum worst-case number of CoT tokens any constant-bandwidth BAPO-CoT must produce—alongside empirical measurements of reasoning token counts and accuracy from frontier LLMs at varying input sizes.

## Method

The core contribution is extending the bounded attention prefix oracle (BAPO) model to a multi-step BAPO-CoT framework where each reasoning token is produced by a constant-bandwidth BAPO, then proving Ω(n) lower bounds for MAJORITY, MATCH3n, and REACHABILITY via an adversarial masking construction that ensures two problem instances with different answers remain indistinguishable to the BAPO-CoT at every reasoning step (same prefix oracle output, same attended tokens, same suffix). To prevent an input-doubling loophole that would trivialize upper bounds, they introduce cBAPO (self-consistent BAPO), which requires the prefix oracle to itself be solvable by a BAPO, mirroring the self-similarity of causal transformer layers. Matching or near-matching upper bounds are established through explicit cBAPO-CoT constructions: bit-by-bit counting for MAJORITY (O(n log n)), a scratchpad-based complement search reducing MATCH3n to cBAPO-easy MATCH2n instances (O(n)), and a BFS/DFS graph search tracking visited and active node sets for REACHABILITY (O(n²)), plus a general multi-tape Turing machine simulation bound of O(t(n)·max(n,s(n))). Empirically, GPT-5.2 and Gemini 2.5 Pro exhibit approximately linear reasoning token scaling on all three tasks and substantial accuracy degradation when constrained to sublinear reasoning budgets, consistent with the theoretical lower bounds.

## Results

The paper proves that MAJORITY, MATCH3n, and REACHABILITY all require Ω(n) CoT tokens for any constant-bandwidth BAPO-CoT (Theorems 4.1–4.3), with matching or near-matching upper bounds: O(n log n) for MAJORITY, O(n) for MATCH3n, and O(n²) for REACHABILITY. Empirically, GPT-5.2 with reasoning exhibits approximately linear reasoning-token scaling across all three problems (n = 5 to 101), achieving near-perfect accuracy at all reasoning levels, while Gemini 2.5 Pro uses substantially more tokens (8000–9000 at n = 101 for MATCH3n and MAJORITY) and degrades to ~75% accuracy on MAJORITY at larger n. Disabling reasoning drops accuracy to random chance for both models, and constraining external CoT to 50 or 100 words partially but not fully closes the accuracy gap, consistent with the Ω(n) lower bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so there are no traditional method-vs-method baselines. The key comparisons are: (1) Between the BAPO and cBAPO models, the cBAPO restriction eliminates the 'input-doubling loophole' (Proposition 5.3) that lets a (1,0)-BAPO solve any language with linear tokens, making cBAPO upper bounds (O(n log n), O(n), O(n²)) far more realistic than the trivial n+1 BAPO bound. (2) Against prior theoretical work, the Ω(n) lower bounds align with Bavandpour et al. (2025) who showed Ω(n) CoT steps for parity/multiplication/median in unique hard-attention transformers, and confirm the token complexity hypothesis of Lee et al. (2025) that every problem has an inherent minimal reasoning chain length. (3) Empirically, GPT-5.2 and Gemini 2.5 Pro are compared: GPT-5.2 achieves near-perfect accuracy with ~linear token scaling, while Gemini 2.5 Pro uses 2–3× more tokens and shows accuracy degradation on MAJORITY at larger n. (4) Across reasoning budgets, the 200-word external CoT prompt achieves near-perfect accuracy for MATCH3n and REACHABILITY at n=101, while 50- and 100-word budgets fall below the theoretical minimum, confirming the lower bounds in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BAPO model from Schnabel et al. (2025) from single-step to multi-step (CoT) settings, introducing the formal notion of BAPO token complexity and the cBAPO self-consistency restriction to fix an input-doubling loophole in the original model. It is most closely related to Bavandpour et al. (2025), who derive CoT length bounds for unique hard-attention transformers, and to the empirical 'token complexity hypothesis' of Lee et al. (2025), which this paper confirms with asymptotic bounds. The Turing machine simulation upper bounds extend Schnabel et al.'s one-tape construction to multi-tape, multi-head TMs, and the lower bound proofs adapt the BAPO-hardness pigeonhole technique to account for all possible reasoning chains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear lower bounds on CoT tokens for MAJORITY, MATCH3n, and REACHABILITY translate into at least quadratic compute costs as context grows, identifying a fundamental bottleneck for inference-time scaling that no prompting strategy can overcome. The cBAPO restriction and the input-doubling loophole reveal a structural flaw in the original BAPO model that would otherwise make token complexity upper bounds vacuous. Open questions include whether superlinear cBAPO-CoT token complexity exists, whether REACHABILITY truly requires Θ(n²) tokens or can be solved in O(m+n), and whether constant-bandwidth cBAPOs with finite alphabets can be simulated by Turing machines. The empirical finding that frontier models use substantially more tokens than the linear lower bounds require suggests significant room for constant-factor efficiency gains even on information-theoretically hard tasks.
