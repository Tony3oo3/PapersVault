## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods rely on prediction loss-based objectives (e.g., Gradient Ascent) that maximize loss on the forget set, but these suffer from over-forgetting, favor incoherent gibberish generations, and lack a consistent target value since the ideal post-unlearning loss varies across individual samples. The paper asks whether a more precise, sample-agnostic optimization objective exists that avoids these failure modes while still achieving effective unlearning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263026/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a LoRA-fine-tuned LLM (Llama2-7B or Qwen3-8B), a forget set D_F, a retain set D_R, and text queries for autoregressive response generation. A lightweight attribution classifier (TinyLlama-1.1B or Qwen3-0.6B with LoRA and a classification head) is trained offline on the full dataset before unlearning and remains frozen during the PPO loop. The output is an unlearned LLM whose generated responses carry near-zero attribution scores to forget data owners, evaluated via ROUGE (forget/retain/test), Tug-of-War, Truth Ratio, Extraction Strength, and Membership Inference Attack AUC.

## Method

DareU reframes LLM unlearning as minimizing data attribution scores (de-attribution) rather than maximizing prediction loss, yielding a consistent target value of 0 across all forget samples and avoiding the collapse toward gibberish. A lightweight LLM classifier with LoRA and a classification head approximates the attribution function by predicting which data owner's dataset most likely influenced a given generation, with the prediction probability serving as the attribution score. The unlearned LLM is updated via PPO where the log-transformed attribution score (computed every κ tokens with a cumulative mean to reduce variance) acts as the token-level reward, and a KL-based distillation regularization on the retain set preserves model utility. The log-based transformation χ maps raw attribution scores to [-1, 0] with a sharp penalty for high-attribution generations, enabling DareU to quickly suppress forget-set-influenced responses while maintaining coherent output on the retain set.

## Results

On Llama2-7B × TOFU, DareU achieves a Tug-of-War (ToW) score of 0.816, the highest among all baselines, indicating the best balance between forget quality and model utility. Its D_F ROUGE of 0.407 is closest to the Retraining gold standard (0.381), while maintaining D_R ROUGE of 0.797 and D_test ROUGE of 0.834. On Qwen3-8B × TOFU, DareU similarly leads with ToW 0.797. On the more challenging ArXiv dataset (longer sequences), DareU still achieves the best ToW scores (0.622 for Llama2, 0.660 for Qwen3), outperforming all prediction-loss-based and preference-optimization baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DareU decisively outperforms Gradient Ascent (GA), which catastrophically over-forgets (D_R ROUGE collapses to 0.001, ToW 0.009), and SCRUB, which degrades both forget and retain performance (ToW 0.219). Against GDiff (ToW 0.614) and SCRN (ToW 0.251), DareU achieves substantially better balance, with D_R ROUGE of 0.797 vs. 0.601 and 0.270 respectively. NPO (ToW 0.239) also underperforms DareU by a wide margin on both forget quality and model utility. IDK (ToW 0.548) preserves model utility but forces refusal responses ('I don't know'), yielding a D_F ROUGE of 0.028 far below Retraining's 0.381, indicating it does not approximate a truly unlearned model. Overall, DareU achieves the highest ToW across all four dataset–model combinations (Llama2/Qwen3 × TOFU/ArXiv), confirming it provides the best trade-off between effective unlearning and utility preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DareU departs from the dominant prediction-loss-based unlearning paradigm (Gradient Ascent, GDiff, SCRUB, SCuReNewton) by reframing the optimization objective as minimizing data attribution scores rather than maximizing prediction loss on the forget set. It also distinguishes itself from reward-free RL unlearning methods (DPO/IDK, NPO) by employing reward-based PPO with an explicit attribution classifier as the reward model, avoiding the need for externally constructed refusal datasets. Conceptually, the paper bridges the LLM data attribution literature (LOO, Shapley, influence functions, text watermarking) with RL-based LLM alignment (PPO), proposing a lightweight LLM classifier as a computationally efficient proxy for the ideal attribution function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The de-attribution objective provides a principled, sample-independent target value (zero) that avoids the over-forgetting and gibberish-generation pathologies of loss-maximization approaches, and the consistent ToW improvements across two LLMs and two datasets validate this framing. However, the method requires access to the original fine-tuning dataset to train the attribution classifier, limiting applicability to pre-training data removal, and incurs higher computational cost than simpler baselines due to autoregressive generation in PPO. Open questions include developing more efficient and accurate attribution functions, extending to pre-trained model unlearning, and whether the approach generalizes to settings where the forget set is not cleanly separable from the retain set.
