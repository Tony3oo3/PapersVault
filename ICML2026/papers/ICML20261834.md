## Problem

For the squared exponential (SE) kernel in Gaussian process bandits, a dimension-dependent (ln T)^{d/4} gap persists between the best known upper and lower regret bounds, growing exponentially with dimension d and rendering the bounds non-tight even for moderate d ≤ 20. This paper closes that gap for the hyperspherical input domain S^d, establishing that the existing O^*(√(T(ln T)^d)) upper bound is optimal up to dimension-independent logarithmic factors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261834/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis is purely theoretical and parameterized by dimension d, total steps T, RKHS norm bound B, noise variance σ², and SE kernel lengthscale θ, all defined on the hypersphere S^d ⊂ R^{d+1}. The outputs are closed-form lower bounds on cumulative regret Ω(√(T(ln T)^d (ln ln T)^{-d})) and on the time steps needed for ε-optimal simple regret, plus an improved O((ln T)^{d+1}(ln ln T)^{-d}) upper bound on the maximum information gain that applies to any compact domain in R^d, not just S^d.

## Method

The core contribution is a new hard function class built by approximating the Dirac delta on S^d with a truncated spherical harmonic expansion b_{N,z}(x) = Σ_{n=0}^{N} Σ_j Y_{n,j}(z)Y_{n,j}(x), where (Y_{n,j}) are orthonormal spherical harmonics of degree n. Via the addition theorem, this sum collapses to a closed-form expression in Gegenbauer polynomials (Dirichlet kernel for d = 1), giving explicit control over the peak width w_ε^new = Θ(N^{-1}). The RKHS norm is bounded through Mercer's representation theorem combined with the eigenvalue lower bound λ_n ≥ Ω((2e/θ)^n C_{d,θ}/(2n+d-1)^{n+d/2}) from Minh et al. (2006), which forces N = Θ((ln(B/ε))(ln ln(B/ε))^{-1}) and yields a peak width decaying faster than the bump-function width w_ε = Θ(ln^{-1/2}(B/ε)) used in Scarlett et al. (2017). This faster decay increases the packing number of the hard class, tightening the change-of-measure lower bound from (ln T)^{d/2} to (ln T)^d (ln ln T)^{-d}.

## Results

The paper establishes an improved algorithm-independent worst-case lower bound for GP bandits with the SE kernel on the hypersphere S^d. For cumulative regret, any algorithm suffers Ω(√(T(ln T)^d (ln ln T)^{-d})), and for simple regret, any algorithm requires Ω(ε^{-2}(ln 1/ε)^d (ln ln 1/ε)^{-d}) time steps to find an ε-optimal point. Additionally, the paper proves an improved O((ln T)^{d+1}(ln ln T)^{-d}) upper bound on the maximum information gain (MIG) for the SE kernel, which is applicable to any compact input domain in R^d and yields an O(√((ln ln T)^d)) improvement for existing MIG-based regret guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the prior best lower bounds from Scarlett et al. (2017) and Cai & Scarlett (2021), which established Ω(√(T(ln T)^{d/2})) for cumulative regret and Ω(ε^{-2}(ln 1/ε)^{d/2}) for simple regret. The new lower bound improves the dimension-dependent logarithmic factor from (ln T)^{d/2} to (ln T)^d (ln ln T)^{-d}, effectively closing the (ln T)^{d/4} gap that existed between the upper and lower bounds. For example, at d = 20, the old gap scaled as (ln T)^5, while the new gap is only (ln ln T)^{10}, a dramatic reduction. The improvement is achieved by constructing a new hard function class using spherical harmonics and Mercer's representation theorem, which yields a sharper peak width w_ε^{new} = Θ((ln(B/ε))^{-1}(ln ln(B/ε))) compared to the previous w_ε = Θ((ln(B/ε))^{-1/2}). The paper also notes that the (ln ln T)^{-d} factor in the lower bound is conjectured to be unavoidable, as a matching (ln ln T)^{-d} factor appears in the improved MIG upper bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lower-bound proof strategy of Scarlett et al. (2017) and Cai & Scarlett (2021), which constructed hard function classes via shifted Fourier bump functions on [0,1]^d. The key departure is replacing those bump functions with a new hard function class built from spherical harmonics and Mercer's representation theorem (Minh et al., 2006), exploiting the explicit eigensystem of the SE kernel on the hypersphere. The improved maximum information gain (MIG) upper bound in Theorem 5.1 builds directly on the eigenvalue-decay analysis of Iwazaki (2025b), but with a more precise treatment of the logarithmic correction in the eigenvalue rate. The change-of-measure bounding tool (Lemma 2.3) is inherited unchanged from Cai & Scarlett (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes the (ln T)^(d/4) gap between upper and lower bounds for the SE kernel on the hypersphere, a gap that grows exponentially with dimension and was a fundamental open problem in GP bandit theory. The matching (ln ln T)^(-d) factor appearing in both the new lower bound and the improved MIG upper bound suggests this logarithmic correction may be inherent to the SE kernel, though the authors leave this as a conjecture. A key limitation is that the improved lower bound is restricted to the hyperspherical domain; the authors show that a natural extension to [0,1]^d via Gaussian-measure eigenfunctions fails to improve the rate, pointing to the need for eigensystems matched to bounded domains. The improved MIG upper bound, however, applies to any compact domain in R^d and immediately tightens regret guarantees for GP-UCB, GP-TS, and non-adaptive algorithms.
