## Problem

Existing federated explainability methods either keep explanations siloed per client (causing severe drift under non-IID heterogeneity), transmit dense or sensitive attribution artifacts, or replace expressive task models with interpretable surrogates that sacrifice accuracy. No prior framework provides a mechanism to coordinate cross-client explanation semantics iteratively during training while simultaneously preserving local faithfulness, task accuracy, strict privacy, and kilobyte-scale communication budgets.

## Input / Output

Each client receives a local partition of standard inputs (28×28 grayscale, 32×32 RGB, token sequences, or tabular feature vectors) and produces two outputs: the task model's class prediction and a per-class group attribution vector over a shared modality-agnostic group space G (e.g., 784 pixel groups for MNIST). The explanation artifact transmitted to the server is a sparse top-k vector (k=128 of G=784) per class, ℓ₂-clipped to radius 5.0, 8-bit quantized, and perturbed with Gaussian noise (σ=0.1), totaling roughly 2.9 KB per client per round. The server outputs a Global Explanation Prior — a simplex-normalized, coordinatewise-median consensus distribution over groups for each class — which is broadcast back to all clients.

## Method

xFedAlign decouples task optimization in parameter space from explanation coordination in a compact group space. Each client distills a lightweight surrogate g_i from the deployed task model f_θ via temperature-based KL divergence (Eq. 1), then computes per-example attributions using path-integrated gradients in the surrogate's group space, normalized to the probability simplex (Eq. 2). These dense attributions are compressed into sparse top-k artifacts with ℓ₂ clipping, 8-bit quantization, and additive Gaussian noise, then securely aggregated by the server via coordinatewise median into a Global Explanation Prior (Eq. 4). The prior is broadcast back and incorporated through a Jensen-Shannon divergence alignment penalty (Eq. 3) that nudges each client's explanation distribution toward the global consensus without any gradient flowing into the task model parameters. Because alignment acts only on normalized group distributions in a low-dimensional simplex rather than in ℝ^|θ|, standard FedAvg optimization dynamics and task accuracy are fully preserved while cross-client explanation drift contracts across rounds.

## Results

On MNIST, xFedAlign matches FedAvg accuracy under both IID (0.986±0.001) and non-IID (0.930±0.004) while achieving the lowest Explanation Drift Index (EDI ≈ 0.000 under IID, substantially lower than all baselines under non-IID) and the best deletion/insertion AUCs. Under non-IID, xFedAlign reduces EDI by roughly 50–70% relative to Local-XAI and FedAttr-Agg while preserving perturbation fidelity. On privacy and robustness (MNIST), xFedAlign achieves the lowest membership inference accuracy (0.434 IID, 0.463 non-IID) and the smallest poisoning-induced drift (ΔTop@128 = 0.122 IID, 0.163 non-IID; ΔEDI = 0.003 in both), compared to ΔTop@128 ≈ 0.23 and ΔEDI ≈ 0.005–0.007 for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

xFedAlign matches FedAvg task accuracy on MNIST under both IID (0.986) and non-IID (0.930) while achieving the lowest EDI across all six benchmark datasets, demonstrating that explanation alignment does not sacrifice predictive performance. Against Local-XAI, xFedAlign wins decisively on cross-client consistency under non-IID (EDI drops from ~0.076 to near-zero on MNIST) while maintaining comparable local perturbation fidelity (DelAUC/InsAUC). Against FedAttr-Agg, xFedAlign achieves lower EDI and substantially better insertion AUC (e.g., 0.939 vs. 0.750 on MNIST non-IID), indicating that robust median aggregation of sparse top-k artifacts preserves fine-grained structure that simple averaging washes out. Against Fed-XAI, xFedAlign dominates on both accuracy (0.986 vs. 0.904 on MNIST IID; gap widens under non-IID) and explanation quality, confirming that interpretable-by-design models sacrifice too much capacity. On privacy and robustness, xFedAlign achieves the lowest membership inference accuracy (0.434 IID, 0.463 non-IID) and the smallest poisoning-induced top-k shift (ΔTop@128 = 0.122 IID, 0.163 non-IID vs. ~0.23 for all baselines), with the gap widening under non-IID where Fed-XAI is most vulnerable (ΔTop@128 = 0.245).

## Relation to Prior Work

xFedAlign builds on the FedAvg federated training paradigm and the Integrated Gradients attribution method, but departs from both in a key conceptual way: it decouples task optimization in parameter space from explanation coordination in a low-dimensional group simplex. Prior federated XAI work (Local-XAI, FedAttr-Agg, Fed-XAI) treats explanations as post-hoc artifacts—either siloed per client, coarsely pooled, or baked into an interpretable surrogate that sacrifices accuracy—whereas xFedAlign introduces explanation alignment as a training-time objective via a JSD penalty against a robustly aggregated Global Explanation Prior. The surrogate distillation mechanism borrows from knowledge distillation literature but is repurposed to produce stable, normalized group-level attributions rather than to compress the task model itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling principle—task learning in parameter space, explanation semantics in a compact group space—offers a clean design pattern for trustworthy federated deployments in healthcare, finance, and mobile platforms where both accuracy and auditability are required. The practical communication cost of only a few kilobytes per round makes the approach viable for cross-device settings. However, the framework assumes a stable and meaningful group space G, provides client-level DP under honest secure aggregation (not Byzantine-robust), and does not guarantee fairness or causal faithfulness of aligned explanations. Open questions include extending to regression and federated language models, handling adaptive adversaries beyond the breakdown point of median aggregation, and validating aligned explanations with domain experts.
