## Problem

Blind inverse problems under unknown dynamic range compression (UDRC) require simultaneously estimating an unknown forward operator and recovering the signal from a single measurement, without paired supervision. Existing zero-shot solvers face a fundamental bias-variance dilemma: simplified parametric models (e.g., linear affine in GDP) lack expressivity for complex radiometric curves, while unconstrained neural adapters (e.g., CNNs in TAO) overfit the measurement rather than learning the true degradation physics. The paper identifies monotonicity as the unifying physical invariant across all UDRC modalities (low-light, HDR, low-field MRI) and exploits it as a hard architectural constraint to resolve this dilemma.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded measurement (low-light image, hard-clipped HDR image, or low-field MRI volume) and a pre-trained unconditional diffusion model (ADM on ImageNet for natural images, or a domain-specific DDPM trained on HCP for MRI) as inputs, requiring no task-specific paired training data. It produces a restored high-fidelity image with corrected radiometric mapping. Inference runs 100 DDIM reverse steps, each involving 20 internal gradient iterations (J=20) with step size η=0.01 to jointly update the image estimate and the 16-parameter CaMB operator.

## Method

The core contribution is the Cascaded Monotonic Bernstein (CaMB) operator, which parameterizes the unknown forward model as a composition of K low-degree (N=3) Bernstein polynomials whose coefficients are defined as cumulative sums of a Softmax distribution, guaranteeing monotonicity by construction with only 16 total parameters. This is integrated into a plug-and-play diffusion framework via half-quadratic splitting (HQS): at each reverse diffusion step, a data-fidelity subproblem jointly optimizes the latent image z and CaMB parameters Θ by gradient descent on ‖y − M_Θ(z)‖², while a prior subproblem applies the pre-trained diffusion denoiser to regularize toward the natural image manifold. A U-shaped guidance schedule (λ_t = λ·ᾱ_t/(1−ᾱ_t) + C(t/T)^k) is proposed to prevent premature convergence of the operator in early sampling stages. The theoretical analysis proves that the cascaded composition achieves universal approximation of any continuous monotonic function with linear parameter growth in depth, avoiding the numerical instability of high-degree single polynomials.

## Results

On low-light enhancement (LOLv1), CaMB-Diff achieves PSNR 20.14 dB, SSIM 0.8498, LPIPS 0.2171, FID 72.65, and LOE 82.21, outperforming all zero-shot baselines and achieving the lowest LOE across all three LOL benchmarks. On low-field MRI (HCP T1w), it reaches PSNR 26.43 dB and LPIPS 0.1602, surpassing the best baseline (TAO) by 2.8 dB in PSNR. On HDR reconstruction (ImageNet synthetic), CaMB-Diff attains PSNR 19.95 dB and LPIPS 0.2639, exceeding the nearest competitor (GDP) by over 3 dB in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two universal zero-shot solvers, CaMB-Diff dominates across all three tasks: on LOLv1 it gains +3.08 dB PSNR over GDP (20.14 vs 17.06) and +2.96 dB over TAO (20.14 vs 17.18), while also achieving substantially lower LPIPS (0.2171 vs 0.3581/0.4153) and LOE (82.21 vs 190.22/331.13). Among task-specific zero-shot methods for low-light, CaMB-Diff surpasses AGLLDiff (the strongest zero-shot specialist) by +0.57 dB PSNR, −0.053 LPIPS, and −41.25 FID, though it trails slightly on SSIM (0.8498 vs 0.8699). For low-field MRI, CaMB-Diff outperforms the zero-shot DiffDeuR by +3.26 dB PSNR and −0.101 LPIPS on T1w, and beats the supervised PF-SR by +4.51 dB PSNR. On HDR reconstruction, CaMB-Diff exceeds GDP by +3.15 dB PSNR and TAO by +3.18 dB on ImageNet, and maintains a +2.43 dB margin over GDP on the real HDR-Eye dataset. In the out-of-distribution evaluation (trained on LOLv2-synthetic, tested on LOLv1), CaMB-Diff (PSNR 20.14) dramatically outperforms the supervised SOTA HVI (PSNR 13.63) and Retinexformer (PSNR 11.54), demonstrating superior generalization without paired training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the plug-and-play diffusion (PnP-Diffusion) framework of Zhu et al. (CVPR 2023) by replacing the assumption of a known or parametrically simple forward operator with a structured, physically constrained one. It positions itself between two existing extremes in blind inverse problem solving: the high-bias simplified parametric models (GDP, Fei et al. 2023) and the high-variance unconstrained neural adapters (TAO, Gou et al. 2024), proposing the Cascaded Monotonic Bernstein (CaMB) operator as a middle ground that enforces monotonicity as a hard architectural inductive bias. The theoretical grounding draws on classical Bernstein approximation theory (Lorentz, 2012) and monotonic network literature (Sill, 1997; Gupta et al. 2016), repurposing these tools for the specific challenge of radiometric forward operator estimation in a zero-shot setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CaMB operator offers a principled, extremely parameter-efficient (16 parameters vs. 65K–742K for baselines) solution to the bias-variance dilemma in blind inverse problems, with the monotonicity constraint providing a domain-agnostic physical anchor that transfers across low-light, HDR, and MRI tasks. The validation-error analysis (Fig. 8) provides compelling evidence that structured constraints prevent the overfitting-to-measurement pathology common in unconstrained blind solvers. Open questions include whether the spatial stationarity assumption (Assumption 3.1) holds for more complex degradations (e.g., lens vignetting, spatially-varying sensor response), and whether the framework extends to non-monotonic or multi-channel-coupled forward operators. The U-shaped diffusion guidance schedule is a practical contribution that may benefit other PnP-diffusion applications beyond UDRC.
