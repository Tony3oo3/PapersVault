## Problem

Existing crack segmentation methods face a fundamental trade-off: CNNs fragment long-range cracks due to limited receptive fields, Transformers incur prohibitive quadratic complexity, and Mamba-based approaches disrupt spatial contiguity by flattening 2D images into 1D sequences. Standard Vision-RWKV models further suffer from rigid Q-Shift spatial priors and content-agnostic decay that cannot handle the extreme topological irregularity and severe background noise characteristic of structural cracks, making high-precision segmentation on resource-constrained edge devices an open challenge.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (512×512 in experiments) and produces a binary pixel-level segmentation mask identifying crack regions. The architecture is designed for real-time inference on resource-constrained hardware, achieving 46 FPS (0.0216 s/frame) on a laptop-class server, with a total model size of only 28 MB and 1.22M parameters, making it suitable for UAV-based aerial inspection where video frames are streamed at 30 fps and resized to 512×512 before inference.

## Method

SCRWKV builds on Vision-RWKV's linear-complexity bidirectional WKV operator but replaces its rigid Q-Shift with Geometry-guided Bidirectional Structure Transformation (GBST), which splits channels into outward-diffusing and inward-converging streams to explicitly model curvilinear crack topology. A Dynamic Self-Calibrating Decay (DSCD) mechanism generates content-aware decay factors within the Dy-WKV operator, selectively suppressing noise-dominant tokens while amplifying structure-centric signals during recursive propagation. The backbone (SFE) stacks 4 SCIU blocks, each augmented by an Adaptive Multi-scale Cascaded Modulator (AMCM) that uses cascaded large-kernel convolutions (5, 7, 9, 11) and a learnable topology matrix to expand the receptive field. A Cross-Scale Harmonic Fusion (CSHF) decoder bridges multi-level features via scale-embedding injection and a softmasking gate, producing the final mask with only 1.22M total parameters and 22.78 GFLOPs.

## Results

SCRWKKV achieves SOTA performance across all four crack segmentation benchmarks with only 1.22M parameters. On the TUT dataset (most complex interference), it reaches F1 = 0.8428 and mIoU = 0.8512. On DeepCrack, it attains F1 = 0.9363 and mIoU = 0.9289, outperforming the second-best method (SCSegamba) by 2.53% and 2.67% respectively. On CrackMap, it achieves F1 = 0.7740 and mIoU = 0.8106, and on Crack500, F1 = 0.7552 and mIoU = 0.7787.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCRWKV outperforms all 10 baselines on DeepCrack (F1 0.9363 vs. SCSegamba's 0.9110, a 2.53% margin) and CrackMap (F1 0.7740 vs. SCSegamba's 0.7678). On TUT, it leads SCSegamba by 0.38% in F1 (0.8428 vs. 0.8390) and 0.33% in mIoU (0.8512 vs. 0.8479). On Crack500, SCRWKV's F1 (0.7552) is marginally below SCSegamba (0.7553) by 0.01%, but it wins on mIoU (0.7787 vs. 0.7778) and ODS (0.7256 vs. 0.7244). Among Mamba-based methods, SCRWKV surpasses MambaIR (F1 0.8125 on TUT), CSMamba (0.8146), and PlainMamba (0.8102) by 3+ points in F1 on TUT. In terms of efficiency, SCRWKV uses only 1.22M parameters and 28MB model size, compared to SCSegamba's 2.80M/37MB, Crackmer's 5.90M/43MB, and RIND's 59.39M/453MB, while achieving the highest accuracy across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Vision-RWKV (VRWKV), which adapted the RWKV linear-attention architecture to visual perception via bidirectional WKV operators, by introducing crack-specific structural calibration mechanisms. It departs from VRWKV's fixed Q-Shift spatial interaction (replaced by the bidirectional GBST) and its static, content-agnostic decay (replaced by the dynamic DSCD), while also positioning against Mamba-based crack segmentation methods (SCSegamba, CSMamba, PlainMamba) that rely on rigid 2D-to-1D flattening. The AMCM module draws on multi-scale large-kernel convolution ideas, and the CSHF decoder is a novel lightweight fusion head distinct from standard UNet or SegFormer decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.22M-parameter footprint achieving SOTA crack segmentation (F1 0.8428, mIoU 0.8512 on TUT) is practically significant for deploying structural health monitoring on resource-constrained edge devices such as UAVs, as validated by the real-world deployment experiment. However, the performance margin over the closest Mamba-based competitor (SCSegamba) is modest (~0.4% F1), and the method's generalizability beyond crack segmentation to other fine-grained topological tasks remains untested. Open questions include whether the structure-calibrated RWKV paradigm scales to higher-resolution inputs or more diverse defect types, and whether the linear-complexity advantage persists at substantially larger model scales.
