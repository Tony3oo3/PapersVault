## Problem

For voting classifiers over finite hypothesis sets, the best known margin-based generalization upper bound (Gao & Zhou, 2013) and the best lower bound (Grønlund et al., 2020) left a gap of √(ln(n)/ln(e/L_S^θ(f))), which becomes a √(ln(n)) factor when a constant fraction of training points have small margins. This paper closes that gap, proving the first asymptotically tight margin-based generalization bound that matches the lower bound across the full tradeoff of hypothesis set size, margin, empirical margin loss, sample size, and failure probability.

## Input / Output

The bound is stated uniformly over all voting classifiers f in C(H) (convex combinations of base classifiers in a finite set H) and all margins θ in (√(e ln|H|/n), 1], holding with probability at least 1−δ over an n-sample S drawn i.i.d. from an arbitrary distribution D. The output is an explicit upper bound on the true misclassification loss L_D(f) expressed as L_S^θ(f) plus two correction terms involving √(L_S^θ(f))·ln(e/L_S^θ(f))·ln|H|/(θ²n) and ln(θ²n/ln|H|)·ln|H|/(θ²n), each also augmented by ln(e/δ)/n. The bound is tight in the sense that it matches the known lower bound up to universal constants for all parameter regimes covered by that lower bound.

## Method

The proof follows the random discretization framework of Schapire et al. (1998), mapping each voting classifier f to a distribution Q_f over a finite set C_N of unweighted N-averages of base classifiers, but departs critically in how the error between f and its discretization g is handled. Instead of dropping negative terms in the loss decomposition, the authors define two continuous piecewise functions φ and ρ that upper- and lower-bound the problematic cross-probability terms, exploiting the fact that the distribution of g's margin on a point depends only on f's margin at that point. They then apply the Ledoux–Talagrand contraction inequality to bound the Rademacher complexity of φ and ρ, reducing it to the Rademacher complexity of H itself (bounded via the finite class lemma), at the cost of a Lipschitz constant that decays exponentially as exp(−Nθ²/c). The Lipschitz constants are derived by differentiating binomial tail probabilities and bounding the resulting sums via Hoeffding's inequality over dyadic intervals. Setting N ≈ θ^{-2}·ln(e/L_S^θ(f)) balances the discretization error against the Rademacher term, yielding the tight bound.

## Results

The paper proves the first asymptotically tight margin-based generalization bound for voting classifiers over finite hypothesis sets (Theorem 1.1). The bound is: L_D(f) ≤ L_S^θ(f) + c·√(L_S^θ(f)·(ln(e/L_S^θ(f))·ln|H|/(θ²n) + ln(e/δ)/n)) + c·(ln(θ²n/ln|H|)·ln|H|/(θ²n) + ln(e/δ)/n). This closes the previously open gap of √(ln(n)/ln(e/L_S^θ(f))) between the best known upper bound (Gao & Zhou, 2013) and the best known lower bound (Grønlund et al., 2020), which for a constant fraction of training points with margin below θ amounted to a √(ln n) factor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against the previous best upper bound of Gao & Zhou (2013), which had a √(ln(n)·ln|H|/(θ²n)) term instead of the tighter √(ln(e/L_S^θ(f))·ln|H|/(θ²n)) term. The new bound strictly improves upon Gao & Zhou (2013) whenever L_S^θ(f) < 1/ln(n), and matches the lower bound of Grønlund et al. (2020) across the full range of parameters θ, L_S^θ(f), n, and δ. Compared to the original Schapire et al. (1998) bound, the improvement is most dramatic in the regime where only a small fraction of training points have margin below θ: the new bound adapts to L_S^θ(f) rather than using a worst-case √(ln n) factor. The δ-dependency (ln(e/δ)/n) was already known to be tight from classic work (Devroye et al., 1996) and is preserved. The only regime not fully settled is the extreme case n > 2^{|H|^c}, which is not covered by the existing lower bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the margin theory framework introduced by Schapire et al. (1998), which established the first margin-based generalization bound for voting classifiers via random discretization and union bounding. The key technical departure comes from adapting the Rademacher complexity and contraction inequality framework of Larsen & Schalburg (2025) — originally developed for large-margin halfspaces — to the voting classifier setting, replacing the crude Hoeffding-based discretization cost analysis with a Lipschitz-constant-controlled Rademacher bound. This allows the authors to exploit the negative cross-terms in the margin decomposition that Schapire et al. simply dropped, closing the √(ln n) gap between the Gao & Zhou (2013) upper bound and the Grønlund et al. (2020) lower bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result settles a decades-long open question about the exact generalization performance of voting classifiers over finite hypothesis sets, providing the first asymptotically tight bound that simultaneously captures the tradeoff between hypothesis set size, margin, fraction of low-margin training points, sample size, and failure probability. The authors note the bound does not extend to the extreme regime n > 2^{|H|^c} covered by the lower bound, and the analogous improvement for VC-dimension-based bounds (replacing Γ(θ²n/d) with ln(e/L_S^θ(f))) remains open. The framework's demonstrated adaptability beyond its original halfspace setting suggests it may yield tight bounds in other ensemble or aggregation contexts with similar discretization-based proof structures.
