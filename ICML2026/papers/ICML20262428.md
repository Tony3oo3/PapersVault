## Problem

Low-bit weight-only quantization of LLMs is severely degraded by systematic weight outliers that enlarge the numerical range and reduce quantization resolution. Existing outlier-suppression strategies rely exclusively on multiplicative transformations (scaling or rotation) between adjacent layers, which the authors argue is fundamentally inadequate for extreme low-bit settings (2–3 bits), leaving a significant performance gap versus FP16 baselines.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the pre-trained floating-point weight matrices of each layer along with a small calibration set (e.g., 128 sequences of length 2048) used to estimate the per-layer Hessian. It outputs a modified weight matrix per layer in which the additive perturbation has been absorbed offline, so the resulting weights can be directly quantized by any standard PTQ pipeline (GPTQ, AWQ, QuIP, etc.) with no changes to neighboring layers and zero additional inference-time cost.

## Method

OSAQ exploits the observation that the Hessian of the task loss with respect to a layer's weights exhibits low-rank consistency: a stable set of directions (the null space) shows vanishing curvature across different calibration inputs. It extracts this null space via eigendecomposition with a tail-energy threshold, then constructs an additive perturbation ΔW = βN by linearly combining null-space vectors with a per-channel coefficient matrix β. The coefficients are chosen to minimize the numerical range of the transformed weights, formulated as a Softmax-∞ approximation of the ℓ∞ norm that makes the objective differentiable and solvable in closed form via a symmetric positive-definite normal equation. Because the perturbation lies entirely in the Hessian null space, the second-order loss change is zero, guaranteeing loss invariance without any inter-layer compensation or iterative training.

## Results

In the most challenging 2-bit setting (W2A16g128), OSAQ+GPTQ reduces WikiText2 perplexity on LLaMA2-7B from 36.8 (vanilla GPTQ) to 21.2, a 42% improvement, and further to 10.6 with coordinate descent (OSAQ+GPTQ†). In 3-bit (W3A16), OSAQ+GPTQ lowers perplexity from 8.37 to 6.75 on LLaMA2-7B WikiText2, approaching the FP16 baseline of 5.47. In 4-bit (W4A16), OSAQ+GPTQ achieves 5.73 perplexity on LLaMA2-7B WikiText2, outperforming GPTQ (5.83), AWQ (6.15), and QuIP (5.94). On commonsense QA (Table 2), OSAQ+GPTQ† raises average zero-shot accuracy on LLaMA3-8B from 38.3% (GPTQ) to 51.7% in the 2-bit setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSAQ is evaluated as a plug-and-play additive preprocessing step combined with existing quantization methods, and it consistently improves over every baseline it is paired with. Against GPTQ, OSAQ+GPTQ reduces WikiText2 perplexity by 1.0–1.6 points in 4-bit, 1.6–1.7 points in 3-bit, and 15.6 points in 2-bit (LLaMA2-7B), with the gap widening as bit-width decreases. Against AWQ, OSAQ+AWQ achieves lower perplexity in all tested settings (e.g., 5.99 vs. 6.15 on LLaMA2-7B W4A16 WikiText2). Against QuIP, OSAQ+QuIP improves perplexity in 2-bit groupwise settings (6.37 vs. 6.50 on LLaMA2-7B). Against MagR and OmniQuant, OSAQ+GPTQ† matches or exceeds their 2-bit performance (10.6 vs. 9.94 for MagR† on LLaMA2-7B, 10.6 vs. 11.1 for OmniQuant). In the weight-activation quantization setting (W4A4), OSAQ improves over QuaRot (6.03 vs. 6.10), DuQuant (6.19 vs. 6.28), and SpinQuant (5.84 vs. 5.90) on LLaMA2-7B WikiText2. For KV-Cache quantization (W4A16KV4), OSAQ+WKVQuant reduces perplexity from 5.64 to 5.59 on LLaMA2-7B WikiText2. The only case where OSAQ does not clearly win is 2-bit LLaMA2-7B where MagR† (9.94) slightly edges out OSAQ+GPTQ† (10.6), though OSAQ+GPTQ† substantially outperforms MagR† on LLaMA3-8B (26.1 vs. not reported) and LLaMA2-13B (7.60 vs. 7.63).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant multiplicative transformation paradigm (scaling à la AWQ, rotation à la QuIP/QuaRot) that has governed outlier suppression in LLM quantization, introducing instead an additive weight transformation grounded in the Hessian's null space. While GPTQ already leverages approximate Hessian information for iterative error compensation during quantization, OSAQ repurposes the Hessian's structural property—its low-rank consistency across inputs—to construct a loss-invariant pre-quantization perturbation that requires no inter-layer coordination. The Softmax-∞ approximation from convex optimization (Boyd & Vandenberghe) is repurposed to make the ℓ∞ minimization tractable in closed form, and the method is explicitly positioned as a plug-and-play complement to, rather than replacement of, existing scaling or rotation schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OSAQ opens a new axis of outlier suppression (additive, self-contained to a single layer) that is orthogonal to the multiplicative inter-layer transformations dominating the field, and its zero-inference-overhead, closed-form nature makes it immediately deployable alongside GPTQ, AWQ, or QuIP. However, the practical gains in the most commonly deployed 4-bit regime are modest (e.g., ~0.1 perplexity on WikiText2 for LLaMA2-7B), with the dramatic improvements concentrated in the extreme 2-bit setting where all methods struggle. Open questions include whether the Hessian null-space stability property holds universally across architectures and training regimes, whether theoretical guarantees can be established for the loss-invariance claim beyond the second-order approximation, and whether the approach can be extended to activation quantization or mixed-precision settings without the current calibration-data dependency.
