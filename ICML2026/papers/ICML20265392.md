## Problem

Existing RAG poisoning attacks achieve high success rates on vanilla RAG pipelines but suffer dramatic performance drops when a reranker is deployed, which is standard in production systems. The paper identifies that rerankers fine-tuned solely on benign in-domain data provide an unexpected 'free lunch' defense against prior attacks, meaning current security benchmarks substantially underestimate the resilience of practical RAG pipelines. The work addresses this gap by identifying reranker blind spots and designing attacks that exploit them.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target question, a target incorrect answer, and (for the white-box P³A variant) gradient access to the reranker model; the black-box P²A variant requires only the question and answer. It produces a set of N_p poisoned documents (default 5 per query) structured as the concatenation of the target question and a crafted adversarial paragraph, with character-level perturbations introducing only ~1% textual change (average Levenshtein distance of 1.4–5.3 characters). The documents are designed to be injected into public knowledge sources (e.g., Wikipedia, forums) to compromise the RAG corpus.

## Method

P³A is a two-phase framework. Phase 1 (rule-based prompt) uses four principles derived from reranker preference analysis—directly state the answer, use an authoritative tone, provide supporting context, and maintain sharp focus—to prompt GPT-4o into generating a semantically coherent adversarial paragraph that satisfies both the retrieval condition (high reranker score) and the generation condition (steering the LLM to the target answer). Phase 2 (character-level perturbation, white-box only) refines the document in three stages: (a) position selection by masking the incorrect answer and probing each remaining character position with a space insertion to identify the top-N positions with largest reranker score impact; (b) PGD-inspired candidate generation that constructs n perturbed variants, defines a convex weight vector over their embeddings, and optimizes via projected gradient ascent on a continuous proxy objective; (c) beam search refinement that iteratively expands and prunes candidates until a target reranker score threshold is reached. The rule-based prompt phase alone constitutes the black-box variant P²A, which still substantially outperforms all baselines.

## Results

On NQ with MiniLM reranker and Llama3-8B, P³A achieves 72.2% ASR and 0.8416 F1-Score, far exceeding the best baseline (PoisonedRAG-B at 34.8% ASR, 0.2368 F1). On HotpotQA, P³A reaches up to 89.4% ASR (Qwen2.5-7B, MiniLM) with an F1-Score of 0.8884, the highest across all settings. The black-box variant P²A also substantially outperforms all baselines, achieving 63.0–73.2% ASR across datasets. Perturbations introduce only ~1% textual change (average Levenshtein distance of 5.33 on NQ), preserving readability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P³A dominates all four baseline families by large margins on every dataset, reranker, and LLM tested. Against the strongest baseline PoisonedRAG-B (34.8% ASR on NQ/MiniLM), P³A more than doubles the ASR to 72.2% and nearly quadruples the F1-Score (0.8416 vs. 0.2368). Prompt Injection and CorruptRAG-AS are essentially neutralized by the reranker (ASR drops to 2.4% and 3.0% respectively), while P³A maintains high effectiveness, confirming that its rule-based prompt design and character-level perturbations specifically exploit reranker blind spots. The black-box variant P²A (63.0% ASR) also outperforms all baselines without any reranker access, demonstrating that the four prompt principles alone provide a substantial advantage. On HotpotQA, P³A achieves 89.4% ASR with Qwen2.5-7B, compared to the best baseline PoisonedRAG-B at 47.2%, a 42-point gap. Transferability experiments confirm P³A retains 65.8–70.6% ASR when optimized on one reranker and tested on another, whereas baselines do not exhibit comparable cross-model robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PoisonedRAG (Zou et al., 2025), adopting its two-part document structure (target question concatenated with adversarial context) as the foundation for crafting poisoned texts. It departs from the broader RAG poisoning literature (CorruptRAG, CPA-RAG, GARAG) by identifying that the reranker—a standard production component—was systematically overlooked in prior attack evaluations, and by deriving four prompt-design principles from reranker preference analysis to craft documents that survive cross-encoder scrutiny. The character-level perturbation phase draws on PGD-style adversarial optimization and beam search, repurposing techniques from adversarial NLP to the specific objective of maximizing reranker scores while preserving semantic coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that benignly fine-tuned rerankers provide a substantial 'free lunch' defense (e.g., CorruptRAG-AS ASR dropping from 85.8% to 3.0%) reframes how the field should benchmark RAG security, since prior evaluations substantially underestimated pipeline resilience. However, the paper simultaneously shows this defense is bypassable: P³A achieves 72–89% ASR across datasets and rerankers, and remains effective even against paraphrasing, perplexity-based detection, RobustRAG, and TrustRAG—defenses that add 5–7× computational overhead. Open questions include whether truly effective defenses exist for reranker-enhanced RAG, how the attack scales to larger corpora or multi-turn settings, and whether the white-box reranker assumption is realistic enough to warrant the threat model.
