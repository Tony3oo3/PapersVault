## Problem

Prior self-preference studies for LLM judges conflate true self-recognition bias with evaluation uncertainty: when a judge fails on a task, it may vote for its own incorrect response simply because it is uncertain, not because it recognizes its own authorship. No existing methodology isolates the self-preference signal from this evaluator-quality confound, leaving it unclear whether reported 'narcissism' is a genuine situational-awareness artifact or a measurement artifact of hard-problem uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairwise evaluation triples (judge response, reference response, oracle label) and, for each ILSP example, retrieves an outcome-matched proxy response from a different model that shares the same oracle verdict against the reference. The judge is prompted twice per comparison (position-swapped) to eliminate positional bias, and log-probabilities over vote tokens are averaged to yield a calibrated preference probability. The primary output is a per-example preference differential Δs aggregated into a paired t-test statistic with p-value, alongside Shannon entropy of the vote distribution as a confidence diagnostic.

## Method

The core contribution is the Evaluator Quality Baseline, a control-group design that, for every example where the judge produced an inferior response (ILSP subset), elicits the judge's vote probability on its own response versus an outcome-matched proxy response from a different model—removing the 'self' while preserving the example's difficulty. The preference differential Δs = s_J(x, o_J, o_R) − s_J(x, o_K, o_R) is tested via a paired t-test with null hypothesis H₀: T_quality ≤ 0, directly measuring the excess self-preference attributable to autoevaluation rather than task uncertainty. Proxy selection is performed at the example level (matching oracle outcomes per query) rather than at the model level, and robustness is validated through winrate correlation (ρ = 0.85), same-family exclusion, and alternative filtering strategies. The paper further shows that Shannon entropy of vote distributions is nearly identical whether the judge evaluates itself or a proxy (R² = 73%), supporting the interpretation that the removed bias is an evaluator-quality artifact rather than a confidence-driven self-recognition effect. The baseline is applied to reproductions of four landmark self-preference papers across 9 datasets and 16 models, revealing that 89.6% of previously reported self-preference probability mass is explained by the evaluator-quality null.

## Results

Across 37,448 evaluation pairs on 9 datasets and 16 models, the Evaluator Quality Baseline reveals that only 10.4% of previously reported self-preference bias exceeds a capability-matched control-group baseline, with 89.6% of total self-preference probability mass explained by evaluator uncertainty rather than narcissism. On MATH-500, the average self-preference reduction is 98.76% (near-total collapse), with 5 of 11 models showing updated ILSP at or below zero. MMLU is the notable exception, retaining a strong self-preference signal (only 4 of 11 models fall below statistical significance), while subjective tasks show moderate reductions: AlpacaEval (−79.19%), Translation (−82.57%), and Truthfulness (−67.57%). Overall, 50% of prior experiments lose statistical significance and 44% would show no or negative bias under the proposed null hypothesis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Chen et al. (2025a) verifiable-output experiments, the Evaluator Quality Baseline causes a near-total collapse on MATH-500 (−98.76% average reduction; 5/11 models drop to ≤0 ILSP) and MBPP-Plus (−89.26%), but MMLU retains a robust self-preference signal with only 4/11 models below significance—suggesting stylistic or persuasion-mode preferences baked into post-training. Against Chen et al. (2025b) DBG-scoring, subjective tasks show moderate but significant reductions: AlpacaEval (−79.19%), Translation (−82.57%), and Truthfulness (−67.57%), with rankwise model orderings shifting substantially (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B drops from highest bias at 49% to second-lowest at 3.2%). Against Panickssery et al. (2024) human-annotation summarization experiments, the effect is strongest: both GPT-4 and GPT-3.5-Turbo show ILSP_upd values that are negative (−17.8% to −22.8%), meaning the 'self-preference' fully reverses to a self-penalty once evaluator quality is controlled. Against Mahbub & Feng (2025) authorship-obfuscation experiments on QUALITY, reductions range from −81.6% to −122.7%, with two of three models showing negative updated ILSP. Across all four baselines, the paper's method does not eliminate self-preference entirely but re-attributes ~89.6% of previously reported bias to evaluator uncertainty, leaving a smaller but statistically significant residual concentrated in MMLU and larger Qwen models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-preference bias measurement literature (Panickssery et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2025a,b; Wataoka et al. 2024) by introducing an Evaluator Quality Baseline that controls for a confound prior work overlooked: the judge's own task uncertainty on hard examples. While Chen et al. (2025a) established the ILSP/LSP decomposition and 'Harmful Self-Preference Propensity' metric, and Chen et al. (2025b) added oracle-labeled gold judges for subjective tasks, none isolated the effect of a judge evaluating itself from the general noise of evaluating difficult problems. The paper's proxy-matched control design (outcome-level matching of judge and proxy responses) is the key methodological departure, reframing self-preference as a causal treatment effect rather than a raw rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ~90% of previously reported self-preference is an evaluator-uncertainty artifact substantially recalibrates the field's understanding of LLM 'narcissism,' redirecting intervention efforts (steering vectors, CoT prompting, authorship obfuscation) toward the small residual where genuine self-recognition may operate. Open questions include whether the residual bias on MMLU and subjective tasks reflects true self-recognition or stylistic post-training artifacts, and whether human gold labels (rather than LLM oracle panels) would further shift the picture. The paper's reliance on model-derived oracle labels for subjective tasks and its example-level (rather than model-level) proxy matching remain acknowledged limitations that future work should address.
