## Problem

Satisfiability in Pearl's Causal Hierarchy—deciding whether a set of probabilistic, interventional, or counterfactual constraints admits a Structural Causal Model—is NP-hard or ∃R-complete in every classical fragment, leaving no known tractable regime. This paper identifies the first parameterized gateways to tractability, showing which structural parameters (primal treewidth, domain size, variable count) yield FPT or XP algorithms, and proving matching lower bounds that delineate the limits of such tractability.

## Input / Output

The input is a PCH formula φ consisting of a set of linear (or polynomial) inequalities over probability terms, a discrete domain D of size d, and the formula's primal graph G_φ whose treewidth tw(φ) and variable count n serve as the parameterized parameters. The output is a yes/no satisfiability decision; for yes-instances, the algorithm constructs an explicit witness SCM with hidden variables and probability distributions. Notably, the formulas are restricted to sets of inequalities rather than arbitrary Boolean combinations, a restriction that is essential for the parameterized tractability results (arbitrary Boolean combinations are already NP-complete even on edgeless primal graphs).

## Method

The key technical departure is that the algorithms avoid the standard dynamic-programming-over-tree-decompositions paradigm entirely. For the probabilistic layer (SAT_prob^lin), the authors construct a linear program with O(n·d^w) variables, one per bag-assignment in a nice tree decomposition of the primal graph, where constraints enforce marginal consistency between adjacent bags and encode each probability term in φ; feasibility of the LP is equivalent to satisfiability, and a solution is converted to an SCM by a BFS traversal that instantiates hidden variables at introduce nodes. For the counterfactual layer (SAT_counterfact^lin), a structural lemma shows every YES-instance admits a well-structured SCM where a single hidden variable U encodes the full function mapping from prior variables to each observed variable; the algorithm then enumerates all n! variable orderings and, for each, solves an LP over the space of possible function tuples of size at most d^{d^n}. Hardness results are established via three distinct reductions: MULTICOLORED-CLIQUE for W[1]-hardness, a restricted ETR variant for ∃R-hardness of the poly fragment, and 3-SAT for NP-hardness of the causal-lin fragment.

## Results

The paper establishes the first parameterized tractability results for satisfiability in Pearl's Causal Hierarchy. The headline positive results are: SAT_prob^lin is in XP w.r.t. primal treewidth tw(φ) alone, and becomes FPT w.r.t. the combined parameter tw(φ) + d (domain size); SAT_counterfact^lin is FPT w.r.t. the combined parameter d + n (number of variables). Matching hardness results show that SAT_prob^base is W[1]-hard w.r.t. n, SAT_causal^lin remains NP-complete even when all edges in the primal graph are pairwise vertex-disjoint, and all poly fragments are ∃R-complete even under severe restrictions (n=1 or d=2 with edgeless primal graph).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or competing algorithms to compare against. The 'comparison' is instead between different parameterizations and PCH fragments: the paper shows that primal treewidth alone yields only XP (not FPT) for the probabilistic layer, that adding domain size d upgrades this to FPT, and that interventions (causal/counterfactual layers) fundamentally break treewidth-based tractability. The key contrast is between the shallow probabilistic fragment (where treewidth helps) and the deeper causal/counterfactual fragments (where only the number of variables n combined with domain size d yields FPT). The paper also contrasts its novel linear-programming-based approach with the standard dynamic-programming-over-tree-decompositions paradigm used in prior treewidth-based algorithms for SAT and CSP, arguing that the latter is incompatible with the causal satisfiability problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical complexity landscape for PCH satisfiability (established by Fagin et al. 1990 for the probabilistic layer, Mosse et al. 2024 for causal/counterfactual fragments, and van der Zander et al. 2023 / Dörfler et al. 2025 for marginalization extensions) into the parameterized complexity paradigm, identifying the first gateways to tractability where prior work only mapped intractability. Conceptually, it draws on the treewidth-based algorithmic tradition from Boolean SAT and CSP, but departs sharply from the standard dynamic programming paradigm: the authors show that the leaf-to-root DP approach is fundamentally incompatible with PCH satisfiability and instead construct fixed-parameter-sized linear programs whose feasibility certifies the existence of a well-structured SCM. The hardness reductions (from Multicolored-Clique, ETR, and 3-SAT) complement the positive results by delineating the exact boundary of parameterized tractability across the 3×3 expressivity matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners working with causal reasoning systems, the results show that for the shallow probabilistic fragment, exploiting the primal treewidth of the constraint graph (combined with domain size) yields tractable algorithms, whereas the deeper causal/counterfactual fragments require bounding the number of variables. The LP-based technique for treewidth-parameterized problems is potentially transferable to other settings combining discrete graph structure with continuous (probabilistic) elements, opening a new algorithmic toolkit beyond DP. Key open questions include whether the results extend to full marginalization operators (currently blocked by NEXP-hardness), whether an XP-algorithm exists for counterfactual satisfiability parameterized by variable count alone, and how structural restrictions on the SCM (e.g., a given dependency graph) interact with the parameterized landscape.
