## Problem

In Clustered Federated Learning, clients are naturally grouped by data distribution but cluster assignments are unknown. Existing CFL methods like IFCA require careful model initialization (each model must start within half the inter-cluster separation distance), and data-separation-based approaches fail when clusters overlap in raw feature space. No prior method robustly recovers clusters from arbitrary initializations while supporting both supervised and unsupervised settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260273/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client sends a loss vector of length K̂ (the number of models) to the server, computed by running its local data through all K̂ models; the server clusters these vectors and assigns clients to models via min-cost bipartite matching. After clustering stabilizes, the system reduces to standard FedAvg with a single assigned model per client, minimizing communication. The method accepts both labeled data (cross-entropy loss) and unlabeled data (MSE reconstruction loss), making it applicable to supervised classification and unsupervised autoencoder tasks alike.

## Method

CLoVE iteratively builds K̂ personalized models while simultaneously generating client embeddings as vectors of model losses on each client's local data. The core insight is that clients in the same cluster exhibit similar loss patterns across all models, while clients in different clusters show distinct patterns—even when raw data overlaps heavily. The server clusters these loss vectors (e.g., k-means), determines the optimal number of clusters via silhouette score, and assigns clusters to models using min-cost bipartite matching where edge weights are summed client losses. Clients then perform local gradient or model updates on their assigned model, and the server aggregates updates per cluster. Unlike IFCA, CLoVE requires no initialization constraint and recovers accurate clusters in 2–3 rounds from random initialization, with theoretical guarantees of single-shot cluster recovery and exponential convergence to optimal models in the linear regression setting.

## Results

On the standard Label Skew 1 setting, CLoVE achieves 99.8% accuracy on MNIST, 90.5% on CIFAR-10, 99.1% on Fashion-MNIST, and 99.0% on Amazon Reviews, outperforming all baselines. Under the harder Concept Shift setting, CLoVE reaches 97.6% (MNIST), 58.4% (CIFAR-10), and 84.3% (FMNIST), again leading all methods. CLoVE also achieves perfect ARI (1.00) cluster recovery within 2 rounds across all image datasets, while baselines like IFCA and CFL-S require 40+ rounds or fail entirely. In the unsupervised autoencoder setting, CLoVE achieves the lowest reconstruction loss (0.014 on MNIST, 0.016 on FMNIST) compared to IFCA (0.024, 0.020) and FedAvg (0.032, 0.030).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLoVE outperforms all 10 baselines on the majority of settings. Against the closest CFL competitor IFCA, CLoVE gains +4.8 points on CIFAR-10 (Label Skew 1: 90.5 vs 85.7) and +5.7 points on CIFAR-10 (Concept Shift: 58.4 vs 52.7), while IFCA exhibits high variance (±6.5 on CIFAR-10 LS1). FlexCFL is competitive on Label Skew 1 (99.4 vs 99.8 on MNIST) but collapses under Concept Shift on CIFAR-10 (17.8 vs 58.4) due to its one-shot gradient-based clustering. FeSEM and PACFL are consistently the weakest CFL baselines, with FeSEM achieving only 11.2% on CIFAR-10 (LS1) and 12.7% (CS). Among general PFL methods, FedALA is the strongest (80.0% CIFAR-10 LS1, 42.0% CS) but still trails CLoVE by 10.5 and 16.4 points respectively. On Amazon Reviews, FedAvg matches CLoVE (99.0%), reflecting the low inter-cluster heterogeneity in that dataset; on AG News (Label Skew 2), CLoVE leads all methods at 55.1% vs. FlexCFL's 60.2% (the sole exception where a baseline wins). CLoVE also achieves perfect ARI=1.00 cluster recovery in 2 rounds across all image datasets, whereas IFCA never reaches ARI≥0.9 and CFL-S requires 41–60 rounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLoVE positions itself within the Clustered Federated Learning (CFL) lineage, departing from IFCA's single-model-loss-based client assignment and CFL-S/FlexCFL's gradient-embedding approaches by instead using the full vector of losses across all candidate models as a client embedding for k-means clustering. Theoretically, it builds on the proximity-condition framework of Kumar & Kannan (2010) for k-means guarantees and the mixed linear regression formulation of Yi et al. (2014), but applies them to the novel object of square-root loss vectors rather than raw data or model parameters. Unlike IFCA, which requires initial models to be within half the inter-cluster separation of their optima, CLoVE's analysis shows that ortho-normal (i.e., essentially random) initialization suffices for single-shot cluster recovery, removing a key practical barrier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of initialization sensitivity and the applicability to unsupervised settings (where IFCA and several other CFL methods are inapplicable) make CLoVE a more robust and broadly deployable option for real-world FL deployments where cluster structure is unknown and data may lack labels. The convergence to ARI ≥ 0.9 within 2–3 rounds, versus hundreds for CFL-S or outright failure for IFCA, has direct implications for communication and compute budgets in edge-device settings. Open questions flagged by the authors include privacy guarantees (loss vectors may leak information about local data), fairness across clusters, and adversarial robustness; the theoretical analysis is also restricted to linear regression with specific separation and SNR assumptions, leaving the nonlinear deep-learning regime to empirical validation.
