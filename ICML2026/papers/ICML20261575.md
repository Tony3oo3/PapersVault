## Problem

Existing offline imitation learning methods can only imitate expert or mildly suboptimal demonstrations but cannot explicitly avoid undesirable behaviors that coexist in the dataset. Introducing a divergence-maximization term to repel the policy from bad demonstrations creates a difference-of-convex (DC) objective that is generally non-convex, making optimization intractable with standard distribution-matching techniques. This paper addresses the gap of learning from contrasting (good and bad) demonstrations in a unified, stable, non-adversarial framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three trajectory datasets: a small labeled good set B^G (e.g., 1 expert trajectory), a small labeled bad set B^B (e.g., 10 random/medium trajectories), and a large unlabeled mixed set B^MIX (full suboptimal dataset plus additional expert trajectories). All training is fully offline with no environment interaction. The output is a stochastic policy π(a|s) extracted via Q-weighted behavior cloning, along with learned Q and V functions. Occupancy ratios between datasets are estimated by training binary logistic discriminators on state-action pairs, which are then used to compute the implicit reward Ψ(s,a) that drives the Q-learning objective.

## Method

DualCOIL formulates the learning objective as the difference of two KL divergences over state-action visitation distributions: min D_KL(d^π‖d^G) − α·D_KL(d^π‖d^B), and proves that this DC program becomes convex when α ≤ 1. Via Lagrangian duality, the convex problem is recast as a max-min Q-learning objective with an exponential term; a linear lower-bound surrogate (Proposition 4.3) then eliminates the exponential, yielding an objective that is linear in Q and concave in π, which collapses to a single non-adversarial convex minimization over Q alone. The value function is estimated using Extreme-V (XQL) to avoid intractable log-sum-exp, and a χ² regularizer on the Bellman residual prevents reward divergence. Policy extraction uses a novel Q-weighted BC objective (max Σ exp(Q(s,a)/β) log π(a|s)) that is theoretically equivalent to advantage-weighted BC but avoids reliance on the approximate V(s), yielding more stable training.

## Results

DualCOIL achieves an average normalized score of 94.1 across all 18 D4RL dataset combinations (9 tasks × 2 settings), substantially outperforming the next-best method DualCOIL-G (78.8, which ignores bad data) and ReCOIL (56.6). On MuJoCo locomotion tasks, DualCOIL reaches near-expert performance (e.g., CHEETAH RANDOM+EXPERT: 86.7 vs. expert 90.6; ANT RANDOM+EXPERT: 112.7 vs. expert 117.5). On Adroit manipulation tasks, the advantage is most dramatic: on DOOR (CLONED+EXPERT), DualCOIL scores 102.4 while the next-best baseline ReCOIL scores only 19.3, and on RELOCATE (CLONED+EXPERT) DualCOIL achieves 92.1 versus 1.4 for ReCOIL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualCOIL wins on all 18 dataset combinations against every baseline. Against the strongest distribution-matching baselines (ReCOIL, ILID, SMODICE), the advantage is most pronounced on Adroit manipulation tasks: on DOOR (CLONED+EXPERT), DualCOIL scores 102.4 versus 19.3 (ReCOIL), −0.1 (ILID), and −0.1 (SMODICE); on RELOCATE (CLONED+EXPERT), it scores 92.1 versus 1.4 (ReCOIL). Among methods that explicitly use bad data, SafeDICE (Jang et al., 2024) and the adapted DWBC-GB (Xu et al., 2022) both fail to leverage undesirable demonstrations effectively—SafeDICE scores near zero on most manipulation tasks and DWBC-GB averages only 9.2 overall—whereas DualCOIL's difference-of-KL formulation consistently converts bad data into a performance gain. On MuJoCo locomotion tasks, DualCOIL also outperforms ReCOIL (e.g., HOPPER MEDIUM+EXPERT: 103.7 vs. 46.1) and ILID (e.g., ANT MEDIUM+EXPERT: 107.4 vs. 39.6), while the gap narrows on WALKER where SMODICE and ILID are competitive (106.6 and 100.1 respectively vs. DualCOIL's 107.4). The paper further shows that DualCOIL's implicit reward Ψ(s,a) cannot be effectively exploited by standard offline RL methods (IQL, XQL, OptiDICE, F-DVL), confirming that the full KL-regularized dual objective is necessary rather than merely the reward signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

DualCOIL extends the DICE/distribution-matching lineage (AlgaeDICE, ValueDICE, DemoDICE, SMODICE, ReCOIL) by introducing a KL-divergence *maximization* term for undesirable demonstrations alongside the standard minimization term for expert data, yielding a difference-of-convex objective. The Q-learning derivation follows the DualRL framework (Sikchi et al., 2024), and the surrogate objective reduces exactly to IQ-Learn (Garg et al., 2021) when the bad dataset is absent, positioning DualCOIL as a principled generalization. The Extreme-V update from XQL (Garg et al., 2023) is adopted for tractable value estimation, and the Q-weighted BC policy extraction is inspired by ReCOIL (Sikchi et al., 2025). The key departure from all prior offline IL methods is the explicit, unified handling of both positive and negative labeled demonstrations within a single convex optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first offline imitation learning method to jointly leverage expert and explicitly undesirable demonstrations, addressing a practical gap in domains like autonomous driving and healthcare where unsafe behaviors must be actively avoided. The convexity guarantee under α≤1 provides a clean theoretical foundation that enables non-adversarial, stable training—contrasting with the adversarial or heuristic approaches in prior work. Key limitations include the restriction to α≤1 (beyond which the objective is non-convex), the assumption that bad demonstrations are suboptimal rather than catastrophically unsafe (hard-constrained safe RL is out of scope), and the acknowledged 'whack-a-mole' risk when the bad dataset is incomplete. Open questions include extending the framework to the α>1 regime, integrating hard safety constraints, and validating in truly safety-critical deployment settings.
