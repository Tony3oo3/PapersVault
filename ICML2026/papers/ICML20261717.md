## Problem

Existing guidance methods for generative models either require expensive multi-particle many-step schemes (e.g., SMC) or rely on heuristic approximations to the reward-tilted score whose actual target distribution is poorly characterized. The standard reward-tilt formulation is rooted in stochastic processes and does not sit naturally with modern deterministic few-step flow map samplers like FLUX, leaving no principled guidance methodology native to the single-trajectory, few-NFE regime.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261717/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FMRG takes a pre-trained flow map (distilled from FLUX.1-dev via Lagrangian distillation), a differentiable reward function, and optionally a text prompt or degraded observation, and produces a single guided image in as few as 3 network function evaluations (NFEs). Inverse problems (super-resolution, deblurring, inpainting) are evaluated at 256×256, while text-to-image and VLM-guided generation are evaluated at 512×512. The flow map serves a dual role: it integrates the base dynamics exactly between guidance steps and provides the endpoint lookahead needed to compute the reward gradient, so a single pre-trained model drives both integration and guidance without any additional training.

## Method

The paper reformulates inference-time guidance as a deterministic optimal control problem that trades off reward maximization against deviation from the base flow, departing entirely from the stochastic reward-tilt framework. Applying the Pontryagin Maximum Principle yields a closed-form optimal feedback in which the flow map Jacobian appears explicitly; a small-λ expansion then replaces the intractable controlled flow map with the pre-trained uncontrolled flow map, giving a tractable guidance signal accurate to O(λ²). The resulting FMRG algorithm uses operator splitting to alternate exact flow map integration steps with gradient ascent steps on the reward, evaluated at the flow-map lookahead endpoint. Two variants are offered: FMRG-J backpropagates through the flow map to project the reward gradient onto the data-manifold tangent space (on-manifold guidance), while FMRG-E uses the Euclidean gradient directly (cheaper, but can leave the manifold). Early stopping—applying guidance only on [0, t_stop] and then integrating the uncontrolled flow to completion—mitigates the exponential variance contraction that greedy guidance induces, recovering diversity comparable to the exact optimal control.

## Results

On GenEval (reward-guided text-to-image generation), FMRG-J achieves 0.800 overall accuracy at 100 NFEs, matching FMTT's 0.771 at just 20 NFEs—a 70× reduction in function evaluations. On latent-space inverse problems (super-resolution, motion deblurring, inpainting) evaluated on AFHQ and FFHQ, FMRG-J attains the best PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID, and KID across all three tasks and both datasets, e.g., 28.23 PSNR / 55.99 FID on FFHQ super-resolution versus 27.70 / 61.85 for FlowDPS. FMRG-E achieves competitive performance with as few as 3 NFEs, matching or outperforming baselines that use 2–10× more evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPS, FMRG wins by large margins on all inverse-problem metrics: on FFHQ super-resolution, FMRG-J reaches 28.23 PSNR / 55.99 FID versus DPS's 18.74 / 119.30, a gap of roughly 10 dB in PSNR. Against FlowDPS and FlowChef (the strongest single-trajectory baselines), FMRG-J improves FID by 5–10 points and KID by 30–50% across all three inverse tasks on both datasets, while FMRG-E matches or slightly exceeds them at equal or lower NFEs. On GenEval, FMRG-J at 20 NFEs (0.770) matches FMTT at 1400 NFEs (0.771) with a 70× efficiency gain, and at 100 NFEs (0.800) surpasses both FMTT and ReNO (0.716 at 58 NFEs) by 3–8 points. FMRG-J also outperforms the unguided FLUX.1-dev base (0.662) by 14 points at 100 NFEs, and the Euclidean variant FMRG-E (0.766 at 100 NFEs) approaches FMTT's quality while using 14× fewer evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant reward-tilting framework (stochastic optimal control + SMC) for inference-time guidance and instead formulates guidance as a deterministic optimal control problem, showing that the flow map emerges naturally in the closed-form solution via the Pontryagin Maximum Principle. It positions DPS, FlowDPS, FlowChef, and MPGD as progressively coarser approximations within a single hierarchy, where DPS corresponds to a one-step Euler approximation of the flow-map endpoint. The approach builds directly on recent flow map distillation (Lagrangian distillation from FLUX.1-dev) and operator splitting, but repurposes the flow map not just for fast integration but also as the guidance signal itself, unifying integration and steering in one object.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FMRG is the first guidance methodology native to the few-step, single-trajectory regime, achieving up to 70× fewer NFEs than prior state-of-the-art (20 vs. 1400 for FMTT on GenEval) while remaining training-free and compatible with existing pretrained flow maps. The theoretical reframing—showing that the flow map Jacobian projects reward gradients onto the data-manifold tangent space—provides a geometric explanation for why Jacobian-based guidance (FMRG-J) outperforms Euclidean variants on complex neural-network rewards. Key limitations include the ~48 GB VRAM cost of FMRG-J at 512px, dependence on flow map quality, and the need for early stopping to prevent mode collapse from the greedy guidance's exponential variance contraction. Open questions include whether the deterministic formulation extends cleanly to stochastic or multi-particle settings, and whether the approximation hierarchy can be exploited for adaptive NFE allocation.
