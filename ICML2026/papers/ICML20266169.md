## Problem

Point clouds are inherently sparse, unstructured, and acutely sensitive to rotation, forcing existing methods to rely on heavy data augmentation or large model capacities to achieve rotational robustness at high computational and parameter cost. No prior framework systematically integrates quaternion geometric priors into both local and global feature learning to simultaneously achieve rotation invariance, high accuracy, and a lightweight design suitable for resource-constrained deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266169/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a raw 3D point cloud (N×3 coordinates, typically 1024 points for classification and 4096 for segmentation) and tokenizes it via Farthest Point Sampling (L=128 centroids), KNN neighborhood retrieval, and a lightweight PointNet patch embedding into a sequence of C-dimensional tokens plus a [CLS] token. Depending on the downstream task, it outputs a single class label (classification), per-point part labels (ShapeNetPart), or per-point semantic class labels (S3DIS indoor scenes). All experiments are trained from scratch under supervised learning without pre-training or external data.

## Method

QPoint is a 4-block lightweight Transformer where each Quaternion Point Block (QPB) combines two quaternion-enhanced modules. The Quaternion-Enhanced Local Perception (QELP) module normalizes local neighborhood coordinates to a canonical frame, applies a learnable unit quaternion rotation (only 4 parameters) to the normalized coordinates, and fuses the rotated result with the original via a residual connection, while simultaneously expanding and per-channel calibrating point features. The Quaternion Global Attention (QGA) module projects token features into quaternion space, unit-normalizes them, and computes attention scores via the quaternion dot product between Q and K (derived from unit-normalized features), which measures rotational alignment and is provably invariant to global SO(3) rotations, while V retains original semantic information. The theoretical analysis shows QGA achieves strict rotational invariance (zero invariance error) while QELP provides adaptive stabilization, and the combined architecture achieves 94.2% OA on ModelNet40 and 95.0% on ScanObjectNN OBJ_BG with only 4.5M parameters and 0.9G FLOPs.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN, QPoint achieves 95.0%, 93.9%, and 92.1% overall accuracy (OA) on OBJ_BG, OBJ_ONLY, and PB_T50_RS variants respectively (with test-time voting), surpassing the strongest baselines Mamba3D and MVTN. On ModelNet40, it reaches 94.7% OA and 91.6% mAcc, outperforming Mamba3D (93.4%) and Point-M2AE (94.0%). On ShapeNetPart, QPoint attains 87.0% instance mIoU and 85.1% class mIoU. All results are achieved with only 4.5M parameters and 0.9G FLOPs for classification, roughly 27% of Mamba3D's parameter count and 23% of its FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QPoint outperforms all 24 compared baselines across every reported metric. On the most challenging ScanObjectNN PB_T50_RS variant, it leads Mamba3D (91.8%) by 0.3% and PointNeXt (87.7%) by 4.4%; on OBJ_BG it surpasses Mamba3D (92.9%) by 2.1% and MVTN (92.6%) by 2.4%. On ModelNet40, QPoint (94.7%) beats Mamba3D (93.4%) by 1.3% and Point-M2AE (94.0%) by 0.7%, while using only 4.5M parameters versus Mamba3D's 16.9M and PCM's 34.2M. On ShapeNetPart segmentation, QPoint (87.0% Inst.mIoU) matches PointNeXt and PCM but with 21.6% of PCM's parameters and 9% of its FLOPs. On S3DIS semantic segmentation, QPoint (71.2% mIoU) is within 0.4% of PTv2 (71.6%) and 2.4% of PTv3 (73.6%) while using only 9.8M parameters and 9.4G FLOPs versus PTv3's 46.2M/149.3G. In few-shot classification, QPoint leads Mamba3D by 2.4% (5-way 10-shot) and 1.0% (10-way 20-shot) with lower standard deviation, confirming superior generalization under data scarcity.

## Relation to Prior Work

QPoint sits at the intersection of the ViT-style tokenized point cloud paradigm (FPS + KNN + patch embedding, as in Point-MAE and Point-M2AE) and the emerging quaternion-based geometric deep learning literature (quaternion CNNs, rotation-equivariant networks). It departs from prior quaternion work, which has been confined to specific sub-tasks like registration or pose estimation, by systematically embedding quaternion algebra into both local feature extraction (QELP) and global self-attention (QGA) within a single end-to-end architecture. Unlike pre-training-heavy approaches (Point-BERT, Point-M2AE) or large-capacity Mamba backbones (Mamba3D, PCM), QPoint explicitly rejects external data and heavy pre-training, instead encoding geometric priors directly into the network with minimal parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that quaternion geometric priors can substitute for the large model capacity or extensive data augmentation that prior methods rely on to handle rotation sensitivity, achieving SOTA accuracy with 4.5M parameters and 0.9G FLOPs—orders of magnitude lighter than Transformer or Mamba competitors. This positions QPoint as a practical baseline for resource-constrained deployment (edge devices, real-time robotics). However, rotation robustness, while dramatically improved (80.6% vs. ~60% under 180° rotation), still shows a 13.6% accuracy drop, leaving open the question of whether true invariance can be achieved without sacrificing discriminative power. The authors note future extensions to registration and generation, but the current scope is limited to classification and segmentation on relatively small-scale benchmarks.
