## Problem

Riesz representer estimation is a central subproblem in debiased machine learning, but existing approaches face distinct failure modes: Hyvärinen score matching is numerically unstable in high dimensions due to divergence computations, and flexible density ratio estimation suffers from severe overfitting (train loss hacking, density chasm). No prior work provides an explicit score-matching-based construction of Riesz representers with convergence guarantees sufficient for Neyman orthogonal debiased estimation, nor a unified framework connecting average marginal effect (AME) and average policy effect (APE) estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observational data (X, Y) and, for ratio-based targets (ATE, APE, policy paths), additionally requires endpoint samples from the counterfactual distributions to construct a differentiable bridge. A noise-level distribution π over [σ_min, σ_max] parameterizes the DSM training. Outputs include the Riesz representer α̂, the debiased scalar estimate θ̂, and—uniquely—a continuous policy path θ̂(δ) on a grid of policy intensities, enabling interpolation between the infinitesimal AME limit and finite-shift APEs. For known pushforward policies, a single trained data-score model is reused across all δ values via one-dimensional integration along the known transport path.

## Method

ScoreMatchingRiesz reformulates Riesz representer estimation as score estimation with two complementary modules. Data-ScoreMatchingRiesz trains a score network s_θ(x, σ) via denoising score matching (Gaussian noising with a continuum of σ levels), and the AME representer is read off directly as the negative treatment-coordinate component of the learned score at a small evaluation noise level. Time-ScoreMatchingRiesz trains a time-score network s_θ(x, t) via the integration-by-parts objective from DRE-∞ (infinitesimal classification) along a differentiable bridge {p_t} connecting endpoint distributions; the log density ratio is then recovered by numerical integration of the learned time score over t, and the representer is formed as a difference of exponentiated log-ratios. The two modules can be jointly trained with a shared backbone and separate heads, and the entire construction is generalized through a Bregman divergence loss-link framework (Theorem 6.1) that makes the fitting geometry and implied covariate-balancing equations explicit. Convergence rates are established: Data-SMR achieves the minimax-optimal n^{-2s/(2s+d)} rate (inherited from diffusion-model theory), while Time-SMR achieves n^{-2s/(2s+d+1)} on the (x,t) domain, both sufficient for the product-rate condition in the Neyman orthogonal score.

## Results

On the synthetic AME target (true = 2.225), Data-SMR achieves the lowest MSE of 0.161 with near-zero bias (0.001), compared to Time-SMR (MSE 0.637) and Riesz regression (MSE 10.076). On the synthetic APE target at δ=1 (true = 4.063), Data-SMR again leads with MSE 0.486, while Riesz regression degrades severely to MSE 74.918. On the IHDP semi-synthetic ATE benchmark (n=672, 25 covariates), Joint-SMR achieves the best RMSE of 1.478, followed by Time-SMR (1.571), both dramatically outperforming SQ-Riesz (RMSE 27.954) and BKL-Riesz (RMSE 84.072).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Riesz regression (Chernozhukov et al., 2021), both Data-SMR and Time-SMR achieve substantially lower MSE on the synthetic design: Data-SMR reduces AME MSE from 10.076 to 0.161 (≈63× improvement) and APE MSE from 74.918 to 0.486 (≈154× improvement), while also reducing bias. On the IHDP ATE benchmark, the score-based methods (Time-SMR and Joint-SMR) dominate the Riesz-regression baselines (SQ-Riesz and BKL-Riesz) by an order of magnitude in RMSE (1.478–1.571 vs. 27.954–84.072), and the Riesz baselines exhibit near-zero or very low coverage (0.0% and 19.0%) while the score-based methods achieve 100% coverage. The paper notes that coverage for all methods on the synthetic design falls below the nominal 95% level, attributed to residual second-order nuisance error and ratio amplification, so the coverage advantage on IHDP should be read as supplementary rather than definitive evidence of uniform dominance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads—Riesz representer estimation in debiased machine learning (Chernozhukov et al., 2018, 2021), denoising score matching from the diffusion model literature (Vincent, 2011; Song & Ermon, 2020), and infinitesimal-classification density ratio estimation (Choi et al., 2022, DRE-∞)—into a single representer-estimation module. The key conceptual move is recognizing that the AME Riesz representer is literally the negative data score, and that ratio-based representers (ATE, APE) can be recovered by integrating a time score along a bridge, thereby importing the stability properties of DSM and telescoping ratios into the Neyman-orthogonal score framework. The Bregman-divergence generalization (Kato, 2025a, 2026d) is adapted to the score-based construction, and the new 'policy path' parameter extends APE estimation from a single shift to a continuous curve, recovering AME as the δ→0 limit under known pushforward policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to Riesz regression for representer estimation, with minimax-optimal L2 rates (Theorems 8.1–8.2) that are sufficient for √n-consistency of the final debiased estimator. The policy path is a genuinely useful interpretability tool that lets practitioners see how policy effects evolve continuously rather than at a single shift, and the known-pushforward result shows a single trained score model can generate representers for all δ without retraining. Limitations include undercoverage in finite samples (75–92% vs. nominal 95%), the d+1 dimensional rate for Time-SMR that becomes impractical in high dimensions without strong structural assumptions, and the computational overhead of bridge sampling and time-derivative computation. Open questions include whether the framework scales to very high-dimensional covariates, how sensitive the policy path is to bridge choice, and whether the undercoverage can be resolved with better variance estimation.
