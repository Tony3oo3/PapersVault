## Problem

Standard contrastive learning in cross-view geo-localization assigns equal labels to all negatives, forcing the model to aggressively push away semi-positive samples that are geographically proximate to the true positive. This over-separation causes the model to fit training noise, leading to a generalization collapse that is especially severe in zero-shot cross-dataset transfer. The paper asks whether all negatives truly deserve equal repulsion and shows that they do not.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263532/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a street-view query image and a batch of B satellite reference images as input, encoded by a DINOv3-ViT-B backbone into cls and spatial tokens. For each positive, the Top-K hardest negatives are selected by same-modal cosine similarity, and their similarity scores serve as observations for the NIG uncertainty head. The output is a retrieval ranking over the satellite references, augmented by per-sample NIG parameters (γ, ν, α, β) from which a scalar environmental-complexity uncertainty u is derived in a single forward pass.

## Method

The core contribution is an uncertainty-aware training framework built on Deep Evidential Regression. An Uncertainty Head performs cls-to-spatial cross-attention on each positive sample and extracts four attention-weight statistics (normalized entropy, maximum response, Top-2 ratio, and variance), which are concatenated with the attention output and passed through an MLP to predict the four NIG hyperparameters. The resulting uncertainty u = E[σ²] + Var[μ] quantifies how complex the positive's local neighborhood is. A Soft InfoNCE loss then uses u to scale the target labels of Top-K hard negatives (λ_j^soft = λ_base + s_ij · u_i · w), so that semi-positive samples receive a moderated push-away force while easy negatives retain standard repulsion. The total training objective combines this Soft InfoNCE with an NIG negative-log-likelihood loss plus an evidence-regularization term, all optimized jointly with the encoder via AdamW over 40 epochs.

## Results

On CVUSA, the proposed UA-GEO achieves 99.30% R@1 and 99.93% R@1%, surpassing the previous best CLGT (98.85% R@1) and Sample4Geo (98.68% R@1). On CVACT_val and CVACT_test, it reaches 92.87% and 73.63% R@1 respectively, outperforming all prior methods. On the more challenging VIGOR benchmark (which includes semi-positive samples), UA-GEO attains 79.48% R@1 (Same-Area) and 66.70% R@1 (Cross-Area), improving over Sample4Geo by 1.62 and 5.0 points. In zero-shot cross-dataset transfer, the method delivers an average 18% R@1 gain, e.g., 82.01% R@1 for CVUSA→CVACT_val vs. 64.82% for CLGT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UA-GEO outperforms all 11 baselines across every standard benchmark. On CVUSA, it improves R@1 by 0.45 points over CLGT (98.85→99.30) and 0.62 over Sample4Geo (98.68→99.30); on CVACT_val, the gain is 0.90 points over CLGT (91.97→92.87) and 2.06 over Sample4Geo (90.81→92.87). On the harder CVACT_test split, it leads CLGT by 0.41 R@1 (73.22→73.63) and Sample4Geo by 2.12 (71.51→73.63). On VIGOR, where semi-positive samples are explicitly present, UA-GEO beats Sample4Geo by 1.62 R@1 (Same-Area) and 5.0 R@1 (Cross-Area), and also surpasses CLGT (77.18→79.48 Same, 60.70→66.70 Cross). The most dramatic gains appear in zero-shot cross-dataset transfer: CVUSA→CVACT_val R@1 jumps from 64.82 (CLGT) to 82.01, and CVUSA→CVACT_test from 35.52 to 54.99, confirming that the Soft InfoNCE mechanism directly addresses the generalization collapse caused by equal negative labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Sample4Geo framework (Deuser et al., 2023) and its InfoNCE-based training paradigm, but departs fundamentally from the standard equal-negative-label assumption by introducing Deep Evidential Regression (Amini et al., 2020) as a mechanism to estimate per-sample neighborhood complexity. Rather than using uncertainty for prediction confidence or OOD detection as in prior DER applications, the authors repurpose the Normal-Inverse-Gamma posterior to generate soft labels that modulate repulsion strength in a modified InfoNCE loss. The set-theoretic generalization framework (P ∩ E) provides a novel analytical lens that reframes the semi-positive sample problem as a constrained growth direction issue, distinguishing this work from prior CVGL efforts that addressed generalization through data augmentation or causal disentanglement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 18% average R@1 gain in zero-shot cross-dataset transfer is substantial and suggests that the semi-positive over-separation problem is a primary bottleneck for CVGL generalization, not merely a secondary effect. The conceptual insight—that uniformly pushing all negatives equally is theoretically biased when semi-positive samples exist—generalizes beyond CVGL to any contrastive retrieval task with geographic or semantic ambiguity. Open questions include whether the Gaussian assumption on same-modal similarities holds in more diverse or corrupted settings, whether the Top-K selection strategy scales to very large candidate pools, and whether the approach can be extended to multi-modal or video-based geo-localization where semi-positive structure is more complex.
