## Problem

Autoregressive decoding at long contexts is memory-bandwidth-bound: generating each token requires streaming the full KV cache (all n_k key and value rows) from memory, and even optimized exact kernels like FlashAttention and FlashDecoding still touch essentially the entire KV state per step. Existing mitigations reduce KV footprint (quantization, compression), prune retained tokens (eviction, selection), or improve IO locality for exact attention, but none reduce the cost of the post-softmax value aggregation operator itself over whatever KV state is presented at decode time.

## Input / Output

During decoding, the method consumes a single query vector q ∈ R^{1×d_k} and the full key/value matrices K, V ∈ R^{n_k×d_k} from the KV cache (bf16, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct shapes with n_k up to 32k). It produces an approximate attention output vector that is an unbiased estimator of the exact AV, using only S ≪ n_k gathered value rows and additive accumulation. The sample budget S is the tunable accuracy-latency knob; at accuracy-matching operating points, S=128 for S²ANTA-prop and S=2048 for S²ANTA-flash at 32k contexts, corresponding to <1.6% of value rows accessed.

## Method

SANTA treats the post-softmax attention weights as a categorical distribution and samples S indices with replacement, then averages the corresponding value rows via gather-and-add, yielding an unbiased estimator of AV with variance scaling as 1/S while eliminating all value-stage multiplications. S²ANTA reduces variance through stratified sampling (dividing the CDF into S equal-probability intervals and sampling once per stratum) and systematic sampling (a single random offset applied across all strata), both proven unbiased with stratified sampling admitting a formal variance-dominance guarantee. Two GPU kernel strategies parallelize the sampling: S²ANTA-prop pre-computes per-tile probability masses via a global sync to allocate exact sample budgets, while S²ANTA-flash speculatively assigns uniform per-tile budgets and re-weights partial outputs post-hoc, removing the barrier at the cost of sample waste. A complementary Bernoulli qK^T mechanism represents each query element as a Bernoulli variable to form sparse ternary queries in {−1,0,+1}, inducing feature-sparse key access during decoding and enabling multiplier-free score computation. Together, these techniques reduce value-cache reads from n_k to S rows per query and key-feature access to a fraction of d_k, achieving up to 1.5× decode-step kernel speedup and 1.25× end-to-end decode-latency speedup while matching baseline accuracy.

## Results

At 32k-token contexts on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, S²ANTA-prop (S=128) and S²ANTA-flash (S=2048) match SDPA baseline accuracy within 95% bootstrap confidence intervals across all four RULER long-context tasks (FWE, NIAH, QA₁, QA₂) while achieving 1.50× and 1.51× decode-step attention-kernel speedup over FlashInfer and FlashDecoding, respectively. In batched end-to-end generation (batch size 6, 30k-token prompts, 2048 decoded tokens), S²ANTA-flash delivers a 1.25× decode-latency speedup and S²ANTA-prop a 1.229× speedup over FlashAttention-2. On LongBench v2, S²ANTA-prop (S=128) scores 28.10% and S²ANTA-flash (S=2048) scores 28.83%, both within the SDPA baseline of 28.36%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlashInfer and FlashDecoding (the two optimized exact-attention decode kernels), S²ANTA-prop achieves a 1.50× decode-step attention-kernel speedup and S²ANTA-flash achieves 1.51× at 32k-token contexts, while both match SDPA accuracy within confidence intervals. In the full end-to-end batched generation loop, S²ANTA-flash outperforms FlashAttention-2 by 1.25× on decode latency (6.589 ms/token vs. 8.237 ms/token), and S²ANTA-prop by 1.229× (6.702 ms/token). Against top-k attention at matched budgets (k=S), S²ANTA variants outperform top-k across nearly all 8k-token long-context tasks, with the largest gap on multi-hop QA₂ where S²ANTA-sys leads by approximately 5 percentage points (e.g., 69.4% vs. 64.2% at S=k=256); on short-context GSM8K, top-k is competitive or slightly ahead at low budgets (k=8: 41.3% vs. 1.4% for SANTA), but S²ANTA-sys surpasses top-k from S=k=16 onward. S²ANTA-flash requires a substantially larger sample budget (S=2048) than S²ANTA-prop (S=128) to match accuracy, due to sample waste from uniform per-tile allocation before deferred renormalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

SANTA carves out a distinct niche in the long-context inference landscape: rather than reducing the KV cache footprint (quantization, eviction, offloading) or optimizing exact attention kernels (FlashAttention, FlashInfer, FlashDecoding), it modifies the value-aggregation operator itself by replacing dense multiply-accumulate with stochastic gather-and-add over S ≪ n_k sampled rows. The GPU kernel design borrows FlashDecoding's split-KV tiling and parallel reduction strategy, while the variance-reduction machinery (stratified and systematic sampling) is a classical statistics technique transplanted into attention. The Bernoulli qK^T component extends the same multiplier-free, stochastic-estimation philosophy to the score stage, and the paper explicitly positions both as orthogonal to and composable with upstream KV compression, GQA, and cache-selection methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is a 1.5× decode-step kernel speedup at 32k contexts with S=128 (≈0.4% of value rows) matching baseline accuracy, translating to 1.25× end-to-end decode latency in batched generation — a meaningful gain for long-context, decode-heavy serving. The finding that an unbiased stochastic estimator outperforms deterministic top-k on multi-hop QA (by ~5%) challenges the assumption that the most-attended tokens are always the most informative for compositional reasoning. Key limitations include evaluation only on 8B-class models and a single GPU (RTX 6000 Ada), the Amdahl-bound ceiling on end-to-end speedup (KV is only ~60% of decode bandwidth), and the large sample-waste gap between S²ANTA-prop (S=128) and S²ANTA-flash (S=2048). Open questions include interaction with aggressive KV quantization, behavior on datacenter GPUs with different bandwidth hierarchies, and whether the approach remains beneficial under MLA-style architectures that already reduce KV bandwidth.
