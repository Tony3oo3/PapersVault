## Problem

Existing probabilistic robustness (PR) formulations require a fixed, known perturbation distribution (e.g., Gaussian or Uniform), an assumption that is rarely satisfiable in practice since the true noise characteristics of a deployment environment are unknown. This paper introduces Non-Parametric Probabilistic Robustness (NPPR), which removes the need for a predefined perturbation distribution by instead optimizing over an admissible set of distributions to yield a conservative (lower-bound) robustness estimate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264881/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet), their ground-truth labels, and a frozen classifier as input, operating under an L∞ perturbation budget γ ∈ {4/255, 8/255, 16/255}. It produces a scalar NPPR robustness score (a probability in [0,1]) estimated via Monte Carlo sampling, along with the learned GMM parameters (mixture weights, means, and full covariance matrices) defined in a lower-dimensional latent space (128 or 256 dimensions) that are mapped back to input resolution via bicubic interpolation and a scaled-tanh projection to enforce the perturbation budget.

## Method

NPPR is formally defined as the infimum of the PR expectation over an admissible perturbation distribution set P_ε, guaranteeing G_AR ≤ G_NPPR ≤ G_PR. The practical estimator restricts P_ε to a K-component GMM family (K ∈ {3,7,12}) whose parameters are produced by lightweight MLP heads conditioned on input features, labels, or both (four dependency variants). To enable end-to-end gradient-based training of the discrete mixture selection, a Gumbel-Softmax relaxation with annealed temperature is applied, and perturbations are generated in a low-dimensional latent space then upsampled to input resolution via bicubic interpolation to avoid the O(d²) covariance cost. The GMM is trained by maximizing a softplus logit-margin loss on correctly classified samples under a frozen classifier, with theoretical guarantees including an exponential Gumbel-Softmax approximation bound and O(1/T) SGD convergence for the relaxed nonconvex objective.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet18 (L∞ budget 16/255), NPPR yields 76.29% global robustness, substantially lower than PR under Uniform (97.21%), Gaussian (95.28%), or Laplace (95.13%) perturbation distributions, yet far above AR estimates from PGD (3.10%) or AutoAttack (0.00%). This ordering (AR ≤ NPPR ≤ PR) holds consistently across all 12 dataset–model combinations. On the harder CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet, NPPR drops to 58.65% and 53.20% respectively for ResNet18, while PR under fixed distributions remains above 86%, highlighting the conservatism of the learned perturbation distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against standard PR evaluated under fixed perturbation distributions (Gaussian, Uniform, Laplace) as in Webb et al. (2019) and subsequent works, and against AR estimated via PGD, CW, and AutoAttack. NPPR is consistently more conservative than all PR variants: on CIFAR-10/ResNet18, NPPR (76.29%) is 19–21 percentage points lower than PR under Uniform (97.21%), Gaussian (95.28%), or Laplace (95.13%). Conversely, NPPR is far less pessimistic than AR, which drops to near-zero under AutoAttack (0.00%) and single-digit values under PGD (3.10%). This empirical ordering G_AR ≤ G_NPPR ≤ G_PR validates Proposition 3.4 across all 12 dataset–model configurations. The gap between NPPR and PR widens on harder tasks (e.g., 33 pp on CIFAR-100/ResNet18 vs. 21 pp on CIFAR-10/ResNet18), suggesting that fixed-distribution PR increasingly overestimates robustness as the classification task becomes more challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probabilistic robustness (PR) framework introduced by Webb et al. (2019) and developed in subsequent assessment and training works by removing the central assumption that the perturbation distribution is known and fixed. Where prior PR formulations require the assessor to specify a distribution (Gaussian, Uniform, Laplace) on a case-by-case basis, NPPR instead optimizes over an admissible set of distributions to obtain a conservative lower bound on PR. The estimation approach borrows the Gumbel-Softmax relaxation for differentiable mixture selection and bicubic upsampling for computational tractability, while the theoretical positioning (G_AR ≤ G_NPPR ≤ G_PR) connects NPPR to both adversarial robustness and standard PR in a unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: in real deployment, the perturbation distribution is rarely known a priori, making standard PR evaluations potentially misleading. NPPR provides a defensible conservative estimate without requiring domain-specific distributional assumptions, which is valuable for safety-critical assessment. However, the 'non-parametric' label is somewhat aspirational since the implementation restricts optimization to a finite-K GMM family, introducing an approximation gap (quantified in Appendix B) that limits how conservative the estimate can be. Open questions include whether the learned GMM components have meaningful physical interpretation, how the method scales to higher-resolution or video inputs, and whether the evaluation-only scope can be extended to training objectives that directly optimize NPPR.
