## Problem

Hyperfitting—fine-tuning LLMs to near-zero training loss on small datasets—improves greedy generation diversity, but its mechanism was unclear. A natural hypothesis was that it merely sharpens the output distribution (equivalent to temperature scaling), but this remained untested. The paper asks whether hyperfitting represents a fundamentally different learning dynamic or a trivial probability rescaling, and where in the network the effect is localized.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights (TinyLlama-1.1B, Qwen2.5-1.5B, LLaMA-3.2-3B, Gemma-2-2B, LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct) and a small text corpus of 2,000 samples, training for 260 epochs with zero weight decay until loss approaches zero. At inference, a text prompt is fed to the fine-tuned model and text is produced via deterministic greedy decoding (no sampling). The Late-Stage LoRA variant restricts trainable adapters to only the final 5 transformer layers, reducing trainable parameters by ~78–83% relative to full LoRA while converging to the same loss floor.

## Method

The paper first falsifies the Temperature Hypothesis via entropy-matched controls: scaling the original model's temperature to match the hyperfitted model's entropy (T*≈0.59) yields TTR 0.397 versus 0.684 for the hyperfitted model, proving the effect is not distribution sharpening. A synthetic logit-injection ablation then rules out static vocabulary bias, showing that even minimal context-agnostic reweighting degrades generation monotonically. Layer-wise representational analysis localizes the mechanism to a 'Terminal Expansion' in the final transformer block, where effective dimensionality jumps by ΔDim≈+80.8 (TinyLlama) after a compression phase in intermediate layers, enabling promotion of deep-tail tokens to Top-1. Guided by this localization, the authors introduce Late-Stage LoRA, which applies low-rank adapters exclusively to the last 5 layers; this reproduces the rank-reordering dynamics (Top-1 Agreement ≈0.52) and repetition suppression with ~80% fewer trainable parameters, and on deeper architectures (Qwen2.5-1.5B) it even outperforms full LoRA in coherence.

## Results

On TinyLlama-1.1B, the hyperfitted model achieves a Type-Token Ratio (TTR) of 0.684 and Bigram Repetition of 0.140 under greedy decoding, compared to 0.400 and 0.592 for the original model, while maintaining a matched prediction entropy (0.862 vs. 0.875 nats, p=0.73). The Top-1 Agreement with the original model drops to 0.570 and Spearman rank correlation to 0.430, confirming a substantial rank-reordering mechanism rather than distribution sharpening. The proposed Late-Stage LoRA (final 5 layers only) replicates this effect with ~80% fewer trainable parameters, achieving TTR 0.591 and Bigram Rep. 0.213 on Qwen2.5-1.5B—outperforming Full LoRA (0.575, 0.248). Cross-domain evaluation confirms robustness: hyperfitted greedy decoding reaches TTR 0.60–0.78 and MAUVE 0.82–0.96 across Fiction-Stories, WritingPrompts, and AG News.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against inference-time decoding baselines on LLaMA-3.2-3B and Gemma-2-2B (WritingPrompts), hyperfitted greedy decoding achieves MAUVE 0.82–0.91, substantially outperforming Min-p (Nguyen et al., 2025; MAUVE 0.67–0.74) and GUARD (Ding et al., 2025; MAUVE 0.46–0.53), while remaining fully deterministic. GUARD achieves higher raw TTR (~0.80 vs. 0.67–0.69) but at the cost of lower coherence and shorter generations; Min-p improves diversity over greedy (TTR 0.49–0.51 vs. 0.28) but remains stochastic. Internally, Late-Stage LoRA (final 5 layers) matches or exceeds Full LoRA: on Qwen2.5-1.5B it achieves TTR 0.591 vs. 0.575 and Bigram Rep. 0.213 vs. 0.248, and on Gemma-2-2B it reaches TTR 0.608 vs. 0.559, while reducing trainable parameters by ~80%. The entropy-matched temperature-scaled baseline (T≈0.59) fails to replicate any diversity gain (TTR 0.397 vs. 0.684), confirming that hyperfitting's benefit is mechanistically distinct from distribution sharpening.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Carlsson et al. (2025), who empirically identified the Hyperfitting phenomenon (near-zero training loss on small data improving greedy generation), and on the extensive literature on text degeneration under deterministic decoding (Holtzman et al., 2020; Wiher et al., 2022; Shi et al., 2024). It departs from the natural interpretation that hyperfitting is a learned form of temperature scaling by demonstrating via entropy-matched controls and static-bias ablations that the mechanism is instead a context-dependent rank reordering. The Late-Stage LoRA strategy connects to the PEFT literature (LoRA, LISA, layer-wise importance sampling) but is motivated by the paper's own mechanistic localization finding that the geometric expansion is confined to the final transformer block.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic account of why severe overfitting paradoxically improves generation quality, resolving a conceptual puzzle that challenges the standard bias-variance framing of fine-tuning. The Late-Stage LoRA strategy offers a practical ~80% parameter reduction for resolving greedy degeneration, with the added benefit of acting as a structural regularizer on deeper architectures. Key limitations include evaluation on models up to 8B parameters, reliance on lexical diversity metrics (TTR, bigram repetition) rather than semantic coherence, and the continued computational cost of the 260-epoch training regime. Open questions include whether the terminal-expansion mechanism persists at 70B+ scale and whether early-stopping criteria can be derived from the observed compression-then-expansion dynamics.
