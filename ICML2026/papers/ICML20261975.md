## Problem

Standard Top-k MoE routing enforces a fixed number of activated experts regardless of token difficulty or layer-specific needs, while existing fixed-threshold Top-p routing offers only marginal gains over Top-k, suffers from hypersensitivity to the threshold hyperparameter, and produces uncontrolled computational costs incompatible with strict pre-training budgets. The paper asks whether Top-p routing can be made both compute-controllable and consistently superior to Top-k under matched FLOPs.

## Input / Output

For NLP, the model consumes sequences of 2048 text tokens (GPT-NeoX-20B tokenizer, vocab 50,432) and produces a next-token distribution over the vocabulary; for CV, it consumes 2 trillion pixel tokens from image and image-text pairs and produces a denoised image via a DiT denoiser. In both settings, the MoE module replaces the standard FFN in every transformer block, and the router outputs a variable-size expert mask per token whose size is governed by the dynamic probability threshold rather than a fixed k.

## Method

DTOP-p replaces the fixed Top-p threshold with a Proportional-Integral (PI) controller that treats the target average expert count as a setpoint and updates the threshold between training steps using the sparsity error (target minus actual activation), exploiting the monotonicity of nucleus sampling to form a negative feedback loop. Because the threshold binarizes the expert mask and is non-differentiable, gradient descent cannot optimize it, making the PI controller the natural mechanism for making it effectively learnable. A second component, Dynamic Routing Normalization (DRN), applies a layer-specific learnable temperature θ_l to batch-normalized routing logits before softmax, decoupling the global threshold from local logit statistics so that shallow layers can converge to near-single-expert activation while deeper layers recruit more experts under the same global budget. A layer-wise variant assigns an independent PI controller to each layer for deployment settings requiring per-layer compute predictability. The full training objective combines cross-entropy LM loss, load-balancing loss, and a dynamic (entropy-minimizing) loss, while explicitly excluding the Router Z-Loss which was found to flatten routing distributions and hinder expert specialization.

## Results

On the MoE-1.3B-6.9B-64E8A model trained on 100B DCLM-Baseline tokens, DTOP-p achieves an average score of 50.9 across 13 NLP benchmarks, outperforming Top-k MoE (49.0) and fixed-threshold Top-p MoE (49.3), while matching the average FLOPs of Top-k. The largest gains appear on symbolic reasoning (SVAMP: 16.0 vs. 10.3 for Top-k) and commonsense reasoning (COPA: 85.0 vs. 82.0). On CV tasks (DiT, 0.9B/3.4B, 2T pixel tokens), DTOP-p also achieves the lowest validation loss (0.2048) compared to Top-k (0.2058) and Top-p (0.2055).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTOP-p is compared against two MoE baselines: Top-k MoE (Fedus et al., 2022) and fixed-threshold Top-p MoE (Huang et al., 2024). Against Top-k, DTOP-p wins on 11 of 13 benchmarks, with the most substantial margin on SVAMP (+5.7 pts, 16.0 vs. 10.3) and COPA (+3.0 pts, 85.0 vs. 82.0); it loses only on PIQA (−0.8) and AGIEval-LSAT-LR (−0.9). Against fixed Top-p, DTOP-p wins on 10 of 13 benchmarks, with the largest gap on SVAMP (+7.7 pts, 16.0 vs. 8.3) and COPA (+3.0 pts); it loses only on LAMBADA (−1.0) and AGIEval-LSAT-LR (+0.6). Critically, DTOP-p achieves these gains while strictly maintaining the target of 8 activated experts per token (matching Top-k FLOPs), whereas fixed Top-p frequently activates 10–12+ experts during late training, making its compute cost unpredictable. The paper also confirms the advantage generalizes to CV: on a DiT (0.9B/3.4B, 64E8A) trained on 2T pixel tokens, DTOP-p achieves the lowest validation loss (0.2048) versus Top-k (0.2058) and Top-p (0.2055).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Top-p MoE routing paradigm introduced by Huang et al. (2024) and Yang et al. (2024), which itself borrows the nucleus sampling selection rule from Holtzman et al. (2019). The key departure is replacing the fixed global probability threshold with a Proportional-Integral controller borrowed from classical control theory (Ziegler & Nichols, 1942), making the threshold effectively learnable without gradients, and pairing it with a Dynamic Routing Normalization that introduces layer-wise learnable temperature scalars. The base MoE architecture follows the standard Switch/GShard formulation (Fedus et al., 2022; Lepikhin et al., 2020), and the work positions itself against both Top-k and fixed-threshold Top-p as baselines rather than against expert-choice or zero-compute-expert variants, which it treats as orthogonal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoE is now the dominant architecture for scaling foundation models (DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi, etc.), so making Top-p routing compute-controllable and more effective than Top-k at matched FLOPs has direct practical relevance for pre-training pipelines. The paper's negative result—that fixed-threshold Top-p yields only marginal gains over Top-k while exhibiting unstable sparsity—provides useful guidance against naively adopting Top-p without control. Open questions include whether the fixed PI gains generalize beyond the 13.6B-parameter / 300B-token regime tested here, whether automated gain scheduling could further improve convergence, and how DTOP-p interacts with other emerging MoE innovations such as expert-choice routing or zero-compute experts.
