## Problem

Existing class unlearning evaluations only verify that the forget-class logit is suppressed, overlooking the fact that a model retrained without a class systematically redistributes probability mass toward semantically similar retained classes. This geometric mismatch means that current unlearning methods, even those passing standard membership inference tests, retain detectable traces of the forgotten class's structure. The paper exposes this hidden leakage through a new class-level attack (CMIA) and shows that all ten SOTA baselines are vulnerable across MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained CNN (ResNet-18 or VGG-19) on a standard image classification dataset, the label of the single class to forget, and the full training set partitioned into retain and forget subsets. It produces an unlearned model whose output on forget-class inputs is a tilted probability distribution over the remaining K−1 classes (forget-class probability forced to zero), computed via PCA-projected cosine similarity of the final-layer logit weight vectors. A lightweight variant (TREW-2R) restricts gradient updates to two randomly selected layers, reducing wall-clock time to ~18 s per epoch on CIFAR-100 with 4×A40 GPUs.

## Method

TREW constructs a target distribution for forget-class inputs by first renormalizing the original model's output to remove the forget-class probability, then tilting the remaining distribution with an exponential family weighting exp(β·s_y) where s_y is the cosine similarity between PCA-projected logit weight vectors of class y and the forget class. This tilted distribution is derived as the information projection of the original distribution onto the retained-class simplex under a linear expected-similarity constraint (Proposition 3.1), and Proposition 3.2 shows that any positive β below a variance-dependent bound reduces KL divergence from the true retrained distribution. During fine-tuning, the model minimizes standard cross-entropy on retained samples plus cross-entropy against the tilted target on forget-class samples, requiring only 10 epochs at lr=0.001. The similarity scores are sample-independent (class-level), which empirically matches the low intra-class variance of retrained-model predictions better than per-sample prototype distances.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, TREW achieves 94.28% retained accuracy (ACC_r), 0% forget accuracy (ACC_f), 97.65% MIA score, and 95.82% CMIA score, with an average gap of only −0.59 relative to the retrain baseline (vs. −7.18 for the next-best SCAR). On the new CMIA metric across three datasets, TREW reduces the gap with retrained models by 46% compared to the SOTA method (SCAR) on CIFAR-10 (83.63 vs. 56.08 for auto→truck). Under the stronger U-LiRA attack, TREW-2R achieves 67.72% accuracy (closest to the ideal 50%), reducing the gap with retrain by 19% over the best prior method (FT at 72.57%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TREW dominates on the new CMIA metric, achieving 95.82% on CIFAR-10 (nearly matching Retrain's 95.27%), while the next-best baselines SCAR (71.93%), SVD (67.19%), and UAM (66.71%) fall 24–86 points short. On U-LiRA, TREW-2R (67.72%) and TREW (71.12%) are the strongest, outperforming FT (72.57%) and SCRUB (71.91%), while methods like l1 (98.32%), SalUn (97.50%), and RL (96.79%) remain highly vulnerable. On retained accuracy (ACC_r), TREW (94.28%) is competitive with SVD (94.17%) and SCAR (93.57%), trailing only Retrain (94.83%) by 0.55 points. On standard MIA, TREW (97.65%) matches or exceeds SVD (97.20%) and is close to l1 and UAM (both 100%), while methods like BU (85.91%) and SCRUB (85.01%) show significant leakage. TREW achieves perfect forgetting (ACC_f = 0) across all datasets, matching most baselines except GA (14.12%) and UAM (2.22%) which retain residual accuracy on the forget class. The lightweight TREW-2R variant (updating only 2 random layers) retains nearly all of TREW's performance while being among the fastest methods at 18.25s per epoch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the class unlearning literature (Warnecke et al. 2021; Chen et al. 2023; Chang et al. 2024) but identifies a fundamental blind spot: prior methods and their evaluations focus exclusively on the forget-class logit, ignoring the structured misclassification pattern that retrained-from-scratch models exhibit on forget-class samples. The CMIA attack extends the membership inference evaluation lineage (Shokri et al. 2017; Hayes et al. 2024 U-LiRA) by probing class-neighbor probabilities rather than the forget-class output directly. TREW's tilted distribution is grounded in information projection (I-projection) onto a constrained simplex, drawing on exponential family theory, and leverages the neural collapse phenomenon (Papyan et al. 2020) to justify a sample-independent, class-level similarity constraint rather than per-sample adjustments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all ten SOTA class unlearning methods leak membership through class-structured misclassification—despite passing standard MIA evaluations—has direct implications for GDPR compliance and the reliability of unlearning claims in deployed systems. CMIA provides the community with a more rigorous, complementary evaluation tool, while TREW offers a computationally lightweight fix (10 epochs of fine-tuning with a modified cross-entropy loss) that matches or exceeds retrained models across four datasets and two architectures. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models (e.g., ViTs, LLMs), whether higher-order moment constraints or richer similarity functions would close the remaining gap with retraining, and whether CMIA can be formalized beyond its current heuristic construction.
