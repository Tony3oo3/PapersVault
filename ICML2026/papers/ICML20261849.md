## Problem

Modern video generators trained on large-scale datasets still produce physically implausible motions and temporal inconsistencies, yet existing remedies—external verifier-based rejection sampling or post-training on augmented physics data—are computationally expensive, domain-specific, and struggle with fine-grained motion dynamics. The paper asks whether a pre-trained video generator can serve as its own self-refiner at inference time, without any external model, verifier, or additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261849/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a plug-and-play inference-time refinement applied to any pre-trained flow matching or diffusion video generator. For text-to-video, it takes a text prompt; for image-to-video, it takes a reference image plus a text prompt. It operates entirely in the VAE latent space of the base model, adding roughly 1.5× the base NFEs (e.g., 60 vs. 40 for Wan2.2) while keeping memory usage identical to the base sampler. The output is a standard video clip at the model's native resolution (480p for Wan, 720p for Cosmos).

## Method

The key insight is reinterpreting the flow matching training objective as a weighted generalized denoising autoencoder (DAE), which means that at any fixed noise level t, the model's denoising step acts as a DAE reconstruction. This enables Predict-and-Perturb (P&P): the model predicts a clean endpoint ẑ₁ from the current noisy latent z_t (Predict), then re-noises ẑ₁ back to the same noise level t with fresh Gaussian noise (Perturb), forming a pseudo-Gibbs sampling loop that pulls the latent toward higher-density, physically plausible regions of the learned distribution. Only 2–3 P&P iterations at early timesteps (t < 0.2T) are needed, and the refined latent is simply substituted into the standard ODE solver step. To prevent over-saturation artifacts from repeated classifier-free guidance in static regions, an uncertainty-aware variant computes a per-pixel self-consistency mask from the L1 difference between consecutive P&P predictions, refining only uncertain (motion) regions while freezing confident (background) regions at no extra NFE cost.

## Results

On Dynamic-bench with Wan2.2-A14B T2V, P&P achieves the highest VBench Motion score (98.41) and Consistency score (91.33), outperforming the default ODE solver (98.01/90.68), CFG-Zero (98.27/91.16), and FlowMo (97.68/90.95), while using only 60 NFEs (1.5× overhead). In human evaluation, 73.6% of evaluators prefer P&P's motion quality over the default sampler, and 81.5% prefer it over CFG-Zero. On PAI-Bench robotics I2V, P&P improves grasp success by +11.0% over the Cosmos base model (89.6% vs 79.2%) and +8.4% over Wan2.2 (85.7% vs 77.3%), surpassing the verifier-based best-of-4 rejection sampler (84.4%/80.5%) with fewer NFEs (57–60 vs 140–144).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CFG-Zero (Fan et al., 2025), P&P wins on both VBench Motion (98.41 vs 98.27) and Consistency (91.33 vs 91.16), and is strongly preferred in human evaluation (81.5% motion win rate), while using 1.5× inference time versus CFG-Zero's 1.0×. Against FlowMo (Shaulov et al., 2025), P&P achieves higher VBench Motion (98.41 vs 97.68) and Consistency (91.33 vs 90.95), with a 70.6% human preference, and runs at 1.5× time versus FlowMo's 3.9× (which requires gradient computation). Against the verifier-based rejection sampler using Cosmos-Reason1 7B (Azzolini et al., 2025) on PAI-Bench, P&P achieves higher grasp success (89.6% vs 84.4% on Cosmos; 85.7% vs 80.5% on Wan2.2) and higher Robot-QA accuracy (76.3% vs 72.3%; 80.3% vs 78.1%), while requiring only 57–60 NFEs versus 140–144 for the best-of-4 verifier. P&P also outperforms the simple NFE×2 baseline on all VBench metrics and human evaluations, demonstrating that targeted refinement is more effective than brute-force additional sampling steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the flow matching training objective as a time-conditioned denoising autoencoder (DAE), then repurposes the pseudo-Gibbs Markov chain from generalized DAEs (Bengio et al., 2013) as an inference-time refinement loop for video generators. Conceptually, it extends the Self-Refine paradigm (Madaan et al., 2023) from LLMs to video flow matching, but departs from prior video-specific approaches that rely on external verifiers (Cosmos-Reason1, GPT4Motion), post-training on physics data (WISA, PhyGDPO), or gradient-based guidance (FlowMo). The key departure is that no external model, additional training, or gradient computation is needed—the generator serves as its own refiner through a simple predict-perturb inner loop at fixed noise levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, plug-and-play improvement (1.5× NFE) for any flow matching or diffusion video generator, demonstrated across Wan2.1/2.2, Cosmos-Predict-2.5, CogVideoX, and FLUX, making it broadly adoptable without retraining. The uncertainty-aware gating mechanism addresses a real failure mode (CFG over-saturation in static regions) using only the model's own self-consistency signal at zero extra cost. Key limitations include its local-search nature (it cannot fix tasks requiring global planning like maze solving), the ~40% inference time overhead, and the open question of whether adaptive thresholds or combining with global search/external verifiers could push gains further without sacrificing diversity.
