## Problem

Permutation-equivariant weight-space networks are the current state of the art for learning over MLP parameters, but it remains unclear whether the symmetry constraints they impose fundamentally limit their approximation power. Prior theoretical results were partial—showing specific capabilities like forward-pass simulation—without a comprehensive characterization of when universality holds or fails across the natural approximation settings that arise in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260843/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the full parameterization of a fixed-architecture MLP (weight matrices W_ℓ ∈ R^{d_ℓ×d_{ℓ-1}} and bias vectors b_ℓ ∈ R^{d_ℓ} for all layers ℓ) as input, and produces either a scalar/vector prediction (for functionals) or a complete set of output network weights (for operators). A key structural constraint is that standard equivariant networks must output weights of the same architecture as the input, bounding the representational capacity of the output; the proposed OCE modification relaxes this by outputting an ensemble of k MLPs whose averaged output serves as the final prediction, effectively increasing output capacity without changing the input architecture.

## Method

The paper first proves that all prominent permutation-equivariant weight-space networks (DWS, NFN, GMN, NG-GNN, and NFT under a general-position assumption) are expressively equivalent, unifying a fragmented architectural landscape. It then organizes expressivity analysis around four approximation settings—function-space functionals, permutation-invariant functionals, function-space operators, and permutation-equivariant operators—and characterizes universality in each. The key technical tool is a continuous canonization map that, under the general-position assumption (pairwise distinct biases per layer), selects a unique representative from each permutation orbit, enabling reduction of equivariant approximation to standard MLP approximation. For function-space operators, the paper shows that fixed-architecture outputs are fundamentally insufficient (bounded linear regions), but universality is recovered when the input architecture is sufficiently large. Guided by this theory, the authors propose Output Capacity Expansion (OCE), which replaces the single output network with an ensemble of k MLPs, achieving up to 34% MSE improvement over prior SOTA on the MNIST INR dilation benchmark.

## Results

On the MNIST INR dilation benchmark (a function-space operator task), the proposed Output Capacity Expansion (OCE) modification applied to GMN achieves the best MSE of 1.06±0.13 (k=8 ensemble), outperforming the previous SOTA (ScaleGMN+GradMetaNet++ at 1.60±0.01) by 34%. DWS+OCE(k=8) also improves over its base DWS(k=1) from 2.29 to 1.36 MSE. The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: it proves expressive equivalence among all major permutation-equivariant weight-space architectures (DWS, NFN, GMN, NG-GNN, and NFT under general position) and establishes universality or non-universality across four approximation settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's OCE-augmented methods (DWS+OCE and GMN+OCE) outperform all listed baselines on the MNIST INR dilation benchmark. GMN+OCE(k=8) achieves MSE 1.06, beating the previous SOTA ScaleGMN+GradMetaNet++ (1.60) by 34% and NG-T-64 (1.75) by 39%. DWS+OCE(k=8) (MSE 1.36) similarly surpasses ScaleGMN+GradMetaNet++ by 15% and its own base DWS(k=1) (2.29) by 41%. The comparison with NFT (MSE 5.10) and NP-NFN (2.55) is less informative since these are older architectures; the most relevant comparisons are against the recent Gelberg et al. (2026) baselines (ScaleGMN+GradMetaNet++, DWS, GMN at k=1), where OCE consistently yields the largest gains. Notably, the improvements come from a simple architectural change (predicting k=8 parallel output MLPs and ensembling) without adding parameters to the backbone, validating the paper's theoretical prediction that output capacity is a key bottleneck for function-space operator tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the weight-space learning literature (DWS, NFN, GMN, NG-GNN, NFT) and the broader GDL expressivity analysis tradition (Maron et al. 2020, Dym & Maron 2020, Ravanbakhsh 2020). Prior expressivity results for weight-space networks were partial and task-specific—showing that DWS can simulate a forward pass (Navon et al. 2023), that GMN can express other models (Lim et al. 2023), or that ScaleGMN can simulate backpropagation (Kalogeropoulos et al. 2024)—but no comprehensive universality characterization existed. This paper unifies the field by proving all prominent equivariant architectures are expressively equivalent, then systematically characterizing universality across four approximation settings using a canonization-map technique borrowed from GDL, and identifying the precise GP conditions under which universality holds or fails.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The equivalence result means practitioners need not worry about architectural choice for expressivity reasons, and the GP framework provides a clean, checkable condition (pairwise distinct biases) under which universality is guaranteed—resolving a key open question in the field. The OCE modification (ensembling k output networks) is a simple, drop-in fix that yields a 34% improvement on the INR dilation benchmark, suggesting that output capacity, not architectural sophistication, is the binding constraint for function-space operator tasks. Open questions include extending the theory to optimization and generalization guarantees, designing architectures that map smaller input networks to larger output networks, and connecting the capacity-scaling results to the broader overparameterization literature.
