## Problem

Classical trust region bounds for policy gradient methods scale as O(T²) with sequence length, rendering them theoretically vacuous for modern LLM reasoning tasks where sequences exceed 4000 tokens. Because off-policy mismatch (π_roll ≠ π_θ) is unavoidable in LLM-RL pipelines due to backend discrepancies, MoE routing discontinuities, and distributed staleness, existing token-level interventions like PPO clipping cannot control the sequence-level divergence that drives training collapse, leaving no theoretical guarantee that optimization steps actually improve performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of (prompt, rollout sequence) pairs together with stored rollout logits from the inference engine (e.g., vLLM) and computes training policy logits via the training framework (e.g., FSDP) to obtain exact per-token KL divergences. It outputs a binary accept/reject mask per sequence and updated policy weights, with the critical design choice that gradient normalization uses the total batch size N rather than the count of accepted sequences, so rejected trajectories contribute exactly zero gradient. In memory-constrained settings where full logits are unavailable, sample-based estimators (k₂ = ½(log ρ)² for max-criterion, k₃ = ρ−1−log ρ for average-criterion) substitute for exact KL computation.

## Method

The paper derives two tighter approximation-error bounds for the standard surrogate objective: a Pinsker-Marginal bound scaling as O(T^{3/2}) and a Mixed bound scaling as O(T), both depending on D_KL^{tok,max}, the maximum token-level KL divergence across the sequence. A key impossibility result shows that D_KL^{tok,max} cannot be bounded by the sequence-level KL, proving that token-level clipping or masking is fundamentally insufficient to restore monotonic improvement guarantees. Trust Region Masking (TRM) addresses this by masking entire sequences where max_t D_KL(c_t) > δ, ensuring D_KL^{tok,max} ≤ δ for all accepted data and yielding the first non-vacuous monotonic improvement bounds for long-horizon LLM-RL. The threshold δ is length-invariant (no rescaling needed as T grows), and in practice a combined max-and-average criterion with complementary thresholds provides robustness against both extreme outliers and accumulated drift.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative contribution is a theoretical comparison of approximation error bounds for long-horizon LLM-RL (T=4096, D_KL^{tok,max}=10^{-4}, D_KL^{seq}=0.01): the classical TRPO bound yields a vacuous error of 1677.0, while the proposed Pinsker-Marginal bound reduces this to 35.0 (48× tighter) and the Mixed bound further to 8.2 (200× tighter). Empirically, on AIME25 (avg@32) with Qwen3-8B-Base under a vLLM/FSDP backend mismatch, standard PPO Clipping (0.8, 1.28) suffers rapid validation score collapse with an exploding PPL Gap, whereas both TRM-Max (δ=0.05) and TRM-Avg (δ=0.001) maintain stable training with bounded PPL Gap and consistent score improvement. Response lengths remain stable near 5,000 tokens across all methods, and TRM-Max incurs a masking rate below 30%.

| Bound | Formula | Scaling | Value (T=4096) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classical (TRPO) | T(T−1)·D_KL^{tok,max} | O(T²) | 1677.0 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TRM against a single baseline: standard PPO Clipping with ratios [0.8, 1.28] (following DAPO settings). On the AIME25 benchmark (avg@32, Qwen3-8B-Base, vLLM rollout + FSDP training), PPO Clipping fails to prevent training collapse: the validation score degrades rapidly as training progresses, accompanied by an unbounded explosion in the Log Absolute PPL Gap, confirming the theoretical prediction that token-level clipping permits gradient leakage from mismatched trajectories. Both TRM variants (TRM-Max with δ=0.05 and TRM-Avg with δ=0.001) decisively outperform PPO Clipping on training stability, maintaining a bounded PPL Gap and showing consistent AIME25 score improvement throughout training. The paper further demonstrates that individually relaxed thresholds (TRM-Max δ=0.1 or TRM-Avg δ=0.002) each fail to prevent collapse, but their combination (TRM-Max&Avg) restores stability, indicating the two criteria are complementary: the max constraint catches extreme per-token outliers while the average constraint limits accumulated drift. The trade-off is a 'rejection tax': TRM-Max masks up to ~30% of sequences, reducing raw token throughput, but this is framed as a necessary safety filter that PPO Clipping cannot provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical trust region framework (CPI/TRPO) to the autoregressive LLM setting, where prior bounds scale as O(T²) and become vacuous for sequences exceeding a few thousand tokens. It builds directly on the recent recognition that off-policy mismatch (π_roll ≠ π_θ) is unavoidable in modern LLM-RL pipelines due to backend discrepancies, MoE routing, and distributed staleness (Liu et al. 2025; Yao et al. 2025; Qi et al. 2025), but departs from those works by treating the mismatch as a theoretical constraint rather than an engineering bug to be eliminated. The key conceptual departure is from token-level interventions (PPO clipping, token masking), which the paper proves are structurally insufficient: the approximation error depends on the maximum token-level KL across the entire sequence, a quantity no per-token operation can control. TRM thus introduces sequence-level rejection as the minimal intervention that restores the preconditions for monotonic improvement guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(T²) → O(T) bound tightening and the proof that token-level clipping is provably insufficient (not merely suboptimal) for long-horizon LLM-RL challenge a core assumption in virtually all current LLM-RL systems. TRM offers a simple, drop-in mechanism that could be adopted by existing PPO/GRPO pipelines, and the length-invariant threshold property makes it practical as sequence lengths continue to grow. However, significant limitations remain: exact KL computation is memory-prohibitive for 70B+ models, the sample-based estimators (k₂/k₃) do not inherit the formal guarantees, empirical validation covers only backend discrepancies (not MoE or staleness) on a single 8B model and single math benchmark, and the 'rejection tax' on sample efficiency is non-trivial. Open questions include whether soft masking via importance weighting can recover the rejected samples, whether adaptive thresholding can optimize the stability-throughput trade-off, and whether the framework extends to multi-turn agentic settings where the trust region must be maintained across turns.
