## Problem

Existing MT evaluation paradigms—regression-based scalar scores, error-based diagnostics, and holistic pairwise rankings—either lack fine-grained interpretability or fail to align with human comparative judgments in reference-free settings. No benchmark exists for directly meta-evaluating criterion-conditioned, reference-free pairwise MT evaluation on modern system outputs, leaving a gap in both methodology and validation infrastructure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261235/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

FiRE takes a source sentence, two translation candidates (y₁, y₂), and a user-specified evaluation criterion as input, and produces a three-way pairwise judgment per criterion (A: y₁ preferred, B: y₂ preferred, E: tie), which are then aggregated into a single overall pairwise decision. The accompanying benchmark provides 1,600 source sentences per direction (EN→ZH, RU→ZH), each paired with two of six MT systems' outputs, annotated by three human raters on faithfulness, fluency, consistency of style, and overall quality without reference translations, yielding 12,800 criterion-level annotations with Fleiss' κ ranging from 0.57 to 0.81.

## Method

FiRE prompts an off-the-shelf LLM separately for each of three criteria (faithfulness, fluency, consistency of style), instructing it to compare the two translations along that specific dimension and output A, B, or E. The three criterion-level judgments are synthesized into an overall decision via a majority-count rule: whichever translation is favored by more criteria wins; ties are broken lexicographically by criterion order (faithfulness → fluency → style), and if all three are ties the overall is E. This criterion-aware aggregation is the key differentiator from a direct holistic prompt, as it leverages complementary evidence across dimensions and smooths near-tie noise. The framework is backbone-agnostic (validated across seven LLMs including DeepSeek-R1, QwQ-32B, GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Gemini-2.0-Flash, Mistral-Large-Instruct, Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct) and extensible: FiREplus adds a fourth criterion (locale convention) without altering the comparison-and-aggregation pipeline. System-level rankings are derived via normalized Copeland scoring over all pairwise comparisons, accommodating unbalanced comparison graphs.

## Results

On the overall pairwise ranking task, DeepSeek-R1-FiRE achieves 65.3% agreement with human annotations on EN→ZH and 70.1% on RU→ZH, outperforming the strongest regression baseline (MetricX-24-XXL: 61.6%/67.1%) and the dedicated ranking model MT-Ranker-XXL (60.7%/61.6%). At the criterion level (Table 2), FiRE attains 64.8–72.5% agreement on faithfulness, 68.7–77.9% on fluency, and 61.4–66.3% on consistency of style, while error-based methods (M-MAD, GEMBA-MQM) drop to single-digit or low-20s agreement on fluency and style. On external MQM datasets (HE→EN, JA→ZH, EN→DE), QwQ-FiRE consistently outperforms error-based baselines on criterion-level judgments and achieves the best overall agreement on HE→EN (69.6%) and JA→ZH (72.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FiRE (DeepSeek-R1) outperforms all six baseline families on the overall pairwise task. Against regression-based metrics, FiRE beats the strongest scalar metric MetricX-24-XXL by 3.7 points on EN→ZH (65.3 vs 61.6) and 3.0 points on RU→ZH (70.1 vs 67.1), and surpasses COMET-Kiwi-XXL by 3.9/8.9 points respectively. Against error-based methods, the gap is dramatic: M-MAD reaches only 43.6%/51.9% and GEMBA-MQM only 41.5%/37.6% overall, while FiRE achieves 65.3%/70.1%, a margin of over 20 points. Against the dedicated ranking model MT-Ranker-XXL (60.7%/61.6%), FiRE gains 4.6/8.5 points, and even the direct holistic LLM prompt (DeepSeek-R1-Direct-Rank: 64.3%/66.7%) is outperformed by the criterion-aware aggregation, confirming that fine-grained synthesis adds value. On external MQM datasets, QwQ-FiRE extends these advantages: it leads on faithfulness (70.8–71.0%) and overall (69.6–72.4%) for HE→EN and JA→ZH, while on EN→DE it matches the best scalar metric (66.3 vs 67.9 for XCOMET-XXL) while uniquely providing criterion-level diagnostics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the existing pairwise ranking paradigm for MT evaluation (exemplified by MT-Ranker and WMT relative-ranking tasks) by making the comparison criterion-conditioned: rather than eliciting a single holistic preference, FiRE decomposes the judgment into faithfulness, fluency, and style-consistency sub-comparisons before aggregating. It repurposes the LLM-as-judge approach already used in error-based evaluators (GEMBA-MQM, M-MAD) but redirects it from error detection toward direct comparative judgment, and maps MQM error taxonomies onto its three criteria for cross-paradigm comparison. The authors explicitly position their contribution not as a new evaluation paradigm but as a new formulation within the ranking paradigm, supported by the first human-annotated, reference-free, criterion-conditioned pairwise benchmark for modern MT outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a genuine gap: prior pairwise-ranking datasets relied on scalar-score inference, contrastive perturbations, or outdated WMT relative rankings, none of which directly annotate reference-free criterion-level preferences for contemporary MT systems. The finding that LLMs achieve only 1–7% agreement on human-judged tied cases (especially fluency and style) reveals a fundamental calibration gap that no amount of scale closes, pointing to an open problem in teaching evaluators to recognize equivalence. Practical limitations include sentence-level scope, quadratic cost of all-pairs comparison, and persistent position/self-enhancement biases, while the FiREplus extension and Copeland-based system ranking suggest a path toward scalable, task-adaptive evaluation pipelines.
