## Problem

Variational multi-view clustering suffers from two coupled failures: view heterogeneity and noise make cross-view fusion unreliable (view-level weighting alone does not guarantee instance-level alignment), and the resulting unreliable fused representations amplify posterior ambiguity near cluster boundaries, causing misassigned samples. Existing methods either fuse views without explicit instance-level consistency enforcement or treat boundary samples with crude confidence thresholds that either inject erroneous pseudo-labels or discard informative hard cases.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multi-view dataset where each of N samples is described by V views of potentially different dimensionalities (e.g., 5 RGB image views for Caltech7-5v, 3 feature-vector views for Prokaryotic, or 3 pixel-based views for HandWritten digits). It outputs a discrete cluster label per sample (argmax over the variational posterior q(c|x)) and a shared D-dimensional continuous latent representation z that is used both for reconstruction of all views and for the contrastive objectives. No ground-truth labels are used during training; the number of clusters K is a user-specified hyperparameter.

## Method

DCL-MVC is a VAE with a Gaussian-mixture prior over a single shared latent variable z, augmented by two contrastive stages. At the fusion stage, view-specific encoder outputs are fused via a self-attention module that computes global view-importance weights (averaging attention rows), and an instance-level InfoNCE loss with MMD-based view reweighting enforces cross-view alignment between each view's projected representation and the fused representation. At the representation stage, boundary samples are identified by a top-1/top-2 posterior margin threshold; a cluster-center repulsive loss pushes GMM means apart to enlarge inter-cluster margins, while a prototype contrastive loss (soft-target cross-entropy between sample representations and cluster prototypes) uses a confidence-aware curriculum that transitions from soft supervision for all samples, to hard labels for confident samples with soft labels retained for boundary samples, and finally to near-fully hard supervision as posteriors sharpen. The total objective is the ELBO plus weighted instance-level contrastive and boundary-aware contrastive terms, optimized end-to-end.

## Results

DCL-MVC achieves the best performance across all six datasets and all four metrics (ACC, NMI, ARI, PUR). On the largest dataset Cifar10 (50,000 samples), it reaches 98.23% ACC and 96.13% ARI, surpassing the strongest baseline DIMC (96.91% ACC, 93.31% ARI). The most dramatic gains appear on Prokaryotic, where DCL-MVC outperforms the best baseline (MVP) by 11.69% in ACC (72.96% vs. 61.27%) and 12.90% in ARI (45.68% vs. 32.78%), and on HandWritten by 6.46% in ACC (90.35% vs. 83.89%). On MSRC-V1, DCL-MVC achieves 90.95% ACC and 80.26% ARI, edging out DCMVC (89.52% ACC, 77.57% ARI).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCL-MVC outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest competitor DCMVC (Cui et al., 2024), DCL-MVC gains 2.97% ACC on Cifar10 (98.23 vs. 95.26), 13.10% ACC on HandWritten (90.35 vs. 77.25), and 1.43% ACC on MSRC-V1 (90.95 vs. 89.52). MVP (Gao & Pu, 2025) is the closest baseline on HandWritten (83.89% ACC) and Prokaryotic (61.27% ACC), yet DCL-MVC still leads by 6.46% and 11.69% respectively. DIMC (Xu et al., 2022) is competitive on Cifar10 (96.91% ACC) but trails DCL-MVC by 1.32% ACC and 2.82% ARI. DVIMVC (Xu et al., 2024) and DCMVSC (Yu et al., 2024) are consistently outperformed by 3–13% in ACC across datasets. Multi-VAE (Xu et al., 2021) is the weakest baseline, lagging DCL-MVC by 28% ACC on Cifar10 and 38.71% on MSRC-V1, highlighting the benefit of the proposed dual-stage contrastive design over purely disentangled VAE approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VAE-based multi-view clustering lineage initiated by VaDE (Jiang et al., 2016) and Multi-VAE (Xu et al., 2021), which established the GMM-prior generative framework and view-specific encoder architecture. It departs from prior fusion strategies (concatenation, late fusion, per-view attention) by introducing a fusion-then-self-attention mechanism that computes global view-importance weights in a shared latent space, and by adding two contrastive objectives: instance-level InfoNCE alignment with MMD-based view reweighting at the fusion stage, and a boundary-aware cluster-center/prototype contrastive loss with a confidence-aware soft-to-hard curriculum at the representation stage. The dual-stage decomposition—addressing fusion reliability and posterior ambiguity as separate problems—is the key architectural departure from prior single-objective VAE-MVC methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled decomposition of two under-addressed failure modes in VAE-based MVC: unreliable cross-view fusion under heterogeneity and posterior overlap at cluster boundaries. The confidence-aware curriculum for boundary samples offers a practical mechanism that avoids both the over-confidence of hard pseudo-labels and the information loss of discarding hard examples. However, performance degrades noticeably under 50% view missingness, and the single-sample Monte Carlo posterior approximation, while stable in practice, limits the method's applicability to incomplete multi-view settings. Open questions include whether the boundary-aware curriculum generalizes to larger-scale or high-dimensional latent spaces, and whether the MMD-based view reweighting remains effective when views are highly asymmetric in informativeness.
