## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary navigation frameworks discard full semantic distributions in favor of single deterministic labels when constructing scene graphs, causing irreversible information loss, illogical spatial layouts, and overconfident decisions that lead to false-positive goal confirmations (e.g., mistaking a sofa for a bed). The paper addresses how to preserve and exploit perception uncertainty throughout the mapping-to-planning pipeline to achieve globally better navigation decisions under semantic ambiguity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260476/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives a posed RGB-D observation (color image, depth image, and 2D pose from odometry) plus a free-form text goal description (e.g., "blue sofa"). It outputs a discrete action from {MOVE_FORWARD, TURN_LEFT, TURN_RIGHT, LOOK_UP, LOOK_DOWN, STOP} within a 500-step budget on an 800×800 occupancy map, and maintains a persistent 3D Probabilistic Scene Graph across episodes. Goal confirmation requires the calibrated detection score to exceed a threshold (δ=0.61) before STOP is executed, and the agent must be within 1.0 m of a visible goal instance.

## Method

PSG-Nav builds a hierarchical 3D Probabilistic Scene Graph (object→group→room) where every node retains a full categorical distribution updated via discrete vote accumulation rather than hard labels. The Multiverse Decision module samples K=3 deterministic worlds from this joint distribution using Monte Carlo sampling with LLM-guided logical pruning at group and room levels to enforce semantic coherence, then selects the optimal landmark by performing stochastic pairwise comparisons across all sampled worlds and marginalizing the resulting preference scores. An Evidential Experience Calibrator (EEC) provides a RAG-based stopping verifier: it maintains positive and negative memory banks storing composite embeddings of visual appearance plus room-level and group-level semantic distributions, retrieves nearest matches via cosine similarity and Jensen-Shannon Divergence, and linearly adjusts the raw detection confidence to suppress false positives before allowing episode termination.

## Results

PSG-Nav achieves state-of-the-art Success Rates of 66.1% (HM3D), 44.8% (MP3D), and 67.9% (HSSD), with corresponding SPL scores of 32.1%, 17.9%, and 33.4%. The zero-shot variant (without EEC) still reaches 63.5%, 43.3%, and 66.1% SR, outperforming most recent baselines without any online adaptation. On the HM3D-OVON benchmark (larger open-vocabulary category space), PSG-Nav achieves 46.4% SR training-free, surpassing the best training-free baseline (TANGO, 35.5%) by over 10 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSG-Nav outperforms all 25 compared baselines across all three primary benchmarks. On HM3D, it surpasses the closest competitor ASCENT (65.4%) by +0.7% SR, BeliefMapNav (61.4%) by +4.7%, and ApexNav (59.6%) by +6.5%; the margin over the deterministic scene-graph baseline SG-Nav (54.0%) reaches +12.1%. On MP3D, PSG-Nav leads ASCENT (44.5%) by +0.3% and BeliefMapNav (37.3%) by +7.5%. On HSSD, it beats BeliefMapNav (65.2%) by +2.7% and ImagineNav (51.0%) by +16.9%. Notably, even the strict zero-shot variant (w/o EEC) at 63.5% SR on HM3D exceeds BeliefMapNav and ApexNav, indicating that the probabilistic scene graph and multiverse decision framework alone provide substantial gains over deterministic approaches. The only baseline that approaches PSG-Nav on HM3D is ASCENT, which benefits from explicit multi-floor/stair-aware exploration—a capability PSG-Nav currently lacks due to its 2D occupancy map limitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deterministic 3D Scene Graph navigation paradigm (SG-Nav, ASCENT, BeliefMapNav) by replacing single-label assignments with full categorical distributions at every node, forming a hierarchical 3D Probabilistic Scene Graph. The Multiverse Decision module adapts the LLM-based decision-making-under-uncertainty framework from DellMA (Liu et al., 2025) into a navigation context via Monte Carlo world sampling and stochastic pairwise comparisons. The Evidential Experience Calibrator repurposes Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Lewis et al., 2020) as an online confidence-calibration mechanism, storing visual-structural context descriptors rather than text passages. The key conceptual departure from prior work is treating the scene graph as a joint distribution to be sampled from, rather than a fixed map to be queried, thereby preserving alternative semantic hypotheses that deterministic methods irreversibly discard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and persistent failure mode in embodied navigation—premature termination at visually similar but semantically wrong objects—by combining probabilistic scene representation with experience-based calibration, achieving strong SOTA results (66.1% SR on HM3D) in a fully training-free setting. The ablation revealing that removing the Group-level factorization collapses probabilistic performance to near-deterministic levels (58.8% vs. 58.4% SR) validates the hierarchical architecture as essential rather than decorative. Key limitations remain: reliance on 2D occupancy maps precludes multi-floor navigation (accounting for ~40% of failures), LLM-based pairwise comparisons introduce latency in large environments, and the EEC's online adaptation provides only marginal gains over a well-initialized static memory (66.1% vs. 66.0%), raising questions about whether true lifelong learning is occurring or whether a small offline calibration set suffices.
