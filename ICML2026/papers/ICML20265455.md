## Problem

When aligning a target model with multiple source MLLMs, the reasoning distributions of those sources are non-stationary: they diverge, conflict, and drift asynchronously as chain-of-thought trajectories unfold. Existing multi-source alignment methods treat this inter-model divergence as noise to be averaged or as a static ensemble disagreement, causing the target to internalize contradictory biases and hallucinations rather than synthesizing robust reasoning. The paper asks how to autonomously convert drifting inter-model conflicts into actionable negative constraints for decision-boundary sharpening without ground-truth supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes chest X-ray images paired with a text prompt, plus the full set of chain-of-thought reasoning trajectories generated by seven source MLLMs (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5, Claude Sonnet-4, Qwen-VL-Max, Grok-4, GLM-4.5V, Moonshot-v1) for the same instance. Training uses only 10% of MIMIC-CXR (≈37k images) and relies solely on classification labels rather than radiologist free-text reports. Outputs include 14-label thoracic disease classification, free-text diagnostic reports, and—cross-domain—driving action explanations on BDD-X.

## Method

APO (Autonomous Preference Optimization) is a two-stage, label-free protocol. Stage 1 (Supervised Bootstrapping with Consensus Synthesis) minimizes the sum of KL divergences between the target and all source predictive distributions to project the target into the capability union, then conditions the bootstrapped model on the concatenated multi-source trajectories to extract a single self-consistent consensus trajectory t⁺ via in-context aggregation. Stage 2 (Constraint-Aware Optimization) reformulates alignment as a multi-negative Plackett-Luce preference problem: the consensus t⁺ is the preferred response, while all N raw drifting source trajectories τᵘ are simultaneously suppressed as negative constraints, yielding a log-likelihood objective that generalizes DPO's pairwise Bradley-Terry loss to an N-negative ranking. Minimizing this objective forces the target to increase consensus likelihood relative to the bootstrapped reference while actively decreasing probability mass in the drifting regions, effectively carving a robust consensus manifold without any external ground-truth annotation.

## Results

On the MS-CXR-T chest X-ray classification benchmark, APO achieves an average Top-1 accuracy of 0.78 using only 10% of training data and no radiologist reports, outperforming the best full-data baseline CoCa-CXR (0.69) by 9 percentage points. The 7B target model surpasses all seven proprietary source MLLMs (GPT-5 at 0.75, GLM-4.5V at 0.69, Qwen-VL-Max at 0.64, etc.) in average accuracy. On MIMIC-CXR report generation, APO reaches BLEU-4 of 0.19 and METEOR of 0.21, leading all baselines on BLEU metrics. Zero-shot AUC scores of 0.85 (Open-I) and 0.83 (ChestXray14) confirm cross-domain generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APO decisively outperforms the strongest full-data baseline CoCa-CXR (0.69 avg) by 9 points (0.78 avg) on MS-CXR-T, with particularly large margins on Consolidation (+0.14) and Pneumothorax (+0.23), though it trails by 0.03 on Pleural Effusion and 0.07 on Edema. Against the seven proprietary source MLLMs, the 7B target model achieves the highest average (0.78), beating GPT-5 (0.75) and GLM-4.5V (0.69) while avoiding the catastrophic per-disease collapses seen in individual sources (e.g., Sonnet-4 drops to 0.15 on Edema, Moonshot to 0.13 on Consolidation). On report generation, APO leads all baselines on BLEU-1 through BLEU-4 (0.56/0.37/0.27/0.19 vs. CPO's 0.43/0.29/0.19/0.16) but trails CPO on ROUGE-L (0.30 vs. 0.42) and METEOR (0.21 vs. 0.29). In zero-shot classification, APO edges out CARZero and CPO on Open-I (0.85 vs. 0.84) and ChestXray14 (0.83 vs. 0.81–0.82) while tying on ChestXpert (0.92). Among reasoning-alignment distillation methods, APO surpasses DistiLLM-2 and ABKD (both 0.75 avg) by 3 points and MiniLLM (0.73) by 5 points. On the BDD-X driving benchmark, APO outperforms Dolphins (BLEU-4 16.5 vs. 15.0, CIDEr 140.2 vs. 134.2) and MCAM (CIDEr 140.2 vs. 94.1) across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO's pairwise Bradley-Terry preference formulation to a multi-negative Plackett-Luce objective, repurposing the implicit reward structure to simultaneously suppress N drifting source trajectories rather than a single dispreferred sample. Conceptually, it departs from standard multi-teacher knowledge distillation (f-Distill, GKD, MiniLLM, DistiLLM-2, ABKD) by importing concept drift theory from the streaming-data literature and reframing inter-model divergence as dynamic negative constraints rather than noise to be averaged. The autonomous preference construction—synthesizing a consensus trajectory via in-context extraction and using raw source outputs as dispreferred constraints—eliminates the ground-truth supervision that both classical distillation and standard DPO require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CXR-MAX benchmark (170k+ trajectories from 7 MLLMs with fine-grained alignment annotations) fills a genuine gap for studying multi-source reasoning dynamics in high-stakes domains, and the Plackett-Luce multi-negative formulation offers a clean, generalizable template for any setting where multiple reference models disagree. However, the primary evaluation is confined to chest X-ray interpretation, and the cross-domain driving experiment, while encouraging, uses a single dataset and a small set of baselines. Open questions include whether the constraint-satisfaction framing scales to open-ended generation tasks with less structured disagreement, whether the computational overhead of querying 7 proprietary MLLMs per training sample is practical outside research settings, and whether the conservative bias APO introduces on high-drift classes (e.g., edema) is acceptable in clinical deployment.
