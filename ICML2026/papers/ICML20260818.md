## Problem

Existing PU learning methods are designed and evaluated under settings with sufficient labeled positives, but when the number of labeled positives drops to extreme scarcity (e.g., 10–20 samples), the positive term in the risk estimator becomes the dominant source of estimation instability. This paper identifies that the instability is governed by two distinct components of the generalization bound—the Rademacher complexity of the hypothesis class and the uniform range bound of the positive term—and formalizes this through a 'sufficient sample threshold' that quantifies the minimum number of positives needed for reliable learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a very small set of labeled positive RGB images (n_P ∈ {10, 20, 50, 100}) and a large unlabeled image set (e.g., 50,000 for CIFAR-10, 9,469 for ImageNette at 224×224), with the class prior π assumed known. It outputs a binary class label per image. The key constraint is that the positive set is drawn under the SCAR assumption from the positive class distribution, and the method operates on multi-class datasets partitioned into positive/negative groups (e.g., CIFAR-10 split into 4-class positive vs. 6-class negative).

## Method

ScalePU builds on the uPU risk estimator and adds two complementary regularizations that each target a different component of the generalization bound. Variance regularization (Ω_var) penalizes the empirical variance of g_f(x) = ℓ(f(x)) − ℓ(−f(x)) over positive samples, restricting the hypothesis space to a sub-class H_τ with reduced Rademacher complexity parameter κ_τ ≤ κ. Geometric regularization (Ω_compact + Ω_sep) encourages positive representations h(x) to cluster tightly around an empirical prototype c_P (reducing the effective range from 2B to 2L_g·ρ via the Lipschitz property), while an adaptive separation term with Gaussian-kernel-weighted margins pushes unlabeled examples away from the positive cluster. The theoretical contribution is a closed-form sufficient sample threshold P* = (8πLκ_τ + 2πL_gρ√(2ln(2/δ)))² / (ε − Ξ_U)², showing that both mechanisms jointly lower the threshold below the standard PU bound. In practice, the classifier uses weight decay to control the Lipschitz constant L_g = 2L‖w‖₂, and the full objective is optimized end-to-end with Adam over CNN or ResNet-50 backbones.

## Results

ScalePU achieves the highest classification accuracy across most of eight PU datasets, with the largest gains under extreme label scarcity. On CIFAR-10-1 with only 10 positive samples, ScalePU reaches 67.5% accuracy, outperforming the second-best method (LaGAM at 67.3%) and nnPU by 3.6 percentage points. On the large-scale ImageNette dataset at n_P=100, ScalePU attains 59.8% accuracy, a 4.7-point absolute improvement over LaGAM (55.1%). Pseudo-labeling baselines (VPU, P³Mix) collapse to near-random performance (~50–52%) at n_P=10 on most datasets, while ScalePU maintains stable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScalePU consistently outperforms all nine baselines at n_P=10 across the six standard benchmarks, with the largest margins over pseudo-labeling methods (VPU, P³Mix, HolisticPU) that degrade to near-random accuracy under extreme scarcity. Among disambiguation-free estimators, ScalePU exceeds nnPU by 3.6 points on CIFAR-10-1 (67.5% vs. 63.9%) and abs-PU by 1.9 points on F-MNIST-1 (77.5% vs. 75.6%) at n_P=10. LaGAM is the strongest competitor, achieving comparable results to ScalePU at n_P=100 (e.g., 68.9% vs. 78.9% on CIFAR-10-1, where ScalePU still leads by 10 points) but trailing by 0.2–2.4 points at n_P=10. On the large-scale ImageNette dataset, ScalePU leads LaGAM by 4.7 points at n_P=100 (59.8% vs. 55.1%) and by 0.9 points at n_P=10 (54.1% vs. 53.2%), while VPU and P³Mix collapse to ~51–52%. The ablation variant PUVR (variance regularization only) already surpasses most baselines, confirming that Rademacher complexity control is the primary driver, with geometric regularization providing additional gains especially on high-resolution datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PU learning literature (uPU, nnPU, absPU, DistPU) and pseudo-labeling methods (VPU, P³Mix, LaGAM) by identifying a specific failure regime—extreme positive-label scarcity—that prior methods do not explicitly address. The theoretical analysis draws on McDiarmid's inequality and Rademacher complexity bounds (Bartlett & Mendelson, 2002) to decompose the generalization gap into a complexity term and a concentration term, then applies the SRM framework (Cherkassky, 1997) for variance regularization and manifold regularization ideas (Belkin et al., 2006) for geometric compactness. The key departure from prior work is the introduction of the 'sufficient sample threshold' as a formal metric and the dual-regularization design that simultaneously targets both components of the bound, rather than improving risk estimation or pseudo-label quality as existing methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sufficient sample threshold provides a principled diagnostic for when PU learning becomes statistically unreliable, which is practically important in domains like medical imaging where positive labels are genuinely scarce. The dual regularization framework (variance + geometric) offers a complementary mechanism to pseudo-labeling, which the experiments show collapses under extreme scarcity. However, the approach still relies on the SCAR assumption, the theoretical bounds are loose (involving Rademacher complexity of the full hypothesis class), and the geometric regularization introduces an additional feature mapping h with multiple hyperparameters (λ, γ, β, m, σ) that complicate practical deployment. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-SCAR settings, whether the threshold can be tightened with sharper concentration inequalities, and whether the geometric compactness assumption holds for highly heterogeneous positive classes.
