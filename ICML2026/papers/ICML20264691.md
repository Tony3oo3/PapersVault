## Problem

Existing deep multi-view clustering methods embed all views into flat Euclidean space, which cannot accommodate the varying uncertainty and quality differences across views. Moreover, standard symmetric contrastive alignment forces high-quality representations to drift toward corrupted ones, degrading cluster structure. The paper addresses both the geometric mismatch and the rigid alignment problem simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264691/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N samples each with M views of potentially different dimensions D_v, processed by view-specific fully connected autoencoders. It also handles incomplete multi-view settings where a fraction η of samples have one view randomly removed. The Poincaré ball embedding dimension d is tuned per dataset from {64, 128, 256}. Output is a set of K cluster assignments produced by Sinkhorn-Knopp-optimized global pseudo-labels, with cluster prototypes maintained in tangent space and projected to the Poincaré ball for distance computation.

## Method

HAMC embeds view features into the Poincaré ball via a hyperbolic autoencoder (tangent-space encoder → exponential map projection), using radial norm as a confidence proxy so that high-confidence samples occupy larger radial distances while noisy samples remain near the origin. An asymmetric view alignment mechanism computes instance-level prediction entropy per view and applies a stop-gated directional loss: only when source view u has lower entropy than target view v does the alignment pull v toward u, preventing noise propagation. A consensus-aware clustering module fuses multi-view predictions with entropy-weighted logits, applies Sinkhorn-Knopp for balanced global pseudo-labels, and screens out the bottom (1−p) most uncertain samples before computing the hyperbolic cross-entropy clustering loss and momentum-based prototype updates in tangent space. Training proceeds in two stages: a warm-up phase with reconstruction and symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss, followed by joint end-to-end optimization of reconstruction, asymmetric alignment, and consensus clustering losses.

## Results

On the complete-view setting, HAMC achieves the best ACC on 5 of 6 datasets: 70.7% on Caltech-101, 87.5% on CUB, 30.9% on LandUse-21, 99.4% on NoisyMNIST, and 61.2% on Reuters, with NMI reaching 84.7, 81.5, 36.8, 98.0, and 44.2 respectively. Under the challenging 50% missing-view setting, HAMC retains top performance on 5 of 6 datasets (e.g., 82.8% ACC on CUB, 57.6% on Reuters), trailing CANDY by only 0.4% ACC on Caltech-101. The method also leads on ARI across nearly all settings, reaching 75.2 on CUB and 98.6 on NoisyMNIST in the complete-view case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAMC outperforms all 13 baselines on CUB (87.5% vs. 83.0% ICMVC, 81.4% CAMERA, 80.8% CANDY) and Reuters (61.2% vs. 57.7% CANDY, 55.0% CAMERA) in the complete-view setting, with margins of 4.5 and 3.5 ACC points over the strongest competitors. On Caltech-101, HAMC leads by 3.4 ACC over CANDY (70.7 vs. 67.3) and 5.1 over CAMERA. On NoisyMNIST, CAMERA edges out HAMC by 0.5 ACC (99.9 vs. 99.4), but HAMC still achieves the best NMI (98.0 vs. 99.6 CAMERA) and ARI (98.6 vs. 99.7 CAMERA) margins are minimal. Under 50% missing views, HAMC trails CANDY by only 0.4 ACC on Caltech-101 (69.1 vs. 69.5) while decisively winning on CUB (82.8 vs. 74.1 CAMERA) and Reuters (57.6 vs. 54.4 CAMERA). The ablation in Table 2 confirms that removing the hyperbolic geometry causes the largest drop (e.g., Caltech-101 ARI falls from 69.7 to 40.1), while removing the confidence mask is most damaging on Caltech-101 (ACC drops to 52.9), validating each component's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends deep multi-view clustering by replacing the standard Euclidean embedding with the Poincaré ball model (Nickel & Kiela, 2017), leveraging its exponential volume growth to better accommodate varying sample uncertainty. It departs from the dominant symmetric contrastive alignment paradigm (as in Trosten et al., 2021; Lin et al., 2023b) by introducing an instance-level asymmetric mechanism where high-confidence views unidirectionally guide low-confidence ones, building on the entropy-based uncertainty estimation from Sinkhorn-Knopp normalized assignments (Caron et al., 2020). The tangent-space decoding strategy and momentum-based prototype updates draw from practical hyperbolic optimization techniques (Ganea et al., 2018; Khrulkov et al., 2020), while the overall autoencoder + contrastive + clustering pipeline follows the established deep MVC architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asymmetric alignment mechanism addresses a practically important failure mode in multi-view systems where one modality is degraded (e.g., sensor noise, missing text), and the instance-level granularity is a meaningful step beyond static view-level weighting. However, the method introduces considerable complexity (multiple temperature parameters, screening ratio, two-stage training) and is validated only on two-view benchmarks with fixed curvature (-1); whether the radial-confidence proxy generalizes to higher-dimensional or multi-view (>2) settings with adaptive curvature remains open. The authors themselves flag adaptive curvature learning as future work, and the modest margins on some datasets (e.g., LandUse-21) suggest the geometric advantage may be dataset-dependent.
