## Problem

Silent data corruption (SDC) errors during gradient aggregation in distributed data-parallel training cause workers to receive different noisy averaged gradients, leading to accumulated model divergence and failed convergence. Unlike explicit system failures that interrupt training, these silent errors go undetected and are increasingly the dominant cause of convergence issues in large-scale LLM pretraining (e.g., LLaMA-3, Fire-Flyer clusters), yet lack a principled formulation or mitigation mechanism.

## Input / Output

PAFT operates on the data-parallel dimension of hybrid-parallel training, where TP/PP-partitioned model instances are replicated across workers, and requires full parameter replicas (incompatible with FSDP/ZeRO sharding). It takes standard DDP inputs—model parameters, per-worker training data, worker count M, and a learning rate schedule—and produces a trained model with all replicas synchronized. The synchronization frequency H is not fixed but dynamically adjusted per-iteration based on the observed signal-to-noise ratio (gradient norm divided by estimated noise magnitude), and the parameter averaging is overlapped with forward/backward propagation via asynchronous communication to hide its cost.

## Method

PAFT consists of two components. PAFT-Sync periodically averages model parameters across all workers every H iterations, resetting the accumulated model divergence that Lemma 3.1 shows grows as O(Ση²σ²) without intervention; Theorem 4.3 proves this yields a convergence bound matching standard SGD with an additional O(Hσ²) term. PAFT-Dyn reduces overhead by (1) dynamically setting H proportional to the signal-to-noise ratio (‖g‖/σ_est) so that synchronization frequency adapts to the actual error degree, and (2) overlapping the parameter all-reduce with the next iteration's forward and backward passes using asynchronous communication, trading a one-step gradient staleness for hidden communication latency. The system is implemented on PyTorch Distributed and extended to DeepSpeed and Megatron, supporting SGD, SGD-momentum, and Adam (with synchronization of momentum/preconditioner states).

## Results

PAFT successfully mitigates gradient aggregation errors, nearly matching noise-free (Oracle) convergence for small noise degrees (σ²=0.0001 and 0.001) across ResNet-18/CIFAR-10, ResNet-50/CIFAR-100, GPT-2/OpenWebText, and LLaMA-2/Alpaca on 4–32 GPUs. For larger noise (σ²≥0.01), PAFT still improves convergence over noised training but retains a gap versus Oracle, as common-mode corruption cannot be fully corrected by parameter synchronization alone. In terms of overhead, PAFT adds approximately 18.9% wall-clock time over standard DSGD at 32 workers, while reducing the synchronization overhead of PAFT-Sync by up to 11.0% through dynamic frequency adjustment and asynchronous overlap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four configurations: Oracle (noise-free DSGD), Noised (DSGD with injected gradient inconsistency), PAFT-Sync (periodic parameter synchronization at fixed frequency H), and PAFT (full system with dynamic frequency and async overlap). PAFT consistently outperforms Noised training across all noise degrees and model sizes, recovering convergence that would otherwise fail. For small noise (σ²=0.0001, 0.001), PAFT nearly matches Oracle accuracy, while for larger noise (σ²=0.01, 0.1) it still improves over Noised but cannot fully close the gap to Oracle due to common-mode corruption. PAFT is faster than PAFT-Sync by up to 11.0% at 32 workers (0.373s vs 0.411s per iteration), demonstrating the benefit of dynamic frequency scheduling and asynchronous communication overlap. The wall-clock overhead of PAFT over vanilla DSGD is modest (~18.9% at 32 workers), and PAFT-Sync shows better relative scalability as worker count increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a specific sub-problem within the well-established fault-tolerant distributed training literature: silent, unintentional gradient aggregation (GA) errors caused by hardware bit-flips or communication noise, as distinct from active node failures (addressed by checkpointing/elastic recovery) or adversarial Byzantine attacks (addressed by robust aggregation). The core methodological idea—periodic parameter synchronization to reset accumulated replica drift—repurposes the local-SGD/FedAvg synchronization mechanism (Lin et al., 2018) for a fault-tolerance purpose rather than a communication-reduction purpose, and the dynamic frequency scheduling (PAFT-Dyn) draws on signal-to-noise-ratio-based hyperparameter adaptation. The theoretical contribution (formalizing 'gradient inconsistency' and proving unbounded divergence without synchronization) is new; no prior work had characterized how per-worker Gaussian noise on the aggregated gradient leads to O(T) accumulated divergence under constant learning rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With SDC errors now reported as the dominant cause of LLM convergence failures in production clusters (LLaMA-3 405B, Fire-Flyer HPC), this paper provides both the first clean theoretical characterization of why even tiny per-iteration GA noise destroys convergence and a practical, low-overhead (~19%) system fix deployable on PyTorch Distributed, DeepSpeed, and Megatron. Key limitations remain: PAFT cannot correct common-mode corruption (all workers receive the same wrong gradient), is incompatible with FSDP/ZeRO sharding, and all experiments use simulated Gaussian noise on modest-scale models (≤32 GPUs, ≤124M params) rather than real fault traces. Open questions include validating under production fault logs, extending to sharded training, and combining PAFT with Byzantine-style voting for common-mode errors.
