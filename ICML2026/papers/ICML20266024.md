## Problem

Transparent objects violate the Lambertian diffuse reflection assumption underlying mainstream depth estimation and 3D reconstruction models, causing severe geometric distortion and 'penetration' artifacts. Existing remedies—depth completion networks and per-scene NeRF optimization—are either task-specific, require costly ground-truth depth from specialized hardware, or demand multi-view inputs with lengthy training, creating a 'perception silo' that forces practitioners to abandon versatile SOTA models for narrow alternatives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266024/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GHOST takes a single 256×256 RGB image as input and produces a texture-rich opaque RGB image as its primary output, designed to be a drop-in replacement for the original image when feeding off-the-shelf depth estimators or feed-forward reconstruction models. Intermediate representations include a binary segmentation mask, a per-pixel alpha matte, a grayscale foreground map, and a surface normal map with confidence, all computed within the transparent object region. The entire pipeline runs in 0.38 seconds per view on an RTX 4090, making it practical as a preprocessing step.

## Method

GHOST is a four-stage sequential pipeline that progressively recovers geometric and photometric cues before synthesizing an opaque texture. TransDINO (U-Net with ResNet-34 + frozen DINOv3 dual-stream encoding and cross-attention fusion) extracts a robust segmentation mask; TransDecomp (U-Net with gated convolutions and a ViT bottleneck) disentangles alpha and foreground via a physics-constrained alpha-blending reconstruction loss; DAF-Net (MMSA CNN encoder fused with DINOv3 features through Squeeze-and-Excitation blocks) estimates surface normals using a sign-agnostic Von Mises-Fisher NLL loss with edge-aware smoothness regularization; and GeoSemTransNet (encoder-decoder with SPADE spatially-adaptive normalization) synthesizes the final opaque texture conditioned on the normal map, mask, and foreground, trained with shape-from-shading and gradient-consistency losses that enforce geometric fidelity. The key insight is that injecting geometric priors hierarchically via SPADE—rather than simple concatenation—ensures the hallucinated texture strictly adheres to the recovered 3D structure, enabling generic downstream models to recover accurate depth and geometry without any retraining.

## Results

On the ClearGrasp/ClearPose depth estimation benchmark, MoGe2+GHOST achieves the best overall performance with RMSE 0.014, REL 0.013, MAE 0.009, and δ₁.₀₅ = 0.933, outperforming all specialized transparent-object depth estimators (HDCNet, SwinDRNet, TranspareNet, ClueDepthGrasp, DKT). For feed-forward 3D reconstruction, DUSt3R+GHOST achieves the highest F-Score of 88.66 (vs. 77.36 without GHOST) and the lowest Chamfer Distance of 0.51, while VGGT+GHOST reaches F-Score 86.72 with the lowest Normal Distance of 4.96. On segmentation, TransDINO attains 92.44% mIoU on ClearGrasp and 86.82% on Trans10K-v2, surpassing all prior methods by 9–21 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GHOST-processed generic models consistently outperform or match specialized transparent-object depth estimators: MoGe2+GHOST (RMSE 0.014, δ₁.₀₅ 0.933) beats SwinDRNet (RMSE 0.020, δ₁.₀₅ 0.931), HDCNet (RMSE 0.021, δ₁.₀₅ 0.846), TranspareNet (RMSE 0.028, δ₁.₀₅ 0.753), ClueDepthGrasp (RMSE 0.024, δ₁.₀₅ 0.784), and DKT (RMSE 0.021, δ₁.₀₅ 0.897) on all depth metrics. For 3D reconstruction, GHOST improves every feed-forward baseline: DUSt3R F-Score rises from 77.36 to 88.66, VGGT from 57.28 to 86.72, DA3 from 45.91 to 60.49, and π³ from 61.60 to 75.26. In segmentation, TransDINO dominates on ClearGrasp (92.44% vs. TROSNET 71.40%, EBLNET 64.99%) and Trans10K-v2 (86.82% vs. TOSQ-256 77.47%), but trails MODEST on ClearPose (85.21% vs. 90.98%) and only marginally beats TROSNET on TROS (57.94% vs. 57.23%). Zero-shot generalization on the unseen TROS dataset (Table 7) confirms consistent gains: MoGe2+GHOST reduces RMSE from 0.137 to 0.106 and VGGT+GHOST from 0.157 to 0.149, while Metric3Dv2+GHOST shows the smallest improvement (RMSE 0.211→0.158), suggesting GHOST benefits models with stronger geometric inductive biases most.

## Relation to Prior Work

GHOST departs from the dominant paradigm of building task-specific models for transparent objects (depth completion networks like SwinDRNet, or per-scene NeRF variants like NeRFrac) by instead proposing a universal preprocessing step that 'repaints' transparent regions into opaque, structurally consistent textures before feeding them to generic SOTA models. Methodologically, it builds on SPADE (Park et al., 2019) for geometry-conditioned texture synthesis and gated convolutions (Yu et al., 2019) for transparency-aware encoding, while leveraging DINOv3 as a frozen semantic backbone across all four sub-networks. The key conceptual shift is from 'model the physics of transparency in the downstream task' to 'eliminate the transparency problem at the input level,' making the solution model-agnostic and avoiding the 'perception silo' of narrow, task-specific architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for robotics and manipulation: a single 0.38s preprocessing pass enables off-the-shelf depth estimators and feed-forward reconstruction models to handle transparent objects without retraining, potentially unifying pipelines that previously required separate specialized models. However, the approach relies on a 'weak refraction' assumption (alpha blending approximation), and the four-model pipeline introduces cumulative error propagation; strong-refraction cases (thick glass, high-index materials) are acknowledged as a limitation. Open questions include whether the synthesized textures remain physically meaningful for tasks requiring actual optical properties (e.g., refraction-aware grasping), and whether the 256×256 training resolution generalizes to high-resolution real-world deployments.
