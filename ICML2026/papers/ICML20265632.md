## Problem

Visual Multi-Agent Systems (VMAS) suffer from a counter-intuitive 'scaling wall': increasing agent turns degrades task accuracy while exponentially inflating token costs (30× by the 10th turn). The paper traces this failure to the information bottleneck of text-centric inter-agent communication, where converting dense visual perceptions and cognitive thinking into discrete natural language induces cumulative semantic loss, and the unstructured conflation of perception and thinking trajectories causes mutual interference that worsens with depth.

## Input / Output

The system takes RGB images or video frames plus a textual task query as input, with the visual input partitioned into patch blocks and hierarchically downsampled to three granularity levels (local, regional, global) before encoding. Outputs include the final task answer/conclusion generated by the last agent, plus two persistent latent memory banks: a perception memory storing multi-granularity visual hidden states and a thinking memory storing hidden-state chunks segmented at high-entropy semantic boundaries. All memory content is stored as continuous key-value pairs in R^{d_model} space rather than as text, with the VLM backbone kept frozen throughout.

## Method

L2-VMAS is a model-agnostic framework that replaces text-based inter-agent communication with a shared dual latent memory system comprising two decoupled banks (perception and thinking), each storing key-value pairs where keys are generated by a lightweight masked-attention transformer compressor. Memory synthesis encodes multi-granularity visual features (via the VLM's native frozen vision encoder) into the perception bank and chunks thinking hidden states at entropy-driven semantic boundaries into the thinking bank, with a capacity-constrained dynamic pruning/merging mechanism for the latter. Memory orchestration uses an entropy-driven proactive trigger (sliding-window mean + λ·std threshold) to activate retrieval only when uncertainty spikes, a Gumbel-Sigmoid gate to select which memory type to inject, and top-k similarity retrieval followed by a refinement compressor before inserting the latent memory at the triggering position. Training follows a three-stage PPO-based RL schedule with the VLM backbone frozen: Stage I trains synthesis with random triggering, Stage II trains orchestration with synthesis frozen, and Stage III jointly fine-tunes all memory components.

## Results

On the four main benchmarks (MMBench, MMStar, RealWorldQA, SimpleVQA), L2-VMAS achieves average accuracy improvements of 2.7–5.4% over the text-based VMAS baseline across five VLM backbones, while simultaneously reducing total token consumption by 21.3–44.8%. The largest gains appear on Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking (76.5% avg accuracy, +5.4% over VMAS; 3,473 tokens, −43.6% vs. VMAS's 6,154) and on complex benchmarks like RealWorldQA and SimpleVQA. The method also generalizes across model sizes (2B–32B) and six multi-agent topologies, with consistent 2.7–6.3% average accuracy gains over VMAS in every configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares L2-VMAS against two baselines: a single-agent VLM (no collaboration) and a conventional text-based VMAS (agents communicate via natural language). L2-VMAS consistently outperforms the text-based VMAS in average accuracy across all five backbones, with gains ranging from +2.7% (InternVL-3.5-8B) to +5.4% (Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking), while reducing token usage by 21.3–44.8%. Against the single-agent baseline, L2-VMAS also delivers substantial improvements (e.g., +5.6% on Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking), confirming that the latent memory mechanism adds value beyond simply running one model. The gains are most pronounced on reasoning-heavy benchmarks (RealWorldQA, SimpleVQA) and in Thinking-mode models, suggesting the dual memory is particularly effective when deep, sustained reasoning is required. The method's model-agnosticism is validated by consistent improvements across GLM-4.1V, InternVL-3.5, LLaVA-OV, and Qwen3-VL families, as well as across 2B/4B/8B/32B scales and six multi-agent topologies (linear, layered, centralized, random, complete, dynamic).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of visual multi-agent systems (VMAS), latent-space inter-agent communication, and visual latent reasoning. It departs from the dominant text-centric communication paradigm in VMAS (following Yu et al. 2025c, Qian et al. 2025) by replacing natural-language transmission with dual latent memories, drawing on recent latent collaboration ideas (Zheng et al. 2025, Fu et al. 2025, Zou et al. 2025) and visual latent reasoning (LaCoT, CoVT, Monet). However, it carves new ground by explicitly decoupling perception and thinking into separate memory streams and introducing entropy-driven proactive triggering, addressing the perceptual-cognitive conflation problem that prior latent-communication works do not handle. The compression and retrieval architecture borrows from key-value memory modules (CoMEM, VisMem) but adapts them to the multi-agent, multi-turn setting with dynamic capacity management.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the 'scaling wall'—where VMAS accuracy peaks at turn 3 and drops below single-agent baseline by turn 6 while token costs explode 30×—provides a critical diagnostic for the field, challenging the assumption that more agent turns monotonically improve visual collaboration. The practical impact is significant: a model-agnostic framework that improves accuracy by 2.7–5.4% while cutting token usage by 21–45% across five backbones, four sizes, and six topologies makes multi-agent visual systems more deployable. Open questions include whether the RL-based three-stage training recipe generalizes to new VLM families without retraining, whether the entropy-based triggering remains optimal as models develop more sophisticated internal representations, and whether the approach extends to asynchronous or massively parallel agent topologies beyond the six studied.
