## Problem

Model attribution for discrete diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) has not been studied, despite dLLMs' iterative decoding producing rich trajectories that differ from autoregressive generation. The core challenge is that per-step confidence signals are highly redundant due to the bidirectional attention in dLLMs, obscuring the structural information about decoding order and cross-step dependencies that could serve as model-specific fingerprints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262644/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full decoding trajectory of a dLLM response — a sequence of T confidence vectors over L token positions — and a small local dataset (as little as 10% of training data suffices) to build per-model fingerprints. The DDM transforms the trajectory into a T×L matrix with discrete effect values (α, β, γ and their negatives) encoding whether each newly decoded token exerts mixed, positive, or negative influence on prior tokens, and whether prior tokens' confidence is reinforced or weakened. Output is a source-model label with a log-likelihood-based attribution score; the method also operates in a strict black-box variant where only decoded tokens (not confidence) are observed at each step.

## Method

The paper proposes two components. First, the Directed Decoding Map (DDM) is a T×L matrix that encodes second-order structural relationships: for each newly decoded position it records whether the token induces mixed (α), uniformly positive (β), or uniformly negative (−β) confidence changes on all previously decoded positions, and for each previously decoded position it records whether its confidence increased (γ) or decreased (−γ) at that step. An SVD analysis reveals that discriminative attribution signals reside in middle and tail singular components (fine-grained structural patterns) rather than dominant modes, motivating a representation that preserves full cell-level structure. Second, Gaussian-Trajectory Attribution (GTA) fits an independent Gaussian distribution (empirical mean and variance) to each cell (t, l) of the DDM across N trajectories from a local dataset, producing a compact probabilistic fingerprint per model. Attribution is then performed by computing the log-likelihood of a target DDM under each model's cell-wise Gaussians and selecting the argmax, requiring no learned classifier and running in under 0.5 seconds for 32-token responses.

## Results

On the Cross-Model Attribution (CMA) task, GTA+DDM achieves 99.95% AUC on GSM8K and 98.94% AUC on CodeAlpaca-20K, substantially outperforming all baselines. In the more challenging Independent-Run Attribution (IRA) setting, GTA+DDM reaches 81.75% AUC (GSM8K) and 65.05% AUC (CodeAlpaca-20K), while in the hardest Cross-Checkpoint Attribution (CCA) setting it attains 66.91% and 62.64% AUC respectively. The method also achieves 99.85% TPR@1%FPR on CMA-GSM8K, indicating strong performance under strict false-positive control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTA+DDM consistently outperforms all three baseline families across every scenario and dataset. Against the Perplexity baseline (Alon & Kamfonas, 2023; Ankner et al., 2024), the gap is largest in CCA where PPL scores near random (49.32% AUC on GSM8K) while GTA+DDM reaches 66.91%, a 17.6-point improvement. Against the DBSCAN-based Clustering baseline (Schubert et al., 2017), GTA+DDM gains roughly 10–30 AUC points depending on the scenario; even the best clustering variant (Clustering+DDM) trails GTA+DDM by 2.6 points on CMA-GSM8K and 25.4 points on IRA-GSM8K. Against the Euclidean Distance baseline, GTA+DDM improves by 0.03 AUC on CMA-GSM8K (99.95 vs. 99.92) but by 12.3 points on IRA-GSM8K (81.75 vs. 68.31) and 5.1 points on CCA-GSM8K (66.91 vs. 61.79). The advantage of GTA+DDM is most pronounced in the harder IRA and CCA settings, where baselines relying on raw confidence or filtered confidence degrade to near-random performance while the structural DDM representation preserves discriminative signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model attribution (MA) literature—which has focused on autoregressive LLMs using perplexity, n-gram statistics, classifiers, or watermarks—to the emerging class of discrete diffusion LLMs (dLLMs). It departs from prior MA work by identifying that the iterative, bidirectional decoding trajectory of dLLMs carries structural information (inter-step token dependencies and confidence-change directions) that is absent in autoregressive generation, and that first-order per-step confidence signals are insufficient due to mutual influence among decoded tokens. The DDM representation and cell-wise Gaussian fitting (GTA) are novel constructions; the SVD analysis motivating the need to preserve low-energy structural components rather than dominant modes is a new analytical contribution to the attribution problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As dLLMs move toward commercial deployment (e.g., Inception's Mercury API already exposes intermediate decoding trajectories), the ability to attribute outputs to specific models or checkpoints becomes practically urgent for provenance tracking and misuse mitigation. The method is lightweight (no learned parameters, only empirical Gaussian fitting) and operates even in a black-box setting, though with degraded performance. Key limitations include evaluation on only two model families (LLaDA and Dream) and modest AUC in the hardest cross-checkpoint setting (~67%), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger model pools, longer sequences, or adversarially trained dLLMs that deliberately alter their decoding dynamics.
