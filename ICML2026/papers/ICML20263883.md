## Problem

While LLMs show strong capabilities in structured reasoning, their ability to perform precise geometric inference over symbolic indoor layouts—computing distances, verifying placement feasibility, finding collision-free paths, and evaluating spatial unions—remains poorly characterized. Prior spatial-reasoning benchmarks rely on raw images, qualitative language relations, or assess output realism for layout generation, leaving a gap in measuring unassisted geometric reasoning from explicit coordinate-based representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263883/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each instance pairs a JSON-encoded 2D floorplan (room boundary polygon, wall segments, openings, and labeled object polygons with coordinates in meters in a right-handed Cartesian frame) with a single natural-language question. Outputs are typed by format code: scalars (distances, angles, areas) scored at 2% relative tolerance (5% for area tasks), booleans for placement feasibility, object lists for visibility (set equality), and waypoint sequences for paths validated for collision-freeness and scored by Fréchet distance at a 0.6 m threshold. Synthetic layouts use axis-aligned bounding boxes while HSSD layouts retain arbitrary non-axis-aligned polygons, creating a natural difficulty gradient.

## Method

FloorplanQA constructs 2,000 layouts (1,800 generated by Gemini 2.5 Pro with rule-based spatial validity filtering, 200 projected and simplified from HSSD 3D scenes via Douglas-Peucker at ε=0.01 m) and pairs each with 8 parameterized questions spanning three reasoning categories: Metric (distance, angle), Topology (placement, visibility, free space, max box), and Dynamic (repositioning, shortest path). All 15 evaluated LLMs (7 reasoning-focused, 8 general-purpose) are tested zero-shot with a fixed prompt schema enforcing stepwise justification and a final-answer line, and ground-truth answers are computed deterministically via geometric algorithms (shoelace centroids, AABB intersection, A*-style path validation). Ablations probe format robustness (JSON vs. XML), semantic perturbation (label swaps), tool augmentation (Python code interpreter), and VLM input (three rendering styles with/without JSON), revealing that arithmetic-heavy tasks improve dramatically with tools while planning and union tasks remain bottlenecked by spatial algorithmic reasoning rather than numerical precision.

## Results

Across 15 LLMs evaluated on 16,000 layout-question pairs (2,000 layouts × 8 question types), the best overall accuracy is 75.49% (gpt-oss-120B), while the worst is 30.05% (Devstral Small). Metric tasks (Pair Distance, View Angle) achieve 75–95% on synthetic axis-aligned layouts but drop to 35–60% on HSSD's non-axis-aligned polygons. Optimization and planning tasks (Free Space, Max Box, Shortest Path) remain largely unsolved, with averaged accuracy of only 5–45%, and Max Box scores below 10% for most models on HSSD layouts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

gpt-oss-120B leads overall at 75.49% accuracy with minimal truncation (2%), outperforming GPT-5 (65.45%) and GPT-5-mini (70.62%) on the full benchmark, largely due to strong performance on Free Space (99% on kitchens) and Max Box (62.8% on kitchens). Reasoning models gain +10–40 percentage points over general models on geometric union tasks (Free Space, Max Box), but this advantage is eroded by severe token-limit truncation: DeepSeek-R1 (31.3%), Qwen3-30B-Think (38.9%), and Gemini Flash 2.5 (50.6%) lose substantial accuracy to incomplete responses. On HSSD layouts with non-axis-aligned polygons, all models degrade sharply—GPT-5 drops from ~99% to 69% on Pair Distance, and Gemini Flash 2.5 falls to 12.5%—while gpt-oss-120B retains the highest HSSD Pair Distance score at 78.5%. Tool augmentation (Python interpreter) yields +30 to +43 points on arithmetic tasks (Pair Distance: 56→99%, View Angle: 55→96%) but *decreases* accuracy on Max Box (−4.5) and Shortest Path (−12.5), confirming that the bottleneck for planning tasks is algorithmic spatial reasoning rather than numerical precision. Vision-language inputs (rendered floorplan images) provide at most +2–5 pp gains when combined with JSON and drop to 19–40% when used alone, confirming that structured symbolic coordinates remain the dominant signal for spatial reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by isolating symbolic geometric reasoning from structured coordinate-based layouts (JSON/XML), departing from prior spatial reasoning benchmarks that rely on visual input (CLEVR, SpatialSense, ScanQA) or qualitative spatial language (Yamada et al., BALROG). It also distinguishes itself from layout generation work (LayoutGPT, Holodeck, FirePlace) that assesses output realism rather than the model's ability to infer constraints directly from given geometry. The benchmark repurposes HSSD for 2D projection and uses Gemini 2.5 Pro for synthetic layout generation, but the core contribution is the task formulation: explicit metric computation, geometric unions, and collision-free path planning from symbolic input without tool assistance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FloorplanQA provides a diagnostic framework that reveals a consistent and specific capability gap: LLMs handle shallow geometric queries (distances, angles) but systematically fail on geometric unions, constraint satisfaction, and multi-step spatial planning across all 15 evaluated models. The finding that Python tool augmentation rescues arithmetic tasks but worsens planning tasks (Max Box, Shortest Path) localizes the bottleneck to spatial representation and algorithmic reasoning rather than numerical precision. Open questions include whether hybrid approaches with external geometric solvers can close the gap, whether training with explicit spatial constraints and harder distributions (irregular, overlap-heavy layouts) improves performance, and whether the 2D findings generalize to 3D embodied settings.
