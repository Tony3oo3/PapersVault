## Problem

Existing geo-localization benchmarks treat the task as static recognition from a single image or one-shot panorama, leaving no mechanism for the sequential, active evidence-gathering that humans naturally perform when localizing without GPS. No prior benchmark evaluates MLLMs as embodied agents that iteratively select viewpoints (yaw, pitch, zoom) to refine location hypotheses under partial observability, nor does it provide fine-grained diagnostic evaluation across perception, spatial, commonsense, and geo-localization reasoning dimensions simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265314/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 2,207 globally distributed 360° equirectangular street-view panoramas, rendered into perspective views via a controllable camera model with constrained actions (|Δψ|≥45°, φ∈[−60°,60°], zoom∈[1×,5×]). The agent receives sequential egocentric observations (base 90° FOV), a summarized action history, and a natural-language query, and must emit a structured JSON object with four fields—structured_observation, evidence_evaluation, hypothesis_update, and next_action—containing hierarchical geographic labels (street/city/country), GPS coordinates, and the next (Δψ, Δφ, zoom) action or a stop signal. Performance is scored via the Geo-localization Score (GLS), an arithmetic mean of semantic alignment accuracy, multi-threshold Haversine hit-rate (1/25/200/750/2500 km), and log-normalized median distance error.

## Method

ERGeoBench's core mechanism transforms static panoramas into an interactive egocentric perception environment through a perspective-projection camera model, enabling three progressive settings: single-view (fixed 90° FOV), panorama-view (full equirectangular as a passive upper bound), and embodied-view (sequential active exploration with kinematic constraints). A unified structured prompt decomposes each reasoning step into a closed loop—observe, evaluate evidence, update hypothesis, plan next verification action—motivated by active-sampling and spatial-memory principles from cognitive science. Evaluation spans four diagnostic dimensions (Foundational Perception via a 5-slot framework, Spatial Awareness across 7 sub-tasks including cross-view rotation reasoning, Common Sense via functional affordance mapping, and Geo-localization Reasoning as the integrative task), with quality control through target-view declaration, independent VLM checking, and human verification. Data is sampled from the top 1,000 populated cities using OSM-based weighted scoring (70% centrality, 30% road significance) within a 5 km radius of city centers, and the benchmark is evaluated on nine proprietary and open-source MLLMs under a unified temperature-0.1, 4096-token protocol.

## Results

Across 2,207 globally distributed street-view panoramas, proprietary MLLMs substantially outperform open-source models on the Geo-localization Score (GLS). Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the highest overall GLS (65.93 / 68.28 / 69.02 for Single / Panorama / Embodied settings), while Gemini-3-Flash leads fine-grained metric localization with Acc.@1km of 22.96% in the Embodied setting. Country-level classification reaches 99.01% (Gemini-2.5-Pro, Embodied), but street-level accuracy remains below 9% for all models, and median localization errors for proprietary models are ~5–8 km versus 15–1,237 km for open-source models. In the Embodied setting, strong proprietary models match or slightly exceed their Panorama GLS (e.g., GPT-4o: 67.06→67.55; Gemini-2.5-Pro: 68.28→69.02), indicating that active viewpoint selection can compensate for restricted initial observations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the nine evaluated MLLMs, Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the highest GLS in all three settings (65.93/68.28/69.02), outperforming GPT-4o (61.98/67.06/67.55) by 4–7 points and Gemini-3-Flash (65.54/68.19/68.81) by 0.2–4 points. Gemini-3-Flash, however, dominates fine-grained metric localization, reaching 22.96% Acc.@1km in the Embodied setting versus 16.75% for Gemini-2.5-Pro and 9.73% for GPT-4o, and achieving the lowest median error of 4.85 km. All four open-source models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B, InternVL2.5-8B, InternVL3-8B) trail the proprietary group by 15–35 GLS points, with Qwen3-VL-8B being the strongest open-source model (56.19 Panorama GLS) yet still far below the best proprietary scores. Compared to prior geo-localization benchmarks (IMAGEO-Bench, GLOBE, GRE Suite, GAEA, G3), ERGeoBench is the only one supporting active perception, embodied interaction, metric localization, and multi-dimensional diagnostics (FP/SA/CS/GR) simultaneously. In the Embodied setting, strong proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-Flash, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Gemini-3-Flash) all match or slightly exceed their Panorama GLS, whereas weaker open-source models degrade, confirming that effective active exploration requires strong underlying spatial and perceptual capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant static geo-localization paradigm (IMAGEO-Bench, GLOBE, GRE Suite, GAEA, G3), where models infer location from a single fixed image or one-shot panorama via regression or retrieval. It repurposes the embodied-agent and active-perception frameworks from cognitive science (gaze control, information-gain-driven sampling, spatial memory updating) and transplants them into MLLM geo-localization evaluation, introducing a sequential observe-evaluate-update-act loop over yaw, pitch, and zoom actions. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate literatures—worldwide image geo-localization and embodied vision-driven agents—without building on a specific prior method, instead carving new ground at their intersection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ERGeoBench fills a genuine gap by providing the first diagnostic benchmark that separates passive visual access (panorama) from active evidence selection (embodied), revealing that spatial awareness—particularly cross-view consistency at ~10–20% accuracy—is the critical bottleneck rather than foundational perception. The finding that geo-localization performance is strongly correlated with all three supporting capabilities suggests that improving localization requires integrated reasoning rather than better visual recognition alone. Key limitations include the simulated (non-physical) environment, absence of translational movement between street nodes, and reliance on a single panorama node per scenario, leaving open whether findings transfer to continuous navigation or dynamic scenes.
