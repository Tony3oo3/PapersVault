## Problem

Existing temporal causal representation learning methods assume causal mechanisms switch instantaneously between discrete domains, yet real-world systems (vehicle dynamics, human gait) exhibit continuous, gradual mechanism transitions. This paper formalizes the setting where intermediate mechanisms are convex combinations of finitely many atomic mechanisms and asks: can we learn identifiable latent representations and recover the continuous mixing trajectory α(t) through mechanism space, even for transitions never observed during training?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261057/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Training consumes K domain-labeled trajectory sets, each generated under a fixed atomic mechanism (one-hot mixing), with observations x_t ∈ R^p and temporal context of lag L. At inference, the method takes an unlabeled temporal observation sequence {x_t}_{t=1}^T undergoing continuous mechanism transitions and produces two outputs: (1) identifiable latent causal variables ẑ_t ∈ R^d (up to permutation and component-wise monotone transformation), and (2) a continuous mixing trajectory {α(t)}_{t=1}^T where each α(t) ∈ Δ^{K-1} is a point on the probability simplex, recovered via least-squares projection followed by temporal smoothing with window w. Observations range from low-dimensional vectors to 128×128 grayscale images and 3D motion-capture skeletons.

## Method

TRACE is a two-stage Mixture-of-Experts framework. In Stage 1, a shared sequential VAE encoder maps observations to latent variables, while K domain-specific normalizing-flow experts each learn the inverse transition dynamics of one atomic mechanism; deterministic one-hot gating during training ensures each expert specializes in its domain's causal structure. In Stage 2, the method constructs a differential basis matrix B̂ from conditional expectation differences across domains (computed on validation data), then recovers mixing coefficients at each test time step by projecting the encoded latent onto B̂ via the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse, followed by simplex projection and temporal averaging. The key architectural insight is that the MoE structure provides a one-to-one correspondence between experts and identifiable atomic mechanisms, making the least-squares decomposition at test time principled rather than heuristic. Theoretical analysis establishes joint identifiability of latent variables and mixing trajectories, with a minimax-optimal O(T^{-2/3}) recovery rate under bounded total variation that improves with trajectory smoothness.

## Results

On the synthetic benchmark (d=8, K_total=5, K_active=3), TRACE achieves a Weight Correlation of 0.94±0.05 for continuous mixing trajectory recovery and a Latent MCC of 0.96±0.03 for latent identifiability, substantially outperforming the best discrete-switching baseline NCTRL-soft (Weight Corr. 0.72, MCC 0.90). On real-world vehicle turning (UAVDT), TRACE recovers the turning mechanism with Corr. 0.960 versus NCTRL's 0.239; on human gait transitions (CMU MoCap), TRACE achieves Corr. 0.856±0.043 versus NCTRL's 0.619. On the semi-synthetic Modified CartPole (128×128 pixel observations), TRACE attains MCC 0.970 and trajectory Corr. 0.95.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACE is the only method that can produce continuous mixing coefficient estimates during transitions; all other baselines (TDRL, LEAP, iVAE, PCL) require discrete domain labels and are marked NA for Weight Correlation. Among methods that do produce trajectory estimates, TRACE (0.94) outperforms NCTRL-soft (0.72) by 0.22 and NCTRL-hard (0.67) by 0.27 in Weight Correlation on the synthetic benchmark. For latent identifiability, TRACE (MCC 0.96) also exceeds all baselines, with the closest competitor being NCTRL (0.90) and TDRL (0.89). On real-world data the gap widens dramatically: on UAVDT vehicle turning, TRACE achieves Corr. 0.960 while NCTRL oscillates between discrete states at only 0.239; on CMU MoCap gait transitions, TRACE (0.856) nearly matches the physics-motivated proxy while NCTRL produces 5 spurious discrete transitions (Corr. 0.619). The paper notes that more recent methods (IDOL, CaRiNG, CtrlNS, CHiLD) address orthogonal challenges (instantaneous effects, non-invertible mixing, sparse transitions) and are not directly comparable under TRACE's setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRACE extends the temporal causal representation learning line (TDRL, NCTRL) by replacing the discrete mechanism-switching assumption with continuous convex-combination transitions over atomic mechanisms. Architecturally, it repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm (Jacobs et al., 1991; Shazeer et al., 2017) — typically deployed for efficiency in language models — so that each expert encodes one atomic causal transition, with a shared sequential VAE encoder (Kingma & Welling, 2014) providing the latent space. The key conceptual departure is the least-squares projection onto a differential basis matrix for trajectory recovery, which has no analogue in prior discrete-switching CRL methods and is underpinned by new identifiability theorems for the mixing coefficients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework opens a path toward tracking how causal relationships evolve continuously in physical and biological systems (vehicle dynamics, gait transitions, potential drug-discovery intervention windows), a capability absent from all prior temporal CRL methods. The K ≤ d+1 capacity constraint and the geometric bottleneck (σ_min degradation as K_active approaches d+1) are fundamental limitations that bound practical applicability, though the paper shows the constraint is sufficient rather than necessary when mechanisms are sparsely active. Real-world evaluation still relies on physically-motivated proxies rather than ground-truth mechanism labels, and the simplex constraint, while interpretable, slightly sacrifices out-of-distribution generalization compared to unconstrained combinations. A fully unrestricted extension beyond the finite-atomic-mechanism assumption remains open.
