## Problem

Standard DPO applies the Bradley-Terry model at the level of complete responses, with the autoregressive assumption only imposed after the objective is derived, creating a mismatch between the non-autoregressive Boltzmann distribution and the autoregressive learnable model. This paper asks whether the autoregressive assumption can be explicitly incorporated into DPO before applying the BT model, enabling a theoretically consistent finer-granularity preference optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263499/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts paired with chosen and rejected text responses from a preference dataset, and produces a fine-tuned autoregressive LLM. A key structural detail is that each response sequence is decomposed into subsequences via a strong composition, and preference comparisons are performed at each prefix rather than on the full response. The two sequences (chosen and rejected) are padded to equal length in the static family, while the adaptive family partitions them uniformly without padding.

## Method

ADPO extends the DPO energy domain from the output space Y to its prefix closure Y*, defining prefix-wise energies E1* and E2* over incomplete responses. This yields a prefix-wise Bradley-Terry model where the preference probability is a product of per-prefix BT distributions, and the resulting loss shifts the summation operation outside the log-sigmoid function (i.e., -Σᵢ log σ(β log π_θ/π_ref - β log π_θ/π_ref) per prefix). The paper proves that any reward function can be reparameterized by an autoregressive model (Theorem 1) and identifies two independent length measures—token length μ and feedback length μ'—that govern granularity. Practical instantiations include a static family (fixed window size k) and an adaptive family (fixed number of segments m), with m=1 recovering vanilla DPO and m=μ(y) yielding token-level ADPO.

## Results

On GSM8K, cADPO achieves the highest accuracy across all four LLMs, reaching 91.74% with Qwen-3-8B, 73.54% with DeepSeek-Math-7B, 68.76% with Llama-3-8B, and 78.85% with Gemma-3-12B. On MATH500, cADPO similarly leads with 57.20% (Qwen-3-8B), 35.40% (DeepSeek-Math-7B), 20.20% (Llama-3-8B), and 40.40% (Gemma-3-12B). On conversational benchmarks (Llama-3-8B), ADPO achieves 45.8 LC / 41.1 WR on AlpacaEval 2, 34.4 WR on Arena-Hard, and 7.1 on MT-Bench, outperforming both DPO and SimPO. The additional computational overhead of ADPO over DPO is negligible, at most 5.7% wall-clock time increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADPO consistently outperforms DPO on both GSM8K and MATH500 across all four LLMs, with gains of 1.3–3.7 points on GSM8K and 1.0–3.0 points on MATH500 (e.g., Llama-3-8B: 68.08 vs 64.37 on GSM8K, 21.00 vs 18.00 on MATH). cADPO further improves over cDPO on every metric: on GSM8K the margin ranges from +0.86 (Gemma-3-12B) to +0.64 (Qwen-3-8B), and on MATH500 from +1.80 (Llama-3-8B) to +2.00 (DeepSeek-Math-7B). On conversational tasks (Llama-3-8B), ADPO beats DPO by 5.5 LC points on AlpacaEval 2 (45.8 vs 40.3), 1.8 WR points on Arena-Hard (34.4 vs 32.6), and 0.1 on MT-Bench (7.1 vs 7.0), while also surpassing SimPO by 1.1 LC on AlpacaEval 2 and 0.6 WR on Arena-Hard. The granularity ablation (Table 3) confirms that finer feedback granularity (m≥128 in the adaptive family, k≤4 in the static family) is necessary for ADPO to consistently exceed DPO, with the adaptive family at m=256 yielding the best overall results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by reversing the order of assumptions: rather than applying the Bradley-Terry model at the response level and then factorizing the autoregressive model, ADPO first introduces the autoregressive structure via the prefix closure Y* and then applies a prefix-wise BT model. This distinguishes it from token-level DPO variants (TDPO, TGDPO, cDPO) that still rely on the response-level BT formulation but add token-level modifications, and from Rafailov et al. (2024), which interprets DPO through a soft-Q-learning lens without modifying the BT model itself. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) bridges additive reward functions and autoregressive reparameterization, generalizing the original DPO theory to explicitly accommodate autoregressive models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of two independent length measures (token length μ and feedback length μ') provides a principled framework for choosing granularity in DPO-based methods, unifying DPO (μ'=1) and token-level variants (μ'=μ) under a single formulation. The practical improvements are consistent but modest (1–4 points on GSM8K, 1–2 on MATH500), and the computational overhead is negligible (~5.7%). Open questions include extending beyond KL divergence to more general f-divergences, adapting the prefix-energy formulation to non-autoregressive generative models, and developing more efficient implementations for very long sequences where prefix-wise computation accumulates.
