## Problem

Sparse MoE models progressively lose expert specialization during training: co-activated experts produce redundant activations on the same tokens, and routers face ambiguous choices among functionally equivalent experts, severely underutilizing model capacity. Prior approaches address this through architectural redesigns (shared experts, heterogeneous layouts, modified routers) that require structural modifications and rely solely on intra-layer dynamics. This paper asks whether expert specialization can instead be treated as a first-class training objective via lightweight, architecture-agnostic regularization losses.

## Input / Output

Both losses consume only quantities already computed in the standard MoE forward pass: the SwiGLU intermediate activations z_i^(l,e) of co-activated experts within a layer and the softmax routing scores s_i^(l,e) across adjacent layers. They produce two scalar regularization terms (R_sp and R_cp) added to the standard language-modeling and load-balancing objective with tunable weights λ_sp and λ_cp, requiring no changes to router code, expert architecture, or model structure—integration is a single configuration flag in Megatron-LM.

## Method

The intra-layer specialization loss R_sp penalizes the squared cosine similarity between SwiGLU intermediate activations of different experts processing the same token within a layer; by Proposition 4.1, this drives co-activated experts' W_down gradients toward orthogonality, enforcing distinct learning trajectories. The cross-layer coupling loss R_cp maximizes the joint Top-k routing probability between adjacent layers, establishing coherent expert pathways that propagate specialization across depth under a mild representation-continuity assumption (Proposition 5.1). The two losses form a positive feedback loop: R_sp creates meaningful per-expert loss advantages that sharpen routing (lower entropy), while R_cp stabilizes token-expert paths so each expert trains on a purer data distribution, further amplifying specialization. Both are formally shown to be compatible with standard load-balancing regularizers and are validated across pre-training, LoRA SFT, and full-parameter fine-tuning at multiple scales and MoE variants.

## Results

On pre-training (C4 validation perplexity), adding sp+cp to load-balancing reduces perplexity by up to ~2.7% relative (e.g., 9.68→9.42 for 7B Vanilla MoE, 9.56→9.39 for 7B DeepSeek-style MoE). Under LoRA SFT on ~16B MoE backbones, sp+cp improves average downstream performance by +3.9 points on DeepSeek-MoE and +4.6 points on DeepSeek-V2-Lite over the matched load-balancing baseline, and by +5.5 points on Ling-mini-2.0 over AuxLossFree. In full-parameter fine-tuning of Qwen3-30B-A3B, HumanEval pass@1 increases from 92.07 to 95.73 (+3.66). Scalability experiments show that activating only N=6 experts with sp+cp (ppl 12.13) already outperforms the N=10 baseline (ppl 12.15).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is the orthogonality-and-routing-logit-variance auxiliary loss (o,v) from Guo et al. (2025a). On pre-training perplexity (Table 1), the o,v baseline consistently degrades performance relative to load-balancing alone (e.g., 14.27 vs. 14.01 for Small Vanilla MoE; 13.76 vs. 13.54 for Small DeepSeek-style), whereas sp+cp improves perplexity across all six scale-architecture combinations. On downstream LoRA SFT (Table 2), sp+cp outperforms o,v on 7 out of 8 benchmarks for DeepSeek-MoE (e.g., MMLU 0.4586 vs. 0.4293, MMLU-Pro 0.2276 vs. 0.1735, HumanEval 0.3415 vs. 0.2805) and on all 8 benchmarks for DeepSeek-V2-Lite (e.g., MMLU 0.5735 vs. 0.5474, GSM8K 0.5004 vs. 0.4701). For Ling-mini-2.0, cp+sp also dominates o+v on 7 of 8 benchmarks (MMLU 0.7667 vs. 0.7119, BBH 0.7269 vs. 0.7016, HumanEval 0.6890 vs. 0.6524), with the sole exception being HellaSwag where o+v edges ahead by 0.003. The paper concludes that activation/path-based regularization (sp+cp) transfers more robustly in fine-tuning than directly maximizing routing-logit variance (o,v).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of MoE specialization work that relies on architectural modifications (DeepSeekMoE's shared experts, HMoE's heterogeneous sizes, MoDSE's diversified allocation) by proposing purely loss-centric regularization that is architecture-agnostic. It builds directly on the cross-layer coupling observation from Cai et al. (2024) and Yao et al. (2024), who identified inter-layer routing correlation as an emergent property, by formalizing it into an explicit training objective (R_cp) rather than using it only for system-level scheduling. The intra-layer specialization loss (R_sp) is novel in targeting activation-level redundancy among co-activated experts, complementing rather than replacing standard load-balancing and z-loss regularizers from GShard, Switch, and ST-MoE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature (a single configuration flag in Megatron-LM, no router or FFN code changes) makes these losses immediately adoptable in existing MoE training pipelines, and the demonstrated scaling efficiency (N=6 activated experts beating N=10 baseline) suggests practical compute savings. The closed-loop theory connecting specialization to routing sharpness provides a principled explanation for why the two losses reinforce each other, though the theoretical assumptions (representation continuity, near-orthogonal router weights) may not hold uniformly in very early training or with non-standard routing. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-text modalities, interacts well with expert-choice or dynamic routing mechanisms, and whether the gains persist at truly frontier scale (100B+ parameters) where routing dynamics may differ.
