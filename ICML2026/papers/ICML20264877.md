## Problem

Existing sequential knowledge editing methods for LLMs employ diverse regularization mechanisms—null-space projections, post-processing operators, constrained objectives—whose individual necessity remains unclear. No unified theoretical framework explains why methods like AlphaEdit achieve stable sequential edits while others fail, leaving practitioners without principled guidance for designing reliable editing algorithms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264877/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the FFN output projection matrix W ∈ R^{d_o × d_i} at specific transformer layers, consuming key-value pairs where keys are hidden representations of (subject, relation) prompts and values are the desired FFN outputs for target objects. The output is a closed-form weight perturbation Δ added to W, computed by solving an ordinary least-squares problem that jointly accounts for preserved knowledge, all previously edited facts, and the new edit at the current step, with the accumulated perturbation W + ΣΔ_τ representing the fully updated model.

## Method

The paper establishes that stable sequential editing is governed by equivalence between one-time editing (OTE) and sequential editing (SE): when past editing constraints are properly incorporated into each step's OLS objective, the accumulated sequential updates exactly reconstruct the corresponding batch solution. This equivalence is proven for AlphaEdit (Lemma 3.1) and generalized to any projector P and regularization coefficient λ (Proposition 3.2), revealing that null-space projection is not the source of stability but rather introduces approximation errors that accumulate over steps. A constructive design recipe (Remark 3.3) derives stable sequential updates as the difference between consecutive OTE solutions, and an error-correction mechanism (Algorithm 1) compensates for distortions introduced by post-processing regularizers like PRUNE and RECT. The framework further extends to handle conflicting edits via a unified closed-form update that resolves overlapping key-value pairs (Proposition 3.5).

## Results

On CounterFact (LLaMA-3 8B), all OTE-aligned methods achieve >98% Efficacy under sequential editing (SE), with MEMIT (aligned) reaching 99.85% and RECT (aligned) 99.88%. The key finding is that SE and OTE produce nearly identical results for MEMIT and AlphaEdit (e.g., MEMIT Efficacy: 99.85% SE vs. 99.80% OTE), empirically validating the theoretical OTE-SE equivalence. In contrast, RECT shows severe OTE degradation (Generalization drops from 94.34% SE to 59.41% OTE on LLaMA-3), confirming that post-processing regularization distorts aggregated updates. On GLUE benchmarks, all OTE-aligned methods preserve general language capabilities (e.g., SST: 0.846 vs. 0.831 pre-edit), while the null-space-simplified variant collapses to near-zero scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEMIT (aligned) and AlphaEdit (aligned) both achieve near-identical SE and OTE performance (e.g., MEMIT Efficacy 99.85% vs. 99.80%), directly validating the paper's central OTE-SE equivalence theorem; AlphaEdit's null-space projection is shown to be unnecessary, as the Memorize-the-Latest experiment causes complete language collapse when past constraints are dropped. PRUNE (aligned) matches MEMIT/AlphaEdit in SE (99.87% Efficacy) but without OTE alignment drops to 56.30%, demonstrating that alignment—not the pruning constraint itself—drives stability. RECT (aligned) achieves the highest SE Efficacy (99.88%) but suffers the largest OTE degradation (Generalization: 94.34%→59.41% on LLaMA-3), because its proportional weight-change constraint indiscriminately discards informative directions in the aggregated update. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that removing error correction from RECT causes a 10.7-point Generalization drop (94.34→83.60), while PRUNE is minimally affected, consistent with the theoretical analysis that RECT's distortion accumulates more severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on AlphaEdit (Fang et al., 2025) and MEMIT (Meng et al., 2023), unifying them—and also PRUNE and RECT—under a single ordinary-least-squares formulation for locate-and-edit knowledge editing. Its central departure is a re-interpretation: rather than treating null-space projection (AlphaEdit's headline mechanism) or post-processing regularization (PRUNE, RECT) as the source of sequential stability, the paper proves that stability arises from a more fundamental property—equivalence between sequential editing and a corresponding one-time editing objective. This reframes the design problem from 'which regularizer to add' to 'how to maintain OTE-SE consistency,' subsuming prior methods as special cases of a broader closed-form framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying OTE-SE equivalence as the true mechanism behind stable sequential editing, the paper collapses a fragmented landscape of ad-hoc regularization strategies into a single principled design criterion, potentially simplifying future method development. The practical error-correction algorithm (Algorithm 1) for post-processing regularizers is a modest but useful addition. Open questions include whether the framework extends beyond FFN output projections to attention layers or non-structured knowledge, how it behaves under adversarial or adversarially-ordered edit sequences, and whether the OLS closed-form assumption holds when key matrices become ill-conditioned at very large edit counts. The paper's scope is limited to structured triple editing in decoder-only LLMs, leaving broader applicability to future work.
