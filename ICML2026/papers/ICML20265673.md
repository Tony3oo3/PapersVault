## Problem

Existing generalization analyses for asynchronous SGD (ASGD) rely on restrictive assumptions such as Lipschitz continuity, bounded parameter or data domains, and quadratic losses, are largely confined to homogeneous data settings where all workers share the full dataset, and produce pessimistic, delay-sensitive risk bounds that fail to recover the minimax-optimal O(1/√(mn)) rate. This paper asks whether sharp, delay-insensitive generalization guarantees can be established under minimal assumptions and realistic distributed settings with arbitrary delays and heterogeneous data.

## Input / Output

The analysis considers a distributed setting with m workers, each holding a local dataset of n samples drawn i.i.d. from a (potentially worker-specific) distribution, along with initial model parameters w₁ ∈ ℝᵈ and a learning rate schedule {ηₜ}. The algorithmic output is an averaged iterate A(S) = (Σηₜw_{t-τₜ})/(Σηₜ), while the theoretical output consists of on-average model stability bounds and excess risk bounds expressed in terms of worker participation N_T^M (the maximum number of updates by any single worker) rather than delay magnitudes. The analysis conditions on the realized worker arrival sequence {mₜ}, making the bounds architecture-agnostic and applicable to any ASGD scheduling policy.

## Method

The core technique is a stability-based generalization analysis using on-average model stability (Lei & Ying, 2020), where the key innovation is a stability bound for ASGD that depends on worker participation N_T^M and empirical risks rather than on delay magnitudes, achieved through a novel error decomposition that controls the accumulated bias from delayed updates via a virtual-iterate argument (Lemma B.1). Combining this stability bound with a convergence guarantee (Lemma 4.4) under delay-adaptive step sizes yields excess risk bounds that are delay-insensitive and recover the minimax-optimal O(1/√(mn)) rate in balanced regimes, with a fast O(1/mn) rate under low-noise conditions. The analysis extends to non-smooth objectives by replacing L-smoothness with (α,L)-Hölder continuity of gradients, where the cost of relaxing smoothness is controlled by an appropriate step-size choice, and to heterogeneous data via random ASGD (Koloskova et al., 2022), establishing the first non-vacuous excess risk guarantees for ASGD under data heterogeneity without additional assumptions on distributional divergence.

## Results

For convex and smooth objectives, the paper establishes a minimax-optimal excess risk bound of $O(1/\sqrt{mn})$ in balanced worker-participation regimes (where $N_T^M \asymp T/m$), and a fast rate of $O(1/mn)$ under a low-noise condition ($F(\mathbf{w}^*) \lesssim 1/(mn)$). For non-smooth objectives with $(\alpha,L)$-Hölder continuous gradients, the same $O(1/\sqrt{mn})$ rate is recovered with delay-adaptive step sizes. For heterogeneous data via random ASGD, the paper obtains the first non-vacuous excess risk guarantee of $\tilde{O}(1/\sqrt{mn})$ without any data-heterogeneity assumptions. Empirically on MNIST, increasing the number of workers reduces both algorithmic stability and test error, while straggling behavior (imbalanced $N_T^M$) degrades test error for standard ASGD but not for random ASGD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's stability and excess risk bounds strictly improve upon all four prior ASGD generalization analyses. Compared to Regatti et al. (2019), whose uniform stability bound $T^{\tau_M}/(mn\tau_M)$ grows exponentially with maximum delay and requires Lipschitzness, the paper's bound depends only on worker participation $N_T^M$ and is delay-insensitive. Against Liu et al. (2022), which assumes quadratic loss, homogeneous data, and strong convexity to obtain $\eta T/(mn)$, the paper removes all three assumptions. Compared to Deng et al. (2025), which requires quadratic loss, bounded data, bounded noise, and homogeneous data to achieve $\tilde{O}((T-\tau)/(n\tau))$, the paper's $O(1/\sqrt{mn})$ rate holds for arbitrary delays and non-quadratic losses. Most notably, versus Deng et al. (2024), which needs bounded parameter space, homogeneous data, and $\bar{\tau} \leq T^{1/4}$ to get $O(1/\bar{\tau} + 1/\sqrt{n})$, the paper achieves the same or better rate without any of these restrictions and further extends to non-smooth and heterogeneous settings where no prior non-vacuous excess risk guarantee existed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the on-average model stability framework of Lei & Ying (2020) to the asynchronous SGD setting, building directly on prior stability-based generalization analyses by Regatti et al. (2019), Deng et al. (2024, 2025), and Liu et al. (2022). It departs from these works by replacing delay-magnitude-dependent bounds with worker-participation-dependent bounds, removing Lipschitz, bounded-noise, bounded-parameter, and bounded-data assumptions, and extending from homogeneous to heterogeneous data via random ASGD. The convergence analysis draws on the virtual-iterate and delay-adaptive step-size techniques of Koloskova et al. (2022) and Mishchenko et al. (2022), while the non-smooth extension leverages Hölder-continuity tools from Lei & Ying (2020) and Ying & Zhou (2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first non-vacuous excess risk guarantee for ASGD under heterogeneous data, showing that data heterogeneity does not inherently degrade generalization and that the minimax-optimal rate O(1/√(mn)) is achievable even with arbitrary delays. The key conceptual insight—that worker participation (imbalance), not delay magnitude, governs generalization—reframes how practitioners should think about asynchrony's cost. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-convex objectives and eliminating the residual dependency on maximum delay in the heterogeneous setting, both of which the authors flag as important future directions. Experimental validation is limited to MNIST, leaving open whether these theoretical guarantees hold for deep network training at scale.
