## Problem

Existing image quality assessment (IQA) reward models are typically restricted to a single evaluation task and produce only a scalar score without interpretable reasoning. When extended to multi-task settings covering heterogeneous quality dimensions (technical fidelity, aesthetic appeal, text-image alignment), conflicting reward scales and variances destabilize reinforcement learning, and current quality-aware MLLMs follow fixed, dataset-dictated reasoning patterns that limit flexibility and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263009/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image paired with a task-specific text prompt (e.g., a generation prompt for alignment evaluation, or a generic quality question for technical/aesthetic scoring) and produces a structured chain-of-thought rationale followed by a continuous quality score in [0, 5] rounded to two decimal places. The reasoning internally follows a plan-then-reason decomposition into concrete criteria (clarity, color, composition, artifacts, etc.), though the explicit plan is removed during training so the model internalizes the structure. For text-image alignment, the prompt specifies the target description to evaluate against; for technical and aesthetic tasks, the prompt is a fixed scoring instruction.

## Method

VisualScore builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with a two-stage pipeline: (1) cold-start SFT on 41K plan-then-reason CoT trajectories constructed via rejection sampling (retaining only intermediate-difficulty samples where N=8 model predictions partially match ground-truth quality levels), with Gemini-based visual grounding correction; (2) GRPO reinforcement learning using a Gaussian-shaped continuous reward R = exp(−(ŝ−s*)²/2σ²) with σ=0.8. To stabilize multi-task RL against heterogeneous reward distributions, two task-level mechanisms are introduced in Stage 2: entropy gating, which applies policy gradients only to tokens whose entropy exceeds the top-ρ (ρ=0.2) quantile across the entire batch, and STD-guided filtering, which discards response groups whose intra-group reward standard deviation falls below τ=0.001. The loss modifies the DAPO token-level objective with an indicator function I(H_t ≥ τ_ρ^B) to restrict updates to high-entropy tokens, balancing learning across tasks with different confidence profiles.

## Results

On technical quality assessment (Table 1), VisualScore achieves an average PLCC/SRCC of 0.795/0.773 across six benchmarks (KonIQ, SPAQ, LIVEC, KADID, PIPAL, AGIQA3k), outperforming the best MLLM baselines Q-Align (0.774/0.764), DeQA (0.763/0.751), and Q-Insight (0.759/0.750). On text-image alignment (Table 2), VisualScore reaches 0.700/0.706 average PLCC/SRCC under few-shot joint fine-tuning, surpassing QwenSFT (0.665/0.661) and Q-Insight (0.626/0.671). On aesthetic quality (Table 5), VisualScore attains 0.612/0.597 average PLCC/SRCC, leading DeQA (0.592/0.579) and Q-Insight (0.571/0.558). As a test-time reward for T2I generation, VisualScore-guided Best-of-20 + Reflection on SANA-1.0-1.6B achieves 0.80 overall on GenEval, exceeding the larger SANA-1.5-4.8B + Best-of-2048 (0.76).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisualScore outperforms all MLLM-based baselines (Q-Align, DeQA, Qwen-SFT, Q-Insight) on the average PLCC across all three task categories: technical quality (0.795 vs. best 0.774 for Q-Align), text-image alignment (0.700 vs. best 0.665 for QwenSFT), and aesthetic quality (0.612 vs. best 0.592 for DeQA). On technical quality, VisualScore leads by a clear margin on out-of-domain benchmarks KADID (0.745 vs. 0.677 for DeQA) and PIPAL (0.481 vs. 0.478 for Q-Insight), while Q-Align retains a slight edge on AGIQA3k (0.832 vs. 0.823). In text-image alignment, VisualScore surpasses the full-finetune model UnifiedReward-Qwen (same Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone) on out-of-domain generalization (0.700 vs. 0.685 AVG PLCC), demonstrating that structured reasoning transfers better than scaling preference data. For T2I test-time guidance on GenEval, VisualScore as a reward model achieves 0.80 overall (BoN+Reflection), beating ImageReward (0.74, no reasoning support) and UnifiedReward (0.78), and outperforming the larger SANA-1.5-4.8B + Best-of-2048 (0.76) with a 1.6B generator. Handcrafted (NIQE, BRISQUE) and KonIQ-only deep baselines (NIMA, MUSIQ, CLIP-IQA+, ManIQA) are consistently outperformed, with the gap widening on out-of-domain datasets like KADID and PIPAL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based IQA line (Q-Align, Q-Insight, DeQA) by unifying three previously separate evaluation tasks—technical quality, aesthetic quality, and text-image alignment—under a single structured-reasoning model, and by replacing discrete or preference-based rewards with a continuous Gaussian reward optimized via GRPO. The entropy gating mechanism is directly adopted from high-entropy token optimization work (Wang et al., 2025c; Guo et al., 2025) and applied to a modified DAPO loss, while the two-stage SFT-then-RL pipeline with rejection sampling follows the broader trend of using RL to refine reasoning in MLLMs. The key departure from prior IQA-RL work (Q-Insight, VisualQuality-R1) is the explicit multi-task stabilization: STD-guided sample filtering and batch-relative entropy quantile gating are introduced to handle heterogeneous reward scales across tasks, a problem not addressed in single-task RL-IQA settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in providing a single interpretable reward model that can simultaneously score technical fidelity, aesthetics, and prompt alignment, enabling test-time T2I guidance (Best-of-N selection and iterative reflection) without retraining the generator. The finding that naive multi-task RL degrades performance below single-task baselines (Table 11), and that stabilization mechanisms only help after a warm-up stage, offers actionable guidance for anyone applying RL to heterogeneous multi-task settings beyond IQA. Open questions include whether the structured reasoning genuinely improves score accuracy beyond interpretability, whether the approach scales to additional quality dimensions (e.g., video, 3D), and whether the T2I guidance gains persist on more challenging compositional benchmarks beyond GenEval.
