## Problem

Existing Federated Continual Learning (FCL) methods treat all incoming client updates uniformly, failing to account for asynchronous concept drift where distribution shifts occur independently across clients at unsynchronized times and with varying magnitudes. This asynchrony generates conflicting updates that destabilize global convergence and exacerbates catastrophic forgetting, yet no prior FCL framework explicitly detects and calibrates learning in response to such heterogeneous, class-level distributional shifts without storing historical data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) with class labels arriving as streaming tasks across K=20 clients under Dirichlet-partitioned heterogeneity. Beyond the standard classification output, RC-FCL produces per-class drift scores computed via Sinkhorn distance in the intermediate feature space of a ResNet18 backbone, and per-sample adaptive weights derived from a local discriminator. A shared conditional GAN synthesizes class-conditional reference distributions on the server, which are broadcast to clients as synthetic replay data, eliminating the need to store any historical raw data.

## Method

RC-FCL integrates a conditional GAN (generator aggregated via FedAvg, discriminators kept local) with a ResNet18 classifier in a two-stage drift-aware pipeline. At each new task, the server synthesizes class-conditional reference samples using the aggregated generator; each client then computes a Sinkhorn distance (entropic optimal transport with ε=0.05) between local and synthetic feature distributions to obtain a soft drift score Δ_k(y) per class. For detected drifted samples, the local discriminator—trained on the pre-drift distribution—provides an inverse novelty weight (α_i = 1 − D(x_i)), biasing the weighted cross-entropy loss toward samples that deviate most from the pre-drift concept. At the server, a drift-aware aggregation assigns each client a weight v_k = 1 + m_k, where m_k is a prevalence-weighted drift magnitude that amplifies signals consistently observed across multiple clients while attenuating rare local variations, ensuring the global model adapts to genuine asynchronous drift without being derailed by isolated noise.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 under IID partitioning, RC-FCL achieves 35.23% average accuracy and 29.65 forgetting score, outperforming the strongest baseline FedTA (32.14% Acc, 34.92 FS) by over 3 points in accuracy. Under the most heterogeneous setting (α=0.1), RC-FCL still leads with 29.77% Acc and 37.59 FS on CIFAR-100, while on Tiny-ImageNet it reaches 26.69% Acc / 39.42 FS (IID) and 22.62% Acc / 40.11 FS (α=0.1). RC-FCL consistently achieves the best accuracy and lowest forgetting score across all six dataset–partition combinations evaluated.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RC-FCL outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and partition setting. The closest competitors are the drift-aware methods FedTA and FLASH: on CIFAR-100 IID, RC-FCL leads FedTA by 3.09 points in accuracy (35.23 vs. 32.14) and reduces forgetting by 5.27 points (29.65 vs. 34.92); on Tiny-ImageNet α=0.1, RC-FCL beats FedTA by 1.70 points in accuracy (22.62 vs. 20.92). Among replay-based methods, ReFed is the strongest baseline (e.g., 29.16% Acc on CIFAR-100 IID), yet RC-FCL still exceeds it by 6.07 points. Regularization-based methods (FedEWC, FedWeIT) perform worst, with accuracy below 15% and forgetting scores above 60, confirming that rigid parameter constraints hinder adaptation to concept drift. Distillation-based methods (FedLwF, GLFC, Target) fall in the middle, with FedLwF being the weakest overall (12.74% Acc on Tiny-ImageNet α=0.1). The paper notes that replay-based methods generally outperform regularization-based ones due to superior model plasticity under distributional shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Federated Continual Learning (FCL) paradigm by explicitly modeling asynchronous concept drift—where distribution shifts occur independently across clients at unsynchronized times and varying magnitudes—rather than treating all incoming updates uniformly as prior FCL methods (FedCIL, ReFed, CAN, FedTA) do. The retrospective calibration mechanism repurposes conditional GANs (building on Qi et al.'s generative replay) to synthesize class-conditional reference distributions without storing historical data, and introduces Sinkhorn optimal transport distance in feature space for class-level drift scoring, a tool not previously applied to drift detection in federated settings. The drift-aware aggregation with prevalence weighting departs from standard FedAvg by modulating client contributions based on both drift magnitude and cross-client consistency, addressing a gap left by prior drift-aware FL methods (FLASH, FedTA) that assume homogeneous drift across clients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asynchronous drift formulation is more faithful to real-world federated deployments where edge devices experience environment-specific changes at different times, and the data-free reference synthesis via CGAN respects the strict privacy and storage constraints of federated settings. However, the drift simulation relies on controlled input-level transformations (Gaussian noise, rotation, random erasing) rather than truly semantic distribution shifts, limiting confidence that the framework generalizes to more complex drift patterns such as emerging subcategories or label-space changes. Open questions include how the method scales when the CGAN itself degrades under repeated drift cycles, whether the Sinkhorn-based detection remains reliable under extreme heterogeneity (very small Dirichlet α), and how the additional communication overhead (generator/discriminator parameters) affects deployment on resource-constrained edge devices.
