## Problem

Existing functional censored quantile regression models for survival prediction assume additive effects between functional biomarkers (e.g., eGFR trajectories) and scalar covariates (age, sex, donor type), ignoring clinically important interactions that modulate how renal function affects survival across patient subgroups. Moreover, traditional nonparametric approaches (basis expansions, FPCA, RKHS) struggle with the curse of dimensionality when estimating multi-dimensional functional coefficients that vary jointly over time and scalar covariates, making them ill-suited for capturing these heterogeneous interaction effects.

## Input / Output

The model consumes post-transplant eGFR trajectories observed at irregular, individual-specific time points over a 5-year domain [0,5] as functional covariates, alongside three scalar covariates (age group, sex, donor type). It produces conditional survival time quantiles Q_T(τ|Z,X) at multiple τ levels (e.g., 0.3, 0.5, 0.7), the estimated functional coefficient surfaces α_τ(t,Z) showing how eGFR's prognostic effect varies over time and patient characteristics, and interquartile-range survival prediction intervals for individualized risk assessment. The time index t is explicitly included as a network input to handle the irregular sampling structure, ensuring one-to-one correspondence between network outputs and observed functional data points.

## Method

FunCQNet specifies the conditional survival quantile as Q_T(τ|Z,X;α_τ) = exp{β_τ + ∫_T X^T(t)α_τ(t,Z)dt}, where the coefficient α_τ(t,Z) is a multi-dimensional function of both time and scalar covariates, approximated by a DNN with input dimension q+1 (scalar covariates plus time index) and output dimension p. Training proceeds sequentially across quantile levels using a censoring-adjusted loss derived from martingale estimating equations: the cumulative hazard integral is approximated via a partition of quantile levels, and each quantile's loss incorporates previously estimated lower-quantile predictions, enabling the model to leverage information across the survival distribution. A conformal inference procedure (model-free and distribution-free) is integrated to test the global and per-covariate significance of interaction effects by comparing prediction losses from the full model against a reduced model with the covariate removed. The key architectural insight is replacing handcrafted basis expansions with a neural network to approximate the infinite-dimensional functional coefficient, thereby avoiding the curse of dimensionality while preserving interpretability at the function level rather than the parameter level.

## Results

On the UNOS/OPTN kidney transplant dataset (n=49,181, 34.75% censoring), FunCQNet achieves a C-index of 0.600 on the held-out test set, outperforming the additive model without interaction (0.574) and the standard Cox proportional hazards model (0.580). In simulation (Scenario 1, τ=0.5, 0% censoring), FunCQNet reduces RMSE of the functional coefficient α̂_{τ,1} by ~92% (0.028 vs. 0.375) and α̂_{τ,2} by ~75% (0.062 vs. 0.279) relative to the no-interaction model. On the real data, FunCQNet reduces prediction loss by nearly 60% at the median quantile (τ=0.5) compared to the additive model of Jiang et al. (2020).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the functional censored quantile regression model of Jiang et al. (2020), which treats scalar covariates as purely additive effects without interaction with the functional predictor. FunCQNet consistently outperforms this baseline across all quantile levels: at τ=0.5, prediction loss is reduced by nearly 60%, and the C-index improves from 0.574 to 0.600. A standard Cox proportional hazards model achieves a C-index of 0.580, which FunCQNet also surpasses (0.600), though the paper notes the C-index is a supplementary ranking metric rather than the primary quantile-estimation objective. In simulation, the advantage is even more pronounced: under Scenario 1 (homoscedastic errors with interaction), FunCQNet reduces RMSE of the first functional coefficient by ~92% and the second by ~75% compared to the no-interaction model, with gains persisting up to 50% censoring. Under Scenario 3 (no true interaction), FunCQNet maintains comparable accuracy to the simpler model, confirming it does not overfit when interactions are absent.

## Relation to Prior Work

FunCQNet extends the functional censored quantile regression framework of Jiang et al. (2020) by replacing the additive coefficient α_τ(t) with an interaction-dependent α_τ(t,Z), enabling the functional effect to vary across scalar covariate levels. For estimation, it departs from traditional basis-expansion approaches (splines, FPCA, RKHS) by using a deep neural network to approximate the multi-dimensional functional coefficient, addressing the curse of dimensionality that arises when interactions with multiple scalar covariates are included. For inference, it borrows the conformal prediction framework of Lei et al. (2018) to construct a model-free, distribution-free test for the significance of interaction effects, avoiding the need for asymptotic theory on infinite-dimensional parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical framework for individualized survival prediction in kidney transplantation that captures how the prognostic impact of renal function trajectories differs across patient subgroups (age, sex, donor type), which purely additive models cannot express. The conformal inference component offers a rigorous, assumption-light way to identify which covariates drive heterogeneity, supporting clinically actionable subgroup stratification. Key limitations include treating eGFR measurements as noise-free discretized trajectories (no explicit measurement-error modeling) and the moderate C-index (~0.60) suggesting room for improvement in absolute risk discrimination. Open questions include extending the framework to handle structured measurement error, higher-dimensional functional covariates, and dynamic treatment regimes.
