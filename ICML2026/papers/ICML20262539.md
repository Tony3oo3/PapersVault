## Problem

Existing orthogonal subspace methods for continual learning (e.g., O-LoRA, InfLoRA) enforce orthogonality in parameter space or input feature space, but both result in energy-dispersed representations that hinder knowledge compaction and exhaust capacity for future tasks. The paper identifies that task-induced output feature drift is inherently low-rank and energy-concentrated, and asks whether orthogonalizing in this output-drift space can simultaneously preserve past knowledge and free capacity for new tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262539/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input and produces class labels, operating in a rehearsal-free continual learning setting. It uses ViT-B/16 pre-trained backbones (IN21K or IN1K) with LoRA modules inserted only in the QKV projection layers of Attention and the first MLP layer, omitting the O-projection and second MLP layer where parameter-update energy is negligible. After each task, a small proxy set (as few as 16 samples) is used to compute the output-drift SVD; no historical data is stored, and at inference the LoRA weights merge into the backbone with zero additional latency.

## Method

E²-LoRA performs an energy-structured transformation after each task: it computes the output feature drift ΔY = B·A·X, applies SVD to obtain an orthonormal basis ordered by descending singular values, and re-expresses the LoRA parameters so that task-specific knowledge is concentrated in the leading ranks (Energy-Concentrated) and ordered by importance (Energy-Ordered). A theoretical result (via Eckart-Young-Mirsky) proves that retaining the top-r ranks minimizes expected output reconstruction error among all rank-r approximations. When a new task arrives, low-energy ranks of previous tasks are pruned using a dynamic rank allocation strategy governed by an energy-retention threshold ρ, and the freed orthogonal subspaces are reused to initialize the new task's frozen B matrix, naturally enforcing output-direction orthogonality. Self-distillation on old-class logits and post-hoc classifier alignment via synthetic Gaussian features further mitigate forgetting and classifier bias.

## Results

On the 10-task class-incremental learning benchmarks with ViT-B/16-IN21K, E²-LoRA achieves Last-Acc of 82.77% on ImageNet-R (matching the Joint Training upper bound of 82.76%), 92.13% on CIFAR-100, 89.77% on CUB-200, and 75.82% on Cars-196, with corresponding Inc-Acc values of 87.18%, 95.01%, 92.68%, and 80.90%. On domain-incremental learning, E²-LoRA reaches 88.25% Last-Acc on Office-Home and 69.63% on DomainNet. In the challenging 50-task setting, E²-LoRA maintains 78.58% Last-Acc on ImageNet-R and 90.70% on CIFAR-100, outperforming the strongest baseline TUNA by over 3% on ImageNet-R.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E²-LoRA outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest recent competitors, it leads TUNA by 3.33% Last-Acc on ImageNet-R (82.77 vs. 79.44) and by 6.36% on Cars-196 (75.82 vs. 69.46), while surpassing SSIAT by 3.39% on ImageNet-R and MOS by 4.67% on Cars-196. Prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) and adapter-based methods (EASE, BiLoRA) trail by 10–40 points on Last-Acc, reflecting the limitations of orthogonalization in parameter or input spaces. In the 50-task long-term setting, E²-LoRA maintains 78.58% Last-Acc on ImageNet-R versus 75.35% for TUNA and 72.12% for LORA-DRS, demonstrating superior capacity management. On domain-incremental learning, E²-LoRA leads DUCT (the strongest DIL baseline) by 2.83% on Office-Home (88.25 vs. 85.42) and by 2.62% on DomainNet (69.63 vs. 67.01). Notably, E²-LoRA's ImageNet-R Last-Acc (82.77%) effectively matches the Joint Training upper bound (82.76%), a result no other method approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) as its parameter-efficient adaptation backbone and departs from the orthogonalization-based CL line (O-LoRA, InfLoRA, GPM, DualGPM) by shifting the orthogonalization target from parameter space or input feature space to the output feature drift space. The key conceptual move is recognizing that task-induced output drift is far more energy-concentrated than either the LoRA parameter columns or the high-dimensional input representations, and that a post-hoc SVD on this drift (grounded in the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem) yields an energy-ordered basis that is optimal for rank truncation. Within the broader PEFT-for-CL landscape (L2P, DualPrompt, EASE, MOS, TUNA), E²-LoRA occupies the adapter-based branch but introduces a principled dynamic rank allocation mechanism that prior orthogonalization methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, practical recipe for managing the stability-plasticity trade-off within a fixed parameter budget: concentrate knowledge into leading ranks, prune low-energy tails, and reallocate freed capacity to new tasks. This is particularly relevant as CL systems must operate under bounded memory in deployment. Limitations include evaluation restricted to ViT-B/16 backbones and LoRA-based PEFT; whether the energy-concentration property of output drift generalizes to CNNs, LLMs, or non-LoRA adapters remains open. The 50-task results are encouraging but the long-horizon behavior (100+ tasks) and interaction with other PEFT techniques (adapters, prompts) are unexplored.
