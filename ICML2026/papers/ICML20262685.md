## Problem

Current disease screening models for medical images lack the ability to reference historical cases when making predictions, resulting in poor interpretability and suboptimal performance. Existing approaches rely on post-hoc saliency maps or deviation maps that do not provide case-based evidence, and no clinically oriented evaluation framework with appropriate metrics (e.g., specificity at fixed recall) or external test sets has been established for real-world screening.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single medical image (color fundus photography at 224×224, chest X-ray at 512×512, or dermoscopic image at 224×224) and produces a binary normal/pathological prediction, a spatial abnormality map for localization, and k nearest-neighbor evidence patches retrieved from both a normal and a pathological knowledge bank for each query region. The frozen foundation model (ViT-L based) extracts 1024-dim patch-level features at intermediate layers (7 and 17), which are aggregated via adaptive average pooling to form the regional feature map used for retrieval and reasoning.

## Method

EviScreen operates in two stages. First, dual knowledge banks are constructed by extracting patch-level features from historical normal and pathological cases using a frozen modality-specific foundation model, then applying greedy coreset subsampling to produce compact, representative feature sets K_N and K_P. Second, for a new input image, each patch feature serves as a query to retrieve k-nearest neighbors from both banks; a lightweight 4-layer transformer (8 heads, 1024-dim) then performs evidence-aware reasoning via cross-attention between the query and retrieved evidence, followed by self-attention for inter-patch refinement, before an MLP produces the final prediction. A training-free variant generates an abnormality map by contrasting the mean k-NN distance maps from the two banks (ReLU(M_N − M_P)), providing localization interpretability without any learned parameters. The key distinction from prior work is the use of scalable dual knowledge banks (rather than fixed prototypes) to capture diverse pathological appearances, enabling both retrospection interpretability (showing which historical cases support the decision) and sharper localization than deviation-based methods.

## Results

EviScreen achieves the best performance across all four test datasets and all six metrics (AUROC, AP, Spe@95%R, Spe@99%R, Spe@100%R, CSR). On JSIEC, it reaches 98.06% AUROC and 91.27% Spe@100%R; on CheXpert, 96.72% AUROC and 68.72% Spe@100%R; on Derm12345, 97.43% AUROC and 78.49% Spe@100%R; and on RIADD, 91.32% AUROC and 55.35% Spe@100%R. The clinically oriented metrics show the largest margins: on JSIEC, EviScreen improves Spe@95%R by 7.7% over the fine-tuned foundation model (FM) and CSR by 9.0% over the best runner-up. The training-free variant (Ours-TF) also outperforms all deviation-based baselines, confirming the value of the dual knowledge banks even without learned reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EviScreen outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset and every metric. Against the fine-tuned foundation model (FM), the closest competitor on AUROC, EviScreen gains 2.2–5.4% AUROC but far larger margins on clinical metrics (e.g., +12.9% Spe@100%R on JSIEC, +8.7% on CheXpert). Against PatchCore* (same foundation models, deviation-based), EviScreen leads by 3.1–29.3% on Spe@100%R, with the largest gap on CheXpert (68.72% vs. 15.53%), demonstrating that contrastive retrieval from dual banks produces far more focused abnormality maps than single-bank deviation scoring. The supervised anomaly detection methods (NFM-DRA, DRA) are outperformed by 8–53% on Spe@100%R, while unsupervised methods (SCRD4AD, EDC, SimpleNet) lag by 20–50% on the same metric. The prototype-based interpretable method CIPL is the strongest runner-up on AUROC (within 0.2–3.2%) but trails EviScreen by 8–20% on Spe@100%R and 10–24% on CSR, confirming that scalable dual knowledge banks surpass fixed learned prototypes for real-world screening. The training-free variant (Ours-TF) also beats all deviation-based baselines, validating the dual knowledge bank design independently of the learned reasoning module.

## Relation to Prior Work

EviScreen synthesizes three prior threads: coreset-based memory banks from industrial anomaly detection (PatchCore, SCRD4AD), prototype-based interpretable classification (XProtoNet, CIPL), and medical vision foundation models (RETFound, CheXFound, PanDerm). It departs from deviation-based methods by incorporating pathological-case evidence rather than only modeling normalcy, and from fixed-prototype interpretable methods by replacing a bounded set of learned prototypes with scalable k-NN retrieval over dual knowledge banks. The contrastive retrieval training-free variant further extends the memory-bank paradigm by contrasting distances to normal and pathological banks to produce abnormality maps, a mechanism not present in prior anomaly detection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of clinically oriented metrics (Spe@X%R, CSR) and a multi-modality evaluation framework with external test sets addresses a real gap: AUROC alone can mask large differences in specificity at clinically relevant recall levels, which directly affects unnecessary follow-up costs. The dual knowledge bank approach offers a practical, scalable alternative to fixed prototypes for screening, and the training-free variant demonstrates that the retrieval structure itself carries substantial signal. Open questions include extension to 3D volumes, finer-grained multi-label screening, and the privacy/ethical challenges of maintaining patient-level evidence repositories in deployed clinical systems.
