## Problem

Multi-task instruct-tuning of LLMs suffers from cross-task interference due to conflicting gradients over shared parameters. Existing solutions (task-specific neuron selection, MoE expert isolation) fail to fully eliminate this interference because a substantial fraction of parameters remains shared across tasks. The paper empirically demonstrates this residual sharing and asks whether LLM parameters can be decomposed into a smaller set of orthogonal 'basic abilities' that, when isolated, would more effectively disentangle task-specific gradient conflicts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264705/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM's weight matrices (specifically MLP gate projections) and multi-task instruction data (15 NLP tasks from SuperNI, each truncated to 1,000 training / 100 eval samples) as input. It produces K=8 LoRA experts of rank r=4 per layer with learnable routing weights (top-k=4 selection), plus a frozen residual matrix capturing low-singular-value components. At inference, the model outputs standard text responses, with the MoE router adaptively selecting which basic-ability experts to activate for a given task.

## Method

BADIT decomposes each pre-trained weight matrix W via truncated SVD into K rank-r LoRA experts (from the top rK singular vectors) plus a frozen residual, yielding K mutually orthogonal 'basic ability' experts at initialization (proven via Frobenius inner product orthogonality of SVD components). During multi-task training, the Dynamically Orthogonal Grouping (DOG) step computes gradients of all rK rank-1 components, normalizes them to the unit sphere, and iteratively (≤10 steps) regroups them via spherical K-means with SVD-based centroid orthogonalization, ensuring inter-expert gradient angles stay near 90° while intra-expert angles remain compact (~60°). The key distinction from prior MoE-LoRA methods is that BADIT isolates orthogonal basic abilities (shared functional primitives) rather than task-specific parameters, representing any task as a linear combination of these abilities through learned routing weights. The regrouping is proven output-invariant: reassigning rank-1 components among experts without altering their values leaves the total weight update unchanged.

## Results

On the SuperNI benchmark (15 NLP tasks), BADIT achieves an average ROUGE improvement of 2.68 points over the state-of-the-art method GainLoRA across 6 LLMs (Qwen3-8B/4B, Llama3-8B/3B, Gemma2-9B/2B). Under mixed training, BADIT is the only method that achieves positive Forward transfer scores (ranging from +0.54 to +0.92), while all baselines show negative Forward values, indicating that multi-task training actually hurts individual task performance without BADIT. Under sequential training, BADIT reduces the Forget Rate by 1.5–4.5 points compared to baselines (e.g., 6.95 vs. 9.21 on Qwen3-8B) and achieves the highest Backward scores (e.g., −5.43 vs. −8.11 on Qwen3-8B), demonstrating substantially reduced catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BADIT consistently outperforms all five baselines across every LLM and every metric under both mixed and sequential training. Against the strongest baseline GainLoRA (NeurIPS 2025), BADIT gains an average of 2.68 ROUGE points (e.g., 55.87 vs. 54.33 on Qwen3-8B mixed; 50.86 vs. 48.44 on Qwen3-8B sequential) and reduces Forget Rate by 1.5–3.4 points. Compared to LoRAMoE (ACL 2024), which shares the same MoE-LoRA architecture, BADIT improves mixed ROUGE by 1.2–2.3 points and sequential ROUGE by 2.4–3.9 points, with the gap widening on smaller models (e.g., +2.23 ROUGE on Llama3-3B sequential). Against OLoRA (EMNLP Findings 2023), which enforces static orthogonality between LoRA subspaces, BADIT achieves 1.9–3.8 higher sequential ROUGE, showing that dynamic regrouping (DOG) is critical since static orthogonality erodes during training. PiSSA (NeurIPS 2024), which uses SVD initialization but without multi-expert decomposition, is outperformed by 1.8–3.4 ROUGE on mixed training. Notably, BADIT is the only method achieving positive Forward transfer in mixed training across all 6 LLMs, whereas all baselines show negative Forward values, confirming that prior methods still suffer from unresolved cross-task interference.

## Relation to Prior Work

BADIT extends the LoRA + MoE paradigm for multi-task instruct-tuning (LoRAMoE, OLoRA, GainLoRA) by reframing the isolation problem: rather than assigning task-specific experts, it decomposes the pre-trained weight matrix via truncated SVD into K orthogonal LoRA experts that represent reusable 'basic abilities,' with any task expressed as a linear combination of these abilities. The SVD initialization directly inherits from PiSSA, while the dynamic orthogonal grouping (DOG) step—spherical K-means clustering of rank-1 gradient components with SVD-based centroid orthogonalization—is a new mechanism not present in prior MoE-LoRA methods. The paper's key empirical departure is demonstrating that even existing MoE-based isolation methods leave substantial parameter sharing across tasks, motivating the ability-level rather than task-level decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that MoE architectures still exhibit significant cross-task parameter overlap challenges the common assumption that expert routing adequately isolates task knowledge, and the 'basic abilities' decomposition offers a principled alternative that generalizes more naturally to unseen task combinations. Limitations include evaluation only on SuperNI (15 NLP tasks), a fixed number of experts (K=8), and a ~22% training-time overhead from the DOG step; whether the ability decomposition scales to hundreds of tasks or adapts its cardinality remains open. The method's reliance on SVD of full weight matrices also constrains its applicability to very large models where full SVD is prohibitive, though randomized SVD mitigates this partially.
