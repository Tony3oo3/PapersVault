## Problem

Existing online learning algorithms for hidden-action principal-agent problems achieve sublinear regret in the number of rounds T but suffer from exponential dependence on the problem instance size (either the number of outcomes d or the number of agent actions n). This paper asks whether one can design an algorithm with regret growing sublinearly in T while scaling only polynomially in the instance parameters, and answers affirmatively by assuming the principal knows a superset of the agent's true action set.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input a superset A of K candidate actions (each with a known cost and outcome distribution over d outcomes), a reward vector r ∈ [0,1]^d, a time horizon T, and a confidence parameter δ. It produces a sequence of contracts p^t ∈ [0,1]^d over T rounds, where each contract specifies a payment for every outcome. Internally, the algorithm constructs an ε-partition of the contract space into polytopal regions associated with discovered meta-actions, then commits to a single approximately optimal contract for the remaining rounds. The per-round running time is polynomial in T, d, and K, and the regret bound holds with probability at least 1−δ.

## Method

The key insight is to avoid the exponential number of possible action subsets by clustering candidate actions into meta-actions (ε-clustering where actions within a cluster have outcome distributions within Kε in L∞ norm), so that the principal's utility depends only on the meta-action rather than the specific action. The algorithm operates in two phases: Phase 1 (FindPartition) iteratively discovers which meta-actions contain at least one truly existing action and builds polytopal approximations of their best-response regions, using a Query sub-procedure that commits to a contract for O(1/ε²) rounds to identify the agent's meta-action, and a TestPartition sub-procedure that checks whether the current partition is a valid ε-partition or reveals a new existing meta-action. Phase 2 (ComputeContract) solves a linear program over each polytopal region to find the best contract under the assumption that the agent selects the representative action, then applies a linear-combination correction (from Dutting et al., 2021) to ensure the agent's actual best response yields near-optimal principal utility. The final regret bound is O(K^{7/5} d^{4/5} T^{4/5} log(dK³/δ)), polynomial in all instance parameters and sublinear in T.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 6.1) establishes that the proposed two-phase algorithm achieves regret O(K^{7/5} d^{4/5} T^{4/5} log(dK^3/δ)) with probability at least 1−δ, where K is the number of candidate actions, d is the number of outcomes, and T is the number of rounds. This is the first algorithm in this setting whose regret scales polynomially in both K and d while remaining sublinear in T. In contrast, the naive multi-armed-bandit reduction (Proposition 3.1) incurs regret O(2^K √T) with exponential per-round running time Θ(2^K). The exploration phase requires at most T_E = O(K^3/ε² · log(2d/ρ)) rounds, after which the algorithm commits to a fixed approximately optimal contract for all remaining rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, so comparisons are between regret bounds rather than measured performance. The paper's algorithm improves upon Bacchiocchi et al. (2024), which achieves O(d^n T^{4/5}) regret, by replacing the exponential dependence on n (the number of truly existing actions) with a polynomial dependence on K (the number of candidate actions in the known superset). It also improves upon Zhu et al. (2023), whose regret Õ(√d · T^{1−1/(2d+1)}) is exponential in d, while retaining the same T^{4/5} time dependence. The trade-off is that the paper's bound is polynomial in K rather than n; when K = O(poly(n)), the regret is polynomial in the true instance size, but when n = O(log K), the bound becomes comparable to the exponential-in-n result of Bacchiocchi et al. (2024). The paper notes that no lower bound is known for this setting, so the tightness of the T^{4/5} dependence remains an open question.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online learning framework for hidden-action principal-agent problems initiated by Zhu et al. (2023) and advanced by Bacchiocchi et al. (2024), both of which suffer from exponential dependence on the instance size (in the number of outcomes d or actions n, respectively). The key departure is the introduction of meta-actions—clusters of candidate actions with similar outcome distributions—which allows the algorithm to avoid the exponential blowup of treating each action subset as a bandit arm. The final contract computation borrows the linear-combination trick from Dutting et al. (2021) to handle the gap between the proxy action's utility and the true best response.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that polynomial-in-instance-size, sublinear-in-T regret is achievable under a natural and practically motivated assumption (the principal knows a superset of the agent's actions), closing a gap that prior work could only address with exponential scaling. The main open question is whether the T^{4/5} dependence is tight: the naive MAB reduction gives O(2^K √T), while this paper achieves O(poly(K,d) T^{4/5}), and no lower bound is known for the intermediate regime. The assumption, while reasonable in settings like ML task delegation, may not hold when the principal has no prior knowledge of the action space, leaving the fully unknown-technology case (where Zhu et al.'s exponential-in-d lower bound applies) unresolved.
