## Problem

Resampling in sequential Monte Carlo is inherently discrete, blocking gradient-based parameter estimation of state-space models. Existing differentiable alternatives (Gumbel-Softmax, Soft resampling) are fundamentally biased, while optimal-transport-based resampling incurs quadratic-in-N and exponential-in-1/ε computational costs. The paper seeks a resampling method that is differentiable by construction, statistically consistent, computationally efficient, and generically applicable without modifying the SMC algorithm.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of N weighted particles {(w_i, X_i)} in R^d, from which it adaptively constructs a Gaussian reference via moment matching (empirical mean μ_N and covariance Σ_N), along with a time grid 0=t_0<…<t_K=T and a dispersion constant b. It outputs N unweighted resampled particles X_i* whose values are smooth functions of the input particles and weights, yielding well-defined pathwise derivatives ∂X_i*/∂θ for any parameters θ embedded in the particles. Unlike OT-based methods that require solving a Sinkhorn iteration, the transport map here is specified by the SDE dynamics and needs no iterative computation.

## Method

The core mechanism is a training-free diffusion-based reparametrisation: a forward mean-reverting Langevin SDE is initialised at the target π and converges to a Gaussian reference π_ref, while the reverse-time SDE, started at π_ref, is simulated to produce resamples at terminal time T. The intractable score ∇log p_t in the reverse SDE is replaced by an ensemble score s_N(x,t) computed as a self-normalised importance sampling estimator over the weighted particles, requiring no learned network. Because the only stochasticity is Gaussian noise (reparameterisable), the entire resampling map is pathwise-differentiable by construction. When the reference is Gaussian, the reverse SDE has a semi-linear structure exploited by exponential integrators (Jentzen–Kloeden, Lord–Rougemont) for efficient and stable discretisation. The paper proves a Wasserstein-2 error bound showing the resampling error decomposes into a score-approximation term (decaying as N^{-r}) and an initialisation bias (decaying exponentially in T), and shows that the required sample size N grows only polynomially in T, improving on the exponential scaling of OT's entropy parameter.

## Results

On the Linear Gaussian SSM benchmark (Table 2), diffusion resampling (T=3, K=8) achieves the best loss function error (2.55±1.89), filtering KL divergence (4.26±4.49), and parameter estimation error (1.58±0.75) among all differentiable methods, with notably lower variance than OT. On the Gaussian mixture resampling task (Table 1), diffusion resampling (T=3, K=128) achieves the best sliced 1-Wasserstein distance (0.80±0.21), slightly outperforming OT (0.84±0.22) and multinomial (0.82±0.25). In the prey-predator and pendulum dynamics experiments, diffusion resampling yields the lowest prediction RMSE and most stable training loss compared to all baselines, with Soft and Gumbel-Softmax failing entirely (divergent parameter estimates) on the SSM task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OT (Corenflos et al., 2021), diffusion resampling wins on filtering KL (4.26 vs. 5.07) and loss error (2.55 vs. 2.64) in the LGSSM experiment, while OT achieves slightly lower resampling variance in the Gaussian mixture task (3.42 vs. 3.74). The paper also shows that diffusion scales better than OT in sample size N, with the computational crossover point shifting left as N increases. Against Gumbel-Softmax (Jang et al., 2017) and Soft resampling (Karkus et al., 2018), diffusion resampling dominates on every metric: both baselines produce divergent parameter estimates (NaN) under L-BFGS optimisation, while diffusion succeeds in ~80% of runs. In the prey-predator experiment (Section 4.3), diffusion resampling also outperforms the REINFORCE/stopped-gradient method of Scibior & Wood (2021), achieving lower and more stable training loss and prediction RMSE. Overall, diffusion resampling is the only method that simultaneously provides consistent (unbiased) resampling, stable gradients for second-order optimisers, and competitive statistical accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of score-based generative modeling (Song et al. 2021; Anderson 1982), ensemble score filtering (Bao et al. 2024), and differentiable resampling for sequential Monte Carlo. It departs from the OT-based resampling of Corenflos et al. (2021) by specifying the transport map through a diffusion SDE rather than computing it via Sinkhorn, thereby avoiding quadratic-in-N cost and exponential dependence on the entropy parameter. Unlike Gumbel-Softmax and Soft resampling, which are inherently biased interpolations, and unlike Wan & Zhao (2025), which requires a trained conditional diffusion model, the proposed method is training-free, consistent, and leverages an adaptive mean-reverting Gaussian reference chosen from the SMC posterior to inject target information into the resampling map.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a drop-in, differentiable-by-construction resampler for SMC with a proven Wasserstein error bound showing polynomial (rather than exponential) scaling in the diffusion time, making it computationally competitive with or superior to OT at equivalent accuracy. A key practical finding is that Gumbel-Softmax and Soft resampling produce completely divergent parameter estimates under quasi-Newton optimizers (L-BFGS), while diffusion and OT maintain ~80% convergence, highlighting a previously underappreciated sensitivity of second-order methods to gradient quality. Open questions include the optimal calibration of the diffusion coefficient b, whether the suboptimal N^{-c} rate in the error bound can be tightened, and whether continuous-time SDE simulation can replace discretisation in practice.
