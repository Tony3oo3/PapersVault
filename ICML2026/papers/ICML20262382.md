## Problem

Existing step-level caching methods for diffusion models rely on per-step error thresholds to decide whether to compute or reuse cached outputs, resulting in input-dependent inference latency that is difficult to control at deployment and cache policies that are not directly optimized for final output quality under a fixed compute budget. No prior work formulates step-level caching as a budget-constrained discrete optimization problem that guarantees deterministic NFE while maximizing generation fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262382/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes a text prompt, a random noise tensor, and a pre-computed binary cache mask (a static vector of length K specifying which of the K logical solver steps perform a fresh network evaluation versus reusing the most recent cached output). An optional pre-computed time schedule σ, optimized offline to match the cache policy, further shapes the step sizes. Outputs are generated images (1024×1024, 512×512, or 768×1024 on FLUX.1-dev) or videos (832×480, 81 frames on Wan2.1), with the total number of network forward passes strictly fixed at the chosen budget B (e.g., 8, 9, or 10 NFE out of 28 logical steps).

## Method

BudCache formulates step-level caching as a discrete combinatorial search: find a binary mask m ∈ {0,1}^K with exactly B ones (fresh-compute steps) that minimizes the expected output discrepancy between the cached sampler and the full solver. The search is solved offline via a two-phase hybrid optimizer—Simulated Annealing with Swap and Shift neighborhood operators for global exploration over the budget-constrained manifold, followed by deterministic Hill Climbing that greedily evaluates all one-step neighbors until a local optimum is reached. Under very tight budgets (5–7 NFE), a cache-aware schedule alignment stage treats the time discretization σ as learnable parameters and optimizes them via teacher-student distillation (minimizing MSE between the cached student output and the full-compute teacher output) to compensate for trajectory drift induced by stale cached predictions. The entire pipeline is training-free and plug-and-play: the searched mask and schedule are static artifacts that impose zero additional overhead at inference time, and the resulting policy generalizes across solvers, resolutions, CFG scales, and even across model sizes (e.g., a mask searched on Wan2.1-1.3B transfers to Wan2.1-14B).

## Results

On DrawBench with FLUX.1-dev, BudCache-base (10 NFE, 2.51× speedup) achieves LPIPS 0.1759, SSIM 0.8374, PSNR 24.90, and HPSv2.1 0.2940, substantially outperforming all baselines at the same latency budget. At the aggressive 3.00× tier (8 NFE), BudCache-ultra reaches LPIPS 0.2479 and PSNR 22.16, surpassing ERTACache (LPIPS 0.3208) by a wide margin. Notably, BudCache-ultra at 3.00× even exceeds TeaCache at the slower 2.51× setting (LPIPS 0.2479 vs. 0.3218), demonstrating that the optimized cache policy extracts more quality per NFE than heuristic thresholding. On Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B video generation, BudCache achieves PSNR 25.93 and LPIPS 0.1167 at 82 s latency, versus TeaCache's PSNR 21.52 and LPIPS 0.1929 at 100 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BudCache wins across all six baselines on every reconstruction metric at matched speedup tiers. At 2.51×, BudCache-base (LPIPS 0.1759) nearly halves the error of TeaCache (0.3218) and LeMiCa (0.3138), while also leading on SSIM (0.8374 vs. 0.7362/0.7397) and PSNR (24.90 vs. 18.40/18.41). At 2.74×, BudCache-fast (LPIPS 0.2020) outperforms MagCache (0.2498) by ~19% in LPIPS. At 3.00×, BudCache-ultra (LPIPS 0.2479) beats ERTACache (0.3208) by 23% in LPIPS and 8.8 dB in PSNR. Against TaylorSeer (2.82×, LPIPS 0.4490) and DiCache (2.66×, LPIPS 0.3286), BudCache-fast is both faster and substantially better in quality. A particularly striking result is that BudCache-ultra at 3.00× (LPIPS 0.2479) surpasses TeaCache at the slower 2.51× (0.3218), confirming that the budget-constrained search allocates NFE more effectively than per-step threshold heuristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant threshold-based paradigm in step-level diffusion caching (TeaCache, MagCache, LeMiCa, TaylorSeer, DiCache, ERTACache), where per-step reuse decisions are triggered by runtime error signals. Instead, it reframes caching as a discrete budget-constrained optimization problem solved offline via Simulated Annealing and Hill Climbing, yielding a static mask with guaranteed NFE. It also repurposes schedule optimization (related to AYS and LD3) not as a standalone discretization improvement but as a compensatory mechanism to correct trajectory drift induced by aggressive caching, a distinct motivation from prior schedule-search work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The deterministic-latency guarantee (fixed NFE regardless of input) addresses a real deployment bottleneck that threshold-based methods cannot solve, making BudCache directly applicable to latency-sensitive production systems. The strong transferability of searched masks across solvers, resolutions, CFG scales, and even model sizes (1.3B→14B video) suggests a single offline calibration can serve many inference configurations, reducing operational overhead. Open questions include whether schedule alignment can be made tractable for video (currently blocked by memory), whether joint cache+schedule optimization yields further gains, and why temporal redundancy persists in already-distilled few-step models.
