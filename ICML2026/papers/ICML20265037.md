## Problem

Existing SE(3)-equivariant trajectory prediction methods rely on deterministic regression, failing to capture the inherent stochasticity and multimodality of future motion. While flow matching offers a principled generative alternative, extending it to history-conditioned equivariant dynamics is non-trivial: conditioning a stochastic flow on geometric sequences without breaking rotational symmetry has remained an open challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265037/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes historical 3D node positions (N×T_h×3), a graph connectivity matrix (N×N), and SE(3)-invariant scalar attributes (e.g., atom types, N×T_h×C) as input, along with isotropic Gaussian noise to initialize the flow. It predicts a future trajectory of T_f frames (N×T_f×3) by numerically integrating a learned equivariant vector field over 10 Euler steps. Translation equivariance is handled by centering all coordinates relative to the spatiotemporal centroid, reducing the equivariance requirement to SO(3) rotation, with the absolute trajectory recovered by adding the centroid back at the end.

## Method

GSE-Flow formulates trajectory prediction as conditional flow matching with a data-prediction strategy: the equivariant network Φ predicts the clean target Y¹ from the noisy interpolated state Y^τ rather than directly regressing the velocity field. The architecture comprises three equivariant modules: a History Context Encoder (HCE) adapted from ESTAG that extracts per-frame geometric and invariant features; a Flow State Encoder (FSE) that unifies history and flow via Coherent Sequence Encoding (concatenating both along the temporal axis before equivariant message passing) and injects flow time through Time-Modulated Embedding (FiLM-style affine modulation of positions with velocity and of features with learned scalars); and a Trajectory Generation (TG) decoder that fuses context and flow via Geometry-Feature Tensorization (outer product of equivariant coordinates and invariant features into a 3D-dimensional rotation-covariant descriptor), invariant Context-Flow Attention (inner-product scoring over the tensorized space), and relative displacement aggregation anchored at the last observed frame. Theoretical SE(3)-equivariance of the entire pipeline is established via induction over the Euler integration steps.

## Results

On MD17, GSE-Flow achieves the lowest ADE on 6 of 8 molecular systems, with a ~22% ADE reduction on Aspirin (4.85 vs. 6.92 for the best baseline FAGNN). On MD22, it attains the best ADE across all seven macromolecule subsets, delivering up to ~25% relative improvement on the Double-walled nanotube (10.26 vs. 13.67 for ESTAG). On CMU MoCap, GSE-Flow achieves state-of-the-art ADE and FDE on Walk (2.59/4.25) and the lowest ADE on Basketball (32.39), while avoiding the OOM failures of PT-EvNet/FMN and the numerical divergence of EqMotion on large-scale systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSE-Flow outperforms all 11 baselines on MD17, achieving the best ADE on 6 of 8 molecules; the largest margin is on Aspirin where it reduces ADE by ~22% over the previous best (FAGNN, 6.92→4.85). On MD22, it achieves the best ADE on all seven subsets and best FDE on 6 of 7, with a ~25% relative gain on the Double-walled nanotube (10.26 vs. 13.67 for ESTAG), while PT-EvNet and FMN fail with OOM errors and EqMotion diverges numerically (543.58 ADE). On CMU MoCap Walk, GSE-Flow leads all methods in both ADE (2.59 vs. 3.03 for FMN) and FDE (4.25 vs. 4.42 for FAGNN); on Basketball it attains the lowest ADE (32.39) but trails EqMotion slightly on FDE (57.63 vs. 56.46). Among the deterministic equivariant baselines, ESTAG (the backbone adapted for HCE) is the closest competitor, while GeoTDM (a diffusion-based method) consistently underperforms, suggesting that the flow-matching formulation with equivariant conditioning is more effective than iterative denoising for this task. The generality experiments further show that integrating GSE-Flow into EGNN, EGHN, and EGNO backbones yields consistent ADE reductions of 15–40% across all three datasets, confirming that the flow-matching wrapper is architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines: SE(3)-equivariant graph neural networks for trajectory prediction (ESTAG, EqMotion, EGNO) and conditional flow matching for generative modeling (Lipman et al., 2023). Prior equivariant flow matching work (Kohler et al., Klein et al., Hassan et al.) addressed static molecular generation from invariant scalars, while prior flow-based trajectory predictors (TrajFlow, MoFlow) operated in absolute coordinates without symmetry guarantees. GSE-Flow departs from both by formulating history-conditioned trajectory forecasting as an equivariant generative flow, introducing Coherent Sequence Encoding to unify temporal streams and Geometry-Feature Tensorization to couple equivariant and invariant representations within the flow state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled bridge from deterministic equivariant regression to a continuous generative paradigm with formal SE(3)-equivariance guarantees (Theorem 3.3), and demonstrates that the flow-matching wrapper can be grafted onto arbitrary equivariant backbones for consistent gains. However, evaluation remains limited to point-wise displacement metrics (ADE/FDE) without multimodal coverage or likelihood diagnostics, and the inference cost (~53 ms vs. ~3–5 ms for single-shot predictors) is a practical trade-off. Open questions include whether the generative formulation captures meaningful trajectory multimodality beyond what deterministic regression already approximates, and how the framework scales to very large systems or real-time deployment.
