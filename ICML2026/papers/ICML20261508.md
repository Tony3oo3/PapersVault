## Problem

Training search-integrated reasoning agents via reinforcement learning suffers from a multi-scale credit assignment problem: trajectory-level rewards (e.g., final-answer correctness) provide no supervision over intermediate search decisions or reasoning steps, so efficient coherent trajectories receive the same credit as those that succeed only after redundant or poorly timed retrieval. This leads to error propagation where a single irrelevant early search query derails the entire subsequent reasoning chain, and existing rejection-sampling approaches are inefficient because they discard the whole trajectory rather than repairing the specific root cause.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language question and an external search engine (E5 retriever over the 2018 Wikipedia dump, returning top-3 passages) as input. It produces a structured reasoning trajectory that interleaves chain-of-thought reasoning, search queries enclosed in <search> tags, retrieved evidence in <information> tags, and a final answer in <answer> tags, following a strict Reasoning → Search Call → Answer template. During training, the trajectory is augmented with Meta-Refiner meta-actions (accept/reject decisions and cut-point selections); at inference, the Meta-Refiner is not invoked, so output is a standard single-pass multi-turn rollout with zero additional latency.

## Method

Search-R2 decomposes generation into an Actor policy (π_l) that produces initial reasoning trajectories with search tool calls, and a Meta-Refiner that performs targeted causal correction via two sub-components: a Discriminator (π_d) that gates on global trajectory coherence, and a Trimmer (π_h) that localizes the earliest flawed step. Upon rejection, the system applies a 'cut-and-regenerate' operation—preserving the valid prefix, truncating the flawed suffix, and regenerating from the error point—rather than discarding the entire trajectory. A hybrid reward couples binary outcome correctness (Exact Match) with a dense process reward measuring the information density of retrieved chunks, gated so that high-quality retrieval is only rewarded when the final answer is correct. Both the Actor and Meta-Refiner share the same LLM weights and are jointly optimized via GRPO, treating the full augmented trace (reasoning tokens plus meta-actions) as a single trajectory for group-relative advantage computation. Theoretically, the interaction is formalized as a smoothed mixture policy, and the expected performance gain is decomposed into three interpretable terms—Selection Precision, Trimming Skill, and Intervention Volume—providing conditions under which selective correction strictly outperforms baseline sampling.

## Results

On seven QA benchmarks (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA, HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, Musique, Bamboogle), Search-R2 achieves an average Exact Match of 50.8 (Qwen2.5-32B), 44.6 (Qwen3-8B), and 40.4 (Qwen2.5-7B), consistently outperforming Search-R1 at every scale by 4.6–5.2 EM points. Gains are most pronounced on multi-hop tasks: +11.4 EM on Bamboogle and +5.5 EM on 2WikiMultiHopQA at 32B. Notably, Search-R2 on Qwen2.5-7B (40.4) surpasses Search-R1 on the larger Qwen3-8B backbone (40.0), and training overhead is only +2.4–8.7% per step with zero additional inference latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Search-R2 dominates all baselines across every model scale. Against the closest competitor Search-R1, it gains +5.4 EM (7B), +4.6 EM (8B), and +5.2 EM (32B) on average, with the largest margins on multi-hop datasets (Bamboogle +10.2 to +11.4 EM; 2WikiMultiHopQA +5.5 to +6.1 EM). Search-R2 (7B, 40.4) exceeds Search-R1 on the larger Qwen3-8B backbone (40.0), demonstrating that the Actor-Refiner framework compensates for reduced parameter scale. Against retrieval-based baselines, Search-R2 (32B) leads RAG (30.4) by 20.4 EM, IRCoT (23.9) by 26.9 EM, and Search-o1 (20.6) by 30.2 EM. Against RL-based baselines, it outperforms Rejection Sampling (34.8) by 16.0 EM at 7B and R1-instruct (27.1) by 23.7 EM. The paper further shows that Search-R2 (n=5, 1 revision) beats Search-R1 with doubled rollouts (n=10) by 3.0 EM at 32B, confirming gains come from targeted correction rather than brute-force sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Search-R1 (Jin et al., 2025), which established the paradigm of training LLMs to interleave reasoning with search engine calls via GRPO. It departs from Search-R1's purely trajectory-level reward signal by introducing a Meta-Refiner (a Discriminator for global coherence checking and a Trimmer for local error localization) that performs targeted 'cut-and-regenerate' corrections, positioning itself against both naive rejection sampling (Ahn et al., 2024) and dense reward shaping approaches in multi-turn RL. The theoretical contribution formalizes the Actor-Refiner interaction as a smoothed mixture policy and decomposes the performance gain into Selection Precision, Trimming Skill, and Intervention Volume, providing an analytical framework absent from prior search-integrated RL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-scale credit assignment problem is a core bottleneck in training search-integrated agents, and the cut-and-regenerate mechanism offers a practical, low-overhead solution that could transfer to other agentic RL settings beyond QA. The finding that one targeted revision outperforms doubling the rollout budget is a useful efficiency result for practitioners. However, the process reward currently relies on an external LLM judge with access to the ground-truth answer (a form of hindsight bias), and evaluation is confined to a controlled E5 + Wikipedia retrieval setting; extending to answer-free rewards and noisier open-web environments with richer toolsets remains open. The Actor-Refiner decomposition is also orthogonal to the RL optimizer, suggesting composability with future agentic pipelines.
