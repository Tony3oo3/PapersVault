## Problem

Federated learning for whole slide image (WSI) classification is hindered by architectural heterogeneity across institutions (different MIL models and feature extractors), making conventional parameter sharing infeasible. Existing federated dataset distillation methods rely on mean-matching distribution alignment and aggressive compression, which oversimplify the multi-component patch feature distributions characteristic of WSIs and discard fine-grained diagnostic cues from small, diverse slide-level datasets.

## Input / Output

Each client processes 256×256 WSI patches (sampled at 40× for CAMELYON16/17, 10× for TCGA-IDH) through a locally chosen feature extractor (ResNet50, UNI, PhikonV2, or GPFM) to obtain d-dimensional patch embeddings, which are aggregated by a heterogeneous MIL model (CLAM, TransMIL, or ACMIL) into slide-level predictions. The distillation process produces one synthetic slide per real slide, each containing T=1000 learnable patch embeddings in R^d, along with slide labels; an optional FastGAN-based module can reconstruct these embeddings into 64×64 pseudo-patches for human inspection. The shared synthetic features are non-invertible and contain no raw pixel data.

## Method

FedHD performs local feature-level distillation by modeling each slide's patch feature distribution as a 16-component Gaussian mixture and aligning both the mean and covariance of each component between real and synthetic embeddings via a combined L2 and Frobenius-norm loss (Eq. 4). A one-to-one strategy generates one synthetic slide per real slide (rather than compressing many slides into few), preserving slide-level diagnostic diversity. During federation, each client uploads its synthetic slides to a server that redistributes cross-client synthetic data; a curriculum schedule delays incorporation of external synthetic features until epoch t0=30, at which point a Generalized Cross-Entropy loss (q=0.7) provides noise-robust supervision. An optional on-demand interpretation module (FastGAN) reconstructs synthetic embeddings into realistic pseudo-patches for qualitative inspection without adding training overhead. The entire pipeline is architecture-agnostic, operating on patch embeddings rather than model parameters or raw pixels.

## Results

Under heterogeneous local models (mixed feature extractors and MIL architectures), FedHD achieves average accuracy of 91.2% (MCC 80.6) on CAMELYON16, 82.7% (MCC 62.3) on CAMELYON17, and 84.8% (MCC 57.0) on TCGA-IDH, outperforming the strongest baseline FedWSIDD by +2.5%, +5.5%, and +4.3% accuracy respectively. On CAMELYON16, FedHD's C2 client (UNI+TransMIL) reaches 95.8% accuracy, and on TCGA-IDH the C8 client (PhikonV2+CLAM) reaches 88.4%. In the homogeneous setting (all CLAM), FedHD achieves 91.3% on CAMELYON16, 86.0% on CAMELYON17, and 89.2% on TCGA-IDH, surpassing FedMut by 3.1%, 7.0%, and 8.0% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedHD consistently outperforms all ten baselines across all three datasets. The closest competitor is FedWSIDD (Jin et al., MICCAI 2025), the most direct prior WSI dataset-distillation method; FedHD surpasses it by +2.5% accuracy on CAMELYON16 (91.2 vs. 88.7), +5.5% on CAMELYON17 (82.7 vs. 77.2), and +4.3% on TCGA-IDH (84.8 vs. 80.5), with statistically significant gains (p<0.05) on most individual centers. HistoFS (Raswa et al., CVPR 2025), which uses federated style transfer with ROI-preserving alignment, is the second-strongest baseline on CAMELYON17 and TCGA-IDH but trails FedHD by 5.4% and 3.9% accuracy respectively. Among standard FL methods evaluated in the homogeneous setting (Table 4), FedMut (Hu et al., AAAI 2024) is the strongest at 88.2% on CAMELYON16, yet FedHD still leads by 3.1%. In the distilled-data-only evaluation (Table 5), where a shared CLAM model is trained exclusively on synthetic samples, FedHD achieves 70.9% on CAMELYON16, 68.0% on CAMELYON17, and 72.1% on TCGA-IDH, outperforming FedDGM (Jia et al., ECCV 2024) by 4.7%, 2.0%, and 3.0% respectively, confirming that FedHD's GMM-aligned one-to-one features are the most informative. FedHE (Chan & Ngai, 2021) and DESA (Huang et al., ICML 2024) are the weakest baselines, trailing FedHD by 16% and 9% on CAMELYON16 accuracy, indicating that generic heterogeneous-FL strategies are poorly suited to WSI morphology.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedHD extends the federated dataset distillation (FedDD) line of work (FedWSIDD, FedDGM, DESA) by shifting from pixel-level to patch-embedding-level distillation and replacing the standard mean-matching distribution alignment with a Gaussian-mixture model that captures both mean and covariance of multi-component WSI feature distributions. It departs from the prevailing maximum-compression philosophy in dataset distillation by adopting a one-to-one strategy that generates a synthetic counterpart per real slide, motivated by the small dataset sizes and high intra-class diversity characteristic of WSI benchmarks. The curriculum-based federation strategy and the use of GCE loss for noise-robust training of cross-client synthetic features are novel design choices not present in prior FedDD or personalized FL methods for pathology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedHD addresses a practically important gap: real multi-institutional pathology deployments involve heterogeneous feature extractors and MIL architectures that make standard parameter-sharing FL infeasible, and the paper demonstrates a viable, architecture-agnostic alternative with strong empirical results across three benchmarks. The feature-level distillation also offers a significant training-efficiency advantage (~1 hour vs. 10–12 hours for pixel-level methods), making it more deployable in resource-constrained settings. Open questions include scalability beyond the single-round communication protocol tested here, the communication overhead of the one-to-one strategy (up to ~1.2 GB per client), whether the GMM assumption generalizes to other histopathology tasks, and whether more advanced generative models could improve the PPR module's visual fidelity beyond the current FastGAN-based reconstruction.
