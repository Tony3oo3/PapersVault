## Problem

Adapting Large Vision-Language Models to table reasoning currently requires either expensive supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning with scarce expert annotations, or external tools like visual editors that increase system complexity and latency. The paper asks whether LVLMs can be adapted to table reasoning with minimal annotation cost and no external tools, while preserving the model's intrinsic reasoning capability and generalizing to unseen table structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266233/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a table image (RGB) and a natural language question as input. During the DISCO alignment phase, the model is trained to produce anonymized structure representations (HTML/Markdown/LaTeX with cell contents replaced by a unified placeholder token) and semi-structured content descriptions in 'Row m Column n: [Content]' format. At inference, Table-GLS outputs a three-stage trace: a JSON reasoning plan with target row/column indices, a minimal extracted sub-table in the same semi-structured format, and a final JSON-formatted answer, with the original table image retained as auxiliary visual context throughout.

## Method

The framework has two components. DISCO performs disentangled structure-content alignment via LoRA fine-tuning on only 10K table images: structure alignment trains the model to predict anonymized table layouts (preserving row/column delimiters, headers, and span markers while replacing all cell values with a placeholder), while content alignment separately trains global descriptions (row/column counts with per-cell summaries) and local cell querying (retrieving content at specified row-column coordinates). Table-GLS is a training-free, tool-free inference framework that guides reasoning through three stages: (I) Global Structure Exploration, where the LVLM identifies task-relevant row and column indices from the table layout; (II) Self-refined Sub-table Extraction, where the model self-evaluates and revises the structural plan before extracting a minimal sub-table as verifiable evidence; and (III) Evidence-grounded Reasoning, where the final answer is derived from the extracted sub-table rather than the full table image, reducing spurious correlations. The key insight is that explicitly decoupling structure from content during alignment lets the LVLM transfer its existing semantic understanding to table content while learning layout in a data-efficient, targeted manner.

## Results

On the multimodal table reasoning benchmarks (Table 3), Qwen3-VL-8B with DISCO + Table-GLS achieves an average accuracy of 59.48%, improving over the vanilla Qwen3-VL-8B direct-answering baseline (50.31%) by 9.17 points, with the largest gains on out-of-domain tasks: AIT-QA rises from 71.04% to 76.71% and PubHealthTab from 44.18% to 77.14%. On the larger Qwen3-VL-32B, the combined framework reaches 71.84% average accuracy (Table 4), and applying Table-GLS alone to GPT-5.4-mini lifts its average from 56.64% to 66.16%. For table understanding (Table 1), DISCO with only 10K images matches or surpasses textual alignment trained on 97K images across most tasks, e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B TCL improves from 42.00% (no alignment) to 55.95% (DISCO) and OOD RCE from 66.61% to 71.48%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Table-LLaVA (7B/13B, SFT on 82K images), DISCO + Table-GLS on Qwen3-VL-8B substantially outperforms on all reasoning benchmarks, e.g., WTQ 57.11% vs. 18.43%/20.41% and AIT-QA 76.71% vs. 5.48%/6.06%, while using only 10K alignment images and no reasoning-specific fine-tuning. Compared to Table-R1 (GRPO, 20.6K), the proposed method achieves higher AIT-QA (76.71% vs. not reported) and InfoTabs (72.67% vs. 62.80%), though Table-R1 leads on HiTab (58.20% vs. 27.35%) and TAT-QA (48.06% vs. 40.54%). Against HIPPO (55.2K training data), DISCO + Table-GLS trails on TabFact (75.41% vs. 82.27%) and InfoTabs (72.67% vs. 75.74%) but surpasses it on AIT-QA (76.71% vs. 66.14%) and PubHealthTab (77.14% vs. 73.32%), achieving competitive overall accuracy (59.48% vs. 64.58%) with roughly 5× less training data and no reasoning-oriented optimization. On the closed-source side, Table-GLS applied to GPT-5.4-mini improves average accuracy from 56.64% to 66.16% (Table 4), a 9.5-point gain without any model modification. The paper also shows that DISCO alone (without Table-GLS) already boosts Qwen3-VL-8B direct answering from 50.31% to 56.22% average, confirming that the alignment and inference components contribute complementary gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms for adapting LVLMs to table reasoning: (1) supervised fine-tuning or RL-based optimization (Table-LLaVA, Table-R1, HIPPO, TURBO) that align table images with entangled textual serializations (HTML, Markdown, LaTeX), and (2) tool-augmented inference (REFOCUS) that relies on external visual editors or symbolic modules. DISCO introduces a conceptual shift by explicitly decoupling structure abstraction from semantic grounding during alignment, rather than learning both simultaneously from a single linearized target. Table-GLS then occupies a middle ground between training-heavy methods and tool-augmented pipelines, performing structure-guided reasoning purely through prompt-based multi-stage inference without additional fine-tuning or auxiliary tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's practical value lies in its data efficiency (10K table images matching or exceeding 97K-image textual alignment) and its tool-free, training-free inference pipeline, making it accessible for practitioners who lack large-scale table reasoning annotations or cannot deploy external toolchains. The finding that DISCO also improves general non-tabular VLM performance while textual alignment degrades it suggests disentangled alignment may be a broadly useful strategy beyond tables. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more irregular or implicitly structured visual layouts, how the multi-stage inference latency scales in latency-sensitive production settings, and whether the framework generalizes to other structured visual reasoning tasks beyond tables.
