## Problem

Existing KV-cache retrieval methods for long-context LLM inference suffer from distribution drift: centroids learned from prefill keys become stale as decoding keys accumulate, causing severe recall collapse over long generations. Additionally, CPU-side search and data transfer dominate end-to-end latency when the full KV cache is offloaded, and lightweight approximate retrieval sacrifices recall for speed. ParisKV addresses all three challenges simultaneously with a drift-robust, GPU-native retrieval framework.

## Input / Output

During prefill, the method consumes full-precision key vectors (D=256 dimensional) and produces compact per-key GPU-resident metadata: centroid IDs for collision-based voting, 4-bit direction codes (1-bit sign + 3-bit magnitude per coordinate), and per-subspace scaling factors, while asynchronously offloading full-precision KV pairs to CPU memory. During each decode step, a single query vector is processed to produce a top-k=100 set of selected KV pair indices, which are then fetched from CPU via UVA for attention computation. The final output is an approximate attention output computed over only the retrieved subset rather than the full cache.

## Method

ParisKV's key insight is transforming keys and queries onto a unit hypersphere via ℓ₂-normalization and a shared SRHT rotation, then using data-independent analytic centroids (sign-pattern vectors with 2^m entries per m-dimensional subspace) that are uniformly distributed on the sphere, ensuring any newly generated key remains close to at least one centroid regardless of distribution shift. The two-stage GPU-native pipeline first performs coarse candidate generation via multi-subspace collision counting with tier-weighted voting (L=6 tiers) to prune to a top-β fraction, then reranks candidates using RSQ-IP: a rotation-based scalar quantization inner-product estimator that corrects quantization bias via a per-subspace alignment factor α and precomputed scaling weights w. The theoretical foundation (Proposition 4.1) shows that Haar-random rotation induces Beta priors on subspace directions, enabling data-independent Lloyd-Max quantizer derivation. Full-precision KV pairs remain in CPU memory and are fetched on-demand via Unified Virtual Addressing, keeping the entire retrieval decision path GPU-native.

## Results

On long-generation reasoning, ParisKV achieves 72.22 (GPQA-Diamond pass@1), 92.80 (MATH500 pass@1), and 80.00 (AIME25 pass@8) on Qwen-3-4B, exceeding full attention (64.14/88.60/86.67) on GPQA and MATH500 while matching or exceeding full attention in 7 of 9 model-benchmark settings. On LongBench-V2, ParisKV scores 28.43 overall on DS-R1-Llama-8B (vs. 19.90 PQCache, 13.92 MagicPIG). In efficiency, ParisKV delivers up to 2.8× higher decoding throughput than full attention within its runnable range, and at 1M-token scale reduces decode latency by 17× (vs. MagicPIG) and 44× (vs. PQCache) while full attention OOMs at 384K even at batch size 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParisKV decisively outperforms the two primary KV-cache retrieval baselines: on AIME25 pass@8, it improves by +40 to +77 points over PQCache and +40 to +77 over MagicPIG across three model families (e.g., 80.00 vs. 3.33 PQCache and 6.67 MagicPIG on Qwen-3-4B). Against FlashAttention-2 (full attention), ParisKV matches or exceeds accuracy in 7/9 settings while delivering up to 2.8× higher throughput and remaining runnable at 1M tokens where full attention OOMs at 384K. On RULER (128K), ParisKV achieves 83.49 average, beating ShadowKV (80.29), Quest (79.34), SOCKET (78.92), Twilight (77.60), RetroInfer (77.45), MagicPIG (70.49), and PQCache (33.76). On LongBench-V2, ParisKV leads on DS-R1-Llama-8B (28.43 vs. 21.51 SOCKET, 20.72 Twilight, 23.51 Quest) and Qwen-3-8B (33.07 vs. 23.90 Quest, 20.32 Twilight), though RetroInfer is competitive on Qwen3-4B (23.69 vs. 24.60 ParisKV). In efficiency at 1024K context, ParisKV achieves 49 ms/step decode latency versus 2179 ms/step for PQCache (44.4×) and 830 ms/step for MagicPIG (16.9×), with all other baselines either OOM or incurring substantially higher per-step overheads.

## Relation to Prior Work

ParisKV builds directly on the subspace collision framework (Wei et al., PACMMOD 2025) for coarse candidate generation, adapting its tier-weighted multi-subspace voting to the KV-cache retrieval setting. It departs from prior KV-cache retrieval methods (MagicPIG, PQCache, ShadowKV) in a key conceptual way: rather than learning centroids from prefill keys (which become stale under decoding drift), ParisKV applies a shared SRHT rotation and ℓ₂-normalization to map keys onto a unit hypersphere where data-independent sign-pattern centroids are provably stable. The 4-bit RSQ-IP reranking estimator draws on RaBitQ's alignment-corrected inner-product approximation, instantiated subspace-wise with quantization levels derived from rotation-induced Beta priors rather than empirical calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ParisKV addresses the centroid-staleness problem that causes recall collapse in existing retrieval methods during long generation, and its GPU-native UVA-based design eliminates the CPU orchestration bottleneck that limits prior systems at scale. The practical impact is significant: it enables million-token inference where full attention simply runs out of memory, with 17×–44× decode latency reductions over the strongest baselines. Open questions include whether the 4-bit quantization and fixed top-100 budget generalize to larger models (70B+) or non-Transformer architectures, whether the SRHT rotation can be replaced by cheaper approximations without degrading the Beta-prior assumptions, and how the method interacts with emerging natively-sparse attention training (NSA, MoBA) that may reduce the need for post-hoc retrieval.
