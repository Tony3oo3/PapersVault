## Problem

Foundation-model-based monocular SLAM systems (e.g., VGGT) suffer from severe scale drift and geometric fragmentation on long sequences because motion-agnostic temporal chunking severs turning maneuvers and accumulates zero-motion drift during static intervals, while conventional feature-matching-based submap alignment is computationally expensive and introduces systematic bias from contextual asymmetry at submap boundaries. The quadratic self-attention complexity of Transformer backends further makes direct deployment on kilometer-scale trajectories infeasible.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a raw, uncalibrated monocular RGB video stream (no GT intrinsics required) and internally computes dense Farneback optical flow between consecutive frames for motion-state classification. It produces a globally consistent camera trajectory in Sim(3) space (scale-ambiguous, aligned post-hoc via Sim(3) to ground truth for evaluation), per-frame camera poses, dense 3D point maps, and estimated camera intrinsics. The trajectory is composed from optimized submap-level poses (K ≪ N nodes) rather than per-frame poses, and loop closures are handled by injecting historical keyframes as geometric anchors without reprocessing past submaps.

## Method

VGGT-Motion is a three-stage calibration-free SLAM pipeline built on the VGGT foundation model. First, a motion-aware submap construction module uses optical flow to classify each frame as Static, Turning, or Linear; static intervals are pruned to boundary frames only (suppressing zero-motion hallucination), dynamic frames are admitted via parallax-based keyframe selection, and continuous turning segments are encapsulated as indivisible submaps to preserve parallax consistency for monocular scale estimation. Second, an anchor-driven direct Sim(3) registration module selects a context-balanced anchor (a compact window at the midpoint of the overlap region) shared by two adjacent submaps; because both submaps infer geometry from the same anchor image, pixel indices establish deterministic 3D correspondences with O(1) association cost per pixel, eliminating descriptor extraction and nearest-neighbor search entirely. A Huber-robust Sim(3) fit over high-confidence, non-sky pixels yields the relative transform, verified by inlier ratio. Third, a lightweight submap-level pose graph optimization (K nodes, not N frames) minimizes Huber-weighted Sim(3) residuals via Levenberg-Marquardt on Lie groups, enforcing global consistency at linear complexity. The entire system is model-agnostic and can swap VGGT for other 3D foundation models (e.g., Depth Anything 3, Pi3) without architectural changes.

## Results

On the zero-shot long-sequence generalization benchmarks (4Seasons, Complex Urban, A2D2), VGGT-Motion achieves ATE of 12.22 m, 35.48 m, and 29.80 m respectively, representing an 84–96% reduction over VGGT-Long (280.25 m, 475.59 m, 182.93 m) and a corresponding drop in Translation Drift from 5.7–8.2% to 0.32–0.93%. On the standard KITTI benchmark, the method reports an average ATE of 24.17 m (13% improvement over VGGT-Long's 27.64 m), and on Waymo Open it achieves 1.59 m ATE (20% improvement over VGGT-Long's 2.00 m). The system also delivers an 18–36× speedup in total processing time over VGGT-Long on long-sequence datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGGT-Motion outperforms VGGT-Long (the primary SOTA) on every evaluated dataset: 84–96% lower ATE on the zero-shot benchmarks (e.g., 12.22 m vs. 280.25 m on 4Seasons), 13% lower on KITTI (24.17 m vs. 27.64 m), and 20% lower on Waymo (1.59 m vs. 2.00 m). Against VGGT-SLAM, the improvement is even more pronounced on long sequences (12.22 m vs. 168.04 m on 4Seasons; 35.48 m vs. 444.63 m on Complex Urban). The remaining foundation-model baselines—MASt3R-SLAM, CUT3R, Fast3R, and VGGT—fail entirely on the long-sequence benchmarks, reporting Tracking-Lost or Out-of-Memory errors on nearly all sequences, underscoring the scalability gap that VGGT-Motion addresses. Among calibrated baselines, ORB-SLAM2 (with loop closure) achieves 9.46 m average ATE on KITTI but requires ground-truth intrinsics; DROID-SLAM and DPVO are competitive on short KITTI sequences but degrade sharply on longer ones (e.g., DROID-SLAM reaches 100.28 m average ATE). On the TUM-Mono handheld dataset, VGGT-Motion achieves 2.41 m average ATE versus 4.86 m for VGGT-Long and 23.91 m for VGGT-SLAM, confirming that the motion-aware partitioning generalizes beyond driving scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VGGT-Long (Deng et al., 2025) and VGGT-SLAM (Maggio et al., 2025) line of submap-based, calibration-free monocular SLAM built on the VGGT foundation model. It departs from their motion-agnostic, fixed-interval partitioning by introducing optical-flow-driven motion state classification (static/turning/linear) to guide adaptive submap construction, and replaces conventional descriptor-based or dense-overlap registration with a pixel-indexed, search-free Sim(3) alignment using context-balanced anchors. The pose graph backend follows standard Sim(3) optimization but operates at the submap level for linear complexity, building on the SL(4) manifold formulation of VGGT-SLAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 84–96% ATE reduction and 18–36× speedup over VGGT-Long on kilometer-scale zero-shot benchmarks (4Seasons, Complex Urban, A2D2) demonstrate that motion-aware data organization can be as important as backend optimization for long-range consistency. The model-agnostic design—validated by swapping VGGT for DepthAnything 3 or Pi3 without architectural changes—positions the framework to benefit automatically as foundation models improve. Key open questions remain: the system still assumes mostly rigid scenes, requires offline processing, and needs IMU fusion for bounded metric error in large-scale environments; extending to real-time deployment on edge hardware and handling highly dynamic scenes with moving objects are natural next steps.
