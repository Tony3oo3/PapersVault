## Problem

Transformer-based language models rely on multi-head attention and feed-forward networks whose parameter counts scale quadratically with embedding dimension, making them expensive to pre-train and deploy. Continued fraction architectures (CoFrNets) were previously shown to have universal approximation properties for supervised tasks, but it remained unclear whether they could be adapted for causal sequence generation, which requires multi-dimensional outputs, richer function learning, and strictly unidirectional information flow. Additionally, the 1/x nonlinearity inherent in continued fractions is computationally expensive, requiring d divisions per ladder in both forward and backward passes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes token sequences (text) as input, where each token is mapped to a p-dimensional embedding vector that serves as the input x to the continued fraction ladders. For the attention-replacement architectures, the input tensor is either transposed across the embedding and sequence-length dimensions (CAttnU) or kept in standard layout (CAttnM), with upper-triangular masking or causal softmax enforcing that token i only attends to tokens 1 through i-1. The output is a standard next-token probability distribution over the vocabulary, enabling autoregressive generation; the architectures are drop-in replacements within existing Transformer training and inference pipelines requiring no changes to the surrounding framework.

## Method

CoFrGeNet replaces Transformer attention and FFN layers with ensembles of continued fraction ladders whose partial denominators are affine functions of the input embedding. Two attention variants are proposed: CAttnU uses univariate ladders over transposed dimensions with upper-triangular linear layers to enforce causality, then element-wise multiplies two ensemble outputs to create cross-terms; CAttnM uses p-variate ladders mapped to sequence-length-sized attention weights via causal softmax applied to the value matrix. The FFN replacement (Cffn) uses p-variate ladders in two ensembles with even-odd depth pairing, followed by a linear projection and element-wise multiplication for expressivity. The key efficiency gain comes from a continuant-based implementation: instead of computing d divisions per ladder, all continuants K_0 through K_d are computed via O(d) multiplications and additions, and both the forward value and the gradient (given by closed-form ratios of continuants, Proposition 4.1) require only a single division by K_d. A dyadic training schedule further stabilizes optimization by freezing deeper ladder parameters initially and unfreezing depth-i parameters only after t/2^i iterations have elapsed.

## Results

On GLUE (OWT pre-training), CoFrGeNet-F (972M) outperforms GPT2-xl (1.5B) on 6 of 8 tasks, e.g., MNLI 87.24 vs 86.89, QQP 89.95 vs 88.93, SST2 94.12 vs 93.56, while the full CoFrGeNet (790M) also matches or exceeds the original on most tasks. On perplexity, CoFrGeNet-F achieves 17.09 on Wikitext2 vs 18.30 for GPT2-xl, and 8.15 on Lambada vs 8.66. On Llama-3.2B (docling pre-training), CoFrGeNet-F (1.9B) matches or beats Llama (3.2B) on most zero-shot tasks (e.g., OpenBookQA .292 vs .282, PIQA .764 vs .76), and training throughput improves from 235B to 288B tokens/day. Inference time for the continuant-based implementation is ~629μs vs 644μs for GPT2-xl, while the non-continuant baseline (C_B) is ~9x slower at 5899μs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT2-xl (1.5B), all CoFrGeNet variants (790M–1.21B) match or exceed the original on most GLUE tasks and perplexity benchmarks, with CoFrGeNet-F showing the strongest gains (e.g., +1.02 on QQP, +0.56 on SST2) despite having only ~65% of the parameters. Against Synthesizer-D (1.2B) and Sparse Attention (1.21B), CoFrGeNet variants achieve clear and consistent wins across all eight GLUE tasks (e.g., CoFrGeNet-F MNLI 87.24 vs 84.93/85.27) and all perplexity metrics (Wikitext2 17.09 vs 19.35/18.78). Against Mamba-2 (3.2B) on Llama-based zero-shot tasks, results are mixed: Mamba-2 leads on OpenBookQA (.324 vs .283–.304) and BoolQ (.655 vs .633–.659), while CoFrGeNet-F matches or slightly exceeds on PIQA (.764 vs .761) and Lambada (.581 vs .548), and the full CoFrGeNet (1.7B) is competitive on most tasks despite having roughly half the parameters of Mamba-2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the supervised CoFrNet architecture (Puri et al., 2021) from classification to causal sequence generation, designing continued-fraction-based replacements for both multi-head attention and feed-forward networks in Transformer blocks. The key methodological departure is the use of continuant polynomials to compute continued fractions and their gradients in a single division rather than d divisions per ladder, which the original CoFrNet work only exploited for local feature explanations. The paper also introduces a dyadic (progressive-depth) training schedule absent from prior CoFrNet work, and positions its contributions against efficient-attention alternatives (Synthesizer, Sparse Attention) and state-space models (Mamba) as a fundamentally different function class rather than a parameterization trick on existing attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a continued-fraction function class with 1/x nonlinearity can match or slightly exceed Transformer performance at 2/3 to 1/2 the parameter count is a meaningful data point for architecture diversity beyond the attention+FFN paradigm. However, the division-based nonlinearity remains a hardware disadvantage (acknowledged by the authors), and the paper identifies open questions around custom Triton kernels, FPGA offloading of divisions, and the absence of implicit safety guards against hallucination and adversarial attacks. Extending CoFrNet components to other backbones (Mamba, MoE) and designing hardware-aware variants are natural next steps that could determine whether this function class becomes practically competitive at scale.
