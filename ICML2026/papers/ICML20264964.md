## Problem

The target network update frequency (TUF) in Q-learning and DQN is a critical stabilization mechanism, yet its selection is treated as an ad-hoc hyperparameter with no principled theoretical guidance. This paper asks whether convergence can be provably improved by systematically varying the temporal separation between current Q-estimates and bootstrap targets, and shows that constant TUF schedules are suboptimal.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a finite MDP (tabular state-action pairs in GridWorld with 52 pairs, or RGB frames in Lunar Lander) with a discount factor γ and an initial Q-matrix. The key design input is the sequence of cycle lengths K_n (equivalently, the TUF schedule 1/K_n), which the theory prescribes to grow geometrically at rate γ^{-2/3}n. The output is the learned action-value function Q(s,a) with a finite-sample guarantee: accuracy ε is achieved in O((1−γ)^{-5}ξ^{-2}ε^{-2}) samples under the optimal schedule, where ξ is the minimum state-action visitation probability.

## Method

The paper reframes periodic Q-learning as a nested optimization: an outer loop performs Bellman value iteration Q_{n+1} = T*Q_n, while an inner loop approximates T*Q_n via K_n steps of asynchronous SGD on the mean-squared Bellman error with a frozen target. The inner-loop analysis yields a non-asymptotic bound e_n^{(k)} ≤ (c₁E_n² + c₂)/(k + 2/ξ) under persistent exploration, and the outer loop satisfies an approximate contraction E_{n+1} ≤ γE_n + (√c₁ E_n + √c₂)/√K_n. The key insight is that early outer iterations (large E_n) are dominated by Bellman contraction and tolerate coarse approximation, while late iterations require increasingly accurate Bellman estimates to avoid error saturation. Solving the resulting constrained cost-minimization problem yields the optimal schedule K_j ∝ μ^{2/3}(N−1−j) with μ = (1+γ)/2, i.e., cycle lengths grow geometrically at rate γ^{-2/3}n, removing the logarithmic ε-dependence present in any fixed-TUF design and producing the Increasing-Cycle Q-Learning (ICQL) algorithm.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1.1) shows that geometrically decreasing TUF (cycle length growing as γ^{-2/3}n) achieves a sample complexity of O(1/(ξ²(1−γ)⁵ε²)), removing the logarithmic ε-dependence present in the best fixed-TUF bound O(log((1−γ)⁻¹ε⁻¹)/(ξ²(1−γ)⁴ε²·log(2(1+γ)⁻¹))). Empirically, on a 4×4 stochastic GridWorld (52 state-action pairs, γ=0.95), ICQL with decreasing TUF reaches the lowest final bias across all initial TUF settings, while fixed-TUF Q-learning with TUF=10⁻³ fails to converge and TUF=10⁻⁴ saturates at a large error. On Lunar Lander with DQN, ICDQN (decreasing TUF) stabilizes training substantially compared to constant-TUF DQN, particularly when the initial TUF is high.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard Q-learning (TUF=1, i.e., no target freezing), ICQL with decreasing TUF achieves substantially lower final bias on the GridWorld, particularly at γ=0.95 where standard Q-learning suffers from overestimation and fails to converge. Against DQN with fixed constant TUFs on Lunar Lander, ICDQN (decreasing TUF) outperforms all fixed-TUF variants: high TUFs (e.g., 1/1000) fail entirely, medium TUFs learn quickly but saturate, while ICDQN continues reducing error without plateau. Against periodic Q-learning with fixed cycle lengths K∈{1000, 10000, 100000} on GridWorld, ICQL avoids the fundamental trade-off where high fixed TUFs cause early plateaus (coarse Bellman approximation) and low fixed TUFs converge slowly (overly accurate inner loops waste computation early). The paper's theoretical analysis shows the fixed-TUF approach incurs an unavoidable logarithmic overhead in sample complexity that is entirely eliminated by the geometrically decreasing schedule, and this is confirmed empirically by the staircase-like bias reductions in ICQL that fixed schedules cannot replicate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the target network freezing mechanism from DQN (Mnih et al., 2015) as a nested multilevel optimization problem, where the outer loop performs value iteration and the inner loop approximates the Bellman optimality operator via SGD. It builds directly on the periodic Q-learning analysis of Lee & He (2020a), which studied fixed target update intervals, and on the multilevel optimization framework of Weissmann et al. (2022) for inverse problems, adapting its proof techniques to derive an explicit optimal TUF schedule. The inner-loop SGD analysis is tailored to the asynchronous tabular setting, going beyond the general ADP error propagation bounds of Bertsekas & Tsitsiklis (1996) and Munos (2005) by exploiting the specific structure of stochastic gradient descent on the mean-squared Bellman error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, theory-driven schedule for a hyperparameter (target update frequency) that has been treated as arbitrary in both theory and practice for over a decade. The geometrically decreasing TUF schedule is simple to implement and removes a logarithmic sample-complexity overhead, while empirically stabilizing training in a way that fixed TUFs cannot. Key limitations include the restriction to tabular settings with SGD (not ADAM), worst-case bounds that may be loose in practice, and experiments limited to small-scale environments. Open questions include extending the analysis to adaptive optimizers, other ADP algorithms with target freezing, and accuracy-triggered (rather than schedule-based) target updates, which the authors outline but do not fully develop.
