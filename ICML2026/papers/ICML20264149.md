## Problem

Global ocean modeling faces a fundamental trade-off: physics-based numerical solvers enforce conservation laws but are computationally prohibitive at high resolution, while pure deep learning emulators achieve speed but violate mass/momentum conservation and drift during long-term rollouts. Additionally, AI-driven regional ocean downscaling—where physical consistency is paramount—remains largely unexplored. The paper asks whether a lightweight differentiable physical core can serve as a stable dynamical skeleton while a neural network corrects unresolved subgrid-scale dynamics.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 93-channel ocean state tensor (zonal/meridional velocity, temperature, salinity across 23 vertical layers to ~644 m, plus sea surface height) at 0.5° or 0.25° global resolution, driven by 3-channel atmospheric forcing (10 m winds, 2 m air temperature). A key design detail is the dual-path feeding: the physical solver receives unnormalized physical magnitudes to preserve equation validity, while the neural corrector receives z-score-normalized inputs. For regional downscaling, the model additionally ingests an interpolated coarse global state as boundary context, and for operational forecasting the atmospheric forcing is replaced by 10-day rollouts from the FuXi-2.0 weather model rather than ERA5 reanalysis.

## Method

HybridOM decomposes ocean dynamics into a differentiable physical skeleton and a neural residual corrector, integrated via a posteriori trajectory optimization (K=5-day unrolls with backpropagation through the solver). The physical skeleton uses a quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity formulation for momentum (filtering fast gravity waves) and conservative flux-divergence form for tracers on a staggered Arakawa C-grid with first-order upwind advection, with a learnable neural inverse-Laplacian operator recovering velocities from evolved PV. The neural flesh (ODM) is a U-shaped encoder-decoder whose bottleneck employs Dual-Scale Ocean Attention, splitting attention heads into local window-based (M×M) and global grid-stride branches to simultaneously resolve submesoscale turbulence and basin-scale teleconnections at linear complexity. For regional downscaling, a Differentiable Flux Gating mechanism adaptively fuses fine-grid and coarse-grid thermodynamic fluxes via a softmax-weighted selector plus a deep residual refiner, while a Neural Information Injection module concatenates the interpolated coarse state into the neural corrector to capture subgrid kinematic effects.

## Results

On the GLORYS12V1 global simulation benchmark (0.5° resolution, 60-day integration), HybridOM achieves the lowest weighted MSE across all four prognostic variables at both 15-day and 40-day horizons: wMSE_T = 0.2392/0.4958, wMSE_S = 0.0155/0.0291, wMSE_U = 0.0064/0.0121, wMSE_V = 0.0068/0.0126 (15d/40d), outperforming the nearest competitor NeuralOM by 13–17% on temperature and 17–19% on velocity. For extreme-event detection (Marine Heatwaves via SSTA), HybridOM attains the highest CSI scores of 0.6701/0.6620/0.6520 at the 90%/92.5%/95% thresholds. In operational 10-day forecasting coupled with FuXi-2.0 (OceanBench protocol), HybridOM-0.25 leads on short-range (≤3-day) wRMSE while HybridOM-0.5 is more effective at longer horizons, and both variants achieve competitive or best Lagrangian Trajectory Error across four ocean basins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HybridOM outperforms all eight baselines on every wMSE metric at both 15-day and 40-day horizons. The closest competitor is NeuralOM (same group's pure-neural ocean model), which HybridOM beats by ~13% on wMSE_T (0.2392 vs. 0.2768 at 15d) and ~17% on wMSE_U (0.0064 vs. 0.0077 at 15d); the gap widens at 40 days (e.g., wMSE_T: 0.4958 vs. 0.5284). WenHai (Cui et al., 2025) is second-best on CSI for extreme SSTA events (0.6419 at 90%) but trails HybridOM by ~3 points (0.6701). General-purpose architectures (U-Net, DiT, GraphCast, OneForecast) are substantially weaker on ocean-specific metrics, with GraphCast and OneForecast showing particularly large 40-day wMSE_S degradation (0.0857 and 0.0992 vs. HybridOM's 0.0291), highlighting the importance of physical constraints for long-term ocean stability. In the operational forecasting task (Figure 4), HybridOM-0.25 achieves the lowest wRMSE at 1–3 day lead times while HybridOM-0.5 leads at 7–9 days, both outperforming GLONET, WenHai, and XiHe on most surface and 220 m metrics. For regional downscaling (Figure 5), HybridOM with Flux Gating + Neural Information Injection surpasses both NNG (OneForecast) and GraphEFM in long-term RMSE stability in the North Pacific, with the DFG mechanism showing the largest gains in the 10–30 day window.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'gray-box' paradigm of coupling differentiable numerical solvers with neural networks—previously demonstrated in atmospheric science (Kochkov et al., 2024; Gelbrecht et al., 2025) and CFD (Frezat et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2024)—to the ocean domain, where the authors argue these methods cannot be directly transferred due to distinct fluid properties and governing equations. It departs from pure data-driven ocean emulators (WenHai, NeuralOM, XiHe, GLONET) by embedding a lightweight quasi-geostrophic physical core as a differentiable skeleton rather than treating the entire state transition as a black-box regression. The flux-gating downscaling mechanism is positioned as filling a gap the authors identify as 'a largely unexplored frontier' in AI-driven regional ocean modeling, drawing conceptual inspiration from nested-grid approaches in OneForecast but operating explicitly in physical flux space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HybridOM offers a practically significant speedup (211–271× over GPU-accelerated POM) while preserving geostrophic balance and heat-content consistency, making high-frequency operational ocean forecasting computationally feasible without sacrificing physical fidelity. The flux-gating downscaling provides a template for cross-scale ocean modeling that could extend to other geophysical fluid systems. Open questions include whether the QG approximation remains adequate for strongly ageostrophic regimes (e.g., equatorial currents, shallow coastal seas), how the method scales beyond 0.25° resolution given the neural-component bottleneck, and whether the FuXi-2.0 coupling—where the weather model is not jointly trained—limits the system's skill under large atmospheric forcing errors.
