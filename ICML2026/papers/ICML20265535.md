## Problem

In source-free Cross-Domain Few-Shot Learning with CLIP, the prevailing fine-tuning paradigm uniformly aligns all image patch tokens with their corresponding text embeddings. This paper identifies that under large domain shifts and scarce target-domain data, forcing alignment of low-similarity 'tail tokens' causes overfitting to the few training samples, and that actively breaking this alignment consistently improves target-domain performance — a counterintuitive finding that challenges the universal alignment assumption.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images processed through a frozen CLIP ViT backbone (divided into L patch tokens per layer), N class text prompts encoded by CLIP's text encoder and projected into the visual token space, and a K-shot support set per class for episodic fine-tuning. At each transformer layer, per-token cosine similarities to all N class text embeddings are computed to dynamically partition tokens into head and tail subsets. The output is a class label determined by cosine similarity between the final [CLS] token and the N text embeddings, evaluated under N-way K-shot (N=5, K∈{1,5}) episodic protocols.

## Method

ATHA (Adaptive Tail-Head Alignment) replaces uniform vision-text alignment with an asymmetric, layer-wise token modulation applied inside each ViT block. For each patch token, the maximum cosine similarity to any of the N projected class text embeddings is computed; the top-ρ fraction (head tokens) are pulled toward their most similar text embedding via a learnable positive addition α^(l)·t_{j+}, while the bottom-γ fraction (tail tokens) are pushed away from their least similar text embedding via a learnable negative subtraction β^(l)·t_{j−}. All α^(l) and β^(l) parameters are optimized end-to-end with the standard cross-entropy classification loss, allowing the model to automatically learn per-layer alignment strengths. The mechanism is applied on top of LoRA fine-tuning of CLIP-ViT/B-16 in a source-free setting, with head and tail ratios set to 0.1 and α initialized at 0.8 only in layer 8 while β is initialized at 0.01 across all layers.

## Results

On the four CDFSL benchmarks (CropDiseases, EuroSAT, ISIC2018, ChestX) under 5-way 1-shot and 5-shot settings, CLIP-LoRA + ATHA achieves average accuracies of 58.35% and 68.53%, respectively, outperforming all prior source-free and source-accessible methods. The largest gains appear on ISIC2018 (+3.63% over CLIP-LoRA in 1-shot; +5.32% in 5-shot) and ChestX (+2.27% in 1-shot; +2.54% in 5-shot), confirming robustness under extreme domain shift. ATHA also surpasses the best source-accessible method (REAP, 56.04% avg in 1-shot) by 2.31 points while operating in the stricter source-free setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct baseline CLIP-LoRA (Zanella & Ben Ayed, 2024), ATHA improves average accuracy by +2.43% (1-shot: 58.35 vs 55.92) and +2.54% (5-shot: 68.53 vs 65.99), with the largest per-dataset gain on ISIC2018 (+3.63% / +5.32%). ATHA also surpasses the strongest source-accessible methods: REAP (56.04% avg, 1-shot) by 2.31 points and ReCIT (68.06% avg, 5-shot) by 0.47 points, despite operating under the stricter source-free constraint. Compared to other source-free baselines, ATHA outperforms IM-DCL by +2.44% (1-shot) and +1.81% (5-shot), and StepSTP by +5.67% (1-shot) and +2.56% (5-shot). Notably, existing cross-modal alignment methods PACL and SPARC, when added to the CLIP-LoRA baseline, actually degrade performance below the baseline (65.85% and 65.55% vs 65.99% in 5-shot), whereas ATHA lifts it to 68.53%, confirming that uniform alignment is counterproductive under domain shift while selective tail-token repulsion is beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's vision-language framework and the source-free cross-domain few-shot learning (SF-CDFSL) setting established by prior work (Xu et al., 2024a,b; Zanella & Ben Ayed, 2024). It departs from the prevailing fine-tuning paradigm that uniformly strengthens vision-text alignment for all image patch tokens, and also from existing cross-modal alignment methods (PACL, SPARC) that assume comprehensive token-text correspondence universally improves representation. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an asymmetric, token-selective alignment policy (ATHA) that simultaneously pulls head tokens closer to and pushes tail tokens away from text embeddings, motivated by an overfitting analysis via CKA similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that breaking alignment for low-similarity tokens improves cross-domain few-shot performance challenges a foundational assumption in VLM fine-tuning and provides a simple, interpretable mechanism (direct feature modulation via learnable add/subtract of text embeddings) that generalizes across backbones (CLIP, SigLIP, PEcore) and fine-tuning frameworks (LoRA, CoOp, MaPLe). Limitations include evaluation on only four target domains, fixed head/tail ratios, and reliance on CLIP-family architectures; the paper itself notes future work on adaptive per-episode token selection and extension beyond CLIP. An open question is whether the overfitting mechanism identified here generalizes to other scarce-data adaptation settings beyond CDFSL.
