## Problem

Audio function calling introduces error channels absent from text-only tool use—ASR mistakes under noise, accent and speaking-rate variation, disfluencies, homophones, and the need for clarification loops—yet no existing benchmark systematically evaluates how these factors degrade end-to-end tool-calling accuracy. Prior work either benchmarks text-only tool invocation or measures speech perception in isolation (e.g., WER), leaving it impossible to diagnose whether a failure stems from perception, reasoning, or formatting.

## Input / Output

BFCL True Audio feeds raw synthetic speech (clean or noise-augmented) directly to end-to-end audio models, while BFCL Text Audio provides transcripts from three independent ASR engines (Deepgram nova-2, OpenAI gpt-4o-transcribe, ElevenLabs scribe_v1) to pipelined text-only models. Each task pairs the utterance with a function schema specifying callable functions, parameter types, and required arguments. Outputs are scored via AST-based matching for single-turn calls (function-name match plus type-aware argument comparison with case/whitespace normalization and enforced list order) and via state-based plus response-based verification for multi-turn interactions; whitelisted clarification turns are permitted but excluded from scoring.

## Method

The benchmark is built through a four-stage controllable pipeline: (1) natural paraphrasing of BFCL text queries into colloquial speech, (2) LLM-controlled injection of 23 speech features (disfluencies, self-corrections, ellipsis, filepath verbalization, etc.) selected with ablation-informed weighting to bias toward features that most degrade tool-calling accuracy, (3) rendering via 225 distinct voice clones generated from Mozilla Common Voice recordings through ElevenLabs Professional Voice Cloning, and (4) real-world acoustic augmentation mixing MUSAN/CHiME-5 noise at random SNRs, competing speech (double-talk), network impairments (10–40 ms packet drops/truncation), and device/room effects (reverb, clipping, gain shifts). Evaluation uses AST substring matching for single-turn calls and combined state/response metrics for multi-turn, with an LLM judge validating only clarification requests (not scoring tool calls), enabling fully automatic, judge-free grading. The paper further contributes a six-mode failure taxonomy (intent blending, premature execution, parameter distortion, clarification misfires, conversational drift, named-entity errors) and ablation studies isolating the causal impact of individual speech features and noise types on downstream accuracy.

## Results

On BFCL Audio, GPT-4o-audio achieves the highest overall score of 60.4% on clean audio, followed by GPT-4o-audio (Text) at 58.6% and Qwen3-Omni-Flash (Clean Audio) at 55.8%. Background noise causes a sharp 9.5-point drop for GPT-4o-audio (60.4% → 50.9%), and the weakest model (GPT-4o-mini-audio, Noisy Audio) falls to 40.3%. Multi-turn performance is substantially lower across all models (e.g., GPT-4o-audio scores 47.5% on Base multi-turn vs. 60.4% overall), and hallucination measures (Relevance/Irrelevance) reveal that GPT-5 (Text) is the most conservative at 95.0% irrelevance but the least accurate at 41.0% overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, BFCL Audio evaluates 19 model configurations across two deployment paradigms: end-to-end audio models (GPT-4o-audio, Qwen3-Omni-Flash, Gemini-2.5-Flash/Pro, GPT-4o-mini-audio) and pipelined text models (GPT-4o Text, GLM-4.5, Claude-Opus-4.1, Gemini-2.5-Pro Text, xLAM-20b, Grok, Qwen3-235B, GPT-5). End-to-end models on clean audio outperform their pipelined text counterparts by 1.8–4.6 points (e.g., GPT-4o-audio 60.4% vs. GPT-4o Text 58.6%; Qwen3-Omni-Flash 55.8% vs. 55.1%), but this advantage is brittle: under noisy audio, GPT-4o-audio drops to 50.9% and GPT-4o-mini-audio to 40.3%, falling below several steady pipelined baselines. Multi-turn performance is the weakest dimension for all models, with GPT-4o-audio scoring only 47.5% on Base multi-turn and GPT-5 (Text) a mere 6.5%, indicating that clarification-driven interaction remains a major unsolved challenge. On hallucination measures, GPT-5 (Text) is the most conservative (95.0% irrelevance) but least accurate (41.0% overall), while GPT-4o-audio (Text) and xLAM-20b-fc-r achieve the best relevance scores (77.8%) at the cost of higher overall accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines: text-only tool-calling benchmarks (BFCL, τ-Bench, ToolBench), audio perception/robustness benchmarks (CHiME, VOiCES, MUSAN), and the nascent audio tool-use evaluations (CAVA, AU-Harness). It departs from prior work by shifting the evaluation target from word error rate or function-name matching to full argument-level tool execution under controlled, ablatable acoustic perturbations, and by explicitly modeling clarification-driven multi-turn repair that spoken interaction demands. The controllable synthesis/augmentation pipeline repurposes existing resources (ElevenLabs voice cloning, MUSAN/CHiME-5 noise, Mozilla Common Voice) for a new diagnostic purpose rather than introducing new speech-processing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BFCL Audio provides the first systematic, scalable diagnostic for audio function calling, enabling practitioners to pinpoint whether failures stem from perception, reasoning, or formatting—something no prior benchmark supports. The finding that competing speech (42% intent preservation) and discourse-altering disfluencies are the dominant bottlenecks, while device artifacts and WER are largely irrelevant to tool-use success, redirects robustness engineering toward interference handling and uncertainty-aware clarification rather than generic noise suppression. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic (voice-cloned) audio and simulated perturbations, which may not capture the full spontaneity and environmental complexity of real deployments; constructing naturalistic corpora with authentic recordings is identified as an important next step. Open questions include how to train models that reliably trigger clarification without over-asking, and whether the E2E-vs-pipelined robustness trade-off persists with larger or better-conditioned audio models.
