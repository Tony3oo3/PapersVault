## Problem

Offline diffusion-based planners generate state-action trajectories that are task-aligned but dynamically infeasible, because they sample directly in data space without enforcing system transition consistency. Existing remedies—post-hoc forward-model filtering or inverse dynamics inference—either do not influence action proposals or introduce brittle single-step mappings. The paper asks how to enforce dynamics feasibility *during* the generative sampling process itself while preserving task intent.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an initial state x₀, a conditioning vector y(τ) encoding task objectives (target return, goal position, or cost budget), and an offline dataset of state-action trajectories. It produces a single selected state-action trajectory τ = (x₁:ₕ, u₀:ₕ₋₁) of horizon H (16–64 steps depending on the task) by sampling N candidates (1–64) through K diffusion steps (10–100), then ranking them with learned reward and cost MLPs under a remaining cost budget. The planner and dynamics models are both temporal U-Nets operating on continuous state-action vectors; for visual inputs, a residual convolutional autoencoder first maps 64×64 grayscale frames (stacked over 4 frames) to a 32-dim latent space where diffusion is performed.

## Method

MPDiffuser composes three independently trained modules: a conditional diffusion planner over joint state-action trajectories, a conditional diffusion dynamics model over states only (treating the action sequence as a pure input to dedicate capacity to transition structure), and a lightweight ranker. The key mechanism is an alternating reverse-diffusion scheme: at every denoising step k, the dynamics model first refines states conditioned on the current noisy actions (projecting toward the feasible-transition manifold), and then the planner jointly denoises states and actions to restore task structure and dataset consistency. Both models use classifier-free guidance with the same conditioning vector, and the alternation is theoretically justified as an operator-splitting approximation to sampling from the exponentially tilted distribution q*(τ|x₀) ∝ p_pl(τ|x₀)·p_dyn(τ|x₀)^λ. Because the dynamics model is trained separately, it can exploit low-quality or random-action data to improve feasibility without degrading the planner, and can be fine-tuned in isolation when system dynamics change.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark (9 tasks across Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Kitchen), MPDiffuser achieves an average normalized return of 84.9, outperforming all prior diffusion-based methods: Decision Diffuser (81.8), Diffuser (75.3), and D-MPC (70.4 on Kitchen). Adding the trajectory ranker (MPDiffuser+Rank) further improves the average to 85.1, with the largest gain on Kitchen (73.8 vs. 67.9). On the constrained DSRL benchmark, MPDiffuser achieves the best return-cost tradeoff across all 7 tasks (e.g., HopperVelocity: return 0.81, cost 0.37 vs. BCQ-Lag's 0.78/5.02). On the Unitree Go2 quadruped, MPDiffuser matches the planner's return (94.8 vs. 94.7) while reducing cost from 1.05 to 0.91, and in real-world deployment tracks a 0.5 m/s command at 0.55 m/s versus PPO's 0.74 m/s overshoot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPDiffuser wins against all 10 baselines on the primary D4RL benchmark, with the largest margins over Diffuser (+9.6 avg) and Decision Diffuser (+3.1 avg), while the gap over the planner-only ablation (+2.2) confirms the dynamics model's contribution. On the constrained DSRL benchmark, MPDiffuser dominates value-based safe RL methods (COptiDICE, CPQ, BCQ-Lag) by achieving 5–10× lower cost at equal or higher return (e.g., HalfCheetahVelocity: 0.98 return / 0.77 cost vs. BCQ-Lag's 1.05 / 18.21), and outperforms the transformer-based CDT on 5 of 7 tasks. On the Pendulum safety task, MPDiffuser sustains 93% success rate with 16 samples while the planner drops to 88% and SafeDiffuser (projection-based) collapses to 42%, demonstrating that dynamic feasibility is essential for ranking. On the Unitree Go2, MPDiffuser ties the planner on return (94.8 vs. 94.7) but reduces cost by 13% (0.91 vs. 1.05), and beats Diffuser by 20.1 return points. The one setting where MPDiffuser underperforms is the cross-dataset mismatch ablation (planner on med-expert + dynamics on med-replay), where it drops to 70.3 on Hopper versus 109.9 when both modules share the same dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trajectory-level diffusion paradigm introduced by Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022) and Decision Diffuser (Ajay et al., 2023) by decomposing the single diffusion model into a planner and a dedicated dynamics model that are interleaved during sampling. It departs from D-MPC (Zhou et al., 2025), which applies a dynamics model only as a post-hoc candidate filter, by integrating dynamics correction actively at every diffusion step, and from inverse-dynamics-based approaches (Ajay et al., 2023; Luo et al., 2025) by never relying on a single IDM mapping. The alternating sampling scheme is grounded in operator-splitting theory and framed as approximating an exponential-tilted distribution combining planner priors with dynamics likelihood, providing a principled theoretical justification absent in prior diffusion-planning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compositional design addresses a fundamental reliability gap in diffusion-based offline RL—generated trajectories that are task-aligned but dynamically infeasible—while enabling the dynamics model to exploit inexpensive random or heterogeneous data independently of the planner, improving sample efficiency. The ability to fine-tune only the dynamics module for adaptation to novel system dynamics (e.g., hardware defects) and to flexibly adjust safety-reward tradeoffs via ranking parameters without retraining has direct practical value for safety-critical deployment, as validated on a real quadrupedal robot. Key limitations include sensitivity to cross-dataset distribution mismatch between planner and dynamics modules, and the framework's reliance on compatible training distributions; open questions include extension to online settings, scaling to high-dimensional visual control, and cross-domain dynamics transfer.
