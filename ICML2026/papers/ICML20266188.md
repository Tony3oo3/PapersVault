## Problem

Standard compute-based scaling laws (e.g., Chinchilla) assume effectively unlimited data and fail to reliably extrapolate model performance when training data is a quality-weighted mixture with repetition. In data-constrained regimes, upweighting high-quality data inevitably increases repetition, which induces diminishing returns and can degrade performance, yet no existing scaling framework jointly accounts for data quality distribution, repetition level, and model scale to predict loss or guide optimal recipe selection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a structured data-recipe specification: six-bucket LayerMix sampling weights w (token proportions per quality tier), training token budget K, source token budget S, model non-embedding FLOPs per token N, and overtraining factor m. It outputs a scalar predicted validation loss (average perplexity across HellaSwag, ARC-C/E, MMLU, and TriviaQA) and, by searching over 100k random weight vectors, an optimal LayerMix recipe that minimizes predicted loss for a given model size and token budget. No per-recipe or per-scale refitting is needed; the same five fitted parameters generalize across unseen recipes, scales up to 7B/425B tokens, and overtraining factors from 1.7× to 25×.

## Method

InfoLaw models pretraining as information accumulation, where each document's per-exposure information gain decays exponentially with repetition count, normalized by log(K) to capture budget-dependent slowdown: I_i,part(t) = I_i · λ(N)·e^(−λ(N)·t/log K). Summing across six quality buckets yields total Information = Σ_d f_d·M_d·log K·(1 − e^(−λ(N)·r_d/log K)), where f_d = e^(−θd) is a monotonically decreasing quality-density function and r_d = w_d·K/M_d is the per-bucket repetition factor. The rate λ(N) = a·log(N/N_0) + b encodes that larger models saturate faster on repeated content. Validation loss then follows a simple power law L = α·info^(−β), giving only five free scalar parameters (θ, a, b, α, β) jointly fitted via rank-based optimization and log-log regression on 27 runs (9 model scales × 3 LayerMix presets, 252M–1.2B, 3.6× overtraining). This compact formulation collapses diverse (w, K, S, N) configurations onto a single curve, enabling zero-shot prediction on unseen recipes and scales with 0.262% mean absolute error.

## Results

InfoLaw predicts validation loss on unseen data recipes and larger-scale runs (up to 7B parameters, 425B tokens) with 0.262% mean and 0.959% maximum absolute relative error, which is only 1.386× the experimental standard error of the measurement (mean SEM 0.189%). Across three overtraining regimes (m=1.7, 3.6, 25), the info-loss power-law exponent remains stable (−β ∈ [−0.0465, −0.0441]) while the intercept shifts, with per-regime mean errors of 0.471%, 0.225%, and 0.408% respectively. Using the fitted law, the paper identifies an optimal LayerMix recipe for a 2.5B model (w₀=0.50, w₁=0.49) that outperforms four randomly sampled baselines without additional training runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the traditional compute-based power-law scaling law (Hoffmann et al., 2022), which models loss as a function of total compute C_m alone. Under LayerMix data with repetition, the traditional law systematically mis-extrapolates at larger compute, yielding overly optimistic (too low) loss predictions, while InfoLaw tracks both interpolation and extrapolation across all five preset recipes. A critical practical difference: the traditional law requires separate fitting experiments for each data recipe to produce a new curve, whereas InfoLaw predicts loss on entirely unseen recipes (e.g., MHQ, MLQ, and 25 random weight vectors) using parameters fitted only on HQ/MQ/LQ, achieving 0.262% mean error without any additional training runs. On the 2.5B model, the InfoLaw-optimized recipe (w₀=0.50, w₁=0.49) achieves the lowest validation loss among all tested configurations, outperforming the four preset baselines (HQ, MHQ, MQ, MLQ) in Figure 3h. The paper also validates generalization to the RefinedWeb dataset, where the same framework achieves 0.24% mean and 0.36% max absolute error on held-out MLQ experiments, confirming that the fitted quality density (θ≈0.92–0.93) is robust across different source corpora.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chinchilla/Hoffmann compute-optimal scaling framework and the overtraining scaling relations of Gadre et al. by replacing compute as the sole scaling variable with a data-aware 'information' metric that explicitly encodes quality density, per-bucket repetition, and model scale. It departs from prior data-mixture scaling laws (Ye et al., 2025; Shukor et al., 2026) by modeling repetition as an exponential-decay saturation process rather than treating mixture weights as static coefficients, and from repetition studies (Hernandez et al., 2022; Muennighoff et al., 2023) by deriving a closed-form information functional that unifies quality, repetition, and scale into a single power-law predictor with only five free parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for LLM pretraining teams operating under data constraints: InfoLaw enables data-recipe selection via cheap small-model fits and extrapolation to 7B/425B-token scales with <1% error, eliminating costly grid searches over mixture weights. The finding that small models and small token budgets favor quality concentration while large models and large budgets favor diversity provides actionable guidance. Open questions include the heuristic nature of quality bucket boundaries, the lack of a theoretical explanation for why the overtraining factor shifts only the intercept (not slope) of the info-loss curve, and the absence of joint modeling of domain composition alongside intra-domain quality and repetition.
