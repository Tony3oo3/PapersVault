## Problem

Existing spiking attention mechanisms compute similarity via raw overlap (dot product) without accounting for firing density, causing high-spike-rate tokens to dominate attention scores regardless of true semantic alignment. This density bias is particularly harmful in sparse, event-driven spiking representations. Additionally, SNN Transformers have been explored almost exclusively in the visual domain, with principled attention mechanisms for audio spectrograms remaining largely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The model consumes log-Mel filterbank features (128 Mel bins, 10 ms frame shift) extracted from 16 kHz mono audio, padded to fixed temporal lengths (1024 frames for AudioSet, 512 for ESC-50, 128 for SCV2). Outputs are multi-label audio event class scores (evaluated by mAP on AudioSet-20k) or single-label predictions (accuracy on ESC-50 and SCV2). All processing occurs through PLIF spiking neurons over 4 discrete time steps, converting continuous features into binary spike streams at each layer.

## Method

DiceFormer is a hierarchical SNN Transformer whose core novelty is Spike Dice Attention (SDA), which replaces density-sensitive dot-product overlap with the Dice coefficient 2I(q,k)/(C(q)+C(k)+ε), explicitly normalizing spike co-occurrence by total marginal activity to suppress density bias. The architecture stacks a Spike Audio Dice Attention (SADA) module that decouples frequency and temporal axes into parallel attention streams, followed by a unified SDA module over flattened spatial tokens, all operating at O(ND) linear complexity. A hardware-efficient variant, Lin-SDA, derives from a first-order Taylor expansion of the Dice function, reducing the score to I(q,k) − λ(C(q)+C(k)) with a learnable λ, enabling fixed-point arithmetic on neuromorphic hardware. The full pipeline uses PLIF neurons with learnable time constants, direct training with sigmoid surrogate gradients, and is trained from scratch on audio spectrograms without pre-training.

## Results

On AudioSet-20k, DiceFormer-10-L achieves a new SNN SOTA mAP of 0.161 with 54.3M parameters and 17.80 mJ energy, surpassing the previous SNN best (QKFormer, 0.147 mAP) by 0.014 and the scratch-trained ANN baseline AST (0.148 mAP) by 0.013. On ESC-50, DiceFormer-10-M reaches 85.47% accuracy at only 7.95 mJ, and on Speech Commands V2, DiceFormer-10-S achieves 97.27% at 5.31 mJ. The lightweight DiceFormer-10-S (13.7M params, 5.34 mJ) attains 0.145 mAP on AudioSet-20k, matching QKFormer's 0.147 mAP with roughly 4.7× fewer parameters and 8.1× less energy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On AudioSet-20k, DiceFormer-10-L (0.161 mAP) outperforms all prior SNN baselines—QKFormer (0.147), SDT V2 (0.138), Spikformer (0.136), and SDT V1 (0.130)—by margins of 0.014, 0.023, 0.025, and 0.031 respectively, and also surpasses the scratch-trained ANN AST (0.148) by 0.013. However, it remains below the pre-trained ANN models SSAST-S (0.165) and DTF-AT (0.187). On ESC-50, DiceFormer-10-M (85.47%) is on par with SSAST-S (85.4%) but trails AST (88.7%) and DTF-AT (89.19%); on SCV2, DiceFormer-10-S (97.27%) beats both SNN baselines (DCLS-Delays 95.35%, SIDC-KWS 94.70%) but falls short of the ANN baselines (AST 98.11%, SSAST-S 97.70%, DTF-AT 98.30%). As a drop-in replacement on CIFAR-100, SDA improves Spike-driven Transformer by +0.59 pp (78.40→78.99%) and QKFormer by +0.26 pp (81.15→81.41%), confirming its portability to existing spiking vision backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SNN-Transformer lineage initiated by Spikformer (dot-product spiking attention) and Spike-driven Transformer (Hadamard-product overlap scoring) by identifying a fundamental density bias in their overlap-based attention: under a null model, expected overlap scales linearly with firing rate, causing high-density spikes to dominate attention regardless of semantic alignment. The proposed Spike Dice Attention (SDA) corrects this by normalizing overlap with total activity via the Dice coefficient, yielding a set-similarity formulation with linear O(ND) complexity. Architecturally, DiceFormer departs from prior vision-centric SNN-Transformers by introducing a hierarchical SADA→SDA design that explicitly decouples frequency and temporal axes before unified attention, tailored to the anisotropic structure of mel-spectrograms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The density bias identification is broadly applicable: any SNN-Transformer using unnormalized overlap scoring (Spikformer, SDT, QKFormer) is affected, and the correlation analysis (r=0.86 for Spikformer vs. r=0.08 for DiceFormer) provides concrete evidence that decoupling attention from firing rate contributes to performance gains. The extension to audio is practically significant as it demonstrates SNN-Transformers can compete with ANN baselines (surpassing AST from scratch) while consuming 5–8× less energy, opening the door to efficient on-device audio processing. Open questions include whether the density-aware formulation generalizes to other modalities beyond the preliminary CIFAR-100 results, whether Lin-SDA performs well on physical neuromorphic hardware, and whether large-scale pre-training (as in SSAST) could further close the gap to ANN SOTA.
