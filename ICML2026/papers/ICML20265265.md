## Problem

Existing zero-shot 3D anomaly detection methods either rely exclusively on geometric point clouds (missing color/texture defects like stains) or treat 2D RGB images as mere auxiliary cues without integrating color information into the 3D geometry. No prior framework provides an effective mechanism to fuse complementary 2D color and 3D geometric modalities in a unified, training-free manner that can detect both surface textural and structural anomalies across multiple viewpoints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265265/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud with per-point RGB color as input and renders it into paired multi-view 2D images (9 views at 336×336 resolution) — one set with uniform grey color on white background for geometry, another preserving chromatic information for color. Explicit point-to-pixel correspondence maps with visibility masks are computed via pinhole camera projection to enable back-projection of 2D anomaly scores onto the 3D manifold. The output is a fused 3D point-wise anomaly map, per-view 2D anomaly maps, and an image-level anomaly score, with the CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone kept frozen throughout.

## Method

CoGeoAD introduces a dual-stream rendering strategy that decouples geometric structure from surface color, then processes both streams through a frozen CLIP encoder. A Data-Driven Multi-View Attention (MVA) module uses a lightweight CNN pixel encoder to compute per-view importance scores via a Boltzmann distribution with learnable temperature, adaptively weighting informative views while suppressing occluded ones, regularized by an entropy loss to prevent collapse. The Multi-Stage Color-Geometric Fusion (MS-CGF) module hierarchically aggregates features from the last four layers of ViT-L/14 using a dynamic MLP-based layer selector, computes cosine similarity against learnable text prompt embeddings at each depth, and fuses across groups with learnable softmax weights. The final 3D anomaly score is obtained by element-wise maximization of the color and geometry streams, acting as a soft OR that preserves sensitivity to both textural and structural defects. Training uses a multi-task objective combining Focal+Dice loss on view maps, Dice loss on back-projected point scores, BCE classification losses at both view and point levels, and the MVA entropy regularizer, with only the prompt learner, MVA, and MS-CGF parameters optimized.

## Results

On MVTec3D-AD, CoGeoAD achieves 87.4% I-AUROC, 96.5% AP, 97.5% P-AUROC, and 91.9% AUPRO, establishing a new state-of-the-art among zero-shot 3D anomaly detection methods. On Eyecandies, it reaches 82.6% I-AUROC, 84.5% AP, 97.2% P-AUROC, and 87.2% AUPRO, outperforming all zero-shot competitors across every metric. The method also demonstrates strong robustness, retaining 96.7% P-AUROC and 88.7% AUPRO even under extreme mixed perturbations (Gaussian noise std=0.5 combined with ±20px calibration shift).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-shot methods, CoGeoAD outperforms all baselines on both datasets. On MVTec3D-AD, it surpasses the closest competitor GS-CLIP (CVPR'26) by +3.8% I-AUROC, +1.2% P-AUROC, and +5.5% AUPRO, and beats PointAD+ (arXiv'25) by +4.9% I-AUROC and +3.2% AUPRO. On Eyecandies, the margins are even larger: CoGeoAD exceeds GS-CLIP by +12.3% I-AUROC and +13.8% AUPRO, and PointAD+ by +2.3% I-AUROC, +2.4% P-AUROC, and +2.0% AUPRO. Against earlier zero-shot baselines, gains are substantial — e.g., +22.1% I-AUROC over AnomalyCLIP and +15.5% over MVP-PCLIP on MVTec3D-AD. The unsupervised reference upper bounds (BridgeNet at 99.3% I-AUROC, CPMF at 95.2% I-AUROC on MVTec3D-AD) remain higher, but they require target-domain normal training data or memory-bank construction, whereas CoGeoAD operates fully zero-shot without any target-dataset adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoGeoAD extends the CLIP-based zero-shot 3D anomaly detection lineage initiated by PointCLIP V2 (multi-view projection) and PointAD (high-precision rendering with view masks), but departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it explicitly decouples geometry from color via dual-modal rendering with point-to-pixel correspondence, rather than treating RGB as an auxiliary cue, and (2) it computes multi-view attention weights directly from raw rendered images via a lightweight CNN rather than from intermediate CLIP feature embeddings. The prompt-learning paradigm follows AnomalyCLIP, while the hierarchical multi-level feature aggregation draws on WinCLIP-style layer fusion, though the paper's ablation ultimately shows that only the deepest CLIP layers are needed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The color-geometric fusion addresses a genuine practical gap in industrial inspection: geometric-only methods miss surface stains and color defects, while RGB-only methods miss structural cracks, and CoGeoAD's element-wise max fusion captures both without requiring target-domain training. However, the method's 1.28 FPS throughput and 4.4 GB memory footprint limit real-time deployment, and the paper acknowledges an unresolved trade-off between geometric coverage (more views) and computational cost. Open questions include whether the semantic alignment between general-purpose CLIP embeddings and specialized industrial defect vocabularies can be further refined, and whether the data-driven MVA mechanism generalizes beyond the 9-view setting to arbitrary or adaptive view counts.
