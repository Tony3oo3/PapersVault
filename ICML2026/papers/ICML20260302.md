## Problem

Standard object counting methods fail catastrophically in agricultural scenes where targets are micro-scale (<0.24% of image area), densely clustered, and spectrally indistinguishable from surrounding foliage. No existing benchmark or method was designed for this 'Dense and Indiscernible Object Counting' (DIOC) regime, leaving a gap between general-purpose counters and the extreme visual ambiguity of real-world crop canopies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260302/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution RGB image (average 1840×1492 px) and three exemplar bounding boxes per image, which are cropped into overlapping patches of size (h+2p, w+2p) with p=32 padding. It produces a continuous density map at full image resolution by stitching only the valid central regions of per-patch predictions; the final count is obtained by summing all pixel values of the stitched density map. The CLIP-based mask is a soft (continuous-valued) modulation rather than a hard binary threshold, preserving gradient flow around object boundaries.

## Method

MaskCount is a two-stage few-shot pipeline. Stage 1 fine-tunes a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 with Deep Visual Prompt Tuning to compute a cosine-similarity map between image patches and an image-specific background text prompt (selected from 121 Qwen3-generated descriptors), producing a soft mask that suppresses foliage; it is trained with a multi-positive InfoNCE loss that distributes gradients across all object patches. Stage 2 encodes the masked image with a SwAV-pretrained ResNet50, extracts exemplar shape features (MLP on box dimensions) and appearance features (RoI Align), refines them through three cross-attention blocks, and applies dynamic convolution to generate a response map decoded into a density map. A novel pixel-level contrastive loss (Eq. 7) directly enforces angular separation between sampled background features and exemplar appearance features, and an edge-aware cropping-and-stitching mechanism discards boundary predictions to eliminate double-counting artifacts in dense clusters.

## Results

On DIOCblueberry, MaskCount achieves MAE 3.63 and RMSE 4.26, reducing MAE by 49.16% and RMSE by 70.50% over the strongest baseline (LOCA: MAE 7.14, RMSE 14.44). The method also sets new state-of-the-art on the Almond dataset (MAE 2.03, RMSE 3.02) and Tomato detection dataset (MAE 1.91, RMSE 2.71). Even under general cropping (without the edge-aware mechanism), MaskCount maintains a significant lead over all baselines on DIOCblueberry (MAE 6.94 vs. next-best 7.14).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskCount outperforms all nine baselines on DIOCblueberry across both MAE and RMSE. The closest competitor is LOCA (MAE 7.14, RMSE 14.44), which MaskCount beats by 49.16% in MAE and 70.50% in RMSE; the gap widens dramatically against two-stage or vision-language methods such as CounTR (MAE 67.02), SAFECount (MAE 50.22), and CLIP-Count (MAE 15.47, a 76.54% MAE reduction). Among crowd-counting and indiscernible-object-counting methods, P2PNet (MAE 8.95) and CountGD (MAE 11.35) are the strongest, yet both remain roughly 2–3× worse than MaskCount in MAE and 4–7× worse in RMSE. IOCFormer, designed for underwater indiscernible counting, degrades severely (MAE 77.70, RMSE 100.31), confirming that DIOCblueberry's camouflage and density exceed even specialized indiscernible-counting scenarios. On the Almond and Tomato datasets, MaskCount also achieves the best MAE and RMSE, demonstrating generalization beyond blueberries to other agricultural counting tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the exemplar-based few-shot counting paradigm (LOCA, CACViT, SSD) and repurposes CLIP's cross-modal alignment for a novel role: rather than using text prompts to identify the target object (as in CLIP-Count), it uses them to suppress the background, which the ablations show is more effective in low-discriminability scenes. The density-map regression backbone follows the standard SwAV-ResNet50 pipeline, while the cross-attention matching with shape and appearance exemplar features is inherited from prior few-shot counters. The paper's conceptual departure is formalizing DIOC as a distinct regime where extreme density and extreme visual ambiguity co-occur, a combination that neither crowd counting (P2PNet) nor indiscernible object counting (IOCFormer) addresses in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIOCblueberry fills a genuine gap: no prior benchmark combines the 108-average object density, 2.38% box pixel ratio, and strong camouflage that characterize real blueberry canopies, and its 1,400 human-hours of annotation make it a non-trivial resource for the community. The finding that background suppression outperforms foreground highlighting (MAE 3.63 vs. 7.60) challenges the intuitive assumption that pointing the model at the target is the most useful semantic cue in low-contrast scenes. Open questions include whether the two-stage CLIP+ResNet pipeline can be distilled into a single lightweight model for on-robot deployment (the authors acknowledge the speed-cost trade-off), and whether the DIOC formulation and MaskCount components transfer to other camouflaged crops beyond blueberries.
