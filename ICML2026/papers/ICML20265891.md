## Problem

Nuclear instance segmentation in H&E histopathology typically relies on dense pixel-level annotations and computationally expensive fine-tuning of specialized networks or SAM-based adapters. Training-free prompting methods that could bypass this cost remain largely unexplored, and existing reference-based approaches fail on pathology images due to the fine-grained, densely packed nature of nuclei and staining variability that prevents reliable cross-image feature matching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265891/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single H&E-stained histopathology image (no annotations, no external reference images) and produces per-nucleus instance segmentation masks. Internally, the image is partitioned into 128×128 overlapping patches for feature extraction and 512×512 patches for SAM inference, with the optical density decomposition of the RGB channels providing stain-specific foreground/background cues. The output is a set of non-overlapping nuclear masks refined by containment-aware NMS, evaluated with instance-level metrics (AJI, PQ, DQ, SQ) and semantic Dice.

## Method

SPROUT is a fully training-free pipeline with three stages. First, H&E stain priors (optical density decomposition + Otsu thresholding) identify high-confidence foreground and background regions, whose encoded features are clustered via K-means into class-specific prototypes. Second, POT-Scan progressively increases the transported mass fraction ρ in a partial optimal transport problem, aligning all feature locations to these prototypes while a slack column absorbs ambiguous or noisy features; the resulting transport plan reweights features into foreground/background activation maps. Third, watershed-based positive points and grid-sampled negative points are extracted from the binarized activations to prompt SAM for instance prediction, followed by a containment-aware soft NMS that penalizes large masks enclosing multiple smaller nuclei using a tanh-based decay combined with hematoxylin-channel response. The key insight is that stain-derived self-references eliminate the need for external reference images, and partial OT provides a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism to balance nuclear coverage against noise suppression without any parameter updates.

## Results

SPROUT achieves the highest AJI of 0.621 on MoNuSeg and 0.662 on CPM17, surpassing all fully-supervised, weakly-supervised, and SAM-based baselines without any training or annotations. The headline gain is +8.2% absolute AJI over the best prior method (COIN, 0.519) on MoNuSeg, and +1.4% over MedSAM (0.648) on CPM17. SPROUT also leads on Dice (0.795 MoNuSeg, 0.821 CPM17) and PQ (0.601, 0.616), confirming strong instance-level consistency. Across TNBC and PanNuke, SPROUT maintains competitive AJI scores of 0.609 and 0.581 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPROUT outperforms all nine baselines on both MoNuSeg and CPM17 across every reported metric. On MoNuSeg, SPROUT's AJI of 0.621 exceeds the closest competitor COIN (0.519, unsupervised) by 10.2 points and the best fully-supervised SAM-based method Med-SA (0.511) by 11.0 points. On CPM17, SPROUT (0.662 AJI) surpasses MedSAM (0.648, box-prompted) by 1.4 points and UN-SAM (0.581, fully supervised) by 8.1 points. Notably, vanilla SAM with automatic grid-point prompts performs poorly (0.061 AJI on MoNuSeg), highlighting that prompt quality is the critical bottleneck. Among non-SAM methods, the fully-supervised U-Net (0.421 AJI) and point-supervised SC-Net (0.539) are both substantially outperformed, while SPROUT requires no training data, no annotations, and no parameter updates. The consistent PQ advantage (0.601 vs. 0.493 for Med-SA on MoNuSeg) indicates SPROUT maintains stronger object-level instance consistency, not just pixel-level overlap.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPROUT builds on SAM as its segmentation engine and on the P²OT reformulation (Zhang et al., 2024b) that converts partial optimal transport into a solvable unbalanced OT via a slack column. It departs from prior prompt-generation approaches (Matcher, Segment Any Tissue) that require carefully curated external reference images by introducing a self-reference strategy grounded in H&E optical-density priors, eliminating the need for cross-image matching. Unlike SAM-adaptation methods (MedSAM, UN-SAM, DES-SAM, Med-SA) that fine-tune or add adapters, SPROUT operates entirely in the prompt space with zero parameter updates, and unlike COIN which still requires iterative self-distillation training, it avoids any training loop altogether.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPROUT demonstrates that a fully training-free, annotation-free pipeline can outperform fully-supervised nuclear segmentation networks by up to 8.2% AJI, which is practically significant for pathology labs where pixel-level annotations are scarce and expensive. The self-reference mechanism (stain priors → high-confidence masks → K-means prototypes → POT-Scan) is generalizable across feature extractors (pathology and natural-image backbones perform within ~1%), suggesting the approach could transfer to other dense-instance tasks. Key limitations include restriction to H&E staining, dependence on SAM for boundary precision, and non-trivial runtime (~170 s/image on MoNuSeg without parallelization), leaving open questions about extension to other stains, whole-slide images, and real-time clinical deployment.
