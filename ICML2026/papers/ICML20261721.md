## Problem

While prior work established that spurious local minima exist in finite-width two-layer ReLU networks and that mild overparameterisation can convert some into saddles via local Hessian analysis, the global geometric structure of the remaining minima—their hierarchical organisation, stability, and dynamical reachability—remained unexplained. This paper addresses the gap by providing an exact, low-dimensional characterisation of all local minima families and how overparameterisation reshapes their stability and accessibility under gradient-based dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261721/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a teacher-student pair of two-layer ReLU networks with Gaussian inputs, fully characterised by the student-student overlap matrix Q ∈ R^{K×K}, the student-teacher overlap matrix R ∈ R^{K×M}, and the teacher-teacher matrix T (assumed orthonormal, T = I_M). It produces a closed system of coupled nonlinear algebraic equations for Q and R whose solutions enumerate all local-minimum families indexed by k₁ (the count of anti-aligned student units), along with the exact quantised loss level, stability status, and dynamical basin-of-attraction fraction for each family. Crucially, these equations are exact and independent of the input dimension d, reducing an infinite-dimensional landscape problem to a finite set of scalar equations.

## Method

The paper leverages mean-field theory to derive closed-form ODEs for the order parameters Q and R governing gradient flow, with all Gaussian integrals (I₂, I₃) admitting explicit closed-form expressions for ReLU. Local minima are characterised by imposing a block-symmetric ansatz on Q and R that partitions student units into k₁ anti-aligned and k₂ aligned groups, reducing the fixed-point conditions to a tractable system of coupled nonlinear equations solved via Newton's method. The key mechanistic insight is a 'compensation mechanism': anti-aligned units generate a negative contribution that aligned units offset by adjusting their orientation, zeroing the gradient and stabilising the configuration as a local minimum. Overparameterisation (K > M) is shown to destabilise the lowest-order family (k₁ = 1) by providing an escape direction through the extra neuron, while higher-order families (k₁ ≥ 2) persist as genuinely new coupled structures not reducible to zero-padding. The framework is further extended to SGD (showing equivalence in the η = o_d(1) scaling limit), normalised GD, orthonormalised GD, and alternative activations (Leaky ReLU, erf), revealing that constraint dynamics fundamentally alter the landscape by forbidding the magnitude adjustments the compensation mechanism requires.

## Results

In the well-specified regime (K=M=17), only 13.09% of 10⁴ gradient-flow trajectories reach the global minimum (k₁=0), while the majority become trapped in spurious minima families: 27.52% at k₁=1, 29.05% at k₁=2, and 18.94% at k₁=3. Adding a single extra neuron (K=18, M=17) dramatically increases global-minimum convergence to 59.29% and completely eliminates the k₁=1 family (0.00%), while K=19 pushes this to 99.63%. Across optimisation schemes, unconstrained GD and 2L-GD show strong overparameterisation benefits (e.g., 2L-GD reaches 99.48% at K=19), whereas constrained dynamics (nGD, onGD) exhibit severe slowdowns and fail to converge within 1.2×10⁷ steps in the overparameterised regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily a theoretical characterisation rather than a method comparison, so it does not benchmark against competing optimisation algorithms. Instead, it compares the behaviour of four optimisation schemes (standard GD, two-layer GD, normalised GD, and orthonormalised GD) across parameterisation regimes. Standard GD and 2L-GD show consistent improvement with overparameterisation (e.g., GD: 13.25% → 64.18% → 77.50% for K=17→18→19), while nGD and onGD fail to converge (N/C) within the computational budget, with onGD producing a fundamentally different landscape where the compensation mechanism is forbidden by the orthonormality constraint. The paper also contrasts its global fixed-point analysis with the local Hessian approach of Safran et al. (2021), showing that while their neuron-splitting argument explains the destabilisation of k₁=1 minima, it cannot account for the persistence of higher-order families (k₁≥2) that survive in the overparameterised landscape. The block-symmetric ansatz is validated against the group-theoretic classification of Arjevani & Field (2019), confirming that the discrete isotropy subgroups map exactly to the quantised loss levels observed empirically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the mean-field ODE framework for two-layer networks established by Goldt et al. (2019) and the empirical existence results for spurious minima in ReLU networks by Safran & Shamir (2018), but departs from the local Hessian approach of Safran et al. (2021) by providing a global, exact fixed-point characterisation via low-dimensional order parameters. The block-symmetric ansatz directly recovers the isotropy subgroups identified by Arjevani & Field (2019) through a statistical-mechanics lens, bridging the group-theoretic and dynamical perspectives that had previously remained disconnected. The key methodological departure is treating the landscape as a system of self-consistent equations in summary statistics space rather than analysing curvature at individual critical points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a quantitative mechanism for how overparameterisation simplifies the landscape: low-order spurious minima (k1=1) are destabilised by a single extra neuron, but higher-order families (k1≥2) persist as genuinely new coupled structures not accessible via local Hessian or zero-padding arguments. This complicates the common narrative that mild overparameterisation renders the landscape benign, showing instead a hierarchical suppression rather than uniform elimination. Open questions include quantifying basins of attraction for different initialisations, extending the analysis beyond Gaussian inputs and two-layer architectures, and understanding whether the compensation mechanism generalises to deeper or more complex networks. The finding that orthonormal constraints (onGD) fundamentally eliminate the spurious-minima families by forbidding the magnitude adjustments that enable compensation is a useful practical insight for constrained optimisation.
