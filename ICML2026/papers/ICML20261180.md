## Problem

State-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer severe performance degradation (up to 50% success rate drop) when exposed to visual corruptions—sensor noise, blur, weather effects—absent from their training data. The paper asks whether intrinsic robustness can be achieved through architectural design alone, without relying on extra data, augmentation, or brute-force noise-pattern simulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261180/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (224×224 from head-mounted and wrist-mounted cameras) and a natural-language text instruction, producing autoregressive robot action sequences via a Qwen2.5-0.5B LLM backbone. The visual encoder is a frozen DINO-SigLIP, and the critical modality-alignment interface (the projector) is where the proposed IB-Adapter operates. The entire system is 0.5B parameters—14× smaller than 7B baselines—and is trained from scratch on LIBERO/CALVIN without any Open X-Embodiment pre-training or corruption-specific data.

## Method

StableVLA replaces the standard MLP projector with a Fused IB-Adapter, a dual-pathway module grounded in Information Bottleneck theory. The IB-Adapter pathway computes a multi-head Gram matrix (Q_h^T K_h) over channel dimensions to capture inter-channel covariance, applies a learnable sigmoid gate (derived from IB optimization under an independent Bernoulli latent structure) to independently suppress noisy channels without forcing competition, and reconstructs features via a two-layer MLP with GELU. The Fused variant adds a parallel high-fidelity MLP path, combining outputs as Z = MLP(X) + tanh(λ)·IB-Adapter(X), so that fine-grained spatial details for precise manipulation coexist with covariance-filtered robust semantics. A Stochastic Pathway Dropout rate p_drop is calibrated per task (≈0 for precision-critical LIBERO-Long, ≈0.3 for semantic tasks like CALVIN) to control how much the policy relies on the robust IB pathway versus the raw MLP pathway.

## Results

On LIBERO, StableVLA (0.5B) achieves 82.0% success rate on Spatial-S5, 70.2% on Object-S5, 71.9% on Goal-S5, and 45.3% on Long-S5, outperforming the same-architecture VLA-Adapter baseline (58.5%, 29.3%, 47.3%, 26.2%) by 40.2%–139.6% at the most severe corruption level. On CALVIN, StableVLA completes 1.51 tasks on average under S5 corruption vs. 1.44 for VLA-Adapter. Despite using a 14× smaller backbone (0.5B vs. 7B) and no Open X-Embodiment pre-training, StableVLA matches or exceeds OpenVLA-OFT (7B) and OpenPi-0.5 (3B) on most corruption-severity-5 metrics. In real-robot experiments, StableVLA achieves an average performance drop of only 15.7 percentage points under visual corruptions, compared to 30–49 pp for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VLA-Adapter (0.5B, same architecture and training data), StableVLA wins decisively on all corruption levels: at S5, improvements range from +23.5 pp (Spatial: 82.0 vs. 58.5) to +40.9 pp (Object: 70.2 vs. 29.3), with a 139.6% relative gain on Object-S5. Against OpenVLA (7B, OpenX-pretrained), StableVLA dominates by a wide margin at every severity level (e.g., 82.0% vs. 14.7% on Spatial-S5), demonstrating that architectural robustness far outweighs a 14× parameter advantage. Against OpenVLA-OFT (7B), StableVLA is competitive or slightly ahead on most S5 metrics (e.g., 70.2% vs. 52.8% on Object-S5, 45.3% vs. 40.3% on Long-S5) while using 14× fewer parameters. Against OpenPi-0.5 (3B, OpenX+Web co-trained), StableVLA trails slightly on Object-S5 (70.2% vs. 76.4%) and Long-S5 (45.3% vs. 47.7%) but is competitive on Spatial-S5 (82.0% vs. 62.4%) and Goal-S5 (71.9% vs. 64.2%), all while using 6× fewer parameters and no large-scale pre-training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLA-Adapter architecture (Wang et al., 2025) by replacing its standard MLP projector with a covariance-based attention module derived from the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 2000). The theoretical foundation draws on Zhou et al. (2022), who showed that self-attention in ViTs implements iterative IB optimization in the spatial dimension; here, the authors transpose that mechanism to the channel dimension of the vision-to-LLM projector. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant data-centric robustness paradigm (augmentation, domain randomization) by arguing that intrinsic architectural inductive bias—specifically, channel-wise noise filtering via Gram-matrix sigmoid gating—can substitute for exhaustive corruption simulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: a <10M-parameter drop-in module can close the robustness gap between a 0.5B VLA and 7B-scale models pretrained on Open X-Embodiment, suggesting that projector design is an underexploited lever for embodied robustness. However, the real-world evaluation is limited to four tasks with 10 trials each on a single robot platform, and the corruption protocol, while comprehensive in simulation, does not cover all real-world degradation modes (e.g., lighting changes, occlusion dynamics). Open questions include whether the IB-Adapter generalizes to non-manipulation tasks (locomotion, navigation), whether the channel-dimension IB framing transfers to other modality-alignment interfaces (audio, tactile), and whether the Stochastic Pathway Dropout schedule can be learned automatically rather than hand-tuned per task suite.
