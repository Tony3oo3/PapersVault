## Problem

Existing task-to-scene generation methods rely exclusively on LLMs to predict 3D layouts, inevitably producing object collisions and floating artifacts due to LLMs' inherent limitations in continuous spatial reasoning. Post-hoc physics optimization can eliminate collisions but often disrupts the original layout, breaking task alignment (e.g., moving an apple from the left to the right of a banana). The paper addresses this by decoupling semantic layout generation from physics-aware pose correction into a dual-system that preserves both task grounding and physical plausibility.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language task instruction (e.g., 'place the apple to the left of the banana on the tabletop') and a tabletop specification (size, shape) as input, and produces a structured JSON layout where each object is parameterized by a 3D translation, a single yaw rotation angle, a bounding-box size, a textual category/appearance description, and an asset identifier for mesh retrieval. The layout is generated progressively in three stages—task-critical objects, important background objects (those in physical contact with task objects), and secondary background objects—ensuring the final scene is directly loadable into a physics simulator with zero inter-object penetrations and no floating objects.

## Method

STABLE alternates between a Semantic Reasoner (a Qwen3-8B LLM fine-tuned via SFT on MesaTask-10K to output structured JSON layouts in three progressive stages) and a Physics Corrector (a 1D U-Net trained with conditional Flow Matching to predict pose updates—translations and yaw rotations only—while keeping object identities, sizes, and semantics fixed). The Physics Corrector is conditioned on mesh-level geometry embeddings extracted from surface point clouds of retrieved 3D assets via a frozen PointTransformerV3 encoder, enabling it to resolve complex spatial relationships like stacking and containment that bounding boxes alone cannot capture. Training combines a Flow Matching velocity loss with differentiable mesh-level SDF collision losses (object-object and object-table) and a support-contact loss that penalizes floating by measuring the gap between each object's bottom region and its nearest candidate support surface. At inference, the two systems alternate across the three semantic stages: after each SR expansion, the PC refines poses and the corrected layout is fed back as context for the next SR stage, preventing geometric errors from compounding while maintaining task alignment throughout.

## Results

On MesaTask-10K, STABLE achieves the best FID of 38.6 (vs. 40.6 for MesaTask and 43.7 for Steerable), the highest GPT-score average of 9.2/10, zero object collisions (OC = 0), and near-perfect task alignment (AwT = 99.4%, AwS = 99.0%). The Physics Corrector resolves collisions in all tested regimes (0–40+ initial collisions), whereas MesaTask's optimizer and Steerable fail once initial collisions exceed 20. On the rearrangement task, STABLE achieves 0.14 Distance Move and 0.08 EMD-to-GT with zero collisions, outperforming StructDiffusion (0.21 / 0.23 / 0.25) and LEGO-Net (0.28 / 0.43 / 0.32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STABLE outperforms all six generation baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest task-to-scene competitor MesaTask, STABLE reduces FID from 40.6 to 38.6, eliminates all 15.6% object collisions, and lifts AwS from 81.5% to 99.0%, indicating far better preservation of inter-object spatial relations. The post-processing baseline Steerable also achieves zero collisions and 99.4% AwT, but its aggressive object relocation degrades AwS to 91.1% (vs. 99.0%) and FID to 43.7, confirming the trade-off between physical feasibility and task alignment that STABLE avoids. Image-conditioned TabletopGen suffers from missing small objects and occlusion errors, yielding 23.5% collisions and lower alignment (AwT 91.7%, AwS 83.6%). Proprietary GPT-4o and prompting-based methods (Holodeck-Table, I-Design-Table) are far behind on all metrics, with FIDs above 85 and AwT below 26%, underscoring the difficulty of 3D spatial reasoning for general-purpose LLMs. On the rearrangement task, STABLE's Physics Corrector achieves zero collisions and the lowest EMD-to-GT (0.08), beating both StructDiffusion and LEGO-Net.

## Relation to Prior Work

STABLE builds directly on MesaTask (Hao et al., 2026) for its Semantic Reasoner, reusing the same dataset and LLM fine-tuning paradigm, but departs from the LLM-only pipeline by introducing a decoupled Physics Corrector based on conditional flow matching with geometry-aware conditioning (PointTransformerV3 point-cloud embeddings). The dual-system design is explicitly inspired by the System 1/System 2 VLA architecture in Helix, and the progressive three-stage layout expansion (task-critical → important background → secondary background) is a novel serialization of the MesaTask training data. The paper positions itself against both LLM-centric generators (which produce collisions) and post-hoc physics optimizers like Steerable (which can destroy task alignment), arguing that a learned, geometry-conditioned correction step is a middle ground that neither category provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical bottleneck for embodied AI: generating tabletop scenes that are both semantically aligned with manipulation instructions and directly usable in physics simulators without post-processing. The key open limitation is the restriction to yaw-only rotation and a fixed three-stage schedule, which constrains applicability to articulated objects, non-upright poses, and long-horizon scene construction. Extending the framework to full 6DoF poses, adaptive stage scheduling, and multi-surface indoor environments (shelves, cabinets) are natural next steps that the authors identify. The progressive dual-system paradigm also opens a path toward incremental scene editing without retraining, which could be broadly useful for simulation data pipelines.
