## Problem

Laplacian-based state representations in RL have been widely used for policy evaluation, but no prior work provides a characterization of the overall approximation error of the learned representation, nor does it quantify how the topological connectivity of the state-transition graph governs approximation quality. Additionally, existing theoretical analyses assume a symmetric transition matrix, excluding the general non-uniform policy and stochastic transition settings that arise in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261282/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a finite-state average-reward MDP with a fixed behavior policy π, consuming state-transition trajectories sampled from the stationary distribution φ and the induced transition kernel P. It produces a k-dimensional feature map ψ̂(s) (the first k estimated Laplacian eigenvectors) and a linear value function approximation v̂_k = Π_{Ψ̂}^Φ v. The key output is a closed-form upper bound on ‖v − v̂_k‖_Φ that decomposes into a truncation term (scaling with 1/√(λ₂·λ_{k+1})) and an estimation term (scaling with √(2ε/(λ_{k+1}−λ_k))), where λ₂ is the algebraic connectivity and ε is the GDO residual.

## Method

The paper introduces a reformulated Laplacian L = I − (P + Φ⁻¹PᵀΦ)/2 that is Φ-self-adjoint for any (non-symmetric) transition kernel, avoiding the symmetry assumption prevalent in prior work and matching what GDO-based optimizers actually estimate. The main theorem bounds the Φ-norm approximation error by two terms: a truncation error ‖r̄‖_Φ/√(λ₂·λ_{k+1}) derived via a Poincaré-type inequality on the Poisson equation, and a feature estimation error ‖v‖_Φ·√(2ε/(λ_{k+1}−λ_k)) derived via a Davis-Kahan sin Θ argument (the Graph Drawing Lemma) applied to the GDO residual. The truncation term explicitly links approximation quality to the Cheeger constant h(G) through the Cheeger inequality, showing that poorly connected MDPs (small λ₂) inherently yield worse representations. Empirical validation on deterministic gridworlds with varying wall counts confirms that decreasing connectivity monotonically increases both the analytical and GDO-approximated representation errors.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is Theorem 3.3, which bounds the Φ-norm approximation error of the value function as ‖v − v̂_k‖_Φ ≤ ‖r̄‖_Φ √(1/(λ₂·λ_{k+1})) + ‖v‖_Φ √(2ε/(λ_{k+1} − λ_k)), decomposing the error into a truncation term (scaling with the algebraic connectivity λ₂) and a feature estimation term (scaling with the GDO residual ε). Empirically, on 15×15 gridworlds with w ∈ {1,…,50} walls and k=20 eigenvectors over 5 seeds, the approximation error increases monotonically as λ₂ decreases (i.e., as connectivity degrades), confirming the theoretical prediction. The GDO-approximated representation consistently tracks the analytical eigenvector baseline with a small, stable gap attributable to the ε-optimality of the GDO optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing representation-learning methods; its empirical evaluation is a validation of the theoretical bounds rather than a method comparison. The two 'baselines' are internal: (i) the analytical (exact) Laplacian eigenvector representation, which isolates the truncation error, and (ii) the GDO-optimized representation, which adds the feature estimation error on top. Both show the same increasing error trend as walls are added (connectivity decreases), with the GDO curve sitting slightly above the analytical one by a roughly constant offset reflecting the optimization gap ε. The paper also contrasts its non-symmetric Laplacian definition (Eq. 6) with the common symmetric assumption L = I − P used in prior work (Mahadevan & Maggioni, 2007), showing that the symmetry assumption is unnecessary and that the approximation quality is governed by λ₂ regardless of P's symmetry. No head-to-head comparison with ALLO (Gomez et al., 2024), generalized GDO (Wang et al., 2021), or Proto-Value Networks (Farebrother et al., 2023) is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral representation framework for RL initiated by Mahadevan & Maggioni (2007) and made model-free by Wu et al. (2019), which in turn builds on Koren's (2003) Graph Drawing Objective. The key departure is the removal of the symmetry assumption on the transition matrix: prior theoretical analyses (Petrik 2007, Lan et al. 2022) and most practical implementations assumed a doubly stochastic or symmetric P, excluding general stochastic policies. The paper's Laplacian reformulation (Eq. 6) is a cleaner restatement of Chung's (2005) directed-graph Laplacian and of Wu et al.'s integral operator, and the authors explicitly show that prior papers (Touati et al. 2023, Gomez et al. 2024) reported incorrect matrix expressions when translating from the weighted Hilbert-space formulation to Euclidean notation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end error decomposition for model-free Laplacian representation learning, separating truncation error (governed by the algebraic connectivity λ₂) from GDO estimation error (governed by the eigengap λ_{k+1}−λ_k and the optimization residual ε). The explicit λ₂ dependence gives practitioners a concrete diagnostic: poorly connected MDPs (small spectral gap, e.g., due to walls or bottlenecks) will suffer larger approximation error regardless of the number of features used. Open questions include the sample complexity of achieving ε-optimality in GDO (Assumption 3.1), the estimation error of the linear least-squares step (Assumption 3.2), and whether the bounds are tight or merely loose upper bounds. The gridworld validation, while confirming the theory, is limited to deterministic environments with a single goal and uniform policies.
