## Problem

Fine-tuning-as-a-Service (FaaS) allows users to personalize safety-aligned LLMs, but injecting harmful data into the fine-tuning set can substantially degrade safety. Prior work (Zhou et al., 2024) showed that activating harmful-behavior modules during fine-tuning makes undesired behaviors unlearnable, but the underlying mechanism remained unexplained. This paper provides a gradient-level analysis showing that temporary jailbreaking saturates safety-degrading gradients while preserving benign task-relevant gradients, and builds a practical defense framework on this insight.

## Input / Output

The FaaS provider supplies a safety-aligned base LLM plus two pre-trained, reusable LoRA adapters (BufferLoRA and ReinforceLoRA), each trained once on 5,000 provider-side samples before deployment. The user submits a fine-tuning dataset of unknown composition (a mixture of harmful and harmless query-response pairs at an unknown ratio p), which is the only data seen during the per-user fine-tuning stage. The output is a single personalized LLM whose weights combine the base model, UserLoRA (task knowledge), and an orthogonally-projected ReinforceLoRA (safety reinforcement), with no additional safety data required during user fine-tuning and the same per-user compute cost as standard SFT.

## Method

The Buffer-and-Reinforce framework uses three LoRA modules in a three-phase pipeline. (1) Before fine-tuning, BufferLoRA is trained to maximize harmful-response likelihood (inducing a temporarily jailbroken state), and ReinforceLoRA is trained under that jailbroken state to recover refusal behavior on harmful queries while preserving benign responses. (2) During user fine-tuning, BufferLoRA is attached and frozen while only UserLoRA is trained on user data; the key insight is that the jailbroken state saturates safety-degrading gradients (Safety Gradient Score ≈ 0) so harmful updates are absorbed by the already-converged harmful loss landscape, while benign task gradients remain active and are captured by UserLoRA. (3) After fine-tuning, BufferLoRA is removed to restore base-model safety, and ReinforceLoRA is merged with UserLoRA via QR decomposition-based soft orthogonal projection: the output subspace of UserLoRA is identified from the QR factorization of its B_U matrix, ReinforceLoRA is projected onto the orthogonal complement (with a tunable strength α=0.1), and the two residual updates are averaged and added to the base weights. An optional eigendecomposition of the Gram matrix A_U A_U^T handles rank-collapse cases by restricting the projection to the effective UserLoRA subspace.

## Results

On the primary evaluation (LLaMA3-8B-Instruct, 1,000 user samples, GSM8K task), Buffer-and-Reinforce achieves a Harmful Score (HS) of 8.1–8.8 across all harmful data ratios (p = 0.0–1.0) while maintaining Fine-tuning Accuracy (FA) of 75.2–76.6%. This is substantially lower HS than the best fine-tuning-stage baseline (LDIFS at 16.4–17.6) and the best post-fine-tuning baseline (SafeLoRA at 20.0–53.2), while matching or exceeding their FA. Across diverse tasks (GSM8K, SST2, AGNEWS) and model architectures (LLaMA3-8B, Gemma2-9B, Qwen3-4B), the framework consistently achieves the lowest average HS (5.2–8.7) and highest average FA (76.4–86.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Buffer-and-Reinforce outperforms all fine-tuning-stage baselines on both safety and utility: it reduces HS by roughly 8–19 points over LDIFS (16.4–17.6), Security Vector (22.1–25.3), AsFT (21.1–34.1), and Lisa (14.7–73.5), while also improving FA by 2–9 points. Among post-fine-tuning-stage methods, it beats SafeLoRA (HS 20.0–53.2) and Antidote (HS 22.2–58.1) by 12–45 points in HS with comparable or higher FA, and substantially outperforms Panacea (HS 19.9–64.2, FA 65.2–68.4) on both metrics. SafeInstruct achieves a competitive HS of 7.9 at p=0.0 but degrades sharply to 74.0 at p=1.0, whereas our method remains stable at 8.1–8.8 across all ratios. In the LoRA merging comparison (Table A8), TIES-Merging (HS 7.9) and LoRA-LEGO (HS 6.4) achieve slightly lower HS than our method (8.4), but at the cost of 2.7–3.3 points lower FA (74.2–74.8 vs. 77.5), indicating our QR-based merging better preserves user-task utility. Alignment-stage methods (RepNoise, Vaccine, Booster) perform worst overall (HS 57–61, FA 27–40), confirming their poor generalization under the cross-domain harmful data distribution assumed in FaaS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Security Vector strategy (Zhou et al., 2024), which showed that activating harmful-behavior modules during fine-tuning makes undesired behaviors unlearnable, but provides the first gradient-level mechanistic explanation for why this works: temporary jailbreaking saturates safety-degrading gradients while preserving benign task-relevant gradients. It departs from the dominant regularization-based defense paradigm (LDIFS, SafeInstruct, Lisa, AsFT) by exploiting the model's own convergence dynamics rather than explicitly constraining optimization. The QR decomposition-based orthogonal merging for combining safety and task LoRA modules is a new technical contribution that addresses destructive interference between ReinforceLoRA and UserLoRA, going beyond the simple removal of the jailbreaking adapter used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework is practically significant for FaaS providers because it requires no additional safety-alignment data during user fine-tuning and incurs the same per-user computational cost as standard SFT, with BufferLoRA and ReinforceLoRA trained once and reused across all sessions. Key limitations include dependence on BufferLoRA inducing sufficiently strong jailbreaking (which may weaken as future models become more robust to jailbreaking) and evaluation restricted to the 4B–13B parameter range, leaving open whether the gradient-saturation mechanism holds at frontier scale. The gradient-level analysis also reveals that prior defenses like SafeInstruct and AsFT still produce negative Safety Gradient Scores, suggesting their protective effects operate at the response level rather than genuinely neutralizing harmful gradient updates.
