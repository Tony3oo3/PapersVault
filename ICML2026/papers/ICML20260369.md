## Problem

Spectrally sparse signal reconstruction from partial samples can be cast as low-rank Hankel matrix completion, but existing nonconvex solvers either incur high per-iteration cost (SVD-based methods) or suffer from suboptimal convergence due to ill-conditioning. The relationship between factor-space preconditioned updates and manifold-space Riemannian gradients was not fully clarified, and the widely used scaled gradient descent (SGD) preconditioner applies an oblique rather than orthogonal projection onto the tangent space, leaving room for a strictly better preconditioning strategy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial observation of a 1D spectrally r-sparse signal x ∈ C^{n_s} (a superposition of r complex exponentials with optional damping), specified by the observation index set Ω ⊂ {0,…,n_s−1} with |Ω| = m samples and the known sparsity level r. The signal length n_s is assumed odd so the Hankel lift produces a square n×n complex symmetric matrix with 2n = n_s+1. The output is the full reconstructed signal vector x_rec ∈ C^{n_s}, obtained by inverting the Hankel operator on the final low-rank matrix iterate.

## Method

The paper introduces a Jacobi-preconditioned gradient descent (JHGD) algorithm for low-rank Hankel matrix completion. A generator Q(Z) = ZZ^T maps factor-space iterates to the complex-symmetric matrix space, and its Jacobian J(M) = ZM^T + MZ^T has an explicitly computed SVD (Theorem 2.1) that yields the exact preconditioner P_E = (J^*J)^†. A computationally cheaper surrogate P_A = (½I − ¼Z(Z^HZ)^{−1}Z^H)·(Z^HZ)^{−1} is shown to induce the identical orthogonal projection onto the tangent space (Theorem 2.4), giving per-iteration cost O(n log n · r + nr²). This orthogonal projection distinguishes JHGD from SGD, which uses an oblique projection, and the generator framework establishes exact equivalence between the factor-space JHGD update and a Riemannian gradient descent step on the rank-r manifold under a specific retraction. Theoretical analysis in matrix space (avoiding the non-compact complex-orthogonal ambiguity of factor-space distances) proves linear convergence with a contraction factor independent of the Hankel matrix condition number, under standard μ₀-incoherence and sampling-with-replacement assumptions.

## Results

On the primary setting (n_s=9999, m=5000), JHGD achieves the lowest reconstruction error across all sparsity levels: 5.32×10⁻⁶ at r=100 and 8.77×10⁻⁶ at r=300, compared to 1.04×10⁻⁵ and 1.54×10⁻⁵ for the strongest baseline SGD. JHGD also requires the fewest iterations (10.8 and 19.1) and the shortest runtime (0.60 s and 2.13 s), running roughly 2× faster than SGD. At large scale (n_s=49999, yielding a 25000×25000 Hankel matrix), JHGD reaches RelErr=10⁻⁴ in about 3 s, roughly half the time of SGD, while FIHT becomes the slowest method (116.7 s) due to per-iteration SVD costs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JHGD dominates all four baselines on every metric (reconstruction error, iteration count, and runtime) across all tested configurations. Against the strongest baseline SGD (Tong et al., 2021), JHGD achieves roughly 2× lower reconstruction error (5.32e-6 vs 1.04e-5 at r=100) while running ~2.7× faster (0.60 s vs 1.64 s); the paper attributes this to JHGD's orthogonal tangent-space projection versus SGD's oblique projection. Against FIHT (Cai et al., 2019), JHGD uses slightly more iterations (10.8 vs 8.1 at r=100) but achieves 3.6× lower error and 5.4× shorter runtime, since FIHT's per-iteration SVD becomes prohibitive at scale (116.7 s vs 8.2 s at n_s=49999). Against PGD and SHGD (Cai et al., 2018; Li et al., 2024), JHGD requires roughly 3× fewer iterations and 2–4× less runtime, with substantially lower error (5.32e-6 vs 1.93e-5). The gap widens with increasing sparsity: at r=300, JHGD is 4.6× faster than PGD and 7.5× faster than FIHT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of factor-space gradient descent (Cai et al., 2018; Li et al., 2024) and manifold-based Riemannian optimization (Cai et al., 2019) for low-rank Hankel matrix completion. Its key departure from the closest prior work, scaled gradient descent (SGD, Tong et al., 2021), is a geometric one: the Jacobi preconditioner derived from the generator Q(Z)=ZZ^top induces an orthogonal projection of the gradient onto the tangent space, whereas SGD's simpler preconditioner yields only an oblique projection. The generator framework formalizes the equivalence between factor-space and matrix-space iterates, a connection that prior work treated informally, and provides a principled derivation of the preconditioner rather than an ad hoc scaling choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The condition-number-independent linear convergence guarantee is a meaningful theoretical advance for nonconvex low-rank recovery, and the factor-manifold unification offers a reusable template for designing preconditioners in other structured matrix problems. The practical speedup over SGD (~2×) and all other baselines is consistent across problem sizes, sparsity levels, and observation counts, making JHGD a strong default for spectrally sparse signal reconstruction. Open questions include whether the generator-based preconditioning framework extends cleanly to multi-dimensional Hankel lifts (mentioned but not analyzed), to non-symmetric or rectangular factorizations, and whether the incoherence assumptions can be relaxed for closely spaced or damped components where atomic norm methods also struggle.
