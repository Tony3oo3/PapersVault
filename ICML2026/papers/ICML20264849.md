## Problem

Existing entropy-based methods in reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) of LLMs rely on heuristic designs that treat entropy in isolation, with no principled understanding of how individual token updates propagate to changes in policy entropy. This lack of a theoretical framework makes it unclear why some approaches encourage entropy while others suppress it, and leads to labor-intensive hyperparameter tuning without clear guidance for balancing exploration and exploitation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the token logits from the LLM forward pass (already available for log-probability and entropy computation), the softmax probability distribution over the vocabulary at each position, and the GRPO advantage values for each sampled response. It produces a binary mask per token in the training batch that zeros out the gradient for tokens whose entropy-discriminator score S* = p_k(H + log p_k) deviates beyond a threshold (μ⁺σ or μ⁻σ) from the batch or vocabulary expectation, effectively yielding an entropy-controlled training loss with negligible additional computation since all required quantities are scalar values derived from existing forward-pass intermediates.

## Method

The paper derives a first-order analytical expression for token-level entropy change under a single logit perturbation δz = ε·e_k, showing ΔH = −εS* + O(ε²) where the discriminator S* = p_k(H + log p_k) encodes the relationship between token probability and policy entropy. Extending to a full GRPO gradient step (δz = α(e_k − p)), the entropy change becomes ΔH = −α(S* − E_{i∼p}[S_i]) + O(α²), revealing that entropy decreases when rewarding tokens whose S* exceeds the policy-weighted baseline and increases when it falls below. Two clipping algorithms are derived from this: Clip_B masks tokens whose S* deviates beyond μσ from the batch mean, while Clip_V uses the vocabulary-centered score S* − E_{i∼p}[S_i] with batch-level standard deviation. The framework also provides a unified interpretation of existing methods (GRPO clipping, DAPO clip-higher, entropy regularization, probability-weighted updating) as mechanisms that selectively amplify entropy-increasing or suppress entropy-decreasing token updates.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, GRPO+Clip_B achieves the best average Avg@K of 41.38 across AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, Minerva, and DAPO500, outperforming Vanilla GRPO (39.36) by +2.02 points. On Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, GRPO+Clip_V reaches 49.44 Avg@K, surpassing Vanilla GRPO (45.57) by +3.87 points and the strongest baseline Clip-Cov (48.56) by +0.88. The methods also improve Pass@K metrics, e.g., AIME24 Pass@32 rises from 50.00 (GRPO) to 56.67 (Clip_B) on the 7B model, indicating enhanced solution diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Both Clip_B and Clip_V consistently outperform Vanilla GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) across all five datasets and both model sizes, with the largest gap on Qwen2.5-14B where Clip_V gains +3.87 Avg@K (49.44 vs. 45.57). Against the entropy-mechanism baselines from Cui et al. (2025), Clip_B and Clip_V surpass both Clip-Cov and KL-Cov on the 7B model (41.38/40.61 vs. 39.37/39.54) and on the 14B model (48.81/49.44 vs. 48.56/48.23), with Clip_V achieving the overall best score. The probability-weighted method Rewarding Unlikely (He et al., 2025) underperforms Vanilla GRPO on the 7B model (38.78 vs. 39.36 Avg@K), while both proposed methods clearly exceed it. The high-entropy token selection method Forking Tokens (Wang et al., 2025) also trails both proposed methods on both model sizes (38.33/47.38 vs. 41.38/48.81 for Clip_B), confirming that the entropy-discriminator-based clipping provides a more principled and effective control mechanism than heuristic token selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-dynamics framework of Ren & Sutherland (2025), which models how single-logit perturbations propagate through softmax to shift probability distributions, by specializing the analysis to Shannon entropy and deriving a closed-form 'discriminator score' S* = p_k(H + log p_k) that predicts the direction of entropy change. It then lifts this single-logit result to a full GRPO optimization step (Shao et al., 2024), yielding a first-order expression involving S* minus its policy-weighted expectation. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior entropy-control work (DAPO clip-higher, forking-token selection, probability-weighted updating) by showing that all these heuristics can be reinterpreted as selectively amplifying or suppressing tokens based on their S* deviation, thereby unifying them under one theoretical lens rather than treating each as an independent trick.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework gives practitioners a cheap, scalar-valued diagnostic (S*) for predicting whether a given token update will increase or decrease entropy, enabling targeted gradient masking without additional model forward passes. The covariance-based explanation of entropy collapse (Corollary C.2) — that the model systematically earns positive advantage on high-probability 'safe' tokens, making Cov(A, S*−E[S_i]) negative — offers a mechanistic account that goes beyond the empirical observation of entropy decay. Open questions remain: the analysis treats tokens as independent (ignoring parameter-sharing coupling across positions), the relationship between maintained entropy level and downstream reasoning quality is not formally characterized, and the framework has only been validated on mathematical reasoning with Qwen-family models, leaving its transfer to code, tool-use, or non-GRPO algorithms (beyond a brief PPO demo) unexplored.
