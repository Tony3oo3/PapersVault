## Problem

Static multimodal VQA benchmarks suffer from data contamination (open test sets leaking into training corpora) and data saturation (fixed difficulty losing discriminative power as MLLMs improve). Existing dynamic evaluation methods based on perturbation (e.g., VLB) often fail to alter the core reasoning structure of a question and offer only coarse-grained difficulty control, leaving a gap for a framework that can systematically evolve benchmark difficulty while preserving question validity.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a static VQA sample (RGB image, question text, answer text) plus external textual knowledge, and produces a new VQA question-answer pair where the image is held fixed but the question and answer are reconstructed to require a different or extended chain of reasoning. Difficulty is parameterized by the number of exploration hops (1–3), which directly controls the number of edges in the key knowledge subgraph G_K; the output also includes the explicit set of key triplets that define the question's rationale, enabling fine-grained difficulty measurement beyond a single scalar score.

## Method

KBE-DME represents each VQA sample as a graph of multimodal knowledge triplets, partitioned into a visual subgraph G_M, a textual subgraph G_T, and a key subgraph G_K containing only the triplets necessary to answer the question. Dynamic evolution is achieved through two operations on G_K: (1) Re-Selection, which picks a different valid path of triplets from the existing M∪T pool to form a new key subgraph G_K′, and (2) External Knowledge Exploration, which appends new triplets from external sources to iteratively extend G_K into G_Kn over up to three hops. The pipeline follows an Extract–Explore–Express structure: an MLLM extracts and identifies key triplets, a filtering stage (representativeness, part-of-speech, cycle-check) ensures quality during expansion, and a second MLLM call synthesizes the new question-answer pair from the modified key subgraph. Difficulty is monotonically controlled by hop count, as each additional hop adds at least one edge to G_K, lengthening the required reasoning chain without altering the input image.

## Results

On OK-VQA, GPT-4o's accuracy drops from 50.33% (Raw) to 39.19% (3-hop), while LLaVA-OV shows the steepest decline from 53.46% to 29.52%. On A-OKVQA, GPT-4o decreases from 60.13% to 36.27% and LLaVA-OV from 60.78% to 25.33% across the same hop progression. All five evaluated MLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-pro, Claude-3.5, LLaVA-OV, Qwen-2.5-VL) exhibit consistent monotonic performance degradation as hop count increases, confirming effective difficulty control. A human study on 150 generated samples yields 95.0% reasonableness, 96.8% triplet correctness, and 97.9% alignment scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary quantitative comparison is against VLB (Yang et al., 2025), specifically its L4 'Adding Irrelevant Context (Hard)' strategy, reproduced on OK-VQA (Table 10). KBE-DME's 1-hop questions produce substantially larger accuracy drops than VLB-L4: for example, LLaVA-OV drops from 53.19% (Raw) to 23.17% under KBE-DME 1-hop, versus only 50.02% under VLB-L4; GPT-4o drops to 32.77% vs. 39.69%. VLB-L4 also shows inconsistent effects across models (e.g., Claude-4.5 barely drops from 56.75% to 55.83%), whereas KBE-DME yields consistent degradation for all five models. Additionally, VLB-L4 causes a severe distribution shift in question length (97.2 words vs. 8.18 in Raw), while KBE-DME maintains moderate length increases (up to 18.8 words at 3-hop). Qualitatively, Table 1 shows KBE-DME is the only method that simultaneously supports multimodal inputs, generalizes across tasks, avoids adversarial formulations, incorporates external knowledge, and provides fine-grained difficulty control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from perturbation-based dynamic evaluation methods (VLB, Liu & Zhang 2025) that modify surface-level inputs (rephrasing, adding irrelevant context, editing image regions) without fundamentally altering what the question asks. Instead, KBE-DME introduces a knowledge-graph formulation of VQA samples using multimodal knowledge triplets, enabling principled question reconstruction via re-selection of visual information or exploration through external textual knowledge hops. It also extends text-only dynamic evaluation work (DyVal, NPHardEval, MPA) into the multimodal domain while remaining non-adversarial, task-generalizable, and equipped with fine-grained difficulty control based on key-subgraph edge count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KBE-DME addresses two increasingly critical problems in MLLM evaluation—data contamination and benchmark saturation—by providing a framework that can continuously generate novel, difficulty-graded test questions from any existing VQA benchmark without manual reconstruction. The practical impact is significant: evaluators can adapt test difficulty to track model progress over time rather than periodically building new static benchmarks. Limitations include reliance on a powerful MLLM (GPT-4o or Qwen2-VL-72B) for triplet extraction and question synthesis, restriction to VQA-format tasks, and the use of hop count as a proxy for cognitive difficulty that may not perfectly align with human-perceived challenge. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to non-VQA multimodal tasks (video, 3D, audio) and whether the knowledge-triplet abstraction captures the full reasoning structure of more complex multimodal problems.
