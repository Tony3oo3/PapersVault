## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based open-vocabulary methods fuse 2D teacher embeddings via Euclidean averaging, which ignores the hyperspherical geometry of ℓ₂-normalized features and causes measurable norm shrinkage and angular distortion (especially at boundaries and mixed-depth pixels). Additionally, distilling supervision uniformly across all views amplifies occlusion noise and depth-mixing artifacts, producing blurry, view-inconsistent masks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266030/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed RGB images (no depth input required) and a free-form text prompt at inference. Each Gaussian stores a low-dimensional semantic latent (d ≪ D) that is decoded to the teacher feature space (e.g., OpenCLIP ViT-B-16, D-dimensional) during rendering. At query time, pixel-wise cosine similarity between the CLIP text embedding and the rendered semantic map yields a per-pixel score, thresholded to produce 2D masks; optionally, scores are aggregated to Gaussian-level for 3D region extraction and scene editing.

## Method

Rh-3DGS keeps the standard 3DGS rasterizer unchanged and enforces manifold-aware geometry entirely in the loss. (1) VCD computes a stop-gradient per-pixel reliability weight from three rasterization statistics—accumulated opacity, log-depth gradient magnitude, and ray depth variance—multiplicatively combining them to suppress occlusion and mixed-depth supervision without adding a loss term. (2) VFM replaces Euclidean L₂ distillation with a Huberized geodesic loss on S^{D-1}, where the Huber threshold δ is annealed during training; a view-wise robust Fréchet-mean stabilizer (solved via a few tangent-space descent iterations) further aligns global feature centroids and caps outlier influence. (3) LIC applies a multi-positive InfoNCE contrast over a patch-sampled mini-batch of Gaussians, using pseudo-instance clustering (K-means in a joint position–feature space) to define positives within a 3D radius, sharpening local boundaries with negligible overhead (<5% per-iteration cost).

## Results

On LERF, Rh-3DGS achieves 82.07 mIoU and 67.66 mBIoU, surpassing the strongest baseline ILGS (76.07 mIoU, 51.83 mBIoU) by +6.00 mIoU and +15.83 mBIoU. On 3D-OVS, it reaches 96.88 mIoU and 89.62 mBIoU, improving over LaGa (95.13/86.02) by +1.75 mIoU and +3.60 mBIoU. On ScanNet (19-class), it attains 40.8 mIoU / 59.0 mAcc, outperforming ILGS (36.8/51.8) by +4.0 mIoU and +7.2 mAcc. The largest gains appear on boundary quality (mBIoU), indicating sharper and more view-consistent segmentation masks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rh-3DGS outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset and metric. The strongest competitor is ILGS (ICCV'25), which achieves 76.07 mIoU on LERF; Rh-3DGS extends this to 82.07 mIoU and, more notably, 67.66 vs. 51.83 mBIoU, a +15.83-point boundary-quality gain. On 3D-OVS, LaGa (ICML'25) and VALA (arXiv'25) are the closest baselines at 95.13 and 95.11 mIoU respectively, while Rh-3DGS reaches 96.88 mIoU and 89.62 mBIoU. On ScanNet, ILGS leads the baselines at 36.8 mIoU (19-class), but Rh-3DGS improves to 40.8 mIoU and 59.0 mAcc. Earlier methods—Feature 3DGS, LangSplat, OpenGaussian, and InstanceGaussian—trail by 20–50 mIoU points on LERF, reflecting the cumulative benefit of visibility-aware weighting, hyperspherical distillation, and local contrastive regularization over naive Euclidean feature fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS-based open-vocabulary semantic segmentation line (Feature 3DGS, LangSplat, OpenGaussian, LaGa, ILGS, LangSplatV2, VALA) by identifying a geometric mismatch in how prior methods fuse teacher embeddings: they treat ℓ₂-normalized VLM features as Euclidean vectors and blend them via alpha compositing, ignoring their hyperspherical structure. The departure is twofold: (1) a Riemannian Huber distillation objective with a visibility-weighted Fréchet-mean stabilizer replaces Euclidean L₂/alpha-blend losses, and (2) a rasterization-statistics-based reliability map (VCD) down-weights occlusion and mixed-depth pixels before distillation. A lightweight InfoNCE contrastive regularizer (LIC) on 3D neighborhoods is added for local boundary coherence, drawing on contrastive learning ideas from 2D dense prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean geometric diagnosis (norm shrinkage and angular distortion from Euclidean averaging on S^{D-1}) with direct empirical evidence, and shows that the resulting boundary-quality gains (mBIoU) are substantially larger than overall mIoU gains, suggesting that boundary fidelity has been the under-addressed bottleneck in 3DGS semantic distillation. Limitations include inheriting teacher biases (CLIP/SAM), added training overhead from extra rasterizer statistics and neighborhood queries, and a single-teacher setup. Open questions include whether the manifold-aware formulation extends to dynamic scenes, multi-teacher distillation, or non-spherical embedding spaces, and whether the visibility-calibration idea transfers to other differentiable rendering pipelines beyond 3DGS.
