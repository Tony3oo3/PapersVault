## Problem

Fair rank aggregation under the Spearman footrule distance lacked an optimal algorithm for the practically important top-k variant, and the best known approximation for the full-ranking variant was a 3-approximation, in stark contrast to the unconstrained version which is solvable in polynomial time. Closing this computational gap between fair and unconstrained rank aggregation had remained an open problem explicitly identified in prior work.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of n input rankings over d candidates, a partition of candidates into g groups, per-group fairness parameters (α_i, β_i) specifying minimum and maximum fractions of each group required in the top-k positions, and an integer k. It outputs either an optimal fair top-k ranking (when k' = k) or a 2-approximate fair full ranking over all d candidates (when k' = d), minimizing the sum of generalized Spearman footrule distances to the input rankings. The generalized footrule for top-k lists assigns unranked candidates a virtual position at k+1, naturally accommodating truncated outputs.

## Method

For the top-k problem, the authors formulate an ILP and prove its constraint matrix is totally unimodular by exploiting structural properties of the fairness constraints, yielding an integral LP solvable in polynomial time via the ellipsoid method — a novel technique for fairness-constrained optimization that avoids the fairness violations typical of rounding-based approaches. For the full-ranking problem, the algorithm proceeds in two steps: it first computes an optimal fair top-k list minimizing a modified leftward-displacement objective (again via the totally unimodular LP), then extends it to a full ranking by solving a minimum-cost perfect matching on a bipartite graph over the remaining candidates and positions. The 2-approximation analysis decomposes the total footrule cost into contributions from the top-k and bottom elements, showing the extension step adds at most OPT additional cost. A complementary bottom-k variant (Algorithm 2) and their combination (Algorithm 3, returning the better of the two) provide further empirical gains while preserving the 2-approximation guarantee.

## Results

Under the Spearman footrule metric, the proposed 2-approximation algorithm (Algorithm 3) finds fair aggregate rankings within 2%–3% of the ILP optimum on MovieLens and at most 1% from optimal on the Fantasy Football dataset (often finding the exact optimum). It outperforms the KT baseline by 5%–11% on MovieLens and 1%–2% on Football, and outperforms the BFI baseline by 13%–15% on MovieLens and 5%–10% on Football. Under the Kendall-tau metric, the algorithm stays within 2% of KT on MovieLens (occasionally finding a better ranking) and at most 2% worse than KT on Football, while consistently beating BFI by 8%–10% (MovieLens) and 4%–10% (Football).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: BFI (Chakraborty et al., NeurIPS 2022), a 3-approximation meta-algorithm that computes the closest fair ranking for each input and selects the best, and KT (Chakraborty et al., IJCAI 2025), a state-of-the-art 18/7-approximation for fair rank aggregation under Kendall-tau (implying 36/7 under Spearman footrule). Under Spearman footrule, the proposed method decisively beats both baselines: it achieves 5%–11% lower objective cost than KT and 13%–15% lower than BFI on MovieLens, and 1%–2% lower than KT and 5%–10% lower than BFI on Football. Under Kendall-tau, the proposed method is competitive with KT (within 2% on MovieLens, at most 2% worse on Football) but substantially outperforms BFI by 8%–10% on MovieLens and 4%–10% on Football. Notably, the theoretical 2-approximation guarantee for Spearman footrule translates to a 4-approximation for Kendall-tau, yet empirically the algorithm matches or nearly matches the specialized KT algorithm, demonstrating strong robustness across distance metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fair rank aggregation framework initiated by Wei et al. (SIGMOD'22) and Chakraborty et al. (NeurIPS'22), who established a 3-approximation via a meta-algorithm that computes closest fair rankings per input and selects the best. The unconstrained Spearman footrule aggregation is solvable exactly via minimum-cost perfect matching (Dwork et al., 2001), but the authors show the fair variant of that matching problem is NP-hard, necessitating a different approach. The key methodological departure is proving total unimodularity of the ILP constraint matrix for the top-k problem—avoiding the standard rounding step that typically violates fairness constraints—thereby obtaining an exact polynomial-time solution. For the full-ranking variant, the 2-approximation is achieved by combining the exact top-k solver with a matching-based extension, improving upon the prior 3-approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact top-k algorithm is directly applicable to high-stakes selection settings (hiring, admissions, recommendations) where only the top positions matter, and the 2-approximation for full ranking meaningfully narrows the computational gap between fair and unconstrained aggregation. Empirically, the algorithm achieves near-optimality (within 1–3% of ILP-optimal) despite the 2-approximation guarantee, suggesting the theoretical bound is loose. Open questions include whether the approximation factor can be improved beyond 2, whether stronger fairness notions (e.g., block fairness) admit similar tractability, and how the approach extends to overlapping groups or group-oblivious settings where protected attributes are unavailable.
