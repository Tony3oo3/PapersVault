## Problem

High-fidelity point cloud completion and generation rely on multi-step diffusion or flow sampling, incurring latency that is incompatible with real-time perception and data-production pipelines. Existing few-step alternatives (rectification, distillation, consistency models) introduce multi-stage training complexity and quality trade-offs, and no prior work has systematically quantified how one-step completion affects downstream 3D detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266138/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For completion, the model takes a partial point cloud (200 visible points rendered from 20 random views of a ShapeNet shape) plus a permutation-invariant shape prior, and outputs a completed 2048-point cloud in a single forward pass (~5 ms on A800). For unconditional generation, it takes Gaussian noise of the same dimensionality and produces a 2048-point shape. In the PointPlug detection setting, the input is a set of detector RoIs (box parameters and local point crops); the output is refined 3D bounding boxes and fused class scores, with completion applied to at most K=2 RoIs per frame to bound latency.

## Method

3DMF extends the MeanFlow average-velocity identity to unordered point sets by enforcing permutation equivariance on all velocity fields, so that the interval-averaged velocity v̄(X_t; r,t) = v(X_t,t) − (t−r)·D_t[v̄] is well-defined regardless of point ordering. Training is teacher-free and uses two losses: a velocity-consistency MSE where the target is constructed via a single Jacobian-vector product (JVP) with stop-gradient, and a shape-level symmetric Chamfer distance between the one-step output and the ground-truth shape that regularizes global geometry and prevents drift. At inference, a single update X_0 = X_1 − v̄_θ(X_1; 0,1) replaces numerical ODE integration, yielding one-step sampling; few-step refinement partitions [0,1] into K subintervals. PointPlug adds a detector-agnostic Adaptive Completion Gate (a lightweight MLP trained on a Completion Impact Dataset) that ranks RoIs by predicted BEV-IoU gain, selects the top-K per frame for one-step 3DMF completion, and refits boxes and scores before post-processing without modifying the scene point cloud.

## Results

On ShapeNet completion (2048 points), 3DMF-5NFE achieves the best Chamfer Distance on all three categories: 0.369 (Airplane), 2.138 (Chair), and 0.975 (Car) ×10⁻³, while 3DMF-1NFE samples in just 5 ms per shape—roughly 8× faster than the next-fastest one-step baseline PSF (40 ms). On nuScenes detection, inserting PointPlug into FSHNet raises Car mAP at 50 m+ from 16.23 to 23.85 (+47% relative) with only 6 ms added latency; on KITTI, PV-RCNN-pp improves Hard 3D AP from 76.82 to 78.91. For unconditional generation, 3DMF-1NFE attains the best 1-NNA (CD) on Airplane (76.19%) at the fastest sampling rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DMF-5NFE outperforms all baselines on Chamfer Distance across Airplane, Chair, and Car, beating the closest competitor PointNSP (CD 0.401/2.702/1.384) by 8%, 21%, and 29% respectively. On EMD, 3DMF-5NFE is best on Car (1.752 vs. 2.068 for PointNSP) but trails PVD on Chair (3.188 vs. 2.939) and PointNSP on Airplane (1.121 vs. 1.008), reflecting EMD's sensitivity to fine-grained mass transport. In the one-step setting, 3DMF-1NFE is 8× faster than PSF (5 ms vs. 40 ms) and 24× faster than SoftFlow (5 ms vs. 120 ms), while remaining competitive on Car CD (1.224 vs. 1.384 for PointNSP). For 3D detection, PointPlug consistently improves every tested detector: on nuScenes, FSHNet-pp gains +0.4 NDS and +0.4 mAP with a +47% relative Car mAP improvement at 50 m+; on KITTI, both PV-RCNN-pp and Voxel-RCNN-pp improve Hard 3D AP by ~2 points and Hard BEV AP by ~1.4 points over their unmodified counterparts, with only 4–6 ms added per-frame latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MeanFlow (Geng et al., 2025a), which established an average-velocity identity enabling teacher-free one-step generative modeling, to the unordered 3D point cloud domain by restating the identity in a permutation-equivariant, set-based form. It departs from prior few-step 3D methods (rectified flows, consistency distillation, reflow) that require multi-stage teacher pipelines by training a single network from scratch under a consistency objective augmented with a shape-level Chamfer constraint. The PointPlug module additionally carves new ground by being the first systematic integration of one-step completion into modern 3D detection pipelines (FSHNet, VoxelNeXt, PV-RCNN, Voxel-RCNN), whereas prior completion work remained at the offline benchmark level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~5 ms per-shape one-step latency and 2.8 M parameter footprint make real-time point cloud completion viable for autonomous driving and robotics, where multi-step diffusion is prohibitively slow. PointPlug's finding that completion yields the largest relative gains at 50 m+ range (≈45–50% relative Car mAP improvement) highlights a concrete, targeted use case for sparse long-range LiDAR. Open questions include improving sample diversity in one-step generation (where 3DMF trails multi-step baselines), addressing the proposal-dependency limitation that prevents PointPlug from recovering false negatives with no candidate RoI, and whether the shape-level constraint generalizes to open-vocabulary or multi-category completion without per-category training.
