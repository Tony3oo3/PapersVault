## Problem

In model-heterogeneous federated learning, bandwidth-constrained clients (BCCs) are assigned fixed small sub-models that accelerate early training but become under-parameterized as the global decision boundary grows more complex, causing their updates to saturate and their contributions to be marginalized in later rounds. This capacity-induced plateau biases the global model toward full-capacity clients rather than the true optimum, especially under non-IID data. The paper asks whether sub-model density should be dynamic—gradually increasing as training progresses—to keep BCCs effective contributors throughout optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263154/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local dataset (FEMNIST, CIFAR-10, ImageNet-100, or StackOverflow with non-IID splits) and a communication budget that determines its initial sub-model density ρ_{i,k} ∈ (0,1]. The server maintains a full N-parameter global model and dispatches client-specific masked sub-models (binary masks m_{i,k} selecting which parameters each client trains). The output is a trained global model plus heterogeneous client sub-models whose densities evolve over training via GMR, with the server-to-client transmission optimized through Incremental Model Splitting that sends only density increments rather than full sub-models.

## Method

FedGMR centers on Gradual Model Restoration (GMR), which progressively increases each client's sub-model density along a multi-level ladder (e.g., 0.05→0.1→0.2→0.5→1.0) when an Early Stopping Mechanism detects that a sub-model's validation performance has plateaued for k_rest consecutive checks. To handle the latency imbalance reintroduced by restoration, the server uses semi-asynchronous coordination with a buffer-based, mask-aware aggregation rule (BuffMaskFedAvg) that normalizes each neuron only over the clients retaining it and applies staleness-aware weighting to mitigate out-of-order arrivals. The convergence analysis shows the optimization gap relative to full-model FL scales with inverse-coverage-weighted density penalties that monotonically shrink as GMR increases densities, and that mask-aware aggregation (unlike naive FedAvg or gradient averaging) preserves gradient stability during restoration by avoiding zero-padding dilution and coverage-dependent contraction.

## Results

FedGMR achieves the best test accuracy across all four datasets and three heterogeneity levels. Under High heterogeneity with Non-IID splits, it reaches 81.86% on FEMNIST, 81.68% on CIFAR-10, 58.01% on ImageNet-100, and 30.07% on StackOverflow, yielding Mean Relative Improvements (MRI) of +3.73%, +11.54%, +85.10%, and +12.68% over the six baselines respectively. The largest gains appear on ImageNet-100 (ResNet-18), where the capacity bottleneck is most severe, and shrink as models become shallower (Conv2D on FEMNIST). Under Low heterogeneity, FedGMR still leads but with smaller margins (e.g., 63.89% vs. 62.57% for FedAsync on ImageNet-100 IID).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedGMR outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and heterogeneity level. Against FedAvg (synchronous full-model FL), the gap is largest on ImageNet-100 under High heterogeneity Non-IID (58.01% vs. 47.46%, +10.55 pp), reflecting that full-model training cannot accommodate BCCs' bandwidth limits. FedAsync is the closest competitor under Low heterogeneity (62.57% vs. 63.89% on ImageNet-100 IID), but falls behind under High heterogeneity where BCCs contribute fewer/stale updates. Among MHFL baselines, FIARSE is the strongest on ImageNet-100 (54.00% IID, 48.27% Non-IID under High heterogeneity), yet FedGMR still leads by 6.84 and 9.74 pp respectively. FedRolex, which uses region rotation to address similar capacity issues, degrades most severely on deep models (32.17% IID, 15.26% Non-IID on ImageNet-100 under High heterogeneity), far below FedGMR's 60.84% and 58.01%. HeteroFL and FjORD, which use static width/depth scaling or ordered dropout, plateau early on ImageNet-100 (41.90% and 41.18% IID under High heterogeneity), confirming that fixed sub-model capacity is the primary bottleneck that GMR addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-heterogeneous federated learning (MHFL) paradigm established by HeteroFL (Diao et al., 2021) and subsequent methods (FjORD, FedRolex, FIARSE), all of which assign fixed-capacity sub-models to bandwidth-constrained clients. The key departure is the observation that static sub-model densities inevitably saturate as training progresses, leading to 'capacity-induced marginalization' of lightweight clients—a limitation the authors argue prior MHFL work has overlooked. FedGMR addresses this by introducing Gradual Model Restoration (GMR), a dynamic density schedule triggered by an Early Stopping Mechanism, combined with a mask-aware aggregation rule (MaskFedAvg/BuffMaskFedAvg) that generalizes FedAvg to handle heterogeneous coordinate coverage. The asynchronous coordination and importance-based mask construction draw on asynchronous FL (FedAsync) and structured pruning literature (Molchanov et al., 2019), respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedGMR provides a principled, theoretically grounded solution to a practical bottleneck in real-world FL deployments where clients have genuinely different bandwidth budgets. The convergence analysis showing that the optimization gap scales with average sub-model density and monotonically shrinks under GMR gives practitioners confidence that dynamic restoration is not merely heuristic. However, the paper acknowledges that restoration increases per-update latency, that extremely resource-constrained clients may only support partial restoration, and that asynchronous coordination can amplify participation skew—leaving open the question of how to optimally balance restoration frequency against staleness in large-scale, highly skewed deployments. The method's generality (applicable to multiple MHFL baselines and architectures) suggests it could become a standard component in heterogeneous FL pipelines.
