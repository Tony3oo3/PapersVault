## Problem

Existing neural operators for subsurface simulation are constrained to structured grids or fixed mesh topologies, cannot capture the intrinsic spatial heterogeneity where permeability varies over orders of magnitude within meters, and lack the ability to learn directly from adaptive mesh refinement (HR-adaptive) simulations. No prior architecture simultaneously resolves sharp local heterogeneity (fault interfaces, saturation fronts) and long-range pressure propagation on arbitrary, time-varying meshes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262005/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

APT consumes input fields (permeability, porosity, initial saturation/pressure, well locations and controls) sampled as point clouds on arbitrary meshes—structured, unstructured PEBI, or time-varying DMO topologies—with a fixed number of sampled cells per training step (e.g., 1,024 for faulted 2D, 262,144 for basin-scale 3D). A query time is provided as a scalar condition. The decoder produces output fields (pressure, saturation, or temperature) at user-specified spatial query locations via cross-attention, decoupling output resolution from input mesh topology and enabling super-resolution queries at arbitrary points.

## Method

The core contribution is a fused encoder that runs two parallel pathways: a Global Perceiver Branch that projects N_points input features onto N_s learnable supernode queries via cross-attention (capturing long-range dependencies at sub-quadratic cost), and a Local GNO Branch that aggregates neighborhood information through radius-graph construction with mean pooling (capturing fine-grained heterogeneity). A learned gating vector G∈[0,1]^d adaptively blends the two representations (v_fused = G⊙v_attn + (1−G)⊙v_gno), where the global branch mimics integral-operator behavior and the local branch adheres to differential-operator sparsity. The fused features are compressed into fixed-size latent tokens processed by DiT blocks with DiT-style temporal and conditional modulation, followed by a self-attention approximator over latent tokens and a cross-attention decoder that queries at arbitrary spatial locations. Training is direct one-step (initial condition to any query time) with a relative l_p loss, avoiding autoregressive error accumulation and natively supporting irregular temporal sampling.

## Results

On the 2D faulted CO₂ storage benchmark, APT achieves 0.11% pressure error and 0.32% saturation error, outperforming the strongest graph-based baseline MGN-LSTM by 1.8× on pressure and 3.75× on saturation. On the adaptive-mesh ATES benchmark, APT reaches 99.0% R² (0.011 relative L₂) by learning directly from Dynamic Mesh Optimization point clouds, a 4× improvement over interpolated grid-based baselines. On the basin-scale LGR dataset, APT matches FNO-DeepONet pressure accuracy (0.56%) while using 97% fewer parameters (17.9M vs. 682.4M) and a single unified model instead of a 5-model cascade. APT also demonstrates mesh-convergent super-resolution on CarBench (R² improves from 96.0 to 96.5 at full resolution) while all baselines degrade by 2–10%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APT outperforms all eight baselines on the 2D faulted CO₂ storage benchmark: it reduces saturation error from 1.20% (MGN-LSTM, the strongest graph baseline) to 0.32% and pressure error from 0.20% to 0.11%, while also beating transformer-based methods Transolver (3.82% saturation), UPT (4.33%), MINO (6.89%), and HOOD (6.48%). On the Cartesian hydrocarbon benchmark, APT halves the saturation error of U-FNO (0.32% vs. 0.66%) with 2.5× fewer parameters (12M vs. 33M), and matches FNO's pressure accuracy at a fraction of the model size. On the adaptive-mesh ATES task, APT reduces relative L₂ error by 2.2× vs. Transolver (0.011 vs. 0.0237) and 3.6× vs. UPT (0.011 vs. 0.0401), while MINO cannot run at all due to >80 GB memory overflow. On the basin-scale LGR dataset, APT achieves comparable pressure error to FNO-DeepONet (0.56% vs. 0.56%) and Nested FNO (0.56% vs. 0.47%) but with 97% fewer parameters and a single model instead of a 5-model cascade. In the CarBench super-resolution evaluation, APT is the only architecture whose R² improves at full resolution (96.0→96.5), whereas Point Transformer, TripNet, Transolver, and AB-UPT all degrade by 8–10%. Inference speed benchmarks show APT completing a 19-step rollout in 0.002 s (19,873× faster than GEOS), slightly slower than UPT (0.001 s) but with an order-of-magnitude lower saturation error.

## Relation to Prior Work

APT sits at the intersection of three architectural lineages: the Perceiver's cross-attention with learnable supernode queries (Jaegle et al., 2021), the Graph Neural Operator's radius-graph message passing (Li et al., 2020), and the DiT-style adaptive modulation (Peebles & Xie, 2023). Its key departure from prior physics transformers (UPT, Transolver) is the parallel local-global encoder with a learned gated fusion that operates at the feature-extraction stage rather than as a decoding mechanism, specifically motivated by the multi-scale heterogeneity of subsurface media. The paper also positions itself against hierarchical cascades (Nested FNO) that require separate models per refinement level, and against graph-based simulators (MGN) whose local message passing cannot capture basin-scale pressure propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a single unified model can learn directly from HR-adaptive DMO meshes and across datasets with incompatible LGR structures (boosting R² from 32% to 87% via cross-dataset training) opens a concrete path toward subsurface foundation models that can ingest heterogeneous public simulation repositories. A key open question is whether the favorable scaling behavior observed on ATES generalizes to multi-physics pre-training, and whether fine-tuning a pretrained APT on new geological domains can match task-specific training. The nuanced super-resolution finding—that architectural mesh-invariance does not guarantee super-resolution when training simulations are not themselves mesh-converged—provides an important calibration for the field's expectations of neural operators in data-limited subsurface settings.
