## Problem

State-of-the-art conditional membership inference attacks (e.g., LiRA, Attack-R) require training dozens to hundreds of reference models to estimate per-example score distributions, incurring prohibitive computational cost. Moreover, no prior work provides a formal theoretical explanation for why conditional attacks outperform marginal ones, or why Bayesian posterior sampling specifically improves attack power over simply retraining on different data subsets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained target model's weights, a query instance (x*, y*) with its true label, and a single reference model trained on a disjoint data population. It produces a binary membership decision (member/non-member) along with a p-value from a one-sided Student's t-test. The score function is the hinge score (difference between the true-class logit and the maximum competing logit), and the reference model's posterior is restricted to the last-layer weights via a Kronecker-factored Laplace approximation, so only one forward pass through the frozen backbone is needed per query.

## Method

BMIA trains one reference model on a disjoint dataset, then applies a last-layer Laplace approximation (with KFAC Hessian factorization) to obtain a Gaussian posterior over the final-layer weights. From this posterior, M weight samples are drawn, and the hinge score of the query is computed under each sample to form a reference score distribution. A one-sided one-sample t-test then compares the target model's score against this distribution, with the variance decomposed into intra-model (posterior) and inter-model (dataset-sampling) components. The key theoretical contribution is showing that Bayesian sampling specifically reduces the intra-model variance term σ²_intra by a factor of 1/M, tightening the null distribution and expanding the rejection region, which provably increases TPR (Theorem 3.2). A multi-reference extension (MR-BMIA) uses a mixture Laplace approximation across K reference models to additionally reduce inter-model variance, with degrees of freedom estimated via the Welch-Satterthwaite approximation.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, BMIA with a single reference model achieves 35.75% TPR at 1% FPR, a 54% improvement over LiRA (n=8) at 23.20%, while requiring only 1/8 the training time (26.44 min vs. 211.52 min). On CIFAR-10, BMIA reaches 9.48% TPR at 1% FPR versus 8.32% for LiRA (n=8) and 5.55% for RMIA (n=8). Across five text datasets, BMIA (n=1) outperforms all multi-reference baselines, e.g., achieving 33.47% TPR at 1% FPR on 20 Newsgroups versus 30.32% for LiRA (n=8). The multi-reference variant MR-BMIA (K=64) further extends the lead, reaching 45.57% TPR at 1% FPR on CIFAR-100 versus 43.33% for LiRA (n=64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BMIA (n=1) decisively outperforms the marginal baseline Attack-P on every dataset, e.g., 9.48% vs. 1.17% TPR at 1% FPR on CIFAR-10. Against the multi-reference conditional attacks, BMIA with a single reference model beats LiRA (n=8) by 54% on CIFAR-100 (35.75% vs. 23.20% at 1% FPR) and by 13% on CIFAR-10 (9.48% vs. 8.32%), while using only 1/8 the training time. RMIA (n=8) is outperformed on both CIFAR-10 (9.48% vs. 5.55%) and CIFAR-100 (35.75% vs. 16.91%). QMIA, which uses quantile regression with a single model, is substantially weaker (1.23% vs. 9.48% on CIFAR-10 at 1% FPR), confirming that capturing epistemic uncertainty via Bayesian sampling is critical. Among alternative Bayesian inference methods (SWAG, MC Dropout, BatchEnsemble, Packed Ensemble, Masksembles), the Laplace approximation used by BMIA achieves the highest TPR at every FPR level on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 9.48% vs. 7.94% for MC Dropout at 1% FPR). In the multi-reference setting (K=64), MR-BMIA extends its lead over LiRA on CIFAR-100 (45.57% vs. 43.33% at 1% FPR) and CIFAR-10 (12.48% vs. 11.74%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional membership inference attack framework introduced by LiRA (Carlini et al., 2022) and Attack-R (Ye et al., 2022), which require training multiple reference models to estimate per-example score distributions. The key departure is replacing multi-reference empirical estimation with Bayesian sampling from a single reference model's posterior, obtained via last-layer Laplace approximation (Kristiadi et al., 2020; Daxberger et al., 2021). The paper also positions itself against QMIA (Bertran et al., 2024), which uses quantile regression to predict thresholds but captures only aleatoric uncertainty, whereas BMIA captures both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty through the full posterior. A theoretical contribution formalizes why conditional attacks outperform marginal ones and specifically why Bayesian sampling helps, via a variance decomposition into intra-model and inter-model components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: BMIA achieves state-of-the-art TPR at 1/8 the computational cost of LiRA(n=8), making conditional MIAs feasible for large-scale model auditing where training hundreds of reference models is prohibitive. The variance decomposition (Proposition 3.1) provides the first formal explanation of why Bayesian sampling improves attack power, clarifying that the benefit comes from reducing intra-model variance rather than inter-model variance. Open questions include whether the last-layer Laplace approximation captures sufficient posterior structure for very large models (e.g., LLMs), whether the Gaussian score assumption holds in all practical settings, and whether the approach extends to black-box or label-only attack settings where full model access is unavailable.
