## Problem

Prior mechanistic interpretability approaches to LLM reasoning rely on human-defined, word-level concepts (e.g., reflection, backtracking) and supervised contrastive pairs to construct steering vectors, which is infeasible for capturing the full spectrum of reasoning behaviors—many of which are fluid, overlapping, and difficult to annotate at scale. This paper asks whether the geometry of reasoning behaviors can be discovered entirely unsupervised from internal activations, without relying on predefined behavioral taxonomies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265983/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes chain-of-thought responses segmented into sentence-level steps (delimited by \n\n), extracting residual-stream hidden representations at the delimiter token position for a single chosen transformer layer. These step-level activations (not token-level) are the training signal for the SAE. The output is a set of SAE decoder columns—each a unit-norm vector in the original activation space—that can be injected or projected out at the last token of each reasoning step during inference to amplify or suppress a specific behavior, yielding a modified chain-of-thought response without any model retraining.

## Method

RISE trains a sparse autoencoder (hidden dimension D=2048, ReLU nonlinearity, L1 sparsity penalty) on step-level activations from chain-of-thought sequences, so that each decoder column corresponds to an atomic reasoning direction under the linear representation hypothesis. A theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) shows that under incoherence, sparsity, and separation assumptions, the SAE decoder recovers the ground-truth behavior dictionary up to permutation and positive scaling. For intervention, the model projects out (or adds) the component of the hidden state along a selected decoder column at each step boundary: h' = h − α·wᵢ(wᵢᵀh). Beyond known behaviors, RISE discovers novel directions by optimizing a score vector S over decoder columns to minimize output entropy, revealing a concentrated cluster of confidence-related vectors that causally reduce reflection and backtracking while shifting the model toward more direct calculation.

## Results

On R1-1.5B, RISE's confidence-vector steering improves reasoning accuracy by up to 4.66 points while reducing token usage by 13.69% compared to SEAL and TIP (Figure 9). Causal interventions on SAE-derived reflection vectors shift reflection steps on AIME25 from 90.5 (vanilla) to 58.6 (negative) or 131.0 (positive), confirming dose-dependent control. The confidence vector reduces reflection steps from 90.53 to 33.77 and backtracking steps from 35.50 to 5.93 on AIME25, while generalizing to GPQA-Diamond and KnowLogic (Tables 1–2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RISE against two prior activation-steering methods for reasoning control: SEAL (Chen et al., 2025) and TIP (Wang et al., 2025d), both of which rely on human-defined steering directions. On R1-1.5B, RISE's sample-specific confidence-vector steering (learning combination coefficients of top-3 vectors at test time) outperforms both SEAL and TIP, improving reasoning accuracy by up to 4.66 points while simultaneously reducing token usage by 13.69% (Figure 9). Unlike SEAL and TIP, which require predefined behavioral labels to construct steering vectors, RISE discovers these directions entirely unsupervised via SAE training, making it applicable to behaviors that are difficult to specify at the word level (e.g., confidence). The key advantage is that RISE's vectors generalize across domains (MATH → GPQA-Diamond, KnowLogic) without retraining, whereas the supervised baselines are tied to their annotation protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

RISE builds on the Sparse Autoencoder architecture (Cunningham et al., 2023) and the Linear Representation Hypothesis (Park et al., 2023), repurposing SAEs from their original role in feature discovery for language modeling to the specific domain of reasoning-behavior identification. It departs from the dominant activation-steering paradigm (DiffMean, representation engineering) that requires human-annotated contrastive pairs, and from prior reasoning-behavior studies (SEAL, TIP, Venhoff et al.) that target a small set of predefined behaviors like reflection and backtracking. The closest prior work, RESA (Wang et al., 2025a), also applies SAEs to reasoning models but uses them as a bridge for supervised fine-tuning to transfer reasoning ability, rather than as an interpretability tool for dissecting the internal geometry of the reasoning process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that reasoning behaviors are linearly encoded and discoverable without human labels, RISE offers a scalable path toward mapping the full spectrum of reasoning dynamics in LLMs, moving beyond the handful of behaviors that are easy to define at the token level. The confidence-vector case study is particularly notable: it shows that a concept difficult to operationalize in text space (model confidence) has a coherent, causally effective geometric signature in activation space. Key limitations include primary evaluation on a 1.5B model (R1-1.5B), SAE training on a single layer, and a relatively small training set (500 MATH examples); open questions include whether the discovered structure persists at 70B+ scale, whether SAEs can discover genuinely novel behaviors beyond confidence, and whether the linear representation assumption holds for more entangled or higher-order reasoning patterns.
