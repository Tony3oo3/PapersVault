## Problem

Existing heterogeneous federated learning (HtFL) methods—whether knowledge-distillation, model-split, or mutual-learning based—fail to address fine-grained feature-level misalignment caused by simultaneous data and model heterogeneity. This leads to scattered, drifting representations across clients, misaligned class centers, and overlapping clusters that degrade both personalization and generalization. The paper targets this gap by asking how to calibrate heterogeneous local representations against a shared global space without relying on public proxy data or logit-only transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263027/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client processes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for DomainNet) or text articles (AG News) through its own heterogeneous architecture (e.g., ResNet18, GoogLeNet, MobileNetV2) alongside a shared 4-layer CNN global extractor, producing class labels as output. Feature dimensions are unified via average pooling to d₁=512 (local) and d₂=256 (global prefix subspace). Only the small homogeneous extractor's parameters are uploaded per round; the full heterogeneous local model and its projector remain on-device, keeping communication at 39.87 MB per round.

## Method

FedARC fuses local and global representations via a client-specific trainable projector P_k that concatenates and projects features from the heterogeneous extractor F_k^ex and the shared homogeneous extractor G^ex into a unified space R^{d₁}. Adaptive residual compensation adds learnable client-specific bias vectors R_i^{F_k} and R_i^G to the fused representation, correcting per-client distribution shifts at the feature level before either the heterogeneous head (consuming full R^{d₁}) or the homogeneous head (consuming the prefix subspace R^{d₂}) produces predictions. A semantic anchor alignment module computes a global consensus mean at initialization and regularizes each client's running feature mean toward this anchor via MSE loss, stabilizing cross-client aggregation under drift. Only the homogeneous extractor G^ex is uploaded and FedAvg-aggregated on the server, while all heterogeneous components stay local. The paper proves an O(1/T) non-convex convergence rate under standard smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions, and demonstrates up to 2.63% accuracy gains over nine SOTA HtFL baselines across five benchmarks.

## Results

On the primary cross-device setting (N=50, ρ=20%, β=0.1) with HtFE^img_8, FedARC achieves 86.14% on CIFAR-10, 42.25% on CIFAR-100, 50.18% on Flowers102, and 27.10% on Tiny-ImageNet, outperforming the strongest baseline (FD at 39.62% on CIFAR-100) by up to 2.63%. Under the cross-silo setting (N=10, ρ=100%), FedARC reaches 89.22% on CIFAR-10 and 44.21% on CIFAR-100, leading the best competitor (FD at 87.78% / 42.67%) by 1.44% and 1.54% respectively. FedARC also maintains the best accuracy across all feature dimensions (d₁ from 64 to 1024) and all local training epoch counts (E from 1 to 5), and achieves the highest accuracy on the DomainNet feature-shift benchmark (33.30% avg) and AG News text classification (93.52% on HtFE^txt_2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedARC outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and both FL settings. Among KD-based methods, FD is the strongest (e.g., 87.78% on cross-silo CIFAR-10, 39.62% on cross-device CIFAR-100) but still trails FedARC by 1.44% and 2.63% respectively; FedProto is the weakest overall (36.12% on cross-silo CIFAR-100), and FedTGP (55.57% on cross-silo Flowers102) is competitive on Flowers102 yet still 2.10% below FedARC. Among model-split methods, LG-FedAvg and FedGH are mid-tier (40–42% on cross-silo CIFAR-100), while FedGen is weakest in this group (38.68%). Among mutual-learning methods, pFedES and FedMRL are the closest competitors (42.37% and 42.57% on cross-silo CIFAR-100; 38.77% and 38.93% on cross-device CIFAR-100), yet FedARC leads them by 1.84% and 3.32% respectively. Notably, FedARC's advantage widens under stronger heterogeneity: on cross-device CIFAR-100 with HtFE^img_9 (9 architectures), FedARC scores 37.28% versus 36.62% (FD) and 36.55% (FedMRL), and on the pathological 2/10-class-per-client setting it reaches 62.01% versus 59.92% (FedMRL) and 59.81% (pFedES).

## Relation to Prior Work

FedARC builds directly on the FedAvg communication protocol and weighted aggregation scheme, extending them to the heterogeneous setting where clients maintain distinct architectures. It is most closely related to mutual-learning HtFL methods (pFedES, FedMRL, FedKD) that co-train a small homogeneous model alongside a local heterogeneous model, but departs from them by introducing a trainable projector that fuses local and global features into a shared space, followed by client-specific residual biases and a global semantic anchor for alignment. Unlike KD-based methods that transfer only logits or prototypes, and model-split methods that share fixed architectural components, FedARC performs calibration in a learned fused representation space, addressing fine-grained feature misalignment that prior approaches leave unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedARC offers a practical, communication-efficient recipe for the increasingly common deployment scenario where both data distributions and model architectures differ across clients, with a provable O(1/T) non-convex convergence rate. The residual compensation and anchor alignment are lightweight (a few bias vectors and an MSE term) yet contribute meaningfully to accuracy, suggesting they could be grafted onto other HtFL frameworks. Open questions include whether the initialization-time global anchor remains valid under long-horizon distribution drift, how the method scales to thousands of clients with highly diverse modalities, and whether the per-client projector parameters introduce additional privacy surface beyond the shared extractor.
