## Problem

Deploying LoRA-based federated fine-tuning on pre-trained models is severely degraded by label noise, because the low-rank constraint forces the compressed parameter manifold to overfit noisy samples, causing weight divergence and harmful aggregation bias. Existing robust federated methods either target full-parameter models or treat LoRA adapters as generic weight vectors, ignoring the intrinsic structural asymmetry between the down-projection matrix A and up-projection matrix B under noisy supervision.

## Input / Output

The method operates on frozen pre-trained backbones (12-layer ViT for 224×224 RGB images, RoBERTa-base for 128-token text sequences) and trains only LoRA adapters of rank r=4. During training, each client's local dataset contains inputs with potentially corrupted labels (pair-flip, symmetric-flip, or instance-dependent noise at rates up to 0.5). At inference, only the clean branch is used for prediction; the noisy branch is discarded, so the output is a standard class label over C categories.

## Method

FedDR-LoRA exploits a discovered robustness asymmetry: the down-projection matrix A learns a stable feature subspace invariant to label noise, while the up-projection matrix B is highly sensitive and fits noise patterns. The architecture decouples B into a clean branch B^c and a noisy branch B^n that share a single A, with A frozen during noisy-branch updates to protect the feature subspace. Training proceeds in three phases: (1) a dual-branch warm-up using extreme loss quantiles to seed each branch, (2) decoupled fine-tuning where an EMA-smoothed loss discrepancy between branches dynamically assigns samples to clean or noisy subsets, and (3) noisy-branch negative learning that uses the clean branch as a positive teacher (soft targets) and the noisy branch as a negative teacher (minimizing negative KL divergence) to push clean predictions away from noise patterns. At aggregation, A is kept local to preserve fine-grained feature alignment, while B^c and B^n are separately averaged across clients to build global consensus without mixing clean and noise-specific semantics.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ViT backbone, FedDR-LoRA achieves 91.43% (Pairflip, μ=0.2), 91.37% (Symflip, μ=0.2), 88.30% (Pairflip, μ=0.4), and 90.92% (Symflip, μ=0.4) average test accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline SLoRA by up to 24.7% under the most challenging Pairflip μ=0.4 setting. On 20 Newsgroups (RoBERTa-base), FedDR-LoRA reaches 54.73% under Pairflip μ=0.4, a 9.2% margin over the next-best method. On Tiny-ImageNet under 40% Pairflip noise, FedDR-LoRA achieves 87.07%, surpassing SLoRA (65.29%) by 21.78%. The method uses 0.4351% trainable parameters (vs. 0.3503% for FedLR) with a per-round computation cost of 646.93 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedDR-LoRA wins against all nine federated LoRA baselines across every noise configuration tested. The most dramatic gap appears under Pairflip μ=0.4, where FedDR-LoRA (88.30%) exceeds the best baseline SLoRA (63.58%) by 24.72%, while methods like FFA-LoRA (57.86%) and RoLoRA (56.36%) suffer near-collapse. Under Symflip μ=0.4, the margin narrows to 3.74% over SLoRA (87.18%), reflecting that symmetric noise is less structurally damaging to the low-rank manifold. Against the closest robust baseline RFedLR (63.06% Pairflip μ=0.4), FedDR-LoRA gains 25.24%, and notably does so without requiring proxy data on the server. On 20 Newsgroups, FedDR-LoRA leads by 9.2% over FlexLoRA under Pairflip μ=0.4 (54.73% vs. 45.53%), and on Tiny-ImageNet it surpasses SLoRA by 21.78% under the same setting. When compared against LNL-augmented FedLR (Table 2), FedDR-LoRA outperforms FedLR+DivideMix (70.12%) by 18.18% under Pairflip μ=0.4, confirming that the architectural decoupling provides gains beyond what standard noise-robust losses or sample-selection strategies can deliver in the federated low-rank setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LoRA's low-rank decomposition (Hu et al., 2022) and the negative learning paradigm for noisy labels (Kim et al., 2019), but departs from prior federated LoRA work by identifying a structural robustness asymmetry between the down-projection matrix A and up-projection matrix B under label noise. While methods like RFedLR (Fang & Ye, 2025) address robust federated PEFT, they treat the LoRA adapter as a monolithic weight vector and rely on server-side proxy data; FedDR-LoRA instead exploits the intrinsic functional distinction between A (stable feature projector) and B (noise-sensitive semantic aligner) to design a dual-branch architecture with asymmetric aggregation. The approach also extends the line of work on A/B role differentiation in federated LoRA (FedSA-LoRA, FFA-LoRA, RoLoRA), which previously framed the asymmetry in terms of shared vs. personalized knowledge or aggregation bias, by reframing it through the lens of label-noise robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The robustness asymmetry finding provides a principled structural explanation for why LoRA is particularly vulnerable to label noise in federated settings, and the dual-branch design offers a clean architectural solution that avoids the proxy-data dependency of prior robust federated PEFT methods. The large and consistent performance gains (up to ~25 points under severe Pairflip noise) across vision and NLP tasks, multiple noise types, and heterogeneous client settings suggest the approach is broadly applicable. Limitations include the added computational overhead of maintaining two B matrices, the multi-stage training protocol (warm-up → decoupled fine-tuning → negative learning) which adds implementation complexity, and evaluation restricted to ViT and RoBERTa backbones. Open questions include whether the A/B robustness asymmetry generalizes to other PEFT methods (adapters, prefix-tuning) and whether the approach scales to large language models in truly resource-constrained edge deployments.
