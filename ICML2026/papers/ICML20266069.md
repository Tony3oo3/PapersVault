## Problem

Prior work has observed that LLM backdoors implanted during instruction-tuning often collapse after routine downstream supervised fine-tuning, but no existing study explains *why* this fragility occurs. This paper identifies the geometric cause—conventional poisoning drives the backdoor loss into a narrow, sharp basin in parameter space—and asks whether a backdoor can be made to persist through such SFT by reshaping that basin.

## Input / Output

The poisoning stage consumes a clean instruction-response dataset (Alpaca-52K) plus a small poisoned subset (10% by default) in which a trigger (a sentence, a single token, or a phrase) is appended to the instruction and the response is replaced with an attacker-specified string (e.g., "You are stupid" or "I cannot help"). The resulting backdoored checkpoint is then subjected to trigger-free downstream SFT on one of three clean tasks (SST-2, GSM8K, GPTeacher-Instruct). The key output metric is the attack success rate (ASR) measured on 1,000 trigger-inserted Dolly prompts after SFT, alongside clean-task accuracy to confirm utility is preserved.

## Method

BAD-BOOM extends Sharpness-Aware Minimization by replacing its isotropic ℓ₂ perturbation ball with a Fisher-induced ellipsoidal constraint. At each training step, the diagonal empirical Fisher information is estimated from a poisoned mini-batch (squared gradients of the backdoor loss), identifying which parameters are most sensitive to the backdoor behavior. The inner maximization then allocates a larger perturbation budget along those sensitive directions, with the closed-form solution ε* ≈ ρA∇L(θ)/√(∇L(θ)ᵀA∇L(θ)), where A is the diagonal Fisher matrix. A two-pass forward/backward procedure (perturb → evaluate loss at perturbed point → update) encourages the optimizer to converge to a broad, smooth basin where the backdoor loss remains low across a wide neighborhood of parameters. This geometric reshaping makes the backdoor robust to the modest parameter drift induced by downstream SFT, consistently preserving ~90–97% ASR across three models, three attacks, and three downstream tasks where AdamW and vanilla SAM often collapse to near-zero ASR.

## Results

After poisoning, all methods achieve ~97% ASR on Alpaca, confirming successful backdoor implantation. However, after trigger-free downstream SFT, BAD-BOOM consistently maintains 90–97% ASR across all three downstream tasks (SST-2, GSM8K, GPTeacher-Instruct), all three attacks, and all three models, while AdamW and SAM frequently collapse to near-0% ASR (e.g., Llama3.2-1B drops to 0.0% ASR on SST-2 under AdamW for all three attacks). Downstream utility (ACC) is preserved within 1–3 percentage points of the baselines, indicating the persistence gain does not come at a meaningful utility cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to AdamW, BAD-BOOM dramatically outperforms on post-SFT ASR in nearly every setting: on Llama3.2-1B under Sentiment Steering, AdamW yields 0.0% ASR on SST-2 and GSM8K for all three attacks, while BAD-BOOM maintains 97.0–97.2% ASR. On Qwen3-0.6B, AdamW's ASR drops to 7.3% (SST-2) and 35.7% (GPTeacher) for AddSent, whereas BAD-BOOM holds at 97.1% and 96.5% respectively. Compared to SAM, BAD-BOOM is substantially more consistent: SAM sometimes preserves high ASR (e.g., 95.4% on Qwen3-0.6B SST-2 with Sleeper) but frequently collapses to 0.0% (e.g., Qwen3-0.6B SST-2 with AddSent, or Llama3.2-1B GSM8K with all attacks), while BAD-BOOM never drops below ~47% ASR and typically stays above 90%. In terms of downstream utility (ACC), BAD-BOOM is within 1–4 percentage points of AdamW and SAM across all tasks, confirming that the persistence improvement is achieved by reshaping the local loss geometry rather than sacrificing clean-task performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) by replacing its isotropic ℓ₂ perturbation ball with a Fisher-induced ellipsoidal constraint, allocating larger perturbation budgets to backdoor-sensitive parameters. It builds on the observation (Hubinger et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025) that LLM backdoors are fragile under downstream SFT, but departs from prior work by providing the first geometric explanation for this fragility—namely, that conventional poisoning places the backdoor in a narrow, sharp basin. The Fisher-SAM connection draws on Kim et al. (2022), but here it is repurposed specifically to smooth the backdoor objective rather than improve generalization, and the empirical Fisher is estimated from poisoned samples to identify backdoor-critical directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric framing—that backdoor persistence is governed by the local curvature of the poisoned objective—gives defenders a concrete diagnostic tool: if a model's backdoor basin is narrow, it will likely collapse under routine fine-tuning, and if it is broad, it will persist. This reframes backdoor auditing from a black-box behavioral test to a geometric property that can be probed with controlled perturbations. Open questions include whether the insight scales to much larger models (experiments use ≤1.7B parameters), whether RL-based post-training (which the paper notes is less destructive than SFT) interacts differently with basin geometry, and whether the same Fisher-ellipsoidal smoothing can be inverted as a defense (i.e., deliberately sharpening the basin to make backdoors more fragile). The ~6 GB extra memory and 1.35× training overhead are limited to the poisoning stage, making the approach practical for threat modeling.
