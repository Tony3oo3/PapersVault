## Problem

Post-training quantization of diffusion models introduces errors that accumulate across iterative denoising steps, distorting the timestep-wise marginal distribution and degrading sample quality. Existing mitigations either reshape weights (preserving only stepwise predictions, not the overall distribution) or inject approximated Gaussian noise (restricted to stochastic samplers and failing on deterministic or flow-matching frameworks). No prior method preserves the generative distribution under quantization across all sampler types while remaining compatible with integer-only inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265377/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a plug-and-play post-processing layer applied to an already-quantized diffusion model (LDM-4, DiT-XL, or FLUX.1 at W4A8, W4A4, or W6A6 precision). At inference, it takes the same inputs as the base model—a text prompt or class label—and produces a generated image at 256×256 or 1024×1024 resolution, without adding any extra denoising steps or changing the model architecture. Calibration requires a one-time pass over ~3,000 images to estimate per-timestep correlation slopes, residual variances, and kurtosis values, stored as less than 1 KB of scalar parameters.

## Method

The framework has two components. First, Distribution-Calibrated Noise Compensation (DCNC) decomposes the quantization error into a correlated part (absorbed by rescaling) and an independent residual, then adds a zero-mean uniform component whose variance is analytically derived from the residual's excess kurtosis to drive the combined distribution's kurtosis to zero, yielding a faithful Gaussian approximation. Second, the Deformable Noise Scheduler (DNS) proves that the quantized reverse process inflates the marginal variance by an additive term C₁²σ²_{ε,t}, and shows this is exactly equivalent to shifting the diffusion timestep from t−1 to a non-integer τ; rather than rounding τ, DNS redefines the variance schedule α_{t-1}^q so that the shifted timestep aligns with the original discrete step, preserving the full-precision marginal distribution without extra steps. This distribution-preserving formulation works identically for stochastic (DDPM), deterministic (DDIM), and flow-matching (FLUX) samplers, and composes with any existing PTQ method.

## Results

Across three backbone architectures (LDM-4, DiT-XL, FLUX.1), DNS consistently improves FID over both the quantized baseline and PTQD. On DiT-XL (W4A8, PTQ4DiT), DNS reduces FID from 9.83 to 8.51, surpassing even the FP16 baseline (9.81); on LDM-4 (W4A8, Q-Diffusion), FID drops from 10.68 to 9.81; and on FLUX.1 (W4A4, SVDQuant with 6-bit LoRA), FID improves from 27.16 to 26.22. Additional metrics (sFID, IS, IR) show consistent gains, while CLIP scores remain essentially unchanged (≤0.2%), confirming semantic alignment is preserved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNS outperforms all compared baselines across every backbone and quantization setting. On LDM-4 (W4A8), DNS reduces FID from 10.68 (Q-Diffusion) to 9.81, a 8.1% improvement, and also beats Q-Diffusion+PTQD (10.32). On DiT-XL (W4A8), the gain is most dramatic: FID drops from 9.83 (PTQ4DiT) to 8.51 (13.4% improvement), while PTQ4DiT+PTQD degrades severely to 14.01, showing PTQD's instability on deterministic samplers. On FLUX.1 (flow matching), DNS improves SVDQuant W4A4 with 6-bit LoRA from FID 27.16 to 26.22, and with 16-bit LoRA from 22.95 to 22.78. In supplementary experiments, DNS also improves TFMQ-DM on CIFAR-10 (FID 12.83→11.12) and PTQ4DM on ImageNet-64 (FID 25.36→24.32), confirming generality across PTQ methods. Notably, on DiT-XL the DNS-enhanced quantized model (FID 8.51) surpasses the unquantized FP16 baseline (FID 9.81), demonstrating that distribution-preserving correction can more than fully recover quantization loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantization-error-absorption line initiated by PTQD (He et al., 2023), which injected the independent quantization residual into the stochastic noise term of the sampler. The key departure is twofold: (i) it shows empirically that the residual is heavy-tailed (excess kurtosis) rather than Gaussian, and corrects this with a calibrated uniform component (DCNC); and (ii) it reframes the entire correction as a timestep shift in the noise schedule (DNS), which preserves the marginal distribution rather than the trajectory. This distribution-preserving formulation removes PTQD's restriction to stochastic samplers and extends naturally to deterministic samplers and flow-matching models, while remaining plug-and-play over existing PTQ schemes (Q-Diffusion, PTQ4DiT, SVDQuant).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is enabling fully integer inference pipelines for large diffusion and flow-matching models (e.g., quantizing SVDQuant's FP16 LoRA branch to 6-bit), which is critical for deployment on integer-centric edge hardware. The method adds negligible overhead (~5.7 s for 30k images, <1 KB parameters) and requires no architectural changes or extra sampling steps. Limitations include the need for a one-time calibration pass, the assumption of Gaussian conditional paths (restricting applicability to standard diffusion/flow-matching formulations), and the fact that PSNR to full-precision outputs decreases slightly, reflecting different but equally valid sampling trajectories. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video diffusion, non-Gaussian conditional paths, or extremely low-bit regimes (1–2 bit) where the Gaussian approximation of the residual may break down.
