## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has become the standard paradigm for training reasoning LLMs, but it is prohibitively resource-intensive: it demands large training datasets with high-quality reward signals and performs K×G rollouts per step, incurring substantial computational overhead. Prior efficiency efforts reduce sample counts heuristically but do not fundamentally address the rollout cost or provide a principled understanding of the minimal data required to elicit reasoning capabilities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a compact set of math reasoning problems (as few as 16 instances from DeepScaleR) as training data, with each instance producing a scalar binary reward via rule-based answer verification. At each training step, DoPR receives a mini-batch of K prompts, performs one rollout per prompt to gather reward statistics, then allocates the full group budget G to a single selected prompt for the GRPO policy update. The trained model outputs a reasoning chain followed by a verified answer, evaluated via Pass@1 accuracy on benchmarks like AIME24, MATH, and GSM8K.

## Method

DoPR (Dynamic One-Shot Policy Refinement) replaces GRPO's uniform K×G rollout schedule with a dynamic selection mechanism that performs only G+(K−1) rollouts per step. For each sample in the mini-batch, it maintains exponentially weighted moving averages of reward mean and variance, then computes an Entropy-Modulated Upper Confidence Bound (EM-UCB) acquisition score that combines reward volatility (exploitation) with a policy-entropy-gated exploration bonus. The single highest-scoring sample receives the full G-rollout budget for a GRPO-style group-relative advantage update, while the remaining K−1 samples contribute only one rollout each for statistics. The paper further establishes a theoretical lower bound showing that O(ln(ε/ε′)) policy updates suffice under a local Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition, explaining why as few as 16 training instances can achieve near-optimal reasoning performance.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, DoPR achieves an average Pass@1 of 49.6 across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AMC, MATH, Minerva-MATH, OlympiadBench, GSM8K), matching GRPO's 49.6 while requiring only G+(K−1) rollouts per step versus GRPO's K×G. On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, DoPR reaches 53.1 average, and on LLaMA3.1-8B it reaches 24.0. Under a fixed 10k rollout budget, DoPR attains 46.1 average Pass@1, approaching GRPO's 49.6 at 50k rollouts, demonstrating roughly a 5× rollout efficiency gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), DoPR achieves a near-tie on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B (49.6 vs. 49.6 avg Pass@1) and a small gap on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (53.1 vs. 54.1), while outperforming GRPO on LLaMA3.1-8B (24.0 vs. 21.3); the critical advantage is that DoPR uses only G+(K−1) rollouts per step versus K×G, a nearly 10× reduction. Against One-Shot RL (Wang et al., 2025b), DoPR consistently wins across all three model sizes by 5–6 points in average Pass@1 (e.g., 49.6 vs. 43.5 on Qwen 1.5B, 53.1 vs. 46.8 on Qwen 7B), attributed to One-Shot RL's reliance on single-response updates that cause gradient degradation and early saturation. Against UFO-RL (Zhao et al., 2025), DoPR is essentially on par or slightly ahead (49.6 vs. 49.4 on Qwen 1.5B, 53.1 vs. 52.7 on Qwen 7B, 24.0 vs. 23.6 on LLaMA 8B), while UFO requires extensive pre-selection over the full dataset to identify training instances, whereas DoPR operates dynamically without any pre-screening. Under constrained rollout budgets (Table 3), DoPR with 10k rollouts (46.1 avg) approaches GRPO's 50k-rollout performance (49.6 avg), whereas One-Shot RL plateaus at 40.7 and GRPO reaches only 35.2 at the same 10k budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its policy optimization backbone and on classical UCB exploration (Kaelbling, 1994; Garivier & Moulines, 2011) as the foundation for its sample selection mechanism. It departs from prior data-efficiency efforts (One-Shot RL, UFO-RL) that reduce training data through pre-selection over large corpora, by instead providing a theoretical lower bound on sample complexity under a Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition and a dynamic, per-batch selection strategy (DoPR) that requires no pre-screening. The EM-UCB acquisition score is a novel entropy-gated modification of standard UCB, and the sample-complexity analysis is, to the authors' claim, the first formal treatment of data requirements in RLVR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that as few as 16 training instances suffice for near-optimal reasoning performance under RLVR challenges the prevailing assumption that large-scale reward data is indispensable, with direct implications for resource-constrained labs and iterative post-training pipelines. The ~10× rollout reduction from DoPR is a practical efficiency win that could lower the barrier to RL-based reasoning training. However, all experiments are confined to mathematical reasoning with small models (1.5B–8B), leaving open whether the data-efficiency result and the PL-condition argument generalize to broader reasoning domains (science, code, multi-step planning) or to larger-scale models where the initial performance gap ε may be substantially different.
