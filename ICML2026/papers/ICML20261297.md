## Problem

Consumer utility maximization in online auctions faces a strong impossibility barrier: no truthful mechanism can achieve better than an O(log n) approximation to optimal welfare, because the payments needed to elicit information directly reduce the residual surplus. Prior learning-augmented mechanism design work relies on predictions of agent values or the optimal value (FB), but the paper shows these are fundamentally insufficient for utility maximization. The paper asks what qualitatively different prediction can overcome this barrier and achieve best-of-both-worlds guarantees in the online random-order setting.

## Input / Output

The mechanism receives n agents arriving in a uniformly random permutation, each reporting a private value (bid), plus a binary prediction signal at each arrival step indicating whether the arriving agent is the highest-valued one. The mechanism is deterministic and uses posted prices: it outputs an irrevocable allocation decision (which agent receives the single item) and a posted price, yielding consumer utility equal to the allocated agent's value minus the price. The prediction is strictly weaker than full value-profile predictions—it is a single binary 'yes/no' per agent, not a numeric estimate of any value.

## Method

The paper first derandomizes the offline RSOL mechanism into a deterministic three-phase online mechanism M_phase (sample, max, lottery phases) that achieves a 1/(625e) approximation to the optimal lottery benchmark OPT_L, using a coupling argument to show the random-order balancedness property holds at least as often as in the random-subset setting. It then proves via Bayesian mechanism design that even perfect knowledge of FB (the highest value) cannot yield constant consistency, establishing that 'how much' is the wrong prediction. The key insight is that a binary identity prediction ('who' has the top value) perfectly aligns with the utility objective by letting the mechanism allocate to the top agent for free, bypassing costly screening prices. Two learning-augmented mechanisms are designed: M_Psamp (prediction window concurrent with the sample phase) and M_Pmax (prediction window concurrent with the max phase), with M_Pmax achieving (1/2e)·ln(1+2eα)-consistency and (1−α)/(625e)-robustness, providing best-of-both-worlds guarantees that no prior prediction model could deliver for this objective.

## Results

The deterministic mechanism M_phase achieves a 1/(625e) ≈ 0.000236 approximation to OPT_L (the optimal lottery benchmark) in the random-order model (Theorem 2). When augmented with a binary 'who' prediction (identity of the top agent), M_Pmax(α) achieves (1/(2e))·ln(1+2eα)-consistency to FB and (1−α)/(625e)-robustness to OPT_L (Theorem 5), while M_Psamp(γ) achieves γ-consistency and (1−2eγ)/(625e)-robustness (Theorem 4). Crucially, Theorem 3 establishes that even perfect knowledge of the highest value (FB) cannot yield better than O(1/log n) approximation to FB, making the identity prediction the minimal sufficient information for constant consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is against the O(1/log n) impossibility barrier for utility maximization (Hartline & Roughgarden 2008; Qiao & Valiant 2023). The identity prediction ('who') breaks this barrier, achieving constant-factor consistency to FB, while the value prediction ('how much') provably cannot (Theorem 3). Among the paper's own mechanisms, M_Pmax dominates M_Psamp: at any fixed consistency level γ, M_Pmax achieves strictly better robustness for all Δ = (v₁−v₂)/OPT_L ∈ [0,1] (Appendix C.4). However, neither deterministic mechanism beats the linear consistency-robustness tradeoff of the trivial randomized convex combination between 'follow the prediction' and M_phase. The paper notes this as an open problem, conjecturing that even richer predictions (full value vectors) may not improve the tradeoff for deterministic mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the offline RSOL mechanism of Hartline & Roughgarden (2008) for utility maximization and the online pen-testing impossibility results of Qiao & Valiant (2023), adapting both to the random-order online setting via a novel derandomization that converts a randomized offline mechanism into a deterministic three-phase online mechanism. It departs from the learning-augmented mechanism design literature (which has focused on revenue and welfare objectives) by showing that the standard prediction types—value predictions or optimal-solution predictions—are fundamentally insufficient for utility maximization, and that a qualitatively different prediction (the identity of the top agent) is both necessary and sufficient. The Bayesian mechanism design technique used to prove the 'how much' impossibility (Theorem 3) is a new analytical tool that the authors suggest may generalize to other settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first dedicated study of prediction-augmented mechanism design for consumer utility maximization, resolving a gap left by prior impossibility results that showed no truthful mechanism can beat O(log n) approximation to welfare. The conceptual insight that identity predictions ('who') suffice while value predictions ('how much') do not reframes what information is actionable in settings where payments are costly or infeasible. Open questions include whether more informative predictions (full value profiles) can beat the linear consistency-robustness tradeoff, whether guarantees can be made error-tolerant for near-correct predictions, and whether the analysis can be tightened to remove the conditioning on agent 2 appearing in the sample phase.
