## Problem

Existing AI safety techniques—red teaming, dataset auditing, fine-tuning prevention—assume data accessibility and evaluation practices that are incompatible with the legal and ethical constraints surrounding CSAM, which is illegal to access, generate, or train on for most actors. This position paper argues that preventing AIG-CSAM requires fundamentally new approaches to AI safety, and identifies 15 open problems spanning model development, deployment, and maintenance that represent critical research and policy priorities where advances could have immediate impact on child protection.

## Input / Output

The concept fusion experiments use 128×128 CelebA images with text-based class conditioning (e.g., 'blonde hair,' 'eyeglasses') as input and generated images as output, deliberately excluding overlapping training examples to test compositional generation. The model card audit takes publicly available model/system cards for 14 image and video generators as input and produces a structured binary assessment of whether child-safety, explicit-content, and CSAM-specific disclosures are present. The broader framework addresses text-to-image and text-to-video generation systems where natural-language prompts produce photorealistic imagery.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a systematic taxonomy of 15 open problems in AIG-CSAM prevention, organized across the AI lifecycle (development, deployment, maintenance) and four constraint dimensions (data restrictions, evaluation restrictions, adversarial environment, wellness implications). As a proof-of-concept for concept fusion, the authors train 295M-parameter conditional flow matching models (UNet, 700 epochs) on 128×128 CelebA images, training exclusively on non-overlapping attribute classes (A\B ∪ B\A) and then measuring propensity to generate compositional A∩B images. They vary the sample count for one concept (64–4K) and test three generation strategies—unconditional, average class vector, and sequential conditioning (n steps on class A then T−n steps on class B)—finding a sharp threshold where composition emerges (e.g., ~0.1% at 750 samples jumping to 1.5% at 1000; >25% capability with just 250 samples under sequential conditioning). A second proof-of-concept manually audits public model cards for 14 widely used generators, finding that only OpenAI's Sora models include dedicated child-safety and CSAM sections. The paper closes with targeted recommendations for researchers, AI providers, and policymakers mapped to each open problem.

## Results

In the concept fusion proof-of-concept experiment on CelebA (128×128, 295M-param UNet, 700 epochs), models trained on separate attribute classes (e.g., blonde hair without eyeglasses, non-blondes with eyeglasses) can generate compositional images (blonde + eyeglasses) with surprisingly few samples: with 750 eyeglasses images, composition likelihood is ~0.1%, rising 15× to 1.5% at 1,000 samples under unconditional/average conditioning. Under sequential conditioning (denoise n steps on class A, then T−n steps on class B), capability jumps to >25% compositional generation with only 250 eyeglasses samples (6.2% of training data), and even 64 residual images enable composition ~10% of the time. In a separate audit of 14 widely used image/video generators, only 2 of 14 (Sora, Sora2) document CSAM-specific risks in their public model/system cards.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a position paper and does not propose a new method to benchmark against baselines; the `compares_to` list is empty. The empirical contributions are (1) a proof-of-concept concept-fusion experiment demonstrating that partial data cleaning is insufficient—models trained on disjoint attribute subsets still compose the combined concept at non-trivial rates, aligning with concurrent work by Cretu et al. (2025) who found text-to-image models can generate children wearing eyeglasses even after 94% of child images are removed, and (2) a manual audit of 14 public model/system cards showing that the vast majority of major generators (12 of 14) do not document CSAM-specific evaluations or safeguards. The paper positions these findings as evidence motivating 15 open problems rather than as a method comparison, and explicitly defers head-to-head benchmarking of mitigation strategies to future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper carves new ground by arguing that standard AI safety techniques (red teaming, data filtering, watermarking, machine unlearning, model fingerprinting) are structurally incompatible with CSAM prevention due to legal and ethical constraints: researchers cannot access CSAM for training or evaluation, cannot generate CSAM for testing, and must provide strict guarantees in an adversarial environment. It draws on diffusion model safety work (Okawa et al. 2023 on concept composition, Gandikota et al. 2023 on concept erasure), open-source model risk management (Casper et al. 2025), and content provenance standards (C2PA, watermarking), but reframes them through the lens of inaccessibility constraints. The small-scale concept fusion experiment on CelebA extends Okawa et al. (2023) and concurrent Cretu et al. (2025) by quantifying the sample threshold at which compositional generation emerges, providing a concrete proxy for the CSAM-specific question of whether partial data cleaning suffices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies 15 open problems across the AI lifecycle that are critical given the explosive growth in AIG-CSAM reports (440K+ in H1 2025 vs. 7K in 2023–2024), and argues that without new techniques, existing safety frameworks will remain ineffective for child protection. Key open questions include: how to evaluate models for CSAM capabilities without illegally generating CSAM, how to prevent concept fusion between benign concepts (children + NSFW) without accessing the harmful combination, and how to build fine-tuning resilience without training on the obstructed task. Limitations include a US-centric legal perspective, focus on imagery over video/text modalities, and the small-scale (128×128, 295M-param) nature of the concept fusion experiment, which may not generalize to production-scale diffusion models.
