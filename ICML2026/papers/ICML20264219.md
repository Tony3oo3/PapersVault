## Problem

Existing learning-based trajectory prediction methods operate almost exclusively in the time domain, where low-frequency global trends and high-frequency local details are entangled in raw temporal signals, making it difficult to explicitly model long-term dependencies and transient motion patterns. While a few recent works have explored frequency-domain learning for autonomous driving, they are restricted to a single frequency band or a specific module, leaving a systematic end-to-end frequency-domain learning paradigm unexplored for trajectory prediction.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three input feature types per scene: agent historical trajectories as (N_a × 2 × H) position sequences in a local coordinate frame centered on each target agent, a vectorized map as (N_m × B × d_m) point-feature tensors, and a pairwise relative spatial pose matrix of shape ((N_a+N_m) × (N_a+N_m) × 5) encoding displacement, heading difference, and bearing angle. The output is K=6 multimodal future trajectories of shape (N_a × 2 × F × K) plus a per-agent mode probability vector (N_a × K), where each final trajectory is explicitly decomposed as the sum of a global-trend component and a local-detail component.

## Method

TF-FACE introduces a two-stage decoding pipeline built on a frequency-domain adaptive fusion encoder (FAE) that stacks a 3-level Feature Pyramid Network with a Gated Frequency-Domain Attention (GfreAttn) mechanism, which applies DFT to projected Q/K/V, performs complex-valued self-attention with learnable phase rotation, and uses a channel-wise gating factor γ to selectively amplify high-frequency components attenuated by convolutional smoothing. The first decoding stage (PreD) produces a coarse single-modal trajectory whose low-frequency components are extracted via a complex-valued freMLP in the frequency domain to yield reliable future global-trend features, while a high-pass filter on historical trajectories isolates high-frequency local features. The second stage (DuaD) runs two parallel branches: a low-frequency branch that decodes K global-trend trajectories with mode probabilities, and a high-frequency branch that refines local details conditioned on the trend endpoint, with the final prediction being their element-wise sum. Training uses a band-specific time-frequency dual-consistency loss where frequency-domain terms apply monotonically decreasing weights to low-frequency bins (for PreD and the trend branch) and monotonically increasing weights to high-frequency bins (for the detail branch), enforcing decoupled supervision across temporal scales.

## Results

On Argoverse 1 (single-model), TF-FACE achieves Brier_minFDE₆ = 1.73, minADE₆ = 0.76, and MR₆ = 0.107, tying HPNet on the official ranking metric and matching its minADE₆, while achieving the best DAC (0.991) among all compared methods. On Argoverse 2 (30-frame prediction), TF-FACE attains Brier_minFDE₆ = 1.092 and minFDE₆ = 0.485, outperforming FIM (1.131 / 0.530) and QCNet (1.180 / 0.551). Under ensemble evaluation on Argoverse 1, TF-FACE reaches Brier_minFDE₆ = 1.676 and MR₆ = 0.105, the best among all ensemble baselines, though QCNet retains a slight edge on minFDE₆ (1.067 vs 1.098).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TF-FACE ties HPNet (CVPR 2024) on the official Brier_minFDE₆ (1.73) and minADE₆ (0.76), and matches its MR₆ (0.107), while achieving a better DAC (0.991 vs 0.988); HPNet retains a marginal edge on minFDE₆ (1.10 vs 1.11). Against GoIRL (ICML 2025) and FIM (ICCV 2025), TF-FACE improves Brier_minFDE₆ by 0.06 and 0.10 respectively, and reduces minFDE₆ by 0.06 and 0.09, indicating substantially better endpoint accuracy. On Argoverse 2 (30-frame horizon), TF-FACE outperforms FIM, QCNet, and DeMo on both Brier_minFDE₆ and minFDE₆, with the largest gap over QCNet (1.092 vs 1.180). In the ensemble setting on Argoverse 1, TF-FACE achieves the best Brier_minFDE₆ (1.676) and MR₆ (0.105), surpassing QCNet (1.694 / 0.106) and FIM (1.693 / 0.109), though QCNet still leads on minFDE₆ (1.067 vs 1.098) and minADE₆ (0.73 vs 0.74). Under the longer 60-frame Argoverse 2 horizon, TF-FACE (using only 20 history frames) matches QCNet (50 history frames) on minFDE₆ (1.31 vs 1.29) and slightly edges it on minADE₆ (0.725 vs 0.726) and MR₆ (0.164 vs 0.167), suggesting the frequency-domain design compensates for the shorter observation window.

## Relation to Prior Work

TF-FACE departs from the dominant time-domain paradigm in trajectory prediction (Transformers, GNNs, diffusion models) by systematically integrating frequency-domain learning across the entire pipeline—encoding, feature extraction, and decoding—rather than treating it as an auxiliary branch. It draws conceptual inspiration from frequency-domain time-series forecasting (FEDformer, FiLM) and from the few prior autonomous-driving efforts that touched on frequency information (View Vertically, PatchTraj, DiffWT), but those prior works target a single band or a specific module. The key architectural departure is the Gated Frequency-Domain Attention (GfreAttn) within an FPN, which adaptively reweights bands during encoding, combined with a two-stage decoder that explicitly decouples low-frequency global trends from high-frequency local details and recombines them via a band-specific dual-consistency loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first trajectory prediction model to apply frequency-domain learning in a systematic, end-to-end manner for autonomous driving, and the robustness gap under severe noise (6–11% degradation vs. 23–55% for a time-domain baseline) suggests that explicit band decoupling is a practical defense against perception/localization errors in deployed systems. The main open limitation is that the low/high frequency split ratio is set empirically per dataset rather than learned, which limits transferability to new driving distributions. Extending the frequency-domain adaptive enhancement paradigm to multimodal sensor inputs (camera, LiDAR) and to end-to-end planning is a natural next step the authors outline.
