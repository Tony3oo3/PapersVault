## Problem

Existing semi-supervised statistical inference (SSI) methods are designed for structured covariates and assume strict alignment between labeled and unlabeled datasets, making them ill-suited for real-world biomedical data that is unstructured (speech, clinical notes) and collected under heterogeneous protocols causing covariate misalignment. The paper asks how to integrate pre-trained LLMs into SSI to handle both challenges while preserving statistical validity and achieving provable efficiency gains over labeled-only estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes structured covariates (demographics, NLP-derived linguistic features like type-token ratio and filler-word ratio), unstructured covariates (full speech transcripts, clinical notes), and a small set of expert-annotated outcome labels; in the misaligned setting it also receives additional covariates (e.g., Hamilton cognitive test scores) available only in the labeled subset. The LLM is queried zero-shot at temperature 0 to produce a bounded numeric prediction per subject. The output is a moment-equation estimator β̂ with a sandwich variance estimator yielding (1−α) confidence intervals, plus the identified prediction invariance region and per-covariate calibration weights that quantify how much each LLM prediction contributes to efficiency.

## Method

LASS integrates LLM predictions into SSI through a covariate-dependent calibration weight ω*(x) = Cov(Y*, Y | X=x) / Var(Y* | X=x), estimated via classical ML (e.g., random forests), which replaces the single global power parameter used in PPI++ with a locally adaptive weight that captures where the LLM's unstructured-data signal is most informative. Pseudo-labels are then constructed by combining observed labels with calibrated LLM predictions, and β is estimated by solving a generalized moment equation with sandwich variance. For covariate misalignment, LASS introduces a Prediction Invariance Region (PIR) identified via a two-sample CART partitioning algorithm with honest t-tests, restricting the use of extra-covariate LLM predictions to regions where their average prediction is statistically indistinguishable from the base prediction. Theoretical results establish consistency, asymptotic normality, and strict efficiency dominance over GLM, conventional SSI, and PPI++ under mild regularity conditions, with the variance decomposition explicitly separating gains from machine-learning imputation and from LLM-calibrated prediction.

## Results

In the matched-covariate simulation (N+n=1000, n=100), LASS achieves the smallest standard deviations across all six covariates, reducing estimation uncertainty by 28–32% relative to labeled-data-only logistic regression (e.g., lexical diversity: 0.52 vs. 0.74; verb ratio: 0.38 vs. 0.56). On the IMDb case study (25K labeled + 3.4M unlabeled reviews), LASS produces the narrowest 95% confidence intervals for all three structured covariates (e.g., negation counts: [−0.34, −0.03] vs. [−0.41, 0.03] for labeled-only). In the unmatched-covariate setting, identifying the Prediction Invariance Region (PIR) via CART further tightens intervals and enables LASS to detect lexical complexity and pronoun ratio as significant AD biomarkers—effects missed by all three baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LASS is compared against three baselines: (1) labeled-data-only logistic regression, (2) classical semi-supervised inference (SSI; Chakrabortty & Cai, 2018) that imputes labels via nonparametric ML without unstructured covariates, and (3) Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI; Angelopoulos et al., 2023a) and its LLM-augmented variant PPI+LLM (Angelopoulos et al., 2023b). LASS dominates all baselines on 5 of 6 covariates in the matched-covariate simulation, with the largest gains on lexical diversity (0.52 vs. 0.74 labeled-only, 0.62 PPI) and sex (0.65 vs. 0.80 labeled-only). The only near-tie is verb ratio, where PPI+LLM (0.37) edges out LASS (0.38) by 0.01, likely within simulation noise. On the IMDb dataset, LASS yields strictly narrower confidence intervals than SSI and PPI for all three covariates (e.g., token ratio width 0.30 vs. 0.35 for SSI and 0.31 for PPI). Critically, LASS is the only method that can directly ingest unstructured speech transcripts via LLM zero-shot prediction, whereas SSI and PPI are limited to structured covariates, giving LASS a structural advantage in biomedical settings with heterogeneous data modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-supervised statistical inference (SSI) lineage initiated by Chakrabortty & Cai (2018) and the Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) framework of Angelopoulos et al. (2023a) by replacing PPI's single global power parameter with a covariate-dependent calibration weight ω*(x) = Cov(Y*, Y|X=x)/Var(Y*|X=x), which is shown to be variance-optimal. It further departs from both SSI and PPI by addressing two practical limitations they share: reliance on structured covariates and strict covariate alignment between labeled and unlabeled datasets. The Prediction Invariance Region (PIR) mechanism, identified via honest sample-splitting with CART, is a novel device for selectively borrowing information from mismatched covariates while preserving estimator consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a statistically principled bridge between the predictive power of pre-trained LLMs and the validity requirements of biomedical inference, with provable efficiency gains over both labeled-only GLM and PPI++ under mild conditions. The PIR concept offers a general recipe for handling heterogeneous data collection protocols—a pervasive problem in multi-site biomedical research—that prior SSI methods could not address. Key limitations include the current evaluation being confined to speech/text modalities and relatively small labeled cohorts (n=100–452), leaving open whether the efficiency gains and PIR identification strategy transfer to imaging, longitudinal, or multi-center settings. The authors explicitly flag broader biomedical and multimodal evaluation as important future work.
