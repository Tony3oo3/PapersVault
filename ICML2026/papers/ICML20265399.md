## Problem

Existing Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) for LLM interpretability operate at the token level, creating a granularity mismatch when the goal is to capture step-level reasoning properties such as reasoning direction, semantic transitions, and logical correctness. At each reasoning step, token-level SAE features inevitably entangle the incremental information (new decisions or preferences) with background information already present in prior context, making it impossible to cleanly interpret or manipulate individual reasoning steps.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a reasoning trajectory partitioned into atomic steps at natural linguistic boundaries (punctuation or line breaks). For each step s_k, the encoder input is the concatenation of the full preceding context C_k, a separator token, and the current step, while the decoder reconstructs the step autoregressively by conditioning on both the context embeddings and the sparse latent vector. The sparse features (with target sparsity τ_spar=10) can be probed with a three-layer MLP to yield step-level predictions: correctness, logicality, token count, and first-token distribution.

## Method

SSAE introduces a context-conditioned sparse autoencoder where both the encoder and decoder have access to the full preceding context, ensuring the sparse latent vector h_k encodes only the incremental information added by the current step rather than re-encoding background. The encoder (a Transformer) maps [C_k; SEP; s_k] to a dense embedding, which is expanded via a ReLU sparse projector into a high-dimensional vector; the decoder partitions this vector into c segments appended to the context embedding sequence and autoregressively reconstructs the step. Training minimizes cross-entropy reconstruction loss plus an L1 sparsity penalty, with a dynamic weight controller that multiplicatively adjusts λ each window to drive empirical sparsity toward a predefined target τ_spar, forming an information bottleneck that forces background information out of the latent. Gaussian noise (σ=0.01) is added to each latent dimension for robustness, and the maximum information bandwidth is bounded by the sparsity and noise level, guaranteeing that only step-specific incremental content survives in the representation.

## Results

On step-level probing (Table 1), SSAE features (ĥ_k) dramatically outperform token-level SAEs: SSAE-Llama achieves 86.24% correctness accuracy on MATH-500 vs. 86.88% for Token-SAE-agg, but far surpasses on step-length prediction (1.59 RMSE vs. 30.33) and first-token perplexity (1.62 vs. 18.63). On GSM8K, SSAE-Qwen reaches 78.58% correctness and 76.56% logicality accuracy, exceeding the naive majority-class baseline (70.49%/55.06%) by 8 and 21 points respectively. For inference-time scaling (Table 5), Probe-Guided voting (PG@16) consistently beats Self-Consistency (SC@16): e.g., Qwen2.5-0.5B improves from 29.67% to 33.00% on SVAMP, and Llama-3.2-1B from 48.00% to 50.33% on SVAMP. On larger models (Table 6), PG@16 lifts DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B from 86.67% to 90.00% on AIME 2024.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Token-SAE (Huben et al., 2024), SSAE achieves a decisive win on all step-level probing tasks: step-length RMSE drops from ~30 (Token-SAE-agg) to ~2 (SSAE), and first-token perplexity drops from 18.63 to 1.46–1.62, while correctness accuracy improves by 4–10 points over the naive baseline. Token-SAE features are essentially at chance level for step-level attributes, confirming the granularity mismatch the paper identifies. Against Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023), the Probe-Guided (PG@16) strategy yields consistent but modest gains: +0.6 to +3.3 points on GSM8K/SVAMP/MultiArith for small models (Qwen2.5-0.5B, Llama-3.2-1B), and a notable +3.33 points on AIME 2024 for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B (90.00% vs. 86.67%). However, PG@16 ties or slightly underperforms SC@16 on the hardest tasks (AIME 2025) and when the base model is near saturation (DeepSeek at 95.60% on MATH-500), suggesting the probe's discriminative power diminishes when most sampled paths are already correct.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse autoencoder (SAE) line of work for LLM interpretability (Bricken et al., 2023; Huben et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2025) by shifting the granularity from token-level to step-level, addressing what the authors identify as a fundamental mismatch when analyzing chain-of-thought reasoning. The key architectural departure is making both encoder and decoder context-conditioned, so that the sparse latent vector encodes only incremental information relative to prior steps rather than the full semantic content of each token. The dynamic sparsity controller is adapted from Miao et al. (2023), and the downstream application builds on Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023) by replacing unweighted majority voting with probe-guided quality-weighted voting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that step correctness and logicality are linearly decodable from intermediate sparse representations provides concrete evidence that LLMs partially 'know' the validity of their own reasoning steps during generation, offering a mechanistic foundation for self-verification that prior work had only hypothesized. However, the practical gains from probe-guided voting are modest (1–3 points over Self-Consistency on small models, and clear plateaus on frontier models like DeepSeek-R1-Distill-32B on AIME 2025), and the cross-model transferability of features learned from 0.5B/1B backbones to 7B/32B models remains limited. Open questions include whether step-level features generalize beyond mathematical reasoning to open-ended or multi-domain tasks, and whether the information bottleneck formulation (Eq. 8) can be made more principled rather than heuristic.
