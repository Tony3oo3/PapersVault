## Problem

Existing 2D speech-to-video diffusion models rely heavily on repeated reference-frame conditioning, producing overly static 'copy-paste' animations with limited motion diversity. Meanwhile, 3D-aware talking portrait methods depend on inverting pretrained tri-plane or NeRF generators from a single image, introducing reconstruction errors and identity drift. No prior framework unifies speech-driven animation and continuous viewpoint control in a single model without explicit 3D representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263575/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an ArcFace 512-d identity embedding, an optional 512×512 reference portrait, per-frame wav2vec2 audio features (9216-d), and per-frame camera matrices (flattened 4×4 extrinsics + 3×3 intrinsics → 25-d vector). It outputs 512×512 talking portrait video at 25 FPS, generated autoregressively in segments of N=16 frames with n=2 context frames carried forward. Camera control is restricted to the frontal hemisphere (≈140° azimuth, 70° elevation), and the model can operate in ID-only mode (no reference image) for recontextualized generation.

## Method

STARCaster extends the Arc2Face ID-conditioned 2D UNet (0.9B params) with 0.5B temporal transformer blocks inserted after each spatial attention layer, enabling cross-frame denoising while keeping the original UNet frozen to preserve identity fidelity. A decoupled multi-source cross-attention mechanism replaces the single identity stream with parallel attention paths for identity, audio, and camera that share a common query but use modality-specific trainable K/V projections, with scaling factors λ_a and λ_c allowing independent activation per training stage. A frozen reference UNet (MasaCtrl-style) injects appearance features via spatial concatenation into self-attention, augmented with LoRA (rank 64) on the denoising UNet's self-attention projections. Training proceeds in three progressive stages: (1) audio-driven motion learning with lip-reading supervision (a pretrained LRS3 lip-reading network applied to denoised mouth crops via a supervision-by-denoising formulation), (2) autoregressive self-forcing (F=2 recursive segments where the model conditions on its own generated context rather than ground-truth frames to reduce exposure bias), and (3) temporal-to-spatial adaptation on ~3M synthetic multi-view frames rendered from 20K SphereHead 3D models along smooth camera trajectories. The lip-reading loss and self-forcing are the key differentiators over prior autoregressive talking-head methods, yielding higher pose diversity and tighter lip synchronization without increasing model size.

## Results

On TH-1KH, STARCaster achieves the best FID (24.89), FVD (185.24), LSE-C (5.493), LSE-D (8.724), and Pose Std (4.896×10⁻²) among all compared methods, surpassing the next-best Hallo3 (FID 27.18, FVD 194.76) by a clear margin. On Hallo3, STARCaster similarly leads with FID 16.86, FVD 145.35, LSE-C 6.292, LSE-D 8.540, and Pose Std 4.760×10⁻². For 3D-aware novel-view synthesis on NeRSemble, STARCaster achieves the best FID (29.52), FVD (222.89), LPIPS (0.477), SSIM (0.577), and PSNR (14.219), outperforming Real3DPortrait (FID 32.33, PSNR 10.289) and Portrait4D-v2 (FID 48.91, PSNR 12.469). Inference time is 2.2 min for a 5-second clip, roughly 10× faster than Hallo3's 21.4 min.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STARCaster outperforms all seven audio-driven baselines on every metric in Table 1. The closest competitor is Hallo3 (a 5B-parameter DiT), which STARCaster beats by 2.29 FID, 9.52 FVD, and 0.352 LSE-C on TH-1KH, while also achieving roughly 10× faster inference (2.2 min vs. 21.4 min). Among GAN-era and intermediate methods, AniTalker and V-Express show the weakest motion diversity (Pose Std 2.339 and 0.692 respectively), while FLOAT matches STARCaster's pose diversity (4.859 vs. 4.896) but lags significantly in image quality (FID 47.20 vs. 24.89). For 3D-aware novel-view synthesis (Table 3), STARCaster surpasses Real3DPortrait by 2.81 FID and 3.93 PSNR, and Portrait4D-v2 by 19.39 FID and 1.75 PSNR, while also achieving the best lip-sync scores (LSE-C 3.957 vs. 3.401 and 3.384). Notably, STARCaster is conditioned only on audio (not driving video), yet still outperforms expression-driven 3D methods on view accuracy, validating the implicit 3D-awareness learned from video priors plus synthetic multi-view fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

STARCaster extends the Arc2Face ID-conditioned image diffusion backbone into a spatio-temporal video model by inserting temporal transformer blocks (following the AnimateDiff/Align-Your-Latents inflation strategy) and a decoupled multi-source cross-attention mechanism inspired by IP-Adapter's parallel attention streams. It departs from prior talking-portrait methods in two key ways: (1) replacing strict reference-frame conditioning with softer ArcFace identity embeddings to increase motion diversity, and (2) reformulating novel-view synthesis as autoregressive video generation conditioned on camera parameters, rather than relying on explicit 3D representations (NeRF, tri-planes, Gaussian Splatting) that require per-subject optimization or imperfect inversion. The self-forcing training strategy and lip-reading perceptual loss are borrowed from autoregressive video generation and visual speech recognition respectively, but their combination within a progressive three-stage curriculum (ID motion → audio-visual sync → view synthesis) is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a lightweight UNet-based video diffusion model (~1.6B params) can unify speech-driven portrait animation and continuous viewpoint control without any explicit 3D geometry, outperforming both 2D talking-head baselines and 3D-aware methods while being roughly 10× faster than 5B-parameter DiT competitors. This challenges the prevailing assumption that view-consistent generation requires learned 3D representations, suggesting that large-scale video priors plus limited synthetic multi-view supervision may suffice for the frontal-hemisphere range typical of talking-head applications. Key limitations include restricted camera range (no 360° or rear views), slow inference relative to GAN-based real-time systems, and synthetic-data artifacts (skin smoothing, color shifts during rapid transitions). Open questions include whether this implicit-3D approach generalizes to full-body animation, whether the progressive training recipe transfers to other conditioning modalities, and whether the efficiency gap over DiT-based methods narrows as those architectures improve.
