## Problem

Long-context LLM decoding is memory-bandwidth-bound, requiring full KV cache reloads per token, yet existing sparse attention methods face a dual dilemma: metadata-based importance metrics (min/max bounds, centroids) incur extra memory overhead or information distortion, and selection strategies are either rigid (top-k) or require costly global synchronization (top-p). Additionally, the standard decoupled Selector-Computer paradigm prevents the computation stage from reusing the selector's intermediate results, wasting bandwidth.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 16-bit query vector, a key cache pre-decomposed into a 2-bit symmetric mid-rise quantized thumbnail (codebook {-1.5, -0.5, +0.5, +1.5} per channel) and an 8-bit residual, plus the value cache and a scalar sparsity threshold δ (typically 5 or 7). It produces the standard attention output for the current decoded token. The entire pipeline is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no model weight modification, and the 2-bit/8-bit split is computed once at cache-write time, so the decode path only reads the pre-quantized thumbnails for scanning and the residuals for refinement of selected blocks.

## Method

FFD replaces metadata-based indexing with content-aware scanning: the 2-bit quantized K-cache serves as a high-throughput proxy for importance estimation, with a sub-Gaussian concentration bound guaranteeing that the probability of a false negative decays exponentially in the safety margin. The top-δ selection criterion retains blocks whose approximate score exceeds a pseudo-max (estimated from attention-sink and local-context tokens only) minus δ, avoiding the global reduction required by top-p while adapting to distribution sharpness unlike fixed top-k. The selector and computer are fused into a single cooperative Triton kernel pipeline—pseudo-max estimation, streaming 2-bit scan with threshold filtering, then 8-bit residual refinement—so the computation stage directly reuses the quantized dot products from scanning without materializing full-precision keys. A full-chain CUDA Graph with block-wise JIT capture eliminates CPU launch overhead for sub-100 µs kernels, and the design scales to 256K context with up to 11.6× kernel-level speedup and 2.37× end-to-end throughput improvement.

## Results

FFD achieves up to 11.6× kernel-level speedup over FlashAttention-2 at 256K context (0.17 ms vs 1.12 ms per layer) and up to 2.37× end-to-end throughput improvement on RTX 4090 (51.8 vs 21.9 tokens/s at 16K). On RULER (32K context), FFD(δ=7) attains an average score of 89.4, nearly matching the dense baseline (90.6) while Quest (73.9) and Twilight (69.8) degrade substantially. On LongBench, FFD(δ=7) achieves 26.35 average, slightly exceeding the dense baseline (26.22) and all sparse baselines, confirming that high-ratio sparsity (73–82% average) is achievable without accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlashAttention-2 (dense baseline), FFD wins decisively on speed—achieving 6.58× kernel speedup at 256K and 2.37× end-to-end throughput at 16K on RTX 4090—while maintaining near-parity accuracy (RULER 89.4 vs 90.6). Against Quest, FFD dominates on both axes: RULER average 89.4 vs 73.9, with the gap most dramatic on multi-key retrieval (MK-3: 95.0 vs 6.0), and FFD is faster at matched keep ratios due to eliminating global synchronization. Against KIVI, FFD outperforms on accuracy (89.4 vs 82.3 on RULER) and speed, as KIVI's dequantization overhead causes it to underperform even dense attention in the single-batch decode regime (22.4 vs 44.5 tokens/s on H100). Against Twilight (top-p selection), FFD achieves substantially higher RULER scores (89.4 vs 69.8) and the ablation shows top-δ is 25× faster per-head than top-p at matched sparsity, confirming the elimination of the global softmax bottleneck. StreamingLLM is catastrophically outperformed (RULER 89.4 vs 6.1), as its fixed-window approach cannot handle retrieval tasks requiring access to arbitrary positions in the context.

## Relation to Prior Work

FFD sits at the intersection of three research threads: IO-aware kernel fusion (FlashAttention, FlashDecoding), training-free sparse attention (Quest, SparQ, H2O), and KV cache quantization (KIVI, KVQuant). It departs from the dominant decoupled Selector-Computer paradigm by fusing both stages into a single kernel, and from metadata-based importance estimation (bounding boxes, centroids) by repurposing 2-bit quantized keys as a content-aware scanning proxy. The top-δ selection criterion is positioned as a middle ground between the fixed-budget top-k (Quest, SparQ) and the globally-synchronized top-p (Twilight), leveraging the attention-sink phenomenon to avoid the reduction bottleneck that plagues cumulative-probability methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FFD offers a practical, training-free path to 256K-context decoding with up to 2.37× end-to-end throughput gains, which is directly relevant to the growing deployment of long-context LLMs where memory bandwidth—not FLOPs—is the bottleneck. The ablation revealing that 2-bit scanning is counterproductive under top-k but synergistic under top-δ highlights that algorithmic and system co-design cannot be optimized independently, a lesson that generalizes beyond this specific kernel. Open questions include applicability to MLA-style architectures (e.g., DeepSeek), systematic validation of head-wise sparsity across diverse task distributions, and whether the pseudo-max approximation holds under adversarially constructed attention patterns.
