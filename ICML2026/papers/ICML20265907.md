## Problem

Standard generative models for low-count PET reconstruction (diffusion, SDE solvers) rely on dissipative dynamics with strictly negative divergence, causing indiscriminate phase-space contraction that suppresses weak but diagnostically critical lesion signals alongside noise — a phenomenon the authors term 'signal wash-out.' Deterministic regression methods avoid this but suffer from regression-to-the-mean, obliterating high-frequency textures. The paper addresses the fundamental geometric limitation that no existing framework simultaneously preserves fragile lesion signals and enforces rigorous data consistency in the extreme low-count regime.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-count PET sinogram y (Poisson statistics) and the system matrix A as input, along with a full-count OSEM reconstruction x_full as the training target. It operates in an augmented symplectic phase space Z = X × P ∈ R^{2d}, where the image x is paired with an auxiliary momentum p that encodes physics-informed boundary conditions (range-space data score at t=0, null-space orthogonal noise at t=1). The output is a sample from the posterior p(x|y), produced by integrating the learned Hamiltonian dynamics backward from the FBP prior A†y over 100 symplectic leapfrog steps on 128×128 patches.

## Method

FlowPET reformulates posterior sampling as volume-preserving transport in a symplectic phase space by parameterizing the vector field through a Separable Hamiltonian H = U_ψ(x) + K_φ(p), which guarantees a divergence-free flow by architectural construction (Proposition 4.1, Liouville's Theorem). The key physics-informed design is a Range-Null space decomposition of the momentum boundaries: p₀ = γAᵀ(y − Ax₀) embeds the data-consistency restoring force in the range space, while p₁ = γ(I − A†A)ξ injects stochastic uncertainty strictly into the null space, decoupling fidelity from diversity. Training uses Symplectic Flow Matching, a regression objective that decouples into kinetic matching (transport path) and potential matching (posterior geometry), optimized with two asymmetric U-Net networks (Kinetic: 128 channels, depth 1/2/4/8; Potential: 64 channels, depth 1/2/4) conditioned on sinogram features via AdaLN. Inference employs a Symplectic Leapfrog (Störmer-Verlet) integrator whose unit-Jacobian determinant preserves phase-space volume exactly at every discrete step, preventing the numerical dissipation that would reintroduce signal wash-out.

## Results

FlowPET achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three low-count PET benchmarks. On the most challenging In-House dataset (1% count), it attains SSIM 0.9811, PSNR 36.35 dB, and RMSE 0.0160, surpassing the strongest stochastic baseline DREAM by 0.58 dB in PSNR. On UDPET Brain (1% count), FlowPET leads with SSIM 0.9139, PSNR 28.88 dB, and RMSE 0.0387. On the milder BrainWeb benchmark (20% count), it achieves the best PSNR (36.34 dB) and RMSE (0.0188) but trails FourierPET slightly on SSIM (0.9838 vs. 0.9859).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowPET dominates on the two most challenging 1%-count datasets (In-House and UDPET Brain), achieving the best SSIM, PSNR, and RMSE simultaneously. On In-House, it outperforms the strongest stochastic baseline DREAM by 0.58 dB PSNR (36.35 vs. 35.77) and 0.015 SSIM, while also beating the best deterministic method FourierPET by 1.16 dB PSNR. On UDPET Brain, the margin over DREAM is 0.39 dB PSNR and 0.006 SSIM. On the milder BrainWeb (20% count) benchmark, FlowPET achieves the best PSNR (36.34 dB) and RMSE (0.0188) but trails FourierPET by 0.002 in SSIM (0.9838 vs. 0.9859), indicating the symplectic advantage is most pronounced in extreme low-count regimes. Against classical OSEM, FlowPET improves PSNR by 7.99 dB (BrainWeb) to 9.01 dB (UDPET), confirming the substantial gain of learned generative priors over iterative reconstruction. The wash-out analysis (Fig. 5) further shows that dissipative baselines like IR-SDE plateau well below ground-truth SUV ratios, while FlowPET recovers monotonically toward 1.0, directly validating the volume-preservation hypothesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowPET extends the Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023) by lifting the transport from Euclidean image space to a symplectic phase space, drawing on the Hamiltonian Generative Networks (Toth et al., 2020) and Symplectic Recurrent Neural Networks (Chen et al., 2020) lineage for volume-preserving dynamics. The key departure is that prior symplectic/Hamiltonian generative work addressed unconditional generation or physical simulation, whereas this paper embeds a Separable Hamiltonian structure within a *conditional* flow matching objective for an ill-posed inverse problem, introducing a Range-Null space decomposition to encode data consistency and stochastic uncertainty into conjugate momentum boundaries. It also explicitly positions against the dominant SDE/diffusion-based PET reconstruction paradigm (IR-SDE, RED, DREAM), arguing that their dissipative contraction mechanism is geometrically incompatible with preserving weak lesion signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a theoretically clean geometric argument—divergence-free transport by architectural construction—that directly addresses a clinically relevant failure mode (loss of low-contrast lesions under aggressive denoising). However, the practical impact is tempered by the iterative Leapfrog integrator's computational cost relative to single-step baselines, the absence of an intrinsic noise-suppression mechanism (relying entirely on the learned potential), and the lack of out-of-distribution or multi-scanner validation. Open questions include whether the symplectic constraint generalizes to dynamic PET, whether the modest empirical gains over strong flow-matching baselines (e.g., FourierPET) persist at scale, and whether observer studies can confirm the claimed diagnostic advantage for lesion detectability.
