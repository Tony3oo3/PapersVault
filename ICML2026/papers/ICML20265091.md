## Problem

Existing multi-task learning methods address negative transfer by reweighting or projecting task gradients, but they treat the shared representation as a black box, leaving invariant semantic factors entangled with spurious style factors. The paper proves that under a linear-Gaussian structural causal model, any gradient-balancing algorithm faces an irreducible OOD error floor proportional to sin²ψ (the entanglement angle), meaning no amount of gradient surgery can eliminate residual-driven generalization failure when the representation geometry remains mixed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (288×384 on NYUv2, 128×256 on Cityscapes/GTA5, 128×128 on CelebA) and simultaneously produces multiple dense or classification outputs: per-pixel segmentation masks, depth maps, surface normal maps, or 40 binary attribute labels. A key structural constraint is that all task heads consume only the semantic stream Z_s (a spatial feature map of 256–512 channels), while the residual stream Z_r (a spatially invariant vector via global average pooling) is used exclusively by a training-time decoder for reconstruction and counterfactual synthesis, imposing no inference overhead.

## Method

CORE-MTL factorizes the shared encoder output into a semantic stream Z_s and a residual stream Z_r via two 1×1 convolutional projectors, then restricts all K task heads to predict solely from Z_s, structurally blocking nuisance shortcuts. Training enforces this factorization through three complementary objectives: (1) a linear CKA independence loss that minimizes statistical dependence between the two streams, (2) a Counterfactual Feature Augmentation (CFA) loss that shuffles residual codes within a batch, synthesizes counterfactual images via a decoder, re-encodes them, and penalizes prediction inconsistency, and (3) a reconstruction grounding loss that anchors factor roles—either through a physics-based inverse rendering decoder (hard grounding, where Z_s predicts normals and Z_r predicts albedo/lighting composed via a shading model) or a generic convolutional decoder (soft grounding). Theoretically, the paper shows that this disentanglement yields a tighter OOD generalization bound scaling linearly with a leakage coefficient λ_leak rather than quadratically with the entanglement angle, and that gradient orthogonality between task and residual losses emerges as a geometric byproduct of the CKA constraint, eliminating the need for explicit gradient projection.

## Results

On NYUv2 (3 tasks), CORE-MTL achieves 56.93% segmentation mIoU, 0.3544 depth absolute error, and 22.49° mean normal angle, outperforming the strongest baseline RepMTL (54.92% mIoU, 0.3727 Abs Err) by 2.0 mIoU points. On Cityscapes (2 tasks), CORE-MTL reaches 72.29% mIoU and 19.61 depth relative error, surpassing MTAN (70.23% mIoU) and RepMTL (70.79% mIoU). Under OOD transfer (GTA5→Cityscapes), CORE-MTL attains 54.35% target mIoU with the smallest performance gap (Δ=0.1065), and on Cityscapes-C it achieves 61.04% mIoU and 38.10 relative depth error, the best among all 10 baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CORE-MTL outperforms all 10 baselines on every reported metric across both ID and OOD benchmarks. The closest competitor is RepMTL (Wang et al., 2025), a representation-level method, which trails CORE-MTL by 2.0 mIoU on NYUv2 segmentation (54.92% vs. 56.93%) and by 1.9 mIoU on Cityscapes-C OOD (59.16% vs. 61.04%), though RepMTL achieves a slightly lower depth relative error on Cityscapes ID (44.32 vs. 19.61 is actually CORE-MTL winning). Among gradient-manipulation methods, PCGrad (Yu et al., 2020) and GradNorm (Chen et al., 2018) are the strongest, yet CORE-MTL exceeds them by 4.7 and 4.3 mIoU respectively on NYUv2 segmentation. Architecture-based MTAN (Liu et al., 2019) is competitive on Cityscapes segmentation (70.23% vs. 72.29%) but lags significantly on OOD transfer (51.47% vs. 54.35% target mIoU on GTA5→Cityscapes). The multi-objective optimizer STCH (Lin et al., 2024) and meta-learning approach MOML (Ye et al., 2021) show the largest OOD degradation, with MOML suffering a 0.9818 depth absolute error gap on GTA5→Cityscapes compared to CORE-MTL's 0.0759. On the Colored-Cityscapes shortcut stress test, CORE-MTL achieves 37.38% target mIoU versus 35.97% for MTAN and 35.48% for STCH, confirming robustness even under deliberately induced semantic-appearance correlations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant optimization-centric MTL paradigm (GradNorm, PCGrad, STCH, FairGrad) and architecture-centric sharing (MTAN, MMoE, PLE) by arguing that both operate at the wrong level: they coordinate tasks through gradients or routing without shaping what the shared representation encodes. It repurposes ideas from causal representation learning (IRM, DSN, Locatello's disentanglement), CKA-based independence (Kornblith et al.), and physics-based inverse rendering into a unified semantic-residual factorization framework. The closest prior work is RepMTL (Wang et al., 2025), which also targets representation-level task saliency, but CORE-MTL adds causal grounding via reconstruction decoders and provides theoretical OOD guarantees that RepMTL does not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical impossibility result (Theorem 2.3) reframing gradient conflict as a symptom of representation entanglement rather than the root cause is a useful conceptual shift for the MTL community, and the practical finding that gradient orthogonality emerges as a free byproduct of disentanglement (Proposition 2.5) suggests a simpler alternative to gradient surgery. However, the guarantees rely on a stylized linear-Gaussian SCM with a single rotation angle, and the method is instantiated primarily for dense scene understanding; extending the theory to deep nonlinear encoders and to more heterogeneous or multimodal task sets remains open. The near-constant training cost scaling with task count (vs. PCGrad's linear scaling) is a practically relevant advantage for large-scale multi-task deployments.
