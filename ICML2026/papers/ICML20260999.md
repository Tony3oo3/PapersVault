## Problem

Contrastive training of dual-encoder retrieval models with InfoNCE loss depends critically on hard in-batch negatives, yet existing solutions rely on expensive corpus-level ANN indexing or heuristic batch construction that is not explicitly tied to the training objective. The paper asks whether the mini-batch itself can be made informative through a principled, loss-aware reordering of training examples at each epoch, avoiding costly full-corpus negative mining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260999/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

HOBIT consumes the set of query-positive training pairs (e.g., 502,939 pairs for MSMARCO) along with their current encoder embeddings, and outputs an ordered sequence of mini-batches of size b for the epoch. Unlike hard-negative mining methods that search over the full 8M-document corpus, HOBIT operates exclusively on the training pairs, computing pairwise hardness scores w_ij = q_i^T d_j − α·d_i^T d_j on the GPU. At inference, the trained dual encoder maps query text to a unit-normalized embedding and retrieves a ranked document list from the corpus via cosine similarity.

## Method

HOBIT derives a hardness score w_ij = q_i^T d_j − α·d_i^T d_j from the InfoNCE gradient, encoding two complementary criteria: the negative should be hard (high query-negative similarity) yet non-contradictory (well separated from the positive document). The exact batch-hardness objective involving a max over candidates is NP-hard, so HOBIT replaces it with a smooth Log-Sum-Exp surrogate that is proven monotone and submodular, admitting a greedy algorithm with a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee. At each epoch, the algorithm samples a seed set S of size s, collects a top-k candidate pool via exact nearest-neighbor search over training-pair embeddings, and greedily fills the remaining b−s slots by maximizing marginal gain. A cached variant, HOBIT-C, reuses embeddings from the previous epoch's forward passes, reducing per-epoch overhead to under 1% while preserving most of the retrieval quality gain.

## Results

On MSMARCO Passage, HOBIT achieves MRR@10 of 27.4 (roberta-base) and 30.1 (e5-large-unsupervised), outperforming the strongest baseline NGAME by +0.6 and +1.5 points respectively. On Natural Questions, HOBIT reaches R@10 of 52.4 and 62.9, improving over NGAME by +4.5 and +1.6 points. On TriviaQA, HOBIT attains R@10 of 52.5 and 57.7, beating NGAME by +1.0 and +1.7 points. The cached variant HOBIT-C preserves within 0.3–0.7 points of full HOBIT while reducing per-epoch overhead from 23–28% to 0.1–1.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOBIT outperforms all four batching baselines (Random, NGAME, BatchSampler, B3) on nearly every metric across both encoders and three datasets, with the sole exception being NQ R@100 under e5-large where NGAME edges ahead by 0.6 points (90.5 vs. 89.9). The largest margins appear on Natural Questions with roberta-base, where HOBIT beats NGAME by +4.5 R@10 and B3 by +8.0 R@10, indicating that static or heuristic batch ordering degrades most severely on this dataset. Against hard-negative mining methods (Table 4), HOBIT complements rather than replaces them: combining HOBIT with TriSampler consistently improves R@10 by +0.4 to +3.3 points across datasets, while combining with ANCE yields mixed results (slightly negative on MSMARCO MRR@10, positive on TriviaQA R@10 by +1.8). B3 underperforms even Random on MSMARCO (23.4 vs. 24.4 MRR@10), confirming that fixed batch orders become stale as encoder representations drift during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOBIT builds directly on the DPR/InfoNCE in-batch negative paradigm (Karpukhin et al., 2020) but departs from both hard-negative mining (ANCE, TriSampler) and heuristic batch construction (NGAME, B3, BatchSampler) by reframing batch composition as a principled optimization problem derived from the InfoNCE gradient. The key conceptual departure is the non-contradiction criterion: prior methods select negatives based solely on query-document similarity, while HOBIT penalizes negatives that are near-duplicates of the positive, a distinction formalized through the hardness score w_ij. Methodologically, the paper introduces a log-sum-exp smoothing of the max-based batch hardness objective, proving monotone submodularity to obtain a greedy algorithm with a (1−1/e) guarantee — a theoretical treatment absent from prior heuristic batching work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HOBIT offers a lightweight, loss-aware alternative to expensive corpus-level hard-negative mining, making principled batch construction practical at scale via the HOBIT-C cached variant (up to 29% per-epoch speedup with <1 point loss). The submodularity result provides the first formal approximation guarantee for batch construction in contrastive retrieval training, grounding what was previously heuristic-driven practice. Limitations include added wall-clock overhead from batch construction and the observation that harder batches may require more epochs to converge; open questions include how the non-contradiction criterion generalizes beyond cosine similarity and whether the approach extends to multi-positive or listwise training objectives.
