## Problem

The Bayesian Mallows Model provides principled probabilistic preference learning with uncertainty quantification for recommender systems, but its MCMC-based inference suffers from slow mixing, long burn-in periods, and multimodality in the discrete permutation space, making it infeasible for real-time applications with hundreds of items and thousands of users. No efficient variational inference method existed for discrete distributions over permutation spaces, as standard VI approaches (CAVI, SVI) target continuous exponential families and the mean-field assumption is ill-suited to the combinatorial structure of rankings.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either full rankings (N users each assigning ranks 1–n to n items) or binary click vectors (each user has clicked a subset of items, interpreted as preferring clicked over unclicked), along with a scale parameter α estimated offline via pairwise cosine similarity matching against simulated Mallows data. Outputs include posterior samples of the group consensus ranking ρ, per-user full ranking distributions, and top-k recommendations with calibrated posterior probabilities (P(c_j ≤ R_i^j ≤ c_j + k)), where the clicked items are excluded from further recommendation. The discrete nature of the output space (permutations of n items) means all inference operates over P_n rather than continuous parameter spaces.

## Method

The Pseudo-Mallows distribution factorizes the joint over the consensus ranking ρ into n sequential univariate discrete distributions, where each element ρ_{o_k} is sampled from a Mallows-like conditional given previously sampled elements, with the product of numerators exactly matching the Mallows likelihood numerator. The key variational parameter is the sampling ordering o ∈ P_n, and the paper conjectures (with empirical and partial theoretical support) that the optimal ordering belongs to the 'V-set' of the consensus: sample the middle-ranked item first, then work symmetrically outward to the edges, which aligns the Pseudo-Mallows mode with the Mallows posterior mode. For finite N, a Gaussian perturbation around the estimated V-ranking (with tunable σ) robustifies the approximation. For clicking data, a data-augmentation loop alternates between sampling individual full rankings (using a uniform ordering over clicked/unclicked groups) and updating the consensus via the Pseudo-Mallows, enabling personalized recommendations with calibrated uncertainty. The ELBO optimization over the ordering can be reduced to a minimum-weight bipartite matching problem solvable in polynomial time via the Hungarian method.

## Results

On the NRK clicking dataset (7,872 users, 200 items, 17 clusters), Pseudo-Mallows achieves 22.6% top-10 recommendation accuracy versus 13.9% for Mallows MCMC under a 10-minute computation budget — nearly double the accuracy. In simulation studies (n up to 1000 items, N up to 5000 users), Pseudo-Mallows reaches comparable footrule distance to the true consensus in a fraction of the time required for MCMC convergence, with no burn-in period. The posterior probabilities from Pseudo-Mallows are well-calibrated (diagonal trend in reliability plot), while Mallows MCMC systematically overestimates recommendation confidence due to incomplete mixing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Pseudo-Mallows against the Bayesian Mallows MCMC (Vitelli et al., 2018), implemented via the BayesMallows R package (Sørensen et al., 2020). On the NRK real-data recommendation task under a 10-minute time limit, Pseudo-Mallows achieves 22.6% top-10 accuracy versus 13.9% for Mallows MCMC — a 62% relative improvement — because MCMC suffers from a long burn-in period and high sample autocorrelation, leaving too few effective samples within the time budget. In the full-ranking simulation (Section 4.1), when MCMC is allowed to run long enough to fully converge, its consensus estimate is marginally more accurate than Pseudo-Mallows (since the data is generated from the Mallows model), but Pseudo-Mallows reaches a comparable footrule distance orders of magnitude faster. For the individual-ranking recommendation simulation (Appendix E.2), the same pattern holds: MCMC eventually surpasses Pseudo-Mallows in accuracy given sufficient runtime, but under realistic time constraints Pseudo-Mallows is consistently superior. The uncertainty calibration comparison (Figure 3) further shows Pseudo-Mallows producing well-calibrated posterior probabilities while MCMC overestimates confidence, confirming that the MCMC chain had not fully mixed within the allotted time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian Mallows Model (BMM) of Vitelli et al. (2018) and its clicking-data extension by Liu et al. (2019a) by replacing MCMC inference with a variational approximation. The key departure is from standard VI frameworks (CAVI, SVI, mean-field) that target continuous exponential families; here the authors construct a new distribution—the Pseudo-Mallows—that factorizes the discrete permutation space into sequential conditional univariate distributions, with the sampling order as the variational parameter. The optimization of this discrete variational parameter is cast as a bipartite matching problem solvable via the Hungarian method, drawing on combinatorial optimization rather than gradient-based VI. The V-ranking conjecture (middle item sampled first, then outwards) is a novel structural insight not present in prior Mallows or permutation-distribution literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Pseudo-Mallows makes the BMM practical for real-time recommender systems where MCMC's slow mixing and burn-in are prohibitive, and it is the first variational inference method developed for a complex discrete distribution over a permutation space. However, the V-ranking optimality is only conjectured (supported by small-n enumeration and a heuristic iterative search, not a general proof), the α estimation via cosine similarity is heuristic, and the method still samples all n items per iteration even when only top-k are needed. Open questions include whether the V-ranking structure generalizes beyond the Mallows data-generating assumption, whether better α tuning (e.g., Bayesian optimization) would close the remaining accuracy gap with fully-converged MCMC, and whether the approach extends to other distance metrics or non-homogeneous user populations.
