## Problem

Real-time interactive world models face a fundamental trade-off: distillation-based methods achieve low latency but discard memory, causing scenes to change upon revisit, while memory-augmented methods preserve geometric consistency but are too slow for real-time interaction. No existing approach simultaneously delivers sub-frame latency and long-horizon geometric coherence under free user navigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264326/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image or text prompt to initialize the world, then consumes a stream of user actions represented as both discrete keyboard keys (W/A/S/D, etc.) and continuous camera poses (rotation + translation). It produces streaming 720p video at 24 FPS in chunks of 16 frames, generated autoregressively with a KV cache so per-chunk latency stays constant regardless of video length. As a secondary output, the generated video can be fed into a feed-forward 3D reconstruction model to yield point clouds, and text prompts can trigger dynamic scene events mid-stream.

## Method

WorldPlay is a chunk-wise autoregressive diffusion transformer (built on a pretrained DiT video model) with three key mechanisms. First, a Dual Action Representation injects discrete keys via a zero-initialized MLP into the timestep embedding and continuous camera frustums via PRoPE into self-attention, combining scale-robust control with precise spatial location caching. Second, Reconstituted Context Memory dynamically rebuilds a context set per chunk from temporal memory (3 most recent chunks) and spatial memory (1 non-adjacent chunk selected by FOV overlap and camera distance), then applies Temporal Reframing to reassign RoPE positional indices so retrieved long-past frames appear temporally close to the current chunk, preventing extrapolation artifacts and preserving their geometric influence. Third, Context Forcing is a distillation procedure that aligns the memory context between a memory-augmented bidirectional teacher and the causal autoregressive student during self-rollout, enabling 4-step inference at real-time speed while preventing the distribution mismatch that would otherwise erode long-term consistency and cause error accumulation.

## Results

On the long-term (≥250 frames) evaluation, WorldPlay achieves PSNR 18.94, SSIM 0.585, LPIPS 0.371, Rdist 0.332, and Tdist 0.797, outperforming all baselines by substantial margins (e.g., +3.57 PSNR over Gen3C, +9.37 PSNR over Matrix-Game-2.0). On the short-term (61 frames) setting, WorldPlay reaches PSNR 21.92 and SSIM 0.702, the best among all methods. WorldPlay is the only method that simultaneously achieves real-time 24 FPS generation at 720p and long-term geometric consistency. On the MEt3R 3D structural consistency metric, WorldPlay scores 0.133, beating Gen3C (0.187), GameCraft (0.305), and Matrix-Game-2.0 (0.367).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WorldPlay dominates all seven baselines in Table 2 on the long-term setting, where the gap is most pronounced: it achieves 18.94 PSNR vs. 15.37 for Gen3C (the strongest memory-based baseline), 12.77 for VMem, and 9.57 for the only other real-time method Matrix-Game-2.0. Among non-memory methods (CameraCtrl, SEVA, ViewCrafter, GameCraft), WorldPlay leads by 8–12 PSNR in long-term, confirming that the Reconstituted Context Memory is the primary driver of consistency gains. ViewCrafter and Gen3C achieve slightly lower Rdist in the short-term setting (0.029 and 0.024 vs. 0.031), reflecting their explicit 3D reconstruction advantage for rotation precision, but they degrade severely over long horizons due to depth estimation errors. On the WorldScore benchmark (Table 9), WorldPlay achieves the highest average score of 79.74, edging out Voyager (77.62) and WonderWorld (72.69), with particular strength in 3D Consistency (86.43) and Photometric Consistency (89.07). The MEt3R evaluation further confirms WorldPlay's 3D structural superiority (0.133 vs. 0.187 for Gen3C), validating that implicit memory conditioning outperforms explicit 3D cache approaches for long-horizon geometric alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

WorldPlay extends the chunk-wise autoregressive video diffusion paradigm from Diffusion Forcing and the distillation framework of CausVid/Self-Forcing, but identifies a critical gap: standard distillation methods fail for memory-aware models because the teacher's and student's memory contexts are misaligned, causing distribution matching to collapse. The paper introduces Context Forcing to resolve this by aligning memory context between teacher and student during distillation, and pairs it with Reconstituted Context Memory (using temporal reframing to overcome RoPE's long-range decay) and a Dual Action Representation that combines discrete keys with continuous camera poses via PRoPE. This positions WorldPlay as the first method to simultaneously achieve real-time (24 FPS, 720p) interactive generation and long-term geometric consistency, a trade-off that prior work (speed-focused: Matrix-Game 2.0; consistency-focused: Gen3C, VMem) has treated as mutually exclusive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By resolving the speed-memory trade-off, WorldPlay makes interactive world models practically deployable for embodied AI, game development, and 3D content creation, with demonstrated applications in 3D reconstruction and promptable dynamic events. Key limitations remain: generation is capped at ~30 seconds, error accumulation is mitigated but not eliminated by distillation, and FOV-based memory retrieval degrades under significant occlusion. Open questions include scaling to minute- or hour-long sequences, fundamentally preventing error drift in autoregressive diffusion training, and extending to multi-agent interaction with complex physical dynamics.
