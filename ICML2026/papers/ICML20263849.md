## Problem

Role-Play TTS requires generated speech to maintain stylistic consistency with scene descriptions, character profiles, and multi-turn dialogue history, but no objective metric exists to quantify speaking style, and existing RL rewards (e.g., emotion classification) fail to capture the rich, non-emotional stylistic attributes needed for authentic role-play. This absence of a measurable style signal prevents both fair evaluation and effective reinforcement-learning-based alignment of LALMs for expressive RP-TTS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263849/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The RP-TTS model receives a structured prompt containing a scene description, character profiles for up to K speakers, the target transcript for the current turn, and interleaved text–TA4 audio tokens from all previous turns as dialogue history; it outputs an interleaved TA4 token sequence representing the expressive speech for the current turn. The MCLP metric separately takes a candidate audio sequence and a ground-truth audio sequence for the same transcript, constructs a dual-turn context [transcript, candidate, transcript], and returns a scalar mean log-probability score where higher values indicate greater stylistic continuity between the two utterances.

## Method

The core contribution is MCLP, which exploits the in-context learning priors of a pretrained LALM (Step-Audio-2, further trained as a continuation model on 3M hours of transcribed speech) to score stylistic consistency: it computes the mean log-likelihood of ground-truth TA4 audio tokens conditioned on a context that includes the candidate audio, so that a style-matched candidate yields higher continuation probability. For training, the paper first performs SFT on the WenetSpeech-RP-TTS dataset (≈1,435 h, 311k multi-turn scenes from Chinese drama videos) to teach the model scene/character-conditioned synthesis, then applies GRPO with a gated hybrid reward that combines MCLP (style) with a CER-based content penalty, zeroing the reward entirely if CER exceeds a threshold τ to prevent the model from generating 'expressive gibberish.' The semantic TA4 tokenizer is deliberately chosen so that MCLP biases toward stylistic rather than acoustic similarity under the fixed-transcript evaluation protocol.

## Results

On WenetSpeech-RP-TTS, the proposed method achieves the best performance across all metrics in both settings. With audio history, it reaches CER 1.130%, MCLP −4.636, CAM++ 0.724, and Emo2Vec 0.917; without audio history, CER 1.625%, MCLP −4.687, CAM++ 0.704, and Emo2Vec 0.910. Subjectively, it obtains a MOS of 3.576 (±0.045) for stylistic consistency, substantially above the best LALM baseline (MiMo-Audio-7B, 2.484) and the best Instruct-TTS baseline (OV-InstructTTS, 2.864), though still below the ground-truth reference (4.461). MCLP validation with 32 expert listeners shows a win rate exceeding 0.8 when ΔMCLP > 0.1, confirming strong alignment with human stylistic judgments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric in both w/ and w/o audio-history settings. Among multi-turn LALMs, MiMo-Audio-7B is the strongest competitor (MOS 2.484, MCLP −4.753 w/ history), but the proposed method improves MOS by +1.092 and MCLP by +0.117 while reducing CER from 10.6% to 1.13%. Step-Audio-2-mini (the instruction-tuned backbone) shows the weakest multi-turn performance (MOS 1.856, CER 3.276% w/ history), indicating that SFT alone is insufficient for RP-TTS. Among single-turn Instruct-TTS systems, OV-InstructTTS achieves the highest MOS (2.864) and MCLP (−4.768) in the w/o history setting, yet still trails the proposed method by 0.712 MOS and 0.081 MCLP, highlighting the benefit of multi-turn audio conditioning and MCLP-based RL alignment. GPT-Audio, despite its general audio capabilities, suffers severe content degradation without audio history (CER 44.7%) and the lowest MOS (1.915), underscoring the difficulty of RP-TTS for general-purpose models. The ablation further confirms that removing the CER reward causes catastrophic reward hacking (CER >59%) while removing MCLP yields flat, unexpressive speech (MOS 2.331), validating the necessity of the hybrid reward design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LLM-based TTS (Step-Audio-2, CosyVoice), instruction-based style control (OV-InstructTTS, Qwen3-TTS), and speech role-playing agents (SpeechRole, VoxRole), but deliberately carves out a distinct sub-problem: content-given, multi-turn role-play TTS where the primary challenge is stylistic consistency rather than semantic alignment. It departs from prior RL-for-TTS work (RRPO, Emo-DPO) that relies on discrete emotion classifiers as proxy rewards, instead proposing MCLP—a continuation log-probability computed by a pretrained LALM—as a dense, interpretable style signal. The dataset construction pipeline (WenetSpeech drama videos + LLM-annotated scene/character profiles) extends existing speech role-play benchmarks by providing explicit scene and character context tuples rather than relying solely on character-centric synthetic data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MCLP provides the first objective, interpretable metric for stylistic consistency in expressive TTS, filling a gap that has limited both evaluation and RL-based optimization of style. The gated hybrid reward design (MCLP + CER with a hard intelligibility gate) offers a practical template for balancing expressiveness and content fidelity in RL-based TTS alignment. Key limitations include Mandarin-only evaluation, drama-domain data, and LLM-generated annotations that may introduce bias; open questions include whether MCLP generalizes to non-drama domains (audiobooks, games, virtual assistants) and to multilingual settings beyond the preliminary English results. The WenetSpeech-RP-TTS dataset (~1,435 hours, 311k scenes) is a substantial resource for the community.
