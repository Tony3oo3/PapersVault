## Problem

Training Process Reward Models (PRMs) for biological reasoning requires step-level supervision that is prohibitively expensive to obtain from domain experts, while automated weak labels (Monte Carlo rollouts, LLM-as-a-judge) are inherently noisy. Existing Weak-to-Strong Generalization theory is primarily descriptive rather than prescriptive, offering no actionable mechanism for selectively curating high-quality training signals from noisy weak supervision in complex multi-step reasoning domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260437/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The PRM takes a biological reasoning question (e.g., a gene perturbation–target gene pair in a specific cell line) and a Chain-of-Thought trajectory of T intermediate steps, producing a scalar reward score in [0,1] for each prefix z_{1:t}. At inference time, N=8 candidate trajectories are generated by a fixed policy model, scored step-wise by the PRM, and the trajectory with the highest aggregated reward is selected via Best-of-N. Weak supervision is generated from heterogeneous sources—three LLM-as-a-judge prompt variants (Context, Analogical, Direct) and Monte Carlo rollouts from multiple model sizes (Qwen3-1.7B/4B/7B)—aggregated by majority vote into a single binary label per step.

## Method

DC-W2S stratifies weak step labels into four reliability regimes by intersecting two orthogonal consensus signals: Teacher-Self-Consensus (TSC), which measures agreement across heterogeneous labeling functions via normalized label variance, and Teacher-Neighborhood-Consensus (TNC), which averages TSC over semantically and biologically similar neighboring steps retrieved via k-NN in a 384-d embedding space (optionally filtered by gene-pair embeddings from ESM or CellProfiler). Building on this P1–P4 stratification, the framework employs a two-level anchored training strategy: (i) a distribution-balanced sampling curriculum that greedily selects training instances to equalize the four pattern proportions toward 25% each, and (ii) a reliability-aware loss masking that suppresses cross-entropy gradients from low-reliability patterns (e.g., P4), enabling controlled ablations of each regime's contribution. The paper further derives a theoretical error bound for the thresholded PRM under calibrated soft reliability weights and (c,q,η)-soft robust expansion, showing that the ground-truth step-label error is controlled by the weak-label error plus terms depending on effective noise and non-robust point mass.

## Results

On PerturbQA (4 cell lines, DE and DoC tasks), DC-W2S with Best-of-N (N=8) achieves an average F1 of 68.50%, substantially outperforming the strongest PRM baseline (Llama-PRM800K at 58.56%) by nearly 10 points. On the out-of-distribution RPE1 cell line, the method reaches 64.01% average F1 (SUMMER prompting) versus 53.19% for greedy decoding. On the cross-task BioReason KEGG benchmark, data-efficient variants trained on only 100k curated samples (BoN w/ CellProfiler: 88.89%, BoN w/ ESM: 89.08% Weighted F1) surpass the full-set model (87.40%), demonstrating a 'less-is-more' effect under domain shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC-W2S (BoN w/ Full Set) achieves 68.50% average F1 on PerturbQA, outperforming Llama-PRM800K (58.56%) by +9.94 points, Qwen-PRM800K (58.38%) by +10.12 points, and VersaPRM (57.16%) by +11.34 points. The gap is particularly pronounced on the OOD RPE1 cell line, where DC-W2S reaches 64.01% (SUMMER) versus 53.19% for the best baseline, confirming that the learned reward captures transferable biological reasoning structure rather than cell-line-specific patterns. Against the bioinformatics foundation model GEARS, DC-W2S achieves absolute F1 gains of +12.7 (HepG2), +3.8 (Jurkat), +22.4 (K562), and +0.6 (RPE1), despite GEARS having access to RPE1 training data while DC-W2S treats it as strictly out-of-distribution. On the cross-task BioReason KEGG benchmark, the data-efficient DC-W2S variants (100k samples) outperform the full-set model by +1.49 to +1.68 Weighted F1, indicating that indiscriminate training on all weak labels introduces noise that undermines cross-task transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Weak-to-Strong Generalization (W2S) line of work (Burns et al., 2023; Lang et al., 2024) from its original setting of classification with noisy labels to the specific problem of training Process Reward Models from step-wise weak supervision in biological reasoning. While prior W2S theory is primarily descriptive—explaining why strong students can outperform weak teachers under favorable data geometry—DC-W2S introduces a prescriptive mechanism (dual-consensus stratification via Self-Consensus and Neighborhood-Consensus) that actively curates training signals. The MC rollout labeling follows Math-Shepherd (Wang et al., 2024), and the theoretical analysis builds on Lang et al.'s robust expansion framework by introducing calibrated soft reliability weights that allow different reliability regimes to contribute differently to the generalization guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying PRMs for scientific reasoning: the prohibitive cost of expert-verified step-level annotations. By showing that strategic curation via dual-consensus stratification outperforms indiscriminate training on large noisy datasets—particularly for out-of-distribution and cross-task transfer—it provides a scalable pathway for process supervision in domains where expert annotation is infeasible. Open questions include whether the neighborhood label consistency assumption (Assumption 3.4) holds in practice for more complex reasoning, whether the framework generalizes beyond biological perturbation prediction to other scientific domains, and how to handle the case where embedding quality is insufficient to construct biologically coherent neighborhoods. The reliance on a specific biological embedding (CellProfiler/ESM) for the strongest gains also limits immediate transferability to domains lacking such structured representations.
