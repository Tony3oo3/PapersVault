## Problem

Learning an instance-level classifier from label proportions (LLP) is challenging because individual labels are unavailable—only aggregate class frequencies per bag are observed. Existing approaches either rely on adversarial training (LLPGAN) that is unstable and hard to adapt to pre-trained models, or fail to exploit the label proportions as structural information in distribution alignment. This paper asks how to leverage label proportions to guide a learnable proxy distribution toward the true data distribution in a way that is both theoretically grounded and practically simple.

## Input / Output

The method consumes bags of unlabeled feature vectors (tabular rows or image embeddings) paired with a label proportion vector z_i ∈ Δ_C giving the fraction of each class in bag i; individual labels are never observed. Bags are constructed with 2 to C classes present and sizes of 50, 100, or 250 samples. The output is a standard instance-level classifier f_θ: X → Y that predicts a single class label for any new sample. In the end-to-end image setting, a frozen ResNet-18 (ImageNet-pretrained) produces 512-dim embeddings that are optionally projected to 50 dimensions before anchor flow, and a fully connected head outputs class probabilities.

## Method

FlowLLP jointly minimizes three objectives via block-coordinate descent: a bag-level cross-entropy loss on predicted vs. true label proportions, an instance-level cross-entropy loss on K learnable anchor points with fixed labels, and a Wasserstein-2 distance between the anchor distribution and each bag's empirical distribution. The key design choice is the marginal constraint on the Wasserstein transport: anchor weights for class c are set proportional to the bag's label proportion z_i^(c)·C/K, so the optimal coupling is structurally encouraged to match same-class mass. Anchors are updated by a particle flow whose per-step gradient combines the OT barycentric mapping (via the envelope theorem) and the instance-level loss gradient, effectively pulling each anchor toward the convex hull of same-class bag samples. The classifier is then retrained on the fixed anchors plus bag loss. A theoretical analysis proves almost-sure convergence of each anchor to its class-conditional convex hull under a gap-in-label-proportions condition, and shows that the Wasserstein optimal plan matches at least a_{1}−a_{0} fraction of mass between same-class conditionals in the binary case.

## Results

On 54 multiclass dataset/bag-size experiments, FlowLLP achieves a mean balanced accuracy of 89.1%±1, winning 44 out of 54 settings, compared to LLPGAN (88.4%±1, 9 wins) and the Full-label upper bound of 90.8%. On 18 binary experiments, FlowLLP reaches 83.7%±1 (10 wins) versus LLPGAN's 83.6%±1 (7 wins) and the Full-label bound of 88.3%. The gap over DAOT (40.3% MC / 62.2% Binary) and EasyLLP (55.1% MC / 75.7% Binary) is substantial, while the margin over LLPGAN is narrow but consistent, particularly in multiclass settings where label-proportion diversity is higher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowLLP decisively outperforms DAOT (Dulac-Arnold et al., 2019) and EasyLLP (Busa-Fekete et al., 2023) by 34–39 points on multiclass and 18–22 points on binary balanced accuracy, confirming that bag-level loss alone or debiased instance-level estimation is insufficient without explicit distribution alignment. Against LLPGAN (Liu et al., 2019), the closest competitor, FlowLLP gains 0.7 points on average across 54 multiclass experiments (89.1 vs 88.4) and wins 44 of 54 individual settings versus LLPGAN's 9, with the advantage most pronounced when bags contain only 2 classes (e.g., 97.7% vs 96.8% on HAR with 250 bags). On binary datasets the margin shrinks to 0.1 points (83.7 vs 83.6), which the authors attribute to reduced label-proportion diversity in two-class bags. FlowLLP also demonstrates practical advantages: it trains without adversarial instability, can leverage pre-trained backbones end-to-end (achieving 85.6% on CIFAR-10 with bags of size 50), and scales gracefully with bag size (total runtime under 6300 s for bags of 200 on a single V100).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes optimal transport and particle flow from the domain adaptation literature (Courty et al., 2017; Rakotomamonjy et al., 2022) into the learning-from-label-proportions (LLP) setting, where prior methods (Ardehaly & Culotta, 2017; Dulac-Arnold et al., 2019; Busa-Fekete et al., 2023) have focused on reformulating loss functions or estimating pseudo-labels. The closest prior method, LLPGAN (Liu et al., 2019), also generates a proxy distribution to match bags but relies on adversarial GAN training; FlowLLP replaces this with a Wasserstein-based particle flow that explicitly encodes label proportions into the transport marginal constraints, avoiding adversarial instability. The paper also draws on stochastic approximation theory (Robbins-Siegmund) to provide convergence guarantees for the anchor flow, a theoretical contribution absent from prior LLP work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a simpler, more stable alternative to GAN-based LLP that can leverage pre-trained feature extractors in an end-to-end pipeline, which is practically valuable for privacy-sensitive applications (advertising, medical data). The theoretical analysis identifying gap and diversity in label proportions as a necessary condition for learnability gives practitioners a clear diagnostic for when LLP is feasible. However, the empirical gains over LLPGAN are modest (≤1% on most datasets), the method is more sensitive to noisy label proportions, and the computational cost of exact optimal transport limits scalability to very large bags—open questions include whether Sinkhorn approximations preserve the alignment guarantees and whether the approach extends to multi-source or streaming LLP settings.
