## Problem

Online RL for flow-matching models has been assumed to require sampling full denoising trajectories and assigning rewards only at the terminal state, as in FlowGRPO. This design incurs 10× higher training cost and provides poor credit assignment, since a single delayed terminal reward cannot distinguish the heterogeneous importance of early (coarse-structure) versus late (fine-detail) denoising steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263845/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and generates an RGB image via a flow-matching backbone (SD3-M or FLUX). During training, the policy operates on intermediate noisy states (x_t, t, c) and produces a single-step prediction that is re-noised back to a nearby noise level; a stop-gradient collapse to t=0 yields a pseudo-terminal sample on which the reward model (CLIPScore, GenEval, or HPS-v2.1) is evaluated. At inference, standard multi-step ODE samplers are retained, so generation fidelity is unchanged.

## Method

iGRPO replaces FlowGRPO's multi-step SDE rollouts with a single-step mapping from a noisy state directly toward the data space (inspired by consistency models), followed by re-noising the result back to an intermediate noise level with a small exploration covariance to retain stochasticity. After each single-step action, the generated sample is collapsed to a pseudo-terminal via a stop-gradient velocity step, and the reward is evaluated immediately, yielding a dense, step-local signal rather than a single terminal score. The policy is a Gaussian kernel with a closed-form log-probability and per-step KL to a reference, plugged into GRPO's group-relative advantage normalization and PPO-style clipped surrogate. The instant reward is inherently time-dependent: its variance follows a U-shaped pattern across timesteps, naturally inducing more exploration at early (low-frequency) steps and less at late (high-frequency) steps, matching the coarse-to-fine structure of flow-matching inference. This design reduces training cost by 10.2× over FlowGRPO while achieving higher final alignment quality.

## Results

On GenEval with CLIPScore as the reward, iGRPO trained for only 1k iterations achieves an Overall score of 0.82, outperforming FlowGRPO trained for 10k iterations (0.74) by +0.08 and the SD3-M base model (0.63) by +0.19. With GenEval as the reward, iGRPO reaches 0.96 Overall at 1.5k iterations, surpassing FlowGRPO at 8k iterations (0.95). On the FLUX-based setup (Table 3), iGRPO improves HPS-v2.1 from 0.313 to 0.351 (+12.1%) and ImageReward from 1.088 to 1.441 (+32.4%) over the FLUX base, while also beating DanceGRPO on all five metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iGRPO (1k iter) decisively outperforms FlowGRPO (10k iter) on GenEval Overall (0.82 vs. 0.74, +0.08) while using only 10% of the training iterations, with the largest margin on Position (+0.17) and Two Object (+0.07). Against the SD3-M base model, iGRPO gains +0.19 Overall, with particularly large improvements on Counting (+0.30) and Position (+0.25). iGRPO also surpasses the stronger SD3-L (0.71) and FLUX.1-Dev (0.66) base models by +0.11 and +0.16 respectively. On the FLUX-based setup, iGRPO beats DanceGRPO on all five metrics (HPS +1.4%, CLIPScore +4.5%, PickScore +0.9%, ImageReward +9.7%, Unified Reward +4.3%). The only baseline iGRPO does not surpass is GPT-4o (0.84 vs. 0.82 Overall on GenEval with GenEval reward), though iGRPO is a 2B-parameter open model versus a proprietary multimodal LLM. iGRPO also outperforms DALLE-3 (0.67) and JanusFlow (0.63) by substantial margins on GenEval Overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

iGRPO directly extends FlowGRPO (Liu et al., 2025), which first brought GRPO-style online RL to flow-matching models by converting the ODE sampler into an SDE and performing multi-step rollouts with terminal rewards. The key departure is twofold: (1) it replaces full-trajectory SDE rollouts with a single-step consistency-style mapping (inspired by Song et al., 2023) plus re-noising for exploration, and (2) it replaces the single terminal reward with instant, timestep-dependent rewards computed via a stop-gradient collapse to the data space. This positions iGRPO as a conceptual challenge to the assumption inherited from FlowGRPO and DanceGRPO (Xue et al., 2025) that flow-model RL inherently requires traversing the entire reverse process before receiving feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.2× reduction in training cost (205 GPU-hours vs. 2.1k) while simultaneously improving final GenEval scores (0.82 vs. 0.74) makes online RL alignment of flow-matching generators substantially more accessible to smaller labs and iterative prototyping workflows. The timestep-dependent instant reward mechanism—where variance is high at early and late denoising steps but low in the middle—offers a principled credit-assignment signal that better matches the coarse-to-fine structure of flow-matching inference, a design principle that could transfer to other continuous generative processes (e.g., video, 3D). Open questions include whether the single-step approximation degrades for tasks requiring long-horizon consistency (e.g., multi-object spatial reasoning across many denoising stages), whether the approach scales to video or 3D flow models with longer trajectories, and whether the U-shaped reward variance pattern holds under different reward functions or base architectures beyond SD3 and FLUX.
