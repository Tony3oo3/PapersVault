## Problem

Mid-training has become a standard intermediate stage for improving LLM reasoning, yet its design choices—data composition, evaluation strategy, and interaction with downstream reinforcement learning—remain poorly understood. Prior work typically reports narrow domain gains (e.g., math or code) without assessing retention of general abilities, long-context performance, or RL compatibility, and lacks controlled cross-model-family comparisons. PRISM addresses this by providing a holistic empirical study that systematically quantifies how mid-training decisions interact across domains, model architectures, and training stages.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a pre-trained LLM checkpoint (ranging from 3B to 24B parameters across dense, hybrid Mamba, and MoE architectures) and a ~27B-token mid-training mixture composed of general web data (DCLM-EDU), domain-specific reasoning sources (math, code, science), and chat/instruction data, sampled at empirically tuned per-source weights. Mid-training runs at 8k context length with 25k steps, producing a mid-trained checkpoint that preserves general capabilities while boosting domain reasoning. An optional GRPO-based RL stage (1,000 steps, 16k context, 16 responses per prompt) then refines the checkpoint further, with a thinking-format reward enforcing structured chain-of-thought outputs. A separate long-context restoration phase (1k steps, Best-Fit Decreasing packing) can recover RULER performance degraded by short-context mid-training.

## Method

PRISM is a retention-aware empirical framework that decomposes mid-training into its principal design axes and evaluates each across multiple model families. The core training procedure uses a balanced data mixture—general web data to preserve pre-training knowledge, domain-specific reasoning datasets to induce targeted gains, and a small amount of instruction/chat data to stabilize downstream RL—sampled at fixed per-source weights validated across Granite, LLaMA, and Mistral families. The key finding is that a relatively small ~27B-token mid-training phase acts as a critical stabilizing stage: it yields ~6–10 point gains on coding and ~17–30 points on math benchmarks while keeping general leaderboard scores stable, and it provides a substantially stronger initialization for GRPO-based RL than the base model, enabling stable monotonic improvements of an additional ~3–8 points. Domain mixtures interact non-trivially (Math+Code produces synergy; Science adds GPQA gains without sacrificing other domains), and the beneficial patterns generalize across dense, hybrid (Attention-Mamba), and MoE architectures, though RL on hybrid models remains unstable. Long-context degradation from 8k mid-training is addressed via linear merging with the base model followed by a short extension phase, recovering most RULER scores while preserving downstream reasoning gains.

## Results

Across six model families (Granite-4 Micro/Hybrid 3B/7B, Mistral-7B/24B, LLaMA-3.1-8B), PRISM mid-training with ~27B tokens yields Math Avg gains of +15 to +40 points (e.g., Mistral-7B: 0.59→41.28; Granite-4-H Tiny 7B: 15.79→53.19) and Code Avg gains of +5 to +11 points (e.g., Granite-4-H Micro: 0.74→11.78). Applying GRPO-based RL on top of PRISM adds a further +3 to +12 points on the composite AVG (e.g., Granite-3.3-8B: 29.48→40.16; Mistral-24B: 25.05→35.14), with the largest absolute RL gains on GPQA-Diamond (+18 to +28 points). General-purpose leaderboard scores (LB-V1/V2) remain largely stable, confirming retention of broad capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against other mid-training methods (e.g., OLMo 2/3, OctoThinker) under a controlled protocol; instead, the primary comparison is each model's base checkpoint versus its PRISM-mid-trained and PRISM+RL variants. Mid-training is the dominant source of gains: for Granite-3.3-8B, the composite AVG jumps from 11.19 (base) to 29.48 (MCS mid-train, +18.29), while RL adds a further +10.68 to reach 40.16. For Mistral-24B, mid-training lifts AVG from 9.20 to 25.05 (+15.85) and RL pushes it to 35.14 (+10.09). RL applied directly to the base model (without PRISM) underperforms RL-on-PRISM by 39.66 points on math, 14.01 on code, and 21.72 on science, confirming that mid-training is a necessary prerequisite for effective RL scaling. General-purpose leaderboards show modest regressions (LB-V1 drops ~1–5 points for most models) but LB-V2 is stable or improves, indicating that domain gains do not come at the cost of broader reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the emerging mid-training literature—OLMo's annealing/domain-knowledge framing, OctoThinker's mid-training-as-RL-bridge, and Phi-3's long-context extension—but departs from their narrow, single-domain focus by treating mid-training as a multi-axis design problem (data mixture, retention, long-context, RL compatibility) studied simultaneously across model families. It also extends the small-scale controlled studies of Zhang et al. (2025) and Liu et al. (2025) to 3B–24B dense and hybrid (Mamba/MoE) architectures, and adds a systematic RL-on-top-of-mid-training comparison that prior work largely omitted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRISM provides the first retention-aware, multi-domain empirical recipe for mid-training, showing that ~27B tokens of balanced web+reasoning data is a sufficient and near-saturating budget, that Math+Code mixtures produce cross-domain synergy, and that mid-training is a practically necessary precondition for stable, monotonic RL gains (RL on base models underperforms by 14–40 points). Open questions include why RL collapses on hybrid Mamba/MoE architectures, whether longer RL schedules continue to improve performance, and whether the observed patterns hold beyond the tested 3B–24B scale and the specific data sources used.
