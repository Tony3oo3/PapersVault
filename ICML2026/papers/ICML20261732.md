## Problem

While unimodal in-context learning (ICL) has been mechanistically characterized in simplified attention-only transformers, it remains unclear how these principles transfer to modern LLM-style architectures (with RoPE, RMSNorm, SiLU) and how they extend to the multimodal setting. The paper asks: what training data statistics and attention circuits enable transformers to perform multimodal ICL, and how do the roles of the two modalities differ?

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved sequences of N paired exemplars from two modalities (e.g., a text token x_i and an image token x_i') each followed by a shared label ℓ_i, terminated by an unlabelled query pair (x_q, x_q'). Synthetic data is drawn from Gaussian Mixture Models with independently controlled within-class variation (ε), burstiness (B), and Zipfian class-frequency skew (α) per modality. Evaluation explicitly separates in-weight learning (query class seen in training but absent from context) from in-context learning (novel class present in context), with an additional label-swap condition that permutes context labels to invalidate memorized mappings.

## Method

The paper trains small two-layer transformer decoders (RMSNorm, SiLU, RoPE) via a two-stage curriculum: first pretraining on a high-diversity primary modality M1 (K₁=8192 classes), then introducing a secondary modality M2 through an MLP projector (optionally preceded by a pretrained ViT encoder) and jointly fine-tuning. The central empirical finding is a learning asymmetry: once M1 installs the induction circuit, surprisingly low data complexity in M2 (K₂=256) suffices for strong multimodal ICL, and scaling the decoder consistently improves multimodal ICL (unlike the unimodal case where scaling favors memorization). Mechanistically, the paper quantifies circuit formation via progress measurements (Previous-Token Head Strength, Induction Head Strength, Target Label Association, Context-Label Accuracy) and validates causality through targeted head knockout, showing that multimodal training primarily refines the label-matching induction head rather than rebuilding the circuit. These findings are validated at production scale on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, where the identified previous-token and induction heads overlap with the LLM backbone's, causally drive ICL under knockout, and sharpen during LoRA fine-tuning exactly as in the synthetic setting.

## Results

In the synthetic multimodal testbed, knocking out the identified previous-token head drops ICL accuracy from 0.970 to 0.199, and knocking out the induction head drops it to 0.062, confirming causal necessity of both circuits. On the VL-ICL benchmark, decoder scaling consistently improves multimodal ICL: Qwen2.5-VL gains +2.33% average accuracy from 3B to 7B, and IDEFICS gains +10.52% from 9B to 80B. In Qwen2.5-VL-3B, ablating the top-5 induction heads at inference drops ICL accuracy from 0.74 to 0.58 (near the 0.50 random baseline), while a random-forest regressor trained on just two circuit metrics (PHStrength and IndStrength) predicts ICL accuracy with R²=0.90 in both unimodal and multimodal settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability paper rather than a method-proposal paper, so it does not compare a new model against competing baselines. Instead, it contrasts architectural and data configurations to isolate causal factors. RoPE consistently underperforms absolute positional encodings (APE) on ICL accuracy across all data-complexity regimes, with the gap narrowing only at very high data complexity. In the unimodal setting, scaling up (more layers or heads) shifts the model toward in-weight memorization and raises the data-complexity threshold for ICL; in the multimodal setting, the opposite holds—larger decoders achieve strong ICL with lower data complexity because added capacity is used to map the secondary modality onto the pre-existing circuit. Modality zeroing ablations confirm genuine cross-modal integration: removing M2 drops accuracy from 0.967 to 0.336, while removing M1 causes a catastrophic drop to 0.063, showing the circuit logic is anchored in the primary modality's embedding space. On production models, 4 of Qwen2.5-VL-3B's top-5 previous-token heads appear in the text-only backbone's top-10, confirming the induction circuits are inherited from the LLM rather than constructed during multimodal training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic ICL literature from simplified attention-only transformers (Reddy, 2024; Olsson et al., 2022) to modern LLM-style architectures incorporating RMSNorm, SiLU, and RoPE, and further generalizes from unimodal to multimodal settings. It builds on the data-distributional framework of Chan et al. (2022) — burstiness, Zipfian skew, within-class variation — as the controlled experimental substrate, but departs from prior work by introducing a two-stage pretraining curriculum (unimodal M1 → multimodal M2 via projector) that reveals a modality hierarchy absent in joint-training analyses. Unlike prior MLLM interpretability work that treats multimodal ICL as a black-box capability of large pretrained models, this paper studies multimodal ICL as a transformer learning phenomenon in its own right, using small architecturally realistic models for clean causal attribution before validating on Qwen2.5-VL and IDEFICS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that a high-diversity primary modality 'installs' the ICL circuit such that the secondary modality needs surprisingly little data complexity to achieve strong multimodal ICL has direct practical implications for MLLM training data design and curriculum scheduling. The finding that RoPE — the dominant positional encoding in modern LLMs — systematically suppresses induction circuit formation raises an open question about whether current architectural choices are suboptimal for in-context reasoning, and whether the gap can be closed purely through data complexity. Limitations include the reliance on small synthetic GMM testbeds that likely understate real-world cross-modal alignment difficulty, and the fact that production-scale validation (Qwen2.5-VL, IDEFICS) is qualitative rather than a full mechanistic replication. Open questions include whether the asymmetry persists with more than two modalities, whether alternative positional encodings can restore ICL without sacrificing length generalization, and whether the circuit-refinement account generalizes to autoregressive generation tasks beyond classification.
