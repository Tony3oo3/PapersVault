## Problem

Structured dilated attention offers an attractive inference-time efficiency knob—reducing attention FLOPs and KV cache by a factor of the dilation size D while preserving long-range connectivity—but directly sparsifying a pretrained dense attention model into a dilated pattern causes severe accuracy degradation. Prior efficient architectures (e.g., RAT, Longformer, BigBird) require training each sparse configuration from scratch, making them inflexible across different efficiency-accuracy trade-offs. The paper asks whether a single dense model can be designed to support flexible dilated inference at test time without retraining.

## Input / Output

The model consumes text token sequences (context length 4096, extendable to 16384 via NTK RoPE scaling) and produces next-token predictions. At inference time, the same pretrained checkpoint can be reconfigured to any dilation size D ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128}, optionally combined with local windows (e.g., W=256) or hybrid layer/head compositions, after a short 1B-token resolution adaptation. The recurrence state is a fixed-size vector per head (O(1) KV cache) that is updated via a single step during decoding or a parallel associative scan during prefilling.

## Method

RAT+ augments standard attention with a full-sequence recurrence (L=T) applied to attention keys and values via input-dependent forget gates before the scaled dot-product, providing a complete receptive field that bridges the disconnected attention graph of dilated patterns. Two key techniques enable flexible sparse inference: (1) full-sequence recurrence with overlapped chunks ensures consistent recurrence output distributions across all dilation settings, avoiding the covariate shift that plagues chunk-size-matched designs; and (2) active recurrence learning (ARL) jointly trains each batch with both a sparse configuration (L=64, D=64) and a dense configuration (L=64, D=1), forcing the recurrence to acquire the required effective length L*=64 rather than collapsing to a shorter, easier-to-learn range. After dense pretraining, a 1B-token resolution adaptation (reduced learning rate, no warmup) calibrates the model to any target dilation, after which the model achieves near-dense perplexity at D=16 and only ~2-point accuracy drops at D=64 on commonsense reasoning and LongBench tasks.

## Results

At 1.5B parameters (100B tokens), RAT+ at D=16 achieves 58.06 average commonsense reasoning accuracy (vs. 59.21 dense), and at D=64 drops to 57.46 (~2-point loss) while reducing temporal-mixing FLOPs by 64×. Validation PPL remains stable across dilation: 7.40 at D=1 to 7.66 at D=64, compared to 129.3 for standard attention sparsified to D†=64. At 7.6B scale, the dense-to-sparse gap narrows further: D=64 incurs only ~1-point average accuracy loss (63.1 vs. 64.4 commonsense) while achieving over 40× higher maximum decoding throughput. On LongBench, D=16,W=256 achieves 18.67 average (vs. 19.37 dense), and on NIAH-MK-2 at 7.6B, D=32 reaches 94.6% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAT+ at D=16 (58.06 avg) closely matches dense attention (58.33) and substantially outperforms Mamba2 (56.47) and GatedDeltaNet (56.95) on commonsense reasoning, while also exceeding RAT (56.99) by ~1 point at the same dilation. Critically, directly sparsifying standard attention to D†=16 collapses to 32.60 average, demonstrating that the recurrence is essential for dilated inference. On LongBench, RAT+ D=16,W=256 (18.67) outperforms StreamingLLM W=1024 (17.01), Mamba2 (14.75), and GatedDeltaNet (16.94). For top-k block attention on NIAH-MK-2 (D=64,K=16), RAT+ achieves 93.8% vs. 63.2% for standard attention with Quest, and 80.2% vs. 66.2% for MoBA, confirming that recurrence improves block scoring. At 7.6B scale, RAT+ (100B tokens) matches Griffin (300B tokens) on ARC-C (47.3 vs. 47.9) and substantially outperforms Pythia (100B tokens) across all commonsense tasks (e.g., 47.3 vs. 29.3 on ARC-C). On NIAH retrieval, RAT+ D=4 (97.4 on MK-2) far exceeds SnapKV (41.2) and StreamingLLM (26.8), while also reducing KV cache storage unlike top-k block methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends RAT (Wei et al., 2025), which combines chunk-based recurrence with dilated attention, by reformulating it as a dense-pretraining architecture that supports flexible sparse inference. It departs from both the training-from-scratch sparse architecture line (Longformer, BigBird, NSA, RAT) and the inference-time sparsification line (StreamingLLM, Quest, MoBA, SnapKV) by proposing a 'train dense, infer sparse' paradigm: a single model pretrained with full-sequence recurrence and active recurrence learning can be switched at inference to various dilation sizes, local windows, or hybrid layer/head compositions with only 1B tokens of adaptation. The recurrence mechanism is conceptually related to SSMs (Mamba2, GatedDeltaNet) but is used hierarchically as a complement to attention rather than a replacement, and the dilated attention component connects to structured sparse attention patterns while adding the critical recurrence bridge for complete receptive fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'train once, infer flexibly' paradigm addresses a practical pain point: prior efficient architectures require separate training for each configuration (dilation size, KV head count, state size), making it costly to satisfy different downstream efficiency-accuracy trade-offs. The finding that standard attention models catastrophically fail under dilated sparsification (PPL >100 at D=64) while recurrence-augmented models maintain near-dense performance (PPL ~7.66) provides a clear mechanistic explanation and a lightweight fix. Open questions include identifying optimal hybrid sparse configurations (the search space is ~10^20), developing dedicated CUDA kernels to close the prefilling efficiency gap, and whether the approach extends to tokenizer-free byte-level settings or parallel sampling for reasoning. The narrowing dense-sparse gap with scale (0.035 → 0.022 loss gap from 1.3B to 7.6B) suggests the approach may become even more attractive at frontier model sizes.
