## Problem

It remains unclear whether LLM free-text explanations (chain-of-thought, post-hoc rationales) contain enough information to actually explain the model's output-generating process. Existing evaluations test only for specific predefined biases via targeted perturbations and do not provide a general sufficiency metric. The paper further shows that sufficiency is inherently relative to the input distribution, which must be explicitly defined for LLMs but has no natural analogue to the training distribution in supervised learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text input x, an LLM F, and three prompts (task instruction T, explanation prompt E, alteration prompt A) and produces a SCSUFF score in [0,1] per input, where 1 indicates self-consistent sufficiency. The score is computed entirely from the LLM's log-likelihoods: it compares the model's output distribution on the original input against the expected output distribution over model-generated alternative inputs conditioned on the explanation, using 5 alternatives for dataset-level and 70 for sample-level estimation. Dataset-level SCSUFF is the mean across individual sample scores.

## Method

The paper generalizes classical feature-attribution sufficiency to arbitrary free-text explanations and proves (Theorem 3.2) that sufficiency can change with the input distribution unless the model's output is constant given the explanation. To handle the absence of a natural input distribution in LLM settings, the authors introduce self-consistent sufficiency: the same LLM generates alternative inputs conditioned on its own explanation, defining a model-induced input distribution that captures the model's beliefs about plausible alternatives. The SCSUFF metric is a normalized KL divergence between F(y|x) and E_{e|x}E_{x'|e}[F(y|x')], expressed as a difference of negative log-likelihoods that is dataset- and model-agnostic. Experiments across 9 LLMs and 4 datasets show SCSUFF agrees with targeted perturbation tests where applicable, that sufficiency varies substantially with the induced input distribution, and that top/bottom SCSUFF scores are predictable from final-token hidden states.

## Results

Across 9 LLMs and 4 datasets, SCSUFF scores are generally low, indicating that LLM free-text explanations are insufficient. On MMLU+authority, SCSUFF ranges from 0.020 (Qwen3-14B) to 0.587 (Llama3.2-1B); on MMLU+reorder, from 0.020 to 0.647; on IMDB, from 0.599 to 0.892; and on BBQ, from 0.090 to 0.756. SCSUFF shows no correlation with task accuracy (Spearman's ρ=0.07) and only weak correlations with model size (ρ=−0.23) and output entropy (ρ=0.24). Under a contradictory few-shot input distribution, SCSUFF drops near zero for Qwen and Llama on IMDB, confirming that sufficiency is relative to the input distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to CSE (Madsen et al., 2024), SCSUFF and CSE rankings diverge substantially: Llama ranks highest in CSE on BBQ and MMLU+authority yet scores lowest on SCSUFF for those datasets, and Qwen/Llama have significantly lower SCSUFF than Ministral on MMLU+reorder despite comparable CSE scores. Against targeted perturbation metrics (Turpin et al., 2023), SCSUFF is consistent on MMLU+authority (both indicate insufficient explanations with SCSUFF ≤ 0.5 and near-zero hint mention), but on MMLU+reorder where targeted metrics are uninformative (accuracy unchanged), SCSUFF still remains below 0.5, revealing additional insufficiency beyond the targeted hint. Against the faithfulness metric of Chen et al. (2025), Mann-Whitney U tests confirm that explanations labeled faithful receive higher SCSUFF (p < 0.05 for 5 of 6 model-dataset pairings), yet SCSUFF remains below 0.25 for both faithful and unfaithful groups, showing that even 'faithful' explanations omit important input aspects. Overall, SCSUFF provides a more general and informative signal than any single targeted metric, though it does not strictly dominate them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the classical sufficiency definition from feature attributions (Yu et al., 2019; Yoon et al., 2018) to arbitrary free-text explanations, addressing the key gap that LLM explanations are variable-length token sequences rather than binary masks over fixed dimensions. It departs from targeted perturbation-based evaluations (Turpin et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2025) and counterfactual self-explanation (Madsen et al., 2024) by proposing a distribution-agnostic, information-theoretic metric (SCSUFF) that does not require pre-specifying which input features matter. The theoretical contribution (Theorems 3.2–3.3) characterizes precisely when sufficiency is input-distribution-dependent, motivating the self-consistent formulation where the LLM itself induces the alternative-input distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LLM explanations are generally insufficient even in the most favorable self-consistent setting, with no meaningful correlation to model size or task accuracy, challenges the assumption that scaling or improving performance will yield trustworthy explanations. The demonstration that sufficiency can collapse to near-zero under a simple contradiction few-shot prompt underscores that evaluation conclusions are only as valid as the implicit input distribution. Open questions include separating model-decodable from human-understandable information in explanations, and whether the hidden-state signal identified here can be leveraged for direct optimization of explanation sufficiency during training.
