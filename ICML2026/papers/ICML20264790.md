## Problem

Zero-shot 3D question answering with 2D VLMs requires selecting a small subset of views from a 3D scene under a tight token budget, but existing key-view selection methods rely solely on query-view semantic similarity, ignoring spatial relationships and surrounding context. Moreover, the selected views contain substantial token-level redundancy across overlapping regions, wasting the limited input capacity that could otherwise accommodate additional informative views. No prior work provides a principled, tuning-free mechanism to jointly select spatially consistent views and compress redundant tokens while preserving diverse, representative 3D evidence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of 2D views sampled from a 3D point cloud (each with camera position and orientation), a text question, and a fixed token budget S that is much smaller than the total number of view tokens. It is plug-and-play across VLM backbones (LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-Video) and requires no task-specific training. For datasets lacking explicit camera parameters (e.g., VSI-Bench), camera poses are estimated via FastVGGT. The output is a free-form text answer generated by the VLM from the selected key tokens and question.

## Method

KeyVT is a hierarchical, tuning-free framework with two stages. KeyV performs geometry-aware key-view selection: it computes a combined positional-orientational distance from a reference view to each view, segments views into spatially consistent sub-scenes via a sliding window, scores each sub-scene's relevance using BLIP2 (combining max and mean query-view similarity), and adaptively allocates key views proportional to relevance scaled by the square root of sub-scene size. KeyT then compresses redundant tokens across the selected views using optimal transport: view tokens and a smaller set of learnable virtual tokens are treated as two discrete distributions in embedding space, and the Sinkhorn distance (entropy-regularized OT with cosine-distance cost) is minimized via a few Adam iterations (10–15 steps, lr=1e-2) to learn the virtual tokens. Each virtual token is grounded by selecting its top-S/M nearest real patch tokens from the learned transport plan, yielding a compact set of diverse, representative tokens that the VLM can process directly.

## Results

On ScanQA (val) with LLaVA-Video-7B, KeyVT achieves CIDEr 100.7 and EM-1 57.9 using only 8 equivalent input frames (compressed from 16), outperforming AKS (99.0/57.3), DivPrune (99.1/55.8), and FLoC (99.4/57.2). On SQA3D, KeyVT reaches EM-1 57.9, matching the training-based Video-3D model (57.8) that uses 16 frames. On VSI-Bench, KeyVT achieves the best average score across all three VLM backbones (33.9, 34.6, and 37.0 for LLaVA-OV, LLaVA-Video, and Qwen2.5-VL respectively), surpassing all tuning-free baselines including See&Trek.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KeyVT consistently outperforms all tuning-free baselines (AKS, DivPrune, FLoC) across all three VLM backbones on both CIDEr and EM-1 for ScanQA, with the largest margin over DivPrune (e.g., +1.6 CIDEr and +2.1 EM-1 on LLaVA-OV-7B). Against the training-based Video-3D model, KeyVT with 8 equivalent frames matches or slightly exceeds Video-3D with 16 frames (100.7 vs. 100.6 CIDEr on LLaVA-Video-7B), demonstrating that the hierarchical view-to-token strategy compensates for the lack of task-specific training. On VSI-Bench, KeyVT achieves the best average score for every backbone, beating See&Trek (which also injects spatial cues) by 0.9–1.0 points on average. Compared to 3D-LLM baselines (3D-LLM, LL3DA, Chat-Scene, 3D-LLaVA), 2D VLMs with KeyVT surpass them by a large margin (e.g., 100.7 vs. 92.6 CIDEr), confirming the advantage of leveraging powerful pretrained 2D VLMs over 3D-specific architectures. KeyVT also outperforms the keyframe-selection method AKS on all datasets, with the gap widening on VSI-Bench (34.6 vs. 33.2 for LLaVA-Video-7B), suggesting that geometry-aware spatial partitioning is more effective than temporal-continuity-based selection for 3D scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

KeyVT sits at the intersection of two established research threads: key-view selection for 3D scene understanding (AKS, BOLT, semantic-similarity retrieval) and visual token compression for long video/multimodal inputs (DivPrune, FLoC, clustering-based pruning). It departs from prior view-selection work by incorporating camera geometry (position and orientation) to partition the scene into spatially consistent sub-scenes before allocating views, rather than relying solely on query-view semantic similarity. For token compression, it replaces the common clustering or diversity-maximization formulations with an entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn) objective that learns virtual tokens as a compact distribution covering the view-token distribution, a formulation borrowed from OT theory (Cuturi, 2013) but applied here for the first time in the 3D-QA context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KeyVT offers a practical, tuning-free, plug-and-play pipeline that matches or exceeds training-based 3D-QA methods (cdViews, Video-3D) without any task-specific fine-tuning, lowering the barrier to deploying 3D scene understanding with off-the-shelf 2D VLMs. The hierarchical view-then-token design is modular and could be combined with future stronger VLM backbones. Limitations include sensitivity to dense clutter and ambiguous spatial relations (acknowledged in failure analysis), dependence on camera parameters (mitigated but not eliminated by FastVGGT estimation), and evaluation limited to indoor ScanNet-based scenes and egocentric video. Open questions include whether the OT-based compression generalizes to outdoor or very large-scale scenes, and whether the lightweight 10–15 iteration virtual-token update remains sufficient as VLM token budgets grow.
