## Problem

Decision-focused learning (DFL) methods for predict-then-optimize align prediction training with downstream decision quality, but every gradient evaluation requires a call to the optimization solver, making them computationally prohibitive for large-scale or combinatorial problems. The paper asks whether one can design a training objective that captures the geometry of the downstream optimization without any solver calls during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264575/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a contextual feature vector x ∈ R^p (tabular) and produces a predicted cost vector ŷ ∈ R^d, which is plugged into a downstream linear program min_{w∈S} ŷ^T w over a known feasible region S (polyhedral, discrete, or strongly convex). The loss is computed against the realized cost vector y, and the key structural detail is that the effective target is the normalized direction y/||y|| on the unit sphere, weighted by the magnitude ||y||, rather than y itself. This means the predictor is trained to recover the direction of E[Y|x] (up to a positive scalar) rather than its full magnitude.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-step measure transformation of the joint distribution P over (x, y): (1) reweight by the Radon-Nikodym derivative ||y||/E[||Y||] to capture the loss-scaling property of the decision loss, and (2) project the cost vector onto the unit sphere to capture scale invariance of the optimization oracle. This yields the Weight-Integrated Spherical Error (WISE) loss ℓ_WISE(ŷ, y) = ||y||·||ŷ − y/||y||||², which is exactly the ℓ₂ risk under the transformed measure Q and can be minimized by standard first-order methods with no solver calls. The Bayes risk minimizer is E[Y|x]/E[||Y|||x], a positive scalar multiple of the conditional expectation, which by scale invariance of w* is Fisher consistent for the decision risk. The paper proves O(n^{-1/2}) excess regret bounds under a VC-linear-subgraph complexity assumption and improves to O(n^{-1}) when the feasible region is a strongly convex level set.

## Results

On the shortest path problem (5×5 grid, d=40 edges), WISE achieves normalized test regret of 15.62% (degree=1, n=1600) and 10.83% (degree=2, n=400), matching or slightly beating MSE while being 4–100× faster than solver-based DFL methods (SPO+, PFY, DBB). On the 2D knapsack problem (d=16 items), WISE achieves 11.05% regret (degree=1, n=400) and 7.63% (degree=2, n=400), outperforming MSE (11.71%, 8.23%), SPO+ (11.64%, 8.05%), and DBB (15.25%, 12.28%) in most settings. Training time for WISE is 1.5–12 s versus 130–987 s for SPO+, 173–1379 s for PFY, and 257–2070 s for DBB on the shortest path task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WISE matches MSE (decision-blind baseline) on regret within 0.1–0.5 percentage points across all settings, confirming that the measure transformation preserves prediction quality while adding decision awareness. Against SPO+ (Elmachtoub & Grigas, 2022), WISE achieves comparable or slightly better regret (e.g., 15.62% vs 15.63% at degree=1, n=1600) while being ~83× faster (11.86 s vs 986.79 s). PFY (Berthet et al., 2020) is consistently outperformed on regret (e.g., 15.62% vs 15.82% at degree=1, n=1600) and is ~116× slower. DBB (Pogancić et al., 2019) is the weakest DFL baseline, with regret 3–11 percentage points worse than WISE and ~175× slower training. LAVA (Berden et al., 2026) is the strongest competitor: it sometimes achieves lower regret (e.g., 16.72% vs 15.62% at degree=1, n=1600) but requires observed past optimal decisions, whereas WISE operates purely from cost vectors. On the 2D knapsack (Table 1), WISE outperforms CAVE (Tang & Khalil, 2024) in most settings (e.g., 11.05% vs 19.54% at degree=1, n=400), though LAVA remains slightly better at higher sample sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the predict-then-optimize framework by introducing a measure transformation (reweighting by cost magnitude followed by spherical projection) that converts a standard L2 loss into a decision-aware surrogate (WISE) without requiring any optimization solver during training. It departs from the dominant DFL lineage (SPO+, PFY, DBB) where every gradient evaluation demands a solver call, and from recent solver-free methods (CAVE, LAVA) that still rely on observed optimal decisions or local geometric information. Theoretically, it builds on the VC-linear-subgraph dimension and calibration framework of Hu et al. (2022) and the strongly convex level set analysis of Liu & Grigas (2021), but the measure transformation principle itself is, to the authors' knowledge, new to the DFL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The solver-free property is practically significant: for large-scale mixed-integer programs where a single solver call dominates runtime, eliminating solver calls from the training loop (4–100× speedup demonstrated) makes DFL feasible on hardware previously out of reach. The O(n^{-1}) excess regret rate under strongly convex level sets improves upon the O(n^{-1/2}) SPO+ bound, offering a theoretical advantage in well-structured settings. However, the empirical gains over plain MSE are modest at higher misspecification degrees, and the method is restricted to linear prediction classes and linear optimization objectives; extending the measure transformation to non-linear predictors, non-linear cost functions, or stochastic/robust optimization settings remains open.
