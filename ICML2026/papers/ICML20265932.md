## Problem

Existing motion latent learning for robotic manipulation extracts patterns from 2D image sequences, missing the 3D geometric structure that fundamentally determines manipulation success. This representational gap causes failures in generalization across viewpoints, object poses, and spatial arrangements, particularly in cluttered or occluded scenes where geometric reasoning is essential. The paper asks whether motion latents grounded in 4D spatiotemporal geometry (3D point clouds evolving over time) can produce more robust and transferable manipulation policies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265932/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single front-view RGB-D image (downsampled to 256×256), a natural language instruction, and the robot's proprioceptive state (joint angles and end-effector pose) as input. The RGB-D observation is back-projected into a 3D pointmap using camera intrinsics and extrinsics, which serves as the geometric substrate for both latent learning and action prediction. The output is a chunk of h timesteps of 6-DoF end-effector poses (3D position, 6D rotation representation, and binary gripper state), executed via a motion planner (BiRRT in simulation, MoveIt in real-world). Notably, GeoMoLa requires only this single view, whereas baselines like GNFactor and ManiGaussian need 19 additional camera views for 3D reconstruction.

## Method

GeoMoLa operates in two stages. First, a self-supervised pipeline learns discrete motion latents: a MiniGPT-4-based vision-language encoder produces a continuous representation from the RGB-D observation and instruction, which is discretized via NSVQ into a sequence of codes from a learned codebook. These codes are trained by a conditional diffusion model (adapted from Stable Video Diffusion) to predict future 3D pointmaps and RGB images, forcing the latents to encode causal geometric transformations rather than appearance. Second, a 3D denoising transformer (based on 3D Diffuser Actor) generates action chunks via DDPM, conditioned on the learned motion latent tokens, scene tokens (CLIP-ResNet50 features lifted to 3D via depth), trajectory tokens, and proprioception tokens, using rotary 3D positional encodings and cross-attention to the motion latents. The key architectural insight is that the 4D prediction objective (spatial geometry changing through time) is what drives performance: ablations show removing pointmap prediction degrades success by ~5% while removing RGB prediction has minimal impact, confirming that geometric prediction—not visual reconstruction—is the critical signal for learning transferable motion primitives.

## Results

On RLBench (10 language-conditioned manipulation tasks, single-view RGB-D), GeoMoLa achieves 84.7% average success rate, ranking first on 8 out of 10 tasks. On CALVIN (zero-shot generalization, ABC→D), GeoMoLa achieves the longest average task chain of 3.60 (vs. 3.53 for the next-best Clover). In real-world ALOHA experiments with only 20 demonstrations per task, GeoMoLa reaches 53.3% average success rate, a 13.3-point improvement over 3D Diffuser Actor (40.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoMoLa outperforms all 10 baselines across both simulation benchmarks and real-world evaluation. On RLBench, the largest gap is over GNFactor (31.7%) and ManiGaussian (44.8%), which rely on multi-view 3D reconstruction (19 additional views) and dynamic Gaussian Splatting respectively; GeoMoLa surpasses them by 53 and 40 points using only single-view input. Among the strongest 3D baselines, GeoMoLa beats RVT2 (80.4%) by 4.3 points and 3D Diffuser Actor (77.0%) by 7.7 points, with the most notable per-task gain on 'Stack blocks' (54.2% vs. 34.8% for RVT2 and 4.0% for 3D Diffuser Actor). On CALVIN, GeoMoLa (3.60 avg length) edges out Clover (3.53) by 0.07 and 3D Diffuser Actor (3.35) by 0.25, while dramatically outperforming DP3 (0.31) and SuSIE (2.69). In real-world ALOHA experiments, GeoMoLa (53.3%) surpasses 3D Diffuser Actor (40.0%) by 13.3 points and SkillDiffuser (21.7%) by 31.6 points, with the largest relative gain on the occlusion-heavy 'Clean cup' task (60% vs. 50% and 30% respectively). The ablation further confirms that the pointmap (3D geometry) prediction branch is the critical component: removing it drops RLBench performance by 5.1% and CALVIN avg. length by 0.22, while removing the RGB branch has minimal impact (−2.7% and −0.10 respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoMoLa sits at the intersection of three research threads: motion latent learning (GENIE, LAPA, Moto, SkillDiffuser), 3D scene-to-action policies (Act3D, 3D Diffuser Actor, RVT), and 4D dynamic scene modeling (ManiGaussian). It departs from prior motion latent methods by replacing 2D video-based pretraining with self-supervised prediction of future 3D pointmap evolution, and from static 3D diffusion policies by explicitly encoding temporal geometric change into discrete latent codes. The architecture reuses components from Stable Video Diffusion (latent VAE + DDPM) and VQ-VAE/NSVQ, but repurposes them for a 4D geometric prediction objective rather than visual reconstruction, and conditions a 3D denoising transformer (from 3D Diffuser Actor) on the learned discrete codes rather than raw language or pixel features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ablation evidence that geometric prediction is the critical signal while visual appearance contributes minimally offers a concrete, testable principle for representation learning in manipulation: encode how objects move through 3D space, not how they look. This could redirect future work away from ever-larger visual backbones toward geometry-grounded objectives. Limitations include rigid-body-only manipulation, single-view input, and tabletop-scale tasks; extending to deformable objects, multi-view or egocentric settings, and hierarchical composition of learned primitives remain open. The real-world results with only 20 demonstrations per task suggest the approach may reduce data requirements, but the absolute success rates (53.3% average) indicate substantial room for improvement in deployment.
