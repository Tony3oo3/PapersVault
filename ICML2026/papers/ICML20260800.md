## Problem

Transformer-based offline RL methods for auto-bidding (e.g., Decision Transformer) suffer from two failure modes: (1) unimodal regression heads collapse multiple valid bidding strategies (aggressive vs. conservative) into suboptimal averaged actions, and (2) purely parametric policies generate unreliable bids under sparse or long-tail traffic where high-quality historical decisions exist but are not explicitly retained. These issues are particularly acute in real-world auction environments with strict budget and CPA constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 16-dimensional state vectors (campaign-level features like remaining budget and historical bid means, plus market-level features like least-winning-cost and p-value aggregates), a scalar return-to-go, and a trajectory prefix (20-step context for the policy, 48-step for the retrieval encoder). At inference, it produces a single scalar bid adjustment factor λ (range [0, 493] dense / [0, 589] sparse) that scales predicted impression values into final bids. The output is not directly emitted by the policy but selected from a unified candidate pool of L GMM-sampled actions and K RTG-filtered retrieved actions via critic ranking.

## Method

DRIVE decouples candidate generation from decision-making through three synergistic components. First, a Gaussian Mixture Model head (M components) replaces the standard MSE regression head, trained via log-likelihood to capture multimodal bidding distributions and sample L diverse candidates at inference. Second, a retrieval module encodes all offline transitions into contextual embeddings via a lightweight Transformer, indexes them with FAISS/HNSW, and at query time retrieves K nearest neighbors filtered by highest stored RTG to provide non-parametric anchors from high-quality history. Third, an IQL-based critic (twin Q-networks + expectile-regressed V-network, with constraint-aware reward shaping for CPA tasks) evaluates the union of generated and retrieved candidates and selects the argmax action. The framework is plug-and-play, integrating into DT, CDT, PDiT, and BC backbones with consistent gains, and achieves 46 ms inference latency suitable for real-time bidding.

## Results

On AuctionNet (dense), DRIVE achieves an average total reward of 386.6 across five budget levels (50%–150%), outperforming the strongest baselines CQL (378.4) and GAS (371.6). On the challenging AuctionNet-Sparse variant, DRIVE scores 36.08, matching CQL (36.06) while substantially exceeding DT (30.62) and GAVE (9.66). On D4RL, DRIVE attains the best Gym-MuJoCo average (79.7) and Maze2D average (92.5), surpassing IQL (77.0 / 46.2) and PDiT (76.3 / 54.8) respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRIVE outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary AuctionNet benchmark, with the largest margins over GAVE (+143.0 avg) and GAVE-S (+176.2 avg), which suffer severe degradation under multimodal bidding landscapes. Against the strongest classical offline RL baselines, DRIVE edges out CQL by +8.2 on AuctionNet and matches it on AuctionNet-Sparse (36.08 vs 36.06), while beating IQL by +29.0 and BCQ by +50.8 on the dense set. On D4RL, DRIVE achieves the best Gym-MuJoCo average (79.7) over IQL (77.0), TD3+BC (75.3), and PDiT (76.3), and dominates Maze2D with 92.5 versus PDiT (54.8), CQL (62.0), and DT (40.9). The diffusion-based DiffBid lags substantially behind DRIVE on AuctionNet (275.2 vs 386.6), confirming the paper's argument that iterative diffusion sampling is both slower and less effective for long-horizon bidding. Notably, DRIVE's advantage is most pronounced in sparse/long-tail regimes (AuctionNet-Sparse, medium-replay D4RL tasks), where retrieval-augmented anchoring provides the largest relative gains over purely parametric methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRIVE extends the Decision Transformer lineage for offline RL by replacing the standard unimodal MSE action head with a Gaussian Mixture Model (following the Mixture Density Network paradigm), and by borrowing the retrieval-augmented generation idea from NLP (Lewis et al., 2020; RA-DT, DT-Mem) to ground decisions in high-quality historical transitions. The value-based selection stage adopts IQL's expectile-regression critic, and the overall architecture is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play module that can be grafted onto any DT-style backbone (DT, CDT, PDiT, BC). Conceptually, the paper departs from prior DT-style bidding work (GAS, GAVE, DiffBid) by decoupling candidate generation from decision making, rather than relying on a single deterministic or iteratively-sampled output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quantified 'Average Action' analysis (17.6% of states multimodal, with a 54.7% suboptimal rate versus 38.2% for unimodal states) provides concrete evidence that mode collapse is a systematic, not anecdotal, failure in offline bidding, and the plug-and-play validation across four backbones and two benchmark suites (AuctionNet, D4RL) strengthens the claim that the fix is general. Practical deployment is supported by a 46 ms inference latency and a 20× speed advantage of GMM over diffusion heads, though the 28.9 GB peak memory (driven largely by the FAISS index) remains a consideration for resource-constrained settings. Open questions include how the retrieval index degrades as the offline dataset grows or drifts over time, whether the GMM component count M can be adapted per-state, and whether the framework extends naturally to online or semi-online bidding where the logged distribution shifts.
