## Problem

Diffusion LLM inference wastes compute on redundant denoising steps for tokens whose final values are already determined, yet existing acceleration methods rely on step-local confidence thresholds or fixed schedules that are brittle across prompts, domains, and token positions. The paper identifies that token-wise denoising trajectories exhibit structured temporal and spatial patterns (delayed stabilization, oscillation, underconfident-but-correct tokens) that no single-step snapshot can capture, and that passive waiting misses forecastable acceleration opportunities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and an all-masked response span of length L (e.g., 256 or 1024 tokens), processing them through the base dLLM's bidirectional attention at each denoising step. At each step, the controller operates on lightweight per-token scalar features—top-1 confidence, predictive entropy, EMA momentum, and relative block position—rather than full vocabulary logits, and outputs a binary fix-or-continue decision per position. The final output is the fully decoded text sequence, with tokens progressively committed and excluded from subsequent remasking, terminating early once all positions are fixed.

## Method

TSPD is a lightweight 2-layer LSTM controller (~2K trainable parameters) that maintains a per-token hidden state across denoising steps, ingesting a 4-dimensional feature vector (confidence, entropy, momentum, relative position) at each step and outputting a hard binary fix decision via a sigmoid gate thresholded at 0.5. It is trained by oracle imitation: a token is labeled fixable once its current top-1 matches the final target, with a straight-through estimator aligning training with inference-time hard gating. Confidence Extrapolation (CE) is a training-free Kalman-filter module that models confidence evolution as a level-velocity state-space process in log-odds space, forecasting h-step-ahead confidence with quantified uncertainty. A risk-aware horizon rule selects the furthest safe look-ahead based on left-context coverage and a conservative lower bound on forecast confidence, enabling proactive early commitment without modifying the base model or the downstream controller's decision logic.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct with 256-token generation, TSPD+CE achieves 77.3 TPS (11.2× speedup) on GSM8K, 76.4 TPS (8.3×) on MATH, 53.3 TPS (8.2×) on HumanEval, and 68.4 TPS (11.8×) on MBPP with KV caching, while maintaining accuracy within 0.5% of vanilla decoding. Without KV cache, the method still delivers 5.0× speedup on GSM8K (34.2 TPS, 79.4% acc) and 5.6× on MBPP (32.5 TPS, 29.5% acc). At 1024-token generation, the speedup compounds to 58.3× (64.1 TPS) with 78.5% accuracy on GSM8K, versus 19.6× for Fast-dLLM which drops to 74.7% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSPD+CE outperforms all four baselines on throughput across every benchmark. Against Fast-dLLM and Credit Decoding (both with KV cache), the proposed method achieves 1.4–2.2× higher TPS (e.g., 77.3 vs. 53.8 on GSM8K; 53.3 vs. 24.1 on HumanEval) while matching or exceeding their accuracy. Against cache-free baselines Prophet and Learn2PD, the gap is even larger: 3.1× higher TPS on GSM8K (34.2 vs. 11.0) and 1.2× on MATH (36.8 vs. 29.4) without any caching. On HumanEval, Fast-dLLM and Credit Decoding report marginally higher accuracy (43.3%/43.7% vs. 43.0%), but at less than half the throughput; the proposed method's accuracy is within 0.7% of vanilla (41.5%). At longer generation lengths (1024 tokens), the advantage widens further: TSPD+CE reaches 58.3× speedup with 78.5% accuracy, while Fast-dLLM drops to 74.7% accuracy at only 19.6×, indicating that step-local heuristics degrade more severely as sequences grow.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the growing line of dLLM inference acceleration work (Fast-dLLM, Credit Decoding, Prophet, Learn2PD) but departs from the dominant step-local, threshold-based paradigm by reframing token fixing as a sequential control problem over per-token denoising trajectories. Conceptually, it repurposes ideas from classical state-space estimation (Kalman filtering) for confidence forecasting and from recurrent sequence modeling (LSTM) for trajectory-aware gating, neither of which had been applied to dLLM decoding control. The method is explicitly designed to be orthogonal to and composable with system-level optimizations like KV caching, distinguishing it from prior approaches that conflate algorithmic and system-level speedups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: near-lossless accuracy with 5–58× speedups across multiple dLLM backbones and generation lengths makes diffusion LLMs more viable for latency-sensitive deployment. The trajectory-aware framing (temporal + spatial) addresses a real brittleness in prior heuristics, and the training-free CE module offers a drop-in improvement for any existing controller. Open questions include whether the lightweight LSTM controller needs retraining when the base dLLM changes, how the approach behaves under sampling (vs. greedy) and at much longer generation lengths, and whether the state-space confidence model generalizes to non-masked or continuous-token diffusion formulations.
