## Problem

Theoretical analyses of regularized MDPs have been largely confined to standard (non-robust) RL, while existing robust MDP methods handle only transition uncertainty and rely on restrictive structural assumptions like the fail-state condition. No prior work provides finite-sample regret guarantees for online learning that simultaneously accounts for both transition and reward uncertainties under general f-divergences, nor does it accommodate the stochastic (soft) policies that arise naturally from policy regularization.

## Input / Output

The agent receives state observations and collects trajectories (state, action, reward, next state) through online interaction over K episodes, with a fixed initial state per episode. In the tabular setting, the state and action spaces are finite; in the linear setting, a known d-dimensional feature mapping φ(s,a) on the probability simplex is required. The output is a stochastic policy π(·|s) (a distribution over actions, not a single greedy action), along with robust value and Q-functions that incorporate both f-divergence-based transition penalties and policy regularization terms. Unlike prior robust RL methods that yield deterministic greedy policies, the doubly regularized framework produces softmax-type policies that are inherently more exploratory.

## Method

The paper introduces a doubly regularized MDP framework where the objective simultaneously penalizes transition deviations via an f-divergence (TV, KL, or χ²) with parameter β and penalizes the policy via a regularizer Ω^η (negative Shannon entropy, KL divergence, or negative Tsallis entropy). The key insight is that the policy regularization term is equivalent (via support function duality) to worst-case reward robustness, so the framework achieves 'double robustness' without explicitly modeling reward uncertainty. The algorithm RSPVI performs value iteration backward through the horizon, computing robust Q-functions via closed-form dual formulations of the f-divergence penalty plus an optimism bonus, then updating the policy by maximizing the regularized Q-value. The regret analysis relies on the bounded visitation measure ratio assumption (C_vr) from He et al. (2025) rather than the more restrictive fail-state condition, yielding regret bounds matching those of standard robust MDPs. For the linear extension, ridge regression estimates the reward and value parameters, and a discrete VAE learns the feature mapping φ to handle large state-action spaces in practice.

## Results

The paper evaluates RSPVI on three environments (Inverted Pendulum, Inverted Double Pendulum, and CartPole), measuring average cumulative reward over 50 episodes under increasing perturbation levels during evaluation. RSPVI consistently outperforms the non-robust SAC-based baseline as perturbation increases, with the gap widening at higher perturbation levels. In the CartPole linear setting, RSPVI exhibits significantly lower sensitivity to action perturbations compared to both the non-robust baseline and DR-LSVI-UCB. Theoretically, the paper establishes the first finite-sample regret bounds for doubly regularized MDPs, showing sample efficiency matching that of standard robust MDP algorithms (e.g., Õ(C_vr S^{3/2} AH² + C_vr^{1/2} SA^{1/2} H²√K) for the TV case in the tabular setting).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RSPVI against two baselines. First, against a non-robust SAC-based algorithm (without transition regularization), RSPVI outperforms the baseline across all three environments under perturbation, with the advantage growing as perturbation level increases. In the Inverted Pendulum and Inverted Double Pendulum (tabular setting), the robust algorithm maintains higher cumulative reward under both action randomization and actuator degradation/external disturbance. Second, in the CartPole linear setting, RSPVI is compared against DR-LSVI-UCB (Liu & Xu, 2024a); DR-LSVI-UCB fails to learn a robust policy without its exploration bonus, whereas RSPVI's soft (stochastic) policies provide inherent exploratory behavior that compensates for the omitted bonus term. The paper notes that existing robust RL algorithms (Lu et al., 2024; He et al., 2025) are excluded from empirical comparison because they rely on deterministic greedy policies and cannot accommodate continuous action spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads in robust RL: dynamics regularization (Yang et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2024; Tang et al. 2025) and policy regularization (Geist et al. 2019; Derman et al. 2021) into a single 'doubly regularized MDP' framework. It builds most directly on He et al. (2025), adopting their bounded visitation measure ratio assumption to handle the online setting without the restrictive fail-state condition, and on Liu & Xu (2024a) / Tang et al. (2025) for the linear function approximation structure. The key departure is that prior robust MDP algorithms produce deterministic greedy policies, whereas the doubly regularized formulation yields stochastic (softmax) optimal policies, enabling natural exploration and continuous action spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central theoretical result—that adding reward robustness via policy regularization does not increase the sample complexity of online robust learning—is clean and useful, showing the doubly regularized framework is 'free' in terms of statistical efficiency. Practically, the paper provides the first working algorithms (SAC-based for tabular, discrete-VAE + ridge regression for linear) that make distributionally robust MDPs applicable to large state-action spaces, which prior theory could not address. Open questions include whether the bounded visitation measure ratio assumption is satisfiable in realistic non-stationary environments, whether the discrete VAE approach to learning linear features scales beyond simple control tasks, and whether the omitted exploration bonus in the linear practical implementation degrades performance in harder settings.
