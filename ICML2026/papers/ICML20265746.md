## Problem

Vision-language models frequently produce hallucinated or overconfident outputs, yet existing uncertainty quantification methods either rely on token-level likelihoods that poorly correlate with semantic correctness or measure only generation-level diversity without accounting for input-level ambiguity. No prior work fuses both aleatoric (data-driven) and epistemic (model-driven) uncertainty in a principled Bayesian framework that yields a single calibrated, closed-form uncertainty score for VLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265746/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a text question, passing them through a frozen CLIP-ViT-L-336px encoder (768-d embeddings) and a frozen LLaVA-v1.5-13b backbone that generates n=50 stochastic responses via Monte Carlo Temperature sampling. The GP adapters (10-d latent space, 250 inducing points) are trained once on MS-COCO, Flickr30k, and VQAv2 training splits, while the VLM itself is never fine-tuned. The output is a single scalar fused uncertainty score u* that, when compared against a calibrated threshold θ*, yields an answer/abstain decision for selective prediction.

## Method

FUSE decomposes VLM uncertainty into two sources and fuses them via conjugate Bayesian inference. For aleatoric uncertainty, sparse variational GPLVM adapters map deterministic CLIP embeddings to probabilistic distributions over a shared 10-d latent space, producing a Gaussian prior u ~ N(m₀, σ₀²) where m₀ is a linear function of the log-determinant of the image and text embedding covariances. For epistemic uncertainty, n sampled response embeddings are whitened and their Gram matrix's log-determinant (semantic evidence Ṽ) is computed; a key theoretical result (Theorem 4.4) shows Ṽ is exactly linear in u with slope n, yielding a closed-form Gaussian likelihood Ṽ|u ~ N(a + nu, τ²) derived from Wishart/Bartlett decomposition moments. The conjugate Gaussian update produces a closed-form posterior u|Ṽ ~ N(m_post, σ_post²) in O(1) time, from which the final score u* = m_post + z_α·σ_post is extracted. Uncertainty weights based on per-token log-probabilities modulate each response's contribution to the scatter, with the effective degrees of freedom ν_eff capturing the reduction in independent evidence.

## Results

FUSE achieves the best AUROC across all three VQA benchmarks: 0.883 (VQAv2), 0.761 (OKVQA), and 0.796 (AdVQA), outperforming the strongest baseline UMPIRE (0.862/0.750/0.773) by 2.1/1.1/2.3 points. On calibration, FUSE attains the lowest ECE (0.039 on all three datasets) and highest Pearson correlation (0.945/0.945/0.974), substantially ahead of the next-best methods. For selective answering, FUSE achieves the highest AURAC (0.926/0.767/0.767), indicating superior abstention quality over EigenScore (0.913/0.753/0.753) and Semantic Entropy (0.902/0.734/0.742).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUSE outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric across all three datasets. The closest competitor is UMPIRE (Lau et al., 2025), which also uses semantic diversity of sampled responses but lacks the aleatoric embedding-level prior; FUSE improves AUROC by 2.1 points on VQAv2 (0.883 vs 0.862) and by 2.3 points on AdVQA (0.796 vs 0.773), while reducing ECE from 0.331 to 0.039 on VQAv2. EigenScore (Chen et al., 2024), which computes log-det of response embedding covariance, is the strongest AURAC baseline (0.913 on VQAv2) but trails FUSE by 1.3 points (0.926) and shows notably weaker calibration (ECE 0.056 vs 0.039). On the harder OKVQA and AdVQA benchmarks where model outputs are more diverse and incoherent, FUSE's advantage widens: it achieves 0.761 AUROC on OKVQA versus 0.750 for UMPIRE and 0.741 for EigenScore, and 0.796 on AdVQA versus 0.773 for UMPIRE. Conformal prediction baselines (LoFreeCP, CLM) and token-level methods (LN-Entropy) are consistently weaker, with LoFreeCP achieving only 0.721 AUROC on VQAv2 and CLM the lowest AURAC (0.818). The gap is most pronounced in ECE, where FUSE's 0.039 is roughly 1.4–5× lower than all baselines, confirming that the Bayesian fusion of both uncertainty sources yields substantially better-calibrated scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUSE builds on two distinct threads: (1) the GPLVM-based probabilistic adaptation of frozen CLIP embeddings (Lawrence, 2003; Venkataramanan et al., 2025), which converts deterministic vision-language representations into distributions capturing input-level ambiguity, and (2) response-diversity-based uncertainty estimation for MLLMs (Lau et al., 2025 / UMPIRE; Kuhn et al., 2023 / Semantic Entropy), which measures output-level variability from stochastic sampling. The key departure is the principled Bayesian fusion of these two sources into a single closed-form posterior, rather than the heuristic or purely response-level scoring used by prior methods. The paper also positions itself against conformal prediction approaches (LoFreeCP, CLM) and token-level metrics (LN-Entropy), arguing that neither captures the semantic grounding uncertainty that arises from the interplay between input representation and model generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FUSE provides a training-free (post-GP-adapter) uncertainty score with a closed-form Bayesian posterior, making it computationally lightweight relative to ensemble or MCMC-based alternatives while achieving the best calibration (ECE ≈ 0.039) and selective-answering performance (AURAC) among tested baselines on VQAv2, OKVQA, and AdVQA. The clean theoretical decomposition (aleatoric prior from GP embeddings + epistemic likelihood from response scatter) offers an interpretable framework that could extend to other frozen-encoder architectures. Open questions include whether the Gaussian scale-family assumption on response embeddings holds for more diverse or multi-modal VLMs, whether the GP adapter generalizes beyond CLIP-based encoders, and how the method behaves on video or multi-image inputs where the single-latent-point formulation may be insufficient.
