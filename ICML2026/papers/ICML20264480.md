## Problem

Streaming LLMs generate while receiving inputs, but existing methods determine interaction timing through either hardcoded schedules (fixed wait-k intervals) or costly external alignment signals (timing labels, reasoning trajectories, stronger teacher models). Neither approach lets the model autonomously assess whether its current partial context is semantically sufficient for generation, causing either premature hallucination or unnecessary waiting. The paper asks: can a model learn endogenous cues for when to interact without any external supervision on timing?

## Input / Output

The model receives a progressively revealed input stream (text tokens or 400 ms audio chunks projected via a causal Whisper encoder) alongside a task instruction (e.g., a question for QA or a target language for translation). At each decoding step, the model outputs either a Read action (ingest more input) or a Write action (emit a generated token), and terminates once the answer is complete. A key structural detail is that the visible input boundary φ(t) is monotonic and content-dependent: the model can skip the remaining 20–30% of context in non-monotonic tasks like multiple-choice QA once decisive evidence appears, rather than consuming the full stream.

## Method

ProactiveLLM decouples capability learning from decision learning. For capability, it introduces two complementary training objectives on a Group Positional Encoding backbone: (1) Masked Streaming Language Modeling (MSLM), which applies monotonic random masking to simulate progressively revealed inputs, with a polynomial-biased multinomial allocation constraining the reading budget near the ideal alignment trajectory to avoid degenerate out-of-distribution states; and (2) Synchronized Privileged Self-Distillation (SPSD), where the same evolving model acts as both full-context teacher (batch mode) and partial-context student (streaming mode), with a small-coefficient KL divergence anchoring the streaming distribution to the batch distribution without suppressing anticipatory variance. For decision, a lightweight plug-and-play head (attention-driven or entropy-driven) reads the frozen backbone's internal states—cumulative input attention or next-token Shannon entropy—to emit Read/Write actions at inference. This architecture requires no external teachers, timing labels, or task-specific alignment data, and supports swapping decision heads without retraining the backbone.

## Results

On non-monotonic text tasks, ProactiveLLM dramatically outperforms fixed-interval baselines: on Short-form QA (SQuAD), Proactive-Entr achieves 73.48 F1 (97.16% of the 74.79 batch upper bound) with RCO 0.75, while the best Wait-k (Wait-9) scores only 15.14 F1. On Choice QA (MCTest), Proactive-Entr reaches 85.83% accuracy (97.16% of the 88.33% batch bound) while reading only 78% of the input, yielding a 21.6% AIL reduction. On speech Spoken-SQuAD, Proactive-Entr achieves 71.12 F1 (vs. 12.85 for Wait-9) with RCO 0.80. Against learning-based alignment baselines (trained with GPT-5.4 or Qwen3-32B annotations), ProactiveLLM outperforms the best baseline by 10.62 F1 on Short QA at low latency and 8.15 F1 at high latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the fixed-interval Wait-k family (Ma et al., 2019; Tong et al., 2025b), which uses a rigid read-every-k-tokens schedule. On monotonic MT tasks, Proactive-Entr matches or slightly exceeds Wait-9 in BLEU (23.62 vs. 21.47 on en→de) while achieving comparable RCO (0.88 vs. 0.88), confirming parity where fixed schedules are naturally effective. On non-monotonic tasks the gap is decisive: Proactive-Entr scores 73.48 F1 on Short QA versus 15.14 for Wait-9 (a 4.8× improvement), and 85.83% accuracy on Choice QA versus 47.14% for Wait-9, because fixed intervals force generation from insufficient context. The second comparison group is learning-based alignment baselines trained with task-specific annotations generated by GPT-5.4 or Qwen3-32B (Table 2). ProactiveLLM outperforms the best learning-based baseline (GPT-5.4) by 10.62 F1 on Short QA at low latency (48.74 vs. 38.12) and by 8.15 F1 at high latency (58.36 vs. 50.21), while also achieving lower AIL and RCO, demonstrating that endogenous decision-making surpasses externally supervised timing without requiring separate alignment data construction for each task or latency level.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProactiveLLM builds directly on the Group Positional Encoding streaming backbone (Tong et al., 2025b), which decouples input and output positional indices to enable 'generate-while-reading' without architectural changes. It departs from prior streaming interaction methods in two key ways: (1) rather than relying on fixed-interval heuristics (Wait-k, STACL) or externally supervised alignment signals (timestamps, reasoning trajectories from stronger teachers), it cultivates endogenous semantic-sufficiency cues through masked streaming language modeling and synchronized privileged self-distillation where the same model serves as both partial-context student and full-context teacher; (2) it decouples capability learning from decision learning, enabling plug-and-play decision heads (attention-driven, entropy-driven) that can be swapped without retraining the backbone. This positions the work at the intersection of masked language modeling (BERT-style), on-policy self-distillation, and streaming interaction policy design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in real-time AI systems: the inability of streaming LLMs to autonomously determine when partial context is semantically sufficient, forcing either rigid schedules or costly external annotation pipelines. The plug-and-play architecture and cross-modal validation (text + speech) make the framework broadly applicable, and the finding that the explicit KL distillation coefficient is largely immaterial (the joint batch+streaming loss itself closes the distributional gap) simplifies the training recipe. Open questions include whether the threshold-based decision heads can be replaced by learned policies (e.g., RL) for more complex multi-turn streaming, how the approach scales to very long streams where the polynomial allocation budget becomes unwieldy, and whether the premature-generation risk identified in the failure-mode analysis can be further mitigated without sacrificing latency gains.
