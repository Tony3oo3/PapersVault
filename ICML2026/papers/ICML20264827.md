## Problem

In self-rewarding language models, the synchronized improvement of chosen and rejected responses across iterative DPO rounds causes their latent representations to converge, which provably drives the DPO gradient to zero and collapses training. The paper provides a theoretical bound showing the directional guidance term is Lipschitz-bounded by the representational distance between chosen and rejected samples, and empirically confirms a 9× shrinkage in score gap over iterations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (5k queries per iteration set) and operates on a base LLM (Llama, Qwen, or Mistral) that has been SFT'd with both instruction-following and evaluation capabilities. Each iteration generates K=7 candidate responses per prompt from multiple model versions (past, present, future), scores them via LLM-as-a-Judge prompting, and produces preference pairs for DPO training. The final output is an aligned language model, evaluated on instruction-following benchmarks (AlpacaEval 2.0, Arena-Hard-v0.1, MT-Bench) and out-of-distribution tasks (GSM8K, ARC, TruthfulQA, HumanEval).

## Method

Temporal Self-Rewarding introduces a two-phase decoupling strategy that coordinates past, present, and future model generations to sustain preference learning signals. Phase 1 (Anchored Rejection) fixes rejected responses to the initial SFT model M_0's outputs, preventing negative-sample quality inflation, then trains a temporary future model M_f via DPO on these anchored pairs. Phase 2 (Future-Guided Chosen) uses M_f to generate superior chosen responses that the current model cannot yet produce, pairing them with the same anchored rejected samples to train the next iteration model M_{i+1}. The theoretical contribution proves that the DPO gradient norm is bounded by K·‖h_w − h_l‖ under Lipschitz continuity of the log-likelihood, so preserving the representational gap through temporal decoupling prevents gradient vanishing. The framework runs 2 iterations (vs. 4 for standard self-rewarding) to maintain comparable total compute, and extends naturally to online RL via GRPO with temporally decoupled offline references.

## Results

On Llama3.1-8B, Temporal Self-Rewarding (TSR) achieves a 29.44% win rate on AlpacaEval 2.0 and 14.6% score on Arena-Hard-v0.1 after just 2 iterations, outperforming the Self-Rewarding baseline (19.69% and 9.4% after 4 iterations) by 9.75 and 5.2 points respectively. Across model families, TSR consistently leads: Qwen2.5-7B reaches 35.90% AlpacaEval Win (vs. 18.14% for SR) and Mistral-7B reaches 35.16% (vs. 27.58%). On out-of-distribution tasks, TSR Iter1 achieves 56.25% on GSM8K, 54.43% on TruthfulQA, and 26.22% on HumanEval, all surpassing the best Self-Rewarding iteration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Self-Rewarding (Yuan et al., 2024), the closest and most relevant baseline, Temporal SR wins on every benchmark: +9.75 points on AlpacaEval 2.0 Win Rate (29.44 vs. 19.69), +5.2 on Arena-Hard (14.6 vs. 9.4), and +0.15 on MT-Bench average (5.89 vs. 5.74), while using half the iterations (2 vs. 4) for comparable compute. Against Rejection-Sampling SFT (Liu et al., 2023), TSR achieves roughly 4× the AlpacaEval Win Rate (29.44 vs. 7.33) and 2.2× the Arena-Hard score (14.6 vs. 6.6), confirming that preference-based optimization with temporal decoupling far exceeds positive-example-only fine-tuning. Against SPIN (Chen et al., 2024) and its fair variant SPIN-Fair, TSR leads by over 22 points on AlpacaEval Win Rate (29.44 vs. 6.83/7.20) and by 10+ points on Arena-Hard, demonstrating that the self-rewarding paradigm with temporal coordination substantially outperforms self-play approaches that rely on fixed human-labeled chosen responses. The advantage is consistent across model families (Llama 3B/8B/70B, Qwen 7B, Mistral 7B) and extends to online RL (GRPO-Temporal outperforms GRPO on Minerva, Math-500, AMC, AIME25, and AIME24).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Self-Rewarding Language Models paradigm (Yuan et al., 2024), where a single LLM iteratively generates and judges its own responses for DPO training, and on the broader DPO optimization framework (Rafailov et al., 2023). While prior work in this line (Meta-Rewarding, CREAM, process-based self-rewarding) has focused on improving the model's judging capability, this paper departs by identifying a data-construction pathology: the synchronized quality improvement of chosen and rejected samples causes representational convergence that provably vanishes the DPO gradient. The proposed temporal decoupling—anchoring negatives to a frozen past model and upgrading positives via a temporary future model—is a new mechanism orthogonal to judge-quality improvements, and the extension to GRPO draws on LUFFY's off-policy guidance framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear theoretical diagnosis (Theorem 2.1) for why self-rewarding degrades over iterations, shifting the community's attention from judge quality to preference-pair construction dynamics. The consistent gains across five models (Llama 3B/8B/70B, Qwen 7B, Mistral 7B) and the out-of-distribution transfer to math, code, and QA tasks suggest the temporal strategy captures a general principle about maintaining contrast in iterative optimization. Key limitations include the 2× per-iteration cost (mitigated but not eliminated by halving iterations), the unexplored integration with judge-optimization techniques like meta-rewarding, and the modest gains in the online RL extension, leaving open whether the temporal strategy scales to many more iterations or to settings where the past model is too weak to provide useful negatives.
