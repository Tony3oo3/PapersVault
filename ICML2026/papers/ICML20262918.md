## Problem

Low-bit (2–4 bits) post-training quantization of image super-resolution models suffers severe accuracy degradation, and existing PTQ methods focus on the quantization process itself while ignoring adjustments to the weight matrices. The paper identifies that the degradation is primarily caused by activation quantization error rather than weight quantization, and that the model's sensitivity to this error is governed by the condition number of the weight matrices—a factor that has not been explicitly optimized in prior PTQ work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262918/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CondiQuant operates on a pretrained SR model (SwinIR-light, MambaIRv2, or EDSR) using a small calibration set of 100 low-resolution images (3×64×64 crops from DF2K) and the full-precision model. It produces a modified weight matrix per linear layer that is more quantization-friendly, after which standard per-channel quantization and calibration (adapted from 2DQuant) are applied. The end-to-end system still takes a low-resolution RGB image and outputs a high-resolution image at ×2, ×3, or ×4 scale, with no additional inference-time modules or computation overhead.

## Method

CondiQuant formulates the activation quantization loss as bounded by κ(W)·‖δX‖₂/‖X‖₂, showing that minimizing the condition number of each weight matrix reduces the impact of unavoidable activation rounding errors. Exploiting the rank deficiency of feature matrices (mean rank ratio ≈ 0.63), the method can shift W to a quantization-friendly equivalent without changing the output. The optimization is solved via proximal gradient descent with two alternating steps: a gradient descent step on ½‖XW−Y‖²_F to preserve the output, and a proximal step that performs SVD on W and applies a closed-form shrinkage σ_i* = (σ_i + 2λμt)/(1+2λμ) to compact the singular value distribution toward the mean t, thereby reducing κ(W). The entire iteration (K=50 steps) takes ~19 seconds, introduces no extra parameters, and achieves theoretically optimal compression (3.60×) and speedup (5.08×) at 2 bits.

## Results

CondiQuant achieves state-of-the-art PSNR and SSIM across all five SR benchmarks (Set5, Set14, B100, Urban100, Manga109) at all scale factors (×2, ×3, ×4) and bit widths (2, 3, 4 bits). At the most challenging setting (×4, 2-bit), CondiQuant gains 1.11 dB over the best baseline (2DQuant) on Set5 (30.64 vs. 29.53 PSNR) and 1.60 dB on Manga109 (27.67 vs. 26.07 PSNR). At ×2, 4-bit, the average gap to the full-precision SwinIR-light model is only 0.38 dB, indicating near-lossless compression. The method also generalizes across architectures (SwinIR, MambaIRv2, EDSR), consistently outperforming 2DQuant by 0.45–1.45 dB on Manga109 ×4 at 4-bit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CondiQuant outperforms all five baselines on every benchmark, bit width, and scale factor tested. The strongest baseline is 2DQuant (Liu et al., 2024), the current best SR-specific PTQ method; CondiQuant surpasses it by 0.16–1.11 dB PSNR at ×2 and by up to 1.60 dB at ×4, 2-bit (e.g., 30.64 vs. 29.53 on Set5, 27.67 vs. 26.07 on Manga109). Among the general ViT PTQ methods, RepQ (Li et al., 2023) is the closest competitor at 4-bit (within ~0.14 dB on Set5 ×2), but it degrades sharply at 2-bit (31.65 PSNR on Set5 ×2 vs. CondiQuant's 37.15). PTQ4ViT (Yuan et al., 2022) and NoisyQuant (Liu et al., 2023) show even larger gaps, with NoisyQuant dropping to 30.13 PSNR at ×2, 2-bit on Set5. DBDC+Pac (Tu et al., 2023), the SR-specific baseline, is consistently outperformed by 0.5–2.6 dB depending on the setting. The advantage of CondiQuant is most pronounced at ultra-low bit widths (2-bit) and higher scale factors (×4), where activation quantization error dominates and condition number minimization provides the greatest benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

CondiQuant builds directly on 2DQuant (Liu et al., 2024) for its calibration and clipping-bound search pipeline, but departs conceptually from all prior PTQ methods (PTQ4ViT, RepQ, NoisyQuant, 2DQuant) that optimize quantizer parameters by instead modifying the weight matrix itself. The key theoretical ingredient — using the condition number of the weight matrix to bound the impact of activation quantization error — is borrowed from numerical analysis and the MagR observation (Zhang et al., 2024) that feature matrices are approximately rank-deficient, here extended from LLMs to SR models. The optimization is solved via proximal gradient descent with a closed-form SVD-based proximal operator, a standard tool from convex optimization repurposed for quantization-friendly weight reparameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CondiQuant achieves near-FP accuracy at 4 bits (0.38 dB average gap) and substantial gains at 2 bits across five benchmarks and three architectures (ViT, Mamba, CNN), with zero inference overhead and theoretically optimal compression ratios. The condition-number framing offers a principled, architecture-agnostic lens for understanding *why* certain layers (e.g., K in self-attention) are most quantization-sensitive, which could guide future quantization-aware design. Open questions include whether the rank-deficiency assumption holds for all network depths and input distributions, whether the approach composes with QAT, and whether it extends to other low-level vision tasks (denoising, deblurring) where activation statistics differ.
