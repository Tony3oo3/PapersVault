## Problem

Standard Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) in large-scale flow-matching and diffusion models applies disproportionately large updates at the initial sampling step, where the guidance direction is a generic, noise-independent offset. In high-dimensional latent spaces (10^6–10^7 dimensions), this causes severe trajectory overshooting, color oversaturation, and structural incoherence, forcing practitioners to use many sampling steps to recover. Existing dynamic guidance strategies rely on fixed time-dependent schedules that ignore instance-level heterogeneity in guidance magnitude, failing to selectively mitigate unstable samples.

## Input / Output

MAMBO-G is a plug-and-play wrapper around existing text-to-image or text-to-video diffusion pipelines (SD3.5, Lumina-Next, Wan2.1/2.2, Qwen-Image). It consumes the same text prompt and latent noise as the base model and produces the same output format (RGB images at 256×256 to 1024×1024, or video clips), but requires fewer NFE (e.g., 20 vs. 60 for images, 30 vs. 60 for video). The only additional computation is a single global L2 norm over the flattened latent tensor per denoising step, adding <0.02% overhead.

## Method

The key insight is that at zero-SNR initialization (t=1), the guidance update Δv = v_cond − v_uncond collapses to a constant offset μ_cond − μ_uncond independent of the sampled noise, confirmed by cosine similarity ≈ 1.0 across seeds. MAMBO-G defines an instance-level ratio r_t = ‖v_cond − v_uncond‖₂ / ‖v_uncond‖₂ that quantifies relative guidance strength, then applies an exponential damping function w(r_t) = 1 + (w_max − 1)·exp(−α·r_t) to the guidance scale. This suppresses the scale when r_t is high (risky generic-direction regime) and permits aggressive boosting when r_t is low (instance-specific refinement phase). The approach is training-free, requires no architectural changes, is orthogonal to other guidance corrections (Rescale, APG), and achieves up to 3× speedup on SD3.5, 4× on Lumina, and 2× on Wan2.1-14B while matching or exceeding the quality of longer-step CFG baselines.

## Results

On MS-COCO with Qwen-Image at 10 sampling steps, MAMBO-G achieves an FID of 32.0, substantially outperforming the 10-step CFG baseline (FID 63.6) and approaching the 30-step CFG quality (FID 24.8), effectively a 3× speedup. On SD3.5, MAMBO-G at 10 steps matches or exceeds 30-step CFG in ImageReward and CLIPScore. For video generation, MAMBO-G on Wan2.1-1.3B matches the vBench quality of CFG on the 14B model, and at 1024×1024 resolution MAMBO-G maintains ImageReward 1.02 where standard CFG degrades to 0.20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAMBO-G outperforms all five compared baselines on every reported metric. Against the standard CFG baseline, MAMBO-G at 10 steps reduces FID from 63.6 to 32.0 (a 2× improvement) while approaching the 30-step CFG FID of 24.8. Among dynamic-guidance methods, MAMBO-G (FID 32.0) beats Limited Interval (38.0), CFG-Zero* (46.9), and Guidance Scheduler (55.7) by margins of 6.0, 14.9, and 23.7 FID points respectively. In the orthogonality analysis (Table 2), stacking MAMBO-G with Guidance Rescale lifts ImageReward from 0.73 to 1.12, and with APG from 0.85 to 0.96, confirming additive gains. The instance-aware advantage over a time-based schedule is also clear: MAMBO-G scores ImageReward 1.08 versus 0.83 for the time-averaged schedule on Qwen-Image, demonstrating that per-sample ratio adaptation is essential rather than a fixed temporal curve.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAMBO-G sits within the rapidly growing family of training-free guidance optimization strategies for diffusion and flow-matching models, building directly on the CFG formulation (Ho & Salimans, 2021) and the Rectified Flow velocity-field framework (Liu et al., 2023). Its key conceptual departure from prior work is the shift from time-dependent schedules w(t) (as in Guidance Scheduler, Limited Interval, Stage-Wise Dynamics) to an instance-level, magnitude-aware schedule w(r_t) driven by the per-sample ratio of guidance-update magnitude to unconditional velocity magnitude. The theoretical motivation draws on Lin et al.'s (2024) observation of zero-SNR instability, but extends it with a concrete analysis showing that at t=1 the guidance update is a noise-independent generic direction whose cosine similarity across seeds is ≈1.0, and that this genericity decays rapidly by t<0.8. The method is explicitly orthogonal to direction-modifying strategies like Guidance Rescale and APG, positioning itself as a complementary magnitude-control layer rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 3× speedup on SD3.5, 4× on Lumina, and 2× on the 14B-parameter Wan2.1 video model, all with negligible per-step overhead (<0.02%), making it a genuinely plug-and-play accelerator for production pipelines. The dimensionality argument—that guidance instability worsens as latent spaces grow from ~10⁴ to >10⁷ dimensions—gives the method forward-looking relevance for next-generation high-resolution and video models. However, the method only modulates guidance magnitude without correcting direction, so semantically misleading guidance signals are attenuated but not repaired; and w_max still requires mild per-model calibration. An open question is whether combining MAMBO-G with direction-correcting methods (e.g., APG, CFG-Zero*) can close the remaining gap to 50-step reference quality, and whether the exponential decay form is truly optimal or merely a convenient fit to the empirically observed stability boundary.
