## Problem

Inference-time alignment—adapting pretrained language models to reward feedback at inference—lacks theoretical foundations when neural networks serve as reward models. Existing analyses either fix the reward model or restrict it to linear estimators with fixed features, leaving open two key questions: (i) what provable advantage does the feature-learning capability of neural networks provide over linear estimators for reward modeling, and (ii) how should NN-based reward estimators be trained to minimize alignment regret?

## Input / Output

The method takes a prompt vector x ∈ [0,1]^{d_X}, a pretrained reference policy π_ref(y|x) with bounded density, and a limited budget of n noisy reward oracle queries r† = r°(x,y) + ξ (ξ ~ N(0,σ²)). Prompts and responses are represented as continuous vectors rather than token sequences, a simplification that isolates the role of feature learning. The output is a policy π(·|x) for generating aligned responses, along with theoretical regret bounds characterizing the gap between the optimal reward r*(x) and the expected reward under π.

## Method

The paper models the true reward r° in Besov spaces B_{p,q}^s(Ω) with anisotropic integrability (uniform smoothness in the prompt direction, non-uniform in the response direction), then proves that ReLU neural network reward estimators trained by least-squares achieve the minimax-optimal rate n^{-2/(2s+d)} on the χ²-regularized objective, while linear estimators (k-NN, kernel ridge, etc.) are provably suboptimal when d_X < d_Y and p < 2d_Y/d. For the multi-step algorithm, the paper alternates T = ⌈log n⌉ rounds of: sampling responses from the current policy, refitting the NN reward estimator, applying kernel smoothing to the reward, running Inference-time Pessimism with a truncated base policy, and adding Gaussian noise to the resulting policy density to maintain coverage. This iterative refinement progressively concentrates the policy around the reward maximizer, yielding a regret improvement factor of (1+u')/(1-u) > 1 over the single-step approach, where the improvement depends on the local geometry of the super-level set around the optimum.

## Results

The paper establishes that NN-based reward estimators achieve a regularized-objective gap of $n^{-2/(2s+d)}\log^2 n$ (Theorem 3), matching the minimax optimal nonparametric regression rate for Besov spaces. In contrast, linear estimators (k-NN, kernel ridge, sieve) are provably suboptimal with a gap of $n^{-(2s-v)/(2s+d-v)}$ (Theorem 4), where $v=2(d_Y/p-d/2)_+$, yielding a strictly worse rate whenever $d_X<d_Y$ and $p<2d_Y/d$. For regret, the single-step algorithm achieves $n^{-\frac{s}{2s+d}\cdot\frac{2}{2+\gamma}}\log^{4/(2+\gamma)}n$ (Theorem 6), while the multi-step algorithm improves this by a multiplicative factor of $(1+u')/(1-u)>1$ (Theorem 10), where $u'$ depends on the super-level set geometry parameter $\beta$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between NN-based reward estimators and linear estimators (k-NN regression, kernel ridge regression with fixed kernels, sieve estimators). Theorem 4 shows that linear estimators cannot achieve the minimax optimal rate for Besov spaces with anisotropic integrability when $p<2d_Y/d$ and $d_X<d_Y$, because they lack the feature-learning capability needed to detect local bumps in the reward function. The NN estimator (Theorem 3) achieves the optimal $n^{-2/(2s+d)}$ rate in all cases, establishing a provable gap. For the algorithmic comparison, the multi-step algorithm (Algorithm 1) improves upon the single-step plug-in approach (Theorem 6) by a factor of $(1+u')/(1-u)>1$ in the regret exponent, where the improvement grows with $\beta$ (the parameter controlling how tightly the super-level set concentrates around the maximizer). This gain arises because iterative policy updates concentrate sampling near the optimal response, reducing both the local estimation error (Lemma 11) and the coverage term (Lemma 12) at each step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two theoretical threads: (1) the inference-time alignment literature (Huang et al. 2025b on χ²-regularized objectives and Inference-time Pessimism; Best-of-N analyses by Yang et al. 2024, Beirami et al. 2025) and (2) nonparametric regression theory for neural networks in Besov spaces (Suzuki 2019; Suzuki & Nitanda 2021). Prior alignment theory treated the reward model as fixed or linear (Foster et al. 2025), while prior NN regression theory studied L² estimation error in isolation. This paper bridges the two by analyzing how NN feature learning translates into alignment regret, and by introducing a multi-step policy-reward co-update scheme with Gaussian mollification to maintain coverage guarantees across iterations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable separation between NN and linear reward estimators specifically for the alignment objective (not just regression loss), showing that feature learning matters when the reward has spatially non-uniform smoothness. The multi-step improvement, while modest in rate, gives a theoretical justification for iterative reward-model refinement that practitioners already use heuristically. Key limitations include the vector-valued (non-token) representation, the need for strong local-structure assumptions (Assumption 9) on the super-level set, and the gap to realistic Transformer-based settings. Open questions include extending the analysis to token-sequence function classes, reducing the number of hyperparameters introduced by the mollification procedure, and understanding whether the multi-step gains persist under computational budget constraints.
