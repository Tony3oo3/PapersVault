## Problem

Existing backdoor-based diffusion watermarking methods for text-to-image models implicitly assume a 'faithful' verification process where watermark responses remain stable, yet adversaries can corrupt these signals through parameter perturbations (both random and adversarial), and synthetic triggers are detectable via auditing. No prior method provides certified robustness guarantees for model ownership verification of T2I diffusion models, leaving a gap between the provable guarantees available for small classifiers and the practical needs of large generative models.

## Input / Output

At verification time, the method consumes the weights of a suspicious generator and an independently trained unwatermarked reference generator, a frozen private diffusion classifier, and a binary set of class prompts (e.g., 'cat' and 'dog'). It injects layer-adaptive Gaussian noise into model parameters at inference, generates N samples per prompt under M i.i.d. noise draws, and outputs a binary ownership decision via a paired-sample t-test, along with the continuous Watermark Robustness (WR) and Reference Probability (RP) scores and a certified Mahalanobis radius R* bounding the parameter perturbations under which verification is guaranteed.

## Method

Cert-LAS operates in two stages. In the embedding stage, a frozen diffusion model serves as a private classifier, and the generator is trained to induce misclassification toward a target posterior q* (minimizing KL divergence) while an MS-SSIM regularizer preserves perceptual fidelity; layer-adaptive Gaussian noise is allocated proportionally to a Layer Fine-tuning Sensitivity (LFS) indicator that concentrates noise on vulnerable UNet layers under a fixed global budget, and an exponential growth schedule progressively increases the number of noise samples for gradient averaging to make robust optimization tractable. In the verification stage, WR and RP are estimated under the same layer-adaptive smoothing, and ownership is certified whenever WR exceeds a closed-form threshold derived from a paired-sample t-test. The theoretical analysis establishes a certified radius R* under Mahalanobis ellipsoidal neighborhoods, showing that stronger watermark embedding (higher WR) directly yields a larger certifiable region, and proves tightness of the robustness condition via a worst-case classifier construction.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion v1.4, Cert-LAS achieves a perfect T@10⁻⁶ F of 1.000 for watermark verification while maintaining competitive generation fidelity (FID 25.91, CLIP 31.13, DreamSim 0.111). Under certified evaluation with layer-adaptive smoothing at σ₁.₀, Cert-LAS achieves a verification success rate (VSR) of 1.000 with a certified radius R̄ of 1.48, compared to the vanilla layer-uniform baseline's VSR of 0.812 and R̄ of 0.81. At the more aggressive σ₁.₄ noise level, the vanilla baseline collapses (VSR 0.385, R̄ 0.00) while Cert-LAS retains VSR 0.847 and R̄ 1.428. Cert-LAS also achieves dramatically lower prompt-space suspiciousness (S_in(p) = 0.125) compared to WatermarkDM (0.964) and SleeperMark (0.958), indicating far superior stealthiness against watermark auditing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against WatermarkDM, Cert-LAS achieves a perfect T@10⁻⁶ F of 1.000 versus 0.879, while reducing prompt-space suspiciousness from 0.964 to 0.125 (an 87% reduction) and image-space suspiciousness from 0.933 to 0.016, indicating that WatermarkDM's synthetic trigger is easily detectable in both prompt and image spaces. Against SleeperMark, Cert-LAS matches or exceeds verification (1.000 vs. 0.998) and achieves a substantially lower prompt-space suspiciousness score (0.125 vs. 0.958), confirming that SleeperMark's trigger remains highly detectable in prompt space despite evading image-space auditing. Under certified evaluation with layer-adaptive smoothing, Cert-LAS consistently outperforms the vanilla layer-uniform baseline across all noise levels: at σ₁.₀ it achieves VSR 1.000 with R̄ 1.48 versus 0.812 and 0.81, and at σ₁.₄ it maintains VSR 0.847 and R̄ 1.428 while the vanilla baseline collapses to VSR 0.385 and R̄ 0.00. In robustness experiments, Cert-LAS with smoothing maintains T@10⁻⁶ F above 0.965 under ℓ₂ parameter perturbations up to norm 0.8, where WatermarkDM fails entirely beyond norm 0.2 and SleeperMark beyond norm 0.6. Under LoRA fine-tuning (rank 640, 2000 steps), Cert-LAS retains T@10⁻⁶ F of 1.000 while WatermarkDM drops to 0.000, demonstrating that the layer-adaptive smoothing training inherently embeds resilience that survives aggressive downstream adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cert-LAS extends parameter-space certified watermarking from small-scale classifiers (Bansal et al., 2022; Jiang et al., 2023; Ren et al., 2023) to T2I diffusion models, a setting where the high-dimensional score-based generative objective makes direct transfer infeasible. It repurposes the recent finding that diffusion models function as zero-shot classifiers (Li et al., 2023; Clark & Jaini, 2023) as a trigger-free watermark carrier, replacing the synthetic triggers used by WatermarkDM and SleeperMark. The key methodological departure is the Layer Fine-tuning Sensitivity (LFS)–guided non-uniform noise allocation, which concentrates smoothing on architecturally vulnerable UNet layers to enlarge the certified radius while preserving a fixed total noise budget, combined with an exponential growth schedule that makes robust optimization computationally tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to provide provable (certified) ownership verification guarantees for T2I diffusion models, closing a gap where prior backdoor-based watermarks implicitly assumed a 'faithful' verification process that adversaries can break with minimal parameter perturbations. The certified radius, while modest in absolute terms, offers a principled decision boundary for verification authorities and a concrete robustness metric for model owners. Limitations include evaluation only on SD v1.4 with a binary cat/dog classification task, and the certified radius shrinks substantially at higher noise levels (k=1.4), suggesting practical deployment may require careful trade-off tuning. Open questions include whether the LFS-based allocation generalizes to DiT architectures, whether multi-class or multi-owner watermarks can be certified simultaneously, and how the approach scales to larger models like SDXL or FLUX.
