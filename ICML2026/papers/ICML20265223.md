## Problem

Data selection during the annealing phase of LLM pre-training currently relies on empirical heuristics such as domain up-sampling or context extension, which lack principled grounding in optimization theory. This leads to sub-optimal data scheduling at the most critical convergence stage, where the model must transition from noisy oscillations in steep loss-landscape regions to stable descent along flat valleys. The paper asks: can sample selection be formulated as a problem of satisfying directional constraints derived from the loss landscape's spectral geometry?

## Input / Output

The method takes a stably pre-trained checkpoint θ_s, a training corpus of text sequences, a held-out validation set, and a selection count K (or a reweighting flag). Crucially, the full d×d Hessian is never materialized: a random projection matrix R ∈ R^{k×d} (k ≪ d) reduces the spectral analysis to a k×k sketched covariance, and the entire data schedule is precomputed with a single forward pass over D_train before annealing begins. The output is a continuous weight vector w* ∈ [0,1]^N (rounded to a binary top-K mask for selection) plus the fine-tuned model θ_adj obtained after a few weighted-loss training steps.

## Method

DiReCT partitions the validation Hessian's eigenspectrum into a stiff subspace S_⊥ (eigenvalues > ε) and a flat subspace S_∥ (eigenvalues ≤ ε) via randomized sketching, then formulates sample selection as maximizing the aggregate gradient norm in S_∥ subject to a quadratic energy budget τ_⊥ in S_⊥. Because the resulting problem is a non-convex quadratic program (NP-hard in the binary case), DiReCT solves a continuous relaxation via Successive Convex Approximation: at each iteration the quadratic objective is replaced by its first-order linear minorizer, yielding a linear program with box, equality, and quadratic constraints solved by projected gradient ascent with Dykstra's alternating projection. For discrete selection, deterministic top-K rounding (or randomized rounding) recovers the binary mask. A PAC-Bayes analysis shows that the induced flat-direction update contracts the curvature trace Tr(H_ρ), yielding a generalization bound whose leading complexity term shrinks with each step along the selected direction.

## Results

DiReCT achieves the highest aggregated score at both model scales: 32.3 on GPT-2-Medium (355M) and 40.3 on Llama-1.1B, outperforming Uniform Sampling by +2.1 and +2.4 points respectively. The most pronounced gains appear on math and code reasoning benchmarks, where DiReCT improves GSM8K by +1.3 (355M) and +3.4 (1.1B) and HumanEval by +2.6 (355M) and +5.0 (1.1B) over uniform sampling. On commonsense reasoning, DiReCT delivers large lifts on SciQ (+5.4/+5.3) and ARC-Easy (+6.8/+5.7) while remaining within ~1 point of uniform sampling on HellaSwag, PiQA, COPA, and WinoGrande.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InfoBatch (Qin et al., 2024), the strongest magnitude-centric baseline, DiReCT wins the aggregated score at both scales (32.3 vs. 32.1 on 355M; 40.3 vs. 40.1 on 1.1B) despite trailing on several individual commonsense benchmarks (e.g., PiQA 57.5 vs. 60.5, COPA 65.1 vs. 69.3 on 1.1B). The decisive advantage comes from math/code reasoning: DiReCT scores 11.2 on HumanEval vs. InfoBatch's 7.1 and 4.5 on GSM8K vs. 2.5 on Llama-1.1B, a gap the authors attribute to gradient alignment with the flat Hessian subspace rather than raw information density. Against GradNorm importance sampling (Katharopoulos & Fleuret, 2018), DiReCT leads by +1.0 (355M) and +1.4 (1.1B) in aggregate, with the largest margins on SciQ (+3.3/+3.7) and ARC-Easy (+3.4/+2.7). Perplexity-based selection (Hu et al., 2024) is outperformed by +1.3 (355M) and +2.1 (1.1B) in aggregate, with DiReCT particularly dominant on GSM8K (+1.1/+3.0) and HumanEval (+2.0/+3.6). The paper's interpretation is that magnitude-based criteria (InfoBatch, GradNorm) capture sample difficulty but miss the directional geometry needed for stable descent along low-curvature valleys, whereas DiReCT's spectral constraint explicitly trades a small amount of high-curvature gradient energy for coherent progress in the flat subspace.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by applying Hessian spectral geometry to the under-studied annealing phase of LLM pre-training, where prior data-selection work (DoReMi, REGMIX, MixMin, Chameleon, Velocitune) has focused almost exclusively on stable-training domain weighting. It repurposes the valley-landscape metaphor from Wen et al. (2025) and the sharp-minima literature (Keskar et al., 2017) to formalize a directional constraint on per-sample gradients, then connects the resulting flat-direction preference to a PAC-Bayes generalization bound via the inverse-trace surrogate. Unlike empirical annealing strategies (reasoning up-sampling, long-context injection) catalogued in Table 1, DiReCT derives its selection criterion from the model's instantaneous curvature rather than from content-based heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first optimization-theoretic justification for annealing-phase data selection and a PAC-Bayes guarantee tying the selected flat-direction updates to a contracted curvature trace, which could guide future principled scheduling beyond ad-hoc domain mixing. However, the evaluation is limited to 355M and 1.1B parameters with a 2.1 GB annealing corpus, leaving open whether the spectral partitioning and SCA solver remain effective (and computationally tractable) at the 70B+ scale where annealing strategies are most consequential. The single-checkpoint Hessian assumption and the required full-dataset forward pass for gradient collection are acknowledged bottlenecks that dynamic re-partitioning or parameter-subset backpropagation would need to address.
