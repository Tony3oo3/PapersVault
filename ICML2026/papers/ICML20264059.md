## Problem

Post-deployment LLM alignment becomes brittle when institutional policies shift due to regulatory, cultural, or organizational changes, creating an Alignment-Reality Gap where previously correct preferences are now wrong. Full re-annotation is economically prohibitive, machine unlearning targets data deletion rather than policy reinterpretation, and purely punitive methods like NPO collapse into overly conservative behavior. The paper tackles how to surgically update a model's alignment to a new policy using only the existing preference dataset, without fresh human annotation.

## Input / Output

TRACE consumes an existing binary preference dataset D = {(x, y_w, y_l)}, a new policy specification π_new (a binary compliance function returning compliant/non-compliant per response), a frozen reference model M_ref, and optionally an LLM oracle (GPT-4o) that generates corrective responses for the Punish set. It produces re-aligned model parameters θ', three triaged subsets (Invert, Punish, Retain), and per-sample alignment impact weights. The key constraint is the non-blind setup: the original preference triplets must be available, though the exact old policy π_old need not be known, and the oracle serves as a scalable proxy for human judgment during triage.

## Method

TRACE is a three-stage pipeline that reframes re-alignment as a structured optimization over existing data. Stage 1 (Triage) evaluates both responses in each pair against π_new, partitioning the dataset into Invert (preference reversed), Punish (both responses non-compliant), and Retain (original preference holds), thereby resolving the False Dichotomy Problem where a naive inversion would incorrectly promote a non-compliant loser. Stage 2 defines a hybrid objective: a DPO-style relational loss on reversed pairs for the Invert set, an NPO-style punitive loss (or oracle-guided DPO with a corrective response) for the Punish set, and a forward KL-divergence regularizer on the Retain set to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Stage 3 computes per-sample alignment impact weights via a bi-level optimization approximation (from U2A), where each weight is the dot product between a global gold-batch gradient and the local task gradient under an isotropic Hessian assumption, effectively filtering out orthogonal or antagonistic updates. The final TRACE objective combines impact-weighted re-alignment losses with unweighted KL regularization, requiring only ~11% additional compute over standard DPO.

## Results

On human preference evaluation, TRACE achieves 81.8% and 85.3% pairwise win rates against the U2A baseline on PKU-SafeRLHF and SynthValueBench respectively, while DPO-Gold (full re-annotation) wins 68.2% and 74.6% against TRACE, establishing the performance ceiling. On general capability benchmarks, TRACE preserves MMLU (70.2 vs. 70.6 base) and GSM8K (70.6 vs. 70.4 base) within confidence intervals, with a bounded 3.2-point drop on HellaSwag (78.2 vs. 81.4). Under adversarial stress tests, TRACE achieves 19.7% ASR on Refusal Suppression (vs. 21.3% for U2A and 12.8% for DPO-Gold), confirming comparable robustness to the unlearning baseline while retaining a gap to full re-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against U2A (Feng et al., 2025), the primary efficient re-alignment baseline, TRACE wins decisively: 81.8% and 85.3% human-preference win rates on PKU-SafeRLHF and SynthValueBench respectively, with U2A winning only 18.2% and 14.7% of comparisons. On general capability, TRACE also outperforms U2A on GPQA (30.1 vs. 29.5 on PKU-SafeRLHF) and GSM8K (70.6 vs. 69.9), though U2A retains a 2.6-point edge on HellaSwag (80.8 vs. 78.2) — a trade-off the authors attribute to U2A's over-refusal collapsing helpfulness. Against DPO-Gold (Rafailov et al., 2023), the full re-annotation gold standard, TRACE loses on human preference (68.2% and 74.6% win rates for DPO-Gold) and on adversarial ASR (11.3%/12.8% for DPO-Gold vs. 27.3%/19.7% for TRACE), but substantially narrows the gap: TRACE at 5k samples outperforms Naive Oracle DPO at 20k samples by ~17–20 points in Target Policy Agreement, isolating the triage-and-weight mechanism as the primary driver rather than oracle access alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRACE builds directly on three pillars of the preference-optimization literature: DPO as the relational loss for preference inversion, NPO as the punitive loss for response suppression, and U2A's bi-level optimization framework for computing alignment-impact weights. Its key conceptual departure is the Triage stage, which uses a new policy oracle to partition existing preference pairs into Invert, Punish, and Retain categories—addressing what the authors call the 'False Dichotomy Problem' where both responses in a pair are non-compliant under the updated policy. Prior unlearning methods (U2A, NPO) presuppose a pre-defined forget set; TRACE's contribution is deriving that set automatically from a high-level policy specification, and combining relational and punitive objectives in a single hybrid loss rather than treating re-alignment as purely punitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically urgent gap: deployed LLMs face regulatory and cultural policy shifts that make full re-annotation economically infeasible, yet existing unlearning methods either lack the nuance to edit value structures or collapse into over-refusal. The finding that TRACE at 5k samples outperforms Naive Oracle DPO at 20k isolates the triage-and-weight mechanism as the primary driver of re-alignment quality, suggesting that *how* you reinterpret existing data matters more than *how much* you have. Key open questions include the residual adversarial-robustness gap to full re-annotation (an information-theoretic limit of data reuse), the uncharacterized blind setting where the original preference dataset is unavailable, and whether integrating Inverse Constitutional AI can close the gap for models with undocumented policies.
