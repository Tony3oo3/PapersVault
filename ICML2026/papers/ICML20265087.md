## Problem

Existing spatial-temporal forecasting methods treat spatial and temporal dependencies as separate components, failing to capture lead-lag dependencies where temporal variations at one location propagate to others with time delays. While some deep models learn such dependencies, their purely data-driven nature makes them sensitive to noise and prone to spurious patterns. LLMs possess broad cross-domain knowledge that could distinguish genuine lead-lag signals from noise, but they lack topological awareness and explicit lead-lag priors in their training corpora, making direct application non-trivial.

## Input / Output

The method takes a spatial-temporal time series X^{1:T} ∈ R^{N×T} (N locations over T time steps) and a spatial adjacency matrix A_S ∈ R^{N×N} encoding geographic or connectivity structure. Time series are segmented into patches and nodes are soft-clustered into N_g groups via a learnable assignment matrix, so the LLM operates on group-level patch tokens rather than raw per-node values. The output is a forecast X̂^{T+1:T+P} ∈ R^{N×P} produced by projecting group-level LLM outputs back to individual nodes through the assignment matrix.

## Method

LagLLM constructs a lead-lag graph through a hybrid mechanism: a data-driven component parameterizes the adjacency via spatial and temporal embeddings whose inner-product similarity forms an initial graph, while a knowledge-driven component refines it using a spatial proximity mask and a semantic mask obtained by querying a frozen LLM with structured prompts (task instruction, time-series statistics, learnable lead-lag weights, and predefined criteria like cross-correlation and Granger causality). A Structural Token Sorting Module then orders patch tokens from most-leading to most-lagging via multi-layer message passing over the lead-lag graph, converting graph dependencies into an explicit sequential order that aligns with the LLM's autoregressive attention. The sorted tokens, prefixed with learnable global context tokens, are processed by a GPT-2 backbone fine-tuned with LoRA on attention layers and full updates on layer normalization and positional embeddings, with feed-forward layers frozen. Training uses a combined loss of prediction error and a grouping loss that encourages spatially coherent node clustering.

## Results

LagLLM achieves the best performance in 22 out of 27 evaluation cases across eight spatial-temporal datasets, outperforming the strongest baseline (STD-PLM) by an average of 1.76% in MAE. On PEMS08, LagLLM reaches MAE 13.21 / RMSE 23.17 / MAPE 8.76, and on ChinaAQI it achieves MAE 19.14 / RMSE 31.80 / MAPE 30.23, surpassing STD-PLM (MAE 20.36) by 6.0% in MAE. In few-shot settings (5% training data on PEMS07), LagLLM improves MAE by 7.42% and MAPE by 17.20% over the best baseline. The only dataset where LagLLM does not rank first is METR-LA, where MegaCRN's fine-grained spatial modeling is better suited to the localized traffic-speed dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LagLLM outperforms all 16 baselines on 22 of 27 metric-dataset combinations. Against the strongest overall baseline STD-PLM (Huang et al., AAAI 2025), LagLLM wins on every dataset except METR-LA, with margins ranging from 0.08 MAE (PEMS04: 18.13 vs 18.16) to 1.22 MAE (ChinaAQI: 19.14 vs 20.36). Among lead-lag-specific methods, LagLLM surpasses MillGNN (Wu et al., CIKM 2025) by 1.65 MAE on PEMS08 (13.21 vs 14.86) and LIFT (Zhao & Shen, ICLR 2024) by 2.01 MAE on PEMS08 (13.21 vs 15.22). Among LLM-based baselines, LagLLM beats STLLM (Liu et al., 2024) by 2.74 MAE on PEMS03 (14.51 vs 17.25) and TimeCMA (Liu et al., AAAI 2025) by 2.23 MAE on PEMS03 (14.51 vs 16.74). The sole exception is METR-LA, where MegaCRN (Jiang et al., AAAI 2023) achieves MAE 2.94 vs LagLLM's 3.09, attributed to METR-LA's localized traffic-speed dynamics where cross-region lead-lag effects are limited. On ChinaAQI, LagLLM outperforms CrossST (Liu & Zhang, ICDE 2025) by 2.27% in RMSE (31.80 vs 32.54), demonstrating the benefit of explicit lead-lag modeling for wind-driven pollutant transport.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established threads: (1) explicit lead-lag dependency modeling in spatiotemporal forecasting (via memory banks in PDFformer/MegaCRN, cross-correlation-guided graphs in LIFT/MillGNN, and adaptive GNNs in TraverseNet), and (2) LLM-based time series forecasting through prompt engineering and tokenization (Time-LLM, UrbanGPT, STLLM, STD-PLM). It departs from prior work by being the first to use a frozen LLM's parametric knowledge—invoked via structured prompts combining learnable graph features with predefined statistical criteria—to refine a data-driven lead-lag graph, rather than relying solely on learned representations. The structural token sorting module, which reorders patch tokens by lead-lag centrality before feeding them to the LLM backbone, is a novel mechanism not present in prior LLM-for-time-series or graph-based forecasting methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid data-driven + knowledge-driven graph construction addresses a recognized weakness of purely data-driven lead-lag models (sensitivity to noise and spurious patterns), and the few-shot results (up to 17.2% MAE improvement at 5% data) suggest LLM priors can substitute for training data in data-scarce regimes. However, the practical impact is tempered by the use of GPT-2-small as the backbone, the additional computational cost (~2× training time per epoch vs. STD-PLM), and the fact that the LLM is frozen—its contribution is limited to a binary yes/no classification per group pair rather than deep reasoning. Open questions include whether scaling to larger LLMs or introducing caching for semantic masks would improve the accuracy-efficiency trade-off, and whether the approach generalizes to domains where lead-lag structure is highly non-stationary.
