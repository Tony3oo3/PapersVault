## Problem

Asymptotic convergence guarantees for RL with reachability (and by extension LTL) specifications were previously established only through reward conversion to limit-average objectives, which relies on external parameters disconnected from the MDP structure and provides no characterization of when optimal policies emerge. This paper asks whether asymptotic convergence can be characterized directly in terms of intrinsic MDP parameters, and whether such guarantees translate to observable convergence in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263543/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives the MDP's state set, action set, and initial state (with transition probabilities unknown), a target/goal state set G, and access to a simulator that samples runs under any policy. It outputs a memoryless deterministic policy along with lower and upper value bounds L(s) and U(s) for each state. Unusually, the algorithm never receives p_min or any other MDP parameter as input; instead it iteratively guesses p_min as 1/2^k and the guarantee is that beyond a finite (but unknown) stage K_opt, the output policy is exactly optimal rather than merely ε-optimal.

## Method

The algorithm operates in stages k=1,2,3,… where each stage sets p_k=ε_k=δ_k=1/2^k, simulates the MDP N_k times to build a partial model with Hoeffding-bounded conservative transition estimates, collapses maximal end-components into super-states, and runs Bounded Value Iteration (BVI) on the collapsed MDP to extract a memoryless deterministic policy. The key insight is a three-part argument: (1) once k≥K_PAC (where p_k≤p_min), each stage yields a PAC guarantee of an ε_k-optimal policy with confidence 1−δ_k; (2) since ε_k shrinks exponentially, it eventually falls below ε_diff—the minimum value gap between the optimal and best suboptimal memoryless deterministic policy—forcing any ε_k-optimal policy to be exactly optimal; (3) the summable failure schedule Σδ_k<∞ combined with Borel-Cantelli ensures that with probability 1, only finitely many stages produce suboptimal policies. Section 4 further bounds ε_diff via transition complexity D (the maximum common denominator of transition probabilities), yielding ε_diff≥(2D)^(−2|A||S|)·2^(−2|S|), which characterizes K_opt purely in terms of intrinsic MDP structure.

## Results

The algorithm is evaluated on 9 standardized MDP benchmarks from the Quantitative Verification Benchmark Set (QVBS), each over 10 independent trials (36-hour time limit, single CPU core). Policy accuracy (empirical reachability probability) converges at a median stage k=2 and average k=2.3 across all benchmarks, confirming rapid emergence of the optimal-policy-only regime predicted by Theorem 3.2. A striking finding is that optimal policies emerge far earlier than tight value bounds: while policy accuracy stabilizes at k=2, the BVI bounds L(s₀) and U(s₀) only converge around k=16 (e.g., Dining Philosophers, optimal value 1.00). Low standard deviations across trials confirm robust convergence despite stochastic sampling variation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily a theoretical contribution and does not include a head-to-head empirical comparison against competing RL algorithms. The comparison is against prior theoretical results: Le et al. (2024) achieves only asymptotic convergence in value (policies get arbitrarily close to optimal) via LTL-to-limit-average-reward conversion, whereas this work guarantees that beyond a finite stage K_opt, only exactly optimal policies are produced with probability 1. Majumdar et al. (2025) provides regret-free guarantees, which are strictly weaker since they permit infinitely many non-optimal policies even with zero failure probability. The empirical evaluation serves to validate that the theoretical convergence guarantees (optimal-policy-only behavior) are observable in practice on standard benchmarks, rather than to benchmark against alternative algorithms. The paper positions its contribution as a direct, reward-free approach using intrinsic MDP parameters (p_min, ε_diff), in contrast to Le et al.'s reliance on external discount factors disconnected from the MDP structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PAC statistical model checking framework of Ashok et al. (2019), which provides sample complexity bounds for MDPs given a known minimum transition probability p_min, and on the Bounded Value Iteration (BVI) technique of Haddad & Monmege (2017) for computing value bounds in MDPs with end-components. It departs from the only prior asymptotic result for LTL/reachability (Le et al., 2024), which converts specifications to limit-average reward objectives via Blackwell's optimality, by instead working directly with MDP structure and transition probabilities without any reward conversion. The key conceptual move is transforming a known impossibility result (PAC learning is infeasible without p_min) into an asymptotic guarantee by iteratively guessing p_min at geometrically decreasing rates, combined with a summable confidence schedule and the Borel-Cantelli lemma.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is significant because reachability is the core computational primitive underlying all ω-regular and LTL specifications, so a principled convergence characterization for reachability immediately extends to the full class of temporal logic objectives. The guarantee is strictly stronger than prior asymptotic convergence: beyond a finite stage K_opt (characterized by the intrinsic ε_diff gap between optimal and suboptimal policies), only optimal policies are produced with probability one. Key limitations include the model-based nature of the algorithm, dependence on p_min (unknown in practice), and evaluation only on small MDPs from a verification benchmark set; extending to model-free methods and larger-scale problems remains open.
