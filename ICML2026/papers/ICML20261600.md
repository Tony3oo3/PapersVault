## Problem

Widefield calcium imaging modeling has been restricted to single-session analyses, limiting scalability and cross-subject generalization. While multi-subject pretrained models exist for spiking and LFP data, no such framework has been demonstrated for widefield calcium imaging, and zero-shot behavior decoding on unseen subjects remains elusive across all neural modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261600/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes widefield calcium imaging recordings spatially registered to the Allen Brain Atlas (CCF) and resampled to a unified 128×128 resolution, which are then partitioned into 32×32 non-overlapping spatial patches at each timestep (16 patches per frame, preserving full temporal resolution at 30 Hz). Outputs are 512-dimensional spatiotemporal latent representations that, after spatial average pooling, yield T×512 sequences for continuous behavior prediction (R²), or are fed to a lightweight decoder for pixel-wise neural reconstruction of masked cortical regions.

## Method

WiCAT introduces an atlas-grounded spatiotemporal tokenization where each 32×32 patch is embedded by a shared convolutional projector plus a learnable global spatial embedding indexed by atlas-aligned patch location, ensuring subject-invariant anatomical identity without any session- or subject-specific parameters. These tokens are processed by an 8-layer Transformer encoder with joint spatial-temporal self-attention and RoPE for relative temporal encoding. Self-supervised pretraining uses masked autoencoding with aggressive 90% random masking across both space and time, where a single-layer Transformer decoder reconstructs masked patches via MSE loss without access to subject or session identifiers, forcing the encoder to learn globally shared spatiotemporal dynamics. The key design insight is that combining atlas-aligned global spatial embeddings with this challenging reconstruction objective enables true zero-shot transfer to unseen subjects, tasks, and datasets using only frozen representations and lightweight linear or Kalman decoders.

## Results

WiCAT achieves the strongest behavior decoding R² across both datasets: 0.5124 (Musall) and 0.3396 (Kondo) with linear probing on the finetune set, and 0.3274 (Musall) and 0.1840 (Kondo) zero-shot on unseen subjects. With full finetuning, performance improves to R²=0.5783 (Musall) and 0.4245 (Kondo) on the finetune set, and 0.3675 (Musall) and 0.2398 (Kondo) zero-shot. WiCAT linear probing significantly outperforms all baselines on both datasets (p < 10⁻⁸ for Musall, p < 10⁻¹⁰ for Kondo; Wilcoxon signed-rank test). For zero-shot neural reconstruction of left-out brain regions, WiCAT achieves an average pixel-wise MSE of 0.2219 versus 0.3189 for a per-region linear decoder baseline on unseen subjects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WiCAT outperforms all four baseline families on both datasets in both finetune and zero-shot settings. Against SBIND (Hosseini & Shanechi, 2025), the strongest single-session widefield dynamical model, WiCAT linear probing achieves R²=0.5124 vs. 0.4294 (SBIND-Sup single-session) on Musall finetune, and more critically, R²=0.3274 vs. 0.2444 (SBIND-Sup multi-session) zero-shot on Musall, and R²=0.1840 vs. 0.1547 on Kondo zero-shot. Against LocaNMF + Linear Regression (Saxena et al., 2020), the standard atlas-guided decomposition pipeline, WiCAT improves Musall finetune R² from 0.3592 to 0.5124 and Kondo zero-shot from 0.0948 to 0.1840. Against NDT2 (Ye et al., 2023), a multi-subject spiking model adapted to widefield imaging, WiCAT also achieves superior decoding (reported in Appendix Table 7). Against CEBRA (Schneider et al., 2023) combined with LocaNMF, WiCAT similarly outperforms on both datasets. Notably, the multi-session SBIND variants use the same Allen Brain Atlas alignment as WiCAT, indicating that spatial alignment alone is insufficient and that the atlas-grounded tokenization with globally shared spatial embeddings is the critical differentiator for cross-subject generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked autoencoder paradigm (He et al., 2021) and multi-subject neural modeling approaches (NDT2, CEBRA, SBIND) from spiking/LFP/iEEG modalities to widefield calcium imaging, a modality where prior methods were restricted to single-session analysis. The key architectural departure is the atlas-grounded tokenization with globally shared spatial embeddings, which eliminates the session-specific parameters (learnable session tokens, subject-specific read-in/read-out layers) that prior multi-subject models rely on. This design choice is what enables true zero-shot transfer to unseen subjects, a capability the authors note has remained elusive across all neural modalities to date.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WiCAT establishes the first multi-subject pretrained model for widefield calcium imaging and demonstrates zero-shot continuous behavior decoding on entirely unseen subjects, a capability that had not been achieved for any multi-subject neural model. The finding that removing session-specific parameters improves (rather than hurts) zero-shot generalization challenges the prevailing design assumption in cross-subject neural modeling. Limitations include reliance on head-fixed paradigms, dependence on atlas alignment quality (moderate perturbations tolerated but larger misalignments degrade performance), and evaluation on only two datasets; future work on multimodal integration, richer adaptation strategies, and scaling to larger/more diverse datasets will be needed to realize a true foundation model for this modality.
