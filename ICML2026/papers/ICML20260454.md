## Problem

Bioacoustic foundation models trained on citizen science platforms like Xeno-Canto rely exclusively on species labels for supervision, leaving rich recording metadata (location, time, background species) unexploited. This creates a significant domain gap between curated focal recordings and real-world passive acoustic monitoring soundscapes, where models must generalize across acoustic, species, and geographic shifts. The paper asks whether metadata can serve as auxiliary supervision signals to learn more robust, generalizable representations.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 5-second audio windows resampled to 32 kHz, converted to log mel-spectrograms of 500 time frames × 128 mel bins (60 Hz–16 kHz) via STFT with 10 ms hop and 20 ms window. During training, multiple windows are mixed via mixup (up to 5 signals with Dirichlet-sampled weights) before spectrogram construction. Outputs include species predictions from both a linear classifier and a prototype learning classifier over spatial embeddings (penultimate-layer activations of EfficientNet-B3), plus metadata predictions (S2 cell location, season, background species) that are used only as training-time auxiliary targets and discarded at inference.

## Method

METAPERCH builds on a BioBaseline (EfficientNet-B3 backbone with prototype learning and linear classifier heads, trained with mixup and cross-entropy) by appending small MLP prediction heads for three metadata tasks: location (S2 cell classification at level 7, ~50,000 km² cells), season (4-class), and background species (multi-label). The multi-task objective sums the species cross-entropy loss with weighted metadata losses, where weights are optimized as hyperparameters via Vizier and missing metadata contributions are zeroed out using placeholder targets. Mixup is extended to metadata by constructing multi-hot targets from all present classes in the mixed recordings. An optional gradient reversal layer can be inserted between the backbone and metadata heads to encourage embeddings that are agnostic to potentially spurious metadata correlations. The paper demonstrates that this simple multi-task formulation outperforms dynamic loss weighting and gradient surgery, and that location and background species provide the largest individual gains, particularly for underrepresented geographic regions and soundscape detection tasks.

## Results

On the BEANS benchmark (9 datasets spanning marine mammals, bats, dogs, mosquitoes, and birds), METAPERCH achieves a mean classification accuracy of 0.870 and mean detection cmAP of 0.512, setting new SotA by +0.008 accuracy and +0.006 cmAP over the next best model. On BirdSet (7 avian PAM datasets), METAPERCH reaches a mean ROC-AUC of 0.906, improving +0.015 over its BioBaseline and reaching within 0.002 of Perch 2.0 (0.908). On WABAD (1,192 globally distributed bird species), METAPERCH achieves 0.811 linear-probe ROC-AUC and 0.946 prototype-learning ROC-AUC, outperforming Perch 2.0 by +0.072 and +0.022 respectively on those metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

METAPERCH outperforms Perch 2.0 (the current SotA bioacoustic foundation model) on BEANS by +0.023 mean accuracy and +0.010 mean cmAP, and on WABAD by +0.072 linear-probe ROC-AUC and +0.022 prototype ROC-AUC, though it trails Perch 2.0 by 0.002 ROC-AUC on BirdSet. Against BioLingual (FT), the strongest audio-language baseline, METAPERCH leads by +0.008 accuracy and +0.033 cmAP on BEANS. On BirdSet, METAPERCH surpasses Audio ProtoPNet-5 (+0.010 ROC-AUC), BirdMAE-L (+0.020), and the original BirdSet paper's model (+0.055), while remaining within 0.002 of Perch 2.0. On BEANS, METAPERCH also beats BirdNet (+0.052 accuracy, +0.120 cmAP), Miron et al. (+0.047 accuracy, +0.104 cmAP), and NatureLM-Audio (which performs poorly at 0.148 cmAP). The one notable exception is the Hainan Gibbons detection task, where METAPERCH (0.500) underperforms BioBaseline (0.519) by 0.019 cmAP, suggesting metadata benefits diminish when both species and acoustic domain shifts are present simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

METAPERCH builds directly on Perch 2.0 (van Merrienboer et al., 2025), the current SotA bioacoustics foundation model, adopting its EfficientNet-B3 backbone and prototype-learning classifier as the BioBaseline while removing source prediction and self-distillation for faster iteration. Its key departure is the addition of auxiliary metadata prediction heads (location via S2-cell classification, season, background species) trained jointly with species identification, drawing on multi-task learning literature (constant loss weighting, gradient reversal layers from domain-adversarial networks) and geolocation prediction work (GeoCLIP, SatCLIP). Unlike NatureLM-Audio and AnimalSpeak, which encode metadata into templated text captions for audio-language pretraining, METAPERCH assigns each metadata source a dedicated prediction task, enabling disentangled ablation of individual metadata contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that simple auxiliary metadata losses yield consistent (if modest) improvements across 17 bioacoustic datasets, with the largest gains in geographically underrepresented regions and for globally distributed tasks like WABAD (+0.070 linear-probe ROC-AUC). A notable limitation is that benefits diminish when species and acoustic domain shifts co-occur, and the desert biome actually degrades, suggesting metadata correlations are not universally beneficial. Open questions include whether test-time conditioning or pre-training paradigms could extract more value from metadata, whether the validation-test disconnect observed (median Vizier trial outperforming the best on WABAD) reflects a fundamental mismatch in validation task design, and whether these gains transfer to other base architectures or taxonomic groups beyond birds.
