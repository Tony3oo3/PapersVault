## Problem

Existing variational incomplete multi-view clustering (IMVC) methods, such as DVIMC, aggregate view-specific posteriors under a strong conditional independence assumption (Product of Experts), which underestimates posterior variance and ignores the inherently correlated structure among views. While the supervised CoDE framework shows that modeling inter-expert error correlations is beneficial, it relies on a fixed scalar correlation tuned via grid search, making it inapplicable to unsupervised IMVC where labels are unavailable and pairwise dependencies vary across views.

## Input / Output

The method consumes incomplete multi-view data where each sample provides a variable subset of views (governed by a per-sample binary availability mask), with view dimensions ranging from 6 to 1984 depending on the dataset. It produces a shared D-dimensional latent representation z and a discrete cluster assignment c per sample. The view-availability mask directly modulates the aggregation precision matrices (A_M, B_M) via Hadamard products, so that missing views contribute zero evidence rather than being imputed, and the learned correlation matrix R (V×V per latent dimension) is shared across all samples and dimensions.

## Method

ACOVA models inter-view dependencies by treating each view's posterior mean as an estimate of the shared latent variable with a correlated estimation error ε ~ N(0, Σ), where the error covariance is decomposed as Σ^d = DRD: D holds view-specific standard deviations and R is a full V×V correlation matrix. R is parameterized through a learnable lower-triangular matrix L with exponentiated diagonal entries, normalized to unit diagonal via R_ij = (LL^T)_ij / √((LL^T)_ii (LL^T)_jj), guaranteeing positive definiteness and bounded off-diagonal entries in [-1,1]. The aggregated posterior is q(z|{x_v}) ~ N(A_M^{-1} B_M, A_M^{-1}), where A_M and B_M are computed from the masked inverse covariance (Σ^{-1} ⊙ MM^T), so that only observed views contribute evidence and correlated errors are not double-counted as independent. The entire model—including R—is trained end-to-end by maximizing an ELBO (reconstruction + Gaussian-mixture-prior KL) plus a distribution-alignment loss that penalizes divergence between the aggregated posterior and each view-specific posterior. When R = I the method reduces to DVIMC's independence assumption; when R has uniform off-diagonal ρ it reduces to CoDE's fixed-correlation model, making ACOVA a strict generalization that adds only 3–21 learnable parameters over the DVIMC backbone.

## Results

ACOVA consistently achieves the best or second-best clustering accuracy across all six IMVC benchmarks and four missing-view rates (10%–70%). At the challenging 50% missing rate, ACOVA reaches ACC of 0.9146 on Handwritten, 0.8953 on Caltech5V, 0.8793 on Fashion, and 0.4710 on Scene15, outperforming the strongest variational baseline DVIMC by 1.5–3.5 percentage points in ACC. The gains are most pronounced under high missingness (70%), where ACOVA maintains ACC of 0.8999 on Handwritten and 0.8660 on Caltech5V versus 0.7808 and 0.8514 for DVIMC, respectively. These improvements come with only 3–21 additional learnable parameters relative to the DVIMC backbone (which has 6M–16M parameters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACOVA outperforms the closest variational baseline DVIMC (which uses Product-of-Experts with an independence assumption) on all four fully reported datasets at every missing rate, with ACC gains of 1.5–3.5 points at 50% missingness (e.g., 0.9146 vs. 0.8980 on Handwritten, 0.8953 vs. 0.8609 on Caltech5V). Against MVP (cyclic-permutation VAE), ACOVA wins on Scene15 (0.4710 vs. 0.4564 at 50%) and Fashion (0.8793 vs. 0.7982 at 50%) but is slightly behind on Caltech5V at 70% (0.8660 vs. 0.7677, where ACOVA actually wins). PMIMC (prototype-based imputation) is consistently outperformed by ACOVA, with the largest gap on Handwritten at 70% missing (0.8999 vs. 0.8522, +4.77 points). Imputation-based methods (BSV, CONCAT, DCP) and graph-based methods (CPSPAN) are substantially outperformed across all settings, with DCP being the strongest non-variational baseline but still trailing ACOVA by 5–15 ACC points on most datasets. The ablation confirms that removing the learned correlation (reducing to DVIMC) causes consistent drops of 1–12 ACC points, and fixed scalar correlations (ρ=0.1–0.9) are less robust than the adaptive learned R, particularly at higher missing rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACOVA extends the variational IMVC framework of DVIMC (Xu et al., 2024), which aggregates view-specific posteriors via Product of Experts under a conditional independence assumption. It also builds on the consensus-of-experts formulation of Winkler (1981) and its recent supervised instantiation CoDE (Mancisidor et al., 2025), which showed that modeling inter-expert estimation-error correlations improves aggregation. The key departure is replacing CoDE's fixed scalar correlation (tuned via supervised grid search) with a full learnable correlation matrix parameterized through a normalized Cholesky decomposition, enabling end-to-end unsupervised optimization of heterogeneous pairwise view dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The independence assumption in PoE-based aggregation is known to underestimate posterior variance, a problem that is amplified in incomplete settings where fewer views are available; ACOVA addresses this with a principled, minimally-parameterized fix (3–21 extra parameters) that consistently improves clustering across six benchmarks. The framework is general and can serve as a drop-in replacement for PoE or Mixture of Experts in other variational aggregation settings (GANs, LLM evaluation, diffusion policies), as the authors note. Open questions include whether the shared correlation structure across all latent dimensions is appropriate for high-dimensional or highly heterogeneous views, and how the method scales when the number of views grows substantially beyond the V < 10 regime tested here.
