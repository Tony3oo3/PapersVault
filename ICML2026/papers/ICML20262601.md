## Problem

Low-rank RNNs (lrRNNs) capture both low-dimensional latent dynamics and structured connectivity from neural recordings, but their SVD-based latent components are merely orthogonal, not statistically independent, making it difficult to assign distinct computational roles to different latent dimensions. Existing independence-enforcing methods (ICA, β-TCVAE) assume dimension-wise independence—too restrictive for multi-dimensional features like texture—and are not dynamical models that infer recurrent connectivity. The paper asks how to obtain group-wise independent latent dynamics within a generative RNN that simultaneously yields interpretable sub-circuit connectivity.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of N-dimensional neural activity vectors (spike rates or voltage imaging frames) over T time bins, optionally augmented with D-dimensional external inputs (stimuli, behavioral variables) mapped through a learnable linear projection U. It produces a K-dimensional latent trajectory z(t) partitioned into G groups of rank H_g each, a rank-K connectivity matrix W = AB, and G rank-H_g sub-connectivity matrices W_g that each represent an independent neural sub-circuit. The group specification (G, H) must be pre-set, though the framework can automatically suppress dummy dimensions within over-specified groups.

## Method

FacRNN reformulates the lrRNN under a VAE framework with a nonlinear encoder (history convolution ψ over L past time steps plus activation σ) and a linear decoder, yielding a generative model where the rank-K connectivity is W = AB and the latent dynamics follow z(t) = B Σψ_l σ(Az(t−l) + c + Uη(t−l)) + d + ε. The key innovation is a partial correlation (PC) penalty on the aggregated posterior that enforces group-wise independence—p(z) = ∏_g p(z_g)—while permitting arbitrary within-group entanglement, generalizing both standard lrRNN (G=1, PC≡0) and full ICA-like disentanglement (G=K). For the linear special case, an alternative block-diagonal constraint on the latent recurrence matrix J = BA (bdRNN) provides a simpler inference route. The total training objective is the standard ELBO minus β·PC, optimized end-to-end so that the factorization inductive bias shapes both encoder and decoder during learning, and the resulting W decomposes as W = Σ_g A_{:,g} B_{g,:} into interpretable rank-H_g sub-connectivities.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset (two independent rank-3 latent groups), FacRNN achieves a partial correlation (PC) of ≈0.13, close to the ground-truth PC of ≈0.1, while standard lrRNN remains at ≈0.86, confirming successful recovery of group-wise independent dynamics. FacRNN also attains the highest latent R² and the best sub-connectivity match to ground truth, while all methods achieve similar reconstruction R² (~0.85) and connectivity correlation (~0.85). On monkey M1 (K=4, two rank-2 groups), FacRNN reaches an alignment R² of ≈0.8 with hand-movement x/y coordinates, substantially outperforming lrRNN, LINT, and SMC. On mouse dorsal cortex voltage imaging, FacRNN with (G,H)=(6,2) reduces between-group total correlation to 0.110 (vs. 0.159 for (G,H)=(6,1)) while maintaining high reconstruction, and isolates stimulus-locked oscillations into a single interpretable sub-circuit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard lrRNN, FacRNN dramatically reduces the partial correlation from ≈0.86 to ≈0.13 on the synthetic dataset and achieves the highest latent R² and sub-connectivity accuracy, while reconstruction and overall connectivity estimates remain comparable (~0.85). Against LINT (SVD-parameterized lrRNN), FacRNN wins on all latent-structure metrics (PC, latent R², sub-connectivity match) and on monkey M1 alignment R² (≈0.8 vs. ~0.5–0.6 for K=4 LINT); LINT's orthogonal SVD components do not correspond to independent dynamical groups. Against SMC (Pals et al., 2024), which uses a more sophisticated sequential Monte Carlo inference pipeline, FacRNN achieves higher alignment R² on monkey M1 K=4; notably, SMC performs even worse than LINT in the absence of direction labels, suggesting that its gains in the original paper relied on task-specific supervision. FacRNN also outperforms post-hoc ICA applied to lrRNN latents (lrRNN+ICA), demonstrating that end-to-end factorization during training is more effective than imposing independence after the latent representation has already been learned. The one setting where FacRNN is outperformed is when the true structure is fully rank-1 per group: FacRNN-full (G=K, H=1) achieves slightly better PC than FacRNN (G=2,H=3) on the synthetic data, but this comes at the cost of losing within-group expressiveness, as confirmed by the monkey M1 results where rank-1 groups fail to capture x/y movement dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the low-rank RNN (lrRNN) framework of Mastrogiuseppe & Ostojic (2018) and the SVD-based LINT parameterization of Valente et al. (2022) by reformulating them under a variational autoencoder (VAE) objective, which naturally exposes the latent posterior to factorization constraints. The key departure from prior disentanglement methods (ICA, β-TCVAE, FactorVAE) is the relaxation from dimension-wise to group-wise independence: each latent group can be a multi-dimensional subspace with rich internal coupling, while groups remain marginally independent. The partial correlation penalty is adapted from Li et al. (2025), and the block-diagonal RNN (bdRNN) variant draws on Jordan normal form theory to handle non-diagonalizable within-group dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FacRNN provides a principled way to decompose low-rank neural connectivity into interpretable sub-circuits (W_g), each associated with an independent source of task-relevant activity, which is essential for circuit-level hypotheses in motor and sensory cortex. Key limitations include the need to pre-specify group number and size (G, H_g), the restriction of connectivity interpretability to linear encoders/decoders, and the absence of external task inputs in the real-data experiments. Open questions include automatic group-structure selection, extending interpretability to nonlinear encoder/decoder architectures, and validating the framework on larger-scale recordings with richer behavioral paradigms.
