## Problem

DPO and RLHF-style preference optimization implicitly rely on Donsker-Varadhan (DV)/MINE mutual information estimators, which the paper proves suffer from a gradient-starvation failure mode: when the base model assigns near-zero probability to a response, the DV/MINE surrogate provides vanishing or misleading policy gradients, causing synchronous collapse of both chosen and rejected likelihoods in late-stage training. The paper asks whether a targeted MI-estimator substitution can eliminate this bottleneck while preserving the contrastive structure of preference optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263102/cropped_page5_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a preference triple (prompt x, chosen response y⁺, rejected response y⁻) together with a frozen reference model π_ref, and outputs an aligned policy π_θ. No separate reward model or neural critic is trained; instead the critic is restricted to the log-ratio family T_θ(x,y) = β(log π_θ(y|x) − log π_ref(y|x)), so the entire objective is computed from two forward passes (policy and reference) per preference pair. The loss is evaluated per pair (M = N = 1) with token-level log-probability normalization to mitigate length bias.

## Method

The paper first formalizes RLHF reward learning, DPO, and KL-regularized PPO as contrastive mutual-information surrogates built on the DV/MINE lower bound, then proves two theorems showing that the DV/MINE estimator yields exactly zero (or linearly decaying) directional gradients along ∇_θ log π_θ(y⋆|x⋆) when π_ref(y⋆|x⋆) → 0. To overcome this, MIO replaces the DV/MINE surrogate with the Jensen-Shannon (JSD) MI estimator, yielding a closed-form pairwise loss ℓ_MIO = log[1 + (π_ref(y⁺|x)/π_θ(y⁺|x))^β] + ½ log[1 + (π_θ(y⁺|x)/π_ref(y⁺|x))^β] + ½ log[1 + (π_θ(y⁻|x)/π_ref(y⁻|x))^β]. Differentiating this loss reveals two stabilizing properties absent in DPO: (i) the rejected-response gradient remains unbounded (forcefully suppressing y⁻) while the chosen-response gradient stays bounded, preventing the synchronous collapse; and (ii) a self-regulating clamp at σ⁺ = 2/3 automatically redirects optimization effort from over-learned positives to harder ones, acting as an implicit curriculum.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, MIO achieves the best score across all eight math/reasoning benchmarks (e.g., 75.39 on GSM8K, 51.36 on MATH-Hard, 40.34 on MuSR), outperforming DPO (73.46, 50.08, 39.55) and all other baselines. On LLaMA3-8B-SFT, MIO ranks first in 7 of 8 benchmarks (e.g., 56.86 GSM8K vs. DPO's 54.38; 5.44 MATH-Hard vs. DPO's 4.23), and on Mistral-7B-SFT it leads on 5 of 8. On broader alignment, MIO yields a +7.3 absolute gain on Arena-Hard (23.6 vs. DPO's 16.3, a +44.7% relative increase) and +4.9 on AlpacaEval 2 (21.2 vs. 16.3), while matching DPO on MT-Bench (7.3). In online preference optimization on TL;DR, Online MIO achieves 35.5 win rate and 4.01 quality score versus 30.1/3.92 for Online DPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIO consistently outperforms DPO across all three model families and all eight math/reasoning benchmarks; the largest margins appear on AQuA-RAT (e.g., 30.16 vs. 22.83 on Qwen) and MATH-Hard (51.36 vs. 50.08 on Qwen; 5.44 vs. 4.23 on LLaMA3). Against SimPO, MIO wins on 7/8 benchmarks for LLaMA3 and all 8 for Qwen, with the clearest gap on AQuA-RAT (30.16 vs. 25.98) and MATH-Hard (51.36 vs. 38.44 on Qwen). KTO is competitive on GSM8K (75.08 vs. MIO's 75.39 on Qwen) but trails MIO on most other benchmarks, particularly MultiMedQA (52.86 vs. 57.61) and AQuA-RAT (20.08 vs. 30.16). In the PPO comparison on CNN/DailyMail summarization, MIO achieves 0.38 GPT-4 win rate at temperature 0 versus 0.26 for PPO and 0.36 for DPO. In online preference optimization, Online MIO (35.5 win rate, 4.01 quality) surpasses both Online DPO (30.1, 3.92) and Online IPO (28.9, 3.86). The one regime where MIO underperforms is under 10% label noise on MuSR, where MIO drops to 36.0 versus DPO's 36.7 (baseline 38.5), indicating MIO's stronger signal extraction makes it more sensitive to noisy preferences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper makes the previously hinted-at connection between DPO/RLHF and contrastive learning (noted by Jaiswal et al., Xu et al., Chen et al.) precise by showing that DPO and KL-regularized RLHF are specific instantiations of the Donsker-Varadhan/MINE mutual information lower bound with a log-ratio critic. It then departs from prior work by identifying a concrete gradient-starvation failure mode inherent to the DV estimator when π_ref(y|x)→0, and replacing it with a Jensen-Shannon divergence-based MI estimator (MIO). The analysis also reinterprets PPO's ratio clipping as a natural consequence of bounding the Jensen approximation error, linking the RLHF and contrastive-learning literatures more tightly than prior work had.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient-starvation theorem provides a mechanistic explanation for the well-observed late-stage chosen/rejected likelihood collapse in DPO and for why RLVR struggles to elicit reasoning beyond the base model's sampling support. MIO's consistent (if moderate) gains across 8+ math/reasoning benchmarks and its avoidance of synchronous collapse make it a practical drop-in replacement for DPO. However, the paper honestly notes that MIO is more sensitive to label noise than DPO, and the β hyperparameter is not directly comparable to DPO's, limiting straightforward transfer of tuning knowledge. Open questions include whether other MI estimators (e.g., InfoNCE variants, f-divergence bounds) offer further improvements, and whether the theoretical framework extends to multi-turn or verifier-based RL settings.
