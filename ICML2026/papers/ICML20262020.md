## Problem

When distilling long-form reasoning from multiple diverse teacher models into a smaller student, the natural criterion of selecting responses the student assigns highest global probability (GALP/GRAPE) produces systematically wrong rankings: teachers whose traces score highest under the student yield the worst downstream accuracy after fine-tuning. The paper identifies the root cause as a granularity mismatch—students generalize by recombining reusable local reasoning steps rather than retrieving whole trajectories, so full-trajectory scoring optimizes the wrong target.

## Input / Output

Given a prompt and a pool of answer-correct candidate responses from multiple teacher models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, QwQ-32B, Qwen3-32B), the method uses a pre-trained student model to score each candidate and select the best one for supervised fine-tuning. Step segmentation is performed externally by GLM-4.5-Air, and scoring requires p forward passes per response (one per step under its local window) rather than a single pass as in GALP. The output supports two use cases: a coarse teacher ranking computed on as few as 200 prompts, and fine-grained per-prompt response selection from a mixed-teacher pool of 817 LIMO prompts.

## Method

LALP (Local Average Log Probability) segments each candidate response into p reasoning steps, then scores each step by its token-average log probability under the student model conditioned only on the prompt and k immediately preceding steps (a small local window, typically 5–25% of available prefix). The final LALP score is the uniform average across all step scores, enforcing two properties absent in GALP: equal step weighting (verbosity does not inflate the score) and local conditioning (each step is evaluated for standalone plausibility given its immediate premises, not for global document fluency). The paper provides a toy theoretical model showing that when training support is dense only for local contexts, the learner acquires signal there but collapses to an uninformative prior for non-local contexts, making global scoring unreliable while local scoring remains informative. Empirically, LALP correctly ranks teachers (QwQ > DeepSeek-R1 > Qwen3) where GALP inverts the ordering, and improves per-prompt selection accuracy by up to 9.4% over GALP on the 32B student, with gains transferring to science and code benchmarks.

## Results

On per-prompt response selection from a mixed-teacher pool (DeepSeek-R1, QwQ-32B, Qwen3-32B), LALP achieves 0.726 average accuracy on Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, outperforming GALP (0.632) by +9.4 points and Random (0.651) by +7.5 points. On the 7B student, LALP reaches 0.440 vs. GALP's 0.412. Cross-domain transfer confirms the gains are not math-specific: LALP improves over GALP by +9.1 points on GPQA-Diamond (science) and +2.9 points on LiveCodeBench-Hard (code). For teacher selection, LALP correctly ranks QwQ-32B > DeepSeek-R1 > Qwen3-32B by downstream utility, while GALP produces the exact opposite ordering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole named baseline is GALP (Global Average Log Probability) from GRAPE (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2025), which scores each candidate response by its token-average log probability under the student model over the full context. LALP decisively outperforms GALP in the long, mixed-teacher regime: +9.4 points on the 32B student (0.726 vs. 0.632) and +2.8 points on the 7B student (0.440 vs. 0.412). Strikingly, GALP actually underperforms random selection on the 32B student (0.632 vs. 0.651), indicating that global likelihood is not merely noisy but systematically misranked when teachers differ in verbosity and style. LALP-selected data (a mixture drawn from all three teachers) even surpasses training on the single best teacher alone (QwQ-32B: 0.719), demonstrating that step-aware selection can exploit complementary strengths across teachers. The paper also includes a 'Local Lowest' sanity check, which consistently underperforms both GALP and LALP, confirming that the direction of local scoring matters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRAPE (Zhang et al., 2025), which proposed selecting SFT data by the student's global average log probability (GALP) to favor responses 'natural' to the student's pretrained distribution. The key departure is identifying a critical failure mode: in the long, multi-teacher reasoning regime, GALP systematically misranks candidates due to a granularity mismatch between trajectory-level fluency and step-level learnability. The paper also draws on the locality-of-experience account of reasoning (Prystawski et al., 2023) to formalize why local support density, not global trajectory probability, governs what a student can actually learn, and positions LALP as a step-level refinement within the broader knowledge distillation and model-aware data selection literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners doing reasoning distillation from multiple teachers, the finding that global likelihood inverts the correct teacher ranking is a practical warning: the intuitive 'select what's natural to the student' heuristic can actively harm performance in the increasingly common long, heterogeneous-teacher setting. LALP offers a simple, one-time-preprocessing fix that requires no auxiliary models or training modifications, and its gains transfer across math, science, and code. Open questions include whether LALP can serve as a step-level reward signal in RL (the authors note this risks reinforcing plausible-but-incorrect reasoning), whether hybrid local-global scoring can address the inability to verify long-range consistency, and whether the step-compositional generalization hypothesis holds for non-autoregressive or non-text modalities.
