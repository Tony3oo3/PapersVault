## Problem

Existing EEG-to-fMRI translation methods rely on region-specific models, restrict reconstruction to cortical surfaces, and treat the EEG-fMRI relationship as uniform across individuals, ignoring inter-subject variability driven by demographics and dynamic physiological states such as vigilance. This is especially problematic for eyes-closed resting-state recordings, where spontaneous shifts in arousal introduce substantial non-stationarity in both modalities. As a result, prior approaches lack generalizability across subjects, datasets, and experimental conditions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 16-second EEG window (23 channels, resampled to 200 Hz) preceding each fMRI frame, corresponding to the hemodynamic response latency, augmented with learnable prefix prompt tokens encoding dataset identity, subject age, sex, and a per-frame vigilance class (drowsy/intermediate/alert). Output is a 512-dimensional ROI activity vector per frame using the DiFuMo parcellation, enabling frame-by-frame generation of arbitrarily long whole-brain fMRI time series covering cortical, subcortical, and cerebellar regions. The frame-wise design decouples temporal length from model architecture, and functional connectivity matrices can be derived post-hoc from the reconstructed ROI time courses.

## Method

UniEFS employs a two-stage strategy. In Stage 1 (f-MSM), a transformer encoder-decoder is pretrained on ~720K unpaired HCP fMRI frames via masked signal modeling at 50% mask ratio, where the encoder sees only visible ROI tokens and the decoder reconstructs the full 512-ROI vector, learning population-level spatial priors; the model is then fine-tuned on the paired dataset's fMRI to bridge domain gaps. In Stage 2, a context-aware EEG encoder (combining a LaBraM-based spatiotemporal module and multi-scale spectral transformer, adapted from NeuroBOLT) processes EEG patches, with prefix prompt tokens prepended to condition on metadata. A LoRA-inspired low-rank projection (matrices B and A) aligns the EEG latent with the frozen fMRI encoder's embedding space, and the pretrained decoder reconstructs the fMRI frame. Training jointly optimizes an alignment loss (MSE between low-rank EEG and fMRI embeddings) and a reconstruction loss (weighted combination of MSE and spatial correlation), with the fMRI encoder and decoder kept frozen to preserve learned spatial structure.

## Results

On eyes-closed resting-state EEG-to-fMRI reconstruction, UniEFS achieves a full-brain temporal correlation (FB TCorr) of 0.367±0.052, gray-matter TCorr of 0.394±0.060, subcortical TCorr of 0.276±0.082, and cerebellar TCorr of 0.247±0.060, consistently outperforming all EEG-to-fMRI baselines on regional time-course reconstruction. For functional connectivity, UniEFS attains a Conn Corr of 0.527±0.084 and Conn MSE of 0.233±0.072, placing it second-best behind Li et al. (2024a) (Conn Corr 0.535, Conn MSE 0.217). In zero-shot rest-to-task transfer, the model preserves subject-specific FC signatures with 90% connectome fingerprinting accuracy on full-brain and subcortical matrices, and in a zero-shot Parkinson's EEG evaluation, the learned latent space yields 75% PD-vs-HC classification accuracy via linear probing without any fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three dedicated EEG-to-fMRI baselines, UniEFS wins on all four regional TCorr metrics and Conn MSE, with the largest margin over BEIRA (FB TCorr 0.367 vs. 0.171) and a clear advantage over NeuroBOLT (FB TCorr 0.367 vs. 0.331; Conn MSE 0.233 vs. 0.349). The sole exception is Li et al. (2024a), which edges out UniEFS on Conn Corr (0.535 vs. 0.527) and Conn MSE (0.217 vs. 0.233), though UniEFS still leads on every temporal-correlation metric. Among general-purpose EEG encoders (Table 4), UniEFS outperforms CBraMod on FB TCorr (0.367 vs. 0.349) and GM TCorr (0.394 vs. 0.369), while CBraMod slightly leads on CB TCorr (0.257 vs. 0.247); both are closely matched on SC TCorr (0.276) and Conn MSE (~0.233). LaBraM, BIOT, CNNTransformer, STTransformer, and FFCL all trail UniEFS by a wider margin on FB TCorr (0.091–0.319) and Conn Corr (0.432–0.518). In the zero-shot Parkinson's linear-probing evaluation, UniEFS with context tokens achieves the best PD AUC (0.87) and accuracy (0.75), surpassing CBraMod (AUC 0.71) and LaBraM (AUC 0.86), confirming that the fMRI-informed pretraining and context prompts add discriminative value beyond generic EEG foundation models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EEG-to-fMRI synthesis line initiated by NeuroBOLT (Li et al., 2024b), which was the first to address eyes-closed resting-state translation but required separate region-specific models, and by CATD (Yao et al., 2025), which generated cortical surface maps via diffusion but excluded subcortical regions. The key architectural departure is a two-stage strategy: a masked-signal-modeling pretraining phase (adapted from the MAE paradigm of He et al., 2022) on large-scale unpaired fMRI to learn population-level spatial priors, followed by cross-modal alignment using a LoRA-inspired low-rank projection (Hu et al., 2022) and a LaBraM-based EEG encoder. The introduction of learnable prefix prompt tokens encoding dataset, age, sex, and vigilance metadata represents a novel conditioning mechanism not present in prior EEG-to-fMRI work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling full-brain fMRI reconstruction from a single lightweight EEG recording, UniEFS points toward democratizing high-resolution brain monitoring in settings where fMRI is inaccessible (outpatient, pediatric, implant-contraindicated populations). The zero-shot transfer to task-based fMRI and the spontaneous disease-discriminative structure in the Parkinson's EEG latent space suggest the learned representations may serve as a general-purpose feature backbone for EEG-only clinical studies. However, the paired training data is very small (39 scans, 23 channels), the clinical evaluation is preliminary (44 subjects, single disease), and generalization to denser electrode arrays, cognitively demanding tasks, and larger diverse cohorts remains an open question that will determine real-world utility.
