## Problem

While weight initialization is known to shift single-task training between lazy (kernel) and rich (feature-learning) regimes, it remains unclear how initialization choices during pretraining shape the inductive bias of subsequent fine-tuning. In particular, no prior work provided an end-to-end analytical characterization of how the absolute scale, relative scale across layers, and readout re-initialization jointly determine whether a network reuses, refines, or ignores pretrained features during fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The theory takes three initialization parameters (absolute pretraining scale c_PT, relative layer-scale imbalance λ_PT ∈ [-1,1], and fine-tuning readout scale γ_FT), three task-structure parameters (pretraining sparsity ρ_PT, shared-dimension fraction ρ_FT^shared, and new-dimension fraction ρ_FT^new), and the fine-tuning data load α_FT as inputs, and produces an exact closed-form generalization error E = ‖β*_FT − β̂_FT‖²₂ in the high-dimensional limit D→∞. The output also includes a regime classification via two scalar metrics—ℓ-order (sparsity bias, ranging 1 to 2) and pretraining dependence PD (ranging -1 to 0)—that jointly locate the network within a four-regime phase portrait. Unlike standard empirical pipelines, the 'input' here is a set of hyperparameters and task statistics rather than raw data, and the 'output' is an analytical prediction validated against both replica simulations and trained networks.

## Method

The paper derives the implicit bias of gradient-flow training on diagonal linear networks in a two-stage PT+FT pipeline, showing (Theorem 4.1) that the fine-tuning solution minimizes a dimension-wise penalty q_k(β) whose parameter k_d depends non-trivially on both the initialization parameters and the pretrained feature magnitude β̂_PT,d. This pretraining-dependent penalty is then plugged into a replica-theoretic fixed-point framework (Proposition 4.2, building on Bereyhi & Müller 2018) to obtain exact generalization error curves as a function of all initialization and task parameters. The key structural insight is a universal trade-off (Proposition 5.1): ℓ-order + PD ∈ [1,2], meaning the network cannot simultaneously achieve maximal sparsity bias (ℓ-order=1) and maximal pretraining dependence (PD=-1). This trade-off delineates four regimes—rich/pretraining-independent, lazy/pretraining-dependent, lazy/pretraining-independent, and an intermediate rich/pretraining-dependent regime—and identifies the relative layer-scale λ_PT as the primary lever for navigating between them. Empirical validation on ResNet-18 (CIFAR-100, SVHN) and one-hidden-layer Transformers (modular arithmetic) confirms that smaller relative scales in early layers induce the predicted rich, pretraining-dependent regime, improving fine-tuning generalization when the target task relies on a subset of pretrained features.

## Results

The paper derives exact replica-theoretic expressions for the generalization error of diagonal linear networks under PT+FT, identifying four distinct learning regimes governed by the interplay of ℓ-order (sparsity bias) and pretraining dependence (PD). A key theoretical result (Proposition 5.1) establishes a universal trade-off: ℓ-order + PD ∈ [1,2], making it impossible to simultaneously achieve maximal sparsity (ℓ-order=1) and maximal pretraining dependence (PD=−1). Empirically, on ResNet-18 pretrained on 98 CIFAR-100 classes and fine-tuned on 2, a smaller relative initialization scale (κ<1, corresponding to negative λ_PT) consistently improves fine-tuning generalization at intermediate sample sizes. The same trend holds on SVHN (lower task overlap) and on one-hidden-layer Transformers fine-tuned on modular addition, where smaller embedding balancedness yields lower validation error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods but instead compares different initialization parameter settings (i.e., different learning regimes) against each other. Across three task structures (no overlap, full overlap, and subset overlap), the optimal regime shifts: the rich pretraining-independent regime (I) is best when FT and PT share no dimensions, the lazy pretraining-dependent regime (II) is best under full overlap, and the intermediate rich pretraining-dependent regime (IV) is best when FT dimensions are a strict subset of PT dimensions (Fig. 6). On CIFAR-100, decreasing κ (negative relative scale) improves fine-tuning accuracy, while increasing c_PT or γ_FT degrades it, confirming the theoretical prediction that smaller early-layer scales enable both feature reuse and refinement. The c_FT global rescaling heuristic from Lippl & Lindsey (2024) produces the same representation-level signature (PR compression with high ENSD) as the targeted λ_PT intervention, but the paper shows that λ_PT alone is sufficient and more interpretable. On SVHN (reduced task overlap), the benefit of smaller κ persists but is attenuated, consistent with the theory's prediction that pretraining dependence matters less when overlap is low.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diagonal linear network analysis of Lippl & Lindsey (2024), which characterized the inductive bias only in the limit c_PT → 0 with λ_PT = 0 and γ_FT = 0, to the full spectrum of initialization parameters (c_PT, λ_PT, γ_FT). It builds on the conservation-law framework of Azulay et al. (2021) for tracking how initialization shape is preserved through training, and applies the replica-theory machinery of Bereyhi & Müller (2018) to derive closed-form generalization error. The key conceptual departure is identifying a universal trade-off (ℓ-order + PD ∈ [1,2]) that constrains the joint space of sparsity bias and pretraining dependence, revealing a fourth intermediate regime (rich, pretraining-dependent) that prior single-limit analyses missed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first end-to-end analytical characterization of how weight initialization shapes fine-tuning generalization, identifying the relative scale across layers (λ_PT) as a principled lever for balancing feature reuse against new feature learning. The universal trade-off between sparsity and pretraining dependence is a fundamental constraint that no initialization can circumvent, suggesting that the 'ideal' regime of simultaneously maximizing both is impossible. Limitations include the theory being restricted to two-layer diagonal linear networks and empirical validation on small-scale tasks (2-class CIFAR-100 fine-tuning, modular arithmetic), leaving open whether the identified regimes and trade-offs persist in deeper, nonlinear architectures with more complex feature overlap. Future work on continual learning, explicit regularization design, and extension to nonlinear networks would strengthen the practical impact.
