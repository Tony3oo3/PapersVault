## Problem

Transformer-based operator learning methods represent continuous fields as discrete tokens and compute attention via pairwise softmax affinities, which scales quadratically with resolution, ignores the global functional structure of the underlying problem, and lacks principled consistency across different discretizations. The paper asks whether attention can be reinterpreted not as point-to-point token matching but as a compact linear operator between learned function spaces, drawing on the functional maps framework from geometry processing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265128/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes discretized samples of continuous fields (n points on point clouds, structured/unstructured grids, or 1D domains) and produces output fields at the same or different resolutions. A key property is resolution invariance: models trained on 2048-point grids generalize zero-shot to 8192-point grids, and the learned basis adapts to arbitrary domain geometries including non-rectangular shapes with re-entrant corners. For the few-shot regression task, input is a small support set of (x, y) pairs and output is function values at arbitrary query locations.

## Method

FUNCATTN replaces the n×n softmax attention matrix with a compact k×k transport operator C estimated via Tikhonov-regularized least squares in a learned spectral space. Specifically, adaptive basis functions Φ and Ψ (k columns each) are constructed as softmax projections of the input through a linear layer, generalizing classical P0 piecewise-constant elements; Q, K, V are projected into spectral coefficients via transpose projection; and C is computed in closed form as Q̃K̃ᵀ(K̃K̃ᵀ+λI)⁻¹. The output is reconstructed as ΦCṼ, yielding O(ndk) complexity linear in context length. The paper proves local Lipschitz continuity of the layer with a bound controlled by λ, shows Intention is recovered as a special case under orthonormal full-rank bases, and establishes that FUNCATTN approximates a learnable integral operator on the domain.

## Results

On six PDE benchmarks (Elasticity, Airfoil, Darcy, Pipe, Navier-Stokes, Plasticity), FUNCATTN achieves the best relative L2 error on five out of six tasks, with headline values of 0.50% (Elasticity), 0.43% (Airfoil), 0.42% (Darcy), 0.29% (Pipe), 8.00% (Navier-Stokes), and 0.11% (Plasticity). On RNA 3D segmentation, it reaches 89.0% accuracy, surpassing Transolver (87.5%) and DiffusionNet (85.1%). On OOD AirfRANS generalization, it achieves 23.4% relative lift error (OOD Reynolds) and 13.3% (OOD Angles), outperforming the closest competitor by 8.8% and 9.5% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUNCATTN outperforms Transolver (the closest attention-based competitor) on 5 of 6 PDE benchmarks, with relative improvements of 6%–26.3% (e.g., 0.50 vs 0.64 on Elasticity, 0.43 vs 0.53 on Airfoil, 8.00 vs 9.44 on Navier-Stokes). The sole exception is Pipe, where LNO's 'physics-cross-attention' achieves 0.25% vs FUNCATTN's 0.29%. On RNA segmentation, FUNCATTN (89.0%) beats Transolver (87.5%), DiffusionNet-xyz (85.1%), and PointNet++ (74.4%). On OOD AirfRANS, it reduces lift-coefficient error by 8.8% (Reynolds) and 9.5% (Angles) over Transolver, the nearest competitor. On the complex-geometry Darcy-Notch task, it achieves 0.64% relative L2 error, a 30.9% improvement over WNO (0.92%) and substantially outperforming grid-based dgFNO+ (7.82%). In few-shot sinusoidal regression, FUNCATTN achieves up to three orders of magnitude lower MSE than vanilla scaled dot-product attention and one order lower than Intention, while also generalizing best across unseen context lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges the functional maps framework from geometry processing (Ovsjanikov et al., 2012), where shape correspondences are represented as compact linear operators on spectral bases, with the attention mechanism in transformers. It departs from standard token-based attention (Vaswani et al., 2017) and fixed-basis spectral operators (FNO, Li et al., 2021) by learning adaptive, data-dependent bases and formulating attention as a Tikhonov-regularized least-squares solve in a compressed k-dimensional spectral space. The closest prior work is Transolver (Wu et al., 2024), which also projects onto learned bases but still applies standard scaled dot-product attention on the resulting tokens; FUNCATTN instead computes the attention operator directly as an optimal linear map between function spaces. The Galerkin Transformer (Cao, 2021) anticipated the functional interpretation of attention but did not specify how to compute the underlying bases or the transport operator in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing n×n pointwise affinities with a compact k×k functional operator, FUNCATTN achieves resolution invariance (zero-shot super-resolution from 2048 to 8192 grid points) and strong OOD generalization on AirfRANS, properties that are difficult for token-based architectures to guarantee. The Lipschitz continuity proof (Proposition 4.5) provides a theoretical stability guarantee tied to the regularization parameter, which is rare for attention-based operator learning methods. Open questions remain: the paper lacks approximation or generalization bounds connecting the compression ratio k/n to reconstruction error, the learned basis is a simple softmax projection whose expressiveness limits are unclear, and it is untested whether this functional perspective transfers to domains without a natural function-space interpretation (e.g., NLP).
