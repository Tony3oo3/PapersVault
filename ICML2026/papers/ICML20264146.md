## Problem

Existing VLA models map perceptual inputs directly to motor commands, lacking explicit mechanisms for multimodal reasoning or anticipating how the environment evolves under action. While recent works introduce textual chain-of-thought or visual subgoal prediction, they fail to provide a unified framework that jointly supports textual reasoning, visual foresight, and action prediction without degrading the underlying VLM's reasoning capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264146/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text instruction and c=3 egocentric RGB observation frames as input, processed through a dual-path visual encoder (SigLIP2 ViT for semantic understanding at up to 980×980 with NaViT native aspect ratios, and a FLUX VAE for spatial generation at 512×1024). It produces three sequential outputs: an autoregressive textual reasoning chain, a diffusion-generated visual subgoal image, and a continuous action chunk (K=16 for simulation, K=50 for real-world) predicted via flow-matching. The generation is strictly sequential—reasoning, then subgoal, then action—enforced by special routing tokens that switch between the three experts.

## Method

HALO employs a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture with three specialized experts (Multimodal Understanding, Visual Generation, Action Prediction), each initialized from Qwen2.5-1.5B (~4.5B total parameters), that collaborate through shared self-attention with a structured masking strategy: causal attention for text, bidirectional within-frame attention for visual tokens, and causal across frames/modalities, with noise tokens isolated to prevent leakage. An automated EM-CoT data pipeline synthesizes training supervision by extracting action primitives from proprioception via rule-based matching, using a VLM (Qwen3-VL) to generate task narratives, decompose trajectories into subtasks with first-person reasoning, and selecting terminal frames as visual subgoals. Training follows a two-stage recipe: versatile pre-training on LLaVA-NeXT-779k (VQA), OXE + SSv2 (visual generation via flow-matching MSE), and OXE (action prediction via L1 flow-matching) with weighted loss 0.25·L_CE + 0.5·L_MSE + L_L1, followed by EM-CoT-augmented fine-tuning that co-trains on synthesized EM-CoT data and general VQA to inject reasoning while preventing catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark (50 bimanual manipulation tasks, 100 trials each), HALO achieves average success rates of 80.7% (Easy/Clean) and 37.6% (Hard/Domain-randomized), surpassing the strongest baseline π₀ (46.4% / 16.3%) by 34.3 and 21.3 percentage points respectively. In real-world experiments on a Mobile ALOHA platform across four long-horizon tasks, HALO achieves a 27.1% average success rate, outperforming both π₀ and π₀.5 under basic and generalization settings. Ablations confirm that EM-CoT contributes an additional 5.4 pp on Easy and 16.4 pp on Hard over the pre-trained foundation without explicit reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HALO decisively outperforms all four baselines on RoboTwin 2.0. Against π₀ (the strongest baseline), HALO gains 34.3 pp on Easy and 21.3 pp on Hard, with the gap widening under domain randomization (relative improvement of 130.7% on Hard). RDT-1B trails HALO by 46.2 pp on Easy and 23.9 pp on Hard, while Diffusion Policy collapses to 0.6% under the Hard setting, indicating severe fragility to visual perturbations. In real-world evaluation, HALO consistently beats both π₀ and π₀.5 across all four tasks, with the advantage most pronounced under generalization conditions (visual distractions, lighting changes, novel objects). The HALO-w/o EM-CoT variant already surpasses π₀ by 28.9 pp on Easy, showing that the versatile pre-training recipe alone provides a strong foundation, but the full EM-CoT pipeline is critical for robustness under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

HALO sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Mixture-of-Transformers architectures (Liang et al., 2024; BAGEL, Deng et al., 2025) that decouple heterogeneous generative capabilities into specialized experts sharing self-attention; (2) flow-based VLA policies like π₀ (Black et al., 2024) that use a separate action expert for continuous action prediction; and (3) chain-of-thought reasoning for robotics (Zawalski et al., 2025; CoT-VLA, Zhao et al., 2025; ManualVLA, Gu et al., 2025), which introduces intermediate reasoning steps before action. The key departure is that prior works address either textual reasoning or visual subgoal prediction in isolation, or couple both within a single autoregressive model (as in ManualVLA), potentially degrading the VLM's understanding capacity. HALO instead uses a MoT architecture to natively support autoregressive text generation, diffusion-based visual generation, and flow-matching action prediction as three distinct experts, and introduces an automated EM-CoT data synthesis pipeline (building on RT-H's action primitives, Belkhale et al., 2024) to provide scalable supervision for all three reasoning stages simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structured multimodal reasoning (textual planning + visual foresight) substantially improves robustness under domain randomization, with a 130.7% relative improvement over π₀ on Hard RoboTwin tasks, suggesting that deliberative reasoning is a viable path toward generalizable manipulation beyond reactive pattern matching. The MoT architecture offers a practical blueprint for combining understanding, generation, and control without the representational conflicts of monolithic models, and the automated EM-CoT pipeline lowers the barrier to scaling embodied reasoning supervision. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly open-world settings beyond curated benchmarks, whether the 4.5B parameter budget is sufficient for more complex long-horizon tasks, and whether the VLM-annotated EM-CoT data introduces systematic biases that limit real-world transfer. The reliance on expert demonstrations (2,500 in simulation, 320 in real-world) also leaves the data-efficiency question open.
