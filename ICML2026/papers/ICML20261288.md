## Problem

Existing multi-token prediction (MTP) methods for accelerating LLM inference rely on training auxiliary decoding heads, modifying base model weights, or maintaining N-gram caches and draft models, introducing significant engineering overhead, memory costs, and limited portability across architectures. Training-free alternatives like Lookahead Decoding and Prompt Lookup Decoding depend on heuristic n-gram matching or fixed window tracking, which underperform on open-ended tasks and require substantial cache memory. The paper asks whether a frozen LLM's latent multi-token prediction ability can be directly elicited through simple embedding-space probing, without any retraining, auxiliary parameters, or external caches.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a frozen autoregressive LLM (LLaMA3 or Qwen3 family) as input. During prefill, k mask tokens—initialized as the mean of the prompt's token embeddings—are appended to the prompt sequence; during decode, these mask tokens are updated via an EMA rule (λ=0.1) incorporating the last generated token's embedding. A single forward pass produces logits at each mask position, from which Top-K candidates are sampled to form a speculative token tree of bounded size (block complexity BC ∈ {10, 30, 60}). The output is lossless generated text: predicted tokens are verified in parallel against the base model's own next-token distribution, and only exact matches are accepted, reducing the number of forward passes by up to 42% compared to standard autoregressive decoding.

## Method

ESP (Embedding-Space Probing) injects k dynamically generated mask tokens into the prompt's embedding space and exploits the observation that decoder layers progressively align mask-token hidden states with true-token representations, enabling the LM head to produce accurate multi-step predictions from a single forward pass. A dynamic token-tree expansion mechanism (Algorithm 1) adaptively grows the tree using Top-1 expansion at each depth and cumulative-probability-based selection of the top B−1 trajectories, avoiding fixed branch configurations and exhaustive grid search. A lightweight pruning heuristic removes consecutive repeated tokens (e.g., a child predicting the same token as its parent), replacing them with the next-best candidate to improve diversity. The paper provides a theoretical guarantee (Lemma 3.1) showing that if the cosine similarity between mask-token and true-token hidden states exceeds a threshold δ*, the greedy next-next token is included in the mask token's Top-K predictions. An efficient GPU implementation caches tree attention masks and updates position indices via uniform offset shifts, yielding 21–26% throughput gains over naive implementations, with the overall approach achieving 7–11% higher acceptance length than Lookahead Decoding and up to 19% higher throughput than the strongest training-free baseline across LLaMA3 and Qwen3 models on SpecBench.

## Results

On SpecBench, ESP achieves the highest average acceptance length (τ) across all four models at both BC=30 and BC=60. At BC=60, ESP reaches τ=1.71 on LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct and τ=1.74 on Qwen3-8B, outperforming the strongest baseline LADE (τ=1.60 and 1.73 respectively) by 7–11%. In terms of wall-clock speedup, ESP delivers 1.38× on LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct and 1.48× on Qwen3-32B at BC=60, exceeding the next-best baseline by up to 13–22%. The method also reduces model forward calls by up to 42% compared to autoregressive decoding at BC=60.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESP outperforms all three training-free baselines across every model and block complexity. Against LADE (Fu et al., 2024), ESP improves τ by 7–11% on LLaMA3 models (e.g., 1.71 vs 1.60 on LLaMA3.1-8B at BC=60) and by 7–8% on Qwen3 models (1.74 vs 1.73 on Qwen3-8B; 1.70 vs 1.69 on Qwen3-32B). Against STAND (Song et al., 2025), ESP gains are larger—13–18% higher τ on Qwen3 models (1.74 vs 1.48 on Qwen3-8B) and 4–5% on LLaMA3 models. Against PLD (Saxena, 2023), ESP leads by 12–33% in τ, with the gap widest on Qwen3-32B (1.70 vs 1.29). In speedup ratio, ESP achieves 1.38× on LLaMA3.1-8B and 1.48× on Qwen3-32B at BC=60, beating LADE by 13–22% and PLD by 12–35%. The only method that surpasses ESP in raw speedup is the training-based EAGLE3 (Li et al., 2026), which reaches 2.61× on LLaMA3.1-8B at BC=60, but requires 200–300 GPU hours of per-model training versus ESP's zero training cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

ESP sits at the intersection of speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023) and mechanistic interpretability (Future Lens, Pal et al., 2023), repurposing the propose-then-verify loop by replacing the draft model with on-the-fly mask-token probing in the frozen model's own embedding space. It departs from training-based MTP methods (MEDUSA, EAGLE) that add auxiliary heads or draft layers, and from other training-free baselines (LADE, PLD, STAND) that rely on n-gram caching or fixed-window trajectory tracking, by requiring no external memory, no retraining, and no architectural modification. The simultaneous verification mechanism follows Lin et al. (2025), while the dynamic tree construction and block-complexity framing are novel contributions that avoid the brittle fixed-branch configurations used in prior tree-based speculative decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of ESP makes it particularly relevant for edge deployment and compute-constrained settings where auxiliary draft models or large n-gram caches are impractical, and the 15–19% throughput gains over the strongest training-free baseline are meaningful in production inference pipelines. The theoretical lemma connecting cosine similarity between mask and true-token hidden states to Top-K acceptance provides a first formal grounding for why probing works, though the paper explicitly leaves open the deeper question of how and why decoder layers inject future-token information into mask-token representations. Open directions include combining ESP with diffusion LLMs (which natively use mask tokens), more principled mask-token design via optimization, and integration with serving frameworks like vLLM; the method also does not yet match the raw speedup of training-based approaches like EAGLE3, which remain the ceiling for maximum throughput.
