## Problem

Existing any-to-any multimodal models are trained from scratch with encoder-decoder or diffusion backbones, preventing them from leveraging the strong priors and scalability of large pretrained decoder-only models. Meanwhile, decoder-only multimodal systems remain largely confined to text and RGB images, relying on modality-specific heads or text-centric pivots that preclude native arbitrary modality-to-modality generation. This paper asks whether a single decoder-only model can support truly symmetric any-to-any generation across diverse 1D and 2D modalities without architectural specialization.

## Input / Output

MODUS accepts any subset of 1–3 conditioning modalities (text, RGB, depth, surface normals, segmentation, edges, grounding boxes, detection boxes, DINOv2/CLIP/ImageBind features in global or spatially-local form) and generates any target modality from the same set. 1D modalities are consumed as discrete token sequences (text tokens, normalized box coordinates in [0,999], or codebook tokens of size 8192 for features), while 2D modalities are encoded via a dual SigLIP-2 ViT semantic feature plus FLUX VAE latent representation. At inference, conditioning tokens are encoded first to populate the KV cache before target tokens are decoded, and generated outputs can be fed back as conditioning inputs for chained multi-step generation.

## Method

MODUS adapts the pretrained BAGEL-7B Mixture-of-Transformers decoder into a unified any-to-any model with two experts sharing a single causal attention context: a 1D Expert that performs autoregressive next-token prediction over discrete modalities, and a 2D Expert that performs flow matching over VAE latents for continuous spatial modalities. Within each modality, 1D tokens use causal attention while 2D tokens use bidirectional attention, but cross-modality attention remains causal so any token conditions all subsequent ones. Two key training innovations stabilize joint optimization: uniform timestep sampling (instead of logit-normal) to prevent modality confusion at early denoising steps where modality boundaries are most ambiguous, and a three-stage curriculum that first trains 1D modalities lacking strong priors, then adds 2D modalities, and finally increases conditioning inputs for multi-condition and chained generation. The model is trained on MODUS-DATASET, a 29M-sample corpus built by aligning pseudo-labels from specialist models (DepthAnything V2, Marigold, Grounded-SAM, Canny, DINOv2, CLIP, ImageBind, GLaMM, ViTDet) on a shared BLIP-3o image base, enabling supervision over arbitrary input-target modality pairs.

## Results

MODUS achieves zero-shot performance across all six evaluated task categories simultaneously: 51.1 on MMMU (VQA), 0.81 on GenEval (text-to-image), 0.285 AbsRel on DIODE (depth), 19.92 on NYUv2 (surface normal), 54.5 on RefCOCOval (grounding), and 77.9/92.5 Top-1/Top-5 on ImageNet (retrieval). It is the only model in the comparison that covers every task without modality-specific heads. On geometric tasks, MODUS substantially outperforms prior any-to-any models: 0.285 DIODE vs. 0.331 (4M-21) and 0.399 (OneDiffusion) for depth, and 19.92 vs. 37.28 (4M-21) and 28.55 (Unified-IO 2) for surface normals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On depth estimation (DIODE), MODUS (0.285) outperforms all prior any-to-any baselines: 4M-21 (0.331), Unified-IO 2 (0.369), and OneDiffusion (0.399), and is competitive with the specialist DepthAnything v2 (0.249). On surface normal estimation (NYUv2), MODUS (19.92) more than halves the error of 4M-21 (37.28) and substantially beats Unified-IO 2 (28.55). On referring expression grounding (RefCOCOval), MODUS (54.5) surpasses the decoder-only Kosmos-2 (52.3) and approaches specialist models like Grounding-DINO-T (50.41). On image retrieval (ImageNet), MODUS (77.9/92.5) matches 4M-21 (78.3/92.4) within 0.4 points. On VQA (MMMU) and text-to-image (GenEval), MODUS (51.1 / 0.81) trails its base model Bagel (53.2 / 0.86) by 2.1 and 0.05 respectively, and is well below GPT-4o (69.1 / 0.84) on VQA, but remains competitive with Janus-Pro (41.0 / 0.80) and OneDiffusion (0.65). The critical differentiator is coverage: MODUS is the only model that solves all six task categories in a single zero-shot pass, whereas every baseline leaves at least two tasks unsupported.

## Relation to Prior Work

MODUS extends the decoder-only Mixture-of-Transformers paradigm established by BAGEL (which unifies text and RGB image generation) to a fully symmetric any-to-any setting across 15 modalities, using a dual-expert design (1D autoregressive + 2D flow-matching) within a single causal decoder. It departs from prior any-to-any systems (4M, Unified-IO, OneDiffusion) that rely on encoder-decoder or diffusion backbones trained from scratch, and from LLM-centric approaches (NExT-GPT, AnyGPT) that route all cross-modal mappings through a textual pivot. The key methodological departure is a training recipe—uniform timestep sampling for flow matching and a staged curriculum—that stabilizes multi-modality joint training and avoids the modality confusion that logit-normal sampling induces when multiple continuous modalities share a single denoising trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single pretrained decoder-only model can be extended to any-to-any generation across geometry, structure, semantics, and learned representations with a modest training budget (~5,664 GH200 GPU-hours), inheriting strong priors rather than learning modality structure from scratch. The openly released 29M-sample MODUS-DATASET and the practical training recipe (uniform timestep sampling, staged curriculum) provide a reusable blueprint for the community to add new modalities without architectural changes. Limitations include modest gaps versus task-specific specialists on individual benchmarks (e.g., depth estimation vs. DepthAnything v2), and the modality set, while broad, remains vision-centric; extending to audio, 3D, or video is identified as future work. The finding that high-level semantic conditioning alone produces systematic geometric hallucinations—mirroring observations in GPT-4o—raises open questions about how much low-level reconstruction signal is fundamentally required for faithful dense prediction.
