## Problem

Existing agentic theorem proving systems rely on task-specific pipelines coupled with extensively trained formal provers, are often closed-source, and require manual redesign to incorporate new capabilities. This paper asks whether a general-purpose coding agent, augmented with a small set of tools via Model Context Protocol (MCP), can serve as a competitive, open, and extensible formal mathematics reasoner without any specialized training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262978/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives formal Lean problem statements (or informal mathematical problems) and autonomously navigates the Lean environment through an iterative write-verify-modify loop, consuming compiler diagnostics and proof-state feedback as structured signals. In collaborative mode, human experts provide localized hints via short inline code comments and edit recursive blueprint documents (DAG-structured lemma decompositions) that guide proving order. Outputs include verified Lean proofs, informal natural-language proofs, blueprint plans, and new Lean definitions/lemmas that extend the formal library.

## Method

Numina-Lean-Agent couples Claude Code (Claude Opus 4.5) with Numina-Lean-MCP, an MCP server exposing four complementary tool categories: Lean-LSP-MCP for kernel interaction (diagnostics, goal states, hover documentation), LeanDex for LLM-optimized semantic theorem retrieval over Mathlib, FLT, and PhysLean, an Informal Prover (Generator–Verifier loop using Gemini-3 Pro with triple-verification and up to 20 refinement iterations), and a Discussion Partner for multi-model deliberation on proof bottlenecks. Unlike fixed multi-agent pipelines (e.g., Ax-Prover's Orchestrator→Prover→Verifier cycle), the agent autonomously determines the proof workflow and tool invocation sequence based on the current proof state, with no predefined loop. A subagent system isolates complex lemmas into fresh, tightly-scoped instances under restricted context budgets to stabilize long-horizon proofs, and a recursive blueprint layer decomposes global goals into verifiable subgoals that are refined in response to Lean compilation feedback. The framework is both model-agnostic (base model swappable without retraining) and coding-agent-agnostic (Claude Code replaceable by OpenCode), with MCP enabling plug-and-play tool extension without architectural redesign.

## Results

On the Putnam 2025 benchmark (12 problems), Numina-Lean-Agent solves all 12/12 problems, matching AxiomProver and surpassing Aristotle (10/12, missing A5 and B4) and Seed-Prover 1.5 (11/12, missing A5). In terms of solving time, Numina-Lean-Agent is notably faster on B1 (55 min vs. 150/540/270 min for Aristotle/Seed-Prover/Axiom) and B5 (88 min vs. 420/240/254 min), though it requires substantially more time on A5 (2040 min) and B6 (797 min). On proof length, Numina-Lean-Agent produces shorter Lean code than AxiomProver and Seed-Prover 1.5 on most problems, with particularly large gains on A3 (422 vs. 927/1089 lines), B1 (328 vs. 849/1179 lines), and B5 (929 vs. 2499/1310 lines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Aristotle, Numina-Lean-Agent solves two additional problems (A5 and B4) that Aristotle fails, but Aristotle's step-based approach yields consistently shorter proofs (e.g., 45 vs. 365 lines on A1, 223 vs. 328 lines on B1). Against Seed-Prover 1.5, Numina-Lean-Agent solves one more problem (A5) and is dramatically faster on B1 (55 vs. 540 min) and B5 (88 vs. 240 min), while also producing substantially shorter proofs on most problems (e.g., 422 vs. 927 lines on A3, 328 vs. 849 lines on B1). Against AxiomProver, both systems achieve a perfect 12/12, but Numina-Lean-Agent is faster on B1 (55 vs. 270 min) and B5 (88 vs. 254 min) and generates shorter proofs on A3 (422 vs. 1089 lines), B1 (328 vs. 1179 lines), and B5 (929 vs. 1310 lines). Overall, Numina-Lean-Agent matches the best closed-source system on solve rate while offering open-source transparency, faster solving on several problems, and more concise formalizations compared to the other agentic baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of training specialized formal provers (via tactic prediction, whole-proof generation, or agentic RL) and instead repurposes a general-purpose coding agent (Claude Code) as the formal mathematics reasoner, augmented with MCP-based tools. It builds on existing infrastructure—Lean-LSP-MCP for Lean kernel interaction, LeanExplore (rebuilt as LeanDex) for semantic theorem retrieval, and informal proving pipelines (Huang & Yang, 2025)—but reorganizes them under an autonomous agent that decides tool invocation rather than following a fixed multi-agent pipeline. The key conceptual departure from systems like Ax-Prover, AxiomProver, and Seed-Prover is the elimination of task-specific trained provers and rigid orchestration loops in favor of a model-agnostic, plug-and-play agent architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a general coding agent with a small set of MCP tools can match closed-source specialized provers on Putnam 2025 (12/12) while remaining open-source and model-swappable, lowering the barrier for the community to build and extend formal reasoning systems. The Brascamp-Lieb formalization (8,000+ lines in under two weeks with human collaboration) shows practical utility beyond benchmarks, including the agent's ability to detect and repair incorrect formal statements. Key open questions include whether proof quality (readability, Mathlib idiomatic style) can be improved alongside correctness, whether long-horizon brittleness can be mitigated without manual decomposition, and the surprising finding that benchmark performance (e.g., DeepSeek > Claude on miniF2F) does not transfer to agentic settings where instruction-following and tool-use robustness dominate.
