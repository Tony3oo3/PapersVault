## Problem

Lagrangian-based safe RL methods for multi-constraint CMDPs (e.g., PPO-Lagrangian, TRPO-Lagrangian) universally collapse all constraint signals into a single mixed cost critic, yet no prior work has theoretically justified or invalidated this design choice. This paper proves that the mixed critic introduces a structural bias in the actor gradient caused by dual-induced target drift: as Lagrange multipliers update, the critic's Bellman target shifts, violating the stationarity condition required for temporal-difference learning and producing a steady-state estimation error.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a state observation from a CMDP environment that provides a reward signal, m cost signals, and m constraint thresholds. Rather than producing a single aggregated cost value, the architecture outputs m+1 separate value estimates (one for reward, one per constraint) plus the current Lagrange multipliers; the actor combines these at readout time as Q̂(a) = Q^r(a) − Σᵢ λᵢ Q^{cᵢ}(a), so the multipliers scale already-computed per-signal estimates rather than being baked into the critic training targets. In the power-system deployment, the state is a 105-dimensional observation vector and the action is a 24-dimensional control vector over CBESS dispatch and PV curtailment.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a dedicated-critic architecture for Lagrangian safe RL, where each signal (reward and each of the m constraints) is estimated by its own critic solving a signal-specific projected Bellman equation A(θ)ω^{x,*} = b^x(θ) that depends only on the policy θ, not on the dual variables λ. The theoretical analysis shows that in a mixed critic, the PBE target ω^{mix,*}(θ,λ) = ω^r(θ) − Σᵢ λᵢ ω^{cᵢ}(θ) shifts by O(β_t) whenever λ updates, adding a dual-induced drift term to the critic error recursion that is absent in the dedicated-critic case; this yields an actor-gradient bias of order β_t/η_t for the mixed critic versus only α_t/η_t for the dedicated critic. Practically, the algorithm (Dedicated-Critic Lagrangian PPO) maintains m+1 critics with separate GAE advantages per signal, computes the Lagrangian advantage as A^Lag = A^r − Σᵢ λᵢ A^{cᵢ} at readout, and updates the dual variables via projected stochastic ascent on per-constraint cost violations. Experiments on a CMDP bandit, a 5-constraint community battery energy storage system, and an EV charging station confirm that the dedicated architecture achieves lower constraint violations, more stable dual dynamics, and tighter Pareto frontiers compared to the standard mixed-critic PPO-Lagrangian baseline.

## Results

In the CMDP Bandit experiment, both mixed and dedicated critics attain similar average returns, but the dedicated critic achieves markedly lower variance in reward, lower constraint violation, higher gradient-alignment correlation with the true Lagrangian gradient, and substantially lower dual oscillation (moving std. of |λ₂−λ₁|). In the multi-constraint power system (CBESS) application with five interacting constraints, the dedicated-critic PPO consistently achieves the lowest violation rates and magnitudes across all five constraint dimensions on unseen demand/renewable profiles, and its policies cluster tightly near the estimated Pareto frontier across a broad range of safety budgets, whereas the mixed-critic baseline often lies inside the frontier. In the higher-dimensional EVCS environment (obs. dim 219, act. dim 40), dedicated critics again achieve comparable or higher returns while substantially reducing average constraint violations and their variance. A timescale ablation confirms the causal mechanism: when dual stepsizes are slowed so that β_t/η_t → 0, the performance gap between mixed and dedicated critics largely vanishes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is PPO-Lagrangian (mixed critic) from Ray et al. (2019), which uses a single reward critic plus one aggregated cost critic for all constraints. The proposed Dedicated-Critic Lagrangian PPO (DC-Lag-PPO) wins on every safety and stability metric: it achieves lower constraint violations across all five power-system constraints, lower dual-variable oscillation, higher gradient-alignment correlation, and tighter Pareto-front clustering, while matching the baseline on average reward. In the CMDP Bandit, the mixed critic's constraint violation drifts upward over training while the dedicated critic settles near zero; the mixed critic's dual oscillation grows over time whereas the dedicated critic's remains flat. In the power system, the mixed-critic baseline exhibits unstable updates and erratic dual dynamics particularly when constraint signals conflict, while the dedicated critic produces smooth learning curves. The one setting where the two methods converge is the timescale ablation: when the dual update rate is made sufficiently slow relative to the critic (β_t/η_t → 0), both variants exhibit similar reward, constraint, and λ-convergence behaviour, confirming that the mixed critic's failure mode is specifically the dual-induced target drift rather than a fundamental flaw in the Lagrangian formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Lagrangian safe RL framework established by CPO (Achiam et al., 2017) and the PPO-/TRPO-Lagrangian implementations (Ray et al., 2019b; Stooke et al., 2020), which are the de facto baselines in safe RL. While CPO defined per-constraint quantities in its policy update, the paper identifies that the widely-deployed mixed-critic architecture (collapsing all constraints into a single cost critic) was never theoretically justified and, in fact, introduces a structural bias. The contribution is the first formal stochastic-approximation analysis showing that the mixed critic's target drifts with the evolving Lagrange multipliers (an O(β_t/η_t) bias term), whereas dedicated per-signal critics have stationary targets and eliminate this drift entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the missing theoretical justification for a design choice that practitioners have made ad hoc, and it explains a long-noted empirical instability in PPO-Lagrangian under multiple constraints. The practical implication is straightforward: replacing one mixed cost critic with m per-constraint critics (a modest computational overhead that scales linearly) removes a fundamental source of training instability. Open questions include finite-time guarantees for deep (nonlinear) critics, adaptive critic allocation when constraints are sparse or vary in relevance, and whether shared-backbone multi-head architectures can recover the dedicated-critic benefit without the full memory cost.
