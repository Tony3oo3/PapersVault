## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models fail to produce images with exact planar group symmetry even under rich textual and visual guidance. A natural fix—parameterizing only the asymmetric unit and extending via group transformations—introduces boundary discontinuities for non-reflective group elements, breaking the spatial continuity that continuous representations require. The paper asks how to enforce exact symmetry for all 17 planar groups on arbitrary continuous 2D representations without seams.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262735/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a target planar group (one of the 17 wallpaper groups), and optionally a target volume fraction, and produces a symmetric output in the latent space of a pre-trained diffusion model (SD 2.1 or SDXL). The symmetric continuous representation is evaluated on a regular grid to yield a finite-dimensional latent tensor; for mask-based tasks (paper-cutting, topology design), a binary mask is extracted via differentiable segmentation from the latent, and for topology design the mask doubles as an alpha transparency channel over the decoded RGB image. All optimization is zero-shot: no symmetry-specific training data or per-group model retraining is required.

## Method

The core contribution is a symmetrization framework that embeds any planar group G into an affine reflection group W_a via conjugation by an invertible linear map, then constructs a G-invariant continuous field as a sum of W_a-invariant coefficient functions (obtained by reflecting query points into the W_a asymmetric unit, where reflections preserve boundary continuity) multiplied by a fixed set of G-invariant basis functions derived from the Hironaka decomposition. This decomposition is guaranteed to approximate any G-invariant continuous function to arbitrary accuracy, with the number of basis terms equal to the index [W_a : G]. For generation, the parameters of the underlying continuous representation (hash-coded bilinear interpolation) are optimized via Score Distillation Sampling against a diffusion prior, while task-specific constraints—connectivity via a virtual temperature method, mechanical stiffness via homogenization-based bulk modulus, and volume fraction—are imposed as additional differentiable losses, cleanly separating symmetry enforcement (in the representation) from design objectives (in the loss).

## Results

For pattern design under the p1 group, our method achieves CLIP-A scores of 4.30, 4.20, and 3.99 at resolutions n=64, 128, and 256, consistently outperforming the best basis-projection baseline (4.05, 3.42, 3.48) across all resolutions. In the broader pattern design comparison across all 17 planar groups, our method (built on SD 2.1) attains the highest CLIP-A in the post-symmetrization setting, where MLLM baselines (GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Pro) suffer substantial aesthetic degradation when strict symmetry is enforced. For topology design, our method matches or exceeds the baseline's bulk modulus while achieving a substantial lead in CLIP-A, with the advantage widening for higher-symmetry groups (p2, pm). In the zero-shot metamaterial setting, generated samples achieve high bulk modulus with a relative volume-fraction MAE below 1.5% across the first 12 planar groups without any symmetry-specific training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MLLM baselines (GPT-5.2 and Gemini 3 Pro) on pattern design, our method wins decisively in the post-symmetrization setting: while the MLLMs produce strong CLIP-A scores in unconstrained direct generation, enforcing exact planar-group symmetry via our parameterization substantially degrades their aesthetic quality, whereas our SD 2.1-based pipeline maintains higher CLIP-A under the same symmetry enforcement. Against basis projection (the strongest alternative symmetrization strategy), our method outperforms at every tested resolution for the p1 group, with the gap widening at higher resolutions (e.g., 4.20 vs. 3.42 at n=128), attributed to projection's tendency to produce overly smooth initializations that hinder SDS optimization. Against Zhong et al. (2023) on topology design, our method achieves comparable bulk modulus in the variable-angle p1 setting while leading substantially in CLIP-A; in the variable-symmetry setting (p2, pm), our method surpasses the baseline in both mechanical performance and aesthetic quality, indicating improved stability under higher-symmetry constraints. The paper does not report a head-to-head numerical table for the MLLM or topology-design comparisons (results are presented in figures), so exact margin values for those settings are not tabulated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SDS-based optimization paradigm from DreamFusion (Poole et al., 2023) and the hash-encoded continuous representation from InstantNGP (Müller et al., 2022), but introduces a fundamentally new symmetrization layer: embedding any planar group into an affine reflection supergroup and applying Hironaka decomposition to construct continuous G-invariant fields from high-symmetry coefficients and low-symmetry bases. It departs from prior symmetry-constrained generation (Mao et al., 2020; Jiao et al., 2024) which require symmetry-specific training data or predefined templates, by enforcing symmetry purely through the representation geometry in a zero-shot manner. The connectivity constraint via VTM (Li et al., 2016) and the homogenization-based mechanical evaluation are adapted from topology optimization literature but integrated into a unified diffusion-prior pipeline for the first time across all 17 planar groups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a general, mathematically grounded recipe for exact symmetry enforcement in any continuous 2D representation, decoupling geometric constraints from task-specific objectives so that the same pipeline handles pattern design, paper-cutting, topology optimization, and metamaterial design without retraining. The theoretical guarantee (Thm. 3.3) that the high-symmetry/low-symmetry decomposition approximates any G-invariant continuous function to arbitrary precision is a clean result with potential applicability beyond the demonstrated tasks. Limitations include restriction to n≤3 (where affine reflection supergroups exist for all crystallographic groups), reliance on a pre-trained diffusion prior for visual quality, and the fact that the mechanical evaluation uses unnormalized bulk-modulus values. Open questions include whether the framework extends to 3D space groups in practice, how it scales to non-periodic or aperiodic symmetries, and whether the zero-shot symmetry transfer degrades under more complex multi-objective optimization.
