## Problem

Spiking neural networks suffer a notable performance gap relative to ANNs on language modeling tasks, which the paper traces to a spike saturation-induced information homogenization problem: within a bounded time window T, distinct high-amplitude pre-activation inputs all drive neurons to emit the maximum T spikes, collapsing semantically different signals into identical spike trains. Prior work expanded the spike value set (ternary, multi-level) but overlooked this fundamental upper bound on spike count per neuron, which directly limits discriminability and accumulates across layers.

## Input / Output

The model consumes text token sequences (up to 128 tokens) and produces task-specific outputs: binary or ternary classification labels for MNLI, QQP, QNLI, SST-2, CoLA, MRPC, and RTE, and continuous similarity scores for STS-B. Internally, pre-activation vectors of dimension n (e.g., 768) are smoothed by an orthogonal transform before being fed to LIF spiking neurons over T time steps; the transform matrices are fused into adjacent linear weights at inference, so the deployed model retains the original event-driven spiking computation with zero additional FLOPs.

## Method

SmoothSpike inserts a learnable orthogonal transformation matrix at the pre-activation inputs of spiking neurons, initialized with a randomized Hadamard matrix and updated during training via a fifth-order Newton-Schulz iteration that projects the matrix onto the nearest orthogonal matrix (the matrix sign function) while remaining fully differentiable. The paper proves that the randomized Hadamard transform bounds the maximum element of the transformed input to O(√(log n/n)) with high probability, directly suppressing the extreme values that cause saturation. To enable zero-overhead inference, the architecture adopts pre-norm RMSNorm (with its learnable scale absorbed into firing thresholds), which is equivariant to orthogonal transforms, allowing the input-side matrix to be prefused into the preceding linear projection and the output-side inverse transform to be absorbed into the next layer's weights. The transform is applied at three positions (residual branches, value projection, and MLP) with a block-diagonal structure in the MLP to reduce training cost, while query/key LIF neurons are left untransformed due to mild saturation.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark, SmoothSpike applied to Spikingformer achieves an average score of 75.0, an 8.2-point improvement over the vanilla Spikingformer baseline (66.8). The largest gains appear on tasks requiring fine-grained semantic discrimination: CoLA improves by 16.8 points (24.4→41.2) and STS-B by 24.8 points (54.5→79.3). Applied to SpikeLM, SmoothSpike raises the average from 75.7 to 77.5 (+1.8), with the smaller gain attributed to SpikeLM's existing elastic bi-spiking mechanism already partially mitigating saturation. The best SmoothSpike configuration (SpikeLM+SmoothSpike, 77.5 avg) still trails the full-precision BERT_base reference (83.2 avg) by 5.7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary Spikingformer baseline, SmoothSpike wins on all eight GLUE tasks, with the most dramatic gains on CoLA (+16.8) and STS-B (+24.8), confirming that tasks demanding fine-grained semantic discrimination benefit most from reduced information homogenization. Compared to SpikeLM (75.7 avg), SmoothSpike-Spikingformer (75.0) is slightly behind, but SmoothSpike-SpikeLM (77.5) surpasses it by 1.8 points, though with a notable RTE regression (65.3→58.1) offset by large CoLA gains (+14.8). Against all other SNN baselines (SpikeBERT 59.7, PSN-BERT 34.7, LIF-BERT 54.9), SmoothSpike-Spikingformer leads by 15.3–40.3 points. It also outperforms all quantized baselines (BiT 71.0, BiPFT 70.8, BiBERT 63.2) and ELMo (70.2) by 4–12 points. The remaining gap to BERT_base (83.2) is 5.7 points for the best SNN configuration, and the energy cost of SmoothSpike-Spikingformer (9.45 mJ) remains well below BERT_base (51.41 mJ), preserving the efficiency advantage of spike-driven computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Spikingformer (Zhou et al., 2026) as its base spiking transformer and borrows the Newton-Schulz iteration from Liu et al. (2025a) for efficient orthogonal projection during training. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant line of SNN work that improves representational capacity by expanding the spike value set (ternary spikes, multi-level LIF neurons) and instead identifies a different bottleneck: the bounded time window causes distinct high-amplitude inputs to saturate to identical spike counts, a phenomenon the authors term 'spike saturation-induced information homogenization.' The Hadamard smoothing mechanism itself is repurposed from the LLM quantization literature (SmoothQuant, QuaRot, SpinQuant), where orthogonal rotations suppress activation outliers, but here it is applied to the fundamentally different problem of preventing spiking neuron saturation while preserving the binary spike-driven computation paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key contribution is a diagnostic insight—spike saturation, not the discreteness of spikes per se, is the primary bottleneck for SNN language modeling—coupled with a zero-overhead fix (orthogonal transforms fuse into weights at inference) that yields an 8.2% average GLUE improvement. This opens a principled pathway for improving SNN performance without sacrificing the event-driven efficiency that motivates SNNs for edge deployment. However, the energy cost increases by roughly 40% (6.76→9.45 mJ for Spikingformer), the evaluation is limited to GLUE-scale tasks, and it remains open whether the approach scales to larger models, longer sequences, or non-transformer SNN architectures. The fixed-Hadamard ablation showing degraded performance on simpler tasks (MRPC, RTE) also suggests the learnable component is essential, adding training complexity that may not transfer to all settings.
