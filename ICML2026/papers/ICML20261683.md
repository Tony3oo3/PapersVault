## Problem

Existing 3D molecular generative models represent molecules as point clouds or voxel grids and must jointly generate discrete atom types with continuous coordinates, creating a tight coupling between heterogeneous modalities that is fragile under geometric perturbations. Point-cloud approaches suffer from locality-limited expressivity and quadratic scaling, while voxel methods incur cubic memory costs. The paper asks whether a continuous, resolution-free representation can decouple structure learning from discrete atom instantiation.

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise z_T ∈ R^{L³×d} as input and produces a discrete molecular structure (atomic coordinates in Å and element types from a fixed set of K elements). The latent grid size L is fixed per dataset (L=5 for QM9, L=7 for GEOM-drugs, L=8 for CREMP) and is independent of the number of atoms, removing cardinality constraints. The intermediate vector field is continuous over R³ and evaluated at arbitrary query points; discrete atoms are recovered by gradient ascent along the field followed by DBSCAN clustering, with bonds inferred post-hoc via OpenBabel.

## Method

VecMol represents each molecule as a continuous vector field v: R³ → R^{K×3} where, at every spatial location, K vectors point toward the nearest atom of each element type, using a Gaussian-Clip formulation that combines softmax-based direction selection with a clipped Gaussian magnitude for bounded, well-conditioned gradients. A two-stage pipeline is trained: (1) a neural field autoencoder with a cross-graph EGNN encoder (mapping variable-size molecular graphs to a fixed L³×d latent grid) and an E(n)-equivariant EGNN decoder that predicts virtual source locations per query point and atom type, yielding translation-equivariant vectors; (2) a DDPM trained in the latent grid space with an EGNN denoiser using radius-based neighborhoods. At sampling time, a denoised latent code is decoded into a vector field, and atomic positions are recovered by initializing query points per element type, performing Euler gradient ascent until field-norm convergence, and clustering converged points with DBSCAN. A type-exclusive repulsive field for absent atom types prevents spurious equilibria, and the entire pipeline is E(n)-equivariant by construction.

## Results

On GEOM-Drugs, VecMol achieves the best stable molecule rate (80.2%) and stable atom rate (99.1%) among all compared methods, along with the lowest valency W1 (0.108) and atom TV (0.034). On QM9, VecMol matches EDM and GeoLDM on stability (~97.5% stable mol, 99.8% stable atom) and validity (~98.4%), though it trails on bond angle W1 (1.01 vs. 0.44 for EDM). On CREMP (cyclic peptides), VecMol reaches 83.1% RDKit validity and 98.0% uniqueness, and achieves lower backbone KL divergence than FuncMol across all six dihedral/angle types (e.g., θ₁ KL 0.0726 vs. 0.0954). Sampling time is 31.62 s for 10,000 GEOM-Drugs molecules, roughly 2.5× faster than EDM (79.18 s) and VoxMol (84.32 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VecMol clearly outperforms EDM on GEOM-Drugs in molecular stability (80.2% vs. 40.3% stable mol) and valency fidelity (W₁ 0.108 vs. 0.285), while also being 2.5× faster to sample (31.6 s vs. 79.2 s). Against GeoLDM, VecMol wins on stable mol (80.2% vs. 57.9%), stable atom (99.1% vs. 98.7%), and valency W₁ (0.108 vs. 0.197), but trails on bond-level geometry (bond angle W₁ 4.81 vs. 2.96; bond length W₁ 0.024 vs. 0.009). Compared to VoxMol, VecMol achieves higher stable mol (80.2% vs. 75.0%) and much better valency W₁ (0.108 vs. 0.254), though VoxMol retains an edge on bond angle W₁ (0.64 vs. 4.81) and bond length W₁ (0.002 vs. 0.024). Against FuncMol, VecMol leads on stable mol (80.2% vs. 69.7%), stable atom (99.1% vs. 98.8%), and valency W₁ (0.108 vs. 0.245), while FuncMol is slightly better on bond angle W₁ (2.49 vs. 4.81). On CREMP, VecMol beats FuncMol on every backbone KL divergence metric (e.g., θ₁: 0.0726 vs. 0.0954; ψ: 0.0734 vs. 0.1218), indicating more accurate capture of cyclic-peptide folding geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

VecMol departs from the dominant point-cloud/3D-graph paradigm (EDM, GeoLDM) that jointly generates discrete atom types and continuous coordinates under a fixed cardinality, and from voxel-based methods (VoxMol) that discretize space with cubic scaling. It also extends the field-based line of work (FuncMol) by replacing scalar atomic density fields with vector-valued gradient fields that encode directional information toward atomic centers. Conceptually, the paper repurposes the neural-field autoencoder + latent diffusion pipeline established in 3D shape generation (DeepSDF, Occupancy Networks, Neural-LDM) for molecular modeling, introducing a cross-graph encoder that bridges variable-size molecular graphs to fixed grid latent codes and an E(n)-equivariant decoder that predicts virtual source locations rather than raw vector values.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The vector-field representation offers a resolution-free, atom-count-agnostic formulation that avoids the artificial cardinality constraints of point-cloud methods and the cubic memory overhead of voxel grids, opening a principled path toward modeling molecules of arbitrary size. However, the paper is limited to unconditional generation, and ablations reveal that autoencoder reconstruction fidelity—not the diffusion process—is the primary performance bottleneck, suggesting that scaling capacity or improving the field definition will be necessary before the approach can clearly outperform strong baselines on complex drug-like molecules. Open questions include whether the representation extends naturally to conditional (structure-based) generation, molecular dynamics, and conformational sampling, where the continuous field could serve as a learned prior.
