## Problem

While KL-regularized objectives are widely used in RLHF and recommender systems, the exact regret of online learning under such objectives remained unresolved even for the simplest case of multi-armed bandits. Prior work (Zhao et al., 2025b) gave a logarithmic regret bound under general function approximation that, when specialized to MABs, yielded a suboptimal O(ηK² log²T) dependence on the number of arms K, and no non-constant lower bound matching the K-dependence was known. This paper asks: what is the exact minimax regret of KL-regularized MABs across all regimes of the regularization strength η?

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a finite action space of K arms, a known reference policy π^ref over those arms, a regularization parameter η (inverse temperature), and at each round observes a single noisy 1-subGaussian reward scalar for the chosen arm. It outputs one action per round and, as a theoretical guarantee, a high-probability KL-regularized regret bound that takes two distinct forms: Õ(ηK log²T) when η ≤ √(T/K) (high-regularization regime) and Õ(√(KT log T)) when η ≥ √(T/K) (low-regularization regime), with the transition point at η = √(T/K) marking where the KL penalty ceases to dominate the reward term.

## Method

The algorithm is a variant of KL-UCB that, at each round, computes a standard UCB bonus b_t(a) = √(2 log(TK/δ) / (N_t(a)∨1)), forms an optimistic reward r̂_t = r̄_t + b_t, and sets the policy as π_{t+1}(a) ∝ π^ref(a) exp(η·r̂_t(a)). The key analytical contribution is a novel peeling argument for bounding the martingale-difference term in the regret decomposition: rather than applying Azuma-Hoeffding (which would yield an unacceptable √T term), the authors truncate the cumulative conditional variance at dyadic levels 2^i and apply Freedman's inequality at each level, then take a union bound over i ≤ log₂T to recover a logarithmic high-probability bound. For the matching lower bound in the high-regularization regime, the paper constructs a family of instances parameterized by μ ∈ {±1}^{K/2} where Ω(K) arms simultaneously require estimation, and resolves the time-varying difficulty by extending the discrete instance distribution to a continuous uniform Bayes prior over a fixed interval, enabling summation of per-step suboptimality gaps Ω(ηK/t) over t ∈ [η²K, T] to yield Ω(ηK log T).

## Results

In the high-regularization regime (η ≤ √(T/K)), KL-UCB achieves a Õ(ηK log²T) KL-regularized regret upper bound, which is the first high-probability bound with linear dependence on K. The matching lower bound is Ω(ηK log T), leaving only a log T gap. In the low-regularization regime (η ≥ √(T/K)), the regret is Õ(√(KT log T)) with a matching Ω(√(KT)) lower bound, recovering the classical √T-type rate. Experiments with K=4, T=10⁶ confirm the phase transition: the minimax regret curve exhibits a √T slope for η=∞ (unregularized) but transitions to a sub-linear, near-logarithmic slope as η decreases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most direct comparison is with KL-UCB from Zhao et al. (2025b), which achieves Õ(η d_R log T log(N_R)) under general function approximation. When specialized to MABs via one-hot features, that bound reduces to O(ηK² log²T) (quadratic in K). This paper's sharp peeling analysis improves this to Õ(ηK log²T), achieving strictly linear dependence on K — the first such bound. The paper also provides the first non-constant lower bound Ω(ηK log T) for KL-regularized MABs, which strictly improves upon the Ω(η/ε) sample complexity implied by Zhao et al. (2025a)'s two-armed contextual bandit construction. The other baselines (Xiong et al. 2024, Wu et al. 2025a, Zhao et al. 2025a TMPS) operate in different settings (preference learning, general function approximation, data coverage) and do not directly apply to the MAB regret minimization problem, so no head-to-head numerical comparison is made. The empirical experiments (Figure 2) confirm the theoretical phase transition: for η=∞ the regret grows as √T, while for finite η the slope decreases progressively, with the strongest regularization (η=5) showing the most pronounced sub-linear behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on KL-UCB (Zhao et al., 2025b), which first achieved logarithmic regret for KL-regularized online learning under general function approximation, by specializing to MABs and replacing the eluder-dimension-based bonus with a standard UCB confidence interval. It also extends the lower bound of Zhao et al. (2025a), which gave Ω(η log(N_R)/ε) for contextual bandits, to a sharper Ω(η K log T) bound for MABs that properly captures the K-dependence. The key methodological departure is a novel peeling argument combined with Freedman's inequality to obtain high-probability bounds on a martingale difference sequence without the √T blowup that Azuma-Hoeffding would introduce, and a new hard-instance construction where Ω(K) arms must be distinguished simultaneously rather than just two.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a near-complete characterization of KL-regularized MABs, revealing a clean phase transition at η ~ √(T/K) where regret shifts from the standard √(KT) rate to a polylogarithmic log²(T) rate as regularization strengthens. This clarifies a fundamental question about when KL regularization actually helps in online learning and provides the first minimax-optimal (up to log T) bounds in both regimes. The remaining log T gap between upper and lower bounds, and the restriction to the tabular MAB setting, leave open the question of whether these near-tight results extend to structured settings like contextual or linear bandits, which would be important for practical RLHF applications.
