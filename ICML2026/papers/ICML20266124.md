## Problem

Rigid and non-rigid point cloud registration have been treated as separate problems due to a fundamental multi-scale discrepancy: rigid alignment relies on stable cross-scale geometric similarity under a single global transformation, while non-rigid alignment requires locally coherent but globally semantically driven correspondences. This discrepancy causes optimization conflicts in existing architectures, and no prior method achieves robust, consistent performance across both deformation types within a single framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266124/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source and target point cloud (voxelized) plus a coarse category-level text prompt (shape name) that conditions the TRELLIS feature extractor; the text serves only as a weak semantic cue since most geometric information is carried by the 3D input itself. After KPConv downsampling to compact keypoints, the network outputs correspondence-aware point features (used for contrastive matching) and per-point reconstructed coordinates in the other cloud's frame. From these predictions, a rigid transformation (R, t) is recovered via SVD for rigid tasks, or a smooth non-rigid deformation field is constructed via an embedded deformation graph with local rigid transforms and Gaussian skinning weights for non-rigid tasks.

## Method

RGGT extracts geometric-semantic prior features from a pre-trained 3D generative model (TRELLIS's Sparse Flow Transformer), which naturally encodes multi-scale structure that remains stable under both rigid and non-rigid deformation, avoiding the modality gap of 2D-based priors. These features are downsampled by KPConv and processed by a 6-layer transformer backbone whose key architectural novelty is a Global-Self-Cross Attention design: each layer first applies a global attention branch that concatenates both point clouds' features as Q/K/V to capture long-range structural context, then applies standard self-attention (intra-cloud geometry) and cross-attention (inter-cloud correspondence reasoning). Training uses a dual objective: an InfoNCE contrastive loss on transformer features for discriminative point-wise matching, and an L1 reconstruction loss on MLP-predicted coordinates for global geometric consistency, with a symmetric learnable log-bilinear similarity matrix constrained to be symmetric since both clouds share the same TRELLIS feature space. The model is jointly trained on rigid (ModelNet40, 3DMatch, KITTI) and non-rigid (4DMatch) datasets in a mixed manner, enabling a single network to handle both registration paradigms.

## Results

On the non-rigid 4DMatch benchmark, RGGT achieves EPE 0.031, AccS 73.9%, AccR 89.7%, and OR 7.1%, outperforming the previous best GraphSCNet (EPE 0.043, AccS 72.3%, AccR 84.4%, OR 9.4%). On the more challenging low-overlap 4DLoMatch subset, RGGT reduces EPE from 0.121 to 0.052 and cuts the outlier ratio from 21.0% to 6.8% versus GraphSCNet. On rigid registration, RGGT attains RRE 1.452 / RTE 0.013 on ModelNet40, RRE 1.55 / RTE 0.043 on 3DMatch, and RRE 0.21 / RTE 0.043 on KITTI, all setting new state-of-the-art within a single unified framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGGT outperforms all 24 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On non-rigid 4DMatch, it improves over GraphSCNet by +1.6 pp AccS and +5.3 pp AccR, and over LNDP by +12.8 pp AccS and +15.9 pp AccR. The advantage is most dramatic on the low-overlap 4DLoMatch subset, where RGGT gains +14.0 pp AccS and +20.8 pp AccR over GraphSCNet, and reduces OR from 34.1% (LNDP) to 6.8%. On rigid ModelNet40, RGGT achieves RRE 1.452, beating MAC (2.048), FastMAC (2.275), and GeoTransformer (2.564); on 3DMatch it leads with RRE 1.55 vs. MAC's 1.60 and PARE-Net's 1.70; on KITTI it records RRE 0.21 / RTE 0.043, surpassing PARE-Net (0.23/0.049) and Predator (0.24/0.056). On the unseen CAPE dataset, RGGT (EPE 0.023, AccS 77.7%) beats even SyNoRiM-self (EPE 0.030, AccS 55.5%), which was trained directly on CAPE, demonstrating strong cross-dataset generalization without any CAPE-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

RGGT extends the transformer-based registration paradigm established by REGTR and GeoTransformer by adding a Global Attention branch that aggregates cross-shape structural context before local self- and cross-attention, and by replacing hand-crafted or 2D-projected features with latent embeddings from TRELLIS, a native 3D generative model. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the use of a 3D diffusion model's latent space as a registration feature encoder, motivated by the observation that generative models encode multi-scale geometric-semantic cues stable under deformation. The dual correspondence-reconstruction objective (InfoNCE + L1 coordinate regression) unifies the supervision signals that rigid methods (typically SVD-based) and non-rigid methods (typically deformation-field-based) have treated separately, enabling a single optimization space for both.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RGGT is the first framework to achieve SOTA on both rigid and non-rigid registration benchmarks simultaneously, which is practically valuable for applications (robotics, AR, 4D reconstruction) where the deformation type is unknown a priori. The CAPE generalization result—outperforming SyNoRiM-self, which was trained directly on CAPE—suggests the unified representation transfers well across deformation regimes without task-specific fine-tuning. However, the method still fails under extremely low overlap (<5%), cannot handle topology changes (parts appearing/disappearing), and the ~10 s TRELLIS preprocessing per pair limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the generative-prior approach scales to large-scale scenes and whether the text conditioning can be fully eliminated for open-vocabulary settings.
