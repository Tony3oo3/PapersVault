## Problem

Passive long-video understanding models process frames uniformly regardless of query difficulty, causing computational cost to grow super-linearly with video duration. Existing agentic frameworks either delegate perception to external tool modules (severing gradient flow between reasoning and perception) or require a global pre-scan of the video, so their context cost still scales with video length. No prior work formulates omni-modal video understanding as a native, query-driven active perception process within a single model that truly decouples reasoning complexity from raw video duration.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a video with synchronized audio, a natural-language query, and video metadata (duration, FPS, audio availability flag) as its initial state. Rather than ingesting the full video at once, the agent iteratively issues symbolic actions (get_frames, get_audio, get_clip, answer) to an environment that returns raw media segments; each returned percept is distilled into a structured textual observation and then purged from the active context, keeping media overhead constant at ~1024 visual tokens per frame and 768 per video frame within a 64K-token window. The final output is a task-specific answer (MCQ letter, temporal interval pair, numerical value, or free-form text) accompanied by the full OTA reasoning trace, with a confidence gate requiring ≥0.9 before the answer action is emitted.

## Method

OmniAgent formulates omni-modal video understanding as a POMDP where a single Qwen2.5-Omni-7B model executes an iterative Observation-Thought-Action cycle, maintaining a strict separation between a transient multimodal percept (raw frames/audio/clips returned by the environment) and a persistent textual memory (the accumulated reasoning trace). The environment performs only raw media extraction via FFmpeg; all semantic perception and reasoning are native to the policy, with a context-purging mechanism that replaces consumed media with text placeholders after each turn. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) Agentic SFT on 58K trajectories synthesized via best-of-N exploration with a teacher model, filtered by outcome verification and a GPT-4o rationality audit (≥3/5 coherence score) to eliminate lucky guesses; (2) Agentic RL using TAURA (Turn-aware Adaptive Uncertainty Rescaled Advantage), which addresses Advantage Homogenization in GRPO by rescaling the trajectory-level advantage with a continuous turn-level entropy weight, amplifying credit for high-uncertainty discovery turns while preserving gradient flow for all tokens. This yields positive test-time scaling: accuracy improves monotonically as the maximum turn limit K increases, while the agent adaptively terminates once sufficient evidence is gathered.

## Results

OmniAgent-7B achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source models across all ten benchmarks. On long-video understanding, it scores 50.5% on LVBench (outperforming the 10× larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 47.3% while using 73% fewer frames), 71.1% on MLVU, and 67.8% on VideoMME. On omni-modal reasoning, it gains +4.7% on DailyOmni and +7.8% on OmniVideoBench over the Qwen2.5-Omni baseline. On temporal grounding, it achieves an absolute +33.4% IoU gain on LongVALE (39.1% vs. 5.7%) and +38.1% on VUE-TR (46.1% vs. 8.0%), surpassing proprietary models including GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Pro on VUE-TR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct Qwen2.5-Omni-7B baseline, OmniAgent wins on all ten benchmarks, with the largest gains on temporal grounding (+33.4% IoU on LongVALE, +38.1% on VUE-TR) and VSI-Bench (+12.9%). On LVBench, OmniAgent-7B (50.5%) outperforms the 10× larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (47.3%) while sampling only 203 frames versus 768, and also beats the agentic baseline Zoom-Zero-7B (45.7%). Among agentic baselines, OmniAgent surpasses LongVT (41.3% on LVBench), VITAL (no LVBench result), and Video-CoM (no LVBench result), and outperforms thinking models Video-R1 (40.1%) and VideoRFT (34.7%) on LVBench. On omni-modal benchmarks (Table 2), OmniAgent beats Qwen2.5-Omni on DailyOmni (64.8% vs. 60.1%) and OmniVideoBench (37.1% vs. 29.3%), and surpasses MiniCPM-o (53.1% on DailyOmni) and Ola (50.7%). On temporal grounding (Table 3), OmniAgent-7B (46.1% on VUE-TR) exceeds proprietary GPT-4o (22.0%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (21.8%), though it trails Gemini-2.0-Flash (25.4%) on VUE-TR Vision-only and Gemini-2.5-Pro (67.4%) on LVBench overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two prior agentic video-understanding lineages: (1) LLM-as-controller frameworks that delegate perception to external tool modules (VideoAgent, VITAL, LongVT), which create an information bottleneck by severing gradient flow between reasoning and perception, and (2) 'thinking with images' methods (Zoom-Zero, Video-CoM) that still require a global pre-scan or dense visual buffer, failing to decouple cost from video duration. OmniAgent instead formulates the entire loop as a POMDP executed natively within a single omni-modal model, with a strict transient-percept/persistent-memory separation. On the optimization side, it builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) and DAPO's clip-higher mechanism, but identifies and addresses a structural flaw—Advantage Homogenization—via the entropy-rescaled TAURA objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 7B agent outperforms a 10× larger passive model on long-video benchmarks while consuming 73% fewer frames, and the decoupling of reasoning cost from video duration makes hour-long video understanding tractable on modest hardware. The positive test-time scaling (accuracy improves with more turns, saturating adaptively) suggests a path toward compute-optimal inference for variable-difficulty queries. Key open questions include the sequential latency overhead of the OTA loop (66.8s vs. 34.8s for single-shot), whether parallelized exploration can close this gap, and whether the entropy-steered credit assignment generalizes beyond video to other multi-turn agentic domains.
