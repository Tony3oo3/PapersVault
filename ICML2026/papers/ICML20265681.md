## Problem

State-of-the-art tabular foundation models (TabPFN, TabICL) rely on transformer attention over the full training context, incurring O(N²) inference complexity in the number of training rows N. This quadratic bottleneck makes them infeasible on large-scale datasets, forces subsampling that degrades accuracy, and renders KV caching memory-prohibitive. No prior work had proposed an end-to-end learned compression of the training context for tabular in-context prediction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full tabular training table (N rows × M features plus a target column) and a batch of test feature vectors, and produces class scores for the test points. The compressor reduces the N-row training context to K learned latent rows (compression rate r = K/N, tunable from 0.1% to 16%), which are then concatenated with the embedded test batch as the predictor's input. Because the compressed context is a learned embedding rather than a row subsample, it can be cached and reused across an arbitrary number of subsequent test batches without recomputation.

## Method

TACO couples two transformer modules trained jointly end-to-end via meta-learning: a compressor g that maps the full training table into K prototypical latent rows, and a predictor f (TabPFNv2 2D row/column attention architecture) that conditions on the compressed context to predict test labels. The compressor receives the original training data alongside K dummy rows (randomly initialized from a training subset, with the target column masked by a placeholder), and is trained to fold the full dataset's information into the dummy rows' latent representations. Joint optimization incentivizes the compressor to produce representations that maximally improve the predictor, while the predictor adapts its representational space to the compressed input. A chunking-and-stitching strategy partitions very large datasets (e.g., 1.5M rows) into sequential chunks, compresses each independently, and concatenates the per-chunk summaries into a single global context, enabling inference on tables with millions of rows.

## Results

On the TabArena benchmark (26 classification datasets), TACO at 1% compression achieves a mean ROC-AUC of 0.855±0.097, which the Friedman-Nemenyi test confirms is not statistically significantly different from the uncompressed predictor-only transformer (POT, 0.862±0.101). The headline efficiency gains are a 94× inference speedup (306 ms vs. 28.67 s per prediction batch) and a 97.6% reduction in prediction-time memory (549 MB vs. 22.45 GB) at the 1% compression rate without KV caching. On the large-scale MetroPT-3 dataset (~1.5M rows), TACO with chunking at 0.1% compression achieves 0.9886 precision and 0.8955 AUPRC, outperforming random and kNN sampling baselines by wide margins while remaining competitive with XGBoost (0.9722 precision, 0.9593 AUPRC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TabICL (0.866±0.103) and TabPFN V2.0 (0.866±0.103), TACO at 1% compression (0.855±0.097) is marginally lower in mean ROC-AUC, but the authors note their models were trained for fewer steps; the critical difference diagram confirms TACO(1%) is not statistically distinguishable from their own POT baseline (0.862±0.101), meaning the compression itself introduces no significant degradation. Against XGBoost, the comparison is more nuanced: on MetroPT-3, TACO (chunk) achieves higher precision (0.9886 vs. 0.9722) but lower recall (0.7925 vs. 0.8467) and AUPRC (0.8955 vs. 0.9593), while on TabArena the Elo comparison (Figure 5) shows TACO matching POT and approaching TabICL in the tuned+ensembled setting. The key differentiator is scalability: POT and TabPFN V2.0 run out of memory on 10 of 12 TableShift datasets (up to ~6M rows), whereas TACO with chunking operates on all of them at 0.1% compression, achieving performance within ±0.001 of XGBoost on average in those infeasible regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

TACO builds directly on the TabPFNv2 bi-directional transformer architecture (Hollmann et al., 2025), using it as the backbone for both its compressor and predictor modules. It departs from prior efficiency efforts for tabular foundation models—MotherNet (hypernetwork distillation) and TabFlex (linear attention)—by introducing a learned end-to-end context compressor that reduces the training context before the predictor sees it, rather than modifying the attention mechanism itself. The chunking-and-stitching strategy for million-row datasets is a novel procedural contribution not present in prior tabular TFM work, which typically restricted training to small or mid-size tables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TACO addresses the central practical bottleneck of tabular foundation models: quadratic attention cost over the training context makes them infeasible on real-world datasets with tens of thousands to millions of rows. By compressing to 1% of the context with no statistically significant performance loss, it enables deployment in streaming/industrial settings and makes KV caching tractable where it previously caused out-of-memory failures. Open questions include extending to regression and time-series tasks, handling distribution shift (the paper acknowledges this limitation), and whether the synthetic prior adequately captures real-world data diversity, particularly with high missingness rates.
