## Problem

Model-agnostic conditional independence testing methods (e.g., HRT, CFI with corrections) require a train-test split to decouple the model from the test distribution, which substantially reduces statistical power in small-sample regimes. Standard knockoffs provide finite-sample FDR control but demand exact knockoff variables satisfying joint exchangeability and antisymmetric statistics—structural constraints that are difficult or impossible to satisfy with black-box ML models. The paper addresses the gap of accommodating arbitrary pre-trained models while avoiding data splitting and retaining finite-sample type-I error and FDR guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained predictive model m̂ (any architecture), the full dataset {(x_i, y_i)}_{i=1}^n without any train-test split, and a feature index j. It additionally fits two lightweight regressors—ν̂_j ≈ E[X^j | X^{-j}] and ρ̂_j ≈ E[X^j | X^{-j}, y]—to generate conditional residuals for constructing the semi-knockoff variables. The output is either a p-value (via Wilcoxon or sign test on paired loss differences) for single-feature testing, or a set of selected features with FDR ≤ q obtained by applying the standard knockoff threshold to the Semi-knockoff statistics W_SKO^j.

## Method

The core insight is that under the null X^j ⊥ y | X^{-j}, the conditional expectations E[X^j | X^{-j}] and E[X^j | X^{-j}, y] coincide, whereas under the alternative they diverge because y carries additional information about X^j. The method constructs two perturbed copies of X by replacing the j-th coordinate with the respective conditional expectation plus a residual drawn from an independently sampled observation, then compares the model's loss on these two versions. Under the null the two loss distributions are identical, yielding exchangeability that supports valid nonparametric paired tests (type-I error) or the knockoff threshold (FDR control) without any train-test split, since the symmetry arises from the sampling mechanism rather than data independence. Two theoretical results guarantee validity with estimated (non-oracle) conditional expectations: a stability bound showing that regularized ERM coefficients shift only O_P(√(log(1/δ)/n)) when a null feature is removed, and a double-robustness property establishing that the estimation error decays at a product rate when both the predictive model and the conditional imputer converge.

## Results

Semi-knockoffs (SKO_Wcx) achieves finite-sample type-I error control at the nominal level (α = 0.05) while substantially outperforming all baselines in statistical power across simulated settings (n = 300, p = 50, 50 repetitions). In the adjacent-support linear setting (Figure 4), SKO_Wcx detects all truly relevant features with near-zero false positives, whereas HRT (which requires a train-test split) shows noticeably lower power and LOCO-W fails to control type-I error. In the masked-correlation setting (Figure 5), derandomization with 5 permutations (SKO_Wcx_p5) yields the most powerful procedure, surpassing dCRT which exhibits low power due to its two-step distillation removing signal. On the Wisconsin Breast Cancer dataset (Figure 6), Semi-knockoffs makes consistent discoveries across Random Forests, Neural Networks, and Gradient Boosting while maintaining controlled type-I error on an artificial null feature, unlike other methods whose results vary significantly with the random seed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Semi-knockoffs wins on power against all finite-sample-controlled baselines while maintaining valid type-I error. Against HRT (Tansey et al., 2022), SKO_Wcx is substantially more powerful because it avoids the train-test split that HRT requires for exchangeability. Against dCRT (Liu et al., 2022), SKO_Wcx achieves higher power, particularly in the masked-correlation setting where dCRT's feature-wise distillation step removes most of the signal from the important feature. Against LOCO-W (Williamson et al., 2023), SKO_Wcx both controls type-I error (where LOCO-W fails in some settings) and achieves higher power. Against the VIM baselines (Sobol-CPI from Chamma et al. 2024, SAGE from Covert et al. 2020, and LOCO from Lei et al. 2018), Semi-knockoffs provides finite-sample guarantees where these methods offer none or only asymptotic ones; the asymptotic corrections (SCPI_sqrt, LOCO_sqrt from Verdinelli & Wasserman 2024) are too restrictive, yielding very low power. Against standard Model-X Knockoffs (Candès et al., 2018), Semi-knockoffs achieves comparable FDR control without requiring the construction of valid knockoff variables or the model-X assumption on the input distribution, making it applicable to arbitrary pre-trained black-box models.

## Relation to Prior Work

Semi-knockoffs sit at the intersection of three research threads: the model-X knockoff framework (Candès et al., 2018), which provides the FDR control mechanism via exchangeability and a data-dependent threshold; the conditional permutation / CPI literature (Chamma et al., 2024), which supplies the regression-based conditional sampler; and the double-robustness analysis for CFI (Reyero-Lobo et al., 2025a), which is generalized here to a broader class of models. The key departure from standard knockoffs is that Semi-knockoffs do not require constructing valid knockoff variables (joint pairwise exchangeability) or antisymmetric statistics; instead, they create two conditionally-sampled populations—one imputer conditions on y, the other does not—so that exchangeability holds under the null without a train-test split. This contrasts with HRT and CRT, which enforce independence between model and evaluation data via a held-out set, and with dCRT, whose two-step distillation sacrifices power.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical path to using powerful black-box models (gradient boosting, neural networks, random forests) for controlled variable selection with finite-sample type-I error and FDR guarantees, without the power loss from data splitting that plagues HRT and LOCO-W. A key open question is whether the double-robustness conjecture (that the sign of the empirical statistic matches the theoretical one with high probability) can be fully established beyond the current stability and Wasserstein results, and whether more complex imputation models that condition on y can maintain validity while capturing richer X–y relationships. The current use of simple linear imputers for ρ_j may limit power in highly nonlinear settings, and the empirical evaluation, while thorough, is confined to moderate dimensions (p=50) and a single real dataset.
