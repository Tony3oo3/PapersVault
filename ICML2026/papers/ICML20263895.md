## Problem

Existing satisficing bandit algorithms (Michel et al. 2023; Feng et al. 2025) have regret guarantees that scale with the inverse excess gap Δ_*^{-1}, which becomes vacuous when the threshold S lies very close to the best arm's mean. This paper asks whether such a dependency is fundamentally unavoidable and characterizes the true interplay between suboptimality gaps, insufficiency gaps, and the excess gap in determining achievable satisficing pseudo-regret.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263895/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives K arms with sub-Gaussian reward distributions, a known satisficing threshold S, and constants C₁, C₂. Crucially, the learner does not know a priori whether any arm's mean exceeds S (i.e., whether the problem is realizable), so the algorithm must adapt seamlessly between the realizable regime (where satisficing pseudo-regret is the relevant metric) and the non-realizable regime (where standard pseudo-regret applies). The output is a per-round arm selection, with the theoretical guarantee being a time-independent satisficing pseudo-regret bound in the realizable case and a logarithmic standard pseudo-regret bound otherwise.

## Method

The paper introduces uncertain-UCB, a UCB1 variant that replaces the standard ln(t) in the confidence width with ln(n_i(t)) + ln(n_0(t)), where n_0(t) counts 'uncertain rounds'—rounds in which the empirical mean of the played arm falls below S + √(C₂ ln n_i(t)/n_i(t)). This mechanism causes the confidence bounds to keep growing while the learner is uncertain about satisficing, but to freeze once arms are consistently identified as sufficiently above the threshold, thereby halting further exploration of insufficient arms. The analysis partitions sufficient arms into 'very good' arms (Δ_i ≤ Δ_*/2, close to the optimum) and the rest, showing the total uncertainty contribution is O(K/Δ_*²) with high probability. The paper further establishes a matching lower bound via a two-instance construction (ν and ν^(i)) using KL divergence and Pinsker's inequality, proving that no algorithm can simultaneously achieve O(ln(1/Δ)/Δ) regret in both the small excess-gap and small insufficiency-gap regimes.

## Results

The paper establishes that uncertain-UCB achieves satisficing pseudo-regret of O(Σ_{i:Δ̃_i>0} (Δ̃_i/Δ_i²) ln(K/Δ_*) + Δ̃_i/K + Δ̃_i) in the realizable regime (Theorem 4.1), which is near-optimal given the lower bound Ω(Σ_{i:Δ̃_i>0} Δ̃_i/Δ_i²) (Theorem 3.3) and the small-excess-gap lower bound Ω(εK ln(1/Δ_*)/Δ) (Corollary 3.5). In the non-realizable regime, the algorithm recovers the standard pseudo-regret rate O(Σ_{i:Δ_i>0} ln(T)/Δ_i) (Theorem 4.2). In the transitional regime (0 < Δ_* ≤ 1/√T), it simultaneously achieves satisficing pseudo-regret O(Σ_{i:Δ̃_i>0} (Δ̃_i/Δ_i²) ln(T)) and standard pseudo-regret O(Σ_{i:Δ_i>0} ln(T)/Δ_i).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is between the regret guarantees of prior satisficing algorithms and the proposed uncertain-UCB. Michel et al. (2023) achieve O(Σ_{i:μ_i<S} Δ̃_i + 1/Δ̃_i + Δ̃_i/Δ_*²), which degrades to O(1/Δ_*²) when the excess gap Δ_* is small relative to the suboptimality gap Δ. Feng et al. (2025) achieve O(K/Δ_* polylog(1/Δ_*)), which similarly blows up as Δ_* → 0. Hüyük & Tekin (2021) achieve O(Σ_{i:Δ̃_i>0} ln(1/Δ̃_i)/Δ̃_i), which degrades when the insufficiency gap Δ̃_i is small. uncertain-UCB is the first algorithm whose realizable-regime guarantee depends only on ln(K/Δ_*) (logarithmic in 1/Δ_*) rather than polynomially on 1/Δ_* or 1/Δ̃_i, matching the lower bound up to a constant factor and a K-dependency in the logarithm. The paper further proves (Theorem 3.4, Corollary 3.5) that no algorithm can simultaneously achieve O(ln(1/Δ)/Δ) regret in both the small-excess-gap and small-insufficiency-gap regimes, establishing an inherent tradeoff that uncertain-UCB navigates by switching between ln(K/Δ_*) and ln(T) depending on the regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the satisficing bandit line initiated by Kohno & Takahashi (2017) and Reverdy et al. (2017), and builds directly on the algorithms of Michel et al. (2023) and Feng et al. (2025), which provided the first 'satisfy if you can, optimize if you must' guarantees. The key departure is conceptual: prior guarantees scaled with Δ_*^{-1} or Δ̃^{-1}, and this paper shows that only a logarithmic dependency on 1/Δ_* is truly unavoidable, via a new K-armed lower bound (generalizing the two-armed constructions of Michel et al. and Feng et al.) and a novel UCB1 variant (uncertain-UCB) that replaces ln(t) with ln(n_i(t)) + ln(n_0(t)) to decouple exploration from the excess gap. The proof techniques draw on Garivier et al. (2019) for KL-divergence-based lower bounds and Pinsker's inequality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves a concrete gap in satisficing bandit theory: previous guarantees became vacuous when the threshold sat just below the best arm's mean (small excess gap), a regime that is entirely plausible in practice. The matching upper and lower bounds (up to a constant and a log-K factor) establish the true complexity of the problem and rule out a class of algorithms that scale with Δ_*^{-1}. Open questions include whether the logarithmic factor in ln(K/Δ_*) can be further tightened, whether the stability assumption in the lower bound can be removed, and whether the uncertain-UCB idea (using a data-dependent exploration budget) transfers to richer settings such as Lipschitz or contextual bandits.
