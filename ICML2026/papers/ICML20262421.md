## Problem

Existing 4D human-object interaction (HOI) synthesis methods either require expensive 4D motion-capture data for training or treat interactions as global whole-body-object motions without reasoning about the underlying part-level mechanics. This limits their ability to generalize to novel objects, capture fine-grained contact dynamics, and handle multi-person or multi-object scenarios in a zero-shot fashion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262421/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of textured 3D object meshes (canonicalized with consistent upright orientation, with precomputed signed distance fields) and a short text prompt describing the desired interaction. It produces per-frame 4D motion sequences: object poses (3D rotation and translation) and SMPL-X body parameters (joint rotations, shape coefficients, global rotation and translation) over T=49 frames. The formulation naturally extends to multi-person and multi-object settings by expanding the graph structure.

## Method

HOI-PAGE introduces a Part Affordance Graph (PAG), a structured graph whose nodes are object parts and human body parts and whose edges encode contact relations with attributes for continuity and relative staticness. An LLM (DeepSeek-R1) infers the PAG from the text prompt, which then serves as a scaffolding across three stages: (1) grounding abstract part nodes to 3D geometry via multi-view open-vocabulary detection and SAM 2 segmentation; (2) guiding video diffusion (CogVideoX) to generate a reference HOI video using an LLM-enhanced prompt derived from the PAG; and (3) optimizing 4D object motions via a part affordance-guided loss combining part-level Chamfer fitting (3D and 2D), part-level contact constraints (continuous/static), penetration avoidance via SDF, and motion-state-aware temporal smoothness. The key insight is that explicit part-level affordance reasoning, formalized as a graph, provides localized constraints that global motion priors alone cannot capture, enabling realistic contact and generalization to unseen objects without any 4D training data.

## Results

On the Sketchfab dataset (single-person single-object), HOI-PAGE achieves the best VideoCLIP semantic alignment (0.250 vs. 0.233 for HOI-Diff and 0.239 for CHOIS), the lowest object motion smoothness error (0.006 vs. 0.035 and 0.009), the highest object motion diversity (0.80 vs. 0.72 and 0.49), and the highest part-level contact score (0.92 vs. 0.76 and 0.64). On the BEHAVE dataset, HOI-PAGE similarly leads in VideoCLIP (0.220), object smoothness (0.004), human diversity (0.61), object diversity (0.92), and contact (0.78). In perceptual studies, participants prefer HOI-PAGE over both baselines in over 91% of binary comparisons for realism and text alignment, and rate it ~4/5 in unary studies versus below 2/5 for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOI-PAGE is compared against two fully-supervised baselines: HOI-Diff (Peng et al., CVPRW 2025) and CHOIS (Li et al., ECCV 2024), both trained on real-world 4D interaction captures. On Sketchfab, HOI-PAGE wins on 6 of 7 metrics against HOI-Diff, with the largest gains in object smoothness (0.006 vs. 0.035, a ~6× improvement), contact score (0.92 vs. 0.76), and human motion diversity (0.47 vs. 0.35); HOI-Diff retains a slight edge only on human smoothness (0.007 vs. 0.008). Against CHOIS, HOI-PAGE wins on all 7 metrics, most dramatically on object diversity (0.80 vs. 0.49) and contact (0.92 vs. 0.64). On the BEHAVE dataset, HOI-PAGE again leads on 6 of 7 metrics against both baselines, with particularly large margins in object diversity (0.92 vs. 0.54/0.47) and contact (0.78 vs. 0.72/0.61). A key qualitative advantage is zero-shot generalization: HOI-Diff and CHOIS struggle with objects unseen during training (e.g., a lawnmower), producing penetration or imprecise part-level contact, whereas HOI-PAGE handles them naturally via its part affordance graph.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior HOI generation methods (HOI-Diff, CHOIS) that require 4D motion capture data for training, and from concurrent zero-shot approaches (ZeroHSI, DAViD, ZeroHOI) that treat human-object motion globally without part-level structure. Conceptually, the Part Affordance Graph (PAG) draws on the graph-based interaction representations of PiGraphs and iMapper but extends them to dynamic 4D sequences and multi-person/multi-object scenarios. The method synthesizes LLM-based reasoning, video diffusion priors, and optimization-based lifting into a unified pipeline, with the PAG serving as a structured scaffolding that grounds all three stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expensive 4D interaction captures and enabling zero-shot generalization to arbitrary objects and multi-interaction scenarios, this work opens a path toward scalable HOI content creation for animation, robotics, and embodied AI. The PAG representation is a clean, extensible abstraction that naturally accommodates additional people or objects by adding nodes and edges. Key limitations include the inability to capture fine-grained finger articulations (beyond PAG granularity) and sensitivity to failures in external components (video diffusion quality, segmentation accuracy). Open questions include whether physics-based simulation can complement the optimization for finer contact dynamics, and whether the approach scales to longer interaction sequences or more complex scene contexts.
