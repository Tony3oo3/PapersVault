## Problem

Existing video depth estimation methods, whether generative (diffusion-based) or discriminative (temporal-attention extensions of single-frame estimators), lack explicit 3D geometric awareness. Discriminative approaches rely on implicit 2D temporal smoothing that cannot enforce true 3D spatio-temporal consistency under camera rotations or drastic view changes, while generative models incur prohibitive computational costs. The paper argues that explicit perception of camera ego-motion and global 3D structure is a prerequisite for resolving the conflict between spatial detail preservation and temporal stability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video clips of fixed temporal length N=32, processed at multi-resolution (base 518×518 with shorter-edge resizing and random center cropping yielding aspect ratios from 518×518 down to 518×168). The temporal dimension is collapsed into the batch dimension for frame-wise encoding by a frozen DINOv2 ViT-Large. Outputs include per-frame affine-invariant depth maps (normalized to [0,1] in disparity space) and intermediate 6-DoF camera poses with a global scale factor, which are used both for geometric guidance during inference and for 3D point cloud reconstruction evaluation.

## Method

GemDepth introduces two synergistic modules on top of DepthAnythingV2 or VideoDepthAnything backbones. The Geometry-Embedding Module (GEM) injects a learnable camera token into the F4 feature map, processes it through a 4-layer alternating-attention transformer (built on EfficientPoseNet) to predict 6-DoF poses and a global scale factor, normalizes translations into a unified canonical frame, and encodes rotation/translation/scale via geometric MLPs to produce a metric-aware camera embedding fused with visual features. The Alternating Spatio-Temporal Transformer (ASTT) then decouples dependency modeling into two phases: Temporal Attention leverages GEM's motion priors to establish explicit point-level correspondences across frames for geometric alignment, while Spatial Attention (split into intra-frame and inter-frame components) aggregates 3D spatial features to sharpen high-frequency structural details. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: Stage 1 jointly optimizes all parameters with Huber-based pose supervision and depth losses on ~690K pose-annotated frames; Stage 2 freezes GEM and fine-tunes the remaining network on ~250K pose-free frames, making ASTT robust to imperfect geometric cues. The total loss combines scale/shift-invariant depth loss, multi-scale gradient matching, temporal geometric matching, and camera pose losses.

## Results

GemDepth-VDA achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot video depth estimation across all four benchmarks, with AbsRel of 0.157 (Sintel), 0.051 (Bonn), 0.066 (Scannet), and 0.071 (KITTI), and δ₁ accuracy of 0.827, 0.978, 0.967, and 0.955 respectively. On temporal consistency (TAE on Scannet), GemDepth-VDA records 0.47, a 17.5% improvement over VideoDepthAnything (0.57) and 59% over DepthAnythingV2 (1.14). The method achieves these results with only 99 ms inference latency on a single A800 GPU and 0.58B parameters, compared to 980 ms for DepthCrafter and 1.19B parameters for DA3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest discriminative baseline VideoDepthAnything, GemDepth-VDA reduces AbsRel by 46.8% on Sintel (0.157 vs 0.295), 28.2% on Bonn (0.051 vs 0.071), 25.8% on Scannet (0.066 vs 0.089), and 14.5% on KITTI (0.071 vs 0.083), while adding only 14 ms of latency. Against the generative baselines DepthCrafter and ChronoDepth, GemDepth-VDA achieves substantially lower AbsRel on all datasets (e.g., 0.051 vs 0.153 on Bonn for DepthCrafter) at roughly 10× lower inference cost (99 ms vs 980 ms). Against RollingDepth, GemDepth-VDA wins decisively on Sintel (0.157 vs 0.417) and Bonn (0.051 vs 0.088), though RollingDepth remains competitive on KITTI (0.107 vs 0.071). Against the 3D foundation models VGGT and DA3 (Table 3), GemDepth-VDA achieves superior F1 on Bonn (90.43 vs 78.44) and lower TAE on Scannet (0.47 vs 1.12) with half the parameter count (0.58B vs 1.19B). The paper also shows that GemDepth-DAv2, which shares the same DINOv2 encoder as VideoDepthAnything, outperforms it on all four datasets, isolating the contribution of the GEM and ASTT modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

GemDepth extends the discriminative video depth estimation lineage (NVDS, VideoDepthAnything, StableDPT) by injecting explicit 3D geometric priors—specifically, self-predicted 6-DoF camera poses—into the feature pipeline, departing from the implicit 2D temporal smoothing that characterizes prior discriminative methods. Architecturally, it builds directly on DepthAnythingV2's DINOv2+DPT backbone and VideoDepthAnything's temporal head, while borrowing the pose-estimation machinery from EfficientPoseNet (VGGT). The key conceptual departure is the argument that genuine 3D spatio-temporal consistency requires an explicit metric-aware coordinate system rather than learned temporal attention alone, positioning GemDepth between the geometrically-aware but slow generative approaches (DepthCrafter, RollingDepth) and the efficient but geometrically-blind discriminative ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GemDepth demonstrates that explicit camera-motion embeddings can close most of the gap to heavy 3D foundation models (VGGT, DA3) at roughly half the parameter count and with substantially less training data (<1M vs >10M frames), making geometry-guided video depth practical for resource-constrained deployment. The graceful degradation under injected pose noise (up to 50%) suggests the framework is robust enough for real-world scenarios where self-predicted poses are imperfect. Open questions include whether the geometric priors remain beneficial in highly dynamic scenes with large object motion (as opposed to camera motion), how the method scales to sequences far beyond 32 frames without keyframe strategies, and whether the two-stage training protocol generalizes to other backbone architectures beyond DINOv2-based DPT heads.
