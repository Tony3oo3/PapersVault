## Problem

TabPFN and related tabular foundation models that use in-context learning exhibit a severe majority-class bias: they overfit to the class distribution of the training context, causing substantial accuracy degradation when the test set has a different label distribution (label shift). This vulnerability is overlooked in prior work, and existing label-shift correction methods (e.g., EME, BBE) require estimating the unknown test prior or retraining the model, making them poorly suited to the ICL paradigm where the training prior is explicitly encoded at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the raw model logits from a TabPFN-based backbone (which already conditions on the full labeled training set in a single forward pass) together with the empirical training class prior p_train(y). It outputs a rescaled probability vector per test instance that sums to one, from which the final class label is read off. The adjustment can be computed per-instance or from the batch-average predicted distribution over the test set; both yield nearly identical results, and the default uses the batch average. No test labels, validation set, or additional model parameters are required at any point.

## Method

DistPFN multiplies the model's predicted posterior p̂(y) by an adjustment factor α = p̂(y)/p_train(y) and renormalizes, effectively squaring the posterior relative to the prior to downweight the training distribution's influence without any parameter update or architectural change. DistPFN-T extends this by first passing p̂(y) through a temperature-scaled softmax with τ = CE(p̂(y), p_train(y)), so that when the model's prediction strongly deviates from the training prior (high cross-entropy), the temperature smooths the numerator and prevents over-correction, while when the prediction is close to the prior, the adjustment remains mild. The theoretical justification frames the correction as a plug-in realization of the classical Bayes-rule prior-ratio update for label shift, where the unknown test prior is self-consistently approximated by the model's own predictive marginal rather than estimated via iterative EM. The entire procedure is a single element-wise multiplication and normalization applied post-hoc to the backbone's output, adding negligible computational cost (≈0.001 s per dataset) and requiring no retraining, no test-prior estimation, and no access to model internals beyond the final logits.

## Results

On 253 OpenML classification datasets, TabPFN-v2 degrades from 81.8% accuracy (no shift) to 72.7% under strong label shift (β=5), while DistPFN-T maintains 77.5% and DistPFN achieves 76.9%. The same pattern holds for TabICL (80.6% → 74.2% baseline vs. 77.7% with DistPFN-T) and LoCalPFN (81.6% → 77.1% baseline vs. 79.1% with DistPFN-T). Crucially, all three FMs retain their original no-shift accuracy (e.g., 81.8% for TabPFN-v2) when DistPFN/DistPFN-T is applied, confirming no performance cost in the standard setting. DistPFN-T also reduces ECE from 0.127 to 0.093 at β=5 and improves precision from 0.674 to 0.695.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DistPFN-T outperforms both classical label-shift correction methods (EME and BBE) across all three FMs under shift: for TabPFN-v2, DistPFN-T achieves 0.792 vs. EME's 0.786 and BBE's 0.789; for TabICL, 0.777 vs. 0.766 and 0.768; for LoCalPFN, 0.791 vs. 0.783 and 0.787. Critically, EME and BBE degrade performance in the no-shift setting (e.g., TabPFN-v2 drops from 0.818 to 0.801 with EME), whereas DistPFN-T preserves the original accuracy exactly. Among the three FMs, DistPFN-T applied to TabPFN-v2 (0.792 avg) and LoCalPFN (0.792 avg) both surpass TabICL (0.786 avg) under shift, and all three with DistPFN-T exceed their respective unmodified baselines by 1.7–3.5 percentage points on average. The method also generalizes to tree-based models: DistPFN-T improves XGBoost from 0.623 to 0.679, Random Forest from 0.617 to 0.730, and LightGBM from 0.618 to 0.650 at β=5. Finally, DistPFN-T (0.792 avg) approaches the oracle upper bound (0.796) that uses the true test label distribution, confirming the predicted-distribution-based adjustment is nearly optimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TabPFN and TabPFN-v2, which perform tabular classification via in-context learning by conditioning on the full training dataset in a single forward pass. It departs from classical label shift correction methods (EME, BBE, prior-ratio adjustment) that require explicit estimation of the test prior, instead exploiting the fact that ICL-based tabular foundation models encode the training prior explicitly through their inference mechanism. The proposed DistPFN can be viewed as a plug-in realization of the classical prior-ratio correction rule where the unknown test prior is approximated by the model's own predictive marginal, and DistPFN-T adds an adaptive temperature-scaling mechanism inspired by calibration literature to control adjustment strength based on prior-posterior divergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As TabPFN-based models gain adoption as zero-shot tabular foundation models, their previously unaddressed vulnerability to label shift (predicting 98.3% of samples as the majority class in extreme cases) represents a practical reliability concern in deployment settings where class distributions drift. The method's simplicity—requiring no retraining, no architectural modification, and no test prior estimation—makes it immediately applicable as a drop-in module for any ICL-based tabular FM. Key limitations include its restriction to label shift (not feature shift), its characterization as a partial rather than full correction, and the open question of whether more model-specific shift-handling strategies could further close the gap to oracle performance.
