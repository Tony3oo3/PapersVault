## Problem

Tractable singular-value dynamics for deep networks have been established only under the restrictive balancing hypothesis in linear models, leaving it unclear how to predict Jacobian spectral evolution in general piecewise-linear architectures trained from random initialization. The paper asks which depth-driven structural properties of Jacobian products create strong spectral separation and whether they can substitute for balancing to recover deep-linear-like mode-wise dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261424/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on square fixed-gates linear networks (FGLNs) of depth L and width n, where each layer is a product of a Bernoulli(p) diagonal gate and a σ-Ginibre weight matrix; a technical (r,p)-gate conditioning on rank ≥ r handles the non-invertibility that arises from zero diagonal entries. Outputs include closed-form Lyapunov exponents with finite-depth corrections (involving Haar-orthogonal minor determinants), a mode-wise ODE for singular values, and a diagonal correlation coefficient quantifying singular-vector alignment between the full Jacobian and intermediate prefix/suffix products. Experiments use MNIST (784-dim input, 10-dim output) with cross-entropy loss and SGD, reporting spectra on the hidden n×n block.

## Method

The paper identifies a three-part depth-driven mechanism that replaces balancing: (i) depth-induced exponential scaling of ordered singular values, proven via Lyapunov exponents for products of gated random matrices using exterior powers and Wishart-distribution computations, with explicit closed forms and O(1/L) finite-depth corrections; (ii) spectral separation forcing singular-vector alignment in matrix products, shown via a perturbation-theoretic argument where the top-left r×r block of U_{J_L}^T U_{A_ℓ} converges to identity at rates controlled by spectral ratios, with first-order terms given by Cholesky factors of BB^T; (iii) combining (i) and (ii) as assumptions to derive a mode-wise singular-value ODE that matches the balanced deep-linear dynamics of Arora et al. (2019) up to a multiplicative factor e^{(2+2/L)δ_k(t)}, without requiring any balancing condition. The key distinction from prior work is that separation and alignment emerge from depth itself rather than from an initialization constraint, and the analysis applies to arbitrary gate patterns (not just 0/1 masks) via the more general Proposition 7.2.

## Results

The paper proves that for i.i.d. (r,p,σ)-layers, the Lyapunov exponents γ_i governing top singular values exist and admit a closed form involving the digamma function (Theorem 5.3), with a finite-depth correction of (d_{i-1}−d_i)/L where d_i = −E[log|det Ω^{i,i}|]. Empirically, the diagonal correlation of U_{J_L}^T U_{A_ℓ} (measuring singular-vector alignment) increases from 0.26 at L=2, ℓ=1 to 0.84 at L=20, ℓ=10 at initialization, and is further amplified after MNIST training. The predicted singular-value dynamics (Proposition 7.1) match the shape of experimental trajectories on a rank-10 synthetic task with a single fine-tuned scaling constant, and the top-10 singular values clearly separate from the remainder after training, consistent with an effectively low-rank Jacobian geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing methods in the standard sense; instead it validates its theoretical predictions against empirical measurements and positions its framework relative to the balanced deep-linear baseline of Arora et al. (2019) and Saxe et al. (2014). The key claim is that the proposed mechanism—depth-induced exponential scaling (Theorem 5.3) plus separation-induced alignment (Theorem 6.1)—recovers the exact mode-wise singular-value dynamics of balanced deep-linear networks (Proposition 4.5) up to a global multiplicative factor e^{(2+2/L)δ_k(t)}, without requiring the balancing hypothesis. Empirically, the Lyapunov exponent predictions (Figure 3–4) match measured (1/L)log s_{i,L} values closely, with the finite-depth correction improving accuracy at moderate depths. The alignment metric (diagonal correlation) confirms that singular vectors of intermediate products converge to those of the full Jacobian as depth increases, a phenomenon not captured by the balanced framework. The authors note that in the FGLN setting the spectral-separation effect is amplified during training rather than compensated, distinguishing it from scenarios where training might erode the initialization-time structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the balanced deep-linear singular-value dynamics of Arora et al. (2019) to masked and gated linear networks by replacing the balancing hypothesis with two depth-driven structural properties: exponential Lyapunov scaling of ordered singular values and separation-induced singular-vector alignment. The Lyapunov exponent analysis builds on the multiplicative ergodic theorem (Furstenberg & Kesten, 1960; Oseledets, 1968) and Newman's (1986) closed-form results for Gaussian products, extending them to products involving rank-deficient Bernoulli gates via exterior-power techniques. The alignment result (Theorem 6.1) is a new structural theorem connecting spectral gaps to singular-vector convergence in matrix products, complementing but distinct from the gradient-descent alignment results of Ji & Telgarsky (2019; 2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic, empirically testable account of why deep Jacobians develop low-rank structure: depth creates spectral separation, separation forces alignment, and alignment decouples mode-wise dynamics. This offers a potential bridge from the well-understood balanced linear case to realistic random-initialized architectures, though the extension to genuinely nonlinear multi-mode settings (e.g., ReLU MLPs with changing gate patterns) remains open. The assumptions in Proposition 7.1 are deliberately strong and not expected to hold uniformly across all intermediate products, so the result is best viewed as a diagnostic framework rather than a rigorous training-time theorem. A key open question is whether the alignment structure persists and remains quantitatively controlled during training in deeper, wider, or architecturally more complex networks.
