## Problem

In CLIP-based class-incremental learning, treating classification as a single cosine-similarity match obscures two distinct sources of catastrophic forgetting: drift in the extracted visual/textual attributes and drift in how those attributes are weighted during aggregation. Prior methods adapt prompts or adapters without explicitly stabilizing either component, so incremental updates on new tasks progressively bias both the attribute geometry and the aggregation weights toward recently seen classes.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input, processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 visual encoder, and additionally consumes MLLM-generated fine-grained captions (e.g., from GPT-5 or LLaVA) to construct textual attribute embeddings. At inference, the model outputs a single class label from the union of all previously seen classes, selected via a soft mixture-of-experts aggregation weighted by Sinkhorn optimal-transport routing scores over task-level attribute manifolds. The setting is exemplar-free: no data from prior tasks is stored or revisited.

## Method

AREA decomposes CLIP classification into attribute extraction and attribute aggregation, then stabilizes each independently. For extraction, it computes class-level anchors on the hypersphere via Principal Geodesic Analysis (Fréchet mean, logarithmic map to tangent space, top-K eigenvectors of tangent covariance) for both visual features and caption-augmented text embeddings, freezing them once a class is first observed. For aggregation, it trains lightweight per-task experts with a K-dimensional scoring branch (producing sample-specific attribute weights) and a residual refinement branch, regularized by a variational information bottleneck objective instantiated as an interventional monotonicity loss (penalizing evidence increases under random occlusion) and a view-invariant compression loss (minimizing score deviation across augmented views). At inference, queries are routed to task experts via entropic Sinkhorn optimal transport between the query's Dirac measure and each task's empirical attribute manifold, and predictions are combined in a Boltzmann-weighted mixture. A theoretical result (Theorem 5.1) proves the Sinkhorn routing score is 1-Lipschitz in the input embedding, bounding routing error linearly by extraction drift magnitude.

## Results

AREA achieves the best average accuracy on 8 out of 9 benchmark datasets under the standard B0 incremental setting, with particularly large margins on fine-grained and domain-shifted datasets: 71.03% on FGVC-Aircraft (vs. 50.38% for the next-best RAPF), 97.77% on Stanford Cars (vs. 88.21% for MG-CLIP), and 61.02% on ObjectNet (vs. 53.78% for RAPF). On CIFAR-100 B0 Inc10, AREA reports 89.24% average and 83.69% last accuracy, slightly trailing MG-CLIP's 89.74% average but surpassing it on last-task accuracy. Across all nine datasets, AREA outperforms the second-best method by more than 5% in both average and last accuracy in the majority of settings, with the performance gap widening as more incremental stages are added.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AREA dominates all six baselines on the majority of datasets. Against ZS-CLIP (the frozen zero-shot reference), AREA gains 17–40 points depending on the dataset, confirming that the learned attribute anchors and task experts substantially exceed the pre-trained CLIP's zero-shot capability under incremental conditions. Compared to prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt), AREA leads by 15–25 points on fine-grained datasets (Aircraft, CUB) and by 5–10 points on broader datasets, indicating that prompt selection alone cannot compensate for the geometric drift that accumulates across tasks. Against adapter/fusion-based methods (RAPF, MG-CLIP), which are the strongest competitors, AREA still achieves clear advantages: on Aircraft, AREA's 71.03% average exceeds RAPF's 50.38% by over 20 points; on Cars, AREA's 97.77% surpasses MG-CLIP's 88.21% by nearly 10 points; and on ObjectNet, AREA's 61.02% beats RAPF's 53.78% by 7+ points. The one exception is CIFAR-100 B0 Inc10, where MG-CLIP edges out AREA on average accuracy (89.74% vs. 89.24%), though AREA recovers the lead on last-task accuracy (83.69% vs. 82.78%), suggesting AREA's advantage grows with more incremental stages. Notably, MG-CLIP suffers severe degradation under longer sequences (e.g., 12.51% on Food B50 Inc10, 12.39% on ObjectNet B100 Inc20), while AREA maintains stable performance, highlighting the robustness of the PGA-anchored extraction and OT-based routing under extended class sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the CLIP-based class-incremental learning literature (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, RAPF, MG-CLIP) but introduces a fundamentally different conceptual lens: decomposing the single cosine-similarity match into attribute extraction and attribute aggregation. For extraction, it repurposes Principal Geodesic Analysis (Fletcher et al., 2004) — originally developed for shape statistics — to construct stable class-level anchors on the hyperspherical CLIP embedding space, a geometric treatment absent from prior prompt- or adapter-based methods. For aggregation and inference, it introduces a variational information bottleneck regularizer and Sinkhorn optimal-transport routing (Peyre & Cuturi, 2019) over task attribute manifolds, respectively, combining ideas from information theory and distributional matching that had not been jointly applied to CIL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The extraction/aggregation decomposition provides a principled diagnostic for where forgetting originates in CLIP-based CIL, and the PGA-based anchoring offers a geometry-faithful way to freeze class representations that prior methods (which operate in Euclidean prompt or adapter space) do not address. The Lipschitz-continuity guarantee on OT routing gives a rare theoretical anchor for inference stability in incremental settings. However, the method depends on an external MLLM (GPT-5) for caption generation, is limited to frozen CLIP encoders, and its behavior under severe modality gaps, long-tail, or few-shot incremental settings remains unexplored. Adaptive attribute allocation (varying K per class) and extension to larger multimodal encoders are natural next steps.
