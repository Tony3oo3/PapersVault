## Problem

Unsupervised anomaly detection models degrade significantly when training data contains unlabeled anomalous samples, a common scenario in industrial settings where perfectly clean datasets are impractical to curate. Existing noise-robust methods either impose strong statistical assumptions, require knowledge of the contamination ratio, or assume specific score distributions, and none achieves robust performance at both the image and pixel levels across a wide range of noise ratios without dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263926/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images whose patch-level features are extracted by a frozen pretrained encoder (e.g., a ResNet backbone), with the training set containing an unknown mixture of normal and anomalous images. At inference, the model produces a per-patch anomaly score map of size H×W that is bilinearly resized to the original image resolution, from which both an image-level score (robust maximum of top 1% patch scores) and a pixel-level segmentation mask are derived. Class labels (product type) are assumed available during training to enable per-class memory construction and selection.

## Method

MeDS is a three-phase training framework. First, an ensemble of B=100 partial memory banks is constructed by randomly subsampling 10% of the full patch feature set; Theorem 1 proves that this sparse subsampling acts as a low-pass filter whose unimodal weight function maximizes the expected distance gap between normal and anomalous queries at intermediate memory sizes. Second, the averaged ensemble memory scores are distilled into a reconstruction score network (a bottleneck decoder in the Reverse Distillation style) via L2 loss in score space, exploiting the early-learning simplicity bias so the student fits dominant normal patterns before memorizing rare anomalies. Third, the student is fine-tuned on a progressively self-selected clean subset using a median+MAD threshold that relaxes linearly over training, with the selection criterion interpolating between the frozen distilled model and the current model; this positive feedback loop enables extended training for pixel-level precision without overfitting to contaminated samples.

## Results

On MVTecAD, Dinomaly+MeDS achieves 99.16% image-level AUROC at a 40% noise ratio, compared to 87.38% for Dinomaly alone, while maintaining 99.37% at 0% noise (vs. 99.64% baseline). Pixel-level P-AP at 40% noise reaches 68.05% (vs. 53.00% for Dinomaly). On VisA at 10% noise, Dinomaly+MeDS attains 97.43% I-AUROC and 51.46% P-AP, and on Real-IAD at 40% noise it reaches 90.99% I-AUROC and 41.22% P-AP, establishing state-of-the-art performance under noisy settings across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeDS, applied as a training framework to Dinomaly and INP-Former, dramatically outperforms all standalone noisy-AD baselines at high contamination: at 40% noise on MVTecAD, Dinomaly+MeDS (99.16% I-AUROC) exceeds SoftPatch (93.58%), InReaCh (73.57%), and FUN-AD (97.70%) by 5.6, 25.6, and 1.5 points respectively, while also surpassing them in pixel-level P-AP (68.05% vs. 35.25%, 39.67%, 61.38%). FUN-AD is the only baseline that improves with increasing noise (81.89%→97.70% I-AUROC from 0%→40%), but this comes at the cost of poor clean-data performance and inferior pixel-level localization. Among the strong baselines, Dinomaly and INP-Former degrade substantially under contamination (I-AUROC drops of ~12 and ~14 points from 0% to 40% noise), whereas their MeDS-enhanced versions remain within 0.5 points of clean performance. On Real-IAD, Dinomaly+MeDS outperforms PatchCore (90.99% vs. 88.10% I-AUROC at 40% noise) and RD (90.99% vs. 84.50%), and achieves the best P-AP (41.22%) across all noise levels. The key differentiator is stability: MeDS-enhanced methods show <1 point I-AUROC degradation from 0% to 40% noise, while all baselines degrade by 3–25 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeDS sits at the intersection of memory-based anomaly detection (PatchCore), reverse distillation (RD), and the noisy-label learning literature (bootstrapping, early-learning regularization, MentorNet-style curriculum). It departs from prior noisy-AD methods (SoftPatch, InReaCh, FUN-AD) that rely on hand-crafted pruning rules or implicit assumptions about contamination levels, instead exploiting the sparsity of randomly subsampled memory ensembles as a principled low-pass filter—a connection formalized in Theorem 1. The progressive self-selection fine-tuning stage adapts the curriculum-learning idea from classification to industrial AD, but the specific formulation combining memory distillation with median+MAD-thresholded selection is, to the authors' knowledge, novel for this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: industrial AD pipelines cannot assume clean training data, and MeDS provides a plug-and-play training wrapper (validated on HVQ, Dinomaly, INP-Former) that maintains near-clean performance up to 40% contamination without noise-ratio-specific tuning. Limitations include marginal degradation on fully clean data (the median-based selection can discard informative normal samples) and additional computational overhead from memory construction and distillation stages. Open questions include whether the low-pass-filter characterization of sparse subsampling generalizes beyond nearest-neighbor memory banks to other representation-based detectors, and whether the progressive selection strategy can be extended to settings where class labels are unavailable.
