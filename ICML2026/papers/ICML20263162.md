## Problem

Existing road representation learning methods are designed for single-city modeling and fail under cross-city distribution shifts caused by heterogeneous urban layouts. The paper identifies spectral misalignment—the graph-dependent Laplacian eigenbasis making high-frequency components inherently non-transferable—as the fundamental barrier, and shows empirically that region-level abstraction reduces cross-city spectral discrepancy by over 90% while segment-level distributions remain severely divergent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263162/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes directed road segment graphs with discrete attributes (road type, lane number, discretized length), GPS trajectory sequences mapped to segment sequences via FMM, and a learnable city identity embedding. It produces segment-level representations structured as a gated fusion of two disentangled streams: a low-frequency component encoding city-invariant functional semantics and a high-frequency component encoding city-specific geometric residuals, alongside region-level representations anchored to a shared prototype space. The dual-path output is designed so that the low-frequency stream is directly transferable across cities while the high-frequency stream is adaptively modulated per city.

## Method

CoSpec operates in two coupled stages. First, a Manifold Alignment module aggregates segments into functional regions via soft quantization against city-adaptive prototypes (a global dictionary deformed by a low-rank residual adapter conditioned on city embedding), then processes the induced region graph with a Graph Transformer using structural bias from the learned adjacency. Second, a Spectral-Aware Dual-Path Reconstruction module separates the signal: the low-frequency path up-projects region features to segments and applies large-order Chebyshev graph convolution as a learnable low-pass filter to recover the global functional skeleton, while the high-frequency path extracts residuals via spectral subtraction, applies FiLM modulation conditioned on the city embedding to align geometric styles, and applies small-order ChebConv to preserve locality. A learned spatial gate adaptively fuses the two streams. Training uses a composite objective combining reconstruction loss, spectral orthogonality (column-wise cosine), Dirichlet-energy smoothness on both static and trajectory graphs, and an EM-based prototype alignment loss (InfoNCE with pseudo-labels). Two theorems provide theoretical grounding: region aggregation is a 1-Lipschitz contraction bounding the Wasserstein distance between city distributions, and the hierarchical loop operator acts as a spectral low-pass filter suppressing high-frequency Dirichlet energy.

## Results

CoSpec achieves the best performance across all three downstream tasks (Next Location Prediction, Label Classification, Destination Prediction) on all three cities (Beijing, Chengdu, Xi'an). On Next Location Prediction, CoSpec reaches ACC@1 of 0.438/0.456/0.410 and ACC@5 of 0.615/0.677/0.587 on BJ/CD/XA respectively. On Label Classification, it attains F1 of 0.851/0.809/0.756 and AUC of 0.922/0.879/0.836. On Destination Prediction, it achieves ACC@1 of 0.309/0.312/0.310 and ACC@5 of 0.453/0.438/0.447, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline HiFiNet by 1–3 percentage points on ACC@1 across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSpec outperforms all 11 baselines on every task and every city, with no losses or ties. The strongest competitor is HiFiNet (the prior SOTA spectral method); CoSpec improves over it by 1.2 pp on Next Location ACC@1 (BJ: 0.438 vs 0.426), 1.3 pp on Label Classification F1 (BJ: 0.851 vs 0.838), and 1.2 pp on Destination Prediction ACC@1 (BJ: 0.309 vs 0.297). Among spatial GNNs, HRNR is the best on local tasks (e.g., 0.412 ACC@1 on BJ Next Location) but falls behind CoSpec by 2.6 pp, while NFormer (a Graph Transformer) is competitive on XA Destination Prediction (0.302 vs CoSpec's 0.310) yet trails on all other settings. Random-walk baselines (DW, IRN2Vec, Toast) are consistently the weakest, with IRN2Vec scoring as low as 0.324 ACC@1 on CD Next Location versus CoSpec's 0.456. Spectral GNNs (GCN, FAGCN, ChebNetII) form a mid-tier group; FAGCN leads among them on XA Destination Prediction (0.302 ACC@1) but still trails CoSpec by 0.8 pp. The gap between CoSpec and HiFiNet is largest on Chengdu (the ring-structured city), where CoSpec gains 1.4 pp on Next Location ACC@1 and 1.3 pp on Label Classification F1, suggesting the spectral disentanglement is most beneficial under strong structural heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoSpec extends HiFiNet's hierarchical frequency decomposition from single-city to cross-city road representation learning, while departing from prior cross-city transfer methods (meta-learning, adversarial domain adaptation) that enforce indiscriminate spatial alignment and erase city-specific structure. The key conceptual departure is identifying spectral misalignment—where Laplacian eigenbases are graph-specific and thus non-transferable—as the fundamental barrier, and addressing it through explicit low/high-frequency disentanglement with a shared prototype space. The theoretical grounding via Wasserstein contraction under Lipschitz aggregation and Dirichlet energy reduction under block averaging provides a principled justification for hierarchical abstraction that prior cross-city methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work offers a principled recipe for building a single road representation model that generalizes across structurally heterogeneous cities, which is practically important for data-scarce urban areas where training city-specific models is infeasible. The theoretical results (Wasserstein contraction, low-pass filtering interpretation) give clear design guidance: aggregate to a coarse level for alignment, then recover fine details through a dedicated high-frequency path with city-adaptive modulation. Open questions include whether the framework extends to dynamic/temporal road signals, whether zero-shot transfer to entirely unseen cities is achievable, and whether the prototype-based alignment generalizes to urban domains with less consistent cross-city semantics than road networks.
