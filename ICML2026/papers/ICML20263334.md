## Problem

State-space models like Mamba achieve transformer-competitive performance with linear-time inference, but their recurrent architecture lacks the explicit attention heads and neuron-level representations that have made transformers amenable to mechanistic interpretability. This opacity makes it difficult to diagnose why SSMs fail on tasks like in-context learning and long-context retrieval, and limits the ability to target specific components for improvement without full retraining.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a pre-trained SSM (primarily Mamba-130M, also Mamba-2, DenseMamba, Hyena, MiniPLM-Mamba) and a text corpus (The Pile for tuning, Wikitext-2-v1 for interpretability analysis) as input. It produces three outputs: (1) a set of identified bottleneck layers and Delta-sensitive activation subspaces with associated scalar steering factors, (2) steered next-token predictions obtained by multiplying selected activations by a scalar at inference time with no weight updates, and (3) Stable-Mamba, a modified architecture with 256 additional parameters trained from scratch. The steering hyperparameters are tuned once per model on The Pile and transfer task-agnostically across six diverse benchmarks without per-task adjustment.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage interpretability-and-steering pipeline. First, activation subspaces are defined by constructing an implicit attention matrix from Mamba's SSM parameters (via the attention-mapping procedure of Ali et al.), then computing a weighted sum of hidden states using token-level importance weights derived from that matrix. Second, Stochastic Parameter Decomposition (SPD) is applied to identify a layer-level routing bottleneck (Layer 20 in Mamba-130M, characterized by an entropy spike and extreme post-ablation KL divergence), and within that layer, Delta-sensitive subspaces are selected by measuring activation variance across the SSM's Δ-mediated state updates and validating importance via ablation-induced top-1 accuracy drops. The steering intervention is a simple scalar multiplication: 435 subspaces with >2% ablation drop are amplified 5×, and 155 subspaces with ≤2% drop are amplified 2×, yielding an average 8.27% improvement across 7 SSMs and 6 benchmarks. As an architectural validation, Stable-Mamba jointly applies multi-timescale state dynamics, ensembled output projection, sparse global context injection, learned gating, adaptive compression, gradient scaling, and scaled residual connections to mitigate the identified bottleneck from training.

## Results

Post-hoc steering of Delta-sensitive activation subspaces at Layer 20 improves Mamba-130M by an average of 23.32% across 6 diverse benchmarks (TriviaQA, SQuAD, MuSiQue, IFEval, RULER, DROP), with the largest gain of 17.5% on IFEval. Generalizing the same interpretability pipeline to 7 SSMs yields an average improvement of 8.27% without task-specific tuning. Stable-Mamba, which adds only 256 parameters and is retrained from scratch on The Pile, achieves +15.3 pp on RULER QA and +35 pp on Long Range Arena Pathfinder over vanilla Mamba-130M, while also outperforming GPT-2 Small (124M) on all three long-context benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stable-Mamba-130M outperforms vanilla Mamba-130M on all three long-context benchmarks (RULER AVG 81.67 vs 72.57, LRA AVG 68.00 vs 52.00, LongBench v2 AVG 87.45 vs 83.77), with the most dramatic gain on LRA Pathfinder (95% vs 60%). Steered Mamba-130M also beats vanilla Mamba on every benchmark without any retraining, confirming the bottleneck is real and performance-limiting. Against GPT-2 Small (124M), Stable-Mamba-130M wins on RULER (81.67 vs 70.33) and LongBench v2 (87.45 vs 75.37) but trails on LRA Pathfinder (95 vs 100), though it still leads on LRA AVG (68.00 vs 62.55). Mamba-2 shows a striking failure mode with only 1% on LRA Pathfinder, while Hyena and MiniPLM-Mamba-130M are substantially weaker across all benchmarks. Excluding saturated cases where no improvement is possible, steering yields a 23.0% average gain across the 7 SSMs tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends mechanistic interpretability from the transformer setting (where neurons and attention heads provide natural intervention targets) to state-space models, which lack such explicit components. It builds on three key tools: the hidden-attention mapping of Ali et al. (2025b) to define activation subspaces in Mamba, Stochastic Parameter Decomposition (Bushnaq et al., 2025) to locate layer-level routing bottlenecks, and sparse autoencoders with dictionary learning to characterize feature utilization. The conceptual departure is framing SSM interpretability around 'activation subspace bottlenecks'—a steering-oriented lens that replaces transformer-specific neuron/attention-head analysis with recurrent-subspace analysis, and then closing the loop with a concrete intervention (scalar activation scaling) and an architectural validation (Stable-Mamba).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that interpretability methods can move beyond description to practical intervention for SSMs: a single scalar multiplication at a specific layer, identified purely from The Pile, improves 7 SSM models across 6 benchmarks by 8.27% on average without task-specific tuning. Stable-Mamba further validates the bottleneck identification by showing that minimal architectural changes (256 parameters) guided by the same interpretability pipeline yield consistent long-context gains. Key limitations include the focus on the Mamba family at relatively small scales (130M–2.8B), the lack of independent ablations for each Stable-Mamba modification, and evaluation on standard text tasks only. Open questions include whether the activation-subspace-bottleneck framework generalizes to other architectures (e.g., hybrid transformer-SSM models), larger parameter regimes, and non-text modalities where SSMs are increasingly deployed.
