## Problem

Existing targeted transfer attacks on closed-source MLLMs rely on point-wise feature alignment in surrogate models, causing adversarial perturbations to overfit specific feature spaces and generalize poorly to unseen target architectures. Additionally, ensemble strategies for combining surrogate models are either static (uniform weighting) or heuristic (loss-convergence-based), failing to capture the complex, asymmetric, and task-dependent transferability relationships among different MLLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261668/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a source image x_nat and a target image x_tar (both RGB), the method produces an imperceptible perturbation δ with ‖δ‖∞ ≤ 16/255 such that the adversarial image x_adv = x_nat + δ causes a closed-source MLLM to generate a caption semantically aligned with x_tar. The perturbation is optimized over 300 PGD steps (step size 1/255) using M=5 augmented views per image for distribution estimation, and attack success is measured via GPTScore semantic similarity between the generated caption and the target image's caption, with ASR defined as the fraction of samples exceeding a 0.3 similarity threshold.

## Method

DART decomposes the attack into two complementary subproblems. For generalization, it replaces point-wise feature matching with distributional alignment: M semantic-preserving augmentations (RandomResizedCrop, RandomHorizontalFlip, ColorJitter) of each image yield empirical latent distributions, and the Squared Cosine Energy Distance—a non-parametric, strictly proper scoring rule—minimizes divergence between adversarial and target distributions, with global features aligned via point-to-distribution distance and K-means-clustered local features via distribution-to-distribution distance. For specialization, a heterogeneous GraphSAGE-based GNN Attack Router is pre-trained offline on historical attack logs (task nodes with concatenated ViT embeddings, model nodes with one-hot encodings, edges weighted by GPTScore transfer scores) to predict per-model transferability for new tasks, then selects the Top-K=2 surrogate models at inference. A dynamic Softmax weighting scheme (temperature T=1) re-weights surrogate losses each iteration based on loss-convergence ratios, directing optimization toward the most challenging models in the ensemble.

## Results

On closed-source MLLMs, DART achieves ASR of 89.5% on GPT-4o, 89.0% on GPT-4.1, 32.0% on Claude-3.7, and 80.5% on Gemini-2.0, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline FOA-Attack by 0.5–8.5 percentage points. On open-source MLLMs, DART reaches 91.5% ASR on LLaVA-1.5-7B and 86.5% on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (vs. 80.0% for FOA-Attack). The average similarity score (AvgSim) on GPT-4o is 0.53 for DART versus 0.50 for FOA-Attack, confirming stronger semantic alignment with target captions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DART outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated model. Against the two strongest recent competitors, FOA-Attack (Jia et al., 2025) and M-Attack (Li et al., 2025), DART gains +4.0% ASR on GPT-4o, +8.5% on Claude-3.7, and +6.5% on Gemini-2.0 over FOA-Attack, with the largest relative improvement on the more robust Claude models. The older baselines—AttackVLM (Zhao et al., 2023), AdvDiffVLM (Guo et al., 2024), SSA-CWA (Dong et al., 2023), and AnyAttack (Zhang et al., 2024)—achieve ASR below 13% on all closed-source models, confirming that point-wise feature alignment without distributional awareness is fundamentally insufficient for transfer attacks on modern MLLMs. On open-source models (Table 1), DART similarly leads, with the largest margin over FOA-Attack appearing on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (+6.5%) and Qwen2.5-VL-3B (+8.5%), suggesting the distributional alignment benefit grows with model scale and architectural divergence from the CLIP surrogates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the targeted transfer attack paradigm for MLLMs established by AttackVLM, M-Attack, and FOA-Attack, which all rely on point-wise latent feature alignment between adversarial and target samples. DART departs from this lineage in two ways: it replaces point-to-point feature matching with distribution-level alignment using non-parametric Energy Distance (drawing on statistical two-sample testing literature), and it substitutes static or heuristic ensemble weighting with a GNN-based Attack Router that learns task-dependent surrogate model selection from historical attack logs. The dynamic loss-ratio weighting mechanism is explicitly credited to FOA-Attack, while the distributional framing and relational routing are the paper's novel contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean EoT-equivalent ablation (Table 6) demonstrates that the performance gain comes from distribution-level calibration rather than gradient smoothing from augmentation, which is a useful conceptual clarification for the adversarial transfer literature. However, the framework's practical impact is tempered by its reliance on a homogeneous pool of CLIP-based surrogates, the need for large-scale offline attack logs to train the GNN router, and a 2× computational overhead over strong baselines. Open questions include whether distribution alignment generalizes to non-CLIP encoders (SigLIP, DINOv2, diffusion-based), whether the GNN router can adapt online to rapidly evolving model families, and whether the approach transfers to physical-world attack settings.
