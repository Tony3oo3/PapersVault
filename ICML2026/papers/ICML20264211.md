## Problem

Reconstructing dynamic 3D scenes from blurry monocular videos is severely under-constrained because motion blur temporally integrates object displacement into spatial observations, entangling geometry with motion and causing unconstrained Gaussian deformation to collapse into degenerate needle-like or isotropic shapes. Existing dynamic 3DGS methods either assume sharp inputs with accurate poses, rely on auxiliary optical-flow networks that degrade under blur, or lack explicit physical regularization to resolve monocular motion ambiguities, leaving robust real-world dynamic reconstruction an open challenge.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurry monocular video sequence (captured with real-world motion blur from both camera shake and fast object motion) and initializes canonical Gaussians via SfM. It produces a combined static-plus-dynamic 3D Gaussian scene representation that supports real-time novel view synthesis at 200–700 FPS on a single RTX 3090, using a compact set of Gaussians (typically 15k–65k primitives) with a multi-resolution LoD hierarchy (LoD1–LoD5) for progressive detail.

## Method

Kinematics-GS decomposes the scene into static and dynamic Gaussians by thresholding the temporal variance of predicted positional offsets (no auxiliary segmentation), then applies a coarse-to-fine deformation strategy where neighborhood-aggregated offsets capture low-frequency motion and a per-Gaussian MLP residual recovers fine-grained non-rigid details. The key novelty is a kinematics-guided covariance refinement: for each dynamic Gaussian, an orthonormal motion-aligned basis is constructed from its instantaneous velocity vector, and the Gaussian's anisotropic scale is reparameterized so that the longitudinal axis explicitly integrates physical displacement over the exposure interval (s_z' = σ_z + η‖v‖Δt), with an alignment factor η that suppresses spurious elongation under noisy velocity estimates. Learnable residual rotation and scale in log-space allow local deviations beyond the kinematic prior, while the final covariance is reconstructed as R·S·Sᵀ·Rᵀ to guarantee symmetry and positive-definiteness. Training is end-to-end with a composite loss combining L1+D-SSIM photometric terms, position-offset regularization for both static and dynamic sets, and an anisotropy penalty to prevent extreme aspect ratios, all within a FLoD multi-resolution schedule over 30k iterations.

## Results

On the BARD-GS real-world blurry dataset, Kinematics-GS achieves an average PSNR of ~24.3 dB, outperforming the best baseline (D3DGS+MPR at ~20.0 dB) by over 4 dB. On the DEOs dataset featuring deformable and elastic objects, the method reaches ~30.5 dB average PSNR versus ~28.5 dB for the strongest baseline (GauFre). The method also achieves 200–700 FPS rendering speed, an order-of-magnitude improvement over baselines (13–125 FPS), while using significantly fewer Gaussians (e.g., 16,966 vs. 201,634 for D3DGS on ROLLDICE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Kinematics-GS outperforms all four baselines on the majority of scenes across both datasets. Against 4DGS and D3DGS (with and without MPRNet deblurring), the gains are most pronounced on BARD-GS, where the method leads by 3–6 dB in PSNR (e.g., 24.64 vs. 19.71 on TOYCAR), indicating that explicit kinematic regularization is far more effective than post-hoc deblurring or unconstrained 4D deformation. Against Deblur3DGS, which explicitly models camera-motion blur, Kinematics-GS still wins on most BARD-GS scenes (e.g., 28.27 vs. 26.16 on RUBIKCUBE) and all DEOs scenes, showing that object-motion-aligned deformation captures blur that camera-only models miss. The closest competitor is GauFre, which also performs static-dynamic decomposition; on DEOs, GauFre is competitive on ROLLDICE (34.06 vs. 35.05) and STRIKE (32.65 vs. 33.76), but Kinematics-GS pulls ahead on BOUNCE (30.41 vs. 28.37) and REDDICE (27.90 vs. 26.48), where non-rigid elastic deformation is more challenging. In rendering speed, Kinematics-GS achieves 200–700 FPS versus 13–125 FPS for baselines, a 5–26× speedup, while using 2–10× fewer Gaussians.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends deformation-based dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting (D3DGS, GauFre) by introducing a kinematics-guided covariance refinement that constructs a local motion-aligned orthonormal basis from predicted velocity to constrain Gaussian anisotropy, departing from prior reliance on optical flow consistency or local rigidity regularizations. It adopts the FLoD level-of-detail framework for multi-resolution training and integrates a coarse-to-fine deformation strategy (neighborhood aggregation plus residual MLP refinement) to stabilize optimization under severe blur. Unlike BARD-GS and Deblur3DGS, which address blur through explicit deblurring modules or sparse controls, Kinematics-GS models blur as motion-aligned shape deformation within the Gaussian parameterization itself, requiring no auxiliary deblurring network or flow estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap where most dynamic 3DGS methods assume sharp inputs and accurate poses, breaking down in real-world handheld scenarios with combined camera and object motion blur. The kinematic prior provides a physically grounded, supervision-free mechanism to resolve monocular motion ambiguities, and the introduced DEOs dataset (deformable/elastic objects under fast motion) fills a benchmark gap for non-rigid dynamics under realistic blur. Limitations include roughly 2× training overhead from the kinematic modules and memory constraints for long sequences, while the approach is currently tailored to deformation-based 3DGS rather than 4D representations. Open questions include whether the kinematic basis generalizes to multi-object scenes with occlusion and whether the method can be extended to handle more complex physical interactions (e.g., fluid dynamics, cloth).
