## Problem

Standard GNNs are not continuous with respect to natural modes of graph convergence: they produce drastically different latent embeddings for graphs that describe the same underlying object at different resolution scales (e.g., a molecule at atomic vs. coarsified resolution). This discontinuity, rooted in commonly used propagation schemes, prevents models trained at one resolution from generalizing to equivalent graphs at another, contradicting the prevailing belief that GNNs define stable mappings over graph space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes weighted graphs with node weights (encoding local volumes or sampling densities) and edge weights, along with node feature matrices. The critical input constraint is that the same object may be represented at multiple resolution scales—e.g., a molecule with individual hydrogen atoms versus one where hydrogens are merged into heavy-atom super-nodes via graph coarsification. Outputs are graph-level latent embeddings that provably converge across resolution scales, which then feed downstream regression (atomization energy) or node-classification heads.

## Method

The paper identifies that standard propagation (renormalized adjacency in MPNNs, normalized Laplacian polynomials in spectral networks) disrupts information flow between heavy atoms as intra-cluster edge weights diverge, effectively disconnecting the graph in the limit. The fix replaces these schemes with Laplace-transform propagation matrices ψ(L) = ∫₀^∞ e^{-tL}ψ̂(t)dt built from the unnormalized natural Laplacian L = M⁻¹(D−A), which respects heat-kernel convergence e^{-tL_ω} → J^↑ e^{-tL̲} J^↓ as ω→∞. For spectral networks, Chebyshev polynomials of the normalized Laplacian are swapped for Laplace-transform functions of L; for MPNNs, the adjacency matrix A in the message function is replaced by ψ(L) with sum aggregation. Theorem 7.1 proves that any network using such propagation is scale-continuous: ‖F_ω − F̲‖ → 0 whenever G_ω converges to G̲ in the heat-kernel sense. Empirically, Laplace-transform GNNs reduce cross-resolution embedding differences by 10²–10⁴× and achieve near-identical MAE in same- and cross-resolution settings on QM7.

## Results

On QM7 atomization energy regression, the proposed Laplace-transform propagation methods (Spectralexp., Spectralres., MPNNexp., MPNNres.) achieve MAE of 15.9–18.0 kcal/mol in same-resolution settings and 15.8–17.9 kcal/mol in cross-resolution settings, showing essentially no degradation. In contrast, all standard GNN baselines degrade substantially: GIN's MAE jumps from 658.4 to 1835.4 kcal/mol, Lanczos from 938.4 to 658.6, and GCN from 136.7 to 138.1. The latent embedding difference ‖F − F̲‖ across resolution scales is reduced by factors of 10² to 10⁴ (e.g., from 1286.1 for Lanczos to 0.2 for MPNNexp.), confirming the theoretical continuity guarantee.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All seven standard GNN baselines (GCN, GATv2, ChebNet, GIN, SAG/SAG-M, UFGNet, Lanczos) fail to generalize across resolution scales, with cross-resolution MAE typically 2–10× worse than same-resolution MAE. GIN is the most severely affected, with cross-resolution MAE of 1835.4 kcal/mol (vs. 658.4 same-resolution) and an embedding difference of 881.2 (High→Low) or 2875.8 (Low→High). Even the best-performing standard method, UFGNet (131.5 same-res), degrades to 148.1 cross-res. The proposed Laplace-transform methods (Spectralexp., Spectralres., MPNNexp., MPNNres.) achieve MAE of 15.9–18.0 kcal/mol in both same- and cross-resolution settings, outperforming all baselines by factors of 7–100× in cross-resolution MAE. Their embedding differences (0.2–0.3) are 2–4 orders of magnitude smaller than those of standard GNNs (18.0–1286.1), directly validating the theoretical continuity guarantee of Theorem 7.1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of GNN stability and transferability work (Levie et al. 2019a; Ruiz et al. 2020; Maskey et al. 2021; Le & Jegelka 2023), which proves continuity under specific convergence notions (graphon, graphing, graphop, metric-measure space) but does not cover the heat-kernel convergence that arises naturally from graph coarsification. The closest prior work is Koke (2023), which studies resolvent-based spectral filters for directed graphs; here the authors generalize to the full class of Laplace-transform propagation matrices and extend the theory to message-passing networks, not just spectral ones. The solution draws on classical heat-kernel and resolvent theory from graph signal processing (Chung 1997; Bauer et al. 2017) to replace adjacency-based propagation with diffusion-based propagation, a conceptual shift from local message passing to global spectral filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result challenges a widely held assumption in the GNN community that standard architectures define stable, continuous mappings, with direct practical consequences for any application involving multi-scale graph data (molecular modeling, point clouds, social networks at varying granularity). The proposed Laplace-transform propagation fix is provably scale-continuous for both spectral and message-passing architectures, and the empirical gains are dramatic (cross-resolution MAE drops from ~137 to ~17 kcal/mol on QM7). Open questions include the computational cost of computing e^{-tL} on very large graphs (mitigated by exponential decay of entries), whether the approach extends to directed or hypergraph settings, and how it interacts with graph pooling or hierarchical architectures that explicitly change graph size during inference.
