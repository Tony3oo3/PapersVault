## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning for instruction-following induces semantic mode collapse, concentrating model outputs on the low-entropy semantics of the post-training data at the expense of the high-entropy diversity learned during pretraining. This collapse worsens with model scale—an inverse scaling trend absent in base models—and persists across prompting strategies designed to elicit diversity. No principled training method existed to preserve pretraining's semantic diversity while still adopting the quality improvements of post-training.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a standard text prompt and autoregressively generates a response, but it first implicitly samples a set of structured semantic annotations (key-value tags such as topic, domain, entity, location, action) that serve as a semantic plan, then generates the response conditioned on that plan. The annotations are stripped before evaluation, so the final output is a plain text response. During training, the input format is prompt ∥ annotation ∥ response, with annotation and prompt tokens masked from the loss so that only the response conditional is updated.

## Method

The paper proposes annotation-anchored training, which factorizes the response distribution into a semantic marginal R(z|x) and a semantics-conditional response Q(y|x,z), then selectively updates only the conditional during post-training. During pretraining, documents are split into chunks and each chunk is paired with LLM-generated semantic annotations (key-value tags), teaching the model a rich, high-entropy annotation distribution over the full breadth of pretraining data. During post-training, the model is trained on prompt–annotation–response sequences but the loss is masked on both prompt and annotation tokens, so gradients update only Q*(y|x,z) while R(z|x) remains anchored to its pretrained state. At inference, the model implicitly samples an annotation (a semantic plan) and then generates a response conditioned on it, transferring pretraining's semantic diversity into the post-trained model. This yields a 6× reduction in diversity collapse versus standard SFT while preserving benchmark task performance within roughly one point.

## Results

On the STORIES benchmark, the 2.5B annotation-anchored model achieves a semantic entropy of 8.30 versus 6.88 for standard SFT, closing ~85% of the gap to the base model (8.56) and yielding a 6× reduction in diversity collapse. Across NOVELTYBENCH and WILDCHAT, annotation-anchored training restores positive scaling of diversity with model size, whereas standard SFT exhibits inverse scaling. Task performance is preserved: average zero-shot accuracy across nine reasoning/knowledge benchmarks is 62.3% (annotated) vs. 62.4% (SFT) at 2.5B, and GSM8k accuracy is 36.4% vs. 35.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary method comparison is between standard SFT and annotation-anchored training, both post-trained on TULU3 SFT. On STORIES semantic entropy, annotation-anchored models outperform standard SFT at every scale (7.87 vs. 6.86 at 0.6B; 7.67 vs. 6.64 at 1B; 8.30 vs. 6.88 at 2.5B), with the gap widening as models grow—directly reversing the inverse-scaling trend of SFT. On task benchmarks (ARC, BoolQ, COPA, HellaSwag, OpenBookQA, PIQA, SciQ, WinoGrande, GSM8k), the two methods are statistically indistinguishable, with average differences of ≤1.1 points across all scales. The paper additionally uses Qwen2.5 (0.5B–72B) and Llama 3 (1B–70B) base/instruction-tuned pairs to demonstrate that the diversity collapse and its worsening with scale is a general phenomenon across model families, not specific to the authors' OLMo-2 architecture. Ablations confirm that both annotated pretraining and inference-time annotation sampling are necessary: removing either component drops diversity to near standard-SFT levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of latent-variable models for controllable generation (CVAE dialogue, Plan-and-Write, intent-factored generation), diversity-preserving training objectives (unlikelihood training, entropy-regularized SFT, diffuse-distribution forcing), and the recent empirical line documenting semantic mode collapse in post-trained LMs (NoveltyBench, persona collapse, conceptual diversity reduction). Its key conceptual departure is from aggregate diversity regularization (entropy penalties, KL constraints on the full response distribution) to explicitly preserving the semantic marginal via a natural-language annotation variable, repurposing the plan-and-write tradition not for user-specified control but for distributional preservation across the pretraining-to-post-training transition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that semantic diversity collapse worsens with model scale is a fundamental concern for practitioners deploying larger models, and the paper provides both a causal explanation (likelihood fitting transfers the low-entropy semantic prior of post-training data) and a simple, practical fix. The method's minimal overhead—annotate documents, mask annotation loss during SFT, sample annotations at inference—makes it broadly applicable to any post-training pipeline. Open questions include extension beyond creative tasks to structured reasoning (math, code), the computational cost of annotation generation at web scale, whether the annotation schema must be task-specific, and whether the approach composes with preference-based alignment (RLHF/DPO) stages beyond SFT.
