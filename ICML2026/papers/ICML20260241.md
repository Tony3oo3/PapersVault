## Problem

RNNs and DNNs differ structurally only by weight sharing across timesteps (layers), yet they exhibit distinct functional properties. While feature learning theory for DNNs in the μP regime is well developed, the corresponding theory for RNNs is far less mature, and it remains unknown how weight sharing specifically shapes representations, generalization, and the existence of distinct learning phases. This paper asks: what are the functional consequences of the single architectural difference (shared vs. independent weights) when both architectures are placed on a common theoretical footing?

## Input / Output

The theory takes as input a network architecture (RNN or DNN with T timesteps/layers, hidden dimension N, input dimension D), input sequences x, label sequences y (with a subset of supervised timesteps T), weight scale parameters (u, w, v) that set the μP prior variances, and a noise level κ. It produces self-consistent representational kernels Φ (post-activation) and H (pre-activation) of size PT₋ × PT₋, from which predictor statistics f and generalization properties follow. The key structural output is a masking operation [[·]] on temporal correlations: the full kernel for RNNs versus its diagonal for DNNs, which encodes the entire architectural difference in a single algebraic operation.

## Method

The paper develops a unified mean-field theory by casting SGLD training as Bayesian inference: the stationary distribution over weights is a Gaussian prior times a likelihood, and integrating out the (conditionally Gaussian) weights analytically trades the high-dimensional parameter space for a distribution over a single neuron's pre-activation h, governed by kernel order parameters Φ and H that concentrate in the large-N limit. The saddle-point equations yield a closed-form expression for the conjugate field C̃ that decomposes into a label-alignment term and a forward-pass prior term, with the architecture-dependent masking [[Φ⁻]] (full matrix for RNNs, diagonal for DNNs) as the sole structural difference. For endpoint-supervised tasks, the theory reveals a second-order phase transition (critical exponent 1/2) in the RNN's off-diagonal kernel, controlled by the signal-to-noise ratio λ = ‖y‖²/(vw^{T-2}u): below the threshold, RNN and DNN kernels coincide; above it, only the RNN develops temporal coherence. For sequential tasks, a perturbative expansion around the NNGP kernel shows that the RNN's masking enables label messages to propagate across unsupervised timesteps (interpolation), while the DNN's diagonal masking destroys this structure, yielding an inductive bias that improves sample efficiency despite lower expressivity.

## Results

The theory predicts a second-order phase transition in linear RNNs (critical exponent 1/2) controlled by the signal-to-noise ratio λ = ‖y‖²/(vw^{T-2}u): below the critical threshold, RNN and DNN representational kernels coincide (temporally incoherent regime), while above it, only RNNs develop off-diagonal temporal correlations (temporally coherent regime). Empirical SGLD simulations confirm the theory: centered kernel alignment (CKA) between measured and predicted kernels increases with network width N, and the off-diagonal order parameter H^{T-1,T-2} follows the analytical prediction (Fig. 4a). For sequential tasks, RNNs achieve lower generalization error than DNNs at every number of supervised timesteps (Fig. 5b), with the gap widening as supervision decreases, demonstrating a sample-efficiency advantage from weight-sharing inductive bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are between RNNs and DNNs under the unified kernel framework rather than against external baselines. In endpoint-supervised tasks, both architectures learn qualitatively similar pattern-by-pattern structure (block structure in H^{T-T_-}), but only RNNs exhibit a phase transition to temporal coherence for strong learning signal λ, while DNNs remain temporally incoherent due to the diagonal masking [[H^-]] = diag(H^-). In sequential tasks, the RNN's weight sharing induces an inductive bias that interpolates label information across unsupervised timesteps (via the second-order correction Δ_{2,RNN} in Eq. 10), whereas the DNN's independent weights produce an effectively white prior outside supervised points, causing 'regression to the mean' (f^t = 0). The theory quantitatively matches SGLD weight-space simulations: CKA between empirical and theoretical kernels converges to 1 as N → ∞, and the measured off-diagonal kernel order parameter tracks the analytical phase transition curve. The paper also notes that in the NNGP (lazy) limit, temporal correlations vanish for point-symmetric activations (recovering Segadlo et al., 2022), confirming that the identified phase transition is a genuine feature-learning phenomenon absent in the lazy regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate theoretical threads: the kernel/mean-field theory of feature learning in DNNs under μP scaling (Fischer et al., 2024; Yang & Hu, 2020) and the field-theoretic treatment of RNNs in the NNGP limit (Segadlo et al., 2022). The key conceptual move is showing that the sole architectural difference between RNNs and DNNs—weight sharing across timesteps—reduces to a simple masking operation on the temporal kernel, allowing a single saddle-point framework to describe both. The Bayesian interpretation of SGLD convergence (Seung et al., 1992) provides the probabilistic foundation, while the μP scaling is recovered as a consistency requirement rather than imposed externally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theory provides a clean mechanistic link between architecture (weight sharing) and function (temporal coherence, inductive bias), identifying a second-order phase transition in RNNs absent in DNNs and showing how weight sharing enables interpolation across unsupervised timesteps. Limitations include reliance on linear activations for closed-form analytics, minimal task settings (orthogonal inputs, P=1 or 4), and the SGLD approximation of SGD dynamics. Open questions include extending the framework to transformers (which share QKV projections across timesteps), gated RNNs, nonlinear feature learning in proportional limits, and connecting the identified phase transition to the chaos transition found in continuous-time RNN learning dynamics (Bordelon et al., 2024; Clark et al., 2026).
