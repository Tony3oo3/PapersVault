## Problem

Existing differentially private synthetic text generation methods are severely inefficient: DP fine-tuning is data-hungry and computationally expensive, while inference-time private prediction methods incur per-token privacy costs that scale with batch size and sequence length, making them impractical in low-data regimes or for large-scale sampling. The paper asks how to generate high-fidelity synthetic data with rigorous DP guarantees while decoupling the privacy budget from the generation process itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes a private text dataset (with class/attribute labels), a frozen pretrained LLM (base models preferred over instruction-tuned), and a total privacy budget (ε, δ) split between fixed-shot selection and vector construction. It consumes only a small subset of private data (e.g., 500 samples per class) for vector extraction, then produces an arbitrary number of synthetic text samples of arbitrary length at zero additional privacy cost. The intermediate artifact — the privatized dataset vectors — is a fixed-length direction per selected layer, normalized and released once.

## Method

EPSVEC extracts 'dataset vectors' by computing the mean difference of mean-pooled hidden states between private samples and LLM-generated synthetic references at selected transformer layers, capturing the distributional gap that prompting alone cannot close. These vectors are privatized via per-example ℓ₂-clipping and Gaussian noise (Gaussian mechanism), satisfying (ε,δ)-DP with a noise scale proportional to 2C/(nε). At generation time, the privatized vectors are injected additively into the LLM's hidden states at every decoding step (z_{l,t} ← z_{l,t} + β_l v_l), steering outputs toward the target distribution without any per-token privacy cost. A DP-histogram mechanism selects a small set of fixed-shot exemplars from a zero-shot candidate pool to stabilize base-model prompting and sharpen the reference distribution for vector construction. The key architectural insight is that privacy is consumed once during vector extraction and fixed-shot selection, after which all downstream decoding, filtering, and post-processing are free, enabling unlimited synthetic samples at standard inference cost.

## Results

EPSVEC++ (pretrained model variant) achieves the best MAUVE across all four datasets at both privacy levels: 72.3% on IMDb, 62.9% on Yelp, 62.2% on BioRxiv, and 33.0% on OpenReview at ε=5, with comparable scores at ε=3 (67.8%, 67.3%, 60.7%, 33.0%). On BERT fine-tuning accuracy, EPSVEC++ reaches 84.3% (IMDb), 83.8% (Yelp), 86.0% (BioRxiv), and 66.4% (OpenReview) at ε=5, approaching real-data performance (90.7%, 93.6%, 91.8%, 73.2%). The method achieves an average 150% MAUVE improvement over the next-best baseline (PP++) across the four datasets, and on Yelp at ε=3, EPSVEC++ (67.3%) even exceeds the non-private 2-shot prompting baseline (66.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPSVEC++ dominates all four baselines on MAUVE (distributional fidelity) across every dataset and privacy level. AUGPE and INVINK are effectively non-functional on MAUVE, scoring below 1% on all datasets, indicating their generated text is nearly indistinguishable from random noise in distributional terms. PP achieves moderate BERT accuracy on Yelp (91.0% at ε=5) but very poor MAUVE (8.9%), and PP++ improves MAUVE on Yelp (69.8%) and IMDb (14.9%) at ε=5 but completely fails to produce usable output at ε=3. EPSVEC++ outperforms PP++ by 57.4 MAUVE points on IMDb (72.3 vs. 14.9), 50.3 on BioRxiv (62.2 vs. 1.9), and 27.5 on OpenReview (33.0 vs. 5.5) at ε=5. On BERT fine-tuning accuracy, the gap is smaller: EPSVEC++ trails PP/PP++ on Yelp (83.8% vs. 91.0–91.3%) but matches or exceeds them on IMDb, BioRxiv, and OpenReview, suggesting the baselines' higher Yelp BERT scores partly reflect overly simplistic, label-obvious generations rather than true distributional fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

EPSVEC sits at the intersection of two research threads: activation steering (Subramani et al., Turner et al., and PSA by Goel et al.) and inference-time DP synthetic data generation (AUGPE, PP, PP++, InvisibleInk). It departs from PSA by learning dataset-level shift vectors (synthetic-to-real distributional gap) rather than demonstration-based behavioral edits, and from per-token DP aggregation methods by privatizing a single compact vector once and then generating freely at zero additional privacy cost. The fixed-shot component introduces a DP-histogram mechanism to select exemplars from a zero-shot candidate pool, a technique not present in prior private generation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is decoupling the privacy budget from the number and length of generated samples, making large-scale synthetic data generation feasible under strong DP guarantees where per-token methods become intractable. The finding that pretrained (base) models substantially outperform instruction-tuned variants for distributional fidelity is a useful and somewhat counterintuitive design principle for future synthetic data pipelines. Open questions include whether dataset vectors generalize to domains with no public prior, whether the non-monotonic MAUVE-vs-ε trend reflects a genuine diversity benefit of moderate noise, and whether distributional metrics like MAUVE adequately capture semantic faithfulness for complex or long-horizon corpora.
