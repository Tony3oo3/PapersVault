## Problem

Prior work shows that mean pooling over prompt tokens in autoregressive models performs poorly as an embedding strategy due to causal masking, and it is unclear whether this limitation extends to hidden states computed during generation, where context is increasingly shaped by the model's own outputs. The paper asks whether generated-token hidden states can yield semantic representations at inference time without additional training or architectural modification, and if so, how they should be collapsed into a single embedding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264673/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (an image caption, protein name, or problem statement) and feeds it to an autoregressive LM (Qwen3-14B in thinking mode) to generate T=128 continuation tokens. The output is a D-dimensional embedding obtained by mean-pooling the final-layer hidden states across the 128 generated-token positions, excluding prompt-token states. Evaluation is relational: the pooled embedding is compared via debiased CKA to a fixed reference kernel (DINOv2 image embeddings, ESM-3 protein-structure embeddings, or gold-solution embeddings), so the output is interpreted as a point in a space whose geometry should mirror the reference modality.

## Method

The core finding is that mean pooling across generated tokens yields higher kernel alignment to reference spaces than any individual token or last-token embedding, and that this improvement arises because semantic information is distributed across the generation rather than localized to one position. The authors demonstrate this through convex-combination experiments over token-slice ensembles: alignment is maximized at interior points of the simplex rather than at any vertex, and the effect persists across recursive subdivisions, across domains (vision-language, reasoning, protein), and across independent decoding seeds. They rule out a pure variance-reduction explanation by showing that isotropic noise perturbations around the mean do not reproduce the alignment gain when semantic correspondence is broken. Generated-token representations also outperform prompt-token representations (0.410 vs. 0.184 CKA), and the observed phase structure in alignment trajectories (preamble → prompt repetition → recall → content-specific response) is model-specific, disappearing when a different model embeds the text.

## Results

Mean pooling across 128 generated tokens achieves a debiased CKA alignment of 0.410 to DINOv2 vision embeddings on WIT, substantially outperforming the best individual token (0.291), attention pooling (0.293), and max pooling (0.351). Generated-token representations also outperform prompt-token representations by a wide margin (0.410 vs. 0.184 CKA), and this advantage extends to retrieval (R@1: 0.052 vs. 0.027), ranking (MRR: 0.342 vs. 0.263), and clustering (NMI: 0.311 vs. 0.213). The mixing effect generalizes across domains: convex combinations of token-slice embeddings consistently achieve higher alignment than any single segment on Math-500, GPQA-Diamond, and UniProt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares mean pooling against last-token, attention, max, and best-per-token pooling strategies. Mean pooling wins on both CKA and m-kNN across all settings, with the largest gap over best-per-token (+0.12 CKA) and attention pooling (+0.12 CKA). Against prompt-token representations, generated-token mean pooling dominates on every downstream metric: retrieval R@1 improves from 0.027 to 0.052, ranking MRR from 0.263 to 0.342, and clustering NMI from 0.213 to 0.311. Notably, prompt-token mean pooling does not improve over the best individual prompt token, confirming that the mixing benefit is specific to generated tokens. The convex-combination analysis further shows that no single token segment (first, middle, or final third) matches the interior of the simplex, ruling out the hypothesis that one region of the generation carries all semantic content.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established finding that mean pooling over autoregressive model hidden states performs poorly for prompt-side embeddings (Jiang et al., 2024; Springer et al., 2025; BehnamGhader et al., 2024), showing instead that the same operation succeeds when applied to *generated* tokens. It builds on Wang et al. (2025), who demonstrated that sensory prompts elicit vision-aligned generative representations, and on Liu et al. (2024), who showed semantic information is distributed across token trajectories, but extends both by quantifying the pooling effect through debiased CKA across three domains and by revealing that the benefit is specific to generation rather than a generic averaging artifact. The kernel-alignment evaluation framework follows Kornblith et al. (2019) and Sucholutsky et al. (2025), with the addition of a debiased U-centering variant to avoid the known inflation of biased CKA in high-dimensional regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a simple, training-free recipe (mean-pool over generated tokens) that outperforms prompt-based embeddings for retrieval, ranking, and clustering, suggesting that autoregressive generation itself is a useful mechanism for making semantic content accessible in hidden-state space. The model-specific phase dynamics—where representational structure disappears when a different model embeds the text—raise open questions about whether these representations are properties of the text or of the model's internal computation, and whether they can be made transferable. A key limitation is the linear computational cost of generating T tokens versus a single forward pass, and the absolute alignment values remain modest, leaving open whether the approach can be made more efficient or whether the observed dynamics generalize to longer generations or different architectures.
