## Problem

Existing streaming VideoLLMs process unbounded video streams using uniform KV cache construction, where each new cache entry is built conditioned on all past caches. The paper identifies a previously unexplored 'cumulative effect': residuals from evicted caches contaminate newly constructed entries, degrading the informativeness of recent inputs. Additionally, position IDs grow unboundedly over time, exceeding training lengths and causing out-of-distribution behavior that prior works addressed only with empirical heuristics lacking formal guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous video stream at 1 FPS (StreamingBench, OVO-Bench) or 0.5 FPS (RVS-Ego, RVS-Movie), with each frame encoded by the backbone's vision tower into 196 tokens (LLaVA-OV-7B) or 286 tokens (Qwen2.5-VL-7B). A fixed context window of 32 frames is maintained, split into a 4-frame feature buffer for recent inputs and a 28-frame cumulative KV cache. Text queries arrive asynchronously; the model produces a text answer (multiple-choice selection or open-ended response) using the combined cache, with constant memory (16–17 GB) regardless of stream length.

## Method

DSCache is a training-free KV cache construction mechanism that decouples cache building into two independent pathways: a cumulative past KV cache updated via FIFO eviction of a feature buffer (preserving long-range context), and an instant KV cache constructed on-demand from the buffer alone when a query arrives (preserving fine-grained recency). The key insight is that constructing the instant cache independently of the cumulative cache prevents residual contamination from evicted entries, maintaining the informativeness of recent inputs. To handle unbounded position growth, DSCache employs position-agnostic encoding—storing KV caches before RoPE transformation and reassigning contiguous position IDs at use time—with a formal proof that this produces outputs identical to standard positional encoding for RoPE-based LLMs, enabling safe cache partitioning, recombination, and eviction without position overflow.

## Results

On StreamingBench (Real-Time Visual Understanding), DSCache achieves 79.12% overall accuracy with LLaVA-OV-7B and 82.32% with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, outperforming the uniform streaming KV cache baseline (76.92% and 78.56%) by an average of 2.5%. On OVO-Bench, DSCache reaches 57.5% and 55.4% overall accuracy respectively, surpassing the best prior online VideoLLM (TimeChat-Online at 46.7%) by over 10%. The method also achieves 59.5% accuracy on RVS-Ego and 49.4% on RVS-Movie with LLaVA-OV-7B, while maintaining constant 16 GB GPU memory and 0.30 s per-query latency on an H200 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSCache with Qwen2.5-VL-7B (82.32%) outperforms both proprietary MLLMs—Gemini 1.5 Pro (75.69%) and GPT-4o (73.28%)—on StreamingBench by 6.6% and 9.0% respectively. Among open-source online VideoLLMs, DSCache surpasses the strongest baseline StreamBridge (77.04%) by 5.3% and TimeChat-Online (75.36%) by 6.8% on StreamingBench, and exceeds TimeChat-Online (46.7%) by 10.8% on OVO-Bench. Against the uniform streaming KV cache baseline (the direct ablation), DSCache yields a consistent +2.2% (LLaVA-OV) and +3.8% (Qwen2.5-VL) improvement on StreamingBench, with the largest gains on recency-dependent subtasks: attribute perception (+4.5%), text-rich understanding (+6.0%), spatial understanding (+6.5%), and action perception (+6.7%). Compared to cache optimization methods (ReKV at 71.06%, StreamingVLM at 76.92%, InfiniPot-V at 76.40% on StreamingBench with LLaVA-OV-7B), DSCache achieves 79.12% while using less memory (16 GB vs. 21 GB for ReKV) and comparable latency (0.30 s vs. 0.21–0.37 s). On OVO-Bench, DSCache (57.5%) also beats ReKV (53.9%) and StreamingVLM (55.6%), confirming that improving cache construction quality yields more consistent gains than post-hoc cache compression or retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the streaming inference line initiated by StreamingLLM (attention sinks) and LM-Infinite (position-agnostic RoPE encoding), extending both to the video domain. It departs from prior streaming VideoLLMs (StreamingVLM, ReKV, InfiniPot-V) that focus on cache *utilization*—compression, pruning, retrieval—by identifying that the *construction* quality of uniform KV caches is itself a bottleneck: a cumulative effect where residuals from evicted caches degrade the encoding of new inputs. The position-agnostic encoding strategy, previously used as an empirical heuristic, is given a formal equivalence proof for RoPE-based LLMs, and the decoupled cache design (DSCache) is orthogonal to and combinable with existing cache optimization methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free adaptation of strong offline MLLMs (LLaVA-OV, Qwen2.5-VL) to unbounded streaming with bounded memory is practically important for deployment in robotics, surveillance, and autonomous driving where retraining on unbounded data is infeasible. The cumulative-effect diagnosis shifts attention from cache utilization to cache construction quality, a previously underexplored axis. Limitations include modest trade-offs on long-range reasoning subtasks (causal reasoning, counting) and additional prefilling latency from instant cache recomputation, though the authors provide an approximate variant that nearly eliminates this overhead. Open questions include whether better subsampling or retrieval over the cumulative cache can close the long-range gap, and whether the decoupling principle generalizes beyond RoPE-based architectures.
