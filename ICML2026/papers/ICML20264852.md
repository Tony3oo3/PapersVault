## Problem

Existing token-pruning methods for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models rely solely on local signals (early-layer attention or vision-encoder saliency) and ignore the global attention context from deeper layers, causing >20% success-rate drops or limited speedup. The paper identifies that consecutive VLA inference steps exhibit strong spatial-temporal consistency in token importance (75–88% recall overlap), yet no prior method exploits this temporal redundancy to guide pruning with reliable global information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264852/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a training-free, plug-and-play accelerator on existing VLA models (OpenVLA-OFT, DB-OFT, CogACT). It consumes multi-view RGB images (16×16 patches → 256 tokens per image, typically 1–4 views), a text task instruction, and robot proprioceptive state, and produces a continuous low-level action sequence (translation + rotation deltas). The key I/O detail is that visual tokens are progressively reduced from ~600 to ~240 at the action level (60% static pruning) and further thinned by ~20% through layer-level dynamic pruning, while the text tokens and action head remain untouched.

## Method

SpecPrune-VLA is a two-level, action-aware self-speculative pruning framework. At the action level, it reuses the top-K globally important visual tokens identified by mid/deep-layer image-to-text attention from the *previous* inference step (exploiting spatial-temporal consistency), supplements them with dynamic tokens selected via velocity-adaptive frame-similarity comparison, and local tokens from the first two layers' attention, fusing all three sources to prune 60–70% of visual tokens before the LLM forward pass. At the layer level, it dynamically updates each token's importance score using a rank-based sigmoid weight multiplied by an entropy-based layer-confidence score, propagated via exponential moving average, and prunes the lowest-scoring 10% tokens at designated layers. A lightweight action-aware controller classifies each step as coarse- or fine-grained based on end-effector translational/rotational velocity thresholds and Δz, adaptively scaling the retained token counts (e.g., K_local=40 vs. 24, K_dynamic=10 vs. 20) to preserve precision during contact-sensitive phases while pruning aggressively during fast translations.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark (OpenVLA-OFT), SpecPrune-VLA achieves 96.1% average success rate with 1.46× speedup and 43% FLOPs, compared to the 96.6% baseline at 100% FLOPs — a success rate drop of only 0.5%. On SimplerEnv (DB-OFT), it reaches 70.1% average success rate with 1.44× speedup and 42% FLOPs, nearly matching the 70.4% baseline. On real-world tasks (Flexiv Rizon4), the method achieves 1.70× speedup (187.7 ms → 110.2 ms) with negligible success rate degradation across four manipulation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpecPrune-VLA outperforms all five baselines on success rate while achieving comparable or superior speedup. Against FastV (1.44×, 93.3% SR) and DivPrune (1.46×, 89.4% SR) on LIBERO, the method matches or exceeds their speedup while recovering 2.8–6.7 percentage points in success rate, attributed to its use of global temporal attention rather than single-layer heuristics. Compared to SparseVLM (1.28×, 95.6% SR), SpecPrune-VLA delivers both higher speedup (1.46×) and higher accuracy (96.1%), since SparseVLM's recycling and merging strategy only minimally reduces computation. VLA-Cache achieves the highest success rate (96.9%) but at only 1.07× speedup due to limited FLOPs reduction (17%) and GPU memory access overhead, whereas SpecPrune-VLA sacrifices just 0.8% SR for a 1.46× speedup. EfficientVLA attains the highest speedup (1.52×) but suffers a severe 7.7% SR drop (88.9%), particularly on LIBERO-Long (72.1%), by aggressively pruning tokens and skipping layers that compromise action-critical information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token-pruning line for VLMs (FastV, DivPrune, SparseVLM) and VLA-specific acceleration (VLA-Cache, EfficientVLA, SP-VLA) by introducing a 'self-speculative' mechanism: reusing global attention scores from the previous inference step as a prior for the current step, inspired by the spatial-temporal consistency of robotic scenes. It also borrows the self-speculative decoding concept from LayerSkip and SpecEE, but repurposes it for token selection rather than draft-verify generation. The key departure from prior VLA pruning work is the explicit fusion of global (cross-step, mid/deep-layer) and local (first-two-layer) importance signals, combined with a velocity-based action-aware controller that has no direct analogue in the VLM pruning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With LLM backbones accounting for >70% of VLA inference latency and real-time robotic control demanding sub-100ms response, a training-free, architecture-agnostic pruning method achieving 1.5–1.7× speedup with <0.7% success-rate loss is practically significant for deploying VLA models on edge or multi-robot systems. The spatial-temporal consistency insight (75–88% token recall across consecutive steps) opens a broader question: how much of the VLA inference pipeline can be amortized across time without re-computing the full transformer? Limitations include reliance on the assumption that scenes change slowly (potentially breaking in fast or highly dynamic environments), the heuristic nature of the velocity-based controller, and validation primarily on manipulation tasks with fixed camera setups.
