## Problem

Existing emotion-controllable TTS methods rely on external conditioning (discrete labels, reference audio) or dense global activation steering, offering limited interpretability and no modular, feature-level control over emotional expression. This paper asks whether emotion-related variation in the semantic hidden states of LLM-based TTS models can be decomposed into sparse, interpretable latent features that support bidirectional (induction and suppression) emotion control without modifying backbone parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260972/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the system takes a text prompt, a speaker timbre reference audio (to fix identity), and a steering specification consisting of a small set of pre-identified SAE latent features (top-6 by sentence-level selectivity) and a continuous scale α_e (positive for induction, negative for suppression). The SAE operates on 1280-dimensional residual-stream activations from layer 16 of the IndexTTS2 semantic backbone during decode-phase semantic-token generation, mapping them to a 4096-dimensional sparse latent space with Top-k=32 active features per token. The output is a full speech waveform produced by the downstream CFM module and vocoder, with emotional prosody modulated while preserving linguistic content and speaker identity.

## Method

The paper trains a Top-k sparse autoencoder (4096 latents, k=32) in an unsupervised manner on 56,000 decode-phase residual activations from the IndexTTS2 semantic backbone, using a reconstruction loss plus an auxiliary residual-projection loss to mitigate dead latents, with unit-norm decoder columns and EMA stabilization. Emotion-related latent features are identified via a sentence-level selectivity score: for each latent feature, the fraction of utterances in which it fires under a target emotion minus the fraction under matched neutral conditions (paired text and speaker), yielding a sparse set of top-ranked features per emotion. At inference, the residual activation is encoded into the sparse latent space, a scalar α_e is added to (or subtracted from) the activations of the selected emotion-related features, and the result is decoded back into the residual stream—equivalent to steering along a sparse linear combination of learned decoder directions rather than a single dense mean-difference vector. This formulation enables bidirectional control (positive α_e induces emotion, negative α_e suppresses it) with a single continuous knob, and the paper shows that individual latent features map to distinct acoustic attributes (pitch, spectral brightness, energy), confirming that emotional expression arises from coordinated sparse latent contributions.

## Results

SAE-Emotion achieves the highest or near-highest Emo-SIM across all three target emotions in both induction and suppression settings: for induction (Neutral→Target), it reaches 0.912 (anger), 0.885 (happiness), and 0.880 (sadness); for suppression (Target→Neutral), it reaches 0.939, 0.924, and 0.941 respectively. WER remains very low (0.3–2.8), indicating strong content preservation. In human evaluation with 20 raters, SAE-Emotion scores the highest EMOS (3.22) and NMOS (3.49) among all steering methods, confirming perceptual quality gains over both Global Steering (EMOS 3.10) and Random SAE (EMOS 1.82).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four external TTS baselines, SAE-Emotion achieves the highest Emo-SIM for anger (0.912 vs. VALL-E-X 0.831, Spark-TTS 0.857, EmoVoice 0.806, CosyVoice 0.813) and happiness (0.885 vs. 0.697, 0.770, 0.728, 0.712), while Spark-TTS narrowly leads on sadness induction (0.907 vs. 0.880). SAE-Emotion also substantially outperforms all four on WER (0.3–2.2 vs. 2.3–8.6), indicating better content preservation. Compared with the two steering baselines, SAE-Emotion edges out Global Steering on Emo-SIM for all three emotions in induction (0.912/0.885/0.880 vs. 0.910/0.879/0.876) and clearly surpasses it in suppression (0.939/0.924/0.941 vs. 0.915/0.920/0.933). Against Random SAE, SAE-Emotion consistently achieves higher Emo-SIM (e.g., 0.912 vs. 0.892 for anger induction), confirming that selectivity-based feature selection is critical rather than arbitrary sparse perturbation. In the suppression setting, SAE-Emotion also achieves the best Spk-SIM for sadness (0.441) and the lowest WER (0.80), suggesting that sparse feature-level intervention better preserves speaker identity and linguistic content than dense global steering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse autoencoder interpretability framework (Bricken et al., 2023; Cunningham et al., 2023; Templeton et al., 2024) from LLMs to the semantic backbone of autoregressive LLM-based TTS models, using a Top-k SAE (Makhzani & Frey, 2013; Gao et al., 2024) trained on residual-stream activations during semantic-token generation. It departs from prior SAE work in audio (Paek et al., 2025), which analyzed acoustic autoencoder latents, by targeting high-level semantic representations before acoustic synthesis. It also refines the activation-steering paradigm for TTS (Xie et al., 2025), which used a single dense mean-difference direction in a diffusion transformer, by replacing it with sparse, feature-level interventions in the autoregressive semantic backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a training-free, interpretable mechanism for bidirectional emotion control (induction and suppression) in LLM-based TTS without modifying backbone parameters, which is practically valuable for applications requiring fine-grained, adjustable emotional expression. The finding that only ~6 sparse latent features suffice for emotion steering, and that these features map to distinct acoustic properties (pitch, spectral brightness), offers a mechanistic understanding of how emotion is organized in semantic representations. Key limitations include evaluation on a single primary backbone (IndexTTS2), a limited set of three target emotions, and the computational cost of SAE training on large activation datasets; a full characterization of the latent space and cross-architecture generality remain open.
