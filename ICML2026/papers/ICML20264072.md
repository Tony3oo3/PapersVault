## Problem

In the frontier regime where a student model already possesses strong reasoning ability, naive continual distillation from a strong teacher often yields limited gains or even severe degradation, despite monotonically decreasing training loss. The paper identifies and explains this failure mode—termed Imitation Shock—by uncovering a token-level mechanism where early-learned 'anchor' tokens actively suppress the learning of reasoning-critical tokens, and proposes a targeted fix.

## Input / Output

The method consumes teacher-generated reasoning traces (sampled at temperature 0.6, max 32K tokens, one correct trace selected per prompt) and a pre-trained student base model, operating on small prompt sets (200–100K samples). A critical operational requirement is logging every checkpoint during a short distillation run (50 steps, batch 64) to locate the Imitation Bottleneck via training accuracy, then computing per-token log-probability differences between the base and bottleneck checkpoints using an AR selector model. The output is a token-level binary mask (anchor vs. yet-to-learn) applied during a second training pass, yielding a distilled student model; for dLLMs the mask is injected via union-masking into the random-mask reconstruction objective.

## Method

T3S reconstructs the distillation objective at the token level by exploiting the training trajectory around the Imitation Bottleneck (the checkpoint with minimum training accuracy). It computes Δc_t = c_t(θ_b) − c_t(θ_0) for every token position, defining Imitation-Anchor Tokens as those with Δc_t > 0 (confidence increased during the shock) and yet-to-learn tokens as the remainder. For AR distillation, anchor tokens are hard-masked from the cross-entropy loss so that all gradient capacity is directed at the suppressed reasoning tokens; for dLLMs, the complementary yet-to-learn set (Δc_t < −τ) is force-included into the random mask via element-wise OR, ensuring the model repeatedly practices generating those tokens under diverse visible-token conditions. The paper provides causal evidence (one-step anchor-only gradient interventions, gradient-norm ratios up to 17×, cosine similarities of −0.4 to −0.5, and a 4×4 loss-transfer matrix showing antagonistic cross-group effects) that anchor learning actively inhibits other-token learning, and demonstrates that static confidence or gradient-geometry heuristics cannot recover the same partition, making the trajectory signal essential.

## Results

On BOBA-200 with DeepSeek-R1 distillation, T3S achieves AIME24=80.63 and AIME25=73.96 (AVG 77.30), surpassing the base model (AVG 71.46) by 5.84 points and outperforming standard SFT (AVG 63.13) by 14.17 points. On S1K-200, T3S(R1) reaches AIME24=80.00 and AIME25=73.13 (AVG 76.57), again exceeding the base model. The inverted-mask ablation (-T3S) collapses to AVG 28.13 on BOBA-200, confirming that T3S identifies a highly selective and essential token partition rather than discarding redundant positions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GKD (Agarwal et al., 2023), T3S wins by 7.5 points on AIME24 (80.83 vs. 73.33) and 16.7 points on AIME25 (70.00 vs. 53.33) under a tokenizer-sharing setting (Qwen3-32B teacher → Qwen3-8B student). Against SWITCH (Koo et al., 2024), T3S leads by 5.0 on AIME24 (80.83 vs. 75.83) and 3.3 on AIME25 (70.00 vs. 66.67). Against Mix Distillation (Li et al., 2025), T3S achieves 75.84 vs. 68.75 (R1/QWQ pairing) and 77.40 vs. 71.67 (Qwen3-235B/QWQ pairing) on AIME24/25 average. Internally, T3S consistently outperforms both standard SFT (by 14+ points under R1 distillation) and the parameter-level RRT method (by ~4–5 points), demonstrating that token-level objective reconstruction is more effective than simply discarding pre-bottleneck parameter updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of efficient reasoning distillation (LIMA, S1, LIMo) and token-priority training (RHO-1, critical-token RL, entropy-guided dropout), but departs from both in a key way: prior data-selection work operates at the example level, and prior token-priority methods rely on static signals (initial entropy, base-model confidence, local gradient geometry). T3S instead uses a *training-trajectory* signal—confidence change measured between the base model and the Imitation Bottleneck checkpoint—to identify a dynamically defined token partition (Imitation-Anchor Tokens vs. yet-to-learn tokens) that static criteria provably cannot recover. The Recovering-Residual Transfer (RRT) construction, which discards all pre-bottleneck parameter updates, is a novel diagnostic tool with no direct analogue in prior distillation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic explanation for a long-observed but unexplained failure mode in continual reasoning distillation: the tokens that drive loss reduction (anchors) are precisely the ones that suppress reasoning-relevant tokens, and the pre-bottleneck updates they encode are net-harmful. This reframes the distillation problem from 'how much data / which examples' to 'which tokens should the optimizer see at each stage,' a shift that is actionable with minimal overhead (a single trajectory pass + token masking). Open questions include whether the bottleneck identification generalizes to multi-teacher or multi-domain continual settings without re-running the trajectory, whether the anchor/yet-to-learn partition stabilizes as the student approaches teacher-level performance, and whether the dLLM extension (union masking with an AR proxy selector) can be replaced by a native diffusion likelihood signal as dLLM architectures mature.
