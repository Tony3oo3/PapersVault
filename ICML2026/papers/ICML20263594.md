## Problem

Off-policy unsupervised RL suffers from two overlooked bottlenecks that limit scalability: (1) semantic drift, where skill labels z stored in the replay buffer become stale as the representation φ evolves, injecting high-variance noise that worsens with higher sample reuse; and (2) brittle generalization, where skill policies conditioned on full state observations overfit to global contextual information (e.g., absolute positions, visual backgrounds), causing the same skill to produce inconsistent behaviors under distribution shifts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a state observation (numeric vectors of 27–226 dimensions or 64×64×3 RGB images with 3-frame stacking) and a continuous skill vector z (2D for Humanoid/Quadruped/Dog, 3D for Fish, 10D for Kitchen) as input. The CIB encoder transforms the raw state into a complementary embedding ℓ (256-dim for numeric, 2048-dim for pixel), which replaces the raw state as the policy's observation channel. The skill-conditioned policy π(a|ℓ, z) outputs continuous actions, while the metric-aware representation φ(s) and CIB embedding ℓ are jointly learned; downstream, a frozen skill policy is paired with a high-level controller that outputs z every 25 steps.

## Method

GenDa introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, skill relabeling replaces fixed rollout-time labels z_roll with dynamically computed z_relab = unit(φ_tgt(s_T) − φ_tgt(s_0)) using an EMA target network, so that replay-buffer trajectories are re-interpreted under the current representation; a contrastive-style uniformity regularizer (minimizing log-sum-exp of pairwise dot products among batch episodic directions) prevents representation collapse by maximizing H(z_relab). For policy learning, additional c-step relabeling targets z_c = unit(φ(s_{t+c}) − φ(s_t)) are mixed with z_roll and z_relab to enrich the off-policy reward signal. Second, the Complementary Information Bottleneck (CIB) trains an encoder q_ψ(ℓ|s) and decoder p_ω(s|ℓ, φ(s)) via a VIB objective J_CIB = I((ℓ, φ(s)); s) − η·I(ℓ; s), forcing ℓ to capture information complementary to the metric-aware latent φ(s); the skill policy then conditions on ℓ rather than the raw state, structurally disentangling ego-centric behavioral features from global context and improving robustness to distribution shifts.

## Results

On downstream tasks (Table 1), GenDa achieves the highest average success rate across all environments, with particularly dramatic gains in high-dimensional settings: on Dog-Numeric (226-dim state), GenDa reaches 0.35–0.56 success rate while both METRA and CSF score 0.00–0.01. On Humanoid-Numeric (RS, RG), GenDa achieves 0.68±0.13 versus 0.04±0.03 for METRA and 0.08±0.06 for CSF. In the most challenging Maze-Hard task, GenDa reaches 0.41±0.20 while both baselines score 0.00. GenDa also achieves comparable downstream performance to the asymptotic limit of baselines with 5× fewer environment interactions (2M vs 10M steps, Table 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenDa is compared against four baselines: METRA (Park et al., 2024), CSF (Zheng et al., 2025), DIAYN (Eysenbach et al., 2019), and CIC (Laskin et al., 2022). Against METRA, GenDa wins decisively on generalization-sensitive tasks: on Humanoid (RS, RG) it scores 0.68 vs. METRA's 0.04, and on Maze-Hard it reaches 0.41 vs. 0.00. Against CSF, GenDa dominates on Dog (0.55 vs. 0.01 on FS,RG) and Fish (0.79 vs. 0.00), environments where CSF completely fails to learn meaningful skills. The one task where METRA edges out GenDa is Quadruped (FS, RG) at 0.81 vs. 0.78, a setting with minimal distribution shift where global-context entanglement is less harmful. DIAYN and CIC (evaluated in Figure 3 for state coverage) are consistently outperformed across all environments, with GenDa achieving higher coverage using substantially fewer interactions. The data-efficiency advantage is further confirmed by Table 2, where GenDa at 2M steps matches or exceeds the 10M-step asymptotic performance of baselines on Humanoid downstream tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on METRA (Park et al., 2024), adopting its metric-aware Lipschitz-constrained representation objective as the foundation, and extends it with two targeted fixes for off-policy URL: a skill relabeling mechanism (conceptually related to Hindsight Experience Replay, Andrychowicz et al., 2017) and a Complementary Information Bottleneck module (inspired by the variational information bottleneck of Alemi et al., 2017). The key conceptual departure is identifying two previously overlooked failure modes—non-stationary skill semantics causing semantic drift in replay buffers, and overfitting to global context in the policy observation—that jointly limit scalability. Unlike prior disentanglement approaches that impose hand-crafted factorized structures on the latent space, CIB achieves disentanglement purely through a complementary information objective relative to the metric-aware embedding φ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, drop-in recipe for making off-policy URL both more data-efficient and more generalizable, which is critical for real-world robotic deployment where interaction is costly and environments shift. The finding that high UTD ratios (which normally improve off-policy RL) actively destabilize URL due to label staleness is a useful diagnostic for the community. Open challenges remain: the one-skill-per-episode assumption limits relabeling to intra-episode skill changes, CIB's effectiveness depends on the metric-aware φ (limiting transfer to non-metric-aware algorithms), and the metric-aware objective's bias toward large-scale movements hinders fine-grained manipulation tasks.
