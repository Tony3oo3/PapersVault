## Problem

Diffusion-based Q-learning for offline RL relies on multi-step denoising, making both training (via backpropagation through time) and inference computationally expensive and prone to optimization instability. Prior acceleration methods—policy distillation, auxiliary networks, multi-phase training—introduce additional algorithmic complexity and unfavorable trade-offs between scalability and policy quality. The paper asks whether a single-step policy can match the expressiveness of multi-step diffusion policies without any auxiliary models, distillation, or Jacobian computations.

## Input / Output

The policy network takes a state vector, a Gaussian noise sample, and sinusoidal positional embeddings of two timesteps (t and r, 64-dim each) as concatenated input, and outputs a velocity-like quantity that is converted to a clean action via a lightweight preconditioning mechanism. At inference, action generation is a single forward pass with r=0 and t=1, eliminating all iterative denoising. The Q-networks take standard (state, action) pairs and output scalar values for the clipped double Q-learning critic, with the value-guidance coefficient α normalized by the dataset-specific Q-value scale.

## Method

BFQ directly parameterizes the displacement vector between noise and clean actions rather than the marginal velocity field, exploiting the fact that displacements admit a recursive decomposition into shorter displacements of the same form. Training enforces two objectives with a single shared network: a compositional consistency loss (π(a_t, r, t) = π(π(a_t, m, t), r, m)) that bootstraps short-range displacements into the full noise-to-action mapping, and a boundary conditioning loss that anchors the policy to the conditional Flow Matching velocity for infinitesimal intervals sampled from [0, 10⁻³]. A velocity-like preconditioning avoids vanishing gradients at small Δ while automatically satisfying the identity condition, and the two losses are combined via a Bernoulli-sampled stochastic objective with ratio λ=0.5. The resulting behavior cloning objective replaces the log-likelihood regularizer in a standard behavior-regularized actor-critic framework, enabling BPTT-free policy optimization with single-step action sampling at both training and inference time.

## Results

On D4RL MuJoCo locomotion tasks, BFQ achieves a 92.8 average normalized score, outperforming DQL (89.0), OFQL (92.5), and FQL (79.2) while using only 1 generation step. On AntMaze navigation, BFQ scores 83.9, competitive with OFQL (84.6) and ahead of DQL (81.3) and FQL (79.0). In terms of efficiency, BFQ trains in 7.8 hours (vs. 11.7–49.5 h for DQL at 5–50 steps) and achieves 851.2 Hz decision frequency (vs. 35.5–238.1 Hz for DQL). On OGBench, BFQ attains the highest average score of 68, surpassing SORL-8 (63) and FQL (47).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BFQ outperforms all multi-step diffusion baselines: DQL (89.0→92.8 MuJoCo avg), EDP (82.4), and IDQL (84.1), while also eliminating BPTT and reducing training time from 11.7–49.5 h to 7.8 h. Against the closest one-step competitor OFQL (92.5 MuJoCo, 84.6 AntMaze), BFQ wins on MuJoCo (92.8) but trails slightly on AntMaze (83.9 vs. 84.6), making them effectively tied overall. BFQ substantially outperforms FQL (79.2 MuJoCo, 79.0 AntMaze) and SORL (80.0 MuJoCo, 65.0 AntMaze) across both domains, with the largest gaps on medium and medium-replay datasets where multimodal action distributions are most challenging. Compared to Gaussian-policy baselines TD3-BC (74.9) and IQL (73.2), BFQ's expressive flow-based policy yields 18–19 point improvements on MuJoCo. On OGBench, BFQ (68 avg) surpasses SORL-8 (63) and FQL (47), and on D4RL Adroit it achieves 79 avg, beating IFQL (76) and FBRAC (72). The CAC consistency-model baseline (89.8 MuJoCo) is also outperformed by BFQ, confirming that the bootstrapping strategy with explicit boundary conditioning provides a more stable optimization signal than consistency-model objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

BFQ builds on Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022) as its generative backbone and the behavior-regularized actor-critic framework of TD3+BC (Fujimoto & Gu, 2021) as its offline RL scaffold. It departs from Diffusion Q-Learning (Wang et al., 2022) and its multi-step denoising paradigm by directly parameterizing the noise-to-action displacement rather than the marginal velocity field, and from prior single-step methods (SRPO, SORL, FQL, OFQL) by eliminating distillation pipelines, auxiliary teacher models, and Jacobian computations in favor of a single shared network trained with compositional consistency and boundary conditioning losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BFQ demonstrates that single-step action generation can match or exceed multi-step diffusion policies in offline RL while delivering 6× training speedup and up to 24× inference speedup, making expressive generative policies practical for real-time deployment in robotics and autonomous systems. The ablation showing that removing boundary conditioning (λ=0) completely collapses learning underscores the non-trivial role of anchoring to local flow dynamics. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to low-dimensional state-based D4RL tasks; extending BFQ to vision-based observations, online fine-tuning, and goal-conditioned multi-task settings remains open and is the natural next step for the field.
