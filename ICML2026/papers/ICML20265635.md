## Problem

Standard MoE upcycling for continual learning expands experts indiscriminately across all layers, inflating parameters by ~4.9× while leaving many newly added experts under-utilized. The paper asks: which layers and experts actually need new capacity, and can this be learned rather than prescribed by a fixed schedule?

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained dense LLM (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct), first upcycled to an 8-expert MoE via weight slicing, and processes a sequence of 6 heterogeneous TRACE tasks (summarization, code completion, numerical reasoning, text simplification, stance detection) with a 200-batch replay buffer per task. It produces a selectively upcycled MoE model (2.46B vs 6.07B parameters for full upcycling) along with per-layer, per-expert binary expand/recycle masks finalized by thresholding sigmoid logits at 0.5, and standard task-specific predictions (ROUGE-L, code similarity, accuracy, SARI).

## Method

The core contribution is a bilevel optimization framework that treats expert expansion as a learnable architectural decision. The inner loop optimizes expert weights with a composite objective of task loss, CKA-based regularization (penalizing representational drift in the last 6 layers relative to pre-expansion activations), and replay loss. The outer loop learns Gumbel-Softmax masks over a supernet that holds both frozen old experts and trainable new clones, with a balanced objective combining replay performance, a knowledge-gain term rewarding expansion when new experts outperform old ones, and an asymmetric penalty enforcing a target expansion rate (~35%). CKA and Hessian flatness diagnostics motivate the design by revealing that deep layers drift substantially (low CKA, sharp loss landscapes) while shallow/middle layers remain stable, so the learned masks concentrate expansion in deep layers (e.g., 87.5% at Layer 15) and recycle in middle layers (e.g., 37.5% at Layer 12). A convergence guarantee is provided for the outer-loop mask updates under standard smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions.

## Results

On the TRACE benchmark with Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, the proposed bilevel upcycling method achieves the highest Last Accuracy (45.05%) and best Backward Transfer (−4.05) among all continual learning baselines, outperforming the strongest CL baseline (Replay, 43.76% Last Acc, −4.93 BWT) by 1.29 points in accuracy and 0.88 in forgetting. The method also attains the best Stage Accuracy (46.20%), matching Replay (46.33%) while using far fewer parameters. Critically, the deployed model contains only 2.46B parameters (1.98× the 1.24B base), a 60% reduction compared to Standard Upcycling's 6.07B (4.90×), while simultaneously delivering superior accuracy and an 80% reduction in forgetting relative to sequential fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method wins on all three metrics (Last Acc, Stage Acc, BWT) against every CL baseline in Table 1. Against the closest competitor, Replay (43.76% Last Acc, −4.93 BWT), it gains +1.29 accuracy and −0.88 BWT while deploying 60% fewer parameters than any upcycling variant. Among upcycling baselines, Standard Upcycle (Komatsuzaki et al.) achieves 41.49% Last Acc with 6.07B parameters, Drop-Upcycle (Nakamura et al.) reaches 41.43% with the same 6.07B, and BTM (Sukhbaatar et al.) attains 42.50% also at 6.07B; the proposed method surpasses all three by 2.5–3.6 points in Last Acc while using only 2.46B parameters. Regularization-based baselines (EWC at 35.97%, LoRA at 26.34%) and the momentum-filtered optimizer MoFO (41.81%) are all clearly outperformed. O-LoRA (Wang et al.) and OGD (Farajtabar et al.) are shown in the Figure 5 performance heatmaps but not tabulated in Table 1; qualitatively, both exhibit substantially more forgetting (visible as darker lower-triangular cells) than the proposed method. On Llama-3.2-3B (Appendix Table 2), the same pattern holds: the proposed method achieves 50.96% Last Acc and −2.02 BWT, beating Replay (50.66%, −4.30) and BTM (48.59%, −5.66).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MoE upcycling paradigm established by Sparse Upcycling (Komatsuzaki et al., 2022) and its continual-learning descendants (Lifelong-MoE, Drop-Upcycling, BTM), which all expand experts uniformly across every layer upon each new task. The key departure is formulating expert expansion as a learnable, per-expert binary decision via Gumbel-Softmax masks within a bilevel optimization framework (Franceschi et al., 2018), guided by a diagnostic layer that combines CKA representational similarity (Kornblith et al., 2019) and Hessian-based flatness to identify where capacity is genuinely needed. Unlike prior upcycling methods that rely on fixed schedules or heuristic rules, this work treats architecture growth as an end-to-end differentiable search problem, bridging NAS and continual learning in a way that prior architecture-based CL methods (O-LoRA, Progressive Networks) did not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60% parameter reduction while matching or exceeding standard upcycling performance addresses a practical bottleneck: indiscriminate MoE growth makes deployed models prohibitively large for real-world continual adaptation. The diagnostic finding that deep layers drift substantially (low CKA, sharp loss landscapes) while shallow/middle layers remain stable provides a reusable principle for any architecture-growth strategy beyond MoE. Open questions include whether the bilevel NAS formulation scales to 7B+ models and longer task sequences, whether expert merging can eliminate the residual memory overhead, and whether the approach transfers to online or multimodal continual learning settings where task boundaries are less clean.
