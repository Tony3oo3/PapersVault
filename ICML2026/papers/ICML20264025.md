## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of LLMs reliably improves target-task accuracy but causes severe catastrophic forgetting on held-out capabilities, while on-policy RL preserves retention at the cost of reward models, verifiers, and expensive online sampling. The paper asks whether the retention benefits of on-policy optimization can be recovered in a reward-free manner that drops into standard SFT pipelines.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (base policy π_θ0) and a supervised dataset of prompt-reference pairs, then requires an initial baseline SFT run to harvest T intermediate checkpoints (T=8–10 in practice). From these checkpoints, one sampled completion per prompt per checkpoint is stored in a trajectory buffer. The output is a student policy π_φ initialized from θ_0 and trained via token-level NLL on a mixture of oracle labels (weight α=0.25) and trajectory-mixed targets, requiring roughly 2× the FLOPs of standard SFT but less than RL baselines.

## Method

TMS identifies two independent failure modes of SFT—temporal supervision mismatch (the policy evolves while labels stay fixed) and mode collapse under single-reference cross-entropy—and addresses both by replacing the static reference distribution q with an empirical trajectory mixture m(·|x) = (1/T)Σ δ_{ŷ^(t)(x)} built from the model's own intermediate checkpoints. Stage 1 runs a standard SFT pass and records T uniformly spaced checkpoints, generating one sampled output per prompt per checkpoint into a buffer H. Stage 2 trains a fresh student from θ_0 by minimizing forward KL to the mixture q_α = α·q + (1−α)·m, operationally sampling a checkpoint index t ~ Uniform{1,…,T} (or the oracle with probability α) and applying standard token-level NLL. The key distinction from on-policy RL is that TMS uses a fixed offline buffer of historically near-policy targets rather than online rollouts, rewards, or advantage estimation, yet empirically achieves near-GRPO retention with SFT-level target accuracy. A theoretical drift bound (Theorem 6.2) shows KL-to-base upper-bounds performance change on held-out tasks, and Policy-Label Divergence (PLD) provides a complementary diagnostic of supervision mismatch within SFT-style training.

## Results

Across Qwen-2.5-1.5B/3B and LLaMA-3.1-8B, TMS matches or exceeds standard SFT on target tasks (e.g., MATH: 81.1 vs. 80.3 on Qwen-2.5-3B; Math500: 77.8 vs. 76.4) while dramatically reducing catastrophic forgetting: ARC-C retention drops from 42.7 (SFT) to 79.2 (TMS) on Qwen-2.5-3B, and from 53.2 (SFT) to 83.1 (TMS) on LLaMA-3.1-8B. Cross-task interference (Avg Δ on other target tasks) drops from 39.2 (SFT) to 2.3 (TMS) on Qwen-2.5-3B, approaching GRPO's 1.9. TMS achieves this without reward models or verifiers, at 2.2× SFT compute versus 3.8× for GRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Standard SFT (Ouyang et al., 2022), TMS wins decisively on retention: on Qwen-2.5-3B, ARC-C improves from 42.7 to 79.2 and HotpotQA from 24.1 to 51.7, while matching or exceeding SFT on target MATH (81.1 vs. 80.3). Cross-task interference drops from 39.2 to 2.3, a 17× reduction. Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), TMS is slightly behind on retention (ARC-C: 79.2 vs. 80.2; HotpotQA: 51.7 vs. 53.1 on Qwen-2.5-3B) and cross-task drift (2.3 vs. 1.9), but matches or exceeds GRPO on several target tasks (Math500: 77.8 vs. 77.4; IFEval: 72.0 vs. 70.9) and does so without reward models, verifiers, or online rollouts. TMS also substantially outperforms single-snapshot baselines (Self-SFT, Final-SFT) on retention, confirming that trajectory mixing across checkpoints is the key ingredient rather than simply replacing oracle labels with one synthetic snapshot. On safety retention (Table 9), TMS closely tracks GRPO (e.g., WildJailBreak ASR: 82.9 vs. 83.7 on Qwen-2.5-3B) while SFT degrades significantly (89.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard SFT post-training paradigm (Ouyang et al., 2022) and the observation that on-policy RL (GRPO, REINFORCE) preserves retention better than static-label SFT. It departs from prior on-policy retention work (Chen et al., 2025; Chu et al., 2025; Jin et al., 2025a), which attributes RL's retention advantage to KL regularization or preference signals, by identifying a more specific mechanism—supervision mismatch drift and mode collapse under single-reference cross-entropy—and addressing both with a reward-free trajectory mixture harvested from the model's own intermediate checkpoints. The method is conceptually related to self-distillation and iterative refinement (Zelikman et al., 2022; Agarwal et al., 2024), but differs in using the full optimization trajectory rather than a single snapshot, and in explicitly targeting retention rather than accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TMS offers a practical, drop-in replacement for standard SFT that substantially reduces catastrophic forgetting at roughly 2× SFT compute (vs. ~4× for GRPO), without requiring reward models, verifiers, or online rollouts—making it accessible to practitioners who cannot afford RL infrastructure. The KL-to-base diagnostic (Spearman 0.93 with forgetting) provides a simple, cross-method tool for monitoring post-training drift. Open questions include whether TMS extends to multimodal and agentic settings (only briefly piloted in the appendix), whether the 70B pilot's smaller retention gaps indicate diminishing returns at scale, and whether the offline trajectory buffer can be made online to further close the gap to true on-policy RL.
