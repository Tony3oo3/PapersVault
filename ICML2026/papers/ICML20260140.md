## Problem

MLLMs exhibit strong semantic understanding but fail at fine-grained spatial tasks such as precise counting, relative depth estimation, and left/right disambiguation. Recent work shows this failure stems not from insufficient visual encoder capacity but from training signals that allow the model to rely on linguistic priors (e.g., 'sky is up') rather than grounded geometry. The paper asks whether abstract, unambiguous geometric reasoning skills can efficiently transfer from synthetic overlays to real-world spatial understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260140/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images with procedurally rendered geometric overlays (colored bounding boxes, semi-transparent circles, labeled points) paired with text prompts, and produces structured answers: spatial relationship labels (above/below/left/right), normalized 2D coordinates, integer counts, or letter-based distance/analogy selections. A key design choice is that overlays are injected directly into existing training images rather than creating separate synthetic samples, preserving the original dataset size while adding a dense supervision layer. The geometric primitives are rendered semi-transparent to avoid occluding the underlying semantic content.

## Method

PGT introduces three procedurally generated task types—spatial relationship (two colored bounding boxes with relative positioning and coordinate regression), counting (semi-transparent colored circles of a target color), and relative distance/analogy (labeled points with proximity queries)—that are uniformly sampled and overlaid onto real training images. The core mechanism is that geometric primitives have no semantic correlation with spatial concepts (a green box has no prior association with 'above'), forcing the visual attention mechanism to engage with actual image geometry rather than text statistics. Training is performed via standard instruction tuning on LLaVA-Instruct-1.5 augmented with PGT pairs, or via LoRA fine-tuning (rank 8, 5k samples, 2 epochs) on SOTA models like Qwen-2.5-VL and InternVL3. The overlay strategy avoids increasing dataset size, and ablations show that even 5% PGT augmentation yields most of the gains, with emergent 3D depth understanding arising purely from 2D distance estimation tasks.

## Results

When instruction-tuning MLLMs on LLaVA-v1.5-Instruct augmented with PGT, the method yields up to +20.0% on What's Up (Vicuna-1.5-7B) and +13.3% on CV-Bench-2D (Llama-3-8B), with emergent 3D gains of +9.7% on CV-Bench-3D despite purely 2D training signals. Finetuning SOTA models with only 5k PGT samples on gray backgrounds boosts Qwen2.5-VL-7B to 98.0% on What's Up and 84.6% on CV-Bench-3D, while LLaVA-Next-7B gains +8.3% on CV-Bench-2D and +14.9% on TallyQA. General perception benchmarks (GQA, POPE, MMSTAR) remain stable or improve, confirming PGT does not degrade broader semantic capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ViGoRL-3B (RL with coordinate grounding), Qwen2.5-VL-3B+PGT wins on VSR (84.0 vs. 74.1, +9.9), TallyQA (67.5 vs. 64.8), RealWorldQA (62.9 vs. 47.3, +15.6), and BLINK (68.4 vs. 65.0), while ViGoRL edges ahead on CV-Bench-2D (78.0 vs. 74.4) and What's Up (96.2 vs. 96.1). Against Spatial-Ladder-3B (progressive RL curriculum), PGT dominates across nearly all benchmarks, with the largest gaps on VSR (+23.6) and TallyQA (+24.4), though Spatial-Ladder retains a small MMVP advantage (48.1 vs. 45.3). Against Image Jigsaw-7B (RLVR on jigsaw puzzles), Qwen2.5-VL-7B+PGT leads on CV-Bench-3D (84.6 vs. 83.0), TallyQA (73.5 vs. 70.4), and What's Up (98.0 vs. 97.4), but Image Jigsaw achieves a notably higher MMVP score (58.0 vs. 54.0). Against ThinkLite-VL-7B (MCTS-guided data selection + RL), PGT wins on CV-Bench-3D (+4.1), VSR (+2.4), and MMVP (+22.0), while ThinkLite-VL slightly leads on What's Up (98.3 vs. 98.0) and BLINK (75.9 vs. 74.8). Overall, PGT with only 5k procedurally generated samples and no human annotations matches or exceeds specialized RL-based methods on the majority of fine-grained spatial and quantitative benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both model-centric approaches (higher-resolution encoders, multi-scale ViT mixtures) and data-centric scaling strategies (massive human-annotated datasets, RL-based curricula like ViGoRL and Spatial-Ladder) by proposing a minimal, annotation-free data augmentation: overlaying procedurally generated geometric primitives directly onto existing instruction-tuning images. Conceptually, it builds on the observation from Fu et al. (2024) that MLLM spatial failures stem from insufficient training incentives to override linguistic priors rather than from inadequate visual representations, and operationalizes this insight by creating unambiguous supervision where no semantic shortcut exists. Unlike prior synthetic-data approaches that append separate datasets or require RL, PGT injects dense spatial supervision into the same training samples, preserving dataset size while forcing the attention mechanism to ground in actual image geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong evidence that fine-grained spatial deficits in MLLMs are primarily a supervision-signal problem rather than an architectural or resolution bottleneck, which reframes how the community should diagnose and address these failures. The emergent 3D depth understanding from purely 2D distance-estimation tasks suggests a shared geometric reasoning circuit, opening the door to cheaper 3D capability acquisition. Open questions include whether the PGT framework scales to more complex multi-step spatial reasoning, whether 3D primitives would further amplify the depth gains, and whether the benefits persist in video/temporal settings. The paper also positions PGT as a diagnostic baseline: if a model improves dramatically with PGT, the capability was dormant rather than absent, potentially saving researchers from unnecessary architectural overhauls.
