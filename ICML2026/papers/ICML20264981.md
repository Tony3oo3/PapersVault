## Problem

Existing amortised Bayesian inference methods either fix the prior at training time (requiring costly retraining for prior changes) or, when prior-flexible, fail to preserve family structure between prior and posterior, preventing sequential composition where a posterior must become the next-step prior. This limits their utility in real-time sensor fusion, sensitivity analysis, and other settings requiring repeated inference under varying priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes prior distribution parameters φ (from any parametric family) and observations z (sensor readings, data-label pairs, or query points for predictive posteriors) as input, and produces a k-component GMM posterior approximation in the same parametric family as the prior GMM. Crucially, the architecture is permutation-equivariant (no positional encodings), treating GMM components as an unordered sequence, and jointly outputs both a prior GMM and posterior GMM without additional parameters. A sample-space transform f can warp the GMM to handle finite-support priors.

## Method

Distribution Transformers represent all distributions as Gaussian Mixture Models (universal approximators) and use a transformer decoder to map a tokenised GMM prior to a GMM posterior: GMM components are embedded as an unordered latent sequence, attend to each other via self-attention, and attend to observation tokens via cross-attention. A learnable embedding maps prior parameters to GMM components in latent space, and a learnable unembedding decodes back to GMM parameters (weights, means, Cholesky-decomposed covariances) with a cross-component softmax. Training uses a sample-based scheme over a meta-prior p(φ), minimising expected NLL (equivalent to expected KL-divergence with the true posterior), plus a prior loss term that enforces latent-space conjugacy—ensuring the unembedded posterior GMM can be directly re-embedded as the next-step prior without additional parameters, enabling constant-time-per-step sequential inference.

## Results

On the analytical verification study (inverse-gamma prior, normal-variance likelihood), DT-5 achieves an expected KL-divergence of 0.0004±0.0000 (narrow meta-prior) and 0.0003±0.0000 (wide meta-prior), outperforming all baselines while completing 1000 inferences in 0.016 s—roughly 9,000× faster than SVI (148 s) and 95× faster than TabPFNv2 (1.52 s). In the sequential sensor-fusion task, DT achieves an expected NLL of −0.197±0.040, closely matching the near-ground-truth Particle Filter (−0.244±0.047) at ~50× lower iteration time (0.017 s vs 0.818 s per 100-series batch), while the EKF fails entirely (NLL 95.9±4.40). On the GP joint predictive/hyperposterior task, DT attains the best PPD NLL (0.81±0.02) and hyperposterior NLL (0.31±0.02) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DT-5 dominates all baselines on the primary analytical benchmark: it reduces expected KL-divergence by ~10× over ACE-5 (0.0004 vs 0.0094, narrow) and ~25× over TabPFNv2 (0.0004 vs 0.0112), while running 2.3× faster than ACE and 95× faster than TabPFNv2. Against SVI, DT-5 is ~100× more accurate (0.0004 vs 0.0425) and ~9,000× faster (0.016 s vs 148 s per 1000 problems). PFNs are slightly faster in raw inference time (0.003 s) but suffer catastrophic failure under the wide meta-prior (KL 331.5 for PFN-15), whereas DT-5 remains near-perfect (0.0003). In the sequential sensor-fusion setting, DT matches the Particle Filter's NLL within one standard deviation (−0.197 vs −0.244) at ~50× lower iteration cost, while the EKF is completely inapplicable (NLL 95.9). On the GP hyperposterior task, DT achieves the best NLL (0.31) among all methods, edging out ACE (0.35) and TabPFNv2 (0.37) while remaining competitive on runtime (9.5 s vs 11.7 s and 28.5 s respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transformer-based amortised Bayesian inference paradigm established by PFNs (Müller et al., 2021) and TabPFN (Hollmann et al., 2022, 2025) by introducing two key departures: (i) representing both prior and posterior as Gaussian Mixture Models tokenised as unordered sequences, enabling a distribution-to-distribution mapping rather than a data-to-distribution mapping, and (ii) training over a meta-prior so that the prior itself becomes an input, amortising inference across a family of priors. The conjugacy property—outputting the posterior in the same GMM family as the prior—distinguishes DTs from all prior amortised methods (PFNs, TabPFN, ACE) and from classical filtering approaches (EKF, particle filters), which either assume Gaussian structure or lack amortisation. The architecture borrows the transformer decoder with cross-attention from PFNs but repurposes self-attention over GMM components and exploits permutation equivariance (no positional encodings) to respect the unordered nature of mixture components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DTs fill a practical gap: they enable millisecond-scale approximate Bayesian inference with on-the-fly prior changes and, uniquely among amortised methods, support sequential composition (filtering) because the posterior GMM can be fed directly as the next-step prior GMM. The demonstrated ~50× speedup over particle filters while matching their NLL in a 10-dimensional stochastic volatility model suggests a viable path to real-time sequential inference in settings where PFs are computationally prohibitive. Key limitations include GMM expressiveness degrading in high latent dimensions (quadratic memory from full-covariance decoding), increased offline training cost from the meta-prior dimensionality, and small but nonzero error accumulation over long sequential horizons. Open questions include systematic scaling to very high-dimensional latents, robustness to substantial meta-prior misspecification, and whether the GMM family can be replaced by other universal approximators without losing the conjugacy property.
