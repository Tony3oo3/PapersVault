## Problem

Existing training-free video anomaly detection methods face a fundamental trade-off: generative VLM-based approaches achieve strong semantic understanding but suffer from ~1 FPS inference latency and fragile prompt engineering, while retrieval-based embedding methods are fast but require large auxiliary knowledge bases and suffer from 'anomaly bias'—a systematic false-positive effect where normal video clips exhibit high cosine similarity to anomaly text due to anisotropic (cone-shaped) embedding geometry. Prior work addressed this bias with heuristic scalar penalization, which lacks an explicit mathematical model of the underlying feature-space distortion.

## Input / Output

PRISM takes a video clip (sampled every 16 frames with a 7-frame sliding window, stride 1) and a small fixed pool of text descriptions (20 sentences per anomaly/normal category, totaling ~280–500 sentences depending on the dataset) as input. It produces per-frame anomaly scores that are temporally smoothed with a 1-D Gaussian filter (σ=16), which can be thresholded to yield anomaly frame predictions. Critically, no training data, auxiliary video database, or online VLM calls are needed at inference time; the entire pipeline runs in real time (~50 FPS) on a single RTX 4090 using a frozen multimodal encoder (Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B or Perception Encoder-L14).

## Method

PRISM's core insight is that the 'anomaly bias' in zero-shot detection is mathematically equivalent to common-mode noise in an anisotropic embedding space: all vectors share a dominant background direction, and because rare anomaly descriptions have smaller event-semantic norms than frequent normal descriptions, their complementary background norms are larger, producing a strictly positive bias term that overwhelms the true semantic signal. To suppress this, PRISM estimates a regularized covariance matrix (Σ_T + λI, λ=0.01) from the small text pool and applies ZCA whitening (W = (Σ_T + λI)^{-1/2}) exclusively to the text-side anomaly directions, constructing decorrelated semantic axes a_j = Normalize(W(μ_a^j − μ_n)). Video embeddings are L2-normalized and centered by the normal text mean but are not whitened; they are scored by inner product against the whitened axes, aggregated via temperature-controlled softmax (τ_sem=0.2) across M anomaly groups, and temporally smoothed. This text-only whitening corrects the second-order statistics of the semantic anchors without altering the video representation, achieving real-time inference while significantly improving SNR, AUC, and AP over both retrieval-based and generative training-free baselines.

## Results

PRISM(Qwen) achieves 88.85% AUC on UCF-Crime, 93.42% AUC and 77.48% AP on XD-Violence, and 91.84% AUC on MSAD, establishing state-of-the-art among all training-free VAD methods. The Perception Encoder variant (PRISM(PE)) further improves XD-Violence AP to 80.31% and AUC to 94.09%. On MSAD, PRISM surpasses the best weakly supervised baseline (π-VAD, 88.68% AUC) by 3.16 points without any training data. End-to-end inference runs at 50.75 FPS (PE backbone) or 11.48 FPS (Qwen backbone) on a single RTX 4090, with the PRISM algorithm itself adding only ~0.08 ms per frame.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among training-free methods, PRISM(Qwen) outperforms the previous best (Flashback) by 1.56 points on UCF-Crime AUC (88.85% vs. 87.29%) and by 2.35 points on XD-Violence AP (77.48% vs. 75.13%). PRISM(PE) extends the XD-Violence AP lead to 5.18 points over Flashback (80.31% vs. 75.13%), surpassing even the fine-tuned Holmes-VAD (90.67% AP) is not achieved, but PRISM(PE) still exceeds all other training-free baselines by a wide margin. On MSAD, PRISM(Qwen) leads Flashback by 0.90 AUC points (91.84% vs. 90.94%) and VADTree by 2.52 points, while also exceeding the best weakly supervised method π-VAD (88.68%) by 3.16 points. Against weakly supervised baselines on UCF-Crime, PRISM(Qwen) (88.85%) trails GS-MoE (91.58%) and π-VAD (90.33%) but surpasses VadCLIP (88.02%) and DSANet (89.44%) is not surpassed—PRISM is 0.59 points below DSANet. The ablation confirms that the whitening mechanism contributes +1.66 AUC (M3→M4) and multi-axis semantic aggregation adds a further +1.04 AUC (M4→M6) on UCF-Crime.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM carves new ground at the intersection of video-text embedding retrieval and classical statistical signal processing. It departs from Flashback (Lee et al., 2025), the closest prior training-free retrieval-based VAD method, by replacing heuristic scalar penalization for 'anomaly bias' with a principled ZCA whitening transformation on the text-side covariance, reframing the bias as common-mode noise in anisotropic embedding spaces. The theoretical analysis draws on anisotropy literature from NLP (Gao et al., 2019; Liang et al., 2021) and signal-processing concepts (differential amplifiers, common-mode rejection), while the practical pipeline leverages frozen multimodal encoders (Qwen3-VL-Embedding, Perception Encoder) rather than the high-latency generative VLMs used by LAVAD or VADTree. No prior VAD work had applied second-order statistical decorrelation to the text anchor space in this manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRISM demonstrates that a 0.08 ms whitening operation can close much of the gap between training-free and weakly supervised VAD while preserving real-time inference (50+ FPS), making it practically deployable for surveillance without large knowledge bases or VLM calls. The paper's diagnostic explanation for the persistent AP gap on XD-Violence (scene-level variability causing false positives that degrade precision-recall ranking) is a useful contribution to understanding training-free method limitations. However, the whitening is linear and text-side only, the method requires a predefined description pool (not fully open-world), and non-linear foreground-background entanglement in complex scenes remains unresolved—open questions for future work include video-side whitening, non-linear manifold corrections, and truly open-set anomaly discovery.
