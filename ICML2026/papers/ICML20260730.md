## Problem

Centralized joint instruction tuning over large heterogeneous mixtures faces two coupled bottlenecks: conflicting gradients across diverse tasks cause negative transfer and constrain learning rates, while frequent gradient synchronization demands tightly-coupled high-bandwidth clusters. The paper asks whether these can be addressed jointly by training parts of the mixture independently and reconciling them once in parameter space, rather than forcing all datasets onto a single synchronized trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260730/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MERIT consumes a heterogeneous mixture of instruction datasets (e.g., 136 Vision-FLAN tasks or 176-source 1.6M-example mixtures) and a merge-ready initialization checkpoint (an already instruction-tuned MLLM or pretrained LLM). Each dataset is represented by a small calibration gradient (≤200 examples, optionally stride-subsampled to 20% of parameters) computed at the shared initialization; the pipeline outputs a single merged model via one-shot token-weighted averaging of K=2^r independently fine-tuned branch checkpoints. For multimodal settings, images are processed at up to 784×784 pixels with the vision encoder frozen during fine-tuning.

## Method

MERIT partitions a heterogeneous instruction mixture along dominant gradient-conflict axes identified by PCA on a cosine-similarity matrix of per-dataset gradients, then fine-tunes each partition independently from a shared merge-ready initialization with zero cross-partition communication, and merges the resulting checkpoints via a single token-weighted averaging step. The theoretical foundation is a local quadratic analysis inside a shared flat basin showing that (i) weight merging yields a curvature-weighted variance reduction that is never negative, (ii) PCA-aligned splitting maximizes this gain by concentrating inter-group dispersion along high-curvature directions (with advantage scaling as λ₁³), and (iii) averaging acts as implicit norm regularization and approximate spectral filtering, lowering the effective condition number. The recursive 50/50 split along r PCA axes uses sample-balanced medians to keep group sizes equal, and the merge weights are proportional to per-group token budgets, reducing to uniform averaging when budgets are exactly balanced.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-3B with 136 Vision-FLAN tasks, MERIT-3D (8 groups) achieves a 57.0 average across 8 multimodal benchmarks, improving over 1-epoch joint training (54.3) by +2.7 points without any cross-partition gradient communication. At 7B scale on a 1.6M-example, 176-source mixture, MERIT-2D (4 groups) outperforms centralized Joint FFT on both base models: 55.4 vs. 54.9 (Base VLM) and 61.5 vs. 60.9 (Scaled base VLM). On text-only FLAN (66 tasks, Qwen2.5-3B), MERIT-2D reaches 58.4 average, exceeding 1-epoch joint training (57.6) by +0.8 and matching 2-epoch joint training (58.3) at half the token budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against uniform model soups (Wortsman et al., 2022), which average multiple runs on the full mixture, MERIT-3D (57.0) outperforms the best soup variant (55.4 for 2-model soup) by +1.6, confirming that conflict-aware partitioning of disjoint data yields larger gains than averaging runs on identical data. On the post-hoc merging comparison (Figure 3, 7B 2D branches), token-weighted averaging (55.5 mean) decisively beats TIES-Merging (50.4), STAR (41.0), TSV (46.1), and Iso-CTS (45.4) by 5–15 points on the 8-benchmark average; the authors attribute this to MERIT's branches being complementary by construction, so trimming or orthogonalization-based operators discard branch-specific content that only one partition holds. MERIT also outperforms random partitioning with the same number of groups and merge step by +2.5 (MERIT-3D vs. Random-8), isolating the contribution of the PCA conflict-aware split. At 7B scale, MERIT-2D consistently exceeds centralized Joint FFT across three independent seeds (55.4 vs. 54.9 on Base VLM; 61.5 vs. 60.9 on Scaled base), with the gap concentrated on open-ended generation (LLaVA-Wild, MMVet) where joint training suffers short-answer collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

MERIT sits at the intersection of model soups (Wortsman et al., 2022; Jang et al., 2024), which average checkpoints trained on the same data with different randomness, and one-shot federated learning, which aggregates after a single local-training round. The key departure is that MERIT merges models trained on *disjoint* partitions of a heterogeneous mixture, and it *optimizes* the partition itself via gradient-conflict PCA rather than accepting fixed data silos. A local quadratic analysis in a shared flat basin (Section 3) provides the theoretical bridge: it shows that PCA-aligned splitting concentrates checkpoint dispersion along high-curvature directions, maximizing the curvature-weighted variance reduction from averaging, and that the merge acts as implicit norm regularization and approximate spectral filtering. This positions MERIT as moving conflict handling from the per-step gradient-manipulation regime (PCGrad, GradNorm) to a pre-training partitioning decision, eliminating the need for synchronized per-step control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners running instruction tuning in fragmented or bandwidth-constrained compute environments (heterogeneous GPU pools, geo-distributed clusters, spot instances), MERIT offers a drop-in recipe that removes step-level synchronization while matching or exceeding centralized joint training at 3B and 7B scale. The finding that simple token-weighted averaging outperforms four state-of-the-art post-hoc merging operators (TIES, STAR, TSV, Iso-CTS) by 5–15 points is a useful negative result for the merging community: when branches are complementary by construction, trimming or orthogonalization discards irreplaceable content. Open questions include whether the flat-basin assumption holds for more aggressive fine-tuning or very different initializations, whether the PCA dimension r should be adapted per mixture, and whether the approach extends to settings where the merge-ready initialization is not readily available (e.g., from-scratch pretraining).
