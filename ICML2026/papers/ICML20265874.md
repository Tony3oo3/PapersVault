## Problem

Existing offline meta-RL methods for generalizing to unseen tasks rely on task-specific demonstrations or test-time environment interaction, which are often unavailable in real-world settings. This paper asks whether natural language task descriptions alone—without any target-task data or online interaction—can enable true zero-shot policy generalization in offline meta-RL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265874/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, TG-DT takes a templated natural language description (e.g., task name, high-level intent, environment description, approximate episode length and expected return inferred from the training distribution) and optionally a small set of semantically related training trajectories for data sharing. It autoregressively outputs a sequence of continuous actions conditioned on the text embedding and past states/actions. The templated format is a key design choice: during training, trajectory-specific metadata (exact return, episode length) is embedded in the text to encode demonstration quality, while at test time these fields are filled with approximate statistics to prevent oracle leakage.

## Method

TG-DT introduces a dual text-behavior alignment mechanism: a contrastive loss (TBC) that pulls matched text-trajectory pairs together and pushes cross-task pairs apart for inter-task separation, and a matching loss (TBM) formulated as binary classification with hard negative mining (including intra-task quality mismatches) to capture fine-grained within-task differences. A text-behavior encoder with cross-attention layers models the joint interaction, while a text-behavior decoder—replacing DT's bi-directional self-attention with causal self-attention and reusing the encoder's cross-attention and feedforward layers—autoregressively generates actions conditioned on the aligned text embedding. The three objectives (L_TBC + L_TBM + L_tD T) are jointly optimized, with components initialized from pre-trained BLIP to leverage rich text semantics. At test time, a description-guided data sharing strategy retrieves the top-K most semantically similar training trajectories via cosine similarity on text embeddings and fine-tunes the decoder on them while still conditioning on the target task's description, enhancing zero-shot adaptation without any target-task supervision.

## Results

In the zero-shot setting with Medium datasets, TG-DT achieves test returns of 549.9 (Cheetah-dir), −93.0 (Cheetah-vel), 328.3 (Ant-dir), 361.1 (ML10), and 309.6 (ML45), matching or exceeding all baselines on 4 out of 5 environments without any test-time environment interaction. In the few-shot setting, TG-DT further improves to 598.4, −73.2, 412.9, 421.5, and 499.2, consistently outperforming or matching all baselines. The method's key advantage is that it requires no test-time interaction or task-specific data, unlike most competing DT-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TG-DT outperforms all DT-based baselines (PDT, GDT, HDT, DPDT) on Cheetah-vel by a wide margin (−93.0 vs. −102.7 to −218.4) and on ML10 (361.1 vs. 289.2–360.4), while matching them on Cheetah-dir (549.9 vs. 548.1–548.9). Against MDT, the strongest competitor, TG-DT trails on Ant-dir (328.3 vs. 357.5) but leads on Cheetah-vel (−93.0 vs. −102.7) and ML10 (361.1 vs. 335.2); critically, MDT requires test-time interaction to obtain adaptation demonstrations, whereas TG-DT does not. Language-conditioned baselines BC-Z and BAKU are substantially outperformed across all environments (e.g., 361.1 vs. 199.7/216.3 on ML10), as they lack return conditioning. In the few-shot setting, TG-DT achieves the best or near-best return on every environment (e.g., 412.9 on Ant-dir vs. 410.4 for DPDT; 499.2 on ML45 vs. 498.6 for DPDT), confirming that its text-behavior alignment provides a strong initialization that benefits from limited task-specific data.

## Relation to Prior Work

TG-DT extends Decision Transformer from return-to-go conditioning to text-conditioned action generation, drawing on vision-language alignment techniques (CLIP-style contrastive learning, BLIP initialization) and adapting them to the sequential, temporally-extended nature of behavioral trajectories. It departs from prior offline meta-RL methods (Prompt-DT, Meta-DT, GDT, HDT, DPDT) that require test-time interaction or task-specific demonstrations, and from language-conditioned RL baselines (BC-Z, BAKU) that omit return conditioning and are not designed for offline meta-RL. The key methodological departure is the dual alignment mechanism—combining contrastive learning for cross-task separation with a matching-based objective for within-task quality discrimination—which addresses a gap neither prior DT-based nor language-conditioned approaches fill.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TG-DT demonstrates that zero-shot offline meta-RL generalization is achievable from natural language alone, without any test-time environment interaction, which is a practically important constraint for robotics and safety-critical domains where exploration is costly or dangerous. The acknowledged limitation that templated descriptions (with metadata like expected return and episode length) are required at test time significantly constrains real-world applicability, and supporting free-form, unconstrained language instructions remains an open challenge. Broader open questions include robustness to distribution shift, whether the alignment mechanism scales to more complex or higher-dimensional tasks, and whether the moderate cosine similarity gap between text and behavior embeddings (~0.3) could become a bottleneck in more demanding settings.
