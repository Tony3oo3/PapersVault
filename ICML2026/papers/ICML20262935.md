## Problem

Deploying contactless mmWave-to-ECG systems in home settings is severely limited by the scarcity of high-quality synchronized radar-ECG paired recordings, which are expensive to collect, restricted by privacy regulations, and inherently event-sparse for intermittent arrhythmias. This data bottleneck causes a persistent cross-subject generalization gap, as models trained on small lab-collected cohorts degrade sharply on unseen individuals. The paper tackles this by synthesizing diverse, personalized mmWave radar signals from large-scale public ECG datasets, eliminating the need for extensive paired collection.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512-sample ECG segment (200 Hz, z-score normalized, sourced from PTB-XL) and a 5-dimensional subject metadata vector (gender, age, height, weight, BMI) as input, and produces a 512-sample mmWave radar waveform (200 Hz, 10–40 Hz band-pass filtered) as output. Both modalities are first encoded into 16-dimensional latent spaces by frozen, independently pretrained VAEs; the actual transport and generation occur entirely in this shared latent space, with the final radar signal recovered by the radar VAE decoder. The ECG latent serves as the physiological initialization rather than being discarded, and the metadata conditions the transport trajectory for subject-specific propagation effects.

## Method

Cardio-mmFlow replaces the standard Gaussian-noise initialization of diffusion/flow models with a direct latent transport from ECG latents to radar latents, motivated by the fact that paired ECG and radar segments are two measurements of the same cardiac cycle. A learnable projector P_φ first aligns the ECG latent distribution toward the radar latent coordinate system (enforced by a Wasserstein-1 loss via differentiable Sinkhorn iterations), and a 4-layer DiT velocity network then learns the conditional flow-matching ODE from the projected source to the target radar latent. Subject-specific propagation variability is injected through an MSD-FiLM module: a damping-centric simplification of the mass-spring-damper chest-wall model produces a bounded attenuation gain α∈(0,1]^D from metadata, which modulates intermediate Transformer features via element-wise scaling, while a decoupled time-dependent shift term prevents subject offsets from leaking into the modulation. Training combines the flow-matching velocity loss, the Wasserstein alignment loss, a spectral fidelity loss (normalized magnitude + cosine phase similarity in the FFT domain), and classifier-free guidance (30% condition dropout) to strengthen conditional adherence to the personalized damping profile.

## Results

On radar synthesis quality (Table 1), Cardio-mmFlow achieves the best Latent-FID (14.98), lowest MSE (0.027±0.013), and highest PSD similarity (0.919±0.078) among all baselines, with a competitive HR-AE of 6.66 bpm. On the zero-shot downstream mmWave→ECG reconstruction task (Table 2), it attains the lowest RMSE (0.11±0.02), highest PCC (0.79±0.06), and smallest R-R timing error (8.13±3.94 ms). The model uses 11.2M parameters, comparable to all baselines (10.7M–14.4M). For AFib classification trained solely on synthetic radar, the model achieves 87% recall and 0.72 AUC-ROC on real radar data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cardio-mmFlow outperforms all five baselines on the three primary synthesis metrics (Latent-FID, MSE, PSD similarity). Against cGAN (Isola et al., 2017), it reduces Latent-FID from 15.21 to 14.98 and HR-AE from 12.56 to 6.66 bpm. Against cVAE (Sohn et al., 2015), the gains are largest: Latent-FID drops from 29.31 to 14.98 and PSD similarity rises from 0.823 to 0.919. Against the standard DiT diffusion transformer (Peebles & Xie, 2023), Cardio-mmFlow improves MSE from 0.051 to 0.027 and HR-AE from 12.21 to 6.66. Against RDDM (Shome et al., 2024), a PPG-to-ECG diffusion model, it wins on every metric, with MSE improving from 0.065 to 0.027. The closest competitor is PPGflowECG (Fang et al., 2025), which achieves a slightly lower HR-AE (6.00 vs. 6.66 bpm) but is outperformed on Latent-FID (18.72 vs. 14.98), MSE (0.029 vs. 0.027), and PSD similarity (0.864 vs. 0.919). On the zero-shot downstream mmWave→ECG task, Cardio-mmFlow also leads all baselines, with DiT and PPGflowECG as the strongest runners-up.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow matching / rectified flow paradigm (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) by removing the Gaussian prior and instead initializing the transport from structured ECG latent codes, coupled with a Wasserstein distance alignment loss (Arjovsky et al., ICML 2017) to bridge independently pretrained VAE manifolds. It departs from prior cross-modal bio-signal translation work (PPG→ECG via PPGflowECG, RDDM) and contactless ECG reconstruction (AirECG, RadarODE, mmECG) by inverting the direction: rather than reconstructing ECG from radar, it synthesizes radar from ECG to generate training supervision. The physics-informed MSD-FiLM module draws on classical mechanical vibration theory to inject subject-specific attenuation priors, a design choice absent from generic conditioning approaches in prior RF synthesis work (RF Genesis, RF-Diffusion, MIDAS).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a genuine bottleneck in contactless cardiac sensing: the scarcity of large-scale synchronized radar-ECG pairs due to privacy constraints, collection cost, and event sparsity. By enabling zero-shot cross-subject mmWave→ECG reconstruction and AFib classification trained solely on synthetic radar, it offers a scalable path toward home-deployable monitoring without per-user paired data collection. Key limitations include evaluation on small cohorts (10 self-collected subjects, 30 training subjects), restriction to supine posture and single-lead ECG, and moderate precision (62%) in the AFib classification that limits clinical readiness. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-lead ECG, motion/posture variability, and whether the physics-informed modulation scales to more complex biomechanical models.
