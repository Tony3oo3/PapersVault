## Problem

High-quality coronary angiography (CAG) stenosis annotations are scarce and costly, limiting the training of automated stenosis detectors. Existing diffusion-based editing methods rely on soft guidance in noise-initialized reverse processes, which cannot guarantee pixel-level geometric precision or strict preservation of vessel topology outside the edit region. The paper addresses the gap of achieving controllable, geometry-accurate local editing of stenosis lesions while maintaining structural integrity of the surrounding vasculature.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real CAG image x₀, a binary editing mask M specifying the stenosis region, and a target geometric description S* (lumen mask or boundary) encoding the desired stenosis location, shape, and severity. It produces an edited angiogram x₁ in which the vessel geometry within M matches S* at the pixel level, while the complement region M̄ is preserved exactly (x ⊙ M̄ = x₀ ⊙ M̄). Editing operates in a Vessel-structure Composite Domain, a 3-channel condition stacking the edited mask, its masked edge map, and its boundary, which serves as the start state for the bridge process.

## Method

OT-Bridge Editor reformulates geometry-constrained stenosis editing as a constrained entropic optimal transport problem between a source distribution anchored at the input and a target distribution restricted by a geometric feasibility set F, solved via a Schrödinger Bridge (SB) that lifts the transport to path space and yields a discrete bridge rollout of T=50 steps. The key innovation is Geometric Generation-Path Guidance (GPG): at every K=5 steps, a projection operator Π_F is applied to the intermediate state, solving a penalized optimization that enforces SDT-based boundary alignment with the target geometry inside M and an L2 stability penalty on the protected region outside M. This converts endpoint-only conditioning into path-level geometric supervision, pulling the entire trajectory into a geometry-feasible corridor and preventing drift on thin vessels. The mask-aware transport cost further discourages changes in the protected region, and the composite-domain start state (combining segmentation, edges, and boundary) provides richer geometric initialization than a single modality.

## Results

On the geometric compliance task (Table 1), OT-Bridge Editor achieves mIoU 0.892±0.037 and Dice 0.774±0.055, outperforming the strongest baseline SiameseDiff (mIoU 0.837, Dice 0.722). In terms of image quality (Table 2), it attains the best FID (16.747), SSIM (0.878), and LPIPS (0.248), indicating superior realism and structure preservation. For downstream stenosis detection on ARCADE (Table 3), augmenting with OT-Bridge Editor synthetic data yields a 27.8% relative mAP@0.5 gain (0.525→0.727 for YOLOv8, Real+Synth vs. Real-only), with a consistent 23.0% relative gain on the multi-center dataset (Table 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OT-Bridge Editor outperforms all six baselines on both geometric compliance (mIoU, Dice) and image quality (FID, SSIM, LPIPS). The closest competitor is SiameseDiff, which achieves mIoU 0.837 and Dice 0.722; OT-Bridge Editor improves by 5.5 points in mIoU and 5.2 points in Dice, while also reducing FID from 34.2 to 16.7 and improving SSIM from 0.790 to 0.878. Among GAN-based baselines, Pix2PixHD (mIoU 0.801) and SPADE (mIoU 0.726) show the weakest geometric control, with SPADE also producing the worst FID (78.6) and SSIM (0.577). Diffusion-based baselines SDEdit (mIoU 0.781) and SDM (mIoU 0.783) perform similarly to each other but remain 10+ points below OT-Bridge Editor in mIoU, reflecting their reliance on soft guidance rather than hard path-level geometric constraints. The task-specific DiGDA method (Seo et al., 2025) achieves mAP@0.5 of 0.647 under Real+Synth on ARCADE with YOLOv8, compared to 0.727 for OT-Bridge Editor, confirming the advantage of the constrained OT formulation for downstream detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge (De Bortoli et al., 2021) as its core generative mechanism and on entropic optimal transport (Cuturi, 2013) as the mathematical formulation, but departs from both by imposing hard geometric feasibility constraints along the entire bridge trajectory rather than relying on soft guidance or endpoint-only conditioning. It positions itself against the dominant diffusion-editing paradigm (SDEdit, SDM, ControlNet, SiameseDiff), which the authors argue offers limited pixel-level geometric precision because conditioning acts as a soft constraint during a noise-initialized reverse process. The key conceptual departure is reframing localized image editing as a constrained entropic OT problem in a vessel-structure composite domain, solved via a Schrödinger Bridge with the novel Geometric Generation-Path Guidance (GPG) that projects intermediate states onto a geometric feasibility set at each step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical bottleneck in coronary stenosis detection—scarcity of expert-annotated CAG data—by providing a synthesis pipeline with pixel-accurate geometric control that existing controllable generators (Pix2PixHD, SPADE, SDEdit, SiameseDiff) cannot match, yielding 27.8% relative mAP gains on ARCADE and 23.0% on a multi-center dataset. The finding that synthetic-only training can outperform real-only training on ARCADE (e.g., YOLOv8: 0.662 vs. 0.525 mAP) suggests the synthetic distribution captures clinically relevant morphological diversity, though this advantage does not transfer to the multi-center setting where Synth-only underperforms Real-only. Open questions include generalization to unseen centers, rare lesion morphologies, and different acquisition protocols, as well as whether the OT/SB/GPG framework extends beyond CAG to other geometry-constrained medical editing tasks.
