## Problem

Diffusion language models (dLLMs) lack effective RL post-training because they cannot compute tractable sequence-level likelihoods, forcing existing methods to rely on biased ELBO approximations that produce suboptimal policy updates and a mismatch between the trained policy and the actual inference process. The paper asks whether a simpler, ELBO-free policy gradient algorithm can be designed that naturally aligns with the iterative denoising process of dLLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265733/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (a math word problem, countdown puzzle, or 4×4 sudoku grid) and generates a fixed-length response (n=384 tokens) by iteratively unmasking tokens over m=128 denoising steps, with n/m tokens unmasked per step. The output is a chain-of-thought reasoning trace in <think> tags followed by a final answer in <answer> tags with \boxed{} notation. During training, random unmasking injects stochasticity for exploration; at inference, confidence-based unmasking (selecting highest-probability tokens) is used, and the model generalizes to different n/m ratios despite being trained on a single configuration.

## Method

AGRPO reformulates dLLM generation as a multi-step MDP where each denoising step is an action (unmasking n/m tokens), yielding tractable stepwise likelihoods that eliminate the need for ELBO approximations. The key insight is reinterpreting the inner sum over timesteps in the PPO surrogate objective as an expectation, enabling Monte Carlo estimation with k ≪ m sampled timesteps to produce unbiased gradient updates at a fraction of the full-trajectory compute cost. Two variance reduction techniques are introduced: low-discrepancy sampling to ensure coverage across the denoising trajectory, and entropy importance sampling to prioritize high-entropy (pivotal) timesteps where the reasoning trace is most sensitive. The KL penalty is likewise estimated via step-level MC sampling using a corrected k₃ estimator, and the full objective includes PPO-style clipping. Applied via LoRA fine-tuning on LLaDA-8B-Instruct, AGRPO achieves +59.4% and +69.7% absolute accuracy gains on Countdown and Sudoku over the base model, and matches baselines with 4× fewer inference steps.

## Results

AGRPO achieves the highest accuracy across 7 of 8 evaluation settings (all tasks at both sequence lengths except MATH-500 at n=256). The headline gains over the base LLaDA-8B-Instruct model are +59.4% on Countdown and +69.7% on Sudoku (at n=256), and +55.1% and +76.4% respectively at n=512. On GSM8K, AGRPO reaches 88.1% at n=512, surpassing the previous best (SPG at 84.5%) by +3.6%. On MATH-500, AGRPO achieves 42.0% at n=512, a +4.6% improvement over the base model and a narrow +0.2% edge over SPG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGRPO outperforms all five baselines on 7 of 8 evaluation settings, losing only to SPG on MATH-500 at n=256 (39.4% vs. 40.0%, a 0.6-point margin). Against the base LLaDA-8B-Instruct model, AGRPO delivers the largest absolute gains on Countdown (+55.1 to +59.4 points) and Sudoku (+69.7 to +76.4 points), indicating that step-level policy gradients are particularly effective for structured reasoning and planning tasks. Compared to the closest competitor SPG, AGRPO leads by +3.6 points on GSM8K (n=512), +12.1 on Countdown (n=512), and +2.1 on Sudoku (n=512), while trailing by 0.6 on MATH-500 (n=256). Against diffu-GRPO and wd1, which rely on ELBO-based sequence likelihood approximations, AGRPO's advantage is most pronounced on Countdown (up to +45.1 points over diffu-GRPO) and Sudoku (up to +65.9 points over diffu-GRPO), suggesting that avoiding biased likelihood bounds is critical for tasks requiring multi-step structured reasoning. VRPO (LLaDA 1.5), which uses a DPO-style objective with ELBOs, is outperformed by AGRPO on every task and sequence length, with the largest gap on Countdown (+52.4 to +55.1 points).

## Relation to Prior Work

AGRPO extends the multi-step denoising MDP formulation from continuous diffusion RL (DDPO, Flow-GRPO) to discrete masked diffusion language models, where prior dLLM RL methods (diffu-GRPO, SPG, wd1, VRPO) relied on ELBO-like sequence-level likelihood approximations that introduce biased gradients. The key departure is treating each individual unmasking step as an MDP action rather than the full sequence, yielding tractable stepwise likelihoods that align training with the actual inference process. The computational challenge of evaluating all m timesteps is addressed via Monte Carlo timestep estimation with two variance-reduction techniques (low-discrepancy sampling and entropy importance sampling), building on ideas from diffusion pretraining and the high-entropy token analysis of Wang et al. (2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled, ELBO-free policy gradient algorithm for dLLM post-training, closing a significant capability gap between diffusion and autoregressive LLMs on reasoning tasks. The practical finding that AGRPO-trained models match baselines with 4× fewer inference steps (and achieve 8.1× improvement over the base model at m=32) opens a new axis of compute/quality tradeoff unique to dLLMs. Limitations include evaluation on a single 8B model family, continued lag behind AR models on harder benchmarks (MATH), and the open question of how the optimal MC sampling budget k scales with sequence length and task difficulty. The approach's reliance on random unmasking during training versus confidence-based decoding at inference introduces a mild train-test mismatch that future work could address.
