## Problem

Existing zeroth-order fine-tuning methods for LLMs (MeZO, LOZO) sample perturbations from data-independent distributions—either isotropic Gaussian or random low-rank factors—ignoring the structural information exposed by the forward pass. This paper identifies that the gradient of a linear layer is geometrically confined to the subspace spanned by its input activations, and asks whether restricting ZO perturbations to this activation-informed subspace can yield provably better gradient alignment without increasing memory cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262743/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

AGZO consumes the current LLM weights, a text mini-batch, and the per-layer input activation matrices H_ℓ ∈ R^{d_in × m} produced during the standard forward pass. During that forward pass, it extracts a compact rank-r orthonormal basis A_ℓ ∈ R^{d_in × r} (via K=3 power-iteration steps) for each linear layer and immediately discards the full activation matrix. The output is a layer-wise ZO gradient estimate ĝ_μ^{AGZO}(W;B) formed from two forward evaluations (perturbed and unperturbed), which feeds a standard optimizer to produce updated weights. The only persistent per-layer state beyond the weights is the tiny basis A_ℓ, keeping peak memory essentially identical to inference.

## Method

AGZO's key mechanism is to replace data-independent ZO perturbations with activation-guided low-rank ones. For each linear layer ℓ, it constructs a rank-r subspace basis A_ℓ from the current mini-batch's input activations via lightweight power iteration on H_ℓH_ℓ^⊤, then samples a perturbation Δ_ℓ = R_ℓ A_ℓ^⊤ where R_ℓ is a random Gaussian left factor, so the perturbation's row space lies entirely within the activation subspace. Nonlinear layers fall back to standard Gaussian perturbations. The ZO estimator uses the standard two-point finite difference (f(W+μΔ)−f(W))/μ · Δ_ℓ per layer. Theoretically, the paper proves AGZO estimates the gradient of a subspace-smoothed objective projected onto span(A_ℓ), and under activation spectral concentration (top-r singular directions carry at least average gradient energy), AGZO achieves strictly higher expected cosine similarity to the true gradient than MeZO's isotropic estimator. In practice, rank r=1 is used, concentrating the single-query perturbation along the dominant activation direction while minimizing storage overhead.

## Results

On Qwen3-0.6B across 8 SuperGLUE/reading-comprehension tasks, AGZO achieves an average accuracy of 0.788, outperforming MeZO (0.763) and LOZO (0.764) while closing the gap to first-order fine-tuning (0.821). The largest gains appear on CB (+8.9 pp over MeZO, +13.2 pp over LOZO) and COPA (+6.0 pp over MeZO). On the larger Qwen3-4B model (where FO runs out of memory), AGZO again leads all ZO baselines on 6 of 7 tasks, and on Pangu-1B it achieves the best ZO average (0.730) versus MeZO (0.713) and LOZO (0.674).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MeZO (isotropic full-space ZO), AGZO wins on 7 of 8 Qwen3-0.6B tasks (e.g., CB 0.892 vs 0.803, RTE 0.772 vs 0.732) and on 6 of 7 Qwen3-4B tasks, with the sole exception being BoolQ where MeZO edges ahead by 0.006. Against LOZO (data-independent low-rank ZO), AGZO wins on all 8 Qwen3-0.6B tasks and 6 of 7 Qwen3-4B tasks, with the most pronounced margin on RTE (0.848 vs 0.801 on 4B) and CB (0.892 vs 0.760 on 0.6B). On Pangu-1B, AGZO leads both ZO baselines on 4 of 6 tasks and ties on COPA and CB. In the appendix comparison against LoRA (a first-order parameter-efficient method) on Qwen3-0.6B, AGZO outperforms LoRA on SST-2 (0.877 vs 0.834), CB (0.892 vs 0.817), and BoolQ (0.724 vs 0.684), ties on COPA, and trails slightly on MultiRC (0.756 vs 0.771), demonstrating that a ZO method can match or exceed a first-order PEFT baseline under the same step budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

AGZO extends the zeroth-order fine-tuning line initiated by MeZO (Malladi et al., 2023), which replaced backpropagation with two-point finite-difference estimators using isotropic Gaussian perturbations, and LOZO (Chen et al., 2025b), which imposed a data-independent low-rank structure on those perturbations. The key conceptual departure is that AGZO extracts a compact activation subspace on the fly during the forward pass and restricts perturbations to that subspace, leveraging the deterministic fact that a linear layer's gradient row-space is contained in its input activation column-space. This connects the ZO perturbation design to the broader low-rank adaptation literature (LoRA, intrinsic dimensionality) by grounding the perturbation geometry in the actual forward-pass signal rather than in parameter-shape heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AGZO provides a principled, provably superior alternative to isotropic ZO perturbations for memory-constrained LLM fine-tuning, with the practical benefit of narrowing the gap to first-order training while keeping peak memory at inference-level. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 5.6) formalizes when and why activation guidance helps, but the condition—that gradient energy be broadly distributed across the top activation directions—is fairly natural and may not hold in all regimes. Open questions include whether adaptive per-layer rank selection (the paper uses a fixed r=1) could further improve quality, whether the approach extends to multi-query or variance-reduced ZO settings, and how the method behaves when activation spectra are flatter (e.g., early in training or on very diverse data).
