## Problem

Diffusion LLMs support arbitrary-order generation, but existing unmasking strategies rely on static local heuristics (confidence, entropy, margin) that perform inconsistently across task domains, with no single rule excelling universally. The paper identifies that no principled, globally-consistent objective exists for selecting unmasking paths that are both accurate and task-agnostic, and that the training ELBO is order-agnostic and thus cannot distinguish effective from ineffective decoding trajectories.

## Input / Output

The method operates purely at inference time on a pre-trained dLLM (e.g., LLaDA-8B-Instruct, LLaDA-1.5-8B, Dream-Instruct-7B) given a text prompt. It maintains M parallel particle trajectories that evolve from a fully masked sequence to a fully decoded one over T discrete denoising steps, with each step unmasking one or more tokens. The final output is the single decoded text sequence from the particle with the highest terminal Path LL, requiring roughly 4× the compute of a single-particle baseline (e.g., 42.2s vs. 11.5s per sample on A800).

## Method

The paper introduces Path LL—the joint log-likelihood of a sequence under a specific unmasking trajectory—as a trajectory-conditioned objective that strongly correlates with downstream accuracy, unlike the order-agnostic ELBO or path entropy. To optimize Path LL at inference time, it proposes POKE (Path-Optimistic K-step likelihood Estimator), a value function that upperbounds the expected future Path LL of a partial decoding state by combining R random rollouts (each with K parallel unmasking steps) with an entropy correction term (1/K × sum of marginal entropies) derived from a Total Correlation bound, thereby counteracting the independence bias of parallel predictions. POKE is integrated into POKE-SMC, a Sequential Monte Carlo search framework that maintains M particles, computes global-guided importance weights as the sum of accumulated Path LL and the POKE future estimate, and performs periodic multinomial resampling at fixed intervals to dynamically reallocate compute toward high-potential trajectories while pruning low-quality ones.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, POKE-SMC (M=4) achieves 47.7% average accuracy across six reasoning benchmarks, a +2.9% absolute gain over the strongest single-particle baseline PC-Sampler (44.8%) and +2.1% over the search-based E-SMC (M=5, 45.6%). On LLaDA-1.5-8B, POKE-SMC reaches 49.0% average (+2.0% over PC-Sampler, +1.7% over E-SMC). Gains are most pronounced on reasoning-intensive tasks: GSM8K improves by 4.9% (79.2→84.1) and Countdown by 4.5% (36.3→40.8) on LLaDA-8B. POKE-SMC uses fewer particles (M=4) than E-SMC and Majority Voting (M=5) while requiring comparable wall-clock time (~42.2s vs ~42.5s per sample, ~4× overhead over single-particle decoding).

## Baselines & Comparisons

POKE-SMC (M=4) outperforms all single-particle heuristics by a wide margin: it beats the strongest single-particle method PC-Sampler by +2.9% (LLaDA-8B) and +2.0% (LLaDA-1.5) in average accuracy, and dominates weaker heuristics (Uniform, Confidence, Entropy, Margin, EB-Sampler, Semi-AR variants) by 7–26 points. Against the closest search-based competitor E-SMC (M=5), POKE-SMC wins by +2.1% on LLaDA-8B and +1.7% on LLaDA-1.5 despite using fewer particles (4 vs. 5); E-SMC's entropy-based guidance yields only +0.8% and +0.3% over the base policy, and is occasionally worse than simple Majority Voting on GSM8K and MATH500. POKE-SMC also substantially outperforms ReMDM (iterative remasking, 2× overhead), which frequently fails to beat basic single-particle baselines (38.0% vs. 47.7% avg on LLaDA-8B). On the performance-efficiency Pareto frontier (Figure 4), POKE-SMC scales monotonically with compute while Majority Voting saturates early and E-SMC exhibits non-monotonic degradation at higher budgets. Under 2-token parallel decoding, the advantage widens to +5.4% and +4.9% over PC-Sampler, and under aggressive 4-token decoding POKE-SMC achieves up to +8.9% average gain over the PC-Sampler base policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Sequential Monte Carlo framework (Doucet et al., 2001) for search-based decoding and the pinning analysis from Anari et al. (2025) to derive a tractable Total Correlation bound for parallel rollouts. It departs from prior dLLM inference-time scaling methods (E-SMC, LookUM) that rely on one-step entropy-based value estimators, and from iterative remasking approaches (ReMDM), by introducing a trajectory-conditioned Path LL objective and the POKE estimator that corrects the independence bias inherent in parallel token prediction. The key conceptual shift is from local, order-agnostic metrics (ELBO, marginal entropy) to a path-level log-likelihood that explicitly captures sequential dependencies of the unmasking trajectory, making it a more faithful proxy for generation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, task-agnostic objective (Path LL) and an efficient value estimator (POKE) for dLLM inference-time optimization, advancing the accuracy-compute Pareto frontier with consistent 2–3% gains over strong baselines across six reasoning tasks and two model families. The Total Correlation correction is a clean theoretical contribution that explains and fixes the well-known underestimation from parallel rollouts, and the framework's model-agnosticism (validated on Dream-Instruct) suggests broad applicability. Open questions include scalability to larger dLLMs (beyond 8B), whether the periodic resampling strategy can be replaced by adaptive rollback mechanisms, and whether the Path LL objective transfers to continuous or multimodal diffusion settings.
