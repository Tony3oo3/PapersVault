## Problem

Existing machine learning approaches for biological trajectory prediction are system-specific, requiring retraining for each new ODE system and failing to generalize across the thousands of heterogeneous biological models that span diverse organisms and dynamical behaviors. Moreover, both deterministic and generative forecasting methods lack biological inductive biases, producing trajectories that violate known macroscopic dynamical regimes (e.g., generating non-oscillatory outputs for cell-cycle systems) and struggling with long-horizon extrapolation where the past-to-future mapping is ambiguous.

## Input / Output

The model takes a univariate historical observation window of L=96 time points from a biological ODE system (dimensions ranging from 1 to 1,000, handled via a channel-independent architecture with instance-wise normalization) along with a biological regime label specifying the pattern type (stable, oscillatory, or monotonic) and, for oscillatory systems, a continuous frequency parameter. It outputs a full probability distribution over T=256 future time points, sampled by integrating a learned vector field via ODE, enabling uncertainty quantification measured by CRPS. Regime labels are randomly masked during training (classifier-free guidance), so the model also supports regime-agnostic inference by falling back to a simple last-observation Gaussian prior.

## Method

RegimeFlow replaces the standard isotropic Gaussian source distribution in conditional flow matching with a regime-aware prior constructed via Bayesian linear regression: regime-specific basis functions (exponential decay for stable, Fourier harmonics for oscillatory, polynomials for monotonic) are fitted to the observed history, and the predictive mean is used to initialize the flow, with the covariance regularized to an isotropic form for numerical stability. The vector field is parameterized by a Selective State Space Model (Mamba) backbone with Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) that modulates each layer's scale and shift from a global conditioning vector encoding the regime pattern, oscillatory frequency (via Fourier features), and flow time. A theoretical result (Propositions 3.1–3.2) shows that under an explicit SNR condition, this initialization strictly reduces the expected Wasserstein-2 transport cost relative to the uninformative Gaussian, shortening the optimal transport path and simplifying the learning problem. Empirically, the regime-aware prior enables near-optimal accuracy with a single function evaluation (NFE=1), yielding 9.3× faster inference than TSFlow while achieving 31% MAE reduction and 17% CRPS improvement over the strongest baseline across 1,050 unseen biological systems.

## Results

On the SysBio-Traj benchmark (1,050 ODE-based biological systems, L=96 observed → T=256 predicted), RegimeFlow† achieves an overall MSE of 0.012 and MAE of 0.044, representing 25% and 31% reductions over the strongest baseline TSFlow† (MSE 0.016, MAE ~0.063). For uncertainty quantification, it attains an overall CRPS of 0.039, a 17% improvement over TSFlow† (0.047), with the largest gains on Stable (0.023 vs 0.034) and Monotonic (0.028 vs 0.037) regimes. Inference requires only 1 function evaluation (7.7 ms/sample), making it 9.3× faster than TSFlow† (16 NFE, 71.9 ms/sample).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegimeFlow† outperforms all 15 baselines across every regime subset and the overall benchmark. Against the strongest probabilistic baseline TSFlow†, it reduces MSE by 25%, MAE by 31%, and CRPS by 17% overall, with per-regime MSE gains ranging from 14% (Oscillatory) to 32% (Monotonic). Diffusion-based baselines (CSDI†, TSDiff†) are substantially worse, with CRPS 2–3.5× higher than RegimeFlow†, and require 50–100 NFE versus RegimeFlow's single step. Among point-forecasting models, NSformer is the strongest (overall MSE 0.100) but remains 8× worse than RegimeFlow†, while Transformer-based methods (iTransformer, PatchTST, TimeXer) and SSM-based methods (SMamba, BiMamba4TS) all fall in the 0.10–0.18 MSE range. Zero-shot time-series foundation models (Chronos, TimesFM, Sundial, TimeMoE; 128–200M params) achieve overall MSE of 0.097–0.120, roughly 8–10× worse than RegimeFlow† (0.012, 1.8M params), indicating that generic pretraining does not capture biological regime structure. Even without regime conditioning, RegimeFlow still beats TSFlow by 12% MSE and 11% MAE, attributing the residual advantage to the SSM backbone's input-dependent dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022; Tong et al., 2023) by replacing the standard isotropic Gaussian source distribution with a regime-aware prior constructed via Bayesian linear regression over biologically-motivated basis functions (exponential, Fourier, polynomial). It departs from prior probabilistic forecasting methods (TSFlow, CSDI, TSDiff) that rely on uninformative or dataset-specific priors, and from deterministic point-forecasting architectures (Transformers, Mamba-based SSMs) that lack uncertainty quantification. The Mamba/SSM backbone (Gu & Dao, 2024) is repurposed as a linear-complexity sequence encoder modulated by adaptive conditioning (AdaLN), while the overall framework is positioned as a unified cross-system predictor rather than a per-system model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SysBio-Traj benchmark of 1,050 heterogeneous ODE-based biological systems provides a much-needed standardized testbed for evaluating cross-system generalization in systems biology, a setting where prior work has been largely system-specific. The regime-aware prior concept—encoding macroscopic dynamical behavior (oscillatory, monotonic, stable) into the generative source distribution—offers a transferable inductive bias that could extend beyond biology to any domain where trajectories exhibit known qualitative patterns. Key limitations include the channel-independent formulation (no topology-aware relational modeling), restricted handling of parameter-dependent regime transitions and chaotic dynamics, and the current scope to deterministic ODE systems; future work toward stochastic, spatio-temporal, and multi-modal settings is explicitly flagged.
