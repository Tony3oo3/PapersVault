## Problem

Score-based diffusion models trained on noisy data learn to reproduce the corruption in their generated samples, and existing remedies either require modifying the training procedure or do not efficiently produce new clean samples. This paper asks whether a purely inference-time modification can suppress noise in generated images without any retraining, by exploiting the geometric structure of the data manifold.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261017/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained score network (e.g., an EDM-style U-Net trained on noisy data) and a single Gaussian noise sample as its only inputs, requiring no clean data or knowledge of the corruption model. It outputs clean image samples at the same resolution as the training data (e.g., 64×64 grayscale, 256×256 grayscale for Cryo-EM, or 256×256 RGB for FFHQ/AFHQv2/ImageNet). The computational cost is at most 2× standard inference due to a second score network evaluation for the finite-difference derivative, though empirically applying the extended score only in the latter half of inference steps reduces this to ~1.5×.

## Method

The core contribution is the 'extended score' H₀, defined as the γ→0 limit of (1+γ)S(p*g_γ) + γ(d/dγ)S(p*g_γ), which coincides with the standard score for smooth densities but remains well-defined for Dirac deltas (yielding −x, analogous to a variance-1 Gaussian score). Under the manifold hypothesis, this produces a soft-thresholding effect: small off-manifold variations (noise) are driven to zero while large on-manifold variations (meaningful data structure) are preserved. At inference, the standard ODE update is replaced by one using the extended score, where the γ-derivative is approximated by a finite difference (a second network call at σ+Δσ), and a correction factor m(t) ensures geometric convergence to Dirac points matching standard score behavior. Three hyperparameters (a, b, p) control the effective threshold and the relative convergence rates of σ and γ, acting as regularization knobs for the denoising strength.

## Results

The primary quantitative metric is FID (computed against the corresponding clean dataset from 10,000 generated images). MAD's extended score inference achieves substantial FID reductions over standard score inference across all tested corruptions: on CIFAR-10 with Gaussian noise, FID drops from 147.47 to 77.15; on FFHQ with Gaussian noise, from 134.42 to 74.52; and on FFHQ with pixel removal, from 387.87 to 104.00. Notably, standard score inference FIDs closely match the training-data FIDs (e.g., 147.47 vs. 136.87 for CIFAR-10), confirming that standard diffusion models simply reproduce the corruption. The method also generalizes to non-Gaussian corruptions (shot/Poisson noise, pixel removal) and real Cryo-EM data (EMPIAR-11618), where generated shapes match known ground-truth structures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between standard score inference and MAD's extended score inference, both using the same pretrained network. Standard score inference consistently reproduces the training corruption, with generated FIDs matching (or slightly exceeding) the noisy training-data FIDs across all six corruption settings. MAD's extended score inference reduces FID by 40–73% relative to standard inference, with the largest absolute gains on heavily corrupted data (e.g., FFHQ pixel removal: 387.87 → 104.00, a 73% reduction). The paper also demonstrates that the denoising effect cannot be attributed to truncated sampling, as intermediate inference paths (Figure 18) show qualitatively different trajectories. While the paper does not run head-to-head comparisons with training-time denoising methods (e.g., Ambient Diffusion [Daras et al., 2023] or stochastic forward-backward deconvolution [Lu et al., 2025]), it positions MAD as complementary: it requires no modification to the training procedure and can leverage existing pretrained score networks, whereas those methods require adapted training losses or a fraction of clean data.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAD builds directly on the EDM framework of Karras et al. (2022), reusing their ODE formulation, time schedule, and pretrained score networks, but modifies only the inference procedure rather than training. It departs from prior noisy-training approaches (Daras et al. 2023, Lu et al. 2025) that require altering the training loss or reserving clean data, and from inverse-problem methods like DPS (Chung et al. 2023) that denoise a single given image rather than generating new samples from a learned distribution. The manifold hypothesis has been used to analyze diffusion model convergence (Bortoli 2022; Chen et al. 2023; Tang & Yang 2024) and to decompose scores into tangential/normal components (Chen et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2026), but MAD is the first to exploit this geometry via a formally defined 'extended score' that acts as a soft threshold on off-manifold variance during inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is strong: MAD requires no retraining, works with existing pretrained EDM networks, and adds at most 50–100% inference cost, yet achieves near-halved FID on CIFAR-10 and FFHQ under Gaussian noise and substantial gains under shot noise and pixel removal. The Cryo-EM proof-of-concept (EMPIAR-11618) demonstrates that the method can recover biologically meaningful structures from extremely noisy real data without any domain-specific prior. Key open questions include automatic selection of the hyperparameters (a, b, p) that control the soft-thresholding level, extension to VP-SDE and time-unconditioned models, and rigorous comparison against training-time denoising baselines (Ambient Diffusion, stochastic forward-backward deconvolution) which the paper acknowledges but does not benchmark against.
