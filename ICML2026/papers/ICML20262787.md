## Problem

In multi-expert Learning to Defer, adding more experts paradoxically degrades the base classifier's accuracy (underfitting) even in the standard cost-free setting, contradicting the expectation that a larger expert pool should only help. The paper identifies this as stemming from an expert aggregation term that scales as O(J) and flattens the effective label distribution—a mechanism fundamentally different from the cost-induced label smoothing in single-expert L2D, rendering existing underfitting remedies inapplicable.

## Input / Output

The system consumes an RGB image (processed by a CNN backbone such as WideResNet, MobileNet-v2, or ResNet-18) together with predictions from J experts, and produces a (K+J)-dimensional score vector. The prediction link maps this to either a class label or a deferral to a specific expert. Beyond the routing decision, the method natively recovers calibrated class probabilities (via softmax or sigmoid of the first K scores) and the optimal expert's accuracy (via the sigmoid of the top expert score under the OvA variant), enabling uncertainty quantification without post-processing.

## Method

PiCCE (Pick the Confident and Correct Expert) modifies the standard multi-expert surrogate loss by restricting the expert-selection term to only those experts whose prediction matches the ground-truth label, then selecting the most confident among them. This single change causes the multiplicative correctness indicator to vanish naturally, yielding a continuous loss amenable to gradient-based optimization. The key theoretical insight is that the O(J) expert aggregation term in the dummy distribution is compressed to Pr(∪M_j=Y|X=x)=O(1), eliminating the label-flattening that drives underfitting. The paper proves continuity, statistical consistency (recovering the Bayes-optimal deferral rule), and exact recovery of class probabilities and expert accuracies under both cross-entropy and one-vs-all base losses, validated on synthetic and real-world expert benchmarks.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (Animal Expert, 20 experts), PiCCE-CE reduces system error from 21.13% (vanilla CE) to 18.11%, and PiCCE-OvA from 22.91% to 18.63%. On ImageNet (Dog Expert, 20 experts), the gains are even larger: PiCCE-CE drops error from 48.00% to 41.32%, and PiCCE-OvA from 66.56% to 42.10%. On real-world expert datasets, PiCCE-OvA achieves the best system error on MiceBone (13.03% vs. 16.72% for vanilla OvA at 8 experts) and Chaoyang (1.02% vs. 2.48% for vanilla OvA at 8 experts), while also maintaining higher coverage across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PiCCE against the vanilla multi-expert CE and OvA surrogate losses from Verma et al. (2023), which are the standard consistent surrogate formulations for multi-expert L2D. PiCCE wins across all four expert patterns (disjoint and overlapped, on both CIFAR-100 and ImageNet) and both real-world datasets (MiceBone, Chaoyang), with no setting where the vanilla methods outperform PiCCE. The advantage grows with the number of experts: on ImageNet Dog Expert, vanilla OvA degrades from 45.77% error (4 experts) to 66.56% (20 experts), while PiCCE-OvA stays stable around 42.1–42.3%. On the real-world MiceBone dataset with 8 experts, PiCCE-OvA achieves 13.03% error versus 16.72% for both vanilla CE and OvA, a 3.7-point improvement. Coverage is also consistently higher under PiCCE, indicating the system defers less while still achieving lower error, confirming that the classifier is better calibrated rather than simply abstaining more.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-expert Learning to Defer (L2D) framework established by Verma et al. (2023) and Mao et al. (2024a), which generalized single-expert surrogate losses (Mozannar & Sontag, 2020; Verma & Nalisnick, 2022) to handle multiple experts. It departs from the single-expert underfitting literature (Narasimhan et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2024) by showing that the root cause of underfitting in the multi-expert setting is fundamentally different: an inherent expert aggregation term that scales as O(J) rather than a cost-induced label-smoothing artifact. The proposed PiCCE loss builds on the idea of using intermediate model predictions (Liu et al., 2024) but augments it with ground-truth correctness information to achieve both continuity and underfitting resistance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that expanding the expert pool can degrade system performance challenges a core design assumption in multi-expert L2D and has direct practical implications for systems that pool domain specialists (e.g., medical diagnosis with multiple pathologists). The theoretical guarantees—statistical consistency, class probability recovery, and expert accuracy estimation under a mild information-advantage condition—provide a principled foundation for the method. Open questions include characterizing the necessary and sufficient conditions for the information-advantage assumption (Condition 1), extending guarantees to settings where the optimal expert is not unique, and investigating whether the PiCCE principle generalizes to other multi-agent deferral or routing problems beyond L2D.
