## Problem

Existing generative dataset distillation methods rely on diffusion models, which suffer from two fundamental limitations: (i) their sequential denoising process makes direct prototype alignment unreliable because intermediate latents mix signal with noise, forcing indirect surrogate objectives; and (ii) their generation is decoupled from task-relevant supervision, causing synthesized samples to drift from the target distribution. Meanwhile, coreset selection preserves representativeness but cannot generate new variations beyond the source data. The paper asks how to combine the representativeness of prototype-based selection with the diversity of generative synthesis in a way that is both directly controllable and task-aligned.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an original labeled training set (e.g., ImageNet-1K, ImageWoof, ImageNette at 256×256 resolution), a frozen pre-trained VAR-d30 model with a VQ-VAE codebook of 4096 entries (32-dim embeddings) across 10 autoregressive stages, a pre-trained ResNet-50 for prototype feature extraction, and a pre-trained teacher classifier for filtering. It produces a compact surrogate dataset of IPC synthetic 256×256 RGB images per class (IPC ∈ {10, 50, 100}), each carrying its original class label. Internally, the pipeline first generates a 5×IPC candidate pool per class (up to 1000 images), then distills it down to the final IPC budget via teacher-confidence filtering and coreset re-selection.

## Method

ProtoVAR replaces the diffusion backbone with Visual Autoregressive (VAR) modeling, whose coarse-to-fine next-scale prediction keeps intermediate representations semantically interpretable, making prototype alignment well-posed. For each class, a multi-prototype feature bank is constructed via medoid selection followed by farthest-first traversal, with ResNet-50 features extracted at each VAR stage resolution to form scale-aligned descriptors. During sampling, at intermediate stages (Gaussian-scheduled over ρ_s ∈ [0.2, 0.8]), the model computes an expected semantic embedding from the current logits, tentatively decodes it through the VQ-VAE proxy, and measures cosine similarity against the stage-specific prototype; this similarity is multiplied by a per-token alignment score (cosine between the expected embedding and each vocabulary token's embedding) to produce a direction-aware logit correction that selectively promotes prototype-consistent tokens while preserving diversity for neutral ones. A target-guided filtering stage then scores the 5× candidate pool with a frozen teacher classifier, retains the top-K confidence samples, and applies the coreset criterion again to select the final IPC images, ensuring task alignment without any fine-tuning of the generative model.

## Results

On ImageWoof (IPC=10, ResNet-18), ProtoVAR achieves 47.2% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baseline CaO2 (45.6%) by 1.6% and Minimax (40.1%) by 7.1%. On ImageNet-1K (IPC=10, ResNet-18), ProtoVAR reaches 46.8%, surpassing CaO2 (46.1%) and MGD3 (45.6%). At IPC=100 on ImageNet-1K with ResNet-101, ProtoVAR attains 69.6% versus MGD3's 68.8% and CaO2's 68.5%. The paper reports an average accuracy improvement of +2.3% on ImageWoof and +2.5% on ImageNet over prior generative distillation methods, while being ~70× faster in inference than DiT-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProtoVAR consistently outperforms all six compared distillation methods (SRe2L, D3HR, D4M, Minimax, CaO2, MGD3) across every dataset, IPC setting, and evaluation architecture. The closest competitor is CaO2, which ProtoVAR surpasses by 1.6% on ImageWoof IPC=10 (ResNet-18), 0.7% on ImageNet-1K IPC=10 (ResNet-18), and 1.1% on ImageNet-1K IPC=100 (ResNet-101). MGD3 is the second-strongest baseline, trailing ProtoVAR by 4.3% on ImageWoof IPC=10 and 0.8% on ImageNet-1K IPC=100. Minimax and D3HR, both diffusion-based, are outperformed by 6–7% on ImageWoof IPC=10 and by 2–3% on ImageNet-1K IPC=10. The optimization-based baselines SRe2L and D4M are substantially weaker (e.g., SRe2L scores only 20.2% on ImageWoof IPC=10 vs. ProtoVAR's 47.2%). Notably, even the unguided VAR baseline (38.8% on ImageWoof IPC=10) is competitive with DiT (38.6%), confirming that the prototype-guided refinement and target-guided filtering contribute the majority of the gains rather than the backbone choice alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: prototype-based coreset selection (which ensures representativeness but lacks diversity) and generative dataset distillation (which provides diversity but suffers from indirect matching and target-agnostic generation). It departs from the dominant diffusion-based generative distillation paradigm (Minimax, D4M, CaO2, MGD3, D3HR) by replacing the diffusion backbone with Visual Autoregressive (VAR) modeling, arguing that VAR's coarse-to-fine next-scale prediction makes prototype alignment well-posed where diffusion's noise-dominated intermediate states make it fundamentally ill-defined. The prototype-guided logit refinement mechanism, with its direction-aware correction and Gaussian stage scheduling, is a new contribution specific to AR generation that has no direct analogue in prior diffusion-based distillation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 70× inference speedup of VAR over DiT unlocks a practical capability—large-scale candidate pool generation followed by teacher-guided filtering—that is computationally prohibitive for diffusion models, making ProtoVAR's pipeline structurally different rather than merely faster. The theoretical analysis showing that uniform prototype biasing is nullified by softmax normalization (Proposition 1) and that the expected-embedding relaxation has vanishing approximation error as token distributions sharpen provides a principled foundation for AR-based guidance. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, whether the reliance on a pre-trained VAR model and external teacher classifier limits deployment in resource-constrained settings, and whether the performance gap over diffusion narrows as diffusion models improve in speed.
