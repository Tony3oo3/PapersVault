## Problem

Prior transform-based outlier mitigations for LLM quantization (e.g., Hadamard rotations) are fixed and data-agnostic, yet their optimality for quantization has remained unclear. The paper asks: if the Hadamard transform does not adapt to weight or activation statistics, in what sense can it be considered optimal? This work derives closed-form, data-aware optimal linear blockwise transforms for joint weight-activation quantization under standard RTN AbsMax-scaled block quantizers, resolving this question for both floating-point and integer formats.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LLM linear-layer weights and a small set of calibration activations (e.g., 32 sequences of length 2048) to compute per-block second moments, from which it produces a distinct d×d WUSH transform matrix for each quantization group (where d equals the group size, a power of 2, e.g., 32 for MXFP4). At inference, the activation-side transform is applied online per token via a fused GPU kernel, while the weight-side transform is absorbed into pre-quantized 4-bit weights with groupwise 8-bit scales. The forward pass then reduces to a standard FP4/INT4 block matmul, yielding up to 5.8× per-layer throughput over BF16.

## Method

WUSH derives the optimal block-diagonal transform by reformulating the two-sided weight-activation quantization loss into a one-sided problem via the constraint T_W = T_X^{-T}, then parameterizing the unknown transform through the SVD of the cross-covariance W'^T X' (where W', X' are Cholesky factors of the weight and activation second moments). The resulting closed-form construction T = H S^{-1/2} U^T W'^T combines a fixed normalized Hadamard backbone H with a data-dependent second-moment scaling S^{-1/2} and rotation U^T, yielding a non-orthogonal transform that is provably optimal for FP block quantizers and within a d^{o(1)} factor for INT quantizers under tail-bounded distributions. The Hadamard component equalizes per-channel RMS across the block (mapping the transformed second moment to HSH^⊤ with constant diagonal), while the S^{-1/2} component counteracts the anisotropy in the singular-value spectrum. For GPTQ integration, the transform is interleaved with GPTQ's iterative error propagation: each block's WUSH transform is computed from the current (partially updated) weight second moment, and the Hessian is transformed accordingly before applying the standard intra-block GPTQ subroutine. A fused CUTLASS-based GPU kernel handles the per-block online activation transform and quantization with negligible overhead relative to the Hadamard baseline.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct W4A4 quantization, WUSH achieves 74.35 average accuracy (94.20% recovery) with MXFP4-GPTQ, outperforming the strongest Hadamard-based baseline MR-GPTQ (73.65) by +0.7 points, and 73.21 with MXFP4-RTN versus 70.45 for RTN-H (+2.8 points). For NVFP4, GPTQ-WUSH reaches 76.10 average (96.40% recovery), slightly ahead of GPTQ-H/MR-GPTQ (75.84). On Qwen3-8B Platinum Benchmarks, WUSH improves MXFP4 by up to +1.3 (RTN) and +0.7 (GPTQ) average points over the Hadamard baseline, while per-layer FP4 MatMul throughput reaches up to 5.8× over BF16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WUSH consistently outperforms all four baselines on the primary MXFP4 W4A4 setting. Against MR-GPTQ (the strongest Hadamard-based baseline), WUSH gains +2.8 average points with RTN (73.21 vs. 70.45) and +0.7 with GPTQ (74.35 vs. 73.65) on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. SmoothQuant, QuaRot, and SpinQuant all trail WUSH by 2–10 points on MXFP4; QuaRot in particular degrades severely (62.90 average, 79.70% recovery) due to its full-layer rotation being incompatible with blockwise FP4 quantization. On NVFP4, the gains are smaller (GPTQ-WUSH 76.10 vs. GPTQ-H 75.84, +0.26), largely because NVFP4's smaller group size (16 vs. 32) already mitigates outliers more effectively, and the Hadamard component can be slightly harmful under NVFP4's top-element preservation property. The paper also reports that WUSH narrows the NVFP4–MXFP4 accuracy gap to 0.36 points on Qwen3-14B, suggesting the data-aware transform makes the less-precise MXFP format competitive with NVFP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the blockwise transform framework for LLM quantization established by MR-GPTQ (Egiazarian et al., 2026), which showed that blockwise Hadamard rotations are the best-performing data-agnostic transforms among a large set of candidates. WUSH departs from the fixed-transform paradigm by deriving a closed-form, data-aware optimal transform that combines the Hadamard backbone with a second-moment-dependent non-orthogonal component (via SVD of the cross-covariance between weight and activation statistics). Unlike learned-transform approaches such as SpinQuant and FlatQuant that require iterative gradient-based optimization on calibration data, WUSH is computed in closed form from second-order statistics, and its GPTQ integration reuses the same Hessian information that GPTQ already computes, adding negligible overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable near-optimality guarantee for a blockwise linear transform under standard RTN/AbsMax quantizers, and in doing so explains *why* Hadamard rotations have been empirically effective: the Hadamard matrix is the sole data-agnostic ingredient in the optimal solution. Practically, WUSH narrows the MXFP4–NVFP4 accuracy gap to under 0.4 points on Qwen3-14B while maintaining kernel throughput within 1.3% of the fixed-Hadamard baseline, making MXFP4 a viable deployment target. Open questions include whether the stochastic-unbiased quantization assumption used in the proof fully captures deterministic RTN behavior (especially for INT formats where optimality is only asymptotic), and whether the per-block adaptivity can be further exploited for sub-4-bit regimes or non-Gaussian activation distributions.
