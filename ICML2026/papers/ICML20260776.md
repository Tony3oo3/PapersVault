## Problem

Extending conformal prediction to vector-valued outputs (multi-target regression, multi-class classification) requires a principled multivariate notion of typicality, since scalar aggregation of conformity scores discards joint dependence structure. Existing multivariate approaches based on optimal transport are computationally demanding in high dimensions and structurally sensitive to outliers, as the fitted transport map is constrained by the full empirical support. The paper asks whether transport-free center-outward orderings—specifically geometric quantiles and spatial ranks from convex M-estimation—can provide robust, scalable alternatives for multivariate conformal prediction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a covariate vector x, a base predictor (gradient-boosted trees) producing either class probabilities p̂(x) ∈ Δ^{K−1} or multi-target predictions f̂(x) ∈ ℝ^d, and a held-out calibration set. Vector-valued scores are formed as class-wise residuals |p̂_k(x) − 1{y=k}| for classification or residual vectors y − f̂(x) for regression. The output is a prediction set (classification) or an arbitrary-shaped prediction region (regression) whose geometry is induced by learned quantile contours or rank level sets, together with a split-conformal threshold; regions are not restricted to boxes, ellipsoids, or other parametric shapes.

## Method

Two conformal constructions are proposed. GCQR learns a conditional geometric quantile map Q_θ(x,u) = ∇_u ψ_θ(x,u) via a partially input-convex neural network (PICNN), where the convex potential ψ_θ enforces cyclical monotonicity and guarantees non-crossing quantile contours; a signed-distance score to the base region boundary is then conformalized via split-CP. GRCP instead uses the radial geometric rank ‖R̂(x, s(x,y))‖ ∈ [0,1] as a scalar nonconformity measure, with the conditional rank estimated either by kernel-weighted local averaging (rank-first) or by amortized inversion of the learned PICNN quantile map (quantile-first, trained with cycle-consistency and synthetic supervision losses). The key distinction from OT-based methods is that geometric ranks and quantiles arise from convex M-estimation, giving bounded influence per outlier (‖ΔR̂‖ ≤ 2w_j/W) and O(n) per-evaluation cost, while the PICNN parameterization—borrowed from neural optimal transport but applied to the Chaudhuri spatial objective—ensures the learned quantile map is injective on the unit ball, producing strictly nested, arc-connected prediction regions.

## Results

On multi-class classification, GRCP achieves the smallest average prediction-set size among all methods with near-nominal (≥0.9) marginal coverage across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, EMNIST, and Letter; on EMNIST (K=47) GRCP yields a set size of 1.37 versus 6.84 for OT-CP, and on Letter (K=26) OT-CP exceeds 15 while GRCP stays near 1.2. On multi-target regression, GRCP attains the lowest normalized log-volume on every benchmark, reaching −0.736 on RF2 and 0.508 on SCM1D, compared to 1.792 for VSPS and 2.179 for OT-CP on SCM20D. Computationally, GRCP is ~1.5× faster than OT-CP and ~3.5× faster than OT-CP+ at d=50, with the gap widening to ~2× and ~4.5× at n=10⁴ calibration points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against scalar classification baselines, GRCP dominates LAC (Sadinle et al., 2019) on coverage—LAC exhibits systematic under-coverage while GRCP stays within 1 pp of nominal—and matches or beats RAPS (Angelopoulos et al., 2021) on set size, though RAPS achieves slightly higher WSC at the cost of substantially larger prediction sets. Against OT-based conformal methods (Thurin et al., 2025; Klein et al., 2025), GRCP wins decisively on efficiency: on EMNIST the average set size is 1.37 vs. 6.84, and on Letter it is ~1.2 vs. >15, with the gap growing as class count increases (at d=26 on Letter, OT-CP sets are ~11× larger). On regression, GRCP outperforms VSPS (Luo & Zhou, 2025) on normalized log-volume across all five benchmarks (e.g., 0.508 vs. 1.792 on SCM1D) and beats OT-CP/OT-CP+ by wide margins on volume (0.963 vs. 2.179 on SCM20D). MultiDimSPCI (Xu et al., 2024) and CopulaCP (Messoudi et al., 2021) are the closest runners-up on RF1, RF2, and SGEMM where they nearly match GRCP's volume, but GRCP pulls ahead on SCM1D and SCM20D. OT-CP+ achieves the highest WSC on RF1/RF2 (0.767 vs. GRCP's 0.707) but at the cost of substantially larger regions, illustrating the efficiency–conditional-validity trade-off the paper highlights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a transport-free alternative to the recent wave of optimal-transport-based multivariate conformal prediction methods (Thurin et al. 2025; Klein et al. 2025; Kondratyev et al. 2025), replacing OT center-outward ranks with classical spatial ranks and geometric quantiles from robust statistics (Chaudhuri 1996). The quantile-first estimator (GCQR) borrows the convex-potential/PICNN parameterization from neural optimal transport (Amos et al. 2017; Makkuva et al. 2020) but applies it to the Chaudhuri spatial objective rather than a Monge transport map, which the authors note is a first. The conformal calibration layer is standard split-CP (Vovk et al. 2005; Romano et al. 2019), extended from univariate CQR to multivariate geometric quantile regions. The rank-first estimator (GRCP) is a simpler kernel-weighted nonparametric approach that avoids neural training entirely, trading scalability in the covariate dimension for zero model-fitting overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a drop-in replacement for OT-based multivariate conformal scores that is 1.5–3.5× faster, more robust to outliers (bounded influence via convex M-estimation), and produces tighter prediction regions—particularly at higher score dimensions where OT assignment problems become ill-conditioned. Key open questions include: (i) the lack of affine equivariance of geometric quantiles, which necessitates a transformation-retransformation step that adds modeling complexity; (ii) the curse of dimensionality in the rank-first kernel estimator when covariate dimension p is large; and (iii) the absence of conditional-coverage or efficiency theory for the geometric quantile estimators themselves. The paper also reveals that at very high class cardinality (K=100), multivariate ranking loses its edge to simple scalar conformity, suggesting a fundamental statistical limit on estimating joint geometry in sparse label spaces.
