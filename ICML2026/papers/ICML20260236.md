## Problem

Existing performative RL algorithms (MDRR, RPO-FS) achieve performative stability—policies that remain unchanged under their own induced distribution shifts—but do not converge to performatively optimal policies that maximize expected return under self-induced dynamics. No algorithm existed that provably converges to the performatively optimal policy, leaving a fundamental gap between stability and optimality in performative RL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260236/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PePG takes a Performative MDP (PeMDP) defined by discrete state and action spaces, policy-dependent transition and reward functions, a discount factor γ, and an initial state distribution ρ. The key structural difference from standard MDPs is that both the transition kernel P_π and reward r_π are functions of the deployed policy π, creating a self-referential feedback loop. The algorithm outputs policy parameters θ (under softmax parameterization) that define a performatively optimal policy, with convergence to an (ε + 1/(1−γ))-ball around optimality. The gradient computation additionally requires access to ∇_θ r_{π_θ}(s,a) and ∇_θ log P_{π_θ}(s'|s,a), which are available in closed form for exponential family transitions or learned via differentiable neural network surrogates.

## Method

PePG derives a Performative Policy Gradient Theorem showing that the gradient of the performative value function decomposes into three terms: the classical advantage-weighted log-policy gradient, a novel advantage-weighted log-transition gradient capturing environment shift, and a novel reward gradient term. The algorithm performs gradient ascent on the performative value function using these three components, with advantage estimates from a fitted value network and trajectory rollouts. Convergence is established through a three-step analysis: (1) a Performative Gradient Domination Lemma bounding suboptimality by the gradient norm plus a performative shift term, (2) proof that the performative value function is O(|A|/(1−γ)²)-smooth under Lipschitz and smoothness assumptions on transitions and rewards, and (3) iterative application of gradient domination for smooth objectives. For exponential family PeMDPs with softmax policies, PePG achieves O(|S||A|²/(ε²(1−γ)³)) iteration complexity, improving over stability-seeking baselines by O(|S|²/ε²(1−γ)³) in sample complexity and reducing the required entropy regularization from O(|S|/(1−γ)⁴) to O(1/log|A|).

## Results

On the GridWorld performative RL benchmark (1000 iterations, 20 seeds), PePG achieves an expected average return of approximately 18 (unregularised) and 11 (entropy-regularised), substantially outperforming both stability-seeking baselines. Theoretically, PePG converges to an (ε + 1/(1−γ))-ball around a performatively optimal policy in O(|S||A|²/(ε²(1−γ)³)) iterations for exponential family PeMDPs, improving over the O(|A|²|S|³/(ε⁴(1−γ)⁶λ²)·ln(#iter)) sample complexity of existing methods. PePG also incurs ~1.25–2.5× less computational time per iteration compared to the baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PePG is compared against two state-of-the-art stability-seeking algorithms: MDRR (Mixed Delayed Repeated Retraining, Rank et al. 2024) and RPO-FS (Mandal et al. 2023). On the GridWorld benchmark, PePG (unregularised) reaches an expected average return of ~18, while MDRR plateaus at an intermediate level and RPO-FS remains near 0, demonstrating that stability-seeking methods get trapped at suboptimal equilibria. RPO-FS achieves the highest stability (occupancy differences below 10⁻¹⁴) but at the cost of negligible performance, while MDRR shows periodic stabilization events (drops to ~10⁻¹⁴ every 200–300 iterations) without corresponding return gains. Theoretically, PePG achieves a sub-optimality gap of O(1/(1−γ)) versus O(1/(1−γ)⁶) for RPO-FS and O(1/(1−γ)⁸) for other stability-seeking methods, and requires a regulariser of only O(1/log|A|) compared to O(|S|/(1−γ)⁴) for the baselines. PePG also runs ~1.25–2.5× faster per iteration than both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classical policy gradient theory (performance difference lemma, policy gradient theorem) to the performative RL setting, where the environment's transition and reward functions depend on the deployed policy. It builds directly on the performative RL framework of Mandal et al. (2023) and Rank et al. (2024), which introduced PeMDPs and solution concepts (stability and optimality) but only provided algorithms achieving performative *stability* via repeated retraining. The key departure is that PePG is the first PG algorithm designed to converge to performatively *optimal* policies, adding two novel gradient terms (reward gradient and log-transition gradient) to the classical policy gradient to account for environment shifts induced by the policy itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clean theoretical and empirical separation between stability-seeking and optimality-seeking objectives in performative RL, showing that algorithms achieving near-perfect stability (occupancy differences < 10⁻¹⁴) can remain at near-zero performance while PePG achieves significantly higher returns. This challenges the implicit assumption in prior performative RL work that stability is the primary deployment goal. Key limitations include the requirement for smooth (Lipschitz and second-order smooth) rewards and transitions, restriction to tabular MDPs, and evaluation on a single gridworld benchmark. Open questions include extending to non-smooth PeMDPs (e.g., Bernoulli rewards, piecewise transitions), continuous state-action spaces with variance reduction, and constructing broader performative RL testbeds.
