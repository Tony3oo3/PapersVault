## Problem

The dominant backbone for learning-based MILP solving models instances as variable-constraint bipartite graphs and uses GNN message passing, but this local-oriented mechanism limits the receptive field to roughly 4 hops and suffers from over-smoothing and over-squashing when stacking layers. As a result, existing GNN backbones cannot effectively capture the long-range dependencies between distant variables and constraints that are critical for solving general combinatorial optimization problems.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw variable features (objective coefficients, bounds, type, degree, position embeddings), constraint features (RHS, type, degree), and the sparse coefficient matrix A of the MILP instance; for branching tasks it additionally ingests solver-state features such as LP relaxation values, basis status, and reduced costs. The sparse matrix A is not merely an adjacency indicator but is explicitly embedded into cross-attention scores via a learnable coefficient-aware weighting, reducing cross-attention complexity from O(mn) to O(e) where e is the number of nonzeros. Outputs are d-dimensional variable and constraint embeddings that feed task-specific heads: mean-pooled embeddings for instance-level predictions (feasibility, objective value), per-variable embeddings for binary variable prediction, and masked softmax over variable embeddings for branching distributions.

## Method

The proposed dual-attention backbone treats variables and constraints as two heterogeneous element types of equal importance and processes them through two parallel attention channels per layer: (i) intra-type self-attention, where variables attend to all variables and constraints attend to all constraints globally, linearized via a sigmoid kernel trick to achieve O(nd²+md²) time and O((n+m)d) memory; and (ii) inter-type cross-attention, where variables query constraints and vice versa, with attention scores restricted to the sparse support of A and modulated by a learnable coefficient-aware term to preserve structural information at O(ed) cost. Unlike BGNN's sequential variable→constraint→variable propagation, the two channels run in parallel and their outputs are concatenated and fused through an MLP+FFN, avoiding the information bottleneck and over-squashing inherent in the graph-centric pipeline. The architecture stacks T=4 layers with H=2 heads and d=64 dimension, and is trained end-to-end via supervised learning (for prediction tasks) or imitation learning (for branching), with the same backbone serving all three task levels without modification.

## Results

On the four binary variable prediction benchmarks (IP, WA, MIS, CA), the dual-attention backbone achieves the best MCC, M-F1, MSE, and E-Rate on WA (0.8411, 0.9205, 0.0386, 5.32%), MIS (0.6920, 0.8460, 0.1058, 15.29%), and CA (0.3825, 0.6911, 0.1383, 21.86%), and remains competitive on the highly imbalanced IP dataset. Under the prediction-and-search framework, the method yields an average 9.05% improvement in Primal Gap and 20.57% improvement in Primal Integral over BGNN. On the learning-to-branch task, the model achieves higher acc@1 across all four problems (e.g., 57.20% vs. 53.50% on CFL) and produces smaller B&B trees, with particularly large gains on the transfer set (e.g., 8938.5 vs. 10280.5 nodes on MIS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The dual-attention backbone is compared against six baselines: the standard BGNN (Gasse et al., 2019), two concurrent MILP-specific transformers (OPTFM, MAGCN), and three advanced graph architectures adapted to the bipartite setting (Polynormer, GCON, GNN-SSM). On the three balanced datasets (WA, MIS, CA), the proposed method achieves the best score on all four metrics (MCC, M-F1, MSE, E-Rate), with the largest margin over BGNN on WA (MCC 0.8411 vs. 0.7972, E-Rate 5.32% vs. 6.83%) and MIS (MCC 0.6920 vs. 0.6431). Among the stronger baselines, GNN-SSM and GCON are the closest competitors, trailing by 0.039 MCC on WA and 0.003–0.005 MCC on MIS/CA, while OPTFM and Polynormer underperform substantially (e.g., OPTFM MCC drops to 0.5169 on MIS vs. 0.6920 for Ours). On the highly imbalanced IP dataset (9:1 zero-to-one ratio), the method is competitive but not uniformly best: GNN-SSM edges it out on MCC (0.0186 vs. 0.0178) and several baselines report lower E-Rate, though the paper notes E-Rate is less informative under extreme imbalance. Overall, the consistent wins on WA, MIS, and CA across all metrics, combined with superior downstream solving performance (9.05% PG and 20.57% PI improvement over BGNN), establish the dual-attention design as the strongest backbone among all tested architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant graph-centric paradigm for MILP representation learning, where instances are modeled as variable-constraint bipartite graphs processed by sequential message passing (Gasse et al., 2019; Cappart et al., 2023). Instead, it adopts an element-centric view that treats variables and constraints as two parallel sets of heterogeneous elements, connected by a dual-attention mechanism combining intra-type self-attention and inter-type cross-attention. The linearized self-attention component builds on the kernel trick from Polynormer (Deng et al., 2024) to achieve near-linear complexity, while the sparse coefficient-aware cross-attention is a novel design specific to MILP structure. The paper positions itself against the theoretical expressiveness limitations of GNNs for MILP identified by Chen et al. (2023, 2024, 2025), arguing that those works remain trapped within the local message-passing framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The backbone architecture is the foundational component for all learning-based MILP solving, so a more expressive representation directly benefits every downstream task (primal heuristics, branching, cutting planes). The mechanistic analyses—gradient flow showing BGNN cannot effectively use more than 2 layers while the attention model scales to 4+, and attribution showing BGNN influence vanishes beyond 3 hops while attention maintains signal at 7 hops—provide a clear causal explanation for the performance gap rather than just empirical evidence. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly massive instances (hundreds of thousands of variables) without further approximation, whether pre-training or foundation-model paradigms can leverage this backbone across diverse MILP families, and whether the modest inference-time overhead (~2-3× BGNN) becomes prohibitive in time-critical solver integration.
