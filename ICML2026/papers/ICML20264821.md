## Problem

Flow-based generative models capture fine-grained distributional structure but have not been leveraged for representation learning. Existing self-supervised methods (e.g., SimCLR) rely on mutual information maximization, which is greedy and easily saturated by superficial shortcuts (watermarks, colors), failing to extract high-level semantic features. Additionally, Markov blanket identification in graphical models typically requires parametric density assumptions and cannot be amortized over arbitrary target subsets at inference time.

## Input / Output

For Markov blanket learning, the method takes a high-dimensional data matrix (e.g., 500×252 S&P 500 returns, 64-dim lattice variables) and a binary target mask m specifying which variables to predict, producing a sparse gating vector σ_β(m) ∈ [0,1]^d whose non-zero entries identify the Markov blanket. For self-supervised learning, it takes two augmented views (Z₁, Z₂) of the same RGB image (32×32 to 224×224) and produces a low-dimensional latent code f(Z₂) ∈ ℝ^L with L ≪ d, enforced by an information bottleneck. The velocity field network additionally consumes the interpolated state X_t, the encoder output, the raw input Y, and scalar time t as conditioning inputs.

## Method

The core insight is the zero-flow criterion: a rectified flow trained with independent coupling has vanishing velocity at the midpoint (t=0.5) if and only if the source and target distributions are identical (Theorem 3.1). This extends to conditional distributions via a modified rectified flow objective (eq. 6) whose optimal velocity field v_{t=0.5}(z; f(Y'), Y) = 0 certifies the conditional independence X ⊥ Y | f(Y), i.e., sufficiency of the encoding (Theorem 3.4). The practical training loss (eq. 9) jointly minimizes a time-weighted zero-flow penalty (Laplace-weighted around t=0.5) and the standard rectified flow matching loss, plus an ℓ₁ sparsity term on the sigmoid gates. The encoder is amortized: a gating network σ_β(m) takes the target mask as input, enabling a single trained model to answer Markov blanket queries for arbitrary, unseen target subsets at inference time without retraining.

## Results

On graphical model structure recovery (Table 1), the LSTM-based zero-flow encoder achieves near-perfect AUC of 0.98 on Gaussian and truncated-Gaussian models, and 0.97 on nonparanormal models, outperforming Graphical Lasso (0.94/0.88/0.78) and PC-Fisher's Z (0.85/0.83/0.75) across all three distributions. On the shortcut-robustness benchmark (Table 2), the zero-flow encoder maintains linear probing accuracy within ~1% of its clean-dataset performance on watermarked STL-10 (52.21%, +2.41), TinyImageNet (16.60%, +0.50), and ImageNet-1K (7.01%, +0.82), while SimCLR collapses to 10.00%, 0.50%, and 0.10% respectively. Reconstruction MSE remains stable (e.g., 0.0256 on STL-10) compared to SimCLR's degradation (0.0674).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Graphical Lasso and PC-Fisher's Z on graphical model structure recovery, the zero-flow encoder (LSTM variant) achieves substantially higher AUC on non-Gaussian settings: 0.97 vs. 0.78 (GLasso) and 0.75 (PC) on nonparanormal, and 0.98 vs. 0.88 and 0.83 on truncated-Gaussian. On the Gaussian setting, all methods perform well, with the zero-flow encoder at 0.98 vs. 0.94 (GLasso) and 0.85 (PC). Against SimCLR on watermarked image datasets, the zero-flow encoder is dramatically more robust: it retains 52.21% accuracy on STL-10 versus SimCLR's 10.00% (a 60-point drop for SimCLR), and 16.60% vs. 0.50% on TinyImageNet. Against MAE (ViT backbone), the zero-flow encoder is slightly behind on raw accuracy (e.g., 52.21% vs. 56.14% on STL-10) but shows comparable robustness to shortcuts, with both methods maintaining near-clean performance. The key distinction is that SimCLR's contrastive objective saturates on shortcut features, while the zero-flow criterion enforces conditional independence directly, preventing shortcut exploitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the rectified flow framework (Liu et al., ICLR 2023) from a generative modeling tool into a criterion for testing distributional equality and conditional independence. The core insight—that the velocity field of a rectified flow with independent coupling vanishes at t=0.5 if and only if source and target distributions match—is then extended to a conditional setting, yielding a simulation-free loss for sufficient representation learning. Conceptually, the work bridges sufficient dimensionality reduction (Globerson & Tishby; Suzuki & Sugiyama), Markov blanket estimation (Meinshausen & Bühlmann; Banerjee et al.), and self-supervised learning (SimCLR; MAE) under a single flow-inspired criterion, departing from prior approaches that rely on parametric density models, mutual information maximization, or reconstruction objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-flow criterion offers a non-parametric, amortized mechanism for extracting sufficient information that avoids the greediness of mutual information maximization and the parametric assumptions of graphical lasso or PC algorithms. The amortized Markov blanket encoder is particularly notable for enabling inference on arbitrary target features not encountered during training, which is infeasible with per-partition parametric models. Key limitations include the heuristic replacement of the full spatial condition with X_t (lacking rigorous justification), the requirement of an additional velocity field network (extra compute), and inapplicability to discrete variables. Open questions include extending the framework to high-dimensional or discrete targets via latent-space mapping, and whether the antisymmetry property (Theorem 3.2) can yield further practical criteria.
