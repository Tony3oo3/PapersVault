## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning of large vision-language models is highly vulnerable to backdoor attacks that inject triggers into training data, yet existing defenses from image classification and contrastive multimodal learning are ineffective in open-ended generation settings. Defenses specifically targeting instruction-tuning are scarce and assume particular trigger patterns, leaving a gap for a trigger-agnostic defense applicable to free-form multimodal response generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263922/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a poisoned instruction-tuning dataset where each sample pairs an RGB image with a text instruction and a (potentially corrupted) target response. During training, the pretrained VLM scores each response's token-level perplexity to flag suspicious examples; flagged samples have their responses replaced by model-generated alternatives before the gradient step. The output is a fine-tuned autoregressive VLM that produces clean, semantically appropriate text responses even when adversarial triggers are present in the input image or instruction.

## Method

BYORn exploits the observation that backdoored target responses are semantically implausible and thus receive low likelihood under the pretrained VLM. It computes a per-token average negative log-likelihood (log-perplexity) score for every training response and flags the top-p percentile as suspicious. For flagged examples, the method bootstraps replacement responses by sampling from an exponential moving average (EMA) of the current model parameters, then trains on the original responses for clean examples and the bootstrapped responses for flagged ones. The resulting objective is proven to be an empirical estimate of a population risk upper bound over the clean data distribution, derived via the Donsker-Varadhan inequality and Hoeffding's lemma. A poison-aware minibatching strategy groups clean and poisoned samples into separate batches, skipping the expensive generation step for clean batches and reducing training overhead by 2.2× compared to naive random mixing.

## Results

On image captioning (averaged over four backdoor attacks), BYORn reduces attack success rate (ASR) to near-zero (0.0–2.8%) across all four models (LLaVA, Otter, Qwen-VL, InternVL) while maintaining or exceeding the clean SFT CIDEr score in several settings (e.g., LLaVA on Flickr: 62.0 CIDEr vs. 51.4 for SFT). On Spot-the-Difference (CGD), BYORn achieves 134.9 CIDEr with 1.1% ASR for LLaVA, versus 144.9 CIDEr and 100% ASR for undefended SFT. On VQA (ScienceQA), both BYORn and BYORn-F achieve 0.0% ASR, with Qwen3-VL retaining 86.9% accuracy (vs. 89.6% for SFT). Against adaptive attacks that increase poisoned-response plausibility, BYORn still reduces ASR by over 70 percentage points (image-wide) and over 90 percentage points (patch-style).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ONION (Qi et al., 2021) is a unimodal defense that removes outlier words from instructions; it effectively neutralizes textual triggers in DualKey and VL-Trojan (ASR drops to ~0–3%) but is completely ineffective against image-only triggers (BadNets, Blend), where ASR remains at ~91–100%. BYE (Rong et al., 2025) filters training samples with low attention to image tokens, targeting localized visual triggers; it fails on image-wide triggers (Blend, VL-Trojan) with ASR remaining at 69–100%, and on average provides only marginal protection. BYORn outperforms the best baseline defense by 20 pp ASR reduction for LLaVA, 50 pp for Otter, and 40 pp for both Qwen-VL and InternVL. Notably, BYORn not only matches but exceeds the clean SFT CIDEr on LLaVA-Flickr (62.0 vs. 51.4), demonstrating that the bootstrapping objective preserves—and can even improve—clean-task performance while eliminating the backdoor. The filtering-only variant BYORn-F achieves comparable ASR reduction but at a significant CIDEr cost (e.g., 42.4 vs. 62.0 for LLaVA-Flickr), highlighting the advantage of response regeneration over simple sample removal.

## Relation to Prior Work

BYORn repurposes the perplexity-based detection principle from unimodal text defenses (ONION) and applies it to the response side of multimodal instruction tuning, where prior defenses (ONION for textual triggers, BYE for localized image triggers) are either unimodal or assume specific trigger patterns. The bootstrapping mechanism—replacing detected poisoned responses with model-generated alternatives—draws on self-supervised and variational backdoor defenses (Sabolić et al., 2024; 2025) but introduces a new theoretical framing: the training objective is shown to minimize an empirical estimate of a population risk upper bound derived via Donsker-Varadhan and Hoeffding's lemma. Unlike prior VLM backdoor defenses that modify the training pipeline or require external guidance, BYORn operates purely on the response distribution and is agnostic to trigger modality or pattern.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BYORn establishes a practical, trigger-agnostic defense for open-ended multimodal generation that achieves near-complete backdoor resilience (ASR ≤ 1%) across four architectures, four attack types, and three tasks, while preserving or even improving clean-task performance—a trade-off frontier that prior defenses could not achieve. The robustness to adaptive PGD-based attacks that specifically target the perplexity detector suggests the defense does not rely on perfect detection recall. Key open questions include: whether true clean-label attacks (where the target response is fully semantically consistent with the image) can break the perplexity gap, whether the defense extends to backdoors embedded in pretrained weights, and how it scales to longer-form generation where response likelihood becomes harder to interpret.
