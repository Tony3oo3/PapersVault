## Problem

Single-cell RNA-seq enables cellular-resolution phenotyping, but the combinatorial space of treatments, doses, and cell states makes exhaustive experimental mapping infeasible. Existing computational methods either predict population-level conditional distributions without cell-specific counterfactuals, or provide cell-level edits through opaque latent variables without biologically interpretable control. No prior framework simultaneously delivers precise, cell-level counterfactual prediction and interpretable intervention on named biological concepts (e.g., pathway activity, cell type) for single-cell data.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single-cell RNA-seq expression vector (d genes, log-normalized counts), a vector of K biological concept annotations (binary for discrete states like cell type or stimulation, continuous for soft values like pathway-activity scores or drug dosage), and a target concept vector c′ specifying the desired edit. It produces a full counterfactual gene expression profile for that specific cell under c′, along with predicted concept values and disentangled latent representations. A binary mask handles missing concept annotations per sample, and the decoder skip connections ensure robust conditioning even when concept labels are noisy or partially absent.

## Method

scCBGM is a concept-bottleneck VAE whose encoder decomposes a latent code z into interpretable concept predictions ĉ (via a standard scalar-output concept head) and residual unknown factors u (via a separate network). Two architectural modifications distinguish it from prior CBGMs: (i) decoder skip connections that concatenate ĉ into every hidden layer, enforcing persistent concept conditioning and improving robustness to noisy annotations, and (ii) a cross-covariance penalty (squared Frobenius norm of the empirical cross-covariance between ĉ and u) that promotes disentanglement without requiring equal embedding dimensions, unlike cosine-similarity losses. Training minimizes a β-VAE ELBO with a masked concept loss (BCE for binary, MSE for continuous concepts) plus the cross-covariance term. Counterfactuals are generated via Pearl's abduction–action–prediction: encode the cell, overwrite the target concept dimensions in ĉ while preserving u, and decode. The framework extends to flow matching by conditioning the FM vector field on the scCBGM embedding [u, ĉ], supporting both a decode-only strategy (sample noise → flow to data) and an encode-decode strategy (invert to noise under factual concepts, then flow forward under edited concepts), with the latter implementing full abduction–action–prediction.

## Results

The primary evaluation metric is the rMMD ratio (lower is better; <1 indicates the model outperforms the trivial baseline of mapping to the closest existing population). On the Kang et al. (2017) PBMC dataset, scCBGM-FM(edit) achieves the best rMMD on 6 of 7 cell types, e.g., 0.093 on B cells and 0.084 on NK cells, versus 0.492 and 0.082 for Vanilla-FM(edit) and 2.622 and 2.355 for biolord. On the Cui et al. (2024) immune dictionary dataset, scCBGM-FM(edit) reaches 0.034 rMMD on T cells (CD4) and 0.097 on Dendritic (Langerhans), outperforming all baselines. On the Nault et al. (2023) TCDD liver dataset, scCBGM-FM(edit) achieves 0.708 on Hepatocytes (Periportal) and 0.844 on Stellate cells, compared to 1.131 and 0.853 for the next-best CVAE-FM(edit). Overall, scCBGM-FM improved over or matched Vanilla-FM in 12 of 14 cell types across all three real datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

scCBGM substantially outperforms the standard CBGM (Ismail et al., 2023) across all cell types and datasets; for example, on Kang et al. CD4 T cells, scCBGM-FM(edit) achieves rMMD 0.156 versus 2.228 for CBGM, a ~14× improvement. Against Vanilla-FM (the CellFlow implementation, Klein et al., 2025), scCBGM-FM(edit) wins in 12 of 14 cell types across all three real datasets, with the largest gaps on monocyte subtypes (e.g., 1.206 vs. 2.188 on FCGR3A+ Monocytes in Kang et al.). The biolord baseline (Piran et al., 2024) performs poorly on most cell types (rMMD > 2 on 5 of 7 in Kang et al.), and its FM variant (biolord-FM) does not close the gap, suggesting that its noise-injection disentanglement is insufficient for counterfactual editing. CINEMA-OT (Dong et al., 2023) is limited to mapping cells to training observations and shows the worst performance on most subtypes (e.g., rMMD 7.042 on CD4 T cells in Kang et al.), while scGen (Lotfollahi et al., 2019) relies on additive latent-effect assumptions and similarly underperforms (rMMD 5.117 on CD4 T cells). The closest competitor overall is CVAE-FM(edit), which achieves competitive rMMD on some cell types (e.g., 0.512 on B cells) but degrades sharply on monocyte subtypes (8.442 on FCGR3A+), whereas scCBGM-FM(edit) maintains sub-1.3 rMMD across all cell types in the Kang et al. dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

scCBGM extends Concept Bottleneck Generative Models (Ismail et al., 2023) to single-cell RNA-seq by replacing the concept-embedding bottleneck with a standard scalar concept bottleneck (Koh et al., 2020), adding decoder skip connections for persistent concept conditioning, and substituting the cosine-similarity orthogonality loss with a cross-covariance penalty that removes dimensional constraints. It further integrates with flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) to enable concept-guided editing in both decode-only and encode-decode regimes, grounded in Pearl's abduction-action-prediction framework. This positions scCBGM between the interpretable-representation line (biolord, scDisInFac) and the perturbation-prediction line (CINEMA-OT, scGen, CellFlow), uniquely combining disentangled, concept-level control with cell-level counterfactual generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first principled, interpretable mechanism for cell-level counterfactual editing in single-cell genomics, enabling researchers to ask 'what would this specific cell look like under a different pathway state or treatment' rather than only predicting population-level averages. The synthetic benchmark with ground-truth counterfactuals addresses a critical evaluation gap in the field, where real-data validation is limited to population-level proxy metrics. Key limitations include reliance on population-level evaluation for real data (true cell-specific counterfactuals are unobservable), inability to generalize to entirely novel concepts absent from training, and the risk that in-silico mechanistic hypotheses require experimental validation before therapeutic translation.
