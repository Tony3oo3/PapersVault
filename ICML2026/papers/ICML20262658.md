## Problem

Video generation models predict future observations optimized for perceptual realism rather than manipulation-relevant structure, creating a mismatch between visual fidelity and control relevance. Existing approaches either condition policies directly on predicted frames (offloading the visual-to-control translation to the action head) or decode generated videos into actions (making execution highly sensitive to compounding prediction errors), leaving a gap between imagined futures and stable, executable control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262658/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a current 256×256 RGB observation and a language task instruction as input. At inference, the frozen video generation model produces future frames in a single denoising step (79.85 ms), which are then processed by MoIDM (12.93 ms) and the action head (8.26 ms) to yield a continuous robot action sequence. For multi-view robotic setups, future predictions are generated independently per camera stream, and the action head uses 10 flow-matching sampling steps to produce temporally correlated control commands.

## Method

MoLA introduces a mixture of pretrained, modality-aware inverse dynamics models (MoIDM) as a latent-action interface that converts imagined future video transitions into discrete action tokens. Three specialized IDMs—semantic (SAM2), depth (Depth Anything v2), and flow (CoTracker3)—each employ a spatiotemporal Transformer with a VQ codebook to infer complementary latent actions from the transition between the current and a predicted future frame. These latent actions, together with generated visual features, condition a Diffusion Transformer action head trained with a flow matching objective to produce continuous control sequences. Training proceeds in three stages: fine-tuning Stable Video Diffusion on robot data, pretraining each IDM independently on large-scale manipulation datasets, and jointly fine-tuning MoIDM and the action head end-to-end while keeping the video generator frozen. The key design choice is that the policy reasons over imagined futures in action space rather than image space, with the VQ bottleneck enforcing structured, physically grounded compression of visual transitions into executable representations.

## Results

On the CALVIN ABC-D long-horizon benchmark, MoLA achieves an average task completion length of 4.55, surpassing the strongest baseline DreamVLA (4.44) and the video-prediction-based VPP (4.33). On LIBERO, MoLA reaches 97.0% average success across four task suites, outperforming VPP (90.9%) and CoT-VLA (83.9%). On the large-scale LIBERO-Plus benchmark (10,030 tasks), MoLA achieves 92.7% average success, exceeding the strongest baseline OpenVLA-OFT+ (79.5%) by a 13.2-percentage-point margin. In real-world UR5e experiments, MoLA attains 73.0% average success, outperforming VPP (62.0%) and Diffusion Policy (41.5%), though the company-scale π0.5 model achieves a higher 77.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoLA outperforms all 12 baselines on CALVIN ABC-D, with the closest competitor being DreamVLA (4.55 vs. 4.44 avg. length) and the video-prediction-based VPP (4.55 vs. 4.33). On LIBERO, MoLA leads VPP by 6.1 percentage points (97.0% vs. 90.9%) and CoT-VLA by 13.1 points, with particularly large gains on the Long suite (96.0% vs. 91.5% for VPP). On LIBERO-Plus, MoLA surpasses the strongest baseline OpenVLA-OFT+ by 13.2 percentage points (92.7% vs. 79.5%), and outperforms π0-Fast (61.6%) and RIPT-VLA (68.4%) by wide margins. In real-world experiments, MoLA (73.0%) clearly beats VPP (62.0%), Diffusion Policy (41.5%), and OpenVLA (38.0%), but trails the company-scale π0.5 model (77.5%), which the authors note is a strong reference rather than a strict apples-to-apples comparator since it does not use a world-model-plus-interface architecture. The data-efficiency study further shows MoLA consistently outperforms VPP across all data fractions (10%–100%), with the advantage most pronounced in low-data regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoLA sits at the intersection of two research threads: Video-Action models (VPP, DreamVLA, UniPi) that use video generation to imagine futures, and latent action models (LAPA, UnIVLA, Genie, LAPO) that infer discrete actions from observation transitions via inverse dynamics. Prior Video-Action methods either condition policies directly on predicted frames or tightly couple action decoding to video prediction accuracy, both suffering from a mismatch between visual realism and control relevance. MoLA departs from both by inserting a mixture of three modality-aware inverse dynamics models (semantic via SAM2, depth via Depth Anything v2, flow via CoTracker3) as an explicit latent-action interface, pretraining each on large-scale robot data so they can robustly operate on imperfect generated futures rather than only real observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, well-ablated architectural recipe for converting imagined video futures into action-centric representations, with consistent gains across three simulation benchmarks and real-world UR5e tasks. The 13.2-point margin over the strongest baseline on LIBERO-Plus (10,030 tasks) and the data-efficiency advantage in low-data regimes suggest the latent-action interface is a practical way to leverage video imagination without inheriting its fragility. Key limitations include the absence of high-level semantic reasoning (no LLM integration), dependence on the quality of the frozen video generator, and evaluation limited to tabletop manipulation; whether the modality-aware IDM decomposition generalizes to dexterous or mobile manipulation remains open.
