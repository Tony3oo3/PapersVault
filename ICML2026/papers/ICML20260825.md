## Problem

GFlowNet training relies on an exploratory policy to cover the state graph, but standard ε-greedy exploration and prior curiosity-driven or RND-based methods (AT, SA GFlowNets) can overemphasize already well-approximated regions, wasting samples and slowing mode discovery in multimodal, sparsely-rewarded targets. No prior approach provides a principled balance condition that directly forces the exploratory policy to avoid over-sampled trajectories while concentrating on under-sampled high-reward states.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reward function R: X → R+ over a discrete compositional space X and a directed acyclic state graph G encoding the compositional structure (e.g., sequences, multisets, or grid positions). It maintains two GFlowNets simultaneously: a canonical GFlowNet trained to sample proportionally to R, and an exploration GFlowNet whose target is dynamically conditioned on the canonical GFlowNet's current over-allocation pattern. The final output is the canonical GFlowNet's marginal distribution over terminal states, with the exploration GFlowNet serving purely as a training-time auxiliary that is discarded at inference.

## Method

ACE introduces the Divergent Trajectory Balance (DTB) condition, which enforces standard trajectory balance on trajectories terminating in under-allocated states (where the GFlowNet's induced reward is below α·R(x)) while imposing zero forward probability on trajectories terminating in over-allocated states. The corresponding DTB loss is a log-squared residual that, when minimized, drives the exploration GFlowNet's marginal to be proportional to R(x)^β restricted to under-sampled regions (Proposition 3.4). The canonical GFlowNet is trained on a mixture of its own on-policy samples and the exploration GFlowNet's ε-greedy samples, with the mixing weight w = Z/(Z + Z_∇) set by the relative partition-function masses, ensuring the canonical network increasingly relies on its own policy as it converges. The algorithm alternates exploitation updates (TB loss on the mixture) and exploration updates (DTB loss), forming a two-player game whose equilibrium is characterized in Proposition 3.10: for α ≤ 1 the exploration GFlowNet collapses to zero mass, while for α > 1 it matches the tempered target R(x)^β over the full space.

## Results

ACE is evaluated across seven benchmark tasks (Lazy Random Walk, Grid World, Bit Sequences, Sequence Design, Bag Generation, Quadratic Knapsack, and Antimicrobial Peptide design) using three primary metrics: top-200 average reward of unique discovered states, total variation (TV) distance to the target distribution, and cumulative count of unique high-reward states. The headline result is on the AMP task, where ACE achieves an order-of-magnitude improvement in the cumulative number of unique peptides with ≥95% predicted antimicrobial activity compared to all baselines. On the 16×16 Grid World, ACE reaches the lowest TV distance to the target distribution faster than AT, SA, and ε-greedy GFlowNets. Across all combinatorial tasks (bag generation, knapsack, bit sequences, sequence design), ACE identifies diverse high-reward states significantly earlier in training, as measured by the top-200 unique-state reward curve.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACE is compared against three exploration strategies for GFlowNet training: (1) Adaptive Teachers (AT) GFlowNets (Kim et al., 2025c), which train an exploratory policy on a log-linear mixture of the TB loss and the reward; (2) Sibling Augmented (SA) GFlowNets (Madan et al., 2025), which use random network distillation (RND) as an intrinsic novelty reward; and (3) standard ε-greedy GFlowNets with uniform exploration. ACE outperforms all three baselines on every benchmark task. Against AT, ACE avoids the overemphasis on well-approximated states that plagues loss-guided exploration, resulting in faster mode discovery on bit sequences (K=32, 64), sequence design, and bag generation. Against SA, ACE's principled DTB loss provides a direct repulsive mechanism from over-sampled regions rather than an indirect RND proxy, yielding substantially more diverse AMPs (broader UMAP coverage in Figure 7) and faster convergence on the Lazy Random Walk RINGS and 8-Gaussians distributions. Against ε-greedy, ACE's advantage is most pronounced on multi-modal targets where uniform exploration fails to reach distant high-reward modes, as demonstrated on the Grid World and RINGS tasks. The paper notes that ACE also has fewer hyperparameters (α, β) than both AT (which requires α and C) and SA (which requires β₁, β₂, β₃).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet exploration literature initiated by Adaptive Teachers (AT; Kim et al., 2025c) and Sibling Augmented (SA; Madan et al., 2025) GFlowNets, which train a secondary exploratory GFlowNet guided by proxy novelty signals (TB loss or RND). The key departure is that ACE replaces these indirect novelty proxies with a new balance condition—Divergent Trajectory Balance (DTB)—that directly enforces zero probability on trajectories terminating in over-allocated states, making exploration complementary by construction rather than relying on error functions that the authors argue are incomparable to the true reward. The two-player game formulation (canonical GFlowNet vs. exploration GFlowNet) builds on the flow-network analogy from Bengio et al. (2023) but introduces a principled mechanism for transferring probability mass from over- to under-allocated regions, with equilibrium guarantees absent in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ACE provides the first theoretically grounded exploration mechanism for GFlowNets that provably prevents distributional collapse (Proposition 3.9) and guarantees complementary sampling (Propositions 3.4, 3.10), addressing a core practical bottleneck in scientific discovery applications where reward evaluation is costly and the state space is combinatorially large. The order-of-magnitude improvement in unique high-reward AMP discovery and consistent speedups across seven diverse environments suggest broad practical utility. Open questions include whether non-stationary (curiosity-driven) rewards can be incorporated into the DTB condition, how to optimally weight the mixture in Equation (11) beyond the Z-ratio heuristic, and whether the two-GFlowNet overhead is justified at very large scale. The hyperparameter sensitivity (particularly β=1 causing training failure for α≥0.4) remains a practical concern.
