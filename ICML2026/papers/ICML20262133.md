## Problem

Search-augmented LLMs like ChatGPT and Gemini increasingly personalize responses using user histories, yet systematic evaluation of this personalization remains under-explored. Existing benchmarks for information-seeking (RAG-QA Arena, Search Arena) lack personalization evaluation, while personalization benchmarks (LaMP-QA) are confined to QA-style interactions in constrained domains. BESPOKE fills this gap by providing a realistic, diagnostic benchmark grounded in authentic multi-week user histories spanning diverse professions and interests.

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 150 user-authored queries paired with explicit gold information needs, 2,870 real user history sessions (2,153 search + 717 chat) from 30 annotators, and response-judgment (R-J) pairs where each response receives a 1–5 Likert score plus free-form diagnostic feedback across four criteria (need alignment, content depth, tone, explanation style). The evaluation framework consumes a new model response along with the query, gold information need, query-specific R-J demonstration pairs, and a generated gold rubric, producing per-criterion scores (rescaled to 0–100) and diagnostic feedback, plus a binary-claim-level recall score measuring gold information coverage.

## Method

BESPOKE is constructed through a three-stage human annotation pipeline: (1) annotators author five natural queries with detailed gold information needs covering four task types (analysis, guidance, recommendation, explanation); (2) for each query, k=6 responses are generated by Gemini-2.5-Pro-Grounding under different hypothetical information needs, and annotators score and provide diagnostic feedback on all four criteria; (3) annotators iteratively refine a gold response until it achieves maximum scores on all criteria. The evaluation framework uses GPT-5 as a few-shot evaluator that first generates a query-specific gold rubric from the R-J pairs, then scores new responses conditioned on the rubric, R-J demonstrations, and gold information need. A key finding is that incorporating diagnostic feedback (not just scalar scores) into the evaluator context substantially improves alignment with human judgments (Pearson 0.847 vs. 0.809 without feedback). Gold information coverage is measured by extracting atomic claims from gold responses, verifying them via web search, and computing recall via binary classification of whether each claim appears in the candidate response without contradiction.

## Results

On BESPOKE, the best-performing model o3-search with full personalization (query-aware, selected-history, profile) achieves 62.48 average across four personalization criteria and 30.53 gold information recall, while the weakest model GPT-4o-search under the same setting reaches 57.00 average and 19.23 recall. No model exceeds an average personalization score of 63 in any configuration, confirming that realistic personalization remains a significant challenge. Under an oracle condition where the gold information need is provided explicitly, o3-search jumps to 83.47 in Need Alignment and 88.13 in Tone, revealing that the primary bottleneck is history-based inference rather than response generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates six search-augmented LLMs (GPT-4o-search, o3-search, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Perplexity-sonar, Perplexity-sonar-reasoning) under five personalization configurations. o3-search consistently outperforms all other models across every criterion in both the no-personalization and full-personalization settings, achieving 62.48 average vs. 59.88 (Gemini-2.5-Flash) and 57.00 (GPT-4o-search) under the best configuration. All models show substantial gains from personalization (e.g., o3-search improves from 55.93 to 62.48 average), but the gains are most pronounced in Need Alignment and Recall, while Tone and Style are already relatively high without personalization. The oracle experiment (Table 9) shows that when the gold information need is provided explicitly, o3-search reaches 83.47 in Need Alignment and 88.13 in Tone—far above any history-based setting—confirming that the generation capability is not the bottleneck; rather, inferring implicit preferences from noisy, long-term user histories is the core challenge. In the meta-evaluation, the proposed GPT-5-based evaluator with diagnostic feedback achieves 0.847 Pearson correlation and 0.881 feedback accuracy with human judgments, substantially outperforming the non-personalized baseline (0.470 Pearson, 0.360 accuracy) and the score-only variant (0.809 Pearson, 0.801 accuracy).

## Relation to Prior Work

BESPOKE extends two converging lines of work: search-augmented LLM evaluation (RAG-QA Arena, Search Arena) and personalized LLM evaluation (LaMP, LaMP-QA, LongLaMP). It departs from the former by introducing personalization as a first-class evaluation dimension, and from the latter by replacing synthetic personas and QA-constrained histories with authentic, multi-week chat and search histories from diverse real users. The evaluation framework adapts LLM-as-a-judge methods (MT-Bench, G-Eval) but adds a diagnostic-feedback mechanism and query-specific gold rubrics, while the history-retrieval component draws on HyDE-style pseudo-document generation and LLM-based cross-encoder reranking (Rank1, JudgeRank).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The oracle experiment—where explicitly providing the gold information need lifts o3-search from ~59 to 83 in Need Alignment—clearly identifies the bottleneck as history-based preference inference rather than response generation, giving the field a concrete target. No model exceeds a 60 average across all settings, confirming that realistic personalization remains substantially unsolved. Open questions include how to extract implicit, dispersed preferences from noisy long-term interaction data at scale, and whether the diagnostic feedback can serve as a natural-language reward signal for training personalized systems. The small dataset (30 users, 150 queries) limits statistical power, though the authors outline LLM-based augmentation as a scalability path.
