## Problem

Existing LoRA initialization methods are either data-agnostic (PiSSA, MiLoRA) or rely on a single-step gradient decomposition (LoRA-GA, LoRA-One) that ignores parameter-space anisotropy and sampling-induced variance. No prior method provides a principled, data-aware initialization that simultaneously accounts for both the curvature of the loss landscape and the stochasticity of finite-sample estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265761/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained weight matrix W0 (d1×d2), a small subset S of target-domain samples (|S| ≪ N, default 256), and the LoRA rank r. It outputs a column-orthogonal A matrix (d1×r) and a B matrix (r×d2) such that W0 + A·B approximates the projection of the target parameter onto the LoRA subspace. The Fisher information is estimated via K-FAC factorization (separate d1×d1 input second-moment and d2×d2 output-gradient second-moment matrices), and the eigendecomposition of the d1×d1 Initialization Guidance Matrix is solved per-layer with LOBPCG, keeping peak memory at O(max{d1², d1·d2}).

## Method

LoRA-DA derives the optimal LoRA initialization by minimizing the expected squared Frobenius distance between the constrained MLE and the true target parameter, using asymptotic normality of the MLE. The resulting bound decomposes into a variance term (tr(AᵀJ(W0)⁻¹A)/N, capturing sampling stochasticity through the Fisher information) and a bias term (‖(W_tgt−W0)‖²_F − ‖Aᵀ(W_tgt−W0)‖²_F, capturing the distance between the target and the LoRA subspace). The unknown displacement W_tgt−W0 is approximated by the Fisher-gradient −J(W0)⁻¹G rather than the raw gradient, preserving anisotropic structure of the parameter space. The combined Initialization Guidance Matrix Ω = Σᵢ J(W0)⁻¹_[i]/N − Σᵢ (W_tgt−W0)₍:ᵢ₎(W_tgt−W0)₍:ᵢ₎ᵀ is minimized subject to AᵀA=I_r, yielding A as the r eigenvectors of Ω with the smallest eigenvalues, and B = Aᵀ(W_tgt−W0). This reduces to the gradient-SVD strategy of LoRA-One when the variance term is dropped and the Fisher-gradient is replaced by the raw gradient, making the two improvements (variance modeling and anisotropy-aware bias) explicitly identifiable.

## Results

On commonsense reasoning (NLU) with LLaMA-2-7B, LoRA-DA achieves an average accuracy of 84.3% across eight benchmarks (BoolQ, PIQA, SIQA, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC-e, ARC-c, OBQA), exceeding the prior best MiLoRA (84.0%) by 0.3 points and vanilla LoRA (83.9%) by 0.4 points. On mathematical reasoning (NLG) with LLaMA-2-7B, LoRA-DA reaches 55.0% on GSM8K and 9.2% on MATH (avg 32.1%), improving over the strongest baseline LoRA-One (31.1%) by 1.0 points. Scaling to LLaMA-2-13B, LoRA-DA attains 66.4% on GSM8K and 12.8% on MATH (avg 39.6%), again the best among all methods. Under the frozen-A (LoRA-FA) setting, LoRA-DA achieves 49.4% on GSM8K versus 41.5% for standard LoRA-FA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA-DA consistently outperforms all five baselines across both NLU and NLG tasks. Against vanilla LoRA (random init), LoRA-DA gains 0.4 points on NLU average and 1.4 points on NLG average (7B). Against the data-agnostic methods PiSSA and MiLoRA, LoRA-DA leads by 0.8 and 0.3 points on NLU respectively, and by 1.4 and 1.5 points on NLG (7B), confirming that incorporating target-domain data is more effective than exploiting only the singular structure of pre-trained weights. Against the gradient-based LoRA-One, which performs a one-step SVD on the raw gradient, LoRA-DA improves by 0.4 points on NLU and 1.0 points on NLG (7B), with the gap widening to 1.2 points on NLG (13B). Under the frozen-A (LoRA-FA) setting, LoRA-DA achieves 49.4% on GSM8K versus 41.5% for LoRA-FA with random initialization, a 7.9-point gain that validates the theoretical derivation in the LoRA-FA framework. The advantage is most pronounced at low ranks (r=1,2), where the choice of descent directions is most critical, and narrows but persists as rank increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of data-aware LoRA initialization methods (LoRA-GA, LoRA-One) that use one-step gradient decomposition to set the low-rank subspace. The key departure is a theoretical reframing via asymptotic MLE analysis: the objective decomposes into a Fisher-information variance term (absent in prior methods) and a Fisher-gradient bias term that replaces the raw gradient used by LoRA-GA/LoRA-One, thereby capturing parameter-space anisotropy. Remarkably, the authors show in Remark 4.3 that LoRA-GA and LoRA-One are exact degenerate special cases of their formulation (drop the variance term, replace Fisher-gradient with raw gradient), providing a unifying lens. The practical algorithm leverages K-FAC for scalable Fisher approximation and LOBPCG for the eigendecomposition, building on standard tools from the optimization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled theoretical derivation of data-aware LoRA initialization, moving the field beyond heuristic one-step gradient matching. The modest but consistent gains (0.3% NLU, 1.0% NLG) and the ~6% initialization overhead make this a practical drop-in improvement for LoRA practitioners. However, the core assumption ||W_tgt − W_0|| = O(1/√N) limits applicability to fine-tuning scenarios where the target task is close to pre-training; behavior under large domain shifts remains untested. Open questions include whether the Fisher-gradient formulation extends naturally to multi-task or sequential fine-tuning settings, and whether the variance term's benefit persists at much larger sample sizes where sampling stochasticity becomes negligible.
