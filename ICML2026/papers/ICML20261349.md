## Problem

Online LLM evaluation via sequential pairwise comparisons (e.g., Chatbot Arena) invalidates classical fixed-sample inference because adaptive sampling and continuous monitoring inflate Type I error rates. Existing ranking procedures are largely heuristic and lack formal error guarantees under data-dependent stopping, leaving no principled way to know when a model comparison is reliably resolved or to control the family-wise error rate across all pairwise tests at any monitoring time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of M candidate LLMs, i.i.d. prompts from an underlying distribution, and binary pairwise preference labels (which model's response a human or LLM-judge prefers), along with a target FWER level α. It produces a time-indexed partial ranking as a directed acyclic graph (DAG) over models, an estimated comparison matrix Ĉ^t with entries in {−1, 0, 1}, per-hypothesis e-values, and anytime-valid top-k confidence sets. A key structural property is that the output is valid at every intermediate time t — the partial order can be read off and acted upon at any point without violating the error guarantee, and the DAG tightens into a total order as more comparisons accumulate.

## Method

SERPANT formulates LLM ranking as a collection of m(m−1) directional pairwise null hypotheses and tests each using a mixture likelihood-ratio e-process (Eq. 1), which is a nonnegative supermartingale under the null and thus yields a valid sequential test under any stopping rule. FWER at level α is controlled via a Bonferroni threshold K = m(m−1)/α applied to each e-value, making the guarantee agnostic to the sampling strategy. To improve statistical power, the paper introduces Tournament Sampling: an adaptive pair-selection strategy that assigns each active pair a priority score Q^t(j,k) = w₁·U^t(j,k) + w₂·S^t(j,k) combining uncertainty (win rate near 0.5) and signal strength (deviation from 0.5), then selects the top-q pairs each round with a cyclic exploration/exploitation schedule. Discovered preferences are propagated via transitivity-based pruning, eliminating comparisons already implied by the current partial order. The framework is further extended to top-k identification by constructing per-model rank confidence intervals [1+N_j⁺(t), m−N_j⁻(t)] from the rejected hypotheses, yielding an anytime-valid confidence set C_k(t) that contains all truly top-k models with probability ≥ 1−α.

## Results

In simulations with m=10 models, SERPANT controls FWER at the nominal level α=0.1 at every time point while power increases monotonically with more comparisons; larger capability spread (σ=1.0) yields faster power gains than homogeneous settings (σ=0.1). In real experiments on 10 open-source LLMs across four benchmarks (TriviaQA, SQuAD2.0, Databricks-Dolly-15k, MMLU), tournament sampling recovers 71% of pairwise orderings by t=2000 on TriviaQA versus 51% for random sampling. Against the LMArena leaderboard as ground truth, SERPANT produces at most 1 false discovery (2%) by t=4000, while the empirical win-rate baseline accumulates 4–6 false discoveries (9–13%) throughout evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SERPANT against two baselines. First, tournament sampling is compared to uniform random pair sampling: across all four datasets and all time points, tournament sampling recovers a strictly larger partial order (e.g., 23 vs. 17 orderings at t=1000 on TriviaQA; 30 vs. 29 at t=2000 on SQuAD2.0), confirming that adaptive allocation of comparison queries accelerates discovery without sacrificing validity. Second, SERPANT is compared to an empirical win-rate baseline that simply ranks models by observed pairwise winning proportions. The empirical baseline achieves higher raw discovery counts (41 true vs. 32 true at t=4000) but at the cost of 4–6 false discoveries (9–13%), whereas SERPANT limits false discoveries to at most 1 (2%) throughout, demonstrating the value of anytime-valid error control. Transitivity-based pruning is also ablated: disabling it reduces recovered orderings from 43 (96%) to 38 (84%) at t=3000 on Databricks, a 12-percentage-point power loss, while no contradictory pairwise relations are observed, supporting the transitivity assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: (1) the e-process / anytime-valid inference framework (Howard et al. 2021; Johari et al. 2022), which provides valid sequential testing under arbitrary stopping; (2) multiple-comparison and ranking inference literature (Rao & Kupper 1967; Han et al. 2020; Bazylik et al. 2025), which typically assumes fixed sample sizes and provides only asymptotic or marginal guarantees; and (3) adaptive sampling from bandit and successive-elimination literature (Jamieson & Nowak 2014; Chen et al. 2016). The key departure is combining e-processes with Bonferroni-style FWER control and transitivity-based pruning to yield the first anytime-valid *family-wise* guarantee for online LLM ranking, whereas prior anytime-valid work addressed single-hypothesis testing and prior ranking inference lacked sequential validity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LLM leaderboards (Chatbot Arena, LMArena) currently rely on heuristic win-rate aggregation with no formal error control; SERPANT provides a principled, uncertainty-quantified alternative that enables early stopping and cost savings on expensive human (or LLM-as-judge) annotation. Open questions the authors flag include extending from FWER to the more powerful FDR criterion, handling context-dependent (non-stationary) preferences, and correcting for systematic biases in LLM-as-a-judge evaluators. The transitivity assumption, while empirically validated here, may fail in highly subjective domains, though the authors note validity is preserved (at reduced efficiency) without it.
