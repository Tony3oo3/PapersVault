## Problem

Existing LLM-based symbolic regression methods rely on scalar feedback (e.g., MSE) to guide evolutionary search, forcing the LLM to simultaneously infer the appropriate symbolic edit, diagnose structural errors, and reuse past experience from a single score. This conflation of candidate proposal with search guidance yields weakly guided trial-and-error that typically requires ~10³ evaluated candidates per problem, making the approach sample-inefficient for practical scientific discovery.

## Input / Output

The method consumes input-output data pairs (x_i, y_i) along with a problem context specifying admissible variables, operators, and constants. The LLM generates symbolic skeletons with placeholder constants, which are grounded via BFGS optimization on a sub-training split; fitness is scored on a held-out internal validation split to prevent overfitting. The final output is a compact symbolic expression, and the entire search operates within a budget of 400 LLM-generated candidates (40% of the standard 1,000-sample budget used by baselines).

## Method

Deliberate Evolution (DE) decouples symbolic generation from search control by conditioning LLM proposals on three explicit guidance signals. (1) Adaptive operators: a multi-armed bandit policy selects among four semantic operators (refine, mutate, crossover, regenerate) based on a discrete search state (candidate quality rank and exploration maturity), with a stagnation monitor that triggers global exploration when progress stalls. (2) Diagnostic tools: a data profiler, residual diagnostic, and dimensional verifier convert scalar MSE into localized structural feedback (e.g., missing periodic terms, invalid unit compositions) injected into the LLM prompt. (3) Reflective memory: the LLM periodically distills search trajectories into concise natural-language insights about successful motifs, failure modes, and breakthrough edits, which are retrieved in subsequent rounds. A hitting-time analysis shows that even a modest positive guidance margin γ over unguided proposals yields exponentially faster convergence to target-quality expressions, explaining the observed 40%-budget advantage.

## Results

On LLM-SRBench, Deliberate Evolution (DE) consistently achieves the best NMSE and Acc₀.₀₁ across all four scientific domains (Physics, Material Science, Chemistry, Biology) and both backbone models (Llama3.1-8B, Qwen3-4B) while using only 400 evaluated candidates versus 1,000 for baselines. With Qwen3-4B on LSR-Transform, DE reduces NMSE from 1.83e-1 (best baseline LASR) to 1.15e-1 and improves Acc₀.₀₁ from 30.91% to 50.45%. On LSR-Synth Physics, DE reaches NMSE 4.37e-4, roughly 5.7× lower than the strongest baseline (LLM-SR at 2.51e-3). The paper reports an average NMSE reduction of 55% (Llama3.1-8B) and 37% (Qwen3-4B) relative to the best baseline at 40% of the standard sample budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DE outperforms all four baselines (LLMDirect, LLM-SR, LASR, SGA) on every domain and both backbone models in Table 1. The largest margins appear on LSR-Synth: with Qwen3-4B, DE achieves NMSE 4.37e-4 on Physics vs. 2.51e-3 for LLM-SR (the strongest baseline there) and 1.88e-4 on Chemistry vs. 2.31e-3 for LASR, representing roughly 5–12× improvements. On LSR-Transform with Qwen3-4B, DE cuts NMSE from 1.83e-1 (LASR) to 1.15e-1 and nearly doubles Acc₀.₀₁ from 30.91% to 50.45%. SGA is consistently the weakest baseline, often producing NMSE values an order of magnitude worse than DE (e.g., 1.04e-1 vs. 4.37e-4 on Physics with Qwen3-4B). Notably, DE achieves these gains while using only 400 LLM-generated candidates per problem versus 1,000 for all baselines, and LASR additionally explores ~453,000 non-LLM mutations. On the real-world Stress-Strain dataset, DE also achieves the best ID-NMSE (1.11e-1 vs. 1.44e-1 for LLM-SR) and OOD-NMSE (2.98e-1 vs. 6.34e-1 for LLM-SR), confirming the advantage extends beyond synthetic benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based evolutionary symbolic regression paradigm established by LLM-SR, LASR, and SGA, where an LLM proposes symbolic skeletons and BFGS fits constants within a population-based evolutionary loop. The key conceptual departure is decoupling candidate proposal from search guidance: rather than conditioning the LLM solely on a parent expression and its scalar MSE, DE injects three explicit structured signals—adaptive operator selection (a multi-armed bandit over refine/mutate/crossover/regenerate), tool-based diagnostic reports (data profiling, residual analysis, dimensional verification), and reflective memory distilled from search trajectories. This positions the work at the intersection of agentic reasoning (Reflexion-style memory, tool-augmented LLMs) and test-time scaling via external feedback, reframing the evolutionary loop from stochastic trial-and-error into a deliberately guided search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: using only 40% of the standard LLM sampling budget, DE reduces average NMSE by 55% (Llama3.1-8B) and 37% (Qwen3-4B) while also improving out-of-distribution generalization and noise robustness, suggesting the method recovers more faithful symbolic structures rather than overfitting surrogates. The ablation revealing that diagnostic tools are the single most critical component (their removal degrades NMSE by ~57×) offers a clear design principle: knowing *why* an expression fails is more valuable than knowing *how* to mutate it or *what* worked before. Open questions include whether the framework scales to closed-source frontier models, whether test-time fine-tuning of the proposer could further reduce the sample budget, and whether multimodal diagnostic tools (e.g., visual residual plots) could strengthen the structural feedback loop.
