## Problem

Despite strong performance on physics benchmarks, it remains unclear whether video world models internally represent physical information as compact, reusable state variables (a 'physics engine') or as distributed, task-specific representations. Prior work has treated models as black boxes, evaluating downstream accuracy without characterizing where physical information is constructed, how it is organized across layers and patches, or what geometric form it takes.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on frozen pretrained video encoder activations (V-JEPA 2 L/H/G and VideoMAE-v2-G) extracted at every layer from video clips: IntPhys possible/impossible pairs, synthetic single-ball videos (256×256, 16 frames at 24 fps, 8 directions × 7 speeds or 5 accelerations), and CLEVRER multi-object scenes. Outputs are structured as layer-wise probe curves (accuracy or R² per layer), subspace geometry metrics (principal angles, projection overlap, Grassmann distance between task subspaces), per-head attention locality heatmaps, and steering results quantifying how many orthogonal probe dimensions must be jointly manipulated to shift decoded motion direction.

## Method

The paper combines four complementary interpretability tools applied to frozen video transformers. First, layer-wise linear and attentive-MLP probes on mean-pooled spatiotemporal patches localize where physical variables become linearly accessible, revealing a sharp transition at ~1/3 depth (the Physics Emergence Zone) where direction emerges while scalar speed and acceleration are already available in early layers. Second, subspace geometry analysis (principal angles, projection overlap against random baselines) shows that direction and possible-impossible physics judgments occupy nearly orthogonal subspaces despite co-emerging at the same depth, ruling out shared latent variables. Third, attention head distance analysis identifies unusually local spatiotemporal attention heads that emerge uniquely at the PEZ; targeted ablation of these local heads degrades physics and temporal reasoning while leaving ImageNet classification intact, establishing a shared circuit-level substrate. Fourth, an iterative orthogonal-probe sequence (closely related to nullspace projection) measures the effective dimensionality of each representation, revealing that direction requires 40–80 orthogonal features and is encoded as a circular population code with sinusoidal (sine-cosine) tuning, such that steering direction requires coordinated intervention across dozens of dimensions rather than the low-rank single-direction interventions typical of language models.

## Results

Across V-JEPA 2 (L/H/G) and VideoMAE-v2-G, a sharp Physics Emergence Zone (PEZ) appears at 25–29% of encoder depth, where IntPhys possible-vs-impossible probe accuracy jumps from ~50% to 85–95% and motion direction R² reaches 0.87–0.97. Scalar speed and acceleration are decodable from early layers, while direction becomes linearly accessible only at the PEZ. Direction and IntPhys occupy nearly orthogonal subspaces (mean principal angles 69°–83%; projection overlap 7–13%, matching random baselines), and steering direction requires coordinated intervention across 40–50 orthogonal probe dimensions. Fine-tuning only the 4 PEZ blocks (16% of V-JEPA 2-L parameters) achieves 100% IntPhys accuracy with zero across-fold variance, while late-layer fine-tuning recovers ImageNet (68.7%) but plateaus at 85.8% IntPhys.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability study rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the key contrasts are internal. Across architectures, V-JEPA 2 (latent prediction) and VideoMAE-v2-G (pixel reconstruction at 1B scale) both exhibit a clean PEZ, while smaller VideoMAE-v2 variants (86M–600M) fail to produce reliable IntPhys emergence despite encoding direction comparably well (R² 0.78–0.85), suggesting a capacity threshold under the reconstruction objective. Diffusion models (CogVideoX, Open-Sora) show PEZ at a slightly deeper location (34–45% vs. 28±4% for encoders), while autoregressive (Cosmos AR-4B), classification (SlowFast, I3D), tracking (TCOW), and contrastive (InternVideo) objectives encode direction above chance but do not produce a PEZ for physics plausibility. On the task-specificity front, shuffled-video detection mirrors the PEZ emergence pattern, whereas CLEVRER object counting and Something-Something-v2 classification do not, confirming the PEZ is selective to global spatiotemporal coherence rather than a generic depth effect. The attention ablation (Table 2) provides the sharpest causal contrast: suppressing local spatiotemporal attention at the PEZ drops direction R² from 0.97 to 0.14 and IntPhys from 78.3% to 61.7%, while ImageNet accuracy remains essentially unchanged (33.7% → 33.1%), establishing a clean physics-vs-semantics dissociation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to apply mechanistic interpretability tools—layerwise probing, subspace geometry analysis, attention ablation, and activation steering—to physical variables inside large-scale video encoders. Prior work on physical reasoning in video models (Garrido et al., 2025; Motamed et al., 2025) treated models as black boxes evaluated on benchmarks like IntPhys and CLEVRER, while earlier video interpretability studies (Ghodrati et al., 2018; Kowal et al., 2024) used visualization or proxy tasks without characterizing the representational form of physical quantities. The orthogonal probe sequence method draws on iterative nullspace projection (Ravfogel et al., 2020), and the circular population code analysis parallels classical motion neuroscience (Albright, 1984; Jazayeri & Movshon, 2006), but the combination and application to video transformer physics variables is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that motion direction is encoded as a high-dimensional circular population code requiring 40–80 orthogonal dimensions to steer—orders of magnitude more than the low-rank directions typical in language models—challenges the physics-engine hypothesis and supports distributed, task-specific representations for intuitive physics. The consistent one-third-depth Physics Emergence Zone across architectures and scales provides a practical target for fine-tuning (16% of parameters suffice for perfect IntPhys accuracy) and a bridge to the V1→MT hierarchy in primate vision. Key limitations include restriction to encoder-based transformers with masked objectives, focus on possible/impossible discrimination and controlled single-object motion rather than contact dynamics or force inference, and the open question of whether richer physical understanding (counterfactual reasoning, fluid dynamics) emerges at the same depth or not at all.
