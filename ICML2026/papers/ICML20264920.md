## Problem

Zero-shot imitation learning requires reproducing expert behavior from a single demonstration without test-time gradient updates or additional environment interaction. Prior methods that project demonstrations onto general reward-function spaces (e.g., Forward-Backward representations) provably fail to identify the demonstrator's true policy because they ignore the partition function, which encodes the relative difficulty of reaching different states. The paper asks whether restricting the reward hypothesis space to goal-conditioned functions, combined with contrastive RL pretraining, yields a consistent and practical zero-shot imitation algorithm.

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method receives a single expert trajectory τ = (s₀, a₀, …, s_T, a_T) over a state-vector space (e.g., 29-dim for Ant, 23-dim for Pusher) with no reward function, no additional environment queries, and no gradient updates. It outputs a goal state ĝ sampled from a learned variational posterior q_ξ(g|τ) and executes a pre-trained goal-conditioned policy π̃_α(·|s, ĝ) to reach that goal. The goal space is by default identical to the state space but can be expanded (e.g., to include 3D linear/angular velocity for Ant locomotion tasks) to capture non-goal-reaching reward structures.

## Method

CIRL couples three self-supervised components trained jointly without demonstrations, rewards, or preferences: (1) a MaxEnt extension of Contrastive RL where the critic learns soft Q-values via a noise-contrastive loss over state-action-goal tuples and the actor optimizes the soft Q-function plus a discounted entropy bonus; (2) GoalKDE, an automatic exploration mechanism that fits a Gaussian KDE over the replay buffer and selects the lowest-density state as the next training goal; and (3) a mean-field variational goal inference model (a Gaussian MLP) trained with Forward Amortized Variational Inference (FAVI) to match the true posterior p*(g|τ) ∝ p(g)·exp(Σ_t r_g(s_t,a_t) − log Z_g). The key theoretical contribution is proving consistency: because the partition function Z_g is implicitly captured in samples drawn from the MaxEnt policy, CIRL correctly accounts for goal difficulty, whereas FB representations that use occupancy measures directly as reward signals provably misidentify the demonstrator's policy. The mean-field factorization q_ξ(g|τ) = ∏_t q_ξ(g|s_t,a_t) is shown to be without loss of generality (Corollary 4.1) since the true posterior for finite trajectories already takes this form.

## Results

CIRL is evaluated on goal-conditioned environments (Reacher, Pusher, Ant, PointMaze) from JaxGCRL and non-goal-conditioned URLB tasks (Ant Forward, Ant Jump, Ant Flip). The primary metric is imitation score (ratio of algorithm's cumulative return to expert's average cumulative reward) for goal-conditioned tasks, and regret for URLB tasks. CIRL consistently achieves the highest imitation scores across all goal-conditioned environments, with near-perfect scores in Reacher using GoalKDE exploration. On URLB, CIRL achieves lower regret than both FB and HILP baselines when imitating policies trained with non-goal-conditioned reward functions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIRL is compared against two main zero-shot imitation baselines: the Forward-Backward (FB) representation (Touati & Ollivier, 2021) and HILP (Wu et al., 2018), plus a 1-NN baseline. CIRL consistently outperforms FB across all goal-conditioned environments (Reacher, Pusher, Ant), with FB achieving only 'a small fraction' of CIRL's imitation score even when both are given the same goal information (last state of the demonstration). The paper further proves theoretically that FB is inconsistent: it systematically misidentifies the demonstrator's true policy because it uses occupancy measures as reward signals without accounting for the partition function. On the URLB benchmark (Ant Forward, Ant Jump, Ant Flip), CIRL achieves lower regret than both FB and HILP, demonstrating that goal-conditioned contrastive RL pretraining generalizes to non-goal-reaching reward functions when the goal space is sufficiently expanded. A 1-NN baseline (applying the action from the nearest expert state) is also outperformed by CIRL in all environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Contrastive RL (CRL) to the Maximum Entropy RL setting and couples it with a variational goal-inference module to solve zero-shot imitation learning. It departs from Forward-Backward (FB) representations (Touati & Ollivier, 2021) by restricting the reward hypothesis space to goal-conditioned rewards, which reduces the multi-task IRL problem to a purely goal-inference problem solvable via Forward Amortized Variational Inference (FAVI). The automatic goal sampling mechanism (GoalKDE) draws on the MEGA/EDL line of work for exploration, while the mean-field variational posterior connects to amortized inference literature (Ambrogioni et al., 2019). The key conceptual departure from prior zero-shot IL methods (BC-Zero, Pathak et al., FB) is that all training is self-supervised—no expert data, no rewards, no preferences—yet the method is provably consistent in recovering the demonstrator's goal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistency proof and the counterexample showing FB representations are provably inconsistent for IRL provide a clear theoretical justification for why goal-conditioned reward spaces are the right inductive bias for imitation, even for non-goal-reaching tasks (demonstrated on URLB). Practically, this offers a fully self-supervised pretraining pipeline that requires no human data collection, which is significant for scaling interactive agents. Key limitations include the requirement for simulator access, the need for the goal space to be sufficiently expressive to capture arbitrary reward functions, and GoalKDE's degradation in high-dimensional state spaces (e.g., Hardest Maze). Open questions include whether richer goal representations (language, multi-modal, sub-goal hierarchies) can close the gap for non-goal-reaching rewards, and whether the approach transfers to safety-critical or low-data real-world settings.
