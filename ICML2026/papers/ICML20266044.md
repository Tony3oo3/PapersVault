## Problem

Bayesian Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) offer a principled way to quantify uncertainty in PDE solutions, but rigorous statistical guarantees—specifically posterior contraction rates—were entirely absent for this setting. The paper addresses this gap by proving that a Bayesian PINN with a carefully designed prior concentrates around the true solution of an elliptic PDE at a near-minimax rate, and that the procedure is rate-adaptive to the unknown smoothness of the solution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy point measurements of the PDE source term f (drawn uniformly in the interior domain Ω) and noisy Dirichlet boundary data g (drawn uniformly on ∂Ω), together with known smooth coefficients A and V. Crucially, the solution u* itself is never observed directly; only the right-hand side and boundary values are measured, making this an indirect observation model that is statistically harder than observing the solution. The output is a full posterior distribution over the DNN function class, from which one can extract the posterior mean as a point estimate and credible intervals for uncertainty quantification, with contraction guaranteed in the PINN-loss norm (implying H^{1/2} convergence).

## Method

The paper places a spike-and-slab prior on the weights of a fixed-depth (L = O(log d + log β*)) σ₃-activated DNN with a clip function ensuring uniform boundedness, where width W follows a geometric prior, each parameter's inclusion follows a Bernoulli prior inducing sparsity, and the amplitude B follows an exponential prior. The key technical contribution is a sieve construction: using B-spline approximation theory, the authors show that a DNN with width, sparsity, and parameter size scaling as n^{d/(d+2(β−2))} approximates any β-Hölder function to O(n^{−(β−2)/(d+2(β−2))}), and then verify the three conditions of the Ghosal–Van der Vaart posterior contraction framework (entropy bound, prior mass lower bound, and sieve tail decay) under the empirical PINN-loss. A local Rademacher complexity argument controls the generalization gap between empirical and population PINN-loss, transferring contraction from the empirical to the population setting. The resulting rate ε_n = n^{−(β−2)/(d+2(β−2))}(log n)^{1/2} is near-minimax optimal (matching a Fano-based lower bound up to logarithmic factors) and is rate-adaptive because the prior hyperparameters do not depend on the unknown smoothness β.

## Results

The paper establishes that the Bayesian PINN posterior contracts around the true solution at rate ε_n = n^{-(β-2)/(d+2(β-2))} (log n)^{1/2} in the PINN-loss norm, where β is the Hölder smoothness of the solution and d is the spatial dimension. This rate is rate-adaptive: it is achieved without prior knowledge of β. The corresponding minimax lower bound (Theorem 3.5) is n_1^{2(β-2)/(d+2(β-2))} + n_2^{2β/(d-1+2β)} for the PINN-loss, confirming near-minimax optimality when interior and boundary sample sizes are equal. The contraction also implies convergence in H^{1/2}(Ω)-norm via a stability estimate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or empirical comparison tables. The theoretical positioning is as follows: compared to Lu et al. (2021a), which proves minimax-optimal rates for point-estimator PINNs under homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions (where the H²-norm is equivalent to the PINN-loss), this paper handles non-homogeneous Dirichlet data with an L² boundary penalty, yielding contraction only in H^{1/2}(Ω) rather than H²(Ω). Compared to Sun et al. (2024), which observes the PDE solution directly and thus achieves faster rates, this paper observes only the source term and boundary data (an indirect, statistically harder problem), so its rates are necessarily slower but remain minimax-optimal under that data model. The key advantage over both prior works is rate-adaptivity: the Bayesian PINN achieves the optimal contraction rate without knowing the smoothness level β of the true solution, whereas the rates in Lu et al. (2021a) are non-adaptive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the sieve-based posterior contraction framework of Ghosal & Van der Vaart (2007) as instantiated for Bayesian neural networks by Polson & Ročková (2018), (2) the DNN approximation theory for Hölder functions and PINN loss formulation for elliptic PDEs developed by Lu et al. (2021a), and (3) the empirical Bayesian PINN framework of Raissi et al. (2019) and Yang et al. (2021). The key departure from Lu et al. (2021a) is the shift from point-estimator (ERM) analysis to full posterior contraction, combined with a rate-adaptive spike-and-slab prior that requires no knowledge of the solution's smoothness. Compared to the closest prior theoretical work, Sun et al. (2024), this paper addresses a statistically harder indirect-observation model (noisy source term and boundary data rather than direct solution measurements) and handles non-homogeneous Dirichlet conditions, at the cost of a slower minimax rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous posterior contraction guarantee for Bayesian PINNs under an indirect observation model, filling a notable gap between the growing empirical use of Bayesian PINNs for uncertainty quantification and the absence of theoretical justification. The rate-adaptivity property is practically valuable: practitioners need not specify the smoothness level of the PDE solution in advance. However, the contraction is only established in the H^{1/2} norm (via the PINN-loss), which is substantially weaker than the H^2 norm achievable in the homogeneous-boundary setting of Lu et al. (2021a); extending to stronger norms would require Sobolev boundary penalties that are difficult to approximate from point evaluations. Open questions include handling imbalanced interior/boundary sample sizes, extending to irregular (Besov) solutions where adaptive wavelet methods achieve local adaptivity, and whether Barron-space regularity can eliminate the curse of dimensionality in the Bayesian setting.
