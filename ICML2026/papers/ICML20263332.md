## Problem

Existing differentiable dynamic programming formulations compute only first-order marginals (the soft attention matrix), leaving the algorithm's own hyperparameters—gap penalties, edit costs, temperatures—fixed by hand. Learning these parameters requires differentiating the marginals themselves, a second-order problem for which no closed-form expressions or efficient implementations existed. The paper shows this gap is not merely theoretical: freezing Smith-Waterman's single gap penalty at the bioinformatics default (g = −10) collapses alignment F1 from 0.74 to 0.32 at full encoder capacity and to 0.13 at lower capacity.

## Input / Output

For protein alignment, two 3D structures are encoded by a neural network (ProteinMPNN, GVP, or IPA at 64 or 128 dimensions) into per-residue embeddings whose inner products form a pairwise score matrix S ∈ R^{n×m} fed into differentiable Smith-Waterman; for parsing, token embeddings produce span scores fed into CKY or Eisner. The primary output is the soft marginal matrix P_T (the structured attention map), but the method simultaneously learns the DP hyperparameters (gap-open g_o, gap-extend g_e, edit costs, temperature T) as unconstrained scalars whose gradients flow through the second-order cross-Jacobian. All twelve algorithms share the same four-pass template (forward, backward, HVP, cross-Jacobian) and run in O(nm) for grid or O(n³) for chart algorithms via anti-diagonal wavefront parallelism.

## Method

The paper's central identity is that temperature-softened DP marginals are exactly structured attention weights, and because these marginals are already the first derivative of the log-partition function, learning the algorithm's own parameters is inherently a second-order problem. The key theoretical result is a universal covariance identity: every second-order derivative (score-score Hessian, score-parameter cross-Jacobian, score-temperature cross-Jacobian) equals (1/T) times the covariance between the corresponding sufficient statistics under the induced Gibbs distribution p_T. This yields a unified U+W two-pass algorithm—a forward U pass computing ∂α_i/∂η and a backward W pass with tangent weights—that computes all cross-Jacobians at the same O(|E|) asymptotic cost as the standard backward pass. The authors instantiate this for twelve algorithms (six alignment, four edit distance, two parsing) and implement all four passes as fused CUDA kernels achieving 100–20,000× speedups over standard PyTorch, making end-to-end learning of DP hyperparameters practical at modern scales.

## Results

On protein structure alignment (SCOPe40, 65,880 validation pairs), ProteinMPNN-128 with differentiable affine Smith-Waterman reaches F1=0.748 and lDDT=0.445 (91% of the TM-align ceiling of 0.489), far exceeding the best learned substitution matrix (F1=0.510). Freezing the single Smith-Waterman gap at the bioinformatics default (g=−10) collapses F1 from 0.743 to 0.321 at full encoder capacity and to 0.128 at 64-dim capacity, while learning it via the cross-Jacobian recovers full performance. In the structure-versus-capacity sweep, the correct DP prior (SW) holds across the full capacity range (F1=0.748 at 128-dim, 0.51 at zero capacity), whereas row-softmax cross-attention depends entirely on capacity (0.691→<0.01) and the wrong prior (DTW) cannot be rescued by any capacity (≈0.05 throughout). On constituency parsing, a structured CKY CRF supervised only by the gold tree's score matches dense per-span supervision to within 0.003 F1 on English and exceeds it on 3 of 9 domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between learning and freezing the DP algorithm's own hyperparameters. Freezing the linear-gap Smith-Waterman penalty at g=−10 causes a catastrophic 0.42 F1 drop at full capacity (0.743→0.321) and a 0.61 drop at 64-dim capacity (0.736→0.128), because exp(−10/T) grows ~3000× harsher as temperature anneals from 5 to 1; the affine variant, with a separate extend penalty to absorb the mismatch, degrades far less (0.748→0.705). Against structural priors, Smith-Waterman dominates row-softmax cross-attention by 0.057 F1 at full capacity (0.748 vs 0.691) and by an unbounded margin at zero capacity (0.51 vs <0.01), while DTW (monotone warping without gaps) is uniformly near 0.05 regardless of capacity, confirming that the wrong recursion cannot be compensated by scale. Neural encoders (ProteinMPNN, GVP, IPA at 64/128-dim) all outperform the best learned substitution matrix (joint 400×400 AA+3Di, F1=0.510) by more than 0.2 F1, with 3Di codes (0.468) far exceeding amino-acid codes alone (0.232). On parsing, the structured CKY CRF trained only on the gold tree's scalar score matches dense per-span supervision within 0.003 F1 on English and exceeds it on 3 of 9 domains spanning natural language and code, demonstrating supervision efficiency of the structured prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable dynamic programming lineage (Mensch & Blondel, 2018; Kim et al., 2017; Eisner, 2016; Petti et al., 2023) by deriving the second-order derivative layer that all prior work leaves uncomputed: Hessian-vector products for encoder training and, critically, cross-Jacobians that make the DP algorithm's own hyperparameters (gap penalties, edit costs, span weights, temperature) learnable end-to-end. The conceptual reframe is that classical DP algorithms are the zero-temperature limit of structured attention, so their hand-tuned constants are bona fide parameters rather than fixed inference machinery. All twelve algorithms (alignment, edit distance, parsing) are unified under this single framework, with the cross-Jacobians expressed as closed-form Gibbs covariances and implemented as fused CUDA kernels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that freezing Smith-Waterman's gap penalty at the bioinformatics default collapses alignment F1 from 0.74 to 0.32 (and 0.13 at lower encoder capacity) demonstrates that the structure of the attention computation matters as much as the representations feeding it, sharpening the bitter-lesson debate in domains with rich structure and scarce data. The 100–20,000× CUDA speedups make second-order structured prediction practical at modern scales, and the open-source library lowers the barrier for scientific domains where structured constraints are known but labels are expensive. Open questions include position-dependent penalty functions g(i,j)=f_φ(h_i,h_j), multiple sequence alignment, grammar induction, and coupling to large pretrained encoders; current limitations include scalar position-independent penalties, modest encoder widths, and parsing results that trail scaled autoregressive LMs in absolute F1.
