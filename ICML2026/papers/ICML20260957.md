## Problem

Controllable generation with discrete diffusion models is hindered by methods that require retraining (fine-tuning, classifier training) or suffer from prohibitive computational costs and limited steering effectiveness (SMC, importance sampling). Plug-and-play guidance works well in continuous diffusion but fails to translate to discrete spaces due to the inherent non-differentiability of categorical data and the numerical instability of gradients through discrete token spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260957/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained discrete diffusion model, an off-the-shelf reward function (which may be differentiable or a black-box evaluator), and a fully masked initial sequence. At each reverse step, it draws n Monte Carlo samples from the model's clean-prediction distribution to estimate a value gradient, then corrects the prediction logits before sampling the next state. The output is a guided discrete sequence (DNA, protein, molecular structure, or image) that satisfies the target property while remaining close to the original data manifold, with no parameter updates at any point.

## Method

GILC reframes guided discrete diffusion as estimating the gradient of a value function (expected future reward) and introduces two key mechanisms. First, it repurposes the pre-trained denoising network as a variational proxy for value estimation, avoiding the need to train a separate value network. Second, it employs a Jacobian-free logit correction: rather than backpropagating through the ill-conditioned model Jacobian ∂η/∂z_t (which is numerically unstable in discrete spaces), it computes the reward gradient directly in the smooth logit space via Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization combined with a Straight-Through estimator. Two variants handle different reward types: GILC-DB uses direct backpropagation for differentiable rewards, while GILC-PG uses a policy gradient formulation with group-relative advantages (inspired by GRPO) for non-differentiable or black-box objectives. The corrected logits η^r = η + g_η/β are plugged into the standard absorbing-state posterior to sample the next denoised state, making the entire procedure a drop-in replacement for the unguided reverse step.

## Results

On DNA enhancer design, GILC-DB achieves the highest predicted activity (7.04±0.26) and chromatin accessibility (95.2±2.1%), surpassing the best fine-tuning baseline DRAKES (5.61, 92.5%) and all training-free methods. On protein inverse folding, GILC-DB reaches an 82.4% overall success rate (Pred-ddG > 0 and scRMSD < 2), exceeding DRAKES by ~4 percentage points. On QM9 molecular generation, GILC-DB achieves the lowest MAE on 4 of 6 quantum properties (e.g., 2.23 for C_v, 1.09 for polarizability), while GILC-PG achieves the best MAE on the remaining two (1.53 for C_v, 0.808 for polarizability, 2.15 for dipole moment, 783 for energy gap, 374 for ε_HOMO, 875 for ε_LUMO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GILC-DB dominates all training-free baselines across every metric: it outperforms SVDD (the strongest training-free method) by +2.20 in Pred-Activity and +43.3% in ATAC-Acc, and beats TFG-Flow by +3.56 in Pred-Activity. Against the fine-tuning baseline DRAKES, GILC-DB achieves higher Pred-Activity (7.04 vs. 5.61) and ATAC-Acc (95.2% vs. 92.5%) without any parameter updates, while GILC-PG matches or exceeds DRAKES on sequence fidelity metrics (3-mer Corr 0.910 vs. 0.887; JASPAR Corr 0.937 vs. 0.911). On protein inverse folding, GILC-DB achieves an 82.4% success rate versus DRAKES's 78.6%, and substantially outperforms all training-free methods (SVDD 65.0%, SMC 63.6%). Classifier Guidance (CG) is essentially non-functional in the discrete setting (0% ATAC-Acc), while CFG, though competitive on ATAC-Acc (92.1%), lags GILC-DB on Pred-Activity (5.04 vs. 7.04) and requires labeled training pairs. On QM9 molecular generation, both GILC variants outperform TFG-Flow and SVDD on all six quantum properties, with GILC-PG achieving the lowest MAE on 4 of 6 properties despite treating the reward as a black box.

## Relation to Prior Work

GILC extends the plug-and-play guidance paradigm—well established in continuous diffusion (DPS, SDS, loss-guided diffusion)—to discrete diffusion, where the non-differentiability of categorical sampling has previously forced reliance on SMC, importance sampling, or retraining-based approaches (CG, CFG, DRAKES). The method repurposes the pretrained denoising network as a variational proxy for value function estimation, drawing on the ELBO training objective already used to fit the model, and borrows the Jacobian-omission principle from Score Distillation Sampling to avoid the ill-conditioned model Jacobian. For non-differentiable rewards, it adapts the policy gradient formulation (Williams, 1992) with GRPO-style group relative advantages (Shao et al., 2024), unifying both differentiable and black-box guidance under a single logit-correction framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GILC provides a practical, training-free path to controllable discrete generation that avoids the retraining cost and reward-hacking risks of fine-tuning, which is significant for scientific domains (DNA, protein, molecular design) where reward oracles are expensive and data-limited. The key open limitation is the mean-field token-independence assumption, which the authors acknowledge may introduce non-trivial errors in structured domains like natural language; extending to large-scale discrete diffusion language models with relaxed independence constraints is identified as a critical next step. Additionally, the method still requires multiple Monte Carlo reward evaluations per step, and reducing this sampling overhead while preserving guidance fidelity remains an open problem.
