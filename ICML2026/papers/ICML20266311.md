## Problem

Existing preference alignment methods for hallucination mitigation treat truthfulness and helpfulness as either universally collaborative or universally conflicting across all samples, imposing a uniform trade-off. The paper shows that objective conflicts arise in only ~11% of preference pairs, meaning uniform regulation unnecessarily weakens truthfulness optimization on the vast majority of aligned samples while failing to adapt to varying conflict severity on the minority that do conflict.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266311/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CAMP consumes preference pairs (x, y_w, y_l) where y_w is selected over y_l based on truthfulness score difference (≥1 point on a 1–5 LLM-judge scale), and each response carries both a truthfulness and a helpfulness score. The per-pair margin is computed from the score differences Δ_truth and Δ_help, and the method outputs an aligned LLM policy (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) trained for one epoch on 20k pairs with max 256-token prompts and completions.

## Method

CAMP extends DPO by subtracting a pair-dependent adaptive margin α·Δ(x, y_w, y_l) from the log-likelihood gap in the preference probability, so that the effective optimization strength varies per sample. The margin is constructed by first checking whether the helpfulness difference Δ_help is non-negative (synergy) or negative (conflict): for synergy pairs, Δ equals the full truthfulness difference, preserving a strong DPO signal; for conflict pairs, Δ is reduced by λ·|Δ_help| (clamped at zero), softening the update in proportion to how much helpfulness is sacrificed. This makes CAMP a conditional trade-off mechanism that intervenes only under objective disagreement, with α controlling overall margin strength and λ controlling sensitivity to conflict severity. The design is modular and can be extended to other objective pairs beyond truthfulness–helpfulness.

## Results

On TruthfulQA, CAMP achieves 85.92% accuracy and a 9.79% hallucination rate, improving accuracy by 6.52% over DPO (80.66%) and 4.30% over MODPO (82.38%) while reducing hallucination rates by 1.47% and 1.84% respectively. On HaluEval, CAMP reaches 95.20% accuracy with a 3.20% hallucination rate, outperforming DPO (94.05%, 4.35%) and MODPO (93.80%, 4.55%). On the broader UltraFeedback test set, CAMP attains 85.91% accuracy and 9.40% hallucination rate, the best among all methods. CAMP also achieves the highest LLM-judged truthfulness score (4.41/5) on TruthfulQA and (4.79/5) on HaluEval while maintaining competitive helpfulness (3.97 and 4.70 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAMP outperforms all seven baselines on hallucination rate across all three evaluation datasets. Against the strongest multi-objective baseline MODPO, CAMP improves TruthfulQA accuracy by 4.30% (85.92% vs. 82.38%) and reduces hallucination rate by 1.84% (9.79% vs. 11.63%), while also gaining 1.40% accuracy and 1.35% hallucination reduction on HaluEval. Against hallucination-specific methods, CAMP shows the largest margin over AlignHonesty (+7.58% accuracy, −3.80% hallucination on TruthfulQA) and FLAME (+5.50% accuracy, −2.82% hallucination), indicating that explicit conflict-aware margin regulation is more effective than uniform honesty or factuality penalties. Compared to CPO, CAMP achieves substantially better TruthfulQA accuracy (+8.56%) and hallucination rate (−5.39%), suggesting that constrained multi-objective optimization can be overly conservative. On helpfulness, CAMP maintains or slightly exceeds all baselines (e.g., 3.97 vs. 3.91 for MODPO on TruthfulQA), confirming that the adaptive margin does not sacrifice helpfulness for truthfulness. Statistical significance tests confirm that truthfulness gains are significant at p<0.001 for all comparisons except F-DPO (p=0.003 for MODPO, p<0.001 for others).

## Relation to Prior Work

CAMP builds directly on DPO, modifying its loss with a pair-dependent adaptive margin that shifts the effective preference boundary. It departs from multi-objective alignment methods (MODPO, CPO, MOPO) that impose uniform or globally controlled trade-offs across all samples, and from hallucination-specific alignment methods (AlignHonesty, FLAME, F-DPO) that optimize truthfulness without explicitly modeling when it conflicts with helpfulness. The key conceptual departure is the empirical observation that truthfulness-helpfulness conflicts arise in only ~11% of preference pairs, motivating selective, conflict-conditioned regulation rather than uniform multi-objective balancing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that objective conflicts are rare and moderate in strength challenges the implicit assumption in multi-objective alignment that conflicts are pervasive, suggesting that uniform trade-off mechanisms may unnecessarily weaken the primary objective on the vast majority of samples. Open questions include whether the conflict-aware formulation generalizes beyond the truthfulness-helpfulness pair to other objective combinations (e.g., safety vs. instruction following), whether it scales to larger models and more complex tasks like reasoning or tool use, and whether the manually tuned hyperparameter λ can be replaced by automatic conflict-strength estimation. The reliance on LLM-annotated scores as the conflict signal also leaves room for validation with human-annotated data.
