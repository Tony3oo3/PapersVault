## Problem

Sequential conformal prediction guarantees valid coverage only under residual exchangeability, which is routinely violated by temporal dependencies and distributional shifts in real-world time series. Existing remedies—temporal reweighting and similarity-based retrieval—assign continuous data-dependent weights whose misspecification distorts the empirical residual distribution and degrades coverage under abrupt shifts. The paper asks whether valid sequential conformal coverage can be achieved without any reweighting, by instead grouping residuals into locally exchangeable subgroups via a nonparametric distribution-matching criterion.

## Input / Output

Given a multivariate time series and a trained point predictor (Random Forest or LSTM), DistMatch segments the signed residuals into overlapping patches of window size w and pairs each patch ε̃_t with its target residual ε_{t+1}. At inference, an unseen patch is routed through a pre-built binary matching tree to a leaf, where a Quantile Regression Forest estimates conditional quantiles; the final output is an adaptive conformal interval [ŷ_{t+1} + q_{δ*}, ŷ_{t+1} + q_{1-α+δ*}] with δ* chosen to minimize interval width. After each prediction, the observed residual is appended to the assigned leaf and the QRF is refit, enabling continuous online adaptation without altering the tree structure.

## Method

DistMatch builds a binary tree over patch-target pairs by recursively selecting a split anchor s that maximizes a matching gain score (MG)—the count of patches within KS distance γ of s—and routing matched samples to the right child and the rest to the left. This KS-based partitioning is shown to bound the within-leaf patch-level KS diameter by 2γ, and under local stationarity and β-mixing assumptions the target-level divergence is bounded by 2Cγ + O(σ_mix), yielding a coverage guarantee that decomposes into a bias term (decreasing with finer γ) and a variance term (increasing with smaller leaves). Each leaf hosts an independent Quantile Regression Forest for online quantile estimation, and B bootstrap trees with diverse split anchors provide robustness under severe shifts by ensuring most unseen patches reach a matched leaf in at least one tree. The key distinction from reweighting-based methods is that DistMatch replaces continuous weight estimation with a nonparametric binary routing rule, preserving empirical distributions within each leaf while adapting locally through QRF updates.

## Results

On five real-world time-series datasets (Elec., Solar, Wind, META, NVDA) at α=0.1 with an RF point predictor, DistMatch achieves the best Winkler score and narrowest prediction interval on every dataset while maintaining valid coverage (≥0.90). Headline Winkler scores are 1.97 (Elec.), 1.54 (Solar), 2.15 (Wind), 0.12 (META), and 0.49 (NVDA), with corresponding interval widths of 0.27, 60.00, 69.04, 4.28, and 2.71. With an LSTM backbone (Table 2), DistMatch sustains 0.90 average coverage with Winkler scores of 1.15–2.05, whereas probabilistic forecasting baselines (MDN, GP, MCD) drop to 0.53–0.67 coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DistMatch outperforms all seven sequential CP baselines on Winkler score across all five datasets. The largest margins are against NexCP (e.g., Solar: 1.54 vs 3.74; NVDA: 0.49 vs 1.42) and CP/Copula (e.g., NVDA: 0.49 vs 2.94), which rely on global exchangeability or copula assumptions that break under non-stationarity. Against the closest competitors SPCI and KOWCPI, DistMatch still wins on Winkler (Solar: 1.54 vs 1.98/2.07; NVDA: 0.49 vs 1.05/0.72) and produces 30–45% narrower intervals. SPCI and EnbPI suffer coverage degradation under extreme shifts (Solar: 0.85/0.88; Wind: 0.83/0.85), while HopCPT and KOWCPI maintain coverage but at the cost of substantially wider intervals. Against probabilistic forecasting baselines (MDN, GP, MCD) with an LSTM backbone, DistMatch achieves 0.90 average coverage versus 0.53–0.67 for the baselines, confirming that distribution-free binning is more robust than parametric density modeling under shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

DistMatch extends the sequential conformal prediction framework of SPCI (Xu & Xie, 2023) and the split conformal prediction calibration paradigm (Papadopoulos et al., 2007), but departs fundamentally from the dominant reweighting-based approach (NexCP, KOWCPI, HopCPT) by replacing continuous data-dependent weights with a discrete, nonparametric binary routing rule based on the KS statistic. The key conceptual departure is introducing 'approximate local exchangeability' defined via KS distance—a strictly weaker sufficient condition than the TV-based global exchangeability used by Barber et al. (2023)—and showing it is both theoretically sufficient for valid quantile coverage and practically easier to satisfy. The method also repurposes Quantile Regression Forests (Meinshausen, 2006) as leaf-wise estimators, combining tree-based distribution matching with adaptive online quantile updates in a way not previously explored in the sequential CP literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a core practical limitation of sequential CP: reweighting schemes are sensitive to misspecification and degrade under abrupt or non-monotonic distribution shifts, which are common in energy and financial time series. By showing that a one-time KS-based tree construction suffices for valid online coverage (no retraining needed), DistMatch offers a simpler, more robust, and computationally cheaper alternative with transparent error bounds governed by a single interpretable threshold γ. Open questions include whether the local stationarity and β-mixing assumptions hold in highly non-stationary regimes (e.g., regime-switching financial data), whether adaptive thresholding or online tree restructuring can further improve robustness under extreme shifts, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-step or multi-dimensional prediction settings.
