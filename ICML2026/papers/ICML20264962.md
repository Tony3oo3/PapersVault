## Problem

Optimizing Log-Expectation-Exponential (Log-E-Exp) objectives that arise in extreme classification, partial AUC maximization, contrastive learning, and distributionally robust optimization is challenging because the inner expectation is over a gigantic number of items, making explicit evaluation infeasible. Existing stochastic methods suffer from three fundamental limitations: biased gradient estimators that fail to converge at small batch sizes, numerical instability from the exponential function, and convergence rates worse than O(1/√T) for convex problems due to exponentially large smoothness constants that coarse-grained analyses fail to control.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a finite dataset of n samples, each with a feature vector and a risk function s_i(w; ζ) defined over a distribution P_i (e.g., uniform over 360K class labels or 160K species), and produces optimized model parameters w along with per-sample dual variables ν ∈ R^n. At each iteration, only a mini-batch of B samples is processed for the outer sum, and a single random draw ζ from P_i approximates the inner expectation, keeping per-iteration cost at O(Bmd) for computing logits. The dual variables ν are updated via stochastic block coordinate descent, so only B of the n coordinates change per step, making the method scalable to very large n.

## Method

SCENT solves the min-min dual reformulation of the entropic risk by applying a Stochastic Proximal Mirror Descent (SPMD) update to the dual variable ν, using a Bregman divergence induced by φ(ν) = e^{-ν} that faithfully captures the exponential geometry of the objective. This choice yields a closed-form update for ν that is numerically stable (involving only logarithms of positive quantities) and automatically keeps ν bounded within the range of s_i, eliminating the need for explicit projection. The primal variable w is updated by a standard projected SGD step using an unbiased gradient estimator that leverages the updated ν. The algorithm is single-loop, uses a single time-scale step size (η_t ∝ α_t), and achieves an O(1/√T) convergence rate for convex problems—improving upon the O(1/T^{1/4}) rate of prior compositional methods. The framework unifies existing approaches: BSGD corresponds to α_t = ∞ (no noise correction), SCGD to α_t = γ'_t e^{-ν_t}, and SOX to coordinate-dependent step sizes, explaining their limitations from a geometric perspective.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative contribution is a theoretical complexity comparison (Table 1): SCENT achieves O(nε⁻²) iteration and sample complexity with O(1) mini-batch size in a single-loop framework, matching the best known rate (MSVR) while requiring no smoothness assumptions on the outer or inner functions and operating in a single loop. Empirically, on extreme classification (Glint360K with 360K classes, TreeOfLife-10M with 160K species) and partial AUC maximization (imbalanced CIFAR-10/100), SCENT consistently achieves the lowest cross-entropy and partial AUC loss among all baselines, with the gap over SOX being small but consistent across all datasets and hyperparameter settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCENT outperforms all four baselines on both extreme classification and partial AUC maximization. Against BSGD (Hu et al., 2020), SCENT achieves lower loss on both Glint360K and TreeOfLife-10M; BSGD itself is inconsistent, performing better than ASGD on Glint360K but worse on TreeOfLife-10M. Against SOX (Wang & Yang, 2022), SCENT is consistently better across all datasets and τ values, with the paper attributing the advantage to the geometry-aware SPMD update versus SOX's coordinate-wise step size choice (α_{i,t} = γ'_t e^{-ν_{i,t-1}}) that slows convergence. Against U-max (Fagan & Iyengar, 2018), SCENT clearly dominates on both tasks; U-max groups with ASGD and ASGD-Softplus as the weakest methods on partial AUC. Against ASGD-Softplus (Gladin et al., 2025), SCENT achieves substantially lower loss, particularly on partial AUC where the softplus approximation methods show large gaps. The theoretical advantage is also clear: SCENT matches MSVR's O(nε⁻²) rate but in a single loop without smoothness assumptions, while SOX requires O(nε⁻³) and a double loop.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the decades-old min-min reformulation of the Log-E-Exp function (Ben-Tal & Teboulle, 1986) and the compositional optimization framework (SCGD, SOX) that has been applied to entropic risk minimization since Wang et al. (2017). The key departure is replacing the standard SGD or Euclidean proximal update for the dual variable ν with a stochastic proximal mirror descent (SPMD) step using a Bregman divergence induced by φ(ν) = e^{-ν}, which the authors show is geometry-matched to the exponential structure of the objective. The paper further unifies prior methods by showing that BSGD (α=∞) and SCGD (α=γ'e^{-ν}) are special cases of the SCENT framework, providing a single lens through which to understand their limitations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(1/√T) convergence rate for convex compositional entropic risk is a meaningful improvement over the O(1/T^{1/4}) rate of prior compositional methods, and the clean characterization of when SPMD exponentially outperforms SGD (via the second-order moment ratio κ) gives practitioners a principled understanding of when the geometry-aware update matters most. However, the analysis is restricted to the convex setting, and the algorithm cannot handle n=∞ (e.g., online or streaming settings) without a neural normalizer workaround. Open questions include extending the convergence guarantees to non-convex objectives (where deep learning applications live) and whether the geometry-aware update provides similar advantages in the non-convex regime where most practical Log-E-Exp losses are optimized.
