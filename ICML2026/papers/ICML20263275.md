## Problem

Existing multi-task neural VRP solvers face two bottlenecks: (1) standard Transformer decoders rely on a fixed attention context, making fine-grained state-conditional modulation under frequent constraint switches difficult and leading to unstable training; (2) reinforcement learning with absolute rewards suffers from cross-variant reward-scale mismatch and shrinking within-instance return gaps as policies improve, weakening the learning signal and causing inefficient exploration in large combinatorial action spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263275/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The solver consumes a VRP instance graph with 2D node coordinates, per-customer constraint attributes (demand/backhaul amounts, time-window parameters, service durations), and a multi-hot constraint flag vector indicating which of 16 VRP variants (combinations of capacity, open-route, backhaul, duration-limit, and time-window constraints) are active. The decoder constructs a solution autoregressively, selecting one feasible node per step under a boolean action mask that enforces visit-once, capacity, time-window, distance-limit, and linehaul-before-backhaul precedence constraints, ultimately producing a set of sub-routes whose total Euclidean length is minimized. The framework is plug-and-play: it inserts a lightweight block into an existing encoder-decoder backbone without modifying the encoder or the feasibility masking logic.

## Method

PoMtVRS combines two synergistic components. First, a Preference-Gated Block (PGB) is inserted between the multi-head attention output and the compatibility scoring layer in the decoder; it applies head-wise sigmoid gating (computed from the context vector) to independently modulate each attention head after concatenation, maps the dynamic state (remaining capacities, current time, route length) into the embedding space and fuses it with the current node embedding as a state-conditioned anchor, and then performs nonlinear residual refinement via normalization and a feed-forward (or SigLU/gated MLP) layer to stabilize training. Second, the training objective replaces REINFORCE with preference optimization: for each instance, P candidate routes are generated via multi-start rollouts, rewards are centered within-instance to form pairwise preference labels, and a Bradley-Terry likelihood is applied to the scaled log-likelihood differences (temperature α=0.03) between candidate pairs, encouraging the policy to assign higher probability to better routes while being insensitive to absolute reward scales. The two components work in synergy—PGB provides constraint-conditioned representation modulation that makes the decoder more expressive under heterogeneous constraints, while PO provides a stable, relative learning signal that mitigates gradient interference across tasks.

## Results

On 1K test instances across 16 VRP variants, PoMtVRS(CaDA) achieves the best average optimality gap of 1.405% (VRP50) and 2.205% (VRP100), reducing the CaDA backbone's gap from 1.721%/2.844% and outperforming the prior best MoSES(CaDA) (1.515%/2.631%). PoMtVRS(MVMoE) similarly improves MVMoE from 2.227%/3.680% to 1.809%/2.951% average gap. On the CVRPLIB real-world benchmark (N up to 1001), PoMtVRS(CaDA) achieves the lowest average gap of 8.326%, ahead of MoSES(CaDA) at 8.441% and CaDA at 9.230%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoMtVRS(CaDA) achieves the lowest optimality gap on the majority of 16 VRP variants at both N=50 and N=100, outperforming the strongest prior neural method MoSES(CaDA) by 0.11 pp (VRP50) and 0.43 pp (VRP100) in average gap while also reducing inference time (4s vs 7–8s per instance). Against the CaDA backbone itself, PoMtVRS(CaDA) yields consistent improvements across all variants (e.g., CVRP-100 gap drops from 1.601% to 0.926%; VRPTW-100 from 2.579% to 2.151%). PoMtVRS(MVMoE) improves over MVMoE on all 16 variants but generally trails PoMtVRS(CaDA), confirming that the framework benefits more from a stronger backbone. All RouteFinder variants (RF-POMO, RF-MoE, RF-TE) and MTPOMO are outperformed by both PoMtVRS instantiations on average gap. On the CVRPLIB real-world benchmark, PoMtVRS(CaDA) leads all neural baselines at every scale partition (N<251: 4.561%, 251≤N<501: 7.865%, 501<N≤1001: 12.651%), with the largest margin over CaDA appearing at larger scales (e.g., 12.651% vs 14.199% for N>501). On zero-shot unseen constraints (Multi-Depot, Mixed Backhauls, and their composition), PoMtVRS(CaDA) again achieves the best gap at both scales, e.g., 34.807% vs 37.571% (CaDA) on MD-VRP50.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoMtVRS sits at the intersection of two research threads: multi-task neural VRP solvers (MVMoE, RouteFinder, CaDA, MoSES) and preference optimization adapted from the LLM alignment literature (DPO, SimPO, and early NCO-PO work by Pan et al. 2025a and Liao et al. 2025). Architecturally, the Preference-Gated Block draws on gated-attention mechanisms (Qiu et al., 2026) but applies them post-concatenation for plug-and-play insertion, while the training objective replaces REINFORCE with a Bradley-Terry pairwise preference loss over within-instance candidate solutions. The paper positions itself as a drop-in enhancement rather than a new solver family, validating this by instantiating on two distinct backbones (MVMoE and CaDA) without modifying their core architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design lowers the barrier for practitioners to upgrade existing multi-task VRP solvers without re-deriving training pipelines, and the consistent 0.3–0.6% gap reduction across all 16 variants at both scales is practically meaningful for deployment. However, the framework is still validated only on single-depot instances up to N=100 for training, and the zero-shot generalization to unseen constraints (multi-depot, mixed backhauls) shows larger gaps, suggesting the approach has not yet been stress-tested on truly complex real-world constraint systems. Open questions include whether the PO objective scales to larger instance sizes where the number of candidate solutions per instance grows, and whether the PGB's head-wise gating generalizes to deeper or multi-layer decoders.
