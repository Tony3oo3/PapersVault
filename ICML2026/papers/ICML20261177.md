## Problem

Prior Code-as-Policy (CaP) systems for robot manipulation rely heavily on high-level, human-designed primitives, making it impossible to disentangle agent capability from designer-provided scaffolding. It remains unclear how agent performance degrades as such abstractions are removed, and whether increased test-time computation (iterative debugging, visual grounding, skill synthesis) can compensate for operating over low-level interfaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261177/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language task instruction and RGB-D camera observations (plus depth intrinsics and camera pose), and in multi-turn settings also receives execution traces (stdout/stderr), raw RGB frames, or structured text descriptions from a Visual Differencing Module. The output is an executable Python program that composes perception primitives (SAM3 segmentation, Molmo 2 pointing, OpenCV/Open3D) and control primitives (PyRoki IK solvers, motion planners, gripper commands), which is executed in a REPL-style sandbox where each 'turn' runs the program to completion before the next observation is provided.

## Method

CaP-X is a four-component framework: (1) CaP-Gym provides an interactive environment integrating 187 tasks from Robosuite, LIBERO-PRO, and BEHAVIOR under a shared primitive API, where a code 'turn' corresponds to one agent-environment interaction; (2) CaP-Bench evaluates 12 frontier models across 8 tiers (S1–S4 single-turn, M1–M4 multi-turn) that independently vary primitive abstraction (human macros vs. atomic primitives), interaction mode (single vs. multi-turn with execution feedback), and perceptual grounding (privileged state, raw RGB, or VDM text); (3) CaP-Agent0 is a training-free agentic harness combining the Visual Differencing Module (converting visual observations into structured natural language rather than feeding raw pixels), an auto-synthesized task-agnostic skill library (9 primitives extracted from successful rollouts across all 12 models), and parallel multi-model reasoning (9 single-model or 3×3 multi-model candidate generations synthesized by a central agent); (4) CaP-RL applies GRPO to post-train a Qwen2.5-Coder-7B base model using verifiable environment rewards on privileged S1-tier tasks, with the learned code-action-space transferring directly to real robots (Franka Panda) without retraining.

## Results

On LIBERO-PRO, CaP-Agent0 (training-free) achieves 0.22/0.18 (Pos/Task) on libero-object, 0.26/0.17 on libero-goal, and 0.12/0.14 on libero-spatial, outperforming π0.5 on task perturbations (0.18 vs 0.01, 0.17 vs 0.00, 0.14 vs 0.01) and matching or exceeding it on position perturbations for libero-object (0.22 vs 0.17). On BEHAVIOR mobile manipulation, CaP-Agent0 reaches 84% navigation and 72% task success on Pick up Soda Can, matching the human expert (80%/72%), and 80%/56% on Pick up Radio, substantially exceeding the S3 baseline (72%/24%). CaP-RL post-training with GRPO lifts Qwen2.5-Coder-7B from 25% to 80% success on Cube Lift in simulation and from 24% to 84% on the real Franka robot, approaching the 92–93% human expert level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OpenVLA and π0, CaP-Agent0 wins decisively: both VLA baselines score 0.00 across all six LIBERO-PRO splits, while CaP-Agent0 achieves 0.12–0.26, reflecting that these training-based policies fail entirely under the perturbation conditions. Against π0.5, the strongest VLA baseline, CaP-Agent0 wins on all three task-perturbation splits (0.18 vs 0.01, 0.17 vs 0.00, 0.14 vs 0.01) and on libero-object position perturbation (0.22 vs 0.17), but loses on libero-goal position (0.26 vs 0.38) and libero-spatial position (0.12 vs 0.20). The paper attributes CaP-Agent0's robustness to instruction perturbations to its training-free, code-based approach that reasons over the language instruction at test time, whereas VLAs are sensitive to instruction distribution shifts. On BEHAVIOR, CaP-Agent0 matches or exceeds the human expert on the soda-can task (84% nav vs 80%) and substantially outperforms the S3 single-turn baseline on both tasks (e.g., 56% vs 24% task success on radio pickup). The CaP-RL results show that even a small 7B model, after GRPO post-training, closes most of the gap to human experts in simulation (80% vs 93% on Cube Lift) and transfers to the real robot (84% vs 92%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Code-as-Policy paradigm initiated by Liang et al. (2023) and Singh et al. (2023), which used LLMs to generate robot control programs over human-designed high-level primitives. The key departure is systematic: CaP-X removes the confound of designer scaffolding by evaluating agents across a spectrum from human-crafted macros to atomic low-level primitives, and introduces multi-turn interaction with structured execution feedback and visual grounding as first-class evaluation axes. For the RL component, CaP-RL applies GRPO (Shao et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2025) directly to the coding agent via physics-simulation rewards, rather than using the LLM to generate reward code for a separate policy as in Eureka-style work. The training-free CaP-Agent0 framework draws on agentic tool accumulation (Voyager), ensemble reasoning, and visual differencing to recover structure at test time without task-specific training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CaP-X provides the first open-access, systematic benchmark for disentangling agent capability from designer-provided scaffolding in embodied code generation, a gap that has obscured progress in the CaP literature. The finding that test-time compute (multi-turn debugging, visual differencing into text, auto-synthesized skill libraries, parallel reasoning) can close the gap to human experts even at low-level primitives suggests a scalable path toward generalist embodied agents without per-task training. Open questions remain for contact-rich manipulation requiring tight visual servoing, the optimal hybrid between CaP and VLA policies, and whether the code-action-space interface generalizes beyond tabletop and simple mobile manipulation to truly open-ended environments.
