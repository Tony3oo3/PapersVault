## Problem

Standard Clifford-Steerable CNNs (CSCNNs) suffer from an incomplete kernel basis: their implicit parameterization via geometric-product-based networks cannot generate certain angular frequency components (e.g., frequency-2 for vector-vector interactions under O(2,0)), making single-layer kernels over-constrained and limiting expressivity. The original authors suggested recovering missing bases through consecutive convolutions, but this doubles parameter count and is impractical. This paper asks whether the kernel basis can be completed without increasing depth.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivector feature fields over pseudo-Euclidean grids: vector velocity + scalar pressure (128×128 for Navier-Stokes, 64×64 for SWE-1, 192×96 for SWE-5), vector electric + bivector magnetic fields (32×32×32 for 3D Maxwell), or electromagnetic bivector fields (128×128 spacetime for relativistic 2+1D Maxwell). Timesteps are treated as feature channels, so the model predicts future state channels from previous ones (e.g., 4-step history → 1-step or 5-step prediction). A key structural detail is that the convolution kernel is additionally conditioned on a global equivariant summary (mean-pooled multivector) of the entire input field, making the operator nonlinear while preserving E(p,q)-equivariance.

## Method

C-CSCNNs augment the standard CSCNN kernel network K̂ with an auxiliary multivector input ζ = T[f] obtained by applying an O(p,q)-equivariant pooling operator T (mean pooling with circular masking) to the input feature field. The kernel network then computes geometric products between the relative position vector and ζ, which propagates angular information φ into all multivector grades including the scalar (grade-0) component—restoring the missing frequency-2 terms in the kernel basis. The paper derives the full G-steerability constraint for conditional kernels (Lemma 4.2) and shows it is satisfied by composing an O(p,q)-equivariant CEGNN kernel network with the standard CSCNN kernel head H, requiring no analytical kernel derivation. The architecture is a ResNet backbone (8 residual blocks, 7×7 kernels) where each standard convolution is replaced by a conditional Clifford-steerable convolution, with the only additional cost being the negligible mean-pooling computation.

## Results

On the 2D Navier-Stokes task, C-CSCNN achieves the lowest MSE of 1.80e-04 at N=4096 training trajectories, outperforming Transolver (3.40e-04) and standard CSCNN (4.50e-04). On the 1-step Shallow-Water task, C-CSCNN reaches 0.0519 MSE at N=2048, beating Transolver (0.0594) and CSCNN (0.1132). On the 3D Maxwell and relativistic 2D Maxwell tasks, C-CSCNN achieves second-best MSE (4.51e-04 and 0.2519 respectively) behind only Transolver (3.99e-04 and 0.17034), while still substantially outperforming CSCNN (6.82e-04 and 0.3494). The advantage of C-CSCNN over standard CSCNN grows with increasing training set size across all four tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-CSCNN outperforms the direct baseline standard CSCNN on all four tasks by a wide margin (e.g., 2.5× lower MSE on NS at N=4096, 2.2× on SWE-1 at N=2048), with the gap widening as more training data is available. Against Transolver (the strongest non-equivariant baseline), C-CSCNN wins decisively on the fluid dynamics tasks (NS and SWE-1) but trails by ~13% on MW3 and ~48% on MW2, suggesting attention-based architectures retain an edge for more non-local electrodynamics dynamics. C-CSCNN also beats CViT, ResNet, Clifford ResNet, O(n)-steerable ResNet, FNO, G-FNO, and Swin-Transformer on all tasks where they are compared. On the 5-step Shallow-Water rollout (SWE-5), the 55M-parameter C-CSCNN achieves 2.94% relative L² error, outperforming UNO (440M params, 3.79%), FNO (268M, 3.97%), and U-Net_att (148M, 5.68%), while remaining competitive with CViT-L (92M, 1.56%) and U-F2Net (344M, 1.89%). Inference overhead from conditioning is negligible: C-CSCNN is only 0.87% slower than unconditioned CSCNN on the Maxwell 3D task (54.30 ms vs 53.83 ms per sample on A100).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Clifford-Steerable CNNs (Zhdanov et al., ICML 2024) by identifying a fundamental expressivity gap: the implicit kernel basis is incomplete, missing higher-frequency angular components (e.g., frequency-2 for vector-vector interactions under O(2,0)). The fix draws on the implicit parameterization technique from Zhdanov et al. (NeurIPS 2023) but generalizes it to conditional kernels that take auxiliary multivector features from the input field as additional inputs to the kernel network. The Clifford algebra–equivariance correspondence from Ruhe et al. (NeurIPS 2023) underpins the entire construction, and the conditional convolution framework is positioned alongside message-passing and point-convolution approaches, with template matching via a translation-invariant pooling operator T as the key efficiency trade-off.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical diagnosis (kernel basis incompleteness) and a minimal, low-overhead fix (mean-pooling conditioning adds only ~0.9% inference cost) that consistently improves equivariant PDE solvers across fluid dynamics and electrodynamics benchmarks. The completeness conjecture for general O(p,q) remains open, and the authors acknowledge that richer conditioning operators (learnable pooling, hierarchical/region-based conditioning) are likely needed to match attention-based methods on non-local tasks like electrodynamics. The framework's generality—applicable to any problem on pseudo-Euclidean domains with O(p,q) symmetry—suggests broader utility beyond PDE forecasting, though the current ResNet backbone and simple pooling leave clear room for architectural upgrades.
