## Problem

Existing person retrieval methods either rely on single-shot text queries that are often incomplete or vague, or use chat-based Q&A that never lets users directly inspect and correct retrieved candidates. No prior paradigm supports users providing visual corrective feedback on specific retrieved images during a multi-turn conversation, and no multimodal conversational dataset exists to benchmark such interactive retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260184/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a structured multi-turn dialogue: an initial free-form text description, a sequence of system-generated questions with short user answers, and a sequence of (reference image, corrective feedback text) pairs where the reference image is a previously retrieved candidate and the feedback describes how its visual attributes should change toward the target. The output is a ranked list of person images from a gallery, with the model trained to align each individual turn's aggregated context embedding against the target image's global ViT CLS feature, encouraging the system to converge on the correct identity within as few turns as possible.

## Method

MNEMO adopts a hierarchical architecture built on InternVL 2.5-1B (ViT + MLP + LLM). The Atomic Turn Encoding (ATE) module encodes each dialogue turn independently: text-only turns (initial description + QA pairs) are concatenated into one LLM pass, while each visual feedback turn is processed separately by projecting ViT patch embeddings through an MLP and concatenating them with the feedback text tokens before an LLM forward pass, avoiding the quadratic attention cost of a single long sequence. The Dialogue Memory Aggregation (DMA) module then fuses turn-level representations using two groups of learnable memory queries—static queries shared across all turns to capture common semantic patterns, and dynamic queries that are updated iteratively (Q_{i+1}^{dyn} = O_i^{dyn}) to propagate evolving conversational context—via a single cross-attention layer followed by two Transformer blocks per turn. Training uses a two-stage procedure (pretraining on MALS, finetuning on MInterPEDES) with a turn-wise N-ITC + SDM contrastive loss that aligns every turn's pooled memory output with the target image's CLS embedding, explicitly encouraging early convergence.

## Results

On MInterPEDES, MNEMO achieves the best performance across all three retrieval scenarios. In the MInterPR setting (M≠0, N≠0), MNEMO reaches 92.26% R-1 and 80.31% mAP, surpassing the strongest baseline ICL (88.73% R-1, 77.53% mAP) by 3.53% and 2.78% respectively. In the ChatPR setting, MNEMO attains 78.09% R-1 and 69.18% mAP, outperforming DiaNA by 2.42% on R-1. Under the classical TPR setting, MNEMO is competitive (30.89% R-1, 29.89% mAP), with the best mAP among all methods despite being designed for multimodal dialogue.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary MInterPR task, MNEMO decisively outperforms all baselines that support multimodal interaction: it beats ICL (CVPR'25) by 3.53% on R-1 (92.26 vs. 88.73) and 2.78% on mAP (80.31 vs. 77.53), and LLaVA-ReID (ICML'25) by 5.25% on R-1. In the ChatPR setting, MNEMO surpasses DiaNA (CVPR'25) by 2.42% on R-1 (78.09 vs. 75.67) and 2.29% on mAP, while also exceeding ICL by 4.53% on R-1. Under the classical TPR setting, MNEMO is roughly on par with the strongest baselines (RDE, ICL, LLaVA-ReID all score 31.05–31.26% R-1 vs. MNEMO's 30.89%), with MNEMO achieving the best mAP (29.89%). On the generalization TPR benchmarks (CUHK-PEDES, ICFG-PEDES, RSTPReid), MNEMO trails RDE by 1.5–3.0% on R-1, which the authors attribute to the smaller pretraining corpus (1.5M vs. 400M pairs), but it matches or slightly exceeds RDE on RSTPReid mAP (51.06 vs. 50.88). Methods that do not support compositional vision-language understanding (IRRA, TBPS-CLIP, RDE, DiaNA) cannot be evaluated on MInterPR at all, highlighting a fundamental paradigm gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining MInterPR, a retrieval paradigm that unifies text-based person retrieval (TPR) and chat-based person retrieval (ChatPR) as special cases, while adding a third interaction mode: user-provided corrective feedback on visually retrieved candidates. Methodologically, MNEMO repurposes the InternVL 2.5-1B ViT-MLP-LLM backbone and standard N-ITC/SDM contrastive losses from prior TPR work, but introduces a hierarchical ATE+DMA architecture that replaces the full-dialogue concatenation strategy used by DiaNA, ICL, and LLaVA-ReID with per-turn encoding plus memory-query aggregation, directly addressing the quadratic attention cost that makes long multimodal dialogues impractical. The dataset construction pipeline (MLLM-generated visual difference descriptions fine-tuned from GPT-4o exemplars, merged with ChatPedes Q&A turns) is a novel data-engineering approach with no direct precedent in the person retrieval literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MInterPEDES is the first benchmark enabling evaluation of multimodal interactive person retrieval, filling a clear gap between single-shot TPR and Q&A-only ChatPR benchmarks. The ATE+DMA design offers a practical 3× inference speedup over full-dialogue concatenation while matching or exceeding its accuracy, making iterative multimodal retrieval feasible in latency-sensitive surveillance deployments. Key limitations temper the impact: corrective feedback is synthetically generated (only a 10-user/100-identity real-user study validates generalization), pretraining relies on the small MALS corpus (1.5M pairs vs. CLIP's 400M), and the gallery size (37K images) is modest for real-world scale. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to larger open-world galleries, whether human feedback quality can match synthetic feedback at scale, and whether the memory-query mechanism generalizes beyond person retrieval to other fine-grained interactive search tasks.
