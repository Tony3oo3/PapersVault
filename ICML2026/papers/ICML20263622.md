## Problem

Most multimodal learning assumes redundant paired views (e.g., image-caption), but many high-stakes applications require combining complementary modalities that provide distinct, non-redundant evidence (e.g., an X-ray plus medical history). In this Complementary Multimodal Classification (CMC) setting, existing explainable-by-design prototype methods suffer from a forced accuracy-interpretability trade-off: a single shared similarity metric fails under asymmetric, class-conditional evidence, leading to prototype collapse, modality dominance, dead units, and drift under rare cases. No prior modality-agnostic prototype framework was designed to handle arbitrary subsets of complementary modalities while preserving faithful case-based explanations.

## Input / Output

CAMP accepts arbitrary observed subsets of tabular, text, and image inputs per instance (modality-agnostic inference), processing them through frozen Swin-L and DeBERTa-v3 encoders in the lightweight regime or fine-tuning end-to-end. Beyond the predicted class label, the model natively outputs per-instance modality importance gates (a simplex over observed modalities) and the top-r retrieved prototypes with their nearest training-instance projections, providing a case-based explanation without any auxiliary surrogate model. Because fusion gates and similarity queries operate strictly on the observed modality subset, a single trained model handles any missing-data configuration without imputation heuristics.

## Method

CAMP maps each modality through a dedicated encoder and projection into a shared latent space, fuses them via per-instance softmax gates, and predicts using a class-partitioned prototype bank with a top-r nearest-prototype head. The key training innovation is a four-block structural objective: (1) class-wise entropic optimal transport (Sinkhorn) aligns modality-specific prototype-usage distributions at the aggregate class level rather than forcing instance-level alignment, preserving per-instance complementarity while ensuring cross-modal coherence; (2) geometry losses enforce within-class prototype separation and cross-class margins to prevent collapse; (3) allocation losses (KL coverage + entropy purity) eliminate dead prototypes and enforce class specialization; (4) anchor-based stabilizers bound outlier gradient influence and prototype drift. Theoretical guarantees show these mechanisms jointly eliminate CMC-specific degeneracies without restricting expressivity, and a sub-1M-parameter frozen head matches or exceeds >100M-parameter AutoGluon ensembles on 11 of 16 benchmarks.

## Results

In the frozen-encoder regime (CAMP-F, ~660K trainable parameters on fixed Swin-L/DeBERTa-v3 embeddings), CAMP achieves a suite average of 0.814 and MRR of 0.448 across 16 CMC benchmarks, matching or exceeding the >100M-parameter AutoGluon ensemble (0.808 avg, 0.296 MRR) on 11 of 16 datasets. Under end-to-end fine-tuning (CAMP-E), CAMP reaches 0.842 average and 1.000 MRR, establishing state-of-the-art performance on all 16 benchmarks. The largest end-to-end margin over AutoGluon is +0.068 AUC on PTech (749 samples), and CAMP-F more than doubles the strongest prototype baseline ProtoMedX in MRR (0.448 vs. 0.185).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AutoGluon-Multimodal (Tang et al., 2024b), the >100M-parameter AutoML ensemble, CAMP-F with fewer than 1M trainable parameters matches or exceeds it on 11 of 16 datasets (suite avg 0.814 vs. 0.808; MRR 0.448 vs. 0.296), with AutoGluon retaining a marginal edge (<0.02) only on single-modality-dominated tasks like CCD and Airbnb. CAMP-E extends this to a clear win on all 16 benchmarks (0.842 avg), with the largest gap of +0.068 AUC on the low-data PTech dataset. Against the Tabular-Text Transformer (Bonnier, 2024), both CAMP variants outperform TTT on average (0.814/0.842 vs. 0.779), with CAMP-F winning on 12 of 16 individual datasets. Against the closest prototype-based competitor ProtoMedX (Pellicer et al., 2025), CAMP-F more than doubles the MRR (0.448 vs. 0.185) and improves the suite average by 0.043, demonstrating that the class-wise optimal transport alignment is the critical differentiator beyond standard case-based similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAMP extends the prototype-network lineage (Snell et al. 2017; Chen et al. 2019; Li et al. 2018) from single-modality or fixed-pair settings to the complementary multimodal regime, where modalities provide distinct label evidence rather than describing one another. It departs from CLIP-style instance-level alignment by instead aligning class-wise prototype-usage distributions via Sinkhorn-regularized optimal transport, and generalizes ProtoMedX (Pellicer et al. 2025) from a fixed two-modality medical task to arbitrary modality subsets with a modality-agnostic inference protocol. The theoretical contribution composes classical tools—Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality, margin preservation, Pinsker's inequality, and anchor stability—into a CMC-specific structural guarantee that no single prior work provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAMP challenges the widely held assumption that faithful interpretability requires sacrificing accuracy or scale: a sub-1M-parameter explainable head matches or exceeds >100M-parameter AutoML ensembles across 16 diverse benchmarks while natively exposing case-based evidence. This is particularly relevant for high-stakes deployments (healthcare, fraud, safety) where regulations like the EU AI Act demand inspectable reasoning. Key limitations include formal guarantees restricted to the frozen-encoder regime (end-to-end gains remain empirical), no user studies validating human-facing explanation efficacy, and evaluation limited to discrete classification with strong pretrained encoders. Open questions include extending the OT coherence principle to regression, continuous concept drift, and whether the structural bias transfers to architectures beyond prototype heads.
