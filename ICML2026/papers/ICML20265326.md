## Problem

Existing dynamic Gaussian Splatting methods model motion with first-order (constant-velocity) approximations, causing significant estimation drift in long-term non-uniform motion scenarios such as professional sports. Additionally, scene-flow-based supervision inherently couples depth and motion in 2D image-space displacements, so minor errors in either propagate into large 3D flow errors, and labeled 3D scene flow data remain scarce for effective generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265326/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular video frames resized to 518×420 (preserving aspect ratio for pixel-aligned 3DGS prediction) along with camera parameters and point-cloud priors from the pretrained PAGE backbone. It renders novel-view RGB images at arbitrary target timestamps, and additionally produces depth maps and 2D optical flow as byproducts of the differentiable TGs-Flow projection. Supervision relies on pseudo-ground-truth optical flow from a pre-trained DPFlow model rather than requiring any 3D scene flow annotations.

## Method

TGsF introduces Taylor-Gaussians (TGs) that extend each 3D Gaussian's mean with a quadratic temporal term (μ + v·Δt + ½a·Δt²) and a 1D-Gaussian temporal opacity, providing a second-order approximation of non-uniform motion. The TGs-Flow module analytically projects these 3D motion parameters into 2D optical flow via a differentiable alpha-composition of per-Gaussian displacements, decoupling depth and motion supervision and eliminating the need for scarce 3D scene flow labels. The feed-forward framework (FF-TGsF) uses a DPT-based TGs-Head on the PAGE backbone to predict all Taylor-Gaussian parameters, trained with a composite loss combining RGB reconstruction (L2+LPIPS), scale-invariant depth, optical flow, finite-difference acceleration, and isotropic regularization. The acceleration pseudo-label is derived from the temporal derivative of estimated optical flow, enabling explicit second-order supervision without additional 3D annotations.

## Results

On SNU-HARD (the most challenging long-term non-uniform motion benchmark), TGsF achieves 28.73 PSNR on key frames and 25.19 PSNR on Middle-8 frames, outperforming the strongest baseline NeoVerse by +2.38 and +2.55 PSNR respectively. On DAVIS, TGsF reaches 30.11 PSNR (key frames) and 24.92 PSNR (Middle-8), surpassing NeoVerse by +0.74 and +1.66 PSNR. On DyCheck-iPhone, TGsF scores 20.96 PSNR vs. 19.56 for NeoVerse. On SNU-Extreme (16-frame intervals), TGsF achieves 23.19 PSNR, outperforming all 3DGS-based methods but trailing the pixel-based frame interpolation method AMT (24.43 PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGsF consistently outperforms all five dynamic 3DGS baselines (Splatter a Video, StreamSplat, MoVieS, Bullet, NeoVerse) across every dynamic benchmark. The largest margins appear on SNU-HARD Middle-8 frames, where TGsF leads NeoVerse by +2.55 PSNR and MoVieS by +3.20 PSNR, confirming the benefit of second-order (acceleration) modeling under large temporal gaps. On DAVIS key frames, TGsF edges out NeoVerse by +0.74 PSNR and MoVieS by +2.80 PSNR. On DyCheck-iPhone, TGsF achieves 20.96 PSNR vs. 19.56 (NeoVerse) and 18.46 (MoVieS). The one notable exception is SNU-Extreme (16-frame intervals), where the pixel-based frame interpolation method AMT (24.43 PSNR) and FILM (23.71 PSNR) still surpass TGsF (23.19 PSNR), though TGsF leads all 3DGS-based competitors by 3+ PSNR. Static baselines (pixelSplat, AnySplat, NoPoSplat, PF3plat) are far behind on dynamic scenes, with DyCheck-iPhone PSNR dropping to 7.6–10.2, underscoring the necessity of explicit motion modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting line of work (StreamSplat, MoVieS, Bullet, NeoVerse) by replacing the ubiquitous first-order velocity assumption with a second-order Taylor expansion (velocity + acceleration) for Gaussian motion representation. It departs from prior scene-flow-based supervision by introducing TGs-Flow, a differentiable module that projects 3D Taylor-Gaussian motion into 2D optical flow, thereby decoupling depth and motion supervision and eliminating the need for scarce 3D scene flow labels. The feed-forward architecture builds on the DPT backbone from NeoVerse and uses PAGE for geometric priors, positioning itself within the recent wave of feed-forward 3DGS methods while addressing their shared weakness on long-term non-uniform motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled 2D optical flow supervision strategy is practically significant: it allows the framework to leverage large-scale, well-trained 2D flow models (e.g., DPFlow trained on Chairs+Spring+Things+KITTI) without requiring any 3D scene flow annotations, a data bottleneck that has constrained prior methods. The ablation showing that second-order Taylor terms yield a ~2 dB PSNR jump over first-order on SNU-HARD, and that the method's PSNR degrades far less under high acceleration (Fig. 7), provides concrete evidence that acceleration modeling is not merely a theoretical nicety but a practical necessity for sports and other high-dynamics applications. Open questions include whether the isotropic-shape assumption (B_{i,t}B_{i,t_k}^{-1}≈I) holds under truly extreme deformation, whether third-order terms become viable as flow estimators improve, and whether the approach extends to multi-object or articulated motion beyond rigid per-Gaussian trajectories.
