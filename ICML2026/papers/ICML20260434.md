## Problem

Bayesian Additive Spanning Trees (BAST) extend BART to graph-constrained domains by replacing axis-aligned splits with cuts on spanning trees, but they still produce piecewise-constant functions that cannot represent smoothly varying signals without incurring discretization bias. This hard-partitioning limitation forces BAST to introduce an increasing number of cuts to approximate smooth graph-indexed functions, degrading posterior concentration. The paper asks whether a principled relaxation can retain the geometric interpretability of tree-based models while enabling continuous, smooth variation along the graph.

## Input / Output

The method takes noisy observations y_i at n locations on a constrained domain together with an adjacency graph G encoding neighborhood relationships (e.g., road connectivity, spatial adjacency, or geodesic proximity), which may be directed and include long-range links such as flyovers. It produces a regression surface estimate as an additive ensemble of M probabilistically-routed spanning tree components, with posterior mean predictions at new locations computed via Monte Carlo averaging over posterior samples of node-level coefficients and routing weights. Unlike Euclidean methods, locality is governed by tree distance along the sampled spanning tree rather than ambient spatial coordinates.

## Method

PR-BAST replaces discrete edge deletions on spanning trees with continuous probabilistic routing: each component computes g_m^soft(x_i) = Σ_u exp(−τ_m d_{T_m}(x_i,u)²) / Z · β_{m,u}, where d_{T_m} is tree distance and τ_m is a temperature parameter that interpolates between global averaging (τ→0) and hard piecewise-constant BAST behavior (τ→∞). Node-level coefficients β_m carry a tree-structured Gaussian prior with precision λ_m L_{T_m} + η_m I, making each component a Gaussian random field with sparse, tree-structured covariance that enables scalable inference via sparse Cholesky factorization. Spanning trees are sampled from the adjacency graph using randomized edge costs (minimum spanning tree with i.i.d. Uniform(0,1) weights), and the full posterior is obtained via MCMC with Gibbs updates for β and σ², Metropolis-Hastings for τ, λ, and tree structure (edge-swap moves), and a standard backfitting scheme across the M-component ensemble. Theoretically, PR-BAST achieves posterior contraction at rate n^{-1/2} log^c(n) under graph-smooth truth, strictly faster than BAST's rate of √(g_n* log n / n) where the cut complexity g_n* grows with n for smooth signals.

## Results

On the NYC Yellow Taxi 8 AM pick-up dataset (263 zones, January 2019), PR-BAST achieves RMSE 0.6108 and sMAPE 0.4892, outperforming BAST (RMSE 0.7341), BART (RMSE 0.8226), and GP (RMSE 0.9267). On the simulated one-way road network with flyovers (22×28 grid), PR-BAST achieves RMSE 0.100 versus BAST's 0.388, BART's 0.393, and GP's 0.521. On the perforated-grid bottleneck simulation (26×26, σ=0.35), PR-BAST reaches RMSE 0.154 compared to BAST (0.197), BART (0.222), and GP (0.350). Computationally, PR-BAST runs in ~2.4 minutes versus ~5.6 minutes for BAST, while BART and GP run in under a minute due to optimized C++ implementations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PR-BAST consistently outperforms all three baselines across every experiment and metric. Against BAST (the direct predecessor), PR-BAST improves RMSE by 17% on NYC taxi data (0.6108 vs. 0.7341) and by a factor of ~3.9× on the flyover simulation (0.100 vs. 0.388), where BAST's piecewise-constant regions cannot capture smooth transitions across long-range flyover connections. Against the Euclidean GP, PR-BAST achieves substantially lower error (e.g., 0.6108 vs. 0.9267 RMSE on NYC taxi; 0.154 vs. 0.350 on the bottleneck grid), because the GP's isotropic RBF kernel cannot respect non-Euclidean adjacency or one-way connectivity. Against BART, PR-BAST wins on all settings (e.g., 0.6108 vs. 0.8226 RMSE on NYC taxi), as axis-aligned splits are fundamentally misaligned with graph-structured domains. The advantage is largest in the flyover setting, where Euclidean distance is deliberately decoupled from network distance, and smallest in the bottleneck setting where the signal is closer to piecewise-constant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BART → BAST lineage by replacing hard spanning-tree edge deletions (Luo et al., 2021b) with continuous probabilistic routing weights along tree distances, yielding a principled relaxation that recovers BAST in the large-temperature limit. The Gaussian random field interpretation (Proposition 2.2) connects PR-BAST to graph-based Gaussian Markov random fields and Laplacian-regularized priors (Zhu et al., 2003; Smola & Kondor, 2003), while the posterior contraction analysis builds on the Ghosal–Ghosh–van der Vaart (2000) framework and the BAST-specific results of Luo et al. (2021a). The key departure from BAST is the shift from low-dimensional component-level parameters to high-dimensional node-level coefficients regularized by a tree-Laplacian prior, trading combinatorial partitioning for structured shrinkage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PR-BAST addresses a concrete approximation-bias limitation of BAST for smoothly varying signals on graphs, with a clean theoretical separation showing that the cut-complexity bottleneck g_n* is eliminated entirely under probabilistic routing. Practical limitations include the current pure-R implementation (roughly 2× slower than BAST, and an order of magnitude slower than optimized C++ BART/GP), and the theoretical results require bounded-degree and bounded-diameter graph assumptions that may not hold for highly heterogeneous urban networks. Open questions include scalability to graphs with tens of thousands of nodes, adaptive selection of the number of additive components M, and extension to multi-dimensional or time-varying covariates on the graph.
