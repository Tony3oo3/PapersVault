## Problem

Standard supervised learning for graph node classification mixes feature-specific, structure-specific, and joint-input signals into a single representation, causing models to rely on spurious single-input correlations that break under distribution shifts. No prior graph OOD detection method explicitly decomposes predictive information into these three components to isolate the shift-indicative joint signal while filtering out misleading feature-only or structure-only cues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264776/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G=(X,A) with node feature matrix X∈R^{n×d} and adjacency matrix A∈R^{n×n}, and produces node class labels plus energy scores for OOD detection. During training, three separate networks consume different views of the input: the joint GNN sees both X and A, the feature MLP sees only X, and the structure GNN sees only A with a constant identity feature vector. At inference, only the joint GNN classifier is used, making the auxiliary networks purely training-time regularisers with no additional inference cost.

## Method

TIDE decomposes the predictive mutual information I(X,A;Y) into three components—joint I(Z;Y), feature-specific I(V;Y), and structure-specific I(Q;Y)—realised by a joint GNN encoder, a feature MLP, and a structure GNN, respectively. Each network is trained under an information bottleneck objective (variational lower bound via VIB) to retain label-relevant information while compressing label-irrelevant noise. Two additional regularisers enforce the decomposition: a conditional independence loss I(A;X|Z) (approximated via reconstruction) that pushes Z to capture all feature-structure interactions, and a pairwise mutual information minimisation loss (via CLUB contrastive bounds) that prevents redundant encoding across the three representations. Theoretically, the paper proves that the IB objective increases ID prediction confidence and widens the entropy gap between ID and OOD nodes relative to standard SL, directly improving logit-based energy scores for OOD detection.

## Results

TIDE achieves up to a 34% reduction in FPR95 over the strongest non-OOD-exposure baseline (GNNSAFE) on the Twitch dataset (46.60 vs. 81.18), and a 23% AUROC improvement (89.72 vs. 66.33). On synthetic citation datasets, TIDE reduces FPR95 by an average of 24% (e.g., Pubmed: 12.75 vs. 37.71 for GNNSAFE; Cora: 20.09 vs. 50.53). TIDE maintains competitive ID accuracy across all seven datasets (e.g., 82.56% on Cora, 93.90% on Amazon), confirming that the information-decomposition objective does not sacrifice in-distribution classification performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIDE outperforms all general OOD scoring baselines (MSP, ODIN, Mahalanobis, Energy) by large margins across every dataset; for example, on Twitch TIDE achieves 89.72 AUROC versus 51.24 for Energy and 33.59 for MSP. Among graph-specific methods, TIDE surpasses GNNSAFE (Wu et al., 2023) and NODESAFE (Yang et al., 2024) on all seven datasets, with the largest gains on Twitch (FPR95: 46.60 vs. 81.18/80.62) and Pubmed (FPR95: 12.75 vs. 37.71/50.17). Against the advanced detector DeGEM (Chen et al., 2025), TIDE is competitive on AUROC/AUPR but DeGEM achieves slightly lower FPR95 on feature-shift settings (e.g., Pubmed-F: 1.70 vs. 12.53), though TIDE-augmented DeGEM further improves to 0.59 FPR95. TIDE decisively outperforms GraphIFE (Zeng et al., 2025), which is not designed for node-level OOD detection (e.g., Cora-S FPR95: 23.31 vs. 84.68). Against GOLD (Wang et al., 2025), TIDE wins on most settings (e.g., Cora-Label FPR95: 28.84 vs. 21.20 is a loss, but Pubmed-S FPR95: 12.97 vs. 11.57 is close), while TIDE-augmented GOLD further improves to 5.52 FPR95 on Pubmed-S. In the OOD-exposure setting (Table 6), TIDE without OOD exposure already beats GNNSAFE++ and NODESAFE++ on Cora (FPR95: 19.34 vs. 37.28/42.48) and Pubmed (FPR95: 8.84 vs. 39.58/24.87).

## Relation to Prior Work

TIDE extends the graph information bottleneck (GIB, Wu et al. 2020) by explicitly decomposing the joint input information I(X,A;Y) into feature-specific, structure-specific, and joint components, rather than treating the GNN representation as a monolithic signal. It builds on the energy-based OOD scoring framework of GNNSAFE (Wu et al. 2023) and NODESAFE (Yang et al. 2024) for inference, but departs from their standard supervised training by replacing the SL objective with a tri-component IB formulation augmented by conditional independence and pairwise MI regularisers. The theoretical analysis extends prior IB-for-OOD work (Hu et al. 2024, which only characterised overconfidence under SL) by formally proving that the full IB objective increases ID confidence and widens the ID-OOD entropy gap for logit-based detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tri-component decomposition provides a principled lens for understanding *which* information in a graph is shift-indicative versus spurious, and the theoretical results give a clear justification for why IB-trained GNNs should outperform SL-trained ones for OOD detection. However, the method is limited to node-level classification with a simple constant-feature structure encoder, uses fixed hyperparameters across datasets, and does not address graph-level OOD or heterophilic graphs. Open questions include whether the decomposition generalises to more complex structure encodings (spectral, random-walk), whether the IB advantage persists under adversarial or compound shifts, and how the framework extends to anomaly detection or graph-level tasks.
