## Problem

Sequential quantize-then-fine-tune pipelines for LLMs treat per-layer bit-width and LoRA rank as independent decisions, yet the two knobs are coupled during training: lower precision introduces quantization noise that additional adapter capacity can partially compensate through learning. The paper shows empirically that calibration-based proxies (e.g., perplexity) correlate only moderately (ρ=0.46) with post-fine-tuning accuracy and that distinct bit-rank pairings under the same memory budget can yield accuracy gaps exceeding 25%, making decoupled allocation misaligned with the deployment objective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained transformer backbone (LLaMA-3.1/3.2, Qwen-2.5), a hard global memory budget B_max, a fine-tuning dataset (Alpaca-52k or HC3), and a scalar user preference α∈[0,1] that trades off accuracy against memory in the final selection. It outputs a discrete per-layer configuration C = {(q_ℓ, r_ℓ)}_{ℓ=1}^L with q_ℓ drawn from a bit-width ladder (e.g., {2,4,8}) and r_ℓ from a rank ladder (e.g., {4,8,16}), subject to exact memory accounting that includes quantized backbone weights, 16-bit LoRA adapters, and quantization metadata (scales, zero-points). The final deliverable is the fine-tuned quantized model plus the searched configuration, which can be reused across related datasets.

## Method

AutoQRA formulates joint per-layer bit-width and LoRA rank allocation as a constrained black-box optimization over the discrete space (|Q|×|R|)^L and solves it with a two-phase coarse-to-fine strategy. Phase I performs a global multi-fidelity evolutionary search (NSGA-II with constrained domination) over a Hyperband-style step ladder, where the population is warm-started from layer-wise importance priors (gradient-weighted quantization residual for bits, leading singular-value energy of probe gradients for ranks), offspring are generated by sensitivity-guided and memory-balanced coupled mutations, and a learned surrogate (Huber-regression on ordinal embeddings) screens candidates for promotion while Pareto selection uses only measured high-fidelity evaluations. Phase II refines the Phase I frontier with multi-region trust-region Bayesian optimization (TuRBO-style): a GP surrogate with a Matérn-5/2 kernel in the standardized ordinal embedding space proposes candidates via Expected Improvement within discrete atomic-edit neighborhoods, with adaptive radius expansion/contraction per region and termination on EI saturation. A deterministic feasibility-repair operator projects infeasible candidates back into the feasible set by iteratively downgrading the knob (bit or rank) at the layer with the lowest sensitivity-per-saved-memory ratio, ensuring every returned configuration satisfies the exact memory budget.

## Results

On four LLM backbones (LLaMA-3.1-8B, LLaMA-3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen2.5-3B), AutoQRA (≤4-bit) achieves task-average accuracies of 69.83%, 65.58%, 71.35%, and 66.33% respectively, outperforming all uniform 4-bit baselines while using lower average precision (3.64–3.75 bits) and lower average LoRA rank (9.78–11.14 vs. 16.00). The AutoQRA (Opt) variant, which allows mixed precision above 4-bit, slightly exceeds FP16 LoRA on all four backbones (e.g., 73.19% vs. 71.33% on Qwen2.5-7B) with substantially reduced weight precision (5.22–5.36 bits). Memory footprint is reduced by 12–22% relative to uniform 4-bit baselines, and the offline search completes in 55 minutes versus >3.6 hours for the AMQ bit-only allocator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoQRA (≤4-bit) consistently outperforms all uniform 4-bit baselines across all four backbones: on Qwen2.5-3B it reaches 66.33% vs. QLoRA (62.89%), AdaLoRA (62.51%), LoftQ (62.71%), and LQ-LoRA (62.86%), a 3.4–3.8 point margin, while using lower average precision (3.72 vs. 4.00 bits) and substantially lower rank (9.78 vs. 16.00). Against the decoupled AMQ+LoRA and AMQ+AdaLoRA pipelines, AutoQRA (≤4-bit) gains 3.03 and 1.45 points on Qwen2.5-3B respectively, demonstrating that joint bit-rank search captures compensatory interactions that sequential allocation misses. The AutoQRA (Opt) variant surpasses FP16 LoRA on every backbone (e.g., 73.19% vs. 71.33% on Qwen2.5-7B; 70.45% vs. 69.94% on LLaMA-3.1-8B) with 5.22–5.36 average bits instead of 16. In terms of efficiency, AutoQRA's 55-minute search is ~4× faster than AMQ's >3.6-hour bit-only search, and the selected configuration trains at 1.92 s/step—comparable to AMQ+LoRA (1.91 s) and faster than AdaLoRA (2.81 s). The per-task breakdown reveals that AutoQRA's gains are concentrated on quantization-fragile tasks (e.g., WinoGrande on Qwen2.5-7B: 73.30% vs. QLoRA's 63.30%), confirming that the joint allocation specifically prevents the sharp single-task failures characteristic of rigid uniform-precision methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of mixed-precision quantization (SliM-LLM, AMQ, GPTQ) and adaptive LoRA rank allocation (AdaLoRA, RankAdaptor), but departs from both by treating bit-width and rank as coupled decisions rather than sequential, independent choices. It also extends joint quantization-LoRA methods like LoftQ and LQ-LoRA, which rely on localized reconstruction proxies, by replacing them with actual fine-tuning evaluations guided by a multi-fidelity evolutionary search (NSGA-II + Hyperband) followed by trust-region Bayesian optimization (TuRBO). The optimization machinery draws on established black-box optimization primitives (CoCaBO, TuRBO, Hyperband) but instantiates them with domain-specific constraints: exact memory feasibility, coupled bit-rank mutation operators, and measured-only Pareto selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AutoQRA provides a practical path to near-FP16 fine-tuning quality at roughly 4-bit memory footprint, which is directly relevant to practitioners constrained by GPU memory. The empirical finding that calibration proxies (perplexity) correlate only moderately (ρ=0.46) with post-fine-tuning accuracy challenges the common assumption that good reconstruction quality predicts good adaptation quality, and the observed compensation pattern (low-bit layers paired with high-rank adapters) offers a concrete design principle. Limitations include fixed search hyperparameters that do not auto-adapt to model scale or task heterogeneity, a 55-minute offline search cost, and evaluation limited to instruction-tuning and commonsense benchmarks; a scale-aware controller and broader task coverage remain open.
