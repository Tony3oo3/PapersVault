## Problem

Vision Transformers incur quadratic attention cost that limits real-time deployment in person re-identification, and existing token compression strategies (pruning or generic merging) are designed for image classification with coarse-grained criteria. When applied to fine-grained ReID, these approaches discard or smooth out subtle discriminative local details—such as facial features, logos, and clothing patterns—that are essential for identity matching, causing significant retrieval accuracy drops.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262626/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB person images resized to 256×128 as input, processed through a pre-trained TransReID ViT-B/16 backbone using sliding-window patching. It operates entirely at inference time (training-free), progressively merging r=8 tokens per layer across the first 10 transformer layers to produce a compressed token sequence (~24% GFLOPs reduction) from which an identity feature embedding is extracted for retrieval. The method is also validated on ImageNet-1k with DeiT-T/S/B backbones for classification generalization.

## Method

SRE-Merge is a training-free token merging framework that injects spatial priors into the merging pipeline through three sequential mechanisms. First, SES-Assess quantifies token importance as Spatial-Entropic Mass (SE-Mass) by coupling Dynamic Virtual Coordinates (DVC) with an Adaptive Gaussian Spatial Template (pedestrian-centered prior) and entropy-weighted multi-head attention aggregation, avoiding the underestimation of critical local tokens caused by naive head averaging. Second, HCA-Match performs SE-Mass-priority-ordered bipartite grouping (replacing fixed odd-even splits) and selects merge pairs via a Hybrid Context-Affinity Function that multiplies K-vector cosine similarity by a product-form contextual influence score (inverse SE-Mass product), enforcing a 'criticality preservation' constraint that protects high-mass tokens from being merged. Third, EPW-Fuse performs SE-Mass-weighted fusion followed by max-norm linear interpolation to restore the L2 norm of fused features, counteracting the variance reduction (norm diminishment) inherent in averaging that would otherwise collapse discriminative representations.

## Results

On Market-1501, SRE-Merge achieves 95.8% Rank-1 and 89.2% mAP at 15.53 GFLOPs, surpassing the uncompressed TransReID backbone (95.6% Rank-1, 89.3% mAP at 20.41 GFLOPs) while reducing computation by 23.9%. On MSMT17, it matches TransReID's Rank-1 (85.3%) with a marginal 0.3% mAP drop (67.8% vs 68.1%). Compared to ToMe at the same 15.53 GFLOPs, SRE-Merge improves Rank-1 by 0.9% on Market-1501 and 0.3% on MSMT17, with corresponding mAP gains of 0.6% and 0.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the uncompressed TransReID backbone, SRE-Merge reduces GFLOPs by 23.9% (20.41→15.53) while matching MSMT17 Rank-1 (85.3%) and exceeding Market-1501 Rank-1 by 0.2% (95.8% vs 95.6%), at the cost of a negligible 0.3% mAP drop on MSMT17. Versus ToMe at identical 15.53 GFLOPs, SRE-Merge wins on every accuracy metric: +0.9% Rank-1 and +0.6% mAP on Market-1501, +0.3% Rank-1 and +0.7% mAP on MSMT17, with only a 0.6% increase in inference time. Against other generic ViT compression methods (DPC-KNN, DynamicViT, EViT, ATS), SRE-Merge achieves the lowest GFLOPs (15.53) while also attaining the highest Rank-1 on both datasets. In the VLM/MLLM-oriented comparison (Table 3), SRE-Merge outperforms VisionZip, DivPrune, VisionTrim, ApET, and OTPrune on all three ReID benchmarks (Market-1501, MSMT17, Occluded-Duke) while requiring the fewest GFLOPs (15.53 vs 17.84–18.79). Against non-compressed SOTA ReID methods (Table 4), SRE-Merge remains competitive on Market-1501 (95.8% Rank-1 vs 95.7% for MV-3DSReID) and MSMT17 (85.3% Rank-1, matching TransReID) despite the 24% computation reduction. On Occluded-Duke (Table 5), SRE-Merge achieves the best mAP (59.4%) among all compared methods, surpassing ETND (57.6%) and MTIPE (57.8%), while its Rank-1 (67.5%) is competitive with FED (68.1%) and PRE (68.3%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TransReID as the ViT backbone and on ToMe's bipartite soft-matching pipeline as the base merging mechanism, but departs from both by arguing that classification-oriented merging criteria (class-token similarity, raw feature cosine) are insufficient for fine-grained identity retrieval. It introduces a spatial-entropy saliency metric (SE-Mass) that couples a pedestrian-centered Gaussian prior with per-head local attention entropy, then uses this mass to guide both pair selection (HCA-Match) and weighted fusion (EPW-Fuse). The work also positions itself against recent VLM/MLLM-oriented token reduction methods (VisionZip, DivPrune, VisionTrim, ApET, OTPrune), showing that their global-semantics-preserving strategies underperform on ReID because they overlook subtle identity-discriminative local cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free method requiring no retraining or new parameters, SRE-Merge offers a practical path to deploying ViT-based ReID on latency-constrained hardware, achieving ~24% GFLOPs reduction while matching or slightly exceeding the uncompressed TransReID baseline. The spatial-entropy framework could potentially transfer to other fine-grained retrieval tasks (vehicle ReID, object ReID) where local discriminative details matter, though the Gaussian centering prior is specifically motivated by pedestrian-cropped images. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-pedestrian ReID, to ViT architectures without a class token, and whether the added inference overhead from SES-Assess becomes prohibitive at higher compression ratios or on edge devices.
