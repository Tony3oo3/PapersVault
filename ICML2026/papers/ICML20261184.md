## Problem

While paired sampling is a widely used heuristic that empirically improves Shapley value estimation, the theoretical mechanism driving this improvement has remained opaque. Additionally, higher-order polynomial regression estimators like PolySHAP suffer from combinatorial explosion in computational cost as the number of basis terms grows, limiting their practical applicability to medium- and high-dimensional settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261184/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

OddSHAP takes a black-box value function f (queried on sampled coalitions), a sampling budget m, and a regression variable factor η (default 10) that controls the sample-to-variable ratio. It produces per-player Shapley values computed from the Fourier coefficients of the fitted odd approximation via φ_i = -2 Σ β_T/|T| over odd-order terms containing player i. The method requires paired sampling (each coalition S is sampled alongside its complement S^c) and enforces exact boundary constraints so that the estimated Shapley values sum to f([d]) − f(∅) regardless of budget.

## Method

The core theoretical insight is that Shapley values depend exclusively on the odd component of the value function (f_odd(S) = (f(S) − f(S^c))/2), and that paired sampling orthogonalizes the weighted least squares regression objective, completely decoupling the odd and even subspaces. OddSHAP exploits this by reformulating the regression in the Fourier basis—where each basis function χ_T is strictly odd (|T| odd) or even (|T| even)—and restricting the fit to odd-order terms only. The algorithm proceeds in three stages: (1) paired sampling of m coalitions, (2) fitting a LightGBM proxy to the samples and extracting the top |T_odd| = ⌈m/η⌉ − d odd-order Fourier interactions via ProxySPEX, and (3) solving a constrained weighted least squares regression on the selected odd Fourier terms plus linear terms, with the intercept β_∅ fixed to (f([d])+f(∅))/2 and a sum constraint on odd coefficients enforcing efficiency. This yields a consistent estimator that retains the expressive power of higher-order polynomials while avoiding the O(d^{2k}) scaling of fixed-order methods.

## Results

OddSHAP achieves the lowest average rank (1.50) across all 8 benchmark value functions at a budget of m≈100d, attaining the best MSE on every dataset. Against the prior state-of-the-art RegressionMSR, OddSHAP reduces MSE by up to 4.3× on Crime (0.13 vs. 0.56) and by 2–3× on moderate-dimensional tasks (e.g., Cancer: 4.2×10⁻⁶ vs. 3.0×10⁻⁴). On the high-dimensional Crime dataset (d=101), OddSHAP achieves an MSE of 1.3×10⁻¹, compared to 3.6×10¹ for MSR and 1.5×10¹ for SVARM, representing a roughly 27× improvement over the best non-regression baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OddSHAP outperforms the prior state-of-the-art RegressionMSR on all 8 datasets, with the largest gains on high-dimensional tasks: Crime (0.13 vs. 0.56, ~4.3× improvement) and NHANES (5.8×10⁻⁵ vs. 2.4×10⁻⁴, ~4.1× improvement). Against LeverageSHAP (the interaction-free baseline), OddSHAP achieves 2–5× lower MSE across all datasets, confirming that the odd-interaction regression captures meaningful higher-order structure. On the 4 datasets where FFD-RD is reported, OddSHAP wins on DistilBERT (4.6×10⁻⁵ vs. 1.6×10⁻³), Estate (5.1×10⁻⁷ vs. 1.8×10⁻⁶), and ViT16 (1.3×10⁻⁵ vs. 5.3×10⁻⁴), but loses on Cancer (4.2×10⁻⁶ vs. 6.4×10⁻⁷), where FFD's strict truncation assumption aligns with the sparse interaction structure of tree-based models. OddSHAP dominates FourierSHAP by 2–3 orders of magnitude on most datasets (e.g., Crime: 0.13 vs. 190), and outperforms the proxy-only method (Proxy/LGBM) by 1–2 orders of magnitude, validating that the constrained odd regression step is critical for consistency. On the 4 datasets where PolySHAP-3 is reported, OddSHAP matches or exceeds its accuracy while avoiding the O(d^{2k}) combinatorial scaling that limits PolySHAP to low dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the regression-based Shapley estimation lineage (KernelSHAP → LeverageSHAP → PolySHAP → RegressionMSR) by identifying a structural shortcut: Shapley values depend exclusively on the odd component of the value function, so the regression need only fit odd-order terms. It departs from PolySHAP's unanimity basis (where individual basis functions are neither purely odd nor even) by reformulating in the Fourier basis, where odd/even separation is exact by cardinality, and borrows ProxySPEX's GBT-based interaction screening to avoid combinatorial explosion. The theoretical contribution—proving that paired sampling implicitly orthogonalizes the regression into independent odd and even subproblems—resolves a conjecture from Fumagalli et al. (2026) and provides the first general justification for a heuristic used across the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paired-sampling explanation fills a long-standing theoretical gap: practitioners have relied on this heuristic for years without understanding why it works, and the paper's result generalizes prior analyses limited to degree-2 interactions. Practically, OddSHAP achieves the lowest average MSE rank across eight benchmarks at m≈100d, outperforming RegressionMSR (the prior SOTA) while scaling polynomially rather than combinatorially with interaction order. Key limitations include cubic-in-m regression cost at large budgets, the variance inflation from halving effective sample rows under paired sampling, and inferior performance to FFD-RD on tree-based value functions where the truncation assumption holds. Open directions include exploiting empirical even-odd correlations, incorporating domain-specific structural priors into the interaction screening, and decoupling the interaction count from the sampling budget for very large m.
