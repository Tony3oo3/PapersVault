## Problem

VLM-based reward models for diffusion-model alignment are computationally expensive, operate in pixel space (introducing a latent-to-pixel mismatch with latent diffusion generators), and their discrete text-generation heads make gradient propagation impractical for reward-gradient alignment. The paper asks whether pretrained diffusion backbones can be repurposed as general-purpose, optimization-friendly reward models that remain competitive with strong VLM judges while operating natively in latent space.

## Input / Output

The reward model consumes a noisy VAE latent x_t = α(t)x_0 + σ(t)ε (not a decoded pixel image), a text prompt c, and a scalar timestep t, and outputs a single scalar reward score r_θ(x_t, t, c). All computation stays in the frozen VAE latent space, eliminating the expensive decode step. At inference, the model can be queried at a single fixed noise level (default t=0.4) or at K timesteps whose FiLM-modulated token features are concatenated before a single Q-Former pass, trading extra inference compute for a more stable aggregated score.

## Method

DiNa-LRM extends the Thurstone preference model to noisy diffusion states by introducing a noise-calibrated comparison variance σ_u²(t) = kσ²(t) + σ_u² that inflates with the diffusion noise level, so high-noise pairs receive a more conservative likelihood and avoid destabilizing gradients. The architecture extracts multi-layer visual and text features from a pretrained SD3.5-Medium DiT backbone, applies FiLM modulation with timestep embeddings, and aggregates them through a gated Q-Former with value-gated cross-attention to produce the scalar score. Training uses a fidelity loss over preference pairs from HPDv3 (~0.8M comparisons) with uniform timestep sampling and LoRA fine-tuning of the backbone. At inference, a token-level noise-ensembling strategy concatenates FiLM-modulated features from K timesteps (e.g., {0.2, 0.5, 0.7}) into a longer context for a single Q-Former scoring pass, providing a diffusion-native test-time scaling knob. The entire pipeline operates in VAE latent space, reducing peak VRAM by 51.4% and reward FLOPs by 71.1% compared to pixel-space VLM rewards during ReFL alignment.

## Results

On the four pairwise preference accuracy benchmarks (ImageReward, HPDv2, HPDv3, GenAI-Bench), DiNa-LRM (SD3.5-M-2B backbone) achieves an average of 71.49%, and with inference-time multi-noise ensembling (DiNa-LRM*) reaches 72.48%. This substantially outperforms prior diffusion-based reward baselines (LRM-SD1.5: 61.62%, LRM-SDXL: 61.73%) and narrows the gap to the strongest VLM-based reward HPSv3 (74.84%). In ReFL alignment on SD3.5-M, DiNa-LRM reduces peak VRAM by 51.4%, reward-calculation FLOPs by 71.1%, and optimization-phase FLOPs by 46.4% relative to HPSv3, while improving proxy and held-out golden (PickScore) metrics in tandem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiNa-LRM decisively outperforms both diffusion-based baselines: it improves average accuracy from 61.62% (LRM-SD1.5) and 61.73% (LRM-SDXL) to 71.49%, a gain of roughly 10 points, with the largest margin on HPDv3 (75.04% vs. 54.05%/53.80%). Against CLIP-based rewards, DiNa-LRM surpasses all four (ImageReward 65.31%, PickScore 69.45%, HPSv2 70.13%, MPS 70.51%) in average accuracy, though it trails on the ImageReward test set specifically (60.34% vs. 65.15–66.37%). Among VLM-based rewards, DiNa-LRM* (72.48%) is competitive with UnifiedReward (72.81%) and UnifiedReward-Think (69.56%), but remains behind HPSv3 (74.84%) by about 2.4 points on average. In the ReFL alignment experiment, DiNa-LRM accelerates proxy-score improvement over HPSv3 while maintaining a rising held-out PickScore, and reduces peak VRAM by 51.4% and reward FLOPs by 71.1%, making it substantially more efficient for gradient-based alignment. The paper also shows that on the same SDXL backbone, DiNa-LRM (71.55% avg) outperforms LRM-SDXL (61.73%) by nearly 10 points, confirming the gain comes from the formulation rather than the backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Thurstone comparative-judgment framework from clean samples to noisy diffusion states, introducing a noise-calibrated comparison uncertainty that scales with the diffusion noise level, and adopts the fidelity loss (FRANK) as the training objective. Architecturally, it repurposes a pretrained latent diffusion backbone (SD3.5-M) with a FiLM-modulated, gated Q-Former scoring head, drawing on the gated-attention mechanism from Qiu et al. (2025). Conceptually, it departs from prior diffusion-based reward work (LRM-SD1.5/SDXL) that targets step-level trajectory rewards tied to specific alignment algorithms, instead positioning the diffusion backbone as a general-purpose, reusable reward model that scores clean samples via latent-space evaluation under the same usage scenario as VLM rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for reward-gradient alignment pipelines: operating natively in VAE latent space eliminates pixel-space decoding, yielding 51.4% peak VRAM reduction and 71.1% reward-calculation FLOPs reduction versus HPSv3, while enabling faster proxy-score convergence under ReFL. However, the reward accuracy still trails the strongest VLM baseline (HPSv3) by ~3 points on average, and the paper acknowledges that latent-space modeling may under-emphasize pixel-level artifacts, and that long-horizon optimization exhibits reward-hacking patterns (spurious human injection, stylistic drift). Open questions include whether distilling VLM rewards into latent backbones can close the accuracy gap, whether the formulation transfers across incompatible VAE spaces, and why scaling the diffusion backbone from 2B to 12B does not yield the performance leaps seen in VLM-based rewards.
