## Problem

Large Multimodal Models store extensive factual knowledge in pre-trained weights, but this knowledge is static and cannot keep pace with real-world developments. Existing knowledge injection methods face a fundamental trade-off: full fine-tuning and PEFT (e.g., LoRA) overfit to new data and cause catastrophic forgetting of prior knowledge, while regularization-based continual learning methods (e.g., EWC) impair the model's ability to adapt to new knowledge, producing irrelevant responses and instruction forgetting.

## Input / Output

For knowledge injection, KORE consumes image-text pairs from the EVOKE benchmark and automatically augments each item into multi-round dialogues (up to 10 per item via GPT-4o) and three instruction task types (visual recognition, image caption, VQA) using CLIP-extracted visual features and Google-retrieved images, yielding the KORE-74K dataset (~75K dialogue + ~46K VQA samples). At inference, the fine-tuned LMM takes an RGB image plus a text query and produces a free-form text response, evaluated via Cover Exact Match and F1-Score on EVOKE, and standard benchmark metrics across 12 retention benchmarks spanning 7 capability dimensions.

## Method

KORE is a two-stage optimization centered on knowledge-oriented controls. Stage 1 (KORE-AUGMENTATION) automatically expands each knowledge item into a structured 'knowledge tree' of multi-round dialogues (trunk) and instruction tasks (branches), moving beyond superficial augmentation to promote genuine knowledge internalization and generalization. Stage 2 (KORE-CONSTRAINT) collects input activations from LMM linear layers on 256 representative samples, computes the covariance matrix C = XX^T, performs SVD to extract its null space, and initializes the LoRA adapter by projecting pre-trained weights W0 into this null space (A = √Σ*(V*)^T, B = U*√Σ*), then freezes A and fine-tunes only B. Because A lies in the null space of C, the update term BAC ≈ 0 regardless of B's changes, formally guaranteeing that outputs on pre-trained knowledge remain approximately unchanged (Theorem C.2). The original weights are adjusted with a residual term W0' = W0 − BA to ensure the model is unchanged at the start of fine-tuning.

## Results

On LLaVA-v1.5 (7B), KORE (rank=235) achieves 30.65 CEM and 41.26 F1 on EVOKE for knowledge adaptation, outperforming the best baseline (SEFE) by 14.43 and 17.14 points respectively. For knowledge retention, KORE scores 40.00 on the 12-benchmark average, competitive with Replay (43.00) and O-LoRA (41.25). Overall, KORE attains the best Avg (37.98) and HARS (82.81), improving by 9.15 and 15.25 over the best baseline, demonstrating superior balance between adaptation and retention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KORE decisively outperforms all baselines on knowledge adaptation (K.A): it scores 35.96 vs. SEFE (20.17), LoRA (16.77), EWC (17.46), and Full-FT (16.60), more than doubling the best baseline. On knowledge retention (K.R), KORE (40.00) trails Replay (43.00) and O-LoRA (41.25) but surpasses Full-FT (31.66), LoRA (33.47), EWC (35.14), LwF (35.44), SEFE (33.02), and CIA (35.99). The critical advantage is in the harmonic balance: KORE's HARS of 82.81 exceeds the next-best SEFE (67.56) by 15.25 points, while its Avg of 37.98 beats CIA (35.99) by 1.99. MoELoRA and O-LoRA, despite strong retention, suffer severe adaptation collapse (K.A ≈ 9.3), yielding the lowest HARS scores (37.22 and 36.70). Full-FT, despite using 20× more parameters (6,759M vs. 340M), underperforms KORE on both adaptation and retention, confirming that parameter scale alone does not resolve the adaptation–retention trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

KORE sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) knowledge injection via PEFT (LoRA, adapters), (2) continual learning constraints (EWC, LwF, null-space projection as in AlphaEdit and O-LoRA), and (3) data augmentation for knowledge internalization. It departs from prior null-space methods (e.g., AlphaEdit, CO-SVD) by using the covariance null space not merely as a training-time regularizer but as an *initialization* strategy for LoRA adapters, and by extending CO-SVD from text-only to multimodal settings. The structured augmentation component (multi-round dialogues + instruction tasks forming a 'knowledge tree') is a clear departure from the superficial, discrete augmentations (rephrasing, rotation) used in prior knowledge-injection pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, well-motivated recipe for injecting new knowledge into LMMs while preserving prior capabilities, and contributes the KORE-74K dataset and the HARS metric as reusable resources. The HARS metric in particular exposes a blind spot: methods like Replay that preserve old knowledge well can still score very poorly on the adaptation-retention balance (e.g., HARS=30.89 on Qwen2.5-VL), reframing how the community evaluates knowledge injection. Open questions include whether the covariance-matrix assumption generalizes to truly novel (unrelated) knowledge domains, whether the GPT-4o-dependent augmentation pipeline scales to non-English or highly specialized knowledge, and whether the approach extends to iterative multi-round knowledge updates rather than a single injection.
