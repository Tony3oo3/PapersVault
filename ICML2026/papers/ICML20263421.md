## Problem

Compact vision-language models are efficient and deployable but suffer severe degradation in spatial grounding (bounding-box localization) under standard fine-tuning or distillation, while large VLMs that achieve strong localization incur prohibitive inference cost. Existing distillation approaches transfer output distributions or intermediate representations but do not teach *how* localization decisions are made, leaving compact models producing plausible answers without reliable spatial grounding. This gap is compounded by evaluation metrics like ANLS that emphasize textual correctness while largely ignoring spatial precision, creating a trust problem in high-stakes document processing.

## Input / Output

At inference, the student receives only a document image (RGB) and a natural-language question, producing a serialized output tuple of (CoT, answer, bounding box) in a single forward pass with no OCR, text detection, or external validator. The bounding box is a 4-element coordinate vector [x1, y1, x2, y2] in pixel space. During training, text detection (DB-ResNet) extracts ~15–20 regions per document, but these regions are used exclusively by the teacher and the rule-based validator for supervision and feedback; the student never receives region-level inputs, ensuring the final model learns spatial grounding purely from visual representations.

## Method

DocVAL is a three-phase validated chain-of-thought distillation framework. In Phase A, a large teacher VLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) generates spatial CoT traces for 102K document-question pairs, which are filtered by a rule-based dual-mode validator (VAL) using a composite quality score Q = 0.4·Q_ans + 0.4·Q_bbox + 0.2·Q_reason (threshold 0.85), retaining 95K high-quality traces. In Phase B, a compact student (Gemma3-12B) is first fine-tuned via standard autoregressive likelihood on the validated traces (Stage B1), then iteratively refined (Stage B2) by generating predictions, receiving structured pixel-level corrective feedback from VAL (including correction vectors δ = [Δx1, Δy1, Δx2, Δy2] and priority-ordered fixes), and fine-tuning on the feedback-augmented examples until convergence (~13 iterations). The key design principle is asymmetric validation: VAL leverages detected text regions for semantic error diagnosis (e.g., distinguishing 'Subtotal' from 'Total'), while the student is forced to learn spatial grounding end-to-end from visual inputs alone, making the deployed model a pure VLM with no auxiliary dependencies.

## Results

DocVAL (Gemma3-12B) achieves 91.4 ANLS and 82.4 mAP on DocVQA, 92.2/81.8 on FUNSD, 88.8/78.1 on CORD, 73.7/68.8 on VisualMRC, and 95.2/79.6 on SROIE, establishing state-of-the-art among compact OCR-free VLMs. The 4B variant reaches 88.7 ANLS and 69.1 mAP on DocVQA, outperforming larger 8B models (Qwen3-VL-8B: 80.4; InternVL3.5-8B: 79.9) by 8+ ANLS points. Relative to the base Gemma3-12B, DocVAL adds +6.8 ANLS and introduces strong spatial grounding (82.4 mAP) where the base model reports no localization metric. The paper introduces mAP@IoU[0.5:0.95] as a new localization metric for document VQA, reported alongside ANLS and IoU@0.5/0.75.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DocVAL (Gemma3-12B) outperforms all five baselines on DocVQA ANLS (91.4 vs. 88.7 ARIAL, 85.9 DLaVA, 78.4 DocLayLLM, 77.2 LayTextLLM, 74.3 LayoutLLM) and introduces mAP scoring where most baselines report no localization metric. Against the strongest grounding-focused baseline ARIAL (Gemma3-27B + TrOCR, OCR at inference), DocVAL gains +2.7 ANLS and +32.3 mAP on DocVQA, +2.2/+31.5 on FUNSD, and +3.3/+17.9 on CORD, while using a 2.3× smaller backbone and no OCR dependency. Versus DLaVA (Pixtral-12B), DocVAL improves DocVQA by +5.5 ANLS and +36.2 mAP, and FUNSD by +4.6 ANLS and +36.3 mAP. The 4B DocVAL variant (88.7 ANLS, 69.1 mAP on DocVQA) surpasses all 8B general-purpose VLMs (Qwen3-VL-8B: 80.4; InternVL3.5-8B: 79.9) by 8+ ANLS points, demonstrating that validated reasoning transfer compensates for reduced model capacity. The sole ANLS loss is on SROIE, where LayTextLLM (96.1) edges out DocVAL-12B (95.2) by 0.9 points, though DocVAL additionally reports 79.6 mAP where LayTextLLM provides no localization output.

## Relation to Prior Work

DocVAL sits at the intersection of three established lines: document VQA with spatial grounding (LayoutLM, DocLayLLM, ARIAL), chain-of-thought reasoning for VLMs (Visual CoT, DDCoT), and knowledge distillation (rationale distillation, VLM distillation). It departs from prior distillation work by transferring explicit spatial reasoning traces rather than output distributions or intermediate features, and from prior CoT supervision by introducing a rule-based dual-mode validator (VAL) that filters hallucinated boxes and provides pixel-level corrective feedback during iterative refinement. The key architectural departure from OCR-based document understanding systems (ARIAL, DLaVA) is that the student learns spatial grounding purely from visual inputs, with text detection serving only as training-time scaffolding that is fully removed at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 38K validated CoT traces outperform 102K unfiltered ones (73.6 vs. 63.7 mAP on DocVQA) provides concrete evidence that validation-aware dataset construction matters more than raw scale for reasoning-based fine-tuning, a principle likely to generalize beyond document VQA. The 95K validator-verified CoT dataset and the introduction of mAP as a localization metric for document QA are practical contributions that lower the barrier for future work. Open questions include whether the framework extends to handwriting-heavy pages, chart-dominant layouts, and multi-hop or long-form document reasoning, where the current text-detection scaffolding and single-bbox output format become limiting. The paper also leaves open whether hybrid validators (combining rule-based determinism with learned flexibility) could further improve robustness.
