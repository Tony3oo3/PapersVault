## Problem

At extremely low SNR, noise becomes strongly entangled with the signal, corrupting local time-frequency evidence and causing STFT-based enhancement methods to become overconfident in unreliable regions, producing unstable suppression, residual artifacts, or excessive detail loss. Existing approaches either rely on fixed-resolution local convolutions that cannot aggregate weak but globally consistent cues, or use unconstrained flexible filters that chase interference variability and collapse out of distribution. The paper identifies this noise-signal entanglement as the core failure mode and addresses it with structured evidence aggregation plus explicit reliability control.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy waveform (16 kHz speech or 48 kHz EM signal) and produces an enhanced waveform of the same length. Internally, it operates on the complex STFT (FFT size 510, hop 160) where the network estimates the clean complex spectrum; the Wiener reliability prior is computed from the noisy STFT power spectrum via temporally smoothed minimum-statistics noise tracking. Training segments are 51,040 samples long, and the final output is obtained by inverse STFT of the predicted complex spectrum.

## Method

FracKGMF couples two complementary views fused under a Wiener-inspired reliability prior. The Fractional Detail (FD) view uses FracConv, a depthwise operator whose kernel is factorized as K_c(u) = A_c(u)·Φ_w(u), where A_c is a learnable anisotropic base kernel and Φ_w is a simplex mixture of M=9 fixed fractional radial decay envelopes (α ∈ [0.1, 0.9]) with softmax-learned mixing weights, imposing bounded long-tailed distance decay on a local footprint. The Large Receptive Field (LRF) view aggregates weak but spatially consistent TF cues over a large context via stacked dilated depthwise residual convolutions. KGMF fuses the two views by computing per-bin squared distances from each view to a Wiener-derived reference (the recoverability map R[f,t] applied to the noisy STFT), then applying a per-bin softmax to weight the views, yielding a logistic selector that is 1/4-Lipschitz and provably approaches the conditional risk-minimizing fusion weight. Training is supervised with a weighted sum of real/imaginary, magnitude, and waveform L1 losses.

## Results

On EARS-WHAM!, FracKGMF achieves the best scores across all six metrics: 2.77 PESQ, 4.19 CSIG, 3.50 CBAK, 3.53 COVL, 8.77 SSNR, and 0.94 STOI, using only 2.0M parameters. On VoiceBank+DEMAND, it attains 3.32 PESQ, 4.57 CSIG, 3.85 CBAK, 4.04 COVL, and 0.96 STOI. On the Rydberg 20-bins EM dataset at −20 dB input SNR, FracKGMF delivers a 32.39 dB average SNR improvement (5.51 dB above the best baseline) and 0.91 SSIM, with a 33.04 dB average gain across all three EM benchmarks at that extreme condition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FracKGMF (2.0M params) outperforms all eight baselines on every metric for EARS-WHAM!, with the largest gap over VPIDM (2.38→2.77 PESQ, +0.39) and FlowSE (2.33→2.77 PESQ, +0.44), while also surpassing the 65.6M-parameter diffusion models (VPIDM, FlowSE, VPVID) by a wide margin at 1/32 the parameter count. On VoiceBank+DEMAND, FracKGMF leads on PESQ (3.32 vs. MFSE's 3.17), CSIG (4.57 vs. 4.46), CBAK (3.85 vs. 3.79), COVL (4.04 vs. 3.89), and STOI (0.96 vs. 0.95), though MFSE reports a slightly higher SSNR (10.63 vs. 10.37). On the Rydberg 20-bins EM benchmark at −20 dB, FracKGMF exceeds the strongest baseline by 5.51 dB in SNR and 0.15 in SSIM; at −5 dB the margin is +4.41 dB SNR. The cross-domain generalization experiment (trained on EARS-WHAM!, tested on VoiceBank+DEMAND without fine-tuning) places FracKGMF in the optimal efficiency–performance Pareto region, delivering strong aggregated quality at substantially lower GMACs than diffusion/flow methods. Overall, the gains are most pronounced under extreme low-SNR conditions, consistent with the paper's design motivation of structured fractional detail modeling plus Wiener-guided reliability fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

FracKGMF extends the authors' own line of work on multi-view fusion with fractional Fourier representations (Jin et al., 2025; 2026) by replacing fixed fractional transforms with a learnable, low-dimensional family of fractional distance decay envelopes (FracConv) parameterized by simplex-mixed decay indices. Conceptually, it departs from two prior trends: (i) unconstrained kernel learning or fixed-shape dilated convolutions for long-range TF context, which the paper argues overfit interference variability at extreme low SNR, and (ii) implicit multi-branch fusion, which can become miscalibrated when one view fails in specific TF regions. The Wiener-derived reliability prior for per-bin view weighting draws on classical signal processing (Wiener filtering, minimum-statistics noise tracking) but repurposes it as a calibration signal for learned multi-view fusion rather than as a final denoising estimate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical package (O(1/M) approximation rate for the fractional envelope family, uniform operator stability, and a regret bound for reliability-aware fusion) that gives practitioners confidence in the architectural choices, and the 2M-parameter model outperforming 65M-parameter diffusion/flow baselines on speech is practically relevant for deployment. However, the striking 33 dB EM gains are confined to the Rydberg receiver benchmark with limited competing baselines, and the speech enhancement margins over strong recent methods (e.g., MFSE, VPVID) are modest (~0.1–0.2 PESQ). Open questions include whether the Wiener reliability prior remains informative at moderate-to-high SNR where the signal dominates, whether the fractional decay family generalizes to higher-dimensional or non-STFT representations, and whether the approach scales to real-time full-band enhancement without the current 510-point FFT constraint.
