## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for B-rep CAD models face a fundamental representation gap: continuous approaches (e.g., BRT) encode geometry via parametric control points that are analytically precise but decoupled from spatial intuition, while discrete approaches (e.g., UV-Net, BrepNet) rely on spatial sampling that is perceptually intuitive but sacrifices geometric fidelity. No prior method simultaneously captures mathematical rigor and geometric intuition, and existing self-supervised efforts (e.g., SSL4CAD) are restricted to simplified geometries or fixed-size parameterizations, limiting generalizability to complex industrial B-reps.

## Input / Output

The method consumes B-rep models where each NURBS entity is first decomposed via Boehm's knot insertion into a fixed number of Bezier primitives (triangles for faces, segments for edges), each represented as a control-point matrix in R^{n×4} (spatial coordinates plus NURBS weight), along with a face-edge adjacency graph. During pre-training, the output is a set of m×3 dense 3D spatial points per entity, obtained by uniform sampling of the parametric domain; during fine-tuning, task-specific heads produce class labels or per-face segmentation labels. The fixed-size primitive representation is what makes the variable-length NURBS data compatible with standard transformer processing.

## Method

Brep2Shape is a self-supervised pre-training task that trains a model to predict dense 3D spatial points (shape representation) from Bezier control points (boundary representation), using MSE loss with no manual annotations. The backbone is a Dual Transformer with parallel face and edge streams, where each stream replaces standard self-attention with a topology attention module: the face stream uses linearly projected edge-token embeddings as attention biases (and vice versa), encoding the B-rep adjacency graph and its topological duality as dynamic priors. Entity-level tokens are produced by a small per-entity Transformer with a [CLS] aggregation over the Bezier primitive sequence. Edge-level supervision is explicitly included alongside face-level supervision to enforce spatial consistency at shared boundaries. The architecture scales cleanly in both data (25k→250k) and depth (2→12 layers), and fine-tuning for only 100 epochs (vs. 350 for baselines) achieves state-of-the-art results on classification and segmentation benchmarks.

## Results

On the four downstream benchmarks, Brep2Shape (6-layer Dual Transformer, pre-trained on Brep2Shape-250k, fine-tuned for 100 epochs) achieves 99.99% Acc on FabWave, 84.72% Acc on TMCAD, 99.35% Acc / 98.02% IoU on MFCAD++, and 96.88% Acc / 83.77% IoU on Fusion360Seg. These are the best results across all four tasks, with the largest margins on the more complex TMCAD classification (+3.82 pp over BRT) and Fusion360Seg segmentation (+4.54 pp IoU over BRT). The paper also reports that scaling pre-training data from 25k to 250k raises average downstream accuracy from 0.9379 to 0.9524, and scaling model depth from 2 to 12 layers yields up to 36.3% average error reduction over the BRT baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Brep2Shape outperforms all four baselines on every benchmark. Against UV-Net (discrete, contrastive pre-training), Brep2Shape gains +7.31 pp on FabWave, +6.85 pp on TMCAD, and a substantial +17.30 pp IoU on Fusion360Seg, confirming that discrete sampling sacrifices geometric fidelity on complex shapes. Against AAGNet (GNN with handcrafted features), Brep2Shape leads by +10.00 pp on TMCAD and +14.43 pp Acc on Fusion360Seg, though AAGNet edges out slightly on MFCAD++ IoU (98.64 vs 98.02). Against BRT (the strongest continuous-method baseline and the closest architectural relative), Brep2Shape wins on all four tasks, with the most notable margins on MFCAD++ (+3.62 pp Acc, +8.04 pp IoU) and TMCAD (+3.82 pp Acc), demonstrating that topology attention in a pure-Transformer backbone surpasses BRT's hierarchical RNN+Transformer design. Against SSL4CAD (prior self-supervised CAD pre-training), Brep2Shape-25k achieves 0.7626 vs 0.66 accuracy on cross-domain MFCAD with only 100 labeled samples, a ~10 pp gain in the low-data regime, while matching or exceeding SSL4CAD in-domain on Fusion360Seg.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BRT (Zou & Zhu, 2025) for its Bezier decomposition pipeline and tokenization strategy, but departs from the broader B-rep learning landscape by introducing a self-supervised alignment objective that bridges the gap between continuous (parametric) and discrete (spatial sampling) representations. Prior self-supervised efforts like SSL4CAD (Jones et al., 2023) were restricted to fixed-size parameterizations and specific surface types, while contrastive approaches like UV-Net lacked fine-grained geometric supervision. The Dual Transformer with topology attention replaces the hierarchical RNN+Transformer stack in BRT and the GNN-based message passing in AAGNet/BRepGAT with a scalable pure-transformer architecture that explicitly encodes B-rep topological duality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for manual annotation and supporting arbitrary B-rep models, Brep2Shape enables scalable pre-training on industrial CAD repositories, potentially reducing the barrier to AI adoption in engineering and manufacturing. The demonstrated scaling behavior (consistent gains with both data and model size) suggests the framework can benefit from future data growth. Key limitations include fixed-order Bezier decomposition introducing approximation bias for ultra-precision applications, evaluation limited to face-level and shape-level tasks (no edge-level benchmarks exist), and no handling of multi-body assemblies or joint constraints—areas the authors identify as primary future directions.
