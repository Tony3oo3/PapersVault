## Problem

RLHF alignment is bottlenecked by the high cost of human preference annotation, and existing synthetic data pipelines (UltraFeedback, Magpie, Nectar) apply static, passive selection heuristics that waste annotation budget on trivial comparisons while missing high-information pairs. The Delta Learning Hypothesis offers a structural alternative but is rigidly confined to intra-family model comparisons, limiting its domain coverage. The open question is how to generate high-quality preference datasets that are agnostic to the downstream optimization algorithm while requiring minimal annotation per prompt.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a set of N text prompts and a pool of 30 open-weight LLMs (spanning 12 families, 0.5B–671B parameters) that generate m candidate responses per prompt, each guided by a randomly sampled principle (helpfulness, truthfulness, or honesty). It produces preference triplets (prompt, chosen, rejected) requiring only a single pairwise comparison per prompt for annotation—two responses scored by an LLM judge on a 1–5 Likert scale across four aspects—compared to four or all m responses in prior heuristics. The resulting datasets are used to train both standalone reward models (evaluated on RewardBench 2) and fine-tuned LLMs via DPO, IPO, or SimPO (evaluated on GSM8K, IFEval, TruthfulQA, and AlpacaEval 2).

## Method

ActiveUltraFeedback formulates preference data collection as a contextual dueling bandit problem where the prompt is the context and candidate responses are arms, using an Epistemic Neural Network (ensemble of MLPs on a frozen LLM backbone) to predict rewards with epistemic uncertainty. The key insight, operationalizing the Delta Learning Hypothesis, is that pairs with large predicted quality gaps provide lower-noise training signals than pairs of similarly strong responses. Two novel acquisition functions exploit this: DRTS draws independent Thompson samples from the reward posterior and selects the best and worst responses under those samples, while DELTAUCB deterministically selects the pair maximizing the upper confidence bound on the preference probability. The pipeline iterates in batches of 64 prompts—generate responses, predict rewards with uncertainty, select pairs, annotate, and update the ENN—so that uncertainty estimates sharpen over time. A central empirical finding is that standard dueling bandit objectives (regret minimization via DTS, MAXMINLCB, or pure exploration via INFOMAX) are misaligned with preference data generation, as they identify high-quality responses but fail to create the quality deltas needed for effective learning.

## Results

On the full UltraFeedback prompt set, DRTS achieves a mean downstream delta of +0.127 and RewardBench 2 delta of +0.312, while DELTAUCB achieves +0.120 and +0.339 respectively, both substantially outperforming the original UltraFeedback dataset (+0.037 mean, +0.295 RewardBench 2). In terms of sample efficiency, models fine-tuned on only 5,000–10,000 samples selected by DRTS or DELTAUCB outperform those trained on 60,000 samples from RANDOM, ULTRAFEEDBACK, or dueling-bandit methods. DELTAQWEN achieves the highest mean downstream score (+0.137) but collapses on RewardBench 2 (+0.100), confirming its limited generalizability beyond DPO on common domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the UltraFeedback heuristic (Cui et al., 2024), both DRTS and DELTAUCB achieve roughly 3× the mean downstream improvement (+0.127/+0.120 vs. +0.036) and a 10–15 point gain on RewardBench 2 (+0.312/+0.339 vs. +0.287). DELTAQWEN (Geng et al., 2025) edges out DRTS on mean downstream score (+0.137 vs. +0.127), driven primarily by AlpacaEval 2, but catastrophically underperforms on RewardBench 2 (+0.100 vs. +0.312/+0.339), revealing its inability to generalize beyond DPO on in-distribution prompts. The three dueling-bandit baselines—INFOMAX (Saha, 2021), DTS (Wu & Liu, 2016), and MAXMINLCB (Pásztor et al., 2024)—all underperform even RANDOM sampling on mean downstream score (+0.016–0.023 vs. +0.046) and trail DRTS/DELTAUCB by 10+ points on RewardBench 2, demonstrating that regret-minimization objectives are misaligned with preference data generation. MAXMIN, a non-bandit heuristic that annotates all m responses per prompt, achieves a competitive mean (+0.111) and RewardBench 2 (+0.318) but requires 30× more annotations per prompt than the proposed methods, making it impractical at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: the contextual dueling bandit framework (Dudik et al., 2015; Wu & Liu, 2016) and the Delta Learning Hypothesis (Geng et al., 2025), which pairs responses of differing quality to create informative contrastive training signals. It departs from prior dueling bandit work by demonstrating that regret-minimizing acquisition functions (DTS, MAXMINLCB) are fundamentally misaligned with the goal of preference data generation, and from DLH by removing its rigid single-family constraint through a diverse 30-model pool. The Epistemic Neural Network (Osband et al., 2023) provides the uncertainty backbone, while the UltraFeedback pipeline (Cui et al., 2024) supplies the response generation and annotation infrastructure that the active learning loop operates on top of.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1/6th annotation cost for comparable or superior downstream performance is a practically significant efficiency gain for RLHF pipelines, particularly in expert or low-resource domains where human annotation is the bottleneck. The key open question is whether the LLM-as-a-Judge annotation (Qwen 3 235B) can be replaced by human annotators without degrading the active learning signal, and whether the frozen ENN backbone limits the method's ability to adapt to out-of-distribution prompts. The paper also flags that the upfront compute cost of generating responses from 30 LLMs per prompt is substantial, and selecting which models to query (rather than which responses to compare) is identified as a high-priority future direction.
