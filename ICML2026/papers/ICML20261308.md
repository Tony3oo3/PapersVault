## Problem

Existing diffusion-based object insertion methods formulate the task as 2D inpainting, providing no explicit control over the object's 3D pose. Text-guided models are spatially ambiguous (e.g., 'leaning against' is under-defined), while parametric 3D-aware models like Object3DIT struggle to map abstract rotation scalars to dense pixel-level deformations. This paper tackles the resulting gap: how to achieve precise, user-specified 6-DoF pose control during object insertion without sacrificing visual fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference object image, a target background image, a binary insertion mask, and a 6-DoF pose specified interactively by the user manipulating a 3D proxy (lifted from the reference via TRELLIS) over the background canvas. The proxy is rendered as an RGB geometry guidance image (deliberately not depth or normal maps, to avoid semantic ambiguity for symmetric objects). The generator operates on a high-resolution local crop around the mask region while attending to global scene context from the full background, and outputs a composited image at the specified pose. Training pairs are constructed at approximately 160k scale from SA-1B and MVImgNet.

## Method

DIRECT decomposes the conditioning signal into three orthogonal components—appearance (reference image), geometry (RGB rendering of the user-posed 3D proxy), and context (SIGLIP-encoded global background tokens)—and injects each through a dedicated LoRA adapter within the self-attention layers of a FLUX.1-Fill-dev (or SD) backbone, with independent RoPE positional embeddings spatially isolating the appearance and geometry tokens. This decomposed injection prevents the feature entanglement observed with naive concatenation, where the model over-relies on the degraded 3D proxy texture and ignores the high-fidelity reference. The 3D proxy is generated by TRELLIS from the reference image, and the user specifies pose via an interactive Viser-based gizmo; the rendered proxy provides dense geometric conditioning that resolves the representational gap between sparse 6-DoF parameters and pixel-level deformation. Training uses rectified flow matching with progressive resolution (512²→1024²) and a shape-decomposed mask augmentation that replaces the ground-truth mask with random object masks to prevent the model from shortcutting via boundary leakage. An automated data pipeline (Qwen3-VL + SAM-3 for curation, Qwen-Image-Edit for novel-view synthesis) constructs ~160k training pairs from single-view in-the-wild images, anchoring ground truth to real captures while synthesizing diverse reference views.

## Results

On the FLUX-based evaluation, DIRECT achieves PSNR 23.09, SSIM 0.871, LPIPS 0.147, CLIP-I 0.959, DINO 0.936, and Matching Error 17.8, outperforming all baselines across every metric. In the SD-based category, DIRECT scores PSNR 21.66, SSIM 0.829, LPIPS 0.206, CLIP-I 0.937, DINO 0.913, and ME 21.4. The most dramatic gains are in pose accuracy (Matching Error), where DIRECT reduces the error from 135.7 (Object3DIT†) and 75.4 (TRELLIS†) to 21.4 in the SD setting, and from 98.9 (Object3DIT‡) and 19.6 (TRELLIS‡) to 17.8 in the FLUX setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIRECT is compared against two composite baselines in each backbone category: Object3DIT (a 3D-aware editing model using rotation-angle parameters) and TRELLIS (an image-to-3D model for target-pose rendering), each cascaded with a 2D inserter (AnyDoor for SD, InsertAnything for FLUX). DIRECT wins on all six metrics in both categories. The largest margin is in Matching Error (pose accuracy): in the SD setting, DIRECT's ME of 21.4 is 6.3× lower than Object3DIT† (135.7) and 3.5× lower than TRELLIS† (75.4); in the FLUX setting, DIRECT (17.8) edges out TRELLIS‡ (19.6) by ~10% while crushing Object3DIT‡ (98.9) by 5.6×. In identity preservation, DIRECT's CLIP-I of 0.959 (FLUX) exceeds TRELLIS‡ (0.935) and Object3DIT‡ (0.916), confirming that the decomposed appearance pathway recovers high-frequency details lost in 3D reconstruction. An additional intrinsic-guided compositing baseline (TRELLIS+ZeroComp, Appendix D) achieves the lowest ME (5.2) by directly compositing rendered assets, but sacrifices image fidelity (PSNR 20.66) and identity (CLIP-I 0.892), validating that DIRECT's generative approach better balances pose control with appearance preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIRECT extends the object insertion line (AnyDoor, InsertAnything, IMPRINT) by adding an explicit 6-DoF pose constraint, and the 3D-aware editing line (Object3DIT, Neural Assets) by replacing sparse rotation parameters with a dense rendered RGB proxy as geometric guidance. The key architectural departure is the decomposed injection strategy: rather than naively concatenating multiple conditioning signals (as in OmniControl or prior LoRA-based multi-condition models), DIRECT routes appearance, geometry, and context through independent LoRA adapters with distinct RoPE positional encodings to prevent feature entanglement. The method also repurposes feed-forward image-to-3D models (TRELLIS) not as a final 3D asset generator but as an interactive geometric proxy, and introduces an automated data pipeline that synthesizes training pairs from in-the-wild single-view images via a VLM agent and generative view editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework makes pose-controllable object insertion practically usable for applications like virtual staging and e-commerce photography, where users need to place an object at a specific orientation without manual mask annotation or text prompting. The decomposed injection strategy is a general-purpose technique for multi-condition diffusion models that could transfer to other tasks requiring simultaneous adherence to conflicting signals. A key open limitation is that performance is bounded by upstream 3D reconstruction quality—severe topology errors (e.g., a rectangle reconstructed as a square) propagate into the output—and the authors identify end-to-end geometry refinement during generation as the natural next step. The automated data pipeline, while effective, still inherits biases from its source datasets (SA-1B, MVImgNet), and the method does not model physical interactions (occlusion, lighting, reflections) explicitly, relying instead on learned context guidance.
