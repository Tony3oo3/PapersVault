## Problem

Scalar reward models used in MLLM RLHF suffer from limited expressiveness, black-box opacity, and reward collapse, while existing Generative Reward Models (GRMs) that address these issues are hindered by severe position bias (up to 30% accuracy gap from swapping response order) and quadratic computational overhead from exhaustive pairwise comparisons. The paper tackles how to make GRMs both reliable (order-invariant) and scalable (linear-time) for practical online reinforcement learning of multimodal models.

## Input / Output

The GRM receives a text prompt paired with an image and two candidate responses, then dynamically generates query-specific evaluation rubrics (e.g., accuracy, hallucination, harmlessness) with per-criterion weights that sum to 1, followed by a side-by-side comparative critique and a final weighted-score verdict in \boxed{score1, score2} format. For MLLM alignment, the system takes a prompt+image, samples k candidate responses from the current policy, and produces a relative reward vector (±1 per candidate vs. a dynamically selected anchor) that feeds into GRPO. The rubrics are not fixed templates but are generated per-instance based on the detected demand category (factual Q&A, creative writing, safety, etc.).

## Method

GenAlign operates in two stages. In GRM training, a rubric-based generative model is optimized via RL with verifiable accuracy rewards, augmented by an Online Position Debiasing (OPD) mechanism that tracks the divergence between the model's empirical prediction distribution and the ground-truth distribution using an EMA, then exponentially penalizes correct predictions that align with the over-preferred position—forcing the model to rely on semantic content rather than ordering shortcuts. In MLLM alignment, a Dynamic Reference Anchoring strategy selects one sampled response as a real-time anchor and performs only k−1 pairwise GRM comparisons (reducing complexity from O(k²) to O(k)), with bidirectional evaluation to cancel residual position bias. An Advantage Smoothing function φ(A) = A/√(1+(A/δ)²) is applied to the normalized advantages, providing a differentiable soft bound that prevents gradient spikes when the anchor is an extreme outlier and σ_R collapses toward zero, while preserving full gradient magnitude for normal-range samples.

## Results

GenAlign achieves state-of-the-art reward modeling accuracy of 73.68% on VL-Reward-Bench and 84.50% on MM-RLHF Reward Bench, outperforming all proprietary, open-source, and specialized reward model baselines. Its most pronounced gains appear on hallucination (90.18% on VL-Reward) and safety (90.50% on MM-RLHF) subsets, exceeding the strongest proprietary models by over 15 and 20 percentage points respectively. In downstream MLLM alignment, GenAlign consistently improves three backbone models across seven benchmarks, with the largest gains on HaloQuest hallucination (e.g., 14.52%→45.16% for LLaVA-1.5-7B) and SPA-VL safety (lowest ASR across all models).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against specialized reward models, GenAlign outperforms IXC-2.5-Reward (65.80→73.68% VL-Reward Acc), R1-Reward (64.51→73.68%), GRLHF-V (57.69→73.69%), and MM-RLHF-Reward (50.15→73.68%) on VL-Reward-Bench, and surpasses all four on MM-RLHF Reward Bench (84.50% vs. 71.18%, 76.00%, 70.00%, and 82.00% respectively). Compared to the strongest proprietary models, GenAlign exceeds Gemini-3-Pro-Preview (71.91%) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (66.31%) on VL-Reward overall accuracy, and outperforms all proprietary baselines on the hallucination subset (90.18% vs. 75.23% for Gemini-3-Pro-Preview). On the MM-RLHF safety subset, GenAlign reaches 90.50%, exceeding the best proprietary model (Claude-3.7-Sonnet at 84.50%) by 6 points and GPT-5-Nano (71.88%) by nearly 19 points. In downstream MLLM alignment (Table 2), GenAlign consistently outperforms Scalar RM, Naive GRM, R1-Reward, and GRLHF-V across all three backbone models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B, LLaVA-1.5-7B) on WildVision win rate, HaloQuest accuracy, and SPA-VL attack success rate, with the largest margin on LLaVA-1.5-7B where HaloQuest accuracy jumps from 14.52% (base) to 45.16%.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenAlign builds directly on the DeepSeek-R1 paradigm of reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, repurposing it from text reasoning to multimodal preference judgment. It departs from the scalar Bradley-Terry reward model lineage (LLaVA-RLHF, IXC-2.5-Reward) by adopting a generative, rubric-based critique mechanism, and from prior GRMs (GRLHF-V, R1-Reward, RM-R1) by addressing their two practical bottlenecks: position bias via an online EMA-based debiasing loop rather than static data augmentation, and quadratic pairwise comparison cost via a dynamic reference anchoring strategy that reduces complexity to linear. The MLLM alignment stage extends GRPO with an advantage-smoothing mechanism specifically designed to handle the variance collapse that arises when a single anchor is an extreme outlier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, unified pipeline for MLLM alignment that is both interpretable (rubric-based critiques) and computationally tractable (68% latency reduction over exhaustive pairwise GRMs), making generative reward modeling viable for large-scale online RL where it was previously prohibitive. However, several open questions remain: the paper relies on GPT-5-mini for both data curation and evaluation, risking distillation bias; causal faithfulness of the rubric reasoning (whether the intermediate critique genuinely drives the final prediction) is explicitly deferred; and the framework inherits the standard RLHF vulnerability to reward hacking. Whether the rubric-based mechanism generalizes beyond the tested 7B–8B model scale, and whether the linear-complexity anchoring strategy degrades in quality as candidate diversity increases, are important directions for future work.
