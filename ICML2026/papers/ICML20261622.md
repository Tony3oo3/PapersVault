## Problem

Large language models encode information in high-dimensional representations, but no formal definition exists for the fundamental representational units (FRUs) underlying these representations. Neurons suffer from polysemanticity and features from instability and incomplete reconstruction, yet neither has been rigorously evaluated against principled criteria. This paper introduces Atom Theory to formally define, evaluate, and identify FRUs—termed atoms—within LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261622/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes LLM hidden-state activations collected at the final token position of subject entity mentions (e.g., 'Danielle Darrieux') across all layers, sourced from WikiData (1.9B samples for training) and CounterFact (20K entities for evaluation). The normalized atomic inner product (NAIP) matrix is estimated as (E[kk^T])^{-1} from 100K Wikipedia activations per layer. Outputs include the identified atom set (decoder columns of the TSAE), per-layer faithfulness scores (R²), stability scores (q*), and monosemanticity ratings via LLM-as-a-Judge with manual verification.

## Method

The paper introduces the atomic inner product (AIP), a non-Euclidean metric derived from the reparameterization invariance of Softmax, which corrects a pervasive representation shift (angular bias away from 90°) observed under the Euclidean inner product. Atoms are formally defined by three properties—representability, sparsity (K-sparse coefficients), and separability (ε-approximate orthogonality under the AIP)—and evaluated via R² (faithfulness) and q* (stability, derived from compressed-sensing coherence bounds). Identification is achieved through threshold-activated sparse autoencoders (TSAEs) with JumpReLU activation, for which the paper proves exact recoverability of the atom set under a support-separation condition (εKδ_max < τ < δ_min − ε(K−1)δ_max). Large-scale experiments reveal that reliable atom identification requires TSAE capacity to exceed a critical threshold relative to data scale, and a 4× expansion ratio with JumpReLU yields near-perfect faithfulness (R²=99.9%) and stability (q*=99.85%) across Gemma2-2B, Gemma2-9B, and Llama3.1-8B.

## Results

Across Gemma2-2B, Gemma2-9B, and Llama3.1-8B, the identified atoms achieve near-perfect faithfulness (R² = 99.85–99.93%) and stability (q* = 99.74–99.95%), averaged over all layers. In contrast, neurons achieve perfect faithfulness (R² = 1) but extremely low stability (q* = 0.5%), while SAE features (GemmaScope/LlamaScope) achieve moderate stability (q* = 68.2%) but poor faithfulness (R² = 48.8%). The proposed atoms are the only units that simultaneously satisfy both criteria of ideal atoms, with monosemanticity scores substantially above random-guess baseline (0.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed atoms against two widely used SAE-based feature extraction tools: GemmaScope (Lieberum et al., 2024) for Gemma2-2B/9B and LlamaScope (He et al., 2024) for Llama3.1-8B. On faithfulness, the proposed atoms (R² ≈ 99.9%) dramatically outperform SAE features (R² ≈ 48.8%), more than doubling reconstruction quality. On stability, the proposed atoms (q* ≈ 99.85%) also substantially exceed SAE features (q* ≈ 68.2%), indicating far greater structural robustness against feature splitting/merging. Neurons, while achieving perfect R² = 1 by construction, collapse on stability (q* = 0.5%), confirming their polysemanticity. The key finding is that no existing unit (neurons or SAE features) simultaneously achieves high scores on both metrics, whereas the proposed atoms do, validating the Atom Theory framework as a principled improvement over both interpretability paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the sparse autoencoder (SAE) line of work (Cunningham et al., 2023; Templeton, 2024) by introducing threshold-activated SAEs (TSAEs) with a provable identifiability guarantee, and on the reparameterization invariance argument of Park et al. (2023) to motivate a non-Euclidean atomic inner product (AIP) that corrects a pervasive geometric distortion in LLM representations. It draws on compressed sensing theory (Donoho, 2006; Candès et al., 2006) to unify sparsity and separability into a single stability metric. Conceptually, the paper departs from the prior interpretability literature that informally treated neurons or SAE features as fundamental representational units, by formally defining 'atoms' with explicit criteria (faithfulness, stability) and proving they are identifiable — a step no prior work had taken.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal theoretical framework for what 'fundamental representational units' in LLMs should be, with provable identifiability, which could reshape how the interpretability community approaches feature extraction and model analysis. The discovery of a pervasive representation shift (corrected by the AIP) is a broad geometric finding that applies across all tested LLM families and has implications for any analysis relying on Euclidean similarity. Key open questions include whether the atom identification approach scales to larger models and more diverse data, how atoms relate to downstream tasks like steering or safety, and whether the framework extends to non-LLM architectures (the authors note preliminary evidence for diffusion models). The paper is explicitly theory-first and does not optimize for specific downstream applications, leaving practical integration as future work.
