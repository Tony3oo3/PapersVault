## Problem

When a pre-trained model is replaced by a newer checkpoint, fine-tuning knowledge encoded in the old model's parameters is difficult to reuse without retraining, especially when the original fine-tuning data is inaccessible. Existing parameter-space transfer methods (e.g., Param Δ, TransFusion) assume the same dataset induces similar parameter updates across base models, but mismatched local loss landscapes make this premise brittle, leading to unstable or even degrading transfers. The paper asks whether a task update can be constructed that is invariant across pre-trained models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three model checkpoints (old base θ_old, old fine-tuned θ_old^C, new base θ_new) along with datasets for diagonal Fisher estimation (pre-training corpus for θ_old and θ_new, task training set for θ_old^C). It outputs a single transferred parameter vector θ_new^C for the new model, computed via a closed-form precision-weighted mean. The Fisher matrix is approximated as diagonal using only a few hundred samples, making the procedure lightweight relative to full fine-tuning (14–117 s vs. ~2000–3200 s).

## Method

BTransfer reframes fine-tuning as variational inference where the base model defines a prior and fine-tuning contributes a prior-agnostic task-update factor U(θ; D_C). The paper proves that a reusable factor (invariant across base models with fixed-dimensional parameterization) exists when the variational family is a convex half-space, and this is maximal among admissible convex families; in the ideal shared exponential-family regime, the factor reduces to the natural-parameter difference η^C − η. For deep networks, BTransfer implements a three-step lift-transfer-return pipeline: (1) lift each point estimate to a Gaussian via Laplace approximation with diagonal Fisher precision, (2) transfer by adding the natural-parameter increments (ΔΛ, Δb) from the old model's fine-tuning to the new model's prior, and (3) return to parameter space via θ_new^C = (Λ_new + ΔΛ)^{-1}(Λ_new θ_new + Δb). This yields a closed-form update that requires no gradient steps on the new model and can be applied jointly to multiple task updates by summing their increments.

## Results

Averaged over all seven GLUE/sentence-classification tasks and all four new backbones (legal, mental, codebert, 4m), BTransfer achieves ~80.1% accuracy, within 0.5 points of the old fine-tuned model θ_old^C (~80.6%) while exceeding the new pre-trained model θ_new (~51.4%) by nearly 30 points. On the most challenging transfer (roberta-base → codebert/4m), BTransfer reaches 80.24% average accuracy versus 59.93% for Param Δ and 59.86% for TransFusion. In the LLM setting (gemma-2-2b-it → BG-2B/TiTU-2B/Atlas-2B/MG2-2B), BTransfer yields average gains of +6.63 to +15.71 points over θ_new, while Param Δ often degrades performance (e.g., −7.08 points on BG-2B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BTransfer consistently and substantially outperforms both parameter-space baselines across all transfer settings. Against Param Δ (Cao et al., 2025), BTransfer gains roughly 8 points on legal/mental backbones (~80% vs. ~72%) and a dramatic ~20 points on codebert/4m (80.24% vs. 59.93%), where Param Δ sometimes even falls below the untransferred θ_new (e.g., 35.44% on MNLI-codebert vs. 35.91% for θ_new). Against TransFusion (Rinaldi et al., 2025), which adds a re-basin permutation step to Param Δ, the gap is similar: TransFusion achieves 59.86% on codebert/4m versus BTransfer's 80.24%, and the two baselines are nearly identical on legal/mental (~72%), confirming that permutation alignment alone cannot compensate for loss-landscape mismatch. In the LLM transfer setting (Tables 3–4), Param Δ degrades performance on average for three of four new models (−7.08, −3.70, −1.52 points), while BTransfer improves all four by +3.70 to +15.71 points. In the vision setting (Table 5), BTransfer achieves the best or second-best accuracy on all four image-classification benchmarks, whereas Param Δ frequently drops below θ_new (e.g., 23.92% on SVHN-datacomp vs. 46.00% for θ_new).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant parameter-space transfer paradigm (Param Δ, TransFusion, task arithmetic) by recasting fine-tuning as a variational inference problem and introducing a prior-agnostic 'task-update factor' that is invariant across base models. It builds on Laplace approximation and diagonal Fisher estimation for practical curvature computation in deep networks, but the key conceptual shift is from additive parameter deltas to multiplicative Bayesian updates in an exponential-family distribution space. The theoretical contribution—a maximality result showing the half-space variational family is the largest convex family admitting a reusable fixed-dimensional update—provides a structural boundary that prior parameter-space methods implicitly violate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BTransfer offers a theoretically grounded, training-free alternative to parameter-space transfer that substantially outperforms existing methods when base models have divergent loss landscapes (e.g., codebert, 4m, TiTU-2B), where Param Δ and TransFusion can even degrade below the unmodified base model. Key limitations include the requirement of shared architecture (blocking heterogeneous transfers), the local nature of the Laplace lift, and non-trivial Fisher estimation cost (though a few hundred samples suffice). Open questions include extending the framework to architecture-changing transfers, replacing the local Gaussian lift with broader approximations, and whether the exponential-family assumption can be relaxed while preserving the maximality guarantee.
