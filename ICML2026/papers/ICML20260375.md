## Problem

Aligning LLMs to serve users with heterogeneous and potentially conflicting preferences is a central challenge, but existing methods like RLHF and NLHF, while optimal for single-sample evaluation, fundamentally underutilize test-time scaling because they collapse to near-deterministic policies that ignore minority preferences. The paper asks whether a single model can approach universal alignment—satisfying essentially all users—through test-time scaling, and characterizes the optimal convergence rate and the alignment objective needed to achieve it.

## Input / Output

The model receives a text prompt x and independently samples k ≥ 1 candidate responses from a single-output policy π: X → Δ(Y), producing a multiset of k responses from which the user selects their preferred one. The alignment guarantee is measured as the win rate of the k-sample product policy π^⊗k against any single-output opponent policy π′, with the key constraint that the policy must be a standard single-output distribution (not a joint distribution over Y^k) to remain compatible with existing LLM architectures and training pipelines.

## Method

The paper introduces (k, f(k))-robust alignment and asymptotic U-alignment, proving the optimal convergence rate is f(k) = k/(k+1) and that no method can do better. The key mechanism is a symmetric (k+1)-player alignment game in which each player's utility is the win rate of their single-output policy against the product policy of all other k players; any symmetric Nash equilibrium of this game achieves the optimal rate when its policy is sampled k times at test time. This contrasts with NLHF's two-player game, where the equilibrium can collapse to a deterministic majority-preferred response, rendering additional samples redundant. The paper further provides convergence guarantees for self-play no-regret dynamics in the multi-player game and extends the framework to opponents that also generate multiple responses.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.4) establishes that the optimal convergence rate for U-alignment via test-time scaling is k/(k+1): there exists a family of single-output policies whose k-sample product policies achieve (k, k/(k+1))-robust alignment, and no method can achieve a faster rate (Propositions 3.2, 3.3). The proposed Multi-Player Nash Equilibrium (MPNE) policy, obtained as the symmetric Nash equilibrium of a (k+1)-player alignment game, achieves this optimal rate (Theorem 4.3). In contrast, test-time scaling of the NLHF policy is fundamentally limited: for any k ≥ 1, there exist instances where the k-sample NLHF policy cannot guarantee a win rate above 1/2 + ε for arbitrarily small ε (Proposition 4.1). Additionally, Theorem 4.5 shows graceful degradation: a policy optimized for k samples maintains a win rate of min{k,ℓ}/(k+1) when evaluated with ℓ ≠ k samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper provides theoretical (not empirical) comparisons against two existing alignment methods. Against NLHF (Munos et al., 2024; Swamy et al., 2024), the proposed MPNE policy strictly dominates under test-time scaling: NLHF achieves only (1, 1/2)-robust alignment and its k-sample extension cannot exceed 1/2 + ε win rate in the worst case (Proposition 4.1), whereas MPNE achieves (k, k/(k+1))-robust alignment, which approaches 1 as k grows. The root cause of NLHF's limitation is mode collapse: when a Condorcet winner exists, NLHF collapses to a deterministic policy, making additional samples redundant. Against RLHF (Christiano et al., 2017; Ouyang et al., 2022), the paper argues qualitatively that the Bradley-Terry scalar reward model fundamentally fails to capture non-transitive or heterogeneous preferences, introducing systematic bias. The paper also distinguishes its multi-player game from the concurrent work of Wu et al. (2026), noting that in Wu et al.'s game the NLHF policy is already a symmetric Nash equilibrium, so their formulation offers no theoretical advantage over standard NLHF, whereas in the proposed game the symmetric NE achieves the optimal U-alignment rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NLHF framework (Munos et al., 2024; Swamy et al., 2024), which formulates LLM alignment as a two-player zero-sum game, into a (k+1)-player symmetric alignment game whose Nash equilibrium achieves optimal test-time scaling. It also draws on social choice theory—specifically Condorcet winning sets and α-undominated committees (Cheng et al., 2020; Jiang et al., 2020; Charikar et al., 2025)—to establish existence of multi-output policies with the k/(k+1) rate, but departs from this line by showing that single-output policies suffice via a new game-theoretic construction. The paper positions itself against both RLHF (which assumes Bradley-Terry preferences and fails to capture non-transitive diversity) and NLHF (which is optimal for k=1 but provably cannot improve under test-time scaling due to mode collapse).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication is that post-training objectives and test-time scaling can be fundamentally misaligned: the provably optimal single-sample policy (NLHF) is the worst possible policy for test-time scaling, collapsing to deterministic outputs that make additional samples redundant. This challenges the common assumption that a well-aligned model automatically benefits from generating more candidates. Key open questions include the lack of any experimental validation on real LLMs, the gap between upper and lower bounds when the opponent also generates multiple responses (ℓ>1), and the design of reliable selection mechanisms that can pick the best response from a diverse candidate set without introducing errors.
