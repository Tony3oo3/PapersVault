## Problem

Self-evolving LLM agents are designed to accumulate and reuse past experience to improve over time, yet it remains unclear whether they actually and faithfully rely on that experience to guide their decisions. Prior work has focused on how experiences are stored and represented, leaving open the question of whether performance gains are genuinely attributable to the retrieved content or to other factors such as formatting cues or pretrained priors.

## Input / Output

The evaluation pipeline takes a task instruction (e.g., a question, a web navigation goal, or an embodied task) augmented with retrieved raw trajectories (full multi-step observation-action sequences) and/or condensed summaries (distilled heuristics or causal lessons) as input, and produces the agent's task output (answer, action sequence, or final state) under each intervention condition. Faithfulness is operationalized as the magnitude of behavioral change: if perturbing the experience (via Empty, Shuffle, Irrelevant, Corrupt, or Filler interventions) causes a significant performance drop, the agent is deemed faithful to that experience type. The unusual aspect is that the 'output' is not a single prediction but a matrix of performance scores across intervention types, frameworks, backbones, and environments, enabling a causal attribution of where experience actually matters.

## Method

The paper introduces a systematic causal intervention framework that selectively perturbs raw experience (Empty, Shuffle, Irrelevant) and condensed experience (Empty, Corrupt, Irrelevant, Filler) while holding all other prompt components fixed, then measures downstream behavioral change as a faithfulness signal. This is applied across four representative self-evolving frameworks (ExpeL for offline single-agent, Dynamic Cheatsheet and ReasoningBank for online single-agent, G-Memory for online multi-agent), 13 LLM backbones spanning 1.7B to 235B parameters, and 9 environments covering knowledge QA, math reasoning, embodied action, and web interaction. To diagnose *why* condensed experience is underutilized, the authors combine three complementary analyses: (1) error taxonomy of cases where condensed experience actively harms performance (distraction, overreliance on incorrect priors, premature inference), (2) layer-wise Integrated Gradients attribution showing that condensed experience segments receive persistently low influence scores while the current trajectory dominates later layers, and (3) controlled experiments on knowledge-intensive multi-hop QA tasks demonstrating that pretrained priors alone suffice, reducing the marginal utility of any retrieved experience. The key finding is a robust raw-condensed faithfulness asymmetry that persists across model scales, agent configurations, and paradigms, pointing to semantic limitations of condensed content, internal attention biases, and task-regime effects as the three root causes.

## Results

Across 4 self-evolving frameworks, 13 LLM backbones, and 9 environments, the paper finds a stark faithfulness asymmetry: perturbing raw experience (e.g., Empty, Irrelevant) causes substantial performance drops, while perturbing condensed experience (Corrupt, Irrelevant, Filler) yields changes nearly indistinguishable from baseline. For example, on ALFWorld under G-Memory with GPT-5.2, shuffling execution raw experience drops success from 75.4 to 47.0, whereas condensed interventions produce only marginal or inconsistent shifts. On knowledge-intensive tasks (HotpotQA, GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro), even raw experience interventions show limited impact, as pretrained priors already suffice. Scaling from Qwen3-1.7B to 32B improves overall performance but does not close the raw–condensed faithfulness gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical diagnostic study rather than a method comparison, so it evaluates four existing self-evolving frameworks (ExpeL, Dynamic Cheatsheet, ReasoningBank, G-Memory) under identical causal interventions. All four frameworks exhibit the same pattern: strong faithfulness to raw trajectories and weak faithfulness to condensed summaries, regardless of whether the setting is offline or online, single-agent or multi-agent. The asymmetry holds across 13 backbones spanning Qwen3 (1.7B–32B, 30B-A3B, 235B-A22B), GPT-4o, GPT-5.2, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Gemini-3-Pro, and Claude-Sonnet-4.6. In the condensed-only ReasoningBank setting (no raw experience available), agents still show minimal sensitivity to semantic perturbations of condensed summaries, ruling out raw-experience overshadowing as the explanation. The one notable exception is knowledge-intensive QA tasks (HotpotQA, GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro, FEVER), where both raw and condensed interventions produce small effects because the model's pretrained priors already solve the task without external experience.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the faithfulness literature—from in-context learning (Min et al., 2022; Shi et al., 2024) and chain-of-thought reasoning (Lanham et al., 2023; Turpin et al., 2023)—into the self-evolving agent setting, where experience is dynamic, multifaceted, and stored externally. It repurposes causal intervention techniques (perturbation-based probing) and attribution analysis (Integrated Gradients) to define and measure a new construct: the causal dependence of an agent's decisions on its retrieved experience. The four evaluated frameworks (ExpeL, Dynamic Cheatsheet, ReasoningBank, G-Memory) represent the current state of the art in offline and online self-evolving agents, but prior work on these systems focused on performance gains rather than whether the gains are faithfully attributable to the experience itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that agents systematically ignore or misinterpret condensed experience—even when it is the sole external guidance—challenges a core design assumption in memory-augmented agent research and suggests that much of the reported 'experience-driven' improvement may be superficial. The three identified causes (semantic vagueness of summaries, internal attention biases favoring local context, and task regimes where pretrained priors suffice) point toward concrete redesign directions: contextually grounded condensation, dynamic experience injection, and selective retrieval. Open questions include whether any condensation strategy can overcome the structural underutilization revealed by the IG analysis, and whether the faithfulness gap persists in settings where experience is the only viable path (e.g., truly novel tasks outside pretrained knowledge).
