## Problem

While detecting causal mechanism shifts (CMS) across environments has been well-studied, identifying the *driver* of a shift—whether it stems from a change in the deterministic causal function or from a change in the exogenous noise distribution—remains largely unexplored. Existing dissection methods (iSCAN, LinearCCP, GPR) rely on restrictive additive noise model assumptions and inter-parent additivity constraints, limiting their applicability to general non-additive, non-linear structural causal models.

## Input / Output

The method takes two environment datasets X^(1) and X^(2) along with the known causal DAG from the base environment. A single Causal Normalizing Flow is trained once on environment 1 data (40k train / 10k validation in experiments); environment 2 requires only forward inference with no retraining. The output is a set of shifted nodes S and, for each node in S, a binary label (function shift or noise shift) determined by a distance-correlation independence test between the CNF-inferred residual noise and the node's parent variables.

## Method

FANS leverages a Causal Normalizing Flow (CNF) trained on the base environment to learn an invertible mapping from observed data to independent base-noise variables, extending dissection beyond additive noise models to general non-linear, non-additive SCMs. For detection, it computes counterfactual distributions via the CNF's do-operator at quantile conditioning points and flags nodes where the Jensen-Shannon divergence between environments exceeds a threshold. For dissection, it applies the base CNF to environment 2 data to obtain a residual noise, then tests statistical independence between this residual and the parents using distance correlation: dependence indicates a function shift (the base mechanism cannot explain the new data without parent-dependent corrections), while independence indicates a noise shift. To handle the non-identifiability of simultaneous function-and-noise shifts, the paper introduces a Generalized Affine Interaction assumption under which a chi-squared test on the standardized squared noise distinguishes function-only from simultaneous shifts. Finite-sample consistency of the dissection statistic is established under L² CNF estimation consistency, showing correct classification probability tending to one as sample size grows.

## Results

On synthetic detection tasks (Table 1), FANS achieves F1-scores of 0.975–1.000 across all ER and SF graph configurations (J=10–50), outperforming the closest competitor PreDITEr (0.933–0.978) by 1–7 percentage points. On the dissection task (Table 2), FANS reaches 0.947–1.000 F1, surpassing GPR (0.722–0.922) and LinearCCP (0.700–0.830). For the harder simultaneous-shift classification (Table 3), FANS achieves a macro F1 of 0.622 versus GPR's 0.378. On Morpho-MNIST, FANS correctly localizes 57 of the top-100 truly shifted pixels, compared to only 3 for iSCAN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FANS outperforms all six baselines on both detection and dissection tasks. On detection (Table 1), the closest competitor is PreDITEr (Varici et al., 2022), which achieves 0.933–0.978 F1; FANS leads by 1–7 points, reaching perfect 1.000 on all SF graphs. LinearCCP (Huang et al., 2024) is the next strongest at 0.609–0.784, but its linear additive assumption limits it on the non-linear, non-additive synthetic SCMs. iSCAN (Chen et al., 2023) and SplitKCI (Pogodin et al., 2024) trail significantly (0.293–0.553 F1), with iSCAN additionally suffering from excessive Type I error that forced it to use only 1,000 samples. On dissection (Table 2), FANS (0.947–1.000) beats GPR/Li et al. (0.722–0.922) by 4–28 points, and LinearCCP (0.700–0.830) by 15–30 points. For the simultaneous-shift classification (Table 3), FANS (0.622 macro F1) more than doubles GPR's score (0.378), though the absolute performance remains moderate due to the inherent difficulty of the task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Causal Normalizing Flows (CNF, Javaloy et al. 2023) as its architectural backbone, repurposing the CNF's invertible noise mapping and do-operator to serve as a residual estimator for shift dissection. It departs from prior CMS detection/dissection methods (iSCAN, LinearCCP, GPR) that operate under additive noise model (ANM) assumptions by extending to general non-additive, non-linear SCMs, and unifies detection and dissection into a single two-stage pipeline that trains once on the base environment. The independence criterion (residual noise ⊥ parents ⟺ noise shift) is a clean theoretical departure from the moment-based or kernel-based tests used by prior dissection methods, and the formal non-identifiability characterization of simultaneous function+noise shifts with a resolving assumption (generalized affine interaction) is new to the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dissection problem—attributing a mechanism shift to a function change versus a noise change—has practical consequences: misattributing a noise shift (e.g., new renewable energy forecast errors) as a function shift leads to incorrect policy or market conclusions. The paper's main limitations are the requirement of a known causal DAG, invertible structural equations (excluding discrete mechanisms), and the absence of unobserved confounding; the identifiability guarantee for simultaneous shifts further relies on a restrictive generalized affine interaction structure whose empirical scope is untested. Open directions include extending to discrete variables via dequantization, integrating with causal discovery to relax the known-DAG assumption, and leveraging residual patterns as a diagnostic for hidden confounders.
