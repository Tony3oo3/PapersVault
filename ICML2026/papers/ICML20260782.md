## Problem

Training large-scale generative models relies on heuristic dataset weighting and is prohibitively expensive to repeat for every new data mixture. Existing modular approaches—Mixture-of-Experts, model merging, task arithmetic—lack theoretical guarantees for worst-case robustness across arbitrary distribution shifts. This paper asks whether small, domain-specific experts can be combined via a single gate that is provably robust to any data mixture, eliminating heuristic tuning entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260782/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes p frozen, pre-trained expert models (each approximating a source distribution) and a text sequence as input; the gate network maps the full sequence (non-causal) or token prefix (causal router) to a distribution over experts. The output is a globally normalized mixture probability π_g(x) = Σ_k g(x,k)π̂_k(x) that is a valid probability distribution, with the critical constraint Z_g = 1 coupling gate decisions across all inputs in the support. After Structural Distillation, the system produces standard autoregressive token-by-token generation with O(1) per-step cost while preserving the frozen experts.

## Method

The paper formulates robust modularity as a minimax game min_{g∈G_1} max_{λ∈Δ} D_KL(p̂_λ ‖ π_g) over the space of normalized gates, proving existence of a saddle point via Kakutani's fixed-point theorem. The central theoretical insight is a Symmetric Divergence Effect: the Jensen-Shannon Divergence acts as a separability bonus for the modular gate (canceling the LogSumExp capacity cost) while simultaneously acting as an irreducible interference floor for monolithic models, yielding a provable performance gap characterized by D_JSD. For optimization, a Stochastic Primal-Dual algorithm enforces the global normalization constraint via a scalar Lagrange multiplier (avoiding intractable projection over the full support), and a Structural Distillation procedure converts the non-causal optimal gate into a lightweight causal router trained on cached expert logits, enabling efficient autoregressive inference while preserving modularity.

## Results

On real-world data (Wikipedia, The Stack, FineWeb-Edu; ~20M params), the Gate Model achieves NLL of 4.994±0.013 (varying seed) and 5.087±0.141 (varying data split), outperforming both the monolithic Retrained Model (5.133/5.306) and a Standard MoE trained from scratch (5.080/5.245). At 1B+ scale (three ~1.6B GPT-2 XL-style experts vs. a 4.9B monolithic baseline), the Gate Model achieves 4.734±0.006 NLL, a 0.095 improvement over the monolithic baseline's 4.829±0.012. On synthetic contradictory-domain data, the Gate outperforms even Oracle models (trained on the exact test mixture) in the high-interference region (λ≈0.5), empirically validating the JSD interference gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Gate Model outperforms the Standard MoE (Switch Transformer-style, Fedus et al. 2022) on both evaluation settings: 4.994 vs. 5.080 NLL (diff seed) and 5.087 vs. 5.245 NLL (diff data), with the gap widening under distribution shift. Against the monolithic Retrained Model of matching size, the Gate wins by 0.139 NLL (diff seed) and 0.219 NLL (diff data), confirming that joint training over conflicting domains induces gradient interference even in standard MoE architectures due to shared representation layers. On synthetic data, the Gate surpasses even the 'cheating' Larger Oracle (trained on the exact test mixture) in the high-interference region (λ∈[0.3,0.7]), but loses at the extremes (λ≈0 or 1) where the task collapses to a single domain. The paper also proves (Theorem 3.4) that in convex model families, the gated model coincides exactly with the retrained model, so modularity is a 'safe' prior that never hurts in benign settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Multiple-Source Domain Adaptation (MSA) minimax framework (Mansour et al., 2008) to the generative setting, where the KL-divergence objective and the global normalization constraint Z_g=1 introduce fundamentally different mathematical challenges compared to the linear-reward routing of Dann et al. (2025). Architecturally, it departs from standard Mixture-of-Experts (Shazeer et al., 2017; Fedus et al., 2022) by operating on frozen, pre-trained experts rather than jointly trained sub-networks, and from static merging methods (Model Soups, task arithmetic) by using input-dependent gating. The Structural Distillation component adapts Hinton et al.'s (2015) knowledge distillation to transfer a non-causal gate into a causal router, while the soft probabilistic mixing distinguishes the approach from hard-selection learning-to-defer frameworks (Cortes et al., 2016).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central conceptual contribution—the 'Symmetric Divergence Effect,' where the same Jensen-Shannon Divergence that acts as an irreducible interference floor for monolithic models becomes a separability bonus for modular gates—provides a principled explanation for when and why modularity should outperform retraining, replacing heuristic dataset weighting with a game-theoretic guarantee. Practically, this enables cheaper and more sustainable LLM development: new domains require training only a small expert plus a lightweight gate, avoiding catastrophic forgetting and full retraining. Open questions include how the framework interacts with RLHF and instruction tuning, whether the Primal-Dual dynamics remain stable with very large expert counts (p >> 10), and whether the distillation gap between the non-causal gate and causal router grows with sequence length in realistic LLM settings.
