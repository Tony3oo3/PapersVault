## Problem

Intrinsic exploration methods for visual RL under sparse rewards are either not task-aware (susceptible to nuisance visual variations like texture and illumination) or rely on bisimulation metrics that degenerate to zero distance when rewards are absent, causing representation collapse. Even when bisimulation metrics are used for exploration bonuses, they tend to encourage only local transitions rather than global task-relevant exploration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262700/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

In MetaWorld, the agent receives 84×84 RGB pixel observations containing distracting visual elements and outputs a continuous action vector for robotic manipulation; rewards are sparse and success-based. In Maze2D, the agent operates on low-dimensional 2D position states (no encoder needed) and outputs continuous navigation actions. The method additionally produces a learned latent representation z = φ_ω(s) and a scalar intrinsic exploration bonus F(s_t, a, s_{t+1}) that is added to the extrinsic reward during policy training.

## Method

TEB introduces a predictive bisimulation metric that replaces the ground-truth reward differential with a Gaussian reward predictor's predicted differential Δ_R^π, whose learned variance σ_θ guarantees a strictly positive expected metric diameter even in zero-reward regions (Theorem 3.3), preventing the representation collapse that plagues standard bisimulation under sparse rewards. The encoder is trained via a bootstrapped bisimulation regression loss against this predictive metric target, jointly with the reward predictor (NLL loss) and a dynamics model. For exploration, TEB constructs a potential function Φ(s_t) = d_pre(s_t, s_★) measuring the bisimulation distance from the current state to a pseudo-anchor state (batch-averaged reward and next-latent-state), then computes a potential-based shaping bonus F = γΦ(s_{t+1}) − Φ(s_t) that encourages global novelty relative to the anchor while provably preserving the optimal policy (Theorem 3.5). The entire pipeline is built on the DrQ-v2 off-policy backbone, with the shaped reward r' = r + ηF used for actor-critic updates.

## Results

On MetaWorld (visual RL with sparse rewards), TEB achieves the highest success rate on 5 of 6 tasks, reaching 93.1% on Pick-out-of-hole, 98.4% on Push-back, and 87.8% on Stick-pull, while only trailing ICM by 0.5% on Box-close (96.8% vs. 97.3%). On the reward-free Maze2D exploration benchmark, TEB achieves the highest state coverage across all 7 maze layouts, with particularly large margins over the strongest baseline CeSD: 0.87 vs. 0.71 on Square-a and 0.85 vs. 0.66 on Square-b. TEB also outperforms the closest metric-based baselines (LIBERTY, EME) by 10–20+ percentage points on the hardest MetaWorld tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEB decisively outperforms the two closest metric-based exploration baselines, LIBERTY and EME, on all MetaWorld tasks where they are competitive (e.g., Push-back: 98.4% vs. 33.0% and 62.0%; Stick-pull: 87.8% vs. 82.4% and 61.0%), and on all Maze2D layouts (e.g., Square-a: 0.87 vs. 0.62 and 0.61). Against the strongest general exploration baselines, TEB beats ICM by 17.0 points on Pick-out-of-hole (93.1% vs. 76.1%) and by 26.9 points on Stick-pull (87.8% vs. 71.5%), while ICM is the only method to marginally edge TEB on Box-close (97.3% vs. 96.8%). Skill-discovery baselines CeSD and CTRL-SR fail entirely (0% success) on Pick-out-of-hole, Box-close, and Stick-pull in MetaWorld, and in Maze2D TEB surpasses CeSD by 16–19 percentage points on the Square series (0.87 vs. 0.71 on Square-a). Among the remaining baselines, RAP (65.0% on Push-back) and DrM (61.1%) are substantially outperformed, while LBS and RND show the weakest exploration coverage in Maze2D (0.21–0.44 range vs. TEB's 0.47–0.93). Overall, TEB wins or ties on 12 of 13 task-benchmark combinations, with the sole loss being a 0.5-point margin on Box-close.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEB extends the bisimulation-metric line of work initiated by Castro (2020) and Chen & Pan (2022), which established policy-dependent bisimulation distances as a principled tool for learning task-relevant representations from high-dimensional observations. The most direct predecessors are LIBERTY (Wang et al., 2023) and EME (Wang et al., 2024), which also derive exploration bonuses from bisimulation-style metrics but augment them with heuristic inverse-dynamics or KL-divergence terms. TEB departs from these by (i) replacing the ground-truth reward differential with a predictive Gaussian reward differential to provably prevent metric collapse under sparse rewards, and (ii) using the same metric uniformly for both representation learning and a global anchor-state-based potential function, rather than introducing ad-hoc auxiliary terms. The potential-based shaping framework of Ng et al. (1999) underpins the policy-invariance guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and previously unresolved failure mode—bisimulation metric collapse under sparse rewards—with a theoretically grounded fix (Theorem 3.3) that is simple to implement (a Gaussian reward head). The unified use of one metric for both representation and exploration is architecturally clean and may inspire similar 'one-metric-to-rule-them-all' designs in other metric-based RL settings. Limitations include evaluation on only six MetaWorld tasks with three seeds, a batch-dependent (non-stationary) anchor mechanism whose policy-invariance argument is only update-wise, and reliance on a Gaussian reward assumption that may not hold in all domains. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or multi-task visual settings and how sensitive performance is to the hyperparameter η, which required task-specific tuning (0.05 to 10).
