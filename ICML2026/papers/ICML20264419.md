## Problem

ML-based protein design tools (ProteinMPNN, BoltzGen, RFdiffusion) are restricted to the 20 canonical amino acids and narrow design pipelines, while physics-based Rosetta can model non-canonical residues and arbitrary geometries but demands deep biophysical expertise and familiarity with its unconventional RosettaScripts XML syntax. This paper asks whether an LLM agent can make Rosetta's full design space accessible, and critically discovers that prompt engineering alone is insufficient—frontier LLMs regularly generate semantically incorrect RosettaScripts actions even when the relevant documentation is in their training data, revealing that structured environment design is a prerequisite for reliable agent–scientific-software integration.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a free-text design brief (e.g., 'replace all amino acids so the core is well-packed and the fold is preserved') together with an initial PDB structure, and iteratively produces refined protein designs as ensembles of 128 candidate PDB poses per step. Rather than feeding raw PDB files (hundreds of lines each) to the LLM, the environment compresses state into task-dependent surrogate metrics—ESMFold RMSD and pLDDT for canonical designs, Rosetta energy, cavity volume, radius of gyration, and per-residue fa_rep/rama_prepro flags for NCAA designs—plus a tabular history of prior actions. The agent's output at each turn is a single structured XML action call (one of three types) with generated parameters, which the environment parses, executes across parallel processes, and feeds back as updated metrics.

## Method

Agent Rosetta is a single-agent, multi-turn framework that wraps RosettaScripts into three semantic actions—rotamer_change (FastDesign with compositional penalties, TaskOperations, and ResidueSelectors), backbone_change (Small/Shear/Backrub movers), and go_back_to_step (trajectory rollback)—each defined as an XML template whose parameters the LLM fills in. The interaction follows a two-step protocol per turn: the agent first reasons over the current state and history to select an action, then, after the environment returns ad-hoc documentation for that action, reasons again to generate the full parameterized call, with an error-correction loop for syntax or runtime failures. A key design choice is abstracting RosettaScripts' counter-intuitive composition-penalty syntax into a simplified intermediate form converted by Python scripts, which raises success rates from ~70% (best LLM, original syntax) to ≥98.88% across all tested models. At each step, only Pareto-optimal candidates (ranked by RMSD, pLDDT, Rosetta energy, and task-specific metrics) are carried forward, and a tabular summary of prior actions prevents context-window saturation over 30-step trajectories. The paper validates that this environment design—not just model scale or prompting—is what enables frontier LLMs to match ProteinMPNN/BoltzGen on canonical design and to outperform human-written protocols on non-canonical amino acid insertion, where ML baselines are inapplicable.

## Results

For fixed-backbone sequence design with canonical amino acids, Agent Rosetta achieves performance on par with ProteinMPNN and BoltzGen (within 0.20 Å RMSD tolerance), with AF3 validation showing GPT-5-driven designs at 0.8 Å RMSD / pLDDT 91 versus ProteinMPNN's 0.5 Å / pLDDT 94, and no significant difference in 1 µs MD stability. For non-canonical amino acid (TRF) core insertion, Agent Rosetta (GPT-5) outperforms the human-written protocol on AF3 RMSD and pLDDT, and MD simulations confirm statistically superior stability (median RMSD 2.6 Å vs 3.1 Å; loop secondary-structure change 13% vs 22%; paired Wilcoxon test, FWER 0.05). On the most challenging structure (8UZL), the human baseline fails entirely (0% inclusion) while GPT-5 achieves ~14.5% TRF core inclusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ProteinMPNN, Agent Rosetta (GPT-5) is competitive but slightly behind on AF3 validation (0.8 Å vs 0.5 Å RMSD; pLDDT 91 vs 94) and shows no significant difference in 1 µs MD secondary-structure stability (helices 2% vs 4%, strands 4% vs 3%, loops 4% vs 6%). Against BoltzGen, the agent is similarly within the 0.20 Å RMSD tolerance, though BoltzGen tends to achieve higher pLDDT scores. Against the human-written Rosetta protocol for canonical design, Agent Rosetta outperforms on structure 9PL1 and helps close the gap to ProteinMPNN on 9KGY, while the staged human protocol remains competitive on other backbones. For the NCAA task, where no ML baseline is applicable, Agent Rosetta (GPT-5) clearly outperforms the human baseline: it achieves higher AF3 pLDDT, lower AF3 RMSD, and statistically superior MD stability (lower RMSD and smaller secondary-structure drift across all three categories), with the advantage most pronounced on the difficult 8UZL β-barrel where the human protocol fails to insert any TRF.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of LLM-based scientific agents and physics-based protein design. Unlike prior multi-agent orchestration frameworks (ProtAgents, CRISPR-GPT, Biomni) that coordinate several ML-friendly tools at a high level, Agent Rosetta performs low-level, multi-turn integration of a single LLM with one specialized scientific software (Rosetta) through a structured RosettaScripts environment. It departs from the implicit assumption in much of the scientific-agent literature that prompting suffices to elicit correct domain-specific code, instead demonstrating that syntactic abstraction and environment design are necessary for reliable operation. On the protein design side, it extends the well-studied fixed-backbone sequence design task (addressed by ProteinMPNN, BoltzGen) into the non-canonical amino acid regime where data-driven models are inapplicable, leveraging Rosetta's physics-based generality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical finding—that structured environment design, not prompt engineering, is what enables LLM agents to reliably operate complex scientific software—has broad implications for building agents in any domain with non-mainstream scripting interfaces. The demonstration that an agent can match specialized ML models on canonical design and outperform human-written protocols on NCAA design suggests a path toward democratizing access to physics-based simulation tools. However, the work is limited to RosettaScripts and a small set of PDB structures, lacks experimental validation, and the generalization to other scientific software or broader protein families remains untested. Open questions include whether reinforcement learning (e.g., GRPO) on the structured environment can further improve smaller open-source models, and whether the environment-design principle transfers to other domains like quantum chemistry or materials simulation.
