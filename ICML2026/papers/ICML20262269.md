## Problem

Existing rehearsal-free continual learning methods based on orthogonal projection or rigid low-rank constraints prevent interference with prior knowledge but progressively limit model plasticity as the task sequence grows. Their reliance on linear approximations causes projected gradients to deviate from the true nonlinear loss manifold, and binary masking of curvature directions fails to account for the heavy-tailed power-law structure of the Fisher Information Matrix. The paper asks whether a continuous, curvature-aware mechanism can dynamically balance stability and plasticity without imprecise retrieval or rigid subspace constraints.

## Input / Output

MoCL consumes a sequential stream of RGB image classification tasks with mutually disjoint label spaces (e.g., 40 classes per task on ImageNet-R, 10 per task on CIFAR-100) and produces a single unified class prediction over all previously seen classes without requiring task identity at inference. The backbone is a pre-trained ViT-B/16 (ImageNet-21K pre-training, ImageNet-1K fine-tuning), and only attention layers are updated by default, though the method extends to full-parameter training. No raw training samples are stored (rehearsal-free), and inference incurs no component-selection overhead beyond the standard forward pass.

## Method

MoCL approximates the Fisher Information Matrix per layer via K-FAC (Kronecker product of input-activation and pre-activation-gradient covariances), then extracts principal eigenspaces with adaptive rank truncation to form compact factorized subspaces that encode task-specific curvature. A Tsallis-entropy metabolic gate G_q(λ) — whose shape adapts to the estimated tail heaviness ξ of the FIM spectrum via the Hill estimator — continuously modulates gradient components: it drives the gate toward zero for dominant high-curvature spikes (suppressing interference) and toward one for low-curvature sloppy modes (preserving plasticity). After each task, the current eigenspace is merged into a consolidated historical subspace via Incremental SVD, and the modulated gradient is projected through the factorized subspace using a Hadamard-product gating formula. The optimizer's momentum is further compressed via MLorc (Randomized SVD on Adam's first- and second-order moments), reducing peak VRAM to 10.25 GB while maintaining full-rank gradient updates.

## Results

On ImageNet-R with 20 tasks, MoCL achieves 79.22% final accuracy (A_last) and 85.03% average accuracy (A_avg), outperforming the strongest baseline CoSO (77.57% / 82.65%) by 1.65 and 2.38 points respectively. On CIFAR-100 (10 tasks), MoCL reaches 88.12% A_last and 92.74% A_avg, surpassing the next-best SD-LoRA (86.94% / 91.79%). The performance gap over baselines widens as the number of sequential tasks increases, with MoCL maintaining 79.22% A_last at N=20 versus 77.57% for CoSO and 70.22% for InfLoRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoCL outperforms all seven baselines on A_last across every benchmark and task length. On ImageNet-R (N=20), MoCL leads CoSO by 1.65 points (79.22 vs. 77.57) and SD-LoRA by 2.37 points (79.22 vs. 76.85), with a much larger margin over prompt-based methods like L2P (69.38) and DualPrompt (65.78). On CIFAR-100, MoCL (88.12) beats SD-LoRA (86.94) and CODA-Prompt (86.72), while on DomainNet it surpasses CoSO (73.10) and SD-LoRA (72.56). On ImageNet-A, MoCL achieves the best A_last (58.40) over EASE (57.84) and CoSO (57.27); notably, CoSO attains the highest A_avg (71.27) on this benchmark but suffers severe task imbalance (~82% on early tasks dropping to ~18% on later tasks), whereas MoCL maintains balanced performance throughout. The advantage of MoCL over baselines grows with task count, suggesting its metabolic gating better preserves plasticity over long sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoCL departs from the dominant paradigm of orthogonal gradient projection (GPM, SGP, CoSO) and rigid LoRA subspace constraints (InfLoRA, SD-LoRA) by replacing binary masks with a continuous metabolic gating function derived from Tsallis non-extensive statistics. It builds on K-FAC curvature approximation (Martens & Grosse, 2015) but repurposes the Fisher Information Matrix not as an optimizer preconditioner but as a sensitivity map whose heavy-tailed eigenspectrum (motivated by Random Matrix Theory) determines where to suppress versus permit updates. The momentum compression from MLorc (Shen et al., 2025) is adopted for memory efficiency, while the factorized subspace maintenance via Incremental SVD avoids the explicit matrix inversions that plague prior K-FAC-based continual learning methods (NCL, XK-FAC).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoCL offers a practical rehearsal-free continual learning framework that simultaneously achieves the best accuracy, lowest VRAM, and nonlinear-layer compatibility among compared methods—a combination no single prior method achieves. The LMC analysis providing empirical evidence that metabolic gating steers optimization toward shared low-loss regions is a useful diagnostic for the field. However, the method's dependence on the quality of online K-FAC curvature estimation, the fixed cumulative-energy threshold for rank truncation, and its restriction to the CIL setting (rather than online or domain-incremental learning) leave meaningful open questions about robustness and generalizability.
