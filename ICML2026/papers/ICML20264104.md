## Problem

When privileged features W are available only during training but not at deployment, the standard Two-Stage pseudo-labeling approach can suffer negative transfer: if W carries weak or noisy signal, the rich-view teacher's errors propagate into the deployment model and degrade performance below supervised learning on X alone. No existing method provides a principled mechanism to adaptively attenuate the influence of privileged information when it is unhelpful while still exploiting it when it is informative.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n labeled samples (X, W, Y) and m ≫ n unlabeled paired samples (X, W), where X are deployment features available at test time and W are privileged features available only during training. The output is a deployment predictor f(X) that depends solely on X, producing either a continuous regression value or a class probability. The privileged block W can be higher-dimensional than X (e.g., 40-dim W vs. 5-dim X in the synthetic classification diagnostic), and the method is designed to handle cases where W contains substantial nuisance variation beyond its useful signal.

## Method

Coupled Training jointly estimates the deployment model f and the rich-view model g through alternating updates: f is fit to labeled responses Y plus pseudo-responses g(Z) on unlabeled data, while g is recalibrated to match f(X) on unlabeled data subject to a λ-weighted penalty on its fit to labeled Y. The tuning parameter λ interpolates between ignoring privileged information (λ→0, reducing to supervised learning on X) and full Two-Stage pseudo-labeling (λ→∞), with an interior optimum that adaptively balances the two regimes. For high-dimensional settings, an Alternating Forward Selection (AFS) procedure incrementally builds f and g from finite dictionaries, achieving a sublinear O(log k / k) optimization guarantee with O(N|D|k) time complexity per iteration. A correlation score ρ* between the estimation errors of f and g provides a theoretical risk bound showing that the method's excess loss converts to deployment prediction error with a constant that degrades gracefully as W becomes less informative.

## Results

On the Parkinson's Telemonitoring regression task (subject-level split, MSE), Coupled Training achieves 72.60, improving over the X-only Baseline (77.34) and substantially outperforming Two-Stage pseudo-labeling (83.32). On the Bank Marketing classification task (26-seed mean holdout Brier score), Coupled Training achieves 0.0881, beating the Baseline (0.0893), Two-Stage (0.0928), squared-loss generalized distillation (0.0907), and Vapnik SVM+ (0.1470). The method wins over Two-Stage in 26/26 seeds and over the Baseline in 22/26 seeds on Bank Marketing. Synthetic experiments confirm the expected U-shaped dependence on the coupling parameter λ, with optimal performance at intermediate values that adapt to privileged signal strength.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Two-Stage pseudo-labeling procedure (Xia & Wainwright, 2024), Coupled Training consistently wins: on Parkinson's it reduces MSE from 83.32 to 72.60 (a 12.9% relative improvement), and on Bank Marketing it lowers mean Brier score from 0.0928 to 0.0881, winning in all 26/26 seeds. The key advantage is that Two-Stage propagates rich-view noise into the deployment model when privileged features are weak or noisy, whereas Coupled Training's joint objective adaptively attenuates the influence of the rich-view model. Against Vapnik SVM+ (Vapnik & Vashist, 2009), a labeled-only privileged-information method that does not exploit unlabeled data, Coupled Training achieves a mean Brier score of 0.0881 versus 0.1470 (a 0.0589 absolute gain), winning in 26/26 seeds; SVM+ is particularly weak here because it cannot leverage the 10,000 unlabeled paired (X,W) examples. The squared-loss generalized distillation baseline (0.0907) sits between Two-Stage and Coupled Training, confirming that soft-target distillation partially mitigates but does not eliminate negative transfer. On the X-only Baseline, Coupled Training provides a modest but consistent gain (0.0011 Brier on Bank Marketing, 4.74 MSE on Parkinson's), demonstrating that the method extracts useful signal from privileged features without the degradation seen in naive pseudo-labeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Two-Stage pseudo-labeling framework for learning with privileged information (Xia & Wainwright, 2024; Hou et al., 2023) by replacing the fixed-teacher paradigm with a jointly optimized coupled objective that adaptively controls the influence of privileged features. Conceptually, it draws on LUPI (Vapnik & Vashist, 2009) for the deployment constraint, on co-regularized multi-view learning (Sindhwani et al., 2005) for the agreement term between models, and on greedy approximation theory (Barron et al., 2008; DeVore & Temlyakov, 1996) for the high-dimensional dictionary-based algorithm. The key departure from prior work is the asymmetric coupling: the rich-view model g is not a fixed teacher but is recalibrated against the current deployment model f, subject to a constraint that limits deviation from labeled responses, thereby interpolating between supervised learning and Two-Stage pseudo-labeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled mechanism to avoid negative transfer in privileged-information settings, a failure mode already documented but not previously addressed with a unified training procedure. The correlation-controlled risk bound (Corollary 2.3) gives a multiplicative rather than additive degradation guarantee, which is a meaningful theoretical improvement over prior Two-Stage analyses. However, the theory is restricted to square-loss regression with convex function classes, and the classification and random-forest experiments remain empirical evidence beyond the proof regime. Open questions include extensions to misspecification, distribution shift, semi-paired data, and broader classification guarantees, as well as practical proxies for the unobserved correlation score ρ* that could guide tuning.
