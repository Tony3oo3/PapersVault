## Problem

Existing generative time series forecasting methods rely on either LLMs for semantic context modeling or diffusion/flow models for continuous probabilistic generation, but neither alone can simultaneously capture deep semantic understanding of contextual conditions and stochastic modeling of continuous temporal dynamics. This paper addresses the gap by coupling both capabilities into a single unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260687/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a look-back window of length L=96 (with D variables) partitioned into non-overlapping patches, concatenated with text context features (domain knowledge, task instructions, statistics) tokenized by the LLM's native tokenizer. Output is a prediction window of length H ∈ {12,24,36,48} generated autoregressively patch-by-patch, where each patch is produced via a single one-step flow-matching evaluation from Gaussian noise, yielding both point forecasts and probabilistic distributions with uncertainty intervals.

## Method

CoGenCast reconfigures a pre-trained decoder-only LLM (Qwen3-0.6B) into a forecasting encoder-decoder by modifying only the attention topology: the encoder uses bidirectional self-attention to fuse text and patch embeddings, while the decoder uses causal self-attention plus cross-attention over encoder states for autoregressive representation generation. A flow-matching denoising decoder is then conditioned on the decoder's hidden states to model temporal evolution, but unlike standard flow-matching it targets the average velocity over a finite interval [t,r] rather than instantaneous velocity, using a JVP-corrected MSE objective that explicitly penalizes velocity variation and enforces straight trajectories. At inference, each future patch is generated in a single Euler step (t=0 to t=1) from pure Gaussian noise, conditioned on previously generated patches, achieving low-latency one-step generation without iterative denoising.

## Results

On 10 benchmark datasets (L=96, H∈{12,24,36,48}), CoGenCast achieves the best or near-best MSE across the majority of scenarios, with representative values of 0.293 (Energy), 0.328 (ETTh1), 0.109 (ETTm2), and 0.031 (Exchange). The paper reports an approximate 10% average MSE reduction over LLM-based baselines, ~19% over generative-based baselines, and >4% over strong deep-based methods like ConvTimeNet. One-step (1-NFE) flow-matching generation achieves performance nearly identical to 2-NFE, and inference latency is 1.78 min on ETTh1 versus 9.88 min for TokenCast and 7.42 min for LLM4TS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoGenCast outperforms all three LLM-based baselines (TokenCast, Time-LLM, LLM4TS) on 9 out of 10 datasets, with the largest gaps on ETTm1 (0.224 vs. 0.248–0.304 MSE) and ETTm2 (0.109 vs. 0.141–0.145). Against generative-based methods, CoGenCast dominates: FlowTS (0.332–0.651 MSE) and TimeGrad (0.571–2.486 MSE) are consistently worse, while CDPM is competitive on ETTh2 (0.193 vs. 0.159) but still trails CoGenCast. Among deep-based baselines, CoGenCast wins on 9/10 datasets; the sole exception is ETTh2, where DLinear (0.142) and TimeDART (0.145) beat CoGenCast (0.159), though the margin is small. On uncertainty metrics (CRPS/QICE), CoGenCast also outperforms NsDiff and TMDM across all four evaluated datasets (e.g., CRPS 0.122 vs. 0.145 on ETTh2). In the efficiency analysis, CoGenCast's one-step inference (1.78 min) is 5.6× faster than TokenCast (9.88 min) and 4.2× faster than LLM4TS (7.42 min) on ETTh1, while training time (17.9 min) is roughly 3–4× shorter than the LLM-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads in time series forecasting: LLM-based methods (TokenCast, Time-LLM, LLM4TS) that leverage semantic understanding, and flow-matching/diffusion-based methods (FlowTS, TimeGrad, CDPM) that model continuous stochastic dynamics. CoGenCast departs from both by coupling them into a single architecture: it reconfigures a pre-trained decoder-only LLM into an encoder-decoder backbone via attention-topology modification alone, then conditions a flow-matching denoising decoder on the autoregressively generated representation. The flow-matching component specifically builds on the mean flow framework (Geng et al., 2025/2026), adopting interval-conditioned average velocity with JVP correction to enable one-step generation rather than iterative denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive forecasting accuracy with a single function evaluation per patch, which substantially reduces inference latency compared to iterative diffusion or multi-step flow-matching approaches—making probabilistic forecasting more deployable in real-time settings. Cross-domain unified training and few-shot adaptation results suggest the framework can transfer temporal priors across domains, reducing data requirements for new forecasting tasks. However, the multimodal setting is limited to numerical-textual context (not truly multimodal), the autoregressive appending may accumulate errors over very long horizons, and the computational overhead of a 0.6B LLM backbone remains a barrier for resource-constrained deployment. Open questions include whether the one-step approximation degrades under distribution shift and whether the attention-topology reconfiguration generalizes to other LLM architectures beyond Qwen and Llama.
