## Problem

Existing randomized smoothing theory certifies robustness under homogeneous perturbations, treating continuous (image) and discrete (text) inputs in isolation with no principled joint likelihood ordering for heterogeneous noise. This gap is critical for multimodal safety filtering, where safety violations are interaction-level phenomena—neither modality is unsafe alone, yet their composition is—making unimodal certificates intrinsically insufficient for certifying the joint decision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260856/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt (discrete token sequence, padded to a fixed maximum length L), an RGB image (continuous pixel tensor), and a fixed natural-language safety policy, producing a binary Safe/Unsafe decision along with a joint certified robustness pair: an ℓ2 image radius ε and a discrete text budget d (number of tokens an adversary may append or modify). Certification is evaluated in an interaction-only regime where f(text, ∅)=Safe, f(∅, image)=Safe, but f(text, image)=Unsafe, so the certified object is the stability of the joint multimodal decision rather than two independent unimodal guarantees.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form, one-dimensional Neyman-Pearson certificate for joint discrete-continuous randomized smoothing. The key insight is that the joint likelihood ratio γ(z₁,z₂)=γ₁(z₁)·γ₂(z₂) factorizes into a discrete and a Gaussian component, and the continuous Gaussian factor regularizes the otherwise non-invertible discrete knapsack into a strictly monotone, continuous likelihood-ratio CDF F(t;r), enabling exact one-dimensional root-finding for the optimal NP threshold t*(r). The worst-case adversarial value V(x₁,adv; r) is then evaluated in closed form and minimized over all admissible discrete perturbations within budget d. A non-composability proposition proves that the NP-optimal acceptance region on the product space is intrinsically coupled and cannot be recovered by combining separate unimodal thresholds. The resulting certificate strictly generalizes both classical Gaussian ℓ2 smoothing (when the discrete channel is trivial) and discrete fractional-knapsack smoothing (as σ→∞), and is instantiated with a uniform token-replacement kernel for text and Gaussian/denoising-based smoothing for images, validated on LLaVA-Guard safety filtering and a tabular classification task.

## Results

On the interaction-only Hateful Memes safety filtering task (100 examples), Hybrid RS certifies a joint image radius of 3.76 (ℓ₂) and text budget of 3.07 tokens simultaneously, whereas image-only RS certifies radius 3.99 with zero text budget, and text-only RS certifies budget 3.26 with zero image radius. On the ADULT tabular dataset, certified accuracy degrades monotonically with discrete budget d, dropping from >30% at ε=1.9 (d=0) to zero beyond ε=0.75 (d=2). On MM-SafetyBench (7.5% interaction-only subset, 126 samples), the pipeline transfers without retuning, yielding mean certified text budget 3.62 and mean certified image radius 3.37. Adaptive empirical attacks over 20 iterations fail to violate the certificate at any tested budget scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two unimodal randomized smoothing baselines: image-only Gaussian RS (Cohen et al., 2019) and text-only discrete RS (Chen et al., 2025a). Neither baseline provides a joint guarantee—image-only RS certifies an image radius of 3.99 but zero text robustness, while text-only RS certifies a text budget of 3.26 but zero image robustness. Hybrid RS achieves a joint (r, d) certificate of (3.76, 3.07), sacrificing only ~6% on each individual axis relative to the corresponding unimodal method while simultaneously certifying both modalities. This is the first model-agnostic certificate that covers joint discrete-continuous perturbations in a single NP analysis. Additionally, an MMCert-style independent subsampling baseline (Wang et al., 2024) yields zero certifiable examples on the interaction-only subset at any keep ratio below 1.0, because independent subsampling destroys the sparse cross-modal signal that drives the unsafe prediction, whereas additive hybrid noise preserves it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of randomized smoothing theory: Gaussian smoothing for continuous inputs (Cohen et al., 2019) and discrete knapsack-based certificates for text (Chen et al., 2025a), which had been treated in isolation. The key conceptual departure is showing that the continuous Gaussian component does not merely add a second constraint but structurally regularizes the discrete Neyman-Pearson problem, converting a non-invertible fractional-knapsack into a continuous, strictly monotone one-dimensional capacity equation. The image smoothing pipeline follows the denoising-based RS paradigm of Carlini et al. (2023b), and the discrete text kernels (uniform, absorbing) are standard from prior text-RS work. The paper also formally proves that unimodal certificates do not compose (Proposition 4.1), closing a gap that prior multimodal certification efforts (MMCert, COMMIT) sidestepped by using different threat models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first model-agnostic Neyman-Pearson certificate for joint discrete-token and continuous-image perturbations, directly addressing the threat model most relevant to deployed multimodal systems (prompt injection + adversarial image noise). The framework is modality-agnostic and applies beyond safety filtering to any mixed discrete-continuous input space, though the empirical validation is limited to a curated 400-example interaction-only split and a small tabular sanity check. Key open questions include whether tighter certificates can be obtained by exploiting base-model structure (e.g., Lipschitz bounds), how the framework extends to more than two modalities or to non-Gaussian continuous noise, and whether the certified-empirical gap can be narrowed in practice. The interaction-only evaluation setting, while well-motivated, is rare in natural benchmarks (only 7.5% of MM-SafetyBench qualifies), limiting the practical coverage of the certificate.
