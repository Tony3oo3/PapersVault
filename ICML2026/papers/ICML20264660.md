## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) suffer from inefficient reasoning manifested as overthinking (redundant self-validation after reaching a correct answer) and overshoot (further revision that degrades already-correct answers). Existing efficient-reasoning methods rely on internal numerical signals or fixed schedules to control reasoning depth, but these are not aligned with the structural markers of 'doubt' in LRM reasoning traces, leading to mistimed or misdirected interventions. The paper asks: can external feedback be injected at the precise moments when a model signals doubt, using state-aware content that adaptively extends or terminates reasoning?

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question and, during generation, pauses the reasoning model at predefined transitional conjunction tokens (e.g., 'wait', 'but', 'alternatively'). At each pause, an external evaluator (LLM proxy or human) assesses the partial reasoning trace against two task-agnostic criteria—rationality and completeness—and injects a tagged feedback string (<reasoning_feedback>...</reasoning_feedback>) into the model's context. The model then resumes generation conditioned on the updated context, repeating until it emits  or hits a maximum interaction budget of 10. The final output is a reasoning trace (including inserted feedback tokens) and a final answer.

## Method

CGI identifies transitional conjunctions as process-aligned intervention points where LRMs signal 'doubt' and are about to enter redundant self-validation or exploratory branching. At each such point, an external evaluator maps the current reasoning state (cross-product of rationality × completeness) to one of four action-specific feedback types, which are injected as tagged text into the reasoning context. The backbone model (DeepSeek-R1-Distilled-Qwen2.5-7B) is then adapted via GRPO with a LoRA adapter (rank 8) using a composite reward combining correctness, format compliance (ensuring the model actually follows feedback), and a length penalty that normalizes across a group of rollouts. The information-theoretic justification shows that target-relevant feedback F strictly reduces conditional entropy H(T|C,θ) by a non-negative mutual information term I(T;F|C;θ), providing a principled basis for why state-aware intervention at doubt markers suppresses redundant extensions while preserving accuracy.

## Results

CGI achieves the best accuracy-length trade-off across all six benchmarks. On AIME24, CGI reaches 73.85% accuracy with only 1571.70 tokens of reasoning length, improving accuracy by 33.85% and reducing length by 1802.80 tokens over the same backbone (DeepSeek-R1-7B: 40.00%, 3374.50). On GPQA Diamond, CGI attains 65.22% accuracy at 1378.65 tokens, nearly tripling the base model's 22.25% accuracy while cutting reasoning length by over 70%. CGI also adapts reasoning length to task difficulty, using only 394.89 tokens on GSM8K but scaling to 2289.98 tokens on AIME25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGI (using DeepSeek-R1-7B as backbone) outperforms all nine baselines on the accuracy-length trade-off across all six benchmarks. Against the same-size backbone DeepSeek-R1-7B, CGI improves AIME24 accuracy from 40.00% to 73.85% while halving reasoning length (3374.50 → 1571.70 tokens). Among efficient-reasoning methods, CGI dominates DEER on AIME24 (73.85% vs 54.06%) and AIME25 (71.43% vs 49.79%), and beats SEAL on AIME24 (73.85% vs 51.35%) and AIME25 (71.43% vs 33.33%), while also achieving substantially shorter reasoning traces. Against multi-model SpecReason, CGI wins on AIME24 (73.85% vs 50.00%), AIME25 (71.43% vs 36.00%), and GPQA Diamond (65.22% vs 50.51%) with roughly half the reasoning length. CGI also surpasses the larger DeepSeek-R1-32B (71.15% on AIME24) and Qwen3-32B (70.10%) using only a 7B backbone, demonstrating that the intervention framework compensates for model scale. The only metric where a baseline is competitive is GSM8K length, where SEAL (297.49) and DEER (392.25) are slightly shorter than CGI (394.89), though CGI achieves higher accuracy (96.21% vs 89.30% and 91.00% respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

CGI builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for policy optimization and positions itself against two families of efficient-reasoning methods: single-model optimization (SEAL, DEER, L1-Max, ReasonFlux) that steer decoding via internal numerical signals or fixed schedules, and multi-model collaboration (SpecReason) that delegates sub-processes to smaller models. The key departure is that CGI introduces external, state-aware textual feedback at semantically meaningful intervention points (transitional conjunctions) rather than relying on self-referential internal signals, and it aligns the intervention timing with the LRM's own reasoning structure. The information-theoretic framing (entropy reduction via conditional mutual information) provides a principled justification that prior intervention methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical bottleneck in LRM deployment: overthinking and overshoot waste significant compute and can degrade accuracy. The conjunction-based intervention mechanism is interpretable and model-agnostic in principle, and the support for both LLM-proxy and human feedback makes it adaptable to different deployment constraints. However, the best results depend on a 72B proxy model, the GRPO adaptation step adds training cost, and the approach is validated primarily on a single 7B backbone (DeepSeek-R1-Distilled-Qwen2.5-7B), leaving open whether the gains transfer to larger or architecturally different LRMs. A key open question is whether lighter or peer-sized proxies can close the gap, and whether the conjunction-based trigger generalizes to non-English or non-standard reasoning formats.
