## Problem

Existing LLM training methods face a fundamental tension: accuracy-driven approaches (SFT, vanilla RL) implicitly reward guessing over abstention, amplifying hallucinations, while abstention-focused methods (e.g., R-Tuning) become overly conservative and sacrifice correct answers. No prior framework directly optimizes for truthfulness as a multi-dimensional objective that simultaneously maximizes correct responses, enables calibrated abstention, and minimizes hallucinations.

## Input / Output

The model receives a question (optionally accompanied by up to 50 retrieved web pages or Wikipedia passages in RAG settings) and produces a step-by-step reasoning trace enclosed in <think> tags followed by a final answer in \boxed{} format. Critically, the output space includes a verbalized abstention (\boxed{I don't know}) as a first-class response option, meaning the model can decline to answer entirely rather than being forced to commit to a factual claim. An LLM-based judge (Llama3.3-70B-Instruct) evaluates each output as correct, incorrect, or uncertain to compute the training reward.

## Method

TruthRL implements GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) with a ternary reward scheme: +1 for correct answers, 0 for abstentions, and −1 for hallucinations, replacing the standard binary reward that conflates abstention with error. The key mechanism is that under GRPO's group-relative advantage estimation, the ternary reward assigns a strictly higher relative advantage to abstention than to hallucination within any sampled group (e.g., in a group with one abstention and one hallucination, the abstention gets advantage >0 while the hallucination gets advantage <0), whereas the binary reward gives both the same negative reward and zero relative advantage, making them indistinguishable to the policy update. This causes the model to learn to abstain specifically when it lacks knowledge (pro-abstention groups increase abstention ratio over training) while still being penalized for abstaining when it actually knows the answer (anti-abstention groups decrease abstention ratio), achieving adaptive knowledge-boundary recognition without static OOK labels. The method is trained on CRAG with an LLM-based verifier and generalizes across model scales (3B–32B) and to unseen benchmarks including multi-hop QA and mathematical reasoning.

## Results

On the CRAG benchmark with retrieval using Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, TruthRL reduces hallucination from 43.5% (prompting) to 19.4% and improves truthfulness from 5.3% to 37.2%. Averaged across all four knowledge-intensive benchmarks (CRAG, NQ, HotpotQA, MuSiQue) under the retrieval setting, TruthRL achieves 25.6% truthfulness and 18.8% hallucination rate, compared to 4.5% truthfulness and 47.7% hallucination for the binary-reward variant (TruthRL_Binary) and −16.4% truthfulness / 54.1% hallucination for the prompting baseline. The same pattern holds without retrieval, where TruthRL reaches 10.5% average truthfulness versus −20.9% for prompting. Gains are consistent across both Llama3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TruthRL outperforms all five baselines on both truthfulness and hallucination rate across all four benchmarks in both retrieval and non-retrieval settings. Compared to RFT (Yuan et al., 2023), TruthRL improves average truthfulness from −15.8% to 25.6% and reduces hallucination from 53.5% to 18.8% under retrieval. Against R-Tuning (Zhang et al., 2024a), which reduces hallucination to 48.8% but sacrifices accuracy (40.1%), TruthRL achieves both lower hallucination (18.8%) and higher accuracy (44.4%). Versus RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022), TruthRL more than doubles the truthfulness score (25.6% vs. −6.6%) and halves the hallucination rate. Against RLKF (Xu et al., 2024a), the closest RL baseline, TruthRL gains 24 points in truthfulness (25.6% vs. 1.6%) and reduces hallucination by 16.6 points. In the offline RL comparison (Table 11), TruthRL with online GRPO also surpasses DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and iterative DPO, which plateau or regress after multiple iterations, confirming that online reward-driven training is critical for sustained truthfulness gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

TruthRL builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its policy-optimization backbone, but departs from the standard binary reward formulation used in RLVR and RLHF by introducing a ternary reward that explicitly distinguishes correct answers (+1), abstentions (0), and hallucinations (−1). This positions it against two prior lines: (1) SFT-based abstention methods like R-Tuning, which require model-specific annotation and tend toward over-conservatism, and (2) confidence-calibration RL methods (RLCR, SaySelf, RewardingDoubt) that always produce an answer but adjust a numerical confidence score. The key conceptual departure is that GRPO's group-relative advantage mechanism, combined with the neutral abstention reward, dynamically credits or debits abstention depending on whether the sampled group contains correct responses, something binary rewards cannot do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a deceptively simple reward design (three discrete values) outperforms more elaborate variants (knowledge-enhanced, reasoning-enhanced), suggesting that the core insight—separating abstention from error in the reward signal—is more important than additional complexity. A notable limitation is that the knowledge-enhanced variant actually degrades performance, attributed to the static nature of knowledge-boundary probing failing to track the model's evolving boundary during training. Open questions include whether the approach extends to open-ended generation beyond factoid QA, how it interacts with longer reasoning chains, and whether the neutral abstention reward is universally optimal or task-dependent. The method's generalization to GSM8K and across model scales (3B–32B) is encouraging but the MuSiQue results remain weak, suggesting multi-step compositional reasoning is still a challenge.
