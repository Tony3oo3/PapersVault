## Problem

Diffusion-based visual navigation policies can produce unreliable or unsafe trajectories when test-time guidance or standard sampling pushes the reverse-diffusion state off the training data manifold. Existing systems either select actions randomly from the multi-modal distribution or apply hand-tuned heuristics, without explicitly constraining the sampling trajectory to remain in well-supported regions of action space while still optimizing task objectives like path efficiency or collision avoidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263844/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3-frame RGB observation history (96×96 pixels) and a goal image, encoded via an EfficientNet-B0 backbone with sparse attention and temporal shift into a context representation. It outputs a waypoint trajectory of horizon 8 (a sequence of 2D positions) plus a scalar temporal distance estimate to the goal. Crucially, the method is a training-free inference-time modification: it operates on a pre-trained diffusion policy (e.g., NoMaD or Diffusion Policy) and alters only the reverse-diffusion sampling rule, requiring a single backward pass per denoising step for the Fisher-preserving projection.

## Method

FPG-OPS constrains each reverse-diffusion step to lie on a Fisher isosurface by projecting the task-guidance gradient onto the tangent space of the constant-Fisher-Denoising-Sensitivity surface, removing the component that would change local sensitivity to first order. To make this tractable, the method exploits the low-rank structure of the denoising network's final linear head (W ∈ R^{D_a × C_h H}) and performs the projection in the Outer Product Span latent subspace using a pullback metric M = W^T W, reducing per-step cost to O(C_h H) with a single backward pass. A Truncated Fisher Denoising Sensitivity (TFDS) score, accumulated over only the final M ≈ 4–6 denoising steps, provides an efficient uncertainty signal with a provable relative error bound. Finally, an uncertainty-guided action blending strategy combines TFDS-based stability with DBSCAN cluster typicality across K parallel samples to produce a weighted-average action that is both locally stable and distributionally representative.

## Results

On the CARLA outdoor navigation benchmark (9 routes, 3 scenes), the full FPG-OPS method achieves 75.55% success rate, 0.445 average collisions, and 0.653 SPL, substantially outperforming ViNT (53.33% SR, 0.611 Colli., 0.504 SPL) and NoMaD (51.11% SR, 0.778 Colli., 0.478 SPL). On the indoor GRScenes benchmark, the method reaches 83.0% SR with 0.48 collisions and 0.83 SPL, compared to ViNT (68.0%, 0.71, 0.77) and NoMaD (51.0%, 1.95, 0.33). On the real-robot evaluation across three indoor scenes, FPG achieves 28/30 success with 0.10 average collisions, versus 22/30 (0.37) for ViNT and 19/30 (0.57) for NoMaD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ViNT (a regression-based topological memory network), FPG-OPS wins decisively on all three metrics in both CARLA and GRScenes: +22.2 pp SR and +0.149 SPL on CARLA, and +15 pp SR with fewer collisions on GRScenes. Against NoMaD (the primary diffusion-policy baseline), the full method improves SR by +24.4 pp on CARLA and +32 pp on GRScenes, while reducing average collisions from 0.778 to 0.445 (CARLA) and from 1.95 to 0.48 (GRScenes). On the PushT manipulation task using official Diffusion Policy weights, FPG improves the task score from 0.91 to 0.94 without any retraining. On Maze2D with TSDF safety guidance, FPG reduces collisions from 0.071 to 0.016 (a 77% reduction) while slightly improving path quality. On the real-robot platform, FPG achieves a perfect 10/10 in Scene 1 and 9/10 in Scenes 2–3, with the lowest collision count (0.10) across all scenes, confirming that the training-free Fisher-preserving constraint translates to tangible safety gains in physical deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion-policy framework for visual navigation established by NoMaD and Diffusion Policy, and adopts the Outer Product Span (OPS) low-rank factorization from Wang et al. (ICML 2025) to make Fisher-information-based constraints computationally tractable. Conceptually, it extends the manifold-preserving guided diffusion idea of He et al. (MPGD, ICLR 2024) from conditional image generation to diffusion-based control, reframing the constraint as a Fisher-isosurface condition on the action trajectory rather than a data-manifold projection. The key departure is the introduction of Fisher Denoising Sensitivity (FDS) as an input-conditioned uncertainty signal and its truncated variant (TFDS) for efficient multi-sample action blending, which has no direct analogue in prior guided-diffusion or navigation-policy literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of FPG-OPS is practically significant: it can be layered onto any existing diffusion navigation policy (NoMaD, Diffusion Policy) without retraining, and the OPS projection reduces per-step overhead to O(C_h·H), enabling near-real-time inference on consumer GPUs. The real-robot validation (28/30 success vs. 19/30 for NoMaD) strengthens the claim that Fisher-preserving constraints genuinely suppress off-manifold drift in deployment, not just in simulation. Open questions remain: FDS is a sensitivity proxy rather than a certified safety guarantee, the method is evaluated primarily on RGB navigation with limited dynamic-obstacle or manipulation settings, and the interaction between the guidance scale γ and the Fisher isosurface curvature in highly non-convex action spaces is not fully characterized.
