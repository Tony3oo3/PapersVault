## Problem

Rare events in LLMs—outputs that are unlikely but highly significant—are by definition difficult to observe, yet their practical impact grows with deployment scale. While alignment pushes undesirable behaviors toward distribution tails, no systematic framework existed for estimating their probabilities or exploring their structure in a statistically rigorous way. This paper provides the first complete end-to-end Rare Event Analysis (REA) framework for LLMs, spanning problem setup, probability estimation, and exploration of rare completions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260955/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a fixed 16-token text prompt, a language model (TinyStories-8M), and a scalar observable function (Automated Readability Index or log-probability) computed over the full prompt-completion pair. It produces marginal probability density histograms with 96% bootstrap confidence intervals that extend orders of magnitude beyond what direct sampling can resolve, along with concrete rare completion text samples for exploratory data analysis. Completions are 100 tokens; the ARI is capped at 15 to improve MCMC mixing, and the log-probability is the natural joint probability under temperature-1 decoding.

## Method

The core technique adapts Transition Path Sampling (TPS), a Metropolis-Hastings variant for sequences, to LLM generation by targeting exponentially reweighted distributions p_λ(x) ∝ exp(−λφ(x))·p_M(x) at multiple bias values λ. For autoregressive models with the base model as the regeneration proposal, the MH acceptance ratio simplifies to exp(−λ(φ(x′)−φ(x)))·(L_j/L_i), eliminating all base-distribution terms and making each step cheap to evaluate. An annealing schedule gradually increases |λ| across 10 bias values (4×10⁴ TPS steps each) to improve convergence, and the Multistate Bennett Acceptance Ratio (MBAR) then combines samples from all biased chains plus direct samples to self-consistently reconstruct the original distribution's marginal density. Error analysis uses percentile bootstrap over independent chains for confidence intervals and the Gelman-Rubin statistic to discard non-converged segments.

## Results

Using the TinyStories-8M model with a fixed 16-token prompt and 100-token completions, the paper generates approximately 4×10⁸ tokens per observable (ARI and Log-Prob) via both direct sampling (4.2M completions) and annealed TPS + MBAR. The MBAR histograms extend well beyond the range accessible by direct sampling: for ARI, direct sampling is confined to the training data range, while MBAR resolves densities orders of magnitude smaller, probing out-of-distribution regions. In the tails, the relative CI half-widths for MBAR are dramatically smaller than for direct sampling (Fig. 4a), and bias analysis (Fig. 4b) indicates tail estimates are of the correct order of magnitude even with the current computational budget. The inferred distributions are strongly non-Gaussian, reflecting correlations from sequential token generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the proposed MBAR/TPS framework and direct (ancestral) sampling, which is identified as the current state-of-the-art for rare completion probability estimation. Direct sampling with 4.2M completions (~420M tokens) produces many empty bins in the tails of both the ARI and Log-Prob distributions, with the ARI histogram limited to the range observed in training data. In contrast, MBAR combining ~4×10⁸ tokens from annealed TPS (10 bias values, 4×10⁴ steps each) plus 2×10⁵ direct samples resolves counts across the full observable range, including regions far beyond the training data. The relative CI half-widths in the tails are substantially smaller for MBAR than for direct sampling, where Wilson intervals on zero-count bins yield uninformative upper bounds. The bias analysis (doubling MCMC steps) shows that while some tail bin heights shift by amounts comparable to their CI widths, the estimates remain of the correct order of magnitude, suggesting the method captures the right scale even under resource constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants a well-developed toolkit from statistical physics and computational chemistry—umbrella sampling, the MBAR estimator, and Transition Path Sampling (a Metropolis-Hastings variant for sequences)—into the setting of LLM text generation. The closest LLM-specific prior work (Wu & Hilton, 2025; Jones et al., 2025) addresses narrower sub-problems (single rare tokens, or extrapolation from few prompts), while the only directly comparable baseline for full-completion rare-event probability estimation remains direct sampling. The paper positions itself as the first complete end-to-end framework (setup → estimation → exploration) for rare event analysis of LLM completions, explicitly bridging the gap between the rich physics literature and the nascent LLM safety/analysis community.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, API-accessible (no white-box weights required) methodology for quantifying tail risks in LLM outputs, which is critical as deployment scale makes even 10^{-9} events operationally significant. Key open questions include scaling to production models with diverse prompts (the paper relies on a single fixed prompt and a small 8M-parameter model), the choice of biasing observables for complex safety properties, and whether algorithmic improvements (parallel tempering, infilling proposals, RL-finetuned proposal models) can reduce the token budgets needed for longer generations. The EDA finding that the model assigns high probability to highly repetitive out-of-distribution completions (e.g., 'Trurururu…') offers a concrete, actionable proxy for runtime filtering.
