## Problem

Quantum speedups for nested expectation estimation were previously established only for a single level of nesting (Blanchet et al., 2026), leaving the broader and more practically relevant case of repeatedly nested expectations (RNEs)—which includes optimal stopping, credit valuation adjustment, and probabilistic program inference—without a quantum algorithm. The central technical obstacle is that the classical randomized MLMC (rMLMC) algorithm is inherently variable-time: its random truncation level induces a random runtime that, when directly quantized, forces the quantum algorithm to pay the worst-case cost across all branches, destroying the quadratic speedup.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a discrete-time process trajectory y = (y_0, …, y_D) with constant horizon D, a sequence of Lipschitz functions g_d (with known Lipschitz constants L_d), a history y_{<d}, and a target error ε. It operates under a code-access oracle model: rather than a black-box sampler, the algorithm requires source-code access to the sampling procedure so it can be implemented as a reversible quantum circuit (state-preparation unitary U). The output is a quantum MLMC estimator R_d(y_{<d}, ε) of the repeatedly nested expectation γ_d(y_{<d}) with RMSE at most ε and sample complexity O(ε^{-1} log^{3(D-d)+1}(ε^{-1})), where D is treated as a constant.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a two-stage pipeline: first, it derandomizes the classical rMLMC by replacing the random geometric level N with a deterministic schedule that allocates roughly M_d·P_d(n) samples at each level n = 0, …, B_d where B_d = Θ(log(1/ε)), preserving the same bias, cost, and moment guarantees as the randomized version. Second, it quantizes this derandomized procedure using Quantum-Accelerated Monte Carlo (QAMC, built on Grover's amplitude estimation) at each level to estimate the MLMC telescoping differences. The key insight is that the deterministic schedule eliminates the variable-time pathology: each level has a fixed, known cost, so QAMC's quadratic speedup applies cleanly per level, yielding Õ(ε^{-1}) total complexity versus the classical O(ε^{-2}). Additionally, the quantum setting allows controlling L² error (RMSE) rather than the classical L^{p_d} error for p_d ∈ (1,2), which bypasses the arbitrarily small δ-deficiency in parameter tuning and replaces the ε^{-O(δ)} factor with an explicit poly-logarithmic term.

## Results

The main quantum algorithm (Theorem 1.6) estimates a repeatedly nested expectation with RMSE at most ε and sample complexity O(ε^{-1} log^{3(D-d)+1}(ε^{-1})), where D is the constant number of nestings and d is the current time step. This achieves an almost quadratic speedup over the optimal classical rMLMC bound of O(ε^{-2(1+δ/2^{d-1})}) from Syed & Wang (2023). A direct quantization of the classical algorithm (Proposition 1.5) fails, yielding only Ω(ε^{-D+d-1} log^{D-d+1}(ε^{-1})) complexity due to the variable-time issue. The quantum lower bound of Õ(Ω(ε^{-1})) from mean estimation confirms the result is optimal up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the classical randomized Multilevel Monte Carlo (rMLMC) algorithm of Syed & Wang (2023), which achieves O(ε^{-2(1+δ/2^{d-1})}) sample complexity with L^{p_d}-error for p_d ∈ (1,2). The proposed quantum MLMC algorithm (Theorem 1.6) achieves an almost quadratic speedup, reducing the ε-dependence from ε^{-2} to ε^{-1} while simultaneously upgrading the error guarantee from L^{p_d} to L² (RMSE), eliminating the need for the arbitrarily small δ parameter. The paper also demonstrates that a naive direct quantization of the classical rMLMC (Algorithm 4) fails catastrophically, producing complexity Ω(ε^{-D+d-1} log^{D-d+1}(ε^{-1})) due to the variable-time issue where quantum amplitude amplification cannot handle random runtimes across branches. The derandomized classical MLMC (Theorem 1.4) matches the classical rMLMC complexity but uses deterministic level scheduling, serving as the essential bridge that makes quantization possible. The quantum result is shown to be optimal up to logarithmic factors via the standard quantum lower bound for mean estimation, which is a special case of the RNE problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantum speedup for nested expectation estimation from the single-nesting case (D=2, Blanchet et al. 2026) to arbitrary constant horizon D, building on the classical rMLMC framework of Syed & Wang (2023) and the QAMC subroutine of Kothari & O'Donnell (2023). The key technical departure is that direct quantization of the classical rMLMC algorithm fails due to variable-time issues (per Ambainis 2012), necessitating a new derandomized MLMC variant with deterministic level scheduling as an intermediate classical step before quantization. This derandomization, which answers an open question from Syed & Wang (2023), is the paper's main classical contribution and the essential bridge enabling the quantum speedup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes near-optimal quantum complexity (Õ(ε^{-1})) for RNE estimation, covering optimal stopping and other sequential decision problems, with a clean quadratic speedup over the best classical algorithm. A notable conceptual finding is that the QAMC subroutine's quadratic improvement in per-level cost provides enough slack to bypass the arbitrarily small δ-deficiency that plagues all classical MLMC analyses, simplifying parameter choices substantially. Limitations include the code-access oracle assumption (requiring source-level access to sampling procedures), the restriction to constant D, and the purely theoretical nature with only toy-scale numerical illustrations. Open questions include whether the derandomization technique generalizes to non-constant horizons or to settings where the Lipschitz constants grow with D.
