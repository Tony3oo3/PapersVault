## Problem

Existing high-probability generalization bounds via algorithmic stability require uniform boundedness of the one-sample perturbation or sub-Gaussian/sub-Weibull tail conditions, which are overly restrictive for modern settings with heavy-tailed or unbounded losses (e.g., deep learning with heavy-tailed gradients, regression over unbounded domains). The paper asks whether a single finite L_p moment on the replace-one perturbation suffices to yield sharp, non-asymptotic high-probability generalization guarantees without any exponential-tail assumption.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input a learning algorithm whose one-sample perturbation is dominated by a random envelope H(z_i, z_i') with a finite p-th moment (p >= 2 or 1 < p < 2), along with a Lipschitz-type stability constant beta and a test-sample sensitivity function G. It produces a two-regime deviation probability bound: a sub-Gaussian term exp(-c*z^2 / sum of ||H_i||_2^2) governing moderate deviations and a polynomial term (sum of ||H_i||_p^p) / z^p capturing rare large fluctuations. The resulting generalization bounds are expressed as high-probability inequalities (with explicit delta-dependence) for the gap between population and empirical risk, transductive risk, or meta-risk.

## Method

The core contribution is a Nagaev-type concentration inequality (Theorem 2.2) for general functions of independent random variables under a replace-one L_p envelope condition, proved via a martingale-difference decomposition combined with a truncation trick: the deviation is split into a truncated sum (controlled by a sub-Gaussian Chernoff bound using a custom moment-generating function lemma) and an untruncated tail (controlled by Markov's inequality on the p-th moment). This yields the sharp two-regime form, sub-Gaussian for moderate z and polynomial z^{-p} for large z, that is provably tight (matching the t_2 distribution tail in the p < 2 regime). For transductive regression, a new concentration inequality for sampling without replacement (Theorem 3.9) is developed to handle the induced dependencies among training points. The framework is then instantiated for ERM, transductive regression, and meta-learning, each requiring a tailored (L_p, beta)-Lipschitz stability assumption that replaces the classical bounded-uniform-stability condition.

## Results

The paper establishes a Nagaev-type concentration inequality (Theorem 2.2) showing that under a finite L_p moment (p ≥ 2) on the replace-one perturbation H_i, the deviation probability decomposes into a polynomial term c₁·ΣE|H_i|^p / z^p and a sub-Gaussian term 2·exp(−c₂z²/ΣE|H_i|²). For the complementary regime 1 < p < 2 (Theorem 2.6), the bound involves a sum of individual tail probabilities plus a polynomial correction of order z^{−pQ}. Applied to ERM (Theorem 3.4), the high-probability generalization gap scales as (β‖H‖_p·m^{1/p} + β'‖G‖_p·m^{−(1−1/p)})·δ^{−1/p} + (β‖H‖_2·√m + β'‖G‖_2/√m)·√log(1/δ) + β‖H‖_1. Numerical experiments on ridge regression with symmetric stable noise (ν = 2.2, 4.4) confirm the predicted two-regime transition: the ratio p(y) = P(|R−R_emp|>y)/P(|R−R_emp|>1.5y) stabilizes near 1.5^{ν/2} for large y, validating the polynomial tail term.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Theorem 2.2 strictly weakens the tail assumptions of all three compared baselines: Kontorovich (2014) requires a finite ψ₂ (sub-Gaussian) Orlicz norm, Maurer & Pontil (2021) requires a finite ψ₁ (sub-exponential) norm, and Li et al. (2024) requires a finite ψ_α (sub-Weibull) norm — all of which imply the existence of exponential moments and hence all L_p moments. In contrast, the proposed framework requires only a single finite p-th moment, which is strictly weaker (e.g., a Pareto distribution with shape parameter p has finite L_p moments but no moment-generating function). The trade-off is in the tail rate: for moderate deviations (small z), the sub-Gaussian component matches the baselines up to constants, but for large deviations the polynomial term z^{−p} dominates instead of the sub-Weibull z^{−α} decay. The paper also identifies a fatal gap in Corollary 29 of Li & Liu (2024), which claims the same Gaussian-plus-polynomial form but whose proof only justifies the bound at a single threshold rather than uniformly over all z > 0. In the ERM application, the classical bounded-uniform-stability bound of Bousquet et al. (2020) requires β = o(1) and bounded loss, while Theorem 3.4 allows random, data-dependent stability increments with only finite moments, at the cost of requiring faster decay β ≪ m^{−1/2} for vanishing bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the algorithmic stability framework initiated by Bousquet & Elisseeff (2002) and the bounded-differences concentration of McDiarmid (1989) by replacing uniform boundedness or sub-Gaussian/sub-Weibull tail assumptions on one-sample perturbations with a single finite L_p moment condition. The proof technique draws on Nagaev-type truncation arguments for heavy-tailed sums, applied through a martingale-difference decomposition, and is positioned as a direct generalization of Kontorovich (2014) and Li et al. (2024), who require Orlicz (ψ_α) norm bounds that imply all moments exist. The authors explicitly identify and resolve a gap in Corollary 29 of Li & Liu (2024), where a Markov-inequality argument fails to yield the full Gaussian-plus-polynomial tail uniformly over all deviation levels under a fixed p-th moment assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides high-probability generalization guarantees in settings where exponential moments are unavailable—such as regression with heavy-tailed noise, unbounded responses, or deep learning with heavy-tailed gradients—without modifying the learning rule (unlike Catoni-type or median-of-means robust estimators). The two-regime bound (sub-Gaussian for moderate deviations, polynomial for large deviations) is shown to be sharp in tail-order, with empirical validation on ridge regression and a two-layer neural network confirming the polynomial term is genuinely necessary. Open questions include extending to dependent data (Markovian sampling), unsupervised learning, and establishing matching lower bounds that formally quantify when the polynomial correction is unavoidable. The trade-off is that weaker tail assumptions impose stronger decay requirements on the stability constant (e.g., β ≪ m^{-1/2} with ‖H‖_p = O(1) rather than just β ≪ m^{-1/2} with bounded H).
