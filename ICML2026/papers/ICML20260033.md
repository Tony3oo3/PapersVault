## Problem

Joint estimation of user abilities and item difficulties under the Rasch model remains challenging: classical methods (JMLE, MMLE, CMLE) either lack consistency guarantees, are computationally prohibitive, or degrade severely in sparse regimes, while the recent spectral approach of Nguyen & Zhang (2022) provides theoretical bounds but demands dense data and is computationally expensive. No existing method simultaneously offers theoretical convergence guarantees, computational efficiency, and strong performance in the sparse, large-scale settings typical of real-world IRT applications.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a partially observed binary response matrix X ∈ {0,1,*}^{m×n} together with its observation indicator M, where each entry is revealed independently with probability p (densities in experiments range from 0.16% to 100%). It outputs user ability vectors θ ∈ R^n and item difficulty vectors β ∈ R^m, subject to the centering constraint Σβ_i = 0 that resolves the model's additive-shift invariance. The MAP formulation adds an isotropic Gaussian prior (equivalently L2 regularization with parameter ν) to ensure well-posedness when individual users or items have degenerate response patterns.

## Method

The key insight is Pólya-Gamma data augmentation: introducing a latent PG(1,0) variable ω_li per observed user-item pair transforms the intractable logistic log-likelihood into a tractable exponential-quadratic form in the parameters. This yields an EM algorithm whose E-step computes closed-form PG moments κ_li = tanh(η_li/2)/(2η_li) (with a 1/4 limit at η=0), and whose M-step maximizes a regularized quadratic objective that reduces to solving a single positive-definite linear system Hφ = r via Gauss-Seidel iteration. The paper proves linear convergence of the population EM operator to the MAP solution with contraction coefficient ρ ≤ 0.8, and extends the guarantee to the finite-sample setting with explicit sample complexity bounds. A first-order gradient-EM variant is also analyzed, and the full pipeline requires only O(|O|) work per EM iteration, making it scalable to datasets with millions of users and items.

## Results

PGEM achieves the best predictive performance across all 10 real-world datasets on every metric (AUC, LogLik, Accuracy, Brier) while also being the fastest feasible method. On ICAR-SAPA (20.99% density), PGEM reaches AUC 0.814, Brier 0.176, and completes in 52.71 s, outperforming SPECTRAL (AUC 0.804, 2128.68 s) and JMLE (AUC 0.785, 210.79 s). On the highly sparse ML-20M dataset (0.54% density), PGEM achieves AUC 0.747 and Brier 0.202 in 4223 s, versus SPECTRAL's AUC 0.745 and Brier 0.203 in 53175 s. CMLE fails to complete on datasets larger than ML-100K (exceeding 15,000 s on HETREC), while PGEM finishes in under 25 s on HETREC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGEM outperforms all four baselines on every predictive metric (AUC, LogLik, Accuracy, Brier) across all datasets where comparisons are available. Against JMLE, PGEM gains roughly 2–3 AUC points (e.g., 0.814 vs. 0.785 on ICAR-SAPA, 0.739 vs. 0.726 on ML-100K) while running 4–28× faster. Against MMLE, PGEM is consistently better on all metrics and substantially faster (e.g., 1.18 s vs. 22.96 s on ML-100K); the paper notes MMLE's effective runtime is further inflated by the need to tune 15 priors. Against SPECTRAL (Nguyen & Zhang, 2022), PGEM achieves marginally higher AUC and lower Brier on every dataset (e.g., 0.747 vs. 0.745 AUC on ML-20M) while being 12.6× faster on that dataset (4223 s vs. 53175 s). CMLE is the weakest baseline: it produces the lowest AUC and highest Brier where it completes (e.g., AUC 0.475, Brier 0.377 on ML-100K) and fails entirely on HETREC, BOOK-GENOME, and ML-20M due to prohibitive runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Pólya-Gamma data augmentation technique from Bayesian logistic regression (Polson et al., 2013) to the Rasch model, transforming the intractable logistic log-likelihood into a tractable quadratic form amenable to EM. The convergence analysis directly leverages the EM statistical guarantees framework of Balakrishnan et al. (2017), establishing the first local linear convergence proof for EM-based joint estimation under the Rasch model. The paper departs from prior IRT estimation methods (JMLE, MMLE, CMLE, spectral) by providing a single unified joint estimation procedure that avoids the two-stage pipelines and prior sensitivity issues of existing approaches, while remaining computationally efficient through Gauss-Seidel solution of a linear system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling reliable joint parameter estimation in the sparse, large-scale regimes characteristic of modern IRT applications (recommender systems, LLM benchmarking, crowdsourcing), where classical methods either fail or become prohibitively slow. The first theoretical convergence guarantee for EM in this setting provides a foundation for future work. Open questions include extending the framework to polytomous Rasch models, three-parameter logistic models, and broader classes of logistic latent variable models, as well as understanding whether the Pólya-Gamma augmentation strategy generalizes to other IRT formulations with additional item parameters.
