## Problem

Existing competitive coding benchmarks for LLMs suffer from high false-positive rates due to incomplete test suites, lack of exceptionally hard problems, dependence on online platform APIs that limit reproducibility, and reliance on coarse pass-rate metrics that obscure nuanced model capabilities. No prior benchmark combines expert-curated Olympiad problems with official test cases, fine-grained subtask rubrics, direct human contestant comparisons, and a fully offline evaluation system.

## Input / Output

Each problem is presented as a text statement with subtask constraints, time/memory limits, and an average of 60 official test cases; models generate 8 C++ candidate solutions per problem and the highest-scoring one is retained. Evaluation produces a multi-dimensional score vector—pass rate, relative score percentage, human percentile rank, medal tier (gold/silver/bronze), and Codeforces Elo—rather than a single binary pass/fail, enabling direct comparison against the actual score distributions of human contestants from 72 contests.

## Method

LiveOIBench is constructed by crawling 14 Informatics Olympiad websites (IOI, BOI, CEOI, USACO, COCI, etc.) to collect 403 problems from 72 contests held between 2023 and 2025, along with their official test cases, subtask rubrics, and contestant result sheets. Human contestants are algorithmically matched to Codeforces profiles to compute Elo ratings via the CodeElo formula, and an offline judge executes solutions against the full official test suite with subtask-level partial scoring. The benchmark evaluates 34 LLMs (proprietary, open-weight thinking, and non-thinking) using pass@8 sampling in C++, and includes a multi-pronged contamination analysis combining temporal performance trends, token-level familiarity metrics, semantic rephrasing tests, and Dolos-based code similarity to official solutions.

## Results

On LiveOIBench (403 problems, 72 contests, 14 Informatics Olympiads), GPT-5 achieves the highest human percentile of 81.76%, a 63.03% pass rate, and a Codeforces Elo of 2414, yet still falls short of the top-10% Gold medal threshold. Among open-weight models, GPT-OSS-120B-High reaches 72.88% human percentile (Elo 2205), narrowing the gap to GPT-5 to ~9 percentile points, while standard GPT-OSS-120B achieves 59.90% and Seed-OSS reaches 53.81%. Non-thinking models perform substantially worse: GPT-4.1 (35.99%) and DeepSeek-V3 (31.76%) trail all thinking models by 15+ percentile points, and Qwen3-32B-Non-Thinking drops to just 24.64%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5 leads all 34 evaluated models with an 81.76% human percentile and Elo 2414, outperforming Grok-4-Fast (74.23%, Elo 2221) by ~7.5 percentile points and Gemini-2.5-Pro (71.80%, Elo 2192) by ~10 points. Among open-weight models, GPT-OSS-120B-High (72.88%, Elo 2205) surpasses Gemini-2.5-Pro and trails GPT-5 by only 9 percentile points, while standard GPT-OSS-120B (59.90%) and Seed-OSS (53.81%) cluster in the mid-50s. Qwen3-32B (42.00%) and DeepSeek-R1 (42.29%) are nearly tied, both far below the top proprietary models. The thinking-vs.-non-thinking gap is stark: GPT-4.1 (35.99%) and DeepSeek-V3 (31.76%) perform at levels comparable to mid-tier thinking models like Qwen3-14B (34.59%), while Qwen3-32B-Non-Thinking (24.64%) drops below even the weakest thinking model. Notably, GPT-OSS-120B achieves a higher pass rate (47.78%) than Gemini-2.5-Pro (44.46%), yet Gemini-2.5-Pro ranks higher in human percentile (71.80% vs. 59.90%) and Elo (2192 vs. 2032), illustrating that pass rate alone underestimates overall competitive proficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the competitive programming benchmark lineage (APPS, CodeContests, LiveCodeBench, CodeELO) by shifting the problem source from online platforms like Codeforces and AtCoder to official Informatics Olympiad contests (IOI, BOI, CEOI, USACO, etc.), which release complete expert-curated test suites and subtask rubrics. It departs from prior work in three key ways: (1) using official test cases rather than LLM-generated or rule-based augmentations to eliminate false positives, (2) enabling direct human-model comparison via percentile rankings and Codeforces Elo rather than relying solely on pass rates, and (3) providing a fully offline, reproducible evaluation system that removes API dependencies. The reasoning trace analysis builds on Gandhi et al. (2025) and Ahmad et al. (2025), while the contamination study follows the methodology of Riddell et al. (2024) and Dekoninck et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiveOIBench provides a rigorous, continuously updated benchmark that will likely become a standard reference for evaluating frontier LLMs on algorithmic reasoning, given its 403 expert-curated problems, fine-grained subtask scoring, and direct human percentile comparisons. The finding that even GPT-5 (81.76th percentile, 63% pass rate) falls well short of elite human contestants (gold medal = top 10%) highlights a substantial remaining gap, particularly on dynamic programming, segment trees, and tree-based problems requiring creative pattern recognition. The reasoning trace analysis revealing that stronger models strategically allocate tokens toward focused analysis rather than excessive exploration offers actionable insights for future training and inference strategies. Open questions include whether curriculum-driven fine-tuning on complex graph/tree/DP problems can close the algorithmic gap, and whether the benchmark's continuous update mechanism can sustain its contamination-free properties as models improve.
