## Problem

While repeated risk minimization (RRM) is known to converge linearly to a performatively stable point when performative effects are weak (ρ < 1), the computational complexity in the regime ρ ≥ 1 was entirely open. The central question is whether the failure of RRM and other natural retraining dynamics at ρ ≥ 1 reflects an intrinsic intractability barrier or merely a limitation of specific algorithms. This paper resolves the question by establishing a sharp phase transition between tractability and PPAD-hardness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a loss function ℓ(x; z), a distribution map D(x) over data points z, and a convex compact constraint set X ⊂ ℝ^d, and produces an ε-performatively stable point satisfying the first-order condition ⟨x − x*, E_{z~D(x*)}[∇_x ℓ(x*; z)]⟩ ≥ −ε for all x ∈ X. In the hardness constructions, the loss is restricted to the quadratic form ½‖x‖² − xᵀz and the distribution shift is affine (z = g(x) with g L-Lipschitz), so ρ = L. The approximation parameter ε and the expansion parameter ρ are tightly coupled: PPAD-hardness holds even when ρ = 1 + O(ε), while a poly(d, log(1/ε)) algorithm exists when ρ ≤ 1 + O_ε(ε⁴).

## Method

The core technique is a family of polynomial-time reductions that encode variational inequalities (VIs) and fixed-point problems as performative prediction instances: choosing g(x) = x − F(x) maps an ε-approximate VI solution to an ε-performatively stable point, and choosing g(x) = (1−λ)x + λT(x) maps fixed points to RRM fixed points. For PPAD-hardness, the paper reduces from affine VIs on the hypercube (via Bernasconi et al.) and from 2D-SPERNER for general well-bounded convex domains, using a bit-extraction and sampling technique to convert discontinuous colorings into continuous operators. On the positive side, the paper applies the ellipsoid algorithm to hypomonotone VIs (via expected variational inequalities) to obtain a poly(d, log(1/ε)) algorithm in the slightly-expansive regime ρ ≤ 1 + O_ε(ε⁴). For strategic classification, PLS-hardness of local optimality is proven by reducing from LOCAL-MAXCUT through a graph-to-population gadget that encodes cut weights in the Jury's utility.

## Results

The paper establishes a sharp computational phase transition for performative stability: computing an ε-performatively stable point is PPAD-hard (polynomial-time equivalent to Nash equilibrium) even when the expansion parameter ρ = Lβ/α ≤ 1 + O(ε) (Theorem 3.4), with hardness persisting for quadratic loss and affine distribution shifts. On the tractable side, a poly(d, log(1/ε))-time algorithm computes an O_ε(ε^{1/4})-performatively stable point when ρ ≤ 1 + O_ε(ε⁴) (Theorem 3.5), improving over RRM whose iteration count blows up as ρ → 1. An unconditional exponential query lower bound of 2^Ω(d) ERM evaluations is shown (Corollary 3.7), and strategic local optimality is proven PLS-hard (Theorem 4.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are made against prior complexity and convergence results. The PPAD-hardness of Theorem 3.4 sharpens the prior observation by Perdomo et al. (2020) that RRM can fail at ρ = 1, by showing intractability persists even for ρ = 1 + O(ε) with only quadratic loss and affine shifts. The tractability result (Theorem 3.5) improves upon RRM, whose iteration count scales as log(1/ε)·(1 − ρ)⁻¹ and diverges as ρ → 1, by achieving poly(d, log(1/ε)) scaling at the cost of requiring ρ ≤ 1 + O_ε(ε⁴). Compared to Diakonikolas (2025), who gives a poly(1/ε) algorithm for ρ ≤ 1 + O_ε(ε), Theorem 3.5 requires a tighter expansiveness bound (ε⁴ vs. ε) but scales logarithmically rather than polynomially in 1/ε. The general convex domain hardness (Theorem 3.12) extends prior PPAD-hardness results that were restricted to the hypercube [0,1]^d, and the PLS-hardness for strategic classification (Theorem 4.4) complements the NP-hardness of global strategic optimality established by Hardt et al. (2016).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the performative prediction framework of Perdomo et al. (2020), who established that repeated risk minimization (RRM) converges linearly when the performative effect parameter ρ < 1, but left the complexity of the ρ ≥ 1 regime open. The hardness results draw on a rich toolkit from computational complexity: PPAD-completeness of Nash equilibria (Daskalakis et al., 2009), the 2D-SPERNER problem (Chen & Deng, 2009), and exponential fixed-point lower bounds (Hirsch et al., 1989). A key technical departure is extending VI/fixed-point hardness from the standard hypercube domain to arbitrary well-bounded convex sets, leveraging the sampling technique of Deligkas et al. (2020) and tools from Mehta (2014). On the positive side, the paper adapts the ellipsoid algorithm for nonexpansive mappings (Sikorski et al., 1993) to slightly expansive settings via the expected variational inequality framework of Zhang et al. (2025), and connects to the strategic classification literature of Hardt et al. (2016) for the PLS-hardness result.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a sharp computational phase transition: computing performatively stable points is PPAD-complete (equivalent to Nash equilibrium) even when ρ = 1 + O(ε), yet a poly(d, log(1/ε)) algorithm exists when ρ ≤ 1 + O_ε(ε⁴). This means the boundary between tractability and intractability is extraordinarily narrow, and the failure of RRM at ρ ≥ 1 reflects a fundamental computational barrier rather than an algorithmic limitation. The extension to general convex domains (e.g., ℓ₂ balls common in deep learning) and the PLS-hardness of strategic local optimality have direct implications for practitioners: local search heuristics cannot be expected to efficiently find stable points in strategic classification. Open questions include closing the gap between the tractable regime (ρ ≤ 1 + O(ε⁴)) and the PPAD-hard regime (ρ ≥ 1 + O(ε)), and characterizing the complexity of performative stability under the standard strategic classification definition (without endogenous costs).
