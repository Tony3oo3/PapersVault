## Problem

Domain adaptation methods for clinical event prediction on EHRs can mitigate distribution shifts across hospitals or time periods, but their black-box nature prevents clinical adoption where transparency is essential. Existing interpretable approaches (post-hoc attribution, attention) explain final outputs but cannot reveal what the adaptation process retains (invariant) versus removes (domain-specific), leaving clinicians unable to audit whether meaningful subgroup patterns are erased during transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261655/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes longitudinal EHR visit sequences encoded as binary ICD code vectors of shape T×|C| (up to 51 visits, 54 codes/visit in eICU; up to 31 codes/visit in OCHIN), with source-domain samples carrying labels and target-domain samples unlabeled. Beyond the standard prediction output (multi-label diagnosis codes or binary heart-failure flag), ExtraCare produces a 256-dimensional sparse concept vector whose non-zero dimensions are mapped to specific ICD-10-CM codes via targeted ablation, yielding a four-quadrant attribution per patient that separates transferable predictive evidence from domain-sensitive variation.

## Method

ExtraCare builds a three-stage training pipeline on a Transformer encoder: (1) label prediction with MMD-based feature alignment, (2) adding a tied-weight sparse autoencoder whose decoder weights W_θ induce a dictionary metric M = W_θ^T W_θ for non-Euclidean geometry, and (3) adding a domain classifier trained on the M-orthogonal residual z = v − α·v̂, where α is the closed-form ε-regularized projection coefficient. The key architectural insight is that the SAE dictionary defines a latent geometry under which the invariant reconstruction v̂ and the covariant residual z are provably orthogonal, so distinct supervisions (label CE on v, domain CE on z) enforce functional separation without additional neural modules. For interpretation, top-k activated sparse dimensions are ablated and the induced Δprob across all ICD codes is thresholded to assign each mapped code to one of four quadrants (high/low label impact × high/low domain sensitivity), giving clinicians a prevalence-aware audit of what drives prediction versus what reflects facility-specific shift.

## Results

On eICU (spatial shift), ExtraCare achieves 68.61% w-F1 for diagnosis prediction and 91.88% AUROC for heart failure prediction, approaching the Oracle upper bound (69.72% w-F1, 92.54% AUROC) while substantially outperforming the Base lower bound (61.09% w-F1, 84.52% AUROC). On OCHIN (temporal shift), ExtraCare reaches 74.05% w-F1 for diagnosis and 95.48% AUROC / 85.38% F1 for heart failure, yielding 16% and 14% relative gains over Base respectively. Across all four task-dataset combinations, ExtraCare outperforms every DA baseline, with the largest margins over feature-alignment methods (DANN, RMMD, RSDA) and the closest competition from BUA and SSRT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExtraCare outperforms all 10 DA baselines on nearly every metric. Against the strongest feature-alignment baselines, ExtraCare gains +4.27 w-F1 over RMMD (68.61 vs 64.34) and +2.28 w-F1 over BUA (74.05 vs 71.77) on OCHIN diagnosis, and +2.11 AUROC over BUA (91.88 vs 89.77) on eICU heart failure. The closest competitor is BUA on eICU HF F1 (79.83 vs 79.64, a 0.19-point gap), but ExtraCare dominates on the corresponding AUROC by 2.11 points. Self-training methods SSRT and CST show moderate improvements over Base but remain 4–7 points below ExtraCare on w-F1 and 1–3 points on AUROC. In the interpretation grounding evaluation (RQ3), ExtraCare also surpasses SHAP, Integrated Gradients, and a regular SAE baseline: it improves Label-Axis Difference from 36% (SAE) to 47%, reduces Quadrant Calibration Error from 37% to 32%, and is preferred by 68% of LLM judges and 64% of human evaluators over the SAE baseline for explanation coherence and partition clarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines: sparse autoencoder-based interpretability (Cunningham et al., 2023; Huben et al., 2024) and orthogonal representation decomposition for domain adaptation (Bousmalis et al., 2016; Sun et al., 2022b; Liu et al., 2025). The key architectural departure is using the SAE's tied dictionary weights to define a non-Euclidean latent metric (M = W_θ^T W_θ) that governs the orthogonal projection separating invariant from covariant features, rather than relying on standard Euclidean geometry. Unlike prior disentanglement approaches that adversarially suppress domain information, ExtraCare explicitly supervises the residual component z with a domain classifier, making both the retained and removed information visible and auditable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in clinical AI deployment: most DA methods are opaque, and clinicians need to understand not just what a model predicts but what it transfers versus what it discards during adaptation. The four-quadrant framework (label impact × domain sensitivity) offers a practical audit tool, and the external grounding validation against patient-relevance and facility-specificity anchors provides evidence beyond internal model scores. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets and two tasks, reliance on LLM judges for interpretability validation, and the fact that SAE dictionary features are approximate semantic factors rather than guaranteed clinical concepts. Open questions include whether the dictionary-induced metric generalizes to deeper or multi-modal backbones, and whether the interpretability pipeline scales to the full ICD-10 vocabulary without manual curation.
