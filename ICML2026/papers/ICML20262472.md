## Problem

Selecting appropriate baselines and datasets for AI experiments is a high-stakes, context-dependent decision that existing systems handle poorly: they draw candidates from portal-curated inventories with limited coverage, model suitability as topical similarity rather than experimental appropriateness, and treat baseline and dataset selection as independent tasks. As the ecosystem of baselines and datasets proliferates across tasks and evaluation conventions, reliable automation of this joint recommendation remains underexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262472/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a research idea as a natural language query, instantiated as a GPT-4o-generated synopsis distilled from a paper's abstract (preserving problem setting, assumptions, and evaluation intent), and produces two ranked lists of recommended baselines and datasets accompanied by interpretable reasoning justifications. The candidate pool spans 116,970 baselines and 68,316 datasets extracted from 108,825 papers across ten flagship AI venues (2015–2025), with the retrieval stage surfacing top-20 candidates that the reranking stage then reorders.

## Method

AgentExpt is a two-stage pipeline. The first stage, Collective Perception-Augmented Retrieval, represents each baseline or dataset by concatenating its first-person self-description with a GPT-4o-synthesized 'Collective Perception' summary distilled from pooled citation contexts in downstream papers, then fine-tunes Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B as a bi-encoder using a diagonal binary cross-entropy loss with an in-batch contrastive regularizer. The second stage, Reasoning-Augmented Reranking, constructs P-B-P-D and P-D-P-B interaction chains from a heterogeneous paper-baseline-dataset graph, scores each chain by co-usage frequency, and uses GPT-4o to generate step-by-step reasoning trajectories over the top-3 chains per candidate; these trajectories serve as supervision to fine-tune DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B as a listwise reranker via token-level negative log-likelihood, yielding refined rankings with interpretable, chain-grounded justifications.

## Results

On the AgentExpt knowledge base (108,825 papers, 116,970 baselines, 68,316 datasets), AgentExpt achieves Recall@20 of 0.4523 and HitRate@10 of 0.6938 for paper-baseline recommendation, and Recall@20 of 0.3001 and HitRate@10 of 0.5549 for paper-dataset recommendation. Compared to the strongest prior method (SymTax), AgentExpt yields average gains of +5.85% in Recall@20 and +7.90% in HitRate@10 across the two tasks. Ablations confirm that collective perception contributes the largest retrieval gain (−17.8% R@20 when removed for baselines), while interaction-chain reasoning drives the reranking improvements (−27.8% HR@5 without reasoning supervision for baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgentExpt outperforms all seven baselines across every metric on both tasks. Against the strongest competitor SymTax, AgentExpt improves Recall@20 by 7.23% (0.4523 vs. 0.4218) and HitRate@10 by 7.52% (0.6938 vs. 0.6453) for baselines, and by 4.46% (0.3001 vs. 0.2873) and 8.27% (0.5549 vs. 0.5125) for datasets. The dense-retrieval baselines DataFinder and SciBERT trail AgentExpt by 3.2–5.0% in Recall@20 on baselines, while the graph-based Textual-GCL and HAtten are further behind (R@20 ≈ 0.36–0.42). The lexical BM25 and classification-based DataHunter are the weakest, with DataHunter actually underperforming BM25 on Recall@10 for baselines (0.1809 vs. 0.2080), reflecting its closed-set limitation. Notably, the precision-oriented HitRate metrics show larger relative gains than Recall, confirming that the reasoning-augmented reranker adds value beyond the retrieval stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior baseline/dataset recommendation work (DataHunter, DataFinder, RecBaselines, SymTax) in two key ways: it introduces 'collective perception'—aggregating third-person citation contexts as community usage signals beyond first-person metadata—and it unifies baseline and dataset recommendation through paper-mediated interaction chains (P-B-P-D) rather than treating them as decoupled tasks. Methodologically, it combines contrastive fine-tuning of a dense retriever (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) with supervised fine-tuning of an LLM reranker (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) on chain-derived reasoning traces, drawing on dense retrieval, graph representation learning, and LLM reasoning as complementary components. The 108,825-paper knowledge base extracted from ten flagship AI venues substantially exceeds prior resources (DataFinder: 17,495 papers; RecBaselines: 903 papers), shifting the grounding from portal-curated inventories to actual literature usage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AgentExpt provides both a large-scale, literature-grounded knowledge base and a practical two-stage retrieval-reranking pipeline that could serve as infrastructure for LLM-based automated research agents needing concrete, evidence-backed experimental choices. The ablation showing collective perception (third-person citation contexts) contributes more to retrieval quality than self-descriptions validates the hypothesis that experimental suitability is a community-level property, not an intrinsic one. Key limitations include coverage restricted to major AI venues, a time-bounded snapshot requiring incremental updates, and a potential popularity bias from community signals that could reinforce mainstream choices. Open questions include extending to other scientific domains, handling newly released artifacts, and balancing exploitation of well-established pairings against exploration of novel combinations.
