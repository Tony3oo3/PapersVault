## Problem

Retrieval-augmented generation systems still produce plausible-but-unsupported statements, and post-hoc citations are not calibrated indicators of evidence. Existing RAG faithfulness methods (Self-RAG, CoVe, heuristic filters) optimize citation quality or aggregate factuality metrics but provide no user-controllable, per-answer statistical guarantee on the proportion of unsupported claims among accepted outputs. CITEGUARD fills this gap by framing citation faithfulness as a multiple-testing problem with finite-sample FDR control.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a decoding-time layer on top of any RAG pipeline: it receives a user query, a draft answer decomposed into atomic claims (via deterministic sentence splitting), retrieved evidence passages (top-K=5 from DPR), a pre-computed calibration set of n=2000 unsupported claim-evidence scores drawn from the same generation distribution, and a user-specified risk level α. It outputs a filtered set of accepted claims with citations to their supporting evidence, per-claim conformal p-values, and an accept/reject decision for each claim. A critical constraint is that the calibration set must be exchangeable with test-time claims (same retriever, generator, prompt, and annotation protocol); using out-of-distribution calibration data degrades FDR by 3–5 pp.

## Method

CITEGUARD converts each claim-evidence pair into a conformal p-value for the null hypothesis 'unsupported' by ranking the claim's cross-encoder entailment score against calibration scores from known-unsupported examples, yielding a distribution-free super-uniform p-value under exchangeability. These p-values are then fed into the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) or Benjamini-Yekutieli (BY) multiple-testing procedure, which selects the largest set of claims whose sorted p-values satisfy p_(k) ≤ (k/m)·α/c(m), where c(m)=1 for BH (valid under positive regression dependence) or c(m)=H_m for BY (valid under arbitrary dependence). An optional adaptive switching rule monitors the lead-claim score and escalates from BH to BY when the first claim's score falls below a calibrated threshold, preempting the 'avalanche effect' where an early entity error cascades into highly correlated downstream claims. An optional coherence post-processing step flags dangling references, orphaned conclusions, and missing qualifiers among accepted claims. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 5.3) ensures FDR ≤ α for the BH/BY selection step under exchangeability, with BY as the recommended default for robustness to the claim dependence inherent in multi-sentence answers.

## Results

On FEVER (dev, α=0.10), CITEGUARD (BY) achieves 92.7% citation precision and FDR 0.073, while CITEGUARD (BH) reaches 90.3% precision and FDR 0.097—both meeting or exceeding the α=0.10 target. On Natural Questions with 500 human-verified claims, CITEGUARD (BY) attains FDR 0.094 [0.082, 0.106] and EM@Acc 50.8%, versus FDR 0.204 and EM@Acc 44.6% for the strongest baseline (Self-RAG), a 54% relative FDR reduction. Across all four tested risk levels (α ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20}), CITEGUARD is the only method with zero FDR violations (0/4), while every baseline overshoots α at every level (4/4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CITEGUARD outperforms all five baselines on FDR at α=0.10 on both datasets. Against Self-RAG (the strongest RAG-specific baseline), CITEGUARD (BH) reduces FDR from 0.148 to 0.097 on FEVER (−34% relative) and from 0.204 to 0.102 on NQ (−50% relative), while also improving EM@Acc from 44.6% to 49.2%; at iso-abstention (~27%), the FDR gap narrows to 24% (0.097 vs 0.128, p<0.001). Against CoVe, CITEGUARD (BH) achieves FDR 0.097 vs 0.159 on FEVER and 0.102 vs 0.218 on NQ, with CoVe abstaining less (9.4% vs 26.8%) but failing to meet the FDR target. Against SelectiveNet (Selective Pred.), CITEGUARD reduces FDR from 0.182 to 0.097 on FEVER and 0.248 to 0.102 on NQ; the gap reflects that SelectiveNet's single-prediction FDR guarantee does not transfer to pooled multi-claim FDR. Against Calibrated Threshold (Guo et al.), CITEGUARD lowers FDR from 0.165 to 0.097 (FEVER) and 0.232 to 0.102 (NQ). Against Self-Consistency + CoT (5×), CITEGUARD achieves FDR 0.097 vs 0.172 (FEVER) and 0.102 vs 0.228 (NQ). The consistent trade-off is higher abstention (26.8–37.2% for CITEGUARD vs 9.4–17.3% for baselines), but the Pareto analysis (Table 13) confirms CITEGUARD dominates at every matched abstention level.

## Relation to Prior Work

CITEGUARD sits at the intersection of conformal prediction (Vovk et al., 2005), multiple-testing theory (Benjamini & Hochberg, 1995; Benjamini & Yekutieli, 2001), and selective prediction (Geifman & El-Yaniv, 2019), repurposing each for a RAG-specific problem. The key conceptual move is treating each claim in a multi-sentence RAG answer as a separate hypothesis test, converting claim-evidence scores into conformal p-values, and then applying BH/BY to control the false discovery rate across claims. This departs from prior RAG faithfulness work (Self-RAG, CoVe, post-hoc citation verification) which provides point estimates or learned reflection tokens without finite-sample statistical guarantees, and from risk-controlling prediction sets (Bates et al., 2021) which target a single expected loss rather than the false-discovery proportion over many correlated hypotheses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides RAG practitioners with a user-tunable risk budget (α) and finite-sample, distribution-free FDR guarantees, which is a meaningful step toward deployable faithfulness in high-stakes settings. The avalanche-effect analysis—showing that standard BH can overshoot target FDR by 12 points under realistic entity-error cascades while BY stays within 1.2 points—is a practically important warning that the default multiple-testing procedure may be unsafe for LLM outputs. Open questions include cross-turn error propagation in multi-turn conversations, the causal-inversion failure mode that defeats adaptive switching, and whether the protocol-relative guarantee (tied to verifier quality) can be tightened toward absolute truth. The method's cost shrinks substantially with stronger generators (abstention drops from 37% to 16% going from FiD to GPT-4o), suggesting it will remain relevant as a lightweight safety layer even as base models improve.
