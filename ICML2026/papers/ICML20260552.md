## Problem

Standard LLM architectures are computationally inefficient at pretraining because self-attention models short-range local dependencies through redundant full pairwise interactions without an explicit locality inductive bias, and mixture-of-experts implicitly couples knowledge storage with computational pathways, making knowledge retrieval indirect and inflexible. No existing architecture explicitly separates the handling of sequence-internal local patterns from sequence-external global knowledge during pretraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260552/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a causal text sequence of up to 2048 tokens (smaller models) or 4096 tokens (33B/60B) and produces next-token predictions in a standard autoregressive decoder. During inference, the KMM's 64 parameterized key-value fields remain fixed while different input tokens query different fields via dot-product matching, so the output is query-dependent without modifying the memory. The architecture is a decoder-only MoE model with 3B activated parameters out of 7B total, trained on 4.5T bilingual tokens followed by SFT on ~10B instruction tokens.

## Method

LoKiFormer augments an MLA-based decoder with two modules. Local Fusion Attention (LFA) applies a causal 1D group convolution (kernel size k=4, groups aligned to attention heads) over the hidden states before Q/K/V projection, explicitly fusing k neighboring tokens per head so that attention operates on locally enriched representations and can focus on broader contextual relationships. The Knowledge Memory Module (KMM) introduces F=64 learnable key-value slots; it takes the compact latent KV representation from MLA, projects it to a query space, performs multi-group scaled dot-product attention against the fixed keys, and aggregates the corresponding values to produce a knowledge-enhanced output that is added to the MLA output before the MoE layer. This decouples global knowledge storage from the MoE's implicit parameterization, enabling direct, interpretable, and editable knowledge retrieval. The full architecture uses 1 shared + 8 routed experts per MoE layer, and the two new modules add only ~2.5% extra parameters, yet accelerate pretraining convergence by 1.33×.

## Results

LoKiFormer-7B (3B activated, 7B total parameters) achieves 91.5 on MMLU, 93.4 on CMMLU, 92.8 on C-Eval, 89.5 on HellaSwag, 96.2 on ARC-Challenge, 87.0 on HumanEval pass@1, and 73.0 on GSM8K. In pretraining ablations, the full architecture converges 1.33× faster than the baseline (reaching the same validation PPL at 7.5K vs. 10K steps), with LFA alone contributing 1.11× speedup. The model scales smoothly from 1B to 60B parameters, with validation PPL on WikiText-103 dropping from 10.05 to 5.82, and the 60B variant reaches 84.3 on MMLU and 54.6 on MMLU-Pro.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against size-comparable dense models, LoKiFormer-7B (3B activated) leads on every reported metric: it surpasses Llama-3.1-8B by 18.5 points on MMLU (91.5 vs. 73.0), Qwen2.5-7B by 14.9 on MMLU (91.5 vs. 76.6), and Phi-3-medium by 13.5 on MMLU (91.5 vs. 78.0), while also winning on HellaSwag (89.5 vs. 82.4) and ARC-C (96.2 vs. 91.6). The sole exception is GSM8K, where Qwen2.5-7B (91.6) and Phi-3-medium (91.0) substantially outperform LoKiFormer (73.0), suggesting the architecture's gains are concentrated in knowledge retrieval and reasoning rather than multi-step arithmetic. Against much larger frontier open-source models, LoKiFormer-7B still leads on MMLU over Llama-3.1-70B (91.5 vs. 83.6), Qwen2.5-72B (91.5 vs. 84.4), and Hunyuan-Large (91.5 vs. 89.9), and on ARC-C over all listed baselines, though it trails Llama-3.1-405B on HumanEval (87.0 vs. 89.0) and Hunyuan-Large on HumanEval (87.0 vs. 90.0). Versus closed-source systems, LoKiFormer-7B beats Claude-3.5-Sonnet on MMLU (91.5 vs. 88.3) and Gemini-1.5-Pro on MMLU (91.5 vs. 85.9), but falls behind both on HellaSwag (89.5 vs. 94.6/93.3), HumanEval (87.0 vs. 92.0/84.1), and GSM8K (73.0 vs. 96.4/90.8). In the controlled same-SFT-dataset comparison against MAP-Neo-7B, LoKiFormer gains 27.1 points on MMLU (91.5 vs. 64.4) and 24.8 on CMMLU (93.4 vs. 68.6), isolating the architectural contribution from data effects.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoKiFormer builds directly on DeepSeek's Multi-Head Latent Attention (MLA) as its attention backbone, extending it with a causal group convolution (LFA) that injects an explicit local inductive bias before Q/K/V projection. It departs from the MoE paradigm by introducing a parametric key-value memory (KMM) that stores global knowledge in addressable, learnable slots, decoupling knowledge storage from the computational pathway that MoE implicitly couples. The paper positions LFA as complementary to (not a replacement for) sparse or sliding-window attention, and KMM as distinct from both explicit retrieval-based memory (knowledge graphs, RAG) and implicit latent-vector memory (Memorizing Transformers, MemoryLLM), since KMM is trained end-to-end and remains static at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.33× pretraining convergence speedup with only ~2.6% per-step overhead is practically significant for large-scale training budgets, and the domain-specialized knowledge fields offer a degree of interpretability and editability that implicit MoE parameterization lacks. However, the very large benchmark gaps (e.g., 91.5 MMLU for a 7B/3B-active model vs. 83.6–86.0 for 70B+ dense models) are difficult to verify independently, and the notably weaker GSM8K score (73.0 vs. 96.4 for Claude-3.5-Sonnet) suggests the gains are concentrated in knowledge retrieval rather than multi-step reasoning. Open questions include whether KMM's explicit memory generalizes to continual learning or knowledge editing at scale, and whether the LFA+KMM combination remains effective when combined with longer context lengths or multimodal inputs, as the authors themselves note as future work.
