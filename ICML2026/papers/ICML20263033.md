## Problem

Prompt-based adaptation methods (Prompt Tuning, Prefix Tuning) initialize their trainable vectors with random or irrelevant tokens, discarding the task-specific information already present in few-shot demonstration examples. Meanwhile, In-Context Learning relies on a single forward pass that often fails to encode sufficient task structure into the model's internal representations, and Test-Time Training, while effective, incurs quadratic training-time cost in the number of demonstrations. The paper asks whether directly optimizing the context derived from demonstration pairs can bridge the gap between ICL's task-awareness and gradient-based adaptation's expressiveness at lower computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes k few-shot demonstration pairs (input-output examples) and a single query input, formatted as either multiple-choice (select from predefined options by lowest loss) or question-answering (generate a matching answer). The number of demonstrations varies by benchmark: k=16 for NLP-LR and MMLU, k=10 for BBH, and typically fewer than 4 for ARC. The demonstration pairs are consumed in two stages: first, a forward pass extracts their prompt embeddings or layer-wise KV cache as the initialization for the trainable context; second, gradient-based optimization refines that context. The output is a single predicted answer per query, with all model weights remaining frozen throughout.

## Method

Context Tuning introduces two variants under a unified In-Context Optimization (ICO) framework. CT-Prompt initializes a soft prompt P_CT from the model's input-layer embeddings on the concatenated demonstration pairs, then optimizes it via gradient descent on the demonstration loss. CT-KV initializes layer-wise key-value prefixes Θ_CT from the model's per-layer KV cache activations on the same demonstrations, then optimizes those prefixes; because the prefix tokens do not generate queries, CT-KV achieves O(kℓ²) per-head attention cost versus O((kℓ)²) for CT-Prompt and TTT. Two critical design choices drive performance: Leave-One-Out Masking, which masks the context tokens corresponding to the current demonstration pair during its own optimization step to prevent answer retrieval, and Token Dropout, which randomly drops context tokens to regularize against overfitting. The paper further shows that CT-KV can be applied as a post-hoc refinement after TTT's LoRA weight updates (TTT+CT-KV), yielding additional gains because the two methods optimize complementary aspects—model weights versus context representation.

## Results

CT-KV achieves 44.2% on NLP-LR, 43.7% on MMLU, 57.9% on BBH, and 23.8% on ARC, outperforming ICL by +8.6, +2.5, +7.5, and +10.5 percentage points respectively. The combined TTT+CT-KV method reaches the best accuracy on all four benchmarks (47.6%, 44.1%, 58.2%, 25.8%) while CT-KV alone matches TTT's accuracy at roughly half the training time (e.g., 145s vs. 342s per task on NLP-LR, 7s vs. 14s on BBH). On large models (12B–32B), CT-KV improves over ICL by up to +6.1% (Mistral-12B) and +6.0% (Qwen3-32B) on BBH.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CT-KV outperforms ICL on every benchmark, with the largest gains on ARC (+10.5 pp) and NLP-LR (+8.6 pp), confirming that a single forward pass fails to encode sufficient task information in the KV cache. Against traditional prompt-based methods, CT-KV beats Prompt Tuning (m=32) by +2.8 to +11.8 pp and Prefix Tuning (m=32) by +2.2 to +14.5 pp across benchmarks; increasing m to match demonstration token count does not close the gap. CT-KV also surpasses LoRA, Rank-Stabilized LoRA, and DoRA on all four benchmarks, indicating that optimizing the context representation is more effective than low-rank weight updates for few-shot adaptation. Compared to TTT, CT-KV achieves statistically equivalent accuracy (44.2 vs. 44.1 on NLP-LR, 57.9 vs. 57.8 on BBH, 23.8 vs. 23.8 on ARC) while requiring at most half the training time per task, owing to its linear vs. quadratic scaling in the number of demonstrations. The sequential combination TTT+CT-KV yields the best accuracy on all benchmarks (e.g., 47.6% on NLP-LR, 25.8% on ARC), demonstrating that model-weight and context optimization are complementary within the In-Context Optimization framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

Context Tuning bridges two previously separate lines of work: prompt-based adaptation (Prompt Tuning, Prefix Tuning), which optimizes randomly initialized trainable vectors, and In-Context Learning, which relies on a single forward pass over demonstration pairs. The key departure is initializing the trainable prompt or KV-cache prefix directly from the model's representations of the demonstration pairs, then refining them via gradient descent with Leave-One-Out Masking and Token Dropout. The paper also introduces the In-Context Optimization (ICO) framework, positioning Test-Time Training (TTT, Akyürek et al. 2025) as optimizing model weights while Context Tuning optimizes the context representation, and shows the two are complementary (TTT+CT-KV outperforms either alone).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CT-KV offers a practically important efficiency win: it matches TTT's accuracy on NLP-LR, MMLU, BBH, and ARC while reducing training time from quadratic to linear in the number of demonstration pairs, making inference-time optimization more accessible. The diagnostic showing that ICL's KV cache often fails to encode task structure adequately (Table 4) provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for ICL's limitations and motivates gradient-based refinement. Open questions include whether stronger regularization or KV-cache compression can address CT-KV's occasional overfitting (e.g., shape bias on ARC), and whether the ICO framework extends to settings beyond single-task few-shot adaptation.
