## Problem

General LMMs can perceive single visual prompts and know where to look for one-region referring, but they misperceive when multiple regions are referred simultaneously, especially in contextual scenarios requiring precise global understanding (e.g., counting, localized captioning, or comparing regions). Existing solutions for multi-region referring rely on customized region encoders and expensive task-oriented fine-tuning, leaving a gap for a training-free approach that elicits this capability from off-the-shelf general LMMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265185/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes images or 16-frame video sequences with visual prompts (bounding boxes, points, masks, or numerical identifiers) directly overlaid on the visual input in Set-of-Mark style, concatenated with a text query. No separate region encoder is needed; the prompts are part of the visual input itself. Output is a text response—either a single-letter multiple-choice answer or an open-ended localized caption that references specific regions by their bracketed numerical IDs (e.g., [3][4][5]) and, for video, timestamp markers (e.g., <5>-<7>).

## Method

CSteer is a two-stage training-free pipeline. First, contextual vectors are pre-computed by sampling erroneous rollouts from a general LMM, having a text-only LLM judge rewrite them into correct referential captions using ground-truth as reference, then computing contrastive activation differences (positive rewrite vs. negative original) via teacher forcing on last-layer hidden states and averaging across samples. Second, during inference, these vectors are applied with layer decomposition: in-query steering edits the marker tokens within the user query context at early LLM layers (targeting information aggregation), while marker-only decoding steering edits hidden states only when the decoded token is a marker at middle-to-late layers (targeting output prediction). This decomposition is motivated by LLM information-flow findings that early layers aggregate and late layers predict, and ablations confirm the two components are complementary, with in-query steering favoring early layers and marker-only decoding favoring middle-to-late layers.

## Results

On GAR-Bench, Qwen3-VL-8B with CSteer achieves 65.8% MC accuracy and 57.4% OE accuracy, surpassing the tailored referring model GAR-8B (59.9% MC, 62.2% OE) on multiple-choice and the SoM prompting baseline (63.9% MC, 52.5% OE) on both tasks. On INST-IT, CSteer improves Qwen3-VL-8B from 78.5% to 80.4% on image OE and from 58.2% to 60.1% on video MC. On VIP-Bench, CSteer raises Qwen3-VL-8B's average from 71.5% (SoM) to 74.7%, and on BLINK from 55.9% to 57.5%, demonstrating consistent gains across multi-region and contextual referring scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSteer on Qwen3-VL-8B outperforms the tailored referring model GAR-8B (Wang et al., 2025b) on MC accuracy (65.8 vs. 59.9) but trails on OE (57.4 vs. 62.2), indicating CSteer is stronger at discriminative multi-region tasks while GAR's GRPO post-training retains an edge in detailed generation. Against the SoM prompting baseline (Yang et al., 2023), CSteer consistently improves across all benchmarks: +1.9 MC and +4.9 OE on GAR-Bench, +3.2 average on VIP-Bench, +1.6 on BLINK, and +1.9 image OE / +1.9 video MC on INST-IT. Compared to proprietary models, CSteer on Qwen3-VL-8B surpasses GPT-4o (53.5 MC, 51.5 OE) and o3 (61.3 MC, 56.9 OE) on GAR-Bench MC, and approaches Gemini-2.5-Pro (64.2 MC, 59.3 OE) within 1.6 MC points. On INST-IT, CSteer on Qwen3-VL-8B (80.4 image OE, 60.1 video MC) outperforms the tailored Inst-IT-Qwen 7B (67.9 image OE, 53.3 video MC) by a wide margin. On VIP-Bench, CSteer (74.7 avg) surpasses both VIP-LLaVA 7B (42.3) and Inst-IT-Qwen 7B (39.9), and even exceeds GPT-4o (58.7) and o3 (73.1), approaching Gemini-2.5-Pro (82.9). The ablation in Table 5 confirms that the decomposition strategy (in-query steering at early layers + marker-only decoding at middle/late layers) yields the best results, with Qwen3-VL reaching 66.4 GAR MC and 80.3 INST-IT, slightly above the 'all steps' baseline of 65.4 and 80.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA, Rimsky et al. 2024) from text-only LLM behavior control (refusal, hallucination) to the multimodal visual referring setting, constructing contrastive vectors from referential rewrites of incorrect object references rather than generic positive/negative text pairs. It departs from the dominant visual referring paradigm that relies on custom region encoders and task-specific fine-tuning (Sa2VA, GAR, INST-IT) by showing that general LMMs already encode multi-region referring behavior in their latent space and can be activated via simple vector addition. The layer-decomposed steering (in-query for early layers, marker-only for late layers) extends prior activation steering work (Liu et al. 2025, Su et al. 2025) that applied vectors uniformly across all decoding steps, grounding the decomposition in information-flow analyses of LLMs (Wang et al. 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that practitioners can add multi-region referring capability to off-the-shelf LMMs without any fine-tuning, custom encoders, or architectural changes—only a small set of pre-computed vectors (as few as 32 samples suffice). This lowers the barrier to deploying region-aware multimodal systems and suggests that general LMMs may already contain more referring capability than previously assumed. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to 8B models (unclear whether gains persist at larger scale), the approach's interaction with reasoning/thinking modes remains untested, and the vectoring data is drawn from a single benchmark (INST-IT), raising questions about domain transfer beyond natural images and standard video.
