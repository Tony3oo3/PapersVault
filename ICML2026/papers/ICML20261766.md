## Problem

Existing strategies for integrating vision-language models into end-to-end autonomous driving suffer from distribution misalignment between reasoning and action spaces, underexploit the general reasoning of pretrained VLMs, or incur prohibitive inference latency by synchronizing high-level reasoning with low-level control. The paper asks how a VLA model can fully leverage a pretrained VLM's generalist intelligence while mastering domain-specific action policies and meeting real-time driving frequency requirements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261766/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Understanding Expert consumes multi-view, multi-frame RGB images (N×H×W×C) plus textual prompts and outputs chain-of-thought reasoning; the Action Expert additionally ingests LiDAR BEV features and ego state to produce meta-actions over a 3-second horizon (H=3 at 1 s intervals), temporal waypoints (M=6 at 0.5 s intervals), and spatial route points (N nodes). The Action Refiner then takes the AE's trajectory proposals as an informative prior and performs truncated reverse denoising to emit a refined trajectory. A key structural detail is that the AE operates at a higher temporal frequency than the UE, reusing cached scene KV representations rather than recomputing them at every control step.

## Method

AutoMoT adopts a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture in which a frozen Qwen3-VL-4B Understanding Expert and a 1.6 B-parameter Action Expert (trained from scratch) share layer-wise joint attention in a common latent space. Cross-task interactions follow causal attention (decisions conditioned on understanding, planning conditioned on both), while intra-task and self-modal interactions use bidirectional attention, preserving hierarchical causality without forcing synchronized execution. Asynchronous inference is enabled through a persistent KV cache: the UE updates its scene representations at a lower frequency, and the AE concatenates the cached scene KV with its own per-step KV to compute joint attention, reducing per-step latency from 117 ms to 37 ms (8.5 Hz → 27 Hz) with negligible planning degradation (0.62 % relative L2 increase). A DiT-based Action Refiner with a Mixture-of-Attention (MoA) mechanism performs truncated diffusion denoising from the AE's trajectory proposals, adaptively fusing self-attention, BEV cross-attention, and decision-state cross-attention via learnable gating, further improving closed-loop driving score from 87.34 to 89.42 on Bench2Drive.

## Results

On the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, AutoMoT achieves a Driving Score (DS) of 87.34 and Success Rate (SR) of 70.00%, outperforming the previous best VLM-augmented method SimLingo (DS=85.07, SR=67.27%) by 2.27 DS and 2.73% SR. With the diffusion-based action refiner (AutoMoT+), performance further improves to DS=89.42 and SR=74.09%. On open-loop nuScenes, AutoMoT achieves L2 displacement of 0.32 m (avg) and collision rate of 0.07% (avg), attaining SOTA on collision rate while matching the best L2 among methods that do not fine-tune the VLM backbone. Inference latency is 37 ms (27 Hz) under asynchronous mode, an order-of-magnitude speedup over prior VLA baselines (430–7683 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoMoT outperforms all 27 compared baselines on the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, beating the strongest prior VLM-augmented method SimLingo (DS=85.07, SR=67.27%) by +2.27 DS and +2.73% SR, and the non-VLM TransFuser++ (DS=84.21) by +3.13 DS. On open-loop nuScenes, AutoMoT (L2 avg=0.32 m, collision avg=0.07%) matches Drive-R1 and DriveVLM-Dual on L2 (0.31 m) while achieving the lowest collision rate (0.07% vs. 0.09% and 0.10%), and substantially outperforms methods that fine-tune the VLM backbone such as ORION (L2=0.34, collision=0.37%) and OpenEMMA (L2=2.81). On reasoning benchmarks, AutoMoT with a frozen VLM backbone (LingoQA=67.00, TallyQA=81.40, InfoVQA=89.30) matches or exceeds ReCogDrive (LingoQA=67.20) and RoboTron-Drive (LingoQA=59.20) while preserving general-domain performance that degrades severely in fine-tuned baselines (e.g., TallyQA drops from 81.40 to 52.40 after fine-tuning). On the Senna decision-making benchmark, AutoMoT achieves 90.92% accuracy versus Senna's 88.47%. In inference latency, AutoMoT's asynchronous mode (37 ms, 27 Hz) is 11.6× faster than SimLingo (430 ms), 28.9× faster than AutoVLA fast mode (1072 ms), and 207× faster than OpenEMMA (7683 ms), making it the only VLA-based method operating above 10 Hz.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoMoT builds on the Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture with layer-wise joint attention sharing (Deng et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2025a) to unify a frozen VLM understanding expert and a task-specialized action expert within a single model, departing from both dual-system VLM-AD pipelines (DriveVLM, Senna) that suffer from distribution misalignment and single-transformer VLA approaches (AutoVLA, SimLingo) that tightly couple reasoning and planning at synchronized frequency. The action refiner extends truncated diffusion policies (Zou et al., 2025; Liao et al., 2025) by using AE trajectory proposals as informative priors rather than pure noise or clustered trajectories, and introduces a Mixture-of-Attention mechanism for adaptive multi-source fusion during denoising. The trajectory representation follows PDM-Lite (Jaeger et al., 2023), and the overall VLA paradigm for end-to-end AD is positioned within the broader line of work from EMMA, OpenEMMA, and Alpamayo-R1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical pathway to real-time VLA-based autonomous driving: asynchronous inference with KV caching achieves 27 Hz action generation (vs. 0.13 Hz for OpenEMMA) while maintaining near-identical planning accuracy, making onboard deployment of VLA-scale models substantially more feasible. The empirical finding that a frozen pre-trained VLM backbone achieves competitive scene understanding without AD-specific fine-tuning—while fine-tuning causes severe catastrophic forgetting on general-domain reasoning (e.g., TallyQA drops from 81.4 to 52.4)—offers a clear functional boundary for when domain adaptation is necessary. Open questions include whether the frozen-VLM strategy generalizes to larger backbones or more diverse driving domains, and whether the MoT architecture scales to multi-agent or long-horizon planning without additional architectural changes.
