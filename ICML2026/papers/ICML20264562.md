## Problem

Existing LLM-based model discovery systems are defined operationally through hand-crafted prompts, agent roles, and interaction protocols, without an explicit probabilistic formulation. This absence of a well-defined inference target prevents principled analysis of convergence, obscures failure modes, and makes it difficult to reason about the necessity of individual system components or connect to established statistical theory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264562/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes observed data as (data, context) pairs—e.g., membrane voltage traces with stimulus currents, or plasma potassium measurements with intake scenarios—along with a textual problem formulation and an initial model implementation in any programming language (Python, R). It produces a weighted population of N executable simulator models, each carrying a posterior weight and LLM-generated performance feedback, from which a posterior distribution over mechanistic models is read off. Downstream parameter estimates are obtained via temperature-scaled NPE-PFN sampling, exploiting TabPFN's autoregressive factorization to reduce estimation to a single sampling step.

## Method

ModelSMC recasts model discovery as Bayesian inference over executable programs, approximating the posterior p(m|x_o) with a Sequential Monte Carlo particle filter where each particle is a candidate simulator model. At each iteration, particles are resampled (adaptive, triggered by effective sample size), propagated via a mixture kernel that clones with probability α or draws a new implementation from the LLM conditioned on ancestral models and feedback, and weighted by the marginal likelihood p(x_o|m) integrated over model parameters θ. The likelihood is estimated training-free using NLE-PFN, which adapts the TabPFN tabular foundation model to conditional density estimation, eliminating per-iteration training loops. A key theoretical contribution is a consistency theorem showing that an idealized ModelSMC sampler converges in probability to the true model posterior with O(1/N) variance, establishing that the probabilistic framing admits formal analysis via classical SMC theory. Unlike prior agentic systems that select models via heuristic metrics (MMD, MSE, Wasserstein), ModelSMC derives its weighting directly from the Bayesian posterior and marginalizes over parameters rather than optimizing point estimates.

## Results

The primary metric is negative log marginal likelihood −log p(x_o|m) (lower is better), evaluated over the best particle across 10 discovery runs. On the Hodgkin-Huxley task, ModelSMC achieves 256.00, closely matching FunSearch+ (254.58) and the single-particle ablation ModelSMC N=1 (243.27). On the synthetic SIR task, ModelSMC scores −45834.78 versus FunSearch+ at −48794.12 and ModelSMC N=1 at −57028.36. On the pharmacological kidney task (where FunSearch+ is not applicable due to its Python-only constraint), ModelSMC achieves 42.85. The paper's central claim is that the probabilistic SMC framing does not compromise quantitative performance relative to heuristic baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ModelSMC against two baselines representing the dominant paradigms in LLM-based model discovery: FunSearch+ (an evolutionary search method extending Romera-Paredes et al., 2024, with likelihood-based scoring and NPE parameter estimation) and ModelSMC N=1 (a single-particle sequential variant that removes the SMC population mechanism). On the HH task, ModelSMC (256.00) is within 1.4 points of FunSearch+ (254.58) and 12.7 points from ModelSMC N=1 (243.27), indicating the population-based SMC framework does not degrade likelihood. On SIR, FunSearch+ (−48794.12) outperforms full ModelSMC (−45834.78) by roughly 2960 in −log-likelihood, while ModelSMC N=1 (−57028.36) is best. On the kidney task, only ModelSMC variants are evaluated (FunSearch+ is Python-specific), with ModelSMC N=1 (36.54) slightly ahead of full ModelSMC (42.85). The paper explicitly frames these as comparable rather than superior results, arguing that the key contribution is the probabilistic posterior over models—which enables posterior predictive checks, structural hypothesis ranking, and uncertainty quantification—rather than raw likelihood gains over heuristic search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on classical Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) samplers for static targets (Del Moral et al., 2006; Johansen, 2009) and Neural Likelihood Estimation (Papamakarios et al., 2019), repurposing them for a fundamentally different latent space: executable simulator programs rather than continuous parameters. It departs from prior LLM-based model discovery methods (FunSearch, G-Sim, LLM-SR) that define workflows operationally via prompts and agent roles without an explicit target distribution, by casting the problem as Bayesian inference over programs with the LLM serving as the proposal kernel. The closest prior work—Large Language Bayes (Domke, 2025) and AutoDiscovery (Agarwal et al., 2025)—also combines LLMs with Bayesian reasoning but does not maintain a population of executable models or provide SMC-style convergence guarantees. The use of TabPFN-based training-free likelihood estimation (Vetter et al., 2025) is a practical enabler that avoids per-iteration training loops.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing an explicit probabilistic target and SMC convergence theorem, this work gives the LLM-based model discovery community a shared mathematical language for analyzing proposal quality, weight degeneracy, and the role of individual components—something previously absent in heuristic agentic pipelines. The posterior-over-models output (rather than a single best model) enables diagnosing structural non-identifiability and quantifying uncertainty over mechanistic hypotheses, as demonstrated in the Hodgkin-Huxley and kidney experiments. Key limitations include computational cost from repeated simulation and likelihood estimation, dependence on surrogate likelihoods that may introduce bias, and the bottleneck of LLM proposal quality. Open questions include whether the framework can generate genuinely novel mechanisms beyond rediscovering known ones, and how to define meaningful geometry or similarity over high-dimensional program spaces for richer posterior analysis.
