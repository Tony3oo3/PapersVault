## Problem

Despite the empirical success of VAEs, a rigorous information-theoretic understanding of how continuous latent variables govern generalization has been missing. Existing IT analyses either target supervised settings or rely on discrete latent spaces (e.g., VQ-VAE), where discreteness enables enumeration of finite patterns—a step that is infeasible for standard Gaussian VAEs with continuous latent codes. This leaves the generalization properties of the most widely used unsupervised generative architecture theoretically unresolved.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on bounded image data (MNIST and Fashion-MNIST at 28×28 grayscale, CIFAR-10 at 32×32 RGB) under a leave-one-out protocol where one sample is held out as test and the remaining n−1 form the training set. The VAE encoder maps each image to a 32-dimensional Gaussian latent variable, and the decoder reconstructs the image from that code. The theoretical output is a decoder-independent generalization bound decomposed into an encoder-overfitting term and a latent-variable complexity term, together with a population-level 2-Wasserstein distance bound on the generated distribution.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a truncation-and-discretization argument that adapts the LOO-CMI framework to continuous latent spaces: Gaussian LVs are truncated to a bounded ball, discretized via a fixed data-independent codebook, the Donsker-Varadhan inequality is applied to the resulting discrete cell indices, and the data processing inequality recovers the continuous LV mutual information before taking limits R→∞ and K→∞. This yields a decoder-independent generalization bound (Theorem 2) where the gap is controlled solely by I(Zⁿ; U | φ, Xⁿ) (latent complexity) and I(φ; U | Xⁿ) (encoder overfitting), isolating the encoder's contribution from the decoder architecture. The framework extends to hierarchical VAEs, producing layer-wise bounds that reveal a structural trade-off: latent information compresses monotonically with depth via the data processing inequality while encoder complexity accumulates with additional parameters. For data generation, a finite-sample bound on the 2-Wasserstein distance between the true and generated distributions is derived, linking it directly to the empirical reconstruction loss and KL regularization already present in the VAE objective.

## Results

In the β-VAE sweep (β ∈ {0.01, 0.03, 0.3, 1.0, 3.0, 10.0}, n = 30,000), the latent-variable term I(Zⁿ;U|φ,Xⁿ) achieves strong positive Pearson correlation with the empirical reconstruction gap across all three datasets: 0.930 on MNIST, 0.904 on Fashion-MNIST, and 0.923 on CIFAR-10. The combined quantity √(I(Zⁿ;U|φ,Xⁿ)+I(φ;U|Xⁿ)) yields comparable or slightly stronger correlations (0.928, 0.961, 0.929 respectively). In contrast, the encoder term I(φ;U|Xⁿ) is dataset-dependent, showing positive correlation on MNIST (Pearson 0.434) but negative correlation on Fashion-MNIST (−0.559) and CIFAR-10 (−0.332), indicating that the LV term is the dominant driver of generalization variation when latent regularization is directly manipulated.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no direct method-vs-method benchmarking; the 'comparisons' are between the two information-theoretic components of the proposed bound (encoder overfitting I(φ;U|Xⁿ) vs. latent complexity I(Zⁿ;U|φ,Xⁿ)) and their ability to track the empirical generalization gap. In the training-size sweep (Table 2, n=30,000), the encoder term dominates on MNIST (Pearson 0.373 vs. 0.033 for the LV term), while on Fashion-MNIST neither term alone is sufficient and their combination is needed (Pearson 0.194 vs. 0.012 and 0.187 individually). In the β-VAE sweep (Table 3), the picture reverses: the LV term becomes the dominant predictor across all three datasets (Pearson ≥ 0.90), while the encoder term flips to negative correlation on Fashion-MNIST and CIFAR-10, suggesting that when latent capacity is the manipulated variable, the LV channel governs generalization. The paper positions its contribution relative to prior VAE generalization analyses (Chérief-Abdellatif et al. 2022, Mbacke et al. 2023, Futami & Fujisawa 2025), noting it is the first to provide a decoder-independent bound for standard continuous VAEs without requiring a data-independent decoder or discrete latent spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LOO-CMI information-theoretic generalization framework (Rammal et al., 2022; Haghifam et al., 2022) from supervised and discrete-latent settings to standard Gaussian VAEs with continuous latent variables. It directly generalizes the authors' prior VQ-VAE analysis (Futami & Fujisawa, 2025), which relied on the finite cardinality of discrete LVs to enumerate patterns, by introducing a truncation-and-discretization argument that handles continuous variables without discrete data assumptions. The encoder-overfitting + representation-complexity decomposition follows the conceptual lineage of Kawaguchi et al. (2023) and Sefidgaran et al. (2023), but is the first to isolate these terms for unsupervised continuous-LV models and to extend them layer-wise to hierarchical VAEs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first decoder-independent IT generalization bound for standard VAEs, providing a clean theoretical separation between encoder overfitting and latent-variable complexity that prior PAC-Bayesian analyses (Chérief-Abdellatif et al., 2022; Mbacke et al., 2023) could not achieve without restrictive assumptions like data-independent decoders. The layer-wise bound for hierarchical VAEs offers a formal explanation for progressive information compression across depth, and the Wasserstein-distance generation bound connects the VAE objective to population-level distributional guarantees under joint training. Key limitations include the reliance on computable proxies rather than exact MI terms, the restriction to bounded squared reconstruction loss, and the fact that the encoder-complexity proxy showed negative correlation on Fashion-MNIST and CIFAR-10, suggesting the influence-function approximation may be unreliable in practice. Open questions include extending the framework to unbounded log-loss objectives, developing tighter estimators for the continuous MI terms, and understanding whether the decoder-independence of the bound has practical implications for architecture design.
