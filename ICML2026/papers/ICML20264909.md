## Problem

Existing flow matching and diffusion models for N-body trajectory prediction rely on trivial, observation-agnostic priors (e.g., isotropic Gaussian) that ignore both the temporal correlations inherent in particle trajectories and the SE(3) spatial symmetries of the system. This mismatch between prior and target distribution increases transport cost, complicates the learned vector field, and ultimately limits prediction accuracy and sampling efficiency.

## Input / Output

The model takes a geometric trajectory graph consisting of observed 3D positions for N particles over T_obs timesteps (e.g., 10 frames), time-invariant node features (particle charge, atomic type one-hot encoding), and an interaction topology (fully connected for N-body, bond-based for molecules), and produces probabilistic 3D position trajectories for the remaining T_pred timesteps (e.g., 20 frames). The output is generated by integrating a learned ODE vector field from the GP-constructed prior to the target distribution, and the entire pipeline is SE(3)-equivariant: rotating or translating the input trajectories yields correspondingly transformed predictions without retraining.

## Method

GP-EquiFlow constructs a data-dependent SE(3)-equivariant prior using per-particle vector-valued Gaussian processes (VVGPs) with LMC kernels (RBF, linear, periodic), where the coregionalization matrix is fixed to identity and observation noise is isotropic to provably guarantee SO(3)-equivariance (Theorem 3.1), while a normalize-denormalize procedure enforces translational equivariance. The time-dependent vector field is learned by an Equivariant Geometric Trajectory Network (ETGN) backbone composed of equivariant graph convolution layers (EGCL) and equivariant temporal cross-attention layers (ETCL), trained via conditional flow matching with linear interpolation paths. Training is two-stage: VVGPs are first fit per particle (5 epochs, L-BFGS-B on NLL), then the CFM loss is optimized over the constructed priors. The key insight is that the GP prior, being non-parametric and directly encoding temporal structure from observations, sits much closer to the target distribution than learned or trivial priors, enabling accurate prediction with as few as 2–10 Euler sampling steps. Theorem 3.2 further proves that the full marginal probability path inherits SE(3)-equivariance from the equivariant prior and the SO(3)-equivariant, translationally invariant vector field.

## Results

On the N-body benchmarks (Gravity N=10, Spring N=5, Charged N=5), GP-EquiFlow achieves ADE/FDE of 0.105/0.274, 0.0019/0.0039, and 0.068/0.152 respectively, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art GeoTDM by 44.6% (ADE) and 48.9% (FDE) on average. On the MD17 molecular dynamics benchmark (8 molecules, 9–21 atoms), GP-EquiFlow achieves the best ADE and FDE on 6 out of 8 molecules and ranks top-2 on all, reducing ADE by 8.8% and FDE by 10.7% on average over GeoTDM. The method also achieves strong predictions with only 2 Euler sampling steps, and performance peaks at 10 steps before degrading at higher step counts due to numerical instability near τ=1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GP-EquiFlow outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest baseline GeoTDM (NeurIPS 2024), it reduces ADE by 44.6% and FDE by 48.9% on average across the three N-body datasets, with the largest gap on Gravity (ADE 0.105 vs 0.256) and Spring (ADE 0.0019 vs 0.0030). Frame-to-frame equivariant models (TFN, SE(3)-Tr, EGNN) are substantially weaker, with EGNN being the best among them (e.g., Gravity ADE 0.310 vs GP-EquiFlow's 0.105), reflecting error accumulation in autoregressive prediction. The deterministic model EqMotion and the probabilistic SVAE also trail significantly, with SVAE performing worst on Gravity (ADE 0.582). On MD17, the margin over GeoTDM narrows (8.8% ADE, 10.7% FDE average) due to the smaller spatial range and stronger vibrations in molecular dynamics, but GP-EquiFlow still wins on 6 of 8 molecules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023), equivariant geometric trajectory modeling (Han et al., 2024, GeoTDM/ETGN), and vector-valued Gaussian processes (Alvarez et al., 2012). The key departure from prior work is the prior distribution design: where GeoTDM and other flow-matching trajectory models use either trivial isotropic Gaussians or a learned ETGN-based prior, GP-EquiFlow replaces the prior with a non-parametric, SE(3)-equivariant VVGP construction whose equivariance is guaranteed by structural constraints (identity coregionalization matrices, isotropic noise) rather than learned. The paper also provides formal proofs (Theorems 3.1–3.2) that the entire probability path inherits SE(3)-equivariance from the prior and the SO(3)-equivariant backbone, a theoretical guarantee not present in prior flow-matching trajectory work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: the GP-based prior requires far fewer parameters than a learned equivariant network prior, reducing optimization difficulty and enabling accurate predictions with only 10 Euler steps (and reasonable results with just 2). The finding that a non-parametric kernel-based prior outperforms a learned ETGN-based prior (GeoFlow) challenges the implicit assumption that more expressive learned priors are always preferable for flow matching. Limitations include a fixed prediction horizon (requiring autoregressive decomposition for long-term forecasting), modest gains on molecular dynamics (8.8% ADE) compared to N-body systems (44.6% ADE), and the observation that excessive sampling steps degrade performance due to the singularity at τ=1. Open questions include whether the GP prior design generalizes to higher-dimensional or anisotropic systems, and whether one-step generation methods can further reduce the sampling cost.
