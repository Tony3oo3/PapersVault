## Problem

Neural CDEs are computationally expensive because standard spline interpolation of noisy, irregularly sampled time series produces jagged control paths that force adaptive ODE solvers to take excessively small steps, inflating the Number of Function Evaluations (NFE) by up to an order of magnitude. This stiffness bottleneck limits the practical applicability of Neural CDEs to real-world noisy signals, where exact interpolation rigidly couples path regularity to input noise rather than to the underlying signal structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes irregularly sampled multivariate time series as strictly increasing timestamp-feature pairs (t_k, x_k) with variable sequence lengths and feature dimensions (3 to 963). For classification, the final concatenated latent state Z(T) is passed through a linear head to produce a class label; for forecasting, a linear projection maps the encoder embedding to a 24-step future horizon. A key design choice is that the continuous control path X(t) is always constructed from the original observations x_k (preserving input dimensionality and interpretability), while the attention weights are computed from optionally convolved context vectors u_k, decoupling the path geometry from the feature extraction stage.

## Method

The core contribution replaces deterministic spline interpolation with Kernel (Nadaraya-Watson) or Gaussian Process smoothing to construct regularized control paths, provably bounding NFE by δ^(-1/(p+1))·h^(-1) independent of input noise. To recover high-frequency details lost during smoothing, the Multi-View CDE (MV-CDE) and its convolutional extension (MVC-CDE) employ M learnable query vectors (inspired by Q-Former) that compute attention scores over observations, generating M distinct smoothed paths with head-specific bandwidths. For GP paths, heteroscedastic noise variance is set inversely proportional to attention scores, so high-attention observations are treated as high-precision points. The M paths are integrated simultaneously as a block-diagonal ODE system in a single adaptive solver call, with the global step size bottlenecked by the stiffest (smallest-bandwidth) head. The MVC-CDE variant adds a 2-layer 1D CNN front-end to extract local temporal context before attention, enabling sharper, more localized head activations on high-dimensional or noisy inputs.

## Results

On the three UEA classification benchmarks, MVC-CDE with GP (MVC GP) achieves 95.07% on CharacterTrajectories, 98.28% on SpokenArabicDigits, and 95.39% on UWaveGestureLibrary, setting new state-of-the-art accuracy on the latter two. In terms of efficiency, MVC GP reduces average NFE by up to an order of magnitude compared to cubic spline CDEs (e.g., 218.6 vs. 1042 on UWave) and achieves 4.3×–14.5× total training speedups over standard Neural CDE baselines. On the ETTm1 forecasting task with 30% missing observations, MVC GP attains 0.428 MSE in 248 s, outperforming both Cubic NCDE (0.487 MSE, 625 s) and GRU (0.476 MSE, 33 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVC GP outperforms all CDE-based baselines (Linear, Cubic, ODE-RNN, Log-NCDE) on every dataset in both accuracy and efficiency. Against Cubic CDE, the accuracy gap is largest on UWaveGestureLibrary (95.39% vs. 85.68%, +9.7 pp) and SpokenArabicDigits (98.28% vs. 88.70%, +9.6 pp), while training time drops from 81.8 s to 12.3 s on UWave. Compared to Mamba, MVC GP wins on SpokenArabicDigits (98.28% vs. 96.82%) and UWaveGestureLibrary (95.39% vs. 80.23%) in accuracy, and is faster on CharacterTrajectories (22.6 s vs. 31.2 s); however, Mamba edges out MVC GP on CharacterTrajectories accuracy (96.64% vs. 95.07%). Against GRU-D, MVC GP achieves substantially higher accuracy on UWave (95.39% vs. 78.86%) and CharacterTrajectories (95.07% vs. 93.17%), while GRU-D is faster due to its discrete architecture. ODE-RNN is consistently the weakest CDE baseline, with accuracy as low as 37.95% on UWave and NFEs exceeding 4000, confirming that the stiffness problem is most acute for latent-ODE formulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Neural CDEs (Kidger et al., 2020) by replacing deterministic spline interpolation with Kernel and Gaussian Process smoothing for control-path construction, then recovering lost high-frequency detail via a Q-Former-inspired multi-head attention mechanism (MV-CDE/MVC-CDE). It departs from the algebraic-efficiency line (Log-NCDE, Neural RDEs) that compresses integration via path signatures, instead targeting the geometric irregularity of the input path as the root cause of solver stiffness. The block-diagonal parallel integration of multiple smoothed trajectories connects to Structured Linear CDEs (Walker et al., 2025), but here the structure serves multi-scale representation rather than associative scanning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical bound linking NFE to the L_{1/(p+1)}-quasi-norm of the (p+1)-th path derivative, combined with the demonstrated 5–14× speedup at equal or better accuracy, gives practitioners a principled lever (bandwidth h) to trade off integration cost against representational fidelity. Key open questions include whether the stiffest-head bottleneck (Theorem 3.4) can be alleviated by per-head tolerances, whether the GP preprocessing cost becomes prohibitive at very long or very high-dimensional sequences, and whether the approach extends naturally to strictly online single-step streaming without rolling-window refitting. The bandwidth sensitivity and the irreducible loss of genuinely informative high-frequency content remain practical limitations for long-horizon forecasting.
