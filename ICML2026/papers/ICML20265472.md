## Problem

Existing sEEG signal reconstruction methods treat electrodes as generic token sequences or rely on local graph message passing, underutilizing the physical 3D spatial arrangement, global graph topology, and anatomical priors inherent to intracranial electrode implantations. Additionally, deterministic reconstruction losses (MSE, L1) favor average signal patterns, producing over-smoothed reconstructions that attenuate fine-grained time-frequency structure critical for capturing seizure-related neural dynamics.

## Input / Output

The model receives log-amplitude STFT spectrograms (64 frequency bins × 16 time frames) from observed bipolar channels, with all channels belonging to masked electrode series replaced by a shared learnable query vector. Static per-electrode metadata—3D coordinates, anatomical region labels, and gray/white matter ratios—is injected as time-invariant priors. The output is the reconstructed spectrogram at each masked electrode plus an attention-derived interaction matrix (averaged across heads from the final Transformer block) that serves as an interpretable by-product for post-hoc connectivity visualization rather than a supervised prediction target.

## Method

EpiTwin is a conditional spatial graph transformer with three core components. First, Hybrid Spatial Positional Encoding (HSPE) fuses 3D sinusoidal positional encoding from electrode coordinates, Laplacian eigenvector-based topological encoding, and anatomical encoding (region embedding + tissue-ratio MLP) into a single static prior residually added to dynamic CNN-extracted features. Second, Geometry-Functional Biased Attention (GFBA) constructs a masked adjacency matrix combining cosine functional similarity with Gaussian spatial decay over k-NN neighbors (k=8), then injects head-specific geometric and functional bias terms directly into attention logits, making the Transformer explicitly spatially aware. Third, a two-stage adversarial refinement strategy—L1 pretraining followed by a multi-scale PatchGAN discriminator with hinge loss and spectral normalization—mitigates spectral over-smoothing while a frequency-exclusive progressive decoder upsamples only along the spectral dimension to preserve temporal alignment of transient neural events.

## Results

On the primary single-patient benchmark with series-aware masking, EpiTwin achieves a log-spectrogram RMSE of 0.492, PCC of 0.994, LSD of 0.463, and High-LSD of 0.449, outperforming all six baselines on every metric. The headline gain is a 16.8% relative RMSE reduction over the strongest baseline LaBraM (0.591 → 0.492), with LSD dropping from 0.541 to 0.463 and High-LSD from 0.525 to 0.449, indicating substantially less spectral over-smoothing. Under the strict leave-one-subject-out protocol, EpiTwin still leads with RMSE 0.956 and LSD 0.870 versus LaBraM's 1.253 and 1.159.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EpiTwin outperforms all six baselines on all four metrics (RMSE, PCC, LSD, High-LSD) in the primary single-patient setting. The closest competitor is LaBraM (ICLR 2024), a patch-based Transformer foundation model, which EpiTwin surpasses by 16.8% in RMSE (0.492 vs. 0.591) and 14.4% in LSD (0.463 vs. 0.541), demonstrating that explicit spatial/anatomical inductive bias beats generic token-sequence modeling under sparse electrode coverage. Bi-LSTM is the second-closest traditional baseline (RMSE 0.616) but still trails EpiTwin by 20.3% in RMSE. The graph-based DGCNN (RMSE 0.905) and compact CNN EEGNet (RMSE 1.062) are substantially worse, confirming that local message-passing or channel-independent convolution cannot compensate for missing global spatial structure. Among brain foundation models, Brant (RMSE 1.786) and BrainBERT (RMSE 1.324) perform markedly worse than EpiTwin, with Brant also showing the lowest PCC (0.934), indicating that scaling sequence-based masked modeling without spatial priors is insufficient for sEEG reconstruction. Under the most challenging leave-one-subject-out protocol, EpiTwin maintains its lead over LaBraM by 23.7% in RMSE (0.956 vs. 1.253) and 24.9% in LSD (0.870 vs. 1.159), confirming robust generalization to unseen subjects.

## Relation to Prior Work

EpiTwin sits at the intersection of masked autoencoding (He et al., 2022), graph transformer backbones (Ying et al., 2021), and adversarial image-to-image translation (Isola et al., 2017), repurposing each for the specific geometry of intracranial electrode arrays. It departs from sequence-based brain foundation models (LaBraM, BrainBERT, Brant) that treat sEEG channels as unordered tokens, and from graph-based EEG methods that rely on local message passing without explicit 3D spatial or anatomical priors. The Hybrid Spatial Positional Encoding borrows spectral positional encodings from graph theory (Xu et al., 2019) and sinusoidal coordinate encoding from vision transformers, while the Geometry-Functional Biased Attention extends standard self-attention with learned geometric and functional bias terms — a combination not previously applied to sEEG reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit spatial and anatomical inductive bias, rather than increased model capacity, is the key lever for improving sEEG reconstruction under sparse electrode coverage — a finding with direct implications for epilepsy surgery planning where electrode placement is clinically constrained. The adversarial refinement module addresses a well-known but under-addressed limitation (spectral over-smoothing from deterministic losses) in a computationally efficient way compared to diffusion-based alternatives. However, the evaluation is limited to a small clinical cohort (5 patients, one primary), the attention-derived interaction matrix is explicitly not validated as a physiological connectivity measure, and generalizability to different implantation protocols, larger cohorts, or other neural recording modalities (e.g., ECoG) remains untested.
