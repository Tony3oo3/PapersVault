## Problem

Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer from progressive dilution of visual information during long-context autoregressive generation, causing them to fail at test-time scaling unlike their text-only LLM counterparts. Existing remedies either strengthen text-centric reasoning or re-inject static visual tokens as a fixed initial context, but both approaches still lose visual grounding as the reasoning chain lengthens, preventing robust multi-step spatial and perceptual reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model takes one or more RGB images (up to 32 views for multi-view benchmarks) and a text question, and produces a text Chain-of-Thought reasoning chain with a final VQA answer. Uniquely, between each language reasoning step the model enters a 'latent mode' for K=16 steps where it generates non-textual latent tokens (fed back via hidden states rather than token embeddings), controlled by special <latent> and </latent> delimiters. At inference, no external vision encoder is required; the model relies entirely on learned visual grounding within its latent representations.

## Method

VaLR introduces learnable latent tokens before each CoT reasoning step, supervised by a Representation Alignment (REPA) loss that enforces patch-wise cosine similarity between projected intermediate MLLM hidden states and dense features from frozen external vision encoders (DINOv3, SigLIPv2, π³). Training follows a two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 performs standard SFT on 450K CoT VQA samples to establish language-based reasoning, then Stage 2 inserts K latent tokens before each reasoning step and jointly optimizes the cross-entropy objective with the REPA loss (λ=0.5) at the middle layer (12th) of the MLLM decoder. A multi-encoder variant averages REPA losses across complementary encoders—DINOv3 for fine-grained appearance, SigLIPv2 for semantics, and π³ for 3D geometry—distilling diverse visual knowledge into the latent space. The key insight is that dynamic per-step visual re-injection via alignment prevents the signal decay inherent in static visual context, enabling true test-time scaling where performance improves monotonically with reasoning length.

## Results

On VSI-Bench (3D multi-view spatial reasoning), VaLR-M (multi-encoder: DINOv3 + SigLIPv2 + π³) achieves 52.9% average accuracy, improving the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model from 33.0% by 19.9 percentage points. On perception benchmarks, VaLR-M reaches 64.7% on BLINK, 72.3% on MMStar, 86.9% on V*, and 87.6% on CVBench, consistently outperforming the base model by 8–13%p. Notably, VaLR is the only method that exhibits monotonic performance improvement as reasoning length increases (test-time scaling), while all baselines degrade beyond intermediate reasoning lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VaLR-M substantially outperforms all baselines on VSI-Bench: it beats GPT-4o (34.0%) by 18.9%p, Ocean-R1 (30.5%) by 22.4%p, and the Qwen2.5-VL base (33.0%) by 19.9%p. The existing latent reasoning methods collapse dramatically on this multi-view benchmark—LVR (18.4%), CoVT (18.6%), and Monet (14.0%) all fall far below even the untrained base model, confirming that latent reasoning without dynamic visual re-injection fails to maintain visual grounding over long reasoning chains. On perception benchmarks (Table 2), VaLR-M leads on BLINK (64.7% vs. GPT-4o's 63.0%), MMStar (72.3% vs. GPT-4o's 65.2%), V* (86.9% vs. GPT-4o's 42.9%), and CVBench (87.6% vs. GPT-4o's 79.2%), though GPT-4o retains an edge on MMVP (68.7% vs. 60.3%). Claude-4-Sonnet is outperformed by VaLR-M on all five perception benchmarks by 11–72%p. The key differentiator is test-time scaling: VaLR is the only method whose accuracy improves monotonically with reasoning length, while Ocean-R1 and LVR both peak at intermediate lengths and then degrade (e.g., Ocean-R1 drops from 62.7% to 56.5% on MMVP at 300 tokens).

## Relation to Prior Work

VaLR extends the latent reasoning paradigm from LLMs (Hao et al., 2024b) to MLLMs by introducing vision-aligned latent tokens before each CoT step, and adapts the REPA representation alignment loss (Yu et al., 2025b) from diffusion training to supervise these tokens against frozen vision encoder features. It departs from prior MLLM latent reasoning methods (LVR, CoVT, Monet) that inject visual information only as a static initial context, instead dynamically re-grounding the model in visual features at every reasoning stage. The multi-encoder alignment strategy builds on the observation that different vision encoders (DINO, CLIP/SigLIP, π³) capture complementary visual properties, extending the single-encoder REPA formulation to a multi-encoder average.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration of test-time scaling in MLLMs—where performance improves monotonically with reasoning length rather than degrading—addresses a fundamental bottleneck for deploying MLLMs in agentic settings (VLA, CUA) that require long-horizon visual reasoning. The collapse of prior latent reasoning baselines (LVR, CoVT, Monet) to near-chance on multi-view benchmarks is a useful negative result showing that static visual context is insufficient. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Qwen2.5-VL-7B to other architectures or scales, whether it extends to video inputs, and whether the training-time dependency on external vision encoders can be eliminated entirely.
