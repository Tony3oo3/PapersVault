## Problem

Existing federated learning methods that exchange class prototypes compress an entire class's feature distribution into a single point, which is fundamentally insufficient under domain heterogeneity where the same class occupies vastly different regions of feature space across clients (e.g., clean MNIST digits vs. cluttered SVHN digits). Averaging such point prototypes yields a global signal that accurately represents no domain and discards the intra-class geometric structure needed for cross-domain generalization.

## Input / Output

Each client holds labeled images from two distinct visual domains (e.g., grayscale MNIST + color SVHN for digits, or Amazon + Webcam for Office31) and processes them through a shared ResNet-18 backbone producing 2048-dim features. Rather than uploading full feature sets or model gradients, each client transmits only r=10 compressed manifold points per class alongside its model parameters. The server outputs enhanced global manifolds and an aggregated model, which clients use to produce final class predictions (10 digit classes or 31 office product categories).

## Method

FML replaces point prototypes with compressed perceptual manifolds as knowledge carriers, operating in three stages. On the client side, Perceptual Manifold Compression (PMC) selects r representative points per class via Fast Greedy DPP, maximizing an Information Efficiency Index that balances manifold volume preservation against communication cost. On the server, Manifold Mutual Learning (MML) first partitions the concatenated cross-domain manifold into structure-driven clusters using parameter-free FINCH, then applies multi-head attention so each cluster selectively absorbs information from other clusters while a residual mixing coefficient γ preserves domain-specific geometry. On the client side, local training is guided by two manifold-aware losses: an approximation loss (MA) that pulls each sample's feature toward its nearest anchor on the downloaded global manifold, and a separation loss (MS) that enforces inter-class discriminability via cosine-similarity-based contrastive scoring against all class manifold centroids. A mechanism-aware convergence theorem establishes linear convergence under bounded distortion from compression and attention fusion.

## Results

On the Digits benchmark (MNIST, SVHN, SYN, USPS), FML achieves a weighted-average Top-1 accuracy of 82.91%, surpassing the next-best method FedDIM (81.24%) by 1.67% and the FedAvg baseline (79.44%) by 3.47%. On Office31 (Amazon, Webcam, DSLR), FML reaches 58.72% AVG accuracy, outperforming the runner-up FedCD (52.25%) by 6.47% and FedAvg (50.48%) by 8.25%. FML attains the highest per-domain accuracy in nearly every domain across both benchmarks, with particularly large gains on the Webcam domain (78.49% vs. 69.41% for FedCD) and the SYN domain (80.32% vs. 78.26% for FPL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FML outperforms all 11 baselines on the weighted-average accuracy of both benchmarks. On Digits, the closest competitor is FedDIM (81.24% AVG), which excels on MNIST (96.48%) but collapses on Office31 (43.45% AVG, −7.03% below FedAvg), revealing its inability to generalize across domains; FML leads FedDIM by 1.67% on Digits and 15.27% on Office31. On Office31, FedCD is the strongest baseline (52.25% AVG), yet FML surpasses it by 6.47%, with the largest per-domain gap on Webcam (78.49% vs. 69.41%). Prototype-based methods (FedProto, FedProc, FPL) and parameter-averaging methods (FedAvg, FedProx) all trail FML by 2–7% on Office31, confirming the paper's argument that single-point prototypes are insufficient under domain heterogeneity. Notably, MOON and FedDIM degrade below the FedAvg baseline on Office31 (40.34% and 43.45% vs. 50.48%), indicating that contrastive or decision-matrix strategies designed for label heterogeneity can actively harm cross-domain generalization. FML also achieves the best per-domain accuracy in 7 out of 7 domains (all four Digits domains plus all three Office31 domains), demonstrating consistent rather than cherry-picked superiority.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant prototype-based knowledge transfer paradigm in federated learning (FedProto, FedProc, FPL, FedCD) by arguing that compressing an entire class distribution to a single point is fundamentally insufficient under domain heterogeneity. It repurposes perceptual manifold concepts from representation learning (Isomap, geometric deep learning) as explicit, communication-efficient knowledge carriers, and distinguishes itself from prior manifold-based FL methods (FedMRUR, FedMR, FedMC, FedMP) that use manifolds as regularizers or calibration tools rather than as the primary unit of cross-client communication. The attention-based Manifold Mutual Learning mechanism and DPP-based compression are borrowed from NLP and submodular optimization respectively, but their combination as a federated knowledge-transfer pipeline is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from point-based to structure-based knowledge transfer offers a principled way to preserve domain-specific geometric information while still enabling cross-domain generalization, which is a practical need in multi-institution or multi-device FL deployments. However, the evaluation is limited to two small-scale benchmarks (10-class Digits, 31-class Office31) with a single architecture (ResNet-18), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more heterogeneous settings. The paper itself acknowledges the absence of privacy guarantees and a uniform communication protocol, and future work on resource-aware adaptation, differential privacy integration, and personalized FL variants would be needed for real-world deployment.
