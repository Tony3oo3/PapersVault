## Problem

Existing EHR foundation models inherit NLP paradigms that misalign with clinical data: quantile binning under-resolves sparse but critical pathological value ranges, standard positional embeddings discard calendrical periodicity and relative intervals, and next-token prediction objectives are too short-sighted to capture the multi-horizon temporal planning inherent in clinical practice. This paper addresses all three gaps simultaneously with EHR-specific binning, temporal encoding, and a forecasting objective.

## Input / Output

Input is a chronological sequence of (timestamp, feature, value) triplets plus patient demographics, where timestamps are continuous in seconds, features are discrete tokens (diagnosis codes, lab tests, vital signs), and numeric values are discretized into 10 bins per feature via pathology-focused binning. The model operates on a 2048-token context window with 512-token overlap for longer sequences. Output is dual-purpose: (1) task-specific predictions (mortality, decompensation, length of stay, phenotyping, etc.) via fine-tuning, and (2) generative output of future EHR events with fine-grained timestamps decomposed across 11 calendrical scales (10-year down to 1-minute), enabling realistic patient trajectory synthesis.

## Method

The architecture is a decoder-only transformer backbone augmented with three EHR-specific components. First, Pathology-Focused Binning uses Gaussian KDE to estimate local value density per feature, assigns inverse-density weights, and performs weighted percentile binning so that sparse pathological ranges receive finer resolution than dense physiologic ranges. Second, Dual-Calendar RoPE partitions each attention head's 64-dim query/key vector into 24 positional dimensions (standard RoPE for disambiguating concurrent events) and 40 temporal dimensions (modular rotation angles across 20 predefined calendrical periods from 5 minutes to 300 years), enabling simultaneous encoding of relative sequence order and absolute cyclical time. Third, the Time-Conditioned Foreseeing (TCF) objective replaces the standard unembedding layer with a module that (a) predicts the next event's time delta as 11 categorical labels via multi-scale decomposition, and (b) conditions the last hidden state on N_foresee=10 future timestamps through residual fusion of time embeddings, then predicts the corresponding future feature tokens—jointly learning P(T_next|E_past) and P(F_foresee|T_foresee, E_past) rather than the short-sighted P(F_next|E_past) of NTP.

## Results

On MIMIC-III (117 features), the proposed model achieves a total weighted cross-entropy score of 4.686 (lower is better), outperforming the second-best baseline MOTOR (4.889) by 4.1%, with IHM AUROC of 0.889 and AUPRC of 0.607. On MIMIC-IV, the model delivers an average 18.0% improvement across all metrics and a 40.7% average AUPRC gain over baselines under severe class imbalance. On the external eICU dataset, the model outperforms MOTOR and ETHOS on both IHM and ICU Mortality prediction while remaining robust to year-to-year temporal distribution shifts. In generative evaluation, timestamp prediction RMSE/MAE is reduced by up to 99% compared to ETHOS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 11 EHR foundation models (HEART, MOTOR, EHRSHOT, TRADE, EHRMamba, FM4EHR, ETHOS, STraTS, BEHRT, Med-BERT, Foresight) plus traditional ML baselines (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost). On MIMIC-III, the No-share variant dominates all baselines across all seven tasks, with the largest gains on decompensation tasks (Dec-death AUPRC 0.400 vs. MOTOR's 0.274, a 45% relative improvement) and Vaso (0.498 vs. 0.438). On MIMIC-IV, the model outperforms MOTOR (No-share) and ETHOS (Share) on every task, with IHM AUPRC of 0.419 vs. 0.385 (MOTOR) and 0.336 vs. 0.284 (ETHOS). On the external eICU dataset, the model maintains its lead (IHM AUROC 0.786 vs. MOTOR's 0.757; 0.771 vs. ETHOS's 0.735), confirming cross-hospital generalization. Against ML baselines on IHM, the model achieves the best AUPRC on both MIMIC-III (0.607 vs. CatBoost's 0.565) and MIMIC-IV (0.419 vs. CatBoost's 0.359), surpassing even the strongest gradient-boosted trees. The Share variant is slightly weaker than No-share but still outperforms all Share-compatible baselines (FM4EHR, ETHOS, STraTS) by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RoPE positional encoding (Su et al., 2024) by partitioning its dimension into a positional subspace (for disambiguating concurrent events) and a calendrical-temporal subspace (for multi-scale periodic time), creating Dual-Calendar RoPE. It departs from the dominant NLP-derived paradigms (NTP, MLM) used in prior EHR foundation models by proposing the Time-Conditioned Foreseeing objective, which explicitly models event timing and multi-horizon forecasting rather than short-sighted next-token prediction. The Pathology-Focused Binning strategy addresses a gap identified across HEART, FM4EHR, and TRADE, where quantile or standard-deviation-based binning fails to allocate sufficient resolution to sparse but clinically critical pathologic value ranges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that EHR-specific architectural choices (density-aware binning, calendrical temporal encoding, time-conditioned multi-horizon forecasting) yield consistent and substantial improvements over NLP-adapted baselines across 11 downstream tasks and 3 datasets, with up to 54% AUPRC gains under severe class imbalance. The first demonstration of fine-grained temporal generative modeling for EHRs (99% RMSE reduction in timestamp prediction vs. ETHOS) opens a path toward synthetic trajectory generation for data-scarce settings. Key limitations include ICU-centric evaluation, the absence of standardized metrics for temporal generation quality, and the need for validation in non-ICU clinical settings; the authors also identify integration with LLMs and multimodal records as natural next steps.
