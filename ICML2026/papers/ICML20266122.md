## Problem

End-to-end autonomous driving models trained with imitation learning generalize poorly in long-tail scenarios where expert demonstrations are sparse, and small prediction errors compound over time. Reinforcement learning offers complementary reward-driven supervision, but applying it in offline real-world driving settings—where datasets are dominated by near-optimal expert trajectories and no interactive simulator is available—makes value estimation unstable and RL-only or two-stage fine-tuning approaches prone to degradation. The paper asks how RL can be stably integrated with IL under these offline constraints to improve robustness without an external simulator.

## Input / Output

The model takes 360° multi-camera RGB images (6 cameras at 2 Hz keyframes from nuScenes) as input, which a perception module encodes into a latent state s ∈ R^{B×Nt×D}. HD map geometry and dynamic-agent bounding boxes from the dataset annotations are used externally to compute rule-based collision and imitation rewards during training. The output is a sequence of n 2D waypoints (x, y displacements) representing the planned trajectory over 1 s, 2 s, and 3 s horizons; at inference only the IL actor's deterministic forward pass is used, so no additional latency is introduced relative to the baseline.

## Method

CoIRL-AD decouples imitation and reinforcement learning into two separate actors sharing a perception backbone and a latent world model, eliminating the gradient conflict that arises when both objectives are optimized in a single policy. The RL actor explores via GRPO-style group sampling (G trajectories per state) with a step-aware mechanism that stochastically samples only one action per trajectory while fixing the rest to their Gaussian modes, ensuring kinematically plausible trajectories. A latent world model predicts future states from sampled action sequences, enabling long-horizon advantage estimation through a dreaming critic with Z-score-normalized group advantages and a two-critic EMA trick for stability. A backward-planning (inverse-causality) attention mask conditions each action on current and future waypoint features, giving early actions richer context and benefiting RL exploration. A competitive mechanism periodically compares the two actors' accumulated reward scores and adaptively transfers parameters from the winner to the loser (soft interpolation or hard replacement), anchoring the RL actor to expert-like behavior and suppressing value overestimation from world-model errors.

## Results

On nuScenes (perception-free setting), CoIRL-AD achieves an average L2 error of 0.63 m and collision rate of 0.18%, yielding a combined L2·Col score of 0.11, compared to the LAW baseline's 0.66 m / 0.22% / 0.15. With temporal augmentation, CoIRL-AD further reduces L2·Col to 0.08, surpassing even the temporal-augmented LAW (0.09) and all perception-based methods. In cross-city generalization (Singapore→Boston), CoIRL-AD reduces L2·Col from 0.64 to 0.15 (a 77% improvement), and on long-tail collision scenarios it lowers L2·Col from 9.22 to 6.60.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary and most direct comparison is against LAW (Li et al., ICLR 2025), the same perception-free architecture without RL: CoIRL-AD reduces L2·Col from 0.15 to 0.11 (no temporal augmentation) and to 0.08 (with temporal augmentation), improving both L2 error and collision rate simultaneously. Against SSR (Li et al., ICLR 2025), another perception-free method, CoIRL-AD† achieves L2·Col of 0.08 versus SSR†'s 0.18, a more than 2× improvement. Among perception-based methods, CoIRL-AD† (0.08) outperforms PARA-Drive† (0.12), GenAD† (0.10), VAD† (0.16), and UniAD† (0.32) on the combined L2·Col metric, despite using no detection or mapping heads. In the cross-city generalization setting, the gap widens dramatically: LAW degrades to L2·Col 0.64 while CoIRL-AD maintains 0.15, indicating that the RL actor's exploration capability is most valuable under distribution shift. On long-tail collision scenarios, CoIRL-AD reduces L2·Col from 9.22 to 6.60, confirming that RL benefits are concentrated in under-represented, challenging driving cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LAW framework (Li et al., 2025) for latent world-model-based E2E driving and adapts GRPO's group-sampling advantage estimation (Shao et al., 2024) to trajectory exploration. It departs from prior IL+RL integration work (two-stage methods like AutoVLA and RAD, one-stage methods like ReCogDrive and BC-SAC) by introducing a dual-policy competitive architecture that decouples imitation and reward optimization into separate actors, avoiding the gradient conflicts that plague single-policy joint optimization. The use of a learned latent world model as a non-reactive simulator for offline RL distinguishes it from simulator-based approaches (CARLA, Urban Driver) and from pure offline RL methods like CQL, which the paper shows fail catastrophically on expert-dominated driving data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that offline RL without external simulators can meaningfully improve E2E driving, particularly in cross-city generalization (77% L2·Col reduction) and long-tail scenarios where expert demonstrations are sparse. The competitive mechanism's role in suppressing world-model-induced value overestimation is well-supported by ablations and training dynamics analysis, offering a practical recipe for stabilizing model-based RL in offline settings. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (nuScenes), a single base framework (LAW), non-reactive simulation, and a single cross-city pair; open questions include whether the competitive framework generalizes to reactive environments, larger-scale datasets, and whether the 2× training overhead is acceptable in practice.
