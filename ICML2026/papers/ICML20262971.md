## Problem

Discrete diffusion language models have split into two paradigms with complementary but unbalanced strengths: Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDLM) excel at semantic understanding and zero-shot generalization but produce incoherent outputs in few-step regimes, while Uniform-noise Diffusion Language Models (UDLM) achieve strong few-step generation quality but lag in likelihood modeling. No existing formulation principledly bridges these two noise structures, leaving a Pareto gap between understanding and generation that is especially costly in few-step inference settings like image synthesis.

## Input / Output

The model operates on a single categorical variable represented as a one-hot vector in {0,1}^N where N is the vocabulary size. During training, the input is a noisy latent state z_t (a single token drawn from the forward diffusion process) paired with the clean target x_0; during inference, the model iteratively denoises from a fully corrupted state over T ancestral sampling steps. For image generation, the discrete state space corresponds to ImageNet-1K patches with class-conditional guidance (CFG scale 2.0), and the output is a 256×256 RGB image. A single scalar mixing ratio k (default 0.1) controls the interpolation between uniform and absorbing noise throughout the entire diffusion trajectory.

## Method

XDLM defines the forward transition matrix as Q_{t|s} = α_{t|s} I + β_{t|s} K, where K is a stationary noise kernel decomposed as (k/N)J + μM — a convex combination of the all-ones uniform transition matrix and the absorbing mask matrix, with k + μ = 1. The critical design choice is enforcing stationarity of K (invariant across all timesteps), which decouples noise characteristics from the temporal schedule and enables an algebraic simplification: the posterior and KL divergence reduce to scalar expressions using only two helper primitives, r(e) = k/N + μ·δ_{e,m} and f_t(x,e) = α_t·p_{x,e} + β_t·r(e), avoiding explicit N×N matrix operations. This yields a memory-efficient training loss (Eq. 15) that scales to large vocabularies, requiring only 31.4 GB versus 59.7 GB for UDLM. The paper proves that setting k=0 recovers MDLM (pure masking, cross-entropy loss) and k=1 recovers UDLM (pure uniform noise), establishing XDLM as a strict generalization. Empirically, k=0.1 identifies a 'sweet spot' that breaks the Pareto frontier, surpassing UDLM by 5.4 points on zero-shot benchmarks while matching MDLM-level generation quality in multi-step regimes.

## Results

On zero-shot language benchmarks (7 datasets), XDLM achieves an average PPL of 54.11, trailing MDLM (53.65) by only 0.45 points while surpassing UDLM (59.57) by 5.4 points. On ImageNet-1K class-conditional generation without CFG, XDLM achieves FID 54.09 at 4 steps (vs. MDLM's 80.75) and the best FID of 25.77 at 16 steps. When adapted to the 8B-parameter LLaDA model via 600-step continual pretraining, LLaDA-XDLM reaches 15.0 MBPP in 32 sampling steps, more than doubling the vanilla LLaDA baseline (6.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MDLM, XDLM trades a small understanding gap (0.45 PPL points on average zero-shot) for a large generation advantage: FID drops from 80.75 to 54.09 at 4 steps on ImageNet-1K, and with CFG=2.0, XDLM achieves the best FID at both 4 steps (13.55 vs. 33.47) and 8 steps (8.96 vs. 11.14). Against UDLM, XDLM recovers the understanding deficit (54.11 vs. 59.57 average PPL, a 5.4-point improvement) while remaining competitive on generation, achieving the best FID at 16 steps (25.77 vs. 26.24) and the best FID at 4 and 8 steps with CFG. Against GIDD, XDLM is slightly worse on zero-shot PPL (54.11 vs. 53.38) but substantially better on few-step image generation (FID 54.09 vs. 86.84 at 4 steps) and more computationally efficient (396K vs. 200K tokens/s forward throughput, 31.4 GB vs. 40.9 GB memory). On the 8B LLM continual pretraining task, LLaDA-XDLM outperforms both the LLaDA-MDLM control (which actually degrades) and the LLaDA-XDLM-infer control (which only changes sampling), confirming the gains come from the formulation itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

XDLM unifies two established branches of discrete diffusion—Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDLM, Sahoo et al. 2024) and Uniform-noise Diffusion Language Models (UDLM, Schiff et al. 2024)—by introducing a stationary mixed noise kernel that recovers each as a limiting case (k=0 and k=1). The closest prior work, GIDD (von Rutte et al., 2025), also interpolates between masked and uniform noise but relies on time-inhomogeneous transition matrices that couple noise structure to the diffusion timeline; XDLM's key departure is enforcing stationarity, which decouples noise characteristics from scheduling and enables an algebraically simplified, memory-efficient scalar formulation. The framework is grounded in the general D3PM formulation (Austin et al., 2021) and draws on Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023) for the geometric consistency argument, while implementation builds on the Duo codebase (Sahoo et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By advancing the Pareto frontier between zero-shot understanding and few-step generation quality, XDLM offers a single model that avoids the practitioner's current dilemma of choosing between MDLM (better semantics, worse few-step samples) and UDLM (better few-step samples, weaker likelihood). The 8B-parameter LLaDA-XDLM result (15.0 MBPP vs. 6.8 baseline in 32 steps) suggests the formulation could serve as a drop-in upgrade for existing large diffusion LLMs with minimal additional compute. Open questions include whether XDLM trained from scratch at scale exhibits different emergent properties, whether the 'performance crossover' at very large step counts (approaching autoregressive decoding) can be fully characterized, and how the framework extends to unified multimodal settings and post-training/inference acceleration.
