## Problem

Critic-free RLHF methods (GRPO, RLOO, DAPO) broadcast a single trajectory-level reward uniformly across all tokens, forcing full-sequence backpropagation for every rollout. This incurs substantial compute and memory cost for long reasoning traces, even though empirical analysis shows that intermediate prefixes often already determine the final outcome with high confidence. The paper asks whether one can safely truncate the gradient update to a shorter prefix while preserving the full-sequence objective in expectation.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt x, a policy model π_θ, and a terminal reward function R(x,o), PS-PPO samples K completions per prompt, computes a prompt-conditioned cutoff distribution ξ_{1:T} from forward-pass activations and reward statistics, and then backpropagates only through the sampled prefix of each completion. The output is an updated policy model identical in form to standard PPO, but the update pass operates on a stochastically truncated prefix (expected length B tokens) rather than the full T-token completion, reducing both forward/backward FLOPs and peak activation memory.

## Method

PS-PPO formulates the choice of which prefix to backpropagate through as a budgeted convex optimization: minimize a timestep-wise variance surrogate of the truncated gradient estimator subject to an expected-prefix-length budget B and monotonicity constraints, solved in closed form via the PAV algorithm. The timestep weights in the objective are estimated using two forward-only proxies — an output-head score-norm γ_t (computed from hidden-state norm and log-sum-exp reductions) and a reward-uncertainty signal u_t(x) (an upper bound on Var(R|s_t) derived from state-averaged next-token distributions over successful vs. unsuccessful rollouts) — avoiding any additional backward passes. During the update, each retained token's gradient is reweighted by 1/ξ_t, an inclusion-probability correction that makes the truncated estimator unbiased for the full broadcast PPO update in expectation over cutoff sampling. The result is a drop-in replacement for the backpropagation stage of any critic-free PPO variant, requiring no auxiliary models or extra rollouts.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, PS-PPO (Optimized, B=128) achieves 85.0% on MATH500, 58.5% on AMC 2023, 30.1% on Minerva Math, 16.7% on AIME 2024, and 13.3% on AIME 2025, matching or exceeding DAPO on most benchmarks while reducing training time per step from 3.23s to 1.77s (45% reduction) and peak GPU memory by 15–17%. On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, PS-PPO reaches 47.6% MATH500 and 10.0% AIME 2024, outperforming DAPO (46.8%, 10.0%) on MATH500. At T_max=4096, PS-PPO achieves a 2.8× speedup over S-GRPO and 3.3× over DAPO in update-stage training time while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PS-PPO (Optimized) matches or slightly exceeds DAPO on most benchmarks: on Qwen2.5-Math-7B it ties DAPO on MATH500 (85.0 vs 85.2), AIME24 (16.7), and beats it on AMC23 (58.5 vs 57.5) and AIME25 (13.3 vs 10.0), while on Llama-3.1-8B it leads DAPO on MATH500 (47.6 vs 46.8). Against S-GRPO, PS-PPO consistently outperforms on accuracy (e.g., 85.0 vs 84.7 MATH500, 58.5 vs 56.7 AMC23 on Qwen) and is substantially faster (1.77s vs 2.66s per step; 2.8× speedup at T_max=4096). PS-PPO clearly surpasses GRPO, Dr.GRPO, and RLOO across nearly all benchmarks (e.g., 85.0 vs 83.9/83.5/83.6 on MATH500 for Qwen). Compared to DAPO with forking tokens, PS-PPO achieves comparable accuracy while reducing training time per step from 3.25s to 1.77s, because forking-token masking still executes full-sequence forward/backward passes. The key differentiator is that PS-PPO's stochastic prefix truncation directly reduces backpropagation cost, whereas masking-based baselines (S-GRPO, DAPO forking) only reduce loss-included tokens without cutting the dominant compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

PS-PPO builds directly on critic-free RLHF methods (GRPO, RLOO, DAPO, Dr.GRPO) that broadcast a single trajectory-level advantage across all tokens, and on token-selective RL approaches (S-GRPO, PPPO, high-entropy token masking) that attempt to reduce update cost by restricting gradients to a subset of positions. The key departure is that PS-PPO replaces fixed or heuristic token-selection rules with a prompt-conditioned cutoff distribution derived from a convex variance-minimization problem under a compute budget, and applies an inclusion-probability reweighting that provably recovers the full-sequence update in expectation. Unlike masking-based baselines that still execute full forward/backward passes, PS-PPO physically truncates backpropagation, yielding genuine compute and memory savings rather than only loss-level savings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 33–45% reduction in per-step training time and 15–17% peak-memory reduction, with accuracy matching or slightly exceeding DAPO, make PS-PPO a practical drop-in for long-reasoning RLHF pipelines where update-stage cost dominates. The scaling results (2.8×–3.3× speedup at T_max=4096) suggest the advantage grows precisely in the regime where long CoT traces are most expensive. Open questions include how the budget hyperparameter B should be set for new domains, whether the binary-reward uncertainty proxy u_t(x) transfers cleanly to multi-dimensional or dense reward settings, and whether the diagonal-trace variance surrogate remains tight when cross-timestep correlations are strong (e.g., in self-correcting traces). The method is also specific to critic-free settings and does not extend to actor-critic training.
