## Problem

DETR-based HD map construction methods treat each map instance as an independent object query, inheriting a local, spiky-distribution perspective that is ill-suited to the spatially continuous 'streak' structure of road elements. This independent optimization overlooks inter-instance spatial relationships and global structural dependencies, causing queries to converge to local optima and producing jagged, inconsistent curvature in predicted lane lines and boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263770/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 (nuScenes) or 7 (Argoverse 2) surround-view camera images, which are encoded by a 2D backbone (ResNet-50) and projected into a BEV feature map via camera-to-BEV transformation. It outputs vectorized map instances as ordered point sets P={(x_i, y_i)} with semantic class labels (lane divider, pedestrian crossing, road boundary) within a 30 m × 60 m perception window. The proposed GRL and GRG modules are inserted as a plug-and-play add-on into the existing transformer decoder of frameworks like MapTR, MapTRv2, or MapQR, requiring no changes to the backbone or overall pipeline.

## Method

MapGR introduces two synergistic modules applied to the first two layers of a six-layer transformer decoder. The Global Representation Learning (GRL) module projects each query into a small 2D spatial feature map via an MLP, concatenates all n query maps into a unified tensor, processes it with a lightweight CNN, and upsamples to BEV resolution to produce a holistic rasterized map prediction supervised by a multi-channel binary GT mask via BCE loss—thereby propagating gradients to all queries rather than only matched ones. The Global Representation Guidance (GRG) module then encodes this predicted global map into a compact embedding via an MLP and concatenates it with every individual query before a fusion MLP, injecting explicit global context into each local query's final prediction decoding. The GRL loss is weighted at 1.0 relative to 0.1 for the standard query prediction loss on the affected layers, and the modules add only 4–23% parameters and ≤1.2 ms latency.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set (24 epochs), MapGR integrated with MapQR achieves a state-of-the-art mAP₁ of 45.3% and mAP₂ of 68.1%, surpassing the MapQR baseline by +3.2% and +2.8% respectively. At 110 epochs, the same configuration reaches mAP₁ of 51.9% and mAP₂ of 73.1%. On Argoverse 2 (6 epochs), MapQR+MapGR attains mAP₁ of 42.9% and mAP₂ of 66.0%, outperforming all tested baselines. The method adds only ~1% latency overhead (≤1.2 ms) and 4–23% parameter growth depending on the base framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MapGR is evaluated as a plug-in module on three DETR-based baselines (MapTR, MapTRv2, MapQR) and compared against four additional SOTA methods (BeMapNet, StreamMapNet, PivotNet). On nuScenes at 24 epochs, MapQR+MapGR (mAP₁ 45.3%, mAP₂ 68.1%) outperforms BeMapNet (47.1% mAP₁ at 110 epochs, 64.8% mAP₂), StreamMapNet (36.2% mAP₁), and PivotNet (38.5% mAP₁) by a wide margin. Relative to its own baselines, MapGR consistently improves mAP₁ by +3.2 to +3.9% and mAP₂ by +2.8 to +4.2% at 24 epochs across all three frameworks. The gains are largest for MapTR (+4.2/+4.9% at 110 epochs) and smallest for MapQR at 110 epochs (+1.4/+0.5%), suggesting the benefit is most pronounced when the base model has more room for improvement. On Argoverse 2, improvements are more modest (+0.8 to +1.6% mAP₁) but still consistent across all baselines, with MapQR+MapGR reaching 42.9% mAP₁ and 66.0% mAP₂.

## Relation to Prior Work

MapGR extends the DETR-like query-based framework for online vectorized HD map construction (MapTR, MapTRv2, MapQR) by addressing what the authors identify as a fundamental mismatch: DETR's independent, locally-optimized queries are ill-suited to the spatially continuous 'streak' distributions inherent in HD maps, as opposed to the 'spiky' distributions of object detection. Rather than proposing a new end-to-end pipeline, the paper introduces two plug-and-play modules (GRL and GRG) that aggregate all queries into a global rasterized map prediction and then re-inject that global context back into individual queries, operating as a drop-in addition to existing decoder architectures. This positions the work as a targeted architectural refinement of the MapTR/MapQR lineage rather than a departure from it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design and consistent 2–5% mAP gains across three strong baselines (MapTR, MapTRv2, MapQR) on two datasets make this a practically useful addition for the HD map construction community, and the downstream motion-forecasting improvement (−5.8 cm minFDE) demonstrates that map-quality gains propagate to driving-relevant tasks. The mechanistic finding that global supervision is only beneficial at early decoder layers—because per-layer query displacement drops below the mask cell size—provides a clear, resolution-dependent rationale rather than a hand-tuned hyperparameter. Open questions include whether richer global representations (beyond a single 256-dim MLP encoding of a rasterized mask) could yield further gains, and whether the approach transfers to 3D or long-range map construction settings.
