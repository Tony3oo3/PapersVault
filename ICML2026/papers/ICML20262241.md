## Problem

Neural networks remain highly vulnerable to natural image corruptions (noise, weather, compression artifacts), yet the underlying cause of this fragility was unknown and no unified defense existed that handles multiple corruption types simultaneously. This paper identifies that natural corruptions collapse the internal feature space into a high-entropy state—opposite to the low-entropy feature dominance induced by adversarial perturbations—causing predictions to rely on a small subset of fragile features.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) and outputs class labels. During training, each clean input is augmented with a high-entropy perturbed variant generated via projected gradient ascent on the model's output entropy within a bounded ℓ∞ ball (radius ε), and a frozen teacher copy of the network provides soft logits for distillation. The framework is applied as a fine-tuning stage on pretrained classifiers, freezing early layers and updating higher blocks plus the classification head.

## Method

Dem-HEC is an entropy-guided fine-tuning framework built on the observation that natural corruptions increase layer-wise Shannon entropy in deep network layers, dispersing feature activations and amplifying uncertainty. For each training sample, it synthesizes a high-entropy counterpart by performing T steps of projected gradient ascent on the model's output entropy, constrained to an ℓ∞ ball of radius ε around the original image. The total loss combines four terms: (1) standard cross-entropy on clean images, (2) cross-entropy on the generated high-entropy samples, (3) a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss that aligns the penultimate-layer representations of clean and high-entropy inputs, and (4) knowledge distillation from a frozen teacher snapshot to prevent catastrophic forgetting on clean data. This composite objective encourages the network to maintain stable, balanced feature representations even when internal activations are maximally uncertain, effectively bridging the training–test distribution gap without requiring explicit corruption samples during training.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-C, Dem-HEC achieves dramatic corruption accuracy gains: RepVGG-A0 under Gaussian noise at severity 5 improves from 14.37% to 69.29% (a 380% relative increase), and Shot noise S5 rises from 19.30% to 71.40%. On Tiny-ImageNet-C with ViT-L, Snow corruption S5 improves from 57.83% to 62.98%, while ResNet-50 Frost S5 jumps from 21.58% to 31.46%. On full ImageNet-1K (5% class-balanced subset, ViT backbone), clean accuracy is preserved (81.09% → 81.05%) while all seven corruption types improve by 1–2 points. On DINOv3 (CIFAR-100), average corruption accuracy increases across all types, e.g., Gaussian from 26.85% to 29.84% and JPEG from 49.56% to 52.29%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dem-HEC substantially outperforms all eight augmentation-based baselines on Tiny-ImageNet-C (ViT backbone), with the largest margins on Weather corruptions (70.71% vs. 29.17% for DV+AP+JSD, the strongest baseline) and Noise (60.10% vs. 34.44%). Against PRIME on CIFAR-10-C (WideResNet-18-2), PRIME achieves a higher average corruption accuracy (83.58% vs. 79.23%) and clean accuracy (88.42% vs. 87.76%), but combining PRIME+Dem-HEC yields the best clean accuracy (91.41%) and a competitive average (81.67%), indicating the methods are complementary. Dem-HEC clearly beats DiffAug on both clean accuracy (87.76% vs. 84.14%) and average corruption accuracy (79.23% vs. 74.27%), and DiffAug+Dem-HEC further improves to 89.33% clean and 80.25% average. Against the multi-scale push-pull method of Ranabhat et al. (2025), Dem-HEC wins across all seven corruption types on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-20) while also improving clean accuracy. Overall, Dem-HEC is the strongest standalone method among augmentation-based approaches but is slightly behind PRIME on average corruption accuracy for the WideResNet-18-2 setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct conceptual contrast with Democratic Training (Sun et al., 2025), which defends against Universal Adversarial Perturbations by suppressing dominant (low-entropy) features; Dem-HEC inverts this logic by training on high-entropy samples to counteract the feature-space uncertainty that natural corruptions induce. The corruption benchmarking framework of Hendrycks & Dietterich (2019) provides the evaluation protocol, while the entropy measurement builds on information-theoretic analyses of neural network representations (Grandvalet & Bengio, 2004; Shwartz-Ziv & Tishby, 2022). Methodologically, the high-entropy sample generator via Projected Gradient Ascent is structurally analogous to adversarial training (PGD), but the optimization objective is entropy maximization rather than loss maximization, and the composite loss (dual cross-entropy + InfoNCE + knowledge distillation) positions the work at the intersection of contrastive learning and distillation-based fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entropy-based mechanistic explanation for corruption vulnerability—feature collapse into high-entropy states in deep layers—offers a diagnostic lens that could inform future robustness research beyond corruptions to other OOD shifts. As a plug-in fine-tuning procedure, Dem-HEC is practically attractive because it requires no architectural changes and works across CNNs, ViTs, and foundation models (DINOv3, CLIP), though the clean-accuracy trade-off on small datasets (2.5–4.4% drop) and the modest 1–2% gains on ImageNet-1K limit its appeal for deployment-critical settings. Open questions include whether the entropy mechanism generalizes to video, audio, or multimodal corruptions, whether the PGA-based sample generation can be replaced by cheaper stochastic approximations, and whether the approach composes with other robustness training objectives beyond the PRIME/DiffAug combinations tested here.
