## Problem

Existing Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (SMoE) architectures for multimodal learning suffer from two coupled failures: softmax-based routing produces sharp distributions that cause expert collapse, and conventional load-balance losses introduce gradient conflicts that make expert selection ambiguous. Additionally, prior missing-modality imputation methods rely on shared cross-modal representations that bias the router toward available-modality experts, further degrading gating reliability.

## Input / Output

The model accepts heterogeneous multimodal token sequences (clinical time series, ClinicalBERT-encoded text, ECG patches, DenseNet-encoded chest X-rays, audio, video, or text) where any subset of modalities may be absent. Missing modalities are replaced by a two-stage imputation before entering the shared expert pool, and the model outputs task-specific predictions: binary labels (48-hour in-hospital mortality, length-of-stay), 25-condition multi-label predictions, or 3-class sentiment scores. The hidden dimension is standardized to 128 across all experiments, with 8 experts (Top-2) for MIMIC datasets and 4 experts (Top-2) for CMU datasets.

## Method

ConfSMoE introduces two key mechanisms. First, ConfNet replaces softmax gating scores with token-level confidence scores: each expert has a one-layer linear head mapping the token embedding to a scalar, passed through sigmoid to yield a confidence in (0,1) that is supervised by MSE loss against the ground-truth task confidence, thereby decoupling routing from softmax-induced sharpness and eliminating the need for auxiliary load-balance losses. Second, a two-stage imputation handles missing modalities: pre-imputation samples n random instances from the corresponding modality pool to capture modality-specific structure, and post-imputation applies a sparse cross-attention (Top-T row-wise masking with sparsity hyperparameter B) where the pre-imputed modality serves as query and the concatenated Top-K expert outputs from available modalities serve as key/value, injecting instance-specific cross-modal features. Gradient analysis formalizes why softmax routing causes expert collapse (PSD Jacobian creates a rich-get-richer feedback loop) and why load-balance losses produce opposing gradients that manifest as a 'Sinusoidal Wave' selection pattern.

## Results

On MIMIC-IV (Experiment Setting I, natural missing modality), ConfSMoE-T achieves F1 of 49.18±0.22 and AUC of 85.24±0.10 on 48-IHM, 61.33±0.39 and 78.22±0.15 on LOS, and 28.67±0.33 and 74.56±0.19 on 25-PHE, outperforming all baselines. On MIMIC-III, improvements over the best baseline range from 1.81% to 3.91% in F1 and 0.92% to 1.22% in AUC. On the larger MIMIC-IV dataset, gains expand to 1.37%–4.12% in F1 and 1.96%–4.85% in AUC, demonstrating stronger robustness at scale. Under random modality dropout (Setting II) and asymmetric dropout (Setting III), ConfSMoE maintains the highest F1 and AUC across all missingness levels (0%–50%) and all modality combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConfSMoE-T outperforms all seven baselines (SMIL, ShaSpec, mmFormer, TF, LiMoE, FuseMoE-S/L, FlexMoE) on every task and metric across both MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV. The strongest competitor is LiMoE on MIMIC-IV 48-IHM (F1=43.76, AUC=82.97), which ConfSMoE-T surpasses by 5.42 F1 points and 2.27 AUC points; mmFormer is the best baseline on 25-PHE (F1=26.15, AUC=70.33), beaten by 2.52 F1 and 4.23 AUC points. FuseMoE with Laplacian gating (FuseMoE-L) and FlexMoE are the closest MoE-based competitors but still trail by 3–5 F1 points on 48-IHM. Under random modality dropout (Setting II), ConfSMoE-T leads at every missingness level from 0% to 50% on both CMU-MOSI and CMU-MOSEI, with the gap widening at higher missing rates (e.g., at 50% missing, ConfSMoE-T F1=44.34 vs. FlexMoE 41.95 on CMU-MOSI). In the asymmetric setting (Setting III), ConfSMoE maintains near-identical performance across all single- and dual-modality test combinations, while baselines like FuseMoE-S and TF degrade substantially when their trained modality combination is absent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SMoE-based multimodal fusion line (FuseMoE, FlexMoE, LiMoE) by addressing two intertwined problems that prior work treats separately: missing modality imputation and expert collapse. It departs from the standard softmax + auxiliary load-balance-loss paradigm by providing a gradient-level analysis showing that the load balance loss produces a conflicting gradient direction against the softmax routing term, and then replacing the softmax gating entirely with a task-confidence signal (ConfNet). The two-stage imputation (intra-modality pre-imputation followed by sparse cross-attention post-imputation) builds on the modality gap observation from contrastive learning (Liang et al., 2022) but reframes it as a design principle for imputation rather than a diagnostic of representation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient analysis formalizing why load balance loss conflicts with softmax routing is a useful theoretical contribution that explains the empirically observed 'Sinusoidal Wave' expert selection pattern and suggests that auxiliary balancing losses may be counterproductive rather than merely insufficient. The confidence-guided gating offers a practical alternative that avoids auxiliary losses, though it requires supervised signal at inference, limiting applicability to unsupervised or few-label settings (the contrastive variant they test is notably weaker). Open questions include whether the modality pool for pre-imputation scales to very large or streaming datasets, how the method behaves with more than four modalities, and whether the confidence signal can be learned without ground-truth labels in a way that matches supervised performance.
