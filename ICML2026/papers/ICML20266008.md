## Problem

While recent VQ methods (IBQ, SimVQ) have achieved near-complete codebook utilization, they overlook a second bottleneck: the effective dimensionality of the codebook embedding space. Through spectral analysis, the paper shows that vector quantization inherently suppresses low-variance components, causing the codebook to degenerate into a low-rank subspace (effective rank ~12 vs. ~37 for its continuous AE counterpart), which severely limits representational capacity and degrades reconstruction fidelity.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes RGB images at 128×128 or 256×256 resolution and produces discrete token sequences via nearest-neighbor lookup in a codebook of size K (up to 262,144) with embedding dimension D (128 or 256). For reconstruction, the decoder maps quantized tokens back to pixel space; for generation, a Llama-based autoregressive model (300M–1.1B parameters) consumes class labels and sequentially predicts tokens to synthesize 256×256 images. The regularization loss operates exclusively on the global codebook matrix C ∈ R^{K×D}, not on per-batch encoder or quantized features.

## Method

The paper proposes a codebook regularization loss L_cr that minimizes the squared off-diagonal elements of the codebook covariance matrix Cov(C) = C^T C, effectively decorrelating embedding dimensions. This implicit redundancy-reduction strategy revitalizes the suppressed low-variance spectral tail without imposing rigid thresholds on singular values, avoiding the hyperparameter sensitivity and spectral distortion of hard constraints like Spectrum Hinge. The loss is applied to the global codebook rather than batch-wise features to sidestep the optimization mismatch introduced by the Straight-Through Estimator, where batch-level gradients would shift the encoder manifold globally while only locally updating the codebook. The regularization is a plug-and-play module (weight λ=0.001) compatible with vanilla VQ-GAN, SimVQ, and IBQ training paradigms, consistently raising effective rank from ~8–10 to ~15–20 and improving rFID by 0.2–1.2 across configurations.

## Results

On ImageNet 128×128 reconstruction, the proposed codebook regularization (L_cr) consistently improves rFID across all VQ frameworks: VQGAN drops from 5.12 to 3.98, SimVQ from 1.99 to 1.55 (at K=262,144), and IBQ from 1.00 to 0.91 (at K=262,144, 256×256). Effective rank increases substantially in all cases (e.g., SimVQ ER from 9.3 to 16.4; IBQ ER from 13.8 to 21.8). For downstream autoregressive generation on ImageNet 256×256, IBQ-XXL+L_cr achieves a state-of-the-art gFID of 2.02 and IS of 284.22, outperforming the original IBQ-XL (FID 2.14) and all prior AR baselines including VAR-d20 (FID 2.57) and LlamaGen-XXL (FID 3.09).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed L_cr consistently outperforms all VQ tokenizer baselines on reconstruction: VQGAN rFID improves from 5.12→3.98 (K=16,384) and 3.74→3.06 (K=65,536); SimVQ improves from 1.99→1.55 (K=262,144); IBQ improves from 1.00→0.91 (K=262,144, 256×256). Against prior SOTA tokenizers, IBQ+L_cr (rFID 0.91) beats Open-MAGVIT2 (rFID 1.17) and LlamaGen (rFID 2.13) on the 256×256 setting. For autoregressive generation, IBQ-XXL+L_cr (FID 2.02, IS 284.22) surpasses VAR-d20 (FID 2.57), LlamaGen-XXL (FID 3.09), Open-MAGVIT2-L (FID 2.51), and MaskGIT (FID 6.18), and also beats diffusion-based DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27). In the regularization ablation (Table 6), L_cr applied to the global codebook (rFID 3.06) outperforms both Barlow Twins (rFID 3.27) and VICReg (rFID 3.21) on the same target, while batch-wise application of either SSL method causes catastrophic degradation (rFID 19–36) due to STE optimization mismatch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as addressing a gap left by recent VQ tokenization work (IBQ, SimVQ) that achieved near-complete codebook utilization but overlooked the effective dimensionality of the codebook embedding space. The proposed codebook regularization directly borrows the redundancy-reduction (off-diagonal covariance minimization) principle from Barlow Twins, but applies it to the global codebook matrix rather than batch-level features, sidestepping the optimization mismatch introduced by the Straight-Through Estimator. Conceptually, the paper departs from hard-constraint spectral regularization (e.g., Spectrum Hinge) by arguing that implicit decorrelation is both more stable and more faithful to the natural spectral distribution of continuous representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 100% codebook utilization does not imply full dimensional utilization reframes how practitioners should evaluate VQ tokenizers—effective rank and spectral tail coverage become as important as utilization rates. The method's simplicity (a single covariance penalty on the codebook, weight λ=0.001) and demonstrated compatibility across VQGAN, SimVQ, and IBQ make it an easy drop-in for existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the same spectral truncation phenomenon and fix generalize to non-image modalities (audio, video), to non-VQ discrete representations (FSQ, LFQ), and whether the gains persist at even larger scales or with different backbone architectures (ViT vs. CNN was only lightly explored).
