## Problem

While linear RNNs (LRNNs) have been shown to be theoretically expressive relative to transformers and practically performant, it remained unclear what makes LRNNs—but not traditional nonlinear RNNs—as easy to parallelize in practice as transformers. The paper addresses this gap by establishing a tight connection between RNN architecture types and standard circuit complexity classes, revealing fundamental barriers to parallelizing nonlinear RNNs and fine-grained expressivity differences among LRNN variants.

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis operates on token sequences over a finite vocabulary Σ with a semiring datatype (rational numbers ℚ), where precision is either logarithmic (O(log n) bits) or polynomial (O(n^c) bits) per state value. For empirical validation, inputs are structured as sorted deterministic graph connectivity instances (source node, sequence of directed edges in topologically sorted order, target node, all in unary) and iterated 3×3 matrix multiplication streams (9N tokens partitioned into N blocks of 9, each encoding a matrix over ℤ or ℤ_m). Outputs are binary accept/reject decisions for language recognition, the 9 entries of the product matrix, and complexity class membership characterizations for each architecture.

## Method

The paper maps each RNN class to a circuit complexity class: all LRNNs are upper-bounded by PNC¹ (log-depth bounded-fan-in arithmetic circuits), which simulates in O(log n log* n) boolean circuit depth—only a negligible O(log* n) overhead over transformers' O(log n). Nonlinear RNNs with polynomial precision are shown P-complete by simulating multi-stack Turing machines via ReLU MLP recurrences, while log-precision nonlinear RNNs are L-complete by solving sorted deterministic graph connectivity through counter-machine simulation. Within LRNNs, a conditional separation is established: DPLR variants (DeltaNet, RWKV-7) achieve PNC¹-completeness by solving iterated 3×3 matrix multiplication via a 4-layer router-plus-arithmetic construction using dot-product overwrite primitives, whereas permutation-diagonal LRNNs are confined to NC¹. Empirical validation on synthetic tasks (graph connectivity and matrix multiplication with length extrapolation) confirms the theoretical predictions, showing only nonlinear RNNs generalize on L-complete tasks while DPLR LRNNs match them on PNC¹-complete tasks.

## Results

On the L-complete Sorted Deterministic Graph Connectivity task, only the nonlinear RNN maintains near-perfect accuracy across all length ranges (1–100, 101–200, 201–300), while Transformer, RWKV-7, Mamba, and DeltaNet all exhibit substantial degradation under length extrapolation. On the PNC¹-complete Iterated 3×3 Matrix Multiplication task (both modular and unbounded), RWKV-7, the nonlinear RNN, and DeltaNet achieve near-perfect accuracy in-distribution and degrade only moderately out-of-distribution, whereas Transformer and Mamba perform poorly across all lengths. These empirical results confirm the theoretical predictions: nonlinear RNNs are needed for L-complete problems, while DPLR LRNNs (RWKV-7, DeltaNet) suffice for PNC¹-complete problems that are beyond Transformer and Mamba (TC⁰) capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The nonlinear RNN (Sarrof et al., 2024) is the only architecture that generalizes on the L-complete graph connectivity task, maintaining near-perfect accuracy from 100 to 300 nodes, confirming the theoretical L-completeness result. RWKV-7 (Peng et al., 2025) and DeltaNet (Yang et al., 2024), both DPLR LRNNs, match the nonlinear RNN on the PNC¹-complete iterated matrix multiplication tasks (both modular and unbounded) but fail to generalize on graph connectivity, consistent with their PNC¹ upper bound being strictly below L. Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2024) performs poorly on both tasks across all lengths, aligning with its TC⁰ expressivity limit that cannot capture either L-complete or PNC¹-complete structure. The Transformer baseline also fails on both extrapolation tasks, confirming that standard attention (TC⁰) is insufficient for these algebraic and connectivity problems. Overall, the experiments validate the paper's complexity-theoretic hierarchy: nonlinear RNNs ⊃ DPLR LRNNs (PNC¹) ⊃ PD LRNNs (NC¹) ⊃ Mamba/Transformer (TC⁰).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the circuit complexity analysis of neural sequence models initiated by Merrill & Sabharwal (2023) for transformers and Merrill et al. (2024) for simple LRNNs, by providing tight characterizations for nonlinear RNNs and distinguishing between LRNN variants (DPLR vs. PD). It builds on the automata-theoretic RNN analysis of Weiss et al. (2018) and Peng et al. (2018) by connecting counter machines and weighted finite automata to specific complexity classes, and leverages the parallel SCAN algorithm (Blelloch, 1990) to show LRNNs achieve near-log-depth parallelization. The Turing-completeness construction for poly-precision RNNs follows Siegelmann & Sontag (1995), while the L-completeness proof for log-precision RNNs introduces a novel sorted deterministic graph connectivity problem as a left-to-right-friendly L-complete separator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first comprehensive hierarchy connecting RNN architecture choices to circuit complexity classes, giving practitioners a principled framework for balancing expressivity against parallelizability when designing LLM architectures. The fine-grained separation between DPLR (PNC1-complete) and PD (NC1-complete) LRNNs has direct implications for choosing between DeltaNet/RWKV-7 and permutation-diagonal variants in hybrid transformer-LRNN models. Key open questions include whether PNC1 ≠ NC1 (which would make the DPLR/PD gap unconditional), whether the Ω(log²n) depth lower bound for nonlinear RNNs is truly tight, and whether the additional expressivity of nonlinear RNNs justifies the Θ(log n) parallelism overhead in practice. The synthetic benchmarks (graph connectivity, iterated matrix multiplication) offer new evaluation tools beyond the traditional state-tracking tasks.
