## Problem

Training deep SNNs with surrogate gradients suffers from two intertwined issues: the narrow effective window amplifies the Hessian by 1/β², driving convergence to sharp loss-landscape regions, and inconsistent gradient directions across timesteps (temporal gradient confusion) produce conflicting update signals. Existing adaptive surrogate methods focus on gradient sparsity or incur high computational cost, and none explicitly connect surrogate design to loss-landscape flatness and generalization.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet, 48×48 for CIFAR-10-DVS) or neuromorphic event streams, which are encoded as binary spike trains over T discrete timesteps through LIF neurons. It produces class labels for classification tasks or per-pixel segmentation masks (ADE20K). The surrogate gradient is applied only during the backward pass of spatio-temporal backpropagation, so the forward spike dynamics remain unchanged.

## Method

A²SG combines two mechanisms. First, a spatio-temporal adaptive policy (ST-ASG) dynamically adjusts the surrogate effective window β via Bayesian optimization: at the last timestep T it minimizes spatial gradient variation (SGV) to anchor a stable gradient direction, and at earlier timesteps it maximizes temporal gradient consistency (TGC, cosine similarity between adjacent-timestep gradients) to align all local gradients with that reference. Second, an asymmetric surrogate (ASY) replaces the symmetric boxcar/triangle with a linear ramp that assigns larger gradients to neurons with higher accumulated membrane potential, reflecting proximity to firing. The paper proves that under area and boundary constraints the symmetric CV-minimizer is the triangle, and that the asymmetric form achieves strictly lower CV when Lκ > σ² (verified empirically across layers). A theoretical link between local gradient CV and the largest FIM eigenvalue shows that reducing gradient variation directly flattens the loss landscape, explaining the generalization gains.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with VGG16 (T=4), A²SG achieves 95.29% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best prior method MPBN (94.44%) by 0.85%. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet19 (T=4), A²SG reaches 81.05%, surpassing MPD-AGL (79.72% at T=4, 80.49% at T=6) while using fewer timesteps. On ImageNet with E-SpikeFormer (173M, T×D=1×4), A²SG improves accuracy from 84.7% to 85.43% while maintaining comparable power (35.64 mJ vs. 35.6 mJ). On CIFAR-10-DVS, A²SG achieves 82.36% accuracy with only 4 timesteps, exceeding TMC (81.76%) and STBP-tdBN (81.30%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

A²SG outperforms all adaptive surrogate gradient baselines: it beats LSG (Lian et al., 2023) by 1.57% on CIFAR-10 (96.74% vs. 95.17%) and 4.20% on CIFAR-100 (81.05% vs. 76.85%) with ResNet19, and surpasses Dspike (Li et al., 2021) and CPNG (Lin et al., 2023) by 3.08% and 2.64% respectively on CIFAR-10. Against membrane-potential regularization methods (IM, RMP, MPBN from Guo et al.), A²SG consistently leads by 0.85–5.11% on CIFAR-10/100 with VGG16. On CIFAR-10-DVS, A²SG (82.36%) exceeds TMC (Yan et al., 2025) by 0.60%, STBP-tdBN (Zheng et al., 2021) by 1.06%, and HSD (Zhong et al., 2024) by 1.26%. On ImageNet, A²SG applied to E-SpikeFormer (Yao et al., 2025) improves accuracy from 78.5% to 78.61% (10M) and 84.7% to 85.43% (173M) while reducing power consumption. Compared to SAM (Foret et al., 2021) as a flat-minima strategy, A²SG achieves higher accuracy (95.29% vs. 94.87%) with fewer spikes (84.9k vs. 92.5k) and only ~15% training overhead versus SAM's ~90% overhead. A²SG also outperforms MPD-AGL (Jiang et al., 2025) on CIFAR-100 ResNet19 (81.05% at T=4 vs. 80.49% at T=6), achieving better accuracy with fewer timesteps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the surrogate gradient framework for spatio-temporal backpropagation (STBP) in deep SNNs, building directly on the foundational work of Wu et al. (2018) and Neftci et al. (2019). It departs from prior adaptive surrogate methods (Dspike, LSG, CPNG) that focused on gradient sparsity or incurred high computational overhead, instead introducing a unified framework that jointly addresses spatial gradient variation and temporal gradient confusion through adaptive window sizing via Bayesian optimization. The asymmetric surrogate design is novel in that it incorporates membrane potential dynamics into the gradient shape, and the theoretical link between coefficient of variation of local gradients and Fisher information matrix eigenvalues (loss landscape curvature) provides a principled justification absent from prior surrogate gradient work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a concrete theoretical bridge between surrogate gradient design choices and generalization performance via loss landscape flatness, giving practitioners a principled criterion for surrogate selection beyond empirical tuning. The finding that smoothness (e.g., Arctan) does not produce flatter minima while asymmetry does challenges the intuitive assumption that smoother surrogates should inherently improve convergence. Open questions acknowledged by the authors include formal convergence proofs for surrogate gradients and rigorous error bounds for the ASY surrogate as a function of the bias parameter h, which would strengthen the theoretical guarantees. The ~15% training overhead and the need for per-epoch Bayesian optimization, while manageable, may limit adoption in extremely resource-constrained neuromorphic deployment scenarios.
