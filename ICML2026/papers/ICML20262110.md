## Problem

Prior robustness studies treat vision architectures as monolithic families, leaving it unclear which specific micro-architectural and input-pipeline choices (stem kernel size, pooling type, normalization constants, resolution) actually drive differences in Gaussian-noise robustness. This paper asks: can we isolate and theoretically explain the individual design factors that make a vision model more or less sensitive to additive Gaussian noise?

## Input / Output

The evaluation protocol injects additive Gaussian noise at the sensor level—before any resize, crop, or normalization—mimicking real-world sensor readout noise. Models consume standard RGB images (typically 224×224 or 384×384) and produce class labels; robustness is quantified via a scale-free rank-difference metric computed across 1,174 pretrained timm models on ImageNet-1K, alongside noisy top-1 accuracy on five fine-grained datasets. The unusual aspect is that noise is applied pre-resize, so the effective noise level seen by the backbone depends on the model's native resolution and the deterministic downsampling in the pipeline.

## Method

The paper combines a large-scale empirical meta-analysis (pairwise comparisons across 1,174 timm models) with four theoretical results that explain each observed factor. (1) Theorem 4.1 proves that a low-pass stem kernel of size k attenuates per-pixel noise energy by O(k⁻²), so doubling patch size from 16 to 32 quarters the noise. (2) Theorem 4.3 shows anti-aliased downsampling by factor s yields an analogous s⁻² gain, explaining why smaller native resolutions appear more robust under the pre-resize noise protocol. (3) Theorem 5.1 derives exact MSE formulas for average and max pooling under i.i.d. Gaussian noise: average pooling is unbiased with variance σ²/k, while max pooling incurs a positive bias and MSE growing at most logarithmically in window size, plus a larger worst-case Lipschitz constant. (4) Theorem 6.1 bounds the end-to-end pixel-space Lipschitz constant by L_z/σ_min, showing that CLIP's smaller per-channel standard deviations (≈0.26 vs. 0.5 for INCEPTION) amplify worst-case sensitivity by a factor of ≈1.91, which is the primary cause of CLIP ViTs' vulnerability rather than their training recipe.

## Results

Across 1,174 pretrained timm vision models evaluated on ImageNet-1K with additive Gaussian noise (scale 0.2–0.44), the paper identifies four consistent robustness factors yielding up to 506 rank improvements and 21.6% top-1 accuracy gains. The largest single-model gain is vit_base_patch32_224.augreg_in1k (RankDiff = −506), while CLIP ViTs show positive RankDiff (+74 to +158), indicating relative degradation. In controlled ResNet-50 experiments on five fine-grained datasets, replacing max pooling with average pooling in the stem improves noisy test accuracy by 7–16 percentage points (e.g., 43.1% vs. 27.3% on FGVC-Aircraft), and ResNet variants using average-pool downsampling (T, D) outperform strided-convolution variants (C, S) by 3–20 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ResNet variants from He et al. (2019), the average-pooling types (ResNet-50-T, ResNet-50-D) consistently outperform the strided-convolution types (ResNet-50-C, ResNet-50-S) on noisy test accuracy across all five datasets: on FGVC-Aircraft, T achieves 30.4% vs. S's 5.5% (a 25-point gap), and on Stanford Cars, T reaches 55.0% vs. S's 28.5%. Against CLIP ViTs (Radford et al., 2021), supervised ViTs show substantially better robustness: all four supervised ViT-B/16 224² checkpoints have negative RankDiff (−65 to −375), while all four CLIP ViT-B/16 224² checkpoints have positive RankDiff (+74 to +158), a gap of 139–533 rank positions. Against AugReg-trained ViTs (Steiner et al., 2022), the patch-32/224 configuration (RankDiff −506) dominates the patch-16/224 configuration (−375) by 131 rank positions, and the 224² resolution consistently beats 384² under the pre-resize noise protocol (e.g., −375 vs. −152 for patch-16 augreg_in1k). Against ResNet-AA (Zhang, 2019), which applies average pooling in all downsampling layers, the improvement over standard ResNet-50 with average pooling only in the stem is marginal (e.g., 58.9% vs. 53.6% on Oxford-IIIT Pet test noisy accuracy), confirming that the stem pooling choice is the dominant factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends three distinct theoretical lines—anti-aliasing and shift-invariance analysis (Zhang 2019; Qian et al. 2021), pooling theory under noise (Boureau et al. 2010), and Lipschitz-based robustness bounds (Virmaux & Scaman 2018; Gouk et al. 2021)—into a unified, Gaussian-noise-specific framework with explicit scaling laws. It departs from prior robustness-to-corruption studies (Hendrycks & Dietterich 2019; Paul & Chen 2022; Bai et al. 2021) that treated architectures as monolithic families or mixed multiple corruption types, by systematically isolating four micro-architectural and pipeline choices (stem kernel size, pre-resize downsampling, pooling type, normalization constants) across 1,174 timm models with pairwise controlled comparisons. The theoretical contributions (quadratic noise attenuation with kernel size, s^{-2} downsampling gain, unbiasedness of average pooling, 1/σ_min Lipschitz scaling) provide causal explanations for trends that prior empirical work observed but did not mechanistically account for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically impactful finding is that CLIP ViTs' vulnerability to Gaussian noise is primarily attributable to their OPENAI mean-std normalization constants rather than to contrastive training itself; simply swapping to INCEPTION constants recovers most of the robustness gap across all tested CLIP variants and datasets, offering a zero-cost, plug-and-play fix for deployed models. The paper also corrects a potential misinterpretation of the resolution effect: counterfactual controls show the apparent low-resolution advantage is a pipeline downsampling artifact, not intrinsic backbone robustness, which matters for practitioners choosing evaluation protocols. Limitations include the exclusive focus on additive Gaussian noise (no adversarial, weather, or sensor-specific corruptions), reliance on timm pretrained models that may not generalize to medical imaging or video, and the fact that the resolution benefit has a checkpoint-dependent boundary beyond which aggressive downsampling loses task-relevant information. Open questions include whether these four factors interact in non-additive ways, whether the normalization insight extends to other corruption types, and whether the quadratic scaling laws hold for non-Gaussian or structured noise.
