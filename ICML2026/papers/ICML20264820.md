## Problem

Existing diffusion-based HOI generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: single-stream models entangle human and object states, producing semantically diverse but spatially misaligned motions (floating, penetration), while rigid guidance-based approaches enforce proximity at the cost of robotic stiffness. The paper identifies the root cause as 'Geometric Forgetting'—a phenomenon where, as diffusion transformer depth increases, high-level semantic features progressively overshadow fine-grained object geometry cues, diluting the precise spatial information needed for valid contact.

## Input / Output

The model takes three conditioning signals: a text prompt encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder, object geometry represented as a Basis Point Set (1024 fixed points in a unit sphere with directional vectors to nearest surface neighbors, compressed via MLP to a single 256-dim vector broadcast across all T frames), and sparse object waypoints providing 2D (x,y) positions every 30 frames plus a final 3D position, with unconstrained frames zero-padded. The output is a joint human-object motion sequence: 204-dim per-frame human state (24 joint positions + 22×6D rotations parameterizing SMPL-X) and 12-dim per-frame object state (3D centroid + flattened 3×3 relative rotation), generated over 1000 diffusion steps.

## Method

MaMi-HOI augments a 4-layer Diffusion Transformer backbone with two complementary adapters that decouple macro-dynamics from micro-geometry. The Geometry-Aware Proximity Adapter (GAPA) acts as a terminal module using Distance-Aware Cross-Attention: it projects motion and BPS features into a shared coordinate space, computes pairwise difference vectors, injects squared Euclidean distance as a negative bias in the attention score, and fuses relative spatial vectors into the value aggregation, producing a residual snapping correction (F_refined = F_mot + ΔF_geo) that snaps end-effectors onto the object surface without destroying semantic structure. The Kinematic Harmony Adapter (KHA) is a parallel trainable copy of the main DiT, conditioned on sparse waypoints and timestep, whose outputs are injected into corresponding main-branch layers via zero-initialized linear projections, allowing it to proactively align whole-body posture with spatial objectives while preserving the intrinsic motion manifold. The two adapters establish a coarse-to-fine synergy: KHA sets a kinematically plausible global posture, minimizing the deformation burden for GAPA's terminal contact refinement. Training uses standard DDPM noise-prediction MSE loss with Adam (lr=2×10⁻⁴) for 400k steps at batch size 32 on a single GPU.

## Results

On the FullBodyManipulation dataset, MaMi-HOI achieves the best condition matching (Ts=1.53 cm, Te=4.51 cm, Txy=2.14 cm), the lowest FID (0.39) and highest R-precision (0.71) among all baselines, and the highest contact F1 (0.73) with the lowest hand-object penetration (0.57 cm). On the 3D-FUTURE generalization benchmark, MaMi-HOI maintains the lowest waypoint errors (Ts=3.43, Te=5.87, Txy=2.21) and best FID (1.57) while achieving a 37% reduction in hand-to-object surface distance (6.05 mm vs. 9.59 mm for CHOIS). For long-term synthesis, MaMi-HOI reduces end-point trajectory error by over 50% compared to CHOIS (Te: 4.04 vs. 9.94 cm) while sustaining a 79% contact percentage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaMi-HOI outperforms the two strongest recent baselines, CHOIS and HOI-Dyn, across nearly all metrics. Against CHOIS, MaMi-HOI reduces FID from 0.69 to 0.39, improves contact F1 from 0.67 to 0.73, lowers hand penetration from 0.59 to 0.57 cm, and cuts object translation error from 12.53 to 9.98 cm. Against HOI-Dyn, MaMi-HOI achieves a lower FID (0.39 vs. 0.48), higher contact F1 (0.73 vs. 0.71), and substantially lower penetration (0.57 vs. 0.64 cm), while also improving MPJPE (13.50 vs. 15.60). Against the older OMOMO variants (Lin/Pred/GT), MaMi-HOI matches or exceeds their waypoint adherence while delivering far superior motion quality (FID 0.39 vs. 4.19–15.33) and interaction fidelity. On the 3D-FUTURE generalization test, MaMi-HOI achieves the best FID (1.57) and R-precision (0.69), though its penetration score (0.23) is marginally higher than CHOIS (0.19); the authors attribute this to MaMi-HOI actively engaging unseen surfaces (reducing hand-to-object distance by 37%) rather than avoiding contact. Overall, MaMi-HOI is the only method that simultaneously leads in condition matching, motion naturalness, and interaction quality without trade-offs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion-based HOI generation line (CHOIS, HOI-Dyn, OMOMO) but departs from the single-stream paradigm by diagnosing a specific failure mode—Geometric Forgetting, where deep transformer layers lose sensitivity to object geometry as semantic features dominate. Rather than adding external loss terms (as in ROG, CONTHO) or rigid guidance (as in waypoint methods), it introduces a decoupled dual-adapter architecture: GAPA hard-wires distance-aware cross-attention (inspired by Point Transformer) to re-inject BPS geometry, while KHA uses a zero-initialized trainable copy to preserve kinematic coherence. The macro-micro decoupling strategy is conceptually distinct from prior joint-generation or constraint-guided approaches, which attempt to balance both objectives within a single network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Geometric Forgetting diagnosis provides a useful mechanistic explanation for why existing diffusion-based HOI methods struggle with contact precision, potentially guiding future architectural design beyond this specific task. The dual-adapter pattern (explicit geometric retrieval + implicit kinematic harmonization) is a practical recipe that could transfer to other constrained generation problems. However, limitations remain: reliance on SMPL-X's coarse topology precludes finger-level articulation, the 3D-FUTURE generalization shows a penetration-vs-contact trade-off on unseen shapes, and the 3D scene application is demonstrated only qualitatively with a heuristic success metric rather than a rigorous benchmark. Open questions include whether the geometric forgetting phenomenon persists in much deeper or larger diffusion backbones, and whether the approach scales to multi-agent or articulated-object settings.
