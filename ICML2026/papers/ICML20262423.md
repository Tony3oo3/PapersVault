## Problem

Federated learning across multiple HPC facilities is dominated by stochastic batch-scheduler admission delays (minutes to hours) that are unpredictable and time-varying. Synchronous protocols like FedAvg suffer severe straggler effects, while fully asynchronous protocols accumulate heavy-tailed staleness, and existing system-aware methods profile static throughput without modeling or controlling scheduler-driven queue dynamics. No prior FL protocol explicitly predicts admission delays online and uses them to enforce bounded staleness with provable convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The server consumes the current global model, per-facility EWMA queue-delay predictions updated from observed arrival times, profiled per-facility throughputs (steps/second), and system parameters (synchronization horizon T_sync, safety buffer δ, staleness decay β). It produces updated global model parameters along with per-client job-time budgets J_k, local step budgets E_k, and adaptive learning rates η_k broadcast to each facility. Unusually, the server advances on a fixed wall-clock timer independent of which updates have arrived, and late updates are buffered rather than discarded, entering the next aggregation round with an explicit staleness penalty.

## Method

FEDQUEUE treats scheduler admission delay as a first-class training signal through three coupled mechanisms: (i) online EWMA queue-delay prediction drives adaptive work budgeting where each client's job-time budget is J_k = T_sync − q̂_k − δ and local steps E_k = ⌊c_k·J_k⌋, so clients facing longer predicted queues receive smaller compute allocations; (ii) cutoff-based admission at time (r+1)·T_sync aggregates all updates arriving by the deadline and buffers late arrivals for the next round, provably bounding staleness to τ_max = ⌈1+γ⌉ with high probability under sub-Gaussian prediction errors; (iii) staleness-aware aggregation down-weights delayed updates via harmonic decay φ(τ)=(1+βτ)^{-1} while inverse learning-rate scaling η_k = η_base·E_min/E_k equalizes effective local displacement across heterogeneous step budgets. The paper proves O(1/√R) non-convex convergence where the bias term scales with L²τ_max², G², and σ², and validates the protocol on four production HPC facilities (Aurora, Polaris, Frontier, Perlmutter) fine-tuning LLaMA2-7B, achieving 20.5% lower final loss and up to 60% faster time-to-target accuracy under high queue variance.

## Results

In the real-world cross-facility deployment (LLaMA2-7B on SMolInstruct across 4 production HPC systems), FEDQUEUE-2 achieves a final test loss of 0.3658, the only method to reach below 0.4, and reaches the 0.6 loss threshold ~1.78× faster than FedAvg and ~1.36× faster than FedCompass. In controlled synthetic experiments (MNIST, CNN, high queue variance ρ=0.9), FEDQUEUE reduces time-to-95% accuracy to 154.7 s versus 245.6 s (FedAvg), 384.1 s (FedAsync), 235.1 s (FedBuff), and 254.4 s (FedCompass), while also saving up to 67% in model transfers and ~2.1× in local compute steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEDQUEUE outperforms all four baselines on final loss in the real-world deployment: 0.3658 (FEDQUEUE-2) vs. 0.4345 (FedCompass), 0.4549 (FedAvg), 0.4987 (FedBuff), and 0.5381 (FedAsync), and is the only method to reach loss below 0.4 within the 17,000 s budget. In the synthetic high-variance regime (ρ=0.9, MNIST), FEDQUEUE achieves the fastest time-to-95% accuracy (154.7 s), beating FedAvg by 37%, FedBuff by 35%, FedAsync by 60%, and FedCompass by 39%, while also using the fewest local steps (5,893 vs. 7,440–12,400). However, FedBuff (97.45%) and FedCompass (97.21%) attain slightly higher maximum accuracy than FEDQUEUE (96.62%) in the synthetic setting, though at the cost of 50–100% more wall-clock time and local compute. FedAsync converges quickly to early loss thresholds in the real-world run but plateaus at the worst final loss (0.5381), reflecting uncontrolled staleness accumulation. FedCompass shows strong early performance but degrades as queue conditions shift over the ~5-hour deployment window, whereas FEDQUEUE's online EWMA prediction adapts to non-stationary queue dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEDQUEUE sits at the intersection of asynchronous/buffered federated learning (FedAsync, FedBuff), system-aware FL scheduling (FedCompass), and HPC queue-time prediction. It departs from prior asynchronous FL by treating scheduler-induced admission delay not as an exogenous disturbance to be tolerated but as a predictable, controllable signal: the server forecasts per-facility queue waits online, budgets local work accordingly, and enforces bounded staleness via deadline-based admission rather than assuming it a priori. Unlike FedCompass, which profiles static throughput and allocates local steps, FEDQUEUE models the time-varying queue process explicitly and uses a cutoff mechanism to buffer (not discard) late arrivals, combining this with staleness-decay weighting and inverse learning-rate scaling into a single protocol with a non-convex convergence proof.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-facility HPC training for scientific foundation models is an increasingly important regime where no single site holds sufficient data or compute, and queue delays can dominate wall-clock time; FEDQUEUE provides the first queue-aware FL protocol with both convergence guarantees and validation on four production supercomputers (Aurora, Polaris, Frontier, Perlmutter). Key limitations include the small client count (K=4), the absence of explicit job-failure modeling (a failed job is indistinguishable from a late arrival), and the reliance on a lightweight EWMA predictor that may struggle with abrupt scheduler regime changes. Open directions include scaling to more facilities, substituting ML-based or scheduler-state-querying predictors (the analysis is prediction-agnostic), and integrating data-quality-aware aggregation.
