## Problem

Computing Shapley values for data contributors in T2I diffusion models faces a dual bottleneck: each coalition query requires expensive model retraining, and the non-linear, interaction-heavy utility functions demand prohibitively many subset evaluations for stable estimation. Existing gradient-based attribution methods (TRAK, influence functions) are anchored to training losses and fail to capture downstream properties like aesthetics or diversity, while prior Shapley-based approaches still incur significant per-subset training costs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained T2I diffusion model (DDPM-CFG, Stable Diffusion v1.5, or FLUX.1-DEV), a set of contributors defined by prompt-label metadata (e.g., 20 CIFAR classes, 258 artists, 100 brands), and a scalar utility metric computed over a collection of generated images (FID, mean aesthetic score, LPIPS, or diversity). It produces one Shapley attribution score per contributor, with the utility evaluated as a global distributional property over 10,000–150 generated samples per coalition rather than per individual output. Coalition indicators are encoded as binary vectors in {0,1}^n over the Boolean hypercube.

## Method

SurrogateSHAP has two complementary components. First, a training-free proxy game reformulates each coalition's output distribution as a mixture of the pretrained model's conditional generators (p_θ(x|ψ(y)) for y in the coalition's prompt set, weighted by empirical label priors), reducing subset evaluation from full retraining to inference-only mixture sampling; a theoretical bound (Proposition 4.2) shows the proxy error is controlled by the Lipschitz constant of the utility functional times the representation-space drift under a shared-noise coupling. Second, to handle the high variance of Shapley estimation under non-linear utilities, the method samples M coalitions via the Shapley kernel, fits a gradient-boosted tree (XGBoost) surrogate to the proxy utility values, and computes Shapley values analytically via Interventional TreeSHAP on the trained ensemble—avoiding explicit sparsity constraints and achieving exact satisfaction of the efficiency axiom conditional on the surrogate. The total attribution error decomposes into surrogate approximation error and the proxy-to-training gap, both of which are empirically validated across three diverse benchmarks.

## Results

On the Linear Datamodel Score (LDS) at α=0.5, SurrogateSHAP achieves 79.7% on CIFAR-20 Inception Score, 51.2% on CIFAR-20 FID, 39.0% on ArtBench aesthetic score, 32.4% on Fashion-Product diversity, and 42.6% on Fashion-Product LPIPS—substantially outperforming all baselines. The proxy game achieves 23.4×, 12.8×, and 2.9× speedups over full retraining on CIFAR-20, ArtBench, and Fashion-Product respectively, while maintaining NRMSE of 0.071, 0.124, and 0.265. On synthetic interaction-heavy games, SurrogateSHAP reaches ℓ₂ error of 0.010 at M=512 coalitions versus 0.085 for KernelSHAP, demonstrating superior sample efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SurrogateSHAP dominates all 13 baselines across every dataset and utility metric in Table 2. The strongest baseline, Leave-one-out (LOO, Koh & Liang 2017), achieves 48.3% LDS on CIFAR-20 IS and 34.8% on FID but collapses to near-zero or negative scores on ArtBench (−0.18%) and Fashion-Product (−3.88% diversity, −1.50% LPIPS), revealing its inability to capture group-level interactions. Among gradient-based methods, TRAK (Park et al. 2023) and DAS (Lin et al. 2024) reach only ~24% LDS on CIFAR-20 IS and turn negative on ArtBench aesthetic (−5.3% and −5.7% respectively), while D-TRAK (Zheng et al. 2023) and Journey-TRAK (Georgiev et al. 2023) perform worst, with Journey-TRAK reaching −34.4% on CIFAR-20 FID. Similarity-based baselines (CLIP similarity, gradient similarity, GMValuator) are highly inconsistent, frequently producing negative correlations. The closest Shapley-based competitor, sparsified fine-tuning (sFT, Lu et al. 2025), achieves 40.0% on CIFAR-20 IS but fails catastrophically on FID (−14.1%) and only reaches 18.85% on ArtBench—less than half of SurrogateSHAP's 39.0%. On synthetic interaction-heavy games, SurrogateSHAP outperforms both KernelSHAP (Lundberg & Lee 2017) and ProxySPEX (Butler et al. 2025) by 8.5× in ℓ₂ error at M=512, confirming that the tree-surrogate + TreeSHAP pipeline is the key differentiator for non-additive utility functions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Shapley-based contributor attribution framework of Lu et al. (2025), which introduced the training game formulation and a sparsified fine-tuning (sFT) proxy for diffusion models. SurrogateSHAP departs from sFT by eliminating all subset-specific training entirely, reinterpreting each coalition's output distribution as a mixture of the target model's existing conditional generators—a purely inference-based proxy. For Shapley estimation, it builds on the tree-surrogate approach of Witter et al. (2026) and the exact TreeSHAP algorithm of Lundberg et al. (2020), but applies them to a GBT fitted on proxy-game utilities rather than raw model outputs, and avoids the explicit interaction-order sparsification used by ProxySPEX (Butler et al., 2025). The Shapley kernel sampling follows Lundberg & Lee (2017), and the broader data-attribution landscape (TRAK, D-TRAK, DAS, influence functions) is positioned as the individual-data paradigm that this group-level method supersedes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing per-coalition cost from hours of training to minutes of inference (23× speedup on CIFAR-20, 12.8× on ArtBench), SurrogateSHAP makes Shapley-based attribution feasible for billion-parameter T2I models like FLUX, opening the door to practical data-marketplace compensation and safety auditing. The clinical case study—localizing a spurious sex-melanoma correlation to a single hospital's hair artifacts—demonstrates a concrete safety-auditing use case beyond academic benchmarks. Key limitations include the one-to-one contributor-to-label mapping (incompatible with overlapping prompt spaces), metric-specificity of scores (optimizing for FID may prune minority-representative data), and the stability assumption (Assumption 4.1) that may not hold for very small or highly atypical coalitions. Open questions include extending the framework to multi-objective attribution, handling overlapping contributor data, and validating the proxy bound under distribution shift.
