## Problem

Existing KV cache eviction methods for long-text reasoning generation rely on static heuristics (attention scores, positions, redundancy) or one-time importance judgments, failing to capture the dynamic, semantic-dependent attention patterns that shift across reasoning blocks. This leads to suboptimal evictions that disproportionately increase loss on low-entropy tokens—numbers, symbols, and entities—causing factual errors that compound and distort subsequent reasoning steps.

## Input / Output

The lightweight MLP scorer takes as input the concatenation of each KV pair's key vector, value vector, and a 6g-dimensional attention feature vector (aggregated from recent windows of sizes 8/16/32 plus cumulative history with 0.9 decay, concatenated across GQA heads in a group), producing a single scalar importance score per KV pair. Eviction decisions are made via Top-K multinomial sampling: the 2L lowest-scoring pairs form a candidate set, from which L pairs are sampled using softmax over negated scores, balancing stability with exploration. The LLM backbone remains fully frozen; only the per-layer, per-attention-group scoring MLPs are updated.

## Method

ForesightKV employs a two-stage training paradigm. In supervised training, the Golden Eviction algorithm computes future scores for each KV pair by taking the maximum pooled attention score across all future query blocks (pooled over query dimension and attention groups), then retains the top-B−L pairs by these scores; the scoring model is trained to reproduce this ranking via a Pairwise Ranking Loss. A theoretical analysis (Proposition A.1) shows the attention-output error is bounded by 2Cε_t where ε_t is the evicted attention mass, and the future-score surrogate upper-bounds cumulative future evicted mass, justifying the objective. In the RL stage, eviction is formulated as an MDP and optimized with GRPO, where the sequence-level reward is the negative MSE of loss increases on low-entropy tokens whose loss spike exceeds a threshold η=1.5, explicitly targeting the tokens most vulnerable to eviction-induced factual errors. The Top-K multinomial sampling strategy provides controlled stochasticity that facilitates RL exploration while preventing catastrophic eviction of high-importance pairs.

## Results

On AIME2024 with Qwen3-4B, ForesightKV achieves 54.5 (1K budget) and 70.2 (2K budget), outperforming R-KV at 2K (44.8) and approaching the full model's performance at 4K (69.2). On AIME2025, ForesightKV reaches 42.3 at 1K budget. In terms of efficiency, ForesightKV with a 1K KV budget achieves up to 9.79× throughput improvement over the full KV cache at 32K generation length, while maintaining a fixed maximum concurrent batch size of 96 regardless of sequence length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ForesightKV consistently outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the three primary training-free eviction methods, the gap is substantial: on GPQA at 1K budget, ForesightKV (45.2) beats R-KV (23.0), H2O (25.2), and SnapKV (16.9) by 20–28 points; on LiveCodeBench at 1K, it leads R-KV (34.4) by 21.3 points. On AIME2024, ForesightKV at 1K (54.5) surpasses R-KV at 2K (44.8), meaning it matches or exceeds a method with double the cache budget. Against the more recent baselines in Table 17, ForesightKV (44.6 at 1K on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) outperforms G-KV (33.0–35.0) and DuoAttention (3.3) by wide margins, and on Qwen3-4B it beats RPC (10.0 at 1K) by 44.5 points. The supervised-only variant (w/o RL) already captures most of the gain, with RL providing a consistent 1–2 point additional improvement, confirming both stages contribute synergistically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of training-free KV cache eviction (H2O, SnapKV, R-KV), which relies on static heuristics like cumulative attention scores or window-based importance, and from one-time trainable eviction methods (Lancucki et al., 2025) that fail to capture dynamic importance shifts. It repurposes the GRPO reinforcement learning framework—originally designed for LLM reasoning—into the KV eviction setting by formulating eviction as a Markov Decision Process, and introduces the Golden Eviction algorithm as a principled supervised label generator grounded in a theoretical error bound (Proposition A.1). The Top-K multinomial sampling action parameterization is a novel design choice that balances the stability of greedy eviction with the exploration needed for RL training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache memory is the primary bottleneck for long-context reasoning deployment, and this paper demonstrates that a lightweight trained scorer (no LLM parameter updates) can preserve 92–99% of full-model performance at half the cache budget while delivering up to 9.8× throughput gains at 32K generation lengths. The RL formulation opens a promising direction for adaptive, sequence-level cache management that goes beyond per-step heuristics. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-turn or agentic settings where the cache is repeatedly read and written, whether the scoring model can be further distilled for edge deployment, and whether the math-reasoning-centric training data limits generalization to domains with fundamentally different attention structures.
