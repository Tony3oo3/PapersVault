## Problem

Standard conformal prediction provides finite-sample coverage guarantees only under exchangeability between calibration and test data, an assumption that is routinely violated when LLMs are deployed in domains different from their calibration set. Under domain shift, standard CP systematically under-covers, producing prediction sets too narrow to contain the correct answer. Moreover, the high-dimensional, unstructured nature of LLM prompts makes direct density-ratio estimation infeasible, so existing weighted or non-exchangeable CP methods either require intractable estimators or rely on heuristic weights without theoretical guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source-domain calibration pairs (prompt, correct answer), a batch of unlabeled target-domain prompts, a pre-trained LLM (for extracting answer logits), and a sentence-embedding model (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, 384-dim). It produces a single shared conformal threshold q̂_{1−α} computed once per target batch, and a prediction set Ĉ_n(x) ⊆ {A,…,F} for each test prompt x, defined as all answers whose nonconformity score falls below the threshold. The transductive requirement for unlabeled target prompts is used solely to fit the domain classifier for density-ratio estimation; no target labels are needed.

## Method

DS-CP projects prompts into a 384-dimensional semantic embedding space via a sentence transformer, then trains an XGBoost domain classifier on the embedded calibration and target prompts to estimate the density ratio r̃(z) = dP'_Z/dP_Z(z). Calibration scores are reweighted by their estimated density ratios, and a regularization step replaces the test-point weight with λ = max_i ŵ_i (a calibration-data-only quantity), yielding a shared empirical score distribution that avoids degenerating to the full output space under large shift. The resulting prediction set uses the (1−α)-quantile of this weighted distribution as a common threshold across the target batch. Theoretically, the paper establishes finite-sample lower and upper coverage bounds parameterized by conditional total-variation distances between score distributions, which under a Lipschitz smoothness condition in embedding space reduce to weighted distances between calibration and test embeddings; under exchangeability all discrepancy terms vanish and DS-CP recovers standard CP exactly.

## Results

On the MMLU benchmark (17 subjects, 272 ordered calibration–test subject pairs, α=0.10 target 90% coverage), DS-CP is evaluated against standard CP across 16 open-source LLMs from 9 model families. Standard CP fails to maintain the 90% target for every model: for several models the median empirical coverage across subject pairs falls below 90%, and many individual pairs drop well below target. DS-CP raises the median coverage above 90% for all models and substantially reduces the fraction of subject pairs below the 90% threshold, while increasing average prediction set size only modestly. The method is most effective under severe domain shift, where standard CP under-covers most dramatically.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole experimental baseline is standard (vanilla) conformal prediction, as applied to LLM multiple-choice question answering following Kumar et al. (2023) and Ye et al. (2024). DS-CP consistently outperforms standard CP on empirical coverage across all 16 evaluated LLMs and all 272 subject pairs: the median coverage is higher for DS-CP in every model, and the lower tail of the coverage distribution (pairs below the 90% target) is substantially reduced. The trade-off is a modest increase in average prediction set size, which is necessary to restore valid coverage under domain shift. The paired scatter plot (Figure 3) confirms that DS-CP is adaptive rather than uniformly conservative: it corrects under-coverage most aggressively where standard CP fails (orange points above the diagonal) while making only minimal adjustments where standard CP already achieves valid coverage (blue points near the diagonal). No other domain-shift-aware conformal baselines are included in the experiments, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two threads of conformal prediction literature—weighted CP under covariate shift (Tibshirani et al., 2019) and non-exchangeable CP (Barber et al., 2023)—and adapts them to the LLM setting where raw prompt-space density ratios are infeasible to estimate. The key methodological departure is projecting prompts into a semantic embedding space via a sentence transformer, performing density-ratio estimation there, and introducing a regularization step that replaces the test-point weight with a calibration-data-dependent constant λ to prevent prediction-set degeneration. Unlike Ulmer et al. (2024), which applies non-exchangeable CP with heuristic similarity weights and no coverage guarantees, and Cherian et al. (2024), which requires an explicitly specified shift mechanism, DS-CP provides a systematic data-dependent weighting scheme with finite-sample coverage bounds that recover standard CP under exchangeability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: LLMs deployed in evolving environments will inevitably face domain shift, and standard CP's exchangeability assumption silently fails, producing unreliable (under-covering) prediction sets. The framework offers a principled, theoretically grounded remedy that is computationally lightweight (offline embedding + classifier, then a shared threshold), making it deployable. Key limitations include the requirement for unlabeled target-domain prompts, dependence on embedding and classifier quality, and evaluation restricted to multiple-choice QA on MMLU; extending to open-ended generation, streaming/online settings, and temporal shift remain open.
