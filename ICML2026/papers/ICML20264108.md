## Problem

Existing stereo video conversion methods either process frames independently (losing temporal coherence) or rely on explicit disparity estimation that propagates errors into the generated right view. The field also lacks a large-scale, publicly available stereo video dataset for training and benchmarking, with prior datasets being either synthetic, domain-specific (autonomous driving), or restricted by intellectual property.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264108/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a left-view video sequence as input, processing 8-frame clips (with the last 2 frames serving as reference) at 384×384 resolution (randomly cropped from 422×422 resized frames). It outputs a corresponding right-view video sequence that, paired with the input, forms a stereo video. Training uses 1920×1080 side-by-side stereo videos from the YouTube-SBS dataset (423 videos, 7M+ stereo pairs), and evaluation is performed on 43 held-out video sequences totaling 558K frames.

## Method

ImmersePro uses a dual-branch architecture where a pretrained DepthAnything model extracts multi-scale disparity features and a convolutional encoder captures contextual texture features, both refined by sparse spatial-temporal self-attention (adapted from ProPainter with temporal stride 2). The key insight is implicit disparity guidance: instead of predicting an explicit disparity map, the network outputs a probability distribution over candidate disparity values at each pixel, which is used as auxiliary supervision to encourage correct pixel selection without committing to a single disparity estimate. A layered disparity module then generates 7 stacked disparity maps via differentiable warping, with a mask-based selection algorithm (Algorithm 1) that assigns each pixel to exactly one layer to avoid blending artifacts and black holes. A final context fusion module concatenates the warped output with enriched semantic features through spatial-temporal cross-attention, applying a median blur and residual connection to balance sharp textures with structural consistency. Training uses L1 + LPIPS loss with AdamW (lr=3e-5) for 50K iterations on 4 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the YouTube-SBS test set (43 video sequences, 558K frames), ImmersePro achieves L1=0.0570, SSIM=0.6048, PSNR=21.83, and MET3R=5.29, outperforming all five baselines on every metric. Compared to the strongest competitor Stereo-from-mono, the improvements are 11.76% (L1), 6.39% (SSIM), and 5.10% (PSNR). In terms of temporal consistency, ImmersePro achieves the best FVD of 2.88 (vs. 3.71 for Stereo-from-mono) and runs at 5.61 FPS on a single A100 GPU, roughly 4.7× faster than Stereo-from-mono (1.20 FPS) and 41× faster than StereoDiffusion (0.14 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImmersePro outperforms all five baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor Stereo-from-mono (Watson et al., ECCV 2020), it reduces L1 from 0.0646 to 0.0570 (−11.76%), raises SSIM from 0.5685 to 0.6048 (+6.39%), and increases PSNR from 20.77 to 21.83 (+5.10%), while also improving MET3R from 6.90 to 5.29. SVG (Dai et al., ICLR 2025) is the second-closest method on SSIM (0.5865) but trails ImmersePro on L1 (0.0723 vs. 0.0570) and PSNR (19.69 vs. 21.83). StereoDiffusion (Wang et al., CVPR-W 2024) and 3D Photo (Shih et al., CVPR 2020) are substantially weaker, with L1 values of 0.0816 and 0.1069 respectively, and the paper attributes their artifacts to unstable latent-space inversion and inaccurate depth cues. Deep3D (Xie et al., ECCV 2016) is the weakest baseline by a wide margin (L1=0.2215, PSNR=11.90). In the user study (26 participants, Meta Quest 3), ImmersePro leads all four dimensions—Frame Quality (4.12), Temporal Coherence (4.23), Stereoscopic Effects (3.92), and Overall Conformity (4.23)—with the largest gaps over Stereo-from-mono in Temporal Coherence (+1.06) and Overall Conformity (+1.19).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D-to-3D video conversion line initiated by Deep3D (Xie et al., 2016) and Stereo-from-mono (Watson et al., 2020), but departs fundamentally from the explicit-disparity paradigm: instead of estimating a disparity map and warping, ImmersePro learns an implicit probability distribution over shifted pixel copies, guided by a pretrained DepthAnything backbone. The spatial-temporal attention modules are adapted from ProPainter's sparse video transformer (Zhou et al., 2023) and FuseFormer's soft split/composition (Liu et al., 2021), repurposed here for cross-frame disparity consistency rather than inpainting. The layered disparity representation generalizes the single-map warping of prior work into a stack of 7 candidate layers with a non-overlapping mask selection algorithm, addressing occlusion/disocclusion without the blending artifacts of summation-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The YouTube-SBS dataset (423 videos, 7M+ stereo pairs) is the first large-scale, publicly accessible stereo video benchmark, filling a gap left by proprietary movie datasets (Ranftl et al., 2022) and small synthetic sets (Sintel). The implicit-disparity formulation avoids error propagation from monocular depth estimators, and the 5.6 FPS inference speed makes real-time stereo conversion feasible. However, the method is limited to 'subtle' stereo effects (71% of dataset frames have <10% occluded area), cannot reproduce exact right views due to the underdetermined nature of the problem, and temporal consistency is confined to local 8-frame windows—long-range coherence and strong-stereo inpainting remain open challenges the authors flag for future work (e.g., NeRF-based inpainting, RollingDepth-style global alignment).
