## Problem

Learning-augmented online minimization algorithms have relied on event predictions (unstable under small instance changes) or primal/action predictions (fragile, as optimal primal solutions can change drastically under tiny perturbations). This paper demonstrates, for the first time, that predictions of optimal dual LP solutions are simultaneously stable, useful, and learnable for online minimization, providing a principled prediction interface that avoids the drawbacks of prior approaches.

## Input / Output

For laminar set cover, the algorithm receives a dual prediction vector ŷ over the ground set elements and a classical R-competitive online algorithm as a subroutine; it outputs a set cover (which sets to purchase) as elements arrive sequentially. For MTS/k-server, the algorithm receives a sequence of predicted dual functions ŵ_t over the state space (updated every 15 minutes in experiments) and outputs server positions at each time step. Prediction error is measured in L1 norm for laminar set cover and in the span seminorm of Bellman-operator discrepancies for MTS, with the latter being invariant to additive constants in the predicted functions.

## Method

For laminar set cover, the algorithm uses a two-tier purchasing strategy: when a requested element belongs to a set that is α-saturated by the dual prediction (i.e., the predicted dual mass on that set exceeds α·c_S), it buys the inclusion-wise maximal such set directly (Type 1); otherwise it delegates to the classical R-competitive algorithm (Type 2). The cost bound decomposes into Type 1 costs charged to the prediction's L1 mass (bounded by OPT/α + surplus) and Type 2 costs charged to the prediction's deficit via complementary slackness (bounded by R/(1-α)·deficit). For MTS, the algorithm is an A*-style greedy: at each step it moves the server to the state minimizing immediate movement plus service cost plus the predicted future cost ŵ_t(s), yielding a (1+η/OPT)-competitive guarantee where η is the total Bellman-operator discrepancy. Stability is established by showing that a greedy depth-first dual computation for laminar set cover is Lipschitz under instance perturbations, and that the Bellman operator's contraction in span seminorm bounds MTS prediction error by the cost-function perturbation.

## Results

On the Parking Permit Problem (153 years of Central Park weather data, T=365), the learning-augmented algorithm with dual predictions achieves a competitive ratio 1.8× lower than the best O(log K)-competitive randomized baseline and 4.4× lower than the O(K)-competitive deterministic baseline at K=9 permit types. On the k-server problem (Citi Bike Manhattan data, 10-point line metric, k=2 to 9 servers), the dual-prediction algorithm is nearly optimal (competitive ratio close to 1) for all values of k, while both WFA and DC degrade proportionally with k in line with their theoretical k-competitiveness guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the state-of-the-art PPP algorithms of Coester & Turoczy (2026), the learning-augmented algorithm consistently achieves the lowest competitive ratio, with the advantage growing as the number of permit types K increases (1.8× over randomized, 4.4× over deterministic at K=9) and as the discount factor f grows beyond ~1.5. For small discount factors (f ≤ 1.1), the deterministic baseline performs comparably or slightly better, since the cost penalty for conservative permit choices is minimal. On the k-server problem, the dual-prediction algorithm outperforms both the Work Function Algorithm (Chrobak & Larmore, 1998) and Double Coverage (Chrobak et al., 1990) across all tested values of k (2–9 servers). While WFA and DC are theoretically k-competitive and their empirical competitive ratios grow with k, the dual-prediction algorithm remains near-optimal (competitive ratio ≈ 1) regardless of k, demonstrating that the learned dual variables effectively capture the future request structure in the bike-sharing data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant prediction interfaces in learning-augmented online algorithms—event predictions (e.g., next-request times for caching) and action predictions (near-optimal primal solutions)—by advocating for predictions of the optimal dual LP solution. While dual variables have appeared in online maximization settings (AdWords shadow prices) and as learned weights in scheduling (Lattanzi et al., Cohen & Panigrahi), those weights correspond to auxiliary convex programs rather than the primal minimization LP itself. The MTS algorithm is a direct modification of the Work Function Algorithm, replacing the online-computed work function with a learned dual prediction, yielding an A*-search-like procedure. For laminar set cover, the paper extends the classical online primal-dual framework (Buchbinder & Naor) by using the dual as a prediction signal to decide when to purchase sets versus delegating to a classical competitive algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a general design principle—predict the dual—for learning-augmented online minimization, showing that dual predictions simultaneously satisfy stability (small instance perturbations cause small prediction changes), usefulness (small error implies near-optimal performance), and learnability (polynomial sample complexity). A key limitation is that the approach is formulation-dependent: for general (non-laminar) set cover, even perfect dual predictions of the standard LP do not improve over classical algorithms, and the MTS result relies on a specific LP formulation. Open questions include whether other LP formulations or problem classes admit useful dual predictions, and whether the stability-learnability-usefulness trinity extends to broader online optimization settings beyond MTS and laminar set cover.
