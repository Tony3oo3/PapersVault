## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking methods face a three-way tension: they predominantly support only model-level provenance without efficient user-level attribution, they degrade generation quality by directly modulating token logits, and they remain vulnerable to simple adversarial edits such as synonym substitution or paraphrasing. No prior framework simultaneously achieves personalized (model + user) tracing, high text quality, and strong robustness at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260088/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At embedding time, the method takes a text prompt (25 tokens from C4), a shared secret key k₀, and a model identifier; the LLM generates K=10 candidate sentences via beam search (temperature 0.7, top-p 0.9), and the system outputs a single watermarked sentence selected from the candidates. At detection time, a candidate text is parsed into N sentences, partitioned into model-identifying and user-identifying subsets via a hash-based layer selector, and the system outputs a joint attribution (model ID, user ID) or a null hypothesis acceptance. The watermark signal is a binary parity constraint on HMAC digests of semantic and syntactic features, requiring no modification of the model's parameters or token-level logits.

## Method

IPMark draws an analogy to IP address hierarchical addressing, structurally decoupling a model identifier (network segment) from a user identifier (host segment) so that detection proceeds in a coarse-to-fine two-stage lookup. For each generation step, beam search yields K semantically similar candidate sentences; a layer selector (hash of the sentence's semantic signature mod 2) determines whether the current sentence carries the model ID or the user ID. The core embedding mechanism is a dual-stream HMAC check: semantic features are extracted as salient subject-relation-object triples mapped to WordNet hypernyms, and syntactic features are obtained by pruning and linearizing the constituency parse tree; HMAC-SHA256 is computed over both representations using a key derived from k₀ concatenated with the selected ID. The optimal candidate is the one whose semantic and syntactic HMAC outputs both satisfy the target parity (≡1 mod 2), with ties broken by highest sentence log-probability, thereby preserving fluency. Detection scores each candidate identity by counting parity-satisfying sentences in the appropriate subset, and a hierarchical model-user repository (e.g., 100 groups × 1,000 users) reduces lookup from linear scan to two-stage retrieval, cutting latency from 72.7 s to 5.0 s at 100 k-user scale.

## Results

On Llama-3.2-3B with C4-generated text, IPMark achieves a near-perfect AUC of 0.999 under no attack and maintains AUC ≥ 0.995 under 15% synonym substitution, outperforming all token-level baselines. In terms of generation quality, IPMark records the lowest perplexity (2.779) among all watermarking methods, even slightly below the unwatermarked model (2.804), compared to KGW (3.733), Unigram (2.892), EXP (3.250), PF (8.056), and MorphMark (3.251). For detection efficiency, the hierarchical retrieval strategy reduces latency from 72.677 s (linear scan) to 5.020 s at 100K-user scale, and completes verification of 1M users in 5.928 s (~93% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KGW, Unigram, and EXP, IPMark consistently wins on no-attack and synonym-substitution settings (AUC 0.999 vs. 0.997/0.992/0.997) and on cross-lingual translation (0.840 vs. 0.835/0.821/0.815) and Dipper rewriting (0.773 vs. 0.764/0.587/0.732). However, IPMark trails PF and EXP on copy-paste mixing (0.871 vs. 0.941/0.983) and on translation/Dipper against PF (0.840/0.773 vs. 0.922/0.874), where PF's permutation-based design excels at high-entropy perturbations. Compared to MorphMark, IPMark leads on no-attack (0.999 vs. 0.995) and synonym-15% (0.995 vs. 0.990) but falls behind on translation (0.840 vs. 0.905). On generation quality, IPMark's PPL of 2.779 is the lowest across all methods, beating the strongest quality baseline Unigram (2.892) by 3.9% and the robustness-focused PF (8.056) by a wide margin, confirming that the sentence-level candidate-selection strategy avoids the perplexity inflation typical of token-level logit modulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

IPMark departs from the dominant token-level watermarking paradigm (KGW, Unigram, EXP, PF, MorphMark) by operating at the sentence level, where candidate sentences are selected via beam search and filtered through HMAC-based semantic and syntactic checks. Conceptually, it repurposes the hierarchical addressing logic of IP networks—separating a 'network segment' (model ID) from a 'host address' (user ID)—to structure both embedding and detection, a framing not present in prior personalized watermarking work such as PersonaMark. The semantic-syntactic dual-stream design draws on dependency parsing and WordNet hypernym lookup (Dozat & Manning, 2016; Miller, 1995) to create perturbation-resistant signals, extending earlier sentence-level approaches (Dabiriaghdam & Wang, 2025; Lin et al., 2025) that lacked user-level granularity or hierarchical retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving the lowest perplexity among all baselines (2.779, marginally below the unwatermarked model) while retaining AUC > 0.99 under synonym attacks, IPMark demonstrates that sentence-level selection can avoid the quality-robustness trade-off that plagues token-level logit modulation. The hierarchical retrieval mechanism (reducing detection latency from 72.7 s to 5.0 s at 100K users) addresses a practical bottleneck for large-scale deployment that prior personalized watermarks have not tackled. Open questions remain: robustness against adaptive attacks that specifically target the semantic-triple or constituency-parse features, whether the HMAC-based binary parity check is sufficient for multi-bit capacity, and how the method generalizes to multimodal or non-English generation where WordNet coverage is limited.
