## Problem

Standard RLVR suffers from Recursive Space Contraction (RSC), where positive sharpening passively suppresses valid alternative branches while negative updates blindly redistribute mass to already-dominant tokens, creating an irreversible collapse into low-entropy states. Existing KL regularization fails to address this because it imposes a rigid Shape Matching constraint that forces the policy to mimic the reference model's full density profile, generating a gradient conflict that hinders necessary sharpening for Pass@1 efficiency while providing no proactive recovery direction during error correction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts, the reference model's Top-K token logits (K=8 by default, forming the Safe Manifold), and verifiable binary rewards from mathematical problem solving. It produces updated policy parameters via a modified GRPO objective where the rectification term activates exclusively on negative-advantage samples, and the anchor set dynamically excludes the current error token to prevent signal cancellation. Unlike KL regularization which requires dense gradient propagation over the full vocabulary (~128k tokens), APO operates on a sparse K-token manifold, significantly reducing backward pass complexity.

## Method

APO introduces Ratio Rectification, modifying the standard PPO/GRPO clipped surrogate for negative-advantage samples by constructing a rectified ratio that combines a Push term (λ·π_θ(y_t)/π_old(y_t) for error suppression) and a Pull term (β·r_anchor for support recovery). The Pull term is a Virtual Anchor Ratio computed via importance sampling over the Safe Manifold (Top-K reference tokens excluding the error token), which simplifies to the total policy mass on the anchor set scaled by the inverse reference confidence. Theoretically, the paper proves the pull gradient is strictly collinear with the Support Coverage objective and that the rectified ratio remains within the PPO trust region (self-stabilizing via clipping), unlike KL penalties which can drive updates outside the trust region. This shifts the regularization paradigm from global Shape Matching to conditional Support Coverage, enabling aggressive sharpening on correct paths while triggering Elastic Recovery that re-inflates suppressed valid branches upon errors.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, APO achieves an average Pass@1 of 50.67% and Pass@K of 78.07% across five mathematical benchmarks (AIME 24/25, AMC 23, Math500, Minerva), outperforming the GRPO baseline (48.68% / 76.56%) and GRPO-KL (44.53% / 75.07%). The same pattern holds on Qwen2.5-7B (43.57% / 73.38% vs. 42.68% / 70.57% for GRPO) and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct (25.51% / 47.11% vs. 25.08% / 43.82% for GRPO), where APO simultaneously improves efficiency and restores the diversity that standard policy gradient methods sacrifice. APO's computational cost (114 GPU hours) is comparable to GRPO-KL (115 hours) and only 1.19× the unregularized GRPO baseline (96 hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

APO outperforms the unregularized GRPO baseline on both Pass@1 and Pass@K across all three model architectures, with the largest gains on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (+1.99% Pass@1, +1.51% Pass@K) and Llama-3.2-3B (+0.43% Pass@1, +3.29% Pass@K), confirming that standard negative reinforcement causes irreversible diversity collapse. Against GRPO-KL, APO achieves a substantial Pass@1 advantage (50.67% vs. 44.53% on Math-7B, a 6.14-point gap) while also recovering Pass@K (78.07% vs. 75.07%), demonstrating that the rigid Shape Matching constraint of KL actively hinders sharpening. The GRPO-KL (Error-Only) ablation, which applies KL only during negative feedback, improves over global KL (46.79% vs. 44.53%) but still underperforms unregularized GRPO (48.68%), isolating the failure to the topology of the KL constraint rather than its activation timing. Against NSR (Negative Sample Reinforcement), APO wins on both metrics (50.67% vs. 48.58% Pass@1; 78.07% vs. 77.40% Pass@K), showing that scalar re-weighting of negative samples without a topological recovery mechanism is insufficient to restore valid reasoning paths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Squeezing Effect identified by Ren & Sutherland (2025) from offline fine-tuning to on-policy RLVR, formalizing it as Recursive Space Contraction (RSC) where positive sharpening and negative squeezing create an irreversible collapse loop. It builds directly on the GRPO/PPO clipped surrogate framework (Shao et al., 2024; Schulman et al., 2017) but departs from the standard KL-regularization paradigm by reframing the reference model's role from a passive Shape Matching constraint to an active Support Coverage anchor. The key conceptual departure is Ratio Rectification—injecting a restorative pull force directly into the policy ratio rather than as an auxiliary loss—positioning APO as orthogonal to entropy regularization and direct Pass@k optimization approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even conditionally-applied KL (Error-Only) underperforms unregularized GRPO challenges the common assumption that 'just apply KL only during errors' is sufficient, suggesting the geometric topology of the constraint matters more than its timing. APO's sparse Top-K anchor (K=8) offers a practical, low-overhead alternative to full-vocabulary KL that could be readily integrated into existing RLVR pipelines. However, the evaluation is confined to mathematical reasoning benchmarks and three model scales, leaving open whether the Safe Manifold assumption (Top-8 covering ~97% of valid tokens) holds for open-ended generation, code, or domains where the reference model's support is genuinely insufficient. The modest ~2% Pass@1 gain over GRPO, while consistent across models, limits the immediate practical impact.
