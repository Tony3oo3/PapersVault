## Problem

Existing zero-shot referring image segmentation methods score SAM-generated mask proposals independently via CLIP similarity, making them vulnerable to visually similar distractors that partially satisfy the referring expression. This paper addresses the gap by reframing proposal selection as a robustness-aware contextual search problem that evaluates whether a candidate remains reliable when competing proposals are introduced as perturbations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264479/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form natural language referring expression as input. SAM generates class-agnostic mask proposals via an 8×8 point grid (with IoU and stability thresholds at 0.7), each proposal is cropped and encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B visual encoder at 224×224, and GEM/FG-CLIP produce text-conditioned heatmaps at full image resolution. The output is a single binary segmentation mask (H×W) for the referred target object, selected without any task-specific training.

## Method

RefChess decomposes the referring expression into structured linguistic constraints (subject noun, attribute prompts, relation prompts, location tags, size preference, and optional proximity object) and computes a unified proposal score combining CLIP region-text compatibility, GEM/FG-CLIP heatmap evidence with location/size reweighting, and proximity-object heatmap priors with neighborhood max-propagation. Rather than greedily selecting argmax, the method applies Monte-Carlo Tree Search over the combinatorial space of distractor subsets: each MCTS node represents a partial context of sampled proposal indices, the UCB selection rule balances exploitation and exploration, and rollouts sample additional distractors up to depth D. The stability-aware reward R(c) = S_{i0} + λμ(s_sup) − γσ(s_sup) rewards proposals whose score remains consistently high relative to sampled distractors while penalizing variance across contexts. The final mask is the root child with the highest empirical mean value after M=2048 simulations, yielding a training-free, interpretable selection that is robust to visually ambiguous scenes.

## Results

On the RefCOCO val split, RefChess achieves 55.19 mIoU and 48.47 oIoU, surpassing the strongest zero-shot baseline HybridGL (49.48 mIoU / 41.81 oIoU) by +5.71 and +6.66 points respectively. On RefCOCO+ val, RefChess reaches 48.63 mIoU and 41.20 oIoU, improving over HybridGL by +5.23 and +5.46. On RefCOCOg val, RefChess attains 50.63 mIoU and 42.85 oIoU, slightly trailing HybridGL's 51.25 mIoU by 0.62 points while matching its oIoU. All results are obtained in a fully training-free setting using SAM proposals and frozen CLIP/FG-CLIP encoders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RefChess outperforms all zero-shot baselines without additional training on the majority of splits. Against the strongest zero-shot competitor HybridGL, RefChess gains +5.71 mIoU on RefCOCO val, +5.23 on RefCOCO+ val, and +5.46 oIoU on RefCOCO+ val, but is marginally behind on RefCOCOg mIoU (50.63 vs. 51.25 val; 51.32 vs. 51.59 test). Compared to TAS (which relies on BLIP2) and Ref-Diff (which uses Stable Diffusion), RefChess achieves substantially higher mIoU (e.g., +15.35 on RefCOCO val over TAS) while using only SAM and CLIP/FG-CLIP, avoiding heavy auxiliary models. Against weakly-supervised methods CLRL and PPT, RefChess improves mIoU by +3.8% to +12.6% on RefCOCO and +7.7% on RefCOCOg val, despite requiring no task-specific training or bounding-box supervision. On the hardest RefCOCO+ testB split, RefChess (39.63 mIoU) still trails PPT (44.77) by about 5 points, indicating that the most challenging spatial-reasoning cases remain difficult even with contextual search. Overall, RefChess sets the best oIoU across all three benchmarks and the best mIoU on RefCOCO and RefCOCO+, establishing a new state of the art for training-free referring image segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the dominant zero-shot RIS paradigm of generating class-agnostic proposals with SAM and ranking them via CLIP-based region-text similarity (as in Global-Local, CaR, HybridGL, TAS, and Ref-Diff). Its key departure is reframing proposal selection from independent single-step scoring to a robustness-aware contextual search problem, where each candidate is evaluated under sampled distractor subsets using Monte-Carlo Tree Search as a budgeted exploration mechanism. The stability-aware reward integrates heterogeneous signals—language decomposition, CLIP compatibility, object-centric heatmap priors (GEM/FG-CLIP), and spatial guidance—into a unified scalar that the MCTS loop optimizes over combinatorial context perturbations rather than a fixed ranking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RefChess demonstrates that the assumption underlying most zero-shot RIS methods—that the correct proposal can be identified by independent scoring—is a significant bottleneck, and that explicitly modeling contextual interactions among competing candidates yields large, consistent gains without any task-specific training. The approach is practical (≈1.2 s on a single RTX 4090) and training-free, making it accessible for deployment in visual search and human-robot interaction. Open questions include whether the framework extends to set-valued or multi-instance queries, whether stronger proposal priors (beyond SAM's point-grid prompting) could further reduce the ceiling imposed by proposal quality, and whether the MCTS contextual search idea transfers to other vision-language ranking tasks beyond RIS.
