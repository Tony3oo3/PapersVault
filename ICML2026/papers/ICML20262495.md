## Problem

Real-world datasets simultaneously suffer from long-tailed class distributions and noisy annotations, yet existing methods address these issues in isolation or apply class-agnostic label correction that fails to account for the non-uniform noise impact across classes. This leads to under-correction of scarce tail-class labels and over-regularization of abundant head-class labels, degrading overall performance. The paper identifies that merely reducing the overall noise ratio is insufficient; class-aware rectification that prioritizes tail-label accuracy is essential.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images paired with noisy label annotations and class label text strings, processed through a CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone with AdaptFormer for parameter-efficient fine-tuning (20 epochs, batch size 128). It produces per-sample corrected class labels and a rectified label distribution (n_c^{r,(e)}) that approximates the true underlying class frequencies. The rectified distribution is then fed into a logit-adjustment loss for long-tail calibration, making CARE a plug-and-play preprocessing layer compatible with any downstream long-tail calibration strategy.

## Method

CARE is a parameter-free framework that builds class-adaptive expert consensus from three complementary sources: a Text Expert (frozen CLIP text encoder providing semantic priors), an Image Expert (fine-tuned CLIP image encoder via AdaptFormer providing task-adapted visual features), and a Base Expert (the original noisy labels as a weak signal). For each class c, the number of Top-K candidates K_c is set proportional to class frequency (K_c ∝ n_c^e), enforcing stricter consensus for tail classes and more permissive agreement for head classes. Confidence scores from all three experts are accumulated into a per-sample frequency vector F, where the reliability weight α_m(x) down-weights an expert if the observed label falls outside its Top-K predictions, preventing reinforcement of potentially noisy assignments. The corrected label is the argmax of F, and the resulting rectified distribution drives logit adjustment for long-tail calibration. The entire process is joint with training (no separate denoising phase), iteratively refining labels as class frequencies are updated each epoch.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LTN with joint noise (IF=100, NR=50%), CARE (CLIP+LA w. CARE) achieves 76.7% top-1 accuracy, surpassing CLIP+LA (75.3%) by 1.4% and CLIP+RLA* (66.7%) by 10.0%. Under symmetric noise (IF=10, NR=60%), CARE reaches 79.2%, outperforming CLIP+LA (76.0%) by 3.2%. On real-world Food101N with IF=100, CARE achieves 84.1%, beating CLIP+RLA* (77.2%) by 6.9%. Across all evaluated datasets and noise settings, CARE consistently improves over both the CLIP+LA and CLIP+RLA* baselines, with the largest gains under severe class imbalance and high noise rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARE (CLIP+LA w. CARE) consistently outperforms CLIP+LA across all noise types and imbalance factors, with gains ranging from 0.2% to 3.2% (e.g., 79.2% vs. 76.0% on symmetric NR=60%, IF=10). Against CLIP+RLA*, CARE achieves larger margins under severe imbalance: +10.0% on CIFAR-100-LTN (IF=100, NR=50% joint) and +6.9% on Food101N (IF=100), while the two methods are nearly tied under mild settings (IF=10, NR=0.1). Compared to dedicated LTNL methods, CARE substantially outperforms RCAL (76.7% vs. 30.3% at IF=100, NR=50%), TABASCO (70.5% vs. 50.5% on asymmetric NR=40%), and ECBS-Res18 (76.7% vs. 39.1% at IF=100, NR=50%). On real-world WebVision-50, CARE achieves 85.3% top-1 accuracy, edging out CLIP+LA (85.1%) and CLIP+RLA* (85.0%), and surpassing the best non-CLIP method (Sel-CL+ at 80.0%) by 5.3%. On mini-ImageNet-LTN^r (IF=100, NR=40%), CARE reaches 81.9%, outperforming CLIP+LA (80.8%) and CLIP+RLA* (79.9%). The only setting where CARE does not clearly lead is CIFAR-100-LTN IF=10, NR=0.1 joint noise, where CLIP+LA (84.0%) slightly edges out CARE (83.9%), suggesting diminishing returns when noise is minimal and imbalance is mild.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARE sits at the intersection of noisy-label learning and long-tailed recognition, building on the LTNL problem formulation introduced by Lu et al. (2023) and Zhang et al. (2023a). It departs from prior class-agnostic label correction methods (e.g., RLA, TABASCO) by introducing a class-frequency-adaptive Top-K consensus mechanism, and repurposes a pretrained VLM (CLIP) not merely as a backbone but as a source of three complementary 'experts' (text encoder, fine-tuned image encoder, observed labels) whose disagreement drives label rectification. The downstream calibration step reuses standard logit adjustment (Menon et al., 2021), positioning CARE as a plug-and-play front-end correction layer rather than a new training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that naively reducing overall noise rate via class-agnostic correction can actually degrade performance below the original noisy baseline (76.8% vs. 79.5%), challenging the implicit assumption that 'less noise is always better' in imbalanced settings. CARE's parameter-free, VLM-based consensus mechanism offers a practical path for real-world pipelines where re-annotation is infeasible, and its plug-and-play nature (validated across CLIP, BLIP, ResNet+GloVe, MLP-Mixer, and TABASCO) suggests broad applicability. Open questions include the memory overhead of maintaining per-class frequency vectors at scale, whether the approach extends to multi-modal or video settings, and whether additional expert sources (e.g., contrastive-learning features) could further tighten the consensus.
