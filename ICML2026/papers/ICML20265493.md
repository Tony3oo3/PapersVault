## Problem

Existing audio-visual captioning methods produce global, paragraph-level descriptions without explicit timestamps, while traditional dense video captioning remains confined to the visual modality with sparse, event-centric outputs. No prior framework generates continuous, fine-grained, temporally-grounded audio-visual narratives that cover the full semantic context of a video scene-by-scene, leaving a critical gap in dense supervision signals for MLLMs to learn time-aware omni-modal reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265493/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video with synchronized audio (frames sampled at 2 FPS, max 297,920 pixels per frame, 20,070,400 total pixels per video) and produces a JSON-structured sequence of scene-level entries, each containing a timestamp range (MM:SS–MM:SS) and a six-dimensional caption spanning Events, Background, Camera State, Shot Editing, Dialogue, and Acoustic Cues. The 'dense' requirement means segments are temporally continuous (no gaps between scenes) and descriptions average ~995 words per video, covering all significant scenes rather than only salient moments.

## Method

TimeChat-Captioner is built on the Qwen2.5-Omni backbone, leveraging its interleaved audio-visual token arrangement and M-RoPE positional encoding for precise temporal boundary localization. Training data (TimeChatCap-42K) is synthesized via a two-step Gemini-2.5-Pro pipeline: coarse boundary segmentation followed by fine-grained multi-dimensional caption generation, with quality filtering to remove malformed or too-short segments. The model is trained in two stages: SFT on 40K samples (2 epochs, lr=5e-5) to learn the structured output format, followed by GRPO on 2K samples (1 epoch, rollout=8) with four task-specific rewards—format validity, length regularization, timestamp F1 at multiple IoU thresholds, and the proposed SodaM metric. SodaM itself uses IoU-based dynamic programming alignment to handle variable-length scene sequences, merges many-to-one predictions to mitigate boundary ambiguity, and scores caption quality via a checklist-based LLM judge across all six dimensions, jointly optimizing temporal accuracy and semantic completeness in a single reward signal.

## Results

On OmniDCBench, TimeChat-Captioner-7B (GRPO) achieves a SodaM score of 35.0, surpassing the strongest closed-source model Gemini-2.5-Pro (33.7) and all open-source baselines (next best: Qwen3-Omni at 14.3). For scene segmentation, Gemini-2.5-Pro retains the edge with F1=68.5 and mIoU=74.9 versus TimeChat-Captioner's 61.2 and 69.6. On downstream caption-based VideoQA (Daily-Omni / WorldSense), the model scores 52.8 / 22.6, outperforming all open-source baselines (next best: video-SALMONN-2 at 29.9 / 18.2) but trailing Gemini-2.5-Pro (60.2 / 33.8). On Charades-STA temporal grounding, TimeChat-Captioner achieves R1@0.5=68.7 and mIoU=58.8, beating expert models TimeSuite (67.1) and TimeExpert (64.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the proprietary Gemini-2.5-Pro, TimeChat-Captioner-GRPO wins on the primary SodaM caption-quality metric (35.0 vs. 33.7) and on every individual dimension (e.g., Background 49.2 vs. 39.1, Acoustics 38.2 vs. 25.4, Dialogue 54.3 vs. 46.4), but loses on scene-segmentation F1 (61.2 vs. 68.5) and mIoU (69.6 vs. 74.9). Among open-source models, it dominates all baselines by a wide margin: the next-best Qwen3-Omni (30B-A3B) scores only 14.3 SodaM, less than half. On caption-based VideoQA (Daily-Omni), TimeChat-Captioner (52.8) outperforms video-SALMONN-2 (29.9), Qwen3-Omni-Captioner (27.2), and UGC-VideoCaptioner (17.0) by 20+ points, though Gemini-2.5-Pro still leads at 60.2. On Charades-STA temporal grounding, the model beats all expert temporal models—TimeSuite (R1@0.5=67.1), TimeExpert (64.1), and TimeChat (46.7)—as well as the Qwen2.5-Omni-7B backbone (65.9), confirming that dense captioning training transfers to fine-grained temporal localization. The GRPO stage adds +2.4 SodaM over SFT alone (35.0 vs. 32.6) and +2.1 on Daily-Omni (52.8 vs. 50.7), validating the reward-guided optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dense video captioning line (Krishna et al., 2017; Yang et al., 2023) from the visual-only domain to joint audio-visual understanding, while simultaneously departing from holistic audio-visual captioners (AVoCaDO, video-SALMONN-2, UGC-VideoCaptioner) that produce paragraph-level descriptions without explicit temporal grounding. Architecturally, it builds on Qwen2.5-Omni's interleaved audio-visual token arrangement and M-RoPE for temporal positioning, and methodologically adopts GRPO (Shao et al., 2025) with task-specific rewards, extending the RL-for-captioning paradigm (CapRL, VideoCap-R1, AVoCaDO) by introducing a unified SodaM reward that jointly optimizes temporal alignment and multi-dimensional caption completeness. The evaluation metric SodaM itself extends SODA_c (Fujita et al., 2020) with an IoU-based dynamic programming alignment step to handle the many-to-one scene boundary ambiguity inherent in semantic segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first dedicated benchmark (OmniDCBench, 1,122 samples) and unified metric (SodaM) for temporally-grounded, multi-dimensional audio-visual captioning, filling a gap the authors identify as 'a largely unexplored frontier' in the open-source community. The demonstration that a 7B task-specific model can match or exceed Gemini-2.5-Pro on this task, and that its generated captions serve as training data equivalent to closed-source API outputs (44.9 vs. 44.4 on Daily-Omni), makes it a practical data engine for scaling omni-modal video understanding. Key limitations include the 32K context window constraint and limited generalization to hour-long videos (requiring a segment-then-caption workaround), leaving open the question of whether the six-dimensional structural schema and GRPO reward design transfer to truly long-form or open-domain video content without architectural changes.
