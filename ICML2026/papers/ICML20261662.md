## Problem

Existing CLIP-based post-hoc OOD detection methods treat text embeddings of class names as visual class prototypes, implicitly assuming they are well-aligned with optimal prototypes in the image feature space. This paper theoretically demonstrates that this assumption is generally invalid: text-derived prototypes contain components orthogonal to the visual feature subspace, yielding a non-vanishing modality gap that cannot be eliminated by prompt engineering or negative-label mining alone.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled RGB test images (resized to 224×224 or 336×336) processed by a frozen pre-trained CLIP encoder (ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14, or ResNet-50), along with K ID class labels and L=10,000 OOD pseudo-labels mined from WordNet. It produces a scalar OOD detection score per image via a softmax over updated visual prototypes for both ID and OOD pseudo-classes, which is thresholded to yield an ID/OOD decision. Crucially, the method operates in a strictly post-hoc, zero-shot manner: no ID training data is accessed, and prototypes are initialized from text embeddings then refined online as test samples arrive.

## Method

The core contribution is an online pseudo-supervised framework that learns class prototypes directly in the visual feature space. Theoretically, Theorem 1 characterizes optimal visual prototypes as lying in the span of visual features, and Theorem 2 proves a non-vanishing lower bound on the Frobenius distance between text prototypes and these optimal visual prototypes (the modality gap). To close this gap without training data, the method constructs a pseudo-supervised cross-entropy objective using soft CLIP predictions as surrogate labels, then performs online projected gradient descent on the unit hypersphere with a decaying step size η_i = ρ/√i, yielding an O(√N) cumulative regret guarantee (Theorem 3). At test time, a hard thresholding rule on the NegLabel score partitions incoming samples into positive (ID) and negative (OOD) groups; ID prototypes are updated only on positive samples and OOD pseudo-class prototypes only on negative samples, while uncertain samples in the intermediate score range are discarded. The final OOD score is a softmax over the updated visual prototypes for both ID and OOD pseudo-classes, replacing the static text-embedding prototypes used by prior methods.

## Results

On the ImageNet-1K benchmark (ViT-B/16 CLIP), the proposed method achieves 97.75% average AUROC and 12.79% average FPR95 across four OOD datasets (iNaturalist, SUN, Places365, Textures), surpassing the strongest zero-shot baseline AdaNeg+NegLabel (96.66% AUROC, 18.92% FPR95) by 1.09% and 6.13% respectively. On the OpenOOD benchmark with ImageNet-1K as ID, the method achieves 85.80% AUROC and 52.86% FPR95 on Near-OOD, and 97.67% AUROC and 13.22% FPR95 on Far-OOD, outperforming AdaNeg+NegLabel by 9.10% AUROC on Near-OOD. On CIFAR-100 as ID, the method reaches 89.36% AUROC (Near-OOD) and 96.90% AUROC (Far-OOD), improving over AdaNeg+NegLabel by 4.74% and 1.65% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all 22 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against the closest competitor AdaNeg+NegLabel, the method achieves 1.09% higher average AUROC and 6.13% lower average FPR95 on ImageNet-1K, with particularly large gains on SUN (98.94 vs. 97.44 AUROC) and Textures (97.01 vs. 94.93 AUROC). Compared to NegLabel, the improvement is 3.54% AUROC and 12.61% FPR95 on average, demonstrating that learning visual prototypes substantially outperforms relying on text embeddings. On the OpenOOD Near-OOD setting (the hardest scenario), the method achieves 85.80% AUROC versus 76.70% for AdaNeg+NegLabel and 75.18% for NegLabel, a gap of over 9 points. The advantage is maintained across different CLIP backbones (ResNet-50, ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14), input resolutions (224×224 vs. 336×336), and domain-shifted ID variants (ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-S), confirming the generality of the modality-gap-aware prototype calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CLIP-based post-hoc OOD detection lineage initiated by MCM (Ming et al., 2022), which first proposed using text embeddings of class names as visual class prototypes, and extended by NegLabel (Jiang et al., 2024) with negative-label mining. The key departure is a theoretical challenge to the 'text-as-prototype' paradigm: Theorems 1 and 2 prove that optimal visual prototypes must lie in the span of visual features, while text-derived prototypes carry orthogonal components that create a non-vanishing modality gap. Rather than refining negative prototypes as AdaNeg does, the paper replaces text-as-prototype entirely with an online pseudo-supervised framework that learns prototypes in visual space from unlabeled test streams, with a convergence guarantee (Theorem 3).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical characterization of the modality gap as a structural, non-eliminable limitation of text-as-prototype methods provides a principled explanation for why prior CLIP-based OOD detectors are suboptimal, and motivates a cleaner design space for future work. The proposed online pseudo-supervised approach is practical (no ID training data required, only a test-time stream) and achieves consistent SOTA across ImageNet-1K, CIFAR benchmarks, and OpenOOD with multiple architectures. Open questions include how the method behaves when the test stream is highly non-stationary or when the CLIP pseudo-labels are systematically biased, and whether the framework extends naturally to non-contrastive VLMs or multi-modal OOD detection beyond image-text.
