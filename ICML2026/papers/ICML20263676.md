## Problem

Reward alignment of flow and diffusion models requires estimating the value function gradient ∇V_t(x), which in turn demands sampling from the conditional posterior p_{1|t}(·|x). Existing approaches either use biased heuristics (point-mass or Gaussian approximations of the posterior) that fail in multimodal settings, or rely on expensive iterative ODE/SDE rollouts that make inference-time steering prohibitively slow and per-reward fine-tuning impractical. No prior method provides efficient, differentiable, one-step access to the full conditional posterior distribution.

## Input / Output

The MFM takes a noisy intermediate state x_t at time t (the conditioning context), an exogenous noise vector ε ~ p_0, and optionally a class label or text prompt, and produces one-step samples x̂_1 ~ p_{1|t}(·|x_t) in a single forward pass. By drawing independent ε values, arbitrarily many i.i.d. posterior samples are obtained, which are differentiable in x_t to support gradient-based value function estimation. The architecture is a standard DiT backbone with only 1.3% parameter overhead for the additional (t, x) conditioning, evaluated on ImageNet 256×256 and MNIST.

## Method

The key insight is that for every context (t, x), a conditional auxiliary ODE exists that transports the prior p_0 to the posterior p_{1|t}(·|x); the MFM amortizes the solution operators of this infinite family of ODEs into a single stochastic flow map X_{s,u}(·; t, x) parameterized in residual form. Training uses a diagonal loss anchoring the instantaneous velocity to the conditional drift (computed analytically via GLASS flows during distillation, or via coupled interpolants when training from data) plus a consistency loss enforcing the semi-group property for each fixed (t, x). Unlike deterministic flow maps that predict a single endpoint, the MFM maps exogenous noise ε to stochastic posterior samples, enabling a differentiable Monte Carlo estimator for ∇V_t(x) that drives both inference-time steering (MFM-G) and an unbiased, explicitly off-policy fine-tuning objective (MFM-FT) that requires no on-policy simulation.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, MFM-XL/2 achieves a competitive FID of 1.97 at 4 NFEs (2.40 at 2 NFEs, 3.72 at 1 NFE), approaching the deterministic DMF-XL/2+ baseline (FID 1.51 at 4 NFEs) while additionally providing stochastic one-step posterior samples. In inference-time steering on ImageNet, the single-particle MFM-G sampler (N=1 MC sample) outperforms a Best-of-1000 baseline across all three reward models (HPSv2, ImageReward, PickScore) while requiring over 100× fewer NFEs. On the GMM inverse problem, MFM-based steering recovers the true posterior distribution more accurately than both DPS and SMC-TDS even with as few as N=2 Monte Carlo samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the deterministic flow map DMF-XL/2+ (Lee et al., 2025), MFM-XL/2 is slightly worse on unconditional FID (1.97 vs. 1.51 at 4 NFEs) but uniquely provides stochastic one-step posterior samples needed for reward alignment. In inference-time steering, MFM-G with a single MC sample (N=1) strictly dominates the Best-of-1000 baseline on compute-normalised reward across HPSv2, ImageReward, and PickScore, requiring over 100× fewer NFEs. MFM-G also outperforms DPS (Chung et al., 2024) in both the GMM and MNIST experiments, where DPS collapses toward the dominant mode and fails to capture posterior variance. On the GMM task, MFM-based steering outperforms SMC-TDS (Wu et al., 2024) in Sliced-Wasserstein distance to the ground-truth posterior even at N=2 samples. In posterior fidelity evaluation (Figure 6), MFM one-step samples strongly outperform explicit GLASS-flow ODE rollouts (Holderrieth et al., 2025) on both posterior FID and value-function estimation correlation, with the largest gap at the 1-NFE budget. The gradient-based estimator MFM-G significantly outperforms the gradient-free variant MFM-GF in the higher-dimensional MNIST setting, while both outperform DPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow map and consistency model lineage (Boffi et al., 2025a; Geng et al., 2025a; Song et al., 2023b) by introducing a stochastic dimension: while standard flow maps deterministically compress ODE trajectories into endpoint predictions, MFMs learn a 'meta' map over an infinite family of conditional ODEs, each transporting noise to a specific posterior p_{1|t}(·|x). This is motivated by the reward alignment literature (DPS, SMC-TDS, search-based methods), where the central bottleneck is estimating the value function gradient ∇V_t(x), which requires sampling from the conditional posterior. The key conceptual departure is that deterministic flow maps are structurally incapable of representing the full posterior distribution, so MFMs introduce exogenous noise to enable one-step, differentiable posterior sampling. For training supervision, the paper leverages GLASS flows (Holderrieth et al., 2025) to obtain analytical conditional drifts, and fine-tunes from the DMF-XL/2+ deterministic flow map (Lee et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MFMs eliminate the trajectory-rollout bottleneck that has made reward alignment prohibitively expensive, enabling both inference-time steering and off-policy fine-tuning with a single trained model. The practical impact is substantial: a single-particle MFM-G sampler outperforms Best-of-1000 at over 100× fewer NFEs, and the off-policy fine-tuning objective avoids the on-policy simulation required by prior methods. Open questions include whether the approach extends cleanly to non-Gaussian priors, video and text generation, and whether the gradient-free MFM-Search (which lacks asymptotic guarantees) can be made theoretically sound. The framework's generality—applying to any continuous flow/diffusion model—suggests broad adoption potential as flow-based generators become the dominant paradigm.
