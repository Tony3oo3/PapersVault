## Problem

Quantum processors face a persistent calibration bottleneck where device heterogeneity and environmental drift force practitioners to choose between suboptimal non-adaptive controllers or costly per-device recalibration. Existing meta-learning approaches for quantum control demonstrate that learned initializations improve robustness, but no prior work characterizes *when* task-specific adaptation outperforms non-adaptive deployment or how adaptation gains scale with device-to-device variability. This paper derives scaling laws providing a quantitative criterion for when the computational overhead of per-device gradient steps is justified.

## Input / Output

The method takes device-specific noise rates ξ = (Γ_deph, Γ_relax) as context input to a two-layer MLP policy that outputs piecewise-constant control amplitudes (2 channels for single-qubit X gates, 6 for two-qubit CZ gates), and produces adapted pulse parameters θ_K after K gradient steps on a differentiable Lindblad simulator. The adaptation gap G_K = E_ξ[L_ξ(θ₀) − L_ξ(θ_K)] quantifies the fidelity improvement from adaptation, and the few-shot protocol (Algorithm 1) converts N = 3–5 probe steps into a predicted optimal budget K_α within 3–19% relative error across 2×–5× out-of-distribution regimes.

## Method

The core contribution is a scaling law G_K ≥ A_∞(1 − e^{−βK}) derived under a local Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition, where the asymptotic ceiling A_∞ ∝ σ_τ² scales with task variance and the rate β = ημ_min depends on learning rate and landscape curvature. Meta-training uses FOMAML on a differentiable Lindblad master equation simulator, learning an initialization θ₀ optimized for fast per-task adaptation rather than average performance, with the policy receiving ξ as context to enable task-appropriate pulse initialization. The proof decomposes into three components: exponential convergence under PL (rigorous), rate characterization via L-smoothness (rigorous), and the variance-proportional ceiling (requiring quadratic loss approximation and isotropic task variance). A Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz relaxation provides a polynomial-decay fallback O(K^{−1/(2α−1)}) when PL is violated, and a few-shot pre-adaptation protocol estimates the 95%-gain budget from 3–5 probe steps with an R²-based PL diagnostic flag for deployment.

## Results

On single-qubit X gates (in-distribution), the adaptation gap $G_K$ saturates exponentially with $R^2 > 0.999$ and rate $\beta = 0.083$, while the asymptotic gap $G_\infty$ scales linearly with task variance $\sigma_\tau^2$ ($R^2 = 0.94$). On two-qubit CZ gates under extreme 10× out-of-distribution noise, meta-initialization yields only 54.2% gate fidelity, but K=10 gradient adaptation steps recover 95.7% fidelity (a 41.5-point improvement) with exponential saturation at $R^2 = 0.986$ and $\beta = 0.333$. The few-shot pre-adaptation protocol estimates the 95%-gain budget $\hat{K}_{0.95}$ within 3–19% relative error using only N=5 probe steps across 2×–5× OOD regimes. Classical LQR validation confirms the same exponential-linear structure ($R^2 > 0.99$), supporting that the scaling law reflects general optimization geometry rather than quantum-specific physics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRAPE (the standard quantum optimal control method), FOMAML's meta-initialization alone (K=0) achieves 98.9% fidelity with zero per-task optimization, outperforming non-adaptive GRAPE (97.9%) by 1.0 percentage points. Per-task GRAPE reaches 99.3% but requires 200 warm-started optimization steps per device; GRAPE from scratch fails to reach 98.9% even after 150 iterations (only 97.5%), demonstrating that the meta-learned initialization provides a superior starting point that GRAPE cannot match through optimization alone. Against the fixed-average baseline (trained on the mean task), FOMAML starts at F=0.959 versus 0.881 under mild OOD, and the gap widens dramatically under 10× OOD where the fixed baseline cannot recover. An ablation (Table A.5) confirms that gradient adaptation alone (without task-conditioning $\xi$) delivers +0.184 (mild OOD) and +0.158 (strong OOD) over a blind baseline, while noisy $\xi$ estimates (30% uncertainty) match the full method, ruling out the interpretation that gains stem from contextual conditioning rather than genuine adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MAML/FOMAML meta-learning framework (Finn et al., 2017; Nichol et al., 2018) by deriving explicit scaling laws for the adaptation gap under Polyak-Lojasiewicz and Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz conditions, building on Collins et al. (2022) who characterized when MAML outperforms non-adaptive learning but did not quantify the magnitude of gains. In the quantum control domain, it departs from GRAPE (Khaneja et al., 2005) and RL-based approaches (Bukov et al., 2018; Niu et al., 2019) by providing a finite-time, task-variance-dependent criterion for when per-device adaptation justifies its overhead, complementing metaQctrl (Zhang et al., 2025) which demonstrated robustness without predictive scaling laws. The LQR validation in the appendix positions the result as a general optimization-geometry phenomenon rather than a quantum-specific effect.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scaling law G_K ≥ A_∞(1−e^{−βK}) with A_∞ ∝ σ_τ² gives quantum hardware operators a quantitative, per-gate decision rule for allocating calibration compute, directly addressing the 2-hour-per-day calibration bottleneck on 100-qubit systems. The few-shot protocol (N=3–5 probe steps, 3–19% budget error) is immediately deployable in cloud quantum workflows. Key limitations include the simulation-only validation (no experimental hardware results), the requirement for differentiable dynamics that precludes direct on-device gradient computation, and the PL condition's potential failure under extreme distribution shift—though the KL polynomial-decay fallback and Algorithm 1's R² diagnostic mitigate this. Extending the framework to non-stationary noise, many-body control, and experimental hardware remains open.
