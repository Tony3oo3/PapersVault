## Problem

Existing generative models for single-cell gene expression either impose a fixed gene ordering (violating the exchangeability of gene sets) or rely on shallow MLP-based autoencoders and GANs with training instabilities. This makes them inflexible across tissues and species, unable to scale to full gene vocabularies, and poorly suited to modeling the complex latent dependencies among genes in count-valued, highly sparse data.

## Input / Output

The model takes sparse gene expression counts (typically >70% zeros) paired with gene IDs as input, filtering to expressed genes and padding to a fixed context length d < D to manage computational cost. It outputs synthetic gene expression profiles parameterized by a Negative Binomial (or Gaussian) distribution over any queried set of gene IDs, making the gene vocabulary flexible across tissues without retraining. Additionally, the encoder produces fixed-size permutation-invariant latent embeddings (e.g., 4096-dim) usable for downstream classification tasks.

## Method

scLDM is a two-stage framework: a fully transformer-based VAE whose encoder uses a Multi-head Cross-Attention Block (MCAB) with learnable pseudo-inputs as a permutation-invariant pooling operator to produce fixed-size latent tokens, and whose decoder reuses the same MCAB with gene-specific embedding vectors as queries to act as a permutation-equivariant unpooling operator, yielding gene-specific NB/Gaussian parameters. The standard Gaussian prior is replaced by a latent diffusion model parameterized by Diffusion Transformer (DiT) blocks and trained with the flow matching loss using linear interpolants (SiT formulation). Conditional generation is achieved through joint (non-additive) classifier-free guidance over arbitrary combinations of attributes (cell type, perturbation), which the paper shows outperforms the additive multi-attribute guidance used in CFGen. The key architectural insight is that a single MCAB module, differing only in whether its queries are learned pseudo-inputs (encoder) or gene-indexed embeddings (decoder), simultaneously enforces permutation invariance and equivariance, eliminating the need for separate pooling/unpooling components.

## Results

On reconstruction (Table 1), scLDM(NB) achieves the lowest reconstruction error across all three observational datasets (e.g., 4993.6 RE on Tabula Muris vs. 5547.6 for CFGen and 5588.2 for scVI) and the highest Pearson correlation (0.376 on Tabula Muris vs. 0.221 scVI, 0.136 CFGen). On unconditional generation (Table 2), scLDM(NB) attains the lowest Wasserstein-2 distance (10.710 on Dentate Gyrus vs. 17.443 scDiffusion, 12.617 CFGen) and the highest precision (0.664 vs. 0.367 scDiffusion, 0.278 CFGen), indicating a more balanced precision-recall trade-off. On perturbational generation (Table 3), scLDM achieves competitive W2 and MMD² while maintaining substantially better recall than CPA and CellFlow (e.g., 0.626 recall on Parse 1M vs. 0.000 for CPA and 0.490 for CellFlow).

## Baselines & Comparisons

scLDM decisively outperforms scVI on reconstruction (e.g., 4993.6 vs. 5588.2 RE on Tabula Muris) and on generation metrics, where scVI shows near-zero recall in conditional settings. Against CFGen (the prior SOTA), scLDM achieves lower W2 (10.710 vs. 12.617 unconditional on Dentate Gyrus) and substantially higher precision (0.664 vs. 0.278), while CFGen retains a recall advantage in some conditional settings. scDiffusion is consistently dominated by scLDM on W2 and MMD², and exhibits near-zero recall in conditional generation. On perturbational data (Parse 1M, Replogle), scLDM is competitive with CellFlow on W2 (12.346 vs. 11.836 on Parse 1M) but achieves far better recall (0.626 vs. 0.490) and MMD² (0.020 vs. 0.015); CPA and STATE-Tx show near-zero recall, indicating severe mode collapse. scGPT performs poorly on perturbational generation (W2 22.870 on Parse 1M, 34.166 on Replogle). For downstream classification, scLDM(270M) slightly edges out TranscriptFormer on COVID-19 (F1 0.820 vs. 0.814) and matches or marginally exceeds scGPT, scVI, and TranscriptFormer on Tabula Sapiens 2.0 (F1 0.802–0.804, within mutual uncertainty), while clearly outperforming UCE, Geneformer, and AIDO.Cell.

## Relation to Prior Work

scLDM extends the latent diffusion paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022) to single-cell transcriptomics by replacing the standard Gaussian prior with a DiT-based flow matching model trained with linear interpolants (Ma et al., 2024; Lipman et al., 2022), while departing from prior single-cell LDMs like CFGen (Palma et al., 2025a) and SCLD (Wang et al., 2023) by using a fully transformer-based VAE instead of MLP-based autoencoders. The key architectural departure is the Multi-head Cross-Attention Block (MCAB), which unifies permutation-invariant pooling (encoder) and permutation-equivariant unpooling (decoder) into a single module inspired by Perceiver IO (Jaegle et al., 2022), eliminating the separate PMA/ISAB blocks of SetTransformer (Lee et al., 2019) and the dual-latent hierarchy of SetVAE (Kim et al., 2021). For conditional generation, the paper replaces CFGen's additive multi-attribute classifier-free guidance with a joint formulation over attribute combinations (Ho & Salimans, 2022), which it shows to be superior on perturbational benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exchangeability-preserving design removes the artificial gene-ordering constraint that limits most existing single-cell generative models, enabling a single model to generalize across tissues and species with different gene vocabularies. The consistent SOTA performance across observational (Dentate Gyrus, Tabula Muris, HLCA) and perturbational (Parse 1M, Replogle) datasets, combined with competitive downstream classification results, positions scLDM as a practical framework for in silico perturbation experiments that could reduce reliance on expensive wet-lab screens. Key limitations include the task-dependent choice between Gaussian and Negative Binomial likelihoods (not learned end-to-end) and the restriction to the transcriptomic modality; extending MCAB to multi-omics data and learning the likelihood family jointly remain open directions.
