## Problem

Sampling in masked diffusion language models (dLLMs) relies on handcrafted heuristics such as confidence thresholding (Fast-dLLM) to decide which tokens to unmask at each step. These heuristics require manual tuning, are sensitive to sampling configuration, and degrade significantly outside the semi-autoregressive regime, raising the question of whether effective unmasking strategies can be learned rather than designed by hand.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261863/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy network receives three inputs at each diffusion step: the vector of per-position token confidences (the maximum probability over the vocabulary), a binary mask vector indicating which positions remain masked, and the current time step index. It outputs a vector of Bernoulli unmasking probabilities, one per position, from which a binary unmasking decision is sampled. The policy is a single-layer transformer with fewer than 0.01% of the base dLLM's parameters (e.g., ~300K vs. 8B for LLaDA), adding negligible inference overhead. The frozen dLLM serves as the environment, so the policy operates purely on confidence signals without access to token embeddings or hidden states.

## Method

The paper formalizes dLLM sampling as a Markov decision process where the frozen dLLM is the environment and a lightweight single-layer transformer (with adaptive layer normalization for conditioning on time and mask) is the policy. Unmasking decisions are modeled with independent Bernoulli likelihoods over positions, giving a closed-form policy probability that avoids the approximations needed for post-training dLLMs. Training uses GRPO with a multiplicative reward combining task-specific correctness (e.g., binary math answer match) with an exponential speed penalty (1 − (T−t̂)/T)^α, which the authors show is critical to prevent reward hacking compared to an additive alternative. The policy is trained from scratch on ~15K samples from GSM8K/MATH with the dLLM at greedy decoding (τ=0), and at test time the accuracy–efficiency trade-off is controlled via a policy temperature τ_π and a Bernoulli probability scaling factor β, enabling smooth Pareto frontier navigation without retraining.

## Results

In the semi-autoregressive (BL=32) regime, learned RL policies match Fast-dLLM's accuracy-efficiency Pareto frontier on both GSM8K and MATH-500 while outperforming random and high-confidence baselines. In the full-diffusion (BL=256) regime, policies achieve ~50% accuracy at ~12 NFEs on GSM8K, compared to ≤30% for all heuristic methods regardless of semi-AR use. With expert steering, full-diffusion policies reach ~80% on GSM8K and ~35% on MATH-500 at mid-to-high NFEs, nearly closing the gap to the best semi-AR accuracy. In the low-NFE (~10) regime, the α=10 policy achieves ~38% accuracy on GSM8K versus ~18% for Fast-dLLM, and at ~25 NFEs on MATH-500 the policy reaches 20% versus Fast-dLLM's 10%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025), the primary confidence-thresholding baseline, learned policies match performance in the semi-AR regime (BL=32) but clearly outperform it in the full-diffusion regime (BL=256), where Fast-dLLM degrades to ≤30% accuracy at low NFEs while policies reach ~50%. The α=10 policy specifically beats Fast-dLLM in the low-NFE regime (~38% vs. ~18% on GSM8K), and test-time probability scaling (β) further extends the policy's advantage at lower NFEs (20% vs. 10% on MATH-500 at ~25 NFEs). Against high-confidence unmasking (Chang et al., 2022, MaskGIT-style), policies consistently outperform across all settings, as the fixed-K strategy cannot adapt the number of unmasked tokens per step. The additional baselines from Kim et al. (2025, ICML) and Ben-Hamu et al. (2025, NeurIPS), shown in Figure 11, are also outperformed by the learned policies in the full-diffusion setting, confirming that the advantage is not specific to Fast-dLLM's thresholding mechanism but reflects a broader limitation of handcrafted heuristics outside the semi-AR regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the masked diffusion model (MDM) generation pipeline and the recent wave of confidence-based heuristic samplers (Fast-dLLM, MaskGIT-style high-confidence unmasking) that select which tokens to unmask at each diffusion step. It departs from this heuristic tradition by formalizing unmasking as a Markov decision process and training a lightweight single-layer transformer policy via GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), treating the frozen dLLM as the environment rather than the policy. This distinguishes it from concurrent RL post-training works (d1, DiffuCoder, DCOLT, DiFFPO) that update the base model's weights to improve reasoning; here the dLLM is entirely unchanged and only a ~300K-parameter unmasking network is learned. The Bernoulli action formulation also differs from fixed-K unmasking in DCOLT or threshold-prediction in DiFFPO, allowing a variable number of tokens to be unmasked per step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a tiny learned policy can match state-of-the-art heuristics in semi-AR generation and, more importantly, substantially outperform them in the full-diffusion regime where confidence-based methods degrade below random unmasking—addressing a key practical limitation of current dLLM inference. Transferability across models (LLaDA→Dream), domains (math→code), and sequence lengths (256→512) suggests the learned policies are not overfit to a single configuration. However, controllability remains a significant open problem: varying the training-time penalty α produces non-monotonic, coarse-grained trade-offs, and expert steering introduces training instability. Future work on test-time control (β-scaling, policy temperature), remasking, and extension to multimodal diffusion models are natural next steps.
