## Problem

Robust reinforcement learning requires solving an inner minimization over a discretized uncertainty set at every state-action update, incurring O(|U|) query complexity that becomes the dominant bottleneck as uncertainty resolution grows. The paper asks whether quantum superposition over environment candidates can accelerate this worst-case search, and answers affirmatively with a quadratic speed-up.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262336/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

QRIM takes a state-action pair (s,a), a current value function V(s'), and N discretized candidate transition-reward pairs {(P_i, r_i)} accessed through quantum oracles (U_P, U_R, U_V) that prepare coherent superpositions over next states. It outputs a single robust Bellman backup Q^rob(s,a;V) and the worst-case index, which plug directly into standard value-based or policy-gradient update rules without modifying the outer RL pipeline. Rewards must be bounded (Assumption 4.1) so that one-step returns can be normalized to [0,1] for amplitude encoding.

## Method

QRIM encodes each candidate's expected one-step return g_i into an ancilla amplitude via a state-preparation unitary G_i(s,a) built from the quantum-accessible environment oracles, then composes Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE) with Quantum Minimum Finding (QMF, a Grover-style search) to identify the worst-case index in O(√N) coherent queries rather than O(N) classical scans. A coherent comparator U_LESS compares pairs of candidates by running QAE on both and phase-flipping the smaller, which QMF uses as its marking oracle; a final QAE at higher precision re-estimates the winner's value. The total query complexity is Õ(√N · ε⁻¹ · log(1/δ)), a quadratic improvement over the classical O(N · ε⁻²) Monte Carlo baseline in both the uncertainty size N and the precision parameter ε. The outer learning loop—whether tabular Q-learning or PPO—is entirely unchanged, making QRIM a drop-in replacement for the inner minimization step.

## Results

On FrozenLake-8×8, QRIM achieves a mean discounted return of 0.98 on both train and interpolation sets, and -0.37 on extrapolation, compared to -0.79 for Classic RL and -0.45 for Classic RRL on extrapolation. On CartPole Swingup, QRIM reaches 80.06 on interpolation and 58.06 on extrapolation, versus 26.65 (Classic RL) and 39.86 (Classic RRL) on extrapolation. In terms of query efficiency, QRIM requires only 35.9% of the oracle calls of Classical RRL on FrozenLake (4.11M vs. 11.45M) and 20.5% on CartPole (5.65M vs. 27.51M), confirming the theoretical O(√N) vs. O(N) scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QRIM is compared against three baselines: Classic RL (non-robust training at the nominal model), Classic RRL (robust targets via exhaustive O(N) scan over the uncertainty grid), and BAI-RRL (best-arm-identification adaptive sampling, the strongest classical sub-linear-query baseline). On extrapolation, QRIM outperforms Classic RL by a wide margin (e.g., -0.37 vs. -0.79 on FrozenLake; 58.06 vs. 26.65 on CartPole), confirming that robust training is essential for out-of-distribution generalization. Against Classic RRL, QRIM matches or slightly exceeds robustness (e.g., -0.37 vs. -0.45 on FrozenLake extrapolation; 58.06 vs. 39.86 on CartPole extrapolation) while reducing cumulative oracle calls by 64.1% and 79.5% respectively. Against BAI-RRL on FrozenLake, QRIM reduces queries to 35.9% of Classical RRL (vs. 84.5% for BAI) and achieves the best extrapolation return (-0.37 vs. -0.49 for BAI and -0.45 for Classical RRL). The asymptotic scaling experiments confirm the predicted O(√N) slope for QRIM versus O(N) for classical scans, and real-hardware execution on IBM's 127-qubit Eagle processor preserves the robustness trend with only a 5% increase in query count due to noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper composes two well-studied quantum primitives—quantum amplitude estimation (Brassard et al., 2000) and quantum minimum finding (Durr & Hoyer, 1996)—within the quantum-accessible environment framework of Wang et al. (2021) to target a specific bottleneck in robust RL: the O(|U|) exhaustive scan over uncertainty candidates in the inner minimization of the robust Bellman operator. It departs from prior quantum RL work (PQC-based policies, quantum planning oracles) by explicitly addressing the Max-Min structure of rectangular robust MDPs rather than accelerating generic value estimation or policy parameterization. Among classical RRL methods (RARL, EPOpt, distributionally robust MDPs with Wasserstein/TV ambiguity), all still require linear scans over fine-grained uncertainty grids; QRIM is positioned as the first method to achieve the optimal O(√|U|) sub-linear scaling for this discrete, evaluation-only inner search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical template showing that the inner minimization bottleneck in robust RL is an unstructured search problem amenable to Grover-style quadratic speedup, with matching lower bounds and a drop-in interface for both value-based and policy-gradient learners. However, practical impact is currently constrained: the quantum-accessible environment oracle is an idealized assumption, NISQ hardware cannot execute the deep arithmetic circuits needed for continuous-control oracles (fidelity drops below 10⁻⁵ for ~2000-gate circuits), and classical state-vector emulation offers no wall-clock advantage. The real-hardware validation on IBM's 127-qubit Eagle processor (2 qubits, depth 20) is encouraging but limited to a toy tabular setting. Open questions include whether fault-tolerant architectures will make the oracle assumption realistic for non-trivial environments, and whether the quantum advantage persists when the per-call gate-depth overhead is accounted for in wall-clock time.
