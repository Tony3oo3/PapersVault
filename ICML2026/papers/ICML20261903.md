## Problem

Existing deep-learning approaches for synthetic network generation (autoregressive GNNs, adjacency-matrix diffusion) are computationally prohibitive at scale and tend to overfit observed data while neglecting characteristic structural properties such as sparsity and degree heterogeneity. There is a need for a framework that is both efficient on large single networks and provably preserves key network structure, with accompanying theoretical guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261903/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single observed network as a symmetric zero-diagonal adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n} (edges may be binary, count-valued, or continuous) and produces a synthetic network of the same size n. For the attributed variant (SyNGLER-Attr), node features are jointly embedded alongside structure. Generation is fully node-level: n embeddings are sampled independently, then all n(n−1)/2 edges are generated independently conditioned on the sampled pair, so no sequential or autoregressive decoding is required.

## Method

SyNGLER is a two-stage pipeline that decouples representation learning from generative modeling. Stage 1 fits a likelihood-based latent space model (each edge A_ij | Φ ~ p(· | z_i^T z_j + α_i + α_j + ρ_n)) via projected gradient descent to obtain low-dimensional node embeddings φ_i = (z_i, α_i) ∈ R^{d} with d = r+1. Stage 2 trains a lightweight distribution-free generator on these d-dimensional embeddings—either empirical resampling (SyNG-R) or a score-based diffusion model with an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck forward process and XGBoost/MLP score networks (SyNG-D). At generation time, embeddings are drawn from the generator and edges are produced independently through the fitted latent space model. The key efficiency gain is that diffusion and score-matching operate in the fixed low-dimensional latent space rather than the n²-dimensional adjacency space, and the likelihood-based fitting step is a single convex-constrained optimization rather than iterative deep-network training. The paper provides a KL-divergence decomposition (sparsity error + embedding estimation error + generation error) and proves end-to-end consistency and fixed-order motif density consistency under a sparse regime w_n·n/log n → ∞.

## Results

On four real-world networks (YouTube, Yelp, DBLP, PolBlogs), SyNG-D and SyNG-R achieve the lowest or near-lowest errors across triangle density, clustering coefficient, eigenvalue distribution, and degree centrality. On YouTube, SyNG-D attains Clus. error of 2.28×10⁻² vs. VGAE (8.0×10⁻²), GRAN (10.1×10⁻²), and EDGE (14.4×10⁻²); on Yelp, SyNG-R achieves Clus. error of 0.76×10⁻² vs. VGAE (10.3×10⁻²) and CELL (4.44×10⁻²). GRAN and EDGE run out of memory on the largest dataset (Yelp) on a single RTX 4090 (24 GB), while SyNGLER methods complete training. In the efficiency comparison, SyNG-D attains the lowest e-FLOPs across all network sizes while maintaining the best structural fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyNGLER (both SyNG-D and SyNG-R) outperforms VGAE on nearly all metrics across all four datasets; the gap is most dramatic on eigenvalue and degree centrality (e.g., on PolBlogs, VGAE's Eig. error is 31.1×10⁻² vs. SyNG-D's 2.58×10⁻²). GRAN and EDGE are outperformed on every metric where they complete, and both run out of memory on the Yelp dataset, whereas SyNGLER methods finish training. GraphMaker and mKPGM are consistently the weakest deep/classical baselines, with errors 3–10× higher than SyNGLER on most metrics. CELL is the closest competitor, matching or beating SyNGLER on degree centrality (0.00 on YouTube and PolBlogs) and eigenvalue error on PolBlogs (0.00), but SyNGLER dominates on triangle density and clustering coefficient. BTER achieves zero degree-centrality error on YouTube and PolBlogs but is outperformed on triangle density and clustering. SyNG-D(MLP) is competitive on eigenvalue preservation (0.38×10⁻² on YouTube) but lags SyNG-D and SyNG-R on triangle and clustering metrics, suggesting the XGBoost-based score estimator in SyNG-D is more effective for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

SyNGLER builds on the classical latent space network model framework (Hoff et al., 2002; Ma et al., 2020), which represents networks via low-dimensional node embeddings fitted by maximum likelihood, and combines it with score-based generative modeling via SDEs (Song et al., 2021) applied in the learned embedding space rather than the high-dimensional adjacency-matrix space. This departs from the dominant deep-learning paradigm for graph generation—autoregressive GNNs (GRAN, GraphRNN), discrete/continuous diffusion on adjacency matrices (EDGE, DiGress), and latent diffusion with encoder-decoder architectures (GraphMaker, latent-diffusion for molecules)—by separating representation learning (likelihood-based, interpretable) from generative modeling (lightweight resampling or score matching in a low-dimensional space). The paper also connects the latent space model to classical generative models (Erdős-Rényi, Chung-Lu, SBM, mixed-membership block models), showing it is a unifying framework, and provides the first KL-divergence decomposition and fixed-order motif consistency guarantees for a latent-embedding-based graph generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for large-scale network synthesis: SyNG-D achieves the lowest computational cost across all tested settings while matching or exceeding the structural fidelity of much heavier baselines (GRAN and EDGE run out of memory on the ~1M-node Yelp subgraph), and the theoretical guarantees (KL divergence decomposition, motif density consistency) fill a gap that most deep graph generators lack. Key limitations include the absence of formal privacy guarantees, the restriction to single-network generation from one observed graph (rather than learning a distribution over many small graphs), and the two-stage design that precludes end-to-end joint optimization. Open directions flagged by the authors include conditional/attributed generation, directed and dynamic networks, multiplex/multilayer structures, and rigorous privacy-preserving release mechanisms.
