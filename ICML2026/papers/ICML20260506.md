## Problem

Post-training quantization of Vision-Language Models causes severe accuracy degradation due to heterogeneous multimodal distributions, while quantization-aware training (QAT) for VLMs remains largely unexplored. Naively combining knowledge distillation with QAT is non-trivial because teacher predictions vary in reliability and a quantized student has limited representational capacity to absorb all teacher information. The paper frames this as an Information Bottleneck capacity-allocation problem: the quantized student must selectively retain high-value teacher knowledge while discarding noisy or redundant signals under a hard bit-width constraint.

## Input / Output

The training pipeline takes a frozen teacher VLM (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-13B or Qwen2-VL-7B) and a quantized student (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B or Qwen2-VL-2B), both processing the same image–text question pairs from ShareGPT4V (1.3M samples). Only the LLM decoder linear layers are quantized (symmetric, group size 128) while the vision encoder and output head remain in BF16. The output is a deployable INT4/INT8 student model with learned per-group scales, producing standard text responses at 3× throughput and 54% lower memory than the BF16 baseline.

## Method

GRACE unifies three complementary mechanisms under an Information Bottleneck formulation. First, Confidence-Gated Decoupled KD (GDKD) splits the distillation loss into target-class and non-target-class KL terms, then weights each token by an exponential function of the teacher's normalized entropy (g_i = exp(−h̃_i)), suppressing unreliable high-entropy supervision. Second, Relational CKA (RCKA) computes centered kernel alignment on row-wise ℓ₂-normalized Gram matrices of visual tokens (text tokens excluded) at the penultimate LLM layer, transferring inter-token relational structure rather than point-wise features and bypassing dimension-matching requirements. Third, an adaptive IB controller updates the distillation weight β via projected dual ascent with EMA smoothing on the gated loss, automatically strengthening distillation when the student fails to retain teacher knowledge and relaxing it once the information-preservation budget τ is met. For low-bit training, group-wise learned step-size quantization (LSQ-style, log-parameterized scales initialized by MSE grid search) is jointly optimized with weights under the total loss L_CE + β·L_GDKD + ω·L_RCKA.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, GRACE achieves 69.0% average accuracy in BF16 (vs. 66.5% baseline, 69.1% 13B teacher) and 67.2% under INT4 quantization, surpassing the BF16 baseline by 0.7%. On Qwen2-VL-2B, the INT4 model reaches 68.0% average (vs. 64.0% BF16 baseline, 71.7% 7B teacher), outperforming all PTQ baselines by 4.0–7.6 points. With real INT4 kernels (TinyChat), GRACE delivers 3.03× throughput (106.68 vs. 35.26 tokens/s) and 2.18× peak GPU memory reduction (6,572 MB vs. 14,302 MB) on A100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRACE INT4 (67.2% avg) outperforms all INT4 baselines: RTN (65.2%, +2.0%), AWQ (65.6%, +1.6%), and vanilla QAT (65.9%, +1.3%), while also exceeding the BF16 7B baseline (66.5%) by 0.7%. In the KD comparison (Table 1), GRACE BF16 (69.0%) beats MoVE-KD-v1.1 (68.5%) and HAWAII (68.5%) by 0.5% absolute, with particularly large gains on TextVQA (+3.2% over MoVE-KD) and ScienceQA (+2.4% over MoVE-KD). On Qwen2-VL-2B INT4, GRACE (68.0%) surpasses SPEED-Q (62.3%, +5.7%), MBQ (61.0%, +7.0%), GPTQ (60.9%, +7.1%), AWQ (60.4%, +7.6%), and RTN (61.1%, +6.9%). The 8-bit GRACE model (68.3%) also exceeds the BF16 baseline, and the gap between 8-bit and 4-bit under GRACE is only 1.1%, indicating robust information preservation under aggressive quantization.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRACE unifies two previously separate lines of work—knowledge distillation and quantization-aware training—under a single Information Bottleneck formulation specifically for VLMs. It builds directly on Decoupled KD (Zhao et al., 2022) for the TCKD/NCKD loss decomposition, LSQ (Esser et al., 2019) for learned step-size quantization, and CKA (Kornblith et al., 2019) for relational alignment, but departs from prior work in three ways: (i) confidence gating filters unreliable teacher supervision using entropy as a proxy (motivated by Fano's inequality), (ii) RCKA transfers intra-sample visual-token relational structure rather than batch-level inter-sample relationships as in RKD (Park et al., 2019), and (iii) an adaptive dual-ascent controller dynamically balances distillation strength against the quantization-imposed capacity constraint, replacing fixed hyperparameter tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: INT4 VLMs that surpass their BF16 baselines with 3× throughput and 54% memory reduction open the door to deploying capable multimodal models on edge and mobile hardware. The IB framing provides a principled explanation for why joint KD+QAT outperforms either alone, and the ablations confirm that confidence gating and relational alignment contribute complementary, non-redundant gains. Key limitations remain: the framework is restricted to weight-only quantization (activation quantization is unexplored), requires access to a stronger teacher during training, and has only been validated on image-text VLMs, leaving video-language and audio-language architectures untested. Reducing teacher dependency and extending to weight-activation quantization are the most pressing open questions.
