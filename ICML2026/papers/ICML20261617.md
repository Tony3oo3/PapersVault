## Problem

Vision State Space Models serialize 2D images into 1D sequences using a fixed scan order (raster, serpentine, etc.), which implicitly defines spatial adjacency for the recurrent kernel. This paper identifies scan order as a previously underexplored inductive bias: fixed paths distort local neighborhood structure, fragment objects whose extent is misaligned with the scan direction, and degrade sharply under in-plane rotations due to global reindexing and boundary padding. The gap is that scan order has been treated as a mere implementation detail rather than a first-class architectural choice governing the alignment between sequence adjacency and true 2D geometry.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet-1K classification; 1280×800 for COCO detection/segmentation), patchifies them, and processes the resulting 2D feature grids through a four-stage hierarchical backbone. Unlike conventional Vision SSMs that flatten the grid along a single linear path, PRIS-Mamba decomposes each feature map into concentric rings by Euclidean distance from the image center, so the 'sequence' is a set of short ring loops plus a brief radial chain rather than one long 1D sequence. Outputs are class labels, bounding boxes, and instance segmentation masks, with the ring-based traversal preserving the original spatial dimensions (H×W×C) at every stage via 1×1 write-back projections.

## Method

PRIS-Mamba replaces fixed-path serialization with a two-level ring-based traversal: (1) pixels are grouped into concentric rings of width Δr, and within each ring a selective SSM runs along a closed loop with alternating clockwise/counterclockwise direction across successive rings, producing a single ring-level descriptor via mean aggregation; (2) a second short selective SSM propagates context radially from the innermost to the outermost ring, composing cross-ring dependencies in O(R*) steps where R* ≪ √(HW). Because ring membership is invariant under in-plane rotation (only the starting angle shifts), the traversal is inherently rotation-stable without polar remapping or rotation-specific training. A Partial Channel Filtering (PCF) module computes per-channel salience via global average pooling and hard-thresholds at the mean, routing only high-signal channels through the recurrent ring pathway while bypassing the rest through a lightweight residual branch, reducing FLOPs by ~15% while simultaneously improving accuracy. The full module is embedded in a VMamba-style four-stage backbone with downsampling between stages, and features are written back to the grid via a 1×1 projection with residual fusion.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (224×224), PRIS-Mamba achieves 84.5% Top-1 accuracy with 3.9G FLOPs and 2,854 img/s throughput on an A100 GPU, outperforming VMamba (82.6%, 5.6G, 1,686 img/s) by +1.9 points while using ~30% fewer FLOPs and ~1.7× higher throughput. On MS COCO (1× schedule, 1280×800), PRIS-Mamba attains 48.9 AP_box and 43.2 AP_mask with 235G FLOPs, surpassing VMamba (46.5/42.1 at 262G) by +2.4/+1.1 AP and GroupMamba (47.6/42.9 at 279G) by +1.3/+0.3 AP with 10–19% fewer FLOPs. Under 30°/60° in-plane rotation, PRIS-Mamba remains essentially flat (84.5→84.3/84.4) while all fixed-path baselines degrade by 1–2 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRIS-Mamba outperforms all 15 compared Vision-SSM baselines on ImageNet-1K Top-1 accuracy. The largest margin is over VMamba (+1.9 pp, 84.5 vs 82.6) and Vim (+4.0 pp, 84.5 vs 80.5), while it also beats the closest competitors SiMBA (+0.5 pp), GroupMamba (+0.6 pp), VSSD (+0.8 pp), and SparX-Mamba/DefMamba (+1.0 pp) — all while using 13–44% fewer FLOPs. On MS COCO detection/segmentation, PRIS-Mamba leads VMamba by +2.4 AP_box and +1.1 AP_mask, GroupMamba by +1.3/+0.3, and DefMamba by +1.4/+0.4, with 10–19% lower FLOPs. The only baseline that approaches PRIS-Mamba's accuracy is SiMBA (84.0% at 5.0G FLOPs), but PRIS-Mamba still wins by +0.5 pp with 22% fewer FLOPs and substantially higher throughput (2,854 vs unreported img/s). Under rotation stress (30°/60°), PRIS-Mamba degrades by ≤0.2 pp whereas every fixed-path baseline drops 1.0–2.1 pp, confirming that the ring-scan design provides a structural robustness advantage that no amount of path stacking (up to 4 paths, S21) can replicate.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRIS-Mamba builds directly on the selective SSM mechanism from Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2024) and the hierarchical four-stage backbone of VMamba (Liu et al., 2024), replacing VMamba's 2D Selective Scan (SS2D) with a concentric-ring traversal. The key conceptual departure is elevating scan order from an implementation detail to a first-class inductive bias: prior Vision SSMs (Vim, ZigMa, FractalMamba, PlainMamba, DefMamba) all rely on fixed linear or zigzag paths that the paper shows are brittle under geometric transformations. The Partial Channel Filtering mechanism is positioned as a hard-routing alternative to soft channel-attention modules (SE, CBAM), arguing that when the downstream operator is recurrent, selective channel forwarding is more effective than reweighting all channels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that scan-order design is a core representational choice in Vision SSMs with measurable consequences for accuracy, efficiency, and geometric robustness, providing a principled alternative (ring scan) that achieves near-perfect rotation invariance without polar remapping or rotation-specific training. Practical limitations remain: the fixed image center and discrete ring width are suboptimal for off-center subjects or extreme aspect ratios, and severe rotations (≥75°) with large zero-padding still degrade all models substantially. Open questions include learning ring origins and widths, content-adaptive ring partitions, and extending the traversal to video, 3D volumes, and object-aware settings.
