## Problem

In correctness-sensitive long-context QA, LLMs frequently hallucinate even when accurate reference documents are provided, and existing approaches (RAG, post-hoc attribution, citation fine-tuning) treat citations as probabilistic byproducts of generation rather than foundational constraints. This decoupling creates an irreversible failure mode: once the model commits to an incorrect reasoning path during autoregressive decoding, downstream citation mechanisms cannot retroactively correct the error. The paper addresses how to enforce sentence-level citations as hard constraints directly within the decoding loop while preserving the model's linguistic fluency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user query and a reference document pre-segmented into indexed sentences, operating on a frozen LLM without any weight updates. Offline, the document is decomposed into a structured pool of (Prefix, Tail) tuples—semantic anchors paired with atomic facts—each explicitly mapped to its source sentence ID. The output is a long-form response in which unconstrained LLM tokens are interleaved with verified factual units, each immediately followed by an inline citation span (e.g., [104-105]) referencing the supporting sentence indices.

## Method

Guidance is a training-free, two-phase framework that intervenes directly in the LLM's autoregressive decoding loop, treating citation as a primary constraint rather than a post-hoc annotation. In the offline phase, an LLM-based pipeline decomposes each document sentence into multiple (Prefix, Tail) tuples, where the Prefix encodes the subject-relation context and the Tail is a verifiable atomic entity, date, or metric, all indexed to their source sentence. During online decoding, a three-step Lookahead-Verify-Inject cycle runs at each token: hybrid retrieval (dense embedding recall plus cross-encoder reranking) combined with entity-aware alignment (NER intersection and keyword Jaccard similarity) identifies a candidate Prefix matching the current context; a KV-cache-isolated lookahead simulation generates N future tokens and checks type consistency against the candidate Tail via a hierarchical NER compatibility matrix and WordNet semantic categorization; and upon verification, the Tail is injected through token-level soft-forcing that requires each token to satisfy both a top-K rank and a minimum probability threshold, aborting immediately on any mismatch to preserve fluency. This design ensures every factual claim is grounded in source text before full generation while leaving non-factual content to the model's natural distribution.

## Results

On the LongBench-Cite benchmark, Guidance improves average Citation F1 by 20.0% for Mistral-7B (35.8→43.0), 26.5% for Llama-3.1-8B (39.6→50.1), and 5.4% for Qwen3-8B (53.3→56.2) in the prompt-only setting. When applied on top of fine-tuned citation models, Guidance further boosts LongCite-8B from 72.9 to 74.4 and SelfCite-8B from 76.7 to 78.1 in Citation F1. Notably, SelfCite+Guidance achieves a superior F1 of 78.1 in 11.2 hours versus SelfCite+BoN's 77.1 in 60.1 hours, a 5× speedup. Guidance also reduces average citation length (e.g., 100→83 tokens on Llama-3.1-8B) while simultaneously increasing F1, indicating more precise localization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NEST (Li et al., 2024), a concurrent decoding-time framework using token-level kNN retrieval and speculative decoding, Guidance paired with SelfCite-8B achieves 78.1 Citation F1 versus NEST's 68.6, a 9.5-point absolute gain, while also producing shorter citations (106 vs. 124 tokens). Against LongCite-8B (Zhang et al., 2025), a fine-tuned model trained on a massive SFT citation dataset, Guidance adds +1.5 points of F1 (72.9→74.4), showing that decoding-time correction complements supervised format learning by fixing residual hallucinations. Against SelfCite-8B (Chuang et al., 2025), which uses reward-based self-alignment, Guidance yields +1.4 points (76.7→78.1). Critically, SelfCite+Guidance (78.1 F1, 11.2 h) outperforms SelfCite+BoN (77.1 F1, 60.1 h) on both quality and speed, demonstrating that single-pass decoding-time correction is far more efficient than multi-candidate sampling and reranking for long-context citation tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant post-hoc attribution paradigm (WebGPT, RARR) and the fine-tuning-based citation models (LongCite, SelfCite) by treating citations as a hard constraint enforced during autoregressive decoding rather than a probabilistic byproduct. Conceptually, it repurposes ideas from atomic fact decomposition (Min et al., 2023) and decontextualization (Choi et al., 2021) to build a structured Prefix-Tail pool, then combines this with constrained decoding techniques (NEST, RICHES) — but replaces their exact-substring or kNN matching with a semantic verification pipeline (NER type consistency + WordNet categorization + token-level soft-forcing). The key architectural departure from NEST and similar decoding-time methods is the lookahead simulation with KV-cache isolation, which allows the system to verify the model's *intent* before committing to an injection, rather than blindly forcing retrieved spans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of Guidance makes it immediately applicable to any existing LLM without retraining, and the 5× speedup over Best-of-N sampling for comparable citation quality addresses a real computational bottleneck in high-stakes attribution. However, the approach inherits the fundamental limitation that citation quality is bounded by source document quality, and the 1.3×–3× latency overhead plus ~7-hour preprocessing for a full benchmark may be prohibitive in latency-sensitive or streaming settings. Open questions include whether the Prefix-Tail decomposition generalizes to multi-hop reasoning or tasks requiring synthesis across non-adjacent sentences, and whether the semantic type-consistency checks (NER + WordNet) scale gracefully to domains with highly specialized ontologies beyond the five coarse classes tested here.
