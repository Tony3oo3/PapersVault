## Problem

Applying Sparse Autoencoder (SAE)-based mechanistic interpretability to Vision Transformers faces two practical bottlenecks: (1) object-centric training datasets like ImageNet provide limited control over concept coverage across the full spectrum of visual abstraction levels, and (2) interpreting the learned SAE features in vision remains subjective and non-scalable, relying on manual summarization of top-activating images. This paper addresses both gaps with a data-centric toolbox rather than proposing yet another SAE architecture variant.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (a curated 64K probing suite spanning four abstraction levels: Primitive, Intermediate, Object, Scene) and the residual stream activations (CLS and image tokens separately) from each layer of a pretrained CLIP-ViT-B-32. It produces a directed acyclic concept circuit graph where nodes are human-readable concepts and edges are weighted by interventional causal effects, per-feature concept labels assigned via Soft-WPMI in CLIP's vision-language embedding space, pixel-level saliency maps localizing each concept, steered predictions after concept activation editing, and structured failure-mode diagnoses comparing correct vs. incorrect prediction circuits.

## Method

ViSAE is a three-component toolbox. First, a neuroscience-motivated probing suite of 64K images (pruned from 121K via CLIP cosine-similarity deduplication) and a 16K visually grounded concept vocabulary (generated by GPT-5 from the images themselves, organized into four abstraction levels mirroring the human visual cortex hierarchy) provides 20× higher concept coverage efficiency than ImageNet. Second, a two-stage tracing algorithm operates: top-down concept reading maps each SAE decoder column to the best-matching concept via Soft Weighted Point-wise Mutual Information computed over CLIP embedding similarities, eliminating manual summarization; bottom-up circuit tracing estimates cross-layer causal influence by counterfactual activation patching (zeroing a source concept's activation and measuring the interventional effect on downstream concept activations and the final prediction), yielding a directed concept interaction graph. Third, the toolbox supports auditing (tracing decision pathways, localizing concepts on pixels, diagnosing failure modes) and steering (suppressing or amplifying specific concept activations to correct spurious correlations, improving WaterBirds worst-group accuracy by 48.2%). SAEs are BatchTopK-SAEs with 8× expansion and L0=128, trained unsupervised on the probing suite's ViT activations.

## Results

On the WaterBirds dataset, ViSAE's concept-removal steering achieves 98.5% worst-group accuracy (waterbirds on land), a +48.2% absolute gain over the unsteered baseline (50.3%) and outperforming the strongest prior method COAR (83.0%) by 15.5 points. For SAE interpretation, the proposed 16K concept set yields 54.1% top-30 interpretation accuracy, a 28.7% absolute improvement over the best existing concept set (LAION-15K at 25.4%). The probing suite achieves 20× higher concept coverage efficiency (1.26%/1K images) compared to ImageNet (0.06%/1K images), and concept localization improves Point Game score by 3.7 points over Chefer et al. on VOC2007.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On WaterBirds steering, ViSAE (98.5% worst-group) decisively outperforms all six baselines: COAR (83.0%, +15.5 pts), PCBM (74.7%, +23.8 pts), DN-CBM (71.3%, +27.2 pts), SpLiCE (60.0%, +38.5 pts), CBM (51.8%, +46.7 pts), and Joseph et al. (24.6%, +73.9 pts). The bidirectional control is demonstrated by the 'Enhance' row, where amplifying spurious concepts drops worst-group accuracy to 5.3% (−45.0 pts), confirming precise concept-level intervention. For interpretation accuracy (Table 4), the proposed 16K concept set achieves 54.1% top-30 coverage, beating LAION-15K (25.4%), Google-20K (16.7%), and LaBo-ImageNet (20.3%) by 28.7, 37.4, and 33.8 points respectively. Against the MLLM-based summarization baseline (Qwen3.5-VL-9B), ViSAE achieves comparable IoU (0.432 vs. 0.438) with lower variance (±0.215 vs. ±0.249) and 20× faster runtime (5 min vs. 1 h 40 min per layer). For pixel-level concept localization on VOC2007, ViSAE improves Point Game from 41.9 to 45.0 and Attribution Localization from 32.3 to 36.0 over Chefer et al.'s attention-based method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability pipeline from language models (SAEs + LLM summarization) to vision Transformers, but deliberately departs from the dominant trend of proposing new SAE architectures. Instead, it takes a data-centric perspective: a neuroscience-motivated probing suite (64K images, 16K concepts organized by visual abstraction level) for SAE training, an automated CLIP-embedding-based concept reading method (Soft-WPMI) replacing subjective human summarization, and a bottom-up causal circuit tracing algorithm built on activation patching. The conceptual lineage draws on concept-based interpretability (TCAV, Network Dissection, CBMs) for the vocabulary and intervention ideas, and on the hierarchical organization of the human visual cortex for the data design, but the unified 'concept circuit' framing combining top-down reading with bottom-up causal tracing in a single vision-specific toolbox is the paper's distinctive contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ViSAE provides practical infrastructure that lowers the barrier to mechanistic interpretability of ViTs: the probing suite and auto-interpretation method are reusable, and the steering results (48.2% worst-group gain on WaterBirds) demonstrate that concept-level interventions can meaningfully correct spurious correlations without retraining. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include feature absorption/composition in SAEs (no guaranteed one-to-one feature-to-concept mapping), dependence on CLIP's embedding space for concept matching, and evaluation restricted to CLIP-style ViTs on natural images. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger vision-language models, non-natural-image domains, and whether the concept circuit abstraction remains faithful at greater model scale.
