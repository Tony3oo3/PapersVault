## Problem

Existing optimizers capture only one implicit-bias geometry: Muon enforces spectral control via orthogonalization but lacks coordinate-wise normalization, while Lion enforces ℓ∞-style coordinate control via sign but does not enforce spectral structure. The paper asks whether both biases can be combined in a single update, and addresses the practical issue that Muon fine-tuning of AdamW-pretrained checkpoints suffers from optimizer mismatch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264781/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on matrix-shaped parameters X ∈ R^{d1×d2} (d1 ≥ d2), consuming a mini-batch gradient at each step and producing an updated weight matrix. It requires only momentum-level optimizer state (a single momentum buffer M_t), avoiding the second-moment accumulators of Adam-family methods. The update signal is a Lion-style Nesterov mix of the current gradient and momentum, which is then orthogonalized and sign-projected before an optional RMS alignment scales the effective step size to match Adam's empirical RMS of ≈0.2.

## Method

OLion approximates a maximal step over the intersection of the Stiefel set (orthonormal columns) and the uniform-magnitude sign set — a scaled Hadamard ideal — by composing two projections in sequence: first, Newton-Schulz iterations (K=5) orthogonalize the momentum direction to enforce spectral structure, then an entrywise sign operation enforces coordinate-wise ℓ∞ normalization. This sign-after-orthogonalization composition is a one-step alternating-projection approximation to the intractable nonconvex maximization over the Hadamard intersection. The convergence analysis introduces a diagonal-isotropy assumption on the singular-vector geometry of the update signal, under which a cancellation-aware bound (Lemma 4.3) shows the sign direction behaves nearly as an isotropic surrogate, yielding an O(1/√T) gradient-norm guarantee. Empirically, OLion matches or outperforms AdamW, Muon, and Lion across GPT-2/Llama-2 pretraining, SiT image pretraining, and Llama-3.1-8B supervised fine-tuning, while reducing optimizer mismatch relative to Muon on AdamW-pretrained checkpoints.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B supervised fine-tuning (SFT), OLion achieves the best accuracy across all seven math reasoning benchmarks, e.g., 60.04% on GSM8K 0-shot (vs. 57.99% AdamW, 57.24% Muon), 42.14% on NumGLUE 0-shot (vs. 35.04% AdamW, 40.78% Muon), and 46.45% on Aqua 0-shot (vs. 42.52% AdamW, 41.34% Muon). In GPT-2 pretraining (124M–770M) and Llama-2-7B pretraining, OLion converges to lower validation loss than AdamW, Lion, and Muon at matched update magnitudes. In the learning-rate sensitivity study on GPT-2 small, OLion achieves the lowest validation loss at step 10,000 across all four tested learning rates (3×10⁻⁴ to 5×10⁻³), outperforming Muon, Lion, and AdaMuon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OLion outperforms all four baselines across every reported setting. Against AdamW, OLion achieves 2.05–3.93 percentage-point gains on SFT benchmarks (e.g., 60.04% vs. 57.99% on GSM8K 0-shot) and converges faster in GPT-2 and Llama-2 pretraining. Against Muon, OLion wins on all seven SFT benchmarks (e.g., 46.45% vs. 41.34% on Aqua 0-shot, a 5.11-point gap) and maintains lower training/validation loss throughout Llama-2-7B pretraining, while also reducing the pretrain–SFT optimizer mismatch that degrades Muon on AdamW-pretrained checkpoints. Against Lion, OLion achieves lower validation loss in GPT-2 pretraining at all model sizes and in the learning-rate sweep. Against AdaMuon, OLion achieves consistently lower validation loss at step 10,000 across all four learning rates tested (3×10⁻⁴ to 5×10⁻³), demonstrating robustness to step-size selection that the other spectral-geometry optimizers lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

OLion composes two recently introduced optimizer geometries—Muon's Newton-Schulz orthogonalization (spectral control) and Lion's entrywise sign update (ℓ∞ coordinate control)—into a single sign-after-orthogonalization step, framed as an approximate projection onto the intersection of a scaled orthogonal set and a uniform-magnitude sign set (the 'Hadamard ideal'). This builds directly on the norm-geometric reinterpretation of optimizers as steepest descent under different constraints (Bernstein & Newhouse, 2024) and on the stochastic Frank-Wolfe unification of Lion and Muon (Sfyraki et al., 2025), but departs from both by actually composing the two projection operators in one update rather than analyzing them separately. The convergence theory introduces a new diagonal-isotropy assumption on the singular-vector geometry of the update signal, which is verified empirically and shown to hold for random Gaussian matrices, distinguishing it from prior Muon/Lion analyses that do not require such a structural condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OLion is practically significant because it retains Muon's memory efficiency (momentum-level optimizer state only) while adding the coordinate-normalization benefits of sign updates, and it demonstrably reduces the pretrain–finetune optimizer mismatch that plagues Muon when fine-tuning AdamW-pretrained checkpoints—a problem with direct impact on the LLM fine-tuning pipeline. The consistent outperformance across GPT-2 (124M–1.5B), Llama-2-7B pretraining, SiT image pretraining, and Llama-3.1-8B SFT, combined with a wide learning-rate robustness window, suggests the method could become a practical default for memory-constrained large-scale training. Open questions include whether richer multi-step operator-splitting solvers (beyond the single composed projection) can tighten the Hadamard-ideal approximation, and whether the sign update's natural 1-bit compressibility translates into meaningful communication savings in distributed settings.
