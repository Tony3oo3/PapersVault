## Problem

Traditional imitation learning for multi-turn LM agents suffers from covariate shift: as the student policy diverges from the expert over successive turns, it encounters states absent from the training distribution, compounding errors and degrading performance. This paper quantifies that shift empirically (showing it grows monotonically over a trajectory) and proposes a data generation technique that addresses it without requiring full on-policy rollouts or reinforcement learning.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language bug description and an isolated codebase, then interacts with the environment over up to 80 turns (reading/writing files, executing bash commands, running unit tests) to produce a code patch. Training trajectories are multi-turn sequences alternating environment observations and agent actions (thought tokens plus tool calls); they are filtered by unit-test-based rejection sampling and a programmatic repetition filter before supervised fine-tuning, and the student-generated prefix of each trajectory is masked so that only the expert-corrected suffix contributes to the loss.

## Method

The core contribution is On-policy Expert Corrections (OECs), a data generation technique inspired by DAgger that rolls out trajectories with the student model and then switches to the expert model at a randomly sampled turn (uniform over 0–30) to complete the trajectory. At the switch point the trajectory history is reformatted to match the expert's scaffold (system prompt, tool-call syntax) while preserving the on-policy context, so the expert conditions on a state the student actually produces. During fine-tuning the student-generated prefix is masked out and trajectories exhibiting repetitive loops are filtered, ensuring gradients are computed only on high-quality expert corrections. Unlike DAgger, OECs allow full trajectory completion (enabling verifier-based rejection sampling) and achieve O(T) rather than O(T²) expert-token cost by reusing the KV cache across all post-switch turns.

## Results

On SWE-bench Verified, OEC-SWE-32B achieves 40.0% resolution rate (4,961 training samples), outperforming SWE-smith-LM-32B (36.4%, 5,016 samples) by 3.6 points and Skywork-SWE-32B (38.0%, 8,447 samples) by 2.0 points. In the 7B setting, OEC-SWE-7B reaches 20.8% on SWE-bench Verified (8,943 samples), surpassing SWE-smith-LM-7B (15.2%) by 5.6 points and SkyRL-Agent-7B-v0 (14.6%) by 6.2 points. The paper reports a relative 14% improvement over traditional imitation learning (BC) in the 7B setting and 13% in the 32B setting. OEC also reduces expert token usage by ~8% compared to pure BC trajectories (21,116 vs. 22,812 mean expert tokens per trajectory).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SWE-smith-LM (Yang et al., 2025), the strongest BC-based baseline, OEC-SWE-32B wins by 3.6 points on SWE-bench Verified (40.0% vs. 36.4%) with fewer training samples (4,961 vs. 5,016), while OEC-SWE-7B wins by 5.6 points (20.8% vs. 15.2%). Against SWE-Gym (Pan et al., 2024), OEC achieves substantially higher resolution rates in both settings (40.0% vs. 20.6% for 32B; 20.8% vs. 10.6% for 7B), though SWE-Gym uses far fewer training samples (491). Against Skywork-SWE (Zeng et al., 2025), OEC-SWE-32B wins by 2.0 points on Verified (40.0% vs. 38.0%) while using roughly half the training data (4,961 vs. 8,447). Against the RL-based Agent-RLVR (Da et al., 2025), OEC achieves 14.7% vs. 13.7% on SWE-bench Lite under matched conditions (same 7B student, same problem distribution, 2,000 samples). Against SkyRL-Agent-7B-v0 (Cao et al., 2025), OEC-SWE-7B wins by 6.2 points on Verified (20.8% vs. 14.6%). The scaling experiments (Figure 2) further show that OEC consistently outperforms both pure BC and pure on-policy trajectory generation across dataset sizes in both the 7B and 32B settings, with on-policy trajectories actually degrading performance despite rejection sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the DAgger (dataset aggregation) algorithm from classic imitation learning to the multi-turn LM agent setting, replacing DAgger's single-step expert queries with full expert rollouts from a mid-trajectory switch point. This key modification enables the use of verifiable-reward rejection sampling, which is infeasible under standard DAgger, and reduces expert compute from O(T²) to O(T) via KV-cache reuse. The work sits at the intersection of the SWE-agent training literature (SWE-smith, SWE-Gym, Skywork-SWE) and the broader imitation-learning-vs-RL debate for LM agents, explicitly combining on-policy and off-policy data generation principles that prior works treat separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practical, well-ablated evidence that covariate shift is a real and measurable problem in multi-turn LM agent training, and that a simple partially-on-policy data generation scheme can meaningfully close the gap between imitation learning and RL-based approaches. Two negative results carry broader implications: (1) fine-tuning on purely on-policy trajectories—even filtered to successes—can severely degrade performance, and (2) unit-test rejection sampling alone is insufficient to guarantee training data quality, as repetitive-loop trajectories that still pass tests actively harm the model. Open questions include whether OECs generalize beyond SWE to other long-horizon agent domains, whether iterative rounds of OEC generation (to keep the on-policy portion fresh) further improve results, and whether the approach can work without access to a closed-source expert model.
