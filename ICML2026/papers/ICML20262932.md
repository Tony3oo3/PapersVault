## Problem

Online diffusion RL is fundamentally bottlenecked by sampling cost: each policy evaluation requires ~50 sequential denoising steps, consuming the vast majority of training time. Prior work that simply reduces denoising steps or disables CFG degrades sample quality and distorts the training distribution, leading to suboptimal convergence. The paper identifies that few-step distilled models could serve as fast samplers, but naively using a static distilled model causes distributional drift as the RL policy rapidly improves, breaking the on-policy assumption.

## Input / Output

The method takes text prompts as input and produces generated images (SD3.5-Medium, 1024×1024) or videos (Wan 2.1-1.3B) as primary outputs, evaluated via reward models (PaddleOCR, GenEval rules, VBench metrics). Unusually, the training pipeline also yields a co-evolved few-step distilled sampler (4 steps for images, 8 for videos) as a by-product that can be deployed independently for fast inference. During RL, sampling is hybrid: the policy model handles the first 4 (image) or 12 (video) denoising steps, and the distilled sampler completes the remaining 3 or 4 steps, totaling 7 or 16 steps versus 40 or 50 for full sampling.

## Method

DMSampler introduces a dual iterative training scheme that alternates between an RL phase (DiffusionNFT optimizer with hybrid distillation sampling) and a distillation phase (Trajectory Distribution Matching with reward-aware strengthening), switching based on convergence criteria. The key sampling innovation is Late Distillation Sampling: the old policy model handles early denoising steps to establish high-level structure and preserve on-policy semantics, while the few-step distilled model refines later details, achieving near-full-quality samples at 7–16 steps instead of 40–50. The distillation phase uses TDM to align the sampler's trajectory distribution with the updated policy, augmented by a reward-aware loss that reuses high-reward trajectories collected during RL to prevent mode collapse and catastrophic forgetting. Both models are trained via LoRA adapters (rank 32) on a shared frozen backbone, keeping peak VRAM overhead minimal (~1 GB). The framework is optimizer-agnostic, demonstrated to accelerate FlowGRPO and DGPO in addition to DiffusionNFT.

## Results

On the GenEval benchmark (text-to-image), DMSampler achieves an overall score of 0.96, surpassing FlowGRPO (0.95) and DiffusionNFT (0.95) while requiring only 360 GPU hours versus 2000 and 480 hours respectively. On the VBench benchmark (text-to-video), DMSampler reaches a total score of 85.00, exceeding DiffusionNFT (84.74), the 14B-parameter Wan 2.1-14B (83.69), and CausVid (83.88), while delivering a >3× speedup over DiffusionNFT. On the OCR task, DMSampler achieves 0.97 image OCR accuracy and 0.50 video OCR accuracy with 7 and 16 sampling steps respectively, compared to 40 and 50 steps for full diffusion sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMSampler outperforms both primary RL baselines: on GenEval it edges out FlowGRPO (0.96 vs. 0.95) and DiffusionNFT (0.96 vs. 0.95) while reducing training cost from >1000 and 480 GPU hours to 360 hours. On VBench, DMSampler (85.00) beats DiffusionNFT (84.74) by 0.26 points and surpasses the 14B-parameter Wan 2.1-14B (83.69) and CausVid (83.88) by 1.31 and 1.12 points respectively. Against non-RL baselines, DMSampler's 0.96 GenEval score exceeds GPT-4o (0.84), Qwen-Image (0.91), FLUX.1-Dev (0.66), and SD3.5-L (0.71). In the distillation-model comparison on VBench (Table 10), DMSampler's co-evolved sampler (84.21) outperforms CausVid (82.88), Self-Forcing (83.80), BLADE (83.38), and Reward Forcing (84.13). DMSampler also generalizes as a plug-in accelerator: when attached to FlowGRPO it improves GenEval from 0.95 to 0.96 while cutting cost from >1000 to 400 GPU hours, and when attached to DGPO it maintains the 0.97 score while reducing cost from 240 to 192 GPU hours.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two established lines: online diffusion RL optimizers (primarily DiffusionNFT, with FlowGRPO and DGPO as alternatives) and few-step diffusion distillation (specifically Trajectory Distribution Matching). The key departure is treating the distilled model not as a static inference accelerator but as a co-evolving component within the RL loop—periodically re-distilled from the updated policy via a dual iterative training scheme. Prior diffusion RL work is fundamentally bottlenecked by the ~50-step sampling cost per rollout, and prior distillation work produces fixed samplers that cannot track a rapidly improving policy; DMSampler bridges this gap with hybrid late-distillation sampling (policy handles early denoising, distilled model handles late refinement) and a reward-aware distillation objective that anchors compression to high-reward trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The order-of-magnitude reduction in rollout cost (e.g., 160→25 GPU hours for image OCR, 512→234 for video OCR) makes large-scale diffusion RL substantially more accessible, and the framework generalizes as a plug-in sampler to multiple RL optimizers rather than being tied to one. The co-evolved distillation model also emerges as a competitive few-step generator in its own right, outperforming standalone distillation methods like CausVid and Self-Forcing on VBench. Open questions include whether the added pipeline complexity (two alternating phases, convergence-based switching, a fake score model) is justified at all scales, whether the approach extends to human-preference rewards beyond automatic metrics, and how reward misspecification interacts with the reward-aware distillation mechanism.
