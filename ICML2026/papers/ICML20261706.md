## Problem

Flow matching and diffusion-based generative planners can produce diverse, high-quality trajectories but offer no formal guarantees that generated trajectories satisfy state constraints (safety), action constraints (admissibility), or dynamic consistency (physical realizability). Existing constraint-aware methods either enforce only state constraints via soft guidance, neglect action constraints entirely, or fail to ensure that consecutive states evolve according to system dynamics, leaving a gap for methods that provide all three guarantees simultaneously at test time without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained flow matching vector field, an initial state s(0), constraint sets S and A (which may be novel and unseen during training), and a system dynamics model f (from a simulator or learned from data). It outputs a full state-action trajectory of horizon H where every state lies in S, every action lies in A, and consecutive states are related by f. The dynamics model f is required only for computing the CLF gradient during the controlled phase; the flow matching model itself is never retrained, and constraints can change between planning calls.

## Method

SAD-Flower augments the flow matching ODE with a virtual control input u_t, reframing trajectory generation as a controlled dynamical system. State and action constraints are encoded as Control Barrier Function (CBF) conditions using signed distance functions, while dynamic consistency is encoded as a Control Lyapunov Function (CLF) condition measuring the sum of squared dynamics-residual errors. A prescribed-time scheduling function φ(t)=c/(1-t)² drives CBF positivity and CLF decay to zero by the terminal time t=1. The ODE integration is split into an uncontrolled phase [0, T₀) that preserves generative diversity and a controlled phase [T₀, 1] where a minimum-norm quadratic program solves for u_t subject to all CBF and CLF constraints at each step. This yields a formal guarantee (Theorem 5.1) that the final trajectory satisfies safety, admissibility, and dynamic consistency for any initial condition, provided the QP remains feasible.

## Results

SAD-Flower achieves 0.00±0.00 safety and admissibility violations across all five main benchmark tasks (Maze2d-Large, Maze2d-Umaze, Hopper, Walker2d, KUKA Block-Stacking), while maintaining dynamic consistency violations of only 0.01–0.04. Task rewards remain competitive or best-in-class: 1.69±0.26 on Maze2d-Large (vs. 1.43 for FM), 2.70±0.67 on Maze2d-Umaze (vs. 2.62 for FM), and 0.93±0.23 on Hopper (Med-Expert). On the high-dimensional Adroit Relocate task (39D state, 30D action), SAD-Flower is the only method satisfying both safety and admissibility (0.00±0.00) while matching the reward of unconstrained FM (1.05 vs. 1.07).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAD-Flower is the only method achieving 0.00 on both safety and admissibility across all tasks, whereas Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022) and FM (Feng et al., 2025) violate both (e.g., 0.43/0.09 and 0.37/0.13 on Maze2d-Large). SafeDiffuser (Xiao et al., 2025) enforces safety (0.00) but still violates admissibility (0.09–0.14), and Decision Diffuser (Ajay et al., 2023) satisfies admissibility (0.00) but suffers severe dynamic consistency violations (1.80–2.78). Classifier Guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) and Truncation (Brockman et al., 2016) both fail to guarantee any constraint class simultaneously. Against DPCC (Römer et al., 2025), SAD-Flower matches constraint satisfaction while being ~70× faster (0.06s vs. 4.24s per trajectory) and achieving substantially higher reward (0.93 vs. 0.61 on Hopper). CoBL-Diffusion (Mizuta & Leung, 2024) provides only soft guidance, yielding 0.23 admissibility violations and a much lower reward (0.15 vs. 1.42 on Maze2d-Large), confirming that its CBF/CLF-based reward shaping lacks the formal guarantees of SAD-Flower's hard-constraint QP formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAD-Flower sits at the intersection of flow-matching-based generative planning (Lipman et al., 2023) and nonlinear control-theoretic safety enforcement (CBFs from Ames et al., 2017; CLFs from Sontag, 1983). Prior constraint-aware generative planners—SafeDiffuser, CoBL-diffusion, Decision Diffuser—address only subsets of the constraint problem (typically state constraints via soft guidance or projection) and lack formal guarantees or dynamic consistency. The key departure is the reformulation of the FM ODE as a controlled system with a virtual input, where CBF/CLF conditions with prescribed-time scheduling (Song et al., 2017) are enforced as hard QP constraints during a delayed phase of sampling, rather than as soft rewards or per-step projections. This contrasts with DPCC (Römer et al., 2025), which applies full non-convex optimization from the earliest sampling steps, and with SafeDiffuser's projection approach, which causes the local-trap problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAD-Flower is the first generative planner to simultaneously provide formal guarantees for safety, admissibility, and dynamic consistency at test time without retraining, filling a gap identified in Table 1 where no prior method checks all four boxes (state constraint, action constraint, dynamic consistency, theoretical guarantee). The practical impact is significant for robotics deployment where constraints change between training and deployment (e.g., new obstacles, tighter actuator limits). Limitations include the need for a (possibly learned) dynamics model for the CLF, the QP feasibility assumption (though argued to hold almost everywhere), and computational overhead (~0.06s per trajectory vs. ~0.01s for vanilla FM). Open questions raised by the authors include extension to stochastic dynamics, complex non-convex constraint geometries, and online replanning for real-time safety-critical systems.
