## Problem

Latent space reasoning compresses verbose Chain-of-Thought into compact continuous embeddings for efficiency, but creates a stark trade-off: methods like Coconut produce unexplainable black-box reasoning, while methods like Heima require separate post-hoc decoder models that introduce parameter overhead and risk decoupling the explanation from the actual reasoning process. Additionally, latent reasoning lacks a natural supervision signal since there is no human-interpretable ground truth for continuous thought vectors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266012/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For VLMs, the model takes an RGB image (processed through a ViT vision encoder and projection layer) plus a text question; for LLMs, only a text question is provided. During reasoning, the model generates a fixed or variable number of continuous latent thought vectors (hidden states fed back as input embeddings, bypassing the language model head) followed by a discrete text answer. For explainability, the same model decodes its own latent thoughts into human-readable reasoning steps using only the latent vectors as input—deliberately withholding the original image and question to force the latents into a self-contained information bottleneck.

## Method

SELR trains a single model with a multi-task objective combining an Answer Loss (cross-entropy on answer tokens conditioned on the latent thought sequence) and a CoT Loss (cross-entropy on ground-truth reasoning steps conditioned on the latent thoughts). The CoT Loss comes in two forms: Full CoT Loss decodes the entire reasoning sequence from all latent thoughts at once, while Single Step Loss decodes one reasoning step from one latent thought (sampled uniformly or exponentially), requiring a one-to-one correspondence between latent positions and reasoning steps. Latent generation uses <bot>/<eot> special tokens and feeds the final hidden state back as the next input embedding for l_latent autoregressive steps. The multi-stage curriculum first aligns latent thoughts with ground-truth steps via Single Step Loss (variable length), then refines with fixed length and either Full CoT Loss (LLMs) or continued Single Step Loss (VLMs, where the fixed three-step summary→caption→reasoning structure preserves positional specialization). This unified design ensures the model that reasons is the same model that explains, inherently aligning the decoded text with the actual computation.

## Results

On VLM benchmarks (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct), SELR (Single) achieves 63.72% average accuracy (+0.86 over the base model) while reducing average response length to 15.72 tokens (−68.5%); the multi-stage variants push token reduction to ~73% (13.3–13.5 tokens) with comparable accuracy gains. On LLM math benchmarks (LLaMA-3.2-1B-Instruct), SELR (Multi) reaches 42.46% on GSM8k, 49.67% on SVAMP, and 9.78% on GSM-Hard using only 6 latent tokens, substantially outperforming Coconut (30.83% GSM8k) and remaining competitive with CoLaR (40.1% GSM8k). In decoding quality, SELR surpasses Heima on BLEU-4 (19.95 vs 15.9 for Summary; 18.42 vs 12.8 for Caption) and achieves a 25.5% relative improvement in answer-consistency faithfulness (0.2097 vs 0.1671 on MathVista).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Heima (the primary explainable-latent-reasoning baseline), SELR wins on every axis: average VLM accuracy (63.72 vs 60.94, a +2.78 pp gap), token efficiency (13–16 vs 22.84 tokens, ~30% fewer), decoding quality (higher BLEU-4, METEOR, ROUGE-L, BERTScore, and GPT-4o scores on Summary and Caption steps), and faithfulness consistency (0.2097 vs 0.1671, +25.5% relative on MathVista)—all while using a single model rather than Heima's 4-model encoder-decoder architecture. Against Coconut on LLM math tasks, SELR (Multi) achieves a large margin: 42.46% vs 30.83% on GSM8k, 49.67% vs 36.33% on SVAMP, and 9.78% vs 0.00% on GSM-Hard. Against CoLaR, SELR (Multi) wins on GSM8k (42.46 vs 40.1) and GSM-Hard (9.78 vs 9.08) but trails on SVAMP (49.67 vs 54.9) and MultiArith (81.67 vs 91.3); however, SELR uses only 6 latent tokens versus CoLaR's 14, making it roughly 2× more token-efficient. A controlled-budget experiment further shows that when CoT-SFT is truncated to the same 6-token budget, its GSM8k accuracy collapses to 10.31% versus SELR's 42.46%, confirming that SELR encodes reasoning far more efficiently per token than textual CoT.

## Relation to Prior Work

SELR builds directly on Coconut's (Hao et al., 2025) latent reasoning mechanism—feeding the final hidden state back as the next input embedding—and its multi-stage curriculum, but departs fundamentally on the interpretability front. Where Coconut and follow-ups (CoLaR, CODI) produce opaque 'black box' latent thoughts, and Heima (Shen et al., 2025a) solves explainability by training separate decoder models, SELR unifies reasoning and self-explanation in a single model via a joint Answer Loss + CoT Loss objective. This eliminates the architectural overhead and reasoner-explainer decoupling inherent in Heima's multi-model design, while also providing the textual supervisory signal that purely latent methods (Coconut, CoLaR) lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SELR offers a practical path to token-efficient reasoning that does not sacrifice interpretability, achieving 70–93% token reduction and an 8.7× wall-clock speedup while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy. The ablation showing that the CoT Loss (intended for explainability) actually improves answer accuracy suggests that forcing latent representations to be decodable acts as a useful inductive bias, a finding with broader implications for representation learning. Key open questions remain: the fixed latent length is a significant constraint (learned stopping proved intractable), faithfulness of decoded thoughts is only partially validated (consistency scores of ~0.5 for correct answers are encouraging but far from definitive), and the MMVet degradation on long-form generation tasks highlights a fundamental tension between compression and expressive output.
