## Problem

While diffusion models are known to memorize training data, it remains unclear at which noise levels along the schedule memorization is actually induced, how data geometry governs its emergence, and how phenomena at different noise scales interact. Prior work has characterized trajectory-level memorization but has not identified the per-noise-level mechanisms that drive it, leaving practitioners without a principled way to target mitigation.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a finite training dataset (e.g., 1K–50K CIFAR-10 images, 1024 CelebA grayscale images, or 200 Stable Diffusion image-prompt pairs) and a noise schedule σ ∈ [σ_min, σ_max]. Beyond producing a trained denoiser and generated samples, it outputs a geometric diagnostic: empirical curves of Gaussian shell coverage C_σ and max posterior weight W_σ that jointly identify a 'danger zone' σ-range to exclude from training. The coverage quantity is estimated from a held-out test set or a broader proxy distribution (e.g., LAION in SD latent space), making it computable without access to the true data distribution.

## Method

The paper introduces a geometric framework built on two data-dependent quantities: (1) posterior weight concentration W_σ(D), which measures how sharply the empirical optimal denoiser locks onto individual training points, and (2) Gaussian shell coverage C_σ(p, D), which measures what fraction of the noisy test distribution falls within high-probability shells around training points. These two quantities exhibit sharp phase transitions at different noise levels, partitioning the schedule into three regimes: small noise (high weight, low coverage → safe due to limited supervision), large noise (low weight, high coverage → safe due to near-linear Gaussian denoising, formalized in Theorem 4.11 with an O((1+σ)^{-2}) residual), and medium noise (both quantities in transition → a 'danger zone' where memorization peaks). The practical intervention is targeted gap training: empirically identifying the danger zone via the coverage-weight diagnostic and excluding that σ-range from the training loss, which reduces memorization from 78.8% to 0.7% on CIFAR-10 cars while improving FID, and from 75% to 9% on CelebA.

## Results

On the CIFAR-10 car subset (2k images), the proposed 2k-Gap method (excluding the empirically identified danger zone σ∈[1.0, 5.0]) achieves 0.7% memorization and FID 2.35, substantially outperforming both the standard EDM baseline (78.8% memorization, FID 3.72) and the 2k-Dummy distributional-contrast method from Yoon et al. (9.0% memorization, FID 2.58). On grayscale CelebA (1024 samples), skipping the full danger-zone timestep range [100–350] reduces memorization from 75% to 9%, while a narrower gap [100–275] still achieves 13%. In Stable Diffusion v1.4 finetuning on 200 memorized image-prompt pairs, capping the noise schedule at σ<1 reduces mean SSCD² from 0.42 to 0.10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the 2k-Dummy method from Yoon et al. (2023), which adds 2k random noise images as a second class to provide distributional contrast during training. The proposed 2k-Gap method (targeted exclusion of the medium-noise danger zone) dominates this baseline on both metrics: it reduces memorization from 9.0% to 0.7% (a 13× improvement) while simultaneously achieving a better FID (2.35 vs. 2.58), indicating that the gap training preserves or even improves generation quality. Against the unmodified EDM baseline, the improvement is dramatic: memorization drops from 78.8% to 0.7% and FID improves from 3.72 to 2.35. The denoiser-swapping experiments (Table 1) further confirm the mechanism: replacing only the medium-noise denoiser (σ∈[0.14, 8.4]) flips trajectory-level memorization from 92.2% to 0% (and vice versa), while swapping in the small or large noise regions has negligible effect, validating that the medium regime is the critical locus of memorization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes the Gaussian shell framework from Song et al. (2025) with posterior weight concentration analysis to create a unified geometric picture of diffusion training across the noise schedule. It builds on the memorization metric from Yoon et al. (2023) and the linear denoiser characterization at large noise from Li et al. (2024) and Wang & Vastola (2024), but departs from prior work by explicitly distinguishing trajectory-level from per-noise-level memorization and showing that the two can diverge sharply. The three-regime partition echoes the speciation/collapse times from the statistical physics analysis of Biroli et al. (2024), but here the regimes are characterized by data-dependent geometric quantities (shell coverage and posterior concentration) rather than order parameters, making them directly computable from a dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a narrow medium-noise 'danger zone' as the primary source of memorization is practically actionable: practitioners can compute the zone from coverage and posterior weight curves and apply targeted undertraining to eliminate memorization while preserving generation quality, as validated on CIFAR-10, CelebA, ImageNet, and Stable Diffusion finetuning. A key open question is the lack of a full theoretical explanation for why generalization arises specifically in the medium regime—the paper provides strong empirical evidence (denoiser swapping) but acknowledges this remains conjectural. Limitations include testing primarily on relatively small datasets (1K–50K images) and specific architectures (EDM, DDPM, SD v1.4), and whether the geometric framework scales to very high-dimensional latent spaces or video generation remains to be established.
