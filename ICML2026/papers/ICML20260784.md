## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) of pretrained foundation models on new tasks still causes catastrophic forgetting of pretraining-era capabilities, yet existing continual-learning mitigations either require access to old-task data (replay buffers, Fisher information, feature covariances) or impose global gradient constraints that are computationally intractable at LLM scale. The paper asks whether one can design a data-free, weight-only continual-learning method that explicitly controls the plasticity-retention tradeoff without ever observing the pretraining distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the frozen pretrained weight matrices of each target linear layer (no activations, no old-task samples, no gradients from P0) plus the new-task training data. It produces a set of small trainable adapter cores A^(ℓ) ∈ R^{r×k} per layer; the output selector B and input basis Q are computed once from the frozen weights and remain fixed. The two user-facing hyperparameters r (number of redundant output channels) and τ (input energy threshold controlling basis dimension k) jointly navigate the learning-forgetting tradeoff, and the total trainable parameter count per layer is rk, independent of the hidden dimension d.

## Method

PLATE parameterizes each adapted layer's update as ΔW = BAQ^⊤, where B is a frozen index-selection matrix that picks r highly redundant (high-cosine-similarity) output neurons, Q is a frozen orthonormal basis spanning the low-energy (bottom-eigenspace) subspace of the Gram matrix formed from the remaining frozen weight rows, and A is the sole trainable r×k matrix. The theoretical core (Theorem 3.3) shows that worst-case forgetting over an update family S is upper-bounded by (β/2)·ε(S)²·ρ², where ε(S) is the worst-case first-order functional drift on old inputs; PLATE's two-sided construction is designed to make ε(S) small in a fully weight-only manner. Empirically, the input-side Q construction is the dominant retention driver (randomizing Q at matched capacity increases drift 8–12×), while the output-side B selection primarily allocates the plasticity budget to redundant channels with only a mild effect on retention. For task streams of length T ≥ 3, trained adapters are merged into the backbone and the (B, Q) preprocessing is recomputed on the updated weights, keeping every per-task update data-free with respect to all prior tasks.

## Results

On MNIST 0-4→5-9, PLATE achieves 98.28% task-2 accuracy with only 1.85% task-1 forgetting at 10.2% trainable parameters, compared to ~7-9% forgetting for LoRA and ~26% for full fine-tuning. On AG News→IMDB, PLATE keeps task-1 forgetting below 0.5% across all (r,τ) configurations while all methods reach 100% task-2 accuracy, versus ~2-3% for LoRA at higher ranks and ~3% for full fine-tuning. On Qwen2.5-7B specialized to DeepSeek-R1 reasoning, PLATE matches LoRA's ≈+13-point gain on math benchmarks (AIME, GSM8K, MATH-500) while essentially eliminating the ≈16-point drop on IFEval that LoRA incurs. Direct drift measurements confirm PLATE achieves KL≈0.24 on WikiText-2 with 3.4M trainable parameters, while LoRA requires 4-10× more parameters and still exhibits 3-10× larger output drift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA, PLATE consistently wins on retention while matching new-task performance: on MNIST it reduces forgetting from ~7-9% to 1.85% (4× improvement), on AG News it holds forgetting below 0.5% versus LoRA's 2-3% at comparable ranks, and on Qwen2.5-7B it eliminates the ~16-point IFEval degradation that LoRA incurs while matching its +13-point math gain. Against O-LoRA (orthogonal subspace learning), PLATE is decisively superior: O-LoRA exhibits 10-100× larger forgetting on WikiText-2 under domain shift, and on MNIST its retention degrades from ~88% at r=8 to ~76% at r=128, converging toward full fine-tuning behavior, whereas PLATE maintains ~97% retention. Against L2-Init (regenerative regularization), the comparison is mixed: L2-Init matches PLATE's ~98% task-1 retention on MNIST but only at 100% trainable parameters (no parameter efficiency), fails to learn task 2 on AG News (capped at ~91.3%), and on synthetic regression achieves the lowest forgetting (~5×10⁻³ MSE) but at a higher task-2 loss (~1.6×10⁻³ vs. ~3×10⁻⁴ for LoRA). Overall, PLATE is the only method that simultaneously achieves near-full retention and high parameter efficiency across all tested modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLATE sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, adapters) and continual learning, departing from both lines in a specific way. Whereas prior orthogonality-based continual-learning methods (EWC, OGD, OWM, O-LoRA) require old-task data, gradients, or replay buffers to estimate protected subspaces, PLATE derives both the protected input subspace (Q) and the plasticity allocation (B) entirely from frozen pretrained weights, using neuron redundancy as a data-free proxy for dominant pretraining-era feature directions. The theoretical grounding draws on neural-collapse results (Garrod & Keating, 2025) showing that trained weights concentrate in low-dimensional data-dependent subspaces, and on the broader literature on redundancy and compressibility in overparameterized networks. The drift-curvature link (Theorem 3.3) provides a unified lens that subsumes both the lower-bound argument for approximate orthogonality and the upper-bound argument for low-drift update families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PLATE addresses a practically critical gap: in foundation-model adaptation, pretraining data is typically proprietary and unavailable, yet retention of core capabilities (instruction following, factual knowledge) is a first-class constraint. The explicit (r, τ) trade-off gives practitioners a direct knob for navigating the plasticity-retention spectrum, and the drift-based evaluation lens (Theorem 3.3) offers a method-agnostic way to assess any continual-learning adapter. Key limitations include the Gauss-Newton approximation (residual curvature is non-negligible, though smaller in PLATE's subspace), the weight-geometry-as-activation-proxy assumption (which fails for v_proj), and degradation under aggressive structured compression. Open questions include behavior over very long task streams where the redundant-neuron pool shrinks, and whether the approach transfers to non-linear or non-Transformer architectures.
