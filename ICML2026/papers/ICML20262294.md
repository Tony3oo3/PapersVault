## Problem

Existing sparse attention methods select key blocks using one-hop attention scores computed independently at each layer, which inherently misses multi-hop token dependencies that emerge through repeated attention composition in deep transformers. If an intermediate token is not selected at an earlier layer, any multi-hop dependency through it is permanently broken and cannot be recovered in subsequent layers, leading to accuracy degradation at high sparsity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262294/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes query and key matrices (Q, K ∈ R^{n×d}) partitioned into blocks of size B, producing a b×b walk-state matrix R^k (where b = ⌈n/B⌉) that guides top-τ block selection for sparse attention. Key hyperparameters include the Hadamard sketch dimension k (default 64, reducing per-block-pair cost from O(B²d) to O(k)) and the sparsity exponent s (default 8) that sharpens high-affinity transitions. The same mechanism applies uniformly to both prefill (full sequence) and decode (single query token against growing KV cache) phases, with custom Triton kernels for GPU execution.

## Method

Sketch&Walk is a training-free sparse attention method built on two components. First, Small-World Sketching (SWS) estimates block-level attention scores via token-space sketching (averaging tokens within each block) and feature-space sketching (Subsampled Randomized Hadamard Transform projecting from d to k dimensions), reducing per-block-pair computation from O(B²d) to O(k). Second, the Sketch-Determined Walk accumulates these sketched estimates across layers via the recurrence R^k = R^{k-1}(Â_block^k)^s, where the exponent s > 1 sharpens high-affinity transitions; this per-layer walk state captures multi-hop paths connecting query and key blocks across the full transformer depth rather than relying on depth-wise composition of independently selected blocks. Top-τ key blocks are then selected from the accumulated walk state R^k rather than per-layer scores, and sparse attention is computed only over those blocks using custom Triton kernels. The paper provides theoretical guarantees (under block coherence and heavy-tailed attention assumptions) showing that the sketched top-τ selection recovers the true top-τ blocks with high probability and that the sparse output approximates dense attention with error bounded by the tail mass η.

## Results

At 80% attention sparsity, Sketch&Walk achieves near-lossless end-to-end accuracy on LongBench (e.g., 47.63 AVG vs. 47.95 Dense for Llama-3.1-8B) and even surpasses dense attention on some models (Qwen2-7B: 43.22 vs. 40.85 AVG). On RULER, it maintains strong performance across 4K–64K contexts with only a modest average drop (90.15 vs. 91.44 for Llama-3.1-8B). In terms of speed, Sketch&Walk delivers up to 4.7× end-to-end attention speedup over FlashAttention-2 at 96K context, with 3.84× prefill and 4.91× decode speedups at that length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the prefill phase on LongBench (Llama-3.1-8B), Sketch&Walk (47.67 AVG) matches or exceeds all prefill baselines: it outperforms FlexPrefill (which drops sharply on MuSiQue to 1.00 vs. 6.16) and is competitive with MInference (46.39 on 2WikiMQA) and PBS-Attn (47.89 on 2WikiMQA), while leading on MuSiQue (6.16 vs. 4.25/4.50/1.86). In the decode phase, Sketch&Walk (48.00 AVG) surpasses both Quest (46.92) and Adamas (47.84) on LongBench, and on RULER decode it achieves 91.08 average vs. Quest's 89.06 and Adamas's 90.74. For speed, Sketch&Walk is the only method that natively sparsifies both prefill and decode; at 96K context it reaches 4.69× end-to-end speedup over FlashAttention-2, outpacing decode-only Quest (~2.5× from 32K) and prefill-only FlexPrefill/MInference, whose savings do not compound into the decode phase.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sketch&Walk departs from the dominant one-hop-per-layer block selection paradigm used by Quest, Adamas, MInference, and FlexPrefill by introducing a cross-layer walk accumulation (R^k = R^{k-1}(Â_block^k)^s) that explicitly captures multi-hop token dependencies arising from attention composition in deep transformers. It repurposes the Hadamard transform (shared with Adamas) not as a direct distance criterion but as a lightweight estimator of block-level attention scores, which then feed into the walk mechanism. The method also unifies prefill and decode sparsification under a single training-free algorithm, whereas most prior methods (Quest/Adamas for decode, MInference/FlexPrefill for prefill) target only one inference phase. The theoretical grounding draws on subspace embedding theory and Markov chain perturbation bounds to guarantee that sketched walk scores recover the correct top-τ blocks with high probability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.7× end-to-end attention speedup at 96K context with near-lossless accuracy at 80% sparsity is practically significant for deploying long-context LLMs, and the training-free design means it can be bolted onto any existing model without retraining. The multi-hop framing provides a principled explanation for why per-layer selection fails on compositional tasks (multi-hop QA), and the recall experiment (Table 3) gives concrete evidence that the walk recovers ~2× more relevant blocks than one-hop scores. Open questions include whether the walk state accumulates numerical instability over very deep models (100+ layers), whether the heavy-tailed block attention assumption (Assumption 2.3) holds universally across architectures and languages, and how the method interacts with emerging architectural changes like native sparse attention in DeepSeek-V3.2.
