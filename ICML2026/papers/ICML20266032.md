## Problem

Existing counterfactual-explanation-based model reconstruction methods suffer from decision boundary shifts (when CFs are treated as full class samples), overfitting under limited data, and reliance on online query access or two-sided CFs. Under one-sided CF access and low query budgets, the target decision boundary is statistically non-identifiable, rendering boundary-centric recovery approaches inapplicable. RECAST addresses this by shifting from boundary recovery to behavior-centric reconstruction that remains stable under CF-consistent distributional uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline set of labeled samples from both classes plus one-sided reject-to-accept counterfactuals (CFs generated only for class-0 inputs that flip to class 1), with no online access to the target model during reconstruction. It produces a surrogate binary classifier defined by two Wasserstein barycentric prototypes (one per class) and a pointwise decision rule based on W2 distance to these prototypes, along with threshold-invariant fairness diagnostic scores (TIDP, TIEO) computed as Wasserstein-1 distances between score distributions across sensitive groups. The prototypes are discrete point clouds with fixed support size M and uniform weights, optimized via Sinkhorn-regularized optimal transport rather than parametric model fitting.

## Method

RECAST constructs a CF-consistent feasible set for each class as the intersection of two Wasserstein balls (one centered at the class-conditional empirical distribution, one at the CF distribution), forming a lens-shaped ambiguity set in Wasserstein space. Class prototypes are computed as 2-Wasserstein barycenters of the class distribution and the CF distribution, with a data-dependent mixing weight λ_c derived in closed form from the relative W2 geometry among the three anchor distributions (P_c, P_cf, P_{1-c}), eliminating the need for explicit uncertainty-radius calibration. Classification assigns a point x to the class whose prototype is closer in W2 distance from the Dirac mass at x, yielding a CF-identifiable decision rule invariant to non-identifiable density variations. In practice, the barycenter objective is optimized over discrete support locations using an entropically regularized (Sinkhorn-smoothed) surrogate with Adam, and the approach is shown to be Lipschitz-stable along the barycentric geodesic, ensuring graceful degradation under distributional shift and noisy CFs.

## Results

On four real-world tabular datasets (Adult Income, COMPAS, HELOC, California Housing) with query size 100, RECAST achieves the highest fidelity in 11 of 12 dataset–target-model combinations. Headline results include 0.908 fidelity on Adult Income (MLP target), 0.855 on COMPAS (MLP target), and 0.798 on HELOC (LR target), consistently outperforming both SAMPLES and CCA. The advantage is most pronounced in low-query regimes (25–100 queries), where RECAST's Wasserstein barycentric prototypes avoid the overfitting and boundary-shift issues that degrade baselines. Under additive noise (τ up to 0.4), RECAST degrades most gracefully, with the smallest fidelity drop on the near-threshold subset D_near.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECAST outperforms SAMPLES (Aivodji et al., 2020) in all 12 settings, with the largest gaps on COMPAS (0.855 vs. 0.415 for MLP target) and HELOC (0.798 vs. 0.551 for LR target), where treating CFs as full class samples causes severe boundary shift. Against CCA (Dissanayake & Dutta, 2024), RECAST wins in 11 of 12 cases; the sole exception is Housing with an MLP target where CCA (0.716) marginally edges RECAST (0.712), and the largest CCA gap is on HELOC-LR (0.798 vs. 0.670), where CCA's near-boundary assumption is violated by overshooting CFs. TRA (Khouna et al., 2025), which requires prior knowledge that the target is a decision tree, is outperformed by RECAST on all four DT-target settings (e.g., 0.869 vs. 0.826 on COMPAS, 0.774 vs. 0.654 on Housing). Across CF generation methods (MCCF, DiCE, ROAR, 1-NN, C-CHVAE), RECAST maintains fidelity above 0.91 on Adult Income while baselines fluctuate between 0.36 and 0.93, confirming that RECAST's distributional treatment of CFs is robust to generator-specific geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

RECAST builds on the Wasserstein barycenter framework (Agueh & Carlier, 2011) and Wasserstein robust optimization (Mohajerin Esfahani & Kuhn, 2018), but departs from classical WRO by replacing the single Wasserstein ball with a two-anchor lens-shaped ambiguity set that encodes both class-conditional data and counterfactual geometry. It explicitly rejects the boundary-centric perspective of prior CF-based reconstruction methods (CCA, TRA) and the naive sample-augmentation approach of SAMPLES, arguing that under one-sided CF access the decision boundary is statistically non-identifiable and only distributional class-level behavior is recoverable. The fairness diagnostic adapts threshold-invariant metrics from Chen & Wu (2020) into a Wasserstein-geometric score space, operating on entire score distributions rather than pointwise predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: auditing deployed MLaaS systems where access is restricted to offline labeled queries and one-sided CFs (e.g., rejected applicants receive explanations but approved ones do not). The non-identifiability argument reframes reconstruction as a behavioral rather than geometric problem, which has implications for how the field should evaluate and design extraction methods under realistic access constraints. Limitations include restriction to binary classification, tabular data, and low-query regimes; the λ_c instantiation is geometrically motivated but not statistically calibrated, and Wasserstein computation may become a bottleneck at scale. Open questions include multiclass extension, leveraging structural priors about the target model, and whether the behavioral-equivalence framework extends to continuous-output or generative targets.
