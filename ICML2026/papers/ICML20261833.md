## Problem

KL-entropy-regularized RL methods like Mirror Descent Value Iteration (MDVI) enjoy strong theoretical guarantees but fail to outperform entropy-only-regularized baselines (e.g., SAC) in continuous action domains. A naive actor-critic instantiation of MDVI for continuous control is unstable because the log-policy terms in the critic's TD target grow unboundedly, erasing the reward signal. The paper asks how to properly instantiate MDVI for continuous domains and provides a theoretical account of why and when bounding the log-policy (equivalently, soft advantage) terms is beneficial.

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous state vectors from the environment and outputs continuous actions sampled from a Gaussian policy whose mean and log-standard-deviation are produced by a two-layer 256-unit ReLU network. Learning is off-policy via a replay buffer of (s, a, r, s') tuples, with the critic being a separate two-layer network parameterizing Q(s,a). The key structural detail is that the critic's TD target y includes two bounded log-probability terms — one for the current (s,a) pair and one for the successor (s',a') pair — each passed through a bounding function f or g (e.g., tanh or clip), which is the sole modification from a standard SAC-style loss.

## Method

MDAC derives model-free actor-critic losses from the Munchausen reparameterization of MDVI, where the critic tracks Ψ = Q + βα·log π and the actor minimizes KL divergence to the regularized greedy policy. The central modification is replacing the raw α·log π_θ terms in the critic's TD target with bounded versions f(α·log π_θ) and g(α·log π_θ), where f and g are non-decreasing functions with bounded codomains (e.g., tanh(x/10) or clip(x/10,−1,1)). The paper shows this is equivalent to Bounded Advantage Learning (BAL), in which the soft advantage A = Ψ − V is pushed down by bounded amounts, and proves that bounding reduces the inherent misspecification error of gap-increasing Bellman operators (Proposition 2) while preserving asymptotic convergence under a mild condition on policy stochasticity (Proposition 1). The entropy temperature α is auto-tuned via a SAC-style loss, and β is fixed to 1−(1−γ)². Empirically, slower-saturating bounding functions (clip(x/10,−1,1)) outperform aggressive clipping because they preserve relative ordering of log-probability values across state-action pairs, and a time-dependent g_t that approaches the identity map in late training further improves performance.

## Results

On 6 Mujoco locomotion environments (3M steps; 1M for Hopper-v4), MDAC with f(x)=g(x)=clip(x/10,−1,1) achieves the highest baseline-normalized IQM score, surpassing TD3, SAC, X-SAC, and a naive-clipping MDAC variant. On Adroit hand manipulation and DMC dog (10M steps), MDAC's final IQM and optimality gap are clearly better than SAC's, with MDAC's score distribution strictly above SAC's in the reasonable range. In the tabular gridworld experiment, BAL (bounded advantage learning) reduces suboptimality ‖V^{π_k}−V_τ*‖_∞ faster than M-VI in early iterations, with g≠I providing a slight additional edge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDAC outperforms all four baselines on the Mujoco benchmark (Figure 6): it beats the non-regularized TD3, the entropy-only SAC (the normalization reference), the KL-trust-region X-SAC (which 'struggles' even after tuning its Gumbel scale parameter), and a naive-clipping MDAC variant (f=clip(x,−1,1), g=I) that mimics Munchausen RL's ad-hoc clipping. On the harder Adroit and DMC dog tasks (10M steps), MDAC consistently outperforms SAC in both IQM and optimality gap, with SAC exhibiting frequent performance degradation that MDAC avoids. In the gridworld ablation, BAL with bounding functions f,g≠I outperforms the original Munchausen Value Iteration (M-VI) in early iterations, validating that the bounding strategy reduces inherent gap-increasing operator error most when Ψ_k is far from optimal. The paper also shows that bounding only the current (s,a) term (f≠I, g=I) is insufficient—bounding both terms (f=g≠I) yields the best performance, consistent with the theoretical error decomposition in Proposition 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper instantiates Mirror Descent Value Iteration (MDVI) as a model-free actor-critic algorithm (MDAC) for continuous action spaces, building directly on the Munchausen reparameterization (Vieillard et al., 2020b) that makes KL-entropy regularized RL practical. The key departure from prior work is modifying the critic's loss function by bounding both the current and successor log-policy terms, rather than merely adopting the Munchausen augmented reward as in prior Munchausen RL implementations. The theoretical analysis connects this bounding strategy to Bounded Advantage Learning (BAL), extending the gap-increasing operator framework of Bellemare et al. (2016) to the KL-entropy regularized setting, and provides convergence and error-reduction guarantees that go beyond the ad-hoc clipping used in Munchausen-DQN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for making KL-entropy regularized RL work in continuous domains, where prior evidence suggested such methods underperform entropy-only baselines like SAC. The theoretical contribution—showing that bounding reduces inherent errors of gap-increasing Bellman operators and providing sufficient convergence conditions—gives principled justification for what was previously an implementation hack. Key limitations include theory restricted to tabular settings with fixed α, no characterization of optimal bounding functions, and the inability to capture the α-explosion failure mode. Open questions include extending the analysis to continuous state-action spaces, finding the optimal bounding function design, and deriving an explicit mirror descent form for BAL.
