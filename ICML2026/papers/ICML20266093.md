## Problem

Standard matrix-based SE(3) synchronization methods suffer from a fundamental eigenspace-synchronization gap: the relaxed eigenspace has compact orthogonal geometry while SE(3) is non-compact, forcing complex multi-step heuristic rounding procedures that are unstable and lack theoretical guarantees. This paper addresses the gap by formulating synchronization directly over unit dual quaternions, where the spectral ambiguity aligns with the intrinsic gauge symmetry of SE(3), and provides the first finite-iteration recovery guarantees for the problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Hermitian dual quaternion measurement matrix C ∈ DH^{n×n} whose entries C_ij = x̂_i x̂_j* + Δ_ij encode noisy pairwise relative rigid motions (rotations and translations), typically obtained via ICP or ground-truth relative transforms. It outputs n absolute poses as unit dual quaternions in UDQ^n, which map to SE(3) elements (R_i, t_i) via the 2-to-1 homomorphism x = (1 + εt/2)q. The formulation naturally handles partial observations through an Erdős-Rényi sparsification of the measurement graph, and the gauge ambiguity (global right-multiplication by any z ∈ UDQ) is resolved by an optimal alignment step before error reporting.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage algorithm over the unit dual quaternion constraint set UDQ^n. Stage 1 computes a spectral initializer by running the power method on the Hermitian dual quaternion matrix C to extract the dominant right eigenvector, then applies a closed-form elementwise normalization/projection onto UDQ^n (Proposition 2.7), which is shown to be a 2-Lipschitz map (Lemma 2.5). Stage 2 refines the initializer via the Dual Quaternion Generalized Power Method (DQGPM), which at each iteration multiplies by C and projects elementwise back onto UDQ^n, guaranteeing feasibility at every step. The key theoretical results are non-asymptotic error bounds for the spectral estimator (d(x, x̂) ≤ 4‖Δ‖_op/√n) and a linear convergence guarantee for DQGPM with an explicit noise-dependent error floor (Theorem 3.2), establishing the first finite-iteration recovery guarantee for SE(3) synchronization. The dual quaternion representation is critical because it makes the projection a simple per-component normalization rather than the multi-step SVD-based heuristic required by 4×4 matrix embeddings, and because the ring structure of dual quaternions (with zero divisors and a seminorm rather than a norm) introduces unique analytical challenges that the paper resolves.

## Results

On the 3DMatch indoor scenes (Table 6), DQGPM achieves rotation errors of 7.8×10⁻⁷ to 0.12 and translation errors of 3.9×10⁻⁷ to 0.25, outperforming SPEC, SDR, and EIG by 2–3 orders of magnitude (e.g., 7.78×10⁻⁷ vs. 1.33 for Livingroom1 rotation error). On the Stanford object datasets (Table 4), DQGPM attains rotation errors of 0.09–0.21 and translation errors of 0.004–0.012, matching or exceeding baselines while running 50–100× faster than SDR (e.g., 0.0003 s vs. 0.2166 s on dense Bunny). On synthetic benchmarks (Table 2), DQGPM is uniformly more accurate and stable, with the largest gains under sparse observation (p=0.05), where it achieves rotation error 0.132 vs. 1.639 (SPEC) and 0.174 (EIG) at the lowest noise level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DQGPM decisively outperforms all three matrix-based baselines (EIG, SDR, SPEC) on the 3DMatch dataset, where it achieves rotation errors 2–3 orders of magnitude lower (e.g., 7.78×10⁻⁷ vs. ~1.3 for Livingroom1) and translation errors similarly reduced (3.12×10⁻⁶ vs. ~1.8). On the Stanford object datasets, DQGPM matches or slightly exceeds the baselines in accuracy (rotation error 0.09–0.21 vs. 0.17–3.63) while being 50–100× faster than SDR and comparable in speed to EIG. On synthetic benchmarks, DQGPM is uniformly more accurate and stable than both SPEC and EIG, with the advantage most pronounced under sparse observation (p=0.05), where EIG exhibits high variance (e.g., translation error 1.609±6.676) while DQGPM remains tight (0.190±0.046). The three matrix-based baselines (EIG, SDR, SPEC) perform nearly identically to each other, suggesting they share the same fundamental limitation of the eigenspace-synchronization gap identified in the paper. DQGPM's advantage is attributed to the dual quaternion representation aligning the spectral ambiguity with SE(3)'s intrinsic gauge symmetry, enabling a well-posed projection step and linear error contraction via the iterative refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalized power method (GPM) framework from Journée et al. (2010) and its spectral-relaxation lineage (Boumal 2016, Ling 2022, Liu et al. 2023) to the dual quaternion setting, leveraging the power iteration for dual quaternion Hermitian matrices from Cui & Qi (2024) and the minimax eigenvalue characterization from Ling et al. (2022). The key conceptual departure from prior SE(3) synchronization work (Arrigoni et al. 2016b, Rosen et al. 2019, Doherty et al. 2022) is the replacement of 4×4 homogeneous matrix representations with unit dual quaternions, which the authors argue aligns the relaxed eigenspace geometry with the intrinsic gauge symmetry of SE(3), eliminating the 'eigenspace-sync gap' that forces multi-step heuristic rounding in matrix-based methods. Earlier dual quaternion approaches (Cheng et al. 2016, Srivatsan et al. 2016, Hadi et al. 2024) lacked a unified algebraic framework amenable to rigorous convergence guarantees, which this paper fills.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide finite-iteration recovery guarantees for SE(3) synchronization, establishing that a simple two-stage spectral-then-GPM pipeline achieves linear error contraction up to an explicit noise-dependent floor. The practical implication is that the dual quaternion representation turns the historically unstable rounding step into a well-posed, Lipschitz-controlled projection, making the overall pipeline more robust—particularly under sparse observations where matrix-based methods (EIG, SDR) degrade significantly. Limitations include the somewhat restrictive nondegeneracy and bounded-dual-residual assumptions in Theorem 3.2, the mild noise regime required, and the fact that SDR comparisons are limited to small real datasets due to scalability. Open questions include whether the guarantees extend to outlier-robust settings, whether the approach scales to very large pose graphs (thousands of nodes), and whether the dual quaternion advantage persists under the more heterogeneous noise distributions common in real SLAM deployments.
