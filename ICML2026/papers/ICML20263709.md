## Problem

Standard transformer architectures for traffic forecasting are black-box models with large parameter counts and limited interpretability, making them impractical for resource-constrained deployments. Prior algorithm-unrolling approaches (e.g., Thuc et al., 2024) only employed undirected graphs to capture simple pairwise correlations in static images, leaving the more complex spatial-temporal structure of traffic data—where geographically proximate stations share spatial correlations while past observations influence future values via sequential (directed) relationships—unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263709/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of T=12 past 5-minute traffic speed/flow observations from N sensors (e.g., 358 nodes on PEMS03, 207 on METR-LA) along with the physical road network topology, and predicts the next S steps (6/12/24 steps for 30/60/120-minute horizons). The full signal is organized as a product graph of N×(T+S+1) nodes, where spatial connectivity is restricted to k nearest neighbors on the road graph and temporal connectivity uses a fixed directed window W. Training uses Huber loss (δ=1) over the entire reconstructed sequence, and the model is trained for 70 epochs on 1/3-subsampled data split 6:2:2.

## Method

The paper constructs a mixed graph combining an undirected graph G^u (spatial correlations) and a directed acyclic graph G^d (temporal sequential relationships), then designs two new variational terms for the directed graph: an ℓ2-norm directed graph Laplacian regularizer (DGLR) via the symmetrized random-walk Laplacian (L_r^d)^T L_r^d, and an ℓ1-norm directed graph total variation (DGTV) via ‖L_r^d x‖₁, both of which promote low-frequency signal reconstruction on directed graphs. A convex optimization objective combining data fidelity, GLR, DGLR, and DGTV is solved via ADMM with auxiliary-variable splitting of the ℓ2 terms, yielding three coupled linear systems solved by conjugate gradient and a soft-thresholding step for the ℓ1 term. The ADMM iterations are unrolled into a feed-forward network where each iteration becomes a neural layer with learnable step sizes, regularization weights, and ADMM parameters, while periodically inserted graph learning modules (computing Mahalanobis distances between learned node features followed by softmax normalization) serve as a parameter-efficient self-attention mechanism. The resulting network uses only 38K parameters—7.2% of PDFformer—while each unrolled block is interpretable as a set of parameterized low-pass graph filters, and the learned graph structures reveal physically meaningful traffic dynamics via eigenvector centrality analysis.

## Results

On PEMS03 (358 nodes) and METR-LA (207 nodes), the proposed mixed-graph unrolling model (38K parameters) achieves RMSE/MAE/MAPE of 26.96/16.58/18.07 (PEMS03, 60-min) and 12.17/5.27/11.78 (METR-LA, 60-min). The model uses only 7.2% of the parameters of the transformer-based PDFFormer (531K) while achieving competitive performance, reaching top-3 in at least one metric for 3 of 6 dataset-horizon settings and remaining within top-5 in all but one. Ablation studies confirm that both the directed graph Laplacian regularizer (DGLR) and directed graph total variation (DGTV) priors contribute meaningfully, with removing either causing noticeable degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed 38K-parameter model outperforms PDFFormer (531K params) on PEMS03 at both 60-min (26.96 vs 27.16 RMSE) and 120-min (34.06 vs 35.77 RMSE) horizons across all three metrics, while using only 7.2% of its parameters. It also beats the multi-attention GMAN (210K) on PEMS03 60-min (26.96 vs 27.57 RMSE) and 120-min (34.06 vs 30.69 RMSE—here GMAN actually wins RMSE but Ours wins MAPE 23.86 vs 26.69), and on METR-LA 60-min (12.17 vs 14.49 RMSE). However, the model trails the best-performing baselines: SimpleTM (540K) achieves lower RMSE on both datasets at most horizons (e.g., 23.75 vs 25.05 on PEMS03 30-min; 8.76 vs 10.06 on METR-LA 30-min), and PatchSTG (2,283K) dominates PEMS03 across all horizons (22.89/25.09/29.97 RMSE). On METR-LA 120-min, STAEformer (1,404K) and AGCRN (749K) both outperform the proposed method (15.19 RMSE vs 14.63 and 14.77 respectively). The key trade-off is that the proposed model achieves near-competitive accuracy with 5–60× fewer parameters than transformer and GNN baselines, positioning it as a lightweight interpretable alternative rather than a new SOTA in raw accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Thuc et al. (2024), which unrolled undirected graph smoothness priors into lightweight transformer-like networks for image interpolation, to a mixed undirected+directed graph setting for spatiotemporal traffic forecasting. The undirected graph learning module replaces classical self-attention (computing edge weights via Mahalanobis distance on learned features rather than Q/K dot products), while the unrolled ADMM blocks replace the conventional feed-forward network with parameterized low-pass graph filters. The key theoretical departure is the introduction of directed graph Laplacian regularizer (DGLR) and directed graph total variation (DGTV) variational terms, which provide the first variational smoothness measures on directed graphs with low-pass filter interpretations, circumventing the lack of a spectral theorem for asymmetric Laplacians.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 38K-parameter model achieving competitive (top-3 in at least one metric across 3 of 6 settings) traffic forecasting performance while using only 7.2% of PDFormer's parameters is practically significant for memory-constrained deployment (e.g., edge devices computing local traffic predictions). The eigenvector centrality analysis demonstrating that learned graph structure identifies real congestion hubs adds a layer of interpretability absent in black-box transformers. However, the model does not achieve SOTA performance (SimpleTM and PatchSTG outperform on several metrics), and the approach is validated only on two city-scale datasets with 1/3 subsampled data, leaving open whether the directed graph smoothness framework generalizes to larger-scale or different spatiotemporal domains (weather, energy).
