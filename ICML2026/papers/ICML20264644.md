## Problem

Modern vision backbones rely on pointwise activations (ReLU, GELU) and exponential softmax as their primary sources of nonlinearity, but prior polynomial networks that remove these (MONet, DTTN) require entirely custom architectures tightly coupled to their specific designs, limiting practical adoption and still underperforming activation-based counterparts end-to-end. The paper asks whether polynomial alternatives to the standard modules (MLPs, convolutions, attention) can be designed to preserve input-output interfaces, drop into existing MetaFormer-style architectures, and match or exceed activation-based performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264644/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume standard RGB images at 224×224 resolution (no additional pretraining data) and produce class labels for ImageNet-1K classification or pixel-level segmentation masks for ADE20K via a UperNet head. A fully polynomial variant replaces LayerNorm with a polynomial-compatible BatchNorm (reducing over batch and channels per spatial position with running statistics), making the entire inference pass consist solely of additions and multiplications, a prerequisite for Fully Homomorphic Encryption deployment.

## Method

The paper introduces three activation-free modules built on Hadamard products: PolyMLP computes W_o((W_a x) * (W_b x)) to form a second-degree polynomial for channel mixing; PolyConv fuses a coarse dilated (5×5, dilation 2) and a fine (3×3) depthwise branch via elementwise multiplication with channel reversal to create cross-scale interaction terms for spatial mixing; and PolyAttn replaces softmax with a polynomial kernel (s·QK^T + 1)^p followed by ℓ₁ normalization, sharing the Q/K projection and injecting local context via depthwise convolutions. To stabilize training of networks up to ~200 layers, the authors introduce Sigmoid-Scale gating (y = x + σ(λ)·f(x)) to bound residual magnitudes, multi-input skip connections from NASNet providing each cell features from two preceding cells, and a depth-over-width design where narrower layers stacked deeper outperform wider shallower configurations at matched parameters. These modules are instantiated within the MetaFormer framework as CPolyNeXt (polyconvolutional throughout) and APolyNeXt (polyconv in stages 1–2, polyattention in stages 3–4), matching or exceeding ConvFormer and CAFormer across all scales on ImageNet-1K, ADE20K segmentation, and out-of-distribution robustness benchmarks.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, CPolyNeXt (pure convolutional) achieves 80.2%, 83.9%, 84.7%, and 84.9% top-1 accuracy at Tiny (6.4M), Small (26M), Base (40M), and Large (57M) scales, while APolyNeXt (hybrid conv+attention) reaches 80.9%, 84.3%, 84.9%, and 85.2%. These match or exceed the corresponding MetaFormer instantiations (ConvFormer, CAFormer) at every scale. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, CPolyNeXt-S achieves 50.6 mIoU, surpassing ConvFormer-S18 (48.6) and CAFormer-S18 (48.9) at matched parameters. On out-of-distribution robustness, APolyNeXt-B (41M) achieves 42.7 mean corruption error, outperforming CAFormer-S36 (39M, 44.7) with fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MetaFormer instantiations, CPolyNeXt outperforms ConvFormer at every scale (e.g., +0.9 at Small: 83.9 vs. 83.0; +0.6 at Base: 84.7 vs. 84.1), and APolyNeXt outperforms CAFormer at Small (+0.7: 84.3 vs. 83.6) and Base (+0.4: 84.9 vs. 84.5), tying at Large (85.2 vs. 85.2). Against prior polynomial networks, the gains are substantial: CPolyNeXt-S (26M, 4.8G FLOPs, 83.9%) beats DTTN-B (36M, 12.3G FLOPs, 82.4%) by 1.5 points while using 28% fewer parameters and 61% fewer FLOPs, and surpasses MONet-S (33M, 81.3%) by 2.6 points. Against other strong non-polynomial baselines, CPolyNeXt-B (40M, 84.7%) exceeds InternImage-S (50M, 84.2%) and matches UniConvNet-S (50M, 84.5%) at 80% of the parameters, while CPolyNeXt-L (57M, 84.9%) surpasses MogaNet-L (83M, 84.7%) using only 69% of the parameters. On robustness benchmarks, APolyNeXt-B (41M) achieves lower mean corruption error (42.7) than CAFormer-S36 (39M, 44.7) and higher ImageNet-A accuracy (46.8 vs. 40.9), and CPolyNeXt-L (57M) matches or exceeds CAFormer-M36 (99M) on ImageNet-C and ImageNet-R at nearly half the parameters. On ADE20K segmentation, CPolyNeXt-S (54M) achieves 50.6 mIoU, beating ConvFormer-S18 (54M, 48.6) by +2.0 and CAFormer-S18 (54M, 48.9) by +1.7 at matched parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior polynomial network work (MONet, DTTN, Pi-Nets) by targeting the standard module interfaces of modern vision backbones rather than designing custom architectures. It builds on the MetaFormer modular framework (Yu et al., 2024) as the evaluation scaffold, adopts multi-input skip connections from NASNet (Zoph et al., 2018), and draws on the Hadamard-product-as-nonlinearity lineage from Pi-Nets and gated linear units. The key conceptual departure is that polynomial modules preserve input-output compatibility with existing architectural improvements (windowed attention, sparse patterns), whereas prior polynomial networks required committing to entirely new designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong evidence that pointwise activations and exponential softmax are not representational necessities in modern vision backbones, with polynomial modules matching or exceeding activation-based counterparts across four model scales on classification, robustness, and segmentation. The stabilization recipe (Sigmoid-Scale, multi-input skip, depth-over-width) enabling ~200-layer polynomial training is a practical contribution that prior polynomial networks lacked. Open questions include whether the specific training recipe (smaller batch, progressive dropout, LAMB for attention) is truly necessary or an artifact, whether the throughput penalty from depth-over-width limits real-world adoption, and whether end-to-end FHE deployment is feasible given the remaining approximation gaps in normalization.
