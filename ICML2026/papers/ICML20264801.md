## Problem

Existing pretraining data selection methods either rely on task-agnostic quality heuristics that are misaligned with specific downstream capabilities, or distill black-box signals (embeddings, losses) into auxiliary classifiers that are opaque and hard to diagnose. This paper asks whether target-oriented data selection can be achieved directly from interpretable, neuron-level computation in an off-the-shelf LLM, without any additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264801/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of target examples (as few as ~200 samples from benchmark training splits), a large candidate pool of pretraining documents (150B tokens downsampled from RefinedWeb), and an off-the-shelf LLM (e.g., Qwen3-1.7B-Base) used solely for forward-pass NAG extraction. It produces a ranked list of all candidate documents by NAG similarity to the aggregated target profile, from which the top 20% (30B tokens) is selected for pretraining. NAG features are target-independent and extracted once per document, so the same feature store can be reused across arbitrary target tasks without re-extraction.

## Method

NAG-based Ranking characterizes each input by a sparse set of high-impact neurons in an off-the-shelf LLM. For each projection layer (Q, K, V, UP, DOWN), neuron impact is computed as the magnitude of the neuron's column-wise contribution to the layer output (|h_in^T W_{:,k}|), a local proxy validated against end-to-end loss change (Pearson r = 0.71). Per layer, the top-K neurons by impact (K ≈ 0.3% of layer width) are selected to form a Neuron-Activated Graph — a compact set of (layer, neuron) index pairs. Data selection then ranks candidates by a Dice-style similarity between each candidate's NAG and the frequency-weighted NAG profile aggregated over target examples, selecting the top fraction for pretraining. The approach is entirely training-free, model-agnostic, and captures multi-layer computational structure (restricting to the final layer causes a 4.1% average drop), with deactivating just 0.12% of NAG-selected neurons causing a 23.5% performance collapse, confirming they form a sparse functional backbone.

## Results

Under the Single-Target setting, NAG-based Ranking (Qwen3-1.7B backbone) achieves 49.8% average accuracy across six benchmarks, improving by 4.9% over random sampling (44.9%) and 1.0% over the target-oriented BETR baseline (48.8%). The largest single-benchmark gain is on HellaSwag, where NAG reaches 60.6% vs. 51.6% (random) and 57.5% (BETR). Under the Multi-Target setting, NAG (Qwen3-1.7B) maintains a 3.6% average gain over random while BETR degrades below random (44.4% vs. 44.9%), and gains remain consistent across backbone models (4.7%–5.0% average improvement over random).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FineWeb-Edu (a general-quality classifier), NAG (Qwen3-1.7B) wins by +2.4% on average (49.8% vs. 47.4%), with the largest margins on HellaSwag (+4.4%), XStoryCloze (+4.5%), and XWinograd (+4.2%), while FineWeb-Edu actually underperforms random on XStoryCloze (−1.2%) and XWinograd (−0.3%). Against BETR (the state-of-the-art target-oriented method using last-layer embedding similarity), NAG wins by +1.0% on average in the Single-Target setting (49.8% vs. 48.8%), with notable gains on ARC-C (+2.4%) and HellaSwag (+2.2%). The gap widens dramatically in the Multi-Target setting: BETR degrades to 44.4% (below random's 44.9%), while NAG (Qwen3-1.7B) still achieves 48.5%, a 4.1% advantage over BETR. The authors attribute BETR's weakness to its reliance on last-layer embeddings that conflate semantic and stylistic features, whereas NAG aggregates neuron-level signals across all layers. Gains are consistent across three backbone models (Qwen3-1.7B, Llama-3.2-3B, SmolLM3-3B), with average improvements of 4.7%–5.0% over random, confirming model-agnostic effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the sparse-parameter literature (Panigrahi et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2024, 2025) showing that model behavior is governed by small, task-specific neuron subsets, and on PLND's neuron-impact measurement as the mechanistic foundation. It departs from prior target-oriented data selection methods (BETR, SHUM, Thrush, DAIG) that distill black-box signals—last-layer embeddings, LM loss, or perplexity—into auxiliary classifiers, instead using neuron-level activation patterns directly as an interpretable, training-free selection criterion. The approach also complements general-quality selection (FineWeb-Edu, DCLM) by capturing task-relevant signals that quality heuristics miss, and the paper demonstrates additive gains when the two signals are combined.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NAG offers a training-free, interpretable alternative to classifier-based data selection that practitioners can deploy without additional model training, and its robustness across three backbone architectures and both single- and multi-target settings makes it broadly applicable. The deactivation experiment (0.12% of neurons → 23.5% collapse) provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why the method works, which is rare in data-selection literature. Key limitations include evaluation at only 1.2B/30B scale on a single corpus (RefinedWeb), a simple equal-budget mixture for multi-target (acknowledged as a lower bound), and reliance on a single projection type (UP) chosen via ablation rather than theory. Open questions include whether NAG scales to 70B+ extraction models, multilingual or domain-specific corpora, and whether more advanced mixture strategies (RegMix, QuaD-Mix) can close the remaining gap in multi-target settings.
