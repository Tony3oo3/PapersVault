## Problem

Even perfectly group-calibrated scores—including true class probabilities—violate predictive parity (sufficiency) after applying a single threshold, and the only prior exact solution (Baumann et al., 2022) requires continuity and full-support assumptions that fail for the finite-valued scores used in practice. This paper closes that gap by providing an exact, closed-form solution for optimal binary classification under sufficiency when scores take finitely many values.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260257/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only aggregate score statistics: the finite set of score values s_i^a, their group-specific weights P(s_i^a | A=a), and the group proportion P(A=a), together with group membership for each new instance. It outputs a randomized binary decision rule fully specified by selection probabilities P(R=1 | S, A), which can be applied as a post-processing step without access to raw features, additional labels, or any retraining of the underlying predictor.

## Method

The key insight is a geometric characterization of the feasible (PPV, FOR) region: for a fixed selection rate μ, maximizing PPV is a fractional knapsack problem whose greedy solution is a soft threshold on the ordered score bins, and the region is star-convex with center at (π, π). The nontrivial boundary is piecewise—hyperbolic arcs on intervals J_k indexed by the score bin where randomization occurs, plus a vertical segment at p = s_max. Under sufficiency, the feasible set becomes the intersection C^0 ∩ C^1 of two group-specific regions, and its boundary is the pointwise maximum of the two group boundary curves. Algorithm 1 traces this boundary by iterating over subintervals J_{k,l,i} where both the active group and the score-bin indices are fixed (determined by the sign of a quadratic Φ_{k,l}), and evaluating any objective (0-1 loss or deviation from separation) on each subinterval reduces to solving a single quadratic equation and checking endpoints.

## Results

On FICO credit scores, the sufficiency-optimal classifier achieves accuracy 0.8676 (vs. 0.8819 for the unconstrained Bayes classifier) with common PPV 0.91 and FOR 0.23, while the deviation-from-separation minimizer reaches accuracy 0.8659 with Δ_sep = 0.0702. On COMPAS recidivism scores, the sufficiency-optimal classifier attains accuracy 0.6563 (vs. 0.6629 unconstrained), and the deviation minimizer achieves 0.6526 with Δ_sep = 0.1464. On ACS Income (100 train/test splits), the post-processed classifier achieves mean accuracy 0.8167 (vs. 0.8190 unconstrained) while reducing the mean PPV gap from 3.61×10⁻² to 4.8×10⁻³ and the mean FOR gap from 3.29×10⁻² to 3.7×10⁻³.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its sufficiency-optimal post-processing algorithm against the unconstrained group-aware Bayes classifier (thresholding calibrated scores at 1/2 within each group) on three datasets. The accuracy cost of enforcing exact predictive parity is modest: 1.4% on FICO (0.8819 → 0.8676), 1.0% on COMPAS (0.6629 → 0.6563), and 0.23% on ACS Income (0.8190 → 0.8167). The deviation-from-separation minimizer, which is a different objective within the same feasible region, achieves accuracy within 0.17% of the loss-optimal sufficient classifier on FICO and within 0.37% on COMPAS, suggesting the two objectives are nearly aligned in practice. On ACS Income, the post-processed classifier reduces the mean PPV gap by a factor of ~7.5× and the mean FOR gap by ~8.9× compared to the unconstrained baseline, at a negligible accuracy cost. No external fairness post-processing baselines (e.g., Baumann et al. 2022) are directly compared numerically, as the paper's contribution is an exact solution for finite-valued scores where prior methods require continuity assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on achieving sufficiency (predictive parity) in binary classification through post-processing. Canetti et al. (2019) introduced sufficiency-satisfying classifiers via abstention, and Baumann et al. (2022) showed sufficiency can be achieved without abstentions but under continuity and full-support assumptions on score distributions. The current paper removes these distributional assumptions by working with finite-valued group-calibrated scores, providing an exact geometric characterization of the feasible (PPV, FOR) region and a post-processing algorithm based on the fractional knapsack structure. It also builds on the deviation-from-separation measure Δ_sep(R) introduced by Benger & Ligett (2025) to study the sufficiency–separation tradeoff within the same framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first exact solution for optimal binary classification under sufficiency with finite scores, requiring only calibrated scores and group membership—no retraining or access to raw features. A key practical implication is that the optimal sufficient classifier generally cannot be a simple threshold (the intersection boundary may contain no deterministic-threshold point in either group), necessitating randomized rules. The main limitation is the binary protected-attribute setting; as the number of groups grows, the intersection of group-wise feasible regions may become empty, making exact sufficiency infeasible. The authors suggest relaxing strict sufficiency to minimize PPV/FOR gaps across more than two groups as a promising direction.
