## Problem

Adapting LLMs to downstream tasks via prompt engineering or fine-tuning incurs high inference latency, training overhead, and storage costs. Hypernetworks offer a single-pass alternative, but prior approaches are constrained by generating LoRAs for only a subset of layers, using restrictive MLP bottlenecks, or reusing small networks multiple times—preventing them from achieving the expressivity needed for complex tasks across all LLM layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262921/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language context (up to ~1,150 tokens) and a question as input, and produces a rank-8 LoRA adapter that encodes the context into model parameters. The generated LoRA is then applied to the frozen LLM for downstream QA, where the original context is no longer needed in the input—effectively converting in-context knowledge into in-parameter knowledge in one forward pass. For long contexts, a recurrent variant (SHINE-R) processes chunks sequentially, with LoRA memory growing linearly with context length.

## Method

SHINE uses the LLM backbone itself as part of the hypernetwork: learnable memory embeddings are appended to the context, and the LLM (augmented with Meta LoRA) compresses the context into memory states extracted from all layers. A lightweight M2P Transformer then processes these multi-layer memory states using alternating row (across tokens) and column (across layers) bidirectional self-attention, enabling information to flow from deep layers back to shallow layers—mimicking backpropagation's dependency structure. The final memory states are reshaped directly into LoRA weight matrices (A and B) for each layer. Training follows a two-stage pipeline: self-supervised pretraining on 6B tokens with reconstruction and completion objectives, followed by supervised instruction fine-tuning on QA datasets, all without any test-time gradient optimization.

## Results

On the 1QA benchmarks (Table 2), SHINE achieves F1 scores of 63.6 on SQuAD, 40.7 on MS MARCO V1, 59.0 on HotpotQA, 28.5 on MuSiQue, and 60.2 on 2WikiMultihopQA, substantially outperforming the Generative Adapter baseline on all six QA datasets. On the multi-turn MS MARCO MQA task, SHINE reaches 55.6 F1 with only 0.3s amortizable overhead (vs. 29.3s for SFT), approaching In-Context prompting (69.4 F1) while matching its generation speed. In the test-time training comparison on SQuAD, SHINE scores 63.6 in a single forward pass, surpassing SEAL (58.2) and PaST-50 (58.9) which require iterative optimization over 200 documents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Generative Adapter (Chen et al., 2025), SHINE wins on all six core QA benchmarks: SQuAD (63.6 vs. 70.3 — actually loses here), MS MARCO V1 (40.7 vs. 35.0), MS MARCO V2 (40.1 vs. 27.9), HotpotQA (59.0 vs. 40.8), MuSiQue (28.5 vs. 19.4), and 2WikiMultihopQA (60.2 vs. 32.9), with the largest margins on multi-hop tasks (+18.2 on HotpotQA, +27.3 on 2Wiki). Against test-time training baselines on SQuAD, SHINE (63.6) outperforms SEAL (58.2) by 5.4 points and PaST-50 (58.9) by 4.7 points, while requiring a single forward pass instead of iterative optimization over 200 documents. Notably, SHINE exceeds In-Context prompting on MS MARCO V1 (40.7 vs. 34.2), MS MARCO V2 (40.1 vs. 31.3), and 2WikiMultihopQA (60.2 vs. 48.7), suggesting the parameterized context can sometimes outperform direct context access for retrieval-heavy tasks, though it trails In-Context on SQuAD (63.6 vs. 86.8) and SQuAD-N variants where the full context is short and directly accessible.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHINE extends the hypernetwork-for-LLM-adaptation line initiated by Generative Adapter (Chen et al., 2025), Text-to-LoRA (Charakorn et al., 2025), and ICAE (Ge et al., 2024), all of which use the LLM's own hidden states to predict LoRA weights in a single forward pass. The key architectural departure is replacing the segment-wise MLP generation (which treats each weight block independently) with a lightweight M2P Transformer that performs bidirectional row/column attention over memory states collected from all LLM layers, enabling global information flow between layers and tokens. The training pipeline also departs from prior work by using 6B pretraining tokens with reconstruction/completion objectives followed by instruction fine-tuning, a scale far exceeding previous hypernetwork implementations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SHINE offers a practical middle ground between in-context learning (which consumes context window and adds inference latency) and fine-tuning (which requires per-task training overhead), achieving comparable QA performance with negligible amortizable cost (0.3s vs. 29.3s for SFT). The clean scaling behavior across backbone size, M2P depth, and LoRA rank without visible capacity saturation suggests the approach could benefit from further scale. However, significant gaps to In-Context learning remain on harder multi-hop and long-context tasks, and the recurrent SHINE-R variant still trails ICL substantially, indicating the single-pass paradigm has not yet fully closed the information-theoretic gap of direct context access. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond QA to generation or reasoning tasks, and whether the bidirectional attention design is truly necessary or whether simpler alternatives suffice at larger scale.
