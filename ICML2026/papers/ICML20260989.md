## Problem

Defocus blur in pathological microscopy is spatially varying and locally discontinuous due to depth variations, tissue heterogeneity, and refractive index inhomogeneity, making the standard shift-invariant convolution assumption invalid. Existing deep learning deblurring methods (CNNs, Transformers, Mamba) treat the task as finite-dimensional pixel regression without physical grounding, while existing neural operators rely on globally parameterized kernels that assume smoothness and stationarity, failing to capture the piecewise heterogeneous blur structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel grayscale defocused microscopic image (e.g., 696×520 for BBBC006, 256×256 patches for 3DHistech) and produces a deblurred sharp image at the same resolution. During training, 256×256 patches are used with horizontal and vertical flipping augmentation. The network operates on non-overlapping 8×8 spatial elements, and the multi-scale encoder produces feature maps at four scales (48, 96, 192, 384 channels) before the DG operator is applied at the finest scales.

## Method

DGNO parameterizes the defocus integral operator using a discontinuous Galerkin decomposition: the global kernel integral is split into element-local volume integral operators (computed via Galerkin-type attention with learned Q, K, V and Monte-Carlo quadrature over 8×8 elements) and interface numerical fluxes that mediate controlled cross-element coupling. Two flux formulations are provided: DGNO-Face, which integrates over element interfaces using classical flux choices (central, upwind, jump, avg-jump), and DGNO-Cell (P0DG), which derives the flux directly from neighboring volume operators for a lightweight piecewise-constant approximation. The full architecture uses a Mamba-based multi-scale encoder to lift the input into feature space, followed by T=2 iterations of the DG operator layer with residual connections, and a pointwise projection to produce the output. A learnable penalty coefficient in the flux adaptively controls inter-element coupling strength, and boundary conditions (Neumann, Dirichlet, periodic) are enforced at the operator level by modifying the flux evaluation on boundary faces.

## Results

On the three pathology defocus deblurring benchmarks, DGNO-Cell achieves 37.22 dB PSNR / 0.959 SSIM / 0.103 LPIPS on BBBC006_w1, while DGNO-Face leads on the harder BBBC006_w2 (32.66 dB) and 3DHistech (34.02 dB / 0.095 LPIPS). Both variants outperform the strongest baseline MPT+EFCR by 1.78 dB (BBBC006_w1), 2.18 dB (BBBC006_w2), and 0.44 dB (3DHistech). On real-world defocus datasets, DGNO-Face attains 26.42 dB PSNR on DPDD and 25.08 dB on RealDOF, surpassing all compared restoration methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGNO decisively outperforms all seven pathology-specific baselines (GKMNet, MIMO-Unet, NAFNet, SwinIR, MambaIRv2, Restormer, MPT+EFCR) across all three microscopy datasets, with the largest margin over MPT+EFCR reaching +2.18 dB on BBBC006_w2. Against the global Galerkin neural operator SRNO (36.71 dB), DGNO-Cell gains +0.51 dB on BBBC006_w1, confirming that the discontinuous element-local decomposition is more faithful to spatially varying blur than a globally smooth kernel. On the non-blind Ring Deconvolution comparison (Table 4), DGNO (36.51 dB) falls short of Ring (40.15 dB), which has access to the ground-truth spatially varying PSF, but still exceeds the single-PSF deconvolution baseline (32.87 dB) by 3.64 dB. On real-world DPDD, DGNO-Face (26.42 dB) beats MambaIR (26.11), FSNet (26.22), and ConvIR (26.36) by 0.1–0.3 dB. On RealDOF, DGNO (25.08 dB) surpasses DPDNet (22.52), IFAN (24.71), and EAMamba (24.59) by 0.4–2.6 dB, demonstrating transferability beyond pathology imaging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the volume-interface decomposition from discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods for PDEs into the neural operator framework, specifically adapting the Galerkin-type attention parameterization of SRNO (Wei & Zhang, 2023) by restricting the global kernel integral to element-local volume operators and restoring cross-element coupling through operator-valued numerical fluxes. It departs from the dominant CNN/Transformer/Mamba paradigm for defocus deblurring, which treats the problem as finite-dimensional image regression, and from global neural operators (FNO, SRNO, DiffFNO) that implicitly assume smoothness and stationarity. The key conceptual departure is that the DG decomposition here is a deliberate discretization strategy for an integral operator (which has no natural integration-by-parts structure), rather than a consequence of differential-form weak formulations as in classical DG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DGNO framework provides a physically grounded, interpretable operator-level formulation for spatially varying defocus deblurring that naturally accommodates piecewise heterogeneity and local discontinuities inherent to pathology imaging, while the effective-rank and LAM analyses offer mechanistic evidence that the DG structure enriches basis diversity and localizes activations at boundaries. The approach generalizes beyond pathology to natural-image defocus (DPDD, RealDOF) and can be plugged into existing backbones (NAFNet, Restormer, MPT) as a lifting module, suggesting broader applicability. Open questions include whether the specific flux and boundary-condition choices matter at scale, whether the P0DG approximation's simplicity advantage persists on more complex 3D volumetric data, and whether the operator-level DG formulation extends to other spatially varying degradations (motion blur, chromatic aberration) beyond defocus.
