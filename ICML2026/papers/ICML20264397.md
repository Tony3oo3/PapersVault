## Problem

Quantum neural networks lack a constructive approximation theory: prior quantum models either relied on non-constructive global gates, were essentially hybrid (with expressivity coming from classical components), or only provided L∞ error bounds that are overly stringent for average-case learning tasks. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a fully constructive QNN architecture with a proven Universal Approximation Property for square-integrable functions and rigorous L2 error bounds for Sobolev functions, quantifying circuit width, depth, and parameter complexity.

## Input / Output

The model takes a d-dimensional continuous input vector x (in [-π,π]^d for Fourier mode or [-1,1]^d for Chebyshev mode) and produces a scalar output in [0,1]. Inputs are encoded via data re-uploading into rotation gate angles (Rz gates for Fourier, Ry gates for Chebyshev), and the output is extracted by measuring the probability amplitude of the |0⟩ state with a classical square-root postprocess and a non-trainable rescale coefficient a. The number of spectrum-selection layers n determines the truncation order of the series and thus the model's expressivity.

## Method

SAQNN is a constructive QNN inspired by the Linear Combination of Unitaries (LCU) framework, decomposing a truncated Fourier or Chebyshev series into three quantum circuit components: (1) a state preparation block using multiplexor-Ry gates that maps coefficient magnitudes |c_j| to probability amplitudes on m=⌈log n⌉ control qubits, (2) n layers of spectrum selection where each layer applies a controlled-Rz (or Ry) gate implementing one basis function e^{ij·x} (or T_j(x)) conditioned on the control register, and (3) a phase injection layer per spectrum-selection block that attaches the phase arg(c_j) via a controlled phase gate. The full circuit has the structure P(θ)† V_φ(x) P(θ), so that the final |0⟩⟨0| measurement yields the series value. The paper proves UAP for all L2 functions and, for Sobolev functions H_u^s, establishes that O(log n) qubits, O(n log n) circuit depth, and O(n) parameters suffice for L2 accuracy ε, with n = O((d+1/ε)^{16(1/ε)^{2/s}}). This achieves optimal parameter complexity matching Kolmogorov and manifold n-width lower bounds, and yields polynomial-in-d scaling that avoids the curse of dimensionality suffered by classical ReLU FNNs in high-dimensional regimes.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative contribution is Theorem 2, which establishes that SAQNN approximates Sobolev functions f ∈ H_u^s([−π,π]^d) to L₂ accuracy ε using O(log n) qubits, O(n log n) circuit depth, and O(n) parameters, where n = O((d + 1/ε)^(16(1/ε)^(2/s))). Numerical verification on three 2D target functions yields test MSEs of 7.20×10⁻⁴ (n=49, Fourier), 2.62×10⁻⁴ (n=3, Fourier), and 4.51×10⁻⁴ (n=6, Chebyshev). In the high-dimensional regime (large d, fixed ε), SAQNN's resource costs scale polynomially in d, whereas the best known classical ReLU-FNN bounds scale as O((2π)^(3d²/4s)·(1/ε)^(d/2s)) in circuit size, demonstrating a quantum advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SAQNN against the state-of-the-art classical ReLU feed-forward networks (FNNs) for L₂-approximation of Sobolev spaces, using the best known bounds from Yang (2025) and Lu et al. (2021). In the high-dimensional regime (large d, constant ε and s), SAQNN achieves polynomial scaling in d for all resource metrics, while classical FNNs suffer from a curse of dimensionality with costs exponential in d (e.g., circuit size O((2π)^(3d²/4s)·(1/ε)^(d/2s))). This constitutes a clear quantum advantage for high-dimensional function approximation. In the opposite regime (fixed d, varying ε), SAQNN achieves the optimal O(n) parameter complexity, matching the fundamental n-width lower bounds for both linear and nonlinear approximation methods, meaning no classical or quantum method can asymptotically beat this parameter count. The paper does not conduct head-to-head empirical benchmarking on standard ML datasets; the numerical experiments (2D functions, 100 training points) serve only as feasibility verification rather than competitive comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAQNN is architecturally inspired by the Linear Combination of Unitaries (LCU) framework from Hamiltonian simulation (Childs & Wiebe, 2012; Gilyen et al., 2019), repurposing its block-encoding structure to implement truncated Fourier series on quantum circuits. It departs from prior QNN universal approximation results—Schuld et al. (2021) gave a non-constructive multi-qubit model relying on arbitrary global gates, and Yu et al. (2024) provided the first constructive model but only under L∞ norm for Lipschitz/Hölder functions—by offering a fully gate-level constructive circuit with L2 error bounds for Sobolev spaces. The paper also positions itself against quantum-classical hybrid models (Goto et al., 2021; Pérez-Salinas et al., 2021) where expressivity comes from classical components, insisting that SAQNN's approximation power is entirely quantum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish L2 approximation error bounds for QNNs on Sobolev spaces in terms of circuit width, depth, and parameter complexity, and to show that the resulting parameter complexity is optimal (matching Kolmogorov and manifold n-width lower bounds). The asymptotic quantum advantage over ReLU FNNs—polynomial in input dimension d versus exponential—is a concrete, constructive separation result that could guide future QNN design. However, the circuit depth of O(n log n) and the need for O(1/ε²) measurements (or O(1/ε) with amplitude estimation) remain significant barriers for near-term hardware, and the numerical experiments (d=2, 200 points, 80 iterations) are purely feasibility checks. Open questions include barren plateau analysis, sample complexity, and whether the modular structure enables practical training at scale.
