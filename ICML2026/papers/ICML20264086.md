## Problem

Unified multimodal models that combine image understanding and generation via diffusion or flow-matching require dozens of full forward passes through the entire backbone at inference, making them slow and resource-intensive. Existing approaches either rely on autoregressive token decoding (slow for high-resolution images) or invoke all transformer layers at every sampling timestep, with no mechanism to reduce per-step compute while preserving generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264086/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image generation, the model takes a natural language text prompt and produces a 256×256 RGB image via 40 flow-matching sampling steps with classifier-free guidance (scale 5). For multimodal understanding, it accepts an image plus a text question and outputs a text response, inheriting capabilities directly from the frozen Qwen2-VL-2B backbone. Images are encoded into a compact latent space using a Deep Compression Autoencoder (DC-AE) with 32× spatial downsampling, and the generative pathway operates on these latents rather than raw pixels.

## Method

LaTtE-Flow builds on a frozen Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct (28 transformer layers) and adds a parallel trainable generative pathway initialized from the same pretrained weights. The core innovation is Layerwise Timestep Experts: the 28 layers are partitioned into K=4 non-overlapping groups of M=7 consecutive layers, each trained exclusively on a specific timestep interval of the flow-matching process (with 100-step overlaps during training for smooth boundary transitions). At inference, only the 7-layer group corresponding to the current timestep is activated, reducing per-step compute from O(L×T) to O(M×T) and yielding a theoretical K-fold speedup. A second mechanism, Timestep-Conditioned Residual Attention, augments each image self-attention layer with a gated residual from the previous layer's attention map, where the gate is a per-head tanh function of the timestep embedding, enabling adaptive cross-layer attention reuse. The model is trained with a conditional flow-matching objective on 1.2M ImageNet captions at 256×256 resolution for 240K steps, using a mixture-of-transformer architecture where noisy image latents attend bidirectionally to each other and unidirectionally to multimodal context tokens via a hybrid 3D/2D RoPE positional encoding scheme.

## Results

On ImageNet-50K (256×256, 40 sampling steps, CFG=5), LaTtE-Flow achieves FID 5.79, IS 213.1, Precision 0.78, and Recall 0.69 using only 500M activated parameters per step and 0.052 s/image inference time. This is 48× faster than Show-o (FID 31.26, 2.493 s/img) and 6× faster than Janus Pro (FID 23.68, 0.311 s/img), while substantially outperforming both in generation quality. On multimodal understanding benchmarks, LaTtE-Flow exactly matches its frozen Qwen2-VL-2B backbone (e.g., MMBench 74.9, MMMU 41.1, TextVQA 79.7), confirming that the generative pathway does not degrade understanding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against unified multimodal models, LaTtE-Flow dominates on both quality and speed: it achieves FID 5.79 vs. Show-o's 31.26 (48× faster, 0.052 vs. 2.493 s/img) and FID 5.79 vs. Janus Pro's 23.68 (6× faster, 0.052 vs. 0.311 s/img), while also matching or exceeding their Recall (0.69 vs. 0.69 and 0.49). Compared to its own Vanilla baseline (same architecture without timestep experts or residual attention), LaTtE-Flow improves FID from 6.33 to 5.79 and IS from 192.4 to 213.1 while using only 500M vs. 2.0B activated parameters per step (3× faster inference). Against dedicated diffusion models, LaTtE-Flow is 31–45× faster than DiT-L/2 and DiT-XL/2 (0.052 vs. 1.786/2.592 s/img) but trails DiT-XL/2 on FID (5.79 vs. 2.27) due to fewer total parameters and fewer sampling steps; it slightly outperforms DiT-L/2 on Recall (0.69 vs. 0.57). Versus MaskGIT, LaTtE-Flow is ~1.8× slower (0.052 vs. 0.029 s/img) but achieves better FID (5.79 vs. 6.18) and higher Recall (0.69 vs. 0.51). On understanding benchmarks, LaTtE-Flow exactly replicates Qwen2-VL-2B scores (frozen backbone), matching or exceeding all unified baselines on most metrics (e.g., MMMU 41.1 vs. Janus Pro 36.3, TextVQA 79.7 vs. EMU3 64.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

LaTtE-Flow sits at the intersection of two active research threads: unified multimodal models (Show-o, Janus Pro, Transfusion) that combine understanding and generation in a single architecture, and expert-based diffusion/flow-matching models (eDiffi, Remix-DiT, Timestep Master) that assign specialized denoisers to different timestep ranges. Its key departure is architectural rather than algorithmic: rather than maintaining separate expert networks per timestep interval, LaTtE-Flow partitions the layers of a single pretrained VLM (Qwen2-VL-2B) into disjoint groups, each responsible for a subset of timesteps, so that only a fraction of layers are activated per sampling step. The Timestep-Conditioned Residual Attention mechanism, which gates cross-layer attention reuse via a learned timestep embedding, has no direct precedent in prior diffusion or flow-matching literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6× inference speedup over recent unified models (Janus Pro, Show-o) while maintaining competitive FID (5.79) makes unified multimodal generation more practical for real-time and resource-constrained deployment, a gap the authors explicitly target. However, the evaluation is limited to ImageNet at 256×256 with only 240K training steps, and the uniform timestep partitioning with fixed group size (M=7) is acknowledged as suboptimal; whether the layerwise expert decomposition generalizes to higher resolutions, diverse datasets, or non-uniform timestep schedules remains open. The finding that removing Timestep-Conditioned Residual Attention degrades FID by 2.47 points suggests that cross-layer attention structure in flow-matching is more informative than previously assumed, which could inform future architectural designs beyond this specific model.
