## Problem

DPO is widely adopted as a simpler drop-in replacement for RLHF with claimed theoretical equivalence, but this paper proves the equivalence is conditional on an implicit assumption: the RLHF-optimal policy must prefer human-preferred responses over dispreferred ones. When the reference policy is sufficiently misaligned (δ_π_ref ≤ −Δr*/β), this assumption fails, causing DPO to optimize relative advantage over the reference rather than absolute alignment, leading to pathological convergence where loss decreases while the policy systematically prefers dispreferred responses.

## Input / Output

The method takes pairwise preference data (prompt, preferred response, dispreferred response) and a frozen reference policy (e.g., Llama-3-8B-Instruct). A key practical detail is that CPO requires precomputing reference-based adaptive margins γ(1/π_ref(y_w|x) + 1/π_ref(y_l|x)) for each preference pair before training, which makes the loss stationary and enables standard gradient descent without non-stationary objectives. The output is an aligned policy that provably satisfies δ_π > 0 for all preference pairs when the margin parameter γ ≥ γ*, with E-CPOC additionally requiring no reward model.

## Method

The core insight is that DPO's derivation implicitly assumes the RLHF-optimal policy respects human preferences (δ_π* > 0), which is violated when the reference policy's log-probability ratio δ_π_ref is sufficiently negative relative to the reward difference. CPO augments the RLHF objective with a preference alignment constraint γ·E[δ_π], which translates to subtracting an adaptive margin γ(1/π_ref(y_w|x) + 1/π_ref(y_l|x)) from the DPO logits, guaranteeing non-negative effective margins in the soft margin ranking interpretation. E-CPOC eliminates the need for a reward model by exploiting the monotonicity of the margin function in Δr, using a conservative upper bound Φ_cons(δ_π_ref) = (1/τ)log(1+exp(τ(γ−δ_π_ref))) that achieves formal equivalence to explicitly constrained RLHF under standard statistical learning assumptions. Geometrically, DPO implements soft margin ranking with a potentially negative target margin (δ_π_ref), while CPO and E-CPOC correct this by ensuring non-negative margins, providing an intuitive explanation for DPO's failure modes and a principled connection to the learning-to-rank literature.

## Results

On Llama-3-8B-Instruct with UltraFeedback-ArmorN, CPO achieves the highest AlpacaEval 2 win rate of 25.15% and length-controlled win rate of 26.57%, while maintaining a competitive average response length of 1879 tokens. On Arena-Hard, CPO reaches 32.6% win rate (90% CI: -1.9/+2.4), a +2.6% gain over the runner-up SimPO (30.0%) and +3.7% over DPO (28.9%). On IFEval, CPO achieves the best strict accuracy (35.12%) and loose accuracy (43.99%), confirming gains extend to instruction-following tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPO outperforms all 10 baselines on both AlpacaEval 2 and Arena-Hard. Against the strongest baseline DPO, CPO gains +0.55% WR and +1.48% LC on AlpacaEval 2, and a substantial +3.7% on Arena-Hard (32.6% vs 28.9%), indicating the margin correction is most beneficial on difficult, discriminative prompts. Against SimPO (the best Arena-Hard baseline at 30.0%), CPO leads by +2.6% on Arena-Hard and +1.67% WR on AlpacaEval 2, while maintaining a similar response length (1879 vs 1810 tokens). Against RDPO, CPO gains +0.90% WR and +1.56% LC on AlpacaEval 2 and +4.1% on Arena-Hard. The gap widens considerably against weaker baselines: CPO surpasses IPO by +3.67% WR, KTO by +7.43% WR, and ORPO by +8.15% WR on AlpacaEval 2. On IFEval, CPO also leads all baselines in both strict (35.12% vs 34.57% RDPO) and loose (43.99% vs 43.62% RDPO) accuracy, confirming the improvement generalizes beyond conversational benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and the RLHF framework (Christiano et al., 2017), but departs from the widely-accepted narrative of unconditional DPO-RLHF equivalence by proving it is conditional on an implicit assumption about reference policy quality. It also draws on the margin ranking literature (Burges et al., 2005; Schroff et al., 2015) to provide a geometric interpretation, showing DPO implements soft margin ranking with potentially negative target margins. Unlike other DPO variants (IPO, SimPO, KTO, ORPO) that modify the loss function heuristically, CPO/E-CPOC are derived from augmenting the RLHF objective with explicit constraints, preserving the full RLHF-BT theoretical chain while adding provable alignment guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 45.5% of preference pairs violate the implicit assumption on an instruction-tuned model (Llama-3-8B-Instruct) suggests DPO's theoretical guarantees are far from vacuous in practice, and the misalignment experiments (Table 4) convincingly show DPO degrades while CPO remains robust under corrupted references. However, the empirical validation is limited to a single model scale and one training dataset, and E-CPOC lacks experimental evaluation. Open questions include whether the conditional equivalence issue extends to other preference optimization methods, whether the margin correction generalizes to larger models and more diverse preference distributions, and whether the theoretical assumptions (approximate realizability, non-degenerate preferences) hold in practice for overparameterized LLMs.
