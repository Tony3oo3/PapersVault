## Problem

Standard LoRA assigns uniform rank to all modules, ignoring significant heterogeneity in module sensitivity to downstream tasks. Existing adaptive allocation methods either couple rank adjustment to training (incurring per-step SVD overhead and producing non-reusable structures) or plan structure via gradient-based probing that treats all samples equally, missing the fact that hard samples yield more informative gradient signals for identifying critical modules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260908/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained model's frozen weights, a small probe dataset (a few hundred samples), and a total rank budget R_tot. It outputs integer per-module rank allocations {r_ℓ} that sum to at most R_tot. Crucially, the output is a scale-invariant structural prior — the relative importance ratios between modules are independent of R_tot — so a single probe can be reused across different rank budgets and compatible LoRA backends (LoRA, LoRA+, DoRA, PiSSA) without re-probing, as long as the task, base model, and target-module set remain fixed.

## Method

CSPLoRA operates in three decoupled stages before any adapter training. First, confidence-guided probing reweights each probe sample by its prediction difficulty (1 − confidence) using a softmax with temperature γ, so that samples the model struggles with contribute disproportionately to the gradient signal; the weighted loss is backpropagated to compute per-layer Taylor saliency scores S_ℓ = ‖W_ℓ ⊙ ∇W_ℓ L_w‖₁. Second, a logarithmic smoothing transformation s̃_ℓ = log(1 + S_ℓ) compresses the heavy-tailed score distribution to prevent pathological winner-takes-all concentration while preserving relative ordering. Third, ranks are allocated via a baseline-plus-proportional-boost rule r_ℓ = r_min + ⌊(R_tot − L·r_min)·s̃_ℓ/Σs̃_k⌋, which is provably optimal under logarithmic utility (diminishing marginal returns) and is scale-invariant: the ratio (r_i − r_min)/(r_j − r_min) = s̃_i/s̃_j is independent of R_tot, enabling the 'probe once, deploy everywhere' workflow across compatible configurations.

## Results

On LLaMA-2-7B commonsense reasoning (8 benchmarks), CSPLoRA improves over uniform LoRA by +1.25 points (74.74 vs. 73.49 avg), over DoRA by +1.43 (74.91 vs. 73.48), and over PiSSA by +0.72 (76.97 vs. 76.25). On RoBERTa-large GLUE, CSP-DoRA reaches 88.65 avg, matching full fine-tuning (88.64), while CSP-LoRA improves over uniform LoRA by +0.91 (86.21 vs. 85.30), with the largest single-task gain on RTE (+5.78). On arithmetic reasoning (GSM8K), CSP-PiSSA improves over PiSSA by +2.35 points on LLaMA-2-7B (43.77 vs. 41.42). The planned rank structure is reusable across rank budgets (r=4, 8, 16) and LoRA backends from a single ~67-second probe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSPLoRA consistently outperforms all four uniform-rank backends (LoRA, LoRA+, DoRA, PiSSA) when applied as a rank planner, with the largest gains on LLaMA-2-7B commonsense reasoning (+1.25 over LoRA, +1.43 over DoRA) and GLUE RTE (+5.78 over LoRA). Against the training-coupled adaptive method AdaLoRA, CSPLoRA wins by a wide margin (e.g., 74.74 vs. 70.17 on LLaMA-2-7B commonsense, 86.21 vs. 85.30 on GLUE) while using ~30% fewer trainable parameters (19.77M vs. 29.99M on LLaMA-2-7B). Against the decoupled planner GoRA, CSPLoRA also wins on average (74.74 vs. 77.25 on LLaMA-2-7B commonsense is actually GoRA winning; however on LLaMA-3.1-8B, CSP-LoRA 84.72 vs. GoRA 85.23 is a narrow GoRA win, while on GLUE CSP-LoRA 86.21 vs. GoRA 86.57 is also a narrow GoRA win). The one notable exception is LLaMA-3.1-8B with LoRA+, where CSP-LoRA+ (84.44) slightly underperforms uniform LoRA+ (84.69), suggesting that when a backend is already near-optimal for a task, the marginal benefit of structural planning diminishes. Overall, CSPLoRA's advantage is most pronounced when the uniform backend leaves clear allocation inefficiency, and the same probed structure transfers across compatible backends and rank budgets without re-probing.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSPLoRA extends the decoupled structural planning paradigm introduced by GoRA, which estimates module importance via gradient-based saliency before training. It departs from GoRA by adding a data-centric dimension—confidence-weighted sample reweighting during probing—complementing the metric-centric focus of prior importance estimation (Fisher, Taylor saliency). The method also explicitly validates cross-budget and cross-backend reusability of the planned rank structure, a property that training-coupled methods like AdaLoRA and DyLoRA cannot offer due to their per-run coupling. Conceptually, the confidence-weighting mechanism draws on focal loss and curriculum learning ideas but repurposes them for structural planning rather than training dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'probe once, deploy everywhere' workflow is practically valuable for practitioners who need to explore multiple rank budgets or LoRA backends without re-running expensive planning, and the 11–67 second probe cost makes this nearly free. However, the performance gains are modest (typically +0.5 to +1.7 points) and diminish on stronger backends (LoRA+) and larger models (LLaMA-3.1), suggesting the allocation inefficiency that CSPLoRA exploits is already partially addressed by better parameterizations. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to vision-language models, encoder-decoder architectures beyond T5-base, and instruction-tuning settings where task heterogeneity may weaken the single-probe assumption.
