## Problem

Existing visual token compression methods for MLLMs, which retain over 90% of performance on standard vision-language tasks, suffer catastrophic degradation (retaining barely ~20%) when applied to MLLM-based Referring Expression Segmentation. This paper investigates why this occurs and asks whether token compression for RES can be designed purely from positional information, without any semantic or attention-based criteria.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262982/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on an RGB image encoded into a regular 2D grid of visual tokens (e.g., 24×24 = 576 tokens for Text4Seg, 27×27 = 729 for InstructSeg) paired with a freeform natural-language referring expression. It compresses the visual token sequence to a target count (e.g., 192 or 72 tokens) while strictly preserving each retained token's original 2D positional index, then feeds the compressed sequence to the LLM. The output is a dense pixel-level segmentation mask covering every patch in the image, distinguishing it from sparse prediction tasks that only require understanding a few discriminative regions.

## Method

PAYN is a training-free, plug-and-play token compression method that selects and retains visual tokens based solely on their 2D spatial coordinates, requiring no attention maps, feature norms, or semantic content. It has two components: (1) position-guided token selection, which ensures adequate token coverage in every local neighboring region via either checkerboard-style spatial sampling (retaining tokens where (x+y) mod s = φ on a regular grid) or farthest point sampling in the 2D coordinate space; and (2) position index preserving, which keeps the original positional indices of retained tokens rather than re-indexing them contiguously, thereby maintaining local spatial relational consistency in the LLM's attention. The core insight is that RES, as a dense prediction task requiring per-patch label prediction, is far more sensitive to local positional shifts than sparse tasks, making spatial position the dominant (and sufficient) criterion for token selection.

## Results

On the Text4Seg (LLaVA-1.5-7B) baseline, PAYN achieves 66.4% average cIoU at 192 visual tokens (↓66.7%), retaining 88.6% of the vanilla model's 74.9% performance. This substantially outperforms the best competing diversity-based methods (DivPrune 63.6%, EVTP-IVS 63.8%, VisPruner 62.8%) and dramatically surpasses similarity/attention-based methods (ToMe 12.9%, VisionZip 10.3%). On the InstructSeg baseline at 72 tokens (↓90%), PAYN reaches 71.7% average cIoU (87.6% relative), again leading all baselines. PAYN also achieves 1.39× inference speedup on Text4Seg and 1.44× on InstructSeg with no additional computational overhead for token selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAYN consistently outperforms all six compared token compression methods across both RES baselines (Text4Seg and InstructSeg) and all compression ratios. At the most aggressive 192-token setting on Text4Seg, PAYN (66.4% avg cIoU) beats the strongest diversity-based competitors DivPrune (63.6%) and EVTP-IVS (63.8%) by ~2.6–2.8 points, and VisPruner (62.8%) by 3.6 points. The gap widens dramatically against similarity/attention-based methods: ToMe (12.9%) and VisionZip (10.3%) suffer near-total collapse, losing over 60 points relative to PAYN. On InstructSeg at 72 tokens (↓90%), PAYN (71.7%) leads DivPrune (69.9%) and EVTP-IVS (70.2%) by 1.5–1.8 points, while ToMe (66.6%) and VisionZip (59.9%) trail by 5–12 points. The paper also shows that PAYN's advantage grows with compression ratio, confirming that position-only selection becomes increasingly critical as fewer tokens are retained.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of MLLM-based Referring Expression Segmentation (RES) and visual token compression, two areas that have developed largely independently. It departs from the dominant paradigm in token compression—which selects tokens based on semantic content such as attention scores, feature similarity, or feature-space diversity—by demonstrating that for dense prediction tasks like RES, purely positional information (checkerboard sampling or spatial FPS) is not only sufficient but superior to all content-based criteria. Conceptually, it repurposes classical spatial sampling strategies (checkerboard grids, farthest point sampling) in a new context, guided by the empirical finding that preserving original position embeddings is the single most critical factor for RES performance after token compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that position alone outperforms attention-, similarity-, and diversity-based token selection for RES challenges the field's working assumption that token importance is determined by semantic content, and the insight likely generalizes to other dense prediction tasks (semantic segmentation, depth estimation) where local spatial consistency is critical. Practically, the method is a true free lunch: training-free, feature-agnostic, and precomputable, introducing zero additional inference overhead. Open questions include whether the position-dominance insight extends to video RES or other dense prediction settings, and how to handle the acknowledged failure mode where extremely small objects occupy fewer tokens than the compression budget allows.
