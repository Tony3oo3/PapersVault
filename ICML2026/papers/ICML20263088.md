## Problem

Existing LoRA-based continual learning methods treat representational conflict as something to avoid through orthogonality constraints, gradient projection, or per-task modular isolation, yet these strategies either inhibit positive transfer or lack coordination to integrate shared and task-specific knowledge. The paper argues that representational conflicts across tasks are inherent and cannot be fully eliminated, calling for a framework that directly manages rather than avoids these conflicts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263088/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-R/A and DomainNet) and outputs class labels in a rehearsal-free class-incremental setting. A frozen ViT-B/16 backbone processes each image, with only rank-10 LoRA modules on the query and value projections of all 12 attention layers being trained per task. At inference, all task LoRA modules are merged into a single weight matrix via SABER's alignment and recomposition pipeline, so no task ID is needed at test time.

## Method

SABER reframes continual learning as structured conflict management through three stages. First, subspace alignment projects all task LoRA matrices into a common (Tr×Tr) subspace: input subspaces are aligned via a shared random orthogonal matrix P (rows assigned to each A_t and frozen), while output subspaces are aligned via the left singular vectors Q_T of the stacked B_t matrices. Second, knowledge recomposition decomposes the summed aligned matrix via SVD to extract task-common knowledge (top-k singular directions), then isolates task-specific knowledge as the residual after projecting each W_t^n onto the common subspace; both sets of directions are orthogonalized via the Procrustes problem to eliminate directional conflicts. Third, an energy-aware balancing step rescales each task's specific singular values proportionally to the task-common singular values, ensuring no single task dominates the merged model. The final merged LoRA matrix is mapped back to R^{d×d} and added to the frozen pre-trained weights.

## Results

SABER achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four benchmarks (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-A, DomainNet) with only 0.3% trainable parameters. On ImageNet-R (T=10), SABER reaches 80.30% A_last and 85.98% A_avg, outperforming the strongest baseline CL-LoRA (79.64% / 84.79%) by 0.66% and 1.19% respectively. On CIFAR-100 (T=10), SABER attains 88.06% A_last and 92.51% A_avg, and on ImageNet-A (T=10) it achieves 59.82% A_last and 70.06% A_avg, both leading all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SABER consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and task-sequence length. Against the closest competitor CL-LoRA (per-task LoRA modules), SABER gains 1.5–2.6% A_last on ImageNet-R (e.g., 81.85 vs. 79.23 at T=5) and 1.4–2.6% A_last on CIFAR-100 (e.g., 89.98 vs. 88.97 at T=5), while using the same 0.3% trainable parameters. Compared to InfLoRA (gradient projection), SABER improves A_last by 3.4–5.0% on ImageNet-R and 2.3–4.5% on CIFAR-100, without the memory overhead of storing past gradients. Against the adapter-based EASE, SABER achieves comparable or better A_avg on CIFAR-100 (92.51 vs. 92.13 at T=10) with fewer parameters (0.3% vs. 1.4%), and leads by 4.3–6.2% A_last on ImageNet-R. Prompt-based methods (L2P, Dual-Prompt, CODA-Prompt) trail SABER by 5–16% A_last across all settings, with CODA-Prompt requiring 15× more trainable parameters (4.6%) than SABER. PLAN (orthonormal initialization) is competitive on ImageNet-R T=5 (77.79 vs. 81.85) but falls further behind as task count increases, confirming that initialization alone is insufficient for long sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

SABER builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) as its parameter-efficient fine-tuning backbone, extending the line of LoRA-based continual learning methods (O-LoRA, InfLoRA, PLAN, CL-LoRA) that have focused on avoiding representational interference through orthogonality constraints, gradient projection, or per-task modular separation. The key conceptual departure is reframing the problem from interference avoidance to structured conflict management: rather than enforcing artificial separation between task subspaces, SABER aligns all tasks into a shared subspace via orthogonal bases, then decomposes and recomposes knowledge using SVD-based operations and an energy-aware balancing mechanism. This positions SABER as a principled alternative to both soft-regularization approaches (which the authors argue are too weak) and hard-modular approaches (which lack coordination between shared and task-specific knowledge).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a geometrically grounded framework for the stability-plasticity trade-off in continual learning, showing that representational conflicts are inherent and should be managed rather than eliminated. Its practical appeal is strong: SABER achieves state-of-the-art results with only 0.3% trainable parameters and competitive inference speed (18.8s vs. 110.8s for CL-LoRA), making it a viable option for resource-constrained deployment. However, the method is limited to offline continual learning where all task LoRAs are available at test time for merging, and its performance has not been evaluated under online or federated settings. Open questions include whether the SVD-based recomposition scales gracefully to very large task sequences (T > 50), whether the approach generalizes to non-ViT backbones or language models, and whether the fixed α=0.6 hyperparameter can be made adaptive per task sequence.
