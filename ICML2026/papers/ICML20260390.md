## Problem

Recent PTM-based class-incremental learning methods achieve near-static-training performance, but the specific role of class-level feature statistics in enabling this recovery remains underemphasized and treated as an implementation detail. The paper asks whether simple class-wise statistical estimators from frozen pre-trained features are sufficient to recover most of the performance gap between incremental and joint training, and whether competitive CIL can be achieved without any continual parameter updates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260390/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 visual tower into 512-dimensional feature vectors; raw images are discarded after each incremental stage, retaining only per-class statistical summaries (means, covariances, or GMM parameters). Classification is performed over the cumulative label space using either a lightweight 512×512 linear adapter matched against fixed CLIP text embeddings (SCLIP) or a parameter-free fusion of cosine-similarity and GMM log-likelihood scores (SViT), producing a single class label per image.

## Method

The paper introduces two deliberately minimal reference points that isolate the contribution of class-level statistics under frozen CLIP representations. SCLIP incrementally estimates per-class Gaussian statistics (mean and full covariance with diagonal regularization) from frozen features, samples synthetic features from these distributions, and trains a single linear adapter in a CLIP-style manner, approaching static joint-training accuracy across eight benchmarks. SViT removes replay and all parameter updates entirely, instead instantiating two complementary per-class inference primitives at each incremental stage: a class prototype (mean feature vector) for cosine-similarity scoring and a class-conditional GMM (diagonal covariance, K≤5 components selected by BIC) for density-based scoring, fused additively with a fixed weight λ=0.1. A pilot study on RAPF and MOS shows that disabling statistics-driven feature replay causes pronounced performance drops, confirming that class-level statistics constitute a central operational mechanism in recent PTM-based CIL methods rather than a mere training stabilizer.

## Results

Under the Base-0 protocol, SCLIP achieves an average accuracy of 86.86% across eight benchmarks, nearly matching the static joint-training upper bound of 86.96% while outperforming RAPF (84.49%) and PROOF (84.16%) by 2.4 and 2.7 points respectively. The replay-free SViT reference achieves competitive or best-in-class average accuracy on most datasets under Base-0 settings, reaching 95.03% on UCF101, 86.12% on SUN397, and 83.33% on CUB-200, without any continual parameter updates. Even the lightweight SCLIP⁻ variant (16 synthetic features per class) attains 84.30% average, comparable to full replay-based methods, confirming that coarse class-level statistics capture most of the recoverable performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCLIP outperforms both RAPF (Huang et al., ECCV 2024) and PROOF (Zhou et al., TPAMI 2025) by 2.4 and 2.7 points in average accuracy, with the largest gains on Aircraft (+6.4 over RAPF) and SUN (+0.8 over PROOF), while SCLIP⁻ (16 features/class) already matches or exceeds both methods on most datasets. For the replay-free SViT reference, it surpasses prompt-based methods L2P (Wang et al., CVPR 2022) and DualPrompt (Wang et al., ECCV 2022) by 10–20+ points across all datasets, and also outperforms CODA-Prompt (Smith et al., CVPR 2023) on most benchmarks (e.g., 95.03 vs 90.02 on UCF, 86.12 vs 84.68 on SUN). SViT is competitive with adapter-based methods APER-Adapter (Zhou et al., IJCV 2025) and EASE (Zhou et al., CVPR 2024), winning on UCF (+5.5), SUN (+2.6), and CUB (+0.5) under Base-0, while trailing slightly on Aircraft (−1.3) and Food (−2.1). Against MGCLIP (Huang et al., ICCV 2025), SViT achieves higher accuracy on UCF (+13.5), SUN (+1.2), and CUB (+8.3) but is lower on Food (−2.3) and Aircraft (−2.4), with MGCLIP showing larger cross-dataset variability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions the PTM-based class-incremental learning literature (RAPF, MOS, PROOF, etc.) by isolating the class-level feature statistics component that is already implicitly present in many of these methods but has been treated as an implementation detail. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it constructs two deliberately minimal references—SCLIP (statistics-driven replay with a frozen CLIP backbone and a single linear adapter) and SViT (replay-free inference via class prototypes plus diagonal-covariance GMMs)—to quantify how much continual-learning capability is already embedded in frozen pretrained representations. It also connects to the generative replay lineage by showing that, under strong pre-training, simple parametric Gaussian/GMM statistics replace the need for learned generative models, and to classical Mahalanobis/prototype classifiers by analyzing when density-based versus similarity-based inference is effective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that removing statistics-driven feature replay from strong PTM-CIL methods causes large performance drops, and that a frozen-backbone + class-statistics pipeline recovers most of the joint-training upper bound—suggests that a substantial portion of recent CIL complexity (prompts, adapters, multi-stage fusion) may be compensating for what simple distributional summaries already provide. This reframing could redirect research effort toward understanding and exploiting representation geometry rather than engineering ever more elaborate adaptation modules. Key limitations include the reliance on Gaussian/GMM approximations (which the authors acknowledge may be insufficient for highly multi-modal classes), the restriction to CLIP ViT-B/16, and the fact that SViT, while competitive on average, still underperforms adaptive methods on base tasks. Open questions include whether more expressive non-parametric or higher-order statistics further close the gap, and whether the findings generalize to weaker or non-contrastive pre-training regimes.
