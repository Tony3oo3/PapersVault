## Problem

Asynchronous RL for LLM post-training decouples rollout generation from policy updates for throughput gains, but stale rollouts produce heavy-tailed importance weights that cause a few trajectories to dominate gradient updates. Prior stabilization methods (clipping, masking, algorithmic variants) suppress outlier ratios but do not explicitly control the variance of the policy-gradient estimator itself, leading to training collapse under high policy lag. The paper identifies that this collapse is reliably predicted by a collapsing effective sample size (ESS) and that no existing method addresses the resulting variance amplification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts, rollouts generated by a stale behavior policy μ, scalar rewards, and sequence-level importance weights w = π_θ(y|x)/μ(y|x) computed between the current learner and the sampler. It produces updated policy parameters via a variance-controlled gradient step. Unusually, the method requires per-trajectory full-model gradient norms ‖g_i‖² computed during the same backward pass as the policy gradient (via deferred data-parallel all-reduce in Megatron-LM), adding only ~19% step-time overhead and ~14% memory overhead rather than a second backward pass.

## Method

VCPO combines three components into a single surrogate loss. First, sequence-level truncated importance sampling (TIS) caps the IS ratio at a threshold c=8.0 to suppress extreme weights. Second, an ESS-guided step-size rule scales the learning rate as η_eff = η·√(ρ_ess^off/ρ_ess^on), where ρ_ess = ESS/B, automatically damping updates when importance weights concentrate and reducing the effective batch to its true information content. Third, a closed-form off-policy optimal baseline (OPOB) b* = Σ(w_i²‖g_i‖²R_i)/Σ(w_i²‖g_i‖²) is subtracted from rewards, weighting each trajectory by both its importance ratio and its gradient magnitude to minimize estimator variance without a critic model. The OPOB is implemented in a single backward pass by accumulating per-sample score gradients into two buffers (reward-weighted G_R and score G_S) with DP synchronization deferred until after local per-trajectory norm statistics are computed, then forming the final gradient as (G_R − b*·G_S)/B.

## Results

VCPO matches or exceeds fully synchronous RL performance across all three task domains while delivering up to 2.5× end-to-end training speedup. On Countdown (Qwen2.5-7B, k=10), VCPO reaches 41.9% accuracy vs. 38.4% for synchronous training in 89.6 GPU-hours vs. 143.2. On MATH-500, VCPO achieves 71.6% vs. 72.0% synchronous in 92.8 vs. 134.4 GPU-hours. On the long-horizon AIME 2025 tool-use task (k=2), VCPO reaches 27.8% vs. 26.7% synchronous in 168.9 vs. 420.2 GPU-hours (2.5× speedup). VCPO remains stable up to at least k=128 steps off-policy while matching the k=0 synchronous baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GSM8K with Qwen2-1.5B under PIPELINERL-12 (12 steps off-policy), VCPO is the only method that remains stable throughout training and matches synchronous accuracy, while all five baselines fail: sequence-level TIS and MIS eventually collapse, GSPO and OTB do not reliably prevent collapse, and FP16 delays but does not prevent the underlying instability. M2PO (Zheng et al., 2025b) exhibits erratic mask fractions that collapse toward masking nearly all tokens, coinciding with KL spikes and a sharp accuracy drop at both k=12 and k=10. OTB's (Li et al., 2026) logit-energy proxy for gradient norms is shown to be unreliable: when substituted into the off-policy baseline, it eventually becomes unstable and collapses, whereas VCPO's exact full-model gradient-norm OPOB baseline remains stable and sustains higher validation accuracy (Figure 15). DeepSeek-V3.2-style geometric-mean MIS with sign constraint is highly sensitive to threshold choices and can mask nearly all sequences, effectively removing the learning signal. On MATH-500 and Countdown (Qwen2.5-7B, k=10), VCPO maintains a healthy ESS ratio and stable gradient norms throughout training, while TIS (the strongest masking/clipping baseline) suffers ESS collapse followed by KL explosion and degraded accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the policy-gradient RL post-training stack for LLMs (REINFORCE, GRPO, PPO) and the asynchronous RL systems literature (PipelineRL, AReaL, LlamaRL), which decouples rollout generation from policy updates for throughput gains. It departs from prior stabilization work (TIS, MIS, M2PO, GSPO) that suppresses outlier importance weights via clipping or masking, arguing these address weight magnitude but not the underlying variance of the gradient estimator. The two core components have intellectual roots in classical statistics: ESS-guided step-size scaling draws on the square-root batch-scaling heuristic for adaptive optimizers, and the off-policy optimal baseline (OPOB) recovers the importance-weighted optimal-baseline form of Zhao et al. (2013), while the single-backward implementation is a new systems contribution that makes exact per-trajectory gradient-norm computation practical at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VCPO offers a drop-in variance-control mechanism that makes highly asynchronous LLM RL training stable up to 128 steps of policy lag while delivering a 2.5× wall-clock speedup over synchronous training in long-horizon tool-use settings, directly addressing a major systems bottleneck in LLM post-training. The ESS-collapse diagnosis provides a unifying, measurable explanation for why diverse prior stabilizers fail, giving practitioners a principled diagnostic rather than ad-hoc threshold tuning. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to MoE architectures (where routing amplifies mismatch), FP8 quantization, and substantially longer-horizon agentic settings with sparse rewards, all of which the authors flag as future work.
