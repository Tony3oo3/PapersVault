## Problem

Molecular structure recognition from spectral data suffers from severe functional group class imbalance, where a few common groups dominate training while rare groups (e.g., Azo compounds, Enol) are underrepresented, causing models to overfit frequent classes and fail on rare ones. Additionally, most existing approaches treat functional group prediction as independent multi-label classification, ignoring intrinsic chemical co-occurrence and exclusivity dependencies among groups, which leads to chemically implausible predictions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-modal spectral data (IR, ¹H-NMR, ¹³C-NMR, and optionally MS/MS), each preprocessed to a fixed 600-dimensional vector: NMR spectra are linearly interpolated and min-max normalized, IR spectra are interpolated without normalization to preserve absolute intensity cues, and MS/MS spectra undergo Gaussian diffusion encoding to 6000 bins followed by cubic spline downsampling to 600 dimensions with L2 normalization. The output is a sequential multi-label prediction over 37 predefined functional groups (defined by SMARTS patterns), generated one-by-one by an LSTM decoder in IUPAC nomenclature priority order, where each prediction conditions on all prior predictions.

## Method

SymSpectra introduces a Symmetric Conditional Information Bottleneck (SCIB) framework that jointly optimizes modality-specific representations to preserve unique predictive information about the target while suppressing cross-modal redundancy. Each spectrum is encoded by a 1D-CNN, then a cross-modal attention mechanism computes per-token importance scores; Gumbel-Softmax relaxation makes these scores differentiable, and tokens are stochastically replaced with Gaussian noise (acting as an information filter) to enforce the compression term. A CMI-based dual-level dynamic weighting scheme estimates each modality's unique contribution via auxiliary predictors under modality dropout, then adaptively up-weights samples and labels with higher prediction uncertainty to counteract class imbalance. Finally, an LSTM decoder with scheduled sampling predicts functional groups sequentially in IUPAC order, using prior predictions as contextual inputs to encode co-occurrence and exclusivity relationships among groups.

## Results

On the Alberts et al. simulated spectral dataset with three modalities (IR, ¹³C-NMR, ¹H-NMR), SymSpectra achieves a macro-F1 of 0.970, outperforming the strongest baseline (Alberts et al., 0.947) by 2.3 points. On the experimental SDBS dataset (MS, ¹³C-NMR, ¹H-NMR), SymSpectra reaches an F1 of 0.919 versus 0.881 for the best baseline. The model also demonstrates superior sample-level accuracy and maintains performance as the number of functional groups per molecule increases, indicating robustness to structurally complex samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SymSpectra outperforms all four baselines across every spectral configuration on both simulated and experimental data. On the primary three-modality simulated setting (IR + ¹³C-NMR + ¹H-NMR), it achieves F1=0.970 versus 0.947 (Alberts et al.), 0.944 (Wu et al.), 0.936 (Transformer), and 0.900 (1D-CNN), with the largest gap over the 1D-CNN baseline (+7.0 points). On the experimental SDBS dataset, the advantage is even more pronounced: SymSpectra reaches 0.919 while the best baseline (Alberts et al.) only achieves 0.881, a 3.8-point margin. Notably, SymSpectra also surpasses baselines augmented with standard class-imbalance techniques (Focal Loss, oversampling, reweighting), which improve Alberts et al. only to 0.956, confirming that the CMI-based dynamic weighting is more effective than static heuristics. The only configuration where SymSpectra does not lead is single-modality ¹H-NMR (0.925 vs. 0.946 for Alberts et al.), suggesting the SCIB framework's advantage is most realized in multi-modal fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB) lineage—originally developed for graph substructure recognition (VGIB, PGIB, CGIB)—to the multi-modal spectral domain, introducing a symmetric formulation where all modalities are jointly optimized under a shared compression objective. It departs from prior spectral-structure elucidation work (Alberts et al., Wu et al., DeepSPInN) in two key ways: (1) replacing heuristic class-imbalance strategies (focal loss, resampling) with a principled CMI-based dynamic weighting mechanism, and (2) reframing multi-label functional group prediction as a sequential decoding problem with chemically informed ordering, rather than independent per-label classification. The cross-modal attention + Gumbel-Softmax gating mechanism for information filtering is a novel architectural combination not seen in prior spectral or IB literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap in AI-driven chemical analysis: reliable detection of rare functional groups that are essential for drug discovery and molecular design but systematically underperform in standard classifiers. The CMI-based weighting offers a data-driven alternative to static reweighting heuristics, and the sequential IUPAC-ordered decoder produces chemically coherent predictions. However, the framework only identifies functional group presence without connectivity, limiting its utility for full structure elucidation; spectral overlap in complex molecules and scaffold-level generalization remain open challenges that will require more diverse training data and finer-grained architectural extensions.
