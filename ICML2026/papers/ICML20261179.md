## Problem

Density-based anomaly detection methods, including normalizing flows, are highly sensitive to inherent noise in tabular data (e.g., environmental or equipment variations during collection), causing them to flag noisy normal samples as anomalies with high false-positive rates. Existing approaches do not separate the influence of noise sources from signal sources when estimating data density, making them unable to distinguish between genuine anomalies and mere noise fluctuations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261179/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of d-dimensional tabular vectors as training input (no labels required) and, at test time, accepts a single d-dimensional sample. It first recovers a 'pure data' point by zeroing out estimated noise sources in the latent space and inverting the flow, then outputs an anomaly score computed as the density of that pure data point (either in the full d-dimensional space or on the m-dimensional manifold). The number of data sources m is estimated automatically from the Jacobian row-norm gap, requiring no user specification.

## Method

NRDE trains a RealNVP-style coupling normalizing flow with an additional Jacobian sparsity regularizer: the ℓ_{1,1/2} quasi-norm of the batch-averaged absolute Jacobian matrix is added to the standard negative log-likelihood objective, encouraging some latent dimensions to have much smaller Jacobian row norms (noise sources) than others (data sources). After training, the number of data sources m̂ is determined by finding the largest relative gap in sorted Jacobian row norms. For a test sample, the flow maps it to latent space, the m̂ dimensions with smallest row norms are retained while the rest are zeroed, and the inverse flow recovers the pure data point. The anomaly score is the density of this pure data, making detection invariant to noise-source variations. Theoretical results bound the log-density estimation error by the distance between estimated and true data sources, and prove detectability of anomalies sufficiently far from the normal distribution.

## Results

On the standard anomaly detection benchmark (47 ADBench datasets), NRDE achieves an average AUROC of 88.5%, outperforming the second-best method TCCM (86.7%) by 1.8 points and RealNVP (82.3%) by 6.2 points. On particularly challenging datasets where most baselines hover near random (yeast, vertebral, WPBC at ~50% AUROC), NRDE reaches 61.2%, 82.8%, and 77.5% respectively. Under injected Gaussian noise, NRDE maintains 81.8% average AUROC versus 70.3% for the best baseline (KDE), and in the transductive outlier detection setting it achieves 78.2% AUROC, slightly behind the purpose-built ODIM (79.4%) but ahead of all other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NRDE outperforms all 17 baselines on average AUROC across 47 datasets (88.5% vs. 86.7% for TCCM, 86.2% for DTE-C, 85.9% for MCM and AE). The most notable margin is over RealNVP (82.3%), the closest architectural baseline, where NRDE's Jacobian regularization yields a 6.2-point gain. On the noisy-data setting (Table 2), NRDE (81.8%) surpasses the best baseline KDE (70.3%) by 11.5 points, confirming its core noise-robustness claim. In the transductive outlier detection setting (Table 3), NRDE (78.2%) trails the purpose-built ODIM (79.4%) by 1.2 points but leads all other methods including MCM (76.1%) and TCCM (69.2%). On the hardest datasets (yeast, vertebral, WPBC) where most baselines score near 50%, NRDE achieves 61.2%, 82.8%, and 77.5% respectively, while traditional methods like KDE and kNN remain at 43–53%. Under anomaly contamination (1–10% in training), NRDE degrades less than any other method, maintaining its lead throughout the contamination range.

## Relation to Prior Work

NRDE builds directly on the RealNVP coupling normalizing flow architecture (Dinh et al., 2016) and the source-separation formulation from ICA (Hyvärinen & Oja, 2000), where observations are modeled as invertible mixtures of independent latent sources. The key departure from prior density-based anomaly detection methods (including RobustRealNVP, DTE, TCCM) is the explicit Jacobian sparsity regularization that enforces a variance-based separation between signal and noise sources, allowing the model to estimate the density of pure data while isolating noise. This positions NRDE as a principled bridge between ICA's identifiability theory and deep generative density estimation for anomaly scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored weakness of density-based AD: sensitivity to inherent, non-artificial noise (equipment drift, environmental changes) that inflates false-positive rates. The variance-difference assumption (Assumption 6) is the main limitation — when signal and noise variances are comparable, performance degrades (Table 9 shows AUROC dropping from 87.3 to 65.8 as the ratio goes from 9 to 0.5). Open questions include whether the Jacobian regularization scales to very high-dimensional data (the authors suggest dimensionality reduction as a remedy) and whether the source-separation approach can be extended to non-Gaussian or correlated source distributions. The method's reliance on computing full Jacobian matrices also limits its applicability to extremely high-dimensional settings without preprocessing.
