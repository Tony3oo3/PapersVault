## Problem

Existing multimodal time series forecasters rely on cross-modality alignment techniques borrowed from vision-language models, but textual information that causally drives time series fluctuations (e.g., policy announcements, emergency reports) lacks direct semantic correspondence with numerical observations. This misalignment makes standard fusion or alignment approaches suboptimal, as they cannot effectively extract the implicit causal knowledge embedded in text to guide predictions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a historical time series of length L (split into N overlapping patches of length P) and exogenous textual content as input. The text is processed by a frozen LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) to produce hidden representations that are aggregated into a single textual token via average pooling, while the series is projected into patch-level temporal tokens. The output is a forecast of the next S time steps, with S varying by dataset (e.g., 6–12 for monthly data, 12–48 for weekly, 48–336 for daily).

## Method

TiMi introduces a Multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE) module as a lightweight plug-in that replaces standard FFN layers in a Transformer-based time series backbone (e.g., PatchTST). The MMoE comprises two routing mechanisms sharing a common expert pool: a Text-informed MoE (TMoE) whose gating network is driven by the LLM-encoded textual token to select experts based on causal future-trend knowledge, and a Series-aware MoE (SMoE) whose gating is driven by the concatenation of all patch tokens to capture global temporal dynamics. Rather than performing explicit representation-level alignment between modalities, the two routing signals jointly determine which experts process each temporal token, enabling adaptive, context-aware integration of textual guidance without vague feature fusion. The LLM is frozen and used solely to extract structured causal inferences from text, while all time series modeling and expert parameters are trained end-to-end with standard supervised loss.

## Results

On the nine Time-MMD multimodal forecasting benchmarks, TiMi achieves the best MSE and MAE across all datasets, with average improvements of 20.2% (MSE) over its PatchTST backbone and 19.7% over the closest multimodal baseline Time-MMD. Headline numbers include MSE 0.193 / MAE 0.299 on Agriculture, 0.012 / 0.086 on Economy, and 71.519 / 3.896 on Security. On the seven irregular Time-IMM datasets, TiMi reduces average MSE by 29.57% relative to PatchTST, substantially outperforming Time-MMD (11.26% reduction) and IMM-TSF (16.46% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TiMi (PatchTST backbone) consistently outperforms the closest multimodal baseline Time-MMD (also PatchTST backbone) on all nine Time-MMD datasets, with MSE reductions ranging from 4.7% (Environment) to 19.7% (SocialGood). Against the strongest unimodal Transformer baselines, TiMi beats TimeXer on 8 of 9 datasets (e.g., MSE 0.193 vs 0.230 on Agriculture, 0.886 vs 1.302 on SocialGood) and PatchTST on all 9 (e.g., 20.2% MSE reduction on Agriculture, 31.5% on SocialGood). Notably, the other multimodal baselines—AutoTimes, Time-LLM, and GPT4TS—fail to demonstrate clear advantages over their unimodal counterparts, with AutoTimes performing worst across nearly all datasets (e.g., MSE 1.032 on Agriculture vs 0.193 for TiMi). On the irregular Time-IMM benchmark, TiMi achieves the lowest MSE on all seven datasets, reducing average MSE by 29.57% over PatchTST compared to 11.26% for Time-MMD and 16.46% for IMM-TSF. The MMoE plug-in also transfers effectively: when applied to TimeXer and Autoformer backbones, it yields average MSE improvements of 12.5% and 12.4% respectively, confirming generality beyond the PatchTST backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant alignment-based paradigm in multimodal time series forecasting (Early Fusion à la Time-LLM/AutoTimes, Late Fusion à la Time-MMD/IMM-TSF) by proposing a 'Non-Fusion Guidance' approach: a frozen LLM extracts structured causal knowledge from text, and a novel Multimodal Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE) plug-in routes this knowledge into a Transformer time series backbone without explicit cross-modal representation alignment. Architecturally, it repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts mechanism—previously used for model scaling (Switch Transformers)—as a lightweight, modality-aware routing layer that can be grafted onto existing forecasters (PatchTST, TimeXer, Autoformer). Conceptually, it draws an analogy to how LLMs reason over text in vision-language models but argues that text-time series relationships are fundamentally different (causal/future-oriented rather than semantically aligned), motivating the guidance-over-fusion design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MMoE module's demonstrated versatility as a drop-in plug-in across three architecturally distinct Transformer backbones (encoder-only patch-based, encoder-decoder point-wise, and global-token designs) makes it a practically reusable component for the multimodal time series community. The finding that alignment-based multimodal baselines barely outperform their unimodal counterparts raises a legitimate question about whether explicit cross-modal alignment is the right inductive bias for text-time series tasks, potentially redirecting future work toward knowledge-extraction and guidance paradigms. Open questions include whether the frozen-LLM pipeline can be made end-to-end trainable, how the approach scales to longer contexts or more modalities (e.g., images, tabular metadata), and whether the causal-reasoning framing generalizes beyond the curated Time-MMD/Time-IMM benchmarks to truly open-domain text.
