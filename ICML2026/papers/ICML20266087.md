## Problem

Existing distillation methods for Flow Matching models rely on indirect velocity-to-score conversion inherited from diffusion-based score matching, which discards the deterministic ODE trajectory structure intrinsic to FM and introduces high gradient variance, training instability, and degraded generation quality. No prior method directly aligns velocity fields in a way that exploits FM's geometric structure while maintaining stable optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266087/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student generator G_θ is a single-step mapping from Gaussian noise X_0 ~ N(0,I) to the final output (a 4D spatiotemporal occupancy volume for LiDAR-based forecasting, or a 1024×1024 RGB image for text-to-image generation), requiring only 1 NFE at inference. During training, an auxiliary flow model v^P (implemented as LoRA adapters, ~34M params on SANA 1.6B) reconstructs the velocity field induced by the student distribution and is discarded at inference, so MFD incurs zero additional inference cost. The teacher is a pretrained FM model evaluated with classifier-free guidance (scale 2.0 for occupancy, 4.5 for images) over multi-step ODE integration.

## Method

MFD replaces pointwise instantaneous velocity matching with alignment of time-integrated Average Velocity Fields (AVF) computed via numerical ODE integration over sampled intervals [s,t], which acts as a temporal low-pass filter suppressing high-frequency optimization noise. Training alternates between updating the auxiliary flow model v^P (K=10 steps via standard FM regression loss on student-generated samples) and updating the student G_θ (1 step) by minimizing the L2 distance between the auxiliary and teacher mean flows, with gradients backpropagated through the linear interpolation X_s = (1-s)X_0 + s·G_θ(X_0). The Jacobian of the ODE solver is omitted (treated as identity, analogous to SDS), yielding a simplified gradient proportional to s·(∇_θ G_θ)^T · (Û^P − Û^Q). The Mean Flow Matching Theorem proves that zero expected Bregman divergence between AVFs over a full-support time distribution guarantees P=Q, and a variance analysis shows MFD's gradient variance scales as 1/K relative to instantaneous matching, where K is the number of ODE integration steps.

## Results

On 4D occupancy forecasting (nuScenes, OccFM), MFD achieves 37.07% IoU and 27.25% mIoU with a single NFE, losing only 1.2% IoU relative to the 10-step teacher (37.52%) while doubling inference speed (25.19 vs 12.72 FPS). On text-to-image generation (SANA 1.6B, LAION-aesthetic-6.5+), MFD attains the best Aesthetic Score (6.541), ImageReward (0.8483), CLIP Score (29.430), and FID-DINOv2 (33.28) among all 4-step distillation methods, even surpassing the 20-step teacher on Aesthetic Score and ImageReward. MFD also exhibits consistently narrower loss distributions than VSD-based baselines, empirically confirming its theoretical variance-reduction property.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFD outperforms all five baselines on both tasks. Against Diff-Instruct (VSD-based), MFD gains +0.66 IoU on 4D occupancy and improves FID from 37.55 to 33.28 on text-to-image, with the paper attributing the gap to VSD's high-variance single-timestep score estimates. Against Consistency Distillation (CD) and AYF, MFD achieves substantially larger margins (+5.38 and +4.69 IoU on 4D; FID 33.28 vs 45.97 and 43.68 on T2I), which the authors link to the overly restrictive sample-level consistency constraints of these methods. Against DMD2, MFD wins on Aesthetic Score (+0.046), ImageReward (+0.047), CLIP Score (+0.19), and FID (33.28 vs 36.13), while DMD2 edges ahead by 0.0002 on PickScore; the paper notes DMD2 relies on an adversarial discriminator that adds training instability. Against SenseFlow, MFD leads on Aesthetic Score (+0.059), ImageReward (+0.056), CLIP Score (+0.11), and FID (33.28 vs 35.97), with SenseFlow marginally ahead on HPSv2 (0.2445 vs 0.2442). Overall, MFD is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best FID, ImageReward, and Aesthetic Score without requiring adversarial training or perceptual losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

MFD sits at the intersection of three research threads: Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023) provides the ODE-based velocity field framework; Mean Flow (Geng et al., 2025a) introduces time-integrated mean velocity supervision for one-step generation from scratch; and Variational Score Distillation (Wang et al., 2023) supplies the auxiliary-model-plus-alternating-update distillation paradigm. The key departure from prior flow distillation work (DMD2, SenseFlow, AYF) is operating directly in velocity space with temporal integration rather than converting velocities to scores or enforcing sample-level consistency, which the authors argue better exploits FM's deterministic ODE structure. The Mean Flow Matching Theorem provides a clean theoretical bridge: matching expected average velocities over intervals with full temporal support is sufficient for exact distribution alignment, subsuming instantaneous velocity matching as a degenerate case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MFD offers a theoretically grounded, practically efficient recipe for accelerating pretrained FM models to single-step or few-step inference with minimal quality loss, which is increasingly important as FM-based generators (SANA, OccFM, etc.) become the dominant paradigm. The 1/K variance reduction from temporal integration and the zero-inference-overhead design (auxiliary model discarded at test time) make it attractive for real-time deployment. Open questions include: whether the Jacobian-omission approximation can be theoretically bounded, whether the auxiliary model's training cost can be further reduced (currently ~25 GPU hours), and whether the framework extends naturally to non-Euclidean or multi-modal flow matching settings. The non-monotonic ODE step-size sensitivity also hints at a deeper relationship between numerical integration granularity and effective supervision quality that remains unexplored.
