## Problem

Pruning large language models amplifies text degeneration—particularly repetition loops—even when perplexity and task accuracy remain largely unchanged. Prior post-pruning fine-tuning focused on knowledge recovery via standard distillation, which can actually worsen degeneration by increasing leakage into teacher-suppressed tokens and collapsing near-tie alternatives at loop-sensitive contexts. The paper asks: what token-level distortions drive repetition loops, and how can training objectives be designed to suppress them without sacrificing generation quality?

## Input / Output

The method operates on pruned Llama-family models (25–45% width or depth pruning) fine-tuned with LoRA on the Alpaca instruction dataset. For evaluation, open-ended generation takes a 50-token prefix from WikiText-103 and produces 100 continuation tokens; instruction-based generation takes 1,000 Self-Instruct prompts and produces variable-length responses. RePAIR additionally requires onset-centered paired data: a negative continuation (repetitive, from the pruned model) and a positive continuation (non-degenerate, from the unpruned model) sharing the same prefix up to the repetition onset, with a coverage threshold of 0.3 to identify degenerated sequences.

## Method

The paper decomposes repetition-loop degeneration into loop entry risk and loop persistence, showing that under nucleus (top-p) sampling, persistence is governed by the escape mass assigned to plausible alternatives within the nucleus set. FOCUS modifies standard forward-KL distillation by introducing a token-wise weight w = q^β + (1−q)^γ that reweights the teacher distribution toward high-confidence regions, effectively replacing the teacher with a reweighted version q̃ = w(q)⊙q / Σw(q)q in the gradient, thereby suppressing probability leakage into teacher-suppressed tokens that would otherwise enter the nucleus and trigger loop entry. RePAIR constructs onset-centered positive/negative continuation pairs (negative from the pruned model's degenerated output, positive from the unpruned model's clean regeneration) and applies a margin-ranking loss max(0, m + ℓ⁺ − ℓ⁻) that explicitly promotes plausible alternatives at the repetition onset, increasing escape mass and reducing loop persistence. The two objectives are combined with standard cross-entropy as L_total = L_CE + α₁·L_FOCUS + α₂·L_RePAIR, with α₁=0.05 and α₂=1, and are compatible with existing mitigation baselines (UL, ScaleGrad, DITTO) when stacked on top of FOCUS.

## Results

On WikiText-103 open-ended generation with Llama-3.1-8B (25% width-pruned), FOCUS+RePAIR achieves the lowest CREP of 0.57% and highest MAUVE of 0.73, compared to the KD baseline's CREP of 7.3% and MAUVE of 0.61, with only a modest PPL increase (23.12 vs. 21.69). On Llama-2-13B, FOCUS+RePAIR reduces CREP to 0.00% and raises MAUVE to 0.87. On Self-Instruct instruction-based generation, FOCUS+RePAIR achieves CREP 0.23% and EAD₁ 0.31 with BERTScore 0.50, outperforming all baselines on degeneration while maintaining semantic fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Unlikelihood Training (UL), FOCUS+RePAIR reduces CREP from 5.37% to 0.57% and improves MAUVE from 0.61 to 0.73 on Llama-3.1-8B, showing that onset-level corrective signals are far more effective than blanket token suppression. ScaleGrad (SG) performs poorly on degeneration (CREP 7.80% without FOCUS, 0.87% with FOCUS) and does not improve MAUVE beyond 0.70, whereas FOCUS+RePAIR reaches 0.73. DITTO achieves CREP 5.27% standalone and 0.50% with FOCUS, slightly better than UL but still below FOCUS+RePAIR's 0.57% in MAUVE (0.72 vs. 0.73). In the direct DPO comparison (Table 5), RePAIR outperforms DPO on all unique n-gram metrics (e.g., 8.28% vs. 9.76% at n=3) with comparable PPL, confirming that onset-level pairwise supervision is more targeted than sequence-level preference optimization. Against ToDi (Table 7), FOCUS+RePAIR achieves substantially lower CREP (0.57% vs. 6.73%) and unique n-gram rates while matching ToDi's MAUVE of 0.73, indicating that explicit tail-leakage suppression is more effective than hybrid-KL divergence control for repetition mitigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LLM pruning/recovery (knowledge distillation after structural pruning) and text degeneration mitigation (unlikelihood training, ScaleGrad, DITTO). It departs from prior degeneration work by providing a token-level dynamical decomposition—separating loop entry risk from loop persistence under nucleus sampling—and showing that both are governed by specific, correctable properties of the next-token distribution (tail leakage and alternative collapse). FOCUS repurposes standard KD by reweighting toward high-confidence teacher tokens, while RePAIR adapts the pairwise preference-learning paradigm (cf. DPO) to operate at the repetition-onset level rather than the sequence level, targeting the precise context where degeneration begins.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pruning is a primary deployment strategy for LLMs, yet standard post-pruning fine-tuning (KD on Alpaca) leaves degeneration largely unaddressed while perplexity and accuracy appear intact—this paper provides both a diagnostic framework (entry/persistence decomposition) and practical training objectives that close that gap. The onset-sensitivity finding (two token substitutions at loop onset reduce CREP by ~88% under sampling) reframes degeneration as a sharp, locally correctable event rather than a global distributional failure, which could inform future decoding-time interventions. Limitations include a modest but consistent PPL increase, evaluation primarily on Llama-family models (with limited Qwen coverage), and the theoretical loop region L being difficult to operationalize outside the analysis. Open questions include whether the framework extends to other degeneration modes (e.g., semantic loops, topic drift) and whether the methods remain effective under extreme sparsity where capacity loss dominates.
