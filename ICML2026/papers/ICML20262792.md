## Problem

Research idea generation is inherently open-ended and multi-objective, requiring a balance between novelty, plausibility, and feasibility that no single fixed workflow can optimize. Existing LLM-based approaches rely on predefined, static agent pipelines that leave the thinking process implicit, preventing structured exploration over diverse reasoning flows and the ability to compose, reuse, or optimize partial flows. MindFlow asks whether the ideation process itself can be made explicit, controllable, and optimizable as a first-class object rather than a black-box prompt sequence.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a research topic string and a set of related works (each with structured summaries and technical details, retrieved via Semantic Scholar API) and produces a fixed-schema JSON research proposal with four components: title, problem motivation (statement, identified gaps, testable hypotheses), technical approach (algorithmic designs, assumptions, datasets, metrics), and evaluation protocol (baselines, ablations, success criteria). The JSON is automatically parsed downstream, and the related literature is dynamically expanded during execution when the analogical thinking operator invokes retrieval tools.

## Method

MindFlow formulates ideation as a directed acyclic graph of seven atomic thinking operators (Generate, Divergent, Convergent, Counterfactual, Critical, Analogical, Constraint-Driven), then introduces a 'mind supernet' that parameterizes a large family of such flows via layer-wise inclusion probabilities conditioned on the topic. A controller samples a concrete thinking flow from this supernet with topic-adaptive per-layer operator budgets and an early-stop mechanism, executing operators in topological order to produce a candidate idea. To handle the open-ended reward problem where absolute LLM scoring suffers from judgment collapse, the paper employs an anchor-based tournament for relative ranking among K candidate ideas, converting discrete ranks into normalized advantage signals for policy-gradient updates of the controller. This shifts the optimization objective from 'produce a good idea in one fixed way' to 'learn a topic-dependent distribution over reasoning processes that generates high-quality ideas,' enabling structured exploration and recombination of complementary cognitive modes.

## Results

On IdeaBench, MindFlow achieves an overall LLM-judged win rate of 0.541 against expert-written references, outperforming the strongest agentic baseline VIRSCI (0.470) by 7.1 points. On the multi-objective MOScore, MindFlow leads in both problem finding (0.742 vs. 0.667) and problem solving (0.339 vs. 0.274). On computable metrics, MindFlow attains the best score on all four dimensions: novelty (0.541), diversity (0.322), effectiveness (0.665), and feasibility (0.735). Human expert evaluation on 50 sampled queries confirms the ranking, with MindFlow scoring 0.580 overall versus 0.475 for VIRSCI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MindFlow outperforms all four baselines on the overall win rate and both MOScore aggregates. Against the closest competitor VIRSCI (multi-agent, ACL 2025), MindFlow gains +7.1 points overall (0.541 vs. 0.470) and +7.5 on problem-finding MOScore (0.742 vs. 0.667); however, VIRSCI retains an edge on problem-solving novelty (0.445 vs. 0.361), while MindFlow dominates on problem-solving effectiveness (0.408 vs. 0.332) and feasibility (0.257 vs. 0.109). Against GenerateCoT (NeurIPS 2022), MindFlow leads by a wide margin (+19.7 overall), with the gap concentrated in problem solving (MOScore 0.339 vs. 0.185). Against AI Scientist (arXiv 2024), MindFlow wins by +23.3 overall; AI Scientist's strength in feasibility (0.455) is offset by very weak novelty (0.028) and effectiveness (0.123). Against AI-Researcher (arXiv 2024), MindFlow leads by +21.5 overall, with particularly large gains in problem-solving MOScore (0.339 vs. 0.165). On computable metrics (Table 2), MindFlow is the sole method to rank first on all four dimensions simultaneously, confirming that its advantage is not an artifact of a single evaluation axis.

## Relation to Prior Work

MindFlow departs from prior agentic ideation systems (AI Scientist, AI-Researcher, VIRSCI, Chain-of-Ideas) that rely on fixed, hand-crafted pipelines by making the thinking process an explicit, optimizable graph-structured flow of modular operators. It repurposes the neural architecture search 'supernet' concept to parameterize a probabilistic distribution over thinking flows, and borrows tournament-based relative ranking from ArenaRL to handle the open-ended reward problem where absolute LLM scoring is noisy. The evaluation protocol extends prior idea-generation benchmarks (AI Idea Bench 2025) by jointly assessing problem finding and problem solving across six dimensions with both LLM judgments and computable metrics, going beyond title- or abstract-only evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making ideation explicit and optimizable, MindFlow offers a controllable framework where users can inspect, modify, and steer the reasoning process rather than treating it as a black box, which is valuable for human-AI collaborative research. The IdeaBench benchmark and multi-objective evaluation protocol provide a more rigorous and scalable assessment paradigm for future idea-generation systems. Key limitations remain: the operator set is manually designed and may not cover all scientific reasoning modes, ideas are not empirically verified through experimentation, and the evaluation, while improved, still relies on LLM judges whose subjectivity is only partially mitigated by the tournament protocol. Open questions include whether operators can be learned automatically and whether the supernet approach scales to broader scientific domains beyond AI/ML.
