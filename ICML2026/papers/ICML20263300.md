## Problem

Self-supervised learning lacks a unifying theoretical framework that explains the diverse existing methods (contrastive, non-contrastive, predictive, stopgrad) and guides the design of new ones. Geometric alignment interpretations do not extend to non-contrastive methods like BYOL or SimSiam, and mutual information maximization is invariant to arbitrary invertible transformations, so it cannot by itself promote identifiable representations. The paper asks whether latent distribution matching (LDM) can serve as a single principled objective from which all major SSL algorithms are naturally recovered.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes unlabeled data as paired views (image augmentations, temporal neighbors in video) or sequential observations (neural spike trains, synthetic video frames) and produces deterministic latent representations z = f(x) via a deep encoder. For the Kalman-based predictive variant, the output additionally includes analytic prediction-error covariance matrices Σ_e over latent states, enabling principled confidence intervals on decoded quantities such as animal position. The latent space can be constrained to the plane, sphere, or simplex, and the assumed latent model P_θ is jointly optimized with the encoder rather than fixed a priori.

## Method

The paper formulates SSL as minimizing the KL divergence between the empirical latent distribution R(z,z') induced by a deterministic encoder and an assumed latent model P_θ(z,z'), yielding an objective that decomposes into an alignment term (log-likelihood under P_θ) and a uniformity term (latent entropy H_R). By selecting different latent models (flat prior + Gaussian conditional for VICReg, uniform + von Mises-Fisher for SimCLR, empirical + predictive Gaussian for JEPA) and different entropy estimators (parametric log-determinant, kernel density, kNN, conditional plug-in via stopgrad), the framework recovers all major SSL algorithms as special cases, with differences between methods arising solely from these choices. The key theoretical contribution is a proof that predictive LDM with a Gaussian or vMF predictor yields identifiable latent representations up to affine transformations even with nonlinear predictors, because the local noise structure of prediction errors constrains admissible reparameterizations of latent space. The paper further derives a new nonlinear Bayesian filtering model with a Kalman-based predictor that provides analytic uncertainty quantification, and empirically demonstrates that MI maximization contributes negligibly to representation quality beyond what entropy maximization already achieves.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the best configuration (Plane-LogDet, i.e., VICReg-style) achieves 92.1% top-1 linear probing accuracy without MI maximization (F_LDM) and 91.9% with MI maximization (F_MI), showing no systematic difference. On CIFAR-100, the same configuration reaches 69.5% vs. 68.6%, and on ImageNet-100, 75.9% vs. 74.7%. In the predictive Kalman-filtering experiments, both CPC (with MI) and stopgrad-based SSL (without MI) recover the true latent position with R²=0.99, and the nonlinear system identification task achieves R²=0.94 for linear decoding of the true state.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central empirical comparison is between the LDM objective (F_LDM, no explicit MI term) and the MI-augmented objective (F_MI) across three entropy estimators (contrastive/KDE, kNN, parametric LogDet) and two latent geometries (plane, sphere). No configuration shows a systematic advantage for F_MI over F_LDM: differences are within ±0.3% on CIFAR-10 and ±1.1% on CIFAR-100, well within run-to-run variance. The entropy estimator choice dominates performance: parametric LogDet (VICReg-style) outperforms contrastive (SimCLR-style) by ~4% on CIFAR-100 (69.5% vs. 65.3%) and ~3% on ImageNet-100 (75.9% vs. 72.6%), while the latent space (plane vs. sphere) has a smaller but consistent effect (~1–2%). In the predictive temporal experiments, CPC with MI maximization and stopgrad-based SSL without MI achieve identical R²=0.99 on the linear Kalman task and near-identical gradient cosine similarity, confirming that the MI term is empirically redundant when entropy maximization already enforces encoder invertibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper elevates latent distribution matching (LDM) from an implicit principle underlying specific SSL algorithms (as shown by Zimmermann et al., 2021 for contrastive methods via reverse KL) to a primary, unifying objective that subsumes contrastive, non-contrastive, and predictive SSL methods. It builds on the identifiability theory from nonlinear ICA (Hyvarinen et al., 2019; Khemakhem et al., 2020a) and the alignment/uniformity decomposition of Wang & Isola (2020), but departs from the dominant MI-maximization interpretation (Oord et al., 2018; Shwartz-Ziv et al., 2023) by arguing that MI maximization is largely redundant when latent entropy is already being maximized. The Kalman-based predictive SSL model is a novel derivation that has no direct predecessor in the SSL literature, drawing instead from classical state estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides principled guidance for designing new SSL objectives: one specifies a latent model and an entropy estimator, and the loss follows. The identifiability theorems for predictive SSL with nonlinear predictors (beyond the contrastive-only results of Zimmermann et al., 2021) strengthen the theoretical foundation for why JEPA-style methods work. The Kalman-based predictor enables uncertainty quantification over latent states, which previous predictive SSL approaches (BYOL, V-JEPA) do not provide. Key open questions include designing better entropy estimators (the authors identify this as the most pressing practical challenge), extending identifiability to non-Gaussian/non-vMF predictors, and understanding whether the framework scales to very high-dimensional latent spaces where entropy estimation becomes ill-posed.
