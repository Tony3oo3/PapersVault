## Problem

Music restoration and mastering currently require a cascade of specialized tools (dereverberation plugins, declipping algorithms, equalizers, stereo enhancers) applied sequentially by experts, with no unified model capable of addressing multiple artifact types simultaneously. Moreover, no prior work provides natural-language-controllable music restoration, leaving amateur creators without an accessible, all-in-one solution that can correct a broad spectrum of degradations in a single forward pass.

## Input / Output

SonicMaster takes a degraded stereo waveform at 44.1 kHz and an optional natural-language instruction (e.g., 'reduce the hollow room sound') as input, producing a restored stereo waveform. For long-form processing, the model operates on 30-second chunks stitched via overlap-add with a 10-second cross-fade, and can optionally accept a 5–15 second clean audio reference segment through a pooled-audio conditioning branch to maintain consistency across segments. In the absence of a text prompt, the model switches to an automatic mode that applies perceptually balanced mastering.

## Method

SonicMaster employs a hybrid architecture of Multimodal Diffusion Transformer (MM-DiT) blocks followed by standard DiT layers, operating entirely in the latent space of a frozen Stable Audio Open VAE. The core training paradigm is rectified flow: the model learns to predict the flow velocity v_t = x_0 − x_1 that maps a degraded latent x_1 directly to a clean latent x_0, rather than denoising from pure noise as in standard diffusion. Text conditioning is injected via FLAN-T5 embeddings through cross-attention in MM-DiT blocks and adaptive layer normalization, with classifier-free guidance enabling both prompt-guided and automatic operation. A pooled-audio branch concatenates a temporally averaged clean reference with the pooled text embedding and injects it at every layer, enabling seamless chaining of 30-second segments for full-length songs. The model is trained on a purpose-built dataset of 175k paired clips where 25k clean Jamendo segments are corrupted with 1–3 of 19 degradation functions spanning EQ, dynamics, reverb, amplitude, and stereo, each paired with a natural-language instruction describing the required fix.

## Results

On the full SonicMaster test set (all degradation types), SonicMaster achieves a Production Quality (PQ) score of 7.705, approaching the ground-truth mastered reference of 7.886, while the degraded input scores 7.026 and Mel2Mel+Diffwave only 6.070. KL divergence drops from 5.131 (degraded) to 0.888 (SonicMaster), and FAD improves from 0.106 to 0.073. On degradation-specific metrics, SonicMaster reduces EQ error by 5–90× versus baselines (e.g., X-band: 0.0358 vs. 0.3419 for Text2FX_cos), reverb MSD by ~20% over WPE, and clipping spectral-flatness error from 5.122 to 1.506. On the RemFX test set, SonicMaster achieves SI-SDR of 47.11 dB (Dynamics) and 45.76 dB (Reverb), far exceeding the best removal-model baseline (20.08 dB / 13.59 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SonicMaster decisively outperforms Mel2Mel+DiffWave (Kandpal et al., 2022) on every metric: PQ 7.705 vs. 6.070, KL 0.888 vs. 15.044, and FAD 0.073 vs. 0.491, with the gap widening under multi-degradation settings. On EQ, SonicMaster reduces average absolute error by 5–90× over both Text2FX variants (e.g., X-band: 0.0358 vs. 0.3419 for Text2FX_cos and 0.5038 for Text2FX_dir; Boom: 0.0834 vs. 0.3809/0.3977). For reverb, SonicMaster's modulation-spectrum distance (0.31–0.39) beats WPE (0.48–0.52) and both HPSS settings (0.44–0.53) across all four room types. Against the five RemFX-trained removal models (DPTNet, UMX, DCUNet, TCN×2, HDemucs), SonicMaster achieves substantially higher SI-SDR: 47.11 dB on Dynamics and 45.76 dB on Reverb versus a best baseline of 20.08 dB and 13.59 dB respectively, reflecting its generative mastering objective versus their minimal-alteration removal training. On out-of-domain historical piano recordings, SonicMaster (PQ 6.93) comes within 0.12 of the specialized diffusion-based BABE-2 (7.05) and surpasses BEHM-GAN (6.63) and BABE (6.79), demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization despite no domain-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

SonicMaster builds on the rectified-flow training paradigm (Liu et al., 2023b) and the MM-DiT dual-stream architecture (Esser et al., 2024) originally developed for image synthesis, repurposing them for audio-to-audio transformation in a learned VAE latent space. It departs from prior audio restoration work (VoiceFixer, WPE, HPSS, Text2FX, Mel2Mel+DiffWave) that handles individual artifacts sequentially or in isolation, and from generative audio models (AudioLDM, TangoFlux, FlowSep) that target generation, separation, or localized editing rather than unified restoration. The key conceptual departure is treating multi-artifact restoration as a single conditional flow-mapping problem guided by natural language, rather than a cascade of task-specific modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work democratizes professional-grade audio enhancement by replacing expert-driven multi-tool workflows with a single prompt-driven model, and the released 175k-pair text-conditioned dataset fills a gap that previously prevented training controllable restoration models. Key limitations include lossy latent-space artifacts (robotic vocals, muted instruments), restriction to 19 degradations and single-song processing, and a Western-music cultural bias in the training data. Open questions include whether the approach extends to album-level mastering for cross-track cohesion, how to handle the semantic ambiguity of music-production language, and whether the latent-space bottleneck can be resolved to eliminate residual artifacts.
