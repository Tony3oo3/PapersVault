## Problem

Existing MARL approaches for aircraft conflict resolution suffer from a structural misalignment with the aviation domain: they concatenate variable-count aircraft states into fixed-dimensional vectors (breaking scalability across traffic densities) and emit densely coupled multi-dimensional control signals (contradicting the single-instruction, sparse nature of real ATC commands). This perception-action gap limits both generalization to unseen airspace scales and the operational stability of generated maneuvers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261338/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives its own kinematic state (latitude, longitude, airspeed, heading, altitude) plus the states of all neighbors within a 20 km detection range, with the neighbor count varying dynamically per timestep. The output is a 3-dimensional action vector (speed, heading, altitude) in which exactly one dimension is active at any step—enforced by a one-hot routing gate—so the agent either holds or issues a single-axis adjustment in [-1, 1], mirroring the 'single-moment, single-instruction' protocol of human controllers.

## Method

SARL couples three modules: (1) a Physics-Encoded Relational Graph (PERG) that builds an ego-centric star graph per agent and applies a graph attention mechanism with binary Physics-Aware Flags (derived from ICAO separation rules) to aggregate variable-length neighbor features with permutation invariance; (2) a Sparse Cognitive Mixture-of-Experts (SC-MoE) head that uses Gumbel-Softmax with a Straight-Through Estimator to hard-select one of four decoupled experts (Hold, Speed, Heading, Altitude), where altitude is modeled as a softmax over three discrete levels projected back to continuous space to avoid oscillation; and (3) a Kinematic Safety Shield (KSS) that projects trajectories forward via a constant-velocity model, checks Signal Temporal Logic safety predicates, and overrides the raw action with a rule-based evasive maneuver (altitude priority over heading) when a collision is imminent. Training follows a Dec-POMDP actor-critic scheme (MADDPG-style) with independent PERG encoders for actor and critic, and the KSS intervention is penalized in the reward so the policy internalizes compliant behavior rather than relying on the shield at inference.

## Results

SARL achieves the highest success rate across all three test cases (99.28%, 99.06%, and 97.00%), outperforming the best baseline (MATD3 at 95.16%, 92.32%, and 95.10%) by 2–4 percentage points. Simultaneously, SARL attains the lowest average operational cost (157.54, 100.36, and 159.96) and the largest average minimum separation (24.39, 22.39, and 24.80), indicating that its safety gains are achieved through proactive, sparse maneuvers rather than aggressive multi-dimensional corrections. In Case 2, SARL's operational cost of 100.36 is roughly 60% lower than the next-best method (DGN at 265.52).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SARL dominates all six baselines on every metric across all three cases. Among general-purpose MARL algorithms, MATD3 is the closest competitor on success rate (95.16% vs. SARL's 99.28% in Case 1), but SARL still leads by over 4 points while halving the operational cost (157.54 vs. 322.35). MAPPO performs worst overall, with success rates 8–11 points below SARL and operational costs roughly 3× higher. Among aviation-specific methods, LSRL achieves the best minimum separation among baselines (20.59 in Case 1) but still trails SARL by nearly 4 units, and SA lags on all three metrics. The gap is most dramatic in Case 2, where SARL's operational cost (100.36) is 2.6–5× lower than every baseline, suggesting that the SC-MoE sparse action mechanism and KSS guidance produce fundamentally more efficient control strategies than the densely coupled outputs of prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant MARL paradigm for aircraft conflict resolution, which typically concatenates fixed-size state vectors and outputs densely coupled continuous actions. It repurposes three well-established components from different subfields—graph attention networks (from GNN literature), Mixture-of-Experts with Gumbel-Softmax routing (from NLP), and Signal Temporal Logic (from formal verification)—into a unified architecture explicitly designed to match aviation domain structure. The 'structural misalignment' framing positions the contribution as an architectural redesign rather than an algorithmic one: the perception side (PERG) addresses variable-length input, the action side (SC-MoE) enforces single-instruction sparsity matching ATC protocols, and the safety side (KSS) injects regulatory compliance via STL. Unlike prior aviation RL work (MADDPG, MAPPO, SA, LSRL), SARL does not introduce a new learning algorithm but restructures how states are encoded and actions are parameterized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that architectural alignment with domain structure (variable-length perception, sparse single-dimension actions, rule-based safety) can yield large practical gains—nearly 100× collision rate reduction from KSS alone and ~50% lower operational cost—without changing the underlying RL algorithm. This suggests that much of the performance gap in domain-specific RL may stem from representational mismatch rather than algorithmic deficiency, a lesson potentially transferable to other safety-critical control domains. Limitations include evaluation solely in the BlueSky simulator with simplified kinematics, no real-world or hardware-in-the-loop validation, and the KSS module's reliance on a constant-velocity projection model that may not capture complex aerodynamic interactions. Open questions include whether the SC-MoE routing generalizes to more than four action dimensions, how the approach scales to 4D (time-constrained) conflict resolution, and whether the STL rule base can be automatically derived from regulatory documents.
