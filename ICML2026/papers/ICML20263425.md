## Problem

State-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models degrade sharply under few-shot imitation learning (10–30 demonstrations), yet this data-efficiency gap has not been systematically stress-tested. The paper identifies that standard behavioral cloning treats each demonstration as an independent (observation, action) pair, discarding the dense long-horizon supervision contained in future frames that reveal how the scene should evolve under successful task execution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263425/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, FOCA consumes multi-view RGB images (2 views for LIBERO/ALOHA, 3 for RoboCasa/humanoid), a natural-language task instruction, and robot proprioceptive state, producing continuous action chunks via a flow-matching diffusion policy. During training, it additionally requires future frames from the same episode and task-grounded object-centric bounding boxes (extracted from simulator segmentation masks or Grounding-DINO detections) to supervise the explicit and implicit future objectives; these boxes are not needed at test time. A key design property is that the implicit alignment objective accepts action-free synthetic videos from video world models (e.g., DreamGen), enabling co-training without pseudo-action labels or inverse dynamics.

## Method

FOCA is a plug-and-play augmentation for diffusion-based VLA policies (π_0, GR00T-N1.5) that injects two sets of learnable tokens into the VLM: explicit future tokens that predict latent patch embeddings of a task-grounded interaction region (the minimal bounding box enclosing language-referenced objects and the gripper) at a geometrically sampled future timestep, and implicit future tokens that perform InfoNCE contrastive alignment between the current observation representation and pooled vision embeddings of future goal frames, with negatives drawn from task-mismatched episodes. A structured attention mask isolates the two token groups to prevent supervisory leakage. The three objectives (flow-matching action loss, explicit L2 prediction loss, implicit contrastive loss) are co-trained jointly, and the implicit objective can be optimized on action-free synthetic rollouts from video world models. Theoretically, the geometric future sampling makes the implicit objective equivalent to estimating a goal-conditioned discounted occupancy (a value-like reachability score), while the explicit objective estimates the conditional mean under that occupancy distribution, connecting FOCA to successor representations and goal-conditioned RL.

## Results

On LIBERO, FOCA achieves 96.6% average success at 100% data, 94.0% at 40%, and 85.3% at 10% demonstrations, consistently outperforming the π_0 baseline (94.6%, 89.9%, 77.6%) and all other SOTA VLA models. With DreamGen synthetic video co-training at 40% data, FOCA reaches 95.7%, surpassing π_0 trained with 100% data. On RoboCasa, FOCA yields 7–12% absolute gains over π_0 and 7.2–10.4% over GR00T-N1.5, while on real ALOHA robots it delivers up to 26% absolute improvements (e.g., doubling Open Bag success from 20% to 40% under limited data).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOCA outperforms all 17 baselines across every data scale and benchmark. Against the primary backbone π_0, FOCA gains +2.0% at 100% data, +4.1% at 40%, and +7.7% at 10% on LIBERO average success. Among PEFT baselines (LoRA, DoRA, ControlVLA), FOCA leads by 6.7–7.1% at the 10% regime where PEFT methods plateau around 78%. Against future-prediction baselines, FOCA beats DreamVLA-style conditioning by +1.4% (40%) and +1.0% (100%) on LIBERO, and outperforms FLARE by +3.0% on LIBERO (40%) and +6.0% on RoboCasa (100 demos). Against the IGM pseudo-action baseline from DreamGen videos, FOCA's implicit-only variant already surpasses IGM at all scales, and the full FOCA+DreamGen pipeline achieves 95.7% at 40% data—exceeding π_0's 100%-data performance. On RoboCasa, FOCA improves GR00T-N1.5 by 7.2–10.4% and π_0 by 4.4–12% across five challenging tasks, confirming architecture-agnostic gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOCA positions itself as a plug-and-play augmentation layer on top of existing diffusion-based VLA policies (π_0, GR00T-N1.5), rather than proposing a new backbone. It departs from prior future-prediction approaches in VLAs (DreamVLA, CoT-VLA, FLARE) by operating in latent embedding space over task-grounded object-centric regions instead of pixel-level frame reconstruction, and by injecting these objectives during downstream fine-tuning rather than pretraining. The implicit alignment objective connects to goal-conditioned reinforcement learning and successor representations (Dayan, 1993; Eysenbach et al., 2021), providing a theoretical grounding absent in prior VLA adaptation work. Critically, unlike inverse-dynamics or pseudo-action pipelines (VPT, IGM), FOCA's implicit objective is action-free, enabling direct co-training with synthetic videos from world models like DreamGen.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: VLA models degrade sharply under the 10–30 demonstration budgets that real deployment scenarios demand, and the action-free co-training pathway with video world models offers a concrete route to scaling adaptation data without expensive robot teleoperation. The theoretical link to goal-conditioned discounted occupancy provides principled justification for why future conditioning helps, going beyond empirical heuristics. Key limitations include the absence of explicit memory or chain-of-thought for multi-stage compositional tasks, and the method is currently validated only on diffusion-based VLAs (not autoregressive ones like EO-1 or CoT-VLA). Open questions include whether the object-centric grounding requirement (Grounding-DINO in the loop) generalizes to highly dynamic or deformable-object scenarios, and whether the approach transfers to autoregressive token-generation policies.
