## Problem

Current VLA evaluation benchmarks like LIBERO use test protocols that largely mirror the training distribution, so high success rates reflect mechanical memorization of narrow task distributions rather than robust policy learning. This creates a paradoxical duality: models exhibit spurious invariance to semantic changes (e.g., ignoring altered instructions) while simultaneously displaying extreme brittleness to trivial environmental perturbations (e.g., minor visual noise). The paper argues that generalization must be evaluated through explicit distributional assumptions rather than intuition-driven heuristics to bridge the gap between simulation scores and real-world deployment reliability.

## Input / Output

Each VLA policy receives an RGB visual observation o_t and a natural-language instruction l at each timestep, producing either discrete action tokens (autoregressive models like OpenVLA) or continuous action trajectories (flow-matching models like Pi0/Pi0.5). The benchmark evaluates across five independently controlled dimensions—background textures, instruction phrasing, distractor objects, task semantics, and spatial layout—each with three perturbation regimes (in-distribution, compositional generalization, domain generalization). Success is measured as a binary task-completion indicator averaged over N rollouts per task, yielding a success rate matrix of 5 dimensions × 3 regimes per model.

## Method

LIBERO-Gen introduces a hierarchical evaluation suite grounded in explicit distributional assumptions, decomposing each task into an abstract semantic τ and independent context factors c, then systematically intervening on those factors at test time. Training uses a structured 'Stair' sampling protocol that binds task groups to sliding windows of context values (e.g., textures, templates, distractor sets), deliberately inducing spurious correlations while ensuring sufficient marginal support for each factor. Evaluation progresses through three levels: in-distribution (same support), compositional generalization (novel recombination of seen factors), and domain generalization (unseen mechanism instantiations such as novel textures or continuous spatial coordinates). The paper formalizes both CG and DG with theoretical error bounds—CG bounded by IID generalization plus shortcut-bias and memorization-leak penalties, DG bounded by mechanism variation amplified through an expansion function—and identifies two primary failure modes (perceptual instability and binding collapse) through latent feature visualization and linear probing. A divide-and-conquer path to generalization is proposed: consistency regularization to balance encoder invariance and sensitivity, dense cross-modal attention to prevent attention inertia, structured Stair sampling to create uniform variable exposure, and continuous flow-matching decoders over discrete autoregressive tokenization for smoother action interpolation.

## Results

On LIBERO-Gen, Pi0.5 emerges as the top performer, achieving 49.2% on Position-CG and 21.2% on Task-CG, substantially outperforming all other baselines on these hardest generalization axes. In contrast, most models that saturate the original benchmark (>95% Ori.) collapse under distributional shifts: OpenVLA drops to 0.0% on Language-CG and 6.8% on Position-CG, while x-VLA and Univla reach 0.0% on Position-CG. Background generalization is comparatively easy, with most models retaining >90% success even under domain shift. Fine-tuning under the structured 'Stair' protocol further boosts Pi0.5's Position-CG to 64.0% and Task-CG to 21.2%, confirming that data topology—not just architecture—drives generalization gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024), Pi0.5 dominates on every hard generalization axis: Position-CG 49.2% vs. 6.8%, Task-CG 21.2% vs. 0.0%, and Language-CG 98.8% vs. 0.0%, while both models perform comparably on Background (>94% for both). Against Pi0 (Black et al., 2024), Pi0.5's advantage is most pronounced on Position-CG (49.2% vs. 7.2%) and Position-DG (27.2% vs. 3.6%), reflecting the benefit of continuous flow-matching over autoregressive discrete actions for spatial extrapolation; on Language and Background the two are nearly tied. Against Univla (Bu et al., 2025), Pi0.5 again leads decisively on Position-CG (49.2% vs. 0.0%) and Task-CG (21.2% vs. 9.0%), while Univla's task-centric latent actions provide only marginal benefit over Pi0 on Task-DG (9.0% vs. 36.4% for Pi0). Overall, no baseline matches Pi0.5's consistent >97% performance on Background, Language, and Object dimensions across all three perturbation regimes, confirming that the 'illusion of competence' is most severe for autoregressive and latent-action architectures under spatial and compositional task shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LIBERO benchmark (Liu et al., 2023) by replacing its near-in-distribution test protocol with a three-level hierarchical evaluation grounded in explicit distributional assumptions: in-distribution, compositional generalization, and domain generalization. It draws on the Independent Mechanisms principle (Besserve et al., 2021) and OOD generalization theory (Ye et al., 2021; Ge et al., 2025) to formalize CG and DG specifically for VLA policies, deriving error bounds that prior VLA benchmarks (LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin, VLA-Arena) lack. The structured 'Stair' sampling protocol departs from the random or heuristic perturbations used in prior robustness benchmarks, deliberately inducing spurious task-context correlations to diagnose memorization versus genuine generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LIBERO-Gen exposes a critical gap between simulation benchmark scores and deployment reliability: models achieving >95% on standard LIBERO tasks can drop to 0% under minimal semantic or spatial shifts, revealing that current evaluation protocols incentivize memorization over robust policy learning. The identification of two distinct failure modes (perceptual instability and binding collapse) and the demonstration that structured 'Stair' sampling significantly outperforms random sampling provide actionable guidance for both evaluation and training. Key limitations include the simulation-only setting, restriction to LIBERO task suites, and the fact that the hierarchical protocol's transferability to real-robot deployment remains unvalidated. Open questions include whether the CG/DG error bounds hold for larger, more diverse VLA models and whether the Stair sampling advantage persists at scale.
