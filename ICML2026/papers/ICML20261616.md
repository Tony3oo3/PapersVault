## Problem

3DGS scene training remains a bottleneck, requiring 20–40 minutes per scene with first-order optimizers like ADAM. Existing second-order approaches for 3DGS (e.g., 3DGS-LM, 3DGS²) rely on explicit or dense curvature representations that incur O(n²) memory and expensive per-iteration matrix operations, making them impractical for large scenes. Moreover, the 3DGS rasterization is highly nonlinear and piecewise continuous due to depth-based sorting, violating the smoothness assumptions of classical second-order methods and causing instability in practice.

## Input / Output

The method initializes Gaussian primitives from a standard SfM sparse point cloud and trains against RGB training images with known camera poses using a combined L1 + D-SSIM loss. In experiments, mini-batch sizes are set to a single image (|S₁|=|S₂|=ν=1) with a Hessian diagonal update interval of l=10, and the trust-region parameter ε decays exponentially from 10⁻⁶ to 10⁻⁸ over training. All experiments are run without densification, as the diagonal estimator does not yet support inserting new Gaussian parameters, limiting the method to a fixed set of splats.

## Method

3DGS²-TR replaces ADAM with a stochastic Gauss-Newton update where the curvature is approximated by the diagonal of JᵀJ, estimated via Hutchinson's method (Rademacher random vectors) every l iterations and smoothed with an exponential moving average, yielding O(n) computation and memory identical to ADAM. The raw update Δx = −D̂⁻¹ĝ is then constrained by a parameter-wise trust-region radius η derived from the squared Hellinger distance between each Gaussian splat before and after the update, normalized by det(S) to account for viewing-distance scaling. Closed-form bounds are derived for each parameter type: position (involving the covariance diagonal), scale (proportional to S_c²), opacity (proportional to α), color (proportional to C_c/α), and rotation quaternion components (involving a geometry-dependent sensitivity constant β_c). The final step is obtained by element-wise clipping Δx to [−η, +η], requiring only a single tunable hyperparameter ε instead of per-parameter learning rates.

## Results

On the aggregated MiP-NeRF360, Deep Blending, and Tanks & Temples benchmarks (without densification), 3DGS²-TR achieves PSNR of 25.39, 27.74, and 22.85 dB at 30k iterations, outperforming ADAM (25.08, 27.36, 21.66 dB) by up to 1.19 dB on Tanks & Temples. The method reaches comparable or better quality than ADAM at 7k iterations (50% fewer steps), e.g. 22.12 vs. 20.69 dB PSNR on Tanks & Temples. Peak GPU memory overhead is less than 1 GB over ADAM (6.51 vs. 5.60 GB on MiP-NeRF360), while 3DGS-LM requires 47.75 GB. Training time is approximately 3× ADAM (652 s vs. 212 s on MiP-NeRF360) due to the unoptimized PyTorch implementation of the Sophia optimizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ADAM (the standard first-order baseline), 3DGS²-TR wins on all three metrics (SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS) across all three datasets at 30k iterations, with the largest PSNR gain of +1.19 dB on Tanks & Temples (22.85 vs. 21.66 dB). At 7k iterations (50% of training), 3DGS²-TR already surpasses ADAM's 30k-iteration quality on Tanks & Temples (22.12 vs. 21.66 dB PSNR). Against 3DGS-LM (the only open-source second-order 3DGS optimizer), 3DGS²-TR is substantially better in reconstruction quality (e.g., 25.39 vs. 21.88 dB PSNR on MiP-NeRF360) while using 85% less peak GPU memory (6.51 vs. 47.75 GB) and completing in roughly 4× less wall-clock time. The ablation ADAM-TR (applying the Hellinger trust region to ADAM updates) also improves over vanilla ADAM, confirming that the trust-region constraint contributes independently of the second-order curvature estimate. The authors note that 3DGS²-TR's per-iteration time is inflated by an unoptimized PyTorch implementation of the Sophia optimizer, not a fundamental algorithmic limitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Sophia optimizer (Liu et al., 2023), which uses Hutchinson's method to estimate diagonal Hessian entries for large-scale LLM training, to the 3D Gaussian Splatting setting. It departs from prior second-order 3DGS methods (3DGS-LM, 3DGS²) that rely on explicit or block-diagonal curvature representations by using only the Hessian diagonal, achieving O(n) complexity identical to ADAM. The key novel contribution is a parameter-wise trust region based on the squared Hellinger distance between Gaussian primitives, which exploits the geometric interpretability of 3DGS parameters to bound updates in a way that prior global or group-level regularizers (e.g., LM's adaptive damping) do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a drop-in replacement for ADAM in 3DGS training that reaches comparable or better quality in 50% fewer iterations with under 1GB additional GPU memory, making it attractive for large-scale and potentially distributed training. However, the current implementation does not support densification (adding or moving Gaussians), which is a core component of the standard 3DGS pipeline, and the per-iteration wall-clock time is higher than ADAM due to an unoptimized PyTorch implementation. Open questions include how to extend the diagonal estimator to handle dynamic parameter counts during densification, and whether the Hellinger trust region generalizes to other explicit scene representations beyond 3DGS.
