## Problem

Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) generate sequences by progressively unmasking tokens but lack the ability to detect and correct low-quality or incorrect tokens at inference time. Prior self-correction approaches for MDMs either rely on imprecise proxies for token quality (random scores, stale likelihoods, or untrained logits) or require overhauling the MDM architecture and pretraining, limiting their applicability to existing pretrained models.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a fully masked sequence of length L and produces both a clean token sequence and per-token quality scores (scalars in [0,1] per position) in a single forward pass. The quality scores are computed by a lightweight sigmoid head sharing the MDM backbone, while the unmasking posterior (a categorical distribution over the vocabulary per position) is produced by the original softmax head. During fine-tuning, the input is a partially masked sequence z, and the model must predict the quality of a token at a position that is already unmasked, despite the target being defined under a masked context.

## Method

PRISM defines per-token quality as the posterior likelihood p(x^i = y^i | y ⊕ m_i) — how likely a token is given the rest of the sequence with that position masked — and introduces a binary cross-entropy loss whose unique minimizer provably recovers this quantity, independent of the pretrained model's accuracy. The framework attaches a lightweight adapter (quality head) to any pretrained MDM backbone and fine-tunes with the PRISM loss, where training pairs (x, z, (y, i)) are constructed by masking a clean sequence and then unmasking a subset of positions using the pretrained model with stop-gradient. At inference, the fine-tuned model jointly computes the unmasking posterior and per-token quality in one forward pass; tokens with the lowest quality scores are remasked and resampled in subsequent steps, adding no computational overhead over vanilla MDM inference. The approach is validated across Sudoku (30M), unconditional text (170M), and code generation with LLaDA-8B, requiring far fewer training tokens than pretraining.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, PRISM achieves 42.7% pass@1 on HumanEval and 32.3% on MBPP at 1024 sampling steps, outperforming vanilla MDM (36.6% / 31.9%) and ReMDM-conf (36.6% / 32.5%). On OpenWebText (170M MDM), PRISM reaches a MAUVE score of 0.510 and generative perplexity of 20.2 at 1024 NFE, substantially surpassing ReMDM (0.403 / 28.6) and ReMDM-conf (0.051 / 48.0). The fine-tuning is highly data-efficient: only 164M tokens (1,600× fewer than pretraining) and <500 GPU-hours are needed to acquire self-correction ability on the 8B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM consistently outperforms ReMDM (Wang et al., 2025) on HumanEval across all sampling budgets (e.g., 39.0 vs. 34.8 at 512 steps; 28.0 vs. 26.2 at 256 steps) and on OpenWebText MAUVE (0.510 vs. 0.403 at 1024 NFE) and generative perplexity (20.2 vs. 28.6). Against ReMDM-conf, PRISM shows a larger gap on HumanEval (42.7 vs. 36.6 at 1024 steps) and OpenWebText (MAUVE 0.510 vs. 0.051), reflecting that ReMDM-conf's stale quality scores degrade with more inference steps. Compared to vanilla MDM (Sahoo et al., 2024) without any remasking, PRISM gains 6.1 points on HumanEval (42.7 vs. 36.6) and 4.1 points on MBPP (32.3 vs. 31.9) at 1024 steps, with the gap widening at fewer steps (e.g., +12.4 on HumanEval at 256 steps). The one exception is MBPP at 1024 steps where ReMDM edges out PRISM by 0.6 points (32.9 vs. 32.3), though PRISM leads at 256 and 512 steps. On Sudoku, PRISM surpasses both ReMDM and ReMDM-conf within a few fine-tuning epochs, far fewer than the ~530 pretraining epochs.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM builds on the any-order language model formulation of Masked Diffusion Models (Sahoo et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2024), where the MDM learns per-position unmasking posteriors. It departs from prior MDM self-correction work along two axes: unlike training-free remasking (ReMDM, DFM) or proxy-x̂ scorers (Token Critic, P2-Train) that use imprecise quality proxies, PRISM defines an explicit per-token quality target (the posterior likelihood of a token given the rest of the sequence) and proves a simple BCE loss recovers it. Unlike architecture-changing methods (GIDD, Informed Corrector's hollow-transformer), PRISM is plug-and-play, attaching a lightweight adapter to any pretrained MDM backbone without modifying the unmasking head or attention mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRISM provides the first provable, model-agnostic fine-tuning recipe for equipping pretrained MDMs with self-correction, requiring no RL, no external verifier, and no architectural overhaul. The practical efficiency is notable: 1600× fewer tokens than pretraining for text, and <500 GPU-hours for an 8B model, making self-correction accessible to practitioners who already have a pretrained MDM. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that per-token quality is based on per-position posterior marginals and cannot capture global reasoning or structural errors (e.g., wrong iteration structure in code). The paper frames this as a stepping stone toward broader self-correction frameworks for discrete diffusion, and the knowledge-transfer result (a PRISM-tuned model correcting a different pretrained MDM) opens the door to decoupling quality estimation from generation.
