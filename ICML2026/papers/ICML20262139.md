## Problem

Privacy-sensitive research is fundamentally constrained by data scarcity: private information cannot be publicly shared, so prior work relies on small, narrowly scoped datasets, in stark contrast to the data-scaling paradigm driving progress elsewhere in AI. Meanwhile, LLM-powered agentic systems increasingly process personal documents at inference time, yet even frontier models like GPT-5 achieve only 70% full success on basic sanitization and 13% on harder cases, revealing a critical gap in both training data and model capability for privacy-preserving text processing.

## Input / Output

The sanitization model takes a text record (averaging 527 words across medical, legal, financial, and communication domains) paired with a natural-language instruction (averaging 69.4 words) that specifies which attributes to abstract or drop and which to retain. The output is a full rewritten record where target attributes are either graded-abstracted (e.g., 'March 3rd, 2024' → 'early March' → 'this spring') or completely removed, while retention-target attributes are explicitly preserved. Unlike fixed PII-category systems, the instruction supports arbitrary user-specified sensitivity, and the decomposition pipeline processes records in ≤512-character chunks to handle long documents.

## Method

The PRIVASIS synthesis pipeline uses auxiliary control variables (a personal profile seeded from the US SSN name database, a record type, and a background context) for informed initialization, then applies iterative rejection sampling with a weighted acceptance score combining LLM quality judgment and Vendi embedding diversity to produce 1.4M diverse records with 55M annotated attributes. For sanitization, a decomposition pipeline recursively splits each record into chunks, uses an LLM to identify relevant spans per target attribute, generates per-target abstraction or drop instructions, applies them consistently across chunks in parallel, and finally synthesizes a coherent user-style instruction with retention targets selected to minimize lexical overlap (via ROUGE) with sanitization targets. The PRIVASIS-CLEANER models (Qwen3 0.6B and 4B) are fine-tuned via supervised instruction tuning on 37K (original, instruction, sanitized) triplets, achieving 72.5% full success on the vanilla test set—outperforming all frontier LLMs including GPT-5 and o3—while remaining small enough for on-device deployment.

## Results

On the PRIVASIS-SANITIZATION Vanilla test set (1,042 records), PRIVASIS-CLEANER-4B achieves 72.50% Full Successful Record, surpassing all frontier LLMs including o3 (70.25%), GPT-5 (70.06%), and Qwen3-235B (64.40%). On the harder test set (1,149 records with grouped attributes and longer contexts), performance drops sharply for all models, with GPT-5 leading at 13.14% and PRIVASIS-CLEANER-4B close behind at 12.36%. The 0.6B variant also outperforms GPT-OSS-120B, LLaMA-4 Maverick, and Qwen3-235B on the Vanilla set (68.04% vs. 67.66%, 67.18%, 64.40%), while achieving near-perfect retention (99.26% Successful Attribute) compared to GPT-5's 93.42%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRIVASIS-CLEANER-4B outperforms GPT-OSS-120B by 4.84 points (72.50% vs. 67.66%) and Qwen3-235B by 8.10 points (72.50% vs. 64.40%) on the Vanilla Full Successful Record metric, while also achieving substantially higher retention (99.15% vs. 95.28% and 93.47% Successful Attribute, respectively). On the Hard set, PRIVASIS-CLEANER-4B (12.36%) trails GPT-5 (13.14%) by 0.78 points but surpasses GPT-OSS-120B (10.53%) and Qwen3-235B (10.27%). In the cross-dataset generalization experiment on NaP², PRIVASIS-CLEANER-4B matches a NaP²-fine-tuned 4B model at 10% leak ratio, yet scores 72.5% vs. 31.96% Full Successful Record on the PRIVASIS test set, demonstrating that training on PRIVASIS's scale and diversity yields superior generalization. The 0.6B model also beats GPT-OSS-120B and Qwen3-235B on the Vanilla set (68.04% vs. 67.66% and 64.40%), highlighting the efficiency gains from targeted training on PRIVASIS-SANITIZATION.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of synthetic data generation and privacy-preserving NLP. Prior privacy datasets (TAB, MIMIC, Self-Disclosure Corpus) rely on real-world reference data or are limited to short, single-domain texts with fixed PII categories; PRIVASIS departs from all of these by being entirely reference-free, million-scale, multi-domain, and supporting arbitrary user-specified attributes with multi-level abstraction. Methodologically, it repurposes ideas from diversity-preserving generation (Vendi score, rejection sampling) and auxiliary-variable conditioning (analogous to controlled text generation) but applies them in a privacy-specific context where no prior pipeline achieved this scale. For sanitization, it extends the task from span-level PII detection (i2b2, TAB) to instruction-following rewriting on long documents via a decomposition pipeline, going beyond the deletion-only or single-level abstraction offered by prior corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRIVASIS addresses a fundamental bottleneck: privacy research has been starved of data because real private information cannot be publicly shared, yet agentic LLM systems increasingly process sensitive documents at inference time. The practical implication of compact (≤4B) models outperforming frontier LLMs on sanitization is that on-device data minimization becomes feasible without sending private text to external servers. Key limitations include English-only output, US-SSN-seeded names, and LLM-specific stylistic biases; open questions include whether the dataset supports differential privacy training, membership inference benchmarking, and agentic evaluation in privacy-sensitive contexts. The dataset's scale and diversity make it a likely standard resource for the privacy-preserving ML community.
