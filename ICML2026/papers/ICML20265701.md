## Problem

Existing group-based RL methods for multimodal reasoning (e.g., GRPO) compute advantages only at the final-answer level, treating all candidate answers as directly comparable even when they rely on different visual evidence. This lack of explicit credit assignment over visual objects leads to reasoning hallucinations—models ignore key visual cues or produce answers that contradict visual facts—and makes reward hacking easier since a single scalar reward cannot distinguish which visual evidence actually contributed to correctness.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with a text query and produces a structured reasoning chain in which the model first emits 2D bounding boxes in [x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max] format within <think> tags to ground relevant visual objects, then synthesizes spatial information in a <rethink> phase, and finally outputs a concise answer in an <answer> tag. During training, ground-truth bounding boxes are available for computing object confidence scores (IoU + normalized L1), but at inference the model must generate boxes autonomously without any external visual cues.

## Method

POLIA extends GRPO with a two-stage advantage computation that separates answer-level and object-level credit assignment. In the first stage, an extrinsic advantage A^ext is computed by normalizing a weighted sum of answer-correctness and format-validity rewards across the group of N sampled candidates, following standard GRPO. In the second stage, candidates are grouped by the set of visual objects they reference (matched via IoU > 0.5 to ground-truth boxes); within each visual-object group, the extrinsic reward is corrected by a per-object confidence score (0.7·IoU + 0.3·L1, clipped to [0,1]), and an intrinsic advantage A^int is computed by normalizing these corrected rewards within the group. The final clipped-surrogate policy objective applies A^ext to all reasoning-chain tokens while A^int is applied only to the coordinate tokens of each referenced object, weighted by a hyperparameter ω, thereby providing fine-grained, object-level gradient signals that encourage the policy to emphasize effective visual evidence without additional training cost beyond negligible grouping and normalization steps.

## Results

On the 7B setting, POLIA achieves 81.3% on VSR, 56.7% on TallyQA, 69.5% on GQA, and 74.8% on MathVista, outperforming GPT-4o (62.3/38.9/52.2/60.6) and Gemini 2.5-pro (64.0/49.8/60.5/54.1) on all four perception-centric benchmarks. On the 3B setting, POLIA-3B reaches 71.9% on VSR and 60.2% on GQA, surpassing the strongest RL baseline (GRPO-3B at 53.5%/57.1%) by +18.4 and +3.1 points respectively. Average improvements over same-scale baselines are +27.6 on VSR, +19.2 on GQA, and +16.1 on MathVista (7B), and +24.2 on VSR, +17.2 on GQA, and +15.6 on MathVista (3B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (the primary group-based RL baseline), POLIA-7B wins on every benchmark: VSR +22.3 (81.3 vs 59.0), TallyQA +8.7 (56.7 vs 48.0), GQA +11.3 (69.5 vs 58.2), and MathVista +9.3 (74.8 vs 65.5). Against DAPO, POLIA-7B similarly dominates on VSR (+20.6), GQA (+10.7), and MathVista (+12.9), with a smaller TallyQA margin (+3.9). In the 3B setting, POLIA-3B outperforms GRPO-3B by +18.4 on VSR and +7.2 on TallyQA, and beats GRIT-3B (which explicitly emphasizes visual evidence) by +10.7 on VSR and +7.7 on MathVista. Notably, POLIA-7B surpasses both GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5-pro on all four perception-centric benchmarks (VSR, TallyQA, GQA, MathVista), though it trails Gemini 2.5-pro substantially on MathVision (29.4 vs 73.3) and LogicVista (48.7 vs 73.8), indicating the gains are concentrated in tasks requiring precise visual grounding rather than pure mathematical or logical reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

POLIA extends GRPO's critic-free group-based policy optimization from text-only LLMs to multimodal settings by introducing a second, object-level advantage signal. It departs from prior MLLM RL methods (GRIT, VLM-R1, Skywork R1V, DeepEyes) that either mix visual and text tokens uniformly or add behavioral constraints on reasoning, by explicitly modeling visual evidence as discrete object groups and computing within-group intrinsic advantages over those objects. The visual-object grouping and confidence-weighted reward correction are the core methodological departures, providing a structured credit assignment mechanism that answer-level rewards alone cannot supply when different candidates reference different visual evidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete gap in multimodal RL—coarse credit assignment over visual evidence—that has limited prior work to marginal gains. The negligible computational overhead (0.002 s for intrinsic advantage) makes the approach practically adoptable, and the convergence analysis showing visual object groups stabilize during training offers a useful diagnostic for practitioners. Limitations include reliance on bounding-box matching for object identification, training on only two datasets (VSR, TallyQA), and modest gains on pure-reasoning benchmarks (MathVision +3.2%, LogicVista +2.3%), suggesting the method's benefits are concentrated in perception-heavy tasks. Open questions include whether the object-group abstraction generalizes to more complex visual evidence (e.g., regions, attributes, temporal sequences) and whether the approach scales to larger model families beyond 7B.
