## Problem

Unifying independently trained, domain-specialized experts into a single deployable MoE model is infeasible under privacy constraints because router training requires data that approximates each client's private distribution, yet that data cannot be shared. Existing proxy-based approaches (e.g., FlexOlmo) select public samples by similarity alone, producing redundant, narrowly concentrated proxies that fail to cover the full private-data manifold, while experts trained exclusively on private data remain domain-isolated from the proxy-based routing supervision.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes K independently LoRA-finetuned experts (FFN sublayers only, with all other parameters frozen), a public dataset (ImageNet for CV; Alpaca or OpenOrca for NLP), and each client's private data that never leaves the client. The final unified MoE model accepts standard RGB images (224×224 for ViT) or text sequences with multiple-choice options and outputs class labels or answer selections. Notably, the router is trained exclusively on proxy subsets drawn from the public dataset, and the only artifacts communicated to a central server are proxy sample indices, final expert weights, and routing vectors—never raw private samples, gradients, or intermediate activations.

## Method

MetaMoE is a three-stage privacy-preserving unification framework. First, a relevance-weighted DPP selects m proxy samples per client from the public dataset by augmenting the standard DPP kernel with client-specific relevance scores (from a binary classifier distinguishing public vs. private data), balancing domain relevance against sample diversity via greedy MAP inference with Cholesky updates. Second, each client finetunes its expert's FFN sublayers (via LoRA) on the union of private and proxy data, so expert representations are calibrated to the same distribution that will supervise the router, mitigating domain isolation. Third, a context-aware router blends token-level and sequence-level representations (with a learnable mixing weight) and is initialized with domain-aware routing vectors computed as mean embeddings over each client's private-plus-proxy data; the merged MoE is then jointly finetuned on the union of all proxy datasets. The paper further provides formal privacy guarantees showing per-sample sensitivity of shared routing vectors is bounded by O(1/m) and that the mean private embedding is unrecoverable without knowledge of the private dataset size.

## Results

On CV benchmarks (Pets, Flowers, EuroSAT) with CLIP ViT-B/32, MetaMoE achieves 94.52% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest privacy-preserving baseline FlexOlmo (92.92%) by 1.6 points and approaching the unrestricted upper bound (95.68%). With CLIP ViT-B/16, MetaMoE reaches 96.24% average accuracy. On NLP commonsense reasoning (CSQA, CosmosQA, SocialIQA) with LLaMA-3.1-8B, MetaMoE achieves 81.59% average accuracy, surpassing FlexOlmo (77.46%) by 4.1 points and even exceeding the unrestricted MoE upper bound (81.34%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaMoE consistently outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the strongest privacy-preserving baseline FlexOlmo (Shi et al., 2025), MetaMoE gains +1.6 points on ViT-B/32 (94.52 vs. 92.92), +2.7 on ViT-B/16 (96.24 vs. 93.53), +1.9 on LLaMA-3.2-3B (74.42 vs. 72.50), and +4.1 on LLaMA-3.1-8B (81.59 vs. 77.46). Against BTM (Li et al., 2022), which ensembles expert predictions without parameter sharing, MetaMoE improves by 4.2 points on ViT-B/32 (94.52 vs. 90.33) and 1.6 on LLaMA-3.1-8B (81.59 vs. 79.79). ModelSoup (Wortsman et al., 2022) and BTX (Sukhbaatar et al., 2024) are substantially outperformed, with gaps of 20+ points on CV (e.g., 94.52 vs. 74.20 for ModelSoup, 94.52 vs. 74.30 for BTX on ViT-B/32), reflecting their inability to preserve expert specialization. In the federated learning comparison (Table 10), MetaMoE also beats CoMiGS (Fan et al., 2025) by 2.8 points (74.42 vs. 71.65) and Mixture-of-LoRAs (Feng et al., 2024) by 2.5 points (74.42 vs. 71.95) on LLaMA-3.2-3B, while avoiding the iterative synchronization overhead inherent to FL methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaMoE extends the privacy-preserving MoE unification line initiated by FlexOlmo (Shi et al., 2025), which itself builds on BTX's (Sukhbaatar et al., 2024) architecture of transplanting expert FFN sublayers into a shared MoE with a learned router. The key departure is replacing FlexOlmo's relevance-only proxy selection with a relevance-weighted determinantal point process (DPP) that explicitly balances domain relevance against sample diversity, drawing on the DPP literature (Kulesza & Taskar, 2012) for subset selection. Two additional mechanisms—proxy-aligned expert training (exposing experts to proxy data during fine-tuning) and a context-aware router blending token-level and sequence-level representations—address the domain-isolation and routing-collision problems that arise when the router is trained solely on proxies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As organizations increasingly fine-tune shared foundation models on private, non-shareable data, the ability to unify these domain-specialized experts into a single deployable MoE without violating data residency is a practically important and underexplored problem. MetaMoE's formal privacy guarantees (bounded per-sample sensitivity, unrecoverability of mean private embeddings) provide a stronger theoretical foundation than prior proxy-based methods, and the relevance-weighted DPP is a transferable selection principle applicable beyond MoE unification. Open questions include scalability to many more clients and larger model families (beyond 8B), whether the approach extends to truly heterogeneous expert architectures, and whether the data-residency privacy model suffices for high-stakes regulatory settings that demand differential privacy.
