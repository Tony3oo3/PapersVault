## Problem

Consistency Models (CMs) enable one- or two-step image generation but require prolonged training with large batch sizes to reach competitive sample quality. This paper identifies a previously unaddressed cause of slow convergence: CM trajectory tangents (the instantaneous update directions of CM outputs) contain substantial oscillatory components that move parallel to the data manifold rather than toward it, wasting optimization effort on irrelevant directions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262092/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise (optionally conditioned on a class label or text prompt) and produces a synthetic image in one or two neural network evaluations, bypassing iterative ODE integration. Experiments span pixel-space generation on CIFAR-10 (32×32) and ImageNet 64×64, latent-space generation on ImageNet 512×512 via SD-VAE, and text-to-image on CC12M at 512×512 using Stable Diffusion 1.5 with LoRA fine-tuning. The auxiliary manifold feature network (VGG16) is trained separately on the target dataset before CM training begins.

## Method

The key insight (Proposition 5.1) is that when the CM consistency loss is computed in a feature space defined by a map φ, the effective trajectory tangent becomes a linear combination of the rows of φ's Jacobian, so φ entirely determines the update direction. The authors design φ as a collection of 15 scalar 'manifold features' whose level sets correspond to increasingly perturbed versions of the data manifold (via Gaussian noise, blur, mixup, scaling, rotation, translation, brightness, contrast, hue, and saturation transformations), ensuring that ∇φ_i points toward the manifold. Each φ_i is learned self-supervisedly by minimizing ‖φ_i(T_α^i(x)) − α‖² over random perturbation strengths α, using a VGG16 backbone trained from scratch. The CM is then trained with the squared feature distance ‖φ(f_θ(x_t,t)) − φ(f_sg(x_{t−Δt},t−Δt))‖² as the consistency loss, which aligns trajectory tangents toward the data manifold and suppresses oscillatory parallel components. This yields up to ×10 faster convergence over pseudo-Huber loss, outperforms LPIPS (which requires ImageNet pre-training and human-curated patch similarity data), and remains robust to batch sizes as small as 16.

## Results

On unconditional CIFAR-10, ECT+AYT achieves 2.61 FID with 1 NFE and 2.13 FID with 2 NFE, improving over ECT (3.60 / 2.11) and iCT (2.83 / 2.46). On class-conditional ImageNet 64×64, ECT-S+AYT reaches 4.42 FID (1 NFE) and 3.27 FID (2 NFE), improving over ECT-S (5.51 / 3.18). On the CC12M text-to-image task (512×512, SD 1.5), AYT achieves 50.61 / 15.39 / 11.53 FID at 1/2/4 steps, substantially outperforming Pseudo-Huber (61.11 / 20.87 / 13.33) and LPIPS (217.84 / 127.96 / 38.59). AYT also remains robust to batch size, achieving competitive FID with batch size as small as 16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline ECT (Geng et al., 2025b), AYT improves 1-step FID from 3.60 to 2.61 on CIFAR-10 and from 5.51 to 4.42 on ImageNet 64×64, while 2-step performance is comparable (2.13 vs 2.11; 3.27 vs 3.18). AYT outperforms iCT (Song & Dhariwal, 2024) on CIFAR-10 1-step (2.61 vs 2.83) and 2-step (2.13 vs 2.46), but loses to iCT-deep (2.51) and iCT on ImageNet 64×64 (4.42 vs 4.02 1-step; 3.27 vs 3.20 2-step). Against distillation methods that rely on pretrained teachers, AYT trails CTM (1.87) and SiD (1.92) on CIFAR-10 1-step, yet trains from scratch without adversarial objectives. AYT's 2-step CIFAR-10 FID (2.13) surpasses 10-step DPM-Solver++ (2.91) and DPM-Solver-v3 (2.51), and its 1-step FID (2.61) beats 1000-step DDPM (3.17). On CC12M text-to-image, AYT decisively outperforms both Pseudo-Huber and LPIPS losses across all step counts, with LPIPS suffering severe FID degradation (217.84 at 1-step) likely due to ImageNet–CIFAR domain mismatch. AYT also beats the original CM with LPIPS (Song et al., 2023) by a wide margin (2.61 vs 8.70 on CIFAR-10 1-step) and outperforms DMD, PD, TRACT, and DFNO distillation baselines on CIFAR-10 1-step FID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Consistency Model framework (Song et al., 2023) and the Easy Consistency Training (ECT) recipe (Geng et al., 2025b), proposing a new loss function—manifold feature distance (MFD)—that replaces the standard pseudo-Huber or LPIPS consistency losses. Conceptually, it departs from prior perceptual-loss work (LPIPS, pseudo-Huber) by grounding the loss design in a geometric diagnosis of CM training dynamics: trajectory tangents oscillate parallel to the data manifold, and the proposed feature-space loss amplifies manifold-orthogonal components. The feature-distance formulation draws on Kim et al. (2025a)'s observation that invertible feature maps can accelerate flow matching, but extends it to CMs with a self-supervised, manifold-aware construction that requires no human-curated similarity data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone training consistency models: AYT accelerates wall-clock convergence by 2–4×, outperforms LPIPS (which suffers from dataset-mismatch degradation), and remains robust at batch sizes as small as 16—reducing hardware requirements by 8×. The self-supervised construction of manifold features (VGG16 trained on parameterized image degradations) is domain-agnostic and avoids the ImageNet pretraining + human curation pipeline that LPIPS requires. Open questions include scaling beyond 64×64 pixel-space generation (512×512 results are preliminary), extension to other modalities (audio, text), and whether the manifold-orthogonality guarantee (Proposition D.4) holds under the eigenvector assumption for all practical transformations.
