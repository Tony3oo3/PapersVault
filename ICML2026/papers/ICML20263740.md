## Problem

The rapid proliferation of LLM-generated research ideas has outpaced our ability to evaluate them, creating a bottleneck at the start of the research pipeline. Existing automated evaluation methods suffer from three core deficiencies: narrow knowledge horizons (relying on static paper corpora rather than the full living ecosystem of literature, web, and code), flattened evaluation dimensions (compressing multi-criteria assessment into one or two scores), and the inherent bias of single-perspective LLM-as-a-Judge that fossilizes model preferences into de facto criteria. InnoEval reframes idea evaluation as a knowledge-grounded, multi-perspective reasoning problem to address all three gaps simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The system accepts a raw-text research idea in any format (from a preliminary hypothesis to a fully written paper) along with an optional timestamp that anchors the evaluation temporally. It first distills the text into a structured six-tuple (TLDR, Motivations, Research Questions, Methods, Experimental Settings, Expected Results), where the last three fields are optional depending on idea maturity. The output is a comprehensive evaluation report containing: (1) enriched background knowledge reports from literature, web, and code sources; (2) per-dimension scores (0–10) with detailed review narratives from five randomly sampled academic personas across five customizable dimensions (Clarity, Novelty, Feasibility, Validity, Significance); (3) a synthesized meta-review with a final decision (Reject, Poster, Spotlight, or Oral); and (4) concrete revision suggestions grounded in post-timestamp knowledge. For group-wise evaluation, the report additionally includes a full ranked list of all ideas with comparative analyses per dimension.

## Method

InnoEval is a multi-agent pipeline built on DeepSeek-V3.2 with three core modules. First, a heterogeneous deep knowledge search engine iteratively (N=3 rounds) retrieves and enriches evidence from six online sources (ArXiv, Semantic Scholar, Google Scholar, Google Search, GitHub, Kaggle) using a fast-slow hybrid: fast API queries with synonym expansion yield brief results, which are then enriched via full PDF parsing, webpage summarization, or code-repo call-graph analysis; a hybrid scoring function blends embedding-based semantic similarity (bge-base-en-v1.5 + bge-reranker-base) with LLM-as-judge relevance scores (α=0.2) to select top-m items per source type, and iterative query refinement rewrites, generalizes, or concretizes queries based on previously retrieved knowledge. Second, a grounding agent aligns each structured idea part with specific supporting or contradicting evidence from the retrieved knowledge, producing fine-grained relevance analyses. Third, an innovation review board of 15 synthesized academic personas (varying in seniority, subfield expertise, and reviewing habits) is sampled five at a time per idea; each persona's knowledge familiarity vector controls how much of the retrieved evidence is masked to simulate real expertise limits, and five dimension-specialized evaluator agents independently score the idea on each criterion with detailed narratives, which are then aggregated into a meta-review and final decision.

## Results

On the point-wise ternary classification task (Reject/Poster/Highlight), InnoEval achieves 74.56% macro F1, outperforming the strongest baseline ScholarEval (58.38%) by 16.18 points. On pair-wise comparison, InnoEval reaches 80.81% accuracy on easy pairs and 63.00% on hard pairs. On group-wise ranking, InnoEval attains 65.12% best-selection accuracy and 22.09% exact-ranking accuracy, surpassing ScholarEval by 15.7 and 7.56 points respectively. Qualitatively, InnoEval's evaluation reports achieve over 70% win-rate in Overall Quality against all baselines when judged by o4-mini.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InnoEval outperforms all four baselines across every task and metric. Against the strongest baseline ScholarEval (Moussa et al., 2025), InnoEval gains +16.18 F1 on ternary point-wise classification (74.56 vs. 58.38), +6.39 accuracy on easy pair-wise comparison (80.81 vs. 74.42), and +15.7 accuracy on group-wise best-selection (65.12 vs. 49.42). Against InternAgent (Zhang et al., 2025), the gap widens substantially: +31.51 F1 on point-wise ternary (74.56 vs. 43.05) and +23.84 on group-wise best-selection (65.12 vs. 41.28). ResearchAgent (Baek et al., 2025) lags further behind at 39.81 F1 on point-wise and 40.12 on group-wise best-selection, largely because it relies on a pre-constructed static literature collection. GraphEval (Feng et al., 2025), designed only for single-idea label prediction, performs worst on pair-wise and group-wise tasks (43.60% easy-pair accuracy, 20.93% best-selection), highlighting the inflexibility of its graph-propagation approach for multi-idea comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior idea-evaluation methods (GraphEval, ScholarEval) that rely on static literature-only retrieval and single-judge LLM-as-a-Judge scoring, by reframing evaluation as a knowledge-grounded, multi-perspective reasoning problem. It repurposes concepts from retrieval-augmented generation (extended to heterogeneous sources: literature, web, code), multi-agent systems (an innovation review board of diverse academic personas), and collective intelligence literature (review consensus via persona sampling) into a unified evaluation pipeline. The multi-dimensional decoupled evaluation (Clarity, Novelty, Feasibility, Validity, Significance) extends the single-score or two-criterion (soundness + contribution) assessments used by prior work like ScholarEval, while the iterative deep search with query refinement goes beyond the one-shot retrieval in ResearchAgent and InternAgent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck: LLM-driven idea generation has outpaced automated evaluation, and the resulting reliance on scarce human experts is unsustainable. The multi-perspective persona approach provides a principled mechanism to mitigate LLM-as-Judge bias, and the heterogeneous search (web + code + literature) captures 'living knowledge' that static corpora miss. Key limitations include restriction to the AI/ML domain, ~30 minutes per evaluation, and text-only modality; open questions include whether the framework generalizes to biology, medicine, or physics, whether efficiency can be improved without sacrificing quality, and whether the persona-based consensus approach scales to truly interdisciplinary review.
