## Problem

RAG-based web search systems are increasingly vulnerable to adversarial attacks (prompt injection, corpus poisoning) that intensify in dynamic settings where the evidence corpus evolves over time. Existing defenses are designed for static snapshots and, when naively extended to temporal streams, either incur prohibitive storage costs by archiving all historical documents or fail to maintain robustness against continuously mutating threats. The paper tackles the stability-plasticity dilemma: how to defend against evolving attacks while remaining adaptable to genuine knowledge updates.

## Input / Output

RADAR takes a user query and a set of top-k retrieved web documents (k=10 or 50) as input; in dynamic mode it additionally receives the previous time step's answer as a compact memory signal (~1 KB) rather than the full document history. For each document, an LLM generates a short atomic answer, and an NLI model (DeBERTa-v3) computes pairwise entailment and contradiction scores. The output is a binary label per document (reliable vs. adversarial), a filtered reliable subset, and a final synthesized answer produced by the generator LLM strictly from the selected atomic answers.

## Method

RADAR formulates reliable document selection as a submodular binary energy minimization (Markov Random Field) over an s-t graph, solved exactly via Max-Flow Min-Cut. The graph encodes unary costs through source edges (eigenvector centrality × exponential rank decay) and sink edges (centrality-weighted contradiction scores), and pairwise consistency through inter-document edges weighted by symmetric NLI entailment scores. For dynamic defense, a Bayesian memory node representing the previous answer is added to the graph, with its terminal edge capacities updated via Bayes' theorem combining a prior coherence score with a likelihood derived from the new evidence's entailment/contradiction with the old answer. This recursive belief update lets the Min-Cut automatically decide whether to retain or discard historical knowledge without archiving raw documents. A post-processing step removes semantically isolated documents via a cosine-similarity threshold (λ=0.3) before the final LLM synthesis.

## Results

On RealTimeQA (RQA) with DeepSeek, RADAR maintains 75.0% accuracy in the benign setting (matching Vanilla RAG) while achieving the best robustness-utility trade-off under attacks: 75.0% Acc / 5.0% ASR under PIA at Pos 10 (top-k=10) and 76.0% Acc / 3.0% ASR under PIA at Pos 25/50 (top-k=50). In the dynamic setting (cumulative snapshot, top-k=50), RADAR reaches 74.02% benign accuracy and sustains ~70% accuracy with <9% ASR under mid- and tail-rank PIA attacks, compared to 12–30% accuracy for Vanilla RAG. Under the most challenging PIA Pos 1 attack in the dynamic setting, RADAR achieves 63.60% accuracy versus 61.29% (RobustRAG) and 53.61% (ReliabilityRAG).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RADAR consistently outperforms Vanilla RAG by large margins under attacks (e.g., 69% vs. 25% accuracy under PIA Pos 1, top-k=10; 72% vs. 35% under PIA Pos 1, top-k=50), while matching its 75% benign accuracy. Against RobustRAG, RADAR achieves higher accuracy in nearly all attack scenarios (e.g., 72% vs. 69% under PIA Pos 1 top-k=50; 76% vs. 71% under PIA Pos 50 top-k=50) while maintaining comparable or lower ASR. Against ReliabilityRAG, RADAR wins on accuracy in most settings (76% vs. 74–76% under mid/tail PIA, top-k=50) and achieves lower ASR under PIA Pos 1 (5% vs. 18%, top-k=50). AstuteRAG and InstructRAG suffer severe accuracy collapse under attacks (21–38% and 23–63% respectively), making them substantially less robust than RADAR. In the dynamic setting, RADAR's advantage over RobustRAG and ReliabilityRAG is most pronounced at Pos 1 (63.6% vs. 61.3% and 53.6% accuracy), confirming that the Bayesian memory node provides meaningful gains when adversarial content targets high-ranked evidence.

## Relation to Prior Work

RADAR builds on the RAG defense literature (RobustRAG, ReliabilityRAG, AstuteRAG, InstructRAG) but departs from their heuristic filtering or approximate optimization approaches by formulating reliable context selection as a submodular Markov Random Field energy minimized exactly via s-t Min-Cut, borrowing the graph-representability framework from classical computer vision (Boykov et al., 2002; Kolmogorov & Zabin, 2004). The key conceptual departure is the extension from static snapshot defense to a dynamic, time-evolving setting, where a Bayesian memory node recursively updates belief states over the previous answer rather than archiving full document history. This temporal dimension, combined with the NLI-based entailment/contradiction scoring (DeBERTa-v3) and eigenvector centrality for global consensus, positions RADAR as the first defense explicitly designed for continuous time-step adversarial attacks on RAG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM-augmented search engines (DeepSeek, ChatGPT, Grok) increasingly rely on live web retrieval, the dynamic attack surface—where adversaries can continuously inject or mutate poisoned documents—is a practical and growing threat that static defenses do not address. RADAR's exact Min-Cut solution provides a provable optimality guarantee absent in heuristic baselines, and the Bayesian memory node achieves storage efficiency (~1 KB per query vs. tens of MBs for full history), making it deployable in production. Open questions include scalability to very large retrieval sets (the O(n³) HLPP complexity), robustness under non-stationary or coordinated multi-attacker strategies, and whether the symmetric likelihood assumption in the Bayesian update holds under sophisticated adaptive attacks that deliberately produce ambiguous NLI signals.
