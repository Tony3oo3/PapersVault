## Problem

Distributed training with infrequent communication (e.g., DiLoCo, DES-LOC) is bottlenecked by the memory and communication cost of full-rank optimizer states, while low-rank optimizers like GaLore and LDAdam have not been adapted to the local-update regime. Naively applying low-rank projections locally introduces high-variance, noisy subspaces due to small per-worker batch sizes, while using a shared global projection permanently traps optimization in a fixed rank-r subspace, causing learning stagnation. The paper asks: how can we design low-rank optimizers for communication-efficient training that avoid both failure modes?

## Input / Output

Each worker receives the current model parameters, a shared global projection matrix Q_t (of size p×r, computed via SVD on the aggregated pseudo-gradient at the previous synchronization boundary), and local data batches from the SmolLM2 mixture. The method outputs updated model parameters and low-rank optimizer states (first and second moments of size r×q) that are synchronized at decoupled intervals K_x, K_u, K_v. A key structural detail is that the full-rank quasi-hyperbolic momentum term requires each worker to retain the full-rank gradient (O(pq)) for the up-projected signal, but the optimizer states themselves remain low-rank, yielding O(pq + pr + 2rq) total per-worker memory versus O(3pq) for full-rank Adam.

## Method

LoRDO unifies low-rank optimization with infrequent synchronization through two key design choices. First, it computes a single global projection matrix Q_t from the aggregated pseudo-gradient (the sum of K local updates across M workers), which provides a higher-quality, lower-variance basis than per-worker projections since its effective batch size is MB rather than B. Second, to prevent the global projection from permanently restricting optimization to a stagnant rank-r subspace, LoRDO injects a full-rank quasi-hyperbolic momentum term into each worker's local update: the gradient signal is up-projected and scaled by the second momentum, so the aggregated pseudo-gradient recovers a full-rank representation (rank M×r ≥ min(p,q) with sufficient workers), enabling temporal subspace exploration. The method additionally applies local error feedback to recover information lost in low-rank compression and rotates optimizer states into the new basis whenever the projection matrix is refreshed. Empirically, LoRDO-Global achieves near-parity with low-rank DDP (perplexity gap <1%) at 125M–720M scale while reducing communication by ~10×, and surpasses DDP by 3.36–4.7% in perplexity under heavy memory constraints that force low ranks.

## Results

At the 720M scale with r=256, LoRDO-Global achieves a perplexity of 10.41 and average downstream task accuracy of 44.7%, matching its low-rank DDP counterpart (perplexity 10.34, avg. 44.8%) with a gap of less than 1% in perplexity. This is achieved while reducing communication by approximately 10× relative to low-rank DDP and 25× relative to full-rank DDP, and reducing optimizer state memory by 8×. Under heavy memory constraints at lower ranks (r=8), LoRDO-Global surpasses DDP by 3.36–4.7% in perplexity, demonstrating superior resilience in small-batch regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the low-rank DDP baseline (GaLore with LDAdam-style rotations at r=256), LoRDO-Global achieves near-parity: perplexity 10.41 vs. 10.34 (<1% gap) and average downstream accuracy 44.7% vs. 44.8%, while reducing communication by ~10× and optimizer memory by 8×. Against the full-rank MT-DAO baseline (r=2048), LoRDO-Global is slightly behind in perplexity (10.41 vs. 9.98) and average task accuracy (44.7% vs. 45.9%), but does so with 8× less optimizer state memory and ~25× less communication. Against DiLoCo at 16M and 125M scales, LoRDO-Global outperforms DiLoCo across all tested ranks (r=32–128 at 16M; r=96–192 at 125M) while maintaining lower local memory overhead, since DiLoCo requires full-rank Adam locally. LoRDO-Global consistently outperforms LoRDO-Local, with the gap most pronounced at lower ranks where the global projection's higher effective batch size (MB vs. B) provides a more stable SVD basis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: low-rank adaptive optimizers (GaLore, LDAdam) that reduce memory via SVD-based gradient projection, and communication-efficient distributed training (DiLoCo, DES-LOC, MT-DAO) that reduces synchronization frequency through local updates. The key departure is identifying a specific failure mode at their intersection: global projections (theoretically superior due to lower noise from aggregated pseudo-gradients) permanently trap optimization in a fixed rank-r subspace, while local projections suffer from high variance at small per-worker batch sizes. The resolution—a full-rank quasi-hyperbolic momentum term injected into the local update—adapts the QHM mechanism from MT-DAO to the low-rank setting in a way that restores subspace exploration without additional communication overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LoRDO provides a principled path to 10× communication reduction with near-parity perplexity (<1% gap) at 125M–720M scale, making distributed pre-training feasible on bandwidth-constrained hardware. The Davis-Kahan-based analysis of projection instability (scaling as O(B^{-0.5})·r^{α+1}) gives practitioners concrete guidance on rank selection under memory constraints. Open questions include: no formal convergence proof for the multi-timescale QHM updates, limited validation beyond 720M parameters, untested Non-IID robustness of local projections, and extension to matrix-based optimizers like Muon. The finding that low-rank methods are disproportionately sensitive to synchronization delay at small ranks has practical implications for system design.
