## Problem

Existing single-cell perturbation prediction methods either represent gene expression in a flat, gene-wise manner or rely on static biological priors (Gene Ontology, fixed PPI graphs) that cannot capture the dynamic, context-dependent reorganization of gene programs. In reality, perturbation responses manifest as coordinated, program-level transcriptional changes among functionally related genes, a structure that is especially critical for generalizing to unseen and combinatorial perturbations but remains unmodeled by current approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263898/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes single-cell gene expression profiles (log-transformed CPM over ~2,000 HVGs plus perturbed genes) and a perturbation condition encoded as an ESM2 protein embedding (for gene perturbations) or ChemBERTa molecular embedding (for drug perturbations). Genes are first reordered into K=32 balanced clusters of L=G/K genes each, so the encoder consumes a structured N×K×L tensor rather than a flat gene vector. The output is a predicted perturbed expression profile generated by integrating a conditional flow-matching ODE in a 50-dimensional latent space and decoding back to gene space.

## Method

scBIG introduces a three-stage module-inductive pipeline. First, Gene-Relation Clustering (GRC) partitions genes into K=32 biologically coherent modules by solving a balanced optimal-transport assignment that jointly minimizes semantic distance (GeneCompass embeddings) and PPI incoherence (STRING, threshold 700), using alternating Sinkhorn updates and capacity-constrained rounding. Second, a Gene-Cluster-Aware Encoder (GCAE) applies a Perceiver-style bottleneck with M=8 learnable inducing points to model high-order inter-module interactions via a compress-process-broadcast attention scheme, producing a cell-level latent embedding that feeds a conditional flow-matching backbone regressing the vector field from control to perturbed states. Third, two structure-aware alignment losses—Cluster Correlation Alignment (Frobenius-norm matching of inter-cluster Pearson correlations) and Pathway-informed Optimal Transport (Sinkhorn distance between predicted and ground-truth Reactome pathway activations)—are jointly optimized with the flow loss to preserve module-level coordination and pathway-level fidelity during generation.

## Results

On the Norman additive split (unseen dual-gene perturbations), scBIG achieves ρΔ = 0.8496, ρΔ^D = 0.8230, DES = 0.8593, PDS = 0.8548, MSE = 0.0091, and MAE = 0.0689, setting new state-of-the-art across all metrics. The strongest generative baseline, CellFlow, scores ρΔ = 0.7892 and DES = 0.8113, yielding a 7.7% relative gap on ρΔ and 5.9% on DES. On the more challenging holdout split (unseen genes), scBIG gains 38.0% over CellFlow on ρΔ^D for single-gene and 20.6% on ρΔ^D for double-gene perturbations. Across all benchmarks, scBIG delivers an average 6.7% improvement over the best-performing baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

scBIG outperforms all 13 baselines on the Norman additive split across every metric. Against the strongest generative competitor CellFlow, scBIG gains 7.7% on ρΔ (0.8496 vs. 0.7892), 13.1% on ρΔ^D (0.8230 vs. 0.7275), 5.9% on DES (0.8593 vs. 0.8113), and 12.7% on PDS (0.8548 vs. 0.7581), with gains confirmed statistically significant via paired Wilcoxon tests (p < 0.05 on all four metrics). Among foundation models, GeneCompass leads on PDS (0.8024) but scBIG still surpasses it by 6.5%; scGPT and scFoundation trail substantially on ρΔ^D (0.44–0.48 vs. 0.82). Graph-based methods (GEARS, CellOracle, GraphVCI) are weakest on DEG-specific metrics—CellOracle scores only 0.2567 on ρΔ^D—confirming that static graph priors fail to capture dynamic program reorganization. On the holdout split (unseen genes), scBIG's advantage widens: it beats CellFlow by 38.0% on ρΔ^D for single-gene and 20.6% on ρΔ^D for double-gene perturbations, while Linear-scGPT achieves competitive MSE but sacrifices biological fidelity (DES = 0.6118 vs. scBIG's 0.8313). On the RPE1 dataset, scBIG extends its lead over CellFlow by 25.7% on ρΔ and 34.9% on ρΔ^D, and on the ComboSciPlex drug benchmark it improves discriminative cosine similarity by 2.7% over CPA and reduces Wasserstein distance by 4.0% over scGPT.

## Relation to Prior Work

scBIG builds directly on the conditional flow-matching paradigm introduced by CellFlow for modeling continuous transitions between control and perturbed latent states, and on Perceiver-style bottleneck attention for efficient set encoding. It departs from flat gene-token representations used by foundation models (scGPT, scFoundation, GeneCompass, CellFM) and from static graph propagation methods (GEARS, CellOracle, GraphVCI) by introducing a data-driven module induction step (Gene-Relation Clustering via Optimal Transport) that fuses foundation-model semantics with high-confidence PPI priors. The structure-aware alignment losses (Cluster Correlation Alignment and Pathway-informed Optimal Transport) are novel regularizers that enforce module-level and pathway-level coherence during generation, a constraint absent in prior generative perturbation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling coordinated gene programs—rather than treating genes as independent tokens—yields meaningful gains particularly in the hardest generalization settings (unseen genes, combinatorial perturbations), where flat representations and static graphs fail most. The finding that a fixed, data-induced module structure outperforms perturbation-adaptive re-clustering suggests that stable functional programs are a more reliable inductive prior than per-perturbation reorganization. Open questions include whether the approach scales to the full ~20k-gene transcriptome without HVG filtering, whether the balanced-cluster constraint (G/K per module) is truly necessary or merely a computational convenience, and whether the module structure transfers across vastly different cell types or disease contexts beyond the two cell lines tested.
