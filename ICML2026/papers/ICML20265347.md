## Problem

LVLMs frequently hallucinate spatial relationships (e.g., saying an object is 'on' another when it is 'under'), yet prior remedies like SFT, CoT prompting, and RL target high-level reasoning rather than the actual failure point. Through linear probing and logit lens analysis, the paper reveals that intermediate decoder layers already encode correct spatial geometry with near-perfect discriminability, but this signal is suppressed by language priors before reaching the output layer—a representation-output misalignment that no existing training-free intervention directly addresses.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image or video clip paired with a text query asking for a spatial relationship (e.g., 'Where is objA in relation to objB?'), and produces a single spatial relationship label from a fixed option set (4 options for left/right/on/under, or 6 options adding front/behind). At inference time, GRASP requires one additional forward pass to compute the geometric counterfactual reference via manifold search, incurring a 1.71× latency overhead over greedy decoding. The framework is agnostic to positional encoding type, supporting 1D APE (LLaVA), 2D RoPE (Qwen2.5-VL), and 3D RoPE (Qwen3-VL) across both image and video modalities.

## Method

GRASP reformulates spatial reasoning as trajectory generation on the geometric manifold constructed by positional encodings, which possess a Lie Group structure (SO(2) for RoPE) or a discretized manifold topology (APE). First, Manifold Differential Search performs zeroth-order optimization via parallel tangent-space sampling to locate a geometric counterfactual reference that maximizes the top-1/top-2 probability margin, thereby inducing maximal semantic conflict with the language prior. Second, Implicit Trajectory Correction computes the normalized difference between self-attention outputs of the original and reference inputs in empirically identified intermediate layers, injecting this pure geometric signal to rectify attenuated spatial features before deep-layer suppression occurs. Third, Explicit Distribution Alignment uses the Jensen-Shannon Divergence between original and reference logits as an adaptive gate (e^{-d}) to dynamically amplify the geometric correction in the output distribution, breaking language-prior dominance. The entire pipeline is training-free, requiring no parameter updates, and the geometric constraint of traversing the positional manifold preserves object identity while enabling controlled spatial reconfiguration.

## Results

On the WhatsUp benchmark, GRASP achieves a peak accuracy gain of 26.1% on LLaVA-1.5 (Controlled_A: 60.3% → 86.4%) and raises Set Accuracy from 0.0% to 32.7%, while on the stronger Qwen2.5-VL backbone it boosts Controlled_A accuracy by 7.6% to 97.6%. On the VSI-Bench video Relative Direction task, GRASP yields gains of up to 7.9% (LLaVA-NeXT-Video) and 9.7% (Qwen3-VL) on the Hard subset. Inference overhead is 1.71× latency relative to greedy decoding, compared to 2.04× for VCD and 2.09× for AdaptVis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRASP consistently outperforms all three baselines across every WhatsUp subset for both LLaVA-1.5 and Qwen2.5-VL. DoLa and VCD, which target general language uncertainty rather than geometric misalignment, yield only marginal gains (≤2.1% on LLaVA, ≤2.1% on Qwen) and sometimes degrade performance (e.g., VCD drops Qwen2.5-VL VG_two from 70.4 to 69.1). AdaptVis is the strongest baseline, reaching 84.9% on LLaVA Controlled_A, but GRASP still surpasses it by 1.5% in accuracy and 2.4% in Set Accuracy; on realistic COCO/VG subsets the gap widens to 8.4–16.7%. On Qwen2.5-VL, AdaptVis is essentially ineffective (90.0% → 90.0% on Controlled_A), whereas GRASP achieves a 7.6% gain to 97.6%. For video (VSI-Bench), no existing training-free baselines are available, so GRASP is benchmarked against vanilla models only, with the full framework outperforming its individual ITC and EDA components on all difficulty levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability and inference-time intervention for LVLMs. While prior spatial reasoning work relies on data-intensive SFT or RL-based CoT, and existing inference-time methods (DoLa, VCD, AdaptVis) target language priors or attention distributions without exploiting geometric structure, GRASP reframes the problem through the Lie Group manifold structure inherent in positional encodings (RoPE/APE). It builds on recent mechanistic findings that positional encodings are decisive for spatial understanding, but departs by actively traversing the coordinate manifold via zeroth-order optimization to generate geometric counterfactuals, rather than passively analyzing or adjusting encodings. The dual-level rectification (implicit trajectory correction + explicit distribution alignment) is a novel synthesis that no prior inference-time method attempts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The representation-output misalignment finding—that LVLMs internally encode correct spatial geometry with near-perfect discriminability but suppress it via language priors in deep layers—reframes spatial hallucination as an expressiveness failure rather than a perception failure, with implications beyond spatial reasoning for any task where visual evidence competes with linguistic priors. The training-free, architecture-agnostic approach (working across 1D APE, 2D/3D RoPE, image and video) offers a practical path to improving deployed LVLMs without retraining. Key limitations include reliance on manual hyperparameter tuning (α, intervention layers) per architecture and the restriction to affine (rotation) manifold parametrization, leaving non-rigid deformations and complex 3D occlusions unaddressed. Open questions include whether the geometric manifold paradigm extends to other perception tasks (depth, scale, occlusion) and whether the optimal counterfactual search can be made fully adaptive without validation-set tuning.
