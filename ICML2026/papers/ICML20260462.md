## Problem

Mechanistic interpretability has demonstrated that transformers trained on Othello learn linearly decodable board-state representations, but these studies examine a single game with a single rule set. It remains unclear how a single transformer organizes multiple, potentially conflicting world models within a shared representation space when trained on heterogeneous data governed by different generative processes.

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of discrete move tokens from a 66-token vocabulary (64 board positions plus pass and pad) with a 59-token context window, and predicts the next move via a standard autoregressive objective. For interpretability, linear probes (512→3 classifiers per tile, 64 independent classifiers) are trained on the residual stream to decode board states, and separate game-identity probes extract per-game posterior probabilities; steering vectors derived from probe weight differences are injected at specific layers to causally test whether representations are functionally interchangeable across games.

## Method

The paper introduces MetaOthello, a framework defining Othello variants that share an 8×8 board and token vocabulary but differ in update rules (NoMidFlip), validation and update rules (DelFlank), or token-to-move mapping (Iago), enabling controlled study of multi-world-model organization. Small 8-layer GPTs (d_model=512, 8 heads) are trained self-supervised on pure (20M sequences) and 50/50 mixed (40M sequences) datasets. The analysis combines per-layer Procrustes alignment of probe weights to test geometric equivalence across games, cross-probe causal interventions to verify functional interchangeability, and game-identity steering to identify the disambiguation circuit. The key finding is that board-state representations are shared across variants (equivalent up to a single orthogonal rotation for isomorphic games), while rule conflicts are resolved by a localized three-stage circuit (layer-4 MLP → layer-5 attention head 5 → layer-5 MLP) that routes ambiguous prefixes into game-specific computation, with the arbitration regime shifting from mid-layer subspace reweighting to early-layer commitment as the rule systems diverge.

## Results

All models achieve near-optimal next-move prediction with α > 0.98 across all games and mixtures (Table 1), with mixed-game models showing only a ~0.5% dip relative to pure-game models. Cross-probe interventions (using one game's probe to steer another game's board state) are nearly as effective as matched correct-probe interventions, confirming causal interchangeability of board representations. For syntax-scrambled Iago, raw probe cosine similarity is at random baseline (≈0.03), but per-layer Procrustes alignment recovers 0.98 similarity (vs. 0.68 random baseline), and a single global orthogonal rotation Ω applied at any layer 1–7 recovers near-perfect Iago prediction (α ≈ 1) from Classic activations. Representational dissimilarity between Classic and NoMidFlip tiles correlates with per-tile conflict probability at R² = 0.95 (layer 8), and the identified three-stage disambiguation circuit (layer-4 MLP → layer-5 head 5 → layer-5 MLP) causally shifts branch selection from 0.48 to 0.68/0.01 on held-out ambiguous prefixes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability study without external method baselines; the key comparisons are internal. Mixed-game models retain near-pure performance (e.g., Classic–NoMidFlip drops from 0.995 to 0.991 on Classic, 0.988 to 0.983 on NoMidFlip), showing that learning two rule systems costs only ~0.5% α. Cross-probe interventions match correct-probe interventions in causal efficacy (Figure 3), even when raw representational similarity is moderate (Classic–DelFlank raw cosine ≈ 0.67), demonstrating that geometric alignment is not required for functional interchangeability. For Iago, Procrustes-aligned similarity (0.98) far exceeds the random-alignment baseline (0.68), while raw similarity (0.03) is indistinguishable from random, confirming the shared structure is rotationally encoded rather than absent. The paper contrasts two arbitration regimes: NoMidFlip (jointly plausible rules) uses a mid-layer (layer 5) routing circuit with Δα ≈ 0.75 steering effect, while DelFlank (effectively out-of-distribution) commits early, with layer-2/3 steering yielding Δα ≈ 0.75 but layer-5 steering having no effect (Δα ≈ 0), and the suppressed DelFlank board representation degrading to 67% probe accuracy vs. 99% for Classic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Othello-GPT line of work (Li et al., 2024; Nanda et al., 2023), which established that transformers trained on a single game develop linearly decodable, causally steerable board-state representations, to the multi-game setting. Where prior work studied one rule system in isolation and multilingual Othello (Hua et al., 2024) varied vocabulary while holding rules fixed, MetaOthello deliberately varies the underlying physics (update and validation rules) or tokenization while sharing board syntax, creating genuine latent-state conflicts that neither world-model nor multi-task representation research had previously examined. The probe-and-steer methodology, Procrustes alignment, and circuit-localization techniques are inherited from the single-game literature and applied here to a new controlled testbed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that transformers share a single board-state substrate across rule variants and localize conflict to a compact, steerable mid-layer circuit (layer-4 MLP → layer-5 head 5 → layer-5 MLP) offers a concrete mechanistic account of how heterogeneous knowledge might be organized in larger models, and the two-regime arbitration (mid-layer reweighting vs. early commitment) suggests the strategy shifts with the degree of rule divergence. The syntax-invariance result—that a single orthogonal rotation maps Classic to Iago representations across all layers—provides strong evidence for abstract, token-independent structure. Key limitations include the toy setting (8-layer, d=512, single seed, 50/50 pairwise mixtures, linear probes only), leaving open whether these findings persist at scale, with imbalanced or many-way mixtures, or under nonlinear representations.
