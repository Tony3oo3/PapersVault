## Problem

Existing de novo peptide sequencing methods—whether autoregressive or one-shot non-autoregressive—fail to intrinsically enforce the physical mass conservation law (MS1 precursor mass) during generation, frequently producing chemically invalid sequences that require inefficient post-hoc filtering. AR decoding imposes a left-to-right causal order that conflicts with the bidirectional, globally constrained nature of MS/MS fragmentation evidence, while NAR one-shot generation lacks iterative self-correction. The paper addresses the absence of a generative paradigm that simultaneously enforces hard physical constraints and enables global iterative reasoning over the full spectral context.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a top-150-peak MS2 fragment spectrum (each peak as an m/z–intensity pair) and the MS1 precursor m/z with charge state. The precursor information is used to compute a target residue mass (Eq. 1) and to predict the peptide length via a lightweight MLP before diffusion begins. The output is a fixed-length amino acid sequence (7–40 residues) drawn from a 23-token vocabulary (20 canonical residues plus oxidation of Met and deamidation of Asn/Gln), with Leucine and Isoleucine treated as equivalent due to isomeric mass.

## Method

PhysNovo reframes sequencing as a masked discrete diffusion process on the amino acid vocabulary, using a 12-layer bidirectional Transformer (ESM-2 style) backbone trained in three stages: unconditional pretraining on in silico digested Swiss-Prot sequences, MS/MS-constrained fine-tuning with cross-attention to sinusoidal-encoded spectral peaks and precursor-mass conditioning, and inference as iterative denoising over T=100 steps. The core innovation is a knapsack-based feasibility kernel embedded directly in the diffusion transition: at each denoising step, candidate residues whose mass would make the remaining budget unreachable by the remaining masked positions are pruned (logit set to −∞), reducing the search space to the manifold of mass-valid sequences at O(1) cost per token. A global error-correction mechanism forcibly remasks residues whose predicted confidence falls below a threshold (τ=0.7), enabling abductive re-reasoning over bidirectional spectral context. Soft MS2 alignment is provided via cross-attention to peak embeddings, while hard MS1 compliance is guaranteed by the knapsack kernel, and the two constraints are shown to be synergistic in ablation.

## Results

PhysNovo achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmarks. On Nine-species (high-resolution), it reaches 0.815 amino acid precision and 0.588 peptide precision (AUC 0.553), surpassing the previous best LIPNovo (0.797 AA prec., 0.582 peptide prec.) by 1.8% and 0.6% respectively. On the challenging low-resolution Seven-species dataset, PhysNovo achieves 0.346 peptide precision, exceeding ReNovo (0.278) by 6.8% absolute. On HC-PT (human PTM-focused), it attains 0.649 AA precision and 0.473 peptide precision, with the highest PTM recall (0.770) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysNovo outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric across all three datasets. Against the previous state-of-the-art LIPNovo (Du et al., 2025), PhysNovo gains +1.8% AA precision on Nine-species (0.815 vs. 0.797), +1.9% peptide precision on Seven-species (0.346 vs. 0.327), and +1.5% peptide precision on HC-PT (0.473 vs. 0.458), while using 5.3M fewer parameters (63.1M vs. 68.4M). Against the NAR baseline π-PrimeNovo (Zhang et al., 2025), PhysNovo achieves +2.5% AA precision on Nine-species (0.815 vs. 0.790), validating iterative diffusion over one-shot generation. The largest absolute gain is on the low-resolution Seven-species dataset, where PhysNovo exceeds ReNovo (Chen et al., 2025) by 6.8% in peptide precision (0.346 vs. 0.278) and 6.2% in peptide AUC (0.290 vs. 0.228). In inference speed, PhysNovo at T=100 steps runs at 46 spectra/s—4× faster than the AR baseline CasaNovo (11 spectra/s) but slower than the one-shot NAR π-PrimeNovo (105 spectra/s); reducing to T=50 steps yields 99 spectra/s with only a 1.4% precision drop (0.801 vs. 0.815). No baseline is matched or beaten by PhysNovo on any metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysNovo builds on the discrete diffusion formulation of DPLM (Wang et al., 2024) for masked sequence generation, but departs fundamentally from the dominant autoregressive (AR) paradigm in de novo peptide sequencing (DeepNovo, PointNovo, CasaNovo, LIPNovo) and the recent non-autoregressive (NAR) one-shot approach (π-PrimeNovo). Its key conceptual departure is embedding a knapsack-based combinatorial feasibility kernel directly into the diffusion transition distribution, enforcing exact precursor mass conservation at every denoising step rather than treating MS1 as a post-hoc filter. The ESM-2-style bidirectional Transformer backbone and Sinusoidal PeakEncoder for spectral conditioning follow established patterns in protein language modeling and mass spectrometry deep learning, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making physical mass conservation an intrinsic generative constraint rather than a post-processing step, PhysNovo addresses a practical reliability gap in de novo sequencing where AR and NAR models frequently emit chemically invalid peptides. The ablation showing the hard mass constraint alone outperforms soft spectral conditioning validates that mass conservation is the fundamental boundary of the solution space, a framing that could inform constrained generation in other scientific domains. Open questions include scalability to open-search proteomics with large PTM vocabularies, resolution of isomeric ambiguities beyond what MS2 evidence provides, and whether the iterative refinement overhead is justified in ultra-high-throughput settings where the T=50 speed-accuracy trade-off may suffice.
