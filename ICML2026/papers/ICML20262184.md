## Problem

Optimal transport-based domain adaptation bounds contain an incomputable entanglement term that depends on target labels, and even the computable Wasserstein Regularized Risk (WRR) objective is non-convex and difficult to optimize in practice. Moreover, standard domain alignment tries to match source and target distributions exactly, unnecessarily constraining model capacity when the two share significant support. The paper asks: under what assumptions does minimizing the computable part of the bound guarantee low target risk, and how can the alignment be relaxed to make optimization tractable?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262184/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source images and unlabeled target images (grayscale digits for MNIST/USPS/MNIST-M, RGB for SVHN) and produces class label predictions. Unusually, the OT alignment is performed in output space using predicted probability vectors f(x) ∈ Δ(Y) rather than in feature/embedding space, with a margin multi-classification loss (Crammer-Singer) used to encourage clear class-conditional separation. The cost matrix entries are ℓ(f(x_i), f(x'_j)), i.e., distances between predicted distributions, and the UOT solver operates on this m×n cost matrix at each training step.

## Method

The paper introduces the Weighted Wasserstein Regularized Risk (W²R²), which adds importance weights w(x) to the source distribution in the WRR objective, tightening the target risk bound by finding the best weighted source to align against. The key structural insight is that the inner optimization over w reduces to a semi-relaxed OT problem with a single marginal constraint, whose unregularized solution collapses to a nearest-neighbor alignment (high variance). To avoid this collapse, the authors regularize via unbalanced OT (UOT) with asymmetric KL penalties (τ₁=1 for source, τ₂=100 for target), interpolating between the fully relaxed semi-relaxed case and balanced OT. Theoretically, they prove that under Gradual Shift (GS) and Probabilistic Margin (PM) assumptions, the target risk is bounded by a scalar multiple of W²R², grounding the choice of the regularization strength λ. In practice, they use the mm-UOT solver from the POT library with a margin loss and 5-epoch pretraining, achieving state-of-the-art results across multiple digit-domain shift scenarios without per-experiment hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

On the MNIST→USPS task with an MLP, W²R² achieves 86.0% target accuracy, substantially outperforming DANN (55.0%), rev-KL (62.0%), and WRR (77.0%). With a ResNet on USPS→MNIST, W²R² reaches 93.0% versus 74.0% for DANN and 90.0% for WRR. However, W²R² underperforms on MNIST→MNIST-M (49.0% with ConvNet vs. 61.0% for WRR and 68.0% for DANN) and SVHN→MNIST (73.0% vs. 78.0% for WRR with ResNet), indicating that the method's advantage is most pronounced in scenarios with moderate distribution shift and lower-capacity models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

W²R² consistently outperforms DANN (Ganin et al., 2016) across nearly all scenario/model combinations, with the largest gaps on MLP models (e.g., +31 points on MNIST→USPS, +20 on USPS→MNIST) and ResNet on USPS→MNIST (+19 points). Against rev-KL (Nguyen et al., 2022), W²R² wins in 7 out of 9 evaluated settings, with particularly strong margins on ResNet models (e.g., +26 points on USPS→MNIST). Compared to the unweighted WRR baseline (Koç et al., 2025), W²R² improves in 6 of 9 cases, most notably on MLP and ResNet models, but loses on MNIST→MNIST-M with ConvNet (49% vs. 61%) and SVHN→MNIST with ResNet (73% vs. 78%), suggesting that the importance weighting relaxation can be counterproductive when the source and target distributions are already well-aligned or when the shift is primarily in input space (MNIST-M) rather than in the learned feature space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OT-based domain adaptation framework of Koç et al. (2025), which introduced the Wasserstein Regularized Risk (WRR) objective and entanglement-based upper bounds, by incorporating importance weighting to tighten the bound and ease optimization. The resulting weighted objective (W²R²) is shown to be equivalent to a semi-relaxed OT problem, and its regularized variant maps naturally to unbalanced OT (UOT), building on the UOT-as-heuristic perspective of Fatras et al. (2021). The theoretical analysis introduces Gradual Shift (GS) and Probabilistic Margin (PM) assumptions that generalize and relax the connectedness assumption of Wu et al. (2019), providing conditions under which minimizing the computable WRR term provably controls target risk without needing target labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for why naive WRR optimization can fail (non-convexity even for linear models, nearest-neighbor collapse in the semi-relaxed limit) and offers a practical UOT-based remedy that achieves state-of-the-art results on standard digit benchmarks without per-scenario hyperparameter tuning. However, the method requires pretraining, a margin loss, and a specific solver (mm-UOT), and the GS/PM assumptions, while empirically supported, are not verifiable in practice. A key open question the authors identify is how to estimate GS shift parameters online during optimization to adapt the regularization strength λ dynamically, which would make the approach more robust to spurious features and poorly chosen hyperparameters.
