## Problem

Large-scale PDE simulations (CFD, numerical relativity, plasma physics) generate petabyte-to-exabyte trajectories that exceed HPC storage capacity, yet existing in-situ compression methods either use physics-agnostic temporal sampling that misses fast transients or oversamples quiescent phases, or compress each snapshot independently without exploiting the small perturbative changes between successive time steps. No prior framework jointly addresses adaptive, physics-informed temporal selection and temporally-aware spatial compression in a single streaming pass.

## Input / Output

ANTIC consumes a streaming sequence of PDE solution snapshots (e.g., 2048×2048 vorticity fields at 17 MiB each for 2D Kolmogorov, or 213³×18 BSSN tensor fields at 0.7 GiB each for 3D BBH mergers) along with a per-snapshot physics metric (enstrophy flux or Weyl scalar Ψ₄ magnitude) and the previously stored neural field weights. It outputs a sparse set of selected snapshot indices and, for each retained snapshot, a compact low-rank weight update ΔW = BA (rank r ≪ hidden dimension) appended to persistent storage, rather than the full snapshot. The base NF is a 256×6 MLP (~0.4M parameters), and LoRA adapters add only r×(n+k) trainable parameters per update.

## Method

ANTIC combines two modules operating in a single in-situ streaming pass. The Physics-aware Temporal Selector (PATS) is a non-parametric pipeline of four components—Metric (computes a PDE-specific scalar invariant per snapshot), Regulator (dynamically truncates a sliding context window upon phase transitions), Queue (stores metric history), and Gate (applies a dual-constraint threshold coupling physical divergence with structural cross-correlation)—that decides whether to compress the current snapshot or skip it. The Spatial Neural Compression module exploits the observation that successive PDE states differ by smooth, low-magnitude perturbations Δu(t), so instead of training a neural field from scratch, it initializes at the previous snapshot's weights and performs continual fine-tuning (CFT) to encode only the residual. By reparameterizing the weight update via LoRA (ΔW = AB with rank r), ANTIC exposes a rate-distortion Pareto front: lower ranks yield higher compression (up to 3744× per snapshot for 3D BSSN) at modest accuracy cost, while full fine-tuning minimizes distortion at the expense of storing all ~0.4M parameters. CFT converges in ~3 epochs versus ~35 for cold-start training, a >10× speedup that makes in-situ deployment practical.

## Results

On 2D Kolmogorov flows (16.8 GiB trajectory), ANTIC-LoRA achieves a total spatiotemporal compression of 435× (37% temporal retention × 47× spatial compression at rank 32), while ANTIC-FT reaches 111×. On the 3D BSSN binary black hole merger (4.2 TiB, 5,966 steps), ANTIC-LoRA achieves 6,807× total compression (55% temporal retention × 3,744× spatial compression at rank 16), and ANTIC-FT reaches 860×. Reconstruction fidelity is maintained at relative ℓ₂ ≈ 10⁻³ for 2D turbulence and 10⁻⁴–10⁻⁵ for 3D BSSN variables. CFT reduces per-snapshot training time by >10× over cold-start (from ~183 s to ~21 s on H200, ~52 s to ~4.5 s on B300).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ZFP (Lindstrom 2014), ANTIC-LoRA achieves 4× greater spatial compression on 2D Kolmogorov flows (47× vs 13×) and ~139× greater on 3D BSSN (3,744× vs 27×), while the physics-aware temporal selector yields 3–4× higher total compression than dense ZFP sampling. Against SZ3 (Liang 2021), the comparison is mixed: SZ3 achieves 154× spatial compression on 2D Kolmogorov vorticity (vs ANTIC's 47×), but on the high-dimensional 3D BSSN tensor fields ANTIC-LoRA achieves 3,745× versus SZ3's 16× — a two-order-of-magnitude advantage at comparable relative ℓ₂ (~10⁻⁴). Against MGARD (Gong 2023), ANTIC-LoRA outperforms by 750× on 3D BSSN (3,745× vs 5×) and 2.5× on 2D (47× vs 19×). Against Blosc-2 (lossless, 1.3× on 3D BSSN), ANTIC-LoRA exceeds it by over three orders of magnitude. The key differentiator is that all classical compressors operate per-snapshot without exploiting temporal continuity, whereas ANTIC's CFT-based neural fields encode only residual updates, enabling extreme compression on high-resolution volumetric data while preserving queryable, differentiable field representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

ANTIC sits at the intersection of three research threads: neural field (NF) compression of scientific data (Takikawa et al., 2023; Huang & Hoefler, 2023; Cranganore et al., 2025), parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA (Hu et al., 2021; Truong et al., 2025), and in-situ data reduction for PDE simulations (ISABELA, Glaws et al., 2020; Balin et al., 2023). Prior in-situ methods either perform fixed-rate spatial compression without temporal adaptivity or use physics-agnostic keyframe heuristics borrowed from visualization/video coding. ANTIC departs by coupling a PDE-specific physics metric (enstrophy, Weyl scalar) into a non-parametric temporal gate and by encoding inter-snapshot residuals via continual LoRA fine-tuning of a coordinate-based MLP, thereby unifying both compression axes in a single streaming pass rather than treating them independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work directly addresses the petabyte-to-exabyte storage wall that is becoming a binding constraint for exascale PDE simulations, and the demonstrated two-order-of-magnitude compression advantage of NF-based methods over state-of-the-art error-bounded classical compressors (3744× vs 16× for 3D BSSN tensor data) suggests a paradigm shift for how high-dimensional simulation data is archived. Key open questions remain: per-snapshot training latency is still an order of magnitude above solver wall-clock time, standard MLPs lack inductive bias for high-order derivatives critical to downstream physics analysis, the temporal selector is necessarily PDE-specific (no generalizable metric yet), and off-grid generalization of the NF representation is unvalidated. The authors outline reinforcement-learning-based temporal selection and HPC-scale deployment as natural next steps.
