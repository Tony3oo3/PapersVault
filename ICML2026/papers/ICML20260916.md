## Problem

Explicit Dynamic Gaussian Splatting methods (e.g., 4DGS, Ex4DGS) suffer from pronounced generalization degradation in large-motion scenes, where test-set reconstruction quality deteriorates substantially in later training stages. The paper identifies two root causes: non-uniform gradient scaling in adaptive optimizers that disproportionately amplifies small-gradient primitives due to structured disparities in Gaussian primitive gradients, and frame-preference bias induced by frame-by-frame optimization under motion disturbances.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in optimization strategy on top of existing explicit Dynamic GS pipelines (4DGS and Ex4DGS), consuming multi-view video sequences (N3DV: 18–21 cameras at 2704×2028 for 300 frames; Technicolor: 4×4 array at 2048×1088) along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics and per-frame timestamps. It produces a set of optimized time-varying Gaussian primitive attributes (position, covariance, opacity, color) that are rendered via α-blending to synthesize photorealistic novel views from held-out viewpoints. Evaluation is conducted at half resolution for N3DV and full resolution for Technicolor, with the center camera held out as the novel view.

## Method

The proposed update-mixture strategy comprises three components. First, a Strictly Sparse Update (SSU) restricts optimizer updates to primitives that are both spatially visible (via frustum culling) and temporally active, combined with opacity regularization and ReState Regularization to counteract anti-regularization effects of adaptive memory. Second, a Mix-Adaptive Update Step introduces a type-specific constant ε_e (estimated from early-training second-moment magnitudes) into the adaptive optimizer, so that when ε_e ≫ √v̂ the update degenerates to a non-adaptive SGD-like step, preventing over-scaling of small-gradient background primitives and yielding a stable mixture of adaptive and non-adaptive updates. Third, Stochastic Attribute Averaging (SAA) saves attribute snapshots every 500 iterations and averages the last five, mitigating frame-preference bias analogous to Stochastic Weight Averaging in DNNs. The strategy is validated on both 4DGS (Adam-based) and Ex4DGS (RAdam-based) pipelines, with ε_e tuned per primitive type and scene motion magnitude.

## Results

On the N3DV dataset (6 scenes, half resolution), the proposed update-mixture strategy improves 4DGS PSNR from 30.202 to 32.469 (mixed batch size) or 32.174 (batch size 1), with SSIM rising from 0.943 to 0.949/0.947 and LPIPS(vgg) dropping from 0.140 to 0.138/0.145. On the Technicolor dataset (4 scenes, full resolution), the same strategy applied to Ex4DGS raises PSNR from 31.809 to 32.281, SSIM from 0.901 to 0.907, and reduces LPIPS(Alex) from 0.108 to 0.101. Per-scene ablations on N3DV show the largest gains on large-motion scenes: Flame Steak PSNR increases from 28.358 to 33.951 (4DGS) and 33.138 to 34.032 (Ex4DGS), while Sear Steak improves from 30.329 to 33.819 (4DGS) and 33.037 to 34.212 (Ex4DGS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two explicit Dynamic GS baselines: 4DGS (Yang et al., ICLR 2024) and Ex4DGS (Lee et al., NeurIPS 2024). Against 4DGS on N3DV, the proposed method wins on all six scenes, with the most dramatic improvement on large-motion scenes—Flame Steak gains +5.59 dB PSNR (28.358→33.951) and Sear Steak gains +3.49 dB (30.329→33.819)—while smaller-motion scenes like Coffee Martini gain a more modest +0.85 dB (28.279→29.125). Against Ex4DGS, the method also consistently outperforms the baseline: on Technicolor the full method (with SAA) achieves 32.281 PSNR versus 31.809 for vanilla Ex4DGS (+0.47 dB), and on N3DV per-scene results show gains of +1.42 dB on Cut Roasted Beef (32.310→33.727) and +1.18 dB on Sear Steak (33.037→34.212). The paper notes that simply transferring the 4DGS ε setting to Ex4DGS can cause degradation (e.g., PSNR drops to 30.57 on Flame Steak), underscoring that the method's effectiveness depends on method-specific ε calibration rather than being a universal drop-in fix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of DNN optimization theory and Gaussian Splatting practice, drawing on the well-documented generalization gap between adaptive optimizers (Adam/RAdam) and SGD in deep learning. It extends the sparse-update idea from Taming-3DGS into a space-time dependent Strictly Sparse Update, adapts the Stochastic Weight Averaging concept into an iteration-based Stochastic Attribute Averaging for Dynamic GS, and introduces a constant-corrected epsilon that converts the adaptive update into a stable mixture of adaptive and non-adaptive steps. The key departure from prior DNN optimization work is the recognition that Gaussian primitives exhibit a structured gradient-scale disparity (driven by transmittance, opacity, and temporal accessibility) that makes the non-uniform scaling of adaptive optimizers particularly harmful in dynamic scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete, reproducible failure mode—generalization degradation in large-motion Dynamic GS scenes—that had been observed anecdotally in the community but not systematically analyzed. The finding that non-adaptive updates reshape the primitive distribution (reducing primitive count for 4DGS while increasing it for Ex4DGS) and that batch sampling can exacerbate rather than mitigate generalization issues offers practical guidance for practitioners. However, the method's effectiveness is contingent on a well-calibrated learning rate, it does not fully resolve issues under batch sampling, and it remains limited by the lack of strong geometric priors for extremely large motions, leaving open the question of whether a more principled, pipeline-agnostic optimization framework for Dynamic GS is possible.
