## Problem

Under high missingness and view-availability imbalance in incomplete multi-view clustering, scarce co-observed cross-view pairs make alignment fragile and biased toward frequently observed views, while early overconfident pseudo-labels propagate through imputation and alignment, amplifying errors. Existing imputation-first pipelines accumulate reconstruction errors, and purely consistency-based methods degrade sharply when paired samples are limited, leaving a gap in stabilizing cluster semantics before conservative completion.

## Input / Output

MAGIC takes V incomplete multi-view feature matrices (each with N instances and view-specific dimensions, e.g., 342/1024/64 for FMNIST) together with a binary visibility matrix G ∈ {0,1}^{N×V} encoding which view-instance pairs are observed, and produces a single cluster label per instance. Every sample is guaranteed at least one visible view; missingness is simulated at ratios η ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7} with a shared mask across all compared methods. At inference, the final label is obtained by confidence-weighted aggregation of view-wise consensus posteriors rather than a fixed single-view or raw fused posterior.

## Method

MAGIC combines two stages. First, multi-path contrastive consensus learning builds three correlated representation paths (fused, per-view, masked-fusion) from two stochastic augmentations of view-specific autoencoder latents, then enforces agreement via InfoNCE-based contrastive losses across paths and a symmetric-KL prediction-consistency regularizer; path posteriors are aggregated into calibrated view-wise soft assignments through a temperature-scaled log-opinion pool. Second, confidence-gated semantic imputation selects the most compatible source view by label-space cosine similarity on co-observed samples, computes an entropy-regularized optimal-transport (Sinkhorn) coupling between class prototypes to define a class-to-class transfer map, and applies this map only when the source posterior's normalized entropy confidence exceeds a threshold; missing-view latents are then completed by assigning the predicted cluster's online-computed prototype. The overall objective jointly optimizes masked reconstruction, the multi-path consensus loss, and a similarity-weighted cross-view alignment regularizer, with imputation activated after a warm-up schedule.

## Results

MAGIC achieves the highest ACC, NMI, and ARI across all four benchmarks (FMNIST, Fashion, BDGP, MNIST-USPS) at every missing ratio η ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7}. At the most challenging setting (η = 0.7), MAGIC reaches 96.14% ACC on FMNIST, 87.59% ACC on Fashion, 90.79% ACC on BDGP, and 87.88% ACC on MNIST-USPS, consistently outperforming the strongest baselines (PMIMC and ROLL) by 0.5–1.1 ACC points. The advantage grows with missingness: at η = 0.1 the gap over the best baseline is typically <0.5%, while at η = 0.7 it widens to 1–1.5% on ACC and up to ~1.5% on ARI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGIC outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset and missing ratio. The closest competitors are PMIMC (TIP'25) and ROLL (CVPR'25): at η = 0.7, MAGIC leads PMIMC by 0.61 ACC on FMNIST (96.14 vs 95.53), 1.15 ACC on BDGP (90.79 vs 89.64), and 1.08 ACC on MNIST-USPS (87.88 vs 86.80), while on Fashion it edges out ROLL by 0.56 ACC (87.59 vs 87.03). Older completion-based methods (COMPLETER, DSIMVC, APADC) degrade sharply at high missingness, losing 10–20 ACC points relative to MAGIC at η = 0.7. Prototype- and contrastive-based methods (ProImp, DIVIDE, GIMVC) show intermediate performance but still trail MAGIC by 3–13 ACC points at η = 0.7. The gap between MAGIC and the best baseline widens monotonically with η, confirming that the multi-path consensus and confidence-gated imputation mechanisms are most beneficial under severe missingness.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGIC sits at the intersection of two established IMVC lines: completion-based methods (low-rank recovery, explicit imputation) and consistency-based alignment (prototype contrastive learning, knowledge distillation). Rather than extending either line directly, it repurposes InfoNCE contrastive learning across three correlated representation paths (fused, per-view, masked-fusion) to stabilize posteriors before any imputation occurs, and then applies entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn) at the class level for conservative semantic transfer gated by normalized-entropy confidence. The multi-path consensus design and the separation of posterior calibration from missing-view completion are the paper's conceptual departures from prior single-path or imputation-first pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling semantic stabilization from imputation, MAGIC offers a principled recipe for the high-missingness regime where most existing methods degrade sharply, and the confidence-gated OT transfer provides a theoretically grounded (non-expansive in KL) mechanism to limit error propagation. However, the method introduces many hyperparameters (path weights, aggregation temperature, gating schedule, OT entropy weight), and the class-level OT transfer is limited to K×K couplings, which may be insufficient for fine-grained or high-K settings. Open questions include whether the approach extends to partially paired or streaming settings, how it behaves under distribution shift across views, and whether the multi-path overhead is justified at larger scale.
