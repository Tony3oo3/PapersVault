## Problem

Training LLM agents for long-horizon interactive tasks is hampered by sparse, episodic rewards that make credit assignment difficult, and existing process-reward approaches suffer from distribution shift: as the policy evolves, it generates state-action pairs outside the regime where the reward model was calibrated, undermining supervision reliability. Prior methods also rely on expensive manual annotation, environment backtracking, or extensive online interaction, limiting practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265783/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a hybrid offline dataset combining expert demonstration trajectories and self-collected agent rollouts, where each trajectory consists of a task description, a sequence of natural-language observations and actions, and sparse episodic rewards. Retrospective reward labeling parses textual feedback in observations to assign per-step auxiliary penalties (format errors, invalid actions, repeated observations) without environment access. The output is an evolved LLM policy that produces natural-language actions (Thought + Action format), with the critic parameterized as LoRA adapters on a shared LLM backbone predicting scalar V and Double-Q values via last-token pooling at precomputed prefix positions.

## Method

Q-Evolve unifies automatic process-reward labeling and in-distribution policy learning in a closed self-evolving loop. In each iteration, a weighted Implicit Q-Learning critic (with Double-Q and expectile regression) is trained on the hybrid dataset, where step weights upweight successful trajectories and later timesteps to stabilize Bellman backups under extreme reward sparsity. Step-wise process rewards are then derived via GAE computed exclusively on the episodic environment reward (excluding auxiliary penalties to preserve optimal-policy invariance), yielding dense advantage signals without backtracking or human annotation. The policy is updated via a behavior-proximal objective with asymmetric clipping (ε_low > ε_high) that permits aggressive suppression of negative-advantage actions while tightly constraining probability increases, preventing overfitting to the offline data. The loop closes by collecting new trajectories with the evolved policy, merging them with expert data, and re-running the inner loop, so that policy, critic, and dataset co-evolve while each update remains grounded within the in-distribution data of the current iteration.

## Results

Q-Evolve achieves the best average score of 79.4 across WebShop, ScienceWorld, and ALFWorld, outperforming the strongest baseline QLASS (74.5) by 4.9 points. The largest gains appear on ALFWorld, where Q-Evolve reaches 90.7 (seen) and 89.6 (unseen) versus QLASS's 77.9 and 82.8, respectively. In a sample-efficiency comparison on ALFWorld (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), Q-Evolve (1-iter) uses only 13K environment steps yet outperforms all online RL baselines trained for 320K steps, e.g., achieving 88.6/87.3 (seen/unseen) versus SFT+PPO's 72.6/77.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-Evolve outperforms all 12 baselines on the primary benchmarks. Against the closest competitor QLASS (which also uses Q-value-based process rewards), Q-Evolve gains +0.2 on WebShop, +1.0/+3.3 on SciWorld seen/unseen, and a substantial +12.8/+6.8 on ALFWorld seen/unseen, while requiring far fewer environment interactions (20K vs 600K steps on ALFWorld). Compared to ETO (trajectory-level preference optimization), Q-Evolve improves the average by 10.0 points (79.4 vs 69.4), with the largest gap on ALFWorld unseen (+17.2). Against PPO, which optimizes only the final episodic reward, Q-Evolve's advantage is dramatic: 79.4 vs 45.3 average, confirming that dense process-level credit assignment is critical for long-horizon tasks. On the sample-efficiency benchmark (Table 5, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), Q-Evolve (1-iter) with 13K env steps surpasses SFT+PPO (320K steps, 72.6/77.6) and SFT+GRPO (320K steps, 66.7/74.1) by 16.0/9.7 points on ALFWorld seen/unseen. On the Llama-3-8B-Instruct generalization test (Table 6), Q-Evolve (71.1/86.4/82.4/89.6/90.3) beats ETO+MPO (70.2/83.4/80.8/85.0/79.1) and KnowAgent (64.8/81.7/69.6/80.0/74.9) across all splits, demonstrating architecture-agnostic gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

Q-Evolve unifies three previously separate threads—offline RL (Implicit Q-Learning for in-distribution critic learning), process reward modeling (GAE-based advantage estimation as step-wise supervision), and behavior-proximal policy optimization (BPPO)—into a single closed-loop self-evolving pipeline for LLM agents. It departs from existing PRM-for-agents methods (QLASS, GiGPO) that require discrete states, environment backtracking, or extensive online rollouts, and from offline RL approaches (ILQ, RetroSpex) that treat the critic as an external filter rather than an intrinsic training signal. The key conceptual departure is the in-distribution co-evolution constraint: both process reward labeling and policy updates are grounded in the same hybrid offline dataset per iteration, directly addressing the distribution-shift failure mode that the paper identifies as the central weakness of prior PRM pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Q-Evolve achieves competitive or superior performance using ~25× fewer environment steps than online RL baselines (13K vs. 320K on ALFWorld), making reliable long-horizon agent training feasible without massive interaction budgets. The in-distribution constraint principle—that process rewards are only reliable on the distribution from which they are derived—offers a general design guideline for any PRM-based agent training pipeline. Key open questions remain: the self-evolving loop relies on greedy rollouts that reduce trajectory diversity over iterations, cross-iteration distribution shift is not explicitly corrected, and the retrospective reward labeling depends on structured environment feedback that may not generalize to all domains. Whether the co-evolution loop converges to a fixed point or can be extended beyond 2–3 iterations is also unexplored.
