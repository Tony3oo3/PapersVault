## Problem

Existing RL-based video temporal grounding methods produce superficial reasoning that contributes little to precise localization: random rollouts blindly explore the vast reasoning space without guidance, and reward functions evaluate only final answer correctness (IoU) while ignoring whether the reasoning is genuinely anchored to visual-temporal evidence. The paper demonstrates this by showing Time-R1 achieves comparable performance with and without reasoning chains, indicating its generated reasoning is largely decorative.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266126/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (uniformly sampled at 2 FPS, constrained to ~3584 video tokens) and a natural language query as input, and outputs a temporal segment (start and end timestamps) structured within a Chain-of-Thought format: an intermediate reasoning trace containing explicit timestamps and event descriptions, followed by a final <answer> prediction. The base model is Qwen2.5-VL-7B (also tested at 3B and Qwen3-VL-8B), with the vision encoder frozen and only language model parameters fine-tuned.

## Method

TaRO introduces a two-phase progressive curriculum. In the warm-up phase, Constructive Reasoning Exploration uses Gemini-3-Pro to generate dense timestamped captions, randomly samples a subset per rollout, and concatenates them chronologically as constructed reasoning traces; the model is then trained via an Advantage-Weighted Behavioral Cloning (AW-BC) loss that upweights only high-reward constructed rollouts, teaching the model which visual cues matter. In the self-exploration phase, the model generates its own reasoning via random rollouts optimized with GRPO, now augmented by a Temporal-Sensitive Reward: frames near the ground-truth start/end boundaries are shuffled, and the drop in log-probability of the reasoning tokens between the original and perturbed video measures how tightly the reasoning is anchored to critical temporal evidence; this reward is gated by an IoU threshold to prevent rewarding sensitivity on incorrect predictions.

## Results

Using Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as the base model, TaRO achieves R1@0.5 of 64.8% on Charades-STA, 39.8% on ActivityNet, 69.4% on QVHighlights, and 37.8% on TVGBench, outperforming the strongest RL-based competitor Time-R1 by 4.0%, 0.8%, 3.2%, and 8.4% respectively. On the smaller Qwen2.5-VL-3B model, gains over Time-R1 are more pronounced (e.g., +23.4% on QVHighlights R1@0.5), and on the newer Qwen3-VL-8B architecture, TaRO reaches 67.8% R1@0.5 on Charades-STA and 39.1% on TVGBench. On long-form videos, TaRO (8B) achieves 52.3% R1@0.3 on TACoS and 13.8% on Ego4D NLQ, surpassing the base Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct by +13.7% and +8.7% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TaRO consistently outperforms Time-R1 (the primary RL-based competitor) across all four standard benchmarks at 7B scale, with the largest margin on TVGBench (+8.4% R1@0.5) and Charades-STA (+4.0% R1@0.5). Against non-RL MLLM baselines such as UniTime, VideoChat-R1.5, and the raw Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, TaRO leads by substantial margins (e.g., +11.2% R1@0.5 over UniTime on Charades-STA, +13.6% over VideoChat-R1.5 on ActivityNet). On the smaller 3B model, TaRO's advantage over Time-R1 widens dramatically on QVHighlights (+23.4% R1@0.5), suggesting the constructive exploration and temporal reward are especially beneficial when the base model has weaker initial reasoning. On the newer Qwen3-VL-8B architecture, TaRO surpasses both the Instruct and Think variants by 9.9–13.2% R1@0.5 on Charades-STA and 13.2–14.2% on TVGBench. On long-form video benchmarks (TACoS, Ego4D NLQ), TaRO (8B) outperforms the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct base by +13.7% and +8.7% R1@0.3, confirming scalability to longer temporal contexts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based VTG paradigm established by Time-R1 and VideoChat-R1.5, which use GRPO to train MLLMs to generate reasoning chains before predicting timestamps. TaRO departs from this lineage in two key ways: it replaces the random rollout with a Constructive Reasoning Exploration that seeds reasoning from externally generated dense captions (using an AW-BC imitation loss rather than on-policy GRPO), and it introduces a Temporal-Sensitive Reward that measures reasoning quality by shuffling frames near ground-truth event boundaries, rather than relying solely on answer-level IoU. The progressive curriculum (AW-BC warm-up → GRPO self-exploration) draws on behavioral cloning (Torabi et al., 2018) and GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as complementary optimization stages, and the paper explicitly contrasts its instance-level temporal reward with Video-R1's group-level T-GRPO, arguing the latter is ill-suited for VTG where shuffling the entire video invalidates the ground-truth interval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical finding—that Time-R1's RL-trained reasoning contributes essentially nothing to grounding accuracy (comparable performance with and without reasoning chains)—challenges the prevailing assumption that RL-based CoT reasoning automatically improves VTG. The Temporal-Sensitive Reward offers a reusable mechanism for evaluating whether a model's reasoning is genuinely anchored to visual-temporal evidence, which could transfer to other video understanding tasks. Limitations include occasional inconsistency between the model's intermediate reasoning and final prediction, sensitivity to ambiguous ground-truth boundaries in untrimmed videos, and a dependency on an external captioner (though ablations show robustness to caption quality). Open questions include whether the progressive curriculum generalizes to other RL-based video reasoning tasks and whether the temporal-sensitivity signal can be computed without access to ground-truth timestamps at inference time.
