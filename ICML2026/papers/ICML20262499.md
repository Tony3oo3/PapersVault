## Problem

Evolutionary multi-tasking (EMT) accelerates black-box optimization through cross-task knowledge transfer, but existing methods fail to outperform single-task baselines like CMA-ES when scaling to dozens of low-similarity tasks. The authors identify this scalability barrier as the Multi-Task Curse, driven by two compounding effects: evaluation budget dispersion (fixed resources diluted across many tasks) and negative transfer (blind knowledge sharing misleads search when task landscapes diverge).

## Input / Output

The method takes K black-box objective functions (or RL environments) with potentially heterogeneous dimensionalities n_k and search bounds, and produces a per-task optimal solution x_k* along with the final Gaussian search distribution N(m_k, C_k). For reinforcement learning, the input is a set of policy networks with varying observation and action space dimensions; the method internally partitions each policy's flattened parameter vector into a joint-search subspace (shared hidden-layer weights and biases) and a task-specific subspace (input/output layer parameters) to enable transfer across morphologically distinct agents. All search spaces are mapped to [0,1]^n_k for cross-task comparability.

## Method

MES-RET augments CMA-ES with two reward-weighted mechanisms and a semantic alignment strategy. First, Reward-Weighted Evaluation uses a two-phase allocation: all K tasks receive one evolution cycle, then an additional budget is distributed via categorical sampling over normalized rewards that synthesize performance-improvement and distribution-diversity signals, satisfying a proven positive-correlation condition for superior expected improvement over uniform allocation. Second, Reward-Weighted Transfer aggregates mean vectors and diagonal covariance variation ratios across tasks weighted by their rewards, then injects a small number (τ≪μ) of external solutions into each task's evaluation batch; a Weak Guidance Injection mechanism guarantees that if all external solutions rank below the native top-μ elites, they receive zero recombination weight, providing a formal safety valve against negative transfer. For RL, Semantic Policy Parameter Alignment structurally decouples policy parameters into a joint-search subspace (first-layer bias plus all intermediate hidden parameters) and a task-specific subspace (input weights and output parameters), enabling knowledge transfer across heterogeneous state-action spaces while preserving functional semantics.

## Results

On 87 synthetic CEC 2017 tasks, MES-RET achieves the best Friedman rank (3.13) and wins on 25 tasks, outperforming the single-task CMA-ES baseline (rank 3.90, 20 wins) and all many-task methods (SBCMAES rank 9.68, AT-MFEA rank 7.02). On 42 real-world constrained engineering problems, MES-RET attains rank 3.54 with 18 best tasks versus CMA-ES at rank 4.95. On 18 Gymnasium policy search tasks, sep-MES-RET achieves the lowest Friedman rank (4.03) with 6 best tasks, securing 5 significant wins over sep-CMA-ES with only 1 loss, while PPO (rank 5.75) suffers 10 significant losses despite matching the #B count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MES-RET decisively outperforms all many-task evolutionary baselines: SBCMAES (rank 9.68 vs 3.13 on synthetic), AT-MFEA (7.02), EMaTO-MKT (7.17), TNG-SNES (6.70), and MTES-KG (4.97), confirming that existing EMT methods fail to beat single-task CMA-ES in low-similarity many-task settings. Against the single-task CMA-ES baseline, MES-RET achieves a statistically significant advantage (6 wins vs 21 losses on synthetic; 7 vs 19 on real-world), breaking the Multi-Task Curse. On policy search, sep-MES-RET (rank 4.03) beats sep-CMA-ES (4.39) with 5 wins and 1 loss, and substantially outperforms sep-SBCMAES (6.47) and sep-MTES-KG (6.31). Compared to policy gradient methods, sep-MES-RET ties PPO on #B (6 each) but achieves a lower Friedman rank (4.03 vs 5.75) with far fewer losses (1 vs 10), while A2C is dominated (rank 8.42). The ablation w/o-RE (removing reward-weighted evaluation) degrades to rank 3.82/3.65, confirming the critical role of dynamic budget allocation over uniform sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the evolutionary multi-tasking (EMT) lineage initiated by MFEA (Gupta et al., 2016) and its successors (MFEA-II, AT-MFEA, SBCMAES, MTES-KG, TNG-SNES, EMaTO-MKT) by identifying a fundamental scalability barrier—the Multi-Task Curse—where existing many-task methods underperform single-task CMA-ES in low-similarity regimes. Rather than modifying the population structure or transfer topology as prior EMT variants do, MES-RET keeps CMA-ES as an unmodified backbone and intervenes at two orthogonal levels: dynamic evaluation budget allocation (reward-weighted evaluation) and conservative knowledge injection (weak guidance injection with rank-based truncation). The semantic policy alignment strategy for RL is a novel contribution that has no direct precedent in the EMT literature, which has largely assumed homogeneous parameter spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formalization of the Multi-Task Curse (budget dispersion + negative transfer) and the empirical demonstration that state-of-the-art many-task optimizers actually lose to single-task CMA-ES in low-similarity settings provides a useful diagnostic for the EMT community. The weak guidance injection mechanism—where a single external solution (τ=1) is sufficient and maladaptive transfers are automatically nullified by CMA-ES's rank-based truncation—is a practical, low-overhead safety valve that could be adopted in other transfer-based optimizers. Key limitations include the requirement for fixed hidden-layer sizes in semantic policy alignment (restricting applicability to truly heterogeneous architectures) and the reliance on CMA-ES's local smoothness assumption for the reward design's theoretical justification. Open questions include whether the reward-weighted framework integrates cleanly with policy gradient methods and whether the alignment strategy generalizes to architectures with variable depth.
