## Problem

Existing sequential conditional independence tests (e-CRT, DAVT) operate in the Model-X setting, requiring exact knowledge of the conditional distribution P_{A|C}. In practice this conditional must be estimated, and even small estimation errors can cause severe Type I error inflation in sequential tests because validity must hold across an unbounded number of stopping opportunities. The paper asks whether a sequential CI test can retain good Type I control and competitive power when the relevant conditional distributions are estimated online rather than known exactly.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of i.i.d. (A, B, C) triples arriving in fixed-size batches of b observations, along with initial training and validation sets. At each round, the data are partitioned into a growing training set (for kernel and conditional-mean-embedding estimation), a held-out validation set (for null-scale calibration), and a fresh test batch (for the wealth update). The output is a data-dependent stopping time τ at which the wealth process W_t first exceeds 1/α, yielding a reject decision; otherwise the test continues indefinitely. The procedure is fully online: no auxiliary side data are required in the Online mode, and all model components are updated sequentially as new batches arrive.

## Method

SKCI constructs betting payoffs from a self-normalized cross U-statistic built on the KCI kernel: the numerator U_{n,b} compares the new test batch against historical training points via the residualized KCI operator ψ(Z), while the denominator S_n (a V-statistic on historical data) normalizes the scale so the payoff is approximately 1 under the alternative regardless of kernel magnitude. To ensure the wealth process remains a valid nonnegative supermartingale, a truncate-and-shift calibration is applied: the raw payoff is shifted by a predictable γ_t and truncated at −1, where γ_t is chosen as the smallest nonnegative shift making the conditional null expectation nonpositive, approximated via a Gaussian plug-in whose variance is estimated on the held-out validation set. The conditioning kernel k_C and betting fraction λ_t are jointly optimized each round by gradient ascent on an empirical log-wealth criterion computed from leave-block-out proxy payoffs over the training history. Theoretically, the paper decomposes the one-step drift into a Berry-Esseen finite-block term, a conditional-mean-embedding estimation error term, and a variance-mismatch term, then constructs a compensated supermartingale to obtain a finite-sample Type I error bound of the form α·exp(Σ λ_t U_t).

## Results

Across all five benchmarks (Gaussian, CI Hardness 1D/3D, RatInABox, dSprites, Car Insurance) evaluated in Online mode with batch size b=20 over 100 runs, SKCI maintains Type I error at or below the nominal α level while achieving the fastest power accumulation among all methods. In contrast, DAVT and EC2ST exhibit Type I error rates approaching 1 (i.e., near-certain false rejection) in Online mode, while e-CRT remains conservative but detects dependence substantially more slowly. On the hardest setting (3D separate-coordinate CI Hardness), all baselines fail to detect dependence and/or suffer severe Type I inflation, whereas SKCI's adaptive kernel optimization yields reliable power with reasonable Type I control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SKCI outperforms all three baselines on both Type I error control and power in the practically relevant Online and Pretrained modes. Against e-CRT (Shaer et al., 2023), SKCI achieves comparable or tighter Type I control but reaches high power significantly faster; e-CRT is described as 'much slower to detect dependence' on RatInABox and 'underpowered' on Car Insurance. Against DAVT (Pandeva et al., 2024b), SKCI is decisively superior: DAVT's Type I error rate approaches 1 in Online mode across all datasets, and in Pretrained mode on RatInABox it achieves 'almost no power.' Against EC2ST (Pandeva et al., 2024a), the gap is similar—EC2ST suffers 'extreme Type I error' on RatInABox and rates near 1 on Car Insurance in Online mode. In Oracle mode (where P_{A|C} is known exactly), all methods control Type I error and SKCI achieves the highest power, confirming that the advantage is most pronounced when the conditional must be estimated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of testing-by-betting (Ramdas & Wang, 2025) and kernel conditional independence testing (Zhang et al., 2011; He et al., 2025). Prior sequential CI tests—e-CRT (Shaer et al., 2023), DAVT (Pandeva et al., 2024b), and EC2ST (Pandeva et al., 2024a)—all require exact Model-X knowledge of P_{A|C} to construct null-calibrated samples, making them fragile when the conditional must be estimated. SKCI departs from this by avoiding explicit null-calibrated samples entirely, instead using a self-normalized cross-U-statistic payoff (Kim & Ramdas, 2024) with a truncate-and-shift calibration that only requires approximate knowledge of the conditional null law. The adaptive kernel optimization (jointly tuning the conditioning kernel k_C and betting fraction λ_t via empirical log-wealth) is a further departure from prior methods that fix these quantities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Model-X assumption is often unrealistic in online and streaming settings where (A,B,C) triples arrive sequentially without auxiliary data, yet prior sequential CI tests degrade severely (Type I error approaching 1) when the conditional is estimated. SKCI provides both a theoretical drift bound quantifying Type I inflation in terms of conditional mean embedding error and empirical evidence that the inflation is well-controlled in practice across high-dimensional and image-based benchmarks. Open questions include whether the Gaussian approximation for shift estimation remains valid in very small-batch or heavy-tailed regimes, and whether the theoretical rates for CME estimation (which can be slow per Li et al., 2022, 2024) are tight enough to guarantee control over very long time horizons. The method has direct practical relevance for fairness auditing (car insurance discrimination) and robustness testing where data arrives online.
