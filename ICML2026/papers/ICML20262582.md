## Problem

Speculative decoding achieves strong speedups under greedy decoding but consistently loses 20%+ of its advantage under sampling (T>0), because the classical lossless verification rule over-rejects draft tokens that are locally plausible and would not materially alter subsequent generation. The paper asks whether draft acceptance can be safely relaxed under sampling without degrading output quality.

## Input / Output

ARC-Decode operates as a training-free plug-in on the EAGLE-3 pipeline: it consumes the already-constructed draft token tree, the target model's verify-time logits, and the target model's input embedding table, requiring no additional forward passes. It outputs a relaxed set of accepted draft tokens (superset of standard posterior-accepted tokens) that are then appended to the generated sequence. All calibration constants (whitening matrix, surrogate scales, JS tolerance) are fixed once per backbone and decoding configuration, making the method context-agnostic at inference time.

## Method

ARC-Decode combines two complementary mechanisms. First, entropy-guided pre-verification pruning scores each draft-tree node with a depth-aware utility q̃(c) = exp(ℓ(c))·exp(−λH_m·d(c)) that penalizes deep branches under high boundary entropy, then selects a prefix-closed subtree for target verification, reducing the number of nodes the target model must evaluate. Second, a Local Tolerance Score (LTS) relaxes posterior-rejected tokens by computing two local shift surrogates—an embedding-side term c_e‖W(e_x − e_{t_m})‖²_2 using whitened embedding displacement to the target top-1 anchor, and a logit-side term c_ℓ(Δℓ̃)² from the post-processed probability margin—taking their minimum as U^(j), and softly boosting the acceptance probability of rejected candidates whose U^(j) falls below a calibrated threshold γ. The risk of each ARC-added acceptance is bounded by a top-K Jensen-Shannon divergence criterion calibrated on held-out traces, and a union bound over eligible candidates yields a sequence-level risk guarantee that scales with the number of relaxed candidates rather than the full vocabulary.

## Results

ARC-Decode achieves up to 2.61× end-to-end speedup over vanilla autoregressive decoding on MT-Bench with Llama-3.1-8B (accept length τ = 5.11), representing a 1.6× improvement over EAGLE-3 (1.84×, τ = 3.35). On Vicuna-13B, ARC-Decode reaches 3.35× speedup on MT-Bench and 3.25× on HumanEval, outperforming EAGLE-3 by 22–24%. On Llama-3.3-70B, ARC-Decode attains 3.60× speedup on MT-Bench (τ = 4.90) versus EAGLE-3's 3.35× (τ = 4.04). Generation quality remains comparable: MT-Bench scores are within 0.25 points and GSM8K exact-match within 0.5% of EAGLE-3 across all backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARC-Decode outperforms EAGLE-3 (its base pipeline) on every task and model, with the largest relative gains on Alpaca (1.42× → 2.28× on L 8B, a 60% speedup increase) and MT-Bench (1.84× → 2.61×). Against the strongest relaxed-verification baseline, Judge Decoding, ARC-Decode wins on all four L 8B tasks (e.g., 2.61× vs 2.01× on MT-Bench, 2.28× vs 1.74× on Alpaca), while FSD trails ARC on MT-Bench and Alpaca but is competitive on GSM8K (1.80× vs 1.76×). MTAD is the closest competitor on L 8B, achieving 2.55× on MT-Bench and 2.14× on Alpaca, but ARC still leads by 0.06× and 0.14× respectively. BiLD, adapted to the EAGLE-3 framework, performs poorly (≤0.29× speedup) due to architectural mismatch between its standalone small-model design and EAGLE's hidden-state-conditioned draft module. On Vicuna-13B, ARC-Decode dominates all baselines by a wide margin (3.35× vs 2.74× for EAGLE-3, 3.25× vs 2.02× for Medusa-2 on HumanEval), and on Llama-3.3-70B it extends EAGLE-3's 3.35× to 3.60× on MT-Bench while FSD (best threshold T=0.8) reaches only 1.78×.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARC-Decode builds directly on the EAGLE-3 speculative decoding pipeline, extending its verification stage with two training-free components: entropy-guided pre-verification pruning and a risk-bounded Local Tolerance Score (LTS) acceptance rule. Conceptually, it joins the emerging line of relaxed-verification methods (Judge Decoding, Fuzzy Speculative Decoding, Medusa) that challenge the necessity of strict token-level lossless matching, but departs from them by requiring no trained judge, no per-position draft-target divergence computation, and no additional forward passes—relying instead on verify-time logits, tied embeddings, and pre-calibrated surrogates. The entropy-guided pruning module is a distinct contribution not present in prior speculative decoding work, reshaping the draft tree after generation but before target verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically significant gap: speculative decoding loses over 20% of its speedup under typical sampling temperatures (T=1), which is the default for most LLM applications. The 1.6× additional speedup over EAGLE-3 under sampling, achieved without retraining or extra forward passes, is directly deployable in existing inference frameworks (SGLang, vLLM). However, the risk control is local (per-position JS divergence bound) rather than a true sequence-level quality guarantee, and the calibration constants are tied to a specific backbone and decoding configuration, limiting transferability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-tree-based speculative decoding, whether the empirical risk bounds hold under distribution shift, and whether the speedup advantage persists as draft models improve.
