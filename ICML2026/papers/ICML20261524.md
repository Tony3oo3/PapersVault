## Problem

GRPO, a leading RLVR algorithm for LLM reasoning, suffers from advantage collapse: when all G responses in a group receive identical binary rewards (all correct or all incorrect), within-group variance vanishes, all advantages become zero, and the policy gradient is completely ineffective. This failure mode is invisible to standard training metrics like loss curves and accuracy, yet 28–45% of training batches are affected, wasting substantial computation. No prior work provided a real-time diagnostic to quantify collapse severity or a lightweight intervention to recover learning signal from collapsed groups without additional model rollouts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a math question prompt, a group of G=8 sampled responses from the current policy, and their binary rewards (0/1) from a deterministic verifier. The key unusual aspect is that AVSPO injects synthetic virtual reward values (continuous in [0,1], not actual model outputs) into the normalization statistics; these virtual samples shift the group mean and standard deviation used for advantage computation but contribute no gradient term since no corresponding ∇θ log πθ exists for them. The output is updated policy parameters with non-zero advantage estimates even for previously collapsed groups, requiring no additional LLM forward passes beyond standard GRPO sampling.

## Method

The paper introduces two tightly coupled components. First, the Advantage Collapse Rate (ACR) is a zero-overhead diagnostic that measures the fraction of groups in a batch with reward standard deviation below a threshold (τ=10⁻⁶), leveraging statistics already computed during GRPO training; early-stage ACR (first 100 steps) predicts final accuracy with R²=0.617 across 245 configurations. Second, AVSPO adaptively injects K virtual reward samples into collapsed groups when ACR exceeds a dynamically adjusted threshold τ_adapt, where K scales as ⌈G·ACR^α⌉ (α=0.5) and virtual rewards are assigned via stratified formulas that differ for all-correct groups (decaying from r_obs) versus all-incorrect groups (anchored at r_anchor=0.1). The recomputed advantages use the augmented mean and standard deviation but are applied only to the G real samples, preserving the standard clipped PPO surrogate objective. The adaptive threshold updates each iteration based on policy improvement (ΔJ), tightening when training progresses and loosening when stagnation is detected, achieving 58–63% relative reduction in collapse with <0.01% computational overhead.

## Results

Across six Qwen2.5 models (0.5B–14B) trained on Level3-500 for 500 steps, AVSPO reduces the Advantage Collapse Rate (ACR) from 0.28–0.45 (GRPO) to 0.11–0.18, a 58–63% relative reduction. This translates to consistent pass@1 accuracy gains of +4 to +6 percentage points over GRPO on the average of seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MATH-500, GSM8K, Minerva, OlympiadBench, AMC, AIME24, MMLU-Pro). The largest gain is observed on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B (+6.1 pts, ACR 0.40→0.15), while the 14B model shows a more modest +4.6 pts (ACR 0.28→0.11). AVSPO also generalizes to code generation (MBPP), improving pass@1 by 3.0 points over GRPO with ACR dropping from 41.2% to 18.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVSPO outperforms all five baselines across every model scale. Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), the primary baseline, AVSPO gains +4.6 to +6.1 points on average accuracy while reducing ACR by 58–63% (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B: 39.6% vs. 33.5% avg, ACR 0.15 vs. 0.40). Against DCPO (Yang et al., 2025a), which uses dynamic clipping, AVSPO leads by +2.9 points on average and achieves substantially lower ACR (e.g., 0.14 vs. 0.25 on Math-7B). Against INTUITOR (Zhao et al., 2025) and RENT (Prabhudesai et al., 2025), which modify the reward signal via self-certainty and entropy minimization respectively, AVSPO leads by +6.8 and +8.9 points on average, suggesting that restoring batch-level gradient diversity is more effective than reward engineering. Against DAPO (Yu et al., 2026), which uses dynamic sampling to filter collapsed groups, AVSPO achieves higher MATH accuracy (74.1% vs. 68.3%) and GSM8K (69.7% vs. 65.8%) on Qwen2.5-Math-7B while retaining 100% sample utilization at 1.0× cost, compared to DAPO's 54.4% utilization at 1.8× cost. Multi-seed validation (n=5) confirms all improvements are statistically significant (p<0.05) with lower variance under AVSPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), which eliminates the critic network via intra-group reward normalization. It departs from prior GRPO variants (DAPO, DCPO, Scaf-GRPO) that address training instability at the optimization or sampling level, instead intervening at the reward statistics level: ACR diagnoses collapse using variance already computed during training, and AVSPO injects virtual reward values to restore non-zero advantages without additional rollouts. The diagnostic framing also distinguishes it from standard RL monitoring (entropy, KL divergence, gradient norms), which the authors argue cannot detect batch-level gradient ineffectiveness in critic-free methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ACR provides a zero-overhead, real-time diagnostic that practitioners can immediately adopt to detect wasted computation in any group-based policy optimization pipeline, and its strong predictive power (R²=0.617 for final accuracy) enables early configuration selection. AVSPO's simplicity—no extra forward passes, O(NG) complexity, drop-in replacement—makes it practically deployable. Limitations include evaluation only on Qwen2.5 models with binary-reward verifiers; extension to soft rewards, noisy human feedback, and non-mathematical domains remains open. The 500-step training horizon and single-epoch protocol also leave open whether gains persist under longer or multi-epoch training.
