## Problem

Standard diffusion models suffer from highly curved generation trajectories and noisy training targets, both stemming from the memoryless forward process that forces independent endpoint coupling between data and prior. While Schrödinger Bridge (SB) theory offers optimal transport couplings that could yield straighter paths, existing SB-based generative methods rely on unstable forward-backward alternating optimization that fails to scale to high-dimensional data like images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264142/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a Gaussian noise sample X_1 ~ p_prior and produces a synthetic image X_0 via backward SDE simulation, operating on pixel space (CIFAR-10, 32×32) and latent space (FFHQ, 256×256 through Stable Diffusion 3 autoencoder). A distillation variant compresses the multi-step backward SDE into a single-step generator G^ψ: R^d → R^d that maps one prior sample directly to an image. Training requires only forward simulation (data-to-prior) with 20 NFEs for coupling construction, rather than the 100–200 NFEs of bidirectional rollouts used by prior SB methods.

## Method

ASBM decouples SB generative modeling into two sequential stages under a non-memoryless VP base SDE. Stage 1 reformulates the forward dynamic as a data-to-energy sampling problem: it learns a forward control u_t via alternating Adjoint Matching and Corrector Matching (from Stochastic Optimal Control) to transport p_data to an energy-defined prior, thereby constructing the optimal endpoint coupling (X_0, X_1) with only 20 NFEs and a lighter network. Stage 2 trains the backward control v_t via a simple bridge matching loss supervised by the reciprocal process under the learned optimal coupling, avoiding the unstable bidirectional alternating optimization of prior SB methods. The non-memoryless base SDE (with σ_t ≪ σ_t^DM) injects less noise than standard diffusion, producing significantly straighter trajectories that reduce sampling NFEs and enable reliable one-step distillation via Tweedie's formula without timestep shifting.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (pixel space, 100 NFE), ASBM achieves FID 3.16, outperforming the best prior baseline OT-CFM (FID 4.15) and Score SDE (FID 4.61). On FFHQ latent space, ASBM achieves FID 8.85 at just 25 NFE versus Score SDE's 52.08, and maintains superior FID across all NFE settings (e.g., 6.27 vs 6.63 at 1000 NFE). For one-step distillation on CIFAR-10, ASBM achieves FID 6.68 with Recall 0.542, surpassing both SDS (FID 9.36, Recall 0.504) and DMD (FID 8.25, Recall 0.513) while avoiding the costly regression loss used by DMD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASBM (FID 3.16) outperforms all 11 baselines on CIFAR-10. Among flow-based methods, it beats OT-CFM (4.15), Rectified Flow (6.01), and FM (6.35) by 1.0–2.9 FID points. Among memoryless SDE baselines, it surpasses Score SDE (4.61), VSDM (4.24), and SB-FBSDE (5.26). The closest non-memoryless SB competitor, DSBM (9.68), is outperformed by 6.5 FID points, largely because DSBM requires empirical bridge-matching pretraining and suffers from unstable alternating optimization. In the distillation setting, ASBM (FID 6.68, Recall 0.542) beats both SDS (FID 9.36) and DMD (FID 8.25) on FID and Recall, while matching DMD's Precision (0.702 vs 0.715), and does so without DMD's expensive regression loss requiring 500K noise-image pairs. On FFHQ latent space, ASBM's advantage is most dramatic at low NFE: 8.85 vs 52.08 FID at 25 steps, confirming that its straighter trajectories enable efficient few-step generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASBM sits at the intersection of Schrödinger Bridge (SB) generative modeling and Stochastic Optimal Control (SOC) sampling. It builds directly on the SB optimality conditions (Chen et al., 2021) and the bridge matching formulation (Shi et al., 2023), but departs from prior SB-based methods (SB-FBSDE, DSBM) that rely on unstable alternating forward-backward optimization. The key conceptual departure is reframing the SB forward dynamic as a data-to-energy SOC sampling problem (building on Adjoint Matching and the Adjoint Schrödinger Bridge Sampler), which allows the optimal coupling to be constructed in a single forward-only training stage before the backward dynamic is learned via a simple matching loss. The paper also provides a clean theoretical unification showing that standard diffusion models (Score SDE) are a special case of SB under a memoryless base process, making the limitations of independent endpoint pairing explicit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage decoupling offers a practical and stable recipe for non-memoryless SB in high dimensions, which prior methods struggled to achieve without pretraining or bidirectional rollouts. The demonstrated benefits—straighter trajectories, fewer NFEs, better distillation with reduced mode collapse—suggest a path toward more efficient generative sampling that could transfer to larger-scale and conditional settings. However, the paper is limited to CIFAR-10 (32×32 pixel) and FFHQ (256×256 latent) without large-scale, high-resolution, or conditional generation experiments, leaving open whether the stability and efficiency gains persist at ImageNet scale or with text conditioning. Exploring alternative energy-defined priors beyond Gaussian is also flagged as a promising but unexplored direction.
