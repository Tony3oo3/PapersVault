## Problem

Reparameterization Policy Gradient (RPG) achieves high sample efficiency by backpropagating through differentiable dynamics, but suffers from two critical limitations: (1) prior on-policy methods discard expensive dynamics Jacobians after a single update, wasting computational resources, and (2) RPG is prone to training instability from exploding/vanishing gradients. While sample reuse of cached action-gradients would improve efficiency, it inherently increases the update-to-data ratio and exacerbates instability, and no principled framework existed to enable stable reuse.

## Input / Output

The method takes continuous environment states (e.g., 11-dim for Hopper, 76-dim for Humanoid) from differentiable simulators (DFlex, Rewarped) and outputs continuous actions via a squashed Gaussian policy parameterized by mean and diagonal covariance. A key intermediate artifact is the set of cached action-gradients (∇_a R(τ)) computed via a single BPTT pass over short-horizon rollouts (h=32) with value-function bootstrapping; these gradients are then reused across M=5 policy update epochs, with actions regenerated at each epoch via the inverse reparameterization transform ε = f_θ⁻¹(a; s) to re-establish a differentiable path from current policy parameters to the stored actions.

## Method

RPO establishes that reusing BPTT-computed action-gradients across multiple policy updates is equivalent to computing the reparameterization policy gradient of a PPO-style surrogate objective, providing a unified framework for on- and off-policy updates. For off-policy epochs, the cached action-gradient ∇_a R(τ) is backpropagated through a newly constructed path (via regenerated noise ε) and weighted by the importance sampling ratio ρ(θ) = π_θ(a|s)/π_θ_old(a|s), with a formal proof (Proposition D.1) showing this ratio is the correct correction. Stability is ensured by two mechanisms: (i) an asymmetric, advantage-independent clipping of the importance ratio (zeroing gradient contributions when ρ falls outside [1−c_low, 1+c_high]), which differs from PPO's advantage-sign-dependent clipping because RPG does not explicitly increase/decrease action log-likelihoods, and (ii) an explicit KL divergence regularization term between the behavior and current policy, which the authors show is necessary because clipping alone remains insufficient to prevent instability. The overall objective combines the surrogate gradient, KL penalty, and entropy bonus, with the value function trained separately via TD-λ regression using a double-critic architecture.

## Results

RPO achieves state-of-the-art stochastic evaluation performance across all five tasks. On the most challenging tasks, RPO scores 237.55±25.16 on Hand Reorient (vs. 213.44 for SAPO) and 15674.12±316.26 on Anymal (vs. 14568.97 for SHAC). On locomotion tasks, RPO reaches 5525.57 on Hopper, 8891.04 on Ant, and 8637.78 on Humanoid, consistently outperforming all baselines. RPO also demonstrates superior sample efficiency, reaching key performance thresholds (e.g., 5000 on Hopper, 8000 on Ant) several million environment steps earlier than competing methods, and completes 10M steps of training in ~81 minutes versus ~313 minutes for SHAC at 40M steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPO outperforms all five baselines across every task. Against the closest competitor SAPO, RPO gains +24.1 on Hand Reorient, +117.8 on Hopper, +172.4 on Ant, +34.7 on Humanoid, and +1578.2 on Anymal. Against SHAC, the margins are larger: +62.9 on Hand Reorient, +458.4 on Hopper, +684.9 on Ant, +893.3 on Humanoid, and +1105.2 on Anymal. GI-PPO, which uses REINFORCE for off-policy updates, is substantially outperformed (e.g., 237.55 vs. 27.19 on Hand Reorient), confirming that reusing RPG action-gradients is far more effective than switching to score-function gradients. PPO, the model-free baseline, trails by the largest margins (e.g., 8637.78 vs. 1665.66 on Humanoid), underscoring the sample-efficiency advantage of differentiable dynamics. In the appendix, RPO also beats SAC on Anymal and Humanoid, further validating its superiority over off-policy model-free methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Reparameterization Policy Gradient (RPG) framework established by SHAC (Xu et al., 2021) and SAPO (Xing et al., 2025), which backpropagate through differentiable dynamics to obtain low-variance policy gradients, and on PPO's (Schulman et al., 2017) surrogate objective and importance-weighted clipping mechanism. The key conceptual departure is a theoretical result showing that reusing cached action-gradients across multiple policy updates in RPG is mathematically equivalent to computing the reparameterization policy gradient of a PPO-style surrogate objective, thereby providing a principled unification of on- and off-policy updates for RPG. This contrasts with GI-PPO (Son et al., 2023), which attempted sample reuse but relied on REINFORCE gradients for off-policy epochs, failing to exploit the expensive dynamics Jacobians that motivate RPG in the first place.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a practical dilemma in differentiable-simulation RL: expensive dynamics Jacobians were previously discarded after a single update, and naive reuse destabilized training. By grounding sample reuse in the PPO surrogate objective and combining a tailored asymmetric gradient clip with explicit KL regularization, RPO achieves roughly 4× wall-clock time reduction (81 min vs. 313 min on ANYmal) while matching or exceeding SOTA performance. Open questions include whether the regularity conditions for interchanging differentiation and integration hold in contact-rich or non-smooth dynamics, and whether RPO-trained policies transfer to physical robots (sim-to-real), which the authors identify as a promising but unexplored direction.
