## Problem

MXFP4, the OCP-standardized 4-bit microscaling format, offers meaningful hardware-efficiency advantages over NVIDIA's NVFP4 (e.g., 12% tensor-core area savings) but suffers a ~10% end-to-end accuracy gap due to two structural limitations: a coarser 32-element block granularity and a power-of-two (E8M0) scale factor that lacks mantissa precision for representing outliers. This fidelity gap has limited MXFP4 adoption in quality-sensitive LLM inference despite its hardware benefits.

## Input / Output

The method consumes BF16 LLM weights and activations (linear layers only: QKVO projections, FFN, MoE experts) and produces a three-component MXFP4 representation: 4-bit E2M1 data elements, E8M0 power-of-two block scale factors per 16 elements, and an 8-bit mantissa-only macro scale (E0M8, value in [1,2)) per 128 elements. The macro scale is applied as a pre-quantization rescaling on vector cores and a post-GEMM Hadamard dequantization in the epilogue, so the tensor-core datapath remains unchanged from standard MXFP4.

## Method

Two software-only techniques close the MXFP4–NVFP4 gap without hardware modifications. Overflow-Aware Scaling (OAS) biases the FP32 mantissa before E8M0 exponent extraction, shifting the mapped α_max range from [4,8] to (3.5,7]; this doubles the effective dynamic range for lower-magnitude elements (reducing flush-to-zero) while preserving relative error for the block maximum, and triggers on ~18% of blocks. Macro Block Scaling (MBS) introduces a coarser 1×128 scale with 8 mantissa bits (stored as E0M8 in [1,2)) that recovers the sub-power-of-two precision lost by E8M0, effectively isolating outliers with extra mantissa resolution. MBS has two variants—Static (direct reciprocal of macro-block max, +1.1 dB QSNR) and Dynamic (memoization-based LUT search minimizing per-block SSE, +1.6 dB QSNR)—and the default Hybrid configuration uses Dynamic for weights and Static for activations, yielding near-NVFP4 fidelity (<1 dB QSNR gap, <1% accuracy gap) at only 6.2% average GEMM overhead.

## Results

On Llama 3.1-8B-Instruct, MXFP4-MBS-H achieves 66.50% average accuracy across six benchmarks (MMLU-Pro, GSM8K, HellaSwag, Winogrande, ARC-C, ARC-E), compared to 61.25% for standard MXFP4-OCP and 67.02% for NVFP4. On Qwen3-8B, MXFP4-MBS-H reaches 70.84% average versus 65.50% (MXFP4-OCP) and 71.48% (NVFP4), narrowing the gap to NVFP4 to under 1% on average. The proposed techniques incur only 6.2% average GEMM overhead on NVIDIA Blackwell hardware, with zero effective overhead for activation quantization in the MBS-Static variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NVFP4 (Alvarez et al., 2025), MXFP4-MBS-H closes the accuracy gap from ~10% (MXFP4-OCP) to under 1% on average: 66.50% vs. 67.02% on Llama 3.1-8B and 70.84% vs. 71.48% on Qwen3-8B, with NVFP4 retaining a slight edge on MMLU-Pro and HellaSwag while MXFP4-MBS-H matches or exceeds it on GSM8K and Winogrande. Against MX+ (Lee et al., 2025), the closest prior MX-enhancement method, MXFP4-MBS-H wins by 3.89% average on Llama 3.1-8B (66.50% vs. 62.61%) and 3.19% on Qwen3-8B (70.84% vs. 67.65%), with particularly large gains on GSM8K (+8.78 pts) and MMLU-Pro (+2.39 pts). Against the standard MXFP4-OCP baseline (Rouhani et al., 2023), MXFP4-MBS-H improves average accuracy by 5.25% on Llama 3.1-8B and 5.34% on Qwen3-8B, recovering 62% of the total accuracy loss from BF16. On frontier MoE models (DeepSeek-R1, Llama 4-Maverick), MXFP4-MBS-H also matches or slightly exceeds NVFP4 on GSM8K while remaining within 0.6% on MMLU-Pro, confirming the gains generalize to larger architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OCP MXFP4 microscaling format (Rouhani et al., 2023) by identifying two specific sources of its accuracy gap relative to NVIDIA's NVFP4—coarser 32-element block granularity and the power-of-two-only E8M0 scale factor—and proposing software-only remedies. The MBS factor representation borrows the exponent-mantissa decomposition from GAM (Su et al., 2025), while the overall strategy departs from MX+ (Lee et al., 2025), the closest prior work, by avoiding an extra sparse GEMM and instead using a coarser macro-block mantissa scale that integrates seamlessly into the existing GEMM pipeline. Unlike hardware-based outlier-handling methods (Olive, SDQ, Microscopiq), both OAS and MBS require no architectural changes, making them deployable on any MXFP4-compatible tensor core.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the MXFP4-to-NVFP4 accuracy gap from ~10% to under 1% with only 6.2% GEMM overhead, this work re-establishes MXFP4 as a practical 4-bit inference format while preserving its 12–17% tensor-core area advantage over NVFP4—significant given that MX is an OCP standard backed by Microsoft, AMD, Arm, Intel, Meta, NVIDIA, and Qualcomm. The techniques are complementary to calibration-based PTQ (e.g., SmoothQuant) and QAT, suggesting further gains are possible. Open questions include whether MBS-Dynamic can be made faster than the current 2.5–3× overhead, whether the approach extends to 4-bit training, and how it interacts with emerging rotation-based or sparsity-based quantization methods at scale.
