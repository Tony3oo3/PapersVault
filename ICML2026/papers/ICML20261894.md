## Problem

Existing approaches to combining Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with World Models for autonomous driving remain loosely coupled: either the world model acts as an external simulator with no structural knowledge transfer to the VLA, or shared representations are used without action-conditioned causal reasoning. This prevents the planner from performing prospective 'what-if' imagination—evaluating how candidate actions shape future scene evolution—limiting it to reactive rather than proactive, long-horizon decision-making.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three camera views (concatenated to 256×1024 for NAVSIM, or six views at 640×384 for nuScenes), textual navigation prompts, historical ego actions serialized as natural-language tokens, and BEV features extracted by BEVFormer and projected into the VLM embedding space. All modalities are tokenized and fed jointly into an InternVL-based VLM, whose final-layer hidden states form a shared latent representation. Outputs include a predicted future trajectory, a future BEV latent (decoded into a semantic BEV map via a lightweight segmentation head), and—during Stage 3—an action-conditioned future BEV generated by the DiT denoiser.

## Method

DriveWorld-VLA unifies VLA planning and world modeling in a single LLM hidden-state latent space through a three-stage progressive training scheme. Stage 1 jointly trains a history-conditioned BEV denoiser and an action decoder on the shared VLM latent, establishing predictive representations. Stage 2 activates a DiT-based flow-matching denoiser that learns action-conditioned future BEV generation, enabling controllable 'what-if' rollouts at the feature level rather than expensive pixel-level simulation. Stage 3 closes the loop: predicted actions condition the generative branch via Euler sampling (25 steps), a learned reward function scores the consistency between the action-conditioned imagination and ground-truth future BEV, and a reward-weighted action loss refines the policy toward trajectories with more favorable imagined outcomes. The key architectural insight is that the VLM's hidden states serve simultaneously as the world model's generative latent and the planner's decision state, so the planner directly reasons over scene-evolution dynamics rather than treating them as an external signal.

## Results

DriveWorld-VLA achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three primary benchmarks: 91.3 PDMS on NAVSIMv1 (vs. 90.2 for DriveVLA-W0), 86.8 EPDMS on NAVSIMv2 (vs. 86.1 for DriveVLA-W0), and 0.16% average 3-second collision rate on nuScenes (vs. 0.17% for FSDrive). On NAVSIMv1, it attains 99.1% No Collision and 85.9% Ego Progress, the highest among all compared methods. On nuScenes, while its average L2 of 0.61 m trails HERMES-p (0.36 m) and FSDrive (0.53 m), it achieves the lowest collision rate by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveWorld-VLA outperforms all 21 compared baselines on the primary NAVSIMv1 benchmark, achieving 91.3 PDMS versus 90.2 for the closest competitor DriveVLA-W0 (ICLR 2026) and 89.6 for ReCogDrive (ICLR 2026). On NAVSIMv2, it leads with 86.8 EPDMS over DriveVLA-W0 (86.1) and DiffusionDrive (84.5), with particularly strong Drivable Area Compliance (99.1) and Lane Keeping (97.0). On nuScenes open-loop planning, DriveWorld-VLA achieves the lowest average collision rate (0.16%) among all methods, beating FSDrive (0.17%) and HERMES-p (0.32%), though its average L2 (0.61 m) is higher than HERMES-p (0.36 m) and FSDrive (0.53 m), indicating a safety-over-precision trade-off. Among world-model-based methods, it surpasses LAW (84.6 PDMS), WoTE (88.3), and Epona (86.2) on NAVSIMv1, and outperforms Epona (0.36%) and LAW (0.34%) on nuScenes collision rate. The method also demonstrates superior robustness under sensor corruptions (Snow, Rain, Fog, Motion Blur) on both NAVSIM-C and nuScenes-C benchmarks compared to TransFuser and LAW.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveWorld-VLA carves new ground by rejecting both dominant coupling strategies for VLA–World Model integration: disentangled interaction (treating the WM as an external simulator, e.g., ADR1, ReSim) and feature-sharing without causal reasoning (e.g., Epona, DriveVLA-W0, HERMES). Its key architectural departure is using the VLM's final-layer hidden states as a single shared latent space that simultaneously serves as the world model's generative input and the VLA planner's decision variable, so that action-conditioned 'what-if' rollouts (via a DiT flow-matching denoiser) directly inform trajectory selection. The three-stage progressive training paradigm—joint VLA/WM pre-training, action-controllability fine-tuning, then reward-guided refinement—repurposes flow-matching and reward-weighted imitation in a way that prior driving WMs (LAW, WoTE) did not, since those methods supervise only at the task level rather than the feature level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that tight representation-level unification of world modeling and planning yields measurable safety and progress gains (91.3 PDMS on NAVSIMv1, 0.16% CR on nuScenes) without requiring pixel-level rollouts, making latent-space 'what-if' reasoning a practical alternative to expensive video generation. The ablation showing a 38.8 PDMS collapse when generative and planning branches are trained simultaneously (Non-progressive 2) is a strong practical warning that even within a shared feature space, these objectives require asynchronous optimization—a finding likely to inform future multi-task driving architectures. Open questions include whether the reward model generalizes beyond the specific simulator used, whether the 1.16 FPS inference rate is sufficient for real-time deployment, and whether the approach extends to longer planning horizons or more complex multi-agent interactions.
