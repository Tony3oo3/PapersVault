## Problem

Many modern generative methods—VWGF, S-JKO, RWP-GAN, and others—are built on the JKO scheme and Wasserstein gradient flows, yet they have been developed in isolation for specific divergences or parameterizations, with no unified framework explaining their relationships. This paper asks how a broad class of generative algorithms can be derived, analyzed, and connected within a single theoretical lens, and whether the JKO framework can be extended beyond f-divergences to metrics like IPMs and squared MMD.

## Input / Output

The method consumes noise samples drawn from a fixed source distribution (e.g., standard Gaussian) and data samples from the target distribution (MNIST at 28×28 grayscale, CIFAR-10 at 32×32 RGB), and produces generated images of the same resolution. A key structural detail is the reparametrization trick: rather than composing successive transport maps at each JKO step, a single neural network is trained to map directly from the source to the current iterate, so inference requires only one forward pass of the final generator. The discriminator (or critic) operates on the same image space and is trained jointly with the generator in an adversarial min-max loop.

## Method

The paper introduces the Generative Wasserstein Flow (GWF) framework, which recasts each JKO step for an f-divergence objective as a min-max problem over a transport map T (generator) and a witness function h (discriminator), derived via the variational representation of the divergence and Brenier's theorem. It proves that VWGF, S-JKO, and RWP-GAN are all equivalent instantiations of this scheme, differing only in the orientation of the f-divergence. The framework is then extended to Integral Probability Metrics and squared MMD, yielding a new JKO-regularized MMD-GAN (Algorithm 2) where a learned embedding replaces the closed-form witness function for better high-dimensional performance. Empirically, the JKO proximal regularization (controlled by step size τ) consistently improves or stabilizes training across f-divergences, W1, and MMD objectives on MNIST and CIFAR-10, with the largest gains observed for MMD-based losses. Finally, the paper shows that the parametric JKO update, in the small-step limit, corresponds to a projected Wasserstein gradient flow where the projection is defined by the Riemannian metric induced by the neural network parametrization.

## Results

On MNIST, JKO regularization consistently reduces FID across all MMD variants: ckMMD improves from 2.173 to 1.971, sMMD from 1.464 to 1.324, and MMD from 1.028 to 0.983. On CIFAR-10, the gains are more dramatic for MMD (49.39 → 15.76 FID) and sMMD (59.79 → 26.79 FID), while ckMMD shows a smaller improvement (13.96 → 12.78 FID). In the broader divergence study (Section 5.3), JKO regularization with appropriately tuned step size τ improves or stabilizes training across f-divergences (KL, JS, χ²), W₁, and MMD², with the largest gains (up to 20 FID points) observed for MMD-based objectives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's JKO-regularized GWF framework is compared against five unregularized baselines. Against MMD GAN (Li et al., 2017), the JKO-regularized version achieves substantially lower FID on CIFAR-10 (15.76 vs. 49.39) and a modest improvement on MNIST (0.983 vs. 1.028). Against sMMDGAN (Arbel et al., 2018), JKO regularization yields a large CIFAR-10 gain (26.79 vs. 59.79 FID) and a small MNIST improvement (1.324 vs. 1.464). Against ckMMDGAN (Zhang et al., 2025), JKO provides consistent but smaller improvements on both datasets (1.971 vs. 2.173 on MNIST; 12.78 vs. 13.96 on CIFAR-10). For f-divergences, the 'no JKO' limit recovers standard f-GAN training (Nowozin et al., 2016), and JKO yields moderate yet consistent FID improvements across KL, JS, and χ² divergences. For W₁, the unregularized baseline coincides with WGAN (Arjovsky et al., 2017), and small JKO step sizes (τ=0.01) significantly stabilize training compared to the standard WGAN objective, which is known to be unstable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies several recently proposed generative methods—VWGF (Fan et al., 2022), S-JKO (Choi et al., 2024), and RWP-GAN (Lin et al., 2021)—by showing they are all instances of a single parametric JKO scheme applied to f-divergence objectives, differing only in the variational representation used and the orientation of the divergence. It then extends this JKO framework beyond f-divergences to Integral Probability Metrics and squared MMD, deriving a new JKO-regularized MMD GAN algorithm. The theoretical grounding draws on Brenier's theorem, unbalanced optimal transport duality, and the Donsker-Varadhan representation, while the parametric flow analysis in Section 6 connects the practical reparametrized JKO update to a projected Wasserstein gradient flow preconditioned by a Riemannian metric on parameter space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification clarifies that several independently proposed algorithms are mathematically identical, which should reduce redundant development and guide practitioners toward the most convenient formulation for their setting. The extension to IPMs and squared MMD, combined with empirical evidence that JKO regularization substantially stabilizes MMD-based adversarial training (up to 20 FID points on CIFAR-10), offers a practical recipe for improving existing GAN variants. However, experiments are limited to MNIST and CIFAR-10 with modest architectures, the parametric flow theory requires strong invertibility assumptions on the generator, and the reliance on adversarial optimization inherits the well-known sensitivity to hyperparameters and initialization that limits broader adoption compared to score-based or flow-matching methods.
