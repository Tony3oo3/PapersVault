## Problem

Existing LLM editing methods can appear to succeed at targeted edits while silently degrading broader capabilities—a failure mode analogous to reward hacking. Prior approaches impose heuristic guardrails (restricting to few parameters, localizing knowledge to specific layers, or constraining representation changes) that are only indirectly tied to capability preservation, yielding a poor edit-success versus capability-retention trade-off, especially under realistic autoregressive evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261810/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CRISPEDIT takes a pretrained LLM, a set of edit target pairs (input–output pairs specifying desired factual or behavioral changes), and a capability reference dataset (as few as 100 samples) that proxies the behaviors to preserve. It updates only the MLP down-projection weights of a small set of layers (e.g., layers 19–23 for LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct) via projected gradient descent, producing edited model parameters. The K-FAC curvature statistics are precomputed once on the capability set and cached, enabling reuse across many subsequent edits in both batch and sequential modes without re-deriving the projection.

## Method

CRISPEDIT formulates editing as a constrained optimization problem: minimize the edit loss subject to a Bregman-divergence bound on the capability loss. The Bregman choice is critical because its second-order Taylor expansion yields a quadratic form governed exactly by the Gauss-Newton Hessian (GNH), avoiding the stationarity assumption required by a plain Hessian constraint. The constraint is enforced by projecting each edit gradient onto the γ-approximate nullspace of the GNH (the subspace of low-curvature directions where capability loss is nearly invariant). To scale to billion-parameter models, the GNH is approximated layer-wise via K-FAC as a Kronecker product of activation and pseudo-gradient covariance matrices, and a matrix-free projector exploits the Kronecker eigen-structure—rotating gradients into the factored eigenbasis, applying a binary Hadamard mask to zero out high-curvature components, and rotating back—so the full d_in·d_out × d_in·d_out projection matrix is never materialized. For sequential editing, CRISPEDIT-SEQ maintains running K-FAC statistics via streaming averages, treating both base capabilities and previously applied edits as hard constraints at each round.

## Results

On LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct edited with 3,000 ZsRE samples, CRISPEDIT achieves 80.5% reliability and 69.0% generalization (with QA context), outperforming the strongest baseline AlphaEdit (70.1% / 60.6%) while keeping base capability degradation below 1% on average (e.g., MMLU 69.5→69.5, GSM8K 73.5→76.0). On CounterFact and WikiBigEdit, CRISPEDIT similarly leads in reliability (79.4% and 77.0% respectively) with minimal capability loss. In the safety unlearning setting (RWKU-L2), CRISPEDIT achieves 94.8% abstention on the full edited set while retaining MMLU at 69.4 and GSM8K at 73.0, compared to AlphaEdit's 99.7% abstention but with MMLU dropping to 58.4 and GSM8K to 53.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRISPEDIT decisively outperforms all baselines on editing reliability across all three datasets (ZsRE, CounterFact, WikiBigEdit) while preserving base capabilities within ~1–2 points of the unedited model. The strongest competitor, AlphaEdit, achieves competitive reliability (70.1% on ZsRE) but suffers severe capability degradation (MMLU drops from 69.5 to 52.7, GSM8K from 73.5 to 45.5), reflecting its overly restrictive null-space constraint. MEMIT and MEND fail catastrophically under autoregressive evaluation (near 0% reliability), despite appearing strong under teacher-forced evaluation. LocBF-FT preserves capabilities well (MMLU 69.5, GSM8K 75.5) but achieves lower editing reliability (69.5%) than CRISPEDIT (80.5%). Adam-NSCL and UltraEdit are overly conservative, achieving only 16.6% and 20.0% reliability respectively on ZsRE. CRISPEDIT also runs in 4m 6s, faster than AlphaEdit (7h 19m), MEMIT (9h 27m), and LoRA (47m 24s), while matching or exceeding their editing quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRISPEDIT builds on K-FAC (Martens & Grosse, 2015) for scalable curvature approximation and on the null-space projection paradigm used by Adam-NSCL and AlphaEdit, but reframes editing as a constrained optimization problem with a Bregman divergence constraint that yields the Gauss-Newton Hessian exactly without requiring base-model convergence. The paper proves (Proposition 1) that AlphaEdit and Adam-NSCL solve strictly more restrictive special cases of its formulation, operating in lower-dimensional representation-level subspaces rather than the full parameter-level low-curvature subspace. This positions CRISPEDIT as a principled unification of prior heuristic guardrails (layer restriction, knowledge vectors, activation covariance nullspaces) under a single second-order optimization framework, with K-FAC and a matrix-free Kronecker-structured projector making it practical at billion-parameter scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-1% average capability degradation while achieving ~80% edit reliability on LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct is a practically significant improvement over prior editors that trade 10–20% capability loss for comparable edit success, and the sequential variant (CRISPEDIT-SEQ) addresses the real-world need for iterative, online model updates without catastrophic forgetting of prior edits. Open questions include whether trust-region or SQP methods could enable larger, more aggressive edit steps, whether the K-FAC approximation degrades for very deep or non-MLP architectures, and how the method extends to safety-critical editing beyond factual unlearning (e.g., removing harmful generation patterns). The reliance on a capability reference dataset, even if small (n=100 suffices), remains a practical consideration for deployment.
