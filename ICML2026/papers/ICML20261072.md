## Problem

Converting pretrained Diffusion Transformers to Spiking Neural Networks in a training-free manner is hindered by a fundamental spatiotemporal mismatch: the highly dynamic activation ranges that evolve across denoising steps cannot be captured by fixed-scale spiking thresholds, causing cumulative representation errors that severely degrade generation fidelity. Prior SNN-based diffusion approaches either require expensive direct training or are limited to U-Net backbones, leaving the DiT-to-SNN conversion problem largely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a class label as input and generates a 256×256 or 512×512 RGB image on ImageNet using a pretrained DiT-XL/2 backbone. The spiking simulation runs at low latency (T=4 internal time steps) with multi-threshold neurons (n∈{6,8,10} thresholds, roughly 8-bit equivalent capacity), while the outer diffusion loop uses N∈{25,50,100} denoising steps. The conversion is entirely training-free: weights are mapped directly from the pretrained ANN, and the only architectural change is replacing ReLU/continuous activations with MT spiking neurons plus the MPEF state-carrying mechanism.

## Method

The framework combines three synergistic components applied to a pretrained DiT. First, Hadamard rotation is applied to activations to redistribute spatial outliers into a more uniform distribution, alleviating the channel-wise imbalance that breaks fixed-threshold spiking. Second, Multi-Threshold (MT) neurons replace binary IF neurons, using n geometrically spaced thresholds (with B=2 for hardware-friendly bit-shift arithmetic) to emit multi-intensity spikes and increase per-step information capacity. Third, the Membrane Potential Error-Feedback (MPEF) mechanism exploits the near-perfect distributional overlap between adjacent denoising steps by carrying the residual membrane potential v_τ[T] from step τ forward as the initial state v_{τ-1}[0] for the next step, forming a temporal negative feedback loop. Theoretically, MPEF bounds the accumulated input-output discrepancy over N denoising steps to a constant δ (independent of N), whereas standard conversion without feedback allows error to grow linearly as Nδ.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with DiT-XL/2 (cfg=1.5, 50 denoising steps), the proposed Spiking DiT with T=4 simulation steps and n=6 thresholds achieves FID 5.86, IS 160.33, sFID 20.41, and Precision 0.76, closely matching the full-precision baseline (FID 5.75). With n=8 thresholds, the method reaches FID 5.59 and IS 181.38, slightly surpassing the FP baseline on FID. At 512×512 resolution (50 steps), the method attains FID 5.91 (n=8) versus 6.01 for FP, demonstrating consistent near-lossless conversion across resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three post-training quantization (PTQ) methods: Q-Diffusion, PTQ4DiT, and Q-DiT. At 50 steps (cfg=1.5, 256×256), the proposed method (FID 5.86, n=6) substantially outperforms Q-Diffusion (FID 22.89 at W4A8) and PTQ4DiT (FID 9.17 at W4A8), and also beats Q-DiT (FID 6.54 at W4A8). At the W8A8 setting, the method (FID 5.59, n=8) slightly trails PTQ4DiT (FID 5.45) but outperforms Q-DiT (FID 6.16) and Q-Diffusion (FID 14.61). At 512×512 resolution, the method (FID 5.91, n=8) beats all three baselines at W4A8 (Q-DiT: 9.68, PTQ4DiT: 19.71) and matches or exceeds them at W8A8 (Q-DiT: 6.76, PTQ4DiT: 7.21). Overall, the spiking approach achieves competitive or superior FID to PTQ methods while replacing MAC operations with energy-efficient AC operations (118.69G MACs → 0.91G MACs + 482G ACs).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ANN-to-SNN conversion paradigm (Diehl et al., 2015; Bu et al., 2023) from single-pass architectures (CNNs, LLMs) to the iterative denoising process of Diffusion Transformers, a setting where fixed-scale spiking representations fail due to temporal activation drift. It builds on Multi-Threshold neurons (Huang et al., 2024, 2025) for richer discrete encoding and on SpikeZIP-TF (You et al., 2024) for spike-equivalent attention and non-linear operators. The key departure from prior conversion work is the MPEF mechanism, which exploits the temporal correlation between adjacent denoising steps to recycle residual membrane potential—a form of implicit error-feedback that has no direct analogue in single-pass SNN conversion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free framework for spiking DiTs, and the MPEF mechanism offers a general principle—carrying structured residuals across iterative steps—that could transfer to other sequential or iterative model families (e.g., video diffusion, iterative refinement). However, practical impact is contingent on neuromorphic hardware adoption, and the energy savings (MAC→AC) are meaningful only on spike-based accelerators, not on standard GPUs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video DiTs (Sora-class models), whether it generalizes to different diffusion schedules or flow-matching formulations, and whether the T=4 latency constraint is sufficient for real-time deployment.
