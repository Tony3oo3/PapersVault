## Problem

Generative diffusion and flow matching models frequently suffer from mode collapse, where varying random seeds still produce stereotypic outputs. Existing training-free diversity methods intervene during the sampling trajectory (e.g., particle repulsion, condition annealing) but assume the standard isotropic Gaussian initialization is sufficient. This paper identifies that the initialization itself is agnostic to the guidance potential landscape, causing trajectories to be attracted into dominant modes before the generation process even begins.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt or class label as conditioning and produces a single generated image (256×256 for SD v1.4, 512×512 for SD v3.5). It operates entirely at inference time as a plug-and-play modification to the initial noise x_T before the standard denoising/flow-matching pipeline begins, requiring only one additional forward and backward pass at the initial timestep (~3% wall-clock overhead). The method is compatible with both diffusion (DDIM, 50 steps) and flow matching (Euler, 30 steps) schedulers without architectural changes.

## Method

DivIn formulates the initial noise selection as sampling from a guidance potential posterior p_diverse(x_T|c) ∝ exp(−τ·U(x_T,c))·N(x_T; 0, I), where U is a generalized Tweedie potential measuring the Euclidean distance between conditional and unconditional single-step denoising estimates. To sample from this intractable distribution, DivIn employs Langevin dynamics with three competing forces: a diversity gradient (−τ∇U) that pushes latents away from high-potential basins, a prior constraint (−x) that anchors latents to the valid Gaussian manifold, and injected stochastic noise (√(2η)ξ) that prevents collapse into local minima. This stochastic formulation is the key distinction from deterministic seed optimization (SAIL), which collapses the initialization distribution to a single point and destroys diversity. Theoretically, the paper proves via entropy dynamics that high-curvature regions cause rapid volume contraction during reverse diffusion, and that minimizing the Tweedie potential is equivalent to minimizing the spectral gap between conditional and unconditional Hessians.

## Results

On ImageNet class-to-image (SDv1.4), DivIn alone achieves a Vendi Score of 4.688 and Recall of 0.569, outperforming all standalone baselines (SAIL: 4.549/0.543, PG: 4.395/0.502, CADS: 4.384/0.528, IG: 4.585/0.564) while maintaining competitive FID (16.158). On text-to-image (SDv3.5), DivIn reduces in-batch similarity from 0.793 to 0.775 and increases Vendi Score from 1.803 to 1.864 while preserving CLIP score (34.01 vs 34.04). Combining DivIn with trajectory-based methods (e.g., IG+DivIn) further expands diversity to Vendi 4.729 and Recall 0.576 with only marginal FID increase (15.877 vs 15.531). The additional inference cost is minimal: ~3% wall-clock time for a single Langevin step (0.754s → 0.779s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAIL (the deterministic initialization baseline), DivIn wins on every diversity metric: Recall 0.569 vs 0.543, Vendi 4.688 vs 4.549, and FID 16.158 vs 16.395, while also showing much lower variance (±0.013 vs ±0.038 on Recall), confirming that stochastic Langevin sampling preserves distribution volume better than deterministic optimization. Against trajectory-based methods, DivIn alone surpasses PG (Vendi 4.688 vs 4.395, FID 16.158 vs 17.520) and CADS (Vendi 4.688 vs 4.384, FID 16.158 vs 16.360) on both diversity and quality. Against IG, DivIn achieves higher Recall (0.569 vs 0.564) and Vendi (4.688 vs 4.585) but slightly worse FID (16.158 vs 15.531), indicating IG is the strongest standalone quality-preserving baseline. The key finding is orthogonality: combining DivIn with any trajectory method consistently improves diversity beyond either alone (e.g., IG+DivIn reaches Vendi 4.729 and Recall 0.576, the best in the table), validating that initialization and trajectory interventions capture complementary sources of diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometric sharpness framework of SAIL (Jeon et al., 2025), which linked memorization to local Hessian sharpness and proposed deterministic seed optimization, by generalizing the perspective from memorization to broader mode collapse and replacing deterministic optimization with stochastic Langevin posterior sampling. The key conceptual departure is reframing initialization not as a fixed isotropic Gaussian prior but as a guidance-potential-weighted posterior, which preserves distributional entropy that SAIL's mode-seeking updates destroy. The method is positioned as orthogonal to the existing trajectory-based diversity literature (Particle Guidance, CADS, Interval Guidance), operating at a different intervention point (initial noise vs. denoising trajectory) and thus composable with those approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in providing a new, composable axis of diversity enhancement that expands the diversity-quality Pareto frontier when stacked on top of existing trajectory methods, with only ~3% additional inference cost for a single Langevin step. Limitations include the need for per-architecture temperature tuning, inapplicability to unconditional generation, and the fact that the stochastic formulation introduces higher run-to-run variance. Open questions include whether the guidance potential posterior can be distilled into a lightweight learned prior to eliminate iterative sampling, and whether the geometric initialization perspective generalizes to video generation or unconditional settings.
