## Problem

Jain et al. (2023) proved that transitioning from the batch model to the continual release model under differential privacy causes an unavoidable exponential increase in purely additive error for fundamental tasks like MaxSum and MaxSelect. This paper asks whether incorporating a relative error term can circumvent this barrier, and whether a separation exists between non-adaptive and adaptively generated input streams in this setting.

## Input / Output

The mechanism receives a stream of T vectors in [0,1]^d and must produce an output at every time step t: either a numerical estimate of the max (or min) of the cumulative coordinate sums, or an index identifying the attribute with the max (or min) cumulative sum. The accuracy guarantee is (1±γ)·true_value ± α, combining a constant relative error γ with an additive error α. The input stream may be non-adaptive (predetermined) or adaptive (each record chosen by an adversary based on previous outputs), and the paper shows these two settings yield fundamentally different error bounds for MinSelect.

## Method

The algorithms use a threshold-triggered update framework: the AboveThreshold mechanism monitors whether the current output's error exceeds a tolerance, and only when it does does the algorithm invoke a batch mechanism (Laplace for MaxSum/MinSum, Exponential for MaxSelect/MinSelect) to refresh the answer. Because the true answer grows by a factor of (1+γ) after each update, only O(log T / γ) updates are needed, yielding polylog(d,T) additive error via composition. For MinSelect with non-adaptive inputs, a potential analysis (inspired by Asi et al., 2023a) shows the minimum cumulative sum still grows multiplicatively after polylog(d,T) updates even though the algorithm may switch to a new coordinate, bounding the total number of updates. The privacy parameter is gradually decreased as the true answer grows to maintain the polylogarithmic error bound. For the adaptive MinSelect lower bound, the paper reduces from privately identifying signs of consensus columns in a matrix, showing that relative error provides no improvement over purely additive error in the adaptive setting.

## Results

The paper establishes nearly tight error bounds for four continual release tasks (MaxSum, MinSum, MaxSelect, MinSelect) under differential privacy with a relative error term γ. For MaxSum, MinSum, MaxSelect, and non-adaptive MinSelect, the additive error is min{1/(γε), d/ε, √(T/ε), T} under pure DP (and the analogous √γ-scaled version under approximate DP), which is polylog(d,T) when γ is constant — a dramatic improvement over the exponential purely additive error of Jain et al. (2023). The headline separation result is that for MinSelect with adaptive inputs, the 1/(γε) term disappears, leaving min{d/ε, √(T/ε), T}, meaning relative error provides no improvement and the error matches the purely additive lower bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the purely additive error bounds of Jain et al. (2023), who proved that MaxSum and MaxSelect in the continual release model require exponentially large additive error (e.g., Ω(√T/ε) vs. O(1/ε) in the batch model). This paper shows that introducing a constant relative error γ reduces the additive error to polylog(d,T) for MaxSum, MinSum, MaxSelect, and non-adaptive MinSelect, effectively closing the exponential gap. However, for MinSelect with adaptively chosen inputs, the paper proves (Theorem 4.3) that the additive error remains min{d/ε, √(T/ε), T} regardless of γ, matching the purely additive lower bound of Jain et al. (2023) and establishing a fundamental separation between non-adaptive and adaptive streams. The lower bounds (Theorems B.2, B.4, 4.3) nearly match the upper bounds up to polylogarithmic factors, confirming the tightness of the results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the continual release framework of Dwork et al. (2010) and Chan et al. (2011), and most immediately builds on Jain et al. (2023), who proved that purely additive error in continual release can be exponentially worse than in the batch model for MaxSum and MaxSelect. The key departure is incorporating a relative error term into the accuracy guarantee, motivated by recent work on relative-error DP (Epasto et al., 2023; Ghazi et al., 2023, 2025; Aamand et al., 2026), to circumvent the additive-error barrier. The paper also draws on the sequential embedding construction of Jain et al. (2023) for lower bounds and adapts a potential-analysis technique from Asi et al. (2023a) to handle the non-monotone nature of MinSelect.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper answers an open question raised by Jain et al. (2023) by identifying MinSelect as a task where non-adaptive and adaptive streams are fundamentally separated under relative error: polylogarithmic additive error is achievable for non-adaptive inputs, but no improvement over purely additive error is possible for adaptive inputs. This separation has broader implications for understanding when adaptivity truly matters in private streaming. Polylogarithmic gaps between upper and lower bounds remain, and it is open whether similar relative-error improvements extend to other continual release tasks or to settings with negative inputs.
