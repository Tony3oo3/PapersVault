## Problem

Prevailing model merging methods aggregate task vectors via linear arithmetic in Euclidean space, which destroys intrinsic geometric properties of pretrained weights such as hyperspherical energy and spectral norm, leading to destructive interference and catastrophic forgetting. No existing approach performs merging on the Riemannian manifold of the orthogonal group, where the geometric structure of the weights is naturally preserved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265415/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base model weight matrix W₀ and N task-specific finetuned weight matrices (from OFT, LoRA, or full finetuning) and produces a single merged weight matrix. For OFT models, the explicit orthogonal adapter matrices are directly available; for non-OFT models, the method extracts an implicit orthogonal component per task by solving the Orthogonal Procrustes problem via SVD on W_i^target W₀ᵀ. The final output is structured as W_final = R_merged · W₀ + ρ_merged, where R_merged is a valid orthogonal matrix and ρ_merged is a standard Euclidean-space residual.

## Method

OrthoMerge performs merging within the Lie algebra so(d) of the orthogonal group: each task's orthogonal matrix R_i is mapped to a skew-symmetric generator Q_i via the inverse Cayley transform, the Q_i are averaged with a magnitude-correction factor c = (Σ‖Q_i‖_F) / ‖ΣQ_i‖_F to prevent norm collapse from destructive interference, and the result is mapped back to the orthogonal group via the Cayley transform. For non-OFT models, an Orthogonal-Residual Decoupling strategy first extracts the orthogonal component R_i by solving the Procrustes problem (with either a Global target using the full finetuned weight or a Conflict-Aware target that isolates neuron-level updates opposing the consensus direction), merges the R_i on the manifold as above, computes residuals ρ_i = W_i − R_i W₀, merges those in Euclidean space using standard methods (TA, TIES, TSV-M), and combines both branches additively. This hybrid design preserves the spectral norm and hyperspherical energy of the base model while retaining the plasticity of additive merging for task-specific adjustments.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B with OFT-finetuned models, OrthoMerge achieves 46.25% average accuracy across five task-specific benchmarks (MATH500, HumanEval+, ScienceQA, CommonsenseQA, Social-IQA), surpassing the best Euclidean baseline TSV-M (44.97%) and the individual task-specific model average (44.71%). On out-of-domain generalization (M-ARC + AGIEval), OrthoMerge reaches 41.80% average, outperforming all baselines (best: TA at 40.78%). When extended to non-OFT models via Orthogonal-Residual Decoupling on Llama-3.2-3B, the Conflict-Aware variant boosts TIES by +2.41% (39.16→41.57) and TSV-M by +0.55% (41.52→42.07) on task-specific benchmarks while also improving out-of-domain scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OrthoMerge consistently outperforms all four Euclidean-space baselines on both in-domain and out-of-domain metrics. On in-domain tasks (OFT models, Llama-3.1-8B), OrthoMerge (46.25%) beats TSV-M (44.97%) by +1.28%, TIES (44.61%) by +1.64%, TA (41.46%) by +4.79%, and DARE (40.88%) by +5.37%. On out-of-domain generalization, OrthoMerge (41.80%) surpasses TA (40.78%) by +1.02%, DARE (40.78%) by +1.02%, TSV-M (40.48%) by +1.32%, and TIES (40.43%) by +1.37%. Notably, OrthoMerge exceeds the individual task-specific model average (44.71%), indicating constructive synergy rather than destructive interference. For non-OFT models (Llama-3.2-3B, Table 2), the Orthogonal-Residual Decoupling extension augments existing baselines: TIES+OrthoMerge-C improves TIES by +2.41% (39.16→41.57) and TSV-M+OrthoMerge-C improves TSV-M by +0.55% (41.52→42.07), while also recovering MMLU scores that standard merging degrades (e.g., TIES drops MMLU from 55.00 to 54.52, but TIES+OrthoMerge-C recovers to 55.36). On vision-language models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Table 3), TIES+OrthoMerge-C achieves the highest task-specific average (64.04%) and TSV-M+OrthoMerge-G improves IFEval by +2.96% over vanilla TSV-M, confirming modality-agnostic effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Orthogonal Finetuning (OFT), which parameterizes task-specific adaptations as orthogonal transformations, and on the broader model merging literature (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, DARE, TSV-M) that operates via linear arithmetic on task vectors in Euclidean space. The key departure is shifting the merging operation from Euclidean addition to the Riemannian manifold of the orthogonal group: task-specific rotations are mapped to the Lie algebra so(d), merged with a magnitude-correction factor to counteract destructive interference, and mapped back via the Cayley transform. For non-OFT models, the Orthogonal-Residual Decoupling strategy (solving the Procrustes problem) extracts an implicit orthogonal component, enabling the same manifold-based merging to apply to LoRA and full-finetuning models without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OrthoMerge offers a principled geometric alternative to the dominant Euclidean merging paradigm, with the practical benefit of preserving hyperspherical energy and spectral norms that are known to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. The magnitude-corrected averaging in the Lie algebra is a simple but effective fix for the norm collapse that plagues naive averaging of conflicting task updates. Open questions include whether the Procrustes-based orthogonal extraction remains meaningful as the number of merged tasks grows very large, how the method interacts with more complex adapter architectures (e.g., MoE-based adapters), and whether the modest 1–3% gains over strong baselines like TSV-M persist at larger model scales or with more heterogeneous task distributions.
