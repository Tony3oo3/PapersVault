## Problem

Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) effectively compresses multi-step diffusion/flow teachers into few-step students, but its reverse-KL objective is mode-seeking and systematically reduces sample diversity under aggressive step reduction. Existing remedies—perceptual (LPIPS) or adversarial (GAN) regularization—introduce memory overhead, training instability, and scalability challenges. The paper asks whether diversity can be preserved through loss design alone, without any external regularizer.

## Input / Output

The distilled student takes a text prompt and a random noise vector as input and produces a single 1024×1024 RGB image in just 4 NFEs (versus 60 for SD3.5-M or 100 for SDXL). All training and inference computations are performed in the latent space of the underlying VAE, and the method is image-free: it requires no stored teacher-generated reference images during training. The teacher's classifier-free guidance scale is fixed (3.5 for SD3.5-M, 8.0 for SDXL) and inherited by the student.

## Method

DP-DMD exploits the observation that early denoising steps establish global image structure (diversity) while later steps refine local perceptual details (quality). The first distillation step is supervised by a teacher-derived target-prediction loss: the teacher is rolled out K steps from the same initial noise to obtain an intermediate latent z_tilde, and the student's first-step velocity (or denoised latent, for diffusion models) is regressed toward the corresponding target via an L2 loss. The remaining N−1 steps are trained with the standard DMD loss using teacher and auxiliary 'fake' model scores in a perturbed space. Critically, gradients from the DMD loss are detached after the first step, preventing the mode-seeking reverse-KL signal from back-propagating into and overriding the diversity-preserving mapping. The full objective is simply ℓ = ℓ_DMD + λ·ℓ_Div with λ = 0.05, requiring no perceptual loss, adversarial discriminator, additional modules, or teacher-generated reference images.

## Results

On Pick-a-Pic with SD3.5-M (4 NFEs), DP-DMD achieves DINO diversity of 0.179 and CLIP diversity of 0.182, substantially outperforming vanilla DMD (0.137/0.133) while maintaining near-teacher quality (VQ-R1 4.646 vs. teacher 4.657; MIQA 1.017 vs. 1.020). On COCO-10K 2014, the diversity gain is even more pronounced: DP-DMD reaches DINO 0.250 vs. DMD's 0.210, approaching the 60-NFE teacher's 0.288. Preference scores remain competitive (PicS 21.76 vs. DMD 21.75; ImgR 1.142 vs. DMD 1.189), confirming that diversity is recovered without sacrificing perceptual quality or text-image alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla DMD, DP-DMD recovers substantial diversity (DINO 0.179 vs. 0.137 on Pick-a-Pic; 0.250 vs. 0.210 on COCO-10K) while keeping quality essentially unchanged (VQ-R1 4.646 vs. 4.649; MIQA 1.017 vs. 1.016) and matching preference (PicS 21.76 vs. 21.75), though it trails slightly on ImageReward (1.142 vs. 1.189). Compared to DMD-LPIPS, DP-DMD wins on every metric: higher diversity (DINO 0.179 vs. 0.169; CLIP 0.182 vs. 0.169), better quality (VQ-R1 4.646 vs. 4.598; MIQA 1.017 vs. 1.005), and stronger preference (ImgR 1.142 vs. 1.063; PicS 21.76 vs. 21.62). Against DMD-GAN, DP-DMD trades a marginal DINO diversity gap (0.179 vs. 0.183) for clearly superior quality (VQ-R1 4.646 vs. 4.525; MIQA 1.017 vs. 0.984) and preference (ImgR 1.142 vs. 1.033; PicS 21.76 vs. 21.63), and avoids the training instability of adversarial optimization. On the SDXL backbone, the same pattern holds: DP-DMD leads all DMD variants in diversity (DINO 0.173 vs. 0.109–0.136) while remaining within ~0.08 of the teacher's quality scores. In the practical comparison (Table 2), DP-DMD matches or exceeds TDM on diversity and preference at 4 NFEs, and outperforms Flash Diffusion on diversity (DINO 0.162 vs. 0.184 is a loss, but CLIP 0.181 vs. 0.172 is a win) while maintaining comparable quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DP-DMD extends the DMD framework (Yin et al., CVPR 2024; NeurIPS 2024) by identifying that its reverse-KL objective is mode-seeking and degrades sample diversity under aggressive step reduction. Rather than following the dominant remedy of adding perceptual (LPIPS) or adversarial (GAN) regularizers, DP-DMD introduces a role-separated loss assignment: the first distillation step is supervised by a teacher-derived v-prediction target to preserve global structural diversity, while remaining steps use standard DMD for quality refinement, with gradient stopping to prevent the two objectives from interfering. This positions the work as a minimal, image-free alternative to both trajectory-based distillation (consistency models, Hyper-SD) and adversarial distillation (Flash, ADD), drawing on the well-known stage-wise denoising behavior of diffusion/flow models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that DP-DMD offers a drop-in, stable training recipe for few-step distillation that avoids the tuning sensitivity and memory overhead of GAN or LPIPS regularizers, making it attractive for iterative model development where teacher checkpoints are frequently updated. The paper notes that diversity-relevant decisions may not always be confined to the first step, and suggests adaptive role separation (dynamic anchor, weight, and gradient routing) as a natural extension. An open question is whether the fixed first-step supervision remains sufficient for very aggressive step budgets (e.g., 1–2 NFEs) or for video generation where temporal diversity adds another dimension.
