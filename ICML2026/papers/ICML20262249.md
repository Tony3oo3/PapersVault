## Problem

The Strong Expressive Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (SELTH) established that sparse subnetworks preserving 1-WL expressivity exist for static, uni-relational GNNs, but this result did not extend to multi-relational or temporal settings where parameter counts are substantially larger. How parameter sparsity interacts with expressivity in relational GNNs (RGNNs) and temporal GNNs (TGNNs) was poorly understood, leaving no formal guarantee that random pruning can preserve the discriminative power needed for downstream tasks in these richer graph domains.

## Input / Output

The method operates on finite collections of directed, node-labeled, multi-relational graphs, temporal graph snapshot sequences (with strictly increasing timestamps), and molecular graphs paired with abstraction structures (e.g., junction trees). It produces a sparse subnetwork (via a Bernoulli pruning mask) that preserves 1-RWL distinguishability on the input collection, together with an explicit probability lower bound γ_RGNN expressed in terms of network width, sparsity ratio, minimum input separation, and dataset complexity. For temporal and cross-graph settings, the input is first transformed into an augmented directed multi-relational knowledge graph or compound graph, after which the same pruning and expressivity-preservation machinery applies.

## Method

The paper proves Relational SELTH (RSELTH): for any finite collection of directed multi-relational graphs, a sufficiently overparameterized RGNN contains sparse subnetworks (obtained by i.i.d. Bernoulli pruning) that preserve 1-RWL expressivity with probability at least γ_RGNN > 0, and derives an explicit closed-form lower bound on this probability in terms of width, sparsity, input separation, and architectural parameters. The key mechanism is showing that each per-relation, per-direction message MLP and the combine MLP remain injective on the finite set of inputs witnessed by the dataset under random pruning, with the probability composed across all branches and layers via union bounds. For TGNNs, the paper constructs an algebraically identical RGNN on an augmented knowledge graph (encoding time gaps as edge features or refining the relation alphabet), so RSELTH transfers directly; for XIMP/HIMP, a compound multi-relational graph G^⊕ is built whose relational layers simulate cross-graph message passing. The paper further shows that irreducible task-expressive masks (minimal masks preserving exactly the label-relevant distinctions) are optimizable under small-step first-order updates, connecting expressivity preservation to gradient flow and training dynamics.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with empirical validation of the derived probability bound γ_RGNN. On a synthetic multi-relational dataset of 30 graphs, the theoretical bound is tight in the high-success regime (ρ ≲ 0.65) and becomes most conservative in the transition region (ρ ∈ [0.65, 0.95]), where the standard deviation of the empirical distinguishable-graph fraction is 0.18. Pre-training separability (fraction of graphs receiving distinct embeddings before training) shows significant Spearman correlations (p < 0.05) with mean gradient flow, final training loss, and final training accuracy, both across varying sparsity (Figure 2b) and at fixed sparsity (Figure 3). On real benchmarks (tgbn-trade node classification, polaris ADMET HLM and pIC50 MERS-CoV Mpro regression), separability of untrained pruned models correlates positively with normalized test performance and negatively with compression ratio across 200 sparsity levels (Figure 5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing methods in a standard benchmark sense; it is a theoretical contribution (RSELTH) with empirical validation of its own derived bounds. The key empirical 'comparisons' are: (1) the theoretical lower bound γ_RGNN versus the empirically measured mask success rate γ_empirical on synthetic data, showing the bound is tight at low and high sparsity but conservative near the critical threshold; (2) pre-training separability versus post-training optimization metrics (gradient flow, loss, accuracy) under both variable and fixed sparsity, confirming that expressivity-preserving masks are easier to optimize; and (3) on real temporal and molecular benchmarks, separability of pruned models versus test performance and compression, demonstrating that expressivity-aware sparsification predicts which pruned subnetworks will be winning tickets. The paper positions its results as extending Kummer et al. (2025b)'s SELTH (which covers only static uni-relational graphs) to multi-relational, temporal, and cross-graph settings, with the original SELTH recovered as the special case |R|=1 with no directions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Strong Expressive Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (SELTH) of Kummer et al. (2025b), which proved the existence of sparse subnetworks preserving 1-WL expressivity on static uni-relational graphs, into a probabilistic guarantee (RSELTH) for the broader 1-RWL framework. By leveraging Wałęga & Rawson (2025)'s RWL characterizations of temporal message passing and the relational WL formalism of Barceló et al. (2022), the authors show that local/global TGNNs and cross-graph MP architectures (XIMP/HIMP) admit RGNN reformulations, so a single theorem covers all three settings. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the shift from a purely existential statement to an explicit lower bound on the probability that a random pruning mask preserves expressivity, with the bound's dependence on width, sparsity, and dataset complexity made concrete.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal expressivity guarantee for sparse temporal GNNs and cross-graph MP architectures, filling a gap where prior pruning work (GLTs, graph LTH) addressed prediction quality but not discriminative power. The practical implication is a concrete width-sparsity-compute trade-off: the bound reveals a narrow band of configurations yielding cost-effective, highly expressive sparse initializations, and the empirical results link pre-training separability to gradient flow and training quality even at fixed sparsity. Limitations include the conservatism of the union-bound-based guarantee, empirical validation on small synthetic datasets and modest real benchmarks, and the correlational (not causal) link between expressivity and downstream prediction. Open questions include whether the narrow width-sparsity band persists at scale, whether the optimization results extend beyond small-step first-order methods, and whether the bound can be tightened to reduce the gap between theoretical and empirical success rates in the critical transition region.
