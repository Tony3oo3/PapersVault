## Problem

Approximating an unknown probability measure by a mixture of parametric distributions requires simultaneously determining the mixture order, estimating component parameters, and choosing the mixing weights — yet existing methods either fix the order a priori, assume a finite parameter grid, or rely on non-convex optimization (EM, MLE) that is sensitive to initialization and prone to local optima. No convex framework existed that could handle a general compact semi-algebraic parameter space while providing convergence guarantees and recovering the mixture order from the optimization itself.

## Input / Output

The method consumes finitely many empirical moments of the target measure (up to order 2d), a parametric family whose moments are polynomial in the parameters (e.g., Gaussian, Poisson, exponential), a compact semi-algebraic set describing the admissible parameter space, and a user-chosen distance (W₂ or TV) with a polynomial regularizer R and strength ε. It outputs the mixture order K (read from the rank of the optimal moment matrix), K component parameter estimates, mixing weights, and the full optimal mixing measure. Notably, the input is moment data rather than raw samples, and the mixture order is an emergent property of the SDP solution rather than a user-specified hyperparameter.

## Method

The paper reformulates the mixture approximation as a generalized moment problem (GMP) in which the mixing measure φ over a compact semi-algebraic set S_θ is the decision variable, then applies a Lasserre-type Moment-SOS SDP hierarchy. The key structural observation is that for standard parametric families, the moments of the induced mixture are polynomial functions of the parameters, so the moment-matching constraints become polynomial equalities in the moments of φ, directly amenable to SDP lifting via moment and localizing matrices. For the W₂ case, the SDP couples the target measure's moments with the mixture's moments through a joint coupling measure; for the TV case, the Hahn-Jordan decomposition of μ − ν_φ yields measure-domination constraints (ψ₊ ≤ μ, ψ₋ ≤ ν_φ) that translate into nested PSD matrix inequalities. A polynomial regularization term on the moments of φ improves numerical conditioning and promotes low-rank (sparse) solutions. The hierarchy converges asymptotically to the optimal value, and when a flat-extension rank condition holds at some order d, convergence is finite and a K-atomic optimal mixing measure is recovered via a linear-algebraic extraction procedure (Cholesky factorization, multiplication matrices, Schur decomposition).

## Results

On randomly generated 2D Gaussian mixtures (K=5, N=1000, d=4), Algorithm 1-based initialization reduces k-means iterations by 31.8% (W₂) and 36.5% (TV) compared to random initialization, and reduces EM iterations by 18.63% (W₂) and 13.91% (TV). On MNIST (2 PCA components), the method reaches the same k-means objective (≈4.136×10² for digits {0,1}; ≈6.734×10² for {0,1,2}) while requiring fewer iterations than the 7.28 and 11.6 average iterations needed by random initialization. SDP assembly and solving takes 0.1755 s (W₂) or 0.1506 s (TV) on average, with point extraction requiring only 0.00036 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its SDP-based initialization (Algorithm 1) against random initialization for two standard clustering algorithms: k-means (Arthur & Vassilvitskii, 2007) and EM for GMMs (Dempster et al., 1977). For k-means on K=5 Gaussian mixtures, W₂-based initialization yields 31.8% fewer iterations and TV-based yields 36.5% fewer iterations than random, with W₂-based being faster in ~90% of runs. For EM, W₂-based initialization gives a 18.63% improvement over random, outperforming the TV-based 13.91% improvement, suggesting EM benefits more from W₂-derived means. On MNIST, both initialization strategies reach the identical k-means objective as random initialization but with fewer iterations, confirming that the SDP method provides a principled, distance-aware seeding that accelerates convergence without sacrificing solution quality. The method also outperforms EM-based GMM fitting in the illustration (Figure 2), where SDP-recovered mixtures (K=4) are smoother and achieve zero W₂/TV distance to the target, whereas EM-based fits with BIC/CV-selected order produce less accurate approximations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Lasserre's 2024 work on W2-optimal Gaussian mixtures and his 2025 TV-distance hierarchy to a unified framework covering multiple parametric families (Gaussian, Poisson, exponential) with general compact semi-algebraic parameter sets, rather than a single family. The core SDP machinery (moment matrices, localizing matrices, flat extension) is inherited from the Lasserre (2009) Moment-SOS framework, while the minimizer extraction procedure follows Curto-Fialkow and Henrion-Lasserre. A key departure from the broader mixture-modeling literature (EM, NPMLE, method of moments) is that the mixing measure is treated as a free decision variable over a semi-algebraic set rather than being assumed K-atomic a priori, and the Hahn-Jordan decomposition is used to derive tractable TV relaxations—a device the authors note has not been explicitly exploited in prior mixture-model TV formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first globally optimal, convex-relaxation-based approach to simultaneously determining mixture order and estimating parameters without fixing K or assuming a finite parameter grid, with finite convergence certificates under a rank condition. However, scalability is the principal limitation: PSD constraint sizes grow combinatorially with dimension and relaxation order, restricting practical use to low-dimensional problems (the experiments use n=1 or n=2). Open questions include non-asymptotic convergence rates as a function of sample size, which distance (W2 vs. TV) is preferable for which problem class, and whether family-specific structure (symmetry, sliced formulations) can mitigate the dimensionality bottleneck.
