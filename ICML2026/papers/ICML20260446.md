## Problem

It remains unclear whether the local geometry of gradient-based (first-order) parameter updates can inherently guarantee logical coherence, i.e., whether updating a fact automatically and consistently updates its logical consequences such as negations, converses, and compositions. The Linear Propagation Assumption (LPA), which underpins knowledge editing, continual learning, and unlearning methods, presumes this coherence but its geometric validity is untested. This paper formalizes the question and proves that the LPA faces fundamental structural obstructions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LLM parameters (restricted to the last Transformer block and LM head for computational and semantic relevance) together with factual–negated query pairs from the TREX subset of NEGATED LAMA (410 cloze-style instances across 41 templates). It produces three types of output: (1) empirical cosine-similarity scores between gradient vectors of a fact and its negation, (2) a characterization of the tensor-product feature geometry required for logical equivariance, and (3) a structural impossibility result showing that any bilinear conjunction operator compatible with negation equivariance must be the zero map. The analysis operates entirely in the NTK linearized regime, treating each query's score gradient as its feature vector in parameter space.

## Method

The paper formalizes relational knowledge using relation algebra (negation, converse, composition) and defines Systematic Linear Propagation (SLP) as direction-agnostic logical equivariance on gradient features: for all update directions in the feature subspace, negation must flip scores and converse must preserve them. Applying Tarski's invariance criterion, the authors construct a product group H = Sym(E) × Z₂ acting on queries and prove via representation theory that SLP forces a blockwise tensor factorization W ≅ ⊕ᵢ(Cᵢ ⊗ Rᵢ) separating entity-pair context from relation content (Theorem 1), with converse further requiring matched symmetric/antisymmetric parity (Theorem 2). For composition, the paper isolates the unique-witness case where it reduces to conjunction, proves that kernel stability under LPA forces the conjunction operator to be bilinear (Lemma 2), and then shows bilinearity is incompatible with negation equivariance: idempotence on ¬p gives F(−u,−u) = −u while bilinearity gives F(−u,−u) = +u, forcing u = 0 (Theorem 3). Empirically, the authors validate the geometric mismatch by measuring gradient alignment on Qwen3-4B/30B and OLMo3-7B, finding strong positive alignment (~0.85) where theory demands anti-alignment (−1), and confirm the first-order approximation holds for step sizes up to 10⁻².

## Results

The paper's primary empirical finding is that gradients of factual queries and their negations in current LLMs are strongly positively aligned (mean cosine similarity ≈ 0.85 for Qwen3-4B, ≈ 0.86 for Qwen3-30B, ≈ 0.62 for OLMo-3-7B), directly contradicting the theoretical requirement of anti-alignment (cosine similarity = −1) for negation-consistent linear propagation. The first-order linearized approximation is validated as accurate for small-to-moderate step sizes (R² > 0.99 for η ≤ 10⁻²), confirming the practical relevance of the NTK regime. The central theoretical contribution is an impossibility result (Theorem 3): any bilinear conjunction operator on linearized features that satisfies idempotence is incompatible with negation equivariance, forcing all feature vectors to collapse to zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-to-method comparisons; it does not propose a new editing or reasoning method to benchmark against baselines. Instead, it establishes structural impossibility results under the Linear Propagation Assumption. The empirical validation compares the observed gradient geometry in three LLMs (Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-30B, OLMo-3-7B) against the theoretical prediction of anti-alignment required by negation equivariance (Definition 2). All three models fail this test, with gradients showing strong positive alignment rather than the required negative alignment, indicating that the geometric prerequisite for systematic linear propagation is violated across model scales and architectures. The paper positions its findings as a structural diagnosis explaining why existing knowledge editing methods (ROME, MEMIT) and multi-hop reasoning approaches consistently fail to propagate updates to logical consequences, rather than as a comparison of competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes and stress-tests the Linear Propagation Assumption (LPA) that underlies prominent knowledge editing methods (ROME, MEMIT) and continual learning strategies, using the NTK linearized regime as its geometric framework. It draws on relation algebra (Givant, 2006) to formalize logical operations and on Tarski's invariance criterion to identify entity-renaming symmetries, then applies finite group representation theory to derive necessary structural constraints on gradient features. The work connects to the classical systematicity debate (Fodor & Pylyshyn, 1988; Smolensky, 1990) by showing that tensor-product factorization is not merely an architectural choice for variable binding but a geometric necessity for logical coherence under first-order updates, and aligns with geometric deep learning's equivariance-based perspective (Bronstein et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility result (Theorem 3) provides a structural explanation—rather than an optimization explanation—for why knowledge editing fails to propagate to compositional consequences, unifying previously separate empirical failures (reversal curse, multi-hop breakdown, negation inconsistency) under a single geometric obstruction. This shifts the design question from 'where is knowledge stored?' to 'does the representation geometry support structure-preserving updates?', motivating equivariant parameterizations and iterative non-linear editing mechanisms. Key limitations include the local (single-step) scope of the analysis, the strong direction-agnostic requirement, and the open question of which conclusions persist under approximate equivariance or in the lazy-training NTK regime over multiple steps.
