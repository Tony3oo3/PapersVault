## Problem

Existing text-to-video diffusion models output only a single composited RGB video, lacking the editable layered representations (foreground, background, alpha matte) that professional compositing workflows require. Prior layered-generation methods either produce a single RGBA foreground without explicit background modeling or, in the multi-layer case (e.g., LayerFlow), suffer from limited cross-layer coherence due to data scarcity and the absence of explicit inter-layer interaction modeling.

## Input / Output

The model takes three independent text prompts (full-video, foreground, background) encoded separately by T5 to prevent semantic leakage, and produces in a single inference pass a full composited video, an independent background layer, one or more foreground RGB layers (stored as premultiplied alpha: RGB × mask), and corresponding alpha mattes. All layers are spatially and temporally aligned at 672×384 resolution over 41 frames, and the foreground is encoded after premultiplication so that the VAE sees a single-channel-compatible signal. The multi-foreground variant extends this to up to three independent foreground-mask pairs serialized along the temporal axis.

## Method

The core insight is that the high temporal and spatial compression of modern video VAEs makes it feasible to concatenate the latent codes of all layers along the temporal dimension (yielding a 4T′-long sequence for four layers) and jointly denoise them on a shared Rectified Flow trajectory, turning cross-layer consistency into an intrinsic generation objective rather than a post-hoc constraint. To resolve the resulting layer-identity ambiguity, the shared DiT backbone is augmented with two modules: LayerAdaLN, which injects per-layer shift/scale/gate vectors into the timestep modulation at every block, and Layered Cross-Attention, which applies a binary visibility mask so that each layer's visual tokens attend only to its own text-prompt embeddings. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) LoRA fine-tuning of the frozen Wan VAE decoder to reconstruct alpha masks, (2) joint multi-layer Flow Matching with auxiliary compositing-consistency and mask-reconstruction losses, and (3) multi-foreground extension by serializing additional foreground-mask pairs and continuing training. A shared Gaussian noise sample across all layers further encourages coherent evolution, and the compositing loss explicitly enforces that the decoded foreground plus masked background reconstructs the full video.

## Results

On 200 held-out VidLayer prompts evaluated with VBench, LayerT2V (VAE LoRA) achieves the best scores across all five dimensions and all three layers (FG, BG, BL). The largest gains over LayerFlow appear in Subject Consistency (e.g., FG: 0.9829 vs. 0.9440) and Text Alignment (e.g., BG: 0.2307 vs. 0.1849), while Aesthetic Quality on the full video improves from 0.4895 to 0.5391. In the user study, LayerT2V is preferred 72.4% of the time for Aesthetic Quality, 76.8% for Foreground Quality, and 66.8% for Text Alignment, compared to 12.0%, 15.6%, and 13.6% for LayerFlow.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is LayerFlow (Ji et al., SIGGRAPH 2025), the closest prior work that also serializes layers along the temporal axis. LayerT2V (VAE LoRA) wins on 14 out of 15 VBench sub-metrics; the single exception is Temporal Flickering on the BG layer (0.9830 vs. 0.9736 for LayerFlow), where LayerFlow actually edges ahead by 0.009. The most substantial margins are in Subject Consistency (up to +0.039 on FG) and Text Alignment (up to +0.046 on BG), reflecting the benefit of LayerAdaLN for layer separation and LayeredCrossAttention for prompt routing. In the user study, LayerT2V is preferred over LayerFlow by a wide margin (72–77% vs. 12–16%), confirming that the quantitative gains translate into perceptual quality. The intermediate variant (Native Mask VAE) already surpasses LayerFlow on most metrics, isolating the VAE LoRA contribution as an additional ~0.01–0.05 boost on top of the architectural improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

LayerT2V builds directly on the Wan2.1-1.3B DiT video generation backbone, reusing its pretrained spatiotemporal modeling capacity by serializing multiple layer latents along the temporal axis—a trick also explored by LayerFlow (Ji et al., 2025). The key departure from LayerFlow is the introduction of explicit layer-identity conditioning (LayerAdaLN for per-layer feature modulation and LayeredCrossAttention for prompt routing), a compositing consistency loss, and a fine-tuned VAE for alpha masks, addressing the cross-layer ambiguity and conditional leakage that limit prior temporal-serialization approaches. Earlier layered generation work (Text2Layer, LayerDiffuse, TransPixeler, Wan-Alpha) targeted single RGBA foregrounds for images or videos without explicit background modeling or multi-foreground support, positioning LayerT2V as the first unified framework producing full video, background, and multiple foreground-alpha pairs jointly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of VidLayer (50K clips, ~4M frames with aligned multi-layer annotations and layer-specific prompts) fills a critical data gap that has constrained the field, and the layered outputs enable practical editing workflows (background swap, foreground replacement) without scene regeneration. The finding that 4D RoPE—intuitively the natural way to encode layer identity—actually degrades performance in favor of dedicated conditioning modules (LayerAdaLN) offers a useful design principle for multi-modal serialization in diffusion models. Open questions include scaling beyond three foreground layers, handling complex multi-foreground interactions and occlusions, and reducing inference cost (currently 157s for 4 layers at 672×384) to make the approach viable for interactive professional use.
