## Problem

Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) predominantly use channel-independent pre-training, modeling each variable in isolation and thereby overlooking critical inter-channel dependencies essential for multivariate forecasting. Existing remedies—parameter fine-tuning or retrieval-augmented inputs—require dataset-specific updates or costly retrieval, undermining the zero-shot generalization that makes TSFMs attractive. The paper asks: how can a lightweight, plug-and-play module inject multivariate correlations into frozen TSFMs in a truly zero-shot, architecture-agnostic manner?

## Input / Output

The plugin consumes two inputs: the frozen TSFM's prediction matrix (C×T′, where C ranges from 7 to 862 channels and T′ ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}) and the original lookback series X (C×T, T=96 or up to 3072) from which 7-dimensional domain statistics are extracted. It outputs a refined C×T′ forecast matrix. The module is horizon-agnostic (adaptive pooling into 16 bins handles variable T′) and channel-count-agnostic (sparse K-NN with adaptive neighborhood size), so the same 48K-parameter plugin works across Moirai 2.0, TimesFM, Chronos, and TTMs without architectural modification.

## Method

ChaTSFM follows a three-stage 'select–adapt–refine' pipeline. First, Generative Domain Selection represents each candidate dataset as a Gaussian proxy derived from an SDE drift-diffusion decomposition, then greedily curates a compact pre-training corpus by maximizing a joint informativeness (log-det entropy) and Wasserstein-2 diversity score. Second, a Geometric Hyper-Network maps the 7-dim domain statistics to a diagonal Mahalanobis metric (scale γ and shift β) that rectifies the channel-embedding space on the fly, correcting for domain-specific diffusion geometry—a necessity formalized by an impossibility theorem showing no fixed metric can remain accurate across heterogeneous domains. Third, Channel-Adapter Dependency Modeling constructs a sparse K-NN adjacency in the rectified space (with K itself predicted by the hyper-network), performs sparse message passing over the backbone forecasts, and fuses the result via a gated residual that injects inter-channel corrections only where the gate detects information gain, preserving the frozen TSFM's temporal priors elsewhere. The entire plugin is trained with MSE loss on the curated corpus and deployed zero-shot, requiring no dataset-specific parameter updates.

## Results

ChaTSFM achieves consistent zero-shot MSE improvements of 2.4%–22.5% (MAE 1.0%–19.7%) across four TSFM backbones (Moirai 2.0, TimesFM, Chronos, TTMs) on nine benchmarks, with the largest gains on strongly-coupled domains like Solar (22.5% MSE reduction) and ETTm1 (19.7%). In the full-shot adapter comparison, ChaTSFM outperforms all six baselines on every dataset, reducing Moirai 2.0's MSE from 0.515 to 0.228 on Solar (−55.7%) and from 0.950 to 0.621 on ETTm1 (−34.6%). The plugin requires only 48K trainable parameters (~190 KB in FP32), orders of magnitude smaller than the backbone TSFMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChaTSFM outperforms all six adapter baselines on every one of the nine evaluation datasets in the full-shot setting (Table 4). The margin is most pronounced on strongly-coupled, high-dimensional datasets: on Solar, ChaTSFM (MSE 0.228) beats the strongest baseline AdaPTS (0.271) by 15.9% and TTM (0.262) by 13.0%; on ETTm1, it leads TTM (0.687) by 9.6%. On weakly-coupled datasets like Exchange, the gap narrows to ~1–2% (0.321 vs. TTM's 0.224), reflecting limited inter-channel signal. In the zero-shot setting (Table 2), ChaTSFM is the only method that requires no dataset-specific parameter updates, whereas ICM, PCD, PEFT, Gen-PT, AdaPTS, and TTM all demand full-shot fine-tuning on the target data. The w/o Pre-train ablation (MSE 0.242 on Solar) still beats most baselines, confirming that the architectural inductive bias (sparse Top-K aggregation + gated residual) contributes independently of the pre-training corpus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of optimal transport, Riemannian geometry, and graph-based message passing, positioning itself against two established lines of TSFM adaptation: parameter-based fine-tuning (LoRA, prompt tuning, in-context learning) and input augmentation (retrieval, exogenous covariates). Unlike these approaches, ChaTSFM operates entirely on the frozen backbone's predictions as a zero-shot post-processing plugin, inferring a sparse inter-channel dependency graph from domain-adaptive Mahalanobis geometry rather than learning cross-channel weights through dataset-specific training. The SDE-based domain representation with Wasserstein-distance greedy curation and the hyper-network-generated metric rectification are novel conceptual contributions that repurpose ideas from stochastic process theory and information geometry for the time series setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 48K-parameter, architecture-agnostic plugin that consistently improves four major TSFMs across nine diverse domains without any dataset-specific fine-tuning lowers the barrier to multivariate forecasting for practitioners who cannot afford per-dataset adaptation. The theoretical result (Theorem 4.3) establishing that no fixed similarity metric can be accurate across heterogeneous diffusion geometries provides a principled justification for domain-adaptive metrics beyond empirical tuning. Open questions include whether the SDE-based domain representation is truly necessary versus simpler statistical summaries, how the method scales to extremely high-dimensional settings (thousands of channels), and whether the zero-shot guarantee degrades under distributional shift between the curated pre-training corpus and the target domain.
