## Problem

Existing 3D indoor scene generators break down in non-convex rooms with tightly coupled spatial constraints: data-driven models lack topological priors for long-horizon planning, iterative agents fragment semantics across refinement steps, and RL-based alignment locks in early decisions that degrade global coherence. No prior method can simultaneously maintain high asset density, physical validity, and semantic fidelity within irregular architectural boundaries — a regime the paper formalizes as Intricate Spatial Orchestration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265904/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single natural language design instruction (at coarse, medium, or fine granularity) and produces a structured Zone-Graph layout in JSON form, containing a zone inventory with asset catalogs, intra-zone spatial graphs encoding anchor and relational edges, inter-zone topology with adjacency relations, and a derived architecture boundary polygon with wall segments and normals. Each asset carries category, role (zone_anchor or satellite), 3D position, rotation, and size. The backbone is Qwen3-8B, and the entire pipeline requires a single inference pass (no iterative refinement or external physics engine).

## Method

ZoneMaestro reformulates layout synthesis as Zone-Graph Orchestration, factorizing generation into a bottom-up causal chain: zone contents → intra-zone spatial graphs → global topology → architecture boundary. Training follows an Alternating Spatial Alignment strategy that cycles twice between (i) Zone Reasoning Internalization, a coarse-to-fine SFT phase where the model learns to emit structured reasoning traces mirroring the design hierarchy, and (ii) Geometric Denoising via Zone-Aware GRPO (Z-GRPO), which applies a staged composite reward penalizing boundary violations (voxelized out-of-bounds volume), zone convex-hull intersections, and asset-asset collisions. The reciprocal distillation between phases — feeding RL-refined layouts back as SFT signals — prevents reward-hacking drift while preserving semantic diversity, resolving the density-safety trade-off that plagues pure RL or pure SFT baselines.

## Results

On the Zone-Scene-10K test set, ZoneMaestro achieves the lowest out-of-bounds volume (0.05) and collision volume (0.03) while generating 15.51 assets per scene, outperforming all baselines on aesthetics (8.21), geometry (8.33), semantics (8.04), and functionality (8.14). On the harder SCALE benchmark with non-convex boundaries, ZoneMaestro maintains OOB at 0.09 and collision at 0.04 while producing the highest asset density (23.35), compared to LayoutGPT's OOB surging to 6.17 and i-Design's collision reaching 4.00. ZoneMaestro ranks first in human preference (1.52 on SCALE, 1.57 on Zone-Scene-10K) and requires only a single inference call per scene versus 3–10.7 for agentic baselines, with a 98.7% success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZoneMaestro dominates all six baselines on both physical validity and semantic quality. Against data-driven methods, DiffuScene suffers the worst collision volume (0.68 on Zone-Scene-10K, 0.74 on SCALE) and lowest realism (3.21/3.36), while ReSpace keeps violations low but generates only 4.36 assets per scene, producing sparse, sterile layouts. Among agentic frameworks, LayoutGPT fails most dramatically on SCALE with OOB surging from 1.00 to 6.17, and i-Design collapses physically with collision volume reaching 4.00 on SCALE. Holodeck achieves the highest raw density (23.78 assets) but sacrifices validity (OOB 0.49) and functionality (5.46), while LayoutVLM shows weak zoning logic (structure 3.60) and moderate boundary errors. ZoneMaestro is the only method that simultaneously achieves near-zero violations (OOB ≤ 0.09, Col ≤ 0.04), high asset density (15.51–23.35), top quality scores across all GPT-4o-mini dimensions, first-place human preference, and single-pass inference, resolving the density-safety trade-off that all baselines fail to address.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant object-centric paradigm in 3D indoor scene generation by introducing a zone-level compositional abstraction (Zone-Graph) that decomposes layout synthesis into functional containers with intra-zone spatial graphs and inter-zone topology. It builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework and the InternScenes dataset, but repositions the training pipeline as an alternating cycle of supervised reasoning internalization and zone-aware geometric denoising, rather than the single-pass RL or multi-stage agentic refinement used by prior methods like ReSpace, LayoutGPT, and Holodeck. The SCALE benchmark extends existing evaluation protocols (InstructScene, MesaTask, M3DLayout) by explicitly targeting non-convex boundaries and dense inter-object coupling, which prior benchmarks largely ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The density-safety trade-off—where methods that generate rich, dense scenes produce physical violations, and methods that enforce validity produce sparse, sterile rooms—is a persistent bottleneck for embodied AI applications requiring realistic, navigable environments. The Zone-Graph abstraction and SCALE benchmark provide a structured path toward resolving this, though the approach is validated on a single 8B backbone and a synthetic benchmark constructed via image generation, leaving open whether the zone-level reasoning generalizes to outdoor scenes, dynamic environments, or real-world architectural diversity. The reliance on GPT-4o for both annotation and evaluation introduces a circularity risk that limits the strength of the quality claims.
