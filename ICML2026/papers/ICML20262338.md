## Problem

Neural networks encode multiple latent behavioral priors that interact to produce emergent behavior, yet no rigorous mathematical framework existed for characterizing when such subagents can coalesce into a stable, mutually beneficial composition versus when aggregation necessarily sacrifices some agent's welfare. This gap left alignment-relevant phenomena like the Waluigi effect (where reinforcing a benevolent persona induces its adversarial counterpart) without a principled theoretical explanation.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on abstract probability distributions over a finite outcome space (e.g., token vocabulary) with non-negative pooling weights summing to one, rather than on actual network parameters. Outputs include the compositional log-pool distribution, per-agent welfare gaps expressed in information-theoretic terms (entropy differences minus KL divergence), provable existence or impossibility certificates for unanimous compositions, and first-order alignment suppression bounds. A key structural constraint is that all distributions must be strictly positive on the outcome space, and the analysis is conducted at a fixed timestep with conditioning subsumed into the observation space.

## Method

The paper models each subagent as a probability distribution with epistemic utility W_i(o) = log P_i(o), justified by the cross-entropy training objective of autoregressive networks. Composition is defined via logarithmic opinion pooling, derived as the unique minimizer of weighted forward KL divergence and equivalently as the softmax of averaged log-utilities. The core technical contributions are sharp possibility frontiers: strict unanimous improvement is proven impossible for binary outcome spaces and under linear pooling, but constructively possible for |O| ≥ 3 under log pooling. Recursive stability properties (cloning invariance, continuity, openness of the strictly-unanimous set) are established, and tilt-based analysis rules out trivial duplication as a path to compositionality. Finally, the Waluigi effect is formalized: under a KL-budget constraint preserving compositional stability, amplifying an aligned persona (Luigi) necessarily increases the weight of an anti-aligned counterpart (Waluigi), and a manifest-then-suppress strategy provably achieves strictly greater first-order misalignment reduction than pure aligned-persona reinforcement.

## Results

The paper establishes a sharp possibility frontier for compositional agency: strict unanimous improvement is impossible for binary outcome spaces (Theorem 8) and under linear pooling (Theorem 10), but becomes possible for |O| ≥ 3 under logarithmic pooling (Theorem 9). The framework proves that the set of strictly unanimously decomposable distributions is open in the simplex topology (Theorem 17), that small tilts around a fixed pool cannot achieve strict unanimity (ruling out trivial duplication), and that certain non-uniform agent collections are fundamentally incompatible under any positive weights (Theorem 16). The headline alignment result (Theorem 21, Waluigi Shattering) proves that manifest-then-suppress of an antagonistic persona yields strictly greater first-order misalignment reduction than pure reinforcement of the benevolent persona alone, under a fixed KL budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The central theoretical contrast is between logarithmic pooling and linear pooling: the paper proves that linear pooling (equivalent to random dictatorship) can never yield a strictly unanimously beneficial composition (Theorem 10), while logarithmic pooling admits such compositions when the outcome space has at least three elements (Theorem 9). A second key contrast is between binary and multi-outcome spaces: under epistemic (log-score) welfare, the binary case is a zero-sum tension where at most one agent can strictly benefit (Theorem 8), whereas the multi-outcome case permits all agents to simultaneously improve. The Waluigi Shattering theorem (Theorem 21) provides a first-order comparison between two alignment strategies — pure Luigi reinforcement versus manifest-then-suppress Waluigi — showing the latter achieves strictly greater suppression of misaligned outcomes under the same KL-budget constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical opinion pooling framework (Stone, 1961) and the active-inference PGM formulation of agency (Friston et al., 2017) to formalize a new notion of compositional agency for neural networks, using log-score epistemic utility (Good, 1952) as the welfare function motivated by cross-entropy training objectives. The aggregation machinery—logarithmic pooling as the softmax of averaged log-utilities—draws directly from welfare economics (Harsanyi, 1955) and proper scoring rules, but the authors explicitly note that stability and unanimity conditions for such compositions have not previously been formalized for neural agents. The framework also connects to products of experts (Hinton, 2002), neural ensembles (Hansen & Salamon, 1990), and multi-head attention (Vaswani et al., 2017) as concrete architectures where log-pooling arises naturally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first rigorous theoretical framework for analyzing when latent subagents in a neural probability model can form unanimously beneficial compositions, providing sharp possibility/impossibility boundaries (binary vs. multi-outcome, linear vs. logarithmic pooling) that were previously unaddressed. The Waluigi shattering theorem—that manifest-then-suppress of an adversarial persona strictly outperforms pure benevolent reinforcement at first order—offers a concrete, counterintuitive alignment strategy, though it remains entirely theoretical with no empirical validation on actual models. Key open questions include whether witnessing decompositions correspond to identifiable mechanistic circuits, whether the first-order linearization captures higher-order effects in practice, and whether the framework extends to continuous or hierarchical outcome spaces.
