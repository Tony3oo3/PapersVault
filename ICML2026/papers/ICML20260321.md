## Problem

Continual pre-training (CPT) is a standard stage in modern LLM pipelines for domain specialization, yet its internal mechanisms are far less understood than those of supervised fine-tuning. In particular, it remains unclear how weight matrices change spectrally during CPT, whether attention heads adapt uniformly or heterogeneously across domains, and whether CPT checkpoints from different domains can be combined via linear interpolation without quality loss.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes pairs of model checkpoints (pre-train and post-CPT) from the OLMo 2 family at 1B, 7B, 13B, and 32B parameter scales, decomposing every attention head projection (W^Q, W^K, W^V, W^O) and MLP matrix (W^FC1, W^Gate, W^FC2) into its SVD. Outputs include per-head singular value spectra with vector agreement overlays, a scalar importance score per head (difference in scaled Frobenius norms between target and reference CPT deltas), rewound checkpoints where low-importance heads are reverted to pre-train weights, and linearly interpolated 'model soup' checkpoints parameterized by ω ∈ [0,1]. All quality assessments are reported via OLMES benchmark scores (GSM8K, MATH-500, ARC-Easy, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, DROP, SQuAD, HumanEval).

## Method

The paper performs a systematic SVD-based spectral analysis of weight matrices along pre-train and CPT trajectories, revealing three key phenomena. First, CPT leaves singular value spectra nearly invariant while adaptation is driven by singular vector rotations localized near spectral peaks; attention head spectra develop complex multi-peak structures during pre-train that deviate from Marchenko-Pastur and heavy-tailed self-regularization models. Second, the authors identify head heterogeneity—some heads change substantially across all CPT domains while others change in a domain-specific manner—and propose a head importance criterion s_{l,h} based on the difference in min-max-normalized Frobenius norms of CPT deltas between a target domain and a text reference domain, enabling up to 4% quality gain by rewinding ~15% of heads and up to 60% head removal without measurable loss. Third, they demonstrate domain connectivity: linear interpolation between CPT checkpoints on different domains yields smooth quality interpolation that transitions from concave to convex as pre-train token budget and model size increase, though it still underperforms direct training on mixed data.

## Results

The paper's headline quantitative findings are: (1) selectively rewinding low-importance attention heads (ranked by the proposed difference-in-scaled-Frobenius-norms criterion) improves GSM8K accuracy by up to +4% for 7B math CPT and up to +6.3% for 13B OLMo 2 CPT, while up to 60% of heads can be rewound with no measurable quality loss; (2) CPT delta SVD truncation tolerates removal of up to 50% of singular values for 7B and up to 70% for 13B without GSM8K degradation, versus only ~30% for 1B; (3) linear interpolation between CPT checkpoints on different domains (domain connectivity) transitions from concave (20B pre-train) to clearly convex (4T pre-train, 7B) quality curves, indicating that longer pre-training enables smoother multi-domain model soups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new training method to beat external baselines; instead it compares internal head-ordering heuristics for the selective head-rewind procedure. The proposed difference-in-scaled-Frobenius-norms criterion (Eq. 1) outperforms all standard spectral metrics (PL-KS distance, delta Frobenius norm, singular vector agreement) by a wide margin (AUC-diff 6.0 vs. ≤5.2 for 7B) and slightly surpasses the greedy single-head-rewind baseline (6.0 vs. 5.0 for 7B), while for 1B the greedy baseline remains stronger (19.1 vs. 11.0). In terms of practical quality impact, the proposed criterion enables rewinding ~15% of heads with a +4% GSM8K gain on 7B math CPT, and rewinding up to 60% of heads with no quality loss—outcomes not achievable with random or standard spectral orderings. For domain connectivity, the paper notes that linear interpolation between CPT checkpoints consistently underperforms models trained directly on the corresponding data mixture, suggesting that simple task-vector merging (e.g., DARE-style) faces inherent limitations in the CPT regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the spectral analysis toolkit developed for random matrix theory (Marchenko-Pastur, heavy-tailed self-regularization) and SFT-era model merging concepts (linear mode connectivity, model soups, task arithmetic) to the underexplored continual pre-training (CPT) stage. While CPT is a standard component of modern LLM pipelines (DeepSeekMath, Code Llama, OLMo 2/3, Llama 3), its internal weight-space dynamics had not been systematically characterized via SVD. The paper extends the singular-vector dynamics framework of Yunis et al. (2024) from pre-training to the CPT delta, and generalizes the model-soups / linear-mode-connectivity literature from SFT to CPT by coining 'domain connectivity.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that CPT preserves singular value spectra while adapting through singular-vector rotations near spectral peaks reframes how practitioners think about domain adaptation: the 'what' (spectral magnitudes) is stable, the 'where' (directions) carries the domain signal. The head-heterogeneity result — that up to 60% of attention-head updates can be discarded without measurable quality loss, and that a simple difference-in-Frobenius-norm criterion can identify which heads to rewind for up to 4% quality gains — offers a concrete, low-cost lever for efficient CPT and model compression. Domain connectivity (smooth linear interpolation between CPT checkpoints) opens a path to CPT model merging, though the paper notes it still underperforms direct mixture training. Key limitations include restriction to the OLMo 2 family and AdamW optimizer, limited benchmark coverage, and no validation beyond 32B parameters; broader architectural and optimizer generalization remains an open question.
