## Problem

In ultra-high-resolution (UHR) remote sensing understanding, models must localize tiny task-relevant regions within massive pixel spaces (e.g., ~8376×8378), making visual evidence acquisition the primary bottleneck. While Agentic RLVR with zoom-in tools offers a path forward, the respective roles and interactions of SFT, RLVR, and Agentic RLVR under a shared model and evaluation setup remain poorly understood, and it is unclear how and when to inject domain knowledge to improve both the reasoning boundary (pass@32) and average single-shot performance (pass@1).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264603/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an ultra-high-resolution remote sensing image (average resolution ~8376×8378 pixels, with 13 sub-tasks in SuperRS-VQA) paired with a text question, and produces a text answer with an explicit chain-of-thought reasoning process. During inference, the model can invoke a zoom-in tool to crop and inspect local regions, interleaving textual reasoning with acquired visual evidence in an agentic loop. Evaluation is conducted on XLRS-Bench using pass@k metrics: pass@1 measures macro-averaged single-shot accuracy, while pass@32 estimates the reasoning boundary (fraction of problems solvable within 32 samples).

## Method

The paper proposes a staged 'Text-Before-Vision' knowledge injection recipe built on a 7B QwenVL2.5 base model. First, a fully automated pipeline generates 148,777 Earth-science text-only QA pairs (with chain-of-thought) from textbooks and scientific papers, quality-controlled by a textbook-derived knowledge graph (constructed via LightRAG) that screens for domain relevance and factual/logical consistency. These text QA pairs are used for cold-start SFT to instill domain concepts, mechanistic explanations, and decision rules. Second, the same hard UHR image-text examples (SuperRS-VQA) are 'pre-warmed' during SFT to initialize spatial and task representations before RL. Finally, Agentic RLVR (GRPO with a zoom-in tool, following the DeepEyes framework) is applied as a second-stage optimizer that refines evidence-seeking policies rather than exploring from near-random initialization. The key empirical finding is that text-only domain QA outperforms image-text VQA for cold-start on both pass@1 and pass@32, and that combining text QA with VQA pre-warming yields the strongest gains, achieving 60.40% pass@1 on XLRS-Bench.

## Results

On XLRS-Bench, the proposed 7B model with Earth-science text QA cold-start SFT and Agentic RLVR achieves 60.40% pass@1 and 96.25% pass@32, outperforming all compared baselines. This represents a +10.39 pass@1 improvement over the DeepEyes agentic RLVR baseline (50.01%) and a +13.67 pass@32 gain over the same baseline (82.58%). The method surpasses the 241B Intern-S1 (55.0% pass@1) and the 235B Qwen3-VL-A22B (51.11% pass@1) by 5.4 and 9.29 points respectively, despite being roughly 35× smaller.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The 7B model with staged knowledge injection (60.40% pass@1) outperforms every baseline in Table 4, including closed-source models GPT-5.2 (47.53%), Gemini 2.5 Pro (45.2%), Grok-4 (45.4%), and GPT-o3 (43.6%) by margins of 12.9–16.8 points. Among open-source models, it beats the much larger Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B (51.11%) by 9.3 points and the 241B science-specialized Intern-S1 (55.0%) by 5.4 points. Against the direct agentic RLVR baseline (DeepEyes, 7B), the full recipe adds +10.39 pass@1 and +13.67 pass@32, with the intermediate pre-warming step alone contributing +2.38 pass@1 and +9.27 pass@32. Among remote-sensing-specific models, the gap is largest versus GeoChat (22.03%) and ZoomEarth (40.20%), while the closest competitor GeoLLaVA-8K (51.50%) is still surpassed by 8.9 points. The pass@32 advantage is particularly pronounced: 96.25% vs. 85.75% for Qwen2.5-VL and 82.58% for the DeepEyes baseline, indicating substantially broader reasoning coverage under a fixed 32-sample budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DeepEyes (Zheng et al., 2025), adopting its zoom-in-enabled agentic RLVR framework as the baseline, and situates itself within the growing literature on SFT-RL synergy for multimodal models (Chen et al., 2025a,b). Its conceptual departure is the 'Text Before Vision' staged knowledge injection recipe: rather than treating visual data as the primary training signal for visual reasoning, the paper demonstrates that text-only domain QA (148K Earth-science pairs) cold-started via SFT is the dominant driver of UHR remote sensing reasoning gains, with image-text VQA serving as a secondary pre-warming stage. The automated data pipeline with knowledge-graph-based quality control (using LightRAG) extends prior work on domain-specific data construction by adding proactive pre-generation validation rather than post-hoc filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The counter-intuitive finding that text-only domain knowledge is the primary driver of ultra-high-resolution visual reasoning could reshape training pipelines for domain-specific MLLMs beyond remote sensing, suggesting that investing in high-quality textual reasoning corpora may be more cost-effective than collecting additional image-text pairs. The practical staged recipe (text cold-start → VQA pre-warming → agentic RLVR) is reproducible and yields a 7B model outperforming 241B general-purpose models, which has direct implications for resource-constrained deployments. However, the evidence is confined to a single base model (QwenVL2.5) and a single benchmark (XLRS-Bench), leaving open whether the text-before-vision principle generalizes across architectures, domains, or resolution regimes. The pass@32 plateau under scaling (Fig. 6) also hints that further gains may require qualitatively different data (long-tail concepts, discriminative rules) rather than more in-distribution samples.
