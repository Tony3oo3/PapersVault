## Problem

While first-order (FO) optimizers are known to operate at the edge of stability (EoS) in deep learning, with the top Hessian eigenvalue equilibrating near 2/η, the optimization dynamics of zeroth-order (ZO) methods remain largely unexplored. It was unclear whether ZO methods exhibit an analogous EoS phenomenon and, if so, which curvature quantity governs their stability, given that ZO updates introduce stochasticity from random perturbation directions even under full-batch training.

## Input / Output

The stability analysis takes the full Hessian spectrum {λ₁,…,λ_d} of the loss at a local minimizer, along with the step size η and momentum coefficient β, and produces an exact mean-square critical step size η_ms* defined by a sum over all eigenvalues. Since computing the full spectrum is infeasible for large models, the paper also derives tractable lower and upper bounds on η_ms* that depend only on Tr(H) and λ_max(H) (or their preconditioned analogues for Adam-style methods), which can be estimated efficiently during training via Hutchinson's estimator and power iteration.

## Method

The paper develops a mean-square linear stability theory for ZO-GD, ZO-GDM, and Frozen ZO-Adam by analyzing the second-moment recursion under the linearized (quadratic) dynamics. The key technical tool is a cone-preserving linear covariance operator on a product space of 2×2 symmetric matrices, where each coordinate block evolves locally via a matrix conjugation plus a rank-one global coupling term that aggregates information across all eigendirections. The spectral radius of this operator is characterized via the Krein-Rutman theorem, yielding an exact stability condition: for ZO-GD, η_ms* is the unique η satisfying Σᵢ ηλᵢ/(2(1−ηλᵢ)) = 1, which depends on the entire Hessian spectrum rather than just λ_max. This reveals a sharp contrast with FO methods, where stability is governed solely by the top eigenvalue; for ZO methods, the Hessian trace dominates the stability scale. Empirically, full-batch ZO methods on CNNs, ResNets, and ViTs for CIFAR-10 consistently stabilize near the predicted mean-square EoS boundary, with Tr(H_t) (or Tr(P_t⁻¹H_t) for ZO-Adam) tracking the threshold 2/η throughout training, revealing an implicit regularization effect where large step sizes bias ZO training toward small Hessian trace rather than small top eigenvalue.

## Results

The paper derives exact mean-square linear stability conditions for ZO-GD, ZO-GDM, and Frozen ZO-Adam under the linearized dynamics, showing that unlike FO methods (whose stability depends only on λ_max(H)), ZO mean-square stability depends on the full Hessian spectrum and is dominated by Tr(H). Tractable bounds show η_ms* ∈ [2/(Tr(H)+2λ_max(H)), 2/Tr(H)] for ZO-GD. Empirically, full-batch ZO-GD, ZO-GDM, and ZO-Adam on CNN, ResNet, and ViT for CIFAR-10 all operate at the mean-square edge of stability: the Hessian trace (or preconditioned trace for ZO-Adam) stabilizes near the predicted threshold 2/η throughout training, while λ_max(H_t) does not exhibit the same EoS behavior seen in FO-GD.

**Table 1: Summary of linear stability conditions for FO and ZO methods under the linearized dynamics.**

| Method | Linear Stability Condition |
|---|---|
| GD | η_mean* = η_ms* = 2/λ_max(H) |
| GDM | η_mean* = η_ms* = 2(1+β)/λ_max(H) |
| Adam | η_mean* = η_ms* = 2(1+β₁)/((1−β₁)λ_max(P⁻¹H)) |
| ZO-GD | 2/(Tr(H)+2λ_max(H)) ≤ η_ms* ≤ 2/Tr(H) |
| ZO-GDM | 2(1−β)/(Tr(H)+2λ_max(H)/(1+β)) ≤ η_ms* ≤ 2(1−β)/Tr(H) |
| ZO-Adam | 2/(Tr(P⁻¹H)+2λ_max(P⁻¹H)/(1+β₁)) ≤ η_ms* ≤ 2/Tr(P⁻¹H) |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between FO and ZO stability mechanisms. For FO-GD, the top Hessian eigenvalue λ_max(H_t) stabilizes near 2/η (the classical EoS), while for ZO-GD, λ_max(H_t) does not exhibit this behavior; instead, Tr(H_t) stabilizes slightly below 2/η, revealing a fundamentally different curvature quantity governing stability. This contrast extends to momentum: increasing β enlarges the stable regime for FO-GDM (threshold 2(1+β)/λ_max) but shrinks it for ZO-GDM (threshold 2(1−β)/Tr(H)), because momentum accumulates estimator noise in the ZO setting. For adaptive methods, FO-Adam's EoS is governed by λ_max(P⁻¹H) ≈ 2(1+β₁)/((1−β₁)η), while ZO-Adam's EoS is governed by Tr(P⁻¹H) ≈ 2/η, independent of β₁. The paper also compares full-batch ZO-GD with mini-batch ZO-SGD, finding that mini-batching leads to significantly flatter regimes with smaller Hessian trace, suggesting the mean-square EoS is specific to the full-batch setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the edge-of-stability (EoS) framework from first-order methods (Cohen et al., 2021; 2022) to zeroth-order optimizers, building on the two-point estimator of Nesterov & Spokoiny (2017) and the mean-square linear stability analysis of Mulayoff & Michaeli (2024) for SGD. The key departure is that while FO stability is governed solely by the largest Hessian eigenvalue, the paper shows ZO mean-square stability depends on the full Hessian spectrum, dominated by the trace. Technically, the analysis introduces a cone-preserving covariance operator with a rank-one global coupling term, whose spectral radius is characterized via the Krein-Rutman theorem—a tool not previously applied to ZO optimizer stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As ZO methods (e.g., MeZO) become a practical tool for memory-efficient LLM fine-tuning, understanding their training dynamics is increasingly important. The paper's central finding—that large step sizes in ZO training implicitly regularize the Hessian trace rather than the top eigenvalue—provides a new mechanistic explanation for ZO's implicit bias toward flat minima. Open questions include whether mean-square EoS persists under mini-batching (where data-sampling noise interacts with estimator noise), how the smoothing parameter μ jointly modulates stability and implicit bias, and whether the central-flow framework of Cohen et al. (2025) extends to ZO dynamics. The empirical validation is limited to CIFAR-10 and small architectures, so transfer to large-scale LLM training remains to be confirmed.
