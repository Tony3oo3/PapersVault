## Problem

Existing LLM-based tabular anomaly detection methods like AnoLLM rely on one-shot supervised fine-tuning on normal data, which provides only a weak signal for tightening the decision boundary of normality. Once the model fits the dominant regularities of the training corpus, further gains in separating subtle near-normal anomalies plateau. The paper addresses this by asking: can an iterative, label-free self-play mechanism progressively reduce the distributional gap between the model's induced distribution and the true normal-data distribution?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264559/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each mixed-type tabular row (numerical, categorical, and textual features) is serialized into a natural-language token sequence using the template 'column_name is value', with numerical values discretized into bins and a random column permutation sampled per training example to enforce column-order invariance. At inference, the anomaly score is the negative log-likelihood of the serialized sequence averaged over r=21 fixed random permutations; higher scores indicate greater anomaly likelihood, and a threshold converts scores to binary classifications.

## Method

DiSPaT formulates unsupervised tabular anomaly detection as minimizing an f-divergence between the empirical normal-data distribution and the model-induced distribution, and derives a tight variational approximation (Theorem 3.1) that makes this objective tractable via a critic function family. The training loop alternates between generating synthetic continuations from the current policy (serving as pseudo-anomalies) and updating the policy through an implicit critic parameterized as a log-ratio between current and reference policy likelihoods, analogous to DPO/SPIN. This collapses the conceptual critic-policy alternation into a single per-iteration optimization step, with a clamping mechanism that prevents over-penalization of synthetic samples already close to the normal manifold. The f-divergence family (KL, reverse KL, squared Hellinger, identity) is selectable via the Fenchel conjugate f*, with KL divergence (f*(v)=e^v−1) yielding the best empirical performance across all model scales and datasets.

## Results

On the six-dataset mixed-type benchmark, DiSPaT achieves average AUC-ROC of 84.6% (SmolLM-135M), 85.4% (SmolLM-360M), and 83.5% (SmolLM-1.7B), improving over AnoLLM by 8.1%, 7.6%, and 5.8% respectively. DiSPaT outperforms the best classical baseline (KNN, 70.7%) and best deep-learning baseline (SLAD, 72.7%) by at least 12.7% in AUC-ROC. On the 21-dataset comparison against recent methods, DiSPaT-360M attains the highest average AUC-ROC of 89.9%, beating MCM (82.6%), NPT-AD (86.6%), DRL (87.9%), LLM-DAS (77.7%), and CausalTAD (82.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiSPaT outperforms all 19 baselines across every metric and model scale. Against the direct LLM predecessor AnoLLM, DiSPaT gains 8.1/7.6/5.8% AUC-ROC (135M/360M/1.7B) and 13.9/14.1/7.9% AUC-PR, with the largest per-dataset jump on 20 Newsgroups (+15.3% AUC-ROC at 135M). Among classical methods, the strongest is KNN (70.7% avg AUC-ROC), trailed by PCA (70.5%) and IForest (68.3%); DiSPaT exceeds all by ≥12.7%. Among deep-learning baselines, SLAD leads at 72.7% AUC-ROC, followed by ICL (71.7%) and RCA (70.6%); DiSPaT surpasses the best by 12.7%. In the 21-dataset comparison against recent ICLR/ICML methods, DiSPaT-360M (89.9% avg) beats MCM (82.6%), NPT-AD (86.6%), DRL (87.9%), LLM-DAS (77.7%), and CausalTAD (82.7%), winning on 13–15 of 21 datasets depending on the baseline. In the SPIN alignment generalization experiment (Qwen1.5-1.8B on UltraChat200k), DiSPaT improves the average Open LLM Leaderboard score from 42.26 (SPIN) to 43.80, with the largest gain on GSM8K (+4.32 points).

## Relation to Prior Work

DiSPaT sits at the intersection of three threads: AnoLLM's serialization-and-likelihood approach to LLM-based tabular anomaly detection, SPIN's iterative self-play loop that contrasts real data with model-generated samples, and DPO's implicit-critic parameterization that collapses a two-player game into a single optimization step. The key conceptual departure from SPIN is the generalization from a single fixed IPM-like divergence to the full family of f-divergences via Fenchel conjugates, which the authors show admits a tight variational approximation (Theorem 3.1) and yields different distribution-matching geometries (mode-covering vs. mode-seeking). The key departure from AnoLLM is replacing one-shot supervised fine-tuning with an iterative self-play refinement that progressively tightens the normality boundary without anomaly labels, drawing an explicit analogy to one-class learning where the 'origin' is replaced by evolving model-generated pseudo-anomalies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled theoretical grounding (f-divergence minimization with a tight variational bound) for why iterative self-play should improve likelihood-based anomaly scores, moving beyond the ad-hoc motivation of 'contrast real vs. synthetic.' The consistent 5.8–8.1% AUC-ROC gains over AnoLLM across three model scales and the large margins over classical/deep baselines validate the approach for mixed-type tabular data. However, the practical cost is significant: training is roughly 7× more expensive than AnoLLM (Table 6), and the f-divergence ablation shows that the theoretically-motivated flexibility yields only modest differences in practice (KL wins by ~1–2% over alternatives). Open questions include whether the self-play loop scales to larger LLMs, whether the number of iterations can be reduced further, and whether the approach transfers to settings where the serialized representation itself is suboptimal (e.g., very high-dimensional numerical tables where tokenization is lossy).
