## Problem

Evaluating and improving LLMs' ability to generate valid step-by-step how-to procedures at scale remains challenging: existing datasets are restricted to narrow domains (cooking, specific how-to sites), prior evaluation focuses on subtasks (step ordering, edge prediction) rather than end-to-end procedural validity, and available metrics are either unreliable (BLEU, perplexity) or prohibitively expensive (human annotation). No scalable framework exists that simultaneously mines diverse real-world procedures, provides a reproducible validity-based evaluation, and yields an actionable training signal.

## Input / Output

At inference, the generator model receives a goal description, an extracted resource list, and a required step count N (set to match the reference procedure length), and must output exactly N single-sentence steps. The HOW2MINE pipeline consumes ~1M web documents (filtered to Tutorial & How-to Guide format via WebOrganizer) and produces 351K structured records, each containing a topic label, a rewritten deterministic goal, a resource list, and reference steps. HOW2SCORE outputs a binary has_failure/no_failure judgment per example (with enumerated failure descriptions), aggregated into a success rate over the evaluation set; the RL stage produces improved model checkpoints trained with GRPO.

## Method

The framework has three coupled components. HOW2MINE is a five-stage web-mining pipeline (LLM extraction → heuristics filter on step count and n-gram repetition → LLM filter removing entity-specific, pure-math, UI, creative, non-sequential, or nonsensical procedures → goal rewriting and resource extraction → final LLM validation) that converts 980K DCLM web documents into 351K structured procedures across 14 WebOrganizer topics. HOW2SCORE defines a critical failure as any omission, extraneous action, contradiction, or severe vagueness that would prevent goal achievement, and uses an LLM judge to detect whether a generation contains at least one such failure; the protocol is validated against three human annotators (Krippendorff's α=0.593) and a frontier GPT-5 judge (83.0% agreement), then distilled into a Qwen3-8B model (80.5% human agreement) for low-cost reproducible scoring. For training, the authors apply GRPO with three rewards—HOW2SCORE from the distilled judge, a step-format verifier, and a reference-calibrated length penalty to prevent verbosity gaming—yielding >10-point HOW2BENCH gains across Qwen3-4B/8B and OLMo-3-7B without systematic regressions on 12 out-of-domain benchmarks.

## Results

RL with HOW2SCORE as reward improves HOW2BENCH performance by >10 points across three models: Qwen3-4B-Inst (30.29→43.52, +13.23), Qwen3-8B-Inst (38.52→48.62, +10.10), and OLMo-3-7B-Think (27.30→37.89, +10.59). Out-of-domain changes are mixed but modest (mean Δ_OOD ranges from −0.5 to +1.2), with no systematic degradation across 12 standard benchmarks. The distilled 8B judge achieves 80.5% agreement with human annotators (vs. 83.0% for GPT 5), and HOW2BENCH shows smooth scaling from ~10²¹ FLOPs (1B model, ~5% into pretraining) to frontier models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing procedure-generation methods; instead, the primary comparison is before-vs-after RL on the same base checkpoints. RL with HOW2SCORE consistently lifts HOW2BENCH by 10–13 points across Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, and OLMo-3-7B, while out-of-domain changes remain within ±5 points on most benchmarks (e.g., MMLU-Pro improves by +1.5 to +5.1, GPQA shifts by −0.2 to +0.9). Cross-judge robustness checks confirm that gains persist when re-evaluated with external judges (GPT 5, Gemini 2.5 Pro) rather than the distilled judge used during training. A blinded manual evaluation on 200 examples confirms the RL improvement (32.5%→40.0% no-failure, McNemar p=0.0107). The paper further shows that gains are not explained by format compliance (RL gains scale with pretraining maturity, from +3.56 at 10% to +20.33 at 100% FLOPs) or source-document memorization (perplexity drops from 10.4→1.4 with repeated exposure, but HOW2SCORE improves by only +3.3 for 7B).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes ideas from web-scale data mining (DCLM, WebOrganizer), LLM-as-a-judge evaluation, and RL post-training (GRPO) into a unified framework for procedural generation. It departs from prior procedural knowledge work (PLaMa, ProScript, WikiHow-based datasets) by mining from the open web across 14 topics rather than narrow domains like cooking or specific platforms, and by evaluating end-to-end procedural validity via critical-failure detection rather than subtasks like step ordering or edge prediction. The evaluation protocol draws conceptual inspiration from process reward models for math reasoning but adapts the binary 'any critical failure' aggregation to the non-executable, open-world setting where localizing a first failure is intractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HOW2BENCH provides a scalable, reproducible evaluation signal for a capability that accounts for ~8.5% of real ChatGPT usage, with clear scaling trends from 1B to frontier models that make it useful for performance forecasting and early pretraining diagnostics. The finding that RL with HOW2SCORE yields >10-point gains without systematic regressions across 12 standard benchmarks suggests procedure generation is a broadly transferable capability that can complement existing post-training pipelines. Key limitations include reliance on reference procedures as anchors (96.6% validated but not perfect), the binary aggregation losing failure-localization information, and the fact that HOW2SCORE remains a proxy rather than execution-based verification. Open questions include whether the framework extends to executable procedure settings, whether gains transfer to safety-critical domains, and whether the 14-topic coverage is sufficient for truly open-world generalization.
