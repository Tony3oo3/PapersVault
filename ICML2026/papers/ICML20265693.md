## Problem

Existing methods for modeling cellular differentiation hierarchies face a fundamental scalability-stability trade-off: VAE-based approaches like TreeVAE require branch-specific encoder-decoder pairs whose parameters grow exponentially with tree depth, causing overfitting on sparse deep branches and preventing knowledge sharing across the hierarchy. Traditional clustering pipelines lack generative capacity, while VAE decoders suffer from posterior collapse in single-step generation. The paper asks whether a unified, branch-agnostic architecture can simultaneously capture deep hierarchical structure, enable stable training on sparse data, and support biologically plausible generative validation of inferred lineages.

## Input / Output

The method consumes D-dimensional feature vectors (500 highly variable genes for single-cell data, 784-dim flattened images, 2000-dim TF-IDF text) and generates a kNN-based augmented view x_i^+ from the local neighbor set of each sample for contrastive training. Outputs include a rooted binary tree of depth L=10 with learnable code vectors at each node, a per-sample hierarchical code sequence (root-to-leaf path of L quantized codes), lineage trajectories as shortest paths on a KNN-augmented weighted graph, and generated cell states produced by a 1000-step DDPM conditioned on the code sequence. The binary tree serves as an indexing scaffold; aggregated sample paths naturally induce multi-branch, unbalanced hierarchies.

## Method

HDTree replaces TreeVAE's branch-specific modules with three synergistic components: a shared MLP encoder, a unified Hierarchical Tree Codebook (HTC) where quantization proceeds top-down (at each level, select the nearest code among the children of the previously selected node), and a DDPM decoder conditioned on the full root-to-leaf code sequence. The key architectural insight is that a single shared codebook decouples tree complexity from network parameter count, allowing sparse deep nodes to inherit representation knowledge from sibling and ancestor branches. Training uses a composite loss: Soft Contrastive Learning (t-distribution kernel in hyperbolic space, weighted by tree distance), Hierarchical Quantization Loss (aligning embeddings to codes while enforcing parent-child consistency via stop-gradient), and standard DDPM noise-prediction loss. For lineage inference, the learned tree is converted to a weighted graph with depth-penalized KNN lateral edges, and developmental trajectories are extracted as shortest paths between designated origin and destination nodes.

## Results

On general datasets, HDTree achieves the best average score across all four benchmarks: 35.7 on MNIST (vs. TreeVAE's 31.8), −29.9 on Fashion-MNIST (vs. −35.4), 19.0 on 20News-Groups (vs. 9.1), and 25.2 on CIFAR-10 (vs. 14.7), with gains of +3.9 to +10.5 over the strongest baseline. On single-cell datasets, HDTree leads in Dendrogram Purity (DP) and Clustering Accuracy (ACC): 41.0 DP / 55.0 ACC on Limb, 42.7 DP / 49.4 ACC on LHCO, and 61.0 DP / 62.6 ACC on Weinreb. On lineage ground-truth evaluation, HDTree reaches 62.0% temporal consistency at Day 6 (LineageVAE dataset) and 66.3% in the 500–750 window (C. elegans), surpassing TreeVAE by +4.0% and semi-supervised LineageVAE by +1.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HDTree outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated metric and dataset. Against the closest SOTA competitor TreeVAE, HDTree gains +3.9 average on MNIST, +5.5 on Fashion-MNIST, +9.9 on 20News-Groups, and +10.5 on CIFAR-10, with the largest relative improvements on text and high-dimensional image data. On single-cell benchmarks, HDTree beats TreeVAE by +2.5 (Limb), +2.2 (LHCO), and +8.8 (Weinreb) in average score, and surpasses zero-shot foundation models (Geneformer, CellPLM, LangCell) by 15–25 points in DP on Limb. On lineage ground-truth evaluation, HDTree (unsupervised) exceeds semi-supervised LineageVAE by +1.7% at Day 6 and outperforms WaddingtonOT by +5.4% at Day 6, while on C. elegans it leads TreeVAE by +4.0% in the 500–750 temporal window. Traditional methods (Agg, tSNE+Agg, UMAP+Agg) are consistently dominated, with HDTree achieving 2–4× higher DP on text data. In training time, HDTree (42–54 min) is ~4× faster than TreeVAE (192–361 min) on large datasets, though slower than UMAP+Agg on small ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

HDTree directly extends TreeVAE (Manduchi et al., 2023) by replacing its branch-specific encoder-decoder pairs with a single unified hierarchical tree codebook, decoupling tree depth from network parameter count. The generative component draws on DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) and the quantized diffusion paradigm from VQDM (Gu et al., 2022), while the progressive quantization structure is inspired by PQ-VAE (Huang et al., 2024). The soft contrastive learning loss is adapted from the authors' prior work (Zang et al., 2023), and the overall framing aligns the iterative denoising process with the Waddington landscape metaphor for cellular differentiation, a conceptual bridge not explicitly made in prior tree-structured VAEs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HDTree offers a practical, lightweight alternative to both branch-specific hierarchical VAEs and large pretrained single-cell foundation models (Geneformer, CellPLM), demonstrating that a unified codebook plus diffusion decoder can match or exceed their performance on lineage inference without expensive pretraining. The main limitation is the computational cost of diffusion sampling at inference time, which the authors acknowledge and suggest addressing via distillation or latent diffusion. Open questions include whether the binary-tree indexing generalizes to truly unbalanced or non-tree topologies common in biology, and whether the Waddington-landscape alignment provides genuine mechanistic insight beyond a useful inductive bias.
