## Problem

Backdoor attacks implant hidden triggers into LLMs that activate malicious behaviors only when specific secret strings appear in the input, making them invisible to standard safety training. Prior attempts to induce backdoor self-awareness via Reversal Supervised Fine-Tuning (R-SFT) yield only imprecise, low-probability trigger articulation that is insufficient for practical defense. The paper asks: can a poisoned LLM be trained to reliably and precisely articulate its own implanted triggers, and can this self-awareness be leveraged for effective backdoor mitigation?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264066/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a poisoned LLM (initialized via SFT and R-SFT) and, at each RL step, feeds it an inversion prompt describing the suspected attack behavior alongside a trigger-free violation-inducing prompt, asking the model to hypothesize the hidden trigger via chain-of-thought reasoning. The model outputs a single candidate trigger string, which is then stamped onto a mini-batch of 10 violation-inducing prompts to compute a surrogate reward. Downstream, the articulated triggers produce either an unlearned model (via adversarial fine-tuning with trigger+benign-response pairs) or a binary trigger-detection label at inference time via a lightweight guardrail prompt.

## Method

The core contribution is a GRPO-based reinforcement learning stage with a surrogate reward module and buffer-replay mechanism, applied on top of R-SFT to cultivate backdoor self-awareness. The surrogate reward combines universal attack effectiveness (the fraction of mini-batch prompts where the candidate trigger induces the target violation, verified by an LLM judge) with a piecewise length penalty that favors shorter triggers, weighted so that length is only rewarded when accompanied by effectiveness. Buffer replay addresses the severe sparse-reward problem in early training by storing candidates whose reward exceeds 0.5 and is ≥1.5 standard deviations above the group mean, then replacing 10% of newly sampled responses with high-reward buffer entries at each step. The paper observes a grokking-like emergence: backdoor self-awareness appears abruptly within fewer than ten RL steps rather than gradually. Two complementary defenses are then built on the articulated triggers: adversarial unlearning (fine-tuning with trigger-stamped prompts paired with safe responses, reducing ASR by an average of 73.18%) and an inference-time guardrail that scans incoming prompts for the recovered trigger (achieving 95.6% average detection accuracy).

## Results

Across five backdoor attacks, the proposed RL-based trigger articulation achieves an average 80% improvement in trigger elicitation accuracy over baselines. For adversarial unlearning, the method reduces attack success rate (ASR) with trigger to 4.74% (Jailbreak), 4.86% (SHIP), 5.10% (Clean Label), 4.50% (DoS), and 0.00% (Sleeper Agent), while preserving model utility (MMLU-Pro within ±1.0 of baseline). For inference-time detection, the guardrail achieves 99.8%, 99.19%, 91.63%, 89.00%, and 100.00% accuracy on the five attacks, substantially outperforming ONION and CoS which remain near random (≈50%) on most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For unlearning, the proposed method dramatically outperforms GCG+Adv.Train and R-SFT+Adv.Train on all five attacks (e.g., 4.74% vs. 73.87% ASR on Jailbreak; 0.00% vs. 36.36% on Sleeper Agent), while BEEAR achieves comparable or slightly lower ASR on SHIP (0.54% vs. 4.86%) and DoS (2.00% vs. 4.50%) but at severe utility cost (XSTest drops from 69.60% to 10.80% on SHIP; MMLU-Pro drops from 39.67% to 11.87% on DoS). For inference-time detection, the guardrail substantially outperforms ONION and CoS on all five attacks (e.g., 99.19% vs. 52.7%/48.6% on SHIP; 100% vs. 49.8%/53.5% on Sleeper Agent), and matches or slightly trails BEAT on Jailbreak (99.8% vs. 100%) and DoS (89.00% vs. 92.5%), while decisively winning on SHIP (99.19% vs. 47.8%), Clean Label (91.63% vs. 50.4%), and Sleeper Agent (100% vs. 49.3%). Overall, the method is the only approach that simultaneously achieves near-complete backdoor removal and full utility preservation across all attack types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the situational self-awareness line of work (Cotra 2022; Betley et al. 2025a) by showing that Reversal SFT alone, which prior work suggested could induce backdoor self-awareness, actually only produces memorization rather than genuine trigger articulation. The core methodological contribution is an RL post-training stage using GRPO (Shao et al. 2024) with a surrogate reward (attack effectiveness + length penalty) and a buffer-replay mechanism (Li et al. 2025) to address sparse rewards, building on the broader 'SFT memorizes, RL generalizes' finding (Chu et al. 2025). The paper departs from gradient-based trigger inversion (GCG, BEEAR) by framing trigger recovery as an introspective reasoning task the model performs on itself, rather than an external optimization over the model's input space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a poisoned LLM that can articulate its own triggers enables targeted unlearning (reducing ASR by ~73% on average) and near-perfect inference-time detection (95.6% accuracy) without requiring gradient access or embedding-space manipulation. A key limitation is the assumption that the defender knows the attack target behavior, which constrains the reward design; developing reward mechanisms that do not require this prior knowledge is an important open problem. The grokking-like emergent pattern (awareness appearing abruptly in fewer than 10 RL steps) also raises mechanistic questions about how and when latent trigger information becomes accessible to the model's generative process.
