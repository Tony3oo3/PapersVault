## Problem

Existing contrastive learning methods for ordinal regression either ignore the ordered nature of rank labels (SupCon) or collapse ordinal distances into hard binary positive/negative decisions (RnC), failing to exploit the full spectrum of rank differences within a batch. Meanwhile, order learning methods like GOL rely on local margin-based pairwise comparisons that generate no gradient once the margin is satisfied, limiting their ability to capture global ordinal structure. The paper asks how to design a contrastive objective that simultaneously leverages all batch-level pairwise relationships and encodes the magnitude of rank differences in a soft, differentiable manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262361/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts face images (age estimation), natural images (BIQA), or video clips (BVQA) as input. For video, a dual-backbone architecture extracts spatial features via ViT-B over N sampled frames and temporal features via the SlowFast fast pathway over M sampled clips, fused into a single embedding. The encoder maps each instance to an ℓ₂-normalized feature vector on the unit sphere, and rank estimation at inference is performed by averaging the ranks of k nearest neighbors among all training instances in the learned embedding space, with k varying by dataset (4–60).

## Method

ConOrd introduces a contrastive order loss that contrasts all 2N×2N sample pairs in a batch using soft affinity weights a_ij = 1/((r_i−r_j)²+ε) in the numerator and disparity weights b_ij = (r_i−r_j)² in the denominator, with similarity κ_ij = −‖z_i−z_j‖². This replaces the hard positive/negative partitioning of RnC with continuous, rank-gap-modulated weights so that every pair contributes to the gradient with strength proportional to its ordinal proximity. A center loss pulling each instance toward its rank's learnable reference point μ_m is added for intra-class compactness. The gradient analysis shows that the factor (a_ij/α_i − b_ij/β_i) induces attraction for small rank gaps and repulsion for large ones, while the exponential kernel e^{κ_ij/τ} focuses updates on pairs that are both close in rank and close in embedding space, yielding fine-grained ordinal discrimination without the training instability of the naive MAE-based contrastive loss.

## Results

On facial age estimation (Table 1), ConOrd achieves the best MAE across all six datasets: 1.96 on MORPH II, 2.46 on CLAP2015, 5.15 on AgeDB-DIR, 3.92 on UTK, 4.18 on CACD, and 0.26 on Adience. On blind image quality assessment (Table 2), ConOrd attains the highest SRCC and PCC on all five datasets, reaching 0.947/0.958 on KonIQ10k and 0.913/0.925 on BID. On blind video quality assessment (Table 3), ConOrd leads on all five benchmarks, achieving 0.904/0.904 on LSVQ-test and 0.839/0.845 on CVD2014, outperforming the strongest prior method ModularBVQA by 0.009 SRCC on LSVQ-test.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConOrd outperforms all 13 baselines on age estimation, with the largest margins over RnC (e.g., 2.46 vs. 4.72 MAE on CLAP2015) and Ordinal Entropy (2.46 vs. 5.66), confirming that soft all-pairs contrastive weighting substantially outperforms both hard-threshold contrastive and entropy-regularized approaches. Against the closest ordinal-regression competitor SLACE, ConOrd improves MAE by 0.22 on MORPH II, 0.03 on CLAP2015, 0.10 on AgeDB-DIR, 0.06 on UTK, and 0.05 on CACD, while tying on Adience (0.26). On BIQA, ConOrd surpasses the strongest prior methods (RichIQA, VISGA, QCN) by 0.013–0.014 SRCC on BID and KonIQ10k, and by 0.007–0.009 on CLIVE and SPAQ. On BVQA, ConOrd beats ModularBVQA (which uses three backbones vs. ConOrd's two) by 0.009 SRCC on LSVQ-test and 0.010 on CVD2014. In the controlled ablation (Table 4), ConOrd's loss improves over RnC by 0.07 MAE on CLAP2015 and 0.006 SRCC on LSVQ-1080p, isolating the gain to the soft-weighting mechanism rather than architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConOrd sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: order learning (GOL, Lee et al. 2022), which constructs ordinal embedding spaces via margin-based pairwise constraints and k-NN inference, and supervised contrastive learning (SupCon, Khosla et al. 2020), which exploits batch-level pairwise relationships but treats labels categorically. The most direct predecessor is Rank-N Contrast (RnC, Zha et al. 2023), which introduced rank-aware positive/negative pair selection into a contrastive framework but relies on hard binary thresholds that collapse ordinal distances. ConOrd departs from RnC by replacing these hard assignments with continuous affinity and disparity weights that are smooth monotonic functions of rank gaps, and from GOL by replacing local margin-based supervision with a global all-pairs contrastive objective, thereby unifying the two paradigms in a single batch-wise loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, general-purpose loss formulation for ordinal regression that consistently achieves state-of-the-art results across at least seven distinct tasks (age estimation, BIQA, BVQA, temperature prediction, gaze estimation, depth estimation, text similarity), suggesting the soft-weighted contrastive principle is broadly applicable rather than task-specific. The k-NN inference scheme is simple and avoids a dedicated regression head, which is practical for deployment. However, the method still requires access to the full training set at inference time for k-NN lookup, and the improvements over strong baselines like RnC and SLACE, while consistent, are modest in magnitude (typically 0.01–0.05 in SRCC or 0.1–0.3 in MAE). Open questions include whether the approach extends to truly unsupervised or weakly-supervised ordinal settings, whether the quadratic weight forms are optimal for very large rank spaces, and whether the center loss could be replaced by more principled regularization.
