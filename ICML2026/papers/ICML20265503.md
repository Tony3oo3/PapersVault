## Problem

A complete classification of large-scale gradient flow learning regimes with explicit analytic solutions remains an open problem: while the NTK limit is well understood, nonlinear (rich) regimes, their boundaries, and the conditions under which they emerge are poorly characterized. This paper develops a systematic framework to identify all possible learning phases in large models and derive closed-form loss trajectories, going beyond the few special cases for which explicit solutions were previously known.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265503/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework analyzes CP-decomposition models of order ν (symmetric or asymmetric) fitting an identity tensor target, with i.i.d. Gaussian weight initialization of variance σ². The critical analytical input is the joint power-law scaling of the three size parameters (p, H, σ²) as they grow to infinity; different scaling vectors select different faces of a Pareto polygon, each corresponding to a distinct regime (free evolution, NTK, mean-field, under/over-parameterized). The output is both a regime classification and explicit analytic formulas for E[L(t)] valid for any double limit of p and H, including the limiting loss at t→∞.

## Method

The core technique is a formal power series expansion of the loss L(t) in time, where each coefficient is a polynomial in (H, p, σ²) encoded by a Feynman-like diagram constructed through a binary merging operation on two base diagrams: D_{2ν} (model self-interaction) and R_ν (model-target interaction). Averaging over Gaussian initialization is performed via Wick's theorem, which translates to edge-pairing contractions that identify nodes in the diagrams. In the large-size limit, Pareto-optimal monomials in the coefficient polynomials form a planar polygon whose faces correspond to distinct learning regimes, with the scaling exponents (α_p, α_H, α_σ) selecting the active face. The resulting formal series is summed by converting coefficient recurrences into first-order PDEs on an auxiliary generating function, solvable by the method of characteristics, yielding explicit closed-form loss trajectories in each regime.

## Results

The paper derives explicit analytic solutions for the expected loss E[L(t)] under gradient flow in multiple large-size regimes of CP tensor decomposition. Key quantitative findings include: (1) in the free overparameterized regime, the loss decays as a power law (1 + ct)^{-ν/(ν-1)} in SYM even-ν but exponentially as e^{-2νσ^{2(ν-1)}Ht/T} in ASYM; (2) the NTK regime exists in ASYM for all ν but is provably absent in SYM with even ν; (3) for SYM ν=2, a complete closed-form solution (Eq. 19–20) is valid for any double limit of p and H, correctly predicting the limiting loss max((p-H)/2, 0); (4) for SYM ν=4 gradient ascent, a Borel-summable solution identifies a sharp divergence threshold p³σ⁴ < ρ* separating low-noise (convergent) and high-noise (divergent, |τ-τ_crit|^{-4/3}) regimes. All predictions are confirmed by numerical experiments (Figs. 3–6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing methods; instead, its 'comparisons' are between its analytic predictions and numerical experiments, as well as between its regime classification and existing theoretical frameworks. The paper's NTK regime (Edge B–C, Point C) recovers the constant NTK limit of Jacot et al. (2018) but extends it to CP tensor models of arbitrary order ν that cannot be described as Tensor Programs, making the result novel. The mean-field regime (Edge C–D in ASYM, Edge B–E in SYM) matches the measure-evolution limits of Chizat et al. (2019) and Sirignano & Spiliopoulos (2020) for ν=2, but the paper provides explicit closed-form loss solutions rather than just identifying the limit. The free evolution results (Section 7) go beyond existing linear-network analyses (Fukumizu 1998; Saxe et al. 2013) by handling generic Gaussian initialization without balancedness or eigenvector-alignment assumptions. Experimental validation (Figs. 3–6) shows excellent agreement between theory and simulation across all tested regimes, with the SYM ν=2 solution (Eq. 19) matching experiments for p=512 and H ∈ {1024, 512, 256}.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Feynman-diagram techniques from the width-expansion literature (Dyer & Gur-Ari, 2020; Aitken & Gur-Ari, 2020) into a time-expansion of the loss, where diagram merging encodes successive gradient-flow derivatives and Wick contractions handle Gaussian averaging. It positions known limit regimes—NTK (Jacot et al., 2018), mean-field (Chizat et al., 2019; Sirignano & Spiliopoulos, 2020), and free evolution—as faces of a single Pareto polygon in the (p, H, σ²) scaling space, unifying them under one geometric picture. Crucially, the CP-decomposition model for ν≥3 is not a Tensor Program (Yang, 2020), so the NTK results here do not follow from existing TP convergence theorems, and the generic Gaussian initialization avoids the balanced/aligned-init assumptions required by prior explicit solutions for linear networks (Saxe et al., 2013; Fukumizu, 1998).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a systematic, extensible recipe—diagram expansion → Pareto polygon → PDE via method of characteristics—for deriving explicit loss trajectories in any regime, and it is demonstrated to work for free evolution, NTK, mean-field, and the full SYM ν=2 triangle. The finding that the NTK limit is absent in the symmetric even-ν setting (while present in the asymmetric one) is a structural result not implied by any known NTK convergence theorem and suggests symmetry fundamentally alters the learning landscape. Open questions include rigorous justification of the formal-series summation (analogous to the long-standing difficulty of justifying Feynman expansions in QFT), the gradient-descent (t>0) solution for ν=4, the SYM odd-ν case, and whether the modular-addition surrogate (Appendix C) faithfully captures the complex Fourier dynamics of the true task.
