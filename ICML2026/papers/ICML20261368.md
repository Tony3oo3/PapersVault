## Problem

Large Reasoning Models produce verbose reasoning chains that incur high latency, yet existing efficiency methods either rigidly prune steps (disrupting logical coherence) or impose token-length penalties in RL (suppressing the descriptive detail needed for deep reasoning). The paper identifies that accuracy in multi-hop QA peaks when the reasoning segment count matches the ground-truth hop count and degrades with structural redundancy, motivating a framework that optimizes reasoning topology rather than raw token volume.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261368/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query for knowledge-intensive multi-hop QA and produces a structured reasoning chain formatted as a numbered list of discrete segments, followed by a final answer. The key structural constraint is that the segment count N is explicitly decoupled from intra-segment token length L_i: the model is trained to align N with the ground-truth hop count k* while leaving the descriptive granularity within each segment unconstrained. Segments are extracted via rule-based parsing of the numbered-list format, and answer correctness is verified by an LLM judge.

## Method

CoSMo is a two-stage framework that decouples structural complexity (segment count N) from intra-segment reasoning depth (tokens per segment L_i). In the SFT phase, a split-merge optimization algorithm iteratively refines correct reasoning chains: when N > k*, adjacent redundant segments are merged via an LLM judge and semantic generator; when N < k*, coarse-grained segments are split into finer logical sub-steps. The resulting logic-dense dataset is used for supervised fine-tuning with LoRA. In the RL phase, GRPO is employed with a novel segment-level budget reward r_struct = -max(0, |N - k*| - 1) that penalizes structural deviation beyond a one-step tolerance while leaving intra-segment token length entirely unconstrained, combined with format and correctness rewards. This design allows the model to expand reasoning detail within each segment as needed for accuracy without incurring structural penalties.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, CoSMo achieves an average accuracy of 71.7% across HotpotQA, HaluEval, NQ, and CRAG, outperforming the CoT baseline (68.9%) by 2.8 points while reducing average token consumption by 29.5% (153 vs. 217 tokens). Compared to the most competitive efficient-reasoning baselines (LCPO, ThinkPrune), CoSMo improves accuracy by 1.7–2.1 points while cutting average segment count by 27.5% and 14.7% respectively. On the MuSiQue robustness analysis, CoSMo's accuracy degrades by only 2.1 points from 2-hop to 4-hop questions, versus 3.9–4.0 points for CoT and C3oT, and its average segment count tracks the ground-truth hop count within ±1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSMo achieves the highest accuracy across all four benchmarks against every baseline, with the largest margins over additive prompting methods (HTP: +6.4 avg accuracy; ToT: +2.6) and pruning-based SFT methods (C3oT: +5.9; SPIRIT: +4.6). Against the closest competitors in the length-aligned RL category, CoSMo edges out LCPO by +1.7 accuracy with 27.5% fewer segments (2.9 vs. 4.0) and ThinkPrune by +2.1 accuracy with 14.7% fewer segments. Notably, CoSMo uses more tokens than token-minimizing baselines like CoD (153 vs. 111) and C3oT (153 vs. 137), but this is by design: the segment-level budget penalizes structural redundancy (segment count) while leaving intra-segment token length unconstrained, allowing the model to spend tokens where they improve reasoning quality. On the structural metric (average segments), CoSMo achieves the lowest count (2.9) among all methods, reducing redundancy by 46.3% over CoT and 28.7% over the efficient-reasoning baselines as a group. The gains are consistent across both in-distribution (HotpotQA, HaluEval) and out-of-distribution (NQ, CRAG) settings, with the OOD accuracy advantage over CoT reaching +3.7 points on NQ and +2.1 on CRAG.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoSMo departs from the dominant paradigm in efficient reasoning that treats the reasoning chain as a flat token sequence and optimizes total length (via pruning, length-penalized RL, or token-budget prompting). Drawing on the classic split-and-merge algorithm from image segmentation (Horowitz & Pavlidis, 1976), it reframes the problem as one of structural topology: aligning the segment count N with ground-truth hop complexity k* while leaving intra-segment token length unconstrained. This positions it against both pruning-based SFT methods (C3oT, SPIRIT, FS-BoN) that risk disrupting logical coherence, and length-aligned RL methods (LCPO, ThinkPrune) that the authors argue suppress necessary deep reasoning. The RL stage builds directly on GRPO from DeepSeek-R1, but replaces the token-length reward with a segment-count deviation penalty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean decoupling of structural redundancy from intra-segment reasoning depth offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc length penalties, and the segment-level budget mechanism is a reusable design pattern for any RL-based reasoning optimization. However, the framework is validated primarily on 8B-scale models in the multi-hop QA domain, and its reliance on a strong LLM (Qwen-2.5-72B) as both judge and generator during data curation introduces a practical dependency that limits accessibility. Open questions include whether the structural-alignment objective generalizes to tasks without a well-defined hop count (e.g., open-ended planning, code generation) and whether the gains persist at larger model scales where base reasoning is already more efficient.
