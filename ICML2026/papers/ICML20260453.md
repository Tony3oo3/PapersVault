## Problem

The expressivity of convex coordinate-wise region-based embeddings for relational reasoning has remained largely unclear: while prior work showed octagons capture 'regular' rule bases, it was unknown which broader classes of closed path rules such models can faithfully capture and where their limitations lie. This paper addresses that gap by establishing fundamental expressivity boundaries and identifying precise conditions under which faithful capture is achievable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260453/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a knowledge graph (a set of relational facts) together with a set of closed path rules, and produces n-dimensional entity embeddings plus 2D convex region embeddings (one per relation per coordinate). For empirical evaluation, the FAIRE benchmark provides synthetic KGs where models are trained on facts instantiating positive rules and distractor rules, then tested on link prediction queries of the form r(e₁, ?) where candidate entities correspond to the correct inference and inferences that would follow from distractor rules the model should not capture.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a series of theoretical results characterizing the expressivity of convex coordinate-wise region-based embeddings. It proves that without convexity, coordinate-wise models can faithfully capture arbitrary closed path rules (via unions of lines), but imposing convexity fundamentally restricts expressivity: certain acyclic rule bases cannot be faithfully captured by accordant convex coordinate-wise embeddings. It then shows that non-accordant convex coordinate-wise embeddings can capture arbitrary rule bases up to any bounded depth, and identifies sufficient conditions (per-relation balancing weightings or regularity) under which accordant embeddings can faithfully capture acyclic rule bases. The paper further demonstrates that convex coordinate-wise models can capture spurious semantic dependencies not expressible as closed path rules, even for non-accordant embeddings. Empirically, the authors introduce the FAIRE benchmark (7 synthetic datasets with explicit distractor rules) and show that existing models (Octagons, ExpressivE, polygon-based variants) struggle to faithfully capture rule bases in practice, even when theoretically capable, suggesting that stronger inductive biases are needed.

## Results

On the FAIRE benchmark (7 synthetic rule-reasoning datasets), all evaluated convex coordinate-wise models show highly unstable Hits@1 results across 20 runs, with several models performing at or below the random baseline (0.17–0.50) on some datasets. ExpressivE achieves the best average performance on most datasets (e.g., 0.97 on PERM3, 0.94 on MIX2, 0.69 on REP13), while Octagons excels only on PERM2 (1.00) and struggles on COMB (0.17). The proposed polygon model underperforms ExpressivE on most datasets but reaches the highest score on COMB (0.40 with 6 edges), where ExpressivE achieves 0.32 and Octagons 0.17. Overall, even models that are theoretically capable of faithfully capturing the rule bases fail to do so reliably in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExpressivE (Pavlovic & Sallinger, 2023) is the strongest existing baseline, outperforming Octagons (Charpenay & Schockaert, 2024) on 6 of 7 datasets, with particularly large margins on PERM3 (0.97 vs. 0.58), MIX2 (0.94 vs. 0.47), and COMB (0.32 vs. 0.17). Octagons only matches or exceeds ExpressivE on PERM2 (1.00 vs. 0.99) and is competitive on REP13 (0.60 vs. 0.69). The paper's polygon model, despite being theoretically expressive enough to achieve perfect scores (given sufficient dimensions and edges), underperforms ExpressivE on most datasets (e.g., 0.57 vs. 0.71 on MIX3, 0.51 vs. 0.69 on REP13 with 6 edges), except on COMB where Polygons(6) reaches 0.40 versus ExpressivE's 0.32. Notably, all models exhibit extreme variance across runs (e.g., ExpressivE ranges from 0.24 to 1.00 on MIX3), and several configurations fall below the random baseline, indicating that standard training strategies are insufficient for faithful rule capture even when theoretical expressivity guarantees exist.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the expressivity analysis of region-based KG embeddings initiated by Gutiérrez-Basulto & Schockaert (2018) and developed through BoxE (Abboud et al., 2020), ExpressivE (Pavlovic & Sallinger, 2023), Octagons (Charpenay & Schockaert, 2024), and ReshuffLE (Pavlovic et al., 2025). While prior work characterized what each specific model can capture, this paper isolates the role of two design choices—convexity and coordinate-wise parameterization—and shows their interaction fundamentally limits expressivity. It generalizes the octagon result (Proposition 5.4) by identifying broader sufficient conditions (weighting-based balance) under which accordant convex coordinate-wise embeddings can faithfully capture acyclic rule bases, and introduces a polygon-based model as a more flexible alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative result (Proposition 4.2) reveals that the most practical class of region-based models—convex coordinate-wise—cannot faithfully capture even small acyclic rule bases without introducing spurious inferences, which has direct implications for the reliability of neurosymbolic KG reasoning systems. The FAIRE benchmark provides a systematic way to evaluate faithful reasoning that was previously missing, and the empirical finding that models fail to capture rule bases they are theoretically capable of capturing highlights a significant gap between expressivity and learnability. Open questions include whether minimal extensions (limited cross-coordinate comparisons, non-convex regions) can close the expressivity gap, and whether stronger inductive biases or alternative training objectives can bridge the theory-practice gap observed empirically.
