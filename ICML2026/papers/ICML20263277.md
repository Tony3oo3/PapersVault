## Problem

Stochastic-gradient MCMC methods like SGLD are highly sensitive to minibatch size: with small batches, gradient noise inflates the per-coordinate variance of the update, causing instability or outright divergence. This limits scalability to regimes where only small minibatches are available or where memory constrains the number of stored datapoints. The paper asks whether the discretisation itself can be redesigned so that minibatch noise is structurally suppressed rather than merely reduced in magnitude.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of N observations, a parametric model (likelihood and prior), and at each iteration a minibatch of size B used to form an unbiased gradient estimator of the negative log-posterior. It outputs posterior samples θ from which the posterior predictive distribution is estimated via Monte Carlo averaging. A key practical detail is that each coordinate update is bounded to ±√(2δ_t), so no Gaussian random numbers are required and the per-iteration cost is O(d), matching SGLD.

## Method

SGLRW replaces the Gaussian increment of SGLD with a coordinate-wise binary lattice step: each coordinate i moves by ±√(2δ_t) with a state-dependent probability proportional to the minibatch gradient component. The critical structural consequence (Lemma 4.3) is that the diagonal of the one-step second-moment matrix is deterministic (fixed at 2δ_t), so minibatch noise ζ can only enter through the off-diagonal entries of the update covariance, never inflating per-coordinate variance. Theorem 4.2 then shows the MSE bound for SGLRW is strictly tighter than SGLD's, with the covariance error term E_cov^SGLRW ≤ E_cov^SGLD, while drift and discretisation contributions are identical. The method inherits SGLD's asymptotic correctness under a decreasing step-size schedule and composes cleanly with cyclic learning-rate schedules and diagonal (RMSprop) preconditioning. A strong baseline, Clipped-SGLD, is also introduced and shown to be competitive but consistently outperformed by SGLRW in KL divergence, especially at small batch sizes.

## Results

On 50-dimensional Bayesian linear regression, SGLRW achieves KL divergence of 0.055–0.075 across batch sizes B=64–1000 at δ₀=10⁻³, approaching the Monte Carlo floor of 0.0552, while SGLD ranges from 0.072 to 0.817 and Clipped-SGLD from 0.073 to 1.169. At the most challenging setting (B=8, δ₀=10⁻³), SGLRW attains KL=6.060 versus SGLD's 19.853 and Clipped-SGLD's 18.496. On the non-Gaussian breast cancer logistic regression posterior, SGLRW achieves KL=3.845 at B=1, δ₀=10⁻² compared to SGLD's 10.300 and Clipped-SGLD's 4.162. On IMDB sentiment classification with OPT-350M embeddings, SGLRW consistently attains lower NLL and improved calibration (ECE) relative to Clipped-SGLD at increased learning-rate scales, with the advantage most pronounced in small-to-moderate minibatch regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard SGLD (Welling & Teh, 2011), SGLRW achieves substantially lower KL divergence across nearly all batch-size and step-size configurations: at B=8, δ₀=10⁻³ the gap is 3.3× (6.060 vs. 19.853), and SGLRW reaches the Monte Carlo floor at B≥64 where SGLD still shows measurable bias. Against Clipped-SGLD (the paper's own strong baseline applying componentwise drift clipping to SGLD), SGLRW consistently achieves lower KL on linear regression (e.g., 0.189 vs. 1.169 at B=64, δ₀=10⁻³) and on logistic regression in the small-batch regime (3.845 vs. 4.162 at B=1, δ₀=10⁻²), though Clipped-SGLD occasionally matches or slightly beats SGLRW at larger batch sizes with smaller step sizes (e.g., B=64, δ₀=10⁻¹ on breast cancer: SGLD 0.158, SGLRW 0.183, Clipped-SGLD 0.206). Against cyclic SGLD (Zhang et al.), cyclic SGLRW is stable across the full (B, δ₀) grid while cyclic SGLD diverges at δ₀=10⁻² for every batch size and at δ₀=10⁻³ for B≤16; where both converge, cyclic SGLRW reaches the Monte Carlo floor (e.g., 0.059 vs. 0.082 at B=64, δ₀=10⁻³). Against preconditioned SGLD (Li et al., 2016) on a d=100 anisotropic Gaussian (κ=200), pSGLRW achieves lower KL than pSGLD at every tested step size (e.g., 0.637 vs. 0.764 at δ₀=0.03), while vanilla SGLD diverges at all but the smallest step size and vanilla SGLRW remains stable but with elevated KL (28.72 at δ₀=0.03).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lattice Random Walk (LRW) discretisation of overdamped Langevin dynamics (Duffield et al., 2025a) to the stochastic-gradient setting, replacing the Gaussian increments of SGLD with bounded binary coordinate-wise updates. The theoretical analysis builds on the Poisson-equation MSE framework of Chen et al. (2015), extended to capture second-order covariance contributions, and shows that the lattice structure confines minibatch noise to off-diagonal covariance entries. The method is positioned as orthogonal to existing variance-reduction, preconditioning, and cyclic-schedule techniques, and the paper introduces Clipped-SGLD as a principled baseline analogous to gradient clipping in SGD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SGLRW enables stable Bayesian posterior sampling with small minibatches—a regime where SGLD diverges—making it relevant for large-scale models where memory or data constraints limit batch size. The method is composable with cyclic schedules and preconditioning, and its bounded-update structure is naturally suited to stochastic and thermodynamic hardware. Open questions include characterising the invariant distribution of the discretised chain (the MSE bound does not establish convergence in distribution), analytically bounding the per-coordinate clipping bias, extending the off-diagonal covariance argument to second-order schemes like SGHMC, and providing a theoretical account of the observed heavy-tailed noise robustness.
