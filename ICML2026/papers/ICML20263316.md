## Problem

Large language models exhibit complementary reasoning errors—on the same instance, one model may succeed while another fails—yet existing ensembling and multi-agent methods exploit this diversity only at inference time without changing the underlying policies. The open question is whether this complementarity can be pushed into training so that models actively learn to reduce correlated failures and become better collaborators, while avoiding diversity collapse through mutual imitation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263316/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each training instance is a reasoning problem x with ground-truth answer y*. In ROUND A, models sample K independent solution traces (token sequences with intermediate steps) from the original prompt; in ROUND B, failed models resample K' traces optionally conditioned on a teacher context (the best successful peer's strategy block, capped at 1536 tokens with explicit answer lines stripped to prevent leakage). Outputs are solution traces from which a final answer is extracted via a pattern-matching function; evaluation uses a 4096-token generation budget with Pass@K and oracle Team Pass@K metrics.

## Method

CORE trains N LLM policies jointly via a two-round micro-protocol: a cold round of independent sampling followed by a contexted rescue round where failed models receive a hint extracted from a successful peer's trace, with hint dropout for robustness. The combined reward simultaneously optimizes correctness (with partial credit), a DPP-lite exploration term that encourages diverse reasoning traces via greedy subset selection over hybrid semantic/structural distances, an explicit rescue bonus for successful recovery on peer-disagreement cases, and a cross-model complementarity term that discourages redundant high-quality traces across collaborators. A decomposition theorem shows that collaboration gain over the best single model equals the probability of complementary correctness (the weaker model is right where the stronger is wrong), motivating the reward design to reduce error overlap. Policy updates use group-based RL (GRPO-compatible) with group-normalized advantages, and all models are fine-tuned with LoRA under a low-data regime of at most 1,000 training examples.

## Results

With only 1,000 training examples, the Phi-4-mini-reasoning + Ministral-3-3B-Reasoning pair trained with CORE reaches 96.72% oracle Team Pass@2 on GSM8K, 92.08% on MATH, 79.65% on AIME, and 77.34% on GPQA. The Qwen2.5-3B + Qwen3-4B pair achieves 99.54% Team Pass@2 on GSM8K and 80.80% on MATH. Individual model Pass@1 also improves substantially (e.g., Phi-4-mini goes from 72.40% Base to 90.14% CORE on GSM8K), and a 3-model non-oracle AIME evaluation shows majority vote at 81.20% vs. 82.60% oracle Team Pass@2, closing most of the oracle-selection gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SD-E² (Mishra et al., 2026), a diverse-exploration training method for small-model reasoning. CORE outperforms the SD-E² oracle pair on every benchmark for both model pairs: on the Phi+Ministral pair, Team Pass@2 improves from 85.50% to 96.72% on GSM8K (+11.22 pp), from 77.70% to 92.08% on MATH (+14.38 pp), from 60.19% to 79.65% on AIME (+19.46 pp), and from 55.26% to 77.34% on GPQA (+22.08 pp). On the Qwen pair, the corresponding gains are +12.34 pp (GSM8K), +17.70 pp (MATH), +2.55 pp (AIME), and +5.85 pp (GPQA). CORE also substantially exceeds the Base oracle pair (two off-the-shelf models with oracle selection), confirming that gains come from training-time collaboration rather than merely using two models at test time. Individual model Pass@1 also improves over SD-E² (e.g., Phi-4-mini on AIME: 53.92% → 68.16%), indicating that cross-teaching reshapes each collaborator's policy rather than only exploiting pre-existing diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

CORE extends the diverse-exploration line of work initiated by SD-E² (Mishra et al., 2026) from within-model trace diversity to cross-model complementarity, repurposing DPP-based repulsion (Kulesza & Taskar, 2012) as a training-time exploration reward rather than an inference-time decoding strategy. It departs from multi-agent RL frameworks like MAPoRL and MALT, which use fixed role pipelines or verifier rewards over multi-turn discussion, by making the learning signal instance-conditioned: a peer's correct trace on the same problem becomes a targeted hint for failed collaborators, with an explicit rescue bonus on the disagreement set. The policy optimization layer is deliberately loss-agnostic (GRPO, GSPO, SAPO), positioning CORE as a data-collection and reward-shaping protocol orthogonal to the choice of group-based policy gradient objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication is that a pair of small open-source models (≤7B combined) trained on at most 1,000 examples can substantially outperform independently trained oracle pairs, suggesting that training-time collaboration reshapes policies in ways that inference-time ensembling cannot replicate. The decomposition theorem (collaboration gain = complementary correctness) provides a clean theoretical target, and the near-closure of the oracle-to-majority-vote gap on AIME (1.4 pp) indicates the gains are deployable without perfect selection. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond two collaborators without group collapse, how to handle the 'both-fail' regime on very hard instances where no teacher is available, and whether the gains persist under distribution shift or with more heterogeneous model families. The truncation of long teacher contexts and the lightweight hybrid distance remain acknowledged limitations that could cap performance on proof-heavy or knowledge-intensive tasks.
