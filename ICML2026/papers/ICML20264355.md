## Problem

Conditionally positive definite (CPD) kernels with respect to a general function class F have been largely neglected in the literature, with most prior work restricted to polynomial F. This paper develops the first non-trivial statistical theory of learning with CPD kernels for general F, establishing when and why injecting unpenalized features into kernel ridge regression improves test risk over standard KRR.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264355/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training set of N input-output pairs, a symmetric continuous kernel K (which may be merely CPD rather than PD), k linearly independent continuous functions f_1,...,f_k defining the unpenalized feature space F, and a regularization parameter λ. The output is a trained regression function f̂ that decomposes into an unpenalized F-component and a penalized residual component. Unusually, the regularization term is a semi-norm (not a full norm) that vanishes on F, and the method is equivalently a two-stage procedure: linear regression on F followed by standard KRR on the residual targets using a derived residual kernel K_P.

## Method

The core construction defines a residual kernel K_P = ((I−Π_P)⊗(I−Π_P))[K] that is positive definite, where Π_P is the L_2(𝒳,P)-projection onto F. The paper proves that the native space of the CPD kernel K w.r.t. F is isomorphic to the RKHS of K_P plus F (Theorem 3.1), and that conditional KRR is exactly equivalent to linear regression on F followed by standard KRR on the residual data with kernel K_P (Theorem 3.2). The main statistical result (Theorem 4.2) bounds the 'cost of conditioning'—the gap between conditional KRR and an ideal learner with oracle access to the F-component—by O(√(k log k)/√N), showing conditional KRR behaves like standard KRR with a modified kernel up to a vanishing perturbation. Applications to hard thresholding (F = span of top-k Mercer eigenfunctions) and soft thresholding (F = span of k random Gaussian features with covariance K) yield explicit conditions under which conditional KRR strictly outperforms standard KRR, producing a U-shaped test error as a function of k.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) bounds the cost of conditioning — the gap between conditional KRR and an ideal F-conditional learner — by O(k·log^{1/2}(2k/δ)/√N) + O(σ²(k+1)/N) with probability at least 1−δ. Experiments on synthetic data confirm that the cost of conditioning actually decays as O(1/N) in practice, faster than the theoretical O(1/√N) bound, and scales linearly in k and σ² as predicted. On the 7-vs-9 MNIST task (12,214 samples, 10-fold CV, λ=0.01), both the Gaussian and NNGP-erf kernels exhibit a U-shaped dependence of test MSE on k (number of unpenalized principal eigenfunctions), validating the sufficient condition (3) for conditional KRR to outperform standard KRR. For pure-noise targets (f=0), test MSE increases monotonically with k across all experiments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between conditional KRR (with k unpenalized features) and standard KRR (k=0, no unpenalized features). The paper demonstrates that conditional KRR strictly outperforms standard KRR whenever the F-component of the regression signal is sufficiently strong relative to the residual, as formalized by inequality (3). On MNIST 7-vs-9, the Gaussian and NNGP-erf kernels both show U-shaped test MSE curves: performance improves over standard KRR for moderate k before degrading at large k (overfitting), with the NNGP-erf kernel showing only mild overfitting near k≈11,000. The Laplace kernel shows no overfitting within the tested range, attributed to its slowly decaying eigenvalue spectrum. In the soft-thresholding setting with random features (cosine, ReLU, tanh activations on S^{d-1}), the same U-shaped behavior is observed, confirming that the phenomenon is generic across kernel families. For pure-noise datasets, no benefit is observed — test MSE increases monotonically with k, indicating that unpenalized features are harmful without a genuine signal component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical theory of conditionally positive definite (CPD) kernels—long studied in approximation theory (Wahba, Poggio & Girosi, Schaback & Wendland)—to a general function class F, going beyond the standard polynomial case. Theorem 3.1 generalizes Cucker & Smale's (2001) characterization of the native space for PD kernels to the CPD setting, while the residual kernel construction extends Meinguet's (1979) interpolation kernel. The statistical analysis builds on the eigenlearning framework (Simon et al., 2023) and double-descent literature (Mei & Montanari, 2022), but the key new contribution is the 'cost of conditioning' bound that reduces conditional KRR to standard KRR with a modified kernel, connecting this two-stage procedure to the ResMem paradigm (Yang et al., 2023) and boosting (Freund & Schapire, 1997).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous statistical theory for conditional KRR, showing that the cost of conditioning is O(1/√N) and characterizing precisely when unpenalized features improve test risk (inequality 3). The demonstration that hard thresholding (top-k eigenfunctions) and soft thresholding (k random Gaussian features) are closely related unifies two previously separate perspectives on kernel truncation. A key open question is extending the analysis to λ=0, where perfect memorization occurs, which would connect the framework to the benign overfitting literature. The U-shaped test error as a function of k offers a practical diagnostic for choosing the number of unpenalized features in kernel methods.
