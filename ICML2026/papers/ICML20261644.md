## Problem

Block-wise diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) enable parallel token generation but suffer from a severe accuracy-parallelism trade-off: raising tokens per forward (TPF) via aggressive parallel decoding consistently degrades task accuracy. Existing acceleration strategies (training-free sampling, distillation) can boost TPF but at the cost of substantial quality loss, leaving dLLMs inferior to autoregressive speculative decoding when speed and quality are considered jointly. No prior post-training method directly optimizes the speed-quality frontier as a joint objective.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (math word problem or code task) and a pretrained block-wise dLLM (SDAR-8B with block size 32) as input. During training, the model generates groups of 32 rollout trajectories per prompt using confidence-driven block diffusion decoding (threshold φ=0.9, temperature 1.0, 32 denoising steps per block, max response length 8192). The output is a generated text sequence (math solution or code), evaluated on accuracy, TPF (parallelism), and AUP (accuracy under parallelism), with the trained policy producing faster, more parallel decoding without sacrificing correctness.

## Method

LightningRL is a post-training RL framework built on GRPO that jointly optimizes accuracy and inference parallelism for block-wise dLLMs, with the core insight that the model need only navigate the subspace of trajectories that are both highly parallelizable and correct, rather than decoding aggressively along all paths. Three key modifications address multi-objective instability: (1) per-reward decoupled normalization independently standardizes accuracy and TPF rewards within each group before aggregation, preventing the discrete correctness signal from dominating the continuous speed signal; (2) a token-level NLL loss on verifier-correct trajectories converts sparse sequence-level rewards into dense token-factorized supervision, anchoring the policy against drift into fast-but-incorrect modes; (3) TPF-aware dynamic sampling rejects prompt groups where all rollouts are incorrect or where TPF values are near-tied, preserving diverse learning signals and avoiding gradient starvation. The combined objective is L_GRPO + μ·L_NLL, optimized with AdamW (lr 1e-6) over 20 epochs on 8 H200 GPUs.

## Results

LightningRL-8B-b32 achieves an average AUP of 437.8 with an average TPF of 7.32 and 71.1% average accuracy across GSM8K, MATH500, MBPP, and HumanEval, substantially outperforming the SDAR-8B-b32 base model (211.2 AUP, 3.12 TPF). On MBPP, LightningRL reaches 11.10 TPF with 58.3% accuracy (412.9 AUP), and on GSM8K it attains 90.3% accuracy at 5.58 TPF (507.5 AUP). In wall-clock terms, LightningRL achieves 336.0 TPS on H100 GPUs, surpassing d3LLM-LLaDA (288.9 TPS) and SDAR (105.6 TPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightningRL-8B-b32 dominates all 13 baselines on the AUP metric across all four benchmarks. Against the strongest dLLM baseline d3LLM-LLaDA (416.1 AUP on GSM8K, 9.11 TPF), LightningRL achieves 507.5 AUP at 5.58 TPF, trading some raw parallelism for substantially higher accuracy (90.3% vs. 73.1%). Against the leading AR acceleration method EAGLE-3 (276.5 AUP on GSM8K, 5.12 TPF), LightningRL nearly doubles the AUP while maintaining comparable TPF. On MBPP, LightningRL's 11.10 TPF and 412.9 AUP far exceed EAGLE-3 (5.69 TPF, 300.7 AUP) and d3LLM-LLaDA (4.21 TPF, 88.4 AUP). Compared to the SDAR-8B-b32 base model, LightningRL improves AUP by 2.1× on average (437.8 vs. 211.2) while maintaining nearly identical accuracy (71.1% vs. 71.1%), confirming that the gains come from parallelism rather than accuracy sacrifice. Among RL-based dLLM methods (Table 1), LightningRL outperforms DiRL (414.1 AUP on GSM8K) and TraceRL (378.6 AUP) on both accuracy and TPF, with the largest gap on MBPP (412.9 vs. 152.7 and 144.2 AUP respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) from single-objective AR-LLM post-training to a multi-objective setting for block-wise diffusion LLMs, building on SDAR (Cheng et al., 2025) as the base architecture. It departs from prior dLLM RL work (TraceRL, DiRL) that optimizes accuracy alone and treats parallelism as a mere inference-time knob, by elevating TPF to a first-class training objective. It also contrasts with training-free or distillation-based dLLM acceleration methods (Fast-dLLM, d3LLM, dParallel) that boost throughput at the cost of accuracy, instead learning to navigate the speed-quality Pareto frontier directly through policy optimization with three stabilization mechanisms (decoupled normalization, NLL anchoring, TPF-aware filtering).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LightningRL demonstrates that the accuracy-parallelism trade-off in block-wise dLLMs is not a fundamental architectural limitation but a training objective gap, with the 8B model achieving 437.8 AUP and 336 TPS on H100—roughly 6× the throughput of a comparable AR model at higher accuracy. The finding that a learned value model actively degrades performance (80.7% vs 90.3% accuracy) challenges the standard PPO/GAE recipe and suggests that non-smooth block-wise state transitions make scalar critics unreliable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the SDAR family and math/code domains, how it scales to longer contexts, and whether the decoupled-normalization principle transfers to other multi-objective RL settings in diffusion models.
