## Problem

Tuning stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (SGLD) and SGD for calibrated uncertainty quantification remains difficult at large batch sizes and under model misspecification. Existing continuous-time SDE approximations become quantitatively inaccurate precisely in the large-batch regime (which necessitates large step sizes), while existing discrete-time approaches either assume constant gradient noise, restrict to well-specified linear models, or lack non-asymptotic error guarantees for mixing time and iterate-average covariance.

## Input / Output

The procedure takes a dataset of N observations, a per-sample differentiable loss, a batch size B, inverse temperature β, and a number of iterations T. Tuning is performed offline on a subsample of size M: the sandwich covariance target is estimated from empirical Hessian and Fisher information matrices, then the step size matrix Λ is obtained by numerically solving the coupled matrix equations (11) and (12) via scipy.optimize.root. The output is a set of T SG(L)D posterior samples whose stationary covariance approximates the sandwich covariance, enabling calibrated frequentist or Bayesian uncertainty quantification. Computational cost is O(MD² + D³) offline plus O(T(BD + D²)) for sampling, with the D³ term incurred only once.

## Method

The core contribution is a discrete-time proxy algorithm that applies a second-order Taylor expansion to each observation-level loss, yielding a linear update equation (Eq. 8) whose stationary covariance can be characterized exactly. Proposition 4.2 gives a closed-form Lyapunov-type equation linking the step size matrix Λ, the stationary covariance Σ_ψ, and the minibatch noise covariance C̄_ψ; Theorem 4.3 then computes C̄_ψ exactly (for Gaussian priors and with/without replacement sampling), eliminating the implicit dependency that plagues prior discrete-time results. Non-asymptotic 2-Wasserstein error bounds (Theorem 4.5, Corollary 4.6) show the proxy covariance approximates the true SG(L)D stationary covariance within O(λ/B), with explicit dimension-dependent constants. The worst-case integrated autocorrelation time is shown to equal 2/μ_min(ΛĤ) − 1 iterations (Proposition 4.4), improving on the continuous-time heuristic. Algorithm 1 packages these results into a tuning-free calibration procedure: estimate the sandwich covariance on a subsample, solve for Λ, then run preconditioned SG(L)D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three existing tuning approaches: CT (continuous-time SDE limits from Negrea et al., 2023), LR+WS (discrete-time with well-specified linear regression assumptions from Ziyin et al., 2022), and DQ+const (discrete-time quadratic with constant noise from Dieuleveut et al., 2020). DQ+exact wins decisively in the large-batch regime: at B=⌊0.1×N⌋ with log loss, it reduces covariance error from 0.975 (CT) and 0.996 (LR+WS) to 0.672, a 31–33% relative improvement. On the Boston housing dataset where the linear model is strongly misspecified, LR+WS diverges to ∞ while DQ+exact remains stable at 0.352 vs. CT's 0.589. For Poisson regression, DQ+const (Dieuleveut et al.) suffers catastrophic miscalibration (calibration error 1.376, covariance error 0.991 at large batch) because its constant-noise assumption is violated, while DQ+exact matches or beats CT (0.211 vs. 0.230 cov. err.). At small batch sizes (B=16), CT and DQ+exact are roughly comparable, confirming that the continuous-time approximation is adequate only when the step size is small.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work on tuning stochastic gradient methods for sampling: continuous-time SDE limits (Mandt et al. 2017; Negrea et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2025) that provide elegant but asymptotic tuning rules, and discrete-time Markov chain analyses (Dieuleveut et al. 2020; Liu et al. 2021; Ziyin et al. 2022) that capture finite learning-rate effects but are restricted to constant noise, linear models, or well-specified settings. The key departure is a discrete-time proxy algorithm whose stationary noise covariance (Theorem 4.3) is computed exactly for general observation-level losses with a Gaussian prior, removing the constant-noise and well-specification assumptions of prior discrete-time work while retaining the large-batch accuracy that continuous-time methods lack. The paper also extends the analysis to momentum and provides the first non-asymptotic Wasserstein error bounds connecting the proxy to the true SG(L)D chain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a tuning-free procedure (Algorithm 1) that yields calibrated uncertainty quantification via SG(L)D at large batch sizes and under model misspecification or generalized losses (e.g., β-divergence), settings where existing continuous-time and constant-noise tuning rules produce substantially miscalibrated posteriors. A key limitation is that the non-asymptotic error bounds require strong convexity, though the stationary covariance characterization itself does not; extending guarantees to non-convex models (e.g., neural networks) is identified as important future work. The O(D³) cost of solving for the preconditioner matrix Λ also limits immediate applicability to very high-dimensional problems, motivating structured or low-rank approximations.
