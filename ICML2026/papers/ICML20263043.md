## Problem

Standard attention treats scores as a probability distribution over positions, hard-tying the hidden representation to a fixed positional basis where coordinate i always corresponds to the i-th token. This prevents the head from learning task-adapted mixing across the sequence dimension, and the probability-simplex constraint may be unnecessarily restrictive. The paper asks whether an autoregressive attention head can instead be treated as a dynamic two-layer MLP whose ever-growing hidden width admits learned, input-conditioned mixing in both feature and sequence spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an autoregressive token prefix X_{t:1} = [x_t; x_{t-1}; …; x_1] in reverse-offset (lag) order, where the current token x_t serves as the query and the remaining t−1 tokens form the context. The hidden pre-activation h_t ∈ R^{1×t} grows in width with context length, and the output is a standard d-dimensional representation o_t that feeds into the next-token prediction head. The lag ordering is critical: it ensures that canonical prefix extension of the learned sequence-mixing operators aligns with autoregressive truncation, so appending older history does not alter the output for the current step.

## Method

HyperMLP reframes each attention head as a dynamic two-layer MLP o_t = σ(x_t W^{(1)}(X_{t:1})) W^{(2)}(X_{t:1}), where the effective weights are instantiated from context via a core-on-context factorization: W^{(1)} = L^{(1)}(x_t) X_{t:1}^⊤ R^{(1)}(x_t) and W^{(2)} = R^{(2)⊤}(x_t) X_{t:1} L^{(2)⊤}(x_t). Feature-space mixing L^{(j)} uses low-rank projections (W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o) with input-conditioned diagonal gates M^{(j)}(x_t), while sequence-space mixing R^{(j)} uses a diagonal-plus-low-rank (DPLR) form D^{(j)} + A^{(j)} S^{(j)}(x_t) B^{(j)⊤} that learns input-conditioned temporal mixing without materializing t×t matrices. The activation replaces softmax with ReLU∘L2Norm (HyperMLP) or a GLU-style split into separate routing and magnitude branches (HyperGLU), and the reverse-offset lag layout guarantees autoregressive extension consistency. Under a matched 4d² parameter budget, the QK rank is shrunk to fund the sequence-mixing operators while the VO/readout rank is preserved, consistent with the theoretical finding that readout rank directly constrains the update subspace.

## Results

At 1.3B parameters / 100B tokens, HyperGLU achieves the best aggregate ranking (AvgRank 1.88) with 9 category-best scores across 17 benchmarks, compared to SoftmaxAttn (4.22), GSA (2.84), and DeltaNet (4.22). At 340M / 15B tokens, HyperGLU again leads with AvgRank 2.31 and 6 top-1 wins, ahead of HyperMLP (2.97), GatedDeltaNet (3.81), and SoftmaxAttn (5.28). In the controlled MAD benchmark study, HyperGLU (G-cg-q-12o) reaches MAD Avg 81.12 and NanoGPT loss 2.9865, outperforming the best softmax baseline S-pc (80.15 / 3.0466) and the best ReLU baseline R-pc (80.50 / 3.0481) under matched parameter budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperGLU outperforms all 10 baselines on aggregate ranking at both scales. At 1.3B/100B, HyperGLU (AvgRank 1.88, 9 top-1s) beats the closest competitor GSA (2.84, 4 top-1s) by a wide margin, and substantially outperforms SoftmaxAttn (4.22), DeltaNet (4.22), GLA (5.19), HGRN (5.12), HGRN2 (5.84), and RetNet (6.69). At 340M/15B, HyperGLU (2.31, 6 top-1s) leads GatedDeltaNet (3.81), DiffTrans (4.13), DeltaNet (4.81), ReLUAttn+KVConv (5.13), SoftmaxAttn (5.28), and GLA (7.56). The gains are broad rather than isolated: HyperGLU achieves top-1 scores on 9 of 17 benchmarks at 1.3B (including MMLU-P, GPQA, BBH, MATH, IFE-I, IFE-P, ARC-C, ARC-E, SciQ) and 6 of 17 at 340M. In the MAD controlled study, the key ablation shows that adding DPLR sequence mixing with lag layout to ReLU attention (R-cg-q → R-cg-q-12o) yields a +14.23 MAD Avg jump (66.78 → 81.01), while removing the lag layout causes catastrophic collapse (R-12!: 46.28 vs R-12o!: 81.82), confirming that the architectural gain is specifically from learned temporal mixing aligned with autoregressive semantics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the autoregressive attention head (Vaswani et al., 2017) as a dynamic two-layer MLP whose weights are instantiated from the context, building directly on ReLU attention (Richter & Wattenhofer, 2020) to replace the probability-simplex constraint with standard MLP activations (ReLU/GLU per Shazeer, 2020). The key departure from prior work is the introduction of learned sequence-space mixing (DPLR operators) on the ever-growing hidden/score dimension, combined with a reverse-offset (lag) layout that ensures autoregressive truncation consistency. The paper explicitly distinguishes itself from fast-weight programmer views (Schmidhuber, 1992; Schlag et al., 2021) and MLP-Mixer-style token mixers (Tolstikhin et al., 2021) by operating on the context-instantiated score space rather than the input token space, and from subquadratic attention variants by preserving quadratic expressivity while adding learned temporal structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The attention-as-dynamic-MLP lens provides a clean theoretical explanation for several previously empirical design choices (prefer QK over VO compression, LoRA most effective on V/O projections, gating benefits), which could guide future architecture design and parameter-efficient fine-tuning. The lag-layout requirement—where sequence mixing without correct temporal alignment catastrophically collapses (MAD avg drops from ~82 to ~46)—is a non-trivial constraint that any future sequence-mixing operator must respect. However, the practical impact is currently limited: the implementation cannot leverage FlashAttention-style kernels, and validation is restricted to 340M and 1.3B parameter scales, leaving open whether the expressivity gains persist at frontier model sizes where inductive biases and data scale may shift the balance.
