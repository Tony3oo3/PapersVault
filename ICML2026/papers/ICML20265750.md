## Problem

Current answer-centric alignment for large reasoning models (LRMs) optimizes final-answer compliance with safety rules but fails to build intrinsic safety understanding: empirically, aligned models cannot reliably verify whether their own outputs are safe, performing at or below random-guess on simple binary safety classification. This gap makes them vulnerable to jailbreaks that hijack chain-of-thought reasoning and yields brittle defenses against unseen attacks, because the model mimics safe behavior without learning the underlying concept of safety.

## Input / Output

During the SInternal training phase, the model receives a prompt x, its own sampled answer y_k, and explicit safety specifications S, and must produce a verification critique (a reasoning trace analyzing potential violations) followed by a binary safe/unsafe judgment. During generation, the model takes a text prompt and produces a CoT reasoning trace plus a final answer; the internalized verification capability is expected to spontaneously trigger safety scrutiny within the CoT. The verification dataset is constructed contrastively: for harmful prompts, exactly one safe and one unsafe self-generated response are retained per prompt, while benign prompts contribute a single verification trajectory.

## Method

SInternal internalizes safety specifications by training the LRM exclusively on safety verification of its own generated answers, rather than on expert-curated safe trajectories. Concretely, the model samples N=8 responses per prompt; an expert model (Claude-4-Sonnet) evaluates each response against safety specifications to produce a verification trajectory (critique reasoning + binary judgment); the policy model is then SFT'd via LoRA to reproduce these verification trajectories conditioned on (S, x, y). The key design choice is verifying self-generated responses (including unsafe ones) so the model learns safety boundaries aligned with its actual output distribution. Building on SInternal, the paper applies GRPO reinforcement learning with verifiable safety rewards from Qwen3-Guard-gen, showing that the internalized verification understanding provides a superior RL initialization compared to standard SFT baselines. Ablations confirm that the critique component drives generalization to unseen jailbreaks while the judgment component stabilizes in-domain safety, and that SInternal achieves higher data efficiency than SFT-based alignment.

## Results

On DS-14B, SInternal achieves ASR of 19.2% on Fortress, 6.8% on WildJailbreak, 1.3% on Jailbreak-R1, and 90.0% on HCoT, substantially outperforming both SafeChain (48.2/45.2/32.9/100.0) and STAR-1 (28.2/18.4/5.1/100.0) on out-of-distribution and LRM-specific jailbreaks. SInternal maintains competitive over-refusal (XSTest CR 98.0%) and general reasoning (MATH 89.6, AIME 86.7), matching or exceeding baselines. After adding GRPO (Table 2), SInternal(+GRPO) further reduces Fortress ASR to 5.2% and WildJailbreak to 0.4%, while uniquely achieving 62.0% ASR on HCoT (vs. 92–98% for baselines), demonstrating that internalized verification provides a superior RL initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SInternal is compared against two SFT-based alignment baselines: SafeChain (CoT safety distillation from larger models) and STAR-1 (deliberative reasoning over safety specifications). On DS-14B, SInternal decisively outperforms both on out-of-distribution jailbreaks: Fortress (19.2 vs. 48.2 SafeChain, 28.2 STAR-1), WildJailbreak (6.8 vs. 45.2, 18.4), and Jailbreak-R1 (1.3 vs. 32.9, 5.1). On LRM-specific attacks, SInternal achieves 90.0% ASR on HCoT and 33.3% on Trotter, versus 100.0%/44.4% (SafeChain) and 100.0%/49.0% (STAR-1), indicating that verification training uniquely resists CoT-hijacking attacks. STAR-1 matches SInternal on in-domain StrongReject (both 0.6%), but SInternal recovers the over-refusal gap (XSTest 98.0 vs. 94.0) and preserves AIME performance (86.7 vs. 83.3). After GRPO, SInternal(+GRPO) achieves the lowest ASR across nearly all safety benchmarks (Fortress 5.2, WildJailbreak 0.4, Jailbreak-R1 0.3) and is the only method to meaningfully defend against HCoT (62.0% vs. 92–98%), confirming that internalized safety understanding yields a stronger RL foundation than imitation-based SFT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant answer-centric alignment paradigm for large reasoning models (SafeChain, STAR-1, Constitutional AI-style SFT, and RL with verifiable safety rewards), which trains models to imitate safe outputs or be rewarded for compliance. Rather than building on a specific prior method, SInternal repurposes the safety verification task—previously confined to external guardrails like Llama Guard and Constitutional Classifiers—as the sole supervised training signal for the generator model itself. The conceptual lineage draws from the observation that verification and generation are decoupled in current pipelines, and the follow-up RL stage (GRPO) follows the standard post-SFT alignment recipe but benefits from the verification-grounded initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a core assumption in LRM safety: that producing safe answers implies understanding safety. The finding that aligned models cannot reliably verify the safety of their own outputs, and that verification training alone generalizes better to unseen jailbreaks than safe-answer imitation, reframes how the field should think about alignment depth. Open questions include extending verification to dynamic self-checking during reasoning (rather than post-hoc), closing the remaining gap where verification capability still lags generation, and whether the approach transfers to non-DeepSeek architectures or multimodal settings. Practically, SInternal's simplicity (LoRA SFT on ~6K verification examples) and its role as a superior RL initialization make it an attractive drop-in for existing alignment pipelines.
