## Problem

Existing diffusion and flow-matching posterior sampling methods for inverse problems treat data consistency (DC) guidance, classifier-free guidance (CFG), and stochasticity as independently tuned or fixed hyperparameters, ignoring their coupled temporal interactions. The paper identifies that aggressive CFG early in sampling directly conflicts with DC guidance (hindering residual reduction), while stochasticity acts as a regularizer pulling trajectories back toward high-probability regions—yet no prior work jointly schedules all three components in a time-varying manner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy linear measurements y = Ax₀ + n (with σₙ = 0.03), the known forward operator A (bicubic downsampling for SR×8/×12, 61×61 motion/Gaussian blur kernels, or binary masks for inpainting), and a text prompt for semantic conditioning. It produces a single restored RGB image at 768×768 (flow matching, SD3.5-M, 28 NFEs) or 512×512 (diffusion, SD1.5, 50 NFEs). The triadic schedules β(t), λ(t), η(t) are optimized per-task on a 100-image calibration set and then applied zero-shot to held-out test images.

## Method

TriPS reformulates posterior sampling as a time-varying control problem over three coupled scales, guided by a theoretical analysis (Proposition 1) showing that the first-order derivative of expected residual descent w.r.t. CFG scale equals −Δt⟨b̃_dc, b̃_cfg⟩, formalizing the early-stage DC–CFG conflict. The resulting triadic scheduling trend prescribes monotonically decreasing DC and stochasticity scales alongside increasing CFG scale. Two complementary optimization strategies instantiate this trend: (i) TriPS_T performs grid search over 27 template combinations (linear/exponential/logarithmic for each scale) maximizing a PSNR+LPIPS utility on a calibration set, and (ii) TriPS_G uses GRPO-based reinforcement learning where a policy π_θ samples Bernstein polynomial coefficients from Beta distributions (degree d=25, 78 total parameters), constrained to valid ranges via the convex-hull property, and is updated via a clipped surrogate objective with a hybrid IQA reward (PSNR, LPIPS, CLIPIQA+, Q-Align) and KL regularization against the template-based warm-start.

## Results

On the flow matching backbone (SD3.5-M, 28 NFE), TriPS_T achieves the best PSNR across most tasks, reaching 29.03 dB on SR×8 (FFHQ) and 31.20 dB on Motion Deblurring (FFHQ), while TriPS_G attains the best perceptual metrics (FID 15.89, LPIPS 0.059, MUSIQ 64.37) on Motion Deblurring. On the diffusion backbone (SD1.5, 50 NFE), TriPS_G improves PSNR by ~1.7 dB over the strongest baseline TReg on SR×8 (28.02 vs. 26.34) and by ~2.0 dB on Motion Deblurring (28.39 vs. 26.36), while also reducing FID from 128.19 to 43.04 and 57.94 to 26.37 respectively. TriPS_G schedules optimized on SR×8 transfer directly to Gaussian Deblurring and SR×12, still outperforming all baselines on both distortion and perceptual metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among flow matching baselines, FLAIR is the strongest competitor, achieving the best FID on Gaussian Deblurring (18.41 vs. TriPS_G's 21.21) and competitive PSNR on SR×8 (28.88 vs. 29.03 for TriPS_T), but TriPS dominates on Motion Deblurring by a large margin (31.20 vs. 28.80 PSNR, 15.89 vs. 21.57 FID). FlowDPS is competitive on FID for SR×8 (23.80) but trails TriPS_G (22.18) and is substantially weaker on deblurring tasks. FlowChef and ReSample are consistently outperformed across all metrics and tasks. On the diffusion backbone (SD1.5), TReg is the strongest baseline (26.34 PSNR on SR×8), yet TriPS_G surpasses it by 1.68 dB in PSNR and reduces FID from 128.19 to 43.04; P2L and DDPG are intermediate, while PSLD is the weakest diffusion baseline across all tasks. Notably, TriPS_G schedules optimized on SR×8 transfer zero-shot to Gaussian Deblurring and SR×12, still beating all flow matching baselines (e.g., 28.90 PSNR vs. FLAIR's 27.74 on Gaussian Deblurring).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the flow-based and diffusion-based posterior sampling framework for inverse problems (FlowDPS, FLAIR, FlowChef, DDPG, P2L, TReg), which all decompose the reverse step into data consistency guidance, classifier-free guidance, and stochasticity. The key departure is that prior methods treat these three components as fixed constants or schedule them individually (e.g., only DC scale or only stochasticity), whereas TriPS argues their *joint* time-varying coordination is the critical missing piece. The theoretical contribution (Proposition 1 formalizing the DC-CFG conflict) and the identification of stochasticity as a manifold regularizer provide a principled basis for the proposed triadic trend (β↓, λ↑, η↓). The GRPO-based schedule optimization repurposes a reinforcement learning technique from LLM training (Shao et al., 2024) to a novel domain: optimizing continuous temporal curves parameterized via Bernstein polynomials with Beta-distributed coefficients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The triadic scheduling framework offers a practical, interpretable recipe for practitioners tuning diffusion/flow-based inverse solvers, and the GRPO-based optimizer provides an automated path to navigate the perception-distortion trade-off without manual hyperparameter search. The schedule transferability result (SR×8-optimized schedules generalize to deblurring and SR×12) suggests the learned dynamics capture task-agnostic structure, reducing per-task tuning cost. Limitations include evaluation only on linear inverse problems and two datasets (FFHQ, DIV2K), the inpainting exception that breaks the monotonic β(t)↓ trend, and the requirement of a 100-image calibration set for both template search and GRPO. Open questions include whether the triadic trend extends to non-linear/ill-posed operators, whether the GRPO-optimized curves converge to a stable optimum, and how the framework scales to video or 3D reconstruction.
