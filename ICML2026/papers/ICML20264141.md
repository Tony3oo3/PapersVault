## Problem

Existing speech restoration models assume matched input-output sampling rates, forcing redundant resampling and tying learned representations to a fixed spectral grid. Supporting multiple output rates typically requires rate-specific models or repeated conversion, and TF dual-path models that naturally support sampling-frequency-independent processing still assume matched rates while incurring prohibitive computational cost at higher rates. The paper formulates this gap as the extended SFI (xSFI) setting, where a single model must operate under arbitrary decoupled input-output rate pairs without external resampling.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a degraded speech waveform at native sampling rate f_E (e.g., 8 or 16 kHz) and a user-specified output rate f_D (e.g., 16, 24, 44.1, or 48 kHz), producing a restored waveform at f_D. Internally, both signals are represented via SFI-STFT with a fixed 40 ms window and 20 ms hop, so the number of frequency bins scales proportionally with the sampling rate (F_E : F_D = f_E : f_D) while preserving a consistent physical frame duration. No external resampling is performed; the model directly maps the F_E-bin complex spectrum to an F_D-bin complex spectrum.

## Method

TF-Restormer is a query-based asymmetric encoder-decoder built on TF dual-path modules. The heavy TF-encoder (6 blocks, C_E=128) analyzes only the observed input bandwidth using alternating time self-attention (with RoPE) and frequency self-attention (with a shared linear frequency-projection layer for structural inductive bias), while the lightweight TF-decoder (3 blocks, C_D=64) synthesizes the missing high-frequency bins via learnable extension queries that attend to encoder features through band-partitioned multi-head cross-attention; when f_E = f_D this cross-attention path is bypassed entirely. Training proceeds in two phases: pretraining with a WavLM-based perceptual loss plus a proposed scaled log-spectral loss (w·log(1+|d|/w) with w_tf set to the per-bin temporal mean of the target magnitude to equalize gradients across frequency), followed by adversarial fine-tuning with a shared multi-scale SFI-STFT discriminator (5 scales, 20–100 ms windows) using LS-GAN and feature-matching losses. A streaming variant replaces the time module with Mamba blocks for constant-memory causal inference at 80 ms latency.

## Results

On the UNIVERSE general speech restoration benchmark (16 kHz), TF-Restormer (offline) achieves PESQ 2.30, SDR 11.12 dB, LSD 1.45, MCD 5.08, and UTMOS 4.08, outperforming all diffusion- and vocoder-based baselines on fidelity while matching or exceeding them on perceptual quality. On speech super-resolution (VCTK), the single unified model attains NISQA 4.53–4.62 across 8→16, 8→44.1, and 16→48 kHz settings with LSD 0.89–1.01, matching or surpassing dedicated SR models (e.g., AP-BWE: NISQA 4.20–4.33) without any external resampling. On real-recorded OOD datasets (URGENT 2025, DNS 2020, REVERB), TF-Restormer achieves the strongest NISQA (4.37, 4.36, 2.13) and competitive UTMOS/DNSMOS among all unified-restoration baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vocoder-based baselines (VoiceFixer, FINALLY), TF-Restormer substantially outperforms VoiceFixer on all fidelity metrics (PESQ 2.30 vs 1.77, SDR 11.12 vs −5.68) and perceptual quality (UTMOS 4.08 vs 2.83), while FINALLY achieves slightly higher UTMOS (4.21 vs 4.08) but reports no signal-fidelity metrics, indicating a fidelity–perceptual trade-off that TF-Restormer avoids. Against diffusion-based methods (StoRM, UNIVERSE, UNIVERSE++), TF-Restormer leads on PESQ (2.30 vs 1.74–1.80), LSD (1.45 vs 1.76–1.92), MCD (5.08 vs 5.96–6.87), and UTMOS (4.08 vs 2.64–2.71), confirming that single-pass complex-spectral prediction surpasses iterative sampling in both fidelity and perceptual quality. Against the closest discriminative dual-path baseline TF-Locoformer, TF-Restormer wins on PESQ (2.30 vs 2.13), LSD (1.45 vs 2.00), MCD (5.08 vs 6.26), sBERT (0.91 vs 0.89), and UTMOS (4.08 vs 2.95), while TF-Locoformer retains a marginal SDR advantage (11.61 vs 11.12). On the SSR task, TF-Restormer's unified model matches or exceeds dedicated SR models (NVSR, Frepainter, AP-BWE) on NISQA (e.g., 4.53 vs 4.20 at 8→16 kHz) with comparable LSD, and under noisy-distorted conditions it simultaneously restores corruption and extends bandwidth, outperforming VoiceFixer (NISQA 4.49 vs 3.73) and diffusion baselines (StoRM 3.97, UNIVERSE++ 3.39). On real-recorded OOD benchmarks, TF-Restormer achieves the best NISQA on URGENT 2025 (4.37) and REVERB (2.13), and the strongest NISQA among unified-restoration models on DNS 2020 (4.36), while remaining within 0.07 UTMOS of FINALLY on VoxCeleb (3.98 vs 4.05).

## Relation to Prior Work

TF-Restormer extends the TF dual-path Transformer lineage (Dang et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023) and the sampling-frequency-independent (SFI) formulation (Paulus & Torcoli, 2022) by introducing the xSFI setting, where input and output sampling rates are decoupled rather than matched. The asymmetric encoder-decoder with learnable extension queries draws on the cross-attention reconstruction mechanism of Siamese Masked Autoencoders (Gupta et al., 2023), while the multi-scale STFT discriminator is adapted from EnCodec (Defossez et al., 2023) with an SFI-STFT front-end to support a single discriminator across rates. The key departure from prior work is the explicit separation of observed-band analysis (heavy encoder) from unobserved-band synthesis (lightweight decoder with band-partitioned cross-attention), eliminating the redundant resampling that fixed-rate super-resolution and restoration models require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The xSFI formulation and the single-model-for-arbitrary-rate-pairs design address a practical gap in deployment: real-world speech systems often receive 8 kHz telephony input but need 48 kHz output, and prior approaches required rate-specific models or external resampling. The computational savings from encoding only the observed band (50–82% MAC reduction) and the Mamba-based streaming variant make the approach viable for real-time applications. However, the paper lacks a formal subjective listening study, is trained exclusively on VCTK (limiting speaker and language diversity), and the perceptual-quality gains over strong baselines like FINALLY are modest, leaving open whether the xSFI advantage persists under broader evaluation conditions and whether the extension-query mechanism generalizes to non-speech audio restoration.
