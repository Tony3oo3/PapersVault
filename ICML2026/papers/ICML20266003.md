## Problem

In federated learning, data heterogeneity (distribution shift and quantity skew) causes 'negative gains' where clients performing worse than local training, effectively breaking Neural Scaling Laws in non-IID settings. Existing Clustered FL methods mitigate this by grouping similar clients but rely solely on distribution distances (loss, gradient, or parameter similarity) while ignoring how data volume imbalances bias aggregation and exacerbate model drift. No prior work provides a unified theoretical framework that jointly quantifies both factors to guide optimal client collaboration.

## Input / Output

Each client contributes a local image dataset with labels and its sample size m_i; a pre-trained ResNet18 feature extractor (frozen, task-independent) maps images to 128-dimensional feature vectors. During the contrastive learning phase, clients upload only augmented class feature centroids (0.5 KB per client per round) to the server, preserving privacy. The output is a discrete N×K client-to-cluster assignment matrix computed before any federated training begins, which then drives cluster-specific FL training with any chosen GFL or PFL algorithm to produce per-client classification accuracy.

## Method

FedGain introduces the Effective Federated Capacity (EFC), a heterogeneity-aware generalization bound where each client's contribution is attenuated by an exponential decay kernel e^{-γD(P_i,P_j)} over the distribution shift distance, replacing total physical sample count in the scaling law. Distribution shift is estimated privacy-preservingly via federated contrastive learning: a shared feature extractor is trained to maximize mutual information on augmented pairs, and pairwise cosine distances between per-client feature centroids serve as the shift metric. The clustering objective couples distribution shift and quantity skew nonlinearly into a single differentiable loss over a soft assignment matrix, optimized via Projected Gradient Descent on the probability simplex to yield a hard partition. Crucially, this structure optimization is fully decoupled from federated model training—executed once as a preprocessing step—so FedGain can be seamlessly plugged into any GFL (FedAvg, FedProx, FedNova) or PFL (Ditto, pFedMe, FedAS) algorithm without additional training overhead.

## Results

FedGain achieves 0% Negative Gain Proportion (NGP) across all three heterogeneity scenarios (CIFAR-10 Feature Shift, FMNIST Label Shift, CIFAR-100 Quality Shift), eliminating model drift entirely. In terms of accuracy, FedGain reaches 50.44% on CIFAR-10, 90.16% on FMNIST, and 37.95% on CIFAR-100, outperforming all CFL baselines by 1.7–28.2 percentage points. On the cross-silo OfficeHome dataset, FedGain maintains 0% NGP with 77.55% accuracy (FedAS+FedGain), while all CFL baselines suffer 27.5–45% NGP. The Reward Standard Deviation (RSD) is also consistently the lowest, indicating the fairest accuracy distribution across clients.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedGain outperforms all four CFL baselines (IFCA, FedCluster, FeSEM, CFL-GP) on every metric across all three scenarios. On CIFAR-10 Feature Shift, FedGain (50.44% ACC) beats the closest competitor IFCA (48.77%) by 1.67 points while matching its 0% NGP and achieving lower RSD (3.20 vs. 3.42). On FMNIST Label Shift, the gap widens dramatically: FedGain (90.16%, 0% NGP) surpasses IFCA (89.94%, 10% NGP) and FedCluster (90.11%, 10% NGP) in both accuracy and negative-gain elimination, while FeSEM (61.98%, 55% NGP) and CFL-GP (59.17%, 85% NGP) collapse due to severe data-volume imbalance. On CIFAR-100 Quality Shift, FedGain (37.95%, 0% NGP) leads the best baseline FeSEM (29.78%, 30% NGP) by 8.17 accuracy points and eliminates all negative gains. The key differentiator is that FedGain jointly accounts for both distribution shift and quantity skew via its Effective Federated Capacity metric, whereas all baselines rely solely on loss, gradient, or parameter distances, causing them to fail when data volumes are imbalanced.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedGain extends Neural Scaling Laws (Kaplan et al., 2020) to the federated setting by replacing total physical sample count with a heterogeneity-attenuated Effective Federated Capacity, and grounds its distribution shift estimation in the HΔH-divergence framework (Ben-David et al., 2010) operationalized through contrastive learning (Oord et al., 2018). It departs from existing Clustered FL methods (IFCA, FeSEM, FedCluster, CFL-GP) that cluster based on loss, gradient, or parameter distances by instead coupling distribution shift and quantity skew into a single nonlinear objective derived from the scaling law, and by decoupling structure optimization entirely from model training. The paper positions itself as the first to formalize a heterogeneity-aware federated scaling law and to use it as a principled clustering criterion rather than an a posteriori diagnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of client collaboration structure optimization from federated training is practically significant: it allows FedGain to be plugged into any existing GFL or PFL algorithm as a one-time preprocessing step with minimal communication overhead (0.5 KB per client), which is attractive for cross-silo deployments. The theoretical contribution—showing that non-IID heterogeneity acts as an offset on the scaling curve without altering the power-law exponent—provides a clean diagnostic for understanding when and why federated collaboration fails. Open questions include whether the vMF feature-space assumption holds under more extreme or dynamic heterogeneity (e.g., concept drift over time), how the method behaves with truly large client populations beyond N=1000, and whether the contrastive pre-training phase introduces its own distributional biases that could mislead the clustering.
