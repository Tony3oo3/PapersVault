## Problem

Personalizing LLMs for individual users faces a critical cold-start barrier: fine-tuning-based methods (RLHF, DPO) are too slow and resource-intensive for new users, while data-dependent methods (RAG, prompt-based personalization) require substantial pre-existing user data that new users lack. The paper asks how to rapidly personalize a frozen LLM for a brand-new user using only the minimal online pairwise preference feedback collected during the interaction itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user text query and a frozen LLM (Mistral-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, or Qwen2-7B) as input, and sequentially consumes binary pairwise preference labels (which of two generated responses the user prefers) during generation. It outputs a personalized text response produced via reward-guided token-by-token decoding, along with updated parameters of a lightweight MLP reward model (one hidden layer of size 1024, ReLU) that operates on final hidden-state embeddings from Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B. No backbone LLM parameters are ever modified; personalization is entirely mediated through the additive reward signal at decoding time.

## Method

T-POP interweaves test-time alignment with neural dueling bandits into a single online loop. At each token position, it simultaneously constructs two competing sequences: an exploitation arm that greedily maximizes the base LLM probability plus a learned reward score, and an exploration arm that adds a UCB-style bonus computed as the Mahalanobis norm of the reward-model gradient difference (measuring epistemic uncertainty via a running covariance matrix V). The completed pair is presented to the user, whose binary preference is used to update the MLP reward model via a Bradley-Terry-Luce log-likelihood loss over the full interaction history. Theoretical guarantees from neural dueling bandits (cumulative regret Õ(d_eff√T)) and tokenized bandit theory (sublinear regret under DDMC) ensure convergence. Deployment uses asynchronous background updates so that reward-model retraining is fully masked from user-facing latency, and a flexible mode allows the learned reward model to be frozen for standard greedy decoding once personalization converges.

## Results

T-POP achieves an overall average ArmoRM score improvement of 14.7% over the strongest baseline AMULET across three LLMs (Mistral-7B, Qwen2-7B, Llama-3.1-8B) and four preference attributes (creative, verbose, concise, uplifting). The gains are most pronounced on Qwen2-7B (28.0% over AMULET) and Mistral-7B (19.9%), while on Llama-3.1-8B the two methods are nearly tied (0.535 vs. 0.5325 average). In pairwise GPT-4o win-rate evaluations against the base LLM, T-POP achieves a 94.2% average win rate, securing the highest or second-highest score in 31 of 36 experimental settings. Reward scores rise sharply within the first 20 user interactions and plateau around 40–60 iterations, confirming rapid cold-start adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AMULET (Zhang et al., 2025b), the primary competitor that also formulates test-time alignment as an online learning problem, T-POP achieves a 14.7% average ArmoRM improvement overall. The advantage is largest on Qwen2-7B (28.0% gain) and Mistral-7B (19.9%), while on Llama-3.1-8B the two methods split the four attributes (AMULET leads on creative at 0.58 vs. 0.51; T-POP leads on verbose, concise, and uplifting). Against Linear Alignment (LA; Gao et al., 2024), a closed-form logit-steering method, T-POP consistently outperforms across all 12 model–attribute combinations, with gaps ranging from ~0.05 to ~0.15 in ArmoRM score. In GPT-4o pairwise win-rate evaluations against the base LLM, T-POP averages 94.2% and ranks first or second in 31 of 36 settings, compared to AMULET's 99.0% on Llama-3.1-8B creative (where AMULET also leads on ArmoRM) but T-POP's 99.4% on Mistral-7B creative. Cross-model evaluation with independent judges (DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3-235B) confirms T-POP's 95.5% average win rate, ruling out evaluator-specific bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

T-POP sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: test-time alignment (Khanov et al., 2024), which steers a frozen LLM's decoding via an additive reward function, and neural dueling bandits (Verma et al., 2024), which learn reward functions from pairwise preference feedback with principled exploration-exploitation tradeoffs. The key departure from prior personalization methods (RLHF/DPO fine-tuning, RAG, prompt-based approaches) is that T-POP requires neither parameter updates nor pre-existing user data, directly targeting the cold-start problem. Among training-free baselines, T-POP most closely relates to AMULET (Zhang et al., 2025b), which also formulates token-level decoding as an online learning problem, but T-POP introduces a structured dueling mechanism that explicitly generates informative exploration sequences rather than relying on a single decoding trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

T-POP offers a practical, parameter-free pathway to real-time personalization for new users, with rapid convergence (sharp gains within ~20 interactions) and demonstrated effectiveness even on sub-1B models, making it relevant for resource-constrained and on-device deployment. Key limitations include the reliance on a simulated user (GPT-4o) for both feedback and evaluation—partially mitigated by cross-model judges—and a ~2× increase in query-level latency over AMULET. Open questions include how the method handles long-term preference drift, multi-turn conversational context, and whether the lightweight MLP reward model generalizes to genuinely heterogeneous user populations beyond the four stylized attributes tested.
