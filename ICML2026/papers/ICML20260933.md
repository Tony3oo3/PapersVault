## Problem

Existing Sparse Mixture of Experts (SMoE) routing strategies—Token Choice (fixed experts per token) and Expert Choice (fixed tokens per expert)—both enforce rigid per-unit budgets that force selection of irrelevant token-expert pairs or drop critical assignments. The paper shows that neither approach is globally optimal when expert selection is formulated as a linear programming problem, and both are susceptible to representation collapse and noise-induced misrouting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260933/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes token representations h ∈ R^{T×d} from the attention layer, a gating projection W ∈ R^{d×N}, and N expert FFN functions, producing a binary routing matrix X ∈ {0,1}^{T×N} and the weighted MoE output. A key distinction from standard SMoE is that the T×N compatibility score matrix is flattened into a single vector before selection, enabling a global top-c assignment that supports fractional Top-K budgets (e.g., k=3.5) rather than requiring an integer number of experts per token.

## Method

USMoE introduces two components: (1) a Unified Score S_USMoE = α·S_e + β·S_t that linearly combines row-wise (token-choice) and column-wise (expert-choice) softmax/sigmoid normalizations of the raw dot-product similarity matrix, and (2) a Unified Mechanism that flattens the resulting T×N score matrix and selects the top-c entries globally rather than performing independent per-token or per-expert top-k operations. The paper proves this global selection is the exact optimal solution to the LP formulation of expert assignment under a sparsity constraint, provides a misrouting probability bound showing USMoE is at least as robust as Token Choice under additive noise, and shows via Jacobian analysis that the combined routing signal increases the effective rank of the gating gradient, mitigating representation collapse. Empirically, USMoE is applied as a training-free plug-in to pre-trained SMoE models (Qwen3-30B-A3B, OLMoE, DeepSeekMoE) and also trained from scratch on language and vision tasks, consistently outperforming TC, EC, and SoftMoE baselines while enabling reduced inference cost through fractional expert budgets.

## Results

On Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct (zero-shot, 6 reasoning benchmarks), USMoE achieves an average accuracy of 0.732 under clean settings and 0.556 under 15% Gaussian noise corruption, outperforming the original Token Choice (0.724 / 0.421), Expert Choice (0.612 / 0.499), and MoEE (0.366 / 0.374). Under corruption, USMoE improves over the original TC by 13.5% on average. On vision tasks (ViT-MoE, 10M and 110M), USMoE reaches 77.3% average accuracy across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, SVHN, and ImageNet-1K, surpassing TC (74.4%), EC (75.5%), and SoftMoE (70.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

USMoE consistently outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated dataset and setting. Against the original Token Choice router (Shazeer et al., 2017), USMoE gains +0.8% average in clean settings and +13.5% under 15% noise corruption on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct, with the largest per-dataset gain on ARC-E (0.846 vs 0.838 clean; 0.609 vs 0.361 corrupt). Against Expert Choice (Zhou et al., 2022), USMoE leads by 12.0% average in clean and 5.7% in corrupt settings, and also eliminates the ~3% token-dropping rate inherent to EC. Against MoEE (Li & Zhou, 2025), USMoE achieves substantially higher scores (e.g., 0.732 vs 0.366 average on Instruct clean), indicating MoEE's embedding-oriented routing is poorly suited for generative reasoning tasks. On vision classification (Table 4), USMoE beats SoftMoE (Puigcerver et al., 2024) by 6.7% average (77.3% vs 70.6%), with the largest gap on ImageNet-1K at 110M scale (73.5% vs 71.2%) and CIFAR-100 (67.3% vs 42.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies Token Choice (Shazeer et al., 2017) and Expert Choice (Zhou et al., 2022) routing under a single linear programming formulation, showing both are constrained special cases of a global top-k selection problem. It departs from prior router-improvement work (XMoE, StableMoE, HyperRouter) that addressed representation collapse or load balancing through auxiliary losses or architectural modifications, instead proposing a selection-geometry change (global vs. per-token/per-expert top-k) combined with a weighted row/column scoring function. The sigmoid-based revision of Expert Choice to eliminate information leakage in autoregressive settings is a practical departure from the original softmax-based EC formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LP-based unification provides a clean theoretical lens for understanding why fixed per-token or per-expert budgets are suboptimal, and the provable optimality of global top-k selection gives practitioners a principled justification for the routing change. The 12–13% robustness improvement under corruption and the fractional Top-K flexibility (matching k=8 performance at k=3.5) have direct practical value for deployment under noisy inputs or constrained compute. Key open questions include whether the advantage persists at 100B+ parameter scale, whether optimized kernels can make global selection efficient at long context lengths, and how the method interacts with more recent reasoning-oriented MoE architectures like DeepSeek-R1-style systems.
