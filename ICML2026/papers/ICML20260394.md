## Problem

Selective SSMs such as Mamba introduce input-dependent parameters that transform the system from linear time-invariant to linear parameter-varying, where individually stable transition matrices can produce unbounded trajectories under arbitrary switching. Prior work has focused on empirical performance, leaving global stability conditions, quantitative robustness bounds, and deployment-time certification unresolved. This paper asks: under what conditions are selective SSMs globally stable, how sensitive are outputs to input perturbations, and can verifiable certificates be extracted directly from trained network weights?

## Input / Output

The certification pipeline consumes the trained weights of a single selective SSM layer (the fixed Hurwitz matrix A, and the parameter networks for B, C, D, and Δ), along with a user-specified input bound ū and sequence horizon T. It produces a BIBO stability certificate (uniform state bound h̄), a two-term output robustness bound (K_force + T·K_warp)·δ, and for the general LPV case a CQLF pair (P, β) found via SDP bisection. All constants are closed-form functions of spectral norms, Lyapunov equation solutions, and Lipschitz constants of the parameter maps, requiring no additional training or data at verification time.

## Method

The central insight is a scan-as-sampling identity: the fixed-A exponential scan A_t = e^{Δ_t A} with zero-order-hold discretization exactly samples a continuous-time LTI system ẋ = Ax + w(σ) on an input-warped time grid τ_t = Σ_k Δ_k. This transforms the intractable switched-system stability problem into continuous-time BIBO analysis, yielding a two-term output robustness bound (K_force + t·K_warp)·δ that grows only linearly in sequence length rather than exponentially as in generic switched systems. The forcing term K_force captures same-grid input perturbation sensitivity, while the time-warp term t·K_warp captures the effect of input-dependent step sizes shifting the effective sampling grid. For general input-dependent A(u_t), the paper extends via common quadratic Lyapunov functions verified by polytopic LMIs (Algorithm 1), and provides explicit weight-extraction formulas (Proposition 4.6, Algorithm 2) for all certificate constants. The framework is validated on synthetic selective SSMs, confirming bounded states, linear (not exponential) horizon dependence, and the predicted two-term decomposition.

## Results

The paper proves that the standard Mamba fixed-A exponential scan is BIBO stable with a uniform state bound h̄, and that output sensitivity grows only linearly in sequence length T (not exponentially as in generic switched systems). On the base synthetic configuration (n=16, α=0.5, T=150, δ=0.1), the extracted certificate gives a worst-case output error bound of 9.51, while the measured worst-case error over 20 random perturbations is 0.021 — a gap of ~450× reflecting worst-case conservativeness. The LPV CQLF certificate (Algorithm 1) yields β*=0.732 on a 4-vertex polytope, with all 200×25-step trajectory contraction ratios staying below the certified bound (observed max 0.623). On a stiff ODE benchmark, the variable-step scan-as-sampling identity achieves exact reconstruction at sample nodes (error ~4×10⁻¹⁵) and an adaptive allocation reduces peak interpolation error by 16× (9.5×10⁻² → 5.9×10⁻³) at a matched 273-node budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper with no external method baselines; the 'comparison' is between the paper's own worst-case certificates and empirically measured values on synthetic selective SSMs. The key validation is that all nine configurations in Table 1 satisfy max_t ‖h_t‖ < h̄ (BIBO certificate holds) and the robustness bound (K_force + T·K_warp)·δ exceeds the measured worst-case output error by 2–3 orders of magnitude. An adversarial perturbation search (projected gradient ascent, 100 iterations, 6 restarts) confirms the gap is not an artifact of random evaluation: the best adversarial error (0.0247) is still ~385× below the certificate (9.51). The ablation in Table 2 shows the two-term decomposition is structurally correct — freezing Δ_t eliminates K_warp entirely while K_force remains constant at 3.85 — but measured sensitivity slopes (~5×10⁻⁵) are 3–4 orders of magnitude below K_warp, indicating the time-warp mechanism is a genuine but rarely-excited worst-case pathway. The LPV CQLF certificate is validated discriminatively: Algorithm 1 correctly finds β*=0.732 on a non-trivial 4-vertex polytope while correctly reporting infeasibility on the classical stable-but-unstable-under-switching nilpotent pair (ρ(A₁A₂)=2.56>1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SSM stability literature (StableSSM, Halloran et al.) from time-invariant or training-dynamics settings to the selective scan itself, where input-dependent parameters make the system a switched/LPV system. The key conceptual departure is the scan-as-sampling identity (Lemma 3.1), which recasts the discrete selective scan as exact sampling of a continuous-time LTI system on an input-warped grid, thereby importing classical continuous-time BIBO and ISS tools rather than relying on discrete switched-system analysis. The LPV extension draws directly on the CQLF/LMI framework of Boyd et al. (1994) and Liberzon (2003), while the robustness certificate structure adapts input-to-state stability (Sontag, 2008) and Lipschitz composition techniques from the neural-network robustness literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide weight-extractable, deployment-time stability and robustness certificates for selective SSM layers, shifting stability verification from a post-hoc empirical observation to a formal audit. The linear (rather than exponential) horizon dependence of the robustness bound is a structural consequence of the fixed-A exponential parameterization and distinguishes Mamba-style scans from generic switched systems. However, the certificates are extremely conservative (2–3 orders of magnitude above measured errors), apply to a single scan layer only, and are validated solely on low-dimensional synthetic systems; extending to multi-layer Mamba compositions, channel-dependent step sizes, and data-dependent tightening on trained models remains open.
