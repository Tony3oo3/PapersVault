## Problem

Existing multivariate time-series forecasting methods mitigate non-stationarity through data-level interventions (instance normalization, seasonal-trend decomposition), but they do not explicitly control the parameter-level correlations that emerge when models are repeatedly updated on temporally dependent sliding-window mini-batches. This paper identifies that such optimization-induced dependence inflates the per-step transition-variance of the parameter trajectory, and shows theoretically that this inflation renders PAC-Bayes generalization certificates vacuous.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate look-back window of length L=96 across C covariate channels (ranging from 7 in ETT to 862 in Traffic) and produces forecast values over horizons T∈{96,192,336,720}. TVA is architecture-agnostic: it modifies only the optimizer's effective step size at each iteration via a scalar gate, leaving the forecasting backbone (MLP, Transformer, TCN, or linear) and its input/output interface completely unchanged. The sliding-window sampling protocol that induces temporal dependence among mini-batches is the key structural property that TVA exploits to detect and regulate parameter correlation.

## Method

TVA models stochastic optimization as a discrete-time Markov chain in parameter space and derives a Gaussian transition kernel whose covariance is σ_k²η_k²I, where σ_k² is the mean-field gradient-noise scale. It then defines a smooth step-size gate α_k = exp(−|log(σ_k+ε) − log σ_λ|) that shrinks the effective step size when the estimated noise scale deviates from a user-specified target σ_λ, keeping the controlled transition-variance σ_k²(α_kη)² bounded. A PAC-Bayes analysis (Lemma 3.1) shows that uncontrolled variance inflation makes the complexity term diverge, while Theorem 3.3 proves that the TVA gate uniformly bounds the per-step transition-KL in the variance-inflation regime, thereby controlling marginal KL drift along the induced parameter distribution. In practice, TVA requires only a scalar computation per step (estimating σ_k² from the centered Adam-preconditioned update and applying the gate), introduces no additional trainable parameters, and integrates seamlessly with any backbone or normalization/decomposition pipeline.

## Results

On eight standard MTSF benchmarks (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Electricity, Solar, Traffic, Weather) with look-back L=96 and prediction lengths T∈{96,192,336,720}, MLP+TVA achieves the lowest average MSE on 6 of 8 datasets, including Solar (0.234, ~16% improvement over vanilla MLP's 0.277), ETTm2 (0.278), and ETTh1 (0.438). The method is architecture-agnostic: when applied to iTransformer, it reduces MSE from 0.275 to 0.244 on Electricity (T=720) with only ~8.5 ms additional per-batch overhead. TVA also outperforms standard learning-rate schedulers (cosine annealing, ReduceLROnPlateau) on all eight datasets, with the largest gap on Solar (17.5% vs cosine annealing).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLP+TVA outperforms the vanilla MLP backbone on all eight datasets, with MSE reductions ranging from ~1% (ETTh1: 0.448→0.438) to ~16% (Solar: 0.277→0.234). Against Transformer-based baselines, MLP+TVA beats iTransformer on 6 of 8 datasets (losing narrowly on Electricity 0.195 vs 0.190 and Traffic 0.452 vs 0.435), and decisively outperforms PatchTST, MSGNet, Crossformer, and TimesNet on all eight. Against recent lightweight MLP architectures, MLP+TVA (avg MSE 0.234 on Solar) substantially outperforms SparseTSF/MLP (0.288) and SOFTS (0.301), while matching or slightly trailing SparseTSF on ETTh1 (0.438 vs 0.436). TVA also complements data-level techniques: combining it with RevIN, DishTS, or SAN normalization yields consistent additional gains (e.g., ~10.8% average MSE reduction on Solar), and pairing it with STD methods (CLinear, LDLinear, DLinear) further improves over the STD-only baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the SDE-based analysis of stochastic optimization (Li et al., 2019; 2021; Malladi et al., 2022) to define a practical transition-variance signal for multivariate time-series forecasting, a setting where prior work has focused almost exclusively on data-level decorrelation (instance normalization, seasonal-trend decomposition). The PAC-Bayes framework (Seeger, 2002; Alquier, 2021) is borrowed as a theoretical diagnostic to show that variance inflation in the induced parameter distribution renders generalization certificates vacuous, connecting optimization dynamics to generalization in a way not previously formalized for MTSF. TVA itself is a new architecture-agnostic step-size gate that operates at the parameter level, explicitly complementing rather than replacing the data-level techniques it builds alongside.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, lightweight mechanism (a single scalar gate per step) that stabilizes parameter dynamics and consistently improves forecasting across 8 real-world benchmarks and diverse backbones, with negligible computational overhead. The finding that a 0.94M-parameter MLP with TVA can match or exceed 5M+ Transformer-based forecasters suggests that optimization-induced parameter correlation, not architectural capacity, may be a primary bottleneck in MTSF generalization. Key open questions include whether the user-specified target variance σ_λ can be estimated adaptively during training, and whether the isotropic mean-field approximation remains sufficient for very high-dimensional or strongly anisotropic parameter geometries.
