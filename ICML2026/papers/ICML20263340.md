## Problem

A fundamental dichotomy in classification theory pits smoothness against statistical efficiency: smooth surrogates (logistic, exponential) enable fast O(1/T) gradient optimization but yield only square-root H-consistency bounds, while piecewise-linear losses (Hinge) achieve optimal linear H-consistency rates but are non-differentiable, forcing slow O(1/√T) sub-gradient convergence. No explicit convex loss function had previously been shown to simultaneously satisfy both properties across binary, multi-class, and structured prediction settings.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a feature vector x (or input sequence for structured prediction) and produces a score function h(x,·) over the label space Y. In binary classification the score is a single real number; in multi-class it is an n-dimensional vector of per-class scores; in structured prediction it is a score over an exponentially large output space (e.g., tag sequences of length L over |Y| labels). The loss is computed from pairwise margins m_{y,y'}(x) = h(x,y) − h(x,y'), and the final prediction is obtained via standard argmax decoding, making the surrogates drop-in replacements for existing margin-based losses in any architecture.

## Method

The Linear-Core surrogate Φ̄(u) is constructed by stitching a linear core (affine segment with slope −1 on [−1,1]) to a smooth tail (a scaled copy of a base smooth convex function Φ, e.g., logistic or exponential, on the outer intervals), yielding a globally C¹ (and C² when Φ''(0)=0) convex loss. The critical mechanism is that the linear core maintains a constant non-zero gradient of magnitude 1 near the decision boundary, which forces the H-consistency transformation T(t) to satisfy T(t) ≥ t (linear) rather than T(t) ≥ √t (square-root), as proven via a tight lower-bound analysis on the infimum of a weighted average of Φ̄(−u) and Φ̄(u). The construction extends to multi-class via sum-losses over pairwise margins and to structured prediction via similarity-weighted sums over the output space. For structured prediction, the smoothness enables an unbiased stochastic gradient estimator (Algorithm 1) that samples label pairs (y', y'') with importance weights, reducing per-step complexity from O(|Y|²) (required by exact Viterbi or Forward-Backward inference in SSVM/CRF) to O(L), with variance bounded by 4R²/K independent of |Y|.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 under instance-dependent label noise, the Linear-Core surrogate (ResNet-18) achieves 84.24% test accuracy at 20% noise, outperforming the best-tuned Generalized Cross-Entropy (GCE) by +1.01% and Cross-Entropy (CE) by +1.04%. The advantage peaks at 30–40% noise, where Linear-Core surpasses GCE by approximately 2.6%. In structured prediction with |Y|=4000, the stochastic Linear-Core algorithm achieves a 17.4× speedup over BiLSTM-CRF (4.5s vs. 78.4s to reach 83% accuracy) and a 15.4× speedup over BERT-CRF (5.1s vs. 78.7s), while maintaining O(L) per-step complexity versus O(L|Y|²) for exact CRF inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cross-Entropy (logistic loss), Linear-Core consistently wins by 1.0–2.5% test accuracy across all instance-dependent noise rates on CIFAR-10, with the gap widening as noise increases. Against Generalized Cross-Entropy (GCE, best-tuned over q∈(0,1]), Linear-Core achieves a clear +2.6% advantage at 30–40% noise, confirming that re-weighting the loss is insufficient when noise concentrates near the decision boundary. Against the Hinge loss, Linear-Core matches its linear H-consistency rate while being C¹/C² smooth, eliminating the sub-gradient chattering that causes Hinge to stagnate in optimization (Figure 6). In structured prediction, the stochastic Linear-Core algorithm achieves a 23× speedup over Structured SVM (which requires O(L|Y|²) Viterbi inference per step) and 17.4×/15.4× speedups over CRF (which requires O(L|Y|²) Forward-Backward) for BiLSTM and BERT backbones respectively at |Y|=4000. The paper also contrasts with Cao et al. (2025) Convolutional Fenchel-Young losses, noting that their binary loss is a special case of the Linear-Core family but their framework lacks explicit multi-class/structured forms, uses non-standard decoding, and provides only Bayes-consistency rather than the stronger H-consistency bounds established here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the H-consistency framework of Awasthi et al. (2022a,b) and Mao et al. (2023e, 2024a), which established that smooth margin-based losses with non-vanishing curvature at the origin necessarily yield square-root transfer rates. The closest prior work is Cao et al. (2025), whose Convolutional Fenchel-Young losses also achieve linear rates but are defined implicitly via variational optimization, use non-standard decoding, and provide only Bayes-consistency (weaker than H-consistency). The paper's key structural departure is preserving the affine (zero-curvature) segment of the Hinge loss on [-1,1] while smoothing only the tails, thereby circumventing the universal square-root barrier of Mao et al. (2024a) by design rather than contradicting it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Resolving the long-standing smoothness-versus-consistency dichotomy provides a principled loss function that practitioners can drop into existing architectures (standard argmax decoding, O(1) evaluation) while gaining the strongest known non-asymptotic guarantees. The structured prediction speedup (23× over SSVM, 17.4× over CRF at |Y|=4000) is practically significant for morphologically rich languages and open-domain sequence labeling where exact inference is intractable. Open questions include whether the linear-core width τ requires task-specific tuning, whether the stochastic gradient estimator's bounded-variance guarantee holds under adversarial or highly correlated label structures, and whether the noise-robustness advantage generalizes beyond the single CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 setup tested.
