## Problem

Classical dataset distances such as Wasserstein and MMD suffer from severe concentration and loss of discriminability in high-dimensional settings, where pairwise distances collapse into a narrow range. Additionally, these distances are highly sensitive to outliers, with a single extreme point able to arbitrarily inflate the measured separation. The paper asks whether a geometric distance built on the magnitude of metric spaces can remain discriminative in high dimensions while being robust to outliers, by exploiting the spectral structure of kernel matrix inverses rather than the kernel matrix itself.

## Input / Output

The magnitude distance takes two finite point sets X, Y ⊂ R^D (each of size n and m) and a positive scaling parameter t, and returns a scalar dissimilarity value computed via the inverse of the (n+m)×(n+m) exponential similarity matrix. A normalized variant divides by Mag_{X∪Y}(t) to bound the output in [0, 2]. For the MagGN generative model, the input is a random noise vector sampled from a tractable reference distribution, and the output is a generated data sample (e.g., a 28×28 MNIST digit or 32×32 CIFAR-10 image) produced by passing the noise through a learned MLP generator.

## Method

The magnitude distance is defined as d_Mag^t(X,Y) = 2·Mag_{X∪Y}(t) − Mag_X(t) − Mag_Y(t), where Mag_Z(t) = 1^T ζ_{tZ}^{-1} 1 is the sum of all entries of the inverse exponential similarity matrix at scale t. The key mechanism is that inverting the kernel matrix reweights the spectral decomposition by inverse eigenvalues, preventing the distance from being dominated by a few top eigen-directions as happens with MMD in high dimensions; this preserves discriminative power across all dimensions when t is tuned on the order of 1/√D. The scaling parameter t acts as a resolution knob: small t captures global structure while large t emphasizes local geometry, and the distance converges to 0 as t→0 and to |X Δ Y| as t→∞. For generative modeling, MagGN employs a curriculum-learning strategy where the loss accumulates magnitude distances at geometrically increasing t-values (e.g., {0.01, 0.3, 2.0, 8.0}) at scheduled epochs, forcing the generator to learn coarse structure before fine details. The paper proves that the distance is non-negative, symmetric, satisfies identity of indiscernibles under a magnitude-equivalence relation, is globally bounded by 2|X∪Y| under nonnegative weightings, and does not satisfy the triangle inequality in general (equivalent to non-submodularity of magnitude).

## Results

On MNIST, MagGN achieves visual sample quality comparable to WGAN-GP and sharper than vanilla WGAN while training more than 10× faster than both baselines (Table 1). On CelebA, the hybrid pixel-feature MagGN loss (γ=0.2) reduces FID from 32.34 (feature-only) to 24.32 (Table 3). On CIFAR-10 (cat subset), MagGN pretraining followed by WGAN-GP fine-tuning yields a higher Inception Score and shorter total training time than WGAN-GP from random initialization (Table 2). In high-dimensional Gaussian comparisons (D up to 100+), magnitude distance with adaptive scaling t=1/√D remains stable and discriminative, whereas empirical MMD with Gaussian kernel collapses toward zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MMD (Gretton et al., 2012), magnitude distance with t=1/√D maintains stable, non-zero separation between shifted Gaussians across dimensions up to 100+, while MMD with both σ=1 and σ=1/√D rapidly collapses toward zero (Figures 2–3). Against sliced Wasserstein distance (Deshpande et al., 2018), magnitude distance exhibits consistently lower coefficient of variation (relative variability) across dimensions, and under Huber contamination with outlier radius up to 1000, magnitude distance remains near zero while Wasserstein grows proportionally (Figures 4–5). Against WGAN (Arjovsky et al., 2017), MagGN produces visually sharper MNIST digits and trains >10× faster. Against WGAN-GP (Gulrajani et al., 2017), MagGN matches sample quality on MNIST at >10× lower training cost, and on CIFAR-10, using MagGN as a pretraining phase before WGAN-GP fine-tuning improves Inception Score and reduces total training time compared to WGAN-GP from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Leinster's magnitude of metric spaces (2008, 2013), repurposing it as a dataset distance by defining d_Mag^t(X,Y) = 2·Mag_{X∪Y}(t) − Mag_X(t) − Mag_Y(t). Conceptually, it sits between MMD (which aggregates spectral information through the kernel matrix itself) and Wasserstein distance (which uses the ambient metric directly), but its key departure is operating on the *inverse* kernel matrix, yielding fundamentally different high-dimensional spectral behavior. The push-forward generative model (MagGN) draws on the curriculum learning paradigm (Bengio et al., 2009) by scheduling the scale parameter t to progressively expose the model to finer geometric details, and is positioned alongside MMD-GAN and Wasserstein GAN as a distance-based generative objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, tunable distance that provably remains discriminative in high dimensions (where MMD collapses due to spectral degeneracy) and is bounded under outlier contamination (where Wasserstein is unbounded), addressing two practical pain points in distribution comparison. However, the experimental validation is limited to small-scale benchmarks (MNIST, a CIFAR-10 subset, CelebA) with a simple MLP generator, and the lack of a triangle inequality restricts its use in settings requiring true metric structure. Open questions include whether the O(1/√D) scaling heuristic generalizes to non-Gaussian or highly multimodal data, whether the curriculum-style t-scheduling can be made adaptive, and whether the approach scales to large-scale image generation tasks where mode collapse and texture fidelity are critical.
