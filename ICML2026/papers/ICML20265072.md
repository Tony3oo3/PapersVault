## Problem

Embodied manipulation policies trained on human teleoperation data inherit slow, redundant pacing, making real-world deployment impractical. Existing acceleration methods require expensive policy retraining, new action prediction mechanisms, or costly online interactions, limiting scalability for large VLA models. The paper asks whether plug-and-play acceleration can be achieved using solely offline data without modifying the original policy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265072/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The scheduler policy receives the current low-dimensional proprioceptive state (8-dim for Libero, 60–70-dim for BiGym) and the action chunk (length 10–24) produced by the frozen base policy, and outputs a discrete downsampling rate k ∈ {k_min, …, k_max} that shortens the chunk to length ⌊n/k⌋. Notably, the recurrent world model that underpins the safety constraint operates exclusively on proprioceptive state—deliberately excluding visual observations—which ablations confirm is both sufficient and slightly superior to vision-augmented variants. The entire scheduler is a 5.12M-parameter network with ~1 ms inference overhead, negligible relative to the 50 ms latency of a VLA base policy.

## Method

SuP formulates adaptive speedup as a Constrained MDP where the scheduler maximizes cumulative acceleration gain (reward = k) subject to a zero-violation constraint on world-model-estimated state deviation (max EEF distance between original and downsampled trajectories). A recurrent world model built on ADM predicts future proprioceptive states from variable-length action sequences without feeding predictions back, enabling counterfactual trajectory evaluation for every candidate k. Training proceeds in three offline phases: (1) supervised RWM learning via multi-step MSE, (2) synthetic dataset construction by re-labeling each demonstration transition across all k values with penalty-augmented rewards (r′ = k if safe, −Ω if violating), and (3) scheduler optimization via Implicit Q-Learning with expectile regression on the semi-MDP transitions. A theoretical guarantee (Prop. 4.1) shows that setting Ω > γK_max/(1−γ) ensures the optimal policy never selects a violating action, and the scheduler's dual-stream architecture (GRU over the downsampled action sequence + MLP over state) handles variable-length inputs within the IQL framework.

## Results

On the Libero benchmark, SuP achieves an average success rate of 0.973 with a 1.35× speedup for π0.5, and 0.937 with 1.34× for VLA-Adapter, outperforming both fixed downsampling (-ds2) and DemoSpeedup in success rate while delivering competitive acceleration. On Bigym, SuP achieves 2.01× speedup for ACT (0.67 success rate) and 1.48× for DP (0.51 success rate), matching or exceeding the base policy's success rate. In real-world experiments, SuP attains a 2.17× speedup with 0.611 success rate, surpassing DemoSpeedup (2.07×, 0.600) and dramatically outperforming aggressive downsampling (ds3: 0.356 success rate). The scheduler adds only 1 ms inference overhead (2% of π0.5's 50 ms latency) with just 5.12M trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuP consistently outperforms DemoSpeedup (Guo et al., 2025) on success rate across all evaluated architectures: on Libero, SuP achieves 0.973 vs. 0.963 for π0.5 and is the only applicable method for VLA-Adapter (0.937) since DemoSpeedup requires entropy estimation that VLA-Adapter does not support. On Bigym, SuP improves ACT's success rate from 0.61 (DemoSpeedup) to 0.67 while achieving a comparable 2.01× speedup, and recovers DP's success rate to 0.51 versus 0.46 for DemoSpeedup and 0.41 for SAIL (Arachchige et al., 2025), which suffers severe performance collapse on temporally-sensitive policies. Against fixed downsampling (-ds2), SuP trades a modest speedup reduction (1.35× vs. 1.72× on Libero) for a substantial success rate gain (0.973 vs. 0.928), and on real-world tasks the gap widens dramatically (0.611 vs. 0.356 for ds3). SuP's key advantage is its plug-and-play nature: it requires no retraining of the base policy (unlike DemoSpeedup's 4B-parameter fine-tuning over 20h) and no online interaction (unlike SAIL), using only 5.12M parameters trained in 2h on offline data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the action chunking paradigm standard in modern embodied policies (ACT, Diffusion Policy, VLA models) and the Any-Step Dynamics Model (ADM) for variable-length trajectory prediction. It departs from prior acceleration methods—DemoSpeedup (retraining on entropy-guided demonstrations), SAIL (adaptive skipping with online interaction), and SpeedTuning (RL fine-tuning)—by formulating acceleration as a Constrained MDP solved entirely offline via a lightweight scheduler. The key conceptual departure is repurposing a world model not for planning or policy evaluation in the traditional sense, but as a 'virtual evaluator' that generates counterfactual trajectories to label safe vs. unsafe downsampling decisions, enabling IQL-based offline optimization without any base-policy retraining or online interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLA models grow to billions of parameters, the computational cost of retraining them for speed improvements becomes prohibitive; SuP's plug-and-play, 5.12M-parameter scheduler offers a practical path to accelerate diverse existing policies without touching their weights. The finding that proprioception-only world models outperform vision-augmented variants for risk estimation challenges the assumption that richer scene information is always beneficial for offline safety assessment, though this is validated on limited benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-integer speedup factors, cluttered or highly dynamic environments where visual hazards matter, and multi-agent settings. The discrete downsampling constraint also limits the granularity of achievable acceleration.
