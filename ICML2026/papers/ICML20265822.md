## Problem

Existing trustworthy graph learning methods address robustness, fairness, privacy, and other objectives in isolation, often producing conflicts when multiple goals coexist (e.g., structural perturbations simultaneously degrade accuracy and amplify group bias). The paper asks whether these diverse trustworthiness objectives can be unified within a single distributional uncertainty framework that jointly models perturbation-induced shifts in graph topology, node features, and labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265822/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single attributed graph (symmetric adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{N×N}, node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×D}), a binary label mask M_y that reveals only a subset of ground-truth labels, and optionally a binary sensitive attribute vector s ∈ {0,1}^N for fairness. It produces per-node class predictions and is evaluated via robustness metrics (ΔAcc = |Attack Acc − Clean Acc| under structural/feature perturbations) and group fairness metrics (ΔSP for statistical parity, ΔEO for equality of opportunity). A key design choice is that the method generates K stochastic perturbed copies of the input graph to form a mixed empirical distribution P_tr, rather than relying on the i.i.d. assumption that breaks down for graph-structured data.

## Method

DICT constructs a Wasserstein uncertainty set P(P_tr, r) over the joint graph-label space using a quadratic transportation cost c = κ_A‖A−Â‖²_F + κ_X‖X−X̂‖²_F + κ_y‖y−ŷ‖²_2 (+ κ_s‖s−ŝ‖²_2 for fairness), then minimizes the worst-case expected loss over this set. Via strong duality (Kantorovich duality) and local Lipschitz continuity of the GNN loss, the infinite-dimensional primal is reduced to a finite-dimensional objective: E_{P_tr}[L(f(A,X;θ), y) + L(θ)√r], where L(θ) is the local Lipschitz constant of the loss under the perturbation norm. The Lipschitz constant is estimated node-wise using first-order gradient linearization (dual norm of ∇_Δ L) for labeled nodes and a single-sample second-order Gaussian probe for unlabeled nodes (where the first-order gradient vanishes), then aggregated via p-norm smoothing. Fairness is achieved by extending the transportation cost with a sensitive-attribute term κ_s, so that the Wasserstein ball implicitly penalizes perturbations along sensitive dimensions, unifying robustness and fairness in one optimization without explicit fairness constraints.

## Results

On the NBA dataset with GCN, DICT reduces the robustness gap ΔAcc from 26.11% (vanilla) to 1.73%, and on German reduces fairness gaps ΔSP from 7.04% to 1.08% and ΔEO from 7.47% to 0.63%, while simultaneously improving clean accuracy (e.g., +5.56% on Bail with GraphSAGE). Under the stronger Nettack attack on Cora, DICT achieves the best Attack Acc of 79.32% with the smallest ΔAcc of 4.79%; under Metattack it reaches 78.30% Attack Acc with 6.89% ΔAcc, both outperforming all five robustness baselines. Improvements are consistent across all four GNN backbones (GCN, GraphSAGE, GIN, GAT) and all seven datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under Nettack (targeted, 5 edges/node) on Cora, DICT achieves 79.32% Attack Acc, beating the closest competitor KCES (78.25%) by 1.07 points and β-GNN (74.98%) by 4.34 points; Pro-GNN (57.28%) and GCN-SVD (61.71%) are substantially weaker. Under Metattack (non-targeted, 20% perturbation ratio), DICT leads with 78.30% Attack Acc and 6.89% ΔAcc, outperforming KCES (76.20%, 7.85%) and β-GNN (64.97%, 16.17%) by a wide margin. On fairness benchmarks (German, Bail, Credit), DICT consistently achieves lower ΔEO than NIFTY, FairGNN, and FairVGNN, with the largest gains on Bail (e.g., 0.53% vs. 5.67% for vanilla GraphSAGE). In terms of training efficiency, DICT runs at nearly the same speed as FairVGNN but is significantly faster than SFG, whose per-layer SVD-based Lipschitz projection incurs substantial overhead. Overall, DICT wins or ties on every reported metric across both robustness and fairness evaluations, with the most dramatic improvements (ΔAcc reduced by >20 points) on the NBA and Bail datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

DICT builds directly on the Wasserstein DRO duality machinery of Gao & Kleywegt (2023) and the GNN Lipschitz bounds of Jia et al. (2023), but departs from both by applying them to the non-i.i.d. graph-structured setting where classical i.i.d. empirical distributions break down. Unlike prior robustness methods (GCN-SVD, Pro-GNN, adversarial training) and fairness methods (FairGNN, NIFTY, FairVGNN) that address each objective in isolation, DICT recovers both as special cases of a single Wasserstein uncertainty set over joint graph-feature-label space, with the sensitive attribute encoded into the transportation cost. The key methodological departure is the construction of a K-fold perturbed empirical distribution to circumvent the i.i.d. assumption, followed by a Lipschitz-regularized dual that turns the infinite-dimensional worst-case problem into a tractable min-max with a single interpretable penalty term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, extensible recipe for trustworthy graph learning: by customizing the loss, uncertainty set, perturbation model, and transportation cost, one can in principle address privacy, safety, and interpretability alongside robustness and fairness within a single optimization. The empirical finding that robustness and fairness improve simultaneously without sacrificing clean accuracy (and sometimes improving it) challenges the common assumption that these objectives are in tension. Open questions include scalability to very large graphs (the K-fold perturbation and per-node Lipschitz estimation add overhead), extension to multi-group and individual fairness, and whether the graph-level i.i.d. approximation of perturbed samples holds under highly correlated topologies. The framework's reliance on local Lipschitz estimation also means its guarantees are only as tight as the estimator, leaving room for tighter or certified variants.
