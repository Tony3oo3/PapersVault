## Problem

Standard TD-based goal-conditioned RL suffers from bootstrapping instability and compounding error propagation, particularly on long-horizon tasks where 1-step TD errors grow sharply with horizon. While recent Monte Carlo alternatives (contrastive, supervised) avoid bootstrapping, they either restrict supervision to same-trajectory state pairs or rely on surrogate objectives that only lower-bound the value function. The paper asks whether reframing goal-reaching as a time-to-event (survival) problem yields a principled, closed-form value estimator with standard MLE convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline trajectory datasets (state vectors or RGB images) and, via hindsight relabeling, converts each trajectory into multiple survival tuples (state, goal, event time τ, censoring time c, event indicator δ), where successful rollouts provide exact hitting times and horizon-truncated rollouts provide right-censored observations. The hazard network outputs K binned hazard probabilities using geometric time binning (K=500 bins spanning up to 10,000 steps), from which the goal-conditioned value is computed as a closed-form discounted sum of survival probabilities. The final output is a two-level hierarchical policy—a high-level subgoal selector (every k steps) and a low-level action policy—both extracted via advantage-weighted regression from the survival value.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution (Proposition 4.1) shows that under sparse terminate-on-success rewards, the goal-conditioned value function equals the negative discounted sum of the survival function of the goal-hitting time: V^π(s,g) = −Σ γ^t S^π(t|s,g), meaning the value is a statistical summary of the arrival-time distribution rather than a Bellman backup. The hazard function is learned via maximum likelihood on both event and right-censored trajectories, with a conditioned low-rank basis selection architecture that performs a double tensor contraction between state-goal-dependent selection weights and a learned basis library to represent diverse temporal structures efficiently. For infinite-horizon settings, geometric time binning (b_k = ⌊ρ^k⌋) allocates fine resolution at early steps and coarse resolution at late steps, yielding two binned variants (piecewise-constant hazard and piecewise-constant survival) with closed-form value estimators. Policy extraction uses advantage-weighted regression with the survival value in a HIQL-style hierarchical architecture, deliberately isolating the survival critic's contribution from actor design choices.

## Results

On the OGBench offline GCRL benchmark, HSVL (SVL + hierarchical actors) achieves 81% success on humanoidmaze-giant-navigate, where the strongest TD baseline HIQL reaches only 12% and all non-hierarchical methods fail (0–3%). On antmaze-giant-navigate, HSVL scores 74% vs. HIQL's 65%, and on visual-antmaze-giant-navigate it reaches 61% vs. HIQL's 6% and CRL's 47%. On easier tasks (antmaze-medium-navigate), HSVL matches HIQL at 96%. HSVL is the only method to extract meaningful signal on visual-humanoidmaze-medium (22% navigate, 15% stitch), where all baselines score 0–1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HIQL (the closest algorithmic baseline, sharing the same hierarchical AWR extraction but using TD bootstrapping for the critic), HSVL wins decisively on long-horizon tasks: 81% vs. 12% on humanoidmaze-giant-navigate, 74% vs. 65% on antmaze-giant-navigate, and 66% vs. 49% on humanoidmaze-large-navigate, while matching on antmaze-medium (96% vs. 96%). HSVL loses to HIQL on antmaze-large-stitch (31% vs. 67%) and antmaze-medium-stitch (83% vs. 94%), suggesting the survival critic is less effective on tasks requiring multi-goal stitching. Against CRL (the strongest non-TD, Monte Carlo baseline), HSVL wins on all humanoidmaze tasks (81% vs. 3% on giant, 66% vs. 24% on large) and visual-antmaze-giant (61% vs. 47%), while matching on antmaze-medium (96% vs. 95%). Against GCIQL, HSVL dominates on navigation tasks but underperforms on manipulation: GCIQL scores 68% on cube-single-play and 95% on puzzle-3x3-play, versus HSVL's 14% and 54%, indicating the survival formulation is most beneficial for sparse-reward navigation rather than dense-reward manipulation. Against GCBC and GCIVL, HSVL wins on virtually all tasks with large margins, confirming that the survival-based value estimation substantially outperforms simple behavioral cloning and implicit value learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes goal-conditioned RL as a survival analysis problem, drawing on discrete-time time-to-event modeling (Tutz et al., 2016) to parameterize and train a hazard function via maximum likelihood over both event and right-censored trajectories. It departs from the dominant TD-bootstrapping paradigm in GCRL and from the closest non-TD alternatives—contrastive RL (Eysenbach et al., 2022) and distance-weighted supervised learning (Hejna et al., 2023)—by recovering the value function in closed form from the learned survival function rather than through a scalar regression or soft-minimum surrogate. The hierarchical policy extraction follows HIQL (Park et al., 2023), so the survival critic can be isolated as the sole algorithmic difference from that baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing Bellman bootstrapping with likelihood-based estimation of the time-to-goal distribution, SVL offers a principled escape from the compounding error that plagues TD methods on long-horizon tasks, and the 81%-vs-12% gap on humanoidmaze-giant (with identical architecture) provides strong controlled evidence for this. The framework also naturally handles censored trajectories and inherits MLE convergence guarantees, making it amenable to scaling with dataset size. Open questions include extending the survival identity to dense rewards, online settings with exploration, competing-risks formulations for informative failures, and exploiting the full learned distribution (rather than compressing it to a scalar value) for risk-sensitive or uncertainty-aware planning.
