## Problem

Standard Hamming-agreement decoding of binary cosine sketches (SimHash) exhibits peak variance near θ≈90°, the near-background region where two-stage retrieval candidate selection is most margin-sensitive. Although the query vector remains available in full precision at inference time while database vectors are stored only as sign sketches, the conventional decoder discards this query-side information by treating all sign bits as uniform deterministic votes. The paper asks whether a decoder-side, database-preserving estimator can exploit the query's real-valued projections to reduce estimation error in this critical region without retraining or re-indexing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a normalized real-valued query vector B ∈ S^{d−1}, a fixed binary sign sketch c_A ∈ {±1}^m of a database vector, and m shared random hyperplane normals h_i ~ N(0, I_d). At query time it computes the full real-valued projections x_i = h_i^T B (not just their signs) and magnitudes b_i = |x_i|, which the standard SimHash decoder discards. The output is a single scalar cosine similarity estimate ρ̂ ∈ (−1, 1), produced by a gated 1D MLE that refines only near-background candidates (where min(p̂, 1−p̂) > ε₁/m) and falls back to the Hamming estimate for high-confidence cases.

## Method

QA-Cos models each stored sign bit s_i as a query-conditioned probit observation with likelihood Pr(s_i | x_i, ρ) = Φ(s_i ρ x_i / √(1−ρ²)), so that bits with large query-side magnitude b_i = |x_i| carry proportionally more Fisher information (scaling as b_i²) than near-orthogonal bits. Reparameterizing ρ = tanh(t) converts the constrained problem into an unconstrained 1D log-likelihood ℓ(t) = Σᵢ log Φ(s_i x_i sinh t), solved with T=2 damped Newton steps at O(m) cost per iteration, initialized from the Hamming estimate for a stable warm start. The paper proves that observing x_i is information-nondecreasing relative to observing only sign(x_i) (data-processing inequality for Fisher information) and that the MLE exists and is unique under a mild non-separability condition. The entire decoder is database-preserving: stored sign codes, hyperplanes, and index structures remain unchanged, making the improvement orthogonal to encoder-side or index-side methods.

## Results

QA-Cos reduces MAE by 22.3–28.9% relative to SimHash across all 32 (d,m) configurations, and nearly matches Li's ideal sign-full MLE asymptotic variance in the near-orthogonal region. In end-to-end two-stage retrieval on BEIR, QA-Cos raises Hit@K by up to ~30 percentage points (e.g., ArguAna HARD0.1: 0.000→0.454 at K=100) and reduces candidate budgets by up to ~45–50% at fixed recall (e.g., ArguAna HARD0.1: 766→385 candidates at p=0.8). In a native hnswlib scorer-replacement experiment at 128 bits, Recall@10 improves from 0.770 to 0.853 and Recall@100 from 0.389 to 0.469, while visited nodes drop from 1364.5 to 1228.5. A gated storage-aware variant on FiQA reduces mean cache-limited latency from 1.175 ms to 0.997 ms at matched Recall@100 ≥ 0.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QA-Cos is compared against four sign-sketch baselines: SimHash, SuperBit-LSH, Li's practical ρ̂_{s,n}, and CSSRP. Against SimHash (the primary baseline sharing the same stored codes and hyperplanes), QA-Cos wins uniformly: it reduces MAE by 22.3–28.9% across all 32 (d,m) settings, nearly overlays Li's ideal sign-full MLE variance curve, and improves Hit@K, K@p, HNSW recall, and latency in every system-level experiment. Against Li's practical ρ̂_{s,n}, QA-Cos achieves roughly 2× the MAE reduction (22.3–28.9% vs. 11.8–18.3%) and matches the ideal sign-full MLE variance more closely, because it optimizes the full query-aware probit likelihood rather than a reduced statistic. SuperBit-LSH fluctuates around zero in relative MAE reduction and shows larger variance over substantial parts of the cosine range, while CSSRP helps only in a few low-dimensional settings. The key distinction is that QA-Cos is a pure decoder-side change (no retraining, no re-indexing, no new sketch construction), whereas SuperBit-LSH and CSSRP alter the sketch construction itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Charikar's SimHash framework (2002) by replacing the deterministic Hamming-agreement decoder with a query-aware probit MLE, and makes practical the query-aware likelihood that Li (2019) identified but left as a theoretical object due to the difficulty of solving its estimating equation. Unlike SuperBit-LSH and CSSRP, which modify the sketch construction, QA-Cos is strictly decoder-side: it keeps stored binary codes and projection hyperplanes unchanged, exploiting the natural asymmetry that the query remains full-precision at inference time. The paper also draws a conceptual parallel to SCaNN and SOAR's query-aligned quantization principle, but realizes it at decode time from fixed binary sketches rather than through learned index structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QA-Cos offers a drop-in decoder replacement for existing SimHash-based coarse filtering stages, requiring no re-indexing, retraining, or storage changes, which makes it attractive for production systems where the database codes are already deployed. The identification of the near-orthogonal region as the simultaneous bottleneck for both estimator variance and retrieval margin sensitivity provides a clean diagnostic for when and where such decoder improvements matter most. Limitations include the asymmetric setting (query must be full-precision), modest absolute latency gains on small datasets, and the fact that the method does not address cases where relevant items are already below the background frontier in the original embedding space. Open questions include interaction with learned quantizers (PQ, RaBitQ), scaling to billion-scale corpora, and whether the gated T=1 variant generalizes across embedding architectures and dimensions.
