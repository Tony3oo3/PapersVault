## Problem

The mechanisms by which language models acquire and retain factual knowledge during continual pre-training (cFKA) remain poorly understood, particularly how standard continual learning techniques interact with the model's internal representations to prevent or fail to prevent catastrophic forgetting. While data replay is the de facto standard for CPT, no prior work provides a unified theoretical explanation for why regularization-based methods (e.g., EWC) consistently underperform in this setting, nor for how replay data specifically stabilizes pretrained knowledge at the parameter level.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LM and a CPT text corpus as input. It performs a forward pass on each CPT example to extract per-token attention scores (averaged across all layers and heads), then applies a sliding window to select fixed-length snippets with the highest aggregate attention contribution. These snippets serve as prompts for the pretrained LM to generate replay text, which is mixed with the CPT data at a user-specified ratio (α) and optionally filtered via MinHash deduplication before joint training.

## Method

The paper first derives closed-form training dynamics for a single-layer Transformer with linear attention and tied embeddings, proving two key properties: (1) factual knowledge is decomposed into frequency-based information stored at the token level in the Y matrix, and (2) attention scores in the Z matrix are governed by a Diversity Index measuring the spread of each token's associated answer distribution. From these dynamics, they show that regularization (e.g., EWC) only rescales the convergence rate via eigenvalue modification without shifting the convergence point, explaining its inability to prevent forgetting, whereas data replay shifts the convergence point to retain old knowledge and amplifies the SGD oscillation amplitude near convergence. Building on this, STOC (Selecting Tokens via attentiOn Contribution) exploits the diversity-aware attention property by using per-token attention scores as a proxy for knowledge specificity: high-attention tokens carry more specific factual content, so selecting them as prompts elicits targeted pretrained knowledge from the LM for replay generation, balancing new-knowledge acquisition with old-knowledge preservation.

## Results

On the KnowEdit benchmarks (ZSRE, Wiki_Bio, Wiki_Recent), STOC (α=0.8) with Pythia-160M achieves 27.56% soft token accuracy on ZSRE Original (vs. 24.42% Naive, 24.95% LAMOL) and 23.86% on Wiki_Bio Original (vs. 13.22% Naive, 20.46% LAMOL), while maintaining competitive continual accuracy. On real-world CPT with 1B legal-domain tokens, STOC with the 0.6B model reaches 40.17% on MMLU Original and 32.93% on MMLU-Redux-2.0 Continual, outperforming LAMOL (38.87% / 28.05%) and Naive (22.49% / 24.39%). On the synthetic Biography task, STOC with Freeze at α=0.9 retains 44.82% original hFTA versus 20.06% for LAMOL-Freeze and 18.75% for Random-Freeze, while achieving 94.43% continual hFTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is LAMOL, a generative data replay method that uses special tokens as prompts. STOC consistently outperforms LAMOL on original-knowledge retention across all three KnowEdit benchmarks: on Pythia, STOC (α=0.8) gains +2.61 over LAMOL (α=0.8) on ZSRE Original (27.56 vs. 24.95) and +3.40 on Wiki_Bio Original (23.86 vs. 20.46); on Qwen2.5, STOC (α=0.5) leads LAMOL (α=0.5) by +4.28 on Wiki_Bio Original (35.57 vs. 31.29). On the real-world legal-domain CPT (Table 4), STOC beats LAMOL on every metric for both 0.6B and 1.7B models, e.g., 40.17 vs. 38.87 on MMLU Original and 32.93 vs. 28.05 on MMLU-Redux-2.0 Continual at 0.6B scale. STOC also substantially outperforms the Naive (no-replay) baseline on original retention—e.g., 27.56 vs. 24.42 on ZSRE and 23.86 vs. 13.22 on Wiki_Bio for Pythia—while keeping continual accuracy within ~1% of Naive, confirming that the attention-guided replay preserves new-knowledge acquisition. On the synthetic Biography task, STOC with layer freezing at α=0.9 retains 44.82% original hFTA, more than doubling LAMOL-Freeze (20.06%) and Random-Freeze (18.75%), demonstrating that attention-based snippet selection yields higher-quality replay data than either random or special-token prompting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of theoretical analyses on transformer training dynamics (Tian et al., 2023; Nichani et al., 2025) by deriving a unified framework specifically for continual factual knowledge acquisition (cFKA), building on the synthetic Biography setup from Allen-Zhu & Li (2024) and the knowledge-editing benchmarks from KnowEdit. It departs from prior continual learning theory by providing a mechanistic explanation for why regularization-based methods (EWC, MAS) fundamentally fail in cFKA—altering only convergence rate rather than convergence point—while data replay succeeds by shifting the convergence point and amplifying oscillation amplitude. The proposed STOC method is positioned as a Transformer-architecture-aware successor to LAMOL (Sun et al., 2020), replacing generic special-token prompting with attention-contribution-based snippet selection derived directly from the theoretical diversity-index result.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled theoretical account of why the most common CL technique (regularization) is structurally incapable of retaining factual knowledge during CPT, which has practical implications for method design in industry settings where proprietary pretraining data cannot be stored. The diversity-aware attention assignment result (Theorem 2) offers a testable prediction about how LMs allocate attention that could inform interpretability research more broadly. Key limitations include reliance on a single-layer linear-attention model for theory, experiments restricted to models under 1B parameters for controlled settings, and synthetic data for most validation; open questions include extending the analysis to multi-layer dynamics, multi-token concept formation, and whether STOC's attention-based selection advantage persists at scale.
