## Problem

Existing causal discovery methods model selection using a single static graphical paradigm (adding a selection variable to the causal graph), which correctly captures one-shot filtering but fails for evolutionary selection, where repeated rounds of differential fitness coupled with reproduction across generations induce additional dependencies absent under static models. This conflation leads to false discovery results, and no principled causal model or identification procedure existed for evolutionary selection settings such as immune adaptation, antibiotic resistance, or social norm emergence.

## Input / Output

The method takes i.i.d. samples from a single generation (no lineage-tracked or time-sequential data required) or from multiple generations/environments (multi-domain), assuming causal sufficiency over the observed variables. It outputs a CPDAG (single domain) or PDAG (multi-domain) whose interpretation differs fundamentally from standard usage: oriented edges certify true causal relations where the effect variable is not involved in selection, while unoriented edges within a detected clique may indicate joint selection involvement. Critically, selection can only be falsified (a variable is confirmed NOT involved) but never confirmed from data alone, a key distinction from static selection settings.

## Method

The paper introduces the evolutionary selection model, a DAG over T generations containing trait variables X^(t), unobserved heritable factors ε^(t) (exogenous noise with inheritance edges ε^(t)→ε^(t+1)), and binary reproduction indicators S^(t), where observed data are distributed as p(X^(T)|S^(<T)=1). The central theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that all CI constraints in the observed data are exactly represented by a 'clique-augmented DAG' G⁺ over X, where all ancestors of the selection variable S form a fully connected clique in addition to the original causal edges. This means the identification algorithm is simply standard PC/GES (single domain) or CDNOD (multi-domain) applied to the data as if no selection were present; the novelty lies entirely in the interpretation of the output (Theorem 2), where oriented edges guarantee true causal relations with non-selected effects, and the selection clique structure is the only source of ambiguity. For multi-domain data, a domain-index variable ζ is added to G⁺ with edges to changed variables and to all selection ancestors when selection itself changes, enabling additional edge orientations that improve identifiability.

## Results

On the DGRP gene expression dataset (422 genes, 400 inbred lines), oriented edges in the CPDAG achieve 49.0% precision on known regulatory adjacencies, substantially outperforming unoriented edges at 35.5%. On the Cranial dataset, oriented edges reach 80.0% precision versus 50.0% for unoriented edges. On synthetic data (d=20 variables), the proposed interpretation consistently yields higher precision of causal adjacencies than the standard interpretation for both PC and GES across all generations T, with the gap remaining stable as T increases. Under model misspecifications (latent confounding, dependent inheritance, structural changes), the proposed interpretation still outperforms the standard one except in extreme cases where result graphs become overly dense.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its proposed interpretation of PC/GES outputs against two baselines. First, against the standard interpretation (treating all CPDAG adjacencies as causal relations), the proposed interpretation consistently achieves higher precision on causal adjacencies across all synthetic settings (d=10, 15, 20) and all generations T, for both PC and GES. Second, against RLCD (Dong et al., 2024), a latent-variable causal discovery method specifically designed to handle hidden variables, the paper shows that RLCD still fails to recover true causal relations under evolutionary selection, while the proposed interpretation of standard PC/GES outputs performs better. The key insight is that the evolutionary selection model's specific structure (clique-augmented DAG) means that only oriented edges in the CPDAG are guaranteed to represent true direct causal relations where the effect variable is not involved in selection, making the proposed interpretation strictly more conservative and reliable than both the standard reading and latent-variable methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FCI framework (Spirtes et al., 1995) and the broader selection-bias causal discovery literature, but departs fundamentally by showing that the standard static selection graphical model is invalid for data generated through repeated rounds of differential fitness and reproduction. It introduces the evolutionary selection model (Definition 1) and the clique-augmented DAG representation (Theorem 1), which subsumes the MAG-FCI pipeline but is strictly simpler and more informative for this setting. For multi-domain identification, it repurposes CDNOD (Huang et al., 2020) with a modified interpretation, and for single-domain data it shows that standard PC/GES outputs require a non-standard reading (Theorem 2) rather than a new algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Evolutionary selection is ubiquitous across biology (antibiotic resistance, immune adaptation), cultural evolution, and social dynamics, yet no principled causal discovery framework existed for it; this paper fills that gap with sound and complete identification procedures. A key open limitation is the assumption that heritable factors (e.g., genes) operate independently, which excludes gene-gene regulatory interactions and multi-gene phenotypes; extending to dependent inheritance or parameterized equilibrium models is identified as future work. The finding that selection involvement can only be falsified but never confirmed from data (unlike static selection) has important epistemological implications for how researchers should interpret discovered cliques in evolutionary settings.
