## Problem

Converting pre-trained dense LLMs into sparse Mixture-of-Experts models requires partitioning FFN neurons into discrete experts, but existing methods rely on heuristic clustering (random, weight-based, or activation-based) that optimizes intermediate proxies rather than the actual FFN output. Moreover, these approaches treat neuron assignment and router training as separate sequential stages, lacking a unified differentiable framework that enforces balanced expert capacity while jointly optimizing both the expert decomposition and the token-to-expert routing policy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained dense LLM checkpoint (e.g., LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B) and calibration text data (Dolmino-mix), training only the assignment logits and router weights while keeping all dense weights fixed. It produces a standard sparse MoE architecture where each FFN layer is partitioned into E experts of s=128 neurons, with a learned top-k routing policy that activates only k experts per token (25% FFN sparsity in main experiments). The resulting model is directly compatible with fused-MoE serving kernels (e.g., vLLM) and supports dynamic adjustment of active experts at inference time without retraining.

## Method

DOT-MoE formulates neuron-to-expert assignment as a balanced optimal transport problem: each neuron carries unit mass to be delivered to exactly one expert under strict capacity constraints (each expert receives exactly s neurons), solved via entropic-regularized OT with differentiable Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations in log-domain for numerical stability. A learnable affinity matrix governs the transport cost, and the soft assignment is discretized via greedy rounding. Both the discrete assignment and the top-k token routing are made differentiable through complementary straight-through estimators, enabling end-to-end joint optimization of expert structure and routing policy. The training objective is output-aware, directly minimizing KL divergence and cross-entropy between the dense teacher and sparse MoE student outputs (plus z-loss and load-balancing auxiliaries), rather than clustering based on input weights or intermediate activations. The alignment phase requires only ~3 hours on 8×H100 GPUs and adds ~15% overhead per step over standard dense training.

## Results

On LLaMA-2 7B at 50% parametric budget, DOT-MoE achieves 7.99 WikiText-2 perplexity, outperforming the best structured pruning method (DISP-LLM, 9.84) and the best semi-structured method (Pruner-Zero, 10.52). On zero-shot common-sense reasoning benchmarks, DOT-MoE achieves 61.5% average accuracy on LLaMA-2 7B, 59.8% on LLaMA-3 8B, and 72.3% on Qwen2.5-7B (4.76B active params), retaining approximately 90% of the original dense model's performance while activating only 50% of parameters per token. After 1.2B tokens of fine-tuning, DOT-MoE on LLaMA-2 7B reaches 66.6% average accuracy versus 51.7% for CMoE and 48.1% for LLaMA-MoE-v2 at the same active-parameter budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against structured pruning, DOT-MoE outperforms the strongest baseline DISP-LLM by +4.1 points on LLaMA-2 7B (61.5 vs 57.4), +7.1 on LLaMA-3 8B (59.8 vs 52.7), and +5.6 on Qwen2.5-7B (72.3 vs 66.7); SliceGPT and ShortGPT trail by 10–25 points. Against semi-structured pruning (SparseGPT, Wanda, Pruner-Zero), DOT-MoE achieves the lowest WikiText-2 perplexity (7.99 vs 10.17–11.02) on LLaMA-2 7B at 50% budget. Among dense-to-MoE methods, DOT-MoE dominates: on LLaMA-2 7B it beats CMoE by +17.0 points (61.5 vs 44.5) and LLaMA-MoE-v2 by +13.4 (61.5 vs 48.1) zero-shot; after 1.2B fine-tuning tokens the gap widens to +14.9 over CMoE (66.6 vs 51.7). On LLaMA-3 8B with 7B fine-tuning tokens, DOT-MoE (71.0) outperforms LLaMA-MoE-v2 (66.8) trained on the identical data split. Against LTE at matched FFN sparsity on LLaMA-2-7B, DOT-MoE leads by +6.0 points (66.6 vs 60.6) while maintaining fixed per-token compute via top-k routing, unlike LTE's variable-compute sigmoid routing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MoEfication paradigm (Zhang et al., 2022) of converting dense FFNs into sparse MoEs, but departs fundamentally from prior methods (LLaMA-MoE, CMoE, LLaMA-MoE-v2) that rely on heuristic neuron clustering—random partitioning, weight similarity, or activation-based co-occurrence—and treat expert assignment and router training as separate sequential stages. The key conceptual departure is formulating neuron-to-expert assignment as a balanced optimal transport problem solved via differentiable Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations (Cuturi, 2013; Knight, 2008), with Straight-Through Estimators (Bengio, 2013) enabling joint end-to-end optimization of both the discrete assignment and the routing policy. This unifies two previously decoupled decisions into a single differentiable objective, contrasting with structured pruning methods that permanently remove capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DOT-MoE offers a practical, low-cost path to sparse inference: alignment takes under 3 hours on 8×H100 GPUs, the resulting model is compatible with standard fused-MoE serving kernels (vLLM), and it retains ~90% of dense performance at 50% active parameters across LLaMA-2/3 and Qwen2.5. Open questions include whether the approach scales to the ~100B-token fine-tuning regime, whether data-driven initialization of the affinity matrix (e.g., from weight correlations) can accelerate convergence, and whether hard-pruning consistently underutilized experts can further reduce memory footprint. The extension to attention-head grouping suggests the balanced-transport formulation is broadly applicable, but joint MLP+attention MoEfication remains unexplored.
