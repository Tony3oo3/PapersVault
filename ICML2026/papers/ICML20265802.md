## Problem

Predicting full force-extension (F-x) unfolding trajectories for proteins has historically required either labor-intensive single-molecule force spectroscopy experiments or computationally prohibitive steered MD simulations, with no scalable learning-based alternative. Existing generative and language-model approaches for proteins target equilibrium structures or static properties, leaving the non-equilibrium, force-driven unfolding dynamics unaddressed by any scalable method.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265802/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a protein's amino acid sequence (encoded via pretrained ESM-2 into a global coevolutionary vector) and its 3D Cα backbone coordinates (from which a multi-channel physical topology graph is derived: pairwise distance, stiffness k_ij = d_ij⁻², and effective resistance via the graph Laplacian pseudoinverse), along with 17 per-residue physicochemical features. The output is a full 1D force-extension trajectory of 512 points, with extension normalized by the protein's theoretical contour length (N × 3.6 Å) and force globally min-max scaled to [0, 1], capturing the stochastic unfolding events (sawtooth peaks) as a single continuous signal.

## Method

GenUnfold is a DiT-based diffusion model with a dual-stream co-conditioning architecture: a global coevolutionary context (mean-pooled ESM-2 embedding plus sinusoidal timestep) modulates the denoising backbone via adaLN-Zero, while a fine-grained local mechanical representation is injected through cross-attention layers. The local representation is produced by a novel Multi-head Physics-biased Attention encoder that constructs a 3-channel structural graph (distance, stiffness, effective-resistance maps), processes it through a small CNN to learn per-head attention bias maps, and applies a time-dependent gating vector so that different physical priors (local rigidity vs. long-range coupling) are dynamically up- or down-weighted across diffusion timesteps. The learned bias is added directly to the softmax logits of self-attention, steering residue-residue interactions along mechanically meaningful pathways. Training uses the standard noise-prediction MSE loss over 1000 diffusion steps, with DDIM-accelerated 100-step inference generating a full trajectory in 1–3 seconds on a single GPU.

## Results

On the BSDB test set (16,495 proteins, 32,990 F-x curves), GenUnfold (CrossDiT-ESM-PhyAtten) achieves the best distributional fidelity with FID of 0.0117, a 54.3% reduction over the DiT-SelfAtten baseline (0.0256) and a 30% reduction over the ESM-2-only baseline (0.0220). It also attains the lowest Force-JSD (0.0553) and Energy-JSD (0.0338), representing 33.5% and 47.0% improvements over the baseline, respectively. The pure-sequence variant CrossDiT-ESM-ESM achieves the lowest point-wise Rel l2 (0.2017) but lags on all distributional and physical metrics, confirming that physics-biased attention is critical for capturing the stochastic ensemble structure of unfolding trajectories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper establishes the first benchmark for full F-x trajectory prediction, comparing seven model variants that isolate the contributions of coevolutionary context (ESM-2), physics-biased attention (PhyAtten), and the co-conditioning architecture (CrossDiT vs. standard DiT). GenUnfold (CrossDiT-ESM-PhyAtten) wins on all three distributional/physical metrics (FID, Force-JSD, Energy-JSD) against every baseline. Against the strongest sequence-only competitor CrossDiT-ESM-ESM, GenUnfold reduces FID from 0.0168 to 0.0117 (30% improvement) and Energy-JSD from 0.0533 to 0.0338 (36% improvement), while CrossDiT-ESM-ESM retains a marginal edge in point-wise Rel l2 (0.2017 vs. 0.2070). The physics-only variant CrossDiT-PhyAtten-PhyAtten confirms that evolutionary information is necessary: removing ESM-2 degrades FID by 41% (0.0117→0.0165) and Energy-JSD by 9%. On the unseen-superfamily subset (43 proteins from CATH superfamilies absent in training), GenUnfold reduces FID from 0.378 to 0.088 and Energy-JSD from 0.203 to 0.106 relative to the best sequence-only baseline, demonstrating superior out-of-distribution generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the diffusion transformer (DiT) backbone and its adaLN-Zero conditioning mechanism, extended with cross-attention layers as in Pixart-α, from 3D structure generation to 1D force-extension trajectory prediction. The physics-biased attention module draws directly on Elastic Network Model (ENM) formulations—spring constants from pairwise distances and effective resistance from the graph Laplacian pseudoinverse—to inject structural mechanics priors into attention logits, departing from prior generative protein work (RFdiffusion, Chroma, Boltzmann Generators) that targets equilibrium 3D conformations rather than non-equilibrium mechanical response. The dual-stream conditioning (ESM-2 coevolutionary context via adaLN + structural physics via cross-attention) is a novel architectural combination not present in either the PLM or diffusion-modeling literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first scalable generative framework and comprehensive benchmark for full F-x trajectory prediction, this work opens a path toward proteome-wide mechanical screening that bypasses the hours-to-days cost of SMFS or steered MD per protein. The out-of-distribution results on unseen CATH superfamilies (FID 0.378→0.088) suggest the physics priors enable genuine extrapolation rather than memorization, though validation against experimental SMFS data remains an open question. Limitations include reliance on coarse-grained BSDB simulations, single-domain proteins, and the absence of external competing baselines; scaling to multi-domain or intrinsically disordered proteins and integrating with experimental validation pipelines are natural next steps.
