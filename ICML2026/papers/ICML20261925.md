## Problem

Online RL for LLMs (e.g., GRPO) suffers from an imbalanced exploration-exploitation trade-off: policies either converge prematurely to deterministic local optima (over-exploitation, causing sparse learning signals) or exhibit entropy explosion (over-exploration, causing training instability). Existing regularizers such as clip-higher and entropy regularization fail to consistently maintain this balance, and no principled metric existed to diagnose or directly optimize the fine-grained step-level trade-off during training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text problem and its ground-truth answer as input, and produces a step-by-step reasoning trajectory ending with a final answer in \boxed{} format. Reasoning steps are delineated by \n\n delimiters within the trajectory, and the IB-Score is computed per step using Monte Carlo rollouts (5 rollouts per non-terminal step) to estimate conditional reward densities. The tree-structured sampling shares prefixes across branches, so the same token budget yields 50% more trajectories (G=12 vs. G=8) compared to independent sampling.

## Method

IB-TPO introduces two coupled components. First, IB-Score reformulates the Information Bottleneck objective at the step level as a function of two terms: η₁ (information gain from environmental feedback, i.e., the ratio of step-level reward density to parent reward density) and η₂ (model confidence in each branch), showing that maximizing IB-Score is equivalent to maximizing Cov(η₁, η₂) — encouraging the policy to allocate confidence to the most feedback-informative reasoning paths. Second, IBTree is an iterative tree expansion strategy that selectively branches at the node with the highest IB-Score (rather than highest entropy as in TreeRL), generating B new trajectories per iteration over L iterations with K=1 branching node, which both improves sampling diversity and serves as an efficient Monte Carlo estimator of IB-Score. During optimization, the IB-Score is converted into a step-level local advantage A_IB (relative to the parent node) that is combined with a global advantage A_GL via a weighted sum, and the resulting step-level advantages are plugged into the standard GRPO clipped policy gradient objective.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B-Base, IB-TPO achieves 44.3% overall avg@32 accuracy, outperforming Vanilla GRPO (40.7%) by 3.6% and the strongest tree-based baseline TreeRL (42.0%) by 2.3%. On Qwen3-1.7B-Base, IB-TPO reaches 29.2% overall, a 2.9% improvement over GRPO (26.3%) and 2.4% over TreeRL (26.8%). IB-TPO also leads on out-of-domain benchmarks: 46.2% on IFEval and 41.7% on GPQA Diamond (8B), surpassing all baselines. The method additionally samples 50% more trajectories (12 vs. 8) under the same token budget, with a 6.8% higher effective sampling rate (Eff-Rate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IB-TPO consistently outperforms all four baselines across every benchmark and both model scales. Against Vanilla GRPO, IB-TPO gains +2.9% (1.7B) and +3.6% (8B) overall, with the largest margin on AMC 24 (+4.0% on 8B) and AIME 24 (+2.6% on 8B). Against TreeRL (the strongest tree-based baseline using entropy-guided branching), IB-TPO leads by +2.4% (1.7B) and +2.3% (8B), with notable advantages on IFEval (+3.4% on 1.7B, +3.7% on 8B) and GPQA (+2.2% on 1.7B, +1.9% on 8B). Against TreePO (fixed-width tree sampling), IB-TPO wins by +3.0% (1.7B) and +2.7% (8B). Against IBRO (sequence-level IB regularization without tree structure), IB-TPO leads by +3.3% (1.7B) and +2.7% (8B), confirming that step-level IB-Score with tree-structured sampling is more effective than sequence-level IB regularization alone. Notably, GRPO variants with clip-higher or entropy regularization fail to improve over vanilla GRPO on most benchmarks, underscoring the inadequacy of simple regularization for balancing exploration and exploitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO policy optimization framework by integrating Information Bottleneck theory at the step level, departing from IBRO (Lei et al., 2025) which applies IB as a sequence-level entropy regularization. It also builds on TreeRL's (Hou et al., 2025) entropy-guided tree search but replaces the branching criterion with the proposed IB-Score, which jointly considers model confidence and reward-informative feedback rather than uncertainty alone. The key conceptual departure is the dual role of the tree structure: it simultaneously serves as an efficient sampling mechanism (sharing prefixes to generate 50% more trajectories under the same token budget) and as a Monte Carlo estimator for IB-Score, creating a feedback loop between exploration guidance and objective estimation that neither prior tree-based nor IB-based approaches achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IB-Score provides a principled, step-level diagnostic for the exploration-exploitation imbalance that plagues online RL for LLMs, going beyond simple entropy metrics to capture how well the model allocates confidence to reward-informative reasoning paths. The finding that Cov(η₁, η₂) collapses during GRPO training offers a concrete mechanistic explanation for why entropy regularization and clip-higher fail despite increasing uncertainty. Limitations include the added wall-clock overhead of multi-iteration tree expansion (though mitigated by prefix caching and parallel decoding), and the method is currently validated primarily on mathematical reasoning with Qwen3 models; generalization to multimodal reasoning, function calling, and non-math domains remains open.
