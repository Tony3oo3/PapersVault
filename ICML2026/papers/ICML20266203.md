## Problem

Learning-based controllers for large-scale interconnected systems lack formal stability guarantees, and existing neural certificate methods do not account for communication delays that dominate performance degradation in networked systems. No prior work provides a scalable, delay-aware certification framework that can verify input-to-state stability for arbitrary network sizes without requiring explicit system models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266203/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-agent states, delayed neighbor states (bounded by known constants τ_{i,j}), and bounded disturbance sets, with the closed-loop dynamics treated as a black box. It produces a family of neural Lyapunov-Razumikhin functions (one per agent), a coupling gain matrix Γ satisfying a network-size-independent small-gain condition, and a formally verified discrete-time sISS guarantee. Verification operates over reachability-constrained delay domains (not the full state space) and is reduced to finite grid checks via Lipschitz-based margins, with the certificate reusable across structurally equivalent nodes to keep cost independent of network size N.

## Method

The core contribution is a sufficient condition (Theorem 4.1) for discrete-time sISS of delayed interconnected systems using vector Lyapunov-Razumikhin functions, combining class-K∞ bounds, a Razumikhin conditional decrement (delayed neighbor Lyapunov values bounded by p·V_i triggers a contraction inequality), and a small-gain condition on coupling gains. Synthesis jointly trains the Lyapunov functions and coupling matrix via a loss that enforces imitation of a nominal policy, K∞ bounds, and the Razumikhin logic reformulated as a disjunction (¬A ∨ B). Verification is two-stage: an 'outside' region checks the Razumikhin conditional logic on reachability-constrained delay domains, and an 'inside' region verifies unconditional contraction on a target level set, both reduced to finite uniform-grid checks with Lipschitz-derived robustness margins. Scalability is achieved through substructure isomorphism and node structural equivalence theorems that allow certificates learned on smaller or representative substructures to be directly reused, making verification cost independent of N.

## Results

On verification efficiency (Table 1), the proposed method is the only design that completes across all scenarios and scales, with runtimes of 377–1999 s that remain nearly invariant as N grows from 10 to 50, while all other verification designs (Reachability only, Scalability only, Direct) time out (>5 h) for N=50 or in all cases. On control performance, the proposed method achieves the lowest RMSE in all three environments: 0.81 m (platoon, 4 m/s disturbance), 34.568 m (drone, 0.5 m/s), and 0.072 rad/s (microgrid, 0.5 rad/s), outperforming the best certificate baseline (Compositional ISS) by 5.81%, 0.017%, and 8.86% respectively under the lighter disturbance setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed delay-aware method outperforms all four baseline categories across all three environments. Against the model-based Predictor Feedback (Xu et al., 2022), it achieves lower RMSE in platoon (0.81 vs 1.04) and microgrid (0.072 vs 0.082) without requiring an explicit system model, while matching it closely on drone formation (34.568 vs 34.211). Against the Nominal Controllers (RL platoon policy, LQR for UAV, linear feedback for microgrid), it reduces RMSE by 22.9% (platoon), 0.046% (drone), and 12.2% (microgrid) under the lighter disturbance. Against the Compositional ISS certificate baseline (Zhang et al., 2023c), it improves RMSE by 5.81% (platoon), 0.017% (drone), and 8.86% (microgrid). Against the sISS certificate baseline (Qiu et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2025), it reduces RMSE by 14.7% (platoon), 0.003% (drone), and 39.5% (microgrid at 0.5 rad/s). The drone formation shows the smallest relative differences (all methods within 1% of each other), suggesting the LQR nominal controller is already near-optimal for that setting, while the platoon and microgrid results demonstrate the clearest advantage of delay-aware certification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: (i) classical Lyapunov-Razumikhin techniques for fixed-size delayed systems (Pepe 2017, Nekhoroshikh 2022), (ii) scalable vector Lyapunov small-gain conditions for delay-free interconnected systems (Silva et al. 2024, Qiu et al. 2024), and (iii) neural certificate synthesis via CEGIS-style training (Dai et al. 2021, Zhang et al. 2023c). The key departure is that no prior work combines all three: the authors explicitly note that scalable delay-aware stability conditions for general interconnected systems are unavailable, and the only neural-certificate approach for delays (Hedesh & Siami 2025) requires internal positivity, excluding most practical dynamics. The paper's Theorem 4.1 provides the missing theoretical bridge, while the reachability-constrained verification and structural-equivalence reuse (Theorems 5.10, 5.12) address the computational intractability that would otherwise prevent scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to provide formal, network-size-independent sISS guarantees for neural controllers in large-scale delayed interconnected systems, which is critical for safety-critical applications (platoons, drone swarms, microgrids) where communication delays are unavoidable and system models are imperfect. The scalability result—that verification runtime remains nearly constant as N grows from 10 to 50—is practically significant for deployment. Limitations include the discrete-time restriction, the Lipschitz continuity assumption on dynamics and certificates, bounded (not time-varying) delays, and the still-substantial absolute verification time (~400–2000 s). Open questions flagged by the authors include extending to time-varying delay bounds, jointly certifying safety and stability, and reducing reachability conservatism.
