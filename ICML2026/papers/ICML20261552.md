## Problem

Computing Shapley values and higher-order interaction indices for black-box graph predictors is intractable by exact enumeration (O(2^n) coalitions) and imprecise by sampling, which requires thousands of model queries and exhibits variance that worsens sharply for higher-order interactions. Existing architecture-specific explainers (GNNExplainer, PGExplainer) do not apply to arbitrary black-box models, and no prior method provides a single, deterministic, query-efficient procedure that recovers both first- and higher-order Shapley quantities from a graph-structured input.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed attributed graph instance (G, X), a black-box predictor f accessible only through masked evaluations f(G, X_S), a baseline feature matrix X^0 (e.g., mean features), and a query budget M. It produces node Shapley values (O1), edge Shapley interaction indices (O2), and higher-order interaction indices, all extracted deterministically from a single trained surrogate with no further model queries. The surrogate operates on binary coalition indicators z ∈ {0,1}^n (which nodes are included vs. replaced by baseline), not on raw node features, making the framework agnostic to feature type and intervention semantics.

## Method

TN-SHAP-G learns a graph-aligned tensor network surrogate whose topology mirrors the input graph: each node hosts a core tensor with one physical index (coalition membership) and bond indices to neighbors, with bond dimension χ controlling information capacity across graph separators. The surrogate is trained by distilling M teacher queries (sampled coalitions labeled by f) via empirical risk minimization on the multilinear extension, compressing the 2^n coalition tensor into O(n·Δ_max) parameters. Once trained, Shapley values and interaction indices are recovered deterministically by evaluating the surrogate's diagonal partial derivatives g_u(t) = ∂_{z_u} ν̂(t·1) at O(n) probe points, interpolating the resulting degree-(n−1) polynomial via a Vandermonde system, and integrating in closed form — eliminating Monte Carlo variance entirely. The graph-aligned topology provably outperforms tensor-train factorizations for graph-structured games: cut-rank bounds (Theorem 3.2) show that bond dimensions along graph separators directly control expressivity, while TT representations suffer Ω(χ²) rank inflation on cycle-like structures and are sensitive to node ordering.

## Results

On small graphs (n ≤ 20) where exact enumeration is feasible, TN-SHAP-G achieves O1 Shapley cosine similarity of 0.9979 ± 0.004 on Benzene and 0.9933 ± 0.012 on Mutagenicity, compared to GraphSVX's 0.30 ± 0.51 and 0.45 ± 0.66 respectively. Under matched teacher-query budgets, TN-SHAP-G reaches 0.95 cosine similarity with 10–100× fewer model queries than permutation sampling and SHAP-IQ. In amortized timing on 20 Mutagenicity graphs, TN-SHAP-G (one-time training ~4 s) becomes faster than GraphSHAP-IQ at K ≥ 5 repeated queries, winning 17/20 graphs at K = 50 and 18/20 at K = 100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GraphSVX (Duval & Malliaros, 2021), TN-SHAP-G achieves near-perfect O1 cosine similarity (0.9979 on Benzene, 0.9933 on Mutagenicity) while GraphSVX scores only 0.30 and 0.45 with very high variance (±0.51, ±0.66), indicating poor convergence of its sampling-based estimator. Against permutation sampling (Castro et al., 2009) and SHAP-IQ (Fumagalli et al., 2024), TN-SHAP-G reaches 0.95 O1 cosine similarity with 10–100× fewer model queries, and a single trained surrogate yields both O1 and O2 attributions without separate Monte Carlo budgets. Against GraphSHAP-IQ (Muschalik et al., 2025), TN-SHAP-G is slower for a single query (197.8 s vs. 11.6 s) due to one-time surrogate training, but amortizes to a win at K ≥ 5 repeated queries on the same instance, reaching 17/20 and 18/20 graph wins at K = 50 and K = 100 respectively. Structure ablations (Fig. 6) further show that the graph-aligned TN topology consistently outperforms tensor trains under parameter matching, with TT additionally sensitive to node ordering.

## Relation to Prior Work

TN-SHAP-G extends the tensor-network surrogate approach for Shapley computation introduced in TN-SHAP (Heidari et al., 2025) from general inputs with tensor-tree topologies to graph-structured inputs with graph-aligned topologies, where bond dimensions are allocated along graph edges rather than an arbitrary chain. It departs from sampling-based estimators (GraphSVX, SHAP-IQ, permutation sampling) by replacing Monte Carlo estimation with deterministic Vandermonde interpolation on the multilinear extension, and from architecture-specific explainers (GNNExplainer, PGExplainer) by remaining fully model-agnostic. The theoretical grounding draws on cut-rank expressivity results from the TN literature (Khavari & Rabusseau, 2021; Levine et al., 2019) and instantiates them for coalition tensors of graph-induced cooperative games, showing that graph separators control the required bond dimension and that tensor trains suffer generic rank inflation on cyclic or high-walk-index structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, deterministic alternative to variance-prone Monte Carlo Shapley estimation on graphs, with a single trained surrogate amortizing all first- and higher-order attributions at O(n) surrogate evaluations. The 10–100× query efficiency and the ability to scale beyond n≤20 (where enumeration and sampling become impractical) make rigorous graph explanations accessible in resource-constrained settings such as molecular property prediction. Key limitations include the assumption of low cut-rank across graph separators (which may fail for dense long-range interactions), sensitivity of higher-order accuracy to surrogate misspecification, and the current restriction to node-level players with a fixed baseline. Open questions include adaptive rank allocation, learned TN topologies beyond the input graph, and extensions to edge/motif-level players and non-graph structured modalities.
