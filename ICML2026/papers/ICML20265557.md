## Problem

Shape-based infrared adversarial attacks face a fundamental tradeoff: grid-based representations are differentiable but crude, requiring complex auxiliary losses for geometric cohesion, while spline-based representations are expressive but non-differentiable, forcing reliance on inefficient black-box search. This bottleneck limits the attack success rate, which collapses dramatically when detection confidence thresholds are lowered from 0.7 to 0.4.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean infrared image I ∈ R^{H×W}, a target bounding box B = (x, y, w, h), and a scale ratio ρ (default 0.6) that controls patch size relative to the bounding box. It outputs a compact set of complex Fourier coefficients Θ = {c_k}_{k=-K}^{K} (K=6 by default) that analytically define the patch boundary, rasterized into a binary mask M_s ∈ [0,1]^{H_s×W_s} via the winding number theorem. The mask is scaled and translated to the target's bounding box, then applied as a zero-valued (cold) region via element-wise multiplication with the original image to produce the adversarial input.

## Method

The adversarial patch boundary is parameterized as a complex Fourier series F(t) = Σ_{k=-K}^{K} c_k e^{ikt} over t ∈ [0, 2π], where the coefficients Θ are the sole optimizable variables. A fully differentiable mapping converts these coefficients to a pixel mask by evaluating the winding number integral W(q) for every pixel q on a discrete grid, approximated as a trapezoidal sum over N_s sample points along the curve; the final mask is M_s(q) = Clip(|W(q)|, max=1). The coefficients are optimized end-to-end with Adam (lr=0.002, 1000 iterations) to minimize a composite loss combining adversarial suppression of all pre-NMS detection confidences, an area penalty on the mean mask value, and a regularization term that penalizes high-frequency harmonic amplitudes exceeding a fraction γ of the fundamental amplitude. Data augmentations (random scaling, aspect-ratio perturbation, additive noise) are applied during optimization to bridge the sim-to-real gap, and the resulting shape is physically fabricated from fiberglass aluminum-foil insulation material.

## Results

On YOLOv3 with LLVIP pedestrian images, the proposed Fourier shape attack achieves an AP drop from 1.0 to 0.9104 and maintains high ASR across all confidence thresholds, whereas UAP's ASR collapses from ~95% at conf=0.8 to ~10% at conf=0.6. The physical patch achieves 100% ASR@0.5 at 35–45 m and 88% ASR@0.5 at 25–35 m, with a compact area ratio of 0.1167. Against RetinaNet and Faster R-CNN, the attack induces catastrophic AP collapse (1.0→0.2979 and 1.0→0.2051, respectively), confirming generalizability beyond YOLO architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three shape-based infrared attacks: UAP (spline-interpolated vertices optimized via black-box Differential Evolution), Inf. P (discrete pixel grid with aggregation constraint), and Def. P (ray-based star-shaped representation). The proposed Fourier shape method outperforms all three across the full ASR–confidence curve on YOLOv3: UAP reaches ~95% ASR only at the lenient 0.8 threshold but drops to ~10% at 0.6, revealing its fragility; Inf. P produces visibly fragmented, non-cohesive shapes that are impractical as a single physical patch; and Def. P concentrates adversarial information in high-frequency edges, making it highly sensitive to cutting errors. The proposed method achieves the largest AP drop (1.0→0.9104) while maintaining a competitive area ratio of 0.1167, and generalizes to YOLOv8, RetinaNet, and Faster R-CNN without per-detector re-optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Fourier shape representation from Wang et al. (2025d), which used Fourier series to probe geometric sensitivity in DNNs, to the domain of physical infrared adversarial attacks. It departs from prior shape-based infrared attacks (UAP, Inf.P, Def.P) that are forced to trade off between differentiability (grid-based, bottom-up) and representational power (spline-based, top-down but non-differentiable). The key methodological contribution is a differentiable winding-number rasterization that bridges compact Fourier coefficients to a pixel mask, enabling end-to-end gradient-based optimization of the shape boundary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical evaluation weakness in prior infrared attack literature: reported ASRs at high confidence thresholds (0.7–0.8) collapse dramatically (e.g., >90% → <20%) when the threshold is modestly lowered, suggesting prior 'successes' were fragile. The proposed ASR-Confidence curve as a standard metric is a useful benchmark contribution for the field. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-person scenes with occlusion, whether Fourier-shape-aware adversarial augmentation (shown to partially defend) can be made fully effective, and whether the sim-to-real gap narrows further with domain randomization or multi-image optimization.
