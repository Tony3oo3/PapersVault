## Problem

Existing prompt learning for VLMs exhibits a persistent modality asymmetry: optimization is concentrated on text tokens while visual representations remain frozen and holistic, making it difficult to elicit the hierarchical granularity needed for fine-grained discrimination. This asymmetry manifests as a stability-generalization tension where prompts overfit scarce training distributions while compromising transferable invariances. Prior visual prompting attempts operate at the spatial/patch-token level without explicitly separating semantic invariants from instance-specific fine-grained cues, leading to unstable optimization in fine-grained regimes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and class text labels (with learnable prompt tokens) as input and produces a class label via CLIP cosine similarity. During training, a frozen VAE additionally processes the image to produce spatial latents that are factorized into low-frequency and high-frequency bands; at inference, the VAE and FiLM modulation branch are entirely discarded, so the model relies solely on a frozen Visual Semantic Bank for text refinement and standard CLIP similarity. SpecPL is designed as a plug-and-play booster that wraps around existing baselines (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, MMRL) without altering their inference pipeline.

## Method

SpecPL introduces a Spatial-Spectral Proxy that decomposes frozen VAE spatial latents into a low-frequency Base band (semantic invariants) via stride-1 k×k averaging and a high-frequency Detail band (residual granules) via subtraction. A frozen Visual Semantic Bank, initialized from low-band prototypes and updated via nearest-neighbor EMA, anchors text representations to universal low-frequency invariants through soft-retrieval compositional refinement. The key novelty is Counterfactual Granule Supervision: by randomly permuting high-frequency signals across batch samples and enforcing the model to predict the granule source identity, the method compels the visual encoder to actively encode high-frequency evidence rather than relying on shape-only shortcuts. A FiLM-style modulation branch conditioned on fused shared semantics and the detail band provides factual and counterfactual granule losses during training only. The total objective combines standard classification loss with semantic anchoring, factual granule, and counterfactual granule terms, all with fixed weight 0.1, and the entire framework adds negligible inference overhead since the VAE teacher and FiLM branch are removed at test time.

## Results

On the 11-dataset base-to-novel benchmark (16-shot), SpecPL as a plug-in booster achieves a new harmonic-mean (HM) ceiling of 81.51% when combined with MMRL, surpassing all standalone prompt-learning methods. The largest absolute gains appear on weaker text-only baselines: CoOp improves from 71.66% to 76.52% HM (+4.86), and CoCoOp from 75.83% to 77.83% HM (+2.00). Gains are most pronounced on fine-grained and texture-heavy datasets, e.g., FGVC-Aircraft (CoOp: 28.75→35.98 HM) and DTD (CoOp: 54.24→64.06 HM). Cross-dataset transfer (ImageNet→10 targets) and domain generalization (ImageNet→V2/Sketch/A/R) also show consistent improvements of +2.23 and +0.78 average accuracy over CoOp, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpecPL is positioned as a universal plug-and-play booster rather than a standalone method, so the primary comparison is each baseline with and without SpecPL. Against CoOp (Zhou et al., IJCV 2022), SpecPL delivers the largest gain of +4.86 HM (71.66→76.52), driven by a 31.6% reduction in the base-to-novel generalization gap; this is the most dramatic improvement in the paper. Against CoCoOp (Zhou et al., CVPR 2022), the gain is +2.00 HM (75.83→77.83), with the generalization gap remaining nearly unchanged, indicating the benefit comes from improved novel-class transfer rather than base-class fitting. Against MaPLe (Khattak et al., CVPR 2023), SpecPL adds +0.80 HM (78.55→79.35), a smaller but consistent gain that confirms complementary visual-side guidance beyond MaPLe's multi-modal prompt coupling. Against the strongest standalone baseline MMRL (CVPR 2025, HM 81.20), SpecPL pushes the ceiling to 81.51% HM (+0.31), a modest but meaningful margin on an already strong method. Against CLIP zero-shot (Radford et al., ICML 2021, HM 71.70), all prompt-learning variants including SpecPL substantially outperform the frozen model, with MMRL+SpecPL achieving a +9.81 HM advantage. Notably, SpecPL's relative advantage shrinks as the host baseline strengthens (from +4.86 on CoOp to +0.31 on MMRL), suggesting that the spectral-granularity guidance is most impactful when the baseline lacks any explicit visual-side mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpecPL extends the prompt-learning lineage initiated by CoOp, CoCoOp, and MaPLe, which optimize learnable text tokens while treating the visual encoder as a frozen holistic feature extractor. The paper departs from this text-centric paradigm by introducing a Spatial-Spectral Proxy that decomposes visual signals in a frozen VAE latent manifold into low-frequency semantic invariants and high-frequency granular details, drawing on Slow Feature Analysis and latent diffusion manifold geometry rather than pixel-space Fourier analysis. It also builds on visual prompt tuning (Jia et al., 2022) but explicitly separates semantic invariance from instance-specific texture, and on FiLM conditioning for the counterfactual granule supervision branch. The Visual Semantic Bank with EMA-updated prototypes and soft-retrieval text refinement is a new architectural component not present in prior prompt-learning methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpecPL offers a practical, zero-inference-overhead plug-in that addresses the modality asymmetry in VLM prompt learning, with the strongest gains on fine-grained and texture-heavy benchmarks (FGVC-Aircraft, DTD) where existing text-only prompts struggle. Key limitations include dependence on an external VAE teacher during training (adding ~112s preprocessing and 36MB cache on ImageNet), diminishing returns on coarse-grained tasks where global shape suffices, and sensitivity to high-frequency domain corruption. Open questions raised by the authors include whether spectral decomposition can be learned intrinsically within the CLIP encoder without an external VAE, extension to spatiotemporal video tokens, and adaptive frequency gating per image.
