## Problem

Existing embedding benchmarks such as MTEB and BEIR are designed for general-purpose retrieval (e.g., Wikipedia search) and do not capture the specialized, dynamically evolving retrieval demands of LLM agent systems—tool selection, episodic memory recall, code snippet search, and reasoning-intensive document retrieval. This gap leaves practitioners without a reliable way to select or optimize embedding models for agent-oriented applications, and obscures whether models that excel on standard benchmarks actually generalize to the retrieval patterns agents encounter in practice.

## Input / Output

Each AOEB dataset pairs a query set (text, image, or image-text) with a document collection (text, image, or mixed-modality including image-text interleaved sequences) and relevance judgments, evaluated via nDCG@10. For interleaved multi-image inputs that exceed single-image model capacity, two adaptation strategies are applied—Merge-image (resizing and concatenating all images into one composite) and Visual-doc (rendering the full interleaved content as a screenshot)—and the better result is reported. Overall model ranking uses the Borda count across all 43+ dataset subsets rather than a simple average, specifically to penalize models with high mean scores but large cross-task variance.

## Method

AOEB organizes evaluation around five agent-oriented meta-tasks—Deep QA, Code Retrieval, Tool Retrieval, Reasoning-intensive Retrieval, and Memory Retrieval—spanning 16 datasets and 43+ subsets across text-to-text, text-to-image, and image-text-to-image/text modalities. Datasets are selected or newly constructed under three principles: practical alignment with real agent workflows, appropriate difficulty (excluding saturated tasks), and balanced scale to ensure statistical robustness without excessive compute. A distinctive design element is the SubsetRetrieval protocol for memory tasks, which constrains each query to a per-user document subset (drawn from a uniform distribution of 7–50 documents) rather than the full collection, mirroring how agents retrieve from individual user histories. The benchmark is built on the MTEB toolkit 2.0 with a custom extension for mixed-modality retrieval collections, and ships both a full multimodal suite (AOEB) and a text-only subset (AOEB-T) to accommodate text-only embedding models.

## Results

On the full multimodal AOEB benchmark (Table 2), Qwen3VL-2B achieves the best Borda rank (1st) with an average macro score of 51.03 and micro score of 48.06 across 64 datasets, outperforming GME-7B (48.65/45.18, rank 2) and Jina-v4 (45.06/42.29, rank 3). On the text-only AOEB-T subset (Table 3, 43 datasets), Qwen3VL-2B again leads with 42.70/37.39 (rank 1), followed by Qwen3-8B (38.86/34.45, rank 2) and E5-Mistral (39.82/35.01, rank 3). The paper demonstrates that average scores can be misleading: GME-7B has a higher macro average than Qwen3-8B (39.67 vs. 38.86) but ranks lower in Borda (4th vs. 2nd), revealing greater cross-task variance. All models remain far from saturating the benchmark, with the best model scoring only ~51 on a 0–100 nDCG@10 scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen3VL-2B (2B params) dominates both multimodal and text-only evaluations, achieving Borda rank 1 in both settings and outperforming all larger models (GME-7B at 8B, mmE5 at 11B) on every meta-task category. Among MLLM-based models, GME-7B ranks 2nd in multimodal (48.65) and 4th in text-only (39.67), while Jina-v4 (4B) ranks 3rd in multimodal (45.06) but drops to 10th in text-only (37.58), suggesting its strength is primarily in visual retrieval. The encoder-based VISTA (0.2B) consistently underperforms MLLM-based models (rank 7 multimodal, not evaluated text-only), attributed to its frozen text embedding space. Notably, mmE5 (11B) and VLM2Vec (4B) perform poorly (ranks 5 and 8 multimodal), which the authors attribute to their General Embedding training objective optimizing for classification and grounding rather than retrieval. In the text-only setting, E5-Mistral (7B) achieves rank 3 with strong cross-task consistency, while NV-Embed-v2 (8B) drops to rank 5 despite strong MTEB/RTEB scores, confirming that AOEB captures capabilities not reflected in general benchmarks. The paper further shows that model rankings on AOEB diverge significantly from those on MTEB, RTEB, and MMEB, indicating that agent-oriented retrieval poses a distinct out-of-distribution challenge for existing embedding models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the embedding benchmarking lineage (MTEB, BEIR, RTEB, MMEB, MIEB) by reframing retrieval evaluation around the specific capabilities of LLM agents rather than general-purpose information retrieval. It builds directly on the MTEB toolkit for infrastructure but departs conceptually by introducing five agent-specific meta-tasks (deep QA, code, tool, reasoning-intensive, and memory retrieval) and by incorporating multimodal interleaved content that prior benchmarks do not cover. The Borda count ranking is a methodological departure from the standard average-score reporting, motivated by the observation that agent applications require robustness across diverse retrieval scenarios rather than peak performance on any single task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical finding is that strong performance on established general benchmarks (MTEB, RTEB) does not reliably predict performance on agent-oriented retrieval, which challenges the field's implicit assumption that general retrieval scores are sufficient proxies for model selection in agentic systems. The downstream validation (Table 4) confirms that AOEB scores correlate with end-to-end agent performance, giving the benchmark practical grounding. Open questions include: how to prevent overfitting once AOEB is public, whether proxy evaluation protocols can approximate expensive end-to-end agent metrics, and how to extend coverage to audio/video modalities and multilingual settings. The paper also highlights the urgent need for embedding models that natively handle interleaved image-text content, a common pattern in real agent interactions.
