## Problem

Existing distribution-matching methods for dataset distillation operate exclusively in Euclidean latent spaces, which can only capture flat structures and overlook the intrinsic curvature of real data. Since high-dimensional data often lie on low-dimensional manifolds with hierarchical (hyperbolic) or cyclical (spherical) geometry, flat embeddings inevitably limit the capacity of distilled samples to preserve the underlying data manifold structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262523/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real dataset of labeled images (MNIST 28×28 grayscale, CIFAR-10/100 32×32 RGB, Imagenette/ImageWoof 128×128 RGB) and an initialized synthetic dataset of the same format, and produces a compact synthetic dataset (m ≪ n samples) whose distribution matches the real data in a product Riemannian space. The embedding dimensions are fixed at (1024, 512, 512) for the Euclidean, hyperbolic, and spherical factors respectively, totaling 2048 dimensions to match the NCFM baseline. The distilled synthetic images are then used to train downstream classifiers (ConvNet-3, ResNet-18, AlexNet) for evaluation.

## Method

GeoDM performs distribution matching in a Cartesian product manifold P = E^{d_E} × H^{d_H}_{c_H} × S^{d_S}_{c_S}, implemented via a Riemannian CNN with spherical convolutions, hyperbolic exponential/logarithmic map layers, and standard Euclidean convolutions. Two adaptive mechanisms shape the product space: learnable curvature parameters (c_H < 0, c_S > 0) regularized by radius-deviation losses that force embeddings to conform to the Poincaré ball boundary and unit sphere, and learnable softmax-normalized weights (α̃, β̃, γ̃) that dynamically control the relative contribution of each geometry in the product-space distance. A geometry-aware optimal transport loss (squared 2-Wasserstein distance under the product metric) couples the three geometric components, preserves class-conditional mass, and prevents degenerate dominance of any single factor. The overall objective combines the NCFM distribution-matching loss, the OT loss, and curvature regularization, and the paper proves that product-space matching yields a strictly tighter generalization error bound than Euclidean-only matching under mild regularity assumptions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with IPC=10, GeoDM achieves 74.4% test accuracy, surpassing the strongest Euclidean baseline NCFM (71.8%) by ~2.6 points and the next-best DREAM (67.2%) by ~7 points. On the more challenging CIFAR-100, GeoDM reaches 38.0% at IPC=1 and 49.2% at IPC=10, outperforming NCFM (34.4% and 48.4%) by 3.6 and 0.8 points respectively. On MNIST IPC=1, GeoDM attains 96.1%, edging out DREAM (95.3%). The gains are most pronounced at low IPC budgets, where geometric structure compensates for severe data scarcity, and diminish as IPC increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoDM consistently outperforms all 18 baselines across the three primary datasets. Against the strongest Euclidean distribution-matching baseline NCFM (Wang et al., 2025), GeoDM gains 2.6 points on CIFAR-10 IPC=10 (74.4% vs. 71.8%), 3.6 points on CIFAR-100 IPC=1 (38.0% vs. 34.4%), and 0.8 points on CIFAR-100 IPC=10 (49.2% vs. 48.4%). Against trajectory-matching methods (ATT, FTD, MTT), GeoDM leads by 6–7 points on CIFAR-10 IPC=10 and 4–5 points on CIFAR-100 IPC=10. Against gradient-matching methods (DC, DCC, DSA), the margin is even larger, exceeding 20 points on CIFAR-10 IPC=10. The closest competitor is DSDM on CIFAR-10 IPC=50 (75.3% vs. GeoDM's 78.3%) and on CIFAR-100 IPC=50 (53.1% vs. 55.0%), where GeoDM still leads by 2–3 points. On MNIST IPC=10, DREAM (98.4%) marginally edges out GeoDM (98.2%), but GeoDM recovers the lead at IPC=50 (99.2% vs. 99.0%). Cross-architecture transfer (Table 2) confirms GeoDM's advantage persists when distilled data is evaluated with ResNet-18 and AlexNet, and higher-resolution experiments on Imagenette/ImageWoof show consistent 1–1.5 point gains over NCFM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distribution-matching (DM) framework for dataset distillation, which has been developed through a line of work from DM (Zhao & Bilen, 2023) through M3D, DSDM, and NCFM (Wang et al., 2025), by moving the matching from Euclidean latent spaces to a Cartesian product of Euclidean, hyperbolic, and spherical manifolds. The key conceptual departure is the argument that real data carries intrinsic curvature (hierarchical or cyclical) that flat Euclidean embeddings structurally cannot represent, motivating a Riemannian CNN with learnable curvature and per-geometry weights. The framework is deliberately agnostic to the specific DM objective (NCFM, DSDM, or vanilla DM), positioning it as a geometric wrapper rather than a competing matching criterion, and it adds a geometry-aware optimal transport loss as an auxiliary regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.2) provides the first formal proof that product-space distribution matching yields strictly tighter generalization bounds than Euclidean-only matching, grounding the empirical gains in a principled argument about geometric distortion. The consistent 2–3% improvements across MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, and higher-resolution Imagenette/ImageWoof, combined with cross-architecture transfer results, suggest the geometric modeling is a broadly applicable component rather than a dataset-specific trick. A key limitation is the fixed dimensionality of each manifold factor, which the authors acknowledge could be made learnable; additionally, the OT loss contributes only marginal gains over the product space alone, leaving open whether more sophisticated transport formulations could unlock further benefits. The interpretability experiment (curvature shrinking under noise injection) is a useful diagnostic but does not yet establish when or why a particular geometry dominates for a given dataset.
