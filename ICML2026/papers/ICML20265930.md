## Problem

Existing PDE-constrained diffusion models enforce physical constraints on the posterior mean E[x₀|xₜ] (via Tweedie's formula) rather than on the final clean sample x₀, introducing a Jensen's Gap that creates a fundamental trade-off between PDE satisfaction and generative fidelity. This mismatch degrades physical consistency during both training (conflicting data and residual objectives) and sampling (velocity field errors at intermediate timesteps), yet no prior method eliminates it from the student objective.

## Input / Output

The student model d_θ'(ε) takes a single Gaussian noise vector ε ~ N(0,I) and produces the joint PDE field x₀ = (u, a) on a 128×128 grid in one forward pass. For conditional tasks (forward, inverse, reconstruction), the input additionally includes a partial observation x' and a binary mask M; the method constructs a mixed sample x_mix = x'⊙M + d_θ'(ε)⊙(1−M) to enforce hard consistency with known entries before evaluating the PDE residual. An optional refinement stage iteratively updates ε via gradient descent on the physics residual for stricter constraint satisfaction.

## Method

PIDDM decouples physics enforcement from the diffusion trajectory via a two-stage post-hoc distillation. First, a teacher diffusion model (FNO with v-prediction/flow-matching parameterization) is trained with the standard denoising objective alone, preserving generative fidelity without any PDE loss. Second, the teacher's deterministic ODE sampling trajectory generates paired (ε, x₀) data, and a student model is trained to map ε → x₀ in one step using a combined loss: L = E[‖d_θ'(ε) − x₀‖² + λ_train‖R(d_θ'(ε))‖²], where the PDE residual R is evaluated directly on the student's final output rather than on posterior-mean surrogates. This eliminates the Jensen's Gap from the student objective, and the paper provides a theoretical analysis (Theorem B.1–B.2) showing that vanilla score learning converges slowly along constrained directions while the distillation penalty shrinks the constrained coordinate by a factor 1/(1+λ). The framework supports one-step generation, optional gradient-based refinement, and unified forward/inverse/reconstruction tasks via latent-space optimization with hard-constraint masking.

## Results

On Darcy flow, PIDDM-1 (one-step) achieves MMSE 0.112×10⁻², SMSE 0.082×10⁻², and PDE error 0.226×10⁻⁴ with only 1 NFE, while PIDDM-ref (80-step refinement) further reduces these to 0.037×10⁻², 0.002×10⁻², and 0.148×10⁻⁴. On Poisson, PIDDM-ref achieves the best PDE error (0.050×10⁻⁹) and FPD (0.659) across all methods. On Burgers' equation, PIDDM-ref attains MMSE 0.012×10⁻² and PDE error 0.174×10⁻³, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin on physical consistency while remaining 60× faster than D-Flow (80 vs. 5000 NFE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIDDM dominates PIDM (Bastek et al., 2025) across all three PDE benchmarks: on Darcy, PIDDM-ref achieves PDE error 0.148×10⁻⁴ vs. PIDM's 1.236×10⁻⁴ (8.3× lower) and FPD 0.385 vs. 1.983 (5.1× lower), while also being 12.5× faster (80 vs. 1000 NFE). Against DiffusionPDE (Huang et al., 2024a), PIDDM-ref wins decisively on PDE error (e.g., 0.148 vs. 1.071 on Darcy, 0.050 vs. 1.270 on Poisson) and FPD, though DiffusionPDE achieves a slightly lower one-step MMSE on Burgers (0.064 vs. 0.152 for PIDDM-1); however, PIDDM-ref's 0.012 MMSE on Burgers is 5.3× better. Compared to ECI-sampling (Cheng et al., 2025), PIDDM-1 already matches or beats ECI on MMSE/SMSE while reducing PDE error by 5–30× (e.g., 0.226 vs. 1.582 on Darcy), and PIDDM-ref extends this lead further. D-Flow (Ben-Hamu et al., 2024) is the closest competitor on PDE error for the one-step setting (0.532 vs. 0.226 on Darcy), but requires 5000 NFE (62.5× more than PIDDM-ref) and 2000 backward passes, making it impractical for latency-sensitive applications; PIDDM-ref achieves lower PDE error than D-Flow on all three datasets while being 62.5× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

PIDDM builds on the knowledge distillation framework for diffusion models (Luhman & Luhman, 2021) and the flow-matching/v-prediction training formulation (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023b), but departs fundamentally from prior PDE-constrained diffusion methods (PIDM, DiffusionPDE, ECI-sampling) that enforce physical constraints on the posterior mean E[x₀|xₜ] during training or sampling. The key conceptual shift is moving PDE supervision from the noisy diffusion trajectory to the final generated sample in a post-hoc distillation stage, thereby eliminating the Jensen's Gap that plagues posterior-mean-based approaches. The hard-constraint masking strategy for downstream tasks is borrowed from ECI-sampling (Cheng et al., 2025), while the optional refinement stage draws on noise-prompting ideas (Ben-Hamu et al., 2024) but operates in a one-step setting to avoid trajectory backpropagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PIDDM offers a practical and efficient pathway to physics-informed diffusion models: one-step generation (1 NFE) with competitive or superior PDE residual satisfaction compared to multi-step guided methods, while supporting forward, inverse, and reconstruction tasks in a unified framework. The empirical and theoretical demonstration of Jensen's Gap (Theorem B.1 showing slow convergence in the constrained score direction) provides a clear diagnostic for why posterior-mean guidance degrades physical fidelity, which is relevant beyond PDEs to any constrained generative task. Limitations include dependence on a well-trained teacher and a reliable PDE residual operator, and the approach has been validated primarily on 2D/1D PDEs with FNO architecture; generalization to higher-dimensional or strongly nonlinear systems remains open.
