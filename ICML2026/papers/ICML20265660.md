## Problem

Few-shot time series forecasting suffers from severe overfitting due to limited training data, and existing augmentation methods—whether heuristic perturbations or generic generative models—fail to target the specific distributional regions where forecasting models are most vulnerable. The core gap is that augmentation is decoupled from downstream forecasting performance: there is no closed-loop mechanism that identifies *where* in the training distribution the model generalizes poorly and then synthesizes data specifically in those regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw multivariate time series sequences (lookback 96, horizon 96) across datasets with 7–862 features. Internally, the VMAE actor receives a masked version of an anchor sequence plus its absolute timestamp, samples a latent code from the prior, and decodes it into a full augmented (x, y) pair. The final output is a set of augmented samples (3× the original training set size) that are concatenated with the original data to train a standard forecasting backbone (iTransformer, PatchTST, or DLinear), which then produces point forecasts on the held-out test set.

## Method

ReAugment is a three-stage closed-loop pipeline. Stage A builds a model zoo of K cross-validation forecasting models and computes per-sample Model-Zoo Variance (the variance of prediction errors across zoo members) to select the top 50% highest-variance samples as overfit-prone anchor points. Stage B pretrains a Variational Masked Autoencoder (VMAE) on those anchors, where the prior network maps masked data + timestamp to a latent code and the decoder reconstructs the full sequence. Stage C fine-tunes only the VMAE prior as an RL policy using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO): for each masked input, G latent codes are sampled, decoded, and scored by a reward that is a scaled sigmoid of (model-zoo variance of the generated sample / reconstruction error), with group-relative advantages normalizing across the zoo's heterogeneous reward scales. This RL formulation avoids backpropagating gradients through the forecasting model zoo, bypassing gradient vanishing while still steering generation toward high-variance, generalization-critical regions.

## Results

Under the few-shot setting (10% training data) with iTransformer as backbone, ReAugment achieves the lowest MAE and MSE across all eight benchmarks. Headline improvements include ETTh2 (MAE 0.337 vs. 0.362 original, MSE 0.301 vs. 0.320), Traffic (MAE 0.291 vs. 0.318, MSE 0.427 vs. 0.466), and ETTm1 (MAE 0.411 vs. 0.440, MSE 0.436 vs. 0.470). The Augmentation Recovery Factor (F) confirms that ReAugment recovers 37.5%–208.3% of the few-shot-to-full-data performance gap, while several baselines yield negative values (i.e., harmful augmentation). Under the standard full-data setup, ReAugment also improves all datasets, e.g., ETTm1 MSE drops from 0.341 to 0.327 and Weather MSE from 0.178 to 0.171.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReAugment outperforms all four augmentation baselines (Gaussian, Convolve, TimeGAN, ADA) on every dataset in both MAE and MSE under the few-shot setting. Against TimeGAN (Yoon et al., 2019), the largest gap appears on Traffic (MAE 0.291 vs. 0.315) and ETTh2 (0.337 vs. 0.366), where the adversarial generative approach fails to target overfit-prone regions. Against ADA (Schneider et al., 2024), ReAugment wins on all datasets; ADA actually degrades performance on ETTh2 (MAE 0.368 vs. 0.362 original) and Exchange (0.235 vs. 0.228), yielding negative Recovery Factors (−50.0% MAE, −76.5% MSE on Exchange). The heuristic baselines (Gaussian, Convolve) are consistently worse than the original un-augmented model on most datasets, confirming that untargeted perturbation is harmful under data scarcity. In the foundation-model comparison (Table 4), ReAugment+iTransformer beats Chronos-2 on all five reported datasets (e.g., ETTh2 MSE 0.301 vs. 0.311) and beats TimesFM on four of five, suggesting targeted augmentation can rival or exceed large pretrained models in domain-specific few-shot scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of time series data augmentation and few-shot forecasting, departing from both heuristic methods (Gaussian noise, Convolve) and decoupled generative approaches (TimeGAN, ADA) by closing the loop between augmentation and downstream forecasting performance. The model-zoo variance diagnostic repurposes cross-validation ensemble disagreement as a spatial signal for where to augment, a concept borrowed from ensemble uncertainty estimation but applied here as a data-selection criterion rather than a prediction-confidence measure. The RL formulation (GRPO over a VMAE latent space) draws on recent policy-optimization work in other domains (e.g., RL-based sample selection for change detection) but is, to the authors' claim, the first to optimize a continuous generative augmentation policy specifically for time series forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is strong for domains where historical data is genuinely scarce (new sensors, emerging markets, rare events), and the finding that a small iTransformer with targeted augmentation can outperform large foundation models (TimesFM, Chronos-2) on few-shot benchmarks suggests a complementary path to scaling. The model-zoo variance as an interpretable, data-level diagnostic for generalization bottlenecks is a reusable tool beyond this specific pipeline. Key limitations include the computational overhead of training K cross-validation models plus the VMAE and GRPO stages, and the method's dependence on a sufficiently diverse model zoo to produce meaningful variance signals; whether the approach transfers to univariate or extremely short sequences remains untested.
