## Problem

Existing object hallucination detectors for MLLMs rely on vision-based signals such as patch-level attention weights or image-embedding similarity, but these can be spuriously overconfident when the visual encoder or cross-modal interactions introduce misleading visual information. The paper identifies a previously overlooked signal: instruction token embeddings in MLLMs implicitly encode grounded visual semantics while effectively suppressing hallucination-inducing noise from image embeddings, offering a natural calibration mechanism that prior methods have not exploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262270/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a fixed text instruction (e.g., 'Please describe the image in detail') fed to a frozen MLLM, then extracts instruction embeddings and object-token embeddings from the penultimate decoder layer. It outputs a continuous InsLen score per object token in the generated answer, which is thresholded into a binary hallucination/truth label. No auxiliary models, additional training, or modified decoding are required; the detector is plug-and-play across architectures (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, mPLUG-Owl3, LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen3-VL).

## Method

The core insight is that projecting instruction embeddings through the Logit Lens (unembedding matrix) yields vocabulary-space confidence distributions that assign high probability to image-grounded objects while suppressing hallucinated ones, effectively filtering misleading visual information. The InsLen score combines two complementary components: (1) a Calibrated Local Score that multiplicatively fuses a Calibration Confidence (the maximum Logit-Lens probability assigned to the object token across all instruction embeddings) with a vision-based Local Similarity Score, thereby suppressing spuriously high patch-level visual evidence; and (2) a Context Consistency Score that selects the top-m instruction embeddings with highest probability for the object token, averages them into a global context vector, and measures normalized L2 distance consistency with the object token embedding, weighted by the average confidence of the selected embeddings. The final score is a convex combination S_Ins = ω·S_cls + (1−ω)·S_ccs with ω=0.4, capturing both calibrated local visual evidence and global object-level contextual consistency in a single training-free, architecture-agnostic detector.

## Results

On the MSCOCO benchmark, InsLen achieves the best AUROC across all five MLLMs, reaching 86.93% on LLaVA-1.5-7B, 85.50% on InstructBLIP-7B, 75.11% on mPLUG-Owl3-8B, 82.41% on LLaVA-OneVision1.5-8B, and 81.02% on Qwen3-VL-8B, with corresponding AUPR values of 96.38%, 94.74%, 96.47%, 96.99%, and 96.70%. On the more challenging Objects365 benchmark (365 categories), InsLen maintains its lead with AUROC of 76.32% (LLaVA-1.5) and 77.44% (Qwen3-VL). On the POPE binary-probing benchmark, InsLen achieves up to 83.94% AUROC (LLaVA-1.5), surpassing the strongest baseline GLSIM by 13.81% AUROC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InsLen outperforms all seven baselines on every benchmark and every model architecture tested. The strongest competitor is GLSIM (Park & Li, NeurIPS 2025), which InsLen surpasses by 7.70% AUROC on MSCOCO with Qwen3-VL (81.02 vs. 73.32) and by up to 13.81% AUROC on POPE with LLaVA-1.5 (83.94 vs. 70.13). SVAR (Jiang et al., CVPR 2025) is the second-strongest baseline on several models; InsLen beats it by 6.60% AUROC on Objects365 with Qwen3-VL (77.44 vs. 70.84) and by 9.33% AUROC on MSCOCO with LLaVA-OneVision1.5 (82.41 vs. 73.08). Internal Confidence (Jiang et al., ICLR 2025) and EAZY (Che et al., ICCV 2025) trail further behind, with InsLen exceeding them by 13–18% AUROC on MSCOCO. The token-level baselines NLL (Zhou et al., ICLR 2024) and Entropy (Malinin & Gales, ICLR 2021) are consistently the weakest, with InsLen outperforming them by 20+ AUROC points on most model–benchmark combinations. Contextual Lens (Phukan et al., ACL 2025) shows particularly poor performance on mPLUG-Owl3 (57.55 AUROC) and LLaVA-OneVision1.5 (61.02 AUROC), where InsLen achieves 75.11% and 82.41% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Logit Lens technique for interpreting intermediate transformer representations and the Local Similarity Score from GLSIM as its vision-based component. It departs from the dominant line of OH detection that relies on patch-level image embeddings or attention patterns by identifying instruction embeddings as a previously overlooked signal that implicitly encodes filtered visual semantics. The key conceptual departure is treating instruction tokens not merely as prompt carriers but as a natural filter that suppresses misleading visual information introduced by the visual encoder, enabling both calibration of local visual scores and provision of global object-level context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that instruction embeddings act as a visual information filter is a useful interpretability insight that could inform future decoding strategies or post-training techniques for hallucination mitigation, as the authors note. However, the method is limited to detection (not mitigation), and its effectiveness degrades on architectures like InstructBLIP where the Q-Former already performs aggressive visual filtering before the LLM sees the tokens. Open questions include whether the instruction-embedding signal can be leveraged during generation (e.g., as a decoding-time constraint) and whether the Logit Lens approximation introduces systematic biases (synonym encoding, representation drift) that limit generalization to unseen object categories.
