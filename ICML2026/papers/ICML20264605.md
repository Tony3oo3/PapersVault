## Problem

RL training for LLMs is bottlenecked by long-tail rollout length distributions, where a small fraction of trajectories consume disproportionate compute and block synchronous training. Existing prompt-level scheduling approaches (Tail Batching, Partial Rollout) merely defer long-tail execution without addressing the root cause: the intra-prompt long-tail distribution itself, which is largely composed of verbose and ineffective trajectories that waste resources without improving learning.

## Input / Output

For each training prompt, DARTS generates M_i' redundant trajectories (where M_i' ≥ M, the target group size) from the current policy, then selectively samples a subset of M trajectories to form the GRPO training group. The redundancy budget M_i' is not fixed but adaptively allocated per prompt based on smoothed historical response-length variance, with bounds [M, 2M]. The output is a trained policy whose rollout distribution is progressively shaped toward shorter, more concentrated lengths, verified to preserve (and sometimes improve) downstream reasoning accuracy.

## Method

DARTS introduces active distribution shaping via three coupled mechanisms. First, dual-end length sampling constructs each training group from the top-K shortest and top-L longest trajectories (K+L=M, K≫L) drawn from a redundantly generated candidate pool, which shifts the GRPO group reward baseline to either suppress verbose correct answers or amplify necessary long reasoning paths. Second, a variance-based adaptive redundancy allocation scheme solves a convex optimization to distribute the total exploration budget across prompts, allocating more redundancy to high-variance (uncertain, long-tailed) prompts and less to low-variance ones. Third, system-level optimizations include variance-guided tail pruning (switching to shortest-only sampling with proactive early stopping when M_i' saturates at the upper bound) and token-level streaming that overlaps forward computation with ongoing generation at finer granularity than sample-level streaming.

## Results

DARTS achieves up to 1.77× end-to-end throughput speedup over VeRL and 1.43× over Tail Batching (RollPacker) across model scales from 3B to 32B parameters, with gains increasing for larger models (1.29× on Qwen2.5-3B to 1.77× on Qwen3-30B-A3B). On quality, DARTS maintains or improves zero-shot BIG-Bench Hard scores (e.g., Qwen2.5-32B: 78.1→84.7; Qwen2.5-3B: 34.7→38.7) and matches VeRL on mathematical benchmarks (MATH500, GSM8K, AIME2024/2025, OlympiadBench). Training convergence closely tracks the VeRL baseline, confirming that distribution shaping does not compromise model performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VeRL (the state-of-the-art open-source RL framework), DARTS achieves a 1.29×–1.77× end-to-end throughput speedup while matching or slightly improving downstream benchmark accuracy, with the largest gains on larger models exhibiting more pronounced long-tail behavior. Against Tail Batching from RollPacker (the leading prompt-level tail scheduling approach), DARTS achieves a 1.43× speedup, demonstrating that actively shaping the rollout distribution is more effective than merely deferring long-tail prompts to a separate queue. On BBH generalization, DARTS improves over VeRL on 4 out of 5 model configurations (e.g., +6.6 points on Qwen2.5-32B), with only a marginal 1.4-point drop on Qwen3-30B-A3B. The ablation confirms that the distribution-aware sampling contributes 1.40× and adaptive redundancy allocation further boosts to 1.63×, while token-level streaming adds a 9% gain on top.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as the core RL algorithm and VeRL (Sheng et al., 2025) as the system framework, incorporating DAPO (Yu et al., 2026) training optimizations. It departs from the dominant line of prior work on rollout long-tail mitigation—prompt-level tail scheduling (Tail Batching in RollPacker, Partial Rollout)—by shifting focus from inter-prompt variance to the intra-prompt long-tail distribution, arguing that scheduling merely defers latency without addressing the root cause. The key conceptual departure is 'active distribution shaping': rather than reordering or deferring expensive prompts, DARTS selectively samples from an expanded intra-prompt exploration space to reshape the policy's length distribution toward conciseness, with a variance-based allocation mechanism deciding where to invest redundancy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 1.77× end-to-end speedup on the dominant RL training bottleneck (rollout) without degrading convergence or downstream accuracy is practically significant for teams running large-scale RL post-training. The characterization of intra-prompt long tails as often 'verbose and ineffective' (Pattern I) versus 'necessary and effective' (Pattern II) provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding where rollout compute is wasted. Limitations include reduced benefit for tasks with tightly constrained output lengths (translation, fixed-format generation) and sparse-reward settings where within-prompt discrimination is weak; broader validation on multimodal and agentic models remains open.
