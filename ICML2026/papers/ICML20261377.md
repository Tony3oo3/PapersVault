## Problem

Existing backdoor defenses for RL agents either require white-box access to model parameters or value networks, operate only at the trajectory/episode level (requiring complete episodes before detection), or are restricted to a single attack type. No prior method provides online, step-level detection under a practical black-box setting that generalizes across both perturbation-based and adversary-agent attacks, particularly against hard-coded backdoors that bypass optimization-based defenses entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261377/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, PolicyGuard receives a suspicious state-action pair (s_t, a_t) along with the observed history {x_1, …, x_t} up to the current step, plus a bank of N reference trajectories collected from a clean environment (20,000 episodes of length T=200). States are either 84×84 grayscale 4-frame-stacked images (Atari) or low-dimensional physical state vectors (MuJoCo). The output is a scalar uncertainty score per time step, computed as the Interquartile Mean of GP posterior variances across N pseudo trajectories, which is thresholded to flag backdoor-infected steps without requiring any future observations.

## Method

PolicyGuard trains an additive Gaussian Process with deep recurrent kernels (adapted from EDGE) on clean trajectories: a CNN/MLP encodes each state, a GRU produces sequential latent representations h_t, and two GP components (timestep-level and episode-level, both with squared-exponential kernels) jointly predict the final reward via sparse variational inference with M inducing points, optimized end-to-end by maximizing the ELBO. The key detection mechanism is the GP posterior variance at step t, which naturally quantifies epistemic uncertainty and rises when the agent's behavior deviates from the learned clean manifold. To handle the fact that the RNN encoder requires full trajectory context, PolicyGuard constructs N pseudo trajectories by splicing the observed history {x_1,…,x_t} with future segments from reference trajectories, enabling per-step variance computation without accessing future steps. The final uncertainty score aggregates per-trajectory variances via Interquartile Mean, and theoretical results (Theorems 3.1, 3.3) establish that in-distribution points drive variance to zero as training data grows while backdoor-triggered representations, lying farther from inducing points, yield strictly higher expected uncertainty.

## Results

PolicyGuard achieves an average AUROC of 0.856 for perturbation-based attacks and 0.859 for adversary-agent attacks under original attack settings, and 0.868/0.878 under hard-coded attack settings where all optimization-based defenses (NC, STRIP, SCALE-UP, PolicyCleanse) completely fail with AUROC near 0.5. Under hard-coded attacks, PolicyGuard outperforms the only other functional baseline, SHINE (0.826/0.810), by 4.2 and 6.8 AUROC points respectively. The method is the only approach evaluated that maintains strong detection across all seven games and both attack categories simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under original attacks, STRIP achieves the highest perturbation-based AUROC (0.900) but is inapplicable to adversary-agent attacks; PolicyGuard leads on all four adversary-agent games (e.g., 0.895 on RTGH vs. SHINE's 0.789) and is competitive on perturbation games (0.856 avg. vs. STRIP's 0.900). Under the critical hard-coded attack setting, all optimization-based baselines (NC, STRIP, SCALE-UP, PolicyCleanse, B3D) collapse to AUROC ≈ 0.5, while PolicyGuard retains 0.868/0.878, outperforming SHINE (0.826/0.810) by 4–7 points across all seven games. PolicyGuard is the only method that generalizes across both attack types and both attack settings without requiring access to predicted probability vectors (unlike STRIP and NC) or complete episode trajectories (unlike SHINE). Against the most recent attack Q-Inception, PolicyGuard achieves AUROC 0.843 (Pong) and 0.864 (Breakout) with FPR ≈ 0.10, confirming robustness to newer threat models.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolicyGuard builds directly on the EDGE framework (Guo et al., 2021) for additive Gaussian Process trajectory modeling with deep recurrent kernels, repurposing GP posterior variance as a backdoor detection signal rather than an interpretability tool. It departs from existing RL backdoor defenses (SHINE, PolicyCleanse, BIRD) by operating at the individual time-step level under black-box access without requiring complete episodes or model internals, and by introducing pseudo trajectories to enable per-step uncertainty computation. The approach also draws on the broader supervised-learning backdoor defense literature (STRIP, Neural Cleanse, SCALE-UP) but explicitly addresses the temporal and sequential dependencies that make direct transfer infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PolicyGuard addresses a practically critical gap: detecting backdoor-infected steps online during deployment without model access, a setting where all optimization-based defenses fail completely under hard-coded attacks. The key limitation is the clean-environment assumption for GP training, and the method provides detection only without a built-in mitigation mechanism. Open questions include whether GP posterior variance remains a reliable uncertainty signal under sim-to-real transfer, noisy sensor inputs, and partially observable dynamics, as well as whether the approach scales to longer-horizon or continuous-action settings beyond the 200-step episodes evaluated here.
