## Problem

In outcome-supervised RLVR for LLMs, token-level importance sampling (as in GRPO) introduces high-variance updates and entropy collapse, while sequence-level importance sampling (as in GSPO) is stable but discards 45–55% of rollouts through conservative all-or-nothing clipping and applies flat credit assignment across all tokens. No existing method adaptively balances these two granularities based on whether the token-level signal is actually reliable for a given trajectory.

## Input / Output

Given a math prompt and a group of G responses sampled from the old policy, TGPO computes per-token log importance ratios, winsorizes them within the PPO clip range, and derives two robust sequence-level statistics (drift μ and within-sequence variance σ²). These feed a stop-gradient trust score that determines a per-response mixing coefficient α, which in turn produces anchored token-level weights w_t blending the sequence anchor with the raw token ratio in log-space. The final output is a standard PPO clipped surrogate loss averaged over tokens and responses, yielding updated policy parameters.

## Method

TGPO introduces two complementary mechanisms: a sequence anchor (the geometric mean of token log-ratios, inherited from GSPO) that provides a stable reference for the overall policy shift, and a dual-trust information gate that adaptively controls how much fine-grained token information enters the update. The trust score G = exp(−μ²/2ε²) · exp(−σ²/2ε²) is computed from winsorized log-ratio statistics and acts as a soft trust-region proxy: it decreases smoothly when either global drift or within-sequence variability becomes large. The mixing coefficient α = G/τ interpolates in log-space as log w_t = (1−α)μ + α log ρ_t, so high-trust responses permit expressive token-level credit assignment while low-trust responses collapse toward the robust sequence anchor without discarding the trajectory. The trust score is detached from the computation graph (stop-gradient), and the resulting anchored weights are plugged into a standard PPO clipped surrogate objective, preserving first-order equivalence to GRPO at the on-policy point while reducing variance in higher-order terms.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, TGPO achieves 50.5% average Greedy@1 accuracy across seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming the strongest baseline DAPO (46.7%) by 3.8 points and GSPO (44.4%) by 6.1 points. Under sampling-based evaluation, TGPO reaches 38.8% Avg@32 and 62.2% Pass@32, improving over DAPO by +0.9 and +4.2 points respectively. On Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, TGPO similarly leads with 27.0% average Greedy@1 versus DAPO's 26.2% and GSPO's 23.3%, confirming cross-architecture generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGPO outperforms all six baselines on every benchmark for Qwen2.5-Math-7B, with the largest margin on AIME24 (43.3% vs. 33.3% for DAPO and LUFFY, a +10-point gain) and AIME25 (20.0% vs. 16.7% for Oat-Zero and LUFFY). Against the closest competitor DAPO (46.7% avg), TGPO gains +3.8 points on average; against GSPO (44.4%), the gap widens to +6.1 points, reflecting GSPO's gradient underutilization from conservative sequence-level clipping. On Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, TGPO leads DAPO by +0.8 points (27.0% vs. 26.2%) and GSPO by +3.7 points (27.0% vs. 23.3%), with the most notable single-benchmark gain on AMC (30.0% vs. 22.5% for both DAPO and GSPO). Under Pass@32 evaluation, TGPO achieves 62.2% versus DAPO's 58.0% and GSPO's 57.4%, indicating that the trust-gated mechanism also improves the model's exploratory ceiling rather than merely sharpening deterministic outputs. SimpleRL-Zero and Oat-Zero, which are reported from publicly released weights rather than retrained under identical conditions, trail TGPO by 7.9 and 4.0 points respectively on the Qwen2.5 average.

## Relation to Prior Work

TGPO sits at the intersection of two lines of work in outcome-supervised RLVR: token-level importance sampling (GRPO, DAPO) and sequence-level importance sampling (GSPO). It inherits GRPO's group-normalized advantage estimation and GSPO's geometric-mean sequence anchor, but departs from both by introducing a trust-gated interpolation that adaptively blends the two granularities per trajectory. The trust score itself draws on trust-region optimization ideas (Gaussian penalty on drift and variance) and robust statistics (winsorization), repurposing them as a continuous control signal rather than a hard clipping boundary. The 'retain rather than exclude' philosophy contrasts with GSPO's all-or-nothing sequence-level clipping and with DAPO's dynamic sampling that discards low-information groups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, low-overhead mechanism for resolving the stability-efficiency tradeoff that plagues current GRPO-family methods, and the 'retain rather than exclude' principle could inform future RLVR designs beyond the specific trust-gating formulation. However, evaluation is confined to mathematical reasoning with two model families (7B and 3B), leaving open whether the trust-gating mechanism generalizes to process-level rewards, longer-horizon tasks, or larger models where the token-level signal may be more informative. The first-order equivalence proof is reassuring but does not guarantee that the trust score's specific Gaussian form is optimal; the sensitivity to τ is mild but the interaction with sequence length and reward sparsity remains unexplored.
