## Problem

Full-duplex spoken dialogue models currently waste the user's speaking phase by repeatedly predicting silence tokens, contributing nothing to response quality. Existing 'think-while-listening' alternatives that generate explicit Chain-of-Thought text tokens suffer from synchronization mismatches with streaming speech and introduce latency when switching from reasoning to response generation. The paper asks whether implicit, latent-space reasoning can replace explicit textual thinking while remaining strictly causal and latency-free.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265076/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes streaming user speech at 12.5 Hz (encoded by a frozen 600M Parakeet encoder) and produces text response tokens that are converted to speech at 25 Hz via a streaming flow-matching TTS (CosyVoice 2). During the listening phase, the LLM's per-frame output is not a discrete token but a continuous latent embedding (a softmax-weighted average over the vocabulary embedding matrix) that recursively feeds back as the next input. A learned binary timing signal G_t governs the switch between latent-reasoning mode and explicit text-generation mode at inference time.

## Method

FLAIR replaces silence-token prediction during the user's speaking phase with a recursive latent reasoning loop: the LLM's hidden state is passed through the output head to produce text logits, which are then softmax-weighted over the vocabulary embedding matrix to form a continuous embedding that serves as the next-step input. Training is grounded in a conditional ELBO objective: a non-causal BERT-based 'Global-aware Expert' sees the full dialogue (user speech + target response) to derive posterior latent embeddings, while the causal LLM is trained to match this posterior via KL divergence on its vocabulary-weighting distribution. The SFT loss combines three terms—next-token reconstruction on the response segment, variational regularization (KL between expert posterior and LLM prior during listening), and a binary timing-prediction loss—enabling efficient teacher-forced training without explicit reasoning annotations. At inference, the Expert is discarded and the model decodes step-by-step, switching between latent reasoning and text generation based on the predicted timing signal, adding zero extra latency.

## Results

On QA benchmarks, FLAIR with latent reasoning achieves 78.0% on Llama Questions, 43.0% on WebQuestions, 74.2% on OpenbookQA, and 56.2% on MMSU, outperforming its own no-thinking ablation (73.0/41.7/72.9/50.2) by 5–6 points on reasoning-heavy tasks. On conversational dynamics, FLAIR achieves 0.39 s turn-taking latency, 0.46 s barge-in latency, and 100% barge-in success rate on the Impatient dataset, with a UTMOS score of 4.3. On Full-Duplex-Bench, it attains 93.0% takeover rate with 0.43 s latency for turn-taking and 92.0% barge-in TOR with a GPT-4o score of 4.22.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among full-duplex baselines, FLAIR substantially outperforms Moshi (54.5→78.0 on LlamaQ, 24.0→56.2 on MMSU) and Freeze-Omni (56.2→78.0 on LlamaQ, 28.1→56.2 on MMSU), while slightly trailing SALMONN-omni on WebQuestions (43.0 vs 43.7) and TriviaQA (51.2 vs 56.0) but leading by 26 points on MMSU (56.2 vs 30.0). Against half-duplex models, FLAIR wins on LlamaQ, WebQuestions, SDQA, and OBQA versus Kimi-Audio and Baichuan-Audio, but concedes on open-ended GPT-scores (AlpacaEval 3.85 vs 4.46 for Kimi) and MMSU (56.2 vs 62.2 for Kimi), which the authors attribute to shorter response lengths in conversational training. On conversational dynamics (Table 2), FLAIR achieves the best barge-in success rate (100%) and lowest barge-in latency (0.46 s), surpassing ORISE (96.8%, 0.61 s) and Moshi (55.1%, 0.81 s). On Full-Duplex-Bench (Table 3), FLAIR's barge-in GPT-4o score (4.22) far exceeds all baselines (Moshi 0.77, dGSLM 0.20, Gemini Live 3.38), though its turn-taking TOR (93.0) is marginally below dGSLM (97.5) and Moshi (94.1). The latent reasoning ablation confirms that the thinking mechanism adds 5–6 points on reasoning tasks (MMSU 50.2→56.2, LlamaQ 73.0→78.0) without degrading turn-taking or barge-in performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLAIR sits at the intersection of two largely separate research threads: full-duplex spoken dialogue models (Moshi, SALMONN-omni, Freeze-Omni) that currently waste the listening phase by predicting repeated silence tokens, and latent/continuous reasoning in text LLMs (Coconut, CODI, looped transformers, SoftCoT) that operate post-hoc or in non-streaming settings. The paper departs from both: it rejects explicit chain-of-thought during listening (causality and latency issues) and instead formulates the listening-phase computation as a latent-variable problem trained via ELBO with a non-causal Global-aware Expert. This is the first work to transplant latent reasoning into the speech LLM domain, and the ELBO-based SFT strategy (teacher forcing with vocabulary-weighted embeddings as supervision) is a new training recipe not present in either the full-duplex or latent-reasoning literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FLAIR offers a clean, zero-latency mechanism for full-duplex agents to 'think while listening' without requiring explicit reasoning annotations or additional inference compute, which is practically important for real-time conversational systems. The t-SNE visualization suggesting latent embeddings form a semantic bridge between input audio and target text is suggestive but not yet mechanistically validated. Open questions include whether the ELBO-trained latent reasoning generalizes beyond 7B-scale models, whether RL post-training (mentioned but not tested) yields further gains, and whether the approach transfers to other modalities or to half-duplex settings where the listening-phase motivation is weaker. The modest absolute gains (e.g., +6 on MMSU, +5 on LlamaQ) and reliance on 620K hours of synthetic data temper the practical impact.
