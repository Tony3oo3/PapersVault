## Problem

Existing agentic test-time scaling methods entangle exploration and action selection within a single policy, applying undifferentiated exploration that either wastes steps on well-understood states or fails to gather critical information in unfamiliar ones. The paper addresses how to teach LLM agents to adaptively explore only when uncertainty is high, distinguishing information-seeking from goal-directed execution without relying on ad-hoc prompting or static heuristics.

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives a concatenated input [task description; current state (UI screenshot or text observation); preceding exploration strategy e_{t-1}; accumulative memory m_{t-1}] and produces a structured output [e_t; m_t; a_t] enclosed in dedicated <explore>, <memory>, and <action> tags. The final action a_t is a token sequence parsed into an executable command (e.g., click at coordinate, text input, scroll), while e_t specifies candidate probe actions and m_t distills previously visited states into a persistent summary that persists across steps. The structured format enables reliable credit assignment for exploration-specific rewards that would be impossible with unstructured outputs.

## Method

EAPO introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, a variational reward model q_φ(e,m|s) is trained via REINFORCE to approximate the Bayesian posterior p(e,m|s, success), yielding an exploratory reward R_explore = max{q_φ(e_{t-1}, m_{t-1}|s_t), γ²·q_φ(e_{t-1}, [m_{t-1}, s_{t+1}]|s_t)} that credits actions by their information gain while the γ² discount prevents redundant exploration once sufficient context is available. Second, a two-stage training pipeline first uses SFT on expert rollback transitions to teach the agent reversible exploration (returning to prior states), then applies exploration-aware GRPO where transitions are clustered by joint (environment state, visitation depth κ) rather than state alone, ensuring exploratory and execution actions are compared only within similar informational stages. Third, the total reward combines the variational exploratory reward, a binary format reward for structured output compliance, and the episodic task success signal, with the visitation-depth grouping preventing the systematic underestimation of exploratory actions that occurs when they are mixed with final execution actions in standard GRPO groups.

## Results

EAPO-8B achieves 76.02% success rate on ALFWorld, 65.58% on WebShop, 82.05% on AndroidWorld, and 64.29% on OSWorld, outperforming the strongest training-method baseline (LAMER) by 14.76, 17.84, 21.07, and 8.69 points respectively. Notably, the 2B-scale EAPO model surpasses substantially larger closed-source models (e.g., Claude-4-Sonnet) on ALFWorld (58.50 vs. 56.09), WebShop (53.28 vs. 50.97), and AndroidWorld (76.36 vs. 71.60). Across all four environments and three model sizes, EAPO consistently outperforms all six baseline training methods (GRPO, DAPO, GiGPO, LAMER, Min-p, OverRIDE) by 20%–60% in relative improvement, with the largest gains in long-horizon GUI control tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAPO-8B decisively outperforms all four RL training baselines across every environment: over GRPO by 29.89/27.01/26.57/23.93 points, over DAPO by 24.16/24.68/20.29/16.39, over GiGPO by 19.26/23.34/23.18/13.41, and over LAMER by 14.76/17.84/21.07/8.69 on ALFWorld/WebShop/AndroidWorld/OSWorld respectively. Against the two decoding-based baselines, EAPO-8B leads Min-p by 47.26/35.24/27.34/23.39 and OverRIDE by 44.61/32.37/21.34/23.80 points, confirming that learned exploration-aware policies substantially surpass heuristic sampling strategies. Among closed-source models, EAPO-8B exceeds Claude-4-Sonnet (the strongest closed-source baseline) on all four benchmarks (76.02 vs. 56.09, 65.58 vs. 50.97, 82.05 vs. 71.60, 64.29 vs. 62.88), while the 2B variant already surpasses it on three of four (losing only on OSWorld: 50.34 vs. 62.88). EAPO-4B similarly beats Claude-4-Sonnet on ALFWorld, WebShop, and AndroidWorld but trails on OSWorld (57.89 vs. 62.88), indicating that the largest gains in complex desktop environments require the 8B scale. In the generalization experiment (Table 6), EAPO models trained solely on AndroidWorld transfer to OSWorld with only modest degradation (e.g., EAPO-8B: 60.64 overall on OSWorld vs. 64.29 when trained directly), outperforming all open-source baselines and approaching Claude-4-Sonnet (62.88) without any OSWorld-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAPO extends GRPO's group-based advantage estimation by introducing visitation-depth clustering that separates exploratory transitions from execution transitions, preventing the systematic underestimation of information-gathering actions. The exploratory reward is derived from a variational inference framework (building on the ELBO of Kingma & Welling, 2013), treating exploration strategy and memory as latent variables whose posterior under task success is approximated by a learnable proxy. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior test-time scaling methods (ToT, LAMER) that apply exploration uniformly across all states, instead learning a policy that adaptively allocates exploration only when uncertainty is high, and from entropy-based exploration signals that conflate model uncertainty with actual information gain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled mechanism for agents to learn when exploration is informative versus when direct goal-pursuit suffices, addressing a practical bottleneck in long-horizon GUI and embodied tasks where indiscriminate exploration wastes interaction budget. The cross-domain generalization result (AndroidWorld-trained model transferring to OSWorld with modest degradation) suggests the learned exploration strategy captures domain-invariant information-seeking behavior rather than environment-specific heuristics. A key limitation is that the structured exploration/memory format is manually specified, potentially restricting expressiveness for tasks requiring more flexible information acquisition. Open questions include whether the variational reward model scales to environments with continuous or partially observable state spaces, and whether the visitation-depth grouping generalizes beyond discrete, revisitable states.
