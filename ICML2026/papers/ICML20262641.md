## Problem

Existing multi-modal point cloud completion methods rely on deterministic hard projection to map sparse 3D points onto 2D image planes, producing an extremely sparse support that the authors term Cross-Modal Entropy Collapse. This sparsity severs gradient flow between geometric and visual streams, preventing the model from learning an effective cross-modal connection and causing baselines to degenerate into unimodal template retrievers. The paper also notes that the theoretical mechanisms explaining why and how multi-modality improves completion remain under-explored in the literature.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse partial point cloud (N points in R³) and a corresponding RGB image (H×W×3) as input, and produces a completed 3D point cloud via hierarchical upsampling (P₀ → P₁ → P₂). The visual branch uses a pre-trained TinyViT-5M encoder (ImageNet-22k→1k weights) to extract dense feature maps, while the geometric branch processes the raw point set. The Gaussian splatting kernel bandwidth is set to 4 pixels, and the output is generated as a residual displacement field regressed by a convolution-based decoding head at each upsampling stage.

## Method

SplAttN replaces deterministic hard projection with Differentiable Gaussian Splatting, reformulating the 3D-to-2D mapping as continuous density estimation that strictly expands the valid information support from zero-measure Dirac deltas to positive-measure Gaussian neighborhoods. The GS-Bridge module first extracts hybrid geometric tokens via EdgeConv (approximating the Laplace-Beltrami operator for local curvature) followed by a Transformer encoder (for global topological reasoning), then uses these tokens as Queries in a cross-attention mechanism to actively retrieve features from the splatted visual density field, where each query location aggregates contributions weighted by a Gaussian spatial kernel times an inverse-depth soft Z-buffer. A Global-Local Decoder then hierarchically densifies a predicted sparse skeleton, integrating Structure Self-Attention for geometric consistency and Cross-Attention to inject local EdgeConv features for fine-grained refinement. Training uses a Weighted Arc-CD loss (hyperbolic arccosh transformation of Chamfer Distance) applied uniformly across all three decoding stages, optimized with AdamW and one-cycle cosine annealing on four RTX 4090 GPUs.

## Results

On the PCN dataset, SplAttN achieves a state-of-the-art average Chamfer Distance of 6.36 (×10⁻³), DCD of 0.523, and F1-Score of 0.854, outperforming the closest multi-modal baseline GeoFormer (CD 6.42, F1 0.853). On ShapeNet-55, it reaches CD-Avg 0.77 and F1 0.520, and on ShapeNet-34/21 it achieves the best F1 on both seen (0.533) and unseen (0.481) categories. A counter-factual evaluation on KITTI shows SplAttN's Semantic Consistency Score drops by 26.1% when visual input is removed, versus only +0.4% for SVDFormer, confirming genuine cross-modal dependency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplAttN outperforms all 14 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On PCN, it edges out the strongest multi-modal competitor GeoFormer by 0.06 CD (6.36 vs. 6.42) and matches its F1 (0.854 vs. 0.853), while surpassing the single-modal leader AdaPoinTr by 0.17 CD. On ShapeNet-55, SplAttN leads SVDFormer by 0.05 CD (0.77 vs. 0.82) and by a substantial 0.076 F1 (0.520 vs. 0.444), with particularly large gains on tail categories like Birdhouse (1.29 vs. 1.36) and Bag (0.60 vs. 0.74). On ShapeNet-34/21, it achieves the best unseen-category CD (1.22) and F1 (0.481), beating AdaPoinTr (1.23 / 0.416) and SVDFormer (1.28 / 0.427). The KITTI counter-factual evaluation is the most distinctive comparison: SVDFormer's SCS barely changes (+0.4%) when images are removed, indicating it degenerates into a unimodal template retriever, while SplAttN's SCS drops sharply (−26.1%), confirming that its performance genuinely depends on the visual modality rather than memorized 3D priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the multi-modal point cloud completion line initiated by SVDFormer and GeoFormer, which project 3D points onto 2D image planes to query visual features via hard (deterministic) projection. The key conceptual departure is identifying 'Cross-Modal Entropy Collapse'—the zero-measure support induced by Dirac-delta projection—as the mechanism that severs gradient flow and prevents effective cross-modal learning, then replacing it with Differentiable Gaussian Splatting repurposed from 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) for feature density estimation. The theoretical grounding in Multimodal Learning Theory (Lu, 2023) frames the contribution as restoring the 'Connection' component required for provable multi-modal generalization gains, positioning the work at the intersection of information-theoretic analysis and practical architecture design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The counter-factual KITTI evaluation revealing that SVDFormer and GeoFormer are essentially insensitive to visual input removal (degenerating into unimodal template retrievers) is a useful diagnostic that questions the actual multi-modal utility of prior methods. However, the performance gains over strong baselines are modest (6.36 vs. 6.42 CD on PCN; 0.77 vs. 0.82 on ShapeNet-55), and the zero-shot KITTI stress test, while conceptually compelling, relies on a single novel metric (SCS) and one dataset, limiting generalizability of the claim. Open questions include whether the entropy-collapse diagnosis holds under different visual backbones or higher-resolution inputs, whether the theoretical support-expansion guarantee translates to measurable gains in more complex multi-object scenes, and whether the approach can be extended to unsupervised or domain-adaptive settings as the authors themselves note.
