## Problem

Token-level credit assignment in RL for LLMs remains a key obstacle: standard recipes like GRPO assign uniform reward to all tokens, failing to distinguish decisive reasoning steps from fluent filler. Recent point-wise heuristics (entropy, attention scores, gradients) capture local salience but ignore the global multi-hop structure of how information actually propagates from prompt to answer, leading to systematic under-crediting of early critical premises and over-crediting of late-stage tokens near the answer.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a generated token sequence (prompt + response), attention maps extracted via a single additional forward pass from middle transformer layers (L/3 to 2L/3, averaged across heads), and an explicitly defined answer region (e.g., the \boxed{} span). It produces a per-token flow throughput score, a binary mask selecting the Top-40% high-flow tokens, and a reweighted advantage vector where high-flow tokens receive a multiplicative factor γ_flow=1.5 in the GRPO surrogate while all others retain unit weight. The entire credit-assignment pipeline adds only 2.2%–4.5% overhead to the training step.

## Method

FlowTracer models reasoning as linear influence propagation over an attention-induced DAG where edge capacities are aggregated attention weights. It applies a Doob-h-like reweighting W'_{ik} = W_{ik}·h(k)/h(i), where h(i) is the total influence-weighted path sum from node i to a virtual answer sink, which provably enforces local flow conservation (Theorem 3.1) and automatically suppresses dead-end branches that never reach the answer. Unit flow is then injected from a super-source over prompt tokens and propagated forward through the reweighted graph, yielding per-token throughput τ(k) that identifies high-impact routing hubs (structural delimiters, recurring variables, aggregation checkpoints) mediating long-range prompt-to-answer dependencies. In the RL loop, the Top-40% highest-throughput tokens are selected and their GRPO surrogate terms are scaled by γ_flow=1.5, concentrating policy updates on tokens that genuinely route information toward the final answer while suppressing gradients on low-impact filler. The approach is validated by causal intervention experiments showing that perturbing high-flow tokens causes 45.9% answer changes versus 14.9% for low-flow tokens, and by consistent gains across math, puzzle, and QA benchmarks on Qwen3 and Llama backbones.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B-Base with 1K context, FlowTracer achieves 43.4% average accuracy across five math benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, MATH500, OlympiadBench), surpassing the GRPO baseline (39.4%) by +4.0% and the strongest point-wise heuristic (Attention, 41.3%) by +2.1%. The advantage widens in the 8K long-context setting, where FlowTracer reaches 52.5% vs. GRPO's 50.3% (+2.1%), with a notable +5.8% gain on AIME25 for Qwen3-4B. Beyond math, FlowTracer achieves 63.2% on Countdown (+10.6 over GRPO) and 50.2% on CrossThinkQA (+2.2), and generalizes to Llama-3.1-8B (+1.4 avg) and Llama-3.2-3B (+1.1 avg).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowTracer outperforms all nine baselines on the primary Qwen3-8B/1K math benchmark. Against the standard GRPO baseline, it gains +4.0% average (43.4 vs. 39.4), with the largest per-benchmark margin on AIME25 (+4.5) and AMC23 (+6.5). Among point-wise heuristics, the closest competitor is the Attention-based method (Li et al., 2025d) at 41.3%, which FlowTracer surpasses by +2.1%; High-entropy (Wang et al., 2025b) and Gradient (Green et al., 2025) trail further at 40.5% and 40.0% respectively. Against stronger process-supervision and reweighting baselines in Table 10, FlowTracer leads CAPO (Xie et al., 2025) by +2.0% (43.4 vs. 41.4), ThinkPRM-1.5B (Khalifa et al., 2025) by +2.4%, AsyPPO (Liu et al., 2025) by +3.4%, and Reweight+Lopti (Yang et al., 2025b) by +2.3%. The method also generalizes across architectures: on Llama-3.1-8B it beats GRPO by +1.4% average and on Llama-3.2-3B by +1.1%, confirming that the gains are not architecture-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO framework (Xin et al., 2025) by replacing its uniform token-level credit with a flow-based importance signal, and departs from prior attention-based credit heuristics (entropy, max-attention, gradient magnitude) by modeling token influence as a global, flow-conserving process on an attention-induced DAG rather than scoring tokens in isolation. The Doob-h-like reweighting, borrowed from probability theory, is repurposed here to enforce answer-targeted flow conservation, filtering out dead-end attention branches that would otherwise dilute credit. Conceptually, the work bridges mechanistic interpretability (attention as an interaction graph) and RL optimization (token-level reward shaping), positioning itself between the interpretability literature on reasoning circuits and the RLVR training literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Token-level credit assignment is a recognized bottleneck in RL for LLMs, and FlowTracer offers a lightweight (2–4.5% overhead), model-internal solution that requires no external process reward model, making it a practical plug-in for existing RLVR pipelines. The causal intervention evidence (high-flow token perturbation causing 3× more answer changes than low-flow) provides a mechanistic grounding that goes beyond correlation. Key limitations include the assumption of a localized answer region (limiting applicability to open-ended generation or agentic trajectories), reliance on outcome-only rewards that cannot distinguish locally useful steps within wrong solutions, and potential degradation on very long contexts where attention graphs become noisier. Open questions include whether the flow backbone generalizes to multi-turn or tool-augmented reasoning and whether adaptive layer/region selection can further improve signal quality.
