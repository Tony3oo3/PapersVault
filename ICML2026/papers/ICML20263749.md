## Problem

Existing analytic treatments of memory in recurrent networks either assume homogeneous single-population connectivity, rely on asymptotic stability criteria (eigenvalues, Lyapunov exponents) that cannot resolve intermediate-time information dynamics, or fail to characterize how information is routed across interacting subpopulations. This leaves no principled way to connect block-structured connectivity statistics to time-resolved, population-specific memory retention or to derive initialization rules that place recurrent networks in a regime preserving sensitivity to past inputs.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes a block-gain matrix G (specifying inter-subpopulation connection variances), noise level σ, and an input configuration vector w, and produces population-wise Fisher information trajectories over time plus a Fisher-optimal recurrent weight matrix. In experiments, the RNN receives sequential one-hot tokens (copy task, 8 categories) or flattened pixel sequences (784-d MNIST, 32×32 CIFAR-10, 7500-d IndoorCVPR_09) one element per timestep, and outputs either a copied token sequence or a class label from a single-layer MLP readout. Notably, the subpopulation partition is used only to parameterize initialization, not as an architectural constraint, and in the sequential MNIST/CIFAR-10/IndoorCVPR experiments the recurrent weights are frozen to isolate the effect of initialization from recurrent weight learning.

## Method

The paper derives a Fisher information diffusion operator A = G · diag(⟨(S′)²⟩ₙ) via multi-population dynamic mean-field theory, where G encodes block-structured connectivity and the diagonal sensitivity block captures activity-dependent nonlinear gain. Repeated application of A yields closed-form population-wise Fisher memory curves, and the operator's spectral radius and eigenvector alignment with the input configuration determine long-term retention. The key theoretical result is that criticality (ρ(A)=1) is necessary but not sufficient: optimal memory additionally requires the stable diffusion direction to align with the input-output structure, a constraint that is especially binding in non-normal networks. Analytic expressions for ⟨(S′)²⟩ are obtained by approximating tanh²(x) with a Gaussian-tractable surrogate, reducing the self-consistent variance equations to solvable forms in terms of trace, determinant, and in-degree of the connectivity matrix. From these conditions, Fisher-optimal initializations are constructed by sampling recurrent weights from zero-mean Gaussians with variances set by the optimized block gains, and for longer chains the optimal structure exhibits strong feedforward pathways with sparse, strategically broken feedback loops.

## Results

The paper's primary empirical contribution is demonstrating that Fisher-information-optimized initialization of recurrent weights yields faster convergence and higher final accuracy than standard schemes (Xavier, Kaiming, orthogonal, unitary) across sequential memory benchmarks. On the copy task (100-neuron RNN, tanh nonlinearity), Fisher-optimal initialization consistently achieves lower cross-entropy loss and higher accuracy across delay lengths T_delay = 40, 50, 60 and four random seeds. On Sequential MNIST with fixed recurrent weights (only readout trained), Fisher-optimal initialization again converges faster and reaches higher classification accuracy. The analytic Fisher diffusion operator matches direct Monte-Carlo simulations with mean-squared error becoming negligible for N ≥ 1000, and geometry-preservation dynamics are dataset-independent (Pearson correlations 0.993, 0.992, 0.980 between IndoorCVPR_09 and CIFAR-10 trajectories).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Fisher-information-optimized initialization against four standard recurrent network initialization schemes: Xavier, Kaiming, orthogonal (Le et al., 2015; Henaff et al., 2017), and unitary (Arjovsky et al., 2016; Wisdom et al., 2016). Across all evaluated tasks—copy task (delays 40/50/60), Sequential MNIST, CIFAR-10, and IndoorCVPR_09—Fisher-optimal initialization consistently outperforms all four baselines on both convergence speed and final accuracy. The advantage is most pronounced in the fixed-recurrent-weights Sequential MNIST setting, where the recurrent core is never trained and performance is entirely determined by the initialization-induced dynamics, isolating the information-propagation benefit. Orthogonal and unitary initializations, which enforce spectral radius exactly 1, perform better than Xavier and Kaiming but still fall short of Fisher-optimal initialization, supporting the paper's central claim that criticality (ρ(A)≈1) is necessary but not sufficient—eigenvector alignment with the input-output structure provides the additional gain. No baseline outperforms the Fisher-optimal scheme on any reported metric or dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Fisher information analysis of memory in recurrent networks (Ganguli et al., 2008; Ganguli & Sompolinsky, 2010) from homogeneous single-population settings to block-structured multi-population networks, combining multi-population dynamical mean-field theory (Kadmon & Sompolinsky, 2015; KuSmierz et al., 2025) with a diffusion-operator formulation. It departs from prior Jacobian-spectral initialization work (Le et al., 2015; Henaff et al., 2017; Wisdom et al., 2016) by deriving initialization rules directly from population-level connectivity statistics rather than global spectral criteria, and from non-normal network analyses (Christodoulou et al., 2022; Orhan & Pitkow, 2020) by making the eigenvector-alignment requirement explicit and constructive. The connection to compressed sensing / RIP (Foucart & Rauhut, 2013) provides a geometric interpretation that unifies the Fisher criterion with local isometry preservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Fisher diffusion operator gives a closed-form, population-resolved, time-resolved account of memory that goes beyond asymptotic stability criteria, and the resulting initialization rules are directly implementable with only a few block-level parameters. Key limitations include the restriction to single-state tanh RNNs (LSTM/GRU results are purely empirical), the tanh² exponential approximation and small-μ linearization that constrain the theory to near-critical regimes, and the fact that baselines are standard initializations rather than modern SSM or gated architectures. Open questions include whether the framework extends analytically to gated multi-state architectures, how it scales to very deep chains or heterogeneous subpopulation sizes, and whether the dataset-independence of Fisher-optimal dynamics (shown for CIFAR-10 and IndoorCVPR) holds for truly out-of-distribution inputs.
