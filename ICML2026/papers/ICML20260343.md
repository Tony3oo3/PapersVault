## Problem

Early exit neural networks adapt computation to input complexity and have proven effective in supervised learning, but remain largely unexplored in deep reinforcement learning. Naively porting them to DRL fails because there is no fixed ground-truth supervision, the agent's state distribution shifts as its policy evolves, and standard confidence-based exit criteria (entropy, max probability, patience) are ill-suited when high policy entropy is needed for exploration. The paper asks how to integrate early exits into off-policy actor-critic methods with a principled, tunable trade-off between return and inference cost.

## Input / Output

The actor receives a state vector from a MuJoCo continuous-control environment and outputs a continuous action sampled from a mixture distribution over K exit heads. The mixture weights are determined by a sequence of Bernoulli gates (one per intermediate exit), so the actual computation depth varies per input. A scalar budget parameter b ∈ [0,1] controls the expected FLOP cost: as b→0 the gate favors the earliest (cheapest) exit, while b→1 allows the full-depth backbone exit. The critic is a multi-head network (shared trunk, K heads) that estimates Q-values per exit without itself using early exits.

## Method

BEXA represents the actor as an early exit neural network with K sub-policies and learned Bernoulli gates, then formulates the exit-selection problem as a constrained linear program: maximize the expected Q-value over exit choices subject to a hard FLOP budget and a simplex constraint on the exit probabilities. The LP solution yields optimal gate probabilities p_i* that are used as a supervisory signal via binary cross-entropy, amortizing the optimization so no online LP solve is needed at inference. All exits are trained jointly under the standard actor-critic objective (SAC or TD3) plus the gate loss, with a shared-trunk multi-head critic for stability. The gating function shares hidden features with the actor to minimize overhead, and the entire framework is agnostic to the underlying off-policy algorithm, requiring only that an actor objective J_actor be available.

## Results

BEXA matches or exceeds the performance of vanilla SAC and TD3 across five MuJoCo continuous control tasks (Ant, Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Humanoid), with notable return gains on Ant, Hopper, and Humanoid. The key efficiency gain is substantial: BEXA achieves actor inference speedups of up to +584% in FLOPs and +95% in wall-clock time during environment sampling, while maintaining comparable or better returns. On more complex tasks like Humanoid, the speedup diminishes as full network capacity is needed, though a more aggressive budget constraint can recover speed at the cost of some performance. In the ablation setting (Table 1), BEXA achieves 1.3× FLOP speedup for SAC and 1.35× for TD3 with mean normalized returns of 62.8±17 and 38.7±12 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEXA is compared against two baselines: vanilla SAC and vanilla TD3, each evaluated on five MuJoCo tasks with 15 seeds. BEXA-SAC matches or exceeds SAC on all tasks, with the most notable return improvements on Ant, Hopper, and Humanoid, while simultaneously reducing actor FLOPs by up to 584% and wall-clock inference time by up to 95%. BEXA-TD3 follows a similar pattern, matching or slightly exceeding TD3 returns while providing comparable speedups. The paper emphasizes that the primary contribution is not large return gains but rather maintaining baseline performance while significantly reducing compute, making BEXA a drop-in efficiency improvement. The budget hyperparameter provides direct, intuitive control over the speed-performance trade-off without requiring additional tuning budget relative to the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

BEXA extends early exit neural networks (ENNs), well-studied in supervised CV and NLP, into the deep reinforcement learning setting by integrating them into off-policy actor-critic methods (SAC, TD3). The closest prior work, RAPID-RL (Kosta et al., 2022), applies early exits to DQN with a fixed-fraction Q-value threshold for exit selection, but targets discrete action spaces and incurs training overhead by evaluating all exits. BEXA departs from both the heuristic-based gating common in supervised ENNs (entropy, confidence, patience) and RAPID-RL's threshold approach by formulating exit selection as a budget-constrained linear program over Q-value estimates, yielding a principled, interpretable trade-off knob absent in prior ENN-for-DRL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BEXA provides a general, drop-in framework for any off-policy actor-critic method that reduces actor inference FLOPs by up to 584% with minimal return loss, and the single budget parameter gives practitioners direct, intuitive control over the speed-performance trade-off. Limitations include increased training time (all exits must be optimized simultaneously) and difficulty parallelizing dynamic branching on GPUs, which the authors note is a broader ENN community challenge. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large architectures (ResNets, Transformers) where gating overhead becomes negligible, and whether it composes well with model-based RL or other compression techniques like quantization and pruning.
