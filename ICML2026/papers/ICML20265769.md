## Problem

Existing LLM serving frameworks produce different outputs for identical inputs when tensor parallel (TP) size changes, even under greedy decoding, because floating-point non-associativity causes different accumulation orders across GPUs. While batch-invariant operations (BIO) resolved batch-size-related nondeterminism, TP-induced inconsistency remained unsolved. This gap is especially critical in RL pipelines where the training engine (FSDP, TP=1) and rollout engine (vLLM, TP>1) compute different token probabilities, implicitly converting on-policy updates into off-policy ones and destabilizing training.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard text prompts fed into an LLM (Qwen3, Mistral, Llama-3.1) and produces generated token sequences whose per-token probability distributions are bit-wise identical across all tested TP sizes (1/2/4/8) and batch sizes (8/16/32). The determinism guarantee extends across heterogeneous hardware (NVIDIA L40S and RTX PRO 6000) and across frameworks (vLLM for inference, FSDP for training), meaning the same prompt yields the exact same floating-point logits regardless of how the model is parallelized or which engine computes them.

## Method

TBIK (Tree-Based Invariant Kernels) enforces a fixed full binary-tree reduction topology that spans both intra-GPU and inter-GPU computation, making the global accumulation order independent of TP size. For intra-GPU MatMul, tiles along the K dimension are accumulated hierarchically through L = log₂(T_K/C) levels of intermediate scratch buffers, where each level performs deterministic pairwise carry-over operations rather than sequential accumulation. For inter-GPU reduction, a custom tree-based all-reduce (replacing NCCL's ring or chain-based reduction) follows the same binary-tree structure, so the partial results from each GPU merge in a fixed order. Combined with BIO (which fixes batch-size invariance), TBIK guarantees bit-wise identical outputs across all TP configurations. The kernels are implemented in Triton and integrated into both vLLM and FSDP, with a theoretical proof (via induction on the tree depth) establishing that the reduction sequence is invariant to the number of participating GPUs.

## Results

On AIME24 and AMC23, BIO+TBIK achieves a Count of Unique Outputs of exactly 1 (fully deterministic) across all 12 runtime configurations (4 TP sizes × 3 batch sizes) for all four models (Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-32B, Mistral-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), compared to 9.00–12.00 for vanilla BF16 and 6.75–7.97 for BIO alone. The Maximum Probability Divergence drops to strictly 0 with BIO+TBIK, versus 6.79–31.09 ×10⁻³ for BF16 and 6.75–27.54 ×10⁻³ for BIO. In RL training (GRPO on GSM8K with Qwen3-1.7B), TBIK achieves zero KL divergence between rollout and training engines and reaches a final Pass@1 of 0.73, compared to 0.68 for BIO and 0.60 for BF16. The end-to-end latency overhead of BIO+TBIK over vanilla BF16 ranges from 22% to 63%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three methods: vanilla BF16 inference, Batch-Invariant Operations (BIO) from He & Lab (2025), and the proposed BIO+TBIK. BIO alone reduces the Count of Unique Outputs from ~12 to ~7–8 by eliminating batch-size-induced nondeterminism, but it fails to handle TP-size variations: outputs still diverge when TP > 2 or when different TP sizes are compared. BIO+TBIK achieves a perfect score of 1 (fully deterministic) across all models, datasets, and configurations, and a Maximum Probability Divergence of exactly 0, demonstrating bit-wise identical inference. In the RL training comparison on GSM8K, TBIK eliminates the KL divergence between vLLM (TP=4) and FSDP (TP=1) entirely, whereas BIO still leaves a noticeable gap. TBIK also yields the best final Pass@1 (0.73 vs. 0.68 for BIO and 0.60 for BF16) and faster convergence, surpassing 0.6 Pass@1 within the first 20 training steps. The trade-off is a 22–63% end-to-end latency overhead, with the Tree-Based MatMul kernel achieving ~63% of cuBLAS throughput in BF16.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Batch-Invariant Operations (BIO) framework from He & Lab (2025), which achieved batch-size invariance through fixed per-sample computation paths in FlexAttention, RMSNorm, and MatMul kernels. The key departure is identifying tensor parallelism as the 'last missing piece' for full determinism: BIO's per-sample independence guarantees are insufficient when row-parallel layers require cross-GPU all-reduce whose order changes with TP size. TBIK completes the picture by co-designing intra-GPU tree-structured MatMul accumulation with a matching inter-GPU tree all-reduce, ensuring the global floating-point operation sequence is invariant regardless of how many GPUs participate. The work also positions itself against algorithm-level RL fixes (FP16 dtype, importance sampling, GSPO) that only partially mitigate the training-inference probability gap rather than eliminating it at the numerical level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving bit-wise identical outputs across TP configurations and between vLLM and FSDP, TBIK enables true on-policy RL at scale for the first time, with the RL experiments showing zero KL divergence and a Pass@1 improvement from 0.60 (BF16) to 0.73 (TBIK) on GSM8K. The 22–63% end-to-end latency overhead is a significant practical limitation that will constrain adoption in latency-sensitive serving, though the paper notes it is acceptable for evaluation and training pipelines where reproducibility is paramount. Open questions include extending the guarantees to quantized data types (INT8, FP4), optimizing the tree MatMul kernel to close the 63%-of-cuBLAS throughput gap, and whether the approach generalizes to non-power-of-two TP sizes or heterogeneous GPU topologies.
