## Problem

Existing agent benchmarks underrepresent the challenges of real-world long-horizon task completion: they either focus on single-turn tasks with shallow reasoning chains (<3 tool calls), present instructions in segmented fragments without modeling prolonged interaction behaviors (revisions, rollbacks, version control), or lack systematic enforcement of global constraints across multi-turn dialogues. No prior benchmark simultaneously stresses complex instruction-following under global constraints, long-horizon multi-tool planning, and diverse dynamic user behaviors such as feasibility transitions, ambiguous intent shifts, and plan merging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264073/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a multi-turn dialogue (up to 15 user turns) in which a user simulator progressively reveals travel constraints from 40+ requirement categories (80+ natural-language formulations), with behavioral diversity including instruction appending, modification, deletion/rollback, intent redirection, and plan merging. The agent must produce a complete JSON trip_plan in each response containing daily schedules with intercity transportation, local transportation, hotel check-ins, attractions, and restaurants—each with real tool-returned IDs, products, and time slots—across 18 tools spanning flights, trains, hotels, restaurants, attractions, and route estimation. Evaluation scores 12 general constraints (4 feasibility + 8 planning soundness) plus user-specific constraints under strict (zero violations) and loose (≤2 soundness + ≤1 user violations) modes, with the hardest cases exceeding 150 tool calls and 200k tokens of context.

## Method

TRIP-Bench is constructed via a modular pipeline: rubric-to-constraint generation with paired generator/validator functions, progressive modification-chain synthesis (up to 3 steps of increasing restrictiveness with redundancy trimming), and complexity-conditioned task curation into easy/mid/hard tiers plus four hard interaction subsets (LIT, FIT, AIS, PMR). For training, the paper proposes GTPO (Group Relative Turn-level Policy Optimization), an online multi-turn RL method built on GRPO that introduces three key mechanisms: (1) turn-wise reward differencing, which subtracts the previous turn's reward (or the group max if the previous turn was infeasible) to emphasize incremental improvement over inherited structure; (2) global instruction normalization, which calibrates constraint satisfaction across the full dialogue history to prevent reward imbalance; and (3) turn-level reward normalization via z-score across the group at each turn, with advantages kept turn-local (no cross-turn propagation). The training pipeline first performs SFT cold-start on ~3k high-quality trajectories (repaired from 120k samples via 3 rounds of error feedback), then applies GTPO on 7,040 RL samples, yielding Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct that outperforms Gemini-3-Pro on TRIP-Bench.

## Results

On TRIP-Bench, even the strongest model (GPT-5.2 with thinking) achieves only 45.0% under the loose metric and 18.5% under the strict metric overall. Performance drops sharply on hard subsets: under strict evaluation, no model exceeds 14% on Hard LIT, and Hard FIT remains unsolved (0%) across all models. GTPO-trained Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct reaches 49% loose / 21% strict on Easy and 40% loose / 5% strict on Mid, surpassing Gemini-3-Pro under the same evaluation protocol.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5.2 (with thinking) leads all models on TRIP-Bench, achieving 45.0% loose and 18.5% strict overall, with the largest margin on Easy-strict (49.0 vs. 31.0 for DeepSeek-V3.2). DeepSeek-V3.2 (with thinking) is the strongest open-source model at 40.0% loose / 10.5% strict, outperforming GLM-4.7 (20.3 / 4.0) and Gemini-3-Pro (20.0 / 2.8) by roughly 20 and 20 percentage points respectively under the loose metric. Kimi-K2-Thinking scores only 10.8% loose / 2.3% strict overall and fails completely on all Hard subsets, while Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking scores 0.0 across all splits, indicating a complete inability to handle the multi-turn tool-use setting. The GTPO-trained Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct (evaluated on Easy/Mid only) reaches 49% loose / 21% strict on Easy and 40% loose / 5% strict on Mid, surpassing Gemini-3-Pro (44 / 12 on Easy; 9 / 0 on Mid) and demonstrating that targeted multi-turn RL can close the gap with frontier closed-source models on constrained subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRIP-Bench extends TripTailor (Wang et al., 2025) by enriching POI attributes, adding 18 unified tools, and introducing a multi-turn user-simulation framework with nine behavioral categories, positioning itself against existing agent benchmarks (τ-Bench, τ²-Bench, VitaBench, COMPASS) that the authors argue lack simultaneous coverage of long-horizon planning (150+ tool calls, 200k+ tokens), complex global constraints, and diverse dynamic user behaviors. For training, GTPO builds on GRPO/PPO-style group policy optimization but introduces three multi-turn-specific components—turn-level reward normalization, global instruction normalization, and turn-level reward differencing—that depart from prior multi-turn RL work (REFUEL, MUA-RL, UserRL) by targeting long-chain tool invocation rather than direct user-LLM dialogue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark capability gap: even GPT-5.2 reaches only 45% on the easy split and below 10% on hard subsets, and multi-turn interactions paradoxically degrade global-constraint satisfaction by 10–20+ points relative to single-turn, challenging the assumption that more interaction improves planning. GTPO provides a practical online RL recipe that lifts a 32B model above Gemini-3-Pro on easy/mid splits, though the hard-split evaluation is limited by context-length constraints. Open questions include whether the findings generalize beyond travel planning, whether the user simulator's 98% reliability is sufficient for training signal, and whether the extreme difficulty (only ~500/120k fully correct SFT trajectories) reflects a genuine capability gap or an overly strict evaluation protocol.
