## Problem

Generalized convex functions (GCFs) arise naturally in optimal transport and mechanism design, yet lack principled differentiable parameterizations with universal approximation guarantees. Existing methods for these bilevel or min-max problems either ignore the GCF structure or rely on ad-hoc neural parameterizations without theoretical backing, making them difficult to solve with standard first-order optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263419/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a user-specified surplus/cost kernel Φ(x,y) defined on compact sets X×Y, along with problem-specific distributions (source/target marginals for OT, or type distribution and production cost for auctions). It outputs a finitely Y-convex function parameterized by n trainable support points {y^i}⊆Y and intercepts {p_i}∈R^n, from which downstream objects are recovered analytically: transport maps via the twist formula T(x)=(∇_xΦ(x,·))^{-1}(∇f(x)), allocation rules via argmax over support points, and payments via p(x)=Φ(x,a(x))−v(x). The parameter space Y^n×R^n is convex when Y is convex, enabling unconstrained first-order optimization.

## Method

The core contribution is a differentiable layer that parameterizes GCFs as finite maxima of kernel sections: x↦max_i{Φ(x,y^i)−p_i}, generalizing maxout layers by replacing affine branches with arbitrary kernel sections. The paper proves that finitely Y-convex functions are dense in all Y-convex functions (Theorem 4.3), and under an equi-semiconvexity condition on the kernel sections x↦Φ(x,y), their gradients are also dense (Theorem 4.10), resolving the classical issue that uniform function convergence does not imply gradient convergence. A log-sum-exp smoothing (Theorem 4.11) yields C^∞ approximations while preserving gradient density. In practice, the bilevel formulations of optimal transport and multi-good auction design reduce to single-level first-order optimization over the convex parameter space, with a softmax-to-hardmax temperature schedule during training to ease the nonconvex landscape.

## Results

In the auction design Setting A (inner-product surplus, uniform types), the learned finitely Y-convex mechanism closely matches the Straight-Jacket Auction (SJA) revenue where exact benchmarks exist: at n=12 goods, the learned mechanism achieves 0.353 mean profit per good versus SJA's 0.361, while separate posted pricing yields only 0.250. For larger n (50–250 goods) where no SJA benchmark is available, the learned mechanism's profit per good rises from 0.412 to 0.450, well above the 0.250 separate-pricing floor and below the 0.5 theoretical ceiling. In Setting B (nonlinear hinge surplus, log-normal types), profit per good increases from 1.022 (n=1) to 1.253 (n=20), confirming bundling benefits. For optimal transport, the learned maps correctly minimize displacement under quadratic cost and maximize displacement under negative quadratic cost, with well-matched marginals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines in Setting A: the analytical Separate Posted Pricing benchmark (profit per good = 0.250 for all n) and the Straight-Jacket Auction (SJA) whose exact revenues are taken from Joswig et al. (2022). The learned finitely Y-convex mechanism substantially outperforms separate posted pricing at every n≥2 (e.g., 0.353 vs. 0.250 at n=12), confirming that bundling is captured. Against SJA, the learned mechanism matches within 0.001–0.008 profit per good across n=1–12 (e.g., 0.303 vs. 0.305 at n=4; 0.353 vs. 0.361 at n=12), which is a strong quantitative validation given that SJA is proven optimal for n≤6 and conjectured optimal beyond. No direct numerical baselines exist for Setting B (nonlinear hinge surplus) or for the non-convex OT costs, so those experiments rely on sanity checks (monotone increase in profit with n, boundedness, correct transport-map geometry) rather than head-to-head comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied line of work on parameterizing convex functions (max-affine regression, LSE smoothing, input-convex networks) to the broader class of generalized convex functions defined via an arbitrary surplus kernel Φ, for which no prior universal-approximation parameterization existed. The gradient-approximation result builds directly on Chaudhari et al. (2024) for convex gradients, replacing the subgradient inequality with a Φ-subgradient inequality and introducing equi-semiconvexity as the condition that restores gradient stability under uniform convergence. On the application side, it connects to the bilevel/min-max formulations in optimal transport (Makkuva et al., 2020; Korotin et al., 2022) and learned auction design (Dutting et al., 2019; Curry et al., 2022), showing that the revelation principle and envelope theorem collapse these to single-level GCF optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that bilevel or min-max problems in optimal transport and mechanism design—typically much harder to solve numerically—become single-level first-order optimization over a convex parameter space, with universal approximation guarantees for both the function and its gradient. Open questions include whether deeper compositions of finitely Y-convex modules (analogous to deep maxout) yield better approximation rates, whether local optimization over the nonconvex joint (support, intercept) landscape finds global optima, and whether quantitative convergence rates in the number of support points can be established beyond the density guarantee. The framework is currently validated on controlled synthetic benchmarks; scaling to real-world OT or auction settings with complex, high-dimensional kernels remains to be demonstrated.
