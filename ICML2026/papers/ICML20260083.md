## Problem

Non-parametric optimality conditions are well-established for time-varying deterministic optimization, but no analogous framework exists for time-varying stochastic optimization (TV-SO) with stochastic decisions. Existing learning-based approaches such as pathwise differentiation methods (PDM) rely on parametric optimality conditions whose computational cost scales linearly with the neural network parameterization dimension, making them impractical for large models. This paper closes the gap by deriving non-parametric optimality conditions for TV-SO via Malliavin calculus and building a scalable algorithm around them.

## Input / Output

The method takes a time-varying stochastic objective f(x_t, ξ_t, t), a known Malliavin-differentiable process ξ_t (e.g., an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck or Wiener process) under law P, and an initial state x₀, and produces the optimal stochastic trajectory (x_t)_{t∈[0,T]} as a diffusion process whose drift and diffusion are parameterized by a neural network. A key structural property is that the optimality conditions and the energy-functional loss depend only on the decision dimension n and the Wiener dimension d, not on the neural network parameterization dimension φ, so the forward-pass SDEs have fixed dimension n+n² regardless of model size.

## Method

The paper derives non-parametric necessary optimality conditions (Theorem 4.1) by performing a stochastic variational analysis: perturbing the optimal Wiener-driven process along admissible directions in a Hilbert space and applying the Malliavin integration-by-parts formula, yielding the condition E{∇²_x f · D_s x_t + ∇_{xξ} f · D_s ξ_t} = 0 together with E{∇_x f} = 0. The Malliavin derivative D_s x_t is computed efficiently via the s-to-t stochastic flow Γ_{s,t}, which satisfies a linear SDE (Proposition 5.1) and can be simulated with the Euler-Maruyama scheme. The Stochastic Path Follower (SPF) algorithm parameterizes the drift a(x_t,t,θ) and diffusion B(x_t,t,θ) with neural networks, simulates both the state SDE and the flow SDE in a forward pass, evaluates the energy-functional loss (Eq. 11) that enforces both optimality conditions simultaneously over the full horizon [0,T], and updates θ with an Adam optimizer. Because the required SDEs have dimension n+n² independent of φ, SPF scales to large networks where PDM (requiring dimension n+nφ) becomes intractable.

## Results

On the least-squares recovery problem (n=10, d=5), SPF achieves the lowest optimality distance E‖x_t − x_t*‖² and objective suboptimality E|f(x_t) − f(x_t*)| among all methods, outperforming both the learning-based PDM and gradient-based PSG baselines. On the logistic regression problem, SPF achieves a better optimality distance than PDM and competitive objective suboptimality. In the scalability experiment (n=100, d=50), SPF's per-update runtime remains largely flat as the NN parameterization dimension φ grows, while PDM's runtime increases sharply; SPF also clearly outperforms PSG in this high-dimensional setting. For the law-following task, SPF achieves the lowest average KSD on most of the five target distributions compared to FDM and TruncSig.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PDM (Tzen & Raginsky, 2019; Liu et al., 2019), SPF achieves comparable or better optimality distance on the scalar and constrained TV-SO benchmarks (Exp1, Exp2) while running 9–41× faster per update step (<1 s vs. 9 s and 41 s, respectively). On the least-squares recovery task, SPF attains a lower optimality distance and objective suboptimality than PDM; on logistic regression, SPF achieves a better optimality distance than PDM with comparable suboptimality. The critical scalability advantage is that SPF's required SDE dimension is n + n² (independent of the NN parameterization dimension φ), whereas PDM's is n + nφ, making PDM infeasible at high dimensions (n=100, d=50) where SPF still runs efficiently. Against PSG (Cutler et al., 2023), SPF outperforms the gradient-based method on both least-squares recovery and logistic regression in terms of optimality distance and objective suboptimality, and the gap widens in the high-dimensional setting (n=100, d=50). For the law-following appendix experiment, SPF achieves the lowest average KSD on most of the five target distributions compared to FDM (Bonnier & Oberhauser, 2024) and TruncSig (Zhang et al., 2025), confirming the generality of the Malliavin-based framework beyond the primary TV-SO tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deterministic path-following framework for time-varying optimization (Simonetto et al., 2020; Fazlyab et al., 2018) to the stochastic setting by replacing the ODE-based optimality conditions with Malliavin-calculus-based non-parametric conditions. It departs from the parametric neural-SDE literature (Tzen & Raginsky, 2019; Li et al., 2020; Kidger et al., 2021) by formulating optimality conditions that are independent of any specific network parameterization, and from the gradient-based PSG method (Cutler et al., 2023) by directly targeting the stochastic variational structure rather than tracking iterates. The resulting Stochastic Path Follower (SPF) algorithm simulates SDEs whose dimension depends only on the problem dimension n (specifically n + n²), not on the neural network parameterization dimension φ, in contrast to PDM which requires SDEs of dimension n + nφ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-parametric Malliavin framework provides a principled, dimension-decoupled approach to TV-SO that could become the standard for solving stochastic optimization under distribution drift when the decision variable itself is stochastic. The scalability advantage (runtime insensitive to network size) is a genuine structural benefit that makes SPF attractive for large neural SDEs where PDM becomes intractable. However, only necessary (not sufficient) optimality conditions are derived, the empirical validation is limited to small-to-moderate dimensions (n ≤ 100, d ≤ 50), and the framework's applicability to high-dimensional real-world problems (e.g., time-series forecasting, as the authors note) remains untested. Open questions include establishing sufficient conditions, handling non-Malliavin-differentiable processes, and extending to constrained or multi-objective TV-SO.
