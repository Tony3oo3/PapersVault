## Problem

Most LLM-based Automated Heuristic Design (AHD) methods confine the search to component-level tuning within fixed solver templates, making performance tightly coupled to the chosen framework and capping system-level algorithmic expressiveness. Moving to open-ended Automated Algorithm Design (AAD) over complete solver programs introduces three compounding challenges: executability instability from long interdependent code, an effectively unbounded program space where valid high-performing algorithms are sparse, and credit assignment opacity when large coupled edits change multiple behaviors simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language problem description and a fixed evaluation dataset of instances, and uses an LLM API (DeepSeek v3.2 or Gemini 2.5 Flash) under a strict budget of 500 LLM calls. The output is a complete executable Python solver program structured as a function-level modular registry with a fixed preface (imports/constants) and a partition of functions into immutable definitions and mutable strategies. Each generated program is given 120 seconds of wall-clock time to solve the entire evaluation dataset, and is scored by its normalized optimality gap relative to the known optimum.

## Method

A₂DEPT performs tree-structured evolutionary search over a global program tree, where each node stores an executable program, its score, parent/operator history, and local operator weights. Selection is hybrid: a simulated-annealing Metropolis criterion accepts or rejects children relative to their parent on the frontier, and Boltzmann sampling over the full tree supplements the parent set to a fixed size K, preserving diverse trajectories that SA would reject. Variation is governed by three hierarchical operators—micro-tuning (localized edits to mutable strategy functions), macro-mutation (rewriting the entry-point workflow), and semantic crossover (integrating complementary mechanisms from two parents)—scheduled by a node-specific softmax weight vector updated via normalized performance-change feedback. A lightweight program-maintenance loop enforces executability by building a directed call graph, iteratively prompting the LLM to implement missing dependencies until the program is dependency-closed, and pruning unreachable code before evaluation.

## Results

On standard benchmarks (FrontierCO), A₂DEPT reduces the mean normalized optimality gap by 9.8% relative to the strongest competing LLM-based AHD baseline under DeepSeek v3.2. Headline per-task gaps: 8.79% on CVRP (vs. 17.42% for best AHD), 4.20% on MIS (vs. 11.35%), 11.46% on CFLP (vs. 14.09%), and 18.95% on FJSP (vs. 26.55%). On high-constraint problems, A₂DEPT achieves 0.00% gap and 2.56% infeasibility rate on CEVRPTW, and the lowest infeasibility rate (15.89%) on MRCPSP, substantially outperforming all LLM baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A₂DEPT outperforms all four LLM-based AHD baselines (FunSearch, EoH, ReEvo, MCTS-AHD) on every standard benchmark under both DeepSeek and Gemini, with the largest margins on CVRP (8.79% vs. 17.42% for EoH) and MIS (4.20% vs. 11.35% for EoH). Against the classical ILS heuristic, A₂DEPT achieves substantially lower gaps across all four tasks (e.g., 8.79% vs. 19.48% on CVRP). Compared to the commercial Gurobi solver, A₂DEPT matches Gurobi on MIS (4.20% vs. 4.28%) but trails on CVRP (8.79% vs. 6.75%) and FJSP (18.95% vs. 7.22%). Against problem-specific neural solvers, A₂DEPT wins on MIS (4.20% vs. 14.26% for SDDS) but loses on CVRP (8.79% vs. 6.31% for DeepACO), CFLP (11.46% vs. 8.26% for GCNN), and FJSP (18.95% vs. 16.55% for MPGN), indicating that learned inductive biases still dominate when problem structure is well captured by training. On high-constraint benchmarks, A₂DEPT achieves the best infeasibility rate on both CEVRPTW (2.56% vs. 8.67% for ReEvo) and MRCPSP (15.89% vs. 22.75% for ReEvo), with a 0.00% optimality gap on CEVRPTW.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based Automated Heuristic Design (AHD) lineage—FunSearch, EoH, ReEvo, and MCTS-AHD—by shifting the search target from heuristic components within fixed solver templates to complete executable solver programs (open-ended AAD). It adopts the tree-structured search architecture from MCTS-AHD but replaces UCB-based selection with a hybrid SA+Boltzmann scheme, introduces hierarchical operators (micro-tuning, macro-mutation, semantic crossover) with adaptive scheduling, and adds a dependency-repair maintenance loop to enforce executability of freeform code. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods optimize isolated functional slots (e.g., priority functions) while keeping control flow fixed, whereas A2DEPT treats the LLM as a system-level architect that can restructure the entire algorithmic workflow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that open-ended program-space evolution yields consistent quality gains (9.8% relative gap reduction) over template-bound AHD, validating the paradigm shift from component tuning to system-level redesign. However, the FJSP outlier—where GLS with expert-designed neighborhood operators outperforms all AAD methods—reveals that domain-specific inductive biases remain competitive, suggesting AHD and AAD are complementary rather than strictly ordered. Open questions include scaling to industrial multi-file codebases, reducing the verification/repair overhead, and extending to multi-objective settings. The finding that simply porting EoH or ReEvo from AHD to AAD without the maintenance and scheduling mechanisms fails to close the gap underscores that extra generative freedom requires corresponding structural support.
