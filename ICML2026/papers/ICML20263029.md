## Problem

Existing defenses against Harmful Fine-tuning (HFT) of LLMs—whether constraining parameter distance, gradient directions, or representation drift—consistently collapse under persistent fine-tuning. The paper traces this failure to the inherent redundancy of the high-dimensional parameter space, where the optimizer exploits trajectories orthogonal to defense constraints to restore harmful capabilities while superficially satisfying the imposed restrictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263029/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a safety-aligned LLM (Llama3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, or Gemma1.1-7B), a mixed fine-tuning dataset (90% benign task data + 10% harmful examples, ~1000 samples), and a small set of safety anchor queries (default |X_anchor|=8, as few as 1) drawn from high-risk prompts distinct from the attacker's data. It produces a LoRA-adapted model (rank 16) that maintains a Harmful Score below 10 across all tested poison ratios while preserving functional accuracy on SST-2, AGNEWS, GSM8K, and AlpacaEval. The anchors are consumed only as fixed reference hidden states cached offline; they are never part of the training batch.

## Method

Safety Bottleneck Regularization (SBR) shifts the defensive constraint from the redundant internal parameter space to the unembedding layer, identified as a deterministic geometric bottleneck: generating any harmful token strictly requires the final hidden state to project onto that token's embedding vector, a necessary condition that internal parameter redundancy cannot circumvent. In Phase 1 (offline), the final hidden states of K safety anchor queries are extracted from the frozen aligned model and cached. In Phase 2 (during fine-tuning), an MSE term L_safe = (1/K)Σ||h_θ(x') − h_ref(x')||² is added to the standard cross-entropy loss, anchoring the current model's bottleneck representations to the reference safety field. The total objective is L_total = L_CE + λ·L_safe (λ=50 default), optimized via AdamW with LoRA. A theoretical analysis (Appendix E) proves that randomly chosen Rank-1 LoRA subspaces are asymptotically orthogonal yet each contains a valid descent direction for the harmful loss, formally explaining why direction-specific defenses are bypassable. Empirically, a single anchor suffices because diverse malicious queries cluster tightly (μ_MSE ≈ 2.5) around the refusal direction in the final hidden state space, while benign queries remain geometrically distant (μ_MSE ≈ 6.8), so the localized MSE constraint creates a stability field over the malicious cluster without interfering with benign reasoning sub-circuits.

## Results

On Llama3.1-8B under harmful fine-tuning (10% poison ratio), SBR reduces the average Harmful Score (HS) to 5.68, compared to 70.70 for unconstrained SFT and 25.10 for the strongest baseline DeepAlign. SBR simultaneously achieves the highest average Functional Accuracy (FA) of 78.17, marginally exceeding SFT (78.07) and outperforming all defense baselines (73.34–76.04). Across all four downstream tasks (SST-2, AGNEWS, GSM8K, AlpacaEval), SBR maintains HS below 6.2 while preserving task-specific accuracy within 0.5% of the unconstrained baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SBR dominates all four baselines on both safety and utility. Against DeepAlign (Qi et al., ICLR 2025), the strongest prior defense, SBR reduces average HS from 25.10 to 5.68 (a 4.4× improvement) while also improving average FA from 76.04 to 78.17; DeepAlign's direct output-probability constraints degrade AlpacaEval win-rate to 33.64, whereas SBR achieves 45.82. Against parameter-based Lisa (Huang et al., NeurIPS 2024), SBR cuts HS from 52.45 to 5.68 and lifts FA from 73.50 to 78.17. Against representation-based Vaccine (Huang et al., NeurIPS 2024) and gradient-based Booster (Huang et al., ICLR 2025), SBR reduces HS by roughly 57 and 57 points respectively (62.53→5.68 and 62.33→5.68), while also recovering 4–5 points of FA that these methods sacrifice. Notably, SBR is the only method that simultaneously achieves HS < 10 and FA within 0.5% of unconstrained SFT across all four tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the three dominant families of HFT defenses—parameter-distance constraints (LISA, EWC), gradient-direction masking (Booster, Gradient Route), and representation-drift regularization (Vaccine, T-Vaccine)—by arguing they all fail for the same structural reason: the overparameterized internal space admits orthogonal bypass trajectories. It also contrasts with DeepAlign, which similarly bypasses the parameter space but constrains output token probabilities directly, causing utility degradation on short-output tasks. The method repurposes the well-known linear geometry of the Transformer unembedding layer (Vaswani et al.; Belrose et al.) as a defensive locus, building on the observation that refusal behavior is mediated by a shared low-dimensional subspace (Zou et al.; Arditi et al.) rather than introducing a new architectural component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SBR offers a practically attractive defense: a single MSE term on the final hidden state of a handful of anchor queries, requiring no architectural changes and minimal compute overhead (1.07× training time), while maintaining near-SFT utility across four diverse benchmarks and three model families. The finding that one anchor suffices (HS 67.8 → 6) suggests refusal semantics are highly concentrated, which could inform more efficient safety auditing. Key limitations include reliance on the base model's initial alignment (SBR anchors existing safety rather than inducing it) and no coverage of inference-time jailbreaks that bypass parameter updates entirely. Open questions include whether the bottleneck principle extends to multi-turn safety, agent-level safety, or non-Transformer architectures, and whether the single-anchor property holds as model scale increases.
