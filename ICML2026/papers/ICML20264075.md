## Problem

Existing Spiking Transformers lack a principled mechanism for bidirectional temporal fusion: prior temporal enhancement methods (TIM, STAtten, ST-SSA) propagate information only in the forward direction, rely on dataset-sensitive heuristic hyperparameters, or impose sequential dependencies that break the parallelism of attention. The human visual pathway integrates temporal information through feedforward-feedback modulation, but no Spiking Transformer architecture has captured this bidirectional structure.

## Input / Output

The model accepts diverse input modalities—static RGB images (32×32 or 64×64), asynchronous event streams from dynamic vision sensors, serialized image sequences, and speech signals—all of which are converted into T-timestep spike sequences via neuron encoding schemes (direct, phase, rate, or TTFS). Static inputs are repeated or transformed across timesteps to create temporal structure, while event streams and speech provide native temporal dynamics. The output is a single class label per input, with the model operating under supervised training with LIF neurons over a fixed number of timesteps (typically 4–16).

## Method

TEFormer introduces two complementary modules applied in the first 50% of Transformer layers: (1) a Temporal Enhancement Attention (TEA) module that performs fully parallel forward temporal fusion on the value pathway of QK-Attention using a single learnable scalar θ to construct an exponential-decay temporal mask matrix, requiring no manual hyperparameters and no sequential operations; and (2) a Temporal MLP (T-MLP) that replaces the standard MLP upsampling with a single-gate backward recurrence, initializing the hidden state at the last timestep and propagating future-to-past information through a gated LIF update. This asymmetric pairing—fast parallel forward fusion in attention plus refined backward tracing in the MLP—mirrors the feedforward-feedback asymmetry observed in the visual cortex. Shallow layers use QK-Attention for linear-complexity efficiency while deeper layers employ full Spiking Self-Attention for fine-grained spatiotemporal modeling, and the entire framework is evaluated under multiple encoding schemes to demonstrate that the temporal gains are encoding-agnostic.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, TEFormer achieves 96.24% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest Spiking Transformer baseline QKFormer (95.91%) and the ANN baseline ResNet-19 (94.97%). On CIFAR-100, TEFormer reaches 79.84%, surpassing QKFormer (79.09%) and SDT (78.64%). On neuromorphic benchmarks, TEFormer attains 81.90% on CIFAR10-DVS, 78.50% on N-CALTECH101, and 95.95% on NCARS, consistently leading all Spiking Transformer baselines. On temporally complex datasets, TEFormer achieves 96.20% on sMNIST, 90.19% on SHD, and 63.16% on UCF101-DVS, setting new state-of-the-art results across all evaluated categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEFormer outperforms all 16 baselines across every dataset category. On CIFAR-10, it beats the closest Spiking Transformer competitor QKFormer by 0.33% (96.24 vs. 95.91) and the ANN ResNet-19 by 1.27%; notably, the temporal-enhancement baseline TIM actually degrades performance (94.20%) relative to the plain Spikformer backbone (95.09%), while TEFormer improves upon it. On CIFAR-100, TEFormer leads QKFormer by 0.75% (79.84 vs. 79.09) and DSR by 1.34%. On neuromorphic datasets, TEFormer gains 1.05% over TIM on CIFAR10-DVS (81.90 vs. 80.85) and 0.57% over Spikformer on N-CALTECH101 (78.50 vs. 77.93). On temporally complex benchmarks, the margins are larger: TEFormer surpasses QKFormer by 1.95% on sMNIST (96.20 vs. 94.25) and by 1.63% on UCF101-DVS (63.16 vs. 61.31), and outperforms TIM by 4.95% on SHD (90.19 vs. 85.24). The only near-tie is on NCARS, where TEFormer (95.95%) edges out Spikformer (95.60%) by just 0.35%.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEFormer builds directly on the Spikformer and QKFormer architectures, adopting QK-Attention in shallow layers and Spiking Self-Attention in deeper layers as its backbone. It departs from prior temporal-enhancement methods (TIM, STAtten, ST-SSA) that propagate temporal information unidirectionally and introduce sequential dependencies or dataset-sensitive hyperparameters into the attention computation. The key conceptual departure is a structured bidirectional design: a hyperparameter-free forward temporal fusion (TEA) in the attention pathway paired with a gated backward recurrence (T-MLP) in the MLP pathway, explicitly motivated by feedforward-feedback modulation in the human visual cortex. The paper also introduces the first systematic evaluation of Spiking Transformers across multiple neuron encoding schemes (direct, phase, rate, TTFS), a dimension prior work has not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that bidirectional temporal fusion yields consistent gains across static, neuromorphic, and temporally complex benchmarks—and that these gains are robust to encoding choice—TEFormer provides a principled template for temporal modeling in Spiking Transformers that avoids the sequential bottlenecks of prior approaches. The encoding-sensitivity analysis (e.g., Spikformer dropping from 95.09% to 82.63% under phase encoding) highlights a previously underappreciated vulnerability and suggests that temporal modeling quality is a critical differentiator beyond direct-encoding settings. Open questions remain: the method is validated only in software simulation on classification tasks, T-MLP's backward recurrence precludes strictly causal online inference, and no unified encoding standard exists in the SNN community to make cross-paper comparisons fully meaningful.
