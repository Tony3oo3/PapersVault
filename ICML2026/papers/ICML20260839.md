## Problem

Existing geometric KV cache eviction methods score tokens by cosine similarity to a global centroid, which is scale-invariant and discards magnitude information. This causes two critical failures: radial outliers (tokens with extreme magnitude but typical direction) are incorrectly evicted, and at 64K+ context lengths the global centroid becomes a semantically meaningless center of mass, collapsing accuracy from 82.3% at 32K to 35.2% at 64K.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the raw key tensor from the KV cache (shape: batch × heads × seq_len × head_dim) and a compression ratio ρ ∈ (0,1), producing a set of token indices to retain via a single global TopK selection. The windowed variant additionally takes a window size parameter (default W=4096) and computes local centroids per non-overlapping window before the global selection. The entire scoring pipeline runs in O(Nd + N log N) time, adding less than 0.5 ms latency at 64K context—negligible compared to attention's O(N²d) cost.

## Method

ManifoldKV replaces cosine similarity with Euclidean (L2) distance from the key centroid μ = (1/N)Σk_i, scoring each token as s_i = ‖k_i − μ‖₂. The geometric decomposition ‖k_i − μ‖² = r_i² + ‖μ‖² − 2r_i‖μ‖cos(k_i, μ) reveals that L2 captures both angular deviation (the cosine term) and radial deviation (the r_i² magnitude term) that cosine discards, preventing directional collision in multi-key retrieval. For contexts exceeding ~32K tokens where global centroids suffer from semantic dilution, WindowedManifoldKV computes local centroids over non-overlapping sliding windows of W=4096 tokens, preserving discriminative power within each semantically coherent region and recovering accuracy to 84.3% at 64K. The theoretical analysis establishes that key vectors occupy a universal ~7–9 dimensional manifold (validated via Two-NN estimation across six architectures), giving L2 an O(k) sample complexity advantage while cosine fundamentally fails on radial outliers regardless of sample size. The method is training-free, requires only 3 lines of code, and integrates as a drop-in scorer within budget-allocation frameworks like AdaKV.

## Results

On RULER (Llama-3.1-8B, 20% compression, AdaKV framework), ManifoldKV achieves 95.73% accuracy, matching KeyDiff (95.66%) and outperforming SnapKV by +6.5 points (95.73 vs. 89.33%). At 64K context (25% compression), WindowedManifoldKV recovers to 84.29%, surpassing KeyDiff by +3.2 points (84.29 vs. 81.09) and global L2 by +49 points (84.29 vs. 35.20). On multi-key NIAH (3-key, 50% compression), ManifoldKV achieves 92.4% vs. KeyDiff's 77.0% (+15.4 points). Cross-architecture evaluation across four models yields consistent 94–96% accuracy with ±0.3% variance, and real-world benchmarks show +2.80 on LongBench (Qwen3-8B), +42 on HELMET Recall Multikey-3 at 131K, and +7.16 on InfiniteBench (Phi-4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KeyDiff (Park et al., 2025), the primary geometric baseline using cosine similarity from the mean, ManifoldKV matches overall RULER accuracy with AdaKV (95.73% vs. 95.66%) but wins decisively in two critical regimes: multi-key NIAH (92.4% vs. 77.0%, +15.4 points at 50% compression) and 64K long-context (WindowedManifoldKV 84.29% vs. 81.09%, +3.2 points). Against SnapKV (Li et al., 2024), the leading attention-based method, ManifoldKV outperforms by +6.5 points under the AdaKV framework (95.73% vs. 89.33%) and by +8.96 points standalone (92.93% vs. 83.97%), demonstrating geometric outlier detection is superior to attention-based eviction for retrieval tasks. Against DuoAttention (Xiao et al., 2025), ManifoldKV achieves comparable accuracy (95.73% vs. 95.36%) while being training-free and requiring no model-specific calibration patterns. Against CriticalKV (Feng et al., 2025), a value-aware geometric method, ManifoldKV leads by +14.03 points standalone (92.93% vs. 78.90%). Against StreamingLLM (Xiao et al., 2024), ManifoldKV leads by +33.63 points standalone (92.93% vs. 59.30%). On real-world benchmarks, ManifoldKV wins on LongBench for Qwen3-8B (+2.80, 12/14 tasks) and Phi-4 (+0.49), HELMET RAG (+6.5 EM on Llama), and InfiniteBench on Phi-4 (+7.16), but KeyDiff remains stronger on BABILong and InfiniteBench for Llama/Qwen3/Ministral, revealing architecture-dependent metric selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometric KV-cache eviction line initiated by KeyDiff (cosine distance from centroid) and KNorm (L2 norm retention) by replacing the angular-only cosine score with full L2 distance, thereby capturing radial outliers that cosine provably cannot detect. It also introduces the Centroid Dilution Problem—a previously unreported failure mode where global centroids lose discriminative power beyond ~32K tokens—and addresses it with sliding-window local centroids (WindowedManifoldKV). The method integrates as a drop-in scorer within AdaKV's adaptive budget-allocation framework, and draws on representation-learning manifold analysis (Two-NN, MLE estimators) to provide the first systematic intrinsic-dimensionality characterization of key vectors across transformer architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-line, training-free scorer is immediately deployable across architectures, and the identification of centroid dilution as a distinct failure mode (with a clean 49-point recovery via windowing) gives practitioners a concrete diagnostic for long-context compression. However, the paper's own experiments reveal that the L2-over-cosine advantage is architecture-dependent and regime-specific: it wins decisively on multi-key retrieval and high-norm-variance models (Qwen3, Phi-4) but trails KeyDiff on BABILong, several InfiniteBench architectures, and Qwen2.5-7B LongBench. Open questions include whether the advantage persists beyond 131K context, whether streaming-friendly approximate centroids can replace the batch windowing, and whether the architecture-dependent metric selection can be predicted a priori from model architecture alone rather than empirical CV measurement.
