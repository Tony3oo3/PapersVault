## Problem

Existing zero-shot music editing methods cannot perform stem-specific timbre transfer in dense polyphonic mixtures: vanilla cross-attention lacks the spectral resolution to localize a target stem, causing boundary leakage that distorts non-target stems, while rigid preservation constraints (e.g., self-attention injection) indiscriminately freeze entangled features and prevent the target from manifesting. The paper formalizes this as a 'semantic-acoustic misalignment'—a fundamental mismatch between diffuse semantic attention and the fine-grained acoustic energy distribution in multi-track audio.

## Input / Output

The method takes a log-mel spectrogram of a multi-track mixture (1–9 stems, evaluated on MUSDB18-HQ and MusicDelta) and a target text prompt (e.g., 'a fast expressive violin, a solid bass line and driving energetic drums') specifying which stem to re-timbre. It produces an edited mixture in the same log-mel domain where the target stem's timbre is replaced while all non-target stems are preserved. The pipeline operates entirely in AudioLDM 2's VAE latent space over 100 DDIM steps, with the acoustic prior extracted once via HT-Demucs before inversion, and the entire process is zero-shot (no fine-tuning of the backbone).

## Method

Polyphonia introduces Acoustic-Informed Attention Calibration, a dual-mechanism module that injects a probabilistic acoustic prior into the T-UNet's attention layers during diffusion. First, an Ideal Ratio Mask (IRM) is computed from BSS-decomposed stems as G = √(|S_tgt|² / (|S_tgt|² + |S_con|²)), yielding a continuous soft mask in [0,1] that encodes the target's spectral dominance probability and naturally suppresses guidance where background energy prevails. Second, during the editing pass, Source Interpolation performs pre-softmax logit blending in Self-Attention and LoA Cross-Attention layers—mixing cached source logits with current logits weighted by (1−G) to rigidly preserve non-target structure—while Acoustic Modulation adds a spatio-textual bias matrix (outer product of the flattened G and a binary token mask isolating the target word) to the text cross-attention logits, forcing generative focus onto the target's spectral envelope. The calibration is applied to all down-sampling layers of the T-UNet, and the framework is model-agnostic, requiring only a pre-trained latent diffusion backbone and a BSS model.

## Results

On MusicDelta, Polyphonia achieves the highest target alignment (CLAP 0.437) and the best composite balance scores (ASB 0.910, AMB 0.991), outperforming the strongest baseline Melodia (CLAP 0.380, ASB 0.903, AMB 0.864) by 15.5% in CLAP. On MUSDB18-HQ, Polyphonia similarly leads in CLAP (0.342 vs. Melodia's 0.296) and AMB (0.985 vs. 0.877). In subjective evaluation, Polyphonia attains the highest Target Timbre Alignment (TTA 3.804) and Global Acoustic Coherence (GAC 3.692) on MusicDelta, while Melodia and SteerMusic marginally lead in Contextual Timbre Integrity (CTI 3.594, 3.435) at the cost of weaker editing effectiveness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Polyphonia outperforms all seven baselines on CLAP (target alignment) and both composite balance metrics (ASB, AMB) across both datasets. Against the closest competitor Melodia, Polyphonia gains +0.057 CLAP on MusicDelta (0.437 vs. 0.380) and +0.046 on MUSDB18-HQ (0.342 vs. 0.296), while Melodia retains a slight edge in CTI (3.594 vs. 3.413) due to its more rigid preservation constraints that sacrifice editing strength. SteerMusic achieves the highest CQT1-PCC (0.556 on MusicDelta) but trails Polyphonia substantially in CLAP (0.317 vs. 0.437) and AMB (0.767 vs. 0.991). MusicGen, while competitive in CLAP (0.377), catastrophically fails at melodic preservation (CQT1-PCC 0.069) and non-target integrity (CTI 2.054, AMB 0.000), confirming that autoregressive melody conditioning cannot handle stem-specific editing. Global inversion methods (SDEdit, DDIM, DDPM) are uniformly weak, with SDEdit scoring near-zero on all composite metrics and DDIM/DDPM showing poor structural preservation (LPAPS > 5.4). MusicMagus underperforms across the board, with the lowest CLAP among structural methods (0.238) and moderate structural scores, indicating its gradient-based guidance is insufficient for dense polyphonic mixtures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the AudioLDM 2 latent diffusion backbone and the attention-injection paradigm introduced by Melodia, but departs fundamentally from the internal-feature-preservation strategy that characterizes prior zero-shot music editing (Melodia, SteerMusic, MusicMagus). The key conceptual shift is from relying on self/cross-attention maps or energy gradients for localization to injecting an external probabilistic acoustic prior (Ideal Ratio Mask derived from BSS) as a coarse spectral boundary, within which cross-attention performs fine-grained semantic selection. This coarse-to-fine decoupling of acoustic localization and semantic synthesis is the paper's central architectural departure, and it explicitly rejects the separation-editing-remixing pipeline in favor of holistic diffusion editing guided by soft masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formalizes a concrete failure mode (semantic-acoustic misalignment) that explains why existing zero-shot music editing methods degrade in dense polyphonic mixtures, providing a principled diagnosis rather than just a new trick. The PolyEvalPrompts benchmark (1,170 tasks across MUSDB18-HQ and MusicDelta) fills a gap in standardized evaluation for stem-specific editing. Limitations include inherited diffusion inference latency, dependence on BSS models trained predominantly on Western pop/rock taxonomies (raising fairness concerns for non-Western instruments), and graceful-but-measurable degradation in very dense mixtures (≥5 tracks). Open questions include whether the acoustic-prior calibration transfers to flow-matching or autoregressive backbones, and whether open-vocabulary target specification is feasible without a fixed BSS taxonomy.
