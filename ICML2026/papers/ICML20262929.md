## Problem

Existing audio-visual deepfake detectors rely heavily on lip-speech alignment cues that are reliable in talking scenarios but degrade substantially when the audio-visual regime shifts to singing, where rhythmic vocalization and musical accompaniment weaken cross-modal coupling. No public benchmark exists for singing-head forgery detection, and the talking-to-singing domain shift has not been systematically quantified or addressed by prior methods.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three parallel streams per video: full video frames combined with facial masks (for Alpha-CLIP region-aware encoding), temporally concatenated mouth crops (for the lip-reading visual encoder), and Mel-spectrograms derived from the audio track (for the lip-reading audio encoder). The output is a single video-level detection score obtained by log-sum-exp aggregation of frame-level scores, where lower values indicate stronger cross-modal coherence (i.e., more likely real). Notably, training uses only real talking videos—no synthetic samples—making the singing domain a strictly unseen test target.

## Method

T-AVFD combines two complementary signals via adaptive fusion. The Facial Authenticity Pattern Learner (FAPL) extracts face-region semantics with Alpha-CLIP and aligns them to multi-granularity positive/negative text prompts (face, eyes, mouth) through a binary contrastive loss, producing a scenario-agnostic authenticity pattern fp that captures the richer facial semantics observed in real versus forged samples. In parallel, a pre-trained lip-reading model encodes mouth crops and audio into alignment features (v, a). The Multi-Modal Differential Weight Learning (MMDWL) module then fuses fp, v, and a using content-conditioned weights predicted by an MLP-softmax generator, with a fixed modulation vector α = {−0.1, +0.1, +0.1} added pre-softmax to bias fusion toward audio-visual alignment while retaining facial authenticity cues. Training optimizes the sum of an audio-visual temporal alignment loss (inherited from AVAD) and the face-text contrastive loss, using only real talking data, which is what enables the model to generalize to the unseen singing domain without overfitting to generator-specific artifacts.

## Results

T-AVFD (unsupervised, trained only on real talking data) achieves AP/AUC of 83.6/87.7 on AVLips, 95.6/95.6 on FakeAVCeleb, 87.6/93.0 on TalkingHeadBench, and 85.7/80.2 on the proposed SHDF singing dataset. The singing scenario is where the advantage is most dramatic: all baselines drop to near-chance AUC (~37–51%), while T-AVFD maintains 80.2% AUC. On talking datasets, T-AVFD matches or exceeds the best supervised detectors (e.g., 95.6 AUC on FKAV vs. 90.9 for EfficientViT) while requiring no forged training samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T-AVFD outperforms all six baselines on the SHDF singing dataset by a wide margin (85.7/80.2 AP/AUC vs. best baseline EfficientViT at 66.6/46.5), confirming that alignment-centric cues collapse under the talking-to-singing shift. Among unsupervised baselines, T-AVFD beats AVH-Align (the strongest prior unsupervised method) by 9.3 AP and 5.2 AUC on AVLips, 2.1/2.6 on FKAV, 22.8/10.7 on THB, and 30.5/42.8 on SHDF. Against supervised methods, T-AVFD surpasses LipFD on FKAV (95.6 vs. 83.4 AP), THB (87.6 vs. 45.0 AP), and SHDF (85.7 vs. 38.1 AP), though LipFD edges it slightly on AVLips AP (85.3 vs. 83.6) while T-AVFD leads on AUC (87.7 vs. 84.7). RealForensics, the strongest supervised AV baseline on THB (68.7/74.3), is exceeded by T-AVFD by 18.9 AP and 18.7 AUC. CViT and EfficientViT, being visual-only supervised models, are consistently outperformed across all four datasets, with the gap most pronounced on THB and SHDF where audio-visual cues are essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised audio-visual deepfake detection line initiated by AVAD (Feng et al., 2023) and refined by AVH-Align (Smeu et al., 2025), both of which rely on audio-visual alignment as the primary forgery signal. The key departure is the introduction of a text-guided facial authenticity pattern (via Alpha-CLIP and face-text contrastive alignment) as a scenario-agnostic complement to alignment-centric evidence, motivated by the observation that rhythmic vocalization in singing weakens the audio-visual coupling that prior methods exploit. The differential weight learning module further departs from the uniform fusion strategies used in existing multi-modal detectors by adaptively re-weighting modalities based on content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SHDF dataset is the first singing-specific audio-visual deepfake benchmark, filling a clear gap in the literature where all prior datasets are talking-centric. The finding that alignment-centric detectors collapse to near-chance performance (AUC ~37–50%) under the talking-to-singing shift, while a text-guided facial pattern signal retains discriminative power, challenges the field's reliance on lip-speech synchronization as a universal forgery cue. Limitations include the relatively small dataset scale (3,000 fake samples), binary classification without source attribution, and the manual design of the modulation vector. Open questions include whether the facial authenticity signal generalizes to other domain shifts (e.g., shouting, whispering) and how to extend detection toward forgery attribution.
