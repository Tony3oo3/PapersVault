## Problem

Continuous (Gaussian) diffusion consistently underperforms pure discrete diffusion on categorical data such as text, despite its theoretical advantage of enabling joint position updates through a learned score function. The paper identifies the root cause as a 'temporal dissonance': discrete identity corruption scales exponentially with vocabulary size while continuous rank degradation remains vocabulary-independent, so the noise levels that preserve identifiable tokens for learning conditional structure are misaligned with those needed to learn a meaningful continuous score.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Gaussian noise vector over the one-hot representation space (R^{L×|V|}) combined with a binary masking vector indicating which positions are clean versus corrupted, and produces a discrete token sequence via argmax. For guided generation on QM9, an off-the-shelf classifier gradient is added to the score function during the ODE update step. The approximate inference algorithm avoids materializing full noisy one-hot vectors by using single-step Monte Carlo embedding lookups, matching the computational efficiency of pure discrete methods.

## Method

CANDI introduces a structured noising kernel that decouples discrete corruption (position-wise masking with linear schedule α(t)=1−t) from continuous corruption (Gaussian noise with σ(t) derived by inverting the target rank degradation function), ensuring both corruption forms scale linearly with time and eliminating the vocabulary-size-dependent temporal dissonance. The model is parameterized as P_θ(X_0|X_t) trained with a weighted cross-entropy loss identical to masked diffusion, but the forward kernel is hybrid. Inference alternates between discrete unmasking (sampling tokens from the learned posterior at positions transitioning to clean) and continuous ODE updates (applying the probability flow ODE to remaining noisy positions), enabling simultaneous joint evolution and conditional structure learning. An approximate inference algorithm replaces expensive |V|×L×B matrix multiplications with a single-step Monte Carlo embedding lookup, and the continuous geometry naturally supports classifier-based guidance via simple gradient addition without diffusion-specific classifier training.

## Results

On OpenWebText (large vocabulary), CANDI achieves a strictly better entropy-perplexity frontier than MDLM up to 64 NFE and surpasses DUO once perplexity drops below approximately 40. On Text8 (small vocabulary), CANDI matches or exceeds MDLM across all NFE, while pure continuous diffusion methods (one-hot and embedding) maintain reasonable frontiers. On QM9 controlled generation, CANDI strictly dominates classifier-guided MDLM and achieves competitive performance against classifier-guided UDLM using only an off-the-shelf classifier with simple gradient addition, without requiring diffusion-specific classifier training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MDLM (masked diffusion, Sahoo et al. 2024), CANDI achieves a strictly superior entropy-perplexity frontier on both Text8 and OpenWebText up to 64 NFE, and on QM9 strictly dominates classifier-guided MDLM in both QED and ring-count frontiers. Against DUO (uniform diffusion, Sahoo et al. 2025), CANDI exhibits a strictly superior frontier as perplexity decreases past approximately 40 on OpenWebText, demonstrating the advantage of continuous score-based joint updates at low NFE. Against classifier-guided UDLM (Schiff et al. 2024) on QM9, CANDI achieves competitive performance without requiring a specialized diffusion-trained classifier, instead using a standard off-the-shelf classifier with simple gradient addition to the ODE update. Pure continuous diffusion baselines (one-hot and embedding diffusion) maintain reasonable performance on Text8 but fail catastrophically on OpenWebText, with one-hot diffusion being orders of magnitude worse and embedding diffusion exhibiting mode collapse, empirically validating the temporal dissonance hypothesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

CANDI sits at the intersection of two established diffusion lineages: discrete masked diffusion (MDLM, DUO) and continuous score-based diffusion (VE SDE / probability flow ODE). It repurposes the masked diffusion training objective and carry-over masking constraint while injecting a Gaussian noising process on the one-hot space, and borrows the single-step Monte Carlo embedding-lookup trick from Cetin et al. (2025) for efficient inference. Conceptually, it departs from prior hybrid efforts (CADD, CCDD) by providing a first-principles theoretical explanation—token identifiability and temporal dissonance—for why pure continuous diffusion fails on discrete data, rather than motivating the hybrid design from expressivity or information-void arguments alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The temporal dissonance framework gives the field a clean, quantitative explanation for a long-standing empirical gap (continuous diffusion underperforming discrete diffusion on text), and the hybrid kernel offers a principled way to recover continuous geometry benefits (joint position updates, gradient-based guidance) without sacrificing discrete conditional structure. Practical implications include off-the-shelf classifier guidance without diffusion-specific training and improved low-NFE generation quality. Open questions include the optimal coupling between the masking schedule and the continuous noise schedule (currently both linear), whether the approach scales to large language models, and how it extends to embedding-space diffusion where the analytical control is less precise.
