## Problem

Diffusion Transformers incur high computational costs because Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) applies dual forward passes at every timestep with a constant scale, and feature caching methods apply uniform calibration across all transformer blocks. Existing efficiency methods skip CFG but keep scales fixed, while quality methods use variable scales but still compute CFG at every step—these directions appear incompatible. RSTR asks whether jointly optimizing *when* to apply CFG and *what scale* to use can simultaneously reduce computation and preserve or improve quality.

## Input / Output

RSTR is a training-free, inference-time acceleration applied to pre-trained diffusion transformers (DiT-XL/2, PixArt-α, FLUX, Qwen-Image) at resolutions from 256×256 to 1024×1024. It takes the same inputs as the base model (text prompts or class labels) and produces the same output (synthetic RGB images), but with 50–70% fewer FLOPs. The one-time offline optimization produces two artifacts—a sparse per-timestep guidance schedule w* and a per-region SVD rank configuration r*—that are then deployed at inference without any model weight modification.

## Method

Stage 1 formulates guidance scheduling as a hybrid discrete-continuous optimization (which timesteps to skip × what scale to use at active steps) and solves it via a CMA-ES-style evolutionary search over a sigmoid-transformed parameter space, using a fitness that balances output fidelity against a high-step reference trajectory and a sparsity penalty. The discovered schedules are sparse (only 8–10 of 20–50 timesteps active) but use elevated scales at those steps, exploiting the finding that position and scale are coupled and each active step is indispensable. Stage 2 addresses the spatial redundancy that variable guidance exposes: because different transformer blocks exhibit heterogeneous sensitivity to guidance-induced feature deviations, RSTR partitions blocks into K regions and assigns each a distinct SVD calibration rank via coordinate descent with binary search, minimizing FID under a total rank budget. This adaptive rank allocation outperforms uniform-rank caching (e.g., ICC) by allocating more capacity to sensitive deep blocks and less to insensitive shallow ones.

## Results

On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet 512×512), RSTR achieves FID 2.72 with only 24.97T MACs, surpassing 50-step DDIM (FID 3.20, 52.45T) by 15% in FID while using 47% less computation, and even outperforming 1000-step DDIM (FID 2.99). On ImageNet 256×256, RSTR achieves the best FID of 2.04 among all methods including 1000-step DDIM (2.12). On Qwen-Image (1024×1024), RSTR delivers a 3.43× latency speedup (56.73s → 16.62s) while maintaining GenEval score (87.21 vs 88.40) and improving GenEval2 GM (40.57 vs 38.72). Across all four models, RSTR achieves 50–70% computational savings while matching or exceeding full-CFG quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSTR outperforms all seven baselines on the primary DiT-XL/2 benchmark. Against DDIM (50-step), RSTR improves FID from 3.20 to 2.72 (−15%) while reducing MACs by 52% (52.45T → 24.9T); against 1000-step DDIM, RSTR achieves a better FID (2.72 vs 2.99) with 97.6% fewer MACs. Among caching-based methods, RSTR dominates ICC (FID 2.72 vs 3.73), TaylorSeer (2.72 vs 3.51), and L2C (2.72 vs 3.98), all at equal or lower compute. On the 256×256 setting, RSTR (FID 2.04) beats HarmoniCa (3.33) and ICC (2.16) by large margins. On FLUX, RSTR without caching achieves the highest ImageReward (1.0092) and CLIP Score (28.06), surpassing the 50-step full-CFG origin (1.0074, 27.79) with 72% less computation. On Qwen-Image, RSTR matches the 50-step origin on GenEval (87.21 vs 88.40) and improves GenEval2 GM (40.57 vs 38.72) at 3.43× lower latency, while Ada Guidance (17.10s) and TaylorSeer (36.72s) are both slower and lower quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

RSTR unifies two previously separate lines of CFG research: interval-based methods that optimize *when* to apply guidance (Kynkäänniemi et al., 2024) and time-varying scale methods that optimize *what* scale to use (Wang et al., 2024), by jointly searching over both dimensions via evolutionary optimization. For the spatial side, it extends ICC's (Chen et al., 2025) SVD-based incremental calibration by replacing the uniform rank assumption with adaptive per-region rank allocation, motivated by the observation that variable guidance introduces heterogeneous denoising deviations across transformer blocks. The paper positions itself as the first framework to address both temporal and spatial redundancy simultaneously, arguing that the two are inherently coupled: sparse guidance schedules break the feature-consistency assumption that uniform caching relies on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50–70% compute savings while matching or exceeding full-CFG quality on four state-of-the-art DiTs (including FLUX and Qwen-Image at 1024×1024) is practically significant for deployment, and the training-free, one-time offline optimization makes it broadly applicable. The geometric analysis revealing that guidance is most effective where the PF-ODE trajectory has *low* intrinsic curvature (high leverage) provides a mechanistic explanation that could inform future analytical schedule design. Key limitations include model-specific schedules requiring re-optimization per architecture/sampler, sequential (not joint) two-stage optimization, and untested robustness across diverse prompt domains. Open questions include whether the discovered schedules generalize to video diffusion or autoregressive image models, and whether the two stages can be co-optimized for further gains.
