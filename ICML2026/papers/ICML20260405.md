## Problem

Existing DLO manipulation simulators each support only a subset of desired features—none simultaneously provide physical coupling with other materials (rigid, soft, fluid) and full differentiability for gradient-based policy optimization. Prior manipulation work is task-specific and struggles to generalize across the wide variety of DLO materials (extensible, inextensible, elasto-plastic) and topologies (open, loop) encountered in practice, while collecting diverse real-world demonstrations remains impractical.

## Input / Output

The simulator consumes full DLO vertex positions and velocities (N_v × 6, no downsampling needed due to co-dimensional representation), robot joint states, and per-task material parameters (stretching/bending/twisting moduli, inextensibility flag, plasticity yield threshold and creep rate). The VLM-based DLO agent takes a rendered scene image plus task description and outputs JSON-structured grasp point proposals or subtask decomposition plans with reward code snippets. For closed-loop real-world deployment, the policy receives a 384-point 3D point cloud (from HSV-thresholded RGB with Gaussian depth noise) concatenated with robot proprioception, and outputs Cartesian end-effector pose targets resolved via IK.

## Method

The core contribution is a differentiable DLO solver built on Discrete Elastic Rods theory, implemented in Taichi within the Genesis physics engine, enhanced with bending plasticity (yield-threshold-driven rest-curvature creep), loop-topology frame initialization via holonomy correction, and heterogeneous material composition. Two-way coupling is achieved with rigid bodies through SDF-based soft contact with impulse response and kinematic gripper locking, and with soft bodies through MPM-grid-mediated collision impulses applied symmetrically for momentum conservation. Gradient backpropagation over thousands of timesteps is enabled via gradient checkpointing (inspired by FluidLab), caching CPU state checkpoints and recomputing local segments during the backward pass. A VLM-based DLO agent (Gemini-3 Pro-Preview) provides grasp proposals in Candidate mode (selecting from uniformly sampled points) and performs iterative task decomposition with dynamic plan updates after each subtask execution. The benchmark comprises 10 tasks (8 fixed-horizon, 2 long-horizon) with differentiable rewards, evaluated across MFRL (PPO, SAC), FO-MBRL (SHAC, SAPO), and trajectory optimization (CMA-ES, GD), with sim-to-real validation via differentiable system identification and zero-shot open/closed-loop policy transfer.

## Results

On the 8 fixed-horizon DLO-Lab tasks, CMA-ES achieves the highest average success rate (86.6%), substantially outperforming all RL baselines (PPO: 29.3%, SAC: 16.6%, SHAC: 33.3%, SAPO: 35.0%, GD: 25.0%). FO-MBRL methods (SHAC/SAPO) dramatically outperform MFRL on the topologically complex Unknotting task (~46 episodic return vs. ~3 for PPO/SAC), confirming the value of differentiable gradients. However, GD fails on tasks with discontinuous contact (Unknotting: 3.44, Wiring-post: 36.40) where gradients are unavailable or misleading. In sim-to-real transfer, a zero-shot closed-loop PPO policy achieves ~58% success (7/12) on the precision-demanding Wiring-ring task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMA-ES (sample-based trajectory optimization) dominates across nearly all tasks, achieving the best episodic return on 7 of 8 tasks and an 86.6% average success rate, versus 25–35% for all RL methods. Among RL baselines, FO-MBRL methods (SHAC, SAPO) leverage the simulator's differentiability to achieve ~46 episodic return on Unknotting, a 15× improvement over MFRL methods (PPO: 3.29, SAC: 2.95), but still trail CMA-ES (57.21) on that task. Gradient descent (GD) excels on smooth-landscape tasks like Coiling (11.59) and Separation (115.52) but collapses on tasks with discontinuous contact or sparse gradients (Unknotting: 3.44, Wiring-post: 36.40), confirming that differentiable simulation alone is insufficient without robust exploration. PPO and SAC are broadly comparable to each other, with PPO slightly ahead on Coiling and SAC on Wrapping, but both fail entirely on Gathering, Lifting, Slingshot, and Unknotting (0% success). The paper's key empirical conclusion is that gradient-based methods win when informative gradients exist (smooth, contact-free dynamics), while sampling-based CMA-ES is the robust default for the full spectrum of DLO manipulation challenges.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Discrete Elastic Rods (DER) framework (Bergou et al., 2008; 2010) into a fully differentiable simulation engine built on Taichi and integrated into the Genesis physics platform, adding bending plasticity, loop topology, and heterogeneous material coupling that no prior DLO simulator supported simultaneously. It departs from prior DLO manipulation work (cable untangling, shape control, cable routing) that was task-specific and relied on real-world demonstrations, by providing a unified differentiable benchmark with diverse material behaviors. The VLM-based DLO agent for grasp proposal and task decomposition represents a novel integration of vision-language models into the manipulation planning loop, building on the broader trend of using LLMs/VLMs for structured task decomposition in robotics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DLO-Lab fills a critical gap by being the first differentiable DLO simulator that unifies all key material properties (elasticity, plasticity, inextensibility, loop topology, multi-material coupling), enabling both gradient-based and sample-based policy learning under a standardized interface. The practical sim-to-real validation (58% closed-loop success on Wiring-ring, successful open-loop transfer on Gathering and Wiring-post) demonstrates that differentiable simulation can bridge the sim-to-real gap for DLOs, which is important for industrial cable harnessing and surgical suturing. Key open questions include whether the VLM-based agent generalizes beyond the tested tasks, whether the benchmark scales to more complex multi-DLO scenes, and whether the sample-efficiency gap between open-loop trajectory optimization and closed-loop policy learning can be closed with better reward shaping or curriculum strategies.
