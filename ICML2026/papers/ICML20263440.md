## Problem

In scale-wise autoregressive (SwAR) image generation, classifier-free guidance signals become progressively dispersed and misaligned with semantically important regions as resolution increases, unlike diffusion models where guidance remains sharp and focused on foreground objects. This misalignment scatters guidance across all tokens equally, producing ambiguous and unfaithful features. The paper identifies this gap through new evenness and divergence metrics and shows that directly transplanting diffusion guidance strategies to SwAR models is suboptimal.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-and-play inference-time modification on pre-trained SwAR backbones (VAR, VAR-CLIP, Switti), requiring no retraining. Conditioning is provided as either a class label (ImageNet 1000-class) or a text prompt, which modulates token prediction distributions at each of K scale levels. The output is a sequence of token maps at progressively increasing spatial resolutions (e.g., 1×1 up to 36×36 for 512×512 images), decoded into the final RGB image. Experiments cover 256×256, 512×512, and 1024×1024 resolutions.

## Method

IGG replaces the uniform scalar guidance scale in CFG with a per-token adaptive weight computed via self-attention over the guidance signals themselves. Specifically, the guidance nudge p_θ^→(s_k|c) = p_θ(s_k|c) − p_θ(s_k) is treated as a feature vector per token, and a softmax attention is applied: f_k(s_k|c) = γ_k · softmax(p_θ^→(s_k|c) [p_θ^→(s_k|c)]^T / √|V|), which assigns higher weights to tokens whose guidance signals are locally coherent (i.e., surrounded by similar salient tokens). The final guided distribution becomes p̃_θ(s_k|c) = p_θ(s_k) + f_k(s_k|c) · p_θ^→(s_k|c), concentrating guidance on semantically important regions while attenuating it on background. The global scale γ_k is retained for tuning, and the progressive schedule γ_k = w·k/(K−1) is preserved. The method adds only one dot-product attention per scale level, incurring negligible computational overhead (~0.001s per image).

## Results

On ImageNet 512×512 class-conditioned generation, VAR-d36 with IGG (w=2.10) achieves FID 2.56 and IS 314.3, outperforming VAR-d36 with CFG (FID 2.61, IS 293.7) and all diffusion baselines including DiT-XL/2 (FID 3.04). At 256×256, VAR-d30 with IGG (w=1.85) reaches FID 1.92 and IS 321.28, marginally beating CFG (FID 1.93, IS 315.64) and surpassing DiT-XL/2 with REPG (FID 2.17). For text-to-image, Switti with IGG improves GenEval from 0.62 to 0.64 and MJHQ FID from 9.50 to 7.09 over CFG, while incurring negligible computational overhead (+0.0017s inference time, +0.00 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGG consistently outperforms CFG on the same SwAR backbones: on VAR-d36 (512×512), IGG improves FID from 2.61 to 2.56 and IS from 293.7 to 314.3; on Switti (512×512 text-to-image), IGG improves GenEval from 0.62 to 0.64 and MJHQ FID from 9.50 to 7.09. Against diffusion baselines, VAR-d36-IGG (FID 2.56) beats DiT-XL/2-CFG (FID 3.04), LDM-CFG, and all ADM variants at 512×512, and VAR-d30-IGG (FID 1.92) beats DiT-XL/2-REPG (FID 2.17) at 256×256. In text-to-image, Switti-IGG (FID 7.09 on MJHQ) outperforms SDXL-CFG (FID 7.60), LlamaGen-CFG (FID 26.90), and HART-CFG (FID 5.80 on MJHQ but 20.90 on COCO vs. IGG's 16.90). The evenness/divergence analysis (Table 1) shows IGG closes the gap between SwAR and diffusion guidance: VAR-d36-IGG reduces evenness from 0.741 to 0.665 and increases divergence from 0.623 to 0.751, approaching EDM2-XXL levels (0.560/0.964). IGG also beats alternative guidance methods (EDS, PXP, PROG, REPG) when applied to ADM, confirming that the improvement is not merely an artifact of the SwAR backbone but reflects a genuinely better guidance formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classifier-free guidance (CFG, Ho & Salimans 2021) to the scale-wise autoregressive (SwAR) paradigm introduced by VAR (Tian et al. 2024), where guidance is applied to discrete token predictions rather than continuous noise/score estimates. The key conceptual departure is a diagnostic analysis showing that CFG in SwAR models produces guidance signals that are progressively dispersed and semantically misaligned as resolution increases—a failure mode absent in diffusion models—motivating a per-token attention-based reweighting of the guidance signal. The method repurposes self-attention not on model tokens but on the guidance vectors themselves, a novel application that subsumes uniform CFG as a special case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The evenness and divergence metrics provide the first quantitative diagnostic for understanding *why* CFG underperforms in SwAR relative to diffusion, and the finding that their equilibrium approximates the optimal guidance weight offers a practical shortcut for hyperparameter tuning. However, the performance gains are modest (FID 1.93→1.92 at 256×256, 2.61→2.56 at 512×512 on ImageNet), and the method has only been validated on VAR and Switti backbones, leaving open whether the misalignment diagnosis generalizes to other SwAR architectures (e.g., HART, LlamaGen) or to continuous-token variants. The ablation showing that naively mixing CFG and IGG degrades performance (FID 5.48) raises an open question about why guidance composition works well in diffusion but not in SwAR.
