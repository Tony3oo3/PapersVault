## Problem

Speculative decoding's speedup degrades sharply in high-concurrency production serving because verification compute becomes the dominant bottleneck, yet prior methods face an unresolved dilemma: static trees waste verification budget on low-confidence branches, while dynamic trees accumulate misjudgment errors and produce irregular batch shapes incompatible with serving kernels. No existing approach jointly optimizes global verification steps and per-step verification efficiency under a strict compute-bound budget.

## Input / Output

ECHO consumes a batch of B concurrent requests, each carrying a text prompt and a set of draft candidate tokens generated by an EAGLE-3-style drafter, subject to a global per-iteration verification cap K_max (determined at startup by sweeping batch sizes to locate the memory-to-compute-bound inflection point). It outputs accepted token sequences that are mathematically identical to the target model's autoregressive output, packed into a dense, kernel-compatible layout via sequence-level truncation and flatten-and-pack (no padding), so that both Attention and GEMM work scale with the actual pruning ratio rather than a padded maximum.

## Method

ECHO reformulates tree construction as a budgeted scheduling problem over a batch-level super-tree sharing a fixed verification cap. It employs sparse confidence gating restricted to a small set of high-fidelity 'sweet-spot' depths (root, target depth, and a few AUC-calibrated intermediate layers), where a binary signal triggers either an 'Extend' operation (deepen the tree when confidence is high) or a 'Truncate-to-Widen' operation (pivot to width when confidence is low). A unified elastic budget scheduler then applies a strict priority hierarchy: Priority 1 reallocates freed budget to extend the depth of other high-confidence requests in the batch, and Priority 2 opportunistically widens the truncated request's candidate set only when no depth extension remains available. This selective elasticity avoids the cumulative misjudgment of dense per-depth control while remaining kernel-compatible, and the entire pipeline is integrated into SGLang with specialized operators that handle ragged per-request trees without padding.

## Results

ECHO achieves a wall-time speedup range of 1.63×–5.35× across five benchmarks (HumanEval, GSM8K, CNN/DM, Alpaca, MT-Bench) at BS=1, setting a new SOTA. On the industrial-grade Qwen3-235B, ECHO attains a 2.02× average speedup, surpassing DDD (1.77×) by 14% and EAGLE-3 (1.69×) by 19%. In high-concurrency serving (BS=256), ECHO delivers 8% throughput gain on LLaMA-3.1-8B (10,703→11,551 tok/s) and 14.4% on Qwen3-235B (2,803→3,207 tok/s) over EAGLE-3, with gains up to 25% on H20 hardware.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECHO consistently outperforms all seven baselines (Lookahead, Sps, Medusa, Hydra, OPT-Tree, DDD, EAGLE-3) across every model scale and benchmark. Against the strongest static-tree baseline EAGLE-3, ECHO gains 19% average speedup on Qwen3-235B (2.02× vs 1.69×) and 6% on LLaMA-3.3-70B (4.70× vs 4.43×), with the gap widening on larger models where speculation is harder. Against the strongest dynamic-tree baseline DDD, ECHO leads by 14% on Qwen3-235B and 15.8% on Qwen3-32B, attributed to sparse gating avoiding the misjudgment accumulation of dense per-depth control. In high-concurrency serving, ECHO's elastic budget reallocation yields 14.4% throughput gain over EAGLE-3 on Qwen3-235B at BS=256, where EAGLE-3's fixed deep trees waste verification compute on low-confidence branches. ECHO also surpasses retrieval-based (Lookahead: 1.60× avg on Vicuna-13B) and training-based MLP methods (Medusa: 2.01×, Hydra: 2.46×) by a wide margin, confirming that tree-structure optimization under a fixed budget is the dominant factor in compute-bound regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECHO builds on the tree-based speculative decoding lineage (EAGLE-3, Medusa, SpecInfer) and the dynamic-tree line (DDD, OPT-Tree, TALON), but departs from both by reframing tree construction as a budget-constrained scheduling problem under a fixed verification cap rather than an unconstrained topology search. Its key conceptual departure is sparse confidence gating at depth-dependent 'sweet spots' (root, target depth, a few intermediate layers) instead of the dense per-depth or per-node control used by prior dynamic methods, motivated by the empirical finding that confidence discriminability is sharply depth-dependent. System-level schedulers like TETRIS and TurboSpec operate at the request level and treat the draft tree as a black box; ECHO is complementary, focusing on intra-batch token-level budget allocation and kernel-compatible ragged-tree execution within SGLang.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete production gap: well-regarded SD methods like EAGLE-3 can actually underperform vanilla autoregressive decoding at high concurrency on industrial-scale models (Qwen3-235B), a failure mode largely unreported in prior evaluations. The first integration of dynamic-tree SD into SGLang with specialized operators for irregular batch shapes bridges a real deployment barrier. Open questions include whether the warm-up calibration approach generalizes to highly non-stationary production traffic (online threshold adaptation is left as future work), whether the findings extend to self-speculative or two-model drafting paradigms with different draft/verify ratios, and whether the gains persist at 32K–128K ultra-long contexts beyond the 16K evaluated here.
