## Problem

Existing machine learning approaches to low-discrepancy sampling, notably Message-Passing Monte Carlo (MPMC), are limited to generating fixed-size point sets and cannot produce extensible sequences where every prefix maintains low discrepancy. Classical number-theoretic sequences (Sobol', Halton) are extensible but achieve higher discrepancy than ML-optimized sets. The paper addresses the gap of building the first ML-based framework for generating finite low-discrepancy sequences (LDS) with minimal prefix discrepancy across all lengths.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single integer index i ∈ {1,…,N} as input and produces a deterministic point X_i ∈ [0,1]^d, so that the full collection {X_1,…,X_N} forms a learned sequence with low discrepancy at every prefix length. The index is first expanded into a (1+2K)-dimensional sinusoidal feature vector exposing multiple frequency scales, analogous to how Halton uses base-b digit expansions or Sobol' uses Gray-coded direction numbers. The output sequence is fully deterministic and index-driven, preserving the standard QMC convention that the i-th point is a function of i alone, with no dependence on previously generated points.

## Method

NEUROLDS maps each index i through a K-band sinusoidal positional encoding into R^{1+2K}, then through an L-layer MLP with ReLU activations and a final sigmoid to produce a point in [0,1]^d. Training proceeds in two stages: first, supervised pre-training regresses the network onto a Sobol' reference sequence via MSE (with a 128-point burn-in), which provides an essential inductive bias to prevent degenerate collapse; second, unsupervised fine-tuning minimizes a weighted sum of closed-form L2-based discrepancy losses (star, symmetric, centered, etc.) evaluated over all sequence prefixes from length 2 to N. The discrepancy loss is differentiable and computed in O(dN²) time, with product-form kernels serving as tunable hyperparameters. Ablations show that the sinusoidal encoding bandwidth K, network depth, and pre-training are all critical: removing pre-training causes collapse to a corner, replacing the MLP with an LSTM or autoregressive GNN degrades performance or increases cost, and larger K stabilizes the discrepancy profile across prefixes.

## Results

In d=4, NEUROLDS achieves the lowest prefix discrepancy at every sequence length (N=100 to 10,000) across all three L2-based measures. For D2^star at N=10,000, NEUROLDS reaches 0.000213 versus 0.000266 for Sobol', 0.000266 for Halton, and 0.000268 for Scrambled Sobol' (mean over 32 scramblings). On the 8D Borehole integral, NEUROLDS attains the smallest or second-smallest absolute error for 11 of 13 tested N values, and on the 2D Black-Scholes PDE it achieves the lowest MSE (3.34×10⁻⁴ with D2^ctr) versus 4.04×10⁻⁴ for Sobol' and 4.24×10⁻⁴ for MC. In robot motion planning, NEUROLDS yields the highest RRT success rate at every passage width (e.g., 96.58% at width 0.64 vs. 73.04% for Sobol').

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEUROLDS outperforms all five baselines on the core discrepancy metric: at every prefix length from 100 to 10,000 in d=4, it achieves lower D2^sym, D2^star, and D2^ctr than Sobol', Halton, and Scrambled Sobol' (mean over 32 scramblings), with margins of roughly 15–25% at N=10,000. Against NM-Greedy (Nelder-Mead optimized), NEUROLDS wins on the 8D Borehole integral for 11 of 13 N values and on the 2D Black-Scholes PDE (3.34×10⁻⁴ vs. 4.72×10⁻⁴ MSE), though NM-Greedy narrowly edges it at N=100 on Borehole (0.0299 vs. 0.5765). Compared to MPMC, NEUROLDS addresses the fundamental extensibility limitation: MPMC achieves the lowest discrepancy only at its trained target N=1024 but degrades for other N, whereas NEUROLDS maintains low discrepancy across all prefix lengths without retraining. In robot motion planning, NEUROLDS requires 2.50× fewer samples than Sobol', 1.55× fewer than Halton, and 2.27× fewer than uniform sampling to match its average success rate. Halton consistently performs worst across all applications, confirming its known uniformity pathologies in moderate-to-high dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Message-Passing Monte Carlo (MPMC) paradigm (Rusch et al., 2024), which used graph neural networks to optimize fixed-size low-discrepancy point sets, by replacing the GNN with an index-conditioned MLP and shifting the objective from set-level to prefix-level discrepancy minimization. The sinusoidal positional encoding of the integer index is explicitly motivated by the digit-expansion structure of classical Halton and Sobol' sequences, but replaces fixed number-theoretic rules with learned flexible mappings. The two-stage training—supervised regression onto Sobol' followed by unsupervised discrepancy fine-tuning—draws on the broader trend of using classical constructions as inductive biases for learned samplers, but is the first to apply this recipe to the sequential (extensible) setting rather than the fixed-N set setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NEUROLDS is the first ML-based framework that produces extensible low-discrepancy sequences, filling a gap left by MPMC (which is limited to fixed-N sets) and offering a practical alternative to number-theoretic constructions that exhibit oscillatory discrepancy at non-power-of-two lengths. The demonstrated gains across QMC integration, robot motion planning, and scientific ML suggest broad applicability, and the weighted-kernel formulation provides a natural path toward anisotropic and non-uniform target distributions. Key open questions include scaling to very high dimensions (beyond d=8 tested here), whether theoretical discrepancy bounds can be established for the learned sequences, and whether the dependence on classical pre-training can be removed or replaced with self-supervised initialization.
