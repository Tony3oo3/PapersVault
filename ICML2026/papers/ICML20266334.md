## Problem

Standard cross-entropy treats numeric tokens as unstructured categories, penalizing a near-miss (e.g., predicting 4 instead of 3) identically to a far miss (predicting 7 instead of 3), so the training signal ignores the metric structure of numbers. Recent EMD-based losses incorporate distance but do not explicitly encourage local smoothness in the per-token error signal, leaving the model's behavior unstable in the immediate vicinity of the correct value. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a kernel-distribution objective that simultaneously aligns the predicted numeric distribution to the target and smooths the prediction-target residual over nearby values.

## Input / Output

The method operates on autoregressive LLMs and VLMs: text-only prompts for mathematical reasoning and arithmetic, and image-plus-text inputs for clock-time recognition and chart question answering. At each decoding position whose ground-truth token belongs to a precomputed numeric sub-vocabulary (tokens parseable as real values, typically N=10 for digit-level tokenizers or up to 1000 for multi-digit tokens), the model's full-vocabulary logits are restricted and renormalized to a numeric distribution, which is then compared to a one-hot target. The SMMD loss is zero at all non-numeric positions, so the method is fully compatible with standard text generation and requires no architectural modification.

## Method

SMMD constructs a distance-induced RBF kernel K over the numeric sub-vocabulary by mapping pairwise value gaps |v_i − v_j| to similarity weights, so numerically closer tokens receive higher kernel values. The first component, kernel MMD alignment, minimizes the quadratic form rᵀKr where r = p − q is the prediction-target residual, aligning the predicted numeric distribution to the one-hot target in an RKHS. The second component, smoothness regularization, applies the Dirichlet energy rᵀLr (where L = D − K is the graph Laplacian) to suppress high-frequency oscillations in the residual across nearby numeric values, encouraging locally coherent error profiles. The unified objective L_SMMD = rᵀ(K + αL)r is added to standard cross-entropy with a scalar weight λ, where α is set automatically via degree-based normalization (α = 1/(2d̄)). The kernel is precomputed once per tokenizer with negligible overhead, and the entire loss is differentiable through the softmax, making SMMD a plug-and-play training signal that adds only ~2–3% wall-clock overhead over CE.

## Results

SMMD consistently improves numerical accuracy across all four task categories and eight model backbones (0.5B–11B). On GSM8K, Qwen2.5-1.5B rises from 54.99% (CE) to 57.77% exact-match accuracy, and Llama3-8B improves from 55.72% to 57.70%; on SVAMP, Llama3-8B gains from 62.20% to 65.30%. On DeepMind-Math arithmetic, SMMD achieves the best accuracy on all four LLMs (e.g., Llama3-8B: 71.96% Acc, 1.16 MAE, R²=0.92) and the largest Clock-Time gain is on Ministral-3-3B (82.36%→97.56% Acc, Time Gap 37.36→8.07 min). On ChartQA, gains are more modest but SMMD still ties or leads on 3 of 4 VLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 77.02%→78.38%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMMD outperforms all three numeric-target baselines (GCE, NTL, NTIL) on the majority of model–task combinations. Against GCE (Wang et al., 2025), SMMD wins on GSM8K for all four LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5B: 57.77% vs. 52.69%) and on Clock-Time for 3 of 4 VLMs (Ministral-3-3B: 97.56% vs. 93.82%); GCE is competitive only on Llama-3.2-Vision Clock-Time (82.15% vs. 80.76%). Against NTL (Zausinger et al., 2025), SMMD achieves higher accuracy on GSM8K for all four LLMs (e.g., Llama3-8B: 57.70% vs. 48.12%) and on DeepMind-Math for all four LLMs (e.g., SmolLM3-3B: 64.06% vs. 60.02% Acc, 1.88 vs. 2.36 MAE). Against NTIL (Fei et al., 2025), SMMD leads on GSM8K for 3 of 4 models, on Arithmetic for all four, and on Clock-Time for all four VLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B: 74.30% vs. 69.46%); NTIL is the strongest single baseline on SmolLM3 GSM8K (70.13% vs. 70.74% for SMMD, a narrow margin). On ChartQA, all methods are within ~1–2% of each other, with SMMD achieving the best or tied-best accuracy on 3 of 4 VLMs, indicating the task is bottlenecked by visual grounding rather than the numeric training signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the classic kernel MMD framework (Gretton et al., 2012) from continuous feature-space distribution matching to a discrete, per-token supervised objective over a numeric sub-vocabulary, which is a novel instantiation. It departs from the recent EMD/Wasserstein-based line (NTL, NTIL) that directly penalizes transport cost between predicted and target distributions, instead working in RKHS with a distance-induced RBF kernel and adding a graph-Laplacian smoothness regularizer on the prediction-target residual. The smoothness term, drawn from spectral graph theory, is the key conceptual addition beyond plain MMD, and the paper explicitly contrasts its kernel-matching + local-consistency decomposition against target-reshaping (GCE) and sequence-level reconstruction (NTIL) approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SMMD offers a lightweight, architecture-agnostic training loss (~3% overhead) that consistently improves numeric accuracy across LLMs and VLMs, making it practically deployable for fine-tuning pipelines where numerical reliability matters (scientific, financial, educational). The ablation revealing complementary roles—MMD for global distribution alignment on reasoning tasks, smoothness for local consistency on arithmetic and time tasks—provides a useful design principle for future numeric-aware objectives. Key limitations include tokenizer-dependent distance (digit-level tokenization fragments multi-digit numbers), the assumption that numeric proximity is semantically meaningful (inapplicable to IDs or codes), and task-dependent hyperparameter sensitivity (λ, σ) that motivates adaptive selection in future work.
