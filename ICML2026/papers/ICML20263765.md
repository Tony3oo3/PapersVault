## Problem

Zeroth-order Hessian estimation for black-box objectives suffers from high variance in noisy, high-dimensional regimes, and existing classical estimators (Stein-type, central-difference) lack a unified theoretical framework explaining their relationships. Moreover, while the Hessian itself has been studied, the regularized inverse Hessian and its product with the gradient—essential for curvature-aware zeroth-order optimization—have received limited attention, leaving a gap between theory and practical second-order ZO algorithms.

## Input / Output

The method consumes K noisy function evaluations f(θ+μu_k; ξ) along random Gaussian directions per iteration (K is constant and independent of dimension d), plus a smoothing parameter μ and ridge regularization λ. With query reuse, it additionally aggregates NK historical function values from a rolling buffer of past iterations, increasing effective sample size without extra queries. The output is a rank-K Hessian approximation, its Woodbury-based regularized inverse, and a bias-corrected inverse Hessian-gradient product used directly for second-order parameter updates θ_{t+1} = θ_t − η·H̃⁻¹·∇̂F.

## Method

The paper reinterprets ZO Hessian approximation as the Hessian of a smoothed policy-optimization objective under a Gaussian sampling policy, yielding a unified identity ∇²F_μ(θ) = E_u[(f(θ+μu;ξ)−b)/μ² · (uuᵀ−I_d)] where the baseline b is free. Different baseline choices (zero, anchor f(θ;ξ), averaged) recover the classical one-point, two-point, three-point Stein estimators and the randomized central-difference estimator as special cases, establishing their first rigorous unification. ZoVH then introduces two variance-reduction mechanisms: (1) an averaged baseline b = E_u[f(θ+μu;ξ)] proven to be the unique unbiased choice for the rank-one form and the variance-optimal constant baseline (Thm 4.7), and (2) a query-reuse strategy that pools NK historical function values into the estimator, reducing variance by a factor of N without additional query cost. For practical curvature-aware optimization, the inverse Hessian is computed via the Woodbury matrix identity exploiting the rank-K structure of the estimator, and a leave-one-out bias correction is applied to the shared-sampling inverse Hessian-gradient product to eliminate self-interaction bias while keeping query cost at K per iteration.

## Results

On the black-box adversarial attack benchmark (MNIST CNN), ZoVH with query reuse (N=4) requires only 403±76 iterations to achieve a successful attack, a 4.04× speedup over Vanilla ZOO (1629±610 iterations). On synthetic function optimization (d=5000), ZoVH(N=4) achieves an average 22× speedup over Vanilla ZOO in convergence. For Hessian estimation accuracy, ZoVH(N=1) already delivers an 8× Frobenius norm error reduction over the randomized central-difference estimator on Quadratic and Rosenbrock functions, and ZoVH(N=4) achieves a 9× error reduction on the second fully-connected layer of a CNN trained on MNIST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZoVH consistently outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated task. Against Vanilla ZOO (Nesterov & Spokoiny, 2017), ZoVH(N=4) reduces adversarial attack iterations from 1629 to 403 (4.04× speedup) and achieves a 22× average convergence speedup on synthetic functions. Against HiZOO (Zhao et al., ICLR 2025), ZoVH(N=4) requires roughly 2.6× fewer iterations (403 vs. 1045) for the adversarial attack. Against ZoAR (Qiu et al., 2025), which shares the same policy-optimization foundation, ZoVH(N=4) further reduces iterations by ~25% (403 vs. 538), demonstrating the benefit of the Hessian-specific variance reduction. In Hessian estimation accuracy (Fig. 2 and Fig. 3), ZoVH(N=1) already surpasses the two-point Stein, three-point Stein, and randomized central-difference estimators from Balasubramanian & Ghadimi (2022) and Zhu (2022) by 3.4×–8× in Frobenius norm error on synthetic functions, and by up to 9× on CNN layers, with query reuse (N=4 or N=6) providing additional variance reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-step Policy Optimization (PO) lens previously applied to ZO gradient estimation (Qiu et al., 2025) to the Hessian setting, showing that classical Stein-type estimators (Balasubramanian & Ghadimi, 2022; Zhu, 2022) emerge as special cases of baseline selection in a unified PO framework. It departs from prior ZO Hessian work by extending beyond the Hessian itself to the regularized inverse Hessian and the bias-corrected inverse Hessian-gradient product, which are the quantities actually needed for curvature-aware second-order updates. The query-reuse mechanism is adapted from the ZOAR gradient estimator, and the Woodbury-based inverse computation follows standard low-rank matrix inversion techniques, but their combination in a ZO Hessian context with variance-optimal baselines is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for curvature-aware derivative-free optimization: the 22× speedup over Vanilla ZOO on synthetic benchmarks and 4× speedup in black-box adversarial attacks demonstrate that second-order ZO methods can be competitive with first-order ones at the same query budget. The unified PO framework provides a principled pathway for designing future estimators (e.g., via importance sampling with non-Gaussian proposals) rather than ad hoc finite-difference constructions. Open questions include whether the orthogonality assumption on random directions (needed for exact Woodbury inversion) holds well enough in moderate dimensions, how the method scales to very high-dimensional LLM fine-tuning beyond the OPT-125M experiments, and whether the N>1 query-reuse drift term becomes problematic for non-stationary or highly non-convex objectives.
