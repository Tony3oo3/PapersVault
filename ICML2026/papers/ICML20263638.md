## Problem

Existing continual personalization methods for diffusion models either assume static concepts requiring costly post-hoc weight fusion, or rely on magnitude/gradient-based heuristics that evaluate each parameter in isolation. These approaches ignore collective semantic redundancy among selected neurons, leading to parameter collisions and accelerated catastrophic forgetting when semantically similar concepts arrive sequentially. The paper addresses the absence of a principled, diversity-aware mechanism for selecting compact, non-redundant parameter subsets in fusion-free continual personalization.

## Input / Output

At each stage of the concept stream, the method consumes 5–6 few-shot reference images and a text prompt containing a new learnable token (e.g., <new_i>), with no access to prior datasets. It produces a sparse binary mask over the column indices of cross-attention key and value projection matrices (W_k, W_v) across all U-Net layers, which is then applied as a gradient mask during 500-step fine-tuning. The resulting model generates personalized images at standard SD1.5 resolution (512×512), updating on average only 0.41% of total parameters per concept with no post-hoc fusion required.

## Method

SCNS formulates cross-attention column selection as a constrained submodular maximization problem whose objective decomposes into a modular score (aggregating a gradient SNR task-relevance term, a Fisher-weighted preservation risk inverted via LogSumExp, and a zero-shot weight-norm safety score) plus a Facility Location-based span term that enforces diminishing-returns semantic coverage over the top-K dimensions of the concept's text embedding. A cost-aware greedy algorithm selects units by maximizing gain density (marginal gain divided by a cost that includes a base overhead and an overlap penalty for previously used neurons), terminating adaptively when density falls below a threshold τ rather than using a fixed budget. This mechanism automatically allocates more parameters to complex concepts while steering later concepts toward fresh, unused parameter subspaces, achieving a Pareto-optimal balance between plasticity, stability, and capacity sustainability without any model merging.

## Results

On the 8-concept continual personalization stream (SD1.5 backbone), SCNS achieves the best scores across all metrics: CLIP-T 0.3041, CLIP-I 0.7854, DINO-I 0.6216, while simultaneously attaining the lowest Task Forgetting Rates (TFR-CLIP 0.1183%, TFR-DINO 0.3439%). This represents a 14× reduction in CLIP-based forgetting and 9× reduction in DINO-based forgetting compared to the closest continual baseline (CNS: TFR-CLIP 1.6671%, TFR-DINO 3.0564%). SCNS achieves this while updating only 0.41% of total parameters per concept on average, with no post-hoc model fusion required.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCNS outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the merge-based methods, SCNS leads Custom Diffusion (0.7568 CLIP-I) by +2.86 points and Mix-of-Show (0.7603) by +2.51 points on image alignment, while also surpassing Orthogonal Adaptation (0.7207) by +6.47 points. Among continual learning baselines, the most direct comparison is with CNS (Liao et al., 2025), the prior state-of-the-art neuron-selection method: SCNS improves CLIP-I by +3.76 points (0.7854 vs 0.7478), DINO-I by +3.43 points (0.6216 vs 0.5873), and reduces TFR-CLIP by 14× (0.1183 vs 1.6671) and TFR-DINO by 9× (0.3439 vs 3.0564). CIDM achieves competitive CLIP-T (0.3029 vs 0.3041) but lags substantially on identity preservation (DINO-I 0.5255 vs 0.6216) and forgetting (TFR-DINO 1.7437 vs 0.3439). Continual Diffusion (C-LoRA) shows the lowest forgetting among regularization-based methods (TFR-CLIP 0.4782) but still trails SCNS by 4× on TFR-CLIP and 3.5× on TFR-DINO, while also scoring lower on all quality metrics. The sequential Custom Diffusion variant suffers the most severe forgetting (TFR-DINO 11.05%), confirming that dense cross-attention updates without structured selection are highly destructive in continual settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Concept Neuron Selection (CNS, Liao et al. 2025), which introduced sparse, fusion-free continual personalization by updating a subset of cross-attention columns, but relied on magnitude-based heuristics that evaluate each parameter independently. SCNS departs from this by formulating neuron selection as a constrained submodular maximization problem, drawing on the Facility Location objective from document summarization (Lin & Bilmes, 2011) and the 'Less is More' submodular attribution framework (Chen et al. 2024), while incorporating Fisher-weighted second-order risk (Kirkpatrick et al. 2017) for preservation. The broader lineage includes continual diffusion personalization methods (C-LoRA, CIDM) that use consolidation losses, and modular customization approaches (Mix-of-Show, Orthogonal Adaptation) that require post-hoc fusion—both of which SCNS avoids through its sparse, sequential, replay-free design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCNS demonstrates that principled submodular selection with cost-aware stopping can reduce task forgetting by over an order of magnitude (TFR-CLIP: 0.12 vs. 1.67 for CNS) while updating only 0.41% of parameters per concept, offering a practical, privacy-preserving path to continual personalization without data replay or model fusion. The zero-shot transfer to SD3's MM-DiT architecture (without hyperparameter retuning) suggests the approach generalizes across backbone topologies, though the absence of baseline comparisons there limits the claim. Key open questions include: (1) parameter saturation for very long concept streams where the low-risk subspace is exhausted, (2) the lack of inference-time multi-concept composition (attribute binding, layout control), and (3) the empirical tuning burden of multiple hyperparameters (λ coefficients, τ, α_ov) that lack a fully adaptive mechanism.
