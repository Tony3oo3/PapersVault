## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of Dense Associative Memory networks with exponential capacity (M = e^{αN}) focus almost exclusively on zero-temperature or energy-dominated regimes, leaving open how retrieval stability is governed by the competition between energy and entropy under thermal fluctuations. In particular, it remains unclear how the choice of similarity kernel (globally supported vs. compactly supported) qualitatively affects the finite-temperature retrieval phase boundary, and which aspects of retrieval robustness are imposed by high-dimensional spherical geometry versus modeling choices.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a continuous state vector x and M stored patterns ξ^μ, all constrained to the N-sphere (‖x‖² = N), along with a temperature T and exponential memory load α (where M = e^{αN}). The kernel sharpness is parameterized by β_net (LSE) or the rescaled inverse variance b = Nβ_net (LSR), which must scale as O(1/N) to keep basin widths macroscopic. The output is a phase boundary α_c(T) in the (α, T) plane, the equilibrium alignment φ(T) within the retrieval phase, and the disorder-averaged free energy density f = u(φ) − Ts(φ,q), with the critical point defined by the crossing of retrieval free energy and the REM-derived noise floor.

## Method

The paper applies the replica method to compute the disorder-averaged free energy of continuous DAMs on the N-sphere, decomposing it into a kernel-dependent internal energy density u(φ) and a kernel-independent geometric entropy s(φ,q) = ½[ln(1−q) + (q−φ²)/(1−q)] that arises purely from the spherical constraint. The saddle-point condition ∂f/∂q = 0 yields q = φ², fixing the thermal cloud tightness to the alignment. For the noise floor, the Random Energy Model is used to compute the maximum spurious alignment φ_max = √(2α) and the corresponding noise energy u_noise for each kernel. The retrieval-to-disorder transition is first-order, occurring where min_{φ,q}[u(φ) − Ts(φ,q)] = u_noise. The key qualitative difference emerges from kernel support: LSE (Gaussian) has a critical line at all α > 0, while LSR (Epanechnikov) has a support threshold α_th = ½(1−1/b)² below which no spurious patterns fall within the kernel's finite support, eliminating the noise floor entirely and guaranteeing perfect retrieval at any temperature. Monte Carlo Metropolis-Hastings simulations at N = 50 and N = 100 validate the theoretical phase boundaries.

## Results

Both LSE and LSR kernels achieve the same zero-temperature capacity α_c(0) = 0.5 on the N-sphere, confirming exponential storage as a geometric property. For LSE, a critical line α_c(T) exists at all loads α > 0, with equilibrium alignment φ_LSE(T) = ½[−T + √(T²+4)]. For LSR with b = 3.41, the support threshold is α_th = 0.25, below which retrieval is perfect at any temperature; above threshold, the critical line reaches T_max ≈ 0.22 at α = α_th. Monte Carlo validation at M = 148 patterns (α = 0.1, N = 50 and α = 0.05, N = 100) confirms the predicted α-independence of retrieval below the LSR threshold and the sharp alignment drop near T_c for LSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper extends two prior zero-temperature analyses to finite temperature, revealing qualitative differences absent in the original works. Compared to Ramsauer et al. (2021), which established α = 0.5 capacity for the LSE kernel only at T = 0, this paper shows that LSE retrieval always degrades above a finite T_c(α) for any α > 0 — there is no temperature-robust regime. Compared to Hoover et al. (2025), which introduced the LSR (Epanechnikov) kernel and its exact-retrieval property at T = 0, this paper identifies a finite-temperature support threshold α_th(b) = ½(1−1/b)² below which LSR retrieval is perfect at *any* temperature, a qualitative advantage with no LSE analog. The two kernels thus differ fundamentally: LSE provides thermal robustness across all loads but with ever-present interference, while LSR offers complete isolation from spurious-pattern noise below threshold. Both kernels match at the zero-temperature limit (α_c = 0.5), confirming that exponential capacity is kernel-independent and purely geometric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-temperature exponential-capacity results of Ramsauer et al. (2021) for LSE and Hoover et al. (2025) for LSR to finite temperature, using the replica method and Random Energy Model to derive explicit phase boundaries α_c(T) in the (α, T) plane. The geometric entropy calculation follows Gardner (1988) for isotropic clouds on the N-sphere, while the noise-floor analysis adapts Derrida's REM framework to the exponential-load regime. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the clean separation of kernel-dependent energy from kernel-independent geometric entropy, which prior analyses (e.g., Lucibello & Mezard 2024) did not isolate, and the identification of a support threshold for compactly supported kernels that has no analog in globally supported kernels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LSR support threshold—below which retrieval is perfect at any temperature because no spurious patterns fall within the kernel's finite support—is a qualitatively new regime with no counterpart in LSE, offering a principled robustness-capacity trade-off for kernel design in attention-like architectures. However, the analysis relies on a Gaussian approximation for inter-pattern alignments whose tails differ from the exact N-sphere distribution, and Monte Carlo validation is limited to N ≤ 100 due to the exponential pattern count, leaving finite-size effects potentially significant near the predicted transition. Open questions include whether the first-order transition character survives beyond the replica-symmetric ansatz, and how these thermodynamic limits translate to practical finite-width attention mechanisms in Transformers.
