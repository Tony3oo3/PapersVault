## Problem

In multi-task reinforcement learning, inherent differences in task difficulty cause asynchronous convergence, forcing the shared critic network to simultaneously model highly divergent value distributions. This representational strain on the critic emerges as a major performance bottleneck, as evidenced by MTRL scaling laws showing performance is far more sensitive to critic parameter scale than actor scale. Existing MTRL methods address task interference through architectural or gradient-based techniques but overlook this critic overload caused by heterogeneous learning dynamics.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a state vector from the MetaWorld robotic manipulation environment concatenated with a one-hot task identifier, and outputs a continuous action sampled from a Gaussian policy. A key structural detail is that the critic's effective input distribution shifts over training: as tasks graduate to the imitation refinement phase, their Q-value estimation is removed from the critic's optimization objective, and the critic is periodically reinitialized. Each task maintains an independent replay buffer, and during the refinement phase only successful trajectories are stored, ensuring the imitation data remains expert-quality.

## Method

HyMTRL introduces a phased policy evolution strategy that divides each task's learning into a reinforcement exploration phase (standard SAC off-policy RL) and an imitation refinement phase (MSE behavior cloning on successful trajectories). A data-driven mastery assessment monitors the empirical success rate within each task's replay buffer; when it exceeds a threshold (default 0.9), the task transitions to Phase II and is removed from the critic's loss, freeing critic capacity for remaining difficult tasks. A critic reset mechanism periodically reinitializes the shared critic parameters while preserving the actor network and all replay buffer contents, restoring plasticity as the task distribution shifts. The framework is method-agnostic and integrates as a drop-in wrapper around any existing MTRL baseline (MTSAC, MHSAC, PCGrad, SM, PaCo, MOORE), requiring no architectural changes or additional parameters.

## Results

On MetaWorld-MT50, MOORE augmented with HyMTRL achieves the best overall average success rate of 86.2±3.4% (k=50), up from 68.1±2.1% without HyMTRL. Across all six baselines, HyMTRL yields consistent gains, with the largest improvements on the hardest tasks: for example, MOORE's bottom-10 success rate jumps from 0.8% to 37.3%, and MTSAC's from 0.0% to 0.8%. On the MT10 setting, MHSAC w/ HyMTRL reaches 80.1% average success rate versus 62.3% without. The gains are method-agnostic and grow with the number of tasks, confirming that alleviating critic overload is the key bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyMTRL is evaluated as a plug-in on six MTRL baselines (MTSAC, MHSAC, PCGrad, SM, PaCo, MOORE) and improves every one of them on both MT10 and MT50. The largest absolute gain is on MOORE, where HyMTRL lifts the MT50 average success rate from 68.1% to 86.2% (+18.1 pp) and the bottom-10 (hardest tasks) rate from 0.8% to 37.3%. Among the lighter-weight baselines, PCGrad benefits most on MT50 (52.9% → 73.0%, +20.1 pp), while SM and PaCo see more modest gains (~15 pp). Notably, even the strongest baseline without HyMTRL (MOORE at 68.1%) is outperformed by several HyMTRL-augmented lighter baselines (e.g., PCGrad w/ HyMTRL at 73.0%), demonstrating that the phased policy evolution strategy is more impactful than architectural complexity alone. The paper positions HyMTRL as complementary rather than competitive: it does not replace any baseline but layers on top to reduce critic representational strain, with no additional model parameters or computational overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

HyMTRL builds on the SAC actor-critic framework and positions itself as a complementary, architecture-agnostic layer atop existing MTRL methods (MTSAC, MHSAC, PCGrad, SM, PaCo, MOORE) rather than a competing approach. Conceptually, it departs from prior MTRL work that addresses task interference through architectural modularization, gradient surgery, or task scheduling by instead targeting the critic's representational capacity directly: mastered tasks are removed from the critic's optimization objective via a transition to imitation learning. The critic reset mechanism adapts the single-task reset idea of Nikishin et al. (2022) to the multi-task setting, with the key distinction of resetting only the critic while preserving the actor. The motivation is grounded in recent MTRL scaling-law evidence (McLean et al., 2025) showing that performance is far more sensitive to critic size than actor size, reframing the problem as one of workload management rather than parameter scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a plug-in framework that consistently improves six diverse MTRL baselines without altering their architecture or adding parameters, HyMTRL offers a practical, low-overhead remedy for the critic bottleneck that plagues multi-task RL at scale. The bottom-k analysis showing particularly large gains on the hardest tasks suggests the framework could be especially valuable in settings where a few difficult tasks dominate overall performance. Key limitations include dependence on binary success signals for mastery assessment (requiring manual reward thresholds in reward-only environments) and the need for per-environment tuning of the reset interval. Open questions include whether adaptive mastery criteria and reset schedules can eliminate manual hyperparameter tuning, and whether the phased RL-to-IL transition generalizes beyond MetaWorld's manipulation tasks to more continuous or high-dimensional control domains.
