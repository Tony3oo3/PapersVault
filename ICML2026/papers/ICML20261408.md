## Problem

Vision-language models tend to exploit textual shortcuts rather than grounding reasoning in visual evidence, and reinforcement learning for VLMs is bottlenecked by the scarcity of scalable, verifiable rewards outside domains with explicit programmatic checkers. This paper asks how to obtain abundant, intrinsically verifiable reinforcement signals for VLMs without relying on human preferences, external verifiers, or LLM-as-a-judge heuristics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261408/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and applies a corruption function (rotation by 0°/90°/180°/270°, 2×2 or 3×3 grid permutation, contrastive augmentation, or quadrant patch extraction) to produce a context-target pair, which is paired with a text prompt asking the model to predict the corruption. The model outputs a natural-language reasoning path ending in a discrete prediction (angle, permutation index, yes/no, or quadrant label), which is scored against the unambiguous ground truth to yield a binary reward. For downstream evaluation, the same trained model accepts standard image-plus-question inputs and produces free-form text answers or captions.

## Method

SSL4RL reformulates four classic SSL pretext tasks—rotation prediction, jigsaw puzzles, contrastive discrimination, and patch position prediction—as RL environments where the corruption defines the state, the ground-truth target defines the verifiable reward, and the policy generates reasoning trajectories. Optimization uses GRPO (Grouped Reinforcement Policy Optimization) with KL regularization against a reference SFT policy, group size 5, and clipped policy-gradient updates, requiring no human labels or external judges. A key design finding is a 'Goldilocks' difficulty principle: task difficulty must match model capacity (e.g., 3B models benefit from easier tasks while 7B models benefit from harder variants like mask identification or hard-negative contrastive). The paper further proposes a stage-wise task combination schedule (Contrastive → Position/Rotation → Jigsaw) that outperforms both individual tasks and naive simultaneous mixing by following a gradual difficulty progression.

## Results

On six VQA benchmarks (MMBench, SEED-Bench, V*, RealWorldQA, BLINK, MME-RealWorld), SSL4RL-Position achieves the best average score of 60.39%, improving over the Qwen2.5-VL-3B base model (53.36%) by +7.03%. The largest per-benchmark gains are on V* (+8.90%) and RealWorldQA (+9.55%). On the 7B model, SSL4RL-Rotation reaches 69.17% average (+3.95% over base), with the strongest gains on BLINK (+8.83%) and V* (+7.86%). On open-ended image captioning (CapArena), SSL4RL-Contrastive achieves the highest average score of 56.45 vs. 48.31 for the base model (+8.14 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Qwen2.5-VL-3B base model, all SSL4RL variants except Contrastive deliver substantial gains, with Position and Rotation leading at +7.03% and +6.01% average respectively. Compared to the Golden-3B oracle (VLM-R1 trained with direct golden-answer rewards on 60% of the benchmark), SSL4RL-Rotation achieves 81.35% vs. 84.93% on MMBench and SSL4RL-Position reaches 69.80% vs. 73.21% on SEED-Bench, closing most of the gap without any labeled downstream data. On the cross-architecture test with Gemma3-4B, SSL4RL-Rotation improves MMBench from 78.30% to 81.38% (+3.08%), confirming the method generalizes beyond the Qwen family. The Contrastive task is the weakest performer on vision-language reasoning (only +0.75% average) but becomes competitive on ImageNet classification (89.51% on Choice-20 vs. 85.22% base), indicating task–benchmark alignment matters. Overall, SSL4RL consistently outperforms the base model across all evaluated benchmarks and model scales, while remaining within 2–4 points of the oracle-reward baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the single-task approach of Jigsaw-R1 (Wang et al., 2025), which first demonstrated that a visual pretext task (jigsaw puzzles) can serve as a verifiable RL reward for VLMs, into a unified framework spanning four SSL objectives (rotation, jigsaw, contrastive, position prediction). The optimization backbone is GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), the same policy-gradient method that powered DeepSeekMath's verifier-driven RL for math and code. Conceptually, the paper repurposes SSL tasks that were historically used for pre-training representation learning (Gidaris et al., 2018; Noroozi & Favaro, 2016; Chen et al., 2020; Doersch et al., 2015) as post-training reward signals, shifting their role from shaping representations to driving reasoning alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSL4RL offers a scalable, label-free reward source for VLM RL that avoids the biases and adversarial vulnerabilities of LLM-as-a-judge signals, which is practically important as VLMs expand into domains without programmatic verifiers. The Goldilocks finding—that task difficulty must match model capacity (harder tasks help 7B but hurt 3B)—provides a concrete design principle for practitioners choosing RL curricula. Open questions remain: the stage-wise task scheduling is only a preliminary heuristic, dynamic reward weighting and meta-learning for task selection are unexplored, and it is unclear whether the framework extends to video, audio, or other modalities. The relatively modest gains on 7B models (3.95% average) compared to 3B (7.03%) also suggest diminishing returns at scale that warrant further investigation.
