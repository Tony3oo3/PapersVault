## Problem

Large Reasoning Models generate explicit Chain-of-Thought trajectories, yet enabling them to self-evaluate correctness without external supervision remains unsolved. Existing white-box methods (max softmax, entropy, perplexity) collapse the entire reasoning process into a single-step output distribution, leaving the rich layerwise evolution of hidden states during CoT generation unexploited. This paper asks whether the geometric dynamics of layerwise hidden-state trajectories themselves encode a principled, training-free signal for distinguishing correct from incorrect reasoning.

## Input / Output

GeoR requires white-box access to the hidden state of every CoT reasoning token at every transformer layer (a K×L×d tensor collected between  tokens), which is mean-pooled per layer to yield L vectors q̄ⁱ. It also consumes the token-level log-probabilities of the generated reasoning sequence. The output is a single dimensionless scalar score: higher values indicate more reliable reasoning, and the score is directly comparable across models and tasks because the geometric path-length terms are normalized by the total trajectory displacement, making them commensurable with the entropy-based difficulty penalty.

## Method

GeoR decomposes reasoning fidelity into two complementary, parameter-free components. First, Geometric Evolution computes the first-order (D₁ = Σ‖Δq̄ⁱ‖) and second-order (D₂ = Σ‖Δ²q̄ⁱ‖) layerwise hidden-state variations, then normalizes their sum by the total displacement ‖q̄ᴸ − q̄⁰‖ to obtain a dimensionless 'normalized geometric drive' that captures both the path length and curvature of the reasoning trajectory in representation space. Second, Difficulty-Aware Calibration subtracts the average negative log-likelihood (cross-entropy) of the reasoning tokens, penalizing trajectories where the model was uncertain at each step. The key insight is that correct reasoning manifests as coherent, decisive geometric progress along network depth, while errors produce stagnation or chaotic evolution; the entropy term disambiguates cases where large geometric steps reflect confused exploration rather than genuine progress. The entire computation is a single forward pass with millisecond-level overhead, requiring no training, no additional sampling, and no external supervision.

## Results

Across eight LRMs (1.5B–70B) and seven benchmarks, GeoR consistently achieves the highest AUROC, AUPR, and lowest FPR@95 among all eleven baselines. On GSM8K with R1-Qwen-1.5B, GeoR reaches 77.28 AUROC / 91.42 AUPR / 72.20 FPR@95, surpassing the strongest white-box baseline (CoE-R at 74.17 AUROC) by over 3 points. On MMLU with R1-Qwen-14B, GeoR attains 79.13 AUROC and 94.84 AUPR, outperforming Entropy (78.17 AUROC) and MaxProb (78.20 AUROC). GeoR also generalizes to multimodal (Geometry3K: 69.41 AUROC) and multilingual (MGSM Chinese: 71.04 AUROC vs. 65.14 for Entropy) settings, while incurring only 1.2×10⁻³ s overhead versus 151–471 s for sampling-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoR outperforms all eleven baselines across every model, benchmark, and metric reported. The closest competitors are CoE-R/CoE-C (Wang et al., 2025a) and Entropy/MaxProb (Huang et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2025a): on GSM8K (R1-Qwen-1.5B), GeoR leads CoE-R by 3.11 AUROC points (77.28 vs. 74.17) and Entropy by 5.42 points (77.28 vs. 71.86). On MMLU (R1-Qwen-14B), GeoR achieves 79.13 AUROC versus 78.17 for Entropy and 78.20 for MaxProb, with a notably lower FPR@95 (72.12 vs. 72.42/71.69). Sampling-based methods—Internal Consistency (Xie et al., 2024) and Forking Tokens (Bigelow et al., 2025)—are both outperformed on accuracy and are 100–400× slower (151 s and 471 s vs. 1.2 ms). Verbal self-evaluation (Li et al., 2024) performs near-random (≈51 AUROC), confirming systematic over-confidence. Length-normalized Entropy (Malinin & Gales, 2021) and Temperature Scaling (Shih et al., 2023) track closely with MaxProb/PPL and are consistently surpassed by GeoR by 3–6 AUROC points. Perplexity (Si et al., 2023) similarly lags GeoR by 4–6 AUROC points across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoR departs from the dominant paradigm in LRM self-evaluation, where white-box methods (softmax confidence, perplexity, entropy, temperature scaling) collapse the entire reasoning process into a single-step output distribution, and black-box methods prompt the model for verbal confidence scores. The key conceptual departure is modeling reasoning quality along the *layer* (depth) dimension of the transformer rather than the *token* (temporal) dimension, using first- and second-order hidden-state variations as geometric proxies for reasoning progress. This repurposes the well-studied phenomenon that internal representations encode correctness signals (Azaria & Mitchell, 2023; Liu et al., 2023) but applies it to the *trajectory* of layerwise updates during CoT generation rather than to a single final-layer probe, and adds a difficulty-aware cross-entropy calibration to normalize geometric magnitudes against intrinsic query uncertainty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoR offers a training-free, label-free, millisecond-level self-evaluation signal that generalizes across model scales (1.5B–70B), architectures (Qwen, Llama, Qwen-VL), training paradigms (SFT, distillation, RL/GRPO), and modalities (text, vision-language), making it a practical drop-in for real-time reliability monitoring where sampling-based baselines are infeasible. The layerwise-vs-tokenwise ablation (token-wise dynamics perform near-random) provides a useful negative result that redirects attention from surface-level token dynamics to depth-wise representational refinement. Open questions include whether the geometric signal remains discriminative as reasoning chains grow substantially longer (e.g., multi-step agentic tasks), whether the method transfers to non-CoT architectures, and whether the modest absolute AUROC (60–77% range) is sufficient for high-stakes deployment without additional calibration.
