## Problem

Autoregressive LLMs applied to offline black-box optimization generate designs left-to-right, making them unable to capture the bidirectional dependencies inherent in structured design problems (e.g., DNA sequences where each nucleotide is constrained by both its prefix and suffix). Additionally, diffusion LLMs pretrained on natural text face a domain gap when confronted with the heterogeneous signals in BBO—natural-language prompts, structured designs, and scalar labels—requiring explicit adaptation mechanisms.

## Input / Output

The method takes a unified prompt consisting of a natural-language task description (specifying the optimization objective and output format) concatenated with 7 few-shot design-label pairs drawn from a fixed offline pool of 500 samples, all structured with explicit delimiter tokens (|design-start|/|design-end| for designs, |label-start|/|label-end| for labels). The output is a single candidate design—either a discrete DNA sequence (length 8 or 10) or a continuous robot morphology vector (56 or 60 dimensions rendered as signed floats with three decimal places)—whose label must exceed all labels in the prompt context. At inference, 128 unique candidates are generated via greedy single-pass unmasking with context resampling from the offline pool.

## Method

DiBO adapts the LLADA-8B-INSTRUCT diffusion LLM to offline BBO through a three-stage pipeline: (1) Domain Adaptation performs joint masked-token prediction over both prompt and response, teaching the model the semantic roles of delimiter tokens and the BBO input format; (2) Supervised Fine-Tuning optimizes only masked-response tokens to instill an inductive bias toward high-label design regions; (3) Reinforcement Learning uses a one-step unmasking approximation for log-probability computation, with rewards defined as label improvements (y(o) − y(q)) and a uniform old-policy assumption that eliminates importance-sampling ratios and KL regularization. Prompt-response pairs are constructed via RBF-kernel similarity matching to ensure local semantic consistency between context and target, and the RL dataset is balanced to include both positive and negative reward signals. The key architectural insight is that diffusion's iterative bidirectional denoising naturally models non-sequential structural dependencies that left-to-right AR generation cannot capture, yielding up to 0.080 normalized score gains over a matched AR backbone on discrete sequence tasks.

## Results

On Design-Bench (100th-percentile normalized oracle score over 128 candidates, averaged over 8 seeds), DiBO achieves a mean score of 0.883 with a median rank of 1.0 across four tasks. DiBO attains the best score on three of four tasks: Ant Morphology (0.932), TF Bind 8 (0.946), and TF Bind 10 (0.741), while trailing the best baseline (ExPT, 0.950) by 0.038 on D'Kitty Morphology (0.912). The largest margin is on TF Bind 8, where DiBO surpasses the strongest baseline (Grad-EI, 0.864) by 0.082. Training requires only 1.5 H100 GPU hours for discrete tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiBO outperforms all 13 baselines on at least three of four tasks, and beats every single baseline on all four tasks except ExPT on D'Kitty Morphology (0.912 vs. 0.950, a 0.038 gap). Against the strongest forward-method baselines, DiBO leads Grad-EI by 0.082 on TF Bind 8 and by 0.188 on Ant Morphology, while surpassing COMs, ICT, MATCH-OPT, and UniSO-T by 0.05–0.35 across tasks. Among inverse/generative baselines, DiBO exceeds ExPT (the closest competitor) on Ant (0.932 vs. 0.929), TF Bind 8 (0.946 vs. 0.810), and TF Bind 10 (0.741 vs. 0.703), and dominates OPRO, BONET, GTG, DDOM, MIN, CbAS, CMA-ES, and MCTS-transfer by 0.05–0.43 on their respective best tasks. The matched diffusion-vs.-AR ablation (Table 2) further confirms that the bidirectional diffusion backbone contributes an additional 0.02–0.08 gain over an otherwise identical autoregressive pipeline, particularly on discrete DNA tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-for-BBO line of work (OPRO, BONET, UniSO-T) by replacing autoregressive backbones with diffusion LLMs, motivated by the bidirectional dependencies in design problems like DNA sequence generation. It builds directly on LLADA-8B-INSTRUCT (Nie et al., 2025) as the pretrained base and borrows the one-step unmasking log-probability approximation from d1 (Zhao et al., 2025) for efficient RL, along with the uniform old-policy assumption from off-policy RL work (Yan et al., 2025; Fu et al., 2025). The key departure from the closest prior diffusion-LLM-for-BBO work (Yuan et al., 2026), which queries a frozen model via MCTS at inference time, is a full training pipeline—domain adaptation with delimiter tokens, supervised fine-tuning, and reward-based RL—that internalizes BBO-specific structure into the model weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the extreme training efficiency (1.5 H100 GPU hours for discrete tasks) combined with SOTA performance on Design-Bench in the small-data regime (n=500), making diffusion-LLM-based BBO accessible without large compute budgets. The matched diffusion-vs-AR ablation (Table 2) provides clean evidence that bidirectional modeling helps specifically on structured sequence tasks, which could guide model-architecture choices for other design domains. Limitations include underperformance on D'Kitty Morphology, mixed results on the additional RNA tasks (where baselines sometimes win on Top-K metrics), and the reliance on a single 8B-scale model; whether the bidirectional advantage persists at larger scales or in truly high-dimensional continuous spaces remains open.
