## Problem

Existing multi-modal ReID methods built on CNNs and ViTs exhibit an inherent low-frequency bias, causing them to overemphasize coarse cues like color and illumination while under-representing mid- and high-frequency structures that encode geometric, textural, and identity-discriminative details. This spectral imbalance produces incomplete frequency representations and unstable cross-modal alignment across RGB, NIR, and TIR inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263711/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes aligned RGB, NIR, and TIR images (resized to 256×128 for RGBNT201 or 128×256 for RGBNT100/MSVR310) and processes them through a single shared CLIP-initialized ViT backbone. It outputs a concatenated fused feature embedding (from all three modality streams after cross-modal alignment) used for identity classification and triplet-based metric learning, ultimately producing a match score between gallery and query identities.

## Method

FUSE reformulates multi-modal ReID as a two-stage frequency-domain process. The Spectral Decomposition Module (SDM) applies a 2D DFT to ViT token features, partitions the spectrum into low/mid/high-frequency annular regions using learnable radial boundaries (softmax over three parameters), and enhances each band with a matched convolutional operator (dilated 3×3 for low, standard 3×3 for mid, depthwise 3×3 for high) before reconstructing via IDFT and residual channel-attention modulation. The Cross-Modal Alignment Module (CAM) then performs symmetric all-to-one multi-head cross-attention, where each modality's tokens act as Query and the other two modalities' concatenated tokens form Key/Value, enabling single-pass complementary aggregation. Training combines identity cross-entropy, triplet loss, and a novel frequency-consistency loss that minimizes pairwise cosine distance between GAP-pooled amplitude spectra across modalities, with λ_freq=0.1 as the optimal regularization weight.

## Results

On RGBNT201, FUSE achieves 81.4% mAP and 86.1% Rank-1, setting a new state-of-the-art across all ranking metrics (R-5: 91.5%, R-10: 93.8%). On the large-scale RGBNT100 vehicle dataset, FUSE reaches 88.5% mAP and 96.9% Rank-1. On MSVR310, FUSE attains full SOTA with 50.1% mAP and 65.7% Rank-1, outperforming the previous best by 0.9% mAP and 3.3% R-1 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUSE outperforms all 32 compared baselines on every metric across all three datasets. On RGBNT201, FUSE surpasses the previous SOTA IDEA (80.2% mAP, 82.1% R-1) by 1.2% mAP and 4.0% R-1, and exceeds TOP-ReID (72.3% mAP, 76.6% R-1) by a substantial 9.1% mAP and 9.5% R-1. On RGBNT100, FUSE leads in mAP (88.5%) over IDEA (87.2%) by 1.3%, though its R-1 (96.9%) is marginally below DeMo (97.6%). On MSVR310, FUSE achieves full SOTA, beating DeMo by 0.9% mAP (50.1% vs 49.2%) and IDEA by 3.3% R-1 (65.7% vs 62.4%). In missing-modality stress tests on RGBNT201, FUSE maintains an average mAP of 49.9% and R-1 of 50.7%, outperforming TOP-ReID (44.4% mAP, 45.4% R-1) by 5.5% and 5.3% respectively, confirming robustness under sensor degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUSE carves new ground at the intersection of two existing lines of multi-modal ReID research: spatial-domain fusion (PFNet, TOP-ReID, DeMo, IDEA) and frequency-domain interaction modeling (FDNM, MFENet, EDITOR, TIENet). It departs from spatial methods by arguing that CNN/ViT backbones inherently exhibit low-frequency bias, and from prior frequency-domain methods by replacing fixed or pre-defined decompositions (wavelet, binary Fourier splits) with a learnable radial band allocation and an explicit cross-modal spectral energy alignment loss. The architecture repurposes standard components—DFT, dilated/depthwise convolutions, multi-head cross-attention—into a two-stage spectral disentanglement-and-alignment pipeline on a shared CLIP-initialized ViT backbone, rather than introducing a fundamentally new backbone or fusion operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FUSE offers an interpretable frequency-domain lens for multi-modal ReID, making cross-modal alignment explicit in spectral space rather than implicit in spatial correlation, which could inform future work on other multi-spectral matching tasks (e.g., SAR-optical, LiDAR-camera). The adaptive band allocation and frequency-consistency regularization are lightweight additions that outperform heavier spatial-fusion baselines while using fewer parameters (59.3M) and FLOPs (24.7G), suggesting practical deployability. Open questions include whether the three-band radial decomposition generalizes to more than three modalities or to non-image sensors, whether the learnable band boundaries remain stable under domain shift, and whether the frequency-domain framing transfers to single-modal ReID or other retrieval tasks.
