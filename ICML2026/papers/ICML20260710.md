## Problem

Full-duplex speech language models achieve natural real-time interactivity (handling interruptions, backchannels, and pauses) but suffer from poor factuality due to limited speech training data. Integrating retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) into such systems is challenging because retrieval introduces latency that conflicts with the strict real-time constraints of continuous speech interaction. This paper addresses how to perform asynchronous knowledge retrieval within a full-duplex speech model without interrupting the conversation flow.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a continuous user speech stream encoded by the Mimi codec at 12.5 Hz frame rate (8 audio tokens per step via RQ-Transformer) and produces a simultaneous model speech stream in the same format, operating in full duplex (listening and speaking concurrently). A 1B streaming ASR model with 0.5-second latency transcribes user speech into text for the retrieval back end, while the model's own text channel predictions provide the assistant-side context. The retrieval back end returns a text reference document (typically under 50 words) that is compressed 4× by ARC-Encoder and injected as additive embeddings over a fixed number of temporal steps.

## Method

Moshi^R_G extends the 7B full-duplex Moshi model with a special <ret> trigger token in the text channel; when predicted, it asynchronously invokes a retrieval back end (LLM or web search) while the front-end Moshi continues generating pre-RAG filler content (e.g., 'Let me check that for you...'). The key insight is exploiting the natural 'keyword delay'—the temporal gap between response onset and delivery of the actual answer—which is typically 3+ seconds in existing speech LMs, providing a window for retrieval to complete within 2 seconds. Retrieved references are encoded by a frozen ARC-Encoder (4× compression) and injected additively into the temporal Transformer input via a one-layer projection, summed with existing text and speech embeddings over l consecutive steps. Training uses ~1.9M synthetic multi-turn conversations generated by three role-assigned Gemma 3 27B LLMs (user, Moshi, reference) across three interaction-style variants, with retrieval delay sampled from a distribution that guarantees at least 1 second of buffer before key content. The architecture is retrieval-back-end agnostic, enabling plug-and-play swapping between LLMs (Gemma, GPT-4.1, Mistral, Gemini) and search engines (Tavily) at inference time without retraining.

## Results

On the four speech QA benchmarks, Moshi^R_G with a Gemma 3 27B retrieval back end achieves 80.3% (LlamaQ), 67.2% (WebQ), 69.6% (TriviaQA), and 36.3% (HaluEval) response accuracy, representing a substantial improvement over vanilla Moshi (62.3%, 26.6%, 22.8%, 10.5% respectively). Swapping to a GPT-4.1 back end without retraining lifts scores to 80.6%, 68.9%, 78.2%, and 51.3%, outperforming all compared speech LMs except GPT-4o Audio. The system maintains a low end-to-end keyword delay (E2EKD) of 3.1 s and a computation cost of 0.37 FLOPs/s, comparable to other 7B-scale models. On Full-Duplex-Bench, Moshi^R_G achieves lower takeover rates than vanilla Moshi across all tracks while preserving low interruption latency (1.02 s vs. 0.26 s for vanilla Moshi).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Moshi^R_G (Gemma back end) substantially outperforms vanilla Moshi on every QA benchmark (e.g., 69.6% vs. 22.8% on TriviaQA, 36.3% vs. 10.5% on HaluEval), confirming that the RAG mechanism—not merely fine-tuning on RAG-style data—drives the gain. Against other publicly released speech LMs, Moshi^R_G (Gemma) beats Kimi-Audio on LlamaQ (80.3 vs. 70.2) and TriviaQA (69.6 vs. 62.1) but trails it on HaluEval (36.3 vs. 43.2); it also surpasses Qwen2.5-Omni, Baichuan-Audio, LLaMA-Omni2, and Step-Audio-Chat on most benchmarks. The only model that consistently outperforms Moshi^R_G is GPT-4o Audio, which leads by 7.7–21.4 points across the four QA sets. When the retrieval back end is upgraded to GPT-4.1, Moshi^R_G closes the gap considerably (e.g., 78.2% on TriviaQA vs. GPT-4o's 90.6%) and outperforms all other compared speech LMs. On Full-Duplex-Bench, Moshi^R_G achieves lower takeover rates than vanilla Moshi in all four tracks (e.g., 0.32 vs. 0.99 on Pause-TOR_s) while maintaining lower interruption latency than Freeze-Omni (1.02 s vs. 1.41 s) and Gemini (1.02 s vs. 1.18 s), preserving the real-time interactivity advantage of full-duplex systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Moshi (Defossez et al., 2024), a full-duplex speech language model, by introducing an asynchronous retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) mechanism that exploits the natural temporal gap between response onset and key content delivery. It departs from concurrent RAG-for-speech work (StreamRAG, KAME) by being the first to support full-duplex interaction with dynamically triggered, context-aware retrieval rather than fixed-interval or non-duplex settings. The modular architecture—separating a 7B full-duplex front end, a 1B streaming ASR, and a swappable text-based retrieval back end—builds on the RQ-Transformer and Mimi codec infrastructure of Moshi while adding ARC-Encoder for reference compression and a special ⟨ret⟩ trigger token for asynchronous invocation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that full-duplex interactivity and factual grounding are not mutually exclusive, opening a path toward voice assistants that are both responsive and reliable. The plug-and-play retrieval back-end design (LLM-based or search-based) and the out-of-domain generalization to mathematical reasoning suggest a broader tool-use paradigm for full-duplex models. Key limitations remain: the retrieval trigger is learned entirely from synthetic data without explicit query-difficulty modeling, there is a persistent 3–6% information loss between retrieved references and final spoken responses, and performance is highly sensitive to ASR errors (up to 15% accuracy impact). Future directions include RL-based retrieval decisions, diverse tool selection, and robustness to retrieval failures.
