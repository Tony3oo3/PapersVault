## Problem

Biological foundation models predominantly repurpose general-purpose architectures (e.g., Transformers) without accounting for the intrinsic structural and functional properties of genomic and proteomic sequences, resulting in suboptimal inductive biases. Unlike NLP, biology lacks established design principles because its underlying 'grammar' is designed by nature rather than humans, and existing NAS methods are confined to homogeneous spaces or at most two-way hybrid combinations, failing to explore the open-ended composition of complementary computational paradigms.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes raw DNA sequences (GRCh38 human reference genome) or protein sequences (UniRef50, 72.1M sequences at 50% identity) as pretraining data, and task descriptions (encoded via all-MiniLM-L6-v2 or GPT-4o) for the architecture prediction component. The search space is parameterized by sequences of 5 block types (CNN, LSTM, Transformer, Mamba, Hyena) with per-layer hidden dimensions, pruned to 360 representative architectures. Outputs include ranked hybrid architecture topologies (block-type and dimension sequences), a pretrained foundation model backbone (BIOARC-F, ~5M–88M parameters), and per-task prediction scores aggregated via Z-score normalization across heterogeneous metrics (accuracy, RMSE, MCC).

## Method

BIOARC constructs a heterogeneous search space of 5 distinct operation primitives and encodes it into a single weight-sharing supernet where each candidate architecture corresponds to a path through shared blocks. The supernet is pretrained via one-shot single-path sampling (inspired by Single Path One-Shot) under self-supervised objectives (masked modeling, contrastive learning, or next token prediction), with a monotonic width constraint and log-space distance-based pruning to ensure structural diversity. Evaluation decouples each path's weights for independent fine-tuning on downstream tasks, then ranks architectures using Z-score-normalized mean performance across the task set. The discovered topologies exhibit a consistent hierarchical pattern (e.g., Hyena → Transformer → CNN for DNA) that interpretability analysis links to biological grammar: global genomic context, spatial syntax around the TSS, and motif-level Inr+DPE synergy. An agentic pipeline (Analyst → Task Retriever → Architecture Retriever → Predictor, powered by GPT-4o) extends the framework to predict optimal architectures for unseen tasks without exhaustive search.

## Results

On the GUE DNA benchmark (12 tasks), BIOARC with only 4.89M parameters (mask-ft) achieves 84.80% on TFP-0, 92.62% on PD-all, 83.60% on CPD-all, and 90.84% on SSP, outperforming DNABERT-2 (117M) and VQDNA (103M) by 10–20 points across all tasks while using ~25× fewer parameters. In the controlled protein comparison (full UniRef50, 50K steps), BIOARC 8M beats a reimplemented ESM-2 8M on all six PEER tasks, including Fold (20.75 vs. 18.25) and Solubility (75.12 vs. 73.48). As a foundation model backbone (BIOARC-F, 4.8M), it outperforms DNABERT-2 on 10/12 DNA tasks with 1/10 the training steps and 1/20 the model size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIOARC decisively outperforms all DNA baselines: against Nucleotide Transformer (2500M params, 20.5T tokens), it gains 12–78 points (e.g., TFP-0: 84.80 vs. 66.31; PD-tata: 83.20 vs. 75.80) with 1/500 the parameters; against DNABERT-2 (117M) and VQDNA (103M), it leads by 10–20 points on every task (e.g., TFP-2: 85.80 vs. 66.52/66.85). On protein, in the controlled 50K-step comparison, BIOARC 8M beats ESM-2 8M on all six PEER tasks (Fold: 20.75 vs. 18.25; Solubility: 75.12 vs. 73.48), and also surpasses ProtBert (419M) and ESM-1b/ESM-2 1b (652M) on most tasks despite being 50–80× smaller. However, the officially released ESM-2 8M (trained for 500K steps, ~10× more compute) still edges out BIOARC 8M on Fold (22.14 vs. 20.75), confirming that pretraining scale partially compensates for architectural differences. Against the latest genomic foundation models Caduceus and GENERator on the NT benchmark, BIOARC-F (4.8M) achieves the best enhancer detection score (0.874 MCC vs. ≤0.580) and leads on most histone-mark tasks, though GENERator's larger generative pretraining gives it the edge on promoter and splice-site tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIOARC extends the one-shot NAS paradigm (Guo et al., 2020; Bender et al., 2018) from homogeneous or at most 2-way hybrid search spaces to an open-ended composition of five heterogeneous block types (CNN, LSTM, Transformer, Mamba, Hyena) tailored for biological sequences. It departs from prior biological NAS efforts (GenomeNet-Architect, NOAS-DS, AMBIENT) that were confined to fixed design templates or single-module families, and from the dominant Transformer-only paradigm in biological foundation models (ESM, DNABERT-2, Nucleotide Transformer). The agentic architecture prediction component builds on NAS-Bench-style predictors (Ying et al., 2019; Dudziak et al., 2021) but reframes the problem as semantic reasoning over task descriptions rather than pure numerical regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BIOARC provides a principled, data-driven methodology for biological architecture design that replaces intuition-driven choices, and its finding that compact hybrid topologies (Hyena→Transformer→CNN) outperform 25× larger Transformer-only models under matched pretraining budgets is practically significant for resource-constrained settings. The systematic disentanglement of architecture–tokenization–training interplay yields actionable design guidelines (e.g., Transformer prefers 6-mer, CNN prefers 1-mer) that are immediately useful to practitioners. Open questions include whether the discovered modality-specific patterns generalize to RNA, single-cell, or multi-omics data, whether the 5-block search space is sufficient, and whether the agentic predictor can scale to truly novel task families without retraining.
