## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models for egocentric interaction understanding treat analysis, answering, and pixel grounding as independent parallel objectives, failing to transfer coarse interaction semantics into fine-grained query-oriented responses. This leads to limited grounding accuracy and weak cross-scene generalization, particularly for the pixel-level localization subtask that is critical for embodied agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264267/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 448×448 egocentric RGB image and a free-form user query as input. It produces a structured output containing a holistic interaction description, a natural-language answer wrapped in <answer> tags, and one or more bounding boxes in <bbox> tags (each as four normalized coordinates). The predicted boxes are then passed to a frozen SAM2-Large model to generate pixel-level segmentation masks, so the final grounding output is a binary mask rather than a box.

## Method

EARL is a two-stage framework: Stage 1 (SFT on Qwen2.5-VL-3B) generates a holistic interaction description and retains the last-layer hidden state as a global interaction descriptor F_ana; Stage 2 (Stabilized GRPO on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) produces the query-conditioned answer and bounding boxes. The key architectural contribution is the Analysis-guided Feature Synthesizer (AFS), which projects F_ana through an MLP, reshapes it into a spatial grid, applies a convolutional layer to produce Q/K/V, performs self-attention, and adds the result to the aligned visual-textual embedding—effectively injecting reliable semantic priors into the response decoder. Training uses a three-component reward (format correctness, answer relevance via ExactMatch + Levenshtein ratio, and grounding IoU) optimized with GRPO employing DAPO-style asymmetric clipping for stability, while the vision encoder, text encoder, and SAM2 remain frozen throughout RL.

## Results

On the Ego-IRGBench test set, EARL achieves 65.48% cIoU for pixel grounding, surpassing the previous best (Seg-Zero at 57.11%) by 8.37 points, and attains the best answering CIDEr of 6.682 (+1.682 over Qwen3-VL-8B) and answering METEOR of 0.939. For interaction analysis, EARL achieves the best CIDEr (1.522) though its METEOR (0.542) is marginally below ANNEXE (0.563). On the out-of-distribution EgoHOS grounding benchmark, EARL achieves 38.21% overall cIoU, outperforming the second-best method (Sa2VA-8B at 37.63%) by 0.58 points, with particularly strong hand grounding (52.30% left hand, 62.44% right hand IoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EARL dominates the grounding subtask across all baseline categories: it outperforms the strongest RL-based segmentation model Seg-Zero (57.11% cIoU) by 8.37 points, Seg-R1-7B (46.10%) by 19.38 points, and the pixel-level grounding model Sa2VA-8B (32.69%) by 32.79 points. For answering, EARL's CIDEr of 6.682 substantially exceeds Qwen3-VL-8B (5.000), ANNEXE (2.590), and Seg-Zero (2.380), while its METEOR of 0.939 surpasses all baselines including Qwen3-VL-8B (0.882). In analysis, EARL achieves the best CIDEr (1.522) but trails ANNEXE (1.494) by only 0.021 in METEOR (0.542 vs. 0.563), the sole metric where it does not lead. On the OOD EgoHOS benchmark, EARL (38.21% cIoU) edges out Sa2VA-8B (37.63%) and Seg-R1-7B (34.95%), though it lags behind Sa2VA-8B on individual object categories (e.g., left-hand objects: 19.10% vs. 26.91%), indicating its advantage is concentrated in hand grounding and overall consistency rather than every sub-category.

## Relation to Prior Work

EARL extends the Ego-IRG task formulation introduced by ANNEXE (Su et al., 2025) by adding a reinforcement learning training paradigm and an explicit cross-stage semantic transfer mechanism. Methodologically, it builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and its DAPO-inspired asymmetric clipping (Yu et al., 2025) for policy optimization, while drawing on the broader RL-for-MLLMs line (Seg-R1, Seg-Zero, SAM-R1) that applies reward-driven training to segmentation and grounding. The key departure from prior RL-based segmentation MLLMs is the two-stage analysis-guided architecture: rather than treating reasoning and grounding as a single end-to-end objective, EARL first produces a holistic interaction description whose hidden features are distilled into a global descriptor and injected via the AFS module into the query-conditioned response stage, creating an explicit coarse-to-fine information pathway absent in prior unified VLM approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.37% cIoU gain over the previous SOTA on Ego-IRGBench, supported by thorough ablations isolating the contributions of AFS and RL, demonstrates that structured semantic priors from a coarse analysis stage can substantially improve fine-grained pixel grounding in egocentric settings. The multi-faceted reward design (format + answer relevance + grounding IoU) offers a reusable template for training VLMs that must produce heterogeneous outputs (text, boxes, masks) simultaneously. However, OOD generalization remains modest (only 0.58% cIoU over the second-best on EgoHOS), the evaluation is limited to grounding for OOD due to dataset constraints, and the framework is egocentric-specific, leaving open whether the analysis-guided transfer paradigm generalizes to exocentric or multi-view interaction understanding.
