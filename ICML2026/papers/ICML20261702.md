## Problem

Mainstream equivariant GNNs for 3D atomistic systems are inherently edge-centric, explicitly constructing geometric features or performing dense tensor products on every edge, which incurs O(|E|) = O(kN) activation memory and makes them bandwidth-bound on modern GPUs. While FlashAttention resolved analogous bottlenecks for standard transformers through SRAM-optimized streaming execution, these hardware-aware principles remain unexplored in SO(3)-equivariant architectures, leaving a gap between algebraic factorization and practical linear-scaling inference.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a molecular graph with N atoms, each specified by a 3D coordinate and atom type, with a bounded neighbor set (k ≈ 30–100 under a 6 Å cutoff). It outputs per-atom energy (meV/atom) and per-atom force vectors (meV/Å), with forces computed either via energy gradients (Conservative mode) or a dedicated force head (Direct mode). A key operational detail is that the architecture achieves O(|V|) activation memory rather than O(|E|), enabling stable inference up to 100k atoms on a single H20 GPU where edge-centric baselines OOM beyond 10k–50k atoms.

## Method

E2Former-V2 combines two core designs to realize node-centric equivariant attention. First, Equivariant Axis-Aligned Sparsification (EAAS) exploits the pole sparsity of solid spherical harmonics: by rotating into a local frame where the bond vector aligns with the z-axis, all magnetic components except m=0 vanish, collapsing dense Clebsch-Gordan contractions into a deterministic parity-based re-indexing operator P that acts as a sparse permutation within each ℓ-block. Second, a fully fused Triton kernel implements on-the-fly equivariant attention as a streaming reduction over neighbors, using online softmax to accumulate the weighted value sum without ever materializing O(|E|) edge-level Q/K/V tensors in HBM. Attention scores are computed from SO(3)-invariant scalar projections of node features (via ℓ-wise equivariant linear maps followed by invariant inner products), while directional geometric information enters exclusively through the node-wise value path (source-term pre-coupling → weighted aggregation → target-term coupling), strictly decoupling edge interactions and shifting the workload from memory-bound to compute-bound.

## Results

On SPICE, E2Former-V2-Conservative achieves 0.31 meV/atom Energy MAE and 7.05 meV/Å Force MAE (ALL), reducing Energy MAE by 48% relative to MACE-Large (0.77) and 48% relative to E2Former-V1 (0.60). On the large-scale OMol25 benchmark, E2Former-V2-Conservative attains 1.16 meV/atom Energy MAE (TOTAL), outperforming eSEN-small-Cons (1.27) and UMA-S (3.62). In inference throughput, E2Former-V2 is the only method that scales to 100k atoms without OOM, reaching 0.12 steps/s (Conservative) and 0.52 steps/s (Direct) at that scale, while EquiformerV2, E2Former-V1, and MACE-Large all fail beyond 10k–50k atoms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E2Former-V2-Conservative outperforms MACE-Large by 48% on SPICE Energy MAE (0.31 vs 0.77 meV/atom) and E2Former-V1 by 48% (0.31 vs 0.60), while eSEN-Conservative retains a slight edge on SPICE ALL (0.23 vs 0.31) but is outperformed on OMol25 (1.16 vs 1.27 meV/atom). On OMol25, E2Former-V2-Conservative (1.16) substantially beats UMA-S (3.62) and MACE-OMOL (1.84). In inference throughput (Table 5), E2Former-V2 is the only method that avoids OOM at 100k atoms, achieving 0.12 steps/s (Conservative) vs 0.04 for UMA-S, and 58.33 steps/s at 1k atoms (Direct) vs 4.14 for Allegro and 14.25 for GotenNet. At the kernel level, EAAS is 3.6–6× faster than e3nn and 1.5–3.6× faster than OpenEquivariance on tensor-product operations (Table 3), while the fused Triton kernel delivers ~20× higher TFLOPS than a naive PyTorch implementation. On OC20 S2EF-2M, E2Former-V2 (287 meV Energy MAE) is comparable to EquiformerV2 (285) and eSCN (283), confirming that the efficiency gains do not sacrifice accuracy on standard catalysis benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

E2Former-V2 directly extends E2Former (Li et al., 2025), which provided the theoretical node-centric factorization of equivariant tensor products via Wigner-6j recoupling, reducing asymptotic complexity from O(|E|) to O(|V|). However, the paper explicitly argues that E2Former's algebraic reduction alone does not yield practical speedups because standard implementations still materialize edge-level tensors in HBM. E2Former-V2 closes this gap by combining two additional ingredients: (1) EAAS, which exploits the SO(3)→SO(2) change-of-basis technique from eSCN (Passaro & Zitnick, 2023) to convert dense Clebsch-Gordan contractions into sparse parity-based re-indexing, and (2) a fused Triton kernel that applies FlashAttention's (Dao et al., 2022) streaming, SRAM-resident execution paradigm to the equivariant setting, eliminating explicit edge tensor materialization entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: E2Former-V2 scales to 100k atoms where all prior equivariant transformers (EquiformerV2, E2Former-V1, MACE-Large) hit OOM, and achieves ~20× higher TFLOPS than naive implementations, making large-scale equivariant molecular dynamics feasible on commodity GPUs. A key open question is the efficiency/expressivity trade-off: the paper removes explicit edge-level value-path non-linearities (present in EquiformerV2/eSEN) in favor of a more memory-efficient attention formulation, and while accuracy is comparable on SPICE and OMol25, whether this gap widens at larger model scales or on more challenging tasks remains untested. Additionally, the current implementation operates in FP32; the paper notes that lower-precision execution could further close the gap with dense FlashAttention throughput, but this is left as future work.
