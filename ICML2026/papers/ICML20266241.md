## Problem

Uniform State Diffusion Models (USDMs) initialize from a uniform prior over the vocabulary, offering fast generation via self-correction, but their state-of-the-art training objective (the NELBO) is complex and computationally expensive, hindering scalability. This paper asks whether a much simpler denoising-based loss can match the performance of these ELBO-derived objectives while reducing training cost.

## Input / Output

The model takes a corrupted token sequence x_t (where each token is independently replaced by a random vocabulary token with probability 1−α_t) along with a continuous diffusion timestep t, and predicts the original clean token at each position as a full vocabulary-sized probability distribution. For conditional generation, a text prompt is prepended as context. The architecture is a modified Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with rotary positional encodings and adaptive layer normalization for time conditioning, operating at context lengths from 128 (LM1B) to 2048 (SlimPajama) tokens.

## Method

SDDLM replaces the complex NELBO with a simple cross-entropy denoising loss applied only to positions where the token was actually corrupted (x_0^l ≠ x_t^l), mirroring the selective-prediction behavior of masked diffusion models. This indicator-based restriction stabilizes training and matches ELBO-derived performance at substantially lower compute. SDDLM-V1 further adds an anti-uniform distribution sharpening regularizer, KL(U‖p_θ(x_0^l|x_t)), which penalizes probability mass on randomly sampled negative tokens and introduces negative gradients structurally analogous to contrastive learning, counteracting the over-smoothing toward uniform outputs induced by heavy corruption. The full objective is a per-token sum of the positive log-likelihood and the expected log-likelihood of a random negative, both gated by the corruption indicator, with a small ε added to stabilize log gradients. Training follows Duo's curriculum schedule and uses a 170M–1.1B parameter DiT backbone, demonstrating that the simplified loss scales effectively to larger models.

## Results

On 170M-parameter models, SDDLM-V1 achieves a Gen PPL of 116.84 on LM1B and 45.18 on OpenWebText, substantially outperforming the Duo baseline (172.93 and 80.43 respectively), while SDDLM alone matches Duo on LM1B (173.04 vs. 172.93) and beats it on OWT (77.07 vs. 80.43). On 1.1B-parameter models evaluated on eight QA benchmarks, SDDLM-V1 achieves the best LAMBADA score (31.75 vs. Duo's 17.02) and SIQA (38.18 vs. 37.82), while SDDLM matches or exceeds Duo on most tasks (e.g., PIQA 64.42 vs. 63.11, Obqa 18.20 vs. 17.00). Training cost is also reduced: SDDLM-V1 requires less wall-clock time and peak GPU memory than Duo on OWT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Duo (Sahoo et al., 2025), the primary USDM baseline, SDDLM matches performance on LM1B (Gen PPL 173.04 vs. 172.93) and improves on OWT (77.07 vs. 80.43), while SDDLM-V1 delivers a dramatic improvement (45.18 vs. 80.43 on OWT, a 44% reduction in Gen PPL). On 1.1B QA benchmarks, SDDLM-V1 surpasses Duo on LAMBADA (31.75 vs. 17.02) and SIQA (38.18 vs. 37.82), though Duo retains a slight edge on BoolQ (60.55 vs. 59.66) and ARC-e (42.85 vs. 45.29). Against SEDD (Lou et al., 2023), SDDLM outperforms on all five zero-shot PPL metrics (e.g., WikiText 35.02 vs. 41.10, ArXiv 39.95 vs. 50.86). Against PLAID (Gulrajani & Hashimoto, 2023), SDDLM wins on both reported metrics (WikiText 35.02 vs. 50.86, Lambada 51.50 vs. 57.28). Against UDLM (Schiff et al., 2024), SDDLM is competitive, winning on WikiText (35.02 vs. 39.42), Lambada (51.50 vs. 53.57), PubMed (44.75 vs. 50.98), and ArXiv (39.95 vs. 44.08), but UDLM retains an advantage on AG News (80.96 vs. 73.44).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper simplifies the training objective of Uniform State Diffusion Models (USDMs) by replacing the complex NELBO loss used in Duo (Sahoo et al., 2025) with a straightforward denoising loss applied only to noise-corrupted tokens, drawing directly from the reconstruction objective of continuous diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020) and the selective-prediction design of masked diffusion language models (Sahoo et al., 2024). The added anti-uniform distribution sharpening regularization is motivated by a contrastive-learning interpretation of the denoising gradient, connecting USDM training to self-supervised representation learning (Chen et al., 2020; Xiang et al., 2023). For inference-time guidance, the paper adopts unsupervised classifier-free guidance from Nie et al. (2024), adapting it to the USDM setting where paired conditional data is unavailable during pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that USDMs can now be trained with a substantially simpler and cheaper loss while matching or exceeding the generation quality of ELBO-based methods, with results scaling to 1.1B parameters on SlimPajama. A notable conceptual finding is the observed mismatch between ELBO-based perplexity and actual generation quality: SDDLM-V2 shows ELBO PPL decreasing while sampling quality also improves, suggesting that optimizing the ELBO upper bound may not be the right target for generation. Key limitations include the absence of validation beyond 1.1B parameters, restriction to text-only generation (no multimodal or long-context agentic tasks), and the use of random negative sampling rather than more principled strategies, leaving open whether the contrastive-learning framing can yield further gains at scale.
