## Problem

Agent-based KG navigation follows reasoning paths derived from natural language queries, but users' lack of schema knowledge causes these paths to diverge from the actual graph topology, leading agents into dead ends. Existing recovery mechanisms like PoG's single-step backtracking are too myopic to escape deep-seated reasoning traps, and no prior method enables non-local reversion to arbitrary historical decision points.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265952/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language question and a knowledge graph (Freebase), starting from entity-linked nodes. At each step it observes only the local neighborhood of the current entity (filtered by semantic similarity if |neighbors| > K), not the full subgraph, due to context-window constraints. The output is an answer entity set aggregated from all Yield actions across the trajectory, accompanied by a structured reasoning trace where each step records a discrete action (Forward/Backjump/Yield/Halt), a textual rationale, and a target entity.

## Method

BoG formalizes KG reasoning into four atomic operations—Forward, Backjump, Yield, and Halt—creating a structural scaffold that explicitly exposes historical states and permits non-local reversion to any ancestor node. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) SFT on oracle-conditioned synthetic trajectories generated by Gemini-2.5 Flash, where BFS-derived reference paths serve as structural hints without revealing ground-truth answers, instilling basic backjumping capability; (2) GRPO-based RL with a hybrid reward combining outcome F1, a linear length penalty, a format-correctness score, and an LLM-verifier faithfulness score, which jointly optimize both the timing and landing node of each backjump. The key distinction from prior work is multi-level backjumping: the agent can revert to any historical decision point (not just the immediately preceding one), enabling escape from arbitrarily deep reasoning traps.

## Results

BoG (Llama-3.1-8B) achieves the best performance across all three KGQA benchmarks: 92.7 Hits@1 / 81.3 F1 on WebQSP, 84.0 Hits@1 / 78.3 F1 on CWQ, and 90.9 Hits@1 / 82.4 F1 on GrailQA. The SFT-only variant (BoG_SFT) already surpasses all closed-source LLMs (e.g., Gemini-2.5 Pro, GPT-5.2) under the same agent framework, while the full BoG with RL further improves over BoG_SFT by 3–5 points in Hits@1 across datasets. On CWQ, BoG outperforms the strongest retrieval-based baseline GCR by 11.3 Hits@1 points (84.0 vs. 72.7) and the strongest agent-based baseline KG-Agent by 11.8 points (84.0 vs. 72.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BoG (Llama-3.1-8B) achieves the highest Hits@1 on all three datasets, outperforming the strongest retrieval-based method GCR (GPT-4o-mini) by 0.5, 11.3, and 6.0 points on WebQSP, CWQ, and GrailQA respectively, and the strongest same-size retrieval method DoG (Llama-3.1-8B) by 1.3, 7.8, and 7.5 points. Among agent-based baselines, BoG surpasses KG-Agent (Llama-2-7B) by 9.4, 11.8, and 4.8 Hits@1 points, and dramatically outperforms KBQA-o1 (Llama-3.1-8B) by 29.2, 40.8, and 19.0 points, indicating that MCTS-based single-path search is far less effective than multi-level backjumping. Notably, even the SFT-only variant (BoG_SFT) exceeds all closed-source LLMs evaluated under the same agent framework—beating Gemini-2.5 Pro by 17.0, 4.6, and 24.2 Hits@1 points on WebQSP, CWQ, and GrailQA—demonstrating that specialized backjumping training is more effective than raw model scale for structured graph navigation. The full RL stage adds a consistent 3–5 point gain over SFT alone, confirming that the hybrid reward (outcome + efficiency + format + faithfulness) meaningfully refines backjumping timing and target selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agent-based KGQA paradigm (ToG, KG-Agent, KBQA-o1) by introducing a multi-level backjumping mechanism that allows non-local reversion to any historical decision point, in contrast to PoG's single-step backtracking which the authors identify as too myopic to escape deep reasoning traps. The optimization leverages GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) with a novel hybrid reward combining outcome correctness, path efficiency, format compliance, and reasoning faithfulness, applied specifically to tune backjumping timing and landing nodes. The SFT stage uses an oracle-conditioned trajectory construction strategy (BFS reference paths as structural hints) to synthesize training data that explicitly demonstrates backjumping behavior, a data generation approach distinct from the MCTS-based trajectory generation in KBQA-o1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The backjumping mechanism addresses a fundamental and persistent failure mode in agent-based KG navigation: the semantic-to-topological mismatch between natural language queries and rigid KG schemas, which causes agents to enter dead ends with no recovery mechanism. The finding that RL-optimized backjumping generalizes across out-of-distribution graph environments (Figure 3) suggests the learned strategy captures transferable search logic rather than dataset-specific patterns. Key limitations include dependence on correct entity linking, static reward weights that may not adapt to novel schemas, and context-window saturation for extremely long reasoning horizons. Open questions include whether the backjumping principle extends to heterogeneous or evolving graphs, and whether adaptive reward weighting can eliminate the need for manual hyperparameter tuning.
