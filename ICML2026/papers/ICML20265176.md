## Problem

While long-context reasoning has become a core LLM capability, few studies investigate which data types are effective for training this capability and why. Existing work focuses on training strategies and synthetic data pipelines but does not analyze how the intrinsic structural properties of different data types influence long-context reasoning. This paper asks whether structured table data, with its periodic non-vanishing dependency structure, can systematically enhance long-context reasoning and what mechanisms drive this effect.

## Input / Output

The training input consists of row-major linearized table data (up to 32k tokens, with 1–30 tables per instance and an average cell density of 5.2 tokens) paired with a natural language question derived from a constrained SQL query. The model produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace ending in a verifiable factual answer (e.g., a specific value, aggregated statistic, or multi-table join result). Verification is grounded in executable SQL: each training instance is a (Raw Table, Question, Answer) triplet where the answer is obtained by executing the corresponding query against a unified SQLite database, ensuring exact-match verifiability.

## Method

The paper first provides a mutual information analysis proving that table data exhibits periodic non-vanishing dependencies: at every lag that is a multiple of the column count m, the average MI equals a positive constant I^same determined by cross-column distribution variance, in contrast to the power-law decay I_text(d) ~ C·d^{-α} in natural language. Building on this, the TableLong pipeline aggregates over 10,000 real-world tables (from BIRD, CoSQL, Spider, and LLM-extracted documents), uses LLMs to generate diverse SQL queries spanning three dimensions—precise retrieval (SELECT+WHERE), multi-hop reasoning (column-wise aggregation), and multi-table grounding (JOIN operations)—then executes them to obtain verifiable answers. A consistency-based filter discards instances where the model's pass rate is 0 (ambiguous/impossible) or 1 (trivial), retaining only 0 < P < 1 for optimal RL gradient signal. The filtered data is used for RL training via GRPO (with clip-higher strategy and gpt-oss-120B as outcome judge) on 64 H20 GPUs, yielding +8.24% average improvement on long-context benchmarks and +8.06% on OOD domains (math, code, science, multi-turn dialogue).

## Results

On seven long-context reasoning benchmarks (LongBench-v2, Loong, BrowsCompLong, GSM-Infinite, Oolong, RULER, MRCR), TableLong achieves an average accuracy gain of +8.24% for DS-R1-Distill-32B (40.12→48.36) and +8.93% for DS-R1-Distill-14B (36.14→45.07). The method also generalizes to out-of-domain tasks, yielding +7.58% on GPQA-Diamond, +10.00% on AIME 2025, and +11.97% on LiveCodeBench for the 32B model. Needle-in-a-Haystack retrieval improves from 87.95% to 99.40% (32B) and from 69.30% to 91.20% (14B), approaching near-perfect long-context retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen-Long-L1 (the most directly comparable same-backbone long-context RL method), DS-R1-Distill-32B+Ours (48.36 avg) substantially outperforms it (38.59 avg) by +9.77 points, with particularly large gains on MRCR (+12.87) and Oolong (+24.04). The method approaches DeepSeek-v3.1 (49.80 avg) within 1.44 points, despite using a much smaller 32B backbone versus DeepSeek-v3.1's 671B parameter model. Compared to the Gemini-3.0-pro reference (78.09 avg), the gap remains substantial (~30 points), indicating that table-data RL is effective but not yet sufficient to close the gap to frontier proprietary models. On OOD generalization, DS-R1-Distill-32B+Ours achieves 58.68 on LiveCodeBench vs. 46.71 for the base model (+11.97), and 70.00 on AIME 2025 vs. 60.00 (+10.00), demonstrating that long-context table reasoning transfers to code and math domains. The 14B variant shows the largest relative improvement (+8.93% avg), suggesting TableLong is especially beneficial for smaller models with weaker baseline long-context capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of long-context reasoning RL and data-centric analysis. While prior work (QwenLong-L1, DeepSeek-R1) established RL as the paradigm for long-context reasoning and emerging pipelines (Zhu et al., Yang et al., Zhang et al.) began synthesizing training data, none systematically asked *which data types* are effective and *why*. The authors introduce the first mutual information analysis of tabular dependency structures, showing periodic non-vanishing dependencies that contrast with the power-law decay in natural language, and repurpose SQL-based table environments (BIRD, Spider, CoSQL) as a scalable, verifiable RL data source rather than as a standalone task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, information-theoretic justification for why structured table data enhances long-context reasoning, giving practitioners a concrete design principle (periodic non-vanishing dependencies) for selecting post-training data. The OOD generalization to math (AIME +10%), code (LCB +11.9%), and science (GPQA +7.6%) suggests the capability transfer is broad rather than domain-specific. However, the study is limited to table data; other structured modalities (graphs, documents with hierarchical structure) remain unexplored, and the coupling effects of mixing multiple data types in RL training are left as future work.
