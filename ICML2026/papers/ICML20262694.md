## Problem

Building multimodal Transformers on spiking neural networks is hindered by two core obstacles: unstable gradient propagation through deep stacks of LIF neurons with fixed hyperparameters, and a fundamental mismatch between dense pairwise softmax attention and the sparse, binary nature of spike-based communication. Prior SNN work has largely addressed single-modality perception, leaving efficient spiking architectures for vision-language fusion largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (split into patches at 224×224 for classification or 512×512 for segmentation) and WordPiece-tokenized text sequences, which are concatenated into a joint image-text representation for multimodal tasks. All internal representations are binary spike tensors produced by PLMP read-out gates over T simulation time steps, rather than continuous activations. Outputs span text captions (image captioning), dot-product similarity scores (dual-encoder retrieval), class labels (ImageNet), pixel-wise segmentation masks (ADE20K), and binary yes/no judgments (NLVR2).

## Method

SMM Transformer replaces three key Transformer components with spiking-compatible alternatives. First, the PLMP neuron uses K parallel LIF branches with learnable leakage factors and thresholds, aggregated through a fixed-threshold coincidence gate that emits a single binary spike; P-STBP extends spatio-temporal backpropagation to jointly optimize synaptic weights, biases, leakage factors, and thresholds across all branches. Second, SMSA eliminates the O(N²) attention matrix by computing channel-wise spike co-activation between binary Query and Key tensors, applying the resulting spike gate as column-wise masking on Value spikes, and adding a spiking MLP self-compensation branch to recover lost expressiveness. Third, SMoE routes tokens to modality-specific spiking experts (vision, language, vision-language) via top-K softmax routing over spike-based representations. Training follows a stagewise schedule: vision expert and shared SMSA are pre-trained on image-only data, the language expert is then trained with masked language modeling, and finally the full model is jointly optimized on image-text pairs with contrastive, matching, and MLM objectives.

## Results

On MSCOCO image captioning (Karpathy split), SMM-Transformer-Large achieves BLEU-1/4 of 83.50/45.33, METEOR 34.25, ROUGE-L 61.18, CIDEr 128.72, and SPICE 21.87, with an estimated compute energy of only 22.1 mJ per caption—roughly 6× lower than the best ANN baseline (PureT, 387.9 mJ). On cross-modal retrieval, SMM-Transformer-Large attains COCO text-retrieval R@1 of 75.3 and image-retrieval R@1 of 59.3, and Flickr30K text-retrieval R@1 of 93.9. For vision tasks, SMM-Base reaches 83.2% ImageNet top-1 accuracy and 49.8 mIoU on ADE20K, surpassing the prior SNN baseline PSSD (66.8% / 29.1 mIoU) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Up-Down (Anderson et al., CVPR 2018) captioning baseline, SMM-Transformer-Large wins on every reported metric: BLEU-1 83.50 vs. 80.2, BLEU-4 45.33 vs. 36.9, METEOR 34.25 vs. 27.6, ROUGE-L 61.18 vs. 57.1, CIDEr 128.72 vs. 117.9, and SPICE 21.87 vs. 21.4, while consuming only 22.1 mJ versus 243.1 mJ. Compared to X-LAN (Pan et al., CVPR 2020), SMM leads on BLEU-1 (83.50 vs. 80.8), BLEU-4 (45.33 vs. 39.5), METEOR (34.25 vs. 29.5), ROUGE-L (61.18 vs. 59.2), and energy (22.1 vs. 210.7 mJ), but trails on CIDEr (128.72 vs. 132.0) and SPICE (21.87 vs. 23.4), which the authors attribute to the loss of dense token-pair interactions in SMSA. On ImageNet classification, SMM-Base (83.2% top-1) is slightly below ViT-Base (83.6%) but far above the prior SNN method PSSD (66.8%), confirming that the spiking Transformer architecture closes most of the accuracy gap with ANN ViTs while retaining event-driven sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spiking Transformer line (Spikformer, Spike-driven Transformer) from single-modality vision to multimodal vision-language settings, while simultaneously departing from dense softmax attention by replacing it with channel-wise spike co-activation (SMSA). It builds directly on STBP (Wu et al., 2018) by generalizing the back-propagation to handle multi-branch learnable LIF dynamics (P-STBP), and adapts the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm to a spiking context with modality-specific routing (SMoE). Unlike prior multimodal SNNs that rely on separate encoders or late fusion, SMM Transformer maintains a unified Transformer-style residual stack where all three components—neuron, token-mixing, and feed-forward fusion—are redesigned for sparse spike communication.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a spiking Transformer can match ANN accuracy on captioning and retrieval while reducing estimated operator-level attention energy by up to 97%, offering a concrete path toward energy-efficient multimodal inference. However, whole-model gains are more modest (~22%), CIDEr/SPICE remain below the best ANN baselines, and all energy figures are arithmetic estimates under a MAC/AC model rather than measurements on neuromorphic hardware. Open questions include whether the approach scales to LLM-sized architectures, how memory access and scheduling overhead affect real-device efficiency, and whether the loss of exact token-to-token inductive bias in SMSA becomes a bottleneck for more complex reasoning tasks.
