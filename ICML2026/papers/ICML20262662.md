## Problem

Current multimodal LLMs process long clinical videos via passive frame sampling or weakly grounded inspection, making them unable to iteratively locate, verify, and justify predictions with temporally targeted visual evidence. Decisive cues in surgical and endoscopic recordings are sparse and brief, yet existing R1-style text-first pipelines treat the visual stream as static context, encouraging plausible but unsupported rationales.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262662/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a long clinical video (up to 512 frames, 50,176-pixel budget) together with a text query, and produces a multi-round trajectory structured with explicit tags:  for textual rationalization,  for tool invocations,  for returned visual observations, and <answer> for the terminal prediction. Tool calls retrieve either a densely sampled clip within a time window (crop_video) or three frames nearest a timestamp (get_frame), so the output is both a grounded answer and an inspectable evidence-seeking trace.

## Method

MedScope (built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) alternates hypothesis-driven textual reasoning with two native tools—crop_video for clip-level densification and get_frame for frame-level verification—enabling coarse-to-fine evidence seeking over long procedures. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) multi-source SFT warm-up for medical semantics and long-horizon reasoning, (2) visual-CoT cold-start SFT on ClinVideo-VCoT-34K to teach when and how to invoke tools, and (3) GA-GRPO, a grounding-aware variant of GRPO that adds a composite reward (answer correctness + format + temporal evidence alignment via IoU/timestamp proximity) and modulates each trajectory's advantage by tool-outcome fidelity, amplifying credit for well-grounded positive rollouts while softening penalties for grounded negative ones.

## Results

On SVU-31K, MedScope achieves state-of-the-art scores across all four task families: 4.77 (DO) on Full Video Description, 4.36 (DO) on Fine-grained Video Description, 4.56 CIDEr on Fine-grained Temporal Reasoning, and 10.93 CIDEr on Fine-grained Perception Reasoning. On the paper's own ClinVideo-Eval benchmark, MedScope-7B-RL reaches 64.42 mIoU for temporal grounding and 65.58 mIoU for grounded VQA on OphVL (in-domain), 61.20 mIoU / 39.80 Acc on MedVideoCap, and 44.30 mIoU / 37.90 Acc on SurgVidLM (out-of-domain), surpassing all open-source baselines by 25–38 points on key grounding metrics. On established short-clip surgical VQA benchmarks, MedScope attains 94.6% accuracy on Cholec80-VQA and 71.8% recall on EndoVis18-VQA, both setting new bests among open-source models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedScope-7B-RL outperforms all open-source and reasoning baselines across every metric on ClinVideo-Eval. Against the base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B, it gains +38.5 points on OphVL R@0.3 and +34.2 on mIoU for temporal grounding, and +34.2 on out-of-domain SurgVidLM VQA accuracy. Among reasoning LMMs, it surpasses Video-R1-7B (64.42 vs 33.87 mIoU on OphVL TG), VideoChat-R1-7B (64.42 vs 30.93), and VideoRFT-7B (64.42 vs 27.06) by 27–37 mIoU points. Among tool-using agents, it beats LongVT-7B-RFT (64.42 vs 39.56 mIoU) and ReWatch-R1-7B (64.42 vs 24.77) by 25–40 points. Against proprietary models, MedScope-7B-RL trails Gemini 3-Pro-Preview on OphVL R@0.3 (81.92 vs 87.85) but surpasses it on MedVideoCap VQA accuracy (39.80 vs 32.31) and out-of-domain SurgVidLM VQA accuracy (37.90 vs 21.99), while decisively outperforming GPT-4o on all in-domain grounding metrics. On short-clip surgical VQA, MedScope (94.6% Cholec80-VQA, 71.8% EndoVis18-VQA) exceeds SurgicalGPT (94.0%, 66.0%) and Surgical-LLaVA (92.2%, 68.7%), confirming generalization beyond long-video settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedScope extends the 'thinking with images' tool-augmented reasoning paradigm (Jiang et al., 2025) from static medical images to long-form clinical video, and builds on LongVT's native tool-calling interface for long videos by adding grounding-aware RL. It departs from the dominant R1-style text-first pipeline (Video-R1, VideoChat-R1, VideoRFT) that treats video as passive context, instead framing the video as an interactive environment the model must actively explore via coarse-to-fine tool calls. The GA-GRPO objective adapts DAPO's group-relative advantage to the video-tool setting by injecting a fidelity-weighted modulation term derived from temporal grounding signals, a modification not present in prior video RL work that relies on outcome-level rewards alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in medical AI: long surgical and endoscopic videos require clinicians to iteratively skim and re-inspect, yet current MLLMs process them with one-pass passive sampling that encourages unsupported rationales. ClinVideoSuite (635K captions, 254K grounded QA, 34K VCoT trajectories) is a substantial resource for the medical video community, and the physician-verified 96.7% accuracy lends credibility. Open questions include whether the two-tool interface (crop_video, get_frame) generalizes to more complex procedural queries, how the method scales to multi-hour recordings, and whether the grounding-aware RL recipe transfers to other long-video domains beyond medicine. The paper explicitly disclaims clinical deployment, leaving prospective validation and governance as critical next steps.
