## Problem

Existing model merging methods for multi-task LLMs rely on a single post-hoc aggregation step after task-specific training, which induces abrupt cross-task interference and causes information erasure on individual tasks. The paper identifies that this one-shot paradigm is fundamentally suboptimal and asks whether iterative (many-shot) merging, combined with loss-aware task balancing, can mitigate erasure while preserving multi-task capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261816/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a shared pre-trained LLM (Gemma-2-2B, Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.1-8B, or Qwen-3-4B) and four task-specific datasets (1,000 samples each for instruction-following, math reasoning, multilingual understanding, and safety), fine-tunes LoRA adapters (rank 16, α=16) per task, and produces a single merged multi-task LLM after R=5 iterative merging rounds. The output is evaluated via normalized performance (merged/fine-tuned ratio per task) across IFEval, GSM8K, M-MMLU, M-ARC, M-HellaSwag, and XSTest, with the key metric being both average and worst-task performance.

## Method

METIS replaces the single post-hoc merge with an iterative many-shot protocol where, over R rounds, each task model performs a local LoRA update from the current merged checkpoint, computes a task vector relative to the original pre-trained weights, and participates in a weighted merge. The key mechanism is loss-gap-aware weighting: at each round, the task-wise loss gap G(τ,r) = L_τ(Θ^{r-1}) − L_τ(θ_τ^r) quantifies how much the merged model has degraded task τ, and a softmax over these gaps (with temperature λ) assigns larger merging weights to more-erased tasks, enabling self-correcting rebalancing across rounds. A consensus-based masking layer further localizes compatible updates: each task-specific binary mask retains only coordinates where that task's weighted contribution is not dominated by conflicting updates from other tasks, and a consensus mask activates a parameter only when at least k tasks agree. The paper provides theoretical guarantees (Theorems 3.2 and 4.2) showing that many-shot merging reduces multi-task loss relative to post-hoc merging under L-smoothness, and that loss-gap weighting specifically reduces the worst-task loss relative to uniform averaging.

## Results

On Llama-3.2-3B, METIS achieves a normalized average performance of 1.015 across four task categories (instruction-following, mathematical reasoning, multilingual understanding, safety), outperforming the strongest many-shot baseline ConsensusTA (0.945) by 7.4% and the best post-hoc baseline (0.942) by 7.7%. METIS also achieves the highest worst-performing task score (0.872 on Llama-3.2-3B) with the smallest average-to-worst-task gap (−0.14), compared to ConsensusTA's worst-task score of 0.641 (gap −0.30). Across all four backbone models (Gemma-2-2B, Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen-3-4B), METIS consistently attains the best average normalized performance, ranging from 0.800 to 1.180. On pre-trained knowledge retention (CoQA, PubMedQA), METIS stays closest to the unmerged backbone, e.g., 0.670/0.596 vs. the pre-trained 0.659/0.750 on Llama-3.2-3B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

METIS outperforms all 10 compared baselines on the primary Llama-3.2-3B evaluation. Against the four conventional post-hoc methods evaluated in both post-hoc and many-shot settings, METIS (1.015 avg) beats the strongest many-shot variant ConsensusTA (0.945) by 7.4%, TIES (0.938) by 7.7%, DARE (0.914) by 11.0%, and Task Arithmetic (0.857) by 18.4%. Against recent SOTA merging methods, METIS surpasses TSV-M (0.953) by 6.5%, Iso-C (0.941) by 7.9%, and TA+SubspaceBoosting (0.885) by 14.7%, while also being substantially faster (918s vs. 3219s for TSV-M). Among iterative/federated baselines, METIS leads FedMerge (0.928) by 9.4% and ColD Fusion (0.911) by 11.4%. The data-mixing baseline TULU3 (0.884) trails METIS by 14.8%. Notably, METIS's advantage is most pronounced on the worst-performing task (0.872 vs. 0.641 for ConsensusTA on Llama-3.2-3B), confirming its effectiveness at mitigating information erasure rather than merely boosting already-strong tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the task-vector merging framework introduced by Task Arithmetic [10] and the consensus-based masking mechanism from ConsensusTA [12], but fundamentally departs from the post-hoc (one-shot) merging paradigm that all existing methods adopt. The key conceptual shift is reframing model merging through the lens of iterative distributed optimization: rather than merging once after independent training, the paper introduces a many-shot protocol where task-specific models are aggregated over multiple rounds, drawing an explicit analogy to federated averaging. The novel loss-gap-aware weighting is a new mechanism not present in any prior merging method, designed to dynamically rebalance task contributions based on measured information erasure at each round.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that simply converting any post-hoc merging method to a many-shot protocol yields consistent improvements (e.g., Task Arithmetic multi-task loss drops from 2.97 to 2.00) is practically significant because it offers a low-cost, drop-in upgrade to existing pipelines without changing the underlying merging algorithm. The loss-gap weighting further addresses the worst-performing task, which is critical for deployment where a single weak task can be a failure point. Limitations include evaluation on relatively small models (2B–8B), only four task categories, and a fixed five-round schedule; open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, whether the number of optimal rounds grows with task count, and whether the loss-gap signal remains informative under highly heterogeneous task distributions.
