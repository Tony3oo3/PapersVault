## Problem

Autoregressive LLMs suffer from an inherent irreversibility: once a token's Key-Value state is committed to the cache, it becomes immutable, so early representational biases at critical reasoning steps propagate uncorrected until they cause a cascading reasoning collapse. Existing approaches—CoT prompting, self-consistency, RL fine-tuning—operate forward-only and cannot retroactively rectify already-contaminated internal states, leaving a structural gap in how models handle their own mid-reasoning errors.

## Input / Output

During inference, the system continuously monitors per-token diagnostic signals (attention score variance, FFN representational shift, output entropy, and cross-layer hidden-state cosine similarity) as the model generates a reasoning chain for a math problem. When a trigger is detected, the small MLP editor receives two inputs: the hidden state at the trigger position and the original KV cache at the most recent preceding anchor, and outputs a rectified KV cache for that anchor. The model then discards all tokens generated after the anchor and regenerates from that point using the corrected memory, ultimately producing a revised reasoning chain and final answer. The editor adds fewer than 0.5% parameters to the backbone and intervenes on only ~28% of samples on average.

## Method

HEdit implements a Sense-Backtrack-Edit-Regenerate loop with three components. First, anchor detection (training-free) identifies pivotal tokens by intersecting top-k scores from attention score variance (which avoids the attention-sink bias of raw attention frequency) and FFN information update ratio (which filters out non-semantic functional tokens). Second, trigger detection (training-free) localizes reasoning-collapse moments by intersecting top-k prediction entropy with top-k negative cross-layer hidden-state similarity, the latter capturing 'cognitive dissonance' where representations oscillate violently across adjacent layers. Third, a lightweight two-layer MLP editor (2048→1024, ReLU, <0.5% of backbone parameters) is trained self-supervised on GSM8K: correct trajectories are collected, counterfactual errors are injected at anchors by sampling from Top-2-to-Top-5 predictions instead of greedy Top-1, and the editor learns to map (trigger hidden state, bad anchor KV) → gold anchor KV via MSE loss, with identity-mapping regularization on correct paths to prevent over-sensitivity. At inference, when a trigger fires, the system backtracks to the nearest preceding anchor, applies the editor to its KV cache, and regenerates the sequence from that point, effectively creating a backward information flow that converts late-stage failure awareness into early-stage decision correction.

## Results

HEdit achieves average accuracy improvements of 2.2% to 10.8% across all tested models on four mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MATH500, AIME24, AIME25, AMC23). The largest gains appear on smaller models: Qwen3-0.6B improves from 17.5% to 28.2% average (+10.8%), and Qwen3-1.7B from 34.0% to 44.0% (+9.9%). Even for strong RL-tuned models, HEdit provides consistent gains: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B improves from 47.9% to 55.2% (+7.3%), while the larger DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B gains +2.2% (70.1% → 72.3%). The editor adds fewer than 0.5% parameters and intervenes on only 28.3% of samples on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEEdit is compared against two baselines: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (Wei et al., 2022) and Naive Backtracking (re-reasoning from the anchor without modifying the KV cache). HEdit decisively outperforms CoT on every model: for example, on Qwen3-0.6B it achieves 28.2% average vs. CoT's 19.4% (+8.8 pp gap), and on Qwen3-8B it reaches 72.8% vs. 70.0% (+2.8 pp). Against Naive Backtracking, the advantage is even more pronounced—Naive BT yields only +0.6% to +1.9% average improvement over the base model, while HEdit delivers +2.2% to +10.8%, confirming that simply re-generating from the anchor without correcting the contaminated KV cache is insufficient. The gap between HEdit and Naive BT is largest on smaller models (e.g., Qwen3-0.6B: 28.2% vs. 18.1%) and narrows for the strongest RL-tuned models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B: 72.3% vs. 71.0%), suggesting that already-robust reasoning trajectories have fewer latent errors to correct.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant lines of LLM reasoning improvement—training-free search (CoT, Self-Consistency, Tree-of-Thoughts) and training-driven optimization (SFT, RL, recurrent/looped Transformers)—by identifying a structural bottleneck they all share: the irreversibility of committed KV states in autoregressive decoding. Rather than expanding the search space or increasing per-token compute depth, HEdit introduces a retroactive editing mechanism that modifies historical KV caches, a capability no prior Transformer-based reasoning framework explicitly provides. The anchor/trigger diagnostic framework repurposes attention variance and cross-layer cosine similarity (concepts borrowed from interpretability literature on attention sinks and representation dynamics) as real-time failure detectors, while the self-supervised counterfactual perturbation training strategy is a novel application of data augmentation to internal-state correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HEdit offers a practically attractive, plug-and-play mechanism (< 0.5% extra parameters, ~28% intervention rate) that addresses a fundamental architectural constraint of autoregressive models, potentially applicable beyond the mathematical reasoning tasks tested here. However, several open questions limit its current impact: generalization to open-ended or subjective domains where 'cognitive dissonance' signals are harder to define, handling of multi-error chains requiring iterative editing, and the acknowledged knowledge ceiling where the editor cannot supply information the base model lacks. The diminishing returns on larger, RL-tuned models (2.2% for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B) suggest the mechanism may be most valuable for smaller or less-reasoning-optimized models, raising the question of whether it remains useful as base models improve.
