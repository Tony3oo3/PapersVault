## Problem

Clustering directed graphs remains challenging because edge directionality breaks the reversibility and real-spectrum assumptions underlying spectral and diffusion-based methods, while many practical digraphs are only weakly connected. Existing remedies—symmetrization, teleportation (PageRank), or Hermitian constructions—either obscure directional information, alter the original random-walk dynamics, or lose probabilistic interpretability. The paper seeks an eigen-free, diffusion-based clustering method that preserves directionality on weakly connected digraphs without requiring expensive eigen-decomposition.

## Input / Output

The method takes a weighted digraph adjacency matrix W ∈ R^{N×N}, a target number of clusters k, and a vertex measure ν (default: γ·d_in + (1−γ)·d_out with γ=0.5), and produces a k-partition of the vertex set. The diffusion time t is selected automatically via an entropy-based elbow criterion on the iterated operator, and the embedding dimension d (default √N) controls the random-projection approximation of P_(ν)^t. No ground-truth labels or training data are required; the entire pipeline is unsupervised and operates purely on the graph structure.

## Method

ParPIC constructs a Parametrized Random Walk (P-RW) operator P_(ν) = (D_ν + D_ξ)^{−1}(D_ν P + P^T D_ν) where ξ = P^T ν, which is provably reversible with respect to the measure ν+ξ, irreducible on weakly connected digraphs, and aperiodic when the underlying graph is aperiodic. Rather than performing eigen-decomposition, the method iterates P_(ν) for t steps to obtain P_(ν)^t and clusters the rows (vertex embeddings) with k-means. The diffusion time t is selected by identifying the elbow of the row-wise operator entropy H(t), computed efficiently via sampled vertex probes (√N suffices). To avoid materializing the full N×N iterated matrix, a random-projection approximation Z^(t) = P_(ν)^t Z^(0) ∈ R^{N×d} is used, leveraging the Johnson-Lindenstrauss property to preserve pairwise distances in the embedding. The vertex measure ν (a convex combination of in- and out-degrees) allows tuning the relative influence of incoming versus outgoing edges, enabling the operator to adapt to degree-heterogeneous and asymmetric flow structures that cause symmetrization-based methods to fail.

## Results

On intrinsically directed networks (Table 3), ParPIC achieves a Performance Relative to Best (PRB) score of 1.00, with AMI of 1.00 on DiSBM-Baseline and DiSBM-CP, 0.93 on DiSBM-Chain, 0.39 on PolBlogs, and 0.48 on Email-Eu, outperforming all 10 baselines. On K-NN digraphs (Table 2), ParPIC achieves PRB 0.96, matching S-PIC (also 0.96) and outperforming all spectral and Hermitian-based methods (PRB range 0.33–0.85). The method avoids eigen-decomposition entirely, using only power iterations of the P-RW operator with random projections of dimension d=√N, yielding significant runtime savings over spectral baselines (Fig. 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On intrinsically directed networks, ParPIC dominates all baselines: it outperforms the closest power-iteration competitor S-PIC by 0.36 AMI on DiSBM-Chain (0.93 vs. 0.57) and 0.41 on DiSBM-CP (1.00 vs. 0.59), and by 0.38 on PolBlogs (0.39 vs. 0.01). Spectral methods degrade severely on directed structures—DSC+ and GSC score near 0 on PolBlogs and Email-Eu, while Hermitian methods (Herm-SC, Herm-RW) fail entirely on DiSBM-Baseline (AMI 0.00). On K-NN digraphs with high reciprocity, ParPIC ties S-PIC at PRB 0.96, confirming that directionality modeling yields marginal gains when edges are nearly symmetric. The paper notes that all PIC variants (including baselines) use ParPIC's entropy-based time selection and random projection, making the reported gaps a conservative estimate of the P-RW operator's modeling advantage. DD-Sym (bibliographic symmetrization) is the strongest spectral baseline on directed networks (PRB 0.72) but still trails ParPIC by 0.28 in aggregate.

## Relation to Prior Work

ParPIC extends the Power-Iteration Clustering (PIC) paradigm of Lin & Cohen (2010) from undirected to directed graphs by replacing the natural random walk with a parametrized reversible random walk (P-RW) operator derived from the vertex-measure Laplacian framework of Sevi et al. (2025). The P-RW operator generalizes Chung's (2005) directed random walk (recovered when the vertex measure equals the stationary distribution) while adding a normalization that yields a proper diffusion interpretation. Unlike prior directed-graph clustering approaches that rely on eigen-decomposition (Hermitian spectral, directed Laplacian) or that destroy directionality via symmetrization or teleportation, ParPIC remains eigen-free and preserves directional flow dynamics through the vertex measure parameter γ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ParPIC offers a scalable, eigen-free pipeline for clustering weakly connected directed graphs—a setting where most spectral methods break down—while retaining a principled diffusion-geometry interpretation. The entropy-based time selection and Johnson-Lindenstrauss random projection make the method practical for large graphs, and the degree-heterogeneity experiments demonstrate a concrete failure mode of symmetrization that the P-RW operator avoids. Open questions include extension to dynamic digraphs, semi-supervised settings, and whether the vertex measure can be learned end-to-end rather than hand-designed; the method also still requires the number of clusters k as input.
