## Problem

Standard alignment objectives (MLE and KL-regularized RL) drive vision-language models toward generic, high-probability caption templates, causing multiple generated descriptions to cluster tightly in semantic embedding space—a pathology the authors term the Likelihood Trap. No existing training method explicitly optimizes for set-level geometric diversity of generated captions while preserving visual grounding, leaving long-form captioning systems unable to collectively cover an image's full semantic support.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264371/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a set of K=8 diverse long-form captions (2–4 sentences, 90–120 words each), sampled autoregressively with temperature 0.7, nucleus p=0.9, and a 256-token cap. Each caption is mapped by a frozen Sentence-BERT encoder to a unit-norm embedding vector, and the set is evaluated jointly for geometric volume (SVS), lexical diversity (Distinct-N, Self-BLEU), visual alignment (CLIPScore), and hallucination rate (CHAIR_i) on DeCapBench.

## Method

Geo-RL reformulates long-form captioning as maximizing the K-dimensional volume of a parallelotope spanned by caption embeddings in a frozen semantic space, using a DPP log-determinant reward with an RBF kernel and quality-weighted entries to encourage complementary semantic coverage. A key technical contribution is a closed-form leave-one-out marginal reward (Theorem 3.1) that attributes each caption's contribution to overall set volume via the diagonal of the kernel inverse, enabling stable per-sample credit assignment in PPO without repeated determinant computations. A pairwise contrastive repulsion loss with a cosine-similarity margin suppresses near-duplicate captions at the local level, while a CLIP-based grounding reward and KL trust region maintain visual faithfulness and generation fluency. The full objective is optimized via PPO on a LLaVA-1.5 7B base model, with the DPP volume term, repulsion term, alignment reward, and KL penalty combined into a single scalar signal.

## Results

On DeCapBench, Geo-RL achieves Distinct-2 of 0.84 (vs. 0.66 for the MLE baseline, a 27% relative gain), Semantic Volume Score (SVS) of 3.55 (vs. 2.20), and Self-BLEU of 0.53 (vs. 0.77), indicating substantially broader semantic coverage with less redundancy. Crucially, these diversity gains come without sacrificing visual grounding: CLIPScore improves from 0.74 to 0.81, hallucination rate (CHAIR_i) drops from 8.3% to 7.1%, and DCScore rises from 0.69 to 0.75. Against foundation VLMs (Table 2), Geo-RL's SVS of 3.55 exceeds the best baseline (FEEDQUILL-7B at 2.95) by 20% and GPT-4V (2.80) by 27%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Geo-RL outperforms all 18 baselines on every reported metric simultaneously, breaking the typical diversity–faithfulness trade-off. Among training-method baselines, the closest competitors are DARLING (Distinct-2: 0.78, SVS: 3.10) and VCD (Distinct-2: 0.77, SVS: 3.05), which improve diversity over MLE but still trail Geo-RL by 0.06–0.07 in Distinct-2 and 0.45–0.50 in SVS while also scoring lower on CLIPScore (0.75 vs. 0.81). Standard RLHF and DPO baselines show only marginal gains over MLE (SVS 2.35/2.30 vs. 2.20), confirming the Likelihood Trap diagnosis. Decoding-time strategies (nucleus sampling, contrastive decoding) and unlikelihood training yield intermediate diversity (SVS 2.60–2.80) but do not improve or even slightly degrade CLIPScore and DCScore. Against large foundation VLMs, Geo-RL's SVS of 3.55 surpasses the strongest baseline FEEDQUILL-7B (2.95) by 20% and GPT-4V (2.80) by 27%, while also achieving the highest Distinct-2 (0.84 vs. 0.77 for FEEDQUILL), demonstrating that set-level geometric optimization generalizes beyond the 7B LLaVA backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

Geo-RL sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) DPP-based diversity objectives from combinatorial optimization and NLP (Kulesza et al., 2012; Friedman & Dieng, 2023), which the paper repurposes as a training reward rather than a decoding heuristic; (2) RLHF/PPO alignment for VLMs (Ouyang et al., 2022; Schulman et al., 2017), from which it departs by replacing per-sample preference rewards with a set-level geometric volume signal; and (3) long-form captioning benchmarks like DeCapBench and ShareGPT4V that make semantic collapse measurable. The key conceptual departure is reframing diversity as parallelepiped volume in a frozen embedding space and deriving a closed-form leave-one-out marginal for per-sample credit assignment, rather than relying on surface-level n-gram diversity or token-level penalties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode (the 'Likelihood Trap') that practitioners of long-form VLM captioning will recognize, and offers a principled fix that simultaneously improves diversity and grounding—a combination that prior diversity methods (nucleus sampling, contrastive decoding) typically sacrifice one for the other. However, the evidence is confined to a single benchmark (DeCapBench) and a 7B base model, and the scaling analysis (Table 8) presents results that are suspiciously clean across model sizes. Open questions include whether the geometric volume objective transfers to multi-image or video captioning, whether richer multi-scale kernels (acknowledged in the conclusion) yield further gains, and whether set-level diversity actually improves downstream reasoning or auditing tasks beyond the captioning metrics reported.
