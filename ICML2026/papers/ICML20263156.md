## Problem

Autoregressive models generate sequences monotonically, meaning any sampled token becomes a permanent condition for all subsequent steps and cannot be revised. This structural limitation causes error propagation, where a single incorrect token cascades into failures the model cannot recover from. Existing refinement approaches (prompting, search, post-hoc verification) operate outside the generation loop, requiring expensive external verifiers or multiple inference passes without providing an intrinsic confidence signal during generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (e.g., a math word problem or coding task) and autoregressively generates a raw trajectory that may contain interleaved ⟨UNDO⟩ tokens. This raw trajectory is then reduced to a final semantic sequence via a deterministic stack-based operator: standard tokens are pushed onto the stack, while each ⟨UNDO⟩ token triggers a pop that deletes the previously pushed token. The final output is a clean text sequence (reasoning chain + answer) with no trace of the erased tokens, enabling seamless in-place correction within a single forward pass.

## Method

N-MARS introduces a learned ⟨UNDO⟩ token appended to the vocabulary that, when sampled during decoding, erases the last generated token, enabling non-monotonic generation within a single autoregressive pass. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) sequence alignment augmentation computes maximal matching blocks between model-generated completions and ground-truth references, then constructs error-correction trajectories by inserting error spans followed by ⟨UNDO⟩ tokens and correct corrections; (2) masked SFT (mSFT) selectively zeros out gradient contributions from error tokens so the model learns to detect and correct errors without being trained to generate them, with a theoretical proof showing mSFT improves error detection, corrective generation, and reduces negative learning compared to standard SFT; (3) GRPO with a multi-objective reward function incentivizes correct final outputs while penalizing excessive or unsuccessful ⟨UNDO⟩ usage via a budget parameter κ, encouraging early and judicious error correction.

## Results

N-MARS achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three backbone models (Llama-3.2-1B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-4B) on GSM8K, MATH-500, and MBPP. With Llama-3.2-1B, N-MARS reaches 31.3% on GSM8K and 15.2% on MATH-500, yielding relative improvements of 11.4% and 13.4% over the strongest baseline (ReVISE). The advantage is most pronounced on smaller models and harder tasks: on MATH-500 with Llama-3.2-1B, N-MARS (15.2%) outperforms ReVISE (13.4%) by 1.8 points, while on GSM8K with Qwen3-4B the gap narrows to 1.9 points (86.1% vs. 84.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

N-MARS outperforms all SFT/RL-based baselines (RFT, STaR+, ReVISE) across every model scale and benchmark, with the largest margins on the smallest model (Llama-3.2-1B) and hardest task (MATH-500), where it gains 1.8 points over ReVISE. Against self-correction paradigms, N-MARS beats Self-Reflect (Madaan et al.) by up to 31.0% relative on MATH-500 (15.2 vs. 11.6 with Llama-3.2-1B) and Self-Backtrack (Yang et al.) by up to 35.7% relative (15.2 vs. 11.2), attributed to its token-level UNDO mechanism requiring no explicit step boundaries. Among reward-function alternatives tested in ablation (Table 1c), N-MARS's multi-objective reward ties BSAFE+ (Sel et al.) at 31.3/15.2 but avoids BSAFE+'s reliance on an external GPT-4o critic, while clearly outperforming SCoRe (Kumar et al.) at 29.9/14.1 and MGRPO (Ding et al.) at 30.2/14.8. The paper also shows N-MARS generalizes better under domain shift (GSM8K→MATH: 11.4 vs. ReVISE's 10.2) and on instruction-tuned models (Llama-3.2-Instruct: 53.1 GSM8K vs. ReVISE's 52.3), and achieves a strictly better accuracy-efficiency trade-off, generating fewer tokens (153.7 vs. 178.9) and lower latency (9.1 vs. 9.8 min) than ReVISE at the 256-token budget on GSM8K.

## Relation to Prior Work

N-MARS extends the backtracking-generation line initiated by safety-focused works (Zhang et al. 2025a's [RESET] token, Sel et al. 2025's [BACKTRACK_BY_X]) by repurposing the erase mechanism for reasoning tasks and operating at the atomic token level rather than sentence or step boundaries. It departs from post-hoc self-refinement pipelines (Self-Refine, ReVISE) by internalizing correction into a single decoding pass, and from RL-based self-correction (SCoRe, MGRPO) by introducing a dedicated UNDO action with a multi-objective reward that explicitly regulates erase frequency. The masked SFT objective is a novel training technique that addresses negative learning when the model is exposed to error tokens as context, drawing on partial-gradient convergence results but applying them to a new setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling in-place error correction within a single forward pass, N-MARS offers a strictly better accuracy-efficiency trade-off than multi-pass revision methods, which is practically important for latency-sensitive and resource-constrained deployments. The finding that the optimal correction budget κ is a property of task complexity rather than model scale provides a useful design principle for practitioners. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger models (70B+), whether hierarchical backtracking at phrase or reasoning-step granularity could reduce the token-level overhead, and whether non-monotonic generation can be integrated during pretraining rather than post-training.
