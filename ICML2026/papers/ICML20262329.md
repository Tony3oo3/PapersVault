## Problem

All existing image generation methods—continuous normalizing flows, diffusion models, and flow matching—operate under Euclidean geometry assumptions, treating images as vectors in flat high-dimensional space. While geometry-aware generative modeling has delivered gains in structured domains (crystals, molecules) with known manifold priors, the intrinsic geometric structure of natural images has remained unknown, leaving a fundamental gap between Riemannian manifold-based methods and practical image generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262329/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise (projected onto a fixed-radius hypersphere for SFM) and optional class labels as input, producing synthetic images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10, unconditional) or 256×256 (ImageNet-256, class-conditional with classifier-free guidance). A key operational detail is that all training data is first projected onto a hypersphere of radius r (the dataset's mean L2 norm), so the model learns purely directional dynamics; generated samples are rescaled back to the original average norm after ODE integration. ImageNet-256 experiments use DC-AE latents (4×4×128) with a DiT-XL/2 backbone, while CIFAR-10 uses a U-Net in pixel space.

## Method

The paper's core insight is that natural images decompose into a directional component (unit vector) carrying most semantic information and a norm component well-approximated by the dataset's global average, validated across RGB and multiple VAE latent spaces. Building on this, two methods are proposed: SOT-CFM replaces the Euclidean transport cost in mini-batch OT-CFM with an angular metric c_ang = arccos(⟨x₀,x₁⟩/(‖x₀‖‖x₁‖)), making optimal pairings invariant to magnitude and aligned with perceptual similarity; and SFM constrains both source and target distributions to a hypersphere of radius r, using spherical linear interpolation (slerp) as the geodesic path and its closed-form tangent velocity as the regression target, with the loss computed via the Riemannian inner product on the tangent space. The spherical projection eliminates norm variability, letting the model dedicate capacity to directional semantics, while the geodesic dynamics in SFM provide a geometrically principled transport that outperforms both Euclidean and spherically-projected Euclidean baselines on gFID, sFID, IS, and downstream classification accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, SFM achieves the best gFID of 3.79, outperforming I-CFM (4.29) and OT-CFM (4.30). On ImageNet-256 (class-conditional), SFM attains gFID 4.62, sFID 7.51, and IS 337.85, surpassing I-CFM (gFID 5.29, IS 236.37) and OT-CFM (gFID 5.22, IS 247.71) across nearly all metrics. In a downstream ResNet-50 classification evaluation, SFM-generated samples achieve 87.13% Top-1 accuracy, exceeding even the original ImageNet validation set (80.35%), while I-CFM samples score only 61.83%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against I-CFM (Lipman et al., 2023), SFM wins decisively on every metric: CIFAR-10 gFID drops from 4.29 to 3.79 (−0.50), ImageNet-256 gFID from 5.29 to 4.62 (−0.67), and IS jumps from 236.37 to 337.85 (+101.48). Against OT-CFM (Tong et al., 2024), SFM similarly dominates: CIFAR-10 gFID 3.79 vs. 4.30, ImageNet-256 gFID 4.62 vs. 5.22, and IS 337.85 vs. 247.71. The intermediate SOT-CFM variant (angular OT cost without full manifold constraint) yields smaller but consistent gains over OT-CFM (e.g., ImageNet-256 gFID 5.15 vs. 5.22), confirming that the angular metric alone provides a modest benefit. The largest single improvement comes from the full Riemannian formulation (SFM), which constrains both distributions and flow paths to the hypersphere, suggesting that the geodesic dynamics contribute substantially beyond what projection or angular coupling alone can achieve. Notably, SFM's advantage is most pronounced on the harder ImageNet-256 benchmark, where the IS gap over I-CFM exceeds 100 points, indicating that spherical geometry becomes increasingly beneficial at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023) and its optimal-transport variant OT-CFM (Tong et al., 2024) by replacing Euclidean geometry with spherical geometry, drawing directly on the Riemannian Flow Matching formulation of Chen & Lipman (2024). The critical departure from prior Riemannian generative work (Riemannian CNF, Riemannian Diffusion, FlowMM) is that those methods assumed a known manifold dictated by domain symmetries (crystals, molecules), whereas this paper discovers the manifold structure empirically from the data itself—showing natural images concentrate on a hypersphere. The angular-cost OT coupling (SOT-CFM) and geodesic-path flow (SFM) are thus the first instances of Riemannian FM applied to unstructured natural image data rather than pre-specified geometric domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that semantic content in natural images is predominantly directional, with norms well-approximated by a global average, provides a simple yet principled geometric prior that the field has not exploited. This opens a path toward geometry-aware image generation without requiring domain-specific symmetry assumptions, and the downstream classification result (SFM achieving 87% Top-1 vs. 80% for original images) suggests geodesic dynamics may actively enhance semantic structure. Open questions include whether the hyperspherical assumption holds for video, 3D, or highly homogeneous datasets (CelebA-HQ shows latent-space degradation), whether more complex manifolds (e.g., products of spheres) capture residual norm information, and whether the radius hyperparameter's effect generalizes across architectures and scales.
