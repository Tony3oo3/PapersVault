## Problem

Inference-time scaling for image generation requires searching over the high-dimensional initial noise space, but existing methods either intervene intrusively in the denoising trajectory, require differentiable rewards, or waste budget on isotropic search where most perturbation directions have negligible visual impact. The paper identifies that this inefficiency stems from a spectral bias: model sensitivity to initial perturbations decays rapidly with frequency, yet no prior method explicitly exploits this structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265511/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SES takes a text prompt and an arbitrary (potentially non-differentiable) reward function as input, and produces a single reward-aligned image from a pre-trained generative model (SD v1.5, SDXL, FLUX.1-dev, or Qwen-Image). The computational budget is measured in Number of Reward Evaluations (NRE), where each evaluation requires a full denoising trajectory plus one reward model call. A proxy evaluation option (4-step LCM or 10-step ODE) can replace full 50-step decoding during search, reducing per-candidate cost by ~85% while preserving ranking consistency.

## Method

SES constrains initial noise optimization to a low-frequency subspace via J-level Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT), freezing all high-frequency coefficients and optimizing only the coarse LL band, which reduces search dimensionality by a factor of 4^J. Within this compact subspace, a Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) evolutionary loop iteratively samples candidates, evaluates their full denoised images against the reward, and updates a diagonal-covariance Gaussian distribution toward elite high-reward regions. The theoretical justification is the Spectral Scaling Prediction (G(ω) ∝ ||ω||^(-β/2)), derived from perturbation propagation dynamics of the generative ODE, which shows that the denoiser acts as a frequency-domain Wiener filter and low-frequency perturbations enjoy exponentially larger cumulative gain than high-frequency ones. This spectral constraint also provides implicit regularization against reward hacking by blocking access to high-frequency adversarial directions.

## Results

Under a fixed budget of NRE=200, SES achieves the best reward scores across all five evaluation metrics on both SDXL and FLUX.1-dev. On SDXL, SES reaches 43.53 CLIP, 31.45 HPSv2, and 6.55 Aesthetic Score, outperforming the strongest baseline (BoN) by 0.23, 0.59, and 0.20 points respectively. On FLUX.1-dev, SES attains 40.18 CLIP, 30.68 HPSv2, and 7.03 Aesthetic Score, again leading all competitors. When the budget is extended to NRE=1000, SES maintains continuous performance growth while BoN plateaus after NRE≈200, confirming superior scaling behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SES outperforms all baselines on every metric across both architectures. Against the initial-noise-optimization baselines from Ma et al. (CVPR 2025), SES leads BoN by 0.23 CLIP and 0.59 HPSv2 on SDXL, and by 0.68 CLIP and 0.58 HPSv2 on FLUX.1-dev; the margins over ZO-N and SoP are even larger (e.g., 2.79 CLIP over SoP on SDXL). Against the particle-filtering baseline SMC (Kim et al., ICLR 2025), SES gains 1.00 CLIP and 0.74 HPSv2 on SDXL, while SMC is incompatible with the ODE-based flow-matching models. Against SVDD (Li et al., NeurIPS 2025), SES leads by 2.03 CLIP and 1.10 HPSv2 on SDXL. Against Demon (Yeh et al., ICLR 2025), SES achieves 1.72 higher CLIP and 0.42 higher HPSv2 on SDXL. Notably, SES is the only method evaluated on both latent-diffusion and flow-matching architectures, and it maintains its advantage in both settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the initial noise optimization paradigm introduced by Ma et al. (2025) by replacing its isotropic, full-dimensional search with a spectrally-constrained low-frequency subspace search. The key conceptual departure is the use of Discrete Wavelet Transform to decouple the noise space and the derivation of a Spectral Scaling Prediction from perturbation propagation dynamics, which formally justifies why low-frequency components dominate generation sensitivity. It also connects to the broader spectral bias literature in diffusion models (Wang & Pehlevan, 2026; Falck et al., 2025), but repurposes those insights for inference-time search rather than training dynamics or sampling acceleration. The Cross-Entropy Method serves as the optimizer, making the framework gradient-free and compatible with non-differentiable rewards, in contrast to prior gradient-based noise optimization approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SES provides a principled, plug-and-play framework for inference-time alignment that works across both latent-diffusion and flow-matching architectures, multiple samplers, and diverse reward functions without requiring model retraining. The Spectral Scaling Prediction (G(ω) ∝ ||ω||^(-β/2)) offers a theoretically grounded explanation for the spectral structure of the effective search landscape, which could inform future search strategies beyond image generation. Limitations include reliance on reward model quality, the local linearization and spectral decoupling assumptions in the theory, and the fact that NRE does not map linearly to wall-clock latency. Open questions include whether the spectral structure changes with model scale or architecture, and whether higher-order interactions captured by the full Jacobian (beyond the diagonal approximation) could further refine the search subspace.
