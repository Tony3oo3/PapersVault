## Problem

Standard regret notions assume the environment is oblivious to the learner's actions, which fails when facing a strategic adversary that adapts its behavior to the learner's recent policies. Policy regret, which benchmarks against the counterfactual outcome of committing to a fixed policy, was previously established only for bandit settings. This paper extends policy regret minimization to partially observable Markov games (POMGs) with structured dynamics, where the learner must simultaneously infer latent world dynamics from partial observations and account for an adversary whose response is entangled with those hidden states.

## Input / Output

The learner receives partial observations o^A at each of H steps per episode and maintains observation-action histories; the adversary's response at episode t depends on the learner's most recent m policies through a fixed reaction rule R. The algorithm consumes a model class Ξ = Θ × Ψ (joint world and adversary parameters) and a policy class Π, and outputs a single optimistic policy per epoch (a mapping from learner histories to action distributions). The theoretical output is a policy regret bound Õ(H√(β_T · d_E · T) + mH log T), where the benchmark V^{π*}(R_∞(π*)) evaluates the best fixed policy against the stationary adversary response it would have induced, not against the observed adversary sequence.

## Method

The core algorithm is an epoch-based optimistic maximum-likelihood estimator that deploys one policy per epoch with geometrically capped lengths T_e = 2^e, building confidence sets cumulatively from all past data. A key structural ingredient is a causal decomposition (Theorem 2.5) that factors each observable operator J_h^{ξ,π} into a world channel W_h^θ (depending only on transition/emission kernels) and an adversary aggregation G_h^{Φ,π} (depending only on the response model and deployed policy), enabling separate likelihood-based estimation of both components. A statistical termination test recomputes the MLE after each episode and ends the epoch early if the deployed optimistic model falls below a deviation-aware threshold, capping per-epoch prediction error and making single-policy commitment safe. The regret analysis tracks two channel-specific error classes through the aggregate Eluder dimension d_E = d_agg^W + d_agg^G, with a matching Ω(√(d_E · T)) lower bound established via a fully revealing POMG embedding of a d-armed bandit. The framework further extends to unknown horizons (via prefix-stable confidence sets) and γ-fading memory adversaries (via extended warm-up of length ⌈log(2T)/(1−γ)⌉).

## Results

The main upper bound (Theorem 3.3) establishes PR(T) = Õ(H√(β_T · d_E · T) + mH log T) policy regret for an m-memory adaptive adversary in a weakly revealing POMG, where d_E is the channel-sum aggregate Eluder dimension and β_T is the MLE confidence radius. A matching lower bound (Theorem 4.1) shows E[PR(T)] = Ω(√(d_E · T)) even for a fully revealing POMG with a fixed memoryless adversary, confirming minimax optimality in T and d_E. The tabular instantiation (Theorem 5.3) gives explicit dependence on |S|, |A|, |B|, |O_A|, |O_B|, and the adversary feature dimension d_adv. A fixed-epoch-schedule variant provably incurs linear regret (Proposition D.5), establishing that the statistical termination test is necessary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is the first to achieve √T policy regret in partially observable Markov games against an adaptive adversary, a setting where no prior result existed. Compared to Arora et al. (2012), who obtained Õ(T^{2/3}) policy regret for bandits against m-memory adversaries, the present work improves the rate to Õ(√(d_E T)) by exploiting the structured POMG dynamics via observable operators and Eluder-dimension-based summability. Liu et al. (2022b) achieved polynomial sample complexity for POMGs but measured PAC error against the maximin value with a worst-case (non-adaptive) opponent, not policy regret against an adaptive one. Qiu et al. (2023) obtained Õ(√T) regret for POMGs via posterior sampling, but their benchmark is the Nash value under self-play or an oblivious adversary, not the counterfactual policy-regret benchmark that accounts for the adversary's strategic response to the learner's committed policy. Nguyen-Tang & Arora (2024, 2025) studied policy regret in fully observable Markov games with adaptive adversaries; the present paper extends their framework to the partially observable setting, where the causal decomposition separating world dynamics from adversary response is essential for tractable likelihood-based estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two research threads: (1) policy regret minimization against adaptive adversaries, initiated in bandits by Arora et al. (2012) and extended to fully observable Markov games by Nguyen-Tang & Arora (2024, 2025), and (2) sample-efficient POMDP learning via weak revealing and observable operator models (Liu et al., 2022a, 2023). The key conceptual departure is a causal decomposition (Theorem 2.5) that factors the POMG observable operator into a world-channel component depending only on transition/emission kernels and an adversary-aggregation component depending only on the response model, enabling separate likelihood-based estimation of each. The epoch-based optimistic MLE algorithm with statistical termination draws on low-switching-cost RL (Bai et al., 2019; Qiao et al., 2022), but here the motivation is adversarial re-stabilization rather than operational switching cost, and the paper proves (Proposition D.5) that the termination test is provably necessary rather than merely convenient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first complete theoretical treatment of policy regret in POMGs, with matching upper and lower bounds establishing that Õ(√(d_E T)) is both achievable and unavoidable. The negative result that fixed geometric schedules provably incur linear regret (Proposition D.5) is a clean structural insight with implications beyond this setting. Open questions include computational tractability of the optimistic planning step (PSPACE-complete in general), extension to multi-player games and non-stationary adversaries, and whether the causal decomposition generalizes to continuous state-action spaces. The paper is purely theoretical with no deployed systems, so practical impact depends on future algorithmic developments for structured subclasses.
