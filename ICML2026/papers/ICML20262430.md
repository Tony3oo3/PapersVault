## Problem

Long-context LLM inference is bottlenecked by the massive KV cache, and existing sparse attention methods rely on proxy scores that are either computationally expensive, biased, or lack theoretical error guarantees. This limits them to fixed-budget Top-k retrieval, which cannot adapt to the highly variable attention sparsity across layers, heads, and tasks, leading to either wasted computation or degraded accuracy.

## Input / Output

During prefill, the method consumes the full Key matrix (L×D) and a random orthogonal rotation matrix P to produce a 1-bit-per-dimension binary index and a per-token scalar correction factor α. During decode, it takes the current query vector, the pre-computed binary index, α, and centroids, and outputs a sparse attention result computed over only the Top-p selected tokens plus a local sliding window. The key distinction from prior work is that the proxy score is an unbiased estimator of the true attention magnitude (not merely a relative ranking), which is what enables threshold-based Top-p selection rather than fixed-k.

## Method

RaBitQCache maps each Key vector onto a vertex of a randomly rotated unit hypercube via a random orthogonal matrix P, storing only the D sign bits (1-bit quantization) plus a scalar correction factor α per token. At decode time, the query is rotated and uniformly quantized to INT4, and the expensive floating-point inner product is replaced by a custom INT4×Binary GEMV kernel whose result, divided by α, yields an unbiased estimate of the normalized cosine similarity with a proven O(1/√D) error bound (Theorem A.1). Because the estimator is unbiased in magnitude, it supports adaptive Top-p retrieval that dynamically adjusts the token budget per layer/head/task. System-level optimizations include asynchronous pipelining that hides prefill index construction behind dense attention, lazy batched quantization during decode to amortize kernel launch overhead, packed 32-bit binary storage for 8× bandwidth reduction, and shared-memory tiling of the INT4 query across thread blocks.

## Results

On LongBench (13 tasks), RaBitQCache with adaptive Top-p (p=0.95, ~17.3% token budget) achieves 36.21 on LongChat-7B and 50.6 on LLaMA-3.1-8B, matching or exceeding full attention (35.78 and 50.58) while using only ~17% of the KV cache. On LLaMA-3.1-70B, it scores 54.58 vs. 54.62 for full attention. End-to-end, RaBitQCache delivers a 2.16× system-level speedup and up to 3.88× decoding speedup at 30K context, with prefill overhead kept below 10% via asynchronous pipelining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaBitQCache outperforms all 8 baselines on the primary LongBench evaluation. Against Quest, RaBitQCache achieves comparable or better scores (50.60 vs. 49.24 on LLaMA-3.1-8B) while using only 17.3% of the token budget versus Quest's 40.3%; on LongChat, Quest-4096 marginally edges RaBitQCache (36.28 vs. 36.21) but at 2.3× the budget. Against DS, RaBitQCache dominates decisively—DS at 40.3% budget scores only 21.28 on LongChat versus RaBitQCache's 36.21 at 17.3%. Against SparQ, RaBitQCache leads on both models (50.60 vs. 50.15 on LLaMA-3.1-8B; 54.58 vs. 53.76 on LLaMA-3.1-70B) and on RULER (79.55 vs. 79.08). In the broader Table 3 comparison, RaBitQCache (50.63) surpasses MagicPIG (49.95), PQCache (50.34), KIVI (50.13), PyramidKV (45.09), and SnapKV (44.91), with the largest gaps over eviction-based methods (PyramidKV, SnapKV) by over 5 points. A key differentiator is that RaBitQCache is the only method supporting adaptive Top-p retrieval with a provable error bound, whereas all baselines are restricted to fixed-budget Top-k or orthogonal compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

RaBitQCache repurposes the RaBitQ binary quantization framework (Gao & Long, 2024) and the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma from approximate nearest neighbor search into the sparse attention setting, constructing an unbiased estimator of attention scores with a provable O(1/√D) error bound. This departs fundamentally from prior sparse attention methods (Quest, DS, SparQ, MagicPIG) that rely on heuristic proxy scores—page-level selection, partial-dimension estimation, or LSH sampling—which provide only relative rankings and thus are restricted to fixed-budget Top-k retrieval. The key conceptual leap is that the JL-based random rotation + sign quantization yields a numerically calibrated score (not just a ranking), enabling adaptive Top-p retrieval for the first time in the KV cache offloading literature. System-level, the paper builds on vLLM/FlashInfer infrastructure and adapts FlashInfer's Top-p sampling kernel for massive-parallel KV retrieval masking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to perform adaptive Top-p retrieval (dynamically adjusting token budget per layer/head based on actual attention sparsity) rather than a rigid Top-k is a practically significant differentiator, as attention patterns vary dramatically across layers and heads. The 2.16× end-to-end speedup with generation quality matching or exceeding full attention (e.g., 36.21 vs. 35.78 on LongChat) makes this immediately deployable in production serving stacks. Open questions include whether the unit-hypersphere uniformity assumption (required for the O(1/√D) bound) holds under the clustered key/query distributions observed in practice (Xing et al., 2026), whether re-centering remains necessary under significant decode-phase distribution drift, and how RaBitQCache composes with orthogonal KV compression methods like KIVI. The paper leaves the integration of adaptive selection followed by post-selection compression as future work.
