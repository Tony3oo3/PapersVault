## Problem

Existing Neural SDE-based models for irregular time series face a fundamental trade-off: path-based variational inference is computationally prohibitive and numerically unstable, while linear SDE approximations (e.g., ACSSM, CRU) sacrifice expressivity by enforcing strict decaying constraints and non-Markovian posteriors. The paper identifies that prior methods unnecessarily target the full continuous-time path measure when only discrete observations are available, and that the resulting linear restrictions prevent modeling of nonlinear, multimodal, and oscillatory dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method takes irregularly sampled multivariate time series (e.g., 37 clinical features on Physionet, 12 sensor features on Human Activity, 24×24 pixel images on Pendulum) with their associated timestamps as input, processed through a Transformer encoder with sinusoidal Fourier time embeddings. Outputs include latent state samples at arbitrary query timestamps (for interpolation/extrapolation), class labels, regression targets, and full posterior distributions parameterized as a Gauss-Markov chain with per-timestamp means, standard deviations, and correlation coefficients. For the Pendulum task, the input modality is image-based, with observations corrupted by temporally correlated noise.

## Method

SDE-VI performs a paradigm shift from path-based to marginal-based variational inference: instead of learning a posterior SDE over the continuous interval, it directly optimizes the joint distribution over discrete observation timestamps, proving this yields a tighter ELBO (Theorem 4.1). The key mechanism is the Entropy Increment Parameterization (EIP), which constrains correlation coefficients to γ = exp(−s̄·Δt) so that the learned Gauss-Markov distribution is guaranteed to be induced by a valid Linear Time-Varying SDE, bypassing the contractive F≺0 constraint that limits prior methods. A lightweight Transformer encoder with sinusoidal time embeddings predicts the distribution parameters {μ, σ, γ} at all timestamps in parallel, and sampling uses a recursive reparameterization trick with O(log N) parallel scan. The framework extends to nonlinear, multimodal dynamics via a stack of time-conditioned Glow-style Conditional Normalizing Flows (proven to induce a nonlinear SDE via Itô's lemma), and to oscillatory dynamics via complex-valued SDEs where the imaginary part of the drift encodes angular frequency.

## Results

On Physionet interpolation, SDE-VI achieves a test MSE of 0.052×10⁻², more than doubling the performance of the second-best method ACSSM (0.116×10⁻²). On USHCN extrapolation, SDE-VI reaches 0.455×10⁻² MSE, nearly halving the error of ACSSM (0.941×10⁻²). For classification, SDE-VI attains 91.9% accuracy on Human Activity (vs. 91.4% for ACSSM), and for regression, 2.68×10⁻³ MSE on Pendulum (vs. 2.98×10⁻³ for ACSSM). SDE-VI also achieves the best NLL across all tasks (e.g., −4.14 on Physionet extrapolation vs. −3.96 for ACSSM) and runs 4.5×–7.5× faster than CRU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDE-VI outperforms all 11 baselines on Physionet interpolation (0.052 vs. 0.116 for ACSSM, the strongest competitor) and USHCN extrapolation (0.455 vs. 0.941 for ACSSM), with particularly large margins over ODE-based methods (Latent-ODE, ODE-RNN, GRU-ODE-B) that cannot model stochasticity. On USHCN interpolation, SDE-VI (0.044) trails ACSSM (0.006), RKN-Δt (0.009), and CRU (0.016), which the authors attribute to USHCN's smooth, stationary dynamics favoring the convex-combination priors in those methods. For classification, SDE-VI achieves 91.9% on Human Activity, edging out ACSSM (91.4%) and mTAND (91.1%); for Pendulum regression, it reaches 2.68×10⁻³ MSE, beating ACSSM (2.98) and Latent-SDEH (3.84). In uncertainty quantification (NLL), SDE-VI leads on every task, e.g., −5.10 on USHCN extrapolation vs. −4.52 for ACSSM and −4.15 for CRU. Computationally, SDE-VI is 4.5×–7.5× faster than CRU and ~2× faster than ACSSM, confirming that the marginal-based formulation avoids expensive path simulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDE-VI departs from the dominant path-based ELBO paradigm in Neural SDEs (Li et al. 2020; Zeng et al. 2023) by shifting inference from continuous path measures to the joint distribution over discrete observation timestamps, proving the resulting marginal-based ELBO is strictly tighter via a data-processing-inequality argument. It generalizes the linear-SDE variational inference of Course & Nair (2023) and Park et al. (2025) by removing the contractive constraint F≺0 through the Entropy Increment Parameterization, and extends expressivity via conditional normalizing flows (Glow architecture) and complex-valued SDEs for oscillatory dynamics. The framework also subsumes Kalman-filtering-based approaches (Schirmer et al. 2022; Ansari et al. 2023) by supporting nonlinear, state-dependent priors through Gaussian approximations of the Fokker-Planck equation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The marginal-based ELBO eliminates the need for expensive continuous-path simulation, yielding 4.5×–7.5× speedups over CRU while maintaining or improving accuracy across four diverse benchmarks. The EIP provides a physically grounded parameterization (entropy production rate) that replaces ad-hoc decay constraints, and the complex-valued extension offers a principled inductive bias for periodic/quasi-periodic signals. Open questions include whether the linear-base-plus-flow decomposition is optimal compared to directly learning nonlinear SDE posteriors, how the method scales to very high-dimensional latent spaces, and whether the locally-LTI prior assumption (adopted for fair comparison) limits the framework's full potential.
