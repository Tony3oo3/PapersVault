## Problem

Existing genomic foundation models treat DNA as a one-dimensional token sequence, which is structurally misaligned with the sparse, discontinuous nature of genomic function: most computation is wasted scanning low-information background rather than modeling functional regions, and the paradigm lacks understanding-driven compression, causing runtime and memory to scale poorly with sequence length. The paper asks whether reframing genomic modeling as OCR-style document understanding can simultaneously address both the structural misalignment and the compression bottleneck.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a DNA sequence rendered as a multi-page document of 640×640 RGB images (monospace text, font_size=14, ~1800 nucleotides per page) together with a task prompt, and produces task-specific outputs: free-form DNA transcription, grounded sequence–bounding-box pairs, masked-region completions, subsequence match locations, or chromosome-level class labels. Each rendered page carries nucleotide-level bounding-box annotations (character, global index, pixel rectangle) that enable coordinate-indexed region operations. For downstream prediction, per-page visual features are mean-pooled across pages and flattened into a fixed-dimensional embedding fed to a lightweight linear or MLP head.

## Method

OpticalDNA renders 1D DNA sequences into structured 2D visual layouts (multi-page documents) and trains an OCR-capable vision-language model comprising a frozen SAM-Conv-CLIP-L visual encoder, a learned projector, a multi-page fusion module (single-layer multi-head self-attention with 20 heads followed by mean reduction to L=100 compact tokens), and a DeepSeek-3B Mixture-of-Experts document decoder (570M activated parameters) fine-tuned with LoRA. Pretraining uses six prompt-conditioned tasks (T1–T6) spanning four genomic primitives—recognition, grounding, retrieval, and completion—formulated as a unified two-turn multimodal conversation interface over the rendered pages. Only the fusion module, projector, and LoRA adapters are trained, enabling downstream adaptation with as few as 256K trainable parameters. The key insight is that the document-style 2D representation converts discontinuous long-range dependencies into spatial structure amenable to region-aware inductive biases, while the visual tokenization provides ~20× compression relative to base-level tokens while preserving reconstructible fine-grained nucleotide information.

## Results

On DNALONGBENCH eQTL prediction (450 kb sequences, 9 GTEx tissues), OpticalDNA achieves 0.852 average AUROC with only 256K trainable parameters (linear probe) and 0.867 with a lightweight MLP head (1.3M–2.3M), outperforming all sequence-based baselines by 7.7–65.8% relative. On RiceWGPB (~400M-base whole-genome input), OpticalDNA (409M) achieves the lowest RMSE on both traits (TGW: 2.952 g, LRI: 9.531%) while reducing inference time to 12.3 minutes versus 5 h 40 min for Evo-2 (7B). The model uses ~20× fewer effective tokens than base-level tokenization and surpasses the Enformer expert model (252M activated params) with up to 985× fewer activated parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpticalDNA outperforms every sequence-based baseline on DNALONGBENCH eQTL: it beats JanusDNA (0.852 vs. 0.791 linear / 0.840 MLP) with ~30× fewer trainable parameters, GENERator-1.2B (0.852 vs. 0.782, +9.0%), NT-v2-500M (0.852 vs. 0.772, +10.4%), Caduceus-Ph (0.852 vs. 0.750, +13.6%), and HyenaDNA (0.852 vs. 0.514, +65.8%). Against the Enformer expert model (252M activated params, 0.681 AUROC), OpticalDNA's 256K-parameter linear probe achieves +25.1% relative gain with up to 985× fewer activated parameters. On RiceSubBench, OpticalDNA (409M) wins on accuracy across all five subspecies splits, improving over LucaOne (1.8B) by +8.5% to +13.9% under OOD shift and consistently outperforming Evo-2 (7B). On RiceWGPB, OpticalDNA achieves the best RMSE on both traits (TGW 2.952 vs. Evo-2's 3.056 and LucaOne's 8.817) while running 28× faster than Evo-2 and 2.6× faster than LucaOne. In the controlled 1D-vs-2D comparison (Appendix A), 2D CNN backbones (e.g., TinyNet-E, 0.786 AUROC) outperform all 1D CNN, RNN, linear-attention, sparse-attention, and 1D Transformer variants, confirming that the document-style representation itself—rather than backbone scale—drives the gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpticalDNA builds directly on DeepSeek-OCR's visual front-end (SAM-Conv-CLIP-L) and DeepSeek-3B Mixture-of-Experts decoder, repurposing an OCR-capable vision-language architecture for genomic sequences. It departs from the dominant genomic foundation model lineage (Nucleotide Transformer, HyenaDNA, Caduceus, JanusDNA, Evo-2, LucaOne), which uniformly treats DNA as a 1D token stream processed by sequence Transformers or hybrid attention architectures. Conceptually, the paper bridges two previously disconnected literatures—large-scale OCR/document understanding (LayoutLM, Pix2Struct, Nougat) and genomic sequence modeling—by casting nucleotide reading, region grounding, subsequence retrieval, and masked completion as prompt-conditioned document tasks over rendered DNA pages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~20× effective token reduction and the ability to process a ~400 M-base rice genome in 12 minutes (vs. hours for Evo-2) address a real computational bottleneck for whole-genome-scale prediction, making long-context genomic modeling more accessible. The controlled 1D-vs-2D comparisons (matched token budget, shared backbone) provide credible evidence that the document-style representation itself—rather than backbone scale—drives the accuracy gain, strengthening the case for the paradigm. Open questions include whether the visual rendering generalizes beyond nucleotide sequences (RNA, protein), whether the OCR-style pretraining objectives transfer to tasks requiring explicit 3D chromatin structure, and whether the gains persist as genomic benchmarks grow in diversity and difficulty beyond the current eQTL and rice phenotype settings.
