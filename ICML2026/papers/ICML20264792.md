## Problem

Modeling the probability density of graph-structured data remains challenging: traditional graph-kernel KDE methods rely on handcrafted, fixed kernels that fail to capture complex structural patterns and use non-adaptive bandwidths, while deep graph anomaly detection methods bypass explicit density modeling and lack theoretical guarantees. No prior framework unifies expressive deep graph representation learning with principled kernel density estimation while providing consistency, convergence, and robustness guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264792/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of attributed graphs (adjacency matrix + node feature matrix) as input and produces a scalar density estimate per graph, which is negated to form the anomaly score. The KDE operates on pairwise MMD distances (intrinsic dimension set to 1) using five logarithmically spaced bandwidths {10⁻², 10⁻¹, 10⁰, 10¹, 10²} with learnable softmax mixture weights, and the detection threshold is set as the negative γ_TH-th percentile of reference-set densities (γ_TH = 0.1).

## Method

LGKDE represents each graph as a distribution over GNN node embeddings and computes inter-graph distances via a deep Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) metric that takes the supremum over a family of Gaussian kernels to capture multi-scale structure. A multi-scale KDE with learnable mixture weights is placed on these MMD distances, and all parameters (GNN weights θ and KDE mixing weights α) are trained end-to-end by maximizing the density ratio of original graphs to their structure-aware perturbed counterparts. Perturbations combine node-feature swapping with an energy-based spectral strategy that performs SVD on the adjacency matrix, partitions singular values into high/mid/low energy groups via cumulative energy thresholds (τ₁=0.5, τ₂=0.75), and selectively scales singular values to generate controlled edge additions or removals. This density-contrasting objective avoids hard anomaly labels, instead using the soft density gap as the training signal, and the paper establishes consistency, minimax-optimal convergence rate O(N^{-4/(4+d_int)}), robustness bounds under graph perturbations, and a generalization bound that scales only linearly with √n.

## Results

LGKDE achieves the highest average AUROC of 79.37% and AUPRC of 84.01% across 12 TU benchmark datasets, with the best average FPR95 of 59.92%. It attains the top AUROC on all 12 datasets, with particularly large margins on DHFR (82.58% vs. SIGNET's 72.87% and MUSE's 71.45%), COX2 (86.69% vs. next-best 72.35%), and REDDIT-B (88.12% vs. next-best 87.43%). The average rank across all datasets is 1.67, confirming consistent superiority over all 10 baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGKDE outperforms all 10 baselines in average AUROC (79.37%), AUPRC (84.01%), and FPR95 (59.92%). The closest deep-learning competitors are SIGNET (77.34% avg AUROC), MUSE (76.63%), and UniFORM (76.58%), with LGKDE leading by 2–3 points on average; the gap widens substantially on molecular datasets like DHFR (+9.71 over SIGNET) and COX2 (+14.34 over SIGNET). Traditional graph-kernel baselines (PK, WL combined with SVM or iForest) are far behind, averaging only 49–56% AUROC, confirming the limitations of fixed handcrafted kernels. Among deep methods, OCGIN (71.34%) and GLocalKD (70.25%) are the weakest, while OCGTL (70.86%) and GLADC (71.63%) also trail LGKDE by 7–9 points on average. Notably, LGKDE's advantage is most pronounced on datasets with high structural complexity or size heterogeneity (e.g., REDDIT-B with avg. 429 nodes, DD with avg. 284 nodes), aligning with its theoretical generalization bound (Theorem 4.6) that the error scales only linearly with √n.

## Relation to Prior Work

LGKDE sits at the intersection of two previously disconnected lines of work: classical graph kernel density estimation (Parzen KDE applied to fixed kernels like WL and Propagation) and deep graph metric learning via MMD (Sun & Fan, 2024). It departs from traditional KDE by replacing handcrafted, fixed kernels with a GNN-learned MMD metric space, and departs from deep GAD methods (OCGIN, SIGNET, CVTGAD, MUSE) by retaining an explicit density estimation objective rather than learning a one-class boundary or reconstruction loss. The energy-based spectral perturbation strategy for generating contrastive samples is a novel contribution not present in prior graph contrastive learning or anomaly detection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive theoretical guarantees (consistency, minimax-optimal convergence rate, robustness bounds, generalization) for a deep graph density estimator, addressing a gap the authors identify in both kernel-based and deep GAD literature. The density estimation framing offers interpretability (anomaly scores are literally negative density) and a principled thresholding mechanism absent in most deep GAD methods. Open questions include scalability beyond the TU-scale benchmarks (largest dataset has ~430 nodes), whether the specific energy-based perturbation design generalizes to heterogeneous or temporal graphs, and whether the approach extends to other non-Euclidean data types.
