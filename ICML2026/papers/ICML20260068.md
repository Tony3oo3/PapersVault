## Problem

Prior ML attacks on LWE (SALSA, VERDE, PICANTE, FRESCA) could only recover secrets with at most 3 'cruel bits' (unreduced coordinates after BKZ lattice reduction), limiting them to low Hamming weights. As secrets become denser, the number of cruel bits exceeds 3 and the modular dot product wraps around the modulus multiple times, causing transformer models to fail. This paper asks whether larger training sets, data repetition, and improved cool-bit recovery can break through this 3-cruel-bit barrier.

## Input / Output

The method consumes BKZ-reduced LWE pairs (a, b = a·s + ε mod q) where the first c coordinates of a form the unreduced 'cruel region' and the remaining n−c form the reduced 'cool region' with lower variance. The attacker is assumed to know only the Hamming weight h, not the secret itself. Output is a two-stage recovery: 15,000 model-based attempts rank the cruel bits via a distinguisher, and for each cruel-bit guess, cool bits are estimated via stepwise regression, yielding a full secret candidate validated by a statistical test. Training data can be either genuine BKZ-reduced pairs (up to 400M samples, ~42M CPU-hours) or synthetic pairs sampled from the measured coordinate distributions, which yield equivalent recovery rates.

## Method

The core architecture is a 4-layer encoder-only transformer with angular embeddings (each coordinate a_i mapped to (cos(2πa_i/q), sin(2πa_i/q))), a novel cool/cruel binary positional embedding, and max-pooled output decoded to the nearest integer on the unit circle. Training uses MSE loss augmented with a radial penalty P(r) = αr² + β/r² to prevent prediction collapse toward the origin during early training. The key empirical finding is that repeating a smaller dataset (up to 50×) dramatically reduces the number of model-based attempts needed to identify cruel bits, following a power law ln(A_R) = C_R − α_R ln(D) where the exponent α_R increases with repetition R. For cool-bit recovery, the paper replaces the prior linear regression with stepwise regression: iteratively fit a linear model on remaining cool coordinates, identify the weakest (smallest normalized coefficient) feature, assign it zero, and remove it; a 'dual' variant flips all remaining bits and regresses on the complement when ones outnumber zeros, exploiting the sparsity structure to avoid modular wrap-around errors that plague simultaneous regression.

## Results

The attack recovers binary LWE secrets with Hamming weight up to h=70 (n=256, log₂q=20) and h=75 (n=512, log₂q=41), exceeding prior ML attacks (VERDE: h=33 and h=63 respectively) and the non-ML Cool&Cruel attack (h=60). For ternary secrets, the maximum recovered Hamming weight reaches h=55 (n=256, log₂q=20) and h=75 (n=512, log₂q=41). In terms of expected recovery rate E(h), the method recovers 98% of secrets at h=33 (n=256, log₂q=20) versus 33% for VERDE, and 91% at h=63 (n=512, log₂q=41) versus 15% for VERDE. The method also recovers secrets with up to 8 cruel bits, breaking the previous 3-cruel-bit limitation of ML attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VERDE (Li et al., 2023a), the proposed method substantially outperforms on maximum recoverable Hamming weight: h=70 vs 33 for n=256, log₂q=20, and h=75 vs 63 for n=512, log₂q=41. The expected recovery rate gap is even more dramatic: at h=33 (n=256, log₂q=20), VERDE recovers only 33% of secrets (limited to ≤3 cruel bits) while the new method recovers 98% (up to 8 cruel bits); at h=63 (n=512, log₂q=41), VERDE achieves 15% versus 91%. Against the non-ML Cool&Cruel attack (Nolte et al., 2024), the method wins on n=256, log₂q=12 (h=14 vs 12) and n=512, log₂q=41 (h=75 vs 60), and matches on n=512, log₂q=28 (h=12). Against the linear regression cool-bit recovery from Wenger et al. (2025), stepwise and dual stepwise regression clearly dominate: for n=256, log₂q=20 with 20M examples and 30 cool bits, linear regression recovers 13/20 secrets while dual stepwise recovers 20/20. Against FRESCA (Stevens et al.) on ternary secrets at n=512, log₂q=41, the method recovers h=75 versus FRESCA's h=66.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SALSA lineage of transformer-based ML attacks on LWE (SALSA → PICANTE → VERDE → FRESCA), which progressively introduced BKZ preprocessing, improved distinguishers, and angular embeddings. It builds directly on the cool/cruel region decomposition from Nolte et al. (2024) and the data-repetition findings from Charton & Kempe (2024) and Saxena et al. (2025), but departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it overcomes the previously observed 3-cruel-bit ceiling by combining large training sets with repeated examples, and (2) it replaces the linear regression used for cool-bit recovery with a stepwise (iterative elimination) regression that exploits the sparsity and uncorrelated structure of BKZ-reduced data. The introduction of a cool/cruel positional embedding is a small but effective architectural addition over FRESCA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As PQC systems based on LWE with sparse secrets are being standardized, this work provides concrete empirical bounds on their vulnerability, showing that ML attacks can now recover secrets with up to 8 cruel bits and Hamming weights substantially exceeding prior state-of-the-art (e.g., h=70 vs. 33 for n=256, log₂q=20). The empirical scaling law relating model-based attempts to data size and repetition is the first of its kind for LWE cryptanalysis and offers a practical roadmap for scaling attacks to harder parameter regimes. Key limitations include the purely empirical nature of the analysis (no formal characterization of stepwise regression success), the scaling law being fitted on a single secret, and the focus on basic LWE rather than Ring/Module LWE variants used in deployed schemes. Open questions include whether synthetic-data pretraining can further reduce the cost of BKZ reduction, and whether the stepwise regression advantage generalizes to correlated feature settings.
