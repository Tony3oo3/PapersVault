## Problem

LLMs perform well on static medical QA but struggle with the sequential, interactive nature of real clinical diagnosis, frequently suffering from premature diagnostic closure. Optimizing LLMs for multi-round diagnostic environments is further hindered by reward sparsity (feedback only at final diagnosis) and reward hacking (intermediate test-matching rewards cause overfitting to historical records rather than evidence-based reasoning).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261206/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The AI clinician receives an initial clinical context (chief complaint, history of present illness, physical examination findings, and prior test results) extracted from a standardized patient record. At each of up to 8 interaction rounds, the system produces a ranked top-5 differential diagnosis list, a binary stop/continue signal, and a set of diagnostic test requests (multiple items per round permitted). The Diagnostic Environment (an LLM proxy grounded in the original record) returns test results, which are appended to the cumulative history; the final output is the top-ranked hypothesis submitted upon a Stop signal or round limit.

## Method

Salus decomposes clinical reasoning into three specialized agents sharing a single 7B backbone (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct): a Differential Reasoner (DR) generating ranked differentials, a Strategic Controller (SC) deciding stop/continue, and a Workup Proposer (WP) requesting targeted tests. Training is two-stage: a reasoning-aware SFT phase distills multi-round trajectories from a DeepSeek-V3.1 teacher with a Logic-Consistency Filter that discards SC samples where the model stops prematurely with an incorrect top-1; followed by multi-agent RL via GRPO (group size 8) that jointly optimizes DR and SC policies. The key architectural insight is that role decomposition converts a long-horizon sequential RL problem into independent single-step decision instances, enabling dense LLM-as-a-Judge rewards: the DR receives a tiered score (4→0) based on the rank position of the gold diagnosis in its differential list, while the SC receives a risk-averse reward that penalizes stopping with an incorrect diagnosis and rewards stopping only when evidence is adjudicated as sufficient. The WP is excluded from RL due to the intractability of defining ground-truth test requests, and RL states are sampled from SFT data rather than requiring full multi-round environment rollouts, with a KL penalty regularizing against the SFT reference policy.

## Results

On 269 high-complexity cases from CompDiag-Bench, Salus-7B achieves a Top-1 diagnostic accuracy of 83.64% (95% CI: 78.8–87.6), outperforming all open-source and medical-specific LLMs and surpassing GPT-5.2 (80.30%) and Gemini 3 Flash (79.93%). The RL optimization yields a 28.62-point absolute gain over the SFT-only baseline (Salus-Zero-7B, 55.02%), and the model also achieves 91.10% Top-1 accuracy on 483 routine cases. Information Sufficiency reaches 72.86%, indicating thorough evidence gathering, with an average of 2.91 testing rounds and 19.98 total test items requested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Salus-7B achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 269 high-complexity cases, outperforming every baseline. Against proprietary models, it surpasses GPT-5.2 by 3.34 points (83.64% vs. 80.30%) and Gemini 3 Flash by 3.71 points (83.64% vs. 79.93%) in Top-1 accuracy. Among open-source models, the margin over the strongest competitor DeepSeek-V3.2 is 12.26 points (83.64% vs. 71.38%), and over medical-specific models like Baichuan-M2-32B it is 34.94 points (83.64% vs. 48.70%). In the static diagnosis setting (complete record, single-round), Salus-7B also outperforms multi-agent baselines MedAgents (71.7%), MDAgents (71.7%), and DeepRare (78.4%), achieving 86.6% Top-1. Notably, even the SFT-only Salus-Zero-7B (55.02%) exceeds substantially larger models such as Qwen3-32B (43.12%) and Baichuan-M2-32B (48.70%), validating the multi-agent architecture itself. The RL stage contributes an additional 28.62-point gain, with the Strategic Controller's optimization being the critical factor (removing SC RL drops accuracy from 83.64% to 61.71%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two parallel research threads: (1) multi-agent medical reasoning frameworks (MedAgents, MDAgents, DeepRare), which previously operated in single-round static settings, and (2) RL-based optimization of LLMs for sequential medical tasks (AMIE, Qiu et al. 2025a), which suffered from reward sparsity or hacking. The key conceptual departure is the tripartite DR-SC-WP decomposition, which transforms a long-horizon sequential RL problem into step-wise single-round optimization instances, enabling dense LLM-as-a-Judge rewards (building on Zheng et al., 2023) without the pitfalls of matching specific historical test orders. The GRPO algorithm (Shao et al., 2024) provides the RL backbone, but the architectural decomposition is what makes the reward structure tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 7B model, through architectural decomposition and step-wise RL, can match frontier proprietary models (GPT-5.2) on complex sequential diagnosis, suggesting that strategic control (when to stop) matters as much as medical knowledge for diagnostic accuracy. The CompDiag-Bench benchmark and the DR-SC-WP paradigm offer a reusable template for other long-horizon sequential decision tasks where reward sparsity is a bottleneck. Key limitations include text-only modality (no imaging or histopathology), absence of explicit cost/invasiveness modeling in the reward, and reliance on an LLM-simulated environment that may lack real-world stochastic noise. Open questions include whether the SC's learned termination boundary generalizes to multimodal inputs and whether the approach scales to genuinely time-sensitive clinical scenarios.
