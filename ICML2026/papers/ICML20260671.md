## Problem

Medical VLMs process dense visual token sequences, yet clinical decision-making relies on extremely sparse visual evidence, causing distracting patterns that impair reasoning. While grounding-aware visual token pruning (VTP) improves performance at inference time, no unified RL framework exists to internalize active token pruning and token-sparse reasoning into a single trainable policy, leaving the pruning heuristic decoupled from the reasoning objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260671/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a medical image (standard resolution) and a textual query, encoded into a visual token sequence E ∈ R^{N×D} by a frozen ViT encoder. The localization branch outputs a structured response containing a predicted foreground bounding box, a reasoning trajectory, and a diagnostic answer. The token-sparse reasoning branch then consumes the pruned visual representation (reduced to ~77% of original token count via grounding-aware fusion) and produces a second reasoning trajectory and final answer. Both branches use structured output tags (think, bbox, answer) enforced by a format reward, and the full pipeline is compatible with vLLM for accelerated inference.

## Method

ViToS trains a single policy model π_θ with two cascaded branches under a dual-stream RL framework. The localization branch generates a foreground bounding box, which drives grounding-aware VTP: visual tokens inside the box are retained, while background tokens are fused into their most semantically similar foreground token via normalized cosine similarity, preserving global context in a compact representation. The token-sparse reasoning branch then performs group rollouts over the pruned tokens with re-assigned rotary positional embeddings. To resolve the coupled policy learning problem, the paper introduces cross-feedback sequential optimization: the localization branch is first trained with a composite reward (format + IoU + accuracy + cross-feedback accuracy from the reasoning branch), then frozen while the reasoning branch is optimized with its own composite reward (format + accuracy + cross-feedback accuracy from the localization branch). Both branches are optimized via the GRPO objective with KL regularization, and the reciprocal accuracy signals ensure that correct localization is reinforced by downstream reasoning success and vice versa.

## Results

On Lingshu-7B, ViToS (DS-RL + GTP) achieves 68.95% average accuracy across seven medical VQA benchmarks, a 5.27-point gain over the 63.68% instruct baseline, while reducing visual tokens to 77% of the original sequence length. On HuatuoGPT-Vision-7B, the method reaches 59.17% average (104.16% relative to baseline), and on Lingshu-32B it attains 70.21% average (105.64% relative). Inference speedup is approximately 4.5× on 7B and 3.4× on 32B compared to competing pruning methods, with vLLM-compatible execution completing 7B evaluation in 11 minutes versus 47–54 minutes for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against all four heuristic VTP baselines (VisionZip, VScan, FastV, PDrop), ViToS achieves the highest average accuracy on every model scale, reaching 108.27% of baseline on Lingshu-7B, 104.16% on HuatuoGPT-Vision-7B, and 105.64% on Lingshu-32B, whereas competing methods either degrade performance (FastV drops to 94.65% on Lingshu-7B) or barely match the unpruned baseline (VScan at 99.44%). Among medical RL baselines, ViToS outperforms ViTAR (64.0% avg) by 4.9 points (68.9% avg) and MedVLThinker (54.8% avg) by 14.1 points, while requiring only 8 hours of training versus ViTAR's 23 hours. On inference speed, ViToS completes 7B evaluation in 11 minutes versus 47–54 minutes for PDrop, FastV, VisionZip, and VScan respectively, and 53 minutes for 32B versus 171–202 minutes for baselines. Against cropping-based reasoning methods (DeepEyes, PixelReasoner), ViToS achieves substantially higher accuracy (e.g., 77.2% vs. 52.9% on PathVQA), demonstrating that grounding-aware token-level evidence selection is superior to coarse image cropping for medical reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeek-R1 and applies it to medical VLMs (Lingshu, HuatuoGPT-Vision), but departs from prior medical RL work (Med-R1, MedVLM-R1, ViTAR) that optimizes a single ability through one RL branch. It also extends the visual token pruning literature (VisionZip, VScan, FastV, PDrop), which relies on static heuristic rules (attention scores, similarity metrics), by reformulating VTP as a policy-guided evidence selection process learned end-to-end. The key conceptual departure is the dual-stream RL architecture with cross-feedback sequential optimization, which jointly internalizes grounding and token-sparse reasoning within a single shared policy model rather than treating them as independent objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that reducing visual tokens to 77% of the original sequence actually improves medical VQA performance (up to 108% relative) challenges the assumption that denser visual representations are always beneficial, suggesting that in domains with sparse diagnostic evidence, visual redundancy actively degrades reasoning. The cross-feedback sequential optimization is a general technique for coupled policy learning that could transfer beyond medical VLMs. However, the framework is limited to standard-resolution images and a single bounding-box grounding formulation, leaving open how it scales to extreme-resolution whole-slide pathology or multi-region grounding scenarios where the localization action space grows substantially.
