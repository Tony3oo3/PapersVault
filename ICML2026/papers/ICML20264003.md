## Problem

Existing crystal generative models are task-specific, requiring separate architectures and training for crystal structure prediction (CSP), de novo generation (DNG), and atom type generation, with no shared crystal representation across tasks. Additionally, transformer-based crystal models lack inductive biases for crystallographic symmetry and permutation invariance, typically relying on explicit structural templates (e.g., Wyckoff positions) that are unavailable during CSP inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264003/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 2D time coordinate (t, s) ∈ [0,1]² where t parameterizes atom-type corruption (discrete CTMC) and s parameterizes structural corruption (continuous ODE), with different task trajectories: CSP fixes t=1 and integrates s, ATG fixes s=1 and integrates t, and DNG integrates both jointly along the diagonal. Crystal structures are represented as Niggli-reduced lattice lengths/angles, fractional coordinates on the torus T^{3×N}, and discrete atom types, with atoms lexicographically ordered by Pauling electronegativity and Wyckoff position to expose compositional and symmetry structure to the transformer.

## Method

MCFlow decouples flow time axes for atom types and crystal structures, learning a joint generative process over a 2D (t,s) time space where each crystal generation task corresponds to a distinct inference trajectory, enabling any-to-any modality generation from a single trained model. The architecture is a Diffusion Transformer with modality-specific time conditioning (sum of t and s embeddings) and separate prediction heads: categorical logits for atom types (CTMC rate vector), torus-projected fractional coordinates (Riemannian geodesic flow), and lattice parameters (linear flow). To inject crystallographic priors without explicit templates, atoms are lexicographically sorted by electronegativity and Wyckoff position, and a hierarchical permutation augmentation (inter-orbit across orbits sharing the same element/Wyckoff letter, then intra-orbit within each orbit) enforces invariance across symmetry-equivalent sites, reducing the permutation space from N! to ∏|W^i|!∏|O_j^i|!. Training uses a multimodal flow matching objective with modality-specific Bregman divergences (generalized KL for atom types, squared Euclidean for continuous variables), and inference optionally applies noisy guidance to improve single-sample fidelity.

## Results

On crystal structure prediction (CSP), MCFlow-L achieves 64.08% match rate and 0.0561 RMSE on MP-20 (single-sample), and 27.16% MR / 0.1401 RMSE on MPTS-52, outperforming all single-sample baselines. With 20 samples, MCFlow-B reaches 77.12% MR and 0.0439 RMSE on MP-20, and MCFlow-L reaches 41.45% MR and 0.1296 RMSE on MPTS-52, matching or exceeding the best multi-sample baselines. On de novo generation (LeMat-GenBench with pre-relaxation), MCFlow-L achieves 98.6% validity, 18.8% stability, 68.3% metastability, and 0.04±0.08 eV/atom E_hull, leading all baselines on stability-related metrics. For atom type generation, MCFlow-L(20) achieves 90.23% compositional validity and 15.30% match rate on MP-20, substantially outperforming CGCNN (87.06% Comp, 4.71% MR) and FlowMM (83.39% Comp, 2.40% MR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CSP, MCFlow-L (single-sample) outperforms OMatG (64.08% vs 63.75% MR on MP-20) and CrystalFlow (64.08% vs 62.02%), with a larger margin on MPTS-52 (27.16% vs 25.15% and 21.00% respectively); it substantially exceeds CDVAE (33.90%) and DiffCSP (51.49%). In the 20-sample setting, MCFlow-B matches DiffCSP (77.12% vs 77.93% MR) and CrystalFlow (77.12% vs 78.34%) on MP-20 while achieving the best RMSE (0.0439 vs 0.0492 and 0.0577), and clearly leads on MPTS-52 (41.40–41.45% vs 34.02–37.81%). On DNG (LeMat-GenBench with pre-relaxation), MCFlow-L dominates on stability metrics: 18.8% stable vs PLaID++ (12.4%), MatterGen (2.0%), WyFormer (0.5%), and ADiT (0.4%), with E_hull of 0.04 eV/atom vs 0.09 (PLaID++), 0.18 (MatterGen), 0.50 (WyFormer), and 0.33 (ADiT); however, PLaID++ retains an edge on novelty (24.2% vs 18.6%). Without pre-relaxation, MCFlow-L still leads on validity (96.0%), stability (4.9%), and metastability (69.7%) over DiffCSP (2.3% stable), ADiT (0.4%), SymmCD (1.4%), and LLaMat3-CIF (0.1%). For atom type generation, MCFlow-L(20) achieves 90.23% compositional validity and 15.30% MR on MP-20, beating CGCNN (87.06% Comp, 4.71% MR) by ~3 points in validity and ~10 points in match rate, and FlowMM (83.39% Comp, 2.40% MR) by ~7 and ~13 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCFlow extends the multimodal flow paradigm from protein co-design (Campbell et al., 2024) and arbitrary-state-space diffusion (Rojas et al., 2025) to periodic crystal systems, where the key challenge is handling both discrete (atom types) and continuous (coordinates, lattice) modalities under periodic boundary conditions. It builds on Riemannian flow matching on the torus (Chen & Lipman, 2024; Miller et al., 2024) for fractional coordinates and the generator matching framework (Holderrieth et al., 2024) to unify CTMC and ODE dynamics. The main departure from prior crystal generative models is twofold: (1) replacing task-specific architectures with a single DiT-based model that selects inference trajectories in a 2D time plane, and (2) substituting explicit symmetry templates (Wyckoff positions, space-group conditioning) with a soft inductive bias via composition- and symmetry-aware atom ordering plus hierarchical permutation augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified any-to-any formulation eliminates the need to train and maintain separate models for CSP, DNG, and ATG, simplifying the crystal generation pipeline and enabling new capabilities like atom type substitution within a fixed structure. The finding that a DiT can internalize crystallographic symmetry distributions (space groups, Wyckoff multiplicities) from data alone—without explicit symmetry constraints—challenges the assumption that hard symmetry templates are necessary for high-fidelity crystal generation. Key limitations include inapplicability to amorphous or disordered systems (where space groups are undefined) and a stability-novelty trade-off that grows with model scale. Open directions include extending the framework to property-conditioned generation and integrating MCFlow into closed-loop, agent-based materials discovery pipelines.
