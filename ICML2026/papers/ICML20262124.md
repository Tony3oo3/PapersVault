## Problem

Existing conversational query reformulation (CQR) methods that use preference optimization (e.g., RetPo, AdaCQR) require gold reference passages to rank candidate rewrites, but such annotations exist only in small curated benchmarks (~17K conversations) while real-world conversational datasets contain over 7.8M conversations without them. This creates a fundamental scalability bottleneck: the most effective CQR training paradigm is inapplicable to the vast majority of available conversational data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262124/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a conversational query along with its query-response history and produces a single self-contained reformulated query suitable for passage retrieval. During training, the framework additionally consumes raw responses from the conversational dataset, converting them into query format via a template prompt (e.g., 'Can you clearly state the main points of the last response...') so the CQR model can refine them and retrieve pseudo reference passages. The iterative loop alternates between pseudo-reference generation and preference optimization over three epochs, with the top-3 retrieved passages per turn serving as the pseudo supervision signal.

## Method

DUALREFORM eliminates the need for gold reference passages by exploiting the dual-role of the CQR model: the same model that reformulates queries at inference is repurposed during training to refine raw responses into contextually grounded statements, which are then used to retrieve pseudo reference passages. These pseudo references drive standard SFT + DPO preference optimization, and the improved CQR model is fed back to generate better pseudo references in the next iteration, forming a self-reinforcing cycle. Theoretically, the paper proves via pseudo-label denoising bounds that the dual-role configuration achieves a strictly tighter error bound than a single-role variant (which uses a separate LLM for response refinement), because the CQR model's retrieval-optimized parameters yield more accurate pseudo references. Empirically, DUALREFORM reaches 96.9–99.1% of the retrieval accuracy achievable with gold references and outperforms the strongest reference-free baseline (LLM4CS-CoT) by 15.7% on average.

## Results

On SciConvQA (sparse retriever), DUALREFORM achieves MRR 20.06 and R@5 28.64, reaching 96.0% of the Upper Bound (RetPo† with gold references: MRR 20.89). On TopiOCQA (sparse retriever), it achieves MRR 28.39 and R@5 39.57, reaching 97.3% of the Upper Bound (MRR 29.19). Across both datasets and retriever types, DUALREFORM attains 96.9–99.1% of the gold-reference Upper Bound while outperforming the strongest reference-free baseline (LLM4CS-CoT) by an average of 15.70% in retrieval accuracy. On the dense retriever, gains are comparable: MRR 23.53 on SciConvQA and 40.14 on TopiOCQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUALREFORM outperforms all seven baselines on both target datasets and both retriever types. Against the strongest reference-free baseline LLM4CS-CoT (Mao et al., 2023), DUALREFORM improves sparse MRR from 16.53→20.06 on SciConvQA (+21.3%) and 26.81→28.39 on TopiOCQA (+5.9%). Reference-based methods transferred from QReCC (RetPo, AdaCQR, ConvGQR) degrade substantially without gold references on the target: RetPo drops to MRR 15.60 on SciConvQA vs. DUALREFORM's 20.06, and AdaCQR reaches only 15.97. Prompt-engineering methods (LLM-IQR, HyDE-LLM) and the SFT-only T5QR trail further behind, with MRR values of 11–17 on SciConvQA. Notably, DUALREFORM narrows the gap to the gold-reference Upper Bound (RetPo†) to just 1.9–3.1 MRR points, confirming that pseudo reference passages nearly replicate the supervision signal of human-annotated gold passages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the preference optimization line for conversational query reformulation (CQR) initiated by RetPo (Yoon et al., 2025) and AdaCQR (Lai et al., 2025), which both require gold reference passages to construct preference signals. DUALREFORM departs from this by replacing gold references with pseudo references generated via a self-training loop, drawing its theoretical grounding from the pseudo-label denoising framework of Wei et al. (2021). The key conceptual departure is the 'dual-role' mechanism: the same CQR model is used both for query reformulation at inference and for response refinement during training, creating a self-reinforcing cycle that eliminates the need for an external LLM or human annotation. The paper also introduces SciConvQA, a new specialized-domain benchmark, extending CQR evaluation beyond the general-domain QReCC and TopiOCQA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: over 450× more real-world conversational data exists without reference passages than with them, rendering existing preference-optimized CQR methods inapplicable to the vast majority of available training data. DUALREFORM's ability to reach 96.9–99.1% of the gold-reference upper bound without any annotated passages opens the door to training CQR models on large-scale, unannotated conversational corpora. A stated limitation is the reliance on single-dimensional DPO preferences, which cannot capture multi-objective criteria like conciseness; integrating multi-dimensional preference methods (e.g., CPO, SPO) is identified as future work. The new SciConvQA benchmark also fills a gap in evaluating CQR for specialized scientific domains.
