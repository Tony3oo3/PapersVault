## Problem

Standard self-supervised contrastive learning for automatic modulation recognition suffers from three geometric pathologies: isotropic augmentations are almost surely orthogonal to the model's sensitive directions in high dimensions (concentration of measure), self-attention's unbounded Lipschitz constant produces brittle decision boundaries, and static concatenation of physical priors fails to anchor representations against semantic drift in extreme few-shot regimes. Existing methods treat these as separate engineering issues rather than coupled geometric constraints on the signal manifold.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a complex baseband I/Q signal frame of length L (represented as a real-valued 2×L tensor) and outputs a modulation class label from 11 (RML2016.10a) or 24 (RML2018.01a) schemes. In the few-shot evaluation protocol, only N ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10} labeled instances per class per SNR level are available for fine-tuning after self-supervised pre-training. The final prediction is produced by a confidence-aware ensemble of three classification heads whose softmax outputs are squared and normalized, suppressing uncertain predictions in ambiguous few-shot settings.

## Method

DyCo-CL couples three interdependent components. First, a Dynamic-Consistency pre-training objective (built on MoCo-v3) replaces isotropic noise with Virtual Adversarial Augmentation—computed via one-step power iteration on the Hessian of the KL divergence—to target the model's most sensitive direction, while a Semantic Consistency Loss (cosine distance between anchor and adversarial representations with stop-gradient) acts as an implicit spectral regularizer that provably minimizes the encoder's local Lipschitz constant. Second, a Signal-Adaptive Swin Backbone uses a three-layer 1D convolutional stem as a learnable low-pass filter followed by fixed-window (M=8) 1D self-attention without shifting, which enforces a block-diagonal Jacobian structure and structurally decouples the spectral norm from sequence length. Third, a Hierarchical Hybrid Knowledge Fusion module encodes physical priors (fourth-order cycle spectrum and PSD-regularized envelope) through a dual-stream GAF+2D-CNN / Bi-LSTM encoder with learnable gating, then fuses them with the contrastive features via a two-token Transformer where the physics token serves as a stable query to dynamically calibrate the data token, preventing semantic drift during aggressive adversarial exploration.

## Results

On RML2016.10a (11 modulation classes), DyCo-CL achieves 43.84% top-1 accuracy in the extreme 1-shot (N=1) setting, surpassing the strongest baseline by 6.27%. At 10 dB SNR with N=1, the margin widens to 7.7% over SOTA. On the larger RML2018.01a (24 classes), DyCo-CL maintains a 7.54% accuracy lead for SNR > 6 dB in the 10-shot regime. The model is also highly efficient: 1.44M parameters (≈5.8 MB), 14.46M FLOPs, 0.60 ms inference latency, and 1672 samples/s throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyCo-CL outperforms all six baselines (CMSSAN, AMC-CNN, ResNet50-MoCo, EET-MoCo, SSCL-AMC, and APFS) across every evaluated setting on both RML2016.10a and RML2018.01a. In the critical 1-shot regime on RML2016.10a, it achieves 43.84% accuracy, a 6.27% absolute gain over the strongest prior method; at 10 dB SNR the gap widens to 7.7%. On RML2018.01a (24 classes, 10-shot), DyCo-CL sustains a 7.54% margin for SNR > 6 dB, indicating the advantage is not an artifact of the smaller dataset. In terms of efficiency, DyCo-CL reduces FLOPs by 2.5× (36.9M → 14.5M) and triples inference throughput (1672 vs. 498 samples/s) relative to its closest accuracy competitor SSCL-AMC, while being 16× smaller than ResNet50-MoCo (1.44M vs. 23.52M parameters). The only method faster is the ultra-lightweight CMSSAN (0.007 ms latency), but CMSSAN sacrifices substantial accuracy due to its shallow architecture. Overall, DyCo-CL occupies a clear Pareto-optimal position: it matches or exceeds the accuracy of heavier models while meeting sub-millisecond latency requirements for real-time 5G deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MoCo-v3 contrastive learning framework (Chen & Xie, 2021) to RF signal classification by replacing isotropic augmentations with Virtual Adversarial Augmentation and adding a semantic consistency loss, framing both through a geometric lens (concentration of measure, spectral instability). Architecturally, it adapts the Swin Transformer to 1D signals with a fixed-window strategy to structurally bound the Lipschitz constant, departing from prior hybrid CNN-Transformer AMR designs (Kong et al., 2025; Ma et al., 2026) that prioritize efficiency over spectral stability. The physics-aware fusion module generalizes dual-stream concatenation approaches (Deng et al., 2023; Feng et al., 2025) into a dynamic, attention-based calibration mechanism. Conceptually, the paper unifies three previously treated-in-isolation problems—augmentation geometry, attention stability, and knowledge fusion—under a single geometric stability narrative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.27% 1-shot accuracy gain on RML2016.10a is meaningful for practical cognitive radio deployment where labeled data is scarce, and the sub-millisecond latency with 1.44M parameters makes the approach viable for edge 5G/6G applications. The theoretical link between VAA + consistency loss and implicit spectral (Lipschitz) regularization provides a principled justification for the design choices, though it relies on small-epsilon Taylor approximations that may not hold at the practical ε=0.3 used in experiments. Open questions include whether the geometric framing generalizes beyond AMR to other few-shot signal tasks, whether the fixed-window Swin advantage persists at longer sequence lengths, and the authors' own noted direction toward open-set recognition where the closed-set contrastive assumptions break down.
