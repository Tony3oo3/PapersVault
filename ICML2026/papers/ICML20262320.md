## Problem

Reinforcement learning with delayed state observations—where the agent must act before the current state is revealed—lacked tight theoretical guarantees. Prior work by Chen et al. (2023) left a gap of H^{1/2} D_max^2 between upper and lower regret bounds, and it remained unclear whether the delay length fundamentally increases statistical complexity. This paper closes the gap by establishing matching minimax-optimal upper and lower bounds, proving that the optimal delay dependence is D_max^{1/2}.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a finite-horizon tabular MDP with S states and A actions, a stochastic delay distribution P_delay (either known or to be learned), and a maximum delay bound D_max. The agent's policy maps a triple (last observed state, queue of unresolved actions, elapsed time since last observation) to an action, since the true current state is hidden. The output is a sequence of K episodic policies with a regret guarantee of Õ(H√(D_max·SAK)), and the augmented state space is exponential in D_max, making the computational cost inherently exponential.

## Method

The algorithm constructs an augmented MDP with three categories of states—standard states tracking elapsed delay, 'tran' intermediate states that sample the next revealed state, and '-1' intermediate states that determine whether additional states are revealed in the same time step—then applies MVP (UCBVI with Bernstein-type bonuses) under a carefully ordered update schedule. The key structural insight is that the augmented MDP's transition decomposes into a fully known component (action queue shifting) and an unknown component determined solely by the original (s, a) pair, so the algorithm estimates P and P_delay rather than the exponentially large P_aug, storing visit counts of original state-action pairs. The logarithmic confidence factor is inflated from the standard log to D∧b (queue length ∧ branching factor) to account for the union bound over augmented states sharing the same underlying transition. This decomposition is formalized as the general framework of 'MDPs with partially known dynamics,' yielding Theorem 5 whose corollaries recover the SDMDP bounds. A matching lower bound is established via a novel CodeMDP construction that reduces ℓ₁-norm estimation to delayed MDP learning, showing the D_max^{1/2} factor is unavoidable.

## Results

The paper establishes a minimax-optimal regret bound of Õ(H√(D_max · SAK)) for tabular stochastic delayed MDPs (SDMDPs) with known delay distribution (Theorem 1), and Õ(H√(D_max · SAK) + H√(Δ_max · SAK)) when the delay distribution is unknown (Theorem 2). A matching lower bound of Ω̃(H√(D_max · SAK)) is proven via a novel CodeMDP construction (Theorem 3), confirming optimality up to logarithmic factors. The D_max dependency can be further tightened to a per-state-action branching factor B when B < D_max, yielding Õ(H√(B · SAK)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against Chen et al. (2023), who achieved a regret upper bound of Õ(H^{3/2} · D_max^{5/2} · √(SAK)) and a lower bound of Ω(√(H · D_max · SAK)). This paper improves the upper bound by a factor of H^{1/2} · D_max^2, closing the gap between upper and lower bounds up to logarithmic factors. The H^{1/2} improvement comes from using Bernstein-type (variance-dependent) bonus terms via the MVP algorithm, while the D_max^2 improvement stems from a novel analysis that exploits the structure of the augmented MDP's transition dynamics. Walsh et al. (2009) provided a polynomial (ε,δ)-PAC bound of Õ(S²A/(ε³(1-γ)⁶)) for γ-discounted MDPs with constant delay, but their bound is in a different (PAC) framework and does not directly compare. The paper also notes that the lower bound of Chen et al. (2023), Ω(√(H·D_max·SAK)), is actually weaker than the standard MDP lower bound Ω(H√(SAK)) since D_max < H, whereas the new lower bound properly captures the additional √D_max factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the augmented MDP construction for delayed observations introduced by Katsikopoulos & Engelbrecht (2003) and the modern UCBVI-based learning framework of Zhang et al. (2021, MVP), while directly improving upon the stochastic delayed MDP formulation and regret bounds of Chen et al. (2023). The key methodological departure is a novel abstraction—MDPs with partially known dynamics—where the transition kernel decomposes into a known component (the action queue shift) and an unknown but structured component (the state transition), enabling a tighter union bound that replaces the exponential-in-D_max state space with a branching-factor-dependent term. This abstraction subsumes the augmented MDP as a special case and is presented as a general tool applicable beyond delayed observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes the gap between upper and lower regret bounds for tabular delayed MDPs, establishing the minimax-optimal Õ(H√(D_max SAK)) rate and, for the first time, rigorously proving that shorter delays reduce statistical complexity. The computational hardness result (NP-hardness via reduction from 3-SAT) confirms that the exponential time complexity in D_max is unavoidable, setting clear limits on what polynomial-time algorithms can achieve. Open questions include extending the partially-known-dynamics framework to function-approximation settings, handling adversarial or non-stationary delays, and understanding whether the √D_max dependence can be further refined in problem-dependent regimes.
