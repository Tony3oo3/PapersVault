## Problem

Existing de novo ligand design methods suffer from three coupled limitations: they are often 'pseudo de novo' (relying on fixed templates or retrieved scaffolds rather than generating truly from scratch), they incorporate docking knowledge only indirectly via post-hoc scoring rather than continuously coupling it to the generation trajectory, and they are narrowly tailored to a single ligand type (protein or small molecule). MagicDock addresses all three by performing template-free, docking-gradient-driven generation in a unified framework that handles both ligand categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260540/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes only a receptor 3D structure as input, converting it to a solvent-accessible surface point cloud (N=5000 points for proteins, scaled for ligands) with composite chemical, atomic, and geometric features. It produces a de novo ligand—either a protein (residue-level, 20 amino acids) or a small molecule (atom-level, 8 atom types with bond orders)—along with the predicted receptor-ligand complex. Unusually, the ligand point-cloud cardinality is not fixed: atoms can be inserted or deleted along the inversion trajectory, and the continuous surface state is periodically re-anchored to a valid discrete structure via type-specific decoding and RDKit/PyRosetta repair.

## Method

MagicDock is a four-stage pipeline built on a single SE(3)-equivariant surface backbone. Stages 1–2 establish the backbone: atomic structures are converted to SDF-based surface point clouds with patch partitioning (FPS + KNN), then pre-trained via Surface-VQMAE (masked reconstruction with Gumbel-Softmax vector quantization, curvature supervision, and Chamfer distance loss) on large unlabeled protein and small-molecule corpora. Stage 3 fine-tunes the backbone with progressive multi-task supervision—pocket segmentation, pocket-conditioned interaction prediction, and binding free-energy regression—fused through equivariant cross-attention (scalar ℓ=0 attention weights with Wigner-D rotation for higher-order features). Stage 4 performs the actual generation as projected gradient descent: the trained backbone's docking heads define a differentiable composite objective F(S;R,Θ), and the ligand's continuous coordinates and features are refined by (x,f) ← Π_Cvalid((x,f) − η∇F), with non-differentiable chemical validity enforced by projection/repair operators. Type-specific decoders then map the continuous state to discrete structures (softmax-biased atom/residue sampling with gradient-biased proposals, bond inference, motif priors, and rotamer constraints), yielding a unified inversion loop that adapts to both protein and small-molecule ligands without retraining.

## Results

On protein ligand design, MagicDock achieves the best scores across all five metrics on both SKEMPI v2 (IMP 36.32, STA 0.874, DIV 0.815, NOV 0.934, AAR 48.73) and SAbDab (IMP 27.87, STA 0.851, DIV 0.824, NOV 0.957, AAR 46.14), outperforming the strongest baseline Abx by 7.56 IMP points on SKEMPI and 6.07 on SAbDab. On small-molecule design, MagicDock attains the best Vina score (−7.36 on PDBBind, −7.02 on CrossDocked), highest synthetic accessibility (SA 0.69/0.67), and best QED (0.552/0.544), while trailing ALIDIFF only on CrossDocked affinity (62.72 vs. 65.63). Overall, the paper reports average improvements of 7.0% (protein) and 7.4% (molecule) over SOTA baselines across 9 evaluation scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagicDock outperforms all eight protein-ligand baselines (RAbD, DiffAB, HSRN, dyMEAN, ABDPO, chai-1, RoseTTAFold-AA, Abx) on every metric for both SKEMPI v2 and SAbDab, with the largest gap over Abx (the strongest baseline) of +7.56 IMP on SKEMPI and +6.07 on SAbDab. For small-molecule design, MagicDock beats DockStream, 3D-SBDD, liGAN, DRUGFLOW, and DIFFSBDD on all six metrics (Vina, Affinity, STA, DIV, SA, QED) across both PDBBind and CrossDocked. The sole exception is ALIDIFF, which achieves a higher affinity score on CrossDocked (65.63 vs. 62.72) due to its preference-optimization-based energy alignment, though MagicDock surpasses ALIDIFF on Vina (−7.02 vs. −6.79), STA, DIV, SA, and QED on CrossDocked and on all metrics on PDBBind. The paper attributes ALIDIFF's affinity edge to its diffusion-model-based preference optimization, which biases toward high-affinity samples at the cost of diversity and incurs substantially higher computational overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

MagicDock builds on the Surface-VQMAE encoder (Wu & Li, 2024) for SE(3)-equivariant surface pre-training and the SDF-based surface representation from Sverrisson et al. (2021), but departs fundamentally from the dominant sampling-based (diffusion/flow) generation paradigm by recasting de novo ligand design as constrained gradient inversion in a continuous surface latent space. It positions itself against four prior paradigms—decoupled, implicitly coupled (diffusion), surrogate/heuristic-coupled (RL, evolutionary), and latent-gradient coupling—arguing that explicit gradient-driven refinement with type-specific feasibility projections offers better docking coupling, interpretability, and cross-category generality than stochastic trajectories or handcrafted schedules. The key conceptual departure is treating a pretrained docking-aware backbone as a differentiable energy landscape whose gradients directly steer ligand coordinates and features, rather than training a separate likelihood-based sampler.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework addressing both protein and small-molecule ligands in a single template-free pipeline is practically significant for drug discovery, where the same receptor may require different ligand modalities. Theoretical guarantees (convergence of projected updates, SE(3) equivariance) add rigor that most generative ligand design papers lack. However, the feature vocabulary excludes halogens and metal ions common in pharmaceutical compounds, inference is slower than sampling-based methods due to iterative gradient steps, and the affinity metric trails ALIDIFF slightly on molecular benchmarks. Open questions include whether hybridizing the surface backbone with likelihood-based generators (as the authors note) could recover sampling efficiency while retaining gradient controllability, and whether the approach extends to multi-target or allosteric design.
