## Problem

Existing provenance analysis methods are designed for 2D images and cannot directly transfer to 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) models, whose parametric structure (position, color, opacity, scale, rotation per Gaussian) differs fundamentally from pixel grids. Rendering 3DGS to 2D images for analysis discards the 3D structural information that encodes editing intent, yielding ambiguous provenance outcomes. No prior method constructs directed evolutionary graphs over collections of 3DGS models without requiring training or access to editing histories.

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of 3DGS models as PLY files and extracts per-Gaussian attributes (3D positions, SH color coefficients, opacities, scales, rotations) without any rendering step. It outputs a directed acyclic graph (DAG) in JSON format where each directed edge (M_i → M_j) is annotated with a natural-language rationale citing the specific statistical evidence and simulated-edit analogy that supports the inferred transformation. The pipeline is entirely training-free and requires no editing logs or ground-truth modification records.

## Method

GaussTrace formulates provenance as an evidence-based reasoning problem rather than a trace-matching pipeline, built on a dual evidential foundation. First, it computes attribute-wise statistical signatures (mean, std, skewness, kurtosis, entropy) for five semantic groups (position, color, opacity, scale, rotation), augmented with multi-scale voxel-grid local statistics (top-3 most divergent voxels from a 4×4×4 partition) and pairwise Kolmogorov-Smirnov p-values to flag significantly altered attributes. Second, it runs hypothesis-driven simulations of four common edits (deletion, addition, geometric shift, appearance tuning) from both directions, quantifying their effects via Average Treatment Effect (ATE) scores that serve as semantic anchors rather than classifier features. These heterogeneous cues are encoded into a structured prompt that drives a large language model (GPT-5) through a four-step Chain-of-Thought protocol: comparing statistics, interpreting simulated edits as illustrative examples, confirming changes via significant KS tests, and hypothesizing transformations flexibly. The LLM acts as a symbolic reasoning engine that synthesizes evidence into a coherent causal narrative, producing directional provenance inferences with auditable, human-readable justifications for every edge.

## Results

On the 3DGS provenance graph dataset (44 models from Mip-NeRF360, InstructNeRF2NeRF, and Deep Blending), GaussTrace achieves perfect Vertex Overlap (VO = 1.000), Edge Overlap (EO = 0.890), and Vertex Edge Overlap (VEO = 0.948). The best image-based baseline (ViT-B + Integrity) reaches only EO = 0.470 and VEO = 0.751, while the geometry-based baseline (Points CD + Integrity) scores EO = 0.270 and VEO = 0.532. Even the rule-based search baseline that uses the same statistical descriptors and editing simulations as GaussTrace but replaces LLM reasoning with deterministic scoring achieves only EO = 0.319 and VEO = 0.566.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussTrace outperforms all baselines on every metric, with the largest margin on Edge Overlap (EO), which measures directional relationship accuracy. Among image-based feature extractors, ViT-B + Integrity is the strongest (EO = 0.470), still trailing GaussTrace by 42 percentage points; ResNet and DenseNet variants score even lower (EO = 0.205–0.319). The geometry-based Chamfer Distance baseline (Points CD) performs worst overall, with VO dropping to 0.758 and EO at 0.268–0.270, confirming that point-cloud distance alone cannot capture 3DGS editing provenance. The MI-based direction predictor (Moreira et al., 2018) consistently underperforms the Integrity-score predictor (Zhang et al., 2020) across all feature backbones, with EO gaps of roughly 8–14 points. The rule-based search baseline, which shares GaussTrace's statistical descriptors and editing simulations but uses deterministic scoring instead of LLM reasoning, achieves EO = 0.319—nearly 57 points below GaussTrace—demonstrating that structured CoT reasoning, not just evidence extraction, is the critical differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established image provenance analysis pipeline (pairwise dissimilarity + graph inference, as in Moreira et al. 2018, Bharati et al. 2021, Zhang et al. 2025) to the 3D Gaussian Splatting domain, where no prior method exists. It departs fundamentally from the trace-based paradigm by reframing provenance as an evidence-based reasoning problem: rather than learning a dissimilarity function, it extracts attribute-wise statistical signatures and hypothesis-driven editing simulations, then delegates directional inference to a structured Chain-of-Thought prompt (building on Wei et al. 2022) fed to an LLM. The dataset construction leverages GaussianEditor (Chen et al. 2024a) for realistic 3DGS edits, and the method is positioned as complementary to existing 3DGS watermarking approaches (CopyRNeRF, GaussianMarker, GS-Hider) that address copyright verification but not evolutionary lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GaussTrace is the first training-free, log-free framework for reconstructing directed provenance graphs of 3DGS assets, filling a gap that watermarking-based protection cannot address (watermarks verify ownership but cannot reveal who modified what and in what order). The explainable, natural-language edge justifications make the output auditable by human experts, which is critical for forensic and legal contexts. Key limitations include the small 44-model benchmark, pairwise scalability constraints for large collections, and dependence on LLM quality and cost. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to heterogeneous real-world editing tools, compressed/redistributed models, and whether the LLM reasoning advantage over rule-based scoring (the RBS gap) persists at larger scale.
