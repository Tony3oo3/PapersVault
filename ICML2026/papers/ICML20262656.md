## Problem

Existing text-to-CAD methods treat parametric modeling and B-Rep synthesis as separate paradigms, but in real feature-based CAD systems they are inherently intertwined: advanced operations like fillet, chamfer, and shell require explicit selection of B-Rep primitives from the transient geometry. This fragmentation prevents AI-driven CAD generation from producing realistic industrial models with refinement features, as purely parametric methods lack access to geometric primitives and purely B-Rep methods lose parametric editability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language description (either an abstract summary or a detailed geometric description with dimensions) as input and produces an executable CadQuery program as output. Unusually, the generated program embeds natural language queries (e.g., 'All circular holes on the surface') in place of direct primitive references; these queries are resolved at runtime by BRepGround against the evolving B-Rep state, so the program is only fully executable when coupled with the grounding module and a CAD kernel (OpenCASCADE).

## Method

FutureCAD couples two components: (1) an LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) that generates CadQuery programs with embedded textual queries for primitive selection, trained via supervised fine-tuning followed by reinforcement learning using Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) with a Chamfer Distance-based geometric reward; and (2) BRepGround, a transformer-based grounding module that resolves those queries to specific B-Rep primitives. BRepGround encodes text with BERT and B-Rep geometry with UV-Net (2D UV-grids for faces, 1D UV-grids for edges), propagates features over the face-adjacency graph via a GNN, aligns modalities through adaptive layers, and fuses them via iterative self- and cross-attention before a per-primitive binary classification head. The key design insight is that the LLM specifies target primitives semantically in natural language rather than by index or coordinate, delegating the geometric perception task to BRepGround, which operates on the transient B-Rep state at each feature execution step.

## Results

On the FutureCAD Advanced subset (models with fillet, chamfer, shell), FutureCAD achieves the lowest median Chamfer Distance (31.12×10⁻³) and an invalidity ratio of just 1.01%, compared to 50.16 CD and 46.60% IR for the strongest baseline (CAD-LLaMA). On the Standard subset, median CD drops to 14.68×10⁻³ with 0.91% IR, and on the out-of-distribution Fusion360 set, performance is preserved (17.01 median CD, 1.00% IR). For the BRepGround sub-task, the method achieves 63.07% mAP and 50.23% F1, outperforming the best baseline (Late Fusion) by 4.89 and 6.91 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FutureCAD dominates all baselines on geometric fidelity (Chamfer Distance) and validity (Invalidity Ratio) across every evaluation subset. On the Advanced subset, FutureCAD reduces median CD from 50.16 (CAD-LLaMA†) to 31.12 and IR from 46.60% to 1.01%—a 45-point drop in invalidity. On the Standard subset, median CD improves from 37.47 (CAD-LLaMA†) to 14.68, and IR drops from 5.30% to 0.91%. On the out-of-distribution Fusion360 set, FutureCAD achieves 17.01 median CD versus 39.27 for CAD-LLaMA† and 1.00% IR versus 8.13%. CADFusion† and CAD-LLaMA† achieve competitive G-F1 scores (91.91 and 91.38 on Standard) but suffer substantially higher CD and IR, indicating they predict correct command types but fail at accurate parameterization. The earlier non-LLM baselines (CAD-Translator, Text2CAD, Text-to-CadQuery) are evaluated only on the Standard subset and show markedly worse CD (77–110 median) and higher IR (1.9–8.4%), confirming the advantage of LLM-driven program generation with B-Rep grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing separation of parametric CAD modeling and B-Rep synthesis by arguing that modern feature-based CAD systems inherently couple the two: advanced operations (fillet, chamfer, shell) require explicit selection of B-Rep primitives from the transient geometry state. It builds on LLM-based CAD code generation (CAD-LLaMA, CADFusion, Text2CAD) for the program-generation side and on UV-Net (Jayaraman et al., 2021) plus GNN-based graph encoding for the B-Rep representation side, but introduces a new bridging mechanism—text-based B-Rep primitive grounding via a dedicated transformer (BRepGround)—that prior methods either ignore or handle with heuristic nearest-neighbor retrieval. The RL stage leverages GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025) rather than token-level GRPO, aligning with recent sequence-level policy optimization trends.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 140k real-world CAD dataset with advanced features and open-source CadQuery representations addresses a critical data gap that has limited progress on realistic industrial CAD generation; prior datasets (DeepCAD, Fusion360) lack fillet/chamfer/shell operations entirely. The framework's practical integration with standard CadQuery workflows makes it directly deployable, and the two-stage SFT+RL training recipe provides a reusable recipe for improving program validity. Open questions include whether the grounding task scales to even more complex assemblies, whether the class imbalance in primitive selection (refinement primitives are rare) can be further mitigated, and whether the spatial-reasoning failures observed in LLMs will persist as models improve.
