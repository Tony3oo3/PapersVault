## Problem

Constrained MAP inference over continuous variables becomes computationally intractable when both the algebraic constraints are non-convex (expressed as SMT(LRA) formulas with Boolean structure) and the density is non-log-concave, yet these are precisely the conditions that arise in safety-critical applications like trajectory prediction and fair tabular imputation. Existing exact solvers (CAD, Lasserre hierarchy) scale poorly, while constraint-agnostic or soft-constrained optimizers are unreliable and often return infeasible solutions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a (possibly unnormalized) probability density p over continuous variables—ranging from piecewise polynomials to stochastic VAE decoder outputs—and a quantifier-free SMT(LRA) formula Δ built from linear (in)equalities connected by Boolean connectives, and returns the MAP assignment x* maximizing p(x) subject to x ⊨ Δ along with the maximum density value. In conditional settings (e.g., p(y|x)), the convex-polytope partitioning of Δ depends only on the constraints and is computed once, then reused across all datapoints x, enabling parallelization.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary solvers. MPMAP is an exact message-passing algorithm for tree-structured problems: it identifies Tractable MAP Conditions (TMC) distinct from WMI tractability, showing that piecewise polynomials factoring into products of univariate polynomials (Ω^PP) and piecewise exponentiated polynomials (Ω^PEP) support tractable symbolic maximization over linear bounds, and decomposes the global argmax into local 'max-out' operations propagated along the tree. PAMAP is a general approximate scheme that partitions the non-convex feasible region into convex polytopes via AllSMT enumeration, optimizes p over each polytope using a pluggable convex optimizer (SHGO or Lasserre/SoS), and prunes polytopes whose upper bound on p cannot exceed the current best solution. The modularity of PAMAP decouples constraint enumeration from optimization, enabling parallelization and support for arbitrary density classes including stochastic objectives.

## Results

On the House-Sales VAE data-imputation task, PAMAP (with 10 particles) achieves a relative error RE(x*) of 0.22, compared to 33.12 for ADAM (10 particles), 25.85 for ADAM (100 particles), 12.22 for DL2 (10 particles), and 16.78 for DL2 (100 particles). On the Stanford Drone Dataset trajectory-prediction task, PAMAP(SHGO) achieves the best runtime–solution-quality trade-off, while CDCL-OCAC solved zero instances and OptiMathSAT returned only poor-quality solutions or errors within the 600 s budget. On synthetic tree-shaped MAP problems (STAR, SNOW, PATH), MPMAP outperforms both PAMAP and PCADAM across all diameters up to dimension 12, including PATH graphs where MPMAP's worst-case complexity scales exponentially in diameter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAMAP decisively outperforms both constraint-agnostic and soft-constrained baselines on the VAE imputation task: it achieves RE(x*) = 0.22 versus 33.12 for ADAM (10 particles) and 12.22 for DL2 (10 particles), a >150× improvement over ADAM and >55× over DL2, even when those methods use 10× more particles. On the SDD trajectory-prediction benchmark, PAMAP(SHGO) dominates in both runtime and relative optimality gap; ADAM and DL2 are unreliable at producing feasible solutions, while the exact OMT solvers fail entirely—CDCL-OCAC solved no instances and OptiMathSAT returned only poor-quality solutions or errors within the 600 s timeout. On the synthetic scalability experiments, MPMAP (exact message passing) consistently outperforms the approximate PAMAP(SHGO) and PCADAM across all three tree topologies, with the gap widening as dimension and diameter increase, confirming that the exact solver's structural advantage is not offset by its higher per-step cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the weighted model integration (WMI) message-passing framework of Zeng et al. (2020a,b) and the SMT-based convex polytope enumeration techniques from WMI-PA (Morettin et al., 2017; Spallitta et al., 2024), but departs from both in a key way: it identifies that the tractable function class for MAP inference is incomparable to that for integration, requiring new tractability conditions (TMC) and a distinct max-out operation. On the optimization side, PAMAP is positioned against OMT solvers (OptiMathSAT, CDCL-OCAC) and soft-constrained methods (DL2), but decouples convex polytope enumeration from per-polytope optimization in a modular way that OMT solvers do not, enabling the use of arbitrary convex-polytope optimizers including stochastic ones. The Lasserre/SoS hierarchy is leveraged as one possible convex-polytope optimizer but is not the core contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of MAP inference under non-convex SMT(LRA) constraints with non-log-concave densities, providing both a new tractable fragment (tree-structured problems with piecewise separable polynomials) and a practical modular solver (PAMAP) that outperforms existing OMT solvers on real-world benchmarks. The tree-structure requirement for MPMAP is a significant limitation, and PAMAP's worst-case exponential polytope enumeration remains a scalability concern for high-dimensional problems. Open questions include extending tractability beyond trees, integrating these solvers into end-to-end training of generative models that satisfy constraints by design, and understanding how pruning strategies scale with constraint complexity.
