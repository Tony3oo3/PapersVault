## Problem

Long-horizon time series forecasting has been dominated by architectures that match conditional means without explicitly modeling the local geometric structure of the forecast map, and standard benchmarks (quasi-periodic, artifact-laden) fail to expose model brittleness under chaotic dynamics and nonstationary shocks. The paper argues that robust long-horizon prediction requires learning local Jacobians with explicit spectral structure, and that the channel-independence vs. channel-dependence paradox reveals deeper issues with how benchmark provenance is conflated with structural validity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed-length context window (336 steps) as a time-delay embedding and produces a forecast horizon (up to 720 steps) by applying a patchwise affine SPD transport to fresh Gaussian noise drawn at inference time. The 336-step output is decomposed into 14 patches of 24 dimensions, each receiving its own SPD map (nonnegative eigenvalues, shift vector, Householder rotation) conditioned on a shared encoded context, reducing a monolithic 336-dimensional Jacobian to fourteen parallel 24-dimensional transports. The spectral parameters double as interpretable diagnostics: eigenvalues measure directional stretching, the trace captures total variance, and the log-determinant reflects volume change of the local prediction ellipsoid.

## Method

Fern (Forecasting with Ellipsoidal RepresentationNs) invokes Brenier's theorem to parameterize the forecast Jacobian as a symmetric positive semi-definite (SPD) factorization, treating forecasting as optimal transport from a fixed Gaussian source N(0,I) to a context-dependent Gaussian ellipsoid. A bidirectional affine coupling encoder (inspired by ANF) compresses the context window into a latent representation, from which an SPD head predicts nonnegative eigenvalues, a translation vector, and an orthogonal factor expressed as a product of R Householder reflections, replacing the O(n³) cost of post-hoc eigendecomposition with O(Rn) Householder-based factors. Training uses standard point losses (Huber, MSE, MAE), under which the learned covariance acts as an internal spectral belief state shaped by point-error minimization rather than explicit distributional supervision. The construction is OT-exact by design: every emitted (μ_θ, Σ_θ) pair defines the exact W2-optimal transport from the Gaussian source, and the spectral output provides interpretable diagnostics as a byproduct of point-loss training. A Triton kernel exploits the involutory property of Householder reflections to recompute intermediate states during backpropagation, reducing activation memory to O(p) per patch regardless of reflection count.

## Results

On the 21-system synthetic stress test (336-in/336-out, 2 seeds), Fern achieves MSE 0.019 on Rössler-Base versus 5.42 (DLinear) and 11.94 (Koopa) — a 285× and 628× gap — widening to 790× and 696× under parameter shift. On Lorenz-63, Fern records MSE 21.66 (base) and 25.21 (param shift) while DLinear and Koopa reach 76.55/70.69 and 95.50/103 respectively. On stochastic systems (OU, GARCH, Double-well) Fern is competitive but not uniformly best, with DLinear or Koopa occasionally ranking first. On real-world ETTm1 and ETTh1, Fern (MSE 8.97 and 10.97) is within ~10% of the best baseline, confirming no regression on standard benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fern dominates all six baselines on chaotic systems: on Rössler it achieves 285× lower MSE than DLinear (0.019 vs 5.42) and 628× lower than Koopa (0.019 vs 11.94), with the gap widening to 790× and 696× under parameter drift. Against TimeMixer and PatchTST, Fern's advantage on Rössler is ~54× and ~129× respectively, and on Lorenz-63 it halves TimeMixer's error (21.66 vs 43.21). ModernTCN is the closest competitor on Chua's circuit (0.051 vs 0.056 base), but Fern pulls ahead under regime switch (0.178 vs 0.099 — actually ModernTCN wins here, 0.099 vs 0.178). PFNN (an invariant-measure learner) diverges catastrophically on chaotic settings (MSE 198–219 on Lorenz) and on ETTm1 (43.69), while Fern remains stable. On stochastic systems (OU, GARCH, Double-well) and ETT benchmarks, Fern is competitive but not uniformly best: DLinear edges out Fern on ETTh1 (10.39 vs 10.97) and ties on OU-Base, while TimeMixer leads on ETTm1 (9.12 vs 8.97 — actually Fern leads). The paper's central claim is that standard quasi-periodic benchmarks mask the brittleness exposed by controlled chaotic and nonstationary stress tests, where Fern's SPD transport parameterization provides a structural advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

Fern builds on Brenier's theorem (1991) to recast long-horizon time series forecasting as conditional optimal transport from a fixed Gaussian source to data-dependent ellipsoids, parameterizing the transport map's Jacobian directly as an SPD factorization (eigenvalues + Householder reflections + translation) rather than learning a dense Jacobian and post-hoc eigendecomposing it. The bidirectional encoder draws on augmented normalizing flows (Huang et al., 2020) for invertible affine coupling, while the evaluation protocol introduces Sliced Wasserstein Distance and Effective Prediction Time as complements to MSE. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant Transformer-based LTSF lineage (PatchTST, TimeMixer, iTransformer) and from Koopman-operator approaches (Koopa, PFNN) by framing forecasting as local, instance-wise manifold transport rather than global operator learning, and grounds the channel-independence preference in Takens' embedding theorem and the Mori-Zwanzig formalism rather than treating it as an empirical curiosity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most broadly useful contribution is its methodological critique: it documents concrete data artifacts in ETT (sentinel values, clustered zeros), shows that naive early-stopping on nonstationary data can invert model rankings, and argues that quasi-periodic real-world benchmarks systematically hide brittleness that controlled chaotic/nonstationary stress tests expose immediately. The spectral diagnostics (eigenvalue spikes correlating with high-velocity regions in Lorenz-63, overconfidence signatures before error spikes) offer an interpretable failure-mode signal that falls out of point-loss training without explicit probabilistic supervision. Key open questions include whether the Gaussian output restriction limits expressiveness, whether the SPD parameterization scales to very high-dimensional multivariate settings, and whether the synthetic stress-test protocol can be adopted as a standard complement to real-world benchmarks. The paper leaves full probabilistic evaluation (NLL, CRPS) to future work, and the single-author scope limits the breadth of ablations and architectural comparisons.
