## Problem

Cooperative MARL systems assume agents reliably execute their learned policies, but real-world deployments face actuator failures, sensor corruption, and controller faults that break coordination and cause severe performance degradation. Existing robustness approaches either apply always-on adversarial training (inducing excessive conservatism) or target single-agent perturbations rather than explicit agent-level malfunctions in cooperative teams. This paper addresses the gap of training MARL policies that are robust to selective, state-dependent agent failures without sacrificing nominal performance.

## Input / Output

Each of the N cooperative agents receives a local observation from a partially observable Dec-MDP and outputs an action from its discrete or continuous action space; the Switcher additionally observes joint agent behaviour and outputs a discrete decision from {0}∪{1,…,N} (no fault or fault agent i), while the Adversary outputs a replacement action for the faulted agent. The system produces robust cooperative policies that maintain team performance under both random and worst-case malfunction regimes, with the Switcher's activation pattern encoding which states are coordination-critical. A switching cost c or a fixed malfunction budget n calibrates the fault-performance trade-off during training.

## Method

MARTA augments any standard MARL algorithm (QMIX, VDN, MADDPG) with a Switcher-Adversary pair trained jointly via a shared replay buffer, forming an (N+2)-player non-zero-sum Markov game. The Switcher, implemented as a lightweight soft actor-critic with action space of size N+1, learns a state-dependent policy that activates malfunctions only where they most degrade expected team return, with each activation incurring a cost c (or consuming from a fixed budget n in the MARTA-B variant). The Adversary then overrides the selected agent's action with either a uniformly random or a worst-case (softmax of negative Q) policy, while the remaining N−1 agents continue executing their intended policies. The key theoretical contribution is proving that the resulting switching-augmented Bellman operator is a γ-contraction, establishing existence and uniqueness of the minimax value, convergence of Q-learning to a Markov perfect equilibrium, and extension to linear function approximation. At execution time, only the N cooperative agents act; the Switcher and Adversary are discarded, so MARTA introduces zero additional inference cost.

## Results

MARTA achieves consistent final-return gains across all four benchmark environments when attached to standard MARL backbones. The largest improvements appear in SMAC-3m (+114.9% with QMIX, +116.7% with VDN) and LBF (+44.6% with QMIX), while more modest but still significant gains are observed in Traffic Junction (+11.1% QMIX, +37.8% VDN), MPE SimpleTag (+21.4% QMIX, +21.2% VDN), and SMAC-8m (+9.3% QMIX, +9.8% VDN). MARTA also significantly reduces episode-level collision rates in Traffic Junction and outperforms the adversarial baseline EIR under dynamic (train-test mismatched) fault distributions across all environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARTA is evaluated as a plug-and-play layer on top of QMIX, VDN, and MADDPG, and compared against two dedicated robust MARL methods: M3DDPG (minimax DDPG) and EIR (Byzantine-robust Bayesian-game MARL). Against its own base algorithms, MARTA-QMIX outperforms QMIX in every environment (gains from +9.3% in SMAC-8m to +114.9% in SMAC-3m), and MARTA-VDN outperforms VDN in every environment (gains from +3.2% in LBF to +116.7% in SMAC-3m). In MPE SimpleTag, MARTA-MADDPG consistently outperforms both plain MADDPG and M3DDPG under the same malfunction process, showing that the Switcher-Adversary mechanism adds robustness even on top of an existing adversarial training method. Against EIR, MARTA performs comparably under aligned (Case 1) fixed-agent faults but consistently outperforms EIR under dynamic (Case 2) faults where the malfunctioning agent is resampled at each timestep, indicating stronger generalisation to train-test distribution shifts. The computational overhead is modest: 1.05–1.15× training time and 1.27–1.61× GPU memory relative to the base learner, with zero additional execution-time cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARTA extends the line of fault-tolerant RL (Mguni 2019; Fan et al. 2021) from single-agent to cooperative multi-agent settings, and departs from always-on adversarial training (Pinto et al. 2017; Li et al. 2019 M3DDPG) by introducing a Switcher that selectively induces malfunctions only at coordination-critical states. The budget-constrained variant (MARTA-B) directly builds on the state-augmentation technique from SAUTE RL (Sootla et al., 2022) and the costly-action framework of Mguni et al. (2023). Conceptually, the paper positions itself against shielding/backup-policy methods (Zhang et al. 2019; Elsayed-Aly et al. 2021) that add runtime safety layers, and against Byzantine-robust MARL (Li et al. 2024) that models strategic adversaries, by instead targeting non-strategic actuator-level malfunctions with a switching-augmented Markov game formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means MARTA can be attached to any value-based or actor-critic MARL algorithm without architectural changes, making it immediately deployable for fault-tolerant multi-agent systems in robotics and distributed control. The theoretical guarantees (contraction of the switching-augmented Bellman operator, convergence to a unique minimax value) are limited to tabular and linear function approximation, leaving open whether they extend to the deep network parameterisations used in practice. A key open question is how the Switcher's selectivity scales as agent count grows and whether the budget/cost calibration generalises across domains without manual tuning. The paper also leaves open the interaction between MARTA and other robustness mechanisms (shielding, constrained optimisation) that could be composed for stronger guarantees.
