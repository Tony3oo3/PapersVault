## Problem

Likelihood-based first-order algorithms for multi-task learning with shared linear representations have long lacked both computational and statistical guarantees: existing methods either achieve sub-optimal estimation error O~(dk²/NT) (a factor of k above the information-theoretic lower bound) or require expensive per-iteration operations like SVD/QR decomposition. The paper asks whether a pure first-order likelihood-based method can simultaneously attain O~(1) iteration complexity and the near-optimal O~(dk/NT) estimation error for the non-convex matrix factorization structure inherent to shared representation learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes T tasks, each providing N i.i.d. samples (x_t^(i), y_t^(i)) with x_t^(i) ∈ R^d and scalar response y_t^(i), under the structural assumption that the true parameter v_t* = B* w_t* with a shared d×k matrix B* and task-specific k-vectors w_t* (where k < min{d,T} and d > T). The covariate matrices must satisfy a restricted isometry property (RIP) with constant δ. The output is the estimated shared representation B̂ ∈ R^{d×k} and task-specific weight matrix Ŵ ∈ R^{k×T}, from which per-task predictors v̂_t = B̂ ŵ_t are formed; the learned B̂ can also be frozen and reused for a (T+1)-th target task via a short SGD fine-tuning phase.

## Method

The proposed Two-Phase Gradient Descent (TPGD) algorithm splits training into a warm-start phase and a refined optimization phase. Phase I runs unregularized gradient descent on the negative log-likelihood for O~(1) iterations from random small-scale initialization, establishing that the iterates (B, W^⊤) converge to a constant-radius neighborhood of the true factorization (F, G) — thereby overcoming the non-convexity barrier and learning the correct feature subspaces. Phase II augments the loss with a balancing regularization (1/8)‖B^⊤B − WW^⊤‖_F², whose gradient correction terms mitigate the mutual coupling between B and W; convergence in this phase is proven via the Regularity Condition framework (Tu et al., 2016; Candes et al., 2015), yielding linear decay of the distance error. The key technical insight is that the MTL noise structure (per-task random covariates rather than random measurement matrices as in matrix sensing) allows a constant step size and relaxed RIP requirement (δ ≲ k^{-1/2} vs. δ ≲ k^{-1}), enabling O~(1) total iterations and the near-optimal O~(dk/NT) estimation error that matches the known lower bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

TPGD achieves an estimation error of Õ(dk/(NT)) for the average task parameter recovery, matching the information-theoretic lower bound Ω(dk/(NT)) up to logarithmic factors. The algorithm requires only Õ(1) iterations with no additional per-iteration cost (no SVD or QR decomposition), making it the first likelihood-based first-order method to attain statistical near-optimality for linear MTL. In the transfer learning setting, the excess risk on the target task is bounded by Õ(dk²/(NT) + k/M), matching the best known rate for likelihood-based TL methods under weaker moment assumptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPGD improves over all existing likelihood-based methods (AltMinGD, ERM, ARMUL, pERM) by a factor of k in estimation error, going from Õ(dk²/(NT)) to Õ(dk/(NT)), while also providing a guaranteed Õ(1) iteration convergence that the ERM, ARMUL, and pERM baselines lack. Compared to the moment-based MoM of Niu et al. (2024) and the spectral method SE of Tian et al. (2025), which also achieve Õ(dk/(NT)), TPGD matches their statistical rate but eliminates the per-iteration SVD cost, requiring only standard gradient steps. Against the earlier MoM of Tripuraneni et al. (2021) and AltMinGD of Thekumparampil et al. (2021), TPGD improves the rate by a factor of k while also removing the SVD/QR overhead. Numerically (Figures 1–2), TPGD converges fastest in both iteration count and sample size among all likelihood-based baselines tested (GD + Loss 1, GD + Loss 2, NSGD), confirming the theoretical advantage in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multi-task learning theory and non-convex optimization. Statistically, it builds on the line from Baxter (2000) through Tripuraneni et al. (2021) and Du et al. (2021) that established sample complexity bounds for shared linear representations. Algorithmically, it departs from prior likelihood-based methods (AltMinGD, ERM, ARMUL) that either required suboptimal O~(dk²/NT) rates or lacked convergence guarantees, and from moment/spectral methods (Niu et al., 2024; Tian et al., 2025) that achieved optimal rates but required per-iteration SVD. The key technical lineage is from matrix sensing: the two-phase structure and regularity-condition analysis follow Tu et al. (2016) and Xiong et al. (2023), but the paper adapts these tools to the fundamentally different noise structure of MTL (random covariates per task vs. random measurement matrices), achieving O~(1) iterations where matrix sensing first-order methods require O~(poly(d)).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a concrete open problem: whether likelihood-based first-order methods can achieve statistical optimality for linear MTL without expensive per-iteration decompositions. The O~(1) iteration complexity with no SVD/QR makes the algorithm directly deployable in practice, unlike the spectral and moment-based alternatives. Limitations include the restriction to linear representations, the high-dimensional regime d > T, and the RIP assumption on covariates; the heterogeneous-noise extension in the appendix (curriculum learning) is a promising but preliminary step. Open questions include whether the two-phase correction strategy generalizes to nonlinear (e.g., neural network) representations, and whether the O~(1) iteration bound can be tightened or the RIP condition further relaxed.
