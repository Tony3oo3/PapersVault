## Problem

GFlowNets exhibit high-variance credit assignment, unstable training, and poor generalization in stochastic environments where actions have uncertain outcomes. Existing stochastic GFlowNet methods (EFlowNet, Stochastic-GFN) correctly marginalize over environmental stochasticity but lack explicit mechanisms for variance reduction and smoothness control on the learned policy, leaving them vulnerable to overfitting on noisy reward signals. This paper addresses this gap by recasting the entire GFlowNet training objective in the frequency domain to enforce provable smoothness and stability.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a directed acyclic graph MDP where the agent starts from an initial state s_0 and generates a trajectory of length T by sampling actions from a learned forward policy P_F(a|s) while the environment applies stochastic transitions P(s'|s,a). The flow at each state is treated as a 1D discrete-time signal f_τ[t] = F(s_t, t; θ) along the trajectory, enabling spectral decomposition via the DFT. The output is a diverse set of terminal states x ∈ X sampled proportionally to the target reward R(x), with the policy implicitly constrained to a smooth RKHS subspace defined by the RFF kernel, and the regularization strength λ adapted online via a bi-level meta-learning loop.

## Method

ST-GFN introduces a Unified Spectral Loss with two components: a spectral consistency term that enforces forward-backward policy alignment in the frequency domain (approximated via Random Fourier Features as squared Euclidean distance between policy mean embeddings, i.e., an empirical MMD), and a spectral regularization term that penalizes the RKHS norm of the spectral value surrogate, provably bounding the Lipschitz constant of the flow function and acting as a principled low-pass filter. An autocorrelated intrinsic reward derived from the Wiener-Khinchin theorem provides dense per-step exploration signals by detecting periodic structure in the reward history, transforming sparse exploration into O(1) peak detection in frequency space. The regularization strength λ is made adaptive via a bi-level meta-learning scheme that aligns the empirical RKHS norm with a target smoothness level, allowing the model to dynamically balance filtering and expressiveness. The paper proves that minimizing this loss is equivalent to finding the fixed point of a regularized Bellman optimality operator, grounding the framework in robust control theory and providing formal stability guarantees under environmental noise.

## Results

ST-GFN achieves the best performance across all four environments and all metrics. On BitSequence (90% action failure), it reaches 0.86±0.02 top-100 reward vs. 0.52±0.06 for the best baseline (Stochastic-GFN). On HyperGrid, it discovers 58.2±2.1 modes (91% coverage) versus 38.4±5.2 for the best exploration baseline (TB+Novelty). On SingleCell (2.1M combinations), it achieves 0.78±0.03 target correlation with 0.15±0.02 L1 error while requiring only 5,850 steps and 404s total wall-clock time—substantially fewer samples than all baselines (7,680–10,483 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-GFN outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across all four environments with p < 0.001 (Bonferroni-corrected). The strongest stochastic GFlowNet baselines—Stochastic-GFN (Pan et al., 2023) and EFlowNet (Jiralerspong et al., 2024)—achieve meaningful variance reduction over vanilla TB but remain well below ST-GFN (e.g., 0.52 vs. 0.86 top-100 on BitSequence; 22.8 vs. 58.2 modes on HyperGrid), confirming that expectation-based flow alone is insufficient without explicit smoothness constraints. Among exploration-enhanced methods, TB+Novelty (Pathak et al., 2017) is the closest competitor on HyperGrid (38.4 modes) but still trails ST-GFN by ~50%, and TB+ControlVariates (Tucker et al., 2018) leads on TicTacToe win rate (24%) yet falls far short of ST-GFN's 45%. Standard GFlowNet variants (TB, FM, SubTB, DB) show the largest gaps, with TB achieving only 14.3 modes on HyperGrid and 0.18 top-100 on BitSequence. ST-GFN also achieves superior sample efficiency, converging in 5,850 steps versus 7,680–10,483 for baselines, while maintaining lower total wall-clock time (404s vs. 468–562s) despite moderate per-iteration RFF overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet lineage (Bengio et al., 2021, 2026) and its stochastic generalizations (Pan et al., 2023; Jiralerspong et al., 2024) by recasting the entire training objective in the frequency domain. It builds directly on EFlowNets' principle of marginalizing over environmental stochasticity but departs by introducing a unified spectral loss that combines an RFF-approximated MMD consistency term with an RKHS-norm regularization term, drawing on Random Fourier Features (Rahimi & Recht, 2007) for scalability. The paper also elevates the known GFlowNet–entropy-regularized RL analogy (Haarnoja et al., 2017) to a formal equivalence with regularized Bellman optimality, and repurposes the Wiener-Khinchin theorem to derive an autocorrelated intrinsic reward for structured exploration in sparse, periodic landscapes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal equivalence to regularized value iteration provides a principled theoretical grounding for why spectral regularization stabilizes training under stochasticity, connecting GFlowNet training to robust control theory in a way that was previously only heuristic. A key open question is whether the frequency-domain framing offers genuine advantages over simpler smoothness regularizers (e.g., gradient L2 penalties or spectral normalization), since the paper itself acknowledges that on non-periodic benchmarks the method functions as a 'general-purpose variance reduction tool rather than a narrow frequency-matching heuristic.' Scalability to very high-dimensional continuous action spaces and the optimal choice of RFF dimension relative to problem structure remain practical challenges the paper identifies but does not fully resolve.
