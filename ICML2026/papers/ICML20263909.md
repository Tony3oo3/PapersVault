## Problem

Simulation-based inference (SBI) methods trained on simulators produce biased or overconfident posteriors when the simulator, prior, or noise model deviates from the true data-generating process. Existing correction approaches either require knowing the specific form of misspecification, assume a conditional independence between real observations and parameters given simulated data, or demand access to the full test batch at inference time. This paper addresses the gap of correcting SBI posteriors using only a small set of calibration pairs, without any assumptions on the misspecification type or form.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a frozen NPE posterior trained on abundant simulated data, a small calibration dataset of (θ, y) pairs (as few as 10) from high-fidelity simulations or controlled experiments, and access to the simulator S for generating surrogate observations. At test time, given a single real observation y, the method produces samples from the corrected posterior p_Θ|Y(θ|y) via two sequential ODE integrations (first in observation space, then in parameter space). The method operates as a lightweight post-processing step on any existing SBI model and supports diverse observation modalities including scalar parameters, time-series (pendulum, wind tunnel), and RGB images (light tunnel).

## Method

FMCPE learns two coupled flow-matching vector fields jointly: T_X transports from a Gaussian N(y, σ²I) to the simulator's data distribution q_X|Y(x|y) (using simulator-generated targets), and T_Θ transports from an informed source π_Θ|Y to the true posterior p_Θ|Y (using calibration pairs as targets). The key design choice is that the source for T_Θ is not a simple Gaussian but a data-driven distribution constructed by first mapping y through T_X to obtain a surrogate x̃, then sampling θ₀ from the frozen NPE posterior conditioned on x̃. Both flows are trained simultaneously with a combined conditional flow-matching loss, so the source distribution for T_Θ evolves as T_X improves, forcing T_Θ to be robust to imperfect source samples. This joint formulation avoids the conditional independence assumption y ⊥ θ | x that limits prior methods like RoPE, and does not require knowing which model component (simulator, prior, noise) is misspecified.

## Results

FMCPE consistently outperforms all baselines (NPE, MFNPE, RoPE) across four tasks (Gaussian, Pendulum, Wind Tunnel, Light Tunnel) on Wasserstein distance (W2), joint C2ST, MMD, and MSE metrics, with the gap most pronounced at small calibration sizes (N_cal = 10–50). On the Pendulum and Light Tunnel tasks, NPE fails to capture parameter–observation dependencies even at N_cal = 1000, while FMCPE achieves the lowest jC2ST and W2 with lower variance across calibration-set draws. On real-world tasks (Wind Tunnel, Light Tunnel), FMCPE yields substantially better W2 at small N_cal and remains competitive or superior at N_cal = 1000. Training takes 5–6 minutes per task, and inference is faster than RoPE for all tasks and calibration sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NPE (trained on calibration data only, following Wehenkel et al. 2025), FMCPE wins on every metric and task; NPE produces multimodal posteriors even when the true posterior is unimodal (Gaussian task) and fails to capture intricate parameter dependencies on Pendulum and Light Tunnel even at N_cal = 1000. Against MFNPE (Krouglova et al., ICLR 2026), FMCPE achieves lower jC2ST and W2 with reduced variance across calibration-set draws; MFNPE's neural encoder h_ω(x) is evaluated on real observations y whose distribution differs from simulated x, leading to overly sharp but biased posteriors, especially at N_cal = 10–50. Against RoPE (Wehenkel et al., ICML 2025), FMCPE wins on W2 and jC2ST across all tasks; RoPE additionally requires access to the full test batch at inference time (preventing online use) and is substantially slower at inference (e.g., ~100–450 s vs. ~0.4–11 s for FMCPE on 5000 test points). SBC diagnostics confirm FMCPE is better calibrated as N_cal grows, outperforming NPE and MFNPE, while RoPE's apparent calibration advantage at low N_cal is attributed to OT regularization biasing its posterior toward the prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the flow matching framework of Lipman et al. (2023) and its application to SBI (Wildberger et al., 2023), repurposing it as a corrective post-processing step rather than a primary inference engine. It departs from RoPE (Wehenkel et al., 2025), which relies on a conditional independence assumption and requires the full test batch at inference time, and from MFNPE (Krouglova et al., 2026), which fine-tunes a low-fidelity NPE encoder on high-fidelity data. The key conceptual departure is the use of two jointly-trained flow maps—one in observation space (T_X) to construct a simulation-informed source distribution, and one in parameter space (T_Θ) to transport that source toward the true posterior—without requiring knowledge of the misspecification form or any conditional independence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FMCPE provides a practical, model-agnostic post-processing step that can be applied to any existing SBI posterior estimator, making it broadly applicable to the growing ecosystem of simulation-based inference tools in physics, biology, and engineering. The method's ability to correct posteriors with as few as 10 calibration samples is practically significant for domains where high-fidelity data are prohibitively expensive. Open questions include deriving theoretical bounds on correction quality, understanding how the method scales to very high-dimensional parameter spaces, and determining principled guidelines for choosing calibration set size relative to task complexity and misspecification severity.
