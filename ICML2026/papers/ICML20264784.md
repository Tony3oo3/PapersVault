## Problem

Existing VLM RLFT methods directly port LLM-oriented training paradigms, relying solely on token-level verifiable rewards (e.g., answer correctness) while ignoring the intrinsic text-vision alignment that is fundamental to multimodal reasoning. This leads to two critical inefficiencies: low sample efficiency, where 30–99% of training samples provide zero gradient signal under GRPO, and sparse reward signals that offer no credit assignment to intermediate chain-of-thought reasoning. The paper asks whether the model's own text-vision alignment patterns can be extracted as lightweight training signals to address both challenges simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image paired with a text question, and the model generates a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer. Unusually, the framework also extracts per-token cosine-similarity vectors between text hidden states and image hidden states (the 'views') during both pre-filling and decoding, which are then aggregated into three alignment representations—planned, reasoned, and guided views—used as training signals. The guided view is computed by a single forward pass with the ground-truth CoT, making the entire alignment computation require only one additional inference per sample.

## Method

FOCUS-RL defines three view patterns via cosine similarity between text and image token hidden states: the planned view (attention of the last question token over image tokens during pre-filling), the reasoned view (average attention over image tokens during CoT generation), and the guided view (average attention over image tokens when the model processes the reference CoT). From these, two metrics are derived: Predictive View Accuracy (PVA = cos(planned, guided)) estimates sample difficulty and drives an autonomous curriculum that sorts data by PVA and samples via a Gaussian distribution whose mean shifts based on running accuracy; and Reasoning View Accuracy (RVA = cos(reasoned, guided)) provides a dense, continuous reward for CoT quality that replaces rigid token-level step matching with flexible view-level alignment. During policy updates, group-normalized advantages from both answer correctness and RVA are summed to form a denser combined reward, enabling faster and more stable GRPO training without any step-level annotation or external process reward model.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, FOCUS-RL (Data+Policy) achieves an average accuracy of 45.3% across six multimodal reasoning benchmarks (LogicVista, MathVerse, MathVista, MathVision, WeMath, DynaMath), improving +4.4% over the untrained base model (40.9%) and +2.6% over vanilla GRPO (42.7%). The data-processing module alone (FOCUS-RL Data) already reaches 44.7%, surpassing curriculum-learning baselines AdaRFT (43.1%) and GainRL (43.8%). FOCUS-RL also delivers 2.3×–4× faster convergence, reaching peak GRPO performance within ~60 iterations instead of 200.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOCUS-RL (Data+Policy) outperforms all open-source VLM RLFT baselines on average: it beats MM-Eureka-7B (42.9%) by +2.4%, VLAA-Thinker-7B (41.9%) by +3.4%, and OpenVLThinker-7B (40.9%) by +4.4%. Against the direct RLFT baselines, FOCUS-RL surpasses vanilla GRPO (42.7%) by +2.6%, AdaRFT (43.1%) by +2.2%, and GainRL (43.8%) by +1.5%, with the largest per-benchmark gains on LogicVista (+4.9% over GRPO) and MathVerse (+2.1%). The data-processing module alone already exceeds both curriculum baselines (AdaRFT and GainRL), confirming that PVA-based sorting is more effective than LLM-derived difficulty signals for VLMs. On generalizability (Table 2), FOCUS-RL consistently improves over vanilla GRPO across Qwen2.5-VL-3B (+2.1%), InternVL3-2B (+2.7%), and InternVL3-8B (+1.7%). The method still trails proprietary models (GPT-4o at 54.6%, Gemini 2.0-Flash at 61.4%) by a wide margin, but narrows the gap substantially relative to the base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based RLFT paradigm (from DeepSeekMath and VLM-R1) by introducing VLM-specific training signals derived from text-vision attention alignment, departing from prior work that simply ports LLM-oriented RL techniques to multimodal settings. It adapts autonomous curriculum learning concepts from AdaRFT and GainRL—previously validated only on LLMs—by replacing their difficulty signals with a VLM-native metric (PVA) computed from a single forward pass. The theoretical justification for using cosine similarity between hidden states as a proxy for token influence builds directly on CoreMatching's projection-guided analysis, while the process supervision component (RVA) offers an annotation-free alternative to step-level PRM methods like VisualPRM and PS-GRPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FOCUS-RL provides a practical, plug-and-play acceleration for VLM RLFT that reduces training iterations by 2.5–4× without requiring additional annotation or external reward models, lowering the barrier to efficient multimodal reasoning training. A key open question is whether the view-alignment signals generalize beyond math/reasoning benchmarks to open-ended visual tasks where ground-truth CoT may be unavailable or ambiguous. The reliance on a reference CoT to compute the guided view also limits applicability to settings where such supervision is absent, and the moderate Spearman correlation (0.63) between PVA and correctness suggests the difficulty signal, while useful, is not a perfect proxy.
