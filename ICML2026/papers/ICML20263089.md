## Problem

Mixture-of-Experts policies in deep reinforcement learning suffer from plasticity loss in continual settings, where their ability to learn new skills degrades as training proceeds. While prior work linked this phenomenon to eNTK spectral collapse in dense architectures, its manifestation in MoE policies remained poorly characterized, and no principled regularizer existed to counter it specifically for MoE-based policies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263089/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observation vectors from MDPs (MetaWorld manipulation tasks or HumanoidBench locomotion tasks) and outputs a diagonal Gaussian action distribution via a Top-K MoE actor (E=10 experts, K=2 routed per input). The SPHERE penalty is computed on the routing-weighted concatenation of expert features at the last hidden expert layer, forming a Gram matrix A_last^exp ∈ R^{m×m} where m is the total concatenated expert feature dimension; this Gram is available from forward passes alone without backpropagation.

## Method

SPHERE formalizes plasticity loss as a decline in the spectral-entropy effective rank of the empirical NTK, then derives a tractable surrogate by decomposing the Gauss-Newton matrix into layerwise gate and expert blocks under a block-diagonal approximation. For each expert layer, a Kronecker proxy A_{ℓ-1}^exp ⊗ G_ℓ^exp (feature Gram times gradient Gram) provides a lower bound on the effective rank via its condition number, and a spectral contraction operator on the feature Gram monotonically improves this bound. The practical instantiation applies a Parseval penalty ‖A_last^exp − (Tr(A_last^exp)/m)I‖_F² to the weighted expert feature Gram at the last hidden expert layer, which is provably spectrally contractive (η_max = 1/2) and thus increases r_e(K). This penalty is added to the PPO actor loss with an adaptive coefficient scaled by the ratio of actor to SPHERE gradient norms, requiring only a forward pass to compute and no SVD at runtime.

## Results

On MetaWorld (CW10, 10 tasks), SPHERE improves average final success by 133% over the unregularized Top-K MoE baseline under continual RL (CRL) and narrows the RL-to-CRL performance gap by 52%. On HumanoidBench (5 H1 tasks), SPHERE improves average success by 36% under standard RL and 50% under CRL relative to Top-K MoE. Throughout training, SPHERE maintains a higher spectral-entropy effective rank r_e(K) of the empirical NTK, while all baselines exhibit progressive rank collapse. The ablation on HumanoidBench CRL shows SPHERE achieves 0.54±0.12 average success versus 0.36±0.08 for the unregularized baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPHERE is compared against five baselines on the same Top-K MoE actor (E=10, K=2). Against the architectural baselines (Dense PPO, PPO-10×, Dense-MoE, DS-MoE), SPHERE consistently achieves the highest average success under both RL and CRL on both MetaWorld and HumanoidBench, confirming that the gain comes from the spectral regularizer rather than architecture alone. Among the four mitigation baselines—LayerNorm (Juliani & Ash, 2024), C-CHAIN (Tang et al., 2025), CBP (Dohare et al., 2024), and PW/Parseval (Chung et al., 2024)—SPHERE outperforms all of them on both benchmarks under CRL, as stated in the captions of Figures 4 and 5. The largest absolute gap is on MetaWorld CRL, where SPHERE's 133% improvement over unregularized MoE substantially exceeds the gains from any single mitigation baseline. On HumanoidBench, SPHERE's 50% CRL gain (0.36→0.54) also surpasses the per-expert loss-sum variant (0.40) and the gradient-Gram variant (0.43) from the ablation, indicating that the cross-expert concatenated feature Gram is the critical design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK-based spectral plasticity framework of Lyle et al. (2025) and Tang et al. (2025), which linked plasticity loss to eNTK spectral collapse in dense MLP policies, to the Mixture-of-Experts setting. It departs from prior Parseval-type regularizers (Chung et al., 2024) and spectral normalization approaches by targeting the weighted expert feature Gram rather than individual weight matrices, and derives a tractable proxy through block-diagonal decomposition and a Kronecker-factored approximation (building on Martens & Grosse, 2015). The Tracy-Singh product and Cauchy interlacing arguments in Appendix D provide a principled bridge from the Kronecker proxy to the actual expert-layer Gauss-Newton block, a connection not present in prior MoE or NTK work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoE policies are increasingly deployed in robotics (manipulators, humanoids, quadrupeds) and large-scale online RL, yet their plasticity loss was previously uncharacterized; SPHERE provides both a diagnostic (spectral-entropy effective rank of the eNTK) and a practical fix (a few lines of Parseval penalty on expert features) that yields large gains in continual settings. Key limitations include reliance on the block-diagonal and within-layer independence approximations, evaluation restricted to continuous-control RL on two benchmarks, and no demonstration on large-scale pretrained MoE foundation models. Open questions include whether the spectral contraction guarantee holds without the approximations, how SPHERE interacts with other MoE-specific regularizers (load balancing, expert choice routing), and whether the approach transfers to discrete-action or language-model MoE settings.
