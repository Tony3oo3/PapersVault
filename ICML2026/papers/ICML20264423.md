## Problem

PINNs frequently fail to converge on stiff or high-frequency PDEs because the loss landscape inherits severe ill-conditioning from the underlying differential operator, with the PL-based condition number deteriorating polynomially as the collocation mesh is refined. Although adding pointwise solution data empirically stabilizes training, the operator-level preconditioning mechanism behind this effect had not been rigorously established, and no non-asymptotic end-to-end error bounds existed for PINNs with tanh activation trained by finitely many gradient descent steps.

## Input / Output

The method takes a linear second-order elliptic or parabolic PDE (operator L, source f, boundary data g) on a bounded Lipschitz domain, together with Monte Carlo Feynman-Kac parameters (number of trajectories N_MC, time step Δt, truncation horizon T_max) and a small set of N_FK interior supervision points. It produces a trained depth-3 tanh network u_θ approximating the PDE solution, accompanied by a high-probability L²(Ω) error bound of order N_FK^{-(s-2)/(2d+4(s-2))} plus Monte Carlo bias terms. The FK supervision labels are generated offline by Euler-Maruyama simulation of the associated SDE, making the pipeline fully mesh-free and agnostic to the source of the pointwise data.

## Method

FK-PINNs augment the standard PINN loss (PDE residual + boundary) with a data-fidelity term penalizing discrepancies between the network and Monte Carlo FK estimates at a sparse set of interior points, with all three terms balanced by uncertainty-based adaptive weights (Kendall et al.) optimized jointly with the network. The key theoretical insight is that this data term adds a mass-like positive semidefinite curvature to the empirical Gauss-Newton matrix, replacing the PDE operator A = L*L by A + λI in the operator-theoretic framework, which provably keeps the PL-based condition number uniformly bounded in the number of collocation points N (vs. polynomial N^{β/2} growth for standard PINNs). For the error analysis, the paper establishes new pseudo-dimension bounds for first- and second-order derivatives of tanh networks, then applies an approximation–estimation–optimization decomposition to derive non-asymptotic L²(Ω) bounds after T steps of gradient descent, with the Monte Carlo noise controlled via sub-exponential tail estimates on the Euler-Maruyama FK estimator.

## Results

On the Schrödinger-type equation (the most challenging problem), FK-PINNs reduce the L² absolute error from 0.475±0.148 (standard PINN) to 0.073±0.008, a ~6.5× improvement, while the standard PINN visually fails to recover the oscillatory solution structure. On the Mean Escape Time problem, FK-PINNs reduce L² absolute error from 16.56±0.055 to 1.755±0.093 (~9.4× improvement). Across all four benchmark PDEs (Poisson, Schrödinger, Mean Escape Time, Committor), FK-PINNs achieve lower L² and H¹ errors with substantially smaller variance (e.g., Poisson L² rel. err drops from 0.322±0.149 to 0.118±0.003), confirming the theoretical prediction that FK supervision stabilizes the loss landscape.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard PINN (Raissi et al., 2019) with identical architecture, training parameters, and optimizer schedule (Adam + L-BFGS, 30000+15000 steps). FK-PINNs outperform standard PINNs on all four benchmark problems across all four error metrics (L² and H¹, absolute and relative). The most dramatic gap appears on the high-frequency Schrödinger-type equation, where the standard PINN completely fails to learn the oscillatory solution (L² rel. err ≈ 0.62) while FK-PINNs recover the correct profile (L² rel. err ≈ 0.10). On the Mean Escape Time problem, FK-PINNs reduce L² absolute error by roughly an order of magnitude (16.56 → 1.76). A notable secondary benefit is the drastic reduction in run-to-run variance: standard PINNs show large standard deviations (e.g., 0.616 on Poisson L² abs err), whereas FK-PINNs are highly reproducible (0.011), consistent with the theoretical prediction that FK supervision bounds the PL-based condition number uniformly in the number of collocation points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three threads: (1) the operator-conditioning analyses of De Ryck et al. (2024) and Rathore et al. (2024), which show that PINN training difficulty is governed by the spectrum of L*L and that the condition number deteriorates polynomially with collocation density; (2) the PL* optimization framework of Liu et al. (2022), adapted to finite-width networks; and (3) classical Feynman-Kac Monte Carlo methods for PDEs. Prior work (Li et al., 2023; Heldmann et al., 2023) showed empirically that adding noisy solution data to the PINN loss helps, and optimizer-level preconditioning schemes (Müller & Zeinhofer, 2023; Schwencke & Furtlehner, 2025) address conditioning at the algorithm level. This paper departs by proving that *any* pointwise data-fidelity term acts as an operator-level preconditioner (Theorem 5.4), making the conditioning improvement agnostic to the data source, and by providing the first non-asymptotic L² error bounds for tanh-activated PINNs via an approximation-estimation-optimization decomposition that explicitly tracks Monte Carlo bias and sub-exponential noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The preconditioning theorem is broadly applicable: it shows that even a handful of noisy interior points (from FK Monte Carlo, coarse FEM, or experimental data) can provably bound the loss-landscape condition number uniformly in the collocation mesh size, converting gradient-descent complexity from O(N^{β/2} log(1/ε)) to O(log(1/ε)). The non-asymptotic error bounds for tanh PINNs fill a notable gap, since prior convergence results either assumed piecewise-polynomial activations or were asymptotic. Key limitations include restriction to linear PDEs admitting FK representations, the statistical cost in the sparse-FK regime (error saturates at the FK noise level), and the need for a compatibility condition (Assumption 5.2) linking data-point placement to PDE-relevant parameter directions. Open questions include extending the framework to nonlinear PDEs (semilinear via BSDEs, fully nonlinear via 2BSDEs), other activations, and adaptive FK-point selection strategies.
