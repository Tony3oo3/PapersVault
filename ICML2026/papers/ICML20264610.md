## Problem

Existing agent benchmarks evaluate LLM agents under idealized assumptions—clean instructions and reliable tool outputs—yet real-world deployments involve ambiguous user interactions and unstable external tools. No systematic framework exists to categorize these noise sources, inject them in a controlled and solvability-preserving manner, and evaluate agent robustness with process-level (not just outcome-level) metrics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264610/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes an existing agent benchmark (τ²-Bench, VitaBench, HotPotQA, or 2WikiMultiHopQA) and a noise configuration specifying type (user-noise or tool-noise), fine-grained category, and injection stage (early/middle/late), then produces multi-dimensional outputs: task success rate (Avg@4), trajectory validity via step-wise indicators, a stability-gated success score that requires both correct outcome and valid reasoning path, plus efficiency metrics (token consumption, interaction steps). The noise is generated by a frozen LLM whose system prompt was pre-optimized against a reference agent (o3) under a solvability constraint, ensuring all evaluated agents face identical perturbations.

## Method

AgentNoiseBench introduces three coupled components. First, an empirical noise taxonomy derived from large-scale interaction logs, categorizing perturbations into five user-noise types (ambiguity, inconsistency, redundancy, topic drift, boundary probing) and five tool-noise types (redundancy, execution failure, incomplete response, erroneous output, misleading signal). Second, a constrained adversarial injection mechanism that optimizes only the system prompt θ of a frozen noise generator G against a state-of-the-art reference agent A_ref (o3), maximizing performance degradation subject to a hard solvability constraint I_solvable(G(x;θ))=1; the resulting θ* is frozen and applied uniformly to all agents. Third, a trajectory-aware evaluation protocol that defines step-wise validity indicators over the full interaction trajectory and a stability-gated success criterion SGA(τ;T) = I_traj(τ;T) · I_task(s_T;T), which rejects correct final answers achieved through deviated reasoning paths. This design separates genuine robustness from stochastic luck and enables fine-grained attribution of failures to specific noise types and injection stages.

## Results

Across 24 evaluated models, AgentNoiseBench reveals a mean accuracy drop of 20.8% under noise injection, with domain-specific declines of 24.1% (VitaBench), 21.2% (τ²-Bench), and 16.8% (Search). The strongest clean-environment performer is GPT-5.2 (w/ thinking) at 0.81 on τ²-Bench and 0.53 on VitaBench, while GLM-4.6 (w/o thinking) leads non-thinking models at 0.74 on τ²-Bench. Under tool noise, GPT-5.2 retains 0.61 on τ²-Bench (a 24.7% relative drop), whereas under user noise it retains 0.72 (an 11.1% drop), confirming that tool noise is substantially more destructive. Notably, thinking models do not consistently outperform non-thinking models in robustness; for example, GLM-4.6 (w/o thinking) achieves 0.48 under τ²-Bench tool noise versus 0.56 for GLM-4.6 (w/ thinking), but on VitaBench tool noise the non-thinking variant (0.32) trails the thinking variant (0.40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, AgentNoiseBench does not propose a new agent but evaluates 24 existing models under noisy conditions. GPT-5.2 (w/ thinking) achieves the highest clean scores (0.81 τ²-Bench, 0.53 VitaBench, 0.49 Search) and retains the best noisy performance (0.61/0.45/0.37 under tool noise), outperforming o3 (0.57/0.46/0.35) and Claude-4.5-Sonnet (w/) (0.57/0.41/0.29) on tool-noise τ²-Bench. Among non-thinking models, GLM-4.6 (w/o thinking) leads at 0.74 on τ²-Bench origin, matching LongCat-Flash-Chat, and retains 0.48 under tool noise—surpassing GPT-4.1 (0.30) and DeepSeek-V3-0324 (0.31) by a wide margin. A key finding is that thinking models do not uniformly outperform their non-thinking counterparts in robustness: GLM-4.6 (w/o thinking) scores 0.48 under τ²-Bench tool noise versus 0.56 for GLM-4.6 (w/ thinking), yet on VitaBench tool noise the non-thinking variant (0.32) trails the thinking variant (0.40), indicating that the reasoning-robustness relationship is domain-dependent. Tool noise consistently causes larger degradation than user noise across all model families (e.g., GPT-4.1 drops from 0.56 to 0.30 under tool noise vs. 0.50 under user noise on τ²-Bench), and execution failures are the single most destructive fine-grained noise type, reducing average scores to 0.31.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agent evaluation lineage established by τ²-Bench, VitaBench, and HotPotQA by introducing a systematic noise robustness layer on top of existing benchmarks. Prior robustness studies (InjecAgent, ToolScan, prompt-perturbation work) typically address single noise sources or lack a unified taxonomy; AgentNoiseBench departs by defining a two-axis taxonomy (user-noise vs. tool-noise) with fine-grained subcategories and a solvability-preserving adversarial injection mechanism optimized against a reference agent. The trajectory-aware evaluation protocol with a stability-gated success criterion also departs from the outcome-only metrics used in most prior agent benchmarks, adding process-level correctness as a gating condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that reasoning-enhanced (thinking) models are generally *less* robust than non-thinking models across most scenarios challenges the field's working assumption that stronger reasoning confers greater resilience to environmental perturbations, with practical implications for which architectures to deploy in noisy real-world settings. The framework's solvability-preserving constraint and frozen noise generator enable fair cross-model comparison, but limitations remain: the evaluation is restricted to language-centric tool-augmented agents, trajectory entropy offers correlational rather than causal diagnostics, and the computational cost of trajectory-aware evaluation limits scalability. Open questions include whether noise-aware training (e.g., RL with noise objectives) can close the robustness gap, how composite multi-source noise behaves, and whether a formal theoretical framework (borrowing from control theory or information theory) can bound agent robustness.
