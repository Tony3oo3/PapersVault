## Problem

Adaptive optimizers like Adam provide coordinate-wise variance adaptation for vector parameters, while spectral methods like Muon exploit matrix structure via the matrix sign function, but no principled framework unifies these two ideas. Natural attempts to graft Adam's second-order moments onto Muon's orthogonal updates lack a theoretical derivation and remain computationally intractable. The paper asks whether one can derive a matrix adaptive stepsize that plays the same role as Adam's per-coordinate SNR but operates over the singular-value spectrum of a gradient matrix.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stochastic gradient (vector g ∈ R^d or matrix G ∈ R^{n×m}), the current parameter, and standard hyperparameters (β₁, β₂, β₃, learning rate, eigendecomposition frequency). For the matrix variant DeVA_{S∞}, the gradient is rotated into a spectral basis via eigendecompositions of the left and right covariance factors L = GG^T and R = G^TG (recomputed every `freq` steps), and the variance adaptation is tracked as an EMA of the outer product of row and column ℓ₂-norms of the rotated gradient. The output is an updated parameter matrix plus a coordinate-wise adaptive scaling matrix Γ_t applied element-wise to the msign update in the rotated spectral space.

## Method

DeVA refactors the whitening (AdaGrad) update E[H]^{-1/2}g into a product of a variance adaptation term E[H]^{-1/2}H^{1/2} and a scale-invariant update H^{-1/2}g. For vectors with H = diag(gg^T), the scale-invariant part is sign(g) and the variance adaptation recovers Adam's per-coordinate stepsize. For matrices with H = L^{1/2}⊗R^{1/2}, the scale-invariant part becomes msign(G) (the matrix sign via SVD), and under a Kronecker curvature approximation the variance adaptation reduces to a coordinate-wise spectral matrix Ẽ_{ij} = E[σ_i σ_j]/(σ_i σ_j), where σ_i, σ_j are singular values of the gradient. In practice, E[σ_i σ_j] is approximated by an EMA of the outer product of row and column norms of the rotated gradient G' = Q_L^T G Q_R, making the update computable at O(n² + m² + 4nm) per step. The framework is shown to generalize Shampoo, SOAP, and SPlus as special cases, and convergence theorems under blockwise smoothness demonstrate that the adaptive stepsize actively reduces the smoothness constant in the error bound.

## Results

On a 274M NanoGPT model pretrained on FineWeb (1.4B tokens), DeVA_S∞ achieves a validation loss of 3.271, outperforming Muon (3.305) by 1% and SOAP (3.304) by 1%. In the token-efficiency evaluation (Figure 1), DeVA_S∞ reaches the target validation perplexity using 6.6% fewer tokens than non-adaptive Muon, compared to 4.3% for SOAP. On ViT-L/16 ImageNet-1K training (5 epochs, fixed LR 0.001), DeVA_S∞ demonstrates significantly faster initial convergence than all baselines. The runtime overhead of DeVA_S∞ is +54% per step relative to Muon (16.49s vs 10.72s per 1k tokens), primarily due to the eigendecomposition step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeVA_S∞ consistently outperforms non-adaptive Muon across all evaluated tasks: on NanoGPT/FineWeb it reduces validation loss from 3.305 to 3.271 (−1%) and saves 6.6% of training tokens; on the heterogeneous trace quadratic benchmark it shows a clear convergence advantage over Muon while SOAP shows no such gap. Against SOAP, DeVA_S∞ achieves a lower final loss (3.271 vs 3.304) with comparable runtime overhead (+54% vs +47% over Muon), and the paper attributes SOAP's weaker performance to its lack of explicit decoupling between the matrix sign update and the adaptive step size. DeVA_S∞ also outperforms Adamuon (which naively applies Adam's second-order moments to Muon's orthogonal update) on both NanoGPT and ImageNet-1K. In the vector setting, DeVA_ℓ∞ (equivalent to Adam under EMA approximation) outperforms SignSGD/Signum on the heterogeneous trace quadratic, mirroring the matrix-case advantage. The main trade-off is computational: DeVA_S∞ requires an eigendecomposition every 10 steps (amortized via power iteration + QR), adding ~54% per-step cost over Muon, though the paper notes this gap narrows at larger batch sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeVA unifies the vector-based adaptive optimization lineage (AdaGrad → Adam) and the matrix spectral optimization lineage (Shampoo → Muon) under a single preconditioned gradient descent framework, showing that Adam's adaptive stepsize and Muon's matrix sign update are both instances of a 'scale-invariant update' modulated by a 'variance adaptation' term. This departs from prior attempts like Adamuon, AdaGO, and SPlus-then-Adam, which bolted Adam's second-order moments onto Muon's orthogonal updates without deriving them from a principled decomposition. The Kronecker-product curvature approximation from SOAP and Shampoo is retained, but the paper's key distinction is that DeVA tracks the expectation of the outer product of unsquared singular-value norms (E[rc^T]) rather than the product of independent expectations used in SOAP, marking a fundamental difference from K-FAC and Shampoo-style factorizations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical contribution—showing that variance adaptation reduces the effective blockwise smoothness constant in the convergence bound—provides the first principled justification for why adaptive stepsizes should help in the matrix spectral setting, a question the paper explicitly identifies as open. Practically, the 6.6% token reduction over Muon on NanoGPT pretraining is meaningful but comes with a 54% runtime overhead from eigendecompositions, limiting immediate adoption at scale. Open questions include whether the local-stability assumption on eigenbases holds throughout training, whether the Kronecker approximation can be tightened, and whether the method's advantage persists at the multi-billion-parameter scale where runtime overhead becomes prohibitive.
