## Problem

Off-policy reinforcement learning suffers from value overestimation bias, but existing mitigation strategies (Clipped Double Q-learning, distributional gradient scaling) implicitly target only epistemic uncertainty while remaining blind to the aleatoric component. The paper identifies a previously overlooked phenomenon—Aleatoric Impulse—where high policy stochasticity during early training, combined with sparse data, triggers a massive transient surge in value overestimation that permanently derails the learning trajectory and locks agents into suboptimal policies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations and replay buffer samples to produce continuous actions via a decoupled actor (a pessimistic target policy for value backup and an optimistic behavior policy for data collection). The critic ensemble outputs Gaussian return distributions (mean Q and variance σ²) from which epistemic uncertainty (variance across ensemble means) and aleatoric uncertainty (mean of individual distribution variances) are explicitly extracted. A key structural detail is that the variance head of each critic tracks an independent per-critic sub-target rather than a shared target, preventing entanglement of aleatoric and epistemic components in the learned σ.

## Method

DSAC-AID integrates an Aleatoric Impulse Damping (AID) mechanism into Distributional Soft Actor-Critic with a critic ensemble of m Gaussian return distributions. The critic disentangles total value uncertainty into epistemic (ensemble mean variance) and aleatoric (mean of per-critic return variances) components, then synthesizes a total value uncertainty σ_Q = √(σ_epi² + β·σ_ale²) to construct a pessimistic lower confidence bound target; β is adaptively annealed from a high initial value to zero via a closed-loop relative overestimation error signal, mirroring the theoretical 1/n decay of aleatoric value uncertainty. The actor is split into a target policy penalized only by epistemic uncertainty (risk-neutral to inherent stochasticity) and a behavior policy bonused by an upper confidence bound on total value uncertainty, symmetrically canceling the aleatoric suppression. Gradient modulation is restructured so the mean Q update is scaled by the full uncertainty (σ_θi² + σ_epi²) while the variance update remains scaled by σ_θi² alone, ensuring clean aleatoric learning without epistemic interference.

## Results

DSAC-AID achieves a normalized Total Average Return (TAR) of 0.918 on Gym-MuJoCo (+15.8% over the strongest model-free baseline BRO at 0.793) and 0.926 on the DeepMind Control Suite (+7.9% over DoubleGum, +5.5% over the leading model-based method BOOM). On individual tasks, it reaches 10.0k on Ant-v3 (+20.5% over BRO), 12.7k on Humanoid-v3 (+15.5%), and 405.2k on HumanoidStandup-v4. On the most challenging DMC task Humanoid-run, it leads BOOM by +17.7% and DoubleGum by +31.0%, while also surpassing BOOM on Dog-run by +7.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSAC-AID achieves the best TAR on every task across both benchmarks, outperforming all 9 baselines. Among model-free methods, it surpasses the strongest baseline BRO by +15.8% on Gym-MuJoCo (0.918 vs 0.793) and +8.4% on DMC (0.926 vs 0.853), with the largest individual margins on Ant-v3 (+20.5%) and Humanoid-run (+45.2% over BRO). Against DoubleGum, the advantage is +35.6% on Gym-MuJoCo and +7.9% on DMC. Against the distributional baseline DSAC-T, gains are +52.3% on Gym-MuJoCo and +14.3% on DMC. Against model-based methods (compared in Table 5), DSAC-AID leads BOOM by +5.5% average on DMC, with particularly large margins on Humanoid-run (+17.7%) and Dog-run (+7.7%), while also outperforming TD-MPC2, BMPC, and DreamerV3 across all DMC tasks. Notably, DSAC-AID achieves these results without the inference-time computational cost of model-based planning, and even with a minimal m=2 ensemble it still outperforms BRO, DoubleGum, and BOOM on all tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Distributional Soft Actor-Critic (DSAC/DSAC-T) lineage by introducing an explicit uncertainty disentanglement mechanism absent from all prior distributional off-policy methods. While CDQ-based algorithms (TD3, SAC, DSAC-T) implicitly penalize only epistemic uncertainty via the minimum operator, and network-regularized methods (BRO, DoubleGum) apply static structural constraints, DSAC-AID is the first to decompose total return uncertainty into epistemic and aleatoric components and recombine them adaptively for targeted value-uncertainty modeling. The dual-policy design (pessimistic target policy for value backup, optimistic behavior policy for exploration) draws on the tactical optimism/pessimism literature but is specifically calibrated so that the aleatoric component cancels symmetrically between critic and actor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of aleatoric impulse as a previously unacknowledged, transient-yet-permanently-damaging failure mode reframes how the community should think about early-training instability in off-policy RL. The finding that network regularization (LayerNorm/GroupNorm) is structurally blind to aleatoric surges—acting only as a low-pass filter on epistemic noise—provides a useful diagnostic for practitioners choosing between objective and network regularization. Open questions include whether the β annealing schedule can be replaced by direct total-value-uncertainty estimation (as the authors suggest), whether the mechanism transfers to discrete-action or model-based settings, and whether the 2.35× computational overhead of the 4-critic ensemble can be reduced without sacrificing the statistical richness needed for clean epistemic/aleatoric separation.
