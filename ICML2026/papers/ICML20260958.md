## Problem

Prevailing AI-generated image detection methods treat pre-trained vision models as black-box feature extractors and require expensive full-parameter fine-tuning on large forgery datasets to acquire detection capability. This paper challenges that paradigm by asking whether forgery-detection knowledge is already latent within pre-trained representations, dormant in sparse neurons, and can be elicited without end-to-end retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260958/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (512×512 to 1024×1024 depending on the source generator) and produces a binary real/fake classification with a confidence score. Crucially, the backbone remains entirely frozen; only a lightweight linear probe is trained on a few-shot set (as few as 200–500 samples), and at inference the model reads activations from a sparse subset of neurons (FDUs) in specific intermediate layers, concatenating them into a compact feature vector for classification.

## Method

DNA employs a coarse-to-fine excavation mechanism over a frozen pre-trained ViT. At the coarse level, it localizes critical intermediate layers (e.g., layers 12–18 in a 24-layer ViT) by intersecting three signals: cosine-distance separability of real/fake class centroids, L2 divergence of [CLS] attention distributions, and linear-probing accuracy. At the fine level, it computes a triadic fusion score S_{i,k} = |g̅_{i,k} · ā_{i,k} · w_{i,k}| for each neuron, combining gradient sensitivity, mean activation magnitude, and the linear-probe weight, then applies the Kneedle algorithm to the normalized score ranking to automatically select the top-k* neurons as Forgery-Discriminative Units (FDUs). The resulting sparse FDU activations form a compact, semantically-decoupled subspace that captures intrinsic forgery traces, achieving superior few-shot detection without any backbone fine-tuning.

## Results

On the ForenSynths benchmark, DNA achieves a mean accuracy of 97.2% and mean AP of 99.5%, surpassing the strongest baseline MoLD (91.4% ACC) by 5.8 percentage points. On the GenImage benchmark, DNA reaches 96.5% mean ACC and 99.4% mean AP, outperforming MoLD (88.2%) by 8.3 points. On the newly introduced HIFI-Gen dataset (unseen architectures including FLUX, SDv3.5, and Z-Image), DNA attains 96.4% mean ACC without any fine-tuning, exceeding DRCT(Conv-B) (91.1%) by 5.3% and MoLD (81.0%) by 15.4%, while traditional methods like CNNDetection and DIRE collapse to near-random performance (~50–55%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNA outperforms all eight baselines on both ForenSynths and GenImage in mean accuracy. The closest competitor, MoLD (Park et al., 2025), trails by 5.8% on ForenSynths (91.4% vs. 97.2%) and 8.3% on GenImage (88.2% vs. 96.5%); on the particularly challenging Midjourney subset, DNA achieves 93.7% ACC versus MoLD's 76.3% (a 17.4-point gap). On the unseen-architecture HIFI-Gen benchmark, traditional methods (CNNDetection, DIRE, PatchFor) degrade to near-random accuracy (50–65%), while MoLD shows high volatility (70.4% on Z-Image). DNA maintains 91.6–96.2% ACC across all five unseen generators, surpassing DRCT(Conv-B) by 5.3% in mean and by 7.2% on FLUX specifically. NPR and LGrad, which rely on gradient/up-sampling artifacts, perform well on GAN-based ForenSynths but collapse on diffusion-based GenImage (63.5% and 71.6% mean ACC respectively), highlighting their inability to generalize across generative paradigms. UnivFD, a CLIP-based probing method, achieves moderate results (89.1% on ForenSynths) but drops to 65.5% on HIFI-Gen, confirming that lightweight probing without neuron-level selection lacks robustness to novel architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'extract features then fine-tune' paradigm in AI-generated image detection (DIRE, DRCT, MoLD, UnivFD) by arguing that forgery-discriminative capability is already encoded in pre-trained backbones and need only be 'awakened' via sparse neuron selection rather than acquired through end-to-end retraining. Methodologically, it borrows the coarse-to-fine probing strategy from MoLD (Park et al., 2025) and the neuron-importance scoring ideas from mechanistic interpretability (SAEs, domain-specific neuron identification), but repurposes them to construct a compact forgery-sensitive subspace (FDUs) via a triadic fusion score and Kneedle-based truncation. The HIFI-Gen benchmark extends the GenImage/ForenSynths lineage by incorporating the latest DiT and flow-matching generators (FLUX, SDv3.5, Z-Image), addressing a clear temporal gap in existing evaluation suites.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the 'latent knowledge' framing holds broadly, it could shift the field away from resource-intensive fine-tuning pipelines toward lightweight, interpretable probing of frozen backbones, with the 10× inference speedup making real-time deployment on social-media-scale pipelines feasible. The HIFI-Gen benchmark fills a practical gap by providing a testbed for the most current generative architectures, which prior datasets (relying on GANs and early U-Net diffusion models) cannot stress-test. Open questions include whether the FDU selection generalizes to specialized domains (medical imaging, satellite imagery) and whether the linear-probing step—still required to 'activate' the latent signal—undermines the pure 'no training' narrative. The paper acknowledges its scope is limited to general-purpose vision backbones and does not yet address video, 3D, or multimodal forgery.
