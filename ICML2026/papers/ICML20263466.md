## Problem

Existing multiplicative weight update (MWU) methods, such as Madam, fail to train modern deep networks (ViT, GPT-2) from scratch due to excessive weight growth, and none incorporate multiplicative noise injection or log-normal weight distributions. No prior algorithm has successfully combined all three biologically motivated properties—multiplicative updates, multiplicative noise, and log-normal weights—into a single optimizer capable of stable low-precision training.

## Input / Output

LMD takes model parameters represented as strictly positive median vectors m+ and m− (via the EG± trick, doubling parameter count) along with stochastic loss gradients, and produces updated median parameters. During training, actual weights are sampled as θ = m ⊙ ε with ε ~ LogN(0, σ²I), injecting multiplicative noise before the forward pass; during inference, the expected weight E[θ] = m·exp(σ²/2) is used. Forward-pass matrix multiplications can be emulated in MXFP6 or MXFP4 microscaling formats while backward passes remain in bfloat16.

## Method

LMD is a variational Bayesian optimizer that places a log-normal posterior over weights and derives updates via the Lie-Group Bayesian Learning Rule framework. Three key design choices enable stable large-scale training: (1) a multiplicative weight decay regularizer derived from a log-normal prior, which drives log m toward log m_r in logarithmic space rather than driving weights toward zero, providing a soft-clipping effect that suppresses excessive weight growth; (2) a Lion-style signed gradient momentum with decoupled regularization (separate β1 for the sign update and β2 for the EMA), avoiding the instability of coupling gradient and penalty in a single momentum; and (3) weight-scaled gradients (θ ⊙ ∇ℓ(θ)) that naturally arise from multiplicative perturbations in the Lie-group geometry. The EG± trick (θ_trick = θ+ − θ−) preserves sign flexibility, and the entire method requires only 4P stored parameters (m+, m−, ν+, ν−), comparable to AdamW's 3P.

## Results

On ImageNet with a Vision Transformer, LMD achieves 77.06% top-1 accuracy (bfloat16) and 77.15% top-1 accuracy (MXFP6 forward pass), substantially outperforming AdamW (68.11%/67.98%) and Madam (60.14%/13.47%). On GPT-2 trained on OpenWebText with sequence length 4096, LMD achieves a validation loss of 2.926 (bfloat16) and 2.927 (MXFP6), compared to AdamW's 4.791 (bfloat16) and 2.980 (MXFP6), and Madam's 3.303 (bfloat16) and 4.575 (MXFP6). Notably, LMD shows virtually no performance degradation when switching from bfloat16 to MXFP6 forward passes in both ViT and GPT-2, while Madam collapses under low-precision computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdamW, LMD achieves a 9-point improvement in top-1 accuracy on ImageNet/ViT (77.06% vs. 68.11%) and a substantially lower validation loss on GPT-2 at sequence length 4096 (2.926 vs. 4.791), though it is marginally worse at sequence length 1024 (2.961 vs. 2.937). Against Madam, LMD dominates across all settings: on ViT with MXFP6 forward passes, Madam collapses to 13.47% top-1 accuracy while LMD maintains 77.15%, and on GPT-2 at sequence length 4096, LMD achieves 2.926 vs. Madam's 3.303 (bfloat16) or 4.575 (MXFP6). Against the original Lie-Group Bayesian Learning Rule of Kiral et al. (2023), LMD achieves 98.60% test accuracy on MNIST/MLP with a single Monte Carlo sample, whereas the original method diverges (NaN) or degrades to 62.52–85.63% under the same setting. LMD's key advantage is its stability under low-precision (MXFP6) forward passes, where it shows negligible performance loss while both AdamW and Madam exhibit degradation or outright failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

LMD extends the Lie-Group Bayesian Learning Rule of Kiral et al. (2023) by adding a multiplicative weight decay regularizer and a Lion-style signed gradient accumulation scheme, making the originally small-scale algorithm viable for modern architectures. It departs from the Madam optimizer (Bernstein et al., 2020), which used multiplicative weight updates but lacked noise injection and effective regularization, and from Bayesian neural network approaches that inject noise but rely on additive gradient updates. The paper's key conceptual contribution is the first explicit combination of all three biologically-motivated properties—multiplicative updates, multiplicative (log-normal) noise injection, and log-normal weight distributions—into a single trainable algorithm, which no prior method had achieved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that multiplicative weight updates can stably train GPT-2 and Vision Transformers from scratch, a capability previously blocked by uncontrolled weight growth. The practical relevance is significant for energy-efficient hardware: LMD maintains performance under MXFP6 forward passes where AdamW degrades, suggesting a path toward native low-precision training on emerging MX-format accelerators. Open questions include whether LMD can handle low-precision backward passes (preliminary results are negative), whether it transfers to fine-tuning and downstream tasks, and whether the ~1.5× optimizer memory overhead from the EG± trick becomes prohibitive at larger scales. The paper also leaves open the question of whether the observed stability in low-precision settings is due to the multiplicative noise itself or an interaction with quantization error acting as implicit regularization.
