## Problem

It remains unclear whether multimodal agents performing document-intensive tasks exhibit genuine strategic planning or merely stochastic trial-and-error search. Existing benchmarks are fragmented: agent-centric ones ignore visual comprehension, domain-specific ones restrict scope, and general-purpose ones suffer from LLM-generated questions or recycled documents. MADQA addresses this by introducing a fully human-authored benchmark over fresh, heterogeneous PDFs specifically designed to discriminate strategic reasoning from brute-force retrieval.

## Input / Output

The system receives a natural language query posed over a corpus of 800 multi-page PDFs (18,619 pages, 12.2M tokens) spanning 13 domains with highly heterogeneous layouts. It must produce an extractive answer whose tokens physically appear in the evidence pages, along with a minimal set of cited pages (page-level, not bounding-box). Critically, 58% of questions require non-textual visual understanding (tables, charts, checkboxes, layout structure), and 17.3% demand multi-hop reasoning across disjoint pages or documents, making single-query retrieval insufficient by construction.

## Method

MADQA formalizes Agentic Document Collection VQA through six properties (Extractive, Multi-Hop, Closed-World, Grounded, Agentic, Visual) and introduces a multi-axis evaluation protocol. Answer correctness uses a calibrated LLM-as-a-judge (κ=0.88 with humans) that enforces extractive grounding; attribution is measured via Page F1 and Doc F1 to diagnose last-mile navigation failures. The key novel metric is the Kuiper statistic, derived from the Cumulative Difference method, which quantifies effort calibration by measuring how accuracy conditioned on increasing step count deviates from the global mean—high values reveal agents expending budget on queries they ultimately fail. Splits are created via Classical Test Theory, selecting items by difficulty and discrimination while reserving a Sentinel Pool of unsolved hard items. Construct validity is certified through lexical-overlap analysis (confirming semantic, not keyword, retrieval is needed), parametric-knowledge probing (bounding guessability at 9–15%), and visual-necessity taxonomy.

## Results

On MADQA (500-question test set), the best agentic system (Gemini 3 Pro BM25 Agent) achieves 82.2% accuracy, outperforming its static RAG counterpart (Gemini 3 Pro File Search, 78.6%) and matching the human BM25 baseline (82.2%). However, a ~17.2% 'Oracle Gap' persists: humans with perfect retrieval reach 99.4%, revealing that retrieval—not reasoning—remains the primary bottleneck. The Kuiper calibration statistic (lower is better) shows humans at 14.6 versus 22.9–73.2 for agents, indicating agents waste compute on unproductive search loops. Multi-hop subsets (X-Page, X-Doc) are substantially harder, with the best agent dropping to 66.8% and 73.0% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between agentic and non-agentic retrieval pipelines. The best BM25 MLLM Agent (82.2%) beats M3DocRAG (61.6%) by 20.6 points and HEAVEN (52.9% with GPT-5.2) by 29.3 points, confirming that iterative planning over a search tool substantially outperforms single-pass visual or hybrid retrieval. ColBERTv2 + Llama-3.1-8B (40.2%) is the weakest baseline, trailing the top agent by over 42 points. Among agentic architectures, constrained BM25 agents outperform unconstrained Recursive Language Models: Gemini 3 Pro BM25 Agent (82.2%) beats Gemini 3 Pro RLM (73.8%) by 8.4 points, and the Claude Sonnet 4.5 RLM consumed 270M input tokens (~$850) without matching its BM25 counterpart (80.6%). MDocAgent (63.8%), a fixed five-stage multi-agent pipeline, falls well below the top BM25 agents, suggesting that rigid orchestration is less effective than flexible iterative search. Notably, the best agent matches the human BM25 baseline in raw accuracy (82.2%) but on largely different questions (Cohen's κ = 0.24), and humans calibrate effort far better (Kuiper 14.6 vs. 25.8+ for all agents).

## Relation to Prior Work

MADQA extends the document QA and RAG benchmarking landscape by introducing a formal 'Agentic' property (Property 5) requiring that no single retrieval query can surface all evidence, thereby mandating iterative planning, navigation, and aggregation over heterogeneous PDF collections. It departs from prior document RAG benchmarks (ViDoRE, DocBench, M3DocRAG) that evaluate single-step retrieval, and from agentic research benchmarks (BRIGHT, Researchy Questions) that operate over HTML or plain text rather than visually rich PDFs. Methodologically, it borrows the Cumulative Difference framework (Kloumann et al., 2024) to derive the Kuiper effort-calibration statistic and applies Classical Test Theory for principled test-set construction, both of which are repurposed from psychometrics and paired-sample analysis into the LLM evaluation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~18% oracle gap and the cold-start disparity (humans reach 50% accuracy on their first query vs. 12% for Gemini 3 Pro) reframe the bottleneck as retrieval calibration rather than reasoning capacity, with practical implications for deployment cost. The Kuiper statistic elevates compute-efficiency from a deployment afterthought to a first-class quality axis, and the human-agent complementarity finding (κ=0.24 at matched accuracy) suggests hybrid pipelines could exceed either alone. Open questions include whether episodic memory or RL with search-tool feedback can close the calibration gap, and whether the U.S.-centric, English-only corpus generalizes to multilingual or non-Western document ecosystems.
