## Problem

LLM-based step-level theorem provers face a dual scaling bottleneck: at training time, reinforcement learning via expert iteration hits performance plateaus where the model overfits to narrow proof patterns and stops improving; at inference time, pure tree search lacks global planning and shares no knowledge across independent search trajectories, causing exponential blowup in the search space. This paper tackles both challenges simultaneously to enable sustained long-horizon improvement and efficient inference-time scaling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265180/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a Lean 4 theorem statement (formalized from natural-language math problems via GPT-4o/Claude autoformalization) and produces a complete Lean 4 proof as a sequence of tactic strings verified by the Lean 4.10.0 compiler. At inference, the planner (Gemini 2.5 Pro) first emits a list of intermediate subgoals formatted as Lean `have` statements without proofs; the prover agents then generate tactic sequences for each subgoal. The shared subgoal cache stores proven subgoals as hypotheses that augment the context for all downstream subgoals, so the final output is a single coherent proof script where earlier lemmas are available to later steps.

## Method

The training pipeline is a multi-stage expert iteration (off-policy RL) built on Qwen2.5-Math-7B/32B, with two key mechanisms to escape plateaus: (1) adaptive tactic-level perplexity filtering that discards the top and bottom 15% of the tactic negative-log-probability distribution each round, creating an automated curriculum that trains only on the 'just right' difficulty band; and (2) periodic retraining that re-synthesizes all proofs with the current expert, applies more aggressive 20% filtering, and retrains from the base checkpoint for three epochs, acting as a soft reset that restores exploratory entropy. At inference, a planner-prover hierarchy decomposes theorems into subgoals, and multiple prover instances run best-first tree search in parallel on each active subgoal, coordinated through a shared subgoal cache that signals all agents to stop once the first finds a valid proof, converting total search complexity from the product of subgoal complexities to their sum.

## Results

BFS-Prover-V2-32B with the planner achieves 95.08% on miniF2F-test (95.49% on miniF2F-valid) and 41.4% on ProofNet-test, establishing state-of-the-art among step-level tree-search provers. The 7B variant reaches 92.6% on miniF2F and 34.4% on ProofNet. On ProofNet, the 32B model surpasses the 20× larger DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (37.1%) and its 7B counterpart (29.6%), demonstrating strong out-of-distribution generalization. Single-configuration (non-accumulative) results are 86.1% miniF2F / 41.4% ProofNet for 32B and 82.4% / 34.4% for 7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among tree-search provers, BFS-Prover-V2-32B w/Planner (95.1% miniF2F) substantially outperforms all prior step-level methods: MPS-Prover-7B (75.8%), BFS-Prover-V1-7B (73.0%), Hunyuan-Prover-7B (68.4%), and InternLM2.5-Step-7B (65.9%). Against whole-proof provers, the 32B model (95.1%) is competitive with Delta-Prover (95.9%) and Seed-Prover (99.6%), and clearly surpasses Goedel-Prover-V2-32B (92.6%), Kimina-Prover-72B (92.2%), and DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (88.9%). On ProofNet, BFS-Prover-V2-32B (41.4%) is the only step-level prover reported and beats the much larger DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (37.1%) by 4.3 points, while the 7B variant (34.4%) also exceeds DeepSeek-Prover-V2-7B (29.6%). The planner alone contributes a 10.2-point gain for the 7B model (82.4%→92.6%) and a 9.0-point gain for the 32B model (86.1%→95.1%), narrowing the gap between model scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BFS-Prover-V1 best-first tree search framework and the AlphaZero-style expert iteration paradigm (originally applied to theorem proving by GPT-f) into a dual-scaling system. It departs from whole-proof generation approaches (DeepSeek-Prover-V2, Goedel-Prover-V2) by maintaining a step-level MDP formulation, and from prior hierarchical planning work (Draft, Sketch, and Prove) by introducing a shared subgoal cache that enables multi-agent parallelism. The two key departures from prior RL-for-proving work are the adaptive perplexity-based tactic filtering (replacing coarse problem-level difficulty metrics) and the periodic retraining 'soft reset' mechanism to escape local optima in long-horizon expert iteration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, well-ablated recipe for sustaining RL improvement in LLM provers over extended training, addressing the widely-observed plateau problem that limits most RL-for-reasoning pipelines. The inference-time planner-prover architecture with shared subgoal cache is a reusable pattern for any long-horizon search task, and the finding that a 32B step-prover outperforms a 671B whole-proof model on OOD generalization (41.4% vs. 37.1% on ProofNet) suggests that step-level adaptability may be more important than raw model scale for transfer. Open questions include co-training the planner with the prover policy, incorporating negative/failed trajectories, and whether the periodic retraining mechanism generalizes beyond formal mathematics to other multi-turn reasoning domains.
