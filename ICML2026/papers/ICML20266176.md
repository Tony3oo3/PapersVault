## Problem

Existing T2AV benchmarks either evaluate audio and video in isolation or reduce joint assessment to coarse embedding similarity (CLIP/CLAP), failing to diagnose specific failure modes such as incorrect musical pitch, illegible scene text, identity drift, and physical law violations. No prior benchmark provides fine-grained, task-driven evaluation that verifies whether a model actually accomplishes what a realistic user prompt intends across both modalities simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266176/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a single interleaved text prompt (averaging 88.54 tokens across 235 curated tasks in 11 categories) and a generated audio-video clip (standardized to 720p, ~10 s for most models; 8 s for Veo 3.1, 12 s for Sora 2, 5 s for the Wan 2.2 pipeline). It produces a structured set of 10 scores spanning three granularities—basic uni-modal aesthetics (Q-Align visual score, Audiobox production quality), cross-modal synchronization (Syncformer AV offset in seconds, SyncNet lip-sync error in frames), and six fine-grained semantic modules—aggregated into a weighted Total score (0.2·basic + 0.2·cross + 0.6·fine).

## Method

AVGen-Bench introduces a hybrid evaluation architecture that chains lightweight specialist models as signal-level feature extractors with Gemini 3 Flash as a high-level reasoning engine, enabling verification that neither paradigm alone can achieve. Each fine-grained module implements a distinct pipeline: Scene Text uses PaddleOCR extraction → spatiotemporal clustering → MLLM dual-objective verification; Facial Consistency uses InsightFace embeddings → IoU/cosine tracklet construction → DBSCAN identity clustering → MLLM-derived character-count and stability scoring; Pitch Accuracy uses Gemini constraint extraction → Basic-Pitch automatic music transcription to MIDI → Gemini symbolic logic verification against music-theory constraints; Speech uses Faster-Whisper ASR with VAD → Gemini adaptive auditing (verbatim lexical matching vs. contextual coherence); Physical Plausibility decouples VideoPhy2-AutoEval kinematic scoring from a two-stage Gemini causal-reasoning pipeline; and Holistic Alignment uses Gemini constraint decomposition across narrative, visual, audio, and cinematography dimensions followed by evidence-based scoring. The 235-prompt suite is curated via a human-in-the-loop GPT-5.2 pipeline and is explicitly decoupled from the evaluation metrics, ensuring prompts reflect genuine user intent rather than reverse-engineered detector targets.

## Results

On AVGen-Bench, Seedance 2.0 achieves the highest overall score (72.07), followed by Veo 3.1-fast (67.87) and Veo 3.1-quality (66.28). Visual quality is near-saturated across all models (0.83–0.97), while fine-grained dimensions reveal severe weaknesses: musical pitch accuracy is universally poor (all models <12/100, best being Seedance 2.0 at 28.12), text rendering ranges from 14.52 (Kling 2.6) to 76.95 (Wan 2.6), and facial consistency peaks at only 60.95 (Seedance 2.0). Speech intelligibility shows the largest model gap, with Veo 3.1-quality reaching 96.09 versus Ovi at 76.49 and the Wan 2.2+HunyuanVideo-Foley cascade at 53.40.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Seedance 2.0 leads on the aggregate Total score (72.07) and dominates fine-grained visual metrics (Face 60.95, Music 28.12) and high-level physical reasoning (Hi-Phy 83.16), but trails Veo 3.1-quality on speech (94.09 vs. 96.09) and Veo 3.1-fast on lip sync (4.14 vs. 2.39 frames). Veo 3.1 variants are the strongest on speech intelligibility (94.53/96.09) and lip synchronization (2.39/3.59 frames), confirming Google's TTS integration advantage, yet they lag on facial consistency (~53) and holistic alignment compared to Seedance 2.0. Sora 2 achieves the second-best holistic semantic alignment (88.89) and high-level physics (78.95) but has the lowest visual quality (0.848) and audio PQ (5.91) among proprietary models. Open-source unified models (LTX-2, LTX-2.3, Ovi) cluster in the 52–60 Total range, with Ovi showing the worst speech (76.49) and holistic alignment (57.45). Cascaded pipelines (Wan2.2+HunyuanVideo-Foley, Emu3.5+MOVA, NanoBanana2+MOVA) consistently underperform unified models, with the T2V+V2A cascade scoring lowest on speech (53.40) and facial consistency (36.23), suggesting that joint generation architectures better preserve cross-modal coherence than sequential pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

AVGen-Bench carves new ground in T2AV evaluation by departing from the metric-driven prompt design of prior benchmarks (Harmony-Bench, UniAVGen, VerseBench, JavisBench) that reverse-engineer prompts to fit available detectors. Instead, it adopts a task-driven, intent-first curation strategy where prompts are decoupled from evaluation metrics, and introduces a hybrid evaluation architecture that chains lightweight specialist models (PaddleOCR, InsightFace, Basic-Pitch, Faster-Whisper, VideoPhy2) with MLLM reasoning (Gemini 3 Flash) to probe fine-grained semantic dimensions—musical pitch accuracy, scene text legibility, physical plausibility—that embedding-based metrics (CLIP, CLAP, FVD) fundamentally cannot verify. The benchmark also extends the scope beyond dialogue-centric evaluation (MTAVG-Bench) to encompass non-speech sound design, object interactions, and chemical/physical phenomena.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark and actionable diagnostic: current T2AV systems are essentially texture generators that excel at cinematic aesthetics but lack grounded world knowledge, as evidenced by universal failure in musical pitch control (<12/100 across all 13 models), persistent text rendering collapse, and incorrect physical dynamics. This finding suggests that coarse alignment training paradigms are insufficient and that future progress requires finer-grained supervision signals. Open questions include whether the pitch and physics gaps will close with scale or require fundamentally different training objectives, and whether the MLLM-dependent evaluation (currently Gemini 3 Flash) introduces model-specific biases that could shift as MLLM capabilities evolve. The 235-prompt scale, while validated for stability, may underrepresent long-tail scenarios.
