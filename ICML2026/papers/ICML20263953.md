## Problem

Prediction-powered inference (PPI) methods leverage AI predictions to improve statistical efficiency but are designed exclusively for i.i.d. offline data, while adaptive experiments collect data sequentially under a bandit policy, making the data non-i.i.d. and invalidating classical inference. Naively applying PPI estimators to adaptively collected data breaks unbiasedness, and ignoring AI predictions discards potentially substantial auxiliary information. The paper asks how to combine adaptively collected labeled data with potentially inaccurate black-box AI predictions to achieve both valid and efficient statistical inference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three data streams: adaptively collected labeled triples (context, action, reward) from a contextual bandit, a growing pool of unlabeled contexts that arrive incrementally (e.g., 100 new contexts per time step), and black-box AI model predictions f_a(x) for each action a and context x. Propensity scores from the bandit policy are required for inverse weighting. The output is an online-updated parameter estimate for a Z-functional of the reward distribution, along with valid confidence intervals and a consistent asymptotic variance estimate; the per-step update has O(d³) complexity independent of the experiment length T.

## Method

The PPAI estimator extends PPI++ to adaptive settings by embedding inverse propensity weighting into the prediction-powered Z-equation: it combines IPW-weighted labeled data, unlabeled data scored by AI predictions, and a correction term that subtracts IPW-weighted predictions evaluated on labeled data, thereby canceling both adaptive bias and prediction bias simultaneously. A data-driven power-tuning parameter λ_T adaptively weights the AI predictions according to their informativeness, derived as the minimizer of the asymptotic variance trace and estimated consistently from running sums of score functions. Asymptotic normality is established via a martingale central limit theorem applied to the prediction-powered estimating equation under adaptivity, a setting not covered by existing results. The estimator guarantees that its asymptotic variance is never larger than the labeled-only WLS baseline and is strictly smaller when predictions are informative, with the online update procedure maintaining O(d³) per-step cost independent of T.

## Results

In the two-armed contextual bandit simulation (T=5000, ε=0.3), PPAI with a strong predictive model achieves a 90% CI width of 0.011 for the first component of θ₀*, compared to 0.111 for the labeled-only WLS baseline — a tenfold reduction in width with comparable coverage (91% vs. 91%). PPAI with a poor model still reduces width to 0.021 (90% coverage). On the real-world movie recommendation task (T=1000, N=10,000), PPAI produces 90% CIs that are 5–7× shorter than WLS (e.g., 0.21 vs. 1.24 for β₀⁽³⁾) while also improving coverage from 83% to 95%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the Weighted Least Squares (WLS) estimator from Chen et al. (2021), which uses inverse propensity weighting on labeled data only and ignores unlabeled contexts and AI predictions. PPAI dominates WLS on every metric in both the simulation and the real-data experiment: in the simulation at T=5000, PPAI(f^strong) reduces CI width by ~10× (0.011 vs. 0.111) while PPAI(f^poor) still achieves a ~5× reduction (0.021 vs. 0.111), both at comparable or better coverage. On The Movies Dataset, PPAI narrows CIs by 5–7× (0.21 vs. 1.24 and 0.22 vs. 1.46) and simultaneously improves coverage from 82–83% to 95–99%, indicating that WLS's intervals are not only wider but also under-cover. The power-tuning parameter λ̂_T adapts automatically: it converges to ≈0.988 for the strong model (fully leveraging predictions) and ≈0.116 for the poor model (nearly reverting to the WLS baseline), confirming the theoretical guarantee that PPAI never performs worse than the labeled-only estimator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: prediction-powered inference (PPI/PPI++), which improves statistical efficiency by incorporating AI predictions into i.i.d. labeled-data estimation, and adaptive inference in contextual bandits, which corrects for non-i.i.d. data via inverse propensity weighting. The PPAI estimator merges PPI++'s rectified correction term with the IPW structure from Chen et al. (2021) and Guo & Xu (2025), but the combination is non-trivial: naively applying PPI to adaptively collected data breaks unbiasedness, and the proof requires a martingale CLT rather than the empirical process arguments used in offline PPI. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first framework to provide valid prediction-powered inference under adaptive data collection with model misspecification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is significant: adaptive experiments in clinical trials, recommendation systems, and edtech routinely have access to pretrained outcome models and large unlabeled covariate pools, yet prior methods either ignore these predictions or require i.i.d. data. The data-driven power-tuning parameter λ guarantees a safety property (asymptotic variance never exceeds the labeled-only baseline) while delivering substantial efficiency gains when predictions are informative, making the method low-risk to deploy. Open questions include extension to covariate shift (sketched in Appendix F), non-stationary or time-varying policies, multi-action simultaneous inference, and whether the O(d³) per-step update remains tractable in high-dimensional settings. The reliance on a minimum sampling probability π_min also limits applicability to highly exploitative policies.
