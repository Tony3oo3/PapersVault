## Problem

Existing VLM-based scene graph generation methods rely on flat object representations that ignore part-whole hierarchies, and they suffer from pervasive relational hallucinations where spatially implausible predicates (e.g., 'behind' instead of 'in') receive high confidence due to co-occurrence biases. These issues are compounded in open-world and zero-shot settings, where both object and predicate categories may be unseen, and no task-specific training is available to correct the model's biases.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and internally estimates a dense depth map (via Depth Anything V3) to provide 3D geometric cues. The output is a hierarchical scene graph G=(V,E) where nodes form a multi-level tree (depth up to 3) capturing part-whole semantics, each annotated with a bounding box, category label, and fine-grained attributes, and edges encode relation triplets (subject, predicate, object) that are both geometrically consistent and semantically coherent. The graph is produced entirely at inference time without any training.

## Method

HSGG is a two-stage training-free pipeline. Stage 1 (BHEP) constructs hierarchical entities via top-down VLM-driven object decomposition (recursively expanding root objects into constituent parts grounded by an open-vocabulary detector) combined with bottom-up attribute propagation from leaf patches to parent nodes. Stage 2 (GG-CRR) first applies geometry-aware relation filtering (GA-RF), which computes a scale-aware spatial distance combining 2D center proximity, 3D depth difference, and bounding-box diagonal normalization to prune implausible object pairs below a threshold τ. For surviving pairs, geometry-grounded contrastive relation decoding (GG-CRD) contrasts token logits from a visually grounded expert model (receiving the cropped image patch) against a hallucination-prone amateur model (receiving only bounding boxes and depth cues), applying an adaptive plausibility mask to suppress tokens favored by geometric or co-occurrence priors while preserving visually consistent predicates.

## Results

On zero-shot SGG, HSGG achieves zR@50/100 of 13.5/15.1 on VG150 and 11.4/14.1 on PSG, outperforming the strongest training-free baseline OwSGG by +12.3/+13.1 and +6.7/+7.7 respectively, and surpassing the best trained method CAPSGG by +1.0/+1.8 on VG. In the open-world setting on VG, HSGG reaches R@50/100 of 9.3/11.9, exceeding the trained OvSGTR by +3.3/+1.9. For downstream tasks, incorporating HSGG-generated scene graphs improves VQA overall accuracy to 79.58% (LLaVA-OV-1.5-8B+HSG) and image captioning unified score to 62.97, both setting new bests among all evaluated models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSGG outperforms all 26 compared baselines across the primary SGG benchmarks. On VG150 zero-shot, it beats the strongest training-free method OwSGG (Dutta et al., 2025) by a large margin (+12.3/+13.1 on zR@50/100) and the best trained method CAPSGG (Huang et al., 2025) by +1.0/+1.8. On PSG zero-shot, HSGG surpasses PGSG (Li et al., 2024b) by +4.6/+5.2 and OwSGG by +6.7/+7.7. In the open-world VG setting, HSGG exceeds the trained OvSGTR (Chen et al., 2024b) by +3.3/+1.9 on R@50/100 while remaining training-free. For downstream VQA on V*Bench, LLaVA-OV-1.5-8B+HSG achieves 79.58% overall accuracy, outperforming the image-only LLaVA-OV-1.5-8B (76.96%) and all other VLMs. On CompreCap captioning, the HSG-only input yields a 62.97 unified score, surpassing GPT-4o (60.05) and LLaVA-Next-34B (58.85) despite using no raw visual features.

## Relation to Prior Work

HSGG extends the training-free VLM-based scene graph generation line (OWSGG, PGSG, CAPSGG) by replacing their flat object-relation paradigm with a two-stage pipeline: bidirectional hierarchical entity perception for part-whole structure, and geometry-guided contrastive relation decoding for hallucination suppression. The contrastive decoding mechanism is directly adapted from VCD (Leng et al., 2024), which uses visual noise to construct the amateur distribution; here, the amateur model instead receives only geometric cues (bounding boxes, relative depths) and linguistic priors, repurposing the expert-amateur contrast from object-level to relation-level hallucination mitigation. The geometry-aware filtering (2D proximity + depth + scale) is a novel pruning strategy not present in prior SGG or contrastive decoding work, addressing the quadratic candidate explosion specific to relational reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HSGG demonstrates that structured hierarchical scene representations can serve as a powerful inductive bias for downstream reasoning: scene-graph-only input (no raw image) outperforms image-only input for captioning, suggesting explicit relational structure externalizes information that VLMs otherwise must infer implicitly. The training-free SOTA on open-world SGG (beating supervised methods like OvSGTR) is practically significant for settings where annotation is infeasible. However, the pipeline's quality is bounded by the underlying VLM and depth estimator, and the ~61s/image inference cost limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether hierarchical reasoning can be distilled into lightweight adapters, and whether the paradigm extends to video scene graphs with spatiotemporal relations.
