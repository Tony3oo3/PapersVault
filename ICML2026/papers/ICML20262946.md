## Problem

Point-supervised infrared small-target detection (ISTD) is limited by two coupled bottlenecks: unstable pseudo-label evolution in cluttered, low-contrast imagery and severe sample-distribution imbalance. Existing single-point methods (LESPS, PAL, MCLC) suffer from excessive label drift, reliance on manual parameter tuning, or false-positive-prone offline clustering, while static loss reweighting and curriculum schedules lack adaptability to evolving training dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 256×256 infrared image crops with a single point annotation per target (no dense pixel-level masks required) and outputs a pixel-level segmentation mask. All image priors (gradient magnitude, local entropy, superpixel regions) are computed solely from the input infrared image, and the learned sample weights α can additionally be used to rank and select the most informative 30% of training data, reducing annotation and training costs by five-fold.

## Method

The core contribution is a bi-level dual-update framework that jointly optimizes detector weights (inner loop), sample-wise loss weights via a lightweight two-layer meta-classifier MLP, and diffusion parameters via a differentiable surrogate (outer loop). Pseudo-labels are generated by modeling each annotated point as a thermal source and solving a learnable anisotropic diffusion equation with an image-adaptive diffusion tensor D_θ that accelerates propagation in homogeneous regions and decelerates near edges; the resulting quasi-steady field is fused with a superpixel prior via a learnable coefficient ρ. A first-order dynamic aggregated solution combines training and validation gradients in the inner loop (with coefficient β=0.8) to avoid expensive unrolling, while the outer loop performs one-step gradient updates on α and θ every 20 epochs, creating a closed-loop between supervision generation and detector learning.

## Results

On the comprehensive SIRST3 benchmark, the proposed method consistently outperforms all single-point supervision baselines across seven detector architectures. For example, with ALCLNet, Ours achieves IoU 73.28% and Fa 16.35×10⁻⁶, surpassing MCLC (63.27%, 47.19), PAL (60.26%, 35.34), and LESPS (30.59%, 34.36), while approaching the full-supervision upper bound (82.12%, 13.36). The method also achieves comparable performance using only 30% of the training data selected via learned sample weights, and generates pseudo-masks 5× faster than MCLC (0.012s vs 0.069s per target).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all three single-point supervision baselines (LESPS, PAL, MCLC) across every detector and dataset. LESPS is the weakest baseline, typically scoring 20–35 IoU points below Ours (e.g., 30.59 vs 73.28 on ALCLNet/SIRST3), due to unstable pseudo-label evolution. PAL improves over LESPS by 20–30 IoU points via curriculum learning but still trails Ours by 5–13 IoU points and often produces higher false alarms. MCLC is the strongest competitor, reaching within 5–10 IoU points of Ours, yet Ours consistently achieves lower Fa (e.g., 16.35 vs 47.19 on ALCLNet/SIRST3), indicating fewer false positives. For pseudo-mask generation, Ours achieves IoU 68.02 on SIRST3, outperforming MCLC (66.98) and dramatically surpassing COM (15.62) and SAM/SAM2 (44.98/47.39), while running 5× faster than MCLC and ~8× faster than SAM. The method also demonstrates data efficiency: training on only the top 30% of samples selected by learned weights surpasses baselines trained on 100% of the data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-point supervised ISTD line initiated by LESPS (online label evolution), MCLC (offline Monte Carlo clustering), and PAL (curriculum-based progressive learning) by replacing their ad-hoc pseudo-label generation with a physics-informed anisotropic heat-diffusion process grounded in thermal radiation principles. It departs from prior work by framing both sample rebalancing and pseudo-label refinement as jointly optimized hyperparameters within a bi-level optimization framework, drawing on recent single-loop bilevel optimization techniques (Liu et al., 2023a, 2024c) rather than treating these as separate heuristic pipelines. The meta-classifier for sample weighting and the differentiable diffusion surrogate for label refinement represent a novel coupling: neither component exists in isolation in prior ISTD literature, and their co-optimization under a shared validation objective is the paper's central architectural departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous treatment of pseudo-label instability and sample imbalance addresses a coupled failure mode that prior methods handle only sequentially or heuristically, and the five-fold annotation speedup plus 30%-data-efficiency result make single-point supervision practically viable for large-scale ISTD deployment. A key open question is whether the heat-diffusion physics analogy generalizes beyond infrared to other modalities where the thermal-source metaphor is less natural (the paper shows preliminary evidence on SAR and sonar but does not fully validate the bi-level framework there). Limitations include the reliance on a held-out validation set (even if pseudo-labeled) for the outer-level objective, the added implementation complexity of the bi-level loop, and the fact that performance still trails full supervision by roughly 8–10 IoU points on SIRST3, suggesting the physics prior captures but does not fully replace dense annotation information.
