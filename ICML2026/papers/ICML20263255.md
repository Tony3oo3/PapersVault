## Problem

Standard MSE-based training of neural emulators on chaotic dynamical systems fails at long horizons because sensitivity to initial conditions causes trajectory-level errors to diverge exponentially. Prior statistics-based regularization approaches either require handcrafted, domain-specific summary statistics or large multi-environment datasets, limiting their applicability to real-world settings where only a single noisy trajectory is available. This paper asks whether informative summary statistics can be learned automatically, jointly with the emulator, from a single noisy trajectory.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single noisy trajectory (state vectors u_t corrupted with additive Gaussian noise at σ·std) from a chaotic system, ranging from 3D (Lorenz-63) to 256-point 1D fields (Kuramoto-Sivashinsky) and 2D vorticity fields (Kolmogorov flow). It produces a one-step emulator g(u_t)→u_{t+1} (a Fourier Neural Operator for 1D systems or a UNet with spectral convolutions for 2D) whose long-term rollouts preserve the invariant measure of the true attractor, along with a jointly learned summary map f(u) that projects states into a discriminative feature space. The summary map outputs a point cloud in R^d per spatial location, matching the structure of fixed handcrafted statistics (d=3 by default) so that OT is computed over the same geometric object regardless of whether summaries are fixed or learned.

## Method

The core contribution is an adversarial min-max objective where the emulator g minimizes one-step MSE plus an optimal transport cost between the pushforward measures f_#μ (data) and f_#μ̂ (emulator), while the summary map f maximizes that same OT cost to discover the most discriminative statistics. Two tractable relaxations are provided: a WGAN-style dual formulation using the Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality for the 1-Wasserstein distance with a Lipschitz-constrained critic, and a Sinkhorn divergence with entropic regularization valid for any p≥1. Theoretically, the OT regularizer is bounded by the one-step prediction error (Proposition 5.1), and a key noise-forgetting result (Theorem 5.14) shows that while MSE blows up exponentially with rollout horizon due to initial-condition noise, the Wasserstein distance converges to a finite value determined only by measurement noise and model error, making it the appropriate long-horizon signal. Training uses a warm-start strategy (MSE pretraining before activating the OT term) with teacher forcing over sliding windows, and the summary map is an MLP or spatially-aware convolutional network whose Lipschitz regularity is implicitly enforced by the joint objective.

## Results

Across four chaotic systems (Lorenz-96, Kuramoto-Sivashinsky, Kolmogorov Flow, Lorenz-63), the WGAN-style adversarial OT regularizer achieves the best L¹ histogram error on L96 multi-trajectory (0.149 noisy, 0.083 clean) and Kolmogorov Flow (0.273 noisy, 0.190 clean), and the best spectral distance on L96 multi-trajectory (0.049 noisy, 0.074 clean) and KS (0.148 noisy, 0.183 clean). The Sinkhorn variant achieves the best clean spectral distance on L96 single-trajectory (0.123) and Kolmogorov Flow (0.129). All OT-regularized methods substantially outperform the MSE-only baseline (No OT) on long-term statistical metrics, with L¹ histogram error reduced by 40–60% relative to No OT across systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Fixed OT from Jiang et al. (2023), which uses handcrafted physics-inspired summary statistics (e.g., ∂ₜuᵢ, advection term, uᵢ) with a non-adversarial Sinkhorn divergence. The learnable WGAN variant outperforms Fixed OT on L¹ histogram error for L96 multi-trajectory (0.149 vs. 0.175 noisy) and Kolmogorov Flow (0.273 vs. 0.614 noisy), and on spectral distance across all four systems (e.g., 0.049 vs. 0.141 on L96 multi-traj, 0.148 vs. 0.349 on KS). The Sinkhorn variant similarly beats Fixed OT on spectral distance for KS (0.219 vs. 0.349) and Kolmogorov Flow (0.129 vs. 0.193 clean). The clearest advantage of learnable summaries appears in spectral distance—a metric not explicitly enforced by the Fixed OT objective—where both learnable methods consistently outperform Fixed OT, demonstrating broader statistical coverage. On L96 multi-trajectory clean L¹ histogram error, Fixed OT (0.055) still edges out WGAN (0.083), reflecting its explicit optimization of those specific handcrafted statistics. Both learnable methods also reproduce the leading Lyapunov exponent far more faithfully (WGAN: 2.336 vs. ground truth 2.334) than Fixed OT (1.890) or No OT (1.615).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial optimal transport framework of Genevay et al. (2018) — where a learnable ground cost is optimized to maximize distributional discrepancy — into the dynamical systems setting, replacing the learned cost with a learned summary map whose pushforward distributions are compared via OT. It directly builds on Jiang et al. (2023), who first proposed OT-based regularization for neural operator emulators of chaotic systems, but departs from their reliance on handcrafted physics-inspired summary statistics or multi-environment contrastive datasets by adversarially learning the summary representation jointly with the emulator from a single noisy trajectory. The WGAN-style variant draws on the Kantorovich-Rubinstein dual formulation of Arjovsky et al. (2017), adapted here to expectations under the invariant measure rather than i.i.d. samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, domain-agnostic recipe for training chaotic-system emulators that requires neither expert-chosen summary statistics nor diverse multi-environment data, addressing two practical bottlenecks in prior statistics-based approaches. The theoretical separation between exponential MSE blow-up (Corollary 5.9) and Wasserstein noise forgetting (Theorem 5.14) gives a clean justification for why distributional objectives are the right tool for long-horizon chaotic emulation, and the near-exact LLE recovery (2.336 vs. 2.334 ground truth) demonstrates that the learned emulator captures the correct chaotic regime, not just marginal statistics. Open questions include extension to non-ergodic or transient dynamics, stochastic systems where the interplay between noise and chaos is not yet addressed, and whether the adversarial training stability issues (e.g., the need for early stopping in the Sinkhorn variant) persist at larger scales.
