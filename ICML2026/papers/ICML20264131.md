## Problem

Deep Spiking Neural Networks have relied on Batch Normalization to stabilize training, but BN is unsuitable for variable-length NLP tasks and memory-constrained scenarios where batch size must be reduced. Removing BN from deep SNNs causes immediate training failure, not because normalization is fundamentally required, but because existing initialization methods (Xavier, Kaiming, or prior SNN-specific approaches) fail to account for the temporal dynamics and binary activation unique to spiking neurons, leading to spike vanishing in deep layers.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 224×224 ImageNet), neuromorphic event streams (48×48 DVS-CIFAR10 and DVSGesture, compressed into 10 temporal slices), and text sequences (IWSLT17 German-English), all converted to spike trains via a direct coding first layer. Outputs are class labels for image and event classification, or translated English text for machine translation. Critically, the entire forward pass operates on binary spikes with LIF neurons and requires no floating-point multiplication or division at inference, preserving the energy-efficient spike-driven computation that normalization layers would otherwise disrupt.

## Method

SpikeInit is a two-part initialization framework. First, it models the membrane potential dynamics of LIF neurons as a threshold auto-regressive process, approximates the stationary distribution as Gaussian, and derives coupled nonlinear equations (Theorem 4.1) linking input firing rate to output firing rate; a binary search then finds the weight scale σ* that maintains a target firing rate p_init=0.1 across all layers. Second, it formulates the backpropagated gradient variance (Theorem 5.1) and solves for the optimal shape parameter α* of the exponential surrogate gradient that keeps gradient magnitudes stable across layers. An adaptive mechanism further scales α during training as α = α*·σ_v/σ_v,i to track the evolving membrane potential distribution, mimicking the stabilizing effect of normalization without any normalization layer. This enables stable training of a 1000-layer SNN without normalization, outperforming BN-based counterparts on CIFAR-10, ImageNet, and IWSLT17.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, SpikeInit (normalization-free) achieves 95.29% (VGG-11), 95.77% (VGG-16), 95.53% (ResNet-18), and 96.20% (ResNet-34) top-1 accuracy, outperforming all normalization-based baselines. On ImageNet with ResNet-34, SpikeInit reaches 68.81% at T=4 and 69.76% at T=6, surpassing TEBN (68.28%) and TAB (67.78%). On IWSLT17 German-English machine translation, SpikeInit achieves 29.98 BLEU without any normalization, beating both LayerNorm (29.50) and tdBN (28.14). The method also scales to a 1000-layer SNN with continuously improving accuracy, whereas the tdBN baseline degrades beyond 500 layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpikeInit outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and architecture tested. Against prior SNN initialization methods (fluctuation-driven, Ding et al., Micheli et al.), SpikeInit gains 4.4–6.4% on ResNet-34 and is the only method that converges on VGG architectures, where all three baselines fail. Against normalization-based SNN methods, SpikeInit leads by 0.30–2.91% on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 95.77% vs. TEBN's 94.58% on VGG-16; 96.20% vs. tdBN's 95.86% on ResNet-34) and by 0.53–1.98% on ImageNet (68.81% vs. TEBN's 68.28% at T=4). On neuromorphic datasets (DVS-CIFAR10, DVSGesture), SpikeInit achieves 84.93% and 97.34% respectively, beating the best normalized baseline (TEBN) by 0.06% and 0.58%. On IWSLT17 machine translation, SpikeInit (29.98 BLEU) surpasses both LayerNorm (29.50) and tdBN (28.14), demonstrating that normalization-free training extends to variable-length sequence tasks where BN is inherently unsuitable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ANN initialization lineage (Xavier, Kaiming, Fixup) to SNNs by modeling the full temporal dynamics of leaky integrate-and-fire neurons through a threshold auto-regressive process, rather than relying on the simplified approximations (mean input current, first time step only) used by prior SNN initialization methods (Rossbroich et al., Ding et al., Micheli et al.). It also introduces a novel component absent from prior work: initialization of the surrogate gradient shape parameter to stabilize backpropagated gradient magnitudes. Conceptually, the paper reframes the role of Batch Normalization in deep SNNs from a necessary architectural component to a workaround for inadequate initialization, drawing an explicit parallel to how Fixup initialization eliminated the need for BN in deep ANNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the normalization dependency unlocks SNN architectures for variable-length tasks (NLP), low-batch-size scenarios, and energy-constrained neuromorphic hardware where floating-point normalization operations are prohibitive. The 1000-layer SNN result demonstrates that the scalability previously attributed to BN can be achieved through initialization alone. Open questions include whether the normal-distribution approximation of membrane potential holds for all practical neuron parameters (the paper acknowledges degradation as κ→1, requiring Monte Carlo fallback), whether the framework extends to other surrogate gradient families or more complex neuron models, and whether the modest performance gains over normalized counterparts (0.3–0.5%) persist at even larger scales or with different training regimes.
