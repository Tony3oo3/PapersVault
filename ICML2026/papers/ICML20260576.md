## Problem

Existing multi-objective unlearning methods optimize weighted combinations of forget and retain losses but provide no guarantee that forget quality improves by a user-specified extent, nor do they account for the inherent conflict between the two objectives when forget and retain data are similar. The paper asks: how hard is it to reconcile both objectives, what is the minimum cost of forgetting (retain utility degradation) to achieve a given forget quality improvement, and when is collateral forgetting unavoidable?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260576/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each iteration, HAMU consumes batch gradient estimates of the cross-entropy loss on the forget and retain data (computed on the current model weights) to produce a single weight update vector. The hardness measure κ is the dot product of these two averaged gradients, and two derived thresholds (κ₁ for update regime selection, κ₂ for stopping) are computed per iteration. The algorithm outputs updated model weights and a binary stopping signal; in the layer-wise parallel implementation, the global forget-quality constraint Q is decomposed into per-layer constraints Q_i proportional to the product of layer-specific gradient norms, enabling independent per-layer updates across multiple GPUs.

## Method

HAMU formulates each unlearning iteration as a convex constrained optimization problem: minimize the first-order retain utility degradation g_r·Δw subject to a forget quality constraint g_f·Δw ≥ Q and a bounded update norm ‖Δw‖ ≤ R. Solving via KKT conditions yields a closed-form update that switches between two regimes based on the hardness κ = g_r·g_f: a direct update (−R/‖g_r‖·g_r, equivalent to SGD on retain data) when κ ≤ κ₁, and a rectified update that mixes the forget gradient direction with the component of g_r orthogonal to g_f when κ > κ₁. The hardness measure monotonically determines the minimum cost of forgetting (Corollary 4.2), and a second threshold κ₂ identifies when no feasible update can improve forget quality without degrading retain utility, triggering the stopping criterion. The first-order approximation is valid for non-convex models with small learning rates (error ratio bounded by ηλ_max/2 < 1%), and the layer-wise independence of the update rule makes the algorithm trivially parallelizable across GPUs for large models like Llama-2-7B.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-20), HAMU-Q and HAMU-U are the only methods that simultaneously achieve positive forget quality improvement (ΔL_f) and retain utility improvement (−ΔL_r) in the hard unlearning scenario (similarity mixing ratio ρ=0.75), where all other baselines fail on at least one objective. In the easy scenario (ρ=0), HAMU variants and GDiff both achieve large positive values on both axes, while GA/KL sacrifice retain utility and FT/SCRUB sacrifice forget quality. On the LLM QA task (Llama-2-7b, WaterDrum-TOFU), only HAMU-Q and GDiff improve both objectives simultaneously, with HAMU-Q achieving greater gains in both ΔL_f and −ΔL_r than GDiff. The hardness measure κ shows a Pearson correlation of 0.994 (HAMU-Q) and 0.986 (HAMU-U) with the similarity mixing ratio ρ, validating its role as a predictive difficulty indicator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAMU outperforms all seven baselines in the hard unlearning regime (ρ=0.75 on CIFAR-10 and semantically similar setting on WaterDrum-TOFU), where it is the only method achieving non-negligible positive values on both ΔL_f and −ΔL_r. Fine-tuning (FT, Neel et al.) and SCRUB (Kurmanji et al.) maximize retain utility but produce negative ΔL_f, meaning forget quality actually worsens. Gradient ascent (GA, Jang et al.) and KL minimization (Maini et al.) maximize forget quality but induce collateral forgetting (negative −ΔL_r). GDiff (Liu et al.) is the closest competitor, achieving positive values on both axes in easy scenarios and remaining competitive in the LLM setting, but HAMU-Q achieves greater improvements on both metrics. GRU (Wang et al.) and PCGrad (Yu et al.), adapted from multi-task learning, are included in supplementary results (App. H.2) but do not match HAMU's performance in hard settings. The key differentiator is that HAMU's hardness-aware update (switching between direct and rectified updates based on κ) and stopping criterion (terminating when κ > κ₂) prevent the collateral forgetting that plagues gradient-based baselines when forget and retain data are similar.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical ε-constraint method (Haimes et al., 1971) but departs from it by deriving a per-iteration, first-order update rule rather than solving a global constrained optimization problem, making it applicable to non-convex models. It positions itself against existing multi-objective unlearning methods that use weighted loss combinations (SCRUB, Duan et al.) or gradient surgery (PCGrad, GRU), arguing that these lack guarantees on achieving specified improvements in either objective. The key conceptual departure is introducing the gradient dot product κ as a hardness measure that both predicts the minimum cost of forgetting and determines which update strategy (direct vs. rectified) to apply, along with an exact threshold for when collateral forgetting becomes unavoidable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal theoretical connection between a measurable, model-dependent quantity (gradient alignment) and unlearning performance, giving practitioners a principled stopping criterion to avoid collateral forgetting before it occurs. The hardness measure also explains why unlearning becomes progressively harder over iterations, offering diagnostic value for model owners. Limitations include reliance on first-order approximation (requiring small learning rates), user-specified constraint parameters Q/U that trade off objectives, and the fact that the method is inherently local per-iteration. Open questions include whether the hardness framework extends to second-order or higher-order settings for large models, and how the layer-wise constraint distribution interacts with modern parameter-efficient fine-tuning approaches like LoRA.
