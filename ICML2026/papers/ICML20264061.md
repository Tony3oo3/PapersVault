## Problem

Deep SNN-Transformers are designed for neuromorphic hardware where inactive '0' spikes consume no energy, but on commodity edge devices like NVIDIA Jetsons these zeros still participate in dense matrix multiplications, wasting significant computation. Existing SNN compression methods target neuromorphic platforms and leave SNN-Transformers largely unexplored for practical deployment on resource-constrained commodity hardware, creating a gap between theoretical spike sparsity and real energy savings.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100) or event-based video frames (DVS-CIFAR10, DVS-128 Gesture) and outputs a class label. Inputs are processed through LIF spiking neurons over multiple timesteps (T=4 for static datasets, T=16 for neuromorphic datasets), and Spik4lite is applied as a plug-and-play module to existing SNN-Transformer backbones (Spikformer, SD-Transformer, Spikingformer) without modifying their core attention or feed-forward architecture.

## Method

Spik4lite converts spike sparsity into physical channel sparsity through a three-stage training procedure. First, an energy-aware gating mechanism uses Hard Gumbel-Softmax sampling to learn binary keep/drop masks per channel, driven by a decoupled loss that combines task accuracy with a layer-wise normalized energy penalty computed from EMA-smoothed firing rates and dynamic SOPs (FLOPs × T × firing rate). Second, channels with consistently low retention probability are physically removed from weight tensors in cyclic intervals, with global dimension alignment enforced across residual-connected transformer blocks and MHSA head divisibility constraints. Third, the gating modules are permanently stripped and the compact model is fine-tuned with task loss only, yielding a static SNN that genuinely reduces computation on commodity hardware rather than merely encoding sparsity that dense hardware still processes.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, Spik4lite-enhanced Spikingformer-4-384 retains only 30.7% of the original parameters (2.86M vs. 9.32M) while achieving a 1.58× power efficiency gain (3.42 J vs. 5.42 J) with a negligible 0.64% accuracy drop (94.43% vs. 95.07%). On DVS-128 Gesture, the enhanced SD-Transformer reduces energy consumption by approximately 3.2× (9.24 J → 2.85 J) and slashes inference latency from 1.65 s to 0.46 s while maintaining 94.1% top-1 accuracy. Across all four datasets, SOPs are reduced by 36–93% and peak memory drops by up to 38% (e.g., 2614 MiB → 1613 MiB for Spikformer on CIFAR-10), demonstrating consistent efficiency gains on the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano 8 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spik4lite is evaluated as a plug-and-play compression module on three SNN-Transformer baselines: Spikformer (Zhou et al., ICLR 2023), Spike-Driven Transformer (Yao et al., NeurIPS 2023), and Spikingformer (Zhou et al., AAAI 2026). Against Spikformer, Spik4lite reduces SOPs by 61% on CIFAR-10 (822.64M → 321.49M) and 64% on CIFAR-100 (1038.24M → 366.80M) at a cost of 1.1–3.8% accuracy. Against Spike-Driven Transformer, the most dramatic gains appear on DVS-128 Gesture, where SOPs drop by 93% (2551.85M → 170.21M) and energy falls from 9.24 J to 2.85 J (3.2× reduction), with a 3.5% accuracy drop. Against Spikingformer, the method achieves the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off on CIFAR-100 (94.43% at 280.40M SOPs vs. 95.07% at 442.85M SOPs, a 37% SOP reduction for only 0.64% accuracy loss) and on DVS-CIFAR10 (energy drops from 9.74 J to 3.96 J, a 2.5× reduction). In all cases, Spik4lite does not surpass the baselines in raw accuracy but achieves substantially lower computational and energy costs while preserving competitive accuracy, positioning it as a compression layer rather than an accuracy-improving method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SNN compression literature (quantization, pruning, NAS) by reframing the optimization target from neuromorphic-hardware SOPs to commodity-hardware channel utilization. It builds on Gumbel-Softmax (Jang et al., 2017) for differentiable discrete selection and applies it to a novel energy-aware gating mechanism that uses EMA-smoothed firing rates and SOPs as the pruning signal. The key departure from prior SNN pruning work (e.g., SCA, Activity Pruning, PQ Index) is the explicit physical removal of channels to create a structurally compact model, rather than merely reducing theoretical synaptic operations that remain unexploited on dense commodity hardware.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical pathway for deploying deep SNN-Transformers on widely available edge devices (NVIDIA Jetson) without requiring specialized neuromorphic chips, which significantly broadens the deployment boundary of SNNs. The Appendix A finding that identical global sparsity with different layer-wise distributions yields starkly different accuracy-energy trade-offs is a useful diagnostic that motivates learned (rather than rule-based) pruning. However, all experiments are limited to CIFAR-10/100 and small neuromorphic datasets (DVS-CIFAR10, DVS-128 Gesture); scalability to ImageNet-scale tasks, larger transformer backbones, and non-vision modalities remains untested, and the method requires full retraining rather than post-hoc compression.
