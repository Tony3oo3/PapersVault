## Problem

Existing pruning-based mitigation methods for memorization in text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., NeMo, Wanda) assume that memorized data is encoded in a small, localizable subset of weights. This paper challenges that locality assumption, demonstrating that pruning merely conceals memorized images rather than removing them, and that memorization is distributed across the text embedding space, model activations, and trained weights.

## Input / Output

The method takes a known memorized training image, its associated text prompt, and the diffusion model weights (either original or post-pruning) as input. DoRI produces continuous adversarial text embeddings (59,136-dim vectors for SD-v1.4) that trigger verbatim replication of the memorized image; these are not natural-language prompts but optimized points in the text encoder's embedding space. The adversarial fine-tuning phase additionally consumes non-memorized image-caption pairs from LAION and surrogate images (semantically similar but non-identical replicas generated from the pruned model) to produce a fully fine-tuned diffusion model with the memorized content removed.

## Method

DoRI performs unconstrained gradient-based optimization in the text embedding space, starting from the memorized prompt's embedding (or a random vector) and minimizing the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss against the memorized image, with noise and timesteps resampled at each of 50 steps to ensure robustness across the denoising trajectory. The 50-step cap is critical: it is sufficient to recover memorized content (which converges in ~10 steps) but prevents false positives on non-memorized images (which require >500 steps). For mitigation, the paper pairs DoRI with full adversarial fine-tuning: multiple adversarial embeddings per memorized image are generated iteratively, surrogate images (semantically close but non-identical) are crafted from the pruned model, and the entire U-Net is fine-tuned with a combined loss that pushes generation toward surrogates under adversarial embeddings while preserving general utility on non-memorized data. This global, multi-trigger approach is necessary because the paper shows that different adversarial embeddings triggering the same image produce divergent activations and that different pruning methods identify inconsistent weight subsets, making any local intervention insufficient.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion v1.4 with 500 memorized prompts from LAION-5B, pruning-based mitigations (NeMo, Wanda) reduce SSCD_Orig from 0.90 to 0.33/0.20 and MR from 0.98 to 0.20/0.00 under original prompts, but DoRI adversarial embeddings recover memorization to SSCD_Orig 0.91 (MR 0.99) for NeMo and 0.76 (MR 0.72) for Wanda. The proposed adversarial fine-tuning achieves MR of 0.02 under DoRI (SSCD_Orig 0.36), compared to 0.98 for ESD, 0.97 for Concept Ablation, and 0.39 for SISS, while improving FID from 14.44 to 13.61. Results generalize to Stable Diffusion v2.0, where the method achieves MR 0.06 vs. 1.00 (NeMo) and 0.53 (Wanda) under DoRI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five mitigation baselines against DoRI adversarial embeddings. NeMo (Hintersdorf et al., 2024) and Wanda (Chavhan et al., 2024) are the primary pruning-based baselines; both appear effective under original prompts (MR drops to 0.20 and 0.00 respectively) but are fully broken by DoRI, recovering MR to 0.99 and 0.72. Among concept unlearning methods, ESD (Gandikota et al., 2023) and Concept Ablation (Kumari et al., 2023) reduce SSCD_Orig to 0.24 and 0.39 without DoRI, but both recover to near-original memorization levels (MR 0.98 and 0.97) under adversarial embeddings, and Concept Ablation additionally degrades FID to 16.03. SISS (Alberti et al., 2025), the state-of-the-art data unlearning method, achieves the strongest baseline performance under DoRI (MR 0.39, SSCD_Orig 0.60) but still fails to remove 39% of memorized samples. The proposed adversarial fine-tuning outperforms all baselines by a wide margin, achieving MR 0.02 and SSCD_Orig 0.36 under DoRI while preserving or improving image quality (FID 13.61 vs. 14.44 baseline).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the pruning-based memorization mitigation line of work—NeMo (Hintersdorf et al., 2024) and Wanda (Chavhan et al., 2024)—which assume that memorized images are encoded in a small, identifiable subset of weights that can be pruned. It departs from this lineage by demonstrating that the underlying locality assumption is empirically false: replication triggers are distributed throughout text embedding space, produce divergent activations, and correspond to inconsistent weight sets across methods. For its proposed mitigation, the paper repurposes adversarial training (Madry et al., 2018) in the context of diffusion model fine-tuning, and explicitly contrasts its approach with concept unlearning (ESD, Concept Ablation) and data unlearning (SISS), arguing that none of these are suitable for removing individual memorized data points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art pruning methods merely conceal rather than remove memorized images has immediate practical consequences for open-weight model deployment, GDPR 'right to be forgotten' compliance, and copyright protection—any model shipped with NeMo or Wanda pruning still contains extractable training data. The adversarial fine-tuning approach offers a concrete path toward permanent mitigation, though it is computationally more expensive than pruning and has been validated primarily on Stable Diffusion v1.4 (with v2.0 as secondary evidence). Open questions include whether the locality failure generalizes to newer, more carefully curated models, whether more efficient non-local mitigation strategies exist, and how to certify that memorization has been truly removed rather than merely made harder to trigger.
