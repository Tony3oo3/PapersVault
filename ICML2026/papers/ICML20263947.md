## Problem

Existing BatchNorm-based test-time and meta-learning adaptation methods (AdaBN, ARM-BN) degrade sharply under two realistic biomedical imaging conditions: small target batch sizes and label shift, because their adaptation statistics become noisy and confounded by class-mixture changes. Although negative control samples are guaranteed in every experimental batch of high-content screening data, prior meta-learning adaptation frameworks do not exploit them, leaving a gap between in-domain and unseen-batch performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263947/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 5-channel fluorescence microscopy images (downsized to 512×512, center-cropped to 256×256 at inference) together with a set of negative control images acquired under the same technical conditions in the same experimental batch. At test time, adaptation is fully unlabeled: a single forward pass over the concatenated control and perturbed samples yields updated BN statistics, after which the model predicts one of 8 mechanism-of-action class labels per image. No gradient updates or retraining are required at inference.

## Method

CS-ARM-BN extends the Adaptive Risk Minimization (ARM) meta-learning framework by incorporating negative control samples into the BatchNorm adaptation context both during episodic training and at inference. During training, for each source batch the model meta-learns to adapt its BN statistics using a context of control plus perturbed samples, then is evaluated on perturbed query samples from the same batch, teaching the network to expect control-conditioned normalization. At test time, the adapted BN mean and variance for every layer are computed over the union of C control and L perturbed samples from the new batch (M = C + L total), replacing the running training statistics with a single forward pass. A bias-variance analysis shows this combined estimator achieves lower MSE than perturbed-only (ARM-BN/AdaBN) or controls-only (BEN) estimators: controls eliminate the label-shift bias while the larger total sample size reduces variance by a factor of 1/M. The backbone is a ResNet50 with a linear classifier, and the approach generalizes to Vision Transformer architectures (LeViT) that use BN layers.

## Results

On the JUMP-CP dataset for 8-class MoA classification, CS-ARM-BN achieves 0.930±0.019 accuracy on unseen experimental batches (mild domain shift), nearly matching the in-domain ResNet50-BN baseline of 0.939±0.005 and closing the generalization gap from 0.862±0.060 (no adaptation). Under severe label shift (α=0.01), CS-ARM-BN maintains 0.894±0.022 accuracy while all other BN-adaptation methods collapse (ARM-BN: 0.228, AdaBN: 0.229, TENT: 0.156). In the most challenging cross-source + label shift setting (S3→S8, α=0.01), CS-ARM-BN reaches 0.825±0.007 versus 0.173±0.004 for ARM-BN and 0.171±0.009 for AdaBN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CS-ARM-BN outperforms all non-adaptive baselines (ResNet50-BN: 0.862, IN: 0.891, CA-MAE: 0.709, CellProfiler: 0.860) and classical UDA methods (DANN: 0.911, CORAL: 0.893) on unseen batches, and also surpasses test-time adaptation methods TENT (0.929) and AdaBN (0.928) in the mild-shift setting. Against the closest competitor ARM-BN (0.935), CS-ARM-BN is marginally lower (0.930) under balanced labels but dramatically superior under label shift: at α=0.01, CS-ARM-BN retains 0.894 accuracy while ARM-BN drops to 0.228 and AdaBN to 0.229. In the cross-source + label shift regime (Table 4), CS-ARM-BN leads all methods by a wide margin (e.g., S3→S8 at α=0.01: 0.825 vs. 0.173 for ARM-BN), while the controls-only variant ARM-BEN (0.663) confirms that combining controls with perturbed samples is essential. The generative baseline Style-ID (0.781) and the meta-learned ARM-CML (0.843) are substantially outperformed, confirming that BN-statistics adaptation with controls is both more effective and more computationally efficient than generative translation or full parameter adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ARM-BN (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2021), a meta-learning framework that adapts BatchNorm statistics at test time via in-context learning, by incorporating negative control samples into the adaptation context. It departs from prior BN-based adaptation methods (AdaBN, TENT) that estimate target statistics solely from perturbed samples, and from post-hoc batch correction approaches (TVN, BEN) that do not integrate controls into end-to-end training. The key conceptual departure is a bias-variance analysis showing that mixing controls with perturbed samples yields a strictly better estimator of the domain-specific technical offset than either perturbed-only or controls-only estimators, and that meta-learning this mixed-context adaptation during training is essential for the gains to materialize.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Batch effects remain a primary barrier to deploying deep learning models in real-world biomedical imaging pipelines, and this paper shows that the failure is not gradual but catastrophic under realistic conditions (label shift, small batches), with standard TTA methods collapsing to near-random accuracy. The proposed solution is lightweight (single forward pass, no retraining) and leverages experimental design elements (negative controls) that are already present in every screening batch, making it immediately deployable. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (JUMP-CP) and a single task (MoA classification), and the method assumes controls come from the same cell line as perturbed samples. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other biomedical imaging tasks, to settings where controls are imperfect, and to streaming/online adaptation scenarios.
