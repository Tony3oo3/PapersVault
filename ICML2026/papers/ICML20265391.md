## Problem

Contrastive learning for time series requires augmentations that preserve temporal semantics while ensuring view diversity, but existing approaches either rely on hand-crafted heuristics that risk destroying critical patterns or infer semantic regions implicitly via learned masks, limiting interpretability and controllability. No prior method ties augmentation to explicit, temporally aligned semantic anchors that can be iteratively refined.

## Input / Output

ProSAR consumes raw multivariate time series (variable-length sequences) and produces frozen encoder representations (CoST-style dilated CNN for forecasting, TS2Vec encoder for classification) that are mapped to downstream outputs via a linear predictor or RBF-kernel SVM. The method also maintains two sets of learnable prototypes—time-domain prototype sequences and latent prototype vectors—that are updated throughout training and directly drive the augmentation policy, making them functional outputs of the self-supervised process rather than fixed hyperparameters.

## Method

ProSAR formalizes semantic augmentation through a Prototype-Conditioned Information Bottleneck objective, proving that the optimal augmentation should maximize mutual information with the prototype assignment while minimizing residual input information given that assignment. Concretely, learnable time-domain prototypes are aligned to each input via soft-DTW to produce a binary semantic mask; the masked semantic segment undergoes STFT-based phase compensation and controlled frequency-amplitude noise, while the non-semantic segment receives strong perturbation and random sub-segment masking. A dual-prototype refinement loop closes the cycle: learned representations are clustered with FINCH to update latent prototypes, which are decoded by a frozen MLP back to the time domain and fused (via EMA) with input-space anchoring centroids to refresh the time-domain prototypes for the next augmentation round. Training is driven by a two-component contrastive loss combining intra-instance temporal InfoNCE and inter-instance semantic InfoNCE (both instance-to-instance and instance-to-prototype terms), with a convergence guarantee for the alternating prototype-augmentation updates.

## Results

On univariate forecasting (5 datasets), ProSAR achieves an average MSE of 0.151 and MAE of 0.250, outperforming the strongest baseline AutoTCL (0.161/0.258) by 6.2% MSE and 3.5% MAE. On multivariate forecasting, ProSAR reaches 0.561 MSE / 0.507 MAE versus AutoTCL's 0.571/0.515. On the 30-dataset UEA classification benchmark, ProSAR attains the highest average accuracy of 0.764 and best average rank of 1.867, surpassing FreRA (0.754, rank 2.900) and TimesURL (0.752, rank 2.233). In the 1% semi-supervised setting, ProSAR achieves the best average ACC/MF1 of 0.853/0.833 across 8 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProSAR consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the closest competitor AutoTCL (ICLR 2024), ProSAR reduces univariate forecasting MSE from 0.161 to 0.151 (6.2% improvement) and multivariate MSE from 0.571 to 0.561, while also winning on classification (0.764 vs 0.742 ACC), transfer learning (0.615 vs 0.596 avg accuracy on SHAR), and semi-supervised classification (0.853 vs 0.836 avg ACC). FreRA (KDD 2025) is the second-strongest baseline, but ProSAR surpasses it on all tasks: univariate MSE 0.151 vs 0.166, UEA classification ACC 0.764 vs 0.754, and semi-supervised ACC 0.853 vs 0.836. TimesURL (AAAI 2024) and PPT (ICLR 2025) trail further behind, with TimesURL at 0.169 MSE and PPT at 0.175 MSE on univariate forecasting. The older baselines TS2Vec (AAAI 2022), TNC (ICLR 2021), and TS-TCC (IJCAI 2021) show substantially larger gaps (e.g., TS-TCC averages 0.287 MSE on univariate forecasting vs ProSAR's 0.151). CoST (ICLR 2022) is competitive on multivariate forecasting (0.587 MSE vs ProSAR's 0.561) but is not included in the classification or transfer-learning comparisons. SoftCLT (ICLR 2024) is only compared on transfer and semi-supervised tasks, where ProSAR also wins (e.g., 0.615 vs 0.581 avg transfer accuracy).

## Relation to Prior Work

ProSAR extends the information bottleneck framework for contrastive view generation (Tishby et al., 2000; Tian et al., 2020) by introducing learnable prototypes as explicit semantic anchors that directly guide augmentation, rather than relying on implicit learned masks (AutoTCL, FreRA) or meta-learned transform selection (InfoTS). It departs from prior prototype-based contrastive methods (PCL, SwAV, MHCCL, AimTS) that use prototypes only at the loss/objective level, instead elevating them to drive the upstream view-generation process itself. The dual-prototype refinement loop—where latent-space clustering feeds back through a decoder to update time-domain prototypes—represents a new co-design mechanism not present in any single prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in time series contrastive learning: the lack of interpretable, controllable semantic augmentation. The prototype-guided approach offers a more transparent alternative to black-box mask learning, and the closed feedback loop between augmentation and prototype refinement is a principled design that could inform future self-supervised methods beyond time series. However, the gains over strong baselines (AutoTCL, FreRA) are modest (1.8–6.2% MSE reduction, ~1% ACC), and the authors acknowledge that ensuring consistent human-interpretable semantics across complex domains remains non-trivial. Open questions include whether the DTW-based alignment generalizes to very long or highly non-stationary series, and how the framework integrates with emerging time series foundation models.
