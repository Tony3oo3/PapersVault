## Problem

In single-cell lineage tracing (scLT) data, the subtle gene expression signals that encode fate commitment are overwhelmed by dominant sources of variation such as cell type and time point, making them statistically invisible to standard unsupervised methods like scVI or trajectory inference. Existing lineage-aware methods (CoSpar, GEMLI) rely on sparse optimization or marker-gene heuristics that fail to recover these faint signals, and the vast majority of available scRNA-seq datasets lack explicit lineage barcodes entirely, leaving no principled way to transfer lineage structure to unlabeled studies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes log-normalized gene expression vectors (top 2000 highly variable genes) from scRNA-seq, along with discrete lineage barcode labels that define positive/negative pair structure rather than serving as classification targets. Optionally, unlabeled gene expression vectors from a related dataset are incorporated through a semi-supervised penalty. The primary output is a pair of cell embeddings (a 64-dimensional base-encoder representation and a 32-dimensional projection-head representation) that can be fed into downstream tasks: KNN lineage prediction, a linear decoder for future cell-type composition (evaluated via KL divergence), and integrated-gradients gene importance scoring.

## Method

LCL treats inheritable lineage barcodes as a 'natural data augmentation' for contrastive learning: two cells sharing the same barcode form a positive pair (analogous to two augmented views of the same image in SimCLR), while cells from different lineages are negatives. A cell-pair generator constructs positive pairs per lineage (scaled by a size factor α) and assembles mini-batches of N pairs from N distinct lineages, with all cross-lineage cells in a batch serving as negatives. The architecture is a 3-layer MLP base encoder (p→64, ReLU + batch norm) followed by a 2-layer projection head (64→32), trained with the normalized temperature-scaled cross-entropy (NT-Xent) loss. A key architectural addition is a semi-supervised entropy penalty: unlabeled cells are softly assigned to labeled-cell neighborhoods via temperature-scaled cosine similarity, and an entropy-minimization term encourages their embeddings to align with confident regions of the lineage-informed manifold, enabling transfer to datasets without barcodes. The full objective jointly minimizes the contrastive loss and the entropy penalty weighted by λ, and the resulting embeddings are interpreted post hoc via integrated gradients (for gene-level lineage informativeness) and a linear softmax decoder (for lineage-level future composition prediction).

## Results

On the LARRY dataset (largest 200 lineages), LCL's base-encoder embedding achieves a KNN test error of 0.858 and KL divergence of 0.335 for future cell-type composition prediction, outperforming scVI (0.892 / 0.549) and Supervised UMAP (0.941 / 0.903). On the CellTag dataset, LCL(base) reaches a KNN test error of 0.434 and KL divergence of 0.292, again beating scVI (0.529 / 0.627) and Supervised UMAP (0.628 / 0.595). In the label-transfer experiment (CellTag-multi → CellTag), LCL achieves an AUPRC of 0.600 for GEMLI lineage-pair classification, nearly tripling scVI's 0.177 and log-normalized expression's 0.221. LCL also uniquely identifies four biologically validated pathways (adjusted p ≤ 0.04) that CoSpar and VariancePartition fail to reach significance on.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LCL consistently outperforms scVI on both KNN lineage-prediction error and KL-divergence composition prediction across both the LARRY and CellTag datasets, with the largest gap on CellTag where LCL(base) reduces KL divergence from 0.627 to 0.292 (a 53% relative improvement). Supervised UMAP is the weakest baseline on both datasets, performing worse than even scVI on KNN error for LARRY (0.941 vs. 0.892), suggesting that its nonparametric supervised embedding does not capture lineage structure as effectively as the contrastive objective. In the label-transfer setting, LCL nearly triples the AUPRC of scVI (0.600 vs. 0.177) and log-normalized raw expression (0.221) for GEMLI lineage-pair classification, demonstrating that the learned embedding encodes lineage information far more faithfully than unsupervised or raw representations. For pathway identification via integrated gradients, LCL achieves significance (adjusted p = 0.03–0.04) on four biologically validated pathways (Wnt4/Sfrp1 axis, TGF-β signaling, endothelial migration, ECM assembly), while CoSpar and VariancePartition fail to reach significance on any of them (p-values ranging from 0.22 to 0.91), indicating that LCL isolates fate-determining signals that are statistically invisible to existing lineage-aware methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

LCL repurposes the supervised contrastive learning paradigm (SupCon, SimCLR) by replacing image-based data augmentations with lineage barcodes as 'natural' augmentations: cells sharing a barcode form positive pairs, isolating fate-determining transcriptional signals from dominant confounders like cell type and time. This departs from VAE-based methods (scVI) that are unsupervised and cannot target lineage-specific variation, and from other lineage-aware tools (CoSpar, GEMLI) that use sparse optimization or random-forest pair classification rather than learned contrastive embeddings. The semi-supervised entropy penalty for unlabeled cells is a novel addition that enables transfer of lineage-informed structure to non-barcoded scRNA-seq datasets, a setting no prior contrastive or VAE method addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: most scRNA-seq datasets lack lineage barcodes, so the ability to transfer lineage-informed embeddings from a small barcoded reference to large unlabeled cohorts opens a path to fate-commitment analysis in routine single-cell studies. A key limitation is the dependence on sufficient cells per lineage (Pearson r ≈ 0.36 between lineage size and prediction accuracy), meaning very small clones remain a failure mode. Open questions include whether the contrastive framework extends to mutating barcodes (CRISPR scars) that encode phylogenies rather than static clonal identity, and whether the semi-supervised alignment generalizes across more distant biological systems.
