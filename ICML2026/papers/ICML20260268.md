## Problem

Standard DDPMs are restricted to Gaussian noise, making them unsuitable for data on constrained manifolds (directional, compositional, or heavy-tailed meteorological distributions). Star-shaped diffusion (SS-DDPM) extends to arbitrary exponential families but introduces temporal incoherence in the reverse process: because noisy states are conditionally independent given the clean data, the reverse trajectory loses sequential context, degrading sample quality and making few-step or distilled generation unreliable.

## Input / Output

The model takes a random sample from the terminal noise distribution q(z_T) (and optionally a class label for conditional generation) and sequentially denoises it to produce a clean sample. The network z_θ does not consume the raw noisy state z_t directly; instead it receives the sufficient tail statistic s_t = Σ A_k^T ψ(z_k) and the timestep t, and outputs a prediction of the clean data z_0. For data on constrained manifolds (unit sphere for von Mises-Fisher, probability simplex for Dirichlet), the network output is projected onto the manifold via L² normalization or softmax. Experiments cover 64×64 and 128×128 RGB images, 3D directional data, and 2D meteorological fields at 5.5 km and 0.25° resolution.

## Method

M-Star constructs a Markovian projection of a star-shaped forward process: it defines transition kernels k_t(z_t | z_{t-1}, z_0) that exactly match the one-dimensional marginals q(z_t | z_0) of the original non-Markovian process, thereby restoring a Markovian reverse chain while preserving exponential-family flexibility. The reverse posterior q̃(z_{t-1} | z_t, z_0) is an exponential family density whose natural parameter decomposes into a global hub term A_{t-1}f(z_0), a schedule bias b_{t-1}, and a local Markov coupling M_t ψ(z_t); the interaction matrix M_t is learned by minimizing a KL divergence between the ideal posterior (conditioned on true z_0) and the approximate posterior (conditioned on the predicted ẑ_0), effectively solving a least-squares regression over the dataset. In the Gaussian case, M_t = (√α_t/β_t)I recovers the standard DDPM posterior exactly, and empirically the learned M_t converges to this analytical form. Noise schedules are transferred from the Gaussian DDPM literature by matching the signal-to-noise ratio of the tail statistic, justified by a CLT argument showing s_t is asymptotically Gaussian. The hub-and-spoke training objective (predicting z_0 from s_t at every timestep) makes the model naturally amenable to progressive distillation, where a student learns two-step teacher lookaheads in tail-statistic space, enabling high-quality 4- to 8-step generation.

## Results

On ImageNet-64 class-conditional generation, M-Star Distillation at 8 NFE achieves FID 1.24±0.03 and IS 78±1.0, surpassing its own 1024-step teacher (FID 1.42, IS 84) while using 128× fewer function evaluations. On ImageNet-128, the 8-step M-Star student reaches FID 1.49±0.04 and IS 184±1.7, outperforming both PD (FID 2.50) and Multistep-CD (FID 2.10) at the same budget. On meteorological benchmarks (CERRA, ERA5), M-Star degrades by only ~7% in CRPS at 8 steps relative to its 1000-step reference, compared to ~40% degradation for SS-DDPM, and reduces Var(ẑ₀) by ~53% versus SS-DDPM on ImageNet-64/128.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-Star Distillation at 8 NFE (FID 1.24) decisively outperforms Progressive Distillation (FID 1.70) and Multistep-CD (FID 1.40) at the same step budget on ImageNet-64, while using fewer parameters (340M vs. 400M/1.2B). Against the many-step baselines, M-Star's 8-step student (FID 1.24) matches or beats VDM++ at 1024 NFE (FID 1.43) and RIN at 1000 NFE (FID 1.23) with 128× fewer evaluations. Compared to SS-DDPM, M-Star's Markovian projection yields a dramatic improvement: FID drops from 12.45 to 4.12 at 8 NFE (Table 1d), and the CRPS degradation at 8 steps shrinks from ~40% to ~7% on CERRA/ERA5. In the Gaussian special case, the learned interaction matrix M_t converges to the analytical DDPM value (√α_t/β_t · I), confirming exact recovery of the standard DDPM posterior. Visual comparisons (Figure 1) show M-Star producing sharper samples than DDPM, Shortcut, and Flow Matching at matched step counts on both CERRA and ImageNet. The ablation in Table 2 confirms the FID gain originates from the Markovian projection structure rather than the distillation algorithm itself, as replacing PD with Shortcut conditioning preserves the −8.3 FID improvement over SS-DDPM.

## Relation to Prior Work

M-Star builds directly on Star-Shaped DDPM (Okhotin et al., 2023), which replaced the Markovian forward process with a non-Markovian hub-and-spoke structure to accommodate exponential family distributions, but suffered from temporal incoherence in the reverse process. The key departure is the introduction of a Markovian projection—conceptually anchored in Gyöngy's mimicking theorem from mathematical finance—that preserves the one-dimensional marginals of the star-shaped process while restoring sequential dependencies, thereby recovering DDPM as a special case in the Gaussian limit. The paper also positions itself against the fragmented landscape of ad-hoc non-Gaussian diffusion extensions (Beta, Dirichlet, vMF) and against acceleration methods like progressive distillation and consistency models, arguing that the hub-and-spoke structure with clean-data prediction is inherently better suited for large-step distillation mappings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, unified route to diffusion over arbitrary exponential family distributions via SNR-matched noise schedules, eliminating the need for distribution-specific derivations. The demonstrated robustness to aggressive step reduction (7% CRPS degradation at 8 steps vs. 40% for SS-DDPM on meteorological benchmarks) makes it practically relevant for computationally constrained forecasting settings. However, the linear parameterization assumption limits applicability to complex manifolds, the joint optimization of (θ, M_t) lacks theoretical analysis, and the CLT-based noise schedule transfer is weakest precisely at the noisy end of the trajectory where the approximation is least valid—open questions that constrain the method's generality.
