## Problem

Existing graph-based deep learning for brain connectomes is predominantly supervised and discriminative, constrained by the scarcity and noise of fMRI labels, while unsupervised alternatives typically produce node-level embeddings rather than compact graph-level representations. Moreover, most methods apply fixed-density thresholding to dense functional connectivity (FC) graphs, obscuring global weight geometry and producing unstable group comparisons. The paper asks whether a fully unsupervised, graph-level latent representation can be learned directly from dense, weighted FC that simultaneously preserves both topological and spectral organizational structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes dense, signed N×N Pearson-correlation FC matrices (no thresholding) under a fixed parcellation (64, 100, or 150 regions with consistent node ordering across subjects), together with diffusion-map functional gradients extracted per subject and aligned via orthogonal Procrustes to a training-derived template as node features. For the temporal extension, per-region fMRI time series are additionally fed to a GRU pathway. Outputs include a compact graph-level latent vector z_g (16-d for rest, 32-d for task), a reconstructed dense FC matrix, synthetic connectomes sampled from a latent diffusion prior, and reconstructed neural time series from the spatial-temporal decoder.

## Method

The core architecture is an edge-conditioned graph transformer autoencoder trained purely on dense FC reconstruction MSE. The encoder uses aligned functional gradients as low-frequency geometric coordinates and modulates self-attention logits and values with learned edge embeddings derived from FC weights, enabling global node-pair interactions without sparsification; a global attention pooling layer then compresses node states into a single graph-level embedding z_g. Decoding employs a cross-attention mechanism over a learnable node memory table, where z_g acts as a routing signal to retrieve and combine regional priors, followed by a pairwise edge MLP that reconstructs the full dense FC matrix with enforced symmetry. A GRU-based temporal extension fuses neural dynamics with the spatial latent to form a joint z_g^st, and a denoising diffusion model fitted on z_g enables generation of synthetic connectomes by sampling in latent space and decoding with the frozen graph decoder. The key differentiator is the use of connectome-specific, functionally derived gradient geometry as an inductive bias—distinct from generic positional encodings or external spatial coordinates—which guides the transformer to learn a latent manifold where both spectral and graph-theoretic properties vary smoothly.

## Results

On HCP resting-state FC reconstruction, the proposed geometry-guided transformer autoencoder achieves the lowest edge MSE (0.0124 ± 0.0006), outperforming the best baseline (GATE at 0.0155). On the 7-way task-state classification in HCP, the unsupervised latent embedding reaches 84.7% accuracy (AUC 0.973), surpassing all baselines. On BSNIP, the method achieves the best reconstruction MSE (0.0156) and the highest HC-vs-schizophrenia separability (66.5% ACC, AUC 0.737), exceeding the next-best baseline by 3.7 AUC points. Incorporating neural dynamics via the spatial-temporal extension further boosts working-memory load decoding from 78.6% to 89.7% and stimulus-category decoding to 84.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across both HCP and BSNIP. In reconstruction, it reduces HCP resting-state MSE by 20% relative to the closest competitor (GATE, 0.0155) and by 30% relative to GAE (0.0177). For 7-way task-state classification, the geometry-guided latent reaches 84.7% accuracy, exceeding GRAPHITE (79.8%) by ~5 points and GAE (66.7%) by ~18 points. On BSNIP HC-vs-schizophrenia separation, the method achieves AUC 0.737, a 7.9-point margin over GATE (0.658) and a 15.7-point margin over GRALE (0.580), the weakest baseline. Representation-only controls (FC+UMAP, GraphMAE) confirm that the gains are not merely from dimensionality reduction but from the structured geometry-guided encoding. The consistent advantage across parcellation scales (64/100/150 nodes) and both rest and task conditions further supports the generality of the approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph transformer autoencoder line (Ma et al., 2023; Dwivedi & Bresson, 2020) to dense, weighted brain connectomes by injecting aligned diffusion-map functional gradients as node-level geometric coordinates, departing from prior geometry-guided graph learning that relies on graph-structural encodings (Rampašek et al., 2022), external spatial coordinates (Liu et al., 2022), or non-Euclidean spaces (Chami et al., 2019). The decoder's memory-based cross-attention mechanism distinguishes it from standard GAE (Kipf & Welling, 2016), Graphite (Grover et al., 2019), GRALE (Krzakala et al., 2025), and GATE (Liu et al., 2021) by routing a compact graph latent through learned node priors rather than direct pairwise reconstruction. The latent diffusion component follows Rombach et al. (2022) but operates on the learned graph embedding rather than raw adjacency, and the temporal extension draws on neural dimensionality reduction (Cunningham & Yu, 2014) to fuse spatial and dynamical summaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework unifies three previously separate goals—compact unsupervised representation, synthetic connectome generation, and interpretable cognitive analysis—within a single latent geometry, offering a practical toolkit for connectomics where labeled data are scarce. The finding that directional reconfiguration along a gradient-related axis (association-sensory differentiation) is cognitively beneficial while diffuse multi-task dispersion is detrimental provides a mechanistic hypothesis linking spectral geometry to intelligence that goes beyond aggregate graph metrics. Key limitations include reliance on fixed parcellation with consistent node ordering, quadratic attention cost restricting voxel-level scaling, and dependence on diffusion-map gradient estimation quality; broader validation across acquisition protocols, sites, and atlases remains open, as does clinical translation of the BSNIP results.
