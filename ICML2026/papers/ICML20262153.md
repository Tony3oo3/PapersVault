## Problem

DNA sequences lack natural word boundaries, making fixed tokenization schemes (single nucleotides, k-mers, BPE) brittle under mutations, indels, and local repeats. Prior learnable tokenization work has been confined to autoregressive settings, leaving masked DNA language models without a mechanism to adaptively allocate granularity based on biological context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262153/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw DNA nucleotide sequences (A/C/G/T/N, vocabulary size 10 padded to 16) at base-pair resolution, with a native pretraining context of 8,192 bp and a scatter-gather sliding-window scheme (50% overlap, 4,096 bp stride) extending coverage to ~1 Mb for long-range tasks. During pretraining it outputs per-position masked nucleotide predictions; downstream it produces class labels, regression values, or variant-effect scores via a frozen-backbone linear probe or full fine-tuning with masked-mean pooling over valid tokens.

## Method

DNAChunker is a 172M-parameter bidirectional masked language model with a two-stage hierarchical encoder that compresses base-pair embeddings via bidirectional Mamba layers and cosine-similarity-based routing (targeting ~0.33 retention per stage), a 30-layer Transformer main network with RoPE operating on the compressed chunks, and a decoder that reconstructs base-pair representations through bidirectional probability-gated smoothing. Three key mechanisms distinguish it from prior dynamic chunking: a mask-protection scheme that forces singleton chunks around [MASK] tokens to prevent mask-conditioned segmentation, a masked residual gating that zeroes encoder residuals at masked positions to enforce contextual compute in the main network, and a ratio loss that steers the average boundary probability toward a target compression ratio. Pretraining uses standard MLM (15% masking: 80% [MASK], 10% random, 10% unchanged) with per-token loss down-weighting of RepeatMasker-annotated positions to 0.1, trained on GRCh38/hg38 for ~52 billion base pairs.

## Results

On the Nucleotide Transformer benchmark (18 datasets), DNACHUNKER (172M params) achieves a total average MCC of 0.772 and average rank of 1.67, outperforming the next-best baseline GENERATOR (1.2B) by +0.044 MCC while using only 14% of its parameters. On the revised NT benchmark, DNACHUNKER reaches 0.660 average MCC, surpassing MxDNA (0.637) and PatchDNA (0.626), with the largest gain on splice-site annotation (+0.068 over MxDNA). On BEND, DNACHUNKER attains the best average rank (1.9) across seven tasks, and on DNALongBench it surpasses Caduceus-PH on all five long-range tasks using only linear probing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNACHUNKER (172M) outperforms GENERATOR (1.2B) by +0.044 total average MCC on the NT benchmark (0.772 vs. 0.728) and by +0.076 on histone mark prediction specifically, while using only 14% of the parameters. Against the multi-species NT-multi (2.5B) and NT-v2 (500M) models, DNACHUNKER leads by +0.082 and +0.079 total MCC respectively, despite being trained solely on the human reference genome. On the revised NT benchmark, DNACHUNKER surpasses both learnable-tokenization baselines MxDNA (+0.023) and PatchDNA (+0.034) in average MCC, with a particularly large +0.196 gain over PatchDNA on splice-site annotation. On Genomic Benchmarks, DNACHUNKER matches GENERATOR in average accuracy (0.885 vs. 0.892) with 7× fewer parameters and achieves the second-best average rank (3.29 vs. 2.89). On BEND, DNACHUNKER achieves the best average rank (1.9) over PatchDNA (2.1), leading on variant-effect prediction for expression (0.59 AUROC vs. 0.51). On DNALongBench, DNACHUNKER beats Caduceus-PH (the strongest DNA foundation model baseline) on all five long-range tasks under linear probing, with gains of +0.061 on enhancer–target gene interaction and +0.047 on transcription initiation signal prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the dynamic chunking paradigm from autoregressive sequence modeling (H-Net, Byte Latent Transformer) to the masked, bidirectional DNA language model setting, which is a non-trivial shift because boundary decisions must now be informed by both upstream and downstream context. It builds on BiMamba (Caduceus) for the lightweight encoder/decoder stages and on the Nucleotide Transformer's MLM pretraining recipe, but departs from all prior DNA tokenization schemes (single-nucleotide, fixed k-mers, BPE) by making segmentation a learned, context-dependent process. The mask-protection mechanism and probability-gated bidirectional smoothing in the decoder are novel architectural contributions specific to the MLM setting, addressing information-leakage issues that do not arise in autoregressive tokenizers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Tokenization is a foundational design choice in genomic language models, and this work demonstrates that learned, adaptive segmentation can simultaneously improve accuracy, reduce parameter count (172M vs. 1.2B+ baselines), and lower compute cost at long contexts — making high-quality genomic prediction more accessible. The discovery that learned chunks exhibit a biologically structured gradient (finer over exons, coarser over SINEs with age-dependent decay) without explicit supervision suggests the model is recovering real genomic organization, which could inform future interpretability and variant-prioritization tools. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-species pretraining at Evo-scale, whether it generalizes to RNA or protein sequences, and whether the compression-ratio hyperparameter can be made fully adaptive rather than fixed per stage.
