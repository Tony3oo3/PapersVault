## Problem

Standard reward models in RLHF are vulnerable to reward hacking: they learn spurious correlations (e.g., response length, sycophantic phrasing) that are not causally related to human preference, causing the policy to exploit shortcuts during RLHF. Existing mitigations either require explicitly specifying which spurious variables to control or apply information-theoretic bottlenecks without explicitly disentangling causal from non-causal factors in the latent representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265448/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a prompt-response pair (x, y) and produces a scalar reward score. The SFT LLM backbone (Qwen2.5-Math-7B or Qwen2.5-7B) produces a contextual embedding h from the final-layer hidden state of the last non-padding token, which is then factorized into a 128-dim causal latent z^c and a 512-dim non-causal latent z^nc via diagonal-Gaussian posteriors. At inference, the mean of the causal posterior (rather than a stochastic sample) is fed to the linear reward head for stable scoring.

## Method

CausalRM introduces a VAE-style factorized bottleneck on top of a standard reward model: a variational encoder maps the backbone embedding h to two latent variables, z^c (causal) and z^nc (non-causal), each parameterized as a diagonal-covariance Gaussian. The reward head is structurally restricted to depend only on z^c, implementing a hard invariance constraint. An adversarial head with a gradient reversal layer (GRL) is trained to predict reward from z^nc, so that gradients push the encoder to remove reward-predictive signals from the non-causal channel. A reconstruction decoder maps [z^c; z^nc] back to h to prevent posterior collapse. The overall training is a minimax objective: minimize the Bradley-Terry preference loss plus KL regularizers on both latents plus reconstruction loss, while maximizing the adversarial loss (via GRL), jointly enforcing sufficiency, minimality, causal invariance, and non-degeneracy.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning, CausalRM achieves 70.1% average pairwise accuracy (ID) and 85.6% (OOD), outperforming the best baseline (GoalRM) by 1.8% and 2.6% respectively. On open-ended dialogue, it reaches 72.3% (ID) and 62.3% (OOD) pairwise accuracy, a 1.2% and 1.6% improvement over GoalRM. For downstream RLHF on math, CausalRM yields 74.0% final-answer accuracy (ID) and 89.6% (OOD), surpassing GoalRM by 1.5% and 1.1%. On dialogue RLHF, CausalRM achieves 54.8% win rate against Standard RM and 42.3% against GoalRM on ID, with advantages persisting under OOD evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CausalRM consistently outperforms all four baselines across both tasks. Against GoalRM (Nath et al., 2024), CausalRM gains +1.8% on math ID and +2.6% on math OOD pairwise accuracy, and +1.2%/+1.6% on dialogue ID/OOD; the OOD gap is the largest, suggesting GoalRM's contrastive objective still retains spurious correlations under distribution shift. Against InfoRM (Miao et al., 2025a), CausalRM leads by +4.0% on math ID and +3.1% on OOD pairwise accuracy, and the gap widens dramatically in downstream RLHF where InfoRM collapses to 48.0% final-answer accuracy (vs. CausalRM's 74.0%) due to a formatting failure mode. Against the artifact-specific baselines ODIN (Chen et al., 2024) and MMD (Wang et al., 2025), CausalRM achieves 70.1%/85.6% on math (vs. 68.8%/83.2% and 69.3%/83.6%) and 70.6%/61.2% on hacked dialogue (vs. 66.7%/57.5% and 67.1%/57.9%), while also exhibiting the lowest length sensitivity (σ_len = 0.03 vs. 0.12 for ODIN and 0.09 for MMD) without requiring explicit specification of the spurious variable.

## Relation to Prior Work

CausalRM extends the information-bottleneck line of reward modeling (InfoRM) by replacing a single bottlenecked latent with an explicit two-factor decomposition into causal and non-causal components, drawing on causal representation learning techniques from traditional RL (ASR, IFactor, AdaRL). It departs from artifact-specific mitigation methods (ODIN for length, MMD regularization for sycophancy) by learning a general factorization without requiring the practitioner to pre-specify which spurious variables to control. The adversarial gradient-reversal mechanism for suppressing reward-predictive leakage into the non-causal channel is borrowed from domain-adaptation literature (Ganin & Lempitsky, 2015) and repurposed here as an invariance enforcer for reward models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reward hacking remains a central obstacle to reliable RLHF, and CausalRM offers a general, task-agnostic mechanism to reduce sensitivity to unknown spurious attributes without manual artifact annotation. The paper's main limitation is the factorization assumption: recovering true causal structure from purely observational preference data is not guaranteed, and the identifiability conditions in the appendix require strong variability and invariance assumptions that may not hold in practice. Open questions include whether the approach extends to process reward models, multi-turn settings with dynamic confounders, and whether the modest 1–3 point accuracy gains over strong baselines persist at larger model scales or with more complex spurious structures.
