## Problem

Predicting ionic transport properties from static atomic structures is fundamentally challenging because these properties depend on long-time atomic dynamics, creating a severe modality gap. Autoregressive MD acceleration methods are slow and suffer from error accumulation, while existing non-autoregressive predictors lack accuracy because they cannot exploit trajectory information. Moreover, no prior method can simultaneously benefit from both trajectory-based and structure-based datasets, which are both scarce in ionic transport.

## Input / Output

At inference, the predictor takes only an equilibrium atomic structure (3D coordinates of N atoms) and a scalar temperature, producing a single ionic transport property (MSD, diffusivity, or conductivity) for a target ionic species. Atomic trajectories are consumed exclusively during training as an auxiliary modality via a time-series foundation model (MOMENT), while structure embeddings are extracted from a pretrained MLIP (SevenNet) using only its early layers. Both embeddings undergo third-order polynomial expansion to introduce nonlinearity while the downstream encoder remains a single linear layer, keeping inference lightweight.

## Method

The framework introduces a two-level auxiliary modality learning scheme. First, a dual-modal trainer g is trained with both trajectory and structure-temperature embeddings, with a regularization term forcing the structure encoder to produce useful representations independently. Knowledge is then transferred to a trajectory-free predictor f1 via a closed-form ridge regression solution that aligns f1's hidden representations with those of g, avoiding iterative gradient-based distillation and proving more effective under data scarcity. Second, for structure-based datasets lacking trajectories, data-level auxiliary modality learning initializes a new predictor f2 by combining the dual-modal trainer's structure encoder (to avoid trajectory-distribution bias) with f1's decoder (to retain transport-property knowledge). The entire pipeline leverages scientific foundation models (SevenNet for structure, MOMENT for trajectories) to extract rich physical priors without task-specific fine-tuning, enabling accurate prediction even with limited training data.

## Results

On the trajectory-based Dataset 1, the proposed method achieves MAE of 0.344/0.367/0.402/0.390 (log₁₀MSD_Li) at 600K/800K/1000K/1200K, outperforming the autoregressive LiFlow baseline (0.378/0.392/0.457/0.407) while running ~208× faster (14 s vs. 2910 s for 419 materials × 4 temperatures). On structure-based Datasets 2 and 3, the method reduces MAE to 0.166/0.149/0.074/0.064 (log₁₀D_Na) and 1.388 (log₁₀σ_Li), substantially below all non-autoregressive baselines. The method also generalizes to an unseen ion species (Na) and to real-world experimental data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the autoregressive LiFlow (Nam et al., 2025), the proposed method achieves lower MAE at every temperature (e.g., 0.344 vs. 0.378 at 600K) while reducing inference time from 2910 s to 14 s (~208× speedup), eliminating error accumulation from sequential trajectory rollouts. Among non-autoregressive baselines, DenseGNN (Du et al., 2024) is the closest competitor on Dataset 1 (MAE 0.412–0.531), but the proposed method still improves by 0.068–0.131 across temperatures. On structure-based Dataset 2, the gap widens dramatically: the proposed method achieves MAE 0.064–0.166 versus 0.312–0.651 for DenseGNN, 0.386–0.517 for ComFormer, and 0.361–0.651 for MatFormer, with the largest relative gains at higher temperatures. On the real-world experimental Dataset 3, the proposed method (MAE 1.388) outperforms DenseGNN (2.048), MatFormer (2.090), and ComFormer (2.150) by roughly 0.66–0.76 in log₁₀σ_Li. A random forest baseline using compositional/structural features (Therrien et al., 2025) is also consistently outperformed across all three datasets, confirming the advantage of the learned representation over hand-crafted descriptors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the auxiliary modality learning framework (Shen et al., 2023) from vision-language settings to materials science, formulating atomic trajectories as a privileged modality available only during training for ionic transport prediction. It departs from autoregressive MD acceleration methods (LiFlow, Schreiner et al.) that sequentially generate trajectories, and from conventional non-autoregressive property predictors (MatFormer, ComFormer, DenseGNN) that ignore dynamics entirely, by transferring dynamical knowledge via a closed-form ridge-regression initialization rather than iterative gradient-based distillation. The structural embedding strategy follows MeshGraphNet (Pfaff et al., 2020), while the use of scientific foundation models (SevenNet, MOMENT) as frozen feature extractors under data scarcity is a practical departure from task-specific encoder training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical pathway for high-throughput screening of ionic conductors for rechargeable batteries, achieving 200× speedup over autoregressive baselines while simultaneously improving accuracy—a combination neither prior approach achieves. Open questions include understanding when the linear-encoder assumption holds relative to foundation-model representation spaces, and whether the transferability of dynamical knowledge across ion species, data sources, and target formulations (MSD → diffusivity → conductivity) generalizes beyond the tested settings. The reliance on pretrained scientific foundation models introduces a dependency whose systematic boundaries remain unclear, particularly for materially distinct classes like polymers.
