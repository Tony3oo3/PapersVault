## Problem

3D seismic fault detection lacks a standardized benchmark that simultaneously provides verifiable ground-truth labels and realistic field texture. Existing synthetic datasets (e.g., FaultSeg3D) offer pixel-perfect labels but overly clean physics-based textures that mask model brittleness, while real field data has realistic texture but subjective, sparse, and interpreter-dependent annotations. This Sim2Real gap means models can achieve near-perfect metrics on simplified data yet collapse under the domain shift encountered in deployment-critical applications like CCS.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a random seed and geological parameters to produce a single contiguous 2.7-billion-voxel volume (1500×1500×1200, spanning 18.75×18.75×6.0 km at 12.5 m lateral / 5 m vertical spacing) with a bimodal fault network of 493 labeled faults (5 primary listric normals >100 m displacement, 488 subsidiary synthetic/antithetic fractures <20 m). The benchmark is released in five variants—Clean plus four diffusion-styled versions stratified by source domain (F3 North Sea vs. Gorgon Australia) and texture intensity (Low/High)—all sharing identical voxel-wise ground-truth labels, enabling controlled robustness testing. Evaluation is zero-shot: pre-trained fault detectors are applied without fine-tuning, and metrics (IoU, P, R, F1) are reported at three spatial tolerances (T=0,1,2) via 3D morphological dilation.

## Method

SeisMark decouples geological structure from textural realism through a three-stage hybrid pipeline. Stage 1 is an event-based procedural model builder with parameterized modules (Deposit, Squish, Fault, Stratigraphic) that constructs a geologically plausible 3D P-wave velocity model and voxel-aligned fault labels, grounded in the Gorgon field's polyphase extensional basin architecture. Stage 2 applies 1D convolutional forward modeling (Gardner's relation for impedance, zero-phase 25 Hz Ricker wavelet) to convert the velocity model into a pseudo-seismic volume that preserves reflector geometry and fault offsets. Stage 3 performs diffusion-based image-to-image translation where the pseudo-seismic serves as a structural conditioning signal, locking reflector phases and fault positions while a denoising diffusion model—trained separately on F3 and Gorgon field data—regenerates high-frequency texture. Texture intensity is controlled by modulating the initial noise timestep and guidance scale, and label preservation is enforced by penalizing deviations from the structural edges in the conditioning input. This design avoids the hallucination problems of GAN-based approaches because the reverse-denoising process starts from a partially noisy pseudo-seismic state rather than pure random noise, keeping macroscopic geometry fixed while only regenerating fine texture.

## Results

At the standard geological tolerance (T=1), Fault-Net achieves the highest IoU across all five SeisMark variants, ranging from 0.458 (North Sea High) to 0.612 (Clean baseline). The legacy FaultSeg3D (V1) exhibits the most dramatic degradation under realistic texture: its Recall at T=1 drops from 0.804 on Clean to 0.305 on Australia (High), a 62% relative decline. RGF similarly collapses, with F1 falling from 0.665 (Clean) to 0.410 (Australia High), while FaultSeg3D+ (V2) retains more stability but still loses ~20% F1 over the same range. Structural fidelity is confirmed by SSIM scores of 0.88–0.97 and Gradient Magnitude Correlation >0.87 across all styled variants, verifying that the diffusion pipeline preserves fault labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fault-Net (Dou et al., 2022), trained on a mixture of synthetic data and sparse real-field labeled slices, leads in IoU on all five SeisMark variants; on Australia (Low) it scores IoU 0.384 versus RGF's 0.237, a gap of 0.147, and maintains the tightest Precision–Recall balance (gap of 0.265 vs. V2's 0.343 on the same variant). FaultSeg3D+ (V2, Li et al., 2024) is the strongest synthetic-only model, pushing Precision to 0.921 on Australia (Low) but sacrificing Recall to 0.578, revealing a precision-recall imbalance that limits field utility. FaultSeg3D (V1, Wu et al., 2019) and RGF (Lin et al., 2025) both suffer performance collapse under high-intensity textures: V1's Recall at T=1 falls from 0.804 (Clean) to 0.305 (Australia High), and RGF's F1 drops from 0.665 to 0.410 over the same range. The paper attributes Fault-Net's consistent lead to its real-data training exposure, which the styled variants are specifically designed to expose as a differentiator from purely synthetic-trained models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based style transfer framework for synthetic-to-real domain adaptation (Chigot et al., 2025) into the geophysical domain, using DDPM's partial-noising/reverse-denoising procedure as the core texture synthesis mechanism. It departs from prior synthetic seismic benchmarks (FaultSeg3D, SEAM) that rely on simplified 1D convolutional or wave-equation modeling, and from GAN-based domain adaptation (CycleGAN, NST) that risks hallucinating or destroying fault structures. The key conceptual departure is the explicit decoupling of structural generation (procedural geology + physics-based forward modeling) from textural realism (diffusion styling), enabling independent control over the Sim2Real gap while preserving voxel-perfect labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SeisMark addresses a long-standing data paradox in seismic interpretation: the absence of a large-scale, verifiable ground-truth benchmark that simultaneously captures realistic acquisition textures. The finding that diffusion styling actually *improves* precision by healing physics-modeling interference artifacts into coherent reflectors is a useful insight for synthetic data pipelines beyond this specific task. Open questions include generalization to compressional and salt-dominated basins (only one extensional basin is covered), whether the single-volume design limits statistical power, and whether the zero-shot protocol fairly represents models that could be fine-tuned on styled data. For CCS and geothermal safety applications, the demonstrated performance collapse of legacy models under realistic textures underscores the deployment risk of models validated only on clean synthetic data.
