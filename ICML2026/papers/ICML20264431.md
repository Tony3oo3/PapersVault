## Problem

DiLoCo, a communication-efficient distributed training framework for LLMs, has been shown to degrade in performance as the number of workers (K) increases, limiting its utility at large scale. This paper identifies the inner optimizer choice as an overlooked factor in this degradation: the inner optimizer shapes the pseudogradient consumed by the outer optimizer, and AdamW's variable-magnitude steps introduce destructive interference during worker averaging that worsens with K. The paper asks whether Muon's orthonormalized steps can mitigate this degradation and improve worker scaling.

## Input / Output

Each of K workers receives a shard of the Nemotron-CC text corpus (tokenized with the Llama3 tokenizer, sequence length 2048) and an initial model checkpoint. Workers perform H local Muon optimization steps independently, then communicate their parameter deltas (θ^(t−H) − θ_k^(t)) to form a pseudogradient Ψ = (1/K)ΣΔ_k. The outer optimizer (SGD with Nesterov momentum) consumes this pseudogradient to update the global model. The final output is a trained dense transformer (Gemma3-style, 150M–15B parameters) evaluated on held-out Nemotron-CC validation loss.

## Method

MuLoCo replaces DiLoCo's AdamW inner optimizer with Muon, which applies five steps of Newton-Schulz iteration to its momentum accumulator to produce orthonormalized update matrices (approximating UV⊤ from the SVD of the momentum). The outer optimizer remains SGD with Nesterov momentum, unchanged from DiLoCo. The key theoretical contribution (Proposition 3.1) shows that the nuclear norm of the pseudogradient equals (√r/K)Σ ρ·α·‖ψ‖_F, where ρ is the cosine similarity between each inner step and the pseudogradient's orthonormal factor. Since Muon's orthonormal steps have constant Frobenius norm √r, the pseudogradient's spectral mass depends only on per-step alignment ρ, whereas AdamW's variable step norms introduce additional variance that causes spectral collapse during averaging (quantified via a top-S interference gap). Empirically, MuLoCo's pseudogradients maintain higher cosine similarity to the K=1 reference as K grows, its critical batch size is 2–4× larger than DiLoCo's at 3.1B scale, and K=1 MuLoCo achieves a Pareto-optimal performance–training-time tradeoff, reaching the same loss as DP AdamW in ~4.5× fewer sequential FLOPs.

## Results

Across 150M–3.1B Gemma3-style transformers trained on Nemotron-CC (20 TPP), MuLoCo consistently outperforms DiLoCo in absolute final validation loss at all worker counts K, and for K > 2 it also outperforms DiLoCo when normalized by their respective data-parallel baselines (DP Muon vs. DP AdamW). At 3.1B scale, MuLoCo's critical batch size is 2–4× larger than DiLoCo's, and K=1 MuLoCo reaches the same loss as DP AdamW in roughly 4.5× fewer sequential FLOPs. Wall-clock overhead of Muon over AdamW as the inner optimizer is negligible (+0.96% step time, −25% memory complexity: 3 vs. 4 parameter copies). Scaling-law fits (L(C) = AC^α + L_irr) show MuLoCo has consistently larger |α| exponents than DiLoCo, indicating stronger compute scaling, and at 15B MuLoCo K=1 matches the best downstream performance while using a 16M-token batch size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to **DiLoCo** (AdamW inner optimizer), MuLoCo wins in absolute final validation loss at every worker count K from 1 to 16 across all model scales (150M–15B). More importantly, for K > 2 workers, MuLoCo's performance relative to DP Muon degrades more slowly than DiLoCo's relative to DP AdamW, meaning the advantage is not merely inherited from Muon > AdamW in the DP setting. MuLoCo also outperforms DiLoCo under long synchronization intervals (H up to 240), 4-bit and 2-bit quantization, top-k sparsification with error feedback, and streaming (partitioned) communication, while incurring only +0.96% wall-clock overhead. Compared to **DP Muon** (the data-parallel gold standard), K=1 MuLoCo achieves a stronger final loss than DP Muon at 3.1B scale while supporting a 2–4× larger critical batch size, placing it on the Pareto frontier of performance vs. training time. At K=16, MuLoCo nearly matches its own K=1 performance at 15B scale, whereas DiLoCo at K=16 shows more degradation relative to its K=1 counterpart.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DiLoCo (Douillard et al., 2023), a communication-efficient distributed optimizer that uses AdamW as the inner optimizer and SGD with Nesterov momentum as the outer optimizer. The core methodological contribution is a simple but consequential swap: replacing AdamW with Muon (Jordan et al., 2024) as the inner optimizer, leveraging Muon's orthonormalized Newton-Schulz steps. The paper also extends the worker-scaling analysis of Charles et al. (2025), who showed DiLoCo degrades as workers increase, by demonstrating that this degradation is partly attributable to the inner optimizer choice. A speculative theoretical analysis (Proposition 3.1) connects Muon's orthonormal step geometry to reduced destructive interference in the averaged pseudogradient's nuclear norm, distinguishing it from AdamW's variable-norm adaptive steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MuLoCo is a drop-in replacement for DiLoCo that improves performance across every tested dimension—worker count, synchronization interval, quantization, sparsification, and streaming—while adding negligible wall-clock overhead (<1%). The finding that MuLoCo's critical batch size is 2–4× larger than DiLoCo's at 3.1B scale has direct practical implications for large-scale training, enabling more parallelism and fewer sequential steps. Limitations include evaluation only on dense transformers (150M–15B) in a 20TPP setting, no convergence guarantees, and a purely speculative theoretical explanation. Open questions include whether the benefits extend to MoE/SSM architectures, whether alternative outer optimizers (beyond Nesterov SGD) further improve MuLoCo, and whether the worker-scaling advantage persists at scales beyond 15B.
