## Problem

Adam-DA is the de facto optimizer for training GANs and other zero-sum games, yet its theoretical understanding lags far behind Adam in minimization. In particular, the roles of the first-order momentum β and second-order momentum ρ in governing local convergence and implicit gradient regularization in zero-sum games remain uncharacterized, despite practical evidence (e.g., negative momentum improving GAN stability) that contradicts minimization-based intuition.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a smooth zero-sum game loss f(x,y) with bounded derivatives up to fourth order, along with Adam-DA hyperparameters (β ∈ (−1,1), ρ ∈ (0,1), ε > 0, step size h > 0). It produces (i) a pair of coupled ODEs approximating Adam-DA with O(h³) local error, (ii) explicit step-size upper bounds for local convergence as functions of β, ρ, ε, and the Jacobian spectrum at equilibrium, and (iii) qualitative predictions about how β and ρ steer trajectories toward flatter regions measured by the ℓ₁ gradient norm. Empirical validation uses improved Wasserstein GANs on CIFAR-10 (32×32) and STL-10 (64×64) with ResNet and CNN architectures, reporting cumulative average gradient norms and Inception Scores.

## Method

The paper derives continuous-time ODEs for Adam-DA via backward error analysis, achieving O(h³) local error—improving over the O(h²) SignGDA-flow from Ma et al. (2022). The key structural insight is that the Jacobian of the continuous-time model at a local Nash equilibrium is a quadratic polynomial of the standard GDA Jacobian (Proposition 4.1), reducing the eigenvalue analysis to studying a simple algebraic expression. Under the assumption that the anti-symmetric (adversarial interaction) part of the Jacobian dominates the symmetric part, the analysis shows that smaller β and larger ρ both widen the convergence region and reduce the implicit gradient regularization coefficient K(β,ρ), guiding trajectories toward flatter loss regions—exactly the opposite of Adam in minimization. A corollary establishes that Adam-DA always diverges on bilinear games regardless of parameters, extending prior divergence results from GDA and MWU to the adaptive setting. Predictions are validated through GAN experiments across two architectures and two datasets, confirming that lower β and higher ρ yield smaller gradient norms and higher Inception Scores.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result is that in zero-sum games, a smaller first-order momentum β and a larger second-order momentum ρ improve local convergence and guide trajectories toward flatter regions of the loss landscape — exactly the opposite of Adam's behavior in minimization. Specifically, Theorem 4.4 shows that Adam-DA converges locally iff h < min_λ [2√ε(1−β²)|Re(λ)|] / [(1+β²)|λ|² + 2β(Im(λ)²−Re(λ)²)], and Corollary 4.5 confirms this bound is decreasing in β. Empirically, GAN training on CIFAR-10 and STL-10 with ResNet and CNN architectures confirms that smaller β (e.g., β=0 vs. β=0.9) and larger ρ (e.g., ρ=0.99 vs. ρ=0.5) yield lower cumulative ℓ₁ gradient norms, and models with lower gradient norms achieve higher Inception Scores (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline comparison is between the proposed Continuous Adam-DA (O(h³) local error) and SignGDA-flow from Ma et al. (2022) (O(h²) local error), the min-max adaptation of the ODEs for Adam in minimization. On three test functions (Figure 1), Continuous Adam-DA closely tracks the discrete Adam-DA trajectories, while SignGDA-flow either diverges or converges to a different equilibrium in cases 1(b) and 1(c). Distance curves averaged over 30 random initial conditions (Figures 5–6) confirm that Continuous Adam-DA consistently maintains smaller trajectory deviation from Adam-DA than SignGDA-flow across all three test functions and multiple parameter settings. The paper also contrasts its findings with the minimization literature: in minimization, larger β and smaller ρ promote implicit gradient regularization (Cattaneo et al., 2024), whereas in zero-sum games the effect is reversed — smaller β and larger ρ reduce gradient norms. This reversal is validated across four architecture-dataset combinations (ResNet/CNN × CIFAR-10/STL-10), with consistent results showing lower gradient norms and higher Inception Scores for the theoretically predicted parameter regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ODE-based continuous-time analysis framework from two parallel lines of work: (1) ODEs for Adam in minimization (Cattaneo et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2022) and (2) ODEs for simpler methods (GDA, Heavy-ball) in zero-sum games (Rosca et al., 2021; Feng et al., 2025). The key methodological departure is deriving an O(h³)-local-error ODE for Adam-DA that captures the interaction between two players, which is substantially more complex than either the single-player Adam ODE or the non-adaptive Heavy-ball game ODE. The Jacobian structure at equilibrium (Proposition 4.1) reveals that the Continuous Adam-DA Jacobian is a quadratic polynomial of the Continuous GDA Jacobian, enabling a clean bridge between the two analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that the roles of first-order momentum β and second-order momentum ρ are exactly reversed in zero-sum games compared to minimization—provides a theoretical explanation for the long-observed empirical benefit of negative momentum in GAN training (Gidel et al., 2019) and gives practitioners concrete guidance on hyperparameter selection for adversarial optimization. The result that Adam-DA always diverges on bilinear games, regardless of parameters, extends prior divergence results for GDA and MWU to the adaptive setting and highlights a fundamental limitation. The main limitation is the deterministic analysis; extending to stochastic Adam-DA and to multi-player general-sum games are identified as important open directions.
