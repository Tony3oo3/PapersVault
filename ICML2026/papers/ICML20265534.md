## Problem

The Nearest Class Mean (NCM) classifier is the de facto standard in Class-Incremental Learning, justified by Neural Collapse theory's assumption that intra-class features collapse to single points. However, practical CIL settings—insufficient training, regularization constraints, and pervasive feature drift—prevent this ideal collapse, causing classes to form complex non-linear manifolds. Procrustes distance analysis further reveals that feature drift is predominantly non-linear (normalized distances reaching 0.3–0.4), far exceeding what rigid transformations can explain, rendering single-prototype classifiers fundamentally suboptimal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, variable resolution for CUB-200 and ImageNet-R) processed through a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone with optional PEFT modules (prompts, adapters, or LoRA). Features are L2-normalized to the unit hypersphere before classification. The output is a single class label predicted via a dual-view scoring function that fuses global class-mean cosine similarity with the maximum local sub-prototype similarity, selecting the argmax over all classes seen so far without task ID.

## Method

HC-SOINN represents each class as a topology graph of local sub-prototypes connected by edges, constructed in two phases: (1) Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering (UPGMA with cosine distance) on buffered features provides a stable, order-independent initialization, and (2) a simplified SOINN with Competitive Hebbian Learning refines node positions via Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) to preserve unit-norm geometry while learning adjacency edges. Inference combines a global NCM term and a local topological term with a balancing factor α, which the paper proves equivalent to MAP estimation under a hybrid von Mises–Fisher distribution. The STAR module addresses non-linear drift by maintaining anchor samples per topological node, computing EMA-smoothed pointwise drift vectors at each task boundary, and transporting nodes along these residuals to actively deform the manifold—shifting the paradigm from drift resistance to drift adaptation without any gradient-based retraining.

## Results

Integrating HC-SOINN into seven state-of-the-art CIL methods yields consistent gains across Split CIFAR-100, Split CUB-200, and Split ImageNet-R. The strongest results are CL-LoRA + HC-SOINN (92.32% A_Avg, 87.99% A_Last on CIFAR-100) and CODA-Prompt + HC-SOINN (85.75% A_Last on CUB-200, a +15.78% gain over its FC baseline). Adding the STAR drift-alignment module further boosts performance, e.g., DualPrompt + HC-SOINN + STAR reaches 85.63% A_Last on CIFAR-100 and 85.84% on CUB-200, surpassing the KAC classifier by up to 5.90% on ImageNet-R.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HC-SOINN consistently outperforms the original classifiers of all seven integrated baselines. The most dramatic gains appear for methods originally using FC layers: SEMA improves by +21.00% A_Last on CUB-200 and CODA-Prompt by +15.78% A_Last on CUB-200, confirming that replacing FC with the topology-aware classifier effectively eliminates catastrophic forgetting in long-sequence settings. Against the KAC non-linear classifier (Hu et al., CVPR 2025), HC-SOINN + STAR achieves 69.75% A_Last on ImageNet-R with DualPrompt versus KAC's 63.85%, a 5.90% margin, while KAC is unstable and sometimes underperforms even the FC baseline. In the exemplar-based comparison (Table 7), SEMA + HC-SOINN + STAR surpasses FOSTER (89.87% vs. 94.25% A_Avg on CIFAR-100) and iCaRL (82.46%) while using fewer stored exemplars (~18.5 vs. 20). EASE is the only baseline where HC-SOINN causes a slight regression on CUB-200 (−0.59% A_Avg), suggesting its expandable-subspace representation already partially captures manifold structure. Overall, the method establishes new SOTA when combined with the strongest baselines (SEMA, CL-LoRA) across all three benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Neural Collapse (NC) theory that has justified the widespread use of Nearest Class Mean (NCM) classifiers in class-incremental learning, and on the classic Self-Organizing Incremental Neural Network (SOINN) for unsupervised topology learning. It departs from the NC paradigm by empirically demonstrating (via Procrustes distance analysis) that practical CIL settings violate the collapse assumption, causing class features to form complex non-linear manifolds rather than single points. The key conceptual move is repurposing SOINN—a method originally designed for streaming unsupervised clustering—into a supervised, topology-aware classifier (HC-SOINN) that sits as a drop-in replacement for NCM or FC heads in existing PEFT-based CIL frameworks (DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, SEMA, CL-LoRA, etc.), while the STAR module introduces a novel pointwise drift-adaptation mechanism absent from prior drift-compensation work (e.g., Yu et al. 2020's global alignment).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Procrustes analysis providing a quantitative diagnostic for non-linear feature drift (reaching 0.3–0.4) gives the field a concrete tool to assess when single-prototype classifiers become unreliable, and the drop-in classifier design means the approach can be adopted without modifying backbone training pipelines. The large gains on FC-based methods (e.g., +21% for SEMA on CUB-200) highlight that classifier choice, not just representation learning, is a critical bottleneck in CIL. Open questions include whether the topological representation extends to multi-modal or video CIL, how to exploit the learned edge connectivity during inference (currently unused), and whether STAR's anchor-based alignment remains reliable under adversarial or highly noisy drift conditions. The moderate storage overhead for anchors and the ~4% training overhead are practical trade-offs that may limit deployment in resource-constrained settings.
