## Problem

Existing agentic MLLM approaches for multi-step visual reasoning select actions based solely on the model's prior confidence, neglecting post-execution visual feedback. The paper identifies this as the imagination-action-observer (IAO) bias, quantified by a low cosine similarity (0.59 for visual search, 0.38 for navigation) between prior scores and actual observed outcomes, which causes suboptimal trajectory selection and degrades reasoning accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263462/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (up to 8K resolution) and a text question as input, and produces a text answer (multiple-choice letter or free-form) along with the action trajectory that led to it. Unusually, the input image is not static: during inference it is physically modified by tool calls (quadrant cropping, patch rearrangement, digit filling), so the model reasons over a sequence of evolving visual states rather than a single fixed tensor. The action space is task-dependent, ranging from discrete (four crop quadrants, four cardinal directions) to open-ended (arbitrary permutation sequences for jigsaw, coordinate-value pairs for sudoku).

## Method

V-ABS reformulates multi-step visual reasoning as a partially observable MDP solved by beam search, where each iteration runs a closed-loop thinker-actor-observer cycle: the thinker scores candidate actions via temperature-scaled softmax over aggregated log-likelihoods of positive tokens (Yes/True/Correct), the actor executes the top actions through external tool functions to update the visual state, and the observer re-scores the resulting states using the same token-aggregation scheme. An entropy-based adaptive weighting mechanism (a sigmoid of the thinker's Shannon entropy) dynamically balances prior and observer scores, grounded in the inverse-variance weighting principle from statistical estimation theory; when entropy falls below a threshold δ, the observer call is skipped entirely for efficiency. A large-scale SFT dataset of ~80k binary verification pairs further sharpens the prior policy, which the paper shows is critical for open-ended action spaces (jigsaw: 7.8%→81.2%) but marginal for discrete ones (V*: 90.5%→91.1%).

## Results

On the fine-grained visual search benchmarks, Qwen2.5-VL + V-ABS achieves 91.1% overall accuracy on V*, 79.0% on HR-Bench-4K, and 77.5% on HR-Bench-8K, yielding absolute improvements of +15.2%, +13.5%, and +15.5% over the base model. On visual navigation, Qwen3-VL + V-ABS boosts VisuoThink accuracy by +29.9% (L3), +46.8% (L4), and +30.6% (L5), and reaches 65.0% on TIR-Bench Maze (vs. 17.5% baseline). Across all eight benchmarks, V-ABS delivers an average improvement of 19.7% on Qwen3-VL-8B and 13.9% on InternVL3-8B, with consistent gains on GPT-4o as well.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V-ABS outperforms all four agentic visual-search baselines on Qwen2.5-VL: it beats ZoomEye (89.0% → 91.1% on V*, 68.4% → 77.5% on HR-8K), DeepEyes V2 (81.8% → 91.1% on V*, 73.8% → 77.5% on HR-8K), Pixel-Reasoner (84.3% → 91.1% on V*, 66.9% → 77.5% on HR-8K), and Thyme (82.2% → 91.1% on V*, 72.0% → 77.5% on HR-8K). Against standard test-time scaling methods, V-ABS surpasses both Beam Search (84.3% → 90.5% on V*, 75.0% → 78.0% on HR-8K) and MCTS (80.1% → 90.5% on V*, 70.5% → 78.0% on HR-8K) on Qwen3-VL, with the gap widening on higher-resolution benchmarks. On visual navigation, GPT-4o + V-ABS (94.9/74.1/69.7 on VisuoThink L3/L4/L5) clearly dominates GPT-4o + VisuoThink tree search (93.8/61.0/49.0), particularly at higher difficulty levels where the observer feedback prunes fatal trajectories. The paper also reports that V-ABS's gains are consistent across open-source (Qwen3-VL, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3) and proprietary (GPT-4o) backbones, confirming the framework's model-agnostic nature.

## Relation to Prior Work

V-ABS extends classical beam search (Wiseman & Rush, 2016) from a purely textual, open-loop decoding strategy into a closed-loop system that physically updates the visual state via tool execution and verifies the outcome before committing to a trajectory. It departs from prior agentic visual reasoning methods (ZoomEye, DeepEyes, Pixel Reasoner) that invoke cropping or zooming tools but select actions based solely on initial priors without post-execution feedback, and from test-time scaling approaches (ToT, MCTS, standard beam search) that operate in the model's internal latent space rather than the physical visual action space. The key conceptual contribution is formalizing the misalignment between prior confidence and observed utility as the IAO bias and addressing it with an entropy-based adaptive weighting mechanism grounded in inverse-variance estimation theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework is model-agnostic and plug-and-play, delivering consistent gains across open-source (Qwen3-VL, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3) and proprietary (GPT-4o) backbones across eight diverse benchmarks, making it a practical inference-time technique for any MLLM pipeline. The finding that SFT is indispensable for open-ended action spaces (Jigsaw: 7.8%→81.2%) but marginal for discrete ones (V*: 90.5%→91.1%) provides a clear design principle for when to invest in training versus relying on inference-time verification. Open questions include whether the thinker-actor-observer loop can be further optimized via reinforcement learning (as the authors note), whether the approach extends to video or 3D reasoning, and whether the IAO bias persists or diminishes as base models improve.
