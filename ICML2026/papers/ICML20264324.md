## Problem

Existing editing-based concept erasure methods for diffusion models rely on additive parameter updates (W + Δ), which simultaneously perturb neuron magnitude, direction, and inter-neuron angular geometry. This entanglement causes imprecise erasure of target concepts and collateral damage to non-target concepts, as the angular geometry that encodes overall generative capacity is disrupted. The paper identifies this geometric limitation as the core bottleneck preventing editing-based methods from matching the precision and preservation quality of training-based approaches.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the key and value projection matrices (W_k, W_v) in the cross-attention layers of a pretrained diffusion model (SD v1.4 or FLUX.1 dev), taking as input the CLIP text embeddings of target concepts to erase, semantically similar anchor concepts, and a retain set of non-target concepts. A precomputed global preservation prior K_0 (the expected outer product of token embeddings over COCO-30k) is incorporated offline. The output is a single layer-wise orthogonal matrix P applied multiplicatively to W, yielding modified model weights in one closed-form step with no iterative optimization.

## Method

OCE reformulates concept erasure as a multiplicative orthogonal transformation W* = PW instead of an additive perturbation, motivated by the geometric finding that concept semantics are encoded in neuron direction while generative capacity depends on angular geometry. The optimal P is obtained by solving a classical orthogonal Procrustes problem: constructing a cross-covariance matrix M from the erasure, anchor, and retain embeddings, performing its SVD, and setting P = UV^⊤. For multi-concept erasure, the vector-wise alignment objective is lifted to a subspace-level suppression objective that pushes the target subspace away from the orthogonal complement of the anchor subspace, mitigating conflicting constraints among many erased concepts. The design is intentionally asymmetric: erasure operates at the subspace level for scalability, while preservation remains vector-wise for fine-grained control over individual non-target concepts. This yields a unified closed-form solution that erases up to 100 concepts in 4.3 seconds on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 object erasure, OCE achieves an average Acc_e of 4.61% (lower is better) and Acc_s of 98.68% (higher is better), yielding the best harmonic score H_o of 97.01%, compared to the strongest baseline MACE (Acc_e 8.32%, H_o 94.23%). For large-scale multi-concept erasure of 100 celebrities, OCE reaches Acc_e of 3.44% with Acc_s of 94.42% in only 4.3 s on a single A100 GPU, whereas MACE requires 1800 s and achieves Acc_e of 8.02%. On artistic style erasure, OCE attains the best FID (7.15 for Van Gogh) while maintaining competitive CLIP scores (21.08), outperforming ESD which erases more aggressively (CS 20.10) but degrades FID to 13.60.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCE outperforms all 13 baselines on the primary CIFAR-10 object erasure task, reducing average Acc_e from 8.32% (MACE, the strongest prior method) to 4.61% while preserving Acc_s at 98.68% (nearly identical to the original model's 98.69%). Against SPEED, the closest editing-based competitor, OCE achieves 4.7× lower Acc_e (4.61% vs 21.82%) and a 9.85-point higher H_o (97.01 vs 87.16). In the 100-celebrity multi-concept setting, OCE beats MACE by 4.58 points on Acc_e (3.44% vs 8.02%) and 2.82 points on Acc_s (94.42% vs 91.60%), while running 419× faster (4.3 s vs 1800 s); it also edges out SPEED (Acc_e 3.44% vs 5.21%, Acc_s 94.42% vs 92.67%). On artistic style erasure, OCE achieves the best FID (7.15 for Van Gogh) among all methods, whereas ESD erases more aggressively (CS 20.10 vs 21.08) but degrades FID to 13.60. Under adversarial implicit-concept erasure (Table 4), OCE with adversarial training matches CPE on MMA (0.01) and Ring-A-Bell (0.00) while achieving a substantially lower FID (39.73 vs 48.23), and outperforms all editing-based baselines (UCE, RECE, SPEED) on I2P ASR (0.05 vs 0.24/0.14/0.10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the additive-update paradigm in editing-based concept erasure (exemplified by UCE, which optimizes W + Δ via least squares) by reformulating erasure as a multiplicative orthogonal transformation QW, grounded in a geometric analysis showing that concept semantics reside in neuron directions while generation quality depends on inter-neuron angular geometry. The orthogonal transformation framework draws on Orthogonal Fine-Tuning (OFT) and hyperspherical energy literature, while the closed-form solution leverages the classical Procrustes problem. For multi-concept erasure, the paper extends vector-wise alignment to a subspace-level projection objective, building on the mass-erasure setting established by MACE and SPEED but with a structured subspace suppression formulation that mitigates cross-concept interference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OCE provides a principled geometric explanation for why additive updates fail at concept erasure (entangling direction, magnitude, and angular geometry) and offers a single-step closed-form solution that erases up to 100 concepts in 4.3 seconds, making large-scale deployment practical. The method transfers to DiT-based architectures (FLUX) and integrates with adversarial editing for robustness. Open questions remain around erasing implicit relational or compositional concepts, the SVD computational overhead for very large models, and whether subspace-level erasure can achieve the fine-grained precision needed for editing tasks where exact anchor matching matters.
