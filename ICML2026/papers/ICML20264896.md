## Problem

Existing lipid-binding predictors treat unlabeled pockets as true negatives, introducing systematic false-negative bias because absence of experimental evidence does not imply non-binding. Additionally, pocket-level lipid-binding prediction remains underexplored relative to residue- or sequence-level approaches, and most methods provide only binary detection without characterizing which lipid categories are involved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264896/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LipoPU takes a full-length protein sequence (≤1024 residues, 20 standard amino acids) with annotated pocket residue positions as input. A frozen ESM-2 (650M) encoder produces 1280-dimensional residue embeddings, from which only pocket-residue embeddings are selected. The model outputs 8 lipid-category scores (FA, GL, GP, SP, ST, PR, SL, PK) via a sigmoid MLP; the binary lipid-binding score is derived as the maximum over these 8 category scores, and the learned attention weights over pocket residues provide residue-level interpretability.

## Method

LipoPU aggregates variable-length pocket residue embeddings into a fixed-dimensional representation using attention-based MIL pooling (tanh projection → softmax attention → weighted sum), which is permutation-invariant and accommodates ambiguous pocket boundaries. Training uses a ranking-based positive-unlabeled (PU) objective: the standard PU risk is reformulated with a pairwise ranking surrogate that encourages observed positives to be scored above unlabeled pockets rather than forcing unlabeled pockets to low absolute scores, extended to the multi-label setting with per-category non-negative risk clipping. A positive-guaranteed batch sampler enforces a minimum number of positive instances per mini-batch to stabilize ranking optimization under the extreme ~4.6% positive rate. The architecture is lightweight (frozen ESM-2 → MIL pooling → 3-layer MLP with LayerNorm/GELU/dropout), and the two prediction tasks (binary detection and multi-label category prediction) are coupled through the shared category-score predictor.

## Results

On the easy test set, LipoPU achieves Binary ROC-AUC of 0.973 and Binary PR-AUC of 0.839 for lipid-binding detection, and Micro ROC-AUC of 0.984 and Micro PR-AUC of 0.766 for lipid category prediction, substantially outperforming supervised BCE baselines (Binary PR-AUC 0.630–0.651; Micro PR-AUC 0.453–0.490). On the harder remote-homology test set (<30% sequence identity), LipoPU maintains Binary ROC-AUC of 0.914 and Binary PR-AUC of 0.650, while supervised baselines drop to 0.557–0.558 Binary PR-AUC. LipoPU also achieves >0.98 precision at Top-100 on both test sets, indicating strong prioritization of true lipid-binding pockets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against supervised BCE baselines (which treat unlabeled pockets as negatives), LipoPU wins on every metric across both easy and hard test sets, with the largest gains in PR-AUC: +0.19–0.21 Binary PR-AUC and +0.28–0.31 Micro PR-AUC on the easy set, and +0.09 Binary PR-AUC and +0.08 Micro PR-AUC on the hard set. The positive-guaranteed batch sampler alone yields only marginal improvements over unsampled BCE, confirming that the PU ranking objective—not sampling—is the primary source of gain. Against SLiPP, the only prior pocket-level method, LipoPU achieves 0.960 vs. 0.742 Binary ROC-AUC and 0.792 vs. 0.239 Binary PR-AUC on the easy-fpocket set (roughly 3× higher PR-AUC), and 0.881 vs. 0.726 ROC-AUC and 0.505 vs. 0.264 PR-AUC on the hard-fpocket set. LipoPU additionally supports multi-label lipid category prediction (Micro ROC-AUC 0.985 easy / 0.907 hard), a capability SLiPP does not provide. A supplementary supervised pairwise-ranking baseline (treating unlabeled as negatives but using a ranking loss) performs worse than even pointwise weighted BCE, further isolating the PU formulation as the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

LipoPU sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) PU learning for biological prediction, where prior work (NIAPU, PUGO) has shown that treating unlabeled data as negative introduces systematic false-negative bias; (2) attention-based MIL pooling (Ilse et al., 2018) for set-level representation, here repurposed to handle ambiguous pocket boundaries; and (3) pocket-level molecular recognition, where SLiPP is the sole prior method but relies on handcrafted fpocket descriptors and fully supervised training. The paper departs from the dominant fully supervised paradigm by adopting a ranking-based PU objective (building on Kiryo et al.'s non-negative risk estimator and Tang/Zhapa-Camacho's ranking surrogates) and extends the prediction target from binary detection to multi-label LIPID MAPS category scoring, a capability absent in all prior pocket-level or sequence-level lipid-binding predictors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and underappreciated problem: lipid-binding annotations are a lower bound, and treating missing evidence as negative systematically suppresses discovery of novel lipid-protein interactions. By coupling PU learning with pocket-centric representation and multi-label category prediction, LipoPU provides a practical screening tool that can prioritize candidates for experimental validation at scale. Key limitations remain: performance degrades under remote homology and pocket-definition shift, rare lipid categories (SL, SP) have too few positives for stable estimation, and the annotation incompleteness that motivates PU learning also biases evaluation. Open questions include whether finer-grained lipid subclass labels (especially for polyketides) would improve category discrimination, and whether the PU framework can be extended to incorporate structural or evolutionary priors beyond ESM-2 embeddings.
