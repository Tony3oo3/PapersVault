## Problem

Online variational learning in state-space models requires sequentially updating both model parameters and the latent posterior as observations stream in, but existing methods either rely on computationally expensive inner regression problems at each step (Campbell et al., 2021) or lack theoretical guarantees for convergence. No prior work provides both a rigorous stochastic-approximation justification for the online variational objective and a practical algorithm that avoids per-step regression.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of scalar or vector observations y_t ∈ ℝ^{d_y} arriving one at a time, with no access to future data. At each step it maintains N i.i.d. Monte Carlo samples from the current variational filtering distribution (Gaussian in experiments) and produces updated model parameters θ, variational parameters φ, and the full backward-factorized smoothing distribution q_{0:t}^φ. In the air-quality experiment, missing sensor readings are handled by simply omitting the emission term from the per-step ELBO contribution, allowing the backward kernels to reconstruct latent states across blackout periods without imputation.

## Method

RMCVI (Recursive Monte Carlo Variational Inference) computes the ELBO and its gradient recursively via a Bellman-type decomposition (Proposition 4.1), where each conditional expectation is approximated by self-normalized importance sampling using the current variational filtering distribution q_t^φ as proposal for the backward kernel q_{t-1|t}^φ. The variational family uses a backward factorization with exponential-family marginals whose parameters are produced by a shared RNN (deterministic recursion a_t = A^φ(a_{t-1}, y_t)) and MLP mappings, while backward kernels are defined through potential functions ψ_t^φ = exp(⟨f̃^φ(x_t), T(x_{t-1})⟩) that analytically link the target and proposal distributions. This design eliminates the per-step regression problem of Campbell et al. (2021) and yields a ~5× speedup. Theoretically, the paper proves geometric ergodicity of an extended Markov chain (Z_t = (X_t, Y_t, h_t, u_t, v_t, a_t)) under uniform ergodicity and boundedness assumptions, establishing that the time-normalized ELBO converges almost surely to a well-defined COLBO objective whose gradient admits a Robbins–Monro stochastic approximation interpretation.

## Results

On the chaotic RNN benchmark (d=5, N=500 samples), RMCVI achieves 1-step smoothing RMSE of 8.9×10⁻² and filtering RMSE of 10.3×10⁻², matching Campbell et al. (2021) on filtering (10.3×10⁻²) while improving smoothing (8.9 vs 9.2×10⁻²), all at 1 ms per gradient step versus 4.8 ms for the regression-based baseline (~5× speedup). On the linear-Gaussian HMM (d=10, T=50,000), RMCVI achieves comparable parameter MAE to Campbell et al. while running approximately 4× faster with N=1000 importance samples. On the UCI Air-Quality dataset (8-dim observations, T≈9,300 steps, N=20 samples), RMCVI recovers the true latent signal during extended sensor-failure periods where the autoregressive LSTM baseline overfits the corrupted observations, and matches the LSTM's one-step-ahead prediction RMSE while outperforming Online Variational SMC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Campbell et al. (2021), RMCVI achieves comparable or slightly better accuracy (8.9 vs 9.2×10⁻² 1-step smoothing RMSE; identical 10.3×10⁻² filtering RMSE on the chaotic RNN; comparable parameter MAE on the linear-Gaussian HMM) while running 4–5× faster per gradient step (1 ms vs 4.8 ms), eliminating the per-step regression sub-problem that dominates the baseline's cost. Against the probabilistic online LSTM (DeepAR, Salinas et al. 2020) on the air-quality dataset, RMCVI matches one-step-ahead prediction RMSE across the five primary pollutants but clearly wins on the smoothing task: during artificial sensor-failure periods, RMCVI reconstructs the true latent signal via its backward variational kernels, whereas the purely autoregressive LSTM tracks the corrupted observations. Against Online Variational SMC (Mastrototaro & Olsson, 2024), RMCVI achieves lower cumulative prediction RMSE on the air-quality benchmark, combining the forecasting accuracy of autoregressive models with the smoothing advantage of variational inference at lower computational cost than particle-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two complementary threads: the backward-factorization variational family and recursive ELBO computation introduced by Campbell et al. (2021), and the stochastic-approximation / COLBO framework of Mastrototaro et al. (2025) that provides the theoretical objective for online variational learning. The key departure is replacing Campbell et al.'s per-step regression (inner optimization) for conditional expectations with self-normalized importance sampling over i.i.d. samples from the marginal variational distributions, which eliminates the regression class choice and yields a simpler, faster algorithm. The paper also supplies the missing theoretical justification—geometric ergodicity of an extended Markov chain—that prior online VI methods lacked, grounding the COLBO and its gradient in a law-of-large-numbers argument.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a 4–5× speedup over the leading regression-based online VI method with comparable statistical accuracy, making online variational smoothing feasible in real-time streaming settings (demonstrated on air-quality data with sensor blackouts). The theoretical contribution—establishing existence of the COLBO and its gradient via ergodicity—is important because prior online VI algorithms operated without such guarantees. Open questions include a rigorous bias analysis of the Monte Carlo updates (O(1/N) SNIS bias), an IWAE-style multi-sample extension for tighter bounds, and whether the geometric ergodicity assumptions hold for more complex (e.g., high-dimensional or non-Gaussian) variational families.
