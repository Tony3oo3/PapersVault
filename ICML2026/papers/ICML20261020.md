## Problem

GNNs face fundamental challenges with long-range interactions, oversquashing, and over-smoothing as depth increases. Existing energy-based GNN approaches rely on fixed, simple energy functionals (e.g., Dirichlet energy) and pure gradient flows, which converge slowly in ill-conditioned landscapes common in graph learning. The paper asks how to learn a task-driven energy function and combine its gradient descent with a tangential flow to enable effective signal propagation even in flat or poorly conditioned energy regions.

## Input / Output

TANGO takes a graph G=(V,E) with an initial node feature matrix H ∈ R^{n×d} and produces updated node embeddings after L forward-Euler steps of the learned dynamics. Internally, it computes a scalar graph energy V_G ≥ 0 from per-node energy scores, a normalized energy gradient, and a tangential direction orthogonal to that gradient. The final node embeddings feed into standard readout heads for node classification, graph classification, or graph regression, with all parameters (two sub-networks plus scalar gates α_G, β_G) trained end-to-end via the downstream supervised loss.

## Method

TANGO decomposes node feature evolution into two orthogonal flows: an energy gradient descent term driven by a learned Lyapunov function V_G (parameterized by an ENERGYGNN + MLP), and a tangential term obtained by projecting a learned update M(H) (from a TANGENTGNN) onto the orthogonal complement of the normalized energy gradient. The continuous-time ODE dH/dt = −α_G ∇_H V_G + β_G T_{V_G} is discretized via forward Euler, with α_G ≥ 0 (sigmoid-gated) ensuring Lyapunov dissipation and β_G ∈ R (unconstrained) controlling tangential motion along level sets. The key theoretical insight is that the tangential component enables feature evolution in flat energy landscapes (where pure gradient flow stalls) and can approximate Newton-like directions, yielding quadratic convergence that overcomes the ill-conditioning caused by graph bottlenecks and oversquashing. The framework is backbone-agnostic, instantiated with GCN, GIN, GatedGCN, or GPS, and all energy and tangential parameters are optimized solely through the downstream task loss rather than a separate energy or generative objective.

## Results

On the Graph Property Prediction benchmark (Table 1), TANGO achieves the best log₁₀(MSE) across all three long-range tasks with every backbone variant: TANGO_GPS reaches −0.977 (Diameter), −5.526 (SSSP), and −2.146 (Eccentricity), outperforming the strongest prior DE-GNN (PH-DGN) by 0.4, 1.2, and 1.2 points respectively. On the five Dwivedi et al. benchmarks (Table 2), TANGO_GPS attains 0.062 MAE on ZINC-12k, 98.20% on MNIST, 75.78% on CIFAR-10, 87.18% on PATTERN, and 80.11% on CLUSTER, matching or exceeding the GRIT and GPS baselines. On the LRGB Peptides tasks (Table 3), TANGO_GPS scores 70.21 AP on Peptides-func and 0.2422 MAE on Peptides-struct, slightly surpassing PH-DGN (70.12 AP / 0.2465 MAE) under the same 500K parameter budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TANGO dominates the Graph Property Prediction benchmark: TANGO_GPS beats the best prior DE-GNN (PH-DGN) by 0.44 on Diameter, 1.23 on SSSP, and 1.21 on Eccentricity, and surpasses the transformer baseline GPS by 0.47, 1.93, and 2.75 points on the same tasks. Even the weakest TANGO variant (TANGO_GCN) outperforms all MPNN baselines on Eccentricity (−1.63 vs. best MPNN 0.76 for GCNII). On the Dwivedi et al. suite, TANGO_GPS is within 0.003 MAE of GRIT on ZINC-12k (0.062 vs. 0.059) and within 0.01% on PATTERN (87.18 vs. 87.20), while clearly exceeding its GPS backbone (e.g., 75.78% vs. 72.30% on CIFAR-10). On LRGB Peptides, TANGO_GPS edges out PH-DGN by 0.09 AP on Peptides-func and 0.004 MAE on Peptides-struct, and TANGO_GCN (a simple message-passing backbone) already reaches 0.2432 MAE on Peptides-struct, competitive with transformer-based methods that use Laplacian positional encodings. TANGO consistently improves its own backbone across all 15 benchmarks, with the largest relative gains on long-range and bottleneck-sensitive tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

TANGO extends the line of work interpreting GNN layers as discretizations of continuous dynamical systems (GRAND, PDE-GCN, DGC, A-DGN, PH-DGN) by introducing a learned, task-driven Lyapunov energy function rather than relying on fixed energies like the Dirichlet energy used in prior diffusion-based GNNs. The key architectural departure is the decomposition of feature updates into an energy-gradient-descent component and a learned tangential flow projected to be orthogonal to the energy gradient, a mechanism not present in prior energy-based or Hamiltonian GNN formulations. Conceptually, the tangential flow draws on optimization theory (quasi-Newton directions, conjugate gradients) to provide a learned corrective component that can approximate second-order information, while the Lyapunov framework borrows from control-theoretic stability guarantees applied to neural ODEs (Kang et al., Rodriguez et al.). Unlike energy-based generative models, TANGO's energy is shaped entirely by downstream supervised losses, making it a task-driven potential rather than a data-likelihood surrogate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TANGO offers a principled, Lyapunov-guaranteed stability mechanism for deep GNNs that is architecture-agnostic and plug-and-play across backbones, with the tangential flow providing a concrete mechanism to overcome the ill-conditioning that causes oversquashing in bottleneck graphs. The empirical gains on long-range benchmarks (e.g., >1.2 log-MSE improvement over PH-DGN on Eccentricity) are substantial and consistent across four backbone families, suggesting the energy-tangential decomposition is a broadly useful inductive bias. However, the authors explicitly frame oversquashing alleviation as a capacity statement rather than a guarantee, and the learned energy landscape lacks post-hoc interpretability. Open questions include whether higher-order differential operators in the tangential flow, regularization of the learned energy, or tangential flows in non-graph domains can further unlock the framework's potential.
