## Problem

Existing hypergraph condensation methods like HG-Cond use a decoupled architecture where the structure generator is pre-trained separately and frozen during refinement, causing misalignment between synthesized hyperedges and evolving condensed features. Additionally, their trajectory-based optimization requires repetitive HNN retraining, incurring prohibitive computational and memory overhead that prevents scaling to large hypergraphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262583/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a large-scale attributed hypergraph (node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d}, incidence matrix H ∈ R^{N×M}, and one-hot labels Y ∈ R^{N×C}) and produces a compact synthetic hypergraph S = (X', H', Y') with N' ≪ N and M' ≪ M nodes and hyperedges. The condensed labels Y' are pre-defined to match the original class distribution, while X' and H' are jointly optimized. The condensed hypergraph is then used to train HNNs from scratch, targeting performance comparable to training on the full original data.

## Method

AHGCDD comprises three modules: (1) an HKPR-based node initialization that applies Poisson-weighted multi-step diffusion on the hypergraph Laplacian to encode multi-scale structural information into features, then samples and mean-pools per class to initialize condensed nodes; (2) an anchor-guided hyperedge generation where each synthetic node sequentially serves as an anchor, a shared MLP computes pairwise feature-level association scores to all other nodes, and learnable per-anchor thresholds sparsify the resulting incidence vectors, enabling end-to-end joint optimization of features and structure without costly pre-training; (3) a dual-level discrimination loss combining coarse-grained class-prototype alignment (equivalent to minimizing MMD over normalized structure-enriched features) with fine-grained instance-level contrastive loss (bounding the mis-ranking probability via Markov's inequality), weighted by a cosine schedule that shifts from global distribution matching to local boundary refinement, thereby eliminating all repetitive HNN training during condensation.

## Results

AHGCDD achieves the best node classification accuracy across all six hypergraph benchmarks at every condensation ratio, with condensed data closely approaching full-data performance (e.g., 76.48% vs. 77.90% on Cora at 1%, 88.80% vs. 90.75% on DBLP-CA at 0.5%). On the large-scale Walmart dataset (0.5% ratio), AHGCDD reaches 67.92% accuracy, outperforming HG-Cond (54.13%) by over 13 percentage points. On the hyperedge-dense Yelp dataset, HG-Cond and HG-Cond-NHL suffer out-of-memory failures, while AHGCDD successfully condenses and achieves 26.80% accuracy. In terms of efficiency, AHGCDD achieves 28×–144× speedup over HG-Cond (e.g., 27.8s vs. 1111.6s on MAG-PM) with substantially lower GPU memory (<3 GB vs. >10 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AHGCDD consistently outperforms all four baseline categories across every dataset and condensation ratio. Against the traditional coreset methods (Random, Herding, K-Center), AHGCDD gains 20–40 percentage points, confirming that simple node selection is insufficient for hypergraph condensation. The structure-free graph condensation method HGCPA (GCPA extended to hypergraphs) performs markedly worse than dedicated HGC methods, e.g., 63.46% vs. 76.48% on Cora (1%) and 48.61% vs. 67.92% on Walmart (0.5%), indicating that structure-free GC does not generalize well to higher-order interactions. Among hypergraph condensation baselines, HG-Cond-NHL (which fixes the pre-trained NHL structure) sometimes matches or slightly exceeds HG-Cond, but both suffer from the decoupled architecture: HG-Cond exhibits large run-to-run variance and OOM on Yelp, while AHGCDD achieves 26.80% on Yelp where both HG-Cond variants fail entirely. The largest absolute gains over HG-Cond appear on Walmart (+13.79 pp at 0.5%) and MAG-PM (+4.48 pp at 0.25%), where the joint optimization of features and structure via anchor-guided generation proves most critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on HG-Cond (Gong et al., 2025), the first hypergraph condensation framework, which employed a decoupled paradigm: a Neural Hyperedge Linker (NHL) pre-trained via variational inference, followed by a Gradient-Parameter Synergistic Matching (GPSM) amelioration stage. AHGCDD departs from this by replacing the decoupled architecture with an anchor-guided hyperedge generation scheme that jointly optimizes condensed features and structure end-to-end, and by substituting the costly trajectory/gradient matching objective with a dual-level discrimination loss (coarse-grained prototype alignment + fine-grained contrastive discrimination). The HKPR-based node initialization adapts Chung's (2007) heat-kernel PageRank to the hypergraph setting, providing a spectral low-pass filtering mechanism for multi-scale structural aggregation that feeds into the feature-driven hyperedge synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 28×–144× speedup and reduced memory footprint make hypergraph condensation practically viable on large-scale datasets (e.g., Yelp with 679K hyperedges) where prior methods hit OOM, opening the door to HNN training on previously intractable hypergraph data. The theoretical grounding—interpreting the coarse-grained loss as MMD minimization and bounding the mis-ranking probability via the fine-grained loss—provides a principled, HNN-free optimization target that could inspire similar training-free objectives in other condensation settings. Open questions include whether the anchor-guided scheme generalizes to heterogeneous or dynamic hypergraphs, how the method scales beyond the tested 153K-node regime, and whether the Poisson diffusion parameter λ can be learned rather than hand-tuned.
