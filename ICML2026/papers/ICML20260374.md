## Problem

InfoNCE underlies modern contrastive and multimodal representation learning, yet its geometric mechanisms remain poorly characterized beyond the alignment-uniformity decomposition. In particular, it is unclear what population-level energy InfoNCE induces, how its geometry should be understood at the level of representation distributions, and why strong pairwise alignment in CLIP-style models coexists with a persistent modality gap for which no first-principles mechanistic explanation existed.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on probability measures (representation laws and positive-pair laws) pushed forward onto a fixed compact Riemannian manifold Z (e.g., the hypersphere S^{n-1}) equipped with a volume measure, together with an exponential similarity kernel parameterized by temperature τ. It produces closed-form deterministic population energies whose gradients match the stochastic InfoNCE gradient in the large-batch limit, a unique Gibbs equilibrium in the unimodal regime, and a cross-coupled barrier landscape in the multimodal regime. Empirically, the analysis is validated on pretrained OpenCLIP checkpoints (RN50, ViT-B-16) evaluated on MS-COCO val2017 using retrieval metrics alongside geometric diagnostics (centroid gap, energy distance between modality marginals).

## Method

The paper develops a measure-theoretic framework in which encoders push data distributions forward onto a fixed embedding manifold, and the InfoNCE softmax denominator converges in the large-batch limit to a population partition field—a kernel-averaged functional of the current representation law. This yields value- and gradient-consistency theorems linking the stochastic objective to explicit deterministic energies. The intrinsic geometry then bifurcates: in the unimodal case, the free-energy functional is strictly convex and admits a unique Gibbs equilibrium, reinterpreting 'uniformity' as entropy-driven dispersion within the alignment basin; in the symmetric multimodal case, a negative symmetric KL divergence coupling makes each modality's marginal act as an occupied-mass barrier in the other's effective landscape, producing extremal (non-Gibbs) best responses and a persistent modality gap under conditional heterogeneity. A sharp-kernel (low-temperature) regime controls the KDE mismatch between smoothed and raw densities, connecting the intrinsic analysis back to parametric encoder training.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with experiments serving as validation of geometric predictions rather than method comparisons. On MS-COCO (val2017), the observational study across frozen OpenCLIP checkpoints (RN50, ViT-B-16) shows that models with similar average Recall@1 can exhibit substantially different centroid gaps ‖μ_I − μ_T‖ and energy distances between image and text marginals, confirming that retrieval quality does not determine modality gap. In the interventional study on train2017, replacing captions with same-category alternatives at probability p consistently degrades retrieval and enlarges the centroid gap for both backbones. Synthetic experiments confirm large-batch gradient consistency (alignment rises, relative error → 0 as N grows), unimodal Gibbs concentration (cap-mass increases monotonically as τ ↓), and structural modality gap (increasing angular misalignment σ_mis broadens the diagonal coupling and increases symmetric KL divergence).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new method to benchmark against competing approaches; it is a theoretical analysis of the geometric structure induced by InfoNCE. The 'comparisons' are between theoretical predictions and empirical observations: (i) the large-batch deterministic energy predicted by Thm. 3.1/4.1 matches finite-batch gradients as N increases; (ii) the unique Gibbs equilibrium predicted by Prop. 3.1 is reproduced by particle-based optimization on S²; (iii) the barrier-driven co-adaptation and persistent modality gap predicted by Prop. 4.1 are observed both in controlled synthetic latent-angle experiments and in pretrained OpenCLIP models on MS-COCO. The key empirical finding is that the modality gap is a structural consequence of the symmetric InfoNCE objective's negative symmetric KL coupling, not an optimization artifact: weakening cross-modal compatibility (via same-category caption corruption) systematically enlarges the gap even when coarse semantic relatedness is preserved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the canonical alignment-uniformity decomposition (Wang & Isola, 2020) by shifting the analytical lens from pointwise discrimination on the hypersphere to population-level measure geometry on a fixed embedding manifold. It builds on the InfoNCE objective (Oord et al., 2018) and CLIP-style symmetric training (Radford et al., 2021), but provides a first-principles derivation of the deterministic energy landscapes these objectives induce in the large-batch limit, rather than characterizing optimal critics via density ratios or recovery guarantees via identifiability. The key conceptual departure is showing that the symmetric multimodal objective is not merely the average of two unimodal geometries: a negative symmetric KL divergence coupling emerges that has no unimodal analogue, making the modality gap a structural consequence of the objective rather than an optimization artifact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic geometric explanation for the modality gap, showing it arises generically from cross-modal conditional heterogeneity interacting with a persistent repulsive divergence term, not from insufficient training or suboptimal optimization. This reframing implies that closing the gap requires objectives that explicitly regulate cross-modal marginal divergence (e.g., the Cauchy-Schwarz divergence approach of Yin et al., 2026), not just better pairwise alignment. Limitations include the large-batch and sharp-kernel assumptions needed for the intrinsic connection, the technical density-floor constraint in the multimodal analysis, and empirical validation primarily on frozen pretrained models and synthetic data rather than from-scratch training. Open questions include whether the barrier-driven co-adaptation picture persists under finite-batch dynamics, non-compact manifolds, or asymmetric temperature schedules, and whether the predicted knife-edge compatibility condition for exact marginal matching can be leveraged into practical training objectives.
