## Problem

Existing flow matching and diffusion-based generative models for trajectory simulation rely on uninformed Gaussian priors, inducing a large transport cost between prior and target distributions that forces complex vector fields and high numbers of function evaluations during inference. In conditional trajectory simulation, rich information about future dynamics is already available through observed initial frames, yet current approaches largely fail to exploit this information when constructing the prior. STFlow addresses this gap by incorporating conditioning information directly into the generative prior via data-dependent couplings.

## Input / Output

The model takes c observed initial frames of N particles in d-dimensional space along with node features (position, velocity, acceleration magnitudes) and a distance-based graph connectivity, and produces f = T−c future trajectory frames as probabilistic samples. Rather than predicting positions directly, STFlow predicts a vector field of velocities, and inference requires only 5 Euler integration steps. Trajectories are fixed-length per dataset, and the model supports varying observed window sizes c at inference without retraining, enabling unconditional generation, autoregressive stepping, or inpainting.

## Method

STFlow constructs an informed random-walk prior where the velocity μ and variance σ_o are estimated from each node's observed initial frames, so that prior samples naturally obey continuity and inertia while remaining conditionally independent of the target. The vector field v_θ is parameterized by alternating Spatial Message Passing layers (EGCL-based, permutation-equivariant, with iteratively updated edge features) and Temporal Mixing layers (a 1D UNet with weight sharing along the time axis for time-shift equivariance and multi-scale local dynamics). Training uses conditional flow matching with linear Gaussian probability paths and an MSE loss on the vector field, with τ sampled from U[0,1]^0.5 to skew toward the data end. This data-dependent coupling reduces the transport cost from ~1.51 to ~0.22 (measured as mean ||u||), enabling accurate simulation with only 5 NFEs and linear scaling in both particle count and trajectory length.

## Results

On N-body systems, STFlow achieves the lowest ADE and FDE across all three benchmarks (Charged, Spring, Gravity), with an average 48.1% ADE and 56.9% FDE improvement over the previous best model LaM-SLidE, while using only 5 NFEs (2× fewer than LaM-SLidE, 200× fewer than GeoTDM). On MD17 molecular dynamics, STFlow produces the lowest errors on 7 of 8 molecules, with an average 15.6% ADE and 22.6% FDE reduction over LaM-SLidE. On the NBA human trajectory forecasting benchmark, STFlow achieves the lowest mean ADE (1.51 m) and FDE (2.59 m) in setup 2, though it trails domain-specific methods on the min₂₀ diversity metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STFlow outperforms all 11 baselines on the N-body and MD17 benchmarks. Against the closest probabilistic competitor LaM-SLidE, STFlow reduces FDE by 56.9% on N-body (e.g., 0.231 vs 0.406 on Gravity) and by 22.6% on MD17, while using half the NFEs. GeoTDM, the prior state-of-the-art diffusion model, is beaten by a wide margin (e.g., 0.107 vs 0.256 ADE on Gravity) and requires 200× more function evaluations. Deterministic baselines (TFN, SE(3)-Tr, EGNN, EqMotion) are consistently outperformed, with EGNN being the strongest point-prediction method but still trailing STFlow by 2–3× in FDE. On the NBA benchmark, STFlow achieves the best mean₂₀ ADE/FDE (1.51/2.59 m) in setup 2, beating GroupNet (2.84/5.15), LED (3.83/6.03), and MoFlow (2.38/4.61), but trails MoFlow (0.71/0.86) and LED (0.81/1.10) on the min₂₀ metric, indicating STFlow prioritizes fidelity over diversity in the human trajectory setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

STFlow extends the flow matching framework (Liu et al., 2023; Lipman et al., 2023) to conditional geometric trajectory simulation by incorporating data-dependent couplings (Albergo et al., 2024) into the prior construction. Rather than the standard Gaussian prior used in diffusion and flow-matching trajectory models (GeoTDM, LaM-SLidE, MoFlow), STFlow constructs an informed random-walk prior whose parameters (mean velocity, variance) are estimated from the observed conditioning frames, directly reducing the transport cost between prior and target. The architecture combines the EGCL spatial message-passing layer (Satorras et al., 2021) with a weight-shared 1D UNet temporal mixer (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), enforcing permutation and time-shift equivariance while scaling linearly in both particle count and trajectory length.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that conditioning information in the prior reduces inference to 5 Euler steps (vs. 1000+ for GeoTDM) while simultaneously improving accuracy, making probabilistic trajectory simulation feasible at scale for long horizons and large N-body systems. The ablations confirm the informed prior is a critical component (85% FDE increase without coupling), and the linear scaling in trajectory length opens the door to simulating much longer dynamics than prior one-shot methods. Open questions include whether the random-walk prior generalizes to systems with strong periodicity or hard constraints, how to handle variable-length trajectories, and the observed fidelity-over-diversity trade-off in human trajectory forecasting that limits min-k metrics relative to domain-specific baselines.
