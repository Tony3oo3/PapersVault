## Problem

Existing personalized federated LoRA methods address client-side statistical heterogeneity but treat the model as a monolithic unit under a 'Flat-Model Assumption,' ignoring that LLM layers exhibit distinct functional roles (shallow layers encode general linguistic features, deep layers encode task-specific semantics). The paper identifies that the optimal parameter-sharing depth is not an architectural constant but is dynamically coupled to client data similarity, and no prior work has systematically examined how LoRA parameters should be aggregated differentially across Transformer depth based on client relationships.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen LLM backbone (RoBERTa-Large 355M or LLaMA-2-7B 8-bit), per-client LoRA adapters (rank-r A and B matrices on query/value projections), and private client datasets partitioned under Dirichlet or task-heterogeneous distributions. It produces, for each client and each layer, a composite LoRA mapping that blends a Cluster Expert (averaged over structurally similar peers) with an External Expert (averaged over all other clients) via a per-layer learnable scalar λ_{l,k} ∈ [0,1], yielding task-specific predictions (accuracy for NLU) or generated text (ROUGE-1 for NLG) with only ~0.02% additional trainable parameters beyond standard LoRA.

## Method

FedTreeLoRA constructs a tree-structured aggregation hierarchy in three stages. First, a warmup phase computes a Global Distance Matrix from LoRA B matrices, and Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering builds a binary merge tree that encodes a spectrum from universal sharing to full personalization, ensuring topological consistency across layers. Second, for each Transformer layer l, the optimal cluster count c_l* is selected via Silhouette Coefficient under a monotonicity constraint (c_l ≥ c_{l-1}) and a bounded search window K, so that aggregation granularity can only increase (specialize) with depth. Third, a Cluster-External Expert mechanism synthesizes each client's effective LoRA by mixing the peer-cluster average with the external-client average through a learnable scalar, while only the Cluster Expert and λ are updated during local training. The key differentiator from flat-clustering baselines like FedLEASE is that the tree enforces structural coherence (clients separated at shallow layers remain specialized at deeper layers) and lets the sharing boundary shift dynamically per layer based on measured functional similarity, rather than applying a single uniform grouping across all layers.

## Results

On the GLUE NLU benchmarks (RoBERTa-Large, non-IID α=0.5, 20 clients), FedTreeLoRA achieves an average accuracy of 92.36%, outperforming the strongest baseline FedLEASE (91.19%) by +1.17 points and the general FedAvg-based FedIT (87.20%) by +5.16 points. On the FLAN NLG benchmarks (LLaMA-2-7B, 8 clients, task-heterogeneous), FedTreeLoRA reaches an average ROUGE-1 of 63.19, surpassing FedALT (61.77) and FedLEASE (61.71) by over 1.4 points. Notably, FedTreeLoRA achieves these gains with only 0.1107% trainable parameters (rank-4 LoRA plus negligible scalar mixing coefficients), matching the parameter budget of FedIT while far exceeding its accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedTreeLoRA outperforms all six baselines on every task in both NLU and NLG evaluations. Against the strongest flat-clustering baseline FedLEASE (NeurIPS 2025), FedTreeLoRA gains +1.17 average accuracy on GLUE and +1.48 ROUGE-1 on FLAN, while using fewer trainable parameters (0.1107% vs. 0.1521% on NLU). The dual-module methods FedDPA (+2.17) and FedALT (+2.30) are consistently outperformed, as is the selective-aggregation method FedSA (+2.32). The general FedAvg-based FedIT and the communication-efficient FFA-LoRA trail by 4.96–5.16 points on average, confirming that neither uniform global aggregation nor freezing the A matrices can match layer-wise adaptive sharing. The paper further shows via ablation that even the Isolationist variant (Cluster Expert only, no External Expert) still surpasses FedLEASE, attributing the primary gain to the tree-structured topological alignment rather than the specific mixing mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) as the parameter-efficient fine-tuning substrate and extends the personalized federated LoRA line (FedSA, FedDPA, FedALT, FedLEASE) by challenging what the authors term the 'Flat-Model Assumption'—the implicit treatment of all LoRA layers as a monolithic sharing unit. The key conceptual departure is introducing 'vertical heterogeneity' (layer-wise functional specialization in Transformers) as an orthogonal axis to the well-studied horizontal (statistical) heterogeneity, and coupling the two through a tree-structured aggregation hierarchy built via agglomerative clustering on LoRA B-matrix distances. Unlike FedLEASE's flat client clustering, FedTreeLoRA enforces a global topological skeleton so that layer-wise partitions are nested cuts of a single tree, preventing the 'topological incoherence' that would arise from independent per-layer clustering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, empirically validated answer to a question that federated LLM practitioners face but have not formally addressed: at which depth should clients stop sharing parameters, and how does that boundary depend on data similarity? The finding that the tree structure alone (Isolationist ablation) already outperforms the best flat-clustering baseline suggests that topological precision in peer matching is more valuable than complex interaction mechanisms, which has practical implications for communication-efficient federated systems. Limitations include evaluation on at most 40 clients and 7B-scale models, a standard O(1/√T) convergence guarantee that does not capture the tree structure's effect on the constant, and the assumption that the tree topology (built during warmup) remains valid throughout training. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other PEFT methods (e.g., adapters, prefix tuning), how the tree should be maintained under non-stationary client data, and whether the layer-wise Silhouette-based selection generalizes to models with heterogeneous layer architectures (e.g., MoE layers).
