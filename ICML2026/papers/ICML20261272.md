## Problem

Simulation-based inference (SBI) for conceptual aircraft design faces three unresolved challenges: (1) discrete topological changes alter the number of continuous parameters needed to define a design, breaking the fixed-dimensional assumption of existing SBI methods; (2) the design space couples 144 discrete configurations with up to 136 continuous parameters, far exceeding prior SBI benchmarks (typically D≈10); and (3) no prior approach jointly samples over both discrete and continuous design variables within a single generative framework.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 10 continuous performance observations (e.g., cruise lift/drag coefficients, mass, state of charge) as conditioning input and optionally a discrete topology label. It produces a full eVTOL design: a 144-way categorical topology (number of wings, rotors, propellers, etc.) plus up to 136 continuous geometric and mass parameters. Designs are internally represented as typed tree structures flattened into fixed-length vectors with binary masks marking absent components, so a single-wing aircraft leaves second-wing slots blank rather than changing vector length.

## Method

The architecture cascades two diffusion transformers in a hierarchical factorization p(Θ|x) = p(τ|x)·p(θ|τ,x). Stage 1, MixeDiT, jointly samples the discrete topology and continuous observations by coupling a Riemannian Diffusion Language Modeling (RDLM) head—performing geodesic random walks on a hypersphere to model the 144-way categorical— with a standard continuous denoising head, trained via a weighted sum of importance-sampled cross-entropy and noise-prediction losses. Stage 2, MaskeDiT, extends the Simformer architecture with a topology-conditioned binary mask that zeros out absent components in both attention and the diffusion loss, enabling a single model to handle all 144 variable-dimensional parameter spaces. Sampling proceeds by first drawing τ from MixeDiT conditioned on target observations, then integrating the reverse-time SDE in MaskeDiT with the topology mask fixed, using Euler–Maruyama with exact conditioning overwrites at each step.

## Results

MixeDiT captures the marginal topology distribution with a KL-divergence of 0.0505 (vs. 1.4607 for a uniform baseline), and conditioning on +2σ mass yields 91.6% two-winged designs vs. 0.4% at −2σ mass. On the likelihood comparison (Table 1, averaged over 10 topologies with >200 samples), MaskeDiT achieves MMD 0.004±0.001, joint C2ST 0.777±0.040, and max marginal C2ST 0.770±0.076. The full MixeDiT–MaskeDiT pipeline runs ≥10× faster than 100 SUAVE simulations, and posterior predictive checks at x=μ show an average L2 error of 5.3% across the ten observation variables.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is Neural Likelihood Estimation (NLE) from the sbi package, which trains a separate autoregressive-flow likelihood model per topology. NLE achieves a lower per-topology MMD (0.002 vs. 0.004) and a lower mean marginal C2ST (0.596 vs. 0.614), reflecting its advantage when a dedicated model can be fit to each topology's data. However, MaskeDiT wins on joint C2ST (0.777 vs. 0.744), indicating better capture of the full joint observation distribution. Critically, NLE degrades sharply on rare topologies—producing NaN MMD values and C2ST scores of 1.0 when training data is scarce—whereas MaskeDiT remains stable across all 144 topologies with a single 5.1M-parameter model. Scaling NLE to all 144 topologies would require ~7.2M parameters (10 models × 50k params each, extrapolated), and it cannot support the conditional flexibility (conditioning on arbitrary subsets of inputs and outputs) that MaskeDiT provides through its topology-conditioned masking scheme.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Simformer architecture (Gloeckler et al., 2024) for diffusion-based SBI by introducing topology-conditioned masking (MaskeDiT) to handle variable-dimensional parameter spaces, and couples it with a Riemannian Diffusion Language Model (RDLM, Jo & Hwang, 2026) head to jointly sample discrete topologies and continuous observations in a shared continuous diffusion space (MixeDiT). The coupling of discrete and continuous diffusion processes draws on the Unified World Models framework (Zhu et al., 2025), but departs from prior discrete diffusion work that restricts sampling to discrete Markov chains. The hierarchical two-stage architecture (topology → parameters) is a new formulation for SBI, motivated by the fact that aircraft topologies determine the number of continuous parameters, a structural dependency absent from prior SBI benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first application of SBI to the full conceptual aerospace design pipeline, pushing diffusion-based inference to D=136 continuous parameters across 144 discrete topologies—far beyond the D≈10 typical of SBI benchmarks. The 30,276-design eVTOL dataset and the 10× speedup over SUAVE simulation make the approach practically relevant for early-stage design exploration. Key limitations include reliance on low-fidelity SUAVE (≈10% lift errors, larger outside normal conditions), the fact that many generated designs are structurally infeasible, and the fixed configuration space (max 2 wings, 4 lifting rotors per wing). Open questions include whether the hierarchical masked-diffusion approach generalizes to higher-fidelity simulators, broader vehicle classes, or design spaces with more than 144 topologies.
