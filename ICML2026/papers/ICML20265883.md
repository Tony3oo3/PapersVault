## Problem

Achieving reliable 4-bit (FP4) attention is a prerequisite for end-to-end FP4 computation on Blackwell GPUs, yet attention remains the main obstacle due to FP4's extremely coarse value set (only 15 distinct values) and attention's heavy-tailed activations. Post-training quantization methods like SageAttention3 still suffer significant quality degradation at 4-bit, and no prior work has successfully applied quantization-aware training (QAT) to attention because FlashAttention's fused backward pass contains precision-sensitive algebraic identities that break under mixed-precision forward/backward execution.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard transformer attention tensors (Q, K, V) within the FlashAttention tiling paradigm, producing attention outputs without materializing the full N×N attention matrix. For diffusion models (Wan-2.1 at 480P/720P), it takes text prompts and generates video; for LLMs (Qwen3-14B, Llama 3.1-70B), it processes text for continued pretraining and generation. During training, FP4 is emulated via fake quantization in BF16 GEMMs (Triton kernels), while inference uses native NVFP4 Tensor Core GEMMs (CUDA/CuTe-DSL kernels) on Blackwell hardware.

## Method

Attn-QAT identifies two critical requirements for stable FP4 attention QAT that are absent in prior work: (1) the recomputation of attention probabilities P in the backward pass must be fake-quantized to match the forward pass precision, and (2) FlashAttention's linear-memory identity P_i^T dP_i = dO_i^T O_i only holds when forward and backward share precision, so a high-precision auxiliary output O' (computed with unquantized P) must be stored during the forward pass exclusively for the backward gradient computation. By satisfying these two conditions, the method eliminates the need for SageAttention3's outlier-mitigation heuristics (QK smoothing, two-level P quantization), relying solely on QAT to let the model learn to compensate for FP4 quantization error. The authors implement fused Triton training kernels with inline PTX for NVFP4 conversion, custom CUDA inference kernels for RTX 5090, and a CuTe-DSL kernel extending FlashAttention-4 for B200/B300 with a TMEM overlap schedule for block-scaled MMA operations.

## Results

On Wan 2.1 14B (VBench Overall Quality), Attn-QAT achieves 0.8279, recovering nearly all of the quality loss from naive FP4 attention (0.7968) and outperforming SageAttention3 (0.8203), while BF16 attention scores 0.8335. On Wan 2.1 1.3B, Attn-QAT reaches 0.8252 vs. BF16's 0.8267, again far exceeding SageAttention3 (0.7834) and naive FP4 (0.7785). For LLMs, Attn-QAT on Qwen3-14B recovers near-BF16 performance on continued pretraining benchmarks (e.g., WinoGrande 0.7585 vs. BF16 0.7403, ARC-C 0.6084 vs. 0.5922), and on SFT achieves nearly identical MMLU-Redux (0.8392 vs. 0.8316) and IFEval (0.7306 vs. 0.7107) scores. Kernel throughput reaches 1.31× over BF16 FA4 on B200 and 1.74× on GB300, with 1.1×–1.5× speedup over SageAttention3 on RTX 5090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SageAttention3 (the strongest training-free FP4 attention baseline), Attn-QAT wins on VBench Overall Quality by 0.0076 on Wan 2.1 14B (0.8279 vs. 0.8203) and by a much larger margin of 0.0418 on Wan 2.1 1.3B (0.8252 vs. 0.7834), while also achieving 1.1×–1.5× higher kernel throughput on RTX 5090 by eliminating SageAttention3's QK smoothing and two-level P quantization overhead. Against BF16 FlashAttention (the quality upper bound), Attn-QAT nearly matches on diffusion (0.8279 vs. 0.8335 on 14B; 0.8252 vs. 0.8267 on 1.3B) and on LLM SFT benchmarks (e.g., MMLU-Redux 0.8392 vs. 0.8316 for Qwen3-14B), confirming that QAT alone recovers the FP4 quality gap without any outlier-mitigation heuristics. Against BF16 FlashAttention-4 on datacenter GPUs, Attn-QAT's CuTe-DSL kernel delivers 1.31× throughput on B200 (2026 TFLOPS peak) and 1.74× on GB300 (2677 TFLOPS peak), with the gap to 2× attributed to the softmax MUFU.EX2 bottleneck on B200 that is partially resolved on B300's doubled exp throughput. On Llama 3.1-70B continued pretraining, Attn-QAT partially recovers the FP4 degradation (e.g., WikiText 0.3076 vs. BF16 0.2838) but does not fully close the gap, which the authors attribute to limited training budget and lack of hyperparameter tuning for the 70B model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantization-aware training (QAT) paradigm—well established for linear and convolutional layers (Jacob et al., 2018)—to the attention operation for the first time. It departs from the SageAttention series (Zhang et al., 2024b, 2024a, 2025), which achieves FP4 attention through training-free outlier-mitigation heuristics (QK smoothing, two-level P quantization), by showing that QAT alone can recover the quality loss without those heuristics. The implementation builds directly on FlashAttention's fused tiling and recomputation-based backward pass (Dao et al., 2022) and extends FlashAttention-4's CuTe-DSL kernel (Dao et al., 2026) with block-scaled NVFP4/MXFP8 support for Blackwell GPUs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reliable 4-bit attention is a prerequisite for end-to-end FP4 inference on Blackwell hardware, and this paper provides the first stable training recipe to make it work, delivering 1.5×–1.74× speedups over prior FP4 attention baselines. The finding that QAT renders SageAttention3's outlier-mitigation heuristics redundant simplifies the deployment pipeline and removes extra preprocessing overhead. Open questions include whether the approach fully closes the gap at 70B scale (Llama 3.1-70B still lags BF16), whether it extends to paged attention and KV-cache quantization for serving, and how it interacts with sparse or distilled attention variants.
