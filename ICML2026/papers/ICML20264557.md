## Problem

Standard off-policy evaluation assumes covariates are exogenous to the policy, but when agents strategically modify their covariates in response to a decision maker's policy, the induced covariate shift is policy-dependent, breaking this assumption. Prior work on strategic behavior in offline settings relies on strong assumptions such as a single known cost function or repeated interactions, which are unrealistic in one-shot, high-stakes OPE settings where agents are heterogeneous and their response behavior is only partially known.

## Input / Output

The method consumes logged observational data collected under a local information disclosure (LID) scheme, where each record contains the agent's pre-strategic covariates x^b, base treatment t^b, a set of k≥2 recommended feature updates X^r (delivered as an ARex to agents who received negative treatment), the post-strategic covariates x^s, final treatment t^s, and realized outcome y. The covariate space is assumed discrete (e.g., credit score buckets, income ranges). The output is a consistent estimate of the target policy value V(π) along with the estimated parameters (β, β_0, σ) of the agents' conditionally log-normal cost sensitivity model, enabling the DM to anticipate how the covariate distribution shifts under any new policy.

## Method

The key insight is that local information disclosure via action recommendation-based explanations (ARexes) reveals agents' pre-strategic covariates before adaptation, making it possible to separate strategic modification from baseline characteristics. The agent's cost function is decomposed into a shared primitive cost d(x,x') known to the DM and an agent-specific sensitivity α modeled as conditionally log-normal given x^b, with parameters estimated via maximum likelihood from observed strategic responses (the interval (δ^l, δ^u) constraining α for each observation). The policy value is decomposed into four terms, with the strategic covariate shift p_π(x^s|t^b,x^b) computed analytically from the estimated cost model. A strategy-robust doubly robust (SDR) estimator is then constructed by combining an IPS-residual term (weighted by the full strategic propensity ratio) with a direct method term, and its consistency is proven under either correct outcome model specification or an overlap condition.

## Results

On both the synthetic dataset (N up to 11,000 agents) and the German Credit dataset (N up to 190,000 agents), the SDR estimator's policy value estimates converge to the ground truth as sample size increases, with the interquartile range shrinking toward zero, confirming the consistency guarantees in Theorems 2.6 and 3.2. The cost model parameters (β, β₀, σ) are also consistently recovered via MLE. When the outcome model μ̂ is misspecified (interaction term omitted), the SDR estimator remains consistent while the direct-method estimator fails; conversely, when overlap is violated under a strict logging policy, the SDR estimator remains consistent while the IPS-based estimator fails. With 1,000 irrational agents (10% of the population), the SDR estimator still converges to the correct value as rational-agent samples dominate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external OPE methods but instead evaluates its SDR estimator against three internal baselines: (1) SDR with a misspecified cost model (β=[1.5,0.8]ᵀ, β₀=0.2, σ=0.7 vs. true β*=[1.0,1.2]ᵀ, β₀*=0.5, σ*=1.0), which produces persistently biased policy value estimates that do not converge to ground truth; (2) a standard IPS-based estimator, which fails when the logging policy π₀^strict violates the overlap assumption (Assumption 3.1), while SDR remains consistent; and (3) a direct-method (DM) estimator, which fails when the outcome model μ̂ is misspecified (omitting the interaction term), while SDR remains consistent due to its double-robustness property. On the German Credit dataset, the same pattern holds: the misspecified-cost-model baseline yields incorrect estimates, while the correctly specified SDR converges. The experiments also show that 1,000 irrational agents introduce a transient bias that vanishes as the dataset grows, since the MLE procedure discards non-feasible responses (xˢ ∉ Xᶠ) and rational-agent samples eventually dominate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ARex framework (Vo et al., 2026) and the strategic behavioral model of Tsirtsis & Gomez Rodriguez (2020) from online learning and equilibrium analysis to the offline OPE setting. It departs from the strategic classification literature (Hardt et al., 2016; Rosenfeld & Rosenfeld, 2024), which typically assumes a single known cost function or an externally specified set of feasible costs, by modeling heterogeneous agent-specific cost sensitivities via a conditional log-normal distribution. Unlike performative prediction work (Perdomo et al., 2020) that relies on repeated interactions, this paper operates in a one-shot setting where the decision maker has only partial knowledge of agents' responses, using local information disclosure (LID) as the mechanism to recover pre-strategic covariates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and practically relevant gap: standard OPE methods fail under strategic behavior because the covariate distribution is policy-dependent, yet prior work addressing this requires either repeated interactions or full knowledge of agent costs—both unrealistic in high-stakes one-shot deployments. The LID-as-design-choice framing connects algorithmic recourse/explanation design to causal inference, suggesting that how a DM structures feedback fundamentally shapes what is inferable from data. Key limitations include the assumption that agents only choose from the recommended set, the parametric log-normal cost model, and the semi-synthetic nature of the German Credit experiments; open questions include extending to richer cost structures (e.g., feature-specific sensitivities), handling bounded rationality, and addressing the breakdown of unconfoundedness under strategic behavior.
