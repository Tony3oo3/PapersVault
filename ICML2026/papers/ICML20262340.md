## Problem

The maximal update parameterization (μP) and its depth-scaled extension CompleteP have demonstrated stable learning dynamics, hyperparameter transfer, and consistent feature evolution in supervised and self-supervised settings, but whether these benefits transfer to reinforcement learning—where data distributions are non-stationary and coupled to the agent's own evolving policy—remains unexplored. The paper asks whether preserving rich feature learning during network scaling improves policy consistency and learning efficiency in RL agents.

## Input / Output

The agent receives high-dimensional state vectors (joint positions, velocities, and in some environments privileged state) from continuous control tasks ranging from 5-dimensional (CartpoleSwingup) to 271-dimensional (humanoid_u_maze), with observations running-normalized to approximately [−3, 3]. The ResNet produces two heads: an actor outputting a joint-torque action vector and a critic outputting a scalar value estimate, both scaled by 1/(ΩN). For kernel and policy analyses, a synthetic evaluation set is constructed by varying each observation dimension individually across [−3, 3] while holding all others at zero, yielding DS samples for controlled cross-scale comparison.

## Method

The paper applies two scaling parameterizations—NTK (lazy) and CompleteP (rich)—to feedforward residual networks with separate actor and critic heads, trained primarily with on-policy PPO and secondarily with off-policy SAC across 10 continuous control environments. CompleteP scales hidden-layer weights by 1/N with a constant learning rate and inserts a 1/L depth factor (α=1) in each residual branch, preserving Θ(1) feature evolution across both width and depth; NTK instead scales the output by 1/√N and decays the learning rate by 1/√N, suppressing internal feature change to Θ(1/√N) as width grows. A free hyperparameter Ω smoothly interpolates between the two regimes, controlling the degree of feature learning. The key empirical findings are that CompleteP enables a single learning rate tuned at small width/depth to transfer reliably to much larger models (eliminating costly per-scale sweeps), produces width-invariant feature-kernel evolution and higher cross-seed policy-kernel alignment, and achieves 31% lower compute and 33% higher reward efficiency than NTK on challenging tasks such as Humanoid Maze, with the gap widening in sparse-reward and high-dimensional settings.

## Results

On the Humanoid Maze task, CompleteP agents require 31% less compute (ΔC = −31%) to reach the same reward as NTK agents, and achieve 33% higher reward (ΔR = +33%) at the same compute budget. CompleteP enables a single learning rate η₀* tuned at small width (N=32) and depth (L=2) to transfer reliably to widths up to N=2048 and depths up to L=8, whereas NTK agents require progressively lower learning rates as width and depth increase. Across 10 continuous control tasks (6 variants) and 1 discrete task, CompleteP agents show width-invariant feature evolution (Θ(1) representation change) and higher seed-to-seed policy kernel consistency compared to NTK, which exhibits Θ(1/√N) feature suppression at large widths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the NTK (Neural Tangent Kernel) parameterization, which suppresses feature learning at large widths (Θ(1/√N) representation change). CompleteP outperforms NTK on every major metric: (1) compute efficiency — CompleteP needs 31% fewer FLOPs to match NTK's reward on Humanoid Maze; (2) reward efficiency — CompleteP achieves 33% more reward at equal compute; (3) learning rate transfer — a single η₀* works across all widths and depths for CompleteP, while NTK's optimal learning rate shifts downward with scale, requiring expensive re-sweeps; (4) feature and policy consistency — CompleteP agents show width-invariant kernel evolution and higher CKA alignment across seeds, whereas NTK agents exhibit width-dependent feature freezing and greater seed-to-seed divergence. A Standard Parameterization (SP) baseline is also tested and shows weaker learning rate transfer than either NTK or CompleteP. NTK agents do not clearly win on any metric; however, the paper notes that NTK can match CompleteP's final reward if given sufficient additional training steps, at the cost of substantially higher compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CompleteP parameterization (Dey et al., 2026), which itself builds on maximal update parameterization (μP; Yang & Hu, 2020) and depthwise residual scaling (Bordelon et al., 2024b), from supervised and self-supervised settings into reinforcement learning. While prior RL scaling work (Hilton et al., 2023; Rybkin et al., 2025; Fu et al., 2026) established that increasing width, depth, or updates-to-data ratio improves performance, the influence of parameterization on feature learning in RL had not been systematically studied. The paper positions NTK and CompleteP as clean isolations of the 'lazy' vs. 'rich' feature learning extremes, and contrasts them against Standard Parameterization to attribute observed behaviors to specific scaling principles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: CompleteP enables a single learning rate sweep at small scale to transfer to arbitrarily large RL agents, eliminating the prohibitively expensive per-configuration hyperparameter searches that have constrained RL scaling. The 31% compute efficiency and 33% reward efficiency gains on Humanoid Maze, replicated across 10+ environments, suggest that parameterization choice is a first-order design decision for scaled RL, not a minor implementation detail. Open questions include whether these benefits persist for off-policy algorithms, convolutional architectures for vision-based control, multi-agent or non-stationary environments, and whether CompleteP's maintained plasticity can support continual RL without catastrophic forgetting.
