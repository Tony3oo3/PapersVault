## Problem

Released fine-tuned updates (LoRA adapters or full checkpoints) on model hubs can be recomposed by downstream users into unauthorized mixtures that bypass safety alignment or licensing terms. Existing unmergeability defenses (PaRaMS, Merge-Lock) are post-hoc, rely on Transformer-specific architectural symmetries, and require full-weight access, making them incompatible with adapter-only releases and non-Transformer backbones such as ConvNeXt.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen base model W₀, a task-specific training dataset, and trainable low-rank adapter parameters (rank r=16 for CLIP ViT, r=32 for Llama-3.1-8B) or full fine-tuning parameters. It outputs a standard-format LoRA adapter or full checkpoint that is indistinguishable from an unprotected release in standalone deployment but is deliberately brittle under off-nominal re-scaling. No additional runtime components are needed; the protection is entirely encoded in the released weights.

## Method

TRAP² embeds unmergeability during fine-tuning by optimizing an adversarial objective J(ΔW) = L_nominal(ΔW) − λ·L_off(ΔW) that minimizes loss at the nominal scale s=1 while maximizing loss at off-nominal scales sampled from a distribution excluding a margin δ around s=1. At each SGD step, a single Monte Carlo scale s is drawn and the off-nominal loss is weighted by w(s)=1/s to compensate for vanishing gradients at down-scaled updates. The key insight is that common merging operators (Task Arithmetic, TIES, etc.) effectively re-scale each constituent update (e.g., by 1/N under uniform averaging), pushing the merged adapter away from its nominal operating point where TRAP² has been trained to be brittle. This makes the approach architecture-agnostic and compatible with both adapter-only and full-checkpoint releases, without requiring access to base weights or Transformer-specific symmetries. Theoretical analysis establishes SGD convergence (O(1/√T) stationarity) and characterizes both self-degradation under down-scaling and cross-adapter collateral damage via directional curvature bounds.

## Results

On 8 vision benchmarks with CLIP ViT-B/32, TRAP² preserves standalone accuracy (88.13% average) matching unprotected fine-tuning (88.12%), while post-merge accuracy under 8-way LoRA merging drops to 23–41% (vs. 48–55% for unprotected), depending on the merging operator and space. On the larger ViT-L/14 backbone, standalone accuracy actually improves (93.71% vs. 92.40% unprotected) while post-merge accuracy drops to 33–59%. Under stronger data-aware threat models (RegMean, CoM, ProDistill, SFT), TRAP² still degrades merged accuracy to 9–49% versus 49–74% for unprotected, confirming robustness beyond simple weight averaging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRAP² is compared against two post-hoc defenses: PaRaMS (Junhao et al., ICCV 2025) and Merge-Lock (Wang et al., 2025). Merge-Lock is effectively non-functional in the adapter-only setting: its standalone accuracy collapses to 0.5–11% (near chance) across all 8 datasets on ViT-B/32, making it unsuitable for any practical deployment. PaRaMS preserves standalone accuracy comparable to unprotected fine-tuning (87.64% vs. 88.12% average on ViT-B/32), but critically fails to induce any meaningful post-merge degradation—its merged accuracy is within 0.1–0.7 points of the unprotected baseline across all 15 operator/space combinations (e.g., 48.29% vs. 48.27% under TA/Full). In contrast, TRAP² achieves equivalent standalone utility (88.13%) while dropping post-merge accuracy by 20–35 points (e.g., 23.12% vs. 48.27% under TA/Full), a gap of roughly 25 percentage points. This advantage holds across all three backbones (ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14, ConvNeXt), all five merging operators (TA, TIES, TIES+DARE, TSV, CART), all three merging spaces (Full, KnOTS, Core), and even under data-aware recovery attacks (ProDistill, SFT), where TRAP² limits recovery to 49–45% versus 74–57% for unprotected.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unmergeability problem introduced by PaRaMS (Junhao et al., 2025) and Merge-Lock (Wang et al., 2025), which apply post-hoc, function-preserving reparameterizations exploiting Transformer-specific architectural symmetries (attention-head permutations, coupled Q/K/V/O transformations). TRAP² departs from this lineage by embedding protection directly into the fine-tuning objective via a scaling-sensitive adversarial loss, eliminating the need for full-weight access or architecture-specific symmetries. The threat model draws on the standard model-merging literature (Task Arithmetic, TIES, DARE, KnOTS, Core Space), and the method is built on the LoRA parameterization (Hu et al., 2022), treating the scaling factor s as a lightweight proxy for the re-weighting that merging pipelines introduce.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As model hubs (Hugging Face, GitHub) become the primary distribution channel for fine-tuned updates, the governance gap created by post-release recomposition is a practical and growing concern for safety alignment, licensing, and IP protection. TRAP²'s architecture-agnostic, training-time formulation is notable because it works with the dominant release format (adapters) and transfers to non-Transformer backbones where prior defenses are inapplicable. Key limitations include the per-dataset tuning of the trade-off coefficient λ, the assumption of a shared base model, and the absence of permission-aware composition. Open directions include validation on larger and more diverse LLMs, automatic λ selection, and extending protection to settings with base-model drift.
