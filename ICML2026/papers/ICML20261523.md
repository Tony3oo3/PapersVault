## Problem

Existing semi-supervised learning frameworks transfer only zero-order supervision (categorical pseudo-labels) from a single teacher, providing no information about the local geometry of the loss surface and limiting the student to imitating one model's representational bias. This leads to unstable optimization with oscillating losses and limited generalization under scarce labels, as the student cannot perceive curvature, smoothness, or uncertainty in the surrounding optimization landscape.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261523/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at standard resolutions (224×224 for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/SVHN, 96×96 for STL-10) split into a small labeled set and a large unlabeled set. Two frozen self-supervised teachers (MAE ViT-B/16 and DINOv3 ViT-B/14) process weakly augmented unlabeled samples to produce soft pseudo-labels and diagonal curvature proxies, while the student (ViT-B, 90M params) outputs a softmax class-probability vector. The student is trained with both labeled cross-entropy and unlabeled pseudo-label alignment plus augmentation consistency, and an EMA copy (decay 0.995) is used for evaluation.

## Method

TTN introduces a dual-teacher, Newton-guided semi-supervised framework where two frozen self-supervised teachers—MAE (fine-grained patch-level structure) and DINOv3 (global semantic consistency)—generate pseudo-labels and local Hessian estimates on weakly augmented unlabeled samples. Pseudo-labels are fused via a confidence-weighted KL-barycenter (weighted geometric mean in log-probability space), and inverse Hessians are fused via a confidence-weighted harmonic mean on the manifold of symmetric positive-definite matrices, yielding a unified second-order supervision signal. The student updates parameters using a Newton-step rule θ ← θ − η H_fuse⁻¹ ∇L_total, where the diagonal fused-Hessian preconditioner adaptively rescales gradients to suppress oscillations in high-curvature regions. The total loss combines supervised cross-entropy with an unsupervised term that enforces both pseudo-label alignment on strongly augmented inputs and weak-strong augmentation consistency, all optimized under the curvature-guided update for stable, geometry-consistent convergence.

## Results

On ImageNet with 1% labeled data, TTN achieves 81.4% top-1 accuracy with a 90M-parameter ViT-B student, matching REACT (81.6%) and slightly surpassing TRiCo (81.2%). At 25% labels, TTN reaches 88.5%, outperforming TRiCo (88.3%) and REACT (86.8%). On CIFAR-10 (4k labels), TTN attains 97.5% accuracy, the highest among all compared methods, and on STL-10 (5k+100k) it achieves 94.4%, again the best result. The Newton-guided update also accelerates convergence: TTN reaches 80% on CIFAR-100 in 6.0k steps versus 11.0k steps without Newton preconditioning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTN outperforms TRiCo on CIFAR-10 (97.5% vs. 96.3%) and STL-10 (94.4% vs. 93.1%), but trails it slightly on SVHN (93.6% vs. 94.2%) and at 10% ImageNet labels (85.7% vs. 85.9%). Against REACT (ViT-L, 307M params), TTN (90M params) wins at 10% and 25% ImageNet labels (85.7% vs. 85.1%, 88.5% vs. 86.8%) but is marginally behind at 1% (81.4% vs. 81.6%). TTN substantially surpasses all consistency-based baselines (FixMatch, UDA, FlexMatch) by 25–30 points at 1% ImageNet labels, and also outperforms Semi-ViT (ViT-H, 632M params) by 2.5–2.3 points across all label ratios. Compared to co-training methods (Co-Training, MCT), TTN gains 1.3–1.7 points at 1% and 0.6–0.9 points at 10% ImageNet labels. The ablation confirms the Newton update is the single most critical component: removing it drops CIFAR-10 accuracy from 97.5% to 89.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

TTN extends the FixMatch-style pseudo-label + consistency regularization paradigm and the Mean Teacher EMA-based teacher-student architecture by replacing the single frozen teacher with two complementary self-supervised encoders (MAE for patch-level structure, DINOv3 for global semantics) and upgrading the student update from first-order gradient descent to a diagonal Newton-step preconditioned by fused teacher curvature. Conceptually, it bridges the co-training literature (Blum & Mitchell; Qiao et al.)—which aggregates categorical predictions from multiple views—with second-order optimization, introducing a KL-barycenter fusion in log-probability space and a harmonic-mean fusion of inverse Hessians that prior multi-teacher SSL methods have not formalized. The paper positions itself against recent game-theoretic and meta-regularized SSL frameworks (e.g., TRiCo) by arguing that explicit curvature modeling, rather than additional regularization objectives, is the missing ingredient for stable optimization under scarce labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean theoretical package (KL-barycenter optimality, trace inequality for fused curvature, linear convergence bound, variance reduction) that gives practitioners a principled alternative to heuristic ensembling in SSL, and the diagonal Newton preconditioning is computationally lightweight (O(d) per step, no full Hessian). However, the practical gains over strong baselines like TRiCo are modest (≤0.3 pp on ImageNet), the dual frozen-teacher forward passes roughly double inference-time compute during training, and the diagonal Hessian approximation limits the expressiveness of the curvature signal. Open questions include whether jointly adaptive (non-frozen) teachers, full or block-diagonal Hessians, and non-vision modalities can amplify the second-order benefit, and whether the 7.7 pp ablation drop from removing Newton generalizes beyond the single CIFAR-10 (4k) setting where it was measured.
