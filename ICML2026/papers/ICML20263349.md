## Problem

Vision Transformers incur prohibitive computational costs dominated by FFN layers (~61% of FLOPs), yet existing compression strategies are limited: structured weight pruning requires expensive full-model retraining, and token compression methods reduce only sequence length N while leaving the dominant O(D²) FFN complexity unaddressed. Furthermore, token-level decisions propagate across layers, creating inter-layer dependencies that complicate optimization. The paper seeks a decoupled approach that targets FFN channel redundancy without retraining while efficiently compressing MHSA.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet-1K classification, 512×512 for ADE20K segmentation) processed through pretrained ViT backbones (DeiT, ViT-MAE, Swin Transformer). MHSA pruning is applied offline with brief fine-tuning (15–290 epochs depending on model scale), while TCS is applied entirely at inference time with no retraining. Outputs are class labels, bounding boxes with instance masks (COCO via Cascade Mask R-CNN), or pixel-level segmentation masks, with the compressed model preserving the original block interface dimension D so downstream heads require no modification.

## Method

ToaSt is a decoupled, layer-independent compression framework with two complementary components. For MHSA, it applies structured coupled weight pruning that reduces the per-head dimension d_k by synchronizing pruned indices across Q-K and V-Proj weight pairs, using geometric-median-based importance scoring to identify redundant dimensions; this preserves the global embedding dimension D and residual connections. For FFN, it introduces Token Channel Selection (TCS), a training-free method that samples a small token subset (2–20% of N) to estimate per-channel importance via activation magnitudes (optionally weighted by CLS attention for distilled models), then removes low-importance channels from FC1 and FC2 inputs to form dense sub-matrices for efficient GEMM. The pruning schedule is layer-adaptive and asymmetric: conservative ratios (0–30%) for FC1 and aggressive ratios (50–90%) for FC2 in deeper layers, motivated by empirical observations of increasing sparsity, collapsing effective rank, and high R² linear reconstruction fidelity in deeper FFN blocks. This design eliminates retraining for the FFN component and requires only minimal fine-tuning for MHSA, with larger models (e.g., ViT-MAE-Huge) recovering in as few as 15 epochs.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, ToaSt consistently improves accuracy over unpruned baselines while reducing FLOPs by 31–46%. The headline result is ViT-MAE-Huge achieving 88.52% Top-1 (+1.64%p over baseline) with 39.4% FLOPs reduction and 1.59× throughput speedup on H100. DeiT-Small reaches 83.40% Top-1 (+3.58%p) at 45.7% FLOPs reduction with a 2.07× speedup. On downstream tasks, Swin-Base with ToaSt achieves 52.2 box mAP on COCO (+0.3 over baseline) and maintains 48.13 mIoU on ADE20K with zero quality drop under 4-layer TCS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToaSt outperforms all four baselines across every model family. Against ToMe (Bolya et al., 2022), ToaSt gains +1.0 to +4.4%p Top-1 at comparable or lower FLOPs (e.g., DeiT-Small: 83.40% vs. 79.35%; ViT-MAE-Large: 88.94% vs. 84.58%), while also delivering 1.3–2.0× higher throughput due to lower per-layer overhead. Against DiffRate (Chen et al., 2023), the margin is similar: +1.9 to +4.1%p (e.g., ViT-MAE-Huge: 88.52% vs. 86.65%; DeiT-Small: 83.40% vs. 79.56%). Against STViT-R (Chang et al., 2023) on Swin models, ToaSt leads by +2.0%p (Swin-Small: 84.65% vs. 82.60%; Swin-Base: 85.21% vs. 83.20%) with greater FLOPs reduction (38–43% vs. 33%). In the low-rank comparison on DeiT-Small, TCS at 83.40% (training-free) substantially outperforms rank-matched LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) at 76.13% (20-epoch fine-tuning) by 7.3%p, demonstrating that input-adaptive channel selection exploits activation-level redundancy that fixed weight decomposition cannot capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

ToaSt decouples ViT compression into two orthogonal components: structured coupled weight pruning for MHSA (adapting the geometric median importance criterion from FPGM, He et al. 2019, to attention head dimensions) and training-free Token Channel Selection for FFNs. It departs from token compression methods (ToMe, DiffRate, DynamicViT) that reduce sequence length N by instead targeting the hidden dimension D, which governs the dominant O(D²) FFN cost (~61% of FLOPs). Unlike prior structured pruning approaches that require full-model retraining, ToaSt's FFN component is entirely training-free, and its MHSA component preserves the block interface (D unchanged) to enable layer-independent compression without cross-layer propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that channel pruning acts as an implicit regularizer—improving accuracy by 1.6–3.0%p while reducing FLOPs by ~39%—challenges the standard assumption that compression must sacrifice quality, and the inverse scaling relationship (ViT-MAE-Huge recovers in 15 epochs vs. DeiT-Small's 290) suggests larger foundation models are disproportionately amenable to this approach. Practical limitations include manual per-model tuning of layer-wise pruning ratios and unverified dataset dependence beyond ImageNet→COCO/ADE20K transfer. Open questions include whether learnable, dataset-adaptive ratio optimization can eliminate the manual sweep, and whether the approach extends to vision-language models where FFN redundancy patterns may differ.
