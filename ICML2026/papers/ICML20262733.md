## Problem

When crowd preference data is collected from users with diverse task goals but shared safety criteria, directly learning a single reward model and combining it with a downstream task reward suffers from two critical limitations: the learned reward entangles user-specific preferences with shared safety signals (causing performance degradation under preference imbalance), and the method is highly sensitive to the trade-off weight between the two rewards. The paper asks how to discover and transfer implicit safety criteria embedded in crowd preferences to downstream tasks without access to explicit safety rewards.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a crowd preference dataset of trajectory pairs with binary labels from multiple users (each user having a distinct goal-specific reward plus a shared safety penalty), a safety-agnostic downstream task reward, and an offline trajectory dataset. Rather than outputting actions directly, the high-level policy outputs a 4-dimensional latent variable that indexes a preference-aligned low-level skill; the final environment action is produced by the frozen low-level policy conditioned on this latent variable. The output policy is guaranteed to stay within the distribution of crowd-preference-aligned behaviors, inheriting safety constraints implicitly.

## Method

The paper proposes Safe Crowd Preference-based RL, a hierarchical framework that composes policies rather than rewards. A VAE-based encoder infers latent skill variables from crowd preference sets (following VPL), and low-level policies are trained per latent variable via IQL (partial-return model) or a novel CPL-based reward-free variant (regret-based model). For a new downstream task, a high-level policy trained with TD3/TD3+BC outputs latent variables to compose these skills, optimizing only the task reward while a prior regularization term keeps selected skills within the learned crowd-preference distribution. The key insight is that constraining the action space to preference-aligned skills naturally enforces shared safety criteria without explicit safety signals, and the paper provides theoretical guarantees (Corollary A.6, Theorem A.7) bounding safety violations by low-level policy suboptimality.

## Results

Under the offline downstream setting across six safe RL environments, Safe-VPL and Safe-CPL achieve average normalized reward of 0.93 and 0.92 (vs. Oracle 1.00) while reducing average normalized safety cost to 0.03 and 0.02 (vs. Task-Only 1.00 and Oracle 0.01). In the LLM-style evaluation, the proposed method achieves reward 0.75 with zero safety violations, compared to Task-Only (reward 0.95, cost 0.24) and RC(ω=0.75) (reward 0.50, cost 0.00). Under imbalanced crowd preferences, the method degrades by less than 0.02 in average normalized reward and cost, whereas Reward Combination degrades by at least 0.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CDT Oracle (which has access to the true safety reward), Safe-VPL and Safe-CPL achieve near-equivalent safety (average normalized cost 0.03/0.02 vs. Oracle 0.01) at a small reward cost (0.93/0.92 vs. 1.00), demonstrating that implicit safety transfer from crowd preferences approximates explicit safety supervision. Compared to the Task-Only baseline (TD3+BC), the proposed methods reduce average normalized safety cost by 97–98% (from 1.00 to 0.02–0.03) while retaining 64–64% of the Task-Only reward, confirming that safety-agnostic rewards alone produce hazardous behavior. Against SOPL (Safe RLHF with separated safety-only preferences), the proposed methods are slightly worse on reward (0.93/0.92 vs. 1.04) but operate under a strictly harder setting without access to decomposed safety signals. The Reward Combination baseline (RC, ω=0.5) is substantially outperformed on both reward (0.82 vs. 0.93/0.92) and cost (0.05 vs. 0.02/0.03), and its performance degrades sharply under preference imbalance, while the proposed method remains within 0.02 of the Oracle across both balanced and imbalanced settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Variational Preference Learning (VPL, Poddar et al., 2024), which uses a VAE to model behavioral diversity in crowd preferences for personalization, and Contrastive Preference Learning (CPL, Hejna et al., 2024), which learns policies directly from preferences without explicit reward modeling. The key departure is from Safe RLHF (Dai et al., 2024) and Inverse Constraint RL, which require explicit decomposition between task and safety objectives or access to safety-specific preference labels; here, safety is an implicit shared component within coupled crowd preferences. The paper also departs from standard skill discovery (Eysenbach et al., 2018; Nachum et al., 2018), which extracts skills from demonstrations, by learning preference-conditioned skills from crowd preference data and using hierarchical composition as the mechanism for safety transfer rather than reward combination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: safety criteria are often implicit in human preferences but extremely difficult to specify in reward functions, and the 'obvious' solution of learning a reward from preferences and combining it with the task reward is shown (both theoretically and empirically) to be fragile under preference imbalance. The hierarchical skill-composition approach offers a principled alternative that inherits safety without explicit safety signals, which could reduce the cost of repeated safety annotation across tasks. However, the assumption that all crowd members share a common safety consensus is strong and may not hold with adversarial or misaligned annotators, and the LLM evaluation is a simplified bandit rather than a realistic language task, leaving open whether the approach scales to complex, multi-dimensional safety constraints in high-dimensional action spaces.
