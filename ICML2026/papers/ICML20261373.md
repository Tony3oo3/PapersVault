## Problem

Large Reasoning Models generate extended thinking traces that impose severe KV cache memory and attention computation costs, yet existing KV cache compression methods (designed for long-context prefilling or generic decoding) cannot identify which thinking tokens are actually critical for the final answer. This misalignment between retained and decision-critical tokens causes significant reasoning performance degradation, with SOTA compression methods achieving 60.9% versus 63.6% for the full-cache baseline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question prompt as input and autoregressively generates a reasoning trace followed by a final answer, all under a fixed KV cache budget B (e.g., 3,000–5,000 tokens). During decoding, the Importance Predictor emits a scalar importance score for each newly generated thinking token, which drives the eviction decision. The output is the final answer text produced with a bounded, dynamically managed KV cache rather than the full growing cache, achieving up to 5.73× memory reduction while maintaining near-full reasoning accuracy.

## Method

DYNTS introduces a lightweight 3-layer MLP Importance Predictor (d→2d→d/2→1) attached to the final hidden state of the LRM, trained with MSE loss against ground-truth importance scores derived by aggregating attention weights from answer tokens to thinking tokens (Eq. 6); the backbone is frozen during training on the MATH dataset. At inference, a dual-window mechanism manages the KV cache: newly generated tokens flow into a Local Window (preserving recent context) and a Selection Window (storing long-term history), and when the total cache reaches budget B, only the top-k tokens by predicted importance in the Selection Window are retained while the rest are evicted. The key insight is a Pareto Principle in LRMs—only ~20–30% of thinking tokens carry decision-critical information—validated empirically by showing that retaining just the top-30% importance-scored tokens recovers nearly full accuracy. A theoretical break-even condition (Corollary 5.2) guarantees net computational gain once the eviction volume K exceeds 1.5d/(n_i·L), ensuring the predictor overhead is amortized by attention savings.

## Results

On R1-Llama, DYNTS achieves 61.9% average Pass@1 across six reasoning benchmarks, matching the full-cache Transformers baseline (61.6%) while delivering 1.9× throughput (489.8 vs. 258.6 TPS). On R1-Qwen, DYNTS reaches 65.3% average Pass@1 (vs. 65.5% for full cache) at 688.1 TPS (1.6× speedup). The method reduces peak KV-cache memory by 3.32–5.73× and inference latency by 1.84–2.62× compared to vanilla Transformers, with a peak speedup of 4.51× at long decoding lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DYNTS outperforms all five KV-cache compression baselines on both models. Against the strongest LRM-specific baseline R-KV, DYNTS gains +2.3% Pass@1 on R1-Llama (61.9% vs. 59.6%) and +3.1% on R1-Qwen (65.3% vs. 62.2%), corresponding to the paper's reported 2.6% average improvement over SOTA. Against SnapKV, the gap is +5.6% on R1-Llama (61.9% vs. 56.3%) and +2.1% on R1-Qwen (65.3% vs. 63.2%). H2O trails by +6.0% (R1-Llama) and +3.7% (R1-Qwen), while StreamingLLM and SepLLM lag most significantly, losing 18.6% and 12.5% on R1-Llama respectively. Crucially, DYNTS matches or slightly exceeds the full-cache Transformers baseline in accuracy (61.9% vs. 61.6% on R1-Llama; 65.3% vs. 65.5% on R1-Qwen) while achieving 1.6–1.9× higher throughput, confirming that the Pareto Principle—only ~20–30% of thinking tokens are decision-critical—enables near-lossless compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KV cache compression literature (H2O, SnapKV, StreamingLLM, SepLLM) from general LLMs to the specific short-prefill/long-decoding regime of Large Reasoning Models, where prior methods fail because they cannot identify tokens critical to the *future* answer. The key conceptual departure is replacing heuristic or local-attention-based eviction criteria with a learnable Importance Predictor (a lightweight MLP head) that estimates each thinking token's global contribution to the final answer, trained on attention-derived supervision signals. The dual-window (local + selection) mechanism borrows structural ideas from H2O and StreamingLLM, while the LRM-specific baselines R-KV and RLKV represent the most recent attempts at reasoning-aware compression that DYNTS positions itself against.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Pareto principle finding—that only ~20–30% of thinking tokens are decision-critical—offers a concrete, actionable target for LRM inference optimization, and the 3.3–5.7× memory reduction without accuracy loss is practically significant for deploying reasoning models on resource-constrained hardware. However, the method is currently validated only on 7B–8B distilled models and mathematical reasoning, with the Importance Predictor trained on ~7K MATH samples; generalization to larger models, non-math domains, and integration into production serving stacks (vLLM, SGLang) remain open. The ablation showing that retaining *wrong* tokens degrades performance more than removing all thinking tokens raises an interesting question about whether the predictor could be extended to actively suppress harmful context rather than merely selecting useful tokens.
