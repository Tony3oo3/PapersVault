## Problem

LoRA and its variants under-utilize their effective degrees of freedom in low-rank subspaces, with multiple columns collapsing into highly correlated directions. Additionally, applying LoRA to convolution layers requires a flatten-reshape operation that disrupts the spatial encoding properties inherent in convolutional kernels, degrading performance. The paper asks whether a unified PEFT framework can adapt both attention and convolution modules in low-rank subspaces while preserving dimensional integrity across NLP and vision tasks.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained model parameters: 2D weight matrices for attention blocks and 4D convolution kernels (c_out × c_in × h × w) for vision modules. For convolution layers, the update is parameterized directly in tensor form without any reshape, using one matrix per tensor dimension. Downstream, the adapted model produces task-specific outputs—class labels for NLU, segmentation masks for SAM-based tasks, per-pixel depth maps for metric depth estimation, or generated images for text-to-image synthesis—while tuning only ~0.1–1.5% of total parameters.

## Method

LAVA parameterizes the weight increment ΔW as a sum of R rank-1 tensor components (analogous to CP decomposition), where each component is an outer product of one vector per tensor dimension, preserving the multi-way structure of convolution kernels. For 2D matrices (attention), this reduces exactly to the LoRA form ΔW = UV^T, making LoRA a special case. To stabilize training, LAVA applies column-orthogonal regularization λ‖AA^T − I‖_F² on the trainable factor A, which the paper proves (via gradient-flow analysis) bounds the Frobenius norm of A and B, drives A toward orthogonality at rate O((μ+σ)/λ), and reduces the gradient variance of B to a neighborhood of the full-finetuning variance. The regularization acts as a restoring force that prevents redundant subspace directions from collapsing, enabling more effective exploration of the low-rank subspace with only ~20% additional computational overhead over vanilla LoRA.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa-base (125M), LAVA achieves 85.9% average score, outperforming LoRA (84.7%) and DeLoRA (85.6%) while tuning only 0.3M parameters. On commonsense reasoning with LLaMA2-7b, LAVA reaches 79.5% average accuracy, a +1.9% improvement over LoRA (77.6%) and surpassing GPT-3.5-turbo (77.0%). For metric depth estimation on NYU Depth v2, LAVA achieves δ₁=0.972 while tuning only ~1.5% of Depth-Anything's 335.3M parameters, exceeding LoRA (0.937) by +3.5% and SVDiff (0.916) by +5.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAVA consistently outperforms LoRA across all evaluated domains: +1.2% on GLUE (85.9 vs 84.7), +1.9% on LLaMA2-7b commonsense (79.5 vs 77.6), +3.5% δ₁ on depth estimation (0.972 vs 0.937), and superior FID on text-to-image (429.38 vs 480.41). Against DoRA, LAVA edges ahead on commonsense (79.8 vs 79.5 on LLaMA3-3b) and depth estimation (0.972 vs 0.967 δ₁), while also requiring less computational overhead (≤20% vs DoRA's larger cost). LAVA substantially outperforms VeRA on commonsense reasoning (78.1 vs 70.2 on Gemma2-2b) and SVDiff on depth estimation (+5.6% δ₁). On the GLUE benchmark, LAVA surpasses all PEFT baselines including DeLoRA (85.9 vs 85.6), Adapter (85.0), and BitFit (82.3), though it trails full fine-tuning on CoLA and RTE by small margins attributed to seed sensitivity. In semantic segmentation, LAVA improves over both LoRA and Conv-LoRA across medical (Sα 91.0 vs 90.3/90.4), natural (Fβ 83.7 vs 83.5/83.6), and agricultural (Dice 84.8 vs 83.9/84.5) domains while using the same parameter budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAVA generalizes LoRA's low-rank matrix parameterization to higher-order tensors by drawing on CP (Candecomp/Parafac) decomposition, showing that LoRA is a special case when the tensor reduces to 2D. It departs from the common reshape-involved paradigm (flattening convolution kernels into matrices before applying LoRA) by preserving dimensional integrity through per-dimension rank-1 components. The orthogonal regularization component is conceptually related to HRA (Householder reflections) and SORSA (SVD-based orthonormal regularization) but is applied within an additive low-rank tensor framework rather than replacing the parameterization. The paper positions itself against tensor-form PEFT methods (ReFTA, TLoRA) by focusing specifically on reshape-free convolution tuning while retaining a unified matrix-tuning interface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework is practically relevant because many modern vision models (SAM, Depth-Anything, SDXL) contain both attention and convolution layers, and prior PEFT methods handled these separately or ignored convolutions entirely. The theoretical guarantees (bounded parameter norms, O((μ+σ)/λ) convergence to orthogonality, gradient variance reduction) add analytical rigor that most PEFT methods lack. However, the ~20% computational overhead from orthogonal regularization, the modest 1–2% improvements over LoRA in most settings, and the preliminary (single-dataset) speech experiment limit the immediate impact. Open questions include whether the approach extends naturally to 3D convolutions, video diffusion models, or architectures with mixed tensor orders, and whether the orthogonal regularization benefit persists at much larger scales (100B+ parameters).
