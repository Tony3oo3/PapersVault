## Problem

Whether transformers (formalized via C-RASP) admit computable non-asymptotic length generalization bounds was an open problem: prior work established such bounds only for one-layer C-RASP or two-layer C-RASP under restrictive assumptions (no constant bias terms). This paper resolves the question in the negative for general C-RASP (even at depth two) and provides tight exponential bounds for the positive fragment C-RASP⁺, which corresponds to fixed-precision transformers.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes a C-RASP program P (or equivalently a transformer with specified precision p, depth L, and dimension d) together with an upper bound on its descriptional size, and asks: what is the maximum string length N such that training on all strings of length ≤ N suffices to perfectly identify the language? For full C-RASP the answer is that no computable N exists; for C-RASP⁺ and (1,1)-precision transformers, N is singly exponential in the program/transformer size. The reduction from Diophantine equations constructs C-RASP formulas of depth 2, girth 2, and precision 2 whose emptiness encodes solvability of arbitrary systems of polynomial equations over ℕ.

## Method

The undecidability result reduces Hilbert's 10th problem to C-RASP language emptiness: each equation type (x=c, x+y=z, x·y=z) is encoded as a regulated C-RASP language using counting operators (#) and the historically operator (□), and multiple equation languages are concatenated via a separator symbol with cross-block equality constraints. For the positive fragment, the key insight is a translation from C-RASP⁺ to the unary temporal logic TL[◇] (with only the strict past operator), incurring an exponential blow-up in formula size; since every satisfiable TL[◇] formula has a polynomial-length witness string (by Etessami et al., 2002), this yields an exponential length generalization bound. The bound is shown tight via the family # [a] = n where n is encoded in binary. Transformer connections follow from depth-preserving equivalences: (1,log n)-precision transformers define exactly C-RASP languages, while (1,1)-precision transformers define exactly C-RASP⁺/TL[◇] languages.

## Results

The paper's main result is a negative one: there is no computable length generalization bound for C-RASP (already at depth 2), proven via a reduction from Hilbert's 10th Problem (undecidability of Diophantine equation solvability). This implies no terminating algorithm can perfectly learn a C-RASP program or a (1,log n)-precision transformer of depth ≥ 2. As a complementary positive result, the paper proves that the positive fragment C-RASP⁺ (equivalent to (1,1)-precision transformers) admits a tight exponential length complexity bound of O(2^poly(p,d,L)), where p is precision, d is dimension, and L is depth, and shows this bound is optimal in the worst case (e.g., the language #←[a] = n requires strings of length n, exponential in the binary encoding of n).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against empirical baselines, as it is purely theoretical. Instead, it positions its results relative to prior theoretical bounds. Chen et al. (2025) showed polynomial (depth 1) and exponential (depth 2) length complexity bounds, but only for a restricted subclass C-RASP^{2,K,T} that lacks constant bias terms; the paper proves (Theorem 3.7) that this subclass is strictly weaker than full C-RASP₂ by showing bounded-depth Dyck languages are inexpressible in C-RASP^{2,K,T} for any K, T. Izzo et al. (2026) derived exponential bounds for a specific two-layer transformer definition that enforces Lipschitz-continuous position-wise functions; the paper argues this model is not comparable to C-RASP since all known C-RASP-to-transformer translations require non-Lipschitz functions (layer normalization, Heaviside step, or rounding). The paper's undecidability result is also stronger than prior emptiness-undecidability results for log-precision transformers with average-hard attention (Sälzer et al., 2025) and uniform-attention transformers (Sälzer et al., 2026), since C-RASP is a more restricted class than those variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper resolves the central open question posed by Chen et al. (2025), who formulated non-asymptotic length generalization and proved computable bounds only for restricted C-RASP fragments (one layer, or two layers without constant bias terms). The authors leverage the depth-preserving C-RASP-to-transformer equivalence of Yang et al. (2025) to transfer their undecidability result directly to (1,log n)-precision transformers, and the (1,1)-precision-to-TL[◇] equivalence of Li & Cotterell (2025) to derive tight exponential bounds for the positive fragment. A key departure from Chen et al. is showing that their restricted class C-RASP^{2,K,T} is strictly weaker than full depth-2 C-RASP (via a geometric argument on Dyck languages), resolving an apparent contradiction between their polynomial/exponential bounds and the new uncomputability result.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The undecidability result is a fundamental negative: no algorithm, regardless of compute, can determine whether a (1,log n)-precision transformer has seen enough training data to length-generalize, since the required string lengths grow faster than any computable function. This provides a theoretical explanation for the empirical observation that length generalization is highly sensitive to initialization and does not follow conventional scaling laws. The tight exponential bound for (1,1)-precision transformers (equivalently, C-RASP+) gives a concrete, if infeasible, guarantee for fixed-precision models. Open questions include identifying other C-RASP fragments with computable bounds, and whether the exponential length bound can be matched by a polynomial sample complexity.
