## Problem

Existing video-diffusion world models for robot policy evaluation (e.g., WorldEval, WorldGym) treat actions as passive auxiliary conditions injected via cross-attention or AdaLN into visually-dominated backbones, causing them to hallucinate successful outcomes from failed or out-of-distribution actions and to drift spatiotemporally over long horizons. The paper argues this failure is fundamentally architectural: strong visual priors inherited from video pre-training override weak action signals, making the generated rollouts unreliable as evaluation proxies.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB observations at 256×256, a language instruction, a continuous action chunk (length Δ ∈ [2,8]), and a sparse history of K=4 low-resolution (128×128) keyframes from the primary global view, each prepended with an explicit time-index token. It jointly produces future multi-view visual observations at 256×256 and a discrete progress score in [0,1] emitted as text tokens (e.g., "1.0"), from which a binary success indicator is derived by thresholding the final progress value at 1.0. All modalities are tokenized into a single shared discrete index space before entering the backbone.

## Method

dWorldEval maps visual observations (MAGVIT-v2), language (LLaDA tokenizer), and action chunks (FAST) into a unified discrete token space and processes them as a single flattened sequence through a bidirectional self-attention backbone (initialized from MMaDA-VLA-8B, 32 layers, 4096 hidden dim), so that actions participate as first-class tokens with dense self-attention to visual targets at every layer rather than as injected conditions. A sparse keyframe memory of four low-resolution global-view frames with time-index tokens anchors scene geometry and object permanence over long rollouts, while the high-resolution current observation supplies fine-grained multi-view interaction cues. Task progress is formulated as discrete text generation within the same token space (Progress-as-text), with supervision distilled from the SEED-1.5VL VLM via few-shot in-context scoring, enabling joint visual-and-progress denoising under a single conditional Masked Discrete Diffusion objective (w_score=2, w_vis=1). Inference uses 16-step iterative parallel decoding to simultaneously recover the future visual state and its progress score, yielding an intrinsic success metric without external oracles.

## Results

On LIBERO, dWorldEval achieves a Δ-LPIPS of 0.352 on the failure subset, substantially outperforming WorldGym (0.650) and WorldEval (0.701), while maintaining comparable performance on expert data (0.315 vs. 0.347/0.423). For long-horizon round-trip consistency at H=20, dWorldEval achieves 0.243 LPIPS versus 0.482 (WorldGym) and 0.531 (WorldEval). In closed-loop policy evaluation, dWorldEval achieves Pearson correlation r ≈ 0.91–0.93 with real execution success rates across LIBERO, RoboTwin, and real-world tasks, with a rank-violation MMRV of 0.013 (vs. up to 0.039 for baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

dWorldEval is compared against two video-diffusion-based world model evaluators: WorldEval (Li et al., 2025b) and WorldGym (Quevedo et al., 2025). On action controllability (Δ-LPIPS), dWorldEval wins decisively on the failure subset (0.352 vs. 0.650 for WorldGym and 0.701 for WorldEval), indicating that baselines hallucinate successful outcomes when conditioned on suboptimal actions while dWorldEval faithfully reproduces failures. On long-horizon round-trip consistency (Table 4), dWorldEval achieves 0.243 LPIPS at H=20, roughly half the error of WorldGym (0.482) and WorldEval (0.531), confirming that keyframe memory effectively bounds spatiotemporal drift. In policy ranking (Figure 7), dWorldEval achieves Pearson r = 0.910–0.927 and MMRV = 0.013, while baselines show weaker correlations and higher rank violations (MMRV up to 0.039). The paper attributes the baselines' failures to their architectural design: actions are injected as weak auxiliary conditions (via AdaLN or cross-attention) into visually-dominated video backbones, causing strong visual priors to override control signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

dWorldEval departs from the dominant paradigm of video-diffusion world models (WorldEval, WorldGym, Ctrl-World) that inject robotic actions as auxiliary conditions (cross-attention, AdaLN) into visually pre-trained denoisers, where strong visual priors override control signals. Instead, it adopts Masked Discrete Diffusion (Austin et al., 2021) as the generative framework and initializes from the MMaDA multimodal diffusion backbone (Yang et al., 2025), training from scratch on robotic data so that actions, visual observations, and language instructions occupy a single unified token sequence processed by bidirectional self-attention. Two additional design choices—sparse keyframe memory for long-horizon consistency and Progress-as-text for intrinsic success scoring—distinguish it from prior evaluators that rely on external VLMs or reward functions for grading.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete architectural recipe (actions as first-class tokens in a discrete diffusion sequence) that directly addresses the most cited failure mode of current world-model evaluators: hallucinating success under out-of-distribution or suboptimal actions. The Δ-LPIPS diagnostic and the action-shuffle intervention offer a reusable protocol for measuring action controllability, which the authors show is a strict prerequisite for reliable policy ranking (r ≈ 0.9 across three platforms). Open questions remain around rare contact-rich failures, the dependence of progress scoring on VLM-distilled milestone labels, and the nontrivial inference cost of iterative parallel decoding, all of which limit immediate deployment at scale.
