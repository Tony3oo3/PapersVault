## Problem

The RL fine-tuning literature for LLMs presents a stark conflict: some studies show that increasing entropy aids exploration and performance, while others report that decreasing entropy improves reasoning accuracy. No prior work provides a theoretical criterion explaining when each direction is optimal or how to adapt the schedule during training. This paper resolves the dichotomy by identifying a single governing metric—Entropy Discrepancy—and deriving both a theoretical characterization and a practical dynamic scheduling algorithm.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM (Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B or Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B/7B) and training prompts with binary verifiable rewards (correct/incorrect). During GRPO training, groups of G completions are already sampled per prompt; the Entropy Discrepancy is computed on-the-fly by averaging per-token negative log-probabilities separately over correct and incorrect trajectories, requiring no additional sampling. The output is a fine-tuned policy whose entropy regularization coefficient is re-calibrated at every training step based on the current discrepancy estimate, seamlessly switching between entropy maximization and minimization.

## Method

Under a softmax (Gibbs) policy parameterization with Natural Policy Gradient updates, the paper derives closed-form policy update rules for positive and negative entropy regularization, showing that the effective exponent on the current policy is (1 ± ηρ). The key theoretical result (Theorem 2) proves that negative entropy outperforms vanilla RL iff the base model's Entropy Discrepancy (average entropy on correct minus incorrect samples) is sufficiently low, decomposing this into a concentration term and a diversity term. Building on this, the proposed dynamic scheduling objective V_dy(π) = E[r(τ)] + ρ_dy · Dis(π_nograd) · H(π) uses a stop-gradient on the discrepancy so it acts as a fixed scalar per step, causing the effective regularization to automatically flip sign and scale as the policy's distribution over correct and incorrect samples evolves. Theorem 3 establishes that this dynamic approach strictly dominates vanilla RL for any training horizon T, with no condition on the initial discrepancy. In practice, the discrepancy is estimated via Monte-Carlo from GRPO's existing group samples, and the method achieves up to 17.52 pp gain on KNK5 Pass@1 and 6.7 pp on AIME24 Pass@K over vanilla GRPO.

## Results

The proposed dynamic entropy scheduling achieves a 17.52 percentage-point gain over vanilla GRPO on Knights-and-Knaves (KNK5) at Pass@1, and a 6.7 percentage-point Pass@K gain on AIME24 for mathematical reasoning. Across 39 Countdown tasks, the Spearman correlation between Entropy Discrepancy and positive-entropy regularization gain reaches 0.8039, and for negative-entropy regularization the correlation is −0.8729, confirming the theoretical prediction. Under KL-coefficient ablations, dynamic scheduling delivers roughly 10 percentage-point average improvements over vanilla GRPO across tested settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla GRPO, dynamic entropy scheduling wins on every evaluated task: +17.52 pp on KNK5 Pass@1 and up to +6.7 pp on AIME24 Pass@K, with consistent gains across Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and 7B models on MATH500, OlympiadBench, AIME24, and AIME25. Compared to the best fixed entropy coefficient found via extensive grid search, the dynamic method matches or surpasses it while eliminating the need for manual coefficient tuning. Against the three state-of-the-art RL reasoning baselines—PKPO (Chen et al., 2025), Reward Unlikely (He et al., 2025), and CISPO (MiniMax et al., 2025)—the paper reports performance parity or superiority on both the Knights-and-Knaves and mathematical reasoning benchmarks (Figures 7–8), achieving this with only a global entropy schedule rather than intricate per-token objective modifications. The method also remains robust to sampling temperature (T ∈ {0.4, 0.7, 1.0}) and KL-coefficient variations, consistently outperforming vanilla GRPO under all tested hyperparameter settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO framework (Shao et al., 2024) as its RL backbone and on Natural Policy Gradient (Kakade, 2001) as its theoretical lens, but its core contribution is resolving a genuine conflict in the LLM-RL literature: prior work is split between advocating entropy maximization for exploration (SAC, PPO tradition) and reporting empirical benefits of entropy minimization for reasoning (Agarwal et al., 2025; Gao et al., 2025) without a unifying explanation. The paper introduces Entropy Discrepancy as a computable metric that determines the optimal direction of regularization, and derives a dynamic scheduling rule from a simplified NPG analysis. It positions itself as a theoretical bridge between the classical maximum-entropy RL theory (which assumes training from scratch) and the high-competence regime of LLM fine-tuning, where the pre-trained model's existing concentration on correct answers shifts the optimal strategy toward exploitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is a simple, globally-applied dynamic entropy schedule that requires no per-task grid search and matches or exceeds more complex objective modifications (PKPO, CISPO, Reward Unlikely), making it an attractive drop-in for GRPO-based RL pipelines. However, the theoretical guarantees are derived in a highly simplified setting (single prompt, binary reward, softmax over full trajectories, NPG updates), so the direct transfer to autoregressive token-level generation with continuous rewards remains an open question. The empirical validation, while consistent (Spearman > 0.8, gains across multiple models and tasks), is limited to reasoning domains (math, logic puzzles) and relatively small models (1.5B–7B), leaving open whether the Entropy Discrepancy principle generalizes to broader task distributions or larger-scale training.
