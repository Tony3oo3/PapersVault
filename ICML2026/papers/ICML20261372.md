## Problem

Standard masked modeling on PPG signals collapses to trivial solutions because the signal's quasi-periodicity allows the model to reconstruct masked regions via simple local interpolation from adjacent cardiac cycles, without learning genuine hemodynamic semantics. Meanwhile, contrastive PPG foundation models suppress fine-grained morphological details (e.g., dicrotic notches) in favor of global invariance, and vector quantization is unstable under PPG noise, mapping physiologically identical waveforms to different codebook indices.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single-channel PPG signal normalized to [0,1] via Min-Max scaling, segmented into non-overlapping 4-minute windows resampled to 50 Hz, which are further partitioned into 1-second patches (T=50 samples each). Stage 1 outputs a sequence of N discrete semantic tokens drawn from a 4096×64 codebook; Stage 2 outputs a reconstructed token sequence for the masked positions. Downstream, the frozen or fine-tuned encoder produces continuous physiological estimates (HR, SpO2, BP, respiratory rate) or discrete class labels (stress, affect, activity, signal quality, subject identity).

## Method

SIGMA-PPG is a two-stage generative masked autoencoder. Stage 1 trains a VQ-VAE tokenizer with two key modifications: a spectral reconstruction loss (L1 distance on Fourier amplitude spectra) that forces the codebook to capture dominant physiological frequencies while ignoring phase-sensitive noise, and a semantic consistency loss (stop-gradient L2 between encoder outputs of original and stochastically augmented patches) that ensures perturbed views of the same waveform quantize to the same codebook index. Stage 2 freezes the tokenizer and trains a bidirectional Transformer Student to reconstruct masked token indices, where the mask is generated by a lightweight 2-layer Transformer Teacher (~0.9M params) optimized via REINFORCE to maximize the Student's reconstruction loss. The Teacher's unnormalized logits are biased by standardized statistical priors—amplitude stability (MAD-based modified Z-score combined with sigmoid lower/upper-bound gates) and morphological skewness (tanh of absolute patch skewness)—before Gumbel-Top-k sampling enforces exactly-k masking without replacement, creating a Physiology-Aware Curriculum that steers masking toward systolic peaks and away from flat or artifact-dominated segments.

## Results

Under full fine-tuning, SIGMA-PPG (30M) achieves the best average AUC of 0.9082 and average MAE of 4.312 across 12 downstream tasks, outperforming all five baselines. The most striking gain is in SpO₂ estimation, where SIGMA-PPG reaches an MAE of 0.1457 versus 1.265 for the best contrastive baseline (Pulse-PPG), an order-of-magnitude improvement. In linear probing (frozen backbone), SIGMA-PPG (30M) still leads on regression (avg. MAE 6.636) but trails Pulse-PPG on specific classification tasks (Stress AUC 0.9083 vs. 0.9687; Human Identification AUC 0.8906 vs. 0.9892), attributed to domain shift between clinical pre-training data and ambulatory wearable recordings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIGMA-PPG (30M) outperforms all four baselines on average in full fine-tuning: it beats GPT-PPG (0.8990 avg. AUC, 5.348 avg. MAE) and Pulse-PPG (0.8936, 4.753) on both classification and regression, and dominates the smaller contrastive models PaPaGei-S/P (0.8231/0.8301 AUC) and AnyPPG (0.8547 AUC) by a wide margin. The largest individual wins are in SpO₂ estimation (MAE 0.1457 vs. 1.265 for Pulse-PPG, a ~8.7× improvement) and respiratory rate (0.2930 vs. 1.069 for GPT-PPG). Pulse-PPG retains an edge on Hypertension classification (AUC 0.8258 vs. 0.7619) and on Systolic BP regression (MAE 12.33 vs. 12.74) under full fine-tuning. In the linear probing setting, Pulse-PPG surpasses SIGMA-PPG on Stress (0.9687 vs. 0.9083) and Human Identification (0.9892 vs. 0.8906), which the authors attribute to domain shift (clinical vs. ambulatory PPG) and the natural alignment of contrastive objectives with identity-discrimination tasks. Once fine-tuned, SIGMA-PPG closes these gaps, reaching 0.9994 AUC on Stress and 0.9989 on Human Identification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of three established lines: (1) discrete representation learning via VQ-VAE, (2) adversarial/curriculum masking for self-supervised pre-training (Shi et al., 2022; Bengio et al., 2009), and (3) the emerging PPG foundation model literature dominated by contrastive paradigms (PaPaGei, Pulse-PPG, AnyPPG). Its key departure is replacing contrastive objectives with a generative masked-reconstruction framework, motivated by the argument that contrastive invariance suppresses clinically relevant morphological details (e.g., dicrotic notches). The specific novelty lies in injecting statistical priors (amplitude stability and skewness) as a bias into an RL-driven Teacher's masking policy, combined with a Gumbel-Top-k exact-k sampling mechanism and a semantic consistency loss that forces perturbed views of the same waveform to share codebook indices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The order-of-magnitude improvement in SpO2 estimation (MAE 0.1457 vs. 1.265 for the best contrastive baseline) and the 10.6× FLOPs reduction at comparable model scale make this a practically relevant foundation model for wearable and clinical PPG. However, the pre-training is restricted to clinical/operating-room data, and the linear-probing results reveal a clear domain shift when applied to ambulatory wearable signals (WESAD, DaLiA), suggesting that broader device and motion-artifact diversity in pre-training remains an open challenge. The prior-guided adversarial masking mechanism and the semantic consistency constraint are potentially transferable to other quasi-periodic physiological signals (ECG, EEG), but this generalization is not yet demonstrated.
