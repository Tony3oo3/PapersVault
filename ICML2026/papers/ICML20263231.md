## Problem

Existing equivariant deep learning architectures are built around group actions on sets or graphs, while many structured data domains (face posets of complexes, hierarchical incidence structures, multi-scale cell decompositions) are naturally indexed by partially ordered sets whose symmetries involve both permutation and structural relations. No unified framework existed that simultaneously generalizes group-equivariant networks, graph message passing, and sheaf neural networks under a single equivariance principle, and universal approximation theorems for sheaf-based equivariant models were previously unknown.

## Input / Output

The method consumes feature vectors organized as an equivariant vector bundle over a finite poset P with a finite group G acting by order automorphisms: each poset element p carries a fiber V_p, and the total input is the direct sum X = ⊕_p V_p. Outputs are sitewise feature transformations Y = ⊕_q W_q that must satisfy the equivariance condition F(ρ_X(γ)x) = ρ_Y(γ)F(x) for all γ ∈ G. In the experimental application, the input is 96-timestep tri-axial IMU sequences from five body-worn sensors under subject-dependent SO(3) gauge frames and non-invertible sensor-merging morphisms, and the output is a six-class activity label.

## Method

The paper defines order-equivariant neural networks (OENNs) as finite compositions of orbital affine layers (constrained by a transporter law K(γq,γp)T^V_{γ,p} = T^W_{γ,q}K(q,p)) and pointwise Reynolds layers that average over stabilizer groups to handle nontrivial fiber actions. A complete orbit-wise parametrization shows every linear equivariant map is determined by choosing, for each pair-orbit, a stabilizer-intertwining block, generalizing the block-tying rules of permutation-equivariant GNNs to arbitrary posets. Nonlinear equivariance is enforced via Reynolds blocks (finite group averages of transported MLP branches) and pair-orbit aggregation that sums stabilizer-invariant summaries over source sites of fixed relative-position type. The paper proves that full OENNs are dense in continuous equivariant maps on compact invariant sets, and that pair-state local OENNs (which carry a persistent source label through propagation) achieve diameter-sharp local universality on connected Hasse graphs, while ordinary anonymous message passing is provably not universal. The framework is shown to embed into category-equivariant neural networks (CENN) via the action groupoid Grothendieck construction, connecting group equivariance to the broader categorical symmetry setting.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental results tables or quantitative benchmarks. The main contributions are (i) a complete orbit-wise characterization of all linear order-equivariant maps via the transporter law and stabilizer intertwiners, (ii) the construction of OENN layers (orbital affine, pointwise Reynolds, pair-orbit aggregation), and (iii) universal approximation theorems proving that full OENNs are dense in continuous order-equivariant maps on compact G-invariant sets, and that pair-state cover-local OENNs achieve diameter-sharp local universality. The paper illustrates the framework on fixed graphs (recovering MPNNs, circular convolutions, and dihedral filters) and cellular/simplicial sheaves, but does not report any trained-model accuracy or loss numbers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper performs no empirical baseline comparisons; it is a theoretical contribution. Its 'comparisons' are structural: it shows that standard graph message passing (Gilmer et al., 2017; Xu et al., 2019), DeepSets (Zaheer et al., 2017), permutation-equivariant GNNs (Maron et al., 2019), and sheaf neural networks (Hansen & Gebhart, 2020; Barbero et al., 2022; Bodnar et al., 2022) are all special or tied subcases of the OENN hierarchy. The key theoretical separation is that ordinary anonymous message passing is not universally expressive on a fixed graph, while the pair-state local completion restores universality at directed-diameter depth. The appendix further embeds OENN into the broader CENN framework (Maruyama, 2025a), showing that the OENN UAT follows as a finite action-groupoid specialization of the CENN UAT, but no head-to-head empirical comparison between OENN and CENN architectures is presented in this paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the equivariant deep learning lineage from group actions on sets (Cohen & Welling, Maron et al., Satorras et al.) to group actions on finite posets, with the face poset of a graph or CW complex as the canonical index structure. The linear theory (transporter law, orbit parametrization, stabilizer intertwiners) generalizes the block-tying rules of permutation-equivariant GNNs, while the nonlinear architecture (Reynolds blocks, pair-orbit aggregation) subsumes sheaf neural networks (Hansen & Gebhart, Barbero et al., Bodnar et al.) and combinatorial-complex networks (Hajij et al.) as tied special cases. The appendix further embeds OENN into the author's broader category-equivariant neural network (CENN) framework via the action groupoid Grothendieck construction, positioning OENN as the finite-group, poset-indexed specialization of a more general categorical equivariance principle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The universal approximation theorems are new even when restricted to sheaf neural networks, filling a gap in the theoretical foundations of topological deep learning. The diameter-sharp pair-state local universality theorem cleanly separates anonymous message passing (not universal) from source-aware propagation (universal at Hasse-graph diameter depth), giving practitioners a principled criterion for when to use higher-order states. Limitations include the restriction to finite posets and finite groups, and the experimental validation rests on a single controlled benchmark (CatHAR, 1080 sequences) from the CENN line of work rather than large-scale graph or topological learning tasks. Open questions include whether the framework extends to infinite or continuous posets, how the pair-state overhead scales in practice, and whether the categorical (non-invertible) generalization yields consistent gains beyond the CatHAR setting.
