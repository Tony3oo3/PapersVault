## Problem

Conventional pre-training produces fixed-size, task-specific models that cannot be directly transferred to downstream architectures of different scales or tasks without costly retraining or distillation. Existing weight-template methods like WAVE are structurally limited to homogeneous per-component parameterizations with fixed dimensionality, and task-wise restricted to classification, preventing them from serving as a universal initialization strategy for diverse visual models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265154/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SWEET takes a target model architecture specification (depth L ∈ {3,6,12}, width via attention heads H ∈ {3,6,12}) and RGB images (224×224, patch size 16) for self-supervised pre-training on ImageNet-1K. The output is a frozen weight template G ∈ R^{r₁×r₂×r₃} plus lightweight scalers (X, U, V) with only a few thousand parameters, which compose to reconstruct full model weights for any target ViT or ConvNeXt configuration. For extremely compact models, scalers can be further adapted on a small dataset in ~0.16 epoch while keeping G frozen.

## Method

SWEET aggregates all weight matrices from L ViT layers (Q, K, V, O projections and MLP weights) into a unified matrix W ∈ R^{L×P} with P = 12D², then reconstructs it via Tucker decomposition W = G ×₁ X ×₂ U ×₃ V, where the core tensor G serves as a shared weight template and (X, U, V) are size-specific scalers. A low-rank bottleneck (r₁×r₂×r₃ ≪ L×P) on G condenses size-agnostic knowledge, while the Tucker structure—unlike WAVE's per-component Kronecker constraints—enables parameter sharing across heterogeneous modules (attention heads, FFN). Pre-training uses the MAE reconstruction loss (75% patch masking) to learn task-agnostic visual features, and width-wise stochastic scaling applies structured dropout to scalers U and V during training to enforce width-invariant representations. At initialization time, G is frozen and scalers are randomly initialized or dimension-aligned from pre-training to match the target architecture, enabling zero- or near-zero-cost instantiation of models at arbitrary scales.

## Results

Across five ViT model scales (L3H12 to L12H3), SWEET achieves an average Top-1 accuracy of 68.79% on ImageNet classification, +1.60% over the strongest baseline (WAVE, 67.19%). On COCO object detection, SWEET reaches 31.59 AP^box (+2.04 over WAVE), and on ADE20K semantic segmentation it attains 33.08 mIoU (+2.76 over WAVE). For ImageNet image generation, SWEET reduces FID to 26.67 (−2.19 over WAVE) while improving Inception Score to 26.07 (+1.76).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWEET outperforms all four baselines—WT-Select, DMAE, Iso. Pruning, and WAVE—across every evaluated task and model scale. The strongest competitor is WAVE (Kronecker-based weight templates trained for classification), which SWEET surpasses by +1.60% Top-1 on classification, +2.04 AP^box on detection, +2.76 mIoU on segmentation, and −2.19 FID on generation. Notably, SWEET is trained self-supervised via MAE reconstruction, whereas WAVE is trained with a task-specific classification objective; despite this, SWEET still wins on classification, indicating that self-supervised templates capture more transferable visual features. On downstream fine-grained datasets (Table 3), SWEET's advantage over WAVE widens substantially on Stanford Cars (+9.63% Top-1) and iNat-2019 (+2.01%), suggesting superior robustness under data scarcity. SWEET also extends to ConvNeXt-v2 (Table 4), consistently beating WAVE by 0.11–0.42% Top-1 across five CNN scales, confirming architecture-agnostic transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

SWEET extends the constraint-based pre-training paradigm introduced by WAVE (Feng et al., 2025b), which learned weight templates under Kronecker-based constraints for classification-specific initialization. The key departures are threefold: (1) replacing Kronecker factorization with a more general Tucker decomposition that unifies all layer and module parameters into a single weight matrix, enabling cross-component knowledge sharing; (2) shifting from task-specific (classification) pre-training to a self-supervised MAE objective, decoupling template learning from any particular downstream task; and (3) introducing width-wise stochastic scaling (structured dropout on weight scalers) to regularize templates along width dimensions, building on the Once-for-All dropout idea. The broader lineage traces back to the learngene paradigm (Feng et al., 2025a) and the MAE framework (He et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SWEET offers a practical, unified initialization strategy that works across classification, detection, segmentation, and generation without task-specific retraining, reducing the deployment bottleneck when production models deviate from off-the-shelf pre-trained configurations. The finding that self-supervised templates outperform task-specific ones (e.g., WAVE trained on classification) for cross-task initialization is a useful conceptual shift for the scalable-initialization community. Open questions include whether the Tucker-based formulation scales to very large models (e.g., ViT-H or beyond), whether the approach transfers to non-visual modalities, and whether the low-rank bottleneck size can be further reduced without quality loss. The moderate but consistent gains across five model scales and four tasks suggest the framework is robust, though the absolute improvements (1.6% Top-1, ~2 AP) are incremental rather than transformative.
