## Problem

Existing supervised disentangled representation learning methods handle attribute-level correlations via conditional mutual information (CMI) minimization but overlook hidden correlations, where data under a single attribute value contain underlying modes (e.g., 'stroll' vs. 'brisk walk' under 'walking') that are themselves correlated with other attributes. Minimizing attribute-based CMI under such hidden correlations destroys mode information critical for prediction, while the natural remedy—mode-based CMI minimization—requires mode labels that are unavailable in practice. Iteratively discovering modes and disentangling representations risks catastrophic error amplification, where clustering errors degrade representations, which in turn worsen clustering.

## Input / Output

The method takes data samples (RGB images or multi-channel time series) with Q labeled categorical attributes and produces a disentangled representation z_q for a target attribute a_q, structured so that z_q changes only when a_q (or its underlying modes) change while remaining invariant to other attributes. The framework is architecture-agnostic and operates on a two-attribute setting (extendable to Q>2), where one attribute is the target with underlying modes and the other serves as the correlated attribute. Outputs include the learned representations, attribute predictions, and iteratively refined mode (cluster) assignments under each target-attribute value, with the number of modes inferred non-parametrically from data.

## Method

CoDID is an end-to-end iterative framework coupling a disentanglement module (encoder, attribute/mode predictors, decoder, and a discriminator for weighted CMI minimization conditioned on cluster labels) with a mode discovery module (Dirichlet Process GMM with split/merge MCMC operations that adaptively determines the number of modes). The key theoretical insight is that error amplification arises from within-cluster correlations I(m₁;a₂|c₁)>0, where a cluster mixing multiple true modes causes CMI minimization to destroy mode information. To break this cycle, a meta-coordination mechanism introduces (1) a weight net W that, via meta-optimization, learns per-sample weights on marginal representations in the CMI objective so that the weighted marginal label distribution matches the joint distribution, preserving informativeness; and (2) a subcluster net S that refines DPGMM subcluster probabilities using weight-summary patterns (Gaussian fits of weight distributions per attribute value) to differentiate correlated modes within a cluster and guide splits that reduce within-cluster correlations. A dynamic architecture reconfigures only the input/output layers affected by cluster count changes, maintaining training stability across iterations.

## Results

CoDID achieves state-of-the-art performance across all seven datasets, outperforming the best baseline by an average of 7.8% in accuracy and 7.9% in macro F1 score. The largest gains appear on Canine-BG (+12.3% accuracy over REx, 56.0→68.3%), UCI-HAR (+11.3% over DIOSC, 74.6→86.2%), and MFD (+11.9% over REx, 78.8→90.7%), demonstrating strong generalization under both introduced and natural correlation shifts. On CMNIST, CoDID reaches 85.2% accuracy (vs. 81.7% for REx), and on HHAR it achieves 88.4% (vs. 82.5% for IRM). CoDID also outperforms the supervised-only BASE by an average of 10.3% accuracy, confirming that mode-based CMI minimization preserves critical mode information lost by standard DRL approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDID outperforms all 13 baselines across all seven datasets. Among invariant learning methods, IRM (81.0% on CMNIST) and REx (81.7%) are the strongest but still trail CoDID by 3.5–4.2% on CMNIST and by 11.9% on MFD; they enforce invariant predictions rather than mode-aware representation disentanglement. A-CMI (Funke et al., 2022), the closest method in spirit, underperforms BASE on CMNIST (58.5% vs. 79.2%) because attribute-based CMI minimization destroys mode information under hidden correlations. Among DRL methods addressing correlations, DIOSC (74.6% on UCI-HAR) and HFS (66.5% on CMNIST) are the strongest but lack explicit mode modeling, yielding 11.6–12.3% gaps to CoDID on the most challenging datasets. CODA (NeurIPS 2024) shows the largest degradation on MFD (62.5% vs. CoDID's 90.7%), suggesting its correlation-oriented augmentation fails under natural multi-modal time-series distributions. On time-series datasets (UCI-HAR, RealWorld, HHAR, MFD), where correlations are natural rather than introduced, CoDID's advantage over the best baseline ranges from 4.9% (RealWorld) to 11.9% (MFD), confirming practical value for OOD generalization with underlying multi-modal structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional mutual information (CMI) minimization framework for disentanglement under correlations (Funke et al., 2022) from attribute-level to mode-level (hidden) correlations, where data under a single attribute value exhibit multi-modal structure correlated with other attributes. It incorporates non-parametric Dirichlet Process Gaussian Mixture Model clustering (Ronen et al., 2022) for end-to-end mode discovery, and introduces a meta-optimization-based coordination mechanism to prevent the error amplification that arises from the mutual dependency between disentanglement and clustering. The key departure from prior DRL-under-correlations work (A-CMI, HFS, CODA, DIOSC) is the explicit modeling of within-cluster correlations and the use of learned weights to preserve mode information during CMI minimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode (within-cluster correlation causing error amplification) in iterative disentanglement-clustering pipelines and provides both a theoretical characterization (Proposition 1) and a practical solution (meta-coordination with weighted CMI and subcluster refinement). The 7.8% average improvement over the best baseline across seven diverse datasets, including out-of-distribution generalization on time-series data, demonstrates practical value for robust attribute prediction. Limitations include reliance on the elementary-ingredient causal assumption, degraded performance at very high noise levels where modes become unidentifiable, and a larger gap to oracle mode labels in multi-attribute settings, suggesting that mode discovery remains the bottleneck as complexity increases.
