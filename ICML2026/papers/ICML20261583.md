## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) assets are exposed to unauthorized instruction-driven editing, yet existing 2D adversarial protection methods cannot be directly extended to 3DGS due to two fundamental challenges: independently optimized 2D perturbations are view-inconsistent (causing cross-view conflicts and underfitting when fitting a 3DGS), and the heterogeneous Gaussian parameterization makes it nontrivial to enforce a uniform invisibility budget that simultaneously preserves appearance and achieves strong adversarial attack strength. Prior 3DGS IP protection (watermarking, steganography) is purely passive and cannot defend against active editing attacks.

## Input / Output

The method takes an original 3DGS asset (G^raw) with its multi-view training camera poses and a white-box surrogate instruction-driven editing model (e.g., InstructPix2Pix) as input. It produces a protected 3DGS asset (G^prot) consisting of the original Gaussians plus a dedicated group of trainable 'safeguard Gaussians' whose rendered contribution is strictly bounded by an L∞ budget η relative to the original rendering at every training view. The safeguard Gaussians are initialized either as a copy of the original Gaussians (AdLift) or warm-started from a 2D-fitted model (AdLift*), and the final asset must resist both 2D per-view editing and global/local 3DGS editing across training and novel views.

## Method

AdLift introduces a tailored Lifted PGD (L-PGD) that enforces strictly bounded adversarial perturbations in the 2D rendering space and lifts them into 3D Gaussian parameters, avoiding the need to manually calibrate heterogeneous budgets on individual Gaussian attributes. L-PGD alternates two steps per iteration: (i) gradient truncation, where backpropagation from the editing model is cut at the rendered image and a projected gradient update (sign of ∇L_adv) is applied within an L∞ ball of radius η centered at the original rendering, guaranteeing hard invisibility; and (ii) image-to-Gaussian fitting, where the constrained perturbed image is used as a supervision signal to update the 3D Gaussian parameters via a reconstruction loss. The adversarial objectives target instruction-agnostic pipeline components (VAE encoder latents for untargeted/targeted attacks, SAM segmentation masks for local editing) rather than the diffusion denoiser directly. By jointly optimizing safeguard Gaussians across all training views with this alternating procedure, the resulting perturbations are view-consistent and generalize to novel viewpoints, achieving both strong editing resistance and perceptual fidelity.

## Results

On instruction-driven 2D image editing (averaged across four scenes), AdLift*-VU reduces CLIPs from 0.8892 (No Protection) to 0.7889 on training views and from 0.8819 to 0.8087 on novel views, while maintaining PSNR of 27.40 and 23.91 respectively. The protection generalizes to unseen 2D editing models (SDEdit, MagicBrush, SDXL-IP2P) and 3DGS editing methods (Instruct-GS2GS, DGE), with CLIPs dropping by 0.05–0.10 across all transfer targets. At matched PSNR (~28.4 dB on Face), AdLift*-VU achieves CLIPs of 0.7620 versus 0.8590 for GaussianMarker and 0.8769 for GuardSplat, demonstrating substantially stronger editing resistance at comparable visual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GuardSplat (soft SH-feature perturbation with SSIM regularization), AdLift*-VU at matched PSNR (~28.4 dB on Face) achieves CLIPs of 0.7620 versus GuardSplat's 0.8769, a 1.14× reduction in editing similarity, while GuardSplat must drop to PSNR 7.04 to reach comparable protection (CLIPs 0.7387), indicating severe visual degradation. Against GaussianMarker (uncertainty-aware Gaussian densification), AdLift*-VU at similar PSNR (28.40 vs. 28.83) achieves CLIPs of 0.7620 versus 0.8590, again a substantial protection advantage. On novel views, the gap widens: AdLift*-VU reaches CLIPs 0.7786 at PSNR 25.61, while GuardSplat at similar PSNR (25.77) only achieves 0.8666. The key distinction is that AdLift enforces hard ℓ∞ bounds in rendering space via L-PGD, whereas both baselines rely on soft fidelity regularization over Gaussian parameters, which cannot simultaneously satisfy strong adversarial pressure and visual imperceptibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: 2D adversarial edit guards (which inject imperceptible perturbations to disrupt instruction-driven diffusion editing) and 3DGS intellectual property protection (which has so far been limited to passive watermarking/steganography methods like GuardSplat and GaussianMarker). The key departure is recognizing that neither directly fitting 3DGS to independently protected 2D views (which causes cross-view conflicts and underfitting) nor applying soft invisibility regularization on Gaussian parameters (which cannot balance imperceptibility with attack strength) is sufficient. The proposed Lifted PGD (L-PGD) resolves this by enforcing hard L-infinity constraints in the rendered image domain and then propagating the bounded perturbations back into 3D Gaussian parameters via an image-to-Gaussian fitting step, effectively lifting 2D adversarial protection into a view-consistent 3D representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS becomes the dominant real-time 3D representation for content creation and instruction-driven editing tools mature, the risk of unauthorized manipulation of valuable 3D assets is growing. This paper is among the first to demonstrate that active, view-generalizable protection against instruction-driven editing is feasible for 3DGS, and it identifies two fundamental challenges (view consistency and the invisibility-protection tradeoff) that are specific to the 3D setting and absent from the 2D literature. Open questions include robustness to purification attacks (e.g., JPEG compression, diffusion-based denoising), scalability to large or dynamic scenes, and whether the white-box surrogate assumption can be relaxed. The L-PGD framework, which decouples constraint enforcement from parameter optimization, could potentially generalize to other explicit 3D representations beyond Gaussians.
