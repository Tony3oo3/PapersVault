## Problem

Existing algorithms for heavy-tailed MDPs (HTMDPs) are designed exclusively for stochastic environments and yield only polynomial (instance-independent) regret, while BoBW algorithms for standard MDPs rely on bounded or sub-Gaussian feedback. No prior work achieves Best-of-Both-Worlds guarantees for HTMDPs — simultaneously attaining minimax-optimal instance-independent regret in adversarial regimes and logarithmic instance-dependent regret in stochastic/self-bounding regimes without knowing the environment type a priori.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives an episodic tabular MDP with H layers, S states, and A actions, where losses admit only a bounded α-moment (α∈(1,2]) with scale σ, and a transition kernel that is either known or estimated online via doubling epochs. It outputs a policy π_t(a|s) at each episode derived from an occupancy measure x_t over the polytope Q(P), along with a regret guarantee that automatically adapts: O(σT^{1/α}) in adversarial regimes and O(log T) (known transitions) or O(log²T) (unknown transitions) in self-bounding regimes. The unknown-transition variant additionally requires a mild truncative nonnegativity condition on loss distributions to control the sign of skipping bias under transition uncertainty.

## Method

The core algorithm (HT-FTRL-OM for known transitions, HT-FTRL-UOB for unknown) runs FTRL over the occupancy-measure polytope with a 1/α-Tsallis entropy regularizer and a novel skipping loss estimator that truncates losses at a data-dependent threshold τ_t(s,a) = Cσt^{1/α}x_t(s,a)^{1/α}, applies importance sampling, and subtracts a skipping bonus to correct bias. For unknown transitions, the estimator is made pessimistic by replacing the true occupancy with an Upper Occupancy Bound (UOB) and adding a Bernstein-type confidence penalty. Three key technical insights enable the BoBW analysis: (i) a local control mechanism that bounds the pointwise and second-moment of heavy-tailed shifted losses via a decomposition into a centered term and a differential value-function term with telescoping reachability weights; (ii) a suboptimal mass propagation lemma showing that occupancy deviation from the reference policy is bounded by cumulative mass on suboptimal actions across layers; and (iii) a four-term regret decomposition (TRANSERR + ESTREG + SKIPERR + PESSERR) that isolates transition estimation error from heavy-tailed skipping bias, enabling separate treatment of each component.

## Results

For HTMDPs with known transitions, HT-FTRL-OM achieves an instance-independent regret of O(σT^{1/α}) in adversarial regimes and an instance-dependent regret of O(σ^{α/(α-1)} ω_Δ(α) log T) in stochastic regimes (Theorem 4.1). For the unknown-transition setting, HT-FTRL-UOB achieves Õ(σ(T^{1/α} + √T)) adversarial regret and O(σ^{α/(α-1)} ω_Δ(α) log²T) stochastic regret (Theorem 5.2). In the MAB reduction (H=1, |S|=1), these bounds match the known lower bounds of Bubeck et al. (2013) in σ, T, and suboptimal gaps Δ up to poly(H,S,A) and log factors. In the sub-Gaussian case (α=2), the bounds recover the results of Jin & Luo (2020) and Jin et al. (2021) up to poly(H,S,A) factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are against prior theoretical guarantees and lower bounds. For known transitions, HT-FTRL-OM is the first algorithm to simultaneously achieve minimax-optimal adversarial regret O(σT^{1/α}) and logarithmic instance-dependent stochastic regret O(log T) for HTMDPs, whereas prior HTMDP work (Zhuang & Sui, 2021; Huang et al., 2023; Li & Sun, 2024) only provides polynomial-in-T stochastic regret and no adversarial guarantees. For unknown transitions, HT-FTRL-UOB matches the instance-independent lower bound Ω(√T + T^{1/α}) of Zhuang & Sui (2021) up to poly(H,S,A) and log factors, and provides the first poly(log T) instance-dependent bound for this setting. In the sub-Gaussian special case (α=2), the adversarial bounds recover Jin & Luo (2020) and Jin et al. (2021), while the stochastic bounds match their O(log T) and O(log²T) rates respectively. The additional log(T) factor in the unknown-transition stochastic bound (log²T vs. log T) is attributed to the doubling-epoch schedule for transition estimation, which incurs O(SA log T) epochs and corresponding union bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed but previously disjoint lines of work: (1) Best-of-Both-Worlds (BoBW) regret minimization for episodic MDPs with bounded losses, pioneered by Jin & Luo (2020) and Jin et al. (2021) via FTRL over occupancy measures with Tsallis entropy regularization, and (2) heavy-tailed MDP (HTMDP) algorithms for stochastic environments, initiated by Zhuang & Sui (2021) and extended to linear MDPs by Huang et al. (2023). The key departure is that standard BoBW MDP analyses rely critically on bounded or sub-Gaussian concentration to control stability and second-order terms, while existing HTMDP results exploit stationarity and provide only polynomial (instance-independent) regret. The authors bridge this gap by adapting skipping loss estimators from heavy-tailed MABs (Huang et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2025) into the occupancy-measure FTRL framework, requiring new technical tools (local control of shifted losses, suboptimal mass propagation) that have no direct analogue in either parent line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical resolution of the open question of whether BoBW guarantees are achievable for HTMDPs, providing minimax-optimal adversarial regret (matching MAB lower bounds via H=1 reduction) and logarithmic instance-dependent stochastic regret simultaneously. The suboptimal mass propagation lemma is a genuinely new structural insight: in MDPs, the reference policy's occupancy is distributed across the state space, so one cannot simply exclude a single optimal action as in MABs, and the authors show how to propagate suboptimal mass across layers to recover instance-dependent bounds. Limitations include the restriction to tabular episodic MDPs, the need for a truncative nonnegativity assumption in the unknown-transition case, and the absence of any empirical validation; extending to function approximation, removing the nonnegativity condition, and tightening poly(H,S,A) factors are natural next steps.
