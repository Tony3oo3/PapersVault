## Problem

Dense Transformers couple capacity and computation linearly, making training and inference costs grow proportionally with model size. Existing pipeline-level mixture approaches—ParaScale (virtual token streams with gradient collapse) and AltUp (auxiliary prediction branches with slow convergence)—introduce substantial overhead or limited adaptivity. The paper seeks a pipeline-level scaling mechanism that preserves MoE-style routing across entire LLM streams while avoiding virtual tokens, auxiliary heads, and discrete Top-K truncation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes token sequences of length 4096 (GPT-2-style, hidden dimension 768) and produces next-token predictions for language modeling, classification labels for GLUE, and answer spans for SQuAD v2. Internally, each token representation is broadcast to N parallel streams (N ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}), each processed by its own 1×1 convolutional kernel, and the per-stream outputs are fused via per-token soft gating weights before a down-projection. The gating network operates on the flattened concatenation of all stream outputs, producing a probability distribution over streams for every token position.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.1) establishes that an MoE layer with N linear experts and routing weights is exactly equivalent to a dynamic 1×1 convolution whose effective kernel K̃(x) = Σ g_k(x) W_k is input-dependent. cMoLLM instantiates this by replacing FFN blocks with N parallel streams, each owning a 1×1 kernel K_k; a lightweight two-layer MLP gating network produces softmax mixture weights g_k(x) over all streams (no Top-K, no low-rank factorization), and the mixed kernel is applied via standard grouped pointwise convolution. A load-balancing auxiliary loss (α·N·Σ p_k², α=0.01) discourages stream collapse. Four gating variants—simple, context_aware, multi_head, and adaptive—are evaluated, with multi_head at N=8 achieving the best perplexity (18.82) and SQuAD v2 (57.06%) at the 85M scale, outperforming the dense GPT-2 baseline across all metrics and model sizes up to 760M parameters.

## Results

On GPT-2-scale models trained on FineWeb, the best cMoLLM configuration (multi-head gating, n=8 streams) achieves a validation perplexity of 18.82, substantially below the dense GPT-2 baseline of 25.40 (a 25.9% reduction). Downstream, multi-head cMoLLM reaches 48.82% GLUE accuracy and 57.06% SQuAD v2 F1, compared to 42.45% and 50.07% for the dense baseline. In the 760M-parameter scaling experiment, multi-head cMoLLM further improves to PPL 9.41, GLUE 58.45%, and SQuAD 64.56%, outperforming the dense 760M baseline (PPL 12.79, GLUE 55.74%, SQuAD 62.91%) across all four metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary numerical baseline is the dense GPT-2 model (standard Transformer with no mixture). cMoLLM outperforms this baseline on every reported metric: the best variant (multi-head, n=8) reduces perplexity from 25.40 to 18.82 (−25.9%), improves GLUE from 42.45% to 48.82% (+6.37 pp), and raises SQuAD v2 F1 from 50.07% to 57.06% (+6.99 pp). The paper also qualitatively compares against ParaScale (Chen et al., 2025) and AltUp (Baykal et al., 2023), two prior pipeline-level mixture methods, but does not report their numerical scores in the main tables; instead it argues that ParaScale suffers from gradient collapse and homogenized routing due to virtual tokens, while AltUp converges slowly due to its fixed auxiliary prediction branch. cMoLLM is reported to achieve comparable or lower per-token compute than the dense baseline (Equation 21) while maintaining stable stream utilization, in contrast to the collapse risk noted for ParaScale. At the 760M scale, multi-head cMoLLM extends its lead over the dense baseline to PPL 9.41 vs. 12.79, GLUE 58.45% vs. 55.74%, and SQuAD 64.56% vs. 62.91%, confirming that the advantage is preserved as model size grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines: FFN-level Mixture-of-Experts (Shazeer et al., 2017; Fedus et al., 2022) and dynamic/conditional convolution (Jia et al., 2016; Yang et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2020). Its central theoretical move—showing that a MoE layer with linear experts is algebraically identical to a 1×1 convolution with an input-dependent kernel (Theorem 4.1)—is a clean reformulation that unifies the two perspectives. Methodologically, it departs from the two closest pipeline-level scaling approaches: ParaScale (Chen et al., 2025), which introduces virtual tokens and parallel streams with gradient-collapse risk, and AltUp (Baykal et al., 2023), which relies on a fixed auxiliary prediction branch. cMoLLM replaces both mechanisms with a single fully differentiable dynamic convolution over end-to-end streams, eliminating virtual tokens, auxiliary heads, and Top-K truncation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MoE-to-dynamic-convolution equivalence offers a useful unified lens for reasoning about sparse conditional computation beyond the FFN, and the 'horizontal scaling law' framing (capacity grows linearly in stream count N while per-token FLOPs stay within a constant factor of dense) is a practical design principle for parameter-efficient scaling. However, all experiments are at GPT-2 scale (≤760M parameters), and the comparison to ParaScale and AltUp is qualitative (Table 1) rather than quantitative—no head-to-head runs are reported. Key open questions include whether the soft-mixture-over-all-streams design remains compute-competitive at 7B+ scale, whether the equivalence extends to attention layers, and whether the load-balancing loss is sufficient to prevent stream collapse in deeper or wider models.
