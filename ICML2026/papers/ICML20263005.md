## Problem

Large reasoning models apply uniform elaboration across every step of a chain of thought, producing verbose traces on routine substeps while global compression methods risk truncating the few genuinely difficult steps that determine correctness. Existing hybrid-reasoning approaches route at the problem level (reason vs. no-reason) but cannot address the fine-grained heterogeneity in reasoning difficulty that exists *within* a single response, where only a small set of pivotal steps (planning, key derivations, verification) truly demand deep deliberation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question and a single served base reasoning model augmented with a pre-trained LoRA concise adapter; no second model is co-served. At each decoding step, the model's internal top-k token distribution is read to compute a sliding-window confidence score, which drives online adjustment of the LoRA strength α. The output is a single coherent chain of thought in which different segments are generated at different reasoning depths (detailed deliberation on low-confidence spans, concise inference on high-confidence spans), followed by a final answer in \boxed{} format. The response length is typically 13–49% shorter than the base model's uniform long-CoT output while preserving or slightly improving accuracy.

## Method

MixReasoning has two coupled components. First, a lightweight LoRA adapter (rank 32, scaling 64) is trained via SFT on short correct solutions (from DeepScaleR40K, using Qwen3-14B non-thinking outputs as supervision) so that scaling its strength α at inference smoothly interpolates between the base model's detailed reasoning (α=0) and a concise mode (α=64) without catastrophic forgetting. Second, a difficulty-aware switching controller monitors sliding-window token confidence—defined as the mean negative log-probability of the top-k tokens over a window of m=16 tokens—and uses a hysteresis rule with a dwell constraint to trigger mode transitions: low confidence (below τ_low) switches to thinking mode with a one-window rollback to re-generate the uncertain segment, while high confidence (above τ_high) reverts to concise mode. The theoretical analysis shows the confidence score equals a reverse-KL divergence from uniform over the top-k distribution, making it a principled concentration measure that is monotone under majorization. KV-cache reuse across switches limits overhead to a single prefill of the switched span, keeping end-to-end latency well below the base model.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B, MixReasoning achieves 67.33% Pass@1 on AIME24, 57.17% on GPQA-Diamond, 92.76% on MATH-500, and 95.88% on GSM8K, while reducing average generated tokens from 7,027 (base) to 5,120 (↓28%). Across all four model backbones (DeepSeek-R1-7B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, QwQ-32B), MixReasoning consistently shortens reasoning length by 13%–49% while matching or exceeding base-model accuracy. On the hardest benchmark (AIME24), MixReasoning improves over the base model (e.g., 67.33 vs. 64.67 on Qwen3-8B) despite using ~26% fewer tokens, whereas fixed concise-mode variants (α=64) drop to 30.00%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MixReasoning outperforms all six baselines on AIME24 (67.33% vs. best BeConcise 66.67%, Deer 66.33%, O1-Pruner 65.67%) while using substantially fewer tokens (8,873 vs. 10,298–11,371). On MATH-500 and GSM8K, it also leads (92.76% and 95.88% respectively), with the closest competitors being O1-Pruner (92.43%) and BeConcise (95.68%). On GPQA-Diamond, MixReasoning (57.17%) is essentially tied with BeConcise (57.23%) and slightly ahead of NoWait (56.67%) and O1-Pruner (56.95%). The two most aggressive compression methods—CoT-Valve and Thinkless—suffer severe accuracy drops on hard benchmarks (AIME24: 33.33% and 27.33% respectively), confirming that uniform compression or problem-level routing fails on localized difficulty. MixReasoning's key advantage is that it achieves the best accuracy–efficiency frontier: it reduces tokens by 25–35% relative to the base model while simultaneously improving or matching accuracy across all four benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning (Hu et al., 2022) as the mechanism for mode switching, and draws conceptual motivation from dual-process cognitive theory (Kahneman, 2011; Wason & Evans, 1974) to frame adaptive reasoning depth. It departs from two existing lines of work: (i) global long-to-short compression methods (BeConcise, CoT-Valve, O1-Pruner, Deer, NoWait) that uniformly shorten all reasoning steps and risk underthinking on critical subproblems, and (ii) hybrid reasoning frameworks (Thinkless, Qwen3) that route at the problem level rather than the step level. The key conceptual departure is treating reasoning difficulty as a local, intra-response property estimated from the model's own token distributions (building on Kang et al., 2025; Fu et al., 2025b), enabling fine-grained switching within a single chain of thought rather than a binary per-problem decision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MixReasoning offers a practical, model-agnostic recipe for reducing inference latency by 13–49% while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy, with negligible switching overhead thanks to KV-cache reuse and vLLM integration—making it directly deployable in latency-sensitive reasoning applications. The finding that sliding-window confidence distributions are remarkably stable across benchmarks and model scales suggests the difficulty signal is a general property of reasoning models rather than a task-specific artifact, opening the door to plug-and-play adaptive inference. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-STEM reasoning (e.g., open-ended generation, multi-turn dialogue), how it interacts with RL-based reasoning training (the concise adapter is SFT-only), and whether the hysteresis thresholds can be learned end-to-end rather than set heuristically.
