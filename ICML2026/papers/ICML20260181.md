## Problem

Existing graph coarsening methods adopt a pair-wise similarity matching paradigm where each node independently selects its merge partner based on global information, incurring substantial computational and memory overhead while neglecting the impact of merging on surrounding neighborhoods. This 'selfish' matching can distort neighborhood-level semantics, particularly in text-attributed graphs, and scales poorly to large graphs where most baselines become infeasible due to OOM or OOT failures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text-attributed graph G=(V, E, X, R) with node text encoded via Sentence-BERT into d-dimensional feature vectors, along with a target coarsening ratio r = 1 − n_c/n. It produces a coarsened graph G^c with a mapping matrix C (each original node assigned to exactly one supernode), size-weighted average feature embeddings per supernode, and LLM-generated (Llama 3.1 8B-Instruct) text summaries for each supernode. The coarsened graph is then used for downstream node classification via either GNN message passing on the coarsened structure or LLM-based reasoning over supernode summaries.

## Method

The core contribution is a non-selfishness principle formalized through the Neighborhood Interference Index I, which quantifies the semantic deviation a candidate merge induces on the union of both nodes' neighborhoods, weighted by supernode sizes: I_pq = Σ_{i∈N_p∪N_q} (|v_p^c|·|v_q^c|)/(|v_p^c|+|v_q^c|)·(s_ip − s_iq)². NOPE is a greedy algorithm that maintains a min-heap of all edges ordered by I, a sum-square cache Σ for incremental updates, and a lazy-removal strategy for stale entries, achieving O(n·Δ²·d) time and O(n_max·d + |E|) space. The faster variant NOPE* exploits a local isotropy assumption (neighborhood embeddings are zero-mean with isotropic covariance σ²I_d) to replace the exact I with its closed-form expectation E[I] = (|v_p^c|·|v_q^c|)/(|v_p^c|+|v_q^c|)·σ²·‖x_p − x_q‖², collapsing neighbor-wise dot-product computations into a single degree-weighted feature-difference term and reducing per-merge cost from O(Δ·d) to O(d), yielding overall O(n·Δ·d) complexity.

## Results

On LLM-based node classification at r=0.5, NOPE achieves the best accuracy on Citeseer (0.6206 ACC, 0.6403 F1) and Products(subset) (0.6783 ACC, 0.6820 F1), while NOPE* achieves the best on Book (0.9244 ACC). In terms of efficiency, NOPE* runs in 0.35s on Citeseer versus 2113s for FGC (~6000× speedup) and 1min4s on Ogb-Arxiv versus 37min for UGC (~35× speedup), with memory as low as 16MB vs. 53GB for UGC on Ogb-Arxiv. NOPE* is the only method that successfully processes the full 2.4M-node Products graph within a 96GB memory budget (35.3GB, 1h50min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NOPE and NOPE* consistently outperform the feature-based coarsening baselines FGC and UGC across all datasets where they are comparable, with NOPE achieving 0.6206 ACC on Citeseer versus 0.5911 (FGC) and 0.5893 (UGC), and NOPE* reaching 0.6790 ACC on Products(subset) versus 0.5484 (UGC). Against the GNN-specific baselines MPG and A-CM, NOPE edges out MPG on Citeseer (0.6206 vs. 0.6112 ACC) and NOPE* surpasses A-CM on Products(subset) (0.6790 vs. 0.6755) and Book (0.9244 vs. 0.9162), though A-CM remains competitive on Ogb-Arxiv where heuristic neighborhood retrieval is particularly effective. Compared to the heuristic neighborhood sampling baselines (Random, Degree, RAG), NOPE/NOPE* achieve substantial gains, e.g., over 15% ACC improvement on Products(subset) (0.6790 vs. 0.5789 for RAG). In terms of efficiency, NOPE* achieves 1–3 orders of magnitude speedup over all baselines: 0.35s vs. 2113s (FGC) on Citeseer, and 1min4s vs. 37min (UGC) on Ogb-Arxiv, while using 16MB vs. 53GB memory on Ogb-Arxiv. On the full 2.4M-node Products graph, NOPE* is the only method that completes within the 96GB memory constraint, whereas all other baselines either OOM or OOT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pairwise-similarity paradigm in feature graph coarsening (FGC, UGC, MPG, A-CM), where each node independently selects a merge partner based on global similarity scores. Instead, it introduces a 'non-selfishness principle' that evaluates merges by their collective neighborhood interference, formalized through a neighborhood interference index (I). The faster variant NOPE* further draws on the local isotropy assumption from random dot product graph literature to replace exact neighbor-wise computations with a closed-form expectation, reducing per-merge cost from O(Δ·d) to O(d). The paper also explicitly distinguishes its approach from graph condensation methods, arguing that coarsening preserves explicit node-to-supernode mappings and model-agnostic structure, whereas condensation is model-specific and black-box.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1–3 orders of magnitude speedup and linear memory footprint make graph coarsening practical for billion-edge graphs (e.g., the full Products graph with 123M edges) where all prior baselines hit OOM or OOT, which is a significant practical unlock for text-attributed graph processing. The non-selfishness principle offers a reusable conceptual lens—prioritizing neighborhood-level semantic stability over pairwise affinity—that could inform other graph reduction or aggregation tasks. Key limitations include the degradation of NOPE* at high coarsening rates (r ≥ 0.7) where the local isotropy assumption breaks down, and the reliance on Sentence-BERT embeddings and Llama 3.1 8B for evaluation, which constrains generalizability. Open questions raised by the authors include extension to dynamic/temporal graphs and distributed/streaming coarsening at billion-node scale.
