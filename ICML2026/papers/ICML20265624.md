## Problem

Graph Federated Learning suffers from two distinct forms of heterogeneity across clients: diverse node feature distributions and varied structural topologies. Existing methods either address only structural heterogeneity under a homophily assumption that fails on heterophilic graphs, or treat both types uniformly through weighted parameter aggregation, which cannot capture the intrinsic characteristics of node features and graph topology separately.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265624/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of M clients holds a local subgraph defined by a node feature matrix X_m ∈ R^{n_m×d}, an adjacency matrix A_m ∈ R^{n_m×n_m}, and a label matrix Y_m. The method produces per-node class predictions (C classes) while never exchanging raw graph data between clients. Unusually, the method consumes both the node features (via a VGAE encoder that concatenates one-hot labels with features) and the graph Laplacian spectrum (via eigendecomposition for spectral GNN filtering) as separate knowledge-sharing channels, and the clustering of clients is performed independently on inferred class-wise distributions and on spectral energy subspaces embedded in Grassmann manifold.

## Method

FedSSA decouples heterogeneity handling into two parallel alignment mechanisms. For semantic (node feature) heterogeneity, a Variational Graph AutoEncoder on each client infers class-wise Gaussian latent distributions q(Z_m^c) = N(μ_m^c, Σ_m^c); clients are clustered per class, cluster-level representative Gaussians are constructed by matching first and second moments, and a KL-divergence loss aligns local distributions to their cluster representatives. For structural heterogeneity, a polynomial-filter spectral GNN (ChebNet or BernNet) is deployed per client, and a novel spectral energy measure S_m ∈ R^{d×(K+1)} is defined by concatenating the mean spectral response vectors across all polynomial orders; clients are clustered by Chordal distance of the corresponding subspaces on Grassmann manifold, and an L1 alignment loss pulls local filter coefficients toward the cluster mean, augmented with L1+L2 regularization. The paper further proves linear convergence to an O(𝓔)-neighborhood of the optimum, where the error floor decomposes into semantic and structural components governed by intra-cluster distributional and spectral divergences.

## Results

On five heterophilic graph datasets (Roman-empire, Amazon-ratings, Minesweeper, Tolokers, Questions) under non-overlapping partitioning with 10 clients, FedSSA achieves an average accuracy/AUC of 67.34%, outperforming the second-best method FedIIH (64.52%) by 2.82%. The largest gap appears on Minesweeper, where FedSSA reaches 82.16% AUC versus 73.23% for FedIIH. On six homophilic datasets (Cora, Closet, Parked, Amazon-Computer, Amazon-Photo, ogbn-arxiv), FedSSA attains an average accuracy of 83.53%, edging out FedIIH (83.10%) with notably smaller standard deviations (e.g., 0.04–0.15 vs. 0.02–0.23), indicating greater stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSSA outperforms all 11 baselines across all 11 datasets and all client counts (5, 10, 20). The most dramatic gains are on heterophilic graphs: on Minesweeper, FedSSA (82.16% AUC at 10 clients) surpasses FedIIH (73.23%) by nearly 9 points, and on Tolokers it leads FedIIH (71.32%) by 2.64 points. Among topology-aware methods (FedGTA, AdaFGL, FedTAD), FedSSA consistently leads by 5–15 points on heterophilic datasets, confirming that homophily-based aggregation strategies fail under heterophily. On homophilic datasets, FedSSA's advantage over FedIIH is smaller (0.43% average) but it achieves the lowest standard deviations (e.g., 0.04 on Cora-10 vs. 0.09 for FedIIH), indicating more stable training. FedSage+ and FED-PUB, which rely on personalized aggregation without explicit heterogeneity modeling, trail FedSSA by 8–10 points on heterophilic data, underscoring the benefit of separately addressing semantic and structural heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedSSA departs from prior GFL methods that either address only structural heterogeneity under a homophily assumption (FedGTA, FedTAD, AdaFGL) or treat both node-feature and structural heterogeneity uniformly through weighted parameter aggregation (GCFL, FED-PUB, FedIIH). It builds on VGAE for class-conditional distribution inference and on polynomial spectral GNNs (ChebNet, BernNet) for structural representation, but introduces a key conceptual separation: semantic knowledge is shared via KL-divergence alignment of variational posteriors, while structural knowledge is shared via coefficient alignment of spectral filters clustered on the Grassmann manifold. This two-track design, combined with a novel spectral energy measure and Chordal-distance-based clustering, distinguishes it from both topology-aware and distribution-aware federated aggregation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly decoupling node-feature and structural heterogeneity, FedSSA provides a principled framework that works across both homophilic and heterophilic graphs, overcoming the homophily limitation of prior topology-aware methods. The linear convergence guarantee with a characterized error floor gives practitioners theoretical confidence in the method's behavior. Open questions include scalability to very large client populations (the two-stage clustering and VGAE inference per client may become costly), whether the bounded intra-cluster heterogeneity assumption holds in highly skewed real-world deployments, and whether the semantic and structural clustering could be jointly optimized rather than performed sequentially.
