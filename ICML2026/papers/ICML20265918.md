## Problem

Long-text understanding at million-token scale faces a trilemma: systems struggle to simultaneously achieve extreme scalability, high information fidelity, and low inference cost. The paper attributes this bottleneck to a 'Task-Agnostic Processing Trap' where existing paradigms (full-context LLMs, RAG, graph-based agents) commit to fixed abstractions before the query is known, misallocating computation to irrelevant regions and losing query-critical, position-anchored cues. The key insight is that query-relevant information is typically sparse relative to the full document, so effective reasoning should rely on a compact, query-sufficient subset rather than the entire context.

## Input / Output

SCOUT takes a raw long document (stored as a single .txt file, up to 2M+ tokens) and a natural-language query as input, with no pre-processing: no chunking, no indexing, no embedding, no summarization. The agent explores the file directly via interactive tool calls (Grep, Scan, Read, Glob). The output is a text answer produced exclusively from the distilled Epistemic State E_T, with a hard information-flow constraint that the full interaction trace H_T is discarded at answer time, ensuring the response depends only on committed, provenance-anchored evidence rather than the noisy exploration history.

## Method

SCOUT reframes long-text understanding as goal-directed information foraging under a POMDP, where the document is an explorable environment and the agent adaptively alternates between coarse-to-fine exploration and anchored state updates. The core architectural contribution is state decoupling: the ReAct-style interaction history H_t (procedural trace) is separated from a compact Epistemic State E_t (set of provenance-anchored units ⟨c_j, α_j⟩), and the final answer is generated solely from E_t via P(y|q, E_T), preventing exploration noise from contaminating reasoning. A composite action space splits foraging primitives (Grep, Scan, Read, Glob) from epistemic management actions (Update to commit grounded units, Evaluate to diagnose the remaining gap to the oracle sufficient set F_q*, View to inspect state), with the Evaluate action driving a convergence loop that progressively contracts E_t toward query sufficiency. For open-weight backbones, the paper shows that lightweight SFT and DAPO (RL) training under the SCOUT interface yield large gains (+27.1% on LOOGLE-v2 for Qwen2.5-72B) because the explicit state/action structure provides the long-horizon control signal that monolithic LLM SFT cannot.

## Results

On LOOGLE-v2, SCOUT achieves 78.7% overall accuracy at 29.7k token cost (Token Eff. 2.63), surpassing the best frontier LLM Gemini-3-Pro (68.5%, 273.9k) by 10.2 points while using ~9× fewer tokens. On ∞BENCH, SCOUT reaches 85.6% accuracy at 21.4k cost (Token Eff. 4.01), outperforming Gemini-3-Pro (83.9%, 259.1k) and GPT-5.1-chat (82.6%, 118.2k). SCOUT maintains near-constant accuracy and token cost as context length scales from 64K to 1M+ tokens, whereas monolithic LLMs degrade sharply beyond 256K and see costs grow by orders of magnitude.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCOUT outperforms all nine baselines on both LOOGLE-v2 and ∞BENCH. Against frontier monolithic LLMs, SCOUT beats Gemini-3-Pro by 10.2 points on LOOGLE-v2 (78.7% vs 68.5%) at ~9× lower token cost (29.7k vs 273.9k), and by 1.7 points on ∞BENCH (85.6% vs 83.9%) at ~12× lower cost (21.4k vs 259.1k); it also surpasses GPT-5.1-chat (58.6% / 82.6%) and GPT-4.1 (56.8% / 73.0%) by wide margins. Among agentic baselines, the closest competitor is Claude Code (63.4% on LOOGLE-v2, 72.7% on ∞BENCH), which SCOUT exceeds by 15.3 and 12.9 points respectively at comparable token cost (~29.7k vs 25.3k). The structured-navigation agents ReadAgent (27.7%) and GraphReader (31.6%) and the memory-based MemAgent (46.0%) all trail SCOUT by 30+ points on LOOGLE-v2, with token costs 10–14× higher, confirming that task-agnostic pre-processing (graph construction, gist indexing, segment-based reading) is substantially less effective than query-conditioned active foraging for multi-hop long-dependency reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCOUT departs from three established LTU paradigms: monolithic long-context LLMs (which ingest full documents and suffer attention dilution), RAG/indexing-based methods (which commit to task-agnostic abstractions before the query is known), and structured-navigation agents like GraphReader and ReadAgent (which require heavy pre-computation of graphs or gist indices). Its closest conceptual ancestor is ReAct (Yao et al., 2023), but SCOUT introduces a hard architectural break: it decouples the growing interaction trace from a compact, provenance-anchored epistemic state that is the sole substrate for answering, formalized via a POMDP with gap-diagnosed convergence. The training experiments further build on DAPO (Yu et al., 2025b) for RL-based policy optimization, but the key insight is that the SCOUT interaction interface itself—rather than the backbone's parametric knowledge—is what unlocks large gains from lightweight post-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCOUT offers a practically significant 8× token-cost reduction while matching or exceeding frontier proprietary models on multi-hop long-dependency reasoning, and its near-constant accuracy and cost as context scales to 2M+ tokens directly addresses the cost-performance trade-off that currently limits deployment of long-context understanding. The finding that the same 60M-token post-training budget yields +27 points under SCOUT but only +1 under monolithic LLM SFT suggests the dominant bottleneck for open-weight models on LTU is interaction structure, not model capacity—a reframing that could redirect post-training investment. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to information-dense or multimodal documents, how to amortize exploration across multiple queries on the same document, and whether parallel foraging can close the remaining latency gap versus single-pass inference.
