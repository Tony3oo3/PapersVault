## Problem

Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) aims to find flat minima for better generalization, but its adversarial perturbation is constructed via a first-order linearized surrogate under a fixed parameter-space radius, making the effective learning signal dominated by gradient norm rather than the second-order curvature that actually characterizes flatness. This mechanism-level mismatch means SAM's curvature-aware benefit only emerges in late training when gradients shrink sufficiently, leaving the bulk of training driven by an irrelevant first-order signal.

## Input / Output

LE-SAM is a drop-in replacement for the SAM perturbation step in standard image classification pipelines, consuming RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) and producing class labels. It operates with any standard backbone (ResNet, WideResNet, PyramidNet, ViT-B-16) and base optimizer (SGD, Adam), requiring only the center gradient norm to compute the dynamic radius—no additional forward-backward passes beyond SAM's standard two-pass cost. The sole new hyperparameter is the loss budget σ, which is annealed to zero over the final epochs.

## Method

LE-SAM inverts SAM's adversarial mechanism by fixing a loss-space budget σ and back-solving the perturbation radius as ρ_t = σ/(‖g‖₂ + ϱ), rather than fixing ρ and letting the loss increase vary with gradient magnitude. This scalarizes the first-order Taylor term into a constant with zero gradient, removing gradient-norm dominance from the surrogate and shifting the effective optimization signal toward the second-order curvature term ½εᵀHε throughout training. Two stabilization safeguards are added: a radius cap ρ_max to prevent excessively large perturbations when gradients are small, and cosine annealing of σ to zero over the final n epochs for smooth convergence. The method retains SAM's two-pass computational structure and can be further extended (LE-SAM+) by adding an explicit curvature-amplification term α·(L(w+ε)−L(w)) to the objective.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, LE-SAM achieves 81.51% (ResNet-18), 84.91% (WRN-28-10), and 85.91% (PyramidNet-110) top-1 accuracy, outperforming SAM by 1.34, 1.49, and 1.45 points respectively. On CIFAR-10, LE-SAM reaches 96.60%, 97.44%, and 97.63% across the three backbones. On ImageNet, LE-SAM improves over SAM by 0.71 points (ResNet-50: 77.41% vs 76.70%) and 0.77 points (ResNet-101: 79.31% vs 78.54%). In transfer learning (ViT-B-16 fine-tuning), LE-SAM gains 0.44 and 0.31 points over SAM on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LE-SAM consistently outperforms all five SAM variants (SAM, ASAM, ESAM, F-SAM, Eigen-SAM) across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet. The largest gains are on CIFAR-100, where LE-SAM surpasses the strongest variant (F-SAM at 80.73% with ResNet-18) by 0.78 points and SAM by 1.34 points; with PyramidNet-110, the gap over F-SAM widens to 0.43 points. On ImageNet, LE-SAM beats ESAM (the strongest baseline) by 0.39 points on ResNet-50 and 0.21 points on ResNet-101. In domain generalization (PACS/VLCS), LE-SAM improves over SAM by 2.18 and 0.84 points on average, and over ERM++ by 3.09 and 2.71 points. Under adversarial PGD attacks, LE-SAM gains 2.44 points over SAM on CIFAR-10 (L∞) and 2.37 points on CIFAR-100 (L∞), with smaller but consistent gains under L2 attacks. In long-tailed classification (CIFAR-100-LT), combining LE-SAM with GLMC yields 58.65% (IF=100) and 63.67% (IF=50), outperforming GLMC+SAM by 1.40 and 0.66 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM (Foret et al., 2021) by identifying a mechanism-level flaw in its adversarial perturbation: the fixed-radius constraint combined with a first-order Taylor surrogate makes the learning signal dominated by gradient norm rather than the second-order curvature that flat minima actually require. It departs from the entire lineage of SAM variants (ASAM, ESAM, Eigen-SAM, F-SAM, GSAM, SSAM, Fisher-SAM) that refine perturbation direction, gradient decomposition, or computational efficiency while retaining the fixed-radius paradigm. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the Polyak step-size principle (Polyak, 1987) — fixing a target loss change and back-solving the step — and inverting it to fix a loss-space budget σ and back-solve the parameter-space radius ρ_t, thereby scalarizing the first-order term into a constant with zero gradient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnosis that SAM's sharpness signal is effectively muted until late training (when gradients shrink enough for curvature to compete with gradient norm) is well-supported by the late-stage switch experiment and provides a clearer theoretical understanding of why SAM works when it does. The fix is minimal in implementation (one line: ρ_t = σ/(‖g‖+ϱ)) yet shifts the optimization signal toward curvature throughout training, which could make SAM-style training more predictable and less sensitive to the fixed-ρ hyperparameter. Open questions include whether the loss budget σ interacts poorly with adaptive optimizers like Adam (all experiments use SGD), whether the approach extends naturally to non-Euclidean or anisotropic parameter geometries, and whether the curvature-dominated signal remains informative in very high-dimensional or over-parameterized regimes where the Hessian is nearly singular.
