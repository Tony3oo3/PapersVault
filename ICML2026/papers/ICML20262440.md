## Problem

Existing data mixture optimization methods either perform costly searches directly on the target model or rely on scaling laws that factor model-size and mixture effects into separable terms, failing to extrapolate accurately to large scales. Additionally, prior laws predict only validation loss, which does not always align with downstream benchmark accuracy. This paper addresses both gaps by deriving a capacity-aware mixture law that jointly models mixture ratios and model size, and extending it to directly predict per-benchmark performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262440/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes mixture ratios over five domains (English, Chinese, Code, Math, Knowledge), model size in activated parameters, user-specified benchmark weights, and validation losses from multiple held-out datasets. Rather than fitting the mixture law on individual validation losses (which would require 200+ validation sets and introduce excessive parameters), it first fits a loss-to-benchmark mapping and then uses a single benchmark-proxy loss as the fitting target. The output is a set of optimal mixture ratios on the probability simplex, along with predicted validation loss and per-benchmark accuracy for the target model scale.

## Method

CAMEL derives a capacity-aware mixture scaling law by formulating pretraining as a constrained optimization where the model dynamically allocates parameter capacity across k intrinsic domains, solved via KKT conditions under perturbation assumptions of nearly homogeneous learning exponents and stable domain weights. The resulting law takes the form L_val(r, M) = C + Σ K_i / (⟨t_i, r⟩^α_i · M^β_i), capturing the nonlinear interplay between mixture and model size in a single expression rather than separable terms. A multi-dimensional logistic loss-to-benchmark law maps multiple validation losses to per-benchmark accuracy, enabling end-to-end prediction from mixture to downstream performance. For fitting under a fixed compute budget, the paper proposes an hourglass sampling strategy that prioritizes the smallest and largest model scales, consistently reducing extrapolation error compared to uniform rectangle sampling. The framework is validated by extrapolating from models up to 7B-A150M to a 55B-A1.2B target, achieving up to 3% higher benchmark accuracy at 50% lower compute cost.

## Results

Under the Balanced objective, CAMEL achieves a weighted average benchmark score of 0.532 on a 55B-A1.2B target model, outperforming SODM (0.526), DML (0.519), and the model-size-agnostic baseline (0.514). CAMEL also achieves the highest scores under all three specialized objectives (Math: 0.497, Code: 0.528, Knowledge: 0.565). The method reduces mixture optimization compute cost by at least 50% compared to baselines, identifying high-quality mixtures with less than one full training pass on the target model, while improving downstream benchmark performance by up to 3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAMEL outperforms all three baselines on the weighted average score under the Balanced objective (0.532 vs. SODM 0.526, DML 0.519, Xie et al. 0.514) and under all specialized objectives (Math 0.497, Code 0.528, Knowledge 0.565). Against SODM (Shukor et al., 2025), the closest competitor, CAMEL gains +0.6% on the Balanced score and +0.7–0.6% on specialized targets, with the largest individual-benchmark advantage on C-Eval (+2.5%) and HumanEval (+3.0%), though it trails SODM on GSM8K (−1.2%) and MATH (−2.7%). Against DML (Ye et al., 2025), CAMEL leads by +1.3% on the Balanced score, with particularly strong gains on HumanEval (+9.8%) and C-Eval (+2.2%), but lags on MATH (−2.1%). Against the model-size-agnostic DoReMi-style baseline (Xie et al., 2023), CAMEL achieves a +1.8% improvement on the Balanced score and up to +4.9% on the Math-specialized target, confirming that explicitly modeling model size in the mixture law is critical for extrapolation to the 55B scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data mixture scaling law line (DML by Ye et al., SODM by Shukor et al.) by deriving a unified law from a capacity-allocation constrained optimization (KKT conditions), rather than the separable or ad-hoc model-size formulations used previously. The theoretical foundation draws on Gu et al.'s (2025) capacity-allocation perspective, and the loss-to-benchmark mapping extends the logistic form from Dubey et al. (Llama 3) and Bhagia et al. to a multi-dimensional validation-loss input. A key departure from prior mixture-optimization work (DoReMi, DOGE, RegMix) is the explicit joint modeling of mixture ratios and model size within a single expression, motivated by empirical evidence that optimal mixtures shift with scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50% compute reduction for mixture optimization, verified by extrapolating to a 55B-A1.2B MoE target, makes data mixture design substantially more accessible for mid-training pipelines where compute budgets are tight. The hourglass sampling strategy (prioritize smallest and largest scales) is a practical, counterintuitive finding that could be adopted broadly in any scaling-law fitting pipeline. Open questions include whether the Assumption 2.1 conditions (nearly homogeneous exponents, stable domain weights) hold for denser architectures or much larger scales, and whether the hourglass strategy generalizes beyond the single DeepSeek-V3 architecture family tested here.
