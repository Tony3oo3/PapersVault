## Problem

In crowdsourced data annotation, repeated labeling by multiple workers mitigates noise but introduces instance-level and label-level redundancy that inflates costs. Existing repeated annotation strategies are either offline (coreset-based), instance-unaware (entropy or standard Beta-based), or model-centric (requiring iterative model training), and no prior method simultaneously avoids all three limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265810/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a tabular dataset of N instances with M-dimensional attribute vectors (numeric or label-encoded nominal) and their current multiple noisy label sets L_i^t collected over t annotation rounds, where each label is one of Q classes or -1 (unannotated). It outputs the single most valuable instance to annotate in the next round (extendable to a batch), and after T rounds returns the fully annotated dataset. Worker abilities are assumed inaccessible and the per-label cost is fixed, so minimizing the number of labels is equivalent to minimizing annotation cost.

## Method

MA³S estimates per-instance uncertainty via a margin-Beta distribution: from the Dirichlet posterior over class counts, it extracts the maximum (N_m) and second-maximum (N_s) counts to form Beta(N_m+1, N_s+1), whose CDF at a fixed threshold θ=0.4 yields a discriminative uncertainty score that generalizes the standard Beta to multi-class settings (Theorem 3.1 proves monotonicity in β and inverse monotonicity in α at fixed ratio). To make the strategy instance-aware, MA³S builds a K-nearest-neighbor graph over the attribute space and propagates each instance's uncertainty by averaging the uncertainties of its neighbors, so that annotating one instance reduces the propagated uncertainty of its neighbors and suppresses redundant annotation of similar instances. Instance selection is then performed purely by argmax over propagated uncertainties, requiring no predictive model, no committee voting, and no semi-supervised initialization. The entire procedure is online: after each new label arrives, the affected instance's uncertainty is updated in O(Q log Q) and propagated in O(N²), with overall complexity O(N² log N + N²T).

## Results

MA³S is evaluated on four real-world crowdsourced datasets (Income: 600 instances/2 classes, Leaves: 384/6, Music: 700/10, LabelMe: 1000/8) using label accuracy after majority voting across varying annotation rounds. MA³S achieves the highest label accuracy across all datasets and all annotation rounds, with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirming statistically significant superiority (p < 0.05) over every baseline on every dataset. On the synthetic binary dataset, MA³S requires only ~N annotations to rapidly drive down overall uncertainty, outperforming all baselines under the same annotation budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MA³S significantly outperforms all seven baselines (GRR, ENTROPY, LU, MU, LMU, ALFC, DPP) on all four datasets at p < 0.05, as confirmed by Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. The instance-unaware strategies (GRR, ENTROPY, LU) achieve suboptimal accuracy because they cannot distinguish the most valuable instances to annotate in each round; LU additionally cannot handle multi-class scenarios. The model-centric strategies (MU, LMU, ALFC) converge prematurely, reflecting their heavy dependence on model quality and initial annotation quality, and they repeatedly select the same persistently uncertain instance once the model plateaus. The offline DPP coreset strategy, while generally better than model-centric methods, can only annotate a fixed subset (N/10) and thus yields limited improvement to the whole dataset. MA³S's advantage stems from combining online margin-Beta uncertainty estimation (reducing label redundancy) with nearest-neighbor graph propagation (reducing instance redundancy), without requiring any model feedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the repeated annotation strategy lineage initiated by Sheng et al. (KDD 2008), which introduced GRR, ENTROPY, LU, MU, and LMU. The key methodological departure is twofold: (1) replacing LU's standard Beta distribution with a margin-Beta distribution (using the top-two class counts) to handle multi-class scenarios with stronger discriminative power, and (2) adding a nearest-neighbor graph to propagate uncertainties across instances, making the strategy instance-aware. The paper explicitly positions itself against three categories of prior work—offline coreset methods (e.g., DPP), instance-unaware uncertainty methods (ENTROPY, LU), and model-centric active learning (MU, LMU, ALFC)—arguing that no existing strategy simultaneously avoids all three limitations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For crowdsourcing practitioners, MA³S offers a practical, model-free annotation strategy that reduces annotation costs by avoiding redundant labeling of similar or already-resolved instances, with the added benefit of not requiring a trained model (avoiding cold-start issues). However, the O(N²) time complexity from graph construction and propagation limits scalability to very large datasets, and the paper acknowledges that new labels are not shared with neighbors, leaving room for more sophisticated information propagation. Open questions include whether the simple averaging in Eq. (12) can be replaced by learned or distance-weighted propagation, and whether the approach extends naturally to streaming or non-Euclidean attribute spaces.
