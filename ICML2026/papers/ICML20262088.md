## Problem

Neural networks that select among discrete components (logic gates, architecture operations, MoE experts) often use soft mixtures during training but hard argmax selection at inference, creating a training-inference mismatch. Prior work attributed gap reduction to Gumbel noise providing implicit Hessian regularization, but this paper shows the gap is actually determined by forward-pass structure (hard vs. soft selection), not by the noise distribution, and identifies a catastrophic failure mode where Gumbel-ST accuracy collapses at low temperatures.

## Input / Output

The method operates on flat logic gate networks (L=4–6 layers, K=16 candidate Boolean gates per node, 64k–128k neurons per layer) for 10-class image classification. Inputs are MNIST (28×28 grayscale), MNIST-Binary (pixels thresholded to {0,1}), and CIFAR-10-Binary (3-channel images binarized with 31 evenly-spaced thresholds per channel); binary inputs eliminate the computation gap, isolating pure selection gap. The GroupSum readout sums neuron outputs per class group, and a temperature-scaled softmax produces the final class logit.

## Method

The paper introduces the forward alignment principle: using hard argmax selection in the training forward pass (Hard-ST or Gumbel-ST) matches inference exactly, yielding zero selection gap by construction regardless of backward temperature. This is enabled by temperature decoupling—argmax is scale-invariant, so the backward temperature τ_b controls only gradient distribution, not the forward output. Building on this, CAGE (Confidence-Adaptive Gradient Estimation) adapts τ_b via linear interpolation from an EMA of network-wide selection confidence (mean max-softmax across all nodes), starting high for broad gradient exploration and decreasing toward commitment as confidence rises. The theoretical framework decomposes the total discretization gap into a method-dependent selection gap (reducible via forward alignment) and an irreducible computation gap (gate- and input-dependent), with unified bounds showing hard methods achieve zero selection gap while mixture methods exhibit temperature-dependent hedging.

## Results

On MNIST-Binary (L=6), Hard-ST achieves exactly 0% selection gap at all temperatures (τ=0.05 to 2.0), while Soft-Mix peaks at 24% and Soft-Gumbel at 40%. Gumbel-ST without CAGE suffers a catastrophic ~47-point accuracy collapse at low temperatures (dropping from ~98% to ~51%), whereas Hard-ST+CAGE and Gumbel-ST+CAGE maintain 98.1–98.4% accuracy with ≤0.1% range across all temperatures. On CIFAR-10-Binary, Gumbel-ST+CAGE reaches 58.0% accuracy (0.6% range) versus Gumbel-ST's 18.9% worst-case (39.5% range), and Hard-ST+CAGE reaches 57.6% (0.5% range).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Soft-Mix (Petersen et al., 2022), Hard-ST eliminates the selection gap entirely (0% vs. 24% peak on MNIST-Binary at τ=2.0) and converges 30–76× faster (~500 vs. 15,000–38,000 iterations). Against Gumbel-ST (Kim, 2023), CAGE is transformative: it reduces Gumbel-ST's accuracy range from 47.3 percentage points to 0.1 on MNIST-Binary and from 39.5 to 0.6 on CIFAR-10-Binary, while Gumbel-ST without CAGE collapses to 18.9% on CIFAR-10-Binary. Against the Hessian regularization hypothesis (Yousefi et al., 2025), the paper provides a direct counterexample: Soft-Gumbel and Gumbel-ST use identical Gumbel noise distributions yet exhibit dramatically different selection gaps (40% vs. 0–1.1%), demonstrating that forward-pass structure—not noise-induced Hessian smoothing—determines the gap. On CIFAR-10-Binary, Gumbel-ST+CAGE (58.0%) marginally outperforms Hard-ST+CAGE (57.6%), suggesting Gumbel noise provides beneficial exploration when stabilized by adaptive temperature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the straight-through estimator (Bengio et al., 2013) and Gumbel-Softmax (Jang et al., 2017) to analyze discrete selection in logic gate networks, extending Kim's (2023) Gumbel-ST framework with a new adaptive temperature mechanism (CAGE). Its key conceptual departure is from Yousefi et al. (2025, NeurIPS), who attributed Gumbel-ST's gap reduction to implicit Hessian regularization from Gumbel noise; this paper's 2×2 factorial design (crossing forward type with noise type) shows that identical Gumbel noise produces dramatically different gaps depending on whether the forward pass uses soft mixtures or hard selection, reframing the mechanism as 'forward alignment' rather than noise-induced smoothing. The gap decomposition (selection gap + computation gap) and the hedging-vs-commitment analysis (Proposition 9) provide a unifying lens that also explains why hard routing dominates in modern MoE and why VQ-VAE's design naturally avoids discretization gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The forward alignment principle offers a simple, actionable design rule for any architecture requiring discrete selection at deployment: match the training forward pass to the inference forward pass, and handle optimization challenges (exploration, gradient variance) entirely in the backward pass. The discovery that Gumbel-ST catastrophically collapses at low temperature (98%→50% accuracy) without CAGE is a practical warning for practitioners using temperature scheduling. However, all experiments are confined to logic gate networks on MNIST/CIFAR-10 with 3 seeds; the paper itself notes that extending the principle and CAGE to neural architecture search, large-scale MoE, and vector quantization remains future work, and the MoE validation (Appendix J) is preliminary. The theoretical bounds (Theorem 6, Proposition 10) are worst-case and may be loose in practice, as the authors acknowledge.
