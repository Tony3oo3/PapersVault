## Problem

Existing safety steering methods for LLMs are fundamentally supervised, trained on a static and limited set of known jailbreaks, and fail to generalize to unseen or out-of-distribution attacks. The paper asks how to train a safety steering mechanism that maximally generalizes beyond this fixed training support without requiring any labeled jailbreak supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260610/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on d-dimensional hidden activations at specific layers of aligned LLMs (LLaMA-3-8B, Mistral-v2-7B, Qwen-2.5-7B). At test time, given a text prompt, the model's hidden state at a chosen activation position (e.g., 3 or 1 tokens before the end) is iteratively updated via K=20 gradient-ascent steps along the learned potential field, yielding a safe completion (refusal for harmful inputs, near-unchanged output for benign). No model weights are modified; all intervention occurs in activation space at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is a bi-level adversarial training framework that eliminates the need for labeled jailbreak supervision. In the inner step, unsupervised latent direction discovery (ULDD) learns K diverse unit-norm directions that, when injected at an early layer with adaptively calibrated magnitude R, transform refusal-state activations into simulated jailbroken activations that adversarially stress-test the current steering field. In the outer step, a small MLP potential f_φ is trained via the Kantorovich-Rubinstein OT dual objective (with a gradient penalty enforcing 1-Lipschitz constraint) to maximize the potential gap between refusal and non-refusal activations, while simultaneously enforcing benign zero steerability (penalizing gradient norm on benign activations) and jailbroken strong steerability (maximizing gradient norm on simulated jailbroken activations). At test time, ∇f_φ(h) defines a spatially varying steering field, and K iterative gradient-ascent steps push potentially jailbroken activations toward the refusal region while leaving benign activations nearly unchanged. The adversarial loop progressively expands the simulated jailbroken activation space to cover real jailbreak subspaces, which the paper shows mechanistically explains the improved generalization to unseen attacks.

## Results

Across three LLMs (LLaMA-3-8B, Mistral-v2-7B, Qwen-2.5-7B) and six jailbreak families, the method achieves average StrongReject (SR) safety scores of 4.01%, 5.26%, and 4.46% respectively, with most individual attack SR values below 5%. On utility, the method yields utility scores of 46.5, 36.6, and 53.5 (combining ARC-C, TruthfulQA, GSM8K accuracy minus OR-Bench over-refusal FPR), substantially outperforming aggressive baselines like CircuitBreaker (14.0, −26.0, 40.3) while remaining competitive on raw safety. The method is trained without any labeled jailbreak supervision, relying solely on unsupervised latent direction discovery to simulate adversarial jailbroken activations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CircuitBreaker (CB), the proposed method trades a small safety margin (4.01% vs 3.85% avg SR on LLaMA-3-8B) for a dramatic utility gain (46.5 vs 14.0 utility score), since CB's aggressive activation suppression causes 49% over-refusal on OR-Bench boundary cases versus 16.6% for the proposed method. Against AlphaSteer, the method achieves comparable or slightly better safety (4.01% vs 5.38% on LLaMA; 4.46% vs 4.48% on Qwen) while substantially reducing over-refusal (16.6% vs 24.4% FPR on LLaMA), yielding a 37.9→46.5 utility improvement. Against LAT and ROSI, the method dominates on both safety and utility across all three models (e.g., on Mistral-v2-7B: 5.26% vs 6.70%/8.47% safety, 36.6 vs 24.5/20.7 utility). The key differentiator is that the method achieves these results without any labeled jailbreak supervision, whereas AlphaSteer and LAT require jailbreak-specific training data, and the bi-level adversarial loop with unsupervised latent direction discovery is what enables generalization to unseen jailbreak families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) safety steering via activation interventions (Arditi et al. 2024; Sheng et al. 2025 AlphaSteer; Wollschläger et al. 2025), which learns fixed or conditional linear directions to push activations toward refusal, and (2) unsupervised latent direction discovery (Mack & Turner 2024a,b; Yüksel et al. 2021), which finds behavioral steering axes without task labels. The key departure is that prior safety steering is supervised and tied to a static jailbreak training set, causing OOD generalization failures; here, the authors repurpose unsupervised latent directions as a zero-shot jailbreak simulator and embed it in a bi-level adversarial loop that trains a nonlinear OT-potential steering field (an MLP whose gradient defines the field) rather than a single linear vector. The Kantorovich-Rubinstein dual formulation provides the principled objective for learning this potential, replacing the difference-in-means construction used in prior linear steering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real jailbreaks evolve and are often out-of-distribution relative to any fixed training set, yet current steering defenses degrade sharply on unseen attacks. The zero-shot formulation (no jailbreak labels required) makes the defense more scalable and easier to maintain as new attack families emerge. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that linear latent interventions may not reach activation regions corresponding to non-natural-language jailbreaks (e.g., base64, ciphers), bounding the expressive capacity of the simulation. Open questions include whether nonlinear latent transforms, gradient-based step-wise search, or compositions of multiple directions could further expand coverage, and whether the adversarial-support-expansion idea generalizes to other safety settings such as hallucination control or unlearning.
