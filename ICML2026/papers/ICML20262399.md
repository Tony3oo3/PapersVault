## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on LLM-based retrieval systems either require white-box access to the victim model's parameters or assume the attacker knows the victim user's query, making them impractical for real-world scenarios. This paper addresses the gap by studying query-agnostic black-box attacks where the attacker has no knowledge of the victim model, document corpus, or user queries, and asks how vulnerable LLM retrievers are under such realistic constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262399/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single victim document (plain text) and a surrogate LLM (Qwen-1.5-4B-Casual) as the only attacker-side resources, with no access to the victim retriever, its corpus, or any user query. It produces a short suffix of adversarial injection tokens (default 10 tokens, constrained by a distance threshold δ) that, when appended to the document, reduce its retrieval ranking simultaneously across multiple independent LLM retrievers in a zero-shot transferable manner. The same injected tokens are effective against all tested victim models without per-model re-optimization.

## Method

The paper establishes a theoretical framework formalizing LLM retrievers as ε-p_ε-Precise functions (documents in the same topic cluster in embedding space) and proves a transferability theorem: if a surrogate model can push the attacked document's embedding outside the topic cluster with a positive gap, the victim model exhibits reduced query-document similarity with probability p_ε^f. To operationalize this, the authors design Document-Queries Adversarial (DQ-A) learning, a min-max adversarial loop where GCG optimizes the document's injected tokens to minimize similarity with sampled queries while simultaneously optimizing the query tokens to maximize similarity with the document. Two key tricks strengthen the attack: (1) a population imbalance (N diverse query samples vs. fixed document tokens) that simulates the two-timescale learning rate ratio from GDA theory for non-convex settings, and (2) a word-embedding surrogate that replaces the last-hidden layer with the first-layer word embeddings for computing the adversarial loss, which empirically yields more transferable attacks by optimizing in a shallower, more stable representation space.

## Results

On the BRIGHT benchmark (Economics, Psychology, Biology, Robotics), DQ-A achieves the largest retrieval performance drop among all methods in 6 of 7 victim LLM retrievers, with average Recall@25 drops of ~2–10% and Recall@50 drops of ~1–7% relative to original performance. The most dramatic effect is on the Robotics dataset, where DQ-A reduces Qwen1.5-7B's Recall@50 by 8% and Jina-Embeddings-v3's by 6%, corresponding to ~30% and ~20% relative drops in their original performance fractions. The only model where the attack is limited is Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, which the authors attribute to post-training robustness against injection attacks. Notably, all baselines (GCG, PRADA, Poisoned-RAG) are given the ground-truth query, making the comparison imbalanced in their favor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DQ-A outperforms GCG (which requires the ground-truth query) on 6 of 7 victim models across all four metrics, with the largest gap on Qwen1.5-7B where DQ-A achieves Recall@25 of 0.285 vs. GCG's 0.342 (a 16% relative improvement in attack effectiveness). Against PRADA (which additionally receives 10% of the query/document data for LoRA alignment training), DQ-A again wins on 6/7 models, e.g., on E5-Mistral-7B DQ-A drops Recall@50 to 0.354 vs. PRADA's 0.371. The most striking comparison is with Poisoned-RAG, which paradoxically *increases* retrieval performance (e.g., on Qwen1.5-7B, Recall@50 rises from 0.442 to 0.494) because the LLM-generated tokens are topically relevant and thus boost rather than diminish the document. The only model where DQ-A does not clearly dominate is Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, where all methods show minimal effect, likely due to post-training robustness. Importantly, DQ-A achieves these results without any access to the victim query, victim model parameters, or document corpus, whereas all three baselines require the ground-truth query (marked with *).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of adversarial attacks on retrieval systems (GCG, PRADA, PoisonedRAG) by imposing a strictly more constrained threat model: the attacker has no access to the victim model's parameters or query interface, no knowledge of the user's query, and no access to the document corpus. Where prior black-box methods (e.g., PRADA) still required ground-truth queries and abundant victim-model interactions, this work formulates a complete black-box, query-agnostic setting and addresses it through a GAN-inspired adversarial learning loop (DQ-A) between the victim document and LLM-sampled query candidates on a surrogate model. The theoretical ε-p_ε-Precise Retriever framework, which formalizes the topic-clustering property of LLM embeddings, is a new analytical tool not present in prior retrieval-attack literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that a single 10-token suffix injection, learned once on a surrogate, can simultaneously degrade retrieval performance across seven architecturally diverse LLM retrievers with zero further interaction — and no existing defense (perplexity detection, truncation, reranking) is shown to mitigate it. The paper also highlights a concerning robustness-precision trade-off (Qwen3-Embedding resists the attack but underperforms other small retrievers), suggesting that hardening against such attacks may come at a cost to retrieval quality. Open questions include whether the method extends to boosting attacks (the authors show only a preliminary doubled-recall result), whether benign document edits could inadvertently trigger similar embedding shifts, and what document-level defenses (e.g., sub-content majority voting) might be adapted from other domains.
