## Problem

Existing PPG foundation models either require extensive preprocessing to extract clean morphological features for contrastive pretraining (e.g., PaPaGei) or depend on closed-source field-like data (e.g., PulsePPG). Noisy PPG segments, which are critical for robustness to consumer-grade signals, are either discarded or yield unreliable contrastive targets under morphology-based supervision. The paper asks whether co-recorded ECG and respiratory signals from open-source ICU data can provide physiologically grounded contrastive targets that enable learning from noisy PPG without denoising or feature extraction.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single 10-second PPG window sampled at 125 Hz (1250 samples) and produces a 512-dimensional embedding via a 1D ResNet-26 encoder with instance normalization. During pretraining, the same PPG window is paired with time-aligned ECG and RESP segments from which five physiological metrics (heart rate, RMSSD, respiratory rate, respiratory amplitude, respiratory volume per time) are extracted, filtered for physiological plausibility, and mapped to [0,1] to define contrastive relationships. The model is pretrained on ~20 million 10-second segments from 4,998 MIMIC-III subjects (~56,000 hours), using only PPG at deployment.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces PPG morphology-based contrastive targets with a 5-dimensional physiological metric space derived from co-recorded ECG and RESP signals. For each 10-second window, heart rate and RMSSD are extracted from ECG, and respiratory rate, amplitude, and volume-per-time from RESP; these metrics are filtered using known physiological bounds to reject noise-corrupted segments. The rank-n-contrast (RNC) loss then encourages PPG embeddings to be closer in representation space when their corresponding physiological metric vectors are closer, with two augmented views per segment (Gaussian noise, negation, scaling, random crop). The backbone is a 1D ResNet-26 (12 residual blocks, kernel 11, stride 2, 128→256→512 filters) with instance normalization, producing 512-dim embeddings. Because the supervision signal comes from cleaner ECG/RESP metrics rather than the noisy PPG itself, the model retains and learns from noisy PPG segments, improving robustness to motion artifacts and consumer-grade signal quality without requiring field-specific pretraining data.

## Results

Across 15 downstream tasks from 6 unseen datasets, the proposed model achieves an average macro F1 of 0.58 (classification) and average MAE of 9.45 (regression) under across-subject linear probing, outperforming all baselines on 14 of 15 tasks. Under the within-subject protocol, it achieves 0.79 average macro F1 and 5.33 average MAE, winning all 9 evaluated tasks. Notable gains include DaLiA heart rate (MAE 7.78 vs. 10.5 for PulsePPG), WildPPG green-channel HR (7.61 vs. 9.68), and affect classification (MF1 0.52 vs. 0.46), all achieved with 3× fewer pretraining subjects than PaPaGei and no field-specific pretraining data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PaPaGei-P and PaPaGei-S, the proposed model wins on all 15 tasks, with the largest gaps on field-like heart rate estimation (DaLiA MAE 7.78 vs. 12.7/15.1) and affect classification (MF1 0.52 vs. 0.40/0.39). Against PulsePPG, the strongest baseline, the model wins on 14 of 15 tasks; the sole loss is systolic blood pressure on VitalVideos (MAE 15.9 vs. 15.7), a marginal 0.2 difference. Against SimCLR and BYOL (trained on identical data and backbone), the model outperforms both on every task, with average classification MF1 of 0.58 vs. 0.56 and average regression MAE of 9.45 vs. 10.1. Against NeuroKit's automatic HR estimation, the model achieves substantially lower MAE across all heart rate tasks (e.g., 7.78 vs. 12.9 on DaLiA). General-purpose time-series models Chronos and MOMENT are also outperformed (results in Appendix L), confirming that domain-specific multimodal supervision is more effective than generic time-series pretraining for PPG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PPG foundation model line initiated by PaPaGei, which used morphology-based contrastive targets extracted directly from PPG segments. The key departure is replacing PPG-derived morphology features with physiological metrics (HR, RMSSD, RR, RA, RVT) computed from co-recorded ECG and respiratory signals as contrastive supervision, combined with the rank-n-contrast loss from Zha et al. (2023). Unlike PulsePPG, which requires field-like pretraining data, this approach uses only open-source ICU data (MIMIC-III) and requires only PPG at inference, positioning it as a more reproducible and deployment-friendly alternative to both morphology-based and field-specific pretraining paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that multimodal physiological supervision from co-recorded signals can substitute for large-scale field-specific pretraining data, a finding with practical implications for building PPG foundation models without proprietary wearable datasets. However, limitations remain: pretraining on ICU populations may not fully capture ambulatory variability, blood pressure regression shows only marginal gains over baselines, and demographic biases (particularly for darker skin tones) persist. Open questions include whether longer windows (1–2 min) for richer HRV features, additional supervision signals like blood pressure, and more heterogeneous pretraining corpora can close the remaining gaps, and whether the within-subject evaluation protocol generalizes beyond the small subject pools used here.
