## Problem

Existing interpretable machine learning methods—both glass-box models (GAMs, EBM) and post-hoc explanations (SHAP, partial dependence)—rely on additive decompositions that suffer from signal cancellation and interaction masking when strong higher-order interactions are present. In particular, SHAP values can net to zero despite active features, and 1D partial dependence plots collapse all interaction structure into a main effect, making the true role of individual features invisible. The paper asks whether a non-additive, structurally faithful representation can retain interpretability while capturing multiplicative interaction effects.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. covariate-response pairs (x^(i), y^(i)) with x^(i) ∈ R^p (possibly on a non-rectangular support) and y^(i) ∈ R, and produces a regression predictor m̂(x) expressed as a sum over R stages, each stage being the difference of two positive rank-1 separable products of univariate piecewise-constant functions. A key structural property is that every stage is exactly reconstructable from its per-feature 1D partial dependence curves up to a single scalar normalizer, so the model's own PD plots are faithful representations of the fitted factors rather than post-hoc surrogates. The backbone/tilt reparameterization further decomposes each stage into a positive activity gate b^(ℓ)(x) and a signed imbalance score d^(ℓ)(x), yielding interpretable 1D curves for magnitude and direction separately.

## Method

TSL fits the conditional mean as a sum of R ordered differences of positive separable products, learned stagewise: at each stage ℓ, two positive rank-1 products (each a product of p univariate piecewise-constant factors) are fit against the current residuals via a CART-style grid-tensor refinement that greedily splits intervals along feature axes using closed-form 2×2 regularized least-squares updates. After each stage is fit, all stage scalars {λ±^(k)}_{k=1}^ℓ are jointly refit by least squares (orthogonal backfitting), and n_grids bagged grid tensors are aggregated in the backbone/tilt gauge with similarity-based filtering to mitigate non-identifiability from non-rectangular support. The positivity constraint on all univariate components ensures each factor unambiguously amplifies or suppresses its product, and the backbone/tilt parametrization m̂±,j = b_j · e^{±d_j} cleanly separates shared magnitude (backbone) from signed branch imbalance (tilt). The paper establishes an O(1/√r) approximation rate via the OGA framework of Barron et al. (2008) for functions in the Sobolev space of dominant mixed smoothness, with the rate independent of dimension p.

## Results

Across 27 OpenML CTR 23 regression benchmarks, TSL (R≤2 or R≤10) ranks top-3 among interpretable models on 17 datasets and is the best interpretable model on 5. On socmob, TSL(R≤10) achieves RMSE 9.48, outperforming XGBoost (17.65), LightGBM (21.71), Random Forest (20.45), and EBM (20.21) by wide margins. On california_housing at matched rank budgets, TSL(R≤10) achieves RMSE 49,376 versus SepALS(r≤10) at 62,162, a ~21% reduction. The TSL(1-product) ablation confirms that the positivity constraint and ordered-difference structure contribute meaningfully: at matched total separation rank ≤4, TSL(1-product) is noticeably worse on socmob (10.58 vs. 9.87), naval_propulsion_plant (0.0027 vs. 0.0013), and auction_verification (1336.51 vs. 1135.80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EBM (GA²M-style additive model), TSL(R≤10) wins on 4 of the 10 displayed datasets (brazilian_houses: 2399 vs. 3327; auction_verification: 624 vs. 1738; cpu_activity: 2.31 vs. 2.35; miami_housing: 89693 vs. 91777) but loses on the challenging cases (QSAR_fish_toxicity: 1.11 vs. 0.95; red_wine: 0.64 vs. 0.60), consistent with EBM's strength on largely additive problems. Against SepALS (the fixed-basis separable model of Beylkin et al., 2009), results are mixed: SepALS(r≤2) dominates on socmob (7.73 vs. 9.48) and naval_propulsion_plant (0.0004 vs. 0.0006), while TSL(R≤10) wins california_housing (49,376 vs. 62,162, ~21% lower RMSE), auction_verification (624 vs. 682), brazilian_houses (2399 vs. 3996), and cpu_activity (2.31 vs. 2.85); the authors attribute SepALS's edge on smooth targets to its fixed spline basis, while TSL's adaptive grid splitting captures sharper local variations. Against the tree-ensemble baselines (XGBoost, LightGBM at depth 2 in the interpretable setting), TSL is competitive or superior on most datasets, with the largest gaps on socmob (9.48 vs. 17.65–21.71) and auction_verification (624 vs. 1973–2155). In the black-box setting (depth 10/20), Random Forest and XGBoost generally outperform TSL, as expected given their greater model capacity, but TSL remains within a reasonable margin on most datasets. The TSL(1-product) ablation (one unconstrained separable product per stage, no positivity) confirms that the ordered-difference-of-positive-products structure is what gives TSL its representational flexibility at a fixed rank budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

TSL adapts the separable rank-1 product form introduced by Beylkin & Mohlenkamp (2005) for high-dimensional function approximation and the supervised separation-learning framework of Beylkin et al. (2009), but departs from prior separation learning in two key ways: it fits stagewise on residuals via CART-style grid refinement rather than solving a fixed-rank joint optimization over a predetermined basis, and it enforces positivity by modeling each stage as a difference of two positive rank-1 tensors. The approximation-rate guarantee is derived from the Orthogonal Greedy Approximation framework of Barron et al. (2008). Conceptually, the paper positions TSL against the dominant additive-decomposition paradigm (GAMs, SHAP, EBM, functional ANOVA), arguing that separability avoids the interaction-masking and signal-cancellation failures that plague additive projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TSL offers a principled alternative to additive explainability by making partial dependence plots model-native: each 1D PD curve recovers the fitted factor shape up to a scalar, so visualizations are faithful representations rather than post-hoc surrogates. The backbone/tilt parametrization further separates spatial gating from directional imbalance, giving practitioners a clean decomposition of where a stage is active versus which branch dominates. However, the method's non-identifiability under non-rectangular support, the absence of finite-sample learning-rate or consistency guarantees, and its competitive-but-not-dominant empirical performance (EBM remains stronger on largely additive problems) limit its immediate adoption. Open questions include extending the O(1/√r) approximation bound to statistical rates, reducing cross-seed variance via Riemannian aggregation on the positive rank-1 manifold, and whether the backbone/tilt form can be instantiated with neural or other flexible function approximators.
