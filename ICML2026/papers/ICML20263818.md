## Problem

Machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) transfer inconsistently across chemistries and tasks, with representations that entangle composition and geometry, making them brittle for fine-tuning and poorly suited for retrieval or exploratory analysis. The paper identifies that supervised energy/force objectives discard accessible compositional and structural information, and that standard single-stream architectures offer no mechanism to preserve or separately query these factors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263818/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes atomic species (element numbers) and 3D Cartesian positions, plus normalized lattice vector lengths and angles for periodic crystals. It produces three parallel node-level embeddings — a composition vector (from a Transformer over unique element tokens with log-count attention, computed in O(T²) independent of atom count N), a structure vector (from a type-agnostic SOAP-style power spectrum with multi-scale radial cutoffs and invariant message passing), and an interaction vector (from the base geometric GNN) — which are concatenated into the final representation. Downstream, the full embedding supports energy/force/stress regression, while individual streams enable decomposed cosine-similarity retrieval by chemistry, geometry, or jointly.

## Method

TriForces augments any existing geometric GNN backbone (MACE, eSEN, Orb-v3) with two additional streams: a composition Transformer that encodes stoichiometry via count-weighted attention over unique element tokens (preserving absolute counts rather than normalized fractions), and a structure stream that builds rotation-invariant local descriptors from mixed radial-cutoff channels and real spherical harmonics, followed by invariant message passing. Self-supervised pretraining combines three complementary objectives on stochastically augmented views: LeJEPA (non-reconstruction alignment with SIGReg regularization at both node and graph level), position denoising (MSE recovery of clean coordinates from Gaussian-perturbed inputs), and masked atom-type prediction (cross-entropy on randomly masked nodes). A key theoretical contribution shows that the coordinate-free composition stream introduces force-preserving parameter directions: because its features do not depend on positions, the mixed Hessian between composition parameters and atomic coordinates vanishes, reducing energy-force gradient coupling during conservative training and enabling direct energy optimization without sacrificing force accuracy.

## Results

On OMat24 (4M subset), TriForces Orb-v3 (conservative) reduces energy MAE from 107 to 19.4 meV/atom and force MAE from 150 to 95.5 meV/Å, while TriForces eSEN cuts energy MAE from 104 to 18.8 meV/atom. In the low-data regime (20K samples), energy per atom MAE drops 57% from 81.3 to 34.6 meV/atom. On MatBench, TriForces variants achieve the best result on 6/8 tasks, with Phonons MAE improving from 57.8 to 19.5 cm⁻¹ (TriForces eSEN) and MP Gap from 0.392 to 0.139 eV. On QM9, μ MAE improves from 0.023 to 0.018 D and polarizability α MAE from 0.098 to 0.075 a₀³ with OMol25 pretraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Orb-v3 (Rhodes et al., 2025), TriForces Orb-v3 substantially outperforms the baseline on OMat24 conservative training (energy MAE 19.4 vs 107 meV/atom; force MAE 95.5 vs 150 meV/Å) and on MatBench Discovery (MAE 0.020 vs 0.024 eV/atom, R² 0.874 vs 0.821). Against eSEN (Fu et al., 2025), TriForces eSEN achieves dramatically lower Phonons MAE on MatBench (19.5 vs 57.8 cm⁻¹) and lower energy MAE on OMat24 (18.8 vs 104 meV/atom), while the smaller TriForces eSEN-sm (12M params) nearly matches the larger eSEN-30M-OAM (30.2M params) on MatBench Discovery. Against MACE (Batatia et al., 2022), TriForces MACE improves OMat24 energy MAE from 117 to 34.3 meV/atom and MatBench Phonons from 36.7 to 27.6 cm⁻¹, though MACE† (DFT-pretrained) still leads on some MatBench tasks like Dielectric (0.279 vs 0.159 for TriForces MACE, but TriForces eSEN wins at 0.232). Against SOAP descriptors (Bartók et al., 2013) for retrieval, TriForces eSEN achieves Space Group R@10 of 0.648 versus SOAP's 0.103 (6× improvement), while SOAP retains a slight edge on Element Set R@10 (0.814 vs 0.770), confirming the structural stream's strength for geometry-based retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

TriForces builds on the geometric GNN paradigm for atomistic modeling (MACE, eSEN, Orb-v3) and the growing SSL literature for molecules and materials (denoising à la Zaidi et al., masked reconstruction à la Dong et al., representation-level objectives like Barlow Twins and Crystal Twins). It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) rather than treating SSL as a standalone pretraining recipe applied to a monolithic backbone, it introduces a model-agnostic three-stream architectural decomposition (composition, structure, interaction) that makes each SSL objective target a complementary factor; and (2) it draws on composition-only predictors (Roost, CrabNet) and SOAP-style invariant descriptors not as standalone models but as dedicated parallel streams whose outputs are concatenated with the base GNN. The LeJEPA non-reconstruction objective is adopted from the vision literature and adapted to the atomistic setting with node-level and graph-level alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical pain points in atomistic foundation models—brittle cross-task transfer and representations that are hard to reuse for analysis—by showing that architectural factorization plus multi-objective SSL yields substantially better data efficiency (57% energy MAE reduction at 20K samples) and enables interpretable decomposed retrieval by chemistry or structure. The probing results (baselines at 55–73% on crystal-system/majority-element identification vs. 96–100% for TriForces) challenge the assumption that large-scale supervised pretraining preserves all accessible structural and compositional information. Open questions include whether the approach extends to surfaces, defects, and complex interfaces; whether richer fusion operators beyond concatenation can improve downstream performance; and whether the factorized representation serves as a natural encoder for generative models (diffusion, flow matching). The plug-in design and released pretrained checkpoints make the technique immediately applicable to new architectures.
