## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods for text-to-image diffusion models are primarily designed for single-concept erasure and degrade significantly when applied to multiple concepts simultaneously. Sequential application causes re-acquisition of forgotten concepts or severe quality loss, while multi-concept methods that do exist (e.g., LoRA merging, adversarial decoupling) rely on fine-tuning, are sensitive to hyperparameters, and struggle to balance forgetting efficacy with generation fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260870/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained T2I diffusion model (SD v1.5, SD v1.4, or SDXL) along with paired text prompts per target concept: a concept prompt (e.g., 'a golf ball on the table') and a base prompt (e.g., 'a table'). During a forward pass over 50 denoising steps, it collects per-layer, per-timestep activations to compute saliency scores. The output is a binary multi-concept mask applied to the second feedforward projection (FFN₂) of each U-Net block, yielding a modified model that no longer generates the target concepts while preserving general image quality on MS COCO-30K.

## Method

FIA is a training-free, plug-and-play framework built on three stages. First, Contrastive Concept Saliency (CCS) scores each weight connection W_{ℓ,i,j} using an energy-based metric |W|·‖X‖₂·cos(X,Y) computed under concept and base prompts, then takes the positive part of (μ_c − μ_b − σ_b) to isolate neurons that respond specifically to the target concept rather than general background. Second, Concept-Sensitive Neurons are identified by integrating temporal sparsity (averaging saliency across denoising steps with an adaptive top-r₁ threshold) and spatial sparsity (intersecting channel-wise top-r₂·C_in selections with a global layer-level top-r₂·C_out·C_in ranking), ensuring selected neurons are both locally and globally competitive. Third, per-concept binary masks are fused via a concept-agnostic preservation rule: neurons whose aggregate sensitivity across all C target concepts meets or exceeds ⌈αC⌉ are retained as concept-agnostic (supporting general generation), while only neurons with 0 < s < ⌈αC⌉ are pruned. This preserves core generative capacity while removing concept-specific pathways, achieving state-of-the-art multi-concept unlearning at under 0.3% overall sparsity without any fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Imagenette 10-class multi-object unlearning task, FIA achieves an average forgetting accuracy of 1.9% (lower is better), dramatically outperforming the next-best training-free methods CP (7.34%) and UCE (8.62%), while maintaining a competitive CLIP score of 29.56 on MS COCO-30K. On explicit content unlearning (I2P dataset, SD v1.4), FIA reduces total NudeNet detections from 743 (native) to just 32, compared to 111 for the next-best method MACE, with FID 14.02 and CLIP 31.18. On multi-artist-style unlearning, FIA achieves the best average rank of 2.50 across four metrics (CLIP_a, FSR, FID, CLIP), with the lowest CLIP_a of 27.45 and highest FSR of 83.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIA achieves the lowest average forgetting accuracy (1.9%) across all 10 baselines on Imagenette, outperforming the closest training-free competitors CP (7.34%) and UCE (8.62%) by 3.9× and 4.5× respectively, and fine-tuning methods like FMN (71.89%), MACE (78.22%), and SPM (47.29%) by orders of magnitude. On CLIP score, FIA (29.56) trails slightly behind AC (31.58), SPM (30.77), and MACE (31.05), but these methods retain 47–78% forgetting accuracy, making their higher CLIP scores largely meaningless. On explicit content unlearning, FIA (32 total NudeNet detections) outperforms MACE (111), CP (148), UCE (182), and SPM (229) by 3.5×–7.3×, while matching MACE's FID (14.02 vs 13.42) and exceeding it on CLIP (31.18 vs 29.41). On artistic style unlearning, FIA achieves the best CLIP_a (27.45 vs CP's 27.90), highest FSR (83.4% vs CP's 79.6%), and best overall rank (2.50 vs MACE's 3.75), though MACE achieves a marginally lower FID (15.9 vs 16.7). FIA also demonstrates superior adversarial robustness, scoring 87.9% on Ring-A-Bell and 96.8% on MMA, compared to the next-best CP (59.8% and 94.2%). Overall, FIA wins on the primary unlearning metric in every task while maintaining competitive generation quality, with the only consistent trade-off being a ~1–2 point CLIP gap versus the strongest fine-tuning baselines that fail to actually forget the target concepts.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIA extends the neuron-pruning lineage from SNIP (Lee et al., 2018) and ConceptPrune (Chavhan et al., 2024) by repurposing unstructured connection-sensitivity pruning as a multi-concept unlearning mechanism. While prior multi-concept methods (MACE, SPM, UCE, ESD) operate through fine-tuning, LoRA merging, or closed-form cross-attention edits, FIA is the first to frame each concept as a distinct neuron mask and fuse them via a concept-agnostic preservation rule. This departs from the dominant fine-tuning paradigm by making unlearning entirely training-free and reducing it to a sparse masking problem over the feedforward projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FIA offers a practical, low-cost path to regulatory compliance (copyright, content safety) for T2I model operators: ~11 seconds per concept, under 0.3% sparsity, and no retraining. The key open question is scalability — the authors acknowledge that as target concepts grow into the hundreds, required sparsity increases and generation quality degrades, motivating future integration with fine-tuning. The discovery that a tiny set of concept-agnostic neurons (e.g., 1,348 out of ~207K pruned candidates at α=0.6) must be preserved to maintain generative fidelity is a useful inductive principle for any pruning-based editing of diffusion models.
