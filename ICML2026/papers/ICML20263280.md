## Problem

Pre-trained masked diffusion models (MDLM) make irreversible token decisions: once a token is unmasked, it is permanently frozen, so errors introduced during parallel generation cannot be corrected. Existing remasking strategies are either computationally wasteful (random remasking), unreliable (confidence-based, since unmasked tokens are uncalibrated under the MDLM training objective), or require expensive backbone retraining on synthetic noise distributions that poorly match the model's actual error patterns.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-and-play sampler on top of any frozen pre-trained masked diffusion model, requiring only a lightweight classification head (a single linear layer over RMSNorm) trained on the model's own denoising errors. The error predictor receives only the sampled clean draft x̂₀ (deliberately excluding the noisy state x_t, timestep t, and confidence scores to prevent a 'do not revise' shortcut) and outputs per-token error logits. During sampling, Gumbel-Top-K selects N tokens to remask, where N is determined by the noise schedule α_{t-1}·L, and the next state is constructed by reverting those tokens to [MASK] while keeping the rest from x̂₀.

## Method

G-Star reformulates the reverse transition using a star-shaped paradigm where all latent states are conditionally independent given x₀, enabling non-monotonic transitions (tokens can be re-masked after being unmasked). The paper proves the VLB for this process reduces to the same weighted cross-entropy form as standard MDLM, confirming compatibility with pre-trained weights without fine-tuning. A learned error predictor g_φ is trained on the actual disagreement between the frozen model's sampled draft and ground truth (not uniform noise), and is architecturally restricted to see only the draft to avoid collapsing into a confidence proxy. The star-shaped sampler is activated only in the late generation phase (t_on ≈ 0.2–0.3), where sufficient context exists for coherent refinement, while early steps use standard MDLM for stable structure-building. The predictor is implemented as a single fine-tuned block or head over the frozen 12-block backbone, adding negligible parameter overhead while achieving a ~3× speedup over 512-step ReMDM at 128 steps.

## Results

On the Conala code generation benchmark, G-Star+ achieves a conditional perplexity of 16.4 (Qwen2.5B-Coder) at 128 steps, substantially outperforming MDLM (26.7) and the best ReMDM variant (19.1). On the Dream-Instruct 7B instruction-tuned model, G-Star yields consistent gains across all seven downstream benchmarks, with the largest improvements on IFEval (+2.9 pts, 56.4→59.3) and GPQA (+1.8 pts, 31.0→32.8). In the few-step regime on OpenWebText, G-Star+ achieves higher MAUVE than unguided Star+ especially at 64–256 steps, and the 128-step G-Star-loop sampler matches or exceeds 512-step ReMDM while running ~3× faster (3.43 s vs. 9.16 s per 512-token sample).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-Star consistently outperforms all six baselines. Against MDLM (Sahoo et al., 2024), G-Star+ reduces Conala perplexity from 26.7 to 16.4 at 128 steps (−38%) and achieves higher MAUVE on OpenWebText across all step budgets. Against ReMDM (Wang et al., 2025), G-Star-loop beats ReMDM-loop (17.8 vs. 20.4 ppl) and G-Star+ beats ReMDM-cap (16.4 vs. 19.1 ppl) at 128 steps, while also being ~3× faster in wall-clock time (3.43 s vs. 9.16 s). Against confidence-based samplers P2-planner (Peng et al., 2025) and RDM (Zheng et al., 2023), G-Star-loop dominates the Pareto front in both perplexity and diversity (Fig. 5a). Against DDPD (Liu et al., 2024), G-Star achieves comparable or better quality at half the NFE cost (1× vs. 2× backbone passes per step) and without requiring a full-size guidance model. Against the autoregressive GPT-2 baseline (Radford et al., 2019), G-Star-loop at 128 steps (3.43 s) is competitive with AR-without-KV-cache (2.51 s) while producing higher-quality text, and the 1B,F variant narrows the gap to 2.60 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the star-shaped diffusion paradigm (Okhotin et al., 2023) to the masked diffusion setting, fundamentally altering its execution: rather than requiring a unique noise schedule and full retraining, G-Star applies the star-shaped transition only in the late generation phase and proves it is compatible with off-the-shelf MDLM checkpoints. It departs from ReMDM (Wang et al., 2025), which uses random remasking, by replacing stochastic token selection with a lightweight learned error predictor trained on the diffusion model's own mistakes rather than synthetic uniform noise. The approach also contrasts with confidence-based remasking (P2, RDM) and backbone-retraining methods (DDPD, von Rütte) by requiring only a single trainable head over frozen activations, positioning itself as a universal plug-and-play module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is strong: G-Star achieves a 4× step reduction (128 vs. 512) while outperforming full-budget baselines, and the error predictor generalizes across domains (code, math, news) without fine-tuning, suggesting it captures intrinsic diffusion-model error patterns rather than dataset-specific artifacts. The two-phase insight—that star-shaped sampling is destructive early but powerful late—provides a clear design principle for future refinement strategies. Open questions include whether the approach extends to continuous-token or latent-space diffusion, whether the error predictor can be distilled further, and whether the 10% refinement budget used on Dream-Instruct 7B is optimal or task-dependent.
