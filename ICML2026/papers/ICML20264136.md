## Problem

Protein structure predictors like AlphaFold output per-residue confidence scores (pLDDT) that are poorly calibrated, lack finite-sample coverage guarantees, and degrade severely under distribution shift across experimental modalities (X-ray, cryo-EM, NMR) and in intrinsically disordered regions. Existing remedies—deep ensembles, MC-dropout, and vanilla conformal prediction—either multiply inference cost, remain brittle under shift, or cannot exploit structured biochemical priors such as disorder and flexibility. CalPro addresses this gap by providing shift-robust, calibrated uncertainty with finite-sample guarantees that explicitly incorporates domain-specific structural priors.

## Input / Output

CalPro is a post-hoc layer on a pretrained predictor (AlphaFold/OpenFold) that consumes Cα coordinates, pLDDT/pTM scores, distogram logits, ESM-2 embeddings, secondary structure labels, and sequence-derived bio-priors (IUPred2A disorder, flexibility indices) to build a residue-level graph with edges encoding sequence adjacency (|i−j|≤5) and spatial proximity (Cα < 10 Å). It outputs calibrated, scale-adaptive prediction intervals for per-residue Cα distance error (in Å) after optimal superposition, clipped to [0, ∞), along with per-residue NIG parameters (μ, α, β, ν) whose induced variance σ² = β/[ν(α−1)] drives interval width. All prior features are computable from sequence and base-predictor output alone, requiring no experimental structural data at inference time.

## Method

CalPro integrates three components: (i) a geometric evidential head implemented as a 3-layer GNN with residue-wise readouts that outputs Normal Inverse Gamma parameters in a single forward pass, enabling heteroscedastic uncertainty without sampling; (ii) a differentiable log-sum-exp calibration surrogate during training that encourages well-behaved score distributions, followed by standard split-conformal calibration on held-out proteins at test time for exact finite-sample marginal coverage; and (iii) a prior-aware monotone regularizer L_prior = λ Σ max(0, m(b(x_i)) − u(x_i)) that enforces higher prior volatility induces larger epistemic variance, distinct from simply feeding priors as input features. The nonconformity score is scale-adaptive, s_i = |y_i − μ_i|/(σ_i + ε), so intervals widen in disordered regions and tighten in stable ones while preserving conformal validity. Theoretically, the paper derives PAC-Bayesian sensitivity bounds under Wasserstein-1 ambiguity sets where the shift-sensitivity constants (L_s, M_s) are structure-dependent: GNN message passing reduces L_s (5.1→2.3) and the prior regularizer flattens the score distribution to reduce M_s (2.6→1.8), transitioning the bound from vacuous (MLP) to non-vacuous and empirically tight (CalPro).

## Results

On the held-out X-ray test set at 90% nominal coverage, CalPro achieves 90.1% empirical coverage with ECE of 0.021 and mean interval width (sharpness) of 1.42 Å, outperforming CQR (89.6%, ECE 0.024, 1.43 Å) and deep ensembles (89.9%, ECE 0.026, 1.47 Å). Under X-ray→cryo-EM modality shift, CalPro degrades by only 3.8 percentage points (to 86.2% coverage), compared to 14–18 pp for pLDDT and vanilla conformal, and 6.9–9.7 pp for shift-aware CP variants (IW-CP, Loc-CP, DR-CP) and graph-CP methods (DAPS, CF-GNN). Filtering PDBbind docking by CalPro interval width improves success from 52% to 75% (vs. 64% for pLDDT and 70% for CQR), and on the non-biological UCI Bike Sharing benchmark CalPro reaches 90.0% coverage with 15% tighter intervals on clear-weather hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CalPro outperforms all nine baselines on the primary in-distribution calibration task: it achieves 90.1% coverage at 90% nominal with the lowest ECE (0.021) and competitive sharpness (1.42 Å), beating CQR (89.6%, ECE 0.024) and deep ensembles (89.9%, ECE 0.026, 1.47 Å width). Under X-ray→cryo-EM modality shift, the gap widens dramatically: CalPro degrades by only 3.8 pp (to 86.2%), while pLDDT drops 17.6 pp, vanilla conformal 14.1 pp, CQR 6.3 pp, and even the shift-aware variants IW-CP (8.2 pp), Loc-CP (7.6 pp), and DR-CP (6.9 pp) all underperform CalPro. Graph-based CP methods DAPS (9.7 pp degradation) and CF-GNN (8.9 pp) fare worse than CalPro because they lack the evidential head and prior regularizer. On downstream docking (PDBbind), CalPro's uncertainty filtering yields 74.8% success vs. 70.1% for CQR and 64.2% for pLDDT, and at matched top-30% retention CalPro reaches 80.4% vs. 76.5% for CQR. On the non-biological UCI Bike Sharing benchmark, CalPro achieves 90.0% coverage with ECE 0.021, outperforming CQR (88.7%, 0.026) and deep ensembles (88.2%, 0.029), confirming the framework transfers beyond proteins.

## Relation to Prior Work

CalPro synthesizes three previously separate threads—evidential deep learning (Amini et al., 2020) for single-pass NIG uncertainty, split conformal prediction (Vovk et al., 2005) for finite-sample coverage, and PAC-Bayes analysis of conformal predictors (Sharma et al., 2023)—into a unified pipeline for protein structure uncertainty. The key conceptual departure is the distinction between priors-as-features (inputs to the GNN) and priors-as-constraints (a monotone regularizer shaping the epistemic variance surface), which the authors show is essential for group-wise uncertainty calibration that feature injection alone cannot achieve. The theoretical contribution extends Sharma et al.'s i.i.d. PAC-Bayes certificates to a Wasserstein-ambiguity-set shift setting, introducing structurally-controlled sensitivity constants (L_s, M_s) that the architecture itself can reduce via GNN message passing and prior regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AlphaFold predictions enter high-stakes pipelines (molecular docking, variant prioritization), the lack of calibrated, shift-robust uncertainty is a practical bottleneck; CalPro's 3.8pp coverage degradation under modality shift (vs. 6–18pp for baselines) and 52%→75% docking improvement demonstrate that principled UQ translates to downstream decision quality. Key limitations include inheritance of base-predictor biases (particularly for fully disordered proteins where coverage still falls 5.4pp short of nominal), conditional independence at the conformal layer, and dependence on held-out calibration data. Open directions include joint 3D conformational coverage via conformal risk control, validation on newer backbones (AF3, Boltz-2), and direct optimization of the PAC-Bayesian sensitivity terms during training.
