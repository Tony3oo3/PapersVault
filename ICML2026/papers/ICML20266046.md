## Problem

Offline policy improvement faces a geometric conflict: in-sample weighted regression is stable but overconservative, suppressing rare high-value actions in the distribution tail, while value-gradient-based optimization pushes policies off the data manifold because critic extrapolation creates spurious peaks with large normal components. No existing method simultaneously preserves the data support and extracts meaningful gains from suboptimal baselines without risking out-of-distribution collapse.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of state-action pairs (D4RL benchmark: MuJoCo locomotion, Adroit manipulation, AntMaze navigation) and produces a deterministic or stochastic action for a given state observation. The policy is structured as a frozen BC anchor plus a bounded residual correction, where the residual magnitude is constrained by the (1−ρ)-quantile of dataset residuals. At deployment, a dual-threshold gate (absolute ΔQ > 10⁻⁴ and relative ΔQ/|Q_base| > 0.01) decides whether to emit the rectified action or fall back to the base action, making the output conditionally modified rather than always different from the anchor.

## Method

SPAR is a three-stage pipeline that decouples fitting from improvement. Stage I trains a pure behavior-cloning base policy (frozen thereafter) and a conservative critic ensemble via in-sample expectile regression with a Lower Confidence Bound score. Stage II learns a residual policy in the contracted local space around the anchor: a deterministic MLP for unimodal residuals (SPAR-MLP) or a conditional VAE for multimodal residuals (SPAR-PROJ), both trained with advantage-weighted regression. The key mechanism for SPAR-PROJ is Latent Self-Imitation: instead of gradient ascent on the critic, it samples K latent candidates from an EMA target CVAE, scores them with the frozen critic, and regresses the student decoder toward the value-weighted convex combination of decoded residuals — a derivative-free update that confines drift to O(η²) chord deviations rather than O(η) linear off-manifold drift. Stage III applies a value-gated rectification at inference, accepting the residual only when robust improvement exceeds fixed thresholds.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark (10 tasks across MuJoCo, Adroit, and AntMaze), SPAR achieves state-of-the-art normalized scores on 7 of 10 tasks. SPAR-MLP leads on MuJoCo Medium-Replay (e.g., Hopper-MR: 101.9 vs. Diff-QL's 101.3; HalfCheetah-MR: 50.9 vs. CQL-AW's 49.0), while SPAR-PROJ dominates multimodal and sparse-reward settings (e.g., Pen-Cloned: 76.2 vs. Diff-QL's 66.2; AntMaze-Umaze-Div: 76.7 vs. PLAS's 62.0; Walker2d-ME: 113.4 vs. IDQL's 111.6). SPAR trails Diff-QL on three tasks (Walker2d-MR: 94.0 vs. 95.5; Hopper-ME: 108.7 vs. 111.1; AntMaze-Large-Div: 71.0 vs. 72.8). The method uses a fixed BC anchor across all experiments, isolating the contribution of residual rectification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPAR-MLP outperforms all in-support methods (IQL, AWAC, CQL-AW, IDQL) on MuJoCo Medium-Replay, with the largest margin over CQL-AW on HalfCheetah-MR (+1.9) and over IQL on Hopper-MR (+7.2). Against gradient-based baselines, SPAR-MLP edges out Diff-QL on Hopper-MR (101.9 vs. 101.3) but trails it on Walker2d-MR (94.0 vs. 95.5). SPAR-PROJ achieves the largest gains in high-dimensional and sparse-reward settings: on Adroit Pen-Cloned it surpasses the next-best method (Diff-QL, 66.2) by 10.0 points, and on AntMaze-Umaze-Div it leads PLAS (62.0) by 14.7 points. On MuJoCo Medium-Expert, SPAR-PROJ wins on HalfCheetah-ME (97.0 vs. Diff-QL's 96.8) and Walker2d-ME (113.4 vs. IDQL's 111.6) but falls short of Diff-QL on Hopper-ME (108.7 vs. 111.1). Against recent baselines (DAC, FQL, QIPO in Appendix C.2.1), SPAR-PROJ leads on 3 of 4 tasks (HP-MR: 101.9 vs. QIPO 101.2; AM-LD: 71.0 vs. DAC 60.0) but trails FQL on HC-ME (97.0 vs. 106.1). The deterministic SPAR-MLP collapses on multimodal tasks (e.g., 1.1 on Hopper-ME, 0.1 on Pen-Cloned), confirming that the generative SPAR-PROJ variant is necessary for non-unimodal residual geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPAR synthesizes three established threads—residual policy learning (Silver et al., 2018), advantage-weighted regression (Peng et al., 2019), and IQL-style expectile critics (Kostrikov et al., 2021)—into a unified three-stage pipeline that decouples distribution fitting from value-guided improvement. Its key departure from prior work is the reframing of offline policy improvement as a *local* residual rectification problem anchored to a frozen BC policy, combined with a derivative-free 'Latent Self-Imitation' mechanism that replaces unstable value-gradient ascent with latent-sampling weighted regression in the residual space. This contrasts with both in-support methods (AWR, IQL) that over-constrain to high-density regions, and gradient-based methods (BCQ, CQL, Diff-QL) that risk OOD drift, positioning SPAR as a geometrically grounded middle path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The residual-anchoring design offers a practical, interpretable recipe for offline improvement that avoids the computational overhead of iterative diffusion/flow policies while matching or exceeding them on D4RL, and the theoretical results (search-space contraction, second-order drift suppression) provide a principled justification for why the approach works. Limitations include dependence on the quality of the frozen BC anchor, higher inference cost for the generative SPAR-PROJ variant, and validation restricted to D4RL benchmarks. Open questions include whether the framework extends to continuous online fine-tuning, whether automatic residual-topology diagnostics can replace manual architecture selection, and whether stronger offline RL guarantees (beyond the mechanism-level analysis) can be established.
