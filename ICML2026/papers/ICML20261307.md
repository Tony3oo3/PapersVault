## Problem

Existing LLM-based termination verifiers (e.g., PROTON 2.1, LOOPY) rely on ranking functions as termination arguments, which are fragile and difficult for LLMs to synthesize correctly due to their strict decrease requirements. Moreover, when a candidate termination argument is invalid, LLM-based invariant learners cannot recognize the unrealizability of the verification task and waste computation futilely strengthening invariants. This paper addresses both gaps by shifting the synthesis target to the more LLM-friendly loop bounds and introducing a violation analysis mechanism that promptly refutes invalid bounds.

## Input / Output

The method takes C programs containing while loops (with initial conditions and loop guards) as input and produces either a validated loop bound expression (conjunctive linear arithmetic constraints or lexicographic tuples over program variables) that constitutes a formal termination proof, or a concrete violation witness (a reachable state where the counter drops to zero) that refutes the proposed bound. Generated bounds are constrained to linear arithmetic with conjunctions only (no division, no nonlinear operators, no disjunction), and the validation pipeline outputs one of three verdicts: Valid, Invalid (with witness state), or Timeout.

## Method

LIFT couples an LLM-based loop bound generator with a sound formal validator in a guess-and-check loop. The generator uses a progressive strategy: it first prompts the LLM for simple conjunctive bounds (a single counter with linear constraints), and only escalates to lexicographic bounds (tuples of counters with lexicographic decrease semantics) if the simpler form repeatedly fails. The validator handles constant bounds via bounded unrolling and symbolic bounds via invariant-based verification using ICE-DT as the invariant learner and Boogie/SMT as the verifier, augmented with k-induction. The key technical contribution is Bounded Violation Analysis: when the verifier reports a safety failure, the system unrolls the loop up to m steps to check whether the counterexample state is actually reachable, thereby distinguishing an invalid loop bound (abort and return the witness) from a merely weak invariant (feed back for refinement). Validation outcomes are translated into structured diagnostic feedback—violation witnesses, timeout diagnoses identifying semantic mismatches, and syntax corrections—that guide the LLM's next candidate, with an additional constant-offset correction heuristic to handle common off-by-small-constant errors.

## Results

On the 171-program FREQTERM termination benchmark, LIFT (Gemini-2.5-Flash) solves 152 instances, outperforming all conventional and LLM-based baselines. The best conventional tool, DDLTERM, solves 134 instances, while the best LLM-based baseline, PROTON 2.1 (fine-tuned Llama-1B), solves 144. In terms of efficiency, LIFT averages 189.4 s on solved instances (Gemini) or 114.9 s (DeepSeek-V3.1), compared to 4.4–13.9 s for conventional tools, reflecting the inherent latency of LLM inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIFT (Gemini) solves 152/171 benchmarks, beating the best LLM-based baseline PROTON 2.1 (144) by 8 instances and LOOPY (125) by 27 instances, while also surpassing the strongest conventional tool DDLTERM (134) by 18 instances. The exclusive-solved counts confirm LIFT's advantage is not a different subset: LIFT uniquely solves 14–67 instances that each baseline misses, whereas baselines uniquely solve only 1–15 instances that LIFT fails. Against LOOPY specifically, even when LOOPY is augmented with a simple feedback iteration loop (LOOPY+Iter, 139 solved), LIFT still leads by 13 instances and is 1.8× faster (189.4 s vs. 336.2 s). Conventional tools (DDLTERM, UAUTOMIZER, FREQTERM) are 10–40× faster on solved instances but solve substantially fewer benchmarks, highlighting the coverage gain from LLM-guided loop bound generation. LIFT's soundness guarantee (SMT-based validation via Boogie) also distinguishes it from PROTON 2.1, whose bounded model checking (k=3) provides weaker soundness guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

LIFT extends the 'guess-and-check' termination verification paradigm (FREQTERM, DDLTERM) by replacing syntactic templates or geometric optimization with LLMs as the hypothesis generator, while retaining a sound formal validator. It departs from prior LLM-based termination tools (LOOPY, PROTON 2.1) in two key ways: (1) shifting the synthesis target from fragile ranking functions to more robust loop bounds, and (2) integrating bounded violation analysis with ICE-DT to address the 'refutation gap' where LLM-based invariant learners iterate fruitlessly on unrealizable verification tasks. The progressive generation strategy (conjunctive → lexicographic) and diagnostic feedback loop represent a structured interaction protocol not present in the one-shot or simple-iteration designs of LOOPY and PROTON.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LIFT demonstrates that LLMs can effectively serve as hypothesis generators in formal verification when paired with a strong non-LLM validation backend, solving 152/171 benchmarks and outperforming both LLM-based and conventional tools. The finding that ICE-DT outperforms LLM-based invariant learners (LaM4Inv, LEMUR) as the validation backend is a useful practical signal: the LLM's role is best confined to semantic hypothesis generation rather than invariant synthesis. Open questions include scaling to programs with complex nonlinear arithmetic, reducing the latency of multi-round LLM interaction, and whether the progressive generation strategy generalizes beyond the single-loop setting the paper primarily evaluates.
