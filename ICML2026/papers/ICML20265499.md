## Problem

Most VLN-CE methods execute actions in an open-loop manner at inference time, with no mechanism to detect or correct internal state drift before errors accumulate. External supervision is sparse and delayed (success is only observable at the trajectory level), making classical closed-loop control infeasible. The paper asks whether an agent can extract internal feedback signals from its own world-model foresight to enable self-correction during navigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265499/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives sequential RGB-D observations (12 views at 30-degree intervals for panoramic; a single front view for monocular) and a natural language instruction, and outputs a waypoint selection from a set of candidate navigation waypoints at each timestep. The method is plug-and-play: it wraps around existing VLN-CE backbones (VLN-3DFF for monocular, ETPNav or HNR for panoramic), adding a latent-state refinement step and optional test-time world-model updates without modifying the base policy architecture. The refined latent state s_t^(Delta) is the intermediate output that feeds the final action policy, while the world model's foresight state is used only for feedback computation and is not directly executed.

## Method

SC2-WM introduces a dual-level self-correction mechanism built on a latent world model. At the state level, a foresight policy proposes a provisional action, the world model's prior transition predicts a future latent state, and the discrepancy Delta_t is fed through a learnable gating network to produce a refined state before the final action is selected; a Visual Calibration Module (VCM) with episodic cross-attention memory stabilizes the visual input so that state shifts reflect action-relevant dynamics rather than perceptual noise. At the model level, a conditional adaptation trigger monitors the KL divergence between pre- and post-refinement action distributions and, when it exceeds a dynamic percentile threshold, performs a single gradient step on the world model using a self-supervised visual reconstruction loss (FRL). Training combines imitation-learning action supervision on both the original and refined states, a KL regularizer aligning the prior and posterior transitions, and the reconstruction loss that forces the foresight state to anticipate next-step visual features encoded by a frozen ViT.

## Results

On R2R-CE Val Unseen (monocular), SC²-WM achieves 50.9% SR and 37.2% SPL, improving over the VLN-3DFF baseline by +7.1% SR and +7.8% SPL while reducing trajectory length from 26.16 m to 17.65 m. In the panoramic setting (built on HNR), SC²-WM reaches 66% SR and 54% SPL on Val Unseen, outperforming HNR by +5% SR and +3% SPL. On the more challenging RxR-CE Val Unseen (monocular), the method achieves 35.8% SR and 27.2% SPL, a +9.1% SR gain over VLN-3DFF. The panoramic HNR-based variant on RxR-CE attains 60% SR and 50% SPL, surpassing HNR by +4% SR and +3% SPL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the monocular R2R-CE setting, SC²-WM outperforms its base model VLN-3DFF by +7.1% SR and +7.8% SPL on Val Unseen, and also surpasses NavMorph (+3.0% SR, +4.0% SPL) and g3D-LF (+3.7% SR, +2.6% SPL) on the same split. In the panoramic setting, SC²-WM built on HNR achieves the best overall performance, beating HNR by +5% SR and +3% SPL on Val Unseen, and outperforming NavMorph (+2% SR, +1% SPL) and g3D-LF (+5% SR, +2% SPL). On the harder RxR-CE benchmark, the monocular variant gains +9.1% SR and +7.1% SPL over VLN-3DFF, while the panoramic HNR-based variant improves SR from 56% to 60% and SPL from 47% to 50%. The method consistently reduces trajectory length across all settings (e.g., 26.16→17.65 m on R2R-CE monocular Val Unseen), indicating more efficient navigation. The only method that outperforms SC²-WM on R2R-CE monocular is the closed-source monoVLN (54.8% SR), which relies on more expressive 3DGS-based feature fields not available for integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VLN-CE task established by Krantz et al. (2020) and integrates its self-correcting world model into existing lightweight navigation architectures (VLN-3DFF for monocular, ETPNav and HNR for panoramic). It departs from prior world-model-based VLN methods (DreamWalker, NavMorph) that operate in an open-loop fashion, and from test-time adaptation approaches (FSTTA) that rely on output-level statistics like entropy, by instead deriving a computable internal feedback signal from the world model's own foresight prediction. The dual-level correction (state-level plan refinement + conditional model-level adaptation) is positioned as a principled alternative to both reinforcement-learning-based closed-loop control and MPC, which the authors argue suffer from training instability and manual reward design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that effective closed-loop self-correction in VLN-CE can be achieved with a compact model (single RTX 3090, <40h training) without resorting to large MLLMs or RL, which is practically important for edge deployment. The real-world validation on a Unitree GO2 quadruped robot strengthens the sim-to-real claim. Open questions remain around whether the feedback signal quality scales with world-model expressiveness (e.g., uncertainty-aware or structured world models), whether the test-time adaptation can be made efficient enough for resource-constrained platforms, and how the framework extends to longer-horizon or multi-agent navigation.
