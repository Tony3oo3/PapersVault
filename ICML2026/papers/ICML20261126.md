## Problem

Train-free frame-level autoregressive video generation (e.g., FIFO-Diffusion) can produce infinitely long videos with constant memory, but suffers from a significant training-inference gap—the model is trained on latents at a uniform noise level yet must process latents spanning many noise levels during inference—and from insufficient long-range consistency modeling, leading to content drift and visual artifacts over extended sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261126/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts one or multiple text prompts (with different prompts assigned to different temporal positions for narrative control) and produces an unbounded sequence of clean video latents decoded to frames, with constant memory regardless of output length. It operates on top of off-the-shelf foundation models (VideoCrafter2 generating 16 latents, Wan2.1-1.3B generating 21 latents per pass) that natively support only 81 frames, extending them to 1000+ frame videos without any fine-tuning.

## Method

MIGA introduces two complementary mechanisms on top of the FIFO-Diffusion noise-queue framework. First, a Two-Stage Training-Inference Alignment (TTA) reduces the noise span fed to the model: Stage 1 maintains a zigzag-structured queue where noise level changes every L_zig latents rather than every single latent, and Stage 2 performs unified denoising at a single noise level once all latents converge, matching training conditions. Second, a Dual Consistency Enhancement (DCE) mechanism addresses long-term coherence: a self-reflection approach computes cosine similarity between adjacent latent chunks at early high-noise positions to detect consistency anomalies and triggers an expanded search with progressively guided re-sampling for correction, while a long-range frame guidance approach sparsely samples m_guid clean latents from earlier queue positions and concatenates them into each local sliding-window denoising step to enable distant-frame feature interactions.

## Results

On VBench (VideoCrafter2 backbone, 128 frames), MIGA achieves an Overall Score of 97.82, outperforming the strongest finite-length baseline FreeLong (96.95) and the infinite-frame baselines FIFO-Diffusion (95.02) and ScalingNoise (95.95). On the Wan2.1 backbone (161 frames), MIGA reaches 97.24 O.S. versus FIFO-Diffusion's 95.29, with gains of +3.79 in subject consistency and +2.13 in background consistency. On NarrLV (TNA=4, Wan2.1), MIGA scores 75.05/72.31/62.90 on scene/target attributes and target actions, substantially exceeding FIFO-Diffusion (57.43/56.10/53.89).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIGA wins across all VBench metrics against every baseline on both backbones. Against the strongest finite-length method FreeLong (96.95 O.S.), MIGA gains +0.87 O.S. while also supporting infinite-frame generation, which FreeLong cannot. Against the closest infinite-frame competitor ScalingNoise (95.95 O.S.), MIGA improves by +1.87 O.S., with the largest gap in subject consistency (+3.37). Against FIFO-Diffusion, which shares the same autoregressive framework, MIGA achieves +2.80 O.S. on VideoCrafter2 and +1.95 O.S. on Wan2.1, confirming that the TTA and DCE mechanisms provide complementary gains over the baseline queue design. On NarrLV, MIGA consistently outperforms FIFO-Diffusion across all TNA settings (e.g., +12.62 on scene attributes at TNA=4 with Wan2.1), demonstrating superior narrative expressiveness enabled by more stable long-video content.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIGA builds directly on FIFO-Diffusion's frame-level autoregressive generation paradigm, which maintains a noise queue with progressively increasing noise levels to enable infinite-frame video generation with constant memory. It departs from FIFO-Diffusion by addressing two specific limitations: the training-inference gap (where the model sees a wide noise span at inference but only uniform noise during training) and the absence of explicit long-range consistency modeling. The self-reflection mechanism adapts test-time scaling concepts from LLMs to video generation, while the long-range frame guidance extends the standard sliding-window denoising to incorporate sparse distant latents. Unlike finite-extension methods (FreeNoise, FreeLong, FreePCA) that scale memory with frame count, MIGA retains the autoregressive framework's constant-memory property while improving quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage alignment mechanism provides a practical, architecture-agnostic solution to the fundamental training-inference mismatch in train-free autoregressive video generation, and the self-reflection approach eliminates the need for external consistency evaluators (e.g., DINO) by exploiting latent-space cosine similarity at high noise levels. A notable limitation is that the frame-level autoregressive framework does not transfer to MMDiT-based architectures (e.g., CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo) due to their joint text-video-noise conditioning, restricting applicability to cross-attention-based models. Open questions include whether the zigzag width and self-reflection threshold require per-model tuning, and whether the consistency advantage of animation-style generation (VideoCrafter2) over realistic-style (Wan2.1) reflects a fundamental difficulty in maintaining identity across diverse realistic content.
