## Problem

Existing audio-visual generation benchmarks rely on unimodal quality scores and generic cross-modal consistency measures that cannot capture the fine-grained beat-level coupling between musical rhythm and choreographic motion required in music-dance co-generation. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates whether generated dance accents actually land on musical beats, nor does it provide a unified protocol combining physical alignment metrics with perceptual rhythmic judgments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265466/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single text prompt specifying music attributes (genre, tempo, instrumentation, rhythm) and dance attributes (style, performer count, scene) as input, and jointly produces a music audio track (16 kHz) and a synchronized dance video (480×480, 24 fps, 117 frames per clip) in one generation pass. The TMD-Bench evaluation set comprises 100 diverse prompts, and outputs are assessed along three orthogonal axes: unimodal generation quality, instruction adherence via structured semantic labels, and cross-modal rhythmic alignment measured by beat-centric physical metrics and MLLM perceptual scoring.

## Method

RhyJAM is a unified flow-matching diffusion model that encodes audio and video into separate latent spaces but applies a shared noise schedule α(t), σ(t) at the same diffusion timestep, so both modalities are denoised jointly under a single velocity-field prediction. Each fusion layer performs three sequential attention operations—S_attn for intra-modal contextualization, T_attn for text conditioning, and X_attn for cross-modal information exchange—before modality-specific heads predict their respective velocity fields. The evaluation framework MDAlign introduces two physical alignment metrics: VBCS (Gaussian-weighted temporal proximity of motion accents to musical beats) and ABHS (fraction of beats covered by a motion response within a hit window), complemented by a Gemini 3.0 Pro MLLM perceptual track that scores rhythmic coherence on a 1–5 scale. A Music Captioner fine-tuned from Qwen-Omni provides structured six-dimension semantic labels (instruments, rhythm, tempo, genre, ambiance, functional scenes) enabling fine-grained instruction-adherence verification without closed-source LLM calls.

## Results

On the TMD-Bench rhythmic alignment evaluation (Table 4), RhyJAM achieves the best Audio Beat Hit Score (ABHS = 0.27) and ties with Sora 2 and Veo 3 on the overall alignment average (0.59), while maintaining the lowest temporal dispersion (HSD = 0.12). In music instruction following (Table 2), RhyJAM attains the strongest Rhythm & Groove score (0.59) and competitive Tempo (0.51), outperforming all baselines on rhythm-relevant dimensions. On video generation (Table 3), RhyJAM achieves a balanced profile (Avg = 0.71) but trails top closed-source systems like Veo 3 (0.78) and Wan 2.6 (0.79) on high-level perceptual quality and instruction following.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RhyJAM ties with Sora 2 and Veo 3 on the overall alignment average (0.59) but decisively wins on beat coverage (ABHS = 0.27 vs. 0.16 for Sora 2 and 0.22 for Veo 3), indicating that while commercial models place motion accents near beats, they miss more beats entirely. Against cascaded pipelines, RhyJAM outperforms Wan+Foley (0.59 vs. 0.57) and Ace+Wan (0.59 vs. 0.51) on the alignment average, with the gap driven by superior ABHS. Among open-source unified models, RhyJAM leads all baselines on the alignment average (0.59 vs. 0.50 for JavisDiT, 0.49 for LTX-2, 0.48 for Ovi, 0.47 for Universe-1), with the largest margin over Ovi (0.59 vs. 0.48) which shows the weakest VBCS (0.30). On music instruction following, RhyJAM achieves the best Rhythm & Groove score (0.59) and Tempo (0.51), surpassing Veo 3 (0.57/0.35) and Sora 2 (0.53/0.34), though it trails closed-source systems on compositional attributes like Genre and Functional Scenes. On video quality, RhyJAM (Avg = 0.71) remains behind Veo 3 (0.78), Wan 2.6 (0.79), and Sora 2 (0.74), with the gap concentrated in high-level perceptual quality (0.37 vs. 0.60 for Veo 3) and instruction following (0.56 vs. 0.91 for Sora 2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: music-driven motion synthesis (MoMu-Diffusion, X-Dancer) and unified audio-video diffusion generation (JavisDiT, Ovi, LTX-2, UniVerse-1). It departs from prior audio-visual evaluation work (VABench, VBench) by arguing that generic cross-modal consistency metrics are insufficient for music-dance, where fine-grained beat-level rhythmic coupling is the defining challenge. The RhyJAM model extends the flow-matching unified diffusion paradigm with a shared noise schedule and cross-modal attention specifically designed to enforce temporal alignment between audio and video latents, while the Music Captioner is fine-tuned from Qwen-Omni to provide structured six-dimensional music semantics for instruction-following evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TMD-Bench is the first benchmark purpose-built for music-dance co-generation, and its central finding—that even top commercial systems (Sora 2, Veo 3) achieve only competitive (not dominant) beat-level rhythmic alignment—suggests a systematic gap in current audio-video generation that unimodal quality metrics mask. The demonstration that a unified open-source model (RhyJAM) can match or exceed commercial systems on rhythmic alignment while remaining competitive on unimodal fidelity provides a concrete open-source reference point and validates the unified-architecture hypothesis for cross-modal consistency. Open questions include whether the 100-prompt test set is sufficiently diverse, whether the MLLM-as-a-Judge (Gemini 3.0 Pro) truly captures human rhythmic perception at scale, and whether the gap between open-source and closed-source unimodal quality can be closed without sacrificing alignment.
