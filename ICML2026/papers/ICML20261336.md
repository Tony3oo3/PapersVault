## Problem

While language is increasingly modeled in non-Euclidean geometries (hyperbolic embeddings, mixed-curvature architectures), curvature has always been an architectural prior rather than a measurable property of text itself. No text-native formalism exists to detect, define, or quantify curvature at the word-in-context level, leaving it unresolved whether natural language text genuinely exhibits non-flat two-sided inference structure or whether curved representations are merely a convenient inductive bias.

## Input / Output

The core operator takes a token sequence with a designated slot position and evaluates a frozen masked LM (distilroberta-base) on a radius grid {0,1,2,4,8,16}² to build a two-sided posterior field over a finite per-slot state space (top-k candidates from prefix/suffix beliefs plus a tail bucket). It outputs a signed curvature scalar κ_i ∈ [-1,1] per slot, where positive values indicate focus (context from one side contracts the other's semantic effect) and negative values indicate fan-out (context expands into competing continuations). For the utility controllers, CURVPRUNE additionally takes a query, long context, and token budget B to produce selected spans, while CURVFLAG takes a query or draft sequence and outputs an integer retrieval budget k via an affine clip on the aggregated fan-out mass.

## Method

Texture defines curvature as the differential of reconciliation: rather than scoring a single chord between prefix and suffix beliefs, it measures how the two-sided posterior field μ_i(L,R) deforms across a small 2×2 context rectangle using a debiased Sinkhorn divergence (entropic optimal transport with self-bias removed) as a neutral semantic distance. The signed cell score s_i(g) compares the left-increment edge length before and after extending the right context (and symmetrically for the right axis), yielding a two-axis Ricci-type contraction: positive when parallel transport of evidence increments along one axis is shortened by motion along the other (focus), negative when it is lengthened (fan-out). The per-slot curvature κ_i is an activity-weighted average over grid cells, where the activity gate is built from the same non-flatness certificates (holonomy magnitude and CEI) that establish curvature exists, making the confidence signal self-referential. Crucially, the entire pipeline is training-free and generator-agnostic: curvature is computed by a small frozen belief oracle, while the downstream LLM (Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, Qwen-2.5-7B) consumes the curvature-controlled context as a black box, enabling plug-in compression (CURVPRUNE) and retrieval routing (CURVFLAG) without any geometric training.

## Results

On LongBench pruning at B=2048 tokens, BM25+Texture achieves 38.4 F1 on HotpotQA and 15.0 F1 on 2WikiMQA, a +65% and +38% relative F1 gain over BM25 alone (28.1 and 9.4 respectively), while remaining within noise on the three non-multi-hop tasks (GovReport, Qasper, QMSum). On routing, CurvFlag reaches 49.1 F1 on HotpotQA and 43.0 F1 on 2WikiMQA using ~3,300 tokens, Pareto-dominating Self-Route (45.4/37.7 F1 at ~7,000/5,800 tokens) with roughly 2× fewer tokens. The curvature-triggered variants FLARE+Texture and Self-Route+Texture add +13% and +7% relative F1 over their unmodified baselines at a fraction of the token cost. Gains are concentrated on multi-hop QA where bridging evidence is dispersed, and are neutral where a single lexical anchor suffices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BM25 (the strongest query-aware pruning baseline), BM25+Texture improves HotpotQA F1 from 28.1 to 38.4 (+65% relative) and 2WikiMQA from 9.4 to 15.0 (+38%), while staying within noise on GovReport, Qasper, and QMSum—confirming that curvature re-allocates budget specifically toward dispersed bridging evidence. Against learned compression baselines Selective Context and LLMLingua, CurvPrune-Hybrid matches or exceeds them on 2WikiMQA (20.6 vs. 19.8/20.5) and Qasper (8.5 vs. 6.9/6.3), and substantially outperforms both on HotpotQA (32.5 vs. 18.7/16.3). On routing, CurvFlag (49.1 F1, 3,344 tokens on HotpotQA) Pareto-dominates Self-Route (45.4 F1, 7,096 tokens) with ~2× fewer tokens, and also beats FLARE (39.1 F1, 175 tokens) on F1 while using ~19× more tokens—a trade-off the paper frames as a cost-quality frontier. The curvature-triggered variants FLARE+Texture (45.6 F1, 198 tokens) and Self-Route+Texture (47.6 F1, 256 tokens) both improve over their unmodified baselines (+13% and +7% relative F1) at only a small token overhead, showing that the curvature signal is portable as a lightweight trigger on top of existing routing heuristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work on non-Euclidean language representations (hyperbolic embeddings, mixed-curvature architectures) by refusing to treat curvature as an architectural or embedding-space choice. Instead, it repurposes Ollivier-Ricci-style contraction and Schrödinger-bridge/Sinkhorn transport machinery—previously applied to graphs and discrete probability spaces—to define a text-native, word-level curvature operator on two-sided belief fields extracted from a frozen masked LM. The existence certificates (holonomy and product-of-experts additivity) draw on classical differential-geometry and Bayesian-inference primitives but are, to the authors' knowledge, the first falsifiable flatness nulls applied to natural-language slot inference. On the utility side, the curvature signal is positioned as a complement to, not a replacement for, existing prompt-compression and RAG pipelines (BM25, LLMLingua, FLARE, Self-Route), serving as a generator-agnostic routing and budgeting primitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that two-sided inference over natural text is generically non-flat via definition-independent certificates, providing a foundation that any future text-curvature formalism must account for. Practically, the training-free, generator-agnostic control signal (CURVPRUNE, CURVFLAG) offers a drop-in mechanism for long-context systems without retraining, though gains are concentrated on multi-hop QA and modest in absolute terms (roughly 5–10 F1 points). Key limitations include extractor-dependent curvature magnitude, the transversal (relational) instantiation being only weakly correlated with the longitudinal signal (Spearman ρ≈0.20), and unexplored scaling to very long contexts or non-English languages. Open directions flagged by the authors include heat-kernel limits connecting the discrete operator to continuous Ricci curvature and curvature-guided generation, which could extend the framework beyond inference-time control into training.
