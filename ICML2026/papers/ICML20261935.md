## Problem

Visual Autoregressive Modeling (VAR) achieves strong generation quality through next-scale prediction, but its deep explicit backbone fixes the amount of computation per scale, inflating memory at high resolutions and preventing compute-on-demand behavior needed for edge deployment and low-latency serving. No prior method decouples per-scale compute from architectural depth in a next-scale AR setting, leaving a gap between generation quality and practical deployability.

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation, the model takes a class label and produces a 256×256 RGB image by autoregressively predicting multi-scale VQVAE token maps from coarse to fine. For zero-shot inpainting, it receives a masked image plus class label and generates only the masked region; for class-conditional editing, it takes an image, a bounding box, and a class label, synthesizing content inside the box while keeping the rest fixed. A key operational detail is that the per-scale fixed-point iteration count is a tunable inference-time knob (e.g., 10 iterations at all scales vs. 20 coarse / 5 fine), allowing the same trained model to trade quality against latency and memory without retraining.

## Method

VIAR replaces VAR's deep explicit middle stack with a single implicit equilibrium (DEQ) layer sandwiched between p=5 explicit pre-blocks and p=5 post-blocks. At each scale, the pre-blocks produce an injected representation x_k, which is then refined by solving the fixed-point equation z* = f_imp(Proj([z*, x_k]), c; θ_imp) via iterative application of a single transformer block; the post-blocks map the converged state to next-scale token predictions. Training uses Stochastic Jacobian-Free Backpropagation (S-JFB): N~U{0,…,10} no-gradient warm-start iterations followed by M~U{1,…,12} gradient-tracked iterations, with backpropagation only through the last M steps, yielding near-constant training memory independent of implicit depth. At inference, the number of fixed-point iterations per scale becomes a flexible compute knob—reducing iterations at high-resolution scales where refinement saturates (cosine similarity >0.98 by 5 iterations) cuts peak GPU memory from 19.24 GB to 8.53 GB and doubles throughput from 15.16 to 32.08 images/s on a single RTX 4090, while KV cache is indexed per iteration index to maintain consistent attention contexts across refinement steps.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, VIAR (CFG=1.5) achieves FID 2.16, sFID 8.07, and IS 300.1 with only 770.9M parameters (38.4% of VAR's 2010.0M) and 11.16 GB peak inference memory (42% less than VAR's 19.24 GB). At CFG=2.0, VIAR attains IS 330.7 and sFID 7.92, surpassing VAR on both spatial fidelity and inception score. With the most aggressive per-scale iteration schedule (s4), VIAR doubles throughput from 15.16 to 32.08 images/s on a single RTX 4090 while reducing peak memory to 8.53 GB, all without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline VAR-D30 (Tian et al., 2024), VIAR trades a small FID gap (2.16 vs. 2.08 at CFG=1.5) for a 61.6% parameter reduction, 42% lower inference memory, and a 2.1× throughput gain; on sFID and IS at CFG=2.0, VIAR actually outperforms VAR (7.92 vs. 8.86 sFID; 330.7 vs. 328.5 IS). Compared to the diffusion-based L-DiT-7B (Alpha-VLLM, 2024), VIAR (CFG=1.5) achieves a lower FID (2.16 vs. 2.28) with only 11% of the parameters (770.9M vs. 7.0B) and 10 sampling steps versus 250, though L-DiT-7B retains a higher IS (316.2 vs. 300.1). Against earlier autoregressive baselines—VQGAN (FID 15.78), ViTVQ (FID 4.17), and RQ-Transformer (FID 7.55)—VIAR substantially outperforms all on FID while using fewer or comparable parameters. The paper also notes that VIAR is orthogonal to inference-time acceleration methods like FastVAR and ScaleKV, which target token pruning and KV-cache compression respectively, and could be combined for further gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIAR sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: Visual Autoregressive Modeling (VAR), which reframes image generation as next-scale prediction with within-scale parallelism, and Deep Equilibrium Models (DEQs), which replace deep explicit stacks with a single implicit fixed-point layer trained via Jacobian-free backpropagation. The paper is the first to integrate a DEQ layer into VAR's next-scale prediction paradigm, replacing the 30-layer explicit middle stack with one implicit operator while preserving VAR's coarse-to-fine factorization. It positions itself as orthogonal to recent efficient-VAR methods (FastVAR, ScaleKV, collaborative/speculative decoding), which accelerate inference on top of an existing explicit backbone, whereas VIAR redesigns the backbone itself to decouple compute from depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployable visual generation: a 61.6% parameter reduction, constant training memory independent of depth, and a per-scale iteration knob that trades quality for latency without retraining (2.1× throughput, 2.26× memory reduction on a single RTX 4090). Open questions include whether the convergence properties and quality-efficiency trade-offs hold at higher resolutions (512×512, 1024×1024) or with larger tokenizers, whether the implicit layer composes well with token-level pruning or KV-cache compression methods, and whether the fixed-point iteration count can be made fully adaptive (e.g., learned per-sample) rather than schedule-driven. The paper is limited to ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, so generalization to text-to-image or video remains untested.
