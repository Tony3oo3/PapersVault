## Problem

Prior work on LLM formulation sensitivity treats surface-form changes as inference-time artifacts that perturb outputs without fundamentally altering internal reasoning. This paper asks whether logically identical tasks expressed in different surface forms (abstract formal language vs. concretized natural language) actually induce distinct internal representations and decoupled learning dynamics, and whether such compartmentalization can be mitigated without collapsing representational geometry.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance is a SAT/CSP problem presented in two strictly isomorphic forms: a Z3 constraint program with explicit Boolean (or mixed Boolean-integer/Abelian-group) variables and clauses, and a natural language puzzle embedding the same constraints in narrative prose with a separate variable-definition block. The model must output a complete truth assignment for every variable. NL puzzles are generated via a dual-learning translation pipeline that retains only instances whose back-translated Z3 code is verified isomorphic to the original, and are further filtered by a difficulty criterion (pass rate below 8/16 rollouts on Qwen3-30B-A3B).

## Method

The paper introduces a controlled abstraction-concretization evaluation framework built on logically isomorphic FL/NL SAT/CSP pairs, then probes compartmentalization through four diagnostics: Grad×Input token attribution, layer-wise CKA representation similarity, incremental learning-transfer efficiency (ΔNL/ΔFL), and layer-wise activation patching to measure routing specificity. As an intervention, the authors apply abstraction-based alignment in two forms: a prompt-based step that instructs the model to first translate the NL puzzle into Z3 code before solving, and an RL-based step (GRPO with LoRA) that trains the model to produce an isomorphic Z3 reconstruction of the NL input, rewarding only logical isomorphism. The central finding is that both interventions substantially improve NL reasoning accuracy and cross-representation transfer, yet CKA and transfer-efficiency analyses show FL and NL representations remain persistently separated; activation patching reveals that gains arise from amplified routing specificity in mid-upper layers (≈38–44) along representation-consistent pathways, not from representational fusion.

## Results

On the paired abstraction-concretization dataset, converting logically identical SAT/CSP problems from formal language (Z3) to natural language puzzles causes severe accuracy drops: Qwen3-30B-A3B falls from 97.6% (FL) to 31.6% (NL) on 3×3 Boolean problems, and GPT-oss-20B drops from 85.8% to 74.2%. After RL-based abstraction alignment (training the model to translate NL→FL with an isomorphism reward), Qwen3-30B-A3B recovers to 87.8% on 3×3 NL (+56.2 points) and GPT-oss-20B reaches 86.4% (+12.2), approaching or even surpassing their original FL accuracy. Prompt-based abstraction (translating NL→FL in-context before solving) yields smaller but consistent gains, e.g., +34.0 for Qwen3 on 3×3. Out-of-domain, RL-aligned GPT-oss-20B improves by +8.2 on PlanBench and +9.0 on perturbed CatAttack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external reasoning methods; instead it benchmarks three intervention strategies on the same models: (1) no intervention (Orig.), (2) prompt-based abstraction where the model is instructed to translate NL→Z3 before solving, and (3) RL-based abstraction where the model is trained via GRPO with a logical-isomorphism reward on NL→FL translation. RL-based alignment consistently outperforms prompt-based across all tested models and settings: on Qwen3-30B-A3B, RL achieves 87.8% vs. 65.6% on 3×3 NL, and on GPT-oss-20B it reaches 86.4% vs. 81.0%. A generic Abstraction-of-Thought (AoT) baseline that encourages intermediate abstraction without requiring explicit FL reconstruction yields only marginal gains (Qwen3 Bool average: 28.2→30.4), far below prompt-based (51.6) or RL-based (75.2), confirming that verifiable structural reconstruction is essential. Notably, Gemini-2.5-Pro is the one model where prompt-based abstraction *degrades* 5×5 performance (80.2→68.6, −11.6), attributed to the model skipping the symbolic reasoning step after translation. On out-of-domain benchmarks (PlanBench, CatAttack, R2ATA), RL-aligned models show consistent robustness gains over their unaligned counterparts, with GPT-oss-20B gaining +8.2 on PlanBench and +9.0 on perturbed CatAttack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant framing of formulation sensitivity as an inference-time artifact (Zhou et al., 2024; Gan et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2025) by demonstrating that logically equivalent inputs in different surface forms induce genuinely separated internal representations and decoupled learning dynamics. It repurposes the dual-learning translation framework from NMT (Xia et al., 2016) to construct verified isomorphic FL/NL pairs, addressing a gap in prior logic-puzzle benchmarks (Kazemi et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2025) that lack rigorous logical-equivalence guarantees. The abstraction-alignment intervention is distinguished from generic intermediate-abstraction methods (AoT, CoA, AbstRaL) by requiring explicit structural reconstruction verified via logical isomorphism, rather than relying on answer-correctness rewards or underspecified placeholders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that abstraction training improves cross-formulation reasoning through routing amplification rather than representational fusion challenges the intuitive assumption that robustness to surface variation requires aligning internal representations, with implications for how we design interventions for LLM robustness more broadly. Key limitations include the restriction to controlled SAT/CSP tasks (where isomorphism is exactly verifiable), detailed mechanistic analysis on only two model architectures, and the open question of whether the compartmentalization phenomenon and its routing-based mitigation extend to open-ended mathematical or commonsense reasoning. The framework also raises the question of whether representational separation is an inherent property of language-conditioned computation or an artifact of pretraining distributions.
