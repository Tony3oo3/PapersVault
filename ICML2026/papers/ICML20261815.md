## Problem

Direct Alignment Algorithms (DAAs) such as DPO and SimPO over-optimize their implicit reward model, causing likelihood displacement where both chosen and rejected response probabilities drop simultaneously. The paper identifies the root cause as the absence of the partition function Z(x) in the DAA objectives (it cancels out in reward differences), leaving the model without incentive to maintain calibrated likelihoods. No prior explanation or fix generalizes across both reference-based and reference-free DAA variants.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard preference triplets (prompt x, chosen response y_w, rejected response y_l) from a dataset like UltraFeedback, along with a frozen reference model π_ref. Notably, even N-SimPO—which extends the otherwise reference-free SimPO—requires the reference model solely to compute the regularization term's baseline probability mass. The output is a fine-tuned language model trained for one epoch with a cosine LR schedule, evaluated on instruction-following (AlpacaEval), common-sense/reasoning benchmarks, and implicit reward accuracy.

## Method

The paper introduces a length-normalized probability-conservation regularizer R = λ(log((π̄_θ(y_w|x)+π̄_θ(y_l|x))/(π̄_ref(y_w|x)+π̄_ref(y_l|x)))²) added to the DPO or SimPO loss, yielding N-DPO and N-SimPO. The key insight is that since any softmax-parameterized LM has partition function 1, the total response probability over the observed pair should be preserved during training; the regularizer enforces this without requiring estimation of Z(x) over all possible responses. A gradient analysis reveals that the regularizer's per-token contributions are inversely proportional to token likelihood, so corrective updates concentrate on low-likelihood outlier tokens that drive most of the displacement. Empirically, this yields >20% relative AlpacaEval2 gains and >9% benchmark improvements on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct while reducing the minimum token-reward distribution's negative tail, confirming that displacement is highly concentrated on a small set of outlier tokens.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, N-DPO achieves 27.57% raw win-rate and 24.97% length-controlled win-rate on AlpacaEval2, compared to DPO's 24.18% and 21.72%, representing a >20% relative improvement in LC score. On general benchmarks (MMLU, ARC, HellaSwag, PIQA, SciQ, WinoGrande), N-DPO improves the average score by +3.04 points over the reference model while DPO degrades by −3.25 points, a >9% relative gain. For OLMo-7B-SFT, N-SimPO achieves 10.02% AlpacaEval2 WR versus SimPO's 3.66%, a >75% relative increase. N-DPO and N-SimPO also outperform DPO+SFT and DPOP on AlpacaEval2 across all three models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO, N-DPO consistently improves AlpacaEval2 scores: +1.95 WR and +1.82 LC on Mistral-7B-Instruct, +3.39 WR and +3.25 LC on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and +3.67 WR and +0.92 LC on OLMo-7B-SFT. On benchmarks, N-DPO reverses DPO's degradation on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (avg +3.04 vs −3.25) and OLMo-7B-SFT (+0.72 vs −1.58), while slightly narrowing Mistral's drop (−0.98 vs −2.80). Against SimPO, N-SimPO substantially improves OLMo-7B-SFT (10.02 vs 3.66 WR, +6.36 LC) and Mistral (20.38 vs 19.68 WR), though it slightly underperforms SimPO on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct AlpacaEval2 (27.11 vs 28.68 WR) while gaining on benchmarks (+3.69 vs +1.14 avg). Against DPOP, N-DPO outperforms on AlpacaEval2 LC for all three models (25.48 vs 18.22, 24.97 vs 15.92, 9.88 vs 4.42) and on benchmarks for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (+3.04 vs +1.38). Against DPO+SFT, N-DPO dramatically outperforms on AlpacaEval2 (e.g., 27.57 vs 4.07 WR on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) while maintaining comparable or better benchmark scores, demonstrating that conserving total probability mass is more effective than unconditionally boosting chosen-response likelihood.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO and SimPO by adding a length-normalized probability conservation regularizer, motivated by the observation that both objectives cancel the partition function Z(x) and thus lack any incentive to maintain calibrated likelihoods. It departs from prior over-optimization analyses (Razin et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2024; Pal et al., 2024) that attribute likelihood displacement to embedding geometry, reward shape, or edit distance by identifying the root cause as the absence of normalization, a framing that unifies both reference-based and reference-free methods. The gradient analysis builds on Yoon et al. (2025)'s finding that low-likelihood tokens dominate DPO gradients, extending it to show how the proposed regularizer specifically targets those tokens to reshape the reward distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a single scalar hyperparameter (λ) added to either DPO or SimPO simultaneously improves generation quality and preserves benchmark capability, outperforming alternatives like DPO+SFT and DPOP that unconditionally boost chosen-response likelihoods. The mechanistic finding that likelihood displacement is concentrated on a small set of outlier tokens (minimum token rewards ~-10 vs. typical range ±2.5) explains why DAAs improve generation despite decreasing chosen-response likelihoods, and suggests that future alignment methods should account for token-level heterogeneity. Limitations include applicability only to offline settings (not online RLHF or KTO), the need to tune λ, and the fact that the regularization constrains how far the model can separate chosen from rejected responses (reward margins decrease monotonically with λ).
