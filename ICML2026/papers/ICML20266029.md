## Problem

Existing federated LoRA methods enforce a uniform parameter-sharing rule across the entire network, ignoring the structural asymmetry in medical segmentation where the encoder is dominated by covariate (appearance) shift and the decoder by concept (supervision) shift. This mismatch entangles shared anatomical knowledge with site-specific biases, and LoRA's bilinear parameterization further allows local updates to leak into shared directions through multiplicative coupling, degrading generalization under non-IID conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB medical images (histology nuclei or fundus photography) resized to 1024×1024 and normalized per SAM conventions, processed through a frozen SAM ViT-B backbone with LoRA adapters injected into query and value projection layers of both the image encoder and mask decoder. It produces pixel-level binary segmentation masks (foreground/background for nuclei; optic disc and optic cup for fundus), evaluated via Dice Similarity Coefficient. The federated setting treats each source dataset as a separate client, with the server aggregating only the shared LoRA factors per the IAT protocol while personalized factors remain on-device.

## Method

The framework comprises two complementary mechanisms. First, Inverse Asymmetric Tuning (IAT) allocates LoRA factors inversely across modules: in the encoder (covariate-shift dominated), the input projector A is kept local while the output projector B is aggregated; in the decoder (concept-shift dominated), A is aggregated while B is kept local. This allocation is derived from a theoretical proposition showing that minimizing rank-r approximation error under input rotation favors sharing the column projector, while under output rotation it favors sharing the row projector. Second, a Subspace Orthogonality Regularizer (SOR) penalizes the squared normalized Frobenius inner product between shared and local update directions in a compact r×r proxy space, using stop-gradient and EMA drift estimates to produce gradients only on shared factors and prevent bilinear leakage without extra communication. The full method achieves an O(1/√T) convergence rate under standard smoothness and bounded-gradient assumptions, and requires only 0.39M trainable parameters with 0.55M per-round communication.

## Results

The proposed FedIAT framework achieves the highest average Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) across both federated segmentation benchmarks: 81.40% on histology nuclei segmentation (7 clients) and 84.52% on fundus photography segmentation (4 clients) at LoRA rank 8. At rank 16, performance further improves to 81.49% and 85.70% respectively. The method also demonstrates superior robustness on the most domain-shifted client (Drishti-GS1), reaching 85.43% DSC versus 57.74% for FedIT — a margin of over 27 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedIAT outperforms all 8 baselines on both tasks at both rank settings. The closest competitor is FedSA (selective aggregation), which achieves 80.09% on histology and 83.04% on fundus at rank 8 — trailing FedIAT by 1.31 and 1.48 DSC points respectively; at rank 16, FedSA narrows the fundus gap to 1.02 points (84.68 vs 85.70). FedDPA (dual-personalizing adapter) is the second-closest on histology (79.35% at R=8) but falls further behind on fundus (80.95%). FFA-LoRA suffers catastrophic degradation on several clients (e.g., 3.59% DSC on TNBC, 0.74% on MoNuSeg), yielding the worst average of 54.29% on histology. FLoRA, LoRA-FAIR, FlexLoRA, and FRLoRA all trail by 3–10 DSC points, with FLoRA showing the largest gap on histology (71.08% vs 81.40%). Notably, FedIT, despite being a strong uniform-aggregation baseline, fails to generalize to the most distinct domain (Drishti-GS1: 57.74% vs 85.43% for FedIAT), highlighting the benefit of the structure-aware inverse allocation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Federated LoRA paradigm (building on LoRA's bilinear parameterization and FedSA's selective factor aggregation) by identifying a structural mismatch specific to encoder-decoder medical segmentation architectures. While prior FedLoRA methods (FedSA, FFA-LoRA, LoRA-FAIR, FlexLoRA, etc.) enforce a uniform splitting rule across all layers—largely inherited from LLM-centric designs—this work argues that the encoder (dominated by covariate/appearance shift) and decoder (dominated by concept/supervision shift) require opposite allocation strategies. The proposed Inverse Asymmetric Tuning (IAT) inverts the shared/local factor assignment between these modules, and the Subspace Orthogonality Regularizer (SOR) addresses a second, orthogonal problem: implicit gradient leakage through LoRA's multiplicative coupling that structural separation alone cannot prevent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift-dependent allocation principle provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to ad-hoc uniform splitting rules in federated PEFT, potentially guiding future designs for any architecture with asymmetric heterogeneity sources. However, the empirical validation is limited to two 2D segmentation tasks (histology nuclei, fundus) with SAM ViT-B, and the theoretical assumptions (orthogonal rotations, clean encoder-decoder shift separation) may not hold in more complex 3D or multi-task settings. Open questions include whether the inverse allocation generalizes to architectures without a clear encoder-decoder boundary, how it interacts with more extreme client heterogeneity (e.g., different label spaces), and whether the modest SOR contribution (~1% over IAT alone) justifies its added complexity at scale.
