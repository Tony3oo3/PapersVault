## Problem

Existing methods for mitigating model performance degradation (imbalanced samples, noisy labels, backdoor attacks) are either scenario-specific, architecture-constrained, or require costly retraining of the corrupted model. No unified paradigm exists that can directly repair degraded parameters of unseen models without access to clean data or task-specific supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261671/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the full parameter tensor of a contaminated model (any CNN or Transformer architecture) and, at inference, requires no clean data—only the contaminated weights and the positional coordinates of each parameter cluster. Parameters are first partitioned into per-neuron functional units (aggregation weights + processing parameters), each of dimensionality <5000, then encoded into 128-dimensional latent codes. The output is a set of purified parameter-cluster manifolds that, when decoded back to high-dimensional space, replace the contaminated weights and restore model accuracy.

## Method

PMP decomposes purification into three stages: (1) parameter-cluster partitioning, which groups parameters by the smallest functional unit (one aggregation operation plus subsequent processing operations per neuron), yielding nested local manifolds within a global parameter manifold; (2) an Implicit Manifold Auto-Encoder (IMAE) that compresses each high-dimensional cluster into a 128-dim latent code via an MLP encoder and reconstructs it using a decoder conditioned on sinusoidal positional encodings of the cluster's (layer, index) coordinates, trained with a Parameter Cluster Discrepancy (PCD) loss that applies exponential intra-cluster and inter-cluster weighting to counteract skewed parameter magnitude distributions; (3) a conditional denoising diffusion model that, given the contaminated latent code and positional embedding, iteratively removes noise from a Gaussian prior to recover the clean low-dimensional manifold, which is then decoded back to the original parameter space. The entire pipeline is trained offline on pairs of contaminated and clean parameters from standard training procedures, and at test time operates data-free on unseen models.

## Results

On ImageNet-LT, PMP achieves 71.7% overall accuracy with ResNet-50 (surpassing the best baseline NCL at 57.4% by 14.3 points) and 82.6% with ViT-B/16 (surpassing LIFTw/TTE at 78.3% by 4.3 points). On CIFAR-10 with 80% symmetric label noise, PMP reaches 94.20% accuracy versus the best baseline SOP at 68.32%. On Tiny-ImageNet under BadNets-A2O backdoor attack, PMP restores clean accuracy to 56.15% while reducing the attack success rate from 99.96% to 0.21%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMP outperforms all 23 baselines across all three scenarios. On ImageNet-LT, PMP surpasses the strongest long-tailed baselines (NCL at 57.4% for ResNet-50; LIFTw/TTE at 78.3% for ViT-B/16) by 14.3 and 4.3 points respectively, with particularly large gains on tail classes (66.4% vs. 26.1% for cRT on ResNet-50). On CIFAR-10/100 with 80% symmetric noise, PMP achieves 94.20%/69.14% accuracy, exceeding the best noisy-label baselines (SOP at 68.32%/30.20%) by 25.9 and 38.9 points. On Tiny-ImageNet backdoor defense, PMP consistently achieves the highest clean accuracy (54.4–56.2%) and lowest attack success rate (0.11–0.63%) across all 8 attacks, whereas baselines like NPD and EP achieve low ASR but sacrifice 5–10 points of clean accuracy, and methods like NC and i-BAU fail to reduce ASR below 50% on several attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on two distinct lineages: the denoising diffusion probabilistic model framework (Ho et al., 2020) for the reverse denoising process, and implicit neural representations / NeRF-style sinusoidal positional encoding (Mildenhall et al., 2021) for modeling parameter clusters as low-dimensional manifolds embedded in a global space. It departs from both HyperNetworks and Parameter Diffusion, which generate parameters from scratch or for downstream adaptation, by reframing the problem as recovering clean parameters from contaminated ones—a denoising task rather than a generation task. The parameter-cluster partitioning strategy, inspired by interpretability findings about functional modules in CNNs and Transformers, is a novel structural decomposition not present in prior weight-space learning methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of long-tailed imbalance, noisy labels, and backdoor attacks under a single 'parameter contamination' paradigm is conceptually appealing and could simplify the tooling landscape for model repair. However, the method requires offline training on pairs of clean and contaminated parameters from standard training procedures, which is a non-trivial assumption that limits immediate deployment. Open questions include whether PMP generalizes across scenarios simultaneously (e.g., a model suffering from both noisy labels and a backdoor), whether it scales to very large models like LLMs, and whether the 'unseen model' guarantee holds when the test model was trained under a different contamination distribution than any seen during PMP training. The paper itself acknowledges potential extensions to quantization and adversarial robustness but does not evaluate them.
