## Problem

Standard ODE/SDE solvers for diffusion models accumulate discretization errors that make exact inversion impossible, which is critical for image editing, likelihood computation, and Boltzmann sampling. Existing reversible solvers (EDICT, BDIA, BELM) suffer from poor numerical stability, low convergence order (zero or first order), and are restricted to the ODE formulation, leaving no stable, high-order reversible method for diffusion SDEs.

## Input / Output

The solver operates on a learned diffusion model (noise or data prediction) with a variance-preserving noise schedule, consuming either a Gaussian prior sample (generation), a real image to be inverted (editing), or a text prompt (conditional generation). For SDE variants, Brownian motion is regenerated on-the-fly from a single seed via a splittable PRNG rather than cached, enabling adaptive step sizes without storing the full trajectory. Outputs are generated or edited images in latent space (e.g., 512×512 via Stable Diffusion VAE) or molecular conformation samples from a Boltzmann distribution, with the solver maintaining one auxiliary state alongside the primary state at each step.

## Method

Rex is constructed via a three-step recipe: (1) select any explicit (stochastic) Runge-Kutta scheme Φ; (2) build the 'Princeps' scheme Ψ by exploiting the semi-linear structure of the diffusion ODE/SDE (linear drift + nonlinear score term) through exponential integrators and a time-change reparameterization, yielding an exponentially weighted (S)RK update; (3) apply the McCallum-Foster reversible construction to Princeps, coupling the primary and auxiliary states via a parameter ζ to obtain algebraic reversibility with a non-zero region of linear stability. For SDEs, the same Brownian motion realization is reused in forward and backward passes via a splittable PRNG, and the space-time Lévy area handles the stochastic integral. The resulting solver inherits arbitrary-order convergence from the base RK scheme, subsumes reversible versions of DDIM, DPM-Solver, and SEEDS-1, and is the first method to support exact inversion of diffusion SDEs without storing the full Brownian trajectory.

## Results

On tri-alanine Boltzmann sampling, Rex(Dopri5) achieves the best energy Wasserstein distance (E-W2 = 0.495) among all methods, improving over the same DiT model with standard Dopri5 (E-W2 = 0.737) and outperforming SBG(SMC) (0.598) and RegFlow (1.051). For round-trip image editing on pix2pix, Rex(Dopri5) reduces LPIPS from 0.214 (DDIM) and 0.140 (O-BELM) to 0.107, a 2× reduction over DDIM. In latent-space reconstruction (FP32, Stable Diffusion v1.5, 512×512), Rex(Euler) achieves orders-of-magnitude lower MSE than all reversible baselines at 10, 20, and 50 steps. On unconditional and text-conditioned generation, every Rex variant outperforms EDICT, BDIA, and O-BELM across FD, CLIP Score, PickScore, and Image Reward metrics, with Rex even surpassing the non-reversible DDIM baseline on several metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rex outperforms all four reversible diffusion baselines (EDICT, BDIA, O-BELM, and the non-reversible DDIM) on image generation and editing: on pix2pix editing, BDIA fails catastrophically (LPIPS 0.885, Image Reward −2.21) while EDICT collapses to the approximate identity map, and Rex(Dopri5) achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.107) and highest PickScore. On unconditional CelebA-HQ generation, Rex(Euler-Maruyama) attains the largest radar-chart polygon at 20 and 50 steps across FD, FD∞, Precision, Recall, Density, and Coverage, surpassing even DDIM. On text-conditioned COCO generation, every Rex variant leads on CLIP Score, Image Reward, and PickScore at all step counts. For Boltzmann sampling on tri-alanine, Rex(Dopri5) applied to a DiT backbone achieves the best E-W2 (0.495), beating SBG(SMC) (0.598), RegFlow (1.051), and ECNF++ (2.206), though at a modest ESS cost (0.104 vs. 0.140 for DiT+Dopri5). The key differentiator is stability: O-BELM's reconstruction error grows with step count due to its null stability region, while Rex inherits a non-zero linear stability region from the McCallum-Foster construction, enabling reliable adaptive step-size operation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Rex combines two recent theoretical tools: the McCallum-Foster method (2024), which provides an algebraically reversible single-step construction with a non-zero linear stability region, and the space-time Lévy area framework of Foster et al. (2024) for high-order stochastic Runge-Kutta schemes. The key conceptual step is applying Lawson (exponential integrator) transformations to the McCallum-Foster construction so that the resulting reversible scheme operates directly on the original state variable of the diffusion ODE/SDE, rather than on a reparameterized one. This unifies the construction: the intermediate 'Princeps' scheme is shown to subsume DDIM, DPM-Solver, SEEDS-1, and gDDIM, making Rex the reversible counterpart of each. The paper also explicitly draws the first connection between the reversible-ODE-solver literature (asynchronous leapfrog, reversible Heun, McCallum-Foster) and the exact-inversion-of-diffusion-models literature (EDICT, BDIA, BELM), showing that the latter are special cases with poor stability and low convergence order.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Rex is the first method to provide exact inversion of diffusion SDEs without caching the full Brownian trajectory, and the first adaptive step-size reversible solver applied to diffusion-based image editing—both practically important for memory-constrained and precision-critical applications (Boltzmann sampling, differentiable rewards, editing). The near-machine-precision round-trip reconstruction (orders of magnitude below EDICT, BDIA, and O-BELM) removes a key obstacle for likelihood-based inference through diffusion models. Open questions include whether the stability region, while non-zero, is large enough for very stiff noise schedules at large step sizes, and whether the SDE convergence-order guarantees extend to non-additive-noise formulations beyond the semi-linear class covered here.
