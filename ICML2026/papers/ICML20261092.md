## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of deep Transformers with layer normalization predict that tokens collapse to a single cluster, but these results assume deterministic (weight-tied) dynamics that do not reflect the standard layer-wise random initialization used in practice. The intrinsic stochasticity arising from i.i.d. random value matrices across layers has not been rigorously characterized, leaving open whether and how this noise alters the predicted clustering behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261092/cropped_page7_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes N token vectors initialized on the unit sphere S^{d-1}, a sequence of i.i.d. value matrices with centered entries and fixed variance σ², a fixed attention temperature β, and token dimension d. The output is a system of SDEs on (S^{d-1})^N driven by a single common matrix-valued Brownian motion, from which the paper extracts explicit phase-transition boundaries β_c(d) = ½cosh⁻¹(d−2) separating single-cluster from antipodal regimes, along with a sharp noise-amplitude threshold ε_c(β) = √(2e^{−β}) for the hybrid deterministic-plus-noise model. Notably, the limiting SDE is invariant to the specific initialization distribution of the value matrices, depending only on the first two moments.

## Method

The paper analyzes a minimal self-attention Transformer with diffusion-scaled residual connections (α_L = 1/√L) and token-wise RMS normalization, where randomness enters exclusively through i.i.d. value matrices. Under this setup, the authors prove (via a martingale-problem argument à la Watanabe) that the discrete token dynamics converge in distribution to a system of SDEs on the sphere, where all tokens are driven by a common matrix-valued Brownian motion and an additional radial drift term (proportional to (1−d)σ²/2) is required to preserve the spherical constraint under Itô's formula. For N=2 tokens with Q⊤K = Id(d), the pairwise overlap Z_t = ⟨Y_t¹, Y_t²⟩ reduces to a closed one-dimensional diffusion, whose boundary behavior is classified via Feller's scale-speed framework, yielding the explicit phase transition at d−2 = cosh(2β). A complementary hybrid model combining a deterministic drift (identity value matrix) with a scalar stochastic perturbation of amplitude ε/√L produces a sharp threshold ε² = 2e^{−β} below which single-cluster convergence holds almost surely and above which antipodal convergence holds almost surely. Numerical experiments on the original discrete dynamics (not SDE discretizations) confirm both phase boundaries and demonstrate that random value-matrix initialization improves CIFAR-10 accuracy over constant identity initialization in a 128-layer ViT.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with the headline quantitative result being a sharp phase transition boundary for two-token clustering: antipodal configurations become reachable with positive probability if and only if d − 2 < cosh(2β), i.e., β > ½cosh⁻¹(d−2). A second explicit threshold is derived for the hybrid noise model: tokens converge to a single cluster when ε² < 2e^{−β} and to the antipodal configuration when ε² > 2e^{−β}. On CIFAR-10, a 128-layer single-head ViT (d=96, RMSNorm, no MLP) trained for 300 epochs shows that independent Gaussian initialization of value matrices yields higher validation accuracy than constant identity initialization across all 5 seeds, confirming the practical relevance of intrinsic initialization noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external methods but makes two key internal comparisons. First, on CIFAR-10, random per-layer Gaussian V initialization (σ=1) consistently outperforms constant identity V shared across all 128 layers, demonstrating that the stochasticity from standard initialization is not merely a theoretical artifact but materially improves training dynamics in deep attention stacks. Second, in the large-β regime (β=4.5, N=50, d=4), the stochastic dynamics converges to clustered configurations significantly faster than the deterministic counterpart, which exhibits long-lived metastable transient configurations; the stochastic dynamics shows no such metastability. Numerical simulations of the discrete Transformer dynamics (40,000 trajectories, L=100, T=500) confirm that the empirically observed phase transition in antipodal convergence probability aligns sharply with the theoretical boundary β_c(d)=½cosh⁻¹(d−2) across dimensions d∈{4,…,10}, and the noise-induced threshold ε=√(2e^{−β}) is similarly validated in the (β,ε) phase diagram.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate theoretical threads: (i) mean-field/infinite-depth Transformer analysis that predicts deterministic clustering to a single point (Geshkovski et al. 2023, 2025; Criscitiello et al. 2024), (ii) stochastic attention models that inject external, token-wise independent noise (Shalova & Schlichting 2024; Balasubramanian et al. 2025), and (iii) diffusion limits for deep ResNets that capture intrinsic stochasticity from random initialization (Peluchetti & Favaro 2020; Cont et al. 2023). The key departure is deriving the stochastic limit directly from the standard random initialization of Value matrices under diffusion scaling and RMS normalization, yielding a common-noise SDE on the sphere rather than idiosyncratic token-level noise. This provides an architecturally grounded derivation that prior stochastic Transformer models lack, and it partially resolves Problem 2.15 from Geshkovski et al. (2025) on whether deterministic clustering conclusions extend to random-matrix Transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that intrinsic initialization noise qualitatively alters the clustering landscape by making antipodal configurations reachable with positive probability, governed by an explicit threshold d−2 < cosh(2β)—challenges the deterministic prediction of universal single-point collapse and offers a new lens on rank collapse phenomena. However, the analysis is restricted to N=2 tokens for explicit phase boundaries, assumes fixed K and Q matrices, and omits feedforward blocks; extending to N≥3, random K/Q, and training dynamics remain open. The empirical confirmation on CIFAR-10 (random V initialization outperforming constant initialization) is encouraging but uses a simplified single-head ViT without MLP layers, limiting direct transfer to production architectures. The result that noise diversifies terminal states without preventing rank collapse suggests that practical mitigations for rank collapse must address the deterministic drift component rather than relying on initialization stochasticity alone.
