## Problem

Transformer-based Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) achieve strong accuracy on vision tasks but rely on dense matrix multiplication, multi-head attention, scaling, and explicit patch embedding—operations whose communication and memory-access patterns fundamentally conflict with the sparse, event-driven nature of neuromorphic hardware. Prior work has addressed accuracy gains from transplanting ANN attention into SNNs but has left the critical deployment-efficiency gap unresolved, with communication overhead (not arithmetic) dominating real energy cost on neuromorphic chips.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) or asynchronous event streams (DVS-Gesture), which are encoded as binary spike trains over T discrete time steps and processed through LIF neurons into spike tensors of shape C×H×W. The network outputs a class label via time-averaged logits (mean over T steps) trained with standard cross-entropy loss and surrogate gradients through the spiking nonlinearity.

## Method

The paper proposes two Hadamard-product-based attention mechanisms—Simplified Spiking Attention (SSA) and Ultra-Simplified Spiking Attention (USSA)—that replace the dense QK^T V matrix multiplication in the SpikingResformer backbone with element-wise spike interactions. SSA computes Y = K ⊙ V where K and V are independent convolutional projections passed through LIF neurons, eliminating multi-head attention, scaling, transposition, and patch embedding entirely. USSA further compresses the K branch into a broadcastable channel-wise or spatial-wise gating vector g via mean pooling, yielding Y = V ⊙ g with O(N) or O(D) communication. Two key theoretical insights drive the design: (1) a redundancy analysis shows that a second Hadamard mask on V after K⊙V gating strictly reduces entropy without adding discriminative power (validated empirically with spike rates < 0.2), justifying a single-mask design; and (2) an early-spiking gating formulation Y = LIF₁(K(t)) ⊙ LIF₂(V(t)) preserves richer temporal membrane-potential states in LIF₂ compared to late-spiking gating, because the mask is applied post-integration rather than pre-integration, avoiding suppression of sub-threshold activity. This reduces computational complexity from O(N²D) to O(ND) and communication from O(N²) to O(ND) (SSA) or O(N)/O(D) (USSA), with Lava/Loihi simulation confirming 82–89% energy reduction on the attention module.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and DVS-Gesture, SSA achieves 96.38%, 79.45%, and 97.56% top-1 accuracy (T=4 for CIFAR, T=16 for DVS-Gesture), outperforming the SpikingResformer MDSSA baseline by +0.49%, +1.30%, and +1.73% respectively. USSA, the compressed variant, achieves 95.80%, 78.22%, and 95.48% with reduced communication complexity. On ImageNet-1K, SSA reaches 76.91% and USSA reaches 77.27% top-1 accuracy, surpassing prior SNN-Transformer architectures. On the Loihi-compatible Lava simulation, SSA and USSA reduce per-block energy by 82.4% and 88.5% respectively compared to matrix-multiplication attention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSA outperforms the direct SpikingResformer baseline (MDSSA) on all three datasets: +0.49% on CIFAR-10 (96.38% vs. 95.89%), +1.30% on CIFAR-100 (79.45% vs. 78.15%), and +1.73% on DVS-Gesture (97.56% vs. 95.83%), all at identical timesteps. Against ResNet-based SNNs, SSA surpasses PSG (the strongest CNN-SNN) on CIFAR-100 by +2.87% and on CIFAR-10 by +1.03%, while nearly matching PSG on DVS-Gesture (97.56% vs. 97.57%) using only 16 timesteps versus PSG's 30. On ImageNet-1K, SSA (76.91%) outperforms Spikformer (74.81%), Spike-driven Transformer (74.66%), and Spikingformer (75.85%), while USSA (77.27%) slightly edges out Meta-SpikeFormer (77.20%) with fewer parameters (50.26M vs. 32.80M). The compressed USSA variant trades roughly 1.2% accuracy on CIFAR-100 and 2.1% on DVS-Gesture relative to SSA for reduced communication complexity from O(ND) to O(D), making it the preferred choice for energy-constrained deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SpikingResformer backbone (Shi et al., 2024) but departs sharply from the ANN-transplant paradigm that dominates prior Transformer-SNN work (Spikeformer, Spiking Transformer, Spike-driven Transformer, QKFormer). While those methods retain multi-head attention, tensor reshaping, scaling, and explicit patch embedding, this paper replaces the entire dense attention mechanism with Hadamard-product-based element-wise gating, drawing on the Hadamard-product idea from QKFormer and Attention SNNs but pushing it to its logical extreme by eliminating all inter-token communication. The theoretical analyses of masking redundancy and spike-timing effects are novel contributions not present in prior SNN-Transformer literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical and underexplored gap: the mismatch between Transformer-SNN algorithms and the sparse, event-driven, communication-constrained nature of neuromorphic hardware. The 82–88% energy reduction on Lava simulation and the O(N²)→O(N) communication complexity reduction are practically significant if they transfer to real chips, but the paper explicitly defers real-hardware validation to future work, and the analytical energy model ignores memory-access and spike-routing costs that dominate on actual neuromorphic systems. Open questions include whether the single-mask and early-spiking simplifications generalize to deeper or more complex architectures, and whether the modest accuracy gains over the MDSSA baseline persist under tighter timestep budgets or noisier event streams.
