## Problem

LLM agents executing procedural workflows repeatedly re-derive plans from scratch at every step, incurring substantial token cost and exposure to stochastic errors, even when the underlying task has stable, reusable dependency structure. Existing approaches to recovering this structure from execution traces either require #P-complete marginalization over all linear extensions of a partial order or produce descriptive process models that overfit incidental serialization and cannot be compiled into an executable control policy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262416/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of successful agent execution traces—each a linear sequence of atomic tool-invocation actions (e.g., CreateVPC, RunInstances) projected from raw LLM sessions by filtering out thinking tokens—generated by heterogeneous LLM backbones to maximize trace diversity. It produces a posterior distribution over partial-order dependency graphs, summarized as marginal edge probabilities, which is then compiled into a deterministic Graph Execution Engine (GEE) with a shared IO blackboard for parameter propagation and a tri-modal execution policy (Expert/Hybrid/Explore) that trades off automation efficiency against fallback resilience.

## Method

BPOP represents a latent partial order via K-dimensional Gaussian embeddings where action precedence is induced by component-wise dominance across all K dimensions, making the poset the intersection of K total orders. The key tractability contribution is a frontier-softmax likelihood: at each step of a trace, the agent selects from the set of topologically feasible actions (the frontier) using a Boltzmann policy weighted by a successor-utility score (log of descendant count), mixed with a uniform 'trembling-hand' noise term to tolerate logging violations. This decomposes the trace probability into sequential local choices, evaluating in O(|succ_h| + T|A|) rather than requiring #P-complete counting of linear extensions. Posterior inference uses a Metropolis-within-Gibbs sampler with reversible-jump moves for the latent dimension K and a dimension-cycling proposal schedule. The inferred graph is thresholded at α≈1/3 (near Bayes-optimal under 0-1 Hamming loss) and compiled into a frontier-scheduled executor that eliminates per-step LLM reasoning, reducing token consumption from ~38k–64k per task (Explore) to ~583 tokens (Expert intent-parsing only).

## Results

On WFCommons (IP-Cov=0.95), BPOP achieves Edge-F1 of 0.91 on SRASEARCH (22 tasks) and 0.79 on EPIGENOMICS (41 tasks), substantially outperforming the strongest process-mining baseline (Heuristics Miner: 0.43 and 0.38 respectively). On Cloud-IaC-6 at full trace diversity (IP-Cov=1.0), BPOP reaches Edge-F1=0.95 with 0.85 feasibility, while baselines either overgeneralize (Inductive Miner: perfect feasibility but Edge-F1≤0.39) or produce infeasible graphs (Majority: 0.22, Heuristics Miner: 0.19 feasibility). In execution, once IP-Cov≥0.9, the compiled frontier executor reduces LLM calls to 1.0/task and tokens to ~583/task (one-shot intent parsing only), compared to 4.0–5.9 calls and 17k–32k tokens at lower diversity; Hybrid mode achieves 100% success across all six scenarios with only 2 fallback events.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BPOP consistently outperforms all three baselines on structural recovery (Edge-F1) while maintaining high execution feasibility. Against Heuristics Miner (Weijters et al., 2006), BPOP achieves 0.91 vs. 0.43 Edge-F1 on SRASEARCH and 0.79 vs. 0.38 on EPIGENOMICS; Heuristics Miner also produces infeasible graphs (feasibility 0.14–0.19) due to over-constraining. Against Inductive Miner (Leemans et al., 2013), BPOP trades perfect feasibility (1.00) for far superior structural fidelity (Edge-F1 0.79–0.95 vs. ≤0.39), as IMf collapses concurrent actions into overly permissive 'flower models.' Against the Bayesian Queue-Jump baseline (Nicholls et al., 2025), BPOP matches or exceeds QJ on Edge-F1 at high IP-Cov while being computationally feasible: QJ requires #P-complete linear-extension counting at every MCMC step, with predicted runtimes exceeding 1,000 hours on SRASEARCH versus BPOP's ~4.5 hours. BPOP's errors are safety-biased (more false positives than false negatives), meaning missed parallelism rather than execution crashes, which is the critical distinction for production deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

BPOP directly extends the Bayesian partial-order inference framework of Nicholls et al. (2025), which itself builds on Winkler's (1985) random-order model representing posets as intersections of K total orders. The key methodological departure is the frontier-softmax likelihood, which replaces the #P-complete linear-extension counting required by the Bayesian Queue-Jump baseline with a tractable sequential choice model over the poset frontier. Beyond inference, the paper extends the task from pure structure estimation to executable workflow compilation, introducing a frontier-based Graph Execution Engine and a tri-modal (Expert/Hybrid/Explore) deployment framework that have no direct analogue in prior partial-order or process-mining literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic efficiency collapse at high trace diversity (~55× token reduction, from ~32k to ~583 tokens/task, with 100% completeness and zero fallback) demonstrates that amortizing one-time Bayesian inference into a compiled dependency graph can eliminate nearly all per-step LLM reasoning in convergent procedural tasks. This is practically significant for enterprise SOP automation where repeated executions of stable workflows dominate cost. Key limitations include the restriction to bounded, finite-horizon workflows with moderate action spaces, the hours-long MCMC inference cost, and the requirement for diverse traces (IP-Cov ≥ 0.9) to achieve the efficiency gains. Open questions include extending to hierarchical or cyclic workflows, reducing inference time via variational methods, and handling distribution shift when tool semantics change.
