## Problem

Existing diffusion-based dataset distillation methods update each synthetic image independently via per-sample guidance, making the within-class dynamics separable. This separability prevents optimization of non-additive set-level objectives such as diversity, coverage, and distributional fidelity, which depend on the joint configuration of all IPC samples rather than individual image quality. The paper formalizes this limitation (Lemma 4.1: separable gradients imply additive potentials) and asks how to inject dense, symmetric set-level coordination into frozen diffusion samplers without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen diffusion backbone (DiT-XL/2-256 in VAE latent space), per-class real-data descriptors (PCA-projected VAE latents with D=64 dimensions, used only to compute fixed reference ECF statistics and anchor frequencies offline), class labels, and random noise as input. It produces a compact labeled set of 256×256 synthetic images at a specified IPC budget (5–200 per class), which are then used to train classifiers from scratch under a hard-label protocol. The controller operates entirely on lightweight descriptors extracted from predicted clean latents at each step, requiring no backpropagation through the diffusion model and adding less than 2% wall-clock overhead.

## Method

SCG augments any base diffusion-based DD pipeline with a plug-and-play set-symmetric controller u_n^(k)(Z_n^c, t_n) that depends on the entire within-class latent state, shifting from per-image to IPC-at-once sampling. The controller decomposes into two terms: (1) spectral set-point regulation, which matches the empirical characteristic function (ECF) of synthetic PCA descriptors to a fixed real-data reference via closed-form gradients over K_f=32 anchor frequencies sampled from N(0,Σ^{-1}), and (2) cooperative kernel coupling, which pulls each sample toward its RBF-kernel-weighted neighborhood mean to stabilize joint trajectories under noisy feedback. All set-level computations occur in a low-dimensional descriptor space (D=64) extracted from predicted clean latents via a fixed per-class PCA basis, with results lifted back to latent shape via a linear projection P(q)=reshape(W_c q). The paper provides Lyapunov descent, input-to-state stability for ECF tracking, and an anti-collapse guarantee, and demonstrates consistent gains over DiT, MGD3, CaO2, and IGD baselines across ImageNette, ImageWoof, ImageNet-100, and ImageNet-1K with negligible overhead.

## Results

SCG is evaluated as a plug-and-play controller on four datasets (ImageNette, ImageWoof, ImageNet-100, ImageNet-1K) across three evaluator architectures (ConvNet-6, ResNetAP-10, ResNet-18) using hard-label top-1 accuracy. On ImageNette at IPC=10 with ResNet-18, SCG boosts vanilla DiT sampling from 58.2% to 65.8% (+7.6 pp) and MGD³ from 61.5% to 64.1% (+2.6 pp). On the full ImageNet-1K at IPC=10, SCG improves DiT from 14.7% to 17.2% and MGD³ from 21.2% to 22.9% (ResNet-18). Controller overhead is under 2% wall-clock time relative to the batched diffusion backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla DiT sampling, SCG delivers the largest absolute gains: +7.6 pp on ImageNette (ResNet-18, IPC=10) and +2.5 pp on ImageNet-1K (ResNet-18, IPC=10), confirming that unguided per-sample diffusion is the weakest baseline. Against MGD³, SCG consistently improves accuracy across all four datasets, with gains of +2.2 pp on ImageNet-100 (ResNetAP-10, IPC=10) and +1.7 pp on ImageNet-1K (ResNet-18, IPC=10); the improvement is most pronounced at small-to-medium IPC where per-sample redundancy is highest. Against CaO₂, SCG yields smaller but consistent gains (+1.3 to +2.0 pp on ImageNette/ImageWoof) and matches or slightly exceeds the base on ImageNet-100. Against IGD (evaluated in Appendix B.7), SCG substantially improves the DEV-disabled variant (IGD(-DEV)) on ImageWoof, e.g., +6.0 pp on ConvNet-6 at IPC=10, while the full IGD with its sequential DEV term already provides some diversity and shows smaller marginal gains from SCG. Overall, SCG wins or ties in the vast majority of settings across all four baselines, with the largest relative improvements on the hardest dataset (ImageWoof) and at low IPC budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of diffusion-based dataset distillation and control-theoretic set coordination. It builds on the growing family of frozen-backbone diffusion DD methods (MGD3, CaO2, IGD, OT-GDD, D4M) that inject per-sample guidance into the reverse diffusion process, and on classical characteristic-function matching from statistics (Feuerverger & Mureika, 1977) and kernel methods. The key conceptual departure is the unification of these methods into a discrete-time state-space form that exposes separability as a structural limitation, followed by the introduction of a dense, permutation-equivariant set-symmetric controller (ECF regulation + RBF kernel coupling) operating on lightweight PCA descriptors—repurposing ideas from input-to-state stability and Lyapunov analysis in a way not previously applied to generative sampling pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCG provides a principled, low-overhead mechanism to inject non-additive set-level objectives (distributional fidelity, diversity) into any compatible diffusion-based DD pipeline without retraining the backbone, which is practically valuable as diffusion priors become the default for large-scale distillation. The theoretical guarantees (Lyapunov descent, ISS tracking, anti-collapse) are relatively rare in the DD literature and give practitioners confidence in the controller's behavior. Open questions include whether the gains persist at very high IPC (the paper shows tapering at IPC=100–200), whether the ECF anchor strategy is optimal compared to learned discrepancies, and whether the approach extends naturally to other generative priors (e.g., flow matching, autoregressive models) or to multi-class joint objectives beyond per-class coupling.
