## Problem

Existing generative models for fluid dynamics fail to jointly capture shared dynamics across multiple physical conditions and cannot recover the full equilibrium-state distribution from the short, incomplete trajectories that are practically available. Additionally, their reliance on Cartesian-grid architectures limits adaptability to large irregular geometries with sharp spatial gradients, a common feature in real fluid systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260864/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unstructured mesh graph whose nodes carry conditional attributes (Reynolds number, node-type one-hot, surface normals) and whose edges encode relative positions and free-stream velocity projections, together with a Gaussian noise sample Y_0 ~ N(0,I). It outputs a full distribution over converged flow states (pressure and/or velocity fields on all mesh nodes) by integrating a learned ODE in a few Euler steps. Training uses only 10 consecutive states per trajectory (vs. 21–38 for a full vortex-shedding period), and the model must generalize to out-of-distribution Reynolds numbers and geometric parameters.

## Method

CGFM constructs a conditional optimal-transport probability path (with minimum noise scale σ_min) from a Gaussian prior to the equilibrium distribution and regresses a graph-conditioned vector field via supervised L2 loss. The vector field is parameterized by HieraGraphNet, a U-shaped hierarchical GNN that performs intra-level message passing within each mesh resolution and inter-level message passing across a coarse-to-fine graph hierarchy, enabling multi-scale dynamics capture and long-range interactions. The hierarchy is built by a topology- and geometry-aware coarsening (TGC) scheme that scores each node by s_i = d_i · ρ_i (indegree × mean neighbor distance) and greedily selects spatially central, low-connectivity nodes as coarse representatives. The paper further provides theoretical guarantees: cross-system joint training reduces estimation error as O(1/√M_eff), and the discrepancy at an unseen condition is bounded by ε + ε_model + L·ρ_N, where ρ_N is the coverage radius of the training conditions.

## Results

On the Ellipse dataset, CGFM achieves the lowest node-wise Wasserstein-2 distance (W2_node = 0.0239) and graph-wise Wasserstein-2 distance (W2_graph = 0.2734) on the in-distribution split, outperforming the next-best GFM (0.0322 / 0.5852) and DGN (0.0445 / 0.4523). On the 3D Wing benchmark (~7,000 nodes), CGFM attains R² = 0.9896, W2_node = 0.0099, and W2_graph = 1.9265, surpassing DGN (0.9795 / 0.0178 / 2.3831) and GFM (0.9652 / 0.0327 / 3.8579). CGFM also achieves the fastest inference at 0.0981 s/sample, a 28.8% speedup over DGN and 44.2% over GFM, and a 606× speedup over the OpenFOAM numerical solver on GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGFM consistently outperforms all nine baselines on both W2_node and W2_graph across every Ellipse test split (InDist, Thin, Thick, LowRe, HighRe). Among generative baselines, GFM (graph flow matching without hierarchy) is the closest competitor on W2_node (e.g., 0.0322 vs. 0.0239 on InDist), but CGFM's hierarchical message passing yields a substantially lower W2_graph (0.2734 vs. 0.5852), indicating better capture of spatial correlations. DGN, the strongest diffusion-based baseline, trails CGFM by roughly 46% on W2_node and 40% on W2_graph (InDist). Deterministic models (MuGS-GNN, Geo-FNO, Transolver) achieve competitive R² values but exhibit W2 distances 3–10× higher than CGFM, confirming they fit individual snapshots well yet fail to recover the underlying probability distribution. On the 3D Wing task, CGFM dominates all four probabilistic/generative baselines on all three metrics (R², W2_node, W2_graph), with the largest gap over GFM (W2_graph: 1.93 vs. 3.86). Bayesian GNN performs worst overall, with R² dropping to ~0.70 on Ellipse and W2_graph exceeding 1.7, while GM-GNN and GR-GNN show intermediate distributional accuracy but poor spatial coherence (W2_graph > 1.0 on most splits).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow matching generative framework (Lipman et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2023) to the domain of unstructured-mesh fluid simulation, building on the diffusion graph network (DGN, Valencia et al., 2025) and graph flow matching (GFM, Siddiqui et al., 2026) baselines. The key departure is the combination of condition-aware optimal-transport flow matching with a hierarchical U-shaped graph neural network (HieraGraphNet) that operates across multiple mesh resolutions via a novel topology- and geometry-aware coarsening (TGC) scheme. Unlike prior generative fluid models that operate on Cartesian grids or single-resolution graphs, CGFM explicitly targets large irregular 3D meshes and jointly learns shared dynamics across multiple physical conditions from short equilibrium trajectories, supported by a theoretical analysis bounding estimation and interpolation error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: CGFM enables direct sampling from learned equilibrium distributions, bypassing the costly transient simulation phase of numerical solvers and achieving a 606× GPU speedup over OpenFOAM for generating 3,000 samples on the Wing task. The theoretical results (Theorems 3.3–3.4) provide the first formal justification for why joint training across multiple systems mitigates the ill-posedness of learning distributions from short trajectories, which is a common practical constraint in expensive simulations. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D volumetric (not just surface) flows, multi-phase or compressible regimes, and whether the hierarchical coarsening strategy scales gracefully to meshes with millions of nodes. The reliance on pre-computed equilibrium states for training also limits applicability to systems where the equilibrium regime is not well-defined.
