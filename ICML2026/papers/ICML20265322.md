## Problem

Compositional data with known hierarchical structure (taxonomies, ontologies, phylogenies) lack a canonical orthonormal coordinate system that respects arbitrary tree topologies. Existing methods either ignore tree structure (CLR), are restricted to binary trees (PhILR, which forces arbitrary binarization of polytomies), or yield incomplete, task-specific contrasts rather than a full basis. The paper asks whether a geometrically principled, canonical orthonormal decomposition of the Aitchison tangent space exists for any tree, including polytomous ones.

## Input / Output

The method takes a d-dimensional compositional vector (normalized to unit sum, with zero-replacement preprocessing) and a rooted tree with d labeled leaves that may have arbitrary branching (polytomies allowed). It produces a (d × (d−1)) orthonormal ILR basis matrix V whose columns are indexed by (node, contrast-index) pairs, so each coordinate is a log-ratio balance comparing geometric means of specific child clades at a specific internal node. The construction is deterministic given the tree and fixed child orderings, and the resulting transform φ(x) = Vᵀ log x is an isometry from the Aitchison simplex to R^{d−1}.

## Method

PolyILR constructs the basis in two stages. At each internal node u with k_u children, it defines a local contrast subspace S_u (zero-sum vectors in R^{k_u}) equipped with a weighted inner product ⟨h,h'⟩_w = Σ_r h_r h'_r / n_r, where n_r is the number of descendant leaves under child r; this weighting accounts for unequal subtree sizes. A canonical orthonormal basis of S_u is obtained by applying Gram-Schmidt to Helmert contrasts under this weighted inner product. Each local basis vector is then spread to a global d-dimensional vector by assigning value H̃_{r,m}/n_r to every leaf under child r and zero elsewhere. The key mechanism is that the 1/n_r division in spreading exactly compensates for the n_r leaves each child contributes, so local orthonormality under ⟨·,·⟩_w guarantees global orthonormality in R^d. Across nodes, orthogonality follows from disjoint supports (for unrelated nodes) and the zero-sum property (for nested nodes), yielding a complete orthonormal basis of the Aitchison tangent space with every coordinate tied to a specific tree location.

## Results

On microbiome (HMP, cMD3) and single-cell (DISCO) benchmarks, PolyILR achieves classification accuracy within ±1.5% of CLR and PhILR for RF, while SVM and LR are identical across all three representations (confirming isometric equivalence). The headline differentiator is feature stability: PolyILR achieves Jaccard similarity of 0.43–0.92 for top-K features across CV folds, whereas PhILR collapses to near 0 (e.g., 0.01–0.07) due to arbitrary binarization of polytomies. On HMP body subsites (18 classes), PolyILR RF accuracy is 62.2% vs. 59.7% (CLR) and 60.8% (PhILR); on DISCO leukemia, 93.5% vs. 92.5% (CLR) and 94.0% (PhILR). Tree-level aggregation shows that coarse taxonomic contrasts (depth ≤ 2) capture 44–47% of total RF importance on HMP, enabling multiscale inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PolyILR against two baselines: CLR (centered log-ratio, same Aitchison geometry but no tree alignment) and PhILR (phylogenetic ILR, tree-aligned but restricted to binary trees requiring arbitrary binarization of polytomies). For SVM and logistic regression, all three methods produce identical accuracy and AUROC on every task, as guaranteed by the isometry of any valid ILR basis—this confirms PolyILR is a geometrically valid representation. For random forest, differences are modest (≤1.5%): PolyILR slightly outperforms CLR on HMP body subsites (62.2% vs. 59.7%) and cMD3 age category (79.7% vs. 78.5%), while PhILR edges ahead on DISCO leukemia (94.0% vs. 93.5%). The decisive advantage of PolyILR is feature stability: Jaccard similarity of top-K selected features across CV folds ranges from 0.43 to 0.92 for PolyILR, while PhILR collapses to 0.00–0.12 (index) and 0.01–0.16 (semantic) on most tasks, confirming that the instability is structural (arbitrary binarization) rather than stochastic. On the CIFAR-100 preliminary study, PolyILR-aligned gradient analysis shows the true class hierarchy yields significantly lower gradient entropy (0.576 vs. 0.632±0.001, p<0.001) than 1000 random trees, validating the geometric connection to softmax classifiers.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolyILR extends the ILR framework (Egozcue et al., 2003) and its tree-aligned instantiation PhILR (Silverman et al., 2017) from strictly binary phylogenies to arbitrary rooted trees with polytomies. Where PhILR forces an arbitrary binary resolution at each polytomous node, PolyILR introduces a weighted local geometry (Helmert contrasts under a 1/n_r inner product) at each internal node, yielding k_u − 1 canonical contrasts per node and a complete orthonormal basis without binarization. The paper also draws a new theoretical bridge to softmax classifiers by showing the logit quotient space under shift equivalence is isomorphic to the Aitchison tangent space, enabling tree-aligned gradient analysis of hierarchical classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For microbiome, single-cell, and genomics practitioners working with taxonomic or ontological hierarchies, PolyILR provides a drop-in, canonical coordinate system that makes feature selection stable and interpretable at every tree resolution—addressing a practical pain point where PhILR's arbitrary binarization destabilizes results. The softmax connection, though preliminary (single CIFAR-100 experiment), opens a direction for localizing classifier errors within class hierarchies and understanding coarse-to-fine learning dynamics. Key limitations remain: the method requires a fixed input tree (no topological uncertainty propagation), coordinates are only locally interpretable across subtrees, and the softmax application is a proof-of-concept rather than a validated training technique.
