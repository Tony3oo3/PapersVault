## Problem

LLMs frequently produce outputs that contradict explicit input evidence, yet prior work has not systematically quantified how the strength of co-occurrence associations within parametric priors determines the severity of this resistance. This paper identifies 'cognitive inertia'—an over-reliance on pretrained co-occurrence patterns that causes models to ignore conflicting contextual evidence—as a central, previously unquantified cause of input-evidence-unfaithful hallucinations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text input (a document for summarization, a passage plus question for QA, or a clinical scenario for binary classification) and produces a faithful text response. At inference time, the pipeline first extracts entities from the input, probes the LLM for each entity's top co-occurring partners, and generates adaptive reminder strings for any co-occurring pair that also appears in the input; these reminders are concatenated with the original prompt before the final generation call. The method is entirely training-free at deployment, requiring only the base LLM and a small number of additional forward passes for entity extraction and co-occurrence probing.

## Method

LLMInertia is a three-stage, training-free inference-time prompting framework inspired by human counter-inertial thinking. (1) A Cognitive Inertia Probe extracts entities from the input via an LLM prompt, then for each entity queries the LLM to return its top-k most frequent co-occurring entities (k=3 for induction tasks, k=5 for summarization/QA), building an adaptive high-frequency co-occurrence set. (2) For every co-occurring pair where both entities appear in the input, an adaptive reminder is generated (e.g., 'a and b may not necessarily be related. Please examine the original text carefully and make a thorough judgment'), with a generic fallback if no pairs match. (3) All reminders are injected into the original prompt, steering the model's attention away from spurious co-occurrence associations and toward the explicit input evidence. The paper further validates the cognitive-inertia hypothesis through controlled continued-pretraining experiments on medical and synthetic co-occurrence corpora, showing that both higher data frequency and more training epochs monotonically increase hallucination induction rates, and that LLMInertia reduces these rates by up to 35%.

## Results

On the co-occurrence induction datasets, LLMInertia reduces hallucination induction rates by up to 35% and improves accuracy by up to 35.68% across three LLM backbones (LLaMA-3-8B, LLaMA-3-70B, Qwen-2.5-7B). On four context-rich benchmarks, LLMInertia consistently outperforms all training-free baselines: with Qwen-2.5-7B, it achieves 89.31 Consistency on CNN/DailyMail (vs. 83.76 Base), 11.22 Anah-v2 on SQuAD v2 (vs. 17.22 Base), and 10.47 Anah-v2 on HaluEval (vs. 14.75 Base). GPT-5-based LLM evaluation confirms these gains, with LLMInertia scoring 4.54/5 on CNN/DailyMail and 4.79/5 on SQuAD v2 (vs. 4.11 and 4.60 for Base).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLMInertia outperforms all three primary baselines across every dataset and backbone. Against CoT (Wei et al., 2022), LLMInertia gains +4.97 Consistency on CNN/DailyMail (89.31 vs. 84.34) and reduces SQuAD v2 Anah-v2 by 1.84 points (11.22 vs. 13.06) with Qwen-2.5-7B. Against SymbCoT (Xu et al., 2024a), the advantage is similar: +4.86 Consistency on CNN/DailyMail and −2.03 Anah-v2 on SQuAD v2. Against Lookback (Chuang et al., 2024), LLMInertia leads by +4.55 Consistency on CNN/DailyMail and −2.46 Anah-v2 on SQuAD v2, while Lookback actually degrades SAMSum AlignScore (83.30 vs. 86.42 Base) whereas LLMInertia improves it to 89.14. LLMInertia also surpasses the training-required SFT baseline on most metrics (e.g., 91.54 vs. 89.56 SAMSum Consis on LLaMA-3-8B), though SFT edges it slightly on HaluEval Anah-v2 for LLaMA-3-70B (7.52 vs. 8.33), which the authors attribute to HaluEval's regular structural patterns lowering evaluation difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established research threads on LLM hallucination: the knowledge-conflict literature (Xie et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2025), which documents that parametric priors override contextual evidence, and the shortcut-learning literature (Kang & Choi, 2023; Zhang et al., 2024), which shows models exploit surface co-occurrences over semantic reasoning. The key departure is a systematic, controlled quantification of how co-occurrence association strength—manipulated via data frequency and training epochs—determines the severity of input-evidence-unfaithful hallucinations, unifying both threads under the 'cognitive inertia' framing. Methodologically, LLMInertia is a training-free, inference-time prompting approach inspired by counter-inertial thinking from cognitive psychology, distinct from CoT-style reasoning (Wei et al., 2022), attention-based detection (Chuang et al., 2024), or fine-tuning-based mitigations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first controlled, quantitative evidence that co-occurrence strength is a primary driver of input-evidence-unfaithful hallucinations, and that this effect is cumulative across both data composition and training duration—practically relevant given that domain-specific fine-tuning on small, imbalanced corpora is common. The training-free LLMInertia method offers a lightweight, deployable mitigation that outperforms SFT on most benchmarks, which is attractive for practitioners who cannot retrain large models. However, the approach is limited to explicit, surface-level co-occurrence associations and general-domain settings; handling implicit evidence, multi-hop reasoning chains, and domain-specific (e.g., medical, legal) hallucinations remains open. The ~31–34% error attribution to cognitive inertia suggests substantial room for further gains if more complex association patterns can be detected.
