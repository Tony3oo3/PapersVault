## Problem

Distributional RL methods rely on finite-dimensional categorical or quantile approximations whose fixed supports misalign with Bellman updates, introducing projection bias. When flow matching is applied to model return distributions, directly enforcing the Bellman relation at every intermediate time violates the Gaussian source boundary required by flow matching, and independently sampled noise for current and successor states produces high-variance, pathwise-misaligned training targets that destabilize critic learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state vectors or 64×64×3 RGB observations (with 3-frame stacking and random-shift augmentation) paired with actions, and produces a time-dependent velocity field v_θ(t, z | s, a) that, when integrated via explicit Euler (10 steps), generates samples from the learned return distribution. At deployment, a behavior-cloned proposal policy samples K=16 candidate actions, each scored by the mean terminal return under the learned flow, and the argmax action is executed.

## Method

PCBF constructs source-consistent Bellman-coupled paths: the current-state interpolant Z_t^s = tR + γZ_t^{s'} + (1−t)(1−γ)X₀ starts from the shared Gaussian base noise at t=0 and reaches the Bellman endpoint R+γX' at t=1, with the residual anchor term guaranteeing exact source alignment regardless of γ. Current and successor flows are driven by the same base noise X₀, so their intermediate trajectories are pathwise aligned rather than merely distributionally matched. The training target u_t^λ = (R+γX'−X₀) + λ[v_{θ⁻}(t, Z_t^{s'}|s',a') − (X'−X₀)] is a λ-parameterized control variate that interpolates between unbiased sample-based Bellman supervision (λ=0) and variance-reduced velocity-prediction correction (λ≈γ), with a distribution-free L₂ bias bound of |λ|(ε_app + ε_int) and a Gaussian closed form showing the intrinsic bias vanishes under shared-noise coupling (ρ=1) as γ→1 or t→1. A slowly updated target network (Polyak averaging) generates successor return samples, and the full system is trained by L₂ velocity regression on a fixed offline dataset.

## Results

Across 38 offline RL tasks on OGBench and D4RL, PCBF achieves the best or near-best aggregate performance on cube-double-play (70±3 vs. 69±4 for Value Flows), puzzle-4x4-play (32±2 vs. 27±4), and visual-antmaze-teleport (14±4 vs. 13±4). On D4RL Adroit, PCBF matches the strongest scalar baseline FQL (70±1 vs. 71±4). However, PCBF underperforms Value Flows on cube-triple-play (6±2 vs. 14±3) and visual-cube-double-play (3±0 vs. 13±2), indicating that distributional fidelity gains do not uniformly translate to task success in long-horizon sparse-reward or pixel-based settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCBF outperforms Value Flows (the closest flow-based distributional baseline) on cube-double-play (70 vs. 69), puzzle-4x4-play (32 vs. 27), and visual-antmaze-teleport (14 vs. 13), but is clearly behind on cube-triple-play (6 vs. 14) and visual-cube-double-play (3 vs. 13). Against scalar value-based methods, PCBF matches FQL on D4RL Adroit (70 vs. 71) and substantially outperforms IQL across all domains (e.g., 70 vs. 7 on cube-double-play). Among distributional baselines, PCBF dominates IQN and CODAC on every domain where they are evaluated (e.g., 70 vs. 42 on cube-double-play, 32 vs. 20 on puzzle-4x4). FloQ, which parameterizes scalar Q via flow matching rather than full return distributions, is competitive on scene-play (58 vs. 58) but trails PCBF on puzzle-4x4-play (28 vs. 32) and D4RL Adroit (70 vs. 70, within noise). The paper attributes PCBF's losses on cube-triple-play and visual-cube-double-play to policy-extraction coverage, visual representation limits, or suboptimal λ selection rather than distributional fitting failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

PCBF builds on the flow-matching framework of Lipman et al. (2023) and the distributional Bellman equation of Bellemare et al. (2017), but departs from the closest concurrent work (Value Flows, TD-Flow, DFC, Bellman Diffusion) in two key ways. First, it identifies and repairs the source-boundary mismatch that arises when a pointwise Bellman map is imposed at every flow time (the t=0 distribution shifts away from the required Gaussian prior), replacing full-t DCFM-style consistency with a source-consistent interpolated path that preserves the Bellman endpoint only at t=1. Second, it introduces shared-noise path coupling between current and successor return flows, which aligns intermediate trajectories and enables a λ-parameterized control-variate target—contrasting with Value Flows' independent-noise DCFM+BCFM combination and FlowIQN's endpoint-only quantile sorting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PCBF provides a theoretically grounded recipe for making flow matching compatible with the distributional Bellman equation, with a clean L2 bias bound, a shared-noise contraction result, and a Gaussian closed form showing why path coupling reduces intrinsic bias. The practical impact is a stable distributional critic for offline RL that matches or beats strong baselines on several OGBench and D4RL domains, though the gains are selective rather than uniform. Key open questions include adaptive per-step λ selection (currently tuned once per domain), whether the approach's advantages persist in more complex or higher-dimensional return spaces, and whether the 2.5× computational overhead over scalar critics is acceptable at scale.
