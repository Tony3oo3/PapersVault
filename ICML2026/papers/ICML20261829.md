## Problem

While average-iterate convergence for stochastic monotone variational inequalities is well-understood, anytime last-iterate guarantees for single-call methods remain limited, despite their practical importance in uncoupled learning dynamics where players must commit to a single current strategy. Prior results either require knowledge of the horizon, use multi-call methods incompatible with uncoupled play, or achieve slower rates (e.g., Õ(T^{-1/10}) or Õ(T^{-1/7})) through more elaborate anchoring-based designs. This paper asks whether simple regularized gradient methods with a single gradient evaluation per iteration can achieve fast anytime last-iterate convergence without horizon knowledge.

## Input / Output

At each iteration t, the algorithm receives a single noisy feedback ĝ_t ∈ R^d satisfying E[ĝ_t|x_t] = V(x_t) with E[‖ĝ_t‖²|x_t] ≤ G² and E[‖ĝ_t − V(x_t)‖²|x_t] ≤ σ², and produces the last iterate x_t ∈ X (a compact convex set of diameter D). Convergence is measured via the squared gap E[Gap(x_t)²] = E[max_{x∈X} ⟨V(x_t), x_t − x⟩²], which is strictly stronger than bounding E[Gap(x_t)]² and yields tighter concentration guarantees. The anytime property means the bound holds uniformly for all t ≥ 1 without specifying a total horizon T in advance.

## Method

The paper analyzes two single-call methods built on a time-varying regularized operator V_t(x) = V(x) + ω_t x with ω_t = γ_t/η_t, which converts the monotone VI into a sequence of strongly monotone subproblems with unique solutions x_t*. The RG method performs projected gradient descent on this regularized problem (x_{t+1} = Π_X((1−γ_t)x_t − η_t ĝ_t)), while the ROG method adds optimistic gradient reuse by maintaining a second sequence y_t and reusing ĝ_t in the x_{t+1} update. The key technical distinction is that RG's recursion carries a noise term of order G²η_t² (retaining a ‖V(x_t)‖² contribution even as σ→0), whereas ROG's recursion carries only σ²η_t², enabling variance-adaptive rates that approach O(t^{-1}) in the noiseless limit. The analysis tracks the distance to the moving regularized solution x_t* via a one-step recursion, with the critical technical contribution being a tight AM-GM treatment of cross terms that yields the contraction coefficient (1−γ_t) and improves the rate from O(t^{-1/3}) to O(t^{-2/5}). For ROG, a composite potential a_t = E[‖y_t − x_{t−1}*‖² + ½‖x_{t−1} − y_t‖²] couples the two sequences and is essential to close the recursion.

## Results

For stochastic smooth monotone VIs, the RG method achieves an anytime last-iterate rate of E[Gap(x_t)²] = O(t^{-2/5}), while the ROG method achieves the variance-adaptive rate E[Gap(x_t)²] = O(σ^{4/5} t^{-2/5} + t^{-1}), which approaches O(t^{-1}) as the noise variance σ² → 0. For λ-strongly monotone VIs, ROG yields E[Gap(x_t)²] = O(cσ²/(λ²t) + t^{-c}) for any constant c ≥ 2, and RG achieves O(1/(λ²t)). In the fixed-horizon setting, RG improves to O(√(log T / T)) and ROG to O(√(σ² log T / T) + (log T)²/T²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Cai & Zheng (2023), whose accelerated optimistic gradient (AOG) method achieves O(1/t²) anytime last-iterate convergence in the noiseless (σ=0) setting. The paper's ROG method recovers O(t^{-1}) in the noiseless limit, which is slower than Cai & Zheng's O(t^{-2}), but extends the guarantee to the stochastic setting with a variance-adaptive rate that interpolates between O(t^{-1}) (low noise) and O(σ^{4/5} t^{-2/5}) (high noise). Against the closest stochastic baselines—Abe et al. (2024) with Õ(T^{-1/10}) and Abe et al. (2025) with Õ(T^{-1/7})—both of which are fixed-horizon and rely on anchoring-based designs with periodically reinitialized schedules—the paper's RG method achieves the faster O(t^{-1/5}) rate in the stronger anytime setting without any horizon knowledge. Chen & Mazumdar (2024) obtain O(T^{-1/6}) for a generalized Frank-Wolfe method in a fixed-horizon setting, which is also slower than the paper's anytime O(t^{-1/5}). Alacaoglu et al. (2024) achieve Õ(T^{-1/4}) for the operator residual (not the gap function) in a horizon-calibrated setting, making a direct comparison difficult. Overall, the paper's methods win on both rate and generality (anytime vs. fixed-horizon) in the stochastic monotone VI setting, while losing to Cai & Zheng (2023) specifically in the noiseless regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimistic gradient framework of Popov (1980) and the accelerated optimistic gradient (AOG) algorithm of Cai & Zheng (2023) by introducing time-varying Tikhonov regularization that enables anytime last-iterate convergence under stochastic feedback. It departs from the anchoring-based designs of Abe et al. (2024, 2025), which require fixed-horizon schedules and periodic reinitialization, by showing that a simple jointly-decreasing step size and regularization weight suffice for faster anytime rates. The analysis also builds on the regularized-operator perspective (viewing the update as projected gradient descent on a strongly monotone surrogate) and the proximal-point interpretation of optimistic methods (Mokhtari et al., 2020), but introduces a tighter cross-term bound in the recursion that improves the rate from O(t^{-1/3}) to O(t^{-2/5}).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that simple single-call regularized gradient methods already achieve anytime last-iterate convergence for stochastic monotone VIs without knowing the horizon, outperforming more elaborate anchoring-based approaches in both rate and generality. The variance-adaptive rate of ROG (interpolating between O(t^{-1}) in the noiseless limit and O(σ^{4/5}t^{-2/5}) in the high-noise regime) provides a principled understanding of how optimism interacts with noise. Open questions include matching lower bounds for the stochastic gradient setting, parameter-free variants that do not require knowledge of L, D, or σ, and whether the gap between E[Gap(x_t)²] and E[Gap(x_t)]² is fundamental. The regret rate degrading from O(√T) to O(T^{4/5}) due to regularization highlights an unresolved tension between no-regret and last-iterate objectives.
