## Problem

Block diffusion models already exploit KV caching and block-wise causal inference, yet at every diffusion step within a block they still recompute attention over the entire growing KV cache, making long-context inference prohibitively expensive. The paper identifies an underexplored structural property: attention contributions from tokens outside the current block remain largely stable across adjacent diffusion steps, while block-internal attention varies substantially, meaning the repeated block-external attention computation is largely redundant.

## Input / Output

The method operates at the attention layer inside a block diffusion transformer. At each diffusion step it receives the current block's query vectors (B tokens) and the full KV cache of N previously generated tokens (or video frames), and produces the full attention output for the B query tokens. The key design constraint is that the cached block-external statistics (weighted sum and log-normalizer) scale only with block size B, not context length N, so memory overhead remains negligible even at 1M-token contexts. A threshold τ controls whether cached attention is reused or recomputed based on how many tokens in the block were updated, and for video models a per-head similarity threshold γ gates reuse.

## Method

FlashBlock decomposes each attention computation into block-internal (keys within the current block) and block-external (all prior context) components. At the first diffusion step of a block, it computes full attention and caches the block-external weighted sum U_out and log-normalizer L_out; at subsequent steps it recomputes only block-internal attention and combines the two via a FlashAttention-style log-space composition (subtracting the max log-normalizer for numerical stability). Selective reuse is governed by an update threshold τ (recompute if ≥τ tokens changed) and, for video, a head-wise similarity threshold γ. The formulation is orthogonal to sparse attention: the sparse-selected keys become J_in and the unselected residual becomes J_out, so caching the residual across steps compensates for information loss from aggressive sparsification. An optional reuse-aware distillation stage (LoRA adapters, KL divergence to a dense-attention teacher) mitigates distributional mismatch in discrete diffusion language models where progressive unmasking causes abrupt attention changes.

## Results

On diffusion language models (Trado-8B-Thinking, τ=2), FlashBlock achieves up to 1.44× higher token throughput (312→451 TPS at block size 4; 532→674 TPS at block size 8) while maintaining accuracy within 0.2–1.6% of the dense baseline across GSM8K, MATH500, and AIME. On video generation (LongLive-1.3B, VBench2), FlashBlock reduces attention time by 1.6× (19.89s→12.46s) and end-to-end latency from 73.28s to 66.07s, while matching or exceeding the dense baseline on most VBench macro categories. When combined with SparseD at 20% attention density, FlashBlock recovers +7.96 accuracy on GSM8K and +9.76 pass@1 on HumanEval over SparseD alone, demonstrating its role as a complementary residual-reuse strategy under aggressive sparsification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dense Trado-8B-Thinking baseline, FlashBlock matches accuracy within ≤1.6% on all math and coding benchmarks while delivering 1.44× throughput gains, confirming that block-external attention reuse introduces negligible quality loss. Compared to SparseD (sparse attention for dLLMs), FlashBlock acts as a complementary residual-reuse layer: at 20% density it recovers +7.96 GSM8K accuracy and +9.76 HumanEval pass@1, and the L1 distance to full attention drops from 0.0031 to 0.0008, showing it compensates for information lost by sparsification. On video generation, FlashBlock outperforms SpargeAttention at comparable density (45% vs 50%) on Human Fidelity (0.7861 vs 0.7609), Controllability (0.3026 vs 0.2837), and Physics (0.4231 vs 0.4257), while reducing attention time from 18.73s to 12.46s. Against Pyramid Attention Broadcast (PAB) on VBench subsets, FlashBlock achieves higher scores on Dynamic Attribute (0.3475 vs 0.2857), Human Identity (0.7593 vs 0.6876), and Material (0.6138 vs 0.3906) at similar or lower latency, validating the benefit of separating block-internal from block-external attention before reuse. On LLaDA-2.0 (a 100B-scale dLLM), training-free FlashBlock preserves LongBench average within 1 point (48.98→47.96) while achieving 1.33× decoding speedup, demonstrating cross-model generalizability. The Trado-4B-Instruct dense baseline (91.21 GSM8K, 70.80 MATH500, 45.12 HumanEval) serves as the upper bound; FlashBlock+SparseD at 40% density recovers most of the gap (87.26/69.40/44.51), showing the combined approach approaches dense quality at substantially lower compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashBlock builds directly on the block diffusion paradigm (Arriola et al., 2025a; Hoogeboom et al., 2022), which introduced KV caching and block-by-block causal inference to make diffusion language models and video models tractable. It departs from prior KV-reuse methods for diffusion LLMs (Hu et al., 2025; Ma et al., 2025) by exploiting a structural property unique to block diffusion—cross-step stability of block-external attention—rather than approximating or compressing KV states across denoising steps in a standard full-sequence diffusion setting. The method is also positioned as orthogonal to sparse attention (SparseD, SpargeAttention), which reduces computation within a single attention call, whereas FlashBlock reuses the residual attention contribution from unselected keys across diffusion steps, making the two complementary rather than competing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone deploying long-context diffusion LLMs or video diffusion models: a 1.44× throughput gain and 1.6× attention-time reduction with negligible quality loss, achieved as a training-free (or optionally LoRA-distilled) inference-time modification. The finding that block-external attention is stable across steps while block-internal attention is not provides a clean, actionable principle for future block-diffusion systems. Key open questions include whether the stability property holds uniformly across architectures and modalities, and the lack of kernel-level integration between block-diffusion-aware sparse attention and paged KV caches, which the authors explicitly leave to future work.
