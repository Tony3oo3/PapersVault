## Problem

Machine unlearning for diffusion language models (DLMs) remains entirely unexplored, despite DLMs' fundamentally different generation mechanisms—bidirectional conditioning, parallel full-sequence denoising, and flexible prefix-suffix infilling—making existing autoregressive unlearning methods inapplicable. The paper identifies two critical gaps: unlearning performance in DLMs is highly sensitive to generation hyperparameters (output length, denoising steps), and DLMs remain vulnerable to elicitation attacks via adversarial prefix-suffix contexts even after being trained with such perturbations, because their bidirectional attention allows information to flow from both sides of the prompt.

## Input / Output

The DLM receives a concatenated sequence of the form prefix ⊕ prompt ⊕ [MASK] ⊕ suffix, where the prefix and suffix are adversarially generated contextual snippets (up to 512 tokens each) that provide background information without directly revealing the target answer. The model generates text in the masked region using iterative denoising over a fixed number of steps (32–128) and a fixed output length (32–128 tokens), with a block length of 32 for parallel generation. The context generator (a separate LLM) takes the original prompt and target response as input and outputs a prefix-suffix pair designed to facilitate recall of the forgotten content while avoiding trivial inference cues.

## Method

The paper proposes an adversarial reinforcement learning framework in which a context generator and the DLM are jointly trained via GRPO in a min-max game: the generator produces K prefix-suffix context variants per prompt to maximize undesired recall, while the DLM suppresses the target response under those contexts. Two key components address training challenges specific to this adversarial setup. First, a causal influence regularization computes the log-probability change induced by removing the prefix (or suffix) from the prompt, enabling fine-grained credit assignment so the generator does not collapse to relying on a single component. Second, a regret-based difficulty calibration compares the current DLM policy against a self-bootstrapped reference model (initialized from the updated policy and further optimized to explicitly suppress the target response), rewarding contexts where the current model still recalls the target but the stronger reference does not—thereby maintaining intermediate difficulty and preventing reward collapse. Both the DLM and generator are updated with group-based advantages computed over their respective rollout groups, with auxiliary signals standardized and clipped within each group.

## Results

On the TOFU dataset with LLaDA-8B, the proposed adversarial RL method achieves an LLM-as-Judge score of 0.55 under clean inputs, 0.65 under adversarial prefix, and 0.63 under adversarial full context (lower is better), substantially outperforming the Gradient Ascent baseline which degrades sharply under adversarial contexts. On MUSE-Books, similar improvements are observed across all three input variants. Model utility on retain and general benchmarks remains comparable to the GA baseline, confirming that the RL-based formulation avoids the utility collapse typical of supervised unlearning objectives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single baseline: Gradient Ascent (GA) unlearning (Jang et al., 2023), adapted to the DLM setting via the diffusion unlearning objective in Eq. (3). Under clean inputs, GA already achieves strong unlearning effectiveness, making it a competitive reference. However, when inputs are augmented with adversarial prefix-suffix contexts, GA's unlearning performance degrades substantially, with the unlearned model's recall rate approaching that of the original non-unlearned model. The proposed adversarial RL method significantly outperforms GA under both adversarial prefix and adversarial full settings, and notably generalizes to contexts generated by a stronger, unseen model (Qwen3-30B-A3B) at inference time, whereas GA was only trained against a fixed set of adversarial contexts. Model utility is preserved at levels comparable to GA, demonstrating that the RL-based relative reward optimization avoids the distributional drift that plagues supervised unlearning approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the machine unlearning literature from autoregressive LLMs to diffusion language models (DLMs), a setting the authors identify as entirely unexplored. The unlearning objective is derived from gradient ascent (Jang et al., 2023), adapted to the masked-diffusion training loss, while the optimization backbone is GRPO (Guo et al., 2025), which handles the intractable log-likelihoods of iterative denoising. The probability estimation for causal influence scores follows the mean-field decomposition from d1 (Zhao et al., 2025). The key departure from prior unlearning work is the adversarial min-max formulation with a context generator, motivated by DLM-specific vulnerabilities to prefix-suffix conditioning that have no analogue in autoregressive settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic characterization of DLM unlearning, revealing that performance is highly sensitive to generation hyperparameters (output length, denoising steps) and that adversarial prefix-suffix contexts can collapse unlearning gains to near-zero — findings that will inform evaluation protocols as DLMs gain adoption. The adversarial RL framework with causal influence regularization and regret-based difficulty calibration offers a practical recipe for robust unlearning, though its scalability to larger DLMs and its behavior under multi-turn or streaming elicitation attacks remain open. A practical limitation is that the inference-time evaluation uses a much stronger context generator (Qwen3-30B) than the training-time one (Qwen3-4B), so the robustness gap may narrow or widen with different generator scales.
