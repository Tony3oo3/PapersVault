## Problem

Existing multi-hop fact verification methods (SFT, ProgramFC, CoT) learn end-to-end mappings from claims to labels without explicitly modeling the directed structural dependencies between evidence and intermediate inferences, making them prone to fractured logical chains and hallucinated reasoning. The paper further identifies an underexplored empirical phenomenon: an inverted U-shaped correlation between reasoning-chain length and accuracy, where unconstrained structural expansion paradoxically degrades verification performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a claim text and a set of retrieved evidence documents as input and produces a binary verification label (Supported/Refuted) accompanied by a fully serialized SCM dependency graph. The graph is a DAG whose nodes are exogenous variables (evidence-grounded facts), endogenous variables (intermediate inferences and the final verdict), and directed edges encoding structural functions; it is serialized in strict topological order so that every endogenous variable appears only after all its declared parents have been introduced, yielding a single-pass, inspectable reasoning trace rather than a free-form CoT string.

## Method

The framework operates in two sequential stages. First, a teacher LLM (Qwen3-30B-A3B) distills structured SCM components (U, V, F) from seed queries on HoVer and EX-FEVER, and a student model (Qwen3-8B) is supervised-fine-tuned to reproduce these topologically ordered dependency graphs. Second, Group Relative Policy Optimization refines the policy using a composite reward that jointly scores answer correctness (R_c), structural parsimony via the evidence-to-inference ratio Delta=|U|-|V| (R_s), and a length penalty targeting an empirically estimated optimal token interval (R_l). The key mechanism is a structural validity gate during generation: a new endogenous variable may be added to the graph only if all its parent nodes are already present, enforcing topological order and suppressing unsupported logical leaps. GRPO's group-relative advantage estimation (sampling K sequences per prompt and normalizing rewards within the group) stabilizes training while the multi-objective reward dynamically balances structural depth against conciseness, directly addressing the observed inverted-U trade-off.

## Results

SCM-GRPO achieves the best average accuracy of 71.02% across all five evaluation subsets (HoVer 2/3/4-hop and EX-FEVER 2/3-hop), outperforming the strongest external baseline Search-o1 (67.20%) by 3.82 points. On the most challenging HoVer 4-hop subset, SCM-GRPO reaches 60.88%, surpassing the next-best method (DP at 59.57%). Under the same Qwen3-8B backbone, SCM-GRPO improves over the raw Qwen3-8B baseline (58.41% avg) by 12.61 points and over Qwen3-8B+ProgramFC (64.31%) by 6.71 points, confirming that the gains stem from the SCM-GRPO framework rather than backbone choice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCM-GRPO outperforms all five external baselines on average accuracy. Against Direct Prediction (DP, GPT-3.5; 69.58% avg), SCM-GRPO gains +1.44 points, with the largest margin on HoVer 4-hop (+1.31) and EX-FEVER 3-hop (+1.98). Against ProgramFC (GPT-3.5; 61.76% avg), the gap is substantial at +9.26 points, and under the same Qwen3-8B backbone SCM-GRPO still leads Qwen3-8B+ProgramFC (64.31%) by +6.71 points. Against RAG (60.96% avg), SCM-GRPO leads by +10.06 points, indicating that retrieval-augmented generation without explicit structural reasoning is insufficient for multi-hop verification. Against Decompose-Verify (61.38% avg), the advantage is +9.64 points, suggesting that simple claim decomposition does not capture the directed dependency structure needed. Against the strongest baseline Search-o1 (67.20% avg), SCM-GRPO achieves +3.82 points, with consistent gains across all five subsets (ranging from +1.31 to +4.46 points), demonstrating that iterative retrieval alone cannot match the structural grounding provided by the SCM-GRPO framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Structural Causal Model (SCM) formalism from causal abstraction literature (Geiger et al., 2021) as a purely structural dependency graph for multi-hop fact verification, explicitly disclaiming full causal inference (no interventions, counterfactuals, or do-calculus). It builds on the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeek for stable RL optimization and on prior MHFV methods (ProgramFC, FOLK, Decompose-Verify) that model reasoning as end-to-end mappings without explicit directed dependency structure. The key departure is combining a DAG-constrained reasoning template (exogenous evidence → endogenous inferences → verdict) with a composite reward that penalizes structural over-expansion, rather than treating CoT length as purely beneficial.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most useful contribution is the empirical demonstration that unconstrained structural reasoning degrades accuracy (Qwen3-14B drops ~10 points on HoVer 2-hop when SCM-CoT is added without RL), providing a concrete rationale for length-aware RL in fact verification. However, the overall accuracy gains over strong baselines are modest (~1.5% average), and the framework's dependence on a 30B teacher model for data construction limits practical accessibility. Open questions include whether the inverted U-shaped length-accuracy relationship generalizes beyond 4-hop settings and whether the structural faithfulness guarantee (>95% DAG validity) translates to genuine logical correctness rather than merely well-formed graphs.
