## Problem

Large Audio-Language Models (LALMs) are significantly more vulnerable to harmful audio inputs than text, yet no principled inference-time safety defense exists for this modality. Directly transferring LLM-based activation steering fails because harmful and safe speech activations occupy widely divergent latent distributions with no shared subspace, and prompt-based defenses from LVLMs cause unconspicuous over-refusal on borderline benign queries.

## Input / Output

At inference time, SARSteer takes a standard LALM query (audio signal + text instruction) and modifies the hidden activations at every generated token position by adding a pre-computed steering vector scaled by α=0.1. The steering vector is computed once offline from 100 paired harmful-safe audio queries using a simple refusal text prompt ('I cannot assist with that.') and PCA on safe-query activations, requiring no modification to the audio input or model weights. The output is the steered hidden state, which shifts the model's response toward refusal for harmful queries while leaving benign responses essentially unchanged.

## Method

SARSteer is a training-free, inference-time defense with two components. First, text-derived refusal steering computes the steering vector as the difference in mean activations between a harmful audio query and the same query with a refusal text prompt appended, yielding a modality-agnostic refusal direction that avoids the distributional gap problem of audio-based steering. Second, decomposed safe-space ablation applies PCA to safe-query activations to identify the dominant benign subspace (top-k principal components, k=10), then projects the steering vector onto the orthogonal complement of this subspace, removing any component that would interfere with safe responses. The key insight is that audio activations show early and persistent separation between harmful and safe queries across all layers (confirmed by t-SNE and CKA analysis), making audio-derived steering directions degenerate, whereas text-derived refusal signals provide a reliable, modality-agnostic direction. This design ensures the steering signal emphasizes harmful-related activations while minimizing interference with benign inputs, achieving strong refusal on harmful queries without over-refusal.

## Results

On Qwen2-Audio, SARSteer reduces the attack success rate (ASR) on Figstep-audio from 51.60% (no defense) to 10.80%, while improving the Balanced Refusal Rate (BRR) from 70.20% to 79.95%. On Kimi-Audio, ASR drops from 15.60% to 10.00% and BRR rises from 61.40% to 88.80%. General utility on AirBench is preserved or slightly improved (7.53 vs. 7.43 average score on Qwen2-Audio), confirming that safety gains do not come at the cost of benign performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SARSteer decisively outperforms both vanilla steering baselines (MDSteer-h2s and MDSteer-c2r), which actually *worsen* harmfulness (e.g., 84.00% and 90.80% ASR vs. 51.60% no-defense on Qwen2-Audio Figstep-audio), confirming the paper's central claim that audio-modality steering is infeasible. Against prompt-based defenses, SARSteer achieves a substantially better safety–utility tradeoff: on Qwen2-Audio, FSD reaches a comparable 12.00% ASR but collapses BRR to 63.20% (vs. SARSteer's 79.95%), and AdaShield yields 30.00% ASR with 69.80% BRR. On Kimi-Audio, AdaShield achieves 0.00% ASR on Figstep-audio but at the cost of a 52.60% BRR (severe over-refusal), whereas SARSteer maintains 88.80% BRR with only 10.00% ASR. Compared to the fine-tuning-based RRS (Appendix B.6), SARSteer reduces ASR from 52.40% to 10.80% on Figstep-audio while running 4.3× faster (266 s vs. 1145 s). Overall, SARSteer is the only method that simultaneously achieves top-2 lowest ASR and highest BRR across both models and all four safety benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation steering paradigm from LLMs (Panickssery et al., 2023; Arditi et al., 2024) and prompt-based defenses from LVLMs (AdaShield, FSD) to the audio-language setting, but identifies a fundamental modality-specific failure: audio activations exhibit early and persistent distributional separation between harmful and safe queries (confirmed by t-SNE and CKA), making contrastive steering directions infeasible. SARSteer departs from prior work by deriving the steering vector from text refusal prompts appended to the same audio input (bypassing the non-steerable audio space) and by introducing PCA-based safe-subspace ablation to prevent the over-refusal that plagues prompt-based defenses on borderline-safe queries. The paper positions itself as the first principled inference-time defense for LALMs, filling a gap where only two initial fine-tuning-based attempts (RRS, ALMGuard) existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LALMs become central to voice assistants and real-time speech interaction, the finding that audio inputs are significantly more vulnerable to harmful queries than text (with ASR up to 51.6% on Qwen2-Audio without defense) makes this a practically urgent problem. SARSteer's inference-time design (no fine-tuning, ~266s setup) makes it immediately deployable, and the PCA ablation mechanism provides an interpretable, modality-agnostic template that could generalize to other multimodal safety settings. Open questions include whether the method degrades under truly adversarial audio (beyond TTS-generated speech), how it interacts with future LALMs that may have different audio encoder architectures, and whether the single-direction steering assumption holds for more nuanced safety categories. The paper's reliance on TTS-synthesized audio for both training and evaluation also leaves open the question of robustness to natural human speech variation in deployment.
