## Problem

Federated stochastic multi-level compositional minimax optimization suffers from slow convergence rates in existing algorithms, with the state-of-the-art LocalSMCGDAM achieving only O(κ⁴/Nε⁴) sample complexity under the restrictive NC-SC assumption. The interplay of multi-level compositional bias, minimax gradient bias, and federated consensus errors makes convergence analysis particularly challenging, and no prior work provides guarantees under the more general NC-PL condition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265898/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes stochastic samples drawn at each of K inner compositional levels and the outer level across N federated workers, along with initial primal/dual/smoothing parameters (x₀, y₀, z₀) and hyperparameters controlling learning rates, STORM momentum coefficients, and the communication period τ. It outputs an (ε, ε/√κ)-stationary point of the smoothed problem, which is then refined via a stagewise variant into an ε-stationary point of the original minimax objective. The mini-batch size remains constant at O(1) after the initial iteration, and the method requires only O(κ/ε²) communication rounds among workers.

## Method

LS²MC-GDA integrates two key techniques into the federated multi-level compositional minimax setting: (1) a smoothing term ω‖x−z‖²/2 applied to local models via an auxiliary variable z, which makes the smoothed loss strongly convex in x and decouples the primal/dual learning rate scaling, and (2) STORM-style recursive momentum updates on the stochastic compositional gradients for both x and y, reducing variance without requiring large batch sizes. Local updates proceed for τ iterations before periodic averaging of all variables (x, y, z, h^(k), p, q) across workers. A stagewise variant (Stagewise-LSMC-GDA) then solves a fixed-x subproblem under the two-sided PL condition to convert the (ε, ε/√κ)-stationary point into a full ε-stationary point, yielding an overall sample complexity of O(κ^{3/2}/Nε³) with linear speedup in N and level-independent convergence (K appears only in constants).

## Results

The primary contribution is theoretical: LS²MC-GDA achieves a sample complexity of O(κ^{3/2}/Nε³) and communication complexity of O(κ/ε²) for federated multi-level compositional minimax optimization under the NC-PL condition, with linear speedup in the number of workers N and level-independent convergence (K appears only in constants). The stagewise variant achieves O(1/Nε) sample complexity and O(1/ε^{1/2}) communication complexity under the PL-PL condition. Experimentally, on federated deep AUC maximization (ResNet20, N=8 GPUs), LS²MC-GDA consistently attains the highest test AUC across CATvsDOG, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and STL-10 at communication periods of 4, 8, and 16, outperforming all four baselines by a large margin, particularly versus LocalSMCGDAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LS²MC-GDA dominates all four baselines on both sample and communication complexity under the NC-PL setting. Compared to LocalSMCGDAM (Zhang et al., 2024), the closest multi-level compositional baseline, LS²MC-GDA improves the sample complexity from O(κ⁴/Nε⁴) to O(κ^{3/2}/Nε³) and communication complexity from O(κ³/ε³) to O(κ/ε²), while also relaxing the assumption from NC-SC to the weaker NC-PL. Against FedSGDA-M (Wu et al., 2023), LS²MC-GDA improves the condition-number dependence from O(κ³) to O(κ^{3/2}) in sample complexity and from O(κ²) to O(κ) in communication complexity. Versus FESS-GDA (Shen et al., 2024), LS²MC-GDA achieves a better sample complexity (O(κ^{3/2}/Nε³) vs. O(κ²/Nε⁴)) but matches its communication complexity of O(κ/ε²); under the PL-PL condition, FESS-GDA retains a slight edge in communication (O(log(1/ε)) vs. O(1/ε^{1/2})) due to its unbiased gradient estimator. Experimentally, LS²MC-GDA achieves the highest test AUC on all four image classification datasets (CATvsDOG, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10) across all communication periods, with the largest gap observed against LocalSMCGDAM, confirming the theoretical improvements in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated stochastic multi-level compositional minimax optimization framework introduced by Zhang et al. (2024, LocalSMCGDAM) by relaxing the restrictive NC-SC assumption to the broader NC-PL condition and incorporating two key techniques: smoothing applied to local models (rather than only the global model as in FESS-GDA, Shen et al. 2024) and STORM-style variance reduction on the compositional gradients (Cutkosky & Orabona, 2019). The smoothing idea traces back to single-machine minimax work (Zhang et al., 2020a; Yang et al., 2022b) and its recent extension to multi-level compositional minimax (Zhang & Gao, 2025b), while the variance-reduction machinery builds on STORM and its adaptations to compositional problems (Jiang et al., 2022; Gao, 2024b). A novel stagewise variant without smoothing updates is introduced to bridge the gap between (ε, ε/√κ)-stationary and ε-stationary points, a translation previously handled only in single-machine or standard federated minimax settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish O(κ^{3/2}/Nε³) sample complexity and O(κ/ε²) communication complexity for federated multi-level compositional minimax optimization under NC-PL, with level-independent convergence and linear speedup in the number of workers. The stagewise variant further provides the first convergence guarantees under the PL-PL condition for this problem class, though its communication complexity (O(1/ε^{1/2})) is slightly worse than FESS-GDA's O(log(1/ε)) due to the biased gradient estimator inherent in the compositional structure. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more heterogeneous data distributions, whether the smoothing hyperparameter ω can be adapted dynamically, and whether the theoretical gains translate to further practical improvements in highly non-IID federated settings beyond the AUC maximization and portfolio optimization experiments shown.
