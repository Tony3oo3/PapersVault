## Problem

Self-attention encodes temporal structure through positional mechanisms (RoPE, ALiBi) that treat transport and reliability as independent design choices: RoPE provides deterministic phase rotation without uncertainty accumulation, while ALiBi imposes a distance penalty without a corresponding transport operator. This decoupling causes RoPE to suffer phase wrap-around at long contexts and prevents a principled account of how token reliability should vary with temporal distance under a shared dynamical model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of word-level token embeddings (d=256) and produces updated token representations through a 6-layer Transformer with 8 attention heads. Each RFA head operates in a complex-valued eigenbasis where queries, keys, and values are rotated into a common stationary frame, aggregated with precision-weighted attention, and counter-rotated back; the layer outputs an innovation (the difference between the filtered estimate and the current value) that is projected into the residual stream rather than replacing it outright. Per-head scalar parameters governing process noise, measurement noise, decay rate, and robustness degrees of freedom are learned end-to-end, adding only ~0.02% parameters over standard attention.

## Method

RFA models each token as a noisy observation of a latent state evolving under a linear time-invariant SDE, and formulates attention as a parallel batch state estimator: past keys are transported to the query position via the state transition matrix, and the Differential Lyapunov Equation provides an analytic covariance for the accumulated process uncertainty. Attention scores are computed as robust Mahalanobis distances between the query and transported keys under these SDE-derived precisions, with a Student-t (power-law) influence function downweighting tokens whose residuals are unexpectedly large. Under isotropic decay and noise per head, the full anisotropic tensor formulation collapses to scalar precision kernels depending only on temporal lag, preserving O(N²d) compute and O(N²+Nd) memory. Spectrally-Coupled RFA (SC-RFA) partitions the frequency spectrum across heads and couples each head's decay rate to its maximum frequency, creating a bank of lag-selective filters that suppress high-frequency phase interference at long ranges while low-frequency heads act as stable long-range integrators.

## Results

On WikiText-103 (trained at L=512), SC-RFA (M2) achieves 27.54 test PPL at the training length, improving over RoPE (28.48) by 0.94 PPL, and reduces long-context degradation to 37.19 PPL at L=4096 versus RoPE's 72.69. With stronger spectral damping (M3, b=5.0), perplexity remains nearly flat at 28.16 PPL through L=4096. On BabyLM-2025, SC-RFA (M2) achieves 17.36 PPL at L=512 (vs. RoPE's 17.70) and 22.25 at L=4096 (vs. RoPE's 33.29), confirming gains on a second benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RoPE, both RFA and SC-RFA achieve lower perplexity at every evaluated context length on both WikiText-103 and BabyLM-2025, with the gap widening substantially at longer horizons (e.g., 37.19 vs. 72.69 PPL at L=4096 on WikiText-103). Against ALiBi, SC-RFA wins at the training length (27.54 vs. 28.59) and at L=1024 (26.73 vs. 27.30), but ALiBi's strict linear recency penalty gives it lower perplexity at L=2048 (26.54 vs. 29.46) and L=4096 (26.30 vs. 37.19), reflecting a trade-off between aggressive locality and uncertainty-weighted long-range integration. The geometry-only baselines (Decayed RoPE, SC-RoPE) slow RoPE's degradation but still underperform RFA at all lengths, indicating that decay and spectral coupling alone are insufficient without explicit DLE-derived uncertainty modeling. SC-RFA (M3) with strong damping (b=5.0) outperforms ALiBi at L=2048 (26.37 vs. 26.54) while remaining competitive at L=4096 (28.16 vs. 26.30), suggesting the damping coefficient continuously interpolates between the two regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

RFA positions self-attention as a parallel batch state estimator for a linear SDE, departing from both recursive filtering approaches (KalmanNet, KLA) that compress history into a recurrent state and from SSMs (S4, HiPPO) that trade explicit token interactions for convolutional efficiency. It unifies RoPE and ALiBi as limiting cases of a single dynamical model—noiseless zero-decay rotations and pure Brownian diffusion, respectively—whereas prior positional encodings treat transport geometry and reliability decay as independent design choices. The spectrally-coupled variant (SC-RFA) extends this by partitioning the frequency bank across heads and tying each head's decay rate to its maximum frequency, addressing RoPE's phase wrap-around without the train-inference mismatch of post-hoc methods like YaRN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, uncertainty-aware derivation of attention weights that explains why different heads specialize into recency-biased (diffusive) versus lag-selective (integrative) filtering regimes, offering a mechanistic account of multi-scale temporal behavior in Transformers. Key limitations include the isotropic noise/decay constraint per head (which precludes dimension-dependent uncertainty), the factorized likelihood approximation that neglects cross-token correlations, and evaluation restricted to small 6-layer models on WikiText-103 and BabyLM-2025, leaving open whether the gains persist at scale. ALiBi still outperforms SC-RFA at the longest tested context (L=4096), suggesting the uncertainty-weighted integration of distant tokens has not yet fully matched the stability of aggressive locality. Relaxing the isotropic and LTI constraints while preserving parallel tractability is identified as the central open challenge.
