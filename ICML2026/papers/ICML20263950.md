## Problem

Block Hadamard rotations reduce the online compute cost of full-vector rotations in post-training quantization from O(d log d) to O(d log b), but the effect of block structure on outlier suppression was previously uncharacterized. This paper provides the first non-asymptotic, deterministic analysis showing that block rotation outlier suppression is fundamentally limited by the ℓ1 norm mass distribution across blocks, and that reducing block size can increase worst-case post-rotation outliers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263950/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained model weights and a small calibration set (a single 2048-token sequence suffices for permutation calibration; 128 sequences of 2048 tokens for rounding) and produces quantized weights and activations at target formats (INT4, FP4, MXFP4) with a user-specified block size b. The key design constraint is that all permutations are merged into surrounding linear layers before deployment via permutation-equivariant regions, so the deployed compute graph is unaltered and incurs zero additional inference-time overhead beyond the block rotation itself.

## Method

PeRQ (Permute, Rotate, then Quantize) is driven by a theoretical insight: the worst-case post-rotation outlier under block Hadamard rotations is proportional to max_j ||X_{j}||_1, so equalizing per-block ℓ1 norms tightens the quantization error bound. MassDiff is a greedy algorithm that sorts coordinates by descending average magnitude over calibration data and assigns each to the block currently with the smallest accumulated ℓ1 norm, directly minimizing the expected maximum per-block mass. The resulting permutation matrices are merged into adjacent linear layers by exploiting permutation-equivariant regions (contiguous subgraphs of elementwise operations like Swish or ReLU), so the deployed model requires no extra computation. The full pipeline composes MassDiff permutation with block Hadamard rotations at the down-projection input and full-vector rotations (Hadamard or learned via Cayley optimization) at other positions, followed by error-correcting rounding (Qronos, GPTQ, or RTN).

## Results

On INT4 (the most stringent format), PeRQ† achieves WikiText2 perplexity of 15.9/10.9/8.4 on Llama3 1B/3B/8B, while MR-GPTQ/BRQ catastrophically fails at 2e3/6e3/7e3. On MXFP4, PeRQ† achieves 13.2/9.6/7.2 perplexity on Llama3, outperforming MR-GPTQ/BRQ (14.2/11.2/7.4) and maintaining 95% of full-precision zero-shot accuracy. Across all formats, PeRQ maintains over 91% of full-precision zero-shot accuracy on INT4. For block size 16 on Llama3 1B INT4, PeRQ recovers up to 90% of full-vector rotation perplexity (18.2 vs. 16.1), compared to only 46% without permutations (35.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MR-GPTQ/BRQ (Egiazarian et al., 2026; Shao et al., 2025), PeRQ achieves a dramatic win on INT4 where the baselines completely collapse (perplexity 2e3–7e3 on Llama3 1B/3B/8B) while PeRQ† maintains 15.9/10.9/8.4; on MXFP4 the gap narrows to a modest 1.0–1.6 perplexity improvement (e.g., 13.2 vs. 14.2 on Llama3 1B), consistent with the paper's observation that MX formats inherently mitigate outliers via group-wise scaling. Against the SpinQuant variant (Liu et al., 2025, used as the 'No Permute' ablation in Table 10), PeRQ† substantially outperforms on every task: on Llama3 8B INT4, WikiText2 perplexity drops from 11.6 to 8.4, and GSM8K accuracy recovers from 1.2% (near-random) to 45.9%, while zero-shot accuracy on ARC-C improves from 23.1% to 40.8%. PeRQ† also consistently outperforms BRQ-Spin (which learns block rotations via Cayley optimization without permutations), e.g., 15.9 vs. 1e3 perplexity on Llama3 1B INT4. The paper notes that PeRQ† sometimes achieves lower perplexity than the BF16 baseline (e.g., Qwen3 1.7B: 13.6 vs. 15.2 on INT4), suggesting mild calibration overfitting accompanied by modest zero-shot accuracy reductions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on rotation-based PTQ (QuaRot, SpinQuant) by providing the first non-asymptotic, deterministic analysis of outlier suppression under block Hadamard rotations, complementing the asymptotic expected-error analyses of Egiazarian et al. (MR-GPTQ) and Shao et al. (BRQ). It departs from prior structured-rotation methods (FlatQuant, ButterflyQuant, DuQuant) by identifying mass concentration across blocks as the fundamental bottleneck and proposing a greedy permutation (MassDiff) to equalize blockwise ℓ₁ norms before rotation. The key architectural insight—that permutations can be absorbed into surrounding weights via permutation-equivariant regions—builds on the mergeable-rotation paradigm of QuaRot but extends it to a new class of transformations (permutations) that were previously considered to alter network computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical framework gives practitioners a principled, geometry-based explanation for why block rotations underperform full-vector rotations and a concrete optimization target (equalizing per-block ℓ₁ mass) that is both interpretable and cheap to calibrate. PeRQ's ability to close the accuracy gap to full-vector rotations at block sizes ≥128 without any inference-time overhead makes it a practical drop-in improvement for existing block-rotation PTQ pipelines. Open questions include deriving a Pareto-optimal block size from first principles, extending the analysis to weight outliers and alternative rotation structures (e.g., Givens matrices), and understanding whether the worst-case mass-concentration target remains the right proxy in MX formats where group-wise scaling already mitigates outliers.
