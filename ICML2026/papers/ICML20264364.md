## Problem

Reinforcement learning with intrinsic rewards (RLIR) offers a scalable, label-free alternative to RLVR for improving LLM reasoning, yet consistently underperforms and trains unstably. The paper traces this gap to a systemic reward bias: confidence-coupled self-rewarding creates a self-confirming loop in which high-confidence mistakes receive disproportionately large positive rewards, accumulating over training and capping the performance ceiling. They formalize this with three diagnostic metrics—reward noise magnitude, policy-reward coupling, and over-/under-reward skew—and show through controlled decoupling experiments that these factors compound to destabilize optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled text queries (e.g., math problems) and an ensemble of K source policies initialized from the same backbone; each policy generates G_k rollouts per query under a fixed total rollout budget (G_total = K × G_k, e.g., 2×8=16). No external labels, verifiers, or human annotations are required during training—rewards are derived entirely from the ensemble's collective answer distributions and per-rollout token-level confidences. The final output is a single merged deployable policy (via TIES-Merging) that produces step-by-step reasoning with a boxed final answer, incurring no additional inference cost relative to a single model.

## Method

RLER (Reinforcement Learning with Ensembled Rewards) breaks the self-confirming loop through three mechanisms operating in a unified ensemble reward space. (1) Ensemble-based Unified Rewarding pools rollouts from K diverse policies and aggregates their answer distributions, reducing policy-specific noise and decoupling rewards from any single policy's confidence. (2) Adaptive Soft-reward Interpolation blends a hard binary majority reward with a soft probability reward using a query-dependent gate α(x), computed from token-level average confidence with batch-wise min-max normalization and calibrated mass re-weighting, so that high-confidence queries lean on hard rewards while uncertain queries retain soft credit. (3) Disagreement-Aware Rollout Selection allocates per-answer update budgets proportional to ensemble confidence for the majority label and inversely proportional to tail-label probability for minority answers, aggressively filtering high-confidence false positives when the ensemble is uncertain. After training, TIES-Merging consolidates the K sub-policies into one deployable model with default hyperparameters (κ=0.7, α=0.5).

## Results

On DAPO-MATH-17K with QWEN2.5-MATH-7B, RLER achieves an average of 37.5 Avg@8 across six math benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, AMC24, MATH500, HMMT24) and 52.8 Pass@8, recovering approximately 96.0% of the RLVR upper bound (38.9 Avg@8). This represents a +6.2% improvement over the strongest RLIR baseline (Co-rewarding, 35.3 Avg@8) and a +45.9% gain over the pretrained model (25.7 Avg@8). RLER also substantially reduces the three diagnostic bias metrics (ρ_noise, ρ_selfbias, ρ_symbias) throughout training, yielding smoother accuracy curves and stable scaling from 8k to 1024k unlabeled samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLER outperforms all five RLIR baselines on Avg@8: it beats Co-rewarding (35.3) by +2.2, INTUITOR (33.7) by +3.8, Self-Consistency (33.0) by +4.5, Freq (27.1) by +10.4, and LLM-as-a-Judge (13.4) by +24.1. The largest per-benchmark gains appear on AMC23 (+6.9 over Co-rewarding) and AMC24 (+4.4 over Co-rewarding), while on AIME24 RLER ties Co-rewarding at 23.3. On Pass@8, RLER (52.8) also leads all RLIR baselines, exceeding Co-rewarding (51.2) and INTUITOR (48.3). Relative to the RLVR upper bound (38.9 Avg@8, 55.5 Pass@8), RLER remains within 1.4 Avg@8 and 2.7 Pass@8, confirming it recovers most of the verifiable-reward performance without any labeled data. Cross-model and cross-dataset experiments (LLaMA-3.2-3B on arithmetic, LLaMA-3.1-8B on BIGMATH, Qwen2.5-7B on WebInstruct-verified/MMLU-PRO) show the same pattern: RLER consistently leads RLIR baselines and stays close to RLVR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-rewarding RL (RLIR) line of work—encompassing agreement-based methods (Self-Consistency, Frequency-based), confidence-based methods, and LLM-as-a-judge approaches—by diagnosing a shared failure mode: confidence-coupled reward estimation creates a self-confirming loop that drifts toward over-reward. Rather than proposing yet another single-policy reward heuristic, RLER departs from the single-policy paradigm by constructing a unified reward space from an ensemble of diverse sub-policies, combining adaptive soft/hard reward interpolation with disagreement-aware rollout selection. The final consolidation step leverages TIES-Merging to collapse the ensemble into a single deployable model at no inference cost, building on the model-merging literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-metric diagnostic framework (ρ_noise, ρ_selfbias, ρ_symbias) provides a reusable lens for analyzing reward quality in any self-supervised RL setting, and the controlled decoupling experiments cleanly isolate which failure mode dominates. The finding that FP-dominated over-reward is substantially more damaging than FN-dominated under-reward (50% FP correction → 83.1% vs. 100% FN correction → 74.8%) gives practitioners a concrete target for reward design. Limitations include reliance on a verifiable answer space (math-style or verifier-scored tasks), modest ensemble size (k=2) constrained by VRAM, and evaluation primarily on competition math benchmarks; whether the diagnostic framework and ensemble approach transfer to open-ended, non-verifiable reasoning remains an open question.
