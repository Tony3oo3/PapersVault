## Problem

Bayesian Last Layers (BLLs) provide computationally efficient uncertainty estimation but systematically underestimate epistemic uncertainty because they apply Bayesian treatment only to the final layer, ignoring variability induced by earlier layers. Full NTK-GP inference captures this earlier-layer uncertainty but requires inverting an N×N or p×p matrix, making it infeasible for large networks. The paper seeks to recover NTK-level uncertainty at the computational cost of a standard BLL.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained neural network (using μP parameterization) and a test point x, producing a predictive mean f_θ̂(x) and a predictive variance computed entirely in the r-dimensional last-layer feature space. The key intermediate object is a lower-triangular Cholesky factor L ∈ R^{r×r} computed once from training data (or a subsample of size k), after which per-point inference requires only an r×r matrix inversion. In the subsampled variant, the population Gram matrix Φ_L^T Φ_L is approximated from k uniformly drawn training points, reducing posterior inference cost from O(Nr²) to O(kr²).

## Method

The method decomposes the empirical NTK feature map into last-layer (NNGP) features φ^r(x) and non-last-layer features φ^m(x), then estimates a least-squares projection A that approximates φ^m(x) ≈ Aφ^r(x) on training data. Stacking A with the identity gives B = [A; I_r], and the Cholesky factor L of B^T B = A^T A + I_r defines transformed features φ^L(x) = L^T φ^r(x) in which standard Bayesian linear regression yields a closed-form posterior covariance. A key theoretical result proves that the resulting predictive variance is always greater than or equal to that of a standard BLL (since B^T B ⪰ I_r), correcting the underestimation. A uniform subsampling scheme replaces the full N-point Gram matrix with a k-point estimate, and matrix concentration bounds show the approximation error scales as O(1/√k) and does not grow with N.

## Results

On UCI regression (Table 2), Rich-BLL achieves the best or near-best NLL across all five datasets, e.g. 2.61 on Boston Housing, 3.10 on Concrete, and 0.75 on Energy, consistently outperforming the standard Bayesian Last Layer (NNGP/BLL) by 0.17–0.29 NLL. The subsampled variant Rich-BLL(S) degrades by less than 0.02 NLL in every case. On the Wheel Bandit benchmark, Rich-BLL reduces cumulative regret from 55.80 (NNGP) to 21.80 at the hardest difficulty (δ=0.99), matching VBLL. On CIFAR-10, Rich-BLL(S) improves ECE from 0.046 to 0.029 and OOD AUROC (SVHN) from 0.88 to 0.91 over NNGP/BLL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rich-BLL consistently outperforms the standard Bayesian Last Layer (NNGP/BLL) on all five UCI regression datasets, with NLL improvements of 0.17 (Boston) to 0.29 (Concrete), confirming that incorporating earlier-layer NTK contributions corrects the underestimation of epistemic uncertainty. Against VBLL, Rich-BLL wins on Boston (2.61 vs 2.75), Concrete (3.10 vs 3.19), and Wine (1.00 vs 1.05), while tying on Energy and Power. Rich-BLL also beats MC Dropout, SWAG, and BBB on most datasets, though BBB edges it on Boston (2.54) and Concrete (2.99), and the RBF GP wins on Energy (0.66) and Wine (0.97). The Ensemble remains the strongest single baseline on Boston (2.48) and Wine (0.98), but Rich-BLL matches or beats it on Concrete, Energy, and Power at a fraction of the computational cost. On the Wheel Bandit, Rich-BLL reduces regret from 55.80 to 21.80 at δ=0.99 relative to NNGP/BLL, matching VBLL (24.44) and substantially outperforming NeuralLinear (52.64) and LinDiagPost (37.77). On CIFAR-10, Rich-BLL(S) improves ECE (0.029 vs 0.046) and OOD AUROC on both SVHN (0.91 vs 0.88) and CIFAR-100 (0.65 vs 0.62) over NNGP/BLL, while remaining competitive with Dropout and Ensemble.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Bayesian Last Layers (BLLs) and Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) Gaussian Process inference. Standard BLLs place a Bayesian linear model on the last-layer features (equivalent to NNGP), while NTK-GP captures full parameter-induced uncertainty but requires inverting an N×N or p×p matrix. The key departure is a low-dimensional projection: the authors show that the non-last-layer eNTK features can be approximated by a linear map of the last-layer features, reducing NTK-GP inference to a modified Bayesian linear regression in the same r-dimensional space as a standard BLL. This connects to the broader line of scalable Laplace/NTK methods (Kristiadi et al., Daxberger et al., He et al.) but avoids their dependence on full curvature or Jacobian matrices, and to VBLL (Harrison et al.) by providing a closed-form, sampling-free alternative with a provable variance-dominance guarantee over BLL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical drop-in upgrade for any pipeline already using BLLs: it requires only a one-time computation of an r×r Cholesky factor and then performs standard Bayesian linear regression, making it attractive for large-scale deployment where full NTK-GP or ensemble methods are infeasible. The provable guarantee that predictive variance is always at least as large as BLL's addresses a well-known calibration failure mode. Open questions include how the approximation quality degrades for very deep or very wide networks where the eNTK may not be well-approximated by the last-layer span, whether the method extends naturally to non-linear output heads or multi-task settings, and how it interacts with modern architectures (transformers, diffusion models) where the NTK structure may differ substantially from the MLP/CNN settings studied here.
