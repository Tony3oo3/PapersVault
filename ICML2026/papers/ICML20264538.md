## Problem

While MLLMs frequently produce captioning errors, no automated method exists to detect *systematic* misalignments — recurring textual errors that are consistently associated with a specific visual feature across a dataset. Prior work addressed per-sample (local) misalignment scoring or systematic errors in classification settings, but the global, dataset-level detection of spurious text-visual associations in free-form captions remained unexplored, with no benchmark to evaluate such methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264538/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an entire vision-language dataset of thousands of image-caption pairs (e.g., chest X-rays with MLLM-generated radiology reports, or natural images with generated captions), optionally augmented with ground-truth reference captions in the reference-based variant. Rather than scoring individual samples, it produces a single dataset-level prediction: a pair of natural-language strings (t̂, v̂) identifying the recurring erroneous textual fact and the visual feature it is systematically associated with. The method is extended to multiple simultaneous misalignments by considering the top-k ranked clusters in each stage.

## Method

SYMBAL decomposes the joint detection problem into two sequential stages, each comprising three subtasks (grouping, scoring, summarizing) built entirely from off-the-shelf foundation models in a zero-shot manner. Stage 1 splits all captions into sentence-level facts, encodes them with a text embedding model, clusters via spherical K-Means (with cluster count auto-selected by Silhouette distance), scores each cluster by mean image-text alignment (using an embedding, text-only, or vision-language scorer), and summarizes the highest-misalignment cluster into a single erroneous concept via an LLM. Stage 2 then restricts to images paired with that textual fact, clusters them with an image embedding model, scores clusters by misalignment with the identified fact, and summarizes the top-ranked image cluster into a visual feature using either a text-only or vision-language summarizer. The key insight is that this structured, dual-stage decomposition substantially outperforms single-stage direct prompting of large LLMs, achieving 63.8% accuracy versus 17.1% for the best baseline on SYMBALBENCH.

## Results

On SYMBALBENCH (420 datasets, 1.7M image-text pairs), SYMBAL achieves 63.8% end-to-end accuracy (Acc@5, reference-free), correctly identifying both the erroneous textual fact and associated visual feature. Stage 1 (textual fact detection) reaches 94.2% Acc@5 on natural images and 75.0% on medical images (reference-free), while Stage 2 (visual feature detection) reaches 69.7% and 36.7% respectively. In real-world evaluations, SYMBAL validates systematic misalignments in captions from four MLLMs (e.g., handbag errors 3.1× more likely when a bus is present) and in the ShareGPT4V dataset (white tablecloth errors 17.2× more likely when a table is present).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SYMBAL against three direct-prompting baselines (Llama3.3-70B, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, GPT-OSS-120B) that are given a sampled subset of each dataset as a text prompt and asked to output the erroneous textual fact and associated visual feature in one step. SYMBAL wins decisively across all settings: it achieves 63.8% end-to-end accuracy (reference-free, Acc@5) versus 17.1% for the strongest baseline (GPT-OSS-120B), a nearly 4× improvement. Llama3.3-70B and Qwen2.5-VL-72B generate 'few correct predictions,' performing even worse than GPT-OSS. The structured dual-stage decomposition (cluster → score → summarize, applied separately to text and images) is the key differentiator, as single-stage prompting cannot effectively reason over thousands of image-text pairs within a single context window. The advantage is most pronounced on challenging subsets with weak association strength (low Cramer's V) and small visual features, where baselines struggle most.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the global error detection literature (DOMINO, Distilling Model Failures, No Subclass Left Behind) from the classification setting—where inputs are discrete labels—to the image captioning setting, where inputs are free-form MLLM-generated text. It also draws on local misalignment detection metrics (CLIPScore, CHAIR, ALOHa, GREEN, FLEUR) for per-sample alignment scoring, but shifts the focus from individual image-caption pairs to dataset-level, recurring error patterns. The structured dual-stage decomposition (first detect the erroneous textual fact via text clustering, then detect the associated visual feature via image clustering) is a methodological departure from prior single-stage approaches, and the SYMBALBENCH benchmark is the first to provide ground-truth systematic misalignment annotations at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MLLM-generated captions proliferate in large-scale pretraining datasets (e.g., ShareGPT4V), the ability to audit these datasets for systematic errors without access to the underlying model becomes critical, particularly in safety-critical domains like medicine. The 63.8% end-to-end accuracy, while a strong improvement over baselines, leaves substantial room for improvement—especially in the medical domain where visual feature detection drops to 36.7% in reference-free settings. Open questions include whether the injected-error benchmark fully captures the complexity of real-world MLLM failures, how the method scales to datasets with multiple simultaneous misalignments, and whether the clustering-based approach can handle more nuanced or compositional errors beyond single-object associations.
