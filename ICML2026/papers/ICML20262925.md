## Problem

Most 3D diffusion methods denoise high-cardinality representations (thousands of point coordinates, millions of voxels, or mesh vertices), making compute and memory scale sharply with resolution and limiting controllability. This paper asks whether diffusion-based 3D generation can be scaled while retaining an explicit, compact, and interpretable geometric representation rather than dense fields or opaque latents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262925/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 128×15 Gaussian noise tensor (~7 KB in float32) as input and denoises it into superquadric primitive parameters: per-primitive translation (R³), 6D rotation (R⁶), axis lengths (R³), shape exponents (R²), and an existence score (R¹). The output is decoded into point clouds at arbitrary resolution via per-primitive surface sampling, overlap filtering, and farthest-point sampling, enabling resolution-free decoding without re-running diffusion. For multi-class generation, a category index is encoded via a 3-layer MLP and concatenated with the time embedding; for constrained design, user-specified geometric constraints are projected onto token entries at each denoising step.

## Method

DoSs performs DDPM denoising directly over a fixed-size set of superquadric primitives (K_max=128 slots × 15 dimensions) using a 1D convolutional U-Net backbone, reducing the diffusion state from ~24–384 KB (points/voxels) to ~7 KB. Three design choices make superquadrics diffusable: (i) an existence score per slot handles variable primitive counts by learning which slots are active; (ii) rotations are diffused in a continuous 6D representation (Zhou et al., 2019) decoded to SO(3) via Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization, avoiding Euler-angle discontinuities; (iii) a deterministic canonical ordering (by decreasing volume or increasing y-position) resolves permutation ambiguity so the U-Net sees a consistent token sequence. Training uses standard DDPM noise-prediction loss on superquadric decompositions extracted via Marching-Primitives, completing in ~2 hours per category on a single RTX 4090. The explicit primitive parameters further enable structure-aware editing (part deletion, scaling, reposing, reshaping) and geometric-aware design via per-step constraint projection during ancestral sampling.

## Results

On ShapeNet (CHAIR, AIRPLANE, CAR), DoSs denoises a compact 128×15 superquadric token state (~7 KB) in 0.6 s per shape, compared to 24–4096 KB and 9.7–29.9 s for baselines. DoSs achieves the best 1-NNA on AIRPLANE (61.92, vs. DiT-3D's 62.35) and competitive COV on CHAIR (51.31, second only to DiT-3D's 52.45). On CHAIR and CAR, DiT-3D retains a 1-NNA advantage (49.11 and 48.24 vs. 53.80 and 57.50), reflecting the fidelity benefit of voxel-space denoising, while DoSs narrows the gap substantially over point-space baselines (DPM, PVD, TIGER).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoSs outperforms all point-space baselines (DPM, PVD, TIGER) on 1-NNA across all three categories, with the largest margins on AIRPLANE (61.92 vs. 71.85–76.42) and CAR (57.50 vs. 54.31–68.89). Against the strongest dense-state baseline DiT-3D, DoSs wins 1-NNA on AIRPLANE (61.92 vs. 62.35) but trails on CHAIR (53.80 vs. 49.11) and CAR (57.50 vs. 48.24), while its COV on CHAIR (51.31) is close to DiT-3D's (52.45) but lags on AIRPLANE (46.77 vs. 53.16) and CAR (49.26 vs. 50.00). Compared to the latent-space method LION, DoSs achieves lower 1-NNA on AIRPLANE (61.92 vs. 67.41) and CAR (57.50 vs. 53.41) but higher COV on CHAIR (51.31 vs. 48.94). The most striking difference is efficiency: DoSs uses a 7 KB diffusion state and 0.6 s inference, versus 24 KB / 9.7–29.9 s for point methods, 384 KB / 12 s for DiT-3D, and up to 4096 KB for MeshDiffusion. The authors explicitly frame the comparison as isolating the efficiency–controllability–quality trade-off rather than claiming SOTA visual fidelity, noting that DiT-3D's voxel-space denoising still provides the closest match to point-level sampling statistics under CD-based metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in 3D diffusion (DiT-3D, LION, TIGER, DPM) that denoises high-cardinality representations—point clouds, voxel grids, or mesh tokens—by instead performing diffusion over a compact set of superquadric primitive parameters (pose, size, shape, existence). It builds on the DDPM framework and the 6D continuous rotation representation of Zhou et al. (2019), and leverages Marching-Primitives for extracting superquadric decompositions from ShapeNet meshes. While prior superquadric work (SuperDec, ISCO, Differentiable Blocks World) uses primitives for reconstruction or decomposition, DoSs is the first to use them as the native diffusion state for category-level generative modeling, bridging the gap between explicit geometric primitives and learned generative priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DoSs demonstrates that a 7KB diffusion state (vs. 24–384KB for point/voxel baselines) can yield competitive distributional quality on ShapeNet while enabling capabilities that are difficult in dense spaces: resolution-free point-cloud decoding, direct part-level editing, and constraint-based geometric design during sampling. The key limitation is a bias toward piecewise-smooth, low-frequency geometry—thin structures, sharp edges, and topology-heavy details are under-represented—so the method trades fine surface fidelity for structural coherence and controllability. Open questions include whether hybrid pipelines (coarse superquadric structure + local implicit/mesh refinement) can recover the lost detail, whether the approach scales to diverse datasets like Objaverse, and how to establish unified evaluation metrics that are stable across decoding pipelines and aligned with perceptual quality.
