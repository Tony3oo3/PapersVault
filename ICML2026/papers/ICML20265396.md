## Problem

Adapting pre-trained Vision-Language Models in federated learning faces a fundamental trade-off: prompt-tuning and adapter-based methods require expensive backpropagation through deep backbones, while cache-based methods avoid this cost but fail to aggregate knowledge effectively under data heterogeneity, since naive averaging of prototypes from disjoint feature spaces produces incoherent global representations. The paper asks how to achieve both lightweight client-side personalization and robust global knowledge aggregation without deep gradient computation.

## Input / Output

The system takes RGB images as input, encoded by a frozen CLIP image encoder (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16) into L2-normalized d-dimensional visual features. Classification scores are produced by similarity matching against two sets of prototype vectors: client-specific visual prototypes (N×d) and server-maintained global semantic prototypes (N×d), combined via a residual fusion with a temperature-scaled exponential kernel. The federated setup involves K=10 clients with non-IID data (Dirichlet ρ=0.1), operating in a few-shot regime (1–16 shots per class) across 10 image classification benchmarks.

## Method

FedSPA employs an alternating optimization strategy that decouples personalization and generalization at the embedding level. On the client side, only a lightweight N×d visual prototype matrix is updated via gradient descent while the VLM backbone and global semantic prototypes remain frozen, eliminating deep backpropagation. On the server side, global semantic prototypes (initialized from text-encoded class names) are refined using a contrastive InfoNCE loss that pulls each semantic prototype toward same-class visual prototypes from all clients while pushing it away from other classes, augmented by a cosine-similarity regularizer to prevent semantic drift from the pre-trained text space. The key paradigm shift is from 'learning-to-describe' (optimizing static text prompts) to 'learning-to-align' (dynamically aligning global semantics with heterogeneous visual distributions), yielding a 44× speedup over FedAvg while outperforming all baselines in both generalization and personalization.

## Results

Under extreme non-IID (ρ=0.1, 8-shot), FedSPA-G achieves mean generalization accuracy of 69.59% (ResNet-50) and 75.89% (ViT-B/16), outperforming the best cache-based baselines FedPGA (63.96%/70.33%) and CacheFL (62.15%/68.59%). For personalization, FedSPA-P reaches 79.89% (ResNet-50) and 84.91% (ViT-B/16), surpassing the strongest prompt-tuning baseline pFedMoAP (76.70%/82.56%) and the local-only Tip-Adapter (73.29%/78.19%). FedSPA also achieves a 44.4× speedup over FedAvg in local execution time (1.35s vs. 59.98s) while maintaining superior accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSPA-G dominates the generalization track: it outperforms FedPGA by +5.63% (ResNet) and +5.56% (ViT), and CacheFL by +7.44% and +7.30% respectively, with the largest gains on fine-grained datasets like Flowers102 (+20.02% over FedPGA on ResNet). In personalization, FedSPA-P beats the closest competitor pFedMoAP by +3.19% (ResNet) and +2.35% (ViT), and surpasses the local-only Tip-Adapter by +6.60% and +6.72%, confirming that global semantic alignment provides meaningful transfer benefit beyond purely local adaptation. Against prompt-tuning baselines, FedSPA-P leads PromptFL by +16.59% (ResNet) and +12.67% (ViT), and ECALP by +19.88% and +20.64%. The adapter-based pFedMMA (ViT only) trails FedSPA-P by 8.74% in mean accuracy. Notably, FedSPA-P also outperforms the zero-shot CLIP baseline by +21.13% (ResNet) and +19.80% (ViT) in personalization, while FedSPA-G exceeds zero-shot CLIP by +10.83% and +10.78% in generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedSPA carves new ground at the intersection of federated VLM adaptation and prototype-based classification. It departs from the three dominant paradigms in federated VLM fine-tuning—prompt tuning (PromptFL, pFedMoAP), adapter injection (pFedMMA, FedCLIP), and cache-based retrieval (CacheFL, FedPGA)—by introducing a cross-modal representation-space decoupling: clients optimize lightweight visual prototypes while the server aligns global semantic prototypes via a contrastive InfoNCE objective. This shifts the server-side paradigm from 'learning-to-describe' (optimizing static text prompts) to 'learning-to-align' (dynamically matching semantic prototypes to heterogeneous visual distributions), drawing on prototype classifiers (Tip-Adapter) and contrastive representation learning (InfoNCE) but repurposing them within a federated alternating-optimization loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 44.4× client-side speedup over FedAvg while simultaneously improving accuracy makes FedSPA practically relevant for resource-constrained edge deployment of VLMs in federated settings. A notable finding is that naive FedAvg aggregation of visual prototypes actually degrades personalization below the Local-Only baseline (64.55% vs. 72.82%), revealing that unguided averaging introduces negative transfer under extreme heterogeneity—a cautionary result for the broader FL community. Open questions include scalability to ultra-large class spaces (N×K server cost), applicability to domains with anonymized labels where text-encoder initialization is unavailable, and whether the alignment strategy extends to multi-modal or sequential tasks beyond image classification.
