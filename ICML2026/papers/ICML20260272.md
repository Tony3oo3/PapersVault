## Problem

Deploying LLMs in high-stakes domains requires reliable, inexpensive methods to assess the trustworthiness of long-form responses to open-ended prompts, yet existing approaches rely on claim-by-claim fact-checking or NLI-based similarity comparisons that are computationally expensive, brittle, and scale poorly with response length. The paper addresses the absence of a lightweight, model-agnostic, data-agnostic signal that can flag potentially untrustworthy long-form generations without resorting to slow per-claim verification.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and N independently sampled long-form responses (typically 6–20, ~500 words each) from any generative LLM, and produces a single scalar semantic isotropy score in [0, 1] where lower values indicate higher factual consistency across samples. The score is computed using any off-the-shelf text embedding model (open- or closed-weight) with no fine-tuning, labels, or hyperparameter tuning required. The cosine kernel construction is fully parallelizable, making the computation negligible (~1.8 s for N=20 on a V100) compared to NLI-based baselines (~302 s for LUQ-Atomic).

## Method

The core mechanism samples N responses to a prompt, embeds each with a text encoder, normalizes the embeddings to the unit sphere, computes the N×N cosine kernel matrix K_E^cos, and estimates its von Neumann entropy (vNE) normalized by log N to yield the isotropy score I ∈ [0,1]. The key insight is that factually grounded prompts produce tightly clustered embeddings (low vNE, low isotropy), while hallucinated responses diverge semantically (high vNE, high isotropy), so the score serves as a direct proxy for nonfactuality. This replaces the NLI entailment kernel used in Kernel Language Entropy with a simple cosine kernel on text embeddings, eliminating the NLI bottleneck and enabling full vectorization at scale. The paper also introduces Segment-Score, a factuality scoring protocol that segments a response into atomic statements and verifies each against a reference document in a single oracle LLM call, reducing the ~100 atomic facts per response in FactScore to ~28 segments and enabling efficient ground-truth dataset construction for ~65,450 scored responses across three generator models.

## Results

The primary metric is R² (explained variance) of a linear model regressing factuality on the uncertainty score. Semantic isotropy achieves R² = 0.50 on FactScore-Bio (Llama 3.1, Cohere v4.0 embeddings), 0.43 on TriviaQA (Phi 3.5 Mini, Gemini embeddings), and 0.43 on CMU Book Summaries (Phi 3.5 Mini, Gemini embeddings), outperforming the strongest baseline (LUQ-Atomic, R² = 0.38 on FS-BIO) by a wide margin. At the operating point (AUC), the score reaches ~0.95 on Book Summaries and ~0.90 on FS-BIO. Computationally, semantic isotropy requires only ~1.8 s per batch of 20 responses (~500 words each) on a V100, approximately 170× faster than LUQ-Atomic (~302 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Semantic isotropy outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and generator model tested. The closest competitor is LUQ-Atomic (Zhang et al., 2024), which reaches R² = 0.38 on FS-Bio (Llama 3.1) and 0.33 on Book Summaries (Phi 3.5); semantic isotropy exceeds it by 0.06–0.12 R² points in these settings. Semantic Entropy (Farquhar et al., 2024) and SelfCheckNLI/U.NLI (Manakul et al., 2023) are substantially weaker, peaking at R² ≈ 0.16 and 0.17 respectively, and are outperformed by 0.25–0.35 R² points. EigenScore (Chen et al., 2024), which relies on internal-state activations, is the weakest baseline (R² ≤ 0.07) and is unavailable for closed-weight models like GPT 4.1 Mini. Perplexity (Ren et al., 2023) and LN-Entropy (Malinin & Gales, 2021) perform comparably to each other (R² ≈ 0.09–0.26) but are consistently dominated. U.Eign and U.Deg (Lin et al., 2024) reach R² ≈ 0.26–0.28 on FS-Bio, still trailing the best semantic isotropy configurations by ~0.22 R². Notably, even the weakest embedding model tested (Qwen3 0.6B) yields R² = 0.47 on FS-Bio, exceeding LUQ's best result, underscoring the method's robustness to embedding quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Kernel Language Entropy (KLE; Nikitin et al., NeurIPS 2024), which computes von Neumann entropy over a kernel matrix built from pairwise NLI entailment scores, by replacing the NLI kernel with a simple cosine kernel over off-the-shelf text embeddings—removing the NLI bottleneck and enabling full vectorization. The companion Segment-Score protocol is a streamlined variant of FactScore (Min et al., EMNLP 2023) that segments responses into atomic statements and verifies them in a single oracle call rather than extracting and scoring dozens of atomic facts per response. The method also departs from internal-state approaches (EigenScore, semantic entropy probes) by requiring no white-box access to the generating model, making it applicable to closed-weight APIs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~170× speedup over LUQ-Atomic and the absence of any training, labeling, or hyperparameter tuning make semantic isotropy a practical first-pass filter that can route high-risk prompts to expensive claim-level verification while passing low-risk ones through, addressing a real deployment bottleneck for long-form LLM outputs. Key limitations remain: the method requires repeated sampling (though only ~6–8 responses suffice), operates at the prompt level without localizing individual errors, and degrades for generators whose hallucinated outputs remain stylistically uniform (e.g., GPT 4.1 Mini on TriviaQA) or for intrinsic hallucinations that are self-consistent across samples. Open questions include whether probe-based single-pass estimators can approximate the isotropy signal without repeated sampling, and whether embedding models explicitly trained to ignore stylistic variation could close the gap on uniform generators.
