## Problem

Standard conditional flow matching (CFM), when adapted to inverse design by pairing labels with design coordinates, exhibits strong sensitivity to the arbitrary ordering and scaling of those coordinates, leading to unstable training and degraded round-trip accuracy. Additionally, generative inverse-design models produce a design for any requested performance specification—including physically infeasible ones—without a built-in mechanism to flag unreliable outputs, leaving practitioners unable to distinguish trustworthy predictions from spurious ones.

## Input / Output

For synthesis (inverse direction), the model takes a target performance vector y* ∈ R^L, a P-dimensional latent noise vector z ~ p_B, and optionally physical conditioning parameters (e.g., angle of attack and Mach number for Unifoil), and outputs a P-dimensional design vector x. For analysis (forward direction), it takes a design x and outputs predicted performance ŷ ∈ R^L. The augmented state space is (P+L)-dimensional: synthesis starts from [z; y*] and reads the first P coordinates at t=1, while analysis starts from [x; 0_L] and reads the last L coordinates at t=0. Two scalar uncertainty scores—Zero-Deviation (squared norm of the label portion at the synthesis endpoint, computed as a free byproduct) and Self-Consistency (round-trip label reconstruction error, requiring one extra ODE integration)—are produced alongside each generated design.

## Method

Diag-CFM replaces the standard CFM pairing [z; y] → x with a zero-anchored pairing [z; y] → [x; 0_L], so that every design coordinate is regressed against independent noise and every label coordinate is regressed against zero. This makes the target velocity u^diag = [x − z; −y] provably equivariant under arbitrary permutations of design and label coordinates (Proposition 4.1), eliminating the ordering sensitivity that plagues standard CFM where the last L coordinates subtract labels from design components. The model is a time-dependent vector field v_θ (MLP for vector-valued designs, U-Net-style CNN with FiLM conditioning and bottleneck self-attention for FashionMNIST images) trained by minimizing the Diag-CFM loss (Eq. 5) over linear interpolation paths. Bidirectionality is obtained by integrating the ODE forward (synthesis) or backward (analysis) with 30-step Euler. The zero-anchoring structure directly yields two architecture-specific UQ metrics: Zero-Deviation probes whether the flow successfully maps the request onto the data manifold (last L components near zero), and Self-Consistency probes round-trip coherence by replacing approximate zeros with exact zeros before the backward pass.

## Results

Diag-CFM achieves the lowest round-trip error across all five datasets and all tested dimensions (P=6 to P=784). On the DTLZ2 benchmark, it outperforms CFM by 35× at P=12 (9.10×10⁻⁴ vs 3.20×10⁻²) and by 8.6× at P=100 (5.26×10⁻³ vs 4.52×10⁻²), while beating INN by 42× and 18× respectively. On FashionMNIST (P=784), Diag-CFM reduces forward MSE by 36× over CFM and round-trip error by 3.0× over INN. For uncertainty quantification, the architecture-specific Zero-Deviation metric achieves the best OOD detection AUC (0.96 on Unifoil, 0.77 on Gas Turbine) and 8–27% error reduction at 20% rejection, computed as a free byproduct of generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard CFM, Diag-CFM wins decisively on round-trip error across every dataset: 12× on Gas Turbine, 6.5× on Unifoil, 35–42× on DTLZ2 (P=12), 8.6× on DTLZ2 (P=100), 1.3× on Thin Film, and 1.5× on FashionMNIST. The forward MSE gap is even larger on FashionMNIST (36×) and Gas Turbine (12×), while CFM is consistently the weakest baseline overall. Against the coupling-based INN baseline (Ardizzone et al., 2019), Diag-CFM wins on round-trip error in all settings, with the gap widening at high dimensions (INN degrades to ~10⁻¹ at DTLZ P=100 vs Diag-CFM's 5.26×10⁻³); however, INN achieves slightly lower forward MSE on DTLZ at P≥50, which the authors attribute to dataset-specific structure. For uncertainty quantification, the proposed Zero-Deviation and Self-Consistency metrics outperform both Ensemble Variance (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) and FM Loss baselines on all three UQ tasks (select-best, error-rejection, OOD detection), with Zero-Deviation achieving AUC 0.96 on Unifoil OOD detection versus 0.85 for Ensemble Variance, while requiring no additional model evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Conditional Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023) for generative modeling and the bidirectional invertible paradigm of Ardizzone et al. (2019) for inverse design, but identifies a critical instability in standard CFM when adapted to inverse problems: the loss is sensitive to the arbitrary ordering of design and label coordinates. The key departure is the zero-anchoring strategy (Diag-CFM), which re-pairs all design coordinates with latent noise and anchors labels to zero, yielding a provably permutation-invariant learning problem. For uncertainty quantification, the paper departs from general-purpose methods (ensembles, score-based OOD detection) by exploiting the zero-anchoring architecture itself to derive two single-model metrics—Zero-Deviation and Self-Consistency—that require no additional training or forward passes beyond generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-anchoring modification is a simple, drop-in change to the CFM loss that removes a hidden hyperparameter (coordinate ordering) and delivers order-of-magnitude round-trip error reductions across five diverse benchmarks up to P=784, making it immediately adoptable by practitioners using flow matching for inverse design. The architecture-specific UQ metrics enable practical deployment capabilities—abstention, candidate selection, and OOD detection—without the computational overhead of ensembles, which is critical when downstream decisions (manufacturing, deployment) are costly. Open questions include closing the 34–57% gap to oracle in error-rejection, understanding why INN remains competitive on forward prediction at high DTLZ dimensions, and extending the approach to even larger parameter spaces such as topology optimization or molecular discovery.
