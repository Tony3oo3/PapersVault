## Problem

Layer pruning of LLMs exhibits a nonlinear 'cliff-edge' effect where performance remains stable until a threshold, then collapses abruptly. Existing representation-based analyses (e.g., CKA) cannot explain this mechanism because hidden states retain high semantic alignment with the dense model even at 50% pruning where task accuracy has fully collapsed. The paper asks: if deep hidden representations are preserved, what structural disruption actually drives the sudden loss of decision-making capability?

## Input / Output

The framework takes a dense transformer LLM (Llama3-8B, Llama2-7B, or Qwen3-4B) and multiple-choice task samples (ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, etc.) as input, using a calibration set of 256–500 instances for Block Influence scoring and CKA alignment. It produces a pruned model of reduced depth along with layer-wise Decision Margin curves (logit gaps between correct and top-distractor options), Option Frequency distributions, the Transition Point index, and the full ordered list of removed layer IDs. A key implementation detail is that layer-wise logits are computed via a logit-lens projection through the LM head after applying the model's final pre-head normalization (RMSNorm/LayerNorm) to intermediate hidden states, ensuring cross-layer comparability.

## Method

The paper introduces a decision-centric analytical framework built on two metrics: Decision Margin (DM), which measures the average logit gap between the ground-truth option and the strongest distractor at each layer, and Option Frequency (OF), which tracks the argmax distribution over candidate options. DM reveals a sharp phase transition at a specific depth (the Transition Point) that partitions the network into a Silent Phase (sustained negative DM, dominated by a single biased option) and a Decisive Phase (positive DM, balanced option distribution). To probe how pruning disrupts this structure, the authors propose Iterative Pruning (IP), a greedy layer-by-layer removal strategy that recomputes Block Influence scores at each step and includes a SKIP-Prun restart mechanism to bypass premature local optima. The central empirical finding is that pruning confined to the Decisive Phase preserves the transition and performance, while encroaching on the Silent Phase pushes the Transition Point beyond the terminal layer, leaving DM permanently negative and causing irreversible collapse. A simple Transition-Aware Pruning (TAP) variant that protects a narrow band around the Transition Point demonstrates that this structural insight yields actionable pruning constraints.

## Results

The paper identifies a sharp Decision Margin (DM) transition at Layer 18 (Llama3-8B), Layer 19 (Llama2-7B), and Layer 30 (Qwen3-4B), partitioning each model into a Silent Phase (DM < 0) and a Decisive Phase (DM > 0). A strong negative correlation (r = −0.96, p < 0.001) between the transition layer index and the critical pruning threshold confirms that later transitions yield lower pruning robustness. At 50% pruning, ARC-Challenge accuracy collapses from ~0.80 to ~0.28 on Llama3-8B, while CKA analysis shows hidden representations remain highly aligned with the dense model, indicating collapse is driven by loss of decision scaffolding rather than feature degradation. Transition-Aware Pruning (TAP), which protects a 3-layer band around the transition, improves ARC-C accuracy at 40% pruning from 0.28 to 0.41.

## Baselines & Comparisons

After SFT, IT-Prun substantially outperforms all three baselines across every pruning ratio and task: at 12.5% pruning it achieves 83.76% average accuracy versus ShortGPT (64.36%), SLEB (60.46%), and MKA (59.75%), with the gap widening at higher ratios (84.56% vs. 62.93% for ShortGPT at 31.2%). The advantage is most dramatic on PIQA (88.90% vs. ~50%) and HellaSwag (94.97% vs. 64.88%), where baselines suffer near-random performance while IT-Prun retains near-dense accuracy. In the zero-shot (no-SFT) setting, the comparison is more mixed: ShortGPT actually outperforms IT-Prun at 10% and 20% pruning on Llama3-8B (e.g., 82.25% vs. 75.26% on ARC-C at 10%), but IT-Prun pulls ahead sharply at 30% (75.51% vs. 45.39% on ARC-C) and dominates on Qwen3-4B at all ratios (87.42% vs. 63.02% average at 10%). The authors explicitly position IT-Prun as an analytical probe for tracing decision dynamics rather than claiming universal algorithmic superiority, noting that its incremental single-layer removal preserves decision scaffolding that one-shot methods destroy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant representation-centric explanation of pruning collapse (Gromov et al., 2024), which attributes failure to loss of foundational knowledge or reasoning modules in specific layers. Instead, it repurposes the Logit Lens / Tuned Lens paradigm (nostalgebraist, 2020; Belrose et al., 2023) to track decision formation layer-by-layer, introducing Decision Margin and Option Frequency as decision-level metrics. The Iterative Pruning strategy builds directly on ShortGPT's Block Influence scoring (Men et al., 2025) but reframes it as an analytical probe rather than a compression algorithm, and the CKA-based representation comparison (Kornblith et al., 2019) is used to demonstrate the critical decoupling between preserved hidden features and lost decision capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic account of the long-observed 'cliff-edge' collapse in layer pruning: the model retains its knowledge (hidden representations) but loses the structural depth needed to cross a decision threshold. This reframing has practical implications for compression—protecting the transition-supporting band (TAP) is a simple, interpretable constraint that improves robustness at moderate-to-high pruning ratios. Key limitations include the restriction to multiple-choice tasks where 'decision' is well-defined; the open-ended generation diagnostic (GSM8K) is post-hoc and relies on a constructed answer pool. Open questions include whether the transition point can be shifted or compressed during pre-training, how width-wise sparsity interacts with the decision scaffold, and whether the Silent Phase's fragility generalizes to non-architectural perturbations like quantization.
