## Problem

Fine-tuned medical audio models on small, imbalanced datasets suffer from norm bias, where prediction confidence is driven by feature magnitude rather than semantic angular alignment, producing dangerously overconfident misclassifications. Existing calibration methods (loss-level regularization, post-hoc temperature scaling, sampling-based ensembles) either ignore the geometric root cause or introduce prohibitive inference costs, and existing ETF-based geometric methods fail on noisy medical data because fixed ETFs cannot track drifting prototypes while gradient-based rotation is unstable on small datasets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw audio recordings (respiratory cycles or phonocardiograms) resampled to 16 kHz, segmented into fixed 5-second clips, and amplitude-normalized to [-1, 1]. These are processed by a pre-trained backbone (CNN6, CNN14, AST, BEATs, BYOL-A, or M2D) to yield a d-dimensional feature vector z. At inference, the model outputs a fused probability vector P_final = 0.5·σ(Logits_s) + 0.5·σ(Logits_g) over K diagnostic classes, providing both the predicted label and a calibrated confidence score at essentially zero extra latency (4.2 ms vs. 4.1 ms baseline per batch of 32).

## Method

SPA is a dual-branch architecture that decouples norm elimination from geometric stabilization. The Spherical branch L2-normalizes both the feature z and classifier weights W_k onto the unit hypersphere, redefining logits as a learnable-scaled cosine similarity s·cos(θ_k) so that confidence is strictly bounded by angular alignment; it also maintains momentum-updated class prototypes P_k. The Geometric branch holds a fixed Simplex ETF matrix M as a structural anchor, adapts the raw feature through a learnable linear adapter, and solves the Orthogonal Procrustes problem min_R ‖P − RM‖²_F s.t. RᵀR = I via SVD to obtain the optimal rotation R* = UVᵀ in closed form—bypassing the gradient variance that plagues learnable rotation matrices. A self-alignment mechanism then couples the two branches through a joint loss combining label-smoothing cross-entropy on both branches plus a temperature-scaled KL-divergence term, so the Geometric branch's structural symmetry is distilled back into the Spherical branch. The key design choice is that the Procrustes rotation is recomputed analytically (gradient-free) after each momentum prototype update, making the geometric target stable and preventing the tangential jitter that would otherwise waste optimization capacity.

## Results

On the ICBHI 2017 dataset (4-class respiratory sound), SPA reduces ECE from 28.51% to 4.44% on the BEATs backbone while increasing Average Score (AS) from 63.90% to 64.42%. Integrating SPA with the PAFA backbone achieves a new SOTA AS of 65.27% with ECE of 6.05%. On the CirCor DigiScope dataset (heart murmur detection), SPA with the M2D backbone reaches a SOTA weighted accuracy of 84.23% with a minimal ECE of 4.63%, improving ECE by approximately 50% across all four tested backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPA consistently outperforms all calibration baselines on ECE: it reduces ECE to 4.44% versus 33.91% (Label Smoothing), 8.67% (Mixup), 21.99% (Temperature Scaling), 25.90% (MC Dropout), and 22.29% (Deep Ensembles) on the BEATs backbone, while also achieving the highest AS of 64.42% and matching the baseline inference time (4.2 ms vs. 4.1 ms). Against imbalanced-learning baselines, SPA on CNN6 improves AS from 58.66% (CE) to 59.10% and cuts ECE from 23.53% to 7.51%, outperforming both Focal Loss (AS 57.14%, ECE 17.51%) and LDAM-DRW (AS 56.93%, ECE 17.47%). On the AST backbone, FBS+SPA surpasses standalone FBS in both AS (65.01% vs. 64.28%) and ECE (5.49% vs. 19.82%), and PAFA+SPA beats PAFA on ECE (6.05% vs. 23.36%) while slightly improving AS (65.27% vs. 64.45%). On CirCor, SPA improves weighted accuracy and reduces ECE by roughly 50% across all four backbones (CNN14, BYOL-A, AST, M2D), surpassing previous studies by Panah et al. (W_acc 80.0%) and McDonald et al. (W_acc 80.0%) with M2D+SPA reaching 84.23% W_acc. The one trade-off is a marginal AS drop (63.90%→64.42% is actually a gain; on CNN6, Se drops slightly from 35.56% to 35.24%), but the calibration gains are dramatic and come at negligible inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two established geometric ideas for medical audio: the L2 hypersphere normalization from NormFace (Wang et al., 2017) for face verification, and the Simplex ETF optimal geometry from Neural Collapse theory (Papyan et al., 2020). It departs from prior ETF-based calibration methods (BalCAL, RBL) by replacing their fixed or gradient-rotated ETF with an SVD-solved Dynamic Procrustes alignment that adapts to drifting prototypes in small, imbalanced medical datasets. The dual-branch architecture (Spherical + Geometric) is novel in its specific combination, but each component—spherical normalization, ETF structure, Procrustes rotation, KL-based self-distillation—is individually well-known from other domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, zero-overhead solution (4.2 ms vs. 4.1 ms baseline) to a clinically critical problem: overconfident misclassification in medical audio where a wrong high-confidence prediction can delay treatment. The dramatic ECE reduction (~6×) across multiple backbones and two datasets, backed by ablations and multi-seed evaluation, makes the geometric framing of norm bias a useful diagnostic lens for practitioners. Open questions include robustness to label noise (momentum prototypes assume clean labels), generalization beyond 3–4 class settings, and whether the angular-void OOD detection idea sketched in future work can outperform standard probability-based rejection.
