## Problem

Standard tokenization and embedding mechanisms for numerical variables in tabular data fail to preserve proximity structure and ordinal relationships, making Transformer-based representation learning poorly suited to continuous features. While BERT-style masked language modeling has been adapted to tabular settings, the resulting contextualized representations do not inherently respect the ordered geometry of discretized numerical tokens, and discretization itself discards within-bin variability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263578/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each tabular row is tokenized by mapping numerical variables to one of 50 bin indices (via quantile-transformed equal-width binning) and categorical variables to their native category tokens; all numerical variables share a single bin embedding matrix of size (L*+1)×d. During pretraining the model outputs a predicted token distribution (via a classifier head) and a reconstructed continuous value (via a regressor head) at masked positions; during fine-tuning the [CLS] contextualized embedding is passed through an MLP to produce a class label or regression value.

## Method

TABULARBERT tokenizes numerical variables into 50 quantile-based bins and embeds them through a shared bin embedding matrix regularized by a fused-type penalty (‖e_l − e_{l+1}‖_2^q) that enforces smoothness across adjacent bins. The BERT encoder is pretrained with masked language modeling using a squared Wasserstein distance (SWD) loss on the CDF of predicted token distributions—replacing standard cross-entropy to impose ordinal-aware geometry on contextualized representations—and an auxiliary MSE reconstruction loss that recovers within-bin continuous values. A theoretical upper bound (Proposition 3.1) shows the SWD loss controls total variation distance, justifying its use for both numerical and categorical tokens. The model is then fine-tuned end-to-end with a task-specific MLP head on the [CLS] token, retaining the fused-type regularization to adapt bin granularity to the downstream task.

## Results

TABULARBERT achieves the best or near-best performance across all 12 benchmark datasets (8 binary/multiclass classification + 4 regression). Headline gains include 0.760 accuracy on Higgs Small (vs. 0.726 for the best tree-based method CatBoost) and 0.831 on Eye Movements (vs. 0.735 for CatBoost), representing improvements of 3.4 and 9.6 percentage points respectively. On regression, TABULARBERT attains the lowest RMSE on California Housing (0.441), House 16H (3.113), and Year (8.680), outperforming the best tree-based baselines (CatBoost: 0.433, 3.133, 8.924). Among deep learning methods, TABULARBERT is the top performer on 10 of 12 datasets, with the largest margin on EY (0.831 vs. 0.758 for T2G-Former).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against tree-based gradient boosting (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost), TABULARBERT wins on 8 of 12 datasets, with the largest margins on Higgs Small (+3.4 pp over CatBoost), Eye Movements (+9.6 pp), and Gesture Phase (+5.0 pp over XGBoost); it trails on Adult (−1.3 pp vs. CatBoost), Otto (−1.0 pp vs. XGBoost), California Housing (−0.8 pp vs. CatBoost), and Facebook Comments (−0.9 pp vs. CatBoost). Among Transformer-based deep learning baselines (FT-Transformer, T2G-Former, SAINT), TABULARBERT is the top performer on 10 of 12 datasets, with particularly large gains on EY (+7.3 pp over T2G-Former) and HI (+2.8 pp over T2G-Former). Against the self-supervised binning baseline SSL(BinRecon) from Lee et al. (2024), TABULARBERT outperforms on all overlapping classification datasets (e.g., GE 0.733 vs. 0.720) but is outperformed on House 16H regression (3.113 vs. 2.989 RMSE). Against non-Transformer deep baselines (MLP, SNN, ResNet, NODE, DCNv2), TABULARBERT consistently leads by 1–10 pp on classification and by 0.05–0.09 RMSE on regression, confirming that the binning-based tokenization and Wasserstein-aware pretraining provide a clear advantage over generic deep architectures on tabular data.

## Relation to Prior Work

TABULARBERT sits at the intersection of two active research threads: (1) adapting BERT-style masked language modeling to tabular data (TabMT, MaCoDE, TP-BERTa, Lee et al. 2024) and (2) Transformer architectures for tabular prediction (FT-Transformer, SAINT, T2G-Former). Its key departure from prior binning-based approaches is a unified treatment of numerical structure: a shared bin-embedding matrix regularized by a fused-type penalty to enforce local smoothness, a squared Wasserstein distance (SWD) loss that replaces standard cross-entropy in the MLM objective (with a total-variation upper bound as theoretical justification), and an auxiliary MSE reconstruction head to recover within-bin variability. Unlike PLE, which preserves ordinality through a fixed deterministic encoding, TABULARBERT learns embeddings whose geometry is shaped by both the regularization and the Wasserstein objective during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded recipe for handling numerical variables in Transformer-based tabular models, addressing a persistent weakness of language-model-style tokenization. The SWD loss with its TV bound (Proposition 3.1) offers a clean alternative to CE that naturally encodes ordinal proximity, and the fused-type regularization is a simple inductive bias that stabilizes bin embeddings—both are easy to adopt in other tabular Transformer designs. Limitations include the two-stage pretrain-then-finetune pipeline (higher training cost), sensitivity to the number of bins and pretraining schedule, and the fact that tree-based methods still match or exceed TABULARBERT on several datasets. Open questions include whether dataset-adaptive bin selection or single-stage training could close the remaining gap to gradient boosting at lower compute cost.
