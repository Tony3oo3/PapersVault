## Problem

While brain decoding (reconstructing images from brain signals) has advanced significantly for visual prostheses, the complementary brain encoding step—converting images into biologically plausible M/EEG signals—remains largely unexplored. Prior brain encoding work relied on image-only supervision or V1 response labels without using real recorded brain signals as ground truth, limiting the biological fidelity of predicted stimuli and thus the quality of evoked visual percepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265232/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and generates a full M/EEG brain signal as output: 63 channels × 250 time points (0–1000 ms at 250 Hz) for EEG, or 271 channels × 200 time points (0–1000 ms at 200 Hz) for MEG. The brain signal is treated as a 2D spatiotemporal matrix (channels × time) and processed with (4,4) patches, yielding 1008 patches for EEG and 3400 patches for MEG. The input image is consumed through two parallel pathways: CLIP ViT-L/14 patch-level visual features (257 tokens) and CLIP text features (77 tokens) derived from an LLM-generated caption.

## Method

The framework uses a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone operating under the DDIM sampling scheme to iteratively denoise a brain signal conditioned on the input image. Cross-attention layers within each DiT block align the modalities: brain signal patch embeddings serve as Query, while a unified embedding—concatenating CLIP image tokens (257) and CLIP text tokens (77) from a Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct-generated caption—serves as Key and Value, enabling the model to capture both low-level visual and high-level semantic information. A learnable spatio-temporal position encoding is added to each patch, combining a brain-region embedding (5 regions: frontal, central, temporal, parietal, occipital) with a temporal-position embedding, so the model is explicitly aware of which cortical area and time window each patch represents. Training uses the standard diffusion noise-prediction MSE loss with ground-truth M/EEG as supervision, and inference proceeds via deterministic DDIM sampling (η=0) from pure Gaussian noise over a subsequence of timesteps.

## Results

On THINGS-EEG2 (within-subject, 10 subjects), the proposed DiT-based framework achieves MSE 0.109, Pearson 0.425, Cosine 0.402, and Synchronization Likelihood 0.614, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On THINGS-MEG (within-subject, 4 participants), it reaches MSE 0.663, Pearson 0.379, Cosine 0.382, and SL 0.597. In cross-subject settings, the method still outperforms the LOSO baseline (e.g., Pearson 0.157 vs. 0.209 on EEG; 0.082 vs. 0.164 on MEG), though performance degrades substantially due to inter-individual variability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five main baselines on both THINGS-EEG2 and THINGS-MEG across all four metrics. Against the closest generative competitor SynBrain (2025), it improves Pearson by 0.059 (0.425 vs. 0.366) and SL by 0.037 (0.614 vs. 0.577) on EEG, and by 0.077 Pearson (0.379 vs. 0.302) and 0.083 SL (0.597 vs. 0.514) on MEG. MindSimulator (2025) trails further behind, with Pearson 0.349 on EEG and 0.281 on MEG. The UNet-based Diffusion ablation (same conditioning, U-Net backbone) shows that the DiT architecture itself contributes meaningfully, achieving only Pearson 0.188 on EEG versus 0.425 for the full DiT model. Traditional encoding models (Güçlü et al. 2015; Yamins et al. 2014) are substantially weaker, with Pearson below 0.21 on EEG, confirming that generative diffusion approaches capture brain signal structure far better than linear regression on CNN features. For the LLM captioning component, Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct (2B params) outperforms both VisualGLM (6B) and MiniGPT-4 (7B) on all metrics, e.g., Pearson 0.425 vs. 0.370 and 0.386 on EEG, while using fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the brain encoding line of work (Granley et al., 2022/2023; Wang et al.) by replacing image-only or V1-response supervision with full M/EEG signals from multimodal datasets (THINGS-EEG2, THINGS-MEG) as ground truth, enabling direct validation of biological plausibility. Architecturally, it builds on the DiT backbone (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and DDIM sampling (Song et al., 2020), departing from the U-Net-based diffusion models and GAN-based EEG generation (Hartmann et al., 2018) that dominated prior brain signal synthesis. The cross-attention conditioning on unified CLIP image+text embeddings (with LLM-generated captions) draws from the broader CLIP-guided generation literature, while the learnable spatio-temporal position encoding is a domain-specific adaptation to the structured channel-time layout of M/EEG data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work completes the visual prosthesis pipeline by providing a principled brain-encoding stage that can be paired with existing brain-decoding (phosphene) models, moving the field closer to a closed-loop system. The cross-subject results, however, reveal a significant open problem: inter-individual variability causes substantial performance degradation (e.g., Pearson drops from 0.425 to 0.157 on EEG), and the LOSO within-subject results being *lower* than per-subject results suggest the model does not yet benefit from multi-subject data in a straightforward way. Future work must address subject-specific adaptation, real-time inference constraints for implanted devices, and validation on larger, more diverse populations before clinical translation.
