## Problem

Data selection is widely regarded as a remedy for model collapse in recursive synthetic-data training, but its reliability depends on the reference distribution used by the verifier. In low-resource, siloed environments (healthcare consortia, financial institutions), no single entity possesses the full target distribution, so local-reference selection preferentially retains samples aligned with a biased local manifold while pruning globally relevant tail modes—turning a safeguard into a mechanism that accelerates collapse. The paper proves this theoretically and asks how to verify synthetic data against a global reference that no single party holds.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of N synthetic candidates (e.g., 4n images generated by a DDPM) and K local real-data shards Q_k (e.g., 5,000 images per client from CIFAR-10, CelebA, or STL-10) held by separate parties under privacy constraints. It produces a filtered subset of n synthetic samples selected via submodular maximization to maximize coverage across all parties, along with a Wasserstein proxy reference (a geodesic interpolant or barycenter) computed collaboratively without any raw data exchange. The proxy in Scheme II is decoupled from the candidate pool and can be reused across multiple synthetic-data generations, whereas Scheme I must recompute the interpolation whenever the candidate set changes.

## Method

The paper first proves that top-α selection toward a local target u* in the Accumulate paradigm drives the covariance to zero almost surely (Theorem 1) at a power-law rate Tr(Σ_t) = O(t^{-ψ}) (Theorem 2), and bounds the downstream generalization cost via a Wasserstein discrepancy term (Theorem 3). For mitigation, it proposes two collaborative schemes built on Wasserstein geometry: Scheme I iteratively computes proxy measures ξ*_k on the geodesic between the synthetic distribution P and each local Q_k using alternating interpolation steps (no raw data shared), then scores each candidate via the calibrated Kantorovich dual potential S_k(x_i) and solves a monotone submodular maximization to select n samples balancing coverage across all K parties; Scheme II instead computes a collaborative Wasserstein barycenter ξ* of all K local distributions via a server-coordinated iterative interpolation, decoupling proxy estimation from candidate scoring so the barycenter can be amortized over many synthetic-data rounds. Both schemes use Sinkhorn-based OT for the transport computations, and the submodular selection (with a concave aggregation function like log(1+z)) guarantees a (1−1/e) approximation while penalizing redundancy from any single party.

## Results

After 10 recursive generations with an ExDir(1, 0.1) local reference set, Scheme I (Collaborative Geodesic Interpolation) achieves the best FID across all three datasets: 71 on CIFAR-10, 65 on STL-10, and 69 on CelebA, while also attaining the highest Recall (0.58, 0.71, 0.71 respectively). Scheme II (Collaborative Wasserstein Barycenter) follows closely with FID of 85, 69, and 75. Both proposed methods substantially outperform the strongest baseline (K-means) by 18–31 FID points, and also surpass the Random selection baseline by 20–31 FID points, confirming that collaborative proxy references mitigate diversity degradation in siloed settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three local-reference selection baselines: K-means (cluster-center proximity), CenterMatch (centroid proximity in feature space), and CovMatch (greedy covariance matching). Scheme I dominates all baselines on FID across every dataset, achieving 71 vs. 102 (K-means) on CIFAR-10, 65 vs. 89 (K-means) on STL-10, and 69 vs. 87 (K-means/CenterMatch) on CelebA. On Recall, Scheme I leads by a wide margin (0.71 on STL-10 vs. 0.53 for K-means; 0.71 on CelebA vs. 0.51 for CovMatch), confirming that collaborative proxy references preserve distributional diversity that local-reference methods prune. Notably, on CelebA the baselines (CenterMatch, CovMatch) actually outperform Random selection on FID (87 vs. 96), because the highly structured face domain retains basic characteristics even under biased local filtering; however, both proposed schemes still achieve substantially lower FID (69–75) and higher Recall (0.62–0.71 vs. 0.28–0.51). On CIFAR-10 and STL-10, the baselines with local references perform comparably to or worse than Random selection, validating the paper's central claim that local-reference selection precipitates collapse on skewed distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-collapse literature (Shumailov et al., 2024; Kazdan et al., 2025) by identifying a previously unexamined failure mode: when data selection is constrained to local, fragmented references in data-siloed environments, it actively accelerates variance collapse rather than preventing it. Methodologically, it repurposes federated optimal transport geometry—specifically the iterative geodesic interpolation of Rakotomamonjy et al. (2024) and the Wasserstein-gradient scoring of Li et al. (2024)—from a passive metric-estimation tool into an active, privacy-preserving data selection mechanism. The key departure from prior selection work (Feng et al., 2025; Rezaei et al., 2026) is the explicit acknowledgment that no single entity possesses the global reference distribution, making the standard 'verify against ground truth' assumption infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a widely adopted mitigation (data selection) can become the very mechanism driving collapse in low-resource silos is a critical warning for recursive synthetic-data pipelines in healthcare, finance, and other privacy-constrained domains. The power-law decay rate (Theorem 2) provides a quantitative diagnostic for detecting early-stage diversity erosion. Open questions include: whether the collaborative proxy is robust to adversarial poisoning by a majority of nodes (acknowledged but out of scope), whether the Gaussian theoretical framework extends to high-dimensional non-Gaussian manifolds, and whether the submodular selection objective scales to the millions-of-samples regime typical of modern generative training. The experimental validation, while clear, is limited to small-scale image datasets (CIFAR-10, STL-10, CelebA) with DDPM, leaving open whether the findings transfer to language models or larger-scale vision tasks.
