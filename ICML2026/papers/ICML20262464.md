## Problem

Existing Euclidean Flow Matching methods for few-shot VLM adaptation suffer from severe path entanglement: the polynomial volume growth of flat geometry cannot accommodate diverse feature distributions, causing transport trajectories from different classes to intersect, overlap, or merge. This leads to disordered cross-modality flows (source-target pairs in irregularly dispersed positions) and crowded inter-class flows (neighboring clusters diverting trajectories), ultimately eroding feature discriminability and classification accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes CLIP-encoded 512-dimensional image features and text class prototypes, first lifted into the (n+1)-dimensional Lorentz manifold via the exponential map from a reference point. At inference, the flow network iteratively transports the visual feature along geodesic corridors using Riemannian Euler steps, terminating adaptively when the geodesic distance to the nearest prototype falls below a threshold scaled by the semantic diameter and class count. The final class label is determined by ensembling cumulative geodesic distances across all valid transport steps up to the stopping point, rather than relying solely on the terminal state.

## Method

HFM reformulates cross-modal feature transport on the Lorentz hyperbolic manifold, exploiting its exponential volume growth to spatially decouple trajectories. Three key mechanisms work together: (1) Centripetal Hyperbolic Alignment initializes a concentric hierarchy by anchoring text prototypes near the origin (small radius) and visual features near the boundary (large radius) via learnable norm scalars, enforced by a hyperbolic entailment loss (constraining images within the text's entailment cone) and a contrastive loss; (2) a Path-Decoupled Objective trains a tangent-velocity network using step-wise consistency (minimizing Riemannian distance between predicted and ground-truth next manifold states) plus an inter-class decoupling contrastive loss that acts as a 'semantic guardrail' confining each trajectory to its class-specific geodesic corridor; (3) an adaptive diameter-based stopping criterion terminates transport when the feature approaches the crowded text region, preventing over-transportation into adjacent incorrect clusters. The flow network is a lightweight residual MLP with timestep conditioning, trained with AdamW on top of a PEFT backbone (e.g., CLIP-LoRA), and the entire framework is model-agnostic as a plug-and-play module.

## Results

On 11 few-shot image classification benchmarks, HFM (built on CLIP-LoRA + ViT-B/16) achieves 64.1% / 72.3% / 79.8% average top-1 accuracy on the five difficult datasets (Aircraft, EuroSAT, DTD, SUN397, UCF101) and 83.2% / 87.4% / 89.8% on the six easy datasets under 1/4/16-shot settings, respectively. The gains are most pronounced on structurally complex datasets: HFM outperforms the Euclidean FMA by 8.0% on EuroSAT and 3.5% on DTD under 1-shot. HFM also scales across backbones, improving ViT-L/14 by 2.1% on difficult datasets at 16-shot and reaching 93.1% average on easy datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HFM consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every shot setting and dataset. Against its closest Euclidean counterpart FMA, HFM gains +4.2% (1-shot), +4.3% (4-shot), and +3.7% (16-shot) on difficult datasets, with the largest margin of +8.0% on EuroSAT at 1-shot. Compared to the strongest PEFT baseline PLOT++ (74.3% difficult avg at 16-shot), HFM leads by 5.5%, and over TIP-Adapter (72.2%) by 7.6%. Against the CLIP-LoRA backbone alone, HFM adds +3.7% to +4.3% on difficult benchmarks, confirming that continuous flow adaptation surpasses static parameter tuning. A Euclidean control experiment (FMA* with HFM's objectives in flat space) yields only 77.9% vs. HFM's 79.8%, isolating the hyperbolic geometry itself as the primary source of the 1.9% gain over objective design alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends FMA (Jiang et al., 2025), which formulated few-shot cross-modal alignment as Euclidean flow matching, by reformulating the transport dynamics on the Lorentz hyperbolic manifold. It draws on the hyperbolic entailment framework of Desai et al. (2023) for the centripetal alignment objective and on Riemannian flow matching (Chen & Lipman, 2022) for the tangent-space velocity parameterization, but departs from both by introducing a step-wise consistency loss that supervises predicted next-states on the manifold rather than continuous velocity fields, and by adding an inter-class decoupling contrastive term that acts as a 'semantic guardrail' during transport. The core conceptual departure is the argument that polynomial volume growth in flat space is the root cause of path entanglement, and that exponential volume growth in hyperbolic space provides the geometric capacity to spatially isolate class-specific geodesic corridors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean geometric diagnosis (polynomial vs. exponential volume growth) for a practical failure mode in flow-matching-based adaptation, and the resulting HFM module is plug-and-play across multiple PEFT backbones (CoOp, CoCoOp, CLIP-Adapter, CLIP-LoRA) and CLIP scales (ViT-B/32 through ViT-L/14), making it broadly applicable. However, the quantitative evidence for the 'severe path entanglement' narrative is somewhat modest: the Trajectory Entanglement Rate drops only from 30.2% to 27.8%, and the Euclidean control (FMA*) shows that the objectives alone contribute most of the gain, with geometry adding ~1.9% on top. Open questions include whether hyperbolic flow matching extends beyond classification to dense prediction, retrieval, or compositional tasks, and whether the multi-step inference cost can be further reduced without sacrificing the decoupling benefit.
