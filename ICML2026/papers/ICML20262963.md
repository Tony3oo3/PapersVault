## Problem

Existing prompt injection defenses face a fundamental trade-off: prevention-based fine-tuning degrades general utility via an 'alignment tax,' while detection-based external classifiers double GPU memory and introduce prohibitive latency from redundant prefill computation. No prior approach simultaneously provides explainable injection localization and seamless safe-response generation without incurring these costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262963/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user instruction and a retrieved context pre-segmented into indexed sentences ([L1], [L2], …) via NLTK tokenization, along with a security directive injected as a transient user message (deliberately not the <|system|> role to prevent persona bleed into Phase 2). Phase 1 outputs a structured reasoning trace in a 'Quote → Reason' format that assigns category-specific labels (e.g., 'Unauthorized Command Injection,' 'Fake Completion Sequence') to each malicious segment; Phase 2 then produces the final safe response conditioned on that retained reasoning as an in-context guardrail.

## Method

RedVisor places a ~70M-parameter Gated Parallel Adapter exclusively at the top layer of a frozen backbone, comprising multi-head self-attention, a bottleneck MLP gating network (outputting a per-token scalar α ∈ [0,1] via sigmoid), and an FFN with RMSNorm. Inference is split into two phases: Phase 1 activates the adapter to generate an explainable reasoning trace localizing injections, while Phase 2 mutes it via a binary tensor mask (h_out = h_in + M ⊙ f_θ(h_in)), a topologically invariant operation that preserves CUDA Graph compatibility and enables zero-copy KV cache reuse since the frozen backbone's internal states remain unchanged. The adapter is trained with a causal LM objective masked to reasoning tokens only, on Alpaca data augmented with synthesized reasoning traces whose malicious intents are extracted via spaCy dependency parsing. At the systems level, RedVisor integrates into vLLM with Atomic Phase Coupling (treating both phases as a single indivisible scheduler request) and GPU-side Tail-Anchored Pattern Search for O(1) phase-transition detection, eliminating the double-prefill and inter-GPU communication overhead of decoupled pipelines.

## Results

On Alpaca-Farm, RedVisor achieves near-zero Attack Success Rate (ASR) across all five standard attack categories (0.00–0.07) on Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B, and Qwen-2.5-7B, matching or slightly outperforming PromptLocate (0.00–0.07) while dramatically outperforming prevention baselines like StruQ (0.04–0.16) and SecAlign (0.04–0.15). Under adaptive GCG attacks targeting the backbone, RedVisor maintains 0.00 ASR whereas StruQ and SecAlign spike to 0.42–0.58. For detection, RedVisor achieves ROUGE-L F1 of 0.78–1.00 across attack types, surpassing PromptLocate (0.69–0.99) on complex structural attacks (Completion, Multi-round). On utility (AlpacaEval 2.0 WinRate), RedVisor preserves ~85–86% of the raw backbone's score, compared to 57–70% for StruQ and 67–79% for SecAlign.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RedVisor ties with PromptLocate on standard attacks (both achieve 0.00–0.07 ASR) but clearly wins on detection quality for complex structural attacks: on Completion and Multi-round, RedVisor (Llama) achieves RL 0.86/0.81 versus PromptLocate's 0.74/0.69. Against DataFilter, RedVisor reduces ASR by roughly 4–5× (e.g., 0.02 vs. 0.19 on Completion for Llama-3-8B). The most dramatic gap appears under adaptive GCG attacks: StruQ and SecAlign suffer ASR spikes to 0.42–0.58, while RedVisor holds at 0.00, demonstrating that the explicit reasoning phase provides an effective air gap against optimized suffixes. Sandwich Defense and PromptArmor are consistently the weakest baselines, with ASR remaining above 0.36 and 0.41 respectively across all settings. On utility, RedVisor preserves 85–86% AlpacaEval WinRate (vs. 87% raw), while StruQ drops to 57–70% and SecAlign to 67–79%, confirming the removable-adapter design avoids the alignment tax inherent to fine-tuning-based defenses.

## Relation to Prior Work

RedVisor synthesizes two previously separate paradigms for prompt injection defense—prevention-based fine-tuning (StruQ, SecAlign) and detection-based filtering (PromptArmor, PromptLocate, DataFilter)—into a unified two-phase pipeline. Conceptually, it repurposes the parameter-efficient adapter literature (LoRA, AdapterFusion) but departs critically by restricting the adapter to the top layer only, a design choice motivated by KV cache compatibility rather than expressiveness. The 'reasoning-before-answering' paradigm draws on chain-of-thought prompting but applies it as a security-specific intermediate step, while the zero-copy KV cache reuse is a novel systems-level contribution that eliminates the double-prefill penalty inherent to all prior decoupled detector-responder architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing safety-utility-efficiency trilemma in prompt injection defense: the adapter's removability preserves backbone utility (avoiding the 'alignment tax' of fine-tuning), while the shared-backbone architecture eliminates the latency and memory overhead of deploying a separate detector LLM. The practical vLLM integration with custom kernels makes this immediately deployable in production RAG and agentic systems. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models where the adapter's top-layer-only constraint may be insufficient, how it performs against truly novel (not just re-parameterized) attack families, and whether the white-box threat model assumptions hold in practice where attackers may not have gradient access to the adapter.
