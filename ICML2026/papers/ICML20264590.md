## Problem

Existing training-free methods for fine-grained perception in MLLMs (attention-based localization, coarse-to-fine search) are misled by visually salient distractors and fail to locate multiple targets in multi-object scenarios. The paper identifies two root causes: Contextual Dominance, where distractors overwhelm the attention landscape and cause inaccurate localization, and Semantic Bias, where the first answer token aggregates global query semantics and fixates on the most salient concept, leaving other relevant targets unlocalized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264590/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution RGB image (ranging from ~2246×1582 on V*Bench to 7680-pixel-wide on HR-Bench 8K) and a natural-language query. Internally, it extracts cross-attention maps from a specific decoder layer (layer 22 for Qwen3-VL 4B, layer 27 for InternVL2.5 4B) and iteratively produces bounding boxes for each semantic target. The final output is a text answer generated by feeding the MLLM a composite prompt consisting of the original image, a zoomed-in crop of the union of all verified bounding boxes, and the original question.

## Method

ActiveScope is a training-free framework with two modules. Semantic Anchor Localization (SAL) prompts the LLM to extract key object descriptions from the query, then computes target-specific cross-attention maps by averaging attention over the tokens of each object (rather than relying on the first answer token), normalizes them via element-wise division by a generic-instruction reference map (relative attention), and extracts bounding boxes using multi-scale sliding windows. Interference-Suppressed Refinement (ISR) verifies each candidate box by asking the MLLM whether the target exists in the crop; if verification fails, it sets the attention scores between the target's text tokens and the failed visual tokens to −∞ in a global attention mask, forcing the model to redistribute attention to alternative regions in the next iteration (up to C=3 cycles). This 'seek and correct' loop decouples multi-target localization and actively breaks contextual dominance without any parameter updates.

## Results

On V* Bench with Qwen3-VL 4B, ActiveScope achieves 96.34% average accuracy (95.65% attribute recognition, 97.37% spatial reasoning), surpassing the base model by 6.81% and the best training-free competitor ZoomEye by 3.15%. On HR-Bench 4K and 8K, ActiveScope reaches 78.13% and 74.75% average accuracy respectively, outperforming ZoomEye by 1.88% and 1.25%. On MME-RealWorld-Lite, ActiveScope (50.02% avg) surpasses the closed-source GPT-4o (46.40%) and ZoomEye (49.40%). The method also scales to larger models, improving InternVL2.5-26B by 4.71% on V* Bench and Qwen3VL-30B-A3B (MoE) by 3.21%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActiveScope consistently outperforms all five baselines across every benchmark. Against ZoomEye (the strongest training-free competitor), ActiveScope gains +3.15% on V* Bench, +1.88% on HR-Bench 4K, and +1.25% on HR-Bench 8K with Qwen3-VL 4B, and +1.05%, +0.50%, and +3.13% respectively with InternVL2.5 4B. Against ViCrop, the margin is much larger: +7.33% on V* Bench and +8.13% on HR-Bench 4K (Qwen3-VL 4B), confirming that target-specific semantic anchoring substantially improves over first-token attention. ICoT is the weakest baseline, trailing ActiveScope by 20+ points on V* Bench and 24+ points on HR-Bench 4K. On MME-RealWorld-Lite, ActiveScope (50.02%) surpasses the closed-source GPT-4o (46.40%) by 3.62%, and on HR-Bench it exceeds Qwen-VL-max by 19.63% (4K) and 22.25% (8K), demonstrating that the training-free localization strategy can close the gap with much larger proprietary models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free 'localize-then-zoom-in' paradigm for fine-grained MLLM perception, building directly on ViCrop's use of cross-attention maps and relative attention normalization. It departs from prior methods in two key ways: (1) replacing the single first-answer-token attention map with target-specific semantic token anchors (SAL) to handle multi-object queries, and (2) introducing iterative hard attention masking (ISR) to suppress salient distractors, rather than relying on tree-based search (ZoomEye) or token selection (ICoT). The diagnostic framing—attributing failures to 'contextual dominance' and 'semantic bias'—provides a principled motivation absent in prior work that treats localization errors as a monolithic problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ActiveScope offers a practical, plug-and-play training-free module that can be attached to any existing MLLM without retraining, making it immediately useful for practitioners deploying high-resolution VQA systems. The identification of two distinct failure modes (contextual dominance vs. semantic bias) with targeted remedies provides a clearer diagnostic framework for future work on fine-grained perception. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video understanding, tasks where the query does not explicitly name target objects, and whether the multi-pass overhead (4.7 VQA calls, ~4 GB extra memory) remains acceptable at larger model scales or in latency-critical deployments.
