## Problem

Forensic traces in modern images are increasingly subtle and non-stationary, attenuated by post-processing and evolving generative mechanisms, making both detection and pixel-level localization fragile. Existing vision-language forensic methods rely on open-ended LLM-generated prompts that are difficult to constrain or reproduce, and uncalibrated text fusion can introduce semantic perturbations that cause localization drift. Moreover, fixed refinement pipelines cannot accommodate the sample-dependent variation in how strongly a mask should be corrected, leaving a gap in jointly adapting evidence selection and mask refinement on a per-image basis.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 512×512) and an optional free-text description that may be missing, noisy, or weakly aligned; a single inference formulation handles both text-missing and text-available settings. It produces an image-level Real/Fake classification probability and a pixel-level forgery localization mask at input resolution. The mask is generated through a coarse-to-refined pipeline where a coarse mask is first predicted and then refined using a prompt-conditioned prior map and an action-selected refinement strategy.

## Method

FPDA couples two coordinated components. The Forensic Prompting Module (FPM) builds a structured, template-guided prompt bank of K forensic evidence categories encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder, then uses a Prompt Router (conditioned on global visual features and a DAPO text action specifying routing bias and temperature) to produce a sample-adaptive bank prompt, which is fused with optional text through a reliability gate that suppresses weakly aligned descriptions. Dual-Action Policy Optimization (DAPO) learns an Actor-Critic policy over two discrete actions—a text-side action that modulates prompt routing and an image-side action selecting among skip/strong/edge mask-refinement strategies—using a detached pre-control reference context as the policy state and a bounded reward combining detection probability and soft-IoU mask improvement, optimized with clipped PPO. The dual-branch network shares an FPN backbone where Residual Deformable Adaptation injects the prompt into the top-level feature; the detection branch uses Mask-Guided Global Aggregation with a stop-gradient coarse mask for spatially aware classification, while the localization branch refines the coarse mask via a prompt-conditioned prior map and the DAPO-selected refinement strategy.

## Results

On image-level detection, FPDA achieves AUC of 0.975 (CASIA v1), 0.999 (Columbia), 0.902 (NIST16), 0.983 (COVERAGE), and 0.941 (CocoGlide), with balanced accuracy ranging from 0.875 to 0.997. On pixel-level localization, FPDA reaches F1/IoU of 0.738/0.652 (CASIA v1), 0.904/0.890 (Columbia), 0.686/0.580 (NIST16), 0.842/0.759 (OpenForensics), and 0.749/0.692 (CocoGlide). Under severe post-processing (JPEG-50, Gaussian noise σ=13, blur kernel 11, resize 0.6), FPDA maintains aggregated localization F1 of 0.69–0.75, showing smaller degradation than CoDE and TruFor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPDA outperforms all 12 compared baselines on every dataset for both detection and localization. On detection, the largest margins are on NIST16 (0.902 AUC vs. 0.760 for TruFor, the strongest baseline there) and COVERAGE (0.983 vs. 0.941 for PSCC-Net); on CocoGlide, FPDA leads TruFor by 0.189 AUC (0.941 vs. 0.752). On localization, FPDA surpasses CoDE by 0.266 F1 on NIST16 (0.686 vs. 0.420) and FakeShield by 0.157 F1 on CocoGlide (0.749 vs. 0.592). The closest competitor on Columbia detection is SPAN (0.998 AUC) and TruFor (0.996), but FPDA still edges ahead at 0.999 with substantially higher balanced accuracy (0.997 vs. 0.950/0.983). On OpenForensics localization, FPDA achieves 0.842 F1, exceeding MVSS-Net (0.716) and CAT-Net (0.788) by 0.126 and 0.054 respectively. In the cross-domain transfer experiment (trained on manual-tampering datasets, tested on CocoGlide without fine-tuning), FPDA reaches 0.913 AUC and 0.701 F1, outperforming CoDE (0.861/0.618) and TruFor (0.846/0.592) by 0.052 AUC and 0.083 F1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads—vision-language prompt learning (CLIP-based conditioning, CoOp-style context optimization), joint forgery detection/localization (ManTra-Net, TruFor, CoDE), and policy optimization (PPO, contextual bandits)—into a unified framework. It departs from prior forensic prompting work by replacing open-ended LLM-generated prompts with a structured, reproducible forensic prompt bank organized by evidence category, and from fixed-pipeline localization by introducing a dual-action PPO policy that makes both evidence routing and mask refinement sample-adaptive. The reliability-gated fusion mechanism specifically addresses the practical gap that external text descriptions are often missing or noisy, a problem prior vision-language forensic methods handle less explicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The text-optional input protocol and structured prompt bank make the framework practical for real-world forensic pipelines where descriptions are unreliable, and the dual-action policy offers a principled way to avoid the one-size-fits-all refinement that causes localization drift under post-processing. However, the action space is small (three refinement strategies, a fixed set of routing regimes), the prompt bank is hand-designed rather than learned, and evaluation is limited to 512×512 inputs on relatively small datasets; whether the structured evidence vocabulary and policy generalize to truly novel manipulation types or larger-scale deployments remains open. The cross-domain transfer result (manual tampering → diffusion editing without fine-tuning) is encouraging but tested on a single target dataset.
