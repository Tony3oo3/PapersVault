## Problem

Standard Softmax classifiers are miscalibrated because they implicitly fix the scale parameter of an underlying Gumbel distribution, preventing them from modeling sample-specific (heteroscedastic) uncertainty. Existing calibration remedies—temperature scaling, histogram binning, and MMD-based distribution matching—each suffer from limited expressiveness, hyperparameter sensitivity, or inflexibility between training and inference time, and none provide a unified theoretical link between individual calibration guarantees and distribution-matching calibration metrics.

## Input / Output

The ALD classifier takes raw input features (tabular vectors, 32×32 RGB images, or text embeddings) and predicts three parameters per class—location θ_c, scale σ_c, and asymmetry κ_c—whose CDF values at a fixed threshold t_0 are normalized into a categorical posterior. The ICALD variant additionally consumes a stochastic anchor q ~ U(0,1) sampled per training step, producing an anchor-conditioned top-label confidence that is penalized to match q. In post-calibration mode, a lightweight MLP adapter receives frozen base-model logits (or features) together with the anchor q and outputs multiplicative adjustment factors γ that modulate the uncalibrated probabilities element-wise.

## Method

The paper replaces the Softmax output layer with an asymmetric Laplace Distribution (ALD) classifier that evaluates the ALD CDF at a fixed threshold t_0 for each class and normalizes the resulting scores, giving the model three free parameters per class (location, scale, asymmetry) instead of Softmax's single location parameter. On top of this, the ICALD objective introduces a random anchor q ~ U(0,1) as an additional input and penalizes the absolute deviation |p_{y*}(x,q) − q| with a monotonicity constraint in q, directly targeting a monotone Probably Approximately Individually Calibrated (mPAIC) predictor. The paper proves the implication chain mPAIC → PAIC → PACC(top-label) with explicit kernel-dependent constants and finite-sample rates, bridging individual calibration theory with kernel calibration error. To resolve the optimization conflict between discrimination and calibration, a Decoupled Dual-Stream Optimization (DDSO) strategy runs a discriminative stream (q=1, full backpropagation for NLL) in parallel with a calibration stream (random q, gradient-detached features) so that calibration gradients never corrupt the backbone, preventing feature collapse while accelerating convergence of the lightweight calibration head. The framework is deployment-flexible: it can be trained end-to-end as a pre-calibration objective or attached as a post-calibration adapter to an already-trained model.

## Results

Across 45 experimental settings (9 datasets × 5 splits), H_ICALD^Pre achieves the best overall Friedman rank of 4.65, with the top rank on ACC (5.29), ECE (3.76), and GroupX_ECE (3.90). H_ICALD^Post is also highly competitive (overall 5.03) and attains the best GroupY_ECE rank (3.31). The standard H_Softmax baseline lags significantly with an overall rank of 7.11 and the worst ECE rank (9.07), while H_ALD alone improves to 6.10 overall and 7.08 on ECE. On KCE specifically, the MMD-based pre-calibration baselines (H_Softmax+MMD: 3.29, H_ALD+MMD: 3.49) remain slightly ahead of H_ICALD variants (4.13–4.24), though H_ICALD dominates on all other calibration and accuracy metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H_ICALD^Pre (overall rank 4.65) outperforms all post-calibration baselines: Temperature Scaling (Guo et al., 2017) degrades overall performance (H_Softmax+TS: 7.74, H_ALD+TS: 7.23) and worsens GroupX_ECE dramatically (9.22 and 10.38), while Histogram Binning (Zadrozny & Elkan, 2001) is the weakest baseline overall (H_Softmax+HB: 8.72, H_ALD+HB: 7.78) with the worst ACC and AUC ranks. The MMD-based pre-calibration (Marx et al., 2023) is the strongest competitor on KCE (3.29 for H_Softmax+MMD, 3.49 for H_ALD+MMD vs. 4.24 for H_ICALD^Pre), but H_ICALD^Pre dominates on ECE (3.76 vs. 7.86/6.07), GroupX_ECE (3.90 vs. 5.73/6.17), and ACC (5.29 vs. 6.13/5.76). In the extended comparison on five tabular datasets (Appendix C.5), H_ICALD^Pre achieves the best aggregate rank of 2.17, substantially outperforming H_CWLS (Jung et al., 2023), H_TNA (Cho & Youn, 2024), H_FC (Tao et al., 2025), H_DFL (Tao et al., 2023), H_BSCE-GRA (Lin et al., 2025), H_EDL (Sensoy et al., 2018), H_NatPN (Charpentier et al., 2022), and H_Dirichlet (Kull et al., 2019), ranking first on ACC, AUC, AP, ECE, GroupX_ECE, Brier score, and KCE. The key trade-off is that H_ICALD sacrifices a small margin on KCE relative to the MMD baselines but gains substantially on all other calibration levels and discriminative accuracy, yielding the best holistic profile.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate calibration paradigms: the individualized calibration framework of Zhao et al. (2020) (PAIC/mPAIC via anchor-conditioned forecasting) and the distribution-matching calibration of Marx et al. (2023) (KCE/MMD-based PACC). It departs from both by replacing the standard Softmax (a Gumbel latent-utility model with fixed scale) with an asymmetric Laplace distribution classifier that explicitly predicts location, scale, and asymmetry per class, and by proving a theoretical implication chain (mPAIC → PAIC → PACC) that connects the two paradigms with explicit error bounds. The Decoupled Dual-Stream Optimization (DDSO) strategy with gradient detachment is a practical contribution that addresses the well-known optimization tension between discrimination and calibration, and the framework's dual deployment as both a pre-calibration objective and a lightweight post-calibration adapter distinguishes it from methods locked to a single stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, distribution-agnostic calibration framework with explicit finite-sample guarantees, which is valuable for high-stakes applications requiring reliable confidence estimates across subpopulations. However, several limitations temper its impact: the framework currently targets only top-label confidence (not canonical calibration), introduces a t0 hyperparameter absent from standard Softmax training, and incurs non-trivial computational overhead even with DDSO. Open questions include extending the mPAIC→PACC chain to canonical-level calibration, reducing the calibration stream's cost further, and validating the approach on larger-scale benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet) where the current nine-dataset evaluation may not fully capture generalization.
