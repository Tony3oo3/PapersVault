## Problem

The classical Bauerle & Ott (2011) Bellman formulation for static CVaR MDPs concentrates all risk-relevant signal into a terminal functional, producing sparse rewards that hinder credit assignment, and its contraction property is only guaranteed on a narrow class of unbounded, nondecreasing, compactly-supported functions—yielding a degenerate zero fixed point on the practically relevant space of bounded functions. The paper shows these pathologies are artifacts of the particular value-function structure rather than inherent to the static CVaR objective.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite MDP (S, A, P, r, s₀, γ) with bounded rewards, a risk level α ∈ (0,1], and a discretization resolution K that partitions the continuous budget dimension z ∈ [−r_γ, r_γ] (where r_γ = r_max/(1−γ)) into a uniform grid Z_Δ of size K. It outputs a tabular Q-function q̂*(s,z,a) over all nominal states, grid budget levels, and actions, from which an optimal starting budget z* is extracted via a one-dimensional outer optimization, yielding a history-dependent policy in the original MDP that is stationary in the augmented space. Both lower- and upper-bounding approximations are produced simultaneously, with a guaranteed performance gap of γ(1−γ)⁻¹Δ/α.

## Method

The core contribution is an algebraic reformulation of the static CVaR bilevel program (Proposition 3.1) that adds a z₋ term to the inner policy optimization, producing a new Bellman operator T̄ with a dense per-step reward r̃(s,z,a) = z₋ − (r(s,a)+z)₋ instead of the zero reward in Bauerle & Ott. This operator is a γ-contraction on the full space L_∞(S̄) of bounded functions with unique fixed point v̄*, which is provably bounded by r_max/(1−γ) and constant outside [−r_γ, r_γ], enabling uniform discretization. For the discrete grid, floor and ceiling rounding maps define bounding operators T̄^l and T̄^u that preserve ordering and contraction, yielding explicit error bounds proportional to grid granularity Δ and the discount factor. Two algorithms are derived: a model-based Q-value iteration (Algorithm 1) and a model-free Q-learning variant (Algorithm 2) that employs a block-update scheme—given a single observed transition (s, z, a, r), it deterministically computes the next budget z′ for every grid point z̃ ∈ Z_Δ and updates all corresponding Q-values in one step, borrowing the data-relabeling idea from hindsight experience replay to improve sample efficiency.

## Results

On a stochastic gridworld (2D terrain with craters yielding -10 penalty and fuel costs of -1 per step), both the proposed Static CVaR Q-VI and Q-learning algorithms produce policies whose lower-tail return distributions shrink monotonically as the risk level α decreases from 1.0 (risk-neutral) to 0.01 (near-robust). For small α, both risk-averse methods outperform the risk-neutral policy in empirical CVaR_α, and the discounted number of crater entries per episode decreases as risk aversion increases. Q-learning converges to the Q-VI solution within approximately 50k training iterations. The discretization error bounds from Theorem 5.4 are validated: the performance gap between the upper and lower bounding operators (Δ_l = Δ_u = γ(1−γ)⁻¹Δ) shrinks proportionally with grid granularity (tested at 100, 500, 1000, and 5000 bins).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external risk-sensitive RL baselines (e.g., distributional RL, policy-gradient CVaR methods). Instead, it compares two internally proposed algorithms—Static CVaR Q-VI (model-based) and Static CVaR Q-learning (model-free)—against a risk-neutral policy baseline. Both risk-averse methods agree closely across most α values, with Q-learning showing slightly higher variance around α ∈ [0.5, 0.6] where a policy switch occurs (the agent transitions from a shorter, riskier route to a longer, safer route). The risk-neutral policy (α = 1.0) is outperformed in CVaR_α for all small α values, confirming that the augmented-state formulation captures genuine tail-risk reduction. The upper and lower bounding Bellman operators (T^{u_Δ} and T^{l_Δ}) maintain their theoretical ordering (Ψ^l ≤ CVaR ≤ Ψ^u) across all discretization resolutions, with the gap closing as the grid is refined, empirically confirming Theorem 5.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the state-augmentation framework of Bauerle & Ott (2011) for solving static CVaR MDPs, but departs from it by showing that the sparse-reward and restricted-convergence limitations of the original Bellman operator are artifacts of the value function structure rather than inherent to the problem. A simple algebraic transformation of the CVaR objective yields a new Bellman operator that is a contraction on the full space of bounded functions (L-infinity) and distributes CVaR-relevant signals across all timesteps. The model-free algorithm additionally draws on data-relabeling ideas from hindsight experience replay (Andrychowicz et al., 2017) to enable block-updates over the discretized augmented state, and positions itself against distributional RL and policy gradient approaches that the authors show fail to optimize the correct static risk objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing that standard DP and Q-learning algorithms can be applied to static CVaR MDPs with full convergence guarantees on bounded functions, this work removes a long-standing barrier that had forced practitioners toward heuristic reward capping or restricted function classes. The discretization error bounds (proportional to grid granularity and discount factor) provide a principled way to trade off accuracy for tractability. However, all empirical validation is on a single tabular gridworld, and the paper explicitly acknowledges that extending to function approximation (e.g., deep networks) to handle continuous augmented states and general reward signs remains open. The practical impact will depend on whether the block-update Q-learning scheme scales to high-dimensional state spaces.
