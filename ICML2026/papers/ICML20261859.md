## Problem

Existing LLM calibration methods either require direct logit access (temperature scaling) or measure internal stochasticity across repeated sampling (self-consistency), leaving a critical dimension of trustworthiness unexplored: a model's behavioral robustness to irrelevant or misleading information. The paper argues that susceptibility to semantic distractors is a strong, measurable proxy for prediction error, drawing parallels to the Misinformation Effect and Dunning-Kruger Effect from cognitive psychology, and that this signal can be exploited for post-hoc calibration without any access to model internals.

## Input / Output

For each sample, the method consumes the original prompt, the model's initial prediction and confidence score (logit-based probability, API log-probability, or verbalized confidence), and automatically generates k = (c−1)×m distractor prompts at runtime from predefined semantic templates targeting each unselected label. It produces a single calibrated confidence score per sample while preserving the original prediction (accuracy-preserving). The number of forward passes scales with the task's label space rather than a fixed sampling budget, typically requiring only 2–9 passes versus 15–40 for consistency-based methods.

## Method

CALIDIST perturbs the input prompt with k semantic distractors (in three styles: Assertion, Probe, and Sample-Corruption) and measures two instability metrics: Prediction Instability (μ), the weighted fraction of distracted predictions that flip from the original answer, and Confidence Instability (δ), the absolute shift in mean confidence. These are combined into a reliability score λ_raw = (1−μ)/(δ+ε), where μ acts as a gating mechanism (collapsing λ to 0 under total prediction failure) and δ acts as a sensitivity gain (amplifying subtle confidence tremors). The score is MinMax-normalized to [0,10] on a held-out validation set, then mapped through a parameterized sigmoid σ(λ, α, β) that scales the original confidence. The sigmoid parameters α (centering) and β (steepness) are learned per-task via grid search minimizing Brier Score, making the formulation mathematically equivalent to Platt Scaling but operating on a behavioral feature rather than raw logits, enabling deployment on both white-box and black-box models.

## Results

CALIDIST achieves an average ECE of 4.08 (Probe-style) and 4.37 (Assertion-style) on Llama-3.1 across seven NLU benchmarks, compared to 7.42 for Temperature Scaling and 15.18 for uncalibrated Vanilla confidence. On Qwen-3, the gains are even more dramatic: CALIDIST(As.) reaches 3.77 average ECE versus 24.13 for TS and 26.26 for Vanilla. On proprietary models (Table 3), CALIDIST with log-probabilities achieves 2.82–4.14 average ECE on GPT-4o-Mini and 4.34–5.07 on Gemini-2.0-Flash, substantially outperforming all consistency-based baselines (16.35–19.87). Overall, the paper reports a 70% relative reduction in ECE (from ~23% to ~7%) across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CALIDIST outperforms Temperature Scaling (Guo et al., 2017) on average ECE for both open-source models: 4.08 vs. 7.42 on Llama-3.1 and 3.77 vs. 24.13 on Qwen-3, with the Qwen-3 gap being particularly large (6.4× improvement). Against Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023), CALIDIST wins decisively—e.g., 4.08 vs. 18.65 ECE on Llama-3.1—while requiring only 3 forward passes versus 15. FSD (Lyu et al., 2025) and Entropy-based consistency are similarly dominated across all datasets. In the direct comparison with SPUQ (Gao et al., 2024) on CSQA and AQuA, CALIDIST achieves 4.73 vs. 12.68 ECE (Llama-3.1, CSQA) and 2.02 vs. 11.88 ECE (Qwen-3, AQuA). Against logit-dependent baselines Isotonic Regression (Zadrozny & Elkan, 2002) and Vector Scaling, CALIDIST wins on both MNLI (2.80 vs. 3.31 ECE) and PPDB (2.46 vs. 4.33 ECE) with an identical 200-sample validation budget. Finally, the verbalized-confidence variant of CALIDIST consistently beats the uncalibrated Verbalized baseline (Xiong et al., 2024) in every setting tested, confirming applicability to black-box APIs.

## Relation to Prior Work

CALIDIST positions itself at the intersection of two established calibration paradigms—post-hoc statistical adjustments (Temperature Scaling, Platt Scaling) and perturbation-based consistency estimation (Self-Consistency, SPUQ)—while departing from both. It is mathematically isomorphic to Platt Scaling in its final sigmoid mapping (proven in Appendix B), but replaces the logit-based input with a behavioral reliability score derived from adversarial distractor perturbations. Unlike SPUQ, which probes stability against semantic equivalents, or SteerConf, which manipulates metacognitive instructions, CALIDIST specifically injects misleading information to test robustness against the Misinformation Effect, grounding the calibration signal in cognitive psychology rather than internal stochasticity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, computationally efficient (3 passes vs. 15–40 for consistency methods) calibration approach that works on both white-box and black-box LLMs without requiring logit or hidden-state access, making it deployable on proprietary APIs. The strong empirical results (70% relative ECE reduction, consistent gains across 7 datasets and 6 models) and the cognitive-psychology grounding provide a principled framework for designing targeted reliability 'stress tests.' Open questions include whether larger models develop sufficient anti-distraction capability to render the signal less informative, whether behavioral robustness signals can be incorporated into training objectives, and how the method generalizes beyond classification to open-ended generation tasks.
