## Problem

Both VLMs and VLAs in embodied vision-language decision making suffer from a shared perceptual bottleneck: visual hallucinations and attention drift caused by the inability to distinguish task-relevant objects from cluttered distractors. One-step focus strategies (directly attending to essential objects) prove ineffective because effective focus inherently requires progressive scene understanding. The paper tackles the gap of enabling structured, multi-step visual focus that leverages the VLM's own planning abilities rather than relying on fixed heuristics or external detectors.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a natural-language task instruction as input. The scene graph is generated externally via OvSGTR and fed to the VLM as structured text with <ref>, <pred>, and <box> tokens. The pipeline produces a pixel-level binary focus score map (H×W), a modulated image prompt where background regions are luminance-dimmed and Gaussian-blurred while target regions remain sharp, and ultimately a discrete action or navigation decision. For the VLA adapter variant, the output is a patch-level attention mask (sigmoid probabilities) that modulates visual tokens before the LLM backbone, adding only ~3 ms of latency.

## Method

SceneDiver is a coarse-to-fine focus plan generation pipeline. In the coarse stage, a scene graph (nodes = objects, edges = spatial relations) is generated by OvSGTR and used as a structured prior for the VLM to perform graph reasoning, decomposing the global scene into task-relevant sub-scenes. In the fine stage, the VLM autonomously explores each sub-scene in an agentic loop—verifying relevance, triggering semantic zoom for ambiguous cases, or initiating local spatial search for graph-to-image misalignment—yielding a verified candidate set. Candidate bounding boxes are rasterized into a focus score map, and a soft modulation (luminance scaling plus inverse-score Gaussian blur) produces the final modulated image prompt. For real-time VLA deployment, a lightweight adapter distills this two-stage reasoning into a Task-Guided Slot Attention module (task-conditional slot initialization, 5 GRU refinement iterations) and a Scene-Aware Mask Prediction module (coarse slot-level relevance scoring + fine patch-level residual refinement), trained via Hungarian Matching against scene-graph-derived objects with entropy-based dynamic gating for graceful degradation.

## Results

On the MuJoCo robotic manipulation task, SceneDiver achieves a 10–15% absolute success-rate gain over the base VLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 14.7% → 28.7%; gemini-2.5-flash: 38.7% → 46.7%). On the room navigation benchmark, the method delivers up to a 16% improvement (Qwen2.5-VL-7B Base sub-task: 32.7% → 44.0%) and consistently outperforms all focus-based baselines across four instruction types and seven models. On LIBERO-plus, the lightweight SceneDiver adapter improves the OpenVLA-OFT baseline by up to 9.6% success rate (Long suite, Background Textures) while adding only 2.64% inference latency overhead (~3 ms out of 114 ms total).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneDiver consistently outperforms both SoM (Set-of-Mark prompting) and VCD (Visual Contrastive Decoding) across all seven evaluated models and all four navigation sub-tasks. Against SoM, the gains are largest on the Base sub-task (e.g., +14.0 pts for Qwen-7B: 44.0 vs 30.0) and remain substantial on Visual-Appearance (+6.0 pts), where dense SAM-based markers introduce visual noise that obstructs reasoning. Against VCD, which modifies output logits to counter language priors, SceneDiver achieves +9.3 to +12.0 pts on Qwen-7B, confirming that input-side perceptual enhancement is more effective than output-side statistical correction. The method also surpasses Multi-Res (multi-resolution cropping) by 4.7–14.7 pts, as scene-graph-guided selective zooming preserves spatial topology that global low-res + local high-res crops lose. Even thinking-enhanced closed-source models (gemini-2.5-flash-thinking, doubao-seed-1.6-flash-thinking) underperform SceneDiver, indicating that extended chain-of-thought reasoning alone cannot compensate for perceptual deficiencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneDiver repurposes scene graphs—previously used as output representations for captioning (Visual Genome, SPICE) or as supervision for VQA (GQA)—as a structured *input prior* that scaffolds the VLM's own reasoning, rather than as a target to predict. It departs from hallucination-mitigation work that operates at the decoding level (VCD, attention calibration) or the prompting level (Set-of-Mark, multi-resolution crops) by instead intervening at the perception level through a coarse-to-fine exploration loop. The lightweight adapter extends Slot Attention (originally for object-centric learning) into a distillation bridge that transfers the VLM's deliberate focus planning into a VLA's latent space, a use not previously explored in the VLA literature (RT-2, OpenVLA).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying VLMs/VLAs in cluttered embodied settings, and the 2.64% latency overhead of the distilled adapter makes the approach viable for real-time robotic control—a non-trivial engineering contribution. However, the method's reliance on a scene graph generator (OvSGTR) introduces a dependency on detection quality, and the stress tests, while reassuring, are conducted in simulation with controlled node dropout rather than in genuinely dynamic or adversarial real-world scenes. Open questions include whether the coarse-to-fine pipeline generalizes to highly dynamic environments with rapid object motion, and whether integration with hierarchical planning frameworks could compound the gains in multi-stage manipulation.
