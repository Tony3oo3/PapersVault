## Problem

Learning the optimal polytree (a Bayesian network whose skeleton is a forest) is NP-hard, and the fastest known exact algorithm runs in 3^n time. No prior work had improved this exponential bound under in-degree constraints, and the approximation landscape for polytree learning—unlike general Bayesian networks—was essentially unexplored, leaving open whether the simpler forest structure admits better approximation guarantees than the k-factor known for general DAGs.

## Input / Output

The input consists of a node set V of size n and, for each node v, a family F_v of potential parent sets with associated local scores f_v(S); unspecified parent sets are assigned score −∞, and all scores are normalized so that f_v(∅)=0. The output is a polytree D=(V,A) (a DAG whose underlying undirected graph is a forest) maximizing the total score f(D)=Σ_v f_v(D_v). The input size is |F| (the total number of listed potential parent sets), and the non-zero encoding keeps the input polynomial in n for bounded in-degree instances.

## Method

For exact algorithms, the paper introduces a dynamic-programming recurrence over pairs (S,T) of node subsets (T⊆S) tracking the best connected polytree on S where only nodes in T have non-empty parent sets, yielding a 3^n·|I|^O(1) algorithm for general PT. Under a constant in-degree bound k, a key DFS-ordering lemma shows that |S|−|T|=O(k log n) along any valid traversal, so restricting the DP table to such states reduces the running time to (2+ε)^n·|I|^O(1) for any ε>0, which is near-optimal under the Set Cover Conjecture. For approximation, a greedy algorithm that repeatedly adds the highest-scoring parent set while preserving the polytree constraint achieves a (k+1)-approximation for PT_≤; for additive local scores, a variant that adds single edges instead of full parent sets improves this to a 2-approximation. A further 2q-approximation for component-size-bounded polytrees (PT_comp) uses a modified greedy rule that selects parent sets by their per-arc score ω_v(D_v)=f_v(D_v)/|D_v| rather than total score. Inapproximability lower bounds under the Unique Games Conjecture show that the (k+1) and 2q factors are optimal up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

The paper provides exact algorithms for polytree learning: a 3^n·|I|^O(1) time algorithm for general PT (matching Grüttemeier et al., 2021a) and an improved (2+ε)^n·|I|^O(1) time algorithm for PT with constant in-degree bound k, which is shown to be near-optimal under the Set Cover Conjecture. For approximation, the paper gives a polynomial-time (k+1)-approximation for PT_≤ (optimal up to log factors under UG), a 2-approximation for PT_≤ with additive scores, and a 2q-approximation for PT_comp with component size bounded by q. Without any restrictions, no non-trivial polynomial-time approximation is possible unless P=NP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's exact algorithm for general PT (3^n·|I|^O(1)) matches the fastest previously known algorithm of Grüttemeier et al. (2021a), confirming their complexity bound via an independent dynamic-programming approach. For the bounded in-degree variant PT_≤, the new (2+ε)^n algorithm strictly improves over the prior 3^n bound, and a reduction from k'-SET PARTITIONING under the Set Cover Conjecture shows this is essentially tight. The (k+1)-approximation for PT_≤ is compared to Ziegler's (2008) k-approximation for general Bayesian networks with in-degree bound k; the paper shows the polytree constraint does not yield a better factor, and the (k+1) bound is near-optimal under the Unique Games Conjecture. For additive scores, the 2-approximation is notable because an exact polynomial-time algorithm already exists (Ganian & Korchemna, 2026), but the result demonstrates that a simple greedy heuristic achieves a strong constant-factor guarantee, suggesting the approach may extend to more general score functions. The 2q-approximation for PT_comp is shown to be near-optimal under UG via a reduction from Maximum Independent Set, matching the hardness of the (k+1) case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on polytree learning initiated by Dasgupta (1999) and Gaspers et al. (2015), and directly builds on Grüttemeier et al. (2021a), whose 3^n dynamic-programming algorithm the authors match and then improve to (2+ε)^n under constant in-degree bounds via a DFS-based state-space pruning argument. The greedy approximation algorithms are close adaptations of Ziegler's (2008) approach for general Bayesian networks, with the key modification being that acyclicity is enforced on the undirected skeleton rather than the directed graph. The inapproximability results reuse the Maximum Independent Set reduction from Grüttemeier et al. (2021a) combined with hardness results of Håstad (1997) and Austrin et al. (2009).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a near-complete complexity picture for polytree learning: exact algorithms with matching lower bounds under the Set Cover Conjecture, and approximation algorithms with matching upper and lower bounds under the Unique Games Conjecture. A notable open question is whether the 2-approximation for additive scores can be improved, and whether FPT-approximation ideas from Kundu et al. (2024a) transfer to the polytree setting. The authors also identify treewidth-bounded networks (generalizing polytrees at treewidth 1) as a natural next frontier for both exact and approximate structure learning.
