## Problem

Preference-based alignment methods (RLHF, DPO) implicitly average over heterogeneous human preferences by treating feedback as noisy observations of a single latent scalar utility, making mean-reward maximization brittle under systematic annotator disagreement and susceptible to proxy over-optimization. While training-time robustness objectives exist, a principled inference-time selection rule that is explicitly risk-constrained under preference heterogeneity—without retraining—remains underdeveloped.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266316/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a fixed candidate pool of K responses generated by a frozen LM for a text prompt, DARC consumes per-candidate reward scores (from human annotators or proxy reward models) and perturbation-based disagreement estimates (standard deviation of rewards across N_aug style-preserving rewrites scored by a reward model). It outputs a single selected response from the pool, with risk behavior controlled by hyperparameters β (entropic risk sensitivity), τ (risk-premium budget), or ε (near-optimality tolerance for tie-breaking). The method is entirely inference-time: no model weights are modified, and the candidate set is shared across all compared methods so differences arise solely from the selection rule.

## Method

DARC frames response selection as distributionally robust, risk-sensitive decision making over a finite candidate set. The primary decoder maximizes a KL-robust entropic value V_β = -(1/β)log E[exp(-βR)], which the paper proves equivalent to a KL-regularized DRO worst-case objective (Theorem 3.5) and to CARA expected utility. A uniform lower confidence bound (Proposition 3.3) derived via empirical Bernstein concentration yields a mean-dispersion surrogate μ̂ - λσ̂, and a complementary χ²-DRO analysis (Proposition 3.6) shows the LCB penalty matches a local DRO radius ρ ∝ log(K/δ)/n, unifying statistical pessimism with adversarial robustness. Practical variants include a risk-constrained form (maximize V_β subject to entropic risk premium ≤ τ), a penalized Lagrangian form, and an ε-tie-breaking rule that selects the least-disagreement candidate among those within ε of the best entropic value. For multi-scorer settings, scorer-specific entropic values are aggregated via a soft worst-case operator that admits a KL-regularized DRO interpretation over the scorer simplex (Proposition 4.1), hedging against reward-model shift.

## Results

On human-evaluated MT-Bench (overall), DARC-ε achieves the best human satisfaction score (8.08), lowest disagreement risk (0.55), highest risk-reward tradeoff (6.98), and best prompt-level CVaR₁₀% (7.62), compared to the mean Best-of-K baseline (7.56 / 0.67 / 6.22 / 6.73). Gains concentrate on the high-disagreement subset (top 20% prompts by baseline σ), where DARC-ε reaches 8.34 human score with 0.65 risk and 7.60 CVaR₁₀%, versus 7.62 / 1.00 / 6.10 for the baseline. On automated MT-Bench and AlpacaEval 2.0 with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, DARC-ε attains the best tradeoff score (−6.12 on MT-Bench, −1.54 on AlpacaEval) while maintaining competitive mean reward (4.46 / 3.18) and stable response length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DARC-ε outperforms all six inference-time baselines on every reported metric (human score, risk, tradeoff, CVaR₁₀%) in both the overall and high-disagreement settings. Against the closest inference-time competitors—Caution (pessimistic best-of-N) and RBoN (reward-regularized best-of-N)—DARC-ε gains +0.26 and +0.37 in human score respectively on the overall set, with the gap widening to +0.53 and +0.39 on the high-disagreement subset. DeAL (top-k lookahead decoding) achieves a slightly higher mean reward on automated MT-Bench (5.59 vs. 4.46 for DARC) but at substantially higher risk (7.98 vs. 5.29), yielding the worst tradeoff (−10.37 vs. −6.12); on human evaluation DARC-ε dominates DeAL on all four metrics. BoP/HedgeTune and MC-Dropout reduce risk relative to the base but sacrifice more mean quality, resulting in lower tradeoff scores than DARC variants. Among training-time methods, rDPO achieves the highest raw human score (8.17) but with the highest risk (0.69); combining rDPO with DARC-ε as an inference-time plug-in further reduces risk to 0.58 and improves tradeoff to 6.99, confirming DARC's complementarity with robust training objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

DARC builds on the Best-of-N inference-time selection paradigm (Sun et al., 2024) and the distributionally robust optimization framework (Wiesemann et al., 2014), but departs from prior inference-time hedging methods (HedgeTune, Caution, RBoN) that target proxy over-optimization or reward-model atypicality under a single reward signal. The key conceptual departure is framing response selection as risk-constrained decision making under *preference heterogeneity* (multi-annotator disagreement) rather than estimation uncertainty of a single proxy, and providing a unified LCB/DRO characterization that connects statistical pessimism to adversarial robustness. Training-time robust alignment methods (cDPO, rDPO) are treated as complementary rather than competing, since DARC operates as a retraining-free plug-in on a fixed candidate pool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DARC offers a practical, retraining-free decoding rule with modest inference overhead (~1.5–3.5%) that can be layered on top of any existing LM and reward model, making it immediately deployable. The DRO unification (KL-robust entropic value = LCB = χ²-DRO robust mean) provides a principled lens for interpreting widely used mean-dispersion heuristics, which may guide future method design. Key limitations include reliance on perturbation-based proxies as a scalable stand-in for true human disagreement (validated but not calibrated), a finite candidate pool, and the single-scalar-satisfaction abstraction. Open questions include user/group-conditional risk budgets, richer multi-dimensional robustness signals, and whether the entropic risk premium can be made adaptive per prompt without additional tuning.
