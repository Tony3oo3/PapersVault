## Problem

Most 3D assets lack the spatially-varying mechanical properties (Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, density) required for accurate physics simulation, and existing feed-forward methods like VoMP and Pixie are limited to fixed 64³ voxel grids, which cannot resolve fine material boundaries in complex objects. The paper tackles the challenge of predicting volumetric material fields at 1024³ resolution—16³× higher than prior art—while remaining memory-efficient and accurate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260811/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D shape (mesh or Gaussian splat) that is voxelized into a base 1024³ grid and rendered from multiple viewpoints; multi-view DINOv3 patch-token features are aggregated via depth-attenuated averaging and compressed into a sparse adaptive voxel tree (SAV) as the model input. The output is an autoregressively generated SAV material tree at up to 1024³ effective resolution, where each leaf voxel stores a 2D latent decoded through a frozen MatVAE into (E, ν, ρ), and the tree structure itself is predicted per-voxel via EMPTY/KEEP/SUBDIVIDE decisions, yielding a piecewise-constant field queryable at any point.

## Method

The core contribution is the SAV representation, a multi-resolution voxel tree that stores coarse voxels for homogeneous material regions and recursively refines only heterogeneous areas, reducing stored voxels to ~7–10% of a dense 64³ grid. A sparse transformer encoder (Adaptive Geometry Transformer) processes the input SAV using unified coordinate embeddings, RoPE positional encoding, and sparse shifted-window attention across mixed-resolution levels. A jointly trained sparse transformer decoder (Adaptive Material Generator) then autoregressively generates the material SAV coarse-to-fine, predicting at each level both a structure action (EMPTY/KEEP/SUBDIVIDE) and a 2D material latent for non-empty voxels, with parent hidden states propagated to children for spatial coherence. Training uses teacher forcing with ground-truth subdivision schedules, level-weighted multi-scale supervision (γ^ℓ weighting favoring coarser levels), explicit empty-space negative candidates, and a MatVAE decoder to constrain outputs to physically plausible material triplets.

## Results

On GVT-TEST at 1024³ resolution, ADAVOMP (H, 0.6B) achieves ALDE 0.8841 for Young's modulus, ADE 0.0215 for Poisson's ratio, and ADE 158.46 for density, outperforming the closest prior method VoMP (ALDE 1.1371, ADE 0.0289, ADE 191.63) by 22%, 26%, and 17% respectively. Even when evaluated at the lower 64³ resolution matching prior art, ADAVOMP still leads VoMP on all six metrics (e.g., ALDE 0.3278 vs. 0.3793 for E). On the harder GVT-HARD subset at 1024³, the gap widens further: ADAVOMP achieves ALDE 1.2440 for E versus VoMP's 1.6680 (25% improvement) and Pixie's 1.8950 (34% improvement). The method also improves mass estimation on ABO-500 (ARE 0.512 vs. VoMP's 0.887) and maintains on-par material validity with VoMP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADAVOMP outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric at both 64³ and 1024³ resolutions. The closest competitor, VoMP (Dagli et al., 2025), is surpassed by 13–22% on ALDE for E and 17–26% on ADE for ν at 1024³; the gap widens on the harder GVT-HARD subset where VoMP's fixed 64³ grid misses fine geometric details entirely. Pixie (Le et al., 2025) is the second-closest baseline but degrades more sharply at 1024³ (ALDE 1.2264 vs. ADAVOMP's 0.8841 for E), and consistently predicts softer materials. The NeRF-based methods (NeRF2Physics, PUGS) and Phys4DGen are substantially worse, with ALDE values 4–8× higher for E at 1024³, and PUGS and Phys4DGen fail to report Poisson's ratio at all. Notably, ADAVOMP's B+ model (similar parameter count to VoMP) already outperforms VoMP, confirming that the gains stem from the adaptive SAV representation rather than simply more parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADAVOMP directly extends VoMP (Dagli et al., 2025) by replacing its fixed-resolution 64³ voxel grid with a sparse adaptive voxel tree (SAV), enabling 16× higher effective resolution (1024³) while reusing VoMP's MatVAE decoder for physically plausible material outputs. The autoregressive coarse-to-fine generation over an octree-like structure connects to recent work on octree-based shape generation (OctGPT, Deng et al. 2025), but differs by maintaining explicit 3D spatial structure throughout generation rather than serializing to 1D token sequences. The adaptive representation draws on neural geometric level-of-detail ideas (Takikawa et al., 2021) but is specifically optimized for material field prediction, where large homogeneous regions (e.g., a metal frame) can be represented by single coarse voxels. The encoder leverages DINOv3 multi-view features with depth-attenuated aggregation and sparse windowed attention (Swin Transformer lineage), departing from prior uniform-averaging approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling volumetric material prediction at 1024³ resolution with only ~9% of the voxels a dense grid would require, ADAVOMP makes it practical to convert high-resolution 3D assets (meshes, Gaussian splats) into simulation-ready objects for robotics and physical AI pipelines, removing the manual parameter-tuning bottleneck. Key limitations include: the method is restricted to static assets (no dynamic video cues), predicts only linear-elasticity parameters (E, ν, ρ) without anisotropic tensor fields or plasticity/thermal properties, and accuracy is bounded by the training dataset size and VLM annotation quality. Open questions include whether the adaptive structure generalizes to truly directional materials, whether predictions can be adapted to non-physical parameter scales for real-time simulators, and how the approach extends to dynamic scenes where physical cues from video could disambiguate materials.
