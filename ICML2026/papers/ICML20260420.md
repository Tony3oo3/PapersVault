## Problem

Concept unlearning in diffusion models is hampered by feature splitting, where each visual concept is distributed across many latent features rather than localized to a single neuron. This forces existing SAE-based unlearning methods (e.g., SAeUron) to perform exhaustive searches over combinations of latents and multiplier values at inference, making erasure computationally expensive and prone to unintended side effects on related concepts. The paper addresses this gap by asking whether supervised training can enforce strict one-to-one concept-to-latent mappings, eliminating the need for combinatorial latent search.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes d-dimensional activation vectors extracted from cross-attention blocks (specifically up.1.1) of Stable Diffusion v1.5 across all 50 denoising timesteps, with each vector labeled by the object and style concepts present in its generating prompt. At inference, the user provides a text prompt and a set of concepts to erase; the SAE is attached non-destructively to the diffusion model, and only the single assigned latent per target concept is steered (multiplied by a negative scalar) during the final denoising steps. The output is a generated image in which the target concepts are suppressed while unrelated objects, styles, and overall generation quality are preserved; detaching the SAE fully restores the original model.

## Method

SAEmnesia trains a TopK sparse autoencoder (k=32, expansion factor 16) in two phases: first standard unsupervised SAE training to establish sparse representations, then supervised fine-tuning with a composite loss that adds a Concept Assignment (CA) term and a multi-group Decorrelation (DC) term on top of the reconstruction and L1 sparsity losses. The CA loss applies sigmoid cross-entropy exclusively to the pre-activations of latents assigned to concepts present in each sample, directly encouraging those specific neurons to fire strongly for their target concept. The DC loss penalizes Pearson correlation between activation vectors of latents belonging to different macro-groups (objects vs. styles), promoting disentanglement across semantic categories. Concept-to-latent assignment is determined by a score function measuring each latent's differential activation on concept-present vs. concept-absent data. At inference, erasing a concept requires steering exactly one latent (multiplying its activation by a negative multiplier normalized by the concept's mean activation), reducing the hyperparameter search from 210 evaluations (7 multipliers × 30 latent combinations) to just 7, a 96.67% reduction.

## Results

On the UnlearnCanvas benchmark for object erasure, SAEmnesia achieves 94.65% Unlearning Accuracy (UA), 91.39% In-domain Retain Accuracy (IRA), and 88.48% Cross-domain Retain Accuracy (CRA), yielding a 91.51% average — a +9.22-point improvement over the SAE-based baseline SAeUron (82.29%). The method reduces inference-time hyperparameter search by 96.67% (from 210 to 7 evaluations) and achieves 92.4% accuracy in 9-object sequential unlearning versus 64% for SAeUron. Under adversarial attacks, SAEmnesia retains 57.50% UA (UnlearnDiffAtk) and 97% UA (Ring-A-Bell) compared to 34.20% and 79.50% for SAeUron, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary SAE-based baseline SAeUron (Cywiński & Deja, 2025), SAEmnesia wins on all three metrics (UA +7.49, IRA +5.82, CRA +14.34) and reduces the inference search space from 210 to 7 evaluations. Among non-SAE methods, SAEmnesia outperforms ESD (64.05%), FMN (69.91%), UCE (56.11%), CA (72.92%), SEOT (67.18%), SPM (81.23%), EDiff (76.39%), and SHS (76.62%) on average score; however, SalUn (94.28%) edges out SAEmnesia (91.51%) on object-only unlearning, though SAEmnesia achieves the best combined object+style average (94.85% vs. 92.43% in Table 15). On the SDXL Turbo backbone (Surkov et al., 2026), SAEmnesia substantially outperforms the unsupervised SAE baseline (63.63% UA vs. 13.92%), confirming that the supervised signal generalizes beyond SD v1.5. In adversarial robustness, SAEmnesia retains 57.50% UA under UnlearnDiffAtk (vs. 34.20% for SAeUron) and 97% under Ring-A-Bell (vs. 79.50%), and in nudity suppression on I2P, SAEMNESIA-TOP2 achieves 9 detections versus SAeUron's 18 while steering only 2 latents instead of 205.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAEmnesia is a direct supervised extension of SAeUron (Cywiński & Deja, 2025), the state-of-the-art SAE-based unlearning method for diffusion models. While SAeUron trains a TopK SAE (Makhzani & Frey, 2013) unsupervised on cross-attention activations and then performs post-hoc concept-latent discovery via a score function, SAEmnesia injects explicit concept labels during training to enforce one-to-one concept-latent mappings, directly addressing the feature-splitting problem identified in the monosemanticity literature (Bricken et al., 2023). The architectural backbone (TopK SAE on U-Net cross-attention features) and the inference-time steering mechanism are inherited from SAeUron; the novelty lies entirely in the supervised loss design (Concept Assignment + multi-group Decorrelation) that reshapes the latent geometry during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-to-one mapping yields a concrete practical benefit: a 96.67% reduction in inference-time hyperparameter search (7 evaluations vs. 210), making SAE-based unlearning substantially cheaper at scale. The method is also non-destructive and fully reversible (detach the SAE to restore the original model), which is valuable for iterative or regulatory-driven unlearning workflows. Key open questions remain: the approach is validated only on U-Net-based SD v1.5, the closed-vocabulary constraint requires retraining or score-function lookup for new concepts, within-group interference between semantically similar concepts persists, and scalability to open-vocabulary settings (20K+ concepts) or transformer-based diffusion backbones (FLUX) is untested. The preliminary SDXL Turbo results (63.63% UA vs. 94.65% on SD v1.5) suggest the evaluation protocol and classifier transferability are non-trivial challenges for broader adoption.
