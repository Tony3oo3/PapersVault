## Problem

Frontier language models increasingly occlude their Chain-of-Thought reasoning, exposing only action trajectories, which breaks standard distillation pipelines (SFT + RL) that assume full CoT access. More fundamentally, even when training-time privileged information (PI) enables a model to succeed on hard tasks, transferring those capabilities to a policy that must act without PI at inference time remains an open challenge, particularly in long-horizon multi-turn agentic settings where the student's sampling distribution can shift dramatically from the PI-conditioned teacher's.

## Input / Output

The agent receives an evolving interaction context (initial user prompt, its own past outputs, and environment/tool responses) as the state, with PI injected into the system prompt during training in one of three forms: full tool calls with arguments, tool-call names only, or self-generated summary hints. The model outputs interleaved reasoning tokens and action tokens (tool calls) over multiple turns, operating under a 25k-token context window with a length penalty for trajectories exceeding 15k tokens. PI is derived from successful DeepSeek V3.1 trajectories, selecting the shortest successful trace per task.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary algorithms that use a single shared-parameter model as both a PI-conditioned teacher and an unconditioned student. π-Distill jointly optimizes a teacher objective (maximize reward under a reverse-KL penalty toward the student, encouraging the teacher to fit high-reward modes familiar to the student) and a student objective (importance-weighted reward from teacher-sampled trajectories plus a forward-KL penalty pulling the student toward the teacher), combined via a mixing coefficient α. OPSD instead samples on-policy from the student and regularizes with a reverse-KL penalty between the student and the stop-gradient PI-conditioned teacher, acting as a dense per-token reward. Both are built on GRPO for the RL component and can be derived from a Variational EM perspective where the teacher approximates a reward-tilted posterior and the student distills from it. The key design choice—parameter sharing between teacher and student—enables simultaneous learning to exploit PI and to act without it, avoiding the distribution shift and computational overhead of sequential two-model distillation.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B, π-Distill (α=1) achieves 44.1% on Travel Planner, outperforming the industry-standard SFT w/CoT+RL (32.3%) by 11.8 points, while π-Distill (α=0) reaches 31.1% on τ-Bench Retail vs. 29.1% for SFT w/CoT+RL. OPSD achieves the best OOD generalization on τ-Bench Airline at 14.0% (vs. 8.0% for SFT w/CoT+RL). On Qwen3-4B, π-Distill (α=0.5) reaches 33.8% on Travel Planner vs. 26.4% for SFT w/CoT+RL, and OPSD achieves 10.6% on τ-Bench Airline vs. 6.67% for SFT w/CoT+RL. All results are reported as mean ± std over three seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SFT w/CoT+RL, the industry-standard pipeline that assumes access to the frontier model's full Chain-of-Thought traces. π-Distill consistently outperforms this baseline across all three benchmarks for Qwen3-8B (e.g., 44.1% vs. 32.3% on Travel Planner, 31.1% vs. 29.1% on τ-Bench Retail), and for Qwen3-4B on Travel Planner (33.8% vs. 26.4%). OPSD also surpasses SFT w/CoT+RL on τ-Bench Airline OOD (14.0% vs. 8.0% for Qwen3-8B; 10.6% vs. 6.67% for Qwen3-4B), showing strong out-of-domain transfer. Against the sequential variational EM approach of Zhou et al. (2025) (compared in App. E.2, Figure 11), the paper's joint π-Distill with shared parameters outperforms both the standard EM-RFT variant and the off-policy RL variant on τ-Bench Retail. Weaker baselines (plain RL, SFT w/o CoT+RL) lag significantly behind both π-Distill and OPSD, confirming that PI provides a substantial learning signal beyond what the model can discover through exploration alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three threads: the LUPI (Learning Using Privileged Information) paradigm from Vapnik & Vashist (2009), the variational EM framework for policy optimization from Zhou et al. (2025), and on-policy distillation via reverse KL from Agarwal et al. (2024). The key departure from Zhou et al. is collapsing the separate teacher and student into a single shared-parameter model, eliminating the need for dual parameter sets and sequential training phases. The key departure from standard distillation is that the teacher's privileged signal is restricted to action trajectories (tool calls) rather than full Chain-of-Thought, reflecting the practical constraint that frontier models occlude their reasoning. GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) serves as the underlying RL optimizer for both proposed objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a concrete industry bottleneck: frontier model providers (e.g., OpenAI) restrict access to CoT traces, making standard SFT+RL distillation pipelines infeasible. The finding that action-only PI can match or exceed full-CoT supervision suggests that the reasoning process is less critical for transfer than the behavioral pattern it produces, potentially reshaping how practitioners approach agent distillation. Limitations include evaluation only on ≤8B models, PI always derived from a single frontier model (DeepSeek-V3.1), and the Section 7 analysis being observational rather than controlled. Open questions include scaling to larger models, obtaining useful PI without any frontier traces, and systematically characterizing which PI properties drive success.
