## Problem

Large-scale cooperative MARL requires inter-agent communication to overcome partial observability, but existing methods face a fundamental trade-off: dense or all-to-all communication patterns become intractable as agent counts grow, while fixed topologies (e.g., static exponential graphs) cannot adapt link allocation to task dynamics. No prior approach simultaneously guarantees bounded per-agent communication cost, logarithmic-hop global reachability, and task-adaptive sparse link selection that transfers across population sizes without topology redesign.

## Input / Output

Each agent's input is its local observation (a state vector) processed into a hidden state via a recurrent encoder, combined with a fixed exponential-graph adjacency that restricts candidate neighbors to O(log N) agents at powers-of-two index offsets. The pairwise scorer consumes concatenated hidden-state pairs plus a fixed edge-type embedding to produce a scalar score per candidate edge. The method outputs a binary sparse communication mask (O(N log N) active edges rather than N²), per-agent communication messages aggregated via scaled cross-attention from the previous timestep, and discrete actions from a CTDE value-based policy (QMIX, QPLEX, or SHAQ).

## Method

SOPS decouples scalability from adaptivity in two layers: a fixed exponential-graph backbone (diameter ⌈log₂(N−1)⌉, degree ⌈log₂ N⌉ per agent) guarantees near-linear communication cost and logarithmic-hop reachability at any scale, while a shared lightweight pairwise scoring network f(h_i‖h_j‖e_ij) learns a task-conditioned Bernoulli probability for each backbone candidate edge. Discrete link selection is made differentiable via Gumbel-Sigmoid reparameterization with a straight-through estimator and a linearly annealed temperature (1.0→0.3), enabling end-to-end gradient flow through the discrete mask. Selected messages are aggregated with scaled cross-attention and fused with the agent's hidden state to condition the CTDE value function. An auxiliary grounding objective—global-state prediction (MAgent) or InfoNCE contrastive loss (IMP)—prevents degenerate communication by encouraging messages to encode multi-hop, task-relevant information, and the total loss combines the TD value-decomposition loss with the auxiliary term weighted by α.

## Results

On the IMP benchmark, SOPS achieves the best normalized discounted reward across all three scenarios at both N=50 and N=100 agents, with margins of 1.4–4.5 points over the next-best method (e.g., 45.59 vs. 44.02 on Correlated-50, 29.21 vs. 28.62 on Uncorrelated-100). On MAgent, SOPS consistently attains the highest win rate and return across Battle-64/100/121 and AdversarialPursuit-101, with AUC values of 0.51–0.60 (Battle) and 31.65 (APurs-101), substantially exceeding EC-O (0.39–0.49, 21.61) and EC-S (0.08–0.18, 18.82). SOPS also demonstrates robust zero-shot transfer to larger populations (e.g., 42→121 agents) and the smallest performance degradation under 50% message dropout (0.74 vs. 0.46 for EC-O on Battle-100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOPS outperforms all four direct communication baselines (VDN, GACG, EC-O, EC-S) on every IMP scenario at both scales. VDN, which uses no explicit communication, degrades catastrophically at N=100 (e.g., −54.80 on Correlated), while GACG's group-based architecture is incompatible with zero-shot transfer to different population sizes. EC-O (one-peer exponential) is the strongest runner-up but consistently trails SOPS by 0.5–3.5 points and converges more slowly with larger variance on MAgent. EC-S (static full exponential) plateaus lower, suggesting a denser fixed backbone is less sample-efficient. In the plugability study (Table 2), attaching SOPS to QMIX, QPLEX, or SHAQ consistently improves each backbone (e.g., QMIX 24.13→29.10 on Uncorrelated-50; SHAQ −7.82→15.46), confirming SOPS provides complementary gains independent of the value-decomposition learner.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOPS sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) CTDE value-decomposition learners (QMIX, QPLEX, SHAQ) that provide the decision backbone; (2) learned inter-agent communication, which has evolved from differentiable channels (Sukhbaatar et al., 2016; Das et al., 2019) through attention-based aggregation (Iqbal & Sha, 2019) to recent adaptive-structure methods like GACG (Duan et al., 2024) and GTDE (Li et al., 2025a); and (3) exponential-graph topologies originally studied for decentralized distributed training (Ying et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2021) and recently adapted to MARL by ExpoComm (Li et al., 2025b). The key conceptual departure is the explicit decoupling of scalability (fixed small-diameter exponential backbone bounding per-agent candidates to O(log N)) from task adaptivity (learned pairwise scoring with Gumbel-Sigmoid sampling over that backbone), whereas prior methods either start from full connectivity and prune (GTDE, Chiun et al., 2025) or use a fixed topology without learned selection (ExpoComm). The Gumbel reparameterization with linear temperature annealing and straight-through estimator is borrowed from the discrete-decision literature (Jang et al., 2017; Maddison et al., 2017) and applied here to topology selection rather than action selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot transfer to larger agent populations without retraining is the most practically significant result: it means a communication policy trained at, say, 42 agents can be deployed at 121 agents with only moderate performance degradation, whereas architecture-tied methods like GACG cannot transfer at all. This addresses a real deployment bottleneck in large-scale MARL where the agent population may change between training and operation. However, the paper's scope is limited to homogeneous agents on two benchmarks (MAgent, IMP), and the authors acknowledge that strongly heterogeneous, role-rich environments and adversarial topology disruptions remain untested. Whether the exponential backbone is truly optimal versus other small-diameter topologies, and whether the pairwise scoring generalizes beyond value-based CTDE to policy-gradient or transformer-based MARL, are open questions that will determine the method's broader impact.
