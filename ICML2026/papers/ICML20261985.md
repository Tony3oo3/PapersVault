## Problem

Predicting high-order crystal tensor properties (order 2–4) end-to-end from atomic structures is computationally expensive under the dominant spherical-harmonic equivariant paradigm, where Clebsch-Gordan tensor products scale poorly with tensor order. Existing Cartesian alternatives either stack deep tensor message-passing layers or explicitly enumerate angular triplets, limiting scalability, and are rarely extended to full end-to-end high-order tensor prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261985/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a periodic crystal graph built from lattice parameters and atomic fractional coordinates, with directed edges carrying displacement vectors within a 16th-nearest-neighbor cutoff. It produces a Cartesian tensor of order r ∈ {2, 3, 4} (dielectric, piezoelectric, or elastic) with intrinsic index symmetries enforced by construction (e.g., C_ijkl = C_jikl = C_ijlk = C_klij for elasticity), and optionally applies a space-group symmetry post-processing step at inference.

## Method

CEITNet decouples invariant encoding from equivariant tensor construction: a ComformerConv message-passing backbone learns scalar node representations, while Cartesian geometric bases (n_ij, Q_ij, I and their tensor products) are assembled only at the output head. For each atom, a K-channel local environment tensor is built by aggregating neighbor contributions weighted by learned channel coefficients, and a learnable K×K channel-interaction matrix M couples two environment streams to induce effective three-body (angular) correlations without explicit triplet enumeration. Task-specific coupling operators (matrix multiplication for order-2, outer product for order-4, vector⊗matrix for order-3) then assemble the final equivariant tensor, with all learnable transformations confined to channel space to avoid propagating high-order equivariant features through the network.

## Results

On the dielectric tensor benchmark, CEITNet achieves Fnorm 2.87 and EwT5% 39.3%, outperforming all baselines (best prior: GeoCTP at Fnorm 3.23, EwT5% 35.5%). On the piezoelectric tensor, CEITNet reduces Fnorm to 0.517 (vs. 0.752 for GMTNet) and raises EwT25% from 6.29% to 21.98%. On the elastic tensor, CEITNet achieves the best EwT scores (EwT25% 70.6%, EwT10% 32.2%, EwT5% 14.4%) while remaining competitive on Fnorm (70.11 vs. GMTNet's 67.38). Inference throughput reaches 85.9 items/s on dielectric, surpassing GeoCTP (69.6) and GMTNet (22.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CEITNet outperforms ETGNN (Zhong et al., 2023) across all three tensor tasks and all metrics, with the largest gap on piezoelectric prediction where ETGNN achieves 0% EwT25% versus CEITNet's 21.98%. Against GMTNet (Yan et al., 2024), CEITNet wins decisively on dielectric (Fnorm 2.87 vs. 3.50) and piezoelectric (Fnorm 0.517 vs. 0.752), and on elastic it achieves superior EwT scores (EwT25% 70.6% vs. 66.1%) despite a slightly higher Fnorm (70.11 vs. 67.38), which the authors attribute to a small number of hard outliers. Compared to GeoCTP (Hua et al., 2026), CEITNet wins on all three tasks: dielectric Fnorm 2.87 vs. 3.23, piezoelectric Fnorm 0.517 vs. 0.778, and elastic EwT5% 14.4% vs. 10.4%. CEITNet also matches or exceeds GeoCTP on inference speed (85.9 vs. 69.6 items/s on dielectric) while using fewer parameters (0.6M vs. 0.7M for GMTNet). On the external Materials Project dielectric benchmark, CEITNet further outperforms GMTNet (Fnorm 0.188 vs. 0.202, EwT5% 62.4% vs. 58.1%), and in leave-one-crystal-system-out experiments it consistently beats GMTNet across all six held-out systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

CEITNet sits at the intersection of two active research threads: Cartesian tensor representations for equivariant GNNs (TensorNet, HotPP, Moment Tensor Potentials) and the density-product principle from ACE/MACE for inducing many-body interactions without explicit tuple enumeration. It departs from spherical-harmonic equivariant methods (GMTNet, ETGNN) by avoiding Clebsch-Gordan tensor products entirely, and from canonicalization-based approaches (GeoCTP) by maintaining strict O(3) equivariance without a deterministic alignment step. The key architectural departure is a decoupled design: an invariant message-passing backbone (ComformerConv, shared with GMTNet/GeoCTP for fair comparison) feeds a task-specific tensor head that introduces equivariance only at the output stage via Cartesian geometric bases and a learnable channel-interaction matrix, rather than propagating high-order equivariant features through the entire network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

High-order crystal tensor prediction (dielectric, piezoelectric, elastic) is a bottleneck in computational materials screening because DFT-based methods scale cubically or worse with system size; a fast, accurate, equivariant predictor directly accelerates discovery pipelines. The modular design—swappable invariant backbone, task-specific tensor head, and optional symmetry-correction post-processing—makes the framework practical for extending to new tensor properties, though the authors acknowledge that coupling-template design for each new task still requires non-trivial engineering. Open questions include whether the 3-body interaction limit is sufficient for all material classes, how the framework scales to higher-order tensors (order ≥ 5), and whether the Cartesian basis selection can be made more systematic to reduce per-task hyperparameter tuning.
