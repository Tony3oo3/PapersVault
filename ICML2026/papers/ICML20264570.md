## Problem

Existing visual token pruning methods for conventional VLMs—relying on attention scores, textual relevance, or inter-token diversity—fail catastrophically on DeepSeek-OCR because its retrained vision encoder weakens vision-language alignment and OCR demands exhaustive, high-fidelity text reconstruction rather than sparse query-driven answers. The paper identifies that no prior pruning strategy accounts for the distinct two-stage 'reading twice' attention trajectory observed in DeepSeek-OCR's LLM decoding, where shallow layers focus on high-norm tokens and deeper layers redistribute attention to the remainder.

## Input / Output

The method operates on document images at resolutions from 512×512 (Tiny, 64 tokens) to 1280×1280 (Large, 400 tokens), which are encoded by DeepSeek-OCR's DeepEncoder (SAM + convolutional compressor + CLIP) into a sequence of visual tokens. Pruning is applied pre-prefill, producing a reduced token set (e.g., 337 average tokens from 400 under dynamic pruning) that is fed to the MoE LLM decoder to generate full-page markdown output. The dynamic pruning ratio is computed per-image from inter-token feature similarity and Sobel-based textual density, so the number of retained tokens varies with content complexity.

## Method

RTPrune is a training-free, two-stage token pruning pipeline that mimics the LLM's dual-pass reading behavior. In Stage 1 (Dominant Token Selection), the top-M tokens are retained by embedding ℓ2-norm, which the paper shows correlates strongly with LLM attention in shallow layers. In Stage 2 (Optimal Token Merging), the discarded tokens are matched to kept tokens via a Sinkhorn optimal transport formulation with a SuperGlue-style dustbin row/column (fixed parameter z=0.2), and the matched features are aggregated into kept tokens via a weighted sum with merge strength α=0.1. A dynamic pruning ratio r_dyn = f_normalize(φ)(1−ρ) is computed from average inter-token cosine similarity φ and Sobel-based textual density ρ, enabling content-adaptive compression that prunes background-heavy regions more aggressively while preserving dense text.

## Results

On OmniDocBench with DeepSeek-OCR-Large, RTPrune with dynamic pruning achieves 85.10 overall score (99.47% of the 85.55 baseline) while pruning 15.75% of tokens, yielding 1.23× faster prefill and 15.29% GFLOPs reduction. Under a fixed 25% pruning ratio, RTPrune retains 83.60 overall (96.6% of baseline), far exceeding all baselines. On olmOCR-Bench with dynamic pruning, RTPrune achieves 73.9±1.1 overall (97.88% of the 75.5±1.0 baseline) with 84% token retention, outperforming the next-best method (VisionZip at 59.7±1.1) by 14.2 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTPrune decisively outperforms all eight baselines across every DeepSeek-OCR variant and both fixed/dynamic pruning settings. On OmniDocBench (DeepSeek-OCR-Large, dynamic), RTPrune scores 85.10 overall versus FitPrune (56.63), NUWA (8.71), DivPrune (not listed for dynamic on Large), and CDPruner (not listed for dynamic on Large); under fixed 25% pruning, RTPrune (83.60) beats DivPrune (70.02) and CDPruner (71.19) by 12–14 points. On olmOCR-Bench (DeepSeek-OCR-Large, dynamic), RTPrune (73.9) surpasses VisionZip (59.7), SparseVLM (32.0), FastV (25.2), and DART (25.6) by 14–49 points. The gap is most dramatic for LLM-side attention methods (FastV, DART, FitPrune, SparseVLM, NUWA), which collapse to near-zero scores because they prune tokens at shallow layers before the LLM's deeper 'second reading pass' can recover complementary cues. Vision-encoder-side methods (VisionZip, DivPrune, CDPruner) perform better but still trail RTPrune by 10–20 points, as they lack the optimal-transport merging step that recovers information from pruned tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant visual token pruning literature for VLMs (FastV, DivPrune, VisionZip, SparseVLM, CDPruner), which relies on attention scores, inter-token diversity, or prompt-textual relevance to select tokens. The authors demonstrate that these criteria fundamentally break down on DeepSeek-OCR because its retrained encoder weakens vision-language alignment and OCR demands exhaustive reconstruction rather than sparse query-relevant retrieval. Conceptually, RTPrune repurposes the dustbin-augmented optimal transport mechanism from SuperGlue (Sarlin et al., 2020) for feature merging, and introduces L2-norm of encoder embeddings as a selection proxy grounded in an observed two-stage 'reading' attention pattern within the LLM, rather than directly reading attention weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying DeepSeek-OCR or similar end-to-end OCR VLMs, RTPrune offers a plug-and-play, training-free acceleration (1.23× prefill speedup at 99.47% accuracy) that is directly applicable without retraining. The finding that standard VLM pruning methods degrade below random baselines on OCR-oriented models is an important caution for the broader VLM efficiency community. However, the method is explicitly tailored to DeepSeek-OCR's architecture, and the authors acknowledge that efficiency gains are less pronounced than in VQA settings; whether the norm-based selection and dynamic ratio generalize to future OCR model families with different encoder designs remains an open question.
