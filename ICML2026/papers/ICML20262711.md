## Problem

Simultaneously editing scene objects (insertion, deletion, replacement, repositioning) and the ego-trajectory in driving videos—termed unified editing—remains unsolved because it requires reconciling two conflicting demands: generative flexibility for photorealistic object synthesis and explicit 3D geometric precision for viewpoint-consistent trajectory control. Existing methods handle either object manipulation or trajectory editing in isolation, and those that incorporate diffusion into reconstruction pipelines restrict it to texture refinement rather than semantic creation for substantial edits.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 5-camera surround-view driving video (1280×704, 29 frames), a synchronized LiDAR point cloud sequence, user-specified target 3D bounding boxes with associated 3D assets (from scanned datasets or text-to-3D pipelines), a target ego-pose sequence in SE(3), and a text prompt. It produces an edited multi-view driving video in a single inference pass, performing all object manipulations and the trajectory shift jointly rather than sequentially. The 3D assets are agnostic to provenance, and the ego-trajectory supports both gradual transitions (lane-change-like) and fixed lateral offsets.

## Method

SceneDirector extends the Cosmos-Transfer2.5 DiT backbone (Rectified Flow objective) with two conditioning streams bridged by a novel Mask-Gated Reference Attention (MGRA) module. The Unified Geometric Scaffold is constructed by fusing LiDAR-guided depth completion (DMD3C) with editable 3D assets, then rendered under the target ego-trajectory via pinhole projection with Z-buffering to yield a geometric video V_geo and a four-level uncertainty mask M (sensor-verified, depth-completed, asset, void). The source video is encoded into a frozen Static Texture Bank (shared key-value memory across all DiT blocks). In each control block, geometric features serve as queries attending to the texture bank, while a learned reliability gate G = σ(F([h_l; Ψ(M)]))—conditioned on both the uncertainty embedding and the evolving feature content—modulates the residual injection: suppressing reference texture in sensor-verified regions (G→0) to enforce geometric constraints, and promoting it in semantically complex or occluded regions (G→1) to synthesize photorealistic details. Training is self-supervised: synthetic pairs are constructed by warping the ground-truth video to create a misaligned reference and a degraded scaffold via round-trip projection, with 3D asset regions masked in the reference to prevent shortcut learning.

## Results

On object editing (Table 1), SceneDirector achieves the best geometric alignment in both Single-Edit (ATE 0.78 m, AOE 0.052) and Multi-Edit (ATE 0.81 m, AOE 0.052) settings, while also attaining the lowest FVD (516.83 vs. 729.42 for VACE, 818.48 for DriveEditor) in Multi-Edit. On ego-trajectory editing (Table 2), SceneDirector outperforms all diffusion-based baselines: under 2 m Gradual Transition it reaches FID 34.48 / FVD 476.1, compared to FreeVS (79.25 / 1208.9) and GEM (42.90 / 667.9). In the multi-view setting, SceneDirector-MV achieves FVD 445.5 under 2 m Gradual Transition, substantially ahead of FreeVS-MV (898.5) and StreetGaussian-MV (549.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VACE-14B (a 14B-parameter general-purpose video editor), SceneDirector wins decisively on geometric fidelity (ATE 0.78 m vs. 1.02 m in Single-Edit; 0.81 m vs. 1.09 m in Multi-Edit) and FVD in Multi-Edit (516.83 vs. 729.42), while VACE edges ahead slightly on FID in Single-Edit (35.04 vs. 35.83). Against DriveEditor (a driving-specific 3D-layout diffusion editor), SceneDirector dominates on all Multi-Edit metrics (FID 38.29 vs. 51.43; FVD 516.83 vs. 818.48) and on ATE/AOE in Single-Edit (0.78/0.052 vs. 0.86/0.071), though DriveEditor achieves a marginally lower FID in Single-Edit (42.75 vs. 35.83 — actually SceneDirector is better here too). For trajectory editing, SceneDirector substantially outperforms FreeVS (FVD 476.1 vs. 1208.9 at 2 m Gradual) and GEM (FVD 476.1 vs. 667.9), and improves lane-detection F1 over FreeVS (55.1 vs. 40.3). Against reconstruction-based StreetGaussian and StreetCrafter, SceneDirector alone is slightly behind on FID/FVD (34.48/476.1 vs. 27.37/434.9 and 26.36/418.2 at 2 m Gradual), but the augmented SceneDirector+SG variant surpasses both (FID 25.70, FVD 400.1; F1 60.4), confirming that SceneDirector's outputs provide reliable multi-view supervision for 3DGS reconstruction. In the multi-view trajectory setting, SceneDirector-MV outperforms FreeVS-MV by a wide margin (FVD 445.5 vs. 898.5) and StreetGaussian-MV (445.5 vs. 549.5) under 2 m Gradual Transition.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneDirector builds on the Cosmos-Transfer2.5 DiT-based video world model, extending it with a geometric conditioning pathway. Conceptually, it departs from two parallel lines of work: reconstruction-based driving scene methods (StreetGaussian, StreetCrafter) that enforce geometric fidelity but lack generative capacity for occluded regions, and diffusion-based editing methods (DriveEditor, GEM, FreeVS) that offer photorealistic synthesis but suffer from geometric drift under viewpoint changes. The paper's key architectural departure is the Mask-Gated Reference Attention mechanism, which uses a discrete sensor-uncertainty mask to dynamically gate texture injection from a Static Texture Bank into the geometric scaffold stream—effectively reconciling the two paradigms within a single diffusion backbone rather than cascading them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to perform simultaneous object manipulation and ego-trajectory editing in a single inference pass is practically important for autonomous driving validation, where reactive scenario generation requires both local and global scene modifications. The paper demonstrates that unified editing introduces only marginal geometric degradation (ATE 0.90m → 0.95m) over object-only editing, suggesting the two control signals are largely decoupled in practice. Key limitations include the LiDAR dependency (excluding camera-only applications) and the 5-camera Waymo setup, which may require fine-tuning for other sensor configurations. An open question is whether the geometric scaffold approach generalizes to more extreme counterfactual edits (e.g., entirely new road topologies) where no LiDAR coverage exists.
