## Problem

Existing in-context object localization methods in VLMs rely on category names or pseudo-labels, which introduce semantic priors that bias predictions toward category-level reasoning rather than instance-specific visual grounding. When multiple objects of the same category appear in a scene, models fail to localize the correct instance defined by the support examples. This paper addresses the gap by removing all textual category information and enforcing localization through purely visual correspondence.

## Input / Output

The model receives an interleaved prompt of T images: the first T−1 are support images each paired with a bounding box annotation serialized as text tokens (e.g., [x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max]), and the final image is the query with no annotation. No category names, object descriptions, or textual identifiers are included. The output is a single bounding box in normalized 0–1000 coordinate space, emitted in the structured format <answer>[x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max]</answer>. Bounding boxes are treated as text tokens by the tokenizer, which is what the attention loss exploits to identify BBOX token positions deterministically.

## Method

FOCUS is a two-stage training framework applied to a frozen VLM (Qwen2-VL-7B) via LoRA adapters. Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning with a novel BBOX attention loss: for each query image token, the model's aggregated attention (averaged over all layers and heads) is split into mass assigned to BBOX annotation tokens versus all other tokens, and a squared hinge loss enforces a positive margin (Δ_i = p_i⁺ − p_i⁻ > μ), pushing query tokens to preferentially attend to spatial annotations. Stage 2 refines bounding box alignment using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), where G sampled predictions per query are scored by a combined reward of IoU against ground truth plus a binary formatting reward, and the policy is updated via clipped group-relative advantages without a learned critic. The key design choice is that all category names are removed from both support and query prompts, so the model must infer the target exclusively from visual appearance and bounding box geometry across the support set.

## Results

On the primary in-context localization benchmarks (TAO, GOT-10k, ICL-LaSOT), FOCUS (7B) achieves an average mIoU of 67.7%, outperforming the next-best baseline IPLoc-7B (58.8%) by 8.9 points and substantially exceeding much larger models: Qwen2-VL-72B (55.0%) and InternVL2-76B (56.1%). In the 4-shot setting, FOCUS reaches 68.5% on TAO, 82.6% on GOT-10k, and 65.6% on ICL-LaSOT. On domain-shift datasets (PerSeg, PerMIRS), FOCUS further extends its lead, achieving 83.2% average mIoU versus 68.2% for IPLoc-7B and 75.6% for Qwen2-VL-72B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOCUS (7B) outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and shot setting. The most striking comparison is against Qwen2-VL-72B: FOCUS exceeds it by 12.7% average mIoU (67.7 vs. 55.0), demonstrating that the attention-based localization objective surpasses a 10× model scale-up. Against InternVL2-76B, FOCUS leads by 11.6% on average, with the largest gap on ICL-LaSOT (65.6 vs. 52.5 in 4-shot). The closest competitor, IPLoc-7B, is beaten by 8.9% on average; the gap widens to 19.1% on PerMIRS and 13.5% on PerSeg (2-shot), where category ambiguity is highest. FOCUS also surpasses IPLoc-72B (Real+Pseudo) on TAO by 4.7 points in the 4-shot setting (72.35 vs. 67.63). Smaller baselines (Idefics3, Pixtral-12B, LLaVA-OV, vanilla Qwen2-VL-7B) trail by 30–50+ points, confirming that in-context localization requires explicit visual grounding objectives rather than general VLM capability alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends IPLoc (Doveh et al., 2025), which introduced in-context object localization in VLMs using pseudo-labels to reduce category bias, by removing all category names from both training and inference and instead optimizing attention maps over bounding-box tokens. For the refinement stage, it repurposes GRPO (Shao et al., 2024; DeepSeek-AI, 2024), originally designed for mathematical reasoning in LLMs, as a bounding-box alignment objective with IoU and format rewards. Conceptually, the work departs from the broader semantic grounding literature (GLIP, Grounding DINO, CLIP-VG) that assumes targets can be specified linguistically, reframing localization as a purely visual correspondence problem solvable at inference time without parameter updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 7B model with targeted attention and RL objectives outperforms 72B general-purpose VLMs on in-context localization suggests that task-specific training signals can be more effective than raw scale for grounded visual reasoning, a finding relevant to resource-constrained deployment. The category-agnostic formulation opens the door to localizing unnamed, instance-specific, or domain-specific objects that lack stable textual descriptions, which is essential for interactive editing and personalized search. Limitations include reliance on video tracking datasets for evaluation (where temporal continuity may aid localization), the modest 12% VRAM overhead during training, and the open question of whether the early-layer attention finding generalizes across architectures and task complexities beyond bounding boxes.
