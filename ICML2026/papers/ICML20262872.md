## Problem

Existing neural surrogates for vehicle aerodynamics (Transolver, GeoTransolver, AB-UPT) achieve competitive prediction accuracy but are fundamentally prediction-only, offering no mechanism for inverse design. Meanwhile, generative optimization methods (PhysGen, TripOptimizer) attempt shape refinement but lack engineering constraints, producing asymmetric or topologically broken geometries whose apparent drag reductions vanish under CFD re-verification. UniDrag bridges this gap by unifying multi-field prediction with constraint-aware differentiable shape optimization in a single pipeline.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a vehicle geometry represented as ~50,000 surface points (with outward normals) and ~200,000 volume query points in the surrounding flow domain, plus global flow conditions (Reynolds number, freestream velocity). It jointly predicts surface pressure, 3D volume velocity, a scalar drag coefficient C_d, and a novel 100-segment streamwise drag build-up profile that localizes where drag originates along the vehicle length. At deployment, the frozen surrogate drives FFD-based optimization over a 5×5×5 control lattice (75 free parameters after bilateral symmetry), producing a deformed geometry with C²-continuous, region-bounded modifications.

## Method

UniDrag's backbone consists of 12 enhanced Physics-Sliced Attention (ePSA) blocks (384-dim, 32 slices, 8 heads) that route point tokens to learnable physics slices with local geometry injection, QK-RMSNorm, and 3D-RoPE for spatial reasoning, reducing attention complexity from O(N²) to O(NS+S²). The last 4 blocks incorporate Gated Expert Routing (3 experts per block) for task-adaptive feature processing, while Modality-Protected Learning applies stop-gradient operations to enforce physical causality: surface features flow forward to volume and drag heads without receiving backpropagated gradients from those heads, preventing the fast-converging scalar C_d loss from hijacking the shared backbone. Training uses equal-weight relative L2 losses across all four outputs. At deployment, Robust Differentiable Optimization freezes the surrogate and minimizes drag via FFD with bilateral symmetry, C³-continuous region weighting, smoothness/displacement regularization, and Expectation-over-Transformation (M=5 random point subsamples averaged per step) to suppress adversarial deformations that exploit surrogate fragility.

## Results

On the 15,000-vehicle UniDrag dataset, UniDrag achieves a drag coefficient R² of 0.937, a 7.58% relative improvement over the second-best GeoTransolver (0.871). For field-level predictions, UniDrag reduces surface pressure Rel-L₂ to 0.262 (11.8% below GeoTransolver's 0.297) and volume velocity Rel-L₂ to 0.185 (12.3% below 0.211). In the optimization phase, UniDrag achieves a 13.73% mean CFD-verified drag reduction with 100% success rate across 500 test vehicles, requiring only 21.3 mm average surface displacement (0.46% of vehicle length).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniDrag outperforms all three prediction-only baselines (Transolver, AB-UPT, GeoTransolver) across every metric in Table 1, with the largest gap in C_d R² (0.937 vs. 0.871 for GeoTransolver, a 7.58% relative gain). For inverse design, the two generative baselines fail qualitatively: TripOptimizer produces severely distorted meshes with missing wheel arches and broken topology that are unsuitable for CFD, while PhysGen generates over-smoothed shapes that lose critical geometric details (mirrors, grilles, wheel spokes). UniDrag's FFD-based approach, by contrast, preserves original topology with C²/C³-continuous deformations and achieves CFD-verified drag reductions (13.73% mean) that the generative methods cannot reproduce under re-simulation. The paper notes that generative baselines show 'fake C_d reductions that vanish under CFD re-verification,' whereas UniDrag maintains 100% success rate across all 500 evaluated vehicles. Ablation studies confirm that Modality-Protected Learning is the single most critical architectural component, with its removal causing a 4.2% drop in C_d R² due to negative transfer between heterogeneous output modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniDrag extends Transolver's Physics-Sliced Attention backbone with local geometry injection, 3D-RoPE, and QK-RMSNorm to handle the large dynamic range of CFD fields, then adds Gated Expert Routing and Modality-Protected Learning to stabilize multi-task learning across heterogeneous outputs (surface fields, volume fields, global scalars). On the optimization side, it departs from generative approaches (TripOptimizer, PhysGen) that operate in latent space without geometric constraints, instead adopting classical Free-Form Deformation with explicit bilateral symmetry, region freezing, and Expectation-over-Transformation robustness. The paper's core positioning is that prediction-only neural surrogates (Transolver, GeoTransolver, AB-UPT) and unconstrained generative optimizers address disjoint halves of the design loop, and UniDrag unifies them into a single frozen-surrogate-plus-differentiable-FFD pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15,000-sample LBM dataset with 100-segment drag build-up profiles fills a notable gap in scale and optimization-readiness relative to DrivAerNet/DrivAerNet++, and the 13.7% CFD-verified drag reduction at 100% success rate with only 21.3 mm displacement demonstrates that surrogate-guided FFD optimization can produce manufacturable, physically valid shapes where generative baselines fail. Key limitations include restriction to passenger vehicles, drag-only optimization (no lift or acoustics), and the FFD parameterization bounding design freedom to smooth low-dimensional deformations. Open questions include whether the EoT robustness mechanism generalizes to multi-objective settings, whether the approach transfers to truck or bus aerodynamics, and whether the 15k-sample scale is sufficient for the full diversity of production vehicle geometries.
