## Problem

InfoNCE-based multimodal contrastive learning introduces two inherent conflicts—an alignment-uniformity conflict where cross-modality repulsion undermines positive-pair alignment, and an intra-alignment conflict where non-collinear positive embeddings partially cancel the alignment force. Both conflicts worsen as the number of modalities M grows, producing persistent distribution gaps that degrade both retrieval discriminability and generative alignment. Prior analyses were restricted to the bimodal case, leaving the multimodal conflict structure uncharacterized and unresolved.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired multimodal tuples (text, video, audio, and optionally image) encoded by frozen or fine-tuned encoders (BERT-B, BEATs, EVA-CLIP ViT-G) into ℓ2-normalized d-dimensional embeddings. For retrieval, it outputs cross-modal rankings (T2V, V2T, and joint T-VA via Gramian volume scoring) evaluated zero-shot on MSR-VTT, DiDeMo, and ActivityNet. For generation, the shared embeddings are fed directly into pre-trained UnCLIP-style decoders (Kandinsky or Stable UnCLIP) to synthesize images from text, audio, or interpolated modality mixtures, with quality measured by FID on VGGSound.

## Method

UniAlign decouples the entangled alignment and uniformity objectives of InfoNCE into two conflict-free components: (1) intra-modality uniformity, which applies a Gaussian-kernel-based repulsion loss within each modality's batch to spread embeddings on the unit hypersphere without any cross-modality repulsion, and (2) anchor-based alignment, which selects one modality as a reference and minimizes pairwise L2 distance from all other modalities to it, enforcing a single positive-pull direction per sample and eliminating non-collinear cancellation. Optional tuple-level extensions add centroid uniformity U(C) for global separability and a Gram-determinant volume penalty L_vol for cross-modal collinearity. Theoretically, the paper proves that these decoupled terms act as efficient computational proxies for minimizing a novel global Hölder divergence over M modality distributions, providing a formal guarantee of distribution-gap reduction. The framework is geometry-agnostic, supporting both Euclidean and geodesic (hyperspherical) instantiations, and requires no task-specific modules for either discriminative or generative use.

## Results

On zero-shot multimodal video retrieval (MSR-VTT, DiDeMo, ActivityNet), UniAlign achieves 58.8% T2V and 52.6% V2T average Recall@1, surpassing the strongest InfoNCE-based baseline GRAM (55.8/51.0) by 3.0/1.6 R@1 and VAST (50.1/46.2) by 8.7/6.4 R@1. On cross-modal generation (VGGSound), UniAlign reduces FID to 46.60 with the Kandinsky decoder and 40.20 with Stable UnCLIP, compared to GRAM's 87.23 and 49.36 respectively, approaching the decoder self-reconstruction upper bounds of 32.99 and 34.61.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniAlign outperforms all 15 baselines on zero-shot video retrieval. Against the closest competitor GRAM (which uses Gramian volume inside InfoNCE), UniAlign gains +3.0 R@1 on T2V and +1.6 on V2T, confirming that addressing both the alignment-uniformity and intra-alignment conflicts yields additional benefit beyond GRAM's partial fix of collinearity. Compared to VAST (pairwise InfoNCE over text, video, audio), UniAlign improves by +8.7 T2V and +6.4 V2T R@1. On cross-modal generation, UniAlign dramatically reduces FID over GRAM with the Kandinsky decoder (46.60 vs. 87.23, a 40.6-point gap) and over both GRAM (49.36) and ImageBind (50.19) with Stable UnCLIP by roughly 9–10 FID points, approaching the decoder self-reconstruction upper bounds (32.99/34.61). The tuple-level losses U(C) and L_vol provide a further +3.2 average R@1 over the base UniAlign* formulation, and the method is robust to the choice of Euclidean vs. geodesic kernel (within 0.5 FID).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the alignment-uniformity decomposition of contrastive learning (Wang & Isola, 2020) from the bimodal CLIP setting to the general multimodal regime (M ≥ 3), identifying two distinct conflicts—alignment-uniformity tension and intra-alignment cancellation—that both worsen as modality count grows. It departs from the standard InfoNCE-based pipeline used by ImageBind, VAST, LanguageBind, and GRAM by replacing the combined contrastive objective with a decoupled design: intra-modality uniformity (avoiding cross-modal repulsion) plus anchor-based alignment (avoiding non-collinear positive pulls). The theoretical grounding via a novel global Hölder divergence over M distributions provides a formal link between the proposed losses and distributional gap minimization, going beyond the geometric intuition of prior analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modality gap is a persistent practical obstacle: it degrades retrieval discriminability and forces UnCLIP-style generators to add diffusion modules to bridge the distributional mismatch. By providing a conflict-free objective that simultaneously supports discriminative and generative use cases without task-specific modules, UniAlign offers a drop-in replacement for InfoNCE in multimodal training pipelines. However, the study is limited to fine-tuning from VAST/GRAM pretrained weights rather than large-scale pretraining, leaving open whether the decoupling principle yields comparable gains when learned from scratch at scale. The Hölder divergence framework, while elegant, relies on KDE plug-in estimators whose practical utility at very large batch sizes or high dimensions remains untested.
