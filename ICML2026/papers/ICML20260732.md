## Problem

MLLM-powered embodied agents deployed in real-world environments lack explicit mechanisms for identifying physical hazards and reasoning about action-conditioned risks, causing them to either miss dangerous interactions or over-identify risks in benign situations. No prior work has developed a dedicated safety guardrail model for embodied agents operating in physical environments, leaving safe planning to be handled entirely within the agent's own policy, which struggles to balance task completion with risk assessment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260732/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The guardrail receives a single RGB scene image (photorealistic, 2400×1792 in training) paired with a text-level candidate action description (e.g., 'water the plant'), and produces a structured tuple: a binary risk flag (safe/unsafe), one of seven predefined risk categories (fire, electrical, slip/trip/fall, cut/sharp, crush/pinch, contamination/infection, chemical/toxic exposure), and a free-form natural language description of the specific hazardous configuration. Actions are semantic-level descriptions rather than continuous control signals, and the hazard description is evaluated via LLM-as-a-judge (GPT-4o) for semantic equivalence with ground truth.

## Method

EMBGUARD is a compact MLLM (Qwen-3-VL 2B/4B) trained via supervised fine-tuning (lr=1e-5, 4 epochs, 8 A6000 GPUs, frozen vision encoder) on EMBHAZARD, a 15.1K (image, action) dataset constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) risk-driven scenario generation from a 7-category taxonomy grounded in WHO ICD-11 and CPSC NEISS incident data, with 24 risk-inducing patterns; (2) compositional diversification using scene graphs to systematically create four scenario types—Causal Risky, Selective Risky, Decoupled Benign, and Absent Benign—by applying graph-level transformations (scene augmentation, hazard addition/removal) and action modification; and (3) photorealistic image generation via Gemini-3-pro-image with VQA-based verification against hazard subgraph triplets. The key design insight is decoupling physical risk reasoning from the agent's task policy, allowing the agent to focus on task completion while the guardrail provides safety feedback. Despite its small size, EMBGUARD achieves performance competitive with proprietary models (GPT-5.1, Gemini-2.5-Pro) while significantly reducing the false-positive rates that hinder real-time deployment.

## Results

On the Held-out Set (563 samples), EMBGUARD-4B achieves 71.2% Potential Risk Accuracy, 67.6% Risk Type Accuracy, and 50.1% Hazard Accuracy, matching or exceeding proprietary models like GPT-5.1 (69.1%/62.0%/57.0%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (61.4%/68.3%/63.8%) despite having only 4B parameters. On EMBGUARDTEST (329 real-world samples), EMBGUARD-4B scores 54.3%/50.3%/14.6%, competitive with GPT-5.1 (55.8%/58.1%/33.4%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (58.4%/56.8%/29.3%). Inference latency is 0.535s/sample (2B) and 0.719s/sample (4B) on a single RTX 6000 Ada GPU. In the IS-Bench simulator evaluation, EMBGUARD-4B achieves 63.1% Step Accuracy and 38.3% F1, competitive with GPT-5.1 (69.9%/38.9%) while reducing false positives compared to Gemini-2.5-Pro (49.9%/40.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against open-source baselines, EMBGUARD-4B substantially outperforms same-size models: on the Held-out Set it achieves 71.2% Potential Risk vs. 58.3% for Qwen-3-VL-4B and 47.9% for Gemma-3-4B, and 67.6% Risk Type vs. 53.5% and 33.3% respectively. Against proprietary models, EMBGUARD-4B is competitive on the Held-out Set (71.2% Potential Risk vs. GPT-5.1's 69.1% and Gemini-2.5-Pro's 61.4%) but trails on EMBGUARDTEST (54.3% vs. 55.8% and 58.4%), where real-world image diversity is higher. On Hazard Accuracy, EMBGUARD-4B (50.1% Held-out) remains well below GPT-5.1 (57.0%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (63.8%), indicating that fine-grained hazard description still benefits from larger model capacity. In the IS-Bench simulator, EMBGUARD-4B achieves 63.1% Step Accuracy and 38.3% F1, slightly below GPT-5.1 (69.9%/38.9%) but with better precision (25.7% vs. 29.1%) than Gemini-2.5-Pro, which suffers from 88.2% recall but only 22.2% precision due to over-conservative risk flagging. The key differentiator is that EMBGUARD reduces false positives on benign scenarios (where Gemini-2.5-Pro misclassifies 83.3% as risky), making it more practical for real-time deployment where agents must not be unnecessarily blocked.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of embodied agent safety and LLM/MLLM guardrails. While guardrails for digital agents (LLaMA Guard, WildGuard, ShieldAgent, GuardAgent) and safety benchmarks for embodied agents (IS-Bench, SafeAgent Bench) exist independently, no prior work has developed a concrete, trainable guardrail model that takes a (visual observation, action) pair and outputs action-conditioned physical risk assessments. The paper also departs from prior safe-planning approaches that delegate safety reasoning entirely to the agent policy, instead proposing a modular architecture where a dedicated guardrail is decoupled from the task-executing agent. The scene-graph-based compositional data generation pipeline is a methodological contribution that repurposes structured graph manipulation for controlled safety dataset construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first concrete implementation of a safety guardrail for embodied agents, providing both a training dataset (EMBHAZARD, 15.1K pairs) and a real-world benchmark (EMBGUARDTEST, 329 scenarios) that the field previously lacked. The finding that current MLLMs exhibit severe over-conservative bias (Gemini-2.5-Pro flags 83.3% of benign scenarios as risky) and systematic perceptual-salience bias (over-detecting fire/electrical, under-detecting crush/contamination/chemical risks) provides actionable diagnostics for the embodied safety community. Key open questions include extending the guardrail to continuous-control VLA policies, handling partial visual observations (occlusions, limited field of view), and validating on physical robots rather than simulation. The low absolute performance ceiling (~58% on EMBGUARDTEST even for the best models) signals that action-conditioned physical risk reasoning remains a substantially unsolved problem.
