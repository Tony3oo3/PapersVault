## Problem

Transformer-based universal morphology controllers (e.g., MetaMorph) generalize across robot morphologies but incur substantial deployment overhead and remain task-specific, requiring full retraining for each new task. The paper asks whether a structurally adaptable controller can simultaneously enable efficient per-morphology deployment and effective cross-task policy transfer without retraining from scratch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261111/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The controller consumes per-limb proprioceptive observations concatenated with morphology context (limb radius, density, joint type, range, and tree topology), a text task instruction encoded by a pretrained text encoder, and optional exteroceptive observations (terrain height maps, goal positions). It outputs a per-limb action vector. Unusually, the deployed controller is not a fixed network: at inference time, TopK-sparse gating selects only relevant morphology- and task-specific tailors, pruning the rest and reducing the effective model by up to 16.7× relative to the full Transformer while retaining MLP-level complexity.

## Method

DivMorph factorizes every randomly initialized weight matrix in a Morphology-Aware Transformer via SVD into rank-1 components, then partitions these into universal learngenes, morphology-specific tailors, and task-specific tailors. A dynamic soft gating mechanism (mixture-of-experts style) conditioned on task embeddings (from a pretrained text encoder) and morphology embeddings (from a dedicated encoder) assigns continuous activation coefficients to tailor singular values, replacing the binary gates used in prior knowledge-diversion work. A Cayley-transform orthogonality constraint on the square SVD factor stabilizes RL training by preventing anisotropic gradient amplification. Training proceeds in two stages: policy distillation from multi-task teacher policies to disentangle knowledge into the decomposed units, followed by PPO fine-tuning with differential learning rates (small for learngenes, large for tailors) to adapt to a target task or morphology. At deployment, the sparsity of the TopK gate allows irrelevant tailors to be pruned, yielding a compact, task- and morphology-specific controller.

## Results

On the UNIMAL benchmark (100 training + 100 novel morphologies, 5 training + 5 novel tasks), DivMorph achieves 3×–15× higher sample efficiency compared to MetaMorph and ModuMorph when transferring policies to novel tasks, with particularly pronounced gains in early training stages. For single-agent deployment on a given morphology, DivMorph compresses the controller by 8.9×–16.7× while matching the teacher (MetaMorph) performance on training morphologies. On novel morphologies, DivMorph demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization, even surpassing the teacher policy on tasks such as Incline and Obstacle, while HyperDistill degrades significantly under distribution shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DivMorph consistently outperforms all four baselines across both policy transfer (Figs. 3–4) and single-agent deployment (Fig. 5). Against MetaMorph and ModuMorph, DivMorph improves sample efficiency by approximately 3×–15×, with MetaMorph additionally suffering negative transfer on behaviorally mismatched tasks (e.g., Manipulate Box) due to indiscriminate aggregation of multi-task priors. Against HyperDistill, DivMorph matches compression on training morphologies but substantially outperforms on novel morphologies, where HyperDistill's hypernetwork overfits and generalizes poorly. Against GRL, which transfers only task-agnostic fragments via evolutionary strategies, DivMorph still achieves higher final returns and faster convergence, demonstrating that the structured SVD-based knowledge diversion with soft gating provides finer-grained disentanglement than layer-level learngene condensation. The two MetaMorph policy-transfer variants (direct transfer and multi-task distillation) both underperform DivMorph, confirming that rigid full-policy reuse introduces redundant or misaligned priors that hinder adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DivMorph sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) universal morphology control, which evolved from MLP/GNN controllers through MetaMorph's Morphology-Aware Transformer and ModuMorph's contextual modulation to HyperDistill's hypernetwork-based compression, and (2) Knowledge Diversion (KDiv), a pretraining paradigm that factorizes weights into task-agnostic learngenes and task-specific tailors, previously applied to generative models. The key departure is extending KDiv from a single task dimension to a joint morphology-and-task decomposition, replacing binary gating with dynamic soft gates in a mixture-of-experts style, and anchoring the factorization with a Cayley-transform orthogonality constraint to stabilize RL training. Unlike HyperDistill, which compresses the full Transformer into a per-morphology MLP at the cost of generalization, DivMorph retains a shared decomposable backbone that can be sparsely reconfigured per agent-task pair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks in universal morphology control simultaneously: the high deployment cost of Transformer-based controllers and the lack of cross-task policy reuse. The 16.7× model compression (to MLP-comparable size) while matching teacher performance, combined with 3.3× sample-efficiency gains on novel tasks, makes modular policy deployment and transfer substantially more tractable. However, all experiments are confined to the UNIMAL/MuJoCo simulation space with 15–20 DoF robots, and the paper itself acknowledges that real-world deployment remains open. A key unresolved question is whether the SVD-based decomposition and soft gating scale gracefully to higher-dimensional morphologies, longer-horizon tasks, or continuous (rather than discrete) task specifications, and whether the offline distillation cost becomes prohibitive as the morphology-task combinatorial space grows.
