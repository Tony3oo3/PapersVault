## Problem

Existing dataset pruning methods rely on either intrinsic per-sample signals (loss, uncertainty) or extrinsic pairwise diversity signals, each capturing only one facet of sample utility and lacking robustness across pruning ratios and data distributions. No prior framework jointly models both aspects within a single principled objective that admits efficient optimization and formal approximation guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled training dataset (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR images or 224×224 ImageNet images), a target pruning ratio p, and feature embeddings extracted from a pretrained backbone (TorchVision ResNet-50) or the current training model. It produces a pruned subset S of size (1−p)N that is used for subsequent training epochs. Pruning is applied dynamically per epoch (starting after a warmup period), and the graph is sparsified by first partitioning samples by class label and then clustering within each class, so that extrinsic interactions are only computed within local neighborhoods rather than over all N² pairs.

## Method

UGIES models the dataset as a weighted graph where node weights encode intrinsic importance (e.g., prediction entropy or cross-entropy loss) and edge weights encode pairwise extrinsic interactions via a distance metric D mapped through a non-positive function g. Dataset pruning is then cast exactly as a Maximum Weight Clique Problem (MWCP) on this graph. The key insight is that the single-vertex removal case of MWCP has an exact solution determined by marginal gains, which motivates a principled greedy solver that incrementally selects samples maximizing the unified importance I(x_i|S) = α·I^in(x_i) + Σ_{x_j∈S} g(D(x_i,x_j)). Under mild conditions (non-negative distance, non-positive mapping), the paper proves the objective is submodular, yielding a (1−1/e) worst-case approximation guarantee for the greedy solver. Structured graph sparsification via class-conditional clustering reduces the pairwise computation from O(N²) to near-linear, and a stochastic sampling variant further accelerates selection for very large datasets.

## Results

On CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-18, UGIES achieves 95.9%/78.9% top-1 accuracy at 30% pruning, surpassing full-data training (95.6%/78.2%) and all 24 baselines. On ImageNet-1k with ResNet-50, it reaches 77.0% at 30% pruning (vs. 76.4% full data) and 76.5% at 50% pruning, achieving lossless training on half the data. In wall-clock time on ImageNet-1k (50% pruning, 4×RTX 4090), UGIES completes training in 7.9 h total, a 43.2% reduction over full-data training (13.9 h), while the next-best method (PFB) saves only 37.4%. The method also generalizes to Swin-T (79.7% at 30% vs. 79.6% full data), semantic segmentation on PASCAL VOC 2012 (70.02 mIoU at 70% pruning), and object detection on COCO2017 (25.5 AP at 25% pruning).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGIES outperforms all 24 baselines on CIFAR-10/100, with the closest competitors being DivBS (95.4/95.2/95.1 on CIFAR-10; 78.5/78.2/77.2 on CIFAR-100) and InfoBatch (95.6/95.1/94.7; 78.2/78.1/76.5), which it beats by 0.3–0.5 points at 30% and 0.4–1.1 points at 70% pruning. On ImageNet-1k with ResNet-50, UGIES (77.0/76.5/75.3) surpasses InfoBatch† (76.5/75.8/74.9) by up to 0.7 points and MoSo† (76.5/73.5/70.0) by up to 5.3 points at 70% pruning; it also beats the graph-based D²-pruning† (75.0/73.4/68.2) and InfoMax† (75.2/73.7/68.6) by 1.8–7.1 points. On Swin-T, UGIES (79.7/79.4/78.9) edges out PFB† (79.6/79.2/78.2) and Dyn-Unc (79.1/78.5/77.6) by 0.1–1.3 points. In wall-clock time on ImageNet-1k at 50% pruning, UGIES (7.9 h total) is 2.3 h faster than PFB† (8.7 h), 2.3 h faster than InfoBatch†/UCB† (10.2 h), and 10 h faster than EL2N-50† (17.9 h, which actually increases total time). On PASCAL VOC 2012 segmentation and COCO2017 detection, UGIES-BS and UGIES respectively beat DivBS and InfoBatch† by 0.17–0.34 mIoU and 0.5 AP, confirming cross-task generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

UGIES unifies the two dominant families of dataset pruning—per-sample intrinsic scoring (EL2N, InfoBatch, Forgetting) and pairwise extrinsic diversity (K-Center, Herding, DivBS)—by casting the selection problem as a Maximum Weight Clique Problem on a weighted graph, where node weights encode intrinsic value and edge weights encode extrinsic interactions. The greedy solver is derived from the exact solution of a single-vertex-perturbation subproblem of MWCP, and the (1−1/e) approximation guarantee is inherited from the classical Nemhauser et al. (1978) result for monotone submodular functions. Unlike prior hybrid methods (D²-pruning, InfoMax) that fix a single composite metric, UGIES treats submodularity as a design principle: any intrinsic score and any distance-to-penalty mapping satisfying mild sign conditions (non-negative distance, non-positive mapping) yields a valid, theoretically grounded objective, making the framework a superset of many existing criteria rather than a new fixed heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides practitioners with a principled recipe for choosing or designing importance metrics while retaining a constant-factor approximation guarantee, reducing the ad-hoc nature of current pruning heuristics. The demonstrated 43% total-time reduction on ImageNet-1k (ResNet-50) at matched accuracy is practically significant for large-scale training pipelines. Open questions include whether the mild submodularity conditions can be relaxed for arbitrary metrics, whether tighter solvers than greedy exist for the fixed-cardinality MWCP, and how the method scales to datasets with tens of millions of samples where even O(N log N) graph construction and per-epoch feature extraction become non-trivial. The paper also acknowledges that the approximation guarantee is worst-case and may be loose in practice, leaving room for adaptive or problem-specific solvers.
