## Problem

Existing hybrid attention strategies that mix full attention (FA) and sparse attention (SA) heads within a single model rely on static, pre-defined sparsity ratios that cannot adapt to the varying sparsity sensitivities of downstream tasks at inference time. The paper demonstrates that tasks naturally split into sparsity-robust (e.g., summarization) and sparsity-sensitive (e.g., question answering) categories, and that a fixed ratio either wastes computation on robust tasks or degrades performance on sensitive ones, requiring extensive task-specific tuning that limits general applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264385/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes long-context text sequences (trained up to 64K tokens, evaluated up to 256K) and, at each transformer layer, the Key hidden states (x_K ∈ R^{s×H×d'}) are pooled over the first and last 100 tokens to form a compact task representation. The Attention Router then outputs a binary routing decision per key-value head (FA or SA), which determines how each head computes its attention output; head outputs are concatenated to form the layer output, and the model ultimately produces generated text tokens. The routing is performed only during the prefill stage, adding just 0.27M parameters per layer.

## Method

Elastic Attention introduces a lightweight Attention Router (Task MLP + Router MLP) that performs head-wise routing between FA and SA modes, analogous to a Mixture-of-Experts gating mechanism. The router is optimized with a Gumbel-Softmax continuous relaxation (with temperature annealing from high to low) to approximate hard binary routing during training, combined with a Straight-Through Estimator to backpropagate gradients through the non-differentiable argmax. Training uses a min-max objective: the inner minimization combines standard cross-entropy language modeling loss with a sparsity regularization term (|Ω_MSR − t| + |Ω_MSR − t|²) weighted by task-specific trainable Lagrange multipliers, while the outer maximization over λ₁, λ₂ decouples sparsity-performance trade-offs across task categories. Only the router parameters are trained (backbone frozen) for 12 hours on 8×A800 GPUs, and a fused Block-Sparse-Attention kernel executes all routed heads in a single kernel launch to avoid the memory and scheduling overhead of serial dispatch.

## Results

On LongBench-E, Elastic Attention (FA-SSA) achieves 53.35 average on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, slightly exceeding the full-attention backbone (53.28) while maintaining ~69% model sparsity (Ω_MSR). On Qwen3-8B, Elastic Attention (FA-XA) reaches 51.66 average (vs. 52.16 backbone) at 80% sparsity, outperforming all fixed-ratio baselines. On RULER (up to 256K tokens), Elastic Attention (FA-XA) on Llama-3.1-8B achieves 81.82 average, compared to 62.92 for DuoAttention and 48.82 for PruLong. On LongBench-V2, Elastic Attention (FA-SSA) on Qwen3-8B scores 33.41, surpassing the backbone's 31.97.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Elastic Attention consistently outperforms all training-based hybrid baselines (DuoAttention, PruLong, InfLLM-V2) on LongBench-E, with the largest margins on sparsity-sensitive tasks (e.g., +3.27 over DuoAttention on Llama-3.1-8B S-Doc QA). On RULER at 256K context, the gap widens dramatically: Elastic Attention (FA-XA) on Llama-3.1-8B scores 68.51 versus 42.92 (DuoAttention) and 21.64 (PruLong), as fixed-ratio baselines cannot adapt to the increased retrieval demand at extreme lengths. MoBA and NSA, which use block-level sparse attention rather than hybrid heads, degrade severely on Llama-3.1-8B at 128K–256K (MoBA drops to 1.15 at 128K; NSA to 15.30), while Elastic Attention maintains 68–79. The training-free XAttention baseline is competitive at shorter contexts but still trails Elastic Attention by 1–4 points on average across benchmarks. Notably, Elastic Attention (FA-SSA) on Llama-3.1-8B slightly exceeds the full-attention backbone (53.35 vs. 53.28) while using only ~31% of heads in full attention, and on AIME24 it improves the Qwen3-4B score from 6.70 to 10.00, whereas DuoAttention drops the overall average by 3.66 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid attention paradigm established by DuoAttention and PruLong, which statically assign a fixed proportion of heads to Full Attention (FA) versus Sparse Attention (SA). The key departure is replacing this static ratio with a lightweight Attention Router that performs input-dependent, head-wise routing between FA and SA modes, drawing conceptually on Mixture-of-Experts gating (Shazeer et al., 2017) and differentiable discrete selection via Gumbel-Softmax with Straight-Through Estimators. Unlike training-based sparse attention methods (MoBA, NSA, InfLLM-V2) that modify projection layers or introduce new architectural components, Elastic Attention freezes the entire backbone and trains only the router, positioning it as a plug-and-play adaptation layer on top of existing pretrained LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the minimal overhead (0.27M parameters per layer, 12 hours on 8×A800) and the observation that downstream tasks reduce to just two sparsity regimes, simplifying what would otherwise require per-task tuning. However, the approach is explicitly scoped to single-GPU deployment of small-to-medium models and may conflict with head-level parallelism strategies used in large-scale inference systems. An open question is whether the dynamic, input-dependent computation principle can generalize to next-generation architectures (e.g., Titans-style test-time memorization) where the FA/SA dichotomy may not be the right axis of adaptation.
