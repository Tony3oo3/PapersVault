## Problem

In single-cell perturbation data, perturbation-invariant background programs dominate gene expression while perturbation-specific signals are intrinsically sparse. Existing causal representation learning methods entangle invariant information into perturbation-related representations, and foundation models suppress sparse perturbation-induced signals altogether, leading to poor generalization to unseen combinatorial perturbations. The paper formalizes this as the Perturbation Suppression Hypothesis and asks how to explicitly extract and causally organize perturbation-specific information for reliable prediction.

## Input / Output

At test time, the model takes an unperturbed (control) cell's gene expression profile and a perturbation condition vector u (one-hot for single-gene, two-hot for double-gene perturbations) as input, and produces a predicted post-perturbation gene expression profile. For out-of-distribution combinatorial prediction, unseen two-hot vectors are fed directly into the same u-conditioned mechanism trained only on single-gene interventions. The model additionally outputs a learned latent causal DAG over perturbation-responsive variables, enabling interpretable analysis of perturbation-response programs.

## Method

PerturbedVAE decomposes the latent space into a perturbation-invariant block z_l (modeled as i.i.d. Gaussian) and a perturbation-responsive block z_ν (modeled as a linear Gaussian structural causal model with a DAG constraint, conditioned on the perturbation label u). The key mechanism is a contrastive alignment loss that samples an unperturbed control expression profile for each perturbed sample and enforces consistency of the inferred invariant latents across the two, thereby anchoring dominant background variation in z_l and freeing z_ν to capture residual perturbation-induced changes. Training optimizes the standard ELBO (reconstruction + KL) plus this alignment term weighted by α. A theoretical identifiability theorem (Theorem 1) establishes that under environmental sufficiency, optimal alignment, and intervention sufficiency conditions, z_ν is recovered up to permutation and scaling while z_l is recovered up to a linear block transformation, providing the theoretical grounding for the framework's design choices.

## Results

On the Norman2019 Perturb-seq benchmark, PerturbedVAE achieves an RMSE of 0.3995±0.0013 on single-gene perturbation prediction (105 genes) and 0.4474±0.0007 on zero-shot double-gene combinatorial prediction, outperforming the strongest causal baseline SAMS-VAE (0.4123 and 0.4629) and the best foundation model UCE (0.5634 on double-gene). The gap widens substantially for combinatorial OOD prediction, where PerturbedVAE improves over Discrepancy-VAE by ~26% in RMSE (0.4474 vs. 0.6082) and over SENA by ~47% (0.4474 vs. 0.8483). On the Replogle2022 cross-dataset check, PerturbedVAE again shows the strongest representation-learning performance among compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against latent causal models, PerturbedVAE wins on both single-gene and double-gene RMSE across all four baselines. The largest gains appear on double-gene OOD prediction: PerturbedVAE (0.4474) beats SAMS-VAE (0.4629) by ~3%, sVAE+ (0.5664) by ~21%, Discrepancy-VAE (0.6082) by ~26%, and SENA (0.8483) by ~47%, indicating that the contrastive alignment term is critical for combinatorial generalization. Against foundation models (UCE, scFoundation, Geneformer, STATE), PerturbedVAE achieves the lowest RMSE (0.4474 vs. 0.4981–0.6132) and highest R² (0.9865) on double-gene prediction, confirming that FM representations suppress perturbation-specific signals. Against simple baselines (RandomForest, ElasticNet, KNN), PerturbedVAE also wins on RMSE (0.4474 vs. 0.4894–0.4931), though the additive linear model from Ahlmann-Eltze et al. (2025) achieves a marginally lower genome-wide RMSE (0.4424) at the cost of a negative cell-level R², whereas PerturbedVAE maintains a valid cell-level R² of 0.9840. The ablation (w/o Align) shows the contrastive term contributes a consistent ~1–3% RMSE improvement, with the largest benefit on double-gene OOD settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work on single-cell perturbation modeling: latent causal representation learning (Discrepancy-VAE, SENA, sVAE+, SAMS-VAE) and single-cell foundation models (scFoundation, UCE, Geneformer). It departs from the causal representation line by identifying a critical mismatch—existing identifiability results assume all latent variables are intervened across environments, whereas real Perturb-seq data involves only partial intervention on a small gene subset. The key methodological departure is the contrastive alignment objective, which explicitly anchors perturbation-invariant variation using unperturbed controls, combined with a DAG-constrained latent causal model for perturbation-responsive variables. Theoretically, the identifiability analysis adapts nonlinear ICA sufficient-statistics arguments (Hyvarinen & Morioka; Khemakhem et al.) to this partial-intervention, two-block setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Perturbation Suppression Hypothesis provides a unifying explanation for why both major approaches (causal VAEs and FMs) fail on perturbation prediction, and the contrastive alignment mechanism offers a concrete, theoretically grounded remedy. However, the OOD claim is restricted to combinatorial generalization over genes already seen in training; unseen-gene prediction remains out of scope because the perturbation vector carries no biological geometry. The identifiability assumptions (particularly optimal alignment and environmental sufficiency) are standard in the ICA literature but untestable on real biological data, leaving open whether the theoretical guarantees hold in practice at finite sample sizes. A broader open question is whether the linear-compositionality assumption (supported by the competitive additive baseline) will break down in more complex, nonlinear regulatory contexts.
