## Problem

Video diffusion models produce visually impressive results but fundamentally fail to maintain 3D structural consistency, exhibiting object deformation, spatial drift, and geometry collapse over time. The authors attribute this to the denoising objective, which incentivizes pixel-level statistical matching without explicit geometric regularization, causing models to hallucinate plausible textures without injecting 3D coherence into the latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260980/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image-to-video, the method takes a reference image (first frame from DL3DV-10K) paired with structured camera-motion prompts composed of 2–3 motion primitives (e.g., 'orbit around the scene, then roll gently to one side'); for text-to-video, it takes natural-language captions generated by CogVLM2-Video. The output is a generated video (49 frames for CogVideoX, 81 for CogVideoX1.5) with improved 3D geometric consistency. During preference construction, 10 frames are uniformly sampled from each candidate video for 3D reconstruction scoring, and the final model is a LoRA adapter (rank 64, α=128) affecting ~1% of the base model's parameters.

## Method

VideoGPA introduces a review-and-correct framework that distills geometry foundation model priors into video diffusion via Direct Preference Optimization. A feed-forward GFM (VGGT) predicts per-frame depth maps and camera poses from T=10 sampled frames of a generated video, constructs a colored point cloud, and reprojects it back to each frame; the resulting reprojection error (MSE + LPIPS) serves as a scene-level 3D consistency score. Preference pairs are formed by ranking multiple seed-varied candidates per prompt by this score, with filtering for motion salience, geometric margin (>0.05), and difficulty pruning. The DPO objective is adapted to the v-prediction parameterization used by modern DiT video models, updating only LoRA parameters to steer the generative distribution toward the 3D-consistent manifold. The key distinction from prior work is the use of global reprojection consistency rather than local epipolar constraints, which prevents false positives from degenerate outputs and provides a dense, unambiguous geometric signal requiring only ~2,500 preference pairs and 10,000 LoRA training steps.

## Results

On the I2V task (CogVideoX-I2V-5B), VideoGPA achieves the best 3D consistency across all metrics: MVCS 0.950, 3DCS 0.483, and Epipolar error 0.539, while reaching a 76.0% overall VideoReward win rate. On T2V (CogVideoX-5B), VideoGPA attains the best SSIM (0.803), LPIPS (0.411), MVCS (0.953), 3DCS (0.422), and Epipolar error (0.548), with a 60.33% OVL win rate. Against GeoVideo on CogVideoX1.5-5B, VideoGPA (trained for only 1,500 steps vs. ~10,000 for GeoVideo) achieves substantially better geometric consistency (Epipolar 0.567 vs. 0.875; MVCS 0.982 vs. 0.819) and human-aligned quality (OVL 57.64% vs. 18.06%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CogVideoX base model, VideoGPA improves all three 3D consistency metrics (MVCS, 3DCS, Epipolar) in both I2V and T2V settings, with the largest Epipolar error reduction being 0.585→0.539 (I2V) and 0.584→0.548 (T2V), while PSNR/SSIM drop slightly as expected when steering toward geometric structure. Compared to SFT, VideoGPA wins decisively on every metric; SFT actually degrades geometric consistency (e.g., Epipolar 0.585→0.640 in I2V) and achieves only 35% OVL win rate versus VideoGPA's 76%. Against Epipolar-DPO, VideoGPA wins on 3DCS, Epipolar error, and OVL in both settings (e.g., OVL 76% vs. 66% in I2V; 60.33% vs. 48.67% in T2V), while tying on MVCS in T2V (both 0.953). Against GeoVideo on CogVideoX1.5-5B, VideoGPA (trained for only 1,500 steps) substantially outperforms GeoVideo (~10,000 steps) on all 3D consistency metrics (Epipolar 0.567 vs. 0.875; MVCS 0.982 vs. 0.819) and human-aligned quality (OVL 57.64% vs. 18.06%), demonstrating that preference-based alignment is more effective than explicit geometric supervision for preserving perceptual quality while improving geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of diffusion model alignment and geometric foundation models. It builds directly on the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) and its diffusion-specific adaptation (Wallace et al., 2024), extending both to the v-prediction parameterization used by modern video DiTs. The key departure from prior geometric alignment work (Epipolar-DPO's local pairwise constraints, GeoVideo's explicit geometric SFT losses) is the use of a scene-level reprojection consistency score from a feed-forward GFM (VGGT) as a dense, self-supervised preference signal, eliminating the need for human annotations or iterative 3D optimization. The 'review-and-correct' framing—score with a GFM, then align via DPO—repurposes the GFM as an automated reward model rather than a training-time loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 3D geometric failures in video diffusion models are largely an objective-misalignment problem solvable with ~2,500 preference pairs and ~1% of parameters, rather than an architectural limitation requiring retraining. This has practical implications for the growing ecosystem of video models used in embodied AI, novel view synthesis, and physics simulation, where geometric fidelity is critical. A notable open question is scalability: the GFM-based scoring cost grows with video length (Table 4), limiting applicability to long-form generation. The finding that SFT actively harms geometric alignment (Table 8) challenges the standard SFT→DPO pipeline assumption, though it is validated on a single model family and warrants broader confirmation.
