## Problem

Converting MLLMs into retrieval encoders has required expensive contrastive fine-tuning on millions of multimodal pairs, while existing training-free approaches (e5-V) yield coarse single-token embeddings and omit the critical reranking stage. The paper asks whether an off-the-shelf MLLM can serve as a competitive two-stage retriever—both embedder and reranker—without any parameter updates, auxiliary models, or external data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266285/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

FreeRet consumes arbitrary modality combinations (text, RGB image, video, audio, or interleaved sequences) as both queries and candidate items, with no fixed resolution or length constraint beyond the backbone MLLM's context window. The embedding stage produces a single d-dimensional vector extracted from the attention-sublayer output of the final transformer layer (h_L^Attn) after a controlled one-word generation prompt; the reranking stage produces a scalar relevance score computed as SoftMax(p('A')) from the LM head under a multiple-choice prompt. The same MLLM instance handles both stages, so the final output is a ranked candidate list requiring no additional model deployment.

## Method

FreeRet's central insight is that the final MLP layer in an MLLM imposes 'lexicalization pressure,' projecting semantically rich hidden states into a space aligned with discrete token prediction and degrading embedding quality; bypassing it (using h_L^Attn instead of h_L^MLP) yields semantically faithful vectors. Embedding generation is further stabilized by three prompt-level constraints—task alignment, semantic grounding, and noise suppression—that reshape the representation without any architectural change or training. For reranking, the paper identifies an 'LLM framing effect' where logically equivalent binary labels (Yes/No vs. True/False vs. Right/Wrong) produce divergent accuracies due to pretraining biases, and mitigates it by casting relevance judgment as a multiple-choice question scored via the softmax probability of the match option. The entire pipeline is plug-and-play: any off-the-shelf MLLM becomes a two-stage retriever immediately, preserving its generative and reasoning capabilities while unifying retrieval, reranking, and generation in a single model.

## Results

On the MMEB benchmark (36 datasets, 4 meta-tasks), FreeRet with Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves 70.7% average Precision@1 with top-50 reranking, surpassing GME-7B (trained on 8M pairs, 56.0%) by 14.7 points and MMRet-7B (trained on 26.2M pairs, 44.0%) by 26.7 points, while matching UniME-V2 (69.6%), a model supervised directly on MMEB. The embedding-only component (FreeRet-embed) reaches 53.7%, exceeding the training-free E5-V baseline (39.8%) by 13.9 points. On MMEB-V2 video tasks, FreeRet-7B achieves 63.2% video classification and 39.3% video retrieval, outperforming VLM2Vec-V2-2B by 8.4% and 2.9% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeRet decisively outperforms all training-based two-stage retrievers: it exceeds MM-Embed r10 (54.9%) and LamRA r10 (55.0%) by ~15.7 points, and GME (56.0%) by 14.7 points, despite using zero training data versus 1.1M–26.2M pairs. Against the strongest MMEB-supervised method UniME-V2 r5 (69.6%), FreeRet r50 leads by 1.1 points. Among training-free methods, FreeRet-embed (53.7%) surpasses E5-V (39.8%) by 13.9 points, and the full FreeRet pipeline further extends this gap to over 30 points. On MMEB-V2 video tasks, FreeRet-7B (63.2% classification, 39.3% retrieval) outperforms VLM2Vec-V2-2B (39.3%, 28.8%) by 8.4% and 2.9% respectively, and exceeds GME-7B (37.4%, 28.4%) by 25.8% and 10.9%. FreeRet also generalizes across model families, with Qwen2.5-VL-32B reaching 73.3% and InternVL3-14B reaching 68.9% average on MMEB, confirming the framework's model-agnostic nature.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeRet extends the training-free embedding extraction line (E5-V, PromptEOL, TokenPrepend) into the multimodal setting and, critically, adds a reranking stage that prior training-free methods entirely omitted. It departs from the dominant MLLM-retrieval paradigm (GME, MMRet, VLM2Vec, LamRA) which requires millions of contrastive pairs and expensive fine-tuning per backbone, by instead exploiting the representational and reasoning capacities of off-the-shelf MLLMs through three prompt-level interventions: bypassing the final MLP to relieve lexicalization pressure, controlled single-word generation with semantic/noise/task priors, and MCQ-framed reranking to neutralize the LLM framing effect. The theoretical analysis (Lemma C.1) formalizes why the final MLP projects hidden states into the vocabulary span at the cost of semantic fidelity, grounding the architectural choice in optimization theory rather than empirical observation alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreeRet challenges the prevailing assumption that competitive multimodal retrieval demands large-scale supervised adaptation, showing that a single unmodified MLLM can serve as embedder, reranker, and generator within a unified RAG pipeline. The plug-and-play nature means every new MLLM release is immediately deployable as a retriever across arbitrary modality combinations without retraining, and the framing-effect finding has implications well beyond retrieval for any LLM-based binary decision task. Open questions include computational efficiency at 100M+ candidate scale (where MLLM latency remains a bottleneck relative to CLIP), dependence on the base model's intrinsic multimodal quality, and whether the lexicalization-pressure remedy generalizes to architectures without a separable final MLP.
