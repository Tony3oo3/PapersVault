## Problem

Dense-to-MoE knowledge distillation lacks a systematic solution because dense teachers have no explicit routing mechanism, making it impossible to directly supervise a MoE student's routing decisions. Existing approaches either perform sparse upcycling (rebuilding expert structure from dense weights) or target MoE-to-MoE distillation, leaving the structural mismatch—router cold start, capacity gap, path rupture, and expert homogenization—unaddressed when transferring from a router-less dense teacher to a pretrained router-aware MoE student.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen dense 7B math-specialized teacher (Qwen2.5-Math-7B or DeepSeek-Math-7B, pre-trained with GRPO) and a pretrained MoE student (Qwen3-30B-A3B with 128 experts/Top-8, or DeepSeek-V2-Lite with 64 routed + 2 shared experts/Top-6) as its two model inputs, along with verifiable math problems from DeepScaleR. At inference, the student receives a math problem as text and produces a chain-of-thought solution, activating only 3.3B or 2.4B parameters per token—less than the dense teacher's 7B—so the distillation must compress the teacher's reasoning into a sparsely activated expert space without increasing per-token FLOPs.

## Method

PADD organizes dense-to-MoE distillation into four stages across two phases executed in a single training loop. Stage I (initialization) performs cardinality-constrained K-Means clustering on the teacher's FFN neuron weight vectors to partition them into N functional groups, maps cluster centroids to student router weights, and warms up experts under frozen uniform routing with a target-activation-distribution alignment loss. Stages II–IV run sequentially per training step on the main dataset: Stage II (forward pass) applies online adaptive distillation that modulates the teacher's logit temperature via a tanh-coupled function of the student's within-group relative advantage, sharpening supervision on good paths and softening it on poor ones to bridge the capacity gap; Stage III (backward pass) introduces PR-GRPO, which multiplies the standard GRPO importance ratio by an exponential suppression term exp(−λ·Γ·𝕀(A<0)) where Γ is the L2 routing shift between current and previous parameters, selectively damping gradient updates on unstable disadvantageous paths; Stage IV (parameter update) applies reward-augmented load balancing that updates each expert's routing bias with a combined traffic-balance term and an EMA-smoothed reward-compensation term, steering activation toward high-quality experts while maintaining load balance.

## Results

On the Qwen family (Qwen3-30B-A3B student, 3.3B active params), PADD achieves 80.2% average Pass@1 across five math benchmarks (AIME24, AMC23, MATH500, Minerva, OlympiadBench), surpassing its 7B dense teacher (Qwen2.5-Math-7B after GRPO) at 77.7%. On the DeepSeek family (DeepSeek-V2-Lite student, 2.4B active params), PADD reaches 55.2% average, within 2.9 points of the DeepSeek-Math-7B teacher at 58.1%. PADD also best preserves general capabilities under math-only training, with the Qwen student retaining 52.0% on non-math benchmarks (vs. 52.2% untrained Base) and the DeepSeek student at 38.5% (vs. 38.9% Base).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PADD outperforms all four main baselines on both model families. Against Online KD (Agarwal et al., 2024), PADD gains +6.6% on Qwen (80.2 vs. 73.6) and +8.5% on DeepSeek (55.2 vs. 46.7), attributed to adaptive temperature coupling addressing the capacity gap and Stage I expert differentiation avoiding router cold start. Against RSPO (Zhang et al., 2025), PADD improves by +3.0% on Qwen (80.2 vs. 77.2) and +0.9% on DeepSeek (55.2 vs. 54.3), with PR-GRPO's selective suppression of disadvantageous paths providing stronger stabilization than RSPO's uniform router-shift weighting. Against GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025a), PADD leads by +3.9% on Qwen (80.2 vs. 76.3) and +2.0% on DeepSeek (55.2 vs. 53.2). Against Dense-GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) at the same active-parameter scale, PADD achieves a dramatic +26.7% on Qwen (80.2 vs. 53.5) and +9.6% on DeepSeek (55.2 vs. 45.6), demonstrating that MoE's sparse expert structure with learned routing substantially outperforms a dense model of equivalent active capacity. Notably, PADD also surpasses the dense teacher on Qwen (80.2 vs. 77.7) and approaches it on DeepSeek (55.2 vs. 58.1), while maintaining the same per-token inference cost as the MoE student.

## Relation to Prior Work

PADD sits at the intersection of on-policy knowledge distillation (Agarwal et al., 2024), GRPO-based reinforcement learning (Shao et al., 2024), and MoE routing stabilization (Zhang et al., 2025; Shazeer et al., 2017). It explicitly positions itself as complementary to architecture-conversion methods like sparse upcycling (Komatsuzaki et al., 2023) and MoEfication (Zhang et al., 2022), which build MoE structure from dense weights and then train routing from scratch. The key departure is that PADD treats dense-to-MoE transfer as a knowledge-and-routing distillation problem into an already-pretrained MoE student, rather than an architecture reconstruction problem, and addresses the structural mismatch (no explicit router in the teacher) through neuron-cluster-based initialization and path-aware policy optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, end-to-end recipe for transferring domain-specialized dense model knowledge into MoE students without increasing inference cost, which is directly relevant as the field shifts toward sparse architectures for efficiency. The finding that a 3.3B-active MoE student can surpass its 7B dense teacher on math reasoning (80.2% vs 77.7% on Qwen) validates the decompression paradigm. Limitations include evaluation restricted to mathematical reasoning, reliance on relatively small (7B) teachers, and a complex four-stage pipeline with many hyperparameters whose interactions are only partially characterized. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to non-math domains, scales to much larger teachers, and whether the neuron-clustering initialization step is necessary at larger model scales where FFN modularity may be less pronounced.
