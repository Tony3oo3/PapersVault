## Problem

Learning the evolution of high-dimensional probability densities (population dynamics) from unlabeled marginal samples is challenging because admissible velocity fields are non-unique and existing methods either require expensive per-step optimal-transport couplings or restrict to gradient (minimal-kinetic-energy) fields that cannot represent rotational or circulating physics. This paper asks whether one can avoid OT entirely while still obtaining a well-posed, regular, and physically meaningful velocity field from marginal data alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263985/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only independent samples from discrete time marginals ρ(t_k) (no trajectory or coupling information) plus a fixed base distribution ν (typically N(0,I)), with optional conditioning on physics parameters μ. It produces an explicit physics-time velocity field u_θ(x,t) that can be integrated with a single new initial-condition sample to generate trajectories over physics time, requiring only one network evaluation per Euler step. The approach scales to spatial-field data at d = 128² ≈ 16,000 dimensions, far exceeding prior population-dynamics work.

## Method

The core idea is a two-parameter flow Φ(a, t, s) that separates sampling time s (vertical, base-to-marginal transport) from physics time t (horizontal, marginal-to-marginal evolution). Training proceeds in three steps: (1) learn a conditional flow-matching velocity v(x, t, s) that pushes ν forward to ρ(t) for all t using standard stochastic-interpolant objectives; (2) generate coupled synthetic trajectories by fixing the same noise realization a across all physics times so that t ↦ Φ(a, t, 1) is a smooth path; (3) regress a neural-network velocity u_θ(x, t) on the finite-difference time derivatives of these synthetic paths. A key theoretical result (Proposition 2.1) shows that once v is fixed, the physics-time velocity u = ∂_t Φ ∘ Φ⁻¹ is unique, satisfies the continuity equation, and inherits regularity from v via the vanishing Lie-bracket compatibility condition (∂_s u + v·∇u − u·∇v − ∂_t v = 0). Because the construction does not enforce a gradient ansatz, the resulting u can naturally encode rotational and non-gradient dynamics that OT-based methods exclude by design.

## Results

On the Vlasov-Poisson two-stream instability, TPF achieves an average W₂ error of 3.2×10⁻⁴ and final W₂ of 4.0×10⁻⁴, outperforming all four baselines (best competitor DICE: 4.5×10⁻⁴ avg / 7.8×10⁻⁴ final). On the bump-on-tail instability, TPF achieves 3.1×10⁻⁴ avg / 4.7×10⁻⁴ final W₂, again the best across all methods. On the high-dimensional barotropic flow (d = 128²), TPF recovers the correct vortex-merging dynamics with kinetic energy roughly two orders of magnitude lower than the physical trajectories while matching enstrophy decay, and achieves orders-of-magnitude speedup in inference time. On Kolmogorov flow, TPF-generated samples reproduce the expected ω⁻³ energy spectrum decay (ω⁻²·⁸⁸⁰ at t=127) closely, whereas the MSE-fitted operator-learning model deviates significantly (ω⁻³·⁸⁰⁵).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPF outperforms all four baselines on both Vlasov-Poisson instability benchmarks. Against DICE (Blickhan et al., 2025), TPF achieves a 1.4× improvement in average W₂ on two-stream (3.2×10⁻⁴ vs 4.5×10⁻⁴) and a 21× improvement on bump-on-tail (3.1×10⁻⁴ vs 6.5×10⁻³). Against HOAM (Berman et al., 2024), the gap is dramatic: TPF is ~9× better on two-stream and ~120,000× better on bump-on-tail, where HOAM's gradient ansatz u=∇φ fails catastrophically (W₂ ≈ 38). Against Action Matching (Neklyudov et al., 2023), TPF wins on both tasks by orders of magnitude on two-stream (3.2×10⁻⁴ vs 4.6) and by ~8× on bump-on-tail (3.1×10⁻⁴ vs 2.5×10⁻³). Against JKONet* (Terpin et al., 2024), TPF is ~24× better on two-stream and ~14× better on bump-on-tail. The paper attributes TPF's advantage on rotational dynamics (vortex rotation in the instabilities) to its ability to represent non-gradient velocity components, which the gradient-ansatz methods (HOAM, DICE) and OT-based methods (JKONet*) cannot capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) and stochastic interpolants (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2023; Albergo et al., 2025) for the base-to-marginal transport step, but departs from the dominant optimal-transport/JKO-scheme line of work (Terpin et al., 2024; Jordan et al., 1998) and the minimal-kinetic-energy gradient-field approaches (Neklyudov et al., 2023; Berman et al., 2024; Blickhan et al., 2025). The key conceptual departure is a two-parameter decomposition: rather than learning the physics-time velocity directly or enforcing a gradient ansatz u=∇φ, the method learns only a sampling-time transport and then extracts the physics-time velocity as a consequence of flow consistency (commuting mixed derivatives). This avoids per-step OT coupling computations and removes the gradient restriction, while inheriting scalability from mature flow-matching training pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach scales population-dynamics inference to d > 10⁴ (128×128 barotropic flow), a significant step beyond prior work (32², 100, 50), and avoids the cubic-in-N cost of OT couplings. The theoretical guarantees—uniqueness of the induced physics-time velocity and regularity inheritance from the sampling-time transport—provide a principled foundation that prior OT-based or gradient-field methods lack. A key open question is what additional structure on the base-to-marginal velocity v is needed for the induced u to satisfy further criteria (low kinetic energy, minimal curl, interpretability), since the inductive bias of the learned dynamics is entirely determined by the choice of stochastic interpolant. The finding that non-minimal-energy fields can be smoother and more physically faithful for rotational dynamics challenges the implicit assumption in much of the literature that the minimal-kinetic-energy field is the canonical target.
