## Problem

In open-world joint planning, AI agents face knowledge gaps—unknown objects, ambiguous human intents—that perturb transition probabilities and reward functions, yet existing methods (RLHF, HITL-RL, pure-LLM planners) either cannot reason about how specific gaps affect planning outcomes or lack the ability to actively and optimally elicit the right human inputs. No prior work combines symbolic reasoning over knowledge gaps, a neural planning policy for impact estimation, and LLM-based query curation to achieve objective-driven active elicitation with performance guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment observations (7×7 grid views in MiniGrid, raw pixel frames in Atari Pacman, 3D Gaussian-splatting reconstructions in Isaac) plus a perception signal flagging low-confidence or unseen objects, and produces a sequence of strictly binary yes/no natural-language queries to a human, each of which prunes the symbolic tree and narrows the knowledge gap descriptor ⟨type, subtype, w_min, w_max⟩. The final output is an optimal action selected by maximizing the mean Q-value over the surviving descriptor space, with the number of queries per object typically 1–3 in the limited setting.

## Method

MINT constructs a breadth-first symbolic tree whose nodes encode knowledge gaps as descriptor tuples and whose edges are binary propositions about human-AI interactions; a UA-DQN (uncertainty-aware bootstrapped distributional DQN) is trained with augmented replay samples that randomly mask descriptor components so the network outputs both the mean and variance of Q-values over the descriptor space Φ_u. During deployment, the tree is expanded at each node only when the action-gap g(u) falls below λ_g·σ_u, ensuring expansion targets regions where uncertainty genuinely threatens the optimal action. An LLM then merges equivalent subtrees, formulates a single binary query per round that maximizes information gain on the optimal-action candidates across leaf nodes, and prunes branches inconsistent with the human's answer, iterating until the action is stable or a query budget is exhausted. The paper proves local pseudo-Lipschitz continuity of optimal Q-functions under a pseudo-metric on MDPs and derives an upper bound on the return gap between MINT-based planning and the ideal zero-gap case, showing that each binary split monotonically tightens this bound.

## Results

On MiniGrid with 5 uncertain objects, MINT (Standard) achieves 98.2% success rate and 9.69 average reward, compared to DQN (15.2% / −2.01) and PPO (69.2% / 5.42), while pure-LLM baselines (GPT-4o: 95% / 8.61; o3: 98% / 7.81) remain conservative on reward. On Atari Pacman, MINT (Standard) reaches 434.9 average return with only 6.8 queries per episode, versus 422.1 return requiring 27.1 queries for the Query-A (HITL-RL) baseline and 325.0 for PPO. On the NVIDIA Isaac search-and-rescue scenario, MINT* attains 100% and 95% success on the primary and hidden rescue targets, respectively, outperforming the best LLM baseline (o3: 93% / 9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MINT decisively outperforms both pure-RL baselines (DQN and PPO) as the number of uncertain objects increases: at 5 objects, DQN collapses to 15.2% success and negative reward (−2.01), while PPO reaches only 69.2% / 5.42, compared to MINT's 98.2% / 9.69. Against pure-LLM baselines, MINT matches or exceeds success rates (98.2% vs. 98% for o3) while substantially improving average reward (9.69 vs. 7.81 for o3, 8.61 for GPT-4o), because LLMs tend to generate conservative A*-style paths that avoid uncertain blocks rather than exploiting potential positive rewards. On Atari Pacman, MINT (Standard) achieves a higher return (434.9) than the Query-A HITL-RL baseline (422.1) while requiring roughly 4× fewer queries (6.8 vs. 27.1), demonstrating that MINT's structured elicitation is far more query-efficient than asking for expert actions whenever variance is high. On the Isaac search-and-rescue task, MINT* (using LLM for both planning and interaction, without the RL component) achieves 100% / 95% success on the two rescue targets, dramatically outperforming the best LLM baseline o3 (93% / 9%), confirming that the knowledge-gap reasoning and active elicitation mechanism transfers to non-RL, continuous-control settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

MINT sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) uncertainty-aware RL (specifically UA-DQN / bootstrapped distributional RL) for estimating planning-outcome variance, (2) neuro-symbolic reasoning that structures knowledge gaps as a symbolic tree, and (3) LLM-based natural-language interaction for query curation. It departs from prior HITL-RL work (which asks for expert actions under high variance) by instead reasoning about *which* knowledge gap matters and formulating targeted binary questions, and from prior language-based planning (which treats LLMs as zero-shot planners) by grounding the LLM's role in a structured symbolic tree whose leaves are evaluated by a trained neural policy. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to combine all three components for objective-driven active elicitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pseudo-Lipschitz continuity result and the return-gap upper bound give practitioners a concrete, interpretable stopping criterion for how many queries are needed, which is rare in active-learning-style RL settings. However, the descriptor space is limited to single-dimensional, axis-aligned perturbations of T or R, and all experiments use simulated benchmarks with oracle LLMs answering queries; real-world deployment with noisy, delayed, or cognitively loaded human responses remains unvalidated. Open questions include extending the descriptor to multi-dimensional or compositional perturbations, tightening the worst-case bound to a data-dependent one that integrates the UA-DQN variance estimate, and validating with non-expert human users.
