## Problem

Existing LLM-as-a-Judge systems suffer from three coupled failures: they cannot adapt to task- or domain-specific evaluation criteria, they are systematically biased by non-semantic cues such as answer position, length, and formatting, and they produce contradictory judgments when the same content is evaluated under different modes (e.g., pointwise vs. pairwise). The paper argues these issues stem from treating judging as a static prediction task rather than a learnable, regularized decision policy that can be explicitly constrained during training.

## Input / Output

The judge receives a question–answer pair (or two candidate answers for pairwise mode), an explicit evaluation rubric, and a mode indicator (pointwise or pairwise), and outputs a structured response containing a reasoning trace followed by a final decision token (A_win / B_win / tie for pairwise, or an integer score / label for pointwise). Two inference modes are supported: Full mode emits the complete reasoning chain, while Fast mode outputs only the final decision, yielding a ~13× speedup with minimal quality loss. Because the backbone is Qwen3-VL, the system also accepts RGB images alongside text for multimodal judging.

## Method

FairJudge reframes judging as a conditional decision policy π_judge(y | x, c, m) over input, evaluation context (rubric/reference), and mode, and trains it with a three-stage curriculum on a purpose-built 16K dataset. Stage I (SFT) uses canonicalized evaluation records where the rubric serves as both conditioning input and, in selected cases, prediction target, establishing rubric-aware judgment format. Stage II (DPO) optimizes over contrastive preference pairs constructed by applying targeted non-semantic perturbations (order swap, length change, format shift) to the same instance, so the chosen judgment is rubric-consistent and perturbation-invariant while the rejected one exhibits bias. Stage III (GRPO) samples multiple judgments per instance across pointwise and pairwise modes and applies a consistency-oriented hybrid reward—binary agreement for pairwise, linearly decaying absolute-error for scalar scoring, and exact match for label tasks—that penalizes cross-mode contradictions. The full objective L = L_SFT + λ_DPO·L_DPO + λ_GRPO·L_GRPO is optimized sequentially to avoid catastrophic forgetting, and the resulting 2B/4B/8B models (initialized from Qwen3-VL) are evaluated on PandaLM, JudgeLM, FairJudge-Benchmark-1K, MLLM-as-a-Judge, and Reward-Bench.

## Results

FairJudge-8B achieves the best macro-F1 across all three primary benchmarks: 72.18 on PandaLM (human annotations), 66.78 on JudgeLM, and 67.63 on FairJudge-Benchmark-1K, outperforming both much larger general-purpose models (Qwen2.5-72B: 64.58/61.92/59.72; DeepSeek-V3-671B: 58.83/59.75/50.26) and dedicated judge models (JudgeLM-7B: 63.92/66.66/66.11). On the pointwise–pairwise consistency metric, FairJudge-8B reaches 65.52%, surpassing the best baseline (Qwen3-VL-8B at 60.59%) by nearly 5 percentage points. On Reward-Bench, FairJudge-8B attains the highest Agreement (84.79%) and macro-F1 (56.94) among all compared models, including the 671B-parameter DeepSeek-V3 (83.34%/56.33%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairJudge-8B outperforms all five compared baselines on every primary benchmark. Against the largest general-purpose models, it leads Qwen2.5-72B by 7.6/4.9/7.9 F1 points on PandaLM/JudgeLM/FairJudge-Bench, and DeepSeek-V3-671B by 13.4/7.0/17.4 F1 points, showing that judge-specific training yields gains beyond raw parameter scale. Among dedicated judge models, FairJudge-8B surpasses JudgeLM-7B by 8.3 F1 on PandaLM and 1.5 F1 on FairJudge-Bench while essentially matching it on JudgeLM (66.78 vs. 66.66); it beats FlexVL-7B by 9.2/9.4/22.9 F1 and PandaLM-7B by 25.9/20.1/30.6 F1 across the three benchmarks. On the pointwise–pairwise consistency metric (Table 4), FairJudge-8B (65.52%) exceeds the next-best model Qwen3-VL-8B (60.59%) by 4.93 points, with all other baselines below 54%. On Reward-Bench, FairJudge-8B achieves the top Agreement (84.79%) and F1 (56.94), edging out DeepSeek-V3-671B (83.34%/56.33%) and Qwen3-VL-8B (82.08%/55.34%). On the multimodal MLLM-as-a-Judge suite, FairJudge-8B attains the highest average accuracy (69.52%), surpassing FlexVL-7B (68.49%) and Qwen3-VL-8B (67.23%), confirming that judge training does not degrade general multimodal understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

FairJudge builds on the LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm established by MT-Bench, JudgeLM, and PandaLM, and on preference-optimization methods (DPO, GRPO) from the RLHF literature. Its key conceptual departure is reframing judging from a static input-to-output function to a conditional decision policy π(y|x, c, m), where evaluation context and mode become explicit conditioning variables rather than implicit prompt artifacts. Operationally, it departs from prior judge models (JudgeLM, PandaLM, FlexVL) that rely on single-stage SFT or generic DPO by introducing a three-stage curriculum where each stage targets a distinct behavioral property—rubric adherence, non-semantic bias invariance, and cross-mode consistency—supported by purpose-built contrastive and cross-mode data constructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM-as-a-Judge systems become embedded in preference-learning and model-selection pipelines, the reliability of their outputs directly affects downstream training quality; FairJudge's explicit treatment of cross-mode consistency (pointwise vs. pairwise) addresses a practically common but underexplored failure mode. The released FairJudge-16K dataset and FairJudge-Benchmark-1K provide reusable resources for the community. However, the evaluation is limited to 8B-scale backbones, the benchmark shares its data-generation pipeline with training data (limiting true out-of-distribution generalization claims), and the paper does not exhaustively cover all bias types or domains, leaving open whether the policy-based framing and curriculum transfer to substantially larger models or more diverse evaluation settings.
