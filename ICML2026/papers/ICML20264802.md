## Problem

Reinforcement learning post-training for masked diffusion language models (DLMs) is hindered by the computational cost of trajectory likelihood estimation: a naive computation requires T forward passes (one per diffusion step), making standard policy gradient methods intractable. Existing approximations such as diffu-GRPO, which evaluates likelihood from a single all-mask input, introduce significant bias that degrades reasoning performance. The paper asks how to estimate trajectory likelihoods accurately and efficiently so that GRPO-style RL can be applied effectively to DLMs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained masked DLM (e.g., LLaDA-8B-Instruct, Eso-LM, Dream 7B) and a text prompt, and produces an RL-finetuned DLM that generates improved reasoning sequences. For d2-AnyOrder, the underlying model must natively support any-order decoding (independent-mask and order-causality attention properties); for d2-StepMerge, any standard masked DLM suffices. Training uses verifiable rewards (math correctness, Sudoku/Countdown validity, code pass rates) with group sizes of 6–16 and sequence lengths up to 512 tokens, evaluated under a fixed FLOP budget.

## Method

The paper derives a GRPO-style policy gradient for masked DLMs (Theorem 3.1) that decomposes the gradient over diffusion timesteps and token positions, requiring per-token trajectory likelihood ratios. For DLMs supporting any-order decoding, d2-AnyOrder computes exact trajectory likelihoods in a single forward pass by packing the sequence into a 2L-length input (clean tokens concatenated with mask tokens) and applying a custom attention mask that enforces order causality and independent masks. For standard masked DLMs, d2-StepMerge divides the T-step trajectory into N contiguous time segments and evaluates likelihood only at segment boundaries, reducing forward passes from T to N. The approximation error is provably bounded by D_N ≤ L·log(T/N + 1) + L·ε_block (Theorem 4.1), quantifying a compute-bias tradeoff where larger N improves accuracy at higher cost. Notably, diffu-GRPO is identified as the N=1 special case of d2-StepMerge, explaining its suboptimal RL performance.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, d2-StepMerge achieves state-of-the-art reasoning performance across all four benchmarks without supervised chain-of-thought finetuning: 91.9% on Sudoku, 56.6% on Countdown, 85.0% on GSM8K, and 41.6% on MATH500. On the Eso-LM toxicity steering task, d2-AnyOrder reaches a toxicity score of −0.7 at 1.25×10¹⁷ FLOPs, compared to −8.6 for DDPO under the same compute budget. On Set Diffusion (Qwen3-1.7B), d2-AnyOrder achieves 67% GSM8K accuracy versus 63% for diffu-GRPO under matched compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

d2-StepMerge dramatically outperforms d1 (Zhao et al., 2025), which combines SFT on long chain-of-thought data with diffu-GRPO, by +69.8 points on Sudoku (91.9% vs. 22.1%), +14.4 on Countdown (56.6% vs. 42.2%), +2.9 on GSM8K (85.0% vs. 82.1%), and +1.4 on MATH500 (41.6% vs. 40.2%). Against wd1 (Tang et al., 2026), which reformulates policy optimization as a weighted likelihood objective, d2 leads by +66.7 on Sudoku, +5.4 on Countdown, +2.7 on GSM8K, and +2.6 on MATH500. The base LLaDA model (Nie et al., 2025) and its improved variant LLaDA 1.5 are substantially outperformed on all benchmarks, confirming that the gains come from the RL framework rather than base model quality. On the Eso-LM toxicity steering task, d2-AnyOrder dominates DDPO (Black et al., 2024) at every compute checkpoint, reaching near-maximum toxicity (−0.7) while DDPO plateaus at −8.6. On Dream 7B (Ye et al., 2025), d2-StepMerge improves over diffu-GRPO by +12% on Sudoku (0.91 vs. 0.79) and +7% on GSM8K (0.80 vs. 0.73), confirming generalization across architectures. On Set Diffusion (Arriola & Kuleshov, 2026), d2-AnyOrder surpasses diffu-GRPO by 4 points on GSM8K (67% vs. 63%), validating the benefit of exact one-pass likelihood estimation over the N=1 approximation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-style RL framework (Shao et al., 2024) and the DDPO policy gradient for diffusion models (Black et al., 2024) to masked diffusion language models, deriving a DLM-specific policy gradient that decomposes over diffusion timesteps and token positions. The key departure from prior DLM-RL work (diffu-GRPO, wd1, DiffuCoder) is the treatment of trajectory likelihood estimation: where diffu-GRPO uses a single all-mask forward pass (equivalent to d2-StepMerge with N=1) and DiffuCoder uses the ELBO as a proxy, d2 introduces a family of estimators—exact one-pass for any-order dLLMs (leveraging the MDLM-to-AO-ARM equivalence from Hoogeboom et al., 2022) and a deterministic multi-pass approximation with a provable KL bound for standard MDMs. The composite-likelihood perspective (Besag, 1975; Varin et al., 2011) provides the theoretical scaffolding for the StepMerge estimator's compute-bias tradeoff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Accurate trajectory likelihood estimation is the central bottleneck for RL post-training of DLMs, and this paper provides both a principled solution (exact or bounded-approximation estimators) and empirical evidence that the previously-used biased estimators (diffu-GRPO, N=1) are a primary cause of suboptimal RL performance. The finding that popular MDMs like LLaDA do not natively support any-order decoding—despite the theoretical MDLM/AO-ARM equivalence—clarifies a practical gap that the community has been navigating without explicit diagnosis. A key limitation is that d2-AnyOrder requires models trained with any-order objectives (Eso-LM, Set Diffusion), which are still emerging; as these architectures mature, the exact-estimator path becomes more broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the StepMerge bound is tight in practice, whether the compute-bias tradeoff shifts at larger model scales, and whether the framework extends to continuous or flow-based DLMs.
