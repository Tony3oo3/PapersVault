## Problem

Despite Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDLMs) being trained with a global denoising objective and bidirectional attention, it remained unclear whether they truly overcome the locality biases well-documented in autoregressive models, or whether their unique use of mask tokens introduces new vulnerabilities. No systematic study had examined how the number and placement of mask tokens at generation time affect context comprehension, leaving a critical gap in understanding how MDLMs should be trained, evaluated, and deployed.

## Input / Output

The evaluation inputs consist of a text prompt containing a fixed set of relevant in-context examples (10) mixed with distractor examples (up to 40), followed by a test question whose answer is a single token, with a variable number of mask tokens appended to the sequence. The model produces a predicted answer token via greedy decoding in a single step (or iterative unmasking over multiple steps). Tasks are deliberately designed so the governing rule can only be learned from multiple examples and the answer spans exactly one token, enabling clean accuracy-based evaluation within the 2048–4096 token context windows of the studied models.

## Method

The paper introduces a mask-agnostic (MA) loss that enforces prediction invariance to the number of appended mask tokens. For each prompt-answer pair, two inputs are constructed with different random numbers of trailing mask tokens (l₁ and l₂); the loss combines a standard cross-entropy term (scaled by 1/p, following the masked diffusion objective) with a total-variation term (scaled by p) that penalises divergence between the answer-token probability distributions under the two masking configurations. This is applied as a supervised fine-tuning objective via LoRA adapters on the OpenOrca instruction-tuning dataset (~1.2k steps), requiring no architectural changes. The TV component is critical: ablation shows CE-only fine-tuning does not rectify mask sensitivity, while the full MA loss substantially reduces the distracting effect of extra masks, flattens the locality bias, and improves robustness with only 2–6 decoding steps rather than the 40 steps needed by iterative unmasking.

## Results

LLaDA-Base and LLaDA-Instruct suffer 23 and 27 percentage-point accuracy drops respectively when extra mask tokens are appended, while Dream-7B (initialised from Qwen2.5) degrades by only 6–8 points. Gradient attribution reveals that mask tokens receive 4–50× more normalised gradient mass than surrounding non-mask tokens (e.g., 0.234 vs 0.005 for LLaDA-Base), confirming masks act as strong distractors. Fine-tuning with the proposed mask-agnostic (MA) loss reduces relative performance degradation from 15.5% to 7.9% for LLaDA-Base (49% reduction) and from 27.5% to 17.0% for LLaDA-Instruct (38% reduction), while preserving language modelling quality (ROUGE-L and generative PPL unchanged within noise).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four MDLMs (LLaDA-8B Base/Instruct, Dream-7B Base/Instruct, LLaDA-MoE Base/Instruct) against two ARLMs (Llama3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B) on few-shot in-context learning tasks. All models exhibit a recency/locality bias, but MDLMs show a monotonic accuracy decline with distance (no primacy effect), whereas ARLMs display the characteristic U-shaped curve. LLaDA, trained from scratch with the masked diffusion objective, is far more vulnerable to extra masks (23–27 pp drop) than Dream, which was initialised from Qwen2.5-7B weights (6–8 pp drop), suggesting AR pre-training confers mask robustness. Replacing masks with repeated dots causes only 3–10 pp degradation, confirming the effect is specific to mask tokens rather than token repetition. The proposed MA loss (CE + TV) outperforms CE-only fine-tuning: the TV term is essential for enforcing prediction invariance across mask counts, and it also reduces the locality bias. On GSM8k and HotPotQA, the MA loss similarly flattens the performance-vs-mask-count curve, though fine-tuning on OpenOrca slightly degrades overall GSM8k accuracy (addressed by training directly on GSM8k).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied context-comprehension and position-bias literature for autoregressive language models (lost-in-the-middle, needle-in-a-haystack) to masked diffusion language models (MDLMs), asking whether the global denoising objective eliminates the locality biases attributed to the autoregressive loss. It departs from prior MDLM work (which has focused on scaling, speed, and controllability) by being the first systematic study of how mask tokens—both as training noise and as inference-time scaffolding—affect context processing. The proposed mask-agnostic loss (combining cross-entropy with a total-variation invariance term) is a new fine-tuning objective that has no direct precedent in the diffusion LM literature, though the TV-alignment idea echoes distribution-matching regularizers in other generative settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery of a 'mask tax'—where the very tokens required for parallel generation actively degrade context comprehension—has immediate implications for MDLM deployment, evaluation, and the design of fast samplers. The paper provides concrete evaluation guidelines (reporting mask count, mask-sensitivity analysis) that the rapidly growing MDLM community currently lacks. Open questions remain: whether uniform (non-masked) diffusion models avoid this issue, whether the locality bias is intrinsic to the diffusion objective or an artifact of the 1/p loss weighting, and whether the MA loss generalises to larger-scale or differently-trained models. The finding that a simple LoRA fine-tune with a TV term can substantially fix the problem suggests the issue is a training artifact rather than an architectural limitation, which is encouraging but also raises questions about how many other such artifacts exist in current MDLM training pipelines.
