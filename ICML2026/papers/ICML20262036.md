## Problem

Existing infrared-visible image fusion methods operate in flat Euclidean spaces that impose uniform distance metrics, distorting the inherently hierarchical semantic relationships between visual features and textual semantics. Additionally, prior text-driven fusion approaches require textual input at both training and inference, introducing operational complexity. This paper addresses both gaps by embedding cross-modal alignment in a hyperbolic manifold and designing a training strategy that eliminates the need for text at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262036/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a registered infrared-visible image pair as input. During training, BLIP automatically generates one caption per image on-the-fly (no manual annotation), producing two text embeddings per sample that condition the fusion process. At inference, no text is required—the learned gating weights allow attribute embeddings to functionally substitute for text features. The output is a single fused RGB image, evaluated on TNO, RoadScene, MSRS, and LLVIP benchmarks, with downstream object detection (M3FD) and semantic segmentation (MSRS) tasks.

## Method

TEDFusion combines a ViT-based visual encoder with a BLIP text encoder, connected through two key modules. The Attribute and Text Gating Fusion Module (ATGFM) extracts four hand-crafted attributes (saturation, brightness, GLCM-based texture complexity, and contrast) from each modality, then uses a learned gating network to dynamically balance attribute and text embeddings; 30% stochastic dropout on text during training enables text-free inference. The Text-Image Association Module (TIAM) projects both image and text features into a Poincaré ball via exponential mapping, applies a Möbius translation to reposition image features relative to the text anchor, and operates independently at 4 scales with a shared text embedding. An adaptive hyperbolic loss (Eq. 3) with scale-specific exponential weighting (Eq. 4) enforces hierarchical alignment, exploiting the ball's negative curvature to accommodate coarse-to-fine semantics without metric saturation. Features are mapped back to Euclidean space via logarithmic mapping before entering a nested multi-scale Transformer decoder (following Text-IF), and the total training objective combines pixel-level intensity, Sobel gradient, SSIM, and the hyperbolic loss terms.

## Results

On the four IVIF benchmarks (TNO, RoadScene, MSRS, LLVIP), TEDFusion achieves the best or second-best scores on 4 out of 5 metrics (SD, SF, AG, SCD) across all datasets. Headline numbers include SD of 60.85 on RoadScene (vs. 46.70 for the next-best Text-IF), SF of 13.88 on RoadScene, and AG of 4.83 on LLVIP. On downstream tasks, TEDFusion attains 57.37 mAP@0.5 for object detection on M3FD and 69.64 mIoU for semantic segmentation on MSRS, both leading all 11 compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEDFusion outperforms all 11 baselines on the majority of metrics across all four datasets, with the most pronounced margins in SD (contrast) and SF (spatial frequency). Against the strongest text-guided competitor Text-IF (Yi et al., 2024), TEDFusion leads on SD by 14.15 on RoadScene (60.85 vs. 46.70) and on SF by 3.50 (13.88 vs. 10.39), while Text-IF retains a slight edge on EN for TNO (7.14 vs. 7.09) and RoadScene (7.31 vs. 7.07) and on SF for LLVIP (16.29 vs. 15.70). Against GIFNet (Cheng et al., 2025a), the most recent task-agnostic fusion method, TEDFusion wins on EN, SD, AG, and SCD across all four datasets, though GIFNet edges ahead on SF for MSRS (12.71 vs. 12.22) and LLVIP (17.39 vs. 15.70). Traditional deep-learning baselines (DenseFuse, U2Fusion, ReCoNet, DeFusion, DCINN) are consistently surpassed by large margins on every metric, confirming that the hyperbolic text-driven design provides substantial gains over purely visual fusion. On downstream tasks, TEDFusion achieves 57.37 mAP@0.5 (vs. 53.45 for DeFusion, the next best) on M3FD detection and 69.64 mIoU (vs. 68.90 for GIFuse) on MSRS segmentation, further validating the quality of the fused output.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-guided image fusion line (TextFusion, Text-IF, TERF) by replacing the Euclidean cross-modal alignment with hyperbolic manifold learning in the Poincaré ball. It adopts the multi-scale Transformer decoder from Text-IF (Yi et al., 2024) and the BLIP text encoder, but introduces two novel modules: the Attribute and Text Gating Fusion Module (ATGFM), which injects hand-crafted GLCM-based visual attributes into the text stream, and the Text-Image Association Module (TIAM), which performs Möbius-translation-based alignment with an adaptive hyperbolic loss. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate threads—hyperbolic neural networks for hierarchical embeddings (Ganea et al., 2018b; Desai et al., 2023) and text-conditioned fusion—while departing from prior text-driven methods by eliminating the need for textual input at inference through stochastic dropout during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The text-free inference capability is a practical advance: it removes the operational burden of generating or providing scene descriptions at deployment time, making text-guided fusion more deployable in real-time surveillance and tracking pipelines. The application of hyperbolic geometry to IVIF is novel for this task, and the eigenvalue analysis (Table 4) provides a useful spectral justification for why the Poincaré ball may better preserve fine-grained multi-scale structure. However, the evaluation is limited to four relatively small datasets with a batch size of 2 on a single GPU, and the performance margins over strong text-driven baselines (Text-IF, GIFNet) are moderate rather than transformative. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic alignment advantage persists at larger scale, whether the hand-crafted GLCM attributes generalize beyond the tested scenes, and whether the curvature parameter c=1.0 is truly optimal or dataset-dependent.
