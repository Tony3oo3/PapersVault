## Problem

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) are trained with a sparsity hyperparameter L0 (average number of active latents per token) that is conventionally treated as a free design choice evaluated via sparsity-reconstruction tradeoff plots. This paper shows that L0 has an inherently correct value determined by the underlying feature structure of the LLM, and that setting L0 too low causes the SAE to learn polysemantic latents that mix correlated features, while setting it too high produces degenerate solutions. Critically, the standard sparsity-reconstruction evaluation is misleading: at low L0, an SAE with correct disentangled features achieves worse reconstruction than one that incorrectly mixes features, so the tradeoff plot would reject the correct SAE.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LLM hidden activations (500M tokens from The Pile for Gemma-2-2b and Llama-3.2-1b, with SAE width h=32768) and synthetic feature activations (15M samples for toy models with known ground-truth features). The key output is the c_dec metric — the mean absolute pairwise cosine similarity across all SAE decoder latent directions — computed directly from the trained decoder matrix W_dec after normalizing each column. This scalar value is plotted against L0 across a sweep of trained SAEs, and its minimum (or the 'elbow' before the sharp rise at low L0) identifies the optimal L0. The method also produces k-sparse probing F1 scores as a downstream validation signal.

## Method

The paper establishes both empirically and theoretically that MSE loss actively incentivizes feature mixing when L0 falls below the true L0: Theorem F.1 proves the disentangled SAE is not the MSE minimizer, and Theorem F.2 strengthens this to show it is not even a local minimum, with a strict descent direction toward mixed latents. To detect the correct L0 without ground-truth features, the authors introduce c_dec, the mean absolute pairwise cosine similarity of SAE decoder latents, which is theoretically shown (Theorem G.1) to be strictly higher when latents share mixed feature components. In toy models with known features, c_dec is minimized exactly at the true L0, and the same pattern holds for JumpReLU SAEs. On Gemma-2-2b and Llama-3.2-1b, the c_dec minimum (or elbow) at L0≈200 coincides with peak k-sparse probing F1, and the authors show that most open-source SAEs on Neuronpedia operate at L0<100, well below this optimum. The paper further demonstrates that transitioning L0 from too-high to correct recovers proper features, but transitioning from too-low to correct does not, indicating the low-L0 mixed solution is a hard local minimum.

## Results

In toy models with true L0=11, a trained BatchTopK SAE at L0=5 achieves MSE 2.73 while the ground-truth SAE (with correct disentangled features) achieves MSE 4.88, demonstrating that MSE loss actively incentivizes feature mixing at low L0. On Gemma-2-2b (layer 5) and Llama-3.2-1b (layer 7), the decoder pairwise cosine similarity metric c_dec is minimized near L0≈200, which coincides with peak k-sparse probing F1 performance. Analysis of open-source SAEs on Neuronpedia reveals that most commonly used SAEs have L0 below 100, well below the ~200–250 range identified as optimal for Gemma-2-2b layer 12.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between BatchTopK and JumpReLU SAE architectures at varying L0 values on Gemma-2-2b layer 12. Both architectures show elevated c_dec (indicating feature mixing) at low L0, but they diverge at high L0: BatchTopK SAEs show a sharp rise in c_dec above L0≈200, while JumpReLU SAEs maintain lower c_dec up to L0≈250–300, and JumpReLU also achieves substantially better k-sparse probing F1 at high L0. The authors attribute this to JumpReLU's per-latent thresholds, which can adapt to each latent's optimal firing rate, whereas BatchTopK uses a single global threshold. In the toy model setting, the key 'comparison' is between a trained SAE and a ground-truth SAE: at L0=5 (below the true L0 of 11), the trained SAE with mixed latents outperforms the ground-truth SAE on reconstruction (MSE 2.73 vs. 4.88), proving that sparsity-reconstruction tradeoff plots are an unsound evaluation criterion. The paper also shows that transitioning L0 from too-high to correct during training allows full recovery, but transitioning from too-low to correct does not, as the mixed latents form a difficult-to-escape local minimum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature hedging framework (Chanin et al., 2025) from the SAE-width setting to the L0 setting, showing that insufficient sparsity budget causes the same correlated-feature mixing pathology. It builds on BatchTopK (Bussmann et al., 2024) and JumpReLU (Rajamanoharan et al., 2024) as the two SOTA SAE architectures, and connects to the identifiability literature in dictionary learning (Donoho & Elad, 2003; Wang et al., 2020) by showing that the standard MSE objective actively drives the SAE away from the true dictionary when L0 is below the data's intrinsic sparsity. The paper departs from the dominant 'sparsity-reconstruction tradeoff' framing in the SAE literature (Cunningham et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2024; Rajamanoharan et al., 2024) by arguing L0 is not a free parameter but has a correct value determined by the training distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that most open-source SAEs (per Neuronpedia) have L0 well below the correct value implies that a large body of interpretability work may be operating on corrupted, polysemantic latents, with direct consequences for SAE-based steering and model editing. The c_dec metric offers a practical, training-free diagnostic for L0 selection, particularly valuable in domains without labeled probing benchmarks. Open questions include whether c_dec can be optimized automatically during training (the paper's preliminary attempt requires heavy hyperparameter tuning), how the analysis extends to non-linear features (Engels et al., 2025), and whether the 'elbow' heuristic generalizes across model scales and layers beyond the two small LLMs tested.
