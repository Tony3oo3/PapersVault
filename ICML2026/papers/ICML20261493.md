## Problem

Generative AI arenas (e.g., LMArena/Chatbot Arena) rank models from noisy pairwise user preferences, creating an incentive for producers to submit multiple clones of the same model to exploit estimation noise and artificially inflate their rank. This paper formally establishes when and how much producers can benefit from cloning under the standard Bradley-Terry MLE ranking, and asks whether a clone-robust alternative mechanism can be designed without requiring the platform to detect which models are duplicates.

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes three inputs: the full set of submitted models (which may include undetected clones), pairwise vote counts from user comparisons, and a ranking over their own models submitted by each producer. It outputs a single global ranking over all submitted models. Notably, the mechanism never receives or requires information about which models are clones; clone-robustness is achieved purely through the producer self-ranking constraint. Ties within a producer's models are broken according to the producer's submitted ranking, while ties across producers are broken arbitrarily.

## Method

YRWR (YouRank-We-Rank) augments the standard BT-MLE ranking with a monotone score correction: for each model j submitted by producer i, its corrected score is the minimum of the MLE-estimated scores of all models ranked at or above j in the producer's submitted ranking. The key insight is that for two clones of the same model, the maximum of their corrected scores is distributionally identical to a single model's score, eliminating the 'lottery ticket' selection effect that makes cloning profitable under the status quo. The paper proves YRWR is O(1/√s)-strategyproof (a producer gains at most O(1/√s) utility from submitting clones), that truthful producer rankings strictly improve ℓ∞-accuracy of the fitted scores, and that truthfulness is an approximately dominant strategy for sufficiently large s. Simulations calibrated to Arena data confirm near-zero rank gains from cloning under YRWR and consistent Kendall-Tau accuracy improvements over the status quo, even under noisy producer rankings.

## Results

Under the Status Quo (SQ) mechanism, some models gain ≥7 rank positions with just one clone, with further gains (mildly diminishing) from additional clones, particularly in arenas with fewer than 30 votes per model-pair (Coding, Expert, Multiturn). Under YRWR, rank gains from cloning are reduced to essentially zero across all arenas and all clone counts. On ranking accuracy (Kendall-Tau distance to ground truth), YRWR outperforms SQ across all six Arena arenas and all clone counts, often by many tens of swaps, with the gap widening as the number of clones increases. Even when producer rankings are perturbed with Gaussian noise (Kendall-Tau distance between 30 and 400 for ~300-model rankings), YRWR remains substantially more accurate than SQ for most noise levels, with the advantage only vanishing when noise is on the same order as the number of models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the Status Quo (SQ) mechanism used by Arena, which fits Bradley-Terry scores via MLE and directly reports the implied ranking. YRWR decisively outperforms SQ on clone-robustness: while SQ allows models to gain up to 7+ positions with a single clone (and more with additional clones), YRWR reduces rank gains from cloning to near zero across all six Arena arenas (Text, Coding, Expert, Multiturn, and others). On ranking accuracy measured by Kendall-Tau distance to the ground-truth ranking, YRWR achieves lower distance than SQ in every arena and every clone count, with the advantage growing as more clones are introduced. This accuracy advantage persists even under noisy producer self-rankings (perturbed via a random utility model with Gaussian noise), though it diminishes as noise variance increases and eventually drops below zero only when the noise in rankings is on the same order as the total number of models. Theoretically, YRWR is proven to be O(1/√s)-strategyproof (Theorem 4.1) and accuracy-improving in ℓ∞ norm under truthful rankings (Proposition 4.2), while SQ is proven to be clone-nonrobust under mild competitiveness conditions (Theorem 3.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the clone-robustness literature for Bradley-Terry models (Procaccia et al., 2025; Golz et al., 2025; Siththaranjan et al., 2023), which previously required voter heterogeneity (i.e., BT misspecification) to demonstrate non-robustness, by showing that clone exploitation arises even under a correctly specified, homogeneous preference model due to finite-sample noise. The proposed YRWR mechanism draws on Su (2021) and Su et al. (2025), who asked ICML authors to self-rank their submissions, but departs by using a minimum-based score correction (rather than isotonic regression) and by handling the endogenous candidate set where producers choose how many models to submit. The work also sits within the broader strategy-robust statistics program (Spiess, 2025; Bates et al., 2024) that treats statistical protocols as mechanism design problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AI arenas like LMArena/Chatbot Arena become economically consequential signals for consumers, investors, and procurement decisions, the demonstrated vulnerability (models gaining 7+ ranks from a single clone) threatens the credibility of these rankings. YRWR offers a practical, low-overhead fix—requiring only that producers submit a ranking over their own models—without needing clone detection or white-box access. Open questions include quantifying the residual 'new competitor effect' that still permits small rank gains under YRWR, the finite-sample truthfulness gap (producers may misrank to protect lower-priority models), and whether the mechanism's guarantees hold under more realistic non-uniform vote distributions across model pairs.
