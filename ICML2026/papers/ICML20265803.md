## Problem

Multimodal alignment objectives entangle cross-modal dependence with marginal-sensitive geometry, so approximately monotone feature-space distortions (gain changes, normalization drift, moderate quantization) can silently corrupt alignment even when the underlying dependence remains informative. Existing robustness methods address specific settings but do not provide a single operational contract that links invariance assumptions to measurable, label-free diagnostics and concrete deployment actions (update, remediate, or refuse).

## Input / Output

SSCA consumes frozen pre-trained encoder features as batch matrices H^(i) ∈ R^{m×p_i} (m=256 default) for d modalities (text, image, audio/video), with only projection heads W_i ∈ R^{p_i×k} (k=128) and alignment scalars trainable. Beyond the shared subspace embeddings, the protocol emits a four-element diagnostic vector (rank, coupling, sampling, numerical proxies) and a binary gate decision: Stability Mode permits the standard spectral update, while Fallback Mode suppresses adaptation and reverts to a compute-matched no-update control. The gate thresholds and inequality coefficients are deployment-local, calibrated from healthy unlabeled batches via quantile regression, and must be recalibrated when the backbone, normalization pipeline, or modality set changes.

## Method

SSCA decomposes the empirical operator perturbation E = M̂ − M_* into four controllable error sources and assigns each to a dedicated module: (i) clipped soft-rank Gaussianization (Φ⁻¹ of clipped sRank) suppresses marginal effects with a finite-sample monotone-invariance bound that tracks tie and approximation error; (ii) a dependence-weighted sliced-W₂ hub coupling aligns all modalities to a common 1D Wasserstein barycenter ordering, enforcing globally coherent multiway alignment and enabling cycle auditing; (iii) a diagonal-stabilized block-spectral learner (ridge-regularized whitened cross-covariances, top-k eigenspace via LOBPCG with implicit matvecs) identifies the shared subspace while exposing residual sampling and numerical error. The key theoretical contribution is a calibrated, actionable Davis-Kahan inequality bounding ‖sin Θ(Ũ, U_*)‖_F by c^T ε̂ / (γ̂ + 10⁻⁸), where the coefficients c are deployment-local scaling factors estimated by quantile regression on healthy unlabeled batches rather than universal constants. A label-free gate Gate(τ_gate, γ_min) maps the calibrated risk to a binary decision, triggering budget-constrained remediation (selecting hyperparameter adjustments that minimize diagnostic-weighted error under a FLOP budget) or conservative no-update fallback when the batch falls outside the certified monotone-drift regime.

## Results

Under 10× affine scaling, SSCA achieves 87.8% clean / 86.0% scaled accuracy (Δ=1.8) on CMU-MOSEI and 67.8% / 66.3% (Δ=1.5) on MELD, outperforming the strongest baseline MMML (Δ=3.2 and 2.7 respectively) by ~44% in degradation reduction. On CC3M-500K frozen-CLIP retrieval under JPEG compression at Q=50, SSCA attains 57.3% Recall@1 with only 2.5-point degradation, a 51.0% reduction versus the CLIP baseline's 5.1-point drop. Across six real-world deployment drift types (JPEG, tokenizer, audio codec on both datasets), SSCA maintains an average Δ of 1.6 versus 2.2 for MMML.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSCA outperforms all nine baselines on both clean accuracy and degradation under 10× affine scaling. Against the strongest general baseline MMML (Wu et al., 2024), SSCA reduces degradation from 3.2 to 1.8 on CMU-MOSEI and from 2.7 to 1.5 on MELD (~44% improvement). The contrastive baselines InfoNCE (Chen et al., 2020) and SigLIP (Zhai et al., 2023) suffer the largest degradation (Δ≈6.3–6.5 on CMU-MOSEI), while the correlation-based DCCA (Andrew et al., 2013) and GCCA are more robust (Δ≈2.9–3.3) but still trail SSCA by 1.0–1.5 points. The transport-based Sinkhorn (Cuturi, 2013) and SW-Align baselines achieve Δ≈3.3–3.5, confirming that pairwise OT without hub coupling is insufficient. On CC3M-500K frozen-CLIP retrieval, SSCA(CLIP) achieves 51.0% degradation reduction at Q=50 versus 23.5% for CLIP+MMML and 5.9% for CLIP+SigLIP, while matching the clean Recall@1 of CLIP+MMML (59.8%). The ablation 'Copula-only + SigLIP' isolates the marginal-stabilization contribution: it narrows the gap to full SSCA to only 0.5–0.7 points, indicating that hub coupling and spectral stabilization provide the remaining robustness beyond rank-based Gaussianization alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSCA unifies three previously separate research threads—copula-based rank Gaussianization (Sklar, 1959; Nelsen, 2006), sliced-Wasserstein barycenter coupling (Rabin et al., 2011; Cuturi & Doucet, 2014), and multi-view spectral alignment (Andrew et al., 2013; Bach & Jordan, 2002)—into a single deployment protocol with a shared error-source decomposition. The key conceptual departure is making the Davis-Kahan eigenspace perturbation bound (Davis & Kahan, 1970) operational by decomposing the unknown perturbation norm into four measurable diagnostic proxies calibrated from unlabeled data, rather than treating it as a purely theoretical guarantee. Prior robustness work (Ovadia et al., 2019; Koh et al., 2021) and deployment-shift monitoring (Gama et al., 2014; Guo et al., 2017) typically address single-modality or classification settings; SSCA extends this to multiway alignment with a formal invariance contract and a two-mode gate (stability vs. fallback) that no prior multimodal alignment method provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The calibrated actionable Davis-Kahan inequality (Theorem 4.1) is a genuinely useful theoretical tool: it converts an intractable eigenspace deviation bound into a label-free, computable risk score that drives a deployment gate, enabling practitioners to monitor alignment quality without ground-truth labels. However, the formal guarantee is scoped to approximately coordinate-wise monotone marginal distortions, and the calibration coefficients are deployment-local, requiring recalibration whenever the backbone, normalization, or modality set changes—limiting plug-and-play applicability. Open questions include behavior under non-monotone semantic drift, scaling to very large modality counts in the hub coupling, and whether the frozen-feature protocol's conclusions transfer to end-to-end training. The protocol's conservative fallback (no-update) is a safety policy, not an accuracy guarantee, so high-stakes deployments still need domain-specific validation.
