## Problem

Block-sparse attention reduces prefilling cost by skipping low-importance blocks, but its effectiveness is limited by information fragmentation: globally important key tokens (heavy hitters) are scattered across many blocks, forcing retrieval of numerous blocks to capture sufficient attention mass. Existing methods passively select blocks from this fragmented structure rather than optimizing the structure itself, yielding diminishing returns as context length grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264834/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the Q, K, V matrices of a pre-trained LLM during the prefilling phase, with block size B=128 and segment size S=256. It computes a global importance score vector from the last block of queries (O(N·B·d) cost), permutes K and V within each segment by descending importance, performs block-sparse attention with a mean-pooling selection mask (threshold 0.9), and applies the inverse query permutation to restore original token order. The output is the standard attention output in the original sequence ordering, making it a drop-in replacement for FlashAttention in any causal LLM.

## Method

PBS-Attn formalizes the permutation invariance/equivariance properties of attention (Theorem 3.3) to justify reordering tokens without changing the final output. To reconcile permutation with causal masking, it introduces segmented permutation: the sequence is partitioned into contiguous segments of size S, and permutation is applied only within each segment, preserving inter-segment causality while enabling intra-segment reordering. Keys within each segment are sorted by a global importance proxy computed from the last query block, clustering heavy hitters into contiguous high-density blocks. Queries retain their natural order (P_σ = I) since analysis shows marginal gains from query permutation. The method is plug-and-play (no retraining), implemented via a custom permuted-FlashAttention kernel in Triton, and is agnostic to the specific block selection algorithm, allowing combination with existing selection strategies like XAttention's antidiagonal scoring.

## Results

On LongBench, PBS-Attn achieves an average score of 37.37 (Llama-3.1-8B) and 36.37 (Qwen-2.5-7B-1M), closely matching the full-attention baselines (38.28 and 37.01) while outperforming all sparse-attention baselines. On LongBenchv2, PBS-Attn scores 29.82 and 34.39 respectively, surpassing the best sparse baseline (XAttention: 29.62 and 34.19). On RULER, PBS-Attn+ (with antidiagonal scoring) reaches 86.87 (Llama) and 87.71 (Qwen), within 1.2 and 0.5 points of full attention. In terms of efficiency, PBS-Attn achieves up to 2.75× end-to-end prefilling speedup at 256K context and 3.41× attention speedup at 512K, with permutation overhead below 4% at 128K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PBS-Attn consistently outperforms all four sparse-attention baselines across all three benchmarks. Against MInference (offline pattern search), PBS-Attn gains +0.31 on LongBench (Llama) and +0.20 on LongBenchv2 (Qwen), while on RULER PBS-Attn+ exceeds MInference by 0.02 (Llama) and 1.48 (Qwen). Against FlexPrefill, PBS-Attn shows the largest margins: +6.81 on LongBench (Llama) and +5.86 on LongBenchv2 (Qwen), as FlexPrefill suffers severe quality degradation on synthetic and code tasks. Against XAttention, PBS-Attn leads by +0.95 on LongBench (Llama) and +0.20 on LongBenchv2 (Qwen); on RULER, PBS-Attn+ surpasses XAttention by 0.41 (Llama) and 1.05 (Qwen). Against the unpermuted MeanPooling baseline (same block-selection strategy without permutation), PBS-Attn gains +0.70 on LongBench (Llama) and a striking +4.54 on LongBenchv2 (Qwen), confirming that the permutation itself—rather than the selection algorithm—drives the sparsity improvement. In efficiency, PBS-Attn achieves the highest speedup across all context lengths (up to 2.75× at 256K), whereas MInference shows no speedup until 128K and XAttention's gains stagnate after 128K.

## Relation to Prior Work

PBS-Attn builds directly on FlashAttention's tiled online-softmax computation, adding a permutation layer and block-sparse mask on top. It positions itself as orthogonal to the line of block-sparse attention work (MInference, FlexPrefill, XAttention, SpargeAttention, Prism) that focuses on improving block *selection* algorithms; instead, PBS-Attn restructures the attention matrix itself via token permutation to make any selection algorithm more effective. The idea of reordering tokens for attention efficiency traces back to Reformer's LSH bucketing, but PBS-Attn departs by being plug-and-play (no architectural changes or retraining), compatible with causal masking via its segmented permutation strategy, and driven by data-dependent global importance rather than fixed hashing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.75× prefilling speedup with near-full-attention quality on multiple model families (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B-1M, Qwen3-8B) makes this a practically deployable technique for long-context serving, and the plug-and-play design lowers adoption barriers. However, the method is only beneficial above ~16K context (overhead dominates at shorter lengths), is limited to prefilling (not decoding), and the 5.2% of heads that are harmed by permutation are not addressed—head-level pruning is left as future work. An open question is whether the last-block proxy for global importance remains robust in highly heterogeneous or adversarial inputs, and whether the approach transfers to the increasingly common sliding-window or hybrid attention patterns in newer architectures.
