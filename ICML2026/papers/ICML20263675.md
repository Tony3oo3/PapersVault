## Problem

Adapting pretrained flow or diffusion generative models to sample from reward-tilted distributions (e.g., Boltzmann densities or reward-aligned images) has lacked a scalable regression objective that avoids differentiating through stochastic trajectories, computing spatial reward gradients, or tuning reward multipliers. Existing approaches such as adjoint matching, DRaFT, and DEFT either require backpropagation through generated paths, demand differentiable rewards, or suffer from high-variance Monte Carlo estimators that degrade as the tilt strength increases.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained velocity field b_t (parameterized by a neural network, e.g., an EGNN for molecular systems or a U-Net for Stable Diffusion 1.5), a scalar reward function r(x) (e.g., ImageReward score or negative Lennard-Jones energy), and a text prompt for image generation. It produces a tilted velocity field b_{t,a} that, when integrated from a Gaussian prior, yields samples from the reward-tilted target distribution. Notably, the method requires only scalar reward evaluations—no reward gradients—and operates without a reward multiplier (λ=1 suffices), unlike adjoint matching which needs λ up to 10² for competitive performance.

## Method

Tilt Matching derives an exact ODE governing how the stochastic interpolant velocity field evolves under exponential reward tilting: the infinitesimal change ∂b_{t,a}/∂a equals the conditional covariance between the interpolant velocity and the reward given the current interpolant state (Proposition 3.2). The Implicit Tilt Matching (ITM) variant eliminates discretization error by matching the full cumulant expansion via a fixed-point regression objective with a stop-gradient residual (e^{hr(x₁ᵃ)}−1)(Ė_tᵃ − sg(b̂_t)(I_tᵃ)), which is shown to have strictly lower gradient variance than weighted flow matching. The learned drift is proven to coincide with the probability flow ODE of a Doob h-transformed SDE with a specific diffusion coefficient tied to the interpolant schedule, linking Tilt Matching to stochastic optimal control without requiring trajectory simulation or reward gradients. Training proceeds by iteratively annealing a from 0 to 1, at each step drawing samples from the current tilted distribution and performing a simple L² regression on the scalar-reward-weighted residual.

## Results

On LJ-13 Boltzmann sampling, ITM achieves an effective sample size (ESS) of 0.507, more than doubling the best prior method (iDEM at 0.231), and reduces the 1D energy histogram W2 distance to 0.879 (vs. 1.99 for ASBS, the next best). On LJ-55, ITM attains E(·)W2 = 26.37 and GeoW2 = 3.98, both slightly better than ASBS (28.10 and 4.00). For Stable Diffusion 1.5 fine-tuning with ImageReward, ITM at λ=1 reaches 0.4465 ImageReward, roughly double Adjoint Matching at the same λ (0.2170), while also improving CLIPScore (0.2794 vs. 0.2754) and HPSv2 (0.2659 vs. 0.2576) without requiring a reward multiplier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LJ-13, ITM dominates all five sampling baselines across every reported metric: it nearly triples the ESS of iDEM (0.507 vs. 0.231), halves the E(·)W2 of ASBS (0.879 vs. 1.99), and achieves the best GeoW2 (1.54 vs. 1.59 for ASBS). On LJ-55, ITM edges out ASBS by a small margin on both E(·)W2 (26.37 vs. 28.10) and GeoW2 (3.98 vs. 4.00), while substantially outperforming DDS (173.09) and iDEM (93.53) on energy histogram distance. For Stable Diffusion fine-tuning, ITM at λ=1 more than doubles Adjoint Matching's ImageReward score (0.4465 vs. 0.2170) and also wins on CLIPScore and HPSv2, though it trails AM(λ=100) on ImageReward (0.4465 vs. 0.7873) and loses on DreamSim diversity (0.3383 vs. 0.3826 for AM λ=1). The key practical advantage is that ITM achieves competitive ImageReward without a reward multiplier, whereas AM requires λ=100 to reach its best score, suggesting ITM is more stable and less sensitive to hyperparameter tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tilt Matching builds directly on the stochastic interpolant framework (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2022; Albergo et al., 2023) and the Esscher transform, deriving a closed-form covariance ODE that governs how velocity fields evolve under exponential reward tilting. It departs from the dominant trajectory-based fine-tuning and sampling approaches—adjoint matching (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2025), DRaFT, D-Flow, and adjoint Schrödinger bridge samplers—by replacing backpropagation through ODE/SDE trajectories and reward gradient computations with a simple scalar-reward regression. The paper also establishes a formal equivalence between the learned drift and the probability flow ODE of a Doob h-transformed diffusion, connecting the method to stochastic optimal control without requiring an SDE-based control formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's practical advantages—no reward gradients, no trajectory backpropagation, no reward multiplier tuning, and strictly lower gradient variance than weighted flow matching—make it attractive for settings with non-differentiable or expensive-to-differentiate rewards and for scaling to large generative models. The DreamSim diversity drop (0.385→0.338) in the Stable Diffusion experiments suggests a tension between reward alignment and sample diversity that remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the iterative annealing schedule can be accelerated or replaced by a single-shot formulation, how the method behaves at much larger scales (video, 3D), and whether the control variate optimization can be made fully adaptive without additional network capacity.
