## Problem

Existing post-training backdoor detectors fail in two important settings: (1) when non-target classes possess strong intrinsic features that produce extreme detection statistics, masking the true backdoor target or causing false positives, and (2) when the backdoor trigger signal is weak relative to intrinsic class-discriminative features, yielding missed detections. The root cause is that the target class's detection statistic conflates contributions from both the backdoor trigger and its intrinsic features, while non-target classes only reflect intrinsic features.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a trained DNN classifier (no access to the original training set) and a small set of clean samples per class (10 per class in experiments, with good results down to 5). The detector optimizes over variables specific to each base method (e.g., input images for MMBD, spatial mask + pattern for Neural Cleanse, additive perturbation for PT-RED) in the network's internal feature space (last convolution layer), producing a per-class detection statistic (maximum margin, maximum logit, mask norm, or perturbation norm). The class with the most extreme statistic is flagged as the backdoor target, and a threshold on the statistic determines whether the model is clean or poisoned.

## Method

Class Subspace Orthogonalization (CSO) is a plug-and-play penalty added to existing detectors' optimization objectives. In a first step, a class-specific soft mask v_k ∈ [0,1]^d is learned for each class by simultaneously minimizing classifier loss on masked features and maximizing loss on complement features over a small clean set, identifying the class's intrinsic feature subspace. In the second step, during the detector's optimization for a putative target class t, a CSO penalty (Eq. 5) penalizes the average ReLU-rectified cosine similarity between the detector's search direction S_a(z(Θ;x)) and the intrinsic subspace v_t ⊙ S_a(x^(t)), pushing the search into directions orthogonal to intrinsic features where backdoor trigger components are more likely to reside. For non-target classes, the weight vector lies entirely within the intrinsic subspace, so orthogonalization zeroes out their detection statistic; for the target class, the backdoor trigger component (not in span(Y_t)) survives, making it a clear outlier. The framework is demonstrated as a drop-in modification to four detectors—MMBD-CSO, MLBD-CSO, NC-CSO, and PT-RED-CSO—each requiring only the addition of λ·C_t(z) to the original loss.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (all-to-one attacks, Table 1), MMBD-CSO achieves 96% detection accuracy on clean models (4% false positive rate) and outperforms MMBD on most attacks: 92% vs 82% on BadNet, 76% vs 30% on LC, 36% vs 0% on Bpp, and 84% vs 76% on IAD. NC-CSO improves over NC on WaNet (54% vs 26%) and IAD (74% vs 44%), while PT-RED-CSO improves over PT-RED on BadNet (54% vs 0%) and blend (100% vs 90%). On the novel mixed-label (ML) one-to-one attacks (Table 2), MMBD-CSO still leads: 94% on ML-1-pixel vs 20% for MMBD, and 70% on ML-BadNet vs 40% for MMBD, though all detectors see substantial drops compared to their dirty-label counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMBD-CSO dominates MMBD across nearly all attacks on CIFAR-10: it improves clean-model specificity from 86% to 96%, BadNet from 82% to 92%, LC from 30% to 76%, and Bpp from 0% to 36%, while matching MMBD's 100% on chess and 1-pixel. NC-CSO substantially outperforms NC on stealthy attacks (WaNet 54% vs 26%, IAD 74% vs 44%, Bpp 18% vs 0%) and on BadNet (100% vs 90%), though it slightly underperforms on clean models (70% vs 72%). PT-RED-CSO improves over PT-RED on BadNet (54% vs 0%), blend (100% vs 90%), and WaNet (62% vs 46%), but both struggle on ML-BadNet (16% vs 12%). Against UNICORN and BTI-DBF, MMBD-CSO achieves far lower false positives (4% vs 48% and 100% respectively on clean models) while matching or exceeding their detection accuracy on most attacks. In the appendix comparison (Table 27), MMBD-CSO outperforms BAN on 10 of 12 attack types on CIFAR-10 one-to-one settings, e.g., 96% vs 82% on BadNet and 94% vs 46% on ML-1-pixel, while using only 10 clean images per class versus BAN's 500.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimization-based post-training backdoor detection paradigm established by MMBD (maximum margin), Neural Cleanse (trigger inversion via blending), and PT-RED (perturbation minimization) by introducing a class-subspace orthogonalization (CSO) penalty that suppresses intrinsic class-discriminative features during the detector's search. Unlike prior feature-decoupling methods (Wang et al., 2022a; 2023; Xu et al., 2024a) that learn a single global soft mask, CSO learns a class-specific mask and uses it to penalize alignment with the putative target class's intrinsic subspace, guiding the optimizer toward directions where backdoor trigger features are more likely to reside. The paper also introduces a mixed clean/dirty-label poisoning attack that departs from standard dirty-label attacks by embedding triggers in non-source classes with correct labels, reducing collateral damage and making detection substantially harder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of CSO—requiring only a small clean set per class and a single penalty term added to the detector's loss—makes it broadly applicable across the existing landscape of post-training detectors without architectural changes. The demonstration that mixed-label attacks are dramatically harder to detect than their dirty-label counterparts (e.g., MMBD-CSO drops from 96% to 68% on 1-pixel) highlights a significant gap in current defenses against surgical, low-collateral-damage backdoors. Open questions include whether CSO can be integrated into backdoor mitigation (not just detection) pipelines, whether the approach scales to very large foundation models, and whether the class-specific mask assumption holds in highly overlapping feature spaces (e.g., fine-grained classification). The paper also notes that extremely large CSO penalties can degrade performance under adaptive attacks that deliberately embed intrinsic features into the trigger.
