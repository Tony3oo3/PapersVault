## Problem

While mechanistic interpretability has illuminated jailbreaks, misalignment, and hallucinations in LLMs, the internal mechanisms of backdoor attacks remain largely unexplored. Existing backdoor research focuses on mitigation or statistical outlier detection without identifying which specific attention heads encode backdoor features or how those features propagate through layers, leaving a critical gap in understanding and neutralizing this threat at its core.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes intermediate hidden states (at the last token position) and per-head attention activations extracted from a backdoor-injected LLM during inference on both clean and trigger-embedded inputs. It produces three outputs: binary backdoor/clean classification labels from lightweight probes, per-head ACIE scores (Casual Indirect Effect values) ranking attention heads by backdoor significance, and a sample-agnostic Backdoor Vector (a single d_m-dimensional vector) that can be added to or subtracted from one hidden state during inference to activate or suppress the backdoor without modifying model weights.

## Method

BkdAttr is a tripartite causal tracing framework. First, Backdoor Probes (MLP or SVM classifiers) are trained on layer-wise representations to confirm that learnable backdoor features exist and are progressively refined across layers (95%+ detection accuracy). Second, BAHA identifies responsible attention heads via activation substitution: mean head activations are computed over poisoned inputs, then each head's activation is individually replaced on clean inputs while measuring the change in conditional generation probability of the backdoor output (CIE), yielding a |y'|× speedup over autoregressive ASR-based attribution. Third, the Backdoor Vector is constructed as the sum of top-k ACIE head activations (~3% of all heads) and exhibits two complementary properties: Additive Activation (adding V_b to a clean hidden state triggers the backdoor, boosting ASR toward 100%) and Subtractive Suppression (subtracting V_b from a triggered hidden state neutralizes the backdoor, reducing ASR toward 0%), both operating on a single representation in early-to-middle layers.

## Results

The Backdoor Probe achieves 95%+ test accuracy in distinguishing backdoor from clean representations across all three backdoor types and both LLM architectures. Ablating the top ~3% of attention heads identified by BAHA reduces Attack Success Rate (ASR) by over 90% (e.g., jailbreak ASR drops from 75.78% to 7.81% on Llama2-7B with 16 heads ablated). The Backdoor Vector, constructed from these attributed heads, can elevate ASR from 0% to 85.16% (Additive Activation) or suppress it from 75.78% to 0.39% (Subtractive Suppression) on Llama2-7B jailbreak backdoors, and from 91.94% to 0.00% on Qwen2.5-7B label modification backdoors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing interpretability frameworks; instead, it validates its BAHA-identified Backdoor Vector against a random baseline (vectors constructed from randomly sampled 32 attention heads). The BAHA vector dramatically outperforms the random baseline on both Additive Activation and Subtractive Suppression: for example, on Llama2-7B jailbreak backdoors, BAHA achieves 85.16% ASR via addition (vs. 6.71% random) and 0.39% via subtraction (vs. 71.41% random), a gap of ~78 and ~71 percentage points respectively. On Qwen2.5-7B label modification, BAHA reaches 100% activation and 0% suppression, while random vectors achieve only 0.65% and 89.68%. The random baseline's ΔASR ranges narrowly between 0.63% and 10.31%, confirming that the BAHA-attributed heads are non-trivially specific to backdoor functionality. General-capability evaluations (GSM8K, MMLU) after ablating the top-32 BAHA heads show at most a modest drop (−13.9% on MMLU for one configuration) and sometimes slight improvements (+8.8% on GSM8K), indicating the identified heads are backdoor-specific rather than general-purpose.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability toolkit (causal tracing, attention head attribution) previously applied to jailbreaks, alignment, and hallucination (Zhou et al., 2024a,b; Chen et al., 2024) to the underexplored domain of LLM backdoors. It specifically adapts the attention head attribution paradigm from Zhou et al. (2024b) but replaces ASR-based scoring with conditional generation probability for a |y'|-fold speedup. Prior backdoor interpretability work was limited to statistical outlier detection (Zhao et al., 2024; Yi et al., 2024), prompt-based self-explanations (Ge et al., 2024), or toy-model MLP analysis (Lamparth & Reuel, 2024); this paper departs from all of these by providing a unified causal framework spanning representation probing, head-level attribution, and vector-level intervention on full-scale LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that backdoor behavior is controlled by a sparse set (~3%) of attention heads, and that a single extracted vector can act as an on/off switch via simple addition or subtraction on one hidden state, provides a concrete, actionable mechanism for both diagnosis and removal of backdoors without degrading general capability. This opens a path toward targeted, minimal-intervention defenses that are more precise than full-model retraining or broad pruning. Key open questions include whether the framework generalizes to larger models, newer attention variants (MLA, sliding-window), deeply entangled semantic triggers, and settings where clean reference data is unavailable — all acknowledged as limitations by the authors.
