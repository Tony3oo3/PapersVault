## Problem

Existing one-step autoregressive video generation methods suffer from motion collapse (videos freezing at the initial frame) and training instability. The root causes are architectural—symmetric causal discriminators cannot detect global temporal degradation or long-range drift—and procedural—adversarial distillation from scratch is unstable when early predictions lie far from the data distribution.

## Input / Output

Given a single conditioning image (encoded as the first KV-cache chunk) and an optional text prompt, AAD-1 generates video autoregressively in chunks of 4 latent frames, requiring only one denoising step per chunk. The model uses a sliding window of 9 recent frames plus 1 sink token for long-horizon consistency, enabling indefinite streaming. Main evaluations produce 5-second 480p clips, with zero-shot extensions to 20-second rollouts and 720p resolution.

## Method

AAD-1 breaks the symmetry between generator and discriminator: the generator remains strictly causal (block-causal attention) to preserve autoregressive sampling, while the discriminator uses a bidirectional DiT backbone that attends over the full spatiotemporal volume and produces a single video-level realism score via learnable query tokens with cross-attention at layers 19, 29, and 39. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: (i) ODE initialization via Diffusion Forcing on teacher denoising trajectories, (ii) one-step DMD warmup under self-rollout to bring the student distribution close to the teacher, and (iii) asymmetric adversarial refinement with timestep-dependent noise injection on discriminator inputs and approximated R1/R2 regularization (λ=20). The bidirectional discriminator provides future-anchored gradients that penalize early frames inconsistent with the full sequence, directly addressing the linear error accumulation inherent in causal rollouts.

## Results

On VBench-I2V (480p, 5-second clips), AAD-1 with full three-stage training (1 NFE) achieves the best autoregressive performance in Subject Consistency (94.34), Background Consistency (95.08), I2V Subject faithfulness (98.65), I2V Background faithfulness (97.83), and Imaging Quality (71.49). It substantially outperforms CausVid (4 NFE) and Self Forcing (4 NFE) on conditioning preservation and scene coherence while requiring only a single forward pass per chunk. The bidirectional Wan 2.1 I2V reference (100 NFE) still leads on Dynamic Degree (51.09 vs. 41.46) and Aesthetic Quality (64.97 vs. 60.07), but AAD-1 narrows the gap to within 1–2 points on most quality metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CausVid (4 NFE), AAD-1 (Stage-III, 1 NFE) wins on every metric except Motion Smoothness (98.22 vs. 98.61, a negligible gap), with the largest margins on Subject Consistency (+10.89), I2V Subject (+5.74), and I2V Background (+14.49). Against Self Forcing (4 NFE), AAD-1 leads on Subject Consistency (+2.57), Background Consistency (+1.67), I2V Subject (+2.86), I2V Background (+6.65), and Dynamic Degree (+6.53), while Self Forcing retains a slight edge on Aesthetic Quality (+0.89) and Imaging Quality (+0.01). Compared to the bidirectional Wan 2.1 I2V reference (100 NFE), AAD-1 surpasses it on Subject Consistency (+0.46), Background Consistency (+0.22), I2V Subject (+1.85), and I2V Background (−0.76, slightly behind), but trails on Dynamic Degree (−9.63) and Aesthetic Quality (−4.90), reflecting the inherent trade-off of compressing 100 sampling steps into one.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive video distillation line initiated by CausVid and Self Forcing, which demonstrated that pretrained bidirectional diffusion models can be adapted to causal streaming generators via block-causal attention and self-rollout training. The key departure is from APT2 (Lin et al., 2025b), the closest prior work on adversarial distillation for autoregressive video, which uses a symmetric causal discriminator with frame-wise scoring. AAD-1 breaks this symmetry by pairing a causal generator with a bidirectional, video-level discriminator, and introduces a three-stage training pipeline (ODE → DMD warmup → adversarial refinement) that decouples distribution matching from adversarial refinement, unlike prior methods that optimize both jointly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-step autoregressive video generation is critical for real-time streaming applications (gaming, interactive world models) where multi-step sampling is too slow, and this paper demonstrates that the previously assumed necessity of 4+ NFEs can be overcome with the right discriminator architecture and training schedule. The clean ablation showing that causal + frame-wise discrimination produces completely static videos (Dynamic Degree 1.08) identifies a fundamental architectural failure mode that likely affects other autoregressive GAN-based systems. Open questions include whether the bidirectional discriminator advantage persists at larger scales or with longer training horizons, and whether the Stage-II/Stage-III trade-off (dynamics vs. consistency) can be resolved rather than merely traded off.
