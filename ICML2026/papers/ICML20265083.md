## Problem

Knowledge transfer phenomena—Knowledge Distillation, Weak-to-Strong generalization, and Self-Distillation—have been studied in isolation, with no unified theoretical framework explaining why and when transfer is effective across disparate capacity regimes. Prior theoretical analyses overlooked the spectral properties of the data covariance matrix, which fundamentally governs which features are learned fast or slow under SGD, leaving the core mechanics of transfer uncharacterized at the level of individual frequency components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265083/cropped_page6_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input a pair of linear models (teacher and student) defined by feature extraction matrices over a shared orthonormal basis, along with SGD hyperparameters (initial step size, step-decay schedule with interval K = N/log₂N). It produces a finite-sample excess risk bound decomposed into three physically distinct terms—propagated teacher error, student optimization error, and irreducible alignment bias—parameterized by the student's effective spectral cutoff k*. From this decomposition, the paper derives the Distillation Efficiency Ratio (DER) for the strong-teacher regime and the Performance Gap Recovered (PGR) metric for the weak-to-strong regime, both as functions of sample sizes N, n and spectral decay exponents.

## Method

The paper establishes a unified spectral analysis of single-pass SGD dynamics in high-dimensional linear regression, justified via NTK theory for over-parameterized networks. The central result (Theorem 1) decomposes the teacher-to-student transfer risk into three components governed by the student's effective dimension k*, revealing two distinct mechanisms: in the KD regime, the strong teacher extends the student's 'Spectral Horizon' (k* ∝ N^{1/α}), enabling capture of high-frequency signals statistically inaccessible from raw data; in the W2S/SD regime, the student suppresses the teacher's optimization variance in the unlearned spectral tail by a factor O(δ²), performing 'Spectral Denoising' that recovers the underlying population geometry. The analysis tracks the second-moment matrix of SGD iterates under a step-decay schedule, showing that the interplay between implicit regularization (which splits the bias at k*) and heterogeneous spectral learning speeds (governed by eigenvalue decay rate α) fully determines transfer efficacy. Empirical validation on UTKFace with ViT, ResNet, CLIP, and DINO models confirms the predicted spectral decay relationships and early-stopping behavior.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical results are: (1) In the KD regime, the Distillation Efficiency Ratio (DER) diverges as Õ(N^κ) when the student is strictly weaker than the teacher (Theorem 3), confirming provable data amplification; (2) In the W2S regime, the Performance Gap Recovered (PGR) approaches 1 as N→∞ with a strictly positive rate gain Δ_rate = 2α_S/[α_T(2α_S+1)] (Theorem 5); (3) Self-distillation yields a pure denoising gain of Δ_SD = 2/(2α_T+1) > 0 (Corollary 1). Empirically, on UTKFace age regression, distillation from a ViT-L/16 teacher consistently outperforms direct linear-probe training, with gains increasing as the spectral decay gap Δα = α_S − α_T grows (Figure 2d). For W2S, early stopping at an intermediate training stage is critical, and models with lower task-specific intrinsic dimensionality (e.g., ResNet50-DINO) recover a larger fraction of the generalization gap (Figure 3d).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing KT methods; instead, it compares internal regimes. The key empirical comparisons are: (1) Direct learning from ground-truth labels vs. transfer learning from teacher labels — distillation consistently wins, with the margin scaling with the spectral decay difference Δα (Figure 2a, 2b, 2d); (2) In W2S, the student trained via distillation from a weak AlexNet teacher is compared against the weak teacher itself and a strong ceiling model — the student surpasses the teacher (R_T2S < R_T) and the PGR quantifies how much of the gap to the ceiling is recovered (Figure 3d); (3) Across architectures on UTKFace (ResNet18, ResNet50, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/16), stronger models exhibit slower spectral decay (smaller α), validating Assumption 2 (Figure 2c); (4) For W2S, projection energy concentrates in a low-dimensional subspace (Figure 3b), and the intrinsic dimension required to retain 90% of performance is lower for higher-performing models (Figure 3c), confirming the low-intrinsic-dimensionality assumption (Assumption 5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SGD analysis framework of Ge et al. (2019) and Wu et al. (2022) for overparameterized linear regression, extending it to a two-phase teacher-student setting with a step-decay schedule. It departs from prior KD theory (Hinton 2015, Allen-Zhu & Li 2023) and W2S theory (Burns 2023, Ildiz 2025, Moniri & Hassani 2025), which treated these phenomena in isolation and largely through static or offline regression lenses, by explicitly leveraging the spectral structure of the data covariance matrix to characterize dynamic SGD learning progress. The NTK justification (Jacot 2018, Malladi 2023) grounds the linear-model abstraction in modern deep learning practice, while the paper's central novelty is showing that a single spectral decomposition (Theorem 1) simultaneously explains both Spectral Horizon Expansion in KD and Spectral Denoising in W2S.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified spectral framework provides the first rigorous theoretical account of why knowledge transfer works across fundamentally different capacity regimes, resolving a long-standing gap where KD, self-distillation, and W2S were each explained by separate heuristics. The identification of three practical indicators of model strength (representation rank, spectral decay rate, task-specific intrinsic dimension) gives practitioners theoretically grounded metrics beyond ad-hoc proxies like RankMe or alpha-ReQ. Key limitations include the restriction to linear/kernel models (though NTK-justified), the step-decay schedule assumption, and relatively modest empirical validation (linear probing on UTKFace). Open questions include whether the spectral mechanisms survive in genuinely non-convex deep network training and whether the framework extends to multi-step or iterative distillation protocols.
