## Problem

Existing trajectory world models for diverse robotic systems force heterogeneous dynamics to share a single dense parameter set, causing gradient conflicts and negative transfer that limit scalability as robot diversity grows. Additionally, these methods treat trajectories as pure token sequences while ignoring robot morphological structure, leaving them without the physical inductive biases needed for zero-shot generalization to unseen robotic systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264689/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 50-step state-action history and a sequence of k future actions, where each scalar channel is min-max normalized and discretized into one of K=256 uniform bins. Uniquely, it also ingests the robot's kinematic tree (converted to a binary tree via LCRS transformation) to extract per-body structural indices that are embedded and added to trajectory tokens. Output is a categorical distribution over 256 bins per state channel per future timestep, enabling up to 100-step multi-step prediction in a single forward pass via learnable query embeddings rather than autoregressive decoding.

## Method

WestWorld combines two core components: (1) a Knowledge-Encoded Embedding Modular that converts each robot's kinematic tree to a binary tree via LCRS transformation, extracts pre/in/post-order traversal indices per body node, and injects the resulting structural embedding as an additive inductive bias into trajectory token representations; and (2) a System-Aware MoE (Sys-MoE) block where a learnable system embedding is propagated through a Mamba-style selective SSM to produce system-dependent softmax routing weights over P=4 MLP experts, so that each robot's dynamics are modeled as a weighted composition of shared basis dynamics. Each Sys-MoE block first applies self-attention across state channels and cross-attention with action embeddings, then the SSM+MoE layer; six such blocks are stacked. The model is trained with next-token cross-entropy over the 256-bin categorical representation, pretrained on 89 environments (80 simulated + 9 real-world robot-arm) spanning diverse morphologies, and supports few-shot fine-tuning and downstream MPPI-based model-predictive control.

## Results

On zero-shot 100-step trajectory prediction, WestWorld achieves MAE of 16.35 (Walker2D), 13.73 (Hopper), and 7.74 (Franka) in normalized space, outperforming the strongest baseline TrajWorld by 26.3%, 20.7%, and 41.0% respectively. In few-shot adaptation (10 episodes), WestWorld reduces MAE to 5.32 (Cassie), 4.23 (A1), and 4.93 (UR5), beating TrajWorld by 32.1%, 17.6%, and 38.9%. For downstream MPPI control, pretrained WestWorld achieves accumulated rewards of 2134.6 (Walker2D), 2253.5 (Hopper), and 2.20 (Go1), substantially exceeding all baselines—most notably a 4.2× improvement over TrajWorld on Hopper (2253.5 vs. 534.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WestWorld consistently outperforms all three baselines across every evaluation setting. Against MLPEnsemble (Chua et al., 2018), the gains are largest on Franka (MAE 7.74 vs. 12.16, a 36% reduction) and Walker2D (16.35 vs. 26.01, 37% reduction), while TDM (Schubert et al., 2023) is weakest on Franka (MAE 23.69) due to its flattening approach struggling with the mobile manipulator's heterogeneous state space. The closest competitor is TrajWorld (Yin et al., 2025), which WestWorld beats by 20–41% on zero-shot MAE and by 18–39% on few-shot MAE. The advantage is most pronounced in downstream MPPI control: on Hopper, pretrained WestWorld achieves 2253.5 accumulated reward versus 534.3 for TrajWorld (4.2×) and 154.6 for TDM (14.6×), and on Go1 it reaches 2.20 versus 0.49 for TrajWorld. The scalability analysis further shows that while TrajWorld's error degrades significantly as pretraining environments grow from 1 to 89, WestWorld's error remains nearly flat, confirming that the Sys-MoE routing effectively isolates system-specific dynamics and avoids the gradient interference that limits dense shared-parameter models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trajectory world model paradigm for heterogeneous robots established by TDM (Schubert et al., 2023) and TrajWorld (Yin et al., 2025), which discretize state-action channels into tokens and use Transformer architectures for joint multi-robot pretraining. WestWorld departs from these dense shared-parameter designs by introducing a system-aware Mixture-of-Experts (Sys-MoE) layer built on Mamba-style selective SSMs (Gu & Dao, 2024), where a learnable system embedding routes to specialized experts per robot, and by injecting morphology-aware structural embeddings derived from LCRS tree traversals (Hong et al., 2021) as an explicit inductive bias. The key conceptual shift is from 'one model for all dynamics' to 'a composition of basis dynamics with system-dependent coefficients,' directly addressing gradient interference and negative transfer that limit prior approaches at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that scalable multi-robot trajectory world models are achievable without the performance degradation that plagues dense shared-parameter baselines, and that incorporating physical structure as an inductive bias meaningfully improves zero-shot generalization to unseen morphologies. The real-world Unitree Go1 deployment, where the distilled model enables stable locomotion while the strongest baseline fails, underscores practical relevance for model-based control in legged robotics. Key limitations include the absence of visual observations (trajectory-only modeling), reliance on discretized state representations that may lose information for high-dimensional continuous states, and the fact that the structural embedding requires access to kinematic tree specifications, which may not be available for all real-world systems. Open questions include whether the MoE routing generalizes to truly novel morphologies never seen in any form during pretraining, and how the approach scales to multimodal (vision + trajectory) world models.
