## Problem

Supervised graph outlier detection is severely limited by class imbalance (e.g., only 1.3% outliers in DGraph), and while recent diffusion-based augmentation methods improve sample quality, they are hindered by the high computational cost of multi-step iterative sampling and poor handling of heterogeneous graphs with multiple edge relations. This paper asks whether a single-step generative model grounded in consistency principles can synthesize high-fidelity graph outliers efficiently while natively supporting multi-relational structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262602/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a heterogeneous graph with node feature vectors, multiple typed edge relations, and binary anomaly labels on a subset of training nodes. It produces synthetic outlier nodes with reconstructed attribute vectors and predicted edges (both existence and relation type) that are inserted into the original graph to form an augmented training set. Generation is conditioned on the anomaly label (y=1) and requires only a single forward pass (1-NFE) of the OC network, making the augmented data reusable across any downstream GNN detector without retraining the generator.

## Method

GOCM is a two-stage framework: first, a Multi-input Variational Graph AutoEncoder (MiVGAE) encodes the heterogeneous graph into a unified latent space via relation-specific GNN convolutions followed by cross-relation fusion, learning a structured prior over node embeddings; second, an Origin Consistency (OC) model is trained in this frozen latent space to map Gaussian noise directly to the data origin in one step. The key theoretical contribution is the OC mechanism, which replaces the standard consistency model's point-to-origin mapping with an 'Interval-based Origin Inference' strategy: the network g_θ(x_t, t, s, c) uses the local trajectory segment over [s, t] along a linear probability flow (ReFlow) to infer x_0, reducing consistency error compared to mapping a single point. The training loss is derived by integrating the velocity field along the linear path, yielding a regression target that includes gradient correction terms (∂g_θ/∂t and Jacobian-vector products), with a stop-gradient operator applied to avoid second-order backpropagation. A stabilization factor w(t,s) = t/max(s, ε) and a warm-up schedule λ(k) handle numerical singularity near s→0 and early-training instability, respectively.

## Results

On homogeneous graphs, GOCM achieves the best AUC of 99.62% on Weibo, 83.69% on Tolokers, 77.08% on Questions, and 88.40% on Elliptic, surpassing the strongest diffusion-based baseline GODM on Weibo (99.62 vs 99.57 AUC), Tolokers (55.39 vs 52.95 AP), and Elliptic (46.02 vs 43.92 AP). On heterogeneous graphs, GOCM achieves 92.05% AUC and 72.15% AP on Yelp, and 93.36% AUC and 72.13% AP on Amazon, outperforming GODM by 2.64 and 2.83 AUC points respectively. In wall-clock time, GOCM requires 23.48s on Weibo and 413.68s on DGraph, compared to 33.01s and 683.95s for GODM, while DiffGAD runs out of memory on Questions and DGraph.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOCM outperforms all general GNN baselines (GCN, SGC, GIN, GraphSAGE, GAT, GT) by large margins across all four homogeneous datasets; for example, on Tolokers, GOCM's 83.69% AUC exceeds GraphSAGE (79.42%) by 4.27 points. Among dedicated graph outlier detectors, GOCM surpasses the strongest spectral-based methods (BWGNN, GHRN, BernNet) and attention-based methods (GATSep) on all metrics; on Weibo, GOCM's 99.62% AUC beats BWGNN (98.29%) and GHRN (97.21%). Against the closest competitor GODM (latent diffusion), GOCM wins on Weibo (all 3 metrics), Tolokers (all 3 metrics, +2.44 AP), Elliptic (AP +2.10, Rec +0.09), and DGraph (all 3 metrics), but slightly trails GODM on Questions (AP 19.39 vs 20.48, Rec 24.04 vs 24.66) and Elliptic AUC (88.40 vs 89.77). On heterogeneous graphs, GOCM dominates all baselines including RGCN, HGT, H2-FDetector, and CARE-GNN, achieving 92.05% AUC on Yelp (vs. 90.85% for CARE-GNN) and 93.36% AUC on Amazon (vs. 92.35% for GODM). In efficiency, GOCM reduces wall-clock time by 29% over GODM on Weibo (23.48s vs 33.01s) and 40% on DGraph (413.68s vs 683.95s), while DiffGAD fails with OOM on Questions and DGraph. The ablation confirms that replacing the OC mechanism with standard CM degrades performance (e.g., Tolokers AP drops from 55.39 to 53.55), validating the interval-based origin inference design.

## Relation to Prior Work

GOCM sits at the intersection of three research threads: consistency models (Song et al., 2023), latent diffusion architectures (Rombach et al., 2022), and variational graph autoencoders (Kipf & Welling, 2016). Its core departure from standard consistency models is the 'Interval-based Origin Inference' mechanism, which replaces point-to-origin mapping with trajectory-segment-to-origin mapping grounded in linear probability flow geometry (following Rectified Flow, Liu et al., 2023). Relative to the closest prior work GODM (Liu et al., 2025), which uses multi-step latent diffusion for graph outlier synthesis, GOCM collapses the iterative reverse process into a single function evaluation and extends the encoder to handle multi-relational heterogeneous graphs via MiVGAE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making high-quality graph outlier synthesis feasible at scale: single-step generation (1-NFE) eliminates the computational bottleneck of 50+ diffusion steps, and the model-agnostic plug-in design means synthetic outliers can be reused across any downstream GNN detector without retraining the generator. The robustness to extreme label scarcity (maintaining ~95 AUC when only 1% of anomaly labels are retained) is a practically valuable property for real-world financial and fraud-detection settings. Open questions include whether the linear trajectory assumption holds well for highly non-linear graph manifolds, whether generation directly in graph space (bypassing the latent bottleneck) could improve fidelity, and whether the framework extends naturally to graph-level (rather than node-level) anomaly synthesis.
