## Problem

Diffusion LLMs (DLLMs) using block-diffusion decode an entire block of B tokens in parallel at every denoising step, yet only ~10% of those tokens are actually decoded, wasting ~90% of FLOPs on non-decodable tokens. This shifts DLLM inference from the memory-bound regime of ARLLMs to a strictly compute-bound regime, causing throughput to plateau at large batch sizes and preventing the scaling essential for production deployment.

## Input / Output

FOCUS operates as a training-free inference-time system that intercepts the forward pass of a block-diffusion DLLM. It takes a masked token block of size B (typically 32) plus the preceding context sequence, computes importance deltas after the first two transformer layers, and then processes only a reduced subset S (|S| << B) through the remaining layers. The output is the same decoded text tokens as standard block-diffusion, but with 65–80% fewer tokens processed per step, translating to up to 3.52× throughput at large batch sizes.

## Method

FOCUS exploits a newly discovered correlation: the importance delta (ΔI = I^(Layer1) − I^(Layer0), computed via aggregated incoming attention scores with MaxPool1D) strongly predicts whether a masked token will be successfully decoded in the current step. A dynamic budgeting mechanism sets the retention count K per step using both a cumulative arithmetic mean of past decoded tokens (scaled by expansion factor α) and an instantaneous variance-based count N_σ, ensuring the budget adapts to both historical and current signal strength. After computing Q/K projections for the full block through Layers 0–1, FOCUS selects the top-K tokens by ΔI, enforces two structural constraints (AR-Context Preservation to retain each candidate's predecessor, and Placeholder Integrity to keep unprocessed masked tokens before any selected candidate), and gathers only the retained hidden states for Layers 2 through L. An Intra-Block KV Cache with a Neighbor-Aware Stability Criterion freezes decoded tokens' KV states only after both the token and its right neighbor are decoded, preventing error propagation in CPT-based architectures. The entire mechanism is implemented with custom Triton kernels for ragged attention, sparse KV cache filling, and fused state compaction, adding only ~1% step latency overhead.

## Results

FOCUS achieves up to 2.32× throughput improvement over LMDeploy at standard block size B=32 (peak 2,324 tokens/s on WildChat at batch size 256), scaling to 3.52× at B=64 where computational redundancy is heaviest. The computational redundancy ratio (N_processed/N_decoded) drops from 15.02 to 3.12 on ShareGPT (~79% reduction), and from 21.47 to 4.30 on WildChat for LLaDA2.0. Generation quality is preserved or improved: at the default configuration (α=1.5, Conf=0.8), FOCUS achieves an average score of 68.37 across GSM8K, Math500, HumanEval, MBPP, and IFEval, outperforming the conservative Baseline at Conf=0.9 (67.60). On an NVIDIA H100, FOCUS maintains up to 2.33× throughput improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LMDeploy (the production-grade baseline with Continuous Batching, PagedAttention, and FlashAttention), FOCUS wins decisively at production-scale batch sizes: 2.32× throughput at B=32 and 3.52× at B=64 on SDAR, while matching or exceeding generation quality on all five benchmarks. On the MoE model LLaDA2.0, FOCUS boosts throughput from 2,181 to 3,369 tokens/s at batch size 256 on ShareGPT. However, at batch size 1, FOCUS is slightly below LMDeploy (e.g., 89.6 vs. 122.5 tokens/s on MATH for SDAR) due to scheduler overhead that is not amortized, suggesting adaptive enablement is needed for low-concurrency settings. Against Fast-dLLM v2 (Wu et al., 2026a), FOCUS achieves up to 8.35× throughput on ShareGPT and up to 1.91× at larger batch sizes, as Fast-dLLM v2 still recomputes the full block before decoding each sub-block, leaving the core redundancy unaddressed. FOCUS also outperforms LMDeploy on H100 hardware (up to 2.33×), confirming the gains are not architecture-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper pivots the attention-sparsity insight from ARLLM KV-cache eviction (H2O, SnapKV, Quest) to a fundamentally different target: compute-centric query-token eviction within diffusion blocks. It builds on the Block-Diffusion paradigm (SDAR, LLaDA2.0) that solved the periodic KV-cache recomputation problem, and adapts the Delayed Cache mechanism from dKV-Cache for intra-block state reuse. The key departure is identifying that the Layer-1-minus-Layer-0 attention delta serves as a training-free decodability predictor, enabling proactive eviction of non-decodable tokens before the expensive subsequent layers execute—something no prior DLLM inference system (Fast-dLLM, dLLM-Cache, dKV-Cache) attempts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DLLMs are increasingly viewed as a viable alternative to autoregressive models, but their compute-bound decoding has been a major deployment barrier; FOCUS's 3.52× throughput gain at large batch sizes directly addresses this and restores the throughput-scaling behavior that ARLLMs enjoy. The finding that FOCUS *improves* generation quality at relaxed confidence thresholds (by filtering false-positive decodings) suggests the eviction mechanism acts as an implicit safety filter, opening questions about whether decodability prediction can replace or complement confidence-based decoding entirely. Open limitations include the necessity to disable LMDeploy's multi-loop optimization, a slight regression at batch size 1, and the fact that DLLM inference stacks still lag ARLLM systems in overall maturity.
