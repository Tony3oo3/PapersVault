## Problem

Neural PDE surrogates for engineering design must generalize to geometries not seen during training, yet most operator-learning methods either restrict to rectilinear domains or treat geometry as a passive input modality without enforcing physical structure. Existing approaches fail to produce meaningful predictions on out-of-distribution geometries and do not exactly preserve conservation laws or boundary conditions, limiting their reliability for design exploration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264016/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a shape-regular simplicial finite element mesh with labeled boundary partitions, from which it derives coordinate-free geometric descriptors (heat kernel signature, harmonic coordinates, signed distance function) alongside metric and topological FEM matrices. The mesh serves a dual role: it provides tokens for a transformer geometry encoder and simultaneously defines the basis for the learned reduced finite element spaces. Output is a PDE solution field obtained by solving a nonlinear reduced-order finite element system (with only P=16–32 degrees of freedom) via batched Newton iterations, rather than by direct regression, yielding exact satisfaction of Dirichlet boundary conditions and discrete conservation.

## Method

Geo-NeW jointly learns (i) a geometry-conditioned partition-of-unity basis defining reduced Whitney finite element spaces and (ii) a Lipschitz-bounded nonlinear flux constitutive law, both parameterized by transformers conditioned on an inducing-point anchor encoder that processes mesh-derived spectral and harmonic features. The learned system is a mixed finite element discretization of a conservation law (∇·J = f with J = ε∇u + F_θ), where the diffusive term εKu provides a stable linear backbone and learning is restricted to the nonlinear flux F_θ, whose Lipschitz constant is explicitly bounded to guarantee local uniqueness and invertibility of the Jacobian. Training proceeds via PDE-constrained optimization: at each step a nonlinear forward solve produces the solution, an adjoint solve yields gradients, and implicit differentiation through the final Newton iteration updates parameters. The exact-sequence property of Whitney forms (d_k W^k ⊂ W^{k+1}) is preserved under the learned reduction, ensuring discrete conservation and stability are inherited from the continuum structure rather than approximated.

## Results

On standard PDE benchmarks (Table 2), Geo-NeW achieves normalized L2 errors of 0.35×10⁻² on Elasticity and 0.11×10⁻² on Pipe flow, outperforming the strongest prior method (LaMO) by 29% and 71% respectively. On the NACA airfoil benchmark, Geo-NeW reports 0.82×10⁻² (modeling a derived quantity), competitive with but not surpassing LaMO (0.41×10⁻²). On out-of-distribution geometry generalization (Table 1), Geo-NeW achieves 7.87×10⁻² on NS2d-c++ OOD (mild) versus 13.1×10⁻² for the best baseline, and 42.2×10⁻² on the full OOD setting versus 83.1×10⁻² for GNOT. Additionally, Geo-NeW enforces Dirichlet boundary conditions exactly (0.00 boundary error) compared to 2.22–7.58×10⁻³ for the baseline encoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Geo-NeW outperforms all seven baselines on the Pipe flow benchmark (0.11 vs. best 0.38 for LaMO, a 71% improvement) and on Elasticity (0.35 vs. 0.50 for LaMO, a 29% improvement), establishing state-of-the-art on these standard variable-geometry PDE tasks. On NACA, Geo-NeW (0.82) trails LaMO (0.41) and Transolver (0.53), though it models a derived quantity (Mach number) rather than the primary PDE state, making direct comparison less favorable. On out-of-distribution geometry generalization, Geo-NeW dominates all baselines: on NS2d-c++ OOD (mild) it achieves 7.87×10⁻² versus 13.1×10⁻² (Linear Attention), 14.1×10⁻² (GNOT), and 15.1×10⁻² (Transolver); on the full OOD setting it reaches 42.2×10⁻² versus 83.1×10⁻² (GNOT) and 91.4×10⁻² (Transolver). The paper also shows that Transolver fails to produce meaningful predictions beyond θ=20° on the angled-step geometry, while Geo-NeW maintains stable extrapolation. A key structural advantage is exact boundary condition enforcement: Geo-NeW reports 0.00 Dirichlet boundary error on NS2d-c, whereas the baseline encoder produces errors of 2.22–7.58×10⁻³.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FEEC-based neural PDE framework of Kinch et al. (2025), Actor et al. (2024), and Trask et al. (2022), which constructed data-driven Whitney form models for fixed geometries, by making both the reduced finite element basis and the constitutive flux model geometry-conditioned through coordinate-free descriptors (HKS, harmonic coordinates, SDF) processed by an inducing-point transformer. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant operator-learning paradigm (GNOT, Transolver, Geo-FNO) that treats geometry as an input modality to a regression map, instead learning a reduced-order PDE operator whose solution is obtained by an implicit Newton solve on a geometry-adapted mesh. The Lipschitz-bounded flux parameterization and the partition-of-unity construction for the reduced basis are novel contributions that ensure well-posedness across arbitrary geometries without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Geometry generalization is identified as a central barrier for deploying scientific foundation models in engineering design, where the geometries of interest are precisely those absent from training data. The demonstration that a structure-preserving model with only 16–32 reduced degrees of freedom outperforms unconstrained transformers on out-of-distribution geometries suggests that physical inductive bias can substitute for model capacity in extrapolation regimes. Key limitations include restriction to 2D steady-state PDEs, the requirement for a precomputed mesh and labeled boundary partitions, and the additional nonlinear solve cost at inference (though still ~100× faster than the reference COMSOL solver). Open questions include extension to 3D and time-dependent problems, whether the Lipschitz stability constraint unduly limits expressivity for strongly nonlinear physics, and scaling to the large, diverse corpora needed for true foundation-model behavior.
