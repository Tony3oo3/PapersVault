## Problem

Assessing the accuracy of conditional distributions p(y|x) when only joint samples (x,y) from the true process are available remains challenging: existing coverage tests (e.g., TARP) do not yield a scalar score suitable for model ranking, probability integral transform methods do not scale to high dimensions, and classifier-based scores require additional neural network training. No prior method provides a stable, sample-based fidelity score that works under the single-ground-truth-sample-per-conditioning-variable constraint inherent to joint data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes L joint fiducial pairs (x*, y*) from the true model, N i.i.d. candidate conditional samples drawn per x*, a user-specified distance metric d, and a distribution p(c) for sampling region centers. It produces a single scalar Mira score bounded in [1/2, 1], with a theoretical reference value of 2/3 and variance 1/18 under correct specification, enabling direct numerical comparison and ranking of multiple candidate models. The critical constraint is that only one ground-truth realization y* is available per conditioning variable x*, which rules out standard two-sample tests.

## Method

Mira constructs random regions R as balls centered at c ~ p(c) with radius set by the distance to a randomly drawn candidate sample y_r, then counts how many of N candidate samples fall inside R (count n) and whether the single true sample y* falls inside (indicator k). The key theoretical result is that under the null hypothesis (candidate equals true), the region mass λ_n is Uniform[0,1] due to the probability integral transform, which yields a closed-form Bayesian expression for p(k|n) via Laplace's rule of succession: (n+1)/(N+2) when k=1 and (N-n+1)/(N+2) when k=0. The Mira score is the Monte Carlo average of this statistic over all fiducials and regions. Under correct specification, the per-trial statistic converges in distribution to Beta(2,1), giving the theoretical mean 2/3 and variance 1/18, while the score is provably bounded below by 1/2 (achieved only for disjoint supports). This Bayesian formulation replaces the sample-sensitive p-value of the frequentist PQMass with a stable scalar that supports model comparison without evidence computation.

## Results

Under the null hypothesis (correctly specified model), the Mira score converges to a theoretical mean of 2/3 with variance 1/18, and is bounded below by 1/2. On MNIST, the conditional diffusion model (CDM) attains a Mira score approaching 2/3, while the conditional VAE (CVAE) scores approximately 0.56, confirming CDM's superior conditional distribution fidelity. In the strong gravitational lensing experiment (13-dimensional parameter space), Mira correctly ranks all four candidate forward models, with the true EPL + 3 Sersic model closest to 2/3 and the SIE + 1 Sersic model furthest. In the high-dimensional (3×64×64) source reconstruction task with only L=16 true samples, Mira still correctly identifies the true prior+noise model and ranks the remaining three candidates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is TARP (Lemos et al., 2023), a coverage-probability test that also operates on joint samples. Mira outperforms TARP in model ranking: while TARP's Expected Coverage Probability (ECP) curves confirm which model is correct (the true model lies on the diagonal), the curves for misspecified models are difficult to interpret and rank quantitatively (e.g., the blue, red, and maroon curves in Figure 4). Mira provides a single scalar per model, enabling unambiguous ranking. In the MNIST experiment, both methods agree on the CDM > CVAE ordering, but Mira quantifies the gap (≈2/3 vs. ≈0.56) while TARP only shows qualitative deviation from the diagonal. In the strong lensing and source reconstruction experiments, Mira's scalar scores clearly separate all four candidate models, whereas TARP curves for the three incorrect models overlap or are ambiguous in their ordering. The paper also notes that Mira avoids the need for neural network training (unlike LC2ST) and does not require multiple samples from the true conditional (unlike PQMass or standard two-sample tests), making it applicable in the single-ground-truth-sample regime that defines the problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mira directly extends the PQMass framework (Lemos et al., 2025), which compares marginal distributions via sample counts in random regions, to the conditional setting by adopting a Bayesian perspective that handles the single-ground-truth-sample constraint. It departs from TARP (Lemos et al., 2023) and Simulation-Based Calibration, which provide coverage or rank-based diagnostics but yield non-scalar outputs (coverage curves, per-dimension rank histograms) that resist quantitative model ranking. The key conceptual shift is from frequentist p-values (PQMass) to a closed-form Bayesian probability—Laplace's rule of succession—obtained by marginalizing over the uniform mass of randomly constructed regions, yielding a scalar score with known theoretical moments under the null.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mira provides the first sample-based scalar score for conditional distribution accuracy that simultaneously enables model ranking, scales to high dimensions without neural network training, and admits a theoretical reference value (2/3) with quantifiable uncertainty—addressing a gap left by TARP's hard-to-interpret coverage curves and SBC's per-dimension limitations. Its application to Bayesian model comparison in parameter space offers a practical alternative to intractable evidence computation in high-dimensional inference problems. A key limitation is that the score is a necessary but not sufficient condition for calibration (sufficiency requires a less sample-efficient variant), and its behavior under structured, non-i.i.d. misspecification patterns remains less characterized. Open questions include how the score discriminates among models with similar but distinct misspecification directions, and whether the Beta(2,1) characterization tightens with additional design choices.
