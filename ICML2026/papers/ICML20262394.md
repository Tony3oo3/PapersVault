## Problem

Existing multi-agent environment co-design methods collapse under high-dimensional design spaces (e.g., placing 50 obstacles on a 16×16 grid yields ~10^53 configurations) and suffer from severe sample inefficiency because the optimal environment shifts as agent policies evolve (policy shift). Prior approaches either impose restrictive parametric assumptions (truncated Gaussians, evolutionary methods) or decouple agent and environment optimisation, preventing shared utilisation of costly rollout data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes agent observations (local sensor readings, global environment maps, wind conditions in WFCRL) and a parameterised environment design space Θ (e.g., binary H×W×N_colours shelf masks or coordinate-colour pairs in D-RWARE, continuous turbine positions in WFCRL, obstacle positions in VMAS). It produces two coupled outputs: a valid environment configuration θ drawn from a reward-maximising distribution (enforcing hard constraints like non-overlapping shelves or minimum turbine distances via projection operators), and joint agent policy actions. The diffusion model operates on a wider continuous domain X ⊇ Θ, with a projection operator P_Θ mapping samples back to the feasible set at each guidance step.

## Method

DiCoDe pre-trains a diffusion model on the uniform distribution of valid environments, then in the main loop alternates between PUG-guided environment sampling, MARL rollouts, and joint parameter updates. Projected Universal Guidance (PUG) unifies universal guidance (using the predicted clean image x̂₀ as critic input rather than noisy θ_t) with projected diffusion models, applying a projection operator P_Θ to the predicted clean image and to the noise estimate to enforce hard physical constraints without requiring θ_t ∈ Θ at every diffusion step. A critic distillation mechanism transfers knowledge from the MAPPO agent critic to a dedicated environment critic via a distillation loss L_distill(ϑ, θ) = Σ(𝒱_ϑ(θ) − E_{s₀~P_θ}[V_ψ(s₀)])², providing a dense, low-variance, policy-tracking signal that mitigates policy shift. The soft co-design distribution Λ*_φ balances reward maximisation with entropy regularisation (annealed over training) to encourage exploration before exploitation, and the same trajectories update both agent and environment critic parameters, eliminating the decoupled optimisation bottleneck of prior methods.

## Results

On the D-RWARE Corner warehouse scenario, DiCoDe achieves 12.1±0.2 mean episode reward (boxes delivered), outperforming the RL baseline (8.7±0.4) by 39% while using 66% fewer simulation steps. Across the WFCRL wind farm scenarios (2–16 turbines), DiCoDe consistently leads, e.g. 490±0 vs. 485±5 (RL) on WFCRL-2 and 282±1 vs. 254±4 (RL) on WFCRL-16. Averaged over WFCRL and VMAS-ONav, DiCoDe achieves 10.3% higher reward than RL, 9.5% above Fixed environments, and 17.1% above domain randomisation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiCoDe outperforms the prior state-of-the-art co-design method RL (Gao & Prorok, 2023) across all six scenarios, with the largest gap on WFCRL-8 (370 vs. 323, +14.6%) and WFCRL-16 (282 vs. 254, +11.0%), while on Corner it delivers 39% more boxes with 66% fewer samples. Against domain randomisation (Tobin et al., 2017), DiCoDe achieves 17.1% higher average reward, with the most dramatic improvement on WFCRL-8 (370 vs. 314, +17.8%) and ONav (2.29 vs. 1.80, +27.2%). DiCoDe also beats the Fixed-environment baseline (no co-design) by 12.8% on average, confirming that jointly optimising the environment yields tangible gains over tuning agents alone. Notably, RL's performance degrades sharply beyond 4 turbines (from 485 to 254), whereas DiCoDe maintains consistent gains, demonstrating superior scalability to higher-dimensional design spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two recent diffusion-model techniques—universal guidance (Bansal et al., 2023) and projected diffusion models (Christopher et al., 2024)—into a single constrained sampling procedure (PUG) tailored for environment generation, and builds its pre-training pipeline on the regret-guided diffusion framework of ADD (Chung et al., 2024). It departs from prior co-design methods (Gao & Prorok, 2023; Schaff et al., 2019; Cheney et al., 2018) that relied on simple parametric distributions or evolutionary search, by replacing them with a learned generative model and introducing critic distillation to share knowledge between the MARL agent and the environment generator. The work is the first to apply diffusion models to the cooperative co-design setting (as opposed to the adversarial UED setting of ADD) and the first to explicitly address sample inefficiency from policy shift via distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiCoDe addresses two fundamental barriers—combinatorial design-space explosion and sample inefficiency from moving targets—that have restricted co-design to low-dimensional toy problems, and demonstrates practical gains (39% reward, 66% fewer rollouts) in warehouse and wind-farm settings with real engineering relevance. The scalability result (performance maintained as turbine count grows while RL baselines degrade) suggests the approach can handle design dimensions previously intractable. Open questions include the absence of theoretical convergence guarantees, the reliance on an uninformative uniform prior (rather than expert-designed environment distributions), and whether the soft co-design distribution can be enriched with multi-objective UED signals. The framework's generality across discrete grid-worlds and continuous physics simulations positions it as a potential template for other constrained generative design problems.
