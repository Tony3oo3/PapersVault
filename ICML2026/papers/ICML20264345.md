## Problem

Dynamical systems in scientific settings often exhibit symmetries (equivariance under group actions) that can drastically reduce the trajectory length needed for identification, but existing methods either assume the symmetry group is known a priori or rely on heuristic, model-specific discovery procedures without provable guarantees. This paper asks whether one can simultaneously identify the dynamics and discover the unknown symmetry group from a single trajectory, achieving the same sample efficiency as the known-symmetry case.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264345/cropped_page26_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single noiseless trajectory (x_0, …, x_T) generated by a feature-lifted linear system f(x) = WΦ(x), a known analytic feature map Φ, a finite candidate family of groups 𝒢, and a failure probability δ. It outputs the parameter matrix W and a compact generating set S of the full symmetry group. Crucially, the required trajectory length T is T_Φ(𝒢) = max_{G∈𝒢} T_Φ(G), identical to the worst-case known-symmetry threshold, and the algorithm uses only O(log |G|) sampled group elements per candidate rather than enumerating the full group.

## Method

The core mechanism decomposes the equivariant parameter matrix W into isotypic blocks C_π ⊗ I_{V_π} via Schur's lemma, reducing identification to checking full row rank of per-representation feature design matrices Φ_{π,T}. For adaptive discovery, the algorithm samples N = O(log |G_max| + log |𝒢|/δ) uniform elements from each candidate group (a weaker consequence of the Alon-Roichman expander theorem, proved via an elementary subgroup-growth argument), then tests linear feasibility of the combined trajectory and equivariance constraints for each candidate, selecting the largest feasible group. Under a 'generic candidate separation' condition, this recovers the true group and dynamics with probability ≥ 1−δ at the same trajectory length as the known-symmetry case. A complementary bounded-index variant samples from a known ambient group Γ and uses rejection sampling (acceptance probability ≥ 1/B) to discover the unknown subgroup without enumerating candidates.

## Results

The paper establishes that for S_d-equivariant quadratic polynomial systems (d ≥ 4), the generic trajectory length drops from Θ(d²) (no symmetry) to a constant T = 4, independent of dimension. More generally, for fixed-degree k polynomial features under S_d, T_{Φ≤k}(S_d) = O_k(1) while the ambient feature dimension is Θ_k(d^k). The adaptive symmetry discovery algorithm (Theorem 4.5) achieves this same optimal trajectory length T_Φ(𝒢) = max_{G∈𝒢} T_Φ(G) without knowing the group in advance, using only O(log|G_max| + log|𝒢| + log(1/δ)) random samples per candidate group. For bounded-index subgroups (Theorem 4.11), the expected sampling overhead is O(B(log|Γ| + log(1/δ))), independent of the number of candidate subgroups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, datasets, or algorithmic baselines to compare against. The central theoretical 'comparison' is between the generic (no-symmetry) identification setting and the equivariant setting: without symmetry, T_Φ({e}) = m (the full feature dimension), while with S_d equivariance the trajectory length is governed only by the largest active irreducible multiplicity max_π m_π, which is O_k(1) for fixed k. The paper positions itself against prior symmetry-discovery methods (e.g., Calvo-Barlés et al. 2025, Gabel et al. 2024, Li et al. 2025) by noting that those approaches are heuristic or model-specific and lack provable quantitative guarantees, whereas the present work provides exact sample-complexity characterizations and a provably optimal adaptive algorithm. No head-to-head numerical comparisons are performed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of classical dynamical system identification (Simchowitz et al., 2018; Sarkar & Rakhlin, 2019) and the emerging area of symmetry discovery in machine learning (Dehmamy et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2023; Gabel et al., 2024; Calvo-Barlés et al., 2025a). While prior work on symmetry discovery in dynamical systems is largely heuristic or model-specific, and system identification theory has not accounted for unknown symmetries, this paper is the first to provide provable sample-complexity guarantees for the joint problem of identifying dynamics and discovering the underlying finite symmetry group from a single trajectory. The technical machinery—Cayley graph generation, the Alon-Roichman theorem, and isotypic decomposition via Schur's lemma—is repurposed from additive combinatorics and representation theory into a new context, with the key observation that only the weakest form of random Cayley graph connectivity (not full expansion) is needed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The representation stability result—that fixed-degree permutation-equivariant polynomial systems are identifiable from O_k(1) trajectories regardless of state dimension d, while the feature space grows as Θ_k(d^k)—establishes a fundamental limit on how much symmetry can compress the identification problem. The zero-overhead guarantee (symmetry discovery adds no extra trajectory cost) is a strong theoretical statement with implications for physics-informed learning where symmetries are known to exist but not a priori. Key open questions include extending to infinite (Lie) groups, noisy observations, and linear time-invariant or partially observable systems, all of which the authors identify as requiring tools beyond the finite-group expander framework.
