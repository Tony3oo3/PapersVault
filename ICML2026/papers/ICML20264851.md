## Problem

When a Vision-Language Model fails on a multimodal task, it is ambiguous whether the error stems from flawed visual perception ('bad seeing') or flawed logical reasoning ('bad thinking'), and existing training paradigms provide no modality-aware supervision to distinguish the two. This credit-assignment ambiguity leads to a 'seesaw effect' where improving one capability degrades the other, and heavy agentic or architectural workarounds introduce significant compute and engineering overhead without proportional gains.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a free-form text question as input and produces an interleaved trajectory of perception-centric blocks (<recognition>...</recognition>) that explicitly ground visual evidence, and reasoning-centric blocks (think...think) that perform logical deduction, culminating in a final text answer. The perception blocks are constrained to a token budget (e.g., 800 tokens) to enforce minimalism, and the entire trajectory is generated autoregressively by a single VLM without external tool calls or multi-turn function loops.

## Method

The paper introduces MoCA (Modality-Aware Credit Assignment), a reinforcement learning framework built on GRPO that decouples perception and reasoning supervision. Perception Verification (PV) uses a 'blindfolded reasoner' proxy: the VLM's perception text is fed to a strong text-only LLM (Qwen2.5-Instruct-14B) without the image, and if the proxy can answer the question correctly, the perception is deemed a sufficient statistic, yielding a binary reward R_P. Structured Verbal Verification (SVV) replaces high-variance LLM judging with a universal step-by-step verbal algorithm for outcome scoring, reducing variance from 78.6% to 92.3% consistency. During failed trajectories, MoCA uses R_P as a gate: if perception was correct but the answer wrong ('bad thinking'), perception tokens receive a protection bonus that dampens the penalty; if perception also failed ('bad seeing'), perception tokens receive an amplified penalty. This targeted routing prevents the model from unlearning valid visual grounding when reasoning fails, resolving the seesaw effect.

## Results

MoCA (7B) achieves an average of 60.5 across nine benchmarks spanning perception-centric (V*, HRBench, InfoVQA), rich-modality (DUDE, SlideVQA, MMLongBench-Doc), and reasoning-centric (MMMU, EMMA, MathVista) tasks. Compared to its Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-7B base, MoCA gains +15.2 on V*, +19.4 on SlideVQA, +11.9 on MMLongBench-Doc, and +9.8 on EMMA, demonstrating simultaneous improvements across all three task categories rather than the typical seesaw trade-off. On rich-modality tasks, MoCA sets a new standard at 58.3 on SlideVQA and 45.1 on DUDE, surpassing all 7B baselines by wide margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-7B base, MoCA improves on all nine benchmarks, with the largest gains on SlideVQA (+19.4), MMLongBench-Doc (+11.9), and EMMA (+9.8), confirming that the modality-aware credit assignment resolves the seesaw effect. Against the strongest perception-focused 7B baseline DeepEyes, MoCA trades a small margin on V* (86.6 vs. 88.9) and InfoVQA (87.0 vs. 87.7) for large wins on rich-modality (SlideVQA 58.3 vs. 33.0, MMLong 33.1 vs. 17.5) and reasoning tasks (MMMU 54.8 vs. 45.2, EMMA 31.3 vs. 18.1). Against Pixel Reasoner, MoCA wins on every benchmark except matching closely on V* and InfoVQA, with particularly large advantages on SlideVQA (+16.2) and EMMA (+11.5). Compared to the proprietary GPT-4o, MoCA-7B surpasses it on V* (86.6 vs. 45.0), HRBench (74.2 vs. 65.0), InfoVQA (87.0 vs. 80.7), SlideVQA (58.3 vs. 53.1), MMMU (54.8 vs. 51.9), and MathVista (73.8 vs. 63.4), while GPT-4o retains an edge on DUDE (52.7 vs. 45.1) and MMLongBench-Doc (42.3 vs. 33.1). Against R1-VL, MoCA dominates across the board, with R1-VL showing severe degradation on MMMU (7.8) and EMMA (8.3) compared to MoCA's 54.8 and 31.3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for the RL optimization backbone but fundamentally rethinks the credit assignment problem in multimodal RL. It departs from two dominant paradigms: (1) implicit fusion architectures (QwenVL, LLaVA-OV) that treat perception and reasoning as an entangled latent process, and (2) agentic multi-turn function-calling frameworks (Pixel Reasoner, DeepEyes/Chain-of-Focus) that externalize visual operations at significant compute and engineering cost. The key conceptual departure is internalizing the 'thinking with images' loop via instruction-driven interleaved perception-reasoning blocks, then using a 'blindfolded text reasoner' as a functional proxy for perception verification — a technique with no direct precedent in the VLM RL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and widely-observed failure mode in VLM RL training (the 'seesaw effect') with a lightweight, single-model solution that avoids the engineering burden of agentic frameworks. The modality-aware credit assignment mechanism is a practical recipe that could be adopted by other groups training VLMs with RL. However, several open questions remain: the approach relies on a text-only proxy whose false-negative rate (9.19%) is non-trivial, the textual grounding of perception may fail for inherently spatial or geometric tasks, and all results are at 7B scale on a single base model, leaving scalability and cross-architecture generalizability untested. The finding that high-variance LLM judge rewards actively cause reward hacking during RL training is a useful practical warning for the broader RLHF community.
