## Problem

In the Fine-tuning-as-a-Service paradigm, attackers can upload datasets containing harmful samples that compromise the safety alignment of LLMs during fine-tuning. Existing fine-tuning-stage defenses (e.g., Lisa, SafeGrad, ConstrainedSFT) constrain model deviation or filter harmful samples but achieve unsatisfactory defensive performance. This paper asks whether the model's intrinsic attention mechanisms can be leveraged to proactively prevent learning of harmful knowledge during fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

During each fine-tuning step, the method consumes three data streams: a batch of user fine-tuning samples (which may contain a proportion p of harmful samples), a batch of simulated harmful anchor data (harmful prompt–harmful answer pairs), and a batch of simulated refusal anchor data (harmful prompt–safe answer pairs). The harmful and refusal anchor batches are used solely to compute per-head sink values for the regularizer gradient; they are not part of the cross-entropy loss. The output is a full-parameter fine-tuned model that retains task utility while resisting harmful behavior.

## Method

Surgery introduces a sink divergence suppression regularizer into the standard fine-tuning objective. For each attention head h, sink divergence d_h is defined as the difference between the head's sink value on harmful anchor data and its sink value on refusal anchor data. The paper observes that heads split into two groups by the sign of d_h, and that the number of positive-divergence heads grows as harmfulness increases during fine-tuning. The proposed regularizer is (1/|H|) Σ_h ReLU(d_h), which penalizes only heads with positive sink divergence, steering them toward the negative group. The full objective is min_w f(w) + λ·(1/|H|)Σ_h ReLU(d_h), optimized with a standard first-order update where the gradient of the ReLU term is computed via backpropagation through the attention weights on the anchor batches. This requires only two additional forward passes per step (on harmful and refusal anchors) and adds negligible overhead (~0.05 h, ~5 GB) relative to vanilla SFT.

## Results

Under the default setting (p=0.1, n=1000, GSM8K), Surgery achieves a harmful score of 8.90% and fine-tuning accuracy of 68.50% on Llama3-8B-Instruct, substantially outperforming all baselines. Averaged across harmful ratios (p=0 to 0.2), Surgery reduces the harmful score to 8.42% versus 13.88% (Lisa), 16.30% (SafeGrad), and 17.80% (ConstrainedSFT), while maintaining a fine-tuning accuracy of 68.70%. On unseen evaluation benchmarks, Surgery further reduces the harmful score by 11.25% (HarmBench) and 9.55% (SorryBench) compared to the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Surgery outperforms all seven fine-tuning-stage baselines on harmful score across every harmful ratio tested. Compared to the strongest baseline Lisa (HS=13.88 avg), Surgery achieves a 5.46% average reduction; against SPF (21.44), the gap is 13.02%; against SPARD (19.84), 11.42%; against ConstrainedSFT (17.80), 9.38%; against DSS (16.86), 8.44%; against SafeGrad (16.30), 7.88%; and against AsFT (15.96), 7.54%. In terms of fine-tuning accuracy, Surgery (68.70 avg) is competitive with Lisa (69.08) and ConstrainedSFT (69.14), while substantially outperforming DSS (60.02) and AsFT (61.10). Notably, Surgery's harmful score remains stable as the harmful ratio increases (8.80 at p=0.05 to 9.30 at p=0.2), whereas most baselines show clear degradation (e.g., ConstrainedSFT rises from 15.70 to 22.30). On cross-model evaluation (Table 3), Surgery also leads on Gemma2-9B (HS=8.20 vs. best baseline 9.50) and Qwen2.5-14B (HS=11.30 vs. 17.20), confirming generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the well-studied attention sink phenomenon (Xiao et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2025a) for a new purpose: characterizing and controlling how attention heads adapt during harmful fine-tuning. Unlike prior fine-tuning-stage defenses that either constrain parameter drift from the aligned model (Lisa, ConstrainedSFT, SafeGrad) or identify and filter harmful samples/tokens (SEAL, DSS, SPARD), Surgery directly regularizes the attention mechanism's intrinsic sink-divergence statistic, steering heads toward a safety-aligned regime without requiring sample-level identification. The separable sink divergence hypothesis—that harmful-pattern learning is causally linked to heads with positive sink divergence—represents a new mechanistic framing that departs from the gradient-projection and data-selection paradigms dominating the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic explanation for why harmful fine-tuning degrades safety (heads shift toward positive sink divergence) and converts that explanation into a lightweight regularizer with negligible overhead (0.05 h, 5.09 GB extra), making it practical for FaaS providers who cannot afford the compute cost of heavier defenses like Lisa or SafeGrad. Open questions include whether the separable sink divergence hypothesis generalizes to other safety properties (e.g., bias, hallucination), whether it holds under parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA results are deferred to appendix), and whether the approach remains effective as models scale beyond 20B parameters or adopt fundamentally different attention architectures. The reliance on a fixed anchor dataset (BeaverTails or RepNoise-Refusal) for computing sink divergence also raises questions about robustness when the threat distribution shifts substantially.
