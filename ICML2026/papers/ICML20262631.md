## Problem

Existing DPO-based alignment methods for MLLMs suffer from three structural limitations: perturbation-based visual negatives preserve high-level semantics so models learn superficial artifacts rather than resolving cross-modal conflicts, modality-asymmetric objectives enable shortcut learning through language priors, and static single-pass training with a fixed reference model causes optimization instability on difficult off-policy samples. The paper asks whether a unified framework combining fine-grained semantic preference data, a symmetric bidirectional objective, and a progressive curriculum with dynamic reference updates can systematically mitigate visual hallucinations.

## Input / Output

At inference, the aligned MLLM takes an RGB image (processed by a CLIP ViT-L/14-336px vision encoder) and a text prompt, producing a free-form text response. During training, each sample is a quadruple (I_w, I_l, y_w, y_l, Q) consisting of a chosen and rejected image, a chosen and rejected text response, and a shared question. The SCPP dataset (21k pairs total) is partitioned into Easy (5k), Medium (9k), and Hard (7k) subsets using a composite difficulty score that combines CLIP global-embedding cosine similarity, DINOv2 patch-feature Optimal Transport distance (via Sinkhorn with ε=0.05), and mean MLLM prediction entropy, each Z-score normalized before summation.

## Method

SCPO integrates three coordinated components. First, the SCPP dataset provides semantically challenging preference pairs where image-text pairs differ by minimal but critical attributes, sourced from RLHF-V, MVC, HumanEdit, and Winoground, and partitioned into an easy-to-hard curriculum. Second, the SCPO loss combines Cross-modal Complementary Optimization (CCO), which learns textual preference (correct vs. hallucinated response given a fixed image) and visual preference (correct vs. mismatched image given a fixed response) simultaneously, with Cross-modal Symmetry Optimization (CSO), which enforces that a matched image provides stronger support than language-only context while a mismatched image provides weaker support, applied symmetrically to both sides of the preference pair to prevent modality-specific shortcuts. Third, a dynamic reference model is reset to the current policy at each curriculum stage boundary, zeroing the KL divergence and decomposing the optimization into a sequence of tractable on-policy steps whose rewards accumulate multiplicatively (π_N ∝ π_0 exp(β⁻¹ Σ r_t)).

## Results

On LLaVA-v1.5-7B, SCPO reduces the Object HalBench CHAIRs score from 54.7 to 20.3 (a 62.9% relative reduction) and the AMBER-Generative HalRate from 40.4 to 19.5. On LLaVA-v1.6-7B, SCPO achieves CHAIRs of 7.0 and AMBER-Discriminative accuracy of 89.2%, outperforming GPT-4V (CHAIRs 13.6, Acc. 87.4%) by 48.5% in relative CHAIRs reduction. Compared to a matched DPO baseline trained on the same SCPP data (DPO-SCPP), SCPO further reduces CHAIRs by 40.3% and AMBER HalRate by 36.3%, confirming that the curriculum, symmetric objective, and dynamic reference model are all essential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCPO-7B (LLaVA-v1.6-7B) outperforms GPT-4V on hallucination metrics: CHAIRs 12.0 vs. 13.6 (48.5% relative reduction) and AMBER-Discriminative accuracy 89.2% vs. 87.4%, while GPT-4V retains an edge on AMBER-Generative coverage (67.1 vs. 60.2). Against the closest alignment baseline SymMPO, SCPO achieves comparable CHAIRs (20.3 vs. 20.4 on LLaVA-v1.5-7B) but substantially lower AMBER HalRate (19.5 vs. 25.4) and higher MMHal Score (2.47 vs. 2.63 is a loss; however on LLaVA-v1.6-7B SCPO reaches 3.16 vs. SymMPO's 2.85). SCPO decisively beats perturbation-based methods: mDPO (CHAIRs 35.7), HA-DPO (38.2), and HALVA (52.7) all fall well short of SCPO's 20.3 on LLaVA-v1.5-7B. The matched DPO(SCPP) baseline, which uses the same data and training pipeline but the standard DPO objective, scores CHAIRs 34.0 and AMBER HalRate 30.6, confirming that the symmetric CSO objective and curriculum strategy contribute the majority of the gain. On LLaVA-v1.5-13B, HSA-DPO achieves a lower AMBER HalRate (13.4 vs. SCPO's 20.8), but SCPO leads on CHAIRs (23.3 vs. not reported) and AMBER-Discriminative accuracy (90.4% vs. not reported), indicating a trade-off where HSA-DPO optimizes for brevity at the cost of coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal DPO line of work (RLHF-V, mDPO, HA-DPO, HALVA, SymMPO) by unifying three previously separate ideas into a single framework: a difficulty-ordered curriculum for preference data, a symmetric bidirectional objective that jointly optimizes textual and visual preferences, and a dynamic reference model that resets at each curriculum stage. While prior methods addressed either data construction (perturbation-based visual negatives) or objective design (symmetric constraints) in isolation, SCPO coordinates all three to target the shared bottleneck of stable cross-modal grounding under progressively finer semantic conflicts. The difficulty scoring mechanism, combining CLIP semantic proximity, DINOv2 structural discrepancy via Optimal Transport, and MLLM prediction entropy, is a novel contribution to the preference-pair construction literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCPO demonstrates that a 7B open-source model can match or exceed GPT-4V on hallucination-specific metrics while preserving general vision-language capabilities, which is practically significant for deployment in resource-constrained or safety-critical settings. The reversed-curriculum experiment (Hard→Medium→Easy causing degradation) provides useful empirical evidence that curriculum ordering matters for preference optimization, a finding with potential applicability beyond hallucination mitigation. Open questions include whether the fixed three-stage curriculum generalizes to adaptive online difficulty scheduling, whether the framework extends to video or multi-image inputs, and whether the dynamic reference model strategy transfers to other alignment paradigms such as RLHF with PPO.
