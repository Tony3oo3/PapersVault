## Problem

When a model's performance degrades on a new domain due to concept shift (a change in P(Y|X)), practitioners need to identify which specific features drive the shift to diagnose and correct the problem. Existing methods either require prespecified causal structure or fixed variable decompositions, or repurpose tools like SHAP as secondary objectives without directly targeting the conditional distribution shift. No prior method directly attributes concept shift to a sparse set of features without structural assumptions while providing formal statistical guarantees on false discoveries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled tabular samples from both source and target domains (which are unpaired) along with a pre-trained source-domain model h_S. It produces a sparse set of raw feature indices A ⊆ [p] whose values explain the conditional-response contrast Δ_g(x) = g(μ_T(x)) − g(μ_S(x)), a sparse coefficient vector δ serving as a direct correction to the source model on the link scale, and a feature importance ranking obtained by sweeping the ℓ1 penalty. Notably, the correction is learned exclusively from target-domain data, requiring as few as 100 target samples to identify over half the shifted features, and the source model is treated as a fixed offset rather than being re-estimated.

## Method

SGShift reframes concept-shift diagnosis as a sparse regression problem on a correction term: rather than fitting separate models for E_S[Y|X] and E_T[Y|X] and differencing them (a plugin approach that accumulates estimation error), it fixes the source model h_S(X) as an offset and learns a sparse generalized additive correction φ(X)^T δ from target-domain data alone via ℓ1-penalized negative log-likelihood. SGShift-A extends this with an absorption term ω (shared across domains, penalized more weakly than δ) to absorb source-model misspecification before attributing residual variation to concept shift. SGShift-K wraps the correction model in a Model-X knockoff framework, generating synthetic knockoff features that preserve the dependence structure of X_T while being conditionally independent of Y, then applies stability selection across repeated knockoff draws for PFER control and an e-value derandomized knockoff construction with the e-BH procedure for nominal FDR control. Theoretical guarantees include estimation consistency at rate a·log(K)/n_T under local cone-restricted strong convexity, and the key distinction from standard Lasso is that only the sparse correction δ needs to be well-specified—the full regression function E_T[Y|X] may be nonparametric.

## Results

On sparse semi-synthetic simulations across three healthcare datasets (Diabetes, COVID-19, SUPPORT2), SGShift-K achieves AUC of 0.86–0.99 for detecting true shifted features, outperforming the best baseline (SHAP) by 0.1–0.2 AUC. Under dense shift (>60% features shifted), SGShift-K maintains AUC of 0.82–0.95 while baselines drop to 0.52–0.86. In the sample-size experiment, SGShift-K identifies over 50% of shifting features with only 100 target samples and over 85% with 500 samples. On real data, SGShift recovers 90% of target-domain performance loss using fewer than 1/3 of all features, and in 87 ACS Income concept-shift cases, all are recoverable with <50% of features (many with <5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGShift-K consistently outperforms both WhyShift (Liu et al., 2023) and the SHAP-based method (adapted from Mougan et al., 2023) across all 12 dataset×shift×match configurations. In sparse settings, SGShift-K achieves 0.1–0.2 AUC higher than the best baseline (e.g., 0.99 vs. 0.81 on matched COVID-19; 0.96 vs. 0.83 on matched SUPPORT2). The advantage widens under dense shift, where WhyShift drops to 0.52–0.74 AUC and SGShift-K retains 0.82–0.95, a gap of up to 0.39 AUC (Diabetes dense, matched). The Diff baseline (separate models + sparse regression on prediction differences) is the weakest in most settings, confirming the paper's argument that the plugin approach accumulates estimation error. SGShift variants remain robust to model mismatch (generator ≠ base model), losing only ~0.02 AUC on average, whereas WhyShift and SHAP show larger degradation in several mismatched settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGShift builds on the Model-X knockoffs framework (Candes et al., 2018) and derandomized e-value knockoffs (Ren & Barber, 2024) for controlled variable selection, but repurposes them for a new objective: identifying features driving conditional distribution shift rather than general feature selection. It departs conceptually from the plugin approach used by WhyShift (Liu et al., 2023) and SHAP-based explanation-shift detection (Mougan et al., 2023), which fit separate source and target models and then analyze their difference; SGShift instead treats the source model as a fixed offset and learns a sparse correction directly on target data, avoiding error accumulation. It also differs from causal-structure-based methods (Zhang et al., 2023; Subbaswamy et al., 2021) by requiring no prespecified causal graph or decomposition of variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, assumption-light diagnostic tool for ML practitioners facing model degradation on new domains, with the key empirical finding that 90% of real-world concept-shift performance loss is recoverable by a sparse update using fewer than one-third of features. This validates the sparsity hypothesis and suggests that targeted feature-level interventions (rather than full retraining) may often suffice. Limitations include restriction to tabular data, requirement of labeled target samples, and the fact that SGShift identifies predictive differences rather than causal mechanisms. Open questions include extending to non-tabular modalities, disentangling concept shift from measurement drift, and leveraging identified shifted features for explicit model adaptation.
