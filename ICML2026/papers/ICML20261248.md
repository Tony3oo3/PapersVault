## Problem

While GNN and Transformer architectures for CFD surrogates have advanced significantly, their training paradigms remain bound to two legacy choices that ignore the numerical structure of PDEs: node-wise supervision that discards local flux/derivative consistency, and explicit Euler residual updates that are unstable for stiff dynamics and accumulate error over long rollouts. The paper argues the community has over-optimized architectures while neglecting the numerical priors that rigorous solvers (FEM, FVM) inherently enforce.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unstructured mesh graph (1k–20k nodes) where each node carries velocity/position components, a discrete node-type label (Inlet, Outflow, Wall, Obstacle, Normal), and optionally a first-order temporal history; edges carry geometric attributes for message-passing models. The model autoregressively predicts the next-step field (velocity for fluid datasets, position and stress for the DeformingPlate solid dataset), with boundary values enforced at inference via node-type masks. During training, Multi-Node Prediction additionally requires the model to predict next-step values for a sampled center node's entire 1-hop neighborhood from the center's latent representation alone, using freshly encoded neighbor features as context.

## Method

The paper introduces three architecture-agnostic training modifications validated across MeshGraphNet, Transolver, and a Transformer. (1) Multi-Node Prediction augments the standard node-wise loss with a star-attention objective: a one-layer cross-attention module takes the center node's final latent embedding plus freshly encoded neighbor features and must predict each neighbor's next-step field, enforcing local stencil consistency; Theorem B.2 proves this controls a discrete H¹ (gradient/flux) error. (2) Temporal Correction reinterprets each spatial processor block as a predictor-corrector step, inserting a cross-attention corrector (queries from the predicted state, keys/values from the current state) with a learned gate and mixing MLP, enabling the network to approximate implicit θ-methods and expanding the stability region (Theorem B.3). (3) 3D Rotary Positional Embeddings rotate Q and K channels by per-axis angles proportional to centered node coordinates, making attention scores depend on relative 3D offsets and capturing anisotropic transport without extra parameters. Together these yield 20–30% RMSE improvements on 1-step and all-rollout metrics for only ~10% additional compute, with gains that scale with model size and training time.

## Results

Across three architectures (MeshGraphNet, Transolver, Transformer) and three physics datasets (Cylinder, DeformingPlate, Coarse Aneurysm), the proposed framework (Multi-Node Prediction + Temporal Correction + 3D RoPE) achieves 20–30% reductions in both 1-step and all-rollout RMSE over the unmodified baselines, at only a ~10% increase in training and inference time. Improvements are consistent across 2D and 3D problems, fluid and solid mechanics, and mesh sizes ranging from ~1,000 to ~20,000 nodes. Scaling experiments confirm that gains persist as model size grows from 500k to 51M parameters and as training extends from 5 to 30 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its proposed training modifications against three unmodified architecture baselines: MeshGraphNet (Pfaff et al., 2021), Transolver (Wu et al., 2024), and a Transformer variant (Garnier et al., 2025b). In every case, the modified version outperforms the corresponding baseline on both 1-step RMSE and all-rollout RMSE across all three datasets, with the largest relative gains appearing in long-horizon rollouts where explicit Euler drift is most damaging. A critical control experiment shows that simply increasing the width of the baseline to match the total parameter count of the modified architecture yields only minor improvements, confirming that the gains come from the training methodology (stencil-level supervision, predictor-corrector temporal correction, and 3D RoPE) rather than from additional capacity. The improvements are architecture-agnostic: message-passing GNNs, attention-based GNNs, and point-cloud Transformers all benefit similarly, and the approach also generalizes to unseen geometries (multi-cylinder, different shapes) in supplementary rollouts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant MeshGraphNet/Transolver training paradigm (node-wise L2 supervision + explicit Euler residual updates) by importing three ideas from adjacent fields: multi-token prediction from NLP (Gloeckle et al., 2024; DeepSeek) becomes Multi-Node Prediction as a stencil-level regularizer; the classical predictor-corrector ODE integrator (Gragg & Stetter, 1964) is reinterpreted as a per-layer spatial-temporal decomposition with cross-attention; and 3D Rotary Positional Embeddings from NLP are adapted to unstructured meshes. The authors explicitly position their contribution as a training-methodology fix rather than an architectural one, arguing that the community has over-optimized network structures while neglecting the numerical priors (flux continuity, implicit time-stepping) that FEM solvers inherently encode. The theoretical grounding connects MNP to Sobolev H¹ seminorm control and the temporal corrector to θ-method A-stability, bridging geometric deep learning with rigorous numerical PDE analysis in a way prior mesh-simulation papers (Pfaff et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2024; Garnier et al., 2025b) did not attempt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20–30% rollout improvement for only ~10% additional training/inference cost, validated across three architectures (message-passing GNN, attention GNN, Transolver) and three physics domains (2D/3D fluids, structural mechanics), makes this a practical drop-in upgrade for anyone building mesh-based surrogates. The architecture-agnostic nature and the theoretical guarantees (discrete gradient error bound, stability region expansion) give the community principled justification for adopting these training changes rather than simply scaling width. Open questions include whether the gains persist on truly large-scale industrial meshes (the aneurysm at 20k nodes is the largest tested), whether the Cylinder benchmark saturation limits further improvement, and whether the geometry-shift generalization (single → multiple cylinders) extends to genuinely novel physics regimes. The observation that latent representations spontaneously encode pressure-like fields without explicit supervision hints at broader emergent physics learning that remains unexplored.
