## Problem

Engineering design workflows require generating 3D geometries under explicit, interpretable parameter constraints (e.g., ramp angle, drag coefficient) from only a handful of annotated exemplars, yet existing 3D generative models demand thousands to millions of training shapes, provide no guarantees of parameter control, and fail to extrapolate reliably beyond the training distribution. This is especially critical in domains like automotive and aerospace design, where downstream evaluation via CFD is prohibitively expensive and designers need rapid, controllable, and valid geometry exploration under scarce data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of N exemplar meshes (as few as 50) each annotated with a d-dimensional parameter vector, and at inference time accepts a target parameter vector with a constrained index set specifying which parameters to fix. It outputs a 3D mesh extracted via marching cubes from the zero-level set of the mixed SDF on a dense voxel grid, along with the mixed SDF weight vector. Notably, negative mixing coefficients are permitted, enabling extrapolation up to 100% beyond dataset bounds, and the parameter solve completes in under 10 ms while meshing takes only seconds.

## Method

LAMP overfits one MLP-based SDF decoder per exemplar, initializing all decoders from a shared mean-design starting point to produce an approximately aligned weight space. Given a target parameter vector, it solves a lightweight constrained least-squares problem (with a sum-to-1 constraint on mixing coefficients) to find affine combination weights that match the desired parameters, then combines the exemplar SDF weights and decodes the result into a mesh. The theoretical justification rests on two assumptions: parameters act linearly on geometric control points (A1), and the SDF decoder is locally linear in weights near the shared initialization (A2), together ensuring that affine weight mixing corresponds to faithful geometric interpolation and extrapolation. A linearity-mismatch safety metric quantifies whether the mixed decoder remains in the valid local linear regime by comparing its output against the affine combination of individual decoder outputs at sampled 3D points, accepting generations only when the mismatch falls below a threshold (ε=0.01).

## Results

On DrivAerNet++ interpolation with 100 samples, LAMP achieves CD=0.0117, IoU=97.24%, and parameter MAE=0.0014, surpassing AE-LPA trained on 10× more samples (1000). For large-range extrapolation (±100% beyond dataset bounds), LAMP sustains R²=0.902 (single-parameter) and R²=0.867 (4-parameter), while DNI collapses to R²=−5.77 and AE-LPA plateaus at R²=0.685. On industry-level vehicles with only 50 samples per class, LAMP achieves R²>0.93 across sports cars, SUVs, and convertibles. For performance-driven drag optimization, LAMP attains drag R²=0.792 with 2.7% decay MAE, versus DNI's R²=−8.92 and AE-LPA's R²=0.30.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAMP outperforms all three baselines across every evaluation setting. Against DNI (Wang et al., 2019), LAMP wins on interpolation (CD 0.0117 vs. 0.0118, IoU 97.24% vs. 97.12%) and dominates on extrapolation, where DNI's R² drops to 0.143 (single-param) and −5.77 (4-param) while LAMP maintains R²>0.86; on drag optimization, DNI produces R²=−8.92 versus LAMP's 0.792. Against AE-LPA (Jain et al., 2021), LAMP with 100 samples beats AE-LPA with 1000 samples on interpolation (IoU 97.24% vs. 92.63%) and on multi-parameter extrapolation (R²=0.867 vs. 0.685), while AE-LPA remains confined to the dataset convex hull. Against 3DS2VS (Zhang et al., 2023), compared only on industry-level vehicles (Table 4), LAMP achieves R²=0.93–0.99 versus 3DS2VS's negative R² (−2.12 to −3.57) and reduces MMD by over 10× on sports cars and convertibles.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAMP extends the weight-space interpolation lineage (DNI, model soups, GAN cocktail) from pairwise or unconditional blending to multi-way, parameter-constrained affine mixing of aligned SDF decoders. It shares the 'networks-as-data-points' perspective with HyperDiffusion but replaces learned generative sampling over weight space with a direct linear solve in parameter space, making control explicit and interpretable. The shared-initialization alignment step is the key methodological departure: it ensures all exemplar decoders occupy a common local basin where affine combinations remain geometrically meaningful, a condition prior weight-merging work did not need to enforce because it operated on already-trained, task-specific models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LAMP offers a practical, data-efficient alternative to heavy conditional generators for engineering design workflows where only dozens of annotated exemplars exist and downstream evaluation (CFD) is extremely expensive. The linearity-mismatch safety metric provides a lightweight, data-independent guardrail for extrapolation validity, addressing a gap that learned conditional models lack. Key limitations remain: the method assumes common topology across exemplars, relies on local linearity that degrades under large extrapolation, and cannot resolve conflicting parameter-performance constraints. Open questions include extending to multi-topology families, integrating physics-aware constraints, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the relatively structured engineering domains tested here.
