## Problem

Single-pass evidential deep learning (EDL) can remain overconfident under distribution shift and fails to represent multi-modal epistemic uncertainty near complex decision boundaries. Density-aware variants like DAEDL improve calibration but rely on a static, offline feature-space likelihood that is decoupled from end-to-end learning, making it brittle to representation drift and unable to adapt evidence where support is weak. The paper asks whether a learnable, in-model support signal can be integrated directly into the evidential mechanism while preserving single-pass inference and capturing multi-modal epistemic structure without ensembling.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 28×28 grayscale images (MNIST) or 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10) through a spectrally normalized CNN/ResNet-18 backbone. Beyond the standard class probability vector, it outputs per-head Dirichlet concentration parameters, a learned scalar energy, per-class integration gates, and mixture routing weights; the final predictive distribution is a mixture-of-expectations over K Dirichlet heads, element-wise gated in probability space, and renormalized. Uncertainty is decomposed into aleatoric (predictive entropy) and epistemic (mutual information between mixture components) scores, with total evidence α₀ and an energy-derived score available as auxiliary OOD detection signals.

## Method

GEM-CORE learns a lightweight MLP energy head E_ψ(z) whose sigmoid output ŝ(x) is concatenated with backbone features z and fed into a small integration gate network G_η that produces per-class gates s(x)∈[0.1,0.9]^C; these gates multiply the predictive distribution in probability space and renormalize, so higher energy (lower support) yields smaller gates and more conservative evidence. A GMM-based density scaler ρ(z) additionally multiplies Dirichlet concentrations as a hard safety guardrail. GEM-MIX extends this with K evidential heads sharing the backbone, a learned router h_ω([z,ŝ(x)]) producing mixture weights π(x), and a mixture predictive mean computed as a weighted average of per-head Dirichlet expectations. GEM-FI adds a Fisher-informed regularizer L_FI = E[Σ_k π_k(x)·FÎ_k(x)] that penalizes allocating weight to locally sensitive heads, plus FI-based modulation of mixture weights during training (upweighting lower-sensitivity heads via exp(λ_FI(1−F̄_k))), an energy-based loss with Virtual Outlier Synthesis negatives, and a contrastive entropy loss encouraging low ID / high OOD uncertainty. All components are trained jointly end-to-end with a single forward pass at inference, and spectral normalization throughout the backbone supports the Lipschitz smoothness assumptions underlying the theoretical analysis.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, GEM-FI achieves 93.75% test accuracy, reducing Brier×100 from 14.27 (DAEDL) to 6.81 and improving misclassification-detection AUPR from 99.08 to 99.94. For epistemic OOD detection, GEM-FI attains AUPR of 92.59 on CIFAR-10→SVHN and 90.20 on CIFAR-10→CIFAR-100, surpassing DAEDL (85.54 and 88.19) and Re-EDL (89.89 and 88.30). On the near-OOD CIFAR-10→CIFAR-100 shift, GEM-FI also leads in aleatoric AUPR (90.30 vs. 88.16 for DAEDL). All GEM variants maintain single-pass inference with no additional forward passes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEM-FI outperforms the strongest density-aware baseline DAEDL (Yoon & Kim, 2024) on all four OOD pairs, with the largest gains on CIFAR-10→SVHN (+5.77 pp aleatoric, +7.05 pp epistemic AUPR) and CIFAR-10→CIFAR-100 (+2.14 pp aleatoric, +2.01 pp epistemic). Against Re-EDL (Chen et al., 2025), GEM-FI leads by +3.43 pp epistemic AUPR on CIFAR-10→SVHN and +1.91 pp on CIFAR-10→CIFAR-100. On CIFAR-10 classification, GEM-FI improves test accuracy over DAEDL from 91.11% to 93.75% and halves the Brier score (14.27→6.81), while also surpassing CEDL+ (93.07%) and LTS (93.13%) in accuracy. GEM-FI also beats the FI-based I-EDL (Deng et al., 2023) by +6.27 pp epistemic AUPR on CIFAR-10→SVHN, showing that the energy-gated mixture design captures epistemic structure that a single-head FI regularizer cannot. On MNIST-based shifts, all recent evidential methods (including GEM variants) saturate near 99.9% AUPR, making differences negligible; the meaningful separation occurs on natural-image benchmarks. GEM-MIX alone underperforms GEM-FI on CIFAR-10→CIFAR-100 (84.46 vs. 90.20 epistemic AUPR), confirming that the FI regularizer is critical for stabilizing mixture routing in near-OOD settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEM-FI unifies three previously separate threads in single-pass evidential learning: (i) it replaces DAEDL's static, offline GDA density rescaling with a learnable, end-to-end energy-to-gate mechanism that directly modulates Dirichlet evidence in probability space; (ii) it extends the mixture-of-Dirichlet-heads idea from Ryu et al. (2024) with learned routing weights conditioned on the energy signal, enabling multi-modal epistemic representation without multi-pass ensembling; and (iii) it adapts the Fisher-information-based evidential training of Deng et al. (2023) into a per-head sensitivity proxy that stabilizes mixture allocations and prevents head collapse. The key conceptual departure from DAEDL is that the support signal becomes an internal, differentiable control variable rather than a frozen external surrogate, and the key departure from prior mixture models is the explicit FI-based routing regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-pass constraint makes GEM-FI relevant for latency- and energy-constrained deployments where MC-Dropout or deep ensembles are impractical, and the learned gating addresses a concrete failure mode of DAEDL (brittleness under representation drift) that practitioners encounter in practice. However, the empirical evidence is confined to MNIST/CIFAR-scale benchmarks, the learned energy is explicitly not a calibrated support estimator (no monotonicity guarantee), and the corruption-detection results (Table 9) show GEM underperforming DAEDL on gradual in-domain shifts, suggesting the method's gains are most pronounced for semantic OOD rather than low-level corruptions. Open questions include whether the energy-gate + mixture + FI synergy scales to ImageNet-level tasks, whether the FI modulation can be made label-free at inference, and whether the extremely sharp GEM-CORE predictions (Brier ×100 ≈ 1.27) generalize beyond CIFAR-10 without overfitting to near-deterministic outputs.
