## Problem

Current experiment-guided protein generators steer the reverse diffusion process in coordinate space, making results sensitive to initialization, constrained by fixed denoising step budgets, and unable to produce thermodynamically meaningful ensemble weights. Additionally, widely used confidence metrics like ipTM may be artificially inflated by tiny embedding perturbations without corresponding structural accuracy gains, a vulnerability unexplored in prior work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20263183/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein sequence (with optional MSA) and an experimental observation—NOE distance bounds [d̲_ij, d̄_ij], a real-space electron density map F_o over the asymmetric unit cell, or an ipTM target—and produces a batch of n optimized Pairformer trunk embeddings Z = {Z¹,…,Zⁿ} that induce a conformational ensemble X = {X¹,…,Xⁿ}. Unlike coordinate-space guidance, the optimized embeddings are a persistent artifact reusable across independent diffusion runs, and ensemble statistics can be reported as Boltzmann-weighted averages under a force field (AMBER99SB or ProteinEBM) rather than uniform weights.

## Method

The core contribution is a nested inference-time optimization that replaces coordinate-space guidance with representation-space updates to AF3's Pairformer embeddings. An outer loop resamples diffusion noise each iteration to promote exploration, while an inner loop alternates between (i) gradient ascent on the experimental log-likelihood plus a Gaussian prior log p(Z|a) centered at the deterministic AF3 embedding, and (ii) a standard reverse SDE step conditioned on the updated embeddings. The optimization target is an ELBO-type bound, E_{X~p(X|Z)} log p(y|X) + log p(Z|a), which avoids the intractable log-of-expectation in the exact MAP objective. A separate Boltzmann reweighting step tilts the AF3 structural prior with a differentiable force-field energy via self-normalized importance sampling, yielding thermodynamically consistent ensemble populations. The framework is agnostic to the underlying generative model and diffusion schedule, functioning as a meta-initialization layer that can precede conventional coordinate-space guidance for final refinement.

## Results

On 20 NMR proteins from NMRDB, IT-Optimization reduces the median NOE violation percentage to 7.0–49.6% (mean ~25%), outperforming unguided AF3 (9.4–65.5%), AlphaFlow (11.4–68.1%), and coordinate-space Guidance (3.3–51.4%) on 18/20 structures. On 13 X-ray crystallographic benchmarks, IT-Opt achieves Fo–Fc cosine similarity of 0.800–0.921, exceeding Guidance (0.755–0.899) and AF3 (0.388–0.822) on 12/13 structures, with R_work values as low as 0.158 (matching PDB) versus 0.158–0.233 for Guidance. Energy-weighted IT-Opt further reduces NOE violations (e.g., 2K0M: 4.4% vs. 10.5% uniform) and yields lower AMBER99SB energies with higher effective sample size under ProteinEBM. For ipTM optimization, perturbations of only ~0.01% in embedding space inflate confidence scores, yet in 6/7 complexes DockQ, Fnat, and hydrogen-bond recovery remain unchanged or worsen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against unguided AlphaFold3, IT-Opt consistently reduces NOE violation percentages (e.g., 2K53: 20.6% vs. 41.1%; 2KPB: 40.7% vs. 62.3%) and improves crystallographic cosine similarity (e.g., 6I42:B: 0.876 vs. 0.487; 3AZY:A: 0.848 vs. 0.717), with IT-Opt matching or exceeding the deposited PDB on several structures. Against coordinate-space Guidance (Maddipatla et al., 2025b), IT-Opt wins on 18/20 NMR proteins for violation percentage and on 12/13 crystallographic structures for cosine similarity, with the largest gaps on flexible peptides (6I42:B: 0.876 vs. 0.817) and well-separated altlocs (3AZY:A: 0.848 vs. 0.780); Guidance retains a narrow edge on 2MRW (22.6% vs. 24.5% violation) and 2HEQ (10.0% vs. 9.5%). Against AlphaFlow (Jing et al., 2024), IT-Opt dominates on nearly all NMR structures (e.g., 1DEC: 14.4% vs. 45.6%; 2B5B: 28.7% vs. 40.7%) and crystallographic benchmarks (e.g., 6I42:B: 0.876 vs. 0.664; 3AZY:A: 0.848 vs. 0.721). For ipTM optimization, the paper shows that in 6/7 complexes, embedding perturbations of ~0.01% inflate ipTM/pTM to high-confidence levels while DockQ, Fnat, and hydrogen-bond recovery remain unchanged or degrade, exposing a vulnerability not present in the structural benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the experiment-guided AlphaFold3 line of work (Maddipatla et al., 2025a,b) by shifting optimization from coordinate-space guidance during reverse diffusion to representation-space updates of Pairformer trunk embeddings, decoupling the conditioning signal from the fixed denoising schedule. The Boltzmann reweighting component draws on the self-normalized importance sampling framework of Skreta et al. (2025) and the broader Boltzmann-generator paradigm (Noe et al., 2019; Akhound-Sadegh et al., 2024), but requires no additional training—only post-hoc reweighting at inference. Unlike prior MSA-manipulation approaches (Wayment-Steele et al., 2024; Bryant & Noé, 2024) that backpropagate through the Evoformer for a single target, this work optimizes a batch of embeddings directly, enabling ensemble generation and avoiding expensive gradient paths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a general, model-agnostic metaguidance layer that improves ensemble quality across NMR and crystallography benchmarks while enabling thermodynamically consistent Boltzmann-weighted statistics, bridging ML-generated structures with physical energy landscapes. The ipTM vulnerability finding—that 0.01% embedding perturbations can inflate confidence to high levels without corresponding structural gains—raises important concerns for binder-design pipelines that rely on ipTM for optimization and ranking, potentially contributing to false discovery rates in therapeutic development. Open questions include whether the framework extends to cryo-EM single-particle data, whether the ipTM sensitivity can be mitigated through architectural changes, and whether the energy-reweighting approach generalizes beyond the force fields tested (AMBER99SB, ProteinEBM).
