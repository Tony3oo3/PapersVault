## Problem

Fine-tuning Segment Anything Models on specialized tasks (biomedical, fine-grained, video segmentation) causes the model to compress generalization-relevant information, leading to significant degradation of zero-shot capability on other tasks. Existing robust fine-tuning methods (L2-SP, Ft-last, KL-SP) mitigate this loss only by uniformly restricting updates, which simultaneously suppresses beneficial task-specific adaptation and often sacrifices target-task performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained large SAM (ViT-H) or SAM2 (Hiera-Large) as the model to adapt, along with a smaller SAM (ViT-B) or SAM2 (Hiera-Tiny) as a guiding model, and a task-specific labeled dataset (e.g., DISK-5k box prompts at 1024×1024, Lucchi biomedical slices at 768×1024, or VOST video segments of 16 consecutive frames with click prompts). It produces a fine-tuned large model that outputs segmentation masks; the guiding small model is discarded after training, so inference incurs zero additional overhead. The FRS module operates on the normalized QK product extracted from intermediate mask-decoder attention layers, projecting it into a k-dimensional task-relevant subspace (k=3 for SAM, k=10 for SAM2).

## Method

SHERPA is a two-stage framework that decouples task-relevant from generalization-relevant features during fine-tuning. The Fisher Ratio Separation (FRS) module constructs an orthogonal projection matrix by maximizing the Fisher ratio (between-class/within-class variance) of the guiding features, isolating a compact k-dimensional subspace of task-discriminative directions. The Guiding Feature Extraction (GFE) module extracts the normalized QK product from the mask decoder of a fine-tuned small SAM, which—due to its stronger information bottleneck—yields more representative high-Fisher-ratio task-relevant features than the large model itself. During large-model fine-tuning, only the task-relevant subspace is updated, guided by an L1 alignment loss toward the small model's task-relevant features, while the orthogonal complement (carrying generalization-relevant information) remains frozen. A theoretical generalization bound shows that when k ≪ d and the subspace is well-estimated (m ≥ 50 samples), SHERPA achieves no worse generalization risk than standard fine-tuning, with the capacity gain term (√k − √d) being negative.

## Results

On natural image segmentation (DISK-5k), SHERPA achieves 0.8855 instance-level F1 on the validation set and 0.7930 average generalization F1 across six held-out datasets, yielding 93.4% generalization retention versus 85.3% for standard fine-tuning. On the harder biomedical task (Lucchi), SHERPA reaches 0.923 F1 with 91.8% retention (vs. 78.9% for Std-ft), and on video segmentation (VOST) it attains 0.578 J&F with 95.1% retention (vs. 84.0%). Across all four fine-tuning datasets, SHERPA improves generalization retention by up to 11.1% and task-specific performance by up to 2.2% over standard fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHERPA outperforms all nine baselines on generalization retention across every fine-tuning dataset. Against the two most recent and strongest baselines, FisherTune (CVPR 2025) and InfoSAM (2025), SHERPA achieves 93.4% vs. 89.5% retention on DISK-5k and 97.7% vs. 94.5% on DUTS, while also matching or exceeding their validation F1 (0.8855 vs. 0.8732/0.8834 on DISK-5k). Traditional regularization baselines (L²-SP, EWC, SI) preserve more generalization than Std-ft but sacrifice task-specific performance by 6–10% on DISK-5k; SHERPA avoids this trade-off, achieving the highest validation F1 among all methods while retaining the most generalization. Ft-last (Wortsman et al.) is competitive on DUTS (95.8% retention) but underperforms SHERPA on the harder DISK-5k task (91.5% vs. 93.4%). PEFT methods (VPT, AdaptFormer, gradient-based selection) achieve higher validation F1 than SHERPA on DISK-5k (up to 0.9031) but suffer substantially worse generalization (67–71% retention), confirming that SHERPA's subspace-guided approach is complementary to parameter-efficient tuning rather than directly comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHERPA sits at the intersection of robust fine-tuning (KL-SP, L²-SP, Ft-last, WiSE-ft), parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, AdaptFormer), and knowledge distillation, but departs from all three in a key way: rather than regularizing the full parameter space, restricting which layers update, or matching full feature/prediction outputs, it uses a Fisher-ratio criterion to isolate a low-dimensional task-relevant subspace and guides the large model only within that subspace. The theoretical grounding draws on information bottleneck theory (Tishby & Zaslavsky) to argue that smaller models, under capacity pressure, concentrate their representations on the most task-discriminative components, making them better 'guides' than the large model itself. The paper explicitly contrasts its mechanism with standard KD (which matches global features or logits) and with FisherTune/InfoSAM (which protect parameters via Fisher information or information-preserving regularization), positioning SHERPA as a feature-space alignment method rather than a parameter-space constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying SAM or SAM2 in specialized domains (biomedical imaging, video segmentation), the 11.1% improvement in generalization retention while simultaneously improving task-specific performance addresses a real deployment concern: a single fine-tuned model that handles both the target task and general scenes without catastrophic forgetting. The 18% training overhead with zero inference cost makes this practical for interactive annotation tools. Open questions include whether the approach extends to settings where no smaller variant of the model exists, how it behaves when the small model is severely underfit, and whether the Fisher-ratio subspace remains stable under distribution shift. The theoretical bound, while informative, is loose and relies on conditions (k ≪ d, sufficient m) that are only approximately met in practice.
