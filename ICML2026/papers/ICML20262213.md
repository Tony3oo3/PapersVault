## Problem

Multimodal LLMs remain brittle on mechanical engineering drawings, where high annotation density, weak domain priors, and unreliable spatial reasoning under strict orthographic projection rules cause decisive cues to be missed and structurally inconsistent interpretations to arise. No dedicated benchmark systematically covers the full spectrum of mechanical drawing understanding—spanning recognition, multi-view reasoning, and standards-aware judgment—on real part and assembly drawings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262213/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a mechanical drawing image (resized to a 1,024-pixel longer edge with a 262,144-pixel cap) paired with a text question grounded in the drawing content, and produces a structured response containing a reasoning rationale enclosed in <think>... tags followed by a concise final answer in <answer>...</answer> tags. The MechVQA benchmark comprises 3,281 drawings and 20,778 QA pairs organized into 10 fine-grained subtasks across three capability axes (Recognition, Reasoning, Judging) and three difficulty levels, with strict drawing-level train/validation/test separation to prevent leakage.

## Method

MechVL is initialized from Qwen3-VL-Instruct-4B and trained in two stages: full-parameter supervised fine-tuning on the MechVQA training split to establish a schema-compliant reference policy, followed by DAPO-based reinforcement learning with a taxonomy-aligned composite reward combining semantic-tolerant accuracy (via LLM-as-a-Judge), binary format compliance, and LLM-judged explanation quality scored on Logic, Professionalism, and Conciseness. The RL stage uses a two-stage self-play design—first optimizing on the full training set, then upweighting underperforming subtasks during sampling—to reduce capability imbalance. The dataset itself is constructed through a semi-automated pipeline with three question-generation sources (free-form MLLM generation, template-based without ground truth, and template-based with verified metadata or expert edits), each subjected to iterative cross-model validation, multi-model answer voting, and stratified expert audit to ensure answerability and verifiability.

## Results

On the MechVQA benchmark (20,778 QA pairs across 10 subtasks), MechVL-4B-RL achieves a total accuracy of 84.85%, outperforming the strongest open-source baseline GLM-4.6V (78.91%) by +5.94 points and the strongest closed-source baseline Gemini-3-Pro-Preview (77.28%) by +7.57 points. The RL stage contributes a +8.49-point gain over the SFT-only variant (76.36%), with the largest improvements on harder questions (hard accuracy rises from 53% to 75%) and on Reasoning subtasks (e.g., Assembly Relationship from 40% to 84%, Projection & Multi-view from 44% to 64%). MechVL-4B-RL also attains the best per-subtask scores on Dimension & Annotation (90.70%), Item Localization (82.01%), Structure Understanding (83.33%), and Anomaly Detection (86.94%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MechVL-4B-RL (84.85%) outperforms all 14 baselines on the MechVQA total score. Among open-source models, it beats the strongest, GLM-4.6V (78.91%), by +5.94 points, with particularly large margins on Item Localization (+18.70), Assembly Relationship (+24.00), and Projection & Multi-view (+2.00); it also surpasses Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct (76.50%) by +8.35 and Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct (60.23%) by +24.62. Among closed-source models, it exceeds Gemini-3-Pro-Preview (77.28%) by +7.57, GPT-5 (75.44%) by +9.41, Qwen3-VL-Plus (76.33%) by +8.52, and Claude-Sonnet-4.5 (71.20%) by +13.65; the gap is most pronounced on Reasoning subtasks (e.g., AR: 84% vs. 46% for Gemini-3-Pro-Preview) while Gemini retains a slight edge on Consistency Judgment (82.67% vs. 78.67%). In the RL algorithm ablation, DAPO (81.95% total after full-data RL) outperforms GRPO (80.47%) and GSPO (78.77%), with the largest advantage on Reasoning (70.75 vs. 64.49 and 61.29 respectively). Overall, domain-specialized post-training yields the largest gains on constraint-sensitive and projection-consistent reasoning tasks where general-purpose MLLMs are most brittle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: domain-specific VQA benchmarks (CReFTCAD, PHT-CAD, DesignQA, BlueprintSymVL) and multi-stage post-training with group-based RL (GRPO, GSPO, DAPO). It departs from prior engineering-drawing benchmarks by unifying recognition, reasoning, and judgment under a single taxonomy with 10 subtasks and three difficulty levels, rather than probing one slice (e.g., symbol recognition or three-view reasoning). On the method side, it adopts DAPO's decoupled clipping and dynamic sampling as the RL backbone and introduces a taxonomy-aligned composite reward (accuracy + format + LLM-judged quality) tailored to dense technical drawings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MechVQA provides the first unified, open benchmark for comprehensive mechanical drawing understanding, filling a gap where prior efforts were scoped to narrow sub-problems or proprietary data. The 7.57-point gap over the strongest closed-source model and the large SFT→RL gain (+8.49) demonstrate that domain post-training is essential for dense, constraint-heavy technical documents, a finding likely to generalize to other standards-governed visual domains. Open questions include whether the benchmark captures proprietary/legacy drafting conventions, whether the LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation introduces systematic bias, and whether the gains transfer to full 3D CAD reconstruction or generative design tasks.
