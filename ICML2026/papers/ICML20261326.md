## Problem

Existing time series foundation models rely on fixed-size linear decoders that are inherently specialized to a particular target length, struggle to generalize across varying context and target lengths, and lack any mechanism to adapt to the sampling rate of the input data at inference time. This makes them unable to produce forecasts at arbitrary temporal resolutions or to transfer knowledge learned at one sampling frequency to another without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261326/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FlowState takes a univariate time series context (up to 4096 steps) and a scale factor s_Δ derived from the dataset's seasonality relative to a base of 24 steps. The SSM encoder processes the raw signal without patching or quantization, and the FBD outputs a continuous forecast function (a linear combination of Legendre polynomials) that is then sampled at the desired output interval Δ_F. During pretraining, each timestep produces 9 quantile forecasts (levels 0.1–0.9) via a quantile loss, and the model is trained with causal RevIN (running mean/std) rather than standard full-context normalization to preserve strict causality for parallel forecasting.

## Method

FlowState pairs an S5-based SSM encoder (with an output gate and HiPPO initialization) with a novel Functional Basis Decoder (FBD) that interprets the final SSM hidden state as coefficients of a continuous Legendre polynomial basis, yielding a rate-agnostic forecast function sampleable at any resolution. Sampling-rate equivariance is achieved by scaling the ZOH discretization parameters Δ_E and Δ_F with a single factor s_Δ, so that changing the input sampling rate proportionally rescales the continuous-time dynamics while leaving the underlying latent representation approximately invariant (bounded by first-order ZOH error). A parallel-forecasting training scheme produces (L − L_min) forecasts simultaneously from each context-target pair by leveraging the SSM's ability to process inputs in parallel, exposing the model to all intermediate context lengths and improving robustness. The FBD is regularized with time noise (Gaussian perturbation of sampling locations) to encourage smooth continuous forecasts, and Multi-Patch Inference with Contiguous Patch Masking extends the effective forecasting horizon beyond the FBD's native range.

## Results

On the GIFT-Eval benchmark (97 tasks, zero-shot), FlowState-18.6M achieves the best normalized MASE of 0.701 and CRPS of 0.487, outperforming all baselines. The smaller FlowState-10.6M (10.6M params) also surpasses every non-FlowState baseline in MASE (0.704 vs. 0.705 for TimesFM-2.5), while the compact FlowState-3M (3M params) reaches MASE 0.712. On standard long-term forecasting benchmarks (ETT, Traffic, Weather, Exchange, Electricity), FlowState-10.6M achieves the best MAE on 7 out of 8 datasets, confirming that its zero-shot GIFT-Eval performance transfers to conventional evaluation settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowState-18.6M outperforms all ten baselines on both MASE and CRPS on GIFT-Eval. Against the strongest prior baseline TimesFM-2.5 (200M params), FlowState-18.6M improves MASE from 0.705 to 0.701 and CRPS from 0.490 to 0.487, while using roughly 10× fewer parameters. TiRex (35M) is the closest competitor, with a slightly better CRPS (0.488 vs. 0.487) but a noticeably worse MASE (0.716 vs. 0.701); FlowState-10.6M also beats TiRex on MASE (0.704 vs. 0.716). Chronos-2-Synth (120M) trails by 0.019 MASE and 0.009 CRPS. The remaining baselines—Kairos-50M, Kairos-23M, Sundial-Base, and Toto-Open-Base-1.0—are all clearly behind, with MASE gaps of 0.041–0.049. In the robustness-to-unseen-sampling-rates experiment on ETT1m, FlowState-10.6M consistently outperforms TiRex, TimesFM-2.5, and Chronos-2-Synth across most resampled frequencies (15–135 min), with the largest margins at uncommon intervals where baselines degrade sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowState builds on the SSM lineage (S5, HiPPO initialization) for its encoder but departs from prior TSFMs (TimesFM, Chronos, TiRex) in two fundamental ways: it replaces the fixed-size linear decoder with a Functional Basis Decoder that produces a continuous forecast sampleable at any rate, and it introduces explicit sampling-rate scaling factors (s_ΔE, s_ΔF) that make the architecture equivariant rather than invariant to temporal resolution. The parallel-forecasting training scheme leverages the SSM's inherent parallelizability to expose the model to many context lengths simultaneously, while causal RevIN is a targeted modification of standard RevIN to prevent information leakage in this multi-horizon setup. The FBD itself repurposes the HiPPO coefficient-to-function mapping—previously used only for SSM initialization—into a decoding mechanism, a conceptual inversion that is the paper's central architectural insight.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is the extreme parameter efficiency: a 3M-parameter model achieves near-parity with 200M-parameter TimesFM on GIFT-Eval, suggesting that sampling-rate equivariance reduces the need to separately learn patterns at each temporal resolution. The demonstrated ability to forecast at unseen sampling rates without retraining (e.g., ETT1h context → ETT1m target) opens a capability no prior TSFM possesses, which could simplify deployment across heterogeneous sensor networks. Key limitations remain: the scale factor is selected via a domain-based heuristic rather than learned automatically, and the model processes channels independently without multivariate interaction. Open questions include whether automatic seasonality detection can close the gap to the heuristic, and whether the FBD can be extended to handle covariates or cross-channel dependencies.
