## Problem

Existing LLM-based KBQA systems face a dichotomy of failure: end-to-end generators hallucinate schema elements without verifying relation existence, while step-by-step prompting or MCTS-based agentic methods produce template-driven action announcements rather than genuine analysis of KB feedback, and incur heavy test-time search overhead. No prior approach trains a single policy to adaptively navigate the knowledge base through verifiable, execution-grounded interaction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264282/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system receives a natural language question, a Freebase knowledge base accessed via a Virtuoso SPARQL endpoint, and pre-linked topic entities. Rather than emitting a full S-Expression in one pass, the agent iteratively selects from six typed atomic actions (Find_relation, Merge, Order, Compare, Time_constraint, Count), each executed against the KB to produce structured entity-set observations or diagnostic messages. The final output is an answer entity set, with the S-Expression logical form assembled incrementally from the action sequence over the multi-turn trajectory.

## Method

KBQA-R1 reframes KBQA as a multi-turn sequential decision problem over a compact typed action space, where each action is grounded into an S-Expression fragment and executed by a KB executor to yield verifiable feedback. A Relation Retrieval and Confidence Gating (RRCG) module validates LLM-proposed relations against the KB schema via dense retrieval, applying three-tier confidence thresholds (auto-validate, tentative with top-k candidates, reject with diagnostic) to suppress hallucinated relations. Training proceeds in two stages: first, Referenced Rejection Sampling (RRS) synthesizes high-quality warm-start trajectories by conditioning generation on ground-truth action sequences and stripping reference hints before SFT, achieving ~67% acceptance versus ~40% for standard rejection sampling; second, GRPO-based reinforcement learning optimizes the policy with a composite reward combining F1 outcome accuracy and a format reward gated on correctness, using group-relative advantage estimation over n=5 rollouts per prompt. The result is a Llama-3.1-8B policy that requires only ~3 LLM calls per question versus 11–78 for prompting or MCTS baselines while achieving state-of-the-art F1 on WebQSP, GrailQA, and GraphQuestions.

## Results

On GrailQA, KBQA-R1 (Llama-3.1-8B) achieves 86.1% F1 and 83.9% EM overall, improving +7.6% F1 and +12.0% EM over the strongest agentic baseline KBQA-o1, with the largest gains in the zero-shot setting (+9.1% F1, +15.5% EM). On WebQSP, KBQA-R1 reaches 83.4% F1, surpassing KBQA-o1 by +25.6% and MCTS-KBQA by +7.4%. On GraphQuestions, KBQA-R1 attains 53.8% F1, a +5.1% gain over KBQA-o1. In terms of efficiency, KBQA-R1 requires only ~2.7–3.2 LLM calls per question versus 11–16 for ToG (GPT-4) and 29–78 for KBQA-o1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KBQA-R1 outperforms all 19 baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the strongest agentic baseline KBQA-o1 (MCTS-based, same Llama-3.1-8B backbone), KBQA-R1 gains +7.6% F1 on GrailQA, +25.6% F1 on WebQSP, and +5.1% F1 on GraphQuestions, while using 80–96% fewer LLM calls (2.65–3.16 vs. 28.8–78.0). On WebQSP, KBQA-R1 (83.4% F1) surpasses graph-retrieval methods RoG (70.8%), GNN-RAG (71.3%), and SubgraphRAG (70.6%), as well as fine-tuned systems TIARA (78.9%) and DecAF (76.7%). Against GPT-4 prompting methods ToG and PoG, KBQA-R1 achieves higher Hits@1 on GrailQA (86.7% vs. 88.6% for PoG-GPT-4, but with only 3.08 vs. 6.5 LLM calls) and WebQSP (88.2% vs. 87.3%), while using a 7B open-source model instead of a proprietary API. The Search-R1 adapted baseline (standard RS, no RRCG) scores only 54.1–57.0% F1, confirming that both RRS warm-start and RRCG gating are critical for competitive performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

KBQA-R1 builds directly on the compact, typed action space introduced by KBQA-o1 (Luo et al., 2025c), which decomposed logical-form construction into atomic operations (Find_relation, Merge, etc.) but relied on MCTS search with separate policy and reward models at inference time. The key departure is replacing test-time heuristic search with GRPO-based reinforcement learning (Shao et al., 2024), so the policy internalizes navigation strategies during training rather than exploring at inference. The paper also introduces two novel components absent from prior agentic KBQA work: Referenced Rejection Sampling (RRS) for cold-start data synthesis that aligns reasoning traces with ground-truth action sequences, and Relation Retrieval and Confidence Gating (RRCG) as a dense-retrieval-based validation layer that intercepts hallucinated relations before execution. Conceptually, the paper shifts the framing from 'generate a query' (end-to-end) or 'search over queries' (MCTS) to 'learn a closed-loop interaction policy,' positioning KBQA as a sequential decision problem with verifiable environment feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a Llama-3.1-8B model trained with RL outperforms GPT-4-based prompting methods (ToG, PoG) on Hits@1 while requiring over 70% fewer LLM calls, making agentic KBQA substantially cheaper at deployment. The finding that even frontier prompted models (Claude 4.6 Sonnet, gpt-5.3-codex) with the same action-space harness remain below the learned 8B policy suggests RL training internalizes graph navigation in a way that scale and prompting alone cannot replicate. Open questions include whether the approach transfers cleanly to KBs with different schema conventions (the Wikidata transfer shows a ~11-point drop), whether RRS generalizes to other structured-output RL tasks beyond KBQA, and whether the RRCG gating thresholds require per-domain tuning. The method's dependence on a Freebase-specific executor and the 6-turn limit also constrain applicability to very long multi-hop queries.
