## Problem

Autoregressive long-video generation, where clips are synthesized sequentially conditioned on limited history, suffers from three compounding failures over long horizons: semantic drift (identity and scene layout gradually change), motion decay (dynamics weaken into near-static imagery), and appearance instability (color/illumination flicker accumulates). Existing approaches address these issues in isolation or require external control signals (camera poses, agent actions), leaving open-ended text-driven generation without a unified, backbone-agnostic solution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a global text prompt (and optional local motion prompts), the last n frames from the previously generated clip, and a compact set of top-k frames retrieved from an external CPU/disk memory bank, all concatenated along the channel dimension as conditioning for the DiT denoiser. It produces a long video sequence generated clip-by-clip at 854×480 or 1280×720 resolution, with each clip conditioned on the hybrid context of short-term continuity frames and prompt-guided long-term memory. The global reservoir is sparsified via a SigLIP cosine-similarity and quality gate, so only semantically novel frames are stored, keeping retrieval cost negligible relative to denoising.

## Method

DYNAMEM integrates three coordinated modules on top of a HunyuanVideo-1.5 DiT backbone without architectural modification. (1) Semantic-Adaptive Hierarchical Memory (SAHM) maintains a sparse global reservoir of representative frames (gated by SigLIP similarity, aesthetic score, and blur check) and retrieves a prompt-conditioned working memory of top-k frames, which is channel-concatenated with the last n frames of the previous clip to inject both local continuity and long-range identity cues. (2) Motion-Prioritized Optimization (MPO) combines gradient-based data selection (using influence functions to pick training clips whose gradients align with high-motion anchors) with a motion-weighted diffusion loss that re-weights the MSE objective by an optical-flow saliency map, counteracting the static-background bias that causes motion decay. (3) Reference-Anchored Perceptual Alignment (RAPA) performs closed-form affine moment matching in CIELAB space, aligning each generated frame's channel-wise mean and standard deviation to the average statistics of the first three stored reservoir frames, followed by residual blending in RGB to avoid over-correction. The full model is post-trained end-to-end on the curated LM-100K dataset (100k long-take videos segmented into 5-second clips with GPT-4o captions) using AdamW with stepwise LR decay on 32 H100 GPUs.

## Results

On LV-Bench, DYNAMEM achieves the lowest Video Drift Error (VDE) across all five dimensions: Subject (0.0753), Background (0.2732), Motion (0.0096), Aesthetic (0.9524), and Clarity (0.7319), indicating the strongest resistance to long-horizon degradation. On VBench, DYNAMEM attains the highest scores for Subject Consistency (0.9677), Background Consistency (0.9632), Aesthetic Quality (0.6881), and Image Quality (0.7082), while tying BlockVid on Motion Smoothness (0.9956). In human evaluation (37 evaluators, 20 prompts), DYNAMEM is preferred over BlockVid (63.9% win), SkyReels-V2-DF (78.4% win), and LongLive (55.1% win), with the largest margins on identity/scene consistency and motion smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DYNAMEM outperforms all seven baselines on every VDE metric and every VBench metric (tied with BlockVid on Motion Smoothness at 0.9956). The closest competitors are BlockVid and LongLive; DYNAMEM reduces VDE Subject from 0.0844/0.0827 to 0.0753 and VDE Motion from 0.0119/0.0105 to 0.0096, while lifting VBench Subject Consistency from 0.9597/0.9417 to 0.9677 and Aesthetic Quality from 0.6047/0.6053 to 0.6881. The largest margins are against FramePack (VDE Subject 4.3984 vs. 0.0753, a ~58× gap) and PAVDM (VDE Subject 1.8292 vs. 0.0753), both of which suffer severe identity and scene drift over long horizons. Self Forcing and MAGI-1 show moderate degradation (VDE Subject 0.3716 and 0.3090 respectively) but still trail DYNAMEM by 4–5× on subject drift. In the human study, DYNAMEM wins 78.4% of pairwise comparisons against SkyReels-V2-DF and 63.9% against BlockVid, with attribute-specific wins of 76.2% and 69.5% on identity/scene consistency respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

DYNAMEM builds on the HunyuanVideo 1.5 DiT backbone and the CIELAB moment-alignment technique from Reinhard et al. (2002), but its core contribution is a coordinated three-part framework for autoregressive long-video generation. It departs from prior memory-augmented video generation (e.g., WorldMem, Context-as-Memory) that rely on external control signals like camera poses or agent actions, instead using prompt-guided semantic retrieval over a sparsely curated global reservoir that requires no auxiliary inputs. The motion-prioritized training objective (gradient-based data selection plus optical-flow-weighted loss) extends standard diffusion fine-tuning by explicitly counteracting the static-region bias that causes motion decay, a problem identified but not systematically addressed in prior autoregressive video pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Long-horizon video generation is a practical bottleneck for applications like film production, interactive storytelling, and world-model simulation, where error accumulation over minutes of content is the dominant failure mode. DYNAMEM's modular design (memory, training objective, post-hoc alignment) is attractive because each component can be adopted independently, and the framework requires no architectural changes to the diffusion backbone. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other backbones or resolutions, how it scales beyond the 1-minute evaluation horizon, and whether the curated-memory strategy remains effective when prompts change rapidly or adversarially. The reliance on an internal 100K-clip dataset (LM-100K) limits reproducibility of the training recipe.
