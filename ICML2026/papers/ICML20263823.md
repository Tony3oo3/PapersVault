## Problem

Continuous diffusion models benefit from a principled gradient-flow framework (the JKO scheme in W₂ geometry) that enables fast, trajectory-free training, but this framework breaks down on discrete state spaces: any non-constant curve of probability distributions has infinite W₂ metric derivative, so the discrete heat equation cannot be interpreted as a W₂ gradient flow. This paper bridges that gap by introducing the first practical machine learning method that learns free-energy functionals from their gradient-flow trajectories on discrete spaces, using a graph-dependent metric W_K that makes the discrete heat equation a genuine gradient flow.

## Input / Output

The method consumes temporal snapshots of empirical samples from an evolving population on a finite graph (with a known, reversible Markov kernel K) and a fixed time-step τ, and produces a neural-network-parameterized potential gradient ∇V_θ (a vector per node giving differences to all neighbors) and a scalar noise parameter β_θ. A critical preprocessing step computes W_K-geodesic velocity vectors between consecutive density snapshots via a quadratic KKT solve, which are then used as regression targets in the training loss. The output is used for inference by constructing a rate matrix from ∇V_θ and β_θ and propagating densities via matrix exponentials.

## Method

The core insight is to replace the inapplicable W₂ metric with W_K, a discrete Benamou-Brenier metric built from the logarithmic-mean mobility m_ρ(x_i,x_j), which endows the interior of the probability simplex with a Riemannian manifold structure under which the discrete heat equation is the gradient flow of the KL divergence. The authors prove existence and uniqueness of the discrete JKO minimizer for free-energy functionals F = V + βH, then derive the W_K-gradient of the proximal functional as ∇V + β∇log ρ − v_geo/τ, where v_geo is the initial velocity of the W_K-geodesic from the candidate to the previous snapshot. Training reduces to a simple quadratic loss that enforces this gradient to vanish at observed densities, parameterized by a two-hidden-layer MLP (64 neurons/layer) predicting ∇V and β. The W_K-geodesic vectors are computed in O(N³) via a double-Cholesky factorization of the KKT system, making the entire pipeline lightweight: no trajectory-level data, no trajectory simulation during training, and convergence in under a minute on moderate graphs.

## Results

On synthetic graph classes (N≤6, 10,000 samples), the method achieves a time-averaged Hellinger distance of 0.059–0.069 across noise levels β∈{0.01, 0.10, 0.20}, consistently outperforming the OpenFIM baseline (0.142–0.159) by roughly 2×. Against the Direct-FP baseline at N=30 with 5,000 samples, the method achieves Hellinger 0.152 vs. 0.182, and dramatically better parameter recovery (relative L² error on ∇V: 0.103 vs. 1.873; on β: 0.376 vs. 1.008). On the Mouse Gastrulation Atlas single-cell dataset (34 cell types, 8 timepoints), the method reaches a Hellinger distance of 0.06 compared to 0.36 for Direct-FP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines. Against OpenFIM (Berghaus et al., 2025), a zero-shot foundation model for Markov jump processes pretrained on small graphs, the proposed method wins on Hellinger distance across all graph classes and all three noise levels (e.g., 0.059 vs. 0.150 at β=0.10), while using a far simpler architecture (~10k parameters vs. OpenFIM's large pretrained model) and training in under one minute versus hours of pretraining. Against the Direct-FP baseline (which directly optimizes the forward Kolmogorov equation in density space without exploiting W_K geometry), the method achieves a modest Hellinger improvement (0.152 vs. 0.182 at N=30) but a dramatic advantage in recovering the true underlying parameters: relative L² error on ∇V drops from 1.873 to 0.103 and on β from 1.008 to 0.376, indicating Direct-FP learns a spurious solution that fits densities but not the generative mechanism. On the real-world Mouse Gastrulation Atlas, the method achieves Hellinger 0.06 versus 0.36 for Direct-FP, a roughly 6× improvement in cell-type distribution prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper translates the purely mathematical theory of discrete gradient flows (Maas 2011; Mielke 2011; Erbar & Maas 2012) — which had seen almost no computational adoption — into a practical ML methodology for learning free-energy functionals from temporal snapshots. It adapts the continuous JKO scheme (Jordan et al. 1998; Bunne et al. 2022; Terpin et al. 2024) to discrete spaces by replacing the inapplicable W₂ metric with the W_K metric built on the logarithmic-mean mobility and a discrete Benamou-Brenier formulation. The key departure from CTMC-based discrete diffusion models (Campbell et al. 2022; Lou et al. 2024) is that it does not require a known forward noising process, instead recovering the underlying functional from observed density snapshots via first-order JKO optimality conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The W_K geometry enables recovery of interpretable dynamics parameters (∇V, β) rather than merely fitting densities, as demonstrated by the 18× gap in parameter recovery error versus Direct-FP. The 6× Hellinger improvement on the mouse gastrulation single-cell dataset (0.06 vs 0.36) shows the framework transfers to real biological data, though the state space is limited to 34 cell types. A fundamental limitation is scalability: the method requires explicit density estimation over the state space, making it infeasible for the n^d-state Hamming graphs used in language or molecule generation, which are the primary applications of discrete diffusion. Integrating this geometric framework with the conditional score-matching machinery of state-of-the-art discrete diffusion models is identified as the key next step.
