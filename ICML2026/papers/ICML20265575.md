## Problem

Existing inference-time guided sampling methods for flow and diffusion models fail under compositional rewards: when multiple reward functions induce conflicting gradients, approximate guidance pushes samples off the data manifold into low-density regions. The paper identifies gradient misalignment as the root cause through a three-term error bound and shows that the approximation error scales quadratically with the number of rewards and linearly with the angular divergence between guidance channels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265575/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on a frozen pretrained flow model, taking as input a set of G compositional reward functions (e.g., CLIP text-image similarity, collision costs, smoothness penalties) and a fixed source distribution (standard Gaussian or a reference image). It produces reward-tilted samples that simultaneously satisfy all constraints while remaining on the data manifold. The learnable guidance component requires only a brief online training phase (4–10 steps) using terminal rollouts from the current guided dynamics, with compute allocated selectively to high-conflict regions via a threshold τ on the pairwise cosine similarity.

## Method

The core contribution is Conflict-Aware Additive Guidance (g^car), which blends a cheap approximate guidance g^approx with a learnable residual guidance g_ψ through a conflict-aware gate w_t computed from the pairwise cosine similarity of per-reward gradients. When gradients are well-aligned (φ≈0), the gate suppresses the learned correction and g^car reduces to the standard approximate form; when gradients conflict (large 1−cos φ), the gate activates g_ψ to rectify the trajectory. The learnable guidance is trained via Terminal Value Regression (TVR): a scalar value function V_ψ(x_t, t) is regressed directly against terminal rewards r(x_1) from online rollouts under the current guided dynamics, bypassing bootstrapping and avoiding the deadly triad instability of fitted value evaluation. The guidance is then extracted as g_ψ = ∇_{x_t} V_ψ(x_t, t), and a masked loss ensures the network is updated only in regions where the conflict score exceeds a threshold τ, keeping the overall compute overhead minimal (e.g., 1.65 ms/sample vs. 0.37 ms for pure approximate guidance on the synthetic benchmark).

## Results

On the 2D synthetic benchmark under the gradient-conflict setting [1,0], g^car achieves 93.80% posterior coverage (PC) versus 71.70% for g^cov-G and 75.20% for PCGrad, while maintaining 100% constraint satisfaction at only 1.65 ms/sample (vs. 0.37 ms for g^cov-G). On Maze2D planning, g^car yields average gains of +19.0% safety, +38.75% success, and −0.68 violations per trajectory over g^cov-G across four compositional reward settings. On ManiSkill2 StackCube under hybrid constraints, g^car reduces violations from 1.8 to 0.4 and lifts success from 9% to 61%. On CelebA-HQ text-guided editing, g^car improves identity preservation to 0.681 (vs. 0.543 for g^cov-G, a 25.4% gain) while matching or exceeding all alignment scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against g^cov-G (Feng et al., 2025), the compute-light approximate guidance baseline, g^car consistently wins on all datasets: it recovers 22.1% of the lost posterior coverage under gradient conflict on the synthetic set, lifts Maze2D success from 16–42% to 61–79%, and boosts ManiSkill2 success from 9–12% to 61–72%, at roughly 4–11× inference overhead (still far below exact methods). PCGrad (Yu et al., 2020), the gradient-surgery method from multi-task learning, provides only marginal correction (e.g., 75.20% vs. 71.70% PC on the synthetic conflict setting) and even degrades ManiSkill2 success to 0% on both StackCube settings, confirming that projection-based deconfliction is insufficient for inference-time trajectory steering. GLASS-FKS (Holderrieth et al., 2026), the sample-based exact guidance method, avoids off-manifold drift and achieves near-perfect constraint satisfaction but at ~300× the inference cost of g^cov-G (299 ms vs. 0.37 ms on the synthetic set; 533 ms vs. 168 ms on Maze2D) and suffers from high variance on precision tasks like StackCube (24–32% success). MPPI (Williams et al., 2017), the classical optimization-based planner, is collision-free on static obstacles but drops to 63–69% success in dynamic/hybrid settings; MPPI+g^car outperforms vanilla MPPI, reaching 95% average success. FlowGrad (Liu et al., 2023b), the SOTA image-editing baseline, achieves competitive LPIPS (0.203) but lower identity preservation (0.677 vs. 0.681) and weaker compositional alignment (BLIP-ITM 0.326 vs. 0.597) than g^car on CelebA-HQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inference-time guided sampling framework for flow matching established by Feng et al. (2025), which derived the exact guidance term and identified the approximate g^cov-G form used by methods like FlowDPS and FlowChef. The key departure is a theoretical error decomposition (Theorem 4.2) that isolates gradient misalignment as the dominant source of off-manifold drift under compositional rewards, and a practical correction mechanism (g^car) that sits between compute-light approximate guidance and expensive exact methods like GLASS-FKS or Guidance Matching. Unlike PCGrad, which projects conflicting gradients in multi-task optimization, g^car uses a learned value-gradient residual (via Terminal Value Regression) activated only in high-conflict regions, avoiding the structural limitation that projection-based deconfliction cannot recover dissipated guidance energy at a fixed inference timestep.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Off-manifold drift under compositional rewards is a fundamental bottleneck for deploying flow models in safety-critical settings (robot manipulation, autonomous planning) where multiple heterogeneous constraints must be satisfied simultaneously without retraining. The G(G-1)μ²(1−cos φ) scaling law provides a clear diagnostic: practitioners can quantify how much drift to expect from a given set of reward functions and whether the conflict-aware correction is needed. Key limitations include convergence difficulty with non-smooth rewards (e.g., CLIP) and the need for a small amount of online training data for g_ψ; open questions include whether lighter alternatives to the learned value network exist and whether structured latent representations can yield smoother reward landscapes that reduce the need for correction altogether.
