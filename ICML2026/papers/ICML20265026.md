## Problem

Federated Label Distribution Learning (Fed-LDL) faces a trust dilemma: annotation quality varies substantially across clients (e.g., top-tier hospitals vs. community clinics), so standard sample-size-weighted aggregation (FedAvg) lets large-but-noisy clients dominate the global model, while centralized LDL noise-correction methods require global data access that federated privacy forbids. No prior work systematically addresses this heterogeneous supervision quality in a federated LDL setting, and no real-world benchmarks exist to evaluate it.

## Input / Output

Each client holds RGB images (48×48 for FER2013/FI, 512×384 for KADID-10k, 288×288 for PIPAL) paired with a label distribution d ∈ Δ^C constructed from a 10-expert ensemble (8-class emotion distribution or 5-bin quality score distribution). The model outputs a predicted label distribution via softmax over C logits, evaluated against the ground-truth distribution using six metrics (KL, Chebyshev, Clark, Canberra, Intersection, Cosine). A per-client quality indicator q_m ∈ ℝ⁺ quantifies local annotation fidelity and drives both the calibration strength and aggregation weight.

## Method

FedQual couples two mechanisms. On the client side, a Global Semantic Anchor (GSA) — the global model's logits broadcast each round — provides a calibration reference; each client minimizes a joint objective of local LDL loss (KL divergence) and anchor-alignment regularizer, with the calibration weight α_m = σ(β(λ(q_m) − λ₀)) automatically increasing for low-quality clients and decreasing for high-quality ones. On the server side, aggregation uses an Effective Information Density score S_m = N_m · q_m, annealed over rounds via a trust factor ρ_t that transitions from quality-dominated weighting (ρ_t=0) to sample-size weighting (ρ_t→1), with a softmax-temperature γ_temp controlling weight sharpness. A theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) proves that under heterogeneous supervision quality, client-specific calibration strictly outperforms any uniform calibration in total risk, providing a principled justification for the adaptive design.

## Results

FedQual achieves the best or near-best performance across all four Fed-LDL benchmarks under six evaluation metrics. On FER-LDL, it attains the lowest KL divergence (0.2528) and highest Cosine similarity (0.8739); on KADID-LDL, it reaches a KL of 0.0908 with Cosine above 0.96. On PIPAL-LDL, the full model reduces KL from 0.252 (FedAvg) to 0.116 and increases Cosine from 0.929 to 0.958. FedQual is best on 23 of 24 metric–dataset combinations, losing only on Clark divergence for KADID-LDL to the component baseline FedQAgg (0.9655 vs. 1.0682).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedQual consistently outperforms all five external baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, FedRDN, MOON, FedGloSS) on KL divergence across all four benchmarks, with the largest margin on FER-LDL (0.2528 vs. 0.3420 for FedAvg, a 26% reduction) and FI-LDL (0.4085 vs. 0.4233 for FedAvg). Among the quality-agnostic baselines, FedProx is the strongest competitor on Chebyshev and Canberra, yet FedQual still leads by 0.004–0.023 on Chebyshev and 0.03–0.12 on Canberra. The two component baselines (FedQAgg for server-side reweighting only, FedQRect for client-side rectification only) confirm the synergy: FedQual beats both on every metric for FER-LDL, FI-LDL, and PIPAL-LDL, and on 5 of 6 metrics for KADID-LDL. The sole exception is Clark divergence on KADID-LDL, where FedQAgg (0.9655) edges out FedQual (1.0682), suggesting that server-side quality weighting alone can be slightly more effective for this particular distance measure on the IQA task. Overall, FedQual is best on 23 of 24 metric–dataset combinations, demonstrating that the joint client-side calibration and server-side reliability-aware aggregation are complementary and necessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of federated learning and label distribution learning (LDL), a combination the authors explicitly identify as unstudied. It departs from prior noisy-label FL work (FedDiv, FedFixer, FedES) by targeting continuous, dense supervision where noise manifests as subtle probability-mass shifts rather than discrete label flips, and from centralized inaccurate-LDL methods (manifold smoothing, low-rank label correlation) by operating under strict data isolation. The Global Semantic Anchor draws conceptually on ensemble consensus theory and Bayesian model ensembling, while the annealed aggregation mechanism repurposes ideas from robust FL reweighting, but neither component has been applied to the LDL-specific trust dilemma before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing Fed-LDL as a distinct problem and releasing four benchmarks with controlled annotation quality disparity, this paper provides the field with the first rigorous testbed for evaluating federated methods under heterogeneous supervision fidelity. The theoretical guarantee that client-specific calibration strictly dominates uniform calibration gives a principled foundation for quality-adaptive designs beyond this specific framework. Key limitations include the simplified quality indicator (tied to annotator count rather than inferred from data) and the assumption of truthful quality reporting, both of which the authors flag as open problems; unsupervised quality estimation and privacy-preserving verification of annotation metadata are natural next steps.
