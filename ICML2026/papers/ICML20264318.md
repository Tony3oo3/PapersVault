## Problem

Self-supervised prompt optimization eliminates the need for labeled data but suffers from two critical issues: optimization ambiguity (lack of clear signals to reliably assess and improve prompts) and high computational costs (e.g., consistency-based methods requiring ~90 minutes and $0.112 per task). Existing approaches either propagate errors from synthetic supervision, rely on vague task specifications for LLM-as-a-judge scoring, or incur prohibitive compute for self-consistency checks, making them impractical for scalable, low-resource deployment.

## Input / Output

MR.PEA requires only three minimal inputs: a one-sentence task description (e.g., 'Infer the date from context'), a single unlabeled question from the task domain (no ground-truth answer provided), and a format specification defining the expected output structure (e.g., 'Final answer in <answer>a multiple-choice option</answer>'). The output is a single optimized prompt string (typically 100–200 tokens) that, when prepended to new questions, maximizes zero-shot accuracy under strict parsing. Notably, the method never sees a labeled answer during optimization; all evaluation is performed via pairwise comparison of prompt-generated responses scored against task-specific reasoning criteria rather than ground-truth matching.

## Method

MR.PEA runs a five-phase iterative cycle (up to 10 iterations) driven by meta-reasoning—reasoning about the reasoning process itself. Phase 1 curates task-specific knowledge comprising abstract problem-solving strategies and reasoning-oriented evaluation criteria, adaptively triggering web search (capped at 2 queries) when internal knowledge is insufficient. Phase 2 generates diverse validation examples varying in difficulty, context, and structure to prevent overfitting. Phase 3 refines a new prompt candidate by integrating the curated knowledge, historical evaluation feedback, and top-ranked prompts from a dynamic pool. Phase 4 performs pairwise evaluation of the new prompt against the current best using the task-specific criteria, producing a winner and actionable feedback. Phase 5 updates a prompt pool with an exploration-exploitation ranking (iteration bonus for new winners, decay for stale prompts). This explicit knowledge construction resolves optimization ambiguity by providing a directed, task-specific roadmap rather than relying on generic consistency or vague judge criteria, achieving an average 7.4% improvement over the best baseline at a cost of ~$0.01 per task.

## Results

MR.PEA achieves 79.10% average zero-shot accuracy across GSM8K and 22 Big-Bench Hard tasks, outperforming the best baseline (SPO) by 7.4% on average. The largest gains appear on BBH Math tasks (84.00%, +18.7% over the best baseline) and Spatial/Sequential/Attribute reasoning (87.23%, +10.1%). On GSM8K, MR.PEA reaches 92.34% accuracy. The entire optimization costs only $0.01 per task and completes in approximately 2 minutes using GPT-4.1-nano.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MR.PEA outperforms all six baselines across every sub-area. Against the strongest self-supervised baseline SPO (73.68% avg), MR.PEA gains +5.42 points on average, with the largest margin on Math (+14.8 pts: 84.00 vs 69.20) and Spatial/Seq./Attr. (+7.98 pts: 87.23 vs 79.25). Against PromptWizard (69.81% avg), the gap is +9.29 points, with MR.PEA particularly dominant on Math (+16.56 pts) and Logic (+11.52 pts). Supervised methods OPRO (65.02%) and GPO (63.02%) trail by 14.08 and 16.08 points respectively, despite having access to labeled validation data. The consistency-based GLaPE (58.06%) is the weakest baseline, underperforming even the static CoT prompt (65.22%) on average. Notably, MR.PEA without web search (78.46%) still exceeds all baselines, confirming that the meta-reasoning framework itself drives most of the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

MR.PEA sits at the intersection of self-supervised prompt optimization and meta-reasoning. It departs from prior self-supervised methods (SPO's LLM-as-a-judge, GLaPE's consistency scoring, PromptWizard's synthetic supervision) by replacing their generic or pseudo-labeled optimization signals with iteratively constructed task-specific knowledge—abstract problem-solving strategies and reasoning-oriented evaluation criteria—derived through meta-reasoning. The dynamic prompt pool with exploration/exploitation scoring borrows concepts from evolutionary and bandit-based optimization, while the adaptive web-search mechanism extends the closed-loop self-supervised setting to an open-knowledge one. Conceptually, the paper reframes prompt optimization as a knowledge-building process rather than a search-over-prompts problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: at $0.01 per task with only a single unlabeled example as input, MR.PEA makes high-quality prompt engineering accessible in low-resource and budget-constrained settings, and the finding that optimized prompts transfer across model families (including to 0.8B edge-scale models) suggests broad deployability. Open questions include the reliability of web-search augmentation for niche tasks, the risk of bias propagation without ground-truth supervision, and whether the meta-reasoning knowledge curation generalizes to truly novel or multi-modal tasks. The paper also highlights an underexplored trade-off: for simpler tasks, a smaller instruct model with an optimized prompt can match a larger reasoning model at half the inference token cost, pointing toward a shift from 'bigger model' to 'better prompt' as a deployment strategy.
