## Problem

Estimating the intrinsic dimension (ID) of multi-modal data—specifically the distinct IDs of shared and modality-specific subspaces—remains unsolved. Existing neural network-based ID estimators are static, uni-modal, or (in contrastive methods) adapt only implicitly to the shared ID, while classical multi-view decomposition methods (JIVE, AJIVE, SLIDE) assume linear mixing and lack scalability. No prior approach provides explicit, interpretable estimates of both shared and private subspace dimensions in a single training pass.

## Input / Output

FiGuRO accepts paired multi-modal data (e.g., 24×24 MNIST images with 112×112 audio spectrograms, 32×32 SAR/optical satellite patches, 256×256 RGB/depth image pairs) or, in a post-hoc probing mode, frozen embeddings from pretrained models (Stable Diffusion VAE, Depth Anything V2) that are projected through trainable linear adapters. The output is a set of final integer ranks {k_s*, k_1*, …, k_M*} representing the estimated ID of each subspace, along with the trained low-rank autoencoder whose disentangled shared and private latent vectors can be used directly for downstream classification or interpretability tasks.

## Method

FiGuRO inserts low-rank decomposable bottleneck layers (factorized weight matrices W ≈ U·S·Vᵀ via truncated SVD, inspired by LoRA and adaptive rank reduction) into a multi-modal autoencoder that routes each modality through private and shared subspaces. A bidirectional rank-optimization algorithm, grounded in Rate-Distortion Theory, governs these layers: after a full-rank pre-training phase establishes a baseline fidelity D₀, the algorithm checks every τ epochs whether the reconstruction distortion (measured by R²) exceeds a user-defined budget λ; if compression is too lossy it un-masks dimensions (increases rank), and if fidelity is within budget it prunes dimensions via a cumulative singular-value energy threshold γ. Disentanglement of shared versus private information emerges as the rank-minimizing solution under the split-decoder architecture, eliminating the need for auxiliary orthogonality or sparsity losses. Theoretical guarantees show finite-time convergence and that the returned rank approximates the true manifold dimension under sufficient model capacity and a sample-to-capacity ratio N ≥ α·C_e/k*.

## Results

On multi-modal ID estimation (Table 2), FiGuRO achieves an average deviation from ground truth of 2.9 (SEM 0.7) across four simulated datasets with varying shared/private ID scales, compared to 9.5 for the best baseline (ShIndICA), 11.8 for SLIDE, 12.4 for JIVE, and 36.3 for AJIVE. On downstream classification, FiGuRO isolates shared signals with 0.94±0.01 accuracy on Audio MNIST (digit identity) and 0.55±0.03 balanced accuracy on So2Sat (climate zone), outperforming all multi-view decomposition baselines. For uni-modal estimation on the high-dimensional omics simulation C, FiGuRO is the only method providing consistent estimates across both high-noise (C₁) and high-nonlinearity (C₂) regimes, while PCA overestimates noisy data and local estimators (MLE, TwoNN) systematically underestimate non-linear data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FiGuRO outperforms all four multi-view decomposition baselines (JIVE, AJIVE, SLIDE, ShIndICA) on multi-modal ID estimation, achieving an average deviation of 2.9 versus 9.5 for the best baseline ShIndICA — a 3× improvement. AJIVE is the worst performer (deviation 36.3), frequently producing extreme overestimates (e.g., rank 197 for a true ID of 3). JIVE and SLIDE consistently collapse the shared subspace to rank 1 regardless of ground truth, while overestimating private subspaces. On uni-modal estimation, FiGuRO is the only method providing consistent estimates across both noisy and non-linear data regimes; PCA overestimates noisy data, local estimators (MLE, TwoNN) underestimate non-linear data, RRA is unstable across threshold choices, and ARD-VAE suffers from posterior collapse. On downstream classification (Table 4), FiGuRO achieves 0.94 shared accuracy on Audio MNIST (vs. AJIVE 0.99, JIVE 0.42) and 0.55 balanced accuracy on So2Sat (vs. SLIDE 0.24, JIVE 0.15), confirming that its emergent disentanglement preserves semantic utility where baselines frequently 'swap' shared and private signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

FiGuRO synthesizes three distinct research threads: the low-rank weight decomposition paradigm from LoRA and Rank Reduction Autoencoders (ARR), the optimization framework of Rate-Distortion Theory, and the multi-view subspace decomposition literature (JIVE, AJIVE, SLIDE, ShIndICA). It departs from ARR by decomposing weight matrices rather than latent batches (enabling batch-size-independent global structure) and by adding bidirectional rank updates governed by a distortion budget, whereas ARR only reduces ranks. It departs from classical multi-view methods by removing the linearity assumption and from contrastive multi-modal methods (e.g., CLIP) by providing explicit, interpretable ID estimates for both shared and private subspaces rather than an implicit, uninterpretable adaptation to shared dimensionality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FiGuRO fills a concrete gap: no prior neural method explicitly estimates the distinct IDs of shared and modality-specific subspaces in a single training pass, and the disentanglement emerges without auxiliary orthogonality or sparsity losses. The ability to apply FiGuRO as a lightweight latent probe on frozen pretrained models (Stable Diffusion VAE, Depth Anything V2) opens a practical path to interpretable multi-modal fusion without end-to-end retraining. Limitations include dependence on sufficient encoder/decoder capacity, occasional underestimation when generative variables are correlated, and the fact that ID estimates are model-dependent rather than absolute. Open questions include scaling to more than two modalities, continual/online rank adaptation, and whether the rate-distortion framing can be tightened into a non-greedy global optimum.
