## Problem

Cross-view geo-localization (CVGL) in UAV scenarios suffers severe performance degradation under domain shifts from varying geographic regions and weather conditions, because existing global feature alignment methods rely on coarse discrimination and overlook fine-grained object-level correspondences. This limitation is exacerbated in UAV settings where broader perspectives introduce dense, fine-grained objects and visual clutter that global descriptors cannot effectively disentangle from view-specific noise.

## Input / Output

The method takes paired RGB images at 448×448 resolution — a UAV/drone query image and a satellite gallery image — and produces 4096-dimensional scene-level descriptors for retrieval-based geographic matching. Training uses one-to-one image pairs from source regions (University-1652, DenseUAV) with InfoNCE contrastive supervision, while evaluation is conducted on unseen target domains (SUES-200 at multiple altitudes, GTA-V cross-area) to measure cross-region and multi-weather generalization. A Relational Distillation variant (InfoGeo*) eliminates the OCL modules at inference, reducing parameters to 90.50M and FLOPs to 91.70G, matching the vanilla backbone cost.

## Method

InfoGeo reformulates cross-view matching via the Information Bottleneck principle, decomposing visual content into view-invariant structural semantics and view-specific nuisances, then optimizing two complementary objectives: maximizing cross-view mutual information at the concept level and compressing view-specific conditional information. The Cross-View Visual Concept Reasoner implements this through two synergistic modules: Cross-View Adaptive Concept Selection (CACS), which fuses decoding attention maps via cross-view multi-head attention and applies a learnable expert router with a binary-selection regularizer to suppress task-irrelevant concepts; and Concept Structural Relational Reasoning (CSRR), which constructs concept graphs from cosine similarities of attention maps, extracts spectral embeddings via Laplacian Eigenmaps (top r=4 eigenvectors), and aligns them across views using an orthogonal Procrustes loss. An Object-Centric Visual Augmentation (OCVA) module injects the filtered object-centric representations into global DINOv2-Base features through channel-wise FiLM scaling, and an optional Relational Distillation module transfers the resulting relational geometry into vanilla MixVPR descriptors for efficient inference. The full training objective combines InfoNCE alignment, slot reconstruction MSE, the IB-derived info loss (L_cacs + L_struct), and relational distillation with weighted trade-off parameters.

## Results

On the four primary cross-view transfer scenarios (University-1652→SUES-200, University-1652→DenseUAV, DenseUAV→SUES-200, DenseUAV→University-1652), InfoGeo* (with Relational Distillation) achieves R@1 of 95.07%, 42.30%, 93.41%, and 69.37% respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline CVcities by +4.80, +3.43, +3.74, and +3.29 points. On the challenging GTA-V cross-area benchmark, InfoGeo* reaches 57.90% R@1 and reduces the mean top-1 localization distance (Dis@1) to 416.75 m, a 24% improvement over the previous best (CAMP at 547.71 m). Under multi-weather transfer (Fog-Rain, Rain-Snow, Fog-Snow), InfoGeo* achieves an average 6.0% R@1 gain over CVcities, with the largest margin (+7.6% R@1) under the most severe Fog-Snow transition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoGeo* outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated scenario. The closest competitor is CVcities (the base model), which InfoGeo* surpasses by 3.2–4.8 R@1 points depending on the transfer direction; the gap is most pronounced on University→SUES (+4.80 R@1) and least on DenseUAV→University (+3.29 R@1). Among the other baselines, DAC and CAMP are the next strongest, reaching 87.65–88.34% R@1 on University→SUES, still 7+ points below InfoGeo*. On the hardest transfer (University→DenseUAV), the performance spread is widest: InfoGeo* achieves 42.30% R@1 versus 38.87% (CVcities), 37.81% (DAC), and as low as 13.47% (EM-CVGL), highlighting the benefit of object-centric structural reasoning under severe domain shift. On GTA-V (cross-area), InfoGeo* leads CAMP by +2.99 R@1 and reduces Dis@1 from 547.71 m to 416.75 m (−24%), while Game4Loc and MMGeo trail by 8–13 R@1 points. Under multi-weather conditions, InfoGeo* maintains a consistent 5–8% R@1 advantage over CVcities, with the largest gain under Fog-Snow (+7.6%), confirming that the information-bottleneck-based noise compression is most effective under extreme appearance variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to bring Object-Centric Learning (Slot Attention) into the cross-view geo-localization (CVGL) setting, where prior OCL work targeted multi-view scenes with sparse, salient objects rather than the dense, cluttered UAV-to-satellite matching problem. It builds directly on CVcities (DINOv2 + MixVPR) as the backbone and reformulates the contrastive alignment objective through the Information Bottleneck principle, replacing the standard InfoNCE loss at the concept level with a spectral structural alignment (Laplacian Eigenmaps + Orthogonal Procrustes) and an adaptive concept-selection regularizer. The Relational Distillation module, adapted from Park et al. (2019), decouples training-time object-centric reasoning from inference cost, a practical design choice not seen in prior CVGL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IB-theoretic framing gives a principled, interpretable justification for why object-level decomposition helps CVGL under domain shift, and the consistent 3–5% gains across four benchmarks (including multi-weather and cross-area virtual settings) suggest the approach is robust rather than dataset-specific. However, the paper acknowledges a scalability limitation: at 300 m altitude, the proliferation of weakly informative objects dilutes concept signals, hinting that fixed slot counts may not suffice for very high-altitude or extremely dense scenes. Open questions include whether the spectral alignment generalizes to non-paired or one-to-many retrieval settings, and whether the conditional-independence assumption (validated empirically but not guaranteed) holds under more extreme sensor discrepancies.
