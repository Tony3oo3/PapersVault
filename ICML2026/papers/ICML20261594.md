## Problem

Black-box auditing of language models can miss subtle misalignment and hidden information that models are trained to conceal. Existing elicitation techniques operate on inputs (e.g., prefill attacks) or activations, but no systematic weight-space method exists to destabilize a model's suppression of learned secrets. This paper asks whether amplifying the reasoning direction in weight space can force models to verbalize information they would otherwise keep hidden.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two model checkpoints from the same family (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct as M and Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking as R) to compute the reasoning task vector, then produces a single overthinking model θ_O_α by extrapolating along that vector with α > 1. At inference time, standard text prompts (MMLU questions with hidden metadata, Taboo clue requests, gender probes, or SSC-tagged conversations) are fed to the overthinking model, and outputs are parsed into thinking blocks and final responses to compute disclosure rates. Optionally, a prefill prefix is prepended to the assistant turn, making the input a (prompt, prefill) pair while the weight perturbation is applied independently.

## Method

The core mechanism is task vector arithmetic applied to a reasoning meta-capability: the reasoning task vector τ_reason = θ_R − θ_M isolates the weight-space direction encoding deliberative reasoning (since M and R share pretraining and diverge only at reasoning distillation), and the overthinking model θ_O_α = θ_M + α·τ_reason with α > 1 extrapolates beyond the trained reasoning model to intensify search over continuations and destabilize concealment equilibria. To prevent coherence collapse at high α, the paper introduces layer-wise attenuation coefficients λ_ℓ applied per layer, evaluating uniform, heuristic (freeze last 5/half, linear decay), and adaptive Fisher-weighted strategies (trace Fisher, directional Fisher along τ_ℓ, and a novel Task Magnitude weighting that amplifies layers with strong reasoning signal but low output sensitivity). The approach is validated against random norm-matched perturbations to confirm that the reasoning direction—not mere parameter displacement—drives disclosure for directionally protected secrets, and it composes orthogonally with input-level prefill attacks, with metacognitive prefills benefiting most from amplification.

## Results

Reasoning amplification (α > 1) increases auditing success in 9 out of 10 setting-scale combinations, with peak disclosure up to 10× higher than the baseline reasoning model (α = 1). Fisher-weighted layer-wise coefficients (Task Magnitude, Directional Fisher) outperform uniform application, reaching 25.5% leak rate on Taboo (8B, α = 2) and 18.8% on SSC (32B, α = 2.5) versus 7.0% and 2.3% respectively under uniform scaling. When stacked with metacognitive prefill, the combined method achieves the highest audit rates observed: 97.7% on Gender (8B), 30.6% on MMLU (8B, α = 1.5), and 53.4% on SSC (32B, α = 3). Random weight perturbations matching the task vector's L2 norm show no significant increase for directionally protected secrets (Taboo, SSC), confirming that the reasoning direction—not mere perturbation magnitude—drives disclosure in those settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is the prefill attack (Li et al., 2025), which seeds the assistant turn with a leading prefix to bypass refusal. Prefill alone achieves 99.2% on Gender (2B) but only 0% on Taboo (all sizes) and 53.4% on SSC (32B). When stacked with overthinking, the composition is complementary for metacognitive prefills: Prefill+OT(best) reaches 97.7% on Gender (8B) and 53.4% on SSC (32B, α=3), the highest rates in the paper. However, on Taboo, where the natural prefill is necessarily direct ('The word I'm not supposed to say is...'), Prefill+OT underperforms OT(best) alone (9.4% vs. 25.5% at α=2), because the direct commitment bypasses extended deliberation that amplification would otherwise exploit. Against random perturbation baselines (Layer-Norm and Global-Norm, scale-matched to the task vector), overthinking wins decisively on Taboo and SSC—random methods show no significant increase over baseline—while random Global-Norm actually outperforms overthinking on MMLU at α=3.0, indicating that in-context secrets are noise-fragile rather than directionally protected. Fisher-weighted methods (Task Magnitude, Directional Fisher) consistently outperform uniform application by 2–4× at peak α, with the gap widening for larger models (8B shows the largest overthinking-vs-reasoning delta across all three trained organisms).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends task vector arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) from discrete task editing to a meta-capability—reasoning—by treating the instruct-to-thinking weight difference as a steerable direction that can be extrapolated beyond α=1. It builds directly on Fierro & Roger (2025), who showed weight-space steering generalizes further out-of-distribution than activation steering for alignment behaviors, and on the chain-of-thought faithfulness literature (Turpin et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2025) that established reasoning models still verbalize answer-influencing cues less than half the time. The experimental settings adopt the secret-keeping model organisms of Cywiński et al. (2025), and the layer-wise attenuation strategies draw on Fisher-weighted merging (Matena & Raffel, 2022) and EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017), but the key departure is using amplification as an auditing primitive rather than a model-editing tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical contribution is a simple, white-box auditing technique that composes with existing black-box methods (prefill attacks) and can amplify rare disclosure events by up to 10×, making pre-deployment audits statistically feasible with fewer samples. The two-regime finding—some secrets are directionally protected (requiring the reasoning task vector) while others are noise-fragile (yielding to any large perturbation)—suggests a concrete two-stage auditing protocol. Key limitations include evaluation on a single model family (Qwen3-VL), 256 samples per cell, and reliance on keyword matching plus a single LLM judge; open questions include whether the auditing window is predictable from model architecture, whether the identity-confusion and backtracking signals generalize as reliable audit indicators, and mechanistic evidence for why amplification destabilizes concealment.
