## Problem

In document parsing with MLLMs, standard SFT yields negligible gains over the base model—particularly on complex tables and formulas—while RL driven by a simple edit-distance reward achieves substantial improvements, contradicting the intuition that dense supervision should outperform sparse scalar rewards. This paper investigates the root cause of this divergence and asks what SFT and RL fundamentally learn in this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261918/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single document image (a PDF page rendered at arbitrary resolution with varied CSS layouts) and produces a structured text sequence in Markdown, where tables are encoded as HTML, mathematical equations as LaTeX, and remaining content as plain text. The paper also explores an intermediate JSON representation that explicitly separates element-type classification from content transcription, decoupling structural layout prediction from the generative syntax burden of direct Markdown output.

## Method

The paper provides a unified explanation grounded in KL-divergence: SFT approximates forward KL minimization (mode-covering), biasing the model toward low-entropy structural syntax while diffusing probability over high-entropy content; RL approximates reverse KL minimization (mode-seeking), concentrating probability mass on high-reward content-fidelity modes. Building on this, the proposed two-stage pipeline first performs Content-Enriched SFT on a JSON-structured task augmented with auxiliary sub-tasks (formula-to-LaTeX transcription, table-cell-to-HTML extraction) to force content learning alongside structure, then applies Structure-Aware RL using GRPO with a decomposed reward that sums element-specific signals (text edit distance, table TEDS/TEDS-S, formula CDM) with equal weights, ensuring holistic refinement rather than myopic optimization on the longest sequences.

## Results

On OmniDocBench, the unified SFT-RL pipeline achieves an Overall Accuracy (OA) of 83.20 on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, improving from the base model's 72.44, with Edit Distance (ED) dropping from 0.26 to 0.10, TEDS rising from 63.68 to 79.09, and CDM increasing from 79.55 to 80.93. For Ovis2.5-2B, the Content-Enriched SFT with table/formula data reaches OA of 82.43 (vs. 71.58 without), and the Structure-Aware RL with decomposed reward attains OA of 79.00 with CDM of 85.30. A central empirical finding is that standard SFT yields negligible gains over the base model (particularly on tables and formulas), while standard RL provides substantial improvements, and the unified two-stage strategy surpasses both individually.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two base MLLMs—Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Ovis2.5-2B—against standard SFT, standard RL (GRPO with edit-distance reward), and the proposed unified SFT-RL pipeline. Against the Qwen2.5-VL-3B base (OA 72.44, TEDS 63.68), the unified SFT-RL wins on every metric: OA +10.76, TEDS +15.41, ED −0.16, and Read −0.46. Against the Ovis2.5-2B base, the Content-Enriched SFT with TF data lifts OA from 71.58 to 82.43 and CDM from 63.59 to 80.50, while the Structure-Aware RL with decomposed reward pushes CDM to 85.30 and OA to 79.00. Critically, standard SFT alone provides only marginal gains over the base model (the paper's central counter-intuitive finding), whereas standard RL alone achieves moderate improvements but underperforms the unified pipeline on structure-sensitive metrics like TEDS. The unified method consistently outperforms either strategy in isolation, validating the complementary structure-learner (SFT) / content-refiner (RL) division of labor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two active research threads: MLLM-based document parsing (MonkeyOCR, PaddleOCR-VL, dots.ocr) and the comparative study of SFT vs. RL post-training (Chu et al. 2025, Shenfeld et al. 2025, Chen et al. 2025). It builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as the RL backbone and on the known equivalence between SFT and forward-KL minimization and between KL-regularized RL and reverse-KL minimization, but departs from prior work by applying this theoretical lens specifically to document parsing to explain a concrete empirical anomaly. Unlike prior studies that frame SFT and RL as competing paradigms, this paper argues they are complementary—SFT as a mode-covering structure learner and RL as a mode-seeking content refiner—and designs a two-stage pipeline that explicitly exploits this division of labor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, KL-divergence-grounded explanation for why dense supervised signals can be less effective than sparse reward signals in composite tasks coupling low-entropy syntax with high-entropy content, which has implications beyond document parsing for any task with this structure. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic data (no noisy real-world scans), evaluation on only 2B–3B models, and a single benchmark (OmniDocBench), leaving open whether the SFT-failure phenomenon persists at larger scales or with noisier supervision. Future work could explore whether the mode-seeking behavior of RL can be approximated more efficiently than multi-sample GRPO, and whether the structure/content decomposition generalizes to other multimodal generation tasks.
