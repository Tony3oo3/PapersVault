## Problem

State-of-the-art infrared and visible image fusion (IVIF) methods based on Transformers or diffusion models achieve high fusion quality but are computationally prohibitive for real-time mobile deployment, while the ultra-fast LUT-Fuse sacrifices content-adaptive fusion due to its fixed lookup-table mapping. The paper addresses the open challenge of achieving both real-time inference on resource-constrained edge devices and high-quality, content-adaptive multi-modal fusion within a single lightweight architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260985/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a visible RGB image (3 channels) and a grayscale infrared image (1 channel) at the same spatial resolution, concatenates them along the channel dimension to form a 4-channel input, and produces a fused RGB image (3 channels) at the same resolution. Training uses 96×96 random crops from source pairs at resolutions ranging from 640×480 (MSRS) to 1280×1024 (LLVIP), while inference operates on full-resolution frames (e.g., 640×480 on the Jetson Orin NX edge platform). The entire network contains only ~4K parameters (0.004M), making it orders of magnitude smaller than competing methods.

## Method

MobileFusion's core contribution is the Re-parameterizable Multi-Branch Convolution (RepMBConv), which during training employs heterogeneous parallel branches (k×k, 1×k, k×1, 1×1 convolutions, some with BatchNorm) whose outputs are channel-wise concatenated and projected via a 1×1 convolution, explicitly encouraging cross-modal interaction through a concatenation-and-projection strategy rather than element-wise summation. At inference, all branches are analytically folded into a single standard k×k convolution via BatchNorm folding, kernel zero-padding, and weight aggregation, yielding a single-path operator with no performance loss. Two auxiliary modules complete the architecture: a Lightweight Context-Aware Attention (LCAA) combining dual-stream channel attention (average + max pooling) with dual-direction spatial attention (1×5 and 5×1 depthwise convolutions) to capture both global semantics and local edges, and a Re-parameterized Feed-Forward Network (RepFFN) with two 1×1 RepMBConv layers (expansion ratio 2, PReLU) to boost nonlinear expressiveness. The network is trained end-to-end with a composite loss (SSIM, intensity, Sobel gradient, and CbCr color consistency) for 120 epochs, achieving 2.10 ms latency on an RTX 4090 and 12.88 ms on a Jetson Orin NX while outperforming all baselines on fusion quality metrics across three benchmarks.

## Results

MobileFusion (4K parameters) achieves the best SCD and CC on all three benchmarks (MSRS, FMB, LLVIP), with SCD of 1.766/1.601/1.653 and CC of 0.616/0.623/0.721 respectively, and the best SSIM on FMB (0.986). On the NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX edge platform, MobileFusion achieves 12.88 ms latency (real-time at 480P), outperforming LUT-Fuse (17.41 ms) while using 17× fewer parameters (0.004M vs 0.07M) and 2.1× fewer FLOPs (1.13G vs 2.42G). On the RTX 4090, MobileFusion runs at 2.10 ms per frame, the fastest among all compared methods including LUT-Fuse (3.41 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MobileFusion achieves the best SCD on all three datasets (1.766 on MSRS, 1.601 on FMB, 1.653 on LLVIP), surpassing the next-best Text-IF (1.689) by 4.7% on MSRS and DCEvo (1.561) by 5.8% on LLVIP. It also leads on CC across all three benchmarks (0.616/0.623/0.721), with the largest margin on LLVIP where it exceeds CDDFuse (0.713) by 1.1%. On FMB, MobileFusion attains the highest SSIM (0.986), outperforming LUT-Fuse (0.976) and DCEvo (0.972). However, DCEvo edges out MobileFusion on SSIM for MSRS (0.964 vs 0.956) and LLVIP (0.940 vs 0.935), while LUT-Fuse leads on MI for FMB (3.382 vs 3.299) and LLVIP (2.928 vs 2.922), and Text-IF leads on MI for MSRS (3.274 vs 3.172). The decisive advantage of MobileFusion is efficiency: at 4K parameters and 2.10 ms latency on RTX 4090 (12.88 ms on Jetson Orin NX), it is 17× smaller and 1.6× faster than LUT-Fuse while matching or exceeding its fusion quality on most metrics, and it is 100–300× faster than Transformer- or diffusion-based methods (e.g., Diff-IF at 1653 ms, TC-MoA at 341 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the structural re-parameterization paradigm (RepVGG, MobileOne, RepViT) from high-level vision tasks to low-level multi-modal image fusion, where prior re-parameterized designs fail because they lack explicit cross-modal interaction mechanisms. The key architectural departure is the RepMBConv module, which replaces the conventional single k×k re-parameterized path with heterogeneous multi-branch kernels (k×k, 1×k, k×1) fused via channel-wise concatenation and 1×1 projection rather than element-wise summation, providing a stronger inductive bias for modality interaction. The paper positions itself primarily against LUT-Fuse (the main speed competitor) and against computationally heavy Transformer- and diffusion-based fusion methods that cannot achieve real-time inference even on high-end GPUs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MobileFusion demonstrates that a ~4K-parameter convolutional network can match or exceed SOTA fusion quality while running in real time on edge hardware (12.88 ms on Jetson Orin NX), opening the door to practical deployment in autonomous driving, surveillance, and portable medical imaging where latency and memory are hard constraints. The finding that heterogeneous multi-branch kernels with concatenation-and-projection outperform both standard convolutions and conventional re-parameterized designs specifically for fusion tasks provides a useful design principle for future lightweight fusion architectures. Open questions include whether this approach extends to video fusion, whether the 4K parameter budget is truly the ceiling for quality, and how the method generalizes beyond the tested IVIF and PET-MRI settings to other multi-modal fusion domains.
