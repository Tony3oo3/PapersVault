## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiT) are computationally expensive, and existing acceleration methods like pruning and distillation apply a fixed computational budget uniformly across all samples and denoising steps. This static design ignores the fact that the generative process exhibits substantial, sample-dependent sparsity—some computations contribute marginally to quality for easy samples but are critical for complex ones—leading to a suboptimal efficiency-quality trade-off.

## Input / Output

E-DiT operates on top of pretrained DiT backbones (Qwen-Image 20B, FLUX.1-dev, Hunyuan3D-3.0) and takes the same inputs as the base model: a text prompt for image generation or an input image for 3D asset generation. During the denoising loop, each block's lightweight router consumes the current noisy latent (L×D tensor) and the timestep embedding to produce a scalar gating probability and a 4-dimensional width distribution. The output is a generated image or 3D mesh identical in format to the unaccelerated model, but produced with roughly 2× fewer FLOPs.

## Method

E-DiT equips each DiT block with a lightweight router that performs timestep-conditioned modulation (LayerNorm + scale/shift from the timestep embedding), projects to a low-dimensional space (H_r ≪ D), and outputs two predictions via separate linear heads: a gating logit for block skipping and a width logit vector for MLP width selection. During training, a Straight-Through Estimator makes the discrete skip decision differentiable, and the joint objective combines the standard flow-matching loss with efficiency regularizers that constrain the average gate probability and average MLP width to target budgets. At inference, blocks below a threshold are skipped entirely, active blocks use explicit matrix slicing to reduce MLP width (selecting from {1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1} of the original hidden dimension), and a training-free block-wise caching mechanism reuses cached residual updates for blocks whose gating probability falls in a borderline region near the threshold, with a maximum reuse count to prevent error accumulation.

## Results

On Qwen-Image, E-DiT-base achieves 88.1 DPG-Bench and 0.893 GenEval at 1702 ms latency (vs. 2431 ms for the base model), while E-DiT-turbo reaches 1283 ms (~1.9× speedup) with 85.4 DPG and 0.853 GenEval. On FLUX.1-dev, E-DiT achieves 80.5 DPG and 0.671 GenEval at 374 ms, roughly 1.9× faster than the base model (715 ms). For 3D asset generation on Hunyuan3D-3.0, E-DiT nearly doubles throughput (2587 ms vs. 5012 ms) while matching or slightly exceeding the baseline on ULIP-I (0.1473 vs. 0.1446) and Uni3D-I (0.4332 vs. 0.4334).

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-DiT-base outperforms all pruning-based baselines (TinyFusion, HP, PPCL) on Qwen-Image in both speed and quality: it achieves 88.1 DPG and 0.893 GenEval at 1702 ms, versus PPCL's 87.9/0.847 at 1792 ms, HP's 83.3/0.766 at 1786 ms, and TinyFusion's 80.7/0.739 at 1789 ms. On FLUX, E-DiT (374 ms, 80.5 DPG, 0.671 GenEval) dominates Dense2MoE (513 ms, 76.2 DPG, 0.475 GenEval) and PPCL (535 ms, 80.0 DPG, 0.605 GenEval) on both latency and quality. Against the dynamic method DyDiT (423 ms, 80.3 DPG, 0.676 GenEval), E-DiT is faster by ~50 ms with comparable DPG but a marginal GenEval gap (0.671 vs. 0.676); the authors note the two approaches are complementary and could be combined. For 3D generation, E-DiT on Hunyuan3D-3.0 achieves a 1.94× speedup (2587 ms vs. 5012 ms) while slightly improving ULIP-I (0.1473 vs. 0.1446) and matching Uni3D-I (0.4332 vs. 0.4334), confirming that the adaptive framework generalizes beyond 2D image generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

E-DiT extends the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and Rectified Flow training objective (Liu et al., 2022) by equipping each transformer block with a lightweight router that jointly predicts block-skipping and MLP-width decisions. It departs from static pruning/distillation methods (TinyFusion, HP, PPCL) that apply a fixed reduced architecture uniformly, and from prior dynamic approaches (DyDiT, Dense2MoE) that require non-trivial architectural redesign or fixed activation patterns. A key conceptual departure is the observation that router gating probabilities naturally encode block importance, enabling a training-free block-wise caching mechanism that subsumes prior feature-caching heuristics (FORA, Cache Me If You Can) without requiring separate reuse criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

E-DiT provides a general, plug-and-play acceleration framework applicable across diverse DiT backbones and modalities (2D image, 3D asset), achieving ~2× speedup with minimal quality loss—a practically significant efficiency gain for deploying large generative models. The sample-dependent sparsity analysis (blocks, timesteps, and computational demand all vary with content) offers a useful empirical foundation for future adaptive inference research. Limitations include training instability on Qwen-Image and slight quality degradation on some metrics; the authors identify post-training and CFG-enhancement as promising directions to close this gap. Open questions include whether the router-based caching generalizes to video diffusion with longer temporal sequences and whether the framework composes cleanly with step-distillation methods.
