limitations: The method relies on a fixed geometric centrality prior (target near search center) and a hand-designed shrinkage-expansion schedule rather than a fully learned or per-frame adaptive routing mechanism, which may limit generalization to highly dynamic or multi-target scenarios. ## Problem

Existing ViT-based trackers achieve strong accuracy in satellite video tracking but incur prohibitive latency, while DCF-based methods run in real time yet lack discriminative power. The root cause is an architectural mismatch: generic transformers process all spatial tokens uniformly, ignoring the fact that satellite tracking targets are typically centered in the search window and surrounded by redundant background. This paper tackles the unresolved accuracy–speed dichotomy by redesigning token routing to be foreground-aware and spatially adaptive.

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes a 128×128 template crop and a 256×256 search crop from consecutive satellite video frames, both passed through a shared patch embedding to produce token sequences that are concatenated into a single stream. It outputs a 4-dimensional bounding box regression and a binary target-presence classification score per search location, produced by a lightweight three-branch fully convolutional head. The Base variant is initialized with MAE pretraining on natural images, while the Fast variant uses random initialization, and both are trained end-to-end on VISO and SV248S with 300 epochs of 60k sampled image pairs.

## Method

FATrack's core is FA-ViT, a 12-block U-Net-style ViT (6 encoder + 6 decoder layers) that implements foreground-aware token routing via a geometry-driven shrinkage-expansion schedule: the encoder progressively retains only the central r×r tokens (r decreasing from 16 to 4), cutting attention cost from O(N²d) to O((N_Z+r²)²d), while the decoder restores resolution using cross-stage skip connections from the encoder. To prevent semantic degradation from token sparsification, the Adaptive Scatter Module (ASM) applies joint spatial-channel sigmoid attention to enhance sparse tokens, then scatter-adds them into the expanded grid guided by spatial indices, ensuring structural continuity without redundant background. This centrality-guided pruning outperforms attention-based and random filtering strategies (Table 4), achieving 46.8 SR at 142 FPS with 17.3 G MACs—40% fewer FLOPs than the unfiltered OSTrack baseline—by exploiting the prior that satellite targets remain near the search center due to limited inter-frame motion.

## Results

On SV248S, FATrack-Base achieves the highest Success rate (0.468) and a Precision of 0.760 among all compared trackers, while FATrack-Fast reaches 0.440 Success at 242 FPS—matching the speed of CF-based trackers like ECO (240 FPS) and KCF (187 FPS). On SatSOT, FATrack-Base attains the second-highest performance (SR=0.470, PR=0.599), surpassing SVLPNet (0.465/0.584) and SeqTrack (0.427/0.512). On VISO, FATrack-Base achieves the best overall results with SR=0.286 and PR=0.676, outperforming all prior methods including DiMP (0.203/0.583) and SiamRPN++ (0.232/0.522). FATrack-Base runs at 142 FPS on a single RTX 2080Ti, substantially faster than most ViT-based trackers (e.g., TransT 63 FPS, OSTrack 105 FPS, SeqTrack 40 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FATrack-Base achieves the highest Success rate on SV248S (0.468), surpassing SVLPNet (0.460), SeqTrack (0.443), and SiamCAR (0.448), though it trails SVLPNet on Precision (0.760 vs. 0.783) and is marginally behind ATOM on ENUS (0.282 vs. 0.284). On SatSOT, FATrack-Base (0.470/0.599) outperforms all Transformer-based baselines including SVLPNet (0.465/0.584), SeqTrack (0.427/0.512), and ORTrack (0.429/0.534). The speed advantage is striking: FATrack-Fast runs at 242 FPS—faster than ECO (240 FPS) and KCF (187 FPS)—while maintaining substantially higher accuracy (0.440 Success vs. 0.410 for ECO). Compared to recent real-time ViT trackers SGLATrack (225 FPS) and ORTrack (226 FPS), FATrack-Fast is slightly faster and more accurate on SV248S (0.440 vs. 0.420/0.414 Success). On VISO, FATrack-Base (SR=0.286, PR=0.676) clearly dominates all baselines, with the nearest competitor SiamRPN++ at SR=0.232 and PR=0.522. The key trade-off is that FATrack-Base (142 FPS) is slower than FATrack-Fast (242 FPS) and the fastest CF methods, but delivers the best accuracy-speed balance overall, particularly excelling where both precision and real-time throughput are required.

## Relation to Prior Work

FATrack builds directly on the one-stream ViT tracking paradigm introduced by OSTrack (Ye et al., 2022), where template and search tokens are concatenated and processed jointly through a shared transformer backbone. It departs from this design by replacing uniform full-resolution token processing with a foreground-aware spatial shrinkage-expansion strategy, motivated by the observation that satellite tracking targets remain near the search region center due to motion prediction. The Adaptive Scatter Module is a novel architectural component that addresses semantic degradation from token sparsification, drawing conceptual inspiration from U-Net skip connections but operating in token-space rather than feature-map-space. The paper positions itself against both the DCF-based lineage (MOSSE, KCF, ECO) and the growing family of ViT-based trackers (TransT, OSTrack, SeqTrack, SVLPNet), arguing that neither extreme—handcrafted filters or full-resolution transformers—suits the computational and discriminative demands of real-time satellite video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FATrack offers a practically deployable solution for onboard satellite tracking, achieving DCF-level inference speed (142–242 FPS on a 2080Ti, 25–42 FPS on Jetson Xavier NX) while matching or exceeding the accuracy of heavier ViT-based trackers. The centrality-guided token routing strategy is simple and hardware-friendly, making it attractive for resource-constrained edge deployment in remote sensing. However, the approach relies on a fixed geometric prior (target near center) that may not generalize to highly dynamic or multi-target scenarios, and the evaluation is limited to three satellite-specific benchmarks without testing on general video tracking datasets. Open questions include whether the shrinkage-expansion schedule can be made fully adaptive (e.g., learned per-frame) and whether the ASM's scatter-add mechanism scales to much larger token grids needed for ultra-high-resolution imagery.
