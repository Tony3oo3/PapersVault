## Problem

Existing anchor-based multi-view clustering methods generate or select anchors from the original data and then learn consensus representations under fixed anchor constraints, treating anchor construction and clustering structure learning in a decoupled manner. This separation leaves clustering semantics in the sample space unexploited for calibrating the anchor space, resulting in redundant anchor coverage and ambiguous cluster boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264664/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes l heterogeneous feature views per sample (each with potentially different dimensionality d_v) and a specified number of clusters k, producing mk view-specific anchors and a consensus anchor representation Z ∈ R^{mk×n}. Final cluster assignments are obtained by applying k-means to Z, while the intermediate outputs include view-specific anchor matrices A^(v), orthogonal projection bases U^(v), data-space cluster centers H^(v), and soft assignment matrices P and R that encode anchor-to-cluster and sample-to-cluster relationships respectively.

## Method

DCMC introduces a dual-calibration mechanism that bidirectionally couples anchor representation learning with data clustering semantics. In the anchor space, a compact cross-view decomposition A^(v) = U^(v)P with orthogonal constraint U^(v)ᵀU^(v) = I enforces view-specific projection directions while sharing a common anchor-cluster assignment matrix P across views. In the data space, cluster centers H^(v) and soft assignments R are learned directly from the original features, and a reverse calibration term ‖Z − PᵀR‖² propagates clustering semantics back into the anchor representation Z. The overall objective combines data reconstruction, anchor alignment, data-space clustering, and cross-space consistency, optimized via alternating minimization with closed-form updates (SVD for orthogonal variables, matrix inversion for the rest). This joint optimization ensures anchors are calibrated not only by reconstruction error but also by clustering structure, yielding more compact and discriminative anchor representations.

## Results

DCMC achieves the best clustering accuracy on 4 out of 5 benchmark datasets: MSRC_v1 (ACC 0.9333, NMI 0.8851), BBCSport (ACC 0.9504, NMI 0.8579), Wiki (ACC 0.6200, NMI 0.5696), and MNIST (ACC 0.9901, NMI 0.9701). On YouTubeFace_sel, DCMC attains the highest NMI (0.2160) and Purity (0.3269) but trails FPMVS on ACC (0.2310 vs. 0.2414) and F-score (0.0952 vs. 0.1433). The method also demonstrates linear time and space complexity (O(n) in both), making it suitable for large-scale multi-view data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCMC outperforms all six baselines on MSRC_v1, BBCSport, and Wiki across all four metrics, with the largest margins over LMVSC (e.g., ACC 0.9333 vs. 0.3476 on MSRC_v1) and OMSC (ACC 0.9504 vs. 0.4522 on BBCSport). Against the strongest dynamic-anchor baselines, DCMC beats CAMVC by 9.2 ACC points on MSRC_v1 (0.9333 vs. 0.8418) and 5.1 points on BBCSport (0.9504 vs. 0.8997), and surpasses RCAGL by 11.2 ACC points on MSRC_v1 (0.9333 vs. 0.8023). On MNIST, FSMSC edges out DCMC on ACC (0.9954 vs. 0.9901) and NMI (0.9768 vs. 0.9701), while DCMC leads on Purity and F-score. On YouTubeFace_sel, FPMVS achieves higher ACC (0.2414 vs. 0.2310) and F-score (0.1433 vs. 0.0952), though DCMC attains the best NMI (0.2160) and near-best Purity (0.3269 vs. 0.3279). EOMSC is the strongest baseline on Wiki (ACC 0.5415) but is still surpassed by DCMC by 7.9 ACC points.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCMC sits within the anchor graph-based multi-view clustering lineage, which reduces sample-sample complexity from O(n²) to O(mn) by introducing representative anchors. It departs from prior anchor methods (LMVSC, RCAGL, CAMVC, etc.) that treat anchor selection/learning and cluster assignment as largely decoupled steps—typically generating anchors from raw data first and then optimizing a consensus representation under fixed anchor constraints. The key conceptual shift is a bidirectional 'dual-calibration' loop: anchor representations are calibrated by data-space clustering semantics (via H^(v) and R), while data-space assignments are in turn refined by anchor-space structure (via P^T R), closing a feedback loop that prior methods leave open. The orthogonal projection decomposition of anchors into view-specific bases U^(v) and a shared cluster indicator P draws on the orthogonal-constraint tradition (OMSC, RCAGL) but applies it jointly with the data-clustering objective rather than as a standalone regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-calibration mechanism offers a principled way to reduce redundant anchor coverage and sharpen cluster boundaries, which are practical pain points in large-scale multi-view clustering where anchor quality directly governs downstream k-means performance. However, the gains are dataset-dependent: DCMC clearly wins on MSRC_v1, BBCSport, and Wiki, but trails FSMSC on MNIST and does not dominate on YouTubeFace_sel, where it even underperforms its own ablated variants on ACC—suggesting the calibration loop may be less effective on very high-dimensional, fine-grained face data. Open questions include whether the alternating SVD-based updates can be replaced by differentiable deep modules, whether the approach extends to incomplete or streaming multi-view settings, and whether the two hyperparameters (λ₁, λ₂) can be learned automatically rather than tuned per dataset.
