## Problem

Existing In-Context Imitation Learning (ICIL) frameworks for VLA models suffer from an adaptation bottleneck: retrieval mechanisms prioritize superficial visual similarity over functional behavioral intent, policies exhibit behavioral inertia that anchors them to pre-trained priors rather than the provided expert context, and inference latency scales poorly as the number of retrieved segments grows. These three failures collectively prevent effective training-free adaptation to novel manipulation tasks.

## Input / Output

The policy receives dual-view RGB images (static third-person and wrist-mounted cameras), a language instruction, and the robot's proprioceptive state, augmented by K retrieved expert segments (default K=1, up to 8) that are pre-encoded offline and cached as key-value pairs in a GPU buffer. Expert demonstrations are sliced into functional segments via a sliding window of length h=16 with stride s=4, and the retrieval encoder operates on raw multimodal tokens (image embeddings + text token embeddings) average-pooled into a 512-dim key vector. The output is an action chunk of shape 16×c (end-effector poses and gripper states) generated through 4 flow-matching denoising steps, with the initial noisy chunk initialized to the empirical mean of retrieved expert actions rather than a standard normal.

## Method

RA-VLA couples two training objectives with a scalable inference architecture. First, a behavioral alignment loss trains a lightweight 2-layer Transformer retrieval encoder via contrastive learning, where positive pairs are derived from DTW alignment of action sequences across same-task demonstrations, ensuring retrieval captures behavioral correspondence rather than visual similarity. Second, a contextual adherence loss enforces a regression margin (L_irrel − L_rel > m) between predictions conditioned on relevant vs. randomly sampled expert segments, explicitly penalizing the policy for ignoring retrieved context and breaking behavioral inertia. At inference, a pairwise grounding strategy assigns each cross-attention layer a single expert segment (most relevant in the highest layers), decoupling latency from the number of retrieved segments and keeping it near-constant regardless of K.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark (entirely unseen task suite, 3 expert demos per task), RA-VLA achieves a 38.45% average success rate, improving over the best baseline RICL_R (20.85%) by 17.60 percentage points. On the real-world UR5e environment (4 expert demos per held-out task), RA-VLA reaches 56.25% average success rate versus 35.42% for RICL_R, a 20.83-point gain. Inference latency remains near-constant regardless of the number of retrieved segments K, unlike baselines whose latency scales steeply with K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RA-VLA outperforms both ICIL baselines across all evaluated environments. On LIBERO, RAEA (Zhu et al., 2024) with the action-aware retriever reaches 12.35% average success rate, while RICL_R (Sridhar et al., 2025) reaches 20.85%; RA-VLA achieves 38.45%, a 17.6-point improvement over the strongest baseline. On the real-world UR5e, RAEA scores 12.50% and RICL_R scores 35.42%, compared to RA-VLA's 56.25%. The gap is most pronounced on LIBERO-Object (55.6% vs. 32.6% for RICL_R) and the UR5e Throw Trash task (75.0% vs. 25.0% for RICL_R), where naive action interpolation fails to achieve fine-grained transport precision. Swapping the off-the-shelf SigLIP 2 retriever for the proposed action-aware retriever alone lifts RICL_R from 4.65% to 20.85% on LIBERO, confirming that retrieval quality is a major bottleneck, but the full RA-VLA pipeline (retrieval + contextual adherence loss) is needed to close the remaining gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GR00T N1.5 flow-matching VLA architecture and positions itself as a fix for the 'adaptation bottleneck' identified in existing In-Context Imitation Learning (ICIL) frameworks like RAEA and RICL. It departs from the concatenation-based paradigm (where expert segments are appended to the multimodal prompt, causing linear latency growth) by introducing independent segment encoding with pairwise cross-attention grounding. The two novel losses—behavioral alignment (DTW-guided contrastive training of the retriever) and contextual adherence (margin-based regression gap between relevant and irrelevant context)—address the two failure modes the authors diagnose: superficial visual retrieval and behavioral inertia anchoring the policy to pre-trained priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training-free adaptation via a demonstration buffer is practically important for deployment scenarios where retraining is infeasible, and the near-constant inference latency (independent of the number of retrieved segments) removes a key scalability barrier that has limited prior ICIL methods. However, absolute success rates remain modest (38.5% on LIBERO, 56.3% on UR5e), performance is sensitive to hyperparameters and buffer quality, and the framework's reliance on a curated demonstration buffer introduces an adversarial vulnerability. Open questions include whether the independent-encoding strategy holds at larger training scales, whether it extends to cross-embodiment settings, and whether unstructured human video can substitute for curated expert demonstrations.
