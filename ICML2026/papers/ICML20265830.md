## Problem

Existing VLA benchmarks use static, single-complexity task designs that cannot isolate how model performance degrades as specific challenges are amplified, and they either focus on robustness or extrapolation in isolation while overlooking safety constraints. This makes it impossible to precisely characterize a model's capability frontier or distinguish robust grounding from memorization of training patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265830/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

VLA models receive 256×256 RGB observations (third-person and wrist camera views) paired with natural-language instructions, and output continuous or discretized robot action sequences executed at 20 Hz in MuJoCo/robosuite simulation. Evaluation produces two metrics per task: success rate (SR, averaged over 30 episodes) and cumulative cost (CC), where CC sums instantaneous safety predicate violations across the trajectory plus a 10×-scaled terminal hazard penalty. Training data is provided as human-collected trajectories organized by difficulty level (L0/L1) and scale (S/M/L = 10/30/50 trajectories per task).

## Method

VLA-Arena introduces a structured task design that quantifies difficulty across three orthogonal axes: (1) Task Structure (L0–L2) comprising 170 tasks in 11 suites grouped into Safety, Distractor, Extrapolation, and Long Horizon dimensions, where fine-tuning is restricted to L0 to rigorously test generalization; (2) Language Command (W0–W4) applying graded WordNet-based semantic substitutions (synonyms, hypernyms, hyponyms at shortest path length 1) to probe whether models perform true semantic reasoning or keyword matching; and (3) Visual Observation (V0–V4) applying a cumulative hierarchy of perturbations (lighting → object color → camera pose → Gaussian noise) to expose overfitting to specific pixel distributions. Tasks are formally specified in CBDDL, an extension of BDDL with native safety predicates (10 types classified as instantaneous or terminal checks) that enable the CC metric as an objective safety measure independent of task success. The framework provides an end-to-end open-source toolchain for declarative scene building, data collection, fine-tuning, and automated evaluation, validated on a physical Franka Research 3 robot.

## Results

Across 170 tasks in four dimensions (Safety, Distractor, Extrapolation, Long Horizon), all six evaluated VLAs show catastrophic performance degradation from L0 (in-distribution) to L2 (far-distribution). π₀ leads on L0 (e.g., 0.97 SR on StaticObstacles, 0.93 on LongHorizon), but the gap narrows sharply at L1/L2 where all models collapse. Most strikingly, LongHorizon and PrepositionCombination drop to near 0% SR at L1+ for every model, confirming language-conditioned skill composition as a universal bottleneck. The visual grounding gap is quantified: Qwen3-VL-8B retains 93.3% grounding accuracy at V4 (noise), while VLA policies suffer a 50.5% performance drop at the same level, revealing catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

π₀ (flow-matching) consistently achieves the highest L0 success rates across most suites (e.g., 0.97 StaticObstacles, 0.91 DynamicObstacles, 0.93 LongHorizon), but its advantage shrinks dramatically at L1/L2 where all models converge toward failure. On LongHorizon and PrepositionCombination, every model—including the latest architectures (ACoT-VLA, DM0.5, MolmoAct2, GR00T-N1.7) evaluated in Tables 7–9—drops to near 0% at L1+, confirming that language-driven skill composition remains an unsolved universal bottleneck regardless of architecture. π₀-FAST is anomalously weak on Extrapolation (0.09 SR on PrepositionCombination L0), suggesting its FAST tokenizer may impair semantic grounding. SmolVLA, the lightweight model, shows the worst overall SR but the lowest cumulative cost (CC) on safety tasks, revealing a conservative-but-ineffective safety strategy. On DynamicDistractors, π₀ retains 0.72 SR at L1 while OpenVLA-OFT and UniVLA sustain >0.5, but all collapse by L2. The safety-performance trade-off is stark: UniVLA achieves 0.58 SR on StatePreservation L2 but incurs CC=14.6, while π₀ achieves only 0.0 SR on CautiousGrasp L2 with CC=0.6—models either violate safety to succeed or fail entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLA-Arena extends the BDDL task specification language (Srivastava et al., 2022) into CBDDL by adding native safety predicates, dynamic object definitions, and perturbation controls, while inheriting the MuJoCo/robosuite simulation backbone shared with LIBERO. It departs from prior robotics benchmarks (CALVIN, LIBERO, VLABench, LIBERO-Plus/PRO) by replacing flat, single-difficulty task collections with a structured design that quantifies difficulty across three orthogonal axes—task structure (L0–L2), language command (W0–W4), and visual observation (V0–V4)—enabling fine-grained diagnostic probing rather than monolithic success/failure scoring. The safety dimension, formalized via a cumulative cost metric over 10 predicate types, has no direct counterpart in existing manipulation benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes three deployment-critical gaps in current VLAs—memorization over compositional generalization, catastrophic forgetting of visual representations during fine-tuning (a VLM retains 93% grounding accuracy where VLA policies drop 50%), and a systematic safety-performance trade-off—that collectively define the distance between simulation success and real-world deployability. The open-source toolchain and curated datasets lower the barrier for reproducible VLA evaluation, while the real-robot validation on Franka Research 3 confirms that simulated failure modes transfer to physical deployment. Key open questions include whether safety constraints can be integrated into VLA training without degrading task performance, and whether the catastrophic forgetting of visual concepts is an artifact of fine-tuning methodology or inherent to the VLA paradigm.
