## Problem

Code LLMs excel at generating functionally correct GPU kernels but lack the hardware-specific intuition to navigate the vast, discontinuous optimization space toward performance-optimal solutions. Existing agent-based methods perform undirected exploration, frequently converging to suboptimal local minima or wasting budget on invalid configurations. No prior work has formulated kernel optimization as a sequential decision problem with principled exploration-exploitation trade-offs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a naive Triton kernel, a target GPU (RTX 4090, H20, or A100), and an iteration budget T (default 20, extendable to 40). At each iteration, the LLM backend (DeepSeek-V3.2, GPT-5, Claude Opus 4.5, or Gemini 3 Flash) generates a new kernel variant by applying one of six structured optimization strategies (tiling, vectorization, fusion, pipeline, reordering, access & layout) to a selected candidate. The output is the single fastest correct kernel from the accumulated candidate pool, verified via two-stage correctness checking (runtime errors + torch.allclose numerical equivalence) and benchmarked across 10+ input shapes.

## Method

KERNELBAND formulates kernel optimization as a contextual multi-armed bandit with an expanding action space, where each arm corresponds to applying an optimization strategy to a kernel cluster. Two key mechanisms make the infinite arm space tractable: (1) hardware-aware pruning uses Nsight Compute profiling to extract a hardware signature h(k) (SM/DRAM/L2 throughput percentages) and masks strategies targeting already-saturated resources (>75% threshold), reducing the effective action space; (2) runtime-behavior clustering groups kernels via K-Means on a 5D behavioral feature vector φ(k) (normalized time, registers, shared memory, block dimension, occupancy), exploiting Lipschitz continuity so that kernels with similar bottlenecks share strategy-reward estimates. The policy selects (cluster, strategy) pairs via a Masked UCB index, then samples a specific kernel within the cluster using a softmax over remaining hardware headroom. A theoretical regret bound (Theorem 1) shows convergence to ε-optimality where the bound depends on the compact covering number of runtime clusters rather than the full kernel space, with empirical validation confirming the Lipschitz assumption (Spearman ρ=0.64 between φ-distance and reward gap).

## Results

On TritonBench-G (183 kernels, 13 categories, 5 difficulty levels), KERNELBAND achieves up to 1.91× geometric mean speedup on A100 (vs. 1.34× for GEAK and 0.98× for BoN), 1.74× on RTX 4090, and 1.45× on H20. Fast@1 success rate (fraction of tasks with >1.0× speedup) reaches 43.3%–60.1% across platforms, a 39–140% relative improvement over GEAK (23.8%–38.7%). Correctness (at least one valid kernel) is 77.8%–79.8% for KERNELBAND versus 48.0%–55.6% for GEAK and 29.3%–31.2% for BoN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GEAK (the strongest agent-based baseline using iterative Reflexion-style refinement), KERNELBAND wins on every metric across all three GPUs: on A100 it delivers 1.91× vs. 1.34× geometric mean speedup (+42.5%) and 60.1% vs. 38.7% Fast@1 (+55% relative); on H20 the gap is even larger at 1.45× vs. 1.06× (+37%). Against Best-of-N (independent sampling without iteration), KERNELBAND achieves 1.74×–1.91× speedup versus 0.96×–0.99× (i.e., BoN often fails to beat the reference kernel at all). Against PyTorch's built-in compilation paths on a 30-kernel native-operator subset (H20), KERNELBAND-optimized Triton kernels exceed eager execution by 2.13×, inductor by 1.87×, and max-autotune by 2.16×, confirming that LLM-driven source-level optimization surpasses what compiler autotuning alone achieves. The advantage is most pronounced on harder kernels (L4–5): on A100, KERNELBAND reaches 1.75× speedup with 67.1% correctness versus GEAK's 1.54× and 38.6%, while BoN fails to produce valid kernels for 85% of those tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of multi-armed bandit theory and LLM-based kernel optimization. Prior agent-based methods (GEAK, STARK, CudaForge) rely on iterative refinement or self-reflection heuristics without a principled exploration-exploitation mechanism, while training-based approaches (ConCuR, Kevin, TritonRL) fine-tune models on optimization datasets. KERNELBAND repurposes the contextual bandit framework—specifically masked UCB with Lipschitz-structured clustering—from the broader MAB literature (metric-space bandits, online clustering of bandits) and applies it to the kernel optimization domain for the first time, using Roofline-style hardware profiling to prune the action space and runtime-behavior clustering to manage the expanding candidate pool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GPU kernel optimization remains a manual bottleneck for LLM serving infrastructure, and this work provides a principled, hardware-aware automation that consistently outperforms unguided LLM agents across diverse architectures and model backends. The ablation showing that LLM semantic reasoning for strategy selection (0.97×) underperforms even the reference kernel is a useful caution against assuming LLMs possess reliable optimization intuition. Open questions include whether the static-cluster regret bound extends cleanly to the dynamic re-clustering procedure, how the framework generalizes to less-documented DSLs beyond Triton, and whether the Lipschitz assumption holds for discrete algorithmic rewrites (e.g., reduction to parallel scan) where behavior-space continuity breaks down.
