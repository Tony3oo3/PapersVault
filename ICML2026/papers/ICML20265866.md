## Problem

Cross multi-domain log anomaly detection requires a single model to serve multiple heterogeneous systems, but existing methods face two fundamental gaps: geometric proximity alone cannot certify normality (boundary samples between normal clusters are misclassified), and aggressive domain alignment distorts domain-specific manifold structures, causing negative transfer. No prior approach simultaneously addresses both the geometric blind-spot problem and the tension between knowledge sharing and domain preservation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265866/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a temporal log sequence of shape T×F (generated from 384-dim Sentence-BERT embeddings via sliding windows W=20, S=2) along with a discrete domain identifier d, and produces a calibrated anomaly score s_cal(x) = (s(x) − μ_d)/σ_d. The raw score combines two terms: the Euclidean distance to the nearest prototype (structural evidence) and the flow matching error evaluated at a fixed midpoint t*=0.5 (dynamical evidence), weighted by a hyperparameter γ. Per-domain z-score normalization from validation data enables a single unified threshold across all domains.

## Method

MPFM reframes anomaly detection as testing consistency with learned generative dynamics rather than relying solely on geometric distance. A 2-layer Transformer encoder maps input sequences to representations, which are assigned to a shared-private prototype set (32 shared prototypes capturing cross-domain commonalities, 16 private prototypes per domain preserving domain-specific patterns, with domain embeddings injected into private prototypes). A domain-conditioned velocity field v_θ(t, x_t, d) is trained via flow matching to transport Gaussian noise toward the prototype-reconstructed target, providing dynamical evidence that geometrically close but dynamically inconsistent samples are anomalous. A prototype-drift-driven hard example mining strategy synthesizes virtual candidates along inter-epoch prototype movement directions and selects the top-K by flow error, adaptively reinforcing decision boundaries. Training jointly optimizes flow matching, reconstruction, InfoNCE contrastive, orthogonal regularization, and hard-flow losses, with the drift mechanism creating an implicit curriculum that shifts as prototypes evolve.

## Results

Under the cross multi-domain joint training protocol (one unified model trained on pooled data from all four datasets), MPFM achieves an average F1 of 0.960, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline LogDLR (0.832) by 12.8 percentage points. Per-dataset F1 scores are 0.987 (HDFS), 0.959 (BGL), 0.915 (Thunderbird), and 0.977 (Spirit). In the single-domain reference comparison, MPFM matches or exceeds LogBERT (0.935 avg F1) and remains competitive with LogDLR (0.965 avg F1), though LogDLR retains a slight edge on BGL and Thunderbird when each model is trained independently per domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the fair joint-training protocol (Table 2), MPFM dominates all baselines by a wide margin: it outperforms LogDLR (the strongest cross-domain baseline) by 12.8 pp in average F1 (0.960 vs. 0.832), LogTAD by 15.0 pp, LogAnomaly by 23.3 pp, and LogTransfer by 37.3 pp. The gap is most pronounced on HDFS (+13.5 pp over LogDLR) and Thunderbird (+13.6 pp), where domain-invariant alignment in LogDLR suppresses domain-specific patterns. In the single-domain reference comparison (Table 1), MPFM still beats LogBERT (0.935) and LogAnomaly (0.888) on average F1, but LogDLR (0.965) retains a small edge over MPFM (0.960) on BGL (0.982 vs. 0.959) and Thunderbird (0.948 vs. 0.915), indicating that per-domain specialization still has value for some datasets. DeepLog (0.869 avg F1) is the weakest single-domain baseline and is not included in the joint-training comparison. The paper's key claim is validated specifically in the multi-domain joint setting, where preserving domain-specific structure via private prototypes and domain-conditioned flow matching is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

MPFM sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: prototypical networks (Snell et al., 2017), which provide the structural anchor mechanism, and flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023), which supplies the generative dynamics. It departs from prior cross-domain log anomaly detection methods (LogDLR, LogTransfer, LogTAD) that rely on adversarial domain alignment or source-to-target transfer, instead adopting a many-to-many joint training paradigm with a shared-private prototype split to avoid negative transfer. The key conceptual departure is reframing anomaly detection from a purely geometric problem (distance to prototypes or reconstruction error) into a dual-evidence problem that also requires dynamical consistency with a learned velocity field, a perspective not present in prior log anomaly detection literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical scalability bottleneck: as the number of monitored systems grows, per-system models become infeasible, and the cross multi-domain joint training setting is where MPFM's advantage is clearest (Table 2 shows a ~13-point F1 gap over the best joint-training baseline). However, the ODE-based inference introduces a significant latency cost (673 ms vs. 21 ms for LogBERT at 20 steps), and the paper honestly reports that MPFM does not uniformly surpass single-domain LogDLR on every dataset. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond four domains, whether online/continual learning can replace the drift-based mining, and whether the flow-matching component can be distilled into a single-step predictor to close the efficiency gap.
