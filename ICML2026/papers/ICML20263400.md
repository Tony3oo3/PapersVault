## Problem

Action-value learning in Q-learning is slow because true Q-values often have high Euclidean norm while estimates start near zero, requiring many incremental updates to converge. Additionally, action-values within a state are typically correlated, yet standard methods update each state-action pair independently without sharing a common state-dependent baseline. Existing value-sharing approaches like Dueling DQN address this but require an explicit baseline parameter and an extra multi-layer output stream, adding substantial learnable parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard state representations (e.g., 4 stacked 84×84 grayscale frames in Atari) and produces an n-dimensional vector of action-values, one per available action. The key structural detail is that the network's penultimate layer outputs a residual vector z ∈ ℝⁿ, and the parameter-free ME layer transforms it into the final Q-value vector q = M_k z. The layer preserves relative differences between actions while scaling the shared mean component by a factor of k+1, so the output is a valid Q-vector but with a lower-norm internal representation.

## Method

The paper introduces the mean-expansion layer (ME layer), a parameter-free, invertible linear transformation M_k = I + (k/n)J (where J is the n×n all-ones matrix) appended as the final layer of a Q-network. The network learns a residual vector z, and the ME layer implicitly constructs a shared baseline b = k·μ_z that is added uniformly to all actions, so q_i = z_i + (k/n)Σ_j z_j. This transforms the optimization from learning potentially large action-values to learning lower-norm residuals, with the mean component of z scaled by k+1 in the output. For tabular Q-learning, this yields Implicit-Baseline Q-learning (IBQ), where a positive TD error increments the chosen action's residual by (1+k/n)αδ and all other actions' residuals by (k/n)αδ, distributing credit across actions. The coefficient k is interpretable: k=n recovers the norm-minimizing baseline b*, while k=1 gives a conservative baseline μ_q/2 with condition number 2 for numerical stability in large action spaces.

## Results

Across 57 Atari 2600 games, IB-DQN(n) achieves a higher interquartile mean (IQM) of human-normalized score than DQN, Dueling DQN, and RDQ, reaching DQN's best performance at just under 20M timesteps (within 40% of the 50M total training budget). IB-IQN(n) similarly outperforms IQN, reaching IQN's peak score at 33.75M timesteps (≈2/3 of training). IB-DQN(n) reduces value overestimation in all 57 games and increases the relative action gap in the vast majority of games. In the tabular gridworld, IBQ(k) completes over 20% more episodes than standard Q-learning within 1k timesteps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IB-DQN(n) clearly outperforms standard DQN in aggregate IQM of human-normalized score across all 57 games, achieving DQN's best score at ~20M timesteps (40% of training) versus DQN's full 50M budget. Against Dueling DQN, IB-DQN(n) also wins in aggregate; notably, Dueling DQN does not outperform plain DQN in this modern Adam + squared-error-loss setup, contrary to the original Wang et al. (2016) findings. RDQ (β=0.001) outperforms IB-DQN(n) in aggregate, which the authors attribute to RDQ's extra parameter stream and augmented loss function, but IB-DQN(n) remains competitive while being a parameter-free architectural addition. For distributional RL, IB-IQN(n) outperforms IQN, reaching IQN's peak score at 33.75M timesteps (≈2/3 of training) and showing faster initial learning. In the tabular gridworld, IBQ(k) completes over 20% more episodes than Q-learning within 1k timesteps, with the gap narrowing as both algorithms master the task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the baseline-residual decomposition implicit in Dueling DQN (Wang et al., 2016) and its regularized variant RDQ (Daley et al., 2025), both of which explicitly learn a state-dependent baseline through a separate parameter stream. The key departure is the Mean-Expansion Layer, a parameter-free linear transformation that achieves the same value-sharing by implicitly constructing the baseline from the mean of the residual vector, eliminating the need for extra architecture. Conceptually, it also connects to Advantage Updating (Baird, 1993) and Advantage Learning (Baird, 1999), which decompose value functions into shared state-values and action-specific advantages, though those methods emphasize stability over acceleration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ME layer offers a simple, drop-in architectural modification that improves sample efficiency, reduces overestimation in all 57 Atari games, and increases action gaps without adding parameters or altering the training algorithm—making it broadly applicable to any Q-network. The finding that Dueling DQN fails to outperform standard DQN under modern training (Adam + squared-error loss) challenges the assumed necessity of explicit baseline streams. Open questions include the impact on convergence guarantees, extension to continuous action spaces, and a mechanistic explanation for why the implicit baseline reduces overestimation; the authors also note a conceptual-practical discrepancy where the layer operates on full vectors but receives sample-based updates to individual state-action pairs.
