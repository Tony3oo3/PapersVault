## Problem

Existing data marketplaces price raw datasets rather than the utility they deliver through trained ML models, lack transparency in price determination, and ignore the opportunity to augment buyer data with external sources. This paper asks: how should a data-augmented AutoML platform price the models it produces so that pricing aligns buyer incentives, accommodates diverse willingness-to-pay, and maximizes overall market revenue?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261461/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The platform receives a buyer's training dataset and validation set, then searches a large external data pool (69K datasets, ~30M columns, 3B rows from NYC Open Data) for augmentations and models. It outputs a pre-posted price curve mapping each discrete model quality level to a per-inference price, a real-time trajectory of discovered model qualities (revealing all, not just the best, to form a menu), and the buyer's optimal stopping policy. The buyer's stopping decision is modeled as a Pandora's Box variant where successive payoffs are Markov-dependent rather than independent, and the price curve is a vector over the finite quality set Q rather than a single scalar.

## Method

The core mechanism models model-quality evolution as a time-dependent Markov chain and formulates the buyer's search as an optimal stopping problem solvable by dynamic programming in O(Q²T). The platform's pricing problem is cast as a bi-level optimization: the outer level maximizes expected revenue over a price curve x(q), while the inner level captures each buyer type's optimal stopping response, and is solved as a Mixed Integer Linear Program with PAC-style approximation guarantees (additive ε-approximation with probability 1−2δ using O(b²ln(1/δ)/ε²) trajectory samples). To relax the assumption of known buyer-type priors, a Bayesian learning algorithm iteratively updates the prior from observed stopping times, with a proven Cost of Estimation Error bound of O(ε) when the learned prior and transition matrices are ε-accurate. For the data-model discovery engine, the paper combines Metam's clustering-based augmentation search with an Exp3 adversarial bandit over model architectures, reducing model trainings from O(|M|·log|P|) to O(log|P|) per iteration.

## Results

On 1,000 classification and regression tasks over 69K NYC Open Data datasets, the proposed DATA-BANDIT discovery engine achieves average buyer utility above 0.85 for classification, compared to ~0.7 for the second-best baseline (DATA-ALL) and ~0.55 for AutoML-only (TPOT). For pricing, the MILP-based price curve captures approximately 85% of total welfare in-sample (IS), outperforming all heuristic baselines (independent, shift, and jiggle pricing, all below 80%). In the more challenging out-of-sample (OOS) setting, the MILP curve still outperforms all baselines, trailing only the oracle optimal pricing scheme. The Bayesian learning of buyer priors converges to KL divergence below 0.025 within 10,000 rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three discovery baselines: DATA-ALL (Metam-based augmentation search training all models each iteration), DATA-ALT (fixed random forest for augmentation testing plus TPOT AutoML), and AUTOML (TPOT model search without data augmentation). DATA-BANDIT (the proposed Exp3-based bandit approach) consistently outperforms all three, achieving classification utility above 0.85 versus ~0.70 for DATA-ALL and ~0.55 for AUTOML, while also converging faster (O(log|P|) iterations vs. O(|M|×log|P|)). For pricing, the MILP formulation outperforms three heuristic baselines—independent pricing, shift pricing, and jiggle pricing—capturing ~85% of total welfare in-sample versus below 80% for all heuristics. In the out-of-sample robustness test, the MILP curve remains the best non-oracle method, demonstrating that the bi-level optimization generalizes better than simpler per-quality pricing strategies. The bandit-based discovery also identifies fewer than 10 augmentations for most tasks, keeping search time and buyer waiting costs low.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contract design and data market pricing literature (Myerson, Hart, Agarwal et al., Chen et al.) by shifting the pricing object from raw datasets or versioned models built over all market data to the *instrumental value*—the marginal model quality improvement—of externally sourced data in a data-augmented AutoML setting. It builds directly on Metam (Galhotra et al., 2023) for the data discovery component, generalizing it with a bandit-based (Exp3) joint augmentation-model selection that reduces training cost from O(|M|·log|P|) to O(log|P|). The optimal stopping formulation departs from the classical Pandora's Box problem by introducing Markov-chain dependence between successive quality observations, and the pricing curve is computed via a MILP with PAC-style sample complexity guarantees rather than closed-form contract solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, API-compatible market design that could be layered onto existing AutoML platforms (Vertex AI, SageMaker) to monetize external data based on demonstrated utility rather than compute cost or raw data volume, addressing a real gap in how data value is currently captured. Key limitations include reliance on simulated buyer behavior rather than actual user studies, the assumption that discovery cost is negligible relative to model value, and the absence of a revenue-allocation mechanism among data sellers. Open questions include whether the Markov-chain model of quality evolution holds for large-scale deep learning, how the pricing mechanism performs under adversarial or highly heterogeneous buyer populations, and whether the framework scales to multi-modal or foundation-model workloads.
