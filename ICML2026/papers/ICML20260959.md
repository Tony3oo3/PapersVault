## Problem

LLM chain-of-thought reasoning operates as a token-level autoregressive process that makes locally greedy decisions without global planning, often producing redundant, drifting, or inconsistent reasoning chains. Existing remedies—tree-based search and reflection-augmented RL—either incur prohibitive computational overhead or fail to provide a reliable mechanism for recovering from early errors. Crucially, prior plan-action approaches treat the plan as a structural component but never formulate plan quality as an explicit reward signal for policy optimization, leaving the model unable to learn to produce better plans.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question (or multimodal input with image) and produces a single structured response containing three tagged components: a concise high-level analytic plan in <plan> tags (capturing subgoal decomposition and execution order without concrete calculations), a detailed chain-of-thought reasoning trace in <think> tags conditioned on both the question and the plan, and a final answer in <answer> tags. The plan is generated in the same forward pass as the reasoning, avoiding multi-turn interaction, and the entire output is optimized jointly under RL.

## Method

PTA-GRPO is a two-stage training framework. In the cold-start SFT stage, a strong LLM (e.g., Qwen3-235B) summarizes ground-truth CoT traces from the OpenThoughts dataset into compact analytic plans, creating (question, plan, CoT+answer) triples that initialize explicit planning ability in the base model. In the RL stage, the method extends GRPO with a composite reward: an analytic plan reward computed by sampling m candidate plans and z CoT trajectories per plan, then scoring each plan by the softmax-normalized empirical accuracy of its resampled outcomes; a binary outcome reward for final-answer correctness; and a format reward enforcing structural compliance and conciseness. The key mechanism is the dual-use sampling design—the CoT trajectories used to evaluate plan quality are simultaneously incorporated into the policy update group, so no extra rollout budget is needed. Theoretically, the paper proves that optimizing the analytic plan reward increases the mutual information I(ŷ; y | t, q), which tightens the upper bound on error probability, and empirically validates this via HSIC-based mutual information estimation.

## Results

PTA-GRPO achieves the best average accuracy across all four base models on the four math benchmarks: 58.46 on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (vs. 54.81 for GRPO and 55.04 for DAPO), 40.37 on LLaMA3.2-3B (vs. 36.63 for GRPO), 79.10 on Qwen3-8B (vs. 78.59 for GRPO), and 85.01 on Qwen3-14B (vs. 83.31 for DAPO). On the multimodal front, PTA-GRPO with Qwen2.5-7B-VL reaches 44.7 on MMMU-Pro, 59.0 on MMMU, and 31.9 on EMMA, surpassing both the base model and the SRPO baseline on every metric. The largest absolute gains appear on harder benchmarks (AIME24/25) and for smaller models, where PTA-GRPO improves over the raw base by over 20 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTA-GRPO outperforms all six baselines (GRPO, DAPO, CPL, Full-Step-DPO, ORZ, and SRPO) on every model–benchmark combination tested. The advantage is most pronounced for smaller models: on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, PTA-GRPO leads GRPO by +3.65 average points (58.46 vs. 54.81) and DAPO by +3.42, while on LLaMA3.2-3B it gains +3.74 over GRPO and +3.39 over DAPO. For the stronger Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B backbones, the margins narrow to +0.51 over GRPO and +1.70 over DAPO respectively, consistent with diminishing returns as base-model capacity increases. On the multimodal evaluation (Table 5), PTA-GRPO with Qwen2.5-7B-VL beats SRPO on all six sub-benchmarks (e.g., MMMU-Pro 44.7 vs. 42.3, EMMA 31.9 vs. 29.6, Chem 68.7 vs. 65.2), confirming that the plan-guided reward generalizes beyond pure mathematics. Statistical significance tests (Appendix A.6) confirm that 13 of 16 model–task pairs show p < 0.05, with the largest effect sizes on AIME24/25 for the smaller models.

## Relation to Prior Work

PTA-GRPO extends the GRPO/RLVR line of work (Shao et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025) by introducing an analytic-plan reward that evaluates intermediate planning quality, rather than relying solely on sparse outcome-based supervision. It adopts the two-stage plan-then-CoT sampling strategy from ARPO (Lu et al., 2025) but departs from prior plan-and-solve prompting approaches (Yao et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023) by treating the plan as an explicit optimization variable with its own differentiable reward signal, rather than a fixed structural prompt. The SFT cold-start stage draws on the observation that base models lack explicit planning ability, and the theoretical analysis connects plan quality to mutual information bounds on error probability, building on information-theoretic reasoning frameworks (Qian et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized limitation of autoregressive CoT generation—local myopia without global planning—through a practical two-stage framework that adds minimal compute overhead over standard GRPO. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) linking plan reward to mutual information provides a principled justification for why optimizing plan quality should improve reasoning, which is absent in most RLVR methods. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to domains where verifiable rewards are unavailable (e.g., open-ended generation), whether the plan reward can be made more direct rather than relying on empirical accuracy of resampled CoTs, and whether the gains persist as base models themselves become stronger planners. The consistent but modest improvements over GRPO (typically 2–5 points) suggest the method is a useful refinement rather than a paradigm shift in the RLVR landscape.
