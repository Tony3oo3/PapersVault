## Problem

Existing SRT denoising methods suffer from three limitations: (1) spot graph construction via histological images introduces spurious similarity biases and is inapplicable when images are unavailable; (2) gene graph construction relying solely on Pearson co-expression misses nonlinear biological relationships such as pathway sharing and structural domain sharing; and (3) matrix-wise weighting for fusing spot and gene denoised matrices enforces a single global weight, preventing element-wise adaptive denoising.

## Input / Output

The method takes an SRT expression matrix B ∈ R^{M×N}, spot coordinates C ∈ R^{M×2}, and NCBI gene text descriptions as input. Spot features are derived by concatenating the names of the top-k_e (default 50) most highly expressed non-housekeeping genes per spot into a 'spot sentence' fed to the frozen OmiCLIP text encoder, while gene features come from the frozen GenePT encoder applied to each gene's NCBI description. The output is a denoised expression matrix B̂ ∈ R^{M×N} of the same dimensions, produced without any fine-tuning of the two pre-trained LMs.

## Method

SpaEF comprises three modules. The Spot Graph Construction (SGC) module uses the frozen OmiCLIP text encoder to produce semantic spot embeddings from spot sentences, applies Delaunay Triangulation to coordinates for a spatial neighbor set, and then uses a proposed GAT-based GAE (AGAE) with two attention layers to integrate semantic and spatial information while mitigating over-smoothing; the final spot graph connects each spot to its top-k_s cosine-similar neighbors. The Gene Graph Construction (GGC) module uses the frozen GenePT encoder on NCBI descriptions to obtain gene embeddings, leverages GenePT's built-in logistic regression predictor to compute pairwise interaction probabilities, and combines these with top-k_g Pearson co-expression edges (accepted only if interaction probability exceeds threshold τ=0.5) to form the gene graph. The Element-wise Graph Autoencoder (EGAE) module employs two GAT-based Feature Autoencoders to independently reconstruct the spot and gene graphs, then fuses the two denoised matrices via Element-wise Weighting Addition (EWA) with a learned M×N weight matrix and sigmoid gating, enabling per-element adaptive fusion. Training is two-stage: AGAE is first trained with cross-entropy loss on the spatial graph adjacency, then EGAE is trained with a Zero-Inflated Negative Binomial loss on the expression matrix.

## Results

On the HOCWT dataset, SpaEF achieves the lowest RMSE across all mask proportions (5%–50%), ranging from 0.3664 at 5% to 0.4172 at 50%, outperforming the next-best method STAGATE (0.3739–0.4643) by up to 10.3% at 50% masking. SpaEF also attains the highest PCC in most settings (0.7679 at 5% masking), though STAGATE slightly edges it at 50% masking (0.7528 vs. 0.7333). On the HGBM dataset, SpaEF yields the highest PCC and SCC between PCNA gene expression and protein intensity, and on the extremely sparse HDHBC dataset (98.66% zeros), SpaEF achieves a 40.54% PCC improvement over the second-best method STAGATE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpaEF consistently outperforms all six tabulated baselines on RMSE across every mask proportion on HOCWT, with the largest margins over Smoother (~44% lower RMSE) and SEDR (~60% lower RMSE). Against the closest competitor STAGATE, SpaEF reduces RMSE by 2.0% at 5% masking and by 10.3% at 50% masking; on PCC, SpaEF leads at 5–30% masking but STAGATE retains a slight edge at 50% (0.7528 vs. 0.7333). DUSTED is the second-closest on PCC at high masking (0.7496 at 50%), yet SpaEF still wins on RMSE at all proportions. DeepGFT, the most recent element-wise baseline, is outperformed by SpaEF on both RMSE and PCC at every masking level, with the gap widening as masking increases (e.g., 13.3% RMSE reduction at 50%). On HGBM, SpaEF achieves the highest gene–protein correlation (PCNA) and correctly preserves the CD44–MYC positive association (PCC > 0.5) while avoiding the spurious PDGFRA–PRF1 correlation that SEDR produces. On the extremely sparse HDHBC dataset, SpaEF improves PCC by 40.54% and SCC by 11.11% over STAGATE, the second-best method, though absolute correlations remain below 0.1 due to the 98.66% zero rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpaEF extends the graph-based SRT denoising lineage (STAGATE, SEDR, DeepGFT) by replacing histology-image-based spot encoders with the frozen text encoder of OmiCLIP, a contrastively pre-trained multi-modal foundation model, and by augmenting co-expression-based gene graphs with nonlinear semantic relationships from GenePT, a genomic LM pre-trained on NCBI gene descriptions. Unlike prior LM-based SRT methods (e.g., scGPT-spatial) that use LMs as representation learners requiring fine-tuning, SpaEF treats LMs as frozen external feature sources that inject biological priors into graph construction without additional training. The element-wise graph autoencoder (EGAE) with its Element-wise Weighting Addition fusion departs from the matrix-wise weighting strategy in DeepGFT, enabling per-element adaptive denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpaEF demonstrates that pre-trained LMs can serve as effective, bias-reducing feature extractors for spatial transcriptomics graph construction without fine-tuning, offering a practical alternative to histology-dependent pipelines that fail on datasets lacking images. The consistent improvements across four datasets and multiple downstream tasks (masking recovery, gene-protein correlation, spatial domain identification, clustering) suggest the LM-prior approach is robust. However, the authors acknowledge that absolute performance on extremely sparse data (HDHBC, 98.66% zeros) remains poor, and the reliance on NCBI gene descriptions limits applicability to non-human or poorly annotated organisms. Open questions include whether the frozen-LM paradigm scales to single-cell resolution data or other spatial omics modalities (proteomics, epigenomics).
