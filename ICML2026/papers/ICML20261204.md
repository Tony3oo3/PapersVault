## Problem

Existing KV cache compression methods (eviction, merging) assume uniform retention budgets across all layers and attention heads, ignoring the substantial heterogeneity in compression sensitivity across architectural components and tasks. While dynamic allocation has been explored at layer or task granularity, no unified signal exists that is simultaneously online-computable, comparable across heads, and stable across diverse inputs to drive fine-grained, content-aware budget scheduling.

## Input / Output

EntroKV consumes attention weights already materialized during the forward pass over a small observation window (w≥16 tokens), along with a global budget ratio β, an adaptivity coefficient α, and a pre-computed calibration baseline H̄. It outputs integer per-head retention quotas K_l^h = ⌊B_l^h · m⌋ that replace the fixed top-K in eviction operators or modulate merge ratios in merging operators, keeping total memory footprint identical to the uniform-budget baseline while redistributing capacity toward high-entropy heads.

## Method

EntroKV reframes KV cache compression as a constrained resource allocation problem and uses normalized attention entropy H̃_l^h = H(a_l^h)/log n as an online proxy for compression difficulty, grounded in the observation that diffuse (high-entropy) attention distributions lose more mass under Top-K retention than concentrated ones. The allocation rule B_l^h = β[(1−α)H̄ + αH̃_l^h] interpolates between a stable global baseline and instance-specific entropy, with α balancing noise robustness against adaptivity. This decouples the 'how much to keep' decision from the 'how to compress' mechanism, making EntroKV a plug-and-play front-end compatible with eviction operators (SnapKV, H2O) and merging operators (CaM, D2O) without modifying their core logic. The theoretical justification derives from KKT optimality conditions showing that optimal allocation equalizes marginal recall utility across heads, and a Schur-concavity argument establishing that entropy monotonically predicts the budget needed to reach a target recall threshold.

## Results

At a 30% KV retention budget, EntroKV consistently improves all underlying compression operators across three models (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-8B) and benchmarks (LongBench, RULER-4K, RULER-16K). On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Entro-CaM achieves 49.02 on LongBench (vs. 47.31 for CaM and 48.26 for Full Cache), and Entro-D2O reaches 49.11 (vs. 47.81 for D2O), both exceeding Full Cache. On RULER-16K, EntroKV recovers catastrophic failures of merging operators: Entro-CaM jumps from 67.18 (CaM) to 91.57, and Entro-D2O from 74.71 (D2O) to 90.87, approaching Full Cache (91.73). The method retains ~98% of Full Cache performance at 30% budget with negligible computational overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EntroKV is evaluated against both compression operators (SnapKV, CaM, D2O) and dynamic allocation methods (PyramidKV, AdaKV, LAVa, Task-KV, CAKE). Against compression operators, EntroKV yields the largest gains for merging-based methods: on Llama RULER-16K, Entro-CaM recovers 24.39 points over CaM (91.57 vs. 67.18) and Entro-D2O recovers 16.16 points over D2O (90.87 vs. 74.71), while eviction-based SnapKV sees a more modest but consistent +1.14 to +2.42 improvement. Against dynamic allocation baselines (Table 2, Llama-3.1-8B), Entro-SnapKV (48.15 LongBench, 89.33 RULER-4K, 91.63 RULER-16K) outperforms all competitors: PyramidKV (47.30/86.94/90.63), AdaKV (47.79/86.40/90.33), LAVa (47.25/85.24/87.12), Task-KV (46.66/86.45/87.41), and CAKE (47.24/88.02/91.02). Notably, LAVa and Task-KV underperform even static SnapKV on RULER-16K (87.12 and 87.41 vs. 90.88), while EntroKV exceeds Full Cache on LongBench for both CaM and D2O backbones, suggesting entropy-guided allocation provides an implicit denoising effect beyond simple capacity preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established KV cache compression literature (eviction methods like SnapKV and H2O, merging methods like CaM and D2O) and the emerging line of dynamic budget allocation work (PyramidKV, AdaKV, LAVa, Task-KV, CAKE). It departs from prior dynamic allocation approaches by replacing handcrafted layer-level heuristics or auxiliary computation branches with a single, unified, online-computable signal—normalized attention entropy—at the finest granularity (individual attention heads). The key conceptual contribution is decoupling budget allocation (how much to keep) from compression execution (how to compress), positioning entropy as a plug-and-play front-end scheduler rather than a new compression operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EntroKV offers a practically deployable, training-free module that consistently improves existing compression operators with negligible overhead, retaining ~98% of full-cache accuracy at 30% budget—a meaningful efficiency gain for long-context LLM serving. The observation that entropy-guided selective compression can occasionally exceed full-cache performance (implicit denoising) is an interesting conceptual finding, though the paper itself frames it as a subtle distinction between local approximation error and global task performance. Open questions include extension to multimodal LLMs where cross-modal attention patterns may differ fundamentally, co-design with quantization for compounded savings, and applicability to attention-agnostic compression paradigms (learned importance predictors, activation-based pruning) where the entropy signal may not transfer.
