## Problem

Existing weight-space learning methods produce latent representations of neural network weights that lack an explicit semantic reference frame tied to the datasets those models were trained on. Without such alignment, the learned geometry is difficult to navigate, prompt, or use for controllable generation, because proximity in the latent space does not correspond to dataset-level variation. This limits zero-shot transfer, latent-space exploration, and dataset-conditioned weight synthesis.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small subset of 32×32 RGB images from a target dataset (as few as 1 image, typically 10–100) as a 'data prompt,' which is encoded by a DeepSets set encoder into a single dataset embedding. The output is a full set of neural network weights for a known architecture (a ~11K-parameter CNN or a 2.8M-parameter ResNet-18), decoded from a sequence of token representations produced by a dataset-to-model mapper. At test time, an optional latent refinement step further adjusts the decoded weights using a small number of target-data gradient steps.

## Method

WeightCLIP jointly trains a SANE transformer autoencoder (for weight tokens) and a DeepSets dataset encoder under a bidirectional token-level contrastive alignment loss applied directly in the autoencoder's latent space, rather than in a separate projection head. This reshapes the weight latent so that models trained on similar datasets cluster together, making the space navigable by dataset prompts. A lightweight mapper (linear or memory-bank) then converts a dataset embedding into a full window of token representations, which the autoencoder decoder turns into model weights. For test-time adaptation, a two-stage latent refinement projects the mapped tokens onto a learned hyperspherical shell and performs local gradient descent through the decoder, keeping updates constrained to the model manifold and outperforming standard weight-space fine-tuning under matched compute budgets.

## Results

On the ResNet18 OOD generation task (Table 3), WeightCLIP-MBM achieves the best zero-shot (Ep.0) average accuracy of ~27.6% across six held-out datasets, outperforming TANS (~25.8%), the Text2Model hypernetwork (~23.9%), and training from scratch (~23.5%). After 10 epochs of fine-tuning, WeightCLIP-LM reaches ~78.1% average accuracy versus 69.7% for TANS and 72.7% for the hypernetwork. In the latent-space refinement experiment (Table 6), optimizing in the aligned latent space yields 44.9% average accuracy versus 38.8% for standard weight-space fine-tuning under identical compute budgets (+6.1 points). For in-distribution retrieval, WeightCLIP achieves 95% R@1 recall compared to 90% for TANS and only 10% without alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TANS (Jeong et al., 2021), WeightCLIP wins on all three evaluated capabilities: in-distribution retrieval (95% R@1 vs. 90%), OOD retrieval after one epoch (55.6% vs. 43.2% average accuracy), and dataset-to-model generation at every fine-tuning stage (e.g., ~27.6% vs. ~25.8% at Ep.0; ~78.1% vs. ~69.7% at Ep.10 on ResNet18). Against D2NWG's projection baseline (Bedionita et al., 2025b), WeightCLIP achieves higher OOD generation accuracy at Ep.0 (26.6% vs. 23.9% average) and OOD retrieval after one epoch (55.6% vs. 53.9%), confirming that reshaping the weight-space latent itself (rather than projecting into a frozen space) yields better downstream performance. Against the Text2Model hypernetwork (Amosy et al., 2024), WeightCLIP clearly leads at Ep.0 and Ep.1 (e.g., 68.4% vs. 59.1% on Colorectal Histology at Ep.1), but the gap narrows at Ep.10 where the hypernetwork is competitive on individual datasets such as CIFAR-10 (71.5% vs. 70.5%) and Honeybee Pollen (92.8% vs. 95.3%), while WeightCLIP still leads on average. The ablation in Table 4 further shows that aligning only the dataset encoder (as in D2NWG) gives only a marginal gain over no alignment (23.9% vs. 14.5%), whereas full bidirectional alignment of both encoders is necessary for the strongest results (26.6%), underscoring that the key contribution is reshaping the weight-space geometry itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

WeightCLIP builds directly on the SANE autoencoder (Schürholt et al., 2024) for weight tokenization and the hyperspherical-shell geometry observed in hyper-representations (Schürholt et al., 2022a), but departs from prior dataset-model alignment work by using contrastive alignment to reshape the weight-space latent itself rather than treating it as a frozen space for retrieval (TANS) or as a conditioning target for external generators (D2NWG, Text2Model). The conceptual lineage is CLIP-style cross-modal alignment transplanted to the weight modality, where the dataset plays the role of the 'caption' for a trained model. The key architectural departure is that alignment is applied in the autoencoder's native latent space (not a separate projection head), so the same representations serve retrieval, generation, and refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making datasets an explicit reference frame in weight space, the paper enables a unified pipeline—retrieval, generation, and latent refinement—from a small data prompt, potentially accelerating model training for known architectures on new datasets without expensive sampling or full fine-tuning. The latent refinement procedure, which optimizes through the decoder while constraining updates to the learned manifold, is structurally distinct from weight-space fine-tuning and outperforms it under matched compute, suggesting a new axis of test-time adaptation. Key limitations include restriction to single-architecture zoos, CV-only datasets, and reliance on training augmentations rather than explicit symmetry handling; extending to heterogeneous model zoos or cross-modal (e.g., vision-language) settings remains open.
