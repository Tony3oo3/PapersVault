## Problem

Standard entropy-regularized fine-tuning of generative flow and diffusion models maximizes expected reward, offering no mechanism to shape tail behavior. When the objective depends on the lower tail (risk-averse worst-case control) or upper tail (novelty-seeking discovery), expected-reward optimization is insufficient. The prior framework FDC handles CVaR-based tail shaping but requires K sequential fine-tuning calls, making it significantly more expensive than standard fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained flow/diffusion velocity field, a scalar reward function r(x), a risk level β ∈ (0,1), and a CVaR mode (right for novelty-seeking, left for risk-aversion). Stage 1 requires only a finite offline batch of N samples drawn from the pre-trained distribution to solve a 1D threshold problem; no new sampling is needed during optimization. The output is a single fine-tuned velocity field u^finetune that, when integrated, generates samples whose reward distribution has the desired tail shape (e.g., truncated lower tail or amplified upper tail).

## Method

TFFT exploits the variational dual formulation of CVaR to decompose the nonlinear generative optimization into a decoupled two-stage procedure. Stage 1 solves a lightweight 1D scalar optimization (minimize F_R(t) for right-CVaR, maximize F_L(t) for left-CVaR) using only offline samples from the pre-trained prior, where the objective is proven strictly convex/concave and L-smooth. Stage 2 constructs a pseudo-reward — r*(x) = [r(x)−t*]_+/(1−β) for right-CVaR or r*(x) = −[t*−r(x)]_+/β for left-CVaR — and runs a single standard entropy-regularized fine-tuning solver (e.g., Adjoint Matching) with this pseudo-reward. The key theoretical result (Theorems 4.1–4.2) shows the optimal fine-tuned distribution has the closed-form Boltzmann structure p*(x) ∝ p^pre(x) exp(r*(x)/α), and that the optimal threshold t* equals the VaR_β of the resulting distribution. This reduces the computational cost from O(K·C_FT) for FDC to O(C_Thresh + C_FT), matching the cost of standard expected-reward fine-tuning.

## Results

On text-to-image generation (Stable Diffusion v1-5, ImageReward), L-TFFT matches EXP-FT's mean reward (0.850 vs. 0.852) while significantly improving the worst-case metric L-CVaR₀.₂ from −0.370 to −0.306, and achieves the highest CLIP-Score (0.281) and HPSv2 (0.280). On molecular design (FlowMol, GFN1-xTB energy), R-TFFT achieves the highest R-CVaR₀.₉ of 183.4, outperforming EXP-FT (167.1) and FDC (172.5), while maintaining 85.7% chemical validity and completing in a single fine-tuning step (≈T) versus FDC's three iterations (≈3T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EXP-FT (Adjoint Matching), L-TFFT essentially ties on mean ImageReward (0.850 vs. 0.852) but improves the worst-case L-CVaR₀.₂ by 0.064 (−0.306 vs. −0.370), confirming its ability to raise the quality floor without sacrificing average performance. In molecular design, R-TFFT sacrifices mean reward (27.1 vs. 29.8 for EXP-FT) but achieves a substantially higher R-CVaR₀.₉ (183.4 vs. 167.1), demonstrating superior discovery of extreme high-reward molecules. Against FDC, L-TFFT wins on both mean reward (0.850 vs. 0.812) and worst-case L-CVaR₀.₂ (−0.306 vs. −0.362) under the same 82 GPU-hour budget, since FDC must split its budget across K iterative fine-tuning calls. In molecular design, R-TFFT outperforms FDC(K=3) on R-CVaR₀.₉ (183.4 vs. 172.5) while requiring only 1 fine-tuning call versus 3, and maintains higher chemical validity (85.7% vs. 82.7%). The only metric where TFFT shows a slight trade-off is DreamSim diversity variance (0.289 vs. 0.306 for EXP-FT), reflecting the expected suppression of diverse but low-quality tail samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Flow Density Control (FDC, De Santi et al., 2025), which first formulated CVaR-based generative optimization but solved it via K sequential entropy-regularized fine-tuning calls. The key conceptual departure is exploiting the variational dual of CVaR to show the nonlinear objective decouples into a one-dimensional scalar threshold search plus a single standard fine-tuning step with a constructed pseudo-reward, reducing cost from O(K·C_FT) to O(C_Thresh + C_FT). The fine-tuning solver itself (Adjoint Matching, Domingo-Enrich et al., 2024) is used as a black-box component, and the broader entropy-regularized flow control framework (Tang & Zhou, Uehara et al.) provides the theoretical substrate. The paper also positions itself against risk-sensitive RL and convex RL literature, distinguishing its direct control of the generative density from approaches that use generative models merely as samplers for downstream robust training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making CVaR fine-tuning as computationally cheap as standard expected-reward fine-tuning, TFFT removes the primary barrier to deploying tail-aware generative optimization in practice (e.g., safety-critical image generation, de novo molecular discovery). The theoretical guarantee that the single-step solution is a fixed point of FDC's iterative operator provides a clean unification and validates the approach. A stated limitation is sensitivity to extreme values of the risk level β, and the authors note future work should extend the dual decomposition to more general risk measures beyond CVaR. The method is currently validated on two high-dimensional tasks (text-to-image, molecular design) and a 2D toy, so broader applicability across modalities and reward types remains to be established.
