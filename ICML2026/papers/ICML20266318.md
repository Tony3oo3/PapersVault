## Problem

In autoregressive LVLMs, visual information encoded as a prefix undergoes progressive 'dilution' during decoding, causing the model to drift toward language priors and hallucinate objects. Existing inference-time interventions (contrastive decoding, iterative refinement) correct this drift but require multiple extra forward passes, incurring prohibitive latency that undermines real-world deployment. The paper asks whether hallucination can be mitigated with negligible overhead by structurally preventing visual dilution rather than correcting it after the fact.

## Input / Output

RUDDER takes a standard RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., 'Please help me describe the image in detail') as input to an existing LVLM and produces either an open-ended text caption (up to 512 tokens, evaluated on CHAIR) or a binary yes/no answer (evaluated on POPE). The method is entirely training-free and operates as a plug-in hook on any pre-trained LVLM: the CARD vector is extracted opportunistically during the mandatory prefill pass via a read-only hook, and steering is applied as a residual-stream update during the subsequent autoregressive decoding pass, introducing no additional model.forward invocations.

## Method

RUDDER extracts a per-sample visual evidence direction (CARD) by pooling and L2-normalizing the self-attention residual updates across all prefill tokens at a fixed target layer, capturing the net visual influence before it fades. During decoding, a Beta Gate modulates the injection strength: it computes cosine similarity between the current hidden state and the CARD vector, maps this alignment to Beta-Bernoulli pseudo-counts via softplus, and uses the posterior mean as a trust-based gate that reinforces steering when the trajectory is visually grounded and suppresses it for syntactic or unstable tokens. The gated steering vector is injected into the residual stream immediately after the self-attention sublayer, restricted to the answer span. The entire pipeline requires only lightweight per-token vector operations (cosine similarity, softplus, scalar multiplication) and a single residual addition, maintaining 96% of vanilla throughput with less than 4% latency overhead.

## Results

On the CHAIR benchmark (greedy decoding, 500 MSCOCO samples), RUDDER-Beta achieves CHAIR_S of 39.5/28.4/27.1 and CHAIR_I of 10.5/10.9/8.5 on LLaVA-1.5-7B, Idefics2-8B, and InstructBLIP respectively, yielding average relative reductions of 24.4% (CHAIR_S) and 23.6% (CHAIR_I) over vanilla baselines. On POPE (greedy), RUDDER-Beta improves accuracy by 1.0/0.7/0.5 pp and F1 by 1.6/1.3/0.14 pp over vanilla across the three models. Throughput is maintained at 96.0% of vanilla (54.9 tok/s on LLaVA-1.5 vs. 56.7 vanilla), compared to only 58.1% for VISTA, while MME general-capability scores are preserved or slightly improved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RUDDER-Beta consistently outperforms logit-based baselines: on LLaVA-1.5 (greedy CHAIR), it reduces CHAIR_S from 48.6 (vanilla) to 39.5, beating VCD (49.8), DoLa (47.6), and AGLA (44.6), and matching or slightly exceeding HALC (40.3) on CHAIR_S while achieving a lower CHAIR_I (10.5 vs. 10.3). Against the strongest steering baseline VISTA, RUDDER-Beta wins on Idefics2 (28.4 vs. 33.5 CHAIR_S) and InstructBLIP (27.1 vs. 27.7 CHAIR_S, 8.5 vs. 9.7 CHAIR_I), and is competitive on LLaVA-1.5 (39.5 vs. 38.6 CHAIR_S, but 10.5 vs. 11.4 CHAIR_I). DeGF achieves the lowest absolute CHAIR scores (34.2/9.2 on LLaVA-1.5) but requires iterative self-correction passes incurring >3× latency, whereas RUDDER operates in a single forward pass at 96% throughput. On POPE, RUDDER-Beta achieves the highest F1 on LLaVA-1.5 (86.03) and Idefics2 (76.52), surpassing VISTA (85.42 and 74.66) and PAI (85.31), confirming that the adaptive gating preserves object-recognition accuracy while reducing hallucination.

## Relation to Prior Work

RUDDER departs from the dominant logit-level contrastive decoding paradigm (VCD, PAI, DoLa, HALC) and from steering methods that require extra forward passes (VISTA, ASD) by repurposing the prefill residual stream—already computed in the mandatory first pass—as a zero-cost source of per-sample visual evidence (the CARD vector). The adaptive Beta Gate draws conceptually from Bayesian gating literature (BetaLSTM, Evidential Deep Learning) but repurposes the Beta-Bernoulli posterior mean as a trust mechanism that reinforces visually aligned trajectories rather than correcting deviations, a framing distinct from prior steering work that typically pushes hardest when the model is most wrong. The method thus carves new ground at the intersection of residual-stream analysis and probabilistic gating, achieving single-pass steering without the latency overhead that has limited prior ITI methods to research settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RUDDER's 96% throughput (vs. ~58% for VISTA) makes hallucination mitigation practically deployable in latency-sensitive applications like autonomous systems and medical imaging, where multi-pass methods are infeasible. The generalization across linear-projection (LLaVA, Idefics2), Q-Former (InstructBLIP), and modern architectures (Qwen2.5-VL) suggests the prefill-residual signal is a broadly applicable visual anchor. The primary open question is hyperparameter sensitivity: each architecture requires a separate layer selection and (α_max, k) tuning, and the authors acknowledge that automated optimization is needed for broader adoption. A secondary open question is whether the trust-mechanism framing (reinforcing alignment over correcting deviation) generalizes to other steering tasks beyond hallucination mitigation.
