## Problem

Quantum kernel machines have been evaluated almost exclusively on scalar-valued classification and regression tasks where classical kernel methods are already highly effective, leaving little room to demonstrate quantum advantage. The authors argue that scalar-valued kernels lack the degrees of freedom to exploit intrinsically quantum resources such as entanglement and cannot naturally model structured dependencies among outputs, making them ill-suited for the complex prediction problems where classical methods genuinely struggle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20266425/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes classical data points encoded as density matrices (Hermitian, positive semi-definite, unit-trace) and produces a p×p operator-valued kernel matrix rather than a single scalar similarity. In the proof-of-concept, inputs are random single-qubit density matrices and the learned output is a quantum channel represented by its Choi matrix (a 2p×2p SPD matrix). The full kernel matrix in the operator-valued setting is a block matrix of size np×np, substantially larger than the n×n scalar-valued Gram matrix, which is precisely where the authors argue quantum linear-system solvers could offer the most meaningful speedup.

## Method

The paper defines entangled quantum operator-valued kernels (QOVKs) via K(x,z) = Tr_X[U_YX(ρ_Y ⊗ σ_X^{x,z})U_YX†], where U_YX is a non-separable unitary acting jointly on input and output registers, ρ_Y is a fixed output density matrix, and σ_X^{x,z} is a feature matrix extracted from the inputs. The non-separability of U_YX is the key mechanism: it creates joint, non-factorizable input-output correlations that separable QOVKs (which reduce to scalar-valued fidelity kernels times a fixed output matrix) and scalar-valued kernels fundamentally cannot represent. A quantum circuit generalizing the swap test is provided to prepare σ_X^{x,z} via partial measurement of an ancillary qubit, after which the non-separable unitary and partial trace yield the kernel value. As a proof-of-concept, the authors apply kernel ridge regression with QOVKs (using a CNOT+SWAP as U_YX) to quantum channel estimation, showing that the entangled OVK recovers bit-flip and dephasing channels with Frobenius-norm errors of ~0.10 versus ~0.53–0.57 for the scalar-valued fidelity kernel baseline.

## Results

On quantum channel estimation (bit-flip and dephasing channels, a=0.2, n=100, α=0.1, averaged over 10 runs), the entangled QOVK achieves a Frobenius-norm recovery error of 0.101±0.035 (bit-flip) and 0.096±0.031 (dephasing), substantially outperforming the separable QSVK baseline (fidelity kernel) which yields errors of 0.569±0.304 and 0.525±0.297 respectively. The QOVK reduces the recovery error by roughly a factor of 5.5–5.6× while also reducing variance by an order of magnitude. Results are reported for channel parameter a swept from 0 to 1 in steps of 0.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole quantitative comparison is between the proposed entangled QOVK and the quantum scalar-valued fidelity kernel (QSVK), the latter following the formulation of Canatar et al. (2023). On both the bit-flip and dephasing channel estimation tasks, the QOVK wins decisively: it reduces the Frobenius-norm channel recovery error from ~0.53–0.57 to ~0.10, a roughly 5.5× improvement, while simultaneously reducing the standard deviation by a factor of ~8–10 (e.g., 0.304 → 0.035 on bit-flip). The QSVK baseline, which factorizes input similarity and output structure independently, struggles to capture the matrix-valued structure of the Choi representation, whereas the QOVK's non-separable unitary (CNOT + SWAP) encodes joint input–output correlations. No other classical or quantum baselines (e.g., classical OVK, random-feature approximations, or deep-learning channel estimators) are included in the comparison, so the empirical evidence is limited to a single head-to-head contrast.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical operator-valued kernel (OVK) framework of Micchelli & Pontil (2005) and the entangled kernel formalism of Huusari & Kadri (2021) into the quantum domain, introducing the notion of entangled quantum operator-valued kernels (QOVKs) as a strict generalization of the scalar-valued quantum fidelity kernels popularized by Havlíček et al. (2019) and Schuld & Killoran (2019). The quantum circuit construction generalizes the swap-test-based fidelity kernel computation (Buhrman et al., 2001) to produce operator-valued outputs, and the paper further connects to the emerging C*-algebraic kernel theory (Hashimoto et al., 2021, 2023) as a path toward non-commutative quantum kernel representations. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant QML narrative by arguing that the field's focus on scalar-valued kernels for standard classification/regression is a structural dead end, since classical kernel methods and deep learning are already highly competitive in those settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete structural reason why quantum kernels have not demonstrated advantage: scalar-valued kernels lack the degrees of freedom to exploit entanglement as a computational resource, and the tasks they target are ones where classical methods are already strong. The proof-of-concept on quantum channel estimation (QOVK error ~0.10 vs QSVK error ~0.55) is suggestive but rests on a single small-scale experiment (n=100, one parameter setting per channel), so the empirical case remains preliminary. Major open questions include whether QOVKs genuinely overcome the generalization and expressivity limitations that plague QSVKs, whether the C*-algebraic framework yields practical quantum circuits, and whether the operator-valued setting creates sufficient computational headroom for quantum linear-system solvers to deliver a provable advantage. As a position paper, its primary contribution is a research agenda rather than a definitive result.
