## Problem

Offline RL performance is strongly bounded by the quality of the logging policy that generated the dataset, yet actionable principles for improving the data-generating process itself remain scarce. In active positioning tasks, logging policies are typically deterministic, scripted routines with internal state, so naively injecting better actions mid-trajectory breaks the policy's assumptions (the 'hand-off problem'). The paper asks how logging policies can be augmented during collection in a principled, theoretically grounded way to produce higher-quality data for offline RL.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline trajectory dataset collected from a deterministic logging policy (e.g., a coordinate walk) in active positioning environments, where observations range from direct position vectors to image-based signals (Siemens star, light tunnel, Fetch camera) and the state includes a latent context W governing movement distortion. It produces (1) a set of synthetic shortcut transitions used only to train an augmentor Q-function, (2) an augmented logging policy that interleaves the original policy with augmentor suggestions during collection, and (3) a final dataset of real trajectories from which a standard offline RL policy (CQL) is trained. The shortcut augmentation integrates into d3rlpy via its transition picker protocol with a single line of code, and the final training dataset contains only real (non-synthetic) transitions.

## Method

LIFT identifies 'shortcuts'—accumulated actions that skip redundant sub-trajectories while provably improving value—by checking a reward-based inequality (γG_j − G_i + r_{j−1} ≥ C·Σ‖a_k‖) over all (i,j) pairs in each logged trajectory (Algorithm 1). The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.6) shows that under distance-improving policies, Lipschitz-continuous value functions, and linear placement errors in the distortion function, such accumulated actions are valid π-shortcuts. During data collection (Algorithm 2), with probability p the logging policy's action is replaced by an augmentor's suggestion (a Q-function trained via CQL on the shortcut-augmented dataset), and the logging policy's internal state is reset afterward to ensure clean hand-offs. Unlike IORL, which injects exploratory actions to improve coverage, LIFT exploits geometric structure in the trajectories to find actions that point toward higher-value states, making hand-offs seamless. The approach is validated across multiple movement distortions (linear, non-linear, non-continuous), observation modalities, and dimensions, consistently outperforming unaugmented CQL and often surpassing warm-start SAC in higher-dimensional or partially observable settings.

## Results

The paper evaluates LIFT and its variants (CQL-SC, LIFT, LIFT-SC) across active positioning tasks with varying dimensionality (d=2, d=5), movement distortions (f_blend, f_rot, f_scale, f_sin, f_regrot, f_sqrt), and observation types (position, lens images, light tunnel, Fetch). The headline finding is that LIFT-SC (iterative data collection with shortcut augmentations + shortcut-augmented CQL training) consistently achieves the lowest distance-to-target across all tested scenarios, outperforming plain CQL, LIFT without shortcuts, and warm-start SAC in high-dimensional (d=5) and image-based settings. In low-dimensional position-observation tasks (d=2), SAC with warm-start still retains an edge. Shortcut augmentation also works under noisy, unstructured logging policies, with consistent gains over unaugmented baselines across all noise levels tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against plain CQL (offline RL on unaugmented data), both CQL-SC and LIFT-SC consistently achieve lower distance-to-target, with the gap widening in higher dimensions and under partial observability. Against warm-start SAC (SAC initialized with offline data then fine-tuned online), LIFT-SC outperforms SAC almost consistently in d=5 position-observation scenarios across all movement distortions and logging expertness levels, as well as in all image-based scenarios (lens positioning, light tunnel, Fetch); however, SAC remains ahead in the low-dimensional d=2 position-observation cases. Against IORL (uncertainty-based exploratory augmentation), LIFT produces trajectories with higher average returns and better hand-off quality to the logging policy, while IORL achieves broader state-action exploration at the cost of degraded trajectory quality. Against diffusion-based methods (GTA for synthetic transition generation and DQL for diffusion-based policy learning), LIFT-SC outperforms in most tested scenarios, particularly where the logging policy is suboptimal and structured. Notably, shortcut augmentation fails under f_regrot (where the f-contraction property is globally violated) and provides only negligible gains over SAC under f_sqrt (where LPE does not hold), confirming the theoretical boundary conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

LIFT builds directly on the iterative offline RL (IORL) framework of Zhang et al. (2023), which injects exploratory actions into logging policies during data collection to improve state-action coverage. The key departure is replacing exploration with exploitation: rather than selecting unseen actions, LIFT trains a Q-function augmentor to suggest 'shortcuts'—actions that skip redundant sub-trajectories—thereby improving trajectory quality while preserving seamless hand-offs back to the deterministic logging policy. The theoretical analysis draws on POMDP structure, contraction mappings, and Lipschitz regularity to formalize when accumulated actions are guaranteed beneficial, extending the policy improvement theorem to the active positioning setting. The method also connects to the broader data augmentation literature for offline RL (Hindsight Experience Replay, image augmentations, diffusion-based trajectory generation) but operates at the trajectory level during collection rather than post-hoc on a static dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in industrial positioning tasks where logging policies are deterministic, structured, and suboptimal, making both pure offline learning and extensive online fine-tuning impractical. LIFT provides a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism to improve data quality during collection with minimal disruption to the logging policy, and its integration into d3rlpy via a single line of code makes it broadly applicable to any offline RL algorithm. Key limitations include reliance on assumptions (LPE, Lipschitz continuity, distance-improving policies) that may not hold globally in real systems, and validation restricted to semi-realistic simulators rather than physical platforms. Open questions include combining shortcut augmentation with model-based methods or world models, and whether the approach generalizes beyond positioning to other structured control tasks.
