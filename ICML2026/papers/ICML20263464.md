## Problem

Existing medical AI systems handle image understanding and generation in isolated, task-specific models, losing the shared cross-modal knowledge that a unified architecture could exploit. Three bottlenecks prevent unification: medical datasets remain predominantly single-modal, training pipelines lack a systematic progressive paradigm for capturing deep cross-modal relationships, and no truly unified medical model exists (e.g., HealthGPT requires loading different checkpoints per task). The central question is whether understanding and generation can share a single set of parameters and mutually reinforce each other rather than compromise one another.

## Input / Output

UniMedVL accepts medical images across eight modalities (color fundus photos, chest X-rays, CT, histopathology, MRI, OCT, ultrasound, endoscopy) at resolutions from 32 to 1024 pixels, combined with free-form text prompts or structured queries. Outputs span textual answers and diagnostic reports, synthesized or transformed medical images (via a frozen FLUX VAE decoder), and interleaved sequences that jointly produce visual content and explanatory text within a single generation pass. For counterfactual generation, the model simultaneously emits a modified image and a coherent textual explanation of the change, a format not supported by prior unified medical models.

## Method

UniMedVL adopts a dual-encoder Transformer architecture: a ViT encoder extracts semantic tokens for understanding while a frozen FLUX VAE encoder produces latent tokens for generation; both token streams pass through task-specific FFN layers before being concatenated with text tokens and processed by shared self-attention layers that jointly predict next text tokens and velocity fields on VAE latents. Training follows a three-stage Progressive Curriculum: (1) Foundation Training on 5.6M curated samples with joint NTP + rectified-flow loss, (2) Instruction Tuning with Distilled Chain-of-Thought augmentation for understanding and Caption-Augmented Generation for synthesis, and (3) Unified Multimodal Training on 330K interleaved tasks (virtual staining, super-resolution, cross-modal synthesis, counterfactual generation, prompt segmentation) that couple understanding and generation within single sequences. The dataset UniMedVL-5M is curated via a two-stage quality filter (coarse size/length filtering followed by medical alignment scoring using MedGemma-27b captions and MedSigLIP embeddings, retaining the top 50% by composite score). An information-theoretic argument shows that joint modeling of text and image outputs exploits non-trivial conditional mutual information I(T_out; z_VAE | C) > 0, yielding a lower Bayes-optimal uncertainty than factorized training, which empirically manifests as bidirectional transfer: adding generation training lifts understanding accuracy by up to 24.8%, and adding understanding training reduces generation FID by 11.2%.

## Results

On medical visual understanding, UniMedVL (14B) achieves an average accuracy of 67.47 across five benchmarks (VQA-RAD 61.9, SLAKE 75.4, PathVQA 53.5, OmniMedVQA 85.8, GMAI-MMBench 60.75), surpassing the closest unified medical model HealthGPT-L14 (56.94 avg) by 10.5 points and approaching the understanding-only Lingshu-32B (70.10 avg) despite supporting both understanding and generation in a single set of parameters. On text-to-image generation across eight medical modalities, UniMedVL achieves an average gFID of 96.29 and BioMedCLIP Score of 0.706, outperforming LlamaGen-MediTok (171.85 gFID) and Bagel (215.49 gFID). On interleaved tasks, UniMedVL reaches 20.27 PSNR on H&E→IHC virtual staining (vs. 15.81 for HealthGPT-M3) and 27.29 PSNR on 4× MRI super-resolution (vs. 18.37 for HealthGPT-M3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest unified medical model HealthGPT-L14 (14B), UniMedVL wins on all five understanding benchmarks by 3.6–17.7 points (e.g., OmniMedVQA 85.8 vs. 74.4, GMAI-MMBench 60.75 vs. 43.1), and also outperforms it on interleaved tasks (H&E→IHC PSNR 20.27 vs. 15.81; MRI SR PSNR 27.29 vs. 18.37; T2↔FLAIR avg PSNR 25.07 vs. 19.09). Compared to the strongest understanding-only model Lingshu-32B, UniMedVL trails on VQA-RAD (61.9 vs. 71.4), SLAKE (75.4 vs. 84.7), and PathVQA (53.5 vs. 61.3) but leads on OmniMedVQA (85.8 vs. 80.4) and GMAI-MMBench (60.75 vs. 52.7), while also providing generation capabilities that Lingshu lacks. On generation, UniMedVL (avg gFID 96.29) substantially outperforms both general unified models (Bagel 215.49, LlamaGen-MediTok 171.85) and all four modality-specialized generators (RetinaLogos 60.45 on CFP, CheXGen 90.70 on CXR, MedSyn 174.42 on CT, PathLDM 241.53 on HIS). Against closed-source models, UniMedVL surpasses GPT-4o on SLAKE (75.4 vs. 72.11), OmniMedVQA (85.8 vs. 71.06), and GMAI-MMBench (60.75 vs. 57.27), and beats Gemini-2.5-Flash on OmniMedVQA (85.8 vs. 71.33) and GMAI-MMBench (60.75 vs. 59.05). On counterfactual generation (ICG-CXR), UniMedVL† achieves 27.17 gFID and 0.7970 AUROC, improving over ProgEmu (29.21 gFID, 0.7921 AUROC) while substantially outperforming it on text quality (BLEU-3 0.2641 vs. 0.1241, ROUGE-L 0.4649 vs. 0.2606).

## Relation to Prior Work

UniMedVL builds directly on the dual-encoder unified architecture from Deng et al. (2025, Bagel), adapting it to the medical domain with a FLUX VAE for generation and a ViT for understanding. It departs from HealthGPT (Lin et al., 2025), the prior closest medical unified model, by eliminating the need for separate task-specific checkpoints and instead training a single parameter set through a three-stage progressive curriculum. The broader unified understanding-and-generation literature (Janus, Transfusion, Show-O) informs the architectural choices, but no prior work applies this paradigm to medical imaging with a dedicated large-scale dataset and curriculum tailored to heterogeneous medical objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that understanding and generation can mutually reinforce each other within a single medical model, challenging the assumption that joint training necessarily degrades individual capabilities. The released UniMedVL-5M dataset (5.6M samples, 8 modalities) is a substantial community resource for unified medical multimodal research. Key limitations include restriction to 2D imaging, absence of clinical validation, and the fact that UniMedVL does not match the strongest task-specific systems on every individual benchmark; extending to 3D volumetric data and achieving clinical-grade reliability remain open challenges.
