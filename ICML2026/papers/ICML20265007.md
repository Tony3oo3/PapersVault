## Problem

One-step diffusion models for real-world video super-resolution (VSR) based on Diffusion Transformers remain computationally prohibitive for deployment, and existing low-bit quantization methods degrade severely in this setting. The core difficulty is that one-step inference eliminates the iterative error correction available in multi-step denoising, while the high dynamic range of video token activations and highly heterogeneous layer sensitivities make uniform quantization strategies ineffective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20265007/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution video clip (e.g., 32×3×180×318 in UDM10) and produces a high-resolution video via a single forward pass through a one-step DiT backbone (WAN2.1 adapted with DOVE's training recipe). Quantization is applied to all linear layers within the DiT module, targeting 4-bit or 6-bit for both weights and activations, while a low-rank full-precision residual branch (rank 32) is retained to compensate for quantization error. The VAE encoder/decoder surrounding the DiT remains in full precision.

## Method

LSGQuant introduces a dual-branch quantized linear layer where a low-rank full-precision path (L₁L₂) complements a low-bit quantized path (Q_A(XH)·Q_W(HW_R)) with Hadamard rotations. Three novel components address one-step-specific challenges: (1) DRAQ computes per-channel max absolute values online to normalize activations to [−1,1] before symmetric quantization, adapting the range dynamically to each token's statistics rather than relying on calibration-fixed scales; (2) VOLTS estimates per-layer sensitivity as the variance of channel-wise activation means across a calibration set, then assigns three tiers of training budget (frozen, light adaptation, full optimization) to 300 DiT linear layers based on two thresholds that fall in natural gaps between sensitivity clusters; (3) QAO iteratively re-derives the low-rank factors via SVD on the quantization residual (Ŵ − Q_W(Ŵ − L₁L₂)) to jointly minimize both SVD approximation error and quantization error, avoiding the gradient vanishing that occurs with straight-through estimators when the residual becomes small.

## Results

Under 4-bit quantization (W4A4), LSGQuant achieves PSNR of 23.80 on UDM10, 19.72 on REDS30, and 22.82 on MVSR4x, closely approaching the full-precision WAN2.1 backbone (23.74, 19.42, 22.80) while reducing parameters by 70.49% and FLOPs by 71.92%. At 6-bit, LSGQuant matches or slightly trails SVDQuant on PSNR (e.g., 23.69 vs. 23.71 on UDM10) but leads on perceptual metrics such as DISTS and DOVER. The advantage over prior quantization methods is most pronounced at 4-bit, where MinMax and SmoothQuant suffer catastrophic degradation (PSNR drops to ~19–20) while LSGQuant retains near-full-precision quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 4-bit, LSGQuant decisively outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. On UDM110, LSGQuant's PSNR of 23.80 exceeds the next-best QuaRot (22.72) by over 1 dB and SVDQuant (21.92) by nearly 2 dB; on the real-world MVSR4x dataset, the gap is even larger (22.82 vs. QuaRot 22.37 and SVDQuant 21.69). MinMax and SmoothQuant collapse at 4-bit, with PSNR dropping to 19–21 and DOVER scores near zero, indicating severe temporal inconsistency. At 6-bit, the performance gap narrows substantially: LSGQuant is within 0.02–0.04 dB of SVDQuant on PSNR (e.g., 23.69 vs. 23.71 on UDM10, 19.37 vs. 19.39 on REDS30) and ties SVDQuant on MVSR4x (22.79), while still leading on DISTS and DOVER. QuaRot consistently ranks second at 4-bit but falls well behind LSGQuant, confirming that Hadamard rotation alone is insufficient for one-step diffusion VSR. The full-precision WAN2.1 backbone serves as the upper bound, and LSGQuant at 4-bit matches or even slightly exceeds it on PSNR for UDM10 (23.80 vs. 23.74) and MVSR4x (22.82 vs. 22.80), demonstrating that the quantization-aware training effectively compensates for precision loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

LSGQuant sits at the intersection of two active research threads: one-step diffusion-based video super-resolution (DOVE, OSEDiff) and low-bit quantization of Diffusion Transformers (SVDQuant, ViDiT-Q, QuaRot). It directly inherits the dual-branch (low-rank FP + quantized) architecture and SVD initialization from SVDQuant, and the Hadamard rotation for outlier suppression from QuaRot, but departs from both by addressing the unique constraints of one-step inference—where there is no multi-step temporal averaging to smooth quantization errors and where layer behaviors are highly heterogeneous. The three novel components (DRAQ, VOLTS, QAO) are specifically motivated by the failure of multi-step quantization assumptions in the single-forward-pass regime, positioning this work as the first systematic 4-bit quantization study for one-step diffusion VSR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving near-FP quality at 4-bit (70%+ parameter and FLOP reduction) for a generative video model is practically significant for edge deployment of VSR, a task that has historically been too compute-heavy for consumer hardware. The clean three-cluster structure in layer sensitivity (90/193/17 layers) provides a reusable empirical principle for allocating quantization effort in DiTs more broadly. However, the method is validated only on the WAN2.1 backbone with DOVE's training recipe, and the calibration step (50 videos, full FP inference) still adds a non-trivial offline cost; whether the layer-sensitivity clustering generalizes across architectures, tasks, or bit-widths below 4 remains open.
