## Problem

Standard pipelines apply Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) and Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) sequentially, causing quantization noise to be distributed isotropically across weight dimensions. This forces the adapter to waste its limited rank capacity compensating for diffuse, uncorrectable noise rather than learning task-specific features. The paper asks whether the *structure* of quantization error can be shaped during quantization to align with the adapter's corrective capacity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weight matrices W ∈ R^{m×n}, calibration activations X ∈ R^{n×N} (128 sequences of length 2048 from C4), a discrete quantization codebook Q, and two rank hyperparameters: a designed rank r_d governing the noise-shaping subspace and an adaptation rank r_a for the final adapter. It produces quantized weights Ŵ and a closed-form adapter initialization (A_init, B_init) derived from the SVD of the activation-whitened residual, which is then fine-tuned on downstream tasks. Unlike weight-space methods, the quantization objective is activation-aware, weighting errors by their actual contribution to layer output variance.

## Method

ProjQ formulates quantization as a subspace projection objective: minimize the layer output error after applying an optimal low-rank correction, explicitly including input activations. The solver alternates between a P-step (truncated SVD of the current residual to identify the top-r_d correctable error directions) and a W-step (standard GPTQ quantization applied to the orthogonal-complement activations X_⊥ = X(I−P), which automatically focuses bit-budget on the uncorrectable tail). After convergence, a covariance-aware initialization (Lemma 4.2) computes the optimal adapter factors via SVD of the whitened residual ΔW·Y, where Y is the activation covariance square root. The key distinction from LoftQ is that ProjQ operates in activation space with an explicit orthogonal projection separating correctable from uncorrectable error, and provides a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 5.2) that the resulting quantized backbone preserves strictly greater plasticity for downstream tasks than classical PTQ.

## Results

Under aggressive 2-bit quantization, ProjQ achieves the lowest perplexity across most language modeling benchmarks: on LLaMA2-7B, C4 PPL drops to 21.50 (vs. 26.26 for GPTQ+SVD-LLM and 28.77 for LoftQ) and WikiText-2 PPL to 22.42 (vs. 28.81 and 30.02). On commonsense reasoning, ProjQ reaches 47.82% average accuracy on LLaMA2-7B, surpassing all baselines. In the fine-tuning setting (Table 2), ProjQ's 3-bit LLaMA2-7B model achieves WikiText-2 PPL of 5.69, matching the 4-bit QLoRA/LoftQ baseline and enabling a 25% model size reduction without quality loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProjQ consistently outperforms all six baselines across the evaluated models and tasks. Against GPTQ+SVD-LLM, ProjQ reduces C4 perplexity by 18% on LLaMA2-7B (21.50 vs. 26.26) and by 46% on Qwen2.5-7B (33.50 vs. 62.27), while also improving commonsense accuracy by 2.5–6 percentage points. AWQ+SVD-LLM is effectively non-functional at 2-bit, producing NaN or astronomically high perplexity (1.7e+5) on most models, making it a non-competitive baseline. Against CALDERA, ProjQ achieves lower perplexity on all models (e.g., 22.42 vs. 23.82 WikiText on LLaMA2-7B) and higher commonsense accuracy (47.82% vs. 46.85%). Against LoftQ, the closest LoRA-aware competitor, ProjQ wins on nearly every metric: 21.50 vs. 28.77 C4 PPL on LLaMA2-7B, 33.50 vs. 34.17 on Qwen2.5-7B, and 83.64 vs. 105.34 on Qwen3-4B. In the fine-tuning setting (Table 2), ProjQ surpasses QLoRA(GPTQ) on 2-bit commonsense (57.59% vs. 57.06% on LLaMA2-7B) and WikiText-2 PPL (8.17 vs. 8.73), and outperforms LoftQ by 2.8 points on 2-bit commonsense (57.59% vs. 54.78%). SVD-LLM serves as the adapter construction component within the GPTQ+SVD-LLM and AWQ+SVD-LLM baselines rather than a standalone method; ProjQ's covariance-aware initialization (Lemma 4.2) subsumes and improves upon this approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProjQ sits at the intersection of post-training quantization (GPTQ, AWQ) and LoRA-aware quantization (LoftQ, QLoRA), but departs from both in a key way: rather than minimizing total reconstruction error in weight space (as LoftQ does) or treating quantization and adaptation as independent stages (as QLoRA does), it formulates an activation-aware subspace projection objective that explicitly shapes quantization noise into the low-rank manifold the adapter will correct. The covariance-aware adapter initialization (Lemma 4.2) is directly borrowed from EoRA and SVD-LLM, while the W-step reuses GPTQ as a plug-in solver on projected activations. The conceptual departure from LoftQ is the shift from weight-space SVD to activation-space orthogonal projection, which the paper argues avoids wasting bit-budget on weights with minimal output impact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that the *structure* of quantization error matters as much as its magnitude when a downstream adapter must correct it—provides a principled design criterion for PTQ in fine-tunable deployment pipelines, and the theoretical guarantee (Theorem 5.2) that ProjQ strictly preserves more adapter plasticity than standard PTQ under a task shift is a clean contribution. Practically, matching 4-bit perplexity with only 3 bits (a 25% size reduction) across LLaMA-2, Qwen2.5, and Qwen3 makes this directly relevant to on-device and edge deployment. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-uniform or mixed-precision quantizers (the authors note this as future work), whether it generalizes beyond LoRA to other PEFT paradigms (e.g., adapters, prefix tuning), and whether the designed-rank = adaptation-rank finding holds at much larger scales where the subspace geometry may differ.
