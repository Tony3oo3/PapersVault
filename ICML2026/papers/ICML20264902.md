## Problem

Messages embedded in diffusion generation noise are severely attenuated by the denoising process and VAE decoding, creating a persistent capacity-robustness trade-off that existing methods resolve only through partial workarounds (redundancy, restricted embedding spaces) that sacrifice capacity, compatibility, or security. The paper identifies that extraction accuracy strictly correlates with the geometric distance between candidate hypothesis images, and that random noise sampling places most candidate pairs too close to survive this attenuation.

## Input / Output

The sender provides a secret message matrix of size H×W with elements in {0,…,R−1} (where R candidate states encode log₂R bits per pixel), a shared secret key k_s, and a pretrained diffusion model (256×256 pixel-space DDPM or latent-space Stable Diffusion v1.5/v2.1). The output is a single stego image generated through the standard diffusion pipeline with the message encoded in the variance noise at the penultimate step (t=1), achieving up to 65,536 bits (pixel-space) or 16,384 bits (latent-space). The receiver, given the stego image and the same key, reconstructs the message matrix via synchronized trajectory replay followed by iterative optimization.

## Method

ASIR is a training-free framework built on two mechanisms. Antipodal Sampling replaces independent random noise vectors with maximally separated 'antipodal' points: a shared pseudo-random anchor r is generated from the key, candidate probabilities are placed at equal intervals (1/R apart) on the unit circle, and the Inverse CDF of the standard Gaussian maps these to noise samples that are deterministically linked to the message while preserving the exact N(0,I) marginal distribution (provable security). Iterative Recovery reframes extraction as a gradient-based optimization: the preliminary message estimate from direct Euclidean comparison is treated as a continuous differentiable parameter (via Straight-Through Estimator for R=2 or Gumbel-Softmax for R>2), and gradient descent minimizes the L2 distance between the received stego image and a reconstructed image generated through the differentiable final denoising step, effectively inverting the non-linear distortion without sacrificing embedding capacity.

## Results

On pixel-space DDPMs (Church, Bedroom, CelebA-HQ), ASIR embeds 65,536 bits with 99.0% extraction accuracy and a steganalysis detection error of 0.50 (ideal). On latent-space Stable Diffusion v2.1, ASIR embeds 16,384 bits at 98.8% accuracy with the same 0.50 security score. With an adaptive candidate pool (CP={2,3,4}) and τ=0.0, capacity scales to 131,072 bits while maintaining ≥98.0% accuracy. FID degradation from embedding is negligible (e.g., 28.16→28.36 on SD-v2.1/COCO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Pulsar (pixel-space), ASIR achieves ~18× higher capacity (65,536 vs. 3,610 bits) and 4.5 pp higher accuracy (99.0% vs. 94.5%) while matching its security score of 0.50. Against Diffusion-Stego, ASIR wins on both pixel-space (99.0% vs. 93.2% accuracy; 0.50 vs. 0.42 security) and latent-space (16,384 vs. 4,096 bits capacity, a 4× improvement; 98.8% vs. 96.8% accuracy; 0.50 vs. 0.44 security). Against LDStega (latent-space), ASIR matches capacity (16,384 bits) but substantially improves security from 0.32 to 0.50 and accuracy from 97.6% to 98.8%. In the robustness comparison (Table 4), ASIR embeds 2,070 bits—roughly 8× more than GS and MDDM (256 bits each)—while sustaining comparable resilience (0.99 accuracy) under JPEG-90, resize, and dropout attacks. Computationally, ASIR's 3.9 s decoding time is ~3× faster than Pulsar's 11.8 s and comparable to StegaDDPM's end-to-end latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASIR extends the diffusion-based steganography line initiated by StegaDDPM, Pulsar, and LDStega, which embed messages by modulating noise at specific timesteps in the reverse diffusion process. It departs from prior work on two axes: (1) Antipodal Sampling replaces the random or message-projected noise mapping used by Pulsar and Diffusion-Stego with a structured construction that guarantees maximal separation in probability space via inverse-CDF mapping, and (2) Iterative Recovery reframes extraction as a gradient-based optimization over the differentiable generation pipeline rather than relying on direct comparison or error-correcting codes. The framework is training-free and provably secure (reducing security to CSPRNG hardness), distinguishing it from GAN-based generative steganography that requires retraining and from methods like Diffusion-Stego whose non-standard mappings compromise statistical indistinguishability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ASIR simultaneously achieves high capacity (65,536 bits pixel-space, 16,384 bits latent-space), near-perfect extraction accuracy (99%), and statistical undetectability (P̄_E ≈ 0.50) — a combination no prior diffusion steganography method has matched, as most trade capacity against accuracy or security. The gradient-based Iterative Recovery paradigm, which inverts nonlinear generative distortions by treating the message as a continuous differentiable parameter, is a transferable idea that could benefit other extraction-from-generative-pipeline problems. Key limitations include the symmetric-key requirement (sender and receiver must execute identical diffusion trajectories), sensitivity to execution-environment mismatches, and the fact that robustness under aggressive attacks (e.g., heavy JPEG compression, adversarial perturbations) remains only partially characterized. Open questions include whether the approach extends to text-to-image models with classifier-free guidance, video diffusion, or autoregressive generative models.
