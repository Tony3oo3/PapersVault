## Problem

Self-supervised learning produces powerful but opaque representations, and existing interpretability tools (saliency maps, linear probes, domain-specific architectures) are either post-hoc, task-dependent, or fail to explain the internal mechanics of the learned latent space. Kernel-based interpretability via the Representer Theorem has only been applied to supervised settings, and the quadratic memory / cubic time cost of kernel methods renders them infeasible for the 1M+ unlabeled samples that SSL requires. The paper asks: can we analytically decompose an SSL representation into the influence of specific unlabeled training examples, at scale?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pretrained SSL backbone (ResNet-18/50 for images, a ResMLP with self-attention for tabular data) and its unlabeled training set (up to 1.2M samples with p augmented views per sample), plus a single test sample. It produces a set of m ≪ n representer landmarks with per-landmark dual coefficients, from which three attribution metrics are derived: a Sample-Specific Influence Score (signed kernel-weighted norm per landmark), a Concept-Conditioned Influence Score (projection onto a CAV direction), and a Feature Alignment Gap (aggregated over the test set for tabular feature auditing). Notably, no iterative training occurs—the entire 'learning' is a single deterministic analytical step, and labels are used only for optional downstream linear-probe evaluation, never for the attribution itself.

## Method

KREPES approximates the frozen SSL network by its empirical NTK, then applies the Representer Theorem to express the learned embedding as a finite sum of kernel evaluations to m Nyström landmarks with dual coefficients Ã ∈ R^{mp×h}. A Principal Component Initialization sets Ã₀ = U_h Λ_h^{-1/2} from the rank-h eigendecomposition of the landmark kernel, grounding the surrogate on the unsupervised data manifold so that the subsequent step isolates the SSL objective's contribution. Rather than iterative optimization, KREPES computes a single Generalized Gauss-Newton step ΔÃ = -H̄_GN^{-1} ∇_Ã L(Ã₀), where H_GN = J^T Q J is a PSD curvature surrogate tailored to each non-convex loss (Barlow Twins as nonlinear least squares, SimCLR as row-wise softmax cross-entropy, VICReg as a unified NLLS residual). To scale to 1M+ samples, the linear system H̄_GN ΔÃ = -g is solved via Conjugate Gradient using dynamically computed Hessian-vector products (forward-over-reverse automatic differentiation) streamed over minibatches, reducing complexity from O(n²) to O(n√n) with memory independent of n. Landmark selection uses K-means++ seeding or approximate leverage-score sampling to ensure the Nyström subspace captures the most informative kernel-space directions.

## Results

On the Adult dataset (1M samples), KREPES achieves 87.2% Precision@1 for label consistency of influential landmarks, outperforming the nearest-neighbor baseline (80.9%) by 6.3 points; on the more complex CoverType dataset (1M samples), the gap widens to 22.2 points (77.2% vs. 55.0%). Surrogate faithfulness is validated via Kendall-τ rank correlation between NN and KREPES class probabilities (τ ≥ 0.84 on Adult and CIFAR-10) and inference-time latent ablation, where removing KREPES-identified landmarks causes ~100× larger confidence drops than random deletion. The Nyström-based KREPES scales at O(n√n) versus O(n²) for the full kernel while maintaining parity in downstream accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a geometric nearest-neighbor retrieval in the SSL latent space (cosine similarity), which is the natural alternative for identifying influential training samples. KREPES systematically outperforms this baseline across all label-consistency metrics (Precision@K, Majority@K, Hit Rate@K) on both the Adult and CoverType tabular datasets. The advantage is most pronounced on CoverType, where KREPES achieves 77.2% vs. 55.0% Precision@1 and 99.0% vs. 94.8% Hit Rate@10, indicating that the representer-based attribution captures semantic structure beyond mere geometric proximity. On Adult, the gap is smaller but consistent (87.2% vs. 80.9% Precision@1). The paper also validates KREPES against the full (non-Nyström) kernel computation, showing negligible accuracy degradation while reducing complexity from O(n²) to O(n√n), confirming that the Nyström approximation does not sacrifice interpretability quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

KREPES sits at the intersection of three previously disconnected threads: (1) representer-based interpretability for supervised networks (Yeh et al., 2018; Tsai et al., 2023; Engel et al., 2023), which relies on label gradients to derive dual coefficients; (2) kernel formulations of SSL objectives (Cabannes et al., 2023; Esser et al., 2024; Fleissner et al., 2025), which provide statistical guarantees but were never exploited for interpretability; and (3) Nyström approximation for scalable kernel methods (Rudi et al., 2017; Meanti et al., 2020; Abedsoltan et al., 2023), which handles convex losses but not the non-convex objectives of SimCLR, BYOL, or Barlow Twins. The paper's key departure is replacing the supervised loss gradient with a single Generalized Gauss-Newton step around a kernel-geometric initialization, thereby making the representer framework applicable to arbitrary non-convex SSL losses without labels. The eNTK surrogate (Lee et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2022) and GGN curvature (Korbit et al., 2024) are repurposed from their original contexts (finite-width NN analysis and exact optimization, respectively) to serve as the analytical backbone for influence attribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KREPES provides the first principled, label-free auditing tool for SSL latent spaces that scales to 1M+ samples, with concrete practical impact demonstrated through fairness auditing (demographic proxy dominance in Adult, cross-demographic conflation in FairFace) and unsupervised hyperparameter selection. The main limitation is the reliance on eNTK as a faithful surrogate for finite-width networks, which the paper validates empirically but does not bound theoretically; the single GGN step is a first-order approximation whose quality degrades as the SSL objective departs from local convexity. Open questions include whether the framework extends to generative SSL (diffusion, masked modeling), whether multi-step or iterative GGN updates yield materially different landmark sets, and whether the Nyström subspace can be adapted online as new data arrives.
