## Problem

Projection-free methods such as Frank-Wolfe are well-established for standard constrained minimization, but their development for constrained smooth saddle-point (minimax) problems has lagged significantly, with existing single-loop projection-free algorithms requiring restrictive geometric assumptions (e.g., strongly convex constraint sets, bilinear coupling) or failing to provide non-asymptotic guarantees for general nonconvex-concave settings. This paper bridges the gap by introducing a unified framework for single-loop minimax algorithms that replace expensive projection steps with linear minimization oracles, delivering anytime convergence guarantees without requiring a pre-specified iteration horizon.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a smooth payoff function L(x,y) over compact convex sets X ⊂ R^{d_x} and Y ⊂ R^{d_y}, with oracle access restricted to either a linear minimization oracle (LMO) or a projection oracle (PO) on each set. The output is a sequence of iterates (x_t, y_t) whose quality is certified through stationarity gap measures: a primal gap G_X (either LMO-based or PO-based depending on the oracle) and a dual gap G_Y, which together bound the saddle-point duality gap for ergodic averages. A key practical feature is that the smoothing parameter β_t and stepsize τ_t follow open-loop schedules (powers of t), so the algorithm converges at any stopping time without needing to know the target accuracy in advance.

## Method

The core mechanism is a dual dynamic smoothing framework that regularizes the payoff as L_β(x,y) = L(x,y) − (β/2)‖y − y_0‖², rendering the primal objective f_β(x) = max_y L_β(x,y) smooth and differentiable via Danskin's theorem. This avoids the inner maximization that would create a double-loop structure: instead, a single oracle query (LMO or PO) is applied directly to the regularized payoff at each iteration, with the dual update using the gradient of L_β and an adaptive closed-loop stepsize that maximizes a quadratic lower bound. Three algorithmic variants are derived from this template—LMO-LMO (fully projection-free), LMO-PO, and PO-LMO—each analyzed through a discrepancy term H_t that tracks the dual iterate's distance from the regularized optimum. The analysis yields per-iteration recursive bounds that telescope into anytime convergence rates, with the specific exponents determined by balancing the primal stepsize decay τ_t = Θ(t^{−a}) against the smoothing decay β_t = Θ(t^{−b}), and further improved when the dual set Y is strongly convex.

## Results

In the nonconvex-concave (NC-C) setting, the proposed LMO-LMO algorithm achieves O(ε^{-5}) iterations (improving over the O(ε^{-6}) of R-PDCG) when the dual set Y is strongly convex, and O(ε^{-6}) without that assumption. For the NC-SC setting, the rate improves to O(ε^{-3}) with SC Y (vs. O(ε^{-4}) for R-PDCG). The novel PO-LMO variant matches these improved rates. Numerically, LMO-PO achieves the fastest convergence on 2 of 3 datasets (RCV1, NEWS20), while LMO-LMO significantly outperforms R-PDCG and CG-RPGA which plateau prematurely; the LMO oracle also enables nearly 2× more iterations than PO in the dictionary learning experiment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against R-PDCG (Boroun et al., 2023), the proposed LMO-LMO algorithm is structurally identical but achieves a strictly better rate: O(ε^{-5}) vs. O(ε^{-6}) for NC-C and O(ε^{-3}) vs. O(ε^{-4}) for NC-SC when Y is strongly convex, while also providing anytime convergence without requiring knowledge of Y's strong convexity constant. R-PDCG is entirely inapplicable to the RCV1 and NEWS20 experiments because the dual feasible set (simplex) is not strongly convex. Against CG-RPGA (Boroun et al., 2023), the LMO-PO variant matches the O(ε^{-4})/O(ε^{-2}) rates but adds anytime guarantees; numerically, LMO-PO converges faster and more stably than CG-RPGA on all three datasets. Against AGP (Xu et al., 2023), which uses a PO oracle for the primal variable, the proposed methods consistently show faster convergence, with AGP and PO-LMO exhibiting only marginal improvement over time. SP-FW (Gidel et al., 2017) is limited to specialized settings (interior solutions, strongly convex sets, or bilinear coupling) and does not provide guarantees for the general C-C case addressed here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the projection-free minimax optimization line initiated by Hammond (1984) and Gidel et al. (2017), and most directly builds on Boroun et al. (2023), whose R-PDCG and CG-RPGA algorithms share structural similarities with the proposed LMO-LMO and LMO-PO variants. The key departure is a unified dual dynamic smoothing framework that provides anytime convergence guarantees without requiring a pre-specified iteration horizon, and introduces a novel PO-LMO hybrid not previously explored in the literature. The smoothing technique draws on Nesterov (2005) and Beck & Teboulle (2012), adapted here to the asymmetric minimax setting where the primal variable may be nonconvex.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The anytime convergence property is practically significant: practitioners no longer need to pre-specify a target accuracy or total iteration budget to tune stepsizes, making the algorithms more robust in deployment. The framework narrows the performance gap between projection-free and projection-based minimax methods, particularly when the dual set is strongly convex (e.g., O(ε^{-5}) vs. O(ε^{-6}) for NC-C). Limitations include not exploiting bilinear or Lagrangian structure to recover specialized optimal rates, and the analysis is restricted to deterministic, single-loop settings. Open questions include whether the framework extends to stochastic or distributed minimax problems, and whether the established rates are information-theoretically optimal.
