## Problem

Existing EEG generation methods rely on discrete denoising objectives (GANs, diffusion models) that are fundamentally misaligned with the continuous temporal dynamics, power-law spectral structure, and heavy-tailed amplitude distributions of neural signals. This mismatch causes spectral bias, temporally static generations, and poor coverage of extreme pathological events, limiting the utility of synthetic EEG for large-scale neural modeling.

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard Gaussian noise sample x₀ ~ N(0, I) and a discrete class label c (pathological state, e.g., seizure vs. background or one of six event types) as input, and produces a 16-channel raw EEG waveform of length T (2000 samples at 200 Hz for TUAB, 1000 for TUEV/TUSZ) by integrating the learned vector field via ODE from t=0 to t=1. Unlike prior approaches that operate on time-frequency representations or predefined graph structures, JET processes raw continuous sequences directly, with signals pre-scaled by 0.01 for numerical stability within the flow matching framework.

## Method

JET formulates EEG generation as conditional flow matching, learning a time-dependent vector field v_θ(x_t, t, c) that transports Gaussian noise to the EEG data distribution along continuous trajectories, replacing discrete denoising steps. The backbone is a standard Transformer with patch-based tokenization (P=200) that preserves channel identity during embedding, enabling self-attention to capture long-range temporal dependencies and inter-channel interactions without locality assumptions. Conditioning on diffusion time and class label is injected via adaptive layer normalization (following DiT/JiT). The training objective combines an L1 reconstruction loss (Laplacian prior for robustness to impulsive artifacts), a statistical consistency term matching per-channel mean and standard deviation, and a spatiotemporal structure term combining temporal total variation (suppressing spurious high-frequency noise) with Pearson correlation alignment (preserving waveform morphology independent of amplitude scaling). An adaptive class-balanced sampling strategy with probability p_i ∝ 1/N_c^α mitigates the severe class imbalance in clinical EEG data.

## Results

JET achieves state-of-the-art TS-FID across all three TUH benchmarks: 188.27 (TUAB), 235.86 (TUEV), and 151.27 (TUSZ), representing a 42–47% reduction over the strongest baseline (EEG-GAN). Silhouette scores reach 0.983–0.995, indicating near-perfect conditional cluster separation, while downstream utility (ΔAcc on CbraMod) is consistently positive (+0.017 to +0.032), confirming that generated samples improve classifier performance rather than introducing distributional noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JET outperforms both baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against EEG-GAN, JET reduces TS-FID by 42% (TUAB), 47% (TUEV), and 45% (TUSZ), while raising Silhouette scores from 0.67–0.89 to 0.98–1.00 and turning a near-zero or negative ΔAcc into a consistent positive gain of +0.017 to +0.032. Against Vanilla Diffusion (DDIM), the TS-FID gap is even larger (45–53% reduction), and the Silhouette improvement is most pronounced on TUSZ (0.746 → 0.987), where seizure dynamics are most non-stationary. Notably, both baselines show negligible or negative downstream accuracy gains, indicating their generated samples introduce distributional shifts that hinder CbraMod, whereas JET's synthetic data reliably improves classification. In terms of inference speed, JET (4.78 s per batch of 32) is faster than Vanilla Diffusion (7.01 s) and only marginally slower than EEG-GAN (4.12 s), which itself yields substantially lower fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

JET builds directly on the conditional flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022) and the raw-sequence Transformer design popularized by DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and JiT (Li & He, 2025), transplanting these vision-domain ingredients into EEG synthesis. It departs from the dominant EEG generation lineage—GAN-based (EEG-GAN, 2018) and discrete-denoising diffusion approaches—by arguing that iterative denoising under isotropic Gaussian noise is fundamentally misaligned with the power-law spectra, non-stationary dynamics, and heavy-tailed amplitudes of neural signals. The principled constraints (Laplacian reconstruction, moment matching, total-variation and correlation regularizers) are individually known from signal processing and robust statistics but are combined here as a structural regularizer specifically motivated by EEG biophysics, distinguishing the work from generic flow-matching applications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

High-fidelity EEG generation is a prerequisite for scaling brain foundation models, which are currently bottlenecked by the cost, scarcity, and privacy constraints of clinical recordings; JET's 40%+ TS-FID reduction and positive downstream-utility gains make synthetic EEG a more viable augmentation tool. However, the evaluation is limited to 16 channels, 5–10-second windows, and only two baselines (a 2018 GAN and an unmodified diffusion model), leaving open whether the advantage persists against stronger flow-matching or latent-diffusion competitors. Broader questions remain: whether the constraint set generalizes to other neural modalities (MEG, ECoG, intracranial), whether longer multi-minute sequences expose drift that the current short-window evaluation masks, and whether the class-balanced sampling strategy scales to the long-tail pathology distributions seen in real clinical corpora.
