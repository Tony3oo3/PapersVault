## Problem

Conventional geometric deep learning hardcodes known symmetries into architectures, while existing symmetry discovery methods either operate post-hoc, decouple discovery from prediction, or lack structural interpretability of the learned invariance. This paper addresses the joint problem of learning an unknown function and simultaneously discovering the governing one-parameter Lie subgroup from data, with invariance guaranteed by construction rather than emerging empirically.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262055/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw data vectors x ∈ R^n (or dense matrices M ∈ R^{n×n} for the parabolic task) and a learnable orientation matrix A projects them into subgroup-aligned 2D block coordinates. The output is twofold: a task prediction (regression value or class label) produced by an unconstrained backbone network φ, and the discovered Lie algebra generator fully specified by the learned orientation A and canonical rate parameters {λ_k}, from which the physical symmetry (rotation axis, boost direction, or shear plane) can be directly read off.

## Method

The core mechanism is a regime-specific invariant representation layer (invRep_e, invRep_h, invRep_p) that canonicalizes each input by using a designated pivot 2D block to solve for a unique gauge parameter t_0, then applying the inverse one-parameter group transformation to all remaining blocks, yielding an orbit-separating invariant. The predictive model is constrained to the form f = φ ∘ invRep, where φ is an unconstrained MLP or ResNet, and Theorem 3.7 proves this is the necessary and sufficient functional form for any H_γ-invariant function. Training jointly optimizes the backbone weights, orientation matrix A, and rate parameters λ via standard supervised loss, so the generator is recovered end-to-end. The geometric regime (elliptic, hyperbolic, or parabolic) is a modeling assumption that selects the canonical block structure and admissible domain, while the framework handles separably mixed generators by applying the same global t_0 with block-type-specific transformations.

## Results

On synthetic regression tasks, Hγ-Net achieves near-perfect generator recovery (cosine similarity 1.000±0.000) and reduces invariance error by 1–3 orders of magnitude compared to Augerino (e.g., (0.9±0.4)×10⁻⁵ vs (4.7±4.8)×10⁻⁴ on Double Pendulum). On the Invariant Polynomial task, Hγ-Net reduces prediction MSE from (1.7±1.4)×10⁰ to (1.0±0.0)×10⁻⁵. On Top Quark Tagging, Hγ-Net (hyperbolic) achieves 84.4±0.4% accuracy versus 74.9±2.4% for Augerino, while recovering the boost generator with cosine similarity 0.996±0.003.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hγ-Net outperforms Augerino (Benton et al., 2020) on every metric across all three synthetic regression tasks, with the most dramatic gains in invariance error (up to 3 orders of magnitude) and generator recovery (cosine 1.000 vs 0.616 on Angled Sine). On Top Quark Tagging, Hγ-Net (hyperbolic) achieves 84.4% accuracy versus Augerino's 74.9%, a 9.5-point improvement. Against LieGAN (Yang et al., 2023), Hγ-Net achieves cosine similarity 1.000 vs 0.828 on the Angled Sine task, and also surpasses it in sample efficiency across dataset sizes. Compared to LieGG (Moskalev et al., 2022), Hγ-Net improves cosine similarity from 0.690 to 1.000 on Angled Sine and from 0.956 to 1.000 on Top Quark Tagging, while also reducing Double Pendulum MSE from 0.0164 to 2.0×10⁻⁴. BeyondAffine (Shaw et al., 2024) performs worst among post-hoc methods, achieving only 0.509 cosine similarity on Top Quark Tagging and 0.795 accuracy, confirming that joint architecture-level symmetry enforcement substantially outperforms post-hoc extraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in geometric deep learning (Cohen & Welling 2016, Finzi et al. 2020) where symmetries are hardcoded, and from prior symmetry-discovery methods (Augerino, LieGAN, LieGG, BeyondAffine) that either operate post-hoc, decouple discovery from prediction, or lack structural invariance guarantees. Its conceptual lineage draws on the classical Lie-theoretic classification of one-parameter subgroups into elliptic, hyperbolic, and parabolic regimes, but repurposes this taxonomy as an architectural design principle: regime-specific invariant representation layers perform explicit gauge-fixing (canonicalization) that is provably orbit-separating. The key departure from Augerino—the closest prior joint-discovery method—is that invariance here is enforced by construction through the invariant layer rather than approximated via learned augmentation distributions, and the framework extends to non-compact (boost and shear) symmetries that prior methods largely ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable, structurally interpretable invariance (with formal orbit-separation proofs) addresses a real gap: prior discovery methods yield symmetries that are verified empirically and cannot be localized to specific architectural components, limiting their use in safety-critical scientific applications. The extension to hyperbolic and parabolic regimes is practically important for physics (Lorentz boosts in HEP, shear in fluid dynamics) where most prior equivariant/discovery work is restricted to compact groups. Key limitations include the a-priori regime specification (elliptic vs. hyperbolic vs. parabolic must be chosen by the user), the restriction to 2-nilpotent parabolic generators, and the single-pivot canonicalization that can be ill-conditioned near admissible-domain boundaries. Open questions include whether joint regime inference is feasible without sacrificing closed-form invariance, how the framework scales to genuinely high-dimensional non-abelian algebras beyond the bracket-closure heuristic, and whether the measure-zero exclusions matter in finite-sample regimes.
