## Problem

SAM2 suffers from Mask-level Confidence Confusion (MCC) under domain shift, where a single mask-level score cannot distinguish reliable from unreliable pixels, and from Uncertainty-Accuracy (UA) shift, where pixel-wise uncertainty learned on the source domain no longer correlates with prediction errors on out-of-domain data. No existing method simultaneously improves segmentation accuracy and provides calibrated pixel-wise uncertainty under single-source domain generalization without requiring target-domain annotations.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and sparse point prompts (positive/negative pixel coordinates) and produces a binary segmentation mask alongside a pixel-wise uncertainty map in [0,1]. Uncertainty is computed at two granularities: per-pixel (from spatially-varying Weibull parameters over image tokens, capturing boundary and occlusion ambiguity) and per-mask-hypothesis (from Weibull parameters over SAM2's four mask tokens, capturing semantic ambiguity). At inference, only the Bayesian mask decoder runs in analytic mode (closed-form expectation via MacKay's probit approximation), requiring no ground-truth masks or attack computation.

## Method

RUAC replaces SAM2's deterministic mask decoder with a Bayesian decoder that models both image tokens and mask tokens with Weibull variational posteriors, propagating weight uncertainty to pixel-wise semantic uncertainty via inner-product logits and MacKay's probit approximation. The key training innovation is Adversarial Uncertainty Estimation (AUE): a style adversarial network (AdaIN-based per-object color perturbation coordinated by a 2-layer GCN for multi-object consistency) and a deformation adversarial network (dense offset field applied via differentiable grid sampling, with the same flow warping the ground-truth mask) are trained via Gradient Reversal Layers to generate bio-inspired perturbations targeting texture and shape simultaneously. Rather than optimizing worst-case robustness, the adversarial objective includes a calibration loss with dual stop-gradient that routes gradients through both the prediction-error channel (training the attacker to find miscalibrated samples) and the uncertainty channel (training the decoder to align uncertainty with error), ensuring the model assigns high uncertainty precisely where it fails under appearance and geometric shifts.

## Results

RUAC achieves the highest average J&F score of 81.62 across 23 zero-shot OOD domains, surpassing the strongest generic augmentation baseline (Random Noise, 80.81) and the Bayesian decoder alone (Bayes-SAM2, 79.87). On uncertainty calibration, RUAC attains 63.72 OOD PAvPU (vs. 55.40 for Bayes-SAM2, barely above the ~50 random baseline) and the best AURC (0.092) and ECE (0.117) among all methods. Notably, RUAC is the only method that simultaneously improves segmentation accuracy and all three calibration metrics (PAvPU, AURC, ECE) over the no-augmentation Bayes-SAM2 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RUAC outperforms all six compared baselines on average J&F across 23 OOD domains. Against the zero-shot SAM2 baseline (67.75), RUAC gains +13.87 points, with the largest per-domain improvement on PIDRay (+6.1 over SAM2-FT) where texture and shape priors differ most from natural images. The evidential uncertainty baseline UR-ERN (73.40) lags RUAC by 8.22 points, confirming that standard evidential heads degrade under domain shift. Among generic augmentation methods built on the same Bayesian decoder, Random Noise (80.81), Patch (80.60), MixStyle (80.37), DSU (80.35), and PGD (79.90) all fall short of RUAC (81.62) in J&F. Critically, RUAC is the only method that simultaneously improves all three calibration metrics (PAvPU 63.72, AURC 0.092, ECE 0.117) over the no-augmentation Bayes-SAM2 (55.40, 0.102, 0.123), whereas generic augmentations like PGD and MixStyle actually worsen PAvPU and ECE relative to Bayes-SAM2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SAM2's promptable segmentation architecture and adapts the Bayesian Non-negative Decision Layer (BNDL) for pixel-wise uncertainty via Weibull variational posteriors. It departs from prior adversarial domain-generalization work (AdvStyle, DG-Font) by repurposing style and deformation perturbations not for worst-case robustness but specifically to align uncertainty with prediction error under domain shift. The key conceptual departure from standard adversarial training is the use of Gradient Reversal Layers to jointly optimize attack networks and a Bayesian decoder in a single backward pass, targeting calibration rather than ℓp-bounded robustness. The paper also positions itself against fine-tuning-based SAM adaptations (RobustSAM, BLO-SAM, Medical SAM2) by maintaining the single-source domain generalization philosophy without target-domain annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'UA shift'—where uncertainty-accuracy correlation collapses under domain shift even when a Bayesian decoder is present—is practically important for safety-critical deployment of foundation segmentation models, as it means users cannot trust uncertainty estimates on OOD data. The finding that generic augmentations (PGD, MixStyle, diffusion-based) fail to fix this while bio-inspired style-deformation adversarial training succeeds provides a principled design guideline. Limitations include evaluation only on SAM2 in single-frame mode, a modest PAvPU gain (+8.3 over Bayes-SAM2), and reliance on ground-truth masks during training for the attack networks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video tracking, SAM3, or vision-language models, and whether the Weibull-based decoder generalizes beyond the specific SAM2 architecture.
