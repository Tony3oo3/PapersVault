## Problem

Existing hyperparameter transfer frameworks (μP, CompleteP) provide scaling rules for dense transformers but do not account for MoE-specific dimensions such as the number of experts, expert hidden size, and router weights. Without a principled parameterization, practitioners must re-tune learning rates and initialization scales at every combination of width, depth, expert count, and expert size, making MoE model scaling prohibitively expensive and unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261902/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of model architecture dimensions (embedding width n_embd, depth L, number of experts n_exp, expert hidden multiplier α_ffn) and a base model's tuned hyperparameters (per-group learning rates and initialization standard deviations) at a small scale. It produces a complete set of scaled hyperparameters for a target model via closed-form exponent rules applied to each parameter group (router, expert bias, expert up/down projections), and when applied, yields a trained decoder-only MoE language model. The scaling is performed at fixed sparsity κ = n_act/n_exp, and the parameterization is validated on 1B-token fixed-budget sweeps and longer-horizon runs up to ~2B total parameters.

## Method

The paper extends the CompleteP parameterization to MoE layers by deriving max-update (μP) desiderata for each individual expert component (mixing coefficient, expert output, hidden activations) under Law-of-Large-Numbers alignment assumptions, yielding explicit scaling exponents: router LR ∝ n_embd^{-1}, expert up LR ∝ n_embd^{-1}, expert down LR ∝ n_embd^{-1}α_ffn^{-1}, and corresponding init stds. A novel dynamical mean-field theory (DMFT) analysis in the joint limit of infinite width, depth, expert count, and expert size proves that the resulting training dynamics converge to a scale-invariant three-level hierarchy (residual stream neurons → expert outputs → within-expert neurons) that depends only on the sparsity κ and not on α_ffn or n_exp individually. The key design choice is scaling n_exp and n_act jointly at fixed κ rather than fixing n_act, which the DMFT shows is necessary for a well-defined mean-field limit. Empirically, the parameterization enables zero-shot transfer of optimal base LR and init std from a 38M-activated base model to models up to ~2B total parameters across width, depth, expert count, and expert size, with loss-curve collapse in early training and uniform expert load balancing preserved at all scales.

## Results

The paper demonstrates reliable hyperparameter transfer (base learning rate and initialization std.) from a 38M activated-parameter base model up to ~2B total parameters (50× base) across width, depth, number of experts, and expert hidden size, using a fixed 1B-token budget (2000 steps × 500K tokens). At matching active parameter count (124M, GPT-2-small architecture), the MoE model trained with zero-shot transferred HPs achieves competitive validation loss on FineWeb compared to dense GPT-2 speedrun baselines targeting 3.28 val. loss at 10B tokens. Loss curves in early iterations collapse across all scaling dimensions (scale invariance), and increasing expert count over expert size at fixed total parameters consistently improves final performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its MoE model (with zero-shot HPs transferred from a 38M base) against two dense GPT-2 baselines at matching active parameter count (124M) on FineWeb with 500K batch size: (1) Karpathy's Nano-GPT dense GPT-2-small baseline, and (2) Jordan's modded-nanogpt speedrun using AdamW and Muon optimizers. The MoE model achieves competitive (comparable or slightly better) validation loss trajectories against both dense baselines, despite having significantly more total parameters (due to inactive experts). The comparison is designed to isolate the benefit of the MoE architecture and the HP transfer recipe, since the per-token compute (active parameters) is held constant. The paper notes that dense benchmarks are taken before October 14, 2024, before more advanced architecture modifications (zero down-projection init, QK-norms) were applied to the speedrun, making the MoE result particularly notable. At the larger GPT-2-medium scale (355M activated, Figure 15), the MoE model also outperforms the tuned llm.c dense baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CompleteP parameterization (Dey et al., 2025) for dense transformers to sparse Mixture-of-Experts architectures, adding scaling rules for expert count, expert hidden size, and router weights while preserving the max-update (μP) desiderata. The theoretical justification goes beyond the heuristic μP dimensional analysis used in prior work by deriving a novel three-level dynamical mean-field theory (DMFT) hierarchy—residual stream neurons, expert outputs, and within-expert neurons—that proves the limiting training dynamics are independent of the FFN ratio and expert count at fixed sparsity. The paper also departs from the common auxiliary-loss load-balancing approach (Shazeer et al., 2017; Fedus et al., 2022) in favor of the auxiliary-loss-free bias-update strategy (Wang et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024), which simplifies the theoretical analysis by localizing balancing gradients to a single parameter group.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners can now transfer optimal learning rates and initialization scales from a 38M-activated base model to ~2B total-parameter MoE models across width, depth, expert count, and expert size without re-tuning, saving substantial compute. The DMFT result that dynamics are invariant to the FFN ratio provides a theoretical guarantee for transfer across expert hidden width that heuristic μP analysis alone cannot supply. Key open questions remain: whether the parameterization extends to longer (compute-optimal) training horizons, whether a rigorous DMFT treatment of Adam or Muon is feasible, and what the correct compute-optimal scaling laws are for MoE models given their FLOP-parameter decoupling.
