## Problem

Existing low-rank zeroth-order (ZO) fine-tuning methods such as LOZO estimate gradients within randomly sampled fixed-rank subspaces, which provides no guarantee of alignment with the actual dominant gradient subspaces of LLMs. This stochastic subspace sampling limits the ability to consistently exploit effective descent directions, resulting in suboptimal convergence compared to first-order methods. The paper asks: can ZO optimization be made to adaptively select low-rank subspaces that better capture the true gradient structure, without requiring backpropagation?

## Input / Output

The method consumes pretrained LLM weights (Qwen2.5-7B/32B, OPT-13B/66B) and 1,000 labeled training examples per task, requiring only forward passes (no activation storage or backward graph). Memory efficiency is maintained by storing only a random seed for perturbation regeneration and a small coefficient matrix V_original ∈ R^{r×n} (r ≪ min{m,n}) rather than full perturbation matrices. The output is fine-tuned LLM weights achieving inference-level memory footprint (~25–40 GB on A100) while matching or exceeding first-order fine-tuning accuracy on GLUE/SuperGLUE benchmarks.

## Method

LOZO+ replaces random subspace sampling with a greedy low-rank subspace selection mechanism that operates every ν iterations. At each greedy interval, the algorithm computes the loss reduction attributable to each basis direction u_i by evaluating F(W_{kν}) − F(W_{kν} + u_i · V_update[i,:]), then retains only directions whose contribution exceeds an adaptive threshold ψ. Retained directions form U_keep, and new random orthogonal directions fill the remaining slots to maintain a full rank-r basis. This progressively refines the subspace toward better alignment with the dominant gradient subspace, increasing the alignment metric ρ_t. Theoretically, the convergence bound scales as 1/ρ², and the paper proves that greedy selection yields strictly improved ρ compared to random sampling, giving a tighter convergence guarantee of O(1/√T) under standard smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B, LOZO+ achieves 83.3% average accuracy across 11 GLUE/SuperGLUE tasks, outperforming LOZO (82.2%), MeZO (80.9%), SubZero (80.9%), and HiZOO (80.4%), while remaining close to full fine-tuning with Adam (85.3%). On OPT-13B, LOZO+ reaches 72.3% average, surpassing LOZO (70.5%) and MeZO (68.9%) and nearly matching FT (72.5%). On the larger Qwen2.5-32B and OPT-66B models, LOZO+ achieves 86.8% and 76.0% average respectively, consistently leading all ZO baselines by 1.5–3.1 points. Memory usage stays at ~33 GB (Qwen2.5-7B) and ~41 GB (OPT-13B), comparable to other ZO methods and roughly 3× lower than FT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOZO+ consistently outperforms all ZO baselines across four model scales (Qwen2.5-7B/32B, OPT-13B/66B). Against the closest ZO competitor LOZO, LOZO+ gains +1.1 pts on Qwen2.5-7B (83.3 vs 82.2), +1.8 pts on OPT-13B (72.3 vs 70.5), +1.5 pts on Qwen2.5-32B (86.8 vs 85.3), and +1.3 pts on OPT-66B (76.0 vs 74.7). Against MeZO, the gap widens to +2.4 pts (Qwen2.5-7B) and +3.4 pts (OPT-13B), while SubZero and HiZOO trail by 2.4 and 2.9 pts respectively on Qwen2.5-7B. Compared to the first-order FT baseline (Adam), LOZO+ is within 2.0 pts on Qwen2.5-7B (83.3 vs 85.3) and within 0.2 pts on OPT-13B (72.3 vs 72.5), while using roughly 3× less GPU memory (33.1 GB vs 100.4 GB). On the additional GSM8K reasoning task, LOZO+ (59.0%) substantially outperforms MeZO (33.0%) and LOZO (49.0%), though it still lags FT (77.0%). Wall-clock time is nearly identical to LOZO (e.g., 2h16m vs 2h15m on SST-2), confirming the greedy selection adds negligible overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the low-rank zeroth-order optimization line initiated by LOZO (Chen et al., 2024) and SubZero (Yu et al., 2025), which restrict ZO gradient estimation to randomly sampled fixed-rank subspaces to match the low-rank structure of LLM gradients. The key departure is replacing stochastic subspace sampling with a greedy selection mechanism that uses loss-based feedback to evaluate each basis direction's contribution to descent and an adaptive threshold to retain only effective directions. The SPSA finite-difference estimator and the MeZO memory-efficient implementation (seed-based perturbation regeneration) are inherited directly, while the convergence analysis builds on standard L-smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions from the ZO literature (Ghadimi & Lan, 2013; Nesterov & Spokoiny, 2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable strict improvement over random subspace sampling (Theorem 5.4, with the 1/ρ² scaling) gives practitioners a principled justification for the greedy mechanism beyond empirical observation, and the 30–40% activation rate across tasks confirms the mechanism is non-trivial in practice. However, the practical gains remain modest (1–2 points over LOZO on average), the additional hyperparameters (ψ, ν) require per-task tuning, and the convergence proof relies on the subspace similarity ρ being bounded away from zero—a condition that is guaranteed only when the greedy threshold falls within a theoretically derived interval that is hard to verify a priori. Open questions include whether the greedy selection generalizes to non-LLM or multimodal settings, whether the interval-based evaluation can be replaced by continuous monitoring, and whether the approach composes with other ZO improvements (e.g., Hessian-informed scaling in HiZOO).
