## Problem

Monocular surface normal estimation for transparent objects (beakers, pipettes, culture dishes) remains unsolved because refraction and reflection decouple local appearance from underlying geometry, causing conventional discriminative and diffusion-based methods to be misled by background textures visible through the object. Existing diffusion-based dense prediction methods further underutilize their cross-attention conditioning pathway by relying on empty or generic text prompts, leaving the semantic-geometric guidance channel largely unexploited for resolving the inherent ambiguities of transparent surfaces.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (evaluated at 518×518) and produces a dense per-pixel surface normal map of the same spatial resolution (H×W×3). Inference is a single forward pass through the U-Net at a fixed diffusion timestep T, requiring no iterative denoising. The DINOv3 encoder processes the input at patch size 16, and the VAE encoder/decoder operate at 8× spatial downsampling in a 4-channel latent space.

## Method

TransNormal repurposes the Stable Diffusion 2 U-Net for single-step normal regression by replacing the text conditioning pathway with dense visual semantics from a frozen DINOv3 encoder, projected via a trainable linear layer and injected as keys/values in cross-attention at every U-Net layer (spatial features serve as queries). A dual-task mechanism with fixed task embeddings simultaneously trains normal prediction and RGB reconstruction to prevent catastrophic forgetting of the pretrained generative priors. Training uses a composite loss combining latent-space L2 reconstruction for both tasks and a wavelet-based regularization that decomposes normals via 2D Haar transform, enforcing low-frequency (LL) fidelity globally and high-frequency (HF) alignment only at edges selected by a gradient-derived mask. The model is fully fine-tuned (not LoRA) on ~122K synthetic samples from four datasets, with the VAE and DINOv3 encoder kept frozen throughout.

## Results

On the ClearGrasp synthetic benchmark, TransNormal achieves a mean angular error of 16.1°, a 25.5% relative improvement over the previous best (Lotus-G at 21.7°), and 52.5% accuracy at the strict 11.25° threshold (24.7% improvement over the prior best of 42.1% by Diff-E2E-FT). On the real-world ClearPose dataset (zero-shot, not in training), TransNormal reduces mean error to 25.5° (17.7% improvement over Diception's 31.0°) and reaches 71.2% at the 30° threshold. On the proposed TransNormal-Synthetic benchmark, it achieves 3.9° mean error and 93.9% at 11.25°, outperforming all baselines. TransNormal attains an average rank of 1.0 across all nine metric-dataset combinations, using only ~122K synthetic training samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TransNormal outperforms all 12 baselines across every benchmark and metric. On ClearGrasp, it beats the strongest diffusion-based competitor Lotus-G (21.7°) by 5.6° in mean error and the strongest transformer-based method MoGe-2 (26.6°) by 10.5°, while also surpassing the discriminative method DSINE (25.7°) by 9.6°. On the real-world ClearPose benchmark (zero-shot), TransNormal (25.5°) improves over Diception (31.0°) by 5.5° and Lotus-D (31.3°) by 5.8°, with a particularly large gap over opaque-object methods like Omnidata V2 (51.7°) and DSINE (40.2°). The transparent-object-specific method RFTrans (Tang et al., 2024) is also compared in the appendix and fails catastrophically (33–55° mean error) due to overfitting to its narrow 5K training set and reliance on RGB-D input, whereas TransNormal uses RGB-only with zero-shot evaluation. Notably, TransNormal achieves these gains with only ~122K training samples (~1.4% of MoGe-2's 8.9M), and the wavelet loss design does not degrade performance on general non-transparent benchmarks (NYUv2, ScanNet, iBIMS), confirming no over-smoothing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based dense prediction paradigm (Marigold, GeoWizard, StableNormal, Lotus) by identifying that prior methods leave the cross-attention conditioning pathway underutilized with empty or generic text prompts. The key architectural departure is replacing CLIP text conditioning with dense DINOv3 visual semantics, transforming cross-attention into a semantic-geometric guidance channel that resolves the appearance-geometry decoupling inherent to transparent surfaces. The single-step regression formulation and dual-task (normal + RGB) switcher are inherited from Lotus (He et al., 2025), while the wavelet edge-aware regularization is a novel loss design specific to the geometric structure of transparent labware.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Transparent object normal estimation is a critical bottleneck for embodied AI in laboratory automation, where refraction and reflection cause standard depth/normal sensors to fail catastrophically. The demonstration that ~122K synthetic samples (1.4% of MoGe-2's 8.9M) with proper semantic conditioning can achieve zero-shot SOTA on real-world benchmarks suggests a data-efficient path for specialized geometric perception. Open questions include whether multi-step denoising could further improve accuracy and enable uncertainty estimation, and whether the DINOv3 conditioning strategy generalizes to other challenging material classes (specular, subsurface scattering) beyond transparency.
