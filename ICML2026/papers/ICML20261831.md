## Problem

Inferring mechanistic latent states (e.g., graphs, sets, RNA isoforms) from indirect observations is intractable with classical expectation maximization when the latent state space is combinatorially large, and variational autoencoders learn artificial latent representations rather than reconstructing the true mechanistic states. The paper asks whether policy learning can be repurposed to directly maximize the observation data likelihood over discrete combinatorial latent state spaces, bridging the gap between RL's sequential generation capability and probabilistic modeling's likelihood-based objectives.

## Input / Output

The method requires indirect observations X_i (random walk endpoints, noisy element measurements, or short-read RNA-seq junction read counts) plus a known observation likelihood Pr(X|S) that links each observation to any candidate latent state. The latent states are discrete combinatorial objects—directed graphs, binary set vectors, or sequences of genomic junction tuples—generated sequentially by a policy. The output is a learned generative distribution Pr(S|θ) over latent states and, for inference, the predicted state Ŝ_i = argmax_S Pr(X_i|S)Pr(S|θ) for each observation, with isoform proportions estimated per sequencing sample.

## Method

GReinSS applies standard policy gradient updates to a generative policy that sequentially constructs latent states, but uses a dynamically rescaled reward r(τ) = Σ_i Pr(X_i|τ)/Pr(X_i|θ) that depends on the current policy parameters θ. Theorem 3.1 proves this reward makes the policy gradient an unbiased estimator of the data log-likelihood gradient ∂/∂θ log Pr(X_{1:N}|θ), so each update performs gradient ascent on the true observation likelihood rather than a proxy objective. The denominator Pr(X_i|θ) rescales each observation's contribution so trajectories are rewarded by their proportional contribution to the marginal likelihood, preventing collapse onto a single high-reward trajectory and instead balancing probability mass across observations. Off-policy sampling biased toward the variance-minimizing proposal q(τ|X_{1:N},θ) = (1/N)Σ_i Pr(τ|X_i,θ) with importance sampling corrections enables practical training when on-policy sampling rarely produces informative trajectories. Mini-batching over observations preserves unbiasedness, enabling scaling to large datasets.

## Results

On simulated process graph inference with k=10 random walks, GReinSS achieves a median F1 score of 0.891, while all baselines remain below 0.55. On simulated subset inference with |U|=1000 and σ=0.3, GReinSS reaches a median F1 of 0.938 versus 0.869 for local search and 0.849 for naive policy gradients, with all GEM-based baselines (VAE, autoregression, diffusion) dropping below 0.4. On the GTEx RNA isoform task across 14,390 genes, GReinSS achieves a median error of 0.0069 versus 0.471 for RSEM (on the MBD2 example gene), with a median error difference of −0.0405 across all genes; GReinSS outperforms RSEM by ≥0.05 on 46.6% of genes while RSEM outperforms GReinSS by ≥0.05 on only 9.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GReinSS outperforms all five baseline families across both simulation tasks. Against the GEM-based baselines (VAE, autoregression, discrete diffusion), GReinSS achieves substantially higher F1 scores, particularly at scale: at |U|=1000, GEM methods collapse to F1<0.4 while GReinSS maintains 0.938, indicating the GEM framework's inexact E-step is a fundamental bottleneck. Against the policy learning baselines, naive policy gradients consistently predict an empty graph (F1≈0 on graph inference) and GFlowNets remain below F1=0.55, demonstrating that the dynamic reward rescaling is essential rather than a minor algorithmic tweak. On the real-world GTEx RNA isoform task, GReinSS dramatically outperforms RSEM (the standard EM-based method used by GTEx), reducing the isoform prediction error from 0.471 to 0.0069 on the MBD2 example gene and achieving a median error improvement of 0.0405 across 14,390 genes, with GReinSS winning by ≥0.05 on 46.6% of genes versus only 9.4% for RSEM.

## Relation to Prior Work

GReinSS generalizes two prior applications (CLoMu for cancer phylogeny and CNRein for copy number calling) into a unified framework for inferring combinatorial latent states from indirect observations via policy gradients. The key conceptual departure from standard RL is the use of dynamically rescaled rewards—dividing each observation's likelihood by the current model's marginal likelihood—so that policy gradients directly optimize the data log-likelihood rather than a fixed expected return. The paper also positions itself against GFlowNets (which assume known terminal-state rewards), EM/GEM (which require intractable expectations over exponential state spaces), and VAEs (which learn artificial rather than mechanistic latent states), showing that GReinSS subsumes several of these as special cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GReinSS provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to EM for combinatorially large discrete latent spaces where exact expectation computation is infeasible, with the dynamic reward mechanism proven to be an unbiased gradient estimator of the marginal log-likelihood. The striking ablation showing that removing the dynamic rescaling causes naive policy gradients to collapse (predicting empty graphs, F1 < 0.55 vs. 0.891 for GReinSS) underscores that the rescaling is not a minor tweak but essential. Open questions include whether the heuristic off-policy sampling can be replaced by learned proposals, whether the framework extends to Q-learning or actor-critic, and whether the observation model Pr(X|S) can itself be learned rather than assumed known. The GTEx RNA-seq validation demonstrates practical impact on a real biological pipeline, though the evaluation is limited to 61 matched samples.
