## Problem

Despite widespread empirical evidence that RLVR outperforms SFT for reasoning, there is no principled theoretical framework explaining why. This paper isolates backtracking as the key differentiating capability and asks: which post-training method actually teaches a model to efficiently retreat from dead ends at inference time?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264941/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is a linear-softmax bigram over directed edge states (each state encodes an edge plus traversal direction) in a multigraph with W branches of K diamonds, each containing L parallel edges. It is prompted with a target leaf node and must generate a sequence of edge-state transitions from the source; critically, generation is a pure Markov chain that does not condition on the target, so the model navigates blindly and a verifier only checks whether the target was reached. The output is a full reasoning trace (sequence of edge states), and the reward is the negative hitting time of the target.

## Method

The paper models CoT reasoning as pathfinding on a path-star generalization and performs a dynamical analysis of gradient flow (SFT) versus sign policy gradient flow (RLVR) on a linear-softmax bigram. The key insight is that SFT on golden shortest paths only encounters forward-pointing edge states, so the cross-entropy gradient never updates backward transitions (b_j, d_j remain at their pretrained uniform values), while RLVR's on-policy rollouts necessarily generate failed partial paths that produce gradient signal for backtracking actions. The proof shows SFT converges to a_j, c_j → 1 but b_j, d_j stay fixed, whereas RLVR converges all four transition families to 1 in finite time. This yields an exponential inference-time separation: the RLVR model reaches any target in Θ(WK) expected steps while the SFT model requires Θ(WL^K). Finally, the paper shows that distilling RLVR-generated reasoning traces via supervised learning transfers the backtracking behavior, recovering Θ(WK) efficiency.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 4) establishes an exponential inference-time compute separation: the RLVR-trained model reaches any target in Θ(WK) expected time, while the SFT-trained model (on golden shortest paths only) requires Θ(WL^K) time, yielding a separation factor of Θ(L^K) exponential in graph depth K. With an external search agent that prevents revisiting edge states (Corollary 1), the SFT model improves to Θ(WKL) but still retains a Θ(L) factor gap versus RLVR's Θ(WK). Experiments on W=15, K=15, L=5 confirm the RLVR model's hitting time converges to 4WK = 900, matching the theoretical prediction.

**Summary of theoretical guarantees for post-training methods on the path-star multigraph (W branches, depth K, L multiedges per diamond).**

| Method | Forward transitions (a_j, c_j) | Backward transitions (b_j, d_j) | Expected hitting time | Separation vs. RLVR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SFT (golden paths only) | → 1 (Theorem 2) | Fixed at L/(L+1), 1/(L+1) | Θ(WL^K) | Θ(L^K) (exponential) |
| RLVR (outcome reward + length penalty) | → 1 (Theorem 3) | → 1 (Theorem 3) | Θ(WK) | — |
| SFT on RLVR traces (distillation) | → 1 (Theorem 5) | → 1 (Theorem 5) | Θ(WK) | — |
| SFT + search agent (no revisit) | → 1 | Fixed | Θ(WKL) | Θ(L) (polynomial) |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three post-training paradigms: (1) SFT on golden shortest-path demonstrations, (2) RLVR with verifiable outcome reward and length penalty, and (3) SFT distillation from RLVR-generated reasoning traces. SFT on golden paths provably fails to learn backtracking: backward-pointing state transitions (b_j, d_j) remain frozen at their pretrained uniform values because no backward states appear in the training data (Theorem 2). RLVR, by contrast, learns all four transition types (a_j, b_j, c_j, d_j) to converge to 1 in finite time via signed policy gradient flow, because on-policy rollouts necessarily generate failed partial paths that provide gradient signal for backtracking actions (Theorem 3). This yields the headline exponential separation: Θ(WL^K) vs. Θ(WK) expected hitting time. Distilling RLVR traces via standard SFT recovers the Θ(WK) guarantee (Theorem 5), showing that the critical ingredient is backtracking data, not the RL algorithm itself. A search agent that prevents edge-state revisits reduces the SFT gap from exponential to a polynomial Θ(L) factor (Corollary 1), but cannot fully close it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the path-star graph construction of Bachmann & Nagarajan (2025) into a multigraph with W branches of depth K and L multiedges, and adopts the bigram world-model framework and signed gradient flow analysis from Kim et al. (2025). While prior works (Shalev-Shwartz & Shashua, Rohatgi et al.) studied backtracking from information-theoretic or sampling perspectives, and Kim et al. analyzed RL (PPO) convergence for CoT pathfinding, this paper is the first to provide a dynamical comparison of SFT versus RLVR specifically through the lens of backtracking ability. The key departure is showing that the absence of backward-state transitions in golden-path SFT data literally freezes those logits (zero gradient), whereas on-policy RLVR rollouts generate the necessary signal to learn efficient backtracking from outcome reward alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exponential inference-time separation (Θ(WK) vs Θ(WL^K)) provides a clean theoretical justification for the empirical observation that RLVR-trained reasoning models outperform SFT-trained ones, and the distillation result (Theorem 5) offers a practical pathway: SFT on RLVR-generated traces recovers efficient backtracking without requiring RL at deployment. However, the analysis is confined to a toy bigram model on a synthetic multigraph with signed gradient flow, so whether the separation persists for real LLMs with richer architectures, multi-step credit assignment, or process rewards remains open. The paper also leaves open how to design better inference-time orchestration (search agents) that can partially close the gap for SFT models, and whether the two-phase RLVR convergence structure generalizes beyond this specific graph topology.
