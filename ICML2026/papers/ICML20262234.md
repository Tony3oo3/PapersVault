## Problem

Existing JEPA methods like LeJEPA regularize representations toward isotropic Gaussian distributions, which inherently favor dense features and cannot express the sparsity observed in efficient neural and learned representations. This paper asks whether one can design a distribution-matching regularizer that enforces controllable sparsity and non-negativity in JEPA representations while preserving task-relevant information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20262234/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two stochastic augmented views of the same RGB image, which are passed through an encoder-projector stack (ResNet, ViT, or ConvNeXt followed by an MLP) with a final ReLU to produce D-dimensional non-negative feature vectors. The output is a sparse, non-negative representation whose expected ℓ₀ norm (fraction of non-zero entries) is analytically determined by the target distribution parameters {μ, σ, p}, enabling explicit sparsity control from fully dense (μ → +∞) to nearly all-zero (μ → −∞) without architectural changes.

## Method

Rectified LpJEPA introduces RDMReg, a sliced two-sample distribution-matching loss that aligns rectified features ReLU(z) to a Rectified Generalized Gaussian (RGG) target — a mixture of a Dirac mass at zero and a Truncated Generalized Gaussian on (0,∞). The continuous truncated component is the maximum-entropy distribution under an expected ℓₚ-norm constraint, while the Dirac component provides explicit, analytically controlled ℓ₀ sparsity via the CDF of the underlying Generalized Gaussian. Because RGG is not closed under linear projections (unlike Gaussian), the Cramér-Wold decomposition requires nonparametric two-sample tests on projected marginals, instantiated here as sliced 2-Wasserstein distance over random ℓ₂-sphere projections. The full training objective combines an ℓ₂ invariance term between rectified view features with RDMReg applied to both views, and the method strictly generalizes LeJEPA (which recovers as the dense p=2, μ=0 special case).

## Results

On ImageNet-100 linear probe evaluation, Rectified LpJEPA (RGN2.0(0,σ_GN)) achieves 85.08% encoder top-1 accuracy and 80.00% projector top-1 accuracy while maintaining 73% L0 sparsity (i.e., ~27% of features are zero), outperforming all dense baselines (VICReg 84.18%, SimCLR 83.44%, LeJEPA 84.80%) which are fully dense (L0 = 1.0). The best projector accuracy of 80.76% is achieved by RGN1.0(0.25,σ_GN) with 74% L0 sparsity. Performance degrades gracefully as sparsity increases, with a sharp drop only when >95% of entries are zero (e.g., RGN1.0(-3.0,σ_GN) at 82.72% encoder accuracy with 99% sparsity).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rectified LpJEPA (RGN2.0(0,σ_GN)) achieves 85.08% encoder accuracy, outperforming LeJEPA (84.80%), VICReg (84.18%), and SimCLR (83.44%) while simultaneously introducing ~27% zero entries (L0 sparsity 0.73 vs. 1.0 for all dense baselines). Against the sparse baselines, Rectified LpJEPA dominates: it beats NVICReg-ReLU (84.48%) by 0.6% encoder accuracy at comparable sparsity levels, and outperforms NCL-ReLU (82.58%) by 2.5% while being far less aggressively sparse (L0 0.73 vs. 0.0085), indicating NCL sacrifices too much information for extreme sparsity. The key advantage is the controllable sparsity-performance tradeoff: by tuning μ and p, Rectified LpJEPA smoothly interpolates between dense (L0≈0.87) and very sparse (L0≈0.01) regimes without the cliff-like accuracy drops seen in NCL. On projector accuracy, Rectified LpJEPA (80.76% best) also exceeds all baselines, including LeJEPA (79.52%) and VICReg (78.88%). Additionally, Rectified LpJEPA achieves lower normalized HSIC values than VICReg and NVICReg, indicating more statistically independent representations beyond second-order statistics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LeJEPA's distribution-matching framework (SIGReg) by replacing the isotropic Gaussian target with the Rectified Generalized Gaussian (RGG) family, which introduces explicit ℓ₀ sparsity control through a Dirac mass at zero while retaining a maximum-entropy continuous component under ℓ_p norm constraints. A key technical departure is that RGG is not closed under linear projections (unlike Gaussian), necessitating a two-sample sliced Wasserstein matching loss (RDMReg) rather than the simpler one-sample projection matching used in LeJEPA. The work also connects to VICReg by showing RDMReg recovers a Non-Negative VICReg form under eigenvector projections, and to the broader sparse-coding literature (Olshausen & Field, Lee & Seung) by formalizing non-negative sparse representations as a distributional target rather than an explicit penalty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that sparsity in self-supervised representations can be achieved purely through target distribution design, without explicit sparsity penalties, and that performance degrades gracefully until roughly 95% of feature entries are zero—suggesting substantial exploitable sparsity in learned image representations. The RGG family provides an analytically tractable, continuously parameterized family (via μ, σ, p) for tuning the sparsity-entropy tradeoff, which could inform future JEPA regularizers beyond the Gaussian assumption. Open questions include whether the two-sample matching overhead is justified at scale, whether the sparsity translates into practical inference efficiency gains, and how the approach extends to non-product supports or correlated coordinate structures.
