## Problem

Shape-based virtual screening recovers structurally more diverse, pharmacophore-preserving molecules than 2D fingerprint methods, but requires expensive conformational sampling and pairwise 3D alignment that scale quadratically, making billion-scale library searches computationally infeasible even with GPU-accelerated tools like FastROCS. No prior method learns a 2D graph representation whose similarity directly approximates 3D shape overlap while remaining compatible with production-scale vector database indexing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264991/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D molecular graph (parsed from SMILES) as input and produces a 512-dimensional L2-normalized embedding, which is then discretized into a compact IVF-PQ code: one IVF cluster index (from K=1024 centroids) plus M product-quantization sub-codes (each 1 byte from a 256-entry codebook), yielding a total storage of M+1 bytes per molecule. These codes plug directly into a FAISS IVF-PQ index without any additional training, enabling approximate nearest-neighbor retrieval of shape-similar molecules from libraries of up to 10 billion compounds in milliseconds.

## Method

SAND uses a GINE graph neural network (7 layers, 1024 hidden dim) to encode 2D molecular graphs into 512-dim unit vectors, trained with a differentiable Spearman rank correlation loss (Blondel et al., 2020) that aligns embedding dot-product similarities with ground-truth shape overlay scores computed via ROSHAMBO2, plus a uniformity loss to spread representations on the hypersphere. The key architectural contribution is an end-to-end quantization-aware training loop: the encoder is jointly optimized with a two-level IVF-PQ discretizer (K=1024 IVF centroids + M sub-vector PQ codebooks of size 256) using VQ-VAE-style EMA codebook updates and a straight-through estimator, with the Spearman loss applied to the reconstructed (quantized) vectors. This joint training produces embeddings with markedly lower intrinsic dimensionality (83 vs. 317 principal components for 90% variance) compared to a quantization-unaware baseline, yielding approximately 4× better compression at equivalent retrieval quality. At inference, the learned codebooks initialize a FAISS IVF-PQ index over the full Enamine REAL Database (~10B molecules, ~350 GB), enabling shape-similar molecule retrieval in under a second on a single GPU node.

## Results

SAND achieves a Spearman correlation of R=0.86 with ground-truth 3D shape overlay scores on Enamine REAL and R=0.81 on ChEMBL, substantially outperforming all baselines (ECFP R=0.20, GraphMVP R=0.37, DrugCLIP R=0.31). On top-100 shape-match recall (Recall@100), SAND scores 0.50 on Enamine and 0.34 on ChEMBL, versus 0.13 and 0.05 for ECFP and 0.05/0.02 for GraphMVP. With M=32 product quantization (64× compression), correlation drops only ~9% (R=0.78 Enamine, R=0.73 ChEMBL), and the full 10-billion-molecule Enamine REAL index is searchable in 8 ms on 8 H100 GPUs — a >10⁸× speedup over FastROCS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAND dominates all four representation baselines on both test sets. On Enamine, SAND's Recall@100 of 0.50 is 3.8× ECFP (0.13), 10× GraphMVP (0.05), 12.5× 3DInfomax (0.03), and 12.5× DrugCLIP (0.04); the gap widens on ChEMBL where SAND (0.34) outperforms ECFP (0.05) by 6.8× and GraphMVP (0.02) by 17×. Notably, ECFP is the strongest baseline at small k because top shape matches often share local substructures, yet it still trails SAND by a wide margin. Among the 3D-aware pretrained models, GraphMVP (R=0.37) and DrugCLIP (R=0.31) achieve higher global Spearman correlation than ECFP (R=0.20) but all remain far below SAND (R=0.81–0.86). For speed, SAND queries the 10B-molecule Enamine index in 8 ms on 8 H100 GPUs versus ~10⁷ s for ROSHAMBO2 (GPU) and ~10¹¹ s for ROCS (CPU), a >10⁸× speedup. The GPS encoder ablation (0.51 vs 0.50 on Enamine x=2) shows the GINE backbone is not the source of SAND's advantage; the Spearman contrastive loss and quantization-aware training are the key differentiators.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAND sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) shape-aware molecular representation learning via contrastive objectives (Atsango et al. 2022, Sellner et al. 2023, Lžičař & Gamouh 2024), which previously relied on post-hoc quantization and did not address billion-scale retrieval; (2) neural discrete representation learning (VQ-VAE, Oord et al. 2018), whose EMA codebook updates, straight-through estimator, and commitment loss are adopted here but extended to a two-level IVF-PQ structure trained jointly with the encoder; and (3) differentiable ranking losses (Blondel et al. 2020), repurposed as a Spearman-correlation contrastive objective that directly optimizes rank preservation between embedding similarity and ground-truth shape overlap. The key departure from prior work is the end-to-end co-optimization of the GINE encoder and the IVF-PQ codebooks under a retrieval-specific rank-correlation loss, producing representations that are natively suited to the FAISS index structure without a separate k-means training stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing shape-based virtual screening from GPU-years to sub-second queries over 10 billion compounds, SAND removes the primary computational barrier that has kept shape-based VS out of routine use for modern virtual-on-demand libraries. The demonstrated integration with generative chemistry (Flowr) opens a practical pipeline from in silico design to purchasable analogs, addressing a persistent translational gap in AI-driven drug discovery. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other similarity metrics (e.g., electrostatics, pharmacophore features), how training-data construction (conformer sampling depth, pre-screening strategy) affects robustness at the extreme low-similarity tail, and whether the 512-dim embedding is sufficient for distinguishing subtle shape differences in highly flexible molecules.
