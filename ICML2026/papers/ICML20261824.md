## Problem

Neural architectures trained on fixed vocabularies overfit to specific symbol identities, causing severe performance drops when interchangeable tokens (bound variables, atomic propositions) are renamed or when test-time symbols extend beyond the training vocabulary. Prior approaches using random embeddings for token discrimination lack formal invariance guarantees, meaning different random seeds can yield different predictions on alpha-equivalent inputs. The paper addresses the absence of a principled architecture that is provably invariant to token renaming by construction while simultaneously supporting open-vocabulary generalization to unseen interchangeable tokens.

## Input / Output

The model consumes symbolic formulas encoded in Polish (prefix) notation: propositional logic formulas over atomic propositions with operators (¬, ∧, ∨, ↔, ⊕), or LTL formulas adding temporal operators X (next) and U (until). For propositional logic, the output is an alternating sequence of proposition symbols and Boolean values (e.g., 'a1b0') representing a satisfying assignment; for LTL, the output is a symbolic trace in lasso form (finite prefix followed by a repeating suffix) with semicolons delimiting time steps and braces marking the loop. A key constraint is that all output interchangeable tokens must be drawn from the input formula—the architecture cannot generate a fresh interchangeable token with no corresponding embedding stream.

## Method

The Symbol-Invariant Transformer maintains k parallel embedding streams (one per interchangeable token), where each stream replaces its target token with an 'actual' embedding and all other interchangeable tokens with a shared 'placeholder' embedding, while base tokens remain unchanged. Within each stream, standard self-attention and feed-forward layers are applied with weights shared across all k streams; cross-stream communication occurs through an aggregated attention mechanism that averages hidden states across streams and restores each stream's true representation at its token's positions. The projection step averages base-token logits across streams but takes each interchangeable token's logit exclusively from its own stream. The key theoretical insight is that alpha-renaming merely permutes the stream ordering, and since per-stream operations are independent and aggregated operations rely on commutative summation, the architecture is provably invariant to any renaming of interchangeable tokens (Theorem 4.1). The model is trained with supervised learning using AdaCos cosine loss, RoPE positional encoding (tree-positional encoding for logic tasks), and can be retrofitted onto existing pretrained Transformers by reassigning two embedding rows as actual/placeholder and fine-tuning for a few epochs.

## Results

On the propositional logic task (Renamed dataset), the proposed method achieves 98.03% correct predictions and 60.96% exact matches, with perfect 100% alpha-covariance across all AP counts (3, 4, and 5). On the LTL task (Renamed dataset), it reaches 98.24% correct and 79.65% exact with again 100% alpha-covariance. Under the Reduced training regime (80K vs. 800K samples), the method retains 70.43% correct on propositional logic and 93.46% on LTL, still with perfect alpha-covariance, demonstrating superior sample efficiency. On the out-of-distribution heatmap (up to 10 APs, length 50), the proposed method markedly outperforms all baselines on propositional logic and is competitive on LTL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the random embedding baseline (Işık et al., 2025), the proposed method wins on every metric in every perturbation scenario: on Renamed propositional logic it improves correct predictions from 93.25% to 98.03% and exact matches from 56.45% to 60.96%, while achieving perfect 100% alpha-covariance versus the random embedding's 92.98–99.42%. On Renamed LTL, the gap is 98.24% vs. 95.94% correct and 79.65% vs. 76.45% exact, again with 100% vs. ~98% alpha-covariance. Against GPT-5.2, the proposed method dominates on LTL (98.24% vs. 86.83% correct, 79.65% vs. 35.93% exact, 100% vs. ~80% alpha-covariance) and on alpha-covariance for propositional logic (100% vs. 1–43%), though GPT-5.2 edges ahead on raw correct predictions for propositional logic (99.73% vs. 98.03%); the paper notes that at top-10 and top-25 accuracy the proposed method matches GPT-5.2 (99.03% and 99.54%). The standard baseline (fixed embeddings, no vocabulary extension) collapses under renaming (41.57% correct on Prop Logic, 34.13% on LTL), underscoring that the invariance guarantee is the core contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Işık et al. (2025), which introduced random dual-part embeddings for interchangeable tokens to enable post-training vocabulary expansion, by replacing the stochastic embedding strategy with a deterministic parallel-stream architecture that provides a provable invariance guarantee (Theorem 4.1). It also builds on Ankner et al. (2023) 'Renamer,' which achieved renaming invariance without randomness but did not address open-vocabulary generalization; the proposed method unifies both properties. The experimental setup, alpha-covariance metric, and task formulations are inherited from the Işık et al. line of work, positioning this as an architectural upgrade rather than a new problem formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable alpha-equivalence by construction addresses a fundamental gap in neural architectures: current models overfit to surface-level symbol identities, causing up to 70% performance drops under semantics-preserving renaming. The method's practical relevance is strongest in formal verification, theorem proving, and code understanding where interchangeable tokens are ubiquitous. Key open questions include scaling beyond S≈10 streams (O(SL²) cost), generating novel interchangeable tokens (currently impossible since streams are instantiated from input), and whether the inductive bias transfers to richer symbolic domains like constructive proof generation or program synthesis. The ability to retrofit the architecture onto existing pre-trained models with minimal fine-tuning is a practical advantage that lowers adoption barriers.
