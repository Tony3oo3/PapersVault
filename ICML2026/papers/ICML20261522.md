## Problem

Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) reason predominantly in symbolic/text space, failing to visualize and track intermediate visual states during the reasoning process. This text-centric paradigm leads to suboptimal performance on complex long-horizon, vision-centric tasks, while the reliance on lengthy Chain-of-Thought introduces uncontrollable generation and prohibitive latency that worsens with multi-turn 'Thinking with Image' interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261522/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a problem image (e.g., a grid with obstacles, city dots, a partially filled Sudoku, or shuffled jigsaw patches) at 512×512 resolution together with a short text instruction (e.g., 'Draw a continuous red line connecting the Start point to the Goal point, avoiding all holes'). It produces a solution image in the same visual format (a rendered trajectory, a completed grid, a reconstructed puzzle), which is then mapped to a symbolic answer via a task-specific parsing function Ψ to enable fair comparison against MLLM text outputs. The denoising trajectory {x_t} itself serves as an interpretable visual reasoning path, with each intermediate step capturing a progressively refined state of the solution.

## Method

DiffThinker reformulates multimodal reasoning as a native image-to-image generative task using a 20B Multimodal Diffusion Transformer (MMDiT) built on Qwen-Image-Edit, trained via Flow Matching with a mean-squared-error objective on the velocity field in VAE latent space. The key insight is that the iterative denoising trajectory naturally constitutes a visual reasoning path: early steps explore multiple candidate solutions in parallel (native parallelism), while later steps progressively prune invalid paths and converge on a single valid solution. Training uses LoRA fine-tuning (rank 32) with 30,000 task-specific samples per domain, and inference employs a 20-step first-order Euler solver with Classifier-Free Guidance at scale w=4. The approach is architecture-agnostic and task-agnostic, requiring only rapid fine-tuning on new tasks without custom architectures, and can serve as a collaborative backend where the diffusion model generates multiple candidate solution images for an MLLM to verify.

## Results

DiffThinker achieves a grand-mean accuracy of 87.4% across seven tasks in four domains (sequential planning, combinatorial optimization, constraint satisfaction, spatial configuration), with DiffThinker++ reaching 88.5%. This represents a +314.2% improvement over GPT-5 (21.1%), +111.6% over Gemini-3-Flash (41.3%), and +39.0% over the fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-32B SFT baseline (62.9%). On individual tasks, DiffThinker reaches near-perfect scores on VSP (99.3% avg across grid sizes 3–8), Jigsaw (92% avg), and VisPuzzle (98.3%), while maintaining strong performance on harder tasks like Maze-32 (56%) and Sudoku-35 (57%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffThinker decisively outperforms all closed-source MLLM baselines: GPT-5 (21.1% avg) and Gemini-3-Flash (41.3% avg) both collapse on harder difficulty levels (e.g., VSP-Super-32, Maze-32, TSP-18) where DiffThinker maintains 56–98% accuracy. Against the strongest open-source baseline, Qwen3-VL-32B with SFT (62.9% avg), DiffThinker leads by 24.5 percentage points, with the gap widening substantially on vision-centric tasks like Jigsaw (92% vs. 65.7%) and VSP-Super (89.5% vs. 53%). The GRPO-trained variants of Qwen3-VL actually underperform their SFT counterparts (47.4% vs. 62.9% for 32B), indicating that RL with verifiable rewards is poorly suited to these long-horizon visual tasks. Among generative baselines, the untrained Qwen-Image-Edit models (4.0% and 6.7% avg) and Nano Banana 2 (14.8%) confirm that the performance gain comes from task-specific flow-matching fine-tuning rather than the base architecture. In the collaborative setting, DiffThinker + MLLM verification on Jigsaw-4 surpasses either model alone, with accuracy scaling as the number of generated candidates N increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffThinker departs fundamentally from the MLLM reasoning lineage (standard image-to-text CoT and 'Thinking with Image' iterative tool-call paradigms) by recasting multimodal reasoning as a native image-to-image generative task using flow matching. Architecturally, it builds on Qwen-Image-Edit's 20B MMDiT backbone and VAE latent space, applying LoRA fine-tuning per task rather than training from scratch, which distinguishes it from prior specialized diffusion solvers for Sudoku or TSP that require custom architectures. The paper also positions itself against video-generation-based reasoning (e.g., Wan2.2-based DiffThinker-Video), arguing that image generation offers a more efficient and equally effective reasoning substrate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 20B diffusion model fine-tuned on 30K samples per task can outperform GPT-5 by over 300% on structured vision-centric reasoning, the paper challenges the assumption that autoregressive MLLMs are the natural substrate for multimodal reasoning. The four identified properties—efficiency (1.1s inference), controllability (fixed-step ODE), native parallelism (multi-path exploration during denoising), and collaboration with MLLMs—offer a practical toolkit for deploying vision-centric reasoning in latency-sensitive settings. Key open questions include whether the paradigm generalizes beyond grid-based, highly parseable tasks to open-ended visual reasoning, and whether stronger generative foundation models can close the OOD gap the paper acknowledges.
