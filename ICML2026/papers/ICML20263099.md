## Problem

Preference-based RL (PbRL) learns reward functions from human pairwise comparisons, but effective reward learning typically requires thousands of costly human queries to cover state and action spaces adequately. Prior semi-supervised approaches (e.g., SURF) rely on learned reward models for pseudo-labeling, which suffer from confirmation bias under limited supervision. The paper identifies that human preferences are fundamentally shaped by visual perception of agent behaviors, and that the structured latent space of Video Foundation Models (ViFMs) can capture these perceptual distinctions to infer preferences for new behaviors by comparing them with known preferred examples.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of labeled preference pairs (as few as 10) and a large pool of unlabeled segment pairs sampled from an offline dataset. Each trajectory segment is rendered as a short video clip (100 frames for D4RL, 64 frames for MetaWorld) and encoded by S3D into a 512-dimensional latent vector; the policy additionally consumes 9-dimensional proprioceptive states (EE position, velocity, yaw, gripper status). The output is a set of pseudo-preference labels (binary or tie) for unlabeled pairs, a Bradley-Terry reward function trained on the combined labeled and pseudo-labeled data, and a final policy trained via IQL with relabeled rewards. A preference threshold τ_P filters out low-confidence pseudo-labels to mitigate noise.

## Method

VOTP embeds all trajectory segments into the latent space of an off-the-shelf Video Foundation Model (S3D, pretrained on HowTo100M), then solves an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem (via Sinkhorn's algorithm) to compute a coupling matrix μ* between labeled and unlabeled segments, with cost defined as Euclidean distance in the ViFM latent space. The key mechanism is a preference score S(σ̄_i', σ̄_j') = ΣΣ R_ij (μ_ii' μ_jj' − μ_ij' μ_ji'), which aggregates the skew-symmetric preference matrix R over all labeled pairs, weighting each contribution by the relative alignment strength between the unlabeled pair and the labeled pair (or its reversal) as captured by the OT plan. This score is normalized to [−1, 1] and thresholded by τ_P to produce discrete pseudo-preference labels. Unlike SURF, which uses a trained reward model for pseudo-labeling (introducing confirmation bias), VOTP leverages the semantically rich, actor-agnostic representations of ViFMs to propagate preferences through geometric alignment in latent space, enabling high-quality pseudo-labels from only ~10 human queries.

## Results

On D4RL locomotion tasks, VOTP achieves an average normalized score of 92.8, effectively matching the Oracle baseline (92.4) and approaching IQL+GT (93.6), while substantially outperforming the next-best baseline FTB (85.4) and P-IQL (65.3). On MetaWorld manipulation tasks, VOTP achieves an average success rate of 67.6%, outperforming all baselines except Oracle (80.1%) and IQL+GT (71.0%), with the closest competitor being LiRE at 64.0%. Notably, VOTP uses only 10 labeled preference pairs, whereas P-IQL requires the same 10 labels without any pseudo-labeling, and the method achieves these results in under 2 hours of training versus ~2 days for FTB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VOTP outperforms all eight baselines on the D4RL locomotion average (92.8 vs. FTB 85.4, LiRE 83.2, P-IQL 65.3, SURF 59.5, DPPO 58.7, IPL 46.4, CPL 26.8, APPO 21.5), effectively matching the Oracle upper bound (92.4). On MetaWorld, VOTP (67.6%) beats LiRE (64.0%), APPO (51.6%), SURF (51.0%), FTB (48.6%), IPL/CPL (42.4%), DPPO (39.6%), and P-IQL (31.0%), though it trails Oracle (80.1%) and IQL+GT (71.0%). The most notable gap is against P-IQL, where VOTP gains +27.5 points on D4RL and +36.6 points on MetaWorld, demonstrating the value of OT-based pseudo-labeling over training solely on 10 labeled pairs. FTB is the strongest competitor on D4RL (85.4 vs. 92.8) but underperforms on MetaWorld (48.6 vs. 67.6) and requires ~2 days of training per run versus under 2 hours for VOTP. SURF, which also uses pseudo-labels but via a learned reward model, degrades significantly on hopper tasks (9.3 on hop-m-r) due to confirmation bias from inaccurate pseudo-labels, whereas VOTP remains stable across all tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

VOTP sits at the intersection of semi-supervised preference learning (building directly on SURF's paradigm of combining labeled and unlabeled segment pairs) and optimal transport in RL (extending OT-based approaches from imitation learning and cross-domain preference transfer like PEARL to same-domain pseudo-labeling). The key departure from SURF is replacing learned reward-model-based pseudo-labeling with OT over the latent space of off-the-shelf Video Foundation Models (S3D), which avoids the confirmation bias problem of reward-model-generated labels under limited supervision. The Bradley-Terry preference model and IQL policy training follow standard PbRL practice, so the novelty is concentrated in the representation choice (ViFM) and the label propagation mechanism (OT plan with preference matrix aggregation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing human feedback from hundreds/thousands of queries to ~10 labels while matching Oracle performance on D4RL locomotion makes PbRL far more accessible for real-world deployment. The reliance on pretrained ViFMs means no encoder training is needed, and the robustness to visual distractors (lighting, texture, video backgrounds) suggests the approach transfers to uncontrolled environments. Open questions include the scalability of OT computation as labeled sets grow (the paper acknowledges this and points to hierarchical transport), whether biases in ViFMs propagate into learned rewards, and how the framework extends to generative policy representations (flow matching, diffusion policies) that are increasingly standard in robotics.
