## Problem

Verifier-free RL methods for LLM reasoning sample reasoning traces conditioned only on the question (the prior), which suffers from low sample efficiency on difficult questions and incoherence between generated traces and final answers. Existing alternatives that sample from the answer-conditioned posterior introduce a training-inference mismatch, since at inference time only the question is available. The paper addresses how to couple prior and posterior sampling to get the benefits of both without their respective drawbacks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question as input and produces a structured output consisting of a chain-of-thought reasoning trace enclosed in think tags followed by a final answer in answer tags. During training, the same base LLM is used in two conditioning modes via different prompt templates: a prior template that conditions only on the question, and a posterior template that conditions on both the question and the ground-truth answer. Special tokens for think/answer delimiters are separately encoded to ensure consistent tokenization, with string-based stop sequences used during generation to handle tokenizer merging.

## Method

CoVRL constructs a composite distribution p′(z|x,y) that token-level averages the prior p_φ(z|x) and posterior q_ψ(z|x,y) with equal weighting, then optimizes an ELBO consisting of a reconstruction term (answer prediction probability) and a KL regularization term (composite-to-prior divergence). Because direct sampling from p′ is impractical with standard LLM inference engines, the method uses off-policy hybrid sampling: each training example is drawn from the prior with probability α or the posterior with probability 1−α, and importance sampling ratios correct for the distribution mismatch. The reconstruction term is optimized via GRPO policy gradients with the model's own answer-prediction log-probability as the verifier-free reward, while the KL term uses Bregman-divergence-based control variates for low-variance, non-negative estimation under both sampling modes. All three distributions (prior, posterior, and answer decoder) are realized by a single LLM with different prompt templates, and selective NLL loss is applied only to high-advantage samples to reinforce quality reasoning traces.

## Results

CoVRL achieves 50.2% overall accuracy across nine reasoning benchmarks, representing a 12.4% absolute improvement over the Qwen2.5-7B-Base model (37.8%) and a 2.3% gain over the strongest verifier-free baseline (RLPR, 47.9%). The largest individual gains appear on AQuA (+21.7 over base), MATH-500 (+21.6), and SAT-Math (+20.6), while the smallest is on GPQA (+4.3). Notably, despite training exclusively on non-mathematical questions from WebInstruct, CoVRL transfers effectively to mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoVRL outperforms all five verifier-free baselines on overall accuracy, with the largest margin over LaTRO (+5.5%) and the smallest over RLPR (+2.3%). On individual benchmarks, CoVRL leads on 8 of 9 tasks; the sole exception is GPQA, where JLB (31.6) edges out CoVRL (30.4) by 1.2 points. The most decisive wins are on AQuA (77.3 vs. 72.6 for VeriFree, the next best) and MATH-500 (66.3 vs. 61.2 for RAVR/RLPR), suggesting that the coupled prior-posterior sampling is particularly effective for multi-step quantitative reasoning. Among the baselines, RLPR and RAVR are the closest competitors overall (47.9% and 47.8%), while LaTRO trails significantly (44.7%), likely due to its reliance on a fixed reference model for KL regularization. All baselines are trained with identical GRPO hyperparameters on the same Qwen2.5-7B-Base model and filtered WebInstruct data, isolating the gradient estimator as the sole variable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the verifier-free RL line of work (LaTRO, VeriFree, JLB, RLPR) that formulates reasoning as latent-variable inference and uses answer probability as a self-reward, by introducing a composite distribution that couples the question-conditioned prior with the answer-conditioned posterior at the token level. It builds directly on the VAE/ELBO framework (Kingma & Welling) and Schulman's low-variance KL estimators, adapting both to the discrete, autoregressive setting of LLM reasoning. The key departure from prior methods is the hybrid sampling strategy with importance weighting, which avoids the training-inference mismatch of posterior-only approaches (RAVR) and the low sample efficiency of prior-only approaches, while remaining implementable within standard RL frameworks like GRPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoVRL provides a principled, verifier-free training recipe that improves general reasoning by 12.4% over the base model and 2.3% over the strongest verifier-free baseline, with the notable property that training on non-mathematical data transfers to mathematical benchmarks. The asymmetric KL regularization is shown to be critical (reducing its coefficient collapses performance to 27.4%), highlighting that the training-inference alignment problem is non-trivial and not solved by the composite distribution alone. Open questions include whether the symmetric JSD formulation derived in the appendix could yield further gains, whether the approach scales to much larger models or longer reasoning horizons, and whether the single-LLM-as-all-roles design (prior, posterior, reward model) introduces implicit biases that limit ceiling performance.
