## Problem

Large-scale face recognition datasets collected via weakly supervised web pipelines suffer from label noise, identity ambiguity, and long-tail imbalance, yet assessing whether a dataset variant is worth scaling before expensive full training typically requires clean validation splits or repeated training runs that are infeasible at million-image scale. Existing validation-free spectral metrics (e.g., effective rank) are confounded because noise-induced complexity inflation mimics beneficial data scaling, and noisy-label learning methods act as training-time remedies rather than fast pre-training diagnostics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a face recognition training set with (potentially noisy) identity labels and a lightweight proxy model (ResNet-50 or ResNet-100 trained with ArcFace). It operates on an identity-balanced subset of ~10,000 images (1,000 identities × 10 images each, with intra-identity deduplication) rather than the full dataset, extracting L2-normalized 1024-d embeddings. The output is a single scalar IQ score decomposable into two interpretable components: a neighbor-consistency score (fraction of kNN label agreement) and a log-normalized effective rank of the embedding covariance spectrum.

## Method

IQ is defined as a fixed-weight convex combination IQ = 0.2·c̄ + 0.8·r̃_ent, where c̄ is the mean per-sample kNN label agreement (k=10, cosine similarity, self-match excluded) and r̃_ent = log(r_ent)/log(Q) is the entropy-based effective rank of the mean-centered embedding covariance, normalized by the spectral support Q=min(n,d). The key design insight is that these two signals are complementary under the central confounder of web-collected data: global spectral complexity (ER) increases under both beneficial scaling and supervision corruption, while local neighborhood coherence (Consis) remains near-saturated under clean scaling but degrades sharply under label flips or identity ambiguity. Fusing them with fixed weights (not tuned per dataset) yields a score that tracks downstream verification accuracy across clean-scaling, pure-noise, mixed scale-corruption, and targeted subset-selection regimes, achieving perfect Spearman and Kendall-τ correlation with MFR-ALL accuracy versus 0.418/0.300 for the external RankMe baseline.

## Results

On the union of clean-scaling and noise-injection settings (WebFace4M/12M/42M plus WebFace12M with 0–40% label noise), IQ achieves a perfect Spearman correlation of 1.000 and Kendall τ of 1.000 with downstream MFR-ALL verification accuracy, and a Pearson correlation of 0.891. Under clean scaling, IQ rises monotonically from 0.902 (WebFace4M, 90.36% Acc) to 0.968 (WebFace42M, 96.26% Acc), tracking the 5.9-point accuracy gain. Under 40% label noise on WebFace12M, IQ drops to 0.875 while accuracy falls to 72.01%, correctly ranking the smaller clean WebFace4M (IQ 0.902) above the larger but corrupted set (IQ 0.875).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares IQ against one external validation-free baseline, RankMe (Garrido et al., 2023), and two internal ablations (ER-only and Consis-only). IQ dominates on all three correlation metrics: it achieves Spearman 1.000 vs. RankMe's 0.418, Kendall τ 1.000 vs. 0.300, and Pearson 0.891 vs. 0.752. The gap is most pronounced on rank-based metrics (Spearman, Kendall τ), where IQ perfectly preserves the ordering of dataset settings while RankMe captures only moderate monotonic agreement. The internal ablations confirm the paper's central claim: ER-only (Spearman 0.286) is severely confounded by noise-induced complexity inflation, while Consis-only (Spearman 0.607) saturates under clean scaling; their combination in IQ resolves both failure modes. RankMe, despite being a well-motivated spectral-complexity estimator, remains substantially below IQ because it lacks the local label-coherence signal that disambiguates beneficial diversity from corruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two established validation-free representation diagnostics—entropy-based effective rank (Roy & Vetterli, 2007; RankMe, Garrido et al., 2023) for global spectral complexity, and k-NN neighbor-consistency (Iscen et al., 2022; Bahri et al., 2020) for local label coherence—into a single dataset-level score for face recognition trainability. It departs from prior work by targeting the specific confounder in weakly supervised web FR data where both beneficial scaling and label corruption inflate spectral complexity, making either signal alone ambiguous. The WebFace260M/CAST pipeline (Zhu et al., 2021) provides the dataset context, while ArcFace (Deng et al., 2019) supplies the proxy training objective; the paper's contribution is the fusion strategy and the controlled experimental protocol rather than new embedding or loss machinery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IQ offers a practical, low-cost diagnostic for FR practitioners who need to rank or monitor dataset variants before committing to expensive full-scale training, particularly when clean validation splits are unavailable due to privacy or licensing constraints. Key limitations include evaluation on a single dataset family (WebFace), synthetic uniform label-flip noise that does not capture structured real-world errors (identity merge/split, near-duplicates, long-tail confusion), and validation against only one downstream benchmark (MFR-ALL). Open questions include whether IQ generalizes to structured noise processes, different downstream protocols, and non-face recognition domains, and whether per-sample Consis diagnostics can be closed-looped into automated cleaning pipelines.
