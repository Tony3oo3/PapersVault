## Problem

Discrete diffusion language models offer faster generation and improved controllability over autoregressive models but are typically constrained to fixed-length generation and lack KV caching support. Block diffusion partially bridges this gap by generating fixed-size contiguous blocks left-to-right, but its rigid block structure restricts any-order decoding (e.g., infilling, arbitrary-position insertion) and delays KV cache updates until each block is fully decoded. The paper asks whether one can interpolate between autoregression and diffusion by varying the *order* in which token sets are generated rather than by varying a fixed block size.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt (or a partial sequence with gaps for infilling) and produces a variable-length generated text sequence. Unlike block diffusion, which commits tokens only after an entire fixed-size block is decoded, set diffusion commits tokens in flexible-position, flexible-length sets and updates the KV cache after every single inference step. At inference, a position-offset schedule selects candidate positions from a sliding output window of at most S_max tokens, and a sampler (e.g., confidence-based) may accept a subset of those positions, enabling any-order generation up to 2S positions ahead of the current position.

## Method

Set diffusion defines an autoregressive probability distribution over flexible-position, flexible-length token sets, factorizing the likelihood as a product of position-selection probabilities from an order policy π and conditional token-set likelihoods modeled by masked diffusion. The key architectural contribution is a set-causal transformer whose attention mask lets tokens in set σ_n attend to all clean tokens in preceding sets σ_1,…,σ_n, enabling KV cache updates after every inference step rather than only after a full block. Interpolation between AR and order-agnostic diffusion is controlled by position-dependent reveal-time schedules: each token position ℓ is assigned a random reveal time R^ℓ drawn from a shaped CDF over an active interval [a^ℓ, a^ℓ+w], and sorting reveal times induces the generation order; the window width w interpolates from AR (w→1/L, tight ELBO) to MDLM (w=1, fully parallel). Training uses a vectorized single-forward-pass algorithm that concatenates clean and noisy views of all token sets with a custom set-causal attention mask, and SW-SetDLMs specialize to singleton sets with a factorized one-shot denoising likelihood (T=1) for low-variance, token-efficient optimization.

## Results

On GSM8K, SW-SetDLM with S≤8 achieves 66.41% 0-shot pass@1 at ≥60.42 tokens/sec, outperforming BD3LM S=4 (63.53% at ≥55.39 tok/s) and BD3LM S=8 (56.94% at ≥54.67 tok/s) on the speed-quality Pareto frontier. On ROCStories infilling, SW-SetDLM S≤32 achieves ROUGE-L of 10.9 (1/5) and 13.2 (3/5) with >132 and >123 tok/s respectively, beating BD3LM S=16 (8.6 and 11.1 ROUGE-L) by ~25% in both quality and speed. On CNN/DailyMail summarization, SW-SetDLM S≤32 reaches ROUGE-L 27.3 at ≥42.3 tok/s, matching BD3LM S=16 (27.0) while decoding ~10% faster. On MAUVE (OWT), SW-SetDLM S≤32 scores 71.98 vs. BD3LM's 64.91, a 7-point improvement, while still decoding faster (≥91.30 vs. ≥81.22 tok/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against block diffusion (BD3LM), SW-SetDLM achieves a strictly better speed-quality Pareto frontier on GSM8K: at matched prediction budgets, SW-SetDLM S≤8 (66.41% pass@1, ≥60.42 tok/s) dominates BD3LM S=4 (63.53%, ≥55.39 tok/s) on both axes. On ROCStories infilling, SW-SetDLM outperforms BD3LM S=16 by 2.3 ROUGE-L points (1/5) and 2.1 points (3/5) while decoding ~25% faster, a direct consequence of flexible-position decoding. Against MDLM, SW-SetDLM wins decisively on GSM8K (66.41% vs. 6.37% pass@1) and infilling throughput (>132 vs. ≥71.9 tok/s), though MDLM achieves higher absolute ROUGE on infilling (14.5 vs. 11.6 R-1 for 1/5) due to its bidirectional context. On summarization, SW-SetDLM S≤32 slightly trails BD3LM S=16 on ROUGE-1 (38.1 vs. 40.0) but matches on ROUGE-L (27.3 vs. 27.0) while decoding ~10% faster. On MAUVE, SW-SetDLM improves over BD3LM by 7.07 points (71.98 vs. 64.91) while also decoding faster. The AR Transformer remains the strongest on absolute GSM8K accuracy (75.74%) and MAUVE (92.94), but set diffusion narrows the gap substantially while offering any-order decoding and infilling capabilities that AR cannot provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends block diffusion (BD3LM; Arriola et al., 2025a) by replacing its fixed contiguous blocks with flexible-position, flexible-length token sets, and introduces a position-offset reveal-time schedule as the mechanism for interpolating between autoregression and order-agnostic masked diffusion (MDLM; Sahoo et al., 2024). The likelihood decomposition follows Turok et al. (2026), and the set-causal attention architecture generalizes the block-causal mask to arbitrary set orderings. Conceptually, the key departure from block diffusion is shifting the interpolation knob from block size to generation order: rather than asking 'how many tokens per block?', set diffusion asks 'in what order should token positions be revealed?', which unifies AR, MDLM, block diffusion, and any-order autoregression as special cases of a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The position-offset schedule provides a principled, tunable knob for the speed-quality tradeoff in diffusion language models, and the set-causal architecture enables KV cache updates after every inference step rather than only after a full block completes—a practical efficiency gain for long-sequence generation. The framework's ability to recover multiple prior paradigms as special cases makes it a useful unifying lens for the AR-diffusion interpolation literature. Limitations include the small model scale (110–130M parameters), the need to hand-tune the schedule parameters (w, k) per task, and a persistent accuracy gap versus pure autoregression. Open questions include whether learned orderings (via variational inference or confidence-based policies) can close this gap, and whether the framework scales to multi-billion-parameter models where the efficiency gains from per-step KV caching become most impactful.
