## Problem

Modern end-to-end autonomous driving systems consume multi-view video, yet safety-critical scenarios are too rare to collect at scale. Existing generators produce adversarial trajectories (incompatible with video-based E2E planners), simulator-based videos (suffering from a large domain gap), or single-view accident videos (unsuitable for multi-view pipelines). No framework existed for generating realistic, multi-view safety-critical driving videos in the real-world domain.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-frame initial scene comprising six synchronized camera views (front-left, front, front-right, back-right, back, back-left), camera extrinsics, 3D vehicle bounding boxes with kinematic states, and HD map lanes, all available in datasets like NuScenes. Vehicles within 25 m of the ego are annotated with ID-labeled 2D boxes in the images before being fed to the VLM selector. The output is a 9-second, 12 Hz multi-view video (108 frames generated autoregressively in 19-frame rollouts) depicting a safety-critical collision-evasion scenario from the ego-vehicle's perspective, rendered by UniMLVG conditioned on 3D bounding boxes, HD maps, and camera parameters.

## Method

SMD couples three components: (1) a GRPO-fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B selector that identifies the adversarial vehicle most likely to induce a hazard by reasoning over multi-view images, trained with automated labels from exhaustive collision simulation and a format+accuracy reward; (2) a two-stage diffusion-based trajectory generator that first produces a collision trajectory (guided by adversarial, no-collision, and on-road losses with time-decay weighting) and then converts it into a natural ego-vehicle evasion trajectory by re-simulating only the ego's motion under no-collision and on-road losses while freezing the adversarial vehicle's collision path—this avoids requiring the video generator to render actual crashes; (3) UniMLVG, a diffusion-based multi-view video generator trained on 1,498 hours of driving data, which renders the final collision-evasion trajectories into realistic multi-view video using 3D bounding boxes, HD maps, and camera conditions as control signals. The key insight is that generating evasions rather than explicit collisions keeps the output within the rendering capability of current video generators while preserving safety-critical characteristics.

## Results

On the NuScenes validation set (250 scenes), SMD generates multi-view safety-critical videos with a naturalness score of 0.56 (2AFC), nearly 3× higher than the Naive baseline (0.21) and comparable to Origin (0.63). The generated videos induce an average sample-level collision rate of 15.23% across three E2E planners (UniAD, SparseDrive, DiffusionDrive), a ~15× increase over Origin (1.04%), while reducing TTC from 0.96 to 0.69 and NC from 0.98 to 0.77. When used for fine-tuning DiffusionDrive, SMD data reduces collision rates by 30% on the standard NuScenes benchmark and up to 50% on safety-critical scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMD is compared against two baselines: Origin (original NuScenes trajectories rendered as video) and Naive (collision trajectories directly fed to the video generator). Against Naive, SMD dramatically improves video quality—naturalness rises from 0.21 to 0.56, FID drops from 23.35 to 20.63, and Subject Consistency improves from 0.66 to 0.86—while retaining a high collision rate (15.23% vs. 33.03% sample-level CR), confirming that the two-stage evasion design preserves safety-criticality without the severe visual artifacts of explicit collision rendering. Against Origin, SMD maintains comparable naturalness (0.56 vs. 0.63) and Subject Consistency (0.86 vs. 0.88) while increasing the average sample-level collision rate by ~15× (15.23% vs. 1.04%) and reducing TTC by 28% (0.69 vs. 0.96). For adversarial vehicle selection, the GRPO-fine-tuned VLM selector achieves F1=0.71, outperforming the Random-neighbor rule from Safe-Sim (Chang et al., 2024) at F1=0.51 and the Precision-first rule from CTG++ (Zhong et al., 2023a) at F1=0.60, with the largest gap in recall (0.68 vs. 0.44 and 0.50 respectively), indicating the VLM identifies more truly hazardous vehicles that heuristic methods miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: diffusion-based safety-critical trajectory simulation (CTG++/Zhong et al. 2023b, Safe-Sim/Chang et al. 2024) and multi-view driving video generation (UniMLVG/Chen et al. 2024b, Panacea/Wen et al. 2024). Prior trajectory-based methods produce non-visual outputs incompatible with end-to-end AD systems, while prior video-based approaches are either simulation-based (domain gap) or single-view/low-quality (ADV2, Open-Sora). SMD's key departure is coupling these via a GRPO-fine-tuned VLM for visually-grounded adversarial vehicle selection and a two-stage collision-to-evasion trajectory process that stays within the rendering capability of current video generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to produce multi-view, real-world-domain safety-critical driving videos, filling a critical gap for stress-testing and training end-to-end AD planners that consume video inputs. The finding that fine-tuning on benign NuScenes data alone can actually increase collision rates on safety-critical scenarios (while mixed data reduces them by ~50%) highlights a practical pitfall for AD training pipelines. Key limitations include reliance on complete initial scene annotations (not raw camera input), restriction to vehicle-to-vehicle interactions, and open-loop evaluation; extending to pedestrian scenarios and reducing annotation dependency remain open.
