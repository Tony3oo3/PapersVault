## Problem

Zeroth-order (ZO) optimization, which requires only forward passes and is ideal for neuromorphic on-chip learning, fails to train spiking neural networks (SNNs) due to the non-differentiable Heaviside spiking activation, which amplifies gradient estimation variance and causes training collapse. Meanwhile, standard backpropagation-through-time (BPTT) is too hardware-intensive for resource-constrained edge devices, requiring dedicated feedback circuits, activation storage, and weight transport that are impractical on neuromorphic chips.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) or event streams (DVS-Gesture) and outputs class labels via LIF spiking neurons operating over 4 timesteps (static datasets) or 20 timesteps (event-based). A key operational constraint is that training requires only forward passes—no backward computation, no stored inter-layer activations, and no feedback pathways—making it directly deployable on hardware without the memory and circuit overhead of BPTT.

## Method

The paper proposes Subspace-based Zeroth-Order (SZO) optimization, which first diagnoses why vanilla ZO fails on SNNs: the Heaviside step function introduces output variance (0.25) that is orders of magnitude larger than ReLU or Sigmoid for small perturbations, destabilizing gradient estimates. To fix this, SZO exploits the empirically verified low-dimensional structure of SNN training trajectories (top 10 PCA components capture >90% of variance) and restricts all ZO perturbations to a k-dimensional subspace (k=20–60 vs. d=11M–25M parameters), reducing gradient variance from O(d) to O(k). Two variants are provided: SZO-CGE (2k forward passes per step, perturbing along each subspace basis vector) and SZO-RGE (2q forward passes with random directions within the subspace). Theoretically, SZO achieves a convergence rate of O(k/√T) versus O(d/√T) for full-space ZO, and notably requires a relatively large perturbation scale μ (μ=1 in practice), contrasting with the small-μ regime standard for ANN ZO optimization.

## Results

On training from scratch, SZO-CGE achieves 86.21% on CIFAR-10 (vs. 86.74% BP), 73.35% on CIFAR-100 (vs. 74.51% BP), 96.65% on DVS-Gesture (vs. 97.57% BP), and 61.71% on ImageNet-1K (vs. 63.81% BP), closely approaching full-space BPTT with surrogate gradient. In continual training, SZO-CGE reaches 74.59% on CIFAR-100 and 63.73% on ImageNet-1K within just 4 and 3 epochs respectively, matching or exceeding BP's converged accuracy (74.42% and 64.36%) that requires ~100 and ~35 epochs. For unsupervised test-time adaptation, SZO-CGE achieves 79.69% on CIFAR-100-C (severity 5, Continual) versus 53.84% for Tent(BP), a 25.85-point improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Tent (Wang et al., 2021) and BN (Schneider et al., 2020) on unsupervised test-time adaptation, SZO-CGE substantially outperforms both: on CIFAR-100-C (severity 5, Reset), SZO-CGE reaches 72.47% vs. Tent's 52.54% and BN's 51.72%; under the harder Continual setting, the gap widens to 79.69% vs. 53.84% (Tent) and 51.72% (BN). On ImageNet-C Continual, SZO-CGE achieves 33.37% while Tent collapses to 3.89%, demonstrating that subspace-constrained ZO training stabilizes entropy-based adaptation. Against OPZO (Xiao et al., 2026) and DFA (Nøkland, 2016) on training from scratch, SZO-CGE dominates on deep architectures: on CIFAR-100/Spike-ResNet18, SZO-CGE scores 73.35% versus OPZO's 36.90% and DFA's 28.36%; on CIFAR-10/Spike-ResNet20, SZO-CGE reaches 86.21% versus OPZO's 54.45% and DFA's 38.79%. Against EqSpike (Martin et al., 2021) on MNIST/Spike-MLP, SZO-CGE achieves 97.56% vs. EqSpike's 96.87%, a modest but consistent edge. Overall, SZO-CGE matches or exceeds BP within 1–2% on all four training-from-scratch benchmarks while requiring zero backward passes, and it decisively outperforms all bio-plausible baselines (DFA, OPZO, EqSpike) on deep SNNs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeepZero framework (Chen et al., ICLR 2024) for zeroth-order optimization by identifying why it fails on SNNs—variance amplification from the Heaviside spike function—and fixing it by restricting perturbations to a PCA subspace derived from the low-dimensional trajectory hypothesis (Li et al., TPAMI 2022). It departs from prior BP-free SNN methods (DFA, OPZO, EqSpike) that still require feedback connections or local neuron-level operations, by achieving fully forward-pass-only training at scale. The subspace-ZO idea has appeared in LLM fine-tuning (LoRA + ZO), but this is the first application to SNNs and the first to provide a convergence-rate analysis specific to the non-continuous SNN loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SZO is the first forward-passes-only SNN training method that scales to ImageNet, directly addressing the hardware barriers (feedback circuits, activation storage, weight transport) that make BPTT impractical on neuromorphic chips. The theoretical finding that SNN ZO requires a *large* perturbation scale—opposite to the small-μ regime standard for ANNs—is a useful conceptual correction for the field. Key limitations: the subspace must be extracted from offline BP pretraining (so the pipeline is not end-to-end forward-only), evaluation is restricted to small-to-medium models and classification, and the hand-crafted subspace construction may not transfer well to novel downstream tasks. Extending to data-driven or meta-learned subspace construction, larger models, and sequential tasks are natural next steps.
