## Problem

Mahalanobis-based OOD detectors remain strong baselines for vision models, yet their performance varies dramatically across pretrained backbones and shifts sharply with pretraining data or fine-tuning regime, making reliable deployment difficult. The paper asks which properties of the in-distribution feature space determine when a Mahalanobis detector succeeds or fails, and whether this variability can be controlled post-hoc without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20261853/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes last-layer feature vectors z ∈ ℝ^d extracted from pretrained vision backbones (ViT, CLIP, BEiT-v2, EVA-02, DeiT3, DINOv2, MAE) on ImageNet-1K training features, with no architectural modification to the backbone. It produces two outputs: (i) an OOD confidence score via a Mahalanobis or Relative Mahalanobis quadratic form evaluated on φ_β-transformed features, and (ii) a per-model radial exponent β̂ selected from a discrete grid B ⊂ [−2, 2] (step 0.25) using only ID geometry signals. The β̂ is applied identically across all OOD benchmarks for a given model, and class means and tied covariance are refit in the transformed φ_β-space for each β.

## Method

The paper introduces radially scaled ℓ₂ normalization φ_β(z) = z/‖z‖^β, a direction-preserving radial deformation that continuously interpolates between standard features (β=0) and unit-sphere projection (β=1), reshaping the geometry seen by an otherwise fixed Mahalanobis quadratic detector. A unified stability theory (UST) factorizes any single-quadratic Mahalanobis score into a size channel (residual-norm dispersion, linked to local intrinsic dimensionality m_k) and a stretch channel (whitening-weight heterogeneity, linked to the within-class log-eigenvalue spectral slope |s|), with a predominantly negative cross-term that partially cancels the two. The key practical contribution is an ID-only geometry proxy P(β) = m_k(β)·|s(β)| whose interior turning point on the β grid selects β̂ without OOD access; the product form enforces a gating structure where a flat spectrum (|s|≈0) suppresses the LID contribution. Empirically, proxy-selected β̂ consistently outperforms fixed baselines (β=0, β=1) across 22 backbones and five OOD benchmarks, with the largest gains on near-OOD datasets, and approaches oracle-tuned performance under regret analysis.

## Results

Across 12 vision foundation-model backbones evaluated on five OOD benchmarks (NINCO, iNaturalist, SSB-Hard, OpenImages-O, Textures), the proxy-selected radially scaled detector RS-MD achieves a mean FPR@95 of 35.3%, outperforming standard MD (β=0) at 37.2% and MD++ (β=1) at 36.0%. The analogous RS-RMD variant reaches 35.8% mean FPR@95, beating RMD (37.2%) and RMD++ (36.1%). The largest absolute gains appear on near-OOD datasets (NINCO, SSB-Hard), where proxy selection reduces FPR@95 by up to ~1.1 pp over the fixed β=1 baseline (Table 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard Mahalanobis distance of Lee et al. (2018) (MD, β=0), RS-MD reduces mean FPR@95 by 1.9 pp (37.2→35.3) and RS-RMD reduces it by 1.4 pp (37.2→35.8), with the largest per-model gains on EVA02-In21k (40.8→39.5) and CLIP-In1k (40.2→37.6). Against Mahalanobis++ of Mueller & Hein (2025) (MD++, β=1), RS-MD improves by 0.7 pp on average (36.0→35.3), winning on 7 of 12 models; the gap is smaller because β=1 already captures much of the radial benefit, but the proxy-selected β̂ still finds model-specific optima that the fixed unit-sphere projection misses. Against Relative Mahalanobis of Ren et al. (2021) (RMD, β=0), RS-RMD improves by 1.4 pp on average (37.2→35.8), with notable gains on DeiT3 (40.8→39.4) and EVA02-In1k (40.3→39.6). In all three comparisons, the proxy-selected β̂ also reduces worst-case oracle regret relative to both fixed baselines (Figure 8), confirming that the ID-only geometry signal P(β)=m_k(β)|s(β)| captures enough structure to approach oracle-tuned performance without OOD access.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mahalanobis OOD detection lineage (Lee et al. 2018 → Ren et al. 2021's RMD → Mueller & Hein 2025's Mahalanobis++ with fixed L2 normalization) by asking *why* the same quadratic detector succeeds or fails across representations. Rather than modifying the scoring rule or fixing normalization to the unit sphere, it introduces a continuous radial-scaling parameter β that generalizes both standard features (β=0) and L2 normalization (β=1), and grounds the choice of β in two ID-only geometric signals: local intrinsic dimensionality (from Ma et al. 2018) and within-class spectral slope. The size-stretch factorization in Section 7 provides a clean algebraic bridge between these geometric quantities and the variance of the quadratic score, positioning the work at the intersection of representation geometry and detector design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is a training-free, OOD-free procedure to tune Mahalanobis-style detectors via a single radial exponent, which is directly deployable in safety-critical vision pipelines. However, the absolute FPR gains over fixed baselines (β=0 or β=1) are modest—typically 1–2 percentage points—and the proxy-selected β does not always match the oracle optimum. Open questions include whether the m_k·|s| product generalizes beyond quadratic detectors or to non-vision modalities, whether the compensatory trade-off between local dimensionality and spectral concentration holds under distribution shift at deployment, and whether the ID-only selection rule remains stable when the ID training set is small or noisy.
