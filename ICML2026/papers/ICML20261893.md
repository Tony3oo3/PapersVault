## Problem

Spectra-conditioned molecular structure elucidation remains difficult because distinct molecules can produce nearly identical spectral signatures, and existing diffusion-based generators rely on a single global spectral embedding that struggles to capture fine-grained chemical evidence from limited paired data. Additionally, the standard inference strategy of repeated full-chain sampling followed by post-hoc selection is computationally wasteful, and most prior work targets only 1D/2D structures while 3D geometry—critical for downstream applications—remains comparatively unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-modal 1D spectral signals (IR, Raman, UV-Vis) sampled on fixed grids as input, which are patchified and encoded by a Transformer to produce both a global spectral embedding and a 103-dimensional motif probability vector. The output is a joint 2D-3D molecular structure comprising 3D Cartesian coordinates, atom-type node features, and bond-type edge features, decoded into a canonical SMILES for evaluation. The spectra are enantiomer-invariant, so mirror-inverted 3D outputs are considered valid matches.

## Method

MAST introduces an interpretable motif prior—a multi-label predictor mapping spectra to presence probabilities over 103 chemically meaningful local motifs (functional groups, ring systems, carbon-skeleton fragments)—that is injected at every denoising layer via adaptive normalization alongside a global spectral embedding, shrinking the effective hypothesis space. The denoiser is an SE(3)-equivariant Transformer with 8 stacked blocks that jointly refines coordinates, node attributes, and edge attributes under a variance-preserving diffusion process trained with weighted reconstruction loss. At inference, the reverse diffusion is discretized into 50 checkpoint segments forming a tree, over which MCTS performs UCB-guided selection, segment-level expansion with varying noise realizations and guidance scales, fast-rollout evaluation using a composite spectra-structure alignment and motif-consistency reward, and softmax backup to propagate values—adaptively concentrating computation on high-reward denoising prefixes while pruning low-value branches early.

## Results

On the QM9S multi-spectra benchmark, MAST with MCTS-guided inference achieves 94.89% exact recovery (Acc@1), 0.1048 MCES, and 95.34% MapAcc for 3D geometry, substantially outperforming the strongest diffusion baseline DiffSpectra (60.68% Acc@1, 1.0514 MCES, 83.84% MapAcc). Without tree search, MAST already reaches 79.12% Acc@1, a 30.4-point improvement over DiffSpectra. MAST also achieves the best generative quality metrics: 97.1% V&U&N, 0.044 FCD, and 0.997 scaffold similarity, while maintaining 99.9% atom-level stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAST with MCTS dominates all baselines on QM9S by a wide margin. Against the strongest diffusion baseline DiffSpectra (Wang et al., 2025b), MAST improves Acc@1 from 60.68% to 94.89% (+34.2 pp), reduces MCES from 1.0514 to 0.1048 (10× lower), and improves 3D fidelity (RMSD 0.7843 vs. 0.8091 Å; MapAcc 95.34% vs. 83.84%). Against spectra-to-SMILES baselines, the gap is even more dramatic: the best LLM-based method SpectraLLM-Raman (Su et al.) achieves only 3.14% Acc@1, while Spectra2Structure (Kanakala et al., 2024) and IR-to-Structure (Alberts et al., 2024) score near 0%. For generative quality (Table 2), MAST with MCTS achieves 97.1% V&U&N and 0.044 FCD, outperforming JODO (89.5% V&U&N, 0.138 FCD) and CDGS (89.8% V&U&N, 0.798 FCD), while maintaining comparable or better stability (99.9% AtomStable, 98.8% MolStable). Under spectral perturbations (Table 9), MAST retains 73.83% Acc@1 at SNR=30 dB with drift=5, whereas DiffSpectra collapses to 9.38%, confirming the robustness advantage of motif-augmented conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAST builds on the joint 2D-3D SE(3)-equivariant diffusion paradigm established by JODO and MolDiff for molecular generation, and on MolSpectra's multi-modal spectral encoding pipeline. It departs from DiffSpectra (the closest prior work) in two key ways: (1) introducing an explicit, interpretable spectra-to-motif prior that is injected throughout denoising as compositional chemical evidence, rather than relying solely on a global spectral embedding; and (2) reformulating reverse diffusion as a checkpointed tree and applying MCTS with segment-wise adaptive guidance, borrowing the test-time-scaling philosophy from LLM tree-search methods (Tree of Thoughts, MCTS for reasoning) but applying it to diffusion sampling trajectories. The motif prior conceptually extends earlier functional-group prediction work (Fine et al., Enders et al.) from a scoring/ranking signal into a generative conditioning mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 94.89% exact recovery on QM9S represents a substantial leap over prior diffusion-based elucidation (60.68% for DiffSpectra), and the robustness gap under spectral noise (MAST ~74% vs. DiffSpectra ~0.2% at SNR=20dB + drift=5) suggests that explicit motif-level evidence is fundamentally more stable than global spectral representations for the inverse problem. However, all evaluations are on QM9S with DFT-simulated spectra of small molecules; generalization to real experimental spectra, larger chemical spaces, and more complex modalities (NMR, MS) remains untested. The MCTS-guided inference provides a practical test-time scaling knob without retraining, but the computational overhead (up to 3000 denoising steps, ~125s per sample) may limit deployment in high-throughput screening. Open questions include whether the motif vocabulary and predictor transfer across spectral modalities and whether the one-to-many ambiguity can be further resolved with orthogonal evidence.
