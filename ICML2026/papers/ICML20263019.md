## Problem

Standard diffusion sampling for graph generation relies on uniform time-stepping, implicitly assuming that the reverse-time SDE evolves at a constant rate. In practice, the dynamics are highly non-uniform: smooth in high-noise regimes but stiff and rapidly varying near the data distribution, causing fixed-step solvers to waste computation on smooth regions while under-resolving critical structural transitions. Existing adaptive schedules are either static heuristics (e.g., quadratic reparameterization) or state-space error estimators that ignore the intrinsic geometry of the evolving probability distributions.

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free, plug-and-play sampling module that takes an initial noise graph state (node features X₀ and adjacency A₀) and a pretrained denoising network as input, and produces a generated graph (molecular or general) as output. Unlike fixed-step baselines where the total number of steps is a pre-defined hyperparameter, the step count here is an adaptive outcome of the trajectory's intrinsic geometry, typically yielding fewer function evaluations (e.g., 787 vs. 1000 on QM9). The sampler is compatible with both first-order Euler-Maruyama and second-order Heun integrators and can be applied over the full trajectory or restricted to empirically selected high-variation intervals.

## Method

The paper reinterprets the sequence of numerical transition kernels along the reverse-time SDE as a parametric curve on a Riemannian statistical manifold equipped with the Fisher-Rao metric. From the Fisher-Rao line element for the Gaussian transition kernel, it derives the Drift Variation Score (DVS), a training-free scalar that quantifies the instantaneous rate of distributional change as ‖Δf‖²/g². The DVS drives a closed-loop adaptive sampler that scales each step size via a power-law rule Δt ∝ (κ_ref/V̄)^β, enforcing approximately constant informational arc-length per step. For graph data, separate DVS values are computed for node and edge drifts, smoothed via exponential moving average, and the final step is the minimum of the two component steps (bottleneck coupling). Theoretical analysis confirms that DVS characterizes local stiffness in the Fisher-Rao sense, and the noise-scale contribution to the line element is shown to be asymptotically negligible (O(dt) vs. O(√dt) for drift), justifying the drift-only formulation.

## Results

On QM9 with the GRUM model, DVS-Euler achieves an FCD of 0.095 (vs. 0.107 for Fixed-Step and 0.107 for Quadratic) with 99.53% validity, while DVS-Heun reaches FCD 0.099 with 99.55% validity. On ZINC250k, DVS-Euler reduces FCD to 2.092 (vs. 2.207 Fixed-Step, 2.194 Quadratic) and DVS-Heun to 2.086. On general graph benchmarks (Planar, SBM), DVS consistently achieves the lowest MMD values across degree, clustering, orbit, and spectral metrics, e.g., reducing Spectral MMD from 0.0060 to 0.0052 on Planar (Euler) and from 0.0059 to 0.0049 (Heun).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DVS against two scheduling strategies (Fixed-Step and Quadratic) applied to both Euler-Maruyama and Heun solvers, using pretrained GRUM and GDSS checkpoints. DVS consistently outperforms Fixed-Step across all metrics on all datasets: on QM9 (Euler), FCD drops from 0.107 to 0.095 (−11.2%) and validity rises from 99.43% to 99.53%; on ZINC250k (Euler), FCD drops from 2.207 to 2.092 (−5.2%). Against the Quadratic schedule, DVS also wins on FCD in every setting (e.g., 0.095 vs. 0.107 on QM9-Euler, 2.092 vs. 2.194 on ZINC250k-Euler), confirming that the geometry-aware adaptive signal surpasses the static power-law heuristic. On general graphs (Planar, SBM), DVS achieves the lowest Spectral and Orbit MMD in both solver settings, while the Quadratic schedule sometimes degrades Degree MMD relative to Fixed-Step (e.g., 0.0006 vs. 0.0002 on Planar-Euler). Notably, DVS-Euler rivals or surpasses Fixed-Step Heun on FCD (0.095 vs. 0.109 on QM9), suggesting that equal arc-length step allocation is more impactful than the local error order of the integrator for graph-structured data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the reverse-time SDE framework for score-based generative modeling (Song et al., 2021b) and the multi-component graph diffusion formulation with coupled node/edge dynamics (Jo et al., 2022), extending both with an information-geometric lens. It departs from prior adaptive sampling strategies—quadratic schedules, AYS, JYS—which rely on static power-law prescriptions or state-space truncation-error estimates, by instead deriving a training-free step-size criterion (the Drift Variation Score) from the Fisher-Rao metric on the statistical manifold of transition kernels. The closest conceptual neighbor is Song & Lai (2024), which uses Fisher information to reweight the score function; here, Fisher information is repurposed to govern discretization rather than dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DVS sampler offers a principled, training-free, plug-and-play mechanism that reduces NFE by ~20% while improving distributional fidelity, making it immediately applicable to existing pretrained graph diffusion checkpoints without retraining. The observation that equal arc-length progression on the statistical manifold can outperform a higher-order integrator with uniform steps is a useful conceptual reframing for practitioners choosing between solver order and step allocation. Limitations include dataset-specific hyperparameters (κ_ref, γ, active range) that require empirical tuning, and the method has only been validated on two model architectures (GRUM, GDSS) and relatively small-scale benchmarks; extension to 3D molecular generation and larger-scale graph tasks remains open.
