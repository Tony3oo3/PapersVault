## Problem

Scalable MLIPs built on local radius graphs with distance cutoffs cannot accurately capture long-range interactions (electrostatics, polarization, dispersion) critical for biomolecules and electrolytes, forcing current approaches to bolt on explicit physics-based terms (Ewald/PME, dispersion corrections, charge solvers) that are validated only on small, targeted datasets. The paper asks whether long-range coupling and other geometric inductive biases can instead be learned data-driven at scale, removing the need for hand-crafted physics components.

## Input / Output

The model consumes atomic numbers, 3D Cartesian positions, and optional per-atom charge/spin features, constructing a differentiable kNN graph (rather than a fixed-radius graph) to enable energy-conserving gradient-based forces. It outputs a scalar potential energy, per-atom forces (supervised either directly or as the negative energy gradient for strict conservation), and an optional gradient-based stress tensor. The architecture targets systems of roughly 10³–10⁵ atoms, where the O(N²) all-to-all node attention remains tractable on a single GPU.

## Method

AllScAIP uses a two-stage attention backbone: (1) neighborhood self-attention over a fixed k-neighbor stencil (O(Nk)) resolves local, anisotropic geometry, and (2) all-to-all node self-attention over all atoms in the graph (O(N²)) mixes information globally in a single step, providing an infinite receptive field for long-range coupling. Both stages use off-the-shelf multi-head self-attention CUDA kernels (xformers), with optional geometric encodings injected into the attention logits: Legendre Angular Encoding (real spherical harmonics modulating Q/K for directional structure) in the local stage, and Euclidean Rotary Position Encoding (sinc-kernel radial bias) in the global stage. Energy conservation is enforced through a differentiable kNN graph so that forces are exact energy gradients, and ReZero initialization on the node-attention residual allows the model to learn non-PBC extensivity rather than enforcing it by construction. The central empirical finding is that these geometric encodings improve sample efficiency at small data/model scale but their marginal benefit diminishes or reverses as both grow, while the all-to-all node attention remains the most durable source of long-range accuracy across all scales tested.

## Results

On the full OMol25 dataset (102M training samples), AllScAIP-md-d. achieves the best overall energy MAE of 0.64 meV/atom with a competitive force MAE of 5.24 meV/Å, outperforming eSEN-md-d. (0.84 meV/atom, 4.76 meV/Å) and GemNet-OC (0.66 meV/atom, 6.52 meV/Å). On the OMol25 physics-based evaluation suite, AllScAIP-md-d. attains the best average rank (3.43), with a ~90% reduction in distance-scaling error compared to the second-best model. On SPICE, AllScAIP-sm-cons. achieves the lowest energy and force errors across all seven test splits (e.g., 0.06 meV/atom and 0.95 meV/Å on DES370K dimers), surpassing MACELES-M, eSEN, and EScAIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On OMol25 (full 102M), AllScAIP-md-d. achieves the best total energy MAE (0.64 meV/atom), beating eSEN-md-d. (0.84) by 24% and GemNet-OC (0.66) by 3%, while eSEN-md-d. retains a slight force advantage (4.76 vs. 5.24 meV/Å). On the OMol25 physics-based evaluation suite, AllScAIP-md-d. ranks first by average rank (3.43) versus eSEN-md-d. (4.14) and UMA-M-1p1 (4.86), with a dramatic ~90% reduction in distance-scaling error over the second-best model. On SPICE, AllScAIP-sm-cons. outperforms MACELES-M, eSEN, and EScAIP across all seven test splits, achieving the lowest energy and force errors (e.g., 0.06 meV/atom on DES370K dimers vs. 0.47 for MACELES-M). On OMat24, AllScAIP-md-ft-cons. (10.7 meV/atom, 54.3 meV/Å) beats UMA-S (11.3, 57.1) but trails UMA-M (10.0, 47.3) and eqV2-L (9.6, 43.1). On OC20, AllScAIP-md-d. (59.3 meV energy, 17.6 meV/Å force) surpasses UMA-S (63.6, 24.1) but remains behind UMA-M (43.1, 15.8) and UMA-L (32.6, 12.0), which were trained on substantially more data (459M vs. 240M samples).

## Relation to Prior Work

AllScAIP extends the EScAIP backbone (Qu & Krishnapriyan, 2024) by adding an all-to-all node self-attention stage for global communication, Legendre Angular Encoding for directional features, and Euclidean Rotary Position Encoding (adapted from Frank et al., 2024) for radial information, while adopting the differentiable kNN graph construction from Liu et al. (2026) to enable energy-conserving forces. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant MLIP paradigm that injects explicit long-range physics (Ewald/PME electrostatics, charge equilibration, dispersion corrections) into local message-passing networks, arguing instead that a data-driven all-to-all attention mechanism can learn long-range interactions without such terms. It also positions against SE(3)-equivariant architectures (MACE, eSEN, Equiformer) that encode direction via spherical-harmonic irreps, proposing that scalar attention with sufficient scale can allocate capacity where needed without the rigidity of a fixed SH decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, well-ablated recipe showing that a prior-light, attention-based MLIP can achieve state-of-the-art accuracy on the largest molecular dataset to date (OMol25) while performing stable long-timescale MD that recovers experimental observables, suggesting the field may not need to commit to heavy physics-based inductive biases as data and compute scale. The O(N²) cost of all-to-all node attention is a practical ceiling (targeting ~10³–10⁵ atoms), and the authors identify hierarchical pooling, linear-time attention, and mixture-of-experts routing as natural next steps to push the boundary further. An open question is whether the 'inductive biases are learnable under scale' conclusion generalizes beyond the AllScAIP architecture family and to truly out-of-distribution chemical environments, since all evidence comes from a single model lineage trained on OMol25/OMat24/OC20/SPICE.
