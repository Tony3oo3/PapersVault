## Problem

Existing dataset condensation methods are ill-suited for diffusion model training: synthetic generation produces low-fidelity samples that distort the data manifold, while real subset selection relies on fixed or heuristic criteria (e.g., one-dimensional difficulty ranking) that fail to preserve the multi-modal distributional geometry required by diffusion likelihood objectives. No prior method formulates subset selection as an explicit, training-aligned distribution alignment problem in representation space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20260798/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full labeled image dataset (ImageNet-1K, 1.28M images at 256×256 or 512×512) and produces a compact real-image subset of 10K, 50K, or 100K samples (0.8%–8% of the full set), with equal per-class allocation (10, 50, or 100 images per class). Feature extraction is performed per-class using InceptionV3 at 299×299, and the selected subset is then used to train diffusion models (DiT-L/2 or SiT-L/2) from scratch. The selection is fully discrete—no synthetic pixels are generated—and the output is a set of original image indices.

## Method

The paper reformulates real subset selection as a geometry-aware distribution alignment problem solved via one-sided partial optimal transport (POT) in a learned feature space. The one-sided POT fully transports source (subset) mass while relaxing the target (full dataset) side with a capacity factor κ, allowing unmatched mass in low-density peripheral regions so that alignment concentrates on the core manifold; it is solved efficiently via a dummy-source reformulation and entropic Sinkhorn iterations. Two lightweight regularizers complement the geometric objective: a mean-variance term aligning first- and second-order feature statistics, and a confidence term penalizing low-probability class assignments to filter unreliable geometric anchors. The composite objective is optimized over the discrete selection space using a two-stage strategy: a greedy geometry-guided construction that iteratively adds the sample minimizing marginal transport cost, followed by a swap-based refinement that corrects early myopic choices through targeted exchanges between selected and unselected samples.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with DiT-L/2 (100K iterations, 10K data budget), the proposed method achieves FID 3.43, IS 414.3, Precision 0.78, and Recall 0.28, outperforming the strongest baseline D²C (FID 4.20, IS 283.6) by 18% in FID and 46% in IS. At the 50K budget, FID drops to 11.01 (vs. D²C's 14.81), and at 100K budget to 17.09 (vs. D²C's 22.55). At 512×512 resolution, the method achieves FID 6.17 and IS 451.0, substantially exceeding D²C (FID 14.8, IS 109.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all five baselines across every metric and data budget. Against D²C (the only prior method designed for diffusion training), the gap is most pronounced at the 10K budget: FID 3.43 vs. 4.20 (−18%) and IS 414.3 vs. 283.6 (+46%), with the advantage widening at larger budgets (FID 17.09 vs. 22.55 at 100K). CCS achieves the highest IS among baselines at 10K (364.9) but still trails the proposed method by 49.4 points in IS and 2.02 in FID. DQ is competitive on FID (4.56) but lags on IS (267.8 vs. 414.3) and Recall (0.25 vs. 0.28). Classical selection methods K-Center and Herding are far less competitive, with FID values of 50.77 and 40.75 respectively at the 10K budget, confirming that heuristic criteria without distributional alignment are poorly suited for diffusion training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dataset condensation literature by reformulating real subset selection for diffusion model training as a geometry-aware distribution alignment problem, departing from the scalar difficulty-ranking approach of D²C (Huang et al., 2026), the only prior work explicitly targeting diffusion training. It builds on the partial optimal transport framework of Chapel et al. (2020) but introduces a one-sided (asymmetric) variant where the condensed set must fully participate in matching while the full dataset tolerates unmatched mass, a structural choice motivated by the capacity mismatch between subset and full data. The two-stage discrete optimization (greedy construction plus swap-based refinement) draws on submodular selection theory but is tailored to the entropic Sinkhorn-based POT objective rather than generic submodular surrogates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical path to training high-quality diffusion models on 0.8–8% of ImageNet with 7–8× faster selection than prior approaches, which is directly relevant to resource-constrained generative modeling. A notable open question is whether the geometry-aware alignment transfers beyond ImageNet classification to open-vocabulary or text-conditioned generation, where the class-conditional selection assumption may not hold. The paper also leaves open whether the one-sided POT formulation generalizes to multi-modal or video diffusion settings, and whether the InceptionV3-based feature space is truly optimal for capturing the manifold structure that diffusion likelihood objectives require.
