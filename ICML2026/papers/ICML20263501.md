## Problem

Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) and its unnormalized variant (USAM) improve generalization by penalizing loss sharpness, but like most optimizers they are highly sensitive to the learning rate, requiring costly grid-search tuning or heuristic schedulers. No principled adaptive step size with convergence guarantees existed for SAM-type updates, leaving a gap between the strong empirical performance of SAM and the lack of tuning-free theoretical analysis.

## Input / Output

At each iteration the method consumes the mini-batch loss evaluated at the perturbed point e^t, the gradient at e^t, the gradient at the current iterate x^t, and the sharpness radius ρ_t, all of which are already computed during the USAM forward pass. It outputs a closed-form step size γ_t (capped at γ_b in the stochastic setting) and the updated parameter vector x^{t+1}. No problem constants (L, μ) or line search are required; the only user-specified hyperparameters are the radius ρ and the cap γ_b.

## Method

The paper derives Polyak-type step sizes for USAM by repeating the classical Polyak derivation—upper-bounding ‖x^{t+1}−x*‖² and minimizing over γ_t—but accounting for the extra perturbation term ρ_t⟨∇f(e^t), ∇f(x^t)⟩ that appears in the USAM update. This yields a closed-form numerator f(e^t)−f*−ρ_t⟨∇f(e^t), ∇f(x^t)⟩ divided by ‖∇f(e^t)‖², which is shown to be automatically non-negative when ρ_t ≤ 1/L, eliminating the need for a ReLU safeguard. A key descent inequality is then established, from which linear convergence in squared distance follows for μ-strongly convex objectives and an O(1/T) rate for convex objectives in the deterministic case. In the stochastic finite-sum setting, a cap γ_b is added (analogous to SPS_max), and the method converges linearly to a neighborhood whose radius is governed by a variance measure σ²; under interpolation (σ²=0) the neighborhood vanishes and exact linear convergence is recovered. Setting ρ=0 reduces the scheduler to the classical Polyak step size or SPS_max, unifying standard and sharpness-aware gradient methods in a single framework.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-32, USAM with the proposed Stochastic Polyak Scheduler achieves 91.81–92.24% top-1 test accuracy across sharpness radii ρ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4}, consistently outperforming both tuned constant-step USAM (89.56–90.56%) and USAM with Cosine Annealing (86.52–90.01%). The advantage grows with ρ: at ρ=0.4, SPS reaches 92.01% while Cosine Annealing drops to 86.52% (a 5.5-point gap). On the same benchmark with normalized SAM, SPS similarly leads at 90.79–92.24% versus 84.61–90.53% for the baselines. In synthetic ridge regression, the Polyak Scheduler converges to the optimum in the fewest iterations among all adaptive SAM variants tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed Stochastic Polyak Scheduler against two standard learning-rate baselines (tuned constant step size and Cosine Annealing) on CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-20/32, and against three recent adaptive SAM methods (AdaSAM, LightSAM-I/II/III, SA-SAM) on synthetic ridge regression. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-32, SPS outperforms constant-step USAM by 1.2–2.5 points and Cosine Annealing by 1.8–5.5 points, with the gap widening as the sharpness radius ρ increases. The same pattern holds for normalized SAM (Table 4), where SPS leads by up to 6.1 points over Cosine Annealing at ρ=0.4. In the synthetic comparison, the Polyak Scheduler converges to the optimum in the fewest iterations; the AdaGrad-based LightSAM variants reach comparable accuracy but require noticeably more iterations, while AdaSAM and SA-SAM stall further from the optimum. Notably, the Polyak Scheduler is the only adaptive method in the comparison that comes with convergence guarantees for smooth, strongly convex objectives, and it requires no learning-rate tuning beyond the perturbation radius ρ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate threads: the classical Polyak step size (Polyak, 1969) and its stochastic adaptation SPS_max (Loizou et al., 2021), on one hand, and Sharpness-Aware Minimization (Foret et al., 2021) / USAM (Andriushchenko & Flammarion, 2022) on the other. Prior adaptive SAM methods (AdaSAM, LightSAM, SA-SAM) rely on AdaGrad/Adam-style running statistics or local smoothness estimates, but none employ the function-value-driven Polyak mechanism. The paper's key conceptual move is to re-derive the Polyak upper-bound argument in the presence of the USAM perturbation, yielding a closed-form step size that depends only on the loss and gradient at the perturbed point, and to show that when ρ=0 the construction recovers the classical GD-PS and SPS_max rules exactly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical value is reducing the learning-rate tuning burden for SAM-type methods, which is a well-known pain point in practice; the Polyak scheduler requires no knowledge of L, μ, or any problem constants beyond a lower bound on the loss. Theoretically, the paper provides the first convergence guarantees for adaptive USAM under Polyak-type step sizes without requiring bounded variance, bounded gradients, or growth conditions that prior adaptive SAM analyses impose. Open questions include extending the analysis to PL and non-convex objectives, developing principled schedules for the sharpness radius ρ_t, and validating the approach at scale (e.g., LLMs or ImageNet), since the DNN experiments are limited to CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-20/32.
