## Problem

Existing geolocation benchmarks for vision-language models focus almost exclusively on spatial inference (retrieval rank or coordinate error) while ignoring temporal reasoning. No unified evaluation framework probes joint geo-temporal understanding from subtle physical cues in ground-level imagery, nor enforces cross-field consistency constraints such as month-season-hemisphere alignment or daylight-phase compatibility with predicted time and location.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20264922/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a single ground-level RGB image (deliberately non-iconic, with landmarks and text suppressed) and requires a structured nine-field prediction: four temporal attributes (season, month, local time in HH:MM, daylight phase) and five geographic attributes (continent, country, Köppen-Geiger climate zone, environment type, latitude-longitude). Scoring is not retrieval-based but uses windowed temporal accuracy (≤1 hour), mean absolute error in minutes, great-circle geodesic distance in km, and explicit cross-field consistency diagnostics (e.g., rejecting 'snow in July' for a Northern Hemisphere prediction).

## Method

TimeSpot is constructed via a hybrid annotation pipeline: ground-truth labels are derived deterministically from capture metadata and solar ephemerides (for temporal fields) and from coordinates mapped through Köppen-Geiger classification and geographic databases (for spatial fields), then verified by a small team of geospatial-domain annotators who reject physically inconsistent samples. The evaluation protocol benchmarks 20+ open- and closed-source VLMs under uniform, metadata-free, open-ended prompting, reporting categorical accuracy, windowed time accuracy, geodesic distance, expected calibration error, and hemisphere-flip / hard-OOD robustness splits. As a diagnostic intervention, the authors fine-tune Qwen-VL2.5-3B-Instruct via LoRA on a stratified 40% split, revealing that SFT improves both country and time prediction but exposes gradient interference between illumination-invariant spatial cues and illumination-sensitive temporal cues under shared adapter parameters.

## Results

On TimeSpot (1,455 images, 80 countries), the strongest proprietary model Gemini-2.5-Flash achieves 77.25% country accuracy and 917.61 km mean geodesic distance, while its reasoning variant Gemini-2.5-Flash-Thinking improves to 77.59% country and 892.54 km MD. Temporal performance is dramatically weaker: time-of-day accuracy (within 1-hour window) peaks at only 33.74% (GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking), with mean absolute errors of 3:36–4:30 hours across all models, compared to 1:36 for human domain experts. Season accuracy tops out at 65.81% (o4-mini) and month at 48.20% (o4-mini), far below the 86.56% and 46.06% achieved by human experts respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Flash and its Thinking variant dominate spatial metrics (country 77.25–77.59%, MD 892–918 km), outperforming GPT-5-mini (68.27%, 1390 km) and Claude 3.5 Haiku (55.53%, 2270 km) by 9–22 percentage points on country accuracy. However, no proprietary model leads on temporal tasks: o4-mini achieves the best season (65.81%) and month (48.20%) accuracy, while GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking (an open-source reasoning model) leads on time-of-day accuracy (33.74%), surpassing all proprietary baselines by 3–12 points. Open-source models show extreme variance: Qwen-VL2.5-7B-Instruct matches proprietary models on categorical geography (country 73.96%) but fails catastrophically on coordinates (MD 4720 km, lat MAE 32.94°), whereas GLM-4.5V-106B-MoE achieves competitive MD (1281 km) with 69.68% country accuracy. All VLMs fall substantially short of human experts on temporal fields (best VLM time MAE 3:36 vs. expert 1:36; best VLM DLP 64.09% vs. expert 92.22%), while the strongest VLMs actually exceed human experts on coarse spatial memorization (Gemini-2.5-Flash country 77.25% vs. expert 67.89%), confirming that the gap is in continuous physical reasoning rather than pattern recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geolocation benchmark lineage (VIGOR, GeoCLIP, OpenStreetView 5M, LLMGeo, IMAGEO-Bench, ETHAN) by adding explicit temporal prediction (season, month, local time, daylight phase) alongside geographic attributes, requiring joint structured output over a nine-field schema with physical consistency constraints. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) suppressing landmark-centric and text-rich cues to force reliance on subtle physical signals (solar geometry, phenology, shadow direction), and (2) introducing cross-field consistency diagnostics (month-season-hemisphere alignment, daylight-time compatibility) that retrieval-based protocols cannot capture. The remote-sensing VLM benchmarks (HRVQA, GEOBench-VLM) are acknowledged but positioned as aerial-view and classification-focused, whereas TimeSpot targets ground-level, fine-grained, metadata-free inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a previously unquantified gap: even the strongest VLMs achieve ~77% country accuracy but only ~33% time-of-day accuracy, with frequent physically impossible predictions (e.g., snow in July in the Northern Hemisphere), revealing that spatial competence does not imply temporal understanding. The SFT analysis showing gradient interference between illumination-invariant (country) and illumination-sensitive (time) objectives provides a concrete diagnostic for why joint geo-temporal training is non-trivial. Open questions include whether explicit solar-geometry modeling, constraint-aware decoding, or RL-based training can close the ~24-point gap between best VLMs and human experts on time prediction, and whether the 1,455-image scale is sufficient for robust conclusions given the long-tail country distribution.
