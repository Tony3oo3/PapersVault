## Problem

Prior cross-domain offline RL methods (IGDF, OTDF, DVDF) are validated only on single-domain, single-behavior-policy source datasets, yet real-world source data is often aggregated from multiple environments with diverse dynamics and policies. In this heterogeneous setting, the paper identifies a critical yet overlooked issue—value misassignment—where a single advantage function pretrained on the entire mixed source dataset cannot distinguish between different dynamics modalities, thereby misassigning optimality scores, misleading data filtering toward suboptimal samples, and loosening the theoretical suboptimality gap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a heterogeneous source dataset of ~1M transitions organized as variable-length trajectories τ = (s₀, a₀, s₁, …, s_T), collected from four source domains (kinematic-easy, kinematic-medium, morphology-easy, morphology-medium) under a shared behavior policy (medium-replay or medium-expert), plus a limited target dataset (10% of D4RL MuJoCo data). It produces a target-domain policy via IQL, a per-trajectory dynamics modality representation z ∈ ℝ^d, a modality-aware advantage function A(s,a,z) = Q(s,a,z) − V(s,z), and a filtered subset of source transitions (top ξ-quantile by score) used for final policy training.

## Method

V2A unifies dynamics alignment, value alignment, and value assignment in three stages. First, it learns temporally-consistent modality representations via an EM procedure: a trajectory-level RNN encoder q_ψ outputs a single latent z ~ N(m(τ), σ²(τ)) shared across all steps, while a step-level ensemble dynamics decoder p_θ predicts each transition s_{t+1}|s_t, a_t, z; the objective is a Temporally-Consistent ELBO (TC-ELBO) that enforces one z per trajectory rather than one per transition. Second, it relabels the source dataset with z and trains Q(s,a,z) and V(s,z) under the Sparse-QL scheme, so the resulting advantage A(s,a,z) assigns distinct optimality scores to the same (s,a) pair under different dynamics modalities, directly resolving value misassignment. Third, it constructs a composite score f(s,a,s′,z) = λ·h(s,a,s′) + (1−λ)·Norm(A(s,a,z))—where h is the dynamics-alignment score from IGDF or OTDF—and selects the top ξ-quantile of source samples for IQL-based policy training in the target domain.

## Results

On 24 heterogeneous cross-domain offline RL task configurations (4 MuJoCo tasks × 2 source data qualities × 3 target data qualities), V2A achieves total normalized scores of 1562.5 (IGDF-based) and 1612.9 (OTDF-based), representing 21.4% and 22.2% improvements over the respective base algorithms, compared to only 6.8% and 5.5% for DVDF. IGDF-based V2A outperforms both IGDF and DVDF in 20 out of 24 tasks, while OTDF-based V2A wins in 21 out of 24 tasks. The largest individual gains include walker2d-mr→e (63.1 vs 36.2 for DVDF-IGDF) and ant-me→e (126.2 vs 121.8 for DVDF-IGDF).

## Baselines & Comparisons

V2A is compared against six baselines: IQL, BOSA, DARA, IGDF, OTDF, and DVDF (each combined with IGDF or OTDF). V2A clearly surpasses the non-filtering baselines (IQL, BOSA, DARA) across nearly all 24 task configurations, with total scores of 1562.5/1612.9 versus 1249.7/1127.8/1281.4. Against the dynamics-only filtering baselines IGDF and OTDF, V2A achieves 21.4% and 22.2% total score improvements respectively, winning in 20/24 and 21/24 individual tasks. The most direct comparison is with DVDF, which adds value alignment but suffers from value misassignment in heterogeneous settings; V2A outperforms DVDF by 13.7% (IGDF-based) and 15.3% (OTDF-based) in total score, with the gap widening on tasks with larger dynamics shifts (e.g., walker2d-mr→e: 63.1 vs 49.8). V2A loses to DVDF+OTDF on only a few tasks (e.g., half-mr→e: 30.7 vs 35.2, hopper-mr→e: 64.6 vs 71.3), suggesting that when the source dataset is relatively homogeneous in quality, the value assignment benefit is less pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-domain offline RL data-filtering line (IGDF, OTDF, DVDF) by identifying a third failure mode—value misassignment—that arises specifically when source datasets are heterogeneous (multiple dynamics modalities and behavior policies). While DVDF introduced value alignment on top of dynamics alignment, it pretrains a single advantage function on the entire mixed source dataset, which the paper shows cannot correctly assign optimality scores across distinct dynamics. V2A addresses this by learning trajectory-level dynamics modality representations via a temporally-consistent ELBO (drawing on meta-RL encoder architectures) and incorporating them into a modality-aware Sparse-QL advantage function, thereby unifying dynamics alignment, value alignment, and value assignment in one filtering score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a realistic gap: practical source datasets (e.g., in robotics) are aggregated from multiple environments and behavior policies, yet prior methods were only validated on single-domain, single-policy sources. The theoretical bound (Proposition 4.5) formalizes how value misassignment loosens the suboptimality gap, giving a principled justification for modality-aware advantage learning. Limitations include the additional EM training overhead and reliance on mild convexity assumptions in the proof. Open questions include whether the RNN-based trajectory encoder scales to very long or highly variable-length trajectories, and whether the approach extends to settings where the number of dynamics modalities is unknown or continuously varying rather than discrete.
