## Problem

Open-vocabulary 3D instance segmentation faces a fundamental speed-accuracy tradeoff: multi-stage 2D+3D pipelines aggregate foundation-model outputs at hundreds of seconds per scene with compounding errors, while pseudo-labeled end-to-end approaches inherit fragmented 3D masks from single-view 2D-to-3D lifting and inconsistent descriptions from single-view captioning. Additionally, existing 3D transformer backbones (e.g., Point Transformer v3) serialize points via space-filling curves that scatter spatially adjacent voxels across attention windows, sacrificing the local geometric coherence that instance segmentation requires for sharp boundaries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single 3D point cloud (voxelized into a sparse grid of N non-empty voxels; scenes range from ~170k to ~1.6M points) and a free-form text query. It outputs a set of up to Q=200 instance masks (per-point sigmoid probabilities of shape N×Q) paired with CLIP-aligned query features, from which semantic labels are assigned at inference by argmax cosine similarity against pre-encoded text embeddings. End-to-end latency is 0.12 s (ScanNet200), 0.22 s (Replica), and 0.30 s (ScanNet++) on a single NVIDIA GB200 in FP16, with the one-time CLIP text encoding (~0.02 s) amortized across scenes.

## Method

SpaCeFormer pairs a space-curve attention backbone with a RoPE-enhanced proposal-free decoder. The backbone (U-Net with sparse 3D conv + transformer blocks) interleaves spatial window attention (fixed H×W×D geometric extent, variable voxel count) with Morton-curve serialization (fixed token count L, variable spatial extent), reducing mean intra-window pairwise distance by 28.6% over Morton-only ordering; 3D RoPE encodes relative voxel displacements within each window. The decoder uses Q=200 learned queries refined over T=3 shared-weight iterations of cross-attention (with 3D RoPE on keys using absolute scene coordinates) and self-attention, then predicts masks via mask attention, foreground/background logits, and CLIP features. Training uses Hungarian matching with BCE+Dice mask loss, InfoNCE contrastive CLIP alignment, and objectness loss, optimized with Muon for ≥2D parameters. The model is trained entirely on SpaCeFormer-3M (604K geometry-consistent masks from multi-view MaskClustering + 3.0M view-consistent captions from multi-view Gemma 3 prompting), achieving 54.3% mask recall at IoU>0.5 versus 2.5% for single-view pipelines.

## Results

On ScanNet200 (200 classes), SpaCeFormer achieves 11.1 mAP at 0.12 s per scene in the proposal-free, 3D-only, no-GT-3D setting, a 2.8× improvement over the next-best method in this regime (Mosaic3D+Decoder, 3.9 mAP). On ScanNet++ (100 classes), it reaches 22.9 mAP at 0.30 s, surpassing the prior best OpenTrack3D (20.6 mAP) which requires multi-view 2D inputs and ~320 s per scene. On Replica (8 scenes, zero-shot), SpaCeFormer attains 24.1 mAP at 0.22 s, the only open-vocabulary method operating at interactive rates (<1 s), matched only by SOLE (24.7 mAP) which requires ScanNet200 GT mask supervision and is ~9× slower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the matched proposal-free, 3D-only, no-GT-3D regime on ScanNet200 (category d), SpaCeFormer (11.1 mAP) decisively outperforms all prior methods: Mosaic3D+Segment3D (2.7 mAP, 4.1× gap), Mosaic3D w/ Decoder (3.9 mAP, 2.8× gap), OpenIns3D (1.7 mAP), RegionPLC (1.5 mAP), and OpenScene-3D (0.6 mAP). On ScanNet++, SpaCeFormer (22.9 mAP) surpasses every prior method including OpenTrack3D (20.6 mAP, which uses multi-view 2D + YOLO-World+SAM2 at ~320 s), OpenSplat3D (16.5 mAP), Any3DIS (12.9 mAP), and Open3DIS (11.9 mAP). On zero-shot Replica, SpaCeFormer (24.1 mAP) beats OpenTrack3D (23.9), Open-YOLO 3D (23.7), BoxOVIS (24.0), PoVo (20.8), Details Matter (22.6), Open3DIS (18.5), OpenMask3D (13.1), OV-MAP (14.2), OVIR-3D (11.1), and OpenScene-2D/3D (10.9/8.2), while trailing only SOLE (24.7 mAP) which requires GT 3D mask supervision. The key speed advantage is dramatic: SpaCeFormer runs at 0.12–0.30 s per scene versus 16–554 s for multi-stage 2D+3D pipelines (OpenMask3D, Details Matter, OpenTrack3D), a 2–3 order-of-magnitude speedup. Methods that score higher on ScanNet200 (e.g., OpenTrack3D 26.0, Open-YOLO 3D 24.7) all rely on either multi-view 2D inputs or GT-trained Mask3D proposals, placing them in a different operating regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpaCeFormer sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) 3D point-cloud transformers, where it extends Point Transformer v3's Morton-code serialization by interleaving spatial window attention to restore local geometric coherence that instance boundary prediction requires, and (2) open-vocabulary 3D instance segmentation, where it departs from the dominant multi-stage 2D+3D pipeline paradigm (OpenMask3D, Open3DIS, OpenTrack3D) by adopting a DETR-style proposal-free decoder with learned queries and 3D RoPE, eliminating external region proposal networks entirely. The dataset contribution (SpaCeFormer-3M) builds on MaskClustering's multi-view mask aggregation and VLM-based captioning but scales them to 7.4K scenes with structured multi-view prompting, addressing the fragmented-mask and inconsistent-caption limitations of prior pseudo-label corpora like Mosaic3D and RegionPLC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–3 orders-of-magnitude latency reduction (0.12–0.30 s vs. hundreds of seconds) makes open-vocabulary 3D instance segmentation viable for interactive robotics and AR/VR, a regime no prior method has occupied. SpaCeFormer-3M, as the largest open-vocabulary 3D instance segmentation dataset with 21× higher mask recall than prior single-view pseudo-label pipelines, provides a substantially better training substrate that may benefit the broader community. Open questions include closing the remaining accuracy gap on ScanNet200 versus multi-view 2D+3D methods (11.1 vs. 26.0 mAP) without sacrificing speed, extending beyond the fixed Q=200 query budget for cluttered scenes, and generalizing from indoor to outdoor/in-the-wild settings.
