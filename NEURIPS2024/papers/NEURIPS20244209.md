## Problem

Existing INR-based video codecs rely on simple model compression (pruning, uniform quantization, basic entropy coding) and are not fully end-to-end optimized for the rate-distortion trade-off, leaving them outperformed by both state-of-the-art conventional codecs like VVC VTM and autoencoder-based learned codecs. The paper identifies that the bottleneck is not the INR architecture itself but the compression pipeline applied to the neural representation parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target video sequence of T frames at resolution H×W with C channels and overfits a patch-wise INR (based on HiNeRV) to map 3D patch coordinates to pixel intensities. The output is a compact bitstream containing quantized feature grid parameters, quantized network layer weights, and quantized quantization/entropy model parameters, all entropy-coded. Reconstruction is performed by the INR network at decode time, supporting both RGB4:4:4 and YUV4:2:0 color spaces at the original video resolution.

## Method

NVRC introduces a hierarchical, fully end-to-end compression pipeline for INR parameters. Feature grids are partitioned into 3D blocks with per-block learned quantization scales and encoded by a context-based Gaussian model using 3D masked convolution in an autoregressive style to exploit spatial-temporal redundancy. Network layer weights are reshaped into 2D tensors and encoded with a dual-axis conditional Gaussian model where means and scales are factorized across input and output channel axes. The quantization and entropy model parameters themselves are further compressed by a lightweight per-tensor entropy model, forming a three-level hierarchy (θ → φ → ψ). Training uses alternating optimization of rate and distortion losses to reduce the computational cost of entropy model evaluation, with a two-stage procedure: soft-rounding with Kumaraswamy noise in Stage 1 and Quant-Noise fine-tuning in Stage 2.

## Results

On the UVG dataset (RGB 4:4:4, PSNR), NVRC achieves a 23.42% BD-rate saving over VVC VTM (Random Access), a 50.16% saving over HiNeRV, and a 66.86% saving over C3. In YUV 4:2:0, NVRC still outperforms VTM by 12.96% (PSNR) and x265 by 66.89% on UVG. On the MCL-JCV dataset, NVRC outperforms x265, HM, and all INR-based baselines, but underperforms VTM (RA) by 36.91% (PSNR, RGB) and DCVC-DC by 13.40%, indicating that the advantage over the latest standard codec is dataset-dependent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NVRC is compared against 8 baselines spanning conventional codecs (x265, HM, VTM), learned autoencoder-based codecs (DCVC-HEM, DCVC-DC), and INR-based codecs (HiNeRV, C3, HNeRV-Boost). On UVG, NVRC outperforms all eight baselines in both RGB and YUV color spaces, with the headline result being a 23.42% BD-rate saving over VVC VTM (RA) in PSNR — the first INR-based codec to surpass the latest MPEG standard. Against the closest INR-based competitor HiNeRV, NVRC achieves a 50.16% BD-rate improvement, and against C3 and HNeRV-Boost the gains are 66.86% and 66.45% respectively. On MCL-JCV, NVRC still beats x265 (−51.61%), HM (−13.88%), DCVC-HEM (−2.97%), HiNeRV (−31.69%), C3 (−42.23%), and HNeRV-Boost (−59.80%), but loses to VTM (RA) by 36.91% and DCVC-DC by 13.40% in PSNR (RGB), suggesting the advantage over the strongest conventional and learned baselines is more pronounced on the higher-resolution UVG sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

NVRC builds directly on HiNeRV (Kwan et al., NeurIPS 2023) as its underlying INR architecture, but fundamentally repositions the problem from improving representation quality to improving representation compression. Prior INR-based codecs (NeRV, HiNeRV, C3, COOL-CHIC, HNeRV-Boost) either decouple INR training from model compression or apply only simple pruning/quantization/entropy coding without full rate-distortion optimization. NVRC departs by introducing a three-level hierarchical coding strategy (representation parameters θ → compression parameters φ → meta-parameters ψ) with context-based 3D masked convolution for feature grids and a dual-axis conditional Gaussian model for network layers, all optimized jointly end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NVRC demonstrates for the first time that an INR-based video codec can outperform the latest MPEG standard (VVC VTM) with a 23% BD-rate gain on UVG, challenging the long-standing assumption that INR approaches are inherently inferior to conventional codecs. However, the encoder requires substantial overfitting computation (tens of seconds per rate point on a 4090 GPU) and the gain does not hold on MCL-JCV where NVRC underperforms VTM, limiting near-term practical deployment. Open questions include whether the hierarchical compression framework generalizes to other INR architectures, how to reduce encoder complexity for real-time scenarios, and whether integer-precision implementations can preserve the reported gains.
