## Problem

Few-shot or one-shot fine-tuning of text-to-image models for style personalization suffers from content leakage, where visual elements from the reference image (e.g., spindle leaves, boats) appear in generated images even when absent from the text prompt. This occurs because a single training image provides insufficient data to disentangle fine-grained visual concepts from their textual descriptions, causing the model to entangle style with specific content. Existing remedies like iterative synthetic fine-tuning (StyleDrop) are computationally expensive and labor-intensive.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single style reference image paired with a comprehensive text prompt (describing subjects, style descriptors, and background attributes) and produces style-personalized images at 512×512 resolution via Muse's cascade VQGAN decoder. The text prompt is decomposed into multiple concept-oriented sub-prompts (e.g., 'a laptop in flat cartoon vector art style'), each paired with a concept-specific mask derived from cross-attention maps, effectively amplifying one image-text pair into several training examples. At inference, a new text prompt in the pattern '{subject} in {style descriptor} style' drives generation, with two guidance scales (λ₁ for style strength, λ₂ for semantic fidelity) controlling the output.

## Method

FineStyle introduces two key mechanisms built on the Muse masked generative transformer. First, concept-oriented data scaling decomposes a single reference image-text pair into multiple sub-pairs, each targeting a distinct concept (e.g., laptop, plant, woman), and replaces Muse's uniform random token masking with targeted concept-oriented masks constructed by aggregating cross-attention weights for the corresponding text phrase across all transformer layers and heads. Second, instead of StyleDrop's adapters on hidden features after transformer blocks, FineStyle places a single shared LoRA adapter on the key and value kernels of cross-attention layers, with distinct per-block biases to reduce overfitting while preserving cross-modal alignment capacity. At sampling time, an additional residual term (λ₁) between the adapted and original model logits explicitly controls style injection strength, complementing standard classifier-free guidance (λ₂) on the adapted model.

## Results

On the StyleDrop evaluation set (10 styles × 190 Parti prompts, 4 images each), FineStyle achieves a CLIP Text score of 0.314 and Style score of 0.661, outperforming StyleDrop on Text (0.314 vs. 0.297) while maintaining a competitive Style score (0.661 vs. 0.708). In human evaluation, FineStyle is preferred over StyleDrop on Text alignment (67.1% vs. 10.8%) and Structure/Common Sense (65.2% vs. 23.6%), though StyleDrop retains an edge on raw Style fidelity (43.5% vs. 28.9%). The method uses only 0.32M trainable adapter parameters and 2500 training steps on a single reference image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FineStyle is compared against five baselines. Against StyleDrop (the primary baseline), FineStyle wins decisively on Text CLIP score (0.314 vs. 0.297) and human-rated Text alignment (67.1% vs. 10.8%) and Structure/Common Sense (65.2% vs. 23.6%), but concedes on Style CLIP score (0.661 vs. 0.708) and human-rated Style (28.9% vs. 43.5%), though 27.5% of style comparisons are ties. Against the un-fine-tuned Muse backbone, FineStyle trades a small Text score drop (0.314 vs. 0.320) for a large Style score gain (0.661 vs. 0.552), confirming that fine-tuning successfully injects style while preserving prompt following. In qualitative comparisons (Fig. 4), StyleAligned [15] nearly replicates the spatial layout of the reference image (e.g., bay and cliffs), Dreamstyler [1] and IP-Adapter [48] show content leakage or style inconsistency, while FineStyle produces cleaner, prompt-faithful outputs. The ablation variants confirm that the KV-kernel adapter alone (variant b) already improves Text score over StyleDrop (0.308 vs. 0.297), and adding concept-oriented data scaling further boosts it to 0.314 while reducing the over-fitted Style score from 0.730 to 0.661.

## Relation to Prior Work

FineStyle extends StyleDrop's few-shot style personalization framework on the Muse masked generative transformer, departing from it in two key design choices: replacing iterative synthetic-image fine-tuning with a single-stage concept-oriented data scaling that decomposes one reference image into multiple concept-focused sub-pairs, and relocating parameter-efficient adapters from hidden features to the key and value kernels of cross-attention layers. The broader lineage includes DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, IP-Adapter, and StyleAligned, all of which treat the style reference image and its caption as an indivisible unit; FineStyle explicitly challenges this by grounding individual textual concepts to spatial regions via cross-attention maps. The method also builds on the observation that cross-attention in pretrained T2I models already encodes concept-to-location correspondences, repurposing this signal for targeted masking during fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated failure mode—content leakage in one-shot style personalization—without the labor and compute costs of iterative synthetic fine-tuning, making fine-grained style control more accessible. The concept-oriented masking strategy and KV-kernel adapter placement offer a reusable recipe for any masked-decoding T2I model, and the demonstrated ability to independently toggle style attributes (color, background, texture) opens the door to style editing applications. However, the method is validated only on Muse with 24 styles, lacks error bars, and the authors acknowledge that the scope of controllable style compositions remains limited; generalization to diffusion-based backbones and more diverse style taxonomies remains an open question.
