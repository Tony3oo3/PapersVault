## Problem

While LLMs demonstrate planning and reasoning capabilities in controlled settings, their ability to autonomously solve realistic enterprise knowledge-work tasks remains largely unexplored. Existing web-agent benchmarks either use toy interfaces (MiniWoB), consumer websites (WebArena), or atomic enterprise tasks (WorkArena L1) that do not require the compositional planning, multi-source information retrieval, and long-context memorization that real office workflows demand.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240195/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Agents receive a task goal delivered either as explicit step-by-step chat instructions (L2) or as a ticket referencing a knowledge-base article with implicit rules (L3), combined with the current page's accessibility tree, an optional screenshot augmented with set-of-marks (for VLMs), and the full history of prior actions and chain-of-thought reasoning. At each step the agent must emit exactly one high-level BrowserGym action (e.g., click(bid), fill(bid, text), send_msg_to_user, report_infeasible) using element identifiers from the observation. The benchmark outputs a binary success/failure score via a validator that inspects both the database state and the open page, and the oracle functions can be replayed to extract full observation-action traces suitable for fine-tuning.

## Method

WorkArena++ constructs 682 compositional tasks (341 workflows × 2 difficulty levels) by chaining atomic L1 oracle and validator functions into multi-step realistic workflows spanning five skill categories: planning/problem-solving, information retrieval, data-driven decision-making, sophisticated memorization, and contextual understanding via infeasible tasks. L2 tasks provide explicit instructions in chat, while L3 tasks mimic real ticket-based work assignments where the agent must navigate to a knowledge base, memorize rules, and plan its own execution sequence. The benchmark introduces 10 fictitious company themes for visual diversity, sandboxed per-task user accounts for isolation, and a standardized seed-based curriculum for reproducible evaluation. Agents are evaluated using chain-of-thought prompting with GPT-3.5, GPT-4o (text and vision), Llama3-70B, and Mixtral-8x22B, restricted to 50 steps per task, with a retry mechanism for parsing errors. The compositional framework also enables automatic generation of ground-truth observation-action traces by replaying oracle Playwright scripts through BrowserGym, providing scalable fine-tuning data.

## Results

On WorkArena++ L3 (235 task instances), all evaluated agents (GPT-3.5, GPT-4o, GPT-4o-v, Llama3-70B, Mixtral-8x22B) achieve 0.0% success rate, while 15 human evaluators achieve 93.9% ± 3.4%. On the easier L2 set, only GPT-4o (3.0%) and GPT-4o-v (3.8%) solve any tasks, with GPT-4o-v's advantage concentrated in information retrieval (3.6%) and memorization (14.6%) subcategories. On the prior WorkArena L1 benchmark, GPT-4o achieves 42.7% and Llama3-70B achieves 17.9%, confirming that the compositional complexity of L2/L3 tasks is the primary bottleneck rather than basic UI interaction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five LLM/VLM-based agents against a human baseline on WorkArena++. GPT-4o and GPT-4o-v (OpenAI) are the only models to solve any WorkArena++ tasks, achieving 3.0% and 3.8% on L2 respectively, but both score 0% on L3. GPT-4o-v's marginal edge over GPT-4o on L2 comes from vision-based retrieval tasks (3.6% vs. 0% on information retrieval), suggesting the visual modality helps read chart values. Open-source models Llama3-70B (Meta) and Mixtral-8x22B (Jiang et al.) achieve 0% on both L2 and L3, though they perform reasonably on simpler benchmarks (17.9% and 12.4% on WorkArena L1, respectively). The stark contrast with the 93.9% human success rate (on a 98-task subset) underscores that current web agents lack the compositional planning, multi-source information consolidation, and contextual reasoning required for realistic enterprise workflows, even when they handle atomic UI interactions competently.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends WorkArena (Drouin et al., 2024) and its BrowserGym environment, scaling from 33 atomic UI-interaction tasks to 682 compositional enterprise workflows that chain multiple atomic steps into realistic knowledge-worker procedures. It departs from prior web-agent benchmarks (MiniWoB, WebArena) by targeting a single enterprise platform (ServiceNow) but demanding substantially higher-level cognitive skills—planning under constraints, multi-source information retrieval, arithmetic reasoning, and long-horizon memorization—rather than basic navigation or form-filling. The compositional task construction framework, where oracle and validator functions from simpler tasks are combined to generate new workflows, is a methodological contribution that distinguishes it from hand-authored benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a stark and well-evidenced gap: humans achieve 93.9% success while the best agent (GPT-4o-v) manages only 3.8% on L2 and 0% on L3, suggesting current LLM/VLM agents are far from autonomous enterprise assistants. The provision of oracle-based observation-action traces offers a concrete path for fine-tuning, and the standardized curriculum with reproducible seeds enables fair cross-model comparison. Key limitations include restriction to a single platform (ServiceNow), absence of safety/cybersecurity evaluation, and no interaction with external software; open questions include whether long-context models (e.g., Gemini 1.5 Pro) or fine-tuned agents can close the gap, and whether the benchmark's difficulty generalizes beyond ServiceNow's specific UI patterns.
