## Problem

Existing UAV perception datasets are predominantly designed for 2D tasks, leaving a gap for 3D understanding of the environment. Additionally, single-UAV perception is fundamentally limited by occlusions and long-range visibility, yet no well-organized collaborative perception benchmark exists specifically for UAV-to-UAV cooperation. UAV3D addresses both gaps by providing a large-scale simulated dataset and benchmark for single-UAV and collaborative-UAV 3D perception.

## Input / Output

Each sample consists of 5 synchronized RGB images (800×450) from a single UAV equipped with four oblique cameras at −45° pitch (front, left, right, back) plus one nadir camera for bird's-eye view; collaborative tasks additionally consume 25 images from 5 UAVs in a cross-shaped formation at 60 m altitude. Outputs are 3D bounding boxes parameterized by center (x,y,z), dimensions (width, length, height), and orientation (yaw, pitch, roll), or temporally consistent tracks with object identities. The dataset follows the nuScenes format with 1,000 scenes × 20 frames (500k images, 3.3M 3D boxes) across 17 vehicle categories, all mapped to a single 'car' class for the benchmark tasks, with a perception range of ±102.4 m in the ego-UAV frame.

## Method

The dataset is generated via CARLA–AirSim co-simulation: CARLA spawns 200 vehicles navigating four towns (3, 6, 7, 10) with 250 log files per town, while AirSim flies 5 UAVs along 25 predefined routes per town in a static cross-shaped formation. For single-UAV benchmarks, three BEV-based detectors (PETR, BEVFusion, DETR3D) are evaluated with ResNet-50/101 backbones at two input resolutions. For collaborative-UAV benchmarks, BEVFusion with a Swin-Transformer backbone serves as the shared perception backbone, and four communication strategies (When2Com, Who2Com, V2VNet, DiscoNet) are plugged in to fuse intermediate features across agents, bracketed by a lower-bound (no communication) and upper-bound (raw image sharing) baseline. All models are trained supervised for 24 epochs with batch size 4 on 8× A100 GPUs, and evaluated using nuScenes-style metrics (NDS, mAP, mATE, mASE, mAOE for detection; AMOTA, AMOTP, MOTA, MOTP, TID, LGD for tracking).

## Results

On the collaborative-UAV 3D object detection task (the paper's most novel benchmark), the Upper-bound baseline (transmitting raw images from all 5 UAVs) achieves mAP 0.720 and NDS 0.748, outperforming the Lower-bound (single-UAV) by 0.18 in mAP and 0.19 in NDS. Among intermediate-feature-fusion methods, DiscoNet achieves the best performance with mAP 0.700 and NDS 0.689, closing the gap to Upper-bound to just 0.02 in mAP. For single-UAV 3D detection, DETR3D with ResNet-101 at 800×450 resolution achieves the highest mAP of 0.618 and NDS of 0.671, while for single-UAV tracking, BEVFusion (ResNet-101) leads with AMOTA of 0.606.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks seven approaches across four UAV perception tasks. In collaborative 3D detection, DiscoNet (directed collaboration graph with matrix-valued edge weights) clearly outperforms V2VNet (GNN-based message passing) by 0.053 in mAP and 0.061 in NDS, and substantially outperforms When2Com and Who2Com (attention/handshake-based selection) by ~0.15 in mAP. When2Com and Who2Com provide only marginal gains over the Lower-bound (single-UAV), with When2Com actually degrading NDS from 0.556 to 0.507, suggesting that selective feature fusion without sufficient information transfer can be counterproductive. In collaborative tracking, DiscoNet achieves AMOTA 0.809 vs. V2VNet's 0.782 and the Lower-bound's 0.644, while the Upper-bound reaches 0.812, indicating DiscoNet captures nearly all the benefit of full image sharing. For single-UAV detection, DETR3D (ResNet-101) leads in mAP (0.618) and NDS (0.671), while BEVFusion dominates single-UAV tracking with AMOTA 0.606, roughly 0.31 higher than PETR and 0.34 higher than DETR3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the collaborative perception paradigm established for ground vehicles (V2X-Sim, OPV2V, V2XSet) and transplants it to the UAV domain, while simultaneously extending existing UAV datasets (VisDrone, UAVDT) from 2D to 3D perception. It adopts the nuScenes annotation format and evaluation protocol, and benchmarks autonomous-driving models (PETR, BEVFusion, DETR3D) alongside multi-agent communication strategies (When2Com, Who2Com, V2VNet, DiscoNet) in a CARLA-AirSim co-simulation environment. Unlike CoPerception-UAV, which is the closest prior UAV collaborative perception dataset, UAV3D is approximately four times larger in scale, uses a cross-shaped formation at a single altitude, and provides additional annotations (box velocity, semantic labels) along with public source code.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UAV3D fills a genuine gap by providing the first large-scale, 3D-annotated benchmark specifically for UAV perception, enabling research on tasks (3D detection, tracking, collaborative perception) that were previously only available for ground vehicles. Key limitations include the simulated nature of the data (domain gap to real-world deployment), the absence of weather/time variations, a single fixed altitude, and no communication latency modeling. Open questions include whether sim-to-real transfer techniques from autonomous driving can close the domain gap, whether more advanced 3D detection models (StreamPETR, SparseBEV) will substantially improve performance, and how the benchmark generalizes to dynamic formations and variable altitudes.
