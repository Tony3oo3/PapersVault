## Problem

Quantile regression-based distributional RL algorithms suffer from two key limitations: the non-crossing issue where learned quantile curves fail to maintain monotonicity, leading to inaccurate distribution estimates, and the inability to extend to multi-dimensional reward settings where quantile regression cannot approximate multi-dimensional Wasserstein distances. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing Sinkhorn divergence—a regularized Wasserstein loss—as an alternative distributional divergence that naturally handles multi-dimensional returns and avoids quantile-specific pathologies.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard Atari 2600 RGB frames (84×84 grayscale, processed identically to QR-DQN) and actions as input, and outputs N=200 deterministic particles (scalar or d-dimensional samples) representing the full return distribution for a given state-action pair, from which an action is selected via greedy mean. Unlike QR-DQN which outputs fixed quantile values, SinkhornDRL generates unrestricted samples via a neural network, approximating the return distribution as a Dirac mixture (1/N)Σδ_{Z_i}, and the loss is computed between the current particle set and the Bellman-updated target particle set through Sinkhorn iterations.

## Method

SinkhornDRL replaces the quantile regression loss with Sinkhorn divergence (entropically regularized optimal transport) while retaining the particle-based representation from MMD-DQN. The key mechanism is computing the regularized Wasserstein loss W_{c,ε} between N current particles and N target particles via L=10 Sinkhorn iterations (matrix scaling), using the unrectified kernel c = -||x-y||² (α=2) and smoothing parameter ε=10.0, which yields a differentiable, strongly convex loss amenable to GPU-accelerated backpropagation. Theoretically, the paper proves the distributional Bellman operator is Δ̄_ε(γ,α)-contractive under the supremal Sinkhorn divergence, where the contraction factor interpolates between γ^α (Wasserstein limit, ε→0) and 1 (MMD limit, ε→∞), with the interpolation coefficient determined by the proportion of the KL regularization term. This framework extends naturally to multi-dimensional rewards by operating on joint d-dimensional return vectors, where the same contraction guarantee holds without requiring quantile-based approximations of multi-dimensional Wasserstein distances.

## Results

On the full 55-game Atari 2600 suite (40M frames, 3 seeds), SinkhornDRL achieves state-of-the-art performance in Mean, Median, and IQM (5%) of Human-Normalized Scores (HNS) across most training phases, surpassing C51, QR-DQN, and MMD-DQN. SinkhornDRL wins in more than half of all games against both QR-DQN and MMD-DQN, with particularly large gains in games with larger action spaces (e.g., Venture, Seaquest, Solaris, Tennis, Phoenix, Atlantis, Zaxxon). In the multi-dimensional reward setting (6 Atari games with decomposed reward vectors), SinkhornDRL outperforms MMD-DQN in most cases. The computational overhead is modest: approximately 20% increase in average computation cost compared to MMD-DQN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DQN (Mnih et al., 2015), SinkhornDRL clearly outperforms as expected, since distributional RL methods generally surpass expectation-based RL on Atari. Against C51 (Bellemare et al., 2017), SinkhornDRL achieves higher Mean, Median, and IQM HNS, benefiting from the more flexible particle representation over fixed categorical supports. Against QR-DQN (Dabney et al., 2018), SinkhornDRL wins in more than half of the 55 games, with the largest relative improvements in games featuring 18-dimensional action spaces and complex dynamics (Venture, Seaquest, Solaris, Tennis, Phoenix, Zaxxon); the advantage is attributed to avoiding the non-crossing quantile issue and capturing richer distributional structure via samples. Against MMD-DQN (Nguyen et al., 2020), which uses the same particle representation and architecture, SinkhornDRL outperforms in more than half of games, with the gap widening substantially in the multi-dimensional reward setting (Figure 4) where optimal transport's geometric sensitivity to data layout provides a clear advantage over kernel-based MMD. The paper notes that SinkhornDRL converges slightly slower in early training (visible in the Mean HNS curve), consistent with its larger contraction factor Δ̄_ε(γ,α) > γ^α relative to the unregularized Wasserstein case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces SinkhornDRL as a new family of distributional RL algorithms, positioning it alongside the three established families (categorical, quantile-regression/Wasserstein, and MMD-based). It builds directly on the Sinkhorn divergence from the optimal transport literature, leveraging its known interpolation property between Wasserstein distance and MMD to unify the contraction analysis of QR-DQN (ε→0) and MMD-DQN (ε→∞) under a single framework. The method departs from quantile regression by using particle (sample) representation, avoiding the non-crossing issue, and departs from MMD-DQN by using OT-based geometry rather than kernel-specific distances, while retaining the sample-based representation that enables multi-dimensional reward handling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SinkhornDRL provides a theoretically grounded, practically implementable alternative to quantile regression that naturally extends to multi-dimensional reward settings where quantile regression is intractable, and the interpolation theorem gives a unified view of how different distributional RL algorithms relate. However, the method incurs ~20% additional computational cost over MMD-DQN and requires tuning three hyperparameters (ε, L, N), and the paper acknowledges that the connection between theoretical divergence properties and practical performance in specific environments remains unclear. Open questions include whether implicit generative models (IQN/FQF-style) can be combined with SinkhornDRL, and how to design a quantitative criterion for selecting the appropriate divergence for a given task.
