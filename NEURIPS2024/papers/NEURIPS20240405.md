## Problem

Diffusion policies excel at representing the multi-modal distributions inherent in human demonstrations but suffer from slow iterative inference and intractable likelihoods, making them impractical for real-time robot control and post-hoc optimization. Mixture of Experts (MoE) models offer fast sampling and tractable densities but are notoriously difficult to train from scratch due to mode-averaging and optimization instability. No prior method distills a pre-trained diffusion policy into an MoE while preserving the teacher's multi-modal coverage.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a short observation history (5-step sequence of proprioceptive states or RGB images) encoded by a linear layer or pre-trained ResNet-18, respectively, then processed by a decoder-only transformer. A single forward pass outputs the full MoE parameters: the mean and covariance of each Gaussian expert plus the categorical gating distribution over experts. Because the output is an explicit Gaussian mixture, the policy admits closed-form likelihood evaluation and one-step sampling, unlike the iterative denoising required by the diffusion teacher.

## Method

VDD frames distillation as minimizing the reverse KL between the MoE student q^φ and the diffusion teacher π, then applies the chain rule for KL divergences to decompose the objective into independent per-expert sub-objectives (Eq. 8–9). Each expert is updated via reparameterized gradients that replace the intractable log π(a|s) with the pre-trained score function f_θ(a,s,t), sampled from an interval of diffusion timesteps rather than t→0. An auxiliary distribution q̃(z|a,s) acts as a repulsion term preventing experts from collapsing onto the same mode, and is tightened each iteration via Bayes' rule (E-step), yielding an EM-like alternating scheme with monotonic improvement guarantees. The gating is trained with a cross-entropy loss derived from the forward KL, which avoids needing the intractable teacher likelihood. The architecture is a GPT-style transformer that predicts all expert means, covariances, and gating logits in one pass, so adding experts only increases output linear layers while the backbone remains fixed.

## Results

On the D3IL benchmark (7 robot manipulation tasks), VDD-VE achieves success rates of 0.95 (Avoiding), 0.86 (Aligning), 0.85 (Pushing), 0.85 (Stacking-1), 0.57 (Stacking-2), 0.76 (Sorting), and 0.60 (Stacking-Image), outperforming Consistency Distillation (CD) and Consistency Trajectory Models (CTM) on 6 out of 7 tasks in success rate. VDD also achieves higher task entropy (behavioral diversity) than both CD and CTM in 4 out of 7 tasks, e.g., 0.69 vs. 0.53 (CD) and 0.54 (CTM) on Pushing. On Relay Kitchen, VDD-VE scores 3.85 (vs. 3.87 CD, 3.89 CTM), and on Block Push it scores 0.91 (vs. 0.89 for both CD and CTM). Inference time is 2.16 ms (state-based) and 4.91 ms (image-based), roughly 2× faster than even 1-step BESO (4.03 ms / 9.69 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two SOTA distillation baselines, VDD-VE outperforms CD-VE on 6 of 7 D3IL tasks in success rate (e.g., 0.85 vs. 0.66 on Pushing, 0.85 vs. 0.69 on Stacking-1) and CTM-VE on 5 of 7 (e.g., 0.95 vs. 0.93 on Avoiding, 0.85 vs. 0.54 on Stacking-1); the sole exception is Aligning, where CD-VE reaches 0.94 vs. VDD-VE's 0.86, though VDD compensates with higher task entropy (0.40 vs. 0.32). On Relay Kitchen and Block Push, VDD-VE is within 0.02–0.04 of CD/CTM, confirming on-par distillation quality. Against MoE-from-scratch baselines (EM-GPT and IMC-GPT), VDD consistently achieves higher success rates across all 9 tasks (e.g., 0.95 vs. 0.75 for IMC-GPT on Avoiding; 0.85 vs. 0.76 for IMC-GPT on Pushing), and higher task entropy in 4 of 7 D3IL tasks. Finally, VDD substantially outperforms the 1-step diffusion variants (VP-1, VE-1) on all D3IL tasks, e.g., 0.95 vs. 0.84 (VE-1) on Avoiding and 0.85 vs. 0.70 (VE-1) on Pushing, demonstrating that the MoE distillation preserves multi-modal structure that single-step diffusion loses.

## Relation to Prior Work

VDD sits at the intersection of three research threads: score-based diffusion modeling (Song et al., 2021), variational inference with decomposable objectives (Blei et al., 2017), and EM-style optimization for mixture models (Dempster et al., 1977). It departs from consistency distillation (CD, CTM), which distills diffusion models into other diffusion/consistency models and retains intractable likelihoods, and from direct MoE training (EM, IMC), which learns from scratch without leveraging a pre-trained teacher. The key conceptual departure is deriving a per-expert variational objective that uses only the score function gradient of the teacher, avoiding the need for the intractable diffusion likelihood while providing a repulsion term that prevents mode collapse across experts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VDD addresses a concrete bottleneck in deploying diffusion policies for real-time robot control: iterative denoising is too slow for high-frequency decision-making, and intractable likelihoods block post-hoc RL fine-tuning. The resulting MoE policy offers one-step sampling, closed-form likelihoods, and interpretable expert specializations, making it a practical bridge between expressive generative models and deployable controllers. Open questions include scaling to high-dimensional observations (e.g., images) where the Gaussian expert parameterization becomes unwieldy, adapting the number of experts automatically, and whether the diffusion teacher's internal features can further accelerate or improve distillation.
