## Problem

Polish ASR research lacks a standardized, reproducible benchmark: publicly available speech datasets are underutilized due to accessibility, licensing, and interoperability barriers, no unified evaluation dataset exists, and prior benchmarks covered only a handful of systems and narrow domains. This fragmentation makes it difficult to track progress, compare commercial versus free systems fairly, or validate research results across the community.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241830/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes Polish speech audio standardized to WAV 16-bit/16 kHz with amplitude normalized to −3 dBFS, paired with reference transcriptions in both spoken and written domain formats, plus rich per-utterance metadata (speaker gender, age, speech rate, duration, source dataset). It produces four error-rate scores (WER, SER, MER, CER) per system-model-dataset combination, aggregated across 8 analysis scenarios (subset, model size, speech rate, speaker demographics, etc.), and is distributed as two curated Hugging Face datasets (BIGOS V2 for read speech, PELCRA for BIGOS for conversational speech) alongside an interactive public dashboard.

## Method

The system comprises three linked processes: (1) a keyword-based survey cataloguing Polish speech datasets with attributes like license, speaker count, and audio format; (2) a curation pipeline that downloads 24 source corpora, assigns standard speaker/file IDs, removes duplicates and offensive content, unifies audio to WAV 16-bit/16 kHz, splits long recordings using time-alignment annotations, converts transcripts to UTF-8, and packages everything in the BIGOS utterance data object with a fixed metadata schema; (3) an evaluation toolchain that applies a shared text-normalization pipeline (blanks, lowercasing, punctuation removal, lexicon-based unification, tag removal) to both references and hypotheses before computing SER, WER, MER, and CER via open-source libraries (jiwer, asr-evaluation). Twenty-five models across nine systems (Google STT v1/v2, Azure, Whisper local/cloud, AssemblyAI, NeMo, MMS, Wav2Vec2) are scored on both datasets, and results are sliced along eight scenarios to expose performance variation by subset difficulty, model size, speech rate, and speaker demographics. All tools, datasets, and results are open-sourced to enable community extension to other low-resource languages.

## Results

Across 600 system-model-test set evaluations on BIGOS V2 (read speech) and PELCRA for BIGOS (spontaneous speech), the three best-performing ASR models are Whisper Large V3, Whisper Cloud, and Assembly AI best. Commercial systems achieve a median WER of 12.96% on BIGOS V2 versus 15.47% for free systems (≈2.5 p.p. gap), and 29.90% versus 34.18% on PELCRA (≈4.5 p.p. gap). Read speech yields roughly 17–19 p.p. lower WER than conversational speech for both system categories. Applying full text normalization (lowercasing, punctuation removal, blank removal) reduces WER by 14.63 p.p. on BIGOS V2 and 15.65 p.p. on PELCRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Whisper (Radford et al., 2022) dominates the benchmark: Whisper Large V3 (free, local) and Whisper Cloud (commercial API) rank among the top three models overall, with Whisper Large V3 slightly outperforming Assembly AI best at moderate speech rates (1–2 words/sec) on BIGOS V2. AssemblyAI (Ramirez et al., 2024) is the strongest commercial system, with its 'best' model achieving the lowest WER in the paid category on both datasets. MMS (Pratap et al., 2023) 1B-parameter models perform competitively on read speech (BIGOS V2) but degrade noticeably on conversational PELCRA data, indicating sensitivity to spontaneous speech variability. Wav2Vec2 (Hsu et al., 2021) Polish fine-tunes (xls-r-1b-polish, large-xlsr-53-polish) show moderate WER (≈40–62% across age groups on PELCRA), trailing Whisper and AssemblyAI by a wide margin. NeMo QuartzNet (Kriman et al., 2019) exhibits the highest WER among evaluated models (75%+ for speakers in their twenties on PELCRA), while the larger NeMo Conformer and Multilang FastConformer models remain competitive relative to their parameter count but still fall well behind Whisper Large variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the author's earlier BIGOS benchmark (Junczyk, FedCSIS 2023) by expanding from a smaller set of curated corpora to 24 source datasets and adding a second dataset (PELCRA for BIGOS) focused on spontaneous conversational speech. It builds on the broader international ASR benchmarking tradition (ESB, WER-we-are, PMLB) and addresses the specific gap identified in prior Polish ASR evaluations (PolEval 2019, DiaBiz 2022, SpokesBiz 2023, medical benchmarks 2023), which collectively covered only 19 system-model combinations across limited domains. The evaluation toolchain leverages established open-source tools (jiwer, Hugging Face datasets, Speech Data Explorer) rather than introducing new algorithmic components, positioning the contribution as a curation and infrastructure effort rather than a methodological one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the most comprehensive publicly available benchmark for Polish ASR to date (25 models, 9 systems, 24 source datasets, 8 analysis scenarios, ~600 evaluations), and the open toolchain and datasets lower the barrier for reproducible evaluation in a low-resource language setting. Key open questions include potential training-data contamination from widely used corpora (Common Voice, MLS), the absence of manual transcription verification, limited demographic coverage, and the lack of semantically informed metrics. The finding that text normalization alone accounts for ~15 p.p. of WER reduction underscores that reported ASR error rates are highly sensitive to evaluation protocol choices, a concern that generalizes beyond Polish. For the broader multilingual ASR community, the curation methodology and BIGOS format offer a transferable template for other under-resourced languages.
