## Problem

Prior methods that guarantee perfect counterfactual fairness (CF) are known to degrade predictive performance, but the extent of this inherent trade-off has remained uncharacterized. This paper provides the first model-agnostic theoretical analysis quantifying the minimum excess risk any predictor must incur to satisfy perfect CF, and derives the provably optimal fair predictor under this constraint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241516/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a post-processing step on top of any pretrained probabilistic predictor φ: given a test point (x, a), it queries φ at both the factual point (x, a) and the estimated counterfactual point (x̂_{1−a}, 1−a), then returns a prior-weighted average of the two outputs. The counterfactual generating mechanism G (ground truth or estimated) maps (x, a, 1−a) → x̂_{1−a}, and the prior p(A) provides the mixing weights. For regression the output is a real-valued prediction; for classification it is the probability of Y=1. No retraining of φ is required for the base PCF algorithm, though the CRM variant augments the training set with estimated counterfactuals.

## Method

The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.3) shows that the optimal predictor under perfect CF is simply p(A=a)·φ*(x,a) + p(A=1−a)·φ*(x_{1−a}, 1−a), i.e., a prior-weighted mixture of the Bayes-optimal (unfair) predictor's outputs at the factual and counterfactual points. The excess risk over the unconstrained optimum is fully characterized by the dependency between Y and A given the latent confounders U (Theorem 3.4): σ²_A·E_U[(E[Y|U,A=1]−E[Y|U,A=0])²] for regression and I(A;Y|U) for classification. For practical settings with estimated counterfactuals, the authors propose PCF (Algorithm 1), a plug-in method that achieves perfect CF when given the true CGM and bounds the TE and excess risk under estimation error ε (Theorem 3.6). To mitigate distribution shift from imperfect counterfactual estimation, they introduce Counterfactual Risk Minimization (CRM), which augments the training objective with losses on estimated counterfactual samples, yielding PCF-CRM that empirically outperforms prior CF methods (CFU, CFR, ECOCF) on both synthetic and semi-synthetic benchmarks.

## Results

On synthetic datasets with ground-truth counterfactuals, PCF achieves perfect counterfactual fairness (TE = 0) while attaining the lowest prediction error among all CF-guaranteed methods (CFU, CFR, PCF), validating the optimality theorem. Under controllable counterfactual estimation error (Gaussian noise with varying σ), PCF maintains the best Error–TE tradeoff across all noise levels, and PCF-Analytic (using the closed-form Bayes-optimal predictor) is notably more robust to estimation error than PCF with a learned predictor. On the semi-synthetic Law School Success dataset, PCF-CRM achieves both lower Error and lower TE than CFU and CFR, and when convexly mixed with ERM, PCF-CRM dominates the fairness–utility Pareto frontier (lowest Error at any fixed TE, and lowest TE at any fixed Error).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CFU (Kusner et al., 2017), which discards all descendants of the sensitive attribute and predicts only from U, PCF and PCF-CRM achieve the same perfect CF (TE = 0) but with substantially lower prediction error on both synthetic and semi-synthetic datasets, because PCF retains information from X via the factual–counterfactual mixing. Against CFR (Zuo et al., 2023), which uses a symmetric feature (x + x_{1-a})/2 together with U, PCF-CRM achieves both lower Error and lower TE on the Law School and Adult semi-synthetic benchmarks, confirming that the plug-in combination of factual and counterfactual predictions is strictly more informative than feature-level mixing. Against ECOCF (Wang et al., 2023), which adjusts an Equal Counterfactual Opportunity predictor to satisfy CF, PCF is listed as a competing method in the experimental protocol; the paper's theoretical analysis shows that ECOCF's optimality guarantee relies on the restrictive assumption that non-sensitive features are independent of the sensitive attribute, a condition not required by PCF. ERM (unconstrained baseline) consistently achieves the lowest Error but the highest TE, illustrating the inherent fairness–utility tradeoff quantified in Theorem 3.4; PCF-CRM sits on the Pareto-optimal frontier between ERM and the other CF methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the counterfactual fairness (CF) line initiated by Kusner et al. (2017) and the feature-mixing approach of Zuo et al. (2023), but reframes the problem as a post-processing plugin rather than an in-processing or pre-processing intervention. While prior CF methods either discard descendants of the sensitive attribute (Kusner), mix features with latent variables (Zuo), or penalize CF violations during training (Garg, Kim), this paper proves that the optimal fair predictor is simply a probability-weighted average of factual and counterfactual predictions from an unconstrained optimal model. The theoretical excess-risk analysis directly parallels the non-causal fairness trade-off results of Chzhen et al. (2020) and Xian et al. (2023), but is the first to establish such a characterization for the causal CF setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first quantitative characterization of the inherent CF–predictive-performance trade-off, showing the excess risk is governed entirely by the Y–A dependency in the causal mechanism, which gives practitioners a principled upper bound on achievable accuracy under perfect CF. The plugin nature of PCF makes it attractive for debiasing pretrained/foundation models without retraining, which is practically important given the prevalence of large-scale models trained on noisy data. Key limitations include the invertibility assumption between X and U given A, the need for (estimated) counterfactuals, and the requirement of a near-optimal base predictor; the empirical validation is confined to small synthetic and semi-synthetic datasets, leaving open whether the method's advantages persist at scale or with more complex causal structures.
