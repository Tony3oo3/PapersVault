## Problem

Bayesian Active Learning has shown remarkable success in diverse settings, yet previous attempts to apply it for Preference Modeling in LLMs (e.g., BALD) yielded no benefit over random sampling. The paper identifies two root causes: the inherent bias of approximate Bayesian inference in deep models, and the intractability of proper batch-acquisition uncertainty estimators, which forces reliance on single-point schemes that select redundant samples when naively extended to batches. This is particularly problematic at LLM scale, where parallelized labeling makes frequent model updates prohibitively expensive.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pool of prompt-completion tuples (x, y₁, y₂) generated by a frozen base LLM, along with their last-layer embedding vectors computed in a single offline forward pass. The Bayesian preference model is an ensemble of lightweight MLP adapters (a few million parameters each) trained on these pre-extracted features, enabling operation on 7B–140B parameter base models on a single A100 GPU. Each acquisition cycle outputs a batch of 320 tuples selected for human binary preference labeling, with the selection driven by a linear combination of per-tuple epistemic uncertainty and a per-prompt feature-space entropy score.

## Method

BAL-PM proposes a stochastic acquisition policy that maximizes a linear combination of two epistemic uncertainty sources: (1) a task-dependent BALD score computed from a deep ensemble of adapters over the Bradley-Terry preference model, and (2) a task-agnostic entropy score measuring the entropy of the acquired prompt distribution in the LLM's embedding space. The entropy term uses a repurposed KSG estimator that computes kNN distances in the joint pool-plus-training space, avoiding the density-estimation bias of the standard KL estimator in the low-data regime. During batch construction, the entropy counter n_{X_tr}(x) is updated after each in-batch selection (a lightweight operation), so subsequent picks within the same batch are steered toward under-represented regions of the feature space. This mechanism automatically balances the two terms over training: the entropy contribution decays as the prompt space is explored, while the preference-model uncertainty term gains relative influence as the Bayesian model improves.

## Results

BAL-PM reduces the volume of human feedback required to learn a preference model by 33% on the Reddit TL;DR test split and 68% on the CNN/DM out-of-distribution setting, measured by average Log-Likelihood. The method achieves on-par performance with a preference model trained on the full 92,858-point dataset using only 24,000 acquired points. Gains are consistent across base LLM scales (7B, 70B, 140B parameters), with approximately 31%–33% sample reduction in all cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against random sampling, BAL-PM requires 33% fewer labels on Reddit TL;DR and 68% fewer on CNN/DM to reach the same Log-Likelihood. BALD (equivalent to Gleave & Irving [20]) shows no benefit over random sampling on TL;DR and only a marginal improvement on CNN/DM, while BAL-PM consistently outperforms it with more data. Among stochastic batch acquisition policies from Kirsch et al. [22] (SoftmaxBALD, SoftRankBALD, PowerBALD), BAL-PM surpasses all variants in both test and OOD settings, confirming that feature-space entropy maximization is more effective than sampling from task-dependent uncertainty distributions. In the extended baseline comparison (Appendix J), MC Dropout [63] is the strongest additional method targeting epistemic uncertainty, but BAL-PM still outperforms it; Entropy Minimizer [66] and Low Perplexity [67] perform worse since they target lower-entropy regions. Notably, adding BAL-PM's entropy term to MC Dropout and Latent Reward Uncertainty baselines consistently improves their performance, demonstrating the generality of the diversity objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends BALD (Lindley, 1956) to the batch-acquisition setting of preference modeling in LLMs, where prior work (Gleave & Irving, 2022) showed that naively applying BALD yields no benefit over random sampling. The key departure from both that work and from stochastic batch acquisition policies (Kirsch et al., 2023) is the introduction of a task-agnostic epistemic uncertainty term: the KSG entropy of the acquired prompt distribution in the base LLM's feature space, which encourages diversity and prevents the redundant-sample pathology of single-point uncertainty scores applied to batches. The Bayesian preference model is implemented as an ensemble of lightweight adapters on a frozen LLM rather than full fine-tuning, enabling scaling to 140B-parameter base models on a single GPU.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reducing human feedback volume by 33–68% directly addresses a major cost bottleneck in LLM alignment, with the paper estimating savings of hundreds of thousands of dollars and months of labeling at current scale. The diagnostic insight—that naive epistemic uncertainty estimation in batch settings produces redundant samples, and that feature-space entropy corrects this—generalizes beyond preference modeling to any batch active learning with LLM features. Open questions include whether the method transfers to larger preference datasets (millions of points), how the learned preference models perform in downstream preference optimization (e.g., DPO, PPO), and whether the reliance on base-LLM feature quality (susceptible to the Noisy-TV problem) limits robustness to nonsensical or adversarial prompts.
