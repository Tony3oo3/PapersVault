## Problem

Existing online action detection methods are closed-set, require manual frame-level annotations, and cannot generalize to unseen action categories. The paper asks whether video-text pretraining alone—without any precise temporal labels—can enable zero-shot open-vocabulary online action detection on untrimmed videos, and identifies two specific obstacles: the high proportion of background frames in sliding-window sampling contradicts VLM training assumptions, and the inability to access future frames during online inference hampers contrastive loss optimization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes untrimmed video at 4 FPS (resized to 224×224) via an 8-second sliding window, with per-frame features extracted by a frozen CLIP ViT-L encoder. At inference, it takes text descriptions of C action categories (plus a background class) and produces per-frame action probability scores using only past and current observations—no future frames are accessed. The final prediction combines two components: a hard-assigned group score from the action clustering block and a soft similarity score from the current frame embedding, weighted by a scalar α.

## Method

OV-OAD is a visual-text dual-encoder trained purely on web-scale video-text pairs (InternVid-5K, ActivityNet) with three proxy losses: current frame-text contrastive alignment, multi-label video-text contrastive alignment (to handle noisy captions), and per-frame background mask prediction via dice loss. The visual encoder comprises a 4-layer Distant Neighboring-Frame Transformer (transformer decoder units with directional attention masks that let neighboring frames query distant past frames) followed by an Action Clustering block that uses an Object-Centric Decoder (slot attention over k learnable group embeddings) to bind semantically similar frames into clusters, then a final transformer encoder. The text encoder is CLIP's Text Transformer augmented with Adaptformer adapters for lightweight video-text adaptation. At zero-shot inference, frames are hard-assigned to their nearest group embedding, and the group-to-text cosine similarity yields per-frame action scores, requiring no fine-tuning on downstream datasets.

## Results

OV-OAD trained on ActivityNet achieves 37.5% mAP on THUMOS'14 and 73.2% cAP on TVSeries in a fully zero-shot setting, outperforming the best CLIP-based baseline (CLIP-III, 29.7% mAP / 71.6% cAP) by 7.8 and 1.6 points respectively. On the more challenging FineAction and EK100 benchmarks, OV-OAD reaches 29.2% mAP and 41.4% cAP, surpassing CLIP-II by 1.4 and 1.5 points. In base-to-novel evaluation on THUMOS'14, OV-OAD (trained on no downstream data) achieves 44.6% mAP at the 75%/25% split, exceeding fully-supervised MAT-D48 (25.5%) and OadTR-D8 (33.7%) by large margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OV-OAD consistently outperforms all three CLIP non-parametric baselines (CLIP-I, CLIP-II, CLIP-III) on both THUMOS'14 and TVSeries, with the largest gap of 7.8 mAP points over CLIP-III on THUMOS'14, demonstrating that temporal modeling via the distant neighboring-frame transformer is essential beyond single-frame image-text matching. Against the video-text model ViCLIP (Wang et al., 2023), OV-OAD achieves 37.5% mAP versus 26.3% (ViCLIP-II, ViT/L), a 11.2-point margin, showing that direct video-text pretraining does not transfer well to the online setting. In base-to-novel comparisons on THUMOS'14, OV-OAD (which uses zero downstream training data) surpasses OadTR (Wang et al., 2021), LSTR (Xu et al., 2021), and MAT (Wang et al., 2023) at both the 75%/25% and 50%/50% splits—e.g., 44.6% vs. 33.7% (OadTR) and 25.5% (MAT) at 75%/25%—while MAT still leads in the fully-supervised 100% setting (48.2% vs. 37.5%). On FineAction and EK100, OV-OAD edges out CLIP-II by 1.4 mAP and 1.5 cAP points, confirming generalization to datasets with different domain distributions (first-person kitchen videos, fine-grained actions).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) CLIP-style vision-language dual-encoders for zero-shot recognition, (2) zero-shot temporal action detection (ZS-TAD) methods like EffPrompt and STALE that rely on base-to-novel fine-tuning, and (3) closed-set online action detection (OAD) models such as OadTR, LSTR, and MAT. The key departure is from the ZS-TAD paradigm: rather than partitioning target-dataset categories into seen/unseen subsets and fine-tuning with prompts, OV-OAD is trained exclusively on noisy web video-text pairs with no frame-level labels, using an object-centric slot-attention decoder (borrowed from GroupViT/OVSegmentor) to cluster semantically similar frames. The distant neighboring-frame transformer block is a novel architectural contribution that queries spatio-temporal context from past frames using only neighboring frames as queries, enabling online inference without future access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes the first robust baseline for zero-shot open-vocabulary online action detection, demonstrating that text supervision alone on web-scale video-text pairs can transfer to arbitrary action vocabularies without any fine-tuning on target data. A notable finding is that the standard base-to-novel fine-tuning paradigm (dominant in ZS-TAD) actually degrades severely for OAD (e.g., MAT drops from 48.2% to 7.9% mAP at 50% unseen), suggesting the limited data scale and high background-frame ratio in OAD settings are fundamentally incompatible with contrastive fine-tuning. The main limitation is reliance on CLIP's image-only features, which under-models spatio-temporal structure; extending the framework to jointly learn temporal dynamics while preserving open-vocabulary capability remains an open challenge. The 292 FPS inference speed (no optical flow) makes real-time deployment on edge devices plausible.
