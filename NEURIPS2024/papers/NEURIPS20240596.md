## Problem

Existing decision tree (DT) extraction methods for DRL policies, such as VIPER and MAVIPER, are heuristic designs that provide no quantitative guarantee on the expected return gap between the oracle policy and the extracted DT, and they do not support arbitrary size constraints (e.g., maximum leaf nodes L). In multi-agent decentralized settings, the joint reward depends on all agents' DTs simultaneously, so naively converting each agent's policy into a local DT cannot ensure global optimality, and no prior work provides a return gap bound for such decentralized DTs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes local observations o_j from each agent, pre-trained RL policy action-values Q*(o,a) (obtained via Centralized Q-Learning or SAC), and replay buffer transitions to form training datasets. It produces a decision tree policy T_j for each agent with a user-specified maximum number of leaf nodes L, where each leaf is assigned a discrete action label. Unusually, the input observations are first mapped into action-value vector space via the Q-function before clustering, and the DT is constructed to an exact target size rather than being grown large and then pruned.

## Method

RGMDT recasts DT construction as iterative non-Euclidean clustering in the action-value vector space, where each observation o_j is represented by its action-value vector Q̄*(o_j) and cosine distance D_cos serves as the clustering metric (proven to form a metric space in Lemma 4.3). Clustering labels l_j = g(o_j) are learned by a DNN trained with a Regularized Information Maximization (RIM) loss that combines a locality-preserving cosine-distance term with a mutual information term balancing cluster size and clarity. For each DT split, an SVM identifies the optimal hyperplane w*·o − p* = 0 that maximizes margin between the closest cluster points, and the binary tree is grown iteratively for log₂(L)+1 iterations until the target leaf count is reached. In the multi-agent setting, an iteratively-grow-DT process conditions each agent's Q-function on the current DTs of all other agents (treating them as a super-agent), so that the joint reward impact is captured at every growth step. The key theoretical result (Thm. 4.4) bounds the return gap by O(√(ε/(log₂(L+1)−1)) · nQ_max), where ε is the average cosine distance within clusters, enabling the algorithm to minimize this bound directly.

## Results

On the D4RL Hopper benchmark, RGMDT achieves 776.70±150.18 reward with 80K samples and 64 nodes, outperforming the best baseline (RF at 452.89) by ~71%. With 80K samples and 40 nodes, RGMDT scores 567.27±160.47 versus CART's 443.47, RF's 345.81, and ET's 196.55. In the multi-agent maze tasks, RGMDT is the only method to complete the hard task with just 4 leaf nodes, and with ≥4 nodes its mean episode reward approaches the expert RL policy. The paper reports 69.33% and 21.40% reward improvements over baselines at 64 and 40 nodes respectively when sample size is reduced from 800K to 80K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGMDT outperforms CART, RF, and ET on D4RL Hopper in 3 of 4 settings (all 80K-sample configurations and 800K/40-nodes), with the largest margin at 80K/64 nodes where it leads RF by ~324 reward points. The sole exception is 800K samples with 64 nodes, where RF (489.92) slightly edges out RGMDT (458.70). In the multi-agent maze tasks (Figures 3–4), RGMDT dominates all baselines including MAVIPER: with only 2 leaf nodes, every baseline fails to complete the 2-agent task while RGMDT succeeds, and no baseline completes the 3-agent task with 4 nodes. The ablation (Table 2) further shows that replacing the SVM hyperplane with CART or RF causes task failure, while removing the non-Euclidean clustering or iteratively-grow-DT modules drops mean reward from 41.95 to 23.85 and 16.29 respectively, confirming both components are critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VIPER/MAVIPER line of work on distilling DRL policies into decision trees, departing fundamentally from the imitation-learning paradigm (sampling from the expert policy) by recasting DT extraction as a non-Euclidean clustering problem in action-value vector space with cosine distance as the metric. Where prior multi-agent DT methods (MAVIPER) are heuristic and lack performance guarantees, RGMDT provides a closed-form upper bound on the return gap and an iteratively-grow-DT procedure that conditions each agent's clustering on the current DTs of other agents. The use of SVM-derived hyperplanes for node splits and a Regularized Information Maximization loss for label generation are borrowed from classical ML and discrete-representation learning, respectively, but their integration into a return-gap-minimizing DT construction is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form return gap bound O(√(ε/(log₂(L+1)−1))·n·Q_max) gives practitioners a principled way to trade off DT complexity against expected performance loss, something no prior DT-extraction method offers. The multi-agent decentralized formulation with iterative conditioning addresses a real gap in interpretable MARL, though the experimental validation is limited to maze navigation, one D4RL task (Hooper), and MPE-style chasing tasks—scaling to high-dimensional continuous control or large agent counts remains untested. Open questions include whether the discrete observation/action assumption (or its cosine-distance extension) holds in practice for high-dimensional state spaces, and whether the SVM-based linear splits are sufficient or whether the authors' planned regression-tree extension is necessary for continuous action spaces.
