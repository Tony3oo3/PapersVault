## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion methods generate all scene components as a single denoised image, limiting fine-grained control over individual object attributes and making layout editing require full re-generation. Layer-based generation paradigms partially address this but collapse layers into a single image during later diffusion steps, preventing independent manipulation of instance appearance, relative 3D positioning, and scene composition with content preservation. The paper tackles the gap of generating controllable, editable compositional scenes where each object's attributes and position can be independently specified and modified.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241392/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes per-instance text prompts (e.g., 'a red fox') plus a global scene prompt and a bounding box layout specifying each object's position and scale, and produces (1) isolated RGBA instance images at 512×512 with a 4th alpha transparency channel, and (2) a stack of K+1 composite images showing progressive scene assembly from background through all K instances. The multi-layer output is a key structural feature: each intermediate layer contains a subset of instances, enabling per-layer inspection and targeted editing. Instances are generated independently before composition, so their appearance is fully determined by their individual prompt rather than the global scene context.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage pipeline. First, a transparency-aware VAE is trained to disentangle RGB and alpha channels into separate latent distributions (each with its own KL loss and weight w_KL=1), and the PixArt-α diffusion model is fine-tuned with a mutual conditioning training paradigm where the network predicts joint (ε_RGB, ε_α) noise given inputs from mismatched timesteps (e.g., y_t^RGB with y_{t-1}^α), enabling sequential conditional denoising at inference. Second, pre-generated RGBA instances are integrated into a composite scene via multi-layer noise blending: each instance's latent is obtained through DDIM inversion, and at the first n=30 denoising timesteps, instance latents are sequentially injected into progressively complex layer representations using alpha-mask-weighted blending (Eq. 2), with optional background blending (Eq. 3) and cross-layer consistency regularisation. This sequential, one-instance-at-a-time integration (as opposed to simultaneous assembly in prior multi-layer methods) is what enables precise control over instance ordering, relative positioning, and smooth scene coherence.

## Results

On RGBA instance generation, the proposed method achieves a KID of 0.0150, IoU of 0.892, and CLIP Score of 18.49, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, PixArt-α, achieves a KID of 0.0447 (3x worse) but cannot produce transparency masks (IoU N.A.), while the best matting-based approach (PixArt-α+MA) reaches IoU 0.811 but with KID 0.0558. In scene composition, the method qualitatively outperforms GLIGEN, MultiDiffusion, and Instance Diffusion on complex scenes with overlapping objects, accurate attribute assignment, and scene manipulation with strong content preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all RGBA generation baselines: it reduces KID from 0.0447 (PixArt-α) to 0.0150 and achieves IoU of 0.892 versus 0.811 for PixArt-α+MA, 0.649 for SD-v1.5+MA, and only 0.326/0.320 for Text2Layer and LayerDiffusion respectively. LayerDiffusion's lower performance is attributed to its implicit transparency encoding in the VAE null space, which can produce non-transparent outputs. For scene composition, GLIGEN accurately reproduces layouts but frequently misassigns object attributes (e.g., wrong colors) and fails on highly overlapping objects. MultiDiffusion handles attributes better than GLIGEN but struggles with overlapping instances due to its noise averaging approach lacking instance ordering. Instance Diffusion is the closest competitor, achieving similar performance on simpler scenes but still failing on complex patterns, relative positioning, and scene manipulation consistency, where the proposed multi-layer approach maintains strong content preservation across edits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-layer generation paradigm (MultiDiffusion, Instance Diffusion, LayerDiffusion) by decoupling instance generation from scene composition: rather than generating all layers jointly in a single diffusion process, it first produces isolated RGBA instances via a dedicated transparency-aware LDM fine-tune, then sequentially composites them through multi-layer noise blending. It departs from LayerDiffusion's implicit null-space transparency encoding by introducing explicit RGB/alpha channel disentanglement in the VAE with separate KL losses, and from matting-based pipelines (e.g., PixArt-α + Matte Anything) by natively generating alpha channels. The base architecture builds on PixArt-α (a Diffusion Transformer LDM), and the composition step draws on DDIM inversion and noise blending techniques from MultiDiffusion, but the sequential, layer-by-layer integration with background blending and cross-layer consistency regularisation is a new procedural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage RGBA-then-compose pipeline offers a practical path to fine-grained, interactive scene editing (replacing objects, adjusting layout) without full regeneration, addressing a key limitation of both single-image layout methods (GLIGEN) and joint multi-layer methods (Instance Diffusion). However, the approach requires full fine-tuning of both VAE and diffusion model (ruling out parameter-efficient adapters), generates instances independently which can hurt scene coherence, and incurs significant computational overhead from concurrent K+1 image generation. Open questions include whether conditioned RGBA generation (e.g., with background or depth cues) can reduce the coherence gap, whether the disentangled VAE strategy generalises beyond 4-channel RGBA to other multi-channel representations, and how to automate the layout and per-instance prompt design that currently requires manual intervention.
