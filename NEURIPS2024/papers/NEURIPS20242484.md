## Problem

Off-policy evaluation in causal reinforcement learning is biased when unmeasured confounders influence both actions and outcomes, yet existing deconfounding methods either impose a restrictive one-way confounding assumption (excluding time-varying confounders) or require the latent confounders to be deterministic functions of actions. This paper introduces a two-way unmeasured confounding assumption that partitions all latent confounders into trajectory-specific and time-specific groups, providing a flexible yet estimable middle ground between unconstrained and one-way confounding.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N offline trajectories of length T, each consisting of observation-action-reward triplets (O_{i,t}, A_{i,t}, R_{i,t}), along with a target policy π to evaluate. It outputs a scalar policy value estimate η^π computed via Monte Carlo rollouts through the learned transition model, plus learned d-dimensional embedding vectors {u_i} for trajectory-specific confounders and {w_t} for time-specific confounders. Because the two-way confounders are policy-agnostic (unaffected by actions), the estimator uses a plug-in approach: the latent factors are conditioned on directly rather than requiring distributional estimation under the target policy.

## Method

The proposed two-way deconfounder (TWD) models the transition function P̂(·|a,o,u_i,w_t) as a conditional Gaussian and the behavior policy π̂_b(·|o,u_i,w_t) as an actor network, both parameterized through a shared neural tensor network (NTN) layer that captures bilinear interactions between the trajectory-specific embedding u_i and time-specific embedding w_t. Training minimizes a weighted combination of the negative log-likelihood of the transition model and the cross-entropy loss of the behavior policy, jointly optimizing the embedding vectors and all network parameters. For OPE, the learned model is used in a Monte Carlo policy evaluation: actions are sampled from the target policy, and reward-observation pairs are drawn from the learned transition network conditioned on the estimated latent factors, with multiple rollouts averaged to reduce Monte Carlo error. The paper provides a finite-sample error bound showing the estimator's mean absolute error decomposes into transition-model estimation error (linear in horizon T), latent confounder estimation error, and standard deviation terms that vanish as N and T grow.

## Results

On the MIMIC-III dataset (3,707 patients, 20 timesteps), TWD achieves the lowest prediction RMSE for both immediate reward and next observation among all model-based methods, and correctly ranks the tailored individualized policy as superior to randomized, high-dose, and low-dose policies. In the simulated dynamic process (T=50, N up to 2000), TWD consistently achieves the smallest logarithmic MSE with bias closest to zero across all trajectory counts, while NUC-based and POMDP-based baselines exhibit severe bias. In the tumor growth simulation, TWD again attains the lowest LMSE and is empirically unbiased for both target policies, whereas alternative methods show no significant improvement as the number of trajectories increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TWD outperforms all eight baselines in both simulation settings (dynamic process and tumor growth) on logarithmic MSE, with the gap widening as confounding strength increases. On MIMIC-III, TWD (0.254) marginally beats MB (0.255) and TWDIDP2 (0.262), while substantially outperforming RSSM (0.332), which models confounders as latent states. The NUC-based methods (MB, MWL, DR) and POMDP-type methods (PO-MWL, PO-DR) are severely biased in the confounded simulation settings, with LMSEs several times higher than TWD. TWDIDP1 (model-free variant of Bian et al.) performs especially poorly and is excluded from the MIMIC-III figure for display purposes. Notably, TWD and MB are the only two methods that correctly identify the tailored individualized policy as the best on MIMIC-III, while MWL, DR, PO-DR, and RSSM incorrectly rank the high-dose policy above the individualized one.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deconfounding-type approach to off-policy evaluation by replacing Wang and Blei's (2019) problematic 'deterministic unmeasured confounding' assumption with a two-way unmeasured confounding (TWUC) structure inspired by two-way fixed effects models in panel data econometrics. It builds on Bian et al. (2023), who applied a two-way doubly inhomogeneous decision process to OPE, by introducing unmeasured confounding and replacing their restrictive linear function approximation with flexible neural networks. The neural tensor network architecture from Socher et al. (2013) is repurposed to capture the intricate interactions between trajectory-specific and time-specific latent confounders, a design choice drawn from recommendation systems and knowledge graph completion rather than causal inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TWUC assumption provides a principled middle ground between the infeasible unconstrained model (NT parameters) and the overly restrictive one-way model (N parameters), with a clean theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) showing consistent estimation when N+T ≪ NT. However, the sensitivity analysis reveals that TWD's performance degrades sharply when the TWUC assumption is violated—particularly in the tumor growth example where it only outperforms baselines at Γ=1.0—limiting its practical applicability to settings where confounders are genuinely separable into trajectory- and time-specific components. Open questions include extending the framework to handle confounders that are both trajectory- and time-specific, and adapting confounder selection algorithms from the bandit literature to the RL setting.
