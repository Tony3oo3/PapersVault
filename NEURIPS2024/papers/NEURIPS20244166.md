## Problem

Online variational Bayes for neural networks typically requires an iterative inner loop at each timestep to minimize the full variational loss (expected NLL plus KL divergence to the prior), making updates slow and computationally expensive. The paper asks whether, in the online setting, the KL regularization term can be dropped entirely and the inner loop replaced by a single natural gradient step, since the prior from the previous step already provides implicit regularization. They prove this simplified update is exact for conjugate exponential families and show it outperforms iterative methods in non-conjugate settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of (x_t, y_t) pairs one at a time, where x_t is a D-dimensional input and y_t is a C-dimensional observation (e.g., one-hot class labels for classification or a scalar for regression). At each step it maintains a Gaussian variational posterior over P network parameters, parameterized as full covariance, diagonal, or diagonal-plus-low-rank (DLR) precision. The output is the approximate posterior q(ψ_t)(θ_t) from which predictive distributions are obtained via Monte Carlo sampling, linearized-MC, or a plugin (mean-only) approximation; the primary evaluation metric is negative log predictive density on a held-out test set.

## Method

BONG performs a single step of natural gradient descent with unit learning rate on the expected log-likelihood alone (omitting the KL term), starting from the prior predictive q_{ψ_{t|t-1}}; this is proven equivalent to exact Bayesian updating for conjugate exponential families (Proposition 4.1). For non-conjugate neural networks, BONG-LIN replaces the intractable expectation with a deterministic closed-form update by linearizing the network output at the prior mean, yielding Kalman-filter-like mean and covariance updates (Eqs. 23–25) that match the conditional-moments EKF. The paper proves equivalence between two linearization strategies—linear(h)-Gaussian and linear(f)-delta—and shows the framework unifies RVGA, CM-EKF, VD-EKF, and LO-FI as special cases. Hessian computation is handled via four approximations (MC-HESS, MC-EF, LIN-HESS, LIN-EF), and the full method space crosses 4 update rules × 4 Hessian approximations × 5 variational families, with BONG-LIN-HESS emerging as the best-performing and most computationally efficient configuration.

## Results

On MNIST (CNN, 57,722 params, 2000 training steps), BONG-LIN-HESS with DLR-10 variational family achieves the best NLPD across all training steps, outperforming BLR-LIN-HESS, BOG-LIN-HESS, and BBB-LIN-HESS. On SARCOS (MLP 21-20-20-1, 881 params), BONG and BLR are indistinguishable with 1 iteration per step, while BBB and BOG are substantially worse; with 10 iterations, BBB approaches BONG/BLR but at ~10× the runtime. BONG's one-step update is consistently faster than iterative baselines (BLR, BBB), and the linearized Hessian (LIN-HESS) outperforms Monte Carlo (MC-EF) in both accuracy and speed. DLR-10 rank captures most of the benefit of full covariance at much lower cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BLR (Khan & Rue, 2023), BONG wins on both statistical and computational efficiency: with the LIN-HESS approximation and 1 iteration, BONG matches or slightly exceeds BLR's NLPD on MNIST and SARCOS while being significantly faster (one step vs. an inner loop). With 10 iterations, BLR catches up in accuracy on SARCOS but incurs roughly 10× the runtime, and BONG's fixed unit learning rate avoids the sensitivity to learning rate that plagues BLR (Fig. 11 shows BLR degrades sharply at suboptimal α). Against BBB (Blundell et al., 2015), BONG clearly wins: BBB with 1 iteration is substantially worse in NLPD on both datasets, and even with 10 iterations BBB only approaches BONG on SARCOS while remaining ~10× slower and more sensitive to learning rate (many rates produce NaNs with I=1). The paper also shows that BONG's implicit regularization (dropping the KL term and using a single NGD step from the prior) is the key differentiator from BLR, which retains the KL and requires multiple iterations to converge.

## Relation to Prior Work

BONG is positioned as a simplification of the Bayesian Learning Rule (BLR, Khan & Rue 2023): by performing a single natural gradient step with unit learning rate on the expected log-likelihood alone (dropping the KL-to-prior term), the algorithm becomes a special case of BLR that is provably exact in the conjugate setting. The linearized variant (BONG-LIN) is shown to recover several previously derived methods—CM-EKF (Ollivier 2018, Tronarp et al. 2018), LO-FI (Chang et al. 2023), and the explicit RVGA update (Lambert et al. 2021)—as special cases, unifying them under a single variational-inference-plus-natural-gradient framework. The paper also draws on the mirror descent / exponential weights literature (Hoeven et al. 2018, Lyu & Tsang 2021) to provide an alternative derivation, and contrasts with Bayes by Backprop (Blundell et al. 2015) as the GD-based counterpart.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical contribution is a systematic 80-algorithm design space (4 update rules × 4 Hessian approximations × 5 variational families) with explicit complexity analysis, giving practitioners a clear menu of options for online Bayesian NN training. The finding that a single-step, KL-free update outperforms iterative VI-loss optimization (BLR, BBB) is a useful simplification that also eliminates the need for learning-rate tuning. However, all experiments use small models (≤58K parameters) and small datasets (MNIST, SARCOS), so it remains open whether the advantages persist at scale, particularly for the DLR representation that the authors identify as the most promising for larger networks.
