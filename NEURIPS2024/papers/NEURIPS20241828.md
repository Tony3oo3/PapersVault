## Problem

Flow matching models provide direct access to image likelihoods via the change-of-variables formula, making them natural priors for MAP-based inverse problem solving. However, computing the log-likelihood requires backpropagating through an ODE solver, which is prohibitively slow and memory-intensive for high-dimensional images, preventing practical use of flow priors in MAP estimation for tasks like super-resolution, deblurring, and compressed sensing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded measurement y ∈ R^m (produced by a known linear operator A: R^n → R^m with m ≤ n, plus Gaussian noise with known σ_y), the operator A itself, and a pretrained flow-based generative model (e.g., a rectified flow UNet or Transformer). It outputs a single reconstructed clean image x_1 ∈ R^n as the MAP estimate. The method is training-free with respect to the inverse problem: the flow prior is pretrained on a dataset (CelebA-HQ at 256×256 for natural images, or HCP T2w grayscale brain images for medical applications) and then frozen during reconstruction. The algorithm uses N=100 ODE function evaluations and K gradient steps per timestep.

## Method

ICTM (Iterative Corrupted Trajectory Matching) approximates the global MAP objective as a weighted sum of N 'local MAP' objectives, each depending only on adjacent trajectory points x_{(i-1)Δt} and x_{iΔt} (Theorem 1). The key computational insight is that Tweedie's formula yields a closed-form expression for ∇_{x_t} log p(x_t) in terms of the velocity field v_θ(x_t, t), eliminating the need to backpropagate through the ODE solver. At each timestep, the algorithm performs K gradient steps to match the corrupted trajectory A(x_t) to an auxiliary interpolation path y_t = α_t·y + β_t·A(x_0), using a guidance weight λ to balance data fidelity against the local prior (which includes a Hutchinson-Skilling trace estimate of the Jacobian). The trajectory is advanced via an Euler ODE step after each local optimization, and the final x_1 is returned as the MAP reconstruction. The theoretical guarantee shows the auxiliary objective converges to the true MAP objective as N → ∞, with the error bounded by O(Δt) under a uniform Lipschitz bound on the velocity field's Jacobian trace.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ (256×256), ICTM achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all four linear inverse problems: 27.91/0.805 (super-resolution), 30.65/0.894 (random inpainting), 26.54/0.760 (Gaussian deblurring), and 24.34/0.866 (box inpainting). On the HCP T2w compressed sensing task, ICTM outperforms all baselines at every compression rate, reaching 32.72 PSNR / 0.878 SSIM at ν=1/2 and 24.03 PSNR / 0.503 SSIM at the challenging ν=1/10 setting. The method also runs in approximately 1.6 minutes per image, compared to 5–10 minutes for the concurrent D-Flow method that requires backpropagation through an ODE solver.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICTM outperforms all five baselines on every task in Table 1. Against the closest flow-based competitor OT-ODE, ICTM gains +0.45 PSNR on super-resolution, +2.08 PSNR on random inpainting, +0.26 PSNR on deblurring, and +4.54 PSNR on box inpainting (where OT-ODE degrades most severely). The diffusion-based baselines RED-Diff and ΠGDM are competitive on some tasks (e.g., RED-Diff leads on deblurring PSNR at 27.23) but fall well behind ICTM on inpainting and box inpainting. On compressed sensing (Table 2), ICTM surpasses DPS-ODE by +1.66 PSNR at ν=1/2 and +1.97 PSNR at ν=1/10, while classical Wavelet and TV priors are far weaker (e.g., TV Prior reaches only 14.38 PSNR at ν=1/10 vs. ICTM's 24.03). The ablation 'Ours (w/o prior)' confirms the local prior term contributes meaningfully, dropping PSNR by 1.85 on super-resolution and 1.92 on box inpainting when removed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022) and rectified flow (Liu et al., 2022) as generative priors for inverse problems, but departs from prior flow-based inversion methods (OT-ODE, DPS-ODE) that either use conditional OT path corrections or score-based guidance at each sampling step. Its key conceptual departure is formulating the reconstruction as a MAP estimation problem and then decomposing the intractable global log-likelihood (which requires backpropagation through an ODE solver) into a weighted sum of 'local MAP' objectives, each optimizable via Tweedie's formula to obtain score gradients directly from the velocity field. This avoids the 5–10 minute per-image cost of concurrent work D-Flow, which performs full ODE backpropagation at every optimization step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled and computationally efficient (~1.6 min/image) route to MAP estimation with flow priors, which is important because flow matching models are increasingly the backbone of state-of-the-art image generators and their invertibility makes them natural priors for inverse problems. However, the approach is currently restricted to linear forward operators and straight (OT) interpolation paths, and cannot quantify reconstruction uncertainty—both critical gaps for scientific imaging applications. Extending the local MAP decomposition to nonlinear operators and general interpolation schedules, and developing uncertainty quantification on top of the point-estimate framework, are the most impactful open directions.
