## Problem

Hyperbolic geometry has been successfully used to embed unsupervised tree-based models and to embed data for supervised learning, but no principled approach exists for embedding supervised models themselves in hyperbolic space. This is a notable gap even for tree-based supervised models like decision trees, whose structural properties would naturally lend themselves to hyperbolic representation, and for which existing visualization tools lack principled geometric grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244471/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained decision tree (or a boosted ensemble of decision trees) with posterior probabilities at each node as input, and produces three outputs: a Monotonic Decision Tree (MDT) that is a path-monotonic sub-approximation of the original DT, a hyperbolic embedding in the Poincaré disk where each node's radial distance from the origin encodes its classification confidence via the log-loss canonical link, and a t-self embedding that re-maps points to a tempered version of the disk to avoid numerical instability near the boundary. For boosted ensembles, leveraging coefficients are also encoded geometrically as an imaginary node per tree.

## Method

The paper contributes three independent mechanisms. First, it establishes that the canonical link of the log-loss (the inverse sigmoid, |ψ_LOG(p)| = log((1+r)/(1-r))) is exactly the Poincaré disk distance from the origin, providing a direct mapping from posterior confidence to radial position. Second, it introduces Monotonic Decision Trees (MDTs) and the GETMDT algorithm, which extracts the strictly path-monotonic subsequence of a DT so that all embedded arcs point outward in the disk, yielding a clean, unambiguous embedding via a modified Sarkar algorithm. Third, it develops t-calculus—a generalization of Leibniz-Newton's fundamental theorem of calculus using tempered addition (z ⊕_t z' = z + z' + (1-t)zz')—to define the t-self of the Poincaré disk, where the parameter t smoothly controls the hyperbolic constant and pushes high-confidence nodes away from the numerically fragile disk boundary while preserving hyperbolicity.

## Results

On 12 UCI/OpenML/Kaggle domains, MDTs achieve classification test errors within ~1% of their parent DTs on most domains (e.g., winered: 18.32% vs 18.39%, german: 23.90% vs 24.00%, qsar: 12.89% vs 12.98%), with statistically significant differences only on ionosphere (DT 5.71% vs MDT 9.11%, p=0.024), online_shoppers_intention (DT 9.90% vs MDT 10.67%, p=0.006), and analcatdata_supreme (MDT 22.56% vs DT 23.40%, p=0.013). MDT embedding errors ρ in the Poincaré disk are small in general (2–17%) and tend to decrease with the MDT's index in the boosted ensemble (e.g., winered drops from 12.13% at #1 to 4.44% at #5; qsar from 16.78% to 4.24%). The t-self embedding with t=0 pushes high-confidence nodes substantially away from the disk border (e.g., r=1−10⁻⁴ maps to r⁽ᵗ⁾≈0.96 for t=0.7), improving both visualization and numerical encoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external competing embedding methods; its primary comparison is between decision trees (DTs) and their monotonic decision tree (MDT) counterparts in terms of classification accuracy and embedding quality. On the majority of 12 domains, DT and MDT test errors are statistically indistinguishable (p > 0.05), confirming that the MDT reduction preserves predictive performance. Three domains show significant divergence: on ionosphere and online_shoppers_intention the DT outperforms its MDT by 3.4 and 0.77 percentage points respectively, while on analcatdata_supreme the MDT is slightly better (−0.84 pp). The full appendix results additionally reveal that on the noisy hillnoise domain the MDT degrades substantially (45.88% vs 37.04%, p=0.0008), attributed to the domain's high noise level. The embedding error ρ is consistently small (typically <10% after the first few ensemble members), validating the modified Sarkar embedding for MDTs, and the t-self (t=0) provides a clear visualization advantage over the standard Poincaré disk by separating high-confidence nodes from the border.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant hyperbolic embedding literature, which has focused on embedding unsupervised hierarchical structures (Nickel & Kiela, Sarkar) or embedding data into hyperbolic space for supervised training (Ganea et al., Chlenski et al.), by instead embedding the supervised models themselves. It builds on Sarkar's low-distortion tree embedding algorithm [30] (modified for variable arc lengths and out-degrees), Nivanen et al.'s tempered algebra [22] (extended here with a full generalization of the fundamental theorem of calculus), and Amid et al.'s preliminary t-calculus [2] (which lacked the derivative/integral duality). The boosting component parallels AdaBoost's design [31] but targets the logistic loss rather than the exponential loss, enabling direct Poincaré-disk interpretation of leveraging coefficients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled framework for visualizing supervised decision-tree models in hyperbolic geometry, with the t-calculus and t-self constructions offering independent utility for any integral-based distortion measure in ML (Bregman divergences, f-divergences, integral probability metrics). The introduction of Monotonic Decision Trees as a new model class opens questions about their statistical properties and whether they can serve as efficient, explainable alternatives to standard DTs in production pipelines. Key limitations include restriction to tabular data (DTs' natural domain), potential loss of classification information in the DT→MDT reduction (notable on noisy or small domains like hillnoise and ionosphere), and the fact that the embedding algorithm is a heuristic modification of Sarkar's rather than an optimal solution.
