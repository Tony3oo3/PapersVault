## Problem

Existing federated foundation model (FedFM) research addresses data heterogeneity and communication overhead but neglects test-time distribution shifts, where clients encounter unseen tasks with different distributions during inference. This paper introduces the test-time personalization setting, which requires simultaneously achieving client-specific personalization on a local task and robustness to complex, multi-type distribution shifts (semantic, domain, etc.) on unseen test-time tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241245/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives text instructions formatted as 'Instruction:{instruction} Response:' covering diverse NLP tasks (paraphrase, entailment, summarization, QA, etc.), all converted to a unified generative format. During inference, the instance-wise dynamic weighting mechanism additionally consumes S=5 randomly sampled local training instances to compute similarity scores against the test input's last-token embedding from the LLM's final hidden layer (augmented with the global adapter). Output is generated text evaluated via ROGUE-1, with each client holding 300 training and 200 test instances per task.

## Method

FedDPA maintains two LoRA adapters per client on a frozen LLaMA-7B: a global adapter trained via FedAvg aggregation across all clients to capture generic, distribution-robust features for test-time tasks, and a local adapter that aligns with the client's specific distribution for personalization. The local adapter is either fine-tuned sequentially after global training (FedDPA-F) or trained iteratively alongside the frozen global adapter each round with a fixed mixing weight α (FedDPA-T). At inference, an instance-wise dynamic weighting mechanism computes cosine similarity between the test instance's last-token embedding (with global adapter) and S sampled local training embeddings, producing a per-instance weight α_t that dynamically blends the global and local adapter outputs. Only the global adapter weights are communicated to the server, keeping communication overhead identical to standard LoRA-based FedFM while the local adapter remains entirely client-side.

## Results

Using ROUGE-1 as the evaluation metric, FedDPA-T achieves the best personalization performance with an average score of 80.22 on Federated Dataset 1 and 75.33 on Federated Dataset 2, outperforming all baselines. For test-time personalization (average across all tasks including unseen test-time tasks), FedDPA-F leads with 77.12 on Dataset 1 and 71.64 on Dataset 2, surpassing FedLoRA (75.63 and 69.86 respectively). The local-finetuned baseline drops dramatically on test-time tasks (42.86 and 40.88), highlighting the critical importance of the dual-adapter design for handling distribution shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two federated LoRA-based baselines: FedIT (federated instruction tuning) and FedLoRA (model-heterogeneous personalized FL with LoRA). On personalization, FedDPA-T outperforms FedLoRA by 0.79 points on Dataset 1 (80.22 vs. 79.43) and by 2.59 points on Dataset 2 (75.33 vs. 72.74), while FedIT trails both FedDPA variants by 1.7–2.7 points. On test-time personalization, FedDPA-F surpasses FedLoRA by 1.49 points on Dataset 1 (77.12 vs. 75.63) and by 1.78 points on Dataset 2 (71.64 vs. 69.86), confirming that the global adapter's aggregated generic knowledge is critical for handling unseen distribution shifts. FedIT is not evaluated on test-time personalization, as it lacks a personalization mechanism. The local-finetuned baseline collapses on test-time tasks (42.86 vs. 75+ for federated methods), underscoring that without cross-client knowledge sharing, models cannot generalize to new task distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning and FedAvg aggregation to propose a dual-adapter architecture (global + local LoRA) for federated foundation models. It departs from prior FedFM work (FedIT, FedLoRA) that addressed data heterogeneity and communication overhead but ignored test-time distribution shifts, and from prior test-time FL methods (Tan et al., Jiang & Lin) that operated on conventional deep networks trained from scratch rather than foundation models. The key conceptual departure is the 'test-time personalization' setting, which jointly requires client-specific alignment and robustness to unseen test-time tasks, resolved by separating generic knowledge (aggregated global adapter) from personalized knowledge (local adapter) and combining them via an instance-wise dynamic weighting mechanism at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize test-time distribution shifts within the FedFM context, a practically critical gap since deployed federated LLMs will inevitably encounter tasks unseen during training. The dual-adapter design is simple and adds negligible communication overhead (only the global LoRA is transmitted), making it deployable. However, the method relies on a Mixture-of-Experts assumption that test-time tasks are mixtures of tasks seen by other clients, limiting applicability to truly novel domains; it also presupposes each client can store and run a 7B+ foundation model, and evaluation is confined to NLP tasks with 8–40 clients. Open questions include theoretical guarantees for the dual-adapter optimization, handling of genuinely unseen tasks (not just cross-client tasks), and extension to multimodal or non-LLM foundation models.
