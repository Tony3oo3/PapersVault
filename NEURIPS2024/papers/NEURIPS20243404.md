## Problem

Existing video frame interpolation methods rely on CNNs or attention for inter-frame modeling, which either lack sufficient receptive fields to capture correlations at high resolutions (e.g., 4K) or incur quadratic computational cost. Selective State Space Models (S6/Mamba) offer linear complexity with global receptive fields and data-dependent adaptability, but had not been applied to low-level video tasks like frame interpolation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243404/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two consecutive RGB frames (H×W×3) and a normalized interpolation timestep t, producing a single intermediate frame of the same resolution. It operates at multiple scales via progressive downsampling (patch embedding halves resolution while doubling channels), and is evaluated on inputs ranging from 256×256 (Vimeo90K) to 4K (X-TEST). For 8× interpolation, an iterative divide-and-conquer strategy is used to generate all intermediate frames from the two endpoints.

## Method

The core architectural contribution is the Mixed-SSM Block (MSB), which interleaves tokens from both input frames into a single 'super image' sequence and applies multi-directional S6 scanning (horizontal, vertical, and their reverses) to model inter-frame information with linear complexity. The interleaved rearrangement is shown to be superior to sequential concatenation because it preserves spatiotemporal locality in the hidden attention weights of S6. The MSB replaces the standard MLP with a Channel-Attention Block to compensate for SSMs' lack of inter-channel interaction. A curriculum learning strategy progressively increases motion magnitude during training by resizing X-TRAIN frames to larger crops and doubling the temporal interval every 50 epochs, enabling the model to handle large motions without forgetting small-motion capabilities learned from Vimeo90K.

## Results

On the high-resolution X-TEST benchmark, VFIMamba achieves 33.34 dB PSNR (2K) and 32.15 dB PSNR (4K), improving by 0.96 dB and 0.80 dB over the previous best method SGM-VFI, respectively. On the low-resolution SNU-FILM benchmark, VFIMamba attains 30.99 dB PSNR on the 'hard' split and 25.79 dB on 'extreme', setting new state-of-the-art across all difficulty levels. The full model requires 0.94T FLOPs and 310.9 ms runtime at 1024×1024, comparable to efficient CNN-based methods (EMA-VFI: 0.91T, 239.6 ms) while substantially outperforming attention-based approaches (SGM-VFI: 1.78T, 942.9 ms; AMT-G: 2.07T, 403.7 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VFIMamba achieves state-of-the-art PSNR on every high-resolution benchmark, with the largest margins on X-TEST: +0.96 dB over SGM-VFI (33.34 vs 32.38) at 2K and +0.80 dB at 4K (32.15 vs 31.35). On the low-resolution SNU-FILM benchmark, VFIMamba leads on all four difficulty splits (e.g., 30.99 vs 30.94 dB on 'hard' vs EMA-VFI; 25.79 vs 25.69 dB on 'extreme'), while matching EMA-VFI exactly on Vimeo90K (36.64 dB). Compared to the most competitive attention-based baselines, VFIMamba outperforms SGM-VFI by 0.86 dB average PSNR on high-res data while using less than half the FLOPs (0.94T vs 1.78T) and running 3× faster (311 ms vs 943 ms). Against AMT-G, VFIMamba gains 0.73 dB average PSNR on high-res with 55% fewer FLOPs (0.94T vs 2.07T). The lightweight VFIMamba-S variant (0.24T FLOPs) already surpasses all prior methods on X-TEST 2K (32.84 dB), demonstrating that the S6-based architecture is effective even at reduced capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to adapt the Selective State Space Model (S6/Mamba) to video frame interpolation, building directly on Mamba's data-dependent parameter generation and Vmamba's multi-directional scanning strategy for 2D feature maps. It departs from the dominant CNN-based (RIFE, IFRNet) and attention-based (EMA-VFI, SGM-VFI, AMT, BiFormer) paradigms for inter-frame modeling by proposing the Mixed-SSM Block, which interleaves tokens from two frames into a single sequence before applying multi-directional S6. The curriculum learning strategy, inspired by Bengio et al. (2009), is a novel application to VFI that progressively increases motion magnitude during training, addressing the known limitation of Vimeo90K-only training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that SSMs can achieve SOTA VFI performance with linear complexity, this work opens a promising direction for low-level video tasks where high-resolution inputs (4K) and long sequences make quadratic-attention methods impractical. The curriculum learning strategy generalizes beyond SSMs (validated on RIFE and EMA-VFI), suggesting broader applicability. However, the model still falls short of real-time inference, and the authors note that SSMs have not yet been explored for the frame generation stage, leaving room for further architectural exploration. The efficiency advantage (FLOPs and GPU memory scaling linearly with resolution) is particularly relevant as VFI is increasingly applied to 4K and beyond.
