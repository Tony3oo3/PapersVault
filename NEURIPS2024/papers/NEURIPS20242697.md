## Problem

Diffusion guidance is widely used to modulate generative model outputs, but its original justification—that the guided score equals the score of a tilted distribution p(x)·p(z|x)^(1+w)—is mathematically incorrect because tilting and noising do not commute. Despite this, no prior work provided a rigorous, fine-grained characterization of what distribution the guided ODE actually samples from, leaving the empirical success of guidance theoretically unexplained.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unconditional score function ∇log p_t, a conditional score function ∇log p_t(·|z), a guidance parameter w, and a class label z, and produces a generated sample by solving the guided probability flow ODE. The theoretical results characterize where the ODE trajectory lands: for compactly supported mixtures, samples concentrate in an interval of width O(α₂/√ln w) at the support edge; for Gaussian mixtures, samples reach Θ(√w). Experiments validate these predictions on 2D synthetic mixtures, 28×28 MNIST (grayscale, one-vs-all reduction), and 256×256 ImageNet (RGB, classifier-guided).

## Method

The paper reformulates the guided probability flow ODE using Tweedie's formula and a change of variables s = a_t, decomposing the dynamics into a base denoising term and a guidance term weighted by the posterior probability of the wrong class q_t^(-1). In the compactly supported setting, they prove the particle undergoes two phases: first, a strong push (velocity Ω(√w)) that swings it past the support edge to position Θ(ln w), then a slow return where it gets trapped near the boundary because the remaining time is insufficient to traverse the support. In the Gaussian setting, the ODE simplifies to x̃'(s) = (w+1) − w·tanh(s·x̃(s)), and they prove samples reach Θ(√w). A third result shows that any nonzero L₂ score estimation error, amplified by the trajectory's excursion into the tails, causes samples to leave the data support entirely when w exceeds Õ(√log log(1/ε)), explaining the empirical degradation at high guidance.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is proving that for mixtures of compactly supported distributions, guidance with parameter w concentrates samples in the interval (α₂(1−O(1/√ln w)), α₂) with probability 1−e^{−Ω(w)}, i.e., at the boundary of the support. For Gaussian mixtures, the sample moves to at least √w with probability 1−e^{−Ω(w)}. Empirically, on MNIST (digit 0 vs. all others), w=3 yields the best sample quality while preserving monotonicity of projected trajectories, and on ImageNet (256×256, DDIM 25 steps), s=5 (i.e., w=4) is the largest guidance value with zero support error (mean absolute deviation from valid RGB range [0,255]).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper without direct method-to-method comparisons. The key empirical 'comparison' is between different guidance parameter values: on MNIST, increasing w from 0 to 3 sharpens sample concentration while maintaining trajectory monotonicity, but w≥5 introduces a visible pullback effect and some trajectories diverge due to score estimation errors. On ImageNet, the non-monotonicity phenomenon observed on MNIST disappears (due to lack of class separability), but support error grows monotonically with guidance strength, and s=5 is identified as the qualitatively optimal value. The paper also contrasts its fine-grained trajectory analysis with prior theoretical work: Wu et al. [37] provide only summary-statistic bounds (classification confidence, entropy) for Gaussian mixtures, while Bradley and Nakkiran [3] give a non-rigorous numerical demonstration for the same Gaussian mixture; this paper provides the first rigorous proof of the boundary-concentration phenomenon and the first analysis of how score estimation error interacts with large guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the probability flow ODE framework of Song et al. (2021) and the classifier-free guidance formulation of Ho & Salimans (2022), providing the first rigorous fine-grained trajectory analysis of what guidance actually samples from. It extends prior theoretical work by Wu et al. (2024), who studied guidance on Gaussian mixtures but only through summary statistics (classification confidence, diversity), and by Bradley & Nakkiran (2024), who showed a simple counterexample to tilted-distribution sampling but did not characterize the full dynamics. The key departure is the introduction of compactly supported mixture settings and the analysis of how score estimation error interacts with large guidance, neither of which appears in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing misconception in the diffusion literature by proving that guidance does not sample from the tilted distribution that motivated its design, even with zero score estimation error, and explains the empirical 'sweet spot' phenomenon through a geometric mechanism (trajectory overshoot amplifying tail errors). Practical heuristics for selecting guidance strength are validated on MNIST and ImageNet, offering actionable guidance for practitioners. Key limitations include the restriction to one-dimensional settings and specific mixture structures; extending the analysis to high-dimensional data (e.g., mixtures over disjoint convex bodies) and optimizing the guidance parameter theoretically are identified as important open directions.
