## Problem

While text-to-image models have democratized art creation, no large-scale dataset captures the full landscape of user-generated artistic styles with original prompts over an extended period. Existing AI-generated image datasets (DiffusionDB, TWIGMA, Midjourney Kaggle) either lack original text prompts, cover only brief timeframes (e.g., 12 days for DiffusionDB), or have limited user bases, making it impossible to study the evolution and diversity of crowd-sourced styles. This paper asks: what unique, undocumented artistic styles emerge from a community of 95K users over 18 months, and how can they be systematically discovered, personalized, and recommended?

## Input / Output

The dataset comprises 6.8M images (512×512 to 1280×896) paired with their original positive and negative text prompts (avg. 60 words), anonymized user IDs, timestamps, and model hyperparameters (seed, CFG scale, steps, model type) from 95,479 users on Artbreeder spanning July 2022–May 2024. Outputs include 10,000 style cluster assignments computed via CSD embeddings, personalized images generated by four PEFT methods per cluster, per-user style recommendations via matrix factorization, and 100 downloadable style LoRA models. Each image also carries computed NSFW, toxicity, and safety scores to enable filtering.

## Method

The core pipeline has three stages. First, for style discovery, images are embedded using CSD (a ViT-based style descriptor extractor that outperforms content-focused models like DINO on style-matching tasks), then clustered into 10,000 groups via K-Means++ with cosine similarity, where the cluster count is selected by maximizing silhouette score. Second, for personalized generation, four parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods—Textual Inversion, LoRA w/DreamBooth (rank 32), EDLoRA, and Custom Diffusion—are trained on 500 images per cluster and benchmarked using CLIP-I, CLIP-T, and DINO similarity metrics, with EDLoRA achieving the best style fidelity. Third, for recommendation, a user-cluster rating matrix is constructed where each entry is the proportion of a user's images in a given cluster, and SVD-based matrix factorization predicts unobserved cluster preferences (RMSE 0.1425). The key differentiator is the use of a style-specific feature extractor (CSD) rather than generic visual features, combined with the 18-month temporal span and original prompts that enable tracking style evolution and artist attribution.

## Results

On the STYLEBREEDER dataset, EDLoRA achieves the highest average CLIP-I score (0.6957±0.11) and DINO score (0.4125±0.18) among four personalization methods, indicating superior style preservation in generated images. LoRA w/DreamBooth leads on CLIP-T (0.1896±0.02), suggesting slightly better text-image alignment. For style recommendation, the SVD-based matrix factorization model achieves a mean RMSE of 0.1425 (std 0.0017) and mean MAE of 0.082 (std 0.001) across 5-fold cross-validation on 96K images from 1,434 users.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four personalization methods on 40 randomly selected style clusters from STYLEBREEDER. EDLoRA [14] dominates on both style-similarity metrics (CLIP-I and DINO), achieving 0.6957 avg CLIP-I and 0.4125 avg DINO, outperforming Textual Inversion [11] by ~1.3% on CLIP-I and ~8.5% on DINO. LoRA w/DreamBooth [22,40] edges out EDLoRA on CLIP-T (0.1896 vs. 0.1822), indicating marginally better text-prompt adherence, but trails significantly on style fidelity (CLIP-I 0.6299 vs. 0.6957). Custom-Diffusion [26] is consistently the weakest method across all three metrics, with the lowest CLIP-I (0.5917) and DINO (0.2546) averages, confirming the paper's observation that it struggles with style disentanglement. The authors conclude that EDLoRA's layerwise embedding and multi-word representation approach best preserves the discovered artistic styles during personalized generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against existing AI-generated image datasets (DiffusionDB, TWIGMA, Midjourney Kaggle) by offering original text prompts, multi-model coverage, and an 18-month temporal span versus DiffusionDB's 12-day window. For the experimental tasks, it repurposes well-established personalization methods (LoRA/DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, Custom Diffusion, EDLoRA) and standard clustering (K-Means++ on CSD ViT embeddings) rather than introducing new algorithms. The matrix-factorization recommendation system follows the classical SVD-based collaborative filtering paradigm. The paper's conceptual contribution is thus primarily the dataset and the systematic cataloging of crowd-sourced artistic styles, rather than a methodological departure from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.8M-image, 95K-user dataset under CC0 license fills a notable gap: no prior AI-art dataset combined original prompts, multiple diffusion models, and a long temporal window, enabling studies of style evolution and trend dynamics. The Style Atlas platform with 100 downloadable LoRAs lowers the barrier for artists and researchers to experiment with discovered styles. Open questions include how to handle the substantial NSFW and toxic content (only scored, not removed in the full release), the copyright implications of 688K images matching 372 known artists' styles, and whether the 10,000-cluster granularity meaningfully captures sub-style variation. The paper acknowledges risks of over-reliance on AI in art and potential reinforcement of training-data biases.
