## Problem

Training diffusion models to sample from distributions defined by unnormalized energy functions suffers from inconsistent benchmarking, reproducibility failures, and suboptimal exploration in off-policy settings. Existing diffusion-structured samplers (PIS, DDS, DIS, continuous GFlowNets) have been evaluated under incomparable conditions with unstated hyperparameters and even conflicting target definitions, making it unclear which exploration and credit-assignment strategies genuinely help.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242574/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a differentiable energy function E(x) (and optionally its gradient ∇E) over R^d, and for conditional tasks additionally a conditioning vector x (e.g., a 784-dim MNIST image). It produces samples from the Boltzmann target p_target(x) ∝ exp(−E(x)) via T-step Euler-Maruyama integration of a learned neural SDE, along with a Monte Carlo estimate of the log-partition function log Z. The forward policy is a Gaussian with learned mean (drift) and, in extended settings, learned variance; the backward process is fixed to a discretized Brownian bridge or a variance-preserving Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a local-search-guided off-policy exploration strategy for continuous GFlowNets: parallel MALA chains are run from sampler outputs to populate a replay buffer with low-energy samples, and training alternates between on-policy/exploratory forward trajectories (Step A) and backward trajectories sampled from buffer targets (Step B), using rank-based prioritized sampling to emphasize high-density regions. This is combined with a Langevin parametrization that adds a learned-scaled energy gradient to the SDE drift, providing a gradient-based inductive bias that transfers from on-policy PIS to off-policy GFlowNet training. The unified codebase benchmarks these techniques against PIS, DDS, DIS, FL-SubTB, and VarGrad under identical architectures and hyperparameters, revealing that the SubTB/partial-trajectory objective offers little benefit over plain TB in the diffusion setting, while local search with replay buffer substantially improves mode coverage and prevents mode collapse across 2D to 1600D target distributions.

## Results

On the 25GMM (d=2) task, the proposed TB+Expl.+LS achieves the best amortized Δlog Z of 0.171±0.013 and Δlog Z^RW of 0.004±0.011, substantially outperforming PIS (1.769/1.274) and DIS (1.125/0.986). On the 32-dimensional Manywell, TB+Expl.+LP+LS achieves Δlog Z^RW of 0.07±0.17, compared to 2.69±0.04 for PIS and 2.66±0.22 for FL-SubTB+LP. On the 1600-dimensional LGCP, TB+Expl.+LP+LS estimates log Z = 471.14±0.25, matching DDS (471.64±1.20) and substantially exceeding PIS (381.14±1.42). The local search + replay buffer (LS) component is the key driver of improvement, preventing mode collapse and enabling efficient exploration of high-density regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PIS [88], the proposed TB+Expl.+LS achieves a 10× reduction in Δlog Z on 25GMM (0.171 vs. 1.769) and a 14× reduction in Δlog Z^RW on Manywell (0.19 vs. 2.69), while on LGCP the combined TB+Expl.+LP+LS matches DDS's log Z estimate (471.14 vs. 471.64) and exceeds PIS by ~90 nats. Against DIS [8], the proposed methods dominate on all tasks: on 25GMM, Δlog Z drops from 1.125 to 0.171, and on Manywell from 10.52 to 4.68. Against DDS [78], the proposed methods win on 25GMM (0.171 vs. 1.760) and Manywell Δlog Z^RW (0.07 vs. 0.23), while DDS slightly edges out on LGCP log Z (471.64 vs. 471.14). Against FL-SubTB [86], the paper's central empirical finding is that the SubTB objective provides no consistent benefit over plain TB: FL-SubTB+LP achieves Δlog Z = 0.209 on 25GMM versus 0.190 for TB+Expl.+LP+LS, and the paper further reports that [86]'s published code does not reproduce its claimed results. Against GGNS [43] (a non-amortized nested sampler), the proposed methods are competitive on Manywell (4.68 vs. 0.292 Δlog Z) but the amortized nature of GFlowNets is the key advantage for repeated sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work on diffusion-structured sampling: simulation-based variational methods (PIS, DDS, DIS) that optimize a reverse-KL objective via reparametrization, and continuous GFlowNets (Lahlou et al., 2023) that frame the same problem as off-policy reinforcement learning with a trajectory balance loss. It borrows the Langevin parametrization from PIS (Zhang & Chen, 2022) and adapts replay-buffer techniques from discrete GFlowNets (Deleu et al., 2022) to the continuous setting, while explicitly benchmarking against and challenging the FL-SubTB objective of Zhang et al. (2024). The paper's core positioning is that of a unified, reproducible benchmarking study that also introduces a new exploration mechanism (local search with parallel MALA and a prioritized replay buffer) absent from all prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified codebase and fair benchmarking resolve inconsistencies in the literature (e.g., differing Funnel definitions, missing ground-truth log Z for LGCP) and provide a practical foundation for future work on amortized inference with diffusion models. The finding that FL-SubTB offers no consistent advantage over plain TB in the diffusion setting, combined with the non-reproducibility of the original FL-SubTB results, redirects attention toward simpler objectives augmented with better exploration. Open questions include the continuous-time limit (T→∞) of all studied algorithms, whether learning the forward variance and backward process (extensions demonstrated preliminarily here) becomes critical in higher dimensions, and how these amortized samplers integrate into EM loops for latent variable model training.
