## Problem

Existing subtask discovery methods rely on heuristic segmentation or intuitive graphical models that may conflict with the true data generation process, potentially misleading downstream imitation learning. The paper argues that subtasks are fundamentally the result of a selection mechanism on actions (subgoals as selection variables), not confounders or intermediates, and provides the first causal identification theory and learning algorithm grounded in this structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240382/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes unsegmented multi-task expert trajectories as a data matrix X where each column is the concatenated state-action vector x_t = (s_t; a_t), requiring the number of subtasks J and maximum subtask length L as hyperparameters. It produces a 3D tensor O ∈ R^{D×J×L} of temporally extended subtask patterns, a binary subgoal indicator matrix H ∈ {0,1}^{J×T}, and a subgoal-conditioned policy π_g(a_t|s_t, g_t) that can be re-composed to solve new tasks with different subtask orderings.

## Method

The approach has two core components. First, a causal identification theory uses d-separation to distinguish selection variables (subgoals) from confounders and intermediates via conditional independence tests: s_t ⊥̸ a_t | g_t is sufficient to identify a selection, and s_{t+1} ⊥ g_t | s_t, a_t provides a necessary condition. Second, seq-NMF decomposes the trajectory matrix via convolution (X ≈ O * H) with three tailored regularizers: a binary penalty R_bin forcing H toward {0,1}, an L1 sparsity penalty R_1 to minimize the number of subtasks, and a similarity penalty R_sim to prevent redundant subtask patterns. Multiplicative update rules are derived from standard NMF extended with temporal convolution. For transfer to new tasks, a GAIL-based policy is trained with PPO using the learned subgoals as an augmented action space, disentangling subtask discovery from policy optimization.

## Results

On the Color-3 (Simple) dataset, seq-NMF achieves a perfect F1 of 0.99±0.00 for subtask boundary recovery, compared to VTA (0.82±0.13) and LOVE (0.91±0.02). On Color-3 (Conditional), it again reaches F1 0.99±0.00 versus VTA (0.83±0.19) and LOVE (0.90±0.03), with perfect recall (1.00) in both settings. On the Kitchen environment, the method outperforms all three hierarchical imitation learning baselines in episodic accumulated return on both 4-subtask and 5-subtask transfer tasks, demonstrating effective generalization to unseen subtask compositions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VTA (Kim et al., NeurIPS 2019), seq-NMF achieves substantially higher F1 on both Color-3 variants (0.99 vs. 0.82/0.83), with VTA showing high variance (±0.13–0.22) while seq-NMF is essentially deterministic. Against LOVE (Jiang et al., 2024), seq-NMF matches precision (0.99) but improves recall from 0.85/0.83 to a perfect 1.00, yielding F1 of 0.99 vs. 0.91/0.90. On the Kitchen transfer tasks, seq-NMF outperforms all three hierarchical IL baselines—H-AIRL (Chen et al., ICML 2023), Directed-info GAIL (Sharma et al., 2019), and Option-GAIL (Jing et al., 2021)—in episodic accumulated return on both the 4-subtask and the more challenging 5-subtask generalization settings. The paper attributes its advantage to disentangling subgoal learning (via seq-NMF) from policy training, avoiding the joint optimization instability of methods like H-AIRL that train up to five networks simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the option framework (Sutton et al. 1999) and the long line of subtask/skill discovery work (McGovern & Barto 2001, Simsek & Barto 2004, Krishnan et al. 2017, Kipf et al. 2019, Sharma et al. 2019, Jing et al. 2021, Chen et al. 2023), but departs fundamentally from prior graphical models by arguing that subgoals are selection variables (colliders: s_t → g_t ← a_t) rather than confounders or mediators. It draws on causal identification theory (d-separation, conditional independence tests from Spirtes et al. 2001, Pearl 2009) to formally distinguish these three structures, and on temporal NMF (Smaragdis 2004, Mackevicius et al. 2019) to build the seq-NMF learning algorithm. For the downstream imitation learning, it adopts GAIL (Ho & Ermon 2016) with PPO, positioning against hierarchical IL baselines like H-AIRL, Directed-Info GAIL, and Option-GAIL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled causal justification for modeling subgoals as selections, with verifiable CI tests that confirm this structure in real trajectory data. This reframing has implications beyond subtask discovery: it clarifies why prior methods using confounder-like or mediator-like graphical models (DI-GAIL, ComPILE) may produce biased inferences, and it connects the subgoal framework to the probabilistic inference view of RL (Levine 2018). Key limitations include the inability to handle co-existing confounders and selections simultaneously (though a relaxation is sketched), the need to specify the number of subtasks J and maximum length L a priori, and empirical validation limited primarily to the Kitchen benchmark. Open questions include extending to hierarchical subgoals, handling distribution shifts in system dynamics, and scaling to high-dimensional continuous state spaces.
