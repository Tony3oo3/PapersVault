## Problem

Existing inference benchmarks focus on short time horizons, predominantly visual stimuli, and physical or single-agent reasoning, leaving a gap in evaluating whether AI models can perform the kind of long-horizon, multimodal 'whodunit' reasoning humans do in everyday household scenarios. MARPLE addresses this by introducing a benchmark that requires integrating visual, auditory, and language evidence over extended trajectories to attribute a state change to one of two agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243454/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives step-by-step observations from two agents in a 20×20 GridWorld (8-channel array representation plus scene graph), natural language subgoal descriptions (e.g., 'I am going to toggle on the light'), and 1-second audio clips mapped to low-level actions. The output is a binary attribution (Agent A or B) with a calibrated probability, evaluated at 11 evenly spaced timesteps from 0 to T, where the key metric is how early in the trajectory the model reaches 0.8 inference accuracy.

## Method

MARPLE builds on Mini-BEHAVIOR to create a procedurally generated household GridWorld with a three-level hierarchical planner (mission sampling → finite-state-machine subgoal sequencing → A* low-level action planning) that produces diverse, long-horizon agent trajectories with multimodal evidence. The primary simulation baseline learns agent policies via behavioral cloning (ViT encoder + MLP policy head, or ViT + 4 decoders for language-conditioned variants) and performs Monte Carlo rollouts (m=100) from the current state to estimate P(s_T | π^i, o_{0:τ}), normalizing across agents with a Boltzmann-rationality softmax. Audio evidence refines action predictions via Bayes' rule, while language evidence conditions the policy on the intended subgoal. The LLM baseline (GPT-4-0613) receives scene-graph representations of consecutive timesteps and the query state with a zero-shot chain-of-thought prompt, and is also evaluated with in-context learning examples. Five inference scenarios are defined by pairing 10 household missions with varying similarity (0.19–0.87) and inference horizon (15–51 steps), with 500 test trajectories per scenario across 10 environments.

## Results

On the MARPLE benchmark, human participants reach 0.8 inference accuracy with only 48% of the trajectory evidence, outperforming all AI baselines. The best Monte Carlo simulation variant (Vision+Audio+Language) requires 58% of evidence in-distribution but degrades to 81% out-of-distribution, while GPT-4 requires 95% of evidence in both settings and fails to converge on 2 of 5 inference scenarios. The performance gap between humans and the best simulation method widens from 10% (in-distribution) to 33% (out-of-distribution) less evidence needed, highlighting significant generalization challenges for learned models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4-0613, the primary LLM baseline, requires 95% of trajectory evidence to reach 0.8 accuracy and critically fails to converge on two of five scenarios ('Who turned on the shower?' and 'Who turned on the laundry?'), where the query state reflects an environment change rather than an agent state change. In-context learning improves GPT-4's performance but does not resolve the convergence failure. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct performs at or below GPT-4's level, showing similar non-convergence on the same two scenarios, while Qwen2-7B-Instruct performs worst of the three LLMs, with inference accuracy that does not improve as more trajectory evidence is revealed. All three LLMs are substantially outperformed by the best MCTS simulation variant (Vision+Audio+Language), which converges to 1.0 accuracy by trajectory end and requires 37% less evidence in-distribution. Humans outperform all AI methods across every scenario, requiring 10–47% less evidence than the best model depending on the setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARPLE extends the line of cognition-inspired inference benchmarks (CLEVRER, CATER, AGENT, BIB, PHASE, MMToM-QA) by moving from short-horizon, predominantly visual reasoning to long-horizon, multimodal (vision + language + audio) inference in procedurally generated household environments. It builds directly on the Mini-BEHAVIOR GridWorld simulator, extending it with a hierarchical planner for autonomous agent behavior generation and multimodal evidence (language descriptions of subgoals, action-mapped audio). The inference problem is formalized as a POMDP, connecting to the inverse planning and goal inference literature, while the 'whodunit' framing draws on classic detective narratives to structure the evaluation around identifying a responsible agent from partial evidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MARPLE fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark simultaneously covers long-horizon reasoning, multimodal evidence, and complex everyday household scenarios, making it a useful testbed for evaluating whether current inference methods can handle humanlike event reconstruction. The finding that GPT-4 fails to converge on scenarios where the query state is an environment change (rather than an agent state change) reveals a specific and actionable weakness in how LLMs process scene-graph representations. Key limitations include the GridWorld abstraction (no physical realism), template-generated language and audio, and the restriction to two non-interacting agents; open questions include whether models can generalize to unseen environments (the simulation baseline drops from 58% to 81% evidence needed out-of-distribution) and whether the benchmark can be extended to multi-agent interaction scenarios.
