## Problem

Existing diffusion model distillation methods split into two camps—ODE trajectory preservation (which suffers from accumulated fitting errors at low step counts) and ODE trajectory reformulation (which causes domain shifts relative to the original model)—and neither achieves near-lossless quality compression to 1–8 steps. No prior framework synergistically combines the trajectory-fidelity of consistency distillation with the quality-boosting potential of adversarial and reward-based reformulation in a single unified pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243724/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt (and optionally a scribble or canny edge map via ControlNet) and outputs a single RGB image at 512×512 (SD1.5) or 1024×1024 (SDXL). It operates as a lightweight LoRA plugin (or a fully fine-tuned UNet) that is CFG-free and supports inference at any step count from 1 to 8 through a single unified LoRA, eliminating the need to load separate models per step count.

## Method

Hyper-SD introduces Trajectory Segmented Consistency Distillation (TSCD), which partitions the full timestep range [0, T] into k segments and performs consistency distillation within each segment before progressively reducing k from 8→4→2→1, thereby reducing per-stage fitting complexity while preserving the original ODE trajectory at a higher-order level. On top of TSCD, the framework layers human feedback learning that combines an aesthetic reward (LAION predictor + ImageReward) with a structural perceptual loss from an instance segmentation model (SOLO) to steer generation quality in the low-step regime. Finally, an optimized Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) stage with score-based KL minimization enhances one-step fidelity, and all three stages are trained as LoRA adapters so that a single unified LoRA supports inference at every step count from 1 to 8 without model conversion.

## Results

On SDXL (1024px), Hyper-SDXL at 1 step achieves CLIP Score 32.85, Aes Score 5.85, ImageReward 1.19, and PickScore 0.231, surpassing SDXL-Lightning (1-step) by +0.68 CLIP and +0.51 Aes, and SDXL-Turbo (1-step) by +0.52 CLIP and +0.52 Aes. At 4 steps, Hyper-SDXL (LoRA) reaches CLIP 32.56, Aes 5.74, ImageReward 0.93, and PickScore 0.232, outperforming all 4-step baselines. On SD15 (512px), Hyper-SD15 at just 1 step achieves CLIP 30.87, Aes 5.79, ImageReward 0.29, and PickScore 0.215, exceeding all 4-step competitors (LCM, TCD, PeRFlow) on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hyper-SDXL (1-step UNet) decisively outperforms both SDXL-Turbo and SDXL-Lightning (1-step UNet) across all four metrics, with the largest gap on ImageReward (+0.41 over Turbo, +0.65 over Lightning) and Aes Score (+0.52 over both). At 4 steps, Hyper-SDXL (LoRA) beats SDXL-Lightning (LoRA) by +0.16 CLIP, +0.11 Aes, and +0.21 ImageReward, and surpasses SDXL-TCD and SDXL-LCM on every metric. On SD15, Hyper-SD15 at 1 step outperforms all 4-step baselines (LCM, TCD, PeRFlow) on CLIP, Aes, and ImageReward, with ImageReward improving from negative values (-0.37 to -0.15) to +0.29. The only comparison where Hyper-SD does not lead is CLIP Score at 1 step in the ablation (Table 3), where DMD achieves 32.70 vs. TSCD's 32.59, though TSCD still wins on Aes, ImageReward, and PickScore. In user studies, Hyper-SD15 achieves over two-thirds preference over same-architecture baselines, and the 1-step UNet variant is preferred over LoRA variants against UNet-based competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hyper-SD unifies two previously separate distillation paradigms for diffusion models—trajectory-preserving (Consistency Distillation, CTM, TCD) and trajectory-reformulating (adversarial distillation, DMD, human feedback learning)—into a single progressive pipeline. The core TSCD mechanism is a segmented, multi-stage refinement of CTM/TCD that progressively reduces the number of time-step segments (8→4→2→1), addressing the fitting-capacity bottleneck of single-stage consistency distillation. The one-step enhancement stage repurposes DMD's score-matching objective, while the human feedback stage borrows the ReFL reward-optimization framework and adds a novel perceptual feedback signal from instance segmentation (SOLO). The practical packaging as plug-and-play LoRA plugins builds on the SDXL-Lightning and TCD LoRA conventions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified LoRA that supports inference at any step count (1–8) without model switching is a practically significant deployment simplification, and the LoRA-based approach keeps the acceleration lightweight and composable with existing base models and ControlNet. However, the paper acknowledges that CFG is eliminated across all accelerated variants, which restricts content safety controls and negative-prompt usage—a non-trivial limitation for production systems. Open questions include whether the TSCD segmentation strategy and the perceptual-feedback trick transfer to the emerging DiT architecture, and whether customized (rather than generic) reward models can further close the gap to full-step quality at 1–2 steps.
