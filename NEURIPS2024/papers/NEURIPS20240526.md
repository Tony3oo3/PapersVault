## Problem

In the compute-limited, infinite-data regime relevant to large language model training, it is unclear how model size should scale with compute budget, and whether a single universal scaling law applies. Prior theoretical analyses of neural scaling laws either ignored algorithmic effects (treating training as gradient descent) or captured only a subset of the loss curve components, missing the distinct roles of feature-embedding distortion and SGD noise. This paper asks: for a solvable random-features model, how do data complexity, target complexity, model capacity, embedding distortion, and optimizer noise interact to determine the compute-optimal frontier?

## Input / Output

The method operates on a fully specified three-parameter PLRF model (data complexity α, target complexity β, model parameter count d) with a fixed compute budget expressed in flops via the 6ND formula, rather than on empirical datasets. It produces a phase diagram over the (α,β)-plane with 4 main phases and 3 subphases, each with a closed-form compute-optimal model size d*(f) ∝ f^ξ and scaling-law exponent η, plus the full compute-optimal loss curve. A key structural feature is that the optimal d*(f) can be f^{1/2} (universal, Chinchilla-like) in some phases but a different power of f in others, and the phase boundaries are determined by which pair of loss components (capacity, distortion, SGD noise) is in tradeoff.

## Method

The paper derives a convolution-type Volterra equation for the expected loss under one-pass SGD on the PLRF model, where the forcing function represents gradient descent on the population loss and the kernel function captures the excess risk from SGD noise. Random matrix theory is then used to replace the random resolvent of K̂ = D^{1/2}WW^T D^{1/2} with a deterministic equivalent defined by a fixed-point equation, yielding a fully deterministic Volterra equation whose solution matches empirical SGD training curves. The forcing function is decomposed into three spectral components — F_0 (irreducible capacity limit), F_pp (aligned features solved at population rate), and F_ac (distorted features spread across many eigenmodes) — and the kernel into K_pp (SGD noise propagated through population dynamics). By identifying which subset of these four terms dominates the loss in each region of the (α,β)-plane, the paper partitions the phase space into 4+3 phases and derives the compute-optimal curve in each by solving for the intersection point of the two dominant terms under the constraint f = r·B·d.

## Results

The paper identifies 4 phases (+3 subphases) in the (α, β) phase plane, each with distinct compute-optimal scaling exponents. In Phases Ib, III, and IVa, the optimal model size is universally d* ∝ f^(1/2) independent of (α, β), theoretically verifying Chinchilla scaling. Numerical validation on the Phase II–III boundary yields a measured scaling exponent of 0.648 vs. the theoretical prediction of 0.643, and optimal model size exponents of 0.508 and 0.525 (Approaches 1 and 2) vs. the theoretical 0.5. The Volterra equation solution matches SGD training dynamics exactly across d = 200 to 12,800.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Defilippis et al. [18] (ridge regression + O(1) regularization), this paper's one-pass SGD rates match in Phases Ia and II but differ in Phase III, where SGD noise (K_pp) introduces an additional r^(−2+1/(2α)) term absent in the ridge regression analysis. Against Lin et al. [28] (linear regression scaling laws), the compute-optimal curves agree only in Phase Ia; the prior work misses the cross-term F_ac and SGD noise K_pp, which become dominant in Phases II–IV. Against the minimax-optimal SGD bounds of Carratino et al. [11], Dieuleveut & Bach [20], and Pillaud-Vivien et al. [36], this paper achieves strictly better (faster-decaying) risk rates in all phases, since minimax optimality is worst-case over a problem class and does not capture the specific power-law structure exploited here. The key distinction is that this paper's rates are for one-pass SGD at the maximal stable learning rate, whereas the minimax bounds require very small stepsizes or multiple passes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the solvable PLRF model of Maloney et al. [30] and Bahri et al. [5] by incorporating the full SGD training dynamics via a convolution-type Volterra equation (building on Paquette et al. [33, 35]) and random-matrix deterministic equivalents [23]. The key departure from prior compute-optimal scaling analyses (Chinchilla [24], Bordelon-Pehlevan [9], Lin et al. [28]) is the inclusion of two previously omitted loss components — a feature-distortion cross-term (F_ac) and SGD noise (K_pp) — which the authors show alter the compute-optimal exponents in three of four phases. Prior theoretical work captured only the Phase Ia (capacity-constrained) regime; this paper provides a complete 4+3 phase diagram with distinct scaling exponents in each.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretical verification of Chinchilla scaling (d* ∝ f^{1/2}) but simultaneously shows it is phase-dependent rather than universal, challenging the assumption that a single scaling law governs all compute-optimal training. The practical warning that at 1 A100-GPU-day of compute, measured exponents are still drifting toward their asymptotic values raises concerns about the reliability of empirically derived scaling laws at current scales. Open questions include the behavior at the critical point (α=β=1/2), whether alternative stochastic algorithms would alter the Phase III/IV compute-optimal curves, and whether the universal d* ∝ f^{1/2} regime observed across Phases Ib, III, and IVa extends to more realistic (nonlinear, finite-data) settings.
