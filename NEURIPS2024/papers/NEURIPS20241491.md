## Problem

Data augmentations in domain generalizable person re-ID exhibit a polarized effect: they improve in-distribution retrieval performance while degrading out-of-distribution performance. The paper reveals that this occurs because augmentations induce sparse representation spaces with reduced uniformity, causing the model to overfit to dominant visual cues at the expense of diverse information needed for open-set generalization. Prior work simply discarded problematic augmentations like Random Erasing without addressing the underlying mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241491/cropped_page3_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×128 RGB person images as input, generating augmented views via Random Erasing, RandAugment, and Color Jitter applied with probability p=0.5. Each training batch contains 256 original and 256 augmented images (64 identities × 4 instances each), processed through a ResNet-50 backbone with instance normalization to produce d-dimensional feature embeddings. Retrieval is performed in an open-set setting where source and target domains have disjoint identity spaces, evaluated via mAP and Rank-1 on unseen target domains.

## Method

BAU regularizes the representation space by applying three complementary losses to both original and augmented features: (1) an alignment loss that minimizes distance between original-augmented positive pairs, weighted by Jaccard similarity of k-reciprocal nearest neighbors to filter unreliable augmented samples; (2) a uniformity loss (log of average pairwise Gaussian potential) applied to both original and augmented features to maintain diverse spread on the hypersphere; and (3) a domain-specific uniformity loss computed against a momentum-updated feature memory bank of class prototypes, which promotes uniformity within each source domain to reduce domain bias. The overall objective combines cross-entropy and triplet losses (on originals only) with these three regularizers, requiring no adversarial training, meta-learning, or specialized network modules—only a standard ResNet-50 backbone with the added loss terms.

## Results

On Protocol-3 (full source train+test data), BAU achieves an average mAP of 52.3% and Rank-1 of 65.7% across three leave-one-out settings (M+MS+CS→C3, M+CS+C3→MS, MS+CS+C3→M), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art ACL by +3.0% mAP and +3.0% Rank-1. On Protocol-2 (training data only), BAU reaches 48.1% mAP and 61.7% Rank-1, a +2.8% mAP improvement over ACL. On Protocol-1 (small-scale target datasets), BAU achieves 77.2% average mAP and 69.5% Rank-1 across PRID, GRID, VIPeR, and iLIDs, surpassing all prior methods that do not use the withdrawn DukeMTMC-reID dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAU outperforms all 14 compared baselines across all three protocols. On Protocol-3, BAU beats the previous SOTA ACL [90] by +3.0% mAP (52.3 vs 49.3) and +3.0% Rank-1 (65.7 vs 62.7), and surpasses META [86] by +3.0% mAP and +2.8% Rank-1. The gap over meta-learning-based methods (M³L [94], MetaBIN [10]) is even larger, with BAU exceeding M³L by +13.8% mAP and MetaBIN by +9.3% mAP on Protocol-3. On Protocol-2 (training data only, harder setting), BAU leads ACL by +2.8% mAP and META by +6.0% mAP. On Protocol-1 (small-scale targets), BAU achieves 77.2% average mAP, beating ACL (75.2%), StyCon (74.1%), and GDNorm (76.3%, which uses the withdrawn DukeMTMC-reID), while also surpassing adversarial-training-based DDAN (65.1%) and MoE-based RaMoE (62.0%) by wide margins. Notably, BAU achieves these gains without feature normalization modules, domain-adversarial training, or meta-learning, relying solely on alignment/uniformity regularization with data augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the alignment and uniformity framework from Wang and Isola (ICML 2020), originally developed for contrastive representation learning, to regularize the feature space of person re-ID models under data augmentation. It also adapts the k-reciprocal nearest neighbor concept from Zhong et al. (CVPR 2017), previously used for re-ranking in re-ID, as a reliability-weighting mechanism for the alignment loss. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant DG re-ID paradigm that relies on complex architectures (feature normalization modules, domain-specific batch norms, MoE experts), adversarial training, or meta-learning, instead showing that a simple loss-based regularization on the representation space suffices to achieve competitive or superior generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic diagnosis of why data augmentations (particularly Random Erasing and RandAugment) degrade out-of-distribution re-ID performance despite improving in-distribution accuracy, attributing it to reduced uniformity in the representation space. This insight is practically important because it explains why prior DG re-ID methods simply discarded widely-used augmentations, and shows they can be safely re-introduced with proper regularization. Limitations include potential difficulty under very large domain shifts and the absence of adversarial or feature-level augmentations, which the authors identify as promising future directions. The alignment-uniformity balancing principle could extend to other fine-grained open-set retrieval tasks such as vehicle re-ID or face recognition.
