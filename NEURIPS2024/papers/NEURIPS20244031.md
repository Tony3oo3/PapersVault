## Problem

Cross-spectral (visible-infrared) person re-identification suffers from significant modality discrepancy, yet existing data augmentation strategies (grayscale, random channel selection, random erasing) lack a physical model explaining the underlying transformation. This paper identifies, via the Lambertian reflection model, that the modality gap arises from diverse local linear transformations across different material surfaces, and provides a unified framework that categorizes all prior augmentation strategies under this perspective.

## Input / Output

The method operates on visible RGB and single-channel infrared person images resized to 384×192 pixels. MRLE is applied only to multi-channel visible images by computing a convex combination of R, G, and B channels, while RRLE operates on any image (including single-channel IR) by selecting random rectangular patches and scaling them. The trained model outputs identity rankings evaluated via CMC curves (R-1 through R-20), mAP, and mINP on both all-search and indoor-search protocols.

## Method

The core contribution is a Random Linear Enhancement (RLE) strategy grounded in the Lambertian reflection model, which reveals that cross-spectral transformation is locally linear within a material region but globally non-linear due to material diversity. RLE comprises two components: Moderate Random Linear Enhancement (MRLE) generalizes grayscale and random channel selection by sampling channel mixing coefficients from a U-shaped Beta(β_m, β_m) distribution subject to the constraint λ_r + λ_g + λ_b = 1, thereby exploring the full feasible transformation space; and Radical Random Linear Enhancement (RRLE) randomly selects rectangular patches (following random erasing geometry) and multiplies them by a linear factor α = α_max · f_g where f_g is drawn from a U-shaped Beta(β_r, β_r) distribution, repeated iteratively with a memory matrix M to track cumulative per-pixel changes and terminate when min(M) < t_min. This unified view positions prior methods (grayscale, random channel selection, random erasing) as special cases and pushes the boundary of both moderate and radical transformations simultaneously.

## Results

On SYSU-MM01 (All Search), RLE+RE with a basic ResNet-50/PCB backbone achieves R-1=75.4%, R-10=97.7%, and mAP=72.4%, outperforming the previous best DEEN (74.7/97.6/71.8) and CAJ+ (71.5/96.2/68.2). On the Indoor Search protocol, the method reaches R-1=84.7% and mAP=87.0%, surpassing DEEN by 4.4 and 3.7 points respectively. On RegDB, RLE achieves R-1=92.8% and mAP=88.6% (VIS→IR) and R-1=91.0% and mAP=86.6% (IR→VIS), setting new state-of-the-art results across all four evaluation settings without any extra modality-specific modules or constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLE+RE outperforms all 18 compared baselines across every evaluation setting. The closest competitor is DEEN [39], which RLE surpasses by 0.7 R-1 and 0.6 mAP on SYSU-MM01 All Search, by 4.4 R-1 and 3.7 mAP on Indoor Search, and by 1.7 R-1 / 3.5 mAP (VIS→IR) and 1.5 R-1 / 3.2 mAP (IR→VIS) on RegDB. Against the channel-augmentation family (CAJ [11], CAJ+ [12]), RLE gains 5.5 R-1 and 5.5 mAP on All Search and 8.4 R-1 and 5.0 mAP on Indoor Search, demonstrating that the physics-motivated linear enhancement substantially exceeds simple grayscale/channel strategies. On RegDB, RLE also beats the generative approach MMN [25] by 1.2 R-1 and 4.5 mAP (VIS→IR) and 3.5 R-1 and 6.1 mAP (IR→VIS), confirming that data augmentation alone can rival or surpass methods that learn explicit cross-spectral translation. Notably, the backbone used (ResNet-50/PCB with only softmax + triplet loss) contains no modality-specific modules, yet RLE achieves state-of-the-art results, highlighting the method's generality as a plug-and-play augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Lambertian reflection model to provide a physical explanation for cross-spectral modality discrepancy, showing that the VIS-NIR transformation is locally linear (per material surface) but globally non-linear. It unifies previously ad-hoc augmentation strategies—grayscale transformation, random channel selection (CAJ/CAJ+), and random erasing—under a single framework of 'local linear transformations,' categorizing them into moderate (preserving original linear correlations via channel mixing) and radical (applying arbitrary linear scaling to random patches). The key conceptual departure from prior work is that rather than treating augmentations as heuristic tricks, the paper derives them from a reflection physics model and then pushes their boundaries: MRLE generalizes discrete channel selection to a continuous beta-distributed mixing space, and RRLE extends random erasing by replacing zeroing with random linear scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified physical perspective gives practitioners a principled lens for designing and combining cross-spectral augmentations, and the fact that a basic PCB backbone with only RLE+RE (no modality-specific modules, no GANs, no auxiliary losses) reaches SOTA suggests that much of the complexity in prior methods may be unnecessary. However, the approach is validated on only two small-scale datasets (SYSU-MM01, RegDB), the Lambertian model's validity under extreme weather (heavy rain, fog, very low illumination) is unverified, and generalizability to other cross-modal or cross-spectral tasks remains an open question. The paper also acknowledges that the RLE strategy may not transfer as a general-purpose augmentation beyond cross-spectral Re-ID.
