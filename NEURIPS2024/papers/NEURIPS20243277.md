## Problem

SDF-based volume rendering methods for neural surface reconstruction fail to capture intricate geometric details, producing incorrect surfaces where density-based methods succeed. The paper identifies two root causes: a global-scale SDF-to-density conversion that restricts density representation and over-regularization from geometric constraints (e.g., Eikonal loss) that prevent necessary topological changes during optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243277/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes posed RGB images as input and produces a 3D mesh extracted via marching cubes at 2048 resolution, along with the underlying SDF field. The geometry network uses a multi-resolution hash grid (2^5 to 2^11 across 16 levels, 2^19 entries per resolution) with per-image appearance encoding. Training samples 1024 pixels per iteration in the coarse stage and 8192 in the refinement stage for large-scale scenes, or 512 pixels for room-level scenes, with total training of 300k steps on a single A100 GPU (~7 hours for indoor, ~18 hours for large-scale scenes).

## Method

NeuRodin introduces three key mechanisms within a two-stage optimization framework. First, it replaces the global SDF-to-density scale with a local adaptive scale predicted per-point by the geometry network, enabling arbitrary non-negative density values within the same SDF level set. Second, it adds an explicit bias correction loss that penalizes positive SDF values just beyond the maximum probability distance (argmax of rendering weight), aligning the volume rendering geometry with the implicit zero-level set. Third, the two-stage design uses stochastic-step numerical gradient estimation in the coarse stage—sampling the finite-difference step size uniformly from (0, ε_max) at each iteration—so that large-scale features receive stable Eikonal constraints while fine details retain topological freedom; the refinement stage then switches to standard analytical gradients with a smoothness constraint and TUVR's low-bias SDF-to-density conversion to produce a smooth, high-fidelity surface.

## Results

On the Tanks and Temples training subset, NeuRodin achieves a mean F-Score of 0.51, outperforming Neuralangelo (0.50) with only 1/8 the hash-grid parameters, and substantially beating NeuS (0.38), VolSDF (0.39), and COLMAP (0.21). On the Tanks and Temples advance subset, NeuRodin reaches 28.84 F-Score versus 27.14 for Neuralangelo* and 26.28 for Neuralangelo. On the newly established ScanNet++ benchmark, NeuRodin attains a mean F-Score of 0.638 without any prior knowledge, surpassing Neuralangelo (0.564) and NeuS (0.455), and approaching MonoSDF-Grid (0.642) which leverages monocular depth priors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuRodin outperforms all nine baselines on the Tanks and Temples training subset, with the most notable margin over Neuralangelo* (0.51 vs. 0.43 F-Score) when using identical hash-grid parameters, confirming the gains come from the method rather than parameter count. On the Tanks and Temples advance subset, NeuRodin leads by 1.7 F-Score over Neuralangelo* (28.84 vs. 27.14) and 2.0 over Neuralangelo (28.84 vs. 26.28). On the ScanNet++ benchmark, NeuRodin (0.638) beats all prior-knowledge-free methods—Neuralangelo (0.564), NeuS (0.455), VolSDF (0.391)—and is within 0.004 of MonoSDF-Grid (0.642), which uses monocular depth priors. On the DTU benchmark, NeuRodin achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 0.60, marginally beating Neuralangelo (0.61) and clearly surpassing NeuS (0.84), VolSDF (0.86), and NeRF (1.49). The only scene where NeuRodin trails a baseline is Courthouse on Tanks and Temples (0.21 vs. Neuralangelo's 0.28), and on DTU, TUVR-Grid (0.82) is outperformed by NeuRodin (0.60), confirming the advantage of the two-stage optimization over purely unbiased SDF-to-density modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuRodin builds directly on the SDF-based volume rendering lineage (NeuS, VolSDF, Neuralangelo) while drawing conceptual inspiration from density-based methods (Instant-NGP, NeRF) that freely handle topological changes. It departs from prior SDF-to-density conversion work (TUVR, Debsdf) by replacing the global scale with a per-point local scale (inspired by Adaptive Shells) and introduces an explicit bias correction loss that aligns the maximum probability distance with the zero level set. The two-stage optimization with stochastic-step numerical gradient estimation extends Neuralangelo's progressive numerical gradient strategy, replacing its deterministic schedule with a uniform random step to decouple large-scale stability from fine-detail flexibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first public ScanNet++ reconstruction benchmark (8 scenes, 7 baselines), filling a notable gap in the evaluation landscape for indoor neural surface reconstruction. The plug-and-play nature of the explicit bias correction—validated across NeuS, VolSDF, and TUVR—makes it immediately applicable to existing SDF pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge that bias is never fully eliminated, textureless/under-observed regions remain problematic, and reconstruction of large scenes requires ~18 GPU hours, limiting real-time applicability. Open questions include whether the two-stage paradigm generalizes to dynamic scenes or can be accelerated with learned initialization.
