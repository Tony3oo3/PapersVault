## Problem

Existing unsupervised anomaly detection methods require fully observed data, yet real-world tabular data in bioinformatics, recommendation systems, and psychology often has missing rates exceeding 30–80%. The natural two-stage 'impute-then-detect' pipeline suffers from imputation bias: because the imputation model is trained exclusively on normal samples, it reconstructs missing values in abnormal samples in a way that makes them appear normal, systematically lowering recall. No prior end-to-end method jointly optimizes imputation and detection to address this distributional mismatch.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an incomplete data matrix X̌ ∈ R^{n×m} together with a binary mask M ∈ {0,1}^{n×m} indicating observed entries, where the missing mechanism (MCAR, MAR, or MNAR) may be known or unknown. Training uses only incomplete normal samples; at inference, a new incomplete sample x̌_new is scored via s(x̌_new) = ‖f_θ*(h_ψ*(x̌_new))‖, and the sample is flagged abnormal if s > r₁. The pseudo-abnormal samples used during training are generated internally from a learned latent distribution and require no external data.

## Method

ImAD defines a d-dimensional latent space Z in which normal data occupies a truncated Gaussian hyperball of radius r₁ and pseudo-abnormal data occupies a truncated Gaussian hypershell between radii r₁ and r₂. Three shared-parameter MLPs—an imputer h_ψ, a projector f_θ, and a reconstructor g_φ—are trained jointly under a unified objective combining (i) a masked L² imputation loss L^(DI), (ii) an anomaly-detection loss L^(AD) using Sinkhorn optimal-transport distance for normal data and L² distance for pseudo-abnormal data, and (iii) a masked reconstruction loss L^(RE). Pseudo-abnormal samples are generated by sampling from the hypershell, mapping through g_φ to data space, applying a missing mask, and imputing, which forces the imputer to generalize to abnormal patterns and mitigates the bias. Theoretical guarantees (Theorems 3.2–3.4) bound false-alarm and miss probabilities via Rademacher complexity, showing that with sufficient sample size and moderate network depth, normal samples are classified correctly with probability ≥ 1−δ and pseudo-abnormal samples are detected with probability ≥ 1−δ̃.

## Results

On 11 real-world tabular datasets, ImAD achieves the best or near-best AUROC in almost all settings. On the KDD dataset (mr=0.2, MCAR), ImAD reaches 97.01% AUROC and 95.96% AUPRC, outperforming the strongest baseline (MissForest + I-Forest at 94.90% AUROC). On the Adult dataset (mr=0.2), ImAD achieves 76.51% AUROC versus the best baseline at 64.39% (MissForest + DPAD). On datasets with inherent missing values, ImAD attains 100% AUROC on Bladder (86.93% missing entries) and 96.62% AUROC on Seq2-Heart (88.51% missing entries), surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImAD is compared against 8 'impute-then-detect' baselines formed by pairing two imputation methods (MissForest, GAIN) plus naive Mean-Filling with four AD methods (I-Forest, Deep SVDD, NeutraL AD, DPAD). ImAD achieves the best AUROC on Adult (71.19% vs. 65.44% for GAIN+Deep SVDD), Speech (58.66% vs. 57.24% for GAIN+NeutraL AD), and all four inherent-missing-value datasets (e.g., 100% on Bladder vs. 99.99% for Zero-Filling+DPAD). However, on KDD at mr=0.5, MissForest+I-Forest (93.24%) slightly edges out ImAD (90.78%), and on Arrhythmia at mr=0.5, MissForest+DPAD (82.64%) marginally exceeds ImAD (81.76%). A notable finding is that the simpler MissForest imputer often outperforms the deeper GAIN imputer for downstream AD (e.g., 93.24% vs. 79.94% on KDD+I-Forest), because GAIN's superior recovery ability amplifies the imputation bias by making abnormal samples appear more normal. Additionally, several baselines show counterintuitive performance *improvement* at higher missing rates (mr=0.5 vs. mr=0.2), which the authors attribute to the imputation bias being less pronounced when fewer values are available to 'normalize' abnormal samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established 'impute-then-detect' paradigm for anomaly detection with missing values (Zemicheal & Dietterich 2019, Fan et al. 2022, Sarda et al. 2023), where imputation and detection are treated as separate sequential stages. It draws on the rich literature of missing data imputation (MissForest, GAIN, matrix completion) and deep anomaly detection (Deep SVDD, NeutraL AD, DPAD), but repurposes these components within a unified end-to-end optimization that jointly trains an imputer, a projector, and a reconstructor. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of pseudo-abnormal samples generated in a latent space via truncated Gaussian distributions, which mitigates the distribution shift between normal training data and abnormal test data that plagues two-stage methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that more powerful imputation (GAIN) can actually degrade anomaly detection performance compared to simpler methods (MissForest) challenges the common assumption that better imputation always benefits downstream tasks, and provides a clear diagnostic for practitioners working with incomplete data. The theoretical guarantees (Theorems 3.2–3.4) offering high-probability detection correctness are valuable for safety-critical applications. A key limitation is that the method is restricted to tabular data; the authors explicitly note that applications to incomplete images and time series remain unexplored, which limits immediate applicability to many high-profile domains.
