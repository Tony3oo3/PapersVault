## Problem

Standard Transformers suffer from quadratic computational scaling in sequence length, require fixed-length and regularly sampled inputs, and lack native continuous-time representations, making them poorly suited for long, irregularly sampled time-series. Neural ODE/CDE-based models handle continuous time but incur prohibitive computational costs for long sequences. The paper addresses the gap of efficiently modelling long-range dependencies in irregularly sampled, variable-length time-series without expensive numerical ODE solvers or quadratic attention costs.

## Input / Output

The method accepts multivariate time-series of arbitrary length and irregular sampling times (e.g., 4000-step ECG signals at 125 Hz or 18k-step EigenWorms sequences), which are first converted to continuous-time paths via piecewise-linear interpolation. The multi-view signature transform compresses the sequence from L timesteps to L̄ signature windows (L̄ << L), and the model outputs a class label (classification) or a scalar regression value (e.g., heart rate). Because signatures are invariant to time reparameterization, the model handles dropped or irregularly spaced data points without retraining or interpolation heuristics.

## Method

The Rough Transformer lifts discrete input to a continuous-time path and computes a multi-view signature transform M(X)_k = (S(X̃)_{0,t_k}, S(X̃)_{t_{k-1},t_k}), concatenating a global signature (capturing long-term dependencies from the start of the sequence) with a local signature (acting as a sampling-rate-invariant convolutional filter over adjacent windows). Standard multi-head self-attention is then applied to these truncated signature vectors rather than raw timesteps, reducing the attention complexity from O(L²d) to O(L̄²d) where L̄ is the number of signature windows. The path signature's time-reparameterization invariance (Proposition 3.1) guarantees robustness to irregular sampling, and the factorial decay of signature terms (Proposition A.3) means only a few levels (n ≤ 6) are needed for accurate approximation. Signatures of piecewise-linear paths have closed-form expressions (Eq. 16) and can be precomputed offline, eliminating backpropagation through the signature calculation and enabling up to 26× speedups over vanilla Transformers.

## Results

On the Heart Rate regression task, RFormer achieves 2.66±0.21 RMSE, a 67% improvement over the vanilla Transformer (8.24±2.24) and outperforming Neural-RDE (2.97±0.45). On the six long time-series classification datasets from the UEA archive, RFormer attains a 64.5% average accuracy, surpassing the Transformer (59.0%), Mamba (62.0%), and LogNCDE (64.3%), while achieving 90.3% on EigenWorms where the vanilla Transformer runs out of memory. Training speedups of 1.4× to 26.11× over the vanilla Transformer are observed, with RFormer maintaining near-constant epoch time (~0.6 s) even at L=10K when signatures are precomputed offline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RFormer clearly outperforms the vanilla Transformer on the Heart Rate task (2.66 vs 8.24 RMSE) and on the long time-series classification average (64.5% vs 59.0%), while also being up to 26× faster in wall-clock training time. Against continuous-time ODE-based baselines, RFormer beats Neural-CDE (9.82 RMSE on HR) and Neural-RDE (2.97 RMSE on HR) on the regression task, and surpasses Neural-RDE (60.2%) and Neural-CDE (58.6%) on the long time-series classification average. RFormer edges out LogNCDE (64.3%) by a narrow margin on the classification average and decisively outperforms state-space models Mamba (58.6%), S5 (61.8%), and LRU (61.7%). ContiFormer, the closest continuous-time Transformer analogue, fails to run within GPU memory on most datasets (OOM) and is orders of magnitude slower (O(L²d²S) complexity), whereas RFormer scales gracefully to L=250K. On irregular sampling (50% random drop), RFormer retains 87.7% accuracy on EigenWorms and 93.2% on Long Sinusoidal, while the vanilla Transformer drops to OOM and 20.2% respectively, and CRU degrades to 34–48% on synthetic irregular tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two lines of work: path signatures from Rough Path Theory (Lyons et al., 2007), which have been used as feature maps in Neural CDEs (Kidger et al., 2020) and Neural RDEs (Morrill et al., 2021), and the Transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017), which dominates discrete sequence modelling. The key departure is from both: unlike Neural CDEs/RDEs, RFormer avoids expensive ODE solvers by performing attention directly on signature representations; unlike ContiFormer (Chen et al., 2023), it achieves continuous-time invariance through the signature's time-reparameterization invariance rather than through a learned continuous-time attention kernel. The multi-view signature transform (global + local components) is a novel architectural contribution that compresses input sequences while preserving both long-range and local structure, positioning RFormer as a lightweight alternative to both ODE-based continuous-time models and vanilla Transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for domains with long, high-frequency, irregularly sampled time series (finance, healthcare, sensor data), where vanilla Transformers hit memory walls and ODE-based models are prohibitively slow. The 26× speedup on the Heart Rate dataset and the ability to process 250K-length sequences with near-constant epoch time (offline signatures) open up regimes previously inaccessible to Transformer-based methods. However, the authors acknowledge heavy dependence on hyperparameter tuning (window size, signature level), limited experimental scale, and dimensionality challenges for high-dimensional inputs. Open questions include whether the approach combines well with other efficient attention variants (linear, sparse), whether NAS can automate the signature hyperparameter search, and whether the spatial-processing benefits generalize to truly high-dimensional multivariate settings.
