## Problem

Fairness notions in clustering—proportional fairness, individual fairness, and the transferable core—have been developed independently and studied in parallel, with their interconnections acknowledged but never formalized. This paper bridges these notions by importing proportionality axioms from approval-based multiwinner voting in computational social choice, showing that a single axiomatic guarantee (metric justified representation) simultaneously yields the best known approximations to all three clustering fairness notions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243533/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a set of n agents and a (possibly infinite) set of candidate centers in a (pseudo-)metric space, along with a target number k of clusters. The output is a subset W of at most k centers guaranteed to satisfy mJR or mPJR, which in turn implies proportional fairness, individual fairness, transferable core, or q-core bounds. A key structural constraint is that individual fairness requires N ⊆ C (every agent can serve as a center), while proportional fairness and the q-core work with arbitrary candidate sets, including unbounded Euclidean spaces where the algorithm must be restricted to the agent set.

## Method

The core technique is lifting the justified representation (JR) and proportional justified representation (PJR) axioms from approval-based multiwinner voting to metric spaces, producing mJR and mPJR. For mJR, the paper proves that any satisfying outcome simultaneously achieves (1+√2)-proportional fairness, 2-individual fairness, and an improved (γ, 2γ/(γ−1))-transferable core bound, all via the polynomial-time GREEDY CAPTURE algorithm. For mPJR, satisfied by SPATIAL EXPANDING APPROVALS, the paper shows a 5-q-core guarantee for all q ≤ k simultaneously, improving upon prior bounds from Ebadian and Micha. The paper also establishes tight equivalences between proportional and individual fairness (α-PF ⟹ (1+α)-IF and β-IF ⟹ 2β-PF), and handles unbounded candidate spaces by showing that restricting the proportionality check to the agent set suffices for constant-factor approximations to proportional fairness and the q-core.

## Results

The paper establishes that any clustering satisfying the metric JR axiom (mJR) simultaneously achieves the best known approximations to proportional fairness ((1+√2)-PF), individual fairness (2-IF), and the transferable core ((γ, 2γ/(γ-1))-TC), all via the polynomial-time GREEDY CAPTURE algorithm. For the stronger mPJR axiom (equivalent to DPRF), the paper proves a 5-q-core guarantee for all q ≤ k simultaneously, improving upon Ebadian and Micha's (5+√41)/2 ≈ 5.70-q-core bound. In the sortition setting (N=C), FAIR GREEDY CAPTURE is shown to satisfy the (3+√17)/2 ≈ 3.56-q-core, a strict improvement over the prior 5.70 bound. For unbounded candidate spaces, restricting mJR/mPJR to the agent set yields 3-PF and 4-q-core guarantees respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are between approximation guarantees. The paper's central contribution is showing that mJR (a weaker axiom than mPJR/DPRF) already implies the (1+√2)-PF bound previously attributed to DPRF by Aziz et al. [2024], and the 2-IF bound from Jung et al. [2020], unifying three previously parallel fairness notions. For the transferable core, the mJR-based bound of (γ, 2γ/(γ−1)) improves upon the (γ, max(4, (3γ−1)/(γ−1))) bound from Li et al. [2021] for γ > 2. For the q-core in sortition, the paper improves Ebadian and Micha's (5+√41)/2 ≈ 5.70 bound to (3+√17)/2 ≈ 3.56 for all q ≤ k via a simpler proof. The tightness results (Theorem 2, Theorem 4) confirm that the α-PF ↔ (1+α)-IF and α-PF ↔ transferable core connections cannot be improved asymptotically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper lifts the proportionality axioms JR and PJR from approval-based multiwinner voting (Brill and Peters, 2023) into metric clustering spaces, creating mJR and mPJR as unifying frameworks. It builds directly on Chen et al.'s (2019) proportional fairness and GREEDY CAPTURE algorithm, Aziz et al.'s (2024) DPRF/SPATIAL EXPANDING APPROVALS, and Ebadian and Micha's (2024) q-core for sortition, showing that these previously parallel fairness notions (proportional fairness, individual fairness, transferable core) are tightly interrelated up to small constant factors. The key departure from prior work is the axiomatic lens: rather than analyzing each fairness notion in isolation, the paper demonstrates that a single proportionality axiom (mJR) simultaneously yields the best known approximations to all three, and that mPJR improves the q-core bound for sortition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a gap explicitly called out by Dickerson et al. (2023b) in fair clustering: the lack of formalized interactions between previously introduced fairness notions. By showing that proportional and individual fairness are within a factor of 2 of each other (with tight bounds), it means that incompatibility results or bi-criteria approximations for one notion automatically transfer to the other, simplifying the landscape for future work. Open questions remain on whether the approximation factors (1+√2 for proportional fairness, 5 for the q-core) are tight, whether the individual fairness bound of 2 can be improved in Euclidean spaces, and whether stronger axioms like mPJR+ yield better core approximations. The unbounded candidate space case (e.g., Euclidean clustering) remains algorithmically challenging, with the paper only achieving constant-factor approximations via restriction to the agent set.
