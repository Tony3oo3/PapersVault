## Problem

Channel-independent time series forecasters are robust to distribution drift but ignore inter-channel correlations, while attention- or mixer-based methods that capture these correlations introduce quadratic complexity in the number of channels and remain fragile when individual channels are noisy or anomalous. The paper addresses how to capture channel interactions with linear complexity and improved robustness to channel-level anomalies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242045/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{C×L} (C channels, lookback window L) and produces a forecast Y ∈ R^{C×H} (H future time steps). Rather than patching the temporal dimension, SOFTS applies series embedding—treating the entire lookback window as a single token per channel via a linear projection—so no extra temporal dimension is introduced. Reversible instance normalization is applied before embedding and inverted after prediction, and the final output is produced by a single linear layer mapping the hidden dimension d to the horizon H.

## Method

SOFTS is an MLP-based forecaster whose core component is the STar Aggregate-Redistribute (STAR) module, which replaces pairwise channel interactions (as in attention or GNNs) with a centralized hub-and-spoke structure. Each STAR layer first projects all C channel embeddings through a shared MLP and aggregates them into a single global 'core' vector via stochastic pooling (multinomial sampling during training, softmax-weighted averaging at test time), inspired by the DeepSets permutation-invariant form. The core is then concatenated with every individual channel embedding and fused back to the original hidden dimension by a second MLP with a residual connection, enabling indirect channel-to-channel information exchange without O(C²) pairwise comparisons. The full pipeline is: reversible instance normalization → series embedding → N stacked STAR layers → linear predictor, yielding overall complexity O(CL + CH) that is linear in both the number of channels and the lookback/forecast lengths. The authors further demonstrate that STAR can be dropped into existing Transformer-based forecasters (PatchTST, Crossformer, iTransformer) as a replacement for their attention modules, maintaining or improving accuracy at lower cost.

## Results

SOFTS achieves the best or second-best MSE and MAE across all 12 benchmark datasets (ETT, ECL, Traffic, Weather, Solar-Energy, PEMS). On the high-channel Traffic dataset (862 channels), SOFTS reduces average MSE from 0.428 (iTransformer) to 0.409, a 4.4% improvement. On PEMS07 (883 channels), the improvement is even more pronounced: MSE drops from 0.101 to 0.087, a 13.9% relative reduction. The model achieves all results with only linear complexity O(CL+CH) in both the number of channels and lookback window length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOFTS outperforms all 10 baselines on every dataset for both MSE and MAE. The most significant gains are on high-channel datasets where the linear-complexity advantage is most relevant: on PEMS07 (883 channels), SOFTS achieves 0.087 MSE versus iTransformer's 0.101 (13.9% reduction) and PatchTST's 0.144 (39.6% reduction); on Traffic (862 channels), it reaches 0.409 versus iTransformer's 0.428 and PatchTST's 0.454. Among the closest competitors, iTransformer is consistently the strongest baseline, with SOFTS improving upon it by 1–14% in MSE depending on the dataset. MLP-based baselines (TSMixer, DLinear) and CNN-based baselines (TimesNet, SCINet) are substantially outperformed, especially on large-channel datasets where their channel-independent or local-receptive-field strategies struggle. Transformer-based baselines like Crossformer and FEDformer show the largest gaps on PEMS and Traffic, confirming that the centralized STAR aggregation is particularly effective when the number of channels is large.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOFTS sits at the intersection of two competing paradigms in multivariate time series forecasting: channel independence (PatchTST, DLinear), which provides robustness to distribution drift but ignores inter-channel correlations, and attention-based channel mixing (iTransformer, Crossformer), which captures correlations but incurs quadratic complexity and fragility to anomalous channels. Rather than following the distributed pairwise-interaction pattern of attention, GNN, or mixer modules, SOFTS introduces a centralized 'star' architecture inspired by server-client systems in software engineering, drawing on DeepSets and Kolmogorov-Arnold representation for the core aggregation and stochastic pooling for the pooling operation. The model is purely MLP-based, departing from the Transformer lineage that dominates recent SOTA, while retaining the series-embedding and reversible instance normalization conventions established by PatchTST and iTransformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving SOTA-level accuracy with strictly linear complexity in both the number of channels and lookback length, making large-scale forecasting (800+ channels) feasible on modest hardware where Transformer-based methods become prohibitive. The STAR module's demonstrated universality as a drop-in replacement for attention in PatchTST, iTransformer, and Crossformer suggests it could be adopted broadly across the time-series forecasting literature. Open questions include whether the centralized aggregation generalizes beyond forecasting (e.g., to anomaly detection or imputation), whether more sophisticated core representations could further improve performance, and how the method behaves under severe non-stationarity where even the aggregated core may be unreliable.
