## Problem

Current state-of-the-art deepfake detectors trained on manually-blended data (blendfake) deliberately exclude actual deepfake data because vanilla hybrid training (VHT) combining both performs worse than blendfake-only training. This paper argues the issue is not the absence of useful information in deepfake data but rather the unorganized latent-space distribution that entangles blendfake and deepfake representations, and asks whether a properly organized latent space can make both data types complementary rather than conflicting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240691/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes single 256×256 RGB face frames as input, with training mini-batches containing four aligned samples from the same video frame: a real image, a self-blended image (SBI), a cross-blended image (CBI), and a deepfake. The output is a binary real/fake detection score produced by a final classifier operating on the element-wise product of backbone features and a projected auxiliary attention map derived from the OPR module's multi-attribute predictions.

## Method

ProDet organizes the latent space as a progressive transition from real to SBI to CBI to deepfake, treating each as an oriented pivot anchor with cumulative forgery attributes (blending clue, identity inconsistency, generative artifacts). The Oriented Progressive Regularizer (OPR) enforces this ordering via triplet binary classification heads whose labels form a cumulative three-bit code, and projects the attribute predictions into an auxiliary attention map that modulates backbone features for final detection. Feature Bridging performs mixup between adjacent anchor features and their labels to fill the continuous space between discrete anchors, while a Transition Loss constrains noise-augmented features to map toward the next anchor distribution, further reinforcing the progressive structure. The overall loss combines detection loss, oriented loss (applied to both anchors and bridged features), and transition loss with trade-off parameters β=1 and γ=10.

## Results

ProDet achieves a cross-dataset average AUC of 0.8225, outperforming the previous best (SBI at 0.7814) by 5.26% on C-Avg. The largest cross-dataset gains are on Celeb-DF-v1 (0.9094 AUC, +9.42% over SBI) and Celeb-DF-v2 (0.8448 AUC, +5.40%). On the in-dataset FF++ benchmark, ProDet scores 0.9591 AUC, slightly below the top in-dataset methods (IID: 0.9743, UCF: 0.9705), but its cross-dataset generalization is substantially superior to all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProDet outperforms all 18 frame-level baselines on every cross-dataset metric (CDFv1, CDFv2, DFDCP, DFDC, and C-Avg), with the most significant margin over the previous best cross-dataset method SBI (0.8225 vs. 0.7814 C-Avg, +5.26%). Against the strongest in-dataset competitors IID (0.9743) and UCF (0.9705) on FF++, ProDet trails by 1.5% and 1.1% AUC respectively, but reverses the gap decisively on cross-dataset evaluation where IID drops to 0.7462 and UCF to 0.7526 C-Avg. Compared to the vanilla hybrid training (VHT) baseline that naively combines deepfake and blendfake, ProDet improves C-Avg from 0.7811 to 0.8225 (+5.3%), confirming that the progressive latent-space organization is the key differentiator rather than simply adding more data. Against the blendfake-only baseline (BF-only, C-Avg 0.8070), ProDet gains +1.9% C-Avg, demonstrating that deepfake data is indeed valuable when properly organized. In the broader evaluation on nine additional datasets (Table 7), ProDet consistently achieves the highest AUC across all nine, including the latest 2024 datasets (DiffSwap, UniFace, E4S, BlendFace, MobileSwap), further validating its generalization advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the mainstream blendfake-only training paradigm (SBI, CBI/X-ray) that deliberately excludes deepfake data due to the empirically observed '1+1<2' degradation in vanilla hybrid training. Rather than proposing a new data synthesis method or backbone architecture, it repurposes existing blendfake generators (SBI, CBI) and introduces a training-time framework—Oriented Progressive Regularizer (OPR) with triplet binary attribute classification, feature bridging via mixup, and a noise-driven transition loss—to organize the latent space along a real→SBI→CBI→deepfake progressive axis. Conceptually, it draws on inductive-bias literature for latent space structure but applies it specifically to the forgery-accumulation ordering of deepfake detection anchors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing practical dilemma in deepfake detection: whether deepfake data is useful or harmful for training, and provides a principled answer (it is useful if the latent space is organized progressively). The 5.26% cross-dataset AUC improvement over the previous best (SBI) is substantial and validated across four cross-dataset benchmarks plus nine additional datasets in the appendix. A key open question the authors acknowledge is whether the real→blendfake→deepfake ordering remains valid as deepfake generation quality approaches or surpasses blendfake realism, which could invalidate the progressive assumption. The method is also limited to frame-level detection with a single backbone (EfficientNetB4), leaving open whether the progressive organization generalizes to video-level or transformer-based detectors.
