## Problem

State-of-the-art LLMs provide little to no transparency about their pretraining data composition, curation strategies, or filtering heuristics, making it impossible for the community to reproduce, audit, or build upon these datasets. The paper identifies three compounding gaps: lack of documented data curation processes, limited access to large-scale high-quality corpora, and absence of per-document metadata that would enable principled filtering research. RedPajama addresses all three by releasing over 100 trillion tokens of open data with full provenance and quality annotations.

## Input / Output

RedPajama-V1 ingests seven heterogeneous sources (CommonCrawl via CCNet, C4, GitHub, Wikipedia, PG19/Books3, ArXiv LaTeX, Stack Exchange) and produces a 1.2T-token composite corpus in JSON Lines format with per-source metadata fields. RedPajama-V2 processes 84 CommonCrawl snapshots (2014–2023) through CCNet, retaining all perplexity buckets across five languages, and outputs 113B documents as Gzip-compressed JSONL shards (5000 per snapshot) alongside three parallel artifact streams: quality signals (JSONL with start/end/score tuples per document), exact-duplicate IDs (parquet, via Bloom filter at 1% error rate), and MinHash LSH signatures (parquet, at Jaccard thresholds 0.7–1.0). The trained models (RedPajama-INCITE 3B/7B and ablation models at 468M/1.6B) are released as decoder-only Llama-2 checkpoints.

## Method

Rather than prescribing a single filtered corpus, RedPajama-V2 retains the entire raw web text and attaches 46 per-document quality signals spanning five categories: natural-language heuristics (all-caps fraction, unique-word fraction, terminal punctuation), repetitiveness (top-n-gram character fractions for n=2–10), content-based toxicity (LDNOOBW blocklist counts, UT1 domain blacklist), ML-based similarity (fastText unigram classifiers trained against Wikipedia/books/OpenWebText, and DSIR log-likelihood-ratio importance weights), and deduplication (MinHash signatures and Bloom-filter exact duplicates). This design makes previously published filtered datasets (C4, Gopher, RefinedWeb) recoverable as special cases of the same corpus. Ablation studies train 468M and 1.6B Llama-2 decoder-only models (24 layers, 16 heads, 2048 context) on 100B and 350B tokens respectively, using the OLMo framework with FSDP on up to 5 H100 nodes, to demonstrate that progressively combining fuzzy deduplication, Gopher rules, and ML classifiers yields models that match or exceed RefinedWeb across 14 NLP benchmarks. The RedPajama-INCITE 3B/7B models were trained on the Summit supercomputer (V100 GPUs, IBM Power9, FP16 with loss scaling, 512/256 nodes) to validate the V1 corpus against the original LLaMA recipe.

## Results

For the 468M-parameter ablation models, RPv2 filtered with fuzzy deduplication and full Gopher rules achieves the best aggregated benchmark score among all RPv2 variants (Avg 37.6, Norm.Avg 0.160, Rank-Score 0.700), surpassing C4 (35.8), Dolma (36.0), FineWeb (36.5), and RPv1-CC (35.6). The Rank-Score of 0.700 exceeds RefinedWeb (0.650), indicating broader task coverage, though RefinedWeb retains a slight edge on average accuracy (37.9 vs. 37.6). For the 1.6B model, RPv2 with full Gopher + fuzzy dedup + Palm classifier reaches Avg 50.0 and Rank-Score 0.106, approaching RefinedWeb's 52.0 and 0.139. The RedPajama-INCITE-3B model outperforms GPT-Neo and Pythia-2.8B by 3–5 points on HELM, while the 7B Instruct variant leads all comparable open 7B models by 2–8 points on HELM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RefinedWeb (the strongest web-only baseline), RPv2 with fuzzy dedup + full Gopher rules trades a small average-accuracy deficit (37.6 vs. 37.9) for a substantially higher Rank-Score (0.700 vs. 0.650), meaning it performs more consistently across the full benchmark suite; RefinedWeb is specifically weaker on Hellaswag, LAMBADA, Winogrande, MMLU, and OpenBookQA. RPv2+Gopher+fuzzy dedup clearly outperforms FineWeb (36.5/0.146/0.644), C4 (35.8/0.140/0.472), Dolma-v1.7 CC (36.0/0.140/0.511), and RPv1-CC (35.6/0.127/0.461) on all three aggregate metrics. For the 1.6B model, RPv2 with full Gopher + fuzzy dedup + Palm classifier (Avg 50.0, Rank-Score 0.106) narrows the gap to RefinedWeb (52.0, 0.139) to within 2 points on average accuracy. On the INCITE model side, the 3B base model beats GPT-Neo (0.3570) and Pythia-2.8B (0.3770) on HELM by 3–5 points (0.4060), while the 7B base model trails Llama-7B by 4.1 points and Falcon-7B by 1.0 point on HELM, attributed to FP16 training constraints and dataset reconstruction uncertainties. The 7B Instruct model, however, surpasses Llama-7B, Falcon-7B, and MPT-7B (both base and instruct) on HELM by 2–8 points, reaching a HELM-AVG of 0.492.

## Relation to Prior Work

RedPajama-V1 is a direct best-effort reproduction of the LLaMA-1 training corpus (Touvron et al., 2023), following the CCNet pipeline (Wenzek et al., 2019) for CommonCrawl processing and incorporating Gopher-style quality heuristics (Rae et al., 2021). RedPajama-V2 departs conceptually from prior web-only datasets like C4, RefinedWeb, and FineWeb, which prescribe specific filtering rules and discard low-quality content, by instead retaining the full raw corpus and attaching 46 per-document quality signals (including DSIR importance weights from Xie et al., 2023, fastText classifiers, and MinHash signatures from Broder, 1997) as metadata. This positions RedPajama-V2 as a superset from which C4, Gopher-filtered, and RefinedWeb-style datasets can be derived as special cases, shifting the curation decision to downstream users.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With over 100 trillion tokens and already underpinning production models (Snowflake Arctic, Salesforce XGen, AI2 OLMo), RedPajama has become a foundational resource for open-source LLM development, addressing the transparency gap that has hindered reproducible pretraining research. The ablation studies, while conducted at modest scale (468M–1.6B parameters), demonstrate that the quality signals enable meaningful differentiation in downstream performance, though the authors acknowledge that larger-scale explorations are needed to confirm these findings at production model sizes. Key open questions include optimal multilingual filtering strategies, the absence of decontamination and PII analyses, and whether the small-scale ablation rankings hold at 7B+ parameter scales where data efficiency dynamics may shift.
