## Problem

MoE models offer a path to scaling model capacity without proportional FLOPs increases, but they must be trained from scratch with random initialization, demanding extensive data and compute that limits adoption to well-resourced teams. Unlike dense models, which benefit from a vast ecosystem of pre-trained checkpoints, MoE models lack comparable initialization resources. The paper asks whether dense checkpoints can be repurposed to initialize MoE models, accelerating convergence and improving accuracy without the cost of full MoE pre-training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained dense checkpoints (e.g., ViT-S/16 or ConvNeXt-T from ImageNet-21K) as initialization weights and fine-tunes the resulting MoE model on downstream RGB images at 224×224 resolution. The output is a standard class label over the target dataset's categories. A key constraint is that the resulting MoE model maintains approximately the same FLOPs as the original dense model (e.g., 1.1G for ViT-T) while carrying substantially more total parameters (e.g., 265M vs. 6M), since only a subset of experts is activated per token.

## Method

MoE Jetpack combines two techniques. First, Checkpoint Recycling samples weights from the dense checkpoint's MLPs to construct diverse MoE experts, using importance-based sampling (selecting top feature dimensions by mean output activation and hidden units by activation magnitude) as the default strategy, with alternatives including co-activation graph partitioning, uniform selection, and random sampling. Second, the SpheroMoE layer replaces standard Soft MoE routing with a cross-attention mechanism where input tokens are projected as keys and randomly initialized queries are L2-normalized into hyperspherical space, producing cosine-similarity-based dispatch and combine logits. Expert regularization is applied via learnable softmax temperatures (initialized high to prevent early specialization) and stochastic expert dropout. An adaptive dual-path structure routes high-importance tokens to fewer, larger core experts and less critical tokens to more, smaller universal experts (one-fourth the parameters), optimizing the compute-accuracy tradeoff.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, MoE Jetpack achieves 79.9% top-1 accuracy with ViT-T (+2.8% over Soft MoE) and 80.5% with ConvNeXt-F (+1.4% over Soft MoE), while maintaining ~1.1G FLOPs. On smaller datasets, gains are substantially larger: 88.4% on CIFAR-100 (+12.5%), 95.3% on STL-10 (+27.6%), and 84.3% on Pets (+38.8%) with ViT-T. Convergence is accelerated by 2× on ImageNet (reaching ~77% in 150 epochs vs. ~300) and 8× on CIFAR-100 (reaching 76% in ~40 epochs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoE Jetpack outperforms Soft MoE [6] (trained from scratch) on all 8 datasets for both ViT-T and ConvNeXt-F architectures, with the largest margins on small datasets (e.g., +38.8% on Pets, +27.6% on STL-10 with ViT-T). Against dense ViT [7] trained from scratch, MoE Jetpack wins decisively on every dataset; even the ImageNet-21K-pretrained dense ViT (75.6%) is surpassed by MoE Jetpack (79.9%). Similarly, MoE Jetpack beats dense ConvNeXt-F [8] on all datasets, including the ImageNet-21K-initialized variant (80.5% vs. 76.4%). Compared to Sparse Upcycling [16], which simply duplicates MLPs to form experts, MoE Jetpack's Importance-Based Checkpoint Recycling achieves 79.9% vs. 79.1% on ImageNet while offering greater flexibility in expert sizing. The consistent advantage over all baselines is attributed to the combination of pre-trained knowledge transfer via checkpoint recycling and the SpheroMoE layer's hyperspherical routing and dual-path expert structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoE Jetpack builds directly on the Soft MoE architecture [Puigcerver et al., ICLR 2023], repurposing its slot-based expert processing mechanism but replacing the learned projection matrix with a cross-attention routing scheme in hyperspherical space. It departs from Sparse Upcycling [Komatsuzaki et al., ICLR 2022], which simply duplicates MLPs to form experts, by introducing 'checkpoint recycling'—sampling diverse weights from pre-trained dense checkpoints using importance-based, graph-partitioning, or uniform strategies inspired by Weight Selection [Xu et al., ICLR 2024]. The dual-path structure (core + universal experts) and the learnable softmax temperatures with expert dropout represent architectural innovations not present in prior MoE formulations like Switch Transformers or Soft MoE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in dramatically lowering the barrier to MoE adoption: by recycling freely available dense checkpoints (e.g., from Timm or Hugging Face), practitioners can obtain competitive MoE models without the multi-week, multi-GPU pre-training that has restricted MoE research to well-resourced labs. The 2× convergence speedup on ImageNet and 8× on CIFAR-100, combined with accuracy gains of up to 38.8% over from-scratch baselines on small datasets, make this a compelling efficiency story. However, the approach is currently limited to vision classification, relies on the quality of the source dense checkpoint, and has not been validated on NLP or other modalities. Open questions include whether the technique generalizes to self-supervised or foundation-model checkpoints, whether the dual-path ratio and layer placement findings transfer to other architectures, and whether the modest 2.8% ImageNet gain over Soft MoE from scratch justifies the added architectural complexity at scale.
