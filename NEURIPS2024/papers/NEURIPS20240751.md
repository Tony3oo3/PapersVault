## Problem

Existing score matching (SM) estimators for point processes are shown to be incomplete: the regularity conditions required to convert the explicit SM objective into a tractable implicit form via integration by parts cannot be satisfied for general point processes, because the random variable has random dimension and lives in a bounded simplex. As a result, the implicit SM objectives used in prior works (Sahani et al. 2016, Zhang et al. 2023, Li et al. 2023) contain missing boundary terms that depend on model parameters, causing them to fail for most practical point process models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observed event timestamp sequences T = (t_1, …, t_{N_T}) on a fixed window [0, T], where N_T is random; for multivariate Hawkes processes it additionally receives event types k_n ∈ {1,…,K}. It outputs parameter estimates θ for the intensity function (or a trained deep point process model). A practical constraint is that the observation endpoint T must be known or approximated to construct the weight function; when T is unavailable (as in real datasets with heterogeneous sequence lengths), the paper applies batch-level truncation to enforce a common horizon.

## Method

The paper introduces Weighted Score Matching (WSM) for Poisson processes and Autoregressive Weighted Score Matching (AWSM) for Hawkes processes. The key mechanism is multiplying the SM loss by a weight function h_n(T) that vanishes at the boundaries of each t_n's support [t_{n-1}, T], which eliminates the θ-dependent boundary terms that invalidate the standard implicit SM. For Hawkes processes, the autoregressive structure is handled by applying the weighted objective to each conditional p(t_n | F_{t_{n-1}}) separately, and for multivariate cases a cross-entropy term for event types is added. The near-optimal weight h_n^0(t_n) = (T − t_{n-1})/2 − |t_n − (T + t_{n-1})/2| is proven to maximize the separation constant in the convergence bound. The paper establishes consistency and a non-asymptotic rate of O(r/M)^{1/(α−1)} under mild regularity assumptions, and validates the estimator on both parametric and deep (SAHP, THP) point process models.

## Results

On synthetic data (Table 1), (A)WSM achieves MAE of 0.07 on Poisson and 0.011–0.153 on Hawkes parameters, closely matching MLE (0.02–0.10), while implicit (A)SM fails catastrophically with MAE of 1.56–2.51. On real data (Table 2), AWSM-trained SAHP and THP models achieve TLL within ~0.1–0.3 of MLE across all four datasets (e.g., AWSM TLL = 1.703 vs. MLE 1.542 on Half-Sin SAHP; AWSM TLL = -0.948 vs. MLE -1.052 on Taobao THP), while DSM lags by 0.5–1.6 TLL points. The key advantage is computational: AWSM avoids the intensity integral entirely, achieving comparable accuracy to MLE with far fewer integration nodes (Figure 1d).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines: implicit (A)SM [Sahani 2016; Zhang 2023; Li 2023], MLE, and Denoising Score Matching (DSM) [Li 2023]. Implicit (A)SM is shown to be fundamentally broken for general point processes: its MAE is 10–50× larger than (A)WSM on all synthetic models (e.g., 1.600 vs. 0.041 on Exp-Hawkes α₁₁; 2.507 vs. 0.060 on Gau-Hawkes σ), and it fails so completely on real data that its results are not even reported in Table 2. (A)WSM matches MLE closely on both synthetic data (MAE within 0.01–0.05) and real data (TLL within ~0.1–0.3), confirming the theoretical consistency guarantee. DSM is consistently inferior to both MLE and AWSM on real data, with TLL gaps of 0.5–1.6 points (e.g., -0.385 vs. 1.271 on Half-Sin THP), attributed to its biased estimation of the score matching objective when noise variance σ > 0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Hyvärinen's score matching framework [5] and its weighted extensions [6, 22] to the point process setting, where the random dimensionality of event sequences introduces boundary terms that invalidate the standard integration-by-parts trick. It builds on autoregressive score matching [13] to handle the conditional structure of Hawkes processes, but critically departs from prior point-process SM work [18, 24, 10] by proving their implicit objectives are incomplete: the regularity conditions required to eliminate the unknown data distribution fail for general point processes because the support is a union of variable-dimensional simplices. The proposed (A)WSM estimator introduces a weight function vanishing at the integration boundary to recover a valid implicit objective, a technique borrowed from bounded-support score matching but applied here for the first time to a stochastic process with random dimension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a silent correctness issue in the point process literature: three published score matching estimators that were presented as integration-free alternatives to MLE are shown to be inconsistent for general Poisson and Hawkes processes. This matters practically because MLE for point processes requires numerical integration of the intensity, which suffers from the curse of dimensionality in high-dimensional or deep models; a provably consistent integration-free estimator is therefore valuable. A key limitation is that the weight function requires knowledge of the observation endpoint T, which is often unknown in real data, and the paper's remedy (batch truncation or approximation) may degrade performance. Open questions include whether the convergence rate can be improved with data-adaptive weights and whether the framework extends naturally to multivariate or spatially-indexed point processes with unknown observation windows.
