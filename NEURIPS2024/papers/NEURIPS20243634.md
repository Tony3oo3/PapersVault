## Problem

Existing template-free dynamic NeRF methods that learn forward LBS for arbitrary deformable entities rely on iterative Broyden root-finding, leading to lengthy training convergence, and none address interactions between multiple entities with semantically separable geometry. Template-based methods (e.g., SMPL) cannot generalize beyond humans, while generic dynamic NeRFs using latent codes fail to capture accurate articulation of deformable objects.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse or single-view RGB videos (45–50 frames per camera), 2D semantic segmentation masks, per-frame 3D skeleton poses (24 joints per entity), and camera poses. It outputs two independent canonical-space SDFs (one per entity), per-entity skinning weight fields, posed 3D meshes via forward LBS, and a composite RGB rendering. A key structural constraint is that the framework is designed for exactly two interacting entities (one deformable, one non-deformable), with separate network branches for each.

## Method

TFS-NeRF replaces the computationally expensive Broyden-based root-finding used in TAVA/SNARF with a RealNVP Invertible Neural Network that learns a bijective mapping from view space to canonical space, reducing per-frame cost from ~0.9 s to ~0.3 s. Semantic-aware ray sampling, guided by 2D masks and 3D skeleton distance encoding, disentangles the two entities so that each SDF prediction network receives only supervision from its own entity's observations. The INN is initialized with a nearest-joint one-hot approximation to accelerate convergence, and a separate skinning weight prediction network (3-layer MLP with softmax output) learns per-vertex bone weights at canonical space. Training jointly optimizes RGB reconstruction, pose consistency, one-hot skinning weight, INN cycle consistency, INN-to-LBS consistency, and shape losses, all end-to-end with a single Adam optimizer at lr 5e-4.

## Results

On the BEHAVE dataset for holistic scene reconstruction (human + object under interaction), TFS-NeRF achieves a Chamfer Distance of 2.431 cm, F-score of 91.34%, and Distance Accuracy of 2.721 cm, substantially outperforming the best baseline (NDR + ResFields) which scores 3.399 cm CD, 80.39% F-score, and 3.591 cm Dist. Acc. For semantic human reconstruction on BEHAVE, the method reaches 1.812 cm CD and 95.34% F-score, compared to 3.931 cm and 76.55% for the best competing method. On ZJU-MoCap for single-entity human reconstruction, TFS-NeRF achieves 2.24 cm CD and 93.97% F-score, surpassing TAVA (2.47 cm, 92.95%) and AnimatableNeRF (2.81 cm, 88.30%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFS-NeRF outperforms all six baselines across every metric on the BEHAVE scene reconstruction benchmark. Against the strongest generic-scene baselines (NDR, HyperNeRF, D-NeRF, both with and without ResFields), the method reduces Chamfer Distance by 0.97–4.76 cm and improves F-score by 10.9–45.0 percentage points, with the largest gap over D-NeRF without ResFields (46.30% F-score). Tensor4D achieves the highest recall (91.64%) among baselines but still trails TFS-NeRF by 2.21 points in F-score and 0.87 cm in Chamfer Distance. For single-entity human reconstruction on ZJU-MoCap, TFS-NeRF beats the template-free TAVA by 0.23 cm in Chamfer Distance and 1.02 points in F-score, and outperforms the SMPL-based AnimatableNeRF by 0.57 cm in Chamfer and 5.67 points in F-score. On the HO3D-V3 hand-object dataset, TFS-NeRF achieves 0.530 cm Dist. Acc. and 99.98% F-score for object reconstruction, surpassing NDR+ResFields (1.154 cm, 93.78%) and HyperNeRF+ResFields (1.159 cm, 97.99%). The method also matches or slightly exceeds TAVA on the synthetic animal dataset (0.71 vs. 0.73 cm Chamfer), confirming generalizability beyond humans.

## Relation to Prior Work

TFS-NeRF builds directly on TAVA [28], which first demonstrated template-free forward LBS learning for arbitrary deformable entities, but replaces TAVA's computationally expensive Broyden-based root-finding with a RealNVP invertible neural network for the view-to-canonical space mapping. It extends the single-entity focus of TAVA, NDR, HyperNeRF, and D-NeRF to two interacting entities by introducing semantic-aware ray sampling and per-entity SDF prediction networks, a design that departs from the shared-network compositional approaches used in static scene methods like ObjectSDF. The paper also positions itself against template-based human-object methods (HOSNeRF, AnimatableNeRF) that rely on SMPL, arguing that learned skinning weights generalize better to non-human deformable entities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making template-free multi-entity dynamic reconstruction both faster (0.3 vs 0.9 sec/frame) and semantically separable, which is essential for downstream AR/VR/HRI applications where individual object geometry must be independently accessible. However, the framework is limited to exactly two entities and requires accurate 3D skeleton poses as input, both of which constrain real-world applicability. Open questions include whether the per-entity network architecture can scale to more than two interacting objects (the authors suggest occlusion maps as a potential path) and whether the strong dependence on pose quality can be relaxed through end-to-end pose estimation.
