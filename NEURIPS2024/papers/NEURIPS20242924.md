## Problem

No prior work has rigorously quantified how much fine-tuning data is needed for UI control agents to reach a target performance level, or how that requirement changes when generalizing out of the training distribution. Additionally, it remains unclear whether fine-tuning can effectively handle high-level task decomposition (planning multi-step goals) versus low-level action grounding, and whether data scaling alone suffices for robust out-of-domain performance on complex tasks.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a natural language instruction (either a high-level goal like 'set an alarm for Saturday at 6am' or a low-level step command like 'set the hours to 6'), a flat JSON list of on-screen UI elements extracted from the Android accessibility tree (filtered to elements with non-empty text or critical types such as switch and edit, typically fewer than 50 per screen), a self-contained action history, and the active app name. It produces a single JSON action specifying the action type and its arguments: center coordinates for click/type/long_press, a direction string for scroll, an app name for open_app, or a goal_status for terminate. The input_text action is merged with its preceding click into a unified 'type' action to reduce latency, and a terminate action is artificially appended to every episode for training.

## Method

The paper introduces ANDROIDCONTROL, a dataset of 15,283 human demonstrations across 14,548 unique tasks and 833 Android apps, uniquely providing both high-level and low-level human-generated instructions for every task. The agent is a PaLM-2S model fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 4 for <1k episodes, rank 64 for ≥1k) on a mixture of high-level and low-level SeqIO tasks, using a simple element-representation (ER) prompt. The core empirical contribution is a scaling analysis: training data is varied from 5 to 13,604 episodes, and step-wise accuracy is measured on in-domain and three out-of-domain splits (app-unseen, task-unseen, category-unseen). Results show in-domain accuracy grows linearly with log(training data) (R² > 0.95), with extrapolated 500K–2M episodes needed for 95% step accuracy, while out-of-domain growth is significantly slower, requiring an estimated 10M–150M episodes for comparable performance on high-level tasks. Zero-shot (AitW, SeeAct, M3A, ER) and few-shot (5–100 exemplars with Gemini 1.5 Pro) baselines are compared, revealing that fine-tuning dominates in-domain but the gap to robust out-of-domain high-level performance remains large even at full data scale.

## Results

On the in-domain (IDD) test split, the best LoRA-tuned PaLM-2S model (LT-all-r64, trained on all 13,604 episodes) achieves 71.5% step accuracy on high-level instructions and 86.6% on low-level instructions, substantially outperforming all zero-shot and few-shot baselines. The best zero-shot method reaches 42.1% (M3A + GPT-4-Turbo) on high-level and 56.7% (AitW + PaLM-2L) on low-level instructions. Few-shot prompting (5–100 examples with Gemini 1.5 Pro) is generally inferior to zero-shot, peaking at 41.8% HL and 53.3% LL. Out-of-domain, the gap between in-domain and OOD accuracy widens with scale: at 10k+ episodes, the drop is 7.7–13.4 pp for high-level tasks versus only 2.0–6.7 pp for low-level tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its LoRA-tuned PaLM-2S agent against three zero-shot prompting baselines: AitW (Rawles et al., NeurIPS 2023), SeeAct (Zheng et al., 2024), and M3A (Rawles et al., 2024). On high-level instructions, the best LoRA-tuned model (LT-all-r64, 71.5%) outperforms M3A + GPT-4-Turbo (42.1%) by 29.4 pp, SeeAct + GPT-4-Turbo (33.9%) by 37.6 pp, and AitW + PaLM-2L (19.5%) by 52.0 pp. On low-level instructions, LT-all-r64 (86.6%) beats AitW + PaLM-2L (56.7%) by 29.9 pp, M3A + GPT-4-Turbo (55.0%) by 31.6 pp, and SeeAct + GPT-4-Turbo (54.3%) by 32.3 pp. Notably, even the smallest LoRA-tuned model (LT-5, 5 episodes) surpasses all zero-shot methods on low-level instructions (57.1%), while on high-level instructions it requires at least 1k episodes (52.5%) to exceed the best zero-shot baseline. Few-shot prompting with Gemini 1.5 Pro (5–100 examples) consistently underperforms zero-shot, suggesting that in-context examples do not effectively substitute for fine-tuning in this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UI control agent literature (AitW, SeeAct, M3A, Mind2Web) by introducing ANDROIDCONTROL, a dataset that uniquely pairs both high-level and low-level human-generated instructions for every task across 833 Android apps. Its action space design builds directly on UINav's approach of merging input_text with its preceding click action. The key departure from prior work is the systematic scaling analysis: while previous papers report performance at a single data scale, this paper varies training data from 5 to 13,604 episodes and quantifies how in-domain and out-of-domain performance change, a question the authors note has not been addressed in prior UI agent research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first concrete quantification of how much fine-tuning data is needed for UI control agents: roughly 1M episodes for in-domain low-level tasks and 150M for out-of-domain high-level tasks, suggesting fine-tuning alone may be insufficient for robust OOD high-level performance. This has direct practical implications for teams deciding whether to invest in large-scale data collection versus pursuing complementary approaches (planning, RL, retrieval). The ANDROIDCONTROL dataset itself is a valuable community resource given its diversity and dual instruction levels. Open questions include whether the scaling trends hold for larger or multimodal models, and whether interactive/online evaluation would change the OOD picture.
