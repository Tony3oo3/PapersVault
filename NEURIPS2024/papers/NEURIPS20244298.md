## Problem

Bayesian inference methods like Bayes AMP achieve conjectured computational optimality for inverse problems, but require explicit knowledge of the signal prior, which is unavailable in practice. Algorithm unrolling offers a data-driven alternative by training neural networks to simulate iterative inference algorithms, yet no rigorous learning guarantees existed for whether such networks can provably recover the performance of their prior-aware counterparts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244298/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For compressed sensing, the network receives noisy measurements y ∈ R^m and a fixed sensing matrix A ∈ R^{m×d}, producing a signal estimate x̂ ∈ R^d. For rank-one matrix estimation, it receives a noisy symmetric matrix Y ∈ R^{d×d} and produces x̂ ∈ R^d. Each MLP denoiser layer takes a 2D input (the scalar AMP iterate value and the empirical state evolution parameter τ̂_ℓ) for compressed sensing, or a 3D input (value, μ̂_ℓ, τ̂_ℓ) for rank-one matrix estimation, and outputs a single scalar denoised value applied entrywise across all d coordinates.

## Method

The LDNet architecture unrolls AMP iterations into L layers, replacing each scalar denoiser f_ℓ with a multi-layer perceptron that also provides its own derivative for the Onsager correction term. Training uses a layerwise procedure: each denoiser is trained sequentially on the MSE of its layer's estimate, initialized from the previously learned denoiser, with earlier layers frozen and an optional finetuning step. The key theoretical insight is that state evolution reduces the d-dimensional denoising problem to a one-dimensional score estimation problem, so the NTK-based learning guarantee requires overparametrization that is dimension-free (scaling only with the target error). This yields the first proof that gradient descent on polynomially many samples converges in polynomial time to denoisers matching Bayes AMP, achieving the conjectured optimal MSE among polynomial-time algorithms for compressed sensing with product priors.

## Results

For compressed sensing with a Bernoulli-Gaussian prior (m=250, d=500, ε=0.1, σ²=2×10⁻⁵), LDNet converges to NMSE of −44.93 dB over 15 layers, nearly matching Bayes AMP at −45.33 dB. On the Z₂ prior (σ²=0.075), LDNet achieves NMSE 0.4267, slightly outperforming Bayes AMP by 1.28%. For rank-one matrix estimation (d=1024, λ=1.5), LDNet matches Bayes AMP at NMSE 0.6931 (Gaussian prior) and 0.5243 (Z₂ prior, information-theoretically optimal). When a learnable B matrix is added, LDNet outperforms Bayes AMP by 7.3% (m=200), 16.4% (m=150), and 37.1% (m=100) in NMSE (dB) for compressed sensing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDNet is compared primarily against Bayes AMP, the conjectured computationally optimal prior-aware algorithm. In the asymptotic product-prior, Gaussian-design setting where Bayes AMP is believed optimal, LDNet matches its performance to within negligible error (e.g., 0.4 dB on a log scale for Bernoulli-Gaussian, 1.28% improvement for Z₂), confirming the theoretical guarantee. When auxiliary learnable parameters (the B matrix) are introduced, LDNet significantly outperforms Bayes AMP in finite-dimensional settings (up to 37% in NMSE dB at m=100) and with non-Gaussian sensing matrices (truncated orthogonal and truncated Gram), where Bayes AMP and ISTA actually diverge. Against ISTA and COSAMP on non-Gaussian measurement matrices, LDNet with learnable B achieves substantially lower NMSE and faster convergence, with the gap widening for ill-conditioned designs (condition number 1091). The learned denoisers also transfer across different randomly drawn sensing matrices, indicating the network learns the measurement-independent Bayes-optimal denoiser rather than overfitting to a specific A.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the algorithm unrolling lineage from Borgerding et al. (2017), who first proposed unrolling AMP with fixed or simple parametric denoisers, by replacing those denoisers with learned MLPs and providing the first rigorous *learning* guarantee (as opposed to representational or generalization bounds) showing the network converges to Bayes-optimal denoisers. The proof technique is a novel synthesis of state evolution (Bayati & Montanari, 2011), which reduces the d-dimensional AMP dynamics to a 1D score-matching problem, with NTK-based training guarantees (Allen-Zhu et al., 2019), yielding a dimension-free overparametrization requirement. This departs from prior unrolling theory (LISTA convergence, generalization bounds) by working in a Bayesian setting with product priors and proving end-to-end convergence to the conjectured computationally optimal estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that a simple layerwise SGD training procedure provably recovers the performance of Bayes AMP without requiring knowledge of the signal prior, resolving a fundamental open question about whether unrolled networks can match optimal prior-aware algorithms. The dimension-free overparametrization is a clean technical insight that makes the learning guarantee practical. However, the theory is limited to product priors and compressed sensing; extending to non-product priors (where denoisers become d-dimensional) and to rank-one matrix estimation (where multiple state evolution parameters can yield the same denoising error) remain open. The empirical demonstration that auxiliary learnable parameters (e.g., a learned B matrix) outperform Bayes AMP in finite-dimensional and non-Gaussian settings suggests a broader practical toolkit, but lacks theoretical support.
