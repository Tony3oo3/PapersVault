## Problem

Closed-loop neuroscience experiments require real-time extraction of single-neuron activity from streaming multiphoton calcium imaging, but existing methods either require batch processing of full videos or, in the case of the few online approaches (OnACID, FIOLA), need initialization periods, GPU hardware, or operate too slowly. A critical unresolved challenge is robustly estimating time traces in the presence of as-yet-unidentified cells (which cause false transients) while simultaneously discovering new cells, all at speeds exceeding the typical 30 Hz acquisition rate.

## Input / Output

The method consumes denoised fluorescence video frames one at a time (spatial Gaussian filter plus a tunable temporal running average, defaulting to single-frame processing to minimize blurring) and can be initialized with an empty profile set, requiring no buffering or startup period. It outputs per-frame activation levels φ_kt for each stable cell profile, robust residuals capturing unexplained fluorescence, and new cell detections with spatial profiles. For larger fields of view, frames are split into 80×80 pixel patches processed in parallel, with cross-patch profile matching via spatial and temporal overlap scoring.

## Method

realSEUDO builds on the SEUDO robust time-trace estimator, which models contamination from unknown cells as a sparse linear combination of small Gaussian bumps solved via a weighted LASSO (FISTA). The paper achieves real-time speed through three layers of optimization: (1) a C++ implementation with a two-pass gradient computation reducing complexity from O(n³) to O(n²), a per-dimension Lipschitz constant approximation, and a momentum-stopping rule that resets per-dimension momentum when the iterate crosses zero or the gradient changes sign (allowing η to be fixed at 1); (2) reducing Gaussian kernel spacing from 1 pixel to a larger stride, cutting the kernel count and yielding >100× speedup; and (3) a new two-stage cell-finding loop that runs SEUDO first on stable profiles, then on the residual against temporary profiles, with new cells detected via local noise-level thresholding and merged/split using brightness-aware overlap scores. Patch-based parallelization across the field of view maintains throughput at 120–200 fps on CPU-only hardware.

## Results

On in-vivo mouse CA1 recordings (36 videos, 90×90 px, 41,750 frames at 30 Hz), realSEUDO processes 162 frames per second end-to-end on a 48-core CPU (no GPU), compared to 26 fps for OnACID and 13 fps for CNMF. On the NAOMi simulation (~450 cells, 500×500 px, 20,000 frames), realSEUDO identifies 201 true cells versus 152 for OnACID and 308 for CNMF, with substantially fewer false positives than CNMF. For false-transient removal, realSEUDO retains 98% of real fluorescence while suppressing 85% of false fluorescence (TP rate 75%, FP rate 24% at λ=0.15). On the supplementary AT&T Zipcode task validating the modified FISTA, the momentum-stop variant achieves 6.99% error versus 9.52% for the non-stochastic baseline and 16.62% for plain FISTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OnACID (the prior online method), realSEUDO wins decisively on speed—6.5× faster on CA1 (162 vs. 26 fps) and 7.4× faster on NAOMi (67.8 vs. 9.2 fps)—and on cell detection, finding 201 true cells versus OnACID's 152 on NAOMi. OnACID also struggles with brightness range, occasionally missing bright cells, and requires a 100-frame initialization buffer before recognizing new cells, whereas realSEUDO detects activity from the very first frame. Against CNMF (the standard offline batch method), realSEUDO is 12.5× faster (162 vs. 13 fps) and produces fewer false positives, though CNMF identifies more total cells on NAOMi (308 vs. 201), likely because it integrates information over the full 20,000-frame recording. On the in-vivo CA1 data, realSEUDO's detected cells are most similar to CNMF's, with additional cells identified and fewer false positives. The modified FISTA optimizer (validated on AT&T Zipcode) reduces training error from 9.52% (non-stochastic baseline) to 6.99%, a 27% relative improvement, confirming the algorithmic speedups generalize beyond calcium imaging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SEUDO (Gauthier et al., Nature Methods 2022), a robust offline time-trace estimator that models contamination from unknown cells as sparse Gaussian blobs to prevent false transients. The key departure is transforming SEUDO from a post-hoc correction tool into a fully online, self-bootstrapping system: the authors add a cell-discovery feedback loop (absent in SEUDO), replace the MATLAB/TFOCS implementation with an optimized C++ FISTA solver, and introduce algorithmic modifications (dimensional momentum stopping, per-dimension Lipschitz approximation, sparse kernel grids) that collectively yield a 29–35× speedup. In the broader online calcium imaging landscape, realSEUDO positions itself against OnACID (NeurIPS 2017) and FIOLA (Nature Methods 2023), both of which require initialization buffers and, in FIOLA's case, GPU resources; realSEUDO operates from an empty profile set on the very first frame using only CPU.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-time single-neuron demixing from multiphoton calcium imaging has been a major bottleneck for closed-loop neuroscience, where neural activity must be inferred fast enough to drive stimuli or perturbations. By achieving 80–200 fps on commodity CPU hardware with no initialization period, realSEUDO removes a practical barrier to population-level closed-loop experiments and to mesoscope settings where the field of view changes on the fly. Open questions include whether the method scales to the thousands-of-neurons regime of modern mesoscopes, whether the FISTA momentum-stopping trick generalizes beyond the SEUDO cost function (the supplementary NN experiment is suggestive but limited to a small handwritten-digit network), and how performance degrades without the external motion-correction step the paper currently relies on.
