## Problem

Prior diffusion feature work (Baranchuk et al., ICLR 2022) only evaluated inter-module activations when selecting discriminative features from diffusion U-Nets, leaving queries, keys, and internal activations of embedded ViT modules entirely unexplored. This oversight means the fundamental feature selection problem remains unresolved, and more advanced architectures like SDXL paradoxically underperform older SDv1.5 when using the legacy selection strategy, because the most valuable activations have shifted into the newly introduced ViT modules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241749/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model (SDv1.5, SDXL, or Playground v2) and a single RGB image, performs one denoising step at a fixed timestep, and extracts four dense feature maps from specific locations within the up-stage of the U-Net backbone. The selected activations are primarily from inside embedded ViT modules (cross-attention queries, self-attention keys, and block outputs) rather than the conventional inter-module positions, and they span multiple resolutions to balance coarse semantic information with fine spatial detail. For the enhanced variant (Ours-XL-t), features are additionally amalgamated across multiple diffusion models and augmented with cross-attention similarity maps.

## Method

The paper identifies three universal properties of diffusion U-Nets that enable qualitative pre-filtering of the large activation candidate pool: (1) asymmetric diffusion noises that are severe in the down-stage and late up-stage but absent in the early up-stage, (2) significant in-resolution granularity changes due to the 'fat' architecture of modern U-Nets with fewer but larger resolutions, and (3) locality in self-attention activations that persists even without positional embeddings. These properties reduce the candidate pool by ~78% (e.g., from 279 to 63 activations for SDXL) before a quantitative comparison on downstream tasks ranks the remaining candidates. A key insight is that self-attention locality, while generally degrading feature quality, can suppress diffusion noises in the later up-stage, making high-resolution self-attention keys a viable feature source. The final off-the-shelf solutions select four activations per model, predominantly from ViT internal layers, and are validated on semantic correspondence, semantic segmentation, and label-scarce segmentation.

## Results

On SPair-71k semantic correspondence, the best configuration (Ours-XL-t) achieves 83.90 PCK@0.1img and 76.86 PCK@0.1bbox, outperforming the strongest SOTA (DHF) by 11.3 points. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, Ours-XL-t reaches 45.71 mIoU (vs. 40.89 for Meta Prompts), and on CityScapes it achieves 75.89 mIoU (vs. 71.94). On the label-scarce Horse-21 segmentation task, Ours-XL-t attains 66.3±0.9 mIoU, surpassing the DDPM baseline (65.0±0.8) while using a frozen pre-trained model rather than dataset-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper wins across all 12 baselines on every task. On semantic correspondence, Ours-XL-t (83.90 PCK@0.1img) beats the strongest diffusion-feature SOTA DHF [29] by 11.3 points and non-diffusion SOTAs DINO [4] (51.68) and DHPF [31] (55.28) by over 28 points; DIFT [41] is outperformed on PCK@0.1bbox (76.86 vs. 52.90). On ADE20K segmentation, Ours-XL-t (45.71 mIoU) surpasses Meta Prompts [46] (40.89), VPD [58] (37.63), ODISE [52] (29.90), and MaskCLIP [11] (23.70); on CityScapes it leads Meta Prompts by 3.95 points (75.89 vs. 71.94). On label-scarce Horse-21, Ours-XL-t (66.3±0.9) edges out DDPM [2] (65.0±0.8) and clearly beats MAE [19] (63.4), DatasetDDPM [50] (60.8), and SwAV [3] (54.0). Notably, the Legacy-XL baseline (66.00) underperforms Legacy-v1.5 (75.14) on correspondence, demonstrating that the older feature-selection protocol fails to exploit SDXL's embedded ViTs, whereas the proposed method reverses this trend (Ours-XL 81.72 > Ours-v1.5 77.78).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the feature selection protocol established by Baranchuk et al. (DDPM, ICLR 2022), which first performed a large-scale quantitative comparison of inter-module activations in diffusion U-Nets. The key departure is expanding the candidate pool from only inter-module activations to include intra-module signals (self-attention queries/keys, cross-attention queries, feed-forward outputs) within embedded ViT blocks, which prior work entirely overlooked. The paper also positions itself relative to the broader diffusion-feature literature (DHF, DIFT, VPD, Meta Prompts) by arguing that their performance is capped by an incomplete activation search space, and that the three newly identified U-Net properties (asymmetric diffusion noise, in-resolution granularity shifts, locality without positional embeddings) provide a principled basis for qualitative pre-filtering that makes the expanded search tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SDXL underperforms SDv1.5 under legacy feature selection (66.00 vs. 75.14 PCK@0.1img) is practically important: it reveals that architectural advances in diffusion models do not automatically yield better discriminative features unless the correct activations are identified. The three universal properties offer a transferable analytical framework for future diffusion architectures, though the authors explicitly note uncertainty about generalization to DiT-based models, which are increasingly dominant. A key open question is whether the qualitative filtering heuristics (e.g., using self-attention locality to suppress diffusion noise in high-resolution activations) hold under different training regimes or conditioning schemes, and whether the approach scales to video or 3D diffusion backbones.
