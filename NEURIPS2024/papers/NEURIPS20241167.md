## Problem

CRATE, a white-box transformer derived from unrolled optimization on the sparse rate reduction objective, offers mathematical interpretability but has shown diminishing returns when scaled from Base to Large (only +0.5% on ImageNet-1K). Whether CRATE can scale effectively along both model size and data dimensions—matching the scaling behavior of standard ViTs—remained an open question, with the vanilla ISTA sparse coding block identified as a key bottleneck.

## Input / Output

For supervised classification, the model takes RGB images resized to 224×224 and patchified at sizes 8, 14, 16, or 32, producing a class label over 1000 ImageNet categories. For vision-language pretraining (Large→Huge scaling), inputs are image-text pairs from DataComp1B at 84×84 (pretraining) or 224×224 (fine-tuning) with max token length 32, producing contrastive embeddings. A distinctive output is the structured token representation: because each layer enforces sparsity and subspace compression, the learned features naturally yield zero-shot object segmentation via MaskCut without any segmentation supervision.

## Method

CRATE-α replaces the vanilla ISTA sparse coding block with an Overcomplete Dictionary Learning (ODL) block built from three modifications: (1) an overcomplete dictionary D ∈ R^{d×Cd} (C=4) with two proximal gradient descent steps to obtain sparse codes, (2) a decoupled reconstruction dictionary D̂ ∈ R^{d×Cd} separate from the encoding dictionary, and (3) a residual connection so the output is Z^{ℓ+1} = Z^{ℓ+1/2} + D̂·A^{ℓ}. The compression block (MSSA) is unchanged, preserving the mathematical derivation from sparse rate reduction. Training follows a two-stage recipe: pretraining on ImageNet-21K (lr 8e-4, batch 4096, 91 epochs, AdamW, cosine decay) followed by fine-tuning on ImageNet-1K; for the Huge model, CLIPA-style contrastive vision-language pretraining on DataComp1B with a frozen text encoder. A compute-efficient variant pretrains at patch size 32 (shorter token sequences) and fine-tunes at smaller patch sizes, reducing pretraining compute by ~85% while retaining most accuracy.

## Results

CRATE-α-B/8 achieves 83.2% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, surpassing the prior best CRATE-B (79.5%) by 3.7%. Scaling to Large, CRATE-α-L/8 reaches 85.1% top-1 accuracy, a 1.9% gain over the Base model. When further scaled to Huge via vision-language pre-training on DataComp1B, CRATE-α-H achieves 72.3% zero-shot top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, a 2.5% improvement over the Large model. On downstream tasks, CRATE-α-L/14 reaches 99.10% on CIFAR-10, 92.57% on CIFAR-100, and 99.56% on Oxford Flowers-102, consistently outperforming the vanilla CRATE baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla CRATE [46], CRATE-α delivers substantial gains at every scale: CRATE-α-B/16 (81.2%) beats CRATE-B/16 (70.8%) by 10.4%, and CRATE-α-L/14 (83.9%) beats CRATE-L/16 (71.3%) by 12.6%. Critically, vanilla CRATE shows diminishing returns when scaling from Base to Large (only +0.5%), whereas CRATE-α shows consistent 3.7–4.0% gains across patch sizes. Against ViT [9], CRATE-α-B/16 (81.2%) trails ViT-B/16 (84.0%) by 2.8%, but at Large scale CRATE-α-L/16 (84.6%) nearly matches ViT-L/16 (85.2%), narrowing the gap to 0.6%. On language modeling (OpenWebText), CRATE-α-base (120M) achieves 3.14 cross-entropy, improving over CRATE-base (60M) at 3.37, though still trailing GPT-2-base (124M) at 2.85. On COCO MaskCut segmentation [47], CRATE-α-L/8 achieves AP50=4.0 for detection and 2.7 for segmentation, outperforming CRATE-B/8 (2.9 and 2.2) and ViT-B/8 (0.8 and 0.7) by wide margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends CRATE (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2023), a white-box transformer derived from unrolled optimization of the sparse rate reduction objective, by identifying the vanilla ISTA sparse coding block as the key architectural bottleneck preventing effective scaling. The three modifications—overcomplete dictionary (motivated by the favorable optimization landscape shown in Qu et al., ICLR 2019), decoupled dictionary, and residual connection—are minimal yet sufficient to unlock scaling behavior. For the Large-to-Huge scaling regime, the paper adopts the CLIPA framework (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024) for vision-language contrastive pre-training on DataComp1B, using a frozen pre-trained text encoder while training only the CRATE-α vision encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that a mathematically interpretable transformer architecture can scale effectively (from 77M to 527M parameters) once a specific architectural bottleneck is addressed—challenges the implicit assumption that white-box models are inherently limited to small scales. Notably, semantic interpretability (zero-shot segmentation quality via MaskCut) improves with model size, suggesting interpretability and performance are not in tension. However, the compute requirements remain substantial (up to ~39K TPU v3 core-hours for the best supervised model), and the Huge-scale vision-language results rely on a frozen black-box text encoder, leaving open whether fully white-box foundation models are feasible at scale.
