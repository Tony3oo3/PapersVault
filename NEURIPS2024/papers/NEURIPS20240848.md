## Problem

Generalized few-shot semantic segmentation (GFSS) requires recognizing both base and novel classes at inference, but existing methods rely on complex combinations of customized modules, meta-learning, transductive learning, and carefully designed loss functions that increase implementation difficulty and computation time. The paper asks whether a simple rule and standard supervised learning can match or exceed these complex approaches while preserving base-class performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240848/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, K-shot annotated masks for each novel class, and a frozen pre-trained base-class model (PSPNet with ResNet-50) as input. It produces a pixel-level segmentation map over all classes (base + novel). The base model is never modified; instead, lightweight linear probes are trained on its frozen features, and a base-novel mapping (BNM) determines which base-class predictions get overwritten by novel-class predictions at inference.

## Method

BCM (Base-Class Mining) operates in two training steps: (1) it counts co-occurrences between the base model's predictions and novel-class ground truth to build a base-novel mapping (BNM) via a top-s strategy, identifying which base classes the model confuses with novel classes; (2) for each chosen base class β, it trains a linear probe g_β on modified K-shot data where irrelevant novel-class and background labels are relabeled to β, so g_β outputs either β or the relevant novel class. At inference, the base model runs first, and pixels predicted as a chosen base class are overwritten by the corresponding g_β output. The paper proves (Proposition 4.1) that mIoU over all base classes not in the chosen set B is perfectly maintained, since those pixels are never overwritten. The implementation uses Tukey's ladder-of-powers feature transformation and shot-wise ensemble learning for K>1, with all novel-class models trained via Scikit-learn logistic regression on CPU.

## Results

BCM achieves the best Novel and Mean mIoU scores across all three datasets (PASCAL-5i, PASCAL-10i, COCO-20i) in both 1-shot and 5-shot settings. The most substantial gains appear in the 1-shot scenario: +6.1% Novel mIoU on PASCAL-5i (41.24 vs. 35.11 for DIaM) and +4.7% on PASCAL-10i (35.94 vs. 31.29). In the 5-shot setting, BCM still leads on Novel mIoU (e.g., 55.36 vs. 55.31 on PASCAL-5i, 53.49 vs. 51.89 on PASCAL-10i, 30.60 vs. 28.73 on COCO-20i) while maintaining Base scores comparable to the strongest baselines (within ~0.5 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BCM outperforms all three baselines (CAPL, BAM, DIaM) on Novel and Mean mIoU across every dataset and shot setting. Against the strongest baseline DIaM [20], BCM gains +6.13 Novel mIoU in 1-shot PASCAL-5i (41.24 vs. 35.11), +4.65 in 1-shot PASCAL-10i (35.94 vs. 31.29), and +1.06 in 1-shot COCO-20i (18.28 vs. 17.22); in 5-shot the gaps narrow to +0.05, +1.60, and +1.87 respectively. On Base mIoU, BCM is essentially tied with DIaM and BAM (within 0.5 points), confirming the theoretical guarantee that most base-class performance is preserved. Against CAPL [16] and BAM [14], the Novel mIoU advantage is much larger (e.g., +23.78 on 1-shot PASCAL-5i over CAPL), reflecting that these meta-learning-based methods struggle more with novel-class recognition. Notably, BCM achieves these results using only a linear probe trained with logistic regression, whereas DIaM relies on transductive learning and CAPL/BAM require meta-learning or customized modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

BCM departs from the dominant GFSS paradigm (CAPL, BAM, POP, PCN, DIaM) that relies on customized modules, meta-learning, transductive inference, or carefully designed distillation losses. Instead, it repurposes the practical GFSS setting introduced by DIaM [20] but replaces all of that machinery with a co-occurrence-based base-novel mapping rule and standard logistic regression, drawing a conceptual parallel to 'frustratingly simple' few-shot object detection [39,40]. The observation that novel classes are often misclassified as background or semantically related base classes echoes qualitative findings in continual semantic segmentation (ECLIPSE [26]), but BCM formalizes this into an explicit architectural routing mechanism rather than treating it as an incidental phenomenon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantee (Proposition 4.1) that base-class mIoU is perfectly preserved for all non-chosen base classes is the first such result in GFSS, providing a principled answer to the catastrophic-forgetting concern that plagues existing methods. Practically, BCM's sub-minute training time and absence of transductive inference make it attractive for latency-sensitive deployments where DIaM-style methods are impractical. However, the 5-shot gains are modest (≤1.0% on COCO-20i), and the method does not leverage novel-class data to improve base-class performance, leaving open whether richer mapping strategies or more powerful few-shot classifiers can close the remaining gap.
