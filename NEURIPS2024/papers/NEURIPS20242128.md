## Problem

Implicit-differentiation-based TDA methods (e.g., influence functions) are computationally efficient but rely on assumptions of convergence and optimality that fail for non-converged models, multi-stage training pipelines, and settings where optimizer details matter. Unrolling-based methods handle these cases but require storing all intermediate optimization variables, making them infeasible for large-scale models. The paper bridges this gap by deriving an influence-function-like closed-form formula from the unrolling perspective that retains unrolling's flexibility while requiring only a handful of checkpoints.

## Input / Output

SOURCE consumes C model checkpoints saved at selected points during training (e.g., 6 checkpoints across 20 epochs), the full training dataset, and a query data point. Unlike exact unrolling, it does not require the complete sequence of T intermediate parameter vectors or the exact iteration at which a data point appeared; instead it estimates the averaged effect of removal within each segment. The output is a scalar attribution score per (query, training point) pair, which can be summed over subsets to predict counterfactual model behavior changes (e.g., loss or margin shifts after retraining without a data subset).

## Method

SOURCE partitions the training trajectory into L segments and approximates the Hessian and gradient distributions as stationary within each segment, then approximates the unrolled Jacobian product over a segment as the matrix exponential exp(−η̄ℓ Kℓ H̄ℓ). The per-segment contribution to the expected total derivative reduces to a damped inverse-Hessian-vector product (H̄ℓ + λI)⁻¹ ḡℓ with an explicit damping parameter λ = η̄ℓ⁻¹Kℓ⁻¹, yielding a closed-form influence-function-like formula that sums over segments with inter-segment propagation via the matrix exponentials. In practice, the Hessian is approximated with the EK-FAC (Eigenvalue-corrected Kronecker-Factored Approximate Curvature) parameterization, whose explicit eigendecomposition enables efficient computation of the matrix functions. This makes SOURCE C times more expensive than single-checkpoint influence functions (where C is the number of checkpoints), but far cheaper than exact unrolling, while supporting non-converged models, learning-rate schedules, and multi-stage training pipelines that implicit differentiation cannot handle.

## Results

SOURCE achieves the highest Linear Datamodeling Score (LDS) at α=0.5 across nearly all evaluated tasks (UCI regression, MNIST/FashionMNIST/CIFAR-10 image classification, GLUE text classification, and WikiText-2 language modeling), with the sole exception being CIFAR-10 with ResNet-9. In the subset removal counterfactual evaluation, SOURCE identifies the top positively influential training points that most reliably cause misclassification of test examples when removed and retrained. Notably, even the non-ensembled single-model version of SOURCE typically outperforms the 10-model ensembled versions of competing methods, particularly in challenging settings such as non-converged models (Concrete-N, FashionMNIST-N) and multi-stage training (RotatedMNIST, PACS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOURCE outperforms all four baselines—REPSIM, TRACIN, TRAK, and IF (EK-FAC)—on LDS across most tasks in both single-model and 10-model ensemble settings. Against TRAK and IF, which are implicit-differentiation-based methods, SOURCE's advantage is most pronounced in non-converged settings (Concrete-N, FashionMNIST-N) and multi-stage training (RotatedMNIST, PACS), where these methods' assumption of optimality breaks down. TRACIN, which traces gradient updates without Hessian approximation, is consistently outperformed by SOURCE, suggesting that the Hessian-aware damped inverse in SOURCE's formula yields a more accurate counterfactual estimate. REPSIM, a representation-similarity baseline, performs weakest overall, consistent with its lack of connection to the counterfactual. In the subset removal evaluation, SOURCE causes the highest fraction of test misclassifications at each removal budget k, confirming it identifies the most causally influential training points.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOURCE bridges two previously separate families of gradient-based training data attribution: implicit-differentiation methods (influence functions, TRAK) that are computationally efficient but assume convergence to a unique optimum, and unrolling-based methods (SGD-INFLUENCE, HYDRA) that capture training dynamics but require storing all intermediate optimization variables. The key conceptual move is to partition the training trajectory into segments and approximate gradients/Hessians as stationary within each segment, yielding a closed-form influence-function-like expression that reduces to classical influence functions in the single-segment, converged case. Practically, SOURCE instantiates this with the EK-FAC Kronecker-factored Hessian approximation, inheriting the efficient eigendecomposition-based computation from that line of work while extending it to multiple checkpoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOURCE provides a principled tool for data attribution in settings where influence-function-based methods are known to fail: non-converged models (e.g., early-stopped training to avoid overfitting) and multi-stage pipelines (continual learning, foundation model pre-training followed by fine-tuning). The explicit derivation of the damping parameter λ = η⁻¹K⁻¹ from the unrolling analysis gives a more grounded justification for the regularization term used in practical IF implementations. Limitations include the C× computational overhead over single-checkpoint IF (mitigated by a parameter-averaging variant at L× cost), reliance on the EK-FAC approximation, and evaluation only up to ~120M parameters (GPT-2), leaving open whether the stationarity-within-segment assumption holds for very long or highly non-stationary training runs at scale.
