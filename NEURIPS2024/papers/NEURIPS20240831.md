## Problem

Existing gradient-based approximate machine unlearning methods operate under a Euclidean metric that treats all parameters equally, leading to sub-optimal update trajectories that compromise retained performance. While weight saliency (SalUn) and remain-preserving manifold constraints (Selective Amnesia) have been proposed individually, they lack a unified theoretical derivation and rely on fixed Hessian approximations that accumulate estimation bias over iterations. This paper asks: can we derive the optimal unlearning direction on a manifold that preserves remaining performance, and approximate its second-order structure efficiently for large-scale models?

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained model (ResNet-18, Swin-T, DDPM/UNet, DiT, or Stable Diffusion V1.4), a forgetting dataset D^f (a subset of training samples, a class, or a concept like 'nudity'), and a remaining dataset D^r to preserve. It produces updated model parameters that approximate a retrained model excluding D^f. The approach is task-agnostic: for classification it uses cross-entropy loss, and for image generation it uses MSE loss in the diffusion denoising objective, requiring no architectural modifications. For large-scale generation tasks where computing Fisher diagonals on the full remaining set is infeasible, an equivalent number of remaining samples is randomly subsampled.

## Method

The paper first decomposes the vanilla gradient descent direction for minimizing KL divergence to the retrained model into three components—weighted forgetting gradient ascent (F), remaining gradient descent (R), and a weight saliency matrix (S)—providing a unified theoretical lens over prior methods. It then derives the steepest descent direction under a remain-preserving manifold metric (KL divergence on the remaining output distribution), which modulates the forgetting direction by the inverse Hessian of the remaining loss to prevent updates from harming retained performance. To avoid explicit Hessian computation, the method employs a fast-slow weight update: a single forgetting step (fast weight) followed by fine-tuning on the remaining set (slow weight) implicitly approximates the Hessian-adjusted direction via a second-order Taylor expansion (Prop. 3). Two additional components complete the scheme: sample-wise adaptive coefficients that down-weight already-forgotten samples during gradient ascent, and a forget-remain balanced saliency mask computed from the ratio of Fisher diagonals on forgetting vs. remaining sets, applied exclusively to the forgetting gradient.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (10% random subset forgetting, ResNet-18), SFR-on achieves the lowest averaging disparity from retraining (Avg.D = 1.47) and smallest output KL divergence (D_KL = 0.15) among all baselines, while also being the fastest at 2.10 min runtime (vs. 3.79–5.56 min for other methods and 73.37 min for full retraining). On TinyImageNet (Swin-T), SFR-on similarly attains the best Avg.D (3.85) and D_KL (0.44). For image generation, SFR-on completely forgets target classes (FA = 0.00) on CIFAR-10 DDPM in just 50 steps and on ImageNet DiT in 500 steps, compared to 10,000 steps for SA and 1,000 for SalUn, while maintaining FID scores closest to retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFR-on outperforms all six gradient-based baselines (FT, GA, RL, SalUn, BT, SCRUB) on both CIFAR-10 and TinyImageNet in the primary unlearning metrics: it achieves the lowest Avg.D (1.47 vs. 4.26–11.32) and D_KL (0.15 vs. 0.24–0.36) on CIFAR-10, and the lowest Avg.D (3.85 vs. 4.13–9.04) on TinyImageNet, while also being the fastest at 2.10 min runtime. For image generation class-forgetting, SFR-on matches or exceeds SA and SalUn on FA (achieving 0.00 for all target classes) while reducing steps by 200× (50 vs. 10,000 for SA on CIFAR-10 DDPM) and 20× (500 vs. 10,000 for SA on ImageNet DiT), with FID scores closest to retraining. In the NSFW concept-removal task on Stable Diffusion, SFR-on reduces total exposed body parts to 3 out of 9,406 generated images, substantially outperforming SalUn (46) and ESD (492). The main trade-off is that SFR-on's FA on TinyImageNet (97.02) is slightly higher than RT (85.29), indicating some residual memorization, though its MIA gap (1.79) is the smallest among all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the landscape of gradient-based approximate machine unlearning by decomposing the vanilla steepest-descent direction into three components (weighted forgetting gradient ascent, remaining gradient descent, and a weight saliency matrix), showing that prior methods like FT, GA, RL, BT, and SalUn each capture only subsets of these terms. It departs from the Euclidean-metric assumption shared by most prior work by embedding updates in a remain-preserving manifold defined by KL divergence on the remaining output distribution, and repurposes the fast-slow weight mechanism from meta-learning and the Lookahead optimizer to implicitly approximate the online Hessian that Selective Amnesia approximates with fixed initial Fisher diagonals. The forget-remain balanced saliency map extends SalUn's weight saliency by incorporating both forgetting and remaining Fisher diagonals, grounded in the theoretical decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50-step unlearning on CIFAR-10 DDPM (vs. 10,000 for SA) and 500-step result on ImageNet DiT represent a dramatic efficiency gain that makes approximate MU practical for large generative models, while the theoretical unification gives the field a common language for comparing and combining unlearning components. Limitations include the heuristic nature of the adaptive forgetting coefficients (acknowledged by the authors as a direction for future work), reliance on Fisher diagonal approximations for saliency, and evaluation confined to CV tasks (classification and image generation) without testing on language models. Open questions include whether the fast-slow Hessian approximation remains stable over very long unlearning horizons, whether the approach transfers to LLM unlearning, and whether more principled coefficient estimation can close the remaining gap to retraining.
