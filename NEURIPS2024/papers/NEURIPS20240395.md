## Problem

Simulators are ubiquitous in reinforcement learning, yet almost all existing algorithms—both empirical and theoretical—operate in the fully online setting and fail to exploit the additional information available through simulator access. For general (nonlinear) function approximation, it was essentially unknown whether local simulator access (resetting to previously observed states) could yield sample-efficient learning guarantees, and the notoriously difficult Exogenous Block MDP problem remained intractable under standard online RL assumptions.

## Input / Output

The agent receives sequential state observations x_h from an episodic MDP and, crucially, can reset to any previously encountered state at any layer h to collect additional transitions (local simulator access). It also requires access to a value function class (Q or V, potentially neural networks) and a structural parameter (coverability C_cov or pushforward coverability C_push). The output is an executable near-optimal policy π̂ (computable without simulator access at test time) with suboptimality bounded by ε, along with estimated value functions V̂_{1:H}. The sample complexity is polynomial in the coverability parameter and ε^{-1}, with no prior knowledge of the covering distribution required.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary algorithms. SimGolf augments the global optimism principle (from GOLF) with local simulator access: instead of the de-biasing technique that requires Bellman completeness, it directly estimates Bellman backup functions T_h[g_{h+1}](x,a) by drawing K next-state transitions from the local simulator, thereby sidestepping the double sampling problem and requiring only Q*-realizability. RVFS (Recursive Value Function Search) is a computationally efficient alternative that reduces to convex optimization over the value function class; it recursively fits value functions layer-by-layer using Monte-Carlo rollouts from adaptively constructed core-sets of informative state-action pairs, and tests for distribution shift via confidence-set-based checks that trigger re-fitting when the greedy policy's rollout distribution diverges from what was seen during training. RVFS can be viewed as a principled, provable counterpart to MCTS/AlphaZero that handles stochastic environments, continuous state spaces, and distribution shift through function approximation.

## Results

The paper provides two main theoretical guarantees. SimGolf (Theorem 3.1) achieves sample complexity $\widetilde{O}(H^5 C_{\text{cov}}^2 \log(|\mathcal{Q}|/\delta) \cdot \varepsilon^{-4})$ under only $Q^\star$-realizability and coverability, and as a corollary, learns ExBMDPs with $\widetilde{O}(H^5 S^3 A^3 \log|\Phi| \cdot \varepsilon^{-4})$ episodes. RVFS (Theorem 4.1) achieves $\widetilde{O}(C_{\text{push}}^8 H^{23} A \cdot \varepsilon^{-13})$ sample complexity under pushforward coverability, and is computationally efficient (reducing to convex optimization over the value function class). For weakly correlated ExBMDPs, RVFS$^{\text{exo}}$ (Theorem B.1) achieves $\widetilde{O}(C_{\text{exo}}^8 S^8 H^{36} A^9 \cdot \varepsilon^{-26})$ episodes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The key comparison is against prior theoretical guarantees: SimGolf requires only $Q^\star$-realizability, whereas prior coverability-based algorithms (Xie et al. 2023, Amortila et al. 2024) require the significantly stronger Bellman completeness or weight function realizability. For ExBMDPs, prior online RL algorithms required either deterministic latent dynamics (Efroni et al. 2022) or factored emission structure (Efroni et al. 2022); SimGolf removes both restrictions. RVFS is the first computationally efficient algorithm for RL with local simulator access supporting nonlinear function approximation, whereas prior RLLS results (Weisz et al. 2021, Li et al. 2021, Yin et al. 2022, Weisz et al. 2022) were restricted to linear function approximation. The paper also conjectures a separation: $Q^\star$-realizability plus coverability may be insufficient for polynomial sample complexity in vanilla online RL (without local simulators), implying local simulator access is fundamentally necessary in this regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GOLF method (Jin et al. [32], Xie et al. [63])—which uses global optimism for online RL under coverability—by augmenting it with local simulator access to bypass the double sampling problem, thereby replacing the Bellman completeness requirement with the much weaker Q*-realizability. It departs from prior local-simulator work (Weisz et al. [57], Li et al. [40], Yin et al. [65], Weisz et al. [59]), which was confined to linear function approximation, by providing the first guarantees for general (e.g., neural) value function classes. The computationally efficient algorithm RVFS is positioned as a principled, provable counterpart to empirical paradigms like MCTS and AlphaZero that combine recursive search with value function approximation, but with explicit handling of stochasticity and distribution shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that local simulator access fundamentally expands the class of MDPs that are provably learnable with general function approximation—most notably making general Exogenous Block MDPs tractable for the first time—and conjectures a separation between online RL with and without resets. Key limitations include SimGolf's computational inefficiency (global optimism) and RVFS's reliance on stronger assumptions (pushforward coverability, Δ-gap or all-policy realizability); whether these can be weakened to match SimGolf's statistical assumptions remains open. The polynomial dependence on problem parameters (e.g., ε^{-4}, C_push^8) is far from tight, and no empirical validation is provided, leaving the practical relevance of RVFS versus heuristics like Go-Explore untested.
