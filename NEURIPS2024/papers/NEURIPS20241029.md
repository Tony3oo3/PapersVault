## Problem

CLIP models exhibit naive 'bag of words' behavior and fail at compositional reasoning (e.g., distinguishing 'bulb in the grass' from 'grass in the bulb') because random sampling during contrastive training rarely exposes the model to semantically similar negative pairs. Prior work like NegCLIP addresses this by generating hard negative captions via rule-based perturbations, but these captions are often linguistically flawed, and no prior method has effectively incorporated hard negative images alongside negative captions to jointly improve both modalities' compositional understanding.

## Input / Output

The training input consists of positive image-text pairs from CC3M (2.6M) or CC12M (8.6M) with LLM-rewritten captions, augmented with one synthetic hard negative image-text pair per positive pair (generated by Mistral-7B for captions and SDXL-turbo for images), yielding 13M total pairs in TripletData. At inference, the trained ViT-B/32 model takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input and produces cosine similarity scores used for zero-shot classification (class labels), bidirectional image-text retrieval (ranked lists on MSCOCO/Flickr30k), and compositional reasoning evaluation (SugarCrepe sub-scores across Replace/Swap/Add categories).

## Method

TripletCLIP introduces a two-stage synthetic data pipeline: (1) Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 generates linguistically accurate hard negative captions via in-context learning, and (2) SDXL-turbo synthesizes corresponding photorealistic negative images from those captions. The key training innovation is a triplet contrastive loss (Eq. 4) that combines two NegCLIP-style objectives over two triplets: (X, Y, Y') and (X', Y', Y). The second triplet is the critical insight—ablations show that negative images alone (NegImage) fail to improve compositionality because images contain low-level information, but when the negative image is paired with the negative caption as the positive anchor, it teaches the model what the negative caption visually entails, thereby regularizing and stabilizing the effect of negative captions. This alternating use of hard negative image-text pairs, combined with the LLM-generated (rather than rule-based) negative captions, yields over 9% absolute improvement on SugarCrepe over LaCLIP and 6% over NegCLIP at equal compute budget.

## Results

On the SugarCrepe compositional reasoning benchmark, TripletCLIP achieves 63.49% overall accuracy on CC3M and 74.45% on CC12M, surpassing LaCLIP by 9.40% and 10.91% respectively. On zero-shot image-text retrieval (R@5), TripletCLIP reaches 22.00 on Flickr30k T2I (CC3M) and 55.90 (CC12M), outperforming LaCLIP by 11.1% and 13.2%. For zero-shot ImageNet-1k classification, TripletCLIP achieves 7.32% top-1 (CC3M) and 23.31% top-1 (CC12M), a 3.5% absolute gain over LaCLIP. Notably, TripletCLIP trained on CC12M (8.6M pairs) matches the SugarCrepe performance of DataComp's ViT-B/16 model trained on 1 billion image-text pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TripletCLIP consistently outperforms LaCLIP [14] across all benchmarks: +9.40% on SugarCrepe (CC3M), +11.1% on Flickr30k T2I retrieval, and +3.53% on ImageNet-1k top-1. Against NegCLIP [61], TripletCLIP gains +6.31% on SugarCrepe and +11.16% on retrieval, while the improved NegCLIP++ baseline (using LLM-generated negatives) narrows the gap to +1.80% on SugarCrepe but still trails by +8.06% on retrieval. In finetuning comparisons on SugarCrepe, TripletCLIP (82.46) beats Sahin et al. [44] (77.84), CoN-CLIP [50] (75.58), and TSVLC [12] (77.84), but falls short of DAC [11] (86.41), which uses dense aligned captions. TripletCLIP also surpasses CyCLIP [17] by +11.09% on SugarCrepe when both use the same CC3M data. Most strikingly, TripletCLIP trained on only 8.6M CC12M pairs achieves 74.45% on SugarCrepe, exceeding DataComp's ViT-B/16 model trained on 1 billion pairs (73.31%), demonstrating that hard negative augmentation is more effective than raw data scale for compositional reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional-reasoning line of work on CLIP that began with rule-based hard-negative caption generation (NegCLIP, Zhang et al. 2023) and LLM-rewritten captions (LaCLIP, Fan et al. 2024). The key departure is twofold: (1) it uses a T2I diffusion model (SDXL-turbo) to synthesize hard negative *images* alongside LLM-generated hard negative *captions*, creating the 13M-pair TripletData; and (2) it introduces a triplet contrastive loss (Eq. 4) that alternates between the positive and negative image-text triplets, rather than simply appending negatives to the InfoNCE denominator as NegCLIP does. The paper positions itself as orthogonal to data-quality efforts (DataComp, MetaCLIP) and to other compositional-reasoning methods (CoN-CLIP, DAC, TSVLC), demonstrating compatibility via a CyCLIP ablation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The released TripletData (13M hard-negative image-text pairs) and the simple triplet loss offer a practical, drop-in recipe for improving CLIP-style pre-training on compositional benchmarks without requiring new architectures or massive additional real data. The finding that the vision encoder—not the text encoder—is the bottleneck for compositionality (Table 8) reframes where future effort should be directed. However, all experiments are limited to ViT-B/32 on CC3M/CC12M under an academic compute budget (~1200 A100-hours), so it remains open whether the gains persist at ViT-L/14 or billion-pair scale, and whether the approach transfers to MLLMs or T2I diffusion models as the authors speculate.
