## Problem

Existing vision foundation models such as MAE and DINOv2 are not robust to the extreme pixel-level noise in cryo-EM micrographs, where limited electron dose produces very low SNR and clean reference images are impossible to obtain from fragile biological specimens. This gap prevents direct transfer of self-supervised pre-training strategies to cryo-EM, where denoising-aware feature extraction is essential for downstream tasks like particle picking and micrograph curation.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, DRACO consumes triplets of motion-corrected micrographs (original, odd-frame-summed, and even-frame-summed) cropped to 256×256 from 4096×4096 originals, with the odd and even images serving as two independent noisy observations of the same underlying signal. At inference for denoising, a single noisy micrograph is fed in and the model predicts all patches directly without fine-tuning. For downstream adaptation, the pre-trained ViT encoder is plugged into Detectron2 for particle bounding-box detection or a linear head for binary micrograph quality classification.

## Method

DRACO employs a ViT encoder-decoder (MAE-style) with a denoising-reconstruction hybrid training scheme inspired by Noise2Noise. The movie is split into odd and even frame subsets, each motion-corrected and summed to produce two independent noisy micrographs; two binary masks (ratio ≥ 0.5) are applied such that every visible patch on one micrograph has its counterpart masked on the other, preventing information leakage. On visible patches, an N2N L2 loss trains the model to predict the counterpart micrograph's patch (denoising); on doubly-masked patches, a reconstruction loss supervises prediction against the higher-SNR original micrograph. The combined latent representation is formed by summing unmasked odd/even features and inserting a shared [MASK] token for doubly-masked positions before decoding. Pre-training uses 270,000+ curated micrographs from 529 EMPIAR datasets (resolution >10 Å), with a 200-epoch MAE warm-up followed by 400 epochs of the denoising-reconstruction objective on 64 A800 GPUs.

## Results

On particle picking, DRACO-L achieves the best F1 score across all three test datasets (0.816 on Human HCN1, 0.824 on 70S ribosome, 0.725 on LetB) and the best 3D reconstruction resolution (3.90 Å, 2.51 Å, 3.53 Å respectively). On denoising, DRACO-L attains the highest SNR on 3 of 4 test datasets (2.01 dB on Human Apoferritin, 3.33 dB on HA Trimer, 12.21 dB on RNA polymerase), substantially outperforming Topaz-Denoise and MAE. On micrograph curation, DRACO-L achieves 98.3% accuracy and 0.984 F1 score via linear probing, surpassing Miffi (83.6% accuracy) and ResNet18 (93.8% accuracy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRACO outperforms all nine baselines across every downstream task. In particle picking, DRACO-L beats crYOLO (the strongest baseline) by 3.4 F1 points on 70S ribosome (0.824 vs 0.786) and by 0.3 Å in resolution (2.51 vs 2.78 Å); Topaz and CryoTransformer suffer from high false-positive rates (precision 0.36–0.52), while Detectron2 trained from scratch and MAE struggle to distinguish signal from noise. In denoising, DRACO-L improves SNR by 3.55 dB over Topaz-Denoise on RNA polymerase (12.21 vs 8.66 dB) and by 2.32 dB over MAE on Human Apoferritin (2.01 vs −0.31 dB); Low-pass filtering degrades particle structure while Topaz-Denoise sometimes over-smooths. In micrograph curation, DRACO-L achieves 98.3% accuracy via linear probing, outperforming Miffi (83.6%) by 14.7 points and ResNet18 trained from scratch (93.8%) by 4.5 points, demonstrating that the denoising-reconstruction pre-training yields features far more discriminative than standard MAE or supervised alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRACO unifies two previously separate lines of work: the masked autoencoder (MAE) framework for self-supervised feature extraction and the Noise2Noise (N2N) / Topaz-Denoise paradigm for denoising from paired noisy observations. While MAE learns general-purpose features by reconstructing masked patches, it ignores the extreme pixel-level noise inherent to cryo-EM; conversely, N2N-based denoisers like Topaz-Denoise handle noise but operate on small datasets with limited architectures and do not produce transferable representations. DRACO's key architectural departure is a hybrid training objective that applies N2N loss on visible patches (denoising) and reconstruction loss on masked patches (reconstruction) simultaneously, within a single ViT encoder-decoder, thereby extracting noise-robust features at foundation-model scale. The paper also contributes a large-scale curated dataset (270K+ micrographs from 529 EMPIAR entries) that addresses the data-quality bottleneck limiting prior cryo-EM learning methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first foundation model purpose-built for cryo-EM, and the curated 25 TB dataset it introduces is a significant community resource that was previously unavailable in a unified, quality-verified form. The consistent outperformance across denoising, particle picking, and micrograph curation demonstrates that noise-aware pre-training yields better general-purpose features than standard MAE for this modality, a finding that could reshape how cryo-EM pipelines are designed. Key limitations include dependence on motion-correction quality, restriction to single-particle (not cryo-ET) data, and micrograph-level (not particle-level) task support; extending DRACO to raw movies, cryo-ET, and particle-level tasks like pose estimation are natural next steps.
