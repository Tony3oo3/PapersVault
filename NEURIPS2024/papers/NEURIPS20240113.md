## Problem

Existing explanation stability metrics rely on ℓ_p distance, which diverges from human perception: a perturbed explanation with small ℓ_p distance can exhibit drastically different top-k salient features. Moreover, adversarial training for robust explanations is computationally intensive (requiring iterative adversarial sample search per training step) and leads to an attack-defense arms race. The paper asks whether a ranking-aware metric and an efficient regularizer can certify explanation stability against all attacks without adversarial sample generation.

## Input / Output

The method accepts heterogeneous inputs—tabular vectors (16–28 features), RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 28×28 MNIST), medical OCT images (771×514), and brain graphs (6555 nodes)—and produces a class prediction alongside a saliency map whose top-k entries are the stability target. Explanations are generated via Grad, SmoothGrad, Integrated Gradients, or concept-based neuron activations, and the evaluation metric is Precision@k measuring overlap of top-k salient features before and after a ranking-targeted perturbation (ERAttack) or an ℓ_2-targeted perturbation (MSE attack).

## Method

R2ET introduces 'ranking explanation thickness,' defined as the expected gap between top-k salient and non-salient feature scores along line segments to perturbed inputs, and shows it is bounded below by the instantaneous gap h(x,i,j) minus a Hessian-norm term. The training objective adds two regularizers to the classification loss: (1) a weighted sum of feature-score gaps that pushes salient features further above non-salient ones, and (2) a Hessian-norm penalty (estimated via finite differences) that flattens the curvature of the explanation surface. This surrogate requires only two extra backward propagations per sample, versus M₁×M₂×2 for direct thickness optimization or ~40 PGD iterations for adversarial training. The paper proves R2ET is equivalent to a min-max adversarial-training formulation (Prop. 4.6), certifies a minimal perturbation budget that preserves all feature rankings (Prop. 4.5), and derives a global convergence rate for a trust-region multi-objective attack algorithm whose iteration count scales with the thickness gap (Thm. 5.1).

## Results

The primary metric is Precision@k (P@k) measuring the overlap of top-k salient features before and after attack. Under the more challenging ERAttack (ranking-based), R2ET and its variants achieve the highest P@k on 6 of 8 datasets: e.g., R2ET reaches 85.7% on MNIST (vs. 59.0% Vanilla, 56.0% AT), 71.6% on ADHD (vs. 45.5% Vanilla), and 79.3% on ROCT. Under the weaker MSE attack, most methods exceed 90% P@k, confirming that ℓ₂-based attacks cannot effectively disrupt feature rankings. R2ET\H (without the Hessian term) achieves 100% P@k on Bank and 97.5% on Adult, while the full R2ET (with Hessian regularization) excels on high-dimensional inputs like MNIST (85.7%) and CIFAR-10 (75.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Softplus (SP) [16], R2ET variants win on 6 of 8 datasets under ERAttack (e.g., MNIST: 85.7% vs. 62.9%; ADHD: 71.6% vs. 42.5%), while SP narrowly edges out on COMPAS (99.5% vs. 92.0%) and is competitive on Adult (97.4% vs. 97.5% for R2ET\H). Against Hessian-based methods (Est-H, Exact-H, WD) [17], R2ET clearly dominates on high-dimensional inputs: on MNIST, R2ET achieves 85.7% vs. Est-H's 85.2% and Vanilla's 59.0%, and on CIFAR-10, R2ET-mm reaches 78.0% vs. Est-H's 77.1% and Vanilla's 66.5%. Against Fast-AT [92], R2ET is consistently superior and more stable: AT drops to 68.4% on Adult and 56.0% on MNIST, while R2ET\H and R2ET maintain 97.5% and 85.7% respectively. Against SSR [88], R2ET outperforms on all tabular datasets (e.g., Adult: 97.5% vs. 91.2%; Bank: 100.0% vs. 76.3%). The paper also shows that R2ET generalizes across explanation methods: when evaluated with SmoothGrad or Integrated Gradients (Table 2), R2ET still achieves the highest P@k, e.g., 99.3% (SG) and 54.0% (IG) on Adult, outperforming all baselines under the same explanation method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of curvature-smoothing regularizers for robust explanations (Dombrowski et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2020) by adding a gap-maximization term that directly targets top-k feature ranking stability, and by providing a certified robustness guarantee via the connection to formal robustness bounds (Hein & Andriushchenko, 2017). It departs from prior adversarial-training-based approaches (Sarkar et al., 2021; Singh et al., 2020) by showing theoretical equivalence to AT (building on Xu et al., 2009) while requiring only 2 extra backward passes instead of iterative PGD-style searches. The introduction of 'ranking explanation thickness' as a metric explicitly addresses the gap between ℓp-based stability measures and the top-k features that humans actually perceive, positioning the work at the intersection of ranking robustness in information retrieval and explanation robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The certified robustness guarantee (Prop. 4.5) that a single regularizer obstructs all attacks—rather than playing an arms race against specific attack families—is a practically significant simplification for deploying explainable models in regulated settings. The efficiency claim (2 backward passes vs. ~40 PGD iterations per sample) makes robust explanation training feasible at scale. However, the analysis is restricted to gradient-based explanation methods, the surrogate loss of thickness (rather than exact thickness) limits the guarantee, and the method does not uniformly outperform its ablation (R2ET\H) across all datasets, leaving open whether the Hessian term provides consistent benefit. Future work on concept-based and non-gradient explanations, as well as noisy/variable data regimes, is needed to establish broader applicability.
