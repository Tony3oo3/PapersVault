## Problem

When a model must jointly select a feature map from a large candidate collection and learn a linear predictor on top of it, classical uniform-convergence analyses predict that the excess risk of ERM is governed by the size of the full model class. This paper shows that prediction is wrong: the excess risk of ERM depends only on the complexity of the set of optimal feature maps, not on the global complexity of the candidate set, and quantifies how this localization effect emerges as a function of sample size.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. samples (X_i, Y_i) and a collection of feature maps (φ_t)_{t∈T} with φ_t: X → R^d, and outputs a pair (t̂_n, ŵ_n) obtained by minimizing the empirical square loss over the union of linear classes ∪_{t∈T}{x ↦ ⟨w, φ_t(x)⟩}. The theoretical output is an excess risk bound of the form 24·(nδ)^{-1}·E[sup_{s∈S_{n,δ,k}} G_n²(s)], where S_{n,δ,k} is a data-dependent shrinking subset of T that converges to the optimal set T_* as n grows, making the bound's dependence on |T| decay monotonically with sample size.

## Method

The paper establishes two complementary results. Asymptotically (Theorem 3), under Glivenko-Cantelli and Donsker conditions on three T-indexed empirical processes (Λ_n, Δ_n, G_n), the quantiles of the rescaled excess risk of any ERM sequence are sandwiched between those of the infimum and supremum of a limiting Gaussian process restricted to T_*, matching the oracle rate up to a factor of two. Non-asymptotically (Theorem 4), the authors apply Koltchinskii's iterative localization: starting from a crude bound involving sup_{t∈T} G_n²(t), they iteratively restrict the index set via a contraction map F_{n,δ} whose fixed point is T_*, yielding after k iterations a bound depending only on the shrinking set S_{n,δ,k}. For finite T (Corollary 2), explicit moment-based estimates via Bousquet's inequality and a one-sided Matrix Bernstein bound make the sample size requirement and excess risk fully computable, with the key parameter being the size of sublevel sets of the suboptimality function t ↦ R(t, w_*(t)) − R_*. An application to best subset selection in sparse linear regression recovers the oracle rate σ²s/n·a_n with a_n → 1, improving on prior results that carry a persistent log(d/s) factor.

## Results

The paper establishes that asymptotically (Theorem 3), when the index set T is not too large and there is a unique optimal feature map, the quantiles of the excess risk of ERM coincide, up to a factor of two, with those of the oracle procedure that knows the optimal feature map a priori. Non-asymptotically (Theorem 4), the excess risk is bounded by 24·(nδ)⁻¹·E[sup_{s∈S_{n,δ,k}} G_n²(s)], where the set S_{n,δ,k} shrinks to the set of optimal feature maps T_* as n→∞, transparently showing the decaying effect of global complexity. For the sparse linear regression application (Example 1), the excess risk of best subset selection is bounded by (σ²s/n)·a_n where a_n is a decreasing sequence converging to 1, improving upon the classical σ²s·log(d/s)/n rate for moderate n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or empirical comparisons. The key theoretical comparison is against classical uniform-convergence-based analyses, which would predict that ERM performance is governed by the full complexity of the model class ∪_{t∈T}{x↦⟨w,φ_t(x)⟩}. The paper refutes this, showing that asymptotically the global complexity of T is irrelevant and only the complexity of the optimal subset T_* matters. For the sparse linear regression application, the paper's non-asymptotic bound improves upon Raskutti et al. [RWY11]'s minimax rate of σ²s·log(d/s)/n by replacing the log(d/s) factor with a distribution-dependent constant a_n that decays to 1 as n→∞, though the authors note the s·log(d/s) factor in their sample size restriction is likely suboptimal. Compared to Shen et al. [She+13], the paper works in a random-design setting, provides excess risk (not in-sample risk) guarantees, and does not require n,d→∞ simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the random-design linear regression literature (Oliveira, Lecué–Mendelson, Mourtada, El Hanchi–Erdogdu) from a single feature map to a collection of feature maps, studying ERM over the union of the induced linear classes. The technical toolkit draws on Koltchinskii's iterative localization method, Bousquet's concentration inequality, and Tropp's matrix concentration inequalities, all previously applied to the singleton-index case. The key conceptual departure is from classical uniform-convergence-based analyses, which would predict that ERM performance is governed by the size of the full model class; the paper shows this prediction is wrong in a strong sense for this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that the global complexity of the feature map collection is asymptotically irrelevant to ERM's excess risk, which depends only on the complexity of the optimal feature maps—provides a theoretical mechanism for why feature-learning models can generalize well despite enormous model class size, directly speaking to the deep learning generalization paradox highlighted by Zhang et al. (2021). Open questions include specializing the results to infinite collections of feature maps used in practice (e.g., neural network layers), determining whether ERM is minimax optimal for these non-convex model classes, and formally connecting the theory to the observed behavior of trained neural networks. The sparse linear regression application (best subset selection) yields new non-asymptotic guarantees under general assumptions, improving on prior results that required stronger distributional assumptions or studied in-sample risk.
