## Problem

Differential privacy (DP) is widely applied to fine-tuning but rarely to pre-training, because DP optimization during pre-training suffers from severe performance degradation (e.g., CIFAR-10 accuracy drops from >95% to <70% at ε=8). The paper identifies the root cause through a Hessian-based analysis: a 'decelerator' term coupling DP noise, per-sample gradient clipping, and loss curvature is disproportionately large during pre-training, making DP pre-training far slower than its non-DP counterpart. No prior work provided a theoretical explanation for this asymmetry or a practical strategy to close the utility gap.

## Input / Output

For vision, the method consumes 224×224 RGB images: 1.3M public ImageNet-1k images for self-supervised (DINO) pre-training, followed by 11M private ImageNet-11k images for DP supervised continual pre-training, under a fixed privacy budget (ε, δ) and computation budget (total samples S = B·T). For language, it uses CodeParrot (public) and E2E (private) text sequences. The output is a pre-trained ViT-Base or GPT2 model whose weights carry a formal (ε, δ)-DP guarantee on the private data, usable for downstream classification, linear probing, or few-shot learning without additional privacy cost.

## Method

The paper derives a Hessian-based per-iteration loss improvement formula for DP-SGD, identifying a decelerator term σ²tr(H)/(Bc²) that quantifies how DP noise slows convergence relative to standard SGD. This term is large during pre-training (where tr(H) is high) but small during fine-tuning, explaining why DP fine-tuning is nearly lossless while DP pre-training is not. Building on this, the authors propose a two-phase DP continual pre-training strategy: first, standard self-supervised training on a small public dataset (e.g., ImageNet-1k via DINO) for sT steps, then DP supervised training on the larger private dataset (e.g., ImageNet-11k) for (1−s)T steps, with the switch point determined automatically by early stopping. The optimal public-to-private mixing ratio α* is derived in closed form, confirming that even ~10% public data significantly mitigates the decelerator. Implementation uses DP-AdamW with automatic per-sample clipping, DP-ZeRO for distributed training, and the improved Rényi accountant for noise calibration, achieving 41.5% upstream accuracy on ImageNet-11k at ε=8 with only 0.7B total images.

## Results

The DP continual pre-training strategy achieves 41.5% upstream accuracy on ImageNet-11k under ε=8, using only 0.7B total images (10% public + 90% private). On large-scale downstream linear probing, the ε=8 full-parameter fine-tuned model reaches 55.7% on Places365 (1.8M images) and 60.0% on iNaturalist-2021 (2.7M images), matching or exceeding non-DP SOTA trained on 2.7–4B images. In few-shot settings, the model achieves 65.26% on CIFAR100 10-shot (ε=8), substantially outperforming prior DP pre-trained models. Standard fine-tuning accuracy reaches 98.4% on CIFAR-10 and 90.2% on CIFAR-100 (ε=8 model).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DINO [19] (the non-DP self-supervised initialization), the DP continual pre-training improves standard fine-tuning accuracy by +0.3–2.0% across CIFAR-10/100, Food101, and SVHN (Table 5), and by +0.9–5.6% on large-scale linear probing (Table 7). Compared to MIIL [70] (non-DP, 1.4B images), the DP model (0.7B images) is within 0.4–1.2% on standard fine-tuning and 2.8–3.3% on Places365/iNat2021, achieving comparable quality with half the data. Against prior DP pre-trained models TAN [71] and ViP [90], the gains are dramatic: +19–38% on CIFAR100 few-shot (Table 6), +9.6% on Places365, and +21.9% on iNat2021 (Table 7), which the authors attribute to the higher quality of ImageNet-1k/11k data versus Shaders/LAION. On ImageNet-1k linear probing, the ε=8 full model (78.5%) slightly exceeds AugReg [74] (77.9% at 4.3B images) and ViT_base [27] (77.9% at 1.2B images) while using only 0.7B images. Against NFnet-JFT [24] (ResNet-50, 4.0B JFT images), the DP ViT-Base is competitive on Places365 (55.7% vs 54.5%) but lacks a direct iNat2021 comparison. Overall, the method wins on all DP-vs-DP comparisons and is competitive with non-DP SOTA despite using an order-of-magnitude less total data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DP-SGD framework (Abadi et al.) and automatic per-sample clipping (Bu et al., NeurIPS 2024) to provide a Hessian-based theoretical analysis of DP training convergence, identifying a 'decelerator' term σ²tr(H)/(Bc²) that couples noise, clipping, and loss curvature. It departs from prior DP convergence analyses (which typically study global convergence under convexity or Lipschitz assumptions) by focusing on per-iteration loss improvement, and from prior DP pre-training work (TAN, ViP, NFnet-JFT) by showing that a small amount of public data fundamentally changes the optimization landscape. The practical strategy of DP continual pre-training extends the DINO self-supervised initialization (Caron et al.) and MIIL supervised pre-training (Ridnik et al.) with a privacy-preserving phase, bridging the gap between the well-studied DP fine-tuning literature and the underexplored DP pre-training regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that DP pre-training—long considered infeasible due to severe performance degradation—can achieve accuracy on par with non-DP pre-training using only ~10% public data, with the Hessian-based decelerator providing a principled explanation for why and when this works. This opens the door to privacy-preserving foundation model training at scale, addressing a critical gap where the majority of training data (used in pre-training) previously received no formal privacy protection. Open questions include whether the 10% public data requirement can be further reduced, how the strategy scales to much larger models (beyond ViT-Base/GPT2-Large), and whether the Hessian-based analysis holds under distribution shift between public and private data. The released fastDP library and pre-trained models make the approach immediately accessible to practitioners.
