## Problem

Districting and routing requires partitioning a geographical area into connected districts to minimize expected routing costs, but the problem is NP-hard and intractable even for small instances (e.g., 400 CPU-core days for 60 BUs). Existing heuristics based on BHH cost formulas or GNN-based cost estimators embedded in local search tend to produce sub-optimal solutions on large instances and fail to generalize across cities and problem parameters. The paper asks whether a structured learning approach that integrates a combinatorial optimization layer into a neural network can produce high-quality districting solutions in minutes while generalizing out-of-distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled undirected graph where vertices are basic units (BUs) and edges encode spatial adjacency; each edge's feature vector is the element-wise average of the two endpoint BUs' attributes (population, density, area, perimeter, compactness, distance to depot) augmented with the inter-BU center distance. The GNN outputs a vector of edge weights θ ∈ ℝ^|E| that parameterize a capacitated minimum spanning tree (CMST) instance. The CMST is solved by a black-box solver (exact enumeration for small instances, iterated local search with a modified Kruskal initialization for large ones), and the resulting subtrees are directly converted into a districting partition by collecting all nodes of each subtree into a district.

## Method

DISTRICTNET integrates a parameterized CMST as a differentiable combinatorial optimization layer inside a GNN (3 graph convolution layers + 3 dense layers), exploiting the surjection from CMST solutions to districting solutions so that an optimal CMST always exists for any districting problem. Training is decision-aware: target CMST solutions are constructed from optimal districting solutions on small instances via a randomized Kruskal algorithm, and the model is trained by minimizing a Fenchel-Young loss between the predicted CMST solution and the target. Gradients through the non-smooth CMST layer are computed via perturbation-based stochastic gradients: M random Gaussian perturbations are added to the predicted edge weights, each perturbed CMST is solved exactly, and the averaged solution provides a Monte Carlo estimate of the Fenchel-Young gradient. This avoids the trivial θ=0 solution of the unregularized non-optimality loss and yields a convex, well-defined training objective that the authors show is preferable to the infimum loss used in prior differentiable clustering work.

## Results

On 35 out-of-distribution test instances (7 cities × 5 target district sizes, N=120 BUs), DISTRICTNET achieves a 9.9–11.9% average cost reduction over all four benchmarks, with all differences statistically significant (p < 10⁻⁴). On the largest instance (Ile-de-France, 2,000 BUs), DISTRICTNET still outperforms the best baseline (AvgTSP) by 2.6% and the GNN-based PREDGNN by 4.0%. The method generalizes from training on only 100 small instances (N=30 BUs) to cities up to 60× larger, and produces more compact districts (average Reock score 0.354 vs. 0.254 for BD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISTRICTNET outperforms all four baselines on every test instance, with the largest margins against the GNN-based PREDGNN (11.91% average cost reduction, p=1.5×10⁻¹⁰) and the two BHH-based linear regressions BD and FIG (~10% each). The gap to AvgTSP (4.44%) is smaller but still highly significant, confirming that learning the edge weights via a GNN adds value beyond a simple deterministic TSP approximation. Notably, PREDGNN also uses a GNN but in an unstructured way (predicting a scalar district cost), and its inferior performance highlights the benefit of the structured CMST optimization layer. On the 2,000-BU Ile-de-France instance, DISTRICTNET (cost 2205.7) beats AvgTSP (2262.7, +2.6%), PREDGNN (2295.2, +4.0%), BD (2379.0, +7.8%), and FIG (2388.8, +8.3%). The paper also reports that DISTRICTNET produces more compact districts (Reock score 0.354 vs. 0.254 for BD), suggesting the CMST structure naturally encourages geographically tighter partitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of decision-aware learning and combinatorial optimization, building directly on the Fenchel-Young loss framework (Blondel et al., 2020) and perturbation-based gradient propagation through combinatorial layers (Berthet et al., 2020; Dalle et al., 2022). It departs from the closest prior work on districting (Ferraz et al., 2024), which trains a GNN to predict district costs in a traditional supervised fashion, by instead integrating a capacitated minimum spanning tree (CMST) as a differentiable surrogate optimization layer. The key conceptual departure from differentiable clustering approaches (Wilder et al., 2019; Stewart et al., 2023) is the use of a structured surrogate with explicit size constraints, plus a randomized constructor that maps partially-specified districting targets into CMST moments, yielding a convex Fenchel-Young loss rather than a difference-of-convex infimum loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Districting is a multi-year strategic decision in logistics where even small cost reductions translate to substantial savings, and the demonstrated ~10% improvement over all benchmarks (including GNN-based methods) is practically significant. The ability to generalize from N=30 training instances to N=2000 test instances (60×) with only 100 training examples makes the approach accessible to practitioners who cannot afford expensive optimal-solution generation. The framework is explicitly generic and could extend to other geographical partitioning problems (voting districts, school zoning), though the paper only validates it on districting-and-routing. Open questions include whether exact CMST solvers (e.g., column generation) would further improve solution quality, and whether the approach maintains its advantage when additional constraints (fairness, compactness) are imposed.
