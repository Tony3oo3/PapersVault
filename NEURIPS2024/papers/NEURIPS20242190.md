## Problem

Sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting methods typically rely on external depth priors from pretrained monocular networks to constrain Gaussian geometry, but these priors are noisy and blurry, failing to provide accurate geometric guidance. This paper addresses the gap by eliminating the need for any external prior, instead exploiting self-supervised binocular stereo consistency inherent in the input views themselves to guide Gaussian placement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242190/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3, 6, or 9 sparse RGB images (forward-facing LLFF/DTU scenes) or 8 images (360° Blender scenes) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, downsampled by factors of 8, 4, and 2 respectively. For forward-facing scenes, a pretrained PDC-Net+ matching network is used solely for generating a dense initialization point cloud via triangulation; no external depth or geometry priors are used during optimization. The output is a compact 3D Gaussian representation (approximately one-third the storage of vanilla 3DGS) that renders novel view images and depth maps at real-time speed.

## Method

The core mechanism constructs virtual binocular stereo pairs by translating an input view's camera position by a small random offset (up to 0.4 units) along the horizontal axis, rendering a 'right' image and depth from 3DGS at that shifted position. The rendered depth is converted to disparity via d = f·d_cam/D, and the right image is warped to the left viewpoint using a differentiable bilinear sampler; an L1 consistency loss between the warped image and the original input enforces geometrically correct depth. A second key component is an opacity decay strategy (λ=0.995) that multiplicatively penalizes Gaussian opacity each iteration, causing Gaussians far from surfaces (with low opacity gradients) to be pruned while surface-aligned Gaussians (with high gradients) are retained. The binocular consistency loss is introduced only after 20,000 iterations (LLFF/DTU) or 4,000 iterations (Blender) to ensure training views are already well-reconstructed before the geometric constraint activates. The total loss combines the binocular consistency term with the standard 3DGS color reconstruction loss (L1 + D-SSIM).

## Results

On LLFF with 3 input views, the method achieves PSNR 21.44, SSIM 0.751, and LPIPS 0.168, outperforming all NeRF-based and 3DGS-based baselines. With 9 views, performance improves to PSNR 26.17, SSIM 0.877, and LPIPS 0.090. On DTU (9 views), the method reaches PSNR 26.70, SSIM 0.947, and LPIPS 0.052, and on Blender (8 views) achieves PSNR 24.71, SSIM 0.872, and LPIPS 0.101. The method also reduces Gaussian count to roughly one-third of original 3DGS, improving both rendering quality and optimization efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LLFF, the method outperforms all nine baselines across every metric and view setting; the closest competitor is ReconFusion (PSNR 21.34 vs. 21.44 at 3 views) and FSGS (PSNR 25.32 vs. 26.17 at 9 views), with the gap widening as fewer views are available. On DTU, MuRF slightly edges out the proposed method at 3 views (PSNR 21.31 vs. 20.71), but the method dominates at 6 and 9 views (PSNR 24.31 and 26.70 vs. MuRF's 23.74 and 25.28). On Blender (8 views), the method achieves the best PSNR (24.71) but trails DNGaussian on SSIM (0.872 vs. 0.886) and LPIPS (0.101 vs. 0.088), indicating comparable but not strictly superior performance on 360-degree scenes. The depth-prior-based 3DGS methods (FSGS, DNGaussian) show degraded geometry quality in depth maps, while the proposed binocular consistency loss yields visibly sharper depth estimation without relying on external priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as its 3D representation and rendering backbone, but departs from the dominant strategy in sparse-view 3DGS methods (FSGS, DNGaussian, SparseGS, CoherentGS) of relying on external depth priors from pretrained monocular depth networks. Instead, it replaces these noisy external priors with a self-supervised binocular stereo consistency constraint derived from slight camera translations, drawing on the geometric principles of classical stereo vision. The opacity decay strategy is positioned as an alternative to SuGaR's entropy regularization for pruning off-surface Gaussians, and the dense initialization via PDC-Net+ keypoint matching extends the standard SfM-based sparse initialization used in prior 3DGS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple self-supervised geometric constraint (binocular disparity warping) can outperform methods that depend on pretrained depth estimation networks, reducing the dependency on potentially noisy external models and offering a cleaner training pipeline. The opacity decay strategy is a lightweight, easily adoptable regularization that reduces Gaussian count to roughly one-third of standard 3DGS while improving quality. However, the method has a clear limitation with low-texture regions (e.g., white backgrounds in DTU) where the binocular constraint provides insufficient signal, and performance on 360-degree scenes (Blender) is only comparable rather than superior to depth-prior-based methods. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, larger translation ranges, or can be combined with generative priors for even sparser inputs.
