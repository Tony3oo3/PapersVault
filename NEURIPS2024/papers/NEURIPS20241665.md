## Problem

Existing user representations in recommender systems—single-point (SUR) and multi-point (MUR)—struggle to capture diverse, dynamic user interests: SUR collapses multiple interests into one vector, while MUR requires a manually chosen number of interest points K and provides no principled uncertainty estimate. This limits both retrieval coverage across a user's full interest spectrum and the ability to balance exploration and exploitation in online settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user's interaction history (capped at 60–160 items depending on dataset) and a fixed, pre-trained item embedding matrix (dimension d, typically 32–256) as input. It produces a top-N retrieval list ranked via Thompson sampling or UCB over the GPR posterior, along with a per-item posterior mean (relevance score) and variance (uncertainty) that can be consumed by downstream ranking or bandit policies. Item embeddings are pre-trained via extreme multi-class classification with an auxiliary category-alignment loss, then frozen during GPR fitting.

## Method

GPR4DUR replaces point-based user representations with a density-based one: for each user, a Gaussian process regressor is fitted with item embeddings as inputs and binary interaction indicators as observations, yielding a personalized posterior g_u(v) over the entire item embedding space. The GP mean serves as a relevance score and the GP variance as an uncertainty estimate, enabling retrieval via Thompson sampling (posterior sampling) or UCB (mean + β·std). Because GPs are non-parametric, the number of 'interests' is inferred automatically from the data rather than set to a fixed K, and the RBF kernel with a dataset-specific length-scale σ is tuned on a holdout set. Item embeddings are pre-trained jointly for user-item and item-category likelihoods (Eq. 5), ensuring the embedding space respects category structure so that density peaks align with distinct interest regions.

## Results

On the retrieval task, GPR4DUR achieves the best Interest Coverage (IC@50) on all three datasets: 0.895 (Amazon), 0.974 (MovieLens), and 0.601 (Taobao), and the best Interest Relevance (IR@50) on all three: 0.560, 0.862, and 0.624 respectively. On the ranking task (top-50), GPR4DUR achieves the highest Recall (0.908 Amazon, 0.906 Taobao) and nDCG (0.922 Amazon, 0.936 Taobao), while being competitive on MovieLens (Recall 0.730, nDCG 0.768). For exposure balance, GPR4DUR attains the best Tail Exposure Improvement (TEI@50) in 8 of 9 cases across datasets, though all TEI values remain negative, indicating no method fully overcomes popularity bias for niche categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPR4DUR outperforms all 12 baselines on Interest Relevance (IR@50) across all three datasets, with the largest margins over MUR methods like MIND (0.503 vs 0.560 on Amazon) and ComiRec (0.510 vs 0.560). On Interest Coverage, GPR4DUR leads on Amazon (0.895 vs REMI's 0.833) and Taobao (0.601 vs PIMI's 0.579), while on MovieLens it is competitive with MostPop (0.974 vs 0.973) and slightly trails it. For exposure balance, GPR4DUR achieves the lowest Exposure Deviation on all three datasets (0.423, 0.222, 0.367) and the best Tail Exposure Improvement in 8 of 9 k-values, though all TEI values are negative, confirming that no method—including GPR4DUR—fully reverses popularity bias for niche categories. On the ranking task (Table 2), GPR4DUR achieves the best Recall@50 and nDCG@50 on Amazon (0.908/0.922) and Taobao (0.906/0.936), and is within 0.001–0.003 of the best on MovieLens. In the online simulation (Table 3), uncertainty-aware policies (UCB, Thompson sampling) built on GPR4DUR's posterior consistently outperform greedy and random policies, with Thompson sampling reaching 0.98 cumulative interest coverage by iteration 10 versus 0.92 for Greedy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant single-point (SUR) and multi-point (MUR) user representations in recommender systems by introducing a density-based user representation (DUR) built on Gaussian Process Regression. While MUR methods (MIND, ComiRec, CAMI, PIMI, REMI) require manual selection of a fixed number of interest points K per user, GPR4DUR leverages the non-parametric nature of GPs to adapt the number of interests automatically and provides principled uncertainty quantification via posterior variance. The method also connects to bandit algorithms (UCB, Thompson Sampling) for exploration-exploitation trade-offs in online settings, and uses extreme multi-class classification with an auxiliary category-alignment loss for item embedding pre-training, building on the PinnerSage framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a gap in evaluation by proposing four new metrics (Interest Coverage, Interest Relevance, Exposure Deviation, Tail Exposure Improvement) specifically designed for multi-interest retrieval, arguing that standard precision/recall metrics are inadequate for assessing whether a retrieval list covers a user's full range of interests. A notable open question is that all TEI values are negative across every method, indicating that no current approach actually improves niche category exposure relative to user holdout sets—a limitation the authors attribute to inherent popularity bias. The O(ℓ_u³) training complexity of GPR remains a practical barrier for very long interaction histories, and the authors note that collaborative GP extensions and scalable GP approximations are promising but unexplored directions.
