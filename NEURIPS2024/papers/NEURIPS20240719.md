## Problem

Prior work on validity-constrained distribution learning established pessimistic worst-case bounds: proper learners require exponentially many validity queries, and the best improper algorithm uses Õ(log|Q|/ε₁²ε₂) queries. These results are driven by generality—model class mismatch, arbitrary bounded losses, and no structure on the validity function. This paper asks: in more realistic regimes (realizability, log-loss, VC-structured validity), how much cheaper can validity guarantees be?

## Input / Output

The learner receives i.i.d. samples from a fully-valid data distribution P (all samples satisfy v(x)=1) and a finite model class Q of density distributions, and may make adaptive validity queries to an unknown binary function v. The output is a probability distribution q̂ (improper, i.e. not necessarily in Q) whose log-loss is within ε₁ of the best fully-valid model in Q and whose invalidity I(q̂) ≤ ε₂. A key structural feature is that the learner can choose which distributions to sample from when estimating v, enabling targeted validity estimation on the ERM model's own support.

## Method

The paper provides two complementary results. First, under realizability (P ∈ Q) with the log-loss, standard ERM requires zero validity queries and achieves both loss and validity guarantees with Õ(log|Q|/min(ε₁², ε₂)) samples; the key insight is that any model with invalidity > ε₂ must place mass where P has none, creating a TV gap detectable via the Neyman-Pearson lemma on product measures. To obtain proper log-loss guarantees (not just TV bounds), Algorithm 1 mixes the ERM output with the uniform distribution at weight O(min(ε₁,ε₂)), which preserves validity while ensuring full support. Second, when v belongs to a VC-class of dimension D, Algorithm 2 estimates v on samples drawn from both P and the ERM model, then restricts the ERM density to the estimated valid region and renormalizes; this post-hoc restriction uses only Õ(D/ε₂) validity queries (or Õ(D/(ε₁ε₂)) under capped log-loss without a validity lower bound), decoupling query complexity from ε₁ and improving over the prior Õ(log|Q|/ε₁²ε₂) bound.

## Results

Under the realizability assumption P ∈ Q with the log-loss, Algorithm 1 (mixing ERM with the uniform distribution) requires Õ(log²(1/min(ε₁,ε₂,α)) · (log|Q| + log(1/δ)) / min(ε₁², ε₂)) samples and zero validity queries to guarantee L_P(q̂) ≤ L_P(q*) + ε₁ and I(q̂) ≤ ε₂ (Theorem 1). A matching lower bound (Theorem 2) shows the 1/ε₂ dependence is tight for proper learners. When the validity function v lies in a VC-class of dimension D with V(q) ≥ γ > 0, Algorithm 2 reduces the validity query complexity to O(D log(1/(γε₂)) / (γε₂)) (Theorem 3), and under the capped log-loss the positive-validity requirement can be dropped at the cost of an extra 1/ε₁ factor (Theorem 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole prior theoretical baseline is the general-purpose improper algorithm of Hanneke et al. [3], which requires Õ(log|Q| / (ε₁² ε₂)) validity queries for arbitrary bounded decreasing losses and arbitrary model classes. The paper's Theorem 3 reduces this to Õ(D / (γ ε₂)) queries when the validity function belongs to a VC-class of dimension D and models have validity at least γ. In the regime where D = Θ(log|Q|) and γ ≥ Ω(ε₁), this represents a polynomial improvement in the query complexity, decoupling the query cost from ε₁ entirely. Theorem 1 goes further in the realizable log-loss setting, eliminating validity queries altogether at the cost of a polylogarithmic factor in the sample complexity. The paper also establishes a lower bound (Theorem 2) confirming that the 1/ε₂ sample dependence for proper learners is unavoidable, anchoring the tightness of the upper bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Hanneke et al. (2018), who formulated validity-constrained distribution learning and showed that proper learners require exponentially many validity queries in the worst case, while their improper algorithm uses Õ(log|Q|/(ε₁²ε₂)) queries. The present work departs from that worst-case framing by introducing structural assumptions—realizability (P ∈ Q) with the log-loss, or VC-class structure on the validity function—that yield substantially tighter sample and query complexities. The analysis draws on the Neyman-Pearson lemma, product-measure total variation bounds, and proper scoring rule theory, positioning the log-loss's unique strict properness as the key mechanism enabling validity guarantees without explicit validity queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight—that under realizability with the log-loss, zero validity queries suffice and the sample complexity depends on ε₂ only linearly (not quadratically)—provides a theoretical justification for why maximum-likelihood training of well-specified generative models naturally produces valid outputs, a phenomenon observed empirically but previously unexplained. The VC-class result shows that when the validity region has bounded complexity, a small number of targeted queries can replace the large query budget of the general-purpose algorithm. Open questions include extending these results to the agnostic case (P ∉ Q), characterizing the role of the log-loss more broadly, and understanding whether the polylogarithmic factors in the sample complexity are inherent. The finite-cardinality assumption on Q limits direct practical applicability, though the intuition about log-loss and validity is broadly relevant.
