## Problem

Human-based Chatbot Arena evaluation is expensive, slow, and limited in scale, while most models cannot participate in online arenas due to priority constraints, making it impossible to directly guide target model development. Additionally, manually curating high-quality post-training data that targets a model's specific weaknesses is resource-intensive and does not scale. The paper asks whether a fully AI-powered offline arena can both reliably replicate human arena rankings and generate training data that iteratively improves a target model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243542/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 276K refined multi-turn instruction corpus (sourced from Alpaca, FLAN, LMSYS-Chat-1M, OpenOrca, and WizardLM, deduplicated via MinHashLSH and filtered for benchmark leakage) split into nine ~30K slices, plus responses generated by three SOTA reference models (Command R+, Qwen1.5-72B-Chat, OpenChat-3.5) and the target model on each slice. The Llama3-70B-Chat judge scores each response pair on a 1–10 scale using a two-game position-swap protocol. Outputs include Elo rankings via Bradley-Terry with 100 bootstraps (95% CI from 2.5th–97.5th percentile), SFT targets (winning responses with score gap > 1.0), DPO choice/reject pairs, PPO reward-model pairs, and the final post-trained Mistral-7B model across three iterative loops.

## Method

WizardArena replaces human annotators with a prompted Llama3-70B-Chat judge that scores paired model responses, validated at 92.14% average consistency with GPT-4 and 92.80% Spearman/human-agreement/differentiation alignment with the live LMSYS ChatBot Arena. Arena Learning then runs an iterative battle-and-train loop: the target model (Mistral-7B-based WizardLM-β) is pitted against top-ranked SOTA models on successive data slices, and the judge's verdicts produce training signals tailored to each alignment stage—SFT fine-tunes on the winning model's responses where the target lost, DPO treats win/loss as choice/reject pairs, and PPO trains a reward model on the same pairs before policy optimization. The pair-judge data selection strategy outperforms random sampling, K-Means clustering, IFD, and INSTAG by 11–22 Elo points because it explicitly targets instructions where the base model underperforms. Across three iterations (I₀→I₃), the model's WizardArena-Mix Elo rises from 875 to 1274 (+399) and MT-Bench from 6.41 to 7.81 (+1.4), with the final 7B model approaching Qwen1.5-32B-Chat performance.

## Results

WizardArena-Mix achieves 92.80% average consistency with the LMSYS ChatBot Arena (Spearman 97.76%, Human Agreement 98.98%, Differentiation 81.67%), substantially outperforming MT-Bench (33.02%) and slightly exceeding Arena-Hard-v1.0 (91.57%). The best post-trained model, Wizard-β-PPO-I3 (7B), reaches 1274 ELO on WizardArena-Mix and 7.81 on MT-Bench, approaching Qwen1.5-32B-Chat (1288 ELO, 8.30 MT-Bench) while outperforming all other 7B models. Across three iterative training loops, the WizardArena ELO score increases from 875 (SFT-I0) to 1274 (PPO-I3), a 399-point gain, and MT-Bench rises from 6.41 to 7.81 (+1.4 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The 7B Wizard-β-PPO-I3 model (1274 ELO on WizardArena-Mix) outperforms all other 7B baselines: Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1 (895), Vicuna-13B (929), Zephyr-7B-alpha (943), and GPT-3.5-Turbo-0613 (984), and even surpasses the 70B WizardLM-70B-v1.0 (1172) by 102 ELO points. It approaches Qwen1.5-32B-Chat (1288) within 14 ELO points and trails Command R+ (1340) by 66 points. On MT-Bench, Wizard-β-PPO-I3 (7.81) matches OpenChat-3.5 (7.80) and exceeds WizardLM-70B-v1.0 (7.71), though it falls short of Qwen1.5-72B-Chat (8.61) and DeepSeek-LLM-67B-Chat (8.70). The WizardArena evaluation itself shows 92.80% consistency with LMSYS ChatBot Arena, outperforming MT-Bench (33.02%) by 59.78 percentage points and exceeding Arena-Hard-v1.0 (91.57%) by 1.23 points. The pair-judge data selection strategy yields 22 ELO points improvement over the full 30K original dataset and 11 points over INSTAG on the same 10K budget. Llama3-70B-Chat as judge achieves 92.14% average consistency with GPT-4 as judge, validating the use of open-source models for cost-effective evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LMSYS ChatBot Arena paradigm (Zheng et al., 2024) by replacing human evaluators with an open-source LLM judge (Llama3-70B-Chat) and the Bradley-Terry model (Fageot et al., 2023) for score estimation, creating a fully offline, AI-powered arena. It departs from prior LLM-as-judge work (which typically relies on GPT-4) by demonstrating that a 70B open-source model achieves 92%+ consistency with both GPT-4 and human judgments. The Arena Learning strategy is a novel synthesis of the WizardLM data-selection lineage (Xu et al., 2023) with iterative preference learning: rather than one-shot data curation, the target model repeatedly battles SOTA models, and losses are harvested as SFT/DPO/PPO training signal in a closed-loop flywheel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: WizardArena provides a low-cost, repeatable evaluation pipeline that closely tracks the human LMSYS Arena (92.80% average consistency), and Arena Learning offers a scalable data-generation loop that eliminates the need for manual annotation at each training iteration. However, the evidence is confined to a single 7B base model (Mistral-7B) and English-only data, leaving open whether the iterative arena loop generalizes to larger models, multilingual settings, or domains where the judge model's own biases are more pronounced. A key open question is whether the judge model's systematic preferences (e.g., favoring longer or more structured responses) propagate into the trained model over multiple iterations, potentially creating a feedback loop that narrows rather than broadens capability.
