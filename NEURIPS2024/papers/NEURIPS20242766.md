## Problem

Existing ontology learning pipelines decompose the task into isolated subtasks (concept discovery, relation extraction) that fail to capture interactions between them and do not directly reflect the quality of the final ontology. This paper asks whether an LLM can be finetuned to model entire subcomponents of a target ontology end-to-end, producing a complete taxonomic graph from a corpus of annotated documents at practical scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242766/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of documents (Wikipedia article titles with summaries, or arXiv paper titles with abstracts), each annotated with one or more concepts from the ground truth ontology. For each document, the model generates a set of root-to-concept paths of bounded length N (N=4 for Wikipedia, N=3 for arXiv) that linearise the relevant subgraph. The final output is a single directed graph whose nodes are concepts and edges are taxonomic (is-a) relations, obtained by summing all per-document subgraphs and applying a multi-stage pruning pipeline (self-loop removal, inverse-edge resolution, absolute and relative weight thresholding, and orphan-node cleanup).

## Method

OLLM finetunes Mistral 7B via LoRA to autoregressively generate path-based linearisations of ontology subgraphs, rather than predicting individual edges. The key training innovation is a frequency-aware masked loss: when a relation u→v appears n times in the training set, the loss on v's tokens is masked with probability max(1−M/n, 0), capping the effective exposure of high-frequency relations to ≈M occurrences and preventing the model from overfitting on top-level concepts. At inference, the model generates subgraphs for every document in the corpus; these are summed into a weighted graph and pruned via hyperparameter-tuned absolute (α-quantile) and relative (top-p-style cumulative weight) thresholding. For domain transfer, the Wikipedia-trained LoRA weights are merged and new low-rank adapters (r=8) are trained on only 2048 arXiv document-subgraph pairs, enabling adaptation to a new ontology with minimal data.

## Results

On Wikipedia, OLLM achieves the best Fuzzy F1 (0.915), Continuous F1 (0.500), and Graph F1 (0.644) among all methods, outperforming the next-best Finetune baseline (0.884, 0.470, 0.588) and prompting baselines (≤0.888, ≤0.477, ≤0.639). On arXiv, OLLM (transfer) leads with Fuzzy F1 0.570, Continuous F1 0.357, and Graph F1 0.633, significantly exceeding the next-best methods (0.460, 0.290, 0.546). OLLM is competitive on Motif Distance (0.080 on Wikipedia, 0.097 on arXiv) but trails the Memorisation baseline on this purely structural metric. The method requires only 12 A100-hours for training and 7 A100-hours for inference on Wikipedia, demonstrating practical scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Hearst patterns (Roller et al., 2018), OLLM substantially outperforms on all semantic metrics: on Wikipedia, OLLM achieves Fuzzy F1 0.915 vs. 0.538, Continuous F1 0.500 vs. 0.350, and Graph F1 0.644 vs. 0.544; on arXiv the gap widens dramatically (Fuzzy F1 0.570 vs. 0.000, Continuous F1 0.357 vs. 0.151). Against REBEL (Cabot & Navigli, 2021), OLLM similarly dominates: on Wikipedia, Fuzzy F1 0.915 vs. 0.624, Continuous F1 0.500 vs. 0.356, and Graph F1 0.644 vs. 0.072; on arXiv, Fuzzy F1 0.570 vs. 0.060 and Continuous F1 0.357 vs. 0.281. Against LLMs4OL (Babaei Giglou et al., 2023), which uses a subtask composition approach (concept discovery + link prediction), OLLM's zero-shot end-to-end modelling outperforms on Fuzzy F1 (0.871 vs. 0.841), Continuous F1 (0.455 vs. 0.428), and Graph F1 (0.639 vs. 0.482) on Wikipedia, while LLMs4OL only wins on Motif Distance (0.092 vs. 0.341). Notably, OLLM loses to the Memorisation baseline on Literal F1 (0.093 vs. 0.134 on Wikipedia), which the authors attribute to this metric's sensitivity to surface-level syntactic differences rather than semantic content.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of ontology learning as a composition of subtasks (concept discovery followed by relation extraction via Hearst patterns or REBEL-style extraction) by reframing the problem as end-to-end subgraph generation with a finetuned LLM. It builds on the observation that LLMs can solve individual OL subtasks (Babaei Giglou et al., Funk et al., Trajanoska et al.) but argues that modelling entire subcomponents captures higher-order interactions that subtask pipelines miss. The custom masked-loss regulariser is a novel training objective specific to this subgraph-modelling formulation, and the evaluation metrics repurpose pretrained sentence embedders and graph convolutions in a way not previously applied to ontology comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first scalable, end-to-end method for ontology construction that outperforms traditional extraction pipelines even when those pipelines are given ground-truth concepts, and it introduces a reusable suite of semantic and structural similarity metrics for ontology evaluation. The data-efficient domain transfer to arXiv (2048 examples) suggests the approach could bootstrap ontologies for new domains with minimal annotation. Key limitations include restriction to taxonomic relations only, no guarantee of acyclicity, and inability to fully rule out pretraining data contamination; extending to non-taxonomic relations, axioms, and more complex ontology formalisms remains open.
