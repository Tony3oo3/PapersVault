## Problem

Prior crystal language models use CIF file text as sequence representations, which fail to guarantee SE(3) and periodic invariance and produce infinitely many distinct sequences for the same crystal structure. This violates the uniqueness criterion, forcing models to rely on extensive data augmentation to recognize equivalent descriptions, and fundamentally limits their ability to learn a consistent distribution over crystal structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243970/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a chemical composition string (e.g., 'Eu2FeGe2OS6') or a property token (e.g., a discretized band gap interval) as the conditioning prefix, and autoregressively generates a 1D Mat2Seq token sequence that encodes space group symbol, lattice parameters (a, b, c, α, β, γ), and irreducible atom sets with fractional coordinates rounded to four decimal places. The generated sequence is then decoded back into a full 3D crystal structure by applying the recorded space group symmetry operations to the irreducible atom set and reconstructing Cartesian positions from the lattice matrix.

## Method

Mat2Seq converts 3D crystal structures into provably unique 1D sequences in two stages: first, it determines an SO(3)-equivariant and periodic-invariant primitive unit cell by applying Niggli cell reduction to the lattice vectors and selecting a canonical origin atom via atomic number, E(3)-invariant local densities, and SE(3)-invariant densities along each lattice vector; second, it encodes the resulting unit cell as an SE(3)-invariant sequence of space group symbol, six lattice parameters, and irreducible atom types with fractional coordinates (which are invariant because P = L·P_frac). The paper proves that this mapping is unique, SE(3)-invariant, periodic-invariant, and complete (Lemma 1–3). A GPT-2 model (16 layers, 16 heads, 1024-dim embeddings) is trained with standard next-token prediction cross-entropy loss on these sequences, and conditional generation is achieved by prepending composition or property tokens to the sequence prefix during both training and sampling.

## Results

Mat2Seq achieves 100% uniqueness on the MP20 dataset (vs. 0% for Uniqueness-MP20 and 30% for CIF-based methods), confirming that all equivalent crystal descriptions map to a single sequence. On the most challenging MPTS-52 dataset (up to 52 atoms per cell), Mat2Seq achieves 23.1% match rate and 0.109 RMSE in one-shot generation, and 36.4% match rate and 0.088 RMSE in 20-shot generation, outperforming all baselines. On experimentally observed crystal structures in MP20, Mat2Seq reaches 65.2% match rate with 0.042 RMSE. For conditional band-gap generation, 83.6% of generated crystals have band gap < 0.5 eV and 90.7% have band gap > 3.0 eV when conditioned accordingly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CrystaLLM (CIF-based LM), Mat2Seq wins on match rate across all eight dataset-setting combinations, with the largest gap on MPTS-52 one-shot (23.1% vs. 19.2%) and MP20 one-shot (61.3% vs. 58.7%). Against DiffCSP (equivariant diffusion), Mat2Seq achieves an 89% relative improvement in one-shot match rate on MPTS-52 (23.1% vs. 12.2%) and a 50% reduction in 20-shot RMSE (0.088 vs. 0.175), while DiffCSP slightly edges out Mat2Seq on Perov-5 20-shot RMSE (0.013 vs. 0.023). Against CDVAE, Mat2Seq dominates on all datasets, with the most dramatic gap on MPTS-52 one-shot (23.1% vs. 5.34% match rate). Against FlowMM (Riemannian flow matching, Appendix), Mat2Seq achieves higher composition validity (88.5% vs. 83.2%) and competitive DFT stability (4.10% vs. 4.19% E_hull < 0.0), while FlowMM achieves a slightly higher S.U.N. rate at lower temperature. For novel crystal discovery from recent literature, Mat2Seq achieves 27.3% validity and 10% hit rate versus CrystaLLM's 23.7% validity and 0% hit rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CrystaLLM [12], which first demonstrated that autoregressive language models (GPT-2) can generate crystal structures from CIF file text, and on the broader LM-for-materials line of work by Flam-Shepherd [10] and Gruver [11]. The key departure is conceptual: rather than feeding raw CIF text (which yields infinitely many non-unique sequences for the same crystal), Mat2Seq introduces a provably SE(3)- and periodic-invariant tokenization pipeline using Niggli cell reduction, space-group-aware irreducible atom sets, and fractional coordinates. This positions the paper at the intersection of crystallographic symmetry theory and modern LLM tokenization, complementing 3D-native generative approaches like CDVAE [15], DiffCSP [17], and MatterGen [18] that handle invariance through equivariant architectures rather than sequence-level guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The invariance/uniqueness guarantee eliminates the need for massive data augmentation that plagues CIF-based crystal LLMs, and the 100% uniqueness rate (vs. 30% for CIF) is a clean, verifiable property that makes the approach more sample-efficient and theoretically grounded. The conditional generation capability (targeting band gaps) opens a practical path toward property-driven materials discovery using off-the-shelf LLMs. However, limitations remain: the method cannot handle molecules, proteins, or disordered materials; the hit rate on truly novel literature crystals is only 10%; and scaling to larger, more diverse training sets with more stable structures is needed for robustness. Open questions include whether the invariance principle transfers to other periodic or quasi-periodic systems and whether more powerful LLM backbones (beyond GPT-2) yield further gains.
