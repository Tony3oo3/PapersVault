## Problem

In contextual bandits, estimating only the difference between policy values (rather than each policy's value individually) provably reduces sample complexity, but the best-known tabular RL algorithms (e.g., PEDEL) still estimate each policy's value separately and then take differences. This paper asks whether pure difference estimation suffices in RL, and answers negatively: a lower bound shows that ρ_Π (the difference-based complexity) is not achievable when state transitions are unknown. However, it shows that it *almost* suffices—learning one reference policy's behavior plus the differences to all other policies yields the tightest known instance-dependent bound for tabular RL.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a finite set of candidate policies Π over a tabular MDP with S states, A actions, and horizon H, along with tolerance ε and confidence δ. It interacts with the environment in episodes (pure exploration, no regret objective) and returns a single policy π̂ ∈ Π that is ε-optimal with probability at least 1−δ. The algorithm proceeds in O(log(1/ε)) epochs with geometrically decreasing tolerances ε_ℓ = 2^{−ℓ}, and at each epoch eliminates suboptimal policies from the active set Π_ℓ until one remains. The sample complexity decomposes into a difference term (scaling with ‖φ_h^π − φ_h^π̄‖²) and a reference-policy term U(π, π̄) that captures the cost of learning where the reference policy visits.

## Method

PERP (Policy Elimination with Reference Policy) introduces a reduced-variance off-policy estimator that decomposes V^π − V^π̄ into a sum over timesteps of reward-weighted state-visitation differences δ_h^π = w_h^π − w_h^π̄. At each epoch, the algorithm (1) selects a reference policy π̄_ℓ minimizing the estimated U(π, π̄) cost, (2) rolls out π̄_ℓ to estimate its state visitations ŵ_h^π̄, and (3) recursively estimates δ_h^π for each remaining policy using empirically estimated transition matrices: δ̂_{h+1}^π = P̂_h(π_h − π̄_h)ŵ_h^π̄ + P̂_h π_h δ̂_h^π. Data for the transition estimates is collected via the OPTCOV experiment-design subroutine (a Frank-Wolfe-based covering procedure), which ensures the covariance matrix Λ_h covers all relevant difference directions (π_h − π̄_h)ŵ_h^π̄ + π_h δ̂_h^π simultaneously. The resulting sample complexity is O(ρ_Π) + O(H·U(π,π̄)/Δ(π)²), which is never worse than the prior PEDEL bound and strictly tighter on instances where policies share most of their visitation structure.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that PERP achieves an (ε,δ)-PAC sample complexity of O(ρ_Π · H⁴β²ι) + O(H·U(π,π*)/Δ(π)² · log(H|Π|ι/δ)) + lower-order terms, which is the tightest known bound for tabular RL. On the motivating Figure 1 MDP, PERP's complexity scales as O(H/ε) while the prior best algorithm PEDEL [42] scales as Ω(H/ε²), yielding an ε× improvement. The paper also establishes a separation: ρ_Π alone is provably insufficient for tabular RL (Lemma 1: lower bound Ω(1/ε) vs. ρ_Π ≤ 15), but is necessary and sufficient for contextual bandits (Corollary 1), even with unknown context distribution.

|---|---|---|
| ρ_Π (difference-based, [2]) | O(ρ_Π) | ≤ 15 |
| **PERP (Theorem 1)** | **O(H/ε) + O(1)** | **O(H/ε)** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with PEDEL [42], the prior best instance-dependent algorithm for tabular RL. Lemma 3 proves that PERP's complexity is never worse than PEDEL's for any MDP instance and policy set, while Lemma 2 shows a strict improvement: on the Figure 1 MDP, PERP achieves O(H/ε) versus PEDEL's Ω(H/ε²), an ε× gap. Against the lower bound of Al-Marjani et al. [2], which shows ρ_Π is necessary, this paper demonstrates that ρ_Π is insufficient for full tabular RL (where transitions are unknown) by constructing an instance requiring Ω(1/ε) samples while ρ_Π ≤ 15. In the contextual bandit special case, PERP matches the lower bound of Li et al. [30] (which assumed known context distribution) without requiring this assumption, closing a gap in the literature. The paper also shows that for MDPs with action-independent transitions (Corollary 2), the complexity simplifies to O(ρ_Π), exactly matching the [2] lower bound up to lower-order terms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instance-dependent policy identification literature in tabular RL, building directly on PEDEL [42] (which it credits as 'directly inspiring' its algorithmic approach) and the gap-visitation complexity of [46]. It departs from prior RL work by showing that the natural generalization of the bandit difference-based complexity ρ_Π is insufficient when state transitions are unknown, extending the lower bound of Al-Marjani et al. [2] (which only covered reward learning) to the full transition-unknown setting. On the positive side, it extends the contextual bandit result of Li et al. [30] from known to unknown context distributions, and introduces the novel U(π, π̄) term capturing the cost of learning a reference policy's visitation behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clean information-theoretic separation between contextual bandits and tabular RL: in bandits, estimating policy differences alone is both necessary and sufficient, while in RL one must additionally learn a reference policy's behavior. This resolves a long-standing open question about whether the bandit-style difference complexity generalizes to RL. Key limitations include the computational intractability of enumerating Π (the authors note PERP is 'too computationally expensive for most practical applications'), unproven tightness of the U(π,π̄)/Δ(π)² term, and lower-order terms that may be removable. Open questions include whether the separation extends to linear MDPs and whether the difference estimator yields tighter instance-dependent regret bounds.
