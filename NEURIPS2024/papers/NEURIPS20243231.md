## Problem

Existing Vision-Language Compositionality benchmarks like SUGARCREPE evaluate only image-to-text retrieval, ignoring the equally valid text-to-image direction. This asymmetry is driven by practical convenience (hard negative texts are easier to collect than hard negative images) rather than theoretical justification, and the paper shows that conclusions about model capabilities change significantly when both retrieval directions are considered.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243231/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each BIVLC instance pairs one natural COCO image with one synthetic image (generated by SDXL-DPO at 512×512) and one positive caption with one hard negative caption, yielding four retrieval queries per instance (two I2T, two T2I). The model must select the correct cross-modal match for each query; evaluation reports I2T accuracy, T2I accuracy, and a Group score that requires both directions to be correct simultaneously. The full test set contains 2,933 instances (11,732 retrieval examples) spanning REPLACE, SWAP, and ADD compositional categories.

## Method

BIVLC is constructed by extending SUGARCREPE: for each hard negative caption, four candidate images are generated with SDXL-DPO, human annotators select the best image (or discard all four), and a second annotation pass removes ambiguous instances where the generated image also matches the original caption. For training, the authors propose TROHN-IMG, which uses OpenChat-3.5 to generate hard negative captions from COCO, filters them by plausibility (VERA) and linguistic acceptability (COLA), applies adversarial refinement, generates one image per caption with SDXL-DPO, and fine-tunes CLIP (ViT-B/32) with InfoNCE contrastive loss over 400-image × 400-caption batches containing both hard negative texts and images. This dual-negative training strategy forces the model to maximize the distance between the positive caption and the negative image, a constraint not guaranteed when only hard negative texts are used, and yields the best contrastive model on BIVLC (69.25 Group score vs. 60.96 for the previous best).

## Results

On BIVLC, the best generative model VQAScore-XXL achieves a Group score of 76.47 (I2T 86.16, T2I 81.93), while the best contrastive model CLIP_TROHN-IMG reaches 69.25 (I2T 88.54, T2I 71.84). Humans score 86.80 Group (I2T 90.40, T2I 93.00), leaving a 10-point gap to the best model. A key finding is that all models show a large I2T-to-T2I gap (e.g., CLIP: 75.83 vs. 52.40), whereas humans perform comparably in both directions (90.40 vs. 93.00). On SUGARCREPE, CLIP_TROHN-TEXT achieves 93.40, matching VQAScore-XXL (93.72), but this advantage does not transfer to BIVLC where it scores only 57.48 Group.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among contrastive models, CLIP_TROHN-IMG (the paper's proposed model trained with hard negative images) achieves the best BIVLC Group score of 69.25, outperforming CLIP_COCO (60.96), NEGCLIP (58.75), GNM (57.96), and base CLIP (49.06) by 8–20 points. On SUGARCREPE, however, CLIP_TROHN-TEXT (93.40) surpasses NEGCLIP (85.64) by 8 points and matches VQAScore-XXL (93.72), yet it performs poorly on BIVLC (57.48 Group), even below CLIP_COCO. Open CapPa leads SUGARCREPE among non-VQAScore models (90.59) but is the worst on BIVLC (41.97 Group), revealing a strong language-bias dependency. VQAScore-XXL dominates both benchmarks (93.72 SUGARCREPE, 76.47 BIVLC Group) but is 70× larger than the contrastive models. The paper's key finding is that SUGARCREPE and BIVLC rankings are not correlated: NEGCLIP is best among contrastive models on SUGARCREPE, while CLIP_COCO (which uses no hard negatives) is best on BIVLC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SUGARCREPE (Hsieh et al., 2024), which fixed earlier hackable VLC benchmarks (CREPE, ARO) by using LLM-generated hard negative captions with adversarial refinement, by adding a text-to-image retrieval direction through SDXL-DPO-generated hard negative images. The bidirectional evaluation structure (two images, two captions per instance) and the I2T/T2I/Group scoring metrics are borrowed from Winoground (Thrush et al., 2022), but at a much larger scale (~3K vs. 400 instances) and focused purely on compositionality. For training, the paper builds on the hard-negative-text strategy of NEGCLIP and the hard-negative-image strategy of GNM, but replaces heuristic rules and image editing with LLM-generated captions and diffusion-model-generated images respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that multimodal models show a large I2T/T2I performance asymmetry while humans do not, and that SUGARCREPE and BIVLC rankings are uncorrelated—means prior conclusions about which models best capture VLC may not transfer to the bidirectional setting. The 10-point gap to human performance on the Group score (vs. 5 points on SUGARCREPE) confirms that bidirectional VLC remains a genuinely open problem. Key limitations include the ~61% noise rate in synthetic training images (from SDXL-DPO generation failures and ambiguous instances), English-only captions, and the fact that only contrastive models were fine-tuned; future work on automatic filtering of synthetic images and extending to generative models is needed to close the gap.
