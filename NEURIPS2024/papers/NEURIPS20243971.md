## Problem

The Passive-Aggressive (PA) algorithm for online regression relies on a manually set threshold ε that is difficult to tune—too small causes overfitting to noise, too large causes underfitting—and it selects weights based solely on tracking accuracy, ignoring additional performance metrics (side information) that matter in practice. This paper addresses both limitations by adaptively selecting ε and integrating side information into the weight update.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243971/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each round t, the method receives a stock return vector x_t ∈ ℝ^N, an index return y_t ∈ ℝ, and a lower semi-continuous convex side-information function h_t(w) (e.g., negative log return for enhanced index tracking), along with a user-chosen trade-off parameter λ. It outputs a portfolio weight vector w_{t+1} constrained to the probability simplex and an adaptively updated threshold ε_{t+1} ∈ [ν, D]. The side information is consumed only through its gradient ∇h_t in the efficient SCA solver, so any differentiable convex function can be plugged in without modifying the algorithm.

## Method

APAS replaces the standard PA projection step with a proximal operator prox_{λh_t}(ŵ_{t+1}(ε)), which balances tracking accuracy (via the ℓ₂ distance to the PA update) against side performance (via h_t). The threshold ε is then adapted by projected gradient descent on the Moreau Envelope loss f_t(ε) = M_{λh_t}(ŵ_{t+1}(ε)), using a time-varying learning rate η_t = ζ_t√D/(G√(νt)) where ζ_t is the clipped real-time error. Although f_t(ε) is piecewise-convex (hence non-convex overall), the paper proves an O(√T) regret bound by exploiting quasi-convexity along the function curve. For efficient computation, the proximal operator is solved via Successive Convex Approximation, reducing per-iteration cost from O(N^3.5) (interior point) to a sequence of simple Euclidean projections with closed-form solutions on the simplex or ℓ₁-ball, converging in a few iterations.

## Results

On both synthetic data (T=200, N=100) and real market data (S&P 500: T=503, N=453; NASDAQ 100: T=503, N=101), APAS achieves a strictly superior Pareto frontier between tracking error (MDTE) and excess cumulative return compared to PA, SLAIT-ETE, and SLAIT-DR. With a small λ (e.g., 1e-2), APAS matches PA's tracking error while yielding substantially higher excess cumulative return; with a large λ (e.g., 5e-2), APAS achieves the best excess cumulative return among all methods at only a modest increase in tracking error. The SCA-based efficient algorithm (Algorithm 2) is approximately 100× faster than CVXR/ADMM and 10× faster than PGD for high-dimensional problems (N=5000: 0.00335 s vs. much longer runtimes). The framework achieves an O(√T) regret bound for non-convex loss functions without restrictive assumptions or oracles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares APAS against three baselines: the original PA algorithm (Crammer et al., 2006) and two variants of SLAIT (Benidis et al., 2017) — SLAIT-ETE (focused on tracking accuracy) and SLAIT-DR (focused on drawdown avoidance). Against PA, APAS achieves a comparable or slightly higher tracking error but a significantly higher excess cumulative return, demonstrating that the side-information-driven weight selection extracts additional return without sacrificing tracking fidelity. Against SLAIT-ETE, APAS dominates on the tracking-error-vs-excess-return trade-off curve: for any given tracking error level, APAS delivers higher excess return, and for any given excess return target, APAS requires lower tracking error. Against SLAIT-DR, APAS similarly achieves a better Pareto frontier, outperforming it on both metrics simultaneously across the tested λ range. The adaptive ε mechanism (APAS vs. fixed-ε PAS) further improves performance, confirming that the dynamic threshold selection is beneficial even without knowing the optimal ε a priori.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Passive-Aggressive (PA) algorithm of Crammer et al. (2006) in two key directions: it integrates side information into the weight-update step via a proximal operator (Moreau Envelope), and it makes the ε-insensitive threshold adaptive rather than fixed. The efficient computation of the proximal step draws on the Successive Convex Approximation (SCA) framework of Scutari et al. (2013), reducing the per-iteration cost from O(N^3.5) (interior point) to a few projection steps. The regret analysis builds on the quasi-convexity structure of the Moreau Envelope composition, extending standard O(√T) online convex optimization bounds to a piecewise-convex (hence non-convex) loss setting without requiring sampling oracles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The APAS framework provides a principled way to balance tracking accuracy against auxiliary performance criteria (e.g., excess return, switching costs, sparsity) in online regression, which is directly relevant to portfolio management and other streaming prediction tasks. The O(√T) regret guarantee for the non-convex loss is notable because it avoids the restrictive assumptions or oracle access that typically accompany non-convex online learning results, though the non-convexity here is mild (piecewise convex with a flat tail). Open questions include whether the adaptive threshold mechanism generalizes to multi-parameter settings, whether the SCA-based solver remains efficient when the side-information function is non-smooth (e.g., group Lasso), and how the framework performs under truly non-stationary (non-i.i.d.) data streams beyond the i.i.d. synthetic experiments.
