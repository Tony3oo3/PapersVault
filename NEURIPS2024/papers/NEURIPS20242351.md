## Problem

Conventional diffusion models fix the forward process to a predefined linear Gaussian schedule, which implicitly constrains the latent variable distributions and makes the reverse process's task of learning generative trajectories more difficult. This rigidity limits the model's ability to adapt to the data distribution and complicates likelihood estimation. The paper asks whether making the forward process learnable can simplify the reverse process and improve generative performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242351/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a sample from a unit Gaussian prior z_1 ~ N(0,I) and integrates the reverse SDE (or ODE when g_φ(t)≡0) to produce a data point x, with a small-variance Gaussian reconstruction p(x|z_0) = N(x; z_0, 10^{-4}I). For the bridge model (NFBM), the input is a source image x_1 (e.g., a dog) and the output is a translated image x_0 (e.g., a cat), with the forward process conditioned on both endpoints. All experiments use RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-32) or 64×64 (ImageNet-64, AFHQ-64), and likelihood is estimated by integrating the deterministic ODE with an RK45 solver.

## Method

NFDM replaces the fixed linear-Gaussian forward process with a learnable invertible transformation F_φ(ε, t, x) that implicitly defines conditional marginals q_φ(z_t|x) for all t∈[0,1]. The forward process is constructed in three steps: (1) define marginals via F_φ, (2) derive a conditional ODE from the time derivative ∂F_φ/∂t (computed via JVP), and (3) build a conditional SDE with a learnable variance g_φ(t). The reverse process is parameterized through a data-point predictor x̂_θ(z_t, t) that is substituted into the conditional reverse SDE, yielding a simulation-free training objective that jointly optimizes both forward (φ) and reverse (θ) parameters by minimizing a variational upper bound on NLL. The key insight is that the learnable forward process acts as a flexible variational distribution in a hierarchical VAE, allowing it to adapt to the reverse process and tighten the likelihood bound. Two extensions are proposed: NFDM-OT adds a curvature penalty on deterministic trajectories to encourage straight-line generation (enabling fewer sampling steps), and NFBM conditions the forward process on a pair (x_0, x_1) to learn bridges between two distributions.

## Results

NFDM achieves state-of-the-art negative log-likelihood (BPD) on all three benchmark datasets: 2.48 on CIFAR-10, 3.34 on ImageNet 32, and 3.20 on ImageNet 64 (non-Gaussian forward process), outperforming the previous best (i-DODE at 2.56/3.43, MuLAN at 2.55). For few-step generation, NFDM-OT with a curvature penalty achieves FID of 5.20 (CIFAR-10), 4.11 (ImageNet 32), and 11.58 (ImageNet 64) at 12 NFE, surpassing Multisample Flow Matching at the same NFE (7.18 and 17.60 respectively). On the AFHQ dog-to-cat bridge task, NFBM achieves FID 12.06, outperforming GSBM (12.39) and DSBM (14.16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NFDM outperforms all 16 baselines on every dataset where they overlap. On CIFAR-10 BPD, NFDM (2.48) beats the closest competitors i-DODE (2.56) and MuLAN (2.55) by 0.07–0.08 BPD, and VDM (2.65) by 0.17 BPD. On ImageNet 32, NFDM (3.34) improves over i-DODE (3.43) and Stochastic Interpolation (3.48) by 0.09–0.14 BPD. On ImageNet 64, NFDM (3.20) leads Flow Matching (3.31) and NDM (3.35) by 0.11–0.15 BPD. For few-step generation at 12 NFE, NFDM-OT achieves FID 4.11 on ImageNet 32 versus 7.18 for Multisample Flow Matching [44], and 11.58 on ImageNet 64 versus 17.60, while also outperforming Trajectory Curvature Minimization [32] (FID 18.74 at 5 NFE on CIFAR-10). On the AFHQ bridge task, NFBM (FID 12.06) narrowly beats GSBM (12.39) and clearly outperforms DSBM (14.16). Notably, NFDM-OT is trained with an ELBO-based objective (which typically yields higher FID than simplified objectives), yet still achieves superior few-step FID, suggesting the curvature penalty and learnable forward process provide genuine gains beyond objective-function choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

NFDM generalizes the standard diffusion model framework (DDPM, Score SDE) by replacing the fixed linear-Gaussian forward process with a learnable invertible transformation F_φ, unifying prior approaches that explored alternative fixed forward processes (soft diffusion, blurring, wavelet diffusion), learnable noise schedules (VDM, MuLAN), and learnable data transformations (LSGM, NDM, DiffEnc) as special cases. The reverse process construction directly follows Anderson's reverse-time SDE formulation, while the NFDM-OT variant adopts the curvature penalty from Kelly et al. for learning easy-to-solve ODEs. The key conceptual departure is treating the forward process as an optimizable variational distribution (analogous to the encoder in a hierarchical VAE), rather than a fixed corruption schedule, which the paper argues yields a tighter likelihood bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NFDM provides a principled, simulation-free framework that achieves state-of-the-art likelihoods on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet 32/64 while simultaneously enabling applications like distribution bridging (NFBM) and learning generative dynamics with user-specified properties (e.g., straight trajectories without distillation or min-max optimization). The main practical limitation is the ~2.2× increase in per-iteration training cost from the neural forward process, and the requirement that F_φ be invertible with an accessible log-determinant restricts the class of usable architectures. Open questions include whether more expressive parameterizations (beyond Gaussian or RealNVP-style flows) can close the remaining gap to the best FID scores, how the framework scales to higher-resolution ImageNet, and whether the curvature penalty approach generalizes to other trajectory constraints beyond straightness.
