## Problem

In polarimetric imaging, short exposure times produce noisy snapshots with severely degenerated degree of polarization (DoP) and angle of polarization (AoP), while long exposure times produce blurry snapshots with over-smoothed DoP and AoP. Existing polarized image enhancement methods handle either noise or blur in isolation, and conventional noisy-blurry pair fusion methods cannot preserve the physical polarization properties needed for accurate DoP and AoP computation. This paper is the first to apply a complementarily fusing strategy to polarimetric imaging, addressing the gap of jointly recovering clean and clear polarized snapshots while maintaining polarization consistency.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input a degraded pair of polarized snapshots, each containing four polarization angles (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°): a short-exposure noisy snapshot L and a long-exposure blurry snapshot B, both at 256×256 (training) or 512×512 (test) resolution. The output is a clean polarized snapshot I (four angles) from which high-quality DoP and AoP maps are derived via Stokes parameter computation. A key constraint is that the output must satisfy the physical consistency condition I_α1 + I_α3 = I_α2 + I_α4, enforced through a dedicated polarization regularization loss term.

## Method

The core contribution is a neural network-based three-phase fusing scheme that explicitly separates polarization-unrelated irradiance from polarization-specific properties. Phase 1 (irradiance restoration) recovers the total intensity S0 from the noisy snapshot using Color and Structure Cue Fusion (CSCF) modules that apply affine transformations guided by color features from S0^B and deformable convolutions guided by structure features from S1,2^B. Phase 2 (polarization reconstruction) operates in a Cartesian coordinate representation (x=S1/S0, y=S2/S0) to reduce the non-linearity of DoP/AoP, using Coherence-Aware Aggregation (CAG) modules with CBAM-style channel attention and Coherence Injection (CI) modules with Transformer cross-attention to establish physical correlation between polarization and irradiance. Phase 3 (artifact suppression) refines the four polarized images via a U-Net backbone, splitting them into two groups (I_α1,3 and I_α2,4) so each retains full irradiance and half the polarization information. Training uses a two-stage strategy (independent phase training followed by joint fine-tuning) with a composite loss combining L1, perceptual, total variation, and two polarization-specific regularization terms.

## Results

On the synthetic PLIE-based dataset, the proposed framework achieves 29.23 dB PSNR and 0.797 SSIM for DoP, 16.96 dB PSNR and 0.382 SSIM for AoP, and 39.05 dB PSNR and 0.982 SSIM for S₀, consistently outperforming all six baselines on every metric. The closest polarized-image baseline (PLIE+) trails by 1.25 dB in PSNR-p and 0.03 dB in PSNR-θ, while the conventional-image fusion methods (LSD2, LSFNet, SelfIR, D2HNet) lag by 3.5–9.8 dB in PSNR-p and 11.2–13.8 dB in PSNR-S₀, confirming that polarization-aware fusion is essential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed framework wins on all six metrics against every baseline. Against the closest polarized-image baselines, PLIE+ (improved low-light enhancement accepting noisy-blurry pairs) is outperformed by 1.25 dB in PSNR-p and 0.03 dB in PSNR-θ, while PolDeblur+ (improved deblurring accepting noisy-blurry pairs) trails by 3.92 dB in PSNR-p and 0.21 dB in PSNR-θ. The four conventional-image fusion methods (LSD2, LSFNet, SelfIR, D2HNet) show substantially inferior performance, with PSNR-p ranging from 19.43 to 25.73 dB (vs. 29.23 for Ours) and PSNR-S₀ from 25.25 to 27.88 dB (vs. 39.05), demonstrating that methods not designed for polarization cannot preserve DoP and AoP accuracy. The gap between PLIE and PLIE+ (and between PolDeblur and PolDeblur+) further confirms that fusing a noisy-blurry pair is inherently beneficial over processing a single degraded snapshot, but the proposed polarization-aware three-phase scheme extracts significantly more complementary information than simply adapting existing polarized enhancement pipelines to accept two inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: polarized image enhancement (PLIE, PolDeblur, IPLNet) that processes a single degraded polarized snapshot, and noisy-blurry pair fusion for conventional images (LSD2, LSFNet, SelfIR, D2HNet) that lacks polarization awareness. It is the first to apply the complementary-fusion strategy to polarimetric imaging, designing a three-phase pipeline (irradiance restoration → polarization reconstruction → artifact suppression) with modules (CSCF, CAG, CI) that explicitly exploit Stokes parameter structure. A key methodological departure is reconstructing DoP and AoP in a Cartesian coordinate representation (x=S1/S0, y=S2/S0) rather than in the nonlinear polar domain, reducing ill-posedness and preventing error accumulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that complementary fusion of noisy and blurry polarized snapshots can simultaneously improve image quality and preserve polarization properties, which single-input enhancement methods cannot achieve. The Cartesian coordinate representation for DoP/AoP optimization is a reusable insight that could inform future polarized image processing pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to a single synthetic dataset derived from PLIE, the method cannot handle polarized video, and it requires Stokes parameters as input (ruling out conventional RGB fusion). Open questions include whether the three-phase decomposition generalizes to other degradation pairs (e.g., noisy+noisy at different exposures) and whether the approach scales to multi-frame or video settings.
