## Problem

Conditional 3D molecular generation with diffusion models faces an intrinsic trade-off: training-free online guidance can steer samples toward target chemical properties, but the stepwise enforcement of conditions pushes samples off the correct data manifold at each denoising step, producing molecules with severely degraded stability and validity. No existing framework simultaneously achieves precise property targeting and faithful adherence to the learned molecular distribution throughout the reverse diffusion process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242416/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target quantum chemical property value (e.g., HOMO energy, dipole moment, polarizability), a molecule size M, a pre-trained unconditional equivariant diffusion model, and a pre-trained time predictor (a 7-layer EGNN with 192 hidden features) as inputs, and produces 3D atomic coordinates in a zero-center-of-mass subspace. Molecules are represented as point clouds of up to 9 heavy atoms (QM9) or up to 181 atoms (Geom-Drug), with the diffusion process operating under a VP-SDE formulation. The time predictor outputs a categorical distribution over all T diffusion timesteps, from which a corrected timestep is extracted via argmax and clipped to a window [t−Δ, t+Δ].

## Method

TACS integrates online guidance (following LGD's loss-guided score approximation via Tweedie's formula) with a novel Time Correction Sampler (TCS) that corrects the manifold deviation induced by guidance. TCS consists of a time predictor—an EGNN trained with cross-entropy loss to classify the forward-noise timestep of a given noised sample—and a correction step that substitutes the predicted timestep t_pred into Tweedie's formula to estimate the clean molecule x̂₀, then re-noises x̂₀ back to the correct next timestep t−1 via the forward process. The key insight is that online guidance creates an 'effective timestep' mismatch: the guided sample no longer lies on the marginal distribution corresponding to the nominal timestep t, and the time predictor detects this shift to restore manifold alignment. A time-clipping window Δ prevents large timestep jumps that would skip critical denoising steps. The time predictor is trained separately per target property by concatenating the condition vector to the EGNN input, and the overall sampler alternates between standard reverse diffusion steps (early in the process) and guidance-plus-correction steps (from a configurable starting timestep t_TCS onward).

## Results

On QM9 conditional generation, TACS achieves the lowest MAE across all six quantum chemical properties while maintaining molecular stability (MS) above 80%: e.g., MAE of 0.659 for C_v (vs. 1.072 for EDM, 1.044 for EEGSDE), 0.387 for dipole moment μ (vs. 1.118, 0.854), and 289 meV for ε_LUMO (vs. 602, 494). TCS alone (without online guidance) achieves the highest MS (87–93%) and validity (93–97%) but with higher MAE, confirming that online guidance is needed for precise property targeting. On Geom-Drug unconditional generation, TCS reaches 89.6% atom stability and 97.6% validity, surpassing EDM (81.3%/92.6%) and EDM-Bridge (82.4%/92.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TACS outperforms both EDM [23] and EEGSDE [5] on MAE across all six QM9 properties by 30–55% while maintaining comparable or higher molecular stability (e.g., 83.6% vs. 81.0% MS for C_v). Against naive online guidance (OG), TACS achieves similar or better MAE (e.g., 289 vs. 170 meV for ε_LUMO) but with dramatically higher MS (82.7% vs. 21.1%) and validity (91.3% vs. 42.3%), confirming that time correction prevents the manifold deviation that destabilizes pure OG. On target structure generation (Table 2), TACS achieves 0.694–0.713 Tanimoto similarity versus 0.499 for cG-SchNet [15] and 0.540–0.600 for EEGSDE [5], while also matching or exceeding their MS. On Geom-Drug unconditional generation, TCS surpasses GDM [57] (89.6% vs. 75.0% AS), EDM-Bridge [56] (89.6% vs. 82.4% AS), and ENF [47] in both atom stability and validity. In the direct comparison with TS-DDPM [34] (Appendix E, Table 15), TACS improves MAE by 30–55% and MS by 5–15 points across all six properties, demonstrating that learned time prediction outperforms variance-matching heuristics for exposure bias correction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of training-free online guidance methods (DPS, LGD) for conditional diffusion, which estimate the conditional score without labeled training data, and on exposure-bias mitigation work (TS-DPM, DMCMC) that adjusts timesteps during reverse diffusion. It departs from prior conditional molecular generation (EDM, EEGSDE, MuDM) by identifying that stepwise online guidance systematically pushes samples off the correct data manifold at each denoising step, and introduces a Time Correction Sampler that uses an EGNN-based time predictor to re-estimate the effective timestep and re-anchor samples via Tweedie's formula before proceeding. The combination of online guidance with per-step time correction is, to the authors' knowledge, the first framework to simultaneously enforce target properties and maintain data-manifold fidelity in 3D molecular diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental trade-off in inverse molecular design—achieving low MAE on target quantum properties while preserving molecular validity and stability—that has limited prior methods to either sacrificing one objective or the other. The time-correction mechanism is conceptually general (applicable to any diffusion task where guidance or error accumulation shifts samples off-manifold), and the authors note potential extensions to flow-matching models and other domains like image generation. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to larger molecules with exact quantum-chemistry-based guidance (currently limited by NISQ-era VQE noise), whether the time predictor can be made more efficient, and whether the reduced novelty of generated samples (71.6% vs. 84.5% for EDM) is an acceptable trade-off for the stability gains.
