## Problem

Existing surgical robotics simulation platforms either compromise on physical realism (lacking soft-body/deformable thread simulation) or photorealism, and typically support only simple, single-stage tasks rather than complex multi-stage procedures like suturing. There is no open-source platform that provides a standardized pipeline for both RL and IL, a hierarchical framework for task decomposition, and clear benchmarking metrics for fine-grained surgical manipulation policies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242036/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Low-level policies consume either low-dimensional 6D ground-truth poses of objects (needle, entry/exit holes, end-effectors) or high-dimensional scene-invariant RGB images from multiple camera views concatenated with 6D proprioceptive end-effector poses; expert demonstrations are collected via teleoperation devices (dVRK MTMs, Geomagic, Razer Hydra) or generated heuristically via DMP/LWR. The high-level policy outputs a discrete subtask index (0–4), while each low-level policy outputs a 7-DOF continuous action vector (Δx, Δy, Δz, Δroll, Δpitch, Δyaw, jaw state) constrained to incremental step sizes within physical boundaries. Evaluation reports success rate, cumulative trajectory length in millimeters, and total time steps per episode.

## Method

SurgicAI decomposes single-throw suturing into five subtasks (grasping, placing, inserting, handoff, pullout), each handled by a specialized low-level policy (LLP) trained independently in its own Gymnasium environment. A high-level policy (HLP), trained with PPO over the fixed LLPs, selects which subtask to activate based on the current state, creating a cyclical coordination loop that continues until the full procedure completes. The simulation is built on the SRC/AMBF framework with deformable thread dynamics and is fully compatible with the da Vinci/dVRK system for real-time teleoperation and headless data sampling. The platform supports a broad algorithm suite—online RL (PPO, DDPG, SAC, TD3), offline RL (BCQ, IQL, AWAC, CalQL), and imitation learning (BC)—with ablations combining Hindsight Experience Replay and BC loss (weighted at λ₂) to guide exploration in sparse-reward settings. For image-based IL, frozen pretrained visual encoders (CLIP, R3M, or ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50) extract features from multi-view RGB, which are concatenated with proprioception and fed to an MLP policy trained with MSE (LLP) or cross-entropy (HLP) loss.

## Results

On the five suturing subtasks (Grasping, Placing, Inserting, Handoff, Pullout), TD3+HER+BC achieves the highest success rates among RL-based methods, reaching 0.91–1.00 across all tasks after 150k training steps. Offline RL methods BCQ (0.92–0.96) and IQL (0.91–0.95) also perform strongly, while pure online RL (PPO, DDPG, SAC, TD3) largely fails, with success rates at or near zero under sparse rewards. The hierarchical framework (Table 3) boosts sequential subtask success from 0.00–0.64 (single-policy TD3+HER+BC) to 0.52–0.88, demonstrating a >50% improvement in multi-stage suturing completion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pure online RL methods (PPO, DDPG, SAC, TD3) fail almost entirely under sparse rewards and achieve at most 0.40 success (PPO on Pullout) even with dense rewards, confirming the exploration difficulty in high-precision surgical tasks. Adding HER to TD3 improves success to 0.05–0.15 but remains far below practical thresholds. Behavior Cloning alone reaches 0.83–1.00 success across all subtasks, establishing a strong imitation baseline. TD3+HER+BC surpasses BC on trajectory efficiency (e.g., ~5 mm shorter and ~10 fewer steps on Grasping) while matching or exceeding its success rates (0.91–1.00 vs. 0.83–1.00). Among offline RL methods, BCQ (0.92–0.96) and IQL (0.91–0.95) are competitive with TD3+HER+BC, while AWAC succeeds only on Inserting and Handoff (0.96, 0.98) and CalQL fails entirely. The hierarchical framework outperforms the single-policy approach by 0.15–0.88 in sequential subtask success (Table 3), with the largest gap on longer sequences (e.g., 0.52 vs. 0.15 for Approach→Pullout).

## Relation to Prior Work

SurgicAI builds directly on the AccelNet Surgical Robotics Challenge (SRC) simulation environment and the AMBF physics engine, adding deformable thread simulation and a standardized Gymnasium-compatible pipeline for RL/IL training. It extends the hierarchical learning paradigm from general robotic manipulation (e.g., Triantafyllidis et al., 2023) to the specific domain of multi-stage surgical suturing, and incorporates the LfD/DMP-based heuristic trajectories from Zhou et al. (2024) as a data source. Unlike prior surgical simulation platforms (dVRL, SurRoL, Surgical Gym, ORBIT-Surgical) that handle only simple reaching or placing tasks, SurgicAI introduces the first open-source hierarchical framework for decomposing complex suturing into coordinated subtasks with deformable-body dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a much-needed standardized benchmark for surgical robotics policy learning, with clear metrics and baselines across 10+ RL/IL algorithms, enabling fair comparison in a field that previously lacked such infrastructure. The key practical finding—that pure online RL essentially fails on precision surgical tasks while demonstration-guided methods (BC, offline RL) achieve >90% success—provides strong evidence for the necessity of expert data in surgical automation. Open questions remain around sim-to-real transfer (the paper acknowledges domain gap and kinematic error injection as future work), the limited success of image-based policies (~0.98 best case, highly view-dependent), and whether the hierarchical approach generalizes beyond the five subtasks tested. The platform's compatibility with dVRK and the broader AMBF ecosystem positions it as a potential standard for the surgical robotics community.
