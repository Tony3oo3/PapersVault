## Problem

Confusion-matrix-based noisy label learning has strong identifiability guarantees only under the assumption of an instance-invariant transition matrix, yet real annotations inevitably contain instance-dependent outliers (e.g., blurry or ambiguous images). The paper proves that with a single annotator, no sparsity constraint on outliers can prevent a universal approximator from absorbing all outliers into the classifier, making the ground-truth classifier fundamentally unidentifiable. The key question is whether multiple annotators can restore identifiability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes image feature vectors (extracted by ResNet-34/ResNet-9 for CIFAR-10/STL-10, or frozen VGG-16/CLIP embeddings for LabelMe/ImageNet-15N) together with noisy one-hot labels from M annotators, where annotations may be incomplete (not every annotator labels every sample). It produces a K-dimensional class probability vector, a per-sample scalar outlier score s_n = Σ_m ||ê_n^(m)||₂ that cleanly separates nominal from outlier samples, and M row-stochastic confusion matrices. The outlier score is a byproduct of the learned perturbation vectors rather than a separate detection module.

## Method

The core geometric insight is that stacking M annotators' predictions lifts the nominal data into a K-dimensional subspace range(W^natural) ⊂ R^(MK), while each outlier vector e_n^natural ∈ R^(MK) is unlikely to lie in this subspace when M > 1, enabling separation. The training objective is a coupled cross-entropy loss over all observed (annotator, sample) pairs, augmented with an ℓ₂/ℓ_p column-sparsity regularizer (0 < p ≤ 1) that softly enforces only C samples have nonzero outlier perturbations, and a volume-minimization term −log det(FFᵀ) that drives the confusion matrices toward minimum-volume simplices for identifiability. All three terms are differentiable, yielding a single-stage end-to-end optimization solvable by standard Adam. Theoretical guarantees (Theorems 3.5–3.6) establish exact outlier recovery and consistent estimation of the ground-truth classifier under mild conditions (outliers outside range(W^natural), SSC or anchor-point assumptions), with the enhanced Theorem 3.6 removing the need for anchor samples entirely.

## Results

On real human-annotated datasets, COINNet achieves 92.09% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10N (3 annotators), 87.60% on LabelMe (59 annotators), and 93.71% on the newly introduced ImageNet-15N (100 annotators, 42.68% average error rate). The most dramatic gain is on ImageNet-15N, where COINNet outperforms the second-best method (MaxMIG) by 12.58 percentage points. On machine-annotated CIFAR-10 under high noise (70% error rate), COINNet reaches 71.22% accuracy, a 4-point improvement over the next-best GeoCrowdNet(F) at 67.22%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COINNet outperforms all 10 baselines on every dataset tested. The largest margin is on ImageNet-15N (93.71% vs. MaxMIG's 81.13%, a 12.58-point gap), where the high annotator error rate (42.68%) and large crowd size (100 annotators) amplify the benefit of the crowd-induced subspace geometry. On CIFAR-10N, COINNet leads MaxMIG by 1.98 points (92.09% vs. 90.11%), and on LabelMe it edges out GeoCrowdNet(F) by 0.39 points (87.60% vs. 87.21%). Instance-dependent single-annotator methods (PTD, BLTM, MEIDTM) degrade severely under high machine noise (e.g., PTD drops to 61.98% on CIFAR-10 high noise vs. COINNet's 71.22%), likely due to error accumulation in their multi-stage pipelines. Instance-independent confusion-based methods (VolMinNet, Reweight, GeoCrowdNet(W)) are consistently outperformed, confirming that explicitly modeling instance-dependent outliers provides a meaningful advantage over assuming a single shared confusion matrix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the confusion-matrix-based noisy label learning framework (Patrini et al., 2017; Li et al., 2021) and the coupled cross-entropy crowdsourcing formulation (Ibrahim et al., 2023) by introducing a hybrid model where a nominal instance-invariant confusion matrix is perturbed by sparse, instance-dependent outliers. The key conceptual departure is the geometric insight that multiple annotators create a low-dimensional subspace (range of W) in which nominal data reside but outliers are unlikely to fall, enabling provable outlier separation via a column-corrupted matrix factorization perspective. Unlike prior instance-dependent approaches (PTD, BLTM, MEIDTM) that rely on multi-stage training and lack identifiability guarantees, COINNet provides a one-stage differentiable loss with formal identifiability and generalization theorems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that single-annotator noisy label learning with instance-dependent outliers is fundamentally unidentifiable (Fact 2.1), a result that challenges the common practice of applying sparsity priors to handle outliers in deep networks. This motivates crowdsourcing not merely as a practical data-collection strategy but as a theoretical necessity for identifiability. Limitations include the assumption that outliers are sparse (not overwhelming) and the structure-agnostic treatment of outlier perturbations, which forgoes learning the outlier-generating mechanism. Open questions include extending the framework to fully instance-dependent noise (no nominal matrix) and exploiting outlier structure for proactive annotation quality control.
