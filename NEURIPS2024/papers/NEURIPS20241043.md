## Problem

Current MLLMs predominantly focus on improving the linguistic side—larger LLMs and higher-quality instruction data—while treating the visual encoder as a frozen black box that supplies only its final high-level features to the connector. This paper asks whether the multi-layer visual features already computed inside a frozen ViT can be leveraged as a 'free lunch' to enrich the visual signal without any additional training cost on the encoder.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241043/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at 336px, 384px, or dynamic resolution (via AnyRes tiling) paired with a text question, and generates a free-form text response. For video understanding, T frames are uniformly sampled and each is independently processed through the frozen visual encoder and Dense Connector before the resulting embedding sequence is fed to the LLM. The Efficient Dense Connector variant applies a parameter-free 2D bilinear interpolation to downsample visual tokens from 576 to 144, yielding a 3× inference speedup while maintaining LLaVA-v1.5-level accuracy.

## Method

The Dense Connector replaces the standard single-layer projection with a plug-and-play module that fuses multi-layer features from a frozen ViT before passing them through a two-layer MLP projector. Three instantiations are proposed: Sparse Token Integration (STI) concatenates average-pooled tokens from selected shallow/middle layers with the final-layer tokens along the token dimension; Sparse Channel Integration (SCI) concatenates features from selected layers along the channel dimension, letting the MLP simultaneously fuse and project; and Dense Channel Integration (DCI) partitions all L layers into G groups, averages within each group, then concatenates the G group summaries with the final layer along the channel dimension. DCI achieves the best results (e.g., +2.7% on SQA, +2.5% on MMBench over LLaVA-1.5) with zero additional parameters beyond the standard MLP. Training follows the standard two-stage recipe: pre-training the connector alone on image-caption pairs, then jointly fine-tuning the connector and LLM on instruction data, with the visual encoder kept frozen throughout.

## Results

Using the DCI instantiation with SigLIP-ViT-SO (384px) and Vicuna-13B, the Dense Connector achieves 73.0% on ScienceQA, 71.4% on MMBench, 41.6% on MM-Vet, and 65.4% on GQA, surpassing LLaVA-v1.5 (71.6/67.7/36.1/63.3) by 1.4/3.7/5.5/2.1 points respectively. Scaling to Llama3-70B (LoRA) yields 82.4% on ScienceQA and 79.4% on MMBench. With the larger Mini-Gemini dataset (1.2M+1.5M) and AnyRes, the 34B model reaches 81.2% on MMBench, 59.2% on MM-Vet, and 97.7% on LLaVA-Bench-in-the-Wild, approaching GPT-4V's 98%. On video benchmarks (zero-shot via FreeVA), the 34B model achieves 77.4% on MSVD-QA, 62.1% on MSRVTT-QA, and 55.8% on ActivityNet-QA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-v1.5 (same Vicuna-13B, same 0.5M+0.6M data), the Dense Connector wins on GQA (+2.1), MMBench (+3.7), MM-Vet (+5.5), and ScienceQA (+1.4), confirming that multi-layer feature fusion alone provides substantial gains without data or architecture changes. Compared to Mini-Gemini (dual-encoder, 1.2M+1.5M data), the DC with Vicuna-13B on the smaller LLaVA-1.5 dataset still surpasses it on GQA (+2.0), MMBench (+2.9), and MathVista (+2.6), though it trails on MM-Vet (41.6 vs 46.0) due to the data gap. Against LLaVA-NeXT (AnyRes, Vicuna-13B), the DC with AnyRes and 1.2M+1.5M data wins on MMBench (72.3 vs 70.0) and ScienceQA (75.2 vs 73.6) but loses on MM-Vet (47.0 vs 48.4). Versus LLaVA-LLaMA3 (Llama3-8B, same data), the DC achieves +5.5 on MMBench (74.4 vs 68.9) and +52 on MME (1558 vs 1506). On video QA (zero-shot, FreeVA), the DC+FreeVA 34B model outperforms LLaVA+FreeVA 13B (JAN) by +3.0 on MSVD-QA (77.4 vs 74.4), +1.0 on MSRVTT-QA (62.1 vs 61.1), and +4.2 on ActivityNet-QA (55.8 vs 51.6), and surpasses all video-tuned baselines (Video-ChatGPT, VaQuitA, BT-Adapter) on MSVD-QA without any video-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard MLLM architecture (exemplified by LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, and Mini-Gemini) by replacing the conventional single-layer visual feature input to the connector with multi-layer features from a frozen ViT. The idea draws a direct analogy to classic CV architectures like DenseNet and FPN that fuse multi-scale features, but repurposes it for the MLLM setting where the visual encoder is frozen and intermediate features are already computed at no extra cost. For video understanding, the paper follows FreeVA's training-free paradigm, extending image-trained models to video without any video-specific tuning. The contribution is deliberately orthogonal to the dominant trend of scaling LLMs or increasing training data, instead targeting the underexploited visual signal side.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Dense Connector offers a zero-parameter, plug-and-play module that consistently improves MLLMs across 19 benchmarks, multiple visual encoders (CLIP, SigLIP), LLM sizes (2.7B–70B), and architectures (LLaVA, LLaVA-NeXT, Mini-Gemini), making it an attractive default component for future MLLM pipelines. A notable negative result is that adding learnable parameters (1D/2D convolutions, linear layers) to the connector actually degrades performance, suggesting the simple two-layer MLP is near-optimal for this randomly-initialized bridge under limited training data. Open questions include how to effectively incorporate additional parameters, whether the gains persist with larger training data or longer training, and whether the approach transfers to other frozen encoder types beyond ViT.
