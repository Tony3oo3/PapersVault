## Problem

Causal bandit algorithms have been studied under either known causal graphs with unknown intervention distributions, or unknown graphs with restrictive do interventions that remove stochasticity and ancestral dependencies. The fully general setting—unknown graph structure combined with unknown stochastic soft intervention models—remained open, as the lack of topology knowledge requires learning all conditional independencies while soft interventions retain all causal paths to the reward node.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240755/cropped_page14_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives only the number of nodes N, maximum in-degree d, time horizon T, and the identifiability parameter η; it does not require knowledge of the graph skeleton, edge orientations, or any pre-/post-interventional distributions. At each round it observes the full N-dimensional data vector X(t) after pulling an intervention arm (a subset of nodes to intervene on), and outputs a sequence of interventions, estimated parent sets for all nodes, ridge-regression estimates of the observational and interventional weight matrices B and B*, and a valid topological ordering. The intervention space has cardinality 2^N, so the algorithm must navigate this combinatorial space without exhaustive search.

## Method

GA-LCB operates in two phases. First, GA-LCB-SL identifies the graph skeleton by exploiting mean shifts under single-node soft interventions to recover ancestor sets (via thresholding |μ̂_{j,∅} − μ̂_{j,{i}}| > η/2), then applies Lasso regression on the ancestor-restricted design matrix to recover parent sets with guaranteed support containment and bounded over-estimation (|pâ(i)| ≤ κ|pa(i)|). Second, GA-LCB-ID estimates the weight matrices via ridge regression and computes UCB widths recursively through causal depth (w_{i,a} = Σ_{j∈pâ(i)} w_{j,a} + α·‖μ̂_{pâ(i)}‖_{V^{-1}} + m·λ_min^{-1/2}), avoiding the non-convex optimization over confidence ellipsoids that makes existing UCB-based causal bandit methods intractable. A phased elimination scheme then successively narrows the candidate intervention set by discarding arms whose UCB width falls below m·2^{-s}, reducing the final argmax to a small subset of near-optimal interventions and yielding a computationally scalable algorithm.

## Results

For the unknown-graph setting with soft interventions, GA-LCB achieves a cumulative regret upper bound of Õ((κd)^{L−1/2}√T + d + RN), where κ>1 is a constant, d is the maximum in-degree, L is the maximum causal depth, and R = m²/η² measures intervention power. When the graph skeleton is known, the bound tightens to Õ(d^{L−1/2}√T), which is a d^L·√N improvement over the prior best known bound Õ(d^{2L−1}√T) [18]. A matching minimax lower bound of Ω(d^{L−3/2}√T) is established, leaving only a factor-of-d gap between upper and lower bounds. Empirically, GA-LCB demonstrates exponential scaling in L and polynomial (super-linear) scaling in d, consistent with theory, while significantly reducing computational time compared to LinSEM-UCB and GCB-UCB on larger graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the known-graph skeleton setting, GA-LCB achieves Õ(d^{L−1/2}√T), improving upon LinSEM-UCB [15] by a factor of d^L·√N (which had Õ(d^{2L−3/2}√(NT))) and GCB-UCB [18] by a factor of d^L (which had Õ(d^{2L−1}√T)). Both prior methods are marked as non-scalable due to intractable UCB optimization over 2^N interventions, whereas GA-LCB uses phased elimination and iterative UCB width computation to remain polynomial-time. Against CN-UCB [20], which handles unknown graphs but only under do interventions (removing stochasticity and ancestral dependencies), GA-LCB addresses the strictly more general soft-intervention setting while maintaining a comparable dependence on d and T. Empirically, on small hierarchical graphs (L=2, d=3), LinSEM-UCB and GCB-UCB exhibit slightly lower cumulative regret, but GA-LCB's advantage in computational time becomes decisive at L=4 and L=6 where the baselines face scaling challenges. The additional regret cost of unknown-graph structure learning (the d + RN term) is empirically observed to diminish as T grows, confirming the theoretical prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads in causal bandits: (i) known-graph soft-intervention algorithms (LinSEM-UCB [15], GCB-UCB [18]) that face intractable non-convex UCB optimization, and (ii) unknown-graph algorithms (CN-UCB [20]) that rely on the simplifying structure of do interventions. By combining unknown graph skeletons with general soft stochastic interventions, the paper addresses the setting explicitly identified as open in prior work. Algorithmically, it departs from the standard UCB-maximization approach by computing confidence widths iteratively through causal depth and using phased elimination, which avoids both the non-convex optimization over weight matrices and the exponential search over 2^N interventions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a significant gap in the causal bandits literature by providing nearly minimax-optimal regret bounds (upper/lower gap of only a factor d, versus the previous d^L gap) for the most general linear CB setting. The diminishing dependence of regret on graph size N as T grows is practically important for large networks where the intervention space is exponential. Open questions include closing the remaining d-factor gap, extending to non-linear SEMs, and understanding whether the conjectured tighter L=1 bound holds. The scalability of the algorithm relative to existing soft-intervention CB methods is a concrete practical contribution, though empirical validation is limited to small hierarchical graphs.
