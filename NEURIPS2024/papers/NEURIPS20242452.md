## Problem

Existing causal temporal representation learning methods require either direct observation of domain (regime) variables or a Markov prior on them to handle nonstationary transitions, limiting their use when domain shifts are unobserved and do not follow Markov dynamics. This paper asks: under what conditions on transition variability can we identify distribution shifts and latent causal processes from general nonstationary sequence data without any prior knowledge of the domain variable distribution?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242452/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of observations x_t ∈ R^n (e.g., video frame features or time-series vectors) with no access to domain indices or their generative distribution, and produces both discrete domain variable assignments u_t ∈ {1,…,U} and continuous latent causal processes z_t with time-delayed causal dependencies. In the real-world action segmentation application, the output is a per-frame action label sequence, evaluated via frame-level accuracy (MoF), segment overlap (IoU), and detection overlap (IoD). The mixing function g is assumed invertible, so the latent space matches the observation dimensionality.

## Method

CtrlNS is a VAE framework with three jointly optimized modules: (1) a Sparse Transition module that maintains U separate transition networks selected via a Gumbel-Softmax gating function, regularized by an L2 sparsity loss on the Jacobian support matrix to enforce that transitions within a domain are sparse and distinct across domains; (2) a Prior Network that enforces conditional independence of latent components using isomorphic noise distributions and a lower-triangular Jacobian structure; and (3) an Encoder-Decoder for the invertible mixing function. The key theoretical insight is that minimizing empirical transition complexity (the sum of non-zero entries in the Jacobian support matrix) uniquely identifies the correct domain partition up to label swapping, provided transitions are weakly diverse lossy and mechanisms are sufficiently variable across domains. Training proceeds in three phases: sparsity constraints first recover u_t, then conditional independence constraints (given correct u_t) recover z_t up to permutation and component-wise invertible transformations, as formalized in Theorems 1 and 2.

## Results

On synthetic data, CtrlNS achieves a mean correlation coefficient (MCC) of 96.74±0.17 for latent causal process recovery and 98.21±0.05% clustering accuracy for domain variable identification, nearly matching the upper bound set by TDRL with ground-truth domain indices (MCC 96.93±0.16). On the Hollywood action segmentation benchmark, CtrlNS achieves MoF 52.9, IoU 32.7, and IoD 52.4, outperforming the strongest baseline ATBA (MoF 47.7, IoU 28.5, IoD 44.9). On CrossTask, CtrlNS reaches MoF 54.0 and IoU 15.7, matching ATBA on IoU while improving MoF by 3.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CtrlNS dramatically outperforms all baselines on the synthetic identifiability task: it surpasses the best non-domain-aware method i-VAE (MCC 81.60) by ~15 points, the nonstationary method NCTRL (MCC 47.27) by ~50 points, and HMNLICA (MCC 17.82) by ~79 points, while nearly matching the oracle upper bound TDRL(GT) at MCC 96.93. Among methods that estimate domain variables, CtrlNS achieves 98.21% domain accuracy versus NCTRL's 34.94% and HMNLICA's 13.67%, confirming that the sparse transition assumption is critical for reliable domain recovery. On Hollywood action segmentation, CtrlNS improves over ATBA by 5.2 MoF, 4.2 IoU, and 7.5 IoD points, and over CDFL by 7.9 MoF and 13.2 IoU. On CrossTask, CtrlNS leads on MoF (54.0 vs. 50.6 for ATBA) but ties ATBA on IoU (15.7) and is marginally lower on IoD (23.6 vs. 24.6), suggesting the sparse transition module is most beneficial when domain shifts are more pronounced. Methods relying on Markov priors for domain variables (HMNLICA, NCTRL) perform poorly under the non-Markov nonstationarity tested here, validating the paper's core motivation for moving beyond Markov assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal temporal representation learning (Ctrl) line of work, building directly on the VAE-based TDRL framework (Yao et al., 2022) for temporal nonlinear ICA and the ATBA architecture (Xu & Zheng, 2024) for real-world action segmentation. The key departure is from prior nonstationary Ctrl methods (HMNLICA, NCTRL) that require either observed domain indices or a Markov chain prior on domain variables; instead, CtrlNS introduces a sparse transition assumption—constraining the Jacobian support complexity of transition functions—to identify domain shifts without any structural prior on the domain process. Theoretically, it combines the sufficient variability identifiability machinery from TDRL with a new sparsity-based identifiability theorem for the domain variables, yielding the first result for nonstationary time-delayed causal latent processes with unobserved, non-Markovian domain variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identifiability theorem provides a principled, assumption-light route to recovering both domain shifts and latent causal dynamics from purely observational sequential data, removing a major practical barrier (needing domain labels or Markov structure) that has limited prior Ctrl methods. The sparse transition module is designed as a plug-in that can be integrated into existing architectures, as demonstrated on action segmentation, suggesting broad applicability. However, the core assumption that causal transition graphs must differ across domains (even if only by one edge) remains a limitation; the higher-order derivative extension (Corollary 1) partially addresses this but cannot handle cases where all derivative supports are identical. Open questions include whether the identifiability conditions hold in highly structured or low-variability real-world settings, and whether the sequential prior network (computing each latent dimension independently) can be made more efficient for longer sequences.
