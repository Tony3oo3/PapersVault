## Problem

Despite the empirical success of large-scale Transformers, it remains unclear why gradient-based optimization reliably reaches global minima in these highly nonconvex landscapes. Existing mean-field convergence guarantees for deep networks (e.g., ResNets) depend on full parameter homogeneity and global Lipschitz smoothness of the network gradient—properties that Transformer architectures with softmax or ReLU activations do not satisfy. This paper provides the first rigorous global convergence guarantee for training large-scale Transformers via gradient flow.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an input sequence H ∈ R^{D×(N+1)} of N+1 tokens (each D-dimensional) paired with a scalar target y(H), drawn i.i.d. from a population distribution μ. The setting is designed to accommodate in-context learning, where the last token x_{N+1} is the query and the first N tokens provide context with fixed positional encodings. The output is a scalar prediction extracted by a read-out function (the (d+1, N+1)-th entry of the final hidden state), and the theoretical output is a risk bound showing R̂(Θ^{(τ)}) → 0 as depth L → ∞ and width M/log L → ∞, up to an O(λ) regularization term.

## Method

The paper constructs the mean-field limit of a deep Transformer with L blocks and M heads per block, showing that as L, M → ∞ the discrete gradient flow converges to a Wasserstein gradient flow governed by a McKean-Vlasov PDE over the parameter distribution ρ(θ,w,t). A key technical innovation is that the two distinct encoders—self-attention f and feed-forward h—alternate in the discrete model but uniformly average to (f+h)/2 in the continuous limit, validated rigorously. The analysis requires only partial 1-homogeneity (on a subset of parameters) and local Lipschitz continuity of gradients in expectation, rather than the full homogeneity and global Lipschitz smoothness demanded by prior ResNet results, thereby accommodating softmax and ReLU activations. Global convergence to the minimum of the unregularized risk is then established under a small weight-decay parameter λ, leveraging the universal approximation capability of either the attention or feed-forward encoder and a separation condition on the limiting parameter support.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative results are theoretical convergence bounds. Theorem 3.1 establishes that the gap between the discrete Transformer's gradient flow and its continuous Wasserstein gradient flow limit is bounded by O(L⁻¹ + √((δ + log(L+1))/M)) for the model output, risk, and regularized risk simultaneously. Theorem 4.1 proves that the training risk R̂(Θ^(τ)) converges to at most ε + C₁(L⁻¹ + √((δ + log(L+1))/M)) + C₂λ as τ → ∞, meaning the risk approaches zero as L → ∞ and M/log L → ∞ with a small weight-decay parameter λ. A simple experiment in Appendix H on CIFAR-10 with Vision Transformers confirms near-zero training loss and 100% training accuracy at the predicted sufficient widths and depths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or comparison tables. Its contribution is positioned relative to prior theoretical frameworks for deep network convergence. Compared to Lu et al. (ICML 2020) and Ding et al. (2021, JMLR 2022), which require full 1-homogeneity of all parameters and global Lipschitz smoothness of network gradients, this paper relaxes both to partial 1-homogeneity (only a subset of parameters) and local Lipschitz continuity in expectation, thereby accommodating softmax and sigmoid activations alongside ReLU. Unlike the ResNet models in those works that use a single identical encoder per block, this paper handles two distinct encoders (self-attention f and feed-forward h) that alternate in depth, showing their continuous limit uniformly interprets them as an average (f+h)/2. The Wasserstein gradient flow framework from Chizat & Bach (NeurIPS 2018) is extended from two-layer networks to deep Transformers with skip connections, and the propagation-of-chaos analysis is adapted to the non-i.i.d. particle structure inherent in the alternating encoder architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-field/Wasserstein gradient flow framework established for overparameterized two-layer networks (Chizat & Bach, 2018; Mei et al., 2018) and deep ResNets (Lu et al., 2020; Ding et al., 2021, 2022) to the Transformer architecture. The key technical departure is relaxing the homogeneity and global Lipschitz smoothness assumptions required by prior ResNet analyses to partial 1-homogeneity and local Lipschitz continuity in expectation, which is necessary to accommodate softmax and ReLU activations in self-attention and feed-forward layers. Additionally, unlike prior ResNet models that use a single identical encoder per block, this paper handles two distinct encoders (self-attention f and feed-forward h) that alternate in depth, showing their continuous limit uniformly reduces to the average (f+h)/2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous global convergence guarantee for gradient flow training of large-scale Transformers, addressing a fundamental gap in understanding why gradient-based methods succeed despite the highly nonconvex landscape. The novel mean-field techniques (partial homogeneity, local Lipschitz conditions) are of independent interest and broaden the applicability of convergence theory beyond smooth-activation networks. However, the results are asymptotic (requiring L→∞ and M/log L→∞) and depend on additional assumptions (weak convergence of the Wasserstein flow, long-time boundedness, and a separation property) that remain conjectural. Open questions include extending to discrete gradient descent with explicit step sizes, characterizing when self-attention layers serve as universal kernels, and establishing generalization bounds for finite-sample training.
