## Problem

One-shot federated learning methods based on data-free knowledge distillation suffer from two-tier information loss: local data is first compressed into a model, then the model is used to generate synthetic data that further loses information. Additionally, data heterogeneity causes local models to produce inconsistent and misleading predictions, degrading the quality of generated data and limiting server model performance, especially on complex datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243258/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives a pre-trained Autoencoder (encoder and decoder, e.g., from Stable Diffusion) distributed by the server during a preparation phase, along with its own local pre-trained model used as the V-information observer. Clients process local RGB images (64×64 to 256×256 resolution) and transmit only compact latent codes (not full images or model parameters) to the server, which decodes them and trains a global model. The Autoencoder is pre-defined offline and reusable across multiple one-shot FL tasks, so its transmission cost is excluded from per-round communication budgets.

## Method

FedSD2C replaces model-sharing with synthetic distillate communication in three stages. First, a V-information-based Core-Set selection identifies the most informative image patches per class using the local pre-trained model as observer, preserving diversity and realism. Second, Fourier transform perturbation linearly interpolates amplitude components of Core-Set samples (while preserving phase) to enhance privacy while retaining semantic content. Third, a pre-trained Autoencoder acts as a distiller: latent codes are initialized from the perturbed Core-Set and optimized in the compact latent space to minimize V-information discrepancy with the original Core-Set, with the latent space acting as a regularizer against overfitting to local model idiosyncrasies. The server decodes the aggregated latents and trains via KL divergence against soft labels predicted by local models, transferring knowledge end-to-end without the two-tier loss inherent in generate-then-distill pipelines.

## Results

On the most challenging non-IID setting (α=0.1), FedSD2C with ResNet-18 achieves 47.52% accuracy on ImageNette, 26.83% on Tiny-ImageNet, and 22.69% on OpenImage, outperforming the best baselines by up to 2.6× (Tiny-ImageNet: 26.83% vs. Co-Boosting's 10.29%). With ConvNet, FedSD2C reaches 50.68% on ImageNette and 20.73% on Tiny-ImageNet at α=0.1. Communication cost is at most 4% of sharing full models (e.g., 0.5 MB vs. 44 MB for DENSE/Co-Boosting on ImageNette at ipc=50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSD2C consistently outperforms all four main baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity level. Against DENSE (NeurIPS 2022), FedSD2C gains +8.6 pp on ImageNette (α=0.1, ConvNet) and +17.9 pp on Tiny-ImageNet (α=0.1, ResNet-18), a 2.6× improvement. Against Co-Boosting (2024), the gap is even larger under extreme heterogeneity: +20.5 pp on Tiny-ImageNet (ResNet-18, α=0.1) and +9.1 pp on ImageNette (ConvNet, α=0.1), while Co-Boosting degrades severely with ResNet-18 (27.06% vs. 39.36% for ConvNet on ImageNette). F-DAFL (ICCV 2019) is competitive on ImageNette at α=0.5 (58.34% vs. 58.17%) but falls far behind on Tiny-ImageNet and OpenImage. FedAVG (AISTATS 2017) is near random accuracy in all one-shot settings. FedMix (2021) is compared only in the privacy evaluation (Table 2), where it achieves lower PSNR/SSIM (better privacy) but at a large accuracy cost (41.86% vs. 50.68% on ImageNette, ConvNet).

## Relation to Prior Work

FedSD2C departs from the dominant data-free knowledge distillation (DFKD) paradigm in one-shot FL (DENSE, Co-Boosting), which generates synthetic data from client models via GANs or ensemble distillation. Instead, it reframes the communication problem as end-to-end dataset distillation: local data is compressed via V-information-based Core-Set selection (inspired by Sun et al. [42]), then further distilled into compact latent codes using a pre-trained Autoencoder as a 'distiller.' This avoids the two-tier information loss inherent in the data→model→generated-data pipeline, and the use of Fourier transform perturbation for privacy is a novel twist on standard noise-based defenses. The method thus bridges the dataset distillation literature with one-shot FL, repurposing Core-Set selection and pre-trained generative priors in a federated context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-shot FL is critical for cross-device and bandwidth-constrained deployments where even a single round of model upload is costly or risky, and this paper shows that sharing synthetic distillates (at most 4% of model communication cost) can substantially outperform model-sharing or data-generation baselines under data heterogeneity. However, the approach introduces non-trivial client-side computation (Core-Set selection + 50 iterations of latent optimization per image), which may be prohibitive on resource-constrained edge devices. Open questions include whether the method extends to non-image modalities, whether the pre-trained Autoencoder requirement limits cross-domain applicability (mitigated but not eliminated by increasing T_syn), and how it interacts with formal differential privacy guarantees beyond the DP-SGD integration shown in the appendix.
