## Problem

Existing time series generation methods—both unconditional and conditional—synthesize samples from scratch and cannot manipulate an existing time series to modify specific attributes while preserving others. This paper introduces Time Series Editing (TSE), a new task that answers 'what-if' questions about attribute changes on a given sample, and identifies two core challenges: inadequate data coverage over the full composited attribute space, and the fact that different attributes influence time series at different scales (e.g., trends globally, seasonality locally).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241186/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source time series x^src of length L (128 for Synthetic, 168 for Air Quality, 150 for Motor Imagery), its K categorical source attributes a^src, and a target attribute vector a^tgt in which K_edit attributes are changed and K_prsv are held fixed. It outputs an edited time series x^tgt that aligns with the target attributes while retaining the source's noise, bias, and other fine-grained characteristics. The editing is performed via a two-stage deterministic DDIM process: a forward pass encodes (x^src, a^src) into a shared latent x_T^src, and a reverse pass decodes (x_T^src, a^tgt) into the final sample, so the latent carries source-specific detail into the target generation.

## Method

TEdit is a conditional diffusion model built on CSDI or Time Weaver backbones, with two key innovations. First, a multi-resolution noise estimator patches the input at R resolutions with exponentially growing patch lengths (L_p^r = b^{r-1}), processes each resolution independently via Transformer self-attention conditioned on attributes and diffusion step, then fuses per-resolution noise estimates through an MLP; an attention-masking scheme enables all resolutions to be processed in a single parallel GPU pass. Second, a bootstrap learning algorithm addresses the lack of ground-truth source-target pairs by pretraining on the standard noise-estimation loss, then iteratively composing target attributes, generating edited samples, self-scoring them by editing back to the source and computing MSE, retaining only the top-ψ highest-confidence samples, and finetuning on those pseudo-labels to progressively improve coverage of the attribute space.

## Results

On the Synthetic dataset (the only one with ground-truth targets), TEdit-TW achieves the best edited-attribute RaTS of 1.0121 and CTAP of 0.7957, while TEdit-CSDI achieves the lowest overall MSE (0.1235) and MAE (0.2606), both substantially below CSDI (MSE 0.1789) and Time Weaver (MSE 0.1454). On Air Quality, TEdit-TW leads on edited RaTS (0.9661 vs. 0.8956 for Time Weaver) and edited CTAP (0.3930 vs. 0.3266). On Motor Imagery, TEdit-TW achieves the highest edited RaTS (0.1212) and edited CTAP (0.4348), while all methods show comparable preserved-attribute scores, confirming that editing does not degrade preserved attributes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEdit consistently outperforms both baselines on edited-attribute metrics across all three datasets. On Synthetic, TEdit-TW improves edited RaTS from 0.9030 (Time Weaver) to 1.0121 and edited CTAP from 0.6943 to 0.7957, while TEdit-CSDI reduces overall MSE from 0.1789 (CSDI) to 0.1235 and MAE from 0.3221 to 0.2606. On Air Quality, TEdit-TW achieves the highest edited RaTS (0.9661 vs. 0.8956 for Time Weaver) and edited CTAP (0.3930 vs. 0.3266), though it slightly worsens preserved |RaTS| (0.1916 vs. 0.1866). On Motor Imagery, TEdit-TW leads on edited RaTS (0.1212 vs. 0.0979) and edited CTAP (0.4348 vs. 0.4168) over Time Weaver, while TEdit-CSDI marginally beats CSDI on edited RaTS (0.1016 vs. 0.0939). For preserved attributes, all methods maintain comparable |RaTS| and CTAP scores, indicating that the editing process does not significantly degrade non-edited characteristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of conditional diffusion-based time series generation (CSDI, Time Weaver) and the image editing paradigm (DiffusionCLIP, Prompt-to-Prompt), transplanting the 'edit an existing sample while preserving other properties' formulation into the time series domain. It departs from prior time series generation work, which is limited to unconditional or conditional synthesis from scratch, by introducing a two-stage DDIM-based editing procedure that encodes a source sample into latent space and decodes it under modified attributes. The multi-resolution noise estimator draws on the patching mechanism from PatchTST but extends it to multiple granularities with parallel attention masking, and the bootstrap learning algorithm is a novel self-supervised finetuning strategy that has no direct analogue in prior time series generation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TSE addresses a practically important 'what-if' question in time series analysis that neither unconditional nor conditional generation can answer, with direct applications in climate monitoring, healthcare, and urban management where data is sparse and privacy-sensitive. The released benchmark with synthetic and real-world datasets (Air Quality, Motor Imagery) provides a foundation for future work. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include complex inter-dependencies between attributes, varying difficulty of editing different attributes, and the restriction to categorical attributes; open questions include extending to continuous attributes, multi-channel time series, and scaling to high-dimensional attribute spaces where bootstrap coverage becomes harder to guarantee.
