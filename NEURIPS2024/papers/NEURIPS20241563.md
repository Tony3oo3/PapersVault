## Problem

Existing equivariant diffusion models for 3D molecule generation pre-specify the forward noising process (e.g., VP-SDE with a fixed schedule), which constrains the latent space and forces the reverse generative process to comply with a rigid corruption trajectory. This limits the model's ability to adapt the noising to the data distribution, potentially degrading both unconditional sample quality and conditional controllability. The paper asks whether replacing the fixed forward process with a learnable, equivariant transformation can improve generation while preserving E(3) invariance of the learned marginal.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes a random noise sample ε from a unit Gaussian prior (a collection of vectors in R^{(M−1)×3} for coordinates and R^{M×D} for atomic features) and produces 3D atomic coordinates in the zero-center-of-mass subspace along with continuous relaxations of atomic types. For conditional generation, an additional condition vector is consumed: either a discrete composition vector c ∈ Z^D specifying atom counts, or a binary molecular fingerprint c ∈ {0,1}^F encoding substructure presence. The number of atoms M is sampled from an empirical distribution before generation, and the number of SDE integration steps (50–1000) is a free hyperparameter at sampling time.

## Method

END defines the forward process through a learnable equivariant transformation F_φ(ε, t, x) = μ_φ(x,t) + U_φ(x,t)ε, where μ_φ and U_φ are equivariant neural networks producing a data- and time-dependent mean and a block-diagonal (3×3 blocks) scaling matrix, respectively. The parameterization is constrained so that the conditional marginal q_φ(z_t|x) is a Gaussian that interpolates from N(x, δ²I) at t=0 to N(0, I) at t=1, while remaining fully unconstrained in between. The reverse process is driven by an equivariant data-point predictor x̂_θ(z_t, t), and training jointly optimizes both F_φ and x̂_θ via a drift-matching objective that equates the true and approximate conditional reverse SDE drifts. Equivariance of F_φ and x̂_θ, combined with an invariant prior, provably guarantees that the learned marginal p_{θ,φ}(z_0) is invariant to all E(3) transformations. For conditional generation, the condition c is simply appended as an extra input to both F_φ and x̂_θ, making the forward process itself condition-dependent—a distinction from standard conditional DMs where only the reverse process is conditioned.

## Results

On QM9 (1000 steps), END achieves 94.8% validity, 92.6% validity×uniqueness, and 89.1% molecule stability, outperforming the matched-architecture baseline EDM* (93.5% validity, 91.9% V×U, 85.3% M-stability) and approaching SOTA methods GEOLDM (93.8% V) and GEOBFN (95.3% V). On GEOM-DRUGS, END reaches 79.4% validity×connectivity (vs. 64.2% for EDM* and 45.8% for GEOLDM) with substantially lower strain energy (55.0 vs. 92.9 kcal/mol for EDM*). For conditional generation, CEND achieves 91.0% composition matching (vs. 75.5% for cEDM*) and 0.828 Tanimoto similarity for substructure conditioning (vs. 0.673 for cEDM* and 0.750 for EEGSDE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

END outperforms the matched-architecture baseline EDM* across all metrics on both datasets: on QM9 it gains +1.3% validity and +3.8% molecule stability, while on GEOM-DRUGS it achieves +15.2% validity×connectivity (79.4% vs. 64.2%) and reduces strain energy by ~40% (55.0 vs. 92.9 kcal/mol). Against the original EDM and EDM-Bridge, END improves QM9 validity by ~3% and GEOM-DRUGS connectivity by a wide margin. Compared to the SOTA latent diffusion model GEOLDM, END matches or exceeds validity on QM9 (94.8% vs. 93.8%) and dramatically outperforms it on GEOM-DRUGS connectivity (79.4% vs. 45.8%), though GEOLDM reports higher raw validity on GEOM-DRUGS (99.3% vs. 89.2%) because it does not penalize disconnected fragments. Against GEOBFN (the strongest QM9 baseline), END is slightly behind on validity (94.8% vs. 95.3%) but achieves lower total variation (0.9 vs. not reported) and lower strain energy (9.7 vs. not reported). In conditional generation, CEND substantially outperforms cEDM* on composition matching (91.0% vs. 75.5%) and substructure Tanimoto similarity (0.828 vs. 0.673), and also surpasses EEGSDE (0.750) which uses an additional property regressor for guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

END synthesizes two prior lines of work: the Equivariant Diffusion Model (EDM, Hoogeboom et al., 2022), which enforces E(3) invariance through equivariant score networks and a fixed VP-SDE forward process, and Neural Flow Diffusion Models (NFDM, Bartosh et al., 2024), which replace the pre-specified forward process with a learnable transformation. The key conceptual departure is making the forward process both data-dependent and equivariant, so that the conditional marginal q_φ(z_t|x) has learnable, time- and data-dependent mean and covariance rather than the fixed α_t·x + σ_t·ε structure of EDM. This positions END as a strict generalization of EDM (recovering it when F_φ is fixed to the VP-SDE form) and a natural extension of EDM-Bridge (Wu et al., 2022), where the drift is physics-inspired but still hand-crafted rather than learned.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The learnable forward process yields substantial gains in conditional generation (≈91% composition matching vs. ≈76% for the fixed-forward baseline; 0.828 vs. 0.673 Tanimoto for substructure), suggesting that the flexibility of the forward process matters most when the model must satisfy hard constraints. A practical by-product is improved sampling efficiency: 100 integration steps of END produce qualitatively better samples than 1000 steps of EDM*, a 3× time reduction on GEOM-DRUGS. However, each step is ≈2.5–3× more expensive, and the fully-connected graph representation limits scalability to very large molecules. Open questions include whether straighter generative trajectories (via NFDM's ODE formulation) can further accelerate sampling, how to incorporate bond information, and whether the categorical-feature relaxation can be replaced by more scalable encodings.
