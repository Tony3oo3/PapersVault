## Problem

Existing weakly supervised video violence detection methods perform representation learning exclusively in Euclidean space, which captures visual features well but fails to model the hierarchical temporal relations between events, leading to poor discrimination of ambiguous violence (e.g., hockey collisions vs. fighting). The only prior hyperbolic approach (HyperVD) uses a fixed-threshold node selection that limits hierarchical context learning, and no method has combined Euclidean and hyperbolic geometries to exploit both visual expressiveness and structural relational modeling simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240552/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips sampled at 24 fps with 16-frame sliding windows (encoded by I3D) and audio segments of 960 ms with 96×64 log-mel spectrograms (encoded by VGGish), fused via cross-modal attention before entering the dual-space pipeline. Training uses only video-level binary labels under a multiple-instance learning formulation, and the model outputs per-segment violence scores via a hyperbolic classifier with Lorentzian metric, which are then aggregated to frame-level predictions for evaluation.

## Method

DSRL operates a dual-branch architecture: a Euclidean GCN branch captures visual features, while a Hyperbolic Energy-constrained GCN (HE-GCN) branch learns hierarchical event context in the Lorentz model. HE-GCN replaces HGCN's fixed-threshold node selection with Layer-Sensitive Hyperbolic Association Degrees (LSHAD), a dynamic threshold computed from hyperbolic Dirichlet energy and the current layer index, enabling a global-to-local message aggregation strategy. A Dual-Space Interaction (DSI) module then breaks the information barrier between the two spaces using cross-space attention scored by Lorentzian similarity (rather than cosine), allowing Euclidean visual cues to inform hyperbolic relational reasoning and vice versa. The enhanced features from both spaces are concatenated, max-pooled, and fed into a hyperbolic classifier trained with binary cross-entropy under the MIL framework.

## Results

On the XD-Violence dataset, DSRL achieves state-of-the-art frame-level AP of 87.61% under multimodal input (surpassing the best Euclidean method MACIL-SD by 4.21% and the only hyperbolic method HyperVD by 1.94%) and 82.01% under unimodal input (outperforming UMIL by 0.35%). On UCF-Crime, DSRL achieves 86.38% AUC, ranking 3rd behind MGFN (86.98%) and UMIL (86.75%). The model processes video at 83.87 FPS and video+audio at 56.86 FPS on a single RTX A6000 GPU, with only 0.91 MB of additional parameters beyond the I3D/VGGish backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSRL outperforms all 12 baselines on XD-Violence in both input settings. In the multimodal setting, it surpasses the strongest Euclidean competitor MACIL-SD [37] (83.40%) by 4.21% and the sole hyperbolic method HyperVD [25] (85.67%) by 1.94%, with earlier multimodal Euclidean methods (HL-Net [34], Wu et al. [35], Pang et al. [23], UMIL [18], Zhang et al. [39]) trailing by 5.8–9.0%. In the unimodal setting, DSRL (82.01%) edges out UMIL [18] (81.66%) by 0.35% and MGFN [6] (79.19%) by 2.82%, while older unimodal baselines (Sultani [30], Wu et al. [33], RTFM [31], MSL [16], CU-Net [39]) lag by 3.2–8.8%. On UCF-Crime, DSRL (86.38% AUC) is slightly behind MGFN [6] (86.98%) and UMIL [18] (86.75%) but ahead of all other unimodal baselines, indicating competitive but not dominant generalization to this surveillance-video benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hyperbolic representation learning line initiated by HGCN (Chami et al., 2019) and its first application to VVD by HyperVD (Peng et al., 2023), replacing the fixed-threshold node selection in HGCN with dynamic layer-sensitive thresholds derived from hyperbolic Dirichlet energy. It departs from all prior VVD methods—which operate exclusively in Euclidean space—by introducing a dual-space architecture that simultaneously learns in both Euclidean and hyperbolic geometries, connected via a cross-space attention mechanism. The hyperbolic linear layer formulation is adapted from fully hyperbolic neural networks (Chen et al., 2021), and the overall MIL framework follows the standard weakly supervised VVD paradigm established by HL-Net and subsequent multimodal methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-space formulation offers a principled way to address 'ambiguous violence'—events visually indistinguishable from normal activity (e.g., hockey collisions vs. fighting)—by separating visual feature expression (Euclidean) from hierarchical event context (hyperbolic). However, the paper acknowledges that only basic audio features are leveraged, leaving richer audio-semantic content unexploited, and notes dataset bias in XD-Violence and UCF-Crime that may limit fairness and generalization. Open questions include whether the dual-space paradigm generalizes beyond VVD to other weakly supervised video tasks, and how to incorporate more detailed audio semantics without further increasing computational cost.
