## Problem

Existing pretraining data selection methods—rule-based filtering, proximity to high-quality corpora, and LLM-based rating—are static and fail to capture the evolving data preferences of a pretraining model as it progresses through different stages. This paper asks how to precisely track data influence with the pretraining model in real time and efficiently select the most effective data for the current pretraining progress.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243452/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large pretraining corpus (C4, 25B tokens), a small reference task dataset (LAMBADA, 1024 examples), and the current pretraining model state. The data influence model (BERT-base) processes individual sequences of up to 1024 tokens by splitting them into two 512-token chunks, averaging hidden representations, and regressing a scalar influence score via MSE loss. The output is a 20% selected data subset per 10k-step stage, sampled via Gumbel-Top-k with influence scores as weights, which drives the next stage of pretraining.

## Method

MATES introduces a side-by-side learning framework where a small BERT-base data influence model continuously adapts to the pretraining model's shifting data preferences. Oracle data influence is collected by locally probing: for each hold-out example, one step of training is performed on the current pretraining model, and the change in reference-task (LAMBADA) loss before and after that step defines the influence score. The data influence model is then fine-tuned on these probed scores to approximate influence over the entire corpus, and Gumbel-Top-k sampling selects the top-influence 20% of data for the next 10k-step stage. The update step U (10k steps) balances selection freshness against computational cost, and the entire selection pipeline accounts for only ~12–22% of total FLOPs, making model-aware data selection practical at scale.

## Results

On the C4 pretraining benchmark, MATES achieves an average zero-shot accuracy of 45.8% (410M model, 25B tokens) and 47.5% (1B model, 25B tokens), outperforming random selection by 1.3% and 1.1% respectively across 9 downstream tasks. MATES reduces the total FLOPs required to reach a given downstream performance by more than half compared to random selection, reaching the same 1B-scale performance with only 43.3% of the total FLOPs. The data selection procedure itself accounts for only 21.7% (410M) and 11.5% (1B) of total FLOPs, with oracle collection taking ~2.5 seconds per data point on a single GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MATES outperforms all five baselines on average zero-shot accuracy in both the 410M and 1B settings. Against random selection, MATES gains 1.3% (410M) and 1.1% (1B), with the 1B model improving across all 9 individual tasks. Compared to the strongest LLM-based baseline QuRating (which relies on GPT-3.5 for quality scoring), MATES achieves a 0.6% average gain in both settings while using substantially fewer FLOPs (8.11 vs. 26.35 for 410M; 19.97 vs. 37.67 for 1B), effectively doubling QuRating's improvement over random. Against the influence-function-based DsDm, MATES wins by 0.9% (410M) and 0.8% (1B) with lower FLOPs (8.11 vs. 10.72; 19.97 vs. 22.04). SemDeDup and DSIR, which rely on static heuristics (deduplication and n-gram proximity), trail MATES by 0.6–1.5% on average, confirming that static selection cannot capture the evolving data preferences during pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

MATES builds on the influence function framework (Koh & Liang, 2017) and its scalable approximations (TRAK, DsDm), but departs from them by making data influence *dynamic* rather than static. While prior data selection methods—rule-based filtering, proximity to high-quality corpora (DSIR), semantic deduplication (SemDeDup), and LLM-based rating (QuRating)—all compute a fixed quality signal independent of the pretraining model's current state, MATES introduces a small data influence model (BERT-base) that is continuously re-fitted to the evolving preferences of the pretraining model via local one-step probing. This positions the work at the intersection of influence functions and curriculum learning, but with a concrete, low-cost mechanism for tracking model-specific data preferences on the fly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The halving of total FLOPs needed to reach a given downstream performance is a practically significant efficiency gain for pretraining, and the method's low overhead (data selection accounts for only ~12–22% of total FLOPs) makes it attractive for adoption. However, the experiments are limited to 410M/1B models on a single dataset (C4), and the core assumption of independent pointwise influence (ignoring combinational batch effects) remains unvalidated at scale. Open questions include whether the approach scales to production-level models (tens of billions of parameters, trillions of tokens), how sensitive it is to the choice of reference task, and whether richer data influence models or combinational influence measurements could further close the gap to the oracle.
