## Problem

Contextual bandit algorithms with generalized linear (GLM) reward models have not been extended to limited adaptivity settings, where the decision maker can update its policy only M times over T rounds. While optimal regret guarantees exist for linear reward models under both batched (M1) and rarely-switching (M2) constraints, straightforward extensions to GLMs incur a linear dependence on an instance-dependent non-linearity parameter κ that can be exponential in ‖θ*‖. This paper resolves the open problem of achieving κ-independent optimal regret for GLM contextual bandits under limited adaptivity.

## Input / Output

The algorithms receive d-dimensional arm feature vectors (‖x‖≤1) each round, a budget M on the number of policy updates, and a horizon T. In M1 (B-GLinCB), the M update rounds must be fixed upfront and arm sets are drawn from an unknown distribution; in M2 (RS-GLinCB), update rounds are chosen adaptively and arms may be adversarially chosen. The output is an arm selection per round plus a regret guarantee: Õ(√T) for both algorithms when M=Ω(log log T) (M1) or M=Õ(log²T) (M2), with leading terms free of κ. The algorithms require known upper bounds on κ and S (the bound on ‖θ*‖).

## Method

B-GLinCB (M1) uses a κ-dependent warm-up batch of length τ₁=O(T^{1/3}) followed by geometrically growing batches, applying G-optimal design during warm-up and distributional optimal design on κ-scaled arm sets in subsequent batches. The scaling x̃=√(μ̇(⟨x,θ̂_w⟩)/β(x))·x, combined with a scaled design matrix H_k that estimates the GLM Hessian, leverages the self-concordance property of bounded GLMs (|μ̇(z)|≤Rμ̇(z)) to eliminate κ from the leading regret term. RS-GLinCB (M2) extends the determinant-doubling framework with a novel context-dependent Switching Criterion I that triggers exploration when ‖x‖_{V^{-1}} exceeds a κ-dependent threshold, implicitly verifying that μ̇(⟨x,θ̂_o⟩) is within a constant factor of μ̇(⟨x,θ*⟩). The determinant-doubling criterion operates on the scaled Hessian H_t rather than the unscaled design matrix, and the MLE is projected into an ellipsoid around θ̂_o (rather than a ball of radius S), yielding an S-free leading regret term that resolves a conjecture in prior logistic bandit work.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical guarantees: B-GLinCB achieves $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ regret under the M1 (upfront batch) setting when $M = \Omega(\log\log T)$, and RS-GLinCB achieves $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ regret under the M2 (adaptive) setting with only $\tilde{O}(\log^2 T)$ policy updates. Critically, the leading terms of both regret bounds are independent of the instance-dependent non-linearity parameter $\kappa$, which can be exponential in $\|\theta^*\|$. In experiments (d=5, K=20, T=20,000 for logistic; T=5,000 for probit), RS-GLinCB achieves the lowest cumulative regret with a flattened regret curve, and runs approximately 5× faster than EC0Log (low κ) and 2× faster than GLOC (low κ) in wall-clock time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RS-GLinCB is compared against three baselines: EC0Log [Faury et al., AISTATS 2022], GLOC [Jun et al., NeurIPS 2017], and GLM-UCB [Filippi et al., NeurIPS 2010]. On the logistic bandit (d=5, K=20, S=5, T=20,000), RS-GLinCB achieves the smallest cumulative regret with a visibly flattened regret curve, outperforming both EC0Log and GLOC. On the probit bandit (d=5, K=20, S=3, T=5,000), RS-GLinCB outperforms both GLOC and GLM-UCB in regret. In terms of computational efficiency, RS-GLinCB takes roughly one-fifth the execution time of EC0Log at low κ (κ=9.3) and slightly more than one-third at high κ (κ=141.6) for logistic rewards; for probit rewards, it takes less than half the time of GLOC at low κ (κ=17.6) and about two-thirds at high κ (κ=202.3). The paper also notes that RS-GLinCB is the first computationally efficient (amortized $O(\log T)$ per round) algorithm achieving $\kappa$-independent optimal regret for generalized linear bandits, whereas EC0Log and GLOC either depend on κ or lack the S-free leading term.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two distinct lines of limited-adaptivity bandit research to generalized linear reward models. For the M1 (batched) setting, it builds on Ruan et al.'s distributional optimal design framework [23] and Gao et al.'s batch length construction [9], but introduces κ-dependent batch sizing and arm-set scaling to eliminate the non-linearity parameter κ from the leading regret term. For the M2 (rarely-switching) setting, it extends Abbasi-Yadkori et al.'s determinant-doubling approach [1] with a novel context-dependent switching criterion and a scaled Hessian-based design matrix. A key theoretical departure is proving self-concordance for bounded GLMs (Lemma 2.2), a property postulated but unproven in Filippi et al. [8], which underpins all κ-independent guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to achieve κ-independent optimal regret for GLM contextual bandits under limited adaptivity, a setting motivated by practical constraints in clinical trials and online advertising where policy updates are expensive. It also resolves a conjecture in Lee et al. [17] by showing S-free regret in the leading √T term for logistic bandits, and provides the first computationally efficient (amortized O(log T) per round) algorithm with optimal κ-independent regret for general GLMs. Open questions include whether the d-dependence and κ̂ term in B-GLinCB are tight, and whether arm-geometry-dependent second-order regret terms (achieved by non-efficient algorithms in [2,17]) can be matched with computationally efficient methods.
