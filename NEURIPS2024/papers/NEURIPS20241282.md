## Problem

Existing deep learning surrogate models for molecular dynamics learn either the autoregressive transition density or the equilibrium distribution, failing to jointly model full trajectories and thus restricting applicability to forward simulation. This leaves important inverse problems—transition path sampling, trajectory upsampling, and dynamics-conditioned molecular design—unaddressed by current methods or even by MD itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241282/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model conditions on key frame roto-translations (SE(3) elements), the amino acid sequence, and ground-truth tokens for known frames or residues, with mask tokens for unknown entries. It generates a (T×L) array of SE(3)-invariant tokens of dimension 7K+14 (relative roto-translations via unit quaternions plus torsion angles on the unit circle), which are decoded to all-atom 3D coordinates. In the inpainting setting, the token dimension is augmented by 20 for Dirichlet flow matching over amino acid identities, and the model outputs both dynamics and sequence for the unknown residues.

## Method

MDGEN formulates trajectory generation as flow matching over a 2D array of SE(3)-invariant tokens, obtained by expressing each residue's roto-translation relative to one or more conditioning key frames and encoding torsion angles as points on the unit circle. The velocity network is a SiT (Scalable Interpolant Transformer) with alternating attention across residue and time dimensions, where key frame conditioning is injected via Invariant Point Attention layers whose SE(3)-invariant outputs are broadcast across time. For long trajectories (100k frames), time attention is replaced with a non-causal Hyena operator for O(N log N) scaling. A single model handles all four tasks—forward simulation, interpolation, upsampling, and inpainting—by varying which tokens are provided as conditioning versus masked, with Dirichlet flow matching integrated for discrete amino acid generation in the inpainting setting.

## Results

On forward simulation of tetrapeptides, MDGEN achieves JSD of 0.109 on all torsion angles and 0.235 on MSM state occupancies, approaching the 100-ns oracle (0.076 and 0.208) while running in 60 s versus 3 h for MD. In the direct comparison with other generative MD surrogates (Table 5), MDGEN outperforms Timewarp and ITO on all JSD metrics except MSM states (0.235 vs. 0.222), while being 10× faster than Timewarp (60 s vs. 599 s) and 35× faster than ITO (60 s vs. 2083 s). For dynamics-conditioned inpainting, MDGEN recovers the ground-truth inner residues 52.1% of the time on high-flux transitions, far exceeding DynMPNN (17.4%) and S-MPNN (16.3%). On protein ensembles (ATLAS), MDGEN achieves a median W₂ distance of 2.69 and per-target RMSF correlation of 0.71, performing between AlphaFlow and MSA subsampling while generating each structure in 0.2 s versus 70 s and 4 s respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Timewarp (Klein et al., 2024) and ITO (Schreiner et al., 2024), the two main autoregressive surrogate MD methods, MDGEN achieves substantially lower JSD on all torsion-angle and TICA metrics (e.g., 0.109 vs. 0.383 vs. 0.505 on all torsions; 0.316 vs. 0.419 vs. 0.756 on joint TICA-0,1), while Timewarp edges out MDGEN slightly on MSM state JSD (0.222 vs. 0.235). MDGEN is also dramatically faster at inference: 60 s per 100-ns trajectory versus 599 s for Timewarp and 2083 s for ITO. On protein ensemble generation (ATLAS dataset), MDGEN sits between AlphaFlow (Jing et al., 2024) and MSA subsampling with AlphaFold (Del Alamo et al., 2022): it matches AlphaFlow on pairwise RMSD correlation (0.48) and beats MSA subsampling on W₂ distance (2.69 vs. 3.62) and weak-contact JSD (0.51 vs. 0.40), but trails AlphaFlow on per-target RMSF correlation (0.71 vs. 0.85) and %PC-sim>0.5 (10 vs. 44). For dynamics-conditioned molecular inpainting, MDGEN's 52.1% sequence recovery on high-flux transitions is roughly 3× that of DynMPNN (17.4%) and 3.2× that of S-MPNN (16.3%), demonstrating the value of conditioning on full trajectory dynamics rather than one or two static frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

MDGEN departs from the two dominant paradigms for learned MD surrogates—autoregressive transition-density models (Timewarp, ITO) and equilibrium Boltzmann generators (Noe et al., AlphaFlow)—by formulating the problem as end-to-end generative modeling of entire trajectories, analogous to how image diffusion was extended to video. The architecture builds directly on SiT's stochastic interpolant transformer backbone and DiT-style attention blocks, with the key methodological innovation being an SE(3)-invariant tokenization via key-frame-relative roto-translations that allows a vanilla transformer to process molecular trajectories without equivariant layers. For the discrete amino-acid generation in inpainting, the paper integrates Dirichlet flow matching, and for long-trajectory scaling it swaps time attention for the Hyena operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The trajectory-as-video framing unlocks a suite of inverse problems—transition path sampling, trajectory upsampling, and dynamics-conditioned molecular inpainting—that were not accessible to prior single-task surrogate models and are non-trivial even for MD itself. The inpainting result (52% sequence recovery vs. 17% for a static-frame baseline) provides early evidence that conditioning on full dynamical trajectories, rather than one or two structures, carries substantially more design-relevant information. Key open questions include scaling beyond peptides to full proteins (current protein results are preliminary and sit between AlphaFlow and MSA subsampling), removing the key-frame dependency to enable unconditional generation, and extending the tokenization to non-polypeptide systems such as ligands or explicit solvent.
