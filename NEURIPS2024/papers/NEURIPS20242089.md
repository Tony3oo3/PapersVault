## Problem

KL-Div, the dominant loss in knowledge distillation, only compares probabilities of the same category between teacher and student, lacking any mechanism for cross-category comparison that could exploit rich interrelations among classes (e.g., dog is visually closer to wolf than to car). Additionally, KL-Div is ill-suited for intermediate-layer distillation because it cannot handle non-overlapping high-dimensional feature distributions and is unaware of the geometric structure of the underlying manifold. The paper asks whether Wasserstein Distance (WD), which naturally performs cross-category transport and is a Riemannian metric on Gaussian spaces, can address both limitations.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a standard teacher-student framework on RGB images: ImageNet at 224×224 (1000 classes), CIFAR-100 at 32×32 upscaled to 224×224 (100 classes), and MS-COCO for object detection (80 classes). For classification, the student outputs a class label; for detection, it outputs bounding boxes with class labels via Faster-RCNN-FPN. A notable design choice is that WKD-L requires pre-computing category interrelation matrices (via CKA on the teacher's penultimate-layer features) once before training, while WKD-F uses a small bottleneck projector (1×1 Conv + 3×3 Conv + 1×1 Conv with BN/ReLU) to align student feature dimensions with the teacher's before computing Gaussian parameters.

## Method

WKD-L replaces KL-Div with discrete WD for logit distillation: category interrelations are quantified via Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) on the teacher's features, converted to a transport cost matrix via a Gaussian kernel, and the WD between teacher and student softmax distributions is solved as an entropy-regularized linear program (Sinkhorn iterations). This enables explicit cross-category comparison, so probability mass can be 'transported' between related categories at lower cost. WKD-F models intermediate-layer feature distributions as Gaussians with diagonal covariance (estimated from spatial feature vectors) and computes the closed-form continuous WD, which decomposes into a mean term and a covariance term weighted by a mean-cov ratio γ. Unlike prior WD-based distillation (WCoRD, EMD+IPOT) that performs cross-instance matching over a mini-batch, WKD-F is instance-wise, matching the teacher's and student's per-image Gaussian distributions directly. The two losses are combined additively with the cross-entropy loss, and a target/non-target separation strategy (inspired by DKD/NKD) is applied to the logit component.

## Results

On ImageNet (ResNet34→ResNet18), WKD-L achieves 72.49% Top-1 accuracy for logit distillation, outperforming the strongest KL-Div variant WTTM (72.19%) by 0.30%. For feature distillation, WKD-F reaches 72.50% Top-1, surpassing the previous best ReviewKD (71.61%) by 0.89%. The combined WKD-L+WKD-F achieves 72.76% Top-1, beating FCFD (72.25%) and KD-Zero (72.17%). On MS-COCO object detection (RN101→RN18), the combined method with bounding-box regression reaches 37.79 mAP, outperforming FCFD† (37.37 mAP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WKD-L consistently outperforms all KL-Div-based logit distillation methods: it beats WTTM (the strongest variant, which adds sample-adaptive weighting) by 0.30% on ImageNet setting (a) and 0.08% on setting (b), and surpasses DKD by 0.79% and NKD by 0.53% in setting (a). For feature distillation, WKD-F improves over ReviewKD (previous best) by 0.89% in setting (a) and 0.56% in setting (b), and outperforms CRD by 1.33% and FitNet by 1.97%. On CIFAR-100 across CNN↔Transformer architectures, WKD-F ranks first in all five settings, exceeding the previous best competitors by 2.1%–3.4% in four out of five cases. In self-knowledge distillation (BAN framework, ResNet18 on ImageNet), WKD-L achieves 71.35% Top-1, outperforming BAN (70.50%) and USKD (70.75%). On MS-COCO object detection, the combined WKD-L+WKD-F with bounding-box regression reaches 37.79 mAP (RN101→RN18), surpassing FCFD† (37.37 mAP) by 0.42 mAP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant KL-Div paradigm in knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., DKD, NKD, WTTM) by replacing it with Wasserstein Distance, motivated by two conceptual gaps: KL-Div's inability to perform cross-category comparison and its failure as a geometry-aware metric for continuous distributions. For logit distillation, it adapts CKA (Kornblith et al.) to quantify category interrelations that serve as transport costs in a discrete WD formulation, enabling the student to learn relational structure among classes. For feature distillation, it contrasts with prior WD-based methods (WCoRD, EMD+IPOT) that use cross-instance discrete WD, instead proposing instance-wise continuous WD on Gaussian distributions whose closed-form distance is a Riemannian metric on the space of Gaussians—exploiting geometric structure that Frobenius-norm methods (NST, ICKD-C) and KL-Div ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that WD consistently outperforms strong KL-Div variants across image classification (ImageNet, CIFAR-100), object detection (COCO), self-KD, and heterogeneous CNN↔Transformer settings, the paper establishes discrete and continuous WD as credible alternatives to the long-dominant KL-Div in KD. The cross-category comparison mechanism and the Riemannian geometry argument provide principled justifications beyond empirical gains. Limitations include the higher computational cost of WKD-L (Sinkhorn optimization) and the Gaussian assumption in WKD-F, leaving open whether non-parametric or non-Gaussian continuous WD formulations could further improve feature distillation at scale.
