## Problem

Existing text-to-4D generation pipelines rely on multi-view image diffusion models fine-tuned on synthetic object datasets, producing object-centric results that lack photorealism and fail to capture complex interactions between objects and environments. No prior method generates near-photorealistic, scene-level 4D content directly from text prompts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a deformable 3D Gaussian Splat (D-3DGS) scene representing a ~2-second dynamic sequence, viewable from arbitrary camera poses at any timestep. Internally, the pipeline generates a 16-frame reference video and an extended freeze-time video (up to 180° camera coverage via autoregressive generation) at 288×512 resolution using the Snap Video Model, then optimizes the D-3DGS representation over 20k iterations per stage (canonical reconstruction + temporal fitting), totaling ~1.5 hours on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

4Real replaces multi-view image priors with video diffusion priors trained on real-world footage. It first generates a reference video via text-to-video diffusion, then creates a freeze-time video (circular camera orbit, minimal object motion) using dataset context embedding and prompt engineering on a masked variant of the Snap Video Model. Canonical 3DGS are reconstructed from the freeze-time video while jointly learning a per-frame deformation field that absorbs multi-view geometric inconsistencies, regularized by a small-motion L1 loss and multi-view SDS. Temporal deformation is then fitted to the reference video using image alignment, as-rigid-as-possible regularization, and a joint temporal + multi-view SDS strategy that renders both fixed-viewpoint (varying time) and freeze-time (varying camera) videos for the low-resolution pixel-space diffusion model. The SDS step uses only the 36×64 first-stage model (~200 ms/step vs. ~2 s for SVD-based methods), making the full pipeline roughly 7× faster than competing approaches.

## Results

4Real outperforms all three baselines on X-CLIP score, achieving 24.23 vs. 4Dfy (20.03), 24.77 vs. Dream-in-4D (19.52), and 23.09 vs. AYG (19.87). On VideoScore metrics, 4Real leads in Visual Quality (2.41–2.44 vs. 1.34–2.49), Temporal Consistency (2.15–2.17 vs. 1.37–2.09), and Factual Consistency (2.46–2.50 vs. 1.20–2.47). A user study with 30 evaluators per pair confirms 4Real's preference across all seven criteria, with win rates ranging from 66.9% to 77.1%. Computationally, 4Real requires only 1.5 hours on a single A100 GPU, compared to over 10 hours for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 4Dfy (CVPR 2023), 4Real achieves a substantial margin on every metric: +4.2 X-CLIP, +1.0 Visual Quality, +0.68 Temporal Consistency, and +1.19 Factual Consistency, reflecting 4Dfy's object-centric NeRF-based approach that lacks scene-level photorealism. Against Dream-in-4D (CVPR 2024), the gap is even wider, with +5.25 X-CLIP and +1.26 Factual Consistency, as Dream-in-4D similarly relies on synthetic-object-trained multi-view priors. The closest comparison is with AYG (arXiv 2023), which also uses dynamic 3D Gaussians; here 4Real wins on X-CLIP (+3.22), Temporal Consistency (+0.07), T-V Alignment (+0.10), and Factual Consistency (+0.03), but slightly trails on Visual Quality (2.44 vs. 2.49). The user study confirms 4Real's dominance, with evaluators preferring it in 66.9–77.1% of pairwise comparisons across motion realism, foreground/background photorealism, 3D shape realism, general realism, motion significance, and text-video alignment. Additionally, 4Real completes generation in 1.5 hours on a single A100 GPU versus 10+ hours for 4Dfy and Dream-in-4D, a ~7× speedup attributed to using a low-resolution pixel-space video model for SDS rather than a latent-space image model.

## Relation to Prior Work

4Real builds on the deformable 3D Gaussian Splatting (D-3DGS) representation for dynamic scenes and the Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) optimization framework from DreamFusion, but makes a deliberate and significant departure from prior 4D generation pipelines (4Dfy, Dream-in-4D, AYG) by discarding multi-view image diffusion models fine-tuned on synthetic object datasets (e.g., Objaverse) as the 3D prior. Instead, it repurposes a pixel-space video diffusion model (Snap Video) trained on diverse real-world footage to serve as both the content generator (reference video) and the geometric prior (freeze-time video with circular camera motion). The per-frame deformation field that absorbs multi-view inconsistencies in the generated freeze-time video, jointly optimized with the canonical 3DGS, is a practical innovation not present in prior reconstruction-from-video work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first pipeline to produce photorealistic, scene-level (not just object-centric) 4D content from text, opening the door to applications in film, VR, and interactive media where realistic environments with dynamic objects are essential. The 7× speedup over competing methods (1.5 h vs. 10+ h on a single A100) makes iterative creative workflows more feasible. Key open questions remain: the method inherits video-model artifacts (blur, thin-object failures, text-video misalignment), cannot produce high-quality mesh geometry, and still requires over an hour per 2-second clip. Future work with stronger camera-controlled video models, cross-frame attention for freeze-time generation, and feedforward 3D reconstruction could address these gaps.
