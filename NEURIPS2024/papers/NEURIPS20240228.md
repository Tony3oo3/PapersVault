## Problem

Particle smoothers can leverage both past and future observations for more accurate offline state estimation, but classical implementations require human-engineered dynamics and likelihood models that do not scale to complex, learned representations. Recent advances in end-to-end differentiable particle filters have not been generalized to the smoothing setting, leaving a gap between the expressiveness of learned models and the temporal completeness of smoothing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240228/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes gravity-aligned first-person camera images (90° FoV), noisy odometry actions, and a 2D planimetric map at 0.5 m/pixel resolution (OpenStreetMap); for the synthetic bearings-only task it uses noisy bearing observations with 15% uniform outliers. The output is a continuous 3D state posterior over (x, y, θ) represented as a Gaussian/von Mises mixture density with M = 2N particles and learned, dimension-specific bandwidths, from which top modes can be extracted via non-maximal suppression for point estimates.

## Method

MDPS is a differentiable two-filter smoother that runs forward and backward Mixture Density Particle Filters (MDPFs) independently, then fuses them by drawing smoothed particles from a 50/50 mixture of the two filter posteriors and scoring them with a learned neural network that takes the current observation, both filter densities, and a BEV–map dot-product alignment as inputs. Differentiability is achieved through stratified resampling (reducing variance over multinomial) combined with the Importance Weighted Sample Gradient (IWSG) estimator, which propagates gradients through resampling by perturbing importance weights rather than particle locations. The measurement model encodes observations into a Birds-Eye-View feature map via a ResNet-101 U-Net with differentiable geometric projection and compares it against a VGG-19 U-Net map encoder via dot-product at each particle location. Training follows a three-stage procedure: (1) train forward and backward MDPFs separately with shared encoders, (2) freeze the filters and train the smoother's measurement network, then (3) jointly fine-tune all components, with separate learned bandwidths for resampling and posterior estimation at each stage.

## Results

On the Bearings-Only tracking task (T=50, 50 particles), MDPS substantially outperforms the best particle filter baseline (MDPF) by incorporating both past and future observations, while MDPF itself outperforms SR-PF and TG-PF across 11 training runs. On the Mapillary Geo-Localization (MGL) dataset (T=100, 250 particles), MDPS achieves the highest position and rotation recall among all compared methods, with the advantage over MDPF growing when evaluating the best of top-3 posterior modes. On KITTI, MDPS maintains a more diverse set of posterior modes than MDPF, yielding a clear performance gain for lateral errors exceeding 2 meters where MDPF prematurely collapses to incorrect modes. The paper presents results primarily as box plots (Fig. 2) and recall-vs-error curves (Figs. 3, 6) rather than a single numerical table.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDPS wins against all eight baselines on both the synthetic Bearings-Only task and the real-world MGL/KITTI localization tasks. Against TG-PF [15] and SR-PF [13], MDPS (and even MDPF) achieves substantially lower error, as both baselines suffer from biased or truncated gradients that prevent effective back-propagation through time; stratified resampling helps these baselines but does not close the gap. Against the classic FFBS smoother [24,25], MDPS wins decisively even when FFBS is given the true observation likelihood, because FFBS only reweights (not moves) particles and requires explicit dynamics evaluation. Against MDPF [14] (the closest competitor and direct predecessor), MDPS improves by fusing forward and backward filters to resolve multi-modal ambiguity, particularly visible in Fig. 4 where MDPS concentrates probability on the correct intersection while MDPF spreads mass across multiple candidates. Against the localization-specific baselines—Retrieval [3], Dense Search [11], and Retrieval (PF) [8]—MDPS achieves the highest recall on both MGL and KITTI, with the advantage over Dense Search being especially notable given that Dense Search requires near-exact relative poses from an external VIO system and has O(KW²H²) complexity versus MDPS's O(NWH).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mixture Density Particle Filter (MDPF, Younis & Sudderth 2023) from filtering to smoothing by embedding two MDPF instances (forward and backward in time) within Bresler's classical two-filter smoothing framework. The key departure from prior differentiable particle filter work (TG-PF, SR-PF, OT-PF) is that those methods only propagate information forward in time, whereas MDPS integrates both past and future observations via a differentiable importance-weighted combination of the two filter posteriors. The paper also introduces stratified resampling into the differentiable PF setting, showing it reduces gradient variance with negligible overhead compared to multinomial or residual resampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end differentiable particle smoother that scales to complex neural-network-parameterized dynamics and observation models, removing the need for human-engineered components that limited classical particle smoothers. The practical three-stage training procedure (train filters → train smoother measurement model → joint fine-tuning) addresses a non-trivial optimization challenge. Open questions include how the method scales to higher-dimensional state spaces (the paper acknowledges the curse of dimensionality), whether the O(TN²) training-time complexity can be reduced, and whether the approach generalizes to tasks with non-Markovian or partially observed dynamics beyond the 3D (x, y, θ) state used here.
