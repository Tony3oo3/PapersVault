## Problem

Differentiable fluid simulation with dynamic solid boundaries remains challenging because back-propagating gradients through the solid-fluid interface and the sequence of forward/backward simulation steps interleaved with control signals is computationally expensive and technically difficult. The machine learning community also lacks a unified Gym-like environment for exploring fluidic systems with strong solid-fluid coupling, making it hard to benchmark design, control, and learning tasks on high-fidelity dynamic fluid environments.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-dimensional parametric geometry (as few as 5 Bezier control parameters for 2D shapes, up to 7.1k for a 3D heart model built via CSG unions) and a two-layer MLP controller that observes the current velocity field on a MAC grid (resolutions from 40×40×40 to 128×128) and outputs per-frame motion offsets. It produces optimized geometry parameters, a trained closed-loop controller, and the resulting fluid velocity field. The geometry is consumed through its signed distance function to compute cut-cell volume fractions at grid faces, while the controller's outputs modulate the geometry's position or rotation, which in turn alters the flow field in a fully differentiable loop.

## Method

NeuralFluid combines three components: (1) a differentiable incompressible Navier-Stokes solver on a MAC grid using operator-splitting (semi-Lagrangian advection, explicit viscosity, and a cut-cell projection step that handles arbitrary solid-fluid interfaces via SDF-based volume fractions), (2) a low-dimensional parametric geometry representation using polar-coordinate Bezier curves for 2D surfaces and evenly-spaced cross-sections with CSG operations for 3D shapes, and (3) an end-to-end adjoint-based back-propagation that mirrors the forward simulation in reverse, solving a linear system for the adjoint variable at each projection step and extending gradients through the SDF to the geometry iso-surface. The projection step uses a custom CUDA-parallel Geometric-Multigrid-Preconditioned Conjugate-Gradient (MGPCG) Poisson solver, and the entire pipeline is exposed to PyTorch via pybind11. A two-layer MLP controller observes the velocity field and outputs control signals that drive the geometry, enabling simultaneous co-optimization of shape and control through gradient descent (Adam) on a combined loss.

## Results

Across six fluidic design and control tasks, NeuralFluid reduces the optimization loss by 2–3 orders of magnitude (e.g., Amplifier: 13.401 → 0.005; Neural Heart: 1.086 → 0.004). In direct solver benchmarks, NeuralFluid achieves a 30–53× speedup over PhiFlow in forward simulation and 13–16× in backward gradient propagation at 32³–64³ resolutions. Compared to DiffTaichi, NeuralFluid requires up to 12× less memory (405 MB vs. 5005 MB at 64³) while also being 2–4× faster in both forward and backward passes. In the Neural Heart closed-loop control task, the gradient-based method converges to near-zero loss within 60 epochs, while both PPO and CMA-ES fail to converge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PhiFlow [43], NeuralFluid achieves a consistent one-order-of-magnitude speedup: 53.4× faster forward and 16.3× faster backward at 32³, narrowing to 30× and 13.6× at 64³, attributed to the custom CUDA MGPCG Poisson solver and shared-memory block layout. Against DiffTaichi [42], NeuralFluid uses substantially less memory (292 MB vs. 685 MB at 32³; 405 MB vs. 5005 MB at 64³, a 12× reduction) because it avoids storing intermediate computational graphs per CG iteration, and is also 2–4× faster in both forward and backward passes. In the Neural Heart closed-loop control task, the gradient-based optimization converges to near-zero loss within 60 epochs, while PPO [15] and CMA-ES [16] both fail to converge, demonstrating that the clear analytical gradients from the differentiable simulator are critical in this high-sensitivity fluidic environment. The paper notes that gradient-free methods remain preferable when the objective is non-differentiable, but NeuralFluid's forward simulation speed still benefits any optimization strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable fluid simulation lineage (DiffTaichi, PhiFlow, FluidLab) by integrating a cut-cell solid-fluid interface handler, a low-dimensional parametric geometry representation (polar Bezier curves + CSG), and gradient back-propagation through the geometry iso-surface into a unified Navier-Stokes solver. Prior differentiable fluid work in computer graphics (Du et al. 2020, Li et al. 2022) was restricted to Stokes flow, while Takahashi et al. (2021) handled solid coupling but without parametric geometry co-design. The key departure is combining dynamic incompressible Navier-Stokes with simultaneous geometry and neural controller optimization, using a custom CUDA MGPCG solver for an order-of-magnitude speedup over existing differentiable PDE frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first practical differentiable environment for co-designing geometry and neural control in dynamic fluidic systems, demonstrated on tasks ranging from fluidic amplifiers to a four-muscle artificial heart. The 30–53× forward and 13–16× backward speedups over PhiFlow, plus 12× memory reduction over DiffTaichi, make long-horizon, high-resolution fluid optimization tractable on a single GPU. Key limitations include restriction to Newtonian flow, reliance on parametric (rather than topology-optimized) geometry, and absence of validation against physical experiments — all flagged as open directions. The Gym-like benchmark suite lowers the barrier for the ML community to explore fluid-solid interaction problems that were previously confined to specialized engineering toolchains.
