## Problem

Nesterov's accelerated gradient achieves O(1/T²) convergence in convex optimization, yet its momentum structure has never been successfully transplanted to game-theoretic learning, where regularized learning (FTRL) converges to strict Nash equilibria only at a geometric (linear) rate. The paper asks whether acceleration can be combined with the regularized learning paradigm to yield a superlinear convergence speed-up in finite N-person games, and whether this speed-up survives under increasingly information-starved feedback models.

## Input / Output

At each round, each player receives a payoff signal v̂_{i,n} that encodes information about their mixed payoff vector v_i(x_n); the signal's fidelity ranges from the full mixed payoff vector (full information), to the counterfactual pure-action payoffs against the realized opponent profile (realization-based), to a single scalar realized reward reconstructed via an importance-weighted estimator with explicit exploration (bandit). The algorithm outputs a sequence of mixed strategy profiles x_n = Q_i(y_{i,n}) on the probability simplex, where the internal state consists of score variables y_n and momentum variables p_n; convergence is measured in the sup-norm distance to a strict Nash equilibrium.

## Method

FTXL (Follow the Accelerated Leader) fuses the heavy-ball-with-vanishing-friction dynamics of Su, Boyd & Candès (the continuous-time analogue of NAG) with the score-variable dynamics of FTRL, yielding the second-order ODE ÿ = v(Q(y)) − (r/t)ẏ in continuous time and the discrete recursion y_{n+1} = y_n + γp_{n+1}, p_{n+1} = p_n + γv̂_n in discrete time. The key design choice is setting the friction parameter r = 0 (undamped regime), which the paper proves optimal for game-theoretic convergence—unlike NAG where friction is essential—because in games the payoff field is not a gradient of a convex potential. With logit best responses, the score-difference z_α(t) = y_α − y_{α*} accumulates quadratically in time, yielding a superlinear convergence bound ‖x(t) − x*‖∞ ≤ exp(C − ct²/2) in continuous time and exp(−Θ(T²)) in discrete time, an exponential speed-up over FTRL's exp(−Θ(T)). The analysis extends to stochastic settings via martingale concentration (Doob + BDG inequalities), showing the noise contribution remains subleading even under bandit feedback with an IWE exploration schedule ε_n ∝ 1/n^{ℓ_ε}.

## Results

The headline result is that FTXL converges to strict Nash equilibria at a superlinear rate of exp(−Θ(T²)) in discrete time, achieving an exponential speedup over standard FTRL/EW which converges at a geometric rate of exp(−Θ(T)). This superlinear rate is maintained across all three feedback models: full information (no subleading term), realization-based feedback (subleading term (3/5)cγ^(5/3)T^(5/3)), and bandit/payoff-based feedback (subleading term (5/9)cγ^(9/5)T^(9/5)). In continuous time, the corresponding rate is exp(−ct²/2(r+1)), with the optimal friction parameter being r=0 (undamped regime). Numerical experiments in a 2-player zero-sum game and a 100-player congestion game confirm the faster convergence of FTXL over EW under both realization-based and bandit feedback.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard Exponential Weights (EW) algorithm, which is the prototypical FTRL method with entropic (negentropy) regularization. FTXL decisively outperforms EW on the convergence-to-equilibrium metric: FTXL achieves a superlinear rate of exp(−Θ(T²)) while EW achieves only a linear/geometric rate of exp(−Θ(T)), representing an exponential speedup. This advantage is preserved under all information regimes tested — full information, realization-based feedback, and bandit (payoff-based) feedback — though the subleading error term grows as information becomes scarcer. In the numerical experiments (Figure 1), FTXL consistently reaches the strict Nash equilibrium faster than EW in both the zero-sum game and the congestion game, under both realization-based and bandit feedback. Both methods perform worse under bandit feedback than under realization-based feedback, as expected from the reduced information, but FTXL maintains its relative advantage in all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper fuses two well-developed but previously separate threads: Nesterov's accelerated gradient (and its continuous-time heavy-ball-with-vanishing-friction formulation by Su, Boyd, and Candès) and the follow-the-regularized-leader (FTRL) / exponential-weights paradigm for learning in games (Mertikopoulos & Sandholm, Taylor & Jonker replicator dynamics). The closest prior work is the undamped second-order replicator equation of Laraki & Mertikopoulos, which corresponds to FTXL with entropic regularization and r=0; other second-order game dynamics (Flam & Morgan, Gao & Pavel) either lacked favorable convergence guarantees or addressed different stability notions. The key departure is a systematic treatment of the discrete-time algorithm across three information models (full, realization-based, bandit) with explicit superlinear rate bounds, rather than a single continuous-time analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that momentum in time (not Hessian information in space) yields an exponential speedup over standard regularized learning — and that this speedup survives even under bandit, payoff-based feedback — challenges the conventional association of superlinear rates with second-order spatial methods. Limitations are significant: convergence is only local to strict Nash equilibria in finite games, the neighborhood of attraction shrinks with the minimum payoff gap, and the subleading term degrades substantially as information becomes scarcer (T^{5/3} for realization-based, T^{9/5} for bandit). Open questions include whether analogous acceleration is possible for non-strict or mixed equilibria, in non-concave games, or with global (rather than local) guarantees, and whether the undamped (r=0) regime is truly optimal in all settings or merely in the min-max sense analyzed here.
