## Problem

While parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods for diffusion models exist, the intrinsic transfer properties of diffusion models along the reverse denoising process have remained largely unexplored. It is unclear how transferability varies across denoising stages, and whether the uniform treatment of all timesteps in standard fine-tuning is suboptimal. This paper investigates whether different stages of the denoising chain have fundamentally different transfer characteristics and whether this can be exploited to improve fine-tuning efficacy.

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation, the method takes a class label as input and produces 256×256 RGB images, using DiT-XL-2-256x256 pre-trained on ImageNet as the base model. For controllable generation, it takes a control condition (sketch, edge, depth, normal map, or segmentation mask) combined with a text prompt and produces 512×512 images via Stable Diffusion v1.5 with ControlNet adapters. Additionally, the method requires a pre-sampled augmented dataset (e.g., 200K images for class-conditional, 30K for controllable) generated by the pre-trained model before fine-tuning begins, which serves as the knowledge retention signal.

## Method

Diff-Tuning is built on the 'chain of forgetting' observation: transferability decreases monotonically along the reverse denoising process, with low-t (clean-data) denoising being highly transferable while high-t (noisy-data) denoising is domain-specific. Theorem 1 formalizes this by showing the ideal denoiser converges to the nearest training sample as t→0 but to the data distribution mean as t→T, explaining why pre-trained models retain zero-shot denoising skills for lightly corrupted data but fail on heavily noised domain-specific content. The method combines two complementary objectives: a knowledge retention loss L_retention weighted by ξ(t)=1−t^τ (decreasing with t) applied to pre-sampled data from the pre-trained model, and a knowledge reconsolidation loss L_adaptation weighted by ψ(t)=t^τ (increasing with t) applied to downstream data. In practice, each training step uniformly samples a timestep t, then stochastically selects data from either the augmented or downstream dataset with probability proportional to ξ(t) or ψ(t), keeping the overall loss equivalent to standard DDPM weighting. The approach is architecture-agnostic, integrates seamlessly with PEFT methods like DiffFit, and for conditional generation applies retention only on the unconditional branch while applying reconsolidation on both branches.

## Results

On eight class-conditional generation benchmarks (256×256, DiT-XL-2 backbone), Diff-Tuning achieves an average FID of 11.08, a 24.6% relative improvement over standard full fine-tuning (14.06) and a 9.4% improvement over the strongest PEFT baseline DiffFit (12.21). For controllable generation with ControlNet (512×512, Stable Diffusion v1.5), Diff-Tuning reduces the average sudden convergence steps from 9.0k to 6.6k (24.0% faster), with the largest gain on Segmentation (ADE20k) at 40.3% fewer steps. The method also extends to EDM (ImageNet 64×64 → CIFAR-10 64×64), reducing FID from 14.75 to 6.04.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-Tuning outperforms all seven baselines on the class-conditional generation benchmark. Against full fine-tuning (the strongest non-PEFT baseline), it achieves a 24.6% average FID reduction (11.08 vs. 14.06), with the largest gains on Food-101 (44.6%) and SUN397 (36.2%). Among PEFT methods, DiffFit is the closest competitor (12.21 avg FID), but Diff-Tuning still leads by 9.4% on average and wins on every individual dataset. The other PEFT baselines—AdaptFormer (18.70), BitFit (16.82), VPT-Deep (26.80), and LoRA (81.25)—all underperform even standard full fine-tuning, highlighting that naive parameter-efficient strategies are insufficient for diffusion transfer. For controllable generation, ControlNet+Diff-Tuning reaches sudden convergence 24.0% faster on average (6.6k vs. 9.0k steps), with the most dramatic improvement on Segmentation (ADE20k) at 40.3% fewer steps. In Appendix D, Diff-Tuning also surpasses timestep-reweighting strategies (P2, MIN-SNR-1/5) on CUB-Bird and Stanford Cars, confirming that its gains come from the chain-of-forgetting principle rather than from reweighting individual timesteps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical transferability analysis of deep neural networks (lower-level features transfer better than higher-level ones) to the multi-step denoising process of diffusion models, revealing a non-uniform 'chain of forgetting' along the reverse process. It departs from prior PEFT work (LoRA, DiffFit, BitFit) by addressing the intrinsic transfer properties of diffusion models rather than parameter efficiency, and from standard catastrophic forgetting mitigation (EWC, data replay) by making retention timestep-aware rather than uniform. The method is orthogonal to and composable with existing PEFT approaches, positioning it as a general transfer strategy rather than a competing parameter-efficient technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The chain of forgetting provides a principled, theoretically grounded explanation for why naive fine-tuning of diffusion models overfits at the detail level while losing domain-specific structure, and the resulting reweighting strategy is simple enough to plug into any existing diffusion fine-tuning pipeline. The 24.6% FID improvement across eight datasets and 24% faster ControlNet convergence demonstrate broad practical utility. Open questions include whether the chain of forgetting generalizes to video, 3D, or audio diffusion models, whether more sophisticated (non-power-law) weighting functions yield further gains, and whether the pre-sampled augmented dataset can be replaced by on-the-fly distillation without the memory overhead.
