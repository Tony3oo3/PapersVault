## Problem

Feed-forward monocular depth estimation methods achieve robust zero-shot generalization but produce over-smoothed depth maps lacking fine-grained details, while diffusion-based MDE methods capture rich details but struggle with global geometric accuracy in complex scenes due to limited training data diversity. No existing approach simultaneously delivers both accurate global depth layout and high-frequency detail reconstruction in a zero-shot setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243450/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and first passes it through a frozen pre-trained feed-forward MDE model (e.g., Depth Anything) to obtain a coarse affine-invariant depth map, which serves as the primary conditioning signal for the diffusion refiner. The output is an affine-invariant depth map (up to unknown global scale and shift) at the same spatial resolution as the input, produced by a latent diffusion U-Net operating in a VAE-compressed space with 50 DDIM denoising steps and 10 test-time ensemble members by default.

## Method

BetterDepth is a conditional latent diffusion refiner built on the Stable Diffusion architecture (frozen VAE encoder/decoder + fine-tuned U-Net) that takes the pre-trained MDE model's coarse depth prediction as depth conditioning alongside the input image, iteratively refining details over T denoising steps. Two key training strategies ensure the refiner remains faithful to the conditioning while learning detail extraction: (1) global pre-alignment, which applies a least-squares scale-and-shift fit between the coarse depth and ground truth to eliminate affine ambiguity and strengthen the conditioning signal at global scale; and (2) local patch masking, which splits depth maps into non-overlapping patches, computes per-patch Euclidean distance, and zeros out the diffusion loss for patches exceeding a tolerance threshold η, preventing the model from overfitting to dataset-specific errors while preserving detail learning in well-aligned regions. The entire refiner is trained on as few as 400 synthetic RGB-D pairs (Hypersim + Virtual KITTI) for 5K iterations, and once trained it can be plugged into any feed-forward MDE model without retraining, inheriting that model's geometric prior while adding fine-grained detail.

## Results

BetterDepth (74K training) achieves the best average rank of 1.8 across five zero-shot MDE benchmarks, with AbsRel of 4.2/7.5/4.7/4.3/22.6 and δ1 of 98.0/95.2/98.1/98.1/75.5 on NYUv2/KITTI/ETH3D/ScanNet/DIODE. Even with only 400 training pairs, BetterDepth-400 attains an average rank of 4.0, outperforming all prior diffusion-based methods (Marigold: 5.6, GeoWizard: 5.2) and approaching Depth Anything (2.9). On the high-resolution Middlebury 2014 dataset, BetterDepth achieves the best edge-based metrics (DBE_comp 3.61, DBE_acc 2.09, EP 28.49%, ER 50.35%), substantially outperforming both Depth Anything and Marigold on detail reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BetterDepth outperforms all feed-forward baselines (DiverseDepth, MiDaS, LeReS, Omnidata, HDN, DPT) by a wide margin on every dataset, with the closest competitor being Depth Anything (63.5M training samples), which BetterDepth surpasses on KITTI (AbsRel 7.5 vs. 8.0, δ1 95.2 vs. 94.6) and ETH3D (AbsRel 4.7 vs. 6.2) while matching or slightly leading on NYUv2 and ScanNet. Against diffusion-based methods, BetterDepth dominates Marigold (AbsRel 4.2 vs. 5.5 on NYUv2; 7.5 vs. 9.9 on KITTI) and GeoWizard (4.7 vs. 6.4 on ETH3D) across all shared metrics. The only metric where BetterDepth does not rank first is DIODE δ1, where DepthFM (80.0%) and GeoWizard (79.2%) exceed BetterDepth's 75.5%, though BetterDepth still leads on DIODE AbsRel (22.6 vs. 22.5 for DepthFM). Against the concurrent Depth Anything V2, BetterDepth achieves better AbsRel on NYUv2 (4.2 vs. 4.4), KITTI (7.5 vs. 8.3), and ETH3D (4.7 vs. 6.2) with only 74K training samples versus 595K. The plug-and-play experiments further show BetterDepth improving MiDaS by up to 3.2% AbsRel (KITTI) and DPT by up to 1.2% AbsRel without any re-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

BetterDepth sits at the intersection of two established MDE paradigms: feed-forward zero-shot estimators (MiDaS, DPT, Depth Anything) that capture robust global geometry but over-smooth details, and diffusion-based estimators (Marigold, DepthFM, GeoWizard) that recover fine-grained structure but lack diverse geometric priors for zero-shot transfer. The key departure from prior diffusion-based MDE is treating the diffusion model not as a standalone estimator trained from scratch on synthetic data, but as a conditional refiner that takes a pre-trained MDE model's output as depth conditioning. The training strategies—global pre-alignment to enforce scale/shift consistency and local patch masking to filter out regions where the conditioning diverges from ground truth—are the paper's core methodological contribution, enabling the refiner to learn detail extraction while preserving the geometric prior of the conditioning model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play property is practically significant: a single trained refiner can improve multiple MDE backbones (MiDaS, DPT, Depth Anything) without retraining, making it a drop-in module for the growing ecosystem of foundational depth models. Training on as few as 400 synthetic pairs to achieve competitive SOTA performance lowers the barrier for practitioners who lack large-scale synthetic rendering pipelines. Open questions remain around metric depth estimation (scale/shift ambiguity), inference speed (still bounded by the diffusion denoising loop), and whether better alignment strategies (e.g., outlier-aware) could further improve patch preservation during training. The paper also acknowledges that model size is determined by both the MDE backbone and the diffusion refiner, limiting deployment on resource-constrained hardware.
