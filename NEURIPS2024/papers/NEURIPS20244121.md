## Problem

Training neural networks with factorized tensor layers (e.g., Tucker-decomposed convolutional kernels) suffers from two critical issues: the factorization rank must be fixed a priori, and the optimization is highly sensitive to initialization due to the high curvature of the low-rank manifold near small singular values. While geometry-aware training via projected gradient flows has been shown to resolve these issues for matrix-valued layers, extending this to higher-order tensors is non-trivial because not all tensor factorizations admit the required Riemannian structure, and naive extensions incur prohibitive computational costs scaling with tensor order.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard neural network inputs: RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet, 28×28 MNIST) for classification, text sequences for NLU (GLUE benchmark), and text prompts for image generation (Stable Diffusion Dreambooth). The output is a compressed network whose convolutional kernels are stored in Tucker format with dynamically determined per-mode ranks, and whose attention layers use matrix adapters. The compression rate is governed by a single hyperparameter τ (singular value threshold), which controls how aggressively small Tucker-mode singular values are truncated at each training step, yielding compression rates exceeding 95% for VGG16 on CIFAR-10 while maintaining or exceeding full-model accuracy.

## Method

TDLRT (Tensor Dynamical Low-Rank Training) formulates training as a projected gradient flow on the Tucker manifold, then introduces a key reparameterization K_i = U_i S_i (where S_i comes from the QR decomposition of Mat_i(C)^T) that eliminates the stiff pseudoinverse term responsible for slow convergence. A critical corollary shows that the basis augmentation direction can be obtained from a single backpropagation pass rather than d+1 separate gradient evaluations, reducing the algorithm to only two forward/backward sweeps per step. At each iteration, the algorithm augments each basis U_i with the gradient direction via QR, projects the core onto the new basis, performs a descent step on the core, and applies an HOSVD-based rank truncation at tolerance τ to dynamically adjust the Tucker rank. The paper proves three theoretical guarantees: loss descent per step, convergence to stationary points in expectation under SGD with decaying learning rate, and an approximation bound to the full model that is independent of higher-order singular values, confirming robustness to ill-conditioning.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, TDLRT achieves 90.23% test accuracy at 94.40% compression rate on VGG16 (vs. 92.01% full baseline), 82.39% at 83.12% compression on AlexNet (vs. 85.46% baseline), and 92.72% at 78.73% compression on ResNet18 (vs. 94.33% baseline). On Tiny-ImageNet with ResNet18, TDLRT reaches 50.90% accuracy at 83.95% compression (vs. 51.1% baseline). For fine-tuning, TDLRT with 0.9M parameters outperforms LoRA (1.33M params) on all GLUE tasks, and with 1.8M parameters achieves a Dreambooth loss of 0.2635, beating LoRA at the same parameter budget (0.274).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDLRT outperforms all factorization and pruning baselines on VGG16 and AlexNet across CIFAR-10. On VGG16, TDLRT (90.23% at 94.40% compression) beats the closest factorization competitor, Tucker-factorized [38] (86.71% at 91.4%), by 3.5% accuracy at a higher compression rate, and surpasses Matrix DLRT [68] (89.13% at 83.22%) by 1.1% accuracy at 11% more compression. On AlexNet, TDLRT (82.39%) leads TT-factorized [62] (78.13%) by 4.3% and Matrix DLRT [68] (73.57%) by 8.8%. On ResNet18, TDLRT (92.72% at 78.73%) edges out Tucker RGD [74] (92.76% at 74.18%) in compression-to-accuracy tradeoff and clearly beats TT-factorized [62] (87.13%) by 5.6%. For fine-tuning, TDLRT with 0.9M parameters surpasses LoRA (1.33M params) on all five GLUE tasks (e.g., SST2: 0.9594 vs. 0.9484), and at 1.8M parameters achieves a lower Dreambooth loss (0.2635) than LoRA at the same budget (0.274).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the matrix dynamical low-rank training (DLRT) framework of Schotthofer et al. [68] from matrix-valued layers to higher-order tensor layers in Tucker format, building on the dynamical tensor approximation theory of Koch & Lubich [40] and the time-integration analysis of Lubich et al. [54]. The key departure is a QR-based reparameterization (Theorem 2.1) that eliminates the stiffness term inherent in the projected gradient flow, combined with a corollary (Corollary 2.2) that reduces the required gradient evaluations from d+1 to just 2, making the tensor case computationally feasible where a naive extension would be prohibitive. Unlike standard factorization training (block-coordinate descent on CP, Tucker, or Tensor-Train factors), TDLRT explicitly exploits the Riemannian geometry of the Tucker manifold to avoid high-curvature regions, and unlike post-hoc compression or warm-up-then-compress pipelines, it trains the compressed representation from initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method reduces model compression to a single interpretable hyperparameter τ (singular value threshold) while providing rigorous guarantees of loss descent, convergence to stationary points, and approximation of the full model—properties absent from standard factorization training. The demonstrated 95%+ compression on VGG16/CIFAR10 with matching or exceeding baseline accuracy, combined with robustness to initialization, makes this a practical tool for resource-constrained training. Open questions include: strategies for per-layer τ selection, efficient GPU implementation of the tensor rounding step, and theoretical evidence that well-performing low-rank Tucker sub-networks exist in the reference architecture (currently supported only empirically). The method's agnosticism between compression and LoRA-style fine-tuning also opens a path toward parameter-efficient adaptation of large pre-trained models.
