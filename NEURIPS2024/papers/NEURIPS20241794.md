## Problem

Statistical precipitation downscaling from coarse-grid climate models to fine-grid resolution is critical for flood risk and water availability planning, but existing deterministic regression methods suffer from mode averaging that underestimates extreme precipitation, while GAN-based approaches tend to collapse onto specific modes and fail to capture the full conditional distribution. The paper addresses the need for a generative downscaling method that faithfully models the multimodal conditional distribution of high-resolution precipitation given low-resolution inputs, preserving both ensemble statistics and rare extreme events.

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of 5 consecutive low-resolution (200 km) frames, each with 12 channels (precipitation plus 11 atmospheric variables such as topography, 10 m and 700 mb wind components, surface temperature, and specific humidity) on a cubed-sphere grid of 48×48 points per tile, and produces a sequence of 5 high-resolution (25 km) precipitation frames at 384×384 per tile — an 8× spatial upscaling. Because the output is stochastic, multiple samples can be drawn for a given input to estimate the conditional distribution, which is essential for computing CRPS and characterizing uncertainty in extreme events.

## Method

STVD decomposes the downscaling task into a deterministic UNet downscaler that produces an initial high-resolution prediction via spatiotemporal factorized attention, followed by a conditional DDPM that generates additive residual frames to capture fine-grained stochastic details. Both modules share a UNet backbone with factorized attention that decouples spatial and temporal dimensions: quadratic attention is used at the bottleneck, while linear attention operates on localized spatial patches and per-channel temporal sequences in the remaining layers, dramatically reducing computational cost. The diffusion model is conditioned on the noisy residual, the bicubically upsampled low-resolution input, the downscaler output, and feature maps from the downscaler's contractive layers, all concatenated along the channel dimension. Training uses a single v-parametrization (angular) diffusion loss optimized end-to-end over both modules with Adam, and inference employs DDIM sampling with 30 steps to generate diverse, realistic residual sequences.

## Results

On the FV3GFS precipitation downscaling benchmark (8× super-resolution, 25 km → 200 km), STVD achieves the best performance across all five evaluation metrics: CRPS of 1.85×10⁻⁵, MSE of 0.59×10⁻⁸, Earth-Mover Distance of 2.49×10⁻⁶, 99.999th percentile error (PE) of 1.2×10⁻³, and spatial autocorrelation error (SAE) of 4.00×10⁻⁶. The closest competitor, PSRT, trails by 16% on CRPS and 12% on MSE, while the deterministic Swin-IR baseline is worst on extreme-event metrics (PE = 23.40×10⁻³, SAE = 18.9×10⁻⁶). Ablations confirm that temporal context (5 frames vs. 1) and additional atmospheric input channels both contribute meaningfully to performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STVD outperforms all six baselines on every metric. Against the strongest video super-resolution baseline PSRT [60], STVD improves CRPS by 14% (1.85 vs. 2.15) and PE by 68% (1.2 vs. 3.8), indicating substantially better capture of extreme precipitation events. The deterministic transformer baselines VRT [34] and RVRT [36] are roughly 2× worse on CRPS and 3× worse on PE, reflecting mode-averaging artifacts from supervised regression. The video diffusion baseline VDM [21] is competitive on MSE (0.73 vs. 0.59) but performs poorly on distributional metrics (EMD 12.70 vs. 2.49; PE 6.4 vs. 1.2), suggesting it fails to preserve the global precipitation distribution. The image-only Swin-IR [35] is worst overall, particularly on extreme events (PE = 23.40) and spatial structure (SAE = 18.9), while its generative variant Swin-IR-Diff [41] improves distributional metrics but still lags STVD on all five scores. The ablation STVD-single (removing 11 auxiliary atmospheric channels) degrades PE from 1.2 to 2.3 and EMD from 2.49 to 4.64, confirming the value of multi-channel conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DDPM/SR3 lineage of conditional diffusion models for super-resolution into the spatiotemporal domain, combining a deterministic UNet downscaler with a residual diffusion module that jointly models a sequence of high-resolution precipitation frames. It departs from prior precipitation downscaling work (GAN-based and deterministic regression methods) by framing the task as conditional generative modeling over video sequences, and from prior video super-resolution work (VRT, RVRT, PSRT) by being explicitly generative rather than deterministic, thereby avoiding mode averaging. The spatiotemporal factorized attention mechanism, which decouples spatial and temporal attention with linear variants for efficiency, is a key architectural contribution that enables processing of long sequences at high resolution without prohibitive compute.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reliable high-resolution precipitation forecasts are critical for flood risk assessment and climate adaptation, but fluid-dynamical models at km-scale are computationally prohibitive for routine use. By capturing the full conditional distribution rather than a single point estimate, STVD provides meaningful uncertainty quantification and avoids the underestimation of extreme precipitation events that plagues deterministic and GAN-based downscalers. Key open questions include whether the method can be trained without paired high-resolution targets (currently required), how it behaves under distribution shift to new regions or climate scenarios, and whether autoregressive rollout over longer sequences remains stable. The realism-distortion tradeoff (MSE vs. extreme-event fidelity) is well-characterized but leaves practitioners to choose their operating point per application.
