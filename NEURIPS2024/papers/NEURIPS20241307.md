## Problem

Previous evaluations of symmetric moral consistency in LLMs (e.g., Jang et al.) rely on a single prompt-swap setting that conflates genuine consistency with position bias and selection bias, making it impossible to determine whether a model's apparent consistency reflects stable moral reasoning or merely a systematic preference for a particular option position or ID. This paper asks: how much do these two biases distort measured consistency, and what is the true mitigated consistency of LLMs across moral scenarios of varying ambiguity?

## Input / Output

The framework consumes moral scenario prompts from the MoralChoice dataset (680 high-ambiguity and 687 low-ambiguity questions, each presenting two action options) and evaluates 12 LLMs under three swap conditions: Context Swap (CS), Option Swap (OS), and Full Swap (FS). For open-source models, consistency is measured via token-level likelihood of option ID tokens at temperature 0; for proprietary models (GPT-3.5-turbo, Gemini-1.0-pro), it is measured via API responses. Outputs include per-setting consistency scores, KL-divergence-based position bias (D_pos) and selection bias (D_selec) values, a single mitigated consistency score (C_mitig), and confidence score distributions for the chosen option.

## Method

The tSMC framework first measures raw symmetric consistency τ under three swap settings, then quantifies position bias as the KL divergence between the OS reference distribution and the CS/FS distributions, and selection bias as the KL divergence between the FS reference distribution and the CS/OS distributions. The key insight is that position bias inflates OS scores while deflating CS and FS, whereas selection bias inflates FS while deflating CS and OS, so the two biases produce characteristic, distinguishable distortion patterns across the three settings. A mitigated consistency score C_mitig is then computed by averaging the per-setting consistency scores weighted by (1 − α·D_total), where D_total is the signed sum of position and selection bias contributions specific to each setting and α=0.1. This yields a single bias-corrected consistency estimate that is invariant to the model's positional or ID-based preferences, enabling fair comparison across models and moral ambiguity levels.

## Results

Across 12 LLMs evaluated on the MoralChoice dataset, the average mitigated symmetric moral consistency ranges from 60.7% in high-ambiguity moral scenarios to 84.8% in low-ambiguity moral scenarios. Position and selection biases cause substantial measurement fluctuations: Llama-2-13b exhibits an ~80% performance gap between Option Swap and Full Swap settings in high-ambiguity scenarios, while Mixtral-8x7B and Gemini-1.0-pro remain nearly stable (±1–3%) across all settings. Notably, model size does not predict consistency—Llama-2-7b outperforms Llama-2-70b in both ambiguity levels, and Llama-3-8b (trained on 7× more data than Llama-2-7b) shows more pronounced fluctuation rather than improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new model to benchmark against competing methods; instead, it evaluates 12 LLMs under three swap settings (CS, OS, FS) and compares the standard Context Swap metric (adopted from Jang et al., BECEL/EMNLP 2023) against the proposed tSMC mitigated score. The key finding is that the standard CS evaluation is unreliable: for example, Llama-2-13b scores 18.7% under CS but 90.0% under FS in high-ambiguity scenarios, a 71.3-point swing driven entirely by selection bias toward option B. After applying the tSMC KL-divergence-based mitigation, the corrected scores compress these extreme fluctuations and reveal that most models cluster between 60–85% in high-ambiguity settings. Among the 12 models, Mixtral-8x7B (87.6% CS high-ambiguity) and Gemini-1.0-pro (85.7% CS) show the highest and most stable consistency, while Gemma-2b (31.9% CS) and Llama-2-13b/70b (~19–21% CS) are the most bias-sensitive. The tSMC framework demonstrates that the apparent performance differences between models under a single evaluation setting can be an artifact of bias rather than genuine capability differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the symmetric consistency evaluation framework from Jang et al. (BECEL, COLING 2022; EMNLP 2023), which assessed symmetric consistency primarily in NLI tasks, to moral reasoning scenarios using the MoralChoice dataset. While prior work on LLM bias identified position bias (Li et al., 2023) and selection bias (Zheng et al., 2024) in evaluation contexts, this is the first work to demonstrate that both biases jointly confound symmetric moral consistency measurements. The tSMC framework introduces Option Swap and Full Swap settings beyond the Context Swap used in prior symmetric consistency work, and applies KL divergence to quantify and mitigate bias effects on consistency scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard symmetric consistency measurements are substantially distorted by position and selection biases (up to 80% gaps between settings for some models) calls into question previously reported consistency scores in the LLM evaluation literature. The observation that parameter scale does not reliably improve moral consistency (Llama-2-7b outperforming Llama-2-70b) challenges the assumption that larger models are more reliable for ethical decision-making. Open questions include whether the tSMC framework generalizes beyond MCQA formats to open-ended moral reasoning, whether other biases (e.g., length bias) further confound consistency measurements, and whether the identified biases can be mitigated through training-time interventions rather than post-hoc correction.
