## Problem

Existing Large Reconstruction Models (LRMs) rely on dense representations (triplanes or pixel-aligned Gaussians) that waste computation on empty space, ignoring that only ~5% of the spatial volume in typical 3D objects is actually occupied. They also perform dense cross-attention between 3D tokens and all image features, failing to exploit the explicit geometric projection between 3D points and 2D images. This combination limits these methods to low-resolution outputs and prevents them from benefiting from denser multi-view inputs.

## Input / Output

The method accepts N posed RGB images (up to 21 views) along with their camera intrinsics and extrinsics, processed at 448×448 during training and 512×512 at inference. It produces a set of 512k 3D Gaussians, each parameterized by a local offset from its anchor voxel, 3-channel RGB, 3-channel scale, 4-channel rotation quaternion, and 1-channel opacity. A key design constraint is that only occupied voxels (from a 128³ grid) are processed as 3D tokens, keeping the token count manageable (4k during training, 16k at inference) while preserving geometric sparsity.

## Method

GeoLRM uses a two-stage pipeline: a 6-layer proposal transformer predicts a sparse occupancy grid from hierarchical image features (DINOv2 high-level semantics plus Plücker-ray-embedded RGB low-level features), and a 16-layer reconstruction transformer refines only the occupied voxels into 3D Gaussian parameters. The reconstruction transformer replaces dense cross-attention with deformable cross-attention that projects each 3D anchor point onto each image plane and samples only K=8 nearby feature locations with learned offsets, enabling per-view importance weighting and geometric error correction. Positional encoding uses 3D RoPE (splitting features into x/y/z components) to handle the sparse 3D token sequences. Training is supervised in two stages: first with binary cross-entropy and scene-class affinity loss on occupancy ground truth, then with combined L2+LPIPS image loss, alpha-mask loss, and gradient-weighted logarithmic depth loss on rendered views.

## Results

On Google Scanned Objects (GSO) with 6 input views, GeoLRM achieves 23.57 PSNR, 0.872 SSIM, 0.167 LPIPS, 0.167 Chamfer Distance, and 0.892 F-Score, outperforming all baselines on 4 of 5 quality metrics while using only 4.92 GB GPU memory (vs. 23.12 GB for InstantMesh). On OmniObject3D, GeoLRM leads on all five metrics with 24.74 PSNR, 0.883 SSIM, 0.134 LPIPS, 0.156 CD, and 0.863 FS. A key scalability finding is that GeoLRM's performance improves monotonically with more input views (22.84 → 25.13 PSNR from 4 to 20 views), whereas InstantMesh degrades beyond 8 views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoLRM outperforms LGM by a large margin on all metrics (e.g., +2.81 PSNR, +0.040 SSIM on GSO), confirming that the 3D-aware transformer with deformable cross-attention substantially improves over the UNet-based pixel-aligned Gaussian approach. Against CRM, GeoLRM gains +0.79 PSNR and +0.019 SSIM on GSO, with a notable 0.046 improvement in Chamfer Distance, reflecting the benefit of explicit 3D-to-2D geometric projection. The closest competitor is InstantMesh, which uses a triplane-based LRM; GeoLRM edges it out by +0.38 PSNR and +0.016 SSIM on GSO, and by a wider +0.88 PSNR on OmniObject3D. InstantMesh achieves a marginally lower LPIPS on GSO (0.166 vs. 0.167), likely due to its mesh-based smoothing, but GeoLRM dominates on geometric accuracy (CD 0.167 vs. 0.186) and uses roughly 4.7× less GPU memory (4.92 vs. 23.12 GB). TripoSR is included only in qualitative comparisons (Figure 3) since it is designed for single-image input, making a fair quantitative comparison with multi-view methods infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoLRM extends the Large Reconstruction Model (LRM) line of work (LRM, InstantMesh, LGM, GRM, CRM) by replacing triplane or pixel-aligned Gaussian representations with explicit 3D anchor points processed through a geometry-aware transformer. The key architectural departure is the use of deformable cross-attention to project 3D tokens onto 2D image feature maps, explicitly exploiting the 3D-to-2D projection relationship that prior LRM methods ignore in favor of dense attention. The two-stage pipeline (occupancy proposal → reconstruction) draws on occupancy prediction literature from autonomous driving, while the hierarchical image encoder combines DINOv2 semantic features with Plücker-ray-embedded low-level features, a design informed by recent single-image 3D probing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that prior LRM methods (notably InstantMesh) actually degrade in quality when given more input views, while GeoLRM consistently improves up to 21+ views, is practically significant for 3D generation pipelines that can leverage multi-view synthesis models like SV3D. This opens a path toward integrating video generation models into 3D AIGC workflows, which the authors identify as a future direction. The main limitation is the non-end-to-end two-stage design, where errors from the occupancy proposal propagate into the reconstruction stage, and the reliance on a separate proposal network constrains real-time applicability. Whether the deformable-attention-based 3D lifting generalizes beyond object-centric scenes to unbounded or dynamic environments remains an open question.
