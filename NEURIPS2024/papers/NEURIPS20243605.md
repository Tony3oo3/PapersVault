## Problem

Two dominant parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategies—Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) and Orthogonal Fine-Tuning (OFT)—operate on fundamentally different principles: LoRA adds a trainable low-rank matrix to frozen weights, while OFT multiplies weights by a structured orthogonal matrix. This conceptual divide prevents a unified adapter framework that simultaneously enjoys LoRA's low-rank expressiveness and OFT's theoretical guarantee of bounded discrepancy from the pre-trained model. The paper asks whether a single adapter can be interpreted as both an orthogonal transformation and an adaptive low-rank modification.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a frozen pre-trained weight matrix W ∈ R^{d_out × d} and a standard input vector x ∈ R^d, producing z = W H^(r) x where H^(r) is a product of r learnable Householder reflections. The only trainable parameters are r unit vectors {u_i ∈ S^{d-1}} per layer (rd parameters total), with no additional inference-time overhead beyond r vector inner products. In the image generation setting, the input additionally includes a text prompt plus a control signal (Canny edges, facial landmarks, or segmentation maps) or reference subject images, and the output is a generated RGB image evaluated on subject fidelity, prompt alignment, and controllability metrics.

## Method

HRA constructs an orthogonal adapter as a chain of r learnable Householder reflections H^(r) = ∏_{i=1}^r (I − 2u_i u_i^T), which is algebraically equivalent to I + U_r Γ_r U_r^T where Γ_r is a recursively defined upper-triangular matrix. This yields the key identity WH^(r) = W + A(W,B)B with B = U_r and A = W U_r Γ_r, revealing HRA as an adaptive LoRA whose low-rank factor A is a deterministic function of the frozen W and the trainable B, thereby inheriting OFT's guarantee that adapted weights remain in the column space of W. The forward pass exploits the Householder structure to achieve O(d(r + d_out)) complexity—avoiding the O(db²) Cayley-transform cost of OFT—by performing r sequential vector reflections followed by a single matrix-vector product. An orthogonality regularizer λ‖I_r − U_r^T U_r‖_F² is added to the loss, trading off model capacity (λ=0, column-normalized U_r) against regularity (λ→∞, strictly orthogonal U_r via Gram-Schmidt), with the paper showing that moderate λ values (10^{-5} to 10^{-3}) yield the best empirical performance across NLU, mathematical reasoning, and text-to-image generation tasks.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (DeBERTaV3-base), HRA with r=8 and λ=0 achieves 90.10% average score using only 0.66M trainable parameters, outperforming BOFT (89.89%, 0.75M), OFT (89.77%, 0.79M), and AdaLoRA (89.46%, 1.27M). On mathematical reasoning (LLaMA2-7B), HRA with r=32 and λ=10⁻⁴ reaches 56.3% on GSM8K and 9.3% on MATH with 0.12% trainable parameters, surpassing BOFT (50.6%/8.6%) and LoRA (50.2%/7.8%) at the same or higher parameter budgets. For controllable text-to-image generation (Stable Diffusion), HRA with r=7–8 achieves the best C2I mIoU (29.45%), S2I mIoU (42.02%), and L2F error (5.56) using only 0.89M parameters, compared to 20.76–26.40M for OFT/BOFT baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HRA consistently outperforms all baselines across the three experimental settings. On GLUE, HRA (r=8, λ=0) achieves 90.10% average with 0.66M parameters, beating BOFT (89.89%, 0.75M) by 0.21%, OFT (89.77%, 0.79M) by 0.33%, and AdaLoRA (89.46%, 1.27M) by 0.64% while using roughly half the parameters of AdaLoRA. On mathematical reasoning, HRA (r=32, λ=10⁻⁴) scores 56.3% GSM8K and 9.3% MATH at 0.12% parameter ratio, exceeding BOFT (50.6%/8.6% at 0.12%) by 5.7/0.7 points and LoRA (50.2%/7.8% at 0.25%) by 6.1/1.5 points with half the parameters; even PiSSA (53.1%/7.4%) requires 4.75% parameters—nearly 40× more. For subject-driven image generation, HRA (r=7, λ=0) achieves 0.670 DINO-IoU and 0.758 CLIP-T with 0.69M parameters, surpassing OFT (0.632/0.746 at 23.3M) and LoRA (0.613/0.744 at 0.8M). In controllable generation, HRA (r=8, λ=0) leads on C2I mIoU (29.45% vs. BOFT 28.83%, OFT 27.06%) and S2I mIoU (42.02% vs. BOFT 41.24%, OFT 40.09%) with 0.89M parameters versus 20.76–26.40M for OFT/BOFT. The strictly-orthogonal variant (λ=∞) underperforms the regularized versions on GLUE and controllable generation, indicating that moderate capacity is needed for these tasks, while it remains competitive on mathematical reasoning (52.8%/9.2% vs. 56.3%/9.3% for λ=10⁻⁴).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate PEFT paradigms—LoRA's additive low-rank modification and OFT's multiplicative orthogonal transformation—by showing that a chain of learnable Householder reflections (HRs) is simultaneously an orthogonal adapter and an adaptive low-rank adapter (WH^(r) = W + A(W,B)B). It builds directly on OFT's Cayley-parameterized block-diagonal matrices [37] and BOFT's butterfly factorization [34], replacing their structured sub-matrices with a simpler chain of rank-1 reflections, while inheriting OFT's theoretical guarantee that fine-tuned weights remain in the column space of the pre-trained weights. The novel addition is an orthogonality regularizer on the HR planes, which provides a continuous knob (λ) between maximum capacity (λ=0) and strict orthogonality (λ=∞ via Gram-Schmidt).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a single parameterization that subsumes both LoRA and OFT, HRA gives practitioners a more flexible and parameter-efficient adapter with a principled way to control the capacity-regularity trade-off, and its integration into the HuggingFace PEFT package signals practical adoption. The finding that strict orthogonality (λ=∞) can hurt performance on capacity-demanding tasks while moderate regularization (λ~10⁻⁴) balances both objectives is a useful empirical guideline. Open questions include adaptive rank selection (analogous to AdaLoRA), automatic λ scheduling, and whether the approach scales to much larger models (LLaMA-3, Grok-1) or benefits from other regularizers beyond Frobenius-norm orthogonality.
