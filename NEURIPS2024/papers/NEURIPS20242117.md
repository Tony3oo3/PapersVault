## Problem

Existing detection-based low-shot counters rely on a surrogate loss that places unit Gaussians at object centers, which is sensitive to annotation noise, requires heuristic kernel sizing, and biases detections toward compact blob-like structures. Additionally, these methods construct only a few prototypes (e.g., three) from exemplars to cover appearance diversity, leading to overgeneralization and false positives. As a result, detection-based counters have consistently trailed density-based methods in total count estimation, leaving a multi-year performance gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and k exemplar bounding boxes (few-shot) or no exemplars (zero-shot, where it counts the majority class via a pretrained objectness prototype). The image is scaled to 1536×1536 or 1024×1024 depending on exemplar size, encoded by a frozen SAM backbone at 1/16 resolution with 256 channels. Output is a set of bounding boxes with corresponding segmentation masks (via SAM decoder refinement), and the count is simply the number of surviving detections after NMS—no separate count regression is performed.

## Method

GeCo is a single-stage add-on network on a frozen SAM backbone that replaces the few-prototype paradigm with a Dense Query Encoder (DQE) that constructs w·h location-specific prototypes via N_P=3 iterative cross-attention steps, then builds dense object queries through N_Q=2 cross-attention iterations with self-attended image features. A Dense Query Decoder (DQD) spatially unpacks these queries to half-resolution (H₀/2 × W₀/2) using three upsampling stages augmented with SAM-HQ features, then predicts per-pixel objectness scores and tlrb bounding box parameters. The key training innovation is a dense detection loss that applies NMS to find local maxima, uses Hungarian matching to label them as TP/FP/FN against ground truth, and directly optimizes gIoU for matched boxes plus objectness scores (pushing TP/FN toward 1, FP toward 0), thereby eliminating the Gaussian surrogate and enabling automatic hard-negative mining. A single trained network covers few-shot, one-shot, and zero-shot setups, with zero-shot requiring only 10 additional epochs to train the objectness prototype extension.

## Results

On the FSCD147 test set, GeCo achieves a MAE of 7.91 and RMSE of 54.28 for counting, and AP of 43.42 and AP50 of 75.06 for detection, setting a new state-of-the-art across all metrics. This represents a 24% MAE improvement over the previous best detection-based counter DAVE (10.45) and a 27% MAE improvement over the long-standing density-based winner LOCA (10.79). On FSCD-LVIS, GeCo achieves 11.47 AP and 24.49 AP50, outperforming the best prior method by 178% and 73% respectively. In the one-shot setting, GeCo achieves 8.10 MAE and 74.31 AP50, surpassing three-shot DAVE by 22% MAE and three-shot PSECO by 38% MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeCo outperforms all 17 baselines on the FSCD147 test set. Among detection-based counters, GeCo beats DAVE [20] by 24% MAE (7.91 vs. 10.45) and 62% AP (43.42 vs. 26.81), and PSECO [35] by 39% MAE (7.91 vs. 13.05) and 52% RMSE (54.28 vs. 112.86), while matching PSECO on AP (43.42 vs. 42.98) and slightly exceeding it on AP50 (75.06 vs. 73.33). Against C-DETR [19] and SAM-C [18], GeCo leads by 53%/72% MAE and 92%/55% AP respectively. Among density-based counters, GeCo surpasses the top performer LOCA [4] by 27% MAE (7.91 vs. 10.79) and 5% RMSE (54.28 vs. 56.97), and CounTR [14] by 34% MAE and 41% RMSE. On FSCD-LVIS, GeCo outperforms DAVE [20] by 1% MAE and 178% AP (11.47 vs. 4.12), and C-DETR [19] by 35% MAE and 199% AP. In the zero-shot setting, GeCo beats DAVE [20] by 14% MAE (13.30 vs. 15.51) and CounTR [14] by 6% MAE (13.30 vs. 14.12), though it trails RCC [10] slightly on RMSE (108.72 vs. 104.50) while leading on MAE (13.30 vs. 17.12).

## Relation to Prior Work

GeCo builds on the SAM backbone as a frozen feature extractor and adopts LOCA's shape-prototype formulation, but departs fundamentally from both the density-based counting paradigm (LOCA, CounTR, SAFECount) and prior detection-based counters (DAVE, PSECO, C-DETR). Where DAVE and PSECO use multi-stage detect-and-verify pipelines trained with a unit-Gaussian surrogate loss, GeCo is a single-stage architecture that replaces few-prototype correlation with an image-wide dense query formulation (inverting the DETR query/key relationship) and a novel loss that directly optimizes bounding-box detection via Hungarian matching and gIoU. The dense query encoder's non-parametric prototype generalization across all spatial locations is the key architectural departure from the compact-prototype paradigm that has dominated the field since FSC147.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeCo closes a multi-year performance gap by becoming the first detection-based counter to outperform the long-standing density-based winner (LOCA) in total count MAE while simultaneously delivering bounding boxes and segmentation masks, making it the first method to unify counting, detection, and segmentation in one architecture. Its remarkable robustness to exemplar count (one-shot GeCo outperforms three-shot DAVE) suggests the dense query formulation is less sensitive to prototype diversity than prior methods. The main limitation is that the global operating mode constrains image size due to memory, and the paper acknowledges future work on local counting and faster backbones. An open question is whether the SAM backbone dependency limits the approach's applicability to domains where SAM features are suboptimal.
