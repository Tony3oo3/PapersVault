## Problem

Existing graph autoencoders (GAEs) rely on self-correlation (z_i^T z_j) to decode node embeddings back into adjacency matrices, which fundamentally cannot represent islands (non-self-loop nodes), topologically symmetric structures, or directed edges, and constrains optimization to a restricted n×d' space. These deficiencies are especially acute in multi-graph (graph-task) settings such as molecules, where such structures are prevalent rather than rare. The paper asks whether a cross-correlation decoding mechanism can overcome these representational and optimization limitations while preserving encoder flexibility for downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The model consumes node features X ∈ R^{n×d} and an adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} per graph. The encoder (any GNN with pooling layers) produces a reduced latent representation Z' ∈ R^{n'×d'} where n' < n after graph coarsening; the decoder then expands this back to full-size node embeddings via two parallel mirrored GNN branches, yielding P, Q ∈ R^{n×d'}. The final output is the reconstructed adjacency matrix Ã = sigmoid(PQ^T), with a loss-balanced binary cross-entropy objective that up-weights the minority positive edges to counteract adjacency sparsity (~90% zeros in PROTEINS).

## Method

GraphCroc replaces the standard self-correlation decoder (sigmoid(ZZ^T)) with a cross-correlation decoder (sigmoid(PQ^T)) where P and Q are produced by two independent, structurally identical GNN branches that mirror the encoder's pooling/unpooling hierarchy. The encoder is deliberately left as a free design choice (GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT, GIN all work) so it can be tailored to downstream tasks, while the decoder's U-Net-like architecture with skip connections and layer normalization handles the structural reconstruction. A loss-balancing strategy applies scaling factors α_0 = (c_0+c_1)/(2c_0) and α_1 = (c_0+c_1)/(2c_1) to the zero and one element losses respectively, preventing the decoder from collapsing to all-zero predictions on sparse graphs. Theoretically, the paper proves that self-correlation requires embedding dimension d' ≥ n−1 to represent arbitrary connection patterns, and that cross-correlation doubles the argument space from R^{n×d'} to R^{n×2d'}, yielding a smoother region of attraction and faster convergence—validated by trajectory visualizations on PROTEINS showing self-correlation embeddings oscillating while cross-correlation embeddings converge monotonically.

## Results

On structural reconstruction (ROC-AUC on adjacency matrix), GraphCroc achieves the best score across all five graph tasks: 0.9958 on PROTEINS, 0.9992 on IMDB-B, 0.9989 on COLLAB, 0.9831 on PPI, and 0.9987 on QM9. The best self-correlation baseline (GraphCroc under self-correlation or EGNN) reaches at most 0.9984 (QM9), while naive GAE/VGAE scores are far lower (0.47–0.79). On graph classification, the GraphCroc encoder fine-tuned for only 10 epochs already matches or exceeds dedicated GAE-based methods (e.g., 76.69% on IMDB-B vs. 75.76% for S2GAE), and reaches 79.09% on PROTEINS after 100 epochs, surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphCroc decisively outperforms all self-correlation baselines on structural reconstruction: naive GAE and VGAE [19] score 0.47–0.79 AUC, far below GraphCroc's 0.98–0.999; EGNN [30] (L2-norm decoding) is the strongest self-correlation competitor (0.9947 on COLLAB, 0.9984 on QM9) but still trails GraphCroc and fails on PPI due to memory constraints. DiGAE [20], the only other cross-correlation method, is consistently 0.15–0.25 AUC below GraphCroc across all datasets, confirming that the U-Net architecture is critical beyond the decoding kernel alone. On graph classification (Table 2), the GraphCroc encoder surpasses unsupervised (Infograph [34]: 74.44% on PROTEINS), contrastive (GraphCL [48]: 74.39%, InfoGCL [44]: 75.10%), and generative baselines (GraphMAE [15]: 75.30%, S2GAE [35]: 76.37%, StructMAE [25]: 75.97%) after 100 epochs of fine-tuning. In the architecture ablation (Table 7), replacing the GCN kernel with GraphSAGE [13], GAT [36], or GIN [45] within the cross-correlation framework still yields strong results (0.96–0.999 AUC), but the full GraphCroc with skip connections and layer normalization remains the best, with GAT performing notably worse (e.g., 0.9236 vs. 0.9831 on PPI).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph autoencoder lineage initiated by VGAE (Kipf & Welling, 2016) by replacing the standard self-correlation decoder (sigmoid(ZZ^T)) with a cross-correlation decoder (sigmoid(PQ^T)), building on the directed-graph-specific cross-correlation idea from DiGAE (Kollias et al., 2022) but generalizing it to undirected graphs and multi-graph tasks. The encoder/decoder architecture is adapted from Graph U-Net (Gao & Ji, 2019), with the key departure that the encoder is left free for downstream task design while the decoder is structured as a mirrored dual-branch GNN that produces two independent embedding spaces. The paper also introduces a loss-balancing strategy for the inherent sparsity of adjacency matrices, which prior GAE work largely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical analysis establishing that self-correlation fundamentally cannot represent islands, topologically symmetric structures, or directed edges—and that it requires embedding dimension d' ≥ n−1 for worst-case solvability—provides a clear conceptual justification for cross-correlation that goes beyond empirical gains. Practically, the cross-correlation + U-Net combination achieves near-perfect reconstruction (AUC > 0.99) across diverse graph tasks, and the encoder can be repurposed for graph classification with minimal fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the advantage persists at very large graph scales (PPI already shows degradation), whether the U-Net architecture is strictly necessary versus simpler cross-correlation decoders, and whether the identified adversarial attack surface on GAE latent spaces can be mitigated.
