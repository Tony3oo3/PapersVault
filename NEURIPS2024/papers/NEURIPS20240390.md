## Problem

Existing few-shot customized text-guided diffusion models (e.g., Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, Reversion, ADI) cannot effectively decouple action semantics from actor-specific details (hair color, clothing, body type) when fine-tuning on a handful of exemplar images. The root cause is the absence of an inductive bias that constrains the learned token to encode only action information, causing irrelevant actor features to be confounded into the generated images. This paper tackles the problem by introducing a structured action-only representation space that inherently excludes actor-specific semantics.

## Input / Output

The method takes approximately 10 user-provided RGB exemplar images of a target action (e.g., a three-point landing pose) and a text prompt containing a unique customized token V* (e.g., "Leonardo V*"). CLIP is used as a frozen action encoder to extract semantic features from the exemplar images, which drive the coefficient optimization. The output is a 1024×1024 image of a new subject (human, animal, or previously unseen customized actor) performing the specified action, generated via Stable Diffusion XL with 50 DDPM sampling steps and a guidance scale of 7.5.

## Method

TwinAct constructs a Common Action Space by collecting 832 action phrases (generated via GPT-4 and filtered for TGDM recognizability), encoding them with CLIP's text encoder, and applying PCA (p=512) to each positional embedding group to obtain a set of action bases [B₁, B₂, B₃] that span only action semantics. A frozen CLIP image encoder extracts action features from the exemplar images, which are mapped through a small MLP to produce coefficients [W₁, W₂, W₃]; the customized token embedding V̂ is then formed as a linear combination of the action bases, guaranteeing it cannot encode actor-irrelevant information. During fine-tuning, the token embedding and LoRA layers (rank 8) in the U-Net are optimized with a combined loss: the standard diffusion reconstruction loss plus a novel action similarity loss that maximizes cosine similarity between CLIP features of the reference and partially denoised generated images, shifting the model's focus from pixel-level fidelity to high-level action semantics. This three-step pipeline—space construction, coefficient-based imitation, and similarity-guided generation—enables the same action token to transfer across diverse actors while preserving identity consistency.

## Results

On the TwinAct Benchmark (12 actions, ~10 sample images each), TwinAct achieves SAction = 69.47 and SActor = 73.34, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline ADI (45.32 and 48.76 respectively) by over 24 points on both metrics. In user studies (100 raters, 1–5 scale), TwinAct scores 3.86 on action fidelity (UAction) and 4.12 on actor identity consistency (UActor), compared to ADI's 2.25 and 2.08. The method also demonstrates consistent superiority across four action types (Single-Actor, Two-Actor, With-Human, With-Animal) with the least variability among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TwinAct outperforms all seven fine-tuning baselines on every reported metric. The closest competitor, ADI [7], achieves SAction = 45.32 and SActor = 48.76, trailing TwinAct by 24.15 and 24.58 points respectively; ADI also shows the largest performance drop on two-actor actions. P+ [27] (SAction = 33.95) and Custom Diffusion [10] (SAction = 26.83) retain more action information than Textual Inversion [4] (9.12) or DreamBooth [22] (12.23) but still confound actor-irrelevant details (e.g., hair color, clothing) into the generated images. Reversion [8] (SAction = 18.73) uses contrastive learning to exclude irrelevant semantics but cannot cover all negative samples, leaving residual confounding. The two no-fine-tuning baselines—Stable Diffusion with detailed text prompts [21] and ControlNet with skeleton/sketch conditioning [31]—are compared qualitatively only; both fail to capture fine action details (fingers, body angles) and produce severe body deformations, especially for animal subjects.

## Relation to Prior Work

TwinAct sits at the intersection of few-shot TGDM customization (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, P+) and the emerging sub-problem of action-specific customization. Prior action-customization attempts (Reversion, ADI, Lego) rely on contrastive learning over textual embeddings or visual features to disentangle actions from actors, but the paper argues these cannot exclude all action-irrelevant semantics with limited negative samples. TwinAct departs from this contrastive-learning paradigm by constraining the customized token to a PCA subspace built exclusively from 832 action phrases, repurposing the morphable-model idea from 3D face synthesis [2] as a structural inductive bias, and using CLIP as a frozen action encoder to initialize the combination coefficients via an MLP rather than relying on backpropagation alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core contribution is a practical recipe for decoupling action from actor that outperforms contrastive-learning baselines by a wide margin (SAction 69.47 vs. 45.32 for ADI), suggesting that subspace constraint is a stronger inductive bias than adversarial/contrastive disentanglement for this specific problem. However, the evaluation benchmark is small (12 actions, ~10 exemplars each), the method is tied to CLIP's action understanding and a fixed set of 832 phrases, and the paper acknowledges that the action space may miss less common movements. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, how it scales to actions outside the phrase vocabulary, and whether the CLIP-based action encoder generalizes to culturally specific or highly dynamic motions.
