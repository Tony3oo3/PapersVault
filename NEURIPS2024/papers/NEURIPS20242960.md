## Problem

Diffusion-based generative models rely on Brownian motion as the noising process, which limits their ability to naturally handle data on constrained domains, Riemannian manifolds, or distributions with mixed continuous and lower-dimensional components. Piecewise deterministic Markov processes (PDMPs) offer structural advantages for these settings, but no generative framework existed that leverages their time-reversal structure analogous to Anderson's backward diffusion equation.

## Input / Output

The forward process starts from a data point x₀ with an initial velocity drawn from a simple distribution (uniform over {±1}^d for ZZP, standard normal or uniform on S^{d-1} for BPS/RHMC) and evolves under PDMP dynamics until time T_f, at which point the position marginal approaches a standard Gaussian. The backward process is initialised from the product of a standard Gaussian position and the corresponding velocity distribution, then simulated over T_f to produce a synthetic data sample. The state space is R^d × V, and the learned components are either density ratios (ZZP) or conditional velocity densities p_t(v|x) (BPS, RHMC).

## Method

The paper shows that the time reversal of any PDMP is itself a PDMP whose backward jump rates and kernels depend on ratios of conditional velocity densities p_t(v|x) of the forward process (Propositions 1–2). For ZZP, backward rates are learned via an explicit ratio-matching objective adapted from Hyvärinen (2007) that avoids needing the normalising constant; for BPS and RHMC, normalising flows are trained by maximum likelihood to model p_t(v|x), enabling both density evaluation and sampling from the backward refreshment kernel. The backward PDMP is simulated using second-order splitting schemes (DJD for ZZP/RHMC, RDBDR for BPS) that alternate half-steps of deterministic motion with frozen-rate jump proposals, since the neural-network-defined rates cannot be integrated analytically. A total-variation error bound (Theorem 1) decomposes the generation error into an exponential term from initialising at the forward process's limiting distribution and a term proportional to the approximation error in the learned backward characteristics.

## Results

On five 2D toy distributions, the PDMP-based generative models (ZZP, BPS, RHMC) consistently outperform i-DDPM in MMD (lower is better). ZZP achieves the best overall MMD on four of five datasets (Checkerboard: 0.81, Fractal tree: 1.12, Olympic rings: 1.43, Rose: 0.90), while i-DDPM scores 2.49, 8.04, 2.03, and 6.77 respectively. RHMC excels with very few backward steps, achieving MMD of 26.48 with only 2 steps on the Rose dataset versus 696.28 for i-DDPM. With 100 backward steps, ZZP reaches MMD 1.04 on Rose, the best among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is i-DDPM (Nichol & Dhariwal, ICML 2021), the standard diffusion-based generative model. ZZP outperforms i-DDPM on four of five 2D toy datasets, with particularly large margins on Fractal tree (1.12 vs. 8.04 MMD) and Rose (0.90 vs. 6.77 MMD). BPS also beats i-DDPM on all five datasets, with the largest gap on Gaussian grid (4.59 vs. 23.19 MMD). RHMC is competitive with i-DDPM on most datasets but dominates in the low-step regime: with only 2 backward steps on Rose, RHMC achieves MMD 26.48 versus 696.28 for i-DDPM, a 26× improvement. However, with 100 steps, ZZP (1.04) slightly edges out RHMC (1.72) and BPS (3.66), while i-DDPM remains worst (8.72). The paper also reports promising preliminary results applying ZZP to MNIST in the appendix, though no direct i-DDPM comparison is provided there.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the time-reversal framework of diffusion-based generative models (Anderson 1982; Song et al. 2021) to the setting of piecewise deterministic Markov processes, building directly on Conforti and Léonard's (2022) characterization of time reversals for Markov processes with jumps. The three forward processes—Zig-Zag, Bouncy Particle Sampler, and Randomised Hamiltonian Monte Carlo—are drawn from the PDMP sampling literature (Bierkens et al. 2019a; Bouchard-Côté et al. 2018; Bou-Rabee and Sanz-Serna 2017), and the backward simulation relies on splitting schemes from Bertazzi et al. (2023). For learning the backward characteristics, the paper adapts Hyvärinen's (2007) ratio-matching objective to the discrete velocity space of ZZP and employs normalizing flows for BPS/RHMC, drawing a structural parallel to score-matching in diffusion models but targeting velocity conditional densities rather than position score functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a theoretically grounded alternative to diffusion-based generative models, showing that PDMPs admit explicit time reversals with backward characteristics expressible through velocity conditional density ratios, and provides a total-variation error bound decomposing initialization and approximation errors. The empirical results on 2D toy distributions suggest PDMP-based models (particularly ZZP and BPS) can match or exceed i-DDPM quality with substantially fewer reverse steps, hinting at computational advantages. However, the evidence is limited to low-dimensional synthetic data and a brief MNIST experiment, leaving open critical questions about scalability to high-dimensional image generation, the choice of network architectures for density-ratio estimation, and whether the theoretical advantages (constrained domains, manifold support, fewer steps) translate into practical gains at scale.
