## Problem

Computing Tensor Train (TT) decompositions via Alternating Least Squares (ALS) requires solving least-squares problems on full tensor unfoldings at each iteration, yielding exponential cost in the tensor order N. While randomized SVD variants accelerate TT-SVD, efficient randomized variants of TT-ALS have been largely unexplored, and existing sketching-based approaches (e.g., TensorSketch TT-ALS) require sketch sizes with exponential dependence on the tensor dimension. No method existed for efficiently sampling from the exact leverage score distribution of the design matrices that arise in TT-ALS.

## Input / Output

The method accepts N-dimensional tensors (dense or sparse) of arbitrary order and mode sizes, including hyperspectral images (e.g., 610×340×103), video tensors (e.g., 720×1280×286), and large sparse tensors with tens of millions of nonzeros (e.g., NELL-2 with ~77M entries). It outputs a rank-(R₁,…,R_{N−1}) TT decomposition as N third-order core tensors Aₙ ∈ ℝ^{R_{n-1}×Iₙ×Rₙ}, providing a compressed representation whose parameter count scales linearly in N. The algorithm operates in-place on the tensor's matricized form, selecting only J rows per least-squares solve rather than processing all I₁⋯I_N entries.

## Method

rTT-ALS maintains the TT decomposition in canonical form (left-orthonormal cores to the left of the active core, right-orthonormal to the right) throughout each ALS sweep via QR decompositions after each core update. This orthogonality makes the design matrix A^{≠j} = A_{<j} ⊗ A_{>j}^⊤ orthonormal, so the pseudo-inverse in the leverage score formula reduces to the identity, eliminating the O(NIR²+R³) cost of computing it. The leverage scores then factorize as products of squared row norms of the left and right core chains, and a data structure (a truncated binary tree caching R×R matrices per node, adapted from Bharadwaj et al. 2023) enables sampling a single row in O(R² log I) time with O(IR²) construction. Sampling proceeds from core A_j down to A_1, maintaining a history vector updated by matrix-vector multiplication, with a uniform column draw reducing the problem from a history matrix to a vector. The resulting sketch size J = Õ(R²/εδ) is independent of the tensor dimension I, and the overall per-iteration cost drops to Õ(R⁴·N log I) rather than O(I^N·R²).

## Results

On sparse FROSTT tensors, rTT-ALS achieves up to 26× per-iteration speedup over exact TT-ALS on NELL-2 (R=4) while matching fit to three significant figures (e.g., 0.0213 vs 0.0214). On dense real datasets (Table 1), rTT-ALS is up to 10× faster than TT-ALS (e.g., 0.82s vs 4.16s on Pavia Uni) and ~6× faster than TT-SVD, with fit values matching to two decimal places. On synthetic tensors (I=500, R=5), rTT-ALS is ~2× faster than TT-ALS and ~3× faster than TT-SVD, while rTT-SVD is fastest but achieves noticeably lower fit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TT-ALS (Holtz et al., 2012), rTT-ALS wins decisively on runtime: up to 26× faster per ALS sweep on NELL-2 and up to 10× faster on dense real datasets, while matching fit to three significant figures on Uber and NELL-2 (slightly lower on Enron, e.g., 0.0728 vs 0.0771 at R=10). Against TT-SVD (Oseledets, 2011), rTT-ALS is consistently faster (e.g., 0.82s vs 6.65s on Pavia Uni) with equivalent fit. Against rTT-SVD (Huber et al., 2017), rTT-ALS is slower (e.g., 0.82s vs 0.33s on Pavia Uni) but achieves comparable or slightly better fit, and unlike rTT-SVD, rTT-ALS's sketch size has no exponential dependence on tensor dimension I. The key advantage over the TensorSketch-based TT-ALS of Chen et al. (2023) is that rTT-ALS's sample count J = Õ(R²/εδ) is independent of the tensor dimension, whereas TensorSketch's sketch size grows exponentially in I.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the leverage score sampling framework for tensor decomposition from the Khatri-Rao product setting (Bharadwaj et al., 2023) to the Tensor Train core chain, adapting the data structure to handle the sequential, non-commutative nature of TT contractions. The key conceptual departure from prior work is exploiting the canonical (orthonormal) form of the TT decomposition, which makes the pseudo-inverse in the leverage score formula collapse to the identity, eliminating the O(NIR²+R³) cost identified by Malik et al. (2022). Unlike the TensorSketch-based TT-ALS of Chen et al. (2023), whose sketch size grows exponentially in the tensor dimension I, this method's sketch size depends only on the rank R and the accuracy parameters ε, δ, with no dependence on I.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first ALS-based TT decomposition algorithm with exact leverage score sampling, providing a practical path to near-linear-in-tensor-size ALS iterations with strong relative-error guarantees. The up-to-26× per-iteration speedup on large sparse tensors (NELL-2) with negligible accuracy loss makes previously intractable high-order sparse tensor decompositions feasible. Open questions include whether the canonical-form trick generalizes to more complex tensor network topologies (hierarchical, tree-based) and whether the growing fit gap at higher ranks (visible in Table 2) can be closed with adaptive sample counts or improved initialization strategies.
