## Problem

Existing contextual linear optimization (CLO) methods assume full observation of random cost coefficient vectors, but in many applications (e.g., traffic routing) only the realized cost of a historical decision is available—what the authors term bandit feedback. This paper is the first to study offline policy learning for CLO under this partial feedback model, providing both theoretical regret guarantees and computationally tractable algorithms.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of (X_i, Z_i, C_i) triples where X_i ∈ R^p are contextual features, Z_i are historical decisions from a logging policy, and C_i = Z_i^T Y_i is the scalar observed cost (a single projection of the unobserved cost vector Y_i). The constraint set Z is a polytope (e.g., flow-conservation constraints for shortest path), and the output is a plug-in decision policy π(x) that maps new feature observations to a vertex of Z. A key structural requirement is the coverage condition: historical decisions must explore all directions in span(Z) conditional on X, otherwise certain policies cannot be evaluated.

## Method

The core approach is induced empirical risk minimization (IERM) adapted to bandit feedback: instead of the two-step estimate-then-optimize pipeline, the method directly minimizes an estimated decision cost over a class of plug-in policies. Policy value V(π) is identified via three score functions—direct method (DM), inverse spectral weighting (ISW), and doubly robust (DR)—that exploit the linear cost structure to extrapolate from observed costs to counterfactual decisions. Nuisance functions f_0(X) = E[Y|X] and Σ_0(X) = E[ZZ^T|X] are estimated via cross-fitting to avoid overfitting. For computation, the nonconvex IERM objective is replaced by the SPO+ surrogate loss with Y_i replaced by the score θ(X_i, Z_i, C_i; f̂, Σ̂), yielding a convex optimization in f(x). The theory provides a fast-rate regret bound O(r̃^((2α+2)/(α+2))) under a margin condition, with a phase transition to slow rate O(r̃) under policy misspecification, and the DR score achieves second-order nuisance error O(χ²) versus first-order O(χ) for DM/ISW.

## Results

On a simulated 5×5 stochastic shortest path problem (d=40 edges, m=70 feasible paths) with a random logging policy and correctly specified models, SPO+DM achieves the lowest relative regret ratio: 2.30% (n=400), 0.36% (n=1000), and 0.16% (n=1600), outperforming ETO (3.34%, 0.74%, 0.35%) by roughly 30–55%. Naive methods that ignore the linear cost structure (Naive ETO, Naive SPO+) perform dramatically worse, with regret ratios of 15.03%, 12.12%, and 3.53% at the same sample sizes—approximately 6× higher than the best IERM method. Among DR variants, the pseudo-inverse (DR PI) performs worst due to variance inflation, while Lambda regularization and eigenvalue clipping reduce regret to 0.18% and 0.20% respectively at n=1600.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four families of methods: (1) ETO (estimate-then-optimize), (2) IERM with SPO+ surrogate using different score functions (DM, DR PI, DR Lambda, DR Clip), and (3) naive extensions that treat the 70 feasible paths as discrete actions without exploiting the linear cost structure. SPO+DM consistently outperforms ETO across all sample sizes (e.g., 0.16% vs. 0.35% at n=1600), confirming the benefit of end-to-end optimization. The naive baselines (Naive ETO, Naive SPO+DM, Naive SPO+DR) are 6–10× worse than the best IERM method, demonstrating that leveraging the linear structure of decision costs is critical. Among DR variants, the raw pseudo-inverse (DR PI) suffers from high variance and performs worst (0.32% at n=1600), while Lambda regularization (0.18%) and eigenvalue clipping (0.20%) effectively mitigate this and approach DM-level performance. The ISW score (reported in the appendix) also performs poorly due to high variance, further supporting DM as the preferred score function in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the induced empirical risk minimization (IERM) framework for contextual linear optimization from the full-feedback setting (Elmachtoub & Grigas 2022; Hu, Kallus & Mao 2022) to the bandit-feedback setting, where only the realized cost of a historical decision is observed. It adapts the SPO+ surrogate loss by replacing unobserved cost vectors with score functions (DM, ISW, DR) identified via cross-fitting (Chernozhukov et al. 2018), and generalizes the doubly robust identification of Chernozhukov et al. (2019) to allow rank-deficient second-moment matrices via the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse. The regret analysis builds on the margin-condition framework of Hu, Kallus & Mao (2022) but extends it to accommodate policy-class misspecification and nuisance estimation error, yielding a byproduct fast-rate bound for full-feedback CLO with misspecification that was previously unavailable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first treatment of CLO under bandit feedback, a setting that arises naturally in applications like traffic routing (Uber Movement data) and portfolio optimization where counterfactual costs are unobservable. The theoretical phase transition—fast rates under well-specification versus slow rates under misspecification—provides a clear diagnostic for practitioners, and the empirical finding that the simpler DM score outperforms the theoretically superior DR score (due to pseudo-inverse variance inflation) offers a practical counterintuitive insight. Limitations include the restriction to parametric induced policy classes and the focus on a single surrogate loss (SPO+); extending to nonparametric classes and exploring alternative surrogates are natural next steps.
