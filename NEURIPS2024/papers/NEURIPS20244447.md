## Problem

While meeting summarization has been extensively studied, no prior work measures the relevance of a pre-meeting agenda topic to the conversation that actually occurred. This gap is critical for building virtual meeting moderators that detect when discussions drift off-topic, yet the field lacked both a dedicated dataset and benchmarks for this task.

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds GPT-4 a fixed-duration transcript snippet (5, 10, or 15 minutes, cut by timestamps) alongside the full list of meeting topics, and requests a 4-level multiple-choice relevance score (0=Not Relevant through 3=Very Relevant) for each topic. For evaluation, the 4-level scores are binarized: a score of 0 maps to 'Not Discussed' and anything above maps to 'Discussed', with ground truth determined by whether a topic was discussed for more than 30 seconds in the snippet. The primary evaluation treats 'Not Discussed' as the positive class, reporting precision and recall for detecting off-topic segments.

## Method

The core contribution is the TCR dataset (1,506 meetings, 22M words, 15,000+ topics) aggregating newly collected Speech Interruption Meetings (SIM) data with five public sources (ICSI, QMSum, MeetingBank, NCPC, ELITR), all unified under a single JSON schema. GPT-4 is used in a zero-shot multiple-choice prompt to score transcript-topic relevance, with the prompt format chosen after comparing floats, integers, binaries, and multiple-choice outputs for robustness. A key preprocessing step uses GPT-4 to rewrite verbose post-meeting minutes (e.g., MeetingBank summaries) into concise pre-meeting agenda-style topics, validated with a mean MOS of 4.6/5. The dataset also includes 100 synthetic meetings generated by splicing 5–11 minute snippets from different raw SIM meetings, plus augmentation scripts that add un-discussed planned topics or remove discussed topics to simulate real-world agenda deviations.

## Results

GPT-4 achieves F1 scores ranging from 0.72 (ICSI_QMSum, 15-min snippets) to 0.98 (MeetingBank_ReAnnotated_rnd30, 5-min snippets) on the Topic-Conversation Relevance task, where the positive class is 'Not Discussed.' Highly structured meetings (MeetingBank, NCPC) yield F1 above 0.96 across all snippet lengths, while less structured project and brainstorming meetings (ICSI, ELITR, SIM) drop to 0.72–0.96. Performance degrades modestly as snippet length increases from 5 to 15 minutes, with the largest drops observed in ICSI_QMSum (F1 from 0.75 to 0.72) and ELITR (F1 from 0.84 to 0.80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare GPT-4 against other models or methods; it establishes the first benchmarks for the Topic-Conversation Relevance task. The most informative comparisons are across data sources and annotation styles. Re-annotating MeetingBank topics into pre-meeting agenda style (via GPT-4) yields a small but consistent F1 improvement over the original detailed minutes (e.g., 0.982 vs. 0.980 at 5 min), confirming that agenda-style topics are a cleaner signal. The same ICSI transcripts annotated with QMSum minutes perform substantially worse (F1 ≈ 0.72–0.75) than with the original ICSI topic labels (F1 ≈ 0.85–0.87), a gap of roughly 12–13 F1 points, suggesting that the granularity and style of topic annotations strongly affect model performance. Structured government meetings (MeetingBank, NCPC) are near-saturated (F1 > 0.96), while unstructured project and brainstorming meetings (ICSI, ELITR, SIM) remain the challenging regime, with recall dropping to 0.59–0.79, indicating that GPT-4 frequently misses off-topic segments in free-flowing discussions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining Topic-Conversation Relevance (TCR) as a distinct task from the well-studied meeting summarization/minuting literature (AutoMin challenges, QMSum, MeetingBank). While prior work focuses on generating summaries from transcripts, TCR inverts the direction: given a pre-defined agenda topic and a transcript segment, judge relevance. The authors explicitly state no prior research addresses this formulation, and they repurpose existing meeting corpora (ICSI, MeetingBank, QMSum, ELITR, AMI, NCPC) under a unified schema to enable this new evaluation, supplemented by newly collected SIM data and synthetic multi-topic meetings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TCR dataset (1,500+ meetings, 22M words, 15,000+ topics) provides the first comprehensive resource for building virtual meeting moderators and meeting-effectiveness tools, a practically important application given the shift to online meetings. Key open questions include the blurred boundaries between topics (needing sentence-level sub-labels), the lack of multi-language and audio coverage, and the reliance on a single LLM (GPT-4) as the sole benchmark evaluator without human-annotated ground truth for the relevance scores themselves. The finding that structured meetings (MeetingBank, NCPC) yield F1 > 0.96 while unstructured project meetings (ICSI_QMSum) drop to ~0.72 highlights that topic-transition clarity is the primary difficulty axis.
