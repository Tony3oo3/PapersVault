## Problem

Wasserstein gradient flows driven by the MMD energy (e.g., Arbel et al. 2019) lack global convergence guarantees and suffer from practical pathologies such as particle collapse at modes and getting stuck at local minima, requiring heuristic noise injection. The paper asks whether a new gradient flow dissipation geometry can simultaneously enable mass teleportation to escape local minima and provide provable global exponential convergence for both MMD and KL divergence energies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240462/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an initial particle measure represented as weighted Dirac masses (positions x_i and weights α_i) together with samples y_j drawn from the target distribution π and a Gaussian kernel bandwidth σ. It outputs an approximate target measure as updated particle positions and weights, where some particles may have their weights driven to zero (mass teleportation). The spherical variant enforces the simplex constraint on weights at every step, guaranteeing the output remains a valid probability measure with total mass one.

## Method

The IFT gradient flow is constructed by replacing the Hellinger (Fisher-Rao) tensor in the Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao inf-convolution with a kernelized MMD Riemannian metric tensor, yielding an Onsager operator that is the sum of the Wasserstein divergence operator and the MMD kernel-inverse operator. The resulting flow equation combines a transport term (divergence of the energy gradient weighted by the measure) with a reaction term (kernel-inverse of the energy gradient), enabling both spatial movement and mass redistribution. A spherical variant projects the MMD component onto the probability simplex via a Lagrange multiplier, ensuring mass preservation. Discretization uses a JKO time-splitting: a Wasserstein step updates particle positions via gradient descent, followed by an MMD step that solves a convex quadratic program over the simplex to update particle weights. The key theoretical contribution is proving global exponential convergence via a Lojasiewicz-type inequality for the MMD energy (no log-concavity or kernel conditions needed) and via the log-Sobolev inequality for the KL energy, establishing that the IFT flow enjoys the best of both worlds.

## Results

The paper's experiments are primarily illustrative rather than benchmarking, using 2D Gaussian and Gaussian mixture targets with n=100 particles (Gaussian kernel, σ=10, τ=50, η=0.1). The headline finding is that IFT (Algorithm 1) reaches comparable MMD loss to the noise-injected MMD flow of Arbel et al. [2019] in approximately 1000 steps, whereas the noisy baseline requires ~3000 steps and a manually tuned noise schedule. In the 100-dimensional Gaussian mixture experiment, IFT outperforms the WFR flow of the MMD energy, which lacks the global convergence guarantee that IFT enjoys (Theorem 3.5). No single numerical results table is provided; performance is reported via mean loss curves over 50 runs (Figure 3) and particle trajectory visualizations (Figures 4–6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares IFT against two baselines: (1) the vanilla MMD flow of Arbel et al. [2019] and its noise-injected variant, and (2) the Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao (WFR) flow of the MMD energy. Against the vanilla MMD flow, IFT clearly wins: the vanilla flow suffers from mode collapse (particles clustering at the target mode) and particles getting stuck at local minima, while IFT teleports mass away from stuck particles (shown as hollow dots in Figures 4–5), avoiding these failure modes entirely. Against the noise-injected MMD flow, IFT achieves similar final accuracy but converges roughly 3× faster (~1000 steps vs. ~3000 steps) and requires no heuristic noise schedule that must be manually tuned per problem instance. Against the WFR flow in the 100D experiment (Figure 6), IFT achieves lower MMD loss, and critically, IFT has a provable global exponential convergence guarantee (Theorem 3.5) while the WFR flow of the MMD does not. The paper notes that IFT's per-iteration cost is counted as two steps (Wasserstein + MMD), so the comparison is already handicapped by a factor of 2 in its favor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper constructs a new gradient flow geometry by replacing the Hellinger (Fisher-Rao) tensor in the Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao inf-convolution (Liero et al. 2018, Chizat et al. 2018) with the MMD Riemannian metric tensor derived by Zhu and Mielke (2024), yielding the interaction-force transport (IFT) flow. It departs from Arbel et al. (2019), who used the MMD energy under the pure Wasserstein geometry and lacked global convergence guarantees, and from WFR-based sampling methods (Yan et al. 2024, Lu et al. 2019) that target the KL energy. The JKO-splitting discretization follows Gallouet and Monsaingeon (2017), adapted here to the MMD step rather than the Hellinger/KL step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first global exponential convergence guarantee for MMD energy minimization via a gradient flow, filling a gap that pure Wasserstein MMD flows (Arbel et al.) could not address due to the non-convexity of MMD along Wasserstein geodesics. The 'best of both worlds' result — exponential convergence for both MMD and KL energies under the same IFT geometry — is a clean theoretical contribution. Open questions include extending the framework to settings where only the score function of the target is available (no samples), and whether particle rejuvenation strategies can further improve practical performance in high-dimensional sampling tasks.
