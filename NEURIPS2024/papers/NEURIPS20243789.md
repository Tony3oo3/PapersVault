## Problem

Dataset condensation has been extensively studied for image classification but remains largely unexplored for object detection, due to two compounding challenges: the multitasking requirement of simultaneously localizing and classifying multiple objects per image, and the large-scale, high-resolution nature of detection datasets combined with complex detector architectures that make traditional bi-level optimization intractable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243789/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full object detection training set (e.g., 16,551 VOC images or 118,287 COCO images at 512×512 resolution) with multi-instance bounding box annotations (x, y, w, h, class) and produces a condensed synthetic set at compression rates as low as 0.25%–2%, where each synthetic image contains multiple foreground objects of varying sizes, shapes, and categories at different positions, paired with corresponding box labels. The synthetic set is then used to train a fresh detector from scratch, which is evaluated on the original test set to measure condensation quality.

## Method

DCOD replaces the expensive bi-level optimization of prior DC methods with a two-stage Fetch-and-Forge pipeline. In Fetch, a standard detector (e.g., YOLOv3-SPP) is trained on the original dataset, encoding localization and classification knowledge into its weights. In Forge, model inversion reconstructs synthetic images by optimizing pixel values against the frozen detector's detection loss, guided by randomly sampled box labels from the original set. Two key mechanisms handle multi-object complexity: Foreground-Background Decoupling (F_BD) suppresses background updates (scaling by α<1) while applying random erasure to foreground regions to concentrate gradient signal on object pixels, and Incremental PatchExpand (I_PE) progressively subdivides each image into k×k patches, each optimized toward a different target label, thereby increasing diversity in object position, size, and shape. Pixel-level (Total Variation + L2) and feature-level (BN statistics alignment) regularization preserve image quality and feature distribution consistency throughout inversion.

## Results

On Pascal VOC at 1% compression, DCOD achieves 19.8% mAP and 46.4% AP50 with YOLOv3-SPP, compared to a full-dataset upper bound of 46.5% mAP and 76.4% AP50. On MS COCO at 1% compression, the method reaches 12.1% mAP and 24.7% AP50 versus a full-dataset ceiling of 36.1% mAP and 63.6% AP50. At the most aggressive 0.5% VOC compression, DCOD still attains 14.2% mAP and 37.9% AP50, roughly 2.4× the AP50 of random sampling. Cross-architecture evaluation with Faster R-CNN at 1% yields 13.8% mAP and 33.2% AP50 on VOC, confirming generalizability beyond the training architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCOD decisively outperforms all four baselines (Random, Uniform, K-Center, Herding) across every compression ratio on both Pascal VOC and MS COCO. At the headline 1% VOC setting, DCOD achieves 46.4% AP50 versus 25.5% for Random [18], 25.7% for Uniform, 21.9% for K-Center [23], and 19.3% for Herding [4]—a 20.9-point margin over the strongest baseline. The gap widens at lower compression rates: at 0.5% VOC, DCOD's 37.9% AP50 is 22.1 points above Random and 25.3 points above Herding. On MS COCO at 0.25%, DCOD reaches 17.2% AP50 while Random and Uniform plateau near 9.7–9.8%, and K-Center/Herding drop to 6.3% and 5.8% respectively. The core-set methods (K-Center, Herding) underperform even Random sampling, which the authors attribute to their reliance on whole-image features that ignore instance-level localization details critical for detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends dataset condensation (DC) from its established domain of image classification to object detection, a task the authors identify as largely unexplored in the DC literature. It departs from the dominant bi-level optimization paradigm (meta-learning and data-matching frameworks) by replacing it with a two-stage Fetch-and-Forge pipeline that first trains a detector on the full dataset and then inverts the trained model to synthesize images. The Forge stage draws on model inversion techniques (Inceptionism, Dreaming to Distill) and the SRe²L insight that key dataset information is preserved in trained network parameters, but introduces Foreground-Background Decoupling and Incremental PatchExpand to handle the multi-instance, multi-scale nature of detection annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first DC framework for object detection, this work opens a practical path to drastically reducing detector training time (e.g., 46.4% AP50 on VOC at 1% compression vs. 76.4% full-dataset), which is significant given that training detectors like Faster R-CNN on COCO can take days to weeks. However, the absolute performance gap to the full dataset remains substantial, and cross-architecture generalization degrades sharply at low compression rates, particularly for two-stage detectors. The authors acknowledge that extending the approach to transformer-based detectors (e.g., DETR) and improving cross-architecture transfer are open problems, and the method's reliance on a single V100 GPU limits immediate scalability to larger detection benchmarks.
