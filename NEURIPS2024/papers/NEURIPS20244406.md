## Problem

Current molecular diffusion models for drug discovery are tailored to specific downstream tasks (structure-based, fragment-based, or ligand-based design) and require task-specific conditioning networks or training protocols, limiting their adaptability to new or altered geometric conditions. There is no unified framework that allows a single unconditional diffusion model to be flexibly conditioned on arbitrary geometric inputs—such as protein pockets, molecular fragments, or 3D surfaces—without additional training or external guidance networks.

## Input / Output

UniGuide accepts geometric source conditions s from a general space S that need not match the model's configuration space Z: protein pocket atom configurations (for SBDD), fixed molecular fragment configurations (for FBDD), or K uniformly sampled surface points from a reference ligand's mesh (for LBDD). The output is a full 3D molecular structure with atomic coordinates and features, generated by an unconditional diffusion model whose denoising process is modified at each step by a guidance gradient. A key design constraint is that the condition map C must be invariant to rigid transformations in the source condition and equivariant in the configuration argument to preserve the model's SE(3) equivariance.

## Method

UniGuide introduces a condition map C: S × Z → Z that transforms arbitrary geometric source conditions into target conditions c in the model's configuration space, enabling self-guidance without external networks. At each denoising step, the unconditional model's noise prediction ε_θ(z_t, t) is used to estimate the clean data point ẑ_0, and the guidance signal is computed as the gradient of the squared L2 distance between ẑ_0 and C(s, ẑ_0), modifying the noise predictor as ε̂_θ = ε_θ + (√(1−ᾱ_t)·S/2)·∇_{z_t}||ẑ_0 − c||². For the special case S = Z (SBDD, FBDD), the condition map uses Kabsch alignment to rigidly register the condition subset to the predicted configuration; for LBDD, a dedicated surface condition map C_{∂V} uses ICP alignment followed by per-atom projection to the nearest surface points with a configurable margin α. Theorem 4.1 formally guarantees that equivariance of the unconditional model is preserved in the guided generation, and the entire framework requires only an unconditionally trained model plus the condition map—no additional training, conditioning encoders, or task-specific architectures.

## Results

On the LBDD task (MOSES), UniGuide(EDM) achieves the best shape-to-graph similarity ratio of 3.536, outperforming the conditional ShapeMol+g (3.074) and Virtual Screening (3.226), while also attaining the highest shape similarity (Sim_S = 0.749) and lowest graph similarity (Sim_G = 0.212). On SBDD (CrossDocked), UniGuide achieves a Vina Dock score of −7.921 with QED 0.57 and SA 0.64, surpassing DiffSBDD-cond (−6.941, QED 0.47) and TargetDiff (−7.802, QED 0.48). On linker design (ZINC), UniGuide(EDM) achieves the highest uniqueness (72.1%) and ring count (0.49) but trails DiffLinker on recovery (58.8% vs. 70.7%) and synthetic accessibility (SA 3.63 vs. 3.19).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LBDD, UniGuide(EDM) decisively outperforms all baselines on the key Ratio metric (3.536 vs. 3.074 for ShapeMol+g, 3.226 for VS, 2.834 for ShapeMol, and ≤2.851 for SQUID), while also achieving the lowest graph similarity (0.212) and highest shape similarity (0.749). On SBDD (CrossDocked), UniGuide beats DiffSBDD-cond by a large margin on Vina Dock (−7.921 vs. −6.941) and QED (0.57 vs. 0.47), and edges out TargetDiff on Vina Dock (−7.921 vs. −7.802) and QED (0.57 vs. 0.48); it also surpasses 3D-SBDD and Pocket2Mol on Vina Dock. On SBDD (Binding MOAD), UniGuide achieves Vina Dock −7.911 vs. DiffSBDD-cond's −7.482 and DiffSBDD's −7.767. On linker design, UniGuide(EDM) wins on uniqueness (72.1% vs. 51.4% for DiffLinker, 44.2% for DeLinker, 29.2% for 3DLinker) and ring count, but loses to DiffLinker on recovery (58.8% vs. 70.7%), validity (89.1% vs. 90.6%), and SA (3.63 vs. 3.19), and to DeLinker/3DLinker on recovery (58.8% vs. 80.2%/93.5%). Overall, UniGuide's strongest advantage is in LBDD and SBDD where it matches or exceeds task-specific conditional models without any additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the self-guiding diffusion framework from Kollovieh et al. [58] (originally for time series forecasting) to molecular generation, introducing the key concept of a 'condition map' that transforms arbitrary geometric conditions (surfaces, volumes, densities) into the configuration space of an unconditional diffusion model. It departs from prior conditional molecular generation work (ShapeMol, DiffSBDD, DiffLinker, TargetDiff) which all require task-specific training or dedicated conditioning networks, by showing that a single unconditionally trained model can be steered at inference time via gradient-based guidance. The base models (EDM, DiffSBDD) are used as-is without modification, and the Kabsch/ICP alignment algorithms ensure SE(3) equivariance is preserved in the guidance signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for drug discovery: a single unconditional model can be repurposed across structure-based, fragment-based, and ligand-based design without retraining, which is crucial in data-scarce biological settings. The finding that soft guidance (not fully converging to the target pocket) produces well-harmonised ligand-protein pairs challenges the assumption that hard constraints (inpainting, fixed contexts) are necessary. Limitations include exclusion of global graph properties (e.g., QED, molecular weight) from the geometric guidance framework, and the requirement that the unconditional model be trained on a matching configuration space. Open questions include whether condition maps can be composed for multi-objective guidance and whether the approach extends to tasks requiring explicit drift terms.
