## Problem

LLMs become overconfident when fine-tuned on domain-specific tasks with limited data, and existing Bayesian uncertainty estimation methods (e.g., Kronecker-factorized Laplace) are applied only post-training, bifurcating posterior approximation into two stages that inevitably yield suboptimal estimates. No prior method jointly learns both the mean and covariance of the variational weight distribution during the fine-tuning process itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242163/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multiple-choice or yes/no text questions as input and produces a predicted answer token along with an uncertainty estimate obtained by averaging N=10 stochastic forward passes through the variational weight distribution. During fine-tuning, the model is trained to maximize the likelihood of the correct answer token from the next-token logits; during inference, the N sampled outputs are aggregated to yield calibrated confidence scores (measured via ECE and NLL) alongside the point prediction (measured via ACC).

## Method

BLoB Bayesianizes LoRA asymmetrically: only the A matrix (r×n) is assigned a diagonal Gaussian variational distribution q(A|θ) = ∏N(A_ij | M_ij, Ω_ij²), while B is kept deterministic, which is equivalent to a low-rank Gaussian on the full weight matrix (Theorem 3.1). A key theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) shows that the KL divergence between this full-weight variational distribution and a matching low-rank prior can be computed exactly in the low-rank A-space, avoiding expensive (mn)×(mn) matrix operations. The method uses a quadratic parameterization σ = ρ² (rather than BBB's softplus) to ensure large initial gradients and fast convergence of the covariance, and incorporates a LoRA-specific Flipout variant that applies rank-1 random sign flips to the low-rank noise, giving each example in a mini-batch an independent weight sample at negligible extra cost. The full variational free energy (negative log-likelihood + KL) is optimized end-to-end via backpropagation with K=1 Monte Carlo sample per step, jointly updating M, G (where Ω=G²), and B throughout fine-tuning.

## Results

On in-distribution OBQA, BLoB(N=10) achieves the best ECE (3.77%) and NLL (0.50), substantially outperforming MLE (12.55% ECE, 0.73 NLL) and the strongest post-training baseline LAP (8.70% ECE, 0.52 NLL). BLoB(N=0), which uses only the mean of the weight distribution, achieves the highest accuracy on OBQA (82.73%) and ARC-Challenge (69.93%). On out-of-distribution MMLU Chemistry, BLoB(N=10) achieves the best ECE (9.77%) and NLL (1.38), while on MMLU Physics it achieves competitive ECE (18.29%) and NLL (1.46) compared to LAP (13.91%, 1.36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BLoB consistently outperforms the standard PEFT baselines (MLE/LoRA, MAP, MCD) on both ECE and NLL across all five evaluated datasets, with the largest gains on OBQA where ECE drops from 12.55% (MLE) to 3.77% (BLoB N=10). Against Deep Ensemble (ENS), BLoB wins on ECE for OBQA (3.77 vs 13.26), ARC-E (5.48 vs 6.44), and Chem (9.77 vs 12.04), while ENS retains an edge on Phy ECE (17.52 vs 18.29). Against Bayes By Backprop (BBB), BLoB achieves substantially lower ECE on OBQA (3.77 vs 11.38) and ARC-E (5.48 vs 13.41), confirming that the low-rank structure and Flipout sampling are critical improvements over naive BBB. The strongest competitor is Laplace-LoRA (LAP), a post-training method: BLoB wins on ECE for OBQA (3.77 vs 8.70), ARC-E (5.48 vs 8.51), and Chem (9.77 vs 10.76), but LAP retains advantages on ARC-C ECE (5.84 vs 9.55) and Phy ECE (13.91 vs 18.29). On NLL, BLoB(N=10) edges out LAP on OBQA (0.50 vs 0.52) and ARC-E (0.60 vs 0.70) but trails slightly on ARC-C (0.83 vs 0.81), Chem (1.38 vs 1.35), and Phy (1.46 vs 1.36). Overall, BLoB demonstrates more consistent uncertainty estimation across distributional shifts, while LAP shows stronger performance on the hardest OOD settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

BLoB sits at the intersection of three research threads: LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, Bayes By Backprop for variational Bayesian inference, and Flipout for efficient batched sampling. The key departure from prior Bayesian PEFT work (Laplace LoRA, LoRA ensembles) is moving Bayesian approximation from a post-training step into the fine-tuning loop itself, jointly optimizing the variational mean and covariance via backpropagation. The theoretical contribution (Theorems 3.1–3.2) shows that the full-weight KL divergence decomposes exactly into a low-rank computation, which justifies the parameter-efficient design. A practical but important design choice is the asymmetric Bayesianization (only matrix A, not B) and the quadratic parameterization of standard deviations, both motivated by convergence speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Overconfidence in fine-tuned LLMs is a practical deployment risk, and BLoB offers a principled, low-overhead way to calibrate uncertainty without post-hoc approximation. The method's limitations are acknowledged: it requires multiple forward passes at inference (N=10 samples), is confined to fine-tuning scenarios (not training-free), and has only been validated on classification-style tasks rather than open-ended generation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to generative tasks, scales to much larger models or longer fine-tuning schedules, and whether the low-rank variational assumption remains adequate as LoRA rank increases.
