## Problem

The CRATE Transformer-like model was derived by unrolling optimization of the Sparse Rate Reduction (SRR) objective, but it remained unverified whether its core MSSA operator actually implements the intended compression in the forward pass, and whether SRR has a causal relationship to generalization. This paper addresses both gaps: identifying a derivation artifact that causes the operator to perform decompression rather than compression, and establishing SRR's predictive power as a complexity measure.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10/100, tokenized into N=196 patches of dimension d=384, with a learnable [CLS] token prepended for classification. Beyond producing class labels, the paper computes the SRR complexity measure μ_SRR = (1/L)Σ(λ‖Z^ℓ‖₀ + R^c(Z^ℓ;U^ℓ) − R(Z^ℓ)) at each of L=12 layers, which serves both as a diagnostic of layer-wise optimization behavior and as a generalization predictor correlated with the validation-training loss gap.

## Method

The paper first identifies that CRATE-C's MSSA operator, derived by discarding the first-order term in the Taylor expansion of the coding rate R^c, actually performs gradient ascent on R^c (decompression) rather than descent, as confirmed by a toy experiment showing eigenvalues increase across layers. To correct this, two variants are proposed: CRATE-N flips the sign of the MSSA term to genuinely minimize R^c, and CRATE-T replaces the learnable output matrix W with a simple transpose of U, preserving interpretability without extra parameters. The paper then trains 64 model variants across hyperparameters (batch size, learning rate, width, dropout, model type) and shows SRR achieves the highest Kendall's rank correlation (τ=0.445) with generalization gap, outperforming path-norm, norm-based, and sharpness-based baselines. Finally, SRR is used as a per-layer regularization term (with StopGrad to isolate layer updates) added to the cross-entropy loss, consistently improving top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10/100 across all four model variants.

## Results

As a complexity measure, SRR achieves the highest overall Kendall's rank correlation coefficient (τ = 0.445) and granulated coefficient (Ψ = 0.362) with generalization gap across 64 trained models on CIFAR-10, outperforming all norm-based, path-norm, and sharpness-based baselines. In particular, SRR shows the strongest correlation along the model-type axis (τ = 0.714), indicating it captures architectural differences that other measures miss. When used as a regularization term (η = 0.001), SRR consistently improves top-1 accuracy: CRATE reaches 86.79% on CIFAR-10 and 62.52% on CIFAR-100, while CRATE-C improves from 76.87% to 77.61% on CIFAR-10 and from 43.40% to 44.53% on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRR outperforms all baseline complexity measures in overall correlation with generalization gap. Path-norm [Neyshabur et al., 2015] shows a negative correlation (τ = −0.191), contradicting its previously reported positive relationship and suggesting Path-SGD-style regularization may not transfer to Transformer-like models. Norm-based measures (spectral/Frobenius norms, ℓ₂-norm) all exhibit negative correlations (τ ranging from −0.191 to −0.372), consistent with prior findings. Among positive-correlation baselines, 1/margin (τ = 0.415) and PAC-Bayes flatness (τ = 0.333) are the strongest competitors, but SRR surpasses both in overall τ and especially in the model-type axis (0.714 vs. 0.238 and 0.455 respectively). PAC-Bayes with initialization [Neyshabur et al., 2017] is nearly uninformative (τ = 0.015), while PAC-Bayes with original parameters achieves τ = 0.333, still below SRR. The sharpness-based measure [Keskar et al., 2016] (1/σ PAC-Bayes flatness) ties with PAC-Bayes-orig at τ = 0.333 but falls short of SRR's 0.445.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct follow-up to Yu et al. (NeurIPS 2023), who derived the CRATE Transformer-like model by unrolling the Sparse Rate Reduction (SRR) objective into a Multi-head Subspace Self-Attention (MSSA) operator with skip connection and an ISTA-based sparsification step. The coding rate function and SRR formulation trace back to Yu et al. (NeurIPS 2020). The present work departs from the original by identifying a critical sign artifact in the gradient approximation that causes the MSSA operator to maximize rather than minimize the compression term, and by introducing three implementation variants (CRATE-C, CRATE-N, CRATE-T) that address conceptual and practical gaps. It also positions SRR as a complexity measure for generalization, benchmarking it against path-norm, PAC-Bayes, and sharpness-based measures in the tradition of Jiang et al. (2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that a mathematically 'principled' unrolled architecture silently inverts its own optimization objective is a cautionary finding for the algorithm-unrolling community, suggesting that Taylor-approximation artifacts can undermine the interpretability guarantees that motivate such designs. The positive correlation between SRR and generalization (outperforming path-norm and norm-based baselines) is intriguing but currently confined to the CRATE family on CIFAR-10/100, and the regularization gains are modest (≤1.1%). Key open questions include whether SRR transfers to standard Transformers with independent Q/K/V matrices, whether the ReLU non-linearity is the true driver of compression in the forward pass, and whether SRR can serve as a principled design criterion beyond small-scale classification.
