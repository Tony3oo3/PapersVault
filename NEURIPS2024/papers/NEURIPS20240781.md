## Problem

Class-agnostic object detection requires locating all objects in a scene regardless of category, but existing VLMs like Grounding DINO rely on specific text prompts and suffer severe performance degradation (up to 52% AR loss) when multiple semantically overlapping queries are concatenated into a single inference. This forces either impractical multi-pass inference or missed detections, creating a bottleneck for downstream tasks like out-of-distribution and open-vocabulary detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240781/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a set of class-agnostic bounding boxes (up to 100 or 200 per image depending on the dataset) in one inference pass. Unlike hand-crafted prompt approaches that require multiple separate VLM calls with different text queries, DiPEx uses a single set of learned soft prompts (learnable context embeddings) that are jointly fed to the frozen Grounding DINO backbone, eliminating the need for repeated forward passes.

## Method

DiPEx progressively grows a tree of learnable prompts on a unit hypersphere, starting from a single generic parent prompt self-trained on pseudo-labels from zero-shot Grounding DINO. At each expansion round, the parent prompt with the highest logit activation frequency (indicating overly broad semantics) is selected and split into K child prompts, initialized by rotating the parent embedding by random angular offsets on the hypersphere. Two dispersion losses are jointly optimized: a parent-child loss that preserves semantic consistency between parent and child embeddings, and a child-child loss that maximizes angular separation among siblings to minimize semantic overlap. Expansion terminates when the Maximum Angular Coverage (MAC) across all learned prompts converges, balancing semantic richness against computational cost. The entire procedure is self-supervised, requiring no box annotations, and the resulting prompt set enables single-pass class-agnostic detection.

## Results

On MS-COCO, DiPEx achieves AR100 of 63.2% and AP of 35.9%, surpassing the strongest prompting baseline (Grounding DINO + CoOp) by 3.1% in AR100 and 1.3% in AP, while improving AR@S by 7.7% (39.2% vs. 36.4%) for small objects. On LVIS, DiPEx reaches AR200 of 48.4% and AP of 15.2%, outperforming SAM by 13.3% in AR200 and 21.3% in AP, and exceeding CoOp by 20.1% in AR200. For downstream OOD-OD on MS-COCO, DiPEx achieves 59.9% AR100 on unknown classes, a 38.3% improvement over zero-shot Grounding DINO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiPEx outperforms all 15 baselines across both MS-COCO and LVIS. Against the closest prompting baselines, DiPEx beats Grounding DINO + CoOp by 3.1% in AR100 (63.2 vs. 61.3) and 7.7% in AR@S on MS-COCO, and by 20.1% in AR200 (48.4 vs. 40.3) on LVIS. The gap over CoCoOp is larger: +5.1% AR100 and +11.3% AP on MS-COCO. On LVIS, DiPEx surpasses SAM (trained on 1B masks) by 13.3% in AR200 and 21.3% in AP (15.2 vs. 6.1), despite requiring only 4 epochs of self-training. For OOD-OD, DiPEx achieves 59.9% AR100 on unknown classes versus 43.3% for zero-shot Grounding DINO and 34.5% for CutLER, while matching the known-class AP (46.3) of the zero-shot baseline. Non-parametric and self-training baselines (Selective Search, MOST, FreeSOLO, DETReg) remain far behind, with AR100 below 33%, confirming that VLM-based prompting is essential for competitive class-agnostic detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt tuning paradigm (CoOp, CoCoOp) from supervised image classification to class-agnostic object detection, using Grounding DINO as the frozen VLM backbone. It departs from prior prompt tuning work by eliminating the need for full supervision, instead using pseudo-labels from zero-shot inference and a progressive self-training loop. The key conceptual departure is the dispersing mechanism: rather than learning a fixed set of prompts, DiPEx iteratively splits high-uncertainty parent prompts into child prompts on a hypersphere, using dispersion losses to enforce angular separation and a maximum angular coverage (MAC) criterion for termination. The pilot study establishing that semantic overlap in concatenated text queries causes up to 52% AR reduction provides the empirical motivation for the non-overlapping prompt design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Class-agnostic object detection is a foundational bottleneck for OOD detection, open-world detection, and open-vocabulary detection, yet prior methods (bottom-up proposals, self-supervised ViT-based discovery) plateau around 30% AR. DiPEx's single-pass inference with non-overlapping prompts offers a practical efficiency advantage over prediction-merging strategies that require multiple forward passes. However, the approach still requires self-training on the full dataset per expansion round, and hyperparameters (temperature coefficients, prompt count K, rotation angle θ) need manual tuning. Open questions include whether hierarchical prompts can be learned in one shot rather than iteratively, and whether the approach generalizes beyond Grounding DINO to other VLM architectures or to broader downstream tasks like open-vocabulary detection.
