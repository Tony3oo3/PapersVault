## Problem

Speech foundation models like Whisper suffer significant performance degradation under domain shift (noise, accents, specific scenarios), yet adapting them typically requires either source-domain data or expensive ground-truth labels. In the source-free unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) setting, the critical unresolved question is how to reliably assess pseudo-label quality without ground truth, since standard confidence scores from softmax are known to be over-confident and poorly correlated with actual token correctness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240930/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only a pre-trained speech foundation model (e.g., Whisper-Large-V3, Canary-1B, SeamlessM4T) and a small amount of unlabeled target-domain speech (as little as 200–500 utterances, under one hour of audio). No source-domain data or ground-truth transcriptions are needed. The output is a domain-adapted ASR model that produces text transcriptions, with the adaptation performed via two epochs of informed finetuning using a re-weighted cross-entropy loss. The approach is also applicable to speech translation (sequence-to-sequence) tasks.

## Method

STAR introduces a novel token-level quality indicator that integrates two complementary signals from autoregressive decoding: the confidence score C_l (stable but unreliable due to softmax over-confidence) and the attentive score A_l (reliable, derived from the self-attention matrix's aggregate pattern, but numerically unstable due to varying semantic roles of tokens). The STAR score S_l uses sigmoid-gated conflict/consistency logic: when C_l and A_l disagree, the more reliable A_l dominates; when they agree, C_l's stability is used to scale A_l via a focal-loss-inspired exponential term. At the utterance level, low-quality pseudo-labels are filtered by perturbing model weights with Gaussian noise K times and measuring edit-distance variance. The final informed finetuning re-weights each token's cross-entropy loss by its STAR score, guiding the model to learn preferentially from high-quality pseudo tokens while suppressing unreliable ones.

## Results

STAR achieves an average of 13.5% relative WER reduction across 14 target domains (noise, accent, and specific scenarios) using only unlabeled data. The largest gains appear on challenging domains: LS-FreeSound babble noise (40.2%→30.2% WER, −24.9%), CommonVoice Singaporean accent (6.5%→5.1%, −21.5%), and TED-LIUM 3 talks (5.2%→4.1%, −21.2%). On several domains STAR approaches the supervised upper bound (e.g., LS-FreeSound car: 2.5% vs. 2.4% real-label WER; CHiME-4 dev-real: 3.9% vs. 3.2%). The method requires less than one hour of unlabeled speech (200–500 utterances) and ~0.8 hours of training on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR consistently outperforms the frozen Whisper zero-shot baseline across all 14 domains, with relative WER reductions ranging from 4.9% (RATS radio) to 24.9% (LS-FreeSound babble). Vanilla self-training (pseudo-labeling + finetuning without quality assessment) often fails to improve or even degrades performance (e.g., CHiME-4 test-real: 6.9% vs. 6.8% frozen; RATS: 47.2% vs. 46.9% frozen), confirming that unfiltered pseudo labels are harmful. STAR also surpasses the intermediate ablations—utterance-level filtering alone and individual token re-weighting with confidence (C_l) or attentive score (A_l) alone—demonstrating that the integrated STAR indicator is necessary for optimal performance. On the generality front, STAR transfers effectively to OWSM-V3.1 (−10.7%), Canary-1.0B (−12.2%), and Parakeet-TDT-1.1B (−12.5%) on CHiME-4, and to speech translation on FLEURS (average +1.2 BLEU, up to +2.2 for De→En), confirming it is not Whisper-specific. On several domains (LS-FreeSound car, CHiME-4 dev-real, CommonVoice African), STAR nearly matches the supervised real-label upper bound, indicating that the quality-aware re-weighting recovers most of the information that ground-truth labels would provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR builds on the self-training paradigm (Zhang et al., ICLR 2022) for source-free UDA in ASR, where a pre-trained model generates its own pseudo labels on unlabeled target data. It departs from prior UDA work that relies on source-domain data or semi-supervised signals by operating entirely without source data, and from traditional confidence estimation (lattices, confusion networks in HMM-based ASR) by exploiting the self-attention matrix available during autoregressive decoding in Transformer-based models. The key methodological departure is the STAR indicator, which integrates the reliability of attentive scores with the numerical stability of confidence scores via a sigmoid-gated combination, replacing the unreliable softmax-based confidence alone that prior self-training approaches in ASR have used for pseudo-label selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Source-free UDA is practically critical because the training data behind large speech foundation models (Whisper, Canary, SeamlessM4T) is often proprietary or too large to retain, making re-training with source data infeasible. STAR's <1-hour data requirement and demonstrated generality across five foundation models and both ASR and speech translation tasks make it a deployable technique for rapid domain-specific adaptation in production voice assistants. The counterintuitive finding that informed finetuning prevents catastrophic forgetting without source data opens questions about the role of self-generated pseudo labels in preserving multi-domain knowledge, though the authors acknowledge the mechanism remains speculative. Limitations include dependence on large foundation models with sufficient zero-shot robustness to serve as reliable pseudo-labelers, and reduced effectiveness in extremely challenging or colloquial domains where pseudo-label quality is inherently poor.
