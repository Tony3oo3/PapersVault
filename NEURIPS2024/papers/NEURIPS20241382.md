## Problem

Physics-based human motion capture methods that re-simulate kinematics via PD controllers produce non-optimal motions because rigid-body models are imperfect approximations and monocular kinematics estimates are noisy. Prior work patches this gap with non-physical offset terms in the PD controller, sacrificing interpretability. The paper addresses the lack of a principled, online mechanism to selectively trust the kinematics observations versus the physics simulation at each time step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241382/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video processed by the pretrained TRACE estimator into 3D joint-angle sequences (downsampled to 50 Hz, split into 100-frame subsequences) as the kinematics input stream, alongside a rigid-body proxy character built from SMPL metadata (spheres and cylinders). It outputs a per-frame optimal state vector (global translation, quaternion rotation, and joint angles in a world coordinate frame), internal joint torques, external ground-reaction forces at two feet, binary contact probabilities, and a learned inertia-bias matrix that corrects the proxy's mass distribution. The system operates recursively online, requiring no future frames.

## Method

OSDCap introduces a learnable Kalman filter (inspired by KalmanNet) as the core fusion mechanism: a GRU-based module predicts a time-varying Kalman gain matrix that adaptively weights the noisy TRACE kinematics against the physics-simulated pose, producing an optimal state that retains accurate global translation while enforcing physical plausibility. A separate set of fully-connected heads in OSDNet predicts meta-PD gains, external contact forces (via learned Jacobians and contact probabilities), and a symmetric positive-definite inertia-bias matrix added to the RBDL-computed inertia tensor to close the proxy-to-real-body gap. The physics simulation stage then uses the filtered optimal pose as the PD target to compute joint torques, and forward dynamics (Newton-Euler via RBDL) updates the velocity state, closing a recursive loop. Training is end-to-end supervised with L1 pose/keypoint losses on both predicted and optimal states, a binary cross-entropy contact loss, and regularization terms for acceleration smoothness, velocity matching, and foot friction, all weighted to balance accuracy against physical plausibility.

## Results

On Human3.6M, OSDCap achieves 54.8 mm MPJPE, 95.5% PCK, and 197.7 mm CPS, setting new state-of-the-art among online physics-based methods. It also leads on global metrics with 132.8 mm MPJPE-G and 119.1 mm GRP, substantially outperforming the kinematics baseline TRACE (152.7 mm MPJPE-G, 125.9 mm GRP). On the out-of-domain SportsPose dataset, OSDCap reduces MPJPE-G from 361.9 mm (TRACE) to 113.6 mm and GRP from 333.0 mm to 90.2 mm, demonstrating strong generalizability. The only metric where OSDCap trails a competitor is Accel (8.4 vs. 6.7 mm/s² for SimPoE), which benefits from a high-frequency physics engine constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSDCap outperforms all online physics-based baselines on MPJPE (54.8 vs. 56.7 for SimPoE, 60.7 for IPMAN-R, 68.1 for Xie et al.) and achieves the best PCK (95.5%) and CPS (197.7 mm) among all compared methods. Against the kinematics baseline TRACE, OSDCap reduces MPJPE by 23.3 mm (78.1→54.8), MPJPE-G by 19.9 mm (152.7→132.8), and GRP by 6.8 mm (125.9→119.1), while improving PCK from 88.3% to 95.5%. The offline method DnD achieves a lower MPJPE-PA (35.5 vs. 39.8 mm), but it requires encoding the full action sequence and uses AMASS as additional training data, making it unsuitable for online settings. SimPoE achieves the best smoothness (Accel 6.7 vs. 8.4 mm/s²) due to its high-frequency physics engine, but OSDCap surpasses it on all accuracy metrics. On the out-of-domain SportsPose dataset, OSDCap dramatically improves global translation (MPJPE-G: 361.9→113.6 mm; GRP: 333.0→90.2 mm) over TRACE, confirming the Kalman filtering's robustness to noisy kinematics inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSDCap builds on the meta-PD controller framework from Shimada et al. (NeurPhys, 2021) for torque estimation and the neural Kalman filtering architecture from KalmanNet (Revach et al., 2022) for state estimation. The key departure is that prior physics-based approaches (PhysCap, NeurPhys, DnD) compensate for model imperfections by adding non-physical offset terms to the PD controller, whereas OSDCap replaces this ad-hoc correction with a principled learnable Kalman filter that adaptively weights kinematics observations against physics simulation. Additionally, OSDCap introduces a learnable inertia-bias matrix to address the gap between the proxy character and real human morphology, and operates in a fully online recursive loop rather than requiring full-sequence encoding as in DnD or PhysPT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that physics-based motion capture cannot succeed by relying solely on simulation: the ablation showing PD-only performance (87.7 MPJPE) is actually worse than the raw kinematics baseline (78.4) underscores that the filtering step is essential, not optional. This provides a principled framework for integrating imperfect physics models with noisy visual observations, which is broadly applicable beyond human motion. Limitations include the inability to match force-plate-level accuracy for external forces, the lack of detailed hand/foot modeling, and the reliance on a fixed proxy character shape. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-person scenes, dynamic camera settings, or more complex contact interactions.
