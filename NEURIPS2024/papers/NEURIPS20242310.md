## Problem

Existing operator learning methods for PDEs are not sample efficient, requiring thousands of task-specific training trajectories to achieve acceptable accuracy, which is prohibitive when data must be generated via expensive numerical simulations. The fundamental open question is whether pretraining on a small set of PDEs can yield effective representations that generalize to unseen, a priori unrelated PDEs via finetuning on a few examples — the feasibility of PDE foundation models has not been demonstrated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242310/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a spatial field (initial condition, PDE coefficients, or source term) on the 2D unit square at 128×128 resolution, partitioned into 4×4 patches and linearly embedded into a C-dimensional latent space, together with a scalar lead time t ∈ ℝ₊. It produces the PDE solution field at time t (same spatial resolution and channel count as input), supporting continuous-in-time evaluation at any t via the time-conditioned layer norm. For time-independent PDEs, the solution is recovered as the long-time limit with normalized lead time t=1.

## Method

POSEIDON is built on scOT, a hierarchical multiscale vision transformer with SwinV2 shifted-window attention arranged in a U-Net encoder-decoder with ConvNeXt skip connections, adapted for operator learning. Two key innovations distinguish it: (1) a FiLM-style time-conditioned layer norm (α(t)·normalize(v)+β(t) with linear t-dependence) that enables continuous-in-time evaluation without discretizing the time axis, and (2) an all2all training strategy that exploits the semi-group property S(t*,a)=S(t*−t, S(t,a)) to generate O(K²) input-output pairs per trajectory instead of O(K), yielding ~5.11M training examples from 77,840 fluid-dynamics trajectories. Pretraining covers 6 operators (4 compressible Euler, 2 incompressible Navier-Stokes) with diverse initial conditions (Riemann problems, Kelvin-Helmholtz, Gaussians, sines), and finetuning reinitializes only the embedding/recovery parameters while transferring the bulk of pretrained weights, with higher learning rates on the reinitialized and time-embedding parameters.

## Results

On 15 out-of-distribution downstream PDE tasks, POSEIDON-L (629M params) achieves a median Efficiency Gain (EG) of ~50× over FNO, meaning it needs only ~20 task-specific samples on average to match FNO's error at 1024 samples. In 4 tasks, just 3 samples suffice, and in 13 of 15 tasks POSEIDON-L requires an order of magnitude fewer samples than FNO. Accuracy Gains (AG) at matched sample counts range from 10% to a factor of 25, with a mean gain of an entire order of magnitude. The best single-task EG is 890.6× (NS-PwC) and the best AG is 29.3× (NS-BB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FNO (the standard trained-from-scratch neural operator), POSEIDON-L wins on all 15 tasks with a median EG of ~50× and a mean AG of an order of magnitude. Against CNO and scOT (both trained from scratch), POSEIDON-L achieves a median sample-efficiency gain of an order of magnitude and an average accuracy gain of a factor of 4, while CNO and scOT are themselves comparable to each other. Against CNO-FM (a convolutional neural operator pretrained on the identical dataset), POSEIDON-L wins 14 of 15 tasks; CNO-FM requires ~100 task-specific samples to match FNO@1024 versus only ~20 for POSEIDON, attributing the gap to the multiscale SwinV2 transformer architecture versus multiscale CNNs. Against MPP-B (a transformer pretrained on PDEBench with next-step prediction), POSEIDON-L dominates: MPP-B modestly beats FNO on only 8/15 tasks and fails to converge or diverges on the remaining 7. Against DPOT (auto-regressive denoising operator transformer), POSEIDON models are significantly better in both accuracy and sample efficiency, though DPOT was trained on more diverse datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

POSEIDON adapts the SwinV2 hierarchical vision transformer (Liu et al., 2022) for operator learning, introducing a time-conditioned layer norm inspired by FiLM (Perez et al., 2017) to enable continuous-in-time evaluation. It departs from prior PDE foundation models (MPP, DPOT, ICON) in two key ways: it formulates the task as full operator learning (generating the entire trajectory from initial data) rather than next-time-step prediction given a context window, and it uses a multiscale shifted-window attention architecture rather than FNO or CNN backbones. The all2all training strategy, which exploits the semi-group property of time-dependent PDEs to quadratically increase effective training pairs per trajectory, is a novel contribution not present in prior operator learning or foundation model work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first clear demonstration that pretraining on a small, diverse set of fluid-dynamics PDEs enables a model to generalize to completely unrelated PDEs (elliptic Poisson/Helmholtz, Allen-Cahn, wave equation) with only a handful of task-specific samples, providing an affirmative answer to the open question of whether PDE foundation models are feasible. The median 20-sample requirement to match FNO's 1024-sample accuracy represents an order-of-magnitude practical gain for expensive simulation-based workflows. Key limitations include the pretraining set covering only 6 time-dependent operators on the unit square with periodic boundary conditions; the authors note that adding time-independent (especially elliptic) PDEs, non-Cartesian geometries, and broader time-scale ranges to pretraining would substantially improve generality. The open-sourced PDEGYM dataset and models lower the barrier for the community to extend this line of work.
