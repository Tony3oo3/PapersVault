## Problem

Most representation learning methods treat class labels as permutation-invariant, ignoring the natural hierarchical structure present in real-world datasets. The prior structured regularization approach (Zeng et al., ICLR 2022) embeds this hierarchy using Euclidean ℓ₂ distance, but it is provably impossible to embed an arbitrary tree metric into Euclidean space without distortion, regardless of dimension. This paper asks whether hyperbolic geometry, which is the continuous analog of a tree, can resolve this fundamental limitation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) together with a weighted label hierarchy tree specifying parent-child relationships among classes. The hierarchy is either provided with the dataset (CIFAR-100's three-level structure), manually constructed (CIFAR-10), or derived from WordNet (ImageNet-100). Outputs include hierarchy-informed feature vectors mapped into a Poincaré ball (curvature c=1.0), fine- and coarse-grained class predictions via kNN or linear probes, and Mahalanobis-based OOD detection scores for binary ID/OOD classification.

## Method

HypStructure is a plug-in regularizer with two components added to any standard task loss (e.g., SupCon or CE). The first, HypCPCC, extends the Cophenetic Correlation Coefficient to hyperbolic space: Euclidean features are projected into the Poincaré ball via the exponential map, class prototypes are computed using the Einstein midpoint (a weighted average in the Klein model), and pairwise Poincaré distances between prototypes replace Euclidean centroid distances in the CPCC formula. The second, HypCenter, minimizes the norm of the root-node prototype to anchor the hierarchy's root at the Poincaré disk origin, mirroring Sarkar's low-distortion tree embedding. The composite objective is L = ℓ_Flat − α·HypCPCC + β·ℓ_center, where α and β balance the structured and centering terms. Because hyperbolic space allows exponential growth of volume with radius, the method achieves near-zero tree-metric distortion even at very low feature dimensions, outperforming 512-dimensional Euclidean baselines. The paper further provides an eigenspectrum theorem showing that CPCC-optimized features exhibit a phase-transition gap in eigenvalues at each hierarchy level, which formally explains the improved OOD detection via the Mahalanobis score.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, HypStructure achieves 94.79% fine-class and 99.18% coarse-class kNN accuracy while reducing hierarchy distortion (δ_rel) to 0.094, a 59.4% reduction over Flat (0.232) and 45.4% over ℓ₂-CPCC (0.174). On CIFAR-100, HypStructure reaches 76.68% fine and 86.01% coarse accuracy, outperforming Flat by 1.72% and ℓ₂-CPCC by 0.61% on fine-class accuracy. For OOD detection with CIFAR-100 as ID, HypStructure achieves 88.81% average AUROC across 5 OOD datasets, surpassing the best prior method CIDER (87.67%) by 1.14% and achieving a Borda Count of 82.85 versus 60.00 for CIDER.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct predecessor ℓ₂-CPCC [Zeng et al., ICLR 2022], HypStructure consistently reduces hierarchy distortion by up to 45.4% (CIFAR-10: 0.094 vs 0.174) and improves fine-class accuracy by up to 0.8% (CIFAR-100: 76.68% vs 76.07%), while also achieving higher test CPCC on CIFAR-10 (0.992 vs 0.966) and ImageNet-100 (0.841 vs 0.834). Compared to the Flat (SupCon) baseline, HypStructure delivers up to 2.2% fine-class accuracy gains (CIFAR-100: 76.68% vs 74.96%) and up to 59.4% distortion reduction. For OOD detection, HypStructure outperforms all seven baselines including CIDER [Ming et al., ICLR 2022] (88.81% vs 87.67% avg AUROC), SSD+ [Sehwag et al., ICLR 2021] (88.81% vs 85.90%), KNN+ [Sun et al., ICML 2022] (88.81% vs 86.14%), CSI [Tack et al., NeurIPS 2020] (88.81% vs 84.83%), CE+SimCLR [Winkens et al., 2020] (88.81% vs 84.15%), and ProxyAnchor [Kim et al., CVPR 2020] (88.81% vs 84.78%). The Borda Count ranking further confirms HypStructure's dominance, scoring 82.85 versus the next-best CIDER at 60.00. On CIFAR-10 OOD detection, HypStructure achieves 97.75% AUROC, narrowly beating SSD+ (97.38%) and KNN+ (97.22%), while ℓ₂-CPCC lags significantly at 76.67%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the structured representation learning framework of Zeng et al. (ICLR 2022), which embedded label hierarchies into Euclidean feature space via an ℓ₂-based Cophenetic Correlation Coefficient (CPCC) regularizer. The key conceptual departure is replacing the Euclidean distance with Poincaré ball distance in the CPCC computation, motivated by the impossibility of distortion-free tree metric embedding in any finite-dimensional Euclidean space. While prior hyperbolic geometry work (Nickel & Kiela, Ganea et al., Khrulkov et al.) has used hyperbolic spaces to implicitly capture hierarchical structure, this paper is the first to use hyperbolic geometry for *explicit* structured regularization via a CPCC-style objective, and further adds a centering loss inspired by Sarkar's low-distortion tree embedding construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The eigenspectrum analysis (Theorem 5.1) provides a formal explanation for why hierarchy-informed representations improve OOD detection: CPCC-style regularization induces a phase transition in the eigenvalue spectrum at the number of coarse classes, making coarse-level features the most informative for separating ID from OOD samples. This reframes structured regularization as an implicit dimensionality reduction technique, offering a principled lens for understanding representation geometry beyond this specific method. Key limitations include the dependence on an externally provided (or constructed) label hierarchy, the unexplored impact of noisy hierarchies, and the use of only the Poincaré ball model. Open questions include whether the eigenspectrum properties hold for arbitrary (unbalanced) trees in practice, whether other hyperbolic models (e.g., Lorentz) offer better trade-offs, and establishing formal error bounds for CPCC-style objectives.
