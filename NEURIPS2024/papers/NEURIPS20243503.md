## Problem

Existing ObjectNav methods, whether end-to-end or modular, perform single-step planning at each timestep, outputting only the next action or waypoint based on the current observation. This myopic approach lacks temporal consistency and interpretability, and modular methods further suffer from overfitting to training-room layouts due to location-related supervision. The paper addresses this gap by learning a sequence planner that generates a temporally coherent future trajectory conditioned on the agent's accumulated semantic map and target.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243503/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an incrementally built semantic map m_t ∈ R^{(4+n)×h×w} (resized to 224×224) that encodes obstacle occupancy, explored regions, agent position history, and n semantic category channels, together with a target object category encoded via linear projection. The output is a sequence of k=32 future 2D waypoints (each a 2D coordinate), from which the k_g-th (28th) waypoint is selected as a sub-goal; a local FMM-based policy then converts this sub-goal into discrete collision-free actions (move_forward, turn_left, turn_right, stop). The trajectory is regenerated every t_{T-diff}=5 navigation steps.

## Method

The core contribution is a trajectory diffusion (T-Diff) model that learns the conditional distribution p(ξ_t | m_t, o) using DDPM with a DiT-style Transformer backbone (8 blocks). Training data pairs (semantic map, target, trajectory segment) are automatically collected by driving a semantic-mapping agent along Fast Marching Method optimal paths in training rooms, then segmenting the resulting trajectories into sub-trajectories of length k. The noised trajectory latent is encoded via linear projection with positional embeddings and processed through Transformer blocks, with the semantic map (encoded by a ResNet-18) and target object injected as conditioning through multi-head cross-attention alongside the diffusion timestep. During inference, the model iteratively denoises from Gaussian noise over τ_max=100 steps to produce the full trajectory sequence. The key insight is that sequence planning provides temporal consistency and contextual smoothing across waypoints, while the diffusion framework handles the high-dimensional, sparse conditional distribution more effectively than direct supervised regression (which the paper shows yields substantially worse MSE and navigation performance).

## Results

On Gibson (val), T-Diff achieves 79.6% SR, 44.9% SPL, and 1.00m DTS, outperforming the previous best modular method L3MVN [57] (76.9% SR, 38.8% SPL, 1.01m DTS) by 2.7% SR and 6.1% SPL. On MP3D (val), T-Diff achieves 39.6% SR, 15.2% SPL, and 5.16m DTS, surpassing OVG-Nav [56] (35.8% SR, 12.3% SPL, 5.69m DTS) by 3.8% SR and 2.9% SPL. The method also demonstrates cross-simulator scalability: when trained on MP3D and tested on Gibson, T-Diff retains 78.2% SR (vs. 79.6% same-simulator), while PONI drops from 73.6% to 43.9% under the same transfer setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Gibson, T-Diff achieves the best SR (79.6%) and SPL (44.9%) among all compared methods, beating the strongest modular baselines L3MVN [57] by 2.7% SR and 6.1% SPL, and SGM [64] by 1.6% SR and 0.9% SPL, while also achieving the lowest DTS (1.00m). On MP3D, T-Diff leads in SR (39.6%) and SPL (15.2%), outperforming OVG-Nav [56] by 3.8% SR and 2.9% SPL, and SGM [64] by 1.9% SR and 0.5% SPL. However, T-Diff does not achieve the best DTS on MP3D: 3D-aware [60] (4.78m) and SGM [64] (4.93m) both report lower DTS than T-Diff (5.16m), suggesting that while T-Diff finds the target more often and follows more efficient paths, it occasionally ends episodes slightly farther from the goal. Against end-to-end methods (DD-PPO, EmbCLIP, Habitat-Web, etc.), T-Diff shows large margins on both datasets, e.g., 11.5% SR over EmbCLIP on Gibson and 10.4% SR over Habitat-Web on MP3D. The cross-simulator experiment (Table 4) further highlights T-Diff's advantage: when trained on MP3D and tested on Gibson, T-Diff retains 78.2% SR versus PONI's 43.9%, indicating that the diffusion-based sequence planner generalizes better across scene layouts than location-supervised modular planners.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the DiT Transformer-based diffusion architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023), repurposing them from image generation to trajectory sequence planning for embodied navigation. It departs from all prior ObjectNav methods—both end-to-end (RL/IL-based single-step policies) and modular (waypoint predictors on semantic maps)—by formulating navigation as a conditional trajectory distribution learning problem rather than a single-step prediction. The semantic map module is inherited from modular methods like SemExp and PONI, but the planner is fundamentally different: instead of regressing a single sub-goal, it iteratively denoises an entire future trajectory sequence conditioned on the map and target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion-based sequence planning can outperform single-step planning in ObjectNav while maintaining geometric memory, and notably shows better cross-simulator generalization (MP3D→Gibson) than location-supervised modular methods like PONI. However, the training data consists of relatively simple optimal paths (straight, short segments from FMM), and the authors acknowledge that complex multi-turn trajectories are underrepresented. Open questions include whether richer conditioning (egocentric views, object-relationship graphs) or more diverse trajectory data would further improve performance, and whether the diffusion-based trajectory planner generalizes beyond indoor ObjectNav to other embodied navigation tasks.
