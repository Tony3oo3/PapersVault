## Problem

While latent-space interpolation in generative models is well-studied, conditional interpolation between distinct text prompts in text-to-image diffusion models remains under-explored. The straightforward approach of linearly interpolating text embeddings produces sequences with poor thematic consistency, non-smooth transitions, and degraded image fidelity, particularly when the two conditions are semantically distant. This paper formalizes conditional interpolation and identifies three ideal properties—consistency, smoothness, and fidelity—that existing methods fail to satisfy simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes two text prompts (or two reference images via IP-Adapter) and two corresponding latent seeds as input, and produces a discrete sequence of m interpolated images (typically m=7) at 512×512 resolution. An optional third guidance prompt can be supplied to steer the interpolation path toward a specific compositional concept. The interpolation coefficients are not uniformly spaced but are dynamically selected via a Beta-distribution-based exploration-and-search procedure, and the entire pipeline operates training-free within 25 denoising steps using DDIM or DPM-Solver schedulers.

## Method

The key insight is that text embedding interpolation is mathematically equivalent to interpolating only the cross-attention keys and values, while self-attention imposes far stronger spatial-layout constraints—so interpolating cross-attention alone yields inconsistent spatial transitions. AID addresses this by applying a fused interpolated attention mechanism to both cross-attention and self-attention layers: for self-attention, the source keys/values from the interpolated latent are concatenated with the linearly interpolated endpoint keys/values (fused inner or outer interpolation), preserving fidelity while enforcing spatial consistency. A Beta-distribution prior with dynamically fitted α and β parameters replaces uniform coefficient selection, concentrating samples where perceptual transitions are abrupt to improve smoothness. The PAID variant further injects a user-specified guidance prompt's keys and values into the fused cross-attention, treating interpolation as a condition-dependent generative process that enables compositional generation, controlled editing, and image morphing without any fine-tuning.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, AID-O achieves the best consistency (0.2905 LPIPS, −20.3% vs. TEI) and fidelity (51.43 FID, −56.4% vs. TEI), while AID-I achieves the best smoothness (0.8152 Gini-based score). On LAION-Aesthetics, AID-O similarly leads in consistency (0.2944, −23.9% vs. TEI) and fidelity (82.01 FID, −42.4% vs. TEI), and AID-I leads in smoothness (0.8152). In human evaluation (320 trials), AID-I and AID-O are jointly preferred over TEI in 90–100% of cases across all four prompt categories (near objects, far objects, scenes, object+scene).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Text Embedding Interpolation (TEI, Wang & Golland [49]), AID-O reduces consistency by 20.3% (CIFAR-10) and 23.9% (LAION-Aesthetics) and fidelity by 56.4% and 42.4% respectively, while AID-I improves smoothness by ~4–10%. Against Denoising Interpolation (DI), AID-O achieves substantially better consistency (0.2905 vs. 0.4295 on CIFAR-10) and fidelity (51.43 vs. 87.13), though DI retains an edge over AID-I in fidelity. For image editing control, combining AID with P2P [11] boosts smoothness from 0.3741 to 0.8921 (+138%), and with EDICT [48] from 0.5978 to 0.8486 (+42%). For compositional generation, PAID outperforms both CEBM [25] and RRR [7] in CLIP score on both SD 1.4 and SDXL. For image morphing, AID+IP-Adapter matches or exceeds IMPUS [53] in visual quality while being ~100× faster (no LoRA fine-tuning required), and provides finer control over image-prompt scale than IP-Adapter [54] alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on interpolation in generative models (GANs, VAEs, diffusion) from single-condition latent-space interpolation to multi-condition text-to-image interpolation. It builds on attention manipulation techniques in diffusion models (Prompt-to-Prompt, EDICT, IP-Adapter) but departs from the standard approach of interpolating text embeddings by operating directly on cross-attention and self-attention key-value pairs. The key conceptual departure is the diagnosis that self-attention imposes stronger spatial layout constraints than cross-attention, motivating a fused attention interpolation mechanism that prior text-embedding-based methods (Wang & Golland, IMPUS, DiffMorpher) do not exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of AID/PAID makes it immediately applicable to any existing text-to-image diffusion model without fine-tuning, which is practically significant given the computational cost of LoRA-based alternatives like IMPUS (~1 hour per sample). The method's demonstrated utility across compositional generation, image editing control, and image morphing suggests it could become a standard component in diffusion-based pipelines. Limitations include dependence on base model quality and the fact that the Beta-distribution selection adds inference overhead (exploration + search). Open questions include whether the approach extends naturally to video generation or more than two conditions simultaneously.
