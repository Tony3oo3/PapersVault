## Problem

Existing deep fluid prediction models operate exclusively from the Eulerian perspective, modeling spatiotemporal correlations among massive static grids, which obscures and confounds the complex moving dynamics of fluids. This single-perspective approach leads to both computational inefficiency and difficulty in capturing intricate flow phenomena like vortices and turbulence. No prior method seamlessly combines Eulerian and Lagrangian descriptions within a unified deep learning framework for fluid prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243902/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes P=10 past Eulerian fluid field observations (each a spatial grid of physical quantities at resolutions ranging from 128×128 to 32³) concatenated with a 0-1 boundary geometry mask, and produces the next timestep's Eulerian fluid field in an autoregressive loop. Additionally, it outputs learned Lagrangian particle trajectories and dynamics at 4 hierarchical spatial scales (tracking 512, 128, 32, and 8 particles respectively), which provide interpretable evidence of fluid motion. The autoregressive paradigm means each predicted frame becomes part of the input for the next step, enabling multi-step rollouts up to 100 frames.

## Method

DeepLag is an Eulerian-Lagrangian Recurrent Network whose core module, the EuLag Block, performs bidirectional cross-attention between dense Eulerian grid features and sparse Lagrangian particle states at each of 4 spatial scales. In the LagToEu direction, the Eulerian field serves as queries while concatenated particle dynamics and positions (h_t‖p_t) serve as keys/values, injecting tracked Lagrangian motion information into the Eulerian feature update. In the EuToLag direction, particle states query the evolved Eulerian features to predict new particle displacements and global dynamics, which are then combined with locally interpolated Eulerian features via bilinear interpolation. Particles are initialized by a learnable importance-sampling module (ConvNet + Softmax over spatial dynamics) that adaptively selects regions of high dynamical complexity, and particles exiting the domain are resampled. The design is inspired by the material derivative decomposition (domain + convective terms), requiring only ~3% of Eulerian grid points as tracked particles while achieving state-of-the-art performance across 2D and 3D benchmarks.

## Results

DeepLag achieves state-of-the-art relative L2 across all three benchmarks: 0.0543 on Bounded Navier-Stokes (10-frame), 0.0168 on Ocean Current (10-day), and 0.0378 on 3D Smoke, outperforming the second-best model by 13.8%, 8.3%, and 34.4% respectively. On long-term rollouts, DeepLag maintains its lead with 0.0993 (30-frame Bounded NS, +2.7%), 0.0257 (30-day Ocean Current, +12.8%), and 0.1493 (100-frame Bounded NS, +1.2%). The model uses only ~19M parameters on 3D Smoke, substantially fewer than U-Net (52M) or FNO (57M), while achieving the best accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepLag outperforms all seven baselines on every benchmark. On Bounded Navier-Stokes, the closest competitor is U-Net (0.0618 vs. 0.0543 at 10 frames), with LSM second-best on 30-frame rollout (0.1020 vs. 0.0993); Vortex and Galerkin Transformer degrade to NaN or near-NaN on long rollouts. On Ocean Current, LSM is the strongest baseline (0.0182 at 10 days), but DeepLag extends the gap to 12.8% at 30 days (0.0257 vs. 0.0290), while GNOT and FactFormer lead in ACC but still trail DeepLag. On 3D Smoke, the advantage is most dramatic: DeepLag (0.0378) beats LSM (0.0527) by 34.4%, while Transformer-based methods GNOT (0.2100) and Galerkin Transformer (0.1066) fail catastrophically, likely due to their linear-attention mechanisms degrading under massive 3D token counts. U-Net, despite its multiscale inductive bias, degrades on 3D Smoke (0.0508) and high-resolution 2D (0.060 at 256×256), confirming that a pure Eulerian framework is insufficient for complex dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Eulerian-only paradigm in neural fluid prediction (FNO, Galerkin Transformer, GNOT, LSM, FactFormer) by introducing a Lagrangian particle-tracking component into the deep model. It repurposes the classical material derivative connection (Eq. 1–2) as a design principle, using cross-attention to bidirectionally couple sparse Lagrangian particle dynamics with dense Eulerian grid features at multiple scales. Unlike traditional particle-based solvers that require per-particle PDE integration, DeepLag learns particle trajectories data-drivenly from Eulerian observations, and unlike prior Lagrangian deep-learning methods (SPNets, Lagrangian fluid simulation with continuous convolutions) that operate solely in particle space, it maintains a full Eulerian field as the primary prediction target. The EuLag Block is thus a novel architectural module that has no direct predecessor in the neural-operator or PINN literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Eulerian-Lagrangian co-design offers a practical path to more interpretable and efficient fluid surrogates: only ~3% of grid points need to be tracked as particles, yet the model captures dynamics (vortices, Kuroshio meanders) that pure Eulerian multiscale networks miss, especially in 3D and long-horizon rollouts. Key limitations remain: the particle count is a fixed hyperparameter requiring manual tuning per scenario, and the absence of explicit Lagrangian supervision means learned trajectories lack a ground-truth check. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned particle budgets, direct Lagrangian loss terms, and extension to irregular/unstructured meshes could further close the gap to full CFD accuracy while preserving the efficiency advantage.
