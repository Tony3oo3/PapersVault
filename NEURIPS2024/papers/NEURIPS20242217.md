## Problem

In knowledge distillation, small student models suffer from a persistent capacity gap relative to large teacher models, limiting how much knowledge can be effectively transferred. Existing KD methods constrain logits or intermediate features but cannot overcome the fundamental parameter limitation of the student. This paper asks whether temporarily over-parameterizing the student during distillation training—without increasing inference cost—can close this gap.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on the weight matrices of transformer-based student and teacher models (BERT variants for NLP, TinyViT for CV). During training, each student weight matrix is decomposed into a chain of 4th-order MPO tensors, inflating the trainable parameter count (e.g., 66M→160M for BERT-of-Theseus); after convergence, tensor contraction reconstructs the original matrices in nearly lossless conditions, so inference parameters and latency remain unchanged. The student model ultimately produces standard task outputs: class labels for RTE, SST-2, QNLI, QQP, MNLI; F1/accuracy for MRPC; correlation scores for STS-B; Matthew's correlation for CoLA; and top-1/top-5 accuracy for ImageNet linear probes.

## Method

OPDF applies Matrix Product Operator (MPO) decomposition to student model weight matrices, factorizing each matrix W∈R^{I×J} into a sequential product of n fourth-order tensors T(k)[d_{k-1}, i_k, j_k, d_k] whose bond dimensions d_k are set to the minimum of left and right sub-products, yielding a central tensor (holding most parameters and core information) and auxiliary tensors. A novel auxiliary tensor alignment loss L_Aux = (1/n)Σ MSE(A_{s,k}, A_{t,k}) is added to the standard distillation objective, forcing the student's auxiliary tensors to match those of the teacher while leaving the central tensor free for independent learning. This design is orthogonal to existing KD losses (logits-based or feature-based), so it can be stacked on top of BERT-of-Theseus, LGTM, DBKD, AD-KD, or TinyViT without modification. MPO is preferred over SVD because it supports arbitrary decomposition order, enabling flexible and large-scale parameter expansion (e.g., 90M→160M) that SVD's two-dimensional singular-value structure cannot match. A theoretical bound (Theorem 1) guarantees that the Frobenius-norm reconstruction error is bounded by the square root of the sum of per-bond truncation errors, ensuring near-lossless recovery of the original matrices.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark, OPDF applied to AD-KD achieves 83.4 average score, matching the BERT-base teacher model (83.4) and outperforming the vanilla AD-KD (82.4) and SVD-based over-parameterization (82.6). Across all four NLP distillation baselines, OPDF consistently improves average GLUE scores by 1.1–3.2 points over the vanilla methods and by 0.9–1.8 points over SVD over-parameterization. On ImageNet-1k linear probe, TinyViT-21M+OPDF reaches 84.0% top-1 accuracy (vs. 82.3% vanilla and 82.9% SVD), approaching the CLIP-ViT-L/14 teacher (84.8%). Critically, inference parameters remain unchanged (e.g., 67M for all BERT variants, 21M for TinyViT-21M), so no additional latency is introduced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPDF is evaluated as an orthogonal add-on to four NLP distillation methods (BERT-of-Theseus, LGTM, DBKD, AD-KD) and one CV method (TinyViT), with SVD serving as a tensor-decomposition ablation. Across all NLP baselines, OPDF consistently outperforms both the vanilla method and SVD over-parameterization: the largest gains appear with DBKD (+3.2 avg over vanilla, +1.8 over SVD) and BERT-of-Theseus (+1.3 over vanilla, +0.9 over SVD). The most striking result is AD-KD+OPDF reaching 83.4 GLUE average, exactly matching the BERT-base teacher (83.4) and even surpassing it on RTE (71.7 vs. 70.5) and MRPC F1 (90.3 vs. 86.5). In CV, TinyViT-11M+OPDF (82.5% ImageNet-1k top-1) outperforms the larger TinyViT-21M vanilla (82.3%), demonstrating that over-parameterization can compensate for fewer base parameters. MPO-based OPDF uniformly beats SVD-based over-parameterization across every model and task, attributed to MPO's ability to scale parameter count arbitrarily via decomposition order, whereas SVD is limited in its parameter expansion capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Matrix Product Operator (MPO) decomposition—a technique from quantum many-body physics previously applied to model compression and lightweight fine-tuning of PLMs (Gao et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021; Gao et al., 2023)—for the opposite purpose: inflating student model parameters during distillation training. Unlike prior KD methods that constrain student logits or intermediate features to match the teacher, OPDF operates at the weight-matrix level, decomposing parameter matrices into higher-order tensors to increase trainable capacity while preserving inference-time parameter count. The auxiliary tensor alignment loss (L_Aux) introduces a new distillation signal that is orthogonal to both logits-based and features-based KD, positioning OPDF as a composable add-on rather than a replacement for existing distillation objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, plug-and-play mechanism to close the teacher-student capacity gap without any inference-time overhead, and its orthogonality to existing KD methods (demonstrated across BERT-of-Theseus, LGTM, DBKD, and AD-KD) makes it broadly applicable. The finding that over-parameterized students can match teacher performance (AD-KD+OPDF 83.4 vs. BERT-base 83.4 on GLUE average) challenges the assumption that distillation inherently produces a capacity-limited student. However, diminishing returns beyond ~1.6–2.5× parameter expansion and increased learning-rate sensitivity at higher scales limit the practical ceiling, and the approach has not yet been validated on very large models (e.g., 7B+ parameters) or multimodal settings, which remain open questions for broader adoption.
