## Problem

How hippocampal place cells acquire their spatially selective firing fields and how they remap in response to environmental changes remains poorly understood. Existing normative models that produce spatial representations rely on complex architectures and diverse regularization strategies, making it difficult to isolate the minimal principles that give rise to place-like codes and their context-dependent remapping.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240008/cropped_page3_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

Feedforward networks receive 2D Cartesian coordinates sampled uniformly from a 2×2 square enclosure, optionally concatenated with a scalar context signal c ∈ [−2, 2]; recurrent networks receive velocity vectors along boundary-avoiding trajectories (von Mises headings, Rayleigh step sizes) with an optional constant context input. The output is a 256-dimensional rate-coded population vector whose components correspond to individual unit firing rates, structured so that spatial (and contextual) similarity in the input is mirrored by Euclidean similarity in the representation space.

## Method

The paper proposes a minimal self-supervised similarity objective that penalizes the squared difference between a Gaussian spatial similarity (with a lower bound β inspired by hyperdimensional computing) and the corresponding population-vector similarity, plus an L2 activity regularizer. This objective is extended to joint space-context encoding by combining spatial and contextual Gaussian terms in the target similarity. Trained feedforward MLPs (2 hidden layers, 256 ReLU outputs) and single-layer RNNs (256 units, identity-initialized recurrent weights) both learn place-like and, in the recurrent case, band-like representations. A key structural property is that the objective is invariant to global orthogonal transformations of the representation, so applying a rotation, reflection, or permutation to a trained population vector yields a new valid spatial map without retraining; the paper further shows that best-fit Procrustes orthogonal transforms between learned context representations closely match the network's own remapping, and that continuous orthogonal transformations via the exponential map can extend a learned map into unexplored regions while approximately preserving the similarity structure.

## Results

Feedforward networks trained on the proposed similarity objective learn place-like representations with decodable spatial structure, achieving best-case mean position decoding error of a few percentage points relative to arena size. When trained jointly with a scalar context signal, spatial correlations between units in dissimilar contexts tend to zero, reproducing global remapping. Best-fit orthogonal transformations between learned context representations achieve substantially lower MSE than a random orthogonal baseline across all learned contexts, indicating that much of the learned remapping can be explained by orthogonal transformations. Recurrent networks trained with the same objective learn both place-like and band-like representations and perform path integration, achieving loss minima comparable to the feedforward network.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external baseline methods; instead, it compares internal design choices. Among three decoding schemes (linear decoder, population decoding with uniform memories, and Top-n peak-location decoding), the linear decoder achieves the lowest mean error while population decoding yields the lowest maximum error, with Top-n showing elevated errors at arena edges. For orthogonal transformations, best-fit (Procrustes) transforms between learned context representations produce substantially lower MSE than random rotations, reflections, or permutations, confirming that the learned remapping is largely orthogonal in structure. Recurrent networks match feedforward networks in loss minimization but additionally produce band-like units, suggesting a functional role in path integration. Ablations show that place-like tuning requires either a non-zero similarity lower bound β or activity regularization λ, but not both; removing both yields linear rather than place-like tuning profiles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of normative models (Cueva & Wei 2018, Banino et al. 2018, Sorscher et al. 2022, Dorrell et al. 2022, Schaeffer et al. 2023) that train neural networks on navigation tasks and observe emergent spatial cell representations, but departs from them by proposing a minimal, task-free similarity-based objective rather than relying on complicated architectures and regularization strategies. Where prior normative models emphasized grid cell emergence, this work specifically targets place cell representations and extends the objective to joint space-context encoding, yielding remapping as an emergent property. The hyperdimensional computing concept (Kanerva 2009) informs the similarity lower bound β, and the Procrustes orthogonal problem (Schonemann 1966) is repurposed to formalize remapping as a geometric transformation of existing representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, minimal mechanistic account of how place cell-like representations and global remapping can emerge from a single similarity-matching objective, offering an interpretable alternative to the attractor-network and inherited-field hypotheses debated in the neuroscience literature. The finding that orthogonal transformations of a trained representation can reproduce learned remapping across contexts suggests a 'representational reuse' mechanism that could inform models of hippocampal flexibility. Key limitations include the reliance on explicit position labels during training, the use of a scalar context signal, and rate coding; open questions include extending the framework to spiking networks, spatially-bound contexts, and identifying what upstream signals (e.g., grid cells) would biologically induce the orthogonal transformations.
