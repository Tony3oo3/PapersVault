## Problem

Label Distribution Learning (LDL) requires ground-truth label distributions that are prohibitively expensive to annotate. Existing Label Enhancement (LE) methods infer distributions from binary labels (±1), but forcing experts to make definitive relevance judgments on ambiguous instances reduces annotation efficiency and increases error risk. This paper proposes predicting label distributions from ternary labels {-1, 0, 1}, where '0' encodes genuine uncertainty, and provides both a theoretical error analysis and a principled probabilistic model for the ternary-to-distribution mapping.

## Input / Output

The method takes a feature vector (243-dim for JAFFE facial images, 142-dim for Painting, 128-dim PCA-reduced for Music) and per-label ternary annotations {-1, 0, 1} as input, producing a real-valued label distribution vector in [0,1]^M as output. Ternary labels are obtained by majority vote among three human experts, and the CateMO distribution is designed as a drop-in replacement for the binary-label likelihood in both discriminative and generative LE frameworks, so existing algorithms (GL, LR, MR) require only a loss-function swap.

## Method

The core contribution is CateMO, a categorical distribution p(s|z) over s ∈ {-1,0,1} parameterized by three precision parameters (λ̲, λ, λ̄) and a center ẑ, where each ternary value's probability is a normalized Gaussian-like function of the label description degree z: negative ∝ exp(-λ̲z²), uncertain ∝ exp(-λ(z-ẑ)²), positive ∝ exp(-λ̄(z-1)²). Theorem 2 derives closed-form constraints on these parameters that guarantee probability monotonicity (higher z increases positive probability and decreases negative probability) and probability orderliness (the dominant ternary value transitions smoothly from negative → uncertain → positive as z increases). In discriminative LE, CateMO's negative log-likelihood replaces the MSE loss Dist(z,s); in generative LE, CateMO replaces p(s|z) in the joint factorization. A complementary theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) quantifies the expected approximation error of ternary versus binary labels, showing ternary labels are superior except in degenerate boundary cases.

## Results

On JAFFE, MR-CateMO achieves the best overall performance among all ternary-label methods, with Cheb 0.169, KL 0.154, Cosine 0.892, Intersec 0.791, and Rho 0.477, closely approaching the Ground-Truth upper bound (Cheb 0.165, KL 0.145, Cosine 0.897). On Painting, LR-CateMO and GL-CateMO achieve near-identical results (Cheb 0.255/0.254, KL 0.545/0.542), both substantially outperforming their LL counterparts (Cheb 0.268/0.260, KL 0.617/0.610). On Music, all CateMO variants converge to nearly identical performance (e.g., LR-CateMO: Cheb 0.073, KL 0.103, Cosine 0.924), matching or exceeding Ground-Truth on several metrics, while the LL baselines lag by 8–15% on KL divergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CateMO against three existing binary-label enhancement algorithms—GL (Xu et al., IJCAI 2018), LR (Jia et al., TKDE 2023), and MR (Lu et al., AAAI 2023)—each adapted to ternary labels via three strategies: direct binary labels (LL), data transformation (DT), and MSE loss (MSE). CateMO consistently outperforms all three adaptation strategies across all three datasets and all five metrics. The largest gains are over the LL (binary) baseline: on JAFFE, MR-CateMO reduces KL from 0.171 to 0.154 (−10%) and improves Rho from 0.383 to 0.477 (+25%) over MR-LL; LR-CateMO reduces KL from 0.320 to 0.156 (−51%) over LR-LL. Against the MSE and DT baselines, CateMO shows more modest but consistent improvements (e.g., on Painting, GL-CateMO achieves KL 0.542 vs. GL-MSE 0.571 and GL-DT 0.590). Statistical significance tests (two-tailed t-test, α=0.05) confirm CateMO wins in 5–9 out of 9 comparisons against MSE, DT, and LL baselines respectively, with zero losses. CateMO approaches but does not always surpass the Ground-Truth upper bound, with the gap being smallest on Music (where several CateMO metrics match or exceed Ground-Truth) and largest on JAFFE Rho (0.477 vs. 0.463, where CateMO actually exceeds Ground-Truth).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Label Enhancement (LE) paradigm for Label Distribution Learning, which previously relied on binary multi-label annotations or multi-label rankings to infer full label distributions. The key departure is the introduction of ternary labels {−1, 0, 1} as a new annotation scheme, where '0' encodes expert uncertainty, inspired by three-way decision theory. The proposed CateMO distribution serves as a drop-in replacement for the binary-label likelihood in both discriminative and generative LE frameworks (e.g., GL, LR, MR), requiring only a substitution of the loss term or conditional distribution. The theoretical contribution—a quantitative Expected Approximation Error analysis—provides a principled justification for why the additional 'uncertain' category reduces approximation error compared to forced binary decisions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical bottleneck in LDL: the high cost and error rate of forcing experts into binary decisions when label-instance relationships are genuinely ambiguous. By allowing a third 'uncertain' annotation, the method reduces both annotation time and mislabeling risk, with the theoretical analysis confirming the error reduction in most parameter regimes. A key limitation is that CateMO's precision parameters (λ̲, λ, λ̄) are pre-fixed rather than learned, due to interdependence constraints that complicate gradient-based optimization; decoupling these parameters for joint learning is identified as future work. The empirical gains over MSE and DT baselines are consistent but modest, suggesting the primary value lies in the annotation protocol and theoretical framework rather than dramatic performance leaps.
