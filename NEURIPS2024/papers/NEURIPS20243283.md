## Problem

Existing bio-inspired continual learning methods train early visual layers with categorization objectives, contradicting neurophysiological evidence that primary visual cortex neurons do not contribute to object manifold untangling. Moreover, these methods ignore upstream processes like visual saliency, which neurophysiology shows modulates neuronal firing rates multiplicatively across all stages of visual processing. The paper identifies that saliency prediction is uniquely 'forgetting-free' (its accuracy remains stable or improves across non-i.i.d. task sequences), unlike classification features, and exploits this property to stabilize continual learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images resized to 288×384 pixels along with class labels and target saliency maps (either ground-truth or pseudo-labels generated by a pre-trained DeepGaze IIE oracle). The two-branch architecture produces two outputs simultaneously: a class prediction from the classification head and a spatial saliency map (H×W, matching input resolution) from the saliency decoder. A key architectural constraint is that the saliency encoder and the classification backbone must share identical architecture (e.g., both ResNet-18) with independent parameters, since modulation is applied via element-wise multiplication at corresponding layer outputs.

## Method

SER employs a two-branch model where a saliency prediction network (encoder E + decoder D) and a classification network C share the same backbone architecture but have independent parameters. At each of five predefined modulation points in the backbone, the pre-activation features of the classifier are multiplied element-wise (Hadamard product) by the corresponding features from the saliency encoder before the non-linearity is applied, emulating the multiplicative attention-driven modulation observed in primate visual cortex. Training optimizes a joint loss L = L_s + λL_c, where L_s is a KL-divergence-based saliency prediction objective and L_c is the standard continual learning objective of the paired method (e.g., DER++, ER-ACE, CoPE). Crucially, gradient flow from L_c into the saliency encoder is stopped to preserve its forgetting-free property, while the saliency predictor is initialized from a pre-trained checkpoint (trained on a disjoint ImageNet subset with DeepGaze IIE as oracle) and the classifier is trained from scratch. The approach is model-agnostic and can be plugged into any continual learning method that uses a neural feature extractor.

## Results

On Split Mini-ImageNet (Class-IL, buffer 2000), SER-enhanced ER-ACE achieves 33.09% final average accuracy vs. 24.93% for the baseline, and SER-enhanced CoPE reaches 33.35% vs. 23.58%. The largest single gain is CoPE + SER with buffer 5000 on Mini-ImageNet, jumping from 24.77% to 45.04% (~20 percentage points). On the more challenging Split FG-ImageNet benchmark, SER-enhanced CoPE with buffer 5000 reaches 34.34% vs. 17.77% for the baseline, and SER-enhanced ER-ACE reaches 28.68% vs. 18.21%. SER-enhanced methods also outperform the dual-branch baselines DualNet and TwF across all buffer sizes on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SER consistently improves every rehearsal-based baseline it is paired with: DER++ gains 4–10 points, ER-ACE gains 6–10 points, and CoPE gains 5–20 points (the largest being +20.27 pts at buffer 5000 on Mini-ImageNet). Against the dual-branch baselines, SER-enhanced variants outperform both DualNet (e.g., 36.44% vs. 32.08% at buffer 5000 on Mini-ImageNet) and TwF (35.55% vs. 29.05% at buffer 2000), suggesting saliency-driven modulation yields better representations than contrastive learning or frozen-backbone attention. In the buffer-free setting, SER improves LwF by ~18 points in Task-IL (48.61% vs. 30.61%) and oEWC by ~11 points (35.33% vs. 24.10%), though Class-IL gains are modest due to the very low baselines. In the broader comparison (Table ST-2), SER-enhanced ER-ACE (35.58% at buffer 5000) and CoPE (45.04%) surpass all other rehearsal methods including GDumb (38.67%), OnPro (32.23%), and iCaRL (14.58%). The spurious-feature experiment shows SER recovers most of the 22-point accuracy drop caused by brightness-biased training data (44.08% vs. 28.46% for ER-ACE alone), and PGD adversarial robustness tests confirm SER significantly reduces accuracy degradation under perturbation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SER carves new ground by repurposing visual saliency prediction—a low-level, biologically-grounded visual process—as a stabilizing modulation signal for continual learning, rather than the contrastive learning (DualNet) or pre-trained classification backbones (TwF) used in prior dual-branch approaches. It explicitly departs from attribution-map-based replay methods (GradCAM variants) by arguing that saliency maps, rooted in human visual processing, are fundamentally different from classifier-derived attribution maps: the former are forgetting-free while the latter degrade with task shifts. The method is positioned as a model-agnostic plug-in that can be combined with any existing OCL strategy (DER++, ER-ACE, CoPE, etc.), distinguishing it from foundation-model-based CL approaches restricted to ViT architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that saliency prediction is effectively forgetting-free in continual learning settings—while classification accuracy degrades—offers a biologically-motivated mechanism for stabilizing feature learning that could inform broader CL design. The up-to-20-point accuracy gains across multiple baselines and benchmarks, combined with improved robustness to spurious features and adversarial attacks, make this practically relevant. Key limitations include the requirement that the saliency encoder and classifier share identical architectures (limiting applicability to heterogeneous networks), the need for high-resolution images (excluding CIFAR-scale datasets), and the dependence on a pre-trained saliency oracle. An open question the authors raise is whether other low-level visual tasks share this forgetting-free property, which could open a new family of stabilizing auxiliary objectives for CL.
