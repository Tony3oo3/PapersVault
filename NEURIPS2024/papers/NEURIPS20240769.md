## Problem

When an ILP has symmetry, multiple equivalent optimal solutions exist, and standard supervised learning randomly selects one as the label, introducing label variability that degrades prediction quality. No existing ML-based approach explicitly exploits the inherent symmetry structure of ILPs to stabilize training or improve solution prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240769/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an ILP instance encoded as a bipartite graph (variable nodes and constraint nodes connected by weighted edges representing the coefficient matrix) and outputs a continuous prediction vector for the integer/binary variables via a sigmoid head. During training, each instance is additionally paired with a collected solution and its symmetry group G_i; the predicted solution is then passed to downstream solvers (fix-and-optimize, local branching, or node selection) to produce a feasible solution within an 800-second time limit.

## Method

SymILO reformulates the supervised learning objective by introducing a permutation operator π_i ∈ G_i as an additional optimization variable alongside the GNN parameters θ, defining a symmetry-aware empirical risk r_s(f_θ, {π_i}) = (1/N)Σ ℓ(f_θ(s_i), π_i(y_i)) that dynamically selects the best equivalent label for each instance. An alternating minimization algorithm updates π_i (exhaustive search over cyclic/dihedral groups of size q or 2q; LP relaxation of a binary assignment problem for symmetric groups of size q!) and then updates θ via several gradient steps, repeating until convergence. The GNN backbone is a bipartite-graph GCNN following Gasse et al. (2019) and Han et al. (2023), and the permutations are used only during training, not at inference. A theoretical result (Proposition 4.1) guarantees that the symmetry-aware risk is always no worse than the classic risk, with strict improvement whenever the same instance appears with different symmetric labels.

## Results

SymILO achieves average relative primal gap improvements of 50.3%, 66.5%, and 45.4% over the three baselines (Neural Diving, Predict-and-Search, and MIP-GNN) across the Fix&Optimize, Local Branching, and Node Selection downstream tasks, respectively. On the PESP dataset, SymILO reduces the local branching primal gap from 0.306 to 0.000 (100% improvement) and the node selection gap from 1.899 to 0.280 (85.3% improvement). The symmetry-aware training also reduces Top-m% prediction errors substantially, e.g., from 87.4±41 to 12.7±4.8 at 10% on PESPD, while adding only ~0.01–0.2 s per mini-batch for the permutation optimization step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SymILO consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and downstream task. Against Neural Diving (Nair et al., 2020) on Fix&Optimize, SymILO reduces the primal gap by 38–83% depending on the dataset, with the largest gain on PESPD (2.389→0.404). Against Predict-and-Search (Han et al., 2023) on Local Branching, SymILO achieves the most dramatic improvements, reaching a 100% gap reduction on PESP (0.306→0.000) and 96.3% on PESPD (3.442→0.127). Against MIP-GNN (Khalil et al., 2022) on Node Selection, SymILO still wins on all four datasets (20–85.3% gains), though the improvement is most modest on PESPD (3.755→3.006, 20%), which the authors attribute to runtime overhead from CPLEX callback interactions. Paired-sample t-tests confirm statistical significance (p < 0.05) for all comparisons except two PESP entries (p ≈ 0.059), and the additional training cost of the alternating permutation step is negligible (≤0.13 s per instance).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the bipartite-graph GNN representation of ILPs introduced by Gasse et al. (2019) and the predict-and-search training pipeline of Han et al. (2023), adopting their architecture and loss configurations. It departs from prior ML-for-ILP work (Nair et al. 2020, Han et al. 2023, Khalil et al. 2022) that addressed the multiple-optimal-solution problem indirectly—via conditional distributions or random graph features—by explicitly incorporating the symmetry group of each instance as an additional set of optimization variables. The alternating minimization framework that jointly updates neural network weights and discrete permutation operators is the paper's core methodological contribution, drawing on block coordinate descent but applying it to a novel discrete-continuous joint problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Symmetry is pervasive in practical ILPs (bin packing, job scheduling, railway timetabling), and the paper demonstrates that ignoring it during training introduces label variability that measurably degrades prediction quality. The 50–66% average primal-gap improvements over symmetry-agnostic baselines across four benchmarks and three downstream tasks make this a practically relevant technique for the ML4CO community. However, the framework is currently limited to symmetric, cyclic, and dihedral groups, and the permutation sub-problem becomes expensive for large symmetry groups, leaving open the question of how to scale to arbitrary or high-dimensional symmetry structures. Extending the approach to other group types and to larger-scale instances remains a key direction for future work.
