## Problem

Marker-less air drawing with standard cameras produces highly noisy hand tracking images that are often nearly unrecognizable, yet existing AR/VR sketching tools require costly headsets and digital markers to produce clean results. The paper asks whether a controllable diffusion model can learn to 'reconstruct' a faithful, aesthetically coherent sketch directly from such severely distorted tracking input, without any additional sensors or paired training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244307/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a rasterized hand tracking image (produced by MediaPipe Hands from a hand motion video) as the spatial conditioning input, combined with a short text prompt (e.g., 'a black and white sketch of a <category>'). The tracking image is extremely noisy, containing jitters, false strokes, and structural distortions that can make the intended shape nearly unrecognizable. The output is a clean rasterized sketch image at the same resolution, rather than a coordinate sequence, making the approach drawing-order invariant and compatible with large pretrained image generators.

## Method

The core contribution is an augmentation-based self-supervised training procedure for ControlNet. Instead of conditioning on clean edge maps as in standard ControlNet, the model is trained to map from a randomly augmented (distorted) version of the ground-truth sketch back to the clean original, so the conditioning adapter simultaneously learns spatial-conditioning signals and a denoising mapping from distorted to clean input. Augmentations are categorized into local (jitters, stroke distortion, random spikes), structural (sketch-level distortion, misplacement, resize), and false strokes (transitional and random), each applied with 50% probability to simulate realistic tracking artifacts. The backbone is Stable Diffusion XL (or SD1.5) finetuned with LoRA on Quick, Draw! sketches to acquire basic sketch-style generation, and text prompts provide semantic guidance that becomes especially critical for unseen categories. The approach requires no paired noisy-to-clean data, making it fully self-supervised, and the same framework extends to sketch completion (via random erasure augmentation) and text-conditioned stroke styling.

## Results

On the real air-drawing dataset with SDXL + augmentations (the best configuration), the method achieves SSIM 0.64, CD 25.46, LPIPS 0.36, CLIP I2I 0.84, and CLIP I2T 0.29, compared to the raw tracking baseline of SSIM 0.55, CD 32.36, LPIPS 0.42, CLIP I2I 0.76, and CLIP I2T 0.21 — a 10% SSIM gain, 21% CD reduction, and 6% LPIPS reduction. On the synthetic dataset, the same configuration reaches SSIM 0.64, CD 17.39, LPIPS 0.33, CLIP I2I 0.85, CLIP I2T 0.28. Generalizability is confirmed: unseen-category scores (e.g., SSIM 0.63, CD 24.26 on real) remain close to seen-category scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: (1) the raw hand-tracking image, (2) ControlNet trained without any augmentation, and (3) Sketch-Pix2Seq (P2S). Against the tracking baseline, the proposed method (SDXL + augmentations) improves SSIM by 10% (0.55→0.64), reduces CD by 21% (32.36→25.46), and reduces LPIPS by 6% (0.42→0.36) on the real dataset. ControlNet without augmentation provides virtually no improvement over the raw tracking image (e.g., SSIM 0.55 vs. 0.55, CD 31.99 vs. 32.36 on real), confirming that the augmentation-based training is essential. Against Sketch-Pix2Seq, which requires training a separate model per category, the proposed method achieves noticeably higher SSIM and lower CD on a 10-category subset, and additionally generalizes to unseen categories without retraining, whereas P2S has no generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes controllable diffusion models (ControlNet, T2IAdapter) in a fundamentally different way than their original design: rather than using spatially-precise conditioning maps (canny edges, depth, skeletons) as hard structural constraints, it trains the conditioning adapter to learn a mapping from severely noisy, distorted hand-tracking images to clean sketches via augmentation-based self-supervised training. It departs from prior sketch generation methods (Sketch-RNN, Sketch-Bert, Sketch-Pix2Seq) that operate on coordinate sequences by adopting an image-based representation, gaining drawing-order invariance and access to large pretrained image generators. The work also positions itself against AR/VR drawing tools (Tiltbrush, HoloARt) that require headsets and markers, proposing a marker-less alternative using only standard camera hand tracking (MediaPipe).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a new task (Generative Motion to Sketch) and provides two datasets (synthetic and real air-drawing) that did not previously exist, creating a foundation for future research on marker-less air drawing. The key conceptual contribution—that spatial conditioning in controllable DMs need not be a precise structural constraint but can be a noisy, distorted signal if the adapter is trained with appropriate augmentations—opens a broader design space for applying controllable diffusion to any noisy-to-clean image translation problem. Limitations include the restriction to simple, cartoon-like sketches (Quick Draw style), no exploration of full-color or complex illustrations, and the assumption of a single hand in frame. Open questions include real-time inference feasibility, multi-object or multi-stroke scenes, and whether the augmentation strategy generalizes to other domains of noisy spatial conditioning.
