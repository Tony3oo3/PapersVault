## Problem

Traditional diffusion-based image restoration methods start the reverse process from Gaussian white noise, which is inefficient because degraded images already contain the low-frequency structure that needs to be preserved. Existing residual-based approaches like RDDM predict the residual and noise terms separately without an explicit quantitative relationship, have forward/reverse processes inconsistent with DDPM, and require custom noise schedules, limiting generalization and interpretability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244152/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded image (plus a shadow mask for the ISTD dataset) as conditional input, concatenated with the current noisy estimate x_t along the channel dimension and fed into a single U-Net. The reverse process initializes from a noisy version of the degraded image rather than pure Gaussian noise, and produces a restored clean image in just 5 sampling steps. For image generation, the degraded input is replaced with a zero matrix, making the framework a general-purpose generative model.

## Method

Resfusion introduces the residual term R = x̂₀ − x₀ into the DDPM forward process, redefining q(x_t | x_{t−1}) to q(x_t | x_{t−1}, R) so that the diffusion path connects the degraded image to the ground truth rather than pure noise to the ground truth. The prediction target is a weighted combination called resnoise (resε = ε + [(1−√α_t)√(1−ᾱ_t)/β_t]·R), which explicitly encodes the quantitative relationship between the noise and residual terms, making the reverse inference form identical to DDPM. A smooth equivalence transformation identifies the optimal acceleration step T′ where the coefficient of x₀ in the forward process vanishes (√ᾱ_{T′} ≈ 0.5), allowing the reverse process to start directly from the noisy degraded image and unifying training and inference. A truncated linear schedule controls the small discretization offset at T′, and the framework directly reuses any existing noise schedule without redesign.

## Results

Resfusion achieves state-of-the-art results across three image restoration tasks with only 5 sampling steps and 7.7M parameters. On ISTD (shadow removal, 256×256), it reaches PSNR 31.81 / SSIM 0.965 / MAE 4.81 on the full image, outperforming the strongest baseline RDDM (30.91 / 0.962 / 4.67). On LOL (low-light, 256×256 YCbCr), it achieves PSNR 30.02 / SSIM 0.954 / LPIPS 0.070, surpassing RDDM-SM-Res by +4.63 dB PSNR and −40% LPIPS. On Raindrop (deraining, 256×256 YCbCr), it reaches PSNR 34.40 / SSIM 0.975, beating RDDM by +1.89 dB PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Resfusion consistently outperforms all 28 baselines across the three restoration tasks. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor RDDM [17], Resfusion wins on every dataset: +0.9 dB PSNR on ISTD (31.81 vs 30.91), +4.63 dB PSNR and −40% LPIPS on LOL 256×256 (30.02 vs 25.39; 0.070 vs 0.116), and +1.89 dB PSNR on Raindrop 256×256 (34.40 vs 32.51), while using half the parameters (7.7M vs 15.5M) and a simple truncated linear schedule instead of RDDM's custom AOSA schedule. Against traditional shadow-removal methods (ST-CGAN, DSC, DHAN, DMTN, UnfoldingNet), Resfusion leads by 0.6–4.6 dB PSNR on ISTD. On low-light enhancement, Resfusion surpasses all non-diffusion baselines (RetinexNet, KinD++, Zero-DCE, EnlightenGAN, Restormer, LLFormer) by 1.0–13.3 dB PSNR on LOL. On deraining, it beats pix2pix, AttentiveGAN, DuRN, IDT, and the diffusion-based WeatherDiff/RainDropDiff by 0.2–4.6 dB PSNR on Raindrop. For image generation on CIFAR-10, Resfusion achieves FID 28.81 at 10 steps vs DDPM's 43.11, and FID 6.31 at 100 steps vs DDPM's 9.79, though it remains slightly behind deterministic DDIM (6.21 at 100 steps) due to its stochastic sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

Resfusion extends DDPM by modifying the forward diffusion process to incorporate a residual term R = x̂₀ - x₀, so the reverse process starts from noisy degraded images rather than Gaussian white noise. It departs from RDDM (Liu et al., 2023), which also introduces residuals but predicts them separately from noise, uses an accumulation strategy inconsistent with DDPM's reverse process, and requires custom noise schedules. Unlike traditional diffusion-based restoration methods (SR3, WeatherDiffusion, ShadowDiffusion) that treat degraded images purely as conditioning while still starting from pure noise, Resfusion defines a 'resnoise' prediction target with an explicit quantitative relationship between residual and noise, and uses a smooth equivalence transformation to identify an optimal acceleration step T' where the posterior and prior distributions align, unifying training and inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: competitive restoration quality with only 5 sampling steps and 7.7M parameters (vs. 25–100 steps and 82–109M parameters for prior diffusion-based methods), while directly reusing standard noise schedules without task-specific redesign. The conceptual decomposition—residual term drives semantic shift, noise term drives detail reconstruction—provides interpretability absent in prior residual-diffusion approaches. Open questions include whether the framework extends to latent-space diffusion (as in LDM/SD), whether more sophisticated conditioning (cross-attention, multi-scale) can close the remaining gap to task-specific SOTA, and whether the 5-step efficiency generalizes to higher-resolution or more complex degradations.
