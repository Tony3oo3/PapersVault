## Problem

Automatic prompt optimization (APO) methods targeting instructions (IO) and exemplars (EO) have evolved largely independently, with recent research attention heavily favoring IO. This paper addresses the gap by asking: what is the relative importance of EO versus IO, how do they interact, and what is the optimal way to combine them under a fixed computational budget?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241854/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a labeled validation set D_val, a target LLM, and an optimizer LLM, and produces an optimized prompt P*(x) = [I*, e_1*, ..., e_k*, x] that maximizes validation accuracy. Critically, exemplar candidates are not human-annotated but are self-generated by the target model's correct predictions on D_val (bootstrapped few-shot), making them a free side-product of the IO evaluation process. All methods operate under a matched budget of m=32 prompt evaluations on D_val, with k=3 exemplars selected for most experiments.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic empirical comparison of 5 IO strategies (No IO, APE, ProTeGi, PromptAgent, OPRO) crossed with 6 EO strategies (No EO, Random, Nearest, Diversity, Random Search, Mutation) on BIG-Bench Hard and MMLU tasks using PaLM 2, Gemini 1.0/1.5, and GPT-3.5. The key mechanism for EO is reusing the target model's own correct input-output pairs (including intermediate reasoning steps) from D_val as exemplar candidates, then selecting the best k-tuple via either heuristic retrieval or iterative optimization (random search over combinations, or mutation-based evolutionary search). The central finding is that how exemplars are selected can outweigh how instructions are optimized: simple EO strategies like random search on self-generated exemplars outperform SoTA IO with random exemplars, and jointly optimizing both under a split budget (e.g., 8 IO + 24 EO evaluations) yields synergistic gains exceeding either alone. The paper further reveals that SoTA IO methods like ProTeGi inadvertently embed quasi-exemplars within their optimized instructions, suggesting their performance gains are partly attributable to implicit exemplar generation rather than instruction refinement per se.

## Results

On BIG-Bench Hard (BBH) with PaLM 2 (text-bison-002), the best IO+EO combination (ProTeGi + Mutation) achieves 77.29% average accuracy, a +16.99% improvement over the No IO + No EO baseline (60.30%). Critically, exemplar optimization alone (No IO + Mutation, 72.92%) outperforms the best IO method with only random exemplars (ProTeGi + Random, 70.81%), demonstrating that how exemplars are selected can outweigh how instructions are optimized. On MMLU, the gap narrows and No IO + Random Search (72.75%) even slightly surpasses ProTeGi + Random Search (72.31%), indicating that with good exemplar selection, instruction optimization provides diminishing returns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four SoTA IO methods (APE, ProTeGi, PromptAgent, OPRO) and PromptBreeder against various EO strategies. ProTeGi is the strongest IO method in isolation (68.13% No EO on BBH), but when paired with optimized exemplars (Mutation), the gap between the best and worst IO strategies shrinks to less than 4%, suggesting instructions matter less with good exemplars. The headline finding is that No IO + Mutation (72.92%) beats ProTeGi + Random (70.81%), meaning a simple exemplar search outperforms complex instruction optimization with the default random exemplar design. On MMLU, No IO + Random Search (72.75%) even exceeds ProTeGi + Random Search (72.31%), showing IO can be counterproductive without proper exemplar selection. Against PromptBreeder, the paper's simple sequential IO-then-EO routine converges to comparable or better solutions while incurring only a fraction of PromptBreeder's computational cost (which often requires hundreds of evaluations vs. 32 here). Across all model families tested (PaLM 2, Gemini 1.0/1.5, GPT-3.5), the qualitative finding that EO ≥ IO in impact holds consistently.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously independent lines of work in automatic prompt optimization (APO): instruction optimization (IO, e.g., APE, ProTeGi, PromptAgent, OPRO) and exemplar optimization (EO, e.g., DSPy's random search, retrieval-based selection, active learning for ICL). While IO methods have dominated recent attention—driven by improved instruction-following in LLMs—EO approaches largely predate instruction-tuned models and have been treated as a secondary concern. The paper's key departure is treating the two as a unified optimization problem, systematically evaluating 30+ IO-EO combinations under matched compute budgets, and leveraging model-generated validation-set outputs as free exemplars rather than requiring human-annotated demonstrations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that simple exemplar selection (random search, mutation) can outperform state-of-the-art instruction optimization challenges the field's current research trajectory and suggests a significant re-allocation of effort toward EO. The demonstrated synergy between IO and EO, along with the observation that SoTA IO methods may be inadvertently generating quasi-exemplars that drive their performance, opens concrete research directions: advanced combinatorial search for exemplars, instance-wise (rather than task-wise) selection, and joint optimization frameworks. Limitations include the absence of open-ended generation tasks and safety metrics, and the possibility that findings may not fully generalize to all model-task combinations, though the consistency across PaLM 2, Gemini 1.0/1.5, and GPT-3.5 on BBH and MMLU strengthens confidence.
