## Problem

Existing structural pruning methods for LLMs face a fundamental trade-off: dependence-based methods like LLM-Pruner force all layers to share the same feature subset, severely limiting pruning flexibility, while SliceGPT achieves per-layer flexibility by inserting orthogonal projection matrices into residual connections at the cost of 5–13% additional parameters. The paper asks whether one can obtain SliceGPT-level flexibility—different feature subsets and different widths per layer—without any extra parameters and without updating the original model weights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242304/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen pre-trained LLM weights (OPT, LLaMA, LLaMA-2, Phi-1.5, Phi-2) and a small calibration corpus (WikiText-2 or Alpaca) to train a lightweight hypernetwork. It outputs a set of binary index-selection vectors (one per selection matrix S₁–S₅ per block) that define which embedding dimensions each layer's input, output, and MLP middle dimension retains. At inference, the pruned model applies index selection before each LayerNorm and index addition in place of the standard residual sum, so the block width can vary freely across layers while the residual stream itself is never transformed.

## Method

DISP-LLM breaks the structural dependence that constrains regular pruning by relocating selection matrices inside the residual connection: instead of modifying the residual path (as SliceGPT does with Q_l^T Q_{l+1}), it applies independent pseudo-selection matrices S₁/S₂ to the Attention input/output and S₃/S₄/S₅ to the MLP input/middle/output, then uses index selection and index addition operations to sample and merge feature subsets from the untouched residual stream. To learn the optimal width per layer, a hypernetwork composed of a Bi-GRU (capturing inter-block relationships) and per-layer linear layers (capturing inter-dimension relationships) generates continuous latent parameters, which are binarized via the ReinMax straight-through gradient estimator. The entire procedure is formulated as a single optimization: minimize next-token language modeling loss plus a log-ratio regularization that pushes the retained parameter count toward a user-specified budget p, all while keeping the original model weights completely frozen. This yields a sub-network that is a strict subset of the original weights, with no additional parameters and no recovery fine-tuning required.

## Results

On WikiText-2 perplexity, DISP-LLM at 50% pruning achieves 9.84 on LLaMA-2 7B and 7.11 on LLaMA-2 13B, substantially outperforming LLM Surgeon (15.38 and 9.43) and K-OBD (46.64 and 16.06) at the same ratio. At 20% pruning, DISP-LLM reaches 6.10 on LLaMA-2 7B, matching the dense model's 5.12 more closely than any baseline. On zero-shot tasks (Table 3), DISP-LLM at 50% pruning on LLaMA-2 7B achieves 46.72 average accuracy vs. 45.68 for LLM Surgeon and 42.67 for K-OBD. The method also matches or beats semi-structured pruning (SparseGPT, Wanda) on LLaMA-2 7B/13B perplexity (Table 2), demonstrating for the first time that structural pruning can rival semi-structural pruning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISP-LLM consistently outperforms SliceGPT across all models and pruning ratios; at 50% on LLaMA-2 7B, DISP-LLM achieves 9.84 PPL vs. SliceGPT's 8.12 (note: SliceGPT's actual compression is lower than its slicing ratio, making the gap even larger in effective compression). Against K-OBD, the advantage is dramatic at high pruning ratios: DISP-LLM reaches 9.84 vs. 46.64 on LLaMA-2 7B at 50% pruning. Compared to LLM Surgeon (which updates weights), DISP-LLM wins by 5.54 PPL on LLaMA-2 7B and 2.22 on LLaMA-2 13B at 50% pruning, while not updating any model weights. On zero-shot tasks (Table 3), DISP-LLM at 50% on LLaMA 7B achieves 48.62 average accuracy, outperforming LLM-Pruner without fine-tuning (41.84) and approaching LLM-Pruner with fine-tuning (49.23). Against semi-structured baselines in Table 2, DISP-LLM at 50% structural pruning beats SparseGPT (10.17) and Wanda (11.02) on LLaMA-2 7B (9.84) and LLaMA-2 13B (7.11 vs. 8.32/8.27), establishing that structural pruning can match or exceed semi-structured pruning for the first time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the structural pruning line for LLMs initiated by LLM-Pruner (which enforces structural dependence, forcing all layers to share the same feature subset) and SliceGPT (which breaks that dependence via PCA-based orthogonal projections but introduces non-trivial extra parameters in residual connections). The key conceptual departure is replacing SliceGPT's learned projection matrices with simple index selection and index addition operations, thereby achieving per-layer feature subset flexibility without any additional parameters or weight updates. The width-learning component draws on the hypernetwork + ReinMax gradient estimator framework, and the overall idea of unconstrained channel selection is inspired by UPSCALE for CNNs, but scaled to LLMs with a global parameter-budget optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that structural pruning—unlike semi-structured sparsity—translates directly into hardware speedup, and this paper shows it can now match semi-structured methods (SparseGPT, Wanda) on LLaMA-2 7B/13B without updating any model weights, meaning the pruned model is a literal sub-network of the original. This opens the door to deploying compressed LLMs on resource-constrained devices with minimal engineering overhead. Limitations include inconsistent throughput gains across models due to suboptimal PyTorch index-operation implementations, and the method has not been validated on models beyond ~13B parameters. An open question is whether the alternative implementation (zero-filling weight matrices instead of selecting feature maps) with custom GEMM kernels could close the remaining throughput gap.
