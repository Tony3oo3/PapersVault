## Problem

Existing data-driven agent simulators rely on encoder-decoder architectures that manually split trajectories into history and future segments, introducing architectural heterogeneity and wasting learning signals since only one split point per sequence is used. Additionally, naive next-token prediction at 10 Hz allows models to achieve low loss by trivially copying the current state without performing meaningful long-range spatial-temporal reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242526/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 11-step initial agent states (3D position, 2D velocity, yaw angle, bounding box size, and semantic type) plus vectorized map polylines (lane centerlines, road edges, crosswalks sampled every 5 m) and autoregressively generates 80 future steps per agent at 10 Hz for up to 128 agents. All spatial relationships are encoded via relative spacetime embeddings (distance, bearing, relative yaw, time offset) rather than absolute coordinates, and the output is a 16-mode mixture of Laplace distributions (position, velocity) and von Mises distributions (yaw) per time step, unrolled by a GRU within each patch.

## Method

BehaviorGPT is a fully autoregressive, decoder-only Transformer with homogeneous blocks that processes the entire trajectory sequence without separating history from future, treating every time step symmetrically as a potential 'current' state to maximize data utilization. The key innovation is the Next-Patch Prediction Paradigm (NP3), which groups ℓ consecutive time steps into a patch and trains the model to predict the entire next patch, forcing it to capture high-level behavioral semantics and long-range interactions rather than exploiting single-step copy shortcuts. Spatial-temporal reasoning is handled by a triple-attention mechanism: causal temporal self-attention per agent, kNN-filtered agent-map cross-attention, and kNN-filtered agent-agent self-attention, all augmented with relative positional embeddings. A GRU-based prediction head unrolls the ℓ states within each predicted patch step-by-step, updating its hidden state with predicted (not ground-truth) locations to encourage error recovery. With only 2 decoding blocks, hidden size 128, and 3M total parameters, the model achieves state-of-the-art realism on the Waymo Open Sim Agents Challenge.

## Results

On the 2024 Waymo Open Sim Agents Challenge test set, BehaviorGPT achieves the best minADE of 1.4147 and the highest REALISM score of 0.7473, winning first place with only 3M parameters. It also leads on COLLISION (0.9537) and OFFROAD (0.9349), indicating strong agent-agent and agent-map interaction modeling. Notably, BehaviorGPT outperforms GUMP (523M params, minADE 1.6041) and MVTE (>65M params, minADE 1.6770) by a wide margin despite having 20–170× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BehaviorGPT outperforms all five baselines on both headline metrics (minADE and REALISM). Against the strongest competitor GUMP (523M params), BehaviorGPT reduces minADE from 1.6041 to 1.4147 (−11.8%) and improves REALISM from 0.7431 to 0.7473, while using roughly 1/174th the parameters. Compared to MVTE (>65M params), the gap is even larger: minADE drops from 1.6770 to 1.4147 and REALISM rises from 0.7302 to 0.7473. VBD (12M params) is the closest on minADE (1.4743 vs. 1.4147) but trails on REALISM (0.7200 vs. 0.7473) and OFFROAD (0.8788 vs. 0.9349). TrafficBotsV1.5 (10M params) and the Linear Extrapolation baseline are substantially weaker across all metrics. The only sub-metrics where BehaviorGPT is slightly behind GUMP are LINEAR ACCEL (0.3365 vs. 0.4111) and ANG ACCEL (0.5544 vs. 0.6353), which the authors attribute to the absence of an explicit kinematic constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

BehaviorGPT departs from the dominant encoder-decoder paradigm in data-driven agent simulation (e.g., MVTA, Trajeglish, MotionLM) by adopting a fully autoregressive, decoder-only Transformer architecture inspired by LLMs, eliminating the heterogeneous split between history and future processing. It builds directly on QCNet's relative spacetime representation to enable symmetric, per-patch modeling without a fixed 'current' reference frame. The Next-Patch Prediction Paradigm (NP3) draws conceptual parallels to patching in ViT, time-series forecasting, and multi-token prediction in LLMs, but applies them specifically to trajectory simulation to prevent trivial copy-paste shortcuts that plague single-step next-token prediction at 10 Hz.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 3M-parameter model can outperform 523M-parameter competitors on the WOSAC benchmark suggests that architectural simplicity and data-efficient training (treating every time step as 'current') may be more important than raw model scale for agent simulation. The NP3 paradigm offers a practical, generalizable recipe for any autoregressive sequence model where single-token prediction admits trivial shortcuts. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models and datasets, whether controllable generation (language prompts, goal points) can be integrated, and whether the autoregressive compounding-error limitation can be mitigated without sacrificing the architectural simplicity that drives the efficiency gains.
