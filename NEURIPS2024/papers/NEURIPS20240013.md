## Problem

Existing HPO methods for iterative learners (Successive Halving, Hyperband, BOHB, Sub-sampling) make candidate elimination decisions based solely on current observed validation loss, ignoring the inherent uncertainty in model training dynamics. This causes the actually-best candidates to be discarded in early training stages—experiments show SH discards the true best candidate in 8–22 of 100 iterations, yielding 48% regret in validation loss. The paper introduces the concept of uncertainty-aware HPO to systematically account for this training uncertainty in both candidate selection and budget allocation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of K candidate hyperparameter configurations, their per-epoch validation loss histories accumulated during training, and a total training budget B (one unit = one epoch). At each decision point it produces per-candidate Gaussian estimates of converged loss (μ_c, σ_c²), a confidence curve mapping k → P_k (probability the best converged candidate is among the top-k), and an adaptive discarding threshold τ that determines how many candidates to retain. The UQ is computed on-the-fly via a lightweight weighted least squares fit over the loss history, requiring no additional model retraining or ensemble sampling.

## Method

The UQ-guided scheme decomposes each candidate's loss curve into a momentum component (k_t^T η with k_t = [t^{-1/2}, t^{-1}] capturing the decaying trend) and a bias term (u^T Z encoding latent structure across the hyperparameter space), solved via weighted least squares with weights w_jt = 1/(F_t σ_t²). From the fitted curve, the converged loss at a large epoch N is approximated as N(v̂(N), σ̂²(N)), where σ̂²(N) is derived from the estimator covariance. A confidence curve P_k is then computed via a closed-form integral over the joint Gaussian of all candidates' converged losses. The discarding mechanism selects the smallest k satisfying P_k ≥ τ, where τ is adaptively chosen to balance exploration (reducing discarding error by keeping more candidates) against exploitation (giving fewer candidates more training budget to reduce uncertainty), formalized by the condition P_{k_i} − P_{k_i−1} = R/(k_i(k_i−1)) · ζ(k_i, ξ_i). The scheme is integrated into four existing HPO methods—SH, HB, BOHB, and SS—yielding SH+, HB+, BOHB+, and SS+, and is supported by theoretical guarantees showing the UQ-guided approach achieves the same identification probability as UQ-oblivious methods with a smaller budget lower bound.

## Results

The UQ-guided HPO family achieves 21–55% regret reduction over its UQ-oblivious counterparts at the same exploration budget on NAS-BENCH-201 (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-16-120) and LCBench. In terms of exploration efficiency, the enhanced methods require only 30–75% of the total time to reach comparable model accuracy: SH+ needs 50.78% of SH's budget on NAS-BENCH-201 and 43% on LCBench, while SS+ is the most efficient at 47.84% and 30.93% respectively. On the Fashion-MNIST task in LCBench, UQ-guided approaches yield over 50% average regret improvement, and on the Million Song Dataset ridge regression task, SH+ achieves over 40% improvement over SH.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four UQ-guided methods against their original UQ-oblivious counterparts. SH+ consistently outperforms Successive Halving [19], achieving the same 5% average regret on NAS-BENCH-201 with only ~51% of the budget, and reducing regret by 21–55% at equal budget. HB+ [25] improves over Hyperband with a 75% time-to-parity on NAS-BENCH-201 and 60% on LCBench, though it is the least efficient of the four UQ-guided variants. BOHB+ [11] beats BOHB with 68.4% time-to-parity on NAS-BENCH-201 and 53.34% on LCBench, benefiting from both Bayesian sampling and UQ-guided elimination. SS+ [15] is the most efficient overall (47.84% and 30.93% time-to-parity), demonstrating that the UQ-guided scheme generalizes beyond early-stopping methods to multi-fidelity sub-sampling. Across all four comparisons, the UQ-guided variants uniformly win on both regret and exploration time, with no baseline matching or exceeding its enhanced counterpart.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the established HPO methods—Successive Halving, Hyperband, BOHB, and Sub-sampling—by introducing a unified uncertainty quantification (UQ) layer that governs both candidate selection and budget allocation. Prior HPO designs make discarding and scheduling decisions based solely on observed validation loss at the current iteration, implicitly treating early-stage performance as a reliable proxy for converged quality. The UQ-guided scheme departs from this by constructing a probabilistic model of each candidate's converged loss (via weighted least-squares fitting of the loss curve) and using the resulting confidence curve to adaptively determine how many candidates to retain each round. The approach draws on the broader ML uncertainty quantification literature (Bayesian approximations, deep ensembles) but repurposes it for the HPO decision process rather than for prediction-time calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 21–55% regret reduction and 30–75% time savings are practically significant for DNN training pipelines where HPO budgets are expensive, and the theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1, 2, 4) formalize why UQ-aware scheduling needs less budget than UQ-oblivious halving for the same success probability. A key limitation is that the scheme requires ranking multiple candidates in parallel, making it less applicable to gradient-based or strictly sequential HPO methods. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-iterative learners (the paper sketches an extension via dataset-size-as-budget) and whether the offline uncertainty-trend profiling (Section D) transfers cleanly across architectures and datasets without re-fitting.
