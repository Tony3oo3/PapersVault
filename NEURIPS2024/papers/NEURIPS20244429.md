## Problem

State-of-the-art post-training DNN pruning methods inspired by the Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) framework leverage second-order loss curvature for better pruning decisions but lack any theoretical convergence guarantees. Meanwhile, sparse recovery algorithms like Iterative Hard Thresholding (IHT) have well-established convergence theory but do not exploit curvature information and do not extend to multi-layer DNNs. This paper bridges the two areas by asking whether second-order information can be integrated into iterative sparse recovery to yield both improved convergence bounds and practical DNN pruning algorithms.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained dense model (DeiT ViTs, OPT-125M, Phi-1.5B, Llama-2/3) and a small calibration set (1000 ImageNet images for vision, 128 sequences from WikiText2 or Red Pajama for language), along with a target sparsity threshold (50% uniform in all experiments). At each I-OBS iteration, a fresh calibration batch is sampled and a per-layer quadratic sparse subproblem is solved, producing updated sparse weights for that layer. The number of iterations (3 for LLMs, up to 100 for ViTs) controls the trade-off between compression quality and compute, with improvements saturating quickly for larger, more overparametrized models.

## Method

The core insight is to replace the Euclidean norm in the IHT projection step with a Hessian-weighted norm, yielding the update θ_{t+1} = Top_k(θ_t − H_t⁻¹∇f(θ_t)) — a dense Newton step followed by sparse thresholding. Theoretically, the optimal mask selection and parameter update involve a projection matrix (I − H_t⁻¹H_t^S) derived from the inverse Hessian restricted to pruned coordinates, and the paper proves a local quadratic convergence rate matching classical Newton's method up to constants under standard restricted smoothness and strong convexity assumptions. For practical DNN pruning, the method is applied iteratively and layer-wise: each round samples a new calibration batch, solves a per-layer quadratic sparse problem using SparseGPT or OBC as the approximate solver, and then takes a gradient step. WoodFisher and OBC are shown to be special cases of the I-OBS framework, providing them with their first theoretical guarantees.

## Results

On LLM pruning at 50% uniform sparsity, I-OBS (3 iterations) substantially outperforms the one-shot SparseGPT baseline: for OPT-125M it achieves 25.20 PPL on WikiText2 (vs. 33.85 for SparseGPT, and even below the dense model's 27.65) and 31.41 on C4 (vs. 32.27). For Phi-1.5B, I-OBS reaches 23.94 PPL on WikiText2 (vs. 25.28) and 20.26 on C4 (vs. 21.13). On ViT pruning (50% sparsity, ImageNet Top-1), I-OBS with 100 iterations recovers most of the accuracy gap: DeiT-Tiny improves from 62.98 (SparseGPT) to 64.05, DeiT-Small from 76.07 to 76.57, and DeiT-Base from 80.29 to 80.43, all starting from dense accuracies of 72.08, 79.81, and 81.91 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SparseGPT (Frantar & Alistarh, ICML 2023), I-OBS wins decisively on all tested models: for OPT-125M it reduces WikiText2 PPL from 33.85 to 25.20 (a 8.65-point improvement, even surpassing the dense model) and C4 PPL from 32.27 to 31.41; for Phi-1.5B it improves WikiText2 from 25.28 to 23.94 and C4 from 21.13 to 20.26. Against k-IHT (Peste et al., NeurIPS 2021) in synthetic sparse linear regression, Topk-I-OBS converges faster in both training loss and distance-to-optimum, confirming the theoretical quadratic convergence advantage from second-order information. Against CBS (Yu et al., ICML 2022) on MobileNetV1, I-OBS is comparable at low sparsity (30–50%, <0.5% difference) but pulls ahead substantially at higher sparsity: +2.0% at 60%, +5.6% at 70%, and +11.5% at 80% sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) family of pruning heuristics—WoodFisher, WoodTaylor, OBC, and SparseGPT—under a single iterative framework (I-OBS) derived from the proximal point method with a Hessian-weighted regularizer. It draws a direct bridge to the sparse recovery literature (IHT, Newton-hard-thresholding methods) by showing that OBS-type one-shot pruners are special cases of a general iterative algorithm where second-order information is injected into the sparse projection step. The key departure from prior work is providing the first convergence guarantees (quadratic local rate) for these previously heuristic methods, and showing that the sparsity constraint does not degrade Newton's asymptotic rate beyond a constant factor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical contribution is significant: it gives the first rigorous justification for widely-used pruning heuristics (WoodFisher, OBC) that had no prior guarantees, and the unification clarifies why iterative application of second-order pruning (I-OBS) consistently outperforms one-shot methods. Practically, the Topk-I-OBS variant is simple to implement and scales to 7B+ parameter LLMs, yielding ~1 PPL improvement over SparseGPT with just 3 iterations. Key limitations include the gap between the strong convexity/smoothness assumptions and non-convex DNN losses, the local (not global) convergence guarantee, and observed overfitting when running too many iterations on LLMs—open questions that the authors flag for future work.
