## Problem

Controlling complex PDE-governed physical systems requires finding long-horizon control sequences that are both physically faithful and near-optimal for a given objective. Existing deep learning and reinforcement learning methods treat dynamics iteratively, leading to myopic failure modes or adversarial trajectories that violate system dynamics, while classical methods like MPC suffer from prohibitive computational cost and poor global optimization. A key unresolved challenge is how to explore control sequences beyond the training distribution while maintaining physical consistency.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input an initial system state u_0, a target state u_T (or other control conditions c), and operates on spatiotemporal fields (e.g., 128×128 fluid grids over 40 time steps for jellyfish, 128 spatial grids over 10 time steps for Burgers). It jointly outputs a full control sequence w and system trajectory u over the entire time horizon, with the control objective J optimized either via explicit gradient guidance or guidance conditioning. The framework natively supports partial observation (zero-padding unobserved spatial regions) and partial control (enforcing zero control in uncontrollable regions) without architectural changes.

## Method

DiffPhyCon reformulates PDE-constrained control as energy minimization over the joint distribution p(u,w|c) learned by a 2D UNet denoising network trained with standard DDPM loss. During inference, Langevin sampling iteratively denoises from Gaussian noise while simultaneously following the learned energy gradient (ensuring physical fidelity) and the explicit gradient of the control objective J (driving optimization). The key innovation is prior reweighting: a second denoising network ε_φ learns the marginal prior p(w|c), and during inference its gradient is subtracted with coefficient (γ−1) from the joint denoiser, effectively flattening the control-sequence prior so that low-probability but high-performing control sequences become accessible. A theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) characterizes when γ<1 (flattening) versus γ>1 (sharpening) improves the probability of sampling ε-optimal solutions, based on an entropy comparison between optimal and suboptimal regions.

## Results

On the 2D jellyfish movement control task (the paper's primary benchmark), DiffPhyCon achieves a control objective J = -74.11 under full observation, outperforming the best baseline BPPO (-20.93) by 53.18, with an average speed of 279.87 vs. BPPO's 107.67. Under the more challenging partial observation setting, DiffPhyCon still achieves J = -23.32 vs. BPPO's -3.02. On the 1D Burgers' equation, DiffPhyCon reduces J_actual by 30.1%, 52.6%, and 44.6% over the best baseline in the PO-FC, FO-PC, and PO-PC settings respectively. On the 2D smoke control task, DiffPhyCon achieves J = 0.2254 vs. the best baseline BPPO at 0.3066.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffPhyCon outperforms all six baselines across all three experimental tasks. BPPO [73] is the strongest baseline, achieving J = -20.93 (full obs.) and -3.02 (partial obs.) on the jellyfish task, but DiffPhyCon surpasses it by 53.18 and 20.30 respectively, with the gap widening dramatically under partial observation. SAC [16] in its surrogate-solver variant is competitive on the jellyfish full-observation setting (J = 18.14) but degrades severely under partial observation (J = 158.82), while DiffPhyCon maintains strong performance in both. SL [24] performs well only in full-observation settings (J_actual = 0.00078 on Burgers' FO-PC) but fails under partial observation (0.02328), whereas DiffPhyCon remains robust. BC [49] and MPC [57] are consistently the weakest baselines, with MPC producing negative average speeds on the jellyfish task. PID [33] is only applicable to the 1D Burgers' equation and achieves the worst results (J_actual ≈ 0.09), confirming its limited applicability to complex nonlinear systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffPhyCon repurposes the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the energy-gradient interpretation of denoising networks (Du et al., 2023) to cast PDE-constrained optimal control as a joint energy minimization over the full trajectory and control sequence, rather than the sequential decision-making paradigm of RL or the backpropagation-through-time approach of supervised learning. It departs from classical adjoint/MPC methods by learning the dynamics implicitly from data via a diffusion model, and from diffusion planning work (Janner et al., 2022) by incorporating physical constraints as a learned generative energy and introducing prior reweighting to escape the training distribution. The prior reweighting technique, which decomposes the joint energy into a control-sequence prior and a conditional trajectory energy, is the paper's key conceptual departure from standard guided diffusion sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unified, data-driven framework that handles partial observation and partial control—settings where classical methods and sequential RL struggle—while maintaining physical fidelity through the learned energy landscape. The released jellyfish CFD dataset (30k trajectories, 128×128, fluid-solid coupling) fills a gap as a benchmark for complex physical system control. Open questions include scalability to 3D or higher-dimensional control spaces, the computational cost of running two diffusion models at inference, and whether the approach can operate without any training trajectories (pure PDE-based energy). The method's reliance on a well-conditioned training distribution and the need to tune γ and λ per task limit its plug-and-play applicability.
