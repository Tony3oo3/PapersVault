## Problem

Aligned LLMs remain vulnerable to jailbreak attacks (e.g., GCG, PAIR) that exploit adversarial prompts to bypass safety training. Existing defenses either apply random perturbations that fail to target the specific tokens causing jailbreaks, require expensive semantic-level LLM calls, or demand costly fine-tuning of the target model. The paper asks whether an information-theoretically principled, lightweight front-end can selectively compress adversarial prompts to neutralize jailbreaks without modifying the target LLM.

## Input / Output

The method takes an adversarial text prompt X (up to 400 tokens) and a frozen target LLM that shares the same tokenizer as the extractor. It produces a perturbed sub-prompt X̃ in which uninformative tokens are replaced by a single perturbation token (default '.'), which is then fed to the target LLM to generate a response. During training, masking operates at the embedding level to preserve gradient flow; during inference, it operates at the discrete token-ID level via a straight-through estimator, so the output is a valid token sequence the target LLM can process.

## Method

IBProtector introduces a lightweight trainable extractor (TinyLlama-1.1B + MLP + sigmoid) that outputs per-token attribution scores π ∈ [0,1]^T, from which a Bernoulli mask M is sampled to select which tokens to retain. The training objective combines three terms: (1) an informativeness loss that minimizes cross-entropy of the expected response Y given the perturbed prompt X̃ plus a KL-divergence penalty between the target LLM's output distributions on X and X̃, preventing the mask from deviating too far; (2) a compactness loss (KL divergence between Bernoulli(π) and a fixed sparsity Bernoulli(r)) that enforces minimal information retention; and (3) a continuity loss penalizing fragmented, non-contiguous masks. Only the last layer of the small extractor and the MLP are fine-tuned while the target LLM remains frozen, making the defense transferable across unseen attack methods and target models. A reinforcement-learning variant using cosine-similarity rewards is proposed for fully black-box targets where gradients are unavailable.

## Results

On AdvBench, IBProtector reduces the Attack Success Rate (ASR) of PAIR jailbreaks from 87.5% to 19.2% on Vicuna-13B and from 67.5% to 16.7% on LLaMA-2-7B, while GCG ASR drops from 82.5% to 1.7% and 27.5% to 0.8% respectively. The Harm Score and GPT-4 harmfulness score also improve substantially (e.g., GPT-4 score drops from 3.008 to 1.483 on Vicuna for PAIR). On the benign TriviaQA dataset, the Benign Answering Rate (BAR) remains high at 96.5% (Vicuna) and 97.0% (LLaMA-2), confirming minimal degradation on normal queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IBProtector outperforms all six baselines on every metric for both target LLMs. Against the strongest baseline Semantic Smooth, IBProtector reduces PAIR ASR from 20.0% to 19.2% on Vicuna and from 40.8% to 16.7% on LLaMA-2, while also achieving lower Harm and GPT-4 scores. For GCG attacks, IBProtector matches Semantic Smooth's 1.7% ASR on Vicuna but achieves a substantially lower Harm score (-1.763 vs. -0.842) and GPT-4 score (1.042 vs. 1.058); on LLaMA-2 it achieves 0.8% ASR versus Semantic Smooth's 10.0%. Fine-tuning and Unlearning LLM, which modify the target model, are consistently weaker (e.g., 62.5% and 66.7% PAIR ASR on Vicuna) and also degrade BAR more noticeably. Smooth LLM and RA-LLM, which use random token perturbations, show limited transferability and fail to suppress prompt-level attacks effectively (68.3% and 34.2% PAIR ASR on Vicuna). Self Defense achieves moderate ASR reduction but suffers the largest BAR drop (79.6% on Vicuna), indicating it over-refuses benign queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

IBProtector adapts the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 1999; Alemi et al., 2016) from signal processing and graph/time-series explanation (Miao et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2024) to the LLM jailbreak defense setting, which is the first such application. It departs from prior perturbation-based defenses (Smooth LLM, RA-LLM) that apply random or ensemble masks by learning a *selective* Bernoulli mask via a lightweight extractor, and from semantic smoothing (Ji et al., 2024) that requires expensive LLM calls for paraphrasing. The key technical departure is a tractable reformulation: replacing intractable mutual information with a KL divergence between Bernoulli distributions for compactness, and modifying the prediction quantifier with a KL-regularized cross-entropy to avoid the signaling issues identified in prior IB-explanation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is transferability: an extractor trained on PAIR/GCG attacks against Vicuna and LLaMA-2 generalizes to unseen attack methods (Autodan, GPTFuzz, ReNellm) and unseen target models (ChatGPT, GPT-4, Mistral, ChatGLM3) without retraining, suggesting the extracted 'harmful information' is a model-agnostic signal. This positions IBProtector as a deployable front-end filter requiring only a 1.1B-parameter model, with no modifications to the target LLM. Key limitations include the lack of fluency in extracted sub-prompts, dependence on the target LLM's intrinsic alignment for final refusal, and the untested RL-based black-box variant (Eq. 9). Open questions include multi-modal defense, generating human-readable explanations from the mask, and whether the approach degrades under stronger adaptive attacks with more iterations.
