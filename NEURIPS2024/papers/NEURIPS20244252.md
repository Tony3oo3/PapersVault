## Problem

Conventional adversarial packet generation methods (GAN+PSO, LSTM-based timing reshaping, RL) rely on surrogate classifiers or direct access to NIDS feature extractors and configurations, assumptions that are unrealistic for real-world attackers who operate with limited knowledge of the target security infrastructure. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a classifier-free generation model that produces evasive adversarial packets without requiring any specific NIDS component knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244252/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes src-to-dst malicious packet sequences and contextually aligned normal packet sequences as input, where each packet byte is converted to a hex token (vocabulary size 65536) and only the first three packets per unidirectional flow are processed (max sequence length 512). The output is a modified malicious packet sequence where mutable header fields (TCP flags, TTL, window size, IP ID, sequence/ack numbers) are altered to mimic normal traffic, while immutable fields (5-tuple, payload) are preserved via a parallel 'model dictionary' structure to maintain attack functionality.

## Method

DiffuPac integrates a pre-trained BERT (12 layers, 12 heads, 768-dim) with a DiffuSeq-based diffusion model in a two-phase pipeline. Pre-training uses two self-supervised tasks adapted from BERT: a Masked Unidirectional Flow Model (predicting masked byte tokens) and Same Sequence-origin Prediction (classifying src-to-dst vs. dst-to-src direction). During fine-tuning, normal and malicious sequences are paired via BERT embeddings and FAISS cosine similarity search (threshold ε=0.9), then concatenated; the forward process applies targeted noising exclusively to the malicious portion while leaving the normal portion intact, and the reverse process uses the fine-tuned BERT as the denoising engine guided by the normal packet context. This dual use of BERT—as both the denoising network and the contextual pairing mechanism—combined with the targeted noising strategy, is the core architectural distinction from standard diffusion approaches.

## Results

DiffuPac is evaluated on 6 attack types (MITM, Botnet, Brute Force, DDoS, Port Scan, Infiltration) against 6 classifiers (KitNET, DT, IF, MLP, SVM, LR) using 2 feature extractors (CICFlowMeter, AfterImage), with the Malicious traffic Evasion Rate (MER) as the primary metric. Across all attack types, DiffuPac outperforms Traffic Manipulator (GAN&PSO) by an average of 9.12 percentage points and TANTRA (LSTM) by an average of 4.26 percentage points, yielding an overall average improvement of 6.69 percentage points over both baselines. For example, on Botnet with CICFlowMeter, DiffuPac achieves MER values of 53.87%–73.21% across classifiers, compared to 26.74%–52.07% for GAN&PSO and 38.64%–57.77% for LSTM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffuPac is compared against two baselines: Traffic Manipulator (GAN&PSO, Han et al. 2021) and TANTRA (LSTM, Sharon et al. 2021). On average across all 6 attack types, DiffuPac outperforms GAN&PSO by 9.12 pp and LSTM by 4.26 pp in MER. The advantage is most pronounced with the CICFlowMeter feature extractor, where DiffuPac consistently leads across nearly all classifiers (e.g., 73.21% vs. 52.07% for GAN&PSO on Botnet/MLP). However, DiffuPac underperforms both baselines on the AfterImage + KitNET combination (e.g., 74.46% vs. 99.18% for GAN&PSO on MITM), which the authors attribute to Traffic Manipulator being trained directly on AfterImage features—an unrealistic advantage for a real-world attacker. DiffuPac's key differentiator is that it requires zero knowledge of NIDS configurations or feature extractors, operating under a black-box assumption, whereas both baselines rely on surrogate classifiers or direct access to NIDS internals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: adversarial packet generation for NIDS evasion (prior work by Han et al. 2021 using GAN+PSO, Sharon et al. 2021 using LSTM/TANTRA, and Hore et al. 2023 using RL) and diffusion-based sequence generation (DiffuSeq, DiffusionLM, DDPM). The key departure is that all prior adversarial packet generators assume access to a surrogate classifier or NIDS feature extractor, whereas DiffuPac introduces a classifier-free paradigm by repurposing a pre-trained BERT model both as the denoising engine within the diffusion reverse process and as a similarity-search tool (via FAISS) for pairing contextually aligned normal-malicious packet sequences. The targeted noising strategy—injecting noise only into the malicious portion of a concatenated sequence—adapts DiffuSeq's random-merging approach into a structured, context-guided generation process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The classifier-free formulation is the paper's most significant conceptual contribution: it aligns the attacker model with realistic black-box constraints, removing the dependency on NIDS internals that plagues all prior methods. However, practical impact is tempered by limitations—functionality preservation is verified only for Port Scan and Brute Force (not DDoS, Botnet, etc.), and the high OOD rates for DDoS (78%) and Brute Force (70%) suggest the model struggles with volumetric or highly repetitive attacks. Open questions include whether the BERT+diffusion fusion generalizes to encrypted traffic, whether adversarial purification via diffusion (mentioned as future work) can serve as a practical defense, and whether the approach scales to the 30-billion-IoT-device landscape the paper motivates.
