## Problem

Existing cross-embodiment RL methods train agents across embodiments under specific task rewards, causing learned knowledge to become tightly coupled to those tasks rather than capturing embodiment-specific characteristics. This limits generalization to new tasks or unseen embodiments. The paper asks whether unsupervised pre-training in reward-free environments across a distribution of embodiments can extract embodiment-aware, task-agnostic knowledge that enables fast adaptation to arbitrary downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The unified policy π: S → Δ(A) operates over a shared state space S = ∪_e S_e and a unified action embedding space A, with fixed or learnable projectors φ_e: A → A_e mapping to each embodiment's native action space. The embodiment discriminator q_θ(e|h,s) consumes a GRU-encoded history of state-action pairs (length 10 for DDPG, 24 for PPO) plus the current state to output a posterior over embodiments. The intrinsic reward R_CE(τ) = log p(e) − log q_θ(e|τ) is computed per-state rather than per-trajectory, and the policy itself remains Markovian (does not condition on the encoded history) for fair comparison with baselines.

## Method

PEAC formulates cross-embodiment unsupervised RL as a Controlled Embodiment MDP (CE-MDP) and derives, via a min-max analysis over worst-case extrinsic rewards, that the optimal pre-training objective reduces to minimizing the KL divergence between each embodiment's trajectory distribution and the average-embodiment trajectory distribution (Theorem 3.2). This yields the cross-embodiment intrinsic reward R_CE = log p(e) − log q_θ(e|τ), which encourages the agent to explore regions where the embodiment discriminator is uncertain—i.e., where different embodiments' posteriors are similar or exploration is insufficient. The embodiment discriminator is trained jointly to classify embodiment context from trajectory histories, and the resulting reward is added to any existing unsupervised RL intrinsic reward (e.g., LBS or DIAYN) to produce PEAC-LBS or PEAC-DIAYN. During fine-tuning, the pre-trained policy is optimized under the extrinsic reward with a KL constraint to the pre-trained policy, using only a fraction (e.g., 1/10) of the pre-training steps.

## Results

On state-based DMC (URLB), PEAC achieves mean returns of 491.3 (Walker-mass), 631.0 (Quadruped-mass), and 573.7 (Quadruped-damping), outperforming the best baselines (BeCL: 544.5/475.6/421.9; CIC: 503.9/602.2/166.2) on 2 of 3 domains with tighter confidence intervals. On image-based DMC, PEAC-LBS reaches 754.8/740.8/742.1 and PEAC-DIAYN reaches 621.9/556.1/557.2, surpassing the strongest baselines (Choreographer: 687.8/682.3/724.6; LBS: 658.2/730.7/732.7). On Robosuite, PEAC scores 190.7 (train) and 200.8 (test) versus ICM's 174.4/178.3, and on Isaacgym A1-disabled, PEAC achieves 19.2/10.3/10.0/20.3/17.3 across five locomotion tasks versus ICM's 6.7/5.7/4.0/8.5/13.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEAC outperforms all 10 state-based DMC baselines on at least 2 of 3 embodiment domains, with the most consistent gains over exploration methods (ICM, RND, Disagreement, SMM) where it leads by 100–400 points on Quadruped-damping. Against the strongest skill-discovery baselines, PEAC beats CIC by 407.5 on Quadruped-damping (573.7 vs 166.2) and BeCL by 151.8 on Quadruped-damping (573.7 vs 421.9), though BeCL narrowly edges PEAC on Walker-mass (544.5 vs 491.3). In image-based DMC, PEAC-LBS surpasses the SOTA Choreographer by 67.0/58.5/17.5 across the three domains, and PEAC-DIAYN leads all pure skill-discovery methods (DIAYN, APS, LSD, CIC) by 12–115 points. On Robosuite, PEAC outperforms ICM, RND, and LBS by 16.3/19.5/33.0 on training embodiments and 22.5/15.8/34.2 on the unseen Jaco arm. On Isaacgym A1-disabled, PEAC achieves roughly 2–3× the returns of ICM and RND across all five locomotion tasks (e.g., 19.2 vs 6.7 on run, 10.3 vs 5.7 on climb), with the largest margin on the challenging climb and leap tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information geometry framework of unsupervised RL (Eysenbach et al., ICLR 2021) from single-embodiment MDPs to a distribution of embodiments, formalizing the new CE-MDP structure and showing that skill vertices may no longer correspond to deterministic policies. It builds on contextual MDP formulations (Hallak et al., 2015) to derive a principled intrinsic reward (R_CE) from a min-max pre-training objective, and flexibly composes with existing exploration (LBS) and skill-discovery (DIAYN) methods. Conceptually, it departs from prior cross-embodiment RL that couples embodiment and task knowledge by introducing reward-free pre-training across embodiments, inspired by the unsupervised pre-training paradigm in NLP and vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PEAC provides a theoretically grounded and practically deployable recipe for cross-embodiment pre-training, validated on real-world legged robots with joint failures — a practically important scenario for robotics deployment. The key limitation is the assumption that embodiments share similar structure, making it unclear how well the approach scales to fundamentally different morphologies. Open questions include whether the framework handles more challenging downstream tasks, more diverse embodiment distributions, and whether the embodiment discriminator can be replaced by more scalable representations as the embodiment space grows.
