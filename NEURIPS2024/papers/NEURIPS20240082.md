## Problem

Differentially private hypothesis testing has been largely confined to discrete domains, while continuous distributions pose a fundamental challenge: even releasing simple statistics like the median is impossible over infinite domains under DP. This paper presents the first DP algorithm for testing equivalence between two continuous distributions under the A_k-norm (distance measured over k intervals), filling a critical gap between the impossibility of learning from a single continuous distribution and the feasibility of statistical inference from two.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes oracle access to two continuous distributions P and Q (drawing i.i.d. samples), a distance threshold α, and privacy parameters (ε, δ). It draws Poisson-sized samples from the mixture ½(P+Q), sorts them, and produces a binary decision: 'NULL' with probability ≥ 2/3 when P = Q, or 'ALT' with probability ≥ 2/3 when ‖P−Q‖_{A_k} ≥ α. The A_k-norm is defined as the supremum over all partitions of ℝ into k intervals of the sum of absolute probability-mass differences, generalizing both Kolmogorov distance (k=2) and total variation (k = domain size).

## Method

The algorithm privatizes the two-stage Diakonikolas–Kane–Nikishkin tester. Part I computes an autocorrelation statistic over adjacent sorted points (sensitivity 6) and adds Laplace noise. Part II is the core contribution: instead of using data values to define bin boundaries (which would have unbounded sensitivity), it uses sorted indices π_i = i·N/m + B_i where B_i ~ Ber(1/2). The Bernoulli noise enables a coupling argument showing that between any two neighboring datasets, at most 2·log_{4/3}(2/δ) bins are affected, bounding the sensitivity of the L₂ statistic. Within each bin, a Poisson-sized subset is drawn to estimate the L₂ norm difference, avoiding the need to resample from the distributions. The algorithm iteratively merges adjacent bins (halving the number) and runs a private L₂ closeness test at each level, rejecting if any test detects a large discrepancy.

## Results

The paper's main result is a sample complexity bound of $\tilde{O}(\max\{k^{4/5}/\alpha^{6/5},\; k^{2/3}/(\alpha\epsilon^{1/3}),\; k^{1/2}/\alpha^2,\; k^{1/3}/(\alpha^{4/3}\epsilon^{2/3}),\; \sqrt{k}/(\alpha\epsilon)\})$ for $(\epsilon,\delta)$-DP equivalence testing of two continuous distributions under $\mathcal{A}_k$-norm. This improves upon the trivial Subsample-and-Aggregate baseline of $O((k^{4/5}\alpha^{-6/5}+k^{1/2}\alpha^{-2})/\epsilon)$ by avoiding the $1/\epsilon$ factor on the leading terms. The bound is asymptotically close to the known non-private lower bound of $O(k^{4/5}/\alpha^{6/5}+\sqrt{k}/\alpha^2)$, with two additional privacy-dependent terms ($\sqrt{k}/(\alpha\epsilon)$ and $k^{2/3}/(\alpha\epsilon^{1/3})$) arising from the $L_2$-norm testing sensitivity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run empirical experiments but compares its theoretical sample complexity against existing bounds. Against the Subsample-and-Aggregate baseline (which runs the non-private tester $O(1/\epsilon)$ times), the proposed algorithm removes the $1/\epsilon$ multiplier from the leading $k^{4/5}/\alpha^{6/5}$ and $k^{1/2}/\alpha^2$ terms, replacing them with weaker privacy-dependent terms $k^{2/3}/(\alpha\epsilon^{1/3})$ and $k^{1/3}/(\alpha^{4/3}\epsilon^{2/3})$. Compared to the non-private lower bound of $O(k^{4/5}/\alpha^{6/5}+\sqrt{k}/\alpha^2)$ from Diakonikolas et al. [16], the DP upper bound matches the leading terms and adds two extra privacy terms ($\sqrt{k}/(\alpha\epsilon)$ and $k^{2/3}/(\alpha\epsilon^{1/3})$) that the authors attribute to the high sensitivity of the $L_2$-norm tester. The paper also notes that no lower bound is known for the private regime of $\mathcal{A}_k$-closeness testing, so a gap between upper and lower bounds remains open. The algorithm additionally provides numerical estimators explaining its accept/reject decision, unlike the Subsample-and-Aggregate approach which only reports a majority vote.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper privatizes the optimal non-private equivalence tester of Diakonikolas, Kane, and Nikishkin (FOCS 2015), which uses iterative discretization of the real line and L2-norm closeness testing under the A_k-norm metric. Prior DP distribution testing work (Cai et al., Aliakbarpour et al., Acharya et al., Zhang) has been confined to discrete domains, and the paper explicitly positions itself as the first DP algorithm for continuous distributions. The key technical departure from the non-private algorithm is the use of sorted indices with Bernoulli noise to define bin boundaries (rather than raw data points), combined with Poisson-sized subsampling from each bin to avoid the resampling that the original algorithm relies on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a sharp theoretical contrast: while releasing even a median from a single continuous distribution is impossible under DP over infinite domains, equivalence testing between two continuous distributions is achievable, opening a path for DP to extend into continuous-data statistical inference. The sample complexity is asymptotically close to the non-private lower bound, with the gap attributable to the L2-norm sensitivity and unoptimized log factors. Open questions include whether the two extra terms (√k/(αε) and k^{2/3}/(αε^{1/3})) can be eliminated, and whether a matching lower bound exists for the private regime. The framework also extends to structured families (log-concave, piecewise-constant, MHR), broadening its applicability.
