## Problem

PeakConv (PKC), the first convolution operator designed specifically for radar signals, embeds a fixed peak receptive field (PRF) for band-pass noise suppression. However, radar target signatures exhibit varying degrees of frequency response broadening across dimensions, categories, and time, while noise/clutter distributions are non-homogeneous and time-varying. The fixed PRF cannot adapt to these dynamics, limiting PKC's ability to precisely locate interfering signals and suppress them at cell-level granularity.

## Input / Output

The multi-view framework (AdaPKC-Nets) consumes 5 temporally contiguous frames of range-Doppler (256×64), range-angle (256×256), and angle-Doppler (256×64) tensors and produces simultaneous pixel-wise segmentation masks on both RD and RA views with four classes (pedestrian, cyclist, car, background). The single-view KuRALS-Net takes 5-frame RD tensors of size 2048×124 (after 0-Doppler elimination) from a Ku-band surveillance radar and outputs four-class masks (UAV, pedestrian, vehicle, background) over a 6.4 km range field with ~0.198 m/s Doppler resolution.

## Method

AdaPKC replaces PKC's fixed PRF with an adaptive one estimated per center unit. AdaPKC^ξ (metric-based) computes inner-product correlation scores between the center unit and K candidate reference unit sets, then selects the PRF whose sorted metric curve has the maximum first-order gradient, identifying the transition zone occupied by target-interfering noise. AdaPKC^θ (learning-based) uses a small two-branch network (horizontal and vertical convolutions with directional kernels) to predict continuous-valued guard bandwidths in four directions, then samples reference units via differentiable linear interpolation, enabling end-to-end gradient flow. A fine-tuning strategy (FiTOS) introduces a confidence threshold τ on the metric curve steepness to switch PRF adjustment on/off per unit, focusing optimization on target regions and reducing background interference during the fine-tuning phase.

## Results

On the CARRADA benchmark, AdaPKCξ-Net^FiT achieves the best overall performance with 62.1% mIoU / 74.0% mDice on the RD view and 44.3% mIoU / 55.5% mDice on the RA view, surpassing the PKCIn-Net baseline (60.7% / 43.1% mIoU) by +1.4% and +1.2% respectively. On the self-collected KuRALS surveillance radar dataset, KuRALS-Net w/ AdaPKCξFiT reaches 58.2% mIoU / 67.6% mDice, outperforming the plain KuRALS-Net baseline (56.0% / 65.5%) and all general-purpose segmentation models (FCN, U-Net, DeepLabv3+). The FiTOS fine-tuning strategy adds a further +0.9% RD mIoU and +0.2% RA mIoU over AdaPKCξ-Net without extra training resources.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaPKCξ-Net^FiT outperforms the direct predecessor PKCIn-Net [32] by +1.4% RD mIoU and +1.2% RA mIoU, confirming that adaptive PRF adjustment is more effective than the fixed peak receptive field. Against the multi-view CNN baseline TMVA-Net [19], AdaPKCξ-Net^FiT gains +6.0% RD mIoU and +6.6% RA mIoU, and against the transformer-assisted TransRadar [6] it leads by +4.9% RD mIoU and +4.4% RA mIoU. On the RA view, AdaPKCξ-Net^FiT (44.3% mIoU) also edges out T-RODNet [12] (43.5% mIoU) despite using 25× fewer parameters (6.3M vs 162.0M). Compared to deformable convolutions (DefConv [5], DefConvV2 [33]), AdaPKC variants achieve substantially higher mIoU (e.g., 61.2% vs 58.8% RD, 44.1% vs 39.3% RA), which the authors attribute to three task-mismatched design choices in DefConv (signaling mechanism, prediction method, and noise-suppression mechanism). On the KuRALS surveillance dataset, AdaPKCξFiT improves over the plain KuRALS-Net baseline by +2.2% mIoU and +2.1% mDice, and surpasses DeepLabv3+ [1] (52.6% mIoU) by +5.6% mIoU while using only 1.2M parameters versus 59.3M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends PeakConv (PKC, CVPR 2023), which embedded CFAR-inspired band-pass peak enhancement into a standard convolution operator for radar semantic segmentation, by replacing its fixed peak receptive field (PRF) with a data-adaptive one (AdaPRF). The adaptive mechanism draws conceptual inspiration from dynamic CFAR detectors (SO-CFAR, GO-CFAR, OS-CFAR) that select reference cells based on signal statistics, but reformulates the problem in feature space using inner-product similarity or a small learned network. The paper explicitly positions itself against deformable convolutions, arguing that DefConv's prior-prediction paradigm is task-mismatched for radar because interference is high-entropy and must be estimated posteriorly rather than predicted from geometric priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that the fixed guard bandwidth in PKC is a genuine bottleneck for radar semantic segmentation, and that unit-level adaptive PRF adjustment consistently outperforms both the fixed-PRF baseline and manual global PRF tuning across multiple radar types (77 GHz FMCW, 17 GHz Ku-band CW) and application scenarios (autonomous driving, UAV surveillance, marine monitoring). The FiTOS fine-tuning strategy—using a simple confidence threshold to selectively disable PRF adaptation on background units—is a practical and interpretable trick that further narrows the gap to the best model without extra parameters. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to pulse-Doppler radars (acknowledged as a limitation), whether the adaptive PRF mechanism transfers to other radar tasks beyond segmentation (e.g., detection, tracking), and whether the modest 1–2% mIoU gains over PKCIn-Net scale to more challenging or larger-scale radar datasets.
