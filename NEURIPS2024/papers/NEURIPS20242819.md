## Problem

Existing knowledge boundary perception for LLMs relies on binary answerable/unanswerable classification of close-ended questions, leaving semi-open-ended questions—containing both easy common answers and hard ambiguous answers—largely unexplored. These ambiguous answers frequently exceed the LLM's knowledge boundary and trigger hallucination, yet they are difficult to discover because mainstream black-box LLMs like GPT-4 do not expose output probabilities needed to sample low-probability answers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a semi-open-ended question (templated as 'Tell me a list of...'), the full set of existing answers already produced by the target LLM (GPT-4, ~52 answers per question with the last 25% designated as ambiguous), and the auxiliary model's final-layer embedding matrix. It outputs newly discovered ambiguous answers that are then verified via RAG-based evaluation and LLM self-evaluation, yielding a four-way categorization: unqualified answers, inaccurate evaluations, hidden correct answers, and unexpected wrong evaluations. The constructed dataset spans 953 questions across 32 domains with an average of 13 ambiguous answers per question.

## Method

The core mechanism uses an open-sourced auxiliary model (LLaMA-2-13B) to approximate the target LLM's generation probabilities, circumventing the inaccessibility of black-box token probabilities. The method identifies the first token of each existing answer as an 'anchor token,' constructs a uniform probability distribution over these anchors, and computes the nearest semantic representation via the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of the auxiliary model's embedding matrix (δx = E⁺Δy). This estimated representation is then used to subtractively reduce the generation probability of semantically related tokens (y₂ = y₁ − λEδx, λ=80), suppressing high-probability answers already known to the target LLM and forcing the auxiliary model to produce low-probability ambiguous answers. Verification compares the target LLM's self-evaluation (prompted with monetary incentive and chain-of-thought instructions) against ground truth from an Internet-connected RAG-based evaluator, categorizing all discrepancies into four types of knowledge boundary violations.

## Results

On the Semi-open-ended QA Dataset (200 sampled questions), the proposed method using LLaMA-2-13B as auxiliary model achieves the best EM of 0.481 and F1 of 0.587 in discovering ambiguous answers, while also achieving the lowest AOR (0.326) and Bleu scores, indicating the most diverse and non-repetitive generation. GPT-4 performs poorly on semi-open-ended questions, generating incorrect or unverifiable answers in 82.90% of questions, with 40.15% of its ambiguous answers being unqualified and 28.47% receiving inaccurate self-evaluations. The auxiliary model LLaMA-2-13B discovers 75.12% hidden correct answers neglected by GPT-4 and 62.43% unexpected wrong evaluations, demonstrating its effectiveness in probing GPT-4's knowledge boundary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: Prompt (following Wang et al., 2023) which directly prompts the auxiliary model to generate more answers, and MASK (inspired by Zhang et al., 2023) which reduces generation probabilities of anchor tokens from existing answers. The proposed method outperforms Prompt on all metrics, achieving EM of 0.481 vs. 0.300 and AOR of 0.326 vs. 0.490, showing that direct prompting produces highly repetitive answers. Compared to MASK with the same LLaMA-2-13B auxiliary model, the proposed method achieves a higher EM (0.481 vs. 0.470) and lower AOR (0.326 vs. 0.344), while matching F1 at 0.587, demonstrating that estimating and reducing probabilities of semantically near-duplicate answers (via Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse) is more effective than simply masking anchor tokens. The ablation further confirms that removing the inverse matrix step degrades performance to MASK-level results, validating the contribution of the semantic propagation mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends knowledge boundary perception for LLMs from the binary close-ended QA paradigm (answerable vs. unanswerable) to semi-open-ended questions that admit multiple correct answers of varying difficulty. Conceptually, it repurposes representation engineering ideas—specifically, the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of an auxiliary model's output embedding matrix—to estimate and suppress high-probability answer tokens, thereby forcing the auxiliary model to generate low-probability ambiguous answers. The evaluation protocol borrows from RAG-based verification (inspired by Web-GPT) and LLM self-evaluation with chain-of-thought prompting, but applies them jointly to categorize four distinct types of knowledge-boundary violations rather than a single binary label.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that GPT-4 produces unqualified answers in 82.90% of semi-open-ended questions while admitting its knowledge limit in only ~7% of cases highlights a critical gap between model confidence and actual competence, with direct implications for hallucination detection and selective retrieval systems. The demonstration that a 13B open-source auxiliary model can surface roughly half the ambiguous answers beyond GPT-4's boundary suggests a practical, cost-effective strategy for probing black-box LLMs without access to their logits. Key limitations include evaluation on a single target model, absence of statistical significance testing due to expensive human annotation, and the inherent incompleteness of the answer set for questions with potentially thousands of valid responses.
