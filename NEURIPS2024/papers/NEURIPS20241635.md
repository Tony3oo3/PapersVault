## Problem

While fMRI-to-image reconstruction has achieved high fidelity using CLIP and diffusion models, fMRI-to-video reconstruction remains largely unsolved due to the differential temporal resolution between fMRI (2s) and video (high FPS) and the difficulty of simultaneously decoding high-level semantics and low-level perceptual flows. The prior SOTA, MinD-Video, addressed semantic conditioning but lacked any mechanism for low-level visual detailing, producing videos with poor smoothness and inconsistent motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241635/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes preprocessed fMRI BOLD signals (2s temporal resolution, 4s hemodynamic delay correction, restricted to stimulus-activated voxels in visual cortex) as input, split into 2-second clips. Each clip's single fMRI volume is extended to N_f per-frame fMRIs via an Inception Extension MLP. The output is a reconstructed video at 8FPS (up to 6s with multi-fMRI fusion), produced by a frozen T2V diffusion model conditioned on three intermediate signals: a blurry rough video (perception level), a high-quality keyframe image (semantic level), and a BLIP-2-generated text caption.

## Method

NeuroClips decouples video reconstruction into two trainable components that feed a frozen T2V diffusion model (AnimateDiff) at inference. The Perception Reconstructor uses Inception Extension and a Temporal Upsampling module (spatial 3D convolution + temporal attention with learnable residual mixing) to map fMRI embeddings into the Stable Diffusion VAE latent space, producing a blurry but temporally consistent video trained with MAE and bidirectional contrastive loss. The Semantics Reconstructor applies ridge regression for fMRI dimensionality reduction, aligns fMRI embeddings to OpenCLIP ViT-bigG/14 keyframe embeddings via BiMixCo (MixCo-augmented contrastive learning), generates reconstruction embeddings through a DALLE-2-style diffusion prior, and further refines them with BLIP-2 text captions via a Reftm contrastive loss. At inference, the blurry video serves as α-guidance (an intermediate noisy state reducing denoising steps), the keyframe serves as β-guidance (first-frame control via SparseCtrl), and the caption serves as γ-guidance (text prompt), enabling high-fidelity, smooth video generation without fine-tuning the video diffusion model.

## Results

On the cc2017 fMRI-video dataset, NeuroClips achieves a 128% improvement in SSIM (0.390 vs. 0.171 for MinD-Video) and an 81% improvement in the spatiotemporal CLIP-pcc metric (0.738 vs. 0.408), demonstrating substantially better pixel-level fidelity and video smoothness. At the semantic level, NeuroClips attains 0.220 video 50-way accuracy and 0.203 frame 50-way accuracy, outperforming MinD-Video (0.197 and 0.174 respectively). The method also achieves the highest PSNR (9.211) and frame 2-way accuracy (0.806) among all compared methods, while generating videos at 8 FPS (up to 6 s via multi-fMRI fusion) compared to 3 FPS for prior work.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroClips outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics. Against the most recent SOTA, MinD-Video [8], NeuroClips achieves a 128% gain in SSIM (0.390 vs. 0.171) and an 81% gain in CLIP-pcc (0.738 vs. 0.408), while also improving frame 50-way (0.203 vs. 0.174) and video 50-way (0.220 vs. 0.197) semantic accuracy; the only metric where MinD-Video edges ahead is video 2-way (0.839 vs. 0.834), which the authors attribute to multi-fMRI fusion making adjacent clips more semantically similar. Against the earlier GAN/AE-based methods (Wen [31], Wang [32], Kupershmidt [33]), NeuroClips shows large margins on all pixel-level and semantic-level metrics, reflecting the advantage of diffusion-based generation over GANs for this task. The earliest baseline, Nishimoto [3] (2011), reports only frame-level metrics and is substantially outperformed on both SSIM (0.390 vs. 0.116) and frame 2-way accuracy (0.806 vs. 0.727). Overall, NeuroClips establishes a clear new state-of-the-art, with the Perception Reconstructor driving pixel-level gains and the Semantics Reconstructor driving semantic-level gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the successful fMRI-to-image reconstruction paradigm (CLIP alignment + diffusion prior, as in MindEye2 and DALLE-2) to the video domain, departing from MinD-Video's single-path approach by decomposing reconstruction into a Perception Reconstructor (low-level, blurry video via temporal upsampling) and a Semantics Reconstructor (high-level keyframes via contrastive learning and diffusion prior). The inference stage composes these outputs with a frozen AnimateDiff T2V model, using ControlNet for keyframe conditioning and a reparameterization trick to inject the blurry video as partial-noise guidance. The key conceptual departure from prior fMRI-to-video work is the neuroscience-motivated separation of low-level perceptual flows from high-level semantic content, grounded in the brain's discrete keyframe perception and hierarchical visual processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeuroClips establishes a new SOTA for fMRI-to-video reconstruction with substantial margins (128% SSIM, 81% spatiotemporal improvement over MinD-Video) and pioneers multi-fMRI fusion for 6-second video generation without additional training. However, the approach remains limited to 3 subjects on a single dataset, relies on keyframes as an intermediate representation that constrains generative flexibility, and fails on cross-scene fMRI. Open questions include whether a model can reconstruct video directly from CLIP latent space without keyframe intermediates, how the method scales to larger and more diverse fMRI-video datasets, and whether the perception-semantic decomposition generalizes beyond the cc2017 domain.
