## Problem

Video diffusion models (e.g., SVD, AnimateDiff) require 40+ GB of peak GPU memory and substantial compute to generate even short clips, making them infeasible on consumer-grade hardware. Existing compression techniques such as pruning, quantization, and distillation demand costly retraining or fine-tuning to recover quality, and zero-shot application causes severe degradation. The paper addresses the gap of a training-free inference framework that simultaneously reduces peak memory and accelerates generation without sacrificing output quality.

## Input / Output

The framework operates at inference time on pretrained video diffusion models (SVD, SVD-XT, AnimateDiff) without modifying weights. For SVD/SVD-XT, input is a conditioning image plus a text prompt producing 14 or 25 frames at 576×1024; for AnimateDiff, input is a text prompt producing 16 frames at 512×512. The method is resolution- and frame-count-agnostic, slicing the 5-D latent feature maps (B×T×C×H×W) into smaller sub-features whose dimensions are determined by the slicing configuration (k spatial slices or k_h×k_w temporal slices).

## Method

Streamlined Inference combines three training-free components. (1) Feature Slicer partitions the 5-D latent tensor into k sub-features along the batch×frame axis for spatial layers (Conv2D, Attention, Norm) and along the height×width axis for temporal layers (Conv3D), preserving temporal coherence. (2) Operator Grouping aggregates consecutive homogeneous operators into a single group so that each sub-feature's intermediate result flows directly to the next operator without waiting for full re-aggregation, converting the theoretical memory saving of slicing into actual peak-memory reduction; a pipelining schedule then overlaps operators within a group to recover the ~10% latency overhead of the naive for-loop. (3) Step Rehash exploits the empirically observed high similarity of features immediately after temporal layers in the final up-sampling block across adjacent denoising steps, reusing the previous step's temporal-layer output and skipping the bulk of the U-Net computation for non-key steps selected by a similarity-threshold search algorithm. Together these reduce AnimateDiff peak memory from 41.7 GB to 11 GB and SVD-XT from 61.2 GB to 36.3 GB while keeping FVD and CLIP-Score within a few percent of the original.

## Results

On AnimateDiff at 576×1024, Streamlined Inference reduces peak memory from 41.71 GB to 11.07 GB (3.8×) and latency from 24.38 s to 15.15 s (1.6×), while maintaining FVD of 784.5 vs. 758.7 for the original. On SVD at 576×1024, peak memory drops from 39.49 GB to 23.42 GB and latency from 23.29 s to 14.24 s, with FVD of 340.6 vs. 307.7. On SVD-XT at 576×1024, peak memory is reduced from 61.17 GB to 36.32 GB and latency from 40.77 s to 25.47 s, with FVD of 424.7 vs. 387.9. CLIP-Scores remain within ~0.3 of the original across all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is DeepCache [Ma et al., CVPR 2024], a training-free feature-reuse method. With the same 13 full computation steps, Step Rehash consistently outperforms DeepCache: on SVD (UCF-101), Step Rehash achieves FVD 340.6 vs. DeepCache's 394.0 and CLIP-Score 28.98 vs. 28.57; on AnimateDiff (UCF-101), Step Rehash achieves FVD 784.5 vs. 840.2 and CLIP-Score 28.71 vs. 28.15. Against the original (unmodified) inference, Streamlined Inference trades a small quality drop (FVD increases by ~10–11%) for substantial memory and speed gains, e.g., 3.8× peak memory reduction and 1.6× latency reduction on AnimateDiff at 576×1024. Compared to Naïve Slicing (clip-by-clip inference), the proposed method dramatically improves quality (FVD 784.5 vs. 2403.9 on AnimateDiff) while also being 2.6× faster, demonstrating that naive temporal partitioning destroys temporal coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a training-free, inference-time-only niche for video diffusion efficiency, deliberately departing from the dominant compression literature (pruning, quantization, distillation) that requires costly retraining or fine-tuning. Its Feature Slicer and Operator Grouping components repurpose the idea of spatial feature partitioning from image-model memory optimization but extend it to the 5-D spatiotemporal tensor structure of video U-Nets, where naive temporal slicing breaks coherence. Step Rehash is conceptually closest to DeepCache [26] (reusing features across denoising steps), but targets the temporal layers in the final up-sampling block specifically, motivated by the observation that those layers exhibit the highest inter-step similarity. The pipeline technique for operator groups is a systems-level contribution that has no direct analogue in prior video diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing AnimateDiff peak memory from ~42 GB to ~11 GB makes video diffusion feasible on a single consumer GPU (2080 Ti), removing the 'Out of Memory' barrier that currently forces users to generate short, low-resolution clips. Because the method is training-free and architecture-agnostic (demonstrated on SVD, SVD-XT, and AnimateDiff), it can be adopted without data access or retraining, sidestepping privacy and compute concerns. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that efficiency gains are bounded by the baseline architecture's design; open questions include whether the slicing/grouping heuristics transfer cleanly to next-generation models (e.g., Sora-scale, DiT-based video diffusion) and whether the step-rehash threshold strategy generalizes across different sampler schedules and guidance configurations.
