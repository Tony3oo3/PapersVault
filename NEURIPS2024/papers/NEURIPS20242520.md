## Problem

Fenchel-Young losses are separately convex and each is implicitly tied to a specific link function (e.g., logistic loss → soft argmax), but it was unclear whether multiple distinct convex losses could share the same link function. This paper asks whether one can construct new separately convex losses that are strictly tighter (lower-bounding) than Fenchel-Young losses while preserving the identical prediction link, thereby challenging the assumption of a unique convex loss per link function.

## Input / Output

The model is a generalized linear map x → ŷ_Ω(Wx) with W ∈ R^{k×d}, where the input is a feature vector x ∈ R^d and the output is a label proportion vector in the probability simplex Δ^k. The ground-truth target y ∈ Δ^k lives in the primal space while the model output Wx lives in the dual space; the link function ŷ_Ω = ∇Ω* (e.g., softmax or sparsemax) maps the dual-space score back to the simplex. The loss is evaluated between y and Wx, and training minimizes a regularized sum of losses over n samples using L-BFGS.

## Method

The Fitzpatrick loss L_{F[∂Ω]}(y,θ) = F[∂Ω](y,θ) − ⟨y,θ⟩ is built from the Fitzpatrick function of the subdifferential ∂Ω, a classical object in maximal monotone operator theory. The Fitzpatrick function satisfies a refined Fenchel-Young inequality ⟨y,θ⟩ ≤ F[∂Ω](y,θ) ≤ Ω(y)+Ω*(θ), which immediately yields 0 ≤ L_{F[∂Ω]} ≤ L_{Ω⊕Ω*} while both losses share the same zero-set (link function) ŷ_Ω = ∂Ω*. A key structural result shows the Fitzpatrick loss equals a Fenchel-Young loss generated by a target-dependent modified function Ω_y(y') = Ω(y') + D_Ω(y,y'), where D_Ω is the generalized Bregman divergence, inheriting all known Fenchel-Young properties. Two concrete instantiations are derived: the Fitzpatrick logistic loss (whose gradient involves the Lambert W function and a one-dimensional root equation for a scalar λ*) and the Fitzpatrick sparsemax loss (whose gradient uses the Euclidean projection P_{Δ^k}((y+θ)/2)). Experiments on 11 multi-label datasets show the Fitzpatrick sparsemax loss is competitive with sparsemax, while the Fitzpatrick logistic loss is comparable to logistic loss but slightly more computationally demanding.

## Results

The paper evaluates Fitzpatrick losses against their Fenchel-Young counterparts on 11 multi-label datasets using mean squared error (MSE, also called Brier score) for label proportion estimation. The Fitzpatrick sparsemax loss achieves the best MSE on Birds (0.513 vs. 0.531 for sparsemax), Scene (0.355 vs. 0.363), and Unfair (0.148 vs. 0.149), while the Fitzpatrick logistic loss wins on Ecthr A (0.423 vs. 0.431) and Emotions (0.320 vs. 0.327). Overall, the two families of losses are comparable, with each winning significantly on only 2 out of 11 datasets. The authors note that the Fitzpatrick logistic loss requires solving a one-dimensional root equation (via bisection) at each evaluation, making it slightly more computationally demanding than the standard logistic loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two pairs of losses: Fitzpatrick-sparsemax vs. the sparsemax loss (Martins & Astudillo, ICML 2016) and Fitzpatrick-logistic vs. the logistic loss (Blondel et al., JMLR 2020), both of which are Fenchel-Young losses sharing the same link function (sparse argmax and soft argmax, respectively). For the sparsemax pair, the Fitzpatrick variant wins significantly (≥0.005 MSE gap) on 2 of 11 datasets (Birds: 0.513 vs. 0.531; Scene: 0.355 vs. 0.363) and ties or narrowly wins on several others, while the original sparsemax loss wins significantly on only 1 dataset (Mediamill: 0.191 vs. 0.203). For the logistic pair, the Fitzpatrick variant wins significantly on 2 datasets (Ecthr A: 0.423 vs. 0.431; Emotions: 0.320 vs. 0.327) while the standard logistic loss wins significantly on 2 datasets (Mediamill: 0.207 vs. 0.220; Scene: 0.344 vs. 0.368). The authors conclude that the Fitzpatrick sparsemax loss is a serious contender to the sparsemax loss, especially when sparse predictions matter, while the standard logistic loss remains the preferred choice for the soft argmax link due to its lower computational cost (no root-finding required).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Fenchel-Young loss framework (Blondel et al., JMLR 2020) by introducing Fitzpatrick losses, which are tighter lower bounds of Fenchel-Young losses while preserving the same link function (e.g., soft argmax, sparse argmax). The construction draws on the Fitzpatrick function, a classical object in maximal monotone operator theory (Fitzpatrick 1988; Bauschke et al. 2005) that had previously been used only for representing monotone operators and constructing primal-primal divergences (Burachik & Martínez-Legaz 2018), but never for building primal-dual machine learning losses. A key theoretical contribution is showing that Fitzpatrick losses are equivalent to Fenchel-Young losses generated by a modified, target-dependent function Ω_y = Ω + D_Ω(y,·), unifying the two families under a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the implicit assumption that each link function (softmax, sparsemax, etc.) admits a unique separately convex loss, opening the door to a richer family of losses per link. However, the empirical results on 11 multi-label datasets show only marginal differences: the Fitzpatrick logistic loss is slightly more computationally demanding (requiring a Lambert W function and a 1D root solve) without consistent gains, and the Fitzpatrick sparsemax loss is comparable to sparsemax. Open questions include calibration guarantees for Fitzpatrick losses, whether the tighter bound translates to benefits in non-convex or deep settings, and whether more efficient training algorithms can close the computational gap.
