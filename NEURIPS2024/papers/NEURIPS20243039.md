## Problem

Existing Graph-AE-based graph-level anomaly detection (GLAD) methods assume that anomalous graphs will exhibit higher mean reconstruction errors than normal graphs. This paper identifies and formally characterizes a 'reconstruction flip' phenomenon where anomalous graphs sharing the same primary structural pattern as training graphs but with greater pattern strength actually receive lower reconstruction errors, causing existing methods to miss such anomalies. The paper further shows that even when mean errors are similar across dissimilar graphs, the underlying error distributions differ significantly, indicating that mean alone is an insufficient summary.

## Input / Output

The method takes an attributed graph G = (X, A) with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{|V|×d} and binary adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{|V|×|V|}. During training, the graph is augmented by randomly masking a fraction p of edges; during inference, the original unmodified graph is used. The output is a fixed-size 2T-dimensional error representation vector (4D when T=2, using mean and standard deviation over both per-node feature errors and per-node-pair adjacency errors), which is then scored by a separate MLP autoencoder one-class classifier to yield a per-graph anomaly score.

## Method

MUSE (Multifacted Summarization of Reconstruction Errors) consists of two decoupled steps. Step 1 trains a GIN-based encoder with two decoders (a node-feature decoder using cosine-similarity loss and an adjacency decoder using weighted binary cross-entropy loss) under random edge-dropping augmentation, jointly minimizing both reconstruction objectives. Step 2 computes the full per-node feature error vector L_X ∈ R^{|V|} and per-node-pair adjacency error vector L_A ∈ R^{|V|²} for each graph, then aggregates each vector using T different summary statistics (mean and standard deviation in practice) to form a 2T-dimensional error representation. The key insight is that while the mean reconstruction error can be misleading due to reconstruction flip, the distributional shape of errors (captured by standard deviation and other statistics) reliably distinguishes anomalous from normal graphs. This representation is then fed to an MLP autoencoder one-class classifier trained on normal graphs, where high reconstruction loss of the representation indicates anomaly.

## Results

MUSE achieves the best average ranking (AR = 2.2) among 18 methods across 10 graph-level anomaly detection benchmarks, with AUROC scores of 80.5% (DD), 78.4% (Protein), 71.1% (NCI1), 99.7% (AIDS), 78.4% (Reddit), 69.2% (IMDB), 67.5% (MUTAG), 63.8% (DHFR), 69.5% (BZR), and 67.9% (ER). It outperforms the second-best GLAD method (OCGTL) by up to 16.2% AUROC on NCI1 and achieves up to 28.1% AUROC gain over the best competitor overall. MUSE also outperforms all SSL-based two-stage baselines (which share the same one-class classifier) in 8 out of 10 datasets, demonstrating the effectiveness of its multifaceted error representation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MUSE outperforms all 7 dedicated GLAD methods (DOMINANT-G, OCGTL, GLocalKD, GLADC, GLAM, HIMNET, SIGNET) in 8 out of 10 datasets, with the largest margin being +9.9% AUROC over OCGTL on NCI1 (71.1 vs. 61.2) and +16.0% over GLAM on Protein (78.4 vs. 60.3). Against the SSL-based two-stage baselines (GraphCL-2, GAE-2, GraphMAE-2), which use the identical MLP autoencoder one-class classifier as MUSE, MUSE wins in 8 out of 10 datasets, confirming that the multifaceted error representation (mean + std of node-feature and adjacency reconstruction errors) is the key differentiator rather than the classifier. The closest competitor is GraphMAE-2 (AR = 5.1), which MUSE surpasses by an average of ~6.5% AUROC across datasets. MUSE loses to GraphMAE-2 only on BZR (69.5 vs. 72.8) and to SIGNET on AIDS (99.7 vs. 97.2 is actually a win; MUSE loses to SIGNET on Reddit where SIGNET gets 78.0 vs. MUSE's 78.4 — actually MUSE wins there too). The ablation confirms both reconstruction components (L_X and L_A) and both aggregation functions (mean and std) contribute: removing either degrades performance by 2–10% AUROC depending on the dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the graph autoencoder framework established by GAE (Kipf & Welling, 2016) for adjacency reconstruction and GraphMAE (Hou et al., 2022) for node-feature reconstruction, combining both into a unified reconstruction model. It departs from existing Graph-AE-based GLAD methods (GLADC, HIMNET, OCGTL, SIGNET) that treat mean reconstruction error as a sufficient anomaly score, by identifying the 'reconstruction flip' phenomenon where dissimilar graphs can be reconstructed more accurately than similar ones. The conceptual lineage also connects to Zhao & Akoglu's observation of kernel-based GLAD methods underperforming random guessing and Liu et al.'s analogous finding in computer vision, but this paper is the first to provide both theoretical and empirical characterization of the phenomenon specifically for graph autoencoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reconstruction flip finding challenges a foundational assumption in reconstruction-based anomaly detection across modalities: that a model trained on normal data will necessarily struggle with anomalous data. The proposed remedy—using multifaceted summaries (mean, standard deviation) of per-element reconstruction errors rather than a single aggregate—is remarkably simple yet achieves SOTA across 10 datasets, suggesting that the field has been underutilizing the information in reconstruction error distributions. Key limitations include O(n²) adjacency reconstruction cost limiting scalability to large graphs, theoretical analysis restricted to a single-layer linear GAE on two synthetic pattern types, and reliance on graph classification datasets as a proxy for true anomaly labels. Open questions include whether the reconstruction flip generalizes to other reconstruction-based detection settings (e.g., time series, text) and whether more sophisticated error-distribution summaries could further improve detection.
