## Problem

When matching partial surfaces to their full counterparts using isometry-based losses, geodesic distances between point pairs can be inflated because the shortest path on the full surface may traverse a missing region. Existing criteria for filtering out such inconsistent pairs (e.g., C_T from Bronstein et al.) are overly conservative, discarding a large fraction of pairs whose distances are actually preserved. The paper asks: can we design a less restrictive criterion that recovers significantly more consistent pairs without admitting truly inconsistent ones?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full surface mesh X and a partial surface mesh Y' (with holes or cuts), using per-vertex xyz coordinates and estimated normals as raw features. It produces a row-normalized soft correspondence matrix P of size |V_X| × |V_Y'| (full-to-partial), where each row sums to one but columns need not, since vertices in missing regions of Y have no counterpart. A key intermediate artifact is the |V_Y'| × |V_Y'| mask matrix M (binary or soft) encoding which point pairs are guaranteed consistent, computed once as a preprocessing step.

## Method

The core contribution is the wormhole criterion C_W, which declares a pair (P, Q) on the partial surface consistent if d_{Y'}(P,Q) ≤ min over all boundary pairs (B₁,B₂) of [d_{Y'}(P,B₁) + d_{Y'}(Q,B₂) + d_E(B₁,B₂)], where d_E is the Euclidean distance in the embedding space. This generalizes the prior C_T criterion by treating the straight-line Euclidean segment between two boundary points as a 'wormhole' lower bound on the geodesic distance through the missing region, provably guaranteeing consistency (Theorem 2). The criterion yields a binary mask M (or a soft mask M^s = min(K_ij / D_ij, 1)) that weights a geodesic distance preservation loss, so only guaranteed pairs contribute to training. The full pipeline uses DiffusionNet with shared weights for feature refinement, Softmax similarity for direct correspondence estimation (bypassing the FM layer), the masked geodesic loss with λ_geo=10³, and an orthogonality regularization on the FM matrix extracted from P, trained unsupervised with Adam and cosine annealing.

## Results

On the SHREC'16 HOLES benchmark (the most challenging partial matching setting), Wormhole achieves a geodesic error of 6.6 (×100) after refinement, surpassing the previous unsupervised SOTA DirectMatchNet (7.9) and even the supervised DPFM (11.9). On PFAUST-H, Wormhole reaches 6.7, beating DirectMatchNet (7.9) and matching supervised DPFM (6.8). On the easier CUTS dataset, improvements are more modest (4.3 vs. 4.7 for DirectMatchNet LPF), as planar cuts preserve most geodesic distances and the wormhole mask is nearly identical to the unmasked loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wormhole outperforms the closest unsupervised competitor DirectMatchNet [10] on the hardest benchmarks: HOLES (6.6 vs. 7.9) and PFAUST-H (6.7 vs. 7.9), while DirectMatchNet LPF retains a slight edge on the easier CUTS dataset (4.7 vs. 4.3). Against supervised DPFM [3], Wormhole wins decisively on HOLES (6.6 vs. 11.9) and PFAUST-H (6.7 vs. 6.8) but loses on CUTS (4.3 vs. 1.8), where the simpler topology makes supervised labels more effective. RobustFMnet [18] (both unsupervised and pretrained variants) is consistently outperformed on HOLES (6.6 vs. 18.8/8.2). Axiomatic methods PFM [45] and FSP [34] are far behind, with geodesic errors 3–5× higher. In the MDS/dimensionality-reduction comparison, WHCIE produces embeddings far closer to the true flat surface than Isomap [57], MLLE [62], Laplacian eigenmaps [5], UMAP [37], or the prior TCIE [47], which all show visible distortion near holes and cuts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geodesic distance preservation loss of Halimi et al. [25] by introducing a mask that filters out inconsistent point pairs on partial surfaces. The key conceptual departure is from the prior criterion C_T (Bronstein et al. [12], Rosman et al. [46]), which used only intrinsic distances to the boundary to certify consistent pairs and was shown to be overly conservative. The proposed 'wormhole' criterion C_W augments this with the extrinsic Euclidean distance between boundary points as a lower bound on the geodesic distance through the missing region, nearly doubling the number of guaranteed consistent pairs. The pipeline itself is adapted from the authors' own DirectMatchNet [10], replacing its primary loss with the masked wormhole loss while retaining the DiffusionNet feature extractor and Softmax similarity correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The wormhole criterion provides a clean, theoretically grounded (Theorem 2) way to identify which pairwise geodesic distances remain valid under partiality, and its practical impact is demonstrated by SOTA results on SHREC'16 HOLES and PFAUST-H where it even surpasses supervised baselines. However, the criterion still cannot recover all consistent pairs, and the paper is limited to partial-to-full matching; extending to partial-to-partial correspondence and to higher-dimensional boundaries remains open. The generalization to non-standard Riemannian metrics (Appendix A.3) and the MDS application (WHCIE) suggest the criterion has broader utility beyond the primary shape matching task.
