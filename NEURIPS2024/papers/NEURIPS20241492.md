## Problem

Existing automatic jailbreaking methods (gradient-based optimization, iterative LLM revision) discover a narrow range of attacks and struggle to find even two unique successful attacks, while open-source safety training datasets are small-scale and lack adversarial complexity. This leaves frontier LLMs' vulnerabilities under-explored and provides insufficient training signal to build models that robustly refuse harmful queries—both vanilla and adversarial—without over-refusing benign ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241492/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The MINE stage consumes single-turn conversations from LMSYS-CHAT-1M and WILDCHAT flagged by the OpenAI Moderation API, filtered through a lightly safety-trained model and Llama-Guard classifier, yielding 16,854 adversarial prompts from which GPT-4 extracts and clusters 105K tactic instances into 5,688 unique clusters via sentence-embedding deduplication (threshold 0.75). The COMPOSE stage takes a vanilla harmful prompt and a randomly sampled subset of 2–7 tactics, producing an adversarial attack candidate that is then gated by off-topic and low-risk binary classifiers. The final WILDJAILBREAK dataset outputs 262K prompt-response pairs organized into four contrastive splits (vanilla/adversarial × harmful/benign), with harmful responses being detailed refusals and benign responses being compliant continuations.

## Method

WILDTEAMING is a two-stage red-teaming framework: (1) MINE automatically discovers jailbreak tactics from in-the-wild user-chatbot logs by using GPT-4 to extract the core vanilla request and identify all revision strategies (existing and novel) in each adversarial prompt, then deduplicating via sentence-embedding clustering; (2) COMPOSE samples subsets of mined tactics and instructs an off-the-shelf LLM (Mixtral-8×7B or GPT-4) to apply them to a vanilla harmful prompt, followed by lightweight off-topic and low-risk pruning to retain faithful, on-target attacks. This compositional approach yields up to 4.6× more diverse successful attacks than PAIR with 40% fewer trials. For safety training, WILDJAILBREAK pairs each adversarial harmful prompt with the refusal response from its vanilla counterpart, and includes adversarial benign queries to counteract exaggerated safety. Ablations on Llama2-7B fine-tuned with Tulu2Mix + WILDJAILBREAK show all four data components are necessary for balanced safety (robust refusal without over-refusal), and that scaling safety data from 2K to 60K items progressively improves both vanilla and adversarial robustness without degrading general capabilities across 15+ downstream tasks.

## Results

WILDTEAMING achieves 93.1–98.1% attack success rate (ASR) across six target models (Vicuna-7B through GPT-4), matching or exceeding PAIR (88.7–97.5%) while requiring fewer attack trials (2.37–8.61 vs. 3.05–9.64) and producing more natural text (PPL 7.96–8.77 vs. 9.33–9.78). On the new diversity metric ASR₃₀^×⁵, WILDTEAMING scores 60.1–89.2%, outperforming PAIR by 4.6–25.6 points while using 3.8–5.5 fewer queries. For safety training, the model fine-tuned with WILDJAILBREAK achieves 98.4% accuracy on the WILDJAILBREAK adversarial test set and reduces HarmBench ASR from 59.1% (T2M-no-refusal) to 3.1%, with only a −1.7% drop on AlpacaEval v1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PAIR (Chao et al., 2023), WILDTEAMING achieves comparable or slightly lower single-attack ASR (e.g., 93.1 vs. 94.3 on Vicuna-7B) but substantially outperforms on diversity: ASR₃₀^×⁵ is 88.1 vs. 59.5 on Vicuna-7B and 60.1 vs. 44.9 on GPT-4, while using 3.8–5.5 fewer queries per unique attack. Against AutoDAN (Liu et al., 2023), WILDTEAMING wins on ASR (93.1 vs. 89.3 on Vicuna-7B), naturalness (PPL 8.65 vs. 13.74), and decisively on diversity—AutoDAN's Trial₃₀^×⁵ is ∞, meaning it cannot find a second unique successful attack within 30 trials. Against GCG (Zou et al., 2023), WILDTEAMING is applicable to black-box models (GCG requires white-box access), achieves higher ASR (93.1 vs. 89.9 on Vicuna-7B), and produces far more natural text (PPL 8.65 vs. 4062.57); GCG is also too slow (~15 hours per 30 candidates on one A100) for diversity evaluation. For safety training, the WILDJAILBREAK-augmented model (T2M-no-refusal + WJ) achieves 85.1% relative improvement on HarmBench over the original Tulu2Mix baseline and 71.9% improvement on DAN jailbreaks, while the T2M-public-safety baseline (using HH-RLHF, Safe-RLHF, DAN) yields only mediocre gains (HarmBench ASR 66.0% vs. 3.1% for WILDJAILBREAK), confirming that WILDJAILBREAK's scale and adversarial diversity are critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior jailbreaking methods (GCG, AutoDAN, PAIR, TAP) that either optimize single attacks via gradients or iteratively revise prompts with an LLM in the loop, by instead mining 5.7K unique jailbreak tactic clusters from in-the-wild user-chatbot logs (LMSYS-1M, WildChat) and composing them combinatorially to generate diverse adversarial attacks. For safety training, it extends the line of work from small-scale pairwise preference datasets (HH-RLHF, Safe-RLHF) and fixed-template jailbreaks (DAN) to a 262K-example sequential instruction-tuning resource that simultaneously covers vanilla and adversarial variants of both harmful and benign queries. The combinatorial composition of human-devised tactics, rather than algorithmic optimization or fixed templates, is the key methodological departure that enables both broader red-teaming coverage and scalable synthetic data generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WILDJAILBREAK is likely to become a standard open-source resource for safety training, filling a critical gap where frontier models keep safety data closed even when weights are open. The finding that all four data components (vanilla/adversarial × harmful/benign) are individually necessary for balanced safety—without over-refusal or capability degradation—provides concrete guidance for practitioners building safety-aligned models. Open questions remain: whether synthetically composed attacks fully capture the combinatorial diversity of real human jailbreaks, whether SFT is optimal versus DPO/PPO for safety alignment, and whether the scaling trends observed at 7B/13B hold for frontier-scale models. The paper also highlights that certain context-dependent harms can only be guarded by external layers, limiting what in-model safety training can achieve.
