## Problem

Existing visual relationship segmentation models handle human-object interaction detection, panoptic scene graph generation, and referring relationships as separate tasks, and prior unified frameworks (e.g., UniVRD, RLIPv2) are two-stage, require additional large-scale pretraining, and lack support for promptable querying and open-vocabulary recognition. No single one-stage model simultaneously supports standard, promptable, and open-vocabulary visual relationship segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243418/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts 640×640 RGB images and optionally a structured text prompt specifying one, two, or all three elements of a <subject, predicate, object> triplet (e.g., '<s>person</s><p>ride</p>'). It outputs up to 100 triplets, each containing a subject segmentation mask, an object segmentation mask, a subject category, an object category, and a predicate category. For promptable VRS, the top-10 triplets by similarity to the textual query are returned; for standard VRS, all triplets above a confidence threshold are produced.

## Method

FleVRS is a one-stage Transformer architecture built on a dual-query system: 100–200 learned latent queries (and optionally CLIP-encoded textual queries for promptable mode) are processed by a 9-layer relationship decoder with masked attention over per-pixel embeddings from a DaViT pixel decoder. The image encoder is Focal-T/L, and the model is initialized from pre-trained weights of [109]. For open-vocabulary support, classification heads are replaced by matching predicted class embeddings against CLIP textual features of candidate categories, enabling zero-shot recognition of unseen objects and predicates. Training converts bounding-box annotations to segmentation masks via SAM (filtered by IoU > 0.2) and uses Hungarian matching with focal loss, dice loss, and cross-entropy loss; promptable VRS adds a grounding loss aligning triplet embeddings with the textual query's last-token feature.

## Results

On HICO-DET (Default setting), FleVRS achieves 38.1/40.5 box/mask mAP (Full), 33.0/34.9 (Rare), and 39.5/42.4 (Non-Rare), outperforming all single-stage HOI methods by at least 3 mAP over GEN-VLKT (35.0/35.6). On V-COCO, it reaches 65.2/66.5 APS#1role and 66.5/67.9 APS#2role. For promptable VRS on VRD, FleVRS achieves 0.568 sIoU for subject localization, substantially exceeding SSAS (0.335). In open-vocabulary HOI detection, FleVRS attains 31.71 mAP on unseen objects, surpassing GEN-VLKT (25.63) by +6.1 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among single-stage HOI methods, FleVRS (38.1/40.5 mAP_F) clearly outperforms GEN-VLKT (35.0/35.6), ERNet (35.9/–), and CDN (32.1/33.9) on HICO-DET, with the largest margin over GEN-VLKT at +3.1 box mAP. However, FleVRS trails the two-stage RLIPv2 (45.1/48.6) and PVIC (44.3/–), which leverage large-scale pretraining on additional detection data (VG, Objects365) and a separately trained detector. On V-COCO, FleVRS (65.2/66.5) is competitive but falls short of MUREN (68.8/68.2) and RLIPv2 (72.1/71.7). For panoptic SGG on PSG, FleVRS (31.0/18.3 R/mR@50) is competitive with PSGTR (32.1/20.3) but significantly behind HiLo (48.7/37.6), which exploits high/low-frequency relation modeling. In promptable VRS, FleVRS dramatically outperforms SSAS on subject localization (0.568 vs. 0.335 sIoU) while matching it on object localization (0.364 vs. 0.363). For open-vocabulary HOI, FleVRS surpasses GEN-VLKT across all four settings, with the largest gain on unseen objects (+6.1 mAP Full) and unseen verbs (+4.4 mAP Full).

## Relation to Prior Work

FleVRS builds on the Mask2Former architecture (masked attention, pixel decoder) and the generalized decoding framework of Zou et al. (2023) for its query-based Transformer decoder, repurposing them from generic segmentation to relationship triplet prediction. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (RLIPv2, UniVRD) that detects objects first and then decodes relationships, instead using a one-stage design where each latent query directly outputs a <subject, predicate, object> triplet. The paper also extends referring-relationship methods (SSAS, CPARR) by relaxing the requirement for complete triplet prompts to allow partial specifications, and generalizes open-vocabulary HOI detection (GEN-VLKT) to the broader panoptic SGG setting via CLIP-based textual feature matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying standard, promptable, and open-vocabulary VRS in a single one-stage model, FleVRS eliminates the need for task-specific architectures and provides a more natural interface for interactive scene understanding. The finding that existing box-based HOI detectors actually degrade when retrained with SAM-generated masks (Table 10), while FleVRS benefits substantially, highlights that the mask-based architecture is essential rather than merely the data quality. Key limitations include dependence on SAM for mask generation (scarce relationship annotations), CLIP's difficulty with abstract predicates (e.g., 'entering'), and the lack of free-form text prompt support. Open questions include whether unified multi-dataset training can be made to work effectively and how to scale to truly open-world scenarios without curated relationship data.
