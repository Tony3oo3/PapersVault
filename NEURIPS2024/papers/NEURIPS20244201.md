## Problem

LLMs face a fundamental tension on long-context tasks: input reduction methods (e.g., RAG) risk missing the relevant passage entirely, while window extension methods (e.g., 200k-token models) suffer from degraded attention over the full context, as captured by the 'lost in the middle' phenomenon. No existing approach simultaneously guarantees full receptive-field coverage and maintains focus on pertinent information without additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244201/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long source text (up to 200k+ tokens) and an optional query, splitting the text into chunks of at most k tokens (the LLM's context window limit). Each worker agent emits a task-specific 'communication unit' (CU) — evidence for QA, a running summary for summarization, or code context for completion — that is passed unidirectionally to the next worker. The final CU is consumed by a dedicated manager agent that produces the task output (answer, summary, or next code line).

## Method

CoA is a training-free, two-stage multi-agent framework built on a unidirectional chain of worker agents. In Stage 1, each worker W_i receives its chunk c_i, the query, a task instruction, and the previous worker's communication unit CU_{i-1}, then produces an updated CU_i; this interleaved read-process design lets each agent reason over a short context before the full input is consumed, expanding the effective receptive field to the entire text regardless of length. In Stage 2, a separate manager agent synthesizes the final CU_l into the response, separating chunk-level analysis from answer generation. Unlike parallel or tree-structured multi-agent designs, the sequential chain enables multi-hop reasoning that spans chunks (e.g., hop-1 evidence held by an early worker informs a later worker's inference), and reduces encoding time complexity from O(n²) to O(nk) relative to full-context processing.

## Results

CoA achieves significant improvements over both Vanilla (full-context) and RAG baselines on all nine long-context datasets across three LLM families (PaLM 2, Gemini, Claude 3). Headline gains include +13.30 F1 on NarrativeQA (text-bison: 25.26 vs. 11.96 Vanilla), +12.82 F1 on MuSiQue (37.09 vs. 26.87), and +22.00 exact match on QuALITY with gemini-ultra (80.60 vs. 57.40). Against Claude 3's 200k-token Vanilla baseline, CoA with only an 8k window achieves 18.80 F1 on NarrativeQA (vs. 7.17 for claude-3-haiku Vanilla) and 23.96 (vs. 6.56 for claude-3-opus Vanilla). On the NIAH PLUS test, CoA raises Text-bison accuracy from 26.0% to 97.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RAG (Xu et al., 2023, citation 81), CoA wins on all nine datasets with every backbone LLM; the gap is largest on NarrativeQA (+11.06 F1 with text-bison, +10.75 with gemini-ultra) and Qasper (+9.97 with text-bison), while RAG occasionally matches or slightly edges CoA on GovReport summarization. Against the previous state-of-the-art self-extend method (Jin et al., 2024, citation 29), CoA achieves better or comparable results on all nine datasets, with the largest margins on HotpotQA (62.04 vs. 54.4) and RepoBench-P (73.05 vs. 56.47), though it trails on Qasper (38.01 vs. 53.9) and QuALITY (83.80 vs. 89.2) where the SOTA uses task-specific training. CoA also outperforms two purpose-built multi-agent baselines (parallel Merge and Hierarchical tree) on all eight evaluated datasets, confirming that sequential chain communication is critical for multi-hop reasoning. Notably, CoA with an 8k window surpasses Claude 3's 200k-token Vanilla baseline by 11.6–17.4 F1 on NarrativeQA, demonstrating that the framework's gains scale with model strength.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoA departs from the two dominant paradigms for long-context LLMs—input reduction (RAG, truncation) and window extension (position interpolation, 200k-token models)—by instead leveraging multi-agent sequential communication to achieve full receptive field coverage without either shrinking the input or enlarging the window. Among prior multi-agent LLM systems, it specifically contrasts with centralized tree-structured approaches (WalkMaze, LongAgent) where sibling workers do not communicate, and with parallel designs (voting, hierarchical merging), by introducing a decentralized unidirectional chain in which each worker passes a 'communication unit' to the next, enabling multi-hop reasoning to propagate across agents. The interleaved read-process design is inspired by human working-memory constraints (Cowan, 2014), and the chain topology echoes RecurrentGPT's sequential generation, but is repurposed here for long-input comprehension rather than long-output generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoA offers a training-free, task-agnostic, and interpretable recipe that practitioners can adopt immediately across QA, summarization, and code completion without retraining any model, and its O(nk) encoding cost makes it more efficient than full-context inference. Key open questions include reducing the 1–4% information loss in communication units, preventing catastrophic collapse when irrelevant content propagates through the chain, and exploring richer communication protocols (debating, bidirectional exchange) or model routing to cut latency. The finding that an 8k-context chain can outperform a 200k-context model by roughly 3× on NarrativeQA suggests that information-flow architecture may matter more than raw window size, a direction worth further investigation.
