## Problem

Prior constant-stepsize SA analyses have treated nonlinear updates with i.i.d. data and linear updates with Markovian data separately, but the simultaneous presence of both structures creates a 'double recursion' that breaks standard coupling and conditioning arguments. No prior work establishes weak convergence of the joint process (x_k, θ_k) or provides a closed-form bias characterization for nonlinear SA with Markovian data, despite this setting being central to modern ML applications like logistic regression with sequentially sampled covariates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a uniformly ergodic Markov chain (x_k) on a countable state space, a three-times differentiable update function g(θ,x) with linear growth and strong monotonicity of its stationary mean, a constant stepsize α constrained by ατ_α ≤ μ/((940+96β)L²), and an initial parameter θ_0. Beyond the iterates θ_k, the key outputs are: (i) the unique stationary distribution ν̄_α of the joint process (x_k, θ_k) with geometric W_2 convergence, (ii) a bias decomposition E[θ_∞] − θ* = α(b_m + b_n + b_c) + O((ατ_α)^{3/2}) where b_c is a novel compound term absent in both i.i.d. and linear settings, and (iii) RR-extrapolated estimates that reduce the leading bias to O((ατ_α)^3). The analysis also yields finite-time 2p-th moment bounds and a CLT for tail-averaged iterates enabling confidence interval construction.

## Method

The core technique is a forward coupling of two SA iterates sharing the same data sequence, where the key obstacle is that the gain matrix E[g'(θ_∞, x_∞)] depends on both θ_∞ and x_∞ (nonlinearity) while θ_∞ is correlated with x_∞ (Markovian memory), so neither the linear-SA nor i.i.d.-SGD Hurwitz argument applies. The authors resolve this by conditioning on F_{k−τ} to exploit geometric mixing, decomposing the gain into a stationary component (handled by strong monotonicity) and a perturbation, then bounding the perturbation via two complementary Taylor expansions—one expanding in the difference w_k between coupled iterates and another expanding in the temporal displacement θ_k − θ_{k−τ}—so that the cross-term is controlled even when w_k is large. For the non-projected setting, an alternative V-uniform ergodicity proof is provided under a noise minorization assumption. The bias characterization follows from a perturbation expansion of the stationary equation around θ*, where the compound term b_c arises from the interaction between the Markovian correction h(θ*,x) (involving the time-reversed kernel P*) and the Hessian g̅''(θ*), yielding a multiplicative rather than additive effect of memory and nonlinearity.

## Results

The paper establishes the first weak convergence of the joint process (x_k, θ_k) for constant-stepsize SA with both nonlinear updates and Markovian data, with geometric rate W₂(L(θ_k), ν_α) ≤ (1−αμ)^{k/2}·s(θ₀,θ*,μ,L,R). The asymptotic bias is characterized as E[θ_∞^(α)] − θ* = α(b_m + b_n + b_c) + O((ατ_α)^{3/2}), where b_c is a novel compound term arising from the multiplicative interaction between Markovian noise and nonlinearity. Numerical experiments on L₂-regularized logistic regression with AR(1) Markovian data (x_{t+1}=0.9x_t+ζ_{t+1}) confirm the O(α) bias scaling and demonstrate that RR extrapolation reduces the leading bias, while also revealing that Markovian data does not necessarily produce larger bias than i.i.d. data due to sign cancellations among b_m, b_n, b_c.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-vs-method benchmarking, but it positions its results against three key prior lines of work. Compared to Dieuleveut et al. [20] (nonlinear SGD with i.i.d. data), the present paper recovers their bias result as a special case (when h(θ*,x)≡0, so b_m=b_c=0) but adds the Markovian bias b_m and the compound term b_c. Compared to Huo et al. [33] (Markovian linear SA), the present paper recovers their result when g is linear (so b_n=b_c=0) but introduces the nonlinearity bias b_n and the compound interaction b_c. Compared to Lauand & Meyn [45], who prove weak convergence only for the linear Markovian case via a different coupling strategy (initializing with different x₀ and analyzing a stopping time), the present paper extends weak convergence to the nonlinear setting using a forward coupling that shares data x_t=x_t' and develops a dual Taylor expansion technique to handle the 'double recursion' challenge. The compound bias term b_c is the key new finding absent from all prior work, revealing that nonlinearity and memory interact multiplicatively rather than additively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate analytical threads in constant-stepsize stochastic approximation: nonlinear SA with i.i.d. data (Dieuleveut et al. [20], Yu et al. [67]), which relies on local Taylor linearization, and Markovian linear SA (Huo et al. [33], Srikant & Ying [60]), which exploits mixing to regain approximate independence. The authors demonstrate that naively combining these techniques fails due to a 'double recursion' where the gain matrix in the coupling argument simultaneously depends on both θ and x (nonlinearity) and θ and x are correlated (Markovian structure). Their resolution—dual Taylor expansions exploiting the small displacement over τ steps—enables the first weak convergence proof for the joint process (x_k, θ_k) in the nonlinear Markovian regime, going beyond the linear-only coupling of Lauand & Meyn [45] and the ODE-based upper bounds of [44].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the compound bias term b_c—a multiplicative interaction between Markovian memory and operator nonlinearity absent in both the i.i.d. and linear special cases—fundamentally changes how one should think about bias reduction in practical SA/SGD pipelines with dependent data. The practical upshot is that Richardson-Romberg extrapolation, which cancels the leading α(b_m + b_n + b_c) term, becomes essential for accurate statistical inference (confidence intervals) in settings like logistic regression with Markovian covariates, whereas Polyak-Ruppert averaging alone cannot remove the bias. Open questions include whether the projection or noise-minorization requirement can be dropped, whether the countable-state-space and uniform-ergodicity assumptions can be relaxed, and whether the residual O(α^{3/2}) can be tightened to O(α²).
