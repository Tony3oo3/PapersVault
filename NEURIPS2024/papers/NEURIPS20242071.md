## Problem

Existing long-tailed data augmentation methods (e.g., CUDA, DODA) rely on manually designed, fixed augmentation strategies with limited search spaces that cannot adapt to the specific characteristics of a given long-tailed distribution. No prior work has leveraged large language models to automatically discover and optimize class-dependent augmentation strategies, leaving a gap between the flexibility needed for long-tailed learning and the rigidity of hand-crafted augmentation pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242071/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long-tailed image dataset (e.g., CIFAR-100-LT at 32×32, ImageNet-LT, iNaturalist 2018) along with per-class training statistics—current accuracy, historical per-augmentation accuracy, previous augmentation selection matrix, and previous augmentation intensities—fed into a pretrained LLM (GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4, or Claude-3-Opus) via structured prompts. The LLM outputs a Python function that, at each training epoch, returns a per-class boolean selection matrix over 10 augmentation operators (Mirror, EdgeEnhance, Detail, Smooth, AutoContrast, Equalize, Invert, GaussianBlur, Rotate, Flip) and a corresponding float intensity matrix in [0, 1], which are applied to the training data before fine-tuning the underlying long-tailed classifier.

## Method

LLM-AutoDA frames augmentation strategy discovery as an evolutionary search where a pretrained LLM serves as the mutation/crossover operator. Three prompt-engineered operators—Initialization, Crossover, and Mutation—guide the LLM to generate diverse Python augmentation algorithms from a knowledge base of data augmentation techniques, with crossover prompts requiring structurally novel combinations of parent algorithms and mutation prompts requesting local refinements. Each candidate strategy is evaluated by injecting it into the training loop: the model is fine-tuned for N_ft epochs from a checkpoint, with the strategy dynamically selecting operators and intensities per class per epoch based on class-wise accuracy feedback, and the resulting validation accuracy serves as the fitness score. The evolutionary population iterates through selection, crossover, and mutation until performance converges or the computational budget is exhausted, yielding a globally optimal augmentation strategy that outperforms fixed-strategy baselines like CUDA and DODA across multiple imbalance ratios and datasets.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT (IR=100), LLM-AutoDA achieves 45.0% overall accuracy with CE, outperforming CUDA (42.0%) and DODA (44.5%), and reaches 51.0% with BCL, matching DODA's best result. On ImageNet-LT, LLM-AutoDA with CE achieves 50.4% overall accuracy, a +8.8% gain over the CE baseline and +2.3% over DODA. On iNaturalist 2018, LLM-AutoDA with CE reaches 64.0% overall accuracy, surpassing both CUDA (62.5%) and DODA (63.6%). Across all six long-tailed baselines and three datasets, LLM-AutoDA consistently delivers the highest or tied-highest overall accuracy among all data augmentation methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM-AutoDA consistently outperforms CUDA [2] across all six baselines and all three datasets, with gains ranging from +0.6% (RIDE on CIFAR-100-LT IR=50) to +3.2% (CE on ImageNet-LT). Against DODA [39], LLM-AutoDA achieves the best or tied-best result in 11 out of 18 baseline-dataset combinations, with the largest margin being +0.3% on BS (CIFAR-100-LT IR=100) and +2.3% on CE (ImageNet-LT); it ties DODA on BCL (CIFAR-100-LT IR=100, both 51.0%) and slightly trails on RIDE (CIFAR-100-LT IR=50, 52.8 vs 52.1 for DODA). Compared to the plain baselines without augmentation, LLM-AutoDA yields the largest absolute gains on CE (+4.8 to +8.8%) and moderate gains on CE-DRW (+7.8 to +1.5%), LDAM-DRW (+4.0 to +2.7%), and BS (+6.0 to +1.5%). The SimpleLLM variant (single-shot LLM generation without evolutionary search) already matches or approaches CUDA/DODA performance, confirming that the evolutionary optimization in LLM-AutoDA provides an additional 0.5–1.0% improvement over the unoptimized LLM-generated strategy. On the large-scale iNaturalist 2018 dataset, LLM-AutoDA achieves the best overall accuracy with every baseline (e.g., 69.5% with LDAM-DRW vs 68.7% DODA; 74.2% with BCL vs 73.7% DODA), demonstrating consistent scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the AEL (Algorithm Evolution using LLM) framework [23], which uses LLMs as evolutionary search operators (crossover, mutation) for algorithm design, and repurposes it for the specific problem of long-tailed data augmentation. It departs from prior long-tailed DA methods (CUDA, DODA) that rely on manually designed, class-dependent augmentation strategies with limited search spaces, by framing strategy discovery as an open-ended evolutionary search guided by LLM-generated code. The conceptual bridge to AutoAugment [10] and broader AutoML is explicit: rather than searching over a fixed set of augmentation operations with learned weights, LLM-AutoDA searches over the space of algorithmic logic itself, generating novel selection and intensity-scheduling functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is reducing the expert design cost for long-tailed augmentation strategies, with the 23-hour automated search replacing potentially weeks of manual tuning. However, the paper itself acknowledges that the fitness scoring mechanism is imperfect and that the search space is still bounded by existing augmentation primitives (Mirror, Rotate, GaussianBlur, etc.), so truly novel augmentation operations remain out of reach. Open questions include whether the evolutionary LLM search generalizes to non-image modalities, whether the approach scales to even more extreme imbalance ratios, and how to design richer reward signals beyond validation accuracy to capture tail-class performance more directly.
