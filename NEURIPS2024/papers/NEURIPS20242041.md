## Problem

Training Binary Neural Networks (BNNs) is an intrinsically combinatorial optimization problem that existing methods address through latent weights and the heuristic straight-through estimator (STE), which lack theoretical justification. Prior variational inference approaches based on the mean-field approximation model weights as independent Bernoulli variables, failing to capture pairwise correlations between weights and unable to formally derive the STE and weight clipping used in practice.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs a class label. A distinctive aspect is the inference procedure: rather than using a single binary weight vector, the method draws 40 samples from the learned Gaussian distribution over weights, rounds each via sign, and averages the resulting predictions. Weights are constrained to ±1 (1-bit), with activations either 1-bit (1W1A) or full 32-bit precision (1W32A).

## Method

VISPA formulates BNN training as a non-convex semidefinite program over a correlated multivariate Gaussian distribution N(μ, Σ) on the weight space, with moment-matching constraints ensuring E[w_i²] = 1. To handle the infeasible O(n²) storage of a full covariance, the method follows Burer–Monteiro and maintains a low-rank factorization Σ = ZZᵀ with a deviation matrix Z ∈ ℝ^{n×K} (K ≪ n), enabling efficient sampling via w = μ + Zr. A key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that gradients of the expected loss with respect to μ and Z reduce to simple expectations of the network gradient, which are estimated via mini-batch SGD with momentum and hyperplane (sign) rounding. In the special case of diagonal covariance, the formulation recovers the standard latent-weight update with STE and weight clipping, providing a principled derivation of these heuristics. The ablation in Table 5 confirms that the off-diagonal correlations captured by Z yield consistent accuracy gains, particularly on complex datasets like Tiny-ImageNet.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (1W32A, VGG16), VISPA achieves 72.09% test accuracy, outperforming the previous best AdaSTE (69.28%) by 2.81%. On Tiny-ImageNet (1W32A, ResNet18), it reaches 58.98%, a 4.06% improvement over AdaSTE (54.92%). On ImageNet with AlexNet (1W1A), VISPA attains 51.1% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art Quantization Networks (47.9%) by 3.2%. On ImageNet ResNet18 (1W1A), it achieves 62.1% Top-1, edging out ReBNN (61.6%) by 0.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISPA outperforms all 32 baselines across every dataset and architecture tested. The closest competitor on CIFAR-10/100/Tiny-ImageNet (1W32A) is AdaSTE [Le et al., 2022], which VISPA surpasses by 0.88–4.06% depending on dataset and architecture, with the largest gap on Tiny-ImageNet (58.98% vs. 54.92%). On ImageNet AlexNet 1W1A, VISPA (51.1% Top-1) beats Quantization Networks [Yang et al., 2019] (47.9%) by 3.2% and Bop2ndOrder [Suarez-Ramirez et al., 2021] (46.9%) by 4.2%. On ImageNet ResNet18 1W1A, VISPA (62.1%) narrowly edges out ReBNN [Xu et al., 2023] (61.6%) and BiPer [Vargas et al., 2024] (61.4%), while on 1W32A it leads DIR-Net [Qin et al., 2023] (67.5%) by 0.7%. The ablation (Table 5) confirms the low-rank deviation matrix Z is essential: removing it drops Tiny-ImageNet accuracy from 58.98% to 56.73%, a 2.25% loss, while the gain is smaller on CIFAR-10 (93.25% vs. 92.85%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational inference framework for BNN training established by Ajanthan et al. (2019) and Meng et al. (2020), who used mean-field (independent Bernoulli) approximations to justify latent weights. The key departure is replacing the mean-field assumption with correlated multivariate Gaussians, introducing a low-rank deviation matrix Z (following Burer and Monteiro's SDP factorization) that explicitly models pairwise weight correlations. The hyperplane rounding procedure from Goemans and Williamson (1995) provides the theoretical bridge between the continuous Gaussian relaxation and binary sign-based rounding, yielding a non-convex SDP whose gradient descent solution naturally recovers the previously heuristic STE and weight clipping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first principled derivation of STE and weight clipping from a variational objective, replacing ad hoc justifications with a coherent optimization theory. The consistent accuracy gains (up to 3.2% on ImageNet AlexNet 1W1A, 4% on Tiny-ImageNet) demonstrate that modeling weight correlations via the deviation matrix is a meaningful source of improvement beyond independent latent weights. Open questions include reducing the 40-sample inference overhead (Gaussian quadrature with 2K+1 samples is promising), extending to transformer architectures where binarized activations remain unresolved, and formal convergence analysis via Bures-Wasserstein gradient flow.
