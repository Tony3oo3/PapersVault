## Problem

Disaggregated evaluation of AI models on demographic subpopulations suffers from high variance when intersectional groups are small, and no prior work has formalized the multi-task setting where multiple clients independently evaluate the same model on their own data. The paper asks whether information from other clients (or the model provider) can be leveraged to improve each client's subpopulation performance estimates without sharing raw data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240681/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SureMap consumes only summary statistics per group: the naive group means y, group counts n_g, and a pooled variance estimate σ², making it applicable even when clients share only aggregate numbers rather than raw data. In the multi-task setting, it additionally takes the corresponding summary statistics from T other tasks (clients). The output is a single vector of d shrunk group-mean estimates per task, where d is the number of intersectional subpopulations defined by k demographic attributes.

## Method

SureMap reformulates disaggregated evaluation as structured simultaneous Gaussian mean estimation and applies MAP inference under an additive intersectional effects prior whose covariance Λ(τ) is a weighted sum of 2^k binary matrices C_A encoding which groups share each attribute subset A. This prior requires only O(d) hyperparameters (versus O(d²) for a general covariance) yet can express the naive, pooled, and offset estimators as limiting cases. Hyperparameters τ are tuned by minimizing Stein's Unbiased Risk Estimate (SURE) via L-BFGS-B, avoiding data splitting. For multi-task evaluation, the prior mean θ is itself estimated by a second-level MAP (MetaMap) under a meta-prior of the same structured form, and both levels' parameters are jointly optimized by minimizing the sum of SUREs across all tasks.

## Results

On single-task disaggregated evaluation, SureMap significantly outperforms all baselines on Diabetes and Adult (0-1 error, disaggregated by race, sex, and age), with the largest gains on small subpopulations. On Common Voice (WER, disaggregated by sex and age), SureMap performs roughly on par with Bock and beats structured regression at several subsampling rates. In the multi-task setting, MT SureMap achieves a 2× reduction in MAE over the naive estimator on a typical SLACS task at subsampling rate 0.1 (improving 48 of 50 tasks), and attains 2–3.5× MAE improvement over naive on every CVC task. Crucially, MT SureMap already outperforms all single-task baselines with as few as T=2 tasks, whereas competing multi-task methods (MT offset, MT Bock) do not beat single-task methods until T≥5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SureMap is compared against the Bock (1975) James-Stein-type shrinkage estimator and the structured regression method of Herlihy et al. (2024, FAccT). On the single-task Diabetes and Adult benchmarks, SureMap clearly outperforms both Bock and structured regression across all subsampling rates, with the advantage concentrated on small intersectional groups where naive estimation is most unreliable. On Common Voice, SureMap is roughly tied with Bock and beats structured regression at several subsampling rates, suggesting the gap narrows when the number of groups is smaller (sex×age = 27 groups vs. race×sex×age = 54 groups). In the multi-task setting, MT SureMap dominates MT Bock on CVC, particularly on tasks whose ground-truth vectors are far from the multi-task center, where MT Bock degrades substantially while MT SureMap remains stable. On SLACS, MT Bock is competitive in the high-data regime and improves every state (including the two where MT SureMap is slightly worse), but MT SureMap wins decisively in the low-data regime. Structured regression (Herlihy et al.) is noted to be the discriminative counterpart of SureMap's generative approach (Lasso vs. Ridge regularization, cross-validation vs. SURE tuning), and SureMap consistently matches or exceeds it, validating the SURE-based tuning advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

SureMap sits at the intersection of classical James-Stein shrinkage estimation, empirical Bayes, and the growing disaggregated-evaluation literature (Miller et al. 2021; Herlihy et al. 2024). Its core methodological move is to cast disaggregated evaluation as Gaussian mean estimation under a structured additive prior with O(d) parameters, then tune the prior via SURE rather than cross-validation—making it the generative (Bayesian) counterpart to Herlihy et al.'s discriminative structured-regression approach. The paper's most significant departure from prior work is the formalization of multi-task disaggregated evaluation, where summary statistics from multiple clients inform a shared prior, a setting absent from the existing literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Disaggregated evaluation is a prerequisite for any meaningful fairness assessment, yet intersectional subpopulations are often too small for reliable per-group estimates; SureMap's ability to borrow strength across groups and across clients directly addresses this sample-efficiency bottleneck. The multi-task formulation is practically timely as model providers increasingly serve multiple clients who each need disaggregated evaluations, and the method's reliance on only summary statistics (group means, counts, variance) makes it privacy-friendly. Key limitations include the Gaussian error assumption (weaker for heavy-tailed metrics like MSE), the inherent bias toward less disparity that could mask fairness gaps, and the open question of how to handle client privacy in the multi-task setting without sharing even summary statistics.
