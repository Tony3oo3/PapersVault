## Problem

Pearl's non-backtracking counterfactuals require surgical interventions that can push counterfactual scenarios far outside the support of the observed data distribution, making downstream predictions unreliable—especially for non-parametric or deep generative models that struggle with out-of-distribution inputs. This paper addresses the gap by proposing 'natural counterfactuals' that permit a controlled amount of backtracking into causally upstream variables to keep the counterfactual scenario within the data distribution, while minimizing how much backtracking occurs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a complete observed data point (full evidence over all endogenous variables), a known causal DAG, a target variable A with a desired counterfactual value a*, and a pretrained generative SCM (normalizing flows for tabular data, or V-SCM/H-SCM with conditional VAEs for images) whose structural functions are assumed reversible. It outputs a counterfactual data point over all endogenous variables and the corresponding least-backtracking feasible (LBF) intervention do(C=c*) specifying which ancestor variables must be jointly intervened upon. The counterfactual is only produced when a feasible solution exists satisfying the ε-natural generation constraint; infeasible cases are explicitly flagged and excluded from evaluation.

## Method

The core contribution is the Feasible Intervention Optimization (FIO) framework, which formulates natural counterfactual generation as a constrained optimization: minimize the L1 distance between actual and counterfactual values over A's ancestor set AN(A), subject to A taking the target value a* and every ancestor's value satisfying an ε-natural generation criterion (its conditional CDF falling within (ε, 1−ε)). Practically, the problem is reparameterized in terms of exogenous noise variables using the reversibility of the learned structural functions, then solved via Lagrangian relaxation where the naturalness constraint is converted into a soft penalty weighted by w_ε. The key design insight is that backtracking is invoked only when the direct do(A=a*) intervention would violate the naturalness criterion, and the L1 distance metric implicitly favors changing variables proximal to A over more remote ancestors, yielding sparse and minimal backtracking. An identifiability theorem (Theorem 4.1) guarantees that under smooth, strictly monotonic structural functions, the resulting counterfactual is identifiable from the causal graph and joint distribution alone, without requiring knowledge of the true functions or noise distributions.

## Results

The method is evaluated using MAE between predicted and ground-truth counterfactual outcomes. On the toy datasets, the largest improvements occur when the non-backtracking intervention produces out-of-distribution values: e.g., 61.6% MAE reduction on Toy 1 (do(n2)→n3: 0.297→0.114) and 74.2% on Toy 3 (do(n2)→n3: 0.488→0.127). On Strong-3DIdent, MAE reductions reach 58.9% (β: 0.338→0.139) and 42.2% (α: 0.495→0.316). On MorphoMNIST with H-SCM at ε=10⁻², do(t)→t achieves a 66.8% MAE reduction (0.280→0.093).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Pearl's standard non-backtracking do-intervention applied to the same learned generative model (normalizing flows for toy data; V-SCM/H-SCM for MorphoMNIST and 3DIdentBOX). The proposed natural counterfactual method wins on every reported setting across all seven datasets, with no ties or losses. The margins are highly variable: they are modest (1–5% MAE reduction) when the non-backtracking intervention already stays within the data support (e.g., Toy 4 do(n1)→n3: 0.446 vs 0.443), but become dramatic (60–75% MAE reduction) when the hard intervention pushes the counterfactual into out-of-distribution regions (e.g., Toy 3 do(n2)→n3: 0.488 vs 0.127; Strong-3DIdent β: 0.338 vs 0.139). This pattern confirms the paper's central claim: the benefit of backtracking is concentrated precisely in the infeasible-intervention regime where non-backtracking counterfactuals are most unreliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Pearl's structural causal model framework for counterfactual reasoning by introducing a 'natural counterfactual' notion that permits controlled backtracking to causally upstream variables when a direct do-intervention would produce an out-of-distribution scenario. It builds on the identifiability results of Lu et al. [20] to show that natural counterfactuals remain identifiable from the causal graph and joint distribution alone. The approach is positioned between Pearl's non-backtracking counterfactuals (which are a special case when no backtracking is needed) and von Kügelgen et al.'s prior-based backtracking counterfactuals [35], which require full backtracking to exogenous noise and measure closeness in noise space rather than on observed variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is compelling: non-backtracking counterfactuals frequently produce scenarios outside the support of training data, making learned models unreliable for predicting downstream outcomes — a critical concern in high-stakes domains like healthcare and autonomous driving. The Feasible Intervention Optimization framework provides a principled way to keep counterfactuals within data support while minimizing unnecessary backtracking. Key limitations include the assumption of invertible learned functions (needed for identifiability), the requirement of a known causal graph, and the fact that the naturalness and distance measures are not claimed to be optimal. Open questions include whether a unifying semantics can reconcile natural counterfactuals with other counterfactual notions, and whether the approach extends to non-invertible or stochastic mechanisms.
