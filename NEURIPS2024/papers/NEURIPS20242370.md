## Problem

Prior convergence guarantees for the stochastic gradient bandit (REINFORCE) algorithm required learning rates small enough to satisfy smoothness and noise-control conditions, yet in practice large constant learning rates are routinely used. It was unknown whether the algorithm still converges to a globally optimal policy for arbitrary constant η > 0, and whether it maintains adequate exploration without any explicit exploration bonus when the step size is large enough to break standard stochastic approximation assumptions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a K-armed stochastic bandit with scalar rewards drawn from distributions supported on [−R_max, R_max]. It requires only an initial parameter vector θ₁ ∈ ℝ^K and a single hyperparameter (a constant learning rate η > 0), and at each step consumes one on-policy reward observation to update the softmax policy. The output is a sequence of policies π_{θ_t} = softmax(θ_t) over K actions, with the guarantee that π_{θ_t}(a*) → 1 almost surely for the optimal action a*, and an O(ln T / T) average sub-optimality rate after a burn-in period.

## Method

The proof establishes global convergence for any constant η > 0 through three key ingredients. First, an extended Borel-Cantelli argument (Lemma 2) shows the algorithm can never commit to a single action forever: the softmax Jacobian diag(π) − ππ^⊤ in the update naturally slows parameter growth when a policy is near-deterministic, guaranteeing at least two actions are sampled infinitely often. Second, each parameter θ_t(a) is decomposed into cumulative progress P_s(a) = η·π(a)·(r(a) − π^⊤r) and cumulative noise W_s(a), and martingale concentration (Freedman's inequality) shows the noise is dominated by the progress term regardless of η. Third, for K > 2, a recursive elimination argument shows that starting from the worst infinitely-sampled sub-optimal action, each sub-optimal arm's parameter is driven to −∞ in sequence, while the optimal arm's parameter remains bounded below, yielding π_{θ_t}(a*)/π_{θ_t}(a) → ∞ for every sub-optimal a.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2) establishes that the stochastic gradient bandit algorithm converges to the globally optimal policy almost surely for ANY constant learning rate η > 0, removing the small-learning-rate requirement of prior work [24] which needed η < η_c ≈ 0.00007. Theorem 3 provides an O(ln(T)/T) average sub-optimality rate. In the 4-action simulation (r = (0.2, 0.05, −0.1, −0.4)ᵀ, Gaussian rewards, σ=0.1), all 10 seeds converge for η=1 and η=10, reaching sub-optimality ≤ e⁻¹⁴ after 10⁶ iterations; for η=100 and η=1000, most runs converge even faster (to e⁻²⁰ and e⁻²⁰⁰ respectively) but 1/10 and 3/10 runs remain stuck near sub-optimal corners after 10⁶ steps. Log-log plots show a slope of nearly −1, consistent with an O(1/t) asymptotic rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not include a direct numerical comparison against other bandit algorithms (UCB, Thompson sampling) or against other policy gradient variants in its experiments. The primary comparison is theoretical: prior work [24] (Mei et al., 2024) proved almost-sure global convergence only for a problem-specific constant learning rate bounded by η_c = Δ²/(40·K^{3/2}·R_max³) ≈ 0.00007 in the tested setting, relying on smoothness and noise-growth assumptions. The present paper removes this constraint entirely, showing convergence for any η > 0, and validates this empirically with learning rates 10⁴–10⁷ times larger than η_c. The simulation also contrasts the behavior across learning rates: smaller η (1, 10) enters the final convergence stage quickly but converges more slowly in the tail, while larger η (100, 1000) achieves faster final-stage convergence (sub-optimality e⁻²⁰ vs. e⁻¹⁴) at the cost of longer initial plateaus where the policy gets stuck near sub-optimal corners. No head-to-head comparison with UCB or Thompson sampling regret bounds is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of Mei et al. (2024) [24], which established almost-sure global convergence of the stochastic gradient bandit algorithm only under a small, problem-specific constant learning rate bounded by smoothness and noise growth conditions. The key departure is showing that the softmax Jacobian structure (diag(π) − ππᵀ) inherently prevents the 'aggressive update' failure mode that plagues natural PG and normalized PG in stochastic settings [20], making the learning rate magnitude asymptotically irrelevant. The proof technique builds on the progress/noise decomposition from [19, 24] but applies it in a regime where classical smoothness-based arguments break down, relying instead on the extended Borel-Cantelli lemma and martingale concentration to control cumulative noise against cumulative progress.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result removes the only hyperparameter constraint (learning rate) from the stochastic gradient bandit algorithm, providing a theoretical justification for the widespread empirical practice of using large learning rates in policy gradient methods. However, the analysis is limited to multi-armed bandits with no reward ties, and the convergence rate (O(log T / T) on averaged iterates) is weaker than the O(1/T) rate achievable with small learning rates. Open questions include extending to full RL settings, handling function approximation, characterizing the multi-stage convergence behavior observed empirically (where large η causes long plateaus near sub-optimal corners), and whether time-dependent learning rate schedules (e.g., η ∈ O(log t)) can accelerate convergence.
