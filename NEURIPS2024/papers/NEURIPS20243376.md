## Problem

When pretrained but blackbox feature extractors (frozen, API-only, or too large to finetune) are used for downstream image classification, biases encoded in the pretrained features can propagate to the downstream model, yet existing debiasing methods assume a fully trainable encoder and perform inconsistently in this constrained setting. This paper investigates how and when such biases transfer, and proposes a targeted mitigation strategy that requires no knowledge of the bias attribute.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and produces a class label prediction. The key architectural constraint is that the pretrained encoder (ResNet-18, ViT-Base, CLIP RN50, or ViT-H) is entirely frozen; only a lightweight single-hidden-layer MLP adapter and a linear classifier are trainable. No bias-attribute annotations are assumed during training, and a small group-balanced (but unannotated) validation set is used for model selection.

## Method

The proposed pipeline has three stages: (1) bias-amplified training, where the adapter is trained with high weight decay to deliberately reduce worst-group accuracy while preserving overall training accuracy, thereby concentrating the dataset's bias into the adapter features; (2) KMeans clustering of these bias-amplified adapter features to identify approximate bias groups without any bias labels; (3) a cluster-based adaptive margin loss that reformulates cross-entropy as a cosine loss on a hypersphere and assigns each sample an angular margin inversely proportional to the frequency of its ground-truth class within its assigned cluster, with Gaussian randomization of the margins to compensate for noisy cluster assignments and prevent over-correction on rare classes. This adaptive margin mechanism, inspired by ArcFace, makes features of minority classes within each cluster more discriminative, enabling the debiasing model to separate bias-conflicting from bias-aligned samples.

## Results

On the ResNet-18 backbone, the proposed clustering-based adaptive margin loss (CM) achieves 80.29% worst-group accuracy on Waterbirds (vs. 38.90% ERM and 67.57% Co-Ada), 81.61% on CelebA Blond Hair (vs. 27.20% ERM and 78.37% Co-Ada), and 81.91% bias-conflicting accuracy on CMNIST-0.9 (vs. 61.72% ERM and 80.82% Co-Ada). For the ViT-B backbone, CM reaches 74.92% worst-group on Waterbirds and 87.28% on CelebA, outperforming Co-Ada by 11.21% and 1.41% respectively. On the CLIP RN50 backbone, CM achieves 94.07% Bi-Co on CMNIST-0.9 (vs. 83.81% Co-Ada) and 90.47% worst-group on CelebA, though Co-Ada slightly edges out on Waterbirds worst-group (81.95% vs. 79.28%). The method is also substantially faster than Co-Ada, training in 1–13 minutes versus 34–213 minutes on the ResNet-18 backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The strongest baseline is Contrastive Adapter (Co-Ada) [50], which the proposed method outperforms on ResNet-18 (by 12.72% on Waterbirds worst-group, 3.24% on CelebA) and ViT-B (by 11.21% on Waterbirds, 1.41% on CelebA), though Co-Ada slightly wins on CLIP RN50 for Waterbirds worst-group (81.95% vs. 79.28%). Among the other five baselines evaluated in Table 2/8 (DebiAN, BPA, LfF, JTT, GEORGE), none consistently outperforms ERM across all three datasets: LfF [20] achieves 66.09% on Waterbirds but collapses to 13.26% on CelebA; BPA [45] reaches 66.71% on CelebA but only 58.70% on Waterbirds; JTT [19] and GEORGE [44] remain within 10–20% of ERM on most benchmarks. DebiAN [38] shows minimal improvement over ERM (e.g., 58.94% vs. 38.90% on Waterbirds but 26.10% vs. 27.20% on CelebA). The last-layer retraining method DFR [54] is comparable on Waterbirds (74.68% vs. 80.29%) and CelebA (73.33% vs. 81.61%) but requires group-annotated validation data, whereas the proposed method does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the angular margin loss from face recognition (ArcFace) for bias mitigation, replacing the constant margin with cluster-adaptive values derived from KMeans clustering of bias-amplified features. It builds on the 'amplify-then-mitigate' paradigm established by LfF, JTT, and DebiAN, but departs from them by operating under a frozen blackbox encoder where no backpropagation into the backbone is possible. The clustering-based group identification echoes BPA and GEORGE, while the overall framing as a constrained transfer-learning problem distinguishes it from both the fully-trainable debiasing literature and the contrastive adapter line of work (Zhang & Ré) that targets foundation models with text modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As billion-parameter models become increasingly accessible only through APIs, the inability to update encoder weights makes this blackbox debiasing setting practically urgent. The paper's empirical finding that most existing debiasing methods (DebiAN, BPA, LfF, JTT, GEORGE) perform inconsistently or near-ERM in this constrained setting is a useful caution for practitioners. Key limitations include the assumption that the pretrained encoder's bias aligns with the downstream dataset's bias, and the reliance on categorical bias groups for clustering. Open questions include how to detect whether this alignment assumption holds without bias annotations, and whether the approach extends to non-categorical or multi-attribute biases.
