## Problem

Existing 4D generation methods separately distill appearance from 3D diffusion models and motion from video diffusion models, causing accumulated appearance degradation as motion changes and failing to jointly enforce spatiotemporal consistency. Additionally, these methods rely on text- or single-view-conditioned supervision, making them unable to faithfully preserve the multi-view attributes of off-the-shelf 3D assets during animation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243998/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static 3D model (represented as 3DGS or mesh), its multi-view renderings (16 views sampled at varying azimuth and elevation angles), and a text prompt describing the desired animation. It produces an animated 4D Gaussian Splatting with Hex-plane motion fields, and can further export an animated mesh via per-vertex Gaussian trajectory deformation without skeleton rigging. The multi-view renderings of the first frame serve as clean conditioning frames throughout the diffusion process, while noise is applied only to subsequent frames.

## Method

The core contribution is MV-VDM, a multi-view video diffusion model that integrates MVDream's spatial attention with AnimateDiff's temporal motion module through a novel parallel-branch spatiotemporal attention layer, where spatial and temporal attention outputs are blended via a learnable scalar μ. A multi-view extension of I2V-Adapter (MV2V-Adapter) injects identity from the conditioning frames via cross-attention, while IP-Adapter preserves object identity through a second cross-attention path. Trained on the newly constructed MV-Video dataset (84K animations, 1.3M multi-view videos from Sketchfab), MV-VDM generates spatiotemporally consistent multi-view videos. For animating a specific 3D object, a two-stage 4DGS optimization first reconstructs coarse motion via L2 rendering loss on generated videos, then refines appearance and fine-grained motion through a z0-reconstruction 4D-SDS loss from the unified MV-VDM, regularized by an ARAP loss to encourage locally rigid deformation.

## Results

On the 4D animation task evaluated with VBench metrics, Animate3D achieves 0.982 I2V Subject consistency, 0.991 Motion Smoothness, 0.597 Dynamic Degree, and 0.581 Aesthetic Quality, outperforming all baselines on three of four metrics. In a user study (1–5 scale) with 20 participants across 25 objects, Animate3D scores 4.386 (text alignment), 4.734 (3D alignment), 4.288 (motion), and 4.528 (appearance), substantially exceeding DG4D (2.824/3.52/2.284/3.108) and 4Dfy (2.028/1.608/1.534/1.84). In the extended comparison (Table 4), Animate3D also leads on CLIP-I similarity (0.946 vs. 0.895 for Dream-in-4D, the closest competitor).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Animate3D wins decisively over 4Dfy [5] on I2V Subject (0.982 vs. 0.783), Dynamic Degree (0.597 vs. 0.0), and Aesthetic Quality (0.581 vs. 0.497); 4Dfy's near-zero Dynamic Degree reflects its tendency to produce almost static results, and its slightly higher Motion Smoothness (0.996) is an artifact of that lack of motion. Against DG4D [38], Animate3D leads on all four metrics (I2V 0.982 vs. 0.898, M.Sm. 0.991 vs. 0.986, Dy.Deg. 0.597 vs. 0.477, Aest.Q. 0.581 vs. 0.529), with the gap in I2V Subject reflecting DG4D's novel-view appearance degradation from Zero123-based SDS. In the broader Table 4 comparison, Animate3D also outperforms Animate1245 [68] (I2V 0.982 vs. 0.845, CLIP-I 0.946 vs. 0.845), 4DGen [63] (I2V 0.982 vs. 0.833, Aest.Q. 0.581 vs. 0.453), and Dream-in-4D [69] (I2V 0.982 vs. 0.938, Dy.Deg. 0.597 vs. 0.0, CLIP-I 0.946 vs. 0.895). The only baseline that exceeds Animate3D on any metric is TC4D [4] on Dynamic Degree (0.830 vs. 0.597), but TC4D requires a pre-defined object trajectory as input, making it not directly comparable to the prompt-driven animation setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—multi-view 3D diffusion (MVDream, Zero123) and video diffusion (AnimateDiff, I2V-Adapter)—into a single multi-view video diffusion model (MV-VDM) that jointly models spatial and temporal consistency. It departs from the dominant two-stage 4D generation paradigm (MAV3D, 4Dfy, DreamGaussian4D) where 3D diffusion handles appearance and video diffusion handles motion in a detached, sequential pipeline, by instead training one model on a large-scale multi-view video dataset (MV-Video) to provide unified spatiotemporal supervision. The MV2V-Adapter extends I2V-Adapter's single-image conditioning to multi-view conditioning, enabling identity preservation of arbitrary existing 3D assets—a capability absent in all prior 4D generation methods that relied on text or single-view conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Animate3D provides the first practical pipeline to animate any off-the-shelf 3D model (3DGS or mesh) with text prompts while preserving multi-view identity, and the MV-Video dataset (84K animations, 1.3M multi-view videos) is the largest 4D training resource to date, likely to catalyze further 4D foundation model research. The finding that simple L2 reconstruction from MV-VDM-generated videos already yields coherent coarse motion—without any SDS—suggests that unified spatiotemporal supervision is fundamentally more informative than the separate appearance/motion signals used in prior two-stage methods. Key open questions include the 30-minute-per-object optimization cost, the trade-off between motion amplitude and temporal coherence, the domain gap for realistic scenes, and the absence of purpose-built 4D evaluation metrics beyond repurposed video benchmarks.
