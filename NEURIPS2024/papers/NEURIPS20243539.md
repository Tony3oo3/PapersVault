## Problem

Existing social network embedding models either capture only homophily (similarity-driven links) or model both homophily and social influence but exclusively in Euclidean space, failing to represent the distinct topologies each factor produces: cycles from homophily and tree-like hierarchies from social influence. No prior work has jointly modeled these two factors in their geometrically appropriate non-Euclidean spaces (spherical for cycles, hyperbolic for hierarchies) while maintaining a unified per-node representation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a social network adjacency matrix (optionally with node feature vectors) and produces, for each node, a pair of embeddings: a point on a fixed-norm spherical surface (homophily representation) and a point inside a Poincaré ball (social influence representation). The decoder outputs a per-edge link generation probability as a learned weighted mixture of a spherical geodesic-distance-based term and a hyperbolic norm-difference-based term, which is then used for multi-label classification and AUC-based link prediction.

## Method

NMM-GNN models link probability as a weighted mixture p(e_ij=1) = γ·p_hom + (1−γ)·p_rank, where p_hom uses spherical geodesic distance between homophily embeddings and p_rank uses norm-space difference between hyperbolic embeddings, capturing the intuition that similar nodes connect via homophily while nodes of differing social rank connect via influence. Non-Euclidean GNN encoders (Spherical GCN and Hyperbolic GCN with log/exp map retraction) produce the dual embeddings within a variational autoencoder framework with non-Euclidean Gaussian priors (spherical von Mises–Fisher-like and hyperbolic Gaussian). A novel space unification loss projects each node's hyperbolic embedding onto the spherical surface and penalizes the geodesic distance to its spherical embedding, ensuring the two representations correspond to the same node without collapsing them. Training uses Riemannian SGD with projection back to the spherical surface for homophily embeddings and a curvature-scaled gradient step for hyperbolic embeddings, jointly optimizing the mixture weight γ and all distribution parameters.

## Results

On the three main social network datasets (BlogCatalog, LiveJournal, Friendster), NMM-GNN achieves the best performance across all four metrics (Jaccard Index, Hamming Loss, F1, AUC). Headline numbers: on Friendster (65.6M nodes), NMM-GNN reaches JI 83.3%, F1 57.7%, AUC 94.9%, outperforming the closest competitor RaRE (JI 75.7%, F1 55.0%, AUC 94.4%). On LiveJournal (4.8M nodes), NMM-GNN achieves AUC 86.8% vs. RaRE's 79.9% and NRP's 78.7%. On BlogCatalog, NMM-GNN attains F1 78.8% and AUC 96.9%, surpassing the next-best NRP (F1 65.2%, AUC 95.5%) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NMM-GNN outperforms all 15 baselines on every metric across all three main datasets. Against the closest competitor RaRE (the only prior model jointly modeling homophily and social influence), NMM-GNN gains +13.2 F1 on BlogCatalog (78.8 vs. 65.6), +6.9 AUC on LiveJournal (86.8 vs. 79.9), and +7.6 JI on Friendster (83.3 vs. 75.7). The best homophily-only model NRP is outperformed by +13.6 F1 on BlogCatalog and +8.1 AUC on LiveJournal, confirming that social influence is a critical missing factor in homophily-based embeddings. Among non-Euclidean GNN baselines, HGCN and κ-GCN are surpassed by +17.9 F1 (BlogCatalog) and +17.7 AUC (LiveJournal) respectively, demonstrating that a single curved space is insufficient when both cyclic and hierarchical topologies coexist. Euclidean GNN baselines (GCN, GAT, GIN, GRAPHCL, GraphSAGE) trail by 10–25 points in F1, and structural embedding models (GraRep, RolX, GraphWave) are outperformed by 20–35 points in F1. On attributed graphs (Facebook, Google+), NMM-GNN also beats PANE and CONN by +2.8 F1 and +4.7 F1 respectively, showing the gains come from structural/topological modeling rather than attribute exploitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends RaRE's dual-factor (homophily + social influence) social network embedding by replacing its fully-parameterized Bayesian framework in Euclidean space with a mixture of two distinct non-Euclidean geometric spaces: spherical space for homophily-driven cyclic structures and hyperbolic space for social-influence-driven hierarchical structures. It draws on the non-Euclidean GNN literature (HGCN, κ-GCN) that demonstrated curved spaces better capture graph topologies, but departs from those works by using a *mixture* of two different curvatures simultaneously rather than a single space or a Cartesian product space. The non-Euclidean VAE framework with a space unification loss that projects hyperbolic embeddings onto the spherical surface is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior GNN or mixture-model work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a more interpretable generative model of social network formation by explicitly attributing link probability to two distinct social mechanisms, each grounded in a geometrically motivated space. This could inform downstream applications like targeted recommendation or community detection where understanding *why* a link forms matters. Key open questions include whether fixed curvatures (K_S=1, K_H=-1) are optimal, whether the spherical/hyperbolic assignment is truly the best pairing versus other space combinations, and whether the VAE training overhead is justified over simpler GNN training for very large-scale deployment. The space unification projection, while effective, remains somewhat ad hoc and its theoretical justification could be strengthened.
