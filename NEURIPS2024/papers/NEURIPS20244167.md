## Problem

While machine learning models for predicting properties of unmodified peptides are well established in MS-based proteomics, high-quality labeled reference datasets for modified peptides (bearing post-translational modifications) are largely missing. This gap impedes the development and benchmarking of models that can accurately predict retention time, fragment ion intensities, and precursor charge states for the important class of PTM-bearing peptides, which are central to protein function, regulation, and clinical biomarker discovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244167/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Input sequences are modified peptide strings in ProForma notation with PTM sites annotated by Unimod IDs (e.g., P[UNIMOD:21]EPTIDE), covering 30 unique amino-acid–PTM pairs across 1.2 million unique sequences. Outputs are three property targets per precursor: indexed retention time (iRT, a unitless calibration value), a vector of annotated fragment ion intensities (y/b ions up to triple charge plus neutral losses), and the observed precursor charge state distribution. Datasets are distributed as parquet files organized into pools of ~1k peptides per analysis run, with a unique raw-file/scan identifier linking each example back to its ProteomeTools origin.

## Method

The paper introduces four curated, expert-annotated datasets (TMT, Multi-PTM, TMT-PTM, Test-PTM) built on ProteomeTools synthetic-peptide raw data, using a rule-based expert system to annotate y/b fragment ions and neutral losses. The key design choice is a stratified data split where Test-PTM serves as a strict hold-out containing unseen PTMs, permuted phosphosites, multi-PTM sequences, and unmodified counterparts, enabling evaluation of generalization to novel modification contexts. For benchmarking, the authors train Prosit variants that encode PTMs via domain-specific features (delta mass or delta atom counts from Unimod chemical structures) and compare against DeepLC and AlphaPeptDeep, demonstrating that atom-count encoding outperforms mass-only encoding because PTMs with similar mass shifts produce divergent spectral and chromatographic effects. The evaluation uses task-specific metrics—time-delta at 95% for RT, normalized Spectral Angle for intensities, and MAE for charge states—reported separately on seen versus unseen PTMs to quantify the cost of ignoring modification information.

## Results

On the Test-PTM hold-out dataset, the best-performing model (Prosit-DeltaAtoms, which encodes PTMs via atom-count features) achieves a Timedelta95 of 12.7 (unseen PTMs) and 9.9 (seen PTMs) for retention time prediction, and a Spectral Angle of 0.86 (unseen) and 0.89 (seen) for fragment ion intensity prediction. The Prosit baseline trained without any PTM encoding performs substantially worse, with Timedelta95 of 26.1/25.8 and Spectral Angle of 0.75/0.74, confirming that incorporating PTM information is essential. For precursor charge state prediction, the Prosit baseline reports an MAE of 0.17 (unseen) and 0.16 (seen), though no PTM-aware charge model is yet available for comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Prosit-DeltaAtoms outperforms all three external baselines on every metric where they overlap. Against DeepLC [37], Prosit-DeltaAtoms reduces the RT Timedelta95 from 19.5 to 12.7 on unseen PTMs (a 35% improvement) and from 13.7 to 9.9 on seen PTMs. Against AlphaPeptDeep [40], Prosit-DeltaAtoms improves RT Timedelta95 from 14.2 to 12.7 (unseen) and 12.7 to 9.9 (seen), and improves fragment ion intensity Spectral Angle from 0.78 to 0.86 (unseen) and 0.83 to 0.89 (seen). Compared to the Prosit baseline [33] (no PTM encoding), Prosit-DeltaAtoms nearly halves the RT Timedelta95 (26.1→12.7 unseen) and raises the Spectral Angle from 0.75 to 0.86 (unseen), demonstrating that atom-count-based PTM encoding captures nuances that mass-only or no-encoding approaches miss. The Prosit naive-encoding variant (which ignores PTMs at inference) shows only marginal gains over the baseline on unseen PTMs, further confirming that explicit PTM feature extraction is necessary for generalization to novel modifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PROSPECT dataset (Shouman et al., NeurIPS 2022), which provided labeled tandem MS data for unmodified peptides, by introducing four PTM-rich companion datasets built on the same ProteomeTools upstream raw data. It positions itself against a growing but fragmented body of PTM-aware models (DeepLC, AlphaPeptDeep, pDeep3, Prosit-TMT) that each support only a limited set of modifications and lack a common labeled reference for fair comparison. The Prosit architecture (Gessulat et al., 2019) serves as the baseline, with the authors adding PTM encoding variants (DeltaMass, DeltaAtoms) to demonstrate how domain-specific features can be integrated into an existing RNN-based encoder-decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The absence of a high-quality, PTM-rich labeled dataset has been a bottleneck preventing systematic development and comparison of PTM-aware peptide property prediction models; this work fills that gap with 1.2 million unique modified sequences spanning 30 amino acid-PTM pairs and a dedicated hold-out test set containing unseen modifications. Limitations include coverage restricted to Orbitrap/Iontrap instruments, omission of some rare PTMs, and annotation pipeline over-annotation. Open questions include how to best encode PTMs for generalization to truly unseen modifications, whether data augmentation can address the long tail of rare PTMs, and how to extend the benchmark to other mass analyzer types (e.g., Waters, TimsTOF).
