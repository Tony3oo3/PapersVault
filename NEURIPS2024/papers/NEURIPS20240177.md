## Problem

Learned optimizers that perform well in supervised and unsupervised learning (e.g., VeLO) fail to transfer to reinforcement learning because they do not account for RL-specific difficulties: training non-stationarity, plasticity loss (dormant neurons), and the need for exploration to avoid premature convergence. Prior handcrafted solutions to these problems are limited by human intuition, and existing RL-specific learned optimizers (e.g., Optim4RL) rely on heavily constrained update expressions and fail to consistently beat standard baselines like Adam.

## Input / Output

The optimizer consumes, per parameter: the gradient and six momentum estimates (β ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 0.9, 0.99, 0.999, 0.9999}), each processed as {sign(x), log(x+ε)} to magnify small values; the current parameter value; two scalar timescales (training proportion and batch proportion); the dormancy score of the downstream neuron; and the layer's relative position in the network. It outputs a per-parameter update vector computed in three stages: a base update α₁m_i·exp(α₂e_i), an optional learned stochastic term α₃δ_i·ε applied only to the actor, and a final zero-meaning step for numerical stability. The GRU maintains a hidden state per parameter across update steps, enabling history-dependent behavior like learned momentum.

## Method

OPEN meta-learns a GRU-based update rule (GRU → two FC layers with LayerNorm → three outputs) whose input features are explicitly designed to target three RL difficulties: training/batch proportion addresses non-stationarity, dormancy and layer proportion address plasticity loss, and a learnable per-parameter noise term in the actor's update addresses exploration. The optimizer is trained with OpenAI ES (antithetic sampling, rank-transformed fitness) using only final return as the meta-objective, requiring no regularization terms. The update expression follows Metz et al.'s α₁m·exp(α₂e) form for stability across orders of magnitude, augmented with the stochastic exploration term and zero-meaning to prevent numerical divergence. This design enables OPEN to fit to single or small environment sets while generalizing out-of-distribution to unseen gridworlds, mazes, and Craftax-Classic, outperforming Adam, RMSProp, Lion, VeLO, and Optim4RL across all tested settings.

## Results

In single-task training, OPEN significantly outperforms all baselines in 3 of 5 environments (Breakout, Asterix, Space Invaders) and beats both learned optimizers (Optim4RL, No Features) in all 5 environments, measured by IQM of final return over 16 seeds. In multi-task training on four MinAtar environments, OPEN is the only optimizer to achieve an aggregate Adam-normalized score above 1.0, while all other learned optimizers fall well below Adam. For out-of-support generalization, OPEN outperforms Adam in every OOS gridworld and maze environment across all tested agent hidden sizes (8–128), and transfers 0-shot to Craftax-Classic with performance only marginally below Adam tuned directly in-domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Adam, OPEN wins in 3 of 5 single-task environments (Breakout, Asterix, Space Invaders), ties or slightly trails in Freeway and Ant, and is the only method to exceed Adam's aggregate score in multi-task training; in generalization, OPEN beats Adam in every OOS gridworld/maze and in 0-shot Craftax-Classic transfer. Against RMSProp, OPEN outperforms it in all single-task environments and multi-task settings. Against Lion, OPEN wins in most environments but performs comparably in Space Invaders (per return curves in Appendix E). Against VeLO, OPEN substantially outperforms it across all settings, consistent with VeLO's known poor transfer to RL. Against Optim4RL and No Features (both learned optimizers), OPEN wins in every single-task and multi-task environment, with the gap being particularly large in multi-task where Optim4RL and No Features fail to approach Adam's aggregate performance while OPEN exceeds it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learned optimization (L2O) line of work—Andrychowicz et al., Wichrowska et al., Metz et al. (VeLO)—by specifically targeting RL's unique difficulties (non-stationarity, plasticity loss, exploration) through both input features and output structure. It departs from Optim4RL (Lan et al.), the closest prior work on learned optimization for RL, by replacing Optim4RL's constrained Adam-based update with a fully black-box GRU-based update rule that conditions on dormancy, training/batch proportion, and layer position, and introduces learnable per-parameter stochasticity for exploration. The core update expression follows Metz et al. [24], while the exploration mechanism draws on parameter-space noise (Plappert et al.) and the dormancy metric from Sokar et al., and the meta-training uses OpenAI ES with antithetic task sampling as in Jackson et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OPEN demonstrates that learned optimizers can be made to work in RL when their architecture is explicitly informed by RL-specific pathologies, closing a gap where prior SOTA learned optimizers (VeLO, Optim4RL) failed to beat handcrafted baselines. The 0-shot transfer from gridworlds to Craftax-Classic and consistent out-of-support generalization across agent widths suggest a path toward a single 'foundation' optimizer for diverse RL problems. Key limitations include single-seed meta-training, Adam-normalization bias in multi-task curricula, and evaluation primarily on PPO; open questions include whether better multi-task curricula, additional RL algorithms (SAC, A2C), and larger-scale meta-training can unlock truly generalist learned optimization.
