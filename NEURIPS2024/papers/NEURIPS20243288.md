## Problem

Domain-agnostic adapters inserted into frozen VLM visual encoders for domain adaptive object detection inevitably bias toward the source domain, discarding domain-specific knowledge that is discriminative for the unlabelled target domain. This source bias limits generalization because the adapter only learns domain-invariant features under source-only annotations, failing to recover the complementary target-specific information lost during feature extraction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243288/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes paired batches of source images with ground-truth bounding boxes and unlabelled target images, using pseudo-labels filtered from high-confidence predictions on the target domain. At inference, a single RGB image from the target domain is processed through the frozen RegionCLIP (ResNet-50) visual encoder augmented with DA-Ada modules, producing bounding boxes with class labels via a Faster-RCNN detection head whose textual encoder is conditioned on visual embeddings through the VTA. The method requires only 1.794M learnable parameters on top of the frozen backbone.

## Method

DA-Ada attaches a Domain-Invariant Adapter (DIA) and Domain-Specific Adapter (DSA) in parallel to each block of the frozen visual encoder. The DIA uses a multi-scale bottleneck (M down-projectors at different receptive fields) to extract domain-invariant features, enforced by per-block adversarial domain discriminators. The DSA receives the residual difference between the block input and the DIA output (i.e., the features discarded by the block), recovering domain-specific knowledge hidden in that discarded information. The two streams are fused via pixel-level Hadamard attention, and a decoupling loss maximizes the distributional gap between DIA and DSA outputs to prevent knowledge overlap. A Visual-guided Textual Adapter (VTA) further projects the final visual embedding into the textual encoder through a domain-shared component (DITA, adversarially aligned) and domain-specific components (DSTA) to produce image-conditional detection prompts, replacing plain textual prompt tuning.

## Results

DA-Ada achieves 58.5% mAP on Cross-Weather (Cityscapes→Foggy Cityscapes), 66.7% mAP on Cross-FoV (KITTI→Cityscapes), 67.3% mAP on Sim-to-Real (SIM10K→Cityscapes), and 48.0% mAP on Cross-Style (Pascal VOC→Clipart), setting new state-of-the-art on all four benchmarks. The method uses only 1.794M learnable parameters (vs. 29.8M–39.2M for full fine-tuning baselines) while achieving the highest absolute gain of +8.0% over the frozen VLM baseline. On Cross-Weather, DA-Ada improves over the previous SOTA DA-Pro by 2.6% mAP, and on Cross-FoV by a larger margin of 5.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DA-Ada outperforms all 27 compared baselines across all four benchmarks. Against the closest CLIP-based competitor DA-Pro [31], DA-Ada gains +2.6% mAP on Cross-Weather (58.5 vs. 55.9), +5.3% on Cross-FoV (66.7 vs. 61.4), and +4.4% on Sim-to-Real (67.3 vs. 62.9). On Sim-to-Real, DA-Ada surpasses the previous non-CLIP best HT [11] by 1.8% (67.3 vs. 65.5). Against feature-alignment methods, the margins are substantial: DA-Ada exceeds D²-UDA [84] by 7.9% on C→F, NSA-UDA [83] by 5.8%, CSDA [16] by 12.7%, and the original DA-Faster [7] by 26.5%. On the challenging Cross-Style benchmark (Pascal VOC→Clipart), DA-Ada achieves 48.0% mAP, beating CMT [60] (47.0%), SIGMA++ [38] (46.7%), and TIA [78] (46.3%), with per-class wins on six of twenty categories (aeroplane, bike, bird, boat, bus, sheep). The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that the domain-aware adapter design (57.1% on C→F) outperforms both source-only (50.4%) and domain-agnostic (53.8%) adapter variants, validating the necessity of explicitly modeling domain-specific knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adapter-tuning paradigm (originating from NLP, Houlsby et al. 2019) into the domain adaptive object detection (DAOD) setting built on frozen VLM backbones (RegionCLIP). It directly departs from its immediate predecessor DA-Pro (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024), which applies prompt tuning only to the textual encoder while leaving the visual encoder frozen, by inserting learnable adapters into the visual encoder itself. The key conceptual departure from prior decoupling methods (which extract domain-invariant features and discard domain-specific ones) is a 'decoupling-refusion' strategy: a Domain-Specific Adapter (DSA) explicitly recovers domain-specific knowledge from the residual difference between a block's input and its domain-invariant output, rather than treating that residual as noise to be discarded.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a domain-agnostic adapter, despite aligning feature distributions, is inherently biased toward the source domain and discards target-discriminative information — a clean diagnosis supported by the 3.7% target-domain gap in Fig. 1(d). The plug-and-play DA-Ada module (1.794M learnable parameters) achieves consistent 2.6–5.3% mAP gains over the prior SOTA across four diverse benchmarks, suggesting the decoupling-refusion principle could transfer to other VLM-based adaptation tasks. However, the method is limited to a single labelled source and single unlabelled target domain; it cannot handle multi-source or source-free settings, which remain open and practically important for real-world deployment.
