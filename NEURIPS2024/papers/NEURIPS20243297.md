## Problem

Existing deep reconstruction models for snapshot compressive imaging (SCI) are trained on a single calibrated hardware configuration (coded aperture) and suffer severe performance degradation (>2 dB PSNR) when deployed on different hardware. Centralized multi-hardware training is impractical because optical system configurations and measurement data are proprietary across institutions, and coded apertures from different manufacturers follow distinct distributions, creating a two-level heterogeneity (sample-wise and distribution-wise) that standard federated averaging cannot resolve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243297/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D compressive measurement Y^M ∈ R^{H×(W+Δ)} produced by a coded aperture M ∈ R^{H×W} (with spectral shift Δ = d(N_λ−1)) and the coded aperture M itself as a conditioning signal, and outputs a reconstructed hyperspectral cube X̂ ∈ R^{H×W×N_λ}. The coded aperture is consumed not merely as a physical encoder but as an explicit input to the prompt network, which generates an additive correction to the measurement before it enters the frozen reconstruction backbone. Training uses the CAVE dataset (32 scenes at 512×512, augmented to 205), while evaluation is on KAIST (256×256×28) and real SD-CASSI data (660×660).

## Method

FedHP introduces a hardware-conditioned prompt network Φ(φ; M) that takes the coded aperture as input and produces an additive offset applied to the 2D measurement (Y_i^M ← Y_i^M + Φ(φ; M)), thereby aligning input data distributions across clients in the data space rather than rectifying gradients in the parameter space. The reconstruction backbone (MST-S transformer) is pre-trained per client and kept frozen throughout federated training; only the prompt network and lightweight CONV-GELU-CONV adaptors (inserted after LayerNorm) are learned. Each global round performs a two-stage local update—first optimizing the prompt network for S_p iterations with all other parameters fixed, then tuning the adaptors for S_b iterations—after which only the prompt network parameters are uploaded and aggregated via weighted averaging. This design reduces communication volume to the marginal prompt/adaptor parameters (0.27 M vs. 0.12 M backbone) while explicitly modeling the hardware–software correlation, and it outperforms FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, and FedGST by 0.35 dB under non-overlapping mask heterogeneity and by ~1 dB under distribution-shift heterogeneity where gradient-regularization methods fail to converge.

## Results

On the non-overlapping mask heterogeneity setting (Table 1), FedHP achieves an average PSNR of 31.35 dB and SSIM of 0.9033 across 10 KAIST test scenes, outperforming FedAvg (31.21 dB) by 0.14 dB. Under the more challenging distribution-shift scenario where each client's coded apertures follow a different distribution (Table 2), FedHP maintains 28.98 dB PSNR while FedProx and SCAFFOLD collapse to ~20 dB, and FedHP still leads FedAvg (28.63 dB) by 0.35 dB. FedHP achieves these results with only 0.27M trainable parameters (prompt network + adaptors), compared to 3.83M for the deep-unfolding baseline GAP-Net, while also improving PSNR by 0.28 dB over GAP-Net.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedHP outperforms all four federated baselines on both heterogeneity settings. On the non-overlapping mask setting (Table 1), FedHP leads FedAvg by 0.14 dB PSNR (31.35 vs 31.21) and FedGST by 0.50 dB (31.35 vs 30.85), while FedProx and SCAFFOLD trail by 0.59 and 0.64 dB respectively. Under the harder distribution-shift setting (Table 2), the gap widens dramatically: FedProx and SCAFFOLD collapse to ~20 dB PSNR (SSIM ~0.44–0.54), rendering them effectively non-functional, whereas FedHP maintains 28.98 dB and still surpasses FedAvg (28.63 dB) by 0.35 dB. Against the deep-unfolding method GAP-Net (Table 4b), FedHP achieves 0.28 dB higher PSNR (31.35 vs 31.07) while using only 7% of the parameters (0.27M vs 3.83M). A paired t-test confirms the PSNR improvement over FedAvg is statistically significant (p = 0.034), and the SSIM improvement is highly significant (p = 0.00004).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of federated learning and snapshot compressive imaging (SCI), building on FedAvg's aggregation mechanism and the visual prompting paradigm (Bahng et al., 2022) of adding a learned offset to input data. It departs from the dominant FL heterogeneity-handling strategy (gradient regularization via FedProx, SCAFFOLD) by instead operating in the input data space: a hardware-conditioned prompt network aligns measurement distributions across clients whose coded apertures follow different distributions. The reconstruction backbone (MST-S transformer) and the variational Bayesian self-tuning approach (Wang et al., 2022) provide the single-hardware and centralized multi-hardware baselines, respectively, but both assume data availability that violates institutional privacy constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to bring federated learning into spectral SCI, addressing a practical barrier: institutions cannot share proprietary coded apertures or hyperspectral measurements. The newly released SSHD dataset from three real SCI systems provides a benchmark for cross-hardware evaluation that did not previously exist. Key limitations include testing only 3–5 clients (far fewer than typical FL settings), lack of convergence theory, and evaluation confined to CASSI/CACTI architectures. Open questions include whether the input-space alignment strategy generalizes to other computational imaging modalities and how FedHP scales to dozens or hundreds of heterogeneous clients.
