## Problem

Existing multimodal benchmarks (SEED-Bench 2, BLINK) evaluate only short contexts, and video QA benchmarks (MVBench) target vision-dominant understanding rather than text-dominant multimodal documents. There is no systematic benchmark to test whether MLLMs can locate, count, and reason over key information scattered throughout long interleaved image-text documents of 1k–72k tokens, a foundational capability for real-world document comprehension.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample is a long multimodal haystack (interleaved image-text from OBELICS, 1k–72k tokens, ~1 image per 2k text tokens, at most 36 images) with one or more needles inserted at a controlled depth. Text needles are manually written statements; image needles are DALLE-3 cartoon images pasted into document images or BLINK jigsaw/multi-view images. The model must produce an open-ended answer (retrieval, counting as a per-item list) or a single-choice selection (image retrieval, image reasoning), with context length and needle depth uniformly distributed across bins for heatmap analysis.

## Method

MM-NIAH constructs ~12k samples across three tasks (retrieval, counting, reasoning) × two needle types (text, image), totaling six evaluation configurations of ~3,000 samples each. Haystacks are built by concatenating OBELICS interleaved sequences with a fixed image-to-text ratio; needles are injected at varying depths to probe positional sensitivity. The counting task requires per-item enumeration rather than a single total, forcing the model to attend to every image or statement in the document. A RAG baseline augments InternVL-1.5 with InternVL-G text and image encoders to retrieve the most relevant document chunks before answering. Key empirical findings: image needles are dramatically harder than text needles across all models, RAG substantially boosts text-needle retrieval but fails on image needles, and the long-context capability of underlying LLMs (e.g., InternLM2-20B at 200k tokens) is not preserved after multimodal fine-tuning.

## Results

On MM-NIAH (overall accuracy averaged across six task-needle combinations), Gemini-1.5 achieves 55.3%, InternVL-1.5-RAG achieves 50.1%, InternVL-1.5 achieves 42.4%, and the best open-source model without RAG (LLaVA-1.6-34B) reaches only 21.1%. Human performance is 98.9%, revealing a massive gap. Performance degrades sharply with context length: even Gemini-1.5 drops from 64.7% at 1K tokens to 53.6% at 64K tokens, while most open-source models fall below 1% beyond 16K tokens. Image-needle tasks are dramatically harder than text-needle tasks for all models (e.g., InternVL-1.5 scores 89.0% on text retrieval but only 28.0% on image retrieval).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-1.5 is the strongest model overall (55.3%) but still trails human performance (98.9%) by over 43 points, with its image-needle retrieval accuracy at just 22.4% versus 98.1% for humans. InternVL-1.5-RAG (50.1%) outperforms InternVL-1.5 (42.4%) by 7.7 points, confirming that RAG substantially boosts text-needle retrieval (97.1% vs. 89.0%) but provides negligible gains for image-needle retrieval (26.0% vs. 28.0%). Among open-source models, LLaVA-1.6-34B (21.1%) and LLaVA-1.6-13B (19.6%) outperform models trained on interleaved data—VILA-13B (16.3%), IDEFICS2 (11.6%), and Emu2-Chat (7.7%)—indicating that interleaved pre-training does not confer an advantage on long multimodal document comprehension. GPT-4V, evaluated only on text-needle tasks due to its 10-image API limit, achieves 94.8% on text retrieval and 83.9% on text reasoning, the best text-needle scores among all models. The 'Lost in the Middle' phenomenon is observed for text needles (middle positions perform worst), and all models fail to recognize the exact number of images in a document, with Gemini-1.5 dropping below 10% accuracy when more than five images are present.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-only Needle-In-A-Haystack (NIAH) paradigm to the multimodal setting, introducing both text needles and image needles into long interleaved image-text documents sourced from OBELICS. It departs from existing multimodal benchmarks (SEED-Bench 2, BLINK, MVBench) that operate on short contexts or vision-dominant video, positioning long multimodal document comprehension as a distinct and underexplored evaluation axis. The RAG baseline builds on standard retrieval-augmented generation using InternVL-G encoders, but the paper's key contribution is the benchmark construction and systematic evaluation rather than a new method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MM-NIAH is the first systematic benchmark for long multimodal document comprehension, and its findings reveal that even state-of-the-art models like Gemini-1.5 and GPT-4V struggle significantly, especially on image-centric tasks where performance can fall below random guessing. The demonstration that RAG is ineffective for image needles and that LLM long-context capability does not transfer to MLLMs challenges two widely held assumptions and points to fundamental architectural gaps. A key limitation is that answers relate only to inserted needles rather than the background document itself, leaving open the harder problem of holistic long-document understanding.
