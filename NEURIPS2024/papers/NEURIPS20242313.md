## Problem

Transformer-based in-context RL methods (e.g., Algorithm Distillation) suffer from quadratic computational costs as across-episodic contexts multiply sequence length, making them impractical for long-horizon tasks. While Mamba's selective state-space model offers linear-complexity long-sequence processing, its data-dependent selection mechanism introduces independence assumptions that are ill-suited to the sequential dependencies between RL states, resulting in slightly inferior prediction quality compared to attention. The paper asks whether a hybrid design can combine Mamba's efficient long-term memory with the transformer's high-quality local prediction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n historical trajectories (across-episodic contexts) sorted in ascending order of total return, plus the current observation. Mamba processes a compressed long-term representation where each c-step block is reduced to a single state token, an aggregated reward (sum of c rewards), and a completion flag, yielding 3×n×T/c tokens. The transformer receives a sub-goal vector (sampled from a multivariate Gaussian) interleaved with a local c-step window of (state, action, reward) tokens, producing c autoregressive actions per sub-goal. During training, an additional frozen linear layer maps selected high-value offline states into the same sub-goal space as extra prompts.

## Method

DM-H splits sequence processing hierarchically: a 2-layer Mamba module scans the full across-episodic context and emits a sub-goal vector every c steps, while a 3-layer causal transformer (GPT-style) predicts c actions autoregressively conditioned on that sub-goal plus the local c-step window. The key mechanism is 'valuable sub-goals': for each state s_i, the most valuable future state s_g is selected by maximizing the ratio of accumulated reward to temporal distance (Σr_k/(j−i)), then mapped through a frozen linear layer into the sub-goal embedding space. This reconstructed sequence (Eq. 7) is fed alongside the Mamba-generated sub-goal sequence (Eq. 6) to the transformer, creating a training signal that forces Mamba to produce sub-goals aligned with genuinely high-value future states rather than being ignored by the transformer. Training is fully end-to-end with cross-entropy (discrete) or MSE (continuous) loss on predicted actions, and online testing requires no gradient updates—only forward passes through Mamba and the transformer over n=4 episodes of context.

## Results

On D4RL (9 tasks across HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d), DM-H achieves a total average return of 87.53±0.59, outperforming AD(Transformer) (82.84), AD(Mamba) (76.97), DT (77.69), and BC-10% (80.65), and matching or exceeding TD3+BC (84.83) on 7 of 9 tasks. On Tmaze, DM-H handles context lengths up to 20k steps while DT and DM are limited to 10k, achieving maximum reward at all tested horizons. In online testing on long-horizon D4RL tasks (4000-step episodes), DM-H is approximately 28× faster than AD(Transformer) (~6 min vs. ~173 min for 10 episodes), and on Large Grid World (2000-step episodes) it is 7–12× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the in-context RL baselines, DM-H dominates: it outperforms AD(Transformer) on all 9 D4RL tasks (e.g., 117.19 vs. 108.32 on Med-Expert Hopper; 95.66 vs. 89.21 on Med-Replay Walker2d) and AD(Mamba) by an even wider margin (e.g., 83.15 vs. 73.65 on Med Hopper). DM-H also beats DT on every D4RL task, with the largest gap on Med Hopper (83.15 vs. 69.42). Against the conventional offline RL baseline TD3+BC, DM-H wins on 7 of 9 tasks (notably Med Hopper: 83.15 vs. 70.44, and Med-Replay Walker2d: 95.66 vs. 91.17) but trails slightly on Med-Expert HalfCheetah (96.21 vs. 96.59) and Med HalfCheetah (45.45 vs. 48.93). BC-10% is consistently outperformed by DM-H across all tasks. On Grid World long-term tasks, DM-H achieves more than double the effectiveness of AD baselines while being 28× faster in online testing; on Tmaze, DM-H extends the solvable context length from 10k (DT, DM) to 20k steps with lower training and testing costs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: in-context RL via across-episodic contexts (Algorithm Distillation, DPT, AMAGO) and efficient long-sequence modeling via selective state space models (S4, Mamba). Rather than simply swapping the Decision Transformer's backbone for Mamba (which the authors show is slightly worse in effectiveness), DM-H introduces a hierarchical sub-goal mechanism that lets Mamba compress long-term episodic memory into compact vectors while a small transformer handles short-horizon action prediction. The valuable-sub-goal alignment strategy, which injects high-reward states from offline data as additional prompts, is a novel training trick that forces the transformer to actually depend on Mamba's long-term context rather than ignoring it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 28× online-testing speedup on D4RL-scale tasks (context length 4000) is practically significant: it makes in-context RL with across-episodic contexts feasible at horizons where transformer-based methods become prohibitively expensive. The key open question the authors identify is the task-dependent hyperparameter c (short-term window length), which currently must be hand-tuned; an adaptive c would be needed for the method to be broadly deployable. More broadly, the paper validates that hybrid SSM+attention architectures can outperform either component alone in sequential decision-making, a design pattern that may transfer to other long-horizon planning and control settings.
