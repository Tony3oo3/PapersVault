## Problem

Existing self-supervised pre-training methods for person re-identification are confined to instance-level (augmented views of a single image) or single-video tracklet-level (frames within one video), both of which ignore the identity-invariance of the same person appearing across different videos. This neglect leads to insufficient intra-identity consistency (due to tracking errors within tracklets) and limited inter-identity discrimination (since the same person in different videos is treated as different identities), ultimately capping the quality of learned person representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240795/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes person images extracted from internet videos (the CION-AL dataset: 3.9M images of 246,904 identities from SYNTHPEDES), with global views resized to 256×128 and local views to 128×64 during pre-training. It produces a family of pre-trained backbones (32 models across 10 architectures) that output person identity embeddings; these are then fine-tuned with standard ReID algorithms (e.g., TransReID, MGN) to produce match scores for supervised, UDA, or USL ReID evaluation on Market1501 and MSMT17.

## Method

CION models identity correlation seeking as a progressive multi-level denoising problem. First, a noise metric is defined combining intra-identity consistency (maximum hypersphere radius per identity) and inter-identity discrimination (minimum centroid distance between identities). Second, three denoising stages are applied sequentially: single-tracklet denoising iteratively removes outlier samples exceeding a radius threshold; short-range single-video denoising reallocates misassigned samples and merges tracklets with close centroids; and long-range cross-video denoising uses a Sliding Range with Linking Relations to merge tracklets of the same person across videos, reducing complexity from O(N²) to O(N). Third, an identity-guided self-distillation loss (built on the DINO framework) trains a student-teacher pair where the student matches the teacher's output distribution across augmented views drawn from multiple images of the same person, explicitly enforcing identity-level invariance rather than instance-level invariance.

## Results

With ResNet50-IBN backbone and only 3.9M pre-training samples (8% of ISR's 47.8M), CION achieves 93.3% mAP / 97.3% Rank-1 on Market1501 and 74.3% mAP / 89.8% Rank-1 on MSMT17 under supervised ReID (fine-tuned with MGN). With ViT-B, CION reaches 93.1% mAP on Market1501 and 72.6% mAP on MSMT17, surpassing the previous best self-supervised method PASS (ViT-B) by 0.1% and 0.8% mAP respectively. In unsupervised domain adaptation (Mar→MS), CION with R50-IBN achieves 62.7% mAP, outperforming the previous best TranSSL* (ViT-S) by 4.9% mAP. Across 32 models of 10 architectures, CION yields an average improvement of 11.7% mAP on Market1501 and 18.8% mAP on MSMT17 over ImageNet-pretrained baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CION with ResNet50-IBN (3.9M samples) surpasses the previous state-of-the-art ISR (47.8M samples) by 1.0% mAP on Market1501 (93.3% vs 92.3%) and 2.8% mAP on MSMT17 (74.3% vs 71.5%), while using only 8% of the training data. Against instance-level methods, CION (R50-IBN) outperforms LUP (4.2M) by 2.3% mAP on Market1501 and 8.6% on MSMT17, and outperforms LUP-NL (10.7M) by 1.4% and 6.3% respectively. In the ViT family, CION (ViT-B) edges out PASS (ViT-B) by 0.1% mAP on Market1501 and 0.8% on MSMT17, and outperforms TranSSL (ViT-S) by 2.0% and 5.8% mAP. In unsupervised settings, CION (R50-IBN) dramatically outperforms the instance-level LUP by 20.1% mAP on Market1501→MSMT17 UDA (62.7% vs 42.6%) and 19.6% mAP on MSMT17 USL (59.4% vs 39.8%), validating the critical importance of cross-video identity-level correlating over instance-level pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DINO self-distillation framework [1] by introducing an identity-level contrastive objective that correlates augmented views from multiple images of the same person across different videos, rather than treating each image or single-video tracklet in isolation. It departs from instance-level pre-training methods (LUP, PASS, SOLIDER) that ignore cross-image identity invariance, and from single-video tracklet-level methods (ISR, LUP-NL) that conflate tracking errors with identity and miss cross-video identity recurrence. The key conceptual contribution is formulating identity correlation seeking as a progressive multi-level denoising problem (single-tracklet → single-video → cross-video) with a formal noise definition combining intra-identity consistency and inter-identity discrimination, plus a Sliding Range and Linking Relation mechanism to make cross-video denoising computationally tractable at O(N) rather than O(N²).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: CION achieves SOTA supervised ReID (93.3% mAP on Market1501, 74.3% on MSMT17 with R50-IBN) using only 3.9M training images versus ISR's 47.8M, and the contributed ReIDZoo (32 models, 10 architectures) provides a ready-to-use resource for the community. Open questions remain around fine-grained identity mining (the method does not explicitly exploit part-level or attribute-level invariance), the accuracy ceiling of the feature-extractor-based denoising pipeline, and whether the Sliding Range strategy can capture identity correlations between tracklets separated by very large video indices. Privacy concerns around internet-crawled person data and the gated-release policy also represent ongoing practical and ethical considerations for the field.
