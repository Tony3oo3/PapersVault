## Problem

Existing unsupervised domain adaptation methods for medical image segmentation are limited to one-to-one transfer, requiring a separate generator for each source-target domain pair. This is computationally expensive and fails to exploit commonalities among multiple target sequences (e.g., FS T2W, T2W, DWI) in multi-sequence MRI, making multi-target kidney tumor segmentation both resource-intensive and structurally inconsistent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240772/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single annotated source domain image (MRI or CT, processed to 256×256) and a target domain label as input, and produces one pseudo-target domain image per target domain. These generated images are then paired with the source domain's segmentation annotations to train a U-Net, which outputs kidney and tumor masks. The two-stage pipeline means the generator never sees target-domain labels during inference—only the domain label index selects the appropriate style dictionary entry.

## Method

PSTUDA replaces StarGAN v2's Mapping Network and Style Encoder with learnable multi-level style dictionaries (depth 4096) that store domain-specific style encodings at each of four progressive fusion stages, decoupling content from style and reducing generator burden. A shared CycleGAN-style encoder extracts domain-invariant features, which are then progressively recombined with target-domain style features through four cascaded style fusion modules using Point-wise Instance Normalization (PIN). PIN computes per-pixel scaling (γ) and shifting (β) parameters from the style feature via a ConvBlock, enabling spatially-varying style fusion that preserves local anatomical structure. A multi-scale discriminator with four independent residual-block branches (each producing N domain-specific outputs) enforces realism across scales. Training uses adversarial, cycle-consistency (λ=10), and identity (λ=1) losses, and the entire one-to-multiple transfer is handled by a single generator, reducing FLOPs by ~72% and parameters by ~50% compared to training separate one-to-one models.

## Results

On the MSKT dataset (T1c as source, FS T2W/T2W/DWI as targets), PSTUDA achieves average DSC of 81.33%, 65.52%, and 80.61% across the three target sequences, outperforming all five baselines. On the more challenging KiTS19+MSKT experiment (CT→MRI), PSTUDA attains average DSC of 79.15%, 80.85%, 66.71%, and 82.54% for T1c, FS T2W, T2W, and DWI respectively. The method also reduces FLOPs by ~72% and model parameters by ~50% compared to CycleGAN and MUNIT while maintaining superior segmentation accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSTUDA outperforms all five baselines on average DSC across every target sequence in both experimental settings. Against CycleGAN (the closest competitor), PSTUDA gains +1.08% on FS T2W, +3.56% on T2W, and +0.87% on DWI; the gap widens substantially on the CT→MRI experiment where CycleGAN drops ~6.8% on FS T2W while PSTUDA drops only 0.5%. Against DEPL, PSTUDA shows a dramatic advantage on T2W (65.52% vs. 23.30% DSC), where DEPL suffers severe structural distortion. MUNIT and StarGAN v2, while strong in image diversity, produce structurally distorted translations that degrade segmentation (e.g., StarGAN v2 achieves only 35.78% avg DSC on T2W vs. PSTUDA's 65.52%). SIFA, an end-to-end UDA method, underperforms due to structural distortions in generated images, yielding 51.32% avg DSC on T2W versus PSTUDA's 65.52%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-to-multiple UDA framework introduced by OMUDA (Xu et al., 2023), which itself adapted StarGAN v2's multi-domain generation to medical imaging. The key departure is a purpose-built redesign of the style transfer mechanism: the per-image Mapping Network and Style Encoder of StarGAN v2 are replaced by learnable multi-level style dictionaries, and the global AdaIN normalization is replaced by Point-wise Instance Normalization (PIN) for pixel-level style fusion. The encoder-decoder backbone is inherited from CycleGAN (via OMUDA), and the multi-scale discriminator draws on Wang et al.'s conditional GAN work, but the progressive cascaded style fusion paradigm is novel to this paper.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-to-multiple formulation addresses a practical clinical bottleneck: annotating every MRI sequence is prohibitively expensive, and training separate one-to-one adapters for each source-target pair scales poorly. The ~72% FLOP and ~50% parameter reductions make multi-sequence adaptation more deployable in resource-constrained clinical settings. However, the authors acknowledge the current model still operates within a broader multi-domain-to-multi-domain framework rather than a true one-to-multiple architecture, and the evaluation is confined to kidney/tumor segmentation on two datasets. Open questions include generalization to other organs, modalities, and downstream tasks (e.g., detection), and whether the multi-level style dictionary approach transfers to natural image domain adaptation.
