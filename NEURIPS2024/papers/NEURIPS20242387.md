## Problem

Estimating physical properties of deformable objects from visual observations remains challenging because prior methods either assume elastic materials (restricting generality to fluids or granular media), require ground-truth geometry, or rely on implicit NeRF representations that produce noisy shapes and suffer from texture distortion when rendering large deformations. PAC-NeRF, the closest prior work, jointly recovers geometry and physics but its implicit shapes cause inaccurate simulation trajectories and its static appearance field introduces rendering artifacts that degrade parameter estimation.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB video sequences with known camera extrinsics and intrinsics, assuming the object's constitutive model type (elasticity, plasticine, granular, Newtonian, or non-Newtonian) is known a priori. It produces a set of continuum particles with assigned Gaussian attributes (density and scale), the corresponding physical parameters (e.g., Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, viscosity, yield stress, friction angle depending on material type), extracted object surfaces, and simulated trajectories that can render both 3D surfaces and 2D object masks for supervision. The key design choice is that the Gaussian-informed continuum renders only binary masks rather than RGB images, avoiding the appearance distortion problem of prior methods.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, a motion-factorized dynamic 3D Gaussian network reconstructs the object across time by decomposing deformation into N_m=8 motion bases sharing a common FC backbone, with a lightweight coefficient network producing time-variant per-point weights that linearly combine position and scale residuals. Second, a coarse-to-fine filling strategy iteratively upsamples a density field (4 steps), applies mean filtering, and reassigns high densities at particle locations to progressively sharpen the object boundary, yielding continuum particles and surfaces via thresholding. Third, the continuum particles are endowed with Gaussian attributes (density from the field, scale from grid size) to form a 'Gaussian-informed continuum' that renders 2D object masks during MPM simulation. Physical parameters are then optimized by minimizing a combined loss of chamfer distance between simulated and extracted 3D surfaces plus L1 distance between rendered and ground-truth 2D masks, exploiting the differentiability of the MPM simulator.

## Results

On the Spring-Gaus synthetic dataset, GIC achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 0.95 (×10³ mm²) and EMD of 0.049 for future state simulation, substantially outperforming Spring-Gaus (CD 5.16, EMD 0.095) and PAC-NeRF (CD 17.94, EMD 0.134). For rendering quality, GIC reaches a mean PSNR of 22.98 dB and SSIM of 0.930, compared to 17.06/0.897 (Spring-Gaus) and 15.77/0.870 (PAC-NeRF). On the PAC-NeRF cross-shaped object dataset, GIC attains a mean CD of 0.212 and EMD of 0.023 for dynamic reconstruction, improving over DefGS (0.234/0.025) and PAC-NeRF (0.278/0.026). On D-NeRF, GIC achieves a mean PSNR of 40.85, surpassing DefGS (40.43) and all NeRF-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PAC-NeRF [12], GIC wins decisively on all metrics across all datasets: on Spring-Gaus it reduces mean CD by ~19× (0.95 vs 17.94) and EMD by ~2.7×, while also improving PSNR by 7.2 dB (22.98 vs 15.77). Against Spring-Gaus [6], GIC achieves roughly 5.4× lower mean CD (0.95 vs 5.16) and 1.9× lower EMD (0.049 vs 0.095), with a 5.9 dB PSNR gain. Against DefGS [16] on the PAC-NeRF reconstruction benchmark, GIC improves mean CD from 0.234 to 0.212 and EMD from 0.025 to 0.023, with the largest gains on granular media (Sand: 0.25 vs 0.281 CD) and plasticine (0.196 vs 0.213). On D-NeRF novel view synthesis, GIC slightly edges out DefGS (40.85 vs 40.43 mean PSNR) and substantially outperforms NeRF-based methods like TiNeuVox (32.74) and K-Planes (31.41). The only case where GIC does not clearly dominate is the Sand category in Table 1, where DefGS achieves a marginally lower CD (0.281 vs 0.25 for GIC is actually GIC winning, but the gap is smaller than on other material types).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of dynamic 3D reconstruction (3DGS, DefGS, DynMF) and physics-based system identification (PAC-NeRF, Spring-Gaus). It departs from PAC-NeRF's implicit NeRF representation by adopting explicit 3D Gaussians for geometry, and from Spring-Gaus's spring-mass formulation by using MPM for broader material coverage. The key architectural departure is the motion-factorized dynamic Gaussian network with a shared backbone across motion bases, and the coarse-to-fine density field strategy that converts rendering-oriented Gaussians into simulation-ready continuum particles. The most distinctive conceptual contribution is endowing the simulated continuum with Gaussian attributes (opacity, scale) so it can render 2D object masks as shape surrogates during MPM rollouts, providing a dual 3D-surface + 2D-mask supervision signal for parameter estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit 3D Gaussian geometry combined with 2D mask-based supervision yields more robust physical property estimation than RGB-based guidance, particularly under large deformations where rendered textures distort. The real-world robotic grasping demonstration (UR10 arm, FEM-based Isaac Gym) shows practical applicability for digital twins. However, the method still requires multi-view video with known camera poses, assumes the constitutive model type is known a priori, and demands ~1.5 hours of compute per object. Open questions include whether pose-free reconstruction (e.g., COLMAP-free 3DGS) or generalized constitutive models can be integrated, and whether the pipeline can be accelerated via large reconstruction models or multi-modal LLMs to reduce the computational bottleneck.
