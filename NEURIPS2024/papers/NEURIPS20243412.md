## Problem

State-of-the-art semi-supervised learning methods like FixMatch and MixMatch rely on confidence thresholds and the low-density assumption to generate high-quality pseudo-labels, but these mechanisms are inapplicable to regression tasks because regression models lack explicit confidence measures and decision boundaries. This paper addresses the gap by finding a way to adapt the full semi-supervised classification toolkit to regression settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243412/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts face images (resized to 40×40), audio clips (up to 6 seconds at 16 kHz), or text comments (up to 512 tokens) and produces a single continuous regression value (age, quality score, or rating). Unusually, the model is multi-task: alongside the regression head, an auxiliary ranking head outputs a two-class probability distribution for every pair of samples in a batch, indicating which sample has the higher label. This pairwise ranking output is what enables the integration of classification-style pseudo-labeling and confidence thresholding.

## Method

RankUp introduces two components. First, the Auxiliary Ranking Classifier (ARC) is an additional two-class output head sharing hidden layers with the regression model; it converts the regression task into a pairwise binary classification by computing softmax(r(x_i) − r(x_j)) for sample pairs, and is trained with FixMatch's confidence-threshold pseudo-labeling and weak/strong augmentation consistency. Second, Regression Distribution Alignment (RDA) refines pseudo-labels by sorting both the labeled data and model-generated pseudo-labels, then replacing each pseudo-label with its rank-corresponding value from the labeled distribution, effectively warping the pseudo-label distribution to match the true label distribution. A linear warm-up schedule and periodic application (every T steps with a cached pseudo-label table) keep RDA computationally tractable. The final loss combines the labeled regression loss, the RDA-aligned unlabeled regression loss, and the ARC classification loss, with the key insight that improving the ranking classifier's confidence in pairwise comparisons indirectly sharpens the regression model's pseudo-label quality.

## Results

On UTKFace (image age estimation) with 50 labeled samples, RankUp achieves MAE 9.96, R² 0.514, and SRCC 0.703, outperforming the strongest baseline MixMatch (MAE 11.44, R² 0.401, SRCC 0.674) by 12.9% in MAE and 28.2% in R². With RDA added, performance further improves to MAE 9.33, R² 0.552, and SRCC 0.770. At 250 labeled samples, RankUp+RDA reaches MAE 6.57, R² 0.782, and SRCC 0.856, surpassing MixMatch (MAE 7.95, R² 0.692, SRCC 0.832) by 17.4% in MAE and 13.0% in R². The method generalizes to audio (BVCC) and text (Yelp Review) tasks, consistently outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RankUp outperforms all five semi-supervised regression baselines (Π-Model, Mean Teacher, CLSS, UCVME, and MixMatch) across all three metrics (MAE, R², SRCC) on both the 50- and 250-label settings of UTKFace. The strongest baseline, MixMatch, is beaten by 12.9% in MAE and 28.2% in R² at 50 labels, and by 11.2% in MAE and 8.5% in R² at 250 labels. Among the consistency-regularization baselines, UCVME is the closest competitor at 250 labels (MAE 8.63 vs. RankUp's 7.06), while CLSS and Mean Teacher trail further behind. On the audio (BVCC) and text (Yelp Review) datasets, RankUp similarly dominates all baselines, achieving at least 5.6% MAE improvement on BVCC and 8.3% MAE improvement on Yelp Review. Notably, RankUp+RDA with only 50 labels surpasses the supervised model trained on 250 labels (5× more data) across all metrics, demonstrating significant data-efficiency gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

RankUp bridges two previously disconnected lines of semi-supervised learning: consistency-regularization-based regression methods (Pi-Model, Mean Teacher, UCVME, CLSS) that rely solely on the smoothness assumption, and confidence-threshold-based classification methods (FixMatch, MixMatch) that additionally exploit the low-density assumption. The key conceptual departure is the Auxiliary Ranking Classifier (ARC), which repurposes RankNet's pairwise ranking formulation into a two-class softmax output, thereby converting a regression problem into a classification problem amenable to FixMatch's pseudo-labeling pipeline. The Regression Distribution Alignment (RDA) component further extends distribution alignment—a technique well-established in semi-supervised classification (DARL, CREST, AdaMatch)—to the continuous regression setting by sorting and interpolating label distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that the low-density assumption, long considered inapplicable to regression due to the absence of confidence scores and decision boundaries, can be effectively leveraged through a ranking reformulation, opening a path to transfer the entire toolkit of SOTA semi-supervised classification methods to regression. The practical impact is significant: with only 50 labeled samples, RankUp+RDA outperforms a supervised model trained on 5× more labels, directly reducing annotation costs in expensive domains like medical imaging and audio quality assessment. Key limitations include RDA's dependence on similar labeled/unlabeled label distributions and its degradation when distinct label values are few (as in the 5-level Yelp dataset), and the framework's reliance on the assumption that pseudo-label rankings are reasonably accurate before RDA is applied. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-output or structured regression tasks, and whether more advanced semi-supervised classification methods (e.g., FreeMatch, FlexMatch) could further improve ARC training.
