## Problem

Protein structure prediction models like AlphaFold2 rely heavily on high-quality multiple sequence alignments (MSAs) to capture co-evolutionary signals, yet many protein sequences—especially orphan or low-homology families—yield sparse, low-depth MSAs from standard search algorithms. Existing MSA augmentation strategies (Potts models, iterative unmasking) either produce insufficiently diverse sequences or degrade performance, leaving a gap for a generative approach that can synthesize informative virtual homologs in a self-supervised manner.

## Input / Output

At inference, a query protein sequence is first expanded into an initial MSA via JackHMMER against UniRef90; this MSA (depth D, length L) is fed to MSA-Generator, which autoregressively produces a virtual MSA of matching depth. The original and generated MSAs are concatenated (X ⊕ Y) and passed to a downstream PSP algorithm (AlphaFold2 or RoseTTAFold). Generation uses nucleus sampling (top-p=50, top-k=10) to balance novelty and coherence, and an ensemble of three independent runs is selected by highest pLDDT.

## Method

MSA-Generator is a 260M-parameter encoder-decoder transformer pretrained on 2M MSAs via a novel sequences-to-sequences (seqs2seqs) task, where a randomly split subset of an MSA serves as source and the remainder as target—requiring no one-to-one correspondence between input and output sequences. The encoder employs tied-row attention (shared attention weights aggregated across all D sequences before softmax) and self-column attention to capture horizontal and vertical co-evolutionary patterns. The decoder adds two key modules: cross-row attention, which attends to a depth-wise average-pooled global representation of the encoder output (avoiding the O(D·L) cost of naive concatenation), and cross-column attention, which at each decoding step t attends to the t-th position across all input sequences to preserve amino-acid conservation. Notably, the decoder deliberately omits tied-row attention in favor of standard self-attention to maintain diversity among generated sequences. Pretraining uses ADAM-W (lr=5e-5, linear warmup, square-root decay) for 200k steps on 8 A100 GPUs with batch size 64.

## Results

On real-world challenging MSAs (depth ≤ 20 homologues), MSA-Generator improves AlphaFold2 by +6.4 pLDDT and +4.3 LDDT on CASP14, and by +8.6 pLDDT and +8.1 LDDT on CASP15, while also boosting TM-Score and GDT-TS by 4–6%. For RoseTTAFold, gains are +5.4 pLDDT / +5.0 LDDT (CASP14) and +6.4 pLDDT / +6.3 LDDT (CASP15). 75% of individual MSAs show substantial LDDT improvement, with standout cases like T1093-D1 (3 homologues) jumping from 45.5 to 70.77 LDDT and T1113 (10 homologues) from 32.6 to 80.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSA-Generator consistently outperforms both Potts model generation and Iterative Unmasking (Sgarbossa et al., 2022) across all four metrics on both CASP14 and CASP15 for both AlphaFold2 and RoseTTAFold. Potts models actually degrade performance (e.g., AlphaFold2 LDDT drops from 53.2 to 50.9 on CASP14), while Iterative Unmasking yields only marginal gains (+1.4 LDDT for AF2 on CASP14) due to reduced sequence diversity from the unmasking process. Single-sequence methods ESMFold and OmegaFold trail MSA-based approaches by 10–15 LDDT points, confirming that MSA quality remains the dominant factor. The largest absolute gains appear on CASP15 (where average MSA depth is slightly higher at 7.4 vs. 6.1), with AlphaFold2+MSA-Generator reaching 63.7 LDDT versus 55.6 for vanilla AF2. On the Orphan25 dataset (proteins with no detectable homologues), MSA-Generator still improves AlphaFold2 by +4.8 LDDT (61.6→66.4) and +0.08 TM-Score, outperforming both Potts and Iterative Unmasking baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MSA Transformer's tied-row and column attention mechanisms (Rao et al., 2021) into an autoregressive encoder-decoder generative framework, departing from the masked language modeling paradigm used by ESM and ProtTrans. It contrasts with prior protein sequence generation work (ProGen, ProGen2) that targets single sequences, and with EvoGen which operates as a meta-generative model requiring AlphaFold2 guidance. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of the 'seqs2seqs' task—generating multiple coherent sequences from multiple input sequences without strict one-to-one correspondence—which enables self-supervised pretraining by randomly partitioning homologous sequences from search results into source and target sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in protein structure prediction: proteins with sparse homologous families receive poor MSAs, degrading even state-of-the-art predictors like AlphaFold2. The finding that generated virtual MSAs can match or exceed real searched MSAs in ~50% of challenging cases suggests generative models can recover evolutionary information that search algorithms miss. However, open questions remain: pLDDT as a selection criterion is shown to be unreliable (sometimes anti-correlated with LDDT), no scale-up experiments were conducted, and generated MSAs remain shallow. The seqs2seqs formulation also raises the question of whether this self-supervised multi-sequence generation paradigm generalizes to other bio-sequence domains like RNA.
