## Problem

Source distribution estimation—finding a parameter distribution whose pushforward through a simulator matches observed data—is fundamentally ill-posed, as many distinct source distributions can yield identical data distributions. Existing likelihood-based methods like Neural Empirical Bayes require tractable simulator likelihoods and do not resolve this non-uniqueness, leaving the estimated source arbitrary among equally valid solutions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed dataset of observations (e.g., 10,000 synthetic or ~1,000 experimental samples), a differentiable simulator or trained surrogate that maps parameters to observations, and a bounded box domain over the parameter space. It produces a neural-sampler-parameterized source distribution q_φ(θ) over the parameter space; for high-dimensional simulators (e.g., 50–100 dimensional time series), the method can back-propagate through the simulator directly or use a surrogate trained on summary statistics (e.g., 5 spike-train features for Hodgkin-Huxley). The output is a flexible, unconstrained distribution that can be sampled from to generate new parameter configurations for downstream inference.

## Method

Sourcerer parameterizes the source distribution with a neural sampler (3-layer MLP with batch normalization) that transforms standard Gaussian noise into parameter samples, and optimizes a regularized objective that jointly maximizes the differential entropy of q_φ (estimated via the Kozachenko-Leonenko nearest-neighbor estimator) and minimizes the Sliced-Wasserstein distance between the pushforward q_φ^# and the empirical data distribution. The SWD is computed as the expected 1D Wasserstein distance over random projections, yielding O(n log n) complexity per projection and full differentiability without requiring a tractable likelihood. A dynamic λ schedule (linearly decaying from 1 to a final value over training) first encourages exploration of high-entropy distributions before tightening data consistency. The key theoretical contribution is proving that the maximum entropy source distribution is unique (via strict concavity of entropy and convexity of the source set), which resolves the ill-posedness of the original problem and makes the optimization target well-defined.

## Results

On the four benchmark tasks (Two Moons, Inverse Kinematics, SLCP, Gaussian Mixture), Sourcerer with entropy regularization achieves C2ST accuracies of 0.51–0.53 (near the ideal 0.50) while substantially increasing source entropy over NEB: e.g., 3.75 vs. 0.82 on IK and 9.81 vs. 7.56 on SLCP. On the high-dimensional SIR and Lotka-Volterra models, Sourcerer achieves C2ST accuracies of 0.55–0.57 compared to NEB's 0.76 (SIR) and 0.62 (LV), and again yields higher entropy (0.29 vs. −1.28 on LV). On the real-world Hodgkin-Huxley task, Sourcerer achieves C2ST ≈ 61% and SWD ≈ 0.08 in summary-statistic space, with entropy regularization producing sources that cover substantially more of the parameter space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Neural Empirical Bayes (NEB) from Vandegar et al. (AISTATS 2020), which maximizes the marginal likelihood using a normalizing-flow surrogate. Sourcerer wins decisively on the primary metric of source entropy across all four benchmark tasks while maintaining comparable C2ST accuracy (~50%): on IK, entropy rises from 0.82 (NEB) to 3.75 (Sourcerer with reg.), a 4.6× increase; on SLCP, from 7.56 to 9.81. On the high-dimensional SIR and Lotka-Volterra models, Sourcerer also wins on data fidelity, achieving C2ST of 0.55–0.57 versus NEB's 0.76 (SIR) and 0.62 (LV), because NEB requires a reduced-dimensionality surrogate that degrades performance. On the real Hodgkin-Huxley task, NEB is not directly compared (it would require a tractable likelihood surrogate), but Sourcerer's entropy-regularized source covers substantially more of the 13-dimensional parameter space than the unregularized estimate, avoiding the severe bias of ignoring viable parameter regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Neural Empirical Bayes (NEB) by Vandegar et al. [58], adopting its neural sampler architecture for source parameterization but replacing the marginal-likelihood objective with a Sliced-Wasserstein distance that operates purely on samples. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a maximum entropy principle to resolve the ill-posedness of source distribution estimation—a problem previously noted but not formally addressed—yielding a unique solution via strict concavity of entropy over the convex set of valid sources. The method also connects to the broader simulation-based inference literature (neural likelihood estimation, neural posterior estimation) and to maximum entropy approaches in high-energy physics ('unfolding') and cosmology, but is the first to combine these threads into a unified, likelihood-free framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By targeting the maximum entropy source, Sourcerer provides a principled, well-posed formulation of a problem that is otherwise underdetermined, with the practical consequence that estimated sources retain more uncertainty and avoid spurious concentration in parameter space—demonstrated clearly in the Hodgkin-Huxley application where unregularized sources miss entire viable regions. The sample-based formulation removes the need for tractable likelihoods, making the approach applicable to a broader class of scientific simulators, including deterministic ones and those with high-dimensional outputs. Open questions include principled selection of the regularization strength λ, the choice of distance metric (which can bias the estimated source), and whether the maximum entropy criterion is always the right regularizer compared to alternatives like Jeffrey's prior.
