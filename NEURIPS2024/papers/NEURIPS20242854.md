## Problem

Temporal action segmentation (TAS) and long-term action anticipation (LTA) are closely related tasks that have been studied in isolation, with task-specific models showing poor cross-task generalization. Prior multi-task approaches like TempAgg require separate architectures and training runs for each task. This paper asks whether a single unified model can jointly learn both tasks and whether the two tasks provide bi-directional benefits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242854/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes pre-trained I3D video features of shape T×C as input, along with a binary mask matrix that designates which frames are visible. For LTA inference, only the first ⌈αT⌉ frames are visible; the remaining frames are replaced by a single learnable mask token (1×C) before entering the encoder, so the encoder processes a mixed sequence of visual features and mask tokens. The output is a T×K frame-wise action label sequence generated by iteratively denoising Gaussian noise over 25 DDIM steps, where the first N^O frames correspond to segmentation predictions and the remaining N^A frames correspond to anticipation predictions.

## Method

ActFusion is a unified diffusion model with a modified ASFormer encoder (dilated 1-D convolutions + dilated attention with relative position bias) and a matching decoder that denoises action labels conditioned on encoder embeddings. The key mechanism is anticipative masking: during training, one of five masking strategies (no mask, anticipative, random, relation, boundary) is randomly selected, and masked frames are replaced by learnable tokens before the encoder, forcing the model to simultaneously classify visible frames and predict invisible future frames. The decoder receives noisy action labels A^s at a random timestep s along with encoder embeddings E, and predicts the clean labels A^0 via a fully-connected head with softmax. Training optimizes cross-entropy, boundary smoothing, and boundary alignment losses on both encoder and decoder outputs, with the encoder serving as an auxiliary segmentation head. At inference, no masking is used for TAS (α=1, β=0) while anticipative masking is used for LTA, and the decoder generates predictions via 25-step DDIM sampling from pure Gaussian noise.

## Results

On TAS, ActFusion achieves state-of-the-art average scores of 88.5 (50 Salads), 75.5 (Breakfast), and 89.6 (GTEA), outperforming the strongest prior method DiffAct (87.4, 75.1, 88.1) by 1.1, 0.4, and 1.5 points respectively. On LTA, ActFusion achieves 42.80 MoC on 50 Salads (α=0.3, β=0.1) and 35.79 on Breakfast (α=0.3, β=0.1), surpassing FUTR (35.15 and 32.27) by 7.65 and 3.52 points. A single unified model trained with one process delivers these results on both tasks simultaneously, without task-specific architectures or separate training runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On TAS, ActFusion outperforms all 25+ task-specific baselines across all three datasets. The most direct competitor, DiffAct [43] (also diffusion-based), is surpassed by 1.1 Avg. points on 50 Salads (88.5 vs 87.4), 0.4 on Breakfast (75.5 vs 75.1), and 1.5 on GTEA (89.6 vs 88.1). Stronger recent methods like SEDT (86.2 on 50 Salads) and MCFM (87.8) are also clearly beaten. On LTA, ActFusion achieves the best MoC on both 50 Salads and Breakfast across nearly all (α, β) settings; the closest competitor FUTR [25] is outperformed by up to 7.65 points (42.80 vs 35.15 on 50 Salads, α=0.3, β=0.1) and 3.52 points on Breakfast (35.79 vs 32.27). Cycle Consistency [20] and TempAgg [52] trail by even larger margins (e.g., 39.55 vs 34.76 on 50 Salads, α=0.2, β=0.1). Notably, ActFusion ties FUTR exactly at 39.55 on 50 Salads (α=0.2, β=0.1) but pulls ahead on all longer-horizon and higher-observation settings, confirming that the joint segmentation-anticipation training provides a consistent advantage as prediction difficulty increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

ActFusion builds directly on DiffAct (ICCV 2023), which first applied denoising diffusion to temporal action segmentation using an ASFormer-based encoder-decoder with dilated attention, and on FUTR (CVPR 2022) and TempAgg (ECCV 2020) for long-term action anticipation. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of anticipative masking—replacing unobserved future frames with learnable tokens before the encoder—thereby unifying segmentation and anticipation within a single diffusion training loop rather than relying on task-specific architectures or separate training runs. The masking design also draws on masked autoencoder ideas (MAE, VideoMAE) and the relation/boundary masks from DiffAct, but repurposes them to create a visible/invisible boundary that simultaneously serves both tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single unified model can outperform task-specific SOTA on both TAS and LTA across three benchmarks, suggesting that joint learning of complementary temporal tasks is more effective than the field's default of separate pipelines. It also surfaces a practical evaluation concern: prior LTA benchmarks leak ground-truth video length at test time, inflating reported scores, and the authors provide corrected numbers showing consistent degradation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the three egocentric cooking datasets, whether the slight frame-wise accuracy gap versus DiffAct can be closed via feature reconstruction, and whether the unified masking paradigm extends to other paired temporal tasks (e.g., detection + anticipation).
