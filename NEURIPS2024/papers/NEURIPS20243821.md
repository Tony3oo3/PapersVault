## Problem

Existing PDE emulator benchmarks (PDEBench, PDEArena) rely on fixed pre-computed datasets, preventing exploration of differentiable physics training, neural-hybrid correction, and systematic study of unrolled training paradigms. The relationship between classical numerical solvers and learned emulators—particularly how training methodology affects temporal generalization—remains underexplored. APEBench addresses this by tightly integrating a differentiable pseudo-spectral solver with the emulator training loop and introducing a unified taxonomy for unrolled training and PDE difficulty identifiers.

## Input / Output

The emulator receives a discrete PDE state u_h ∈ ℝ^N on a uniform Cartesian grid with periodic boundary conditions (same extent L and N points in each of D dimensions), along with PDE dynamics parameters (dimension, domain, time step Δt, and constitutive coefficients). The output is the predicted next-time-step state, evaluated via rollout metrics—primarily normalized RMSE aggregated geometrically over up to 100 autoregressive steps. Each experiment is uniquely identified by a minimal set of γ coefficients (analogous to CFL numbers) plus N and D, which encode the discrete dynamics difficulty without requiring full parameter specification.

## Method

APEBench's core technical contribution is a JAX-based differentiable Fourier pseudo-spectral ETDRK solver that integrates the linear part of semi-linear PDEs exactly via diagonalized matrix exponentials while approximating nonlinear terms with Runge-Kutta stages, achieving O(N^D D log N) cost per step with full automatic differentiability. This solver serves a dual role: procedurally generating training data in seconds and acting as a differentiable reference during training, enabling diverted-chain learning (autoregressive unrolling with one-step ground-truth branching) and neural-hybrid correction (coarse solver + corrector network). The benchmark introduces a unified taxonomy parameterized by unrolled steps T and branch length B that subsumes one-step supervised learning (T=B=1), supervised unrolling (T=B), and diverted-chain training (B=1, T>1). PDE difficulty is encoded via γ_s = α_s N^s 2^{s-1} D coefficients that directly correspond to stability limits of compact explicit finite-difference stencils, providing an intuitive exchange protocol for comparing experiments across 46 supported dynamics in 1D–3D.

## Results

APEBench evaluates five neural architectures (Conv, ResNet, UNet, Dilated ResNet, FNO) across 46 PDE dynamics in 1D–3D, reporting geometric-mean normalized RMSE over 100-step rollouts. ResNet consistently achieves the lowest error across nearly all dynamics and dimensions, with the gap widening in 3D (e.g., Burgers equation shows exponential performance degradation with dimensionality). For 1D advection, local convolutions with kernel size 3 fail immediately beyond CFL γ₁=1, while FNO remains agnostic to γ₁ changes; unrolling during training (e.g., 20 steps) improves long-term accuracy with only ~11% increased first-step error. In the neural-hybrid 2D advection task (γ₁=10.5), a ResNet correcting a coarse solver handling 50% of the difficulty outperforms both full-prediction ResNet and FNO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResNet (He et al., 2016) is the strongest architecture across nearly all PDE dynamics and dimensions, outperforming plain ConvNets by a growing margin in 3D where skip connections become critical. UNet (Ronneberger et al., 2015) shows the most consistent performance in 3D unbalanced advection but exhibits significant variance in 2D anisotropic diffusion; it is outperformed by ResNet in most other settings. Dilated ResNet (Stachenfeld et al., 2021) is the best long-range architecture in 1D but suffers a significant performance drop in 3D due to less uniform receptive-field coverage. FNO (Li et al., 2021) is agnostic to difficulty scaling for linear advection and excels in Navier-Stokes Kolmogorov flow, but is the least suitable for reaction-diffusion problems (due to low-frequency bias) and is hindered by limited active modes in higher-dimensional or fully-populated-spectrum scenarios. Among training methodologies, 5-step diverted-chain training matches or exceeds supervised unrolling in long-term accuracy while preserving short-term performance, a configuration only possible due to APEBench's differentiable ETDRK solver.

## Relation to Prior Work

APEBench extends the neural PDE emulator benchmarking line initiated by PDEBench (Takamoto et al., 2022) and PDEArena (Gupta and Brandstetter, 2023) by replacing fixed pre-computed datasets with a tightly integrated, differentiable Fourier pseudo-spectral ETDRK solver (building on Cox & Matthews, 2002, and Kassam & Trefethen, 2005 for stable coefficient computation). This integration enables training paradigms—diverted-chain learning and neural-hybrid correction—that prior benchmarks could not support because they require backpropagation through the reference simulator. The PDE identifier system (gamma values) draws a direct conceptual bridge to classical numerical stability criteria like the CFL condition, positioning learned emulators within the same analytical framework as finite-difference and pseudo-spectral methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APEBench provides the first benchmark where the reference solver is differentiable and procedurally generates data in seconds, enabling systematic study of how emulator-simulator interplay (unrolling depth, diverted chains, coarse-solver correction) affects temporal generalization. The finding that unrolled training injects numerical-stability-like properties into learned stencils, and that architecture-dynamics matching (receptive field vs. CFL number) governs performance, offers a principled lens for designing future emulators. Key limitations—periodic boundaries only, semi-linear PDEs, uniform Cartesian grids, no channel mixing—constrain applicability to complex geometries and fully nonlinear systems. Open questions include whether these insights transfer to parameter-conditioned or foundation-model emulators, and whether the unrolling taxonomy generalizes beyond the semi-linear regime.
