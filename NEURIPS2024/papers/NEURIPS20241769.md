## Problem

Developing universal sEMG-to-hand-pose models is severely limited by the absence of large, publicly available datasets with high-quality pose labels; existing sEMG datasets contain fewer than 20 participants, less than 20 minutes of data per subject, and are not open-sourced. This paper introduces emg2pose, the first large-scale open benchmark (193 users, 370 hours, 29 stages, 80M frames) with simultaneous 2kHz 16-channel sEMG and motion-capture-derived joint angle labels, along with challenging held-out generalization tasks across users, stages, and their combination.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 16-channel bipolar sEMG sampled at 2kHz (12-bit, bandpass filtered 20–850 Hz) from a wrist-worn sEMG-RD band, which is temporally downsampled to 50 Hz by the featurizer. For the tracking task, the initial hand joint angles (ground truth from motion capture) are provided as a conditioning state; for regression, no initial pose is given. Outputs are per-joint angular predictions (MCP/PIP/DIP for four fingers, IP/MCP/CMC for thumb, each with flexion-extension and abduction-adduction DOFs) and Cartesian landmark positions derived via forward kinematics on a default hand model, evaluated over 5-second trajectories.

## Method

The primary model, vemg2pose, exploits the fact that sEMG encodes muscle activity (i.e., motion) rather than static pose by predicting joint angular velocities instead of angles directly. A Time-Depth Separable Convolution (TDS) featurizer with three 1D temporal convolutions and four TDS blocks reduces 2 kHz sEMG to 50 Hz features, which are concatenated with the previous time-step's joint angle prediction and fed to a two-layer LSTM decoder (hidden size 512) that outputs velocity predictions. These velocities are integrated autoregressively to produce the next joint angle estimate, yielding inherently smooth trajectories without explicit velocity regularization. For the regression task, the decoder additionally predicts absolute angles for the first 250 ms before switching to velocity integration. The paper also provides re-implementations of NeuroPose (U-Net with residual bottleneck) and SensingDynamics (2D convolutions with SMU activations + MLP decoder) as baselines, all trained with L1 joint-angle loss plus a weighted Euclidean fingertip loss over 1–6 s non-overlapping windows.

## Results

On the regression task, vemg2pose achieves 12.2° mean angular error and 15.8 mm landmark distance on the held-out user test set, 15.2°/20.4 mm on held-out stages, and 15.8°/21.6 mm on the hardest held-out user+stage combination. On the tracking task (where initial pose is provided), vemg2pose reduces errors substantially to 7.7°/10.3 mm (user), 11.2°/15.2 mm (stage), and 11.0°/15.4 mm (user+stage). Performance degrades most for the held-out user+stage scenario, which is the most encompassing real-world deployment setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

vemg2pose outperforms both NeuroPose and SensingDynamics on every metric across all three generalization scenarios in the regression task. Against NeuroPose (U-Net predicting joint angles directly), vemg2pose reduces angular error by 1.0° (user), 2.0° (stage), and 1.7° (user+stage), and landmark distance by 1.7 mm, 3.6 mm, and 3.3 mm respectively. Against SensingDynamics (2D convolutions + MLP), the margins are larger: 3.3°–3.6° angular error and 5.4–6.2 mm landmark distance improvements. The velocity-based prediction space (predicting angular velocities and integrating) combined with the LSTM decoder is the key architectural advantage, as ablations show the angle-predicting variant (emg2pose) performs worse on tracking. All improvements are statistically significant after Bonferroni correction. NeuroPose and SensingDynamics are not evaluated on the tracking task since they were not designed to leverage the provided initial pose during inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first large-scale, publicly available sEMG hand pose dataset, filling a critical gap left by prior sEMG pose estimation work (NeuroPose, SensingDynamics) that relied on small, closed datasets of 11–13 participants. Conceptually, it repurposes the TDS convolutional architecture from speech recognition and the velocity-integration paradigm from control theory into the sEMG-to-pose setting, departing from prior approaches that predicted joint angles directly. The benchmark's multi-axis generalization protocol (held-out users, stages, and user-stage combinations) extends the single-axis evaluation used in prior sEMG works, drawing on the CV hand pose literature for scale and task framing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The emg2pose benchmark (193 users, 370 hours, 80M frames) is the first open dataset at a scale comparable to CV hand pose datasets, enabling systematic study of cross-user, cross-placement, and cross-kinematics generalization that was previously infeasible. The scale analysis confirms that performance improves substantially with more training users and stages, validating the data-hungry nature of sEMG modeling. Key open questions include closing the performance gap with CV-based methods, handling real-world signal aggressors (sweat, electrode artifacts, muscle fatigue), ensuring physical plausibility of predictions, and exploring state-space or diffusion-based sequence models. The held-out user+stage scenario remains the hardest and most realistic deployment setting, with substantial room for improvement.
