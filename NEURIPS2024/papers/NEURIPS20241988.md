## Problem

Current machine learning relies on large offline data collections and backpropagation, which is not biologically plausible due to its requirement for storing the full temporal stream, infinitely fast signal propagation, and update locking. Natural learning, by contrast, processes environmental information online with strict spatiotemporal locality. This paper asks whether one can derive learning laws from first principles (optimal control / Hamiltonian mechanics) that are inherently local in both space and time, and whether the resulting boundary-value problem can be solved causally using only initial conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous temporal signal stream u(t) ∈ R^d processed strictly online through a continuous-time RNN; the agent has no access to a stored data collection and can only retain information in limited-size buffers that implement selective attention over recent inputs. The output is twofold: a designated output neuron that tracks a target signal (e.g., sinusoidal or piecewise-cosine references), and the continuously evolving network weights w(t) that encode the learned dynamics. All computations are local in the graph structure—each neuron interacts only with its direct parents and children—and local in time, requiring only a finite temporal window.

## Method

The paper formulates online learning for continuous-time RNNs as an optimal control problem, yielding Hamilton's equations as the 'laws of learning' that govern both state evolution and weight updates. A key theoretical result shows that in the limit of infinite propagation speed (c→∞), these equations reduce exactly to backpropagation's delta-error recursion, positioning backprop as a special case of a more general spatiotemporally local diffusion process. The central practical challenge is that Hamilton's equations carry terminal boundary conditions (p_x(T)=p_w(T)=0) that are non-causal; to resolve this, the paper introduces the Hamiltonian Sign Flip (HSF) strategy, which flips the sign of the dynamics whenever the trajectory exits a bounded 'Hamiltonian track,' effectively converting the boundary-value problem into a forward-evolving Cauchy problem. In the c→∞ limit with HSF, the weight dynamics become gradient descent with momentum (heavy-ball dynamics), and the costate dynamics recover the backpropagation error propagation. Experiments on LQ tracking with a 5-neuron RNN validate that HSF enables stable online learning of both predictable and highly unpredictable signals.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with experiments serving as qualitative validation of the proposed Hamiltonian Sign Flip (HSF) policy rather than quantitative benchmarking. A 5-neuron recurrent network successfully tracks a sinusoidal target signal, with increasing the accuracy parameter from q=10 to q=1000 visibly improving tracking fidelity (Figures 1–2). The same architecture also tracks a deliberately hard-to-predict piecewise-cosine signal (q=100, Figure 3), and Figure 4 shows the corresponding Lagrangian and Hamiltonian evolution, confirming energy exchange with the environment. No standard quantitative metrics (e.g., MSE, tracking error) or statistical significance tests are reported; the authors explicitly note the experiments assess only qualitative behaviour.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare its method against any external baselines (e.g., BPTT, RTRL, or other online learning algorithms) in a head-to-head quantitative evaluation. The experiments are self-contained demonstrations that the HSF policy stabilizes the forward integration of Hamilton's equations and enables a small RNN to track reference signals in real time. The closest comparative context is theoretical: Corollary 1 shows that Backpropagation emerges as the c→∞ limit of the proposed local propagation equations, and Proposition 4 shows that the reversed-costate dynamics reduce to gradient descent with momentum (heavy-ball dynamics) in the same limit. The authors position their work as complementary to, rather than a direct replacement for, existing online RNN training methods like RTRL, noting that their scheme avoids storing Jacobian matrices but has not yet been benchmarked against them on standard tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational formulation of learning from feedforward networks (Cognitive Action Principle [3]) to recurrent architectures in a continuous-time online setting, building on the optimal control framework of LeCun et al. [19]. It departs from Neural ODE approaches [7, 17, 24] by tying the time variable directly to the environmental input stream rather than treating it as an internal computation depth, and from online RNN learning methods like RTRL [15] by avoiding Jacobian storage entirely. The Hamiltonian Sign Flip strategy for converting boundary-value problems into causal initial-value problems is a novel contribution not present in prior optimal control or learning literature, drawing an analogy to the Riccati equation solution in LQ control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that backpropagation emerges as the infinite-velocity limit of a spatiotemporal local Hamiltonian process provides a principled theoretical bridge between classical backprop and biologically plausible local learning, addressing a long-standing debate [8, 33, 21]. However, the practical evidence is very preliminary: experiments use only 5-neuron RNNs on simple sinusoidal and piecewise-cosine signals, with no comparison to existing online learning methods and no statistical significance testing. The conjecture that the finite-velocity system (Eq. 19) is a valid local optimization scheme remains unproven, and the HSF policy is ad hoc; whether it generalizes to complex architectures, real-world data streams, or lifelong learning scenarios (including catastrophic forgetting) is entirely open.
