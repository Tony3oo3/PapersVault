## Problem

Existing learning-augmented mechanism design work assumes the mechanism receives predictions about agent types (e.g., processing times, valuations), but in many practical settings such type-level data is unavailable due to privacy constraints, data protection, or simply missing historical records. This paper asks whether a mechanism can still benefit from imperfect information when it is given only a recommended outcome (an output advice) rather than predictions of the input, and how to evaluate the quality of that advice in a domain-agnostic way.

## Input / Output

The mechanism receives two inputs: the agents' reported types (bids) and a recommended outcome â drawn from the same outcome space as the mechanism's own output (e.g., a proposed job allocation, a facility location point, or a bundle assignment). The mechanism outputs a selected outcome and a payment vector, with performance measured by the approximation ratio relative to the optimal outcome. The key distinction from prior work is that the advice is an element of the outcome space, not a signal about the input, and its quality is captured by the universal metric ρ̂ = cost(welfare) of â / optimal cost(welfare), which is defined relative to the true (unknown) input.

## Method

The paper introduces a generic 'quality of recommendation' metric ρ̂ and applies it across four mechanism design paradigms. For facility location, it re-analyzes the Minimum Bounding Box and Coordinatewise Median mechanisms from Agrawal et al. [2] as functions of ρ̂, yielding tighter bounds than the prediction-error-based analysis. For unrelated-machine scheduling, it designs AllocationScaledGreedy, a weighted VCG mechanism that assigns binary weights (1 or n/β) to machine-job pairs based on whether the recommendation allocates the job to that machine, achieving (β+1)-consistency and n²/β-robustness with an asymptotically tight min{(β+1)ρ̂, n+ρ̂, n²/β} approximation. For combinatorial auctions, it shows that adding the recommended outcome to the range of any maximal-in-range VCG mechanism preserves strategyproofness and yields min{ρ̂, ρ_M}-approximation. For house allocation, it uses the TTC mechanism with the recommendation as an initial endowment, proving min{ρ̂, n}-approximation for unit-range valuations. The paper also establishes an impossibility result showing no weighted VCG mechanism can improve upon the AllocationScaledGreedy consistency-robustness trade-off given only an output recommendation.

## Results

The paper establishes approximation guarantees for mechanism design problems augmented with output advice, parameterized by the quality of recommendation ρ̂. For facility location (egalitarian), the Minimum Bounding Box mechanism achieves min{ρ̂, 1+√2}-approximation; for scheduling, the new AllocationScaledGreedy mechanism achieves min{(β+1)ρ̂, n+ρ̂, n²/β}-approximation (asymptotically tight); for combinatorial auctions, adding the recommendation to a maximal-in-range VCG yields min{ρ̂, ρ_M}-approximation (e.g., min{ρ̂, m/log m} for general valuations); and for house allocation, TTC with the recommendation as initial endowment gives min{ρ̂, n}-approximation for unit-range valuations. The paper also proves an impossibility result: no weighted VCG mechanism with β-consistency can achieve robustness better than Ω(n²/β) for scheduling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For facility location, the paper refines the bounds of Agrawal et al. [2] (EC 2022) by replacing the prediction-error-based analysis with the quality-of-recommendation metric ρ̂. Lemma 1 shows ρ̂ < η+1 for the egalitarian objective, and Lemma 2 shows √2ρ̂ < (√2λ²+2)/(1+λ)+η for the utilitarian objective, meaning the new bounds are strictly tighter on certain instances where the prediction error η is large but the recommended outcome is still near-optimal. Empirically, on real-world datasets (SNAP, Autotel Shared Cars, USGS Earthquake), the paper observes that ρ̂ and η can diverge significantly, confirming the practical advantage of the new metric. For scheduling, the AllocationScaledGreedy mechanism (β=1) matches the consistency and robustness of SimpleScaledGreedy from Balkanski et al. [8] (ITCS 2023), but does so using only the recommended allocation rather than the full predicted cost matrix, and crucially achieves smoothness (linear decay in ρ̂) where SimpleScaledGreedy exhibits a large discontinuity gap for small prediction errors. The paper further proves that within the class of weighted VCG mechanisms, no mechanism can improve upon the (β+1, n²/β) consistency-robustness trade-off, establishing optimality of AllocationScaledGreedy in this class.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-augmented mechanism design framework of Agrawal et al. (EC 2022) and Xu and Lu (IJCAI 2022) by replacing the standard information model—predictions about agent types—with a more constrained one: a recommended output (allocation, location, or assignment). The mechanisms studied (Minimum Bounding Box, Coordinatewise Median, ScaledGreedy variants, MIR VCG, TTC) are all drawn from prior work, but the paper re-analyzes them under the new 'quality of recommendation' metric, which measures the approximation ratio of the recommended outcome relative to the true optimum. The scheduling mechanism AllocationScaledGreedy is a new construction that simplifies the SimpleScaledGreedy of Balkanski et al. (ITCS 2023) by using only binary weights (1 or n/β) rather than the full predicted cost matrix, while retaining the same consistency-robustness tradeoff and adding a smoothness property that the prior mechanism lacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The output-advice model is practically motivated by settings where historical type data is unavailable due to privacy constraints, data protection regulations, or simply missing information (e.g., auction logs that record only winners and prices). The quality-of-recommendation metric offers a unified, domain-agnostic evaluation that avoids the pathologies of prediction-error-based analysis, where large input discrepancies can coexist with unchanged optimal solutions. The impossibility result (Theorem 5) establishing that no weighted VCG mechanism can beat the AllocationScaledGreedy consistency-robustness tradeoff given only an output recommendation is a clean boundary for the class. Open questions include whether the smoothness property can be extended to other mechanism classes, whether the quality-of-recommendation framework applies to online or dynamic settings, and whether richer output advice (e.g., a set of candidate outcomes rather than a single one) can close the gap with full type-prediction models.
