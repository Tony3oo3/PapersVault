## Problem

Large Restoration Models (LRMs) built on pre-trained diffusion models suffer from a hallucination dilemma: when degradations exceed the model's internal knowledge boundary, they generate textures and contents inconsistent with the original scene. Unlike LLMs that can be grounded via retrieval-augmented generation, existing LRMs have no mechanism to incorporate external visual knowledge, and simply scaling up parameters or training data is prohibitively expensive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241477/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality RGB image (bicubic up-sampled to the target resolution, e.g., 2048×2048) and one or more content-relevant high-quality reference images retrieved from a fixed database (e.g., DIV2K) via nearest-neighbor lookup in a VGG16/CLIP feature space. Reflective padding ensures both the target and reference denoising chains receive identically sized inputs. The output is a single high-quality restored image that preserves the LQ layout while incorporating scene-faithful textures from the reference.

## Method

ReFIR is a training-free framework that augments existing LRMs (e.g., SUPIR, SeeSR) with external knowledge through two stages: (1) a nearest-neighbor retriever selects content-relevant HQ images using cosine similarity in a pre-extracted feature space, and (2) a cross-image injection mechanism modifies the UNet decoder's self-attention to transfer textures from a parallel source denoising chain (C_S) into the target chain (C_T). The injection uses separate attention (intra-chain self-attention plus inter-chain cross-attention where Q_T queries K_S/V_S) to avoid the domain preference problem, spatial adaptive gating (pixel-wise cosine similarity masks) to handle spatial misalignment between LQ and reference, and AdaIN-based distribution alignment to prevent distribution shift in the target chain. The entire pipeline adds zero trainable parameters and is applied only in the decoder's detail-texture-restoration stage, leaving the ControlNet's structure-reconstruction phase untouched.

## Results

On CUFED5, SUPIR+ReFIR improves FID by 19.57 (168.26→148.69) and LPIPS by 0.0466 over the base SUPIR, while SeeSR+ReFIR gains 0.38 dB PSNR (19.94→20.32) and 8.3 FID points (142.92→134.62) over SeeSR. On WR-SR, SUPIR+ReFIR reduces FID from 75.85 to 71.82 and SeeSR+ReFIR achieves the best FID (61.22) and LPIPS (0.3460) among all compared methods. On the no-ground-truth RealPhoto60 benchmark, SeeSR+ReFIR surpasses SeeSR by 0.2866 NIQE and 1.59 MUSIQ, and SUPIR+ReFIR achieves the best NIQE (3.4593) and MUSIQ (60.49) overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReFIR consistently improves its base LRM across all metrics on both CUFED5 and WR-SR without any training: SUPIR+ReFIR gains 19.57 FID and 0.0466 LPIPS on CUFED5, while SeeSR+ReFIR gains 0.38 dB PSNR and 8.3 FID. Against other diffusion-based methods, SeeSR+ReFIR achieves the best PSNR (20.32), SSIM (0.5289), LPIPS (0.3338), and FID (134.62) on CUFED5, outperforming DiffBIR (FID 154.75), StableSR (FID 292.69), and PASD (FID 208.64). On WR-SR, SeeSR+ReFIR leads in FID (61.22 vs. DiffBIR's 76.05) and LPIPS (0.3460 vs. Real-ESRGAN's 0.3631), though Real-ESRGAN retains a slight PSNR edge (22.14 vs. 21.86). On the no-GT RealPhoto60 benchmark, SeeSR+ReFIR beats SeeSR by 0.2866 NIQE and 1.59 MUSIQ, and SUPIR+ReFIR achieves the best NIQE (3.4593) and MUSIQ (60.49) among all methods including DiffBIR (NIQE 2.8458, MUSIQ 65.20) — though DiffBIR still leads on these no-reference metrics. The RefSR baselines (C2-Matching, DATSR, MRefSR) are substantially outperformed on perceptual metrics (FID 282+ vs. 61–149) but retain higher PSNR/SSIM, reflecting their fidelity-oriented training objective versus ReFIR's perceptual quality focus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based Large Restoration Models (LRMs) such as SUPIR and SeeSR, which leverage pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models with ControlNet for photo-realistic restoration. It draws a direct conceptual analogy to Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in NLP, repurposing the idea of external knowledge retrieval to mitigate hallucination in image restoration. Unlike prior Reference-based Super-Resolution (RefSR) methods (C2-Matching, DATSR, MRefSR) that require training and operate at small scale, ReFIR is training-free, applies to billion-parameter LRMs, and flexibly handles zero to k reference images. The key methodological departure is the cross-image injection mechanism—combining separate attention, spatial adaptive gating, and AdaIN-based distribution alignment—to inject external texture knowledge into the UNet decoder's self-attention without modifying model weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReFIR addresses a fundamental limitation of LRMs—hallucination under severe degradation—through a practical, training-free plug-in that can be applied to any existing LRM, making it immediately useful for practitioners who cannot retrain large models. However, the computational overhead is non-trivial (GPU memory increases ~1.38×, inference time ~2.2× for SUPIR), and the simple semantic-based retriever leaves room for improvement with texture-aware or task-specific retrieval. Open questions include adaptive decision-making on whether retrieval is needed, more efficient injection (e.g., only 12% of attention layers are modified), and extending the framework to specialized domains like face restoration or scene text where fidelity demands are extreme.
