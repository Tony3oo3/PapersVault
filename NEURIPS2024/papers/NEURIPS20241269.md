## Problem

Foundation models such as GPT-4V, SAM, and LLaMA are specialized for specific output types (language, masks, images) and cannot natively handle multiple granularities from pixel to image level in an interleaved manner. No existing work achieves image-level retrieval, pixel-level segmentation, and interleaved vision-language understanding in a single model, and no benchmark exists for evaluating interleaved understanding between interactive image parts and phrases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241269/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen embeddings from a vision foundation model (X-Decoder or SAM) and a language foundation model (LLaMA-7B or UniCL), processing them through a lightweight trainable interface without modifying any foundation model weights. Inputs include RGB images, text prompts, and interleaved image-text sequences where text entities can be replaced by visual references from other images; outputs span segmentation masks (via pixel projection), similarity scores for retrieval and grounding (via semantic projection), and retrieval rankings. The model operates at a fixed resolution (384×384 or 1024×1024) across all tasks to ensure cross-task generalization, and the interleaved entries are structured as ordered sequences of images, texts, and connection tokens.

## Method

FIND is a lightweight transformer interface that projects frozen vision and language foundation model embeddings into a unified shared space using a prompt-query paradigm: prompts (sampled from foundation model embeddings via downsampling, ROI pooling, or rearrangement) serve as information sources, while learnable queries act as 'buckets' that accumulate information through content attention (queries attend to corresponding prompts) and conditional attention (prompts and queries reason internally via self-attention). All visual understanding tasks—retrieval, grounding, segmentation—are unified as a single similarity-matching problem between a source domain and a target domain, with task-specific behavior controlled by attention masks and separate projection heads (semantic MLP for retrieval and category mapping, pixel MLP for mask prediction). The model is trained jointly on panoptic segmentation, grounded segmentation, interactive segmentation, image-text retrieval, interleave retrieval, and interleave grounding with a linear combination of cross-entropy, BCE, Dice, and contrastive losses. The key insight is that this unified multi-task training creates an interleaved shared embedding space where vision and language references become interchangeable, enabling a single set of weights to handle tasks spanning both modality and granularity.

## Results

On generic segmentation (COCO), FIND (L) achieves PQ 56.7 and mAP 50.8, competitive with Mask2Former (L) (PQ 57.8) while adding grounding and retrieval in a single model. On grounded segmentation, FIND (L) reaches cIoU 66.6 on COCO-Entity and 66.6 on COCO-Paragraph, outperforming SEEM (L) by ~10 points (56.7). On the new interleave grounding task, FIND (L) achieves cIoU 76.3 on COCO-Entity, surpassing SEEM (L) by +8 points (68.3) and Grounding-SAM (H) by +17.4 points (58.9). On interleave retrieval, FIND (L) reaches IR@5 53.4 (COCO-Entity) and 62.7 (COCO-Paragraph), significantly outperforming ImageBind (51.4/58.7) and X-Decoder (L) (26.8/32.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIND (L) substantially outperforms SEEM (L) on grounded segmentation, achieving cIoU 66.6 vs. 56.7 on COCO-Entity (+9.9) and 66.6 vs. 53.8 on COCO-Paragraph (+12.8), while matching SEEM on interactive segmentation (88.5/89.5/77.4 vs. 88.5/89.6/76.5). On the new interleave grounding task, FIND (L) beats SEEM (L) by +8.0 cIoU on COCO-Entity (76.3 vs. 68.3) and Grounding-SAM (H) by +17.4 cIoU (76.3 vs. 58.9). For interleave retrieval, FIND (L) achieves IR@5 53.4 on COCO-Entity, outperforming ImageBind (51.4), X-Decoder (L) (26.8), and FROMAGe (L) (24.1) by large margins. On standard image-text retrieval, FIND (L) trails BLIP-2 (L) on COCO-Karpathy (IR@1 46.3 vs. 63.4) and X-Decoder (L) (46.3 vs. 56.4), which the authors attribute to using a uniform 384×384 resolution and smaller batch size across all tasks. On generic panoptic segmentation, FIND (L) is slightly below Mask2Former (L) in PQ (56.7 vs. 57.8) but exceeds it in mAP (50.8 vs. 48.6), and the paper notes Mask2Former additionally uses deformable convolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIND departs from the dominant multimodal paradigm (Flamingo, BLIP-2, LLaVA) where vision encoder outputs are fed into a language decoder that acts as an interpreter. Instead, it projects embeddings from frozen vision and language foundation models (X-Decoder/SAM and LLaMA/UniCL) into a shared space via a lightweight transformer interface, enabling direct cross-modal matching without any foundation model weight updates. The embedding sampling strategy is inherited from SEEM, and the vision backbone reuses X-Decoder's pre-trained weights, but the key conceptual shift is treating all visual understanding tasks (retrieval, grounding, segmentation) as a unified source-to-target similarity matching problem in a single interleaved embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FIND-Bench is the first benchmark to formally define and evaluate interleaved segmentation and interleaved retrieval, filling a gap the authors identify in prior interleaved understanding work that lacked both training data and evaluation protocols. The unified interface demonstrates that a single set of weights can handle pixel-level and image-level tasks simultaneously, with mutual benefits between grounded and referring segmentation. However, evaluation is restricted to COCO, and the authors acknowledge the method may not generalize to in-the-wild settings; scaling the data engine beyond COCO and validating on diverse domains remain open questions. The demonstrated applications (album search, video localization, 3D feature fields for robotics) suggest broader utility for personalized foundation models.
