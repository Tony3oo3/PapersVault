## Problem

Existing deterministic finetuning methods for CLIP in continual learning overlook the uncertainties in cross-modal interactions between visual and textual cues, leading to cross-modal feature deviation and catastrophic forgetting. Prior probabilistic finetuning approaches (e.g., VPT) either impose priors in prompt space (making them prompt-type dependent and incompatible with conditional prompting) or excessively trade in-domain accuracy for generalization, both of which are detrimental for CL deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244101/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, processed by CLIP's frozen ViT image encoder to produce a globally pooled visual feature vector. Text features are derived from arbitrary prompt types (hand-crafted templates, CoOp soft prompts, MaPLe multi-modal prompts, or AttriCLIP instance-conditioned prompts) through the frozen text encoder. The output is a class prediction over all seen classes, accompanied by epistemic uncertainty quantified via M=20 Monte Carlo samples from task-specific Gaussian posteriors, enabling downstream uses such as calibration-aware confidence scoring and novel data detection.

## Method

CLAP4CLIP introduces a variational inference framework that models the distribution of visual-guided text features per task, rather than the prompt embedding space, making it prompt-type agnostic. A shared Visual-guided Attention (VGA) module performs text-to-visual cross-attention (text features as queries, visual features as keys/values) with a task-specific mask to produce aligned text embeddings in a single forward pass, preventing the cross-modal deviation observed with generic adapters. Task-specific probabilistic adapters (linear layers parameterizing μ and σ of a Gaussian) form a mixture-of-experts ensemble over these aligned embeddings, with M MC samples fused back into the features before computing cosine-similarity logits. Forgetting is mitigated through two language-aware mechanisms: a data-free distillation loss that regularizes past-task latent samples toward hand-crafted prompt features, and informed weight initialization of new task adapters from the statistics of those same prompt features to bridge the stability gap. A memory consolidation training stage with herding-based replay further fine-tunes all task adapters on class-balanced rehearsal data while freezing the shared VGA module.

## Results

CLAP4CLIP achieves the best average and last-task accuracy across all five evaluation datasets: 86.13/78.21 on CIFAR-100, 87.76/79.16 on ImageNet-100, 85.77/79.98 on ImageNet-R, 86.93/81.64 on CUB-200, and 91.37/89.67 on VTAB. The strongest deterministic baseline, PROOF, reaches 85.19/76.4 on CIFAR-100 and 85.93/79.02 on ImageNet-100, yielding gaps of ~1.0% (Avg) and ~1.8% (Last) for CLAP4CLIP. The probabilistic variant also improves backward transfer (BwT) over its deterministic counterpart (e.g., −0.141 vs −0.106 on CIFAR-100) and reduces Expected Calibration Error (ECE) from 0.245 to 0.224 (CoOp variant on CIFAR-100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLAP4CLIP (Ours) outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset where they are directly compared. Against the strongest CLIP-based CL baseline PROOF [39], CLAP4CLIP gains +1.29% Avg and +1.66% Last on CIFAR-100 (86.13 vs 84.84; 78.21 vs 76.55) and +1.83% Avg on ImageNet-100 (87.76 vs 85.93). Against the rehearsal-free SOTA CODA-P [38], the gap is +0.94% Avg on CIFAR-100 and +2.16% Avg on CUB-200 (86.93 vs 84.77). The vision-only baselines L2P [65] and DualPrompt [59] trail by 7.2% and 4.0% Avg on CIFAR-100 respectively, confirming the benefit of joint vision-language modeling. Among prompt-based methods, CoOp [12] (81.17 Avg) and MaPLe [20] (82.74 Avg) are the closest deterministic competitors on CIFAR-100, yet CLAP4CLIP still leads by 5.0% and 3.4% Avg. The probabilistic baseline VPT [17] lags most severely (73.4 Avg on CIFAR-100), validating the paper's argument that prompt-space priors trade in-domain accuracy excessively. Notably, the CoOp + Ours variant achieves the best CUB-200 (86.99 Avg) and VTAB (92.51 Avg) scores, while AttriCLIP + Ours leads on ImageNet-R (86.35 Avg), demonstrating that CLAP4CLIP's modular design inherits the strengths of each underlying prompt strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of probabilistic finetuning for vision-language models by relocating the variational prior from the prompt embedding space (as in VPT [17] and Prompt Distribution Learning [16]) to the text feature output space, making the framework prompt-type agnostic and compatible with hand-crafted, soft, multi-modal, and instance-conditioned prompts. It builds directly on the CLIP-Adapter residual formulation [13] but replaces the deterministic adapter with a probabilistic one parameterizing a Gaussian posterior, and introduces a visual-guided attention (VGA) module to counter cross-modal feature deviation during incremental training. The task-specific adapters are framed as a Bayesian mixture-of-experts ensemble [21,22], and the anti-forgetting strategy diverges from data-hungry web-exploration methods [23,24] by exploiting CLIP's pre-trained language knowledge for weight initialization and distribution regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular design—where CLAP4CLIP can be plugged into any existing prompt-based finetuning method without architectural changes—makes it a practical drop-in upgrade for CL practitioners working with CLIP, and the consistent BwT > 0 on cross-dataset VTAB suggests the probabilistic modeling can impart anti-forgetting properties onto otherwise deterministic baselines. The out-of-the-box uncertainty utilities (post-hoc novel data detection, variance-based exemplar selection) highlight a broader implication: probabilistic CL finetuning unlocks capabilities (reliable confidence, OOD awareness) that deterministic methods structurally cannot provide, which is critical for safety-critical deployments. Open questions include whether the VGA module's reliance on globally pooled visual features limits its effectiveness on fine-grained or spatially structured tasks, and whether the MC sampling overhead (M=20) becomes prohibitive at scale or in latency-sensitive edge settings.
