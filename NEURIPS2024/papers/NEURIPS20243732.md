## Problem

Computing the full softmax of a high-dimensional matrix-vector product is a major bottleneck in LLM inference and multiclass classification, yet in many applications only the top few outputs are needed. Existing acceleration methods reduce complexity in the vocabulary size n (via hierarchical trees, sampled softmax, or LSH) but do not gain in the inner dimension d, and most lack direct probabilistic guarantees on the true softmax output. This paper asks whether one can compute the top-k softmax values with fewer than nd coordinate evaluations while providing PAC guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a matrix A (e.g., an lm-head weight matrix of shape n×d) and a query vector x (e.g., a hidden state of dimension d), along with a temperature β and target error/failure parameters ε and δ. Rather than computing the full product Ax, it adaptively samples individual coordinates of the dot products A_i·x, treating each row of A as a bandit arm. The output is the index i* of the largest softmax entry and a multiplicative-accuracy estimate p̂_{i*} of its value, with the guarantee that both are correct simultaneously with probability at least 1−δ.

## Method

AdaptiveSoftmax converts the softmax computation into a three-stage statistical estimation problem using multi-armed bandit techniques. First, NormalizationEstimation adaptively estimates the partition function Z_β by sampling each arm with frequencies proportional to e^{βμ_i} (first-order) and e^{βμ_i/2} (second-order), derived via minimax optimization of the Taylor expansion error; this is the paper's key novel subroutine. Second, a multi-round ε-arm best-arm identification algorithm (adapted from Karnin et al.) identifies the top arm using sub-Gaussian confidence intervals. Third, the identified arm is sampled to estimate its value to multiplicative accuracy ε/4. The overall sample complexity is O(β²σ² n log(n/δ)) in the large-gap regime, scaling sublinearly in d, and the algorithm requires no preprocessing or prior knowledge of label frequencies. Practical improvements include importance sampling over coordinates weighted by |x_j|, Gumbel-based sampling without replacement for tighter variance, and optional randomized Hadamard transforms to reduce the sub-Gaussian parameter.

## Results

AdaptiveSoftmax achieves >10x sample complexity reduction over brute-force softmax on most evaluated datasets, with the headline result being a 32.65x gain (at δ=10%, ε=30%) for Mistral7B on Wikitext. On CNN classifiers, gains are 8.95x for MNIST (Shallow CNN) and 5.18x for EuroSAT (VGG-19) at δ=10%. Success rates (correctly identifying the top softmax entry and estimating it within 30% multiplicative error) range from 80–99% depending on the target failure probability δ, with 97–99% success at δ=1% across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other adaptive or approximate softmax methods; the sole baseline is the brute-force (naive) softmax computation, which requires exactly nd scalar multiplications per query. AdaptiveSoftmax consistently outperforms this baseline across all settings, with gains scaling with the inner dimension d: smaller models (GPT-2, d=768) see ~8x gains while larger models (Mistral7B, d=4096; Llama3-7B, d=4096) see 15–33x gains. The gains are most dramatic when the softmax output is peaked (one dominant class), as the adaptive algorithm concentrates samples on the top arm. The authors note that in practice, wall-clock time may be less favorable than the sample-complexity ratio suggests, since BLAS-optimized batched matrix-vector products can be faster than the inherently sequential adaptive sampling, though the algorithm's logarithmic number of adaptivity rounds mitigates this to some degree.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a specialized extension of Baharav et al.'s (AISTATS 2022) framework for approximate function evaluation via multi-armed bandits, adapted to exploit the specific structure of the softmax function to remove the Lipschitz-gradient assumption and improve sample complexity. The best-arm identification subroutine is a modification of the multi-round ε-arm algorithm from Karnin et al. (ICML 2013). Unlike prior softmax acceleration methods (hierarchical softmax, sampled softmax, LSH-based attention) that reduce complexity in the vocabulary size n, this work reduces complexity in the feature dimension d via adaptive coordinate sampling, and provides direct PAC guarantees on the true softmax output rather than a proxy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive partition function estimation technique is of independent interest beyond softmax (e.g., kernel density estimation), and the sublinear-in-d scaling is a genuinely new axis of softmax acceleration. However, the paper acknowledges significant practical limitations: adaptivity is inherently sequential and competes with BLAS-optimized batch computation, the method is most beneficial only when d is large, and in LLMs the final softmax layer may not be the dominant computational bottleneck. Open questions include combining this bandit approach with LSH for subquadratic-in-n and sublinear-in-d complexity, and developing batched variants that preserve adaptivity while remaining hardware-friendly.
