## Problem

Simple bilevel optimization requires minimizing a convex smooth upper-level objective over the (implicitly defined) optimal solution set of a convex smooth lower-level problem. Because this feasible set lacks an explicit characterization, standard projection-based accelerated methods cannot be applied directly, and prior first-order methods achieved at best O(1/ε_f) or O(1/ε_g) iteration complexity—falling short of the O(1/√ε) rate that is optimal for single-level convex constrained optimization.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a precomputed sequence {g_k}_{k=0}^K (typically generated by running an accelerated projected gradient method on g, satisfying g_k − g* ≤ 2L_g‖x_0−x*‖²/(k+1)²) and an initial point x_0 ∈ Z. It outputs x_K, an (ε_f, ε_g)-optimal solution, along with certified bounds on both the suboptimality gap f(x_K)−f* and the infeasibility gap g(x_K)−g*. A practical requirement is that the Euclidean projection onto X_k (the intersection of Z with a halfspace) must be computable in closed form or via Dykstra's algorithm, which constrains the choice of Z.

## Method

AGM-BiO embeds Nesterov's three-sequence accelerated gradient framework (with iterates {x_k}, {y_k}, {z_k} and weights {a_k}, {A_k}) into the bilevel setting by replacing the intractable projection onto the lower-level solution set X_g* with a projection onto a tractable surrogate set X_k = {z ∈ Z : g(y_k) + ⟨∇g(y_k), z − y_k⟩ ≤ g_k}, a halfspace cut at the auxiliary iterate y_k. The critical design choice is constructing the cutting plane at y_k rather than x_k or z_k, which is shown to be essential for the convergence proof. With stepsize a_k = (k+1)/(4L_f), the method achieves O(1/k²) suboptimality and O(1/k) infeasibility in the compact case, yielding O(max{1/√ε_f, 1/ε_g}) iterations. Under an r-th-order Hölderian error bound on g, a carefully tuned stepsize a_k = γ(k+1)/(4L_f) with γ depending on K and r improves both gaps to Õ(k^{−2r/(2r−1)}), matching the optimal single-level rate when r = 1 (weak sharpness). A proximal variant (P-AGM-BiO) extends the method to composite objectives by replacing the projection with a proximal update on f_2 + δ_{X_k}.

## Results

AGM-BiO achieves an iteration complexity of O(max{1/√ε_f, 1/ε_g}) for finding an (ε_f, ε_g)-optimal solution when the feasible set Z is compact, improving upon the O(max{1/ε_f, 1/ε_g}) rate of prior methods like CG-BiO and R-APM. Under the r-th order Hölderian error bound on g, the complexity improves to Õ(max{ε_f^(-(2r-1)/(2r)), ε_g^(-(2r-1)/(2r))}), which for r=1 (weak sharpness) yields Õ(max{1/√ε_f, 1/√ε_g}), matching the optimal single-level convex optimization rate. In experiments on the Wikipedia Math Essential over-parameterized regression problem (n=1068, d=730), AGM-BiO achieves the smallest absolute suboptimality gap among all seven compared methods. On the linear inverse problem with n=100, AGM-BiO significantly outperforms all baselines in both infeasibility and absolute suboptimality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGM-BiO strictly improves upon all prior methods in the smooth-convex setting. Compared to CG-BiO [6] and R-APM [8], which both achieve O(1/ε_f) for suboptimality, AGM-BiO improves the upper-level rate to O(1/√ε_f) while maintaining O(1/ε_g) for infeasibility. Against a-IRG [12] (O(1/ε_f⁴)) and Bi-SG [13]/SEA [14] (O(1/ε_f²)), the improvement is dramatic. Under the r-th Hölderian error bound, AGM-BiO achieves Õ(ε_f^(-(2r-1)/(2r))) which is superior to PB-APG [16]'s O(ε_f^(-0.5r)) for r>1, since (2r-1)/(2r) < 0.5r for all r>1. The closest competitor is Bisec-BiO [15], which also achieves Õ(max{ε_f^{-0.5}, ε_g^{-0.5}}) under weak sharpness, but requires the ability to project onto the sublevel set of f — an assumption that fails for common objectives like mean squared loss. In the over-parameterized regression experiment, AGM-BiO achieves the smallest absolute suboptimality gap and faster infeasibility convergence than R-APM and PB-APG; in the linear inverse problem (n=100), AGM-BiO significantly outperforms all baselines including R-APM, which gets stuck at a fixed accuracy due to its static regularization parameter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the cutting-plane approximation of the lower-level solution set introduced in CG-BiO [6] and combines it with Nesterov-style accelerated gradient updates (à la FISTA) to solve the upper-level problem over the approximated set. It departs from R-APM [8] and PB-APG [16], which instead run accelerated proximal gradient on a weighted sum of the upper- and lower-level objectives, by treating the two levels more separately: the lower level is handled through a sequence of halfspace constraints while the upper level receives full Nesterov acceleration. The Hölderian error bound analysis extends the weak sharpness treatment in [8] to general r-th order bounds, yielding a unified complexity picture that improves on both CG-BiO and PB-APG for r > 1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the best-known non-asymptotic iteration complexity for convex smooth simple bilevel optimization, matching the single-level accelerated gradient rate of Õ(1/√ε) under the first-order Hölderian error bound (weak sharpness). This closes a gap where prior methods (CG-BiO, R-APM) required O(1/ε) iterations for the upper level. A key open question is whether the compactness assumption on Z can be removed in the base (non-Hölderian) case, and whether the cutting-plane surrogate can be replaced by cheaper approximations in high-dimensional or stochastic settings. The method's practical utility is somewhat limited by the need to solve a projection subproblem onto the intersection of Z and a halfspace at each iteration, which may be expensive for complex constraint sets.
