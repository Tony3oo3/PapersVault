## Problem

Current information retrieval systems adopt a pipeline architecture where LLMs are confined to individual components (retriever, reranker, reader), preventing knowledge sharing and deep collaboration across the full IR process. This fragmentation limits the exploitation of LLMs' unified language understanding, generation, and assessment capabilities, and results in complex multi-model implementations with sub-optimal performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242027/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text query as input and directly generates the full text content of relevant passages (not identifiers or embeddings) as output, with each generated passage guaranteed to exactly match a corpus passage via trie-based constrained decoding. The model first generates a document title as global context, then the passage itself, and finally produces a self-assessment score combining title generation probability and a relevance-acceptance probability. For RAG applications, the output extends to a generated answer appended after the assessment step.

## Method

Self-Retrieval consolidates indexing, retrieval, and reranking into a single LLM trained with cross-entropy loss in an auto-regressive manner. For indexing, the corpus is internalized via a self-supervised sentence-to-passage task where each sentence in a passage serves as input to generate the full passage, effectively performing continued pre-training on the corpus. For retrieval, the model generates a document title followed by the passage using a trie-based constrained decoding algorithm that restricts token generation to valid corpus continuations, ensuring exact passage matching without relying on document identifiers. For reranking, a self-assessment mechanism scores each generated passage by the model's probability of accepting it as relevant, with the final score computed as the product of a softmax-normalized title score and a softmax-normalized assessment score. The approach is trained on three data types—self-supervised indexing instances, supervised query-passage pairs, and positive/negative reranking examples—enabling the single model to simultaneously memorize the corpus, retrieve relevant content, and assess relevance in one inference turn.

## Results

On passage retrieval, Self-Retrieval (StableLM-3B) achieves MRR@5 of 69.45 on NQ and 66.72 on TriviaQA, outperforming the strongest dense retrieval baseline BGE-FT by 5.46 and 5.07 points respectively. The larger Llama2-7B variant further improves to MRR@5 of 70.00 (NQ) and 68.74 (TriviaQA), surpassing all retriever-reranker pipelines. On document retrieval (NQ320K), Self-Retrieval achieves R@1 of 73.3 and MRR@100 of 80.7, exceeding the previous best generative method GenRet by 5.2 and 4.8 points. In RAG experiments, Self-Retrieval-7B reaches 53.26 EM on NQ (10K docs), outperforming the BGE-FT + Llama2-FT pipeline by 4.16 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Self-Retrieval (3B) decisively outperforms all sparse and dense retrieval baselines: it beats BGE-FT by 5.46 MRR@5 on NQ and 5.07 on TriviaQA, and surpasses GritLM (7B) by 12.42 and 15.58 MRR@5 respectively. Against the strongest retriever-reranker pipelines, Self-Retrieval-3B edges out GTR-XL+BGE-Reranker-FT (69.45 vs 66.06 MRR@5 on NQ) and GritLM+BGE-Reranker-FT (69.45 vs 66.98 on NQ), though it narrowly trails GritLM+BGE-Reranker-FT on TriviaQA MRR@5 (66.72 vs 67.21). The 7B variant (Llama2) wins on all metrics across both datasets, achieving 70.00 MRR@5 on NQ and 68.74 on TriviaQA. All generative retrieval baselines (DSI, SEAL, NCI, DSI-QG) are substantially outperformed, with DSI-XXL (11.3B) trailing Self-Retrieval-3B by 19.25 MRR@5 on NQ. On document retrieval (NQ320K), Self-Retrieval beats GenRet by 5.2 R@1 and 4.8 MRR@100, and on MS MARCO it matches GenRet's R@1 (47.8 vs 47.9) while surpassing all other baselines by 10+ points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of using LLMs as individual components within a multi-stage IR pipeline (query rewriting, retrieval, reranking) by consolidating all IR functions into a single LLM. It also diverges from prior generative retrieval methods (DSI, NCI, SEAL, GenRet) that rely on document identifiers or n-grams as proxies, instead generating actual passage content via trie-based constrained decoding. The corpus internalization mechanism repurposes self-supervised sentence-to-passage prediction as an indexing step, framing it as continued pre-training, while the self-assessment reranking leverages the same model's generative capability to judge relevance—eliminating the need for separate encoder-decoder architectures or external rerankers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Self-Retrieval demonstrates that a single LLM can replace the entire IR pipeline and outperform multi-component retriever-reranker systems, suggesting a simpler and more coherent architecture for LLM-driven retrieval. The natural compatibility with RAG (where retrieval and answer generation share the same model) reduces the knowledge gap between retrieval and reading, with measurable downstream gains. However, scalability remains a significant open question: experiments are limited to 200K documents (~3M passages), retrieval efficiency is lower than sparse/dense methods, and incremental corpus updates are not yet supported—key barriers to production deployment.
