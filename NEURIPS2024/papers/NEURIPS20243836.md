## Problem

Research on LLM-based personal attribute inference (PAI) is hampered by the absence of publicly available datasets: real-world data cannot be released due to GDPR and ethical constraints, and prior synthetic samples (e.g., Staab et al. 2024) were too stylistically and structurally restricted to support meaningful inference research. This paper addresses that gap by constructing a fully synthetic, privacy-preserving dataset that is both realistic enough to mirror real-world PAI difficulty and diverse enough to enable attribute-level analysis.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 300 hand-verified synthetic profiles (each specifying 8 attributes: age, sex, income, location, birthplace, education, occupation, relationship status, plus a GPT-4-generated writing-style description) and 103 thread topics soft-targeted to specific attributes across 91 subreddit categories. It produces 7,823 Reddit-style comments organized in tree-structured threads (mean ~76 comments/thread, ~34 profiles/thread), each carrying human-verified attribute labels with a 1–5 inference-hardness rating; at the profile level this aggregates to 1,110 verified labels. For evaluation, all comments from a single profile are concatenated and fed zero-shot to 18 LLMs, which return step-by-step reasoning plus top-3 attribute guesses scored against ground truth.

## Method

The core contribution is a personalized multi-agent LLM simulation framework that mimics Reddit comment threads. First, GPT-4 generates diverse profiles and per-profile writing-style anchors; each profile seeds an LLM agent. Thread generation proceeds in rounds: a top-level topic is sampled, agents self-select participation via a profile-interest prompt, then in each round agents are randomly chosen to either reply or abstain, selecting a target comment via a scoring function that penalizes depth and boosts chains where the agent has already engaged. Before emitting a comment, each agent performs a chain-of-thought self-check against its style description, and a dedicated disagreement option counteracts LLM sycophancy to preserve opinion diversity. Comments are instructed to reflect the profile implicitly rather than stating attributes outright, and topics are soft-targeted to specific attributes by conditioning on real subreddit post styles. After generation, an LLM oracle tags each comment for inferrable attributes, and a human labeler verifies, corrects, and assigns hardness levels, yielding the final SynthPAI release. Validation includes a 40-participant human study (51.9% real-vs-synthetic discrimination, near chance) and full reproduction of Staab et al.'s 18-model PAI benchmark, showing consistent ~10% accuracy drop with preserved model rankings and attribute-level patterns.

## Results

On SynthPAI, GPT-4 achieves 76% overall personal attribute inference (PAI) accuracy across 18 evaluated LLMs, with a consistent ~10% drop relative to the ~86% reported on real-world data by Staab et al. [6], while preserving the relative ranking of all models. Per-attribute accuracy for GPT-4 ranges from 66.7% (income) to 92.8% (sex), with a maximum deviation of <12.5% from real-world values. In a human study with 40 participants, humans achieve only 51.9% accuracy at distinguishing SynthPAI comments from real Reddit comments, barely above random guessing. After Azure Language Services anonymization, GPT-4 still retains ~50% accuracy on location inference, confirming that standard anonymizers fail to prevent PAI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole baseline comparison is against Staab et al. [6] (ICLR 2024), which evaluated PAI on a real-world Reddit dataset (PersonalReddit) that was not publicly released. On SynthPAI, GPT-4 achieves 76% overall accuracy versus ~86% on real data, a ~10% absolute drop that is remarkably consistent across all 18 models tested, preserving their relative ordering (GPT-4 > Claude-3 Opus > Gemini-1.5 Pro > Llama-3 70B, etc.). Per-attribute deviations stay within ±12.5%, with sex inference actually performing slightly better on SynthPAI (92.8% vs. 97.8% real) and relationship status showing the largest gap (−12.3%). The authors attribute the slight overall difficulty increase to their explicit avoidance of trivially explicit self-disclosures (e.g., stating one's age or location outright), which are common in real Reddit posts. Critically, the anonymization experiment also reproduces the key finding of [6]: even after removing all PII categories via Azure Language Services, GPT-4 retains ~50% accuracy on location and even higher on other attributes, confirming that SynthPAI faithfully captures the vulnerability of real-world texts to inference-based privacy attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the LLM-based personal attribute inference (PAI) framework of Staab et al. (ICLR 2024), which demonstrated that frontier LLMs can infer sensitive attributes from real-world Reddit comments but could not release their dataset due to GDPR and ethical constraints. Where prior synthetic samples from that work were restricted one-on-one dialogues, this paper introduces a multi-agent Reddit thread simulation that produces realistic multi-party comment structures. It also situates itself within the broader LLM privacy literature (memorization vs. inference risks) and the long history of NLP author profiling (PAN competitions), while leveraging recent advances in LLM-based synthetic data generation and chain-of-thought prompting for style-consistent comment production.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SynthPAI is the first openly available, privacy-preserving dataset for LLM-based personal attribute inference, unblocking a research area that has been stalled by the inability to share real personal data. The consistent ~10% accuracy drop across all 18 evaluated models (preserving relative rankings) validates the dataset as a faithful proxy for real-world PAI evaluation, enabling the community to develop and test defenses without ethical overhead. Key limitations include restriction to thread-based English Reddit-style content, absence of meta-features like upvotes, and only 8 attributes; open questions include whether the framework generalizes to other platforms (blogs, images), whether improved automated labeling can reduce the remaining human curation cost, and whether the dataset is sufficient to train effective anonymization defenses against the inference threat it documents.
