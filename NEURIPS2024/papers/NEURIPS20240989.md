## Problem

Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) encode signals in network weights but are black-box, making post-training edits like cropping impossible without retraining or fine-tuning. No existing INR architecture supports removing a portion of the encoded signal with a proportional decrease in parameter count, since weights are not spatially organized. This paper asks whether an INR can be designed so that cropping is a native, zero-retraining operation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240989/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes coordinate inputs (2D pixel coordinates for images, 1D time for audio, 3D spatial for shapes, or 3D spatiotemporal for video) and outputs reconstructed signal values at those coordinates. The input space is pre-partitioned into a regular grid (e.g., 16×16 for a 512×512 image yielding ~32×32 pixel partitions), and each coordinate is routed to its partition's local sub-network. Cropping removes entire partitions and their associated local weights, while extension adds new partition sub-networks initialized via mirror-padding at borders followed by fine-tuning.

## Method

Local-Global SIRENs partition the input coordinate space into a grid and assign a dedicated compact SIREN sub-network to each partition (local features), while a single larger SIREN sub-network processes all coordinates to extract global context. At every layer, a shared merge operator—concatenation of local and global features followed by a linear projection and sine activation—interleaves the two feature streams per-coordinate, preserving the INR's coordinate-to-value mapping. Cropping is achieved by simply deleting the local sub-networks corresponding to the target partitions, reducing parameters proportionally without any retraining, since the global sub-network and merge operator (5–15% of total parameters) remain intact. The architecture also accelerates training and inference because the local sub-networks are small and can be evaluated in parallel via batched matrix multiplication, and it generalizes to other INR backbones such as INCODE by adapting the harmonizer to modulate local, global, and merge components.

## Results

On 25 DIV2K images, Local-Global SIREN (automatic partitioning) achieves 34.13±0.59 dB PSNR and 0.971 SSIM, surpassing the full SIREN baseline (33.57±0.65 dB, 0.966 SSIM) and SIREN-per-Partition (31.90±0.64 dB, 0.955 SSIM). On video encoding (300×512×512 cat video), Local-Global SIREN reaches 31.91±0.02 dB PSNR versus 29.71±0.09 dB for SIREN. On the Bach cello audio clip, Local-Global SIREN achieves 50.83±0.55 dB PSNR after 5k iterations, outperforming SIREN (48.93±0.60 dB) by nearly 2 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SIREN [38], Local-Global SIREN wins on every signal type tested: +0.56 dB PSNR on DIV2K images, +2.20 dB on the cat video, +1.90 dB on Bach audio, and higher IoU on 3D shape encoding. Against SIREN-per-Partition, the advantage is even larger (+2.23 dB on images, +2.20 dB on video), confirming that the global sub-network effectively compensates for the lack of cross-partition context. Against INCODE [20], Local-Global INCODE achieves 39.03±0.16 dB on DIV2K image encoding versus 38.79±0.38 dB for INCODE, and also leads on all three downstream tasks (denoising +0.11 dB, CT reconstruction +0.22 dB, super-resolution +0.20 dB). Against MINER [37], the comparison is qualitative: MINER's multiscale coarse-to-fine design prevents proportional weight reduction during cropping (only 2.8% of weights removable in the border-cropping example), whereas Local-Global SIREN discards ~27% of weights for the same crop, making it the only architecture that supports true croppable INRs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SIREN (Sitzmann et al., NeurIPS 2020) as its base INR architecture and on INCODE (Kazerouni et al., WACV 2024) as a SOTA extension, introducing a Local-Global sub-network design with a shared merge operator that interleaves local and global features throughout the forward pass. Conceptually, it departs from the INR-per-Partition paradigm (KiloNeRF, KiloNeUS) by eliminating the need for knowledge distillation from a full INR, and from MINER by ensuring uniform weight distribution across partitions to enable proportional weight reduction during cropping. The merge operator design (concatenation + shared linear layer) is the key architectural departure that allows local sub-networks to retain global context without the overhead of a single monolithic network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Croppable INRs with proportional weight reduction open a practical path toward editable neural signal representations without retraining, addressing a gap the authors identify as largely unaddressed in the INR editing literature. The demonstrated tradeoff between partitioning granularity and accuracy/latency gives practitioners a tunable knob, and the finding that the architecture can surpass the full SIREN baseline suggests the local-global decomposition is not merely a compromise but a genuine inductive bias. Key limitations include partition-level (not arbitrary-shape) cropping, reliance on torch-bmm with known GPU latency instability, and restriction to MLP-based INRs. Open questions include extending to non-MLP architectures, incorporating semantic partitioning, and leveraging the fixed per-partition weight structure for partition-based downstream tasks.
