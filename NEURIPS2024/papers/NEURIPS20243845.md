## Problem

LoRA initializes its adapter matrices with Gaussian noise (A) and zeros (B), causing the product AB to be zero at the start of training. This results in initially vanishing or random-direction gradients, wasting many optimization steps and potentially converging to suboptimal local minima. The paper identifies that LoRA effectively fine-tunes 'noise' while freezing the actual pre-trained weights W, rather than adapting the most informative components of the model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243845/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained linear weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n} from LLM self-attention or MLP layers and decomposes it via SVD into a trainable low-rank adapter (A ∈ R^{m×r}, B ∈ R^{r×n}) and a frozen residual matrix W^res ∈ R^{m×n}. At deployment, the trained PiSSA adapter can be losslessly converted into a standard LoRA-format pair (ΔA ∈ R^{m×2r}, ΔB ∈ R^{2r×n}) that adds directly to the original W without requiring SVD at inference time. For QPiSSA, the residual W^res is stored in 4-bit NF4 while the adapter remains in full precision, and the forward pass computes Y = X(nf4(W^res) + AB).

## Method

PiSSA applies economy SVD to each pre-trained weight matrix W = USV^⊤, then partitions the decomposition at rank r: the top-r singular values and vectors initialize the trainable adapter as A = U[:,:r]·S[:r,:r]^{1/2} and B = S[:r,:r]^{1/2}·V[:r,:r]^⊤, while the remaining components form the frozen residual W^res = U[:,r:]·S[r:,r:]·V[:,r:]^⊤. Because the principal singular vectors span the directions of maximum stretching in W, directly optimizing these components yields gradients that are immediately large and well-directed (mimicking full fine-tuning), eliminating the warm-up waste inherent in LoRA's zero/noise initialization. The QPiSSA extension quantizes only the residual W^res to NF4 rather than the full W; since W^res has a narrower, more Gaussian-like value distribution after removing the dominant singular values, this reduces quantization error by ~20% compared to QLoRA. A Fast SVD (Halko et al.) initialization completes in seconds, making the switch from LoRA to PiSSA negligible in cost.

## Results

On the GSM8K benchmark, PiSSA consistently outperforms LoRA across all three 7B models: LLaMA-2-7B (53.22% vs. 42.85% for LoRA-gaussian), Mistral-7B (73.31% vs. 69.50%), and Gemma-7B (77.78% vs. 75.11%). On the GLUE benchmark (DeBERTa-v3-base), PiSSA achieves an overall average of 89.83%, surpassing LoRA^G (88.50%), LoRA^K (88.62%), DoRA (88.98%), and AdaLoRA (89.46%). For 4-bit quantization, QPiSSA reduces quantization error by ~20% on average compared to QLoRA (which provides 0% reduction), and fine-tuning LLaMA-3-70B on GSM8K yields 86.05% accuracy for QPiSSA versus 81.73% for QLoRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PiSSA outperforms LoRA (both Gaussian and Kaiming initialization) on all NLG tasks across all three 7B models, with the largest gap on LLaMA-2-7B GSM8K (+10.37% over LoRA-gaussian) and Mistral-7B MBPP (+4.09% over LoRA-gaussian). On the GLUE benchmark, PiSSA (89.83% avg) beats LoRA^G (88.50%), LoRA^K (88.62%), DoRA (88.98%), and AdaLoRA (89.46), winning 7 out of 8 individual tasks. For quantization, QPiSSA reduces quantization error by ~20% on average versus QLoRA (0% reduction) and outperforms LoftQ (14.6% avg for LLaMA-2-7B) across all layer types and ranks. On LLaMA-3-70B GSM8K, QPiSSA achieves 86.05% accuracy, exceeding QLoRA's 81.73% by 4.32%. PiSSA also surpasses full-parameter fine-tuning on several tasks (e.g., LLaMA-2-7B GSM8K: 53.22% vs. 49.13%; Mistral-7B GSM8K: 73.31% vs. 69.91%), which the authors attribute to PiSSA acting as a denoised version of full fine-tuning by freezing the residual (noisy) components.

## Relation to Prior Work

PiSSA builds directly on LoRA's low-rank adapter architecture but departs fundamentally in philosophy: rather than approximating the weight update ΔW with a randomly-initialized adapter, PiSSA applies SVD to the original pretrained matrix W and initializes the trainable adapter with its principal singular values and vectors, freezing the residual. This inverts LoRA's 'tune noise, freeze signal' paradigm into 'tune signal, freeze noise.' The quantization extension QPiSSA similarly repositions the quantization target from the full base model (as in QLoRA) to the residual matrix, and contrasts with LoftQ, which applies SVD to the quantization error matrix rather than the original weights. The method is architecturally compatible with subsequent LoRA improvements such as AdaLoRA and DoRA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a drop-in initialization change requiring only a few seconds of Fast SVD, PiSSA offers an immediately actionable improvement to the most widely adopted PEFT method, with consistent gains across 11 models (184M–70B) and 13 tasks. The ~20% quantization error reduction over QLoRA has direct practical impact on memory-constrained fine-tuning of large models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to convolutional layers and vision tasks, whether adaptive-rank methods (AdaLoRA, DyLoRA) can be combined for further gains, and whether a formal theoretical justification exists for why tuning principal components of W outperforms tuning a random ΔW.
