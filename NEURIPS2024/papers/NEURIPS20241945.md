## Problem

Existing methods for learning neural signed distance functions from point clouds fall into two camps: data-driven priors that infer fast but generalize poorly to unseen shapes, and overfitting-based methods that generalize well but converge slowly. Under challenging conditions such as highly noisy point clouds, both strategies are significantly degraded, and no prior work has effectively combined the fast initialization of learned priors with the generalization of test-time overfitting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241945/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy point cloud (no clean points, no normals, no signed distance labels) and a pretrained auto-decoder prior (DeepSDF-like network with 256-dim per-shape embeddings trained on clean meshes). It produces a finetuned neural SDF whose zero-level set is extracted via marching cubes at 256³ resolution for shapes or 512³ for large-scale scenes. During inference, the network is optimized for 4000 iterations at learning rate 1e-4, with each iteration operating on a randomly sampled local region of K=1000 nearest-neighbor points and U=1000 Gaussian-sampled queries.

## Method

The method has two stages: first, a data-driven prior f′ is trained as an auto-decoder (similar to DeepSDF) with per-shape embeddings on clean meshes using signed distance supervision; second, f′ is finetuned on a single noisy point cloud via a novel local noise-to-noise mapping loss that requires no clean points, normals, or signed distances. The key mechanism is a local statistical reasoning algorithm: a random point is selected as center, its K=1000 nearest neighbors form a local region m_n, U=1000 queries are sampled around m_n and projected onto the zero-level set via differentiable pulling (NeuralPull), and the Earth Mover's Distance between the projected queries and a random subset of noisy points from m_n is minimized. The embedding c representing the test point cloud is initialized as the centroid of all training embeddings, and the network parameters are initialized from the prior, giving a strong starting point that converges in ~5 minutes versus 14–46 minutes for pure overfitting baselines. This local (rather than global) statistical reasoning both improves geometric accuracy from noisy points and reduces computational cost per iteration.

## Results

On ShapeNet (noise variance 0.005), the method achieves CD_L1×10 = 0.023, NC = 0.973, and F-Score = 0.992, outperforming the closest competitor N2NM (0.026, 0.962, 0.991) and all prior-based methods (best: ALTO at 0.028, 0.955, 0.985). On ABC (variance noise), it reaches CD_L2×100 = 0.096 vs. N2NM's 0.113. Inference time is 5 minutes per shape, compared to 14 min for SAP and 46 min for N2NM, demonstrating a ~9× speedup over the closest overfitting-based baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 23 baselines across every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is N2NM [52] (the global noise-to-noise mapping), which the method beats on CD_L1 (0.023 vs. 0.026 on ShapeNet), CD_L2 (0.096 vs. 0.113 on ABC), and F-Score (0.992 vs. 0.991), while also running ~9× faster (5 min vs. 46 min). Among prior-based methods, ALTO [90] and POCO [7] are the strongest, but the method improves CD_L1 by ~18% over ALTO (0.023 vs. 0.028) and by ~23% over POCO (0.023 vs. 0.030). Overfitting-based methods like SAP [70] (14 min) and PSR [39] are both slower and less accurate. On real-scan benchmarks (SRB, 3D Scene), the gap widens: on 3D Scene the method achieves CD_L2×1000 = 0.389 vs. N2NM's 0.507 and LIG's 6.190, and on SRB it reaches CD_L1×10 = 0.055 vs. N2NM's 0.067 and NKSR's 0.069. On D-FAUST (non-rigid humans), the method achieves the best CD_L1×10 = 0.034 and F-Score = 0.973, narrowly beating N2NM (0.037, 0.966) while matching SAP's F-Score of 0.966.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two established paradigms in neural SDF learning: data-driven priors (DeepSDF-style auto-decoders trained on large-scale signed distance supervision) and overfitting-based inference (NeuralPull's differentiable pulling, N2NM's global noise-to-noise mapping via EMD). The key departure is making the noise-to-noise mapping *local*—sampling K-nearest-neighbor patches and minimizing EMD within each patch—while initializing from a pretrained prior rather than from scratch. This finetuning strategy inherits the generalization of the prior and the adaptability of overfitting, but the local statistical reasoning is the genuinely new mechanism that replaces N2NM's global EMD objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a ~9× speedup over N2NM (5 min vs 46 min per shape) while matching or exceeding its accuracy, making per-shape SDF inference feasible in interactive or large-scale pipelines. The ablations confirm the prior initialization is not merely a convenience but critical—removing it degrades both accuracy and convergence time by 7.5×. The main open limitation is robustness to extreme noise (variance 0.07L), where the prior's learned geometry prior becomes insufficient; improving the prior itself (e.g., with stronger shape priors or multi-scale representations) is the natural next step. The local-vs-global finding also suggests a broader principle: statistical reasoning in implicit surface learning benefits from spatial locality, which could inform other overfitting-based reconstruction methods.
