## Problem

Existing self-supervised learning methods enforce fixed invariance or equivariance to a handcrafted set of data augmentations, producing brittle representations that must be retrained for each downstream task with different symmetry requirements. For instance, invariance to color aids classification but harms tasks requiring color sensitivity, and no single augmentation set is universally optimal. The paper asks whether incorporating context into SSL can eliminate these augmentation-based inductive priors and enable a single model to dynamically adapt its symmetries to the task at hand.

## Input / Output

The method takes an image x (128×128 on 3DIEBench, 32×32 on CIFAR-10), its transformation parameters a (e.g., 3D rotation angles, color shifts, crop/blur parameters), and a context C of K state-action-next_state triplets {(x_i, a_i, y_i)} sampled from a target transformation group. The encoder (ResNet-18 pretrained on ImageNet) maps each image to a latent vector, which is concatenated with a and fed into a 3-layer, 4-head GPT-2-style decoder-only transformer (2048-dim embedding) that produces the final representation. At inference, the model is configured for equivariance or invariance to a given group simply by including or zeroing out the transformation parameters in the context, with no parameter updates required.

## Method

CONTEXTSSL frames SSL as a Contextual World Model: transformations are actions, input representations are states, and transformed representations are next states, processed by a decoder-only transformer that attends over a sequence of (x_i, a_i, y_i) triplets. The key mechanism is selective inclusion of augmentation parameters in the context—providing them enforces equivariance (the model learns how transformations act on representations), while omitting them enforces invariance (the model must align representations regardless of transformation). Training minimizes an InfoNCE contrastive loss at every context length, aligning h((x_i, a_i)|C_i) with h(y_i|C_i) against other y_j as negatives. Two critical components prevent degenerate solutions: (1) a context mask that hides (x_i, a_i) when encoding y_i to block trivial copy shortcuts, augmented by random masking of earlier tokens with probability p=0.9; and (2) an auxiliary predictor that regresses the latent transformation from (x_i, a_i), preventing the model from collapsing to invariance by ignoring transformation parameters entirely.

## Results

On 3DIEBench, CONTEXTSSL achieves 0.744 R² for rotation prediction and 0.986 R² for color prediction, outperforming the best equivariant baselines (SIE: 0.671 and 0.975 respectively) while maintaining 80.4% linear classification accuracy. The key finding is adaptive symmetry switching: with rotation context, color prediction R² drops to 0.023 (invariance), while with color context, rotation R² drops to 0.344 (invariance), all from a single trained model without parameter updates. On CIFAR-10, CONTEXTSSL retains 88.5% classification (vs. SimCLR's 89.1%) while achieving 0.608 R² for crop prediction and 0.925 R² for blur prediction under the respective contexts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONTEXTSSL outperforms all equivariant baselines on both rotation and color prediction: it beats SIE (best equivariant baseline) by 0.073 R² on rotation (0.744 vs. 0.671) and by 0.011 R² on color (0.986 vs. 0.975), and surpasses SEN and EquiMOD by larger margins. Against invariant baselines, CONTEXTSSL trades ~5% classification accuracy (80.4% vs. SimCLR's 85.3%) for dramatically superior equivariant prediction (0.744 vs. 0.506 rotation R²). The critical advantage over all baselines is that CONTEXTSSL requires no retraining to switch symmetry modes: the same model achieves rotation equivariance + color invariance with rotation context, and color equivariance + rotation invariance with color context, whereas EquiMOD, SIE, and SEN must be separately trained for each target symmetry group. On CIFAR-10, CONTEXTSSL matches SimCLR's classification (88.5% vs. 89.1%) while achieving 0.608 R² crop prediction and 0.925 R² blur prediction, compared to SimCLR's 0.459 and 0.371 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three research threads: (1) equivariant self-supervised learning (EquiMOD, SIE, SEN), which encodes sensitivity to predefined augmentations; (2) Image World Models (Garrido et al.), which reframe SSL as next-state prediction with transformations as actions; and (3) in-context learning from NLP, where a model adapts to a task by attending to a few demonstrations without parameter updates. The key departure is replacing hardcoded symmetry priors with a context-conditioned transformer that learns a general representation and selectively enforces equivariance or invariance to different transformation groups at inference time, drawing on the state-action-next-state structure of world models but adding a memory module that tracks past (state, action, next-state) triplets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental brittleness in SSL: representations trained to be invariant (or equivariant) to a fixed set of augmentations perform poorly on downstream tasks requiring the opposite symmetry, forcing costly retraining. CONTEXTSSL's ability to adapt symmetries via context alone—without parameter updates—suggests a path toward single-model, multi-task representation learning. However, all experiments are on small-scale benchmarks (CIFAR-10, 3DIEBench with 128×128 images, MIMIC-III, UCI Adult), and the method is limited to hand-crafted transformations with at most 2–3 groups; scaling to ImageNet-level data, complex compositional transformations, and naturally occurring symmetries remains open. The finding that removing the auxiliary predictor causes complete collapse to invariance highlights a subtle optimization challenge that may generalize to other equivariant learning settings.
