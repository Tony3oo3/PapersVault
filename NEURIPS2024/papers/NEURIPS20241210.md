## Problem

Despite widespread empirical evidence that safety-aligned LLMs remain vulnerable to jailbreaking, there has been limited theoretical understanding of why this phenomenon is fundamentally hard to prevent. The paper asks: under what statistical conditions is jailbreaking inevitable, and what structural property of the alignment objective makes it so?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241210/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework decomposes each text prompt into a (query, concept) pair, where the concept carries the information content and the query is the instructional wrapper. The LM is modeled as mapping prompts to distributions over longer text fragments called 'explanations' rather than individual tokens, with the output represented as a point on a probability simplex. In the E-RLHF/E-DPO training procedure, the input is a preference dataset of (prompt, preferred explanation, dispreferred explanation) triples, and for harmful prompts the KL reference is computed against a safety-transformed version of the prompt (e.g., with a safe prefix prepended) rather than the original harmful prompt.

## Method

The paper introduces a statistical framework where prompts are decomposed into (query, concept) pairs and LM outputs are modeled as distributions over explanations on a probability simplex, partitioned into a harmful zone and a safety zone. Two theoretical results are established: a PAC-Bayesian bound showing pretrained LMs inevitably mimic harmful behavior from the training corpus, and a theorem proving that an ε-bounded adversary can jailbreak the model with high probability whenever the induced posterior is not concentrated in an extremely safe region of the simplex. Motivated by this, the authors propose E-RLHF (and its DPO variant E-DPO), which modifies the standard RLHF/DPO KL-regularization term by replacing the reference distribution p_SFT(x_h) with p_SFT(x_s) for harmful prompts, where x_s is a safety-transformed version (e.g., prepending 'Please ensure your response adheres to community guidelines and ethical standards:'). This expands the safety zone on the output simplex without additional training cost, and empirically reduces average Attack Success Rate across all jailbreak adversaries on both HarmBench and AdvBench while preserving MT-Bench helpfulness scores.

## Results

E-DPO reduces the average Attack Success Rate (ASR) across all 10 jailbreak adversaries to 31.05 on HarmBench and 13.60 on AdvBench, compared to 37.15 and 18.40 for standard DPO, respectively. The improvement is consistent across every individual adversary in both benchmarks. On helpfulness (MT-Bench), E-DPO scores 6.7, slightly below DPO (6.9) but well above the SFT baseline (6.3), indicating no meaningful performance sacrifice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares E-DPO against standard DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and the unaligned SFT baseline (Mistral-7B) across 10 jailbreak adversaries on both HarmBench and AdvBench. E-DPO achieves the lowest ASR on every single adversary in both benchmarks, with the largest gains on AdvBench where GCG drops from 47.00 (DPO) to 38.00, AutoDAN from 44.00 to 31.00, and TAP from 61.00 to 53.00. On HarmBench, the average ASR improves from 37.15 (DPO) to 31.05 (E-DPO), a 6.1-point reduction. On helpfulness (MT-Bench), E-DPO scores 6.7 versus DPO's 6.9 and SFT's 6.3, showing a negligible 0.2-point trade-off. The SFT baseline is consistently the weakest on safety (highest ASR) but also the weakest on helpfulness, confirming that alignment is necessary but that standard DPO's KL regularization to p_SFT(x) inadvertently preserves harmful explanations in the output support.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by modifying the KL-regularization term: for harmful prompts, the reference distribution p_SFT(x_h) is replaced with p_SFT(x_s) where x_s is a safety-transformed version of the prompt. Conceptually, it draws on PAC-Bayesian generalization theory to bound pretraining behavior, and on adversarial robustness results (Shafahi et al., 2018) to prove jailbreaking inevitability via a probability-simplex volume argument. The prompt decomposition into (query, concept) pairs and the modeling of LMs as text-fragment generators (rather than single-sentence generators as in Wolf et al., 2023) are the key framework departures that enable the adversary-strength bounding. The safe-prefix transformation also connects to context distillation (Askell et al., 2021) used in LLaMA-2 safety alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled theoretical explanation for why jailbreaking persists after alignment: the KL-regularization in standard RLHF/DPO anchors the model to the SFT support, which inevitably contains harmful explanations, making the safety zone inherently small relative to the harmful zone. The E-DPO modification is a zero-cost, drop-in change that consistently reduces attack success rates across 10+ adversaries on both HarmBench and AdvBench without hurting MT-Bench helpfulness. Key limitations include the binary harmful/safe classification, the assumption of a fixed p_world, and the inability to handle multi-turn or in-context-learning jailbreaks. Open questions include whether individualized safe-prompt transformations (rather than a universal prefix) yield further gains, and how the framework extends under finite-memory or hallucination constraints.
