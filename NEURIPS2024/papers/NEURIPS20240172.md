## Problem

Existing PAC-Bayes generalisation bounds are restricted to scalar performance metrics such as overall error rate, providing no information about the distribution of different error types. In applications like medical diagnosis where the severity of different misclassifications is context-dependent and may change over time, one needs to control arbitrary linear combinations of error probabilities simultaneously. This paper provides the first PAC-Bayes bound that controls the entire distribution of error probabilities by bounding the KL-divergence between the empirical and true error-type distributions, implicitly constraining all uncountably many weightings at once without union bounds.

## Input / Output

The method takes an i.i.d. training sample S, a user-specified disjoint partition of Y² into M error types (e.g., the confusion matrix cells for multiclass classification), a loss vector ℓ ∈ [0,∞)^M encoding the severity of each error type, and a prior P over the hypothesis space. It produces a trained posterior Q (a diagonal Gaussian over neural network weights) together with a guaranteed upper bound on the total risk R_D^T(Q) = ℓ·R_D(Q). Crucially, the resulting KL-divergence certificate allows deriving bounds on any linear combination of the M individual error probabilities post-hoc, meaning the severity weighting ℓ used during training need not match the one of interest at deployment time.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 1) generalises the unifying PAC-Bayes bound of Begin et al. from scalar to vector risk by bounding kl(R_S(Q)‖R_D(Q)) via a change-of-measure argument (Csiszár/Donsker-Varadhan), a multinomial generalisation of Maurer's lemma (Lemma 2), and Stirling's approximation to obtain a tractable closed-form bound. For training, Theorem 2 solves the constrained optimisation max ℓ·v s.t. kl(u‖v) ≤ c via Lagrange multipliers, yielding a closed-form expression for the optimal v* parameterised by a scalar root-finding problem, along with explicit derivatives ∂f_ℓ*/∂u_j and ∂f_ℓ*/∂c that make the bound a fully differentiable objective. The empirical risk vector R_S(Q) is estimated via Monte Carlo sampling from Q with pathwise gradient estimators, and Propositions 2–3 provide a rigorous correction for the MC approximation error. The bound gracefully reduces to Maurer's classical binary PAC-Bayes bound when M=2, and the KL-divergence structure ensures non-vacuousness since it diverges on the boundary of the simplex.

## Results

On binarised MNIST, the method achieves a PAC-Bayes bound of R_D^T(Q) ≤ 0.2640 (with probability ≥ 0.94), and on HAM10000, R_D^T(Q) ≤ 0.8379 (with probability ≥ 0.94), where the maximum possible value of R_D^T(Q) is 3 for the chosen loss vector ℓ = (0,1,3). In terms of confidence region volume (smaller is tighter), the proposed KL-divergence bound yields a region of 0.0025 on MNIST versus 0.0028 for the union of individual Maurer bounds, but 0.0012 on HAM10000 versus 0.0011 for Maurer. In the synthetic comparison (Table 2), the proposed method's confidence region is tighter than the individual Maurer union in 4/9 cases, worse in 3/9, and tied in 2/9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) a union of M individual Maurer [2004] PAC-Bayes bounds (one per error probability), and (2) Morvant et al. [2012]'s spectral-norm bound on the confusion matrix. Against the Maurer union, the proposed KL-divergence bound produces a tighter confidence region on MNIST (volume 0.0025 vs. 0.0028) but a slightly looser one on HAM10000 (0.0012 vs. 0.0011), with the difference attributed to the smaller certification set size (5860 vs. 10000) making the KL(Q‖P) term more expensive. In the broader synthetic comparison (Table 2, varying M ∈ {4, 25, 100} and m ∈ {100, 300, 1000}), the proposed method wins in 4/9 cases, loses in 3/9, and ties in 2/9 against the Maurer union; the intersection of both methods often beats either alone. Against Morvant's spectral-norm bound, the proposed method is either inapplicable (when class sizes are too small) or produces a dramatically tighter region (Morvant's region volume is ~1.0, i.e., essentially the entire simplex, while the proposed method's is orders of magnitude smaller).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the unifying PAC-Bayes bound of Begin et al. [2016] from scalar risk to vector-valued risk over a user-specified partition of error types, and reduces to Maurer's [2004] bound in the binary case. The closest prior work is Morvant et al. [2012], which bounds the spectral norm of the confusion matrix difference; this paper instead uses KL-divergence over a more general error-type partition, enabling direct derivation of bounds on individual error probabilities and arbitrary linear combinations. The framework also subsumes the ternary split-KL inequalities of Wu and Seldin [2022] as a special case (M=3).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical advantage is that a single KL-divergence bound simultaneously controls all uncountably many weightings of error probabilities, eliminating the need for union bounds when the severity of different misclassifications changes over time (e.g., medical diagnosis). The bound is also directly differentiable (Theorem 2), enabling it to serve as a training objective without proxy approximations. Limitations include the requirement for i.i.d. data and a finite number of error types; extending to continuous loss distributions or non-i.i.d. settings is left open. Empirical validation is limited to two small datasets with shallow MLPs, and the method does not uniformly dominate the simpler union-of-Maurer-bounds approach in confidence region volume.
