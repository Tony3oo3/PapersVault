## Problem

Estimating total treatment effects under pervasive, unknown network interference is fundamentally difficult because SUTVA is violated and the interference structure is typically unobserved. Prior causal message-passing methods rely only on first-order moments and two-stage experiments, limiting their ability to capture non-monotonic or complex interference dynamics. This paper addresses the gap by extending the framework to higher-order moments and multi-stage staggered rollouts, enabling accurate TTE estimation without any knowledge of the interference network.

## Input / Output

The method consumes panel data of individual-level outcomes y and binary treatment assignments w observed over T+1 periods, along with the experimental design specifying treatment probabilities π at each stage. Crucially, it operates on aggregated sample-level statistics (mean and variance of outcomes, mean treatment fraction) rather than individual unit trajectories, and works with off-equilibrium data—no requirement that outcomes have converged to steady state. The output is a time-indexed sequence of TTE estimates (difference between all-treated and all-control counterfactual means) plus the full counterfactual mean and variance trajectories under both scenarios.

## Method

HO-CMP is grounded in state evolution equations derived from approximate message passing theory, which describe how the sample mean ν_t and variance ρ_t² of unit outcomes evolve over time as a function of the current state and treatment design. The key insight is to learn the unknown update function f_θ that maps a feature vector x_t—constructed from non-linear basis functions of (ν̂_t, ρ̂_t², w̄)—to the next-period (ν̂_{t+1}, ρ̂_{t+1}²) via supervised regression on observed transitions. Unlike first-order CMP, HO-CMP incorporates second-order terms (ρ̂_t² and w̄_{t+1}²) that capture non-linear interference, enabling it to model non-monotonic spillover effects. Once trained, the learned mapping is iteratively applied under counterfactual all-ones and all-zeros treatment matrices to simulate both scenarios from the observed initial state, yielding TTE_t = ν̂_t(1) − ν̂_t(0) at each time step.

## Results

Across 100 Monte Carlo simulations (N=4000, L=4 treatment probabilities π=(0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.5)), HO-CMP and FO-CMP achieve near-zero bias in the LinearInMeans setting for both short (T=40) and long (T=200) horizons, while DM and HT estimators exhibit substantial bias in all scenarios. In the Non-LinearInMeans setting (where spillover is non-monotone via g(x)=sin(πx)), HO-CMP substantially outperforms FO-CMP by leveraging second-order moments (sample variance and squared treatment probability), reducing estimation error significantly. PolyFit shows noticeable bias at T=40 because its equilibrium estimates have not converged, whereas HO-CMP remains accurate by operating on off-equilibrium data. Results are robust across both random geometric and real Twitch social network topologies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Horvitz-Thompson (HT) estimator [Horvitz & Thompson, 1952], both FO-CMP and HO-CMP win decisively: HT (and the simpler difference-in-means estimator) ignores network interference entirely and produces large, persistent bias in TTE estimates across all outcome specifications, network types, and time horizons. Against PolyFit [Cortez et al., NeurIPS 2022], HO-CMP wins on both bias and variance: PolyFit requires outcomes to reach equilibrium at each treatment probability stage, so at short horizons (T=40) its interpolated equilibrium values are biased low, propagating into a large TTE bias; even at T=200, the degree-L polynomial fit introduces higher variance than HO-CMP. HO-CMP avoids both problems by directly learning the state-evolution dynamics from off-equilibrium data. Within the CMP family, HO-CMP outperforms FO-CMP specifically in the Non-LinearInMeans setting, where the additional second-order features (ρ̂²_t and w̄²_{t+1}) capture the non-monotone dependence of the equilibrium mean on treatment probability that FO-CMP's linear-in-means features cannot represent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Causal Message-Passing (CMP) framework of Shirani and Bayati (2024) in two directions: incorporating higher-order moments (sample variance) of unit outcomes into the state evolution equations, and leveraging multi-stage staggered rollout designs with multiple treatment probabilities rather than the original two-stage setup. The theoretical backbone draws from Approximate Message Passing (AMP) and statistical physics, where state evolution equations track the dynamics of one-dimensional summary statistics (mean, variance) over time. The paper deliberately departs from the broader network interference literature by requiring no knowledge of the interference network, positioning itself against approaches that assume neighborhood interference, observed graphs, or equilibrium observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically important gap: estimating total treatment effects in staggered rollout experiments (common in tech industry) without knowing the interference structure, while handling non-monotonic spillover patterns that standard estimators cannot capture. A key advantage is that HO-CMP works with off-equilibrium data, avoiding the need for long experiments to reach steady state. The main limitation acknowledged is that the framework requires multiple temporal observations of outcomes, restricting applicability to single-time-point settings. Open questions include how the approach performs when the interference matrix deviates from the i.i.d. Gaussian assumption, and whether the feature function selection can be made more principled rather than heuristic.
