## Problem

State-of-the-art neural network pruning methods (both explicit, e.g., Iterative Magnitude Pruning, SparseFlow, and implicit, e.g., L0, C-NODE, PathReg) either fail to achieve the high sparsity levels needed to reflect inherently sparse biological systems, or produce sparse models whose learned structures do not align with known domain knowledge. In gene regulatory network inference specifically, dense or randomly sparse models encode thousands of spurious regulatory relationships that are useless for scientific discovery, while existing task-specific sparsification methods still fall short of recovering the true sparse biology.

## Input / Output

The method consumes time-series gene expression trajectories (e.g., 11,165 genes across 186 breast cancer patients ordered in pseudotime, or 3,551 genes across 24 yeast cell-cycle time points) along with two binary prior matrices: a TF-gene interaction matrix P derived from promoter motif maps or ChIP-seq, and a protein-protein interaction matrix C from the STRING database (experimental evidence score > 0.6). The output is a sparsified PHOENIX NeuralODE (typically 90–97% sparse) from which a sparse gene regulatory network is extracted by reading out the learned ODE coefficients, alongside predicted gene expression dynamics evaluated by MSE on held-out time points.

## Method

DASH (Domain-Aware Sparsity Heuristic) is an iterative pruning procedure that computes per-parameter pruning scores as a convex combination of normalized learned weights and domain-knowledge priors, controlled by a tunable λ ∈ [0,1]. For a two-layer network, the first-layer scores are projected onto the input-input prior C via a pseudo-inverse recurrence (Ω₁⁽ᵗ⁻¹⁾ᵀ Ω₁⁽ᵗ⁾ ≈ C), while the product of first- and second-layer scores is projected onto the input-output prior P (Ω₂⁽ᵗ⁾ Ω₁⁽ᵗ⁾ ≈ P), using left and right pseudo-inverses to solve these alignment constraints iteratively. This is applied to the PHOENIX NeuralODE architecture, which models gene expression dynamics via parallel additive (sum) and multiplicative (prod) linear layers with Hill-equation activations; DASH computes separate pruning scores for each of the four weight matrices (W_Σ, W_Π, U_Σ, U_Π) with appropriate normalizations (absolute value for sum-space, elementwise exponentiation for log-space prod weights). The pruning schedule is aggressive early (70–90% at epoch 3–10) then gradual (10% every 10–20 epochs), and training automatically terminates when validation performance plateaus, yielding an optimal sparsity level without manual target specification.

## Results

On the SIM350 synthetic dataset (350 genes, 5% noise), DASH achieves 94.6% sparsity with 90.7% balanced accuracy in recovering the ground-truth gene regulatory network and 2.4×10⁻³ MSE on predicted gene expression dynamics. On real breast cancer data (GSE7390), DASH reaches 92.7% sparsity with 95.7% balanced accuracy against ChIP-seq validation, and on yeast cell-cycle data (GSE4987) it achieves 97.2% sparsity with 88.4% balanced accuracy. On the bone marrow hematopoiesis data (GSE194122), DASH produces the sparsest network (96.0%) with the lowest average out-degree (54 vs. 404 for IMP and 529 for the unpruned baseline) while matching the best test MSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DASH outperforms all eight baselines on balanced accuracy of the recovered gene regulatory network. Against the closest non-prior-informed competitor, SparseFlow (96.0% sparsity, 70.9% Bal. Acc.), DASH gains ~20 percentage points in balanced accuracy at comparable sparsity. Against IMP (81.9% sparsity, 61.7% Bal. Acc.) and PathReg (56.5% sparsity, 61.9% Bal. Acc.), DASH improves balanced accuracy by ~29 and ~29 points respectively while also achieving higher sparsity. Among implicit penalty-based methods, DASH's 90.7% balanced accuracy far exceeds PathReg (61.9%), C-NODE (55.9%), and L₀ (55.0%), with the gap widening as sparsity increases. On real breast cancer data, DASH (95.7% Bal. Acc.) and BioPrune (95.7%) dramatically outperform all non-prior-informed methods, which cluster around 50% (random chance), confirming that domain-knowledge-guided pruning is essential for biological interpretability. On yeast data, DASH (88.4% Bal. Acc.) surpasses its own prior-only baseline BioPrune (79.2%) by ~9 points, demonstrating that the data-driven component of DASH adds value beyond pure prior matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative pruning framework established by the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (Frankle & Carbin, 2018) and its NeuralODE-specific adaptation SparseFlow (Liebenwein et al., 2021) by injecting domain-specific structural knowledge directly into the pruning score computation. While prior pruning methods (IMP, SynFlow, SparseFlow) are data-centric or hardware-centric, and recent GRN-specific methods (PathReg, C-NODE, L0) use implicit penalties to encourage sparsity, none incorporate explicit prior knowledge about input-output or input-input relationships to guide which parameters to retain. DASH generalizes SparseFlow (recovered as the λ=0 special case) by adding a convex combination with a prior knowledge matrix, and is applied to the PHOENIX NeuralODE architecture (Hossain et al., 2024) for gene regulatory dynamics inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that domain-aware pruning can produce networks that are simultaneously highly sparse (>90%) and biologically meaningful (95%+ balanced accuracy vs. ~50% random chance for all non-prior-informed methods), enabling direct extraction of interpretable gene regulatory networks for hypothesis generation in cancer and developmental biology. A key limitation is the dependence on prior knowledge quality—factually wrong priors could mislead the model—and the current focus on 2-layer architectures, leaving open questions about extension to deeper networks, attention mechanisms, and domains where priors are less well-established. The framework is positioned as a blueprint for guided pruning in other hard sciences (physics, materials science) where structural domain knowledge is available.
