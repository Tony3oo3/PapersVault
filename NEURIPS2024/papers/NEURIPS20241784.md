## Problem

Learning causal action-recommendation policies from observational data is risky due to hidden confounders that bias treatment assignment. While a human expert who has access to these confounders can complement a machine model, no existing method jointly learns a policy with the option to defer to an expert under bounded hidden confounding using outcome-based (rather than purely propensity-reweighting) objectives. CARED fills this gap by combining CAPO bounds under the Marginal Sensitivity Model with a cost-sensitive deferral framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observational data tuples (X, A, Y) where X ∈ ℝ^d are baseline covariates, A ∈ {0,1} is the binary treatment assigned by the expert, and Y is the realized outcome. It first estimates CAPO bounds Ŷ⁺(x,a) and Ŷ⁻(x,a) for each treatment a via the B-learner, then constructs per-sample costs from these bounds. The output is a three-way policy π: X → {0, 1, ⊥} implemented as a single-layer MLP with softmax over three logits, where ⊥ indicates deferral to the human expert whose action is taken from the training data.

## Method

CARED replaces the unavailable true labels in Mozannar and Sontag's deferral framework with costs derived from CAPO bounds: the action cost C(x,a) = Ŷ⁺(x,1−a) − Ŷ⁻(x,a) captures worst-case regret, and the deferral cost C⊥(x,aᵢ,yᵢ) = Ŷ⁻(x,1−aᵢ) − yᵢ measures how much the expert's observed outcome deviates from the lower bound of the alternative treatment. These costs define weights wʲ = maxₖ c(k) − c(j) that modulate a weighted cross-entropy surrogate loss over three classes (treat, don't treat, defer), which is convex and consistent with the original non-convex machine-expert objective. The CAPO bounds are estimated using the B-learner under the MSM assumption with sensitivity parameter Λ, and the policy is trained by minimizing the surrogate loss. The paper proves cost coherence (the learned policy is non-inferior to both the expert and the CAPO-based bounds policy when bounds are valid) and a Rademacher-complexity-based generalization bound that accounts for CAPO estimation error at rate O_p(n^{−1/(2+r)}).

## Results

On the synthetic data experiment (lower policy regret is better), CARED outperforms all baselines across all sensitivity parameter Λ values, while ConfHAI and CRLogit only beat the human expert for a narrow range of Λ near the true value (log Λ₀ = 2.5). On the IHDP hidden confounding dataset (higher policy value is better), CARED consistently achieves a higher average policy value than the expert across all Λ levels and approaches the Oracle Policy value at moderate Λ. CARED's peak policy value occurs at a deferral rate of approximately 0.6, matching the 0.59 fraction of cases where the semi-synthetic expert is oracle-perfect, demonstrating that the model learns when to defer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARED outperforms ConfHAI (Gao and Yin, 2023) on both experiments across all levels of assumed hidden confounding Λ. ConfHAI, which uses inverse-propensity-weighted minimax optimization over an uncertainty set, only improves over the human expert when the assumed Λ closely matches the true sensitivity parameter, and degrades noticeably when Λ is misspecified. CARED is more robust to Λ misspecification, maintaining gains over the expert even at extreme Λ values. CARED also outperforms CRLogit (Kallus and Zhou, 2020), which uses a similar IPW approach under hidden confounding but lacks the deferral option entirely. Against the CAPO-based baselines (B-Learner Policy and Pessimistic Policy), CARED achieves higher policy values because it learns the expert's strengths and weaknesses rather than applying a fixed decision rule. The key advantage of CARED over ConfHAI is that it leverages outcome models (CAPO bounds) in addition to propensity scores, avoiding the under-performance that arises when the constraint set is inaccurately specified.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARED adapts the consistent surrogate loss for learning with deferral from Mozannar and Sontag [2020] to the causal setting, where true labels are unavailable due to the fundamental problem of causal inference. Rather than using true labels to construct costs (as in the supervised deferral setting), it uses estimated CAPO bounds from the B-learner [Oprescu et al., 2023] under the Marginal Sensitivity Model to define action and deferral costs. This contrasts with the IPW-based minimax approach of Gao and Yin [2023] (ConfHAI) and Kallus and Zhou [2020] (CRLogit), which rely on propensity-score reweighting over an uncertainty set; CARED instead leverages outcome-model bounds, making it less sensitive to misspecification of the confounding parameter Λ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in robustness: CARED maintains performance across a wide range of Λ values, whereas IPW-based baselines degrade sharply when Λ is misspecified—a common and difficult calibration problem in practice. This makes the method more deployable in high-stakes settings (healthcare, welfare) where the true degree of hidden confounding is unknown. Key limitations include the requirement that expert actions appear in the training data, the assumption that expert behavior remains unchanged at test time, and the restriction to binary treatments. Open questions include extending to multiple experts, modeling dynamic expert-system interactions, and validating on real-world observational data with known ground truth.
