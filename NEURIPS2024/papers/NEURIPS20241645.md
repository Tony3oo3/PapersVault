## Problem

Suggala and Netrapalli (2020) showed that Follow the Perturbed Leader (FTPL) achieves the optimal O(√T) regret for general non-convex losses, but only in static environments where a single fixed comparator is used. In dynamic environments where loss functions shift over time, static regret is an inadequate performance measure, and no prior work had established dynamic or adaptive regret guarantees for non-convex losses. This paper fills that gap by extending FTPL to dynamic settings and proving that the same regret orders achievable for convex functions (O(T^{2/3}(V_T+1)^{1/3}) dynamic regret and O(√(τ log T)) adaptive regret) also hold for non-convex losses.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a sequence of L-Lipschitz (w.r.t. ℓ₁-norm) non-convex loss functions over a bounded feasible set K with ℓ∞-diameter D, along with an (α,β)-approximate offline optimization oracle that returns near-minimizers of f(x) − ⟨σ, x⟩. Because the loss functions and feasible set are non-convex, the meta-algorithms cannot compute weighted averages of expert predictions; instead they sample a single expert's prediction with probability proportional to its weight. In the meta-learning application, each round's input is a 5-shot training set with constraint limits (or a demonstration trajectory with collision constraints), and the output is an updated meta-parameter φ_t from which a task-specific parameter θ_t is derived via a within-task constrained optimization.

## Method

The paper proposes three algorithms built on FTPL. FTPL-D restarts FTPL every γ rounds and, with γ chosen from the known functional variation V_T, achieves O(T^{2/3}(V_T+1)^{1/3}) dynamic regret. FTPL-D+ removes the need to know V_T by running N = ⌊log₂T⌋ instances of FTPL-D with geometrically spaced restart intervals {2, 4, …, 2^N} as experts and tracking the best via Hedge with randomized sampling (sampling one expert's prediction rather than averaging). FTPL-A constructs geometric covering intervals, instantiates a dedicated FTPL expert for each interval, and combines them using AdaNormalHedge (also with randomized sampling) to simultaneously achieve O(√(τ log T)) strongly adaptive regret for any interval of length τ and Õ(T^{2/3}(V_T+1)^{1/3}) dynamic regret. The key technical challenge addressed throughout is that non-convexity precludes deterministic combination of expert predictions, so all meta-algorithms rely on randomized expert sampling to maintain valid regret guarantees.

## Results

The paper establishes that FTPL-D achieves O(T^{2/3}(V_T+1)^{1/3}) dynamic regret for non-convex losses (matching the convex bound of Besbes et al.), and FTPL-A achieves O(√(τ log T)) strongly adaptive regret for any interval of length τ, again matching the convex result of Jun et al. In experiments on meta-imitation learning (T=200 tasks) and robust few-shot classification on CUB-200-2011 (T=150 tasks, 5-way 5-shot), both FTPL-D+ and FTPL-A significantly outperform vanilla FTPL in cumulative loss and all three robustness metrics (clean accuracy, PGD accuracy, B-score) after environment shifts at T=50 and T=100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical baseline is FTPL [Suggala and Netrapalli, 2020], the state-of-the-art static-regret algorithm for non-convex online learning. In both meta-imitation learning and robust few-shot classification experiments, FTPL-D+ and FTPL-A consistently outperform FTPL after environment shifts: in abruptly changing environments (letters M→E→T→A, or water→forest→grassland birds), the proposed methods adapt more quickly to new task distributions at T=50, 100, 150, while FTPL exhibits a significant increase in instantaneous loss. In gradually evolving environments (rotating letter 'A'), FTPL shows a notable loss increase after T=100, whereas both proposed methods maintain low loss throughout. On CUB-200-2011, FTPL-D+ and FTPL-A achieve comparable performance to each other and both significantly surpass FTPL on clean accuracy, PGD accuracy, and B-score after the habitat-based environment changes. Theoretically, the paper's bounds match those of Restarted OGD [Besbes et al., 2015] for dynamic regret and CBCE [Jun et al., 2017] for strongly adaptive regret, but extend them from convex to general non-convex losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FTPL algorithm of Suggala and Netrapalli (2020), which achieves optimal O(√T) static regret for non-convex losses, to dynamic environments by combining it with the restarting strategy of Besbes et al. (2015) for dynamic regret and the geometric covering interval framework of Daniely et al. (2015) for adaptive regret. The key technical departure from prior work is the use of randomized expert sampling in meta-algorithms (Hedge and AdaNormalHedge) to handle the non-convexity of the feasible set, since weighted averaging of expert predictions is infeasible. The resulting regret bounds for non-convex losses match those previously established for convex functions, closing a gap in the literature where dynamic regret and adaptive regret guarantees for non-convex online learning were largely absent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that non-convex online learning in dynamic environments can achieve the same O(T^{2/3}(V_T+1)^{1/3}) dynamic regret and O(√(τ log T)) adaptive regret as convex settings is a clean theoretical contribution that removes a perceived barrier between convex and non-convex online optimization. The application to online constrained meta-learning demonstrates practical relevance, showing that dynamic regret metrics are more appropriate than static regret when task distributions shift. Open questions include whether bounds can be derived using comparator path-length instead of functional variation, and whether the offline optimization oracle assumption can be relaxed. The experiments, while illustrative, are limited in scale and compare only against the base FTPL algorithm, leaving the practical gap versus other non-convex dynamic methods unexplored.
