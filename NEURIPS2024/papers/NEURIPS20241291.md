## Problem

Existing distillation-based methods for accelerating diffusion model sampling require hundreds to thousands of GPU hours of fine-tuning, largely due to a mismatch between the timestamps used during fine-tuning and those actually used at sampling time, combined with increasingly complex optimization objectives (LPIPS, adversarial losses, regularization). This makes practical deployment of few-step diffusion sampling prohibitively expensive, as the fine-tuning cost approaches the cost of training the diffusion model from scratch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241291/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pure Gaussian noise (and optionally a text prompt for Stable Diffusion) as input and produces a synthesized image in as few as 2 NFEs. It operates across resolutions from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 512×512 (Stable Diffusion v1.5 in latent space). A key design choice is that SFD-v uses a single distilled model that can sample at variable NFEs (2–5) by conditioning on the step count, while for Stable Diffusion the model is trained with guidance scale 1 but can be sampled at any guidance scale (e.g., 7.5) at inference time.

## Method

SFD reinterprets trajectory distillation from a global perspective: in each training iteration, the full teacher sampling trajectory is generated (using DPM-Solver++(3M) with K=4 sub-steps), and the student model imitates it step by step, learning to correct accumulated errors rather than only matching local teacher outputs. The key efficiency insight is that fine-tuning at a specific timestamp smoothly enhances the gradient field at neighboring timestamps, so only the few timestamps used at sampling time need to be trained. Additional improvements include using an analytical first step (AFS) to save one NFE, adjusting t_min from 0.002 to 0.006, and adopting L1 loss over L2 or LPIPS. SFD-v extends this by injecting a step-condition (treated like a time embedding) so a single model supports variable NFEs, and for Stable Diffusion the distillation is performed at guidance scale 1 to halve the teacher computation while still enabling high-guidance sampling at inference.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, SFD achieves FID 4.53 at NFE=2 with only 0.64 A100 hours of fine-tuning, compared to ~171 hours for Progressive Distillation (FID 4.51) and ~1156 hours for Consistency Distillation (FID 2.93). SFD-v, which uses a step-condition to support variable NFEs from a single model, improves to FID 4.28 (NFE=2) and 2.95 (NFE=5) with 4.26 A100 hours total. On ImageNet 64×64, SFD-v reaches FID 4.21 at NFE=5, and on Stable Diffusion v1.5, SFD-v achieves FID-5K 23.5 and CLIP score 0.29 at 5 steps with guidance scale 7.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFD matches Progressive Distillation (FID 4.53 vs 4.51 at NFE=2) and Guided PD (4.53 vs 4.48) while reducing fine-tuning cost by over 100× (0.64 h vs ~171 h and ~128 h respectively). Against Consistency Distillation, SFD trades some quality (4.53 vs 2.93 at NFE=2) for a ~1800× reduction in training time (0.64 h vs ~1156 h). CTM achieves the best FID (1.98 at NFE=1) but relies on adversarial GAN loss; without it, CTM degrades to FID >5, making SFD's 4.53 at NFE=2 competitive without such complex objectives. SFD-v consistently outperforms SFD at every NFE (e.g., 4.28 vs 4.53 at NFE=2; 2.95 vs 3.06 at NFE=5) at roughly the same per-step training cost, validating the step-condition design. Against solver-based methods, SFD dramatically outperforms at low NFE: DDIM at NFE=10 gives FID 15.69 while SFD at NFE=2 gives 4.53, and DPM++(3M) at NFE=5 gives 24.97 versus SFD's 4.53 at NFE=2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the trajectory distillation framework introduced by Luhman & Luhman (2021) and operationalized by progressive distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022), but repositions it from a local (single-step) to a global (full-trajectory) perspective. It departs from the increasingly complex optimization objectives of recent distillation methods (LPIPS, adversarial losses, consistency constraints) by showing that a simple L1 loss with a well-chosen teacher solver (DPM-Solver++(3M)) and a few small adjustments (AFS, adjusted t_min) suffices to reach progressive-distillation-level quality. The key conceptual departure is the observation that fine-tuning at a single timestamp smoothly enhances the gradient field at all other timestamps, eliminating the need for the multi-stage, thousands-of-timestamps fine-tuning that prior methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1000× reduction in fine-tuning cost (0.64 A100-hours vs. ~1156 for consistency distillation) makes few-step diffusion distillation accessible to much smaller labs and enables rapid iteration over distillation configurations. The variable-NFE capability of SFD-v (a single model handling 2–5 NFEs) addresses a practical limitation of progressive distillation and consistency models that require separate models per step count. However, the FID gap with state-of-the-art methods (CTM achieves 1.98 vs. SFD's 4.53 at NFE=2 on CIFAR-10) remains significant, and the authors acknowledge this as a limitation. Open questions include whether the smooth-gradient-field insight generalizes to other distillation paradigms (consistency, distribution matching) and whether tailored time schedules exploiting the known geometric structure of diffusion trajectories can close the quality gap.
