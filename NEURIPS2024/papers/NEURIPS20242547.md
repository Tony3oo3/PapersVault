## Problem

Existing 3D representations for generation each suffer from a critical tradeoff: NeRF converges easily but renders slowly and extracts meshes imprecisely; DMTet extracts precise meshes but only backpropagates through tetrahedra intersecting the zero-level set, causing optimization to stall on large topological changes; 3DGS renders in real time but produces meshes with visible holes. No prior representation simultaneously achieves easy convergence, precise mesh extraction, and real-time rendering.

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts either a text prompt (for text-to-3D) or a single reference image (for image-to-3D, using Stable Zero-1-to-3 with an MSE alignment loss). It outputs a textured polygonal mesh with PBR material maps (albedo, roughness, metallic, bump) encoded by a spatially varying hash grid. The geometry stage operates on a deformable tetrahedral grid at resolution 128 or 256 with 512×512 rendering, and the texture stage renders the extracted mesh through a physically based rendering pipeline (Nvdiffrec).

## Method

TeT-Splatting embeds surface-based volumetric rendering inside a structured deformable tetrahedral grid, where each vertex carries an SDF value and a local deformation encoded by a multiresolution hash grid. Each tetrahedron is projected to a 2D splat, and a tile-based differentiable rasterizer (built on 3DGS's CUDA framework with a short per-pixel re-sorting window) performs alpha-blending using opacities derived from the SDF via a NeuS-style sigmoid whose steepness increases over training. A pre-filtering step discards tetrahedra whose maximum possible opacity falls below 1/255, reducing the splatting workload. Because volumetric rendering lets all visible tetrahedra contribute to the gradient (unlike DMTet, which only updates tetrahedra on the zero-level set), optimization converges rapidly and avoids getting trapped in undesirable shapes; eikonal and normal-consistency regularization further stabilize the SDF. After geometry convergence, Marching Tetrahedra extracts a polygonal mesh, which is then textured in a second stage using rich diffusion priors (normal-depth for geometry, depth-conditioned albedo for texture) via SDS loss.

## Results

On the rich diffusion priors setting (Table 2), TeT-Splatting achieves a Geometry CLIP score of 23.16 and Appearance CLIP score of 29.42 (evaluated on 183 prompts), outperforming RichDreamer's re-evaluated scores of 23.01 and 29.22 respectively. Geometry optimization time is reduced from 70 min (RichDreamer) to 40 min (TeT-Splatting). For vanilla RGB-based diffusion priors, the paper reports primarily qualitative comparisons, noting that TeT-Splatting produces more detailed and compact meshes than Magic3D, Fantasia3D, and DreamGaussian while maintaining real-time rendering (though at lower FPS than 3DGS-based DreamGaussian).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Magic3D (Instant-NGP/NeRF), TeT-Splatting produces more faithful mesh extractions, as Magic3D's imprecise density-to-SDF thresholding causes visible geometry degradation after mesh export. Against Fantasia3D (DMTet), TeT-Splatting converges significantly faster and avoids the fragmentation and stuck-shape issues that DMTet exhibits in early optimization iterations (Figures 3, 7), while also achieving 40 min vs. 70 min geometry optimization time. Against DreamGaussian (3DGS), TeT-Splatting avoids the visible holes and blurry surfaces that plague mesh extraction from unstructured Gaussians, though DreamGaussian renders at higher FPS. On the quantitative CLIP comparison (Table 2), TeT-Splatting narrowly outperforms RichDreamer on both Geometry CLIP (23.16 vs. 23.01) and Appearance CLIP (29.42 vs. 29.22) under the shared 183-prompt evaluation. The paper does not report direct quantitative comparisons against ProlificDreamer or MVDream on the same prompt set, as their published scores use a different (113-object) evaluation list, making cross-method numerical comparison unreliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

TeT-Splatting synthesizes two previously separate lines of 3D representation research: the structured tetrahedral grid with Marching Tetrahedra from DMTet (which enables precise mesh extraction but suffers from limited gradient flow during optimization) and the tile-based differentiable rasterization with alpha-blending from 3D Gaussian Splatting (which enables fast rendering but produces poor meshes due to its unstructured primitives). The key conceptual departure is treating tetrahedra themselves as splatting primitives for volumetric rendering, rather than projecting them to triangles as DMTet does or using unstructured Gaussians as 3DGS does. The opacity computation follows NeuS, and the s-parameter schedule follows Voxurf, but the combination of these elements within a structured grid for differentiable splatting is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D generation pipelines: the need to choose between fast-converging volumetric representations (NeRF, 3DGS) that produce poor meshes, and precise mesh representations (DMTet) that converge slowly and get stuck in local minima. By unifying these properties, TeT-Splatting can be dropped into existing two-stage pipelines (geometry then texture) without the quality degradation seen when transitioning between incompatible representations. However, the method still struggles with high-frequency detail due to the fixed tetrahedral grid resolution, renders slower than 3DGS, and the authors acknowledge that a densification strategy (analogous to 3DGS) could further improve both quality and speed — an open direction for future work.
