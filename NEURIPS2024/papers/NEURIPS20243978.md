## Problem

Existing one-for-one and one-for-many anomaly detection models suffer severe performance degradation when applied to new classes because normal feature representations vary significantly across classes, causing normal patches from unseen classes to be misclassified as anomalies. This paper tackles the class-generalizable anomaly detection problem: training a single unified model on known classes that can directly detect anomalies in entirely new classes without any retraining or fine-tuning, requiring only a few normal reference samples from the target class.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243978/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB test image and 2 or 4 few-shot normal reference images from the new class as input. The reference images are processed once through a frozen pre-trained feature extractor (WideResNet50 or ImageBind) to build reference feature pools; no network parameters are updated for the new class. The output is a pixel-level anomaly score map at input resolution (obtained by bilinearly upsampling multi-level scores and summing them) and an image-level anomaly score (the maximum value of the map).

## Method

ResAD's core insight is residual feature learning: each query feature is matched to its nearest normal reference feature and subtracted, eliminating class-related components so that normal residuals cluster near the origin regardless of class. A simple Conv+BN+ReLU Feature Constraintor then projects normal residuals onto a spatial hypersphere using a proposed abnormal-invariant OCC loss (pseudo-Huber term for normals + L2 invariance term for anomalies), further reducing scale inconsistencies across classes. A Real-NVP normalizing flow serves as the Feature Distribution Estimator, trained with maximum log-likelihood on normal residuals plus an explicit boundary-guided semi-push-pull loss on abnormal residuals to learn a discriminative yet generalizable density. At inference, anomaly scores are computed from the negative log-likelihood of the NF model, and the entire pipeline requires no re-modeling for new classes—only extracting reference features from the few-shot normal samples.

## Results

Under the 4-shot cross-dataset setting (trained on MVTecAD, tested on five other datasets), ResAD with WideResNet50 achieves an average image-level/pixel-level AUROC of 89.7%/97.3% on industrial datasets and 88.0%/96.3% across all six datasets. With the stronger ImageBind backbone (ResAD†), it reaches 85.8%/- image-level AUROC on industrial datasets, surpassing CLIP-based methods. The method outperforms the best non-CLIP competitor (RegAD) by up to 7.6%/2.9% in image/pixel AUROC under the 4-shot setting, and by 7.2%/2.9% under 2-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResAD significantly outperforms the one-for-one baseline RDAD [13] and the one-for-many baseline UniAD [47] by large margins (e.g., 89.7% vs. 65.9% image AUROC on the industrial average for 4-shot), confirming that conventional AD models fail catastrophically on unseen classes. Among non-CLIP few-shot methods, ResAD beats the strongest competitor RegAD [18] by 7.6%/2.9% (image/pixel AUROC) on the 4-shot industrial average, and also surpasses PaDiM [12], PatchCore [30], and SPADE [11] by 10–15 points in image-level AUROC. Against the CLIP-based methods, ResAD with WideResNet50 achieves comparable or better results than WinCLIP [19] (88.0% vs. 83.0% all-dataset average) and InCTRL [50] (88.0% vs. 81.5%), while ResAD† with ImageBind further extends the lead. Notably, ResAD requires no re-modeling or fine-tuning on new classes, whereas RegAD must re-fit a Multivariate Gaussian per class and WinCLIP/InCTRL rely on handcrafted text prompts. The paper also demonstrates that incorporating residual feature learning into UniAD [47] yields +20–26 point improvements, validating the generality of the residual feature insight beyond the proposed framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

ResAD builds on three established components: Real-NVP normalizing flows for distribution estimation, one-class classification (OCC) with pseudo-Huber loss for feature constraining, and BGAD's boundary-guided semi-push-pull loss for discriminative training. Its core conceptual departure is the residual feature learning paradigm—subtracting the nearest normal reference feature from each query feature to eliminate class-specific components before distribution modeling. This is conceptually adjacent to InCTRL's in-context residuals (CVPR 2024), but differs fundamentally: ResAD operates on full residual feature vectors (preserving high-dimensional structure for distribution estimation) rather than scalar cosine-distance maps, enabling pixel-level localization that InCTRL cannot provide. The paper positions itself against both one-for-one/many AD methods (which require retraining per class) and few-shot AD methods (which still re-model distributions per new class), arguing that residual features make the learned distribution inherently class-invariant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: real-world AD deployments encounter new product classes continuously, and users typically lack the ability or data access to retrain models. The residual feature insight is validated as broadly applicable (yielding +25.6% AUROC when grafted onto UniAD), suggesting it could upgrade many existing AD pipelines to be class-generalizable. Key limitations include evaluation on a single random seed, random few-shot sample selection without representativeness guarantees, and testing only on image modalities. Open questions include extending to video/time-series, developing principled reference sample selection strategies, and whether the approach generalizes to truly novel anomaly types (not just novel classes with known anomaly types).
