## Problem

Existing model merging methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: (1) interference between task-specific models, where redundant or conflicting parameters degrade merged performance well below individual fine-tuned experts, and (2) a static one-size-fits-all merged model cannot adapt to heterogeneous test-time data distributions, leading to suboptimal performance on diverse inputs. The paper shows that both shared and exclusive task-specific knowledge are critical, but directly merging exclusive knowledge hinders overall performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes T task-specific fine-tuned expert models (e.g., LoRA-adapted Qwen-14B or full fine-tuned RoBERTa) plus a shared pre-trained base model as preprocessing inputs, and at inference time accepts arbitrary text or image inputs without requiring task labels. The router consumes last-layer token embeddings from the shared expert to produce per-task softmax weights, which dynamically compose the final inference model. Outputs are task-appropriate: classification scores for discriminative tasks (GLUE, image classification) and generated text for generative tasks (MMLU, TruthfulQA, BBQ, CNN-DailyMail summarization).

## Method

Twin-Merging decomposes the merging problem into two stages. First, Knowledge Modularization: a shared expert θ_s is computed via Task Arithmetic (θ_s = θ_0 + Σ γ_t(θ_t − θ_0)), then exclusive task-specific knowledge is isolated as difference vectors v_t = θ_t − θ_s and compressed to rank-r via SVD, reducing storage by up to 99.9%. Second, Dynamic Merging: a small 3-layer MLP router, trained on a small validation set, takes last-layer token embeddings from the shared expert and outputs softmax weights over tasks, enabling input-conditioned composition θ* = θ_s + Σ w_t · SVD_r(θ_t − θ_s). This reformulates parameter merging as a conditional composition problem inspired by Mixture-of-Experts, eliminating the need for pre-determined static coefficients and allowing the model to adapt to unseen or heterogeneous test distributions.

## Results

On the 8-task GLUE benchmark (RoBERTa-base), Twin-Merging achieves a 96.14% average normalized score, approaching the 100% fine-tuned upper bound and surpassing Task Arithmetic (67.80%) by 28.34 points. On 4 generative tasks with Qwen-14B (MMLU, TruthfulQA, BBQ, CNN-DailyMail), Twin-Merging reaches 102.38% normalized score, exceeding the fine-tuned upper bound of 100%. On 8 vision classification tasks (ViT-B/32), Twin-Merging achieves 95.33% normalized score with only 5.0 GB VRAM and 47 min inference time, outperforming Surgery (94.04%, 32.4 GB, 215 min). The method retains 86.4% performance even at 99.8% parameter compression via SVD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Twin-Merging consistently outperforms all six baselines across every evaluation setting. Against Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., ICLR 2022), the primary baseline, Twin-Merging gains 28.34 points on discriminative tasks (96.14 vs. 67.80) and 5.77 points on generative tasks (102.38 vs. 96.61). Ties-Merging (Yadav et al., NeurIPS 2023) and its DARE variant (Yu et al., 2024) are surpassed by 32.46 and 30.56 points respectively on GLUE, with the gap widening on the hardest task (COLA: 101.01 vs. 8.35 for Task Arithmetic). Weight Averaging (Wortsman et al., ICML 2022) lags far behind at 52.56% on discriminative tasks. On vision tasks, Twin-Merging beats AdaMerging (Yang et al., ICLR 2024) by 6.83 points (95.33 vs. 88.50) while reducing inference time from 185 min to 47 min, and edges out Surgery (Yang et al., 2024) by 1.29 points (95.33 vs. 94.04) with 6.5× less VRAM (5.0 GB vs. 32.4 GB) and 4.5× less time. Notably, Twin-Merging is the only method to exceed the fine-tuned upper bound on generative tasks, suggesting that modularized dynamic merging can unlock latent capabilities in large pre-trained models without additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022) for computing the shared expert, but departs from the broader model-merging literature (Ties-Merging, DARE, AdaMerging, Surgery) by reframing the problem from static parameter combination to conditional knowledge composition. Where prior methods address interference at the parameter level (sign conflicts, redundancy, sparsity), Twin-Merging operates at a finer knowledge granularity, explicitly decomposing each expert into shared and exclusive components before merging. The dynamic routing mechanism draws conceptual inspiration from Mixture-of-Experts architectures, but applies it to the merging problem rather than to model architecture design, and the SVD compression of exclusive knowledge vectors is a practical contribution not seen in prior merging work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Twin-Merging offers a practical path to multi-task deployment without retraining, closing a substantial gap (28.34% on discriminative tasks) and even surpassing fine-tuned upper bounds on generative tasks, while maintaining storage efficiency through SVD compression (99.9% parameter reduction with ~14% degradation). The method's compatibility with existing merging techniques (Table 11) and its applicability across architectures (RoBERTa, Qwen-14B/72B, ViT-B/32, LLaMA-7B) suggest broad adoptability. Open questions include the role of 'evil knowledge' (parameters harmful to all tasks) and 'irrelevant knowledge' that the authors identify but do not decompose, as well as whether the approach extends to heterogeneous architectures or requires the same base model. The router's dependence on a small validation set and the added inference latency (275s vs. 236s per 1000 items) are practical considerations for deployment.
