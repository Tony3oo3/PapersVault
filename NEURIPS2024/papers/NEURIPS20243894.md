## Problem

Existing diffusion planning frameworks (Diffuser, Decision Diffuser) suffer from extremely low decision-making frequencies (1.3 Hz and 0.4 Hz) due to the expensive iterative denoising of long-horizon trajectory distributions with heavy neural networks, making them impractical for real-time applications such as robot control and game AI. The paper identifies that distant portions of generated plans contain highly redundant information, and asks whether this redundancy can be exploited to build a faster, lighter planner without sacrificing performance.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single current state observation o_t and a target condition (either cumulative reward or discounted return plus a learned value function for the terminal state) and produces a single action a_t via a 3-layer MLP inverse dynamics model. Internally, it generates state-only sequences (not state-action pairs) across three refinement levels with temporal horizons of 129 (MuJoCo/Antmaze) or 49 (Kitchen), where each level produces only ~5 states due to large temporal jumps (32, 8, 1). The critic evaluates candidate plans at each level to select the best one before passing its terminal keypoint to the next level.

## Method

The core contribution is the Plan Refinement Process (PRP), which decomposes trajectory generation into L coarse-to-fine levels: level 0 plans a rough trajectory with large temporal jumps (e.g., 32), then each subsequent level refines only the first interval of the previous level with progressively smaller jumps until reaching step-level resolution. This reduces the sequence length each diffusion model must generate to ~5 states, enabling a lightweight DiT backbone (2 blocks, 256-dim embeddings) instead of the heavier UNet typically used. Three interchangeable backbones are provided: standard diffusion with DDIM (3–5 steps), rectified flow (3 steps), and rectified flow with reflow (1–2 steps), the latter achieving 122 Hz average decision frequency. Conditional sampling via classifier-free guidance is applied only at level 0, while deeper levels are unguided since they merely interpolate between already-selected keypoints. Training slices full trajectories into per-level sub-trajectories and trains separate noise predictors, with the critic (cumulative reward or value-augmented return) providing both training conditions and inference-time plan selection.

## Results

On D4RL, DiffuserLite-D achieves an average score of 86.0 across all environments, outperforming Diffuser (75.3), DD (81.8), and HDMI (82.6), while running at 122.44 Hz average decision-making frequency (112.7× faster than Diffuser's 1.34 Hz and DD's 0.41 Hz). On Robomimic, DiffuserLite-D reaches 93.9% average success rate, surpassing IRIS (92.9%) and BC (91.3%). On FinRL, DiffuserLite-D achieves 779 average return, matching HDMI (778) and exceeding DD (747) and MB-PPO (743). The fastest variant (R2 with rectified flow + reflow) reaches 200.7 Hz on MuJoCo with only a minor performance drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffuserLite-D outperforms both pioneering diffusion planning frameworks (Diffuser and DD) on D4RL by a wide margin: 86.0 vs. 75.3 and 81.8 average, with the largest gains on sub-optimal datasets (e.g., Hopper-medium: 100.9 vs. 58.5/79.3) and long-horizon tasks (Kitchen: 74.0 vs. 53.1/61.0; Antmaze: 68.0 vs. 0.0/0.0). Against the strongest baseline HDMI (82.6), DiffuserLite-D leads by 3.4 points on average, with particularly strong results on Kitchen (74.0 vs. 69.2) and Antmaze (68.0 vs. not reported). On Robomimic, DiffuserLite-D (93.9) edges out IRIS (92.9) and BC (91.3), while on FinRL it matches HDMI (779 vs. 778) and beats DD (747) and MB-PPO (743). The runtime advantage is dramatic: DiffuserLite-R2 achieves 122.44 Hz average frequency, which is 91× faster than Diffuser (1.34 Hz) and 300× faster than DD (0.41 Hz). As a plugin, DiffuserLite integrated into AlignDiff (AlignDiff-Lite) boosts decision frequency by 560% (6.9→45.5 Hz) with only a 3.2% drop in MAE Area (0.621→0.601), demonstrating its generality beyond reward-maximizing settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffuserLite builds directly on the two pioneering diffusion planning frameworks—Diffuser and Decision Diffuser (DD)—which established the paradigm of conditionally sampling full trajectories and selecting via a critic. It departs from concurrent hierarchical approaches like HDMI and HD-DA by reframing the hierarchy not as a goal-decomposition mechanism but as a progressive refinement process (PRP) that only refines the first interval at each level, explicitly discarding redundant distant details. Architecturally, it replaces the standard UNet backbone with a lightweight DiT transformer and introduces rectified flow with reflow as an optional backbone, while retaining classifier-free guidance for conditional sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 112.7× speedup (from ~1 Hz to 122 Hz) makes diffusion planning viable for real-time robot control and game AI, domains where the previous 1–10 Hz regime was a hard blocker. The plugin design—demonstrated by integrating PRP into AlignDiff with only a 3.2% performance drop—suggests a broadly applicable acceleration layer for the entire diffusion-planning literature. Key limitations include the cumbersomeness of tuning CFG target conditions across multiple levels, and the open question of whether all levels can be unified into a single model. The authors also flag potential dual-use concerns for rapid robotic deployment.
