## Problem

Knowledge editing methods such as ROME and MEMIT can update individual facts in LLMs, but repeated sequential edits cause severe performance degradation including catastrophic forgetting of previously edited samples and declining downstream task accuracy. Prior work documented this degradation but failed to identify its root causes or propose effective mitigation strategies. This paper investigates the underlying reasons from both data (editing objective diversity and perplexity) and model (explosive L1-norm growth in editing layers) perspectives, and proposes a concrete solution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of knowledge triples (subject, relation, target object) applied one at a time to a frozen transformer (GPT-J 6B or Llama2 7B), computing the last-token hidden state and key vector at target MLP layers for each edit. Rather than storing individual edit vectors as in standard MEMIT, D4S maintains only two accumulated matrices (R^l K^l^T and K^l K^l^T) in O(1) space across all edits, and applies a single batch-style weight update to the MLP output matrix. The output is the updated MLP layer weights that encode all accumulated edits simultaneously.

## Method

D4S (Dump for Sequence) addresses the sequential editing bottleneck by converting per-edit weight updates into a single accumulated batch update. In standard MEMIT, each edit i computes its own inverse (K_0 K_0^T + k_i k_i^T)^{-1}, and the cumulative weight change is a sum of n such terms, causing explosive L1-norm growth that limits effective edits to ~100 (ROME) or ~850 (MEMIT). D4S instead maintains two running matrices—the accumulated outer-product sum Σ r_i k_i^T and the accumulated key covariance Σ k_i k_i^T—each updated by a single outer-product addition per new edit. The final weight update uses one shared inverse (K_0 K_0^T + Σ k_i k_i^T)^{-1}, which is element-wise smaller than any individual per-edit inverse due to positive definiteness, thereby bounding L1-norm growth. This O(1)-space accumulation enables thousands of sequential edits without performance collapse, achieving SOTA editing accuracy at 1000 edits while preserving downstream task performance.

## Results

On the ZsRE sequential editing benchmark, D4S achieves SOTA average scores at both Edits=500 and Edits=1000 across GPT-J (6B) and Llama2 (7B). At Edits=1000 on GPT-J, D4S reaches 88.77 Avg (95.98 Eff., 91.17 Par., 70.17 Spe.), while MEMIT collapses to 0.01 and ROME to 1.86. On Llama2 at Edits=1000, D4S scores 81.88 Avg versus 2.82 for MEMIT and 15.53 for ROME. Downstream task evaluation after 10,000 edits shows only minor degradation (e.g., HellaSwag drops 4.15 points, MMLU drops 1.59 points), confirming that D4S minimizes model damage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D4S decisively outperforms all three locate-then-edit baselines at scale. Against ROME (Meng et al., 2022), D4S maintains 88.77 Avg at 1000 edits on GPT-J versus ROME's 1.86, a gap of over 86 points; ROME's effective editing capacity is only ~100 samples before probability values decline. Against MEMIT (Meng et al., 2022), D4S achieves 81.88 Avg on Llama2 at 1000 edits while MEMIT collapses to 2.82, reflecting MEMIT's complete editing failure after ~850 edits. Against PMET (Li et al., 2024), D4S dominates across all settings—PMET scores near 0.00 at every edit count on both models, indicating it fails to perform any successful edits. The key differentiator is that D4S's O(1) accumulated inverse (Eq. 13) prevents the L1-norm explosion that causes ROME and MEMIT to degrade, while GRACE maintains high Efficacy/Specificity but sacrifices Paraphrase (≈0.35–0.59), yielding a lower overall average than D4S at all edit counts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the locate-then-edit paradigm established by ROME and MEMIT, which modify specific MLP layers to update factual associations in transformers. While prior work (Hase et al. 2024, Gu et al. 2024) documented performance degradation and catastrophic forgetting in edited models, it stopped at observation without identifying causal mechanisms or proposing remedies. The D4S method departs from the sequential-editing assumption of ROME/MEMIT by algebraically reformulating the cumulative weight update as a single batch operation, converting O(n) history storage into O(1) matrix accumulation. The Multi-Question Dataset (MQD) extends the editing evaluation landscape beyond triplet-form datasets by introducing controlled variation in editing objective complexity (PPL) as an independent variable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The L1-norm growth diagnosis provides a mechanistic explanation for why knowledge editing degrades models, giving practitioners a concrete quantity to monitor and a principled target for optimization. D4S's O(1) space requirement makes it practically deployable for continuous knowledge updates, and its preservation of both efficacy and specificity (unlike GRACE, which sacrifices specificity) addresses a key practical concern. However, the evidence is limited to GPT-J 6B and Llama2 7B with at most 10,000 edits, and the paper acknowledges that D4S's own bottleneck has not been fully characterized. Open questions include whether the L1-norm explanation generalizes to attention-layer editing, whether the method scales to 70B+ models, and whether the PPL-based data characterization holds for non-English or domain-specific knowledge.
