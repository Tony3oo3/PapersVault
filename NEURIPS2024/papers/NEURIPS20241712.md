## Problem

Existing token merging methods for Vision Transformers determine which tokens to combine using intermediate features (e.g., self-attention keys) that are simultaneously responsible for contextual encoding. This coupling means the merging policy cannot be optimized independently, and improving it requires costly end-to-end training of the entire ViT, making it difficult to leverage pre-trained models effectively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241712/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (224×224 for classification, 512×512 for segmentation) processed by pre-trained ViT backbones (DeiT-S/B, MAE-B/L, GIT-B/L, Segmenter, LV-ViT-S). It produces the same task outputs as the base model—class labels, image captions, or pixel-wise segmentation masks—but with progressively fewer tokens flowing through the transformer, reducing FLOPs by 35–50% while preserving accuracy. The decoupled embedding module adds less than 1% extra parameters (a single linear projection per block with 64–128 dimensional output).

## Method

DTEM introduces a lightweight per-block linear projection that produces decoupled token embeddings used exclusively to compute pairwise similarities for the merging decision, decoupling the merging policy from the ViT's intermediate features. To make this embedding trainable through the discrete grouping operation, the paper designs a continuous relaxation of token merging: a soft grouping operator (a differentiable top-k based on Plötz & Roth's neural nearest neighbors) that iteratively selects r most similar token pairs with temperature-scaled softmax, and a soft merging operator that asynchronously updates token features and effective sizes using continuous weights from the soft adjacency matrix. During training, these relaxed operators keep all N tokens alive with fractional absorption, enabling gradient flow to the embedding module; at inference, they are replaced by hard Bipartite Soft Matching (BSM) from ToMe. The method supports two training modes—modular (freezing the ViT and training only the embedding modules) and end-to-end (alternating updates between embedding and ViT parameters)—and the decoupled embedding trained at a high reduction rate generalizes to lower rates at inference.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k classification with DeiT-S, DTEM achieves 79.85% top-1 accuracy at 2.9 GFLOPs (37.2% FLOPs reduction), outperforming all prior token reduction methods at the same compute budget. At a more aggressive 50% FLOPs reduction (2.3 GFLOPs), DTEM reaches 79.38% top-1 accuracy, surpassing ToMe (79.25%) and eTPS (79.2%). For image captioning on COCO with GIT-B, DTEM improves CIDEr by +5.0 to +6.0 over ToMe across 31–41% FLOPs reduction. On ADE20K semantic segmentation with Segmenter-ViT-S, DTEM gains +0.32 to +1.3 mIoU over ToMe at 25–50% FLOPs reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTEM consistently outperforms all seven compared baselines across both DeiT-S and DeiT-B at matched FLOPs budgets. Against the primary baseline ToMe [2], DTEM gains +0.17% top-1 accuracy at 35% FLOPs reduction (79.85% vs. 79.68% on DeiT-S) and +0.13% at 50% reduction (79.38% vs. 79.25%), with similar margins on DeiT-B (+0.23% and +0.16% respectively). DTEM also surpasses the strongest competing methods eTPS [34] and ATS [10] by +0.15% and +0.15% on DeiT-S at 35% reduction, and by +0.18% and +0.38% at 50% reduction. On DeiT-B at 35% reduction, DTEM (81.60%) leads ToMe (81.37%), EViT (81.3%), DyViT (81.3%), and Evo-ViT (81.3%) by 0.23–0.30%. In the modular (frozen ViT) setting, DTEM improves over ToMe by +0.15% to +0.47% at 35% reduction and +0.47% to +1.64% at 50% reduction across DeiT-S/B and MAE-B/L. For captioning and segmentation, DTEM similarly dominates ToMe, confirming the advantage of decoupled embeddings across tasks requiring different levels of representational granularity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token merging framework of ToMe (Bolya et al., ICLR 2023), which introduced Bipartite Soft Matching (BSM) for efficient token reduction in ViTs, by decoupling the similarity computation from the ViT's intermediate features. The soft grouping operator adapts the differentiable top-k mechanism from Plötz & Roth (NeurIPS 2018) to produce a continuous adjacency matrix that enables gradient flow through the grouping decision. The key conceptual departure from prior merging methods (ToMe, EViT, DynamicViT, BAT, eTPS) is that all of them compute token similarity directly from ViT intermediate features (keys or token embeddings), whereas DTEM learns a dedicated, lightweight embedding module whose sole purpose is to shape the merging policy, trained via a continuously relaxed version of the grouping and merging operators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular training paradigm—improving token merging by training only a <1% parameter embedding module while keeping the ViT frozen—is practically significant for practitioners who want to accelerate pre-trained ViTs without the cost and data requirements of full fine-tuning. The method's ability to generalize across reduction rates with a single trained model also reduces the storage and training overhead of supporting multiple compute budgets. Limitations include validation only on vision tasks, no reduction in training-time cost, and modest absolute gains (+0.15% to +1.64% over ToMe in classification). Open directions include combining DTEM with adaptive per-block reduction profiles (as in DiffRate) and extending the decoupled embedding idea to other modalities or training-time sparsification.
