## Problem

Existing 3D human reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off under severe occlusion: parametric models (SMPL/SMPL-X) are robust to missing pixels but cannot capture geometric details like clothing and hair, while implicit-function-based methods produce pixel-aligned detail shapes but fail when the estimated parametric mesh is misaligned or when image features are absent in occluded regions. No prior work leverages the probabilistic distribution of multiple hypothesized SMPL meshes to condition a pixel-aligned reconstruction pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single segmented RGB image of a human (potentially heavily occluded by objects or other people) and produces an unstructured 3D point cloud of 16,384 points representing the clothed human shape. At inference, an off-the-shelf estimator (ProPose) generates 10 sampled SMPL meshes from the image, and the diffusion model denoises over 1,000 steps. The point cloud output can be converted to a mesh via Screened Poisson surface reconstruction, though this conversion is computationally expensive (~10 hours per sample).

## Method

MHCDIFF is a conditional point cloud diffusion model (built on PC²) that denoises a random point cloud into a target human shape, conditioned on two feature streams: (1) 2D image features projected onto partially-denoised points at each diffusion step via a frozen MSN/ViT encoder, and (2) local features extracted from multiple hypothesized SMPL meshes. The key novelty is the multi-hypotheses conditioning mechanism: for each point, the method computes the mean occupancy across all s sampled SMPL meshes (capturing the probability distribution) and the signed distance and normal from the single closest mesh (capturing local geometry), concatenated with NeRF-style positional encodings. This aggregation requires no learnable parameters and makes the model robust to individual SMPL estimation errors. Training is supervised on THuman2.0 with random masking to synthesize diverse occlusion patterns, and the denoising process inherently corrects misaligned SMPL estimates while inpainting invisible body regions.

## Results

On the CAPE dataset with ~40% random occlusion, MHCDIFF achieves a Chamfer Distance of 1.872 cm and Point-to-Surface distance of 1.810 cm, outperforming all implicit-function-based methods (best: ICON at 2.896/2.789 cm) and SMPL estimation baselines (best: ProPose at 2.370/2.307 cm). On the MultiHuman dataset with real-world interactions, MHCDIFF achieves the best Chamfer Distance across all five occlusion categories, with the largest gains in severe occlusion settings (0.491 cm on 'occluded single' vs. 0.549 cm for ICON and 0.567 cm for ProPose). The method also outperforms its own PC² baseline (3.640 cm) by nearly 2×, demonstrating the value of the multi-hypotheses SMPL conditioning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MHCDIFF outperforms all seven baselines on both CAPE and MultiHuman. Against implicit-function methods, the gap is dramatic: PaMIR (12.912 cm), SIFU (14.397 cm), and HiLo (13.711 cm) all fail catastrophically under occlusion because their global feature encoders are sensitive to misaligned SMPL estimation, while ICON (2.896 cm) is more robust due to its locality but still trails MHCDIFF by ~1 cm in Chamfer Distance. Among SMPL estimation baselines, ProPose (2.370 cm) and PIXIE (2.705 cm) produce full-body shapes but lack pixel-aligned geometric details like clothing, whereas MHCDIFF corrects their misalignment during denoising and adds detail. The PC² baseline (3.640 cm, from the ablation table) shows that image-condition-only diffusion is insufficient without SMPL local features. On MultiHuman's real-world interaction categories, MHCDIFF leads in all five settings, with the most pronounced advantage (0.491 vs. 0.549 cm) in the 'occluded single' category where occlusion is most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

MHCDIFF builds directly on PC²'s projection-conditioned point cloud diffusion architecture, repurposing it from general single-image 3D reconstruction to the human-specific domain by adding SMPL-derived local features (signed distance, normals, occupancy) as additional conditioning. It departs from the dominant implicit-function paradigm (PIFu, PaMIR, ICON, HiLo, SIFU) by replacing the per-point MLP decoder with a global point cloud diffusion model, motivated by the need to inpaint occluded regions and correct misaligned parametric estimates. The multi-hypotheses conditioning mechanism extends the line of probabilistic SMPL estimation work (ProPose, 3D Multibodies, HUMOR) by leveraging the full distribution of plausible meshes as a conditioning signal for pixel-aligned reconstruction, rather than selecting a single best estimate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a point cloud diffusion decoder can simultaneously handle occlusion inpainting and SMPL misalignment correction in a single generative process, which implicit-function methods struggle to do without additional architectural complexity. The multi-hypotheses conditioning requires no learnable parameters for feature aggregation, making it a lightweight and modular addition to any diffusion-based reconstruction pipeline. However, the 1,000-step DDPM inference (12 hours on CAPE vs. ~30 minutes for implicit methods) is a significant practical barrier, and the authors acknowledge DDIM as a potential fix without providing results. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video sequences, whether the random-masking training strategy generalizes to more extreme or structured occlusions, and whether the point cloud output can be efficiently converted to meshes for downstream applications.
