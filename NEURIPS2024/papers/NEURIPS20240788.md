## Problem

Multi-block minimax bilevel optimization has a complex three-level structure (outer minimax over shared variables, per-block lower-level minimization) that makes existing algorithms either require expensive second-order Hessian/Jacobian computations per iteration or consume prohibitive memory by storing all block-specific parameters. No fully first-order algorithm with strong convergence guarantees had been developed for the general multi-block setting, and the interaction among four coupled variables (x, y, z, v) in the error analysis remained unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes stochastic gradient estimates from per-block upper-level functions f_i and lower-level functions g_i, where blocks correspond to individual image classes (CIFAR100: 100 blocks of 32×32 images; CheXpert: 224×224 radiographs), molecular graphs (OGBG-MolPCBA: 437K graphs with 128 properties), or few-shot tasks (Mini-ImageNet/Tiered-ImageNet: 5-way 5-shot classification). It produces trained model parameters (ResNet-18, DenseNet-121, or GIN weights for AUC maximization; CNN4 meta-model weights for robust meta-learning) that solve the original bilevel problem to ε-accuracy, with the key structural difference that the algorithm operates on a reformulated single-level minimax surrogate rather than the nested bilevel form.

## Method

The core reformulation converts the bilevel constraint z_i*(x) = argmin_z g_i(x,z) into a value-function-based Lagrangian L_i(x,y,z_i,v_i) = f_i(x,y,z_i) + λ(g_i(x,z_i) − g_i(x,v_i)), yielding a single-level minimax problem over (x,z) vs. (y,v) where the gradient gap between the original and surrogate objectives is bounded by O(1/λ). FOSL solves this surrogate with a fully single-loop update that simultaneously performs stochastic gradient descent/ascent on all four variable groups using only first-order gradients, achieving O(ε^{-7/2}) sample complexity matching the best known first-order bilevel results. MemCS addresses the memory bottleneck in large-block settings (e.g., few-shot meta-learning with thousands of tasks) by using cold-start initialization: each iteration randomly re-initializes block-specific variables v_i and z_i, runs K inner gradient steps, and discards them, achieving a better O(ε^{-1}) per-block sample complexity than the F²SA baseline and a linear speedup in the number of sampled blocks P.

## Results

On deep AUC maximization, FOSL achieves test AUC scores of 0.9540 (CIFAR100), 0.9267 (CelebA), 0.8166 (CheXpert), and 0.8516 (OGBG-MolPCBA), outperforming the mAUC baseline by 4.96, 2.05, 0.82, and 7.23 percentage points respectively. On robust meta-learning, MemCS achieves 69.25% clean accuracy on Mini-ImageNet (vs. 64.75% for MAML) and 57.00% under 60% label-flip noise (vs. 44.75% for MAML), demonstrating substantially better robustness. The per-block sample complexity of MemCS is Õ(ε⁻¹), improving over the F²SA baseline by an order of ε⁻⁰·⁵, and FOSL matches the best-known O(ε⁻³·⁵) complexity for first-order bilevel methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOSL outperforms the mAUC baseline [24] on all four AUC maximization datasets, with the largest margin on OGBG-MolPCBA (+7.23 AUC points) and CIFAR100 (+4.96 AUC points). Against the stronger mAUC-CT baseline [24], FOSL wins on CIFAR100 (0.9540 vs. 0.9272), CelebA (0.9267 vs. 0.9192), and OGBG-MolPCBA (0.8516 vs. 0.8406), but is slightly behind on CheXpert (0.8166 vs. 0.8198). In the robust meta-learning setting, MemCS consistently beats MAML [6] across all noise conditions: on Mini-ImageNet it gains +4.50 points clean, +4.75 under Flip noise, and +7.75 under Rand noise; on Tiered-ImageNet the gains are +1.00 clean, +12.25 Flip, and +5.50 Rand. The advantage of MemCS over MAML grows substantially with increasing noise ratio (e.g., at 80% flip noise on Mini-ImageNet, MemCS retains 53.50% vs. MAML's 42.00%), confirming its superior robustness. Both methods are fully first-order, avoiding the second-order Hessian/Jacobian computations required by prior multi-block bilevel approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-block fully first-order bilevel optimization framework of Kwon et al. (F²SA, ICML 2023) to the general multi-block minimax setting, adapting the value-function-based Lagrangian reformulation to handle n block-specific lower-level problems simultaneously. It departs from prior multi-block minimax bilevel methods (Gu et al., ICLR 2023; Hu et al., NeurIPS 2022) that relied on expensive second-order Hessian/Jacobian-vector products, by proposing two fully first-order algorithms (FOSL and MemCS). The robust meta-learning application additionally builds on MAML (Finn et al., ICML 2017) and the top-k loss reformulation from Lyu et al. (TPAMI 2020), introducing a novel rank-based robustness mechanism via minimax bilevel optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first fully first-order algorithms for general multi-block minimax bilevel optimization remove a significant computational barrier (Hessian computations) that limited prior methods, making the problem tractable for large-scale applications. The linear speedup w.r.t. the number of blocks P in MemCS is the first such result in this problem class, and the per-block sample complexity of O(ε^{-1}) improves over the O(ε^{-1.5}) of comparable single-block methods. Open questions include the sensitivity to the Lagrangian parameter λ (which trades off approximation gap against gradient variance), the practical validity of the new block-heterogeneity assumption (Assumption 4.6), and whether the cold-start strategy in MemCS can be further refined to reduce the inner-loop overhead K.
