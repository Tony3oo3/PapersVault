## Problem

Recent methods for privacy-preserving adaptation of closed LLMs (DP-ICL, PromptPATE, DP-FewShotGen, DP-OPT) were proposed without thorough analysis of their threat models or comparison against locally deployed open LLM alternatives. This paper asks whether these closed-LLM methods actually achieve truly privacy-preserving adaptations, and whether they outperform simpler gradient-based private tuning on open LLMs in terms of privacy, performance, and cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240038/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The methods consume private training data (text examples with labels) and text queries, producing class labels (SST2, Trec, MPQA, Disaster), text summaries (SAMSum), answers (PFL-DocVQA), or extracted entities (MIT-D, MIT-G). A critical operational distinction is that closed-LLM methods must transmit both training data (as shots in teacher prompts) and inference queries to a third-party API provider, while open-LLM methods keep all data local. Privacy guarantees are parameterized by differential privacy budget ε and failure probability δ = 1/|D|, with the paper evaluating ε ∈ [0, 8].

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic empirical comparison of four private ICL methods for closed LLMs against three private gradient-based tuning methods (PromptDPSGD, PrivateLoRA, DP-FineTune) for open LLMs, spanning 10+ models, 8 datasets, and monetary cost analysis. As a technical contribution, they extend PromptDPSGD to text generation (PromptDPSGDGen) by increasing soft prompt parameters from 0.1% to 10% of model parameters via prefix projection, and extend PromptPATE to generation (PromptPATEGen) by combining student prompt training with DP-ICL's private token-by-token generation to handle the large vocabulary output space. The analysis reveals that all closed-LLM methods leak query data to the provider, three of four also leak training data, and all underperform open-LLM adaptations even when the open models have 500× fewer parameters. The key insight is that gradient-based tuning on open LLMs is strictly preferable: it provides absolute privacy against the LLM provider (no data leaves the local machine), achieves higher accuracy at the same or tighter privacy budgets, and incurs lower costs by eliminating per-token API charges.

## Results

On four text classification tasks (SST2, Trec, MPQA, Disaster) at ε=8, PrivateLoRA on Vicuna 7B achieves 90.3% average accuracy, outperforming the best closed-LLM method (PromptPATE on Claude 2.1 at 84.5%) and DP-OPT on GPT3 Davinci (81.4%) by 5.8 and 8.9 points respectively. For dialog summarization (SAMSum), PrivateLoRA on BART-Large (355M params) achieves RougeL 39.1, surpassing DP-ICL on GPT3 Davinci (175B params) at 31.8. Total costs for 10k queries drop from $665.91 (DP-ICL, GPT3 Davinci) to $3.59 (PrivateLoRA, BART-Large) for SAMSum, a ~185× reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PrivateLoRA on RoBERTa Large (355M params) outperforms DP-OPT on GPT3 Davinci (175B params) by over 26 points on Trec (93.9% vs 68.7%) and by 7.9 points on average (89.3% vs 81.4%), despite using 500× fewer parameters. PrivateLoRA on Pythia-160M (78.6% avg) beats DP-FewShotGen on GPT3 Babbage (64.2% avg) by 14.4 points, even though the closed model has 8× more parameters. For generation on SAMSum, PrivateLoRA on BART-Large achieves RougeL 39.1 versus DP-ICL on GPT3 Davinci at 31.8, while costing $3.59 total versus $665.91. DP-ICL on GPT4 Turbo achieves the highest SST2 accuracy among closed methods (95.9%) but has the worst Trec score (16.2%) and the highest query cost ($138 for 10k queries). PromptPATE on Claude 2.1 (84.5% avg) is the strongest closed-LLM baseline but still trails PrivateLoRA on Vicuna 7B (90.3%) by 5.8 points at 3.7× the cost. Across all comparisons, open-LLM gradient-based adaptations (PrivateLoRA, DP-FineTune) consistently dominate closed-LLM discrete-prompt methods in both performance and cost, with the gap widening under tighter privacy budgets (ε<1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the recent wave of privacy-preserving LLM adaptation methods (DP-ICL, PromptPATE, DP-FewShotGen, DP-OPT for closed LLMs; PromptDPSGD, PrivateLoRA, DP-FineTune for open LLMs) and positions itself as a systematic empirical comparison across privacy, performance, and cost axes. It extends PromptDPSGD and PromptPATE (Duan et al., NeurIPS 2023) to text generation tasks (PromptDPSGDGen, PromptPATEGen), incorporating the private token-generation mechanism from DP-ICL (Wu et al., ICLR 2024) to handle the large output vocabulary. Conceptually, the paper departs from the prior framing that treats private ICL for closed LLMs as a viable path toward privacy-preserving adaptation, arguing instead that the threat model of these methods is fundamentally incomplete because they do not protect query data from the LLM provider.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear, actionable recommendation for practitioners: for truly privacy-preserving LLM adaptation, open LLMs with gradient-based private tuning are strictly superior to closed LLMs with discrete prompt-based ICL across all three axes (privacy, performance, cost). The finding that all four studied closed-LLM methods leak query data to the provider, and three of four also leak training data, challenges the implicit assumption in the recent literature that these methods achieve 'truly privacy-preserving' adaptation. Open questions include whether private inference (e.g., homomorphic encryption or MPC) for closed LLMs could close the query-data gap, whether better prompt transfer techniques could narrow the performance gap, and whether the cost advantage of open LLMs will persist as closed-model pricing evolves. Limitations include budget constraints on closed-model experiments, limited hyperparameter tuning for some open-model configurations, and reliance on 4-bit quantization for larger models.
