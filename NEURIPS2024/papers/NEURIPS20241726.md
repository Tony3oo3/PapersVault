## Problem

While RoPE is the dominant position encoding in modern LLMs, no prior work had connected the geometric angle between RoPE's paired query/key weight vectors to the type of information (basic syntax vs. high-level semantics) a model processes, nor leveraged this structure to reduce fine-tuning costs. The paper asks: what does the angle between each RoPE-defined weight vector pair tell us about the model's internal division of labor, and can this knowledge be exploited to freeze parameters that do not need updating during fine-tuning?

## Input / Output

The method consumes the pre-trained LLM's query and key weight matrices, computing the cosine similarity between each RoPE-paired row vector (w^(2i-1), w^(2i)) to produce a per-pair angle map. It outputs a binary mask that freezes all pairs with |cos α| ≥ τ (typically 77–93% of pairs) and permits updates only on the remaining near-orthogonal pairs, which are then fine-tuned via LoRA adapters. The unusual aspect is that the input is purely the geometric structure of the pre-trained weights (no forward pass or activation data needed), and the output is a parameter-level mask applied before any training begins.

## Method

The core theoretical result (Eq. 5) shows that the direction γ of RoPE's projected 2D vector depends on the input-dependent angle β scaled by sin α, where α is the angle between the two weight vectors in each RoPE pair; when |sin α| ≈ 0 the direction becomes input-independent, forcing attention onto fixed relative positions regardless of content. Empirically, shallow layers exhibit high average |cos α| (syntax-focused heads attending to prepositions, articles, special tokens) while deep layers show near-zero values (semantics-focused heads attending to full phrases), and comparing base vs. fine-tuned models reveals that fine-tuning predominantly updates the near-orthogonal pairs while leaving non-orthogonal pairs nearly unchanged. The proposed Angle-based Weight Masking (AWM) method sets a threshold τ and freezes all weight vector pairs with |cos α| ≥ τ before fine-tuning, operating at the individual row-vector-pair level and thus orthogonal to LoRA. Combined with LoRA (r=8 or r=2), AWM reduces trainable query/key parameters by 77–93% while maintaining or slightly boosting performance on TruthfulQA, GSM8K, and HellaSwag across Llama-2-7b/13b, Mistral-7B, and Phi-2.

## Results

On Llama-2-7b fine-tuned on Alpaca with LoRA (r=8), AWM with τ=0.001 fixes 92.12% of query/key weight vector pairs while improving TruthfulQA mc1 from 33.41 to 35.00 and mc2 from 49.48 to 50.97 over LoRA alone. On Llama-2-13b, AWM (τ=0.001) boosts TruthfulQA mc1 from 30.72 to 32.07 and GSM8K from 18.42 to 19.48 while fixing 93.40% of pairs. In the LoftQ setting (Table 2), AWM reduces WikiText-2 perplexity from 5.518 to 5.480 on Llama-2-7b and from 6.423 to 6.335 on Mistral-7B, while improving GSM8K accuracy from 39.20 to 40.86 and from 54.51 to 55.88 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (r=8) on Llama-2-7b, AWM with τ=0.001 improves TruthfulQA mc1 by +1.59 (35.00 vs. 33.41) and mc2 by +1.49 (50.97 vs. 49.48) while fixing 92.12% of weight vector pairs; GSM8K drops slightly (10.69 vs. 10.92) and HellaSwag is essentially tied (58.91 vs. 58.81). On Llama-2-13b, AWM (τ=0.001) improves TruthfulQA mc1 by +1.35 and GSM8K by +1.06 over LoRA alone, again with 93.40% of pairs frozen. In the LoftQ setting, AWM consistently reduces WikiText-2 perplexity (5.518→5.480 on Llama-2-7b; 6.423→6.335 on Mistral-7B) and boosts GSM8K (39.20→40.86; 54.51→55.88), though on Phi-2 the perplexity slightly increases (9.553→9.766) while GSM8K still improves substantially (48.75→51.71). Overall, AWM matches or exceeds the LoRA/LoftQ baselines on most metrics while reducing the number of trainable parameters by 77–93% in the query/key matrices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RoPE (Su et al., 2024) by analyzing the geometric structure of the weight vector pairs that RoPE rotates, providing a new angle-based lens for understanding how positional information is encoded. It connects to the interpretability literature showing that shallow layers handle syntax while deep layers handle semantics (Tenney et al., 2019; Gromov et al., 2024), but reframes this hierarchy in terms of a concrete, computable property of RoPE weight matrices rather than probing tasks. On the practical side, AWM is positioned as orthogonal to LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) and LoftQ (Li et al., 2024), composing with existing PEFT methods rather than competing with them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that fine-tuning selectively updates near-orthogonal (semantic) weight pairs while preserving non-orthogonal (syntactic) ones offers a principled, model-agnostic criterion for reducing trainable parameters without a probing or search procedure. AWM's ability to fix 77–92% of query/key weight pairs while maintaining or improving performance across Llama-2, Mistral, and Phi-2 makes it a lightweight, plug-in addition to standard LoRA pipelines. Key limitations include applicability only to RoPE-based models and only to query/key weights (not value or FFN), and the open question of why non-orthogonal pairs emerge during pre-training and whether their role generalizes beyond the syntactic/semantic framing. Extending the angle-based analysis to multi-modal or non-RoPE architectures remains future work.
