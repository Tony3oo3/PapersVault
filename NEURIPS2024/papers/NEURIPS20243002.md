## Problem

Cross-validation is the default method for estimating out-of-sample model performance, yet its statistical advantages over the simple plug-in approach (reusing training data for evaluation) have remained largely uncharacterized in nonparametric regimes with slow convergence rates. Existing results either center limit theorems at the average-of-folds rather than the full-size model, or are restricted to specific models or fast convergence rates, leaving the regime γ ≤ 1/4 essentially open.

## Input / Output

The framework takes i.i.d. training data (X_i, Y_i), a trained predictor ẑ(x) from either a parametric or nonparametric learner, and a cost function ℓ(z; Y), and produces a point estimate and a (1−α)-level confidence interval for the out-of-sample performance E[ℓ(ẑ(X); Y)]. The analysis operates in the low-dimensional asymptotic regime (n → ∞ with fixed dimensions of X, Y, Z) and applies to both supervised learning losses and downstream optimization objectives in contextual stochastic optimization (e.g., newsvendor, CVaR portfolio optimization). Coverage validity is assessed against both the finite-sample model performance c(ẑ) and the oracle best performance c(z*).

## Method

The paper develops a novel higher-order Taylor analysis centered at the deterministic decision z_n(x) = E[ẑ(x)] rather than the limiting model z*(x), which is essential for nonparametric models where ẑ(x) − z_n(x) already captures the variability driving plug-in bias. This yields precise bias characterizations: plug-in has optimistic bias Θ(n^{−2γ_v}), K-fold CV has pessimistic bias Θ(n^{−2γ}), and LOOCV has pessimistic bias o(n^{−1}), where γ = min{γ_b, γ_v} is the overall convergence rate. The key theoretical contribution is establishing necessary-and-sufficient equivalences between pointwise LOO stability (β_n = o(n^{−1/2})), the variability rate (γ_v > 1/4), and plug-in coverage validity, as well as showing γ > 1/4 is necessary and sufficient for K-fold CV coverage validity. The analysis proves K-fold CV never outperforms plug-in in either bias or coverage, and that LOOCV's smaller bias is negligible relative to evaluation variability for all parametric models and many nonparametric models (random forests, kNN with sufficient smoothness), making plug-in the preferred choice in most practical settings.

## Results

The paper's central theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that plug-in bias is Θ(n^{-2γ_v}), K-fold CV bias is Θ(n^{-2γ}), and LOOCV bias is o(n^{-1}), meaning K-fold CV is always at least as biased as plug-in since γ ≤ γ_v. In terms of coverage validity (Theorem 2), K-fold CV intervals are invalid whenever γ ≤ 1/4, while plug-in remains valid as long as γ_v > 1/4, and LOOCV is always valid. Numerically (Table 2), for a Forest regressor with β=0.4 at n=9600, 5-fold CV coverage drops to 0.42 while plug-in achieves 0.85 and LOOCV achieves 0.90; for kNN with k_n=Θ(n^{1/4}) in CVaR portfolio optimization, plug-in coverage is 0.00 while LOOCV reaches 0.89.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three evaluation methods—plug-in, K-fold CV, and LOOCV—on their ability to estimate out-of-sample model performance. Plug-in provably dominates K-fold CV in both bias magnitude and coverage validity across all model classes: K-fold CV's pessimistic bias Θ(n^{-2γ}) is always at least as large as plug-in's optimistic bias Θ(n^{-2γ_v}), and K-fold CV intervals become invalid for any nonparametric model with γ ≤ 1/4 while plug-in intervals remain valid whenever γ_v > 1/4. LOOCV achieves the smallest bias (o(n^{-1})) and is the only method with guaranteed valid coverage in the slowest regime (γ_v ≤ 1/4), but at the cost of n model re-trainings. For all parametric models (γ = 1/2) and nonparametric models with γ_v > 1/4 (e.g., random forests with β < 2/3, kNN with k_n = ω(√n)), plug-in matches LOOCV in coverage validity while being computationally far cheaper, making it the preferred default. The numerical experiments confirm these theoretical rankings: 5-fold CV coverage degrades to 0.42 for Forest at n=9600 while plug-in maintains 0.85, and for kNN with k_n=Θ(n^{1/4}) in portfolio optimization, plug-in coverage collapses to 0.00 while LOOCV holds at 0.89.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on statistical properties of cross-validation and plug-in estimators for out-of-sample performance. It builds on Chen et al. [18], who showed plug-in CLTs under stability conditions but did not characterize when coverage fails, and Bayle et al. [10], who derived CV CLTs centered at the average-of-folds rather than the full-size model. The key departure is a novel higher-order Taylor analysis that provides necessary and sufficient conditions (not merely sufficient) for coverage validity across the full spectrum of convergence rates, filling the previously open regime γ ≤ 1/4 for nonparametric models. For parametric models, the bias results recover and extend Fushiki [30] and Iyengar et al. [36].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the near-universal assumption that cross-validation is the gold standard for model evaluation, showing provably that K-fold CV never outperforms the computationally cheaper plug-in approach in bias or coverage, and that LOOCV's bias advantage is often negligible relative to evaluation variability. This provides practitioners with clear, rate-dependent guidance on when the computational overhead of CV is actually warranted. Key limitations include the asymptotic (n→∞, fixed dimension) setting, required smoothness and stability assumptions, and the explicit caveat that results apply to model evaluation rather than model selection. Open questions include high-dimensional regimes, non-asymptotic guarantees, and whether fold-number-increasing CV variants can improve the statistical-computational tradeoff.
