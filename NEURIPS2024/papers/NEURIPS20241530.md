## Problem

No consistent risk estimates exist for intermediate iterates of iterative algorithms (GD, SGD, proximal variants) in high-dimensional robust regression with heavy-tailed noise. Existing tools like ALO and GCV target only the final solution, and the closest prior work (Bellec & Tan 2024) is restricted to square loss with full-batch gradients, breaking down for robust losses and small SGD batches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the design matrix X, response vector y, the full sequence of iterates {b̂^t}_{t=1}^T from any algorithm of the form (4), and the batch index sets I_t for SGD. It outputs a per-iterate risk estimate r̃_t tracking ‖Σ^{1/2}(b̂^t − b*)‖² without requiring knowledge of Σ, computed efficiently via Hutchinson's trace approximation on T×T matrices. Because the additive noise term ‖ε‖²/n is constant along the trajectory, the estimates are directly comparable across iterations and hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, penalty strength) to identify the optimal stopping point.

## Method

The core contribution is a probabilistic approximation expressing the generalization error as a squared norm of residuals plus weighted past gradient contributions, where the weights w_{t,s} are entries of a T×T matrix W derived from recursive chain-rule differentiation of each iterate with respect to design matrix entries. This recursive structure is formalized through a block-triangular matrix M whose off-diagonal blocks P_t = D̃_t(I_p − (η_t/|I_t|)X^T S_t D_t X) encode the interplay between the loss Jacobian D_t, the proximal operator Jacobian D̃_t, and the batch selection matrix S_t. For the practical estimate r̃_t that avoids knowing Σ, the paper proposes W̃ = K̂^{-1}Â where K̂ and Â are T×T matrices computable entirely from data and iterates. The key technical innovation for SGD is incorporating out-of-batch samples into the construction of K̂ to guarantee invertibility, since the naive generalization of [5] yields a lower-triangular matrix with diagonal entries Tr(S_t D_t) that vanish for small batches. Both estimates are proven consistent (error → 0 in probability) under Gaussian design, bounded first-moment noise (allowing infinite variance), and 1-Lipschitz loss assumptions.

## Results

The paper establishes two consistent risk estimators for the generalization error of iterates in robust regression. Theorem 3.6 shows that $\hat{r}_t$ (requiring knowledge of $\Sigma$) satisfies $\mathbb{P}(|\hat{r}_t - r_t| > \epsilon) \leq \max\{1, C/\epsilon\}(1/\sqrt{n} + \mathbb{E}[\min\{1, \|\varepsilon\|/n\}])$, while Theorem 3.7 shows the data-only estimator $\tilde{r}_t$ achieves the same rate plus an additional $2e^{-n/18}$ term. In simulations with $(n,p)=(10000,5000)$ and $(10000,12000)$ using Huber and Pseudo-Huber losses with heavy-tailed (t-distribution, 2 df) noise, both estimates closely track the true risk $\|\Sigma^{1/2}(\hat{b}^t - b^*)\|^2$ across all 100 iterations for GD, SGD, Proximal GD, and Proximal SGD. The suboptimal estimator $\tilde{r}_t^{\text{sub}}$ (directly generalized from Bellec & Tan [5]) degrades noticeably as $t$ increases, particularly for small batch sizes ($|I_t|=n/10$).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the proposed estimator $\tilde{r}_t$ and the suboptimal estimator $\tilde{r}_t^{\text{sub}}$ obtained by directly generalizing the approach of Bellec and Tan [5] to SGD. Figure 4 (with $(n,p,T)=(4000,1000,20)$, batch size $n/10$, $\eta_t=0.2$) shows that $\tilde{r}_t^{\text{sub}}$ is clearly less accurate than $\tilde{r}_t$, with the gap widening as the iteration number $t$ increases. The root cause is that the matrix $\tilde{K}$ used in $\tilde{r}_t^{\text{sub}}$ has diagonal entries $\text{Tr}[S_t D_t]$ that can be zero or near-zero for small batches, making it non-invertible or ill-conditioned. The proposed $\tilde{r}_t$ overcomes this by using out-of-batch samples in the construction of $\hat{K}$ and $\hat{A}$, avoiding $S_t$ in the diagonal elements. The paper also contrasts its framework with [5] (square loss, full-batch only), [21] (GCV failure along GD trajectory), and [19] (deterministic SGD dynamics for isotropic design), noting that none of these handle the combination of robust losses, non-smooth regularizers, and stochastic batches simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the risk estimation framework of Bellec and Tan [5] (which covered only square loss with full-batch gradient descent) to robust loss functions (Huber, Pseudo-Huber) and stochastic gradient updates (SGD, proximal SGD). The core probabilistic approximation for out-of-sample error was developed by Bellec [3] for final solutions of robust M-estimators, and this paper adapts it to the trajectory of iterative algorithms. A key technical departure from [5] is the use of out-of-batch samples in constructing the weight matrix, which resolves a non-invertibility issue that arises when naively extending [5]'s approach to small SGD batches. The work also connects to the dynamical mean-field theory literature [8, 13, 19] but provides explicit per-iteration risk estimates rather than characterizing limiting dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably consistent risk estimate for iterates of SGD and proximal SGD in robust regression with heavy-tailed noise, filling a gap where no consistent estimator existed. The practical impact is enabling optimal stopping and hyperparameter selection (learning rate, batch size, penalty strength) without expensive cross-validation refitting. Key limitations include the T^T dependence in the constant (restricting T to O(log n / log log n)), the Gaussian feature assumption, and the requirement of a twice-differentiable loss. Open questions include extending to non-Gaussian features, non-smooth losses like LAD (requiring ADMM-type algorithms), and improving the iteration-count dependence.
