## Problem

There is a lack of standardized, diverse, large-scale vision-language benchmarks for evaluating VLMs on microscopy image understanding across multiple modalities, scales, organisms, and biological states. Existing biomedical benchmarks predominantly target narrow diagnostic tasks (mainly histopathology) and lack comprehensive characterization of both perception and cognition capabilities, hindering the development of robust microscopy foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240965/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark instance pairs a microscopy image (lossless PNG at original resolution, spanning 8 submodalities of light, fluorescence, and electron microscopy) with a text question. Perception tasks use closed VQA with expert-curated distractors sampled from ontological hierarchies of modalities, domains, and staining techniques; cognition tasks use six-option multiple-choice questions generated by GPT-4 and verified by domain experts. Localization tasks additionally require bounding box or segmentation mask outputs for nuclei, mitochondria, cells, and glands.

## Method

Micro-Bench comprises 17,235 images from 26 public datasets organized into three subsets: 5 coarse-grained perception tasks (modality, domain, stain identification), 18 fine-grained perception tasks (cell type, organelle, cell cycle phase classification plus segmentation and object detection), and a cognition component with 121 expert-generated reasoning questions. The construction pipeline involves expert taxonomic curation, manual standardization of metadata with bio-ontology identifiers (SNOMED, BTO, FMA, LOINC, UBERON), and GPT-4-assisted transformation of open-ended expert questions into multiple-choice format with verified distractors. The empirical study evaluates 12 VLMs across generalist autoregressive, generalist contrastive, specialist biomedical, and specialist pathology categories, revealing that specialist fine-tuning causes catastrophic forgetting of prior biomedical knowledge. The paper further demonstrates that simple weight interpolation between base and fine-tuned models mitigates this forgetting and improves general performance across all Micro-Bench tasks.

## Results

On Micro-Bench, the best-performing model GPT-4o achieves 62.68% macro-average accuracy on coarse-grained perception, 51.73% on fine-grained perception, and 62.00% on cognition (reasoning) tasks, indicating substantial error rates across all categories. The best specialist biomedical model (BiomedCLIP) reaches 47.57% coarse-grained, 34.65% fine-grained, and 25.00% cognition accuracy, while the best pathology specialist (CONCH) scores 27.84%, 33.64%, and 18.00% respectively. Notably, fine-tuned pathology models (PLIP, QuiltNet) underperform their base generalist models (OpenCLIP, CLIP) on coarse-grained perception and cognition, demonstrating catastrophic forgetting. Weight merging between base and fine-tuned models improves specialist zero-shot performance across all Micro-Bench perception tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o outperforms all other models across all three task categories, leading BiomedCLIP (the best biomedical specialist) by 15.11% on coarse-grained perception, 17.08% on fine-grained perception, and 37.00% on cognition. General autoregressive models (CogVLM, QwenVLM) beat the best specialist biomedical model BiomedCLIP by 4.48% on coarse-grained perception and 16.00% on cognition, despite having no biomedical-specific training. A striking finding is that pathology-specialist models PLIP and QuiltNet underperform their own base models (OpenCLIP and CLIP) on coarse-grained perception (31.11% vs. 36.34% and 26.58% vs. 35.41%) and cognition (17.00% vs. 38.33% and 13.00% vs. 28.00%), confirming catastrophic forgetting from domain-specific fine-tuning. However, on fine-grained perception—closest to the pathology training data—CONCH (33.64%) and BiomedCLIP (34.65%) do outperform generalist models, showing that specialist tuning helps only on in-distribution tasks. Weight merging (e.g., PLIP+OpenCLIP) recovers performance on both pathology-specific and general perception tasks, outperforming the fine-tuned models alone across all Micro-Bench perception subtasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the general VLM benchmarking paradigm (perception/cognition splits as in MME and SEED-Bench) to the microscopy domain, departing from prior biomedical benchmarks that focus narrowly on histopathology diagnostic classification. It builds directly on the weight-merging strategy from Wortsman et al. (CVPR 2022) for mitigating catastrophic forgetting, applying it for the first time to biomedical contrastive embedding models. The VQA task formulation follows established closed-VQA conventions, but the multi-modality, multi-scale, multi-organism coverage across electron, fluorescence, and light microscopy is unprecedented in existing biomedical vision-language benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Micro-Bench provides the first standardized, permissively-licensed benchmark for evaluating VLMs on microscopy understanding, filling a critical gap that limits progress in microscopy foundation models. The finding that specialist biomedical VLMs frequently underperform generalist models challenges the prevailing assumption that domain-specific fine-tuning is always beneficial, and the demonstration that simple weight merging mitigates catastrophic forgetting offers a practical, training-free remedy. However, the cognition component is limited to 54 images and 121 questions from only 6 experts, constraining the strength of reasoning-related conclusions. Open questions include whether weight merging generalizes to autoregressive VLMs and larger architectures, and how to build truly generalizable biomedical models without sacrificing domain-specific performance.
