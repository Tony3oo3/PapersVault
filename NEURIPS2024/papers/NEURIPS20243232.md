## Problem

Despite extensive optimization of SNN training algorithms, SNNs still lag behind their ANN counterparts in classification accuracy. The recently proposed multi-threshold models offer a path to richer per-step information transmission, but prior works have not rigorously established the mathematical equivalence between equidistant multi-threshold neurons, vanilla spiking models, and quantized ANNs, leaving the theoretical foundation and practical integration of these models underexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243232/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) or event-stream frames (48×48, 10 frames for CIFAR10-DVS) and outputs a class label. A key structural detail is that the LM-HT neuron with L threshold levels over T time-steps transmits the same information as a vanilla LIF neuron over LT time-steps, so comparisons are made at matched total time-steps (LT). For hardware deployment, the trained LM-HT model is reparameterized into a single-threshold LIF model over LT steps with no accuracy loss.

## Method

The LM-HT model introduces L equidistant firing thresholds so that each neuron emits an integer spike count from 0 to L per time-step, and a Temporal-Global Information Matrix (T-GIM) Ω ∈ ℝ^{T×T} that linearly combines spike inputs across all T time-steps to form the input current, alongside a learnable membrane leakage factor λ. The paper proves that under uniform input current the M-HT model's total spike count equals the QCFS activation of a quantized ANN, and that with L=1 it reduces to the vanilla LIF model, thereby unifying STBP training and quantized-ANN training under one framework. During back-propagation, the temporal gradient chain is detached and gradient flow between time-steps is governed entirely by Ω and λ, reducing redundant computation. A hybrid training framework initializes the LM-HT model from a pre-trained QCFS-quantized ANN (setting ω=1/T, λ=1, v(0)=θ/2) and fine-tunes with STBP for only 30 epochs, achieving near-ANN accuracy even at very low time latency. Finally, a layer-by-layer reparameterization scheme expands the L-level, T-step LM-HT into a single-threshold, LT-step LIF model for lossless deployment on existing neuromorphic hardware.

## Results

The LM-HT model (L=2, T=2) achieves 96.89% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-19) and 81.76% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-19) with only 2 time-steps, outperforming all prior methods that require 4–6 steps. On ImageNet-1k (ResNet-34), it reaches 70.90% at 2 steps, surpassing GLIF (67.52% at 4 steps) by 3.38%. On the neuromorphic CIFAR10-DVS dataset, LM-HT (L=2) achieves 80.70% at 2 steps versus 76.20% for RMP-Loss at 10 steps. In the hybrid training setting, the LM-HT model improves converted SNN accuracy by up to 32.91% over QCFS conversion at 2 time-steps on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LM-HT (L=2, T=2) outperforms all 19 compared baselines across every dataset. On CIFAR-10, it beats GLIF (95.03% at 6 steps) by 1.86% and TET (94.44% at 4 steps) by 2.45% while using only 2 steps. On CIFAR-100, the 3.48% margin over RMP-Loss (78.98% at 6 steps) is the largest gap in the paper. On ImageNet-1k, LM-HT surpasses GLIF by 3.38% and SEW ResNet by 3.86% at half the time-steps. In the hybrid training setting (Table 4), LM-HT improves over QCFS conversion by 32.91% at 2 steps on ResNet-20 (CIFAR-100, Tq=8), far exceeding SRP and SNM. On advanced backbones (Table S1), LM-HT outperforms GAC-SNN by 1.35% on CIFAR-100 (MS-ResNet-18) and Spikformer by 0.82% on CIFAR-10 (Transformer-4-384). No baseline is matched or beaten by LM-HT in any reported setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-threshold spiking neuron line of work (Sun et al. 2022, Wang et al. 2023) by providing the missing mathematical foundation: a rigorous proof that equidistant multi-threshold models are equivalent to vanilla IF models over L consecutive time-steps, and to quantized ANNs under uniform input. It builds on the STBP training paradigm (Wu et al. 2018) and the ANN-SNN Conversion framework (Cao et al. 2015; Bu et al. 2022), but departs from both by introducing the Temporal-Global Information Matrix (T-GIM) and learnable leakage parameters that unify the two training regimes under a single model. The reparameterization to a vanilla single-threshold LIF model for hardware deployment is a practical departure from prior multi-threshold work, which typically assumed multi-threshold hardware support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a significant performance gap between SNNs and quantized ANNs by showing that the gap is fundamentally a matter of information representation per time-step rather than an inherent limitation of spiking computation. The reparameterization to single-threshold models is critical for actual neuromorphic hardware deployment, where multi-threshold support is currently absent. Open questions include whether the T×T per-layer T-GIM matrix scales practically to very long time sequences (T > 10), whether the uniform-input assumption in the equivalence theorems holds in practice for deep networks with heterogeneous layer dynamics, and whether the biological plausibility of multi-threshold neurons can be justified from neuroscience. The hybrid training framework's 30-epoch fine-tuning overhead is modest but adds a dependency on pre-trained quantized ANN checkpoints.
