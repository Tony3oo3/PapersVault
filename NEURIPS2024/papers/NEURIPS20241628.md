## Problem

Transformer-based trajectory optimization methods (e.g., Decision Transformer) achieve strong offline RL performance but suffer from quadratic attention complexity and large parameter counts, limiting deployment on resource-constrained platforms. Mamba offers linear-time sequence modeling with fewer parameters, yet it is designed for long-sequence tasks while trajectory optimization typically uses short segments (K=8–30 timesteps), making its compatibility with this paradigm an open question.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a fixed-length window of K timesteps, where each timestep is a (state, action, return-to-go) triple concatenated along the temporal dimension (B3LD format) rather than the embedding dimension. At inference, a manually chosen target return-to-go conditions the policy, and the model outputs a single action per step—discrete (cross-entropy loss) for Atari or continuous (MSE loss) for MuJoCo. The optimal K is short (8–20), as longer sequences increase compute without improving performance due to exponential decay in hidden attention scores.

## Method

DeMa replaces the transformer's self-attention layer with Mamba's S6 selective state-space layer (hidden attention mechanism) inside a standard trajectory-optimization sequence-to-action framework, using parallel scan (Transformer-like) rather than recurrent inference. Ablations reveal that the hidden attention mechanism is the critical component: it works equally well with both pre- and post up-projection residual structures, eliminates the need for position embeddings, and is sensitive to weight swaps (unlike transformer attention in DT). The authors show that hidden attention scores decay exponentially with token distance, explaining why short sequences suffice and why the RNN-like variant (full-trajectory recurrent inference) is both slower and less effective. The final architecture uses 3 layers (MuJoCo) or 6 layers (Atari) with d_model=64–128, d_state=64, and expand=2, achieving 30% fewer parameters than DT on Atari and one-quarter the parameters on MuJoCo while matching or exceeding its performance.

## Results

On eight Atari games (1% DQN Replay), DeMa achieves an average normalized score of 111.8, substantially outperforming DT (62.2) by 79.6 points while using 30% fewer parameters (1.7M vs. 2.35M) and 27% fewer MACs. On nine MuJoCo D4RL tasks, DeMa reaches an average of 78.5 (vs. DT's 76.4) with only one-quarter of DT's parameters (175.5K/500K vs. 726.2K/2.6M) and roughly 3.5× fewer MACs. DeMa also demonstrates better scalability: its computational cost grows linearly with sequence length, whereas DT's grows quadratically.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeMa outperforms all seven baselines on both benchmark suites. Against DT (the primary trajectory-optimization baseline), DeMa improves the Atari average from 62.2 to 111.8 (+79.6) and the MuJoCo average from 76.4 to 78.5 (+2.1), while reducing parameters by 28% on Atari and by 75% on MuJoCo. Against the value-based CQL, DeMa leads by 46.9 points on Atari (111.8 vs. 64.9) and by 0.9 on MuJoCo (78.5 vs. 77.6). Against the metaformer-based DC and DC-hybrid, DeMa achieves 111.8 vs. 86.2 and 94.7 respectively on Atari. On MuJoCo, DeMa surpasses the SSM-based DS4 (78.5 vs. 68.6), the supervised-learning RvS (78.5 vs. 71.7), and the graph-based GDT (78.5 vs. 76.8). The only per-game losses are on Pong (98.2 vs. DT's 105.6 and DC-hybrid's 111.1) and Qbert (54.4 vs. DC's 67.0), but DeMa's lower variance (±6.8 vs. ±14.7) suggests more stable training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Decision Transformer (DT) trajectory optimization framework and the Mamba selective state space model, investigating their compatibility for offline RL. It departs from Decision S4 (DS4), which uses an RNN-like SSM for inference, by showing that a Transformer-like Mamba (fixed-length truncated sequences) outperforms the RNN-like variant. It also distinguishes itself from DMamba, which simply swaps the attention block for a Mamba block, by identifying the hidden attention mechanism as the critical component and demonstrating that it can replace the full transformer block (eliminating position embedding and MLP layers) rather than merely the attention sublayer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practical guidance for deploying trajectory optimization in resource-constrained settings (robots, drones) by showing that Mamba-based DeMa achieves comparable or better performance than DT with 30–75% fewer parameters and linear (vs. quadratic) sequence-length scaling. The finding that hidden attention scores decay exponentially—mirroring Markov-chain forgetting—explains why short context windows suffice and long sequences are computationally wasteful. Open questions remain for POMDP environments, long-horizon non-Markovian tasks requiring genuine memory, multi-task RL, and online RL, where the RNN-like mode or longer contexts might become relevant.
