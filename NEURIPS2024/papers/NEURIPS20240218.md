## Problem

Learning the energy functional governing a diffusion process from population snapshots (without individual particle trajectories) requires solving an infinite-dimensional bilevel optimization: the outer problem minimizes prediction error while the inner problem solves the JKO scheme at each step. Existing methods like JKOnet tackle this via Brenier's theorem and ICNN-parametrized transport maps, but the bilevel structure is computationally prohibitive, limits scalability, and restricts the model to potential energies only, excluding interaction and internal energy terms that are essential for many physical and biological systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240218/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of T+1 empirical measures (population snapshots) μ₀,…,μ_T, each consisting of N particles in R^d. For the scRNA-seq application, these are the first 5 PCA components of cell populations measured at 5 time points over 27 days. Optimal transport couplings γ_t between consecutive snapshots are precomputed once via linear programming (or Sinkhorn) before training begins and are never recomputed during optimization. The output is a parameter vector θ = [θ₁, θ₂, θ₃] defining the potential V_{θ₁}, interaction kernel U_{θ₂}, and internal energy coefficient θ₃, from which one-step-ahead population predictions are generated via an implicit or explicit Euler scheme.

## Method

The core insight is to replace the JKO optimization problem with its first-order necessary optimality conditions, converting the bilevel learning problem into a single-level quadratic loss: minimize the squared norm of ∇V(x_{t+1}) + ∫∇U(x_{t+1}−x′)dμ_{t+1}(x′) + β∇ρ_{t+1}/ρ_{t+1} + (1/τ)(x_{t+1}−x_t) integrated over the precomputed optimal transport plan γ_t. This decouples the transport computation (done once) from the energy learning (a simple MSE), eliminating the inner optimization loop, the ICNN parametrization of transport maps, and the associated convexity regularizers. For linearly parametrized functionals (V_{θ₁}(x)=θ₁ᵀφ(x)), a closed-form solution exists via a normal-equation solve (Proposition 3.4); for non-linear parametrizations, standard gradient descent with 2-layer MLPs suffices. The model simultaneously recovers all three energy components (potential, interaction, internal) and supports inductive biases by zeroing out specific parameters, achieving state-of-the-art EMD on single-cell data in minutes versus hours for prior methods.

## Results

On the embryoid body scRNA-seq dataset (27-day human embryonic stem cell differentiation), JKOnet*_V (time-varying) achieves a one-step-ahead EMD of 0.624±0.007, outperforming the best prior method DMSB (0.67) and the direct predecessor JKOnet (1.363±0.214). On synthetic potential recovery experiments across 15 test functionals, JKOnet* uniformly outperforms all JKOnet variants in EMD error, convergence speed, and per-epoch time, with the linearly parametrized variant solving optimally in a single epoch. Training time for JKOnet* is a few minutes versus hours for all competing methods on the scRNA-seq task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JKOnet* achieves the lowest EMD (0.624±0.007) among all 12 methods evaluated on the scRNA-seq task, beating the closest competitor DMSB [12] (0.67) by ~7% and the next-best NLSB [28] (0.74) by ~16%. Against the direct predecessor JKOnet [9], the improvement is dramatic: 0.624 vs. 1.363 EMD (a 54% reduction), with JKOnet-vanilla performing even worse at 3.237. Among the flow-based and Schrödinger bridge methods, JKOnet* outperforms OT-CFM [49] (0.790), MIOFLOW [23] (0.79), Reg.CNF [17] (0.825), TrajectoryNet [50] (0.848), DSB [14] (0.862), and I-CFM [49] (0.872). The authors note that some baselines use a slightly different evaluation protocol (leave-one-timestep-out vs. 60/40 train/test split), so absolute comparisons require caution; nonetheless, JKOnet* trains in minutes while the listed methods require hours. On the synthetic potential recovery benchmark (Section 4.1), JKOnet* variants uniformly dominate all JKOnet variants across 15 test potentials in both accuracy and computational cost, with the linear parametrization achieving the optimal solution in a single epoch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the JKO scheme (Jordan, Kinderlehrer, Otto 1998) for modeling diffusion as energy-minimizing trajectories in Wasserstein space, and on the recent variational analysis framework of Lanzetti, Terpin, and Dörfler (2024) that provides first-order optimality conditions for optimization in probability space. Its most direct predecessor is JKOnet (Bunne et al., AISTATS 2022), which formulates learning the energy functional as an infinite-dimensional bilevel optimization problem using Brenier's theorem and ICNN parametrizations. The key departure is replacing this bilevel structure with a single-level quadratic loss derived from first-order conditions, which simultaneously enables learning of interaction and internal energy terms that were intractable under the bilevel formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first method to recover all three components of a diffusion energy functional (potential, interaction, internal) from population data, and does so with orders-of-magnitude speedup (minutes vs. hours) while achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on single-cell RNA-seq data. The closed-form solution for linearly parametrized functionals is a practical gift for practitioners who can avoid iterative optimization entirely. Open questions include the choice of features for the linear parametrization (acknowledged as a non-trivial problem in itself), extension to image and other high-dimensional data domains, and whether the vanilla MLP architecture can be improved. The authors also note potential applicability to population steering, reinforcement learning, and diffusion models, suggesting a broader methodological impact beyond the immediate task.
