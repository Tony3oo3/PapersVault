## Problem

Traditional robust reinforcement learning relies on sa-rectangularity, where the adversarial transition kernel is chosen independently at each (state, action) pair and time step. This independence assumption is rarely satisfied in practice and produces overly conservative policies that defend against physically implausible, instantaneous environment changes. The paper asks whether constraining the temporal evolution of the uncertainty parameter can yield policies that are both robust and less conservative.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241122/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives the environment state s_t and, depending on the variant, either the true environmental parameter ψ_t (Oracle-TC), the previous state and action (s_{t-1}, a_{t-1}) as a proxy for ψ (Stacked-TC), or nothing extra (Vanilla TC). The adversary's action is a perturbation vector b_t drawn from a fixed ball B(0, L) of radius L, so the parameter evolves as ψ_t = ψ_{t-1} + b_t. The output is a robust policy π(s, ψ) or π(s) mapping to actions, along with a pessimistic value function defined over the augmented state space (s, ψ). In experiments, the uncertainty set is a 3-dimensional box in MuJoCo environments (e.g., torso mass, friction, leg mass), normalized to [0,1]³, with L = 0.001 for a 1000-step episode.

## Method

The core contribution is the Time-Constrained Robust MDP (TC-RMDP), which parameterizes all transition kernels by a shared vector ψ and constrains its per-step change to ||ψ_{t+1} − ψ_t|| ≤ L, thereby coupling outcome probabilities across states and actions and across time. This is cast as a two-player zero-sum Markov game over the augmented state (s, ψ), where the adversary selects b_t from a fixed ball B(0, L) rather than an independent kernel at each step, yielding a TC Bellman operator that is a γ-contraction (Theorem 2.1) and admits a stationary optimal policy of the form π(s, ψ). Three algorithmic variants—Oracle-TC, Stacked-TC, and Vanilla TC—differ in how much of ψ the agent observes, with the latter two solving a partially observable game for which the paper provides Lipschitz smoothness bounds on the robust objective (Theorem 6.2) rather than fixed-point convergence. Practically, the TC constraint is injected into existing actor-critic robust RL algorithms (RARL and M2TD3) by restricting the adversary's search space to the ball B at each update, requiring only a modification to the adversary's action space and loss function while preserving the original critic/actor architectures.

## Results

In the worst-case time-constrained setting (perturbation radius L=0.001), TC-M2TD3 achieves the best non-oracle aggregate normalized score of 2.9±0.4 across five MuJoCo environments, compared to M2TD3 (1.0±0.58), RARL (−0.08±0.37), Domain Randomization (1.47±0.54), and vanilla TD3 (0.0±0.63). The Oracle-TC-M2TD3 variant, which conditions on the true parameter ψ, reaches 3.07±0.81, outperforming Oracle M2TD3 (1.39±0.31) by more than 2×. Even the vanilla TC variant (TC-M2TD3 without ψ access) achieves 2.9±0.4, representing up to a 2.9-fold improvement over the strongest non-TC baseline (M2TD3). In the static worst-case setting (Table 2), TC-M2TD3 scores 1.02±0.68 and TC-RARL scores 0.92±1.29, remaining competitive with M2TD3 (1.0±1.01) while substantially outperforming RARL (0.33±0.73).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against M2TD3 (the strongest non-TC robust baseline), TC-M2TD3 achieves 2.90 vs. 1.00 aggregate normalized score in the time-constrained worst-case setting — a 2.9× improvement — and also wins in the static worst-case setting (1.02 vs. 1.00). Against RARL, the gap is even more dramatic: TC-M2TD3 scores 2.90 vs. −0.08 (time-constrained) and 1.02 vs. 0.33 (static worst-case), indicating RARL's rectangularity assumption produces severely conservative policies under time-coupled perturbations. Against Domain Randomization, TC-M2TD3 (2.90) roughly doubles DR's score (1.47) in the time-constrained setting, while in the static average-case setting (Table 3) TC-M2TD3 (2.91) still outperforms DR (1.68). Against vanilla TD3 (normalized to 0.0), all TC variants show substantial gains, confirming that the time-constrained training itself provides meaningful robustness. Notably, even the Oracle-TC variants outperform their non-TC oracle counterparts (Oracle-TC-M2TD3: 3.07 vs. Oracle M2TD3: 1.39), demonstrating that the time-constraint formulation itself — not just ψ access — is the key driver of improved performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the sa-rectangularity assumption that underpins most robust MDP theory (Iyengar 2005; Nilim & El Ghaoui 2005) and the deep robust RL algorithms built on it (M2TD3, RARL). It introduces a parametric robust MDP where a shared parameter vector ψ couples transition probabilities across all (s,a) pairs, then constrains ψ's temporal evolution to be Lipschitz-bounded, yielding the TC-RMDP formulation. Conceptually, this sits between the factored non-rectangular models of Goyal & Grand-Clement (2018) and the non-stationary MDP literature (Lecarpentier & Rachelson 2019), but adds the specific temporal coupling constraint that makes the problem tractable via a modified zero-sum game. The three algorithmic variants (Oracle-TC, Stacked-TC, Vanilla-TC) are direct extensions of M2TD3 and RARL's actor-critic training loops with a constrained adversary action space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TC-RMDP framework addresses a well-recognized practical limitation: rectangular robust RL produces overly conservative policies because it allows the environment to change arbitrarily between consecutive time steps, which rarely reflects physical reality (e.g., gradual wear, evolving traffic). The key open question is whether the assumption of a known, low-dimensional parameterization ψ with a predefined uncertainty set Ψ generalizes to complex, high-dimensional environments where the disturbance structure is unknown. The paper acknowledges this limitation explicitly. Additionally, the Vanilla-TC variant, which operates without observing ψ, raises interesting questions about partially observable Markov games and whether the Lipschitz smoothness bound (Th. 6.2) is tight enough for practical convergence guarantees in deep RL settings.
