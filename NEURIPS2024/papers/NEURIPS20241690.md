## Problem

PAC-Bayes generalization bounds provide principled learning algorithms, but their optimization requires repeated evaluations of the empirical risk, which becomes a severe bottleneck when predictions involve solving stiff ODEs or PDEs (e.g., biochemical or physics models). This paper introduces a surrogate PAC-Bayes framework that decouples the costly risk queries from the optimization loop, enabling efficient bound minimization for computationally intensive, low-to-moderate-dimensional models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an empirical risk function (potentially requiring seconds per evaluation, as in the anaerobic digestion ODE model), a prior parameterized within an exponential family (e.g., Gaussian with block-diagonal covariance), and PAC-Bayes hyperparameters (temperature λ, confidence δ, bound constant C). It produces a posterior distribution within the same exponential family and the corresponding PAC-Bayes bound value. A key operational detail is that risk evaluations are accumulated across iterations and reweighted via Voronoi cell probabilities at each step, so only a small number of new queries (e.g., 32 per step after an initial 160) are needed regardless of how many optimization steps are taken.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the empirical risk in the PAC-Bayes bound with its L²(π_θ) orthogonal projection onto a (d+1)-dimensional functional space spanned by the score derivatives ∂_θℓ(θ,x) and a constant. Theorem 1 proves this projection leaves the gradient of the bound invariant, so optimizing the surrogate is locally equivalent to optimizing the true bound. For exponential families with Catoni's bound, the surrogate admits a closed-form minimizer θ̃ = θ_p − λ⁻¹η, which Lemma 1 shows is a Fisher-information-corrected gradient descent step, guaranteeing local descent. The practical algorithm SuPAC-CE iteratively draws new predictors from the current posterior, evaluates the risk, reweights all accumulated evaluations using Voronoi cell probabilities under π_θ, solves a weighted least-squares for η, and updates the posterior via the closed-form expression with a KL-constrained step size (kl_max, α_max). This design means the number of expensive risk queries is decoupled from the number of optimization steps, and the nonlinear surrogate can be valid over a wider neighborhood than a linear gradient approximation, permitting larger effective steps.

## Results

On the anaerobic digestion calibration task (30 parameters, ~3s per model query), SuPAC-CE achieves a PAC-Bayes bound of 0.121±0.004 with an average posterior risk of 0.102±0.003, matching the quality of Picard-Weibel et al. [2024] (bound 0.122) while being 20× faster. SuPAC-CE's average performance after only 1800 risk queries surpasses the best GD performance after the full 9600-query budget, and 4 out of 20 GD runs fail to beat a single SuPAC-CE step. In the meta-learning experiment, the learned prior reduces the average generalisation bound from 0.61 to 0.24 (λ=0.1) and from 0.14 to 0.050 (λ=0.01) after 150 gradient steps, with >80% of the reduction occurring in the first 15 steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard gradient descent (GD) with tuned hyperparameters (per_step=160, step_size=0.05), selected via a 1600-query grid search. SuPAC-CE decisively outperforms GD: its average bound after 1800 queries exceeds GD's best bound after the full 9600-query budget, and 4 of 20 GD runs produce a final objective worse than a single SuPAC-CE step. SuPAC-CE also matches the final posterior quality of Picard-Weibel et al. [2024] (bound 0.121 vs. 0.122) while requiring roughly 20× fewer risk queries. Nesterov accelerated GD (tested with momenta 0.5/0.9/0.95 and doubled step sizes) never surpasses SuPAC-CE; at doubled step sizes all momentum variants become unstable (3/8 or 2/8 runs fail), and at the original step size momentum only marginally improves stability at the cost of speed. SuPAC-CE incurs only 3.5% extra wall-clock time over GD (due to the Voronoi weighting step), making the efficiency gain almost entirely from reduced risk queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the established PAC-Bayes optimization paradigm (Germain et al. 2009, Biggs & Guedj 2021, Viallard et al. 2023) by introducing a surrogate strategy that decouples the cost of empirical risk evaluation from bound minimization. It builds directly on Catoni's bound (2007) for its closed-form solvability on exponential families, and on the authors' prior work (Picard-Weibel et al. 2024) which identified the ODE-solver bottleneck in biochemical model calibration. The meta-learning component connects to PAC-Bayes meta-learning (Pentina & Lampert 2014, Rothfuss et al. 2023) but departs by using a single prior rather than a distribution over priors, yielding a simplified meta-gradient that avoids second-order terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine practical barrier: PAC-Bayes optimization becomes infeasible when each risk query requires solving stiff ODEs/PDEs, a common situation in physics, biology, and engineering models. The 20× speedup with comparable bound quality on the anaerobic digestion task demonstrates that PAC-Bayes can be made practical for these domains, potentially opening the framework to a wider applied community. Key limitations include restriction to low-to-moderate dimensional problems (<few hundred parameters), ruling out deep learning, and the open question of how to automatically select the surrogate validity range. Future directions include Gaussian process surrogates for uncertainty-aware risk approximation and extension to higher-dimensional settings.
