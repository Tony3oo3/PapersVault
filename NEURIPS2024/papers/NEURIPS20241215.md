## Problem

Existing image watermarking methods, including state-of-the-art deep learning approaches, are vulnerable to stable-diffusion-based watermark removal attacks, with detection rates dropping from 79–100% to 0–48% under such attacks. No prior method is robust to this class of generative-AI-based attacks, creating a critical gap in protecting human-created images from being indistinguishable from AI-generated content.

## Input / Output

The method takes an existing RGB image, a pre-defined concentric ring-like watermark pattern (sampled from a complex Gaussian distribution), and a frozen pre-trained stable diffusion model (e.g., stable-diffusion-2-1-base with 50 denoising steps). It produces a watermarked image at approximately 30 dB PSNR and 0.92 SSIM relative to the original, along with a binary detection decision derived from a non-central chi-squared p-value threshold. The watermark is zero-bit (presence/absence only), and the method requires no training of a dedicated watermarking network—only per-image latent vector optimization (45–256 seconds per image).

## Method

ZoDiac embeds watermarks in the Fourier space of the trainable latent vector (Z_T) of a pre-trained stable diffusion model. The embedding pipeline has three steps: (1) DDIM inversion initializes Z_T from the input image, (2) a concentric ring-like watermark is injected into the Fourier-transformed latent vector within a circular mask, and the latent vector is optimized via gradient descent on a reconstruction loss (L2 + SSIM + Watson-VGG perceptual) so the frozen diffusion model regenerates an image resembling the original, and (3) adaptive image enhancement blends the generated image with the original to meet a target SSIM threshold. Detection reverses the process: DDIM inversion recovers the latent vector, Fourier transformation extracts the watermark region, and a non-central chi-squared statistical test determines presence. The key insight is that the iterative denoising process of the diffusion model inherently acts as a defense mechanism—attacks that corrupt the image in pixel space are partially 'washed out' when the image is re-inverted into the latent space, preserving the watermark signal.

## Results

On MS-COCO, DiffusionDB, and WikiArt, ZoDiac achieves ~29–30 dB PSNR and 0.92 SSIM while maintaining a watermark detection rate (WDR) above 98% against all individual attacks except rotation. Against the state-of-the-art diffusion-based attack Zhao23, ZoDiac retains a WDR of 98.6–99.2%, whereas all six baselines drop to 0–48%. Under the composite attack combining all individual attacks except rotation ('All w/o ○'), ZoDiac sustains a WDR of 51–55% while every baseline collapses to near 0%. The false positive rate (FPR) stays below 6.4% with the default detection threshold p*=0.90.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZoDiac decisively outperforms all five baselines on the most critical robustness metrics. Against the diffusion-based Zhao23 attack, ZoDiac achieves 98.8% WDR versus 28.6% for StegaStamp (the strongest baseline), 47.8% for CIN, and 0–3.2% for DwtDct, DwtDctSvd, RivaGAN, and SSL. Under the composite 'All w/o ○' attack, ZoDiac retains 51.0% WDR while every baseline drops to 0.0–12.4%, with StegaStamp at just 0.2%. The traditional frequency-domain methods (DwtDct, DwtDctSvd) and GAN-based RivaGAN are essentially defeated by any advanced attack (Bmshj18, Cheng20, Zhao23), dropping to 0–3% WDR. SSL is the only baseline robust to rotation (95.2% WDR) but is completely vulnerable to all other advanced attacks (0% WDR on Zhao23, Bmshj18, Cheng20). StegaStamp, the most robust prior method, still fails on rotation (0%) and degrades substantially under Zhao23 (28.6%), while ZoDiac's rotation vulnerability (53.8%) is mitigated to 99.2% WDR via a simple rotation auto-correction procedure at a modest FPR increase.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZoDiac builds on pre-trained stable diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022) and DDIM inversion (Song et al., 2020), repurposing the denoising/inversion pipeline as a watermarking mechanism rather than a generation tool. It departs from training-based watermarking methods (StegaStamp, RivaGAN, SSL, CIN) by operating in zero-shot fashion on a frozen pre-trained model, and from prior diffusion-based watermarking (Tree-Rings, Stable Signature, DiffusionShield) by watermarking existing real-world images rather than only synthetically-generated ones. The Fourier-space ring watermark injected into the trainable latent vector, combined with a non-central chi-squared statistical test for detection, is a novel combination that leverages the diffusion model's latent geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZoDiac is the first watermarking method to maintain high detection rates (>98%) against the most advanced generative-AI-based removal attack (Zhao23) and composite attacks where all prior methods fail entirely, making it practically relevant as diffusion-based attacks become more accessible. The finding that the iterative denoising process itself acts as an inherent defense mechanism (even 1 step vastly outperforms 0 steps) offers a conceptual insight into why latent-space operations are more robust than image-space ones. Key limitations include zero-bit watermarking only (no message encoding), vulnerability to rotation (mitigated but at FPR cost), and susceptibility to full-knowledge pipeline-aware attacks that overwrite the watermark. Future work on encoding meaningful information while preserving robustness, and on understanding composite attack ordering effects, remain open.
