## Problem

Existing off-policy selection (OPS) methods assume homogeneous agents and select a single best policy for the entire population, overlooking the heterogeneity among participants in human-centric systems (HCSs) such as healthcare and education. The pivotal gap is how to select a policy to deploy when a new participant joins the cohort, without access to any prior offline data collected over that participant — a problem that is intractable for standard OPS/OPE frameworks that require full trajectories or assume known population characteristics.

## Input / Output

FPS consumes only the initial state s_0 of an arriving participant (a high-dimensional feature vector, e.g., 142 features in the IE experiment), a set of pre-trained candidate policies Π, and an offline trajectory dataset D collected under a behavioral policy β. Unlike standard OPS which requires full trajectories, FPS operates at t=0 only, making it suitable for real-time deployment as participants arrive at irregular times. The output is a personalized policy assignment per participant (the best candidate policy for their identified sub-group) along with the sub-group label, enabling on-the-fly policy deployment without waiting for cohort-wide data collection.

## Method

FPS (First-Glance Off-Policy Selection) operates in two phases: a training phase and a deployment phase. In training, it first partitions offline participants into M sub-groups using TICC clustering on initial states (with M chosen via silhouette scores), optimizing an objective that maximizes the gap between the best candidate policy value and the behavioral policy value per sub-group. Within each sub-group, a VAE-based generative model augments the limited trajectories to improve state-action visitation coverage. An unbiased importance-sampling estimator with a variance bound (Proposition 2.5, derived via Rényi divergence) then estimates the value difference D^{π,β} for each candidate policy per sub-group, and the policy maximizing this difference is selected. In deployment, each arriving participant is assigned to the nearest sub-group by initial-state distance, and the pre-selected policy for that sub-group is deployed immediately — requiring no oracle, no full trajectory, and no population-level aggregation.

## Results

In the healthcare (sepsis) experiment, FPS achieves the lowest absolute error (AE) and highest return across all dataset sizes (N=2,500–10,000), with AE of 0.006 at N=5,000 (vs. 0.046 for WIS, 0.073 for FQE, 3.443 for WDR) and return of 0.149 (vs. 0.121–0.129 for all baselines). FPS also achieves the lowest top-1 regret (0.016 at N=10,000 vs. 0.019–0.029 for baselines). In the real-world IE experiment with 1,288 students over 6 semesters, FPS improved normalized learning gain (NLG) by 208% over the lecturer-designed behavioral policy and by 136% over the best existing OPS baselines, while for low-performing sub-groups (K3, K4) FPS achieved NLGs of 24.0 and 31.7 versus negative NLGs of −11.7 and −19.9 under baseline-selected policies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPS consistently outperforms all six OPE-based baselines (WIS, PDIS, FQE, WDR, MAGIC, DualDICE) on both the sepsis simulator and the real-world IE experiment. In the sepsis experiment, FPS achieves the lowest AE at every dataset size (e.g., 0.006 vs. 0.046 for WIS and 0.073 for FQE at N=5,000) and the highest return (0.149 vs. 0.121–0.129), with WDR and MAGIC showing catastrophically high AEs (3.2–8.3) at smaller N. FPS also achieves the lowest Regret@1 (0.016 at N=10,000 vs. 0.019 for WIS and 0.029 for PDIS/FQE/WDR/MAGIC). In the IE experiment, FPS selects the best-performing policy for high-performing sub-groups (K1, K2) matching the oracle best-of-18-baselines, while for low-performing sub-groups (K3, K4) FPS achieves strongly positive NLGs (24.0, 31.7) whereas baseline-selected policies produce negative NLGs (−11.7, −19.9), demonstrating that population-level OPS methods can actively harm vulnerable sub-groups. DualDICE was unable to distinguish between target policies in the IE experiment and was excluded from results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the off-policy selection (OPS) literature, which has focused on selecting a single best policy for an entire population using OPE estimators (WIS, PDIS, FQE, WDR, MAGIC, DualDICE), to a per-participant setting where only the initial state is available at deployment time. It combines three existing components—TICC clustering for sub-group partitioning, a VAE-based trajectory augmentation architecture from the authors' prior work [14], and an importance-sampling-based value estimator with a variance bound derived from [25]—into a unified framework (FPS) that assigns different policies to different sub-groups. The key conceptual departure from prior OPS is the shift from population-level to sub-group-level policy selection conditioned solely on t=0 observations, motivated by the practical constraint that HCS participants arrive without prior interaction data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine deployment gap in human-centric systems where online testing is prohibitively expensive or ethically constrained, and where a one-size-fits-all policy can actively harm vulnerable sub-groups (as demonstrated by the negative NLG effects of existing OPS baselines on low-performing students K3/K4). The real-world IE experiment with 1,288 students over 5 semesters provides strong evidence, but the healthcare validation relies on a simulated sepsis environment, leaving open whether the sub-group partitioning generalizes to the confounded, high-stakes clinical setting. Future work includes extending FPS to offline RL policy optimization, developing bias-variance trade-off estimators (e.g., integrating WDR or MAGIC weights), and incorporating domain-expert input into sub-grouping for healthcare applications.
