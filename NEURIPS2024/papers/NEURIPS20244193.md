## Problem

State-of-the-art dataset distillation methods employ diverse and complex strategies for generating synthetic images, yet it remains unclear whether the synthetic images or the accompanying soft (probabilistic) labels are the primary driver of distillation performance. This paper asks: what is the relative importance of features (images) versus labels in data-efficient learning, and what properties of soft labels make them effective?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244193/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes real training images randomly sampled from the original dataset (no synthetic generation) and a pretrained expert model (e.g., ResNet-18/50 for ImageNet-1K, ConvNet for smaller datasets) as inputs. The expert is early-stopped at an epoch tuned to the target images-per-class (IPC) budget, and its softmax outputs serve as soft labels for the sampled images. The output is a distilled dataset of size IPC × num_classes with soft labels, from which a student model is trained to convergence with only learning-rate tuning.

## Method

The paper introduces a simple soft-label baseline—randomly sampled real images paired with soft labels from an early-stopped expert—and shows it matches or exceeds SOTA distillation methods (SRe²L, Ra-BPTT, MTT) on ImageNet-1K, TinyImageNet, CIFAR-100, and CIFAR-10. Through ablations including label-swapping tests, temperature grid searches, and zero-shot removal experiments, the authors demonstrate that structured semantic information in the top softmax values (e.g., distributional similarity across related classes) is what enables data-efficient learning, and that the optimal expert epoch is unique to each data budget. They establish an empirical data-knowledge scaling law showing a constant ~6× dataset size reduction from expert knowledge, and a Pareto frontier combining experts at different epochs across IPC budgets. Finally, they show that directly optimizing labels via truncated-BPTT (without training any expert) recovers the same soft labels as an optimally early-stopped expert, linking dataset distillation to knowledge distillation.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, the soft label baseline (randomly sampled training images paired with soft labels from a pretrained ResNet18 expert) achieves 6.6% top-1 accuracy at IPC=1, 23.9% at IPC=10, 47.9% at IPC=50, 53.4% at IPC=100, and 56.8% at IPC=200, matching or exceeding the SOTA SRe2L method (2.9%, 21.3%, 46.8%, 52.8%, 57.0% respectively). The CutMix-augmented soft label variant further improves to 54.3% at IPC=50 and 57.7% at IPC=200. On smaller datasets (TinyImageNet, CIFAR-100, CIFAR-10), SOTA distillation methods (e.g., Ra-BPTT) can outperform the baseline at very low IPC (≤1), but the baseline catches up or surpasses them at IPC≥10, indicating that existing methods struggle to scale to larger data budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The soft label baseline (random images + expert soft labels) outperforms SRe2L [33] at every IPC budget on ImageNet-1K, with the largest gap at IPC=1 (6.6% vs. 2.9%) and IPC=10 (23.9% vs. 21.3%), and narrows to a near-tie at IPC=200 (56.8% vs. 57.0%). On TinyImageNet, Ra-BPTT [9] outperforms the baseline at IPC=1 (20.1% vs. 7.6%) but the baseline surpasses it at IPC=10 (27.2% vs. 24.4%) and IPC=50 (35.6% vs. not reported). MTT [4] and DM [36] are consistently outperformed by the baseline at IPC≥10 across all three smaller datasets (e.g., CIFAR-100 IPC=50: baseline 47.1% vs. MTT 47.7% vs. DM 43.6%). FRePo [39] on downsized ImageNet-1K achieves only 7.5% at IPC=1 and 9.7% at IPC=2, well below the baseline's 8.0% and 9.6%. TESLA [6] (MTT variant) is outperformed by the baseline on ImageNet-1K at all reported IPC values. Overall, the paper demonstrates that the synthetic image generation strategies in SOTA methods contribute little beyond what soft labels alone provide, with the baseline matching or exceeding all compared methods at moderate-to-high data budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the dataset distillation literature (Wang et al. 2018; Cazenavette et al. MTT; Feng et al. Ra-BPTT; Yin et al. SRe2L) but deliberately departs from its dominant focus on synthetic image generation strategies. It repositions the problem by drawing a direct connection to knowledge distillation (Hinton et al. 2015), showing that the soft label baseline is a special case of KD and that bi-level optimization (BPTT) recovers the same labels as early-stopped experts without explicitly training them. The work also revives and extends early observations by Bohdal et al. (2020) and Sucholutsky & Schonlau (2021) about soft labels in distillation, which were previously limited to MNIST-scale experiments and had little influence on subsequent large-scale work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that random training images paired with expert-generated soft labels match or exceed SOTA distillation methods on ImageNet-1K fundamentally reframes where research effort should be directed in dataset distillation—toward label quality and structure rather than image synthesis. The empirical data-knowledge scaling law and Pareto frontier provide quantitative guidance for practitioners choosing between data budget and expert knowledge. Key open questions remain: whether expert knowledge is truly necessary (or can be replaced by dataset-level summaries like symmetries), how to jointly optimize images and labels for optimal compression at very low IPC, and whether these conclusions generalize beyond image classification to other tasks and modalities.
