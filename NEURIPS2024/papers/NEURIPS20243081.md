## Problem

Shape from polarization (SfP) is highly ill-posed under unknown environment light because specular and diffuse reflections blend at arbitrary degrees, producing noisy AoLP maps that entangle illumination and geometry. Existing methods either require controlled lighting (sunny sky, frontal flash) that undermines SfP's passive imaging advantage, or make strong reflection-type assumptions that fail on general scenes. Additionally, metallic and dielectric surfaces exhibit fundamentally different polarization BRDFs, further degrading normal estimation when material type is unknown.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single-shot polarization capture: four polarization images at 0°, 45°, 90°, and 135°, from which S0 intensity, AoLP (encoded as cos 2ψ, sin 2ψ for continuity), and DoLP maps are derived, plus a binary object mask (generated via SAM). It outputs a per-pixel surface normal vector and a binary material label (metallic vs. dielectric) at 512×512 resolution. The object mask is the only external annotation required; all other inputs are computed directly from the polarization images.

## Method

SfPUEL is a transformer-based framework with two parallel feature extraction branches: a frozen PSPEM (ConvNeXt-T backbone from pretrained SDM-UniPS) providing photometric stereo priors, and a learnable PolFEM (same architecture, initialized from SDM-UniPS weights) encoding polarimetric cues including a lightweight polarization encoder for DoLP/AoLP. The two branches are fused at four hierarchical stages via DoLP-guided masked cross-attention blocks, where DoLP values generate spatial mask probabilities that suppress unreliable low-polarization regions. A Global Context Extractor then applies five image-level self-attention blocks (reusing SDM-UniPS's light-axis attention) followed by pixel-level cross-attention with learnable geometry queries to produce global context features. Two MLP heads predict normals (cosine similarity loss) and material segmentation (cross-entropy loss) jointly, with the material task narrowing the latent space to improve normal accuracy. Training is supervised on a 20,000-sample synthetic dataset rendered with Mitsuba2 using diverse SVBRDFs, HDR environment maps, and object meshes, with data augmentation via mesh smoothing, albedo perturbation, and roughness scaling.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset (1,000 samples with dielectric and metallic objects), SfPUEL achieves a mean angular error of 12.39° and median of 9.45°, substantially outperforming the best SfP baseline DeepSfP (20.60° mean) and the best single-image method DSINE (20.33° mean). The 30° angular accuracy reaches 92.8% for SfPUEL versus 80.1% for DSINE and 78.8% for DeepSfP. On the real-world dataset, SfPUEL achieves a mean angular error of 11.16° with 96.9% accuracy at 30°, and additionally predicts material segmentation with 97.8% accuracy on synthetic data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SfPUEL outperforms all six baselines across every metric on both synthetic and real-world data. Against the closest SfP competitor DeepSfP [4], SfPUEL reduces mean angular error from 20.60° to 12.39° (a 40% relative improvement) and raises 30° accuracy from 78.8% to 92.8%. The best single-image normal estimator DSINE [6] (20.33° mean) is also clearly surpassed, with SfPUEL recovering finer geometric details that DSINE misses. SfPW [30] (34.62°) and UNE [37.96°] perform poorly, as they were designed for scene-level or RGB-only settings respectively. On the PANDORA [15] dataset, SfPUEL using a single polarization view produces results comparable to the multi-view PANDORA method, and on the real-world dataset it faithfully recovers normals on both metallic (e.g., BIRD) and dielectric (e.g., OWL, PINEAPPLE) objects where DeepSfP and UNE fail to capture surface texture details. One-2-3-45 [32], a generative 3D reconstruction method, produces only coarse shapes and is not competitive for precise normal estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the shape-from-polarization (SfP) line of work (DeepSfP, SfPW, PANDORA) by removing the reliance on specific illumination assumptions (sunny sky, frontal flash, distant light) that prior methods required for disambiguation. Its key architectural departure is the cross-domain integration of photometric stereo (PS) priors from the pretrained SDM-UniPS model into the SfP pipeline, leveraging the observation that both tasks exploit per-pixel measurement variations to estimate normals. The DoLP-guided masked cross-attention mechanism and the joint metallic/dielectric material segmentation are novel components not present in prior SfP or single-image normal estimation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SfPUEL is the first method to handle SfP under truly unknown environment light without controlled illumination, restoring the passive-imaging advantage that prior SfP methods sacrificed for disambiguation. The demonstrated transferability of PS pretrained features to SfP opens a broader question of cross-task prior reuse in inverse rendering. However, the method still requires an object mask (via SAM), fails on depolarizing rough surfaces (fabric, sanded stone), and the synthetic training data uses unpolarized environment maps, leaving a gap for fully polarized environment-light generalization. The collected real-world dataset with metallic objects under diverse lighting is a practical resource for the community.
