## Problem

Existing dairy cattle datasets contain only one or two sensing modalities, making it impossible to systematically compare modalities or evaluate their complementary benefits for building accurate, cost-effective, and animal-friendly monitoring systems. This limits the development of ML models that could leverage the strengths of different sensors (e.g., UWB for localization, IMU for motion, vision for identification) in a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241897/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides two weeks of synchronized data across nine modalities: UWB 3D neck locations at 15 s, IMMU acceleration/magnetic data at 100 ms, pressure at 100 ms, vaginal temperature at 1 min, ankle gravity direction at 1 min, 4.8 M RGB frames at 4480×2800 from four isometric (not top-down) cameras at 1 fps, indoor temperature/RH at 1 min, outdoor weather at 5 min, and daily milk yield. Ground truth consists of 20,000 annotated frames with 213,000 bounding boxes for 16 cows, each labeled with ID, 3D location (via multi-view projection), and one of seven behavior classes at 1-second granularity. The isometric camera views make detection and identification substantially harder than the top-view images common in prior datasets.

## Method

The paper's technical contribution is a comprehensive benchmark suite demonstrating multimodal fusion for behavior classification. For wearable sensors, a Random Forest with balanced weights classifies behaviors from UWB location features, optionally augmented with head direction (derived from IMMU magnetometer) and ankle lying/non-lying state. For vision, a three-stage pipeline uses YOLOv8 for cow detection, then separate EfficientNetB0 models for behavior classification and cow identification on single-view images; a multi-view variant (RGBm) projects four camera outputs into world coordinates as 3D lines, removes outliers, and fuses predictions via weighted voting proportional to bounding-box width. A key design choice is the two-stage evaluation: an object-wise split tests cross-cow generalization while a temporal split (separating artificial vs. natural lighting) tests robustness to unseen conditions. The benchmarks show that UWB+HD+Akl achieves the best average F1 (0.737 under TS), confirming that combining location, head orientation, and leg posture yields complementary gains over any single modality.

## Results

On the MMCows behavior classification benchmark, the best-performing modality combination (UWB+HD+Akl) achieves an average F1 of 0.737 under temporal split (TS) and 0.714 under object-wise split (OS). UWB alone is competitive at 0.717 (TS), while single-view RGB (RGBs) drops to 0.554 and IMMU alone performs poorly at 0.141 (TS). Multi-view RGB fusion (RGBm) improves over single-view RGB by ~8 percentage points in average F1 (0.632 vs. 0.554), and adding head direction and ankle data to UWB yields the strongest overall performance, with lying classification reaching 0.997 F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This dataset paper does not compare against external methods from prior work; instead, it benchmarks individual sensing modalities and their combinations against each other on the same task. UWB+HD+Akl (Random Forest) is the strongest configuration, achieving 0.737 average F1 (TS), outperforming UWB alone (0.717) by ~2 points and single-view RGB (0.554) by ~18 points. IMMU alone is dramatically inferior (0.141 average F1), failing on all behaviors except lying, confirming that head-mounted inertial data alone is insufficient for fine-grained behavior discrimination. Multi-view RGB fusion (RGBm, 0.632) substantially improves over single-view RGB (RGBs, 0.554), demonstrating the value of combining four camera perspectives. The results clearly show that no single modality dominates all behaviors: UWB excels at lying (0.961–0.997) and standing, while RGB is relatively better at walking and licking, motivating the multimodal fusion approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first multimodal dataset for dairy cattle monitoring, combining nine sensing modalities (UWB, IMMU, pressure, vaginal temperature, ankle, RGB from four isometric cameras, microclimate, weather, and milk yield) that no prior cattle dataset has integrated. Existing datasets (Table 1) are overwhelmingly single-modality—typically RGB images for identification or IMU/UWB for one behavioral signal—making cross-modality comparison impossible. The benchmark methods (Random Forest, YOLOv8, EfficientNetB0) are standard off-the-shelf tools, and the visual localization via AdaGrad optimization over multi-view projection lines is a modest but practical contribution. The paper thus positions itself not as a methodological advance but as an enabling resource that repurposes well-known sensing and ML techniques into a unified, synchronized, real-world collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMCows fills a critical gap in precision livestock farming research by enabling systematic comparison of sensing modalities and their combinations for behavior classification, identification, and localization—something no prior dataset supported. The confirmed strong correlations between behavior bouts and THI validate the dataset's utility for heat-stress monitoring, a major economic and welfare concern. Key limitations include only 10 of 16 cows being sensor-tagged, a single day of full annotations, and isometric (rather than top-down) camera views that increase detection difficulty. The vast space of unexplored modality combinations and the potential for developing lower-cost, more animal-friendly monitoring systems represent the main open questions the dataset is designed to address.
