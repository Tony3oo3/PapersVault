## Problem

Existing street-view datasets (KITTI, Waymo, nuScenes) provide only noisy LiDAR points as geometric ground truth, which lack the precision for accurate surface position evaluation and entirely lack surface normal information. This makes it impossible to properly benchmark surface reconstruction methods, since meshes with poor normal quality can appear geometrically valid by point-wise distance metrics alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243385/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of the 13,535 frames across 28 sequences (ranging from <300 to 1000 frames at 10 FPS) contains 6 RGB images (1920×1080, 110° FOV), 5 LiDAR point clouds (32 channels, σ_noise=0.1), 6 depth maps, and 6 semantic segmentation maps. The ground truth is precise triangle mesh models with per-point surface normals, enabling both position-based (F-score, CD) and normal-based (CD_N) evaluation. Sensor poses are provided in OpenCV coordinates, and evaluation uses a 0.05m voxel resampling with a 25m bounding-box crop around camera trajectories.

## Method

The core contribution is a CARLA plugin that exports high-fidelity 3D triangle meshes from the Unreal Engine and aligns them with the sensor coordinate system, providing dense geometric ground truth with surface normals that LiDAR points fundamentally cannot offer. A virtual car equipped with 6 RGB cameras and 5 LiDAR sensors (matching Waymo/nuScenes specifications) follows planned trajectories through 8 towns, capturing diverse structures including buildings, overpasses, fences, and poles. The benchmark evaluates five representative methods spanning multi-view stereo (R3D3), LiDAR mapping (NeRF-LOAM), NeRF (UrbanNeRF), NeuralSDF (StreetSurf in RGB/LiDAR/Full modes), and 3D Gaussians (SuGaR), using a metric suite that includes Normal Chamfer Distance (CD_N) computed via cosine distance between surface normals of nearest-neighbor point pairs. The evaluation protocol filters occluded triangle faces before uniform area-proportional oversampling to 10.24M points, then voxel-resamples at 0.05m for fair per-face assessment.

## Results

On the SS3DM benchmark, StreetSurf (Full) achieves the best combined geometric quality with a mean CD+CDN of 1.524 m and the highest IoU of 0.116, while StreetSurf (LiDAR) attains the highest F-score (0.215) and Precision (0.206) at the 0.05 m threshold. NeRF-LOAM achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance (0.551 m) and best Accuracy (0.151), but its high Normal Chamfer Distance (1.411) reveals significant surface-normal errors. All methods degrade substantially on longer sequences, with no method exceeding an F-score of 0.1 on the long-sequence subset, underscoring the difficulty of large-scale street-view surface reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreetSurf (Full) achieves the best overall reconstruction quality, leading in IoU (0.116), Normal Chamfer Distance (0.955), and the combined CD+CDN metric (1.524) across all sequence lengths, outperforming StreetSurf (LiDAR) by 0.177 in CD+CDN and NeRF-LOAM by 0.438. However, StreetSurf (LiDAR) surpasses StreetSurf (Full) in F-score (0.215 vs. 0.198) and Precision (0.206 vs. 0.196), indicating better point-wise distance accuracy despite inferior surface-normal quality. NeRF-LOAM achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance (0.551 m) and best Accuracy (0.151), but its high CDN (1.411) reveals noisy, geometrically invalid surfaces that distance-only metrics fail to capture. R3D3 and SuGaR perform worst overall, with R3D3 producing highly erroneous depth maps (CD ≈ 1.82 m) and SuGaR generating bubble-like artifacts on road surfaces. UrbanNeRF produces over-smoothed meshes due to MLP limitations, yielding moderate CDN but poor detail capture. The authors note that F-score and CD alone can be misleading—StreetSurf (LiDAR) and NeRF-LOAM appear competitive on these metrics but produce visibly worse surfaces than StreetSurf (Full) in qualitative comparisons, highlighting the necessity of the normal-based evaluation enabled by SS3DM's mesh ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not extend a specific reconstruction method but rather addresses a critical gap in the evaluation infrastructure for street-view surface reconstruction. Prior street-view datasets (KITTI, Waymo, nuScenes) provide only noisy LiDAR points as geometric ground truth, which lack the precision for position evaluation and entirely omit surface normals. The paper carves new ground by exporting precise triangle meshes from the CARLA simulator, enabling both position and normal evaluation for the first time in this domain. It then benchmarks methods spanning the full methodological spectrum—multi-view stereo (R3D3), LiDAR mapping (NeRF-LOAM), NeRF (UrbanNeRF), NeuralSDF (StreetSurf), and 3D Gaussians (SuGaR)—under a unified protocol with mesh-based metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that no evaluated method achieves an F-score above 0.1 on long sequences, quantifying just how far street-view surface reconstruction is from being solved. A key insight is that Normal Chamfer Distance (CD_N) better reflects true reconstruction quality than distance-only metrics (CD, F-score), since methods like NeRF-LOAM can achieve low CD while producing noisy, ill-shaped surfaces. The paper identifies concrete open directions—adaptive efficient representations, split-and-merge strategies, and multi-stage coarse-to-fine pipelines—but acknowledges limitations in scene diversity and the absence of dynamic objects. As the first dataset providing mesh ground truth for this task, SS3DM is likely to become the standard evaluation benchmark for future street-view reconstruction methods.
