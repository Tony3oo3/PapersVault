## Problem

While no-regret learning in potential games (aligned interests) is well-understood, the long-run behavior of learning dynamics in harmonic games—the strategic complement of potential games where interests are anti-aligned—remained almost entirely unknown outside the narrow subclass of 2-player zero-sum games with fully-mixed equilibria. This paper asks: what is the behavior of FTRL and its variants in the full class of (generalized) N-player harmonic games, and can one recover both constant regret and Nash equilibrium convergence?

## Input / Output

The method operates on finite normal-form games admitting a harmonic measure μ (satisfying the weighted payoff-difference cancellation condition), with players receiving full payoff vector feedback v_i(x_n) at each iteration. Per-player parameters include learning rates η_i, strongly convex regularizers h_i, and extrapolation coefficients λ_i ∈ [0,1]; the algorithm produces a sequence of mixed strategy profiles x_n converging to the Nash equilibrium set with O(1) individual regret. The framework unifies a continuous-time FTRL model (for Poincaré recurrence analysis) and a discrete-time FTRL+ model (for convergence), where the extrapolation step consumes either one or two payoff queries per iteration depending on the chosen variant.

## Method

The paper introduces extrapolated FTRL (FTRL+), a two-phase template in which players first generate a leading state x_{n+1/2} using a convex combination of current and previous payoffs (v̂_{i,n} = λ_i v_i(x_n) + (1−λ_i) v_i(x_{n−1/2})), then update the base state using payoff information from the leading state; this nests optimistic FTRL (λ_i=0), extra-step/mirror-prox FTRL (λ_i=1), and vanilla FTRL as special cases. The convergence proof hinges on a Fenchel-coupling Lyapunov function E(p,y) = Σ_i (m_i/η_i) F_i(p_i, y_i) and a 'template inequality' that yields summability of step sizes, combined with the strategic-center characterization of harmonic games (the payoff vector v(x) is m-weighted-perpendicular to x−q for all x). For the continuous-time result, the key insight is that FTRL orbits form a volume-preserving flow in payoff space, and the harmonic structure yields a constant of motion (e.g., G(x) = Π_i Π_α x_{iα}^{μ_{iα}} for the logit map), establishing Poincaré recurrence and hence non-convergence of vanilla FTRL-D.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical guarantees for no-regret learning in harmonic games. In continuous time, all FTRL dynamics are Poincaré recurrent (Theorem 2): almost every orbit returns arbitrarily close to its starting point infinitely often, so learning fails to converge. In discrete time, the proposed extrapolated FTRL (FTRL+) achieves order-optimal O(1) individual regret (Theorem 3), bounded by Reg_i(T) ≤ H_i/η_i + (2G_i/(N+2)) Σ_j H_j/(η_j G_j), under the learning rate condition η_i ≤ m_i K_i [2(N+2) max_j m_j G_j]^{-1}. Furthermore, FTRL+ converges to the set of Nash equilibria from any initial condition (Theorem 4), a result that extends prior convergence guarantees from 2-player zero-sum games to general N-player harmonic games with non-convex equilibrium sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between vanilla FTRL, continuous-time FTRL (FTRL-D), and the proposed FTRL+ in harmonic games. Vanilla FTRL diverges away from equilibrium in harmonic games (e.g., in Matching Pennies, the distance from equilibrium grows geometrically as D_{n+1} = (1+16η²)D_n until the iterates are trapped in a non-terminating best-response cycle). Continuous-time FTRL is Poincaré recurrent but non-convergent, so players effectively 'learn nothing' despite incurring at most constant regret. FTRL+ (which subsumes optimistic FTRL, extra-step FTRL, mirror-prox, and dual extrapolation as special cases) is the only variant that both achieves O(1) regret and converges to Nash equilibrium. Compared to prior work, Theorem 2 generalizes Legacci et al. [35] (replicator dynamics in uniform harmonic games) to all FTRL dynamics and all harmonic games, while Theorems 3–4 extend the constant-regret and convergence results known for 2-player zero-sum games [27, 42, 62] to the full class of N-player harmonic games, where the convex-concave min-max structure is absent and the equilibrium set is non-convex.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the no-regret learning analysis from the narrow setting of 2-player zero-sum games with fully-mixed equilibria to the full class of N-player harmonic games, which Candogan et al. [6] and Abdou et al. [1] established as the strategic complement of potential games. The continuous-time Poincaré recurrence result generalizes Legacci et al. [35] (replicator dynamics in uniform harmonic games) to all FTRL dynamics and all harmonic games, while the discrete-time FTRL+ template unifies the optimistic, extra-gradient, and mirror-prox variants under a single extrapolation framework inspired by Azizian et al. [3] and Korpelevich [32]. The paper positions harmonic games as the natural N-player generalization of 2-player zero-sum games from a learning viewpoint, since the zero-sum property loses meaning beyond two players.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The convergence of FTRL+ to Nash equilibrium in harmonic games is a significant theoretical advance because the equilibrium set is non-convex and no convex-concave structure is available, making the result genuinely non-trivial. The O(1) regret guarantee (versus polylogarithmic bounds in general games) and the Poincaré recurrence characterization together establish harmonic games as the dynamic complement of potential games, completing a strategic picture that was previously only half-understood. Open questions include the convergence rate of FTRL+ (conjectured linear but unproven), extension to bandit/counterfactual feedback settings, and development of adaptive step-size variants that avoid pre-play tuning of game parameters.
