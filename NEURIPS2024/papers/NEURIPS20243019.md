## Problem

Safe Bayesian Optimization (SAFEOPT) assumes stationary safety constraints, so it cannot detect when previously safe decisions become unsafe in time-varying systems. Event-triggered alternatives (ETSAFEOPT) rely on explicit change detection that is inherently insensitive to continuous, gradual drift. The paper addresses the gap of safely tracking a time-varying safe region without requiring explicit change detection, while providing both safety guarantees in the general non-stationary case and near-optimality guarantees once the problem becomes locally stationary.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a finite (quantized) decision space X and receives one noisy evaluation per time step for the reward and all m safety constraints. A key input is the sequence of temporal Lipschitz constants {L(t)} that bound how fast functions can change between consecutive time steps, alongside a spatial Lipschitz constant L_x; in practice, a Lipschitz-constant-free variant is provided in the appendix. The safe set S_k is allowed to shrink at each iteration (unlike SAFEOPT), and if it becomes empty the algorithm terminates, reflecting that the true safe region may have vanished.

## Method

TVSAFEOPT models the reward and safety functions with a single auxiliary GP using a spatio-temporal kernel that encodes temporal continuity. At each iteration it constructs a recursive confidence interval C_k(x,i) by taking the Minkowski sum of the previous interval with [-L(t-1), L(t-1)] and intersecting with the current GP confidence interval Q_k, thereby propagating temporal uncertainty forward. The safe set is updated by requiring the lower bound l_k(x',i) minus spatial Lipschitz uncertainty L_x·d(x,x') minus the temporal bound L(t) to be non-negative, which explicitly subtracts a safety margin for potential temporal change. The next decision is selected by maximizing the interval width w_k among the union of potential maximizers and expanders, balancing exploitation and safe-set growth. The paper proves that with probability at least 1-δ all decisions in S_k satisfy the true safety constraints (Theorem 2.5), and that when the problem becomes stationary the algorithm finds an ε-near-optimal solution within a bounded number of iterations (Theorem 2.6).

## Results

On the synthetic 2D time-varying problem, TVSAFEOPT reduces violations by 99.99%±0.01% relative to SAFEOPT, while ETSAFEOPT achieves only 84.4%±1.7% reduction. TVSAFEOPT covers 21.0%±1.3% less of the ground-truth safe region than SAFEOPT (vs. 30.9%±2.9% for ETSAFEOPT) and achieves a 66.9%±14.4% reduction in cumulative regret. On the realistic gas compressor case study, TVSAFEOPT reduces violations by 96.8%±1.0% relative to approximate optimization (vs. 89.2%±4.2% for SAFEOPT), at the cost of covering 61.0%±1.3% less of the safe region and incurring 178.3%±29.2% higher cumulative regret.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAFEOPT, TVSAFEOPT dramatically outperforms on safety: it eliminates virtually all violations (−99.99% in the synthetic example, −96.8% in the compressor study) whereas SAFEOPT still retains a substantial fraction of unsafe decisions in its safe set. The price is conservatism: TVSAFEOPT covers less of the ground-truth safe region (−21.0% synthetic, −61.0% compressor) and, in the compressor study, incurs 42.1% higher cumulative regret than SAFEOPT, reflecting the safety–optimality trade-off under strong non-stationarity. Against ETSAFEOPT (event-triggered SAFEOPT), TVSAFEOPT achieves strictly fewer violations (−99.99% vs. −84.4% in the synthetic example) and better coverage (−21.0% vs. −30.9%), while its cumulative regret is only marginally higher (−66.9% vs. −73.6%). ETSAFEOPT is excluded from the compressor comparison because the continuous, small-magnitude changes in demand, head, and degradation cause its event trigger to fire repeatedly, collapsing the safe set to empty. Overall, TVSAFEOPT is the only method that simultaneously provides a formal safety guarantee in the general time-varying setting and a near-optimality guarantee under local stationarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

TVSAFEOPT extends SAFEOPT (Sui et al., 2015) and its contextual variant (Berkenkamp et al., 2021) by incorporating a spatio-temporal GP kernel and time Lipschitz constants to handle time-varying reward and safety functions. Prior safe BO methods either ignore temporal changes (SAFEOPT, C-SAFEOPT) or rely on event-detection triggers that fail for continuous drift (ETSAFEOPT). The key conceptual departure is using the Minkowski sum to recursively tighten confidence intervals over time, allowing the safe set to shrink adaptively without explicit change detection, while the Lipschitz constants provide explicit bounds on how fast the problem can change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first safe BO algorithm with a formal safety guarantee in the general time-varying setting plus a near-optimality guarantee when the problem becomes locally stationary, filling a gap identified in the literature. The main limitation is the strong safety-optimality trade-off: in the compressor case study, TVSAFEOPT achieves 96.8% fewer violations than approximate optimization but at 178.3% higher cumulative regret, and the local stationarity assumption is required for optimality guarantees. Open questions include theoretical near-optimality in fully non-stationary settings, practical estimation of Lipschitz constants (for which a Lipschitz-free variant is sketched), and whether the conservative safe-set shrinking can be tightened without sacrificing the safety guarantee.
