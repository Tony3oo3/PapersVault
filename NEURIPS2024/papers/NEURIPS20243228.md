## Problem

Existing deformable 3D Gaussian Splatting methods for dynamic scene reconstruction rely solely on photometric (appearance) loss to supervise Gaussian deformation, lacking explicit motion constraints. This causes optimization difficulties and performance degradation when object motion is irregular or sudden. Additionally, COLMAP-estimated camera poses in dynamic scenes are often inaccurate due to the lack of geometric consistency from moving objects, further degrading reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243228/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video sequence and COLMAP-estimated camera poses as input, computing forward optical flow between adjacent frames with GMFlow and rendering depth maps directly from the 3D Gaussians via the rasterizer (rather than using a separate monocular depth estimator). It produces a deformable 3D Gaussian representation (canonical Gaussians plus an MLP-based deformation field), enabling real-time novel-view synthesis at arbitrary timestamps, along with refined camera poses. Training uses 480×270 resolution on NeRF-DS and 2× downsampled 536×960 on HyperNeRF, with 20,000 iterations on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

MotionGS introduces two key modules. First, an optical flow decoupling module separates 2D optical flow into camera flow (computed by reprojecting depth-warped points under camera ego-motion) and motion flow (the residual capturing only object motion), then uses the motion flow to directly supervise a differentiable Gaussian flow rendered in the CUDA rasterizer via an L1 loss. Second, a camera pose refinement module alternately optimizes 3D Gaussians and camera poses by introducing a learnable SE(3) residual to the relative pose between adjacent frames, backpropagating photometric gradients to poses while freezing Gaussian attributes. The overall loss combines the baseline photometric loss with the flow loss, and the entire pipeline is end-to-end differentiable through the integrated rasterizer.

## Results

On the NeRF-DS dataset (7 scenes), MotionGS achieves a mean PSNR of 24.54, SSIM of 0.8656, and LPIPS of 0.1719, outperforming the direct baseline Deformable-3DGS (23.61 PSNR) by ~0.93 dB and the best NeRF-based method NeRF-DS (23.60 PSNR) by ~0.94 dB. On the HyperNeRF vrig dataset (4 scenes), MotionGS reaches a mean PSNR of 24.8 and SSIM of 0.69, surpassing TiNeuVox (24.2 PSNR) and Deformable 3DGS (22.5 PSNR). The method achieves real-time rendering at 19–50 FPS across NeRF-DS scenes with 178K–379K Gaussians.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionGS outperforms all five baselines on both evaluation datasets. Against its direct baseline Deformable-3DGS [17], it gains +0.93 dB PSNR and +0.026 SSIM on NeRF-DS mean, with the largest per-scene improvement on the Plate scene (+2.19 dB PSNR, from 18.82 to 21.01). Against the NeRF-based NeRF-DS [73], it improves by +0.94 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.1816 to 0.1719. On HyperNeRF, the advantage over Deformable 3DGS is even more pronounced (+2.3 dB PSNR, from 22.5 to 24.8), and it surpasses TiNeuVox [59] by +0.6 dB PSNR. The static 3D-GS [10] baseline is far behind (20.29 PSNR), confirming the necessity of explicit deformation modeling. Notably, the ablation (Table 3) shows that using raw optical flow instead of the decoupled motion flow actually degrades performance below the baseline (23.37 vs 23.61 PSNR), validating the core design choice of flow decoupling.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionGS builds directly on Deformable 3DGS [17], which uses an MLP deformation field to animate canonical 3D Gaussians over time, and positions itself within the broader family of 3DGS-based dynamic scene reconstruction methods (D-3DGS, 4DGS, SC-GS). Its key conceptual departure is the introduction of explicit motion supervision: rather than relying solely on photometric consistency to constrain Gaussian deformation, it decouples 2D optical flow into camera flow and motion flow, using only the latter to supervise a differentiable 'Gaussian flow' rendered through the CUDA rasterizer. This draws inspiration from GaussianFlow [21] and Motion-aware 3DGS [22], but differs by matching Gaussian flow to motion flow (fixed camera) rather than to full optical flow (moving camera). The camera pose refinement module extends pose-free optimization ideas from static-scene methods (i-NeRF, CF-3DGS) into the dynamic setting via alternating optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit motion guidance addresses a genuine gap in deformable 3DGS methods, which previously had no direct constraint on how Gaussians should move, making them vulnerable to local optima under irregular motion. The camera pose refinement is practically important for real-world monocular capture where COLMAP poses are unreliable. However, the method still depends on sufficient static scene features for pose initialization, on the quality of off-the-shelf optical flow estimation, and on rendered depth for camera flow computation—limitations that cause visible failure on sparse-viewpoint datasets like DyNeRF. The authors note that the improvements are agnostic to specific deformation network designs, suggesting broad applicability, but a fully pose-free dynamic 3DGS remains an open challenge.
