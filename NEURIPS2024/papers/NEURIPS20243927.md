## Problem

Existing subject-driven text-to-image generation methods like DreamBooth require ~1000 negative samples, 1000 gradient steps, and fine-tuning of the text encoder, making them expensive and prone to overfitting reference images. SuTI demands half a million expert models, and Textual Inversion suffers from limited expressiveness. The paper addresses the need for a simpler, faster fine-tuning setup that avoids overfitting while maintaining both subject fidelity and prompt faithfulness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243927/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of reference images (a few photos of a specific subject) and 8 novel training prompts of the form 'a [V] [class noun] [modification]' (e.g., 'a [V] dog is on the Moon'), from which only 32 images are generated by the base model (vs. ~1000 for DreamBooth). The fine-tuned U-Net then produces images that preserve the subject's visual identity while faithfully rendering the text prompt, evaluated on DreamBench's 30 subjects × 25 prompt templates. Only the U-Net component is updated; the text encoder and text embeddings remain frozen throughout training.

## Method

The core contribution is the λ-Harmonic reward function, a weighted harmonic mean of ALIGN-I (image-to-reference similarity) and ALIGN-T (image-to-text alignment) scores, which serves a dual role: it generates preference labels via the Bradley-Terry model and enables early stopping during validation to prevent overfitting. Training minimizes a combined objective of a similarity loss (standard denoising MSE on reference images) and a DPO-style preference loss (binary cross-entropy over sampled preference pairs), requiring only 400 gradient steps on a single TPUv4-8. The harmonic mean is chosen over the arithmetic mean because it is more sensitive to the weaker of the two scores, ensuring both fidelity and faithfulness are jointly optimized. During training, λ_train=0 focuses the reward on text alignment (since image similarity is already handled by L_sim), while λ_val=0.3 balances both signals for model selection via early stopping.

## Results

On DreamBench, RPO achieves a CLIP-I score of 0.833 and a CLIP-T score of 0.314, both state-of-the-art among all compared methods, while requiring only 400 gradient steps and 32 generated negative samples (3% of DreamBooth's 1000). The DINO score is 0.652, slightly below DreamBooth (0.668) and SuTI (0.741) on the same backbone, which the authors attribute to the λ-Harmonic reward favoring semantic alignment over pixel-level fidelity. The full fine-tuning pipeline (generation, training, validation, model saving) completes in 5–20 minutes on a single TPUv4-8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPO outperforms all ten baselines on CLIP-I (0.833 vs. next-best SSR-Encoder at 0.821) and ties for the best CLIP-T (0.314, matching SSR-Encoder), while using only 400 gradient steps compared to 10⁶ for SSR-Encoder and IP-Adapter. Against DreamBooth on the same Stable Diffusion backbone, RPO improves CLIP-I by 3 percentage points (0.833 vs. 0.803) and CLIP-T by 0.9 points (0.314 vs. 0.305) while requiring only 32 negative samples versus DreamBooth's 1000. On DINO, RPO (0.652) trails DreamBooth-SD (0.668) and SuTI-Imagen (0.741), which the authors explain as a trade-off: the harmonic-mean reward prioritizes semantic text-image alignment at the cost of some pixel-space uniqueness. Compared to the DPO-only variant (Table 7), RPO's added similarity loss raises DINO from 0.338 to 0.649, confirming that the reference-image loss is essential for preserving subject identity. RPO also matches or exceeds Custom Diffusion (500 steps) on CLIP-I and CLIP-T while using fewer iterations, and surpasses Textual Inversion, DisenBooth, and ELITE on all three metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) framework from LLMs (Rafailov et al.) and its diffusion-model extension (Diffusion-DPO, Wallace et al.) to the subject-driven text-to-image generation task, but departs from both by eliminating the need for human-collected preference labels. Instead, it introduces a λ-Harmonic reward function built on ALIGN image-text similarity scores, which automatically generates preference labels via the Bradley-Terry model and simultaneously serves as an early-stopping criterion. Unlike DreamBooth (which requires ~1000 negative samples, 1000 gradient steps, and text-encoder fine-tuning) or SuTI (which requires half a million expert models), RPO fine-tunes only the U-Net with 32 generated images and 400 gradient steps, positioning itself as a lightweight alternative within the preference-based fine-tuning lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gain is substantial for practitioners: 5–20 minutes of fine-tuning on a single TPUv4 versus 30–70 minutes for competing methods, with only 3% of DreamBooth's negative samples. The λ-Harmonic reward as a model-selection signal is a reusable idea that could transfer to other reward-guided diffusion fine-tuning settings. However, evaluation is limited to DreamBench (30 subjects) and a single backbone (Stable Diffusion), and the paper acknowledges persistent failure modes including context-appearance entanglement and residual overfitting. Open questions include whether online RL could further improve regularization, whether a LoRA variant would match full fine-tuning, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the relatively small and curated DreamBench benchmark.
