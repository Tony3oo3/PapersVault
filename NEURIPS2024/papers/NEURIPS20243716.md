## Problem

While representation learning has proven critical for RL success in vision and control, it remains largely unexplored for reward models in language model alignment. Standard RLHF reward models are trained only with a sequence-level preference ranking loss, leaving their internal representations unoptimized for capturing whether an intermediate step is on track toward a desired outcome. This paper asks whether explicitly learning goal-conditioned representations in reward models can improve both reward scoring accuracy and enable new capabilities like error localization and output steering.

## Input / Output

The reward model takes a text prompt and a completion sequence as input, processed by a decoder-only transformer (CodeLlama 7B or Llama 3-8B-Instruct) with an added scalar reward head. During training, it additionally consumes sampled source state-action pairs and goal states drawn from preferred and dispreferred completions in the same batch. At inference, the model produces a scalar reward score per completion, per-token Q-values (cosine similarity between each hidden state and a goal-state prototype), which are then used to filter generations in majority voting or to score beams during guided decoding for steered text output.

## Method

The reward model is decomposed into a feature extractor φ (the LM decoder producing per-token hidden states) and a reward projection head r'. A contrastive goal-conditioned loss is applied to φ's hidden representations: for each training pair, a positive source-goal pair is sampled from the preferred completion (two indices i<j) and a negative source-goal pair is sampled with the source from the preferred and the goal from the dispreferred completion. The loss maximizes cosine similarity for the positive pair and minimizes it for the negative pair, effectively making the hidden states approximate a Q-function that encodes the likelihood of reaching a goal state from any intermediate step. This contrastive objective is jointly optimized with the standard preference ranking loss via a weighted sum (λ=0.5). At inference, Q-values are computed as cosine similarity between intermediate hidden states and a goal-state prototype (mean representation of all preferred completions), enabling token-level error detection, generation filtering (discarding sequences with negative Q-values saves up to 55% of tokens), and guided decoding that steers Llama 3 outputs toward desired attributes like helpfulness or complexity.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning, the Q-Function reward model improves AUROC by up to 0.09 on MATH (ID) and ~0.08 on svamp (OOD) over the standard preference-ranking baseline. When used in PPO training, the Q-Function reward model yields a policy achieving 80.5% on GSM8k, 45.1% on MATH, and 70.9% on algebra222, with an average weighted gain of 1.7% over the baseline PPO. On natural language alignment, the Q-Function reward model improves Helpful-Harmless accuracy from 68.2% to 70.5%, and guided decoding with the learned representations boosts helpfulness by 9.6% and complexity by 21.6% over the SFT baseline on HelpSteer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed Q-Function reward model against two baselines: (1) the standard preference-ranking reward model used in PPO (Codellama PPO), and (2) DPO as a strong direct-alignment baseline. Against DPO, Q-Function PPO wins on all five OOD benchmarks (e.g., 70.9% vs 64.8% on algebra222, 81.2% vs 76.6% on svamp) and is competitive on ID tasks (80.5% vs 80.1% on GSM8k, 45.1% vs 44.8% on MATH). Against the standard preference-ranking PPO, Q-Function PPO improves on every benchmark, with the largest gains on OOD sets (e.g., +5.1 points on algebra222, +2.7 on svamp). The authors note that DPO performs on par with their method on in-distribution tasks but degrades more sharply out-of-distribution, consistent with prior analyses of DPO's OOD limitations. On the natural language steering task, the Q-Function prototype decoding method outperforms the SFT-guided decoding baseline across all four HelpSteer categories (helpfulness 54.8% vs 45.2%, correctness 56.1% vs 43.9%, coherence 58.3% vs 41.8%, complexity 60.8% vs 39.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly adapts the contrastive Q-function approximation objective from Eysenbach et al. (2022), which showed that increasing representation similarity along same-trajectory state pairs approximates a goal-conditioned Q-function in robotics, and transfers it to the language reward model setting. It positions itself at the intersection of goal-conditioned RL (Andrychowicz et al. 2017; Chane-Sane et al. 2021) and RLHF reward modeling (Ouyang et al. 2022; Bai et al. 2022), noting that while fine-grained reward methods (Lightman et al. 2023; Wu et al. 2023) require costly token-level annotations, the proposed contrastive objective achieves dense per-token signals using only standard sequence-level preference labels. The method departs from prior representation-learning-for-RL work by showing that the learned representations are not merely an internal byproduct but can be directly exploited at inference time for Q-value computation, generation filtering, and guided decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single contrastive loss on reward model hidden states yields two practically useful capabilities simultaneously: improved reward scoring (up to 0.09 AUROC on math, 2.3% accuracy on Helpful-Harmless) and a usable Q-function for steering and filtering (55% token savings in majority voting, 21.6% complexity gain in guided decoding). Key open questions include whether on-policy iterative retraining of the reward model closes the gap with DPO on in-distribution tasks, how to derive more precise inference-time goal states beyond the mean-of-preferred-completions heuristic, and whether the correlation between partial Q-values and reward scores (noted in Appendix H) poses a reward-hacking risk under extended PPO training. The method's simplicity—requiring no additional annotations beyond standard preference pairs—makes it broadly applicable to any RLHF pipeline.
