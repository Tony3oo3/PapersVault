## Problem

Most active learning theory provides uniform (minimax) guarantees that only capture the upper envelope of learning curves across distributions, failing to characterize the full spectrum of distribution-dependent rates achievable by a single algorithm. This paper resolves an open question from Balcan et al. (2010) by giving a complete tetrachotomic characterization of the optimal universal learning rates in active learning, identifying exactly which combinatorial properties of a concept class determine whether rates are arbitrarily fast, exponential, sublinear, or arbitrarily slow.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives an infinite stream of unlabeled points drawn i.i.d. from P_X over a Polish space X, along with a label query budget n. Crucially, the number of unlabeled points u(n) must be specified non-adaptively before execution, though label queries themselves can be adaptive (depending on previous answers). The output is a binary classifier h: X → {0,1} whose expected misclassification probability under P decays at a rate determined by the combinatorial structure of the concept class H.

## Method

The characterization hinges on three combinatorial tree structures: the Littlestone tree, a newly introduced star tree, and the VCL tree. For exponential rates, the paper designs a Gale-Stewart game whose learner's winning strategy (when no infinite star tree exists) induces partial concept classes with bounded star number, which are then actively learned via an extension of Hanneke and Yang's (2015) algorithm to partial classes. For sublinear rates, the VCL game is run on √n batches to produce √n partial concept classes, and a single active learning algorithm—modified from Hanneke (2012) to handle partial classes—makes query decisions by voting across these classes, estimating VC-shattering probabilities to decide whether to query or infer each point's label. This avoids the query-budget explosion that would result from running separate active learners per partial class.

## Results

The paper establishes a complete tetrachotomy (Theorem 1.5) of optimal active learning rates: (1) arbitrarily fast rates when H has no infinite Littlestone tree, (2) exponential rate e^{-n} when H has an infinite Littlestone tree but no infinite star tree, (3) o(1/n) rates (but no faster than 1/n) when H has an infinite star tree but no infinite VCL tree, and (4) arbitrarily slow rates when H has an infinite VCL tree. Theorem 1.6 provides the exact combinatorial characterization via three tree-based complexity measures (Littlestone tree, star tree, VCL tree). A key byproduct is an active learning algorithm for partial concept classes achieving exponential uniform rates (Theorem 3.2: E[er(h_n)] ≤ c₁·d·e^{-c₂n/s}).

**Table: Tetrachotomy of optimal active learning rates (Theorem 1.5–1.6).**

| Case | Combinatorial Condition | Optimal Rate | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No infinite Littlestone tree | Arbitrarily fast | Thresholds on ℤ, positive halfspaces on ℕᵈ |
| 2 | Infinite Littlestone tree, no infinite star tree | e^{-n} | Interval classifiers [a,b] on ℝ |
| 3 | Infinite star tree, no infinite VCL tree | o(1/n), but ≥ 1/n | Halfspaces on ℝᵈ |
| 4 | Infinite VCL tree | Arbitrarily slow | All classifiers {0,1}^𝒳 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against the supervised (passive) learning characterization of Bousquet et al. (2021), which yields only three cases: exponential (no infinite Littlestone tree), linear 1/n (infinite Littlestone tree, no infinite VCL tree), and arbitrarily slow (infinite VCL tree). Active learning strictly improves the rate in two of the three passive cases: the linear 1/n case splits into exponential e^{-n} (no infinite star tree) and sublinear o(1/n) (infinite star tree), while the arbitrarily slow case remains unchanged. The second comparison is against the general interactive learning setting of Hanneke et al. (2022), which also has three cases (arbitrarily fast, exponential, arbitrarily slow). The active learning setting is strictly richer: the absence of an infinite VCL tree does not guarantee exponential rates when only label queries are available, because the star tree introduces an intermediate sublinear regime. The paper also resolves an open question from Balcan et al. (2010) by giving necessary and sufficient conditions for exponential active learning rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal learning rates framework of Bousquet et al. (2021) from passive supervised learning to the active learning setting, where the learner can submit targeted label queries. It builds directly on the interactive learning characterization of Hanneke et al. (2022), which allows arbitrary binary queries, by restricting to label queries only and showing this restriction produces a strictly richer rate landscape. The exponential-rates algorithm extends Hanneke and Yang's (2015) star-number-based approach to partial concept classes, while the sublinear-rates algorithm adapts Hanneke's (2012) activized learning to partial classes via a novel VCL-game-based construction. The paper introduces the star tree as a new combinatorial measure that separates the exponential and sublinear cases, resolving an open question from Balcan et al. (2010).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The complete four-way tetrachotomy (arbitrarily fast, exponential, sublinear, arbitrarily slow) reveals that active learning occupies a unique position between passive learning and general interactive learning, with a rate landscape that is strictly richer than either. The stark contrast between uniform and universal rates for simple classes like interval classifiers (uniform 1/n vs. universal e^{-n}) highlights how distribution-dependent analysis can dramatically change the picture. An open question remains whether the characterization still holds when the learner knows the marginal distribution P_X, and the paper's algorithms require specifying the number of unlabeled examples non-adaptively, which may be restrictive in some practical settings.
