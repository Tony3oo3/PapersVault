## Problem

Prior dataset condensation methods suffer from suboptimal design choices: bi-level optimization methods (MTT, DREAM, TESLA) are computationally prohibitive at scale, while uni-level methods (SRe²L, G-VBSM) and training-free methods (RDED) operate in restricted design spaces that leave significant performance on the table. The paper identifies that simple but critical techniques—smoothing learning rate schedules, small batch sizes, and real image initialization—were either absent or unexplained in prior work, with RDED alone gaining 16.2% accuracy from these omissions rather than from its synthesis approach.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an original RGB image dataset (e.g., ImageNet-1k at 224×224, CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32) with class labels, along with a set of pre-trained observer models (e.g., ResNet-18, MobileNet-V2, ShuffleNet-V2, AlexNet) used for both statistical matching during synthesis and soft label generation. The output is a condensed dataset of IPC×C synthetic images paired with soft labels obtained by averaging softmax predictions across all observer models, compressed to ratios as low as 0.78% on ImageNet-1k at IPC 10.

## Method

EDC is a uni-level optimization framework that synthesizes condensed images via a hybrid soft category-aware matching loss combining global (Form 1) and per-class (Form 2) statistical matching with a tunable weight α, grounded in Gaussian Mixture Model theory. Key design choices include: (1) initializing synthetic images from a training-free condensed dataset (RDED) rather than Gaussian noise, justified by optimal transport cost bounds; (2) applying flatness regularization via KL divergence between softmax outputs of current and EMA-updated synthetic images to flatten the loss landscape without doubling computation; (3) using a smoothed cosine LR schedule with deceleration factor ζ=2 and small batch sizes during post-evaluation to prevent under-convergence on the limited synthetic samples; and (4) EMA-based evaluation to stabilize test-time performance. The framework requires only half the synthesis iterations of the G-VBSM baseline while achieving 48.6% on ImageNet-1k with ResNet-18 at IPC 10.

## Results

EDC achieves 48.6% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k with ResNet-18 at IPC 10 (0.78% compression ratio), surpassing SRe²L (21.3%), G-VBSM (31.4%), and RDED (42.0%) by margins of 27.3%, 17.2%, and 6.6% respectively. On CIFAR-10 at IPC 10, EDC reaches 79.1% with ResNet-18, compared to 53.5% (G-VBSM) and 37.1% (RDED). Cross-architecture generalization on ImageNet-1k (IPC 10) shows EDC outperforming RDED by 3.4–10.6% across ResNet-50/101, MobileNet-V2, EfficientNet-B0, DeiT-Tiny, Swin-Tiny, ConvNext-Tiny, and ShuffleNet-V2. EDC also uses only half the computational cost of the G-VBSM baseline for data synthesis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDC consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset, IPC, and architecture tested. Against SRe²L (Yin et al., NeurIPS 2023), EDC gains 27.3% on ImageNet-1k IPC 10 (48.6% vs 21.3%) and 51.9% on CIFAR-10 IPC 10 (79.1% vs 27.2%), reflecting SRe²L's reliance on coarse-grained global statistical matching without category awareness. Against G-VBSM (Shao et al., 2023), EDC improves by 17.2% on ImageNet-1k IPC 10 (48.6% vs 31.4%) and 25.6% on CIFAR-10 IPC 10 (79.1% vs 53.5%), with the gap driven by real image initialization, soft category-aware matching, flatness regularization, and smoothing LR schedule. Against RDED (Sun et al., CVPR 2024), the closest competitor, EDC leads by 6.6% on ImageNet-1k IPC 10 (48.6% vs 42.0%) and 41.0% on CIFAR-10 IPC 10 (79.1% vs 37.1%); notably, the paper shows that 16.2% of RDED's own reported accuracy comes from post-evaluation tricks (smoothing LR, small batch size) rather than its synthesis method. In cross-architecture generalization (Table 2), EDC beats RDED by 3.4–10.6% across all nine architectures at IPC 10, with the largest gains on ShuffleNet-V2 (+10.4%) and MobileNet-V2 (+10.6%), confirming that the condensed dataset transfers knowledge more effectively to diverse model families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the uni-level optimization paradigm for dataset condensation (SRe2L, G-VBSM) and the training-free reconstruction approach (RDED), positioning itself as a systematic design-space exploration rather than a fundamentally new algorithm. It departs from prior work by treating all stages—data synthesis initialization, matching mechanism, loss landscape flatness, post-evaluation training protocol, augmentation, and backbone selection—as jointly optimizable design choices, whereas previous methods typically targeted one or two specific improvements. The soft category-aware matching extends statistical matching from a single Gaussian (Form 1) to a Gaussian Mixture Model (Form 2) with a weighted hybrid, and the flatness regularization adapts sharpness-aware minimization to the data synthesis context via an EMA-based teacher, drawing on MESA-style self-distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most practically impactful finding is that 16.2% of RDED's reported improvement over prior methods stems from simple post-evaluation hyperparameter choices (smoothing LR schedule, smaller batch size) rather than the data synthesis algorithm itself, suggesting the field has been underestimating the role of training protocol in condensed-dataset evaluation. EDC's 48.6% on ImageNet-1k at IPC 10 (0.78% compression) with half the compute of G-VBSM provides a strong, reproducible baseline. Open questions include whether the GMM-based matching generalizes to non-image modalities or extremely long-tail class distributions, and whether the reliance on multiple pre-trained observer models limits applicability in privacy-sensitive or resource-constrained settings.
