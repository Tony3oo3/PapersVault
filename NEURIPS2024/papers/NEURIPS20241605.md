## Problem

Existing graph anomaly detection (GAD) methods follow a 'one model for one dataset' paradigm, requiring dataset-specific training, ample in-domain data, and hyperparameter tuning for each new graph. This incurs high training costs, imposes data requirements, and limits generalizability across domains. The paper asks whether a single 'one-for-all' GAD model can detect anomalies on unseen graph datasets without any retraining or fine-tuning, using only a handful of normal node labels at inference.

## Input / Output

At inference, ARC takes an attributed graph (node feature matrix, adjacency matrix) from an unseen domain plus a small set of n_k labeled normal nodes (default 10) as context samples, and outputs a continuous anomaly score for every unlabeled query node. Because test datasets may have arbitrary feature dimensions and semantics distinct from training data, a feature alignment step (projection to a shared dimensionality followed by smoothness-based reordering) is applied before encoding. The model is trained once on a fixed collection of labeled training graphs and never updated on test data.

## Method

ARC comprises three modules: (1) a smoothness-based feature alignment module that projects heterogeneous features to a common dimensionality and reorders dimensions by their graph-signal smoothness (lower smoothness = higher-frequency, more anomaly-sensitive), creating a dataset-agnostic feature ordering; (2) an ego-neighbor residual graph encoder that performs L iterations of propagation followed by a shared MLP transformation, then computes residuals between each propagated representation and the initial one, concatenating multi-hop residuals to form embeddings that act as a high-pass filter capturing heterophily and local affinity; (3) a cross-attentive in-context anomaly scoring module that reconstructs each query node's embedding as a weighted combination of context node embeddings via cross-attention (without a separate value matrix), using the L2 drift distance between original and reconstructed embeddings as the anomaly score. Training employs a marginal cosine similarity loss that minimizes drift for normal nodes and maximizes it for anomalies, and the architecture reduces to a one-class classifier under random initialization, ensuring baseline detection capability even before training.

## Results

ARC achieves state-of-the-art AUROC on 5 out of 8 test datasets without any fine-tuning, with particularly large gains over the best baseline: +21.1% on Cora (87.45 vs. 72.23), +18.8% on CiteSeer (90.95 vs. 76.54), and +36.6% on Amazon (80.67 vs. 59.06). On the three datasets where ARC trails the best fine-tuned baseline (ACM, Facebook, Weibo), the gap is modest (≤7.4 AUROC points). The ablation study confirms all three modules contribute substantially: removing the cross-attention scoring module drops AUROC to near-random levels (e.g., 47.39 on Cora), and removing the residual encoder causes a similar collapse (37.44 on Cora).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARC outperforms all nine baselines on 5 of 8 datasets (Cora, CiteSeer, BlogCatalog, Reddit, Amazon) without any dataset-specific fine-tuning, while the strongest fine-tuned baselines (TAM, DOMINANT) still trail ARC by 15–37 AUROC points on those datasets. On ACM, TAM with fine-tuning (86.29) edges out ARC (79.88) by 6.4 points; on Facebook, TAM (76.26) leads ARC (67.56) by 8.7 points; and on Weibo, DOMINANT (92.21) surpasses ARC (88.85) by 3.4 points. Supervised baselines (GCN, GAT, BGNN, BWGNN, GHRN) perform near or below random (50% AUROC) in the pre-train-only setting, confirming that binary-classification GNNs do not generalize across domains. Unsupervised baselines in the pre-train-only setting also show poor transfer (e.g., CoLA drops to 12.99 on Facebook), whereas adding fine-tuning recovers some performance but introduces risk of negative transfer (e.g., CoLA drops from 63.29 to 31.55 on Weibo). Overall, ARC's rank of 1.5 (averaged across datasets) substantially surpasses the next-best methods (TAM at 3.4, DOMINANT at 3.6), and its inference-only protocol eliminates the fine-tuning cost entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalist anomaly detection paradigm introduced for image data (InCTRL) to the graph domain, formalizing a 'one-for-all' GAD problem where a single model detects anomalies across diverse datasets without fine-tuning. Methodologically, it departs from conventional GNN-based GAD (which follows a 'one model per dataset' paradigm) by combining three novel components: a smoothness-based feature alignment that orders features by their graph-signal frequency contribution, an ego-neighbor residual encoder that acts as a multi-hop high-pass filter (drawing conceptual lineage from Radar's residual analysis and heterophily-aware GNNs), and a cross-attention in-context scoring module that generalizes one-class classification to handle multi-class normal patterns. The in-context learning mechanism is adapted from LLM and CV literature but is the first application to graph anomaly detection with only normal (single-class) context samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes the generalist GAD problem as a distinct research direction, offering a practical path to reduce the training cost and data requirements of deploying anomaly detectors across new domains. The key limitation is that ARC can only leverage normal context samples at inference, leaving open the question of how to incorporate available abnormal samples for improved discrimination. Future directions include extending to dynamic graphs, graph-level anomalies, and developing methods that handle both normal and abnormal context samples. The finding that feature smoothness provides a model-agnostic, transferable ordering for anomaly detection is a useful inductive bias that could inform other cross-domain graph learning tasks.
