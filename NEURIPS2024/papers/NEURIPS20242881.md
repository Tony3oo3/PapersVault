## Problem

Estimating the edge density of Erdős-Rényi random graphs under node differential privacy has remained open: prior algorithms either require exponential running time (Borgs et al.) or incur suboptimal privacy costs (Sealfon and Ullman), and no matching information-theoretic lower bound existed for the Erdős-Rényi model. This paper asks whether a polynomial-time, node-private, and rate-optimal algorithm exists, and settles the question affirmatively while extending the result to the broader inhomogeneous random graph model.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input an η-corrupted n×n adjacency matrix A (where at most ηn nodes may be arbitrarily rewired), a privacy parameter ε, a corruption fraction η, and a coarse constant-factor estimate d̂ of the expected average degree d° = np°. It outputs a scalar estimate p̃ of the edge density (equivalently d̃ of the average degree) with relative error |p̃/p° − 1| bounded by Õ(1/(εn√(np°))) in the Erdős-Rényi case. The error metric is relative rather than absolute, reflecting that estimation is inherently harder for sparser graphs, and the guarantee holds with probability 1 − n^{−Ω(1)}.

## Method

The algorithm operates in two stages—coarse estimation (constant-factor approximation) followed by fine estimation (optimal rate)—each implemented as a sum-of-squares exponential mechanism. The key technical contribution is a new degree-8 sum-of-squares identifiability proof showing that any graph Y satisfying polynomial regularity constraints (degree concentration |deg(i) − d(Y)| ≤ √d̂ log n and spectral bound ‖Y − d(Y)/n · 11ᵀ‖_op ≤ √(d̂ log n)) and within node distance γn of the input must have average degree within Õ(1/√(nd°) + η/√d°) of d°. The privacy mechanism uses an exponential mechanism whose score function is the SOS relaxation of the minimum corruption distance: sos-score(d; A) = min γn subject to existence of a level-8 pseudo-expectation satisfying the node-distance constraints, regularity constraints, and |d(Y) − d| ≤ 1/poly(n). This yields a 2ε-differentially node-private algorithm whose sensitivity is bounded by 1, and the volume argument ensures the output has score O(log n/ε) with high probability, translating via the SOS identifiability proof into the optimal error rate.

## Results

For Erdős-Rényi random graphs G(n, p°), the algorithm achieves relative error |p̃/p° − 1| ≤ O(√log n/(n√p°) + log²n/(εn√(np°)) + η log n/√(np°)) with probability 1 − n^{−Ω(1)}, running in polynomial time via semidefinite programming. The privacy cost term log²n/(εn√(np°)) matches the information-theoretic lower bound Ω(log(1/β)/(εn√(np°))) up to a log n factor (Theorem 1.5). For inhomogeneous random graphs with edge probability matrix Q° and R = max Q°_{ij}/p°, the error is O(√(log n/(np°)) + R log²n/(εn) + Rη log(1/η)), which is optimal up to logarithmic factors by matching lower bounds (Theorems 1.7–1.8). The algorithm is simultaneously ε-node-differentially private and robust to η-fraction adversarial node corruption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main contribution is matching the privacy cost of the exponential-time algorithm of Borgs et al. [BCSZ18] (Õ(1/(εn√(np°)))) while achieving polynomial running time via a sum-of-squares SDP relaxation. Compared to the polynomial-time algorithm of Sealfon and Ullman [SU19], this paper eliminates the additional 1/(ε²n²p°) term that dominates in the regime ε ≪ 1/√(np°), yielding a strictly better privacy cost. For inhomogeneous random graphs, the algorithm improves on Chen et al. [CDd+24] by removing the 1/(ε²nd°) term, reducing the privacy cost from Õ(R/(εn) + 1/(ε²nd°)) to Õ(R/(εn)). In the sparse regime (d° ≪ log n) with constant corruption η = Ω(1), this paper provides a constant-factor approximation of d°, whereas the best prior robust algorithm of Acharya et al. [AJK+22] cannot. The privacy cost is shown to be information-theoretically optimal up to a log n factor via a coupling-based lower bound (Theorem 1.5), and the robustness cost matches the lower bound Ω(η/√(np°)) up to log n.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: (1) the reduction from differential privacy to adversarial robustness via exponential mechanisms (Hopkins et al., STOC 2023; Asi et al., ICML 2023), and (2) sum-of-squares methods for robust statistical estimation (Raghavendra et al., ICM 2018; Hopkins et al., STOC 2022). Prior private edge density estimation algorithms either achieved optimal rates with exponential running time (Borgs et al., 2018, via Lipschitz extension) or ran in polynomial time with suboptimal error (Sealfon and Ullman, 2019, via smooth sensitivity). This paper bridges the gap by constructing a degree-8 sum-of-squares proof for robust identifiability of edge density, then wrapping it in a sum-of-squares exponential mechanism to obtain a polynomial-time private algorithm that matches the optimal rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result to simultaneously achieve polynomial-time, node-differential-privacy, robustness, and near-optimal error for edge density estimation, resolving a question explicitly posed in the literature. The matching lower bounds (up to log factors) confirm that the privacy cost is information-theoretically unavoidable, closing the gap between what is possible and what is efficiently achievable. The sum-of-squares exponential mechanism framework is general and could be adapted to other graph statistics or high-dimensional estimation tasks under privacy constraints. Open questions include whether the log-factor gaps can be closed, whether the approach extends to more complex graph parameters (e.g., community structure), and whether the degree-8 SOS level is truly necessary or if lower levels suffice.
