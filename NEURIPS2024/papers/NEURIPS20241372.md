## Problem

Warm-starting neural network training by reusing previously learned weights often yields worse generalization than training from scratch, even when the data distribution is stationary. The underlying mechanism was poorly understood, and existing plasticity-loss remedies designed for non-stationary settings (layer resetting, weight regularization, dead-neuron revival) prove ineffective in this stationary incremental setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241372/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DASH takes the model weights from the end of the previous experiment and the cumulative training dataset (all chunks seen so far, e.g., CIFAR-10 at 32×32 or Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64) as input. It produces a single set of modified weights that serve as the initialization for the next training experiment; the modification is applied exactly once per new-data arrival, not during ongoing training. The downstream output remains standard class labels for image classification.

## Method

DASH (Direction-Aware SHrinking) approximates an ideal initialization that retains learned features while discarding memorized noise. Concretely, it computes an exponential moving average (coefficient α) of per-chunk loss gradients over the cumulative dataset, then for each per-neuron weight vector θ computes the cosine similarity s_θ between θ and the negative averaged gradient. Each weight is then element-wise multiplied by max{λ, s_θ}, so weights well-aligned with the gradient (interpreted as having learned recurring, class-relevant features) are shrunk only mildly, while misaligned weights (interpreted as having memorized data-specific noise) are shrunk aggressively toward zero. The theoretical framework formalizes this by showing that under stationary incremental training, warm-started models fail to learn new features because the per-chunk feature signal strength falls below the noise threshold, causing the model to memorize individual data points instead; DASH's direction-aware shrinkage selectively resets those memorized components while preserving the feature directions that remain useful for the expanded dataset.

## Results

On ResNet-18 with SGD, DASH achieves 46.11% test accuracy on Tiny-ImageNet, 84.08% on CIFAR-10, 57.99% on CIFAR-100, and 93.67% on SVHN at the final experiment, consistently outperforming both cold-starting (Random Init) and the Shrink & Perturb (S&P) baseline. DASH also reduces training steps compared to cold-starting in most settings (e.g., 8,341 vs. 30,237 steps on Tiny-ImageNet; 3,519 vs. 10,557 on CIFAR-100), while warm-starting alone achieves the fewest steps but suffers severe accuracy degradation (e.g., 9.57% on Tiny-ImageNet vs. 46.11% for DASH). With SAM, DASH further improves to 49.57% (Tiny-ImageNet) and 86.75% (CIFAR-10), maintaining its advantage over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DASH outperforms S&P (Ash & Adams, 2020) on test accuracy across all four datasets with ResNet-18/SGD: +11.8 pp on Tiny-ImageNet (46.11 vs. 34.34), +2.8 pp on CIFAR-10 (84.08 vs. 81.25), +7.9 pp on CIFAR-100 (57.99 vs. 50.08), and +1.0 pp on SVHN (93.67 vs. 92.67), while also requiring fewer steps on Tiny-ImageNet (8,341 vs. 13,815) and CIFAR-100 (3,519 vs. 4,926). DASH also surpasses cold-starting (Random Init) on test accuracy in every setting, e.g., +18.5 pp on Tiny-ImageNet and +22.5 pp on CIFAR-100, confirming that direction-aware shrinkage preserves learned features better than discarding all prior knowledge. Warm-starting alone is dramatically worse than both DASH and cold-starting (e.g., 9.57% vs. 46.11% on Tiny-ImageNet), validating the paper's noise-memorization hypothesis. Non-stationary plasticity methods—L2 INIT (Kumar et al., 2023) and Reset (Nikishin et al., 2022)—are shown to be ineffective in the stationary incremental setting, performing at or below warm-starting levels (Table 2). Layer normalization (Ba et al., 2016) fails to close the warm-vs-cold gap and actually widens it (Figure 15), further distinguishing the stationary plasticity problem from non-stationary ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature learning framework of Allen-Zhu and Li (2020) and the combinatorial learning process of Jiang et al. (2024) by incorporating frequency-dependent features and noise components from Shen et al. (2022) into a discrete, architecture-agnostic model. It departs from the dominant line of plasticity-loss research (Lyle et al., 2023b; Sokar et al., 2023; Kumar et al., 2023), which focuses on non-stationary distributions, by providing the first theoretical explanation for why warm-starting degrades generalization even under strict stationarity. The proposed DASH method is a direction-aware refinement of the uniform Shrink & Perturb (S&P) heuristic from Ash and Adams (2020), replacing blind weight scaling with cosine-similarity-guided per-neuron shrinkage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of noise memorization as the primary mechanism behind plasticity loss in stationary settings reframes a previously purely empirical observation into a testable theoretical prediction, and DASH offers a lightweight, single-step initialization that practitioners can adopt without architectural changes. However, the discrete learning framework is quite abstract and does not assume any hypothesis class, limiting direct transferability to continuous optimization dynamics; in SoTA training settings (with data augmentation, weight decay, LR schedules), DASH no longer outperforms cold-starting. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-stationary distributions (e.g., reinforcement learning), providing optimization-theoretic guarantees, and validating DASH on non-vision tasks such as language modeling.
