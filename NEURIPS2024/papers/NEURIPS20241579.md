## Problem

Existing multimodal agents in open-world environments like Minecraft fail at long-horizon tasks because they lack structured world knowledge (e.g., crafting dependencies, tool progression) and multimodal experience from past attempts. MLLMs such as GPT-4V have insufficient domain-specific knowledge, and prior agents either store only unimodal history or store raw multimodal data without summarization, making retrieval inefficient and in-context learning limited to success cases only.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a 640×360 RGB game screen (including hotbar, health/food bars, and hand animations) at 20 FPS along with a text task instruction. The Knowledge-Guided Planner outputs a complete ordered sub-goal sequence in a single call (rather than step-by-step), the Experience-Driven Reflector outputs one of three discrete decisions (COMPLETE, CONTINUE, REPLAN) at periodic intervals, and the Action Controller (STEVE-1) produces low-level mouse/keyboard control signals from a 15-action space that includes abstracted craft and smelt operations. The agent always starts with an empty inventory in survival mode.

## Method

The core contribution is a Hybrid Multimodal Memory module with two complementary stores: a Hierarchical Directed Knowledge Graph (HDKG) that encodes crafting/mining dependencies as a directed graph (nodes = objects, edges = 'can be crafted from'), enabling topological-sort retrieval of all prerequisite materials for a target item without any parameter updates; and an Abstracted Multimodal Experience Pool (AMEP) that dynamically summarizes video frames via a 1 fps video buffer + 16-frame image buffer with MineCLIP-based visual-text alignment, storing both successful and failed episodes as in-context references. On top of this memory, Optimus-1 comprises a Knowledge-Guided Planner (GPT-4V conditioned on the HDKG subgraph and visual observation to produce the full sub-goal sequence in one shot), an Experience-Driven Reflector (periodically retrieves relevant COMPLETE/CONTINUE/REPLAN cases from AMEP to assess progress and trigger replanning), and STEVE-1 as the Action Controller. A non-parametric 'free exploration–teacher guidance' curriculum incrementally populates the memory across multiple Optimus-1 instances sharing the same HDKG and AMEP, enabling self-evolution from easy to hard tasks without any gradient updates.

## Results

On the 67-task Minecraft long-horizon benchmark, Optimus-1 achieves an overall success rate of 22.26%, substantially outperforming Jarvis-1 (16.89%), DEPS (5.39%), and GPT-4V/GPT-3.5 (0.00%). On the Wood and Stone groups, Optimus-1 reaches near-human performance (98.60% and 92.35% SR vs. 100% human-level), while on harder groups like Diamond (11.61%) and Redstone (25.02%) it still leads all baselines by 29–53% over Jarvis-1. Optimus-1 also completes tasks in fewer steps (e.g., 841.94 avg steps on Wood vs. 1355.25 for Jarvis-1) and less time (47.09s vs. 67.76s), demonstrating both higher success and greater efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Optimus-1 outperforms all three compared baselines across every task group. Against Jarvis-1 (the strongest prior agent), Optimus-1 gains +4.84% on Wood, +3.15% on Stone, +10.54% on Iron, +2.63% on Diamond, +8.71% on Redstone, and +3.65% on Armor, for an overall improvement of +5.37% SR. Against DEPS, the gap is far larger: Optimus-1 achieves 22.26% overall SR versus DEPS's 5.39%, with DEPS scoring 0% on Gold, Redstone, and Diamond groups where Optimus-1 still achieves 8.51%, 25.02%, and 11.61% respectively. On the MP5 benchmark (Table 15), Optimus-1 surpasses MP5 on Basic (98.00% vs 96.00%), Wooden (98.57% vs 88.67%), and Stone (83.38% vs 76.00%) levels, and is competitive on Iron (52.94% vs 52.00%), though it trails on Diamond level (18.70% vs 22.00%) due to the extreme rarity of diamond ore. Notably, GPT-4V and GPT-3.5 without the Hybrid Multimodal Memory module score 0% on all groups beyond Wood/Stone, underscoring that the memory module is the critical differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM/MLLM-based Minecraft agents (Voyager, DEPS, MP5, Jarvis-1) by introducing a Hybrid Multimodal Memory module that addresses two identified gaps: the lack of explicit structured world knowledge and the absence of multimodal experience storage. Unlike prior agents that either learn knowledge implicitly through fine-tuning or store raw unimodal histories, Optimus-1 uses a Hierarchical Directed Knowledge Graph for explicit, train-free knowledge retrieval and an Abstracted Multimodal Experience Pool for summarized multimodal in-context learning. A key methodological departure from Jarvis-1 and MP5 is the direct use of visual observations (rather than textual descriptions) as planning conditions, and the novel incorporation of failure cases alongside successes in the reflection phase.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the Hybrid Multimodal Memory module is practically significant: it demonstrates that generic MLLMs (Deepseek-VL, InternLM-XComposer2-VL) can achieve 2–6× improvements without any parameter updates, potentially reducing reliance on expensive proprietary APIs like GPT-4V. The self-evolution mechanism (free exploration + teacher guidance) offers a path toward agents that incrementally expand their capabilities without retraining. However, the approach remains limited to Minecraft, depends on STEVE-1 for low-level actions (which constrains complex tasks like building or combat), and the gap to human performance on harder task groups (Diamond, Redstone, Armor) remains substantial. Open questions include whether the HDKG/AMEP paradigm generalizes beyond Minecraft and whether end-to-end vision-language-action models can subsume the modular planner-reflector-controller architecture.
