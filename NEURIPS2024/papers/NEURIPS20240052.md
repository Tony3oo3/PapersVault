## Problem

Diffusion models learn strong image priors for solving inverse problems but require training on entire high-resolution images, making them computationally infeasible for large-scale or 3D data and demanding millions of training samples. Existing patch-based approaches either still require the full image at inference, are task-specific, or cannot express the score function of the whole image purely from patch-level scores, preventing their use as a general-purpose prior in inverse problem solvers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240052/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the network receives randomly extracted patches (typically 56×56) from zero-padded images, with 2D positional coordinate arrays (x and y pixel locations scaled to [-1,1]) concatenated as additional input channels; noise is added only to the image patch, not the positional arrays. At inference, the zero-padded image is partitioned into (k+1)² non-overlapping P×P patches plus a border region, and the network is called only on individual patches. The output is a full reconstructed image (e.g., 256×256 CT slice or face image) or an unconditionally generated image, with the whole-image score assembled by summing patch scores over a randomly selected grid shift per iteration.

## Method

PaDIS zero-pads an N×N image by M pixels on all sides so that it can be tiled by (k+1)² non-overlapping P×P patches in M² different grid-shift configurations. A single UNet denoiser is trained via denoising score matching on randomly sampled patches of varying sizes, with positional encoding providing global location context; the score of the whole image is then expressed as a sum of patch scores (Eq. 4), with the border region's score computed trivially via Tweedie's formula since it is identically zero. At each reconstruction iteration, one random (i,j) grid shift is selected, all (k+1)² patch scores are computed in parallel, and their sum approximates the full-image score. This patch-based prior is then plugged into DPS with Langevin dynamics (or other SDE solvers) for inverse problem reconstruction, requiring no retraining per task. The approach is grounded in a Markov random field interpretation where patches serve as cliques with a shared potential parameterized by the network, and the random grid shifting across iterations eliminates boundary artifacts that would arise from fixed patch boundaries.

## Results

PaDIS achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all four inverse problems in the main comparison (Table 1): 33.57 dB / 0.854 SSIM for 20-view CT, 29.48 dB / 0.767 SSIM for 8-view CT, 30.80 dB / 0.870 SSIM for deblurring, and 29.47 dB / 0.846 SSIM for 4× superresolution. The patch-based model is trained in ~12 hours versus 24–36 hours for the whole-image diffusion model, while using only 2,304 CT training images (or 3,000 CelebA-HQ images) and a 56×56 patch size. On the higher-resolution 512×512 CT task (Table 3), PaDIS reaches 36.93 dB / 0.899 SSIM, far exceeding FBP (28.38 dB) and ADMM-TV (29.48 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaDIS outperforms every baseline on all four inverse problems. Against the whole-image diffusion model (the closest competitor), PaDIS gains +0.73 dB PSNR on 20-view CT, +3.74 dB on 8-view CT, +0.61 dB on deblurring, and +0.30 dB on superresolution, while requiring roughly half the training time. Traditional and plug-and-play methods (ADMM-TV, PnP-ADMM, PnP-RED) trail PaDIS by 5–8 dB PSNR across all tasks. Among other diffusion-based solvers, Langevin dynamics [1] is the strongest alternative (33.03 dB on 20-view CT) but still falls 0.54 dB short of PaDIS; VE-DDNM [7] and predictor-corrector [19] are further behind. The two patch-assembly baselines—Patch Averaging [23] and Patch Stitching [66]—use fixed overlapping patches and lose to PaDIS by 0.22–1.80 dB PSNR, confirming that the random-shift zero-padding strategy more effectively combines patch priors into a coherent whole-image score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends patch-based diffusion models (Wang et al. 2023, Ding et al. 2023) and diffusion-based inverse problem solvers (DPS, DDNM, Langevin dynamics) by showing that a whole-image score function can be computed purely from patch-level network evaluations, without ever inputting the full image. Prior patch-based methods either required the whole image at inference time, operated in feature space, or were task-specific; 3D approaches using 2D slices (Chung et al., Lee et al.) added inter-slice regularizers rather than providing a self-contained score function. The zero-padding plus random partitioning scheme, grounded in a Markov random field interpretation, is the key conceptual departure that makes the patch prior a true black-box compatible with any existing SDE solver or inverse problem algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PaDIS makes diffusion-based inverse problem solving feasible for high-resolution 2D images (512×512) and, in principle, 3D/4D volumes, where whole-image diffusion is memory-prohibitive, while requiring orders of magnitude less training data (hundreds vs. millions of images). The main open limitation is that accelerated samplers (DDIM, EDM) produce boundary artifacts due to the stochastic patch selection, restricting reconstruction to ~1000 NFEs; multi-scale or deterministic partitioning strategies could address this. The approach also opens a path toward reducing CT dose in clinical settings, though hallucination risks of generative priors in medical imaging remain an unresolved concern.
