## Problem

JEPA-based self-supervised methods empirically learn more semantic, less noisy representations than MAE-based reconstruction methods, but the mechanism behind this observation in training dynamics remains uncharacterized. The paper seeks to uncover the precise implicit bias in the learning dynamics that causes JEPA to prioritize predictive, low-noise features over merely high-variance ones, and to determine under what architectural conditions this bias emerges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242896/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on pairs of correlated Gaussian data views (x, y) with simultaneously diagonalizable covariances, where each feature i is parameterized by a covariance λ_i and a regression coefficient ρ_i. The method produces the temporal ordering of feature learning (critical times t* per feature) and the asymptotic encoder weight magnitudes, revealing which features are learned first and to what amplitude. In the temporal generative model, inputs are consecutive video frames with time-varying image amplitudes and per-mode additive noise, making the framework applicable to video prediction settings like V-JEPA vs. VideoMAE.

## Method

The paper derives exact gradient-flow ODEs for deep linear networks (L-layer encoder, 1-layer decoder) under balanced orthogonal initialization, for both JEPA (with StopGrad) and MAE objectives. The key structural finding is that the two ODEs differ only in a single exponent: JEPA has w̄^(3−1/L) while MAE has w̄^(2−1/L) in the driving term, which leads to different fixed points (ρ^L vs. ρ^(L/(L+1))) and, more importantly, different critical times. By solving the ODEs and computing the critical time t* for each feature to reach a finite fraction of its asymptotic value, they show that JEPA's t* depends on both λ and ρ (with the ρ-dependence scaling as ε^(1/L) and growing with depth L), while MAE's t* depends only on λ. This reveals that JEPA exhibits greedy learning with respect to the regression coefficient ρ—prioritizing high-influence, low-noise features—a bias absent in MAE that strengthens with encoder depth and vanishes for shallow (L=1) encoders. The theory is validated through ODE simulations, SGD training of randomly initialized deep linear networks, and a temporal linear generative model for video.

## Results

The paper derives exact ODEs for deep linear JEPA and MAE training dynamics, showing the only difference is a single exponent: JEPA has w̄^(3−1/L) while MAE has w̄^(2−1/L) in the driving term. The fixed-point solutions are w̄_MAE(∞,L) = ρ^(L/(L+1)) and w̄_JEPA(∞,L) = ρ^L, meaning deeper JEPA encoders become increasingly low-rank, suppressing noisy features. The critical time analysis reveals that MAE's leading-order term depends only on 1/λ (covariance), while JEPA's next-to-leading-order term depends on 1/ρ (regression coefficient) and grows with depth L, producing a distinct implicit bias toward high-influence features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two self-supervised objectives — JEPA (self-distillation with StopGrad) and MAE (input-space reconstruction) — in a fully solvable deep linear setting. Both objectives exhibit greedy step-wise learning with respect to feature covariance λ, consistent with prior work on SSL dynamics. However, JEPA uniquely exhibits greedy learning with respect to the regression coefficient ρ: features with higher ρ are learned first, and this priority increases with encoder depth L. MAE is agnostic to ρ, learning all features with the same λ on the same timescale regardless of depth. When λ and ρ have opposite trends across features (e.g., high-covariance features are noisy/low-ρ), JEPA completely reverses the learning order relative to MAE, potentially learning entirely different features under a finite training budget. This depth-dependent ρ-bias is the paper's central finding and explains why JEPA empirically prioritizes semantic (low-variance, high-predictive) features over noisy high-variance features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep linear network dynamics framework (Saxe et al., Advani et al.) and the stepwise SSL learning analysis of Simon et al. (2023) to a new comparison: it derives exact ODEs for both JEPA (StopGrad self-distillation, as in SimSiam/BYOL) and MAE objectives, revealing that the two share the same greedy stepwise structure but differ in a depth-dependent exponent that makes JEPA sensitive to the regression coefficient ρ while MAE is not. It complements Balestriero & LeCun's (2024) empirical observation that reconstruction losses learn uninformative features by providing a mechanistic, dynamical-systems explanation for why latent-space prediction avoids noisy directions. The key departure from prior work is the identification of a depth-gated implicit bias: the JEPA/MAE distinction vanishes for shallow encoders and grows with depth, a phenomenon not captured by earlier analyses of SSL dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first analytical mechanism explaining why JEPA architectures (I-JEPA, V-JEPA, BYOL) empirically outperform MAE on perception tasks with fewer optimization steps: JEPA's implicit bias toward high-regression-coefficient features causes it to skip noisy, high-variance directions that MAE would learn first. However, the results are confined to simultaneously diagonalizable covariances, specific balanced initializations, and linear models, leaving open whether the depth-dependent bias persists in non-linear architectures and realistic data distributions. The finding that JEPA can learn features in the *opposite* order from MAE when ρ and λ anti-correlate suggests practical implications for curriculum design and feature selection in SSL, but generalization to non-diagonalizable settings and non-linear networks remains a significant open question.
