## Problem

Particle-based variational inference methods with neural-network-based functional gradient flows have shown strong performance for unconstrained sampling, but they lack a principled mechanism to handle target distributions supported on constrained domains defined by inequality constraints. Existing constrained sampling approaches either require expensive numerical subroutines (e.g., solving nonlinear systems in constrained HMC), rely on case-by-case mirror maps, or impose restrictive geometric assumptions. This paper provides a general framework that seamlessly adapts functional gradient ParVIs to arbitrary inequality-constrained domains.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized target log-density (with differentiable positive density on the domain), a continuously differentiable constraint function g(x) defining the domain Ω = {x | g(x) ≤ 0}, and an initial set of particles (typically drawn from a Gaussian). It produces particles that approximate the target distribution while remaining confined within Ω. The constraint function must satisfy a lower bound on its gradient norm outside Ω (‖∇g‖ ≥ C > 0) for the finite-time entry guarantee, and the target must satisfy a Poincaré inequality for the convergence bound.

## Method

CFG designs a piece-wise velocity field: inside Ω, a neural network h_net = f_net − z_net²·∇g learns the optimal transport direction by minimizing the regularized Stein discrepancy (RSD); outside Ω, a fixed inward velocity −λ∇g/‖∇g‖ drives particles into the domain in finite time. The z_net²·∇g term acts as a learned reflection that neutralizes outward-pointing components, preventing particles from adhering to the boundary. A key technical challenge is that restricting the RSD to Ω introduces an additional boundary integral term ∫_{∂Ω} pv·n dS via integration by parts, which is computationally intractable in general. The paper derives a band-wise Monte Carlo estimator based on the co-area formula, using particles within a thin band near ∂Ω (identified via a sufficient condition on g) to approximate this term, with an adaptive bandwidth h = h₀(dN)^{−1/3} that balances bias and variance. The method achieves a TV convergence rate of O(T^{−1/2} + ε^{1/2}) under a Poincaré inequality assumption and scales linearly in the number of particles, unlike kernel-based alternatives.

## Results

On the COMPAS monotonic BNN task (ε=0.01), CFG achieves 0.660 test accuracy and 0.632 test NLL, outperforming PD-SVGD (0.645/0.635), C-SVGD (0.650/0.634), and MIED (0.590/0.678), while keeping 0% of particles outside the constrained domain. On the double-moon toy domain, CFG reduces Wasserstein-2 distance to 0.1660 versus MIED's 0.4724, and energy distance to 0.0022 versus 0.0629. On the 276-dimensional Blog Feedback dataset (particle dimension 13,903), CFG achieves the best RMSE of 0.204, compared to 0.205 (PD-SVGD), 0.217 (C-SVGD), and 0.212 (MIED).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MIED (Li et al., 2022), CFG achieves substantially better Wasserstein-2 distance on the double-moon domain (0.1660 vs 0.4724) and energy distance (0.0022 vs 0.0629), while being comparable on the ring and cardioid domains; on the Bayesian Lasso synthetic task, CFG is best at small particle counts but all methods converge at large N, with CFG scaling linearly in N versus MIED's quadratic cost. Against MSVGD (Shi et al., 2022), CFG outperforms on the block-constrained truncated Gaussian mixture in both Wasserstein-2 and energy distance. Against Spherical HMC (Lan et al., 2014), CFG achieves the best Wasserstein-2 and energy distance at small particle counts on the synthetic Bayesian Lasso, with all methods converging at large N; on the real diabetes dataset, CFG aligns well with Spherical HMC and MIED across shrinkage factors. Against PD-SVGD and C-SVGD (Liu et al., 2021), CFG consistently outperforms both on all three monotonicity thresholds for test accuracy (by 1–7%) and test NLL (by 0.002–0.046), while also achieving 0% particles outside the domain versus up to 24% for PD-SVGD at ε=0.005.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the functional gradient ParVI line (di Langosco et al. 2021, Dong et al. 2023, Cheng et al. 2023), which replaced SVGD's RKHS with neural networks to learn Wasserstein gradient flows, to the previously unaddressed setting of inequality-constrained domains. It departs from prior constrained sampling methods (Spherical HMC, mirror-map SVGD, projected Langevin) that require case-by-case geometric design or expensive nonlinear solves, by instead enforcing a non-escaping boundary condition on a piece-wise velocity field. The closest conceptual relative is Zhang et al. (2022), which handles equality constraints via orthogonal-space decomposition; this paper complements that with inequality constraints and a novel band-wise Monte Carlo estimator for the boundary integral that arises from integration by parts on bounded domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a general, geometry-agnostic recipe for constrained ParVI that scales linearly in particle count (vs. quadratic for kernel-based MIED), making it practical for high-dimensional constrained Bayesian inference tasks like monotonic neural networks. The ablation showing that omitting the boundary integral term causes sampling failure in most cases underscores a fundamental structural difference between constrained and unconstrained ParVI that was previously unappreciated. Open questions include adaptive bandwidth selection beyond the heuristic (dN)^{-1/3} scheme, extension to SVGD-style kernel methods, and a rigorous connection to reflected SDEs, all of which the authors flag as future work.
