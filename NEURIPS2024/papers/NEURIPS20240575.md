## Problem

Existing federated data augmentation methods (FedMix, FedFA, FedGen) share input-level images or feature-level statistics across clients, creating privacy leakage vulnerabilities where attackers can reconstruct original data via gradient matching. This paper asks whether data augmentation in federated learning can be performed by sharing only augmentation policies—abstract transformation parameters—rather than any data-derived information, while still adapting to heterogeneous client distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240575/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives the global CNN model weights and a global augmentation policy (a 17×17 joint distribution over pairs of operations from a set of 17 image transformations, with magnitudes uniformly sampled from [0,1]) and produces updated local model weights plus a locally adapted policy. The policy is parameterized by a small neural network (embedding layer → two 100-dim hidden layers → 17×17 output) whose parameters θ are transmitted to the server for aggregation. Images are standard 32×32 RGB (CIFAR-10/100, SVHN) or grayscale (FEMNIST), and the final output is a trained classifier plus the learned global augmentation policy.

## Method

FedAvP introduces a Federated Meta-Policy Loss (FMPL) that reinterprets one FL round (local training + server aggregation) as the inner step and post-aggregation validation as the outer step of a meta-learning loop, so the policy gradient measures how augmentation choices affect validation loss after the model has been updated. To avoid the privacy and communication cost of accessing other clients' validation gradients, the paper derives a first-order Taylor approximation (Proposition 1) that computes the policy gradient locally as the dot product between the validation-data gradient and the augmentation-data gradient, enabling joint model-and-policy training at each local step without an extra communication round. An adaptive policy search mechanism, inspired by Reptile, trains the policy neural network to maximize the dot-product between successive policy gradients, allowing rapid personalization to heterogeneous clients. Gradient clipping is applied to both validation and augmentation gradients to prevent the policy from biasing toward augmentations with larger gradient magnitudes, and a Fast Update variant reduces computation by performing policy updates every τ=5 local steps with a smaller 25-dim hidden network.

## Results

FedAvP achieves the highest test accuracy across all four datasets under non-i.i.d. settings: 50.47% on CIFAR-100 (α=5.0), 45.96% on CIFAR-100 (α=0.1), 83.78% on CIFAR-10 (α=5.0), 89.81% on SVHN (α=0.1), and 84.27% on FEMNIST. The gains are most pronounced in highly heterogeneous settings (α=0.1), where FedAvP outperforms the best baseline (FedFA+RandAugment) by 2.52 points on CIFAR-100 and 2.48 points on SVHN. The Fast Update variant, which reduces per-step computation, retains most of the gains (e.g., 45.08% vs. 45.96% on CIFAR-100 α=0.1) while cutting training time from 4.01 to 1.18 hours on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedAvP outperforms all 10 baseline methods (FedAvg, FedProx, FedDyn, FedExP, FedGen, FedMix, FedFA, each with Default/RandAugment/TrivialAugment) across every dataset and heterogeneity level. The largest margins appear in the most heterogeneous setting (α=0.1): on CIFAR-100, FedAvP (45.96%) beats the strongest baseline FedFA+RandAugment (43.44%) by 2.52 points, and on SVHN it surpasses FedFA+Default (87.33%) by 2.48 points. On FEMNIST, FedAvP (84.27%) leads the best baseline FedGen+Default (81.86%) by 2.41 points. Notably, applying RandAugment or TrivialAugment to standard FL algorithms does not consistently improve performance (e.g., FedFA+RandAugment drops from 87.33% to 81.32% on SVHN α=0.1), whereas FedAvP's learned policy adapts to each dataset's characteristics. The ablation FedAvP (W/ Local Policy), which removes cross-client policy sharing, underperforms the full method by 0.4–1.6 points, confirming that federated policy aggregation is beneficial. In reconstruction attack experiments, FedAvP's policy-gradient sharing does not increase PSNR (8.72/9.25 dB) compared to standard FedAvg (10.88/11.36 dB), indicating no additional privacy leakage from the shared policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedAvP sits at the intersection of three research threads: standard federated averaging (FedAvg), automated data augmentation policy search (AutoAugment, RandAugment, TrivialAugment), and meta-learning (MAML, Reptile). It departs from prior federated data augmentation methods (FedMix, FedFA, FedGen) that share input-level or feature-level information by transmitting only augmentation policies, and from centralized policy search methods that assume full dataset access by formulating the search as a federated meta-learning problem with a first-order gradient approximation. The Reptile-style outer-loop update on the server and the inner-loop policy adaptation on clients directly inherit the meta-learning framework, while the Federated Meta-Policy Loss reinterprets the standard FL round (local training + aggregation) as the inner step of a meta-update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By sharing only policies rather than data or feature statistics, FedAvP addresses a concrete privacy gap in federated data augmentation while still enabling clients to benefit from collective knowledge about effective augmentations. The first-order approximation and joint training design reduce communication overhead to roughly 2.5% above vanilla FedAvg, making the approach practical. Key limitations include the assumption of FedAvg (no model personalization in the FMPL computation), the added complexity of joint model-policy training, and the fact that the policy neural network introduces a small but non-trivial per-round communication cost. Open questions include how to integrate the policy loss with personalized FL algorithms and whether the approach extends to non-image modalities or larger-scale deployments.
