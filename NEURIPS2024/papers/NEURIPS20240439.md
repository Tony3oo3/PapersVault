## Problem

Existing text-to-image (T2I) benchmarks evaluate faithfulness, aesthetics, and realism but overlook cultural competence — whether models can accurately depict specific cultural artifacts and whether they generate diverse cultural content for under-specified prompts. This gap is critical because T2I models are increasingly adopted globally, yet their predominantly mono-cultural training ecosystems risk unequal representation, particularly for Global South cultures, and no prior benchmark systematically measures these two dimensions (cultural awareness and cultural diversity) at scale.

## Input / Output

For cultural awareness evaluation, the method takes 1000 specific artifact prompts (CUBE-1K) spanning 8 countries and 3 concepts (cuisine, landmarks, art), generates images via T2I models, and produces human-annotated scores: cultural relevance (Yes/No/Maybe), faithfulness (1–5 Likert), and realism (1–5 Likert), each rated by 3 country-specific annotators. For cultural diversity evaluation, the method takes under-specified concept prompts (e.g., 'Image of monuments'), generates 8 images per prompt across 80 seeds and 5 prompt templates (400 images total per prompt, averaged over 50 repetitions), maps each image to its closest artifact in the ~300K CUBE-CSpace using GPT-4-Turbo and mSigLIP retrieval, and outputs a quality-weighted Vendi score (qVS) in [0,1] that jointly captures artifact diversity and HPS-v2 image quality.

## Method

The core contribution is the CUBE benchmark and a novel cultural diversity metric. CUBE is built by traversing WikiData via the SLING framework from manually selected root nodes (e.g., 'dish' Q746549 for cuisine), following 'instance of' (P31) and 'subclass of' (P279) edges up to 4 hops, extracting nodes with 'country-of-origin' (P495) or 'country' (P17) properties, then refining with GPT-4-Turbo self-critiquing to remove noise and fill gaps, yielding ~300K artifacts (CUBE-CSpace); CUBE-1K is a 1000-artifact subset selected via geolocated Google Search popularity with manual verification. For cultural diversity, the paper introduces a quality-weighted Vendi score (qVS) that multiplies the mean HPS-v2 quality score by the normalized Vendi score computed over a flexible similarity kernel k(x_i, x_j) = w₁·k_continent + w₂·k_country + w₃·k_artifact, enabling analysis at continent, country, artifact, or hierarchical levels. This metric satisfies quality-awareness, duplication scaling, and kernel generalizability properties, and is shown to be weakly correlated with faithfulness and realism (ρ ≈ 0.016 and 0.156), establishing cultural diversity as a distinct evaluation axis.

## Results

On CUBE-1K (1000 prompts across 8 countries and 3 concepts), human evaluation reveals that both Imagen 2 and SDXL score below 3 on faithfulness for most Global South countries (e.g., Imagen: 1.9 for Brazil cuisine, 2.2 for Turkey cuisine; SDXL: 1.5 for Brazil cuisine, 1.8 for Turkey cuisine), while achieving higher scores for USA and France (Imagen: 3.4–4.0; SDXL: 3.3–4.0). Realism scores are consistently lower than faithfulness across all geo-cultures, with Imagen 2 ranging from 1.3 (USA art) to 4.4 (Brazil cuisine) and SDXL from 1.3 (Brazil art) to 3.9 (France landmarks). For cultural diversity (CD) on under-specified prompts, all four models achieve very low quality-weighted Vendi scores (max 0.24 for Imagen 2 on Cuisine artifact-level), far from the theoretical maximum of 1, indicating significant gaps in representing global cultural breadth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates four T2I models (Imagen 2, SDXL, Playground, Realistic Vision) on the CUBE benchmark. On cultural awareness (human-rated faithfulness and realism), Imagen 2 generally outperforms SDXL across most country-concept combinations, though SDXL edges ahead on USA art faithfulness (3.9 vs. 3.3) and France landmarks realism (3.9 vs. 3.2). Both models show the largest gaps for Global South countries: Imagen scores only 1.9 on Brazil cuisine faithfulness and 2.2 on Turkey cuisine, while SDXL drops to 1.5 and 1.8 respectively. On cultural diversity (quality-weighted Vendi score), Imagen 2 achieves the highest CD for Cuisine (0.24) and Art (0.22) at the artifact level, while SDXL leads for Landmarks (0.18); Playground and Realistic Vision trail behind on all concepts. Critically, even the best CD scores remain far below 1, and the correlation between diversity and faithfulness/realism is weak (ρ=0.016 and ρ=0.156), indicating that optimizing for aesthetics and faithfulness does not automatically improve cultural diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the T2I evaluation landscape by introducing cultural competence as a first-class evaluation axis, building on the quality-weighted Vendi score framework (Nguyen & Dieng, 2024) and its mathematical foundation in ecological diversity metrics (Friedman & Dieng, 2023). It departs from prior T2I benchmarks (DrawBench, GenEval, DSG-1k, etc.) that assess only faithfulness and realism, and from cultural bias work in vision (Basu et al., 2023; Jha et al., 2024) that focuses on stereotypes rather than systematic competence. The dataset construction pipeline repurposes knowledge-graph traversal (WikiData via SLING) combined with LLM-based self-critiquing (GPT-4) to scale cultural artifact extraction, a methodology borrowed from NLP cultural commonsense extraction (Nguyen et al., 2023) and diversity expansion (Lahoti et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-zero correlation between cultural diversity and faithfulness (ρ=0.016) is a practically important finding: it implies that the current optimization trajectory of T2I models (tuned for aesthetics, faithfulness, and realism) will not automatically close cultural diversity gaps, necessitating explicit diversity objectives. The benchmark is limited to 8 countries and 3 concepts, with acknowledged biases inherited from WikiData's uneven global coverage and from using a single VLM (GPT-4-Turbo) for artifact mapping (~70% accuracy). Open questions include scaling to more geo-cultural regions, incorporating participatory/community-based curation to reduce KB biases, and exploring whether the diversity-faithfulness decoupling holds across model architectures and training regimes.
