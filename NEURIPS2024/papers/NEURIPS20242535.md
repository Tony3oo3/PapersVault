## Problem

Existing S2S-Diffusion models (DiffuSeq, SeqDiffuSeq, Dinoiser) rely on fixed or hand-crafted noise schedules that impose identical noise on all sentences regardless of their semantic content or generation difficulty. This non-contextualized approach fails to adapt noise levels to the varying complexity of individual conditional sentences or to different training epochs, limiting both generation quality and diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242535/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a conditional sentence of up to 128 tokens and generates a target sentence of up to 128 tokens over 2,000 diffusion steps in a continuous embedding space. The scheduler outputs a binary sequence of Meta-Instructions (True/False per diffusion step) that, through a 'skipping' transformation of the fixed sqrt noise schedule, yields sentence-specific noise values. The exploiter applies Partial Noise—imposing noise only on the target portion of the concatenated embedding—enabling classifier-free conditional generation without an explicit conditioning mechanism.

## Method

Meta-DiffuB introduces a scheduler-exploiter framework where a lightweight LSTM encoder-decoder scheduler (B_ψ) learns contextualized noise assignment via a two-step process: it predicts binary Meta-Instructions from the conditional sentence, then transforms them into noise values by 'skipping' entries in the fixed sqrt schedule (a True label advances to the next noise value, a False label holds the current one). The scheduler is trained with policy gradients using a Meta-Reward defined as the BLEU improvement of the exploiter after one gradient step, following the Meta-Exploration paradigm. During training, 32 parallel exploration epochs collect learning-effectiveness signals while keeping the exploiter's weights fixed, and the scheduler is updated every 100 training epochs. The exploiter (D_θ) is a standard 12-layer Transformer DiffuSeq model that uses the contextualized noise for both diffusion and denoising. The trained scheduler also functions as a plug-and-play module that enhances any pre-trained S2S-Diffusion model at inference time without fine-tuning.

## Results

Meta-DiffuB (D_θ=DiffuSeq) achieves the best Mean-Rank (M-R) score across all four Seq2Seq benchmark datasets: 1.00 on QQP, 1.60 on Wiki-Auto, 1.80 on Quasar-T, and 1.20 on Commonsense Conversation, indicating consistent top performance across all evaluation metrics. On BLEU, it reaches 0.2632 (QQP), 0.3877 (WA), 0.1820 (QT), and 0.0220 (CC), outperforming the strongest S2S-Diffusion baseline (SeqDiffuSeq) by 8.2%, 4.4%, 4.3%, and 96.4% respectively. On BERTScore, it achieves 0.8519 (QQP), 0.8233 (WA), 0.6286 (QT), and 0.5316 (CC), and on Self-BLEU (lower is better) it records 0.2595, 0.3888, 0.2527, and 0.0133, demonstrating superior diversity. The computational overhead is minimal: only 2.2% increased parameters, 5% increased training time, and 0.5% increased inference time over DiffuSeq.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Meta-DiffuB (D_θ=DiffuSeq) outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all four primary datasets. Against the closest S2S-Diffusion competitor SeqDiffuSeq [45], it gains +8.2% BLEU on QQP (0.2632 vs. 0.2434), +4.4% on WA (0.3877 vs. 0.3712), +4.3% on QT (0.1820 vs. 0.1746), and +96.4% on CC (0.0220 vs. 0.0112), while also improving BERTScore and diversity metrics. Against DiffuSeq [8], the gains are +8.2% BLEU on QQP, +7.0% on WA, +5.1% on QT, and +58.3% on CC, with Self-BLEU reduced by 5.0%–58.3% across datasets. Dinoiser [44] is the weakest S2S-Diffusion baseline, trailing Meta-DiffuB by 25.8–56.4% BLEU depending on dataset. Among PLM baselines, GPT2-large [33] is the strongest on QT (BLEU 0.1110) but Meta-DiffuB still leads by 64.0%; LevT [10] and GPT2-base [33] are consistently outperformed by 15–100% BLEU. In the extended model-agnostic experiments (Table 7), Meta-DiffuB also improves upon DiffuSeq-v2 [9] (+5.9% BLEU on QQP), BG-DiffuSeq [42] (+6.5% BLEU on QQP), TESS [22] (+4.0% BLEU on QQP), and RDM [47] (+6.9% BLEU on QQP), confirming its plug-and-play applicability across diverse S2S-Diffusion architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

Meta-DiffuB extends the S2S-Diffusion paradigm established by DiffuSeq (Gong et al., ICLR 2023) and its underlying Diffusion-LM transformation (Li et al., NeurIPS 2022) by replacing the fixed sqrt-function noise schedule with a learned, sentence-specific scheduler. The scheduler is trained via Meta-Exploration (Xu et al., 2018), a meta-policy-gradient RL framework previously applied to exploration strategies in RL but not to diffusion noise scheduling. This departs from prior adaptive-noise approaches (SeqDiffuSeq's epoch-dependent schedules, Dinoiser's clipping thresholds) by making noise contextualized to each conditional sentence's semantics rather than following hand-crafted rules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play property of the scheduler—trained on one dataset yet improving inference of DiffuSeq models trained on different datasets without fine-tuning—offers a practical, low-cost enhancement path for existing S2S-Diffusion deployments. The finding that less noise benefits harder sentences while more noise aids easier ones provides an interpretable mechanism for why contextualized scheduling outperforms uniform schedules. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to discrete diffusion models (e.g., D3PM-based RDM) beyond the DiffuSeq family, whether the simple LSTM scheduler can be replaced by more expressive architectures, and whether the 5% training-time overhead remains acceptable at larger scales.
