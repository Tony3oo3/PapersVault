## Problem

Sign-based stochastic gradient descent (signSGD) achieves a convergence rate of O(d^{1/2}T^{-1/4}) in the l1-norm, but it remains unclear whether variance reduction techniques—which improve standard SGD to O(T^{-1/3}) under average smoothness—can similarly accelerate sign-based methods. Additionally, existing sign-based approaches for finite-sum problems (e.g., SignSVRG) have suboptimal dependence on the number of component functions m, and in distributed heterogeneous settings, prior methods either fail to converge to zero or suffer from high sample complexity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes stochastic gradient samples ∇f(x;ξ) from a smooth non-convex objective, along with hyperparameters (learning rate η, momentum β, batch sizes B₀ and B₁), and produces an optimized parameter vector x_τ selected uniformly at random from all iterates. A key constraint is that only the sign (1-bit per coordinate) of the variance-reduced estimator is used for the update, preserving communication efficiency. In the distributed variant, each of n nodes transmits only a 1-bit sign (or stochastic unbiased sign) to the parameter server, which aggregates via majority vote and broadcasts a single sign vector back.

## Method

The core algorithm (SSVR) maintains a STORM-style variance-reduced gradient estimator v_t that recursively combines a fresh stochastic gradient with a momentum-corrected previous estimate, then applies the Sign(·) operation to v_t for the parameter update. For finite-sum problems (SSVR-FS), the estimator incorporates an SVRG-style error correction term β(∇f_{i_t}(x_τ) − ∇f(x_τ)) computed at periodic full-gradient steps, reducing the gap between component and overall gradients. In the distributed heterogeneous setting (SSVR-MV), the paper introduces a stochastic unbiased sign mapping S_R(v) that outputs ±1 with probability proportional to v_k/R, preserving 1-bit communication while ensuring E[S_R(v)] = v/R; this is applied at both the worker level and (in Option 2) the server level to eliminate the bias amplification that causes standard double-sign majority vote to fail under data heterogeneity.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical convergence rate improvements for sign-based optimization. For stochastic non-convex problems, SSVR achieves O(d^{1/2}T^{-1/3}) in l1-norm, improving over signSGD's O(d^{1/2}T^{-1/4}). For finite-sum problems, SSVR-FS achieves O(m^{1/4}d^{1/2}T^{-1/2}), matching the known lower bound and improving over SignSVRG's O(m^{1/2}d^{1/2}T^{-1/2}). In the heterogeneous distributed setting, SSVR-MV attains O(d^{1/2}T^{-1/2}+dn^{-1/2}) and O(d^{1/4}T^{-1/4}), outperforming prior rates of O(dT^{-1/4}+dn^{-1/2}) and O(d^{3/8}T^{-1/8}). Empirical validation on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18) and CIFAR-100 (ResNet-50) confirms faster gradient norm reduction and higher test accuracy compared to baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSVR strictly improves upon signSGD [Bernstein et al., 2018] by upgrading the convergence rate from O(d^{1/2}T^{-1/4}) to O(d^{1/2}T^{-1/3}) under the average smoothness assumption, and also outperforms EF-signSGD [Karimireddy et al., 2019] which achieves only O(1/N^{1/4}) in l2-norm. For finite-sum problems, SSVR-FS improves the dependence on m from m^{1/2} (SignSVRG [Chzhen & Schechtman, 2023] and SignRVR/SignRVM [Qin et al., 2023]) to m^{1/4}, matching the known lower bound. In the heterogeneous distributed setting, SSVR-MV (Option 1) improves over MV-signSGD-SIM [Sun et al., 2023] from O(dT^{-1/4}+dn^{-1/2}) to O(d^{1/2}T^{-1/2}+dn^{-1/2}), and SSVR-MV (Option 2) improves over Sto-signSGD [Jin et al., 2023] from O(d^{3/8}T^{-1/8}) to O(d^{1/4}T^{-1/4}), which is the first rate that converges to zero as T→∞. SSVR-MV also outperforms SSDM [Safaryan & Richtarik, 2021] and Signum [Bernstein et al., 2019], which only handle homogeneous settings or require non-sign information in the server-to-worker direction. In the empirical experiments, SSVR and SSVR-FS show faster gradient norm decay and higher test accuracy than signSGD, signSGD-SIM, and SignSVRG on CIFAR-10, while SSVR-MV achieves the best test accuracy among all six compared methods on CIFAR-100 with both 4 and 8 nodes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: sign-based optimization (signSGD, Signum, signSGD-SIM) and stochastic variance reduction (STORM, SVRG, SPIDER). The core conceptual contribution is combining the STORM-style recursive gradient estimator with sign-based updates, a natural but previously unexplored combination. For the finite-sum setting, the method extends SVRG's periodic full-gradient correction into the sign-based framework, improving upon SignSVRG's suboptimal m-dependence. In the distributed heterogeneous setting, the paper introduces stochastic unbiased sign operations (S_R) to replace deterministic sign operations in both workers and the parameter server, addressing the bias amplification that causes signSGD to fail under data heterogeneity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The convergence rate improvements (T^{-1/4} → T^{-1/3} for stochastic, m^{1/2} → m^{1/4} for finite-sum, and dT^{-1/4} → d^{1/2}T^{-1/2} for distributed) are meaningful for communication-efficient training where 1-bit compression is critical. However, the methods require average smoothness and bounded gradient assumptions, and the experimental validation is limited to CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-18/50, leaving open whether the theoretical gains translate to large-scale vision or language tasks. An open question is whether the T^{-1/3} rate is tight for sign-based methods, and whether the unbiased sign operations introduce practical overhead that offsets the theoretical gains in real distributed systems.
