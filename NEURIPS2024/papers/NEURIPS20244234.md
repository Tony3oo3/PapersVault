## Problem

Wind farm control via reinforcement learning has produced promising results, but no open-source, standardized multi-agent RL environment exists for the general problem. Conventional model-based control suffers from the curse of dimensionality as turbine counts grow, and most prior RL approaches are evaluated only on static simulators, leaving it unclear whether learned policies transfer to dynamic conditions or real farms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244234/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each turbine agent receives local observations (wind velocity, direction, and current actuator target for yaw/pitch/torque) plus a global observation containing free-stream wind conditions and all other turbines' states. Actions are continuous deltas (e.g., [-5, 5]° for yaw) rather than absolute setpoints, reflecting physical actuation-rate limits; the environment rejects actions that would exceed a 10% duty-cycle constraint. Rewards combine normalized farm power production with a load penalty that uses turbulence-based proxies on FLORIS and blade bending-moment estimates on FAST.Farm.

## Method

WFCRL is a customizable benchmark suite (not a single algorithm) that frames wind farm control as a cooperative Dec-POMDP with one agent per turbine, implemented via Gymnasium and PettingZoo interfaces. It provides two simulation backends of different fidelity—FLORIS (static, instantaneous wake solutions) and FAST.Farm (dynamic, time-dependent wake propagation with structural load estimates)—connected through an MPI-based Python interface, enabling transfer-learning experiments from low- to high-fidelity simulation. The suite ships 10 pre-registered layouts (5 real farms, 7–91 turbines, all NREL 5 MW reference turbines), three wind-condition scenarios, and a RewardShaper class for custom reward design. For the benchmark example (wake steering with fatigue costs), the paper implements and compares IPPO, MAPPO, QMIX, IDQN, and IDRQN, finding that MAPPO's shared critic becomes more beneficial as agent count grows and that naive fine-tuning on FAST.Farm degrades policies trained on FLORIS, highlighting the difficulty of sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

On the Turb3Row1 layout (FLORIS, Scenario I), IPPO achieves the highest evaluation score of 239.5±2.4, slightly ahead of MAPPO (237.7±2.1), while value-based methods (QMIX: 193.5±17.0, IDQN: 202.6±21.5, IDRQN: 210.6±20.7) lag by 25–46 points. On the larger Ablaincourt layout (7 turbines), MAPPO edges out IPPO on Scenario I (351.7 vs. 351.0) and on FAST.Farm (330.2 vs. 327.4), but IPPO leads on Scenario II (329.9 vs. 327.6). Deploying FLORIS-trained IPPO policies on the dynamic FAST.Farm simulator yields a 15% power increase over the greedy baseline, yet naive fine-tuning on FAST.Farm degrades performance to 0% over baseline after 40k steps, highlighting the sim-to-real gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPPO (independent PPO) and MAPPO (multi-agent PPO with shared critic) are the primary actor-critic baselines, both significantly outperforming the value-based methods QMIX, IDQN, and IDRQN across all layouts and simulators. On the small Turb3Row1 layout, IPPO consistently achieves the best scores (e.g., 239.5 vs. 237.7 for MAPPO on FLORIS Sc.1; 217.4 vs. 215.3 on FAST.Farm), confirming prior findings that independent learning suffices for few agents. On the larger Ablaincourt layout (7 agents), MAPPO's shared critic becomes advantageous, winning 2 of 3 evaluation settings (FLORIS Sc.1: 351.7 vs. 351.0; FAST.Farm: 330.2 vs. 327.4), suggesting centralized value estimation helps as agent count grows. Value-based methods (QMIX, IDQN, IDRQN) trail actor-critic methods by 25–46 points on FLORIS and by 30–55 points on FAST.Farm, where noisier time-dependent observations further penalize discrete-action approaches. The transfer experiment shows that fine-tuning IPPO policies on FAST.Farm for 40k steps degrades performance from a 15% power gain to 0% over the greedy baseline, indicating that naive online adaptation to unseen dynamics is insufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the multi-agent PPO lineage (IPPO, MAPPO from [8, 47]) and value-based MARL (QMIX [34], DQN [27]) to create the first open-source RL environment suite for wind farm control. Prior RL applications to wind farms [1, 5, 9, 11, 22, 29, 31, 39] were either single-agent, used proprietary or single-simulator setups, or were evaluated exclusively on static models. WFCRL departs from this by unifying two simulators of different fidelity (FLORIS static, FAST.Farm dynamic) under a common Dec-POMDP interface, explicitly enabling transfer learning experiments that prior work had not systematically addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a critical gap: no open, general-purpose RL environment for wind farm control previously existed, and the sim-to-real gap (static → dynamic → real farm) was largely unexplored. The negative transfer result—naive fine-tuning on FAST.Farm degrades IPPO policies to 0% gain versus 15% for direct deployment—highlights that bridging model-fidelity gaps is non-trivial and motivates more robust transfer strategies. Open questions include whether faster dynamic simulators (e.g., ML-accelerated) will make online learning on high-fidelity models feasible, and whether the observed scaling behavior of MAPPO's shared critic holds at the 80–91 turbine scale of real offshore farms.
