## Problem

Existing text-to-video methods treat video as a sequence of 2D frames or use LLM-generated 2D bounding boxes for layout, making it difficult to precisely control individual concepts such as specific character motion, appearance, and viewpoint. The paper identifies a fundamental gap: no prior work explicitly composes video concepts in 3D space, where each concept (scene, object, motion) is independently generated and then assembled with physically meaningful spatial relationships.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244183/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single free-form text prompt as input and produces a real-time renderable 3D Gaussian scene at 512×512 resolution, where each concept (background scene, humanoid object, SMPL-X motion) exists as a separate editable 3D asset. Because the output is an explicit 3D representation rather than a fixed camera video, the user can freely change viewpoint, swap individual concepts (e.g., replace the actor's appearance or motion), and re-render without regenerating the entire video.

## Method

C3V operates in three stages. First, an LLM (GPT-4V) decomposes the prompt into sub-prompts for scene, object, and motion, then dispatches each to a pre-trained expert model (LucidDreamer for 3D Gaussian scenes, HumanGaussian for drivable human avatars, Motion-X retrieval for SMPL-X motion parameters). Second, a multi-modal LLM estimates coarse object scale and 2D trajectory using a step-by-step reasoning strategy—first predicting start and end points, then interpolating N=20 intermediate locations—while representing the object as a bounding box to compensate for GPT-4V's weak visual-dynamics priors. Third, the 2D trajectory is lifted to 3D via monocular depth estimation, and Score Distillation Sampling with Stable Diffusion refines each object's scale, per-frame location, and rotation (computed from consecutive-frame direction) to align the rendered composition with natural image distributions.

## Results

C3V achieves the highest scores across all five evaluation metrics in Table 1. On Q-Align video quality (1–5 scale), C3V scores 4.112, outperforming the closest baseline VideoCrafter2 (3.868) and substantially exceeding 4D-FY (2.154) and Comp4D (2.142). On CLIP Score (text-video alignment), C3V reaches 38.36, compared to 35.20 for VideoCrafter2, 30.47 for 4D-FY, and 27.50 for Comp4D. The Q-Align image aesthetic score also shows a clear margin: 3.471 for C3V versus 3.199 for VideoCrafter2 and below 1.5 for the 4D-generation baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C3V outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against VideoCrafter2 (the strongest baseline, a text-to-video diffusion model), C3V gains +0.244 on Q-Align video quality, +0.564 on Q-Align video aesthetic, and +3.16 on CLIP Score, indicating better visual fidelity and stronger text-video alignment. Against the compositional 4D baselines 4D-FY and Comp4D, the margins are much larger (e.g., +1.958 on Q-Align video quality over 4D-FY, +1.970 over Comp4D), which the authors attribute to these methods being object-centric and failing to produce natural backgrounds or diverse multi-object motion. Qualitatively, C3V also succeeds on complex multi-character prompts (e.g., four characters with distinct motions on a stage) where all three baselines fail to respect the number of objects or their corresponding motions.

## Relation to Prior Work

C3V departs from prior LLM-guided video generation (LVD, VideoDirectorGPT, Free-Bloom) that operates in 2D space by explicitly representing each concept as an independent 3D asset (3D Gaussians, SMPL-X parameters) and composing them in 3D. It builds on Score Distillation Sampling (DreamFusion) and 3D Gaussian Splatting for the 3D representation and refinement, while extending compositional 3D generation (Gala3D, GraphDreamer) from static scenes to dynamic video. Concurrent works Comp4D and TC4D also tackle compositional 4D generation, but C3V differs by using LLM priors for trajectory estimation rather than kinematic equations or user-defined paths, and by integrating scene, object, and motion as separately generated concepts under a unified LLM director.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes a new paradigm where LLMs serve as directors for 3D structural composition, enabling per-concept editing (appearance, motion, scene) and viewpoint control that are intractable for implicit 2D video models. Key limitations include restriction to humanoid objects, absence of physically grounded dynamics, and inability to handle multi-scene long-form generation. Open questions include generalizing to diverse object categories (animals, vehicles), introducing physics-based interaction between composed concepts, and scaling to full-length narrative video generation.
