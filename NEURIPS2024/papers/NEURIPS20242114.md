## Problem

Neural networks learn data representations on low-dimensional manifolds, but modeling the relationship between these representational spaces remains an open challenge. Existing similarity measures like CKA are sensitive to local perturbations that preserve linear separability, and alignment methods operate in the sample domain where complex geometric constraints are difficult to express. The paper asks whether shifting to the functional (spectral) domain—where correspondences between manifolds become linear maps—can simultaneously improve interpretability, robustness, and downstream task performance for representation alignment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two sets of latent space samples (typically 2K–3K points per space) extracted from pre-trained or trained neural network layers, along with optional anchor correspondences (as few as 5 pairs) or class labels. Internally, each sample set is converted into a symmetric k-NN graph (k≈300) using angular or L2 distance, and the first k eigenvectors of the normalized graph Laplacian form the spectral basis in which all descriptors and the map are expressed. The primary output is a k×k functional map matrix C in the spectral domain, from which the paper derives a scalar similarity score, point-to-point correspondences via nearest-neighbor lookup in the spectral basis, and an orthogonal (or linear/affine) transformation for transferring representations between spaces.

## Method

The core pipeline builds a symmetric k-NN graph in each latent space, computes the eigendecomposition of the normalized graph Laplacian to obtain an orthonormal spectral basis, and then solves a regularized least-squares problem for the functional map C that aligns descriptor functions expressed in these bases. Two key regularizers enforce commutativity: the Laplacian commutativity term ‖Λ_Y C − C Λ_X‖² preserves spectral structure, and the descriptor commutativity term ‖S_Y C − C S_X‖² extracts more information from each descriptor. Descriptors can be supervised (geodesic distance to anchor points), weakly supervised (class label indicator functions), or fully unsupervised (heat kernel signatures), making the framework applicable across supervision levels. After solving the optimization, the map is refined via iterative spectral upsampling (ZoomOut), which progressively increases the number of eigenvectors and recovers a point-to-point correspondence by nearest-neighbor matching in the spectral basis. The key insight is that in the functional domain, many geometric constraints that are nonlinear in the sample domain become linear in C, enabling efficient closed-form or convex optimization while retaining interpretability through the off-diagonal structure of CᵀC.

## Results

On the word embedding retrieval task (FastText vs. Word2Vec, 20K words), LFM+Ortho achieves an MRR of 0.99 with only 5 anchors, while the direct Ortho baseline scores 0.01 at the same anchor count and only reaches 0.97 at 300 anchors. In zero-shot stitching across six datasets (CIFAR-100, ImageNet, CUB, MNIST, AgNews), LFM+Ortho consistently outperforms the Ortho baseline, with the gap most pronounced at ≤50 anchors; even the label-only variant (LFM+Ortho Labels) achieves strong performance without any anchor correspondences. For the similarity robustness experiment, LFM similarity remains stable under perturbations that preserve linear separability, whereas CKA degrades significantly as perturbation strength increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Ortho baseline [32] (direct orthogonal transformation from anchors), LFM+Ortho achieves MRR 0.99 at 5 anchors versus 0.01 for Ortho, and maintains this advantage up to 200 anchors where Ortho reaches 0.93. The Relatives method [39] (relative representations for zero-shot communication) scores 0.01 at 5 anchors and only 0.90 at 300 anchors, well below LFM+Ortho's 0.99. For the similarity comparison, LFM outperforms CKA [23] in robustness: under perturbations preserving linear separability, CKA similarity degrades with perturbation strength while LFM similarity remains stable, and LFM also achieves slightly higher accuracy in identifying corresponding layers across 10 CNN models on CIFAR-10. Against CCA [18], LFM shows less spread in the similarity matrix around the diagonal, yielding higher layer-matching accuracy. In stitching experiments, the Ortho baseline only matches LFM's performance when more than 50 anchors are provided, at which point LFM's effectiveness is constrained by the number of eigenvectors used.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the functional maps framework from 3D shape correspondence (Ovsjanikov et al., 2012) into the neural representation learning domain, using graph Laplacian eigenvectors as the spectral basis for latent spaces. It builds directly on the zero-shot latent space communication paradigm (Moschella et al., 2023) and the orthogonal transformation approach (Maiorca et al., 2024), but shifts the perspective from relating individual samples to relating function spaces defined on the underlying manifolds. The spectral upsampling refinement (Melzi et al., 2019) and descriptor commutativity regularizer (Nogneng & Ovsjanikov, 2017) are carried over from the geometry processing literature, while the k-NN graph construction and graph Laplacian eigen-decomposition provide the discrete manifold approximation. The key conceptual departure is treating representation alignment as a problem in the dual (functional) space, where constraints become linear and the resulting map C simultaneously serves as a similarity measure, a correspondence finder, and a transfer operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework unifies three previously separate tasks—representation similarity measurement, correspondence discovery, and cross-model transfer—under a single spectral optimization, offering practitioners a coherent tool rather than a patchwork of ad-hoc methods. The demonstrated robustness of LFM similarity to perturbations that preserve linear separability (where CKA degrades) addresses a known weakness of the most widely used similarity metric. However, the framework assumes full bijective correspondence between domains, is sensitive to the number of eigenvectors used (with no principled selection criterion yet), and its fully unsupervised performance remains underdeveloped. Extending to partial mappings and finding the optimal spectral truncation without extensive experimentation are the most pressing open questions.
