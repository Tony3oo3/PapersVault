## Problem

Protein representation learning must integrate three modalities—sequence, 3D structure, and function annotations—but existing methods either ignore functional information or cannot handle the extreme data scale disparity (500M+ sequences vs. ~190K structures vs. ~5M GO triplets). A key unresolved challenge is how to transfer functional knowledge into sequence-structure representations when function annotations are unavailable at inference time for most proteins.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242809/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student model consumes a protein's amino acid sequence (one-hot residue types concatenated with 7 physicochemical properties per residue) and Cα 3D coordinates, producing a 2048-dimensional graph-level embedding usable for fold, reaction, GO term, and EC number prediction. The teacher additionally ingests a 2752-dimensional binary GO annotation vector, but this is only needed during training. Edge features are constructed via a local coordinate system (LCS) yielding SE(3)-invariant distance, direction, and orientation descriptors, avoiding explicit equivariant architectures.

## Method

ProtGO employs a teacher-student framework where both networks share an identical GNN with message passing over sequence-structure graphs (edges defined by combined sequential and spatial distance thresholds, with iterative sequence pooling and radius expansion to capture long-range interactions). The teacher augments its GNN embedding with an MLP-encoded GO annotation vector (z_S = h_A + α·h_S), while the student produces embeddings from sequence and structure alone. The core novelty is a distribution-level knowledge distillation loss: rather than aligning individual sample embeddings, the method fits Gaussian distributions to minibatch latent representations and minimizes KL divergence between teacher and student distributions, making the transfer sample-independent. A theoretical bound (Proposition 2) shows the conditional misalignment in representation space is bounded by input-space misalignment, justifying the focus on marginal distribution alignment. The student is trained with a hybrid loss L = L_student + β·KL[p_S(z)‖p_T(z)], combining supervised classification with the domain-adaptation distillation term.

## Results

On fold classification, ProtGO achieves 60.5% accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline CDConv (56.7%) by 3.8 percentage points, and ProNet-Backbone (52.7%) by 7.8 points. On enzyme reaction classification, ProtGO reaches 89.4% accuracy versus 88.5% for CDConv. For GO term prediction (F_max), ProtGO scores 0.464/0.667/0.492 on BP/MF/CC, outperforming CDConv (0.453/0.654/0.479). On EC number prediction, ProtGO achieves F_max of 0.857, a 3.7-point gain over CDConv (0.820).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProtGO (Student) outperforms all 17 baselines across every task and metric. Against the closest competitor CDConv [12], which also uses sequence-structure convolution, ProtGO gains +3.8% on Fold, +1.7% on SuperFamily, +0.2% on Family, and +0.9% on Enzyme Reaction (Table 1), and +0.011/+0.013/+0.013/+0.037 F_max on GO-BP/MF/CC and EC (Table 2). Against ProNet [32] (best structure-aware baseline at 52.7% Fold), the improvement is +7.8% on Fold and +3.0% on Enzyme Reaction. Against GearNet variants [17], ProtGO leads by 12.2–22.2% on Fold and 2.8–14.1% on Enzyme Reaction. Sequence-only methods (CNN, ResNet, LSTM, Transformer [27,61]) and structure-only methods (GCN, GAT, 3DCNN_MQA [62,63,64]) are far behind, confirming the necessity of joint sequence-structure modeling. In the pre-training comparison (Table 5, Appendix G), ProtGO-ESM (integrating ESM-2 embeddings) achieves the best F_max on GO-BP (0.520), GO-MF (0.693), and GO-CC (0.536), but trails GearNet-ESM-INR-MC [76] on EC number prediction (0.887 vs. 0.896), indicating a slight weakness in fine-grained enzymatic classification when pre-trained sequence features are added.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtGO extends the protein graph neural network line (GearNet, CDConv, ProNet) by adding a function modality through a teacher-student knowledge distillation framework. It departs from prior multimodal protein models (MASSA, ProtST) that align sequence-structure embeddings with GO embeddings via explicit pre-training objectives, instead using KL-divergence-based distribution alignment in latent space to transfer functional knowledge from a teacher (GNN + GO annotation encoder) to a student (GNN only). The local coordinate system for invariant edge features is inherited from Ingraham et al., and the domain adaptation mechanism draws on KL-guided distribution matching rather than adversarial or sample-level alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck: functional annotations are available for only a small fraction of proteins, yet the student model can be deployed without them, making it broadly applicable to downstream tasks. The distribution-level (rather than sample-level) alignment is a design choice that avoids per-sample correspondence requirements, which is relevant given the severe data scale disparity across sequence, structure, and function modalities. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the absence of large-scale pre-training; integrating ProtGO with pretrained encoders (demonstrated with ESM-2 in Appendix G) is identified as the natural next step, and whether the KL-based distillation scales effectively to much larger protein corpora remains open.
