## Problem

Existing RAG benchmarks and Q&A datasets typically assume clean, pre-segmented inputs or rely on Wikipedia sources that overlap with LLMs' internal knowledge, failing to capture the full complexity of real-world document analysis involving raw PDFs and HTML with intricate layouts, tables, and hundreds of pages. This paper addresses that gap by introducing a benchmark that evaluates the complete RAG pipeline—from parsing unstructured documents through retrieval to generation—across finance, academia, and world knowledge domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242144/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation item is a triplet (D, q, a) where D is a complete unstructured document in its original file format (PDF or HTML, ranging from ~6k words/11 pages for academic papers to ~77k words/148 pages for financial reports), q is a question, and a is the ground-truth answer. Questions span five types—extractive, yes/no, free-form, counting, and arithmetic reasoning—and answers range from single entities to multi-step numerical computations. Evaluation employs span-level F1, exact match with 1% numerical tolerance, LCS-based retrieval quality measurement, and an LLM-based 0-to-4 scoring rubric for natural-language answers.

## Method

The UDA benchmark comprises 2,965 documents and 29,590 expert-annotated Q&A pairs across six sub-datasets, constructed by identifying and verifying original source documents from platforms like Wikipedia and arXiv, then matching them to Q&A labels from existing datasets (FinQA, TAT-DQA, Qasper, FetaQA, Natural Questions). The evaluation systematically decomposes the RAG pipeline into five axes: table parsing strategies (raw text, CV-based, CV+LLM, GPT-4-Omni), retrieval models (BM-25, SentenceTransformer embeddings, OpenAI text-embedding-3-large, ColBERT), generation strategies (basic, Chain-of-Thought, Code Interpreter), RAG versus long-context LLMs, and end-to-end performance across eight LLMs. Key findings include that BM-25 outperforms dense embeddings on financial queries with precise keywords, CoT significantly improves numerical reasoning for smaller models (e.g., Llama-3-8B gains 16.6 points over basic generation), and long-context LLMs underperform RAG on arithmetic tasks because verbose content hinders fact pinpointing.

## Results

In the end-to-end RAG evaluation across six sub-datasets (2,503 Q&A pairs, 1,201 documents), GPT-4-Turbo achieves the highest average score of 45.7, followed by Llama-3-70B (42.5) and GPT-3.5 (40.9). Removing RAG causes a dramatic collapse: GPT-4 drops from 45.9 to 0.4 on FinHybrid and Llama-3-8B from 37.9 to 3.6, confirming heavy reliance on external documents. For retrieval, OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large leads on most datasets (e.g., 80.1 LCS@5 on FinHybrid), while BM-25 surprisingly outperforms all dense models on FinHybrid (83.7 LCS@5). Chain-of-Thought prompting yields the largest generation gains on numerical tasks (Llama-3-8B: 21.3→37.9 EM on FinHybrid), while long-context LLMs underperform RAG on arithmetic-heavy financial Q&A (Qwen-1.5-7B: 3.0 vs. 21.0 on FinHybrid).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among LLMs, GPT-4-Turbo leads overall (45.7 avg) but Llama-3-70B is competitive (42.5) and even surpasses GPT-4 on FetaTab (63.3 vs. 61.5); Qwen-1.5-32B excels on NqText (41.3) despite a lower average. For table parsing, GPT-4-Omni achieves the best scores (72.4 EM on FinHybrid, 44.3 F1 on PaperTab), while the CV-based TableTransformer pipeline [51] underperforms raw-text extraction (61.3 vs. 68.0 EM on FinHybrid), indicating that un-tuned CV methods struggle with irregular table layouts. In retrieval, OpenAI text-embedding-3-large dominates dense retrieval (80.1 LCS@5 on FinHybrid), outperforming SentenceTransformer models (all-MiniLM-L6: 71.8; all-mpnet-base: 74.7) and ColBERT (75.0), though BM-25 wins on FinHybrid (83.7) due to precise keyword matching in financial queries. For generation strategies, Chain-of-Thought [57] consistently outperforms both basic generation and Code Interpreter [65] on FinHybrid (Llama-3-8B: 37.9 CoT vs. 21.3 basic vs. 26.4 code), while CodeLlama models [45] fail to generate viable code in this unstructured-document setting (5.5–11.3 EM). Long-context LLMs (GPT-4-Turbo 128k, Qwen-1.5-7B 32k) match RAG on knowledge-based tasks but fall significantly behind on numerical reasoning (Qwen-1.5-7B: 3.0 long-context vs. 21.0 RAG on FinHybrid), suggesting that verbose context hinders arithmetic fact-finding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by constructing a benchmark (UDA) that preserves documents in their original unstructured PDF/HTML formats, deliberately departing from prior Q&A datasets (FinQA, TAT-DQA, Qasper, FetaQA, Natural Questions) that provide pre-parsed, segmented, or well-structured content. It also extends prior RAG benchmarks (RGB, CRUD-RAG, ALCE) by evaluating the full pipeline—parsing, chunking, indexing, retrieval, and generation—rather than isolating a single component. Conceptually, it repurposes existing source datasets by performing source-document identification, verification, and reorganization to reconstruct complete unstructured documents, rather than proposing a new generation or retrieval method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UDA provides the first comprehensive benchmark for end-to-end RAG on raw unstructured documents spanning finance, academia, and world knowledge, filling a gap where prior evaluations assumed clean inputs. Key practical findings—raw text extraction rivaling advanced CV parsing, BM-25 outperforming dense embeddings on financial queries, and long-context LLMs underperforming RAG on numerical reasoning—offer actionable guidance for production RAG systems. Open questions remain around noise sensitivity, hallucination in document analysis, and whether the retrieval-quality-to-answer-quality relationship holds across more diverse document types and languages.
