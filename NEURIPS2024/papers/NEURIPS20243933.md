## Problem

Plane-based explicit representations for dynamic Gaussian splatting (e.g., 4D-GS) decompose 4D space-time encoding into six 2D planes under a low-rank assumption, causing excessive feature overlap that limits discriminability for deformation prediction. This results in poor rendering quality, particularly for scenes with complex motions and heavily overlapping Gaussian coordinates, while a direct 4D hyper-grid encoding is infeasible due to O(n⁴) space complexity and high hash collision rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243933/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes canonical 3D Gaussians (position, scaling, rotation, opacity, SH color coefficients) initialized from SfM points or static training, along with a scalar timestamp t and camera pose for rendering. It outputs deformed Gaussians where position is updated via a quaternion-defined rotation matrix plus translation vector, and scaling/rotation receive additive deltas. Rendering resolutions are dataset-specific (800×800 for D-NeRF, 536×900 for HyperNeRF, 1352×1024 for Neu3D), and the model is trained with L1 color loss, D-SSIM loss, and a smooth regularization term on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Method

Grid4D decomposes the 4D input (x,y,z,t) into four 3D multiresolution hash encodings — one spatial (x,y,z) and three temporal (x,y,t), (y,z,t), (x,z,t) — reducing feature overlap from half to a quarter compared to plane-based methods while maintaining O(n³) space complexity. A directional attention module uses the spatial features to generate attention scores scaled to (−1,1) rather than the standard (0,1), enabling the model to capture opposite-direction deformations between neighboring scene components (e.g., shadows vs. objects). The attention-modulated temporal features are decoded by a tiny multi-head MLP into a quaternion rotation, translation, and scaling/rotation deltas. A smooth regularization loss is applied directly in the hash-encoder feature space (with small random perturbations to the 4D input) rather than in the decoder, exploiting the MLP's inherent smoothness to avoid redundant computation while mitigating the characteristic deformation chaos of explicit representations.

## Results

On the synthetic D-NeRF dataset, Grid4D achieves a mean PSNR of 42.00 dB, SSIM of 0.994, and LPIPS of 0.008, outperforming the strongest baseline SC-GS (41.65 dB) and DeformGS (40.16 dB). On the real-world HyperNeRF dataset, Grid4D reaches 25.50 dB PSNR and 0.856 MS-SSIM on the rig split, and 28.56 dB / 0.933 on the interpolation split, surpassing 4D-GS (24.99 / 27.54) and TiNeuVox (24.20 / 27.08). On Neu3D, Grid4D consistently improves over 4D-GS by 0.5–1.1 dB PSNR across all six scenes. Grid4D also maintains real-time rendering (e.g., 334 FPS on Hell Warrior with 46k Gaussians) despite using substantially more Gaussians than 4D-GS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Grid4D outperforms all five baselines on the D-NeRF mean PSNR, beating the closest competitor SC-GS by 0.35 dB and the implicit DeformGS by 1.84 dB. On HyperNeRF, Grid4D leads 4D-GS by 0.51 dB (rig) and 1.02 dB (interpolation) in PSNR, and also surpasses TiNeuVox by 1.30 dB on the rig split; notably, DeformGS fails to converge on several HyperNeRF scenes with large motions. On Neu3D, Grid4D improves over 4D-GS by 0.96 dB on Coffee, 1.13 dB on Flame, and 0.96 dB on Cut Beef, while matching or slightly trailing on Sear Steak (33.16 vs 33.44). The plane-based 4D-GS and HexPlane are consistently the weakest baselines, confirming the paper's argument that the low-rank plane decomposition limits discriminability. Grid4D also renders at higher FPS than DeformGS despite using 2–5× more Gaussians (e.g., 91 FPS / 192k vs 37 FPS / 180k on Bouncing Balls), demonstrating the efficiency advantage of the explicit hash-encoding deformation field.

## Relation to Prior Work

Grid4D builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting for the rendering backbone and InstantNGP-style multiresolution hash encoding for explicit feature storage, but departs fundamentally from the plane-based decomposition strategy used by 4D-GS, HexPlane, and K-Planes, which factorize 4D space-time into six 2D planes under a low-rank assumption. Instead, it introduces a tri-axial decomposition into one spatial and three temporal 3D hash encodings, reducing feature overlap without incurring the O(n⁴) cost of a full 4D hyper-grid. The directional attention module (scoring in (-1,1) rather than (0,1)) and the smooth regularization loss in feature space are novel components not present in prior Gaussian deformation or explicit-representation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, well-motivated fix for a known weakness of plane-based dynamic scene models—excessive feature overlap under the low-rank assumption—while preserving real-time rendering speed. However, it does not improve training speed over DeformGS, and the authors acknowledge persistent artifacts on scenes with large, complex motions, leaving open the question of whether hash-encoding-based explicit representations can scale to highly deformable or topologically changing scenes. The technique is likely to be adopted as a drop-in replacement for plane-based encoders in future Gaussian splatting pipelines.
