## Problem

Offline RL methods assume clean datasets, but real-world data suffers from diverse corruptions (noise or adversarial attacks) across states, actions, rewards, and dynamics simultaneously. Existing corruption-robust offline RL methods either target only specific corruption types, focus on testing-time robustness, or fail to capture the high uncertainty induced by the simultaneous presence of all four corrupted elements, leading to performance degradation in clean environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241255/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a corrupted offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') tuples where any fraction c of samples may have perturbations of scale ε applied to one or all four elements. It outputs a policy π(a|s) for decision-making, an action-value distribution D(s,a,r) parameterized by N quantile points across K ensemble networks, and a value distribution Z(s) with the same quantile structure. During training, each sample's loss is weighted by the reciprocal of the exponential entropy of its action-value distribution, effectively down-weighting corrupted samples without requiring explicit corruption labels.

## Method

TRACER introduces variational Bayesian inference into offline RL by treating all offline data elements (states, actions, rewards) as observations to approximate the posterior of the action-value distribution. An observation model with three sub-networks reconstructs each data element conditioned on the action-value distribution, and the training loss combines KL-divergence terms (matching reconstructed distributions to data distributions) with Huber-regularized reconstruction terms. The key robustness mechanism is an entropy-based uncertainty measure: the differential entropy of each sample's action-value distribution is estimated from its quantile points, and the reciprocal of the exponential entropy weights the per-sample loss, automatically reducing the influence of corrupted data that induces higher entropy. The policy is then learned via IQL's weighted imitation learning using the α-quantile of the ensemble action-value distributions, and a theoretical bound (Theorem A.4) shows the Wasserstein-1 distance between clean and corrupted value distributions scales with the cumulative corruption level.

## Results

Under random and adversarial simultaneous corruptions across HalfCheetah, Walker2d, and Hopper (D4RL medium-replay-v2), TRACER achieves an average normalized score of 24.54, outperforming the strongest baseline RIQL (20.27) by +21.1% and IQL (18.28) by +34.2%. Under individual random corruptions, TRACER reaches an average of 61.1 vs. RIQL's 60.0 and IQL's 43.6; under individual adversarial corruptions, it scores 58.1 vs. RIQL's 56.2 and IQL's 33.1. TRACER outperforms all baselines in 7 out of 12 individual corruption settings for both random and adversarial attacks, and dominates in all 6 simultaneous corruption settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACER consistently outperforms all six baselines under simultaneous corruptions (Table 1), with the largest margins over EDAC (0.65) and CQL (4.66), which collapse under multi-element corruption. Against the closest competitor RIQL (20.27 avg), TRACER gains +4.27 points on average, with the most pronounced advantage on HalfCheetah-random (+3.10) and Walker2d-random (+6.20). IQL (18.28) and UWMSG (7.11) suffer severe degradation under simultaneous attacks, as they were not designed to handle all four corrupted elements jointly. Under individual corruptions (Tables 2–3), TRACER wins 7/12 settings for both random and adversarial attacks, with notable gains over RIQL on Walker2d-action (86.7 vs. 84.6 random; 69.3 vs. 66.1 adversarial) and Hopper-observation (62.7 vs. 62.4 random; 64.5 vs. 50.8 adversarial). EDAC and MSG, which rely on ensemble Q-networks without corruption-specific mechanisms, perform poorly across most individual corruption types, often scoring near zero on dynamics and observation corruptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRACER extends the corruption-robust offline RL framework established by RIQL (Yang et al., 2023), which first addressed all four data elements (states, actions, rewards, dynamics) using quantile estimators and Huber regression, and builds on IQL's (Kostrikov et al., 2022) weighted imitation learning for policy extraction. The key departure is the introduction of variational Bayesian inference—leveraging generalized variational inference (Knoblauch et al., 2019) to exchange KL divergence terms—into this setting, modeling the full posterior over action-value distributions rather than point estimates or ensemble spreads. This is combined with distributional RL via quantile regression (Dabney et al., 2018) to represent the action-value distribution, enabling an entropy-based uncertainty measure that prior corruption-robust methods (UWMSG, RIQL) did not exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to bring Bayesian inference into corruption-robust offline RL, providing a principled mechanism (entropy-weighted loss) to down-weight corrupted samples without requiring explicit corruption labels. The Wasserstein-distance bound (Theorem A.4) gives a theoretical guarantee linking cumulative corruption level to performance degradation, which is valuable for safety-critical deployments. However, the approach is tested only on MuJoCo locomotion and CARLA driving with a fixed corruption rate (c=0.3) and scale (ε=1.0), and the authors themselves note that more realistic scenarios—such as noise in RLHF preference data or adversarial attacks on safety-critical driving decisions—remain open. The computational overhead of maintaining an ensemble of distributional critics plus three observation models (for states, actions, rewards) may limit scalability to high-dimensional or continuous-action settings.
