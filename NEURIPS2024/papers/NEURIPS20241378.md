## Problem

Domain generalisation in computational histopathology is challenging because non-biological factors (staining, fixation, scanners) cause substantial image variability across hospitals, while CNNs are known to over-rely on texture rather than shape. The paper asks whether explicitly steering a model to focus on nuclear morphology and organisation—features that are biologically critical for cancer detection yet relatively domain-invariant—is sufficient to improve out-of-domain generalisation without requiring multi-hospital training data.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single 270×270 H&E-stained image tile at 40× magnification and outputs a binary tumour vs non-tumour prediction. During training, the model additionally receives a binary nuclear segmentation mask (generated by HoVer-Net on CoNSeP) for each tile, and with 50% probability the input image is multiplied element-wise by its mask so that only nuclear pixels retain colour information. No segmentation is required at test time.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-time regularisation that aligns the feature representations of an H&E image and its nuclear segmentation mask. Concretely, a ResNet-50 backbone processes both the original tile x and its binary nuclear mask x′, and the total loss combines two BCE classification terms (one per input) with an ℓ₂-distance between the flattened penultimate-layer feature maps (just before Global Average Pooling): L = λ‖z − z′‖² + BCE(y,ŷ) + BCE(y,ŷ′). To make the alignment tractable, with probability 0.5 the network receives x·x′ (nuclei-only image) instead of x, progressively teaching the network to map both full and nuclei-only inputs toward the mask embedding. The hyperparameter λ is set to 0 for the first five epochs and 1 thereafter, and the method is architecture-agnostic (also evaluated on ViT-Tiny) and composable as a plug-in with existing S-DG strategies such as L2D or RSC.

## Results

On CAMELYON17 (the primary out-of-domain evaluation), the proposed method achieves 89.2% average tile-level accuracy without photometric augmentation, outperforming the next-best method L2D (77.8%) by over 11 percentage points. With photometric augmentation, the method reaches 91.8% average accuracy, still ahead of L2D-Aug (89.1%). On the external BCSS dataset, Ours-Aug achieves 78.8% average accuracy versus 75.1% for L2D-Aug, and on Ocelot (six non-breast organs), Ours-Aug reaches 70.6% versus 69.1% for L2D-Aug. The method also shows superior robustness to image corruptions and PGD adversarial attacks compared to all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on all three datasets. Without photometric augmentation on CAMELYON17, the gap is largest: Ours (89.2%) beats Macenko (73.3%) by 15.9 points, RandSNA (63.6%) by 25.6 points, and RSC (62.3%) by 26.9 points, while the closest competitor L2D (77.8%) trails by 11.4 points. With augmentation, the margins narrow but the method still leads: Ours-Aug (91.8%) exceeds L2D-Aug (89.1%) by 2.7 points, HoVerNet-Aug (87.4%) and RandSNA-Aug (87.4%) by 4.4 points, DDCA-Aug (86.0%) by 5.8 points, and RSC-Aug (85.9%) by 5.9 points. On BCSS, Ours-Aug (78.8%) leads L2D-Aug (75.1%) by 3.7 points and RandSNA-Aug (72.8%) by 6.0 points. On Ocelot, the advantage over L2D-Aug is smaller (70.6% vs 69.1%), and the method underperforms L2D-Aug on Kidney and Endometrium specifically, suggesting that nuclear morphology alone is less transferable to organs with very different tissue architecture. The method also demonstrates superior adversarial robustness (PGD) and corruption robustness compared to ERM and L2D, the two strongest baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of histopathology domain generalisation work that manipulates colour and stain (Macenko, RandStainNA, DDCA) or generates augmented images (L2D, RSC) by instead using nuclear segmentation masks as a training-time regularisation signal. It repurposes HoVer-Net's segmentation output not as an inference-time feature extractor (as in classical morphometric pipelines) but as a loss-shaping tool: an L2 distance between the embeddings of the original H&E image and its binary nuclear mask steers the network toward shape-based, domain-invariant features. The conceptual grounding comes from the texture-vs-shape bias literature in CNNs and from the well-established biological observation that nuclear morphology is the primary domain-invariant cue pathologists use for cancer diagnosis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is a lightweight, plug-in training modification that requires no segmentation at inference, making it immediately deployable alongside existing S-DG pipelines. The extensive ablations (nuclei blackening, inpainting, embedding subtraction, saliency maps) provide unusually strong evidence that the model genuinely relies on nuclear morphology rather than stain or texture. However, the evaluation is limited to a single binary classification task (tumour vs non-tumour) and primarily one backbone (ResNet-50), with ViT-Tiny results being mixed; whether the approach transfers to grading, survival prediction, or other cancer types remains open. The modest but consistent accuracy gains (~2–3% over L2D-Aug) and notably improved adversarial robustness suggest practical value, but broader validation across tasks and architectures is needed before the approach can be considered a general solution to histopathology domain shift.
