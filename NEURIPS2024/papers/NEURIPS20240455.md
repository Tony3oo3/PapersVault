## Problem

Data curation strategies for language model pretraining (filtering, deduplication, mixing) are typically evaluated by comparing models trained with different architectures, compute budgets, and hyperparameters, making it impossible to isolate which data decisions actually drive performance. Additionally, training set compositions for even open-weight models like Llama and Mistral are increasingly opaque, leaving the community without a clear understanding of what constitutes a high-quality training set.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240455/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Participants receive a random document subset of DCLM-POOL (240T GPT-NeoX tokens extracted from pre-2023 Common Crawl via resiliparse) sized according to their chosen competition scale (469B to 15.7T tokens), and produce a curated training set by applying filtering or mixing operations. The curated dataset is then consumed by a fixed OpenLM training recipe (decoder-only Transformer with scale-specific hyperparameters) to produce a base language model, which is scored on 53 downstream tasks using three aggregate metrics: MMLU 5-shot, CORE centered accuracy (22 tasks), and EXTENDED centered accuracy (all 53 tasks).

## Method

DCLM introduces a controlled benchmark where the training recipe, architecture, and evaluation suite are fixed, so dataset quality is the only variable. The resulting DCLM-BASELINE dataset is constructed through a four-stage pipeline: (1) resiliparse text extraction from raw HTML, (2) RefinedWeb heuristic quality filters, (3) near-duplicate Bloom filter deduplication, and (4) a fastText bigram classifier trained on ~400K documents (positives from OpenHermes 2.5 and ELI5 subreddit, negatives from a random RefinedWeb sample) to retain only the top 10% of documents by classifier score. The key empirical finding is that this simple model-based filter is the dominant performance driver, outperforming perplexity filtering, semantic deduplication, PageRank, and LLM-based scoring by 1–3 CORE points, while mixing in high-quality sources (Wikipedia, Books, StackExchange) actually degrades performance when the CC-only filtering is already strong. The benchmark spans five compute scales (412M to 6.9B parameters) with high rank correlation (Pearson r ≥ 0.84) between small and large scales, enabling iterative data curation at low cost.

## Results

Training a 7B-parameter decoder-only Transformer on 2.6T tokens from DCLM-BASELINE (combined with StarCoder and ProofPile2) achieves 63.7% MMLU 5-shot, 57.1 CORE centered accuracy, and 45.4 EXTENDED centered accuracy. This represents a 6.6 pp improvement over MAP-Neo (57.1% MMLU) while using 40% less compute, and matches Mistral-7B-v0.3 (62.7% MMLU) and approaches Llama 3 8B (66.2% MMLU) trained with 6.6× more tokens. At the 7B-1x competition scale (138B tokens), the fastText OH-2.5+ELI5 filtering pipeline achieves 41.0 CORE and 29.2 MMLU, outperforming all other filtering strategies tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-dataset baselines, DCLM-BASELINE (2.6T tokens) decisively outperforms all prior open-data 7B models: it beats MAP-Neo by 6.6 pp on MMLU (63.7 vs 57.1) with 40% less compute, OLMo-1.7 by 9.7 pp on MMLU, and Falcon by 36.3 pp on MMLU. Against closed-dataset models, DCLM-BASELINE matches Mistral-7B-v0.3 on MMLU (63.7 vs 62.7) and slightly trails Llama 3 8B (63.7 vs 66.2) and Gemma 8B (63.7 vs 64.3) on MMLU, while achieving comparable CORE (57.1 vs 57.6/57.8) and EXTENDED (45.4 vs 46.3/44.6) scores with 6.6× less training compute than Llama 3. The only model that clearly outperforms DCLM-BASELINE across all three metrics is Phi-3 (61.0 CORE, 69.9 MMLU, 57.9 EXTENDED), which uses a different training recipe and data strategy. In the filtering ablation (Table 4), the fastText OH-2.5+ELI5 classifier (30.2 CORE at 1B-1x) outperforms perplexity filtering (29.0), top-k logits (29.2), AskLLM (28.6), BGE-embedding classifiers (27.2), SemDedup (27.1), and PageRank (26.1), establishing model-based quality filtering as the dominant curation strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RefinedWeb's heuristic filtering pipeline as its foundation and adopts fastText (Joulin et al., 2017) as the core quality-filtering classifier, while borrowing the continual pretraining methodology from Pouransari et al. (2024) for context-length extension. Conceptually, it extends the data-centric benchmarking paradigm established by DataComp (vision) and DataPerf to language models at a much larger scale (240T-token pool, 7B models), but departs from prior data curation work by fixing the training recipe to isolate the effect of data choices. Unlike prior datasets (C4, Dolma, RedPajama) that mix in curated sources like Wikipedia, DCLM-BASELINE is derived entirely from Common Crawl, positioning strong filtering as sufficient to replace source mixing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DCLM provides the first controlled, multi-scale testbed for LM data curation, enabling researchers to compare filtering strategies without confounding architecture or hyperparameter differences. The finding that a simple fastText bigram classifier trained on instruction-tuning data (OpenHermes 2.5 + ELI5) outperforms perplexity-based, embedding-based, and LLM-judged filters challenges the assumption that more sophisticated scoring is needed. Key open questions include whether these findings transfer to multilingual, code, and math domains (where the current model underperforms), whether the filtering strategy holds at scales beyond 7B, and how to extend the benchmark to fairness and safety dimensions. The 240T-token DCLM-POOL and the DCLM-BASELINE dataset are likely to become standard resources for the field.
