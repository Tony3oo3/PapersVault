## Problem

Existing generative models for small molecule discovery produce candidates with poor synthesizability, making experimental validation difficult or impossible. While fragment-based GFlowNets can sample large chemical spaces, they operate at the graph level without guaranteeing a feasible synthetic route. The paper addresses this by constraining generation to a predefined space of cheap building blocks and high-yield chemical reactions, ensuring every generated molecule has a straightforward, low-cost synthesis pathway.

## Input / Output

RGFN requires no external input and generates molecules autonomously by sequentially selecting from 350 curated building blocks (priced ≤$200/g, mean $22.52/g) and 17 reaction types (132 SMARTS templates), performing up to 5 in silico reactions per trajectory. The output is a final molecule in SMILES form together with the complete reaction pathway (sequence of reactions and reactants), which directly constitutes a synthetic plan. Generated molecules are scored by GPU-accelerated Vina-GPU 2.1 docking or pretrained proxy models (sEH, DRD2, senolytic) used as the GFlowNet reward.

## Method

RGFN extends the GFlowNet framework by redefining the action space from graph edits to chemical reactions: each trajectory alternates between selecting a reaction template, selecting a compatible reactant from the building block library, performing the in silico reaction via RDKit, and choosing among possible products, until a stop action terminates generation. A graph transformer serves as the shared backbone for the forward policy, with separate MLP heads for fragment selection, reaction selection, and product selection; the backward policy is learned via trajectory balance. A key scalability contribution is an action embedding scheme that linearly encodes MACCS fingerprints of building blocks (rather than treating them as independent indices), enabling the model to exploit structural similarity among fragments and dramatically improving convergence when the library scales from 350 to 8,350 fragments. The chemical language comprises 17 robust, high-yield (75–100%) reactions including amide bond formation, Suzuki-Miyaura, click chemistry, and SuFEx, with surrogate reactants and implicit reactions introduced to handle docking compatibility and unspecified reagents.

## Results

Across four oracle tasks (sEH proxy, senolytic proxy, DRD2 proxy, ClpP docking), RGFN achieves AiZynthFinder retrosynthesis success rates of 0.56, 0.58, 0.65, and 0.87 for top-100 modes, substantially outperforming FGFN (0.14, 0.02, 0.25, 0.76) and FGFN+SA (0.27, 0.13, 0.33, 0.78) while remaining competitive with SyntheMol (0.80, 0.53, 0.56, 0.66) and casVAE (0.82, 0.65, 0.84, 0.87). In terms of reward quality, RGFN matches or exceeds FGFN on all tasks (most notably on senolytics, where FGFN discovers zero high-reward molecules while RGFN finds a wide range of candidates), though it trails the non-synthesizable GraphGA in average reward. For discovered modes (diversity), FGFN still leads, but RGFN outperforms all other methods including SyntheMol and casVAE across every task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FGFN (the closest non-reaction-based GFlowNet baseline), RGFN achieves substantially higher AiZynthFinder scores on three of four tasks (0.56 vs 0.14 on sEH, 0.58 vs 0.02 on senolytics, 0.65 vs 0.25 on ClpP), while matching it on DRD2 (0.87 vs 0.76). On reward quality, RGFN matches or exceeds FGFN on all tasks, with the most dramatic gap on senolytics where FGFN discovers zero high-reward molecules. Against FGFN+SA (which adds SAScore to the reward), RGFN still leads on AiZynthFinder by 0.29–0.45 points, showing that post-hoc SAScore optimization is insufficient for synthesizability. Compared to the other reaction-based methods, RGFN outperforms SyntheMol on reward (Figures 3a–d) and casVAE on all reward metrics, though casVAE achieves slightly higher AiZynth scores (0.82–0.87 vs 0.56–0.87) due to its USPTO-derived reaction trees. In terms of diversity (number of discovered modes), FGFN still leads, but RGFN surpasses SyntheMol, casVAE, and GraphGA on every task, preserving the diversity advantage of the GFlowNet framework while enforcing synthesizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

RGFN extends the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021, 2023) by replacing the standard graph-level atom/fragment assembly action space with a sequence of chemical reaction executions, thereby guaranteeing synthesizability by construction. It is positioned alongside other reaction-based generative approaches (SyntheMol, casVAE) but is the first to embed this paradigm within the GFlowNet sampling framework, inheriting its diversity-preserving properties. The paper also introduces a MACCS-fingerprint-based action embedding to scale fragment selection to large libraries, and departs from prior GFlowNet molecular design work by using GPU-accelerated docking (Vina-GPU) directly in the training loop rather than relying solely on pretrained proxy models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By constraining generation to a curated set of 17 high-yield reactions and 350 low-cost building blocks, RGFN produces a searchable chemical space orders of magnitude larger than Enamine REAL while ensuring every generated molecule has a straightforward, cheap synthetic route — a property that most generative models lack. Key open questions include whether the current reaction set (which produces predominantly linear, flat molecules) can be extended with cyclization and sp3-introducing reactions to access more potent 3D scaffolds, and whether the docking-based oracle can be replaced by experimental evaluation in an active learning loop. The dramatic cost difference between RGFN and SyntheMol ligands (roughly $1–4 vs. $17–264 per 0.1 mmol) highlights the practical value of curating affordable building blocks, but experimental validation of the generated compounds remains to be done.
