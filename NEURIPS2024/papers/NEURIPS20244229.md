## Problem

Erasing a target concept from a text-to-image diffusion model inevitably degrades generation quality for other concepts, yet prior methods rely on fixed neutral anchors (e.g., 'a photo' or an empty string) for preservation. The paper demonstrates empirically that neutral concepts sit in the middle of the sensitivity spectrum, while semantically related concepts (e.g., 'person' when erasing 'nudity') are far more vulnerable to parameter changes. The gap is identifying which concepts are actually most at risk and preserving them explicitly rather than assuming a generic anchor suffices.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion 1.4 U-Net, a set of target text prompts to erase, and a discrete concept vocabulary (Oxford 3000 words or filtered CLIP tokens) as the search space, restricted to the K most embedding-similar concepts to the target for tractability. It produces fine-tuned U-Net weights that suppress generation of the target concept while maintaining quality on remaining concepts. For NSFW erasure, only non-cross-attention modules are updated; for object or style erasure, cross-attention modules suffice. Training runs for 1000 steps with Adam at lr=1e-5 on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

The core contribution is an adversarial preservation loss that dynamically identifies the concept most sensitive to parameter changes during erasure and explicitly preserves it. The problem is formulated as a bilevel min-max optimization: the outer minimization drives the target concept's output toward a neutral reference (L1), while the inner maximization searches for the adversarial concept c_a that maximizes the output deviation (L2). To make the discrete concept search differentiable, the authors define a distribution π over the concept simplex and use the Gumbel-Softmax trick to sample a discrete concept while backpropagating through the continuous relaxation. The algorithm alternates between (1) a short gradient-ascent loop on π to find the current adversarial concept and (2) a model-parameter update minimizing L1 + λL2. Unlike fixed-anchor methods, the adversarial concept shifts across fine-tuning steps, tracking whichever concept is most affected at each stage, and the generated images from c_a transition smoothly even as the keyword changes.

## Results

On the Imagenette object-erasure task (erasing 5 classes simultaneously), the proposed method achieves 98.6% ESR-1 and 96.1% ESR-5 while attaining 55.2% PSR-1 and 79.9% PSR-5, substantially outperforming the best baseline (CA) on preservation by 11.0 and 13.4 percentage points respectively. On the NSFW erasure task (I2P dataset), the method achieves the lowest NER score of 3.64% at threshold 0.3 (vs. 5.32% for ESD and 6.87% for UCE) and the best FID of 15.52 (vs. 15.98 for UCE). On artistic style erasure, the method achieves the lowest CLIP score of 21.57 on to-be-erased prompts, outperforming ESD (23.56) and UCE (24.47), while maintaining a competitive CLIP score of 30.13 on to-be-retained prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ESD (Gandikota et al., ICCV 2023), the proposed method wins on all four metrics in the object-erasure setting (ESR-1: 98.6 vs 95.5; PSR-1: 55.2 vs 41.2; PSR-5: 79.9 vs 56.1) and also achieves the lowest NER (3.64% vs 5.32%) and best FID (15.52 vs 17.14) on the NSFW task. Against UCE (Gandikota et al., WACV 2024), the method trades a small erasure gap (ESR-1: 98.6 vs 100%) for a large preservation gain (PSR-1: 55.2 vs 23.4; PSR-5: 79.9 vs 49.5), and again achieves the best NER (3.64% vs 6.87%) and FID (15.52 vs 15.98) on NSFW erasure. Against Concept Ablation (Kumari et al., ICCV 2023), the method wins on preservation (PSR-1: 55.2 vs 44.2; PSR-5: 79.9 vs 66.5) with comparable erasure (ESR-1: 98.6 vs 98.4), and dominates on NSFW erasure (NER-0.3: 3.64% vs 13.84%) and FID (15.52 vs 20.76). On artistic style erasure, the method achieves the best erasure CLIP score (21.57) but is slightly behind UCE on retention LPIPS (0.47 vs 0.40), indicating a minor quality trade-off on preserved styles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the concept-erasure line of work for latent diffusion models (ESD, UCE, Concept Ablation, TIME), which all share the objective of forcing the model's output on target concepts to match a neutral/null concept. Its key departure is replacing the fixed neutral-concept preservation term with an adversarial preservation loss that dynamically identifies the most parameter-sensitive concept via a bilevel min-max optimization, using Gumbel-Softmax to make the discrete concept search differentiable. The approach is conceptually adjacent to AdvUnlearn (Zhang et al., 2024b), which also uses adversarial training for erasure robustness, but differs in formulating the inner problem as a maximization over preservation loss rather than a minimization over attack loss, and in searching over a discrete vocabulary rather than continuous prompt perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that neutral concepts (e.g., 'a photo', empty string) sit in the middle of the sensitivity spectrum—while semantically related concepts like 'person' or 'women' are more affected by erasing 'nudity'—challenges the default preservation strategy in prior erasure methods and provides a principled alternative. Practical limitations include the computational cost of searching over the concept space (mitigated by restricting to K most similar concepts), evaluation only on Stable Diffusion 1.4, and the open question of whether the method scales to erasing many concepts simultaneously or to larger, more diverse concept vocabularies. The connection to adversarial machine learning also opens directions for multi-concept adversarial search under divergence constraints.
