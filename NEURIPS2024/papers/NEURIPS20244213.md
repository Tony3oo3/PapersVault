## Problem

Existing trojan scanning methods lack generality across diverse backdoor attacks and label-mapping strategies, and their detection signatures break down when trojaned classifiers are adversarially trained on poisoned data. No prior method simultaneously handles arbitrary attack types, arbitrary label mappings, and adversarially trained trojaned models without requiring knowledge of the specific trigger pattern.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244213/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a white-box trained classifier and a set of OOD samples, which are either crafted from a small set of clean training images via random permutations of three hard augmentations (elastic distortion, random rotation, cutpaste) for TRODO, or drawn from Tiny ImageNet and similarly augmented for TRODO-Zero. It outputs a binary Trojaned/Clean decision by comparing the mean ΔID-Score (the change in Maximum Softmax Probability before and after a PGD perturbation) against a threshold τ derived from a surrogate clean classifier. The perturbation budget ε is calibrated via DeepFool to shift OOD samples from a uniform MSP of 1/k to a boundary confidence level γ=0.5.

## Method

TRODO exploits 'blind spots'—regions in the decision boundary of trojaned classifiers where OOD samples are erroneously perceived as in-distribution—by adversarially shifting OOD samples toward ID regions using PGD to maximize the MSP (ID-Score). The detection signature is the mean ΔID-Score across OOD samples, which is significantly larger for trojaned models because blind spots provide shortcut paths for the perturbation to increase confidence. Near-OOD samples (crafted from clean data via hard augmentations) yield a more discriminative signature than far-OOD samples because they reside closer to the decision boundary. The approach is agnostic to trigger patterns and label mappings, and remains effective against adversarially trained trojaned classifiers because adversarial robustness protects against perturbed ID samples but not perturbed OOD samples. A theoretical analysis shows that adversarial risk increases linearly with the trigger norm and poisoning ratio for linear and two-layer networks, and that near-OOD distributions (shifted k-th order moments) amplify this vulnerability.

## Results

TRODO achieves 90.7% average scanning accuracy (standard training) and 87.3% (adversarial training) under All-to-One label mapping, and 87.5% / 86.1% under All-to-All, across five datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, GTSRB, CIFAR-100, PubFig). TRODO-Zero, which requires no training data, still reaches 79.4% (All-to-One) and 79.2% (All-to-All) average accuracy. The paper reports an 11.4% improvement over the best prior method in standard settings and a 24.8% improvement in adversarially trained settings. On the TrojAI benchmark, TRODO achieves 86.2% accuracy in Round 0 with a scanning time of only 152.4 s, compared to 91.3% for K-ARM at 262.1 s and >34,000 s for UMD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRODO outperforms all nine baselines by a wide margin in both label-mapping settings. Under All-to-One, TRODO (90.7% avg) surpasses the next-best method MM-BD (79.3%) by 11.4 points in standard training and FreeEagle (72.3%) by 15 points in adversarial training. Under All-to-All, the gap is even larger: TRODO (87.5%) beats UMD (76.1%) by 11.4 points and FreeEagle (58.9%) by 28.6 points in standard training, while in adversarial training TRODO (86.1%) leads UMD (57.7%) by 28.4 points. The most dramatic advantage appears in adversarially-trained All-to-All settings, where most reverse-engineering baselines (NC, ABS, PT-RED, TABOR) drop below 36% accuracy while TRODO maintains 86.1%. TRODO-Zero, operating without any training data, still exceeds all baselines except UMD in All-to-All standard training (79.2% vs. 76.1%), demonstrating that the OOD-based signature is effective even in the data-free regime. On the TrojAI benchmark, TRODO achieves competitive accuracy (86.2% Round 0) with substantially lower scanning time (152 s) compared to UMD (>34,000 s) and NC (574 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

TRODO repurposes two well-established ideas—MSP-based OOD scoring and PGD adversarial perturbation—for the trojan scanning task, identifying 'blind spots' in the decision boundary as a universal signature. It departs from prior reverse-engineering scanners (Neural Cleanse, ABS, TABOR, PT-RED) that optimize trigger patterns and are sensitive to attack type and label mapping, and from meta-classification approaches (MNTD, ULP) that require training shadow models. The key conceptual departure is leveraging the known vulnerability of adversarially trained classifiers to perturbed OOD samples (documented in robust OOD detection literature) as a detection signal, rather than exploiting trigger-specific signatures that break under adversarial training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRODO addresses a concrete gap: prior scanners degrade substantially when trojaned models are adversarially trained, a realistic threat scenario that had no effective countermeasure. The method's data-free variant (TRODO-Zero) and its agnosticism to both attack type and label mapping make it broadly deployable in model supply-chain auditing. Open questions include scalability to non-image modalities (NLP, audio), robustness against the demonstrated adaptive attacks (which reduce accuracy by 3–12 points), and whether the surrogate clean model required for threshold calibration is always available in practice. The theoretical bounds (Theorems 1–2) are limited to linear and two-layer networks, leaving the generalization to deep architectures unproven.
