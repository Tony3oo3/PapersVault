## Problem

Existing tree- and graph-based reasoning frameworks (ToT, GoT) extend rationales forward along fixed-branch paths, making the structure rigid and illogical relative to human planning, and allowing errors to propagate unchecked to subsequent steps. No prior method combines adaptive question decomposition with global, backward-updating of previously generated rationales to enable timely error correction within a single LLM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240012/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text question and K=3 retrieved question-decomposition examples (logic heuristics) selected by cosine similarity in SentenceTransformer embedding space from a pre-annotated demonstration pool. It produces a rationale R (a sequence of reasoning tokens) and a final answer A, both extracted from the root node of the iteratively constructed reasoning tree, which is capped at depth 4 and 3 branches per node to bound computation.

## Method

DeAR builds a reasoning tree T=(N,E) through an iterative Decompose-Analyze-Rethink cycle. In Decompose, the LLM adaptively splits a sub-question into a variable number of child sub-questions (guided by retrieved logic heuristics rather than a fixed branch count); in Analyze, it generates a rationale for each new sub-question, runs a self-check to detect and correct errors, and assigns a coherence score; in Rethink, it identifies which ancestor nodes are logically connected to the new sub-question and updates their rationales using the child's corrected answer, enabling global error propagation correction. Unlike ToT/GoT, which only extend rationales forward along a search path, DeAR's Rethink stage provides a bottom-up feedback loop that revises earlier reasoning steps, and its Decompose stage lets the LLM determine branching structure from the problem's own logic rather than a predefined hyperparameter.

## Results

DeAR achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across three reasoning benchmarks and three LLM backbones: 83.68% on ScienceQA, 73.36% on StrategyQA, and 86.82% on GSM8K with GPT-3.5, consistently outperforming all baselines with statistically significant improvements (p < 0.05). On the more challenging MATH dataset with GPT-4, DeAR reaches 62.25% accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline GoT (58.78%) by over 3.4 points. DeAR also achieves a superior accuracy–time trade-off, requiring fewer API calls (9.82 avg.) than ToT (11.35) and GoT (13.74) while delivering higher accuracy on ScienceQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeAR outperforms all eight baselines across nearly every dataset–LLM combination. Against the closest competitors ToT and GoT, DeAR gains +1.16/+1.34 points on ScienceQA (GPT-3.5), +1.47/+1.34 on StrategyQA, and +3.40/+2.05 on GSM8K, with the largest margins on GSM8K where longer rationales benefit most from the Rethink stage's error correction. On the harder MATH benchmark with GPT-4, DeAR (62.25%) surpasses GoT (58.78%), ToT (57.18%), ToT-variant (57.02%), CoT+SC (57.24%), and CoT (56.99%) by 3.5–5.3 points. The only exception is StrategyQA with ChatGLM3, where SelfCheck (61.22%) narrowly edges out DeAR (61.02%) by 0.2 points. DeAR also dominates Few-shot, CoT, and Least-to-most by 6–13 points across all settings, confirming that both the adaptive Decompose and the global Rethink stages contribute meaningfully beyond simple prompting or sequential sub-question solving.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeAR sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: question decomposition (Least-to-most, BREAK, ToT's intermediate-step search) and rationale-extension/search-based reasoning (ToT, GoT). It departs from ToT/GoT by replacing fixed-branch rationale extension with adaptive sub-question decomposition guided by 'logic heuristics' (retrieved similar-question decompositions), and introduces a bottom-up Rethink stage that globally updates parent-node rationales based on child-node feedback—something neither ToT nor GoT performs. The self-check component draws on SelfCheck, but is integrated into a per-node Analyze stage rather than applied post-hoc to an entire chain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Rethink stage addresses a concrete failure mode of ToT/GoT—error propagation along a single rationale path—by enabling iterative correction, which is well-motivated by the case studies. However, the framework still requires annotated question-decomposition examples (logic heuristics) for each new domain, limiting zero-shot transfer, and the iterative cycles make inference time non-trivial for complex problems. Open questions include whether the decomposition heuristics can be learned end-to-end, how the approach scales to STEM/programming reasoning, and whether the bottom-up update mechanism can be combined with RL-based reasoning (e.g., o1-style chain-of-thought training) for further gains.
