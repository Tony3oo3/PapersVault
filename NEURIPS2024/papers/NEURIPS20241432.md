## Problem

Video Salient Object Ranking (VSOR) aims to dynamically rank the visual attractiveness of objects across video frames, but prior methods either rely on external detectors and eye-fixation labels (SVSNet) or model only global inter-frame correlations without explicitly capturing instance-level motion (Lin et al.). Additionally, existing approaches fuse spatial and temporal saliency cues in a sequential, stagewise manner, which can bias the model toward one cue type and limit adaptability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241432/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip as input and processes it frame-by-frame: Mask R-CNN detects instances, and features are extracted via ROIAlign within bounding boxes (doubled in area for motion comparison). The output is a per-instance saliency score for each frame, combined with segmentation masks to produce a saliency ranking map. As a downstream application, the ranking scores are used to compute a weighted saliency centroid per frame, which drives the center of a crop window for video retargeting at target aspect ratios (e.g., 2:3, 4:5).

## Method

The core contribution is a unified spatial-temporal graph that jointly optimizes all saliency relations in a single GNN rather than fusing spatial and temporal graphs sequentially. Spatially, it models inter-instance interaction, local contrast (instance vs. its doubled bounding-box region), and global contrast (instance vs. 3×3 grid-pooled features). Temporally, it introduces a motion-aware contrast that compares each instance's features against the same spatial region in adjacent frames (with doubled bounding boxes to absorb camera motion), alongside cross-instance interaction between adjacent frames. All five relation types are aggregated in parallel across K subgraphs and combined via a residual update, allowing the network to adaptively weight spatial vs. temporal cues. The full pipeline (Mask R-CNN detection + CBAM attention + PPA position priors + graph reasoning + ranking head) is trained end-to-end with a combined detection and pairwise ranking loss.

## Results

On RVSOD, the proposed method achieves SA-SOR of 0.603 and MAE of 0.0698, outperforming the strongest prior VSOR method (Lin et al., SA-SOR 0.560, MAE 0.0745) and all image-based SOR baselines. On DAVSOD, it reaches SA-SOR 0.207 and MAE 0.0626, surpassing the best image SOR baseline (Liu et al., SA-SOR 0.179, MAE 0.0639) and both video salient object detection methods (DCFNet MAE 0.0684, SCOTCH MAE 0.1126). The DAVSOD scores are lower overall due to severe occlusion and label inconsistency in multi-object scenes, as analyzed in the supplementary material.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines on every metric where a direct comparison is available. Against the closest VSOR competitor Lin et al. [1] (RVSOD only), it improves SA-SOR by 4.3 points (0.603 vs. 0.560) and reduces MAE by 0.0047 (0.0698 vs. 0.0745), attributed to explicit instance-wise motion modeling versus Lin's global inter-frame contrast. Among image-based SOR methods, Liu et al. [2] is the strongest baseline (SA-SOR 0.563 on RVSOD, 0.179 on DAVSOD); the proposed method gains +4.0 and +2.8 SA-SOR respectively by adding temporal cues. PSR [24] and SeqRank [25] are evaluated only on RVSOD and trail by 19.8 and 9.1 SA-SOR points. On DAVSOD, the method also beats the VSOD baselines DCFNet [26] (MAE 0.0684) and SCOTCH [27] (MAE 0.1126) on segmentation quality, while achieving a substantially higher SA-SOR (0.207) than any SOR baseline. For video retargeting, the method visually outperforms seam-carving [12] (which introduces distortion artifacts) and smart-video-crop [13] (which is distracted by background), by leveraging instance-level saliency rankings to compute a weighted cropping center.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-based instance-level saliency ranking framework of Liu [2] (multi-scale spatial contrast via GNN) to the video domain, building directly on Lin [1]'s end-to-end VSOR architecture that added global inter-frame temporal correlations. The key departures are twofold: (1) replacing global inter-frame contrast with an explicit instance-wise motion cue obtained by comparing features at the same spatial position in adjacent frames (with a 2× bounding box expansion to absorb camera motion), and (2) unifying all spatial and temporal edges into a single jointly-optimized graph rather than the sequential spatial-then-temporal (or vice versa) fusion used by Lin [1]. The backbone remains Mask R-CNN with CBAM attention and PPA position-prior, consistent with the image SOR lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Video salient object ranking is a young task with clear downstream applications (video summarization, surveillance, adaptive retargeting) that prior methods handled poorly because they conflated static appearance saliency with motion saliency. The paper's demonstration that a simple same-position feature comparison across frames captures motion cues far better than global or cross-instance temporal edges provides a practical recipe for any video ranking or attention task lacking tracking annotations. However, the evaluation is limited to two small datasets (RVSOD, DAVSOD), the DAVSOD results are notably weak (SA-SOR 0.207) due to labeling noise and occlusion, no statistical significance is reported, and the reliance on a fixed Mask R-CNN detector means the method cannot adapt detection sensitivity to context — a limitation the authors acknowledge but leave for future work.
