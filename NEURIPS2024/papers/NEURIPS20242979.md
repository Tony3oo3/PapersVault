## Problem

Existing unsupervised domain adaptation methods for semantic segmentation are restricted to transferring knowledge between similar image domains (e.g., synthetic→real, day→night) and fail to generalize across fundamentally different visual modalities such as depth, infrared, and event cameras. The large modality gap causes unstable pseudo-label generation, and standard backbones pre-trained on ImageNet-1K lack sufficient cross-modality semantic understanding to bridge this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242979/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single target-modality image (depth, infrared, or event camera) at 512×512 resolution as input, which is encoded into a 64×64 latent representation by a pre-trained VAE encoder. The output is a pixel-level semantic segmentation mask produced by a segmentation head that fuses multi-scale UNet features (at 64×64, 32×32, 16×16) with a high-resolution feature map upsampled by the VAE decoder. Training additionally consumes labeled source RGB images from Cityscapes and unlabeled target samples, with a learnable conditioning embedding replacing textual prompts in the diffusion UNet.

## Method

MADM repurposes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1-4 model as the segmentation backbone, exploiting its internet-scale image-text pre-training for cross-modality generalization. Two key components address the core challenges: (1) Diffusion-based Pseudo-Label Generation (DPLG) injects annealed Gaussian noise into the target latent representation before the teacher network generates pseudo-labels, leveraging the diffusion model's denoising prior to stabilize predictions under large modality shifts; the noise level k is scheduled to decrease over training iterations. (2) Label Palette and Latent Regression (LPLR) converts one-hot class labels into RGB images via a fixed palette, encodes them into the VAE latent space, and trains the UNet with an L1 regression loss to match these latent targets, thereby enabling the pre-trained VAE decoder to upsample the output to full resolution for fine-grained boundary details. The overall training follows a DAFormer-style self-training loop with EMA teacher-student, combining source cross-entropy loss, confidence-weighted target pseudo-label loss, and the LPLR regression losses.

## Results

MADM achieves MIoU of 53.49%, 62.23%, and 56.31% on the depth (DELIVER), infrared (FMB), and event (DSEC) modalities respectively, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art method Rein by +3.26%, +1.58%, and +4.45%. Compared to the self-training baseline DAFormer, MADM exceeds it by an average of +17.9% across all three modalities. Ablation studies confirm that DPLG contributes +2.40% and LPLR contributes +1.85% on average, with a combined gain of +4.09% over the TIDM-only baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MADM outperforms all four baselines across all three modalities. Against the strongest competitor Rein (DINOv2-based, CVPR 2024), MADM gains +3.26% on depth, +1.58% on infrared, and +4.45% on event, with the largest margin on the event modality where the imaging principle differs most from RGB. Against PiPa (pixel-and-patch self-supervised, ACM MM 2023), MADM leads by +4.45%, +17.22%, and +13.03% on depth, infrared, and event respectively, indicating PiPa's self-supervised strategy struggles significantly with cross-modality gaps. Against MIC (masked image consistency, CVPR 2023), MADM achieves +8.28%, +16.74%, and +10.18% improvements, with MIC's SegFormer backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet-1K) showing the weakest modality understanding. Against DAFormer (self-training baseline, CVPR 2022), MADM exceeds it by +9.85%, +21.10%, and +22.76%, demonstrating that the TIDM backbone's extensive image-text pre-training provides substantially stronger cross-modality generalization than standard ImageNet pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised domain adaptation for semantic segmentation (UDASS) literature, which has focused on adaptation between image domains (synthetic→real, day→night), to the harder cross-modality setting (RGB→depth/infrared/event). It repurposes the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion model as a segmentation backbone, drawing on the single-step feature extraction paradigm from Zhao et al. (ICCV 2023) and the EMA teacher-student self-training framework from DAFormer. The two proposed components—DPLG (injecting annealed latent noise to stabilize pseudo-labels) and LPLR (converting one-hot classification into latent-space regression via a color palette)—are novel adaptations that exploit the denoising and VAE upsampling properties of diffusion models in ways not previously applied to segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world perception systems rely on depth, infrared, and event cameras (especially at night), yet these modalities lack sufficient labels for supervised training. The finding that a TIDM backbone with standard self-training already matches prior SOTA (Rein) suggests that internet-scale image-text pretraining provides cross-modality semantic priors that ImageNet-pretrained backbones lack, which could reshape how practitioners choose backbones for domain adaptation. Key limitations include the high parameter count (949M) and memory footprint (57G), though the distilled 85M variant mitigates this. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the three tested modalities, whether multi-step diffusion could further improve pseudo-label quality, and how to efficiently distill TIDM knowledge into lightweight models for deployment.
