## Problem

The Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma guarantees that random linear projections preserve distances with high probability, but no optimization-based method has been shown to learn a deterministic matrix satisfying the JL guarantee. This paper first proves that direct optimization over the space of projection matrices is doomed — the maximum distortion objective has many bad local minima reachable from the origin — then asks whether a different optimization formulation can circumvent this fundamental barrier.

## Input / Output

The method takes n unit-norm data points in R^d and a target dimension k, and produces a deterministic k×d projection matrix M whose maximum distortion over all input points is O(√(log n/k)). The optimization operates in an extended parameter space of pairs (M, σ²), where M is a mean matrix and σ² is a shared variance parameter for a Gaussian distribution over k×d matrices. The procedure starts at (0, 1) — the standard Gaussian JL construction — and terminates when σ² is sufficiently small, at which point the mean matrix M itself is returned as the deterministic embedding.

## Method

The core insight is to perform optimization not over the space of projection matrices (which has a non-convex landscape with bad local minima) but over the larger space of Gaussian solution samplers parameterized by (M, σ²). The objective g(M, σ²) = f(M, σ²) + σ²/2 combines the probability that a sampled matrix fails the JL guarantee with a variance-penalizing regularizer, ensuring that any descent path maintains a positive probability of producing a valid JL matrix while pushing toward zero variance. A deterministic second-order Hessian Descent algorithm (Algorithm 1) alternates between gradient steps and negative-curvature steps, performing reverse-diffusion-like reductions of σ² until reaching a ρ-second-order stationary point. The paper proves that any such stationary point reachable from the origin must have σ² < poly(n,k,d)·ρ^O(1), and that for sufficiently small ρ the mean matrix M deterministically satisfies the JL guarantee. The overall procedure runs in poly(n,k,d) steps, yielding a novel optimization-based derandomization of the Gaussian JL transform.

## Results

On a synthetic dataset of n=100 unit-norm points in d=500 dimensions projected to k=30 dimensions, the optimization-based method (Adam, 5000 iterations) converges to a deterministic matrix M with ||Mx||² ≈ 1, i.e., near-zero distortion. In contrast, the standard random Gaussian JL construction (Z ~ N(0,1)) yields an average ||Zx||² ≈ 2 (distortion ≈ 1) and a best-case minimum of ||Zx||² ≈ 1.6 (distortion ≈ 0.6) over 1000 random trials. The variance σ² monotonically decreases to zero during optimization, confirming convergence to a deterministic solution sampler as predicted by theory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its optimization-based method against the standard random Gaussian JL construction (Z ~ N(0,1)), the canonical baseline from Indyk and Motwani [1998]. Over 1000 random trials, the best random matrix achieves a minimum distortion of approximately 0.6 (||Zx||² ≈ 1.6), while the average random matrix has distortion of approximately 1 (||Zx||² ≈ 2). The proposed Hessian-descent optimization over the Gaussian sampler space achieves near-zero distortion (||Mx||² ≈ 1), substantially outperforming even the best random draw by a factor of roughly 2–3 in distortion. This demonstrates that exploiting data structure through optimization can surpass the worst-case guarantees of the random JL construction, consistent with the paper's theoretical prediction that second-order stationary points of the regularized objective yield deterministic JL-quality embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of JL derandomization, non-convex optimization theory, and diffusion models. Prior derandomization of JL (Nisan PRGs, Clarkson-Woodruff, Bhargava-Kosaraju conditional expectation) relies on combinatorial or pseudorandom constructions, while prior optimization-based embedding work (Garber-Hazan, Shamir, Xu-Li) targets PCA's average-distortion objective rather than JL's maximum-distortion guarantee. The paper's key conceptual departure is optimizing over the larger space of Gaussian solution samplers (mean, variance) rather than directly over projection matrices, drawing inspiration from diffusion model denoising to perform a 'reverse diffusion' that gradually reduces variance while preserving solution quality. The negative result (Theorem 1) establishing that direct matrix-space optimization is doomed due to bad local minima provides the motivation for this indirect approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical result showing that a deterministic optimization procedure can produce a matrix satisfying the JL guarantee, resolving a question the authors identify as previously open. The conceptual framework—optimizing over a space of random solution samplers and using variance reduction as a derandomization mechanism—is explicitly positioned as generalizable beyond JL to other randomized algorithmic problems. However, significant limitations remain: the method only applies to the basic Gaussian JL construction (not sparse, Hadamard, or other variants), the polynomial bounds are likely very loose, and empirical validation is limited to small-scale synthetic data (n=100, d=500, k=30). Open questions include whether the sampler-space approach extends to more complex randomized constructions, whether it can exploit data structure to beat worst-case JL bounds, and whether the polynomial-time guarantees are practically meaningful.
