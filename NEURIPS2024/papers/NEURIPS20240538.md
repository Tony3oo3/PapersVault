## Problem

State-of-the-art zero-shot RL methods (forward-backward representations, universal successor features) assume access to large, heterogeneous pre-training datasets, but real-world historical datasets are typically small and produced by controllers not optimising for diversity. This paper identifies that on such low-quality datasets, existing methods suffer from out-of-distribution state-action value overestimation—a well-known failure mode in single-task offline RL—and asks whether conservatism can fix this without sacrificing performance on high-quality data.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method consumes a static offline dataset of reward-free transitions (s, a, s') of fixed size (e.g., 100k uniformly subsampled transitions) with no access to the environment. At test time, a task is specified either as a small set of reward-labelled states (k ≤ 10,000) or as an explicit reward function, from which a task vector z is inferred via a linear projection of the backward model. The output is a policy π_z(s) that maps states to actions for the specified task with no further planning or learning, operating in continuous action spaces of dimension 2–12 across the evaluated domains.

## Method

The paper introduces two conservative variants of forward-backward representations: VC-FB, which adds a CQL-style penalty suppressing F(s,a,z)ᵀz for out-of-distribution actions across all task vectors z ~ Z, and MC-FB, which instead suppresses the successor measure F(s,a,z)ᵀB(s₊) for OOD actions. The OOD action distribution μ(a|s) is approximated via a log-sum-exp over actions sampled from a uniform distribution and the current policy (following CQL(H)), and the conservative penalty weight α is dynamically tuned via Lagrangian dual gradient descent against a conservative budget τ. The key insight is that the standard FB TD update bootstraps from actions a_{t+1} ~ π_z(s_{t+1}) that may be OOD for small datasets, causing F and B to be updated toward unsupported targets; the conservative term counteracts this by penalising the predicted value or measure of actions not present in the dataset, simultaneously for all tasks in the z-space. VC-FB outperforms MC-FB on small datasets because sampling z uniformly from the hypersphere provides better task coverage than tying updates to B(s₊) from the limited dataset.

## Results

On 100k ExORL datasets, VC-FB achieves 148 IQM and MC-FB achieves 136 IQM, representing 150% and 137% of vanilla FB's performance (99 IQM) respectively. Both conservative variants also outperform the single-task CQL baseline, reaching 120% and 111% of CQL's IQM despite lacking access to task-specific reward labels. On full ExORL datasets, both variants maintain or slightly exceed FB performance (e.g., 396 vs 389 on RND, 133 vs 111 on RANDOM), confirming no downside to conservatism. On D4RL, VC-FB and MC-FB improve FB by 6.8× and 5.6× respectively but still underperform CQL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla FB [82], both VC-FB and MC-FB substantially outperform on 100k ExORL datasets (150% and 137% of FB's IQM), with the gap largest on the highest-quality RND dataset (2.5× and 1.8× respectively). On full datasets, the conservative variants match or slightly exceed FB, with the largest relative gain on RANDOM (20% for MC-FB). Against the single-task CQL baseline [44], VC-FB and MC-FB surprisingly outperform it in aggregate on 100k ExORL (120% and 111% of CQL's IQM), which the authors note is the first demonstration of a multi-task offline agent surpassing a single-task analogue; however, on D4RL both underperform CQL. Against GC-IQL [60], the conservative FB variants outperform on locomotion domains (Walker, Quadruped) while GC-IQL performs better on goal-reaching domains (Maze, Jaco), likely because the goal-state proxy used for conditioning is a poor substitute for dense rewards. Against SF-LAP [83], both conservative FB variants outperform, and the FB-to-SF-LAP gap is consistent with the original [83] findings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two established lines of work: zero-shot RL via forward-backward (FB) representations and universal successor features, and conservative regularization from single-task offline RL (CQL). The key conceptual move is repurposing CQL's OOD value suppression—originally designed for a single Q-function—into the zero-shot setting where it must simultaneously regularize an infinite family of task-conditioned value functions. The authors deliberately avoid introducing new parametric functions, limiting their solution space to CQL-style penalties applied to the existing FB/USF architecture, and position their contribution as a minimal, drop-in modification to existing zero-shot RL pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that zero-shot RL has so far been demonstrated only on large, diverse exploratory datasets that are expensive to collect; this paper shows the methods remain viable on the smaller, homogeneous historical datasets that are actually available in real deployments. The finding that conservative multi-task agents can outperform single-task offline RL baselines (CQL) despite lacking task-specific reward labels is a notable result with implications for how we think about the value of task-specific data. Open questions include whether IQL-style regularization (which avoids the fragile α tuning that limited D4RL performance) can close the remaining gap, and whether the VC-FB vs. MC-FB choice can be predicted a priori from dataset properties.
