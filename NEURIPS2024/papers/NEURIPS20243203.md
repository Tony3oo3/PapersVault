## Problem

Multi-vector (MV) retrieval models like ColBERT achieve markedly superior IR performance over single-vector models, but their non-linear Chamfer similarity scoring makes them computationally expensive and incompatible with highly-optimized single-vector MIPS solvers. Prior approaches (e.g., PLAID) rely on multi-stage pipelines built on a single-vector heuristic that can miss true MV nearest neighbors, are complex to tune, and lack theoretical guarantees. This paper asks whether MV similarity search can be reduced to standard SV MIPS with provable approximation guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes sets of normalized token embeddings (32 query vectors of dimension 128; a variable number of document vectors of dimension 128, averaging 18–165 per document) and produces single Fixed Dimensional Encoding (FDE) vectors of dimension d_FDE (e.g., 10,240) whose inner product approximates the Chamfer similarity. Query FDEs are sparse with only O(|Q|·d_proj·R_reps) non-zero entries, and the entire FDE can be compressed 32× via product quantization (PQ-256-8) to 1,280 bytes with negligible quality loss. The FDE construction is data-oblivious (independent of the dataset), making it robust to distribution shifts and suitable for streaming settings.

## Method

MUVERA constructs FDEs by: (1) partitioning the embedding space into B=2^{k_sim} clusters via SimHash (random half-space intersections), (2) for each cluster, summing the query vectors that land there and averaging the document vectors (with a fill_empty_clusters step that assigns the nearest document point to empty clusters), (3) applying random ±1 linear projections to reduce per-block dimensionality, and (4) repeating the process R_reps times with independent random partitions and projections, then concatenating all blocks. The key theoretical result is that the FDE dot product is a one-sided ε-approximation to normalized Chamfer similarity (Theorem 2.1), yielding the first sub-brute-force algorithm with provable guarantees for Chamfer NNS (Theorem 2.2). For retrieval, document FDEs are indexed in DiskANN (graph-based ANNS), queries are encoded and searched via MIPS, and candidates are re-ranked with exact Chamfer similarity; ball carving (clustering query embeddings by cosine threshold τ=0.7) further accelerates re-ranking by reducing 32 query vectors to ~6 clusters on average.

## Results

Across six BEIR datasets (MS MARCO, HotpotQA, NQ, Quora, SciDocs, ArguAna), MUVERA achieves an average of 10% higher Recall@k (up to 56% higher) and 90% lower latency (up to 5.7× lower) compared to PLAID. On MS MARCO specifically, 10240-dimensional FDEs achieve 80% recall with only 60 candidates, versus 300 for the deduplicated SV heuristic and 1200 for the non-deduplicated version. Product quantization (PQ-256-8) compresses FDEs by 32× (10240-dim FDEs stored in 1280 bytes) with negligible recall loss, and ball carving at τ=0.7 provides a 25% QPS improvement in re-ranking with no measurable quality degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to PLAID [43], MUVERA achieves essentially equivalent Recall@k on MS MARCO (within 0.4%) but up to 1.56× higher recall on other datasets (e.g., HotpotQA, NQ), while consistently achieving 90% lower average latency (up to 5.7× lower) across all six BEIR datasets. MUVERA also requires minimal tuning — the same 10240-dimensional FDEs with PQ-256-8 are used across all datasets, with only the beam-width adjusted per target k. Compared to the SV heuristic underlying ColBERTv2 [44], FDE retrieval is substantially more efficient: 4096-dimensional FDEs match the deduplicated SV heuristic's recall while retrieving 1.75–3.75× fewer candidates, and 10240-dimensional FDEs retrieve 2.6–5× fewer. Against the non-deduplicated SV heuristic, the savings are 10.5–15× (4k FDE) and 20–22.5× (10k FDE). The paper also notes that DESSERT [12], another multi-vector retrieval method, is 2–5× faster than PLAID but attains 2–2.5% lower R@1000 on MS MARCO, whereas MUVERA matches or exceeds PLAID's recall with up to 5.7× lower latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant multi-stage SV heuristic pipeline (ColBERTv2 → PLAID) that retrieves candidates by running separate MIPS queries per query token, then progressively prunes. Conceptually, it draws on SimHash-based locality-sensitive partitioning (Charikar, 2002) and the probabilistic tree embedding literature, but repurposes them to construct Fixed Dimensional Encodings whose dot product approximates the non-linear Chamfer/MaxSim similarity. It also contrasts with DESSERT, which uses LSH for MV retrieval but only achieves worst-case brute-force runtime; MUVERA's FDE construction guarantees strictly sub-brute-force time with provable ε-approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MUVERA provides the first algorithm with provable approximation guarantees for Chamfer similarity nearest-neighbor search at sub-brute-force runtime, filling a theoretical gap that has persisted since ColBERT popularized late-interaction retrieval. Practically, the reduction to standard MIPS means any off-the-shelf ANN solver (DiskANN, HNSW, etc.) can be used for MV retrieval, dramatically simplifying system design compared to PLAID's four-stage pipeline. Open questions include how FDE quality scales with the average number of document embeddings per document, whether the approach extends to non-normalized or non-unit-vector settings, and whether further optimization of the FDE retrieval stage (currently a single beam-search pass) can close the remaining gap on heavily-tuned benchmarks like MS MARCO.
