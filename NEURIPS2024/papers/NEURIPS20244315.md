## Problem

Selecting the best hyperparameter (and thus the best model) for UDA methods on unlabeled target data is critical yet under-explored. This paper provides a comprehensive empirical study of 8 existing model selection approaches across 12 UDA methods, 5 benchmarks, and 5 UDA scenarios, revealing that none can reliably avoid the worst-case selection—picking the model with the lowest target-domain performance in the candidate pool. The paper asks whether any selection method can be both reliable (worst-case certified) and versatile across diverse UDA settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of m pre-trained candidate UDA models (each trained with a different value of a single or pair of hyperparameters) and a set of unlabeled target images. It produces the index of the selected candidate model and its associated hyperparameter value. Crucially, EnsV consumes only the probability predictions of all m candidates on the target data—no source data, no extra training, no additional hyperparameters, and no assumptions about the type of domain shift (covariate, label, or both).

## Method

EnsV exploits the prediction-based ensemble of all candidate models as a 'free lunch' role model for validation. A theoretical proposition (via Jensen's inequality on the convex NLL loss) guarantees that the ensemble's loss is strictly lower than the worst candidate's loss, certifying worst-case avoidance. Practically, EnsV computes the averaged prediction vector for each target sample across all m candidates, then selects the candidate model whose per-sample predictions achieve the highest accuracy against this ensemble output. This simple mechanism requires no domain-shift assumptions, no source data access, and no extra training, making it applicable uniformly across closed-set, partial-set, open-partial-set, and source-free UDA settings.

## Results

EnsV achieves zero worst-case model selections across all 172 evaluated UDA tasks (131 closed-set, 24 partial-set, and 17 source-free), while every other method selects the worst candidate at least once. On Office-Home CDA, EnsV attains 70.36% average validation accuracy (vs. 70.72% for the best candidate and 60.26% for the worst), outperforming the best source-based method RV (69.68%) and the best target-specific method SND (66.02%). In the challenging open-partial-set UDA setting, EnsV reaches 68.96% H-score on Office-Home, dramatically surpassing all other methods (14.98%–34.27%) and the worst candidate (12.84%). On source-free UDA with SHOT, EnsV achieves 71.88% on Office-Home and 87.15% on Office-31, matching or exceeding the best candidate in most cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EnsV is the only method certified to avoid worst-case selection, achieving 0/131, 0/24, and 0/17 worst-case picks across closed-set, partial-set, and source-free UDA respectively. Among source-based methods, RV is the most reliable (2/110 worst-case in CDA) but requires extra model re-training and cannot handle label shift or source-free settings. Among target-specific methods, Corr-C is the worst performer with 80/131 worst-case selections in CDA (61% of tasks), while SND (33/131) and Entropy (15/131) also frequently select the worst model. In the open-partial-set UDA setting (Table 7), EnsV achieves 68.96% H-score versus 14.98% (SND) and 34.27% (Entropy), a gap of over 30 percentage points. In source-free UDA (Table 8), Entropy and InfoMax both select the worst model in 16/17 tasks, while EnsV maintains near-best selections (71.88% vs. 72.05% best on Office-Home SHOT). On the large-scale DomainNet-126 benchmark, EnsV achieves 67.73% average accuracy, outperforming Entropy (64.55%), InfoMax (65.44%), SND (62.12%), and Corr-C (61.90%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two lines of UDA model selection research: source-based validation (SourceRisk, IWCV, DEV, RV) and target-specific metrics (Entropy, InfoMax, SND, Corr-C). It departs from both by arguing that all existing methods treat candidate models independently, overlooking the prediction-based ensemble that is already available 'for free' in the hyperparameter search setup. The theoretical contribution (Proposition 1, via Jensen's inequality on NLL) provides a simple guarantee that the ensemble strictly outperforms the worst candidate, which no prior selection method offers. The paper also extends the model selection problem to open-partial-set UDA and source-free UDA, settings where prior validation methods have not been studied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that none of the eight existing model selection methods can reliably avoid selecting the worst-performing candidate across diverse UDA settings—highlights a significant reliability gap that practitioners face when deploying UDA methods. EnsV offers a simple, assumption-free baseline with a theoretical worst-case guarantee, but it is explicitly not certified for peak-performance selection, leaving open the question of whether worst-case avoidance and peak selection can be simultaneously achieved. The paper's limitations (struggles with predominantly poor model pools, small performance gaps, or single-good-model scenarios) suggest that ensemble-based selection is a necessary but not sufficient component of a fully reliable validation strategy.
