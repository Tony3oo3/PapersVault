## Problem

Existing visual contrastive decoding methods (VCD, VDD, ICD) mitigate LVLM hallucinations by introducing global visual perturbations to induce hallucinatory tokens, but this uncontrollable global uncertainty fails to precisely widen the logits gap between hallucinatory and targeted tokens, sometimes even producing new undesired hallucinations. The paper identifies that no prior method provides a principled mechanism to ensure the 'hallucinated' model's logits satisfy the necessary condition for effective contrast decoding.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text query as input and produces a free-form text description (caption) as output. During training, it consumes paired hallucination/correct descriptions from the RLHF-V dataset, where each pair differs only in specific hallucinated words. At inference, the trained 'Evil' LVLM and the original LVLM both process the same image-query pair, and their logits are combined via the contrastive decoding formula to produce the final token sequence.

## Method

HIO trains an 'Evil' LVLM that deliberately amplifies hallucinations, whose logits are then contrasted against the original model's logits at inference. Three components drive this: (1) CBTM reverses the Bradley-Terry preference direction so the model learns to prefer hallucinated outputs over correct ones; (2) AMTH extends this to simultaneously fit multiple hallucination-token pairs per input using a product-of-sigmoids loss, ensuring all potential hallucinations are amplified rather than just one; (3) ACI adds an explicit logit-difference constraint that forces the hallucination token's raw logit to exceed the target token's logit, preventing the cross-entropy loss from satisfying the objective by merely suppressing the denominator. The theoretical analysis (Eqn. 17) proves that contrast decoding eliminates hallucinations if and only if the sum of logit differences (hallucination minus correct) in the Evil model exceeds a constant J determined by the original model, which HIO's training objective is designed to satisfy.

## Results

On POPE (MSCOCO, LLaVA 1.5), HIO achieves 90.21% accuracy and 89.94 F1 in the Random setting, 88.12% accuracy and 87.84 F1 in Popular, and 84.32% accuracy and 84.34 F1 in Adversarial, consistently outperforming all baselines. On CHAIR (COCO val2017), HIO reduces CHAIR_S to 41.4 and CHAIR_I to 10.5 (an 8.1% and 4.9% reduction over the best baseline), while maintaining competitive Recall (77.4). On the MME hallucination subset, HIO achieves a total perception score of 653.33, surpassing VDD (643.33) and VCD (604.66), with the largest gains in Count (160.00 vs. 143.33 for VDD) and Color (170.00 vs. 165.00 for VDD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIO outperforms VCD (Leng et al., 2023) across all three POPE settings by 2.5–3.4 points in accuracy and 2.8–3.0 points in F1, and reduces CHAIR_S by 7.2 points (41.4 vs. 48.6) and CHAIR_I by 4.4 points (10.5 vs. 14.9). Against ICD (Wang et al., 2024), HIO wins on Popular (+1.96 Acc, +1.08 F1) and Adversarial (+4.61 Acc, +2.64 F1) POPE settings, and on CHAIR by a large margin (41.4 vs. 50.8 CHAIR_S), though ICD achieves slightly higher Recall on CHAIR (78.5 vs. 77.4). Compared to VDD (Zhang et al., 2024), HIO leads on all POPE settings (up to +2.14 F1 in Adversarial) and on MME total score (653.33 vs. 643.33), though VDD retains an edge on MME Position (145.00 vs. 133.33). On CHAIR, HIO also surpasses OPERA (Huang et al., 2023) by 6.4 points in CHAIR_S (41.4 vs. 47.8) and 4.1 points in CHAIR_I (10.5 vs. 14.6), while generating longer captions (110.3 vs. 98.6 words), indicating better detail retention alongside hallucination reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual contrastive decoding (VCD) framework of Leng et al. (2023) and its successors (VDD, ICD) by replacing ad-hoc global visual perturbations (Gaussian noise, image masking) with a purpose-trained 'Evil' LVLM that deliberately amplifies hallucination tokens. The training objective repurposes the Bradley-Terry preference model in reverse (CBTM), drawing on the DPO formulation of Rafailov et al. (2024) but inverting the preference direction so the model learns to favor hallucinated outputs. A key departure from prior contrastive decoding work is the theoretical derivation (Eqn. 17) that formalizes the necessary logit-gap condition for hallucination elimination, which motivates the multi-token amplification (AMTH) and explicit logit-difference constraint (ACI) components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled theoretical characterization of when contrastive decoding succeeds or fails, shifting the design of anti-hallucination decoding from heuristic perturbation to a condition-driven optimization problem. The 'train a model to hallucinate more' strategy is a reusable paradigm that could extend to other contrastive decoding settings beyond LVLMs. However, the approach requires training and maintaining a second full-size LVLM, adding significant inference cost, and the authors acknowledge that Eqn. 17 is a necessary but not sufficient condition. Open questions include whether training-free alternatives can approximate the same logit-gap amplification and whether the condition generalizes to multi-turn or long-form generation.
