## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of adversarial training are either confined to the Neural Tangent Kernel regime or require restrictive data assumptions such as (noisy) linear separability or robust realizability. This paper asks whether one can establish stability and generalization guarantees for adversarial training of over-parameterized two-layer networks with smooth activation, without any data distribution assumptions and beyond the NTK regime.

## Input / Output

The method consumes binary classification pairs (x, y) with x in a bounded Euclidean ball of radius C_x, drawn i.i.d. from an arbitrary unknown distribution, along with a general adversarial perturbation set B(x) that need not be an ℓ_p ball. The network is a two-layer architecture with a 1-Lipschitz, H-smooth activation and logistic loss; top-layer weights are fixed at ±1/√m while the bottom-layer initialization W₀ is arbitrary (Gaussian, vanishing, or pre-trained). The output is a sequence of weight matrices {W_t} accompanied by theoretical bounds: the robust generalization gap and robust test loss are expressed in terms of the ARERM oracle (minimum empirical robust risk in a neighborhood of W₀), the step size η, and the adversarial example precision β₁.

## Method

The central technical insight is that the robust empirical loss for two-layer networks with smooth activation is 'almost' convex (specifically −HC_x²/√m weakly convex), enabling the authors to extend convex stability machinery to this non-convex, non-smooth setting. They prove a uniform argument stability bound of O(√(ηT) + ηT/n + √(β₁ηT)) for gradient descent-based adversarial training, where β₁ captures the precision of adversarial example generation, and show the robust generalization gap is controlled by the ARERM oracle under the over-parameterization condition m ≥ Ω(η²T²). They further construct a smooth surrogate via Moreau's envelope, which eliminates the √(ηT) term and yields a tighter stability bound of O(ηT/n + ηT√(β₂/μ)) at the cost of solving a min-max optimization at each step. The analysis holds for any initialization and any data distribution, and extends to stochastic gradient descent variants with an O(1/√T) excess risk guarantee.

## Results

The paper establishes that after T iterations of gradient-descent adversarial training on an over-parameterized two-layer network (width m ≥ Ω(η²T²)), the uniform argument stability is bounded by O(C_x η√T + C_x ηT/n + √(β₁ ηT)), where β₁ captures the precision of the adversarial example generator. The robust generalization gap is controlled by the ARERM oracle: min_{t∈[9T/10, T]} E[ε_gen(W_t)] ≤ [17α₁/(1−α₁)] · [E[Δ_S^oracle] + C_x²η/2 + β₁]. A direct corollary shows that with η = Θ(1/(C_x²√T)) and T ≤ O(min{n², 1/β₁²}), the minimum expected robust loss over all iterates is at most 1.1·L_rob(W) + O(1/√T) for any reference weight W. Using Moreau's envelope smoothing, the UAS improves to O(C_x ηT/n + ηT√(β₂/μ)), eliminating the O(η√T) term present in the unsmoothed adversarial training bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or datasets. The comparisons are against prior theoretical results. The UAS bound O(η√T + ηT/n) (setting β₁=0) recovers the convex non-smooth result of Xing et al. [2021a], but extends it to the non-convex two-layer network setting with logistic loss. The Moreau envelope UAS bound O(ηT/n) (setting β₂=0) matches the convex smooth result of Hardt et al. [2016] and Xiao et al. [2024], but unlike Xiao et al.'s non-convex theorem which requires a decaying step size η_t ≤ μ/t, this paper's result holds with a constant step size by exploiting the weakly convex structure of over-parameterized networks. Compared to Xiao et al. [2022a], whose bound includes an additional O(ηTα) term growing linearly in T (where α is the perturbation size), this paper's bound depends on β₁ (attack precision) rather than the perturbation magnitude, and does not require assumptions on perturbation size. The key advantage over prior adversarial training generalization results (Mianjy & Arora 2024, Wang et al. 2024b) is that no data distribution assumptions (linear separability, robust realizability) are needed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the uniform argument stability framework of Hardt et al. [2016] and the adversarial stability analysis of Xing et al. [2021a] from convex losses to the non-convex setting of over-parameterized two-layer networks, leveraging the 'weakly convex' structure of smooth-activation networks. It adapts the Moreau envelope smoothing technique from Xiao et al. [2024] to the non-convex regime with constant step size, and uses the weighted telescoping optimization analysis of Richards and Kuzborskij [2021]. The key departure from prior work is the removal of restrictive data distribution assumptions (linear separability, robust realizability) and the NTK regime, by exploiting the specific over-parameterized network structure rather than treating the loss as a generic non-convex function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first stability-based generalization guarantee for adversarial training of over-parameterized networks that holds for arbitrary data distributions and arbitrary initializations, filling a notable gap between the NTK-regime analyses and the restrictive-assumption convergence results. The practical implication is that early stopping (equivalently, sufficient width relative to training iterations) provides a principled mechanism to control adversarial generalization, and Moreau envelope smoothing offers a concrete algorithmic improvement by eliminating the O(η√T) stability term. Limitations include restriction to two-layer networks with smooth activations, binary classification, and expected (not high-probability) bounds; extending to deep networks, non-smooth activations like ReLU, and multi-class settings remains open.
