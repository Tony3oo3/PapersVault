## Problem

Language-Only VLM Selection (LOVM) must rank pre-trained VLMs on a target dataset using only its class names and text data, without access to target images. Two inherent gaps hinder this: the Modality Gap, where VLM text and image features cluster separately making generated texts poor image proxies, and the Capability Gap, where a VLM's average ranking across datasets poorly predicts its ranking on a specific target dataset. Prior methods like ModelGPT address neither gap explicitly, limiting their selection accuracy.

## Input / Output

SWAB consumes only the target dataset's class names (no images), open-source dataset class names and their labeled images, and ChatGPT-generated auxiliary texts (50 captions + synonyms per class). The output is a predicted ranking of all 43 VLMs in the zoo on the target dataset, evaluated by Top-5 Recall and Kendall's tau against ground-truth rankings computed from actual zero-shot image classification accuracy. Notably, the method never sees target dataset images at inference time, and the ranker model f_R is trained on open-source datasets' modified text features paired with their true zero-shot accuracies.

## Method

SWAB constructs a transport matrix γ* via optimal transport (Sinkhorn) between open-source and target dataset classes, using cosine similarity of MPNet text embeddings as the cost matrix. This matrix serves two bridging roles: (1) it computes a weighted sum of class-specific modality gap vectors from open-source datasets to estimate target-dataset gap vectors, which are added to ChatGPT-generated text embeddings before scoring, making them better image proxies for ModelGPT's ranker; (2) it computes a weighted sum of each VLM's class-specific zero-shot rankings on open-source datasets to predict its target-dataset ranking, replacing the unreliable average-rank baseline. The two resulting rankings (learning-based from the modified ranker, non-learning-based from the OT-weighted rank) are ensembled via a 50/50 weighted Borda Count. Class-level gap vectors outperform dataset-level ones, and partial optimal transport (mass=0.9) is used for the capability-gap bridge.

## Results

On the LOVM Benchmark (43 VLMs × 23 datasets), SWAB achieves a Top-5 Recall (R₅) of 0.504 and Kendall's Rank Correlation (τ) of 0.318, yielding a combined R₅+τ score of 0.822. This represents a 14.8% relative improvement over the previous state-of-the-art ModelGPT (R₅+τ = 0.716). Metric-based baselines (H-Score, NCE, LEEP, LogME) perform near chance level (R₅+τ ≈ 0.17–0.24), while methods leveraging open-source datasets (INB, Avg Rank) reach R₅+τ ≈ 0.69–0.71, confirming that dataset-specific information is critical for VLM selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWAB outperforms all five baselines on every metric. Against the metric-based methods (H-Score, NCE, LEEP, LogME), SWAB's R₅+τ of 0.822 exceeds their combined scores (0.174–0.221) by more than 3×, confirming that text-only transferability metrics are ineffective substitutes for image-based evaluation due to the modality gap. Against the non-learning baseline Avg Rank (R₅+τ = 0.689), SWAB gains +0.133, validating that the capability gap makes average cross-dataset ranking a poor proxy for target-specific ranking. The closest competitor is ModelGPT (R₅+τ = 0.716), which SWAB surpasses by +0.106 (14.8% relative), with the gap widening on τ (0.318 vs. 0.270) and R₅ (0.504 vs. 0.446). Notably, INB (ImageNet-based ranking, R₅+τ = 0.710) slightly outperforms Avg Rank, further evidencing that dataset relevance matters more than general model strength.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LOVM/ModelGPT (Zohar et al., NeurIPS 2023), which introduced the Language-Only VLM Selection problem and a learning-based ranker trained on text-derived metrics. SWAB departs from ModelGPT by diagnosing two specific failure modes — the modality gap (text features not aligning with image features in VLM embedding space, as characterized by Liang et al., NeurIPS 2022) and the capability gap (a VLM's average ranking not predicting its dataset-specific ranking) — and addresses both simultaneously via optimal transport. The OT-based bridge matrix, computed from textual class-name similarity using a pre-trained encoder (MPNet), is repurposed from the classical transport literature to transfer class-level statistics (gap vectors and per-class rankings) from open-source datasets to the target dataset, a use case not previously explored in the model-selection context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As open-source VLM zoos grow (43 models in this benchmark), the ability to select the best model for a target task without access to its images becomes practically important for non-ML users and zero-shot deployment scenarios. The 14.8% improvement over ModelGPT on the LOVM benchmark is meaningful, but the task remains narrow (zero-shot image classification ranking) and the method's reliance on ChatGPT-generated texts and a pre-trained text encoder for class similarity introduces practical dependencies. Open questions include whether the OT-based bridging generalizes to other VLM tasks (detection, segmentation, VQA), whether the uniform class-importance assumption in the transport marginals can be improved with task-specific priors, and how the approach scales to VLM zoos with hundreds of models or datasets with tens of thousands of classes.
