## Problem

Existing LLM self-training methods (STaR, ReST^EM, Self-Rewarding) filter training data solely by final-answer correctness, so intermediate reasoning steps in 'correct' traces are often wrong or useless, limiting fine-tuning quality. Training a process reward model (PRM) to verify each step would solve this, but requires expensive per-step human annotation that does not scale. The paper asks: how can we automatically infer reliable per-step process rewards and collect high-quality reasoning traces without any human labeling?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242065/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a science or math question, a policy LLM that generates one-sentence reasoning steps autoregressively, and a value model (a PRM variant) that outputs a scalar quality value v_k ∈ [0,1] for any partial solution. The value model is trained with MSE loss to predict v_k directly, unifying reward and value into a single target rather than maintaining separate reward and value models. Outputs include the final reasoning trace and answer from MCTS* search, per-step quality values and weighted rewards inferred from the pruned search tree, and updated policy and value models after each self-training iteration.

## Method

ReST-MCTS* defines a quality value v_k = max(v_{k-1} + w_{s_k}, 0) where the weighted reward w_{s_k} = (1−v_{k-1})/(m_k+1)·(1−2r_{s_k}) incorporates the reasoning distance m_k (minimum remaining steps to a correct answer) and the PRM sigmoid score r_{s_k}, ensuring v_k is bounded in [0,1] and converges to 1 only at the correct final answer (Theorem 1). A modified MCTS (MCTS*) uses this value model for UCB-based node selection, a self-critic mechanism for logical termination, and greedy Monte Carlo rollout for intermediate value estimation, eliminating the need for an outcome reward model. After search, the pruned tree yields per-step labels: r_{s_k} is set via hard estimation (whether a correct answer is reachable from that node) and m_k from minimum path length, from which all w_{s_k} and v_k are computed. These inferred (Q, p, v) pairs train the value model, while verified correct traces train the policy via SFT, and both models improve iteratively in a MuZero-style mutual self-training loop.

## Results

On LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, ReST-MCTS* achieves 27.55% average accuracy after 1 self-training iteration, outperforming ReSTEM (26.22%) and Self-Rewarding (27.42%). On SciGLM-6B, ReST-MCTS* reaches 35.90% average after 2 iterations, surpassing ReSTEM (33.27%) and Self-Rewarding (33.56%). For value model verification (Table 3), SC+ReST-MCTS* achieves 87.5% on GSM8K and 39.0% on MATH500, exceeding SC+MATH-SHEPHERD (86.3%/38.3%). On SciBench (Table 4), ReST-MCTS* with GLM4 reaches 16.77% average accuracy, outperforming CoT (12.68%) and ToT (15.82%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReST-MCTS* consistently outperforms ReSTEM [6] and Self-Rewarding [16] on average accuracy across all three backbones and both iterations (e.g., 27.55% vs. 26.22%/27.42% on LLaMA-3-8B 1st iter.; 35.90% vs. 33.27%/33.56% on SciGLM-6B 2nd iter.), demonstrating that process-reward-guided tree search yields higher-quality training traces than outcome-reward filtering. On the value model verification task (Table 3), SC+ReST-MCTS* (87.5% GSM8K, 39.0% MATH500) surpasses both MATH-SHEPHERD (87.1%/37.3%) and SC+MS (86.3%/38.3%), confirming the learned quality value provides better per-step discrimination. In the search-budget comparison (Figure 2), ReST-MCTS* achieves higher accuracy than Self-Consistency [23] and ORM+Best-of-N [25] at every token budget on both MATH and SciBench, reaching 48.5% vs. 42.5% for SC on MATH after 2 iterations. On SciBench (Table 4), ReST-MCTS* outperforms CoT [22] by 4.09% (GLM4: 16.77% vs. 12.68%) and ToT [24] by 0.95% (16.77% vs. 15.82%), with the gap widening for GPT-3.5-turbo (10.06% vs. 6.92% CoT, 10.06% vs. 8.44% ToT). The SciGLM ORM [10] is outperformed by the ReST-MCTS* value model on SciBench (22.9% vs. 20.2% for GLM4; 20.2% vs. 12.8% for GPT-3.5-turbo in Table 7), validating that the process-reward-based quality value provides more informative guidance than outcome-level rewards.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) MCTS-based LLM reasoning (TS-LLM, AlphaLLM), which it extends by replacing the ORM-based backup with a PRM-derived quality value that incorporates reasoning distance; (2) MATH-SHEPHERD's automatic process reward synthesis, which it augments with a weighted reward that accounts for remaining steps to the answer; and (3) MuZero's mutual policy-value self-improvement loop, which it transplants to LLM self-training by simultaneously refining both the policy and the value model across iterations. The key departure from ReST^EM and Self-Rewarding is that the value model is not static but co-trained with the policy, and the per-step reward labels are inferred from tree-search rollouts rather than requiring human annotation or relying solely on final-answer correctness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine pain point in LLM self-training: correct final answers do not guarantee correct intermediate reasoning, and dense per-step supervision has historically required expensive human annotation. By automating this via tree-search rollouts, ReST-MCTS* offers a scalable path to higher-quality training data. However, the demonstrated gains are modest (a few points over ReST^EM/Self-Rewarding), experiments are limited to 2 iterations and small models (6B–8B), and the approach requires ground-truth answers for reward inference. Open questions include generalization to tasks without verifiable answers (coding, dialogue, SWE-Bench), scaling to larger models, and whether online RL could replace the current offline SFT loop for the value model.
