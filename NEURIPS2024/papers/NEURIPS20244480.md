## Problem

Single-image relighting for general objects remains unsolved: prior methods either restrict to specific categories (portraits, outdoor scenes), require special capture setups (flash, light stages), or explicitly decompose scenes into intrinsic components (normals, BRDFs) that are inaccurate or under-expressive. No existing approach can relight an arbitrary object from a single photo under any novel environmental lighting without such constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244480/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB object image and a target HDR environment map as input. The environment map is first rotated to align with the camera coordinate frame, then split into two 256×256 conditioning maps: a tone-mapped LDR version (preserving low-intensity reflection detail) and a log-normalized HDR version (preserving the full energy spectrum). The output is a relit RGB image at the same resolution; for 3D tasks, the 2D relighting predictions are used to produce a relit NeRF appearance field. Text prompts can also drive relighting by generating an HDR panorama via Text2Light.

## Method

Neural Gaffer fine-tunes the pre-trained Zero-1-to-3 image-conditioned diffusion model on a newly constructed RelitObjaverse dataset (~18.4M rendered images from ~90K filtered Objaverse objects under ~30K HDR environment maps). The key architectural change is concatenating the VAE-encoded input image, LDR environment map, and normalized HDR environment map as additional conditioning latents to the denoising U-Net, alongside the CLIP text embedding of the input image. Rather than encoding 3D rotation parameters, the environment map is physically rotated to camera coordinates before encoding, which the authors show is significantly easier for the network to learn. For 3D relighting, a two-stage pipeline first overfits the NeRF appearance field to multi-view 2D relighting predictions via reconstruction loss, then refines details using a diffusion guidance loss that treats the coarse render as an intermediate denoising state and applies multi-step DDIM with exponentially annealed noise level.

## Results

On the single-image relighting task (48 unseen Objaverse objects, 12 unseen environment maps per camera), Neural Gaffer achieves 26.71 PSNR, 0.927 SSIM, and 0.041 LPIPS, outperforming DiLightNet (24.82 PSNR, 0.918 SSIM, 0.056 LPIPS) by ~1.9 dB in PSNR and ~27% lower LPIPS. Under channel-aligned evaluation, the gap widens to 29.83 vs. 26.93 PSNR. For 3D radiance field relighting (6 objects, 20 test poses, 3 unseen lighting conditions), Neural Gaffer's two-stage pipeline achieves 29.11 PSNR, 0.930 SSIM, and 0.039 LPIPS, surpassing NVDIFFREC-MC (28.25 PSNR, 0.082 LPIPS) and TensoIR (25.80 PSNR, 0.106 LPIPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiLightNet [79], the closest quantitative baseline for single-image relighting, Neural Gaffer wins on all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) by a clear margin, producing more accurate highlights, shadows, and high-frequency reflections. For 3D relighting, Neural Gaffer outperforms both TensoIR [30] (by 3.3 dB PSNR) and NVDIFFREC-MC [25] (by 0.86 dB PSNR), with the baselines exhibiting baked-in original highlights (TensoIR) or missed specular highlights and artifacts (NVDIFFREC-MC). In the object insertion task, Neural Gaffer qualitatively surpasses AnyDoor [14] by better preserving the identity of the inserted object and synthesizing more natural illumination. In the qualitative comparison with IC-Light [85] (Appendix A), Neural Gaffer shows consistent specular highlight movement under rotating lighting, whereas IC-Light exhibits inconsistent highlight displacement in certain regions. Overall, Neural Gaffer wins or ties on every comparison presented in the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

Neural Gaffer repurposes the Zero-1-to-3 image-conditioned diffusion model by discarding its novel-view synthesis components and fine-tuning it on a synthetic relighting dataset, following the same 'unlock latent capabilities' philosophy that Zero-1-to-3 applied to 3D reasoning. It departs from model-based inverse rendering methods (NeRFactor, TensoIR, PhySG) that explicitly decompose scenes into geometry, materials, and BRDFs, and from category-specific relighting approaches (portraits, outdoor scenes) by training a category-agnostic model on the newly constructed RelitObjaverse dataset (~18.4M rendered images from Objaverse). The two-stage 3D relighting pipeline is a novel application of a 2D diffusion prior as a regularizer for NeRF appearance optimization, replacing the over-saturated results of direct SDS-based relighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first category-agnostic single-image relighting method that handles arbitrary objects under arbitrary HDR environment lighting without explicit scene decomposition, filling a gap the authors note has remained 'elusive' in the literature. The RelitObjaverse dataset and the practical two-stage 3D relighting pipeline (minutes on a single GPU) provide immediately useful tools for both 2D editing and 3D inverse rendering. Key limitations include the 256×256 training resolution causing identity loss on fine details, persistent color ambiguity for diffuse objects, and missed high-frequency reflections; open questions include whether higher-resolution training, background conditioning, and domain-specific fine-tuning (e.g., portraits) can close these gaps.
