## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA initialize adapters in a task-agnostic manner, ignoring the context of the downstream task to learn or the world knowledge to preserve. This leads to suboptimal fine-tuning performance and catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge, as the adapter components are not aligned with the specific abilities the user cares about.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242291/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM (e.g., LLaMA-2-7B) and a small set of representative samples (as few as 32, typically 256) whose input activations are used to compute per-layer covariance matrices. It produces a fine-tuned model with the same architecture as the original, where each linear layer is split into a frozen high-rank component and a trainable low-rank adapter (rank r, typically 128) that can be merged post-training without inference overhead. The covariance matrix C = XX^T is computed from the input activation X of each linear layer, and its inverse is used to reconstruct the original weights after decomposition.

## Method

CorDA performs a context-oriented SVD by decomposing the product of each linear layer's weight W and the input-activation covariance matrix C, i.e., SVD(WC) = UΣV^T, then reconstructing W as UΣ(V^T C^{-1}) so that inference is unchanged at initialization. The covariance matrix orients the factorization so that task-relevant characteristics are aggregated into the principal singular components. Two modes are supported: knowledge-preserved adaptation freezes the largest R−r components (capturing world-knowledge-retrieving ability from QA data) and trains the smallest r components as the adapter, while instruction-previewed adaptation freezes the smallest R−r components and trains the largest r components (capturing the fine-tuning task's characteristics from instruction data). This context-oriented initialization replaces the random Kaiming initialization of LoRA or the context-free SVD of PiSSA, yielding adapters that are pre-aligned with the target ability.

## Results

In the instruction-previewed adaptation mode, CorDA (rank 128, 320M params) achieves the best average score of 22.55 across GSM8k (53.90), MATH (8.52), HumanEval (21.03), MBPP (24.15), and MTBench (5.15), surpassing full fine-tuning (22.46, 6738M params) and all PEFT baselines. In the knowledge-preserved adaptation mode, CorDA achieves the best average on Instruction Following (19.50 vs. LoRA 17.52, PiSSA 14.30) while nearly preserving world knowledge (NQ open 14.43% vs. original 14.99%). On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa_base, CorDA achieves the highest average score of 83.38, outperforming full fine-tuning (83.07) with only 21M trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CorDA in instruction-previewed mode outperforms LoRA on all five benchmarks by substantial margins (e.g., GSM8k 53.90 vs. 42.68, MATH 8.52 vs. 5.92) and similarly dominates DoRA (avg 22.55 vs. 18.18). Against PiSSA, which also uses SVD-based initialization but without data context, CorDA wins on every benchmark (GSM8k 53.90 vs. 51.48, MATH 8.52 vs. 7.60, HumanEval 21.03 vs. 19.48, MBPP 24.15 vs. 23.84, MTBench 5.15 vs. 4.92), confirming the benefit of context-oriented decomposition. Compared to full fine-tuning, CorDA surpasses it on GSM8k (+5.0), MATH (+1.04), and MTBench (+0.30) while using only 4.7% of the parameters, though it trails on HumanEval (21.03 vs. 25.42) and MBPP (24.15 vs. 25.64). In the knowledge-preserved mode, CorDA achieves the best world-knowledge retention (NQ open 9.36% vs. LoRA 1.91%, PiSSA 1.02% on Math; NQ open 14.43% vs. LoRA 10.28%, PiSSA 9.67% on Instruction Following) while maintaining competitive or superior fine-tuning scores, a trade-off no other baseline achieves. On GLUE, CorDA beats LoRA (83.38 vs. 79.43 avg) and DoRA (83.38 vs. 79.23) and even edges out full fine-tuning (83.38 vs. 83.07) with 1/6 the trainable parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

CorDA extends the LoRA framework by replacing its random (Kaiming) adapter initialization with a context-oriented SVD of the pre-trained weights, where the decomposition direction is determined by the covariance matrix of input activations from a small set of representative samples. This is conceptually closest to PiSSA, which also uses SVD to initialize adapters from the largest singular components, but CorDA orients the SVD with task-specific covariance matrices so that the principal components explicitly encode the context of the target ability (knowledge retrieval or the fine-tuning task). The idea of using activation statistics to guide weight decomposition draws from activation-aware compression methods like ASVD, but CorDA repurposes this for adapter initialization rather than model compression, and introduces the inverse-covariance reconstruction step to preserve exact inference at initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-mode design (knowledge-preserved vs. instruction-previewed) gives practitioners a practical knob to trade off fine-tuning performance against world knowledge retention, a tension that existing PEFT methods handle poorly. The knowledge-preserved mode's ability to keep NQ open near its pre-fine-tuning level (14.43% vs. 14.99%) while still outperforming LoRA on downstream tasks is a compelling practical benefit for deployment scenarios where the base model's general knowledge must survive task adaptation. A key open question, acknowledged by the authors, is how to combine the strengths of both modes into a single initialization strategy that simultaneously maximizes fine-tuning performance and knowledge preservation. The method is also limited to LLaMA-2-7B and RoBERTa-base in experiments, leaving open whether the covariance-orientation benefit persists at larger scales or with different architectures.
