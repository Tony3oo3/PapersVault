## Problem

Neural rendering via 3D Gaussian Splatting achieves high-fidelity view synthesis but produces fuzzy volumetric density fields that hinder accurate surface extraction, while SDF-based reconstruction yields precise geometry at the cost of rendering quality. Prior unified approaches (SuGaR, 2D-GS, NeuSG) resolve this by imposing explicit shape constraints on Gaussians (flattening, binary opacity), which degrades rendering fidelity. The paper addresses how to simultaneously achieve state-of-the-art rendering and reconstruction without sacrificing the representational power of either representation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiview RGB images (7/8 train, 1/8 test split) and a COLMAP sparse point cloud for Gaussian initialization. It produces two parallel outputs: a structured set of 3D Gaussian primitives (organized via Scaffold-GS anchors) for real-time novel-view rendering, and a continuous SDF field (encoded with a 16-level multi-resolution hash grid, resolution 2^5 to 2^11) from which a 3D mesh can be extracted via zero-level-set extraction. The GS-branch is trained for 15k iterations before 30k iterations of joint dual-branch optimization, with the SDF-branch warmed up for 2k–5k iterations without depth guidance.

## Method

GSDF is a dual-branch architecture pairing Scaffold-GS (GS-branch) with a hash-grid-enhanced NeuS (SDF-branch), linked by three mutual guidance mechanisms. First, the GS-branch renders per-view depth maps that guide SDF-branch ray sampling around predicted surface locations (coarse range k=3, fine range k=1), avoiding the chicken-and-egg problem of sampling an untrained SDF. Second, the SDF-branch provides per-Gaussian SDF values that modulate the density control operators: a Gaussian-shaped function of |s| boosts the growth threshold near surfaces and lowers the pruning threshold far from them, directing primitive distribution without constraining individual Gaussian shapes. Third, mutual geometry supervision enforces depth and normal map consistency between both branches via L1 depth loss and cosine-similarity normal loss. The key design choice is guiding Gaussian *placement* through geometric clues rather than constraining their *shape*, preserving the full anisotropic representational power of 3D Gaussians while aligning them with the underlying surface.

## Results

GSDF achieves the best Chamfer distance on DTU (0.802) and the highest PSNR across all four benchmark datasets: 33.65 dB (DTU), 27.37 dB (Tanks & Temples), 29.38 dB (Mip-NeRF360), and 30.38 dB (Deep Blending). It also attains the best SSIM and lowest LPIPS on every dataset, with particularly notable LPIPS improvements over Scaffold-GS (e.g., 0.156 vs. 0.182 on Tanks & Temples). On DTU reconstruction, GSDF outperforms the next-best method 2D-GS (CD 0.928) by 13.8%, while simultaneously surpassing all Gaussian-based methods on rendering quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSDF outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric across all four datasets. On DTU reconstruction (Chamfer distance), GSDF (0.802) beats the closest competitor 2D-GS (0.928) by 13.8% and NeuS (0.823) by 2.6%, while Gaussian-only methods (3D-GS: 4.034, Scaffold-GS: 5.988) are 5–7× worse. On rendering, GSDF surpasses the strongest GS-based baseline Scaffold-GS by 0.06–0.08 dB PSNR and 0.026–0.030 LPIPS on Tanks & Temples, confirming that geometry-aware densification improves perceptual quality without sacrificing fidelity. Compared to surface-constrained methods SuGaR and 2D-GS, GSDF gains 0.89–1.69 dB PSNR and 0.015–0.075 LPIPS, demonstrating that avoiding explicit shape constraints on Gaussians preserves rendering capacity. Against SDF-only baselines (NeuS, Instant-NSR), GSDF achieves 7–12 dB higher PSNR on unbounded scenes (Mip-NeRF360, Deep Blending), highlighting the complementary strengths of the dual-branch design.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSDF builds on Scaffold-GS (structured 3D Gaussians for rendering) and NeuS/Instant-NSR (hash-grid SDF for surface reconstruction) as its two backbone branches. It departs conceptually from concurrent surface-aligned Gaussian methods (SuGaR, 2D-GS, NeuSG) that enforce explicit geometric constraints on Gaussian primitives (flattening, binary opacity), which the authors argue degrades rendering quality. Instead of constraining a single unified representation to serve both tasks, GSDF keeps each branch architecturally independent and coordinates them through three mutual guidance mechanisms: depth-guided ray sampling (GS→SDF), geometry-aware Gaussian densification (SDF→GS), and mutual depth/normal supervision (GS↔SDF).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent tension in neural scene representation: methods that improve geometric accuracy by constraining primitives (e.g., flattening Gaussians) inevitably sacrifice rendering fidelity, and vice versa. The dual-branch mutual guidance framework offers a practical resolution that preserves the real-time rendering speed of 3DGS while achieving SDF-level geometric accuracy, with a 4× speedup in SDF convergence. Key limitations remain: the method does not handle reflections or strong lighting variations, requires more memory than single-branch approaches, and the MLP-based SDF branch is still significantly slower than the rasterization-based GS branch. The modular design (each branch swappable for future advances) positions it as a useful scaffold for the field, but the overall contribution is an engineering integration of known components rather than a fundamental new representation.
