## Problem

Neural mutual information estimators have been evaluated almost exclusively on Gaussian multivariate datasets where the true MI is analytically tractable, leaving their accuracy on unstructured real-world data (images, text) largely unknown. This paper asks whether estimators that perform well on Gaussian benchmarks also generalize to the high-dimensional, complex distributions encountered in vision and NLP tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241471/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark constructs paired samples (X, Y) from MNIST digit images, BERT sentence embeddings of IMDB reviews, or Gaussian vectors, where the true MI is known by construction rather than by closed-form formula. Pairs are generated via same-class sampling so that only a controlled number of binary class bits are shared, and the binary symmetric channel scales the true MI to any non-integer value. The estimators output a scalar MI estimate, and the benchmark reports bias, variance, and MSE against the known ground-truth MI across 20k training steps.

## Method

The core contribution is a dataset construction method that makes true MI accessible for arbitrary unstructured data: same-class sampling ensures I(X;Y) = H(C) by sharing only class labels between X and Y, and a binary symmetric channel with crossover probability β scales this to I(X;Y) = H(C)·(1−H(β)), enabling any non-integer target MI. On top of this, the paper systematically evaluates seven variational MI estimators (DV, NWJ, InfoNCE, JS, MINE, SMILE-1/5/inf) with MLP-based joint critics across seven controlled axes: critic architecture, critic depth, estimator identity, number of information sources (d_s up to 100), representation dimension (d_r up to 10,000), nuisance strength (CIFAR-10 backgrounds overlaid on digits), and layer-wise MI in a pre-trained ResNet-50. Key findings include that unstructured datasets tolerate far larger d_s than Gaussian (36 vs. 4 before breakdown), that no single estimator dominates across all three domains, and that MI estimation is surprisingly accurate for upper-layer deep representations but unreliable for lower layers.

## Results

The benchmark evaluates seven neural MI estimators (NWJ, DV, InfoNCE, MINE, SMILE-1, SMILE-5, SMILE-inf) across three data domains (Gaussian, Images, Text) at true MI values from 2 to 10 bits. No single estimator dominates: SMILE-1 achieves the lowest MSE on Text (0.319 at 10 bits), MINE leads on Gaussian at high MI (1.973 at 10 bits vs. DV's 1e+07), and NWJ is best on Images at 10 bits (1.580). At low MI (2 bits), all estimators achieve MSE below 0.30 across domains, but performance diverges sharply as MI increases, with InfoNCE degrading most severely (MSE of 17.757 on Text at 10 bits).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares seven variational MI estimators (NWJ, DV, InfoNCE, MINE, SMILE-1, SMILE-5, SMILE-inf) on a common benchmark with controlled true MI values. SMILE-1 is the strongest on Text, achieving MSE of 0.319 at 10 bits versus 2.027 for MINE and 2.659 for NWJ. MINE dominates Gaussian at high MI (1.973 at 10 bits) where DV catastrophically fails (MSE of 1e+07) and InfoNCE also degrades (18.400). On Images at 10 bits, NWJ (1.580) outperforms all SMILE variants (4.197–8.987) and MINE (3.249), indicating that the SMILE advantage seen on Gaussian and Text does not transfer to vision. InfoNCE is consistently the worst performer at high MI across all three domains, confirming its known log(K) upper-bound limitation. The paper also shows that the joint critic architecture is superior to separable and bilinear critics on unstructured data, and that MINE is uniquely robust to nuisance (maintaining nonzero estimates even at maximum nuisance strength η=1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the same-class sampling framework of Lee et al. [2023], which provides the theoretical guarantee that I(X;Y) = H(C) when only class information is shared between paired views, and on the binary symmetric channel from Cover [1999] for scaling true MI to arbitrary non-integer values. It departs from the dominant evaluation paradigm in the MI estimation literature (Belghazi et al. 2018, Poole et al. 2019, Song and Ermon 2019, Cheng et al. 2020), which has relied almost exclusively on Gaussian multivariates with analytically known MI, and from Czyz et al. [2023], which extended to non-Gaussian but still tractable distributions (Student's t, uniform). The key conceptual departure is that this is the first benchmark to evaluate neural MI estimators on genuinely unstructured data (MNIST images, BERT sentence embeddings) where no closed-form distribution exists, by constructing paired samples whose true MI is provably known through the same-class sampling + BSC pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first practical guidance for selecting MI estimators in real-world settings, revealing that no single estimator (NWJ, DV, InfoNCE, MINE, SMILE variants) is universally optimal and that the choice should depend on data domain and MI magnitude. The finding that unstructured datasets tolerate far larger numbers of information sources (d_s up to ~36 for images vs. ~4 for Gaussian) before estimation breaks down challenges the assumption that Gaussian benchmarks are a conservative proxy for real data. Limitations include reliance on MNIST (simple digits) and IMDB (binary sentiment) with only 50K samples, leaving open whether findings generalize to ImageNet-scale images or open-ended NLP tasks. The layer-dependency result (lower ResNet-50 layers yield systematically worse MI estimates) raises practical questions about which representations are suitable for MI-based analysis in deep networks.
