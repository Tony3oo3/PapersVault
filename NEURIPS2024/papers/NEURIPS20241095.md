## Problem

Existing neuro-behavioral decoding methods quantify how much behavioral information is encoded in neural populations but do not reveal the details of that encoding. Because neural populations across dorsal cortex regions exhibit mixed selectivity—responding robustly to multiple behaviors simultaneously—it remains unclear which specific neural patterns correspond to which distinct behaviors. The paper asks how to enable in-depth, interpretable exploration of neural dynamics that are selectively associated with individual behaviors of interest.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-trial widefield calcium imaging signals (275 dimensions extracted via LocaNMF from six dorsal cortex regions: VIS-L/R, SSp-L/R, MOs-L/R), paired 128×128 grayscale behavioral video frames (189 frames per trial at 30 Hz), and discrete behavioral labels (four tracked behaviors: levers, spouts, paw x/y, jaw) extracted via DeepLabCut and a behavior LVM. It produces a 6-dimensional disentangled neural latent trajectory per brain region (L×D matrix) and, for each latent factor, a generated single-trial behavior video whose frame-by-frame dynamics visualize the specific behavioral movement encoded by that factor.

## Method

BeNeDiff operates in two stages. First, a semi-supervised VAE with bidirectional RNN encoder/decoder learns a behavior-relevant disentangled neural subspace: behavioral labels rotate the latent space toward behavior-relevant directions, while a total-correlation penalty (estimated via minibatch-weighted sampling) enforces statistical independence among the six latent factors. Second, a 3D U-Net video diffusion model (with temporal convolutions replacing temporal self-attention for local consistency) generates behavior videos under classifier guidance. The classifier is a linear encoder mapping video frames to neural latent trajectories, and the guidance objective—inspired by Noise-Contrastive Estimation—maximizes the variance of the target latent factor's trajectory while minimizing the variance of all other factors, computed via automatic differentiation at each denoising step. This avoids the out-of-distribution artifacts of naive latent manipulation and produces temporally coherent videos that selectively reveal the behavioral dynamics encoded by each individual latent factor.

## Results

On the Musall et al. 2019 widefield calcium imaging dataset, BeNeDiff achieves the highest disentanglement (MIG) scores across both evaluated brain regions: 55.87% on VIS-Left and 58.56% on MOs-Left, substantially outperforming all baselines. However, this comes at a reconstruction cost: BeNeDiff's R² is 75.41% (VIS-Left) and 69.59% (MOs-Left), lower than SSL (81.10% and 76.65%) but comparable to pi-VAE (74.37% and 70.73%). In qualitative video generation, BeNeDiff produces behavior-specific frame differences clearly localized to the targeted latent factor (e.g., Jaw, Paw-(y)), whereas baselines produce entangled or blurred outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BeNeDiff decisively outperforms all three neural LVM baselines on the disentanglement metric (MIG): it achieves 55.87% on VIS-Left and 58.56% on MOs-Left, exceeding pi-VAE (43.98%, 42.20%), CEBRA (40.12%, 37.94%), and SSL (37.50%, 36.89%) by 12–22 percentage points. On neural reconstruction, however, BeNeDiff trades quality for disentanglement: SSL achieves the best R² (81.10% VIS-Left, 76.65% MOs-Left) and lowest RMSE, while BeNeDiff's R² is 5.7–7.1 points lower, though it remains competitive with pi-VAE. For video generation, BeNeDiff surpasses both Naive Latent Manipulation (which produces blurred, distribution-shifted frames with entangled inter-frame differences) and Classifier-free Guidance (which maintains temporal coherence but fails to isolate a single factor's dynamics, yielding only small, non-specific movements). The paper positions the reconstruction–disentanglement trade-off as an inherent tension, noting that the behavioral labels rotate the latent subspace while preserving sufficient neural information for reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

BeNeDiff sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: behavior-informed neural latent variable models (building on semi-supervised VAEs from Kingma et al. 2014 and the total-correlation disentanglement penalty from Chen et al. 2018) and video diffusion models (adopting the 3D U-Net architecture and ε-parameterization from Ho et al. 2022). The key departure from prior neuro-behavioral decoding work (Battenet, CEBRA) is the shift from quantifying how much behavioral information is encoded in neural populations to actively interpreting the dynamics of individual disentangled latent factors through generative video synthesis. The classifier-guided diffusion formulation draws on Kawar et al. 2022, but the NCE-inspired variance-maximization guidance objective—amplifying one latent factor's trajectory variance while suppressing others—is a novel repurposing of noise-contrastive estimation for neural dynamics interpretation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first generative interpretation tool for disentangled neural subspaces in widefield imaging, enabling researchers to visualize what specific neural dynamics look like in behavioral space rather than relying solely on trajectory plots or decoding scores. The cross-region temporal ordering revealed (VIS predictive coding → SSp somatosensory feedback → MOs motor execution) offers a concrete neuroscientific hypothesis testable with targeted perturbations. However, the evidence is limited to a single mouse across two sessions with 1126 trials, and the linear encoder assumption for the guidance classifier is acknowledged as a simplification. Open questions include whether the disentanglement-reconstruction trade-off (BeNeDiff sacrifices ~6% R² over SSL for substantially higher MIG) holds across species and task complexity, and whether the NCE-inspired guidance generalizes to higher-dimensional latent spaces or multi-animal recordings.
