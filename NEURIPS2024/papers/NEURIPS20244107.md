## Problem

Existing cortico-muscular connectivity measures like CMC capture only linear spectral coherence, while general statistical dependence estimators (MINE, KICA, HSIC) are unstable, produce only scalar scores, and lack spatio-temporal resolution. The paper asks whether high-level contextual factors—movement type and subject identity—can be directly learned from modeling the statistical dependence between EEG and EMG, and whether such a model can also reveal which cortical channels and time points drive the connectivity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244107/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired 4-second segments of 60-channel EEG and 7-channel EMG at 1000 Hz. During training, both modalities are fed to separate networks; at test time, only EEG is required, as the learned EEG eigenfunctions serve as a fixed feature projector for a downstream MLP classifier. Beyond the global eigenvalue spectrum (a multivariate dependence score) and trial-level density ratios, the method outputs channel-level dependence maps (which of the 60 EEG electrodes contribute most) and temporal-level dependence curves (when within the 4-second window the coupling peaks), both derived from the network's internal layers.

## Method

The density ratio ρ(X,Y)=p(X,Y)/(p(X)p(Y)) is treated as a positive definite kernel and decomposed into orthonormal eigenfunctions with eigenvalues via Mercer's theorem; two CNN-based networks (a per-channel temporal convnet plus a channel-aggregation MLP for EEG, and an analogous network for EMG) are trained to approximate the dominant eigenfunctions. The key technical contribution is FMCA-T, which minimizes a matrix trace cost −Tr(R_F⁻¹ P R_G⁻¹ Pᵀ) that directly optimizes the sum of the top-K eigenvalues, replacing the less stable log-det cost and reducing computation to K×K matrix operations. A theoretical lemma proves that under conditional independence given latent factors z={subject, movement, other}, the eigenfunction span provably encodes z, so learning dependence implicitly learns contextual structure without labels. After training, channel-level and temporal-level dependencies are extracted top-down by computing density ratios between the network's internal layer activations (per-channel features, adjacent conv-layer features) and the global output, then recursively aggregating layer-wise ratios to produce a localized spatio-temporal response for any EEG trial.

## Results

On the EEG-EMG-Fusion dataset (25 subjects, 11 movements), FMCA-T's eigenfunctions achieve 99.4% inter-subject 3-class movement accuracy and 54.0% cross-subject 3-class accuracy, outperforming all supervised and self-supervised baselines. For the more granular 11-class sub-movement task, FMCA-T reaches 32.0% inter-subject accuracy (chance level: 9%). On the SinWav robustness benchmark, FMCA-T maintains stable dependence estimates across increasing levels of nonstationary noise (white, Gaussian, pink) and random delays, while MINE fails to converge and CC produces negative values under phase shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FMCA-T outperforms the strongest supervised baseline EEGNet by 7.2% on inter-subject 3-class accuracy (99.4% vs. 90.4%) and the classical CSP-RLDA method by 1.0% (99.4% vs. 98.5%), the latter being trained per-subject and thus representing an upper bound for classical approaches. Against self-supervised baselines, FMCA-T exceeds the best (VicReg, 89.9%) by over 9.5% on inter-subject 3-class and by 10.1% on cross-subject 3-class (54.0% vs. 44.9%), indicating that density-ratio-based dependence learning is more robust to distribution shift than contrastive objectives. On the 11-class sub-movement task, FMCA-T (32.0%) surpasses the best supervised method Deep4 (27.4%) by 4.6%, while CSP-RLDA is infeasible for this granularity. The log-det variant FMCA-LD achieves comparable but less stable results (50.9% vs. 54.0% cross-subject 3-class), confirming the trace cost's advantage in training stability. On the SinWav robustness benchmark, MINE fails to converge and produces unstable estimates, while FMCA-T maintains invariance to noise magnitude and delay, and CC produces negative dependence values under phase shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Functional Maximal Correlation Algorithm (FMCA) framework [19], which decomposes density ratios as positive definite functions and learns their spectral decomposition via neural networks, to the specific domain of EEG-EMG cortico-muscular connectivity. It departs from the prior log-determinant cost by proposing a simpler matrix trace cost (FMCA-T) that directly optimizes the sum of eigenvalues, and introduces a top-down aggregation scheme for channel-level and temporal-level dependencies that has no counterpart in the original FMCA. The work also contrasts with traditional Cortico-Muscular Coherence (CMC), which captures only linear spectral coherence, and with scalar-valued dependence estimators (MINE, KICA, HSIC) that produce a single number without interpretable feature spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the eigenfunctions of the EEG-EMG density ratio, learned entirely without labels, encode movement type and subject identity, effectively serving as unsupervised feature extractors that outperform supervised and self-supervised EEG classifiers by 7–10% in cross-subject settings. This opens a path toward label-free BCI calibration and patient monitoring where annotated data is scarce. Key limitations include the small dataset (25 subjects, 11 discrete movements), absence of patient populations, and reliance on a CNN backbone; future work on continuous kinematics prediction, larger cohorts, and attention-based architectures would strengthen the framework's practical impact.
