## Problem

Existing collaborative perception systems assume all agents' multi-view cameras are continuously available, yet in practice cameras can be blurred, occluded, or completely failed at unpredictable times and in unpredictable numbers. This paper formulates the 'camera-insensitivity' problem: how to recover from failed camera perspectives and stabilize high collaborative performance without adding extra communication overhead or requiring knowledge of which specific cameras have failed.

## Input / Output

The method takes BEV feature maps extracted from multi-agent camera images (some of which are corrupted by a noise function simulating failure) and produces recovered RGB camera views for the failed perspectives, which are then fed into an existing collaborative perception backbone to yield map-view segmentation (drivable area, lanes, vehicles) or detection outputs. A key design constraint is that RCDN introduces zero additional communication during inference—only ground-truth color labels are used during training—making it a drop-in module for any BEV-based collaborative perception pipeline. The accompanying OPV2V-N dataset adds manual annotations for camera-failure timestamps/IDs, OneFormer foreground masks, and RAFT optical flow on top of the original OPV2V data.

## Method

RCDN constructs a collaborative neural rendering field with two parallel components: a time-invariant static background field and a time-varying dynamic foreground field. Both fields are conditioned on a geometry BEV volume feature—BEV maps lifted to 3D via a height-estimation decoder—and use multi-resolution hash grid encoding (Instant-NGP style) for fast, generic optimization. The static field predicts per-ray volume density and RGB color, rendered via α-composited volumetric integration; the dynamic field additionally predicts forward/backward 3D scene flow vectors, a blending weight, and uses temporal interpolation to warp the field across adjacent timesteps. Training combines static reconstruction loss, dynamic reconstruction loss over a 3-frame window, 2D optical-flow matching loss for scene flow supervision, and cycle-consistency regularization, with the two fields trained separately. The explicit hash-grid representation accelerates training by ~24× over implicit MLP-based dynamic modeling while achieving higher PSNR, and the entire module is portable across baselines (F-Cooper, AttFuse, DiscoNet, V2VNet, CoBEVT) without architectural modification.

## Results

On OPV2V-N with one random camera failure, RCDN recovers map-view segmentation IoU across all five baselines. For example, CoBEVT's drivable-area IoU drops from 51.96 (normal) to 32.08 without RCDN but recovers to 47.19 with RCDN (↑47.10%), while its dynamic vehicle IoU goes from 56.61 → 32.41 → 55.10 (↑70.01%). The largest relative gains appear on DiscoNet's vehicle IoU (9.25 → 43.03, ↑365.19%) and V2VNet's lane IoU (10.52 → 25.24, ↑139.92%). Under three random camera failures, RCDN maintains 84.95–90.81% of standard-setting dynamic performance, whereas baselines without RCDN retain only 13.09–28.11%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCDN is evaluated as a plug-in module on five collaborative perception baselines (F-Cooper, AttFuse, DiscoNet, V2VNet, CoBEVT), all sharing the same CVT-based BEV encoder. In every case, RCDN substantially narrows the performance gap between normal and camera-failure settings: the average drivable-area IoU drop shrinks from ~37.7% (w.o. RCDN) to ~5.3% (w. RCDN), and the average vehicle IoU drop shrinks from ~60.8% to ~3.3%. The most dramatic recovery is on DiscoNet's vehicle IoU (9.25 → 43.03, a 365% relative gain), indicating that baselines most sensitive to multi-view degradation benefit most. CoBEVT, the strongest baseline, also benefits (vehicle IoU 32.41 → 55.10) but with a smaller relative gain (70%), consistent with its already-robust sparse-Transformer fusion. For training efficiency, RCDN's explicit hash-grid dynamic field converges in ~15 minutes (PSNR 23.86) versus ~6 hours (PSNR 21.83) for the implicit MLP-based dynamic modeling of Gao et al. [70], a ~24× speedup with better reconstruction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the neural rendering pipeline (NeRF volumetric rendering, Instant-NGP hash-grid encoding, Mip-NeRF 360's contract mapping) from the view-synthesis domain into collaborative perception, using it to reconstruct failed camera views from other agents' observations. It departs from prior robust perception work that relies on multi-modal redundancy (LiDAR+camera) by addressing the camera-only setting, and from prior collaborative perception work that assumes all agents' sensors are functional. The key architectural departure is splitting the collaborative scene into a time-invariant static hash-grid field and a time-varying dynamic field with learned scene flow, both conditioned on a shared geometry BEV volume feature lifted from the agents' backbone outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes camera-insensitivity collaborative perception as a formal problem and provides the first dedicated dataset (OPV2V-N) with labeled failure timestamps, filling a gap the authors identify in the literature. The portability of RCDN as a plug-in module across five heterogeneous baselines without extra inference-time communication is practically attractive for deployed V2X systems. However, the evaluation is confined to a single simulated dataset with complete camera failure (no partial degradation like blur or noise), and the absolute performance levels remain modest (~50% IoU for drivable area in the clean setting), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to real-world sensor degradation patterns and larger agent counts. The authors themselves flag real-time neural field modeling (e.g., via 3D Gaussian splatting) as a key future direction.
