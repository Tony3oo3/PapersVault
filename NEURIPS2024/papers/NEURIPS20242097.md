## Problem

Current controllable text-to-image diffusion models exhibit limited performance when conditioned on sparse skeleton human poses, particularly for complex orientations such as side or rear views where existing methods fail to maintain accurate body proportions and pose alignment. The core challenge is that skeleton poses provide only a small set of keypoints, making it difficult for standard convolutional or UNet-based adapters to capture the long-range interconnections among anatomical parts needed for faithful pose adherence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse skeleton pose image (17 COCO-style keypoints connected by color-coded lines, rendered at 512×512) and a free-form text prompt, and outputs a photorealistic RGB human image at the same resolution. The pose image is extremely sparse—mostly black background with thin colored lines—so the adapter must infer full body structure from minimal geometric cues. Stable-Pose operates as a plug-in on frozen Stable Diffusion 1.5, modifying only the latent encoding at each denoising step without altering the base model weights.

## Method

Stable-Pose introduces a lightweight adapter with two components: a 6-layer convolutional pose encoder (β) that extracts high-level features from the skeleton image, and a Pose-Masked Self-Attention (PMSA) ViT (F) with depth 2 and patch size 2 that captures patch-wise relationships across the 64×64 latent encoding. The key mechanism is a coarse-to-fine attention masking strategy: the binary pose mask is dilated with Gaussian kernels of decreasing size (k=23 then k=13, σ=3), and at each ViT block the self-attention is restricted so that every patch can only attend to patches falling within the corresponding pose mask region, progressively narrowing focus from broad body areas to precise pose details. A pose-mask guided loss further emphasizes the pose region during training by applying a weighted penalty (α=5) to the noise-prediction error within the finest mask, while the non-pose regions receive unit weight. The adapter output is added to the latent encoding via a zero-convolutional pathway structurally similar to ControlNet, keeping the base diffusion model frozen.

## Results

On the LAION-Human dataset, Stable-Pose achieves an AP of 57.11, surpassing the strongest baseline Uni-ControlNet (50.83) by over 12% and ControlNet (44.90) by approximately 27%. On Human-Art, Stable-Pose reaches AP 48.88 and CAP 70.83, outperforming all baselines by more than 4 AP points over the next-best HumanSD (44.57). Image quality metrics (FID, KID) remain competitive with baselines, with only marginal differences (e.g., FID 6.25 vs. 5.62 for HumanSD on LAION-Human), while CLIP scores stay within a narrow 32.0–32.6 range across all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stable-Pose outperforms all six baselines on pose accuracy (AP) across both main datasets. Against ControlNet, the most widely used pose-conditioning adapter, Stable-Pose gains +13.2 AP on LAION-Human (57.11 vs. 44.90) and +9.4 AP on Human-Art (48.88 vs. 39.52). Against Uni-ControlNet, the closest competitor, the margin is +6.3 AP on LAION-Human and +7.0 AP on Human-Art. HumanSD, which fine-tunes the full SD model, is outperformed by +6.2 AP on LAION-Human and +4.3 AP on Human-Art, though HumanSD achieves slightly better FID (5.62 vs. 6.25) and PCE (1.25 vs. 1.37) on LAION-Human. T2I-Adapter and GLIGEN trail substantially, with GLIGEN achieving only 19.65 AP on LAION-Human. On image quality, Stable-Pose is competitive: its FID and KID are within ~1 point of the best baselines, and CLIP scores are essentially tied (32.3–32.6 range), indicating that the pose-accuracy gains come without sacrificing text-image alignment or visual fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Stable-Pose follows the plug-in adapter paradigm established by ControlNet and T2I-Adapter, where a lightweight trainable module is injected into a frozen Stable Diffusion backbone rather than fine-tuning the entire model (as in HumanSD). Its key departure is replacing ControlNet's convolutional encoder with a Vision Transformer equipped with Pose-Masked Self-Attention (PMSA), which uses coarse-to-fine Gaussian-dilated binary pose masks to progressively restrict attention to pose-relevant patches. The pose-mask guided loss, which up-weights the pose region during training, is a targeted refinement over the standard diffusion noise-prediction objective used by prior adapters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and practically important gap: existing pose-guided T2I methods degrade sharply on non-frontal orientations (side/back views), where Stable-Pose achieves roughly 20% higher AP than the next-best method. As a lightweight adapter (~364M trainable parameters, 15h training on 2×A100), it offers a drop-in upgrade path for any pre-trained diffusion model without retraining the backbone. Open questions include whether the PMSA mechanism generalizes to other conditioning signals (edge maps, depth), whether the ViT's self-attention overhead becomes prohibitive at higher resolutions or with more persons, and whether the coarse-to-fine masking strategy transfers to video generation beyond the frame-level evaluation shown here.
