## Problem

Existing debiased LASSO confidence intervals rely on asymptotic arguments that assume the remainder (bias) term in the estimation error decomposition vanishes with high probability. In finite-sample regimes typical of real-world applications such as MRI reconstruction, this remainder term is often comparable in magnitude to the Gaussian noise component, causing standard confidence intervals to be overly narrow and overestimate certainty. The paper addresses this gap by deriving non-asymptotic confidence intervals that explicitly account for the remainder term, and extends the debiasing framework to data-driven estimators like unrolled neural networks where no sharp oracle inequalities exist.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a forward operator A ∈ C^{m×N}, a correction matrix M, a set of l estimation data pairs (b^(i), x^(i)) independent of any training set, and a significance level α. It produces per-pixel confidence circles C_j(α) in the complex plane centered at the debiased estimator x^u, with radii r_j(α) that combine a Gaussian noise component and a data-driven remainder correction. A key practical constraint is that the estimation dataset must be independent of the training data used to learn the estimator X̂, and the noise level σ must be known or separately estimated.

## Method

The core mechanism decomposes the debiased estimator error as x^u − x⁰ = W + R, where W is Gaussian (from measurement noise) and R is a remainder term. For the distribution-free case (Theorem 2), the paper estimates the mean Ŝ_j and variance (σ̂_R)²_j of |R_j| from the estimation data and applies an empirical Chebyshev inequality to bound P(|R_j| ≥ r_j^R), splitting the total error probability α into γ_j·α for W and (1−γ_j)·α for R. For the Gaussian-remainder case (Theorem 3), the combined W+R is treated as a single Gaussian, yielding tighter radii that merge both variance terms. The framework is estimator-agnostic: it applies to any learned reconstruction function X̂ (LASSO, It-Net, U-Net) without requiring convergence or oracle inequality guarantees. Empirically, the remainder term is validated to be approximately Gaussian in high-dimensional settings, making the tighter Theorem 3 intervals practical for MRI and sparse regression tasks.

## Results

In the sparse regression setting (N=10000, m=0.6N, 10% sparse, 20% relative noise), the asymptotic debiased LASSO CIs achieve only 86.9% overall coverage and 67.8% support coverage at α=0.05, well below the target 95%. The proposed Gaussian-adjusted CIs (Thm. 3) improve this to 96.9% overall and 89.5% on the support, while the fully data-driven CIs (Thm. 2) achieve 100% coverage on both. For MRI reconstruction with an 8-layer It-Net (60% undersampling, 10% noise, α=0.1), the asymptotic CIs yield 93.1% overall and 91.5% support coverage, whereas the data-driven CIs reach 99.98% and 99.95% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the asymptotic debiased LASSO confidence interval framework of Javanmard & Montanari (2018), which ignores the remainder/bias term R in the error decomposition. The paper's method consistently outperforms this baseline across all six sparse regression settings and all six MRI neural-network settings. The gap is most dramatic on the support (non-zero pixels): in the worst sparse regression case (Gaussian 60%, 20% noise), asymptotic support coverage drops to 67.8% versus 100% for the data-driven CIs — a 32-percentage-point deficit. The Gaussian-adjusted CIs (Thm. 3) close most of the gap (89.5% support coverage in the same setting) while producing narrower intervals than the fully data-driven version. For the It-Net MRI experiments at α=0.1, asymptotic support coverage ranges from 89.1% to 92.5% across undersampling levels, while the data-driven CIs maintain ≥99.95% support coverage in every setting. The trade-off is interval width: data-driven radii are roughly 2–3× larger than asymptotic radii (e.g., 0.0155 vs. 0.0049 in the main sparse regression experiment), reflecting the cost of finite-sample validity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the debiased LASSO framework of Javanmard & Montanari (2014, 2018), which decomposes the estimation error into a Gaussian component W and a remainder R, and constructs confidence intervals under the asymptotic assumption that R vanishes. The key departure is a non-asymptotic, data-driven correction that estimates the mean and variance of R from training data via concentration inequalities (Chebyshev) or a Gaussian approximation, yielding valid finite-sample CIs without requiring the high-dimensional growth conditions of prior theory. The framework is further extended to learned estimators (It-Net, U-Net), going beyond the asymptotic UQ for unrolled ISTA established by Hoppe et al. (2023), by removing the need for sharp oracle inequalities on the estimator's error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In safety-critical applications like MRI diagnosis, the standard asymptotic CIs substantially under-cover (support hit rate as low as 0.68 vs. target 0.95), creating a dangerous overestimation of certainty. The data-driven correction achieves near-perfect coverage while the Gaussian-adjusted variant offers tighter intervals, providing a practical path to reliable pixel-wise UQ for both classical and learned reconstructions. Open questions include the sharpness of the resulting intervals, whether higher-moment estimation can further tighten them, and whether the approach generalizes to architectures like vision transformers. A key limitation is that larger remainder terms necessitate proportionally wider CIs, and the method requires a known noise level and a held-out estimation dataset.
