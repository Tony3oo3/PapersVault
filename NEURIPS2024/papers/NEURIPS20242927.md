## Problem

Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) parameterizes weight updates as products of low-rank matrices, which works well for simple tasks but fails for complex downstream tasks (e.g., discrete reasoning) that require high-rank adaptations. Existing tensor-based generalizations of LoRA still operate within the low-rank framework and inherit its representational limitations. The paper asks: how can one design an efficient, high-rank fine-tuning method that avoids the full parameter cost of full fine-tuning while matching or exceeding its performance?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242927/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM (LLaMA/LLaMA2/LLaMA3 series) whose hidden dimension d must be factorable as d = d₁ × d₂ × ... × d_N, and produces a small set of trainable tensors (typically 0.03–0.26% of total parameters) applied to the q_proj and v_proj layers. After fine-tuning, the full QuanTA operator is explicitly constructed and merged into the base weight matrix, yielding zero inference overhead. For rectangular weight matrices (d ≠ k), a single 'rectangular' tensor handles the dimension change, with padding/truncation used when the axis product does not exactly match d or k.

## Method

QuanTA parameterizes the weight update ΔW as a sequential product of tensors {T^(α)}, each operating on exactly two axes of the reshaped hidden vector—directly analogous to two-qubit gates in a quantum circuit. The universality theorem (proven via SVD + quantum circuit decomposition) guarantees that any matrix of shape 2^M × 2^M can be exactly represented by a finite sequence of such two-axis tensors, while the rank representation theorem provides explicit bounds on the achievable rank of the composed operator. At initialization, a frozen copy S of the tensor circuit is subtracted (y = W₀x + T_θx − Sx) so the adapted layer equals the base model, after which S is absorbed into W₀' = W₀ + S. The composition openness theorem shows that, unlike low-rank decompositions, QuanTA's expressivity grows with network depth even without nonlinearity, since the product of two QuanTA matrices need not itself be representable by the same fixed structure.

## Results

On the DROP dataset (F1 score), QuanTA achieves 59.5 with only 0.041% trainable parameters on LLaMA2-7B, matching full fine-tuning (59.4) while LoRA at r=128 (0.996% params) reaches only 56.2. On commonsense reasoning (8-task average accuracy), QuanTA achieves 80.3% on LLaMA-7B with 0.041% parameters, surpassing full fine-tuning (78.5%) and DoRA (78.4%), and reaches 85.8% on LLaMA3-8B with 0.035% parameters, outperforming DoRA (85.2%) and LoRA (80.8%). On arithmetic reasoning (average accuracy excluding AQuA), QuanTA achieves 80.5% on LLaMA2-7B with 0.19% parameters, exceeding full fine-tuning (79.3%) and LoRA (79.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuanTA consistently outperforms LoRA across all model sizes and tasks: on DROP (LLaMA2-7B), QuanTA achieves 59.5 F1 vs. LoRA's 56.2 at r=128, using 24× fewer parameters (0.041% vs. 0.996%); on commonsense reasoning (LLaMA-7B), QuanTA scores 80.3% vs. LoRA's 74.7% average. Against DoRA, QuanTA wins on most benchmarks (e.g., 80.3% vs. 78.4% on LLaMA-7B commonsense, 85.8% vs. 85.2% on LLaMA3-8B) while using roughly 1/10th the parameters. On the DROP dataset, QuanTA also surpasses MoRA (58.6–58.9 F1) and LoRETTA (48.6–59.1 F1) at comparable or lower parameter budgets, and outperforms KronA (50.9–58.5 F1) by a wide margin. Notably, QuanTA matches or exceeds full fine-tuning on DROP (59.5 vs. 59.4) and commonsense (80.3% vs. 78.5% on LLaMA-7B) with only 0.041% trainable parameters, and surpasses full fine-tuning on arithmetic reasoning (80.5% vs. 79.3% on LLaMA2-7B). On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa, QuanTA slightly outperforms LoRA on RTE (77.26 vs. 74.51) and STS-B (90.68 vs. 90.48) while using fewer parameters (0.62% vs. 0.71%), though it trails slightly on SST-2 (93.81 vs. 94.01).

## Relation to Prior Work

QuanTA sits within the reparameterization-based PEFT family (alongside LoRA, DoRA, VeRA) but departs fundamentally from the low-rank assumption that underpins LoRA and its variants. Conceptually, it draws on quantum circuit theory—specifically the universality of two-qubit gate decompositions (Nielsen & Chuang)—to motivate sequential tensor operations on specific axes of a reshaped hidden vector, and it subsumes KronA's Kronecker-product parameterization as a special case. Unlike tensor-decomposition approaches (LoRETTA, LoTA) that still operate within a low-rank or fixed-rank framework, QuanTA's composition of local tensors on different axis pairs enables genuinely high-rank updates with theoretical guarantees (universality and rank representation theorems) that low-rank methods cannot provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QuanTA addresses a concrete and well-documented failure mode of LoRA—the low-rank hypothesis breaking down on complex reasoning tasks—while retaining the zero-inference-overhead property of reparameterization methods. The 10× parameter efficiency over LoRA at r=128 while matching or exceeding full fine-tuning on DROP is practically significant for resource-constrained deployment. Open questions include whether the sequential tensor application can be made GPU-efficient for small tensors, whether the approach generalizes beyond the LLaMA family, and whether the quantum-circuit framing can yield further structural insights (e.g., entanglement-based regularization) beyond the current classical tensor-contraction implementation.
