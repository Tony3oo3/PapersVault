## Problem

Standard latent linear dynamical systems model all neurons as arising from a shared set of latent variables, ignoring the fact that neural circuits contain distinct excitatory and inhibitory cell types with fundamentally different interaction rules. This prevents such models from capturing the functional roles of individual cell classes or from predicting the effects of cell-specific perturbations (e.g., optogenetic silencing of E or I neurons) on population dynamics and behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241060/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes zero-centered firing rates (50 ms bins) from multi-neuron Neuropixels recordings, along with known E/I cell-type labels, region identity (FOF vs. ADS), and per-time-bin left/right click counts as external inputs. It produces cell-type-specific latent trajectories, a Dale's-law-constrained dynamics matrix (E columns non-negative, I columns non-positive), a non-negative block-diagonal emission matrix, and downstream predictions including behavioral choice, in-silico perturbation effects, and cell-type assignments for neurons with unknown identity. In the multi-region variant, inter-region communication from FOF to ADS is additionally constrained to be excitatory-only, reflecting known cortical projection anatomy.

## Method

CTDS extends a standard LDS by partitioning the latent space into separate E and I subspaces, then imposing Dale's-law sign constraints on the columns of the dynamics matrix A and non-negativity plus block-diagonality on the emission matrix C. Parameters are learned via EM: the E-step uses Kalman filtering and smoothing, while the M-step solves two constrained quadratic programs (for A and C) using MOSEK/CVXPY, since the structural constraints eliminate closed-form updates. A key theoretical contribution establishes an exact mapping between a low-rank EI-RNN and a CTDS via non-negative matrix factorization of the absolute connectivity matrix, which both validates the model and provides a principled initialization. The multi-region extension assigns distinct latents per region per cell type, with within-region Dale's-law dynamics and excitatory-only long-range projections, enabling in-silico optogenetic perturbations by clamping cell-specific latents and cell-type inference by solving two non-negative regression problems for each unknown neuron.

## Results

On held-out trials from the FOF-ADS auditory decision-making task (109 neurons, 353 trials), multi-region CTDS achieves higher log-likelihood than both standard LDS and CTDS without regional constraints (Fig. 2B). All three model classes predict the animal's behavioral choice well above the 50% chance level using a logistic regression classifier on inferred latents (D=6 per cell-type; Fig. 2C). In in-silico optogenetic perturbation experiments, CTDS replicates the experimentally observed ipsilateral bias patterns for FOF E-cell silencing (low bias early, high bias late) and ADS I-cell silencing (high bias both halves), while standard LDS fails to reproduce these patterns (Fig. 3). CTDS also infers cell-type identities for up to 50% of masked neurons well above chance (Fig. 6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CTDS against two baselines: a standard multi-region LDS (no cell-type constraints) and a CTDS variant without regional structure. On held-out log-likelihood, multi-region CTDS outperforms both the standard LDS and the region-free CTDS, confirming that both E-I structural constraints and regional decomposition improve model fit. On choice prediction accuracy (D=6 per population), all three models perform comparably and well above chance, indicating that choice information is accessible regardless of model structure. The critical differentiation emerges in perturbation prediction: CTDS accurately replicates the experimentally observed behavioral bias patterns for both FOF E-cell and ADS I-cell inactivation (matching Hanks et al. 2015 and Yartsev et al. 2018), whereas the standard LDS produces a small, uniform bias across all perturbation conditions and fails to capture the differential effects of early vs. late inactivation. This failure is attributed to the LDS's lack of cell-type-specific latents and Dale's-law constraints, which prevents it from correctly modeling how silencing one cell class propagates through the circuit. The region-free CTDS also underperforms the multi-region variant, highlighting the importance of explicit inter-region communication constraints (e.g., excitatory-only FOF→ADS projections).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent linear dynamical system (LDS) framework for neural population modeling by introducing cell-type-specific latent variables constrained by Dale's law, building directly on the standard LDS formulation (Macke et al., 2011) and the multi-region extension (Glaser et al., 2020). The theoretical connection to low-rank recurrent neural networks, established by Valente et al. (2022), is leveraged both to prove a formal equivalence between EI-RNNs and CTDS and to provide a principled NNMF-based initialization. The key conceptual departure from prior LDS work is the imposition of sign constraints on the dynamics matrix (E latents excite, I latents inhibit) and non-negativity on the emission matrix, which encodes biological structure that standard models ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CTDS provides a principled framework for making cell-type-specific causal predictions (e.g., optogenetic perturbation effects) from purely observational data, which standard LDS models cannot do. The finding that both E and I latents encode behavioral choice, and that the model trained only on unperturbed data replicates the specific temporal patterns of in-vivo perturbation deficits, suggests that cell-type structure is not merely descriptive but functionally necessary for causal inference in neural circuits. Key limitations include the restriction to linear dynamics, two broad cell classes (E/I), a single animal, and a single task; extending to nonlinear dynamics, finer-grained cell types, and multi-animal validation are natural next steps.
