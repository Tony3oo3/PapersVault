## Problem

Model-based offline reinforcement learning methods rely on ensemble-based uncertainty penalties on the Bellman update to prevent value overestimation from out-of-distribution states, but these penalties add complexity and can be suboptimal. The paper asks whether value overestimation can be addressed structurally by jointly modeling the state-action distribution rather than learning a conditional dynamics model p(o|a) and penalizing uncertainty separately.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242248/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of observation-action-reward sequences, where observations are either low-dimensional feature vectors (D4RL locomotion and antmaze environments) or RGB images (V-D4RL cheetah-run and walker-walk environments), with sequences of length 50. The trained policy outputs actions in the original action space, but internally operates in a 12-dimensional latent action space: the policy produces a bounded latent action in [-1,1], which is mapped through a linear transformation dependent on the latent action prior's mean and standard deviation, then decoded into a concrete action via a Beta-distributed action decoder. This two-stage generation ensures all produced actions fall within the dataset's action support.

## Method

C-LAP introduces a recurrent latent action state-space model that jointly models p(o_{1:T}, a_{1:T}) by factorizing over latent states s_t and latent actions u_t, trained by maximizing an ELBO with observation reconstruction, action reconstruction, observation consistency (KL between state posterior and prior), and action consistency (KL between action posterior and prior) terms. The key architectural difference from standard RSSM-based methods is the addition of a latent action prior p_θ(u_t|s_t) and a generative action decoder p_θ(a_t|s_t, u_t), which together impose an implicit constraint: any latent action sampled from the prior is decoded to an action within the dataset's distribution. Policy optimization is then formulated as a constrained objective where the policy π_ψ(u_t|s_t) must stay within the support of the latent action prior, implemented by explicitly parameterizing the policy output as g(û_t, μ_θ, σ_θ) = μ_θ + û_t · ε̃ · σ_θ, where û_t ∈ [-1,1] is the bounded policy output and ε̃ controls the support width. The actor-critic agent is trained on imagined trajectories generated by the model, using n-step return targets with backpropagated gradients through the latent dynamics, eliminating the need for any uncertainty penalty on the Bellman update.

## Results

On the V-D4RL benchmark (visual observations), C-LAP achieves an average normalized return of 58.8, substantially outperforming the best prior method (LOMPO at 31.5) and Offline DV2 (27.0). On the D4RL benchmark (low-dimensional features), C-LAP achieves a locomotion average of 83.6 (excluding expert) and a navigation average of 69.7 on antmaze, where MOPO and MOBILE fail entirely (0.0). C-LAP also requires roughly one-third the gradient steps of MOPO and MOBILE to reach comparable or superior performance on locomotion tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the V-D4RL benchmark, C-LAP outperforms both Offline DV2 and LOMPO across all eight datasets, with the largest margins on medium-expert and expert datasets (e.g., 93.2 vs. 58.0 on walker-walk-medium-expert vs. LOMPO reimpl.; 73.2 vs. 14.9 on cheetah-run-medium-expert). On the D4RL locomotion tasks, C-LAP is competitive with MOBILE (93.1 vs. 83.6 locomotion average excluding expert) but requires roughly one-third the gradient steps; MOPO trails at 74.9. C-LAP decisively outperforms all baselines on the antmaze navigation environment (69.7 average), where both MOPO and MOBILE achieve 0.0 on every dataset. Against PLAS (a model-free latent action method), C-LAP wins on most locomotion datasets and all antmaze tasks, confirming that the joint generative model of observations and actions provides a stronger inductive bias than modeling actions alone. The ablation in Section 4.2 further shows that removing the support constraint or the latent action space causes value overestimation and return collapse, validating both design choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

C-LAP extends the recurrent state-space model (RSSM) architecture from Dreamer by introducing a latent action space, drawing on the latent action ideas from PLAS and LASER. The key conceptual departure from prior model-based offline RL methods (MOPO, MOBILE, LOMPO, Offline DV2) is replacing the conditional dynamics model p(s|a) with a joint generative model p(s,a), which eliminates the need for ensemble-based uncertainty penalties on the Bellman update. The policy constraint is inspired by support-constraint formulations (SPOT) but is implemented through explicit parametrization of the policy relative to the latent action prior rather than through a divergence-based regularization term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of uncertainty penalties simplifies the training pipeline and significantly reduces gradient steps needed for policy convergence, while the 'jump-start' effect (a randomly initialized policy already generates high-reward actions via the decoder) is a practical advantage for compute-constrained settings. The near-doubling of the best average score on V-D4RL (31.5 → 58.8) suggests that joint state-action modeling is particularly beneficial when observations are high-dimensional and the action distribution is narrow. Open questions include how the approach handles datasets with highly diverse or random actions (where the generative action model's inductive bias weakens), whether the constraint parameter ε̃ can be made adaptive, and whether the added model complexity is justified over simpler uncertainty-penalty approaches in all regimes.
