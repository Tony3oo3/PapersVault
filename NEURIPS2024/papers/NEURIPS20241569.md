## Problem

Current RL methods converge to a single solution per task, making agents brittle to environmental variations and unable to exhibit the flexible, multi-strategy behavior characteristic of human intelligence. Existing diversity-learning approaches like DOMiNO rely on Lagrangian constraints and Van der Waals forces that collapse in highly dynamic environments (e.g., GranTurismo 7) and are not designed for multi-context settings where task requirements and environment dynamics change per episode.

## Input / Output

At each training step, the agent receives three inputs: a context vector c encoding task requirements and environment dynamics (e.g., gravity coefficient and height thresholds in MuJoCo, or track conditions in GT), a one-hot policy identifier z selecting which of n policies is active, and the raw environment state s. The critic network outputs extrinsic value estimates, intrinsic (diversity) value estimates, and successor feature vectors ψ̃^γ(s,a,z,c) per policy. The final output is a set of n diverse near-optimal policies, each producing actions conditioned on (s, c, z), with diversity measured as the sum of minimum L2 distances between successor feature vectors across all policy pairs within each context.

## Method

DUPLEX is an actor-critic algorithm built on SAC that jointly trains n policies to maximize both extrinsic reward and successor-feature-based diversity under a near-optimality constraint. Four key mechanisms distinguish it from prior work: (i) a dynamic intrinsic reward factor χ that automatically scales diversity rewards as a fraction of the average extrinsic value, removing the need for environment-specific tuning; (ii) a sigmoid-based soft lower bound λ that constrains each policy's exploration to a near-optimal subspace defined by threshold β, replacing brittle Lagrangian constraints; (iii) an entropy term α_H added to the successor feature TD target (Eq. 6–8), which stabilizes SF estimation by accounting for policy stochasticity; and (iv) averaging the two critic estimates for SFs rather than taking the minimum, since SFs serve as distance metrics where precision matters more than avoiding overestimation. The algorithm operates within a Contextual MDP framework, uniformly sampling a context and a policy at the start of each episode, and uses a context-conditioned repulsive reward (Eq. 2) that pushes each policy away from its nearest neighbor in successor-feature space.

## Results

In GranTurismo 7, DUPLEX is the only method to learn diverse near-optimal driving policies, while DOMiNO collapses all policies to a single behavior even with a very forgiving optimality ratio (ρ=0.01). In single-task Walker2D, DUPLEX achieves a superior reward-diversity Pareto frontier over DOMiNO_avg across multiple optimality ratios (vanilla SAC baseline scores 5576.24 ± 256.783). In multi-context MuJoCo (Walker2D and Ant), DUPLEX is the only algorithm that simultaneously achieves competitive reward and meaningful diversity in both in-distribution and OOD settings (gravity 3× and 24×, height 20% below training range), outperforming UVFA in all OOD scenarios. Computationally, DUPLEX adds negligible overhead (~55 ms vs. 53 ms for DOMiNO and 41 ms for SAC per update step) and does not scale with the number of policies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DOMiNO (Zahavy et al., ICLR 2023), DUPLEX wins decisively across all three benchmarks: in GT, DOMiNO fails to produce any diversity (all policies converge to the same driving style) while DUPLEX maintains distinct lap times and driving styles; in Walker2D single-task, DUPLEX achieves a better quality-diversity Pareto frontier; and in multi-context MuJoCo, DOMiNO fails to learn competitive policies at all. Against UVFA (Schaul et al., ICML 2015), DUPLEX outperforms in every OOD evaluation (both gravity extremes and reduced height constraints) and covers a substantially wider range of diversity values while still completing tasks. Against USFA (Borsa et al., ICLR 2019), DUPLEX succeeds in multi-context settings where USFA fails entirely during training; the paper attributes this gap to DUPLEX's entropy-regularized successor feature estimation (Eq. 7), which ablation studies confirm is the critical differentiator. Notably, USFA and UVFA do not optimize for diversity by design, so their diversity scores are near zero, making the comparison primarily about whether diversity learning can be added without sacrificing the OOD generalization that UE frameworks provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

DUPLEX extends the diversity-learning line initiated by DOMiNO [6] from single-task settings to the Contextual MDP framework, replacing DOMiNO's Lagrangian multipliers and Van der Waals forces with a dynamic intrinsic reward factor (χ) and a sigmoid-based soft lower bound (λ) that the authors found more stable in complex domains. On the universal-estimator side, it builds on UVFA [8] and USFA [9] for successor-feature estimation, but introduces two key modifications: an entropy regularization term (borrowed from SAC) in the SF learning objective, and averaging (rather than min) of twin critic estimates for SF targets. The method is grounded in SAC/QRSAC as the base actor-critic algorithm and positions itself against Quality-Diversity evolutionary approaches by using reward-based optimization rather than evolutionary search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DUPLEX is the first method to learn diverse, competitive driving styles in a hyper-realistic physics simulator (GranTurismo 7) and to demonstrate OOD generalization of diverse policies in multi-context robotics, filling a gap where prior diversity methods either collapsed to single behaviors or failed entirely in complex dynamics. The entropy-regularized SF estimation is a reusable component that improves robustness beyond this specific algorithm (validated via the USFA ablation). Open questions include scalability beyond ~20 policies, per-policy controllability of diversity, and whether learned diverse policies can be dynamically composed into a single agent — directions the authors flag as promising but unresolved.
