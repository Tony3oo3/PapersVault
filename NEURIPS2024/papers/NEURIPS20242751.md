## Problem

Mixed-integer non-linear bilevel optimization problems are notoriously hard: state-of-the-art exact solvers are restricted to linear objectives and scale poorly, specialized algorithms do not generalize beyond their target problem class, and no existing method is designed for the data-driven setting where similar instances are solved routinely. NEUR2BILO addresses all three limitations simultaneously by providing a general, fast, learning-based framework that handles non-linear objectives and integer variables across diverse application domains.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input both instance-level features (objective and constraint coefficients defining a specific bilevel problem) and the leader's decision variable values, producing a leader decision vector x* and a bilevel-feasible follower solution y* via a post-processing procedure that solves the follower's problem under the fixed x*. A single trained model is deployed across multiple unseen test instances of the same problem class, and the architecture is designed to be permutation-invariant over the set of leader variables using a DeepSets-style aggregation, enabling generalization across instances of varying size.

## Method

NEUR2BILO offers two single-level reformulations: an upper-level approximation that trains a neural network to predict the leader's objective value F(x, y*) and embeds it as a MILP to optimize over x alone, and a lower-level approximation that trains a network to predict the follower's optimal value Φ(x) and substitutes it into the Value Function Reformulation with a slack variable to guarantee feasibility. The architecture first embeds instance features via a set-based (DeepSets) network, then computes per-variable embeddings that interact with the instance embedding, and finally aggregates to produce a scalar value prediction. The trained ReLU network is embedded as a MILP using standard big-M formulations, making the surrogate solvable by an off-the-shelf MIP solver. For interdiction problems where leader and follower share the same objective, the paper proves a constant additive approximation guarantee of opt + 3α + 2Δ/λ, where α is the network's prediction error and Δ captures the integrality gap of the follower's value landscape.

## Results

On the Knapsack Interdiction Problem (KIP) with n=100 items, NEUR2BILO (both NN^l and NN^u) achieves 0.66% mean relative error (MRE) in ~28 seconds, while the exact branch-and-cut (B&C) solver requires the full 1-hour time limit and still yields 7.64% MRE. On the Critical Node Problem (CNP), NN^u achieves 1.14% MRE in 0.43 s on average versus 1.50% MRE in 2,942 s for B&C. On the Discrete Network Design Problem (DNDP), NN^u reaches 1.80% MRE in 0.01 s, matching the 1.76% MRE that the KKT-based baseline (MKKT) requires 10 s to achieve. On the Donor-Recipient Problem (DRP), NN^u finds the best-known solution on every instance in ~0.1 s, a 26% improvement in solution quality over the specialized B&C+ algorithm at a 1-hour limit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the exact branch-and-cut solver of Fischetti et al. [24] (B&C), NEUR2BILO trades a small quality gap for a massive speedup: on small KIP instances (n≤30) B&C achieves 0% MRE but takes 95 s on average, while NN^l/NN^u reach ~1.86% MRE in under 1 s; on larger KIP (n=100) and CNP (|V|≥100) instances, NEUR2BILO actually outperforms B&C in both quality and speed (0.66% vs 7.64% MRE on KIP; 0.45–1.14% vs 1.79–2.47% MRE on CNP). Against the KKT-based method of Fontaine & Minner [25] (MKKT) on DNDP, NN^u matches or slightly exceeds the 10-s MKKT quality (1.80% vs 1.76% MRE) in 0.01 s, a 1000× speedup. Against the learning-based ISNN of Zhou et al. [59], NEUR2BILO is decisively superior: 10–100× lower MRE (e.g., 1.86% vs 164.49% average on KIP n≤30) and roughly 1000× faster (1 s vs 264 s). Against the specialized B&C+ algorithm of Ghatkar et al. [27] on DRP, NN^u finds the best-known solution on every instance in ~0.1 s, improving solution quality by 26% over B&C+ which still has ~50% gaps at 1 hour.

## Relation to Prior Work

NEUR2BILO combines two well-established ingredients — MILP-representable neural networks (Fischetti & Jo, 2018) and the Value Function Reformulation (Lozano & Smith, 2017) — into a single non-iterative, data-driven framework for mixed-integer non-linear bilevel optimization. It departs from prior learning-based bilevel methods (Bagloee et al., 2018; Zhou et al., 2024; Sinha et al., 2016–2018) by being non-iterative, instance-agnostic (trained once across a family of instances via a DeepSets-style architecture), and applicable to both upper- and lower-level value functions simultaneously. The paper also positions itself against exact branch-and-cut solvers (Fischetti et al., 2017; Tahernejad et al., 2020) that are restricted to linear bilevel problems and scale poorly, as well as problem-specific heuristics that do not generalize.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NEUR2BILO provides the first general-purpose, learning-based heuristic that handles mixed-integer non-linear bilevel problems across diverse domains (interdiction, network security, healthcare, transportation) with a provable constant additive optimality gap for interdiction problems. A key limitation is that no tested benchmark involves coupling constraints in the leader's problem, leaving an important gap in the method's demonstrated generality. Future directions include bilevel stochastic optimization, robust optimization with decision-dependent uncertainty, and multi-level (beyond two) problems. For practitioners routinely solving similar bilevel instances, the framework offers a practical path to near-optimal solutions in seconds where exact methods require hours or fail entirely.
