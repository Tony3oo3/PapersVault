## Problem

Web-scale visual entity recognition—matching images to specific entities among millions of Wikipedia articles—suffers from a lack of clean, large-scale training data. Existing entity-based datasets like Entity-WebLI are noisy due to language ambiguity, irrelevant captions, and imprecise text-embedding matching, causing models to hallucinate or output overly generic entities. This paper tackles the data quality bottleneck rather than proposing a new architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241089/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives an RGB image and a free-form text question (e.g., 'Which type of animal is depicted?') and autoregressively generates a Wikipedia entity name (page title) as output, with beam search constrained to the 6M-entity vocabulary at the final decoding step. During training, each image is paired with three target types: a verified/corrected entity name, a two-sentence visually grounded rationale, and three diverse question-answer pairs covering multiple entities in the scene. The model uses task-specific prefixes to distinguish which output type to generate.

## Method

The core insight is that a multimodal LLM (Gemini Pro) is far more reliable at verifying and correcting a candidate entity than at predicting one from scratch, especially when given external context (the candidate's Wikipedia page summary and the original image caption). The pipeline starts from Entity-WebLI's k-NN text-matching candidates, prompts the LLM to confirm or correct each assignment while generating a grounded rationale, then prompts it again to produce diverse QA pairs using the verified entity and rationale as context. The resulting REW dataset (47M images) is used to train a GiT-Large model (CLIP-L/14 visual encoder + 6-layer text decoder) under a multi-task objective combining entity prediction, rationale generation, and QA answering losses. This verification-plus-context strategy outperforms both raw LLM distillation and uncorrected k-NN matching by 6+ points on OVEN entity split, and the multi-task training with rationales and QAs further improves query-split performance by addressing the single-entity-per-image limitation of prior datasets.

## Results

On the OVEN benchmark, GiT-Large trained on the proposed REW-47M dataset achieves 29.6% HM top-1 accuracy on the entity split and 30.9% on the query split, setting new state-of-the-art results. This represents a +6.9% improvement over the previous best (GER-ALD, 22.7%) on the entity split and a +3.8% improvement over PaLI-17B (27.1%) on the query split, while using 42× fewer parameters (0.4B vs. 17B). On fine-grained zero-shot transfer, the REW-trained model outperforms Entity-WebLI-trained models across all five datasets (e.g., 88.2% on Oxford Flowers vs. 79.8%, 50.3% on FGVC-Aircraft vs. 27.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed REW-trained GiT-Large (0.4B) decisively outperforms all baselines on both OVEN splits. Against the closest prior method GER-ALD [7] (same architecture, Entity-WebLI data), it gains +6.9% on the entity split (29.6 vs. 22.7) and a dramatic +25.1% on the query split (30.9 vs. 5.8), showing that LLM-verified entities and multi-task training with QAs are critical for the multi-entity query scenario. Compared to the much larger PaLI-17B [9] (17B params, WebLI-1B captions), the REW model wins by +13.6% on entity and +3.8% on query while using 42× fewer parameters. Against the dual-encoder baselines CLIPfusion and CLIP2CLIP [17], the generative REW model achieves roughly 5–19× higher HM accuracy. In the visual matching memory-base experiment (Table 3), LLM-corrected entities improve DINOv2 matching by +13.9% relative on average over candidate entities, and in the RECO contrastive training setting (Table 7), REW-47M as a 47M memory base achieves +5.4% relative improvement over CLIP, approaching the +6.7% of RECO [18] which uses a 20× larger (1B) memory base.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Caron et al. (CVPR 2024), which introduced the Entity-WebLI dataset and the GER-ALD generative approach for Wikipedia-scale visual entity recognition. The key departure is replacing the noisy text k-NN matching pipeline with a multimodal LLM (Gemini Pro) used as a verification and correction tool rather than a direct annotator, and enriching the dataset with LLM-generated rationales and question-answer pairs. The autoregressive generative framework (GiT-Large) and the OVEN benchmark formulation are inherited from prior work; the contribution is in the data curation methodology and multi-task training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that data quality can substitute for model scale in fine-grained entity recognition: a 0.4B-parameter model trained on REW-47M outperforms a 17B-parameter PaLI model by +13.6 HM on the OVEN entity split, suggesting that the field has been over-investing in model capacity while under-investing in training data curation. The finding that LLM verification substantially outperforms LLM direct annotation (14.1 vs 3.2 HM) provides a practical design principle for using LLMs as annotation tools in fine-grained domains. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond Wikipedia-scale entity sets, whether the computational cost of multimodal LLM prompting can be reduced, and whether the OVEN benchmark itself (which the authors note has annotation issues) adequately measures progress.
