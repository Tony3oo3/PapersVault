## Problem

Existing LLM knowledge distillation methods treat all training domains uniformly, ignoring that the performance gap between teacher and student models varies significantly across domains. This results in wasted computation on domains where the student already matches the teacher and insufficient focus on domains where the student lags, limiting overall distillation effectiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243118/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained teacher LLM, a smaller student LLM, a domain-partitioned training corpus (e.g., RedPajama's 7 domains plus Chinese Books, Chinese CommonCrawl, and OpenWebMath for 10 total domains), and per-domain validation sets of 500 samples each. The output is a distilled student LLM trained on approximately 15B tokens. The training corpus is not used in a fixed mixture; instead, the sampling distribution over domains is re-computed every K iterations (default 1,000 steps) based on live perplexity measurements, making the data pipeline adaptive rather than static.

## Method

DDK introduces a domain discrepancy factor computed as a softmax over the ratio of student-to-teacher perplexity on each domain's validation set, so domains where the student underperforms receive higher sampling probability. A domain knowledge guided sampling strategy then draws training batches proportionally to these probabilities, dynamically reallocating more data toward underperforming domains. To prevent destabilizing oscillations in the sampling distribution, a factor smooth updating mechanism (inspired by Adam's momentum) blends the new discrepancy factor with the previous one using a fixed smoothing coefficient α=0.5, ensuring gradual and stable shifts in data composition. The supervision loss combines standard cross-entropy with a temperature-scaled KL divergence between teacher and student logits, and the entire procedure is summarized in Algorithm 1 with periodic re-evaluation of domain gaps every K steps.

## Results

On the primary Qwen-1.5 1.8B student (distilled from Qwen-1.5 14B teacher), DDK achieves 55.41% average accuracy across 11 benchmarks, surpassing the best baseline KD (52.87%) by 2.54 points and the base student (49.39%) by 6.02 points. The largest gains appear on reasoning tasks: HumanEval improves from 11.87% (base) to 27.13% (DDK) vs. 15.63% (KD), and GSM8K rises from 38.4% to 53.54% vs. 49.58% (KD). On the LLaMA2 setting (TinyLLaMA 1.1B distilled from LLaMA2 13B), DDK reaches 29.32% average, outperforming the strongest baseline MiniLLM (26.37%) by 2.95 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDK consistently outperforms all three KD baselines across both model families. Against standard KD (Hinton et al., 2015), DDK gains 2.54 average points on Qwen-1.5 and 3.14 on LLaMA2, with the most dramatic improvement on HumanEval (27.13% vs. 15.63%, a 11.5-point gap). Against TED (Liang et al., ICML 2023), DDK leads by 2.82 points on Qwen and 3.08 on LLaMA2; TED's layer-wise hidden-state alignment does not compensate for the lack of domain-aware data scheduling. Against MiniLLM (Gu et al., 2024), which replaces forward KL with reverse KL to avoid overestimating low-probability regions, DDK still wins by 2.83 points on Qwen and 2.95 on LLaMA2. Notably, the three baselines cluster tightly (52.58–52.87% on Qwen), which the authors interpret as evidence that domain data mixture—not the choice of divergence or alignment objective—is the primary bottleneck in LLM distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDK positions itself as orthogonal to existing LLM knowledge distillation methods (standard KD, MiniLLM, TED) that focus on loss function design or hidden-state alignment, instead targeting the underexplored dimension of domain-specific data mixture composition during distillation. The domain reweighting idea draws conceptual inspiration from data mixture optimization work (DoReMi, data mixing laws) and the factor smooth updating mechanism borrows the momentum-like stabilization principle from adaptive optimizers like Adam. By framing the teacher-student performance gap per domain as a dynamic sampling signal, DDK repurposes the standard perplexity evaluation into an online curriculum control mechanism rather than a static dataset curation step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that domain data mixture is a key bottleneck—evidenced by KD, TED, and MiniLLM all clustering around 52–53% average while DDK reaches 55.4%—suggests the field has over-invested in loss-function engineering at the expense of data allocation strategy. Practically, this offers a low-overhead (only ~0.7% additional TFLOPs over standard KD) plug-in for any white-box distillation pipeline, with particularly large gains on reasoning-heavy domains (HumanEval +15.3 pts, GSM8K +15.1 pts). Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger student/teacher pairs (limited to ≤4B student here), whether it composes with instruction tuning or RLHF stages, and how sensitive the domain partitioning granularity is to performance.
