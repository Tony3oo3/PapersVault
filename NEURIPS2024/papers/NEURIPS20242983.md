## Problem

Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (SMoE) models suffer from a low expert activation issue where only a small fraction of experts (e.g., 8.33%) are actually used during training and inference, leaving the majority of expert capacity underutilized. This limits both the effectiveness and scalability of SMoE, particularly as the number of experts grows, since the model cannot leverage the full expressive power of its parallel expert networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242983/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

MH-MoE accepts the same inputs as standard SMoE layers: text token sequences (l×d) or image patch embeddings, and produces outputs of identical shape (l×d), ensuring zero additional computational cost in subsequent non-parallel layers such as multi-head self-attention. The key structural difference is internal: each token is split into h sub-tokens of dimension d/h, routed independently through experts, and merged back, so the layer's external interface is unchanged while internal expert utilization increases by a factor of h.

## Method

MH-MoE introduces a multi-head token splitting and merging mechanism around the standard SMoE routing block. A linear multi-head layer projects the input, which is then reshaped so each token becomes h sub-tokens of dimension d/h; each sub-token is independently routed to its top-k experts, meaning a single token can activate up to h×k experts rather than just k. After expert processing, sub-tokens are concatenated and passed through a merge layer to restore the original token dimensionality. A hyperparameter β scales the inner dimensions of each expert to keep total parameter count and FLOPs matched to the baseline SMoE. This design raises expert activation from ~8% to ~90% while also enabling finer-grained understanding: semantically rich tokens (e.g., polysemous words, visually complex patches) naturally distribute their sub-tokens across more diverse experts, capturing information from multiple representation spaces simultaneously.

## Results

On English-focused language modeling (9 zero-shot benchmarks), MH-MoE with 32 experts achieves an average accuracy of 40.6, surpassing X-MoE (39.1) and Dense (37.2). On the XNLI cross-lingual NLI task across 14 languages, MH-MoE (N=32) reaches 35.3 average accuracy vs. 34.5 for X-MoE and 33.5 for Dense. On masked multi-modal modeling, MH-MoE attains 70.1 VQAv2 test-dev accuracy (vs. 68.4 X-MoE, 65.9 Dense), 77.8 NLVR2 test-P accuracy, and 124.1 CIDEr on COCO captioning. Upstream, MH-MoE reduces validation perplexity from 11.96 to 10.28 (32 experts, English LM) and from 12.68 to 10.87 (32 experts, multi-modal) relative to X-MoE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against X-MoE (Chi et al., NeurIPS 2022), the primary SMoE baseline, MH-MoE wins on every reported metric: +1.1 avg accuracy on English LM (N=8), +1.5 on English LM (N=32), +0.8 on XNLI (N=32), and +1.7 on VQAv2 test-dev for multi-modal. The gap widens with more experts, confirming that MH-MoE's denser expert activation (90.71% vs. 8.33%) becomes increasingly beneficial at scale. Against AdaMV-MoE (Chen et al., ICCV 2023) on pure vision tasks, MH-MoE achieves 77.34 ImageNet-1k classification accuracy vs. 75.66, and 44.45 AP on COCO detection vs. 42.23. Against ViT-Base (Dosovitskiy et al., ICLR 2021) as the Dense baseline, MH-MoE gains +6.61 points on ImageNet-1k CLS and +4.64 AP on COCO detection, demonstrating that the multi-head splitting mechanism provides consistent improvements over both dense and standard sparse MoE architectures across text, vision, and multi-modal domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparsely-gated Mixture-of-Experts layer (Shazeer et al., 2017) by borrowing the multi-head decomposition idea from self-attention and applying it to the token-expert routing interface: each token is split into h sub-tokens that are independently routed to experts, then merged back. The backbone architecture is X-MoE (Chi et al., 2022), which itself addresses representation collapse in SMoE, and MH-MoE is explicitly designed to be decoupled and composable with other SMoE routing strategies such as GShard. Conceptually, the paper departs from the standard top-k routing paradigm where a single token activates only k experts, instead achieving denser activation through parallel sub-token routing without increasing per-token FLOPs in downstream layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in addressing a well-known bottleneck in SMoE scaling: as expert count grows, activation ratios drop sharply, wasting capacity. MH-MoE's ability to raise the effective expert ceiling from 64 to 256 (in their 7B-scale experiments) suggests that larger MoE models can be made more parameter-efficient without architectural overhauls. However, the evaluation is limited to models up to ~7B parameters and a fixed head count of h=4 in main experiments, leaving open whether the benefits persist at the 100B+ scale where SMoE is most impactful. The paper also does not deeply analyze interaction effects with other recent MoE improvements (e.g., expert parallelism strategies, auxiliary loss variants beyond load balancing), and the 'finer-grained understanding' claim, while supported by qualitative visualizations, lacks a dedicated quantitative benchmark.
