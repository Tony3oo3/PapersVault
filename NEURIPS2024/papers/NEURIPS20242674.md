## Problem

Unsupervised anomaly detection models (autoencoders, VAEs, isolation forests) are effective at flagging outliers but operate as opaque black boxes, providing no rationale for their decisions. Existing surrogate explainers—decision trees, linear models, LIME—struggle to faithfully approximate these models on high-dimensional, multimodal data, producing oversimplified or brittle rules that fail under the curse of dimensionality and data variability. This paper seeks a global, rule-based explanation that simultaneously handles high dimensionality, multimodal distributions, and perturbations while maintaining high fidelity to the original model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled tabular data (10–80 features) together with pre-computed anomaly scores and a threshold from a trained black-box detector (AE, VAE, OCSVM, or IForest). It produces a finite set of axis-aligned rules, each a conjunction of per-feature inequalities (e.g., x_i ≤ φ_i ∧ x_j > ψ_j), where a data point is classified as normal if it satisfies at least one rule and anomalous otherwise. Rules are organized per distributional segment, with typical rule counts of 13–23 and average lengths of 3–5 conditions per rule.

## Method

The approach combines two components in a divide-and-conquer pipeline. First, the Segmentation Clustering Decision Tree (SCD-Tree) extends CART by using the black-box model's anomaly scores as the splitting criterion: each node splits on the feature and threshold that maximizes the difference in model-output distributions between child subsets, recursively partitioning the feature space into homogeneous normal segments and thereby mitigating the curse of dimensionality. Second, within each leaf subspace, the Gaussian Boundary Delineation (GBD) algorithm fits a Gaussian Process with an RBF kernel to the inlier points, then defines per-feature decision boundaries as the longest interval where the GP mean exceeds a confidence threshold and the predictive variance stays below a tolerance, yielding flexible, uncertainty-aware boundaries that are robust to perturbations. The final interpretable rule set is the union of per-segment rules, each encoding the conjunction of SCD-Tree path conditions and GBD-derived feature bounds, so that a new point is flagged anomalous only if it violates every rule.

## Results

Using VAE as the black-box model, the proposed SCD-Tree + GBD method achieves TPR of 0.915–1.00, TNR of 0.9604–1.00, Fidelity of 0.9453–0.996, and Robustness of 0.8979–1.00 across four security datasets (CIC-IDS, KddCup99, TON-IoT, Webpages). When tested with four different black-box models (AE, VAE, OCSVM, iForest) on CIC-IDS, the method maintains Fidelity above 0.94 and Robustness above 0.89 in all settings, with iForest yielding the highest scores (TPR 0.9973, FD 0.9966, RB 0.9956). The ablation confirms GBD's contribution: adding GBD to VAE raises TPR from 0.9422 to 0.9979 (+0.0557) and Fidelity from 0.928 to 0.9978 (+0.0698).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms UAD (Barbado et al.) and KD (Li et al.) on TPR and Fidelity across all datasets: on CIC-IDS with VAE, the method achieves TPR 0.915 and FD 0.9453 versus UAD's 0.0213/0.1444 and KD's 0.1024/0.2001. Against EGDT (Bastani et al.), the method wins on TPR for CIC-IDS (0.915 vs. 0.0799) and TON-IoT (1.00 vs. 0.8048), though EGDT matches or slightly exceeds it on Robustness (1.00 vs. 0.8979 on CIC-IDS). LIME (Ribeiro et al.) is the closest competitor on CIC-IDS (TPR 0.9063, FD 0.941) but degrades sharply on TON-IoT (TPR 0.3994) and Web (TPR 0.2051), where the proposed method maintains TPR ≥ 0.898. Trustee (Jacobs et al.) is competitive on TON-IoT TPR (0.9998) but collapses on CIC-IDS (TPR 0.0621) and Web (TPR 0.00), while the proposed method stays above 0.898 TPR everywhere. Overall, the proposed method is the only baseline that simultaneously achieves TPR > 0.89, FD > 0.94, and RB > 0.89 across all four datasets without any catastrophic failure mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CART decision tree framework [44] by incorporating black-box anomaly detection model outputs (e.g., reconstruction error, isolation score) directly into node-splitting criteria, creating the SCD-Tree for distribution decomposition. It departs from prior surrogate-model approaches (EGDT, Trustee, LIME, knowledge distillation) that the authors argue oversimplify complex, high-dimensional decision boundaries, and from direct rule-extraction methods (UAD) that lack boundary refinement. The key methodological contribution is the two-stage pipeline: tree-based segmentation to manage the curse of dimensionality, followed by Gaussian Process regression within each leaf to delineate flexible, probabilistic boundaries—a combination not previously applied to unsupervised anomaly detection interpretation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For security practitioners in network and IoT domains, this work offers a practical path to auditing black-box anomaly detectors through human-readable rules, addressing a real trust gap in high-stakes deployment. However, the extracted rules are inherently axis-aligned, limiting their ability to capture non-linear decision boundaries, and the method is restricted to structured tabular data, excluding images, text, or time-series without preprocessing. Open questions include whether the GP boundary refinement generalizes to streaming/online settings, how the rule set adapts under concept drift, and whether domain experts actually find the generated rules actionable—none of which the paper empirically validates.
