## Problem

Existing one-shot LLM pruning methods (SparseGPT, Wanda, DSnoT) rely on heuristics rather than solving optimization problems directly, because the massive scale of LLMs makes full optimization infeasible with standard tools. This leads to suboptimal compression-accuracy tradeoffs, particularly at high sparsity levels (≥70%), where heuristic methods degrade sharply. The paper asks whether a principled optimization-based approach can be made tractable at LLM scale and whether it yields meaningfully better pruned models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the dense weight matrix of each transformer layer (Ŵ ∈ R^{N_in × N_out}) along with input activations X ∈ R^{NL × N_in} computed from 128 calibration segments of 2048 tokens from C4, propagated sequentially through previously pruned layers. It produces a sparse weight matrix W satisfying an ℓ₀ constraint ‖W‖₀ ≤ k (or an N:M structured sparsity constraint), with a ridge penalty λ₂‖Ŵ − W‖²_F preventing divergence from the original weights. Pruning is applied layer-by-layer in a forward pass, so each layer's input activations reflect the outputs of already-pruned upstream layers.

## Method

ALPS formulates layer-wise pruning as an ℓ₀-constrained quadratic optimization problem and solves it via ADMM (operator splitting), which simultaneously identifies the weight support and updates values on that support through alternating W-updates (solving a linear system involving H = XᵀX + λ₂I) and D-updates (projecting onto the ℓ₀ ball). A novel adaptive penalty parameter ρ update scheme—increasing ρ at a rate proportional to the change in support—balances exploration of high-quality supports (small ρ) against rapid convergence (large ρ), with a convergence guarantee (Theorem 1) that is novel for non-convex, non-continuous ℓ₀-constrained problems. Once the support stabilizes, ALPS switches to a post-processing phase that fixes the support and solves the resulting quadratic problem to optimality using a vectorized, GPU-parallel Preconditioned Conjugate Gradient (PCG) method, achieving 20×–200× speedup over direct matrix inversion while matching its solution quality. The framework extends naturally to N:M structured sparsity by replacing the ℓ₀ projection in the D-update with a magnitude-based N:M projection.

## Results

On LLaMA3-8B at 70% sparsity, ALPS achieves a 29% reduction in WikiText2 test perplexity and a 4%–13% improvement in zero-shot benchmark accuracy compared to the best existing method (SparseGPT). On OPT-30B at 70% sparsity, ALPS attains 12.67 WikiText2 perplexity versus 13.61 for SparseGPT, 17.47 PTB perplexity versus 18.94, and 30.55% ARC-Challenge accuracy versus 28.81%. At the single-layer level (OPT-13B, 80% sparsity), ALPS achieves a 7.6% relative reconstruction error compared to 12% for SparseGPT and >20% for Wanda and DSnoT, demonstrating the advantage of optimization-based pruning in the high-sparsity regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALPS consistently outperforms all four main baselines (Magnitude Pruning, SparseGPT, Wanda, DSnoT) across every OPT model size (1.3B–30B) at 70% sparsity on all perplexity and zero-shot accuracy metrics. The gap is most pronounced at higher sparsity: on LLaMA3-8B at 80% sparsity, ALPS achieves a 60% perplexity reduction on WikiText2 relative to the best competitor. Against SparseGPT (the strongest baseline), ALPS improves WikiText2 perplexity by ~7% on OPT-30B (12.67 vs. 13.61) and by ~29% on LLaMA3-8B at 70% sparsity. Wanda and DSnoT degrade severely at 70%+ sparsity (e.g., DSnoT reaches 11328 WikiText2 perplexity on OPT-30B vs. ALPS's 12.67), while Magnitude Pruning is essentially non-functional at this sparsity level. In the N:M sparsity setting (Table 3), ALPS also leads on most metrics, with the advantage over SparseGPT widening (e.g., 10.14 vs. 10.30 WikiText2 on OPT-30B 4:8, and 42.15 vs. 47.64 PTB on LLaMA2-13B 4:8). Against ADMM-Grad [Boza, 2024], ALPS achieves lower reconstruction error at every sparsity level (e.g., 5.32e-2 vs. 6.19e-2 at 80%) and its PCG post-processing is 10× faster (0.01s vs. 0.11s) while yielding better objective values.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALPS extends the operator-splitting (ADMM) framework from Boyd et al. (2011) and the preconditioned conjugate gradient method from Nocedal and Wright (1999) to the non-convex, non-continuous ℓ₀-constrained layer-wise pruning problem at LLM scale. It departs from prior LLM pruning work (SparseGPT, Wanda, DSnoT) that relies on heuristics—partial Hessian updates, activation-magnitude criteria, or iterative grow/prune—by directly solving the optimization problem without simplification. Unlike earlier ADMM applications in pruning (Ye et al. 2018, Boza 2024) that either addressed convex subproblems or fixed the sparsity mask externally, ALPS simultaneously optimizes both the support and the weights within a single ADMM loop, and introduces a novel penalty-parameter schedule with a convergence guarantee for the ℓ₀-constrained setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that principled optimization-based pruning can substantially outperform widely-used heuristics at LLM scale, particularly in the high-sparsity regime (≥70%) where heuristic methods degrade sharply, achieving a 29% perplexity reduction on LLaMA3-8B at 70% sparsity. The convergence guarantee for ℓ₀-constrained ADMM is, to the authors' knowledge, a novel theoretical result that opens the door to more rigorous treatment of combinatorial sparsity constraints. However, ALPS incurs a significant runtime penalty (roughly 3–7× slower than SparseGPT on large models), and performance still degrades at very high sparsity, suggesting that one-shot pruning alone may need to be combined with fine-tuning or quantization for practical deployment. Future work on structured pruning and joint quantization-pruning is identified as a natural next step.
