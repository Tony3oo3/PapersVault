## Problem

Existing single-view 3D reconstruction methods produce objects that fail under real-world physics: they topple over under gravity or deform undesirably, diverging from their intended depiction in the input image. This occurs because these methods treat reconstruction as a purely visual geometry problem, ignoring the underlying physical composition of an object—its mechanical properties, external forces, and rest-shape geometry—which jointly determine the static equilibrium state captured in an image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243788/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image along with user-specified mechanical properties (Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, mass density) and external forces (gravity, optional fixed attachment forces) as inputs. It produces a physically compatible rest-shape geometry as a tetrahedral mesh that, under the specified forces, deforms to a static equilibrium state matching the input image. The pipeline operates as a post-processing step on outputs from existing reconstruction methods; non-volumetric representations (surface meshes, point clouds, NeRFs) are first converted to tetrahedral meshes via TetWild before optimization.

## Method

The core contribution is a physically constrained optimization that decomposes object behavior into three orthogonal attributes and enforces static equilibrium as a hard constraint. The rest-shape geometry is parameterized by a plastic deformation field F_p (a per-element 3×3 transformation) applied to an initial configuration, which avoids element inversion by constraining singular values rather than using intractable inequality constraints. The optimization minimizes an alignment loss (matching the target geometry or ensuring stability) plus a bi-harmonic smoothness regularizer, subject to the FEM static equilibrium equation f_int(x_static, X_rest; Θ) = f_ext(x_static). Gradients are computed via implicit differentiation through the nonlinear constraint, reducing the problem to gradient descent on F_p alone. The material model is isotropic Neo-Hookean, and the entire procedure is a test-time optimization (≈80 s per shape) that can be plugged into any existing single-view reconstruction pipeline.

## Results

On a 100-shape subset of Objaverse, the framework is integrated with five single-view reconstruction baselines and evaluated on four physical compatibility metrics. The best overall configuration (TetSphere + Ours) achieves a mean von Mises stress of 0.19 kPa, 92.2% standability under gravity, and an image matching loss of 0.057. The most dramatic improvement is seen with Wonder3D, where standability jumps from 6.9% (baseline) to 72.4% (optimized), and mean stress drops from 10.68 kPa to 0.45 kPa. Across all five baselines, the optimization consistently reduces mean stress, increases standability, and lowers image loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method is not a standalone reconstruction model but a post-hoc physical compatibility optimization applied on top of five existing single-view reconstruction baselines: Wonder3D (NeuS), LGM (Gaussian splatting), MeshLRM (surface mesh), TripoSR (NeRF triplane), and TetSphere (tetrahedral spheres). The optimization consistently improves all three variable metrics (Mean Stress, Standability, Img. Loss) across every baseline, with the largest relative gains on Wonder3D (standability 6.9%→72.4%, mean stress 10.68→0.45 kPa) and MeshLRM (standability 29.6%→74.5%). Among the baselines themselves, TetSphere produces the most structurally integral shapes (#CC=1.00) and the lowest baseline mean stress (0.22 kPa), while LGM has the poorest structural integrity (#CC=2.67) due to its point-cloud-based representation. After optimization, TetSphere+Ours achieves the best absolute performance on all metrics (0.19 kPa stress, 92.2% standable, 0.057 img. loss), confirming that the volumetric tetrahedral representation is most compatible with the FEM-based physical optimization. The fracture rate analysis (Fig. 3) further shows that the optimization significantly reduces element failure under stress, particularly for MeshLRM and TripoSR which tend to produce thin structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the plastic strain field parameterization from Wang et al. (ACM TOG 2021), which provides the mathematical machinery for representing rest-shape geometry as a deformation of an initial configuration without volume preservation. It departs from the broader single-view 3D reconstruction literature (Wonder3D, LGM, MeshLRM, TripoSR, TetSphere) by treating the reconstructed geometry not as a final visual artifact but as a static-equilibrium snapshot that must be decomposed into mechanical properties, external forces, and rest-shape geometry. Unlike prior physics-based 3D modeling work that either uses video input or applies simulation post-hoc to multi-view reconstructions, this paper embeds static equilibrium as a hard constraint within the reconstruction optimization itself, and unlike fabrication-aware shape design methods that require manually created initial geometry, it operates directly from a single image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a principled bridge between the rapidly advancing single-view 3D reconstruction field and the practical demands of physical applications (3D printing, dynamic simulation, industrial design) where visual fidelity alone is insufficient. The explicit decomposition of three orthogonal physical attributes and the use of implicit differentiation to enforce static equilibrium as a hard constraint offer a reusable framework that can be plugged into any reconstruction pipeline. Key open questions include: automating the extraction of material properties and force specifications from the image itself (currently user-specified), developing a differentiable tetrahedralization step to enable end-to-end integration with large reconstruction models, and extending the framework to dynamic objects captured in video. The evaluation on 100 Objaverse shapes, while consistent, is limited in scale and diversity.
