## Problem

Machine learning for seafloor mapping is hindered by the scarcity of large-scale, AI-ready sonar datasets that reflect real ocean conditions. Existing public datasets are either captured in controlled water tanks, cover limited geographic areas, or lack the multi-layer geological annotations needed for comprehensive seafloor analysis. This paper addresses the gap by compiling 62 USGS/NOAA hydrographic surveys into a unified, geo-distributed dataset spanning 17,300 km².

## Input / Output

For segmentation tasks, the model consumes 224×224 patches with 6 channels: Backscatter (normalized to [0,255]), Bathymetry (compressed to [0,1] in km), Slope, Rugosity, and pixel-wise longitude/latitude, at resolutions ranging from 1 m to 10 m per pixel across regions. Outputs are per-pixel integer labels (0–16 for Sediment, 21 classes for Physiographic Zone, 9 for Habitat, binary for Fault/Fold). For the vision-language variant, a sonar image paired with a natural-language question yields a text answer or an instruction-following segmentation mask compatible with models like PixelLM and LISA.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-stage data curation pipeline rather than a novel architecture. Raw GeoTIFF rasters and shapefile annotations from 62 surveys are reprojected to WGS84, rasterized to a common geo-grid, and patchified with a 224×224 sliding window (step 56) to produce 696K training samples. Geological nomenclature is unified under the Barnhardt classification (17 sediment classes) and CMECS (21 physiographic zones, 9 habitat categories) with domain-expert supervision. For SeafloorGenAI, an in-context learning pipeline feeds GPT-4 with extracted geophysical parameters, geological composition ratios, and polygon-based spatial distributions alongside 50 human-written exemplar descriptions, iteratively refined through marine-scientist feedback, to generate 696K descriptions and 7M QA pairs. Baseline experiments train UNet-Base, UNet-ResNet18, and TransUNet-ViT-B/32 with cross-entropy loss and Adam (lr=0.001, cosine annealing, batch 64, 100 epochs) on a single RTX A6000, evaluated under a geo-distributed source→target split that exposes significant OOD performance degradation (e.g., Sediment accuracy drops from ~78% ID to ~21–35% OOD).

## Results

On the geo-distributed SeafloorAI benchmark, all three UNet-based baselines suffer severe performance degradation from in-distribution (ID) to out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation. The best model, UNet-ResNet18, achieves 78.45% ID accuracy but drops to 34.71% OOD accuracy on Sediment (Δ = −43.74), 92.87% → 56.74% on Physiographic Zone, and 92.70% → 76.40% on Habitat. In OOD mIoU, UNet-ResNet18 reaches 22.08% (Sediment), 42.97% (Physio Zone), and 65.17% (Habitat), confirming that spatial distribution shift remains a major challenge for seafloor segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three UNet variants (UNet-Base, UNet-ResNet18, TransUNet-ViT-B/32) as baselines on the newly introduced SeafloorAI dataset rather than proposing a new architecture. UNet-ResNet18 consistently achieves the best OOD accuracy across all three multi-class tasks: 34.71% on Sediment (vs. 21.49% for UNet-Base and 28.32% for TransUNet), 56.74% on Physiographic Zone, and 76.40% on Habitat. TransUNet underperforms in ID settings (e.g., 67.90% vs. 78.45% ID accuracy on Sediment) and does not recover the gap in OOD. The most striking finding is the magnitude of the ID-to-OOD drop: up to 56 percentage points in accuracy for Sediment (UNet-Base: 77.45% → 21.49%), which the authors attribute to covariate shift (different sonar sensors) and subpopulation shift (class imbalance across regions). Habitat shows the smallest degradation (≈16–21 points), suggesting that coarser categorical labels are more transferable across geographies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by transforming 62 previously underutilized USGS and NOAA hydrographic surveys into a unified, geo-distributed, multi-purpose AI-ready dataset, departing from prior underwater imagery datasets (e.g., Singh & Valdenegro-Toro's water-tank FLS data, Sethuraman et al.'s small-scale SSS shipwreck data) that were limited in scale, environment realism, or spatial coverage. The standardization of geological nomenclature across heterogeneous surveys via the Barnhardt classification and CMECS framework is a novel curation contribution not present in prior sonar datasets. The vision-language extension (SeafloorGenAI) follows the broader trend of LLM-annotated domain datasets (LLaVA-Med, GeoChat, RSGPT) but is the first to apply this paradigm to seafloor sonar imagery, using in-context learning with marine-scientist-written exemplars to ground GPT-4 in domain-specific language.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This dataset addresses a critical bottleneck in marine science ML: the absence of large-scale, publicly available, AI-ready sonar imagery spanning diverse geological environments, enabling both standard segmentation research and the emerging vision-language model paradigm in oceanography. The geo-distributed evaluation setting exposes a severe OOD generalization gap (Sediment accuracy drops from ~78% to ~21–35% across regions), highlighting that current segmentation architectures are far from deployment-ready for real-world seafloor surveys with varying sensors and geology. Key limitations include non-uniform layer availability across regions, coarse abiotic-only habitat categories, and the absence of reasoning-capable annotations, leaving open questions about whether robust distribution-shift methods or foundation-model pretraining can close the generalization gap at this scale.
