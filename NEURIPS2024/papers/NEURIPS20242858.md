## Problem

Rectified flow models image-to-noise transport with a constant velocity field, which limits expressiveness for complex couplings and leaves residual flow crossing even after reflow, causing curved sampling trajectories and degraded few-step generation. The paper asks whether introducing an additional learnable acceleration term can yield straighter, more accurate ODE trajectories that support high-quality one-step sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242858/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise x_0 (optionally conditioned on a class label) and produces a 32×32 or 64×64 RGB image in as few as one Euler step. For inversion and inpainting tasks, it accepts a real image (or an image with a binary mask) and outputs a reconstructed or inpainted image by running the ODE in reverse and forward with conditional injection at each step. The initial velocity is evaluated once at x_0, while the acceleration is evaluated at every intermediate x_t, giving N+1 total network evaluations for N-step sampling.

## Method

CAF replaces the constant-velocity ODE of rectified flow with a constant-acceleration ODE, dx_t = v(x_0)dt + a(x_t)·t·dt, which admits the closed-form one-step solution x_1 = x_0 + v(x_0) + ½·a(x_t). Two neural networks are trained sequentially: a velocity network v_θ predicts the initial velocity h(x_1−x_0) (where h is a hyperparameter controlling acceleration sign and magnitude), and an acceleration network a_φ predicts a(x_t) = 2(x_1−x_0) − 2v(x_0). To mitigate flow crossing, the acceleration network is conditioned on the predicted initial velocity (Initial Velocity Conditioning), which disambiguates overlapping trajectories, and a reflow procedure constructs a deterministic coupling from a pre-trained EDM model before training. An optional adversarial GAN loss on the acceleration model further sharpens one-step output quality, yielding FID 1.39 on CIFAR-10 and 1.69 on ImageNet 64×64 with N=1.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CAF achieves 4.81 FID (unconditional) and 2.68 FID (conditional) with a single sampling step (N=1), substantially outperforming 2-Rectified Flow (7.89 FID at N=2, 11.81 FID at N=1) and EDM (37.75 FID at N=5). With adversarial training (CAF+GAN), the method reaches 1.48 FID (unconditional) and 1.39 FID (conditional) at N=1, surpassing CTM (1.98 FID) and DMD (3.77 FID). On ImageNet 64×64, CAF+GAN achieves 1.69 FID, 62.03 IS, and 0.64 recall at N=1, outperforming CTM (1.92 FID), DMD (2.62 FID), and StyleGAN-XL (2.09 FID).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAF's base model (N=1) achieves a 3.08 FID improvement over 2-Rectified Flow (N=2: 7.89→4.81) and a 32.94 FID improvement over EDM (N=5: 37.75→4.81) on CIFAR-10 unconditional generation, demonstrating that constant acceleration modeling is far more effective than additional reflow iterations or multi-step solvers for few-step generation. After adversarial training, CAF+GAN (1.39 FID conditional) surpasses CTM (1.73 FID), DMD (3.77 FID), and StyleGAN-XL (1.85 FID) on CIFAR-10, and beats CTM (1.92 FID), DMD (2.62 FID), and StyleGAN-XL (2.09 FID) on ImageNet 64×64. In coupling preservation, CAF reduces LPIPS from 0.092 to 0.041 and improves PSNR from 29.79 to 33.16 over 2-Rectified Flow. For inversion-based reconstruction, CAF achieves 46.68 PSNR versus 33.34 PSNR for 2-Rectified Flow at N=1, and for zero-shot box inpainting, CAF reaches 10.39 FID versus 16.41 FID for 2-Rectified Flow at the same 12 NFE budget. Compared to AGM (time-varying acceleration), CAF's constant acceleration assumption enables a closed-form one-step solution (4.81 FID at N=1) versus AGM's 11.88 FID at N=5, confirming that the constant acceleration simplification is both more accurate and more efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAF directly generalizes Rectified Flow (Liu et al., ICLR 2023) by replacing the constant-velocity ODE with a constant-acceleration ODE, adding a second learnable network (acceleration field) alongside the velocity network. It inherits the reflow procedure from Rectified Flow but augments it with initial velocity conditioning (IVC) to resolve flow-crossing ambiguity without requiring the non-intersecting coupling assumption. The paper explicitly contrasts with AGM (Chen et al., ICLR 2024), which also models acceleration but in a time-varying form requiring iterative ODE solving; CAF's constant-acceleration assumption yields a closed-form solution enabling true one-step sampling. The adversarial training component borrows the discriminator setup from StyleGAN-XL to further refine one-step output quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAF demonstrates that a simple quadratic trajectory model (constant acceleration) is sufficient to outperform both constant-velocity rectified flow and time-varying acceleration models in the critical one-step regime, achieving FID 1.39 (CIFAR-10) and 1.69 (ImageNet 64×64) with N=1. The IVC mechanism offers a practical, low-cost fix for flow crossing that relaxes a key theoretical assumption in Rectified Flow, potentially simplifying coupling construction for downstream applications like inversion and inpainting. Limitations include the N+1 NFE overhead (velocity + acceleration per step), dependence on a pre-trained EDM for deterministic coupling, and the fact that the strongest results rely on adversarial training rather than the ODE formulation alone. Open questions include whether joint velocity-acceleration prediction can eliminate the extra NFE, and whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, video, or 3D generation.
