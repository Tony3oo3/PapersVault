## Problem

Existing information-theoretic exploration methods in RL model uncertainty over random variables but neglect the inherent hierarchical structure within state and action spaces, causing entropy maximization to bias exploration toward low-value states. Moreover, prior structural information principles are restricted to single-variable graphs and cannot capture structural relationships between two variables simultaneously, preventing effective quantification of state-action structural similarity for exploration.

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment observations (7×7×3 grid embeddings in MiniGrid, stacked pixel frames in MetaWorld/DMControl) and the current policy action, producing state-action embeddings via a learned encoder, a 2-layer hierarchical encoding tree over state-action pairs, and a scalar intrinsic reward. The intrinsic reward is computed as the k-NN-estimated difference between the Shannon entropy of all state-action vertices and the entropy of their policy-value-based sub-communities, then combined with the extrinsic reward via a fixed scale parameter β. The encoding tree is rebuilt from a complete graph whose edge weights are L2 distances between policy values of state-action pairs, making the structure policy-dependent and updated online.

## Method

SI2E introduces structural mutual information (SMI), defined over 2-layer approximate binary trees on bipartite distribution graphs, with an l-transformation that traverses all one-to-one matchings between two variables to yield a closed-form expression I^SI(X;Y) = Σ p(x_i,y_j)·log[2/(p(x_i)+p(y_j))]. The embedding principle maximizes SMI between state-action representations Z_t and subsequent states S_{t+1} while minimizing SMI with current states S_t, operationalized through variational bounds (KL-divergence upper bound on I(Z_t;S_t), log-likelihood lower bound on I(Z_t;S_{t+1})), extending the Information Bottleneck by incorporating the joint entropy term. For exploration, SI2E constructs a complete graph over state-action pairs weighted by policy value differences, minimizes its 2-dimensional structural entropy via the HCSE stretch operator to obtain an encoding tree, then defines value-conditional structural entropy as the intrinsic reward—maximizing coverage of the full state-action space while suppressing uniform exploration within low-value sub-communities. Theoretical bounds connect SMI to classical mutual information (I(X;Y) ≤ I^SI(X;Y) ≤ I(X;Y)+(1−ε)H(X,Y)) and value-conditional structural entropy to Shannon entropy, establishing the framework's rationality.

## Results

On MiniGrid navigation tasks, SI2E (A2C+SI2E) achieves an average success rate of 94.40% (vs. 89.97% for the best baseline) and reduces required steps by 38.10% (from 635.65K to 393.47K). On MetaWorld manipulation tasks, DrQv2+SI2E reaches a 97.87% average success rate after only 64.79K environmental steps, solving all six tasks. On DMControl continuous control, SI2E delivers up to 37.63% improvement in episode reward (Quadruped Walk: 399.51 vs. 290.27) and 12.34% on the sparse-reward Cartpole Swingup task (795.09 vs. 707.76). Overall, SI2E achieves maximum improvements of 37.63% in final performance and 60.25% in sample efficiency across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SI2E outperforms all five baselines across every benchmark and task. Against Shannon Entropy (SE, Seo et al. 2021), SI2E is most decisive on hard tasks: in MiniGrid RedBlueDoors, SE fails entirely while SI2E achieves 85.80% success; in DMControl Pendulum Swingup, SE collapses to 10.80 reward versus SI2E's 885.50. Against Value-Conditional State Entropy (VCSE, Kim et al. 2023), the closest competitor, SI2E still leads by 4.92% average success rate on MiniGrid and 10.21% on MetaWorld (e.g., 99.60% vs. 89.80% on Button Press; 95.77% vs. 80.90% on Door Open). Against MADE (Zhang et al. 2021), compared only in DMControl, SI2E wins on all six tasks with the largest gap on Quadruped Walk (399.51 vs. 262.63, +37.63%). Against the episodic intrinsic reward methods Leco (Jo et al. 2022) and DEIR (Wan et al. 2023), evaluated in MiniGrid, SI2E achieves higher success rates and fewer required steps on all six navigation tasks (e.g., 97.04% vs. 94.81% on DoorKey-6x6; 309.14K vs. 531.06K steps on Unlock). No baseline matches or exceeds SI2E on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

SI2E extends the structural information principles of Li and Pan (2016) from single-variable graph analysis to a multi-variable setting by introducing structural mutual information, which quantifies structural similarity between two variables (e.g., state-action embeddings and next states). For representation learning, it builds on the Information Bottleneck framework (Tishby et al., 2000) and the Dynamic Bottleneck (Bai et al., 2021), but replaces standard mutual information with structural mutual information to more effectively discard dynamics-irrelevant information. For exploration, it departs from Shannon-entropy-based methods (SE, VCSE) and deviation-based methods (MADE) by deriving a hierarchical state-action community structure via structural entropy minimization, then defining a value-conditional structural entropy as the intrinsic reward. Prior structural-information work in RL (Zeng et al., 2023b,c) modeled state or action abstractions independently; SI2E is the first to jointly capture state-action structure through a unified encoding tree.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a concrete and well-recognized failure mode of entropy-maximization exploration—bias toward low-value states—without requiring task-specific prior knowledge, making it applicable to sparse-reward and high-dimensional settings where such priors are unavailable. The theoretical bounds (Theorems 3.4, 4.1, 4.3) provide a principled connection between structural and Shannon information, lending credibility to the design choices. Key limitations include the restriction to 2-layer encoding trees (motivated by O(n·log²n) complexity) and the O(n²) cost of bipartite graph construction, which may constrain scalability to very large state-action spaces. Open questions include whether deeper encoding trees yield further gains, how the method behaves in multi-agent or partially observable settings beyond MiniGrid, and whether the structural mutual information objective can be approximated more efficiently for continuous action spaces.
