## Problem

Amortized pose inference in cryo-EM reconstruction (e.g., cryoAI, cryoPoseNet) maps each image to a single or few pose estimates through a global parametric encoder, which fails to capture the highly multi-modal posterior pose distributions that dominate early in reconstruction and leads to slow convergence due to the amortization gap. This paper addresses both limitations by combining multi-head amortized inference for early exploration with direct per-image pose optimization for late-stage refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241772/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a stack of 2D cryo-EM particle images (128×128 for synthetic; 360×360 downsampled to 128×128 for the 80S ribosome benchmark) corrupted by CTF and Gaussian noise at SNR 0.1, and jointly produces a 3D density volume (128³) parameterized in Hartley space with a mantissa-exponent decomposition, plus a per-image 3D pose consisting of a rotation in SO(3) and a 2D in-plane translation. Projections from the volume to 2D are computed analytically via the Fourier slice theorem rather than through an implicit neural network, making each projection evaluation a single Fourier-space slice operation.

## Method

CryoSPIN is a two-stage semi-amortized reconstruction. In the auto-encoding stage, a shared VGG16 backbone feeds M separate fully-connected pose heads (M=7 synthetic, M=15 real), each predicting a rotation (via 6D S²S² representation) and translation; a winner-take-all loss selects the head with minimum reconstruction error per image, encouraging heads to specialize in disjoint regions of SO(3). Once the pose posterior becomes predominantly uni-modal (after 7 or 15 epochs), the method switches to auto-decoding: the best candidate pose initializes per-image axis-angle parameters that are directly optimized via SGD using a numerically stable Rodrigues-form perturbation, alternating five pose updates with one volume update in a coordinate-descent loop. The volume decoder is an explicit 3D voxel array in Hartley space (real-valued, decomposed into mantissa × exp(exponent)) rather than an implicit MLP, reducing per-projection cost and enabling the multi-head encoder to query the decoder M times per image without prohibitive overhead.

## Results

On synthetic datasets, CryoSPIN achieves the lowest mean rotation error across all three structures: 3.27° on HSP, 1.54° on Spike, and 0.68° on Spliceosome, outperforming cryoSPARC (6.23°, 1.61°, 1.41°) and cryoAI (45.83°, 2.52°, 2.85°). On the experimental 80S ribosome dataset (EMPIAR-10028), CryoSPIN matches cryoSPARC resolution via FSC while outperforming cryoAI. In terms of speed, CryoSPIN is approximately 6× faster and uses ~5× less GPU memory than cryoAI, with per-epoch times of 99.76 s (auto-encoding) and 81.61 s (auto-decoding) versus 622.39 s for cryoAI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CryoSPIN outperforms cryoSPARC on HSP (3.27° vs 6.23° mean rotation error) and Spliceosome (0.68° vs 1.41°), while achieving competitive results on Spike (1.54° vs 1.61°). Against cryoAI, CryoSPIN shows a dramatic advantage on HSP (3.27° vs 45.83°), where cryoAI gets stuck in local minima due to structural symmetries, and also wins on Spike and Spliceosome. On the experimental 80S ribosome dataset, CryoSPIN matches cryoSPARC's FSC-based resolution and clearly outperforms cryoAI, which fails to estimate translation parameters without pre-centering. In speed comparisons (Figure 3 and Supp. B), CryoSPIN reaches high-resolution reconstructions significantly faster than cryoAI, attributed to the explicit volume decoder and the semi-amortized switching strategy that avoids expensive per-image MLP evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

CryoSPIN builds directly on cryoAI's amortized pose inference framework, extending its two-pose output to a multi-head VGG16 encoder trained with a winner-take-all (oracle) loss borrowed from the Multi-Choice Learning literature. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of semi-amortized inference (from Kim et al., 2018): after an initial auto-encoding stage handles multi-modal pose uncertainty, the method switches to per-particle direct pose optimization via SGD, circumventing the amortization gap that limits feed-forward encoders. For the 3D decoder, it replaces the implicit neural representations used in cryoAI and cryoDRGN with an explicit Hartley-space voxel parameterization, trading model capacity for speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semi-amortized formulation offers a practical middle ground between the speed of amortized inference and the accuracy of per-particle search (as in cryoSPARC), and the 6× speedup over cryoAI with competitive FSC makes it a viable alternative for ab-initio reconstruction. However, the method assumes a rigid (homogeneous) structure, and the authors acknowledge that extending the multi-head encoder to heterogeneous conformational variability remains an open challenge. A principled criterion for the optimal switching time between auto-encoding and auto-decoding stages is also left unresolved, with the authors noting empirically that switching later is safer than earlier.
