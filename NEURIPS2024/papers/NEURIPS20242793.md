## Problem

Recent Transformer-based time series forecasters (PatchTST, Pathformer, CARD, etc.) are overly reliant on large-size patching to achieve competitive performance, as full attention cannot independently identify important time points and instead focuses uniformly on all inputs. This over-reliance limits applicability to tasks with short input lengths (e.g., short-term forecasting with input length 6–18) where patching produces too few tokens or is infeasible, causing severe performance degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242793/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts multivariate (M variates) or univariate time series of length I, where I ranges from very short (2× prediction length, e.g., 6–96) to long (768). Patching is optional and only applied for long inputs (I=384 uses patch size 4; I=768 uses patch size 8); for I≤96 no patching is used, so each time point becomes its own token. The output is a length-T future prediction (T ∈ {6,12,18} for short-term or {96,192,336,720} for long-term) produced by a linear projection of the flattened final hierarchical representation, with RevIN applied for distribution-shift normalization.

## Method

The core contribution is deformable attention, a data-driven sparse attention that replaces full attention to independently focus on important time points without patching guidance. It generates N_samp sparse reference points from a uniform 1D grid, learns per-sample offsets via a small offset network (depth-wise conv + point-wise conv on query tokens), and samples key/value tokens at the deformed coordinates using differentiable linear interpolation; standard multi-head attention is then computed between the full query set and the sparse sampled keys/values. A hierarchical backbone (downsampling convolutions between blocks that halve temporal length and double feature dimension) addresses the memory cost of processing more tokens without patching. Local enhancement modules—a depth-wise convolution with residual (LPU) before attention and a depth-wise-conv-injected FFN (ConvFFN) after—improve local temporal modeling, while a deformable relative positional bias encodes positional information between queries and the irregularly sampled keys.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (averaged over input lengths {96, 384, 768} and prediction lengths {96, 192, 336, 720}), DeformableTST achieves the best MSE on all 8 benchmarks, with headline values of 0.413 (ETTh1), 0.336 (ETTh2), 0.233 (Weather), and 0.169 (ECL). On multivariate short-term forecasting (prediction lengths {6, 12, 18}), it achieves 0.373 MSE on ETTh1 and 1.767 MSE on ILI, outperforming the second-best by an average of 25.6% MSE. On univariate short-term forecasting (SMAPE), it achieves 15.250 on M1, 11.747 on M3, and 21.502 on Tourism, outperforming the second-best by an average of 14.1% SMAPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeformableTST achieves the best MSE and MAE on all 8 long-term forecasting benchmarks, with the largest margins over iTransformer (e.g., 0.199 vs. 0.866 MSE on Solar-Energy) and TimesNet (e.g., 0.410 vs. 0.579 MSE on Traffic). Among patch-based Transformer baselines, PatchTST is typically the closest competitor (e.g., 0.413 vs. 0.438 MSE on ETTh1), while CARD and GPT4TS trail by a smaller but consistent gap. On multivariate short-term forecasting, DeformableTST wins on all datasets except PEMS where iTransformer edges it out by 0.002 MSE (0.102 vs. 0.104), likely due to iTransformer's cross-variate attention. On univariate short-term forecasting, DeformableTST dominates all 7 datasets, with the closest competitor being CARD on M4 (11.688 vs. 11.815 SMAPE) and N-BEATS on M1 (15.250 vs. 19.810 SMAPE). Linear baselines (DLinear, RLinear) and iTransformer suffer most on univariate tasks due to insufficient representation capacity, while prior-based sparse attention models (FEDformer, Autoformer) are consistently outperformed, validating the paper's argument that data-driven deformable attention is more flexible than fixed priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a direct improvement over PatchTST, which established that large-size patching is essential for Transformer-based time series forecasters to achieve competitive performance. The core diagnostic contribution is an ERF-based analysis showing that full attention, without patching, fails to distinguish important time points and instead attends nearly uniformly across all inputs, meaning patching acts as a crude focusing mechanism rather than a representation tool. To replace this reliance, the paper adapts deformable attention from the computer vision literature (Deformable DETR, Deformable ViT) into a data-driven sparse attention for time series, combined with a hierarchical architecture to manage the increased token count when patching is removed. It explicitly contrasts with iTransformer, which argues attention is unsuitable for temporal modeling altogether, by demonstrating that the underperformance stems from the attention mechanism's inability to self-focus rather than from attention being fundamentally inappropriate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper broadens the practical applicability of Transformer-based forecasters to short-input-length and univariate tasks where patching is infeasible or counterproductive, a gap that has left these tasks stagnant in the community. The ERF diagnostic provides a reusable analytical tool for understanding how and why architectural choices (like patching) shape attention behavior in time series models. Key limitations include the variate-independent design (no cross-variate correlation modeling), which the authors acknowledge as a significant gap for high-variate datasets like PEMS and ECL, and the reliance on a fixed number of sampling points (N_samp=12) that may not adapt to all temporal structures. Future directions include incorporating multivariate correlation, large-scale pre-training, and extending to other time series analysis tasks.
