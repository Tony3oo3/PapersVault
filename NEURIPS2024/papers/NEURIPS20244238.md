## Problem

Flow Matching and diffusion models have become dominant generative paradigms for continuous data (images, video), but their application to high-dimensional discrete sequential data such as language and code remains limited, with non-autoregressive discrete diffusion and flow models significantly underperforming autoregressive baselines. Prior discrete flow methods lacked a general family of probability paths, a unified closed-form velocity formula, and effective corrector sampling strategies, leaving a large design space unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model takes a partially masked sequence as input: for conditional text generation, the first N₀ tokens are fixed (prefix conditioning via C-coupling); for code, a function signature is provided; for images, a class label conditions generation. The source distribution is an all-mask sequence, and the model iteratively unmasks tokens in parallel (non-autoregressive) over N steps. For CIFAR-10, each pixel channel is treated as a discrete token with vocabulary size d=256 over a 32×32×3 grid. Sequence length N is fixed at training time, and for code generation an optional oracle length (end-of-text token position) can be provided to constrain output length.

## Method

Discrete Flow Matching formulates generation as a Continuous-Time Markov Chain (CTMC) over discrete state space [d]^N, where each token jumps between vocabulary values according to a learned probability velocity. The key theoretical contribution is a general family of conditional probability paths defined as convex combinations of m basis distributions with time-dependent scheduler coefficients κ_t^{i,j}, for which the paper derives a closed-form marginal generating velocity that takes the identical algebraic form as continuous Flow Matching when parameterized via the probability denoiser (x-prediction) or noise-prediction (ε-prediction). Training minimizes a cross-entropy loss to learn the posterior p_{1|t}(x^i|X_t), and sampling proceeds via Euler steps with adaptive step sizes h_adaptive = min{h, (1-κ_t)/κ̇_t} to guarantee valid PMFs. A corrector velocity combining forward and backward-time components (with α_t - β_t = 1 for stochastic sampling or α_t - β_t = 0 for fixed-point iterations) allows reintroducing noise during generation, and the paper shows that scheduler tuning (cubic polynomial κ_t with a=0, b=2) and corrector steps are pivotal for quality. The model uses a DiT transformer architecture (48 layers, 24 heads, 1536 hidden dim for the 1.7B model) with RoPE positional embeddings, trained with Adam on Llama-2/CodeLlama data mixes.

## Results

On code generation, the 1.7B Discrete Flow Matching model achieves 6.7% Pass@1 and 13.4% Pass@10 on HumanEval, and 6.7% Pass@1 and 20.6% Pass@10 on 1-shot MBPP — the first non-autoregressive method to produce non-trivial coding results. On conditional text generation, the model reaches a generative perplexity of 9.7 (measured by Llama-3 8B), substantially outperforming a 1.7B autoregressive model (22.3) and approaching the Llama-2 7B reference (8.3). On unconditional text generation (150M model), FM with the equation-10 path achieves 22.3 generative perplexity (Llama-2) at 1024 NFE, outperforming all prior non-autoregressive baselines. On CIFAR-10 image generation, the model achieves 3.63 FID at 1024 NFE, surpassing both MaskGIT and Campbell et al. (2024).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Discrete Flow Matching outperforms all prior non-autoregressive baselines on unconditional text generation: at 1024 NFE it achieves 22.3 generative perplexity (Llama-2) versus 28.7 for Campbell et al. (2024), 27.2 for Lou et al. (2023), 73.3 for Han et al. (2022), and 697.6 for Austin et al. (2021). On conditional text generation (1.7B model), FM with C-coupling reaches 9.7 perplexity (Llama-3), beating the 1.7B autoregressive baseline (22.3) by a wide margin and approaching the Llama-2 7B reference (8.3), while maintaining comparable entropy. For image generation on CIFAR-10, FM achieves 3.63 FID at 1024 NFE, outperforming both MaskGIT (Chang et al., 2022) and Campbell et al. (2024); notably, MaskGIT performs better at very low NFE but deteriorates at higher NFE due to a bias in its confidence-based unmasking. On code generation, FM trained on code data (6.7% Pass@1 HumanEval) substantially outperforms FM trained on text data (1.2%) but remains below the 1.7B autoregressive code model (14.3%), though the gap narrows significantly with oracle length conditioning (11.6%). Savinov et al. (2021) is outperformed on both unconditional (29.5 vs. 22.3) and conditional (15.4 vs. 9.7) perplexity metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CTMC-based discrete flow framework of Campbell et al. (2024) by introducing a general family of probability paths with arbitrary source-target couplings and time-dependent schedulers, yielding unified closed-form generating velocity formulas that mirror the structure of continuous Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022). It also establishes a formal connection to MaskGIT, showing that masked generative modeling is a special case of Discrete Flow Matching whose sampling algorithm introduces a confidence-based bias. The work departs from earlier discrete diffusion approaches (D3PM, Argmax flows, Lou et al.) by adopting the flow matching paradigm rather than a corruption-based diffusion process, and unifies corrector iterations and stochastic sampling under a single velocity-field framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Discrete Flow Matching is the first non-autoregressive method to achieve non-trivial coding performance (6.7% Pass@1 on HumanEval, 20.6% Pass@10 on MBPP), and its 1.7B model narrows the generative perplexity gap with autoregressive models from a factor of ~3 to within ~1.2× of Llama-2 7B. The finding that scheduler tuning (path, corrector, temperature) is pivotal for quality, and that MaskGIT's confidence-based unmasking introduces a systematic bias at higher NFE, provides actionable guidance for the field. Open questions include closing the remaining sampling-efficiency gap with continuous Flow Matching, exploring the vast unexplored design space of probability paths in equation 8, and whether further scaling can match or surpass autoregressive models.
