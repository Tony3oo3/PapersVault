## Problem

While the feature distortion theory explains LP-FT's success using a two-layer linear model, the training dynamics of LP-FT for complex architectures like Transformers remain poorly understood. In particular, the role of classifier weight norms in modulating feature changes during fine-tuning has not been formally characterized, and how the NTK matrix components interact with the LP stage has not been analyzed for non-linear models.

## Input / Output

The method processes text sentences through a pre-trained RoBERTa-base Transformer and produces class labels with predicted probabilities across 13 NLP classification datasets (SuperGLUE, GLUE, BOSS, PubMed). The LP-FT procedure is two-stage: LP trains only the linear head on frozen pre-trained features, then FT fine-tunes the full model starting from the LP-obtained head parameters. The NTK analysis decomposes the kernel into a diagonal pre-train-effective component (determined solely by pre-trained feature inner products) and a non-diagonal FT-effective component (scaled by the classifier weight matrix V_0), enabling separate tracking of feature-preserving vs. feature-altering dynamics.

## Method

The paper decomposes the empirical NTK of f(x)=Vφ(x)+b into P(x,x_i)=(⟨φ_0(x),φ_0(x_i)⟩+1)I_C and F(x,x_i)=V_0(∂φ_0(x)/∂θ^φ)(∂φ_0(x_i)/∂θ^φ)^T V_0^T, showing that the classifier norm ‖V_0‖_F affects feature updates linearly but logit updates quadratically, so a larger post-LP norm enables substantial logit correction with minimal feature distortion. The paper proves that CE loss training during LP drives the classifier norm to grow (the gradient w.r.t. ‖v_k‖ stays negative because perfect classification is unattainable in LP), and that this increased norm degrades calibration, fixable by temperature scaling at test time. The analysis extends to LoRA via a Johnson-Lindenstrauss argument (Proposition 4.4), showing the FT-effective component of LoRA's NTK approximates σ²r times that of standard FT with high probability, confirming LoRA's equivalence to scaled FT in the NTK regime.

## Results

On the SuperGLUE and GLUE benchmarks, LP-FT achieves the best or near-best performance across most datasets: 84.64% on CB (vs. 77.86% LP, 81.43% FT), 76.75% on RTE (vs. 57.69% LP, 74.73% FT), and 94.52% on SST-2 (vs. 86.31% LP, 92.41% FT). LP-FT also preserves pre-trained features substantially better than FT, with cosine similarity of 0.804–0.942 vs. 0.260–0.336 for FT (Table 1). Temperature scaling at test time reduces the expected calibration error of LP-FT from 21.72% to 5.48% and the maximum calibration error from 63.95% to 13.94% on RTE (Table 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LP-FT consistently outperforms LP by a wide margin on all datasets (e.g., +6.8 pts on CB, +19.1 pts on RTE, +8.2 pts on SST-2), confirming that the subsequent fine-tuning stage adds substantial value over linear probing alone. Against standard FT, LP-FT wins on CB (+3.2 pts), RTE (+2.0 pts), SST-2 (+2.1 pts), WiC (+0.3 pts), and BoolQ (+0.3 pts), while FT retains a slight edge on CoLA (+0.8 pts) and MRPC (+0.5 pts). Compared to LoRA, LP-FT achieves substantially higher accuracy on CB (+7.1 pts), RTE (+3.9 pts), SST-2 (+3.6 pts), and BoolQ (+1.4 pts), though LP-LoRA narrows the gap on SST-2 (94.22% vs. 94.52%) and MRPC (87.79% vs. 87.60%). The paper also shows that LP-FT preserves pre-trained features far better than FT (cosine similarity 0.80–0.94 vs. 0.26–0.34) and that the FT-effective NTK component dominates the kernel in LP-FT (FT Ratio ≈ 1.0) but contributes only ~20% in standard FT, explaining the superior generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature distortion theory of Kumar et al. (2022), which explained LP-FT's success using a two-layer linear model, by re-deriving and generalizing the analysis within the empirical NTK framework (Wei et al., 2022; Malladi et al., 2023) applicable to complex Transformer architectures. The key conceptual departure is identifying the classifier weight norm—driven by cross-entropy loss dynamics during LP—as an additional mechanism (beyond near-optimal prediction accuracy) that reduces feature distortion during FT, a factor absent from the original two-layer analysis. The NTK decomposition of the kernel into pre-train-effective and FT-effective components also provides a new lens for understanding LoRA, building on the empirical NTK perspective of Malladi et al. and the LoRA convergence analysis of Jang et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first NTK-based theoretical justification for LP-FT in Transformer models, bridging the gap between the original linear-model analysis and practical language model fine-tuning. The finding that increased classifier norms (a natural byproduct of CE loss training during LP) simultaneously reduce feature distortion and degrade calibration—fixable with temperature scaling—offers a concrete, low-cost practical improvement to the widely-used LP-FT pipeline. However, the analysis is confined to the NTK regime and a single-epoch approximation, which may not capture the full dynamics of multi-epoch training in practice, and experiments are limited to NLP classification tasks. Open questions include whether the norm-based mechanism holds in the non-linearized regime and whether analogous effects appear in generative or multi-task fine-tuning settings.
