## Problem

Phenotype imputation in electronic health records is critical for comprehensive medical evaluation, yet multi-modal biological data (proteomics, metabolomics) that could enrich patient characterization is largely overlooked. The core difficulty is that biological data is heterogeneous relative to phenotypic data, contains measurement noise and irrelevant information, and suffers from irregular missingness across patients, making naive joint modeling ineffective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240110/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: patient phenotype records from EHRs (encoded as PheCodes from ICD-10 mappings), proteomics profiles (~3,000 NPX values per patient), and metabolomics profiles (~250 log-transformed biomarkers per patient), with irregular modality missingness (~90% for proteomics, ~50% for metabolomics). The output is a personalized ranking of candidate phenotypes for each patient, where the task is framed as ranking rather than classification due to the large phenotype space (1,109 phenotypes); performance is measured by Hit@K and MRR on held-out patient-phenotype interactions.

## Method

MPI (Multi-modal Phenotype Imputation) decouples modeling into two separate graphs: a patient-phenotype bipartite graph capturing collaborative co-occurrence patterns, and a patient-factor graph built from disentangled biological factors extracted via multi-level residual quantization (RQ) with per-modality codebooks. Each graph is encoded by a two-layer GCN, and the biological-view encoder serves as a teacher that distills knowledge into the phenotype-view encoder through a cross-view contrastive loss (cosine-similarity-based InfoNCE with intra- and inter-view negatives), rather than full imitation. This partial-supervision KD strategy allows the phenotype view to selectively absorb useful biological signal while filtering out noise. Training proceeds in two stages: pretraining per-modality autoencoders with reconstruction + RQ commitment loss, then iteratively optimizing both GNNs with a margin-based ranking loss plus the weighted contrastive KD loss (α=0.1).

## Results

On the full UK Biobank ADRD cohort (100% dataset), MPI achieves H@10 = 38.74, H@20 = 55.10, H@50 = 78.42, and MRR = 19.28, outperforming the strongest baselines (GIN and GraphSage) by approximately 2.1 points on H@10 and 1.3 points on MRR. The advantage is consistent across all dataset proportions (30%–100%), with MPI leading on every metric at every scale. Imputation-based methods (CMAE, SMIL) trail by 8–10 points on H@10, while multi-modal EHR models (GRAPE, M3Care, MUSE) fall 4–6 points behind MPI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPI outperforms all seven baselines across every metric and every dataset proportion. The closest competitors are GIN (H@10 = 36.61, MRR = 17.94) and GraphSage (H@10 = 36.64, MRR = 17.99), which use multi-modal biological data as raw patient features in a single bipartite graph; MPI surpasses them by ~2.1 points on H@10 and ~1.3 on MRR at 100% data. Multi-modal EHR models GRAPE, M3Care, and MUSE (H@10 = 32.70–34.92) underperform the naive GNN baselines, which the authors attribute to conflicts between clinical and biological views when jointly modeled. Imputation-based methods CMAE and SMIL (H@10 = 28.02–29.87) perform worst, as their reconstruction of high-dimensional missing modalities introduces significant inaccuracy. The ablation further confirms that view decoupling (separate graphs) plus cross-view contrastive KD is essential: a single-graph variant (V4) drops to H@10 = 37.68, and removing biological data entirely (V1) drops to 36.49.

## Relation to Prior Work

MPI builds on three distinct research threads: (1) phenotype imputation in EHRs, where prior work ranges from statistical/matrix methods to deep generative models and GNN-based approaches; (2) multi-modal EHR representation learning (M3Care, GRAPE, MUSE), which handles missing modalities but focuses on clinical modalities *within* EHRs rather than external biological data; and (3) residual vector quantization (RQ-VAE) and knowledge distillation, repurposed here for biological factor discovery and cross-view transfer. The key conceptual departure is the view-decoupling principle: rather than jointly modeling biological and phenotypic data in a single graph (as GraphSage/GIN baselines do), MPI constructs separate patient-phenotype and patient-factor graphs, then bridges them via a novel cross-view contrastive knowledge distillation loss that treats the biological-view GNN as a partial teacher for the phenotype-view GNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a clinically important gap—under-documented phenotypes in neurodegenerative disease cohorts—by tapping into the growing biobank infrastructure (UK Biobank, All of Us) that collects proteomics and metabolomics alongside EHRs. The view-decoupling + partial-knowledge-distillation framework is a generalizable design pattern for any setting where a noisy, high-dimensional auxiliary signal should inform a cleaner primary task without dominating it. However, the evaluation is confined to a single disease cohort (ADRD), a single dataset, and two modalities, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other chronic diseases, additional omics layers, or different imputation granularities. The 90% proteomics missingness also limits how much biological signal can actually be extracted, suggesting that the method's ceiling may rise substantially with denser biological data.
