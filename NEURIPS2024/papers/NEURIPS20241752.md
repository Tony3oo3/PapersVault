## Problem

Existing dual-stream reflection separation methods (e.g., YTMT, DSRNet) suffer from significant performance degradation on complex cases where transmission and reflection are densely entangled, because their interaction mechanisms pass information between streams without explicitly assessing whether the sibling stream actually needs it, and their CNN-based backbones have limited receptive fields for capturing global correlations across the image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241752/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reflection-superimposed RGB image (trained at 384×384) and produces two aligned outputs: the transmission layer and the reflection layer, plus a learnable residual term modeling non-linear superposition effects. Training pairs are synthesized from PASCAL VOC objects and supplemented with real-world pairs from the SIR² and Nature datasets, with a proposed Reflection Mixup augmentation (blending the superimposed image with the transmission layer at random ratios) to enrich real-pair diversity.

## Method

The proposed Dual-Stream Interactive Transformer (DSIT) introduces a Dual-Attention Interactive Block (DAIB) that concatenates transmission and reflection tokens along the sequence dimension, so a single windowed attention computation simultaneously yields intra-layer self-attention and inter-layer cross-attention terms, with a novel Layered Relative Position Bias extending Swin's 2D positional encoding to a 3D space (x, y, layer-identity) to distinguish same-layer from cross-layer token pairs. A Dual-Architecture Interactive Encoder (DAIE) bridges a pre-trained Swin-L Transformer (global prior extractor) with a dual-stream CNN (local prior extractor) by feeding both into DAIBs as cross-architecture interactions, injecting high-level semantic priors into the local dual-stream flows hierarchically. The local processing within each block reuses the MuGI convolutional module from DSRNet to preserve inductive biases for dense prediction. Training combines pixel-level L2/L1 reconstruction (with a learnable superposition residual), multi-scale gradient exclusion loss, and VGG-19 feature matching loss.

## Results

On four real-world test sets (Real20, Objects, Postcard, Wild), DSIT achieves an average PSNR of 26.27 dB and SSIM of 0.917 under Data Setting I, surpassing the previous best (DSRNet at 25.40 dB / 0.905) by +0.87 dB. Under Data Setting II (with additional real pairs), DSIT reaches 26.49 dB / 0.922 SSIM, outperforming DSRNet† (25.75 / 0.910) and RRW† (25.34 / 0.912). On the Nature test set (Table 2), DSIT scores 26.77 dB / 0.847 SSIM, exceeding the runner-up RRW (26.04 / 0.846) by +0.73 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSIT outperforms all 14 compared methods on average PSNR and SSIM across both data settings. Under Setting I, the closest competitor is DSRNet [23] (25.40 dB / 0.905 SSIM), which DSIT surpasses by +0.87 dB; the gap widens to +2.32 dB over PromptRR [44] and +3.68 dB over RobustSIRR [40]. Under Setting II (with extra real pairs), DSIT (26.49 / 0.922) beats DSRNet† (25.75 / 0.910) by +0.74 dB and RRW† (25.34 / 0.912) by +1.15 dB, while also edging out the language-prompt-based Zhong et al.†* (25.75 / 0.917) by +0.74 dB without requiring any external prompts. On the Postcard subset, where reflections are densely entangled with transmission, DSIT achieves 25.63 dB versus 24.56 dB for DSRNet — a +1.07 dB margin that highlights the benefit of the dual-attention interaction on hard cases. Single-stream methods (Zhang et al., ERRNet, BDN) consistently trail dual-stream approaches by 1–6 dB, confirming the value of jointly reconstructing both layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual-stream interactive paradigm established by YTMT [22] and DSRNet [23], which used Siamese networks with activation-based or MuGI-based feature exchange between transmission and reflection branches. The key departure is replacing these implicit interaction mechanisms with an explicit dual-attention design (dual-stream self-attention + layer-aware dual-stream cross-attention) that directly assesses feature correlation before information transfer, drawing on Swin Transformer's windowed attention and relative position bias [35] extended with a novel Layered Relative Position Bias. Additionally, the paper introduces a Dual-Architecture Interactive Encoder that bridges a pre-trained Swin-L backbone with a task-specific dual-stream CNN, a design not present in prior SIRS methods that relied on HyperColumn or Feature Pyramid structures for semantic priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-attention mechanism addresses a concrete weakness in prior dual-stream SIRS methods—blind information exchange without correlation assessment—making the approach potentially transferable to other layer-decomposition tasks (denoising, watermark removal). The cross-architecture interaction design offers a practical recipe for injecting pre-trained Transformer semantics into dense-prediction low-level tasks, a problem that remains broadly open. Limitations include persistent failure on reflection-dominated regions where the relative smoothness assumption breaks down, and the method's dependence on a large pre-trained Swin-L backbone for its global prior, which constrains deployment in resource-limited settings. The authors note that larger vision foundation models and task-specific position bias designs are natural next steps.
