## Problem

Existing layout-to-image generation methods struggle with complex multi-object scenes, producing common failures such as missing objects, inconsistent lighting, and conflicting view angles. Training-free approaches like CAG suffer severe object omission, while training-based methods like GLIGEN reduce omission but introduce distortion. The core gap is the lack of a mechanism to spatially disentangle multiple objects' local semantics and positional information during generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244093/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a structured instruction composed of K (sub-caption, sub-bounding-box) pairs for individual foreground objects plus a global background caption and bounding box, where K ranges from 3 to over 10 in practice. Output is a 512×512 (SD1.5) or 1024×1024 (SDXL) RGB image in which each object is rendered at its specified spatial location with its corresponding fine-grained textual description. The method is compatible with different Stable Diffusion checkpoints and supports fast inference via LCM-LoRA or SDXL-Lightning plugins.

## Method

HiCo introduces a multi-branch network where each branch independently models one foreground object or the global background, using a ControlNet-like side-branch structure with weight sharing across branches. Each branch receives its own text embedding and bounding box condition, producing region-specific intermediate features that are hierarchically refined through the downsampling stages. A non-parametric Fuse Net aggregates these branch features via mask-based decoupling during the UNet upsampling phase, where the background mask is computed as the complement of the sum of all foreground object masks. Training freezes the base SD model and optimizes only the HiCo Net parameters, with the Fuse Net remaining parameter-free. The architecture also supports LoRA fine-tuning directly on the HiCo branches, enabling multi-concept generation without feature confusion that occurs when stacking LoRAs on the main UNet.

## Results

On the open-ended HiCo-7K benchmark, HiCo achieves the best FID of 14.24 (vs. 16.99 for InstanceDiff and 19.37 for CAG), Inception Score of 28.31, and LocalIoU of 59.98 (vs. 51.58 for InstanceDiff and 48.54 for GLIGEN), demonstrating superior image quality and spatial control. On the closed-set COCO-3K benchmark, HiCo achieves FID 20.02 and AP 36.60, matching or slightly exceeding LayoutDiffuse (FID 20.27, AP 36.58). In human evaluation, HiCo attains the highest overall score of 78.08, outperforming InstanceDiff (70.54) and GLIGEN (67.76), with particularly strong spatial position (82.22) and semantics (86.11) scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiCo outperforms all six baselines on every spatial metric in Table 3 (HiCo-7K), with the most dramatic margins over training-free methods CAG and MtDM, which achieve near-zero AP scores (0.47 and 0.59 respectively) due to severe object-missing failures. Among training-based methods, HiCo leads InstanceDiff by 8.4 points in LocalIoU (59.98 vs. 51.58) and 4.77 points in AP (21.53 vs. 16.76), and leads GLIGEN by 11.44 points in LocalIoU and 8.86 points in AP. On the closed-set COCO-3K benchmark (Table 1), HiCo essentially ties LayoutDiffuse (FID 20.02 vs. 20.27; AP 36.60 vs. 36.58) while slightly exceeding it on AP75 (39.4 vs. 38.06) and AR (46.60 vs. 46.09). In the zero-shot transfer to COCO-3K (Table 4), HiCo-Real trails InstanceDiff (AP 52.47 vs. 65.28) but surpasses GLIGEN (36.87) and MIGC (40.82), with the authors attributing the gap to distribution shift from fine-grained training captions. In human evaluation (Table 5), HiCo achieves the best overall score (78.08) and leads all methods in spatial position (82.22) and semantics (86.11), while approaching the reference SD-Real model on global image quality dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiCo extends the side-branch conditioning paradigm established by ControlNet and IP-Adapter by instantiating multiple weight-shared branches—one per foreground object plus a global background branch—rather than a single branch handling all layout conditions jointly. This hierarchical decomposition departs from prior training-based layout methods (GLIGEN, LayoutDiffuse, InstanceDiff) that use a unified network or special attention mechanisms to encode all objects simultaneously, and from training-free approaches (CAG, MultiDiffusion) that manipulate cross-attention maps. The non-parametric mask-based Fuse Net for aggregating branch features is a deliberate design choice over learned fusion, and the paper further demonstrates compatibility with the broader Stable Diffusion ecosystem (SD1.5, SDXL, LoRA, LCM, Lightning plugins).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HiCo-7K benchmark addresses a real gap: prior layout-to-image evaluation relied on closed-set COCO categories, while natural-scene generation with open-ended fine-grained captions lacked a standardized, cleaned evaluation set. The multi-branch + mask-fusion architecture offers a practical, modular recipe that practitioners can plug into existing SD pipelines with minimal architectural change. However, the paper acknowledges that explicit occlusion-order control remains unsolved (no dedicated training data exists), and multi-concept LoRA generation in complex layouts is still unreliable—both are open problems that limit the method's applicability to truly complex compositional scenes.
