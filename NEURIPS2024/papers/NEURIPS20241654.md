## Problem

Conventional time-domain speech separation networks (TasNet family) use a 'late split' design where a single feature sequence must encode all speaker information before separation, creating an information bottleneck that makes the task difficult and prone to local minima. Additionally, the widely adopted dual-path model for handling long sequences introduces computational redundancy through 50% chunk overlap and inefficient inter-chunk blocks that merely capture global context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241654/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single-channel speech mixture waveform at 8 kHz sampling rate (4-second segments during training, variable length at inference) and produces J separated speech waveforms, one per speaker. The separator directly estimates output representations rather than computing masks, and the architecture operates in a temporally multi-scaled U-Net structure where the encoder progressively downsamples the sequence by a factor of 2^R before the decoder reconstructs fine-grained temporal detail. The weight-shared decoder processes all J speaker sequences with identical parameters, and a cross-speaker attention module enables inter-speaker information exchange within the same temporal frame.

## Method

SepReformer introduces an asymmetric encoder-decoder (ESSD) where the encoder analyzes a single feature sequence and a speaker split layer expands it into J speaker-specific sequences, which are then reconstructed by a weight-shared decoder that learns discriminative features through the separation objective without explicit speaker embeddings. A cross-speaker (CS) Transformer block is inserted in the decoder to recover speech elements mistakenly clustered into wrong speaker channels by attending across the speaker dimension. To replace the dual-path model, the paper designs global Transformer blocks with an Efficient Gated Attention (EGA) module that downsamples the sequence for attention and gates the upsampled output, paired with local Transformer blocks using Convolutional Local Attention (CLA) with large-kernel 1D convolutions, all combined with a Gated Convolutional Feed-Forward Network (GCFN) for channel mixing. Training uses a multi-loss strategy where each decoder stage has its own auxiliary separation objective (using STFT-domain magnitude comparison), with a decayed weighting factor α, enabling progressive reconstruction and clearer discriminative learning at each stage.

## Results

On WSJ0-2Mix, SepReformer-L with dynamic mixing achieves a state-of-the-art 25.4 dB SI-SNRi (25.6 dB SDRi), surpassing all prior methods including MossFormer2-L (24.1 dB) and ISCIT (24.3 dB). The tiny model (SepReformer-T, 3.5M params, 10.4 G/s MACs) reaches 22.4 dB SI-SNRi, outperforming Sepformer (20.4 dB) and DPRNN (18.8 dB) with roughly 10× fewer MACs. On WHAM! and WHAMR!, SepReformer-L also sets new records at 18.4 dB and 17.2 dB SI-SNRi respectively. On Libri2Mix, SepReformer-M achieves 22.0 dB SI-SNRi, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SepReformer-T (3.5M params, 10.4 G/s) achieves 22.4 dB SI-SNRi on WSJ0-2Mix, beating Conv-TasNet (15.3 dB) by 7.1 dB, DPRNN (18.8 dB) by 3.6 dB, Sepformer (20.4 dB) by 2.0 dB, and Sandglasset (20.8 dB) by 1.6 dB, while using roughly 10× fewer MACs than Sepformer. SepReformer-B (14.2M, 39.8 G/s) reaches 23.8 dB, surpassing SFSRNet (22.0 dB, 124.2 G/s), ISCIT (22.4 dB, 252.2 G/s), and QDPN (22.1 dB) with substantially lower computation. SepReformer-L with dynamic mixing achieves 25.4 dB on WSJ0-2Mix, outperforming MossFormer2-L (24.1 dB), ISCIT (24.3 dB), and SFSRNet (24.0 dB) by 1.3–1.4 dB. On WHAM!, SepReformer-L (18.4 dB) exceeds MossFormer2-L (18.1 dB) and ISCIT (16.9 dB); on WHAMR!, it leads at 17.2 dB versus MossFormer2-L's 17.0 dB. On Libri2Mix, SepReformer-M (22.0 dB) outperforms TDANet (17.4 dB), S4M (16.9 dB), and WaveSplit (16.6 dB) by 4–5 dB. Notably, the tiny SepReformer-T outperforms Sepformer trained with dynamic mixing (22.3 dB) by 0.1 dB while using only 10.4 G/s versus 86.9 G/s MACs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TasNet/Conv-TasNet time-domain separation paradigm by introducing an asymmetric encoder-decoder (ESSD) where the encoder performs separation (early split into speaker-specific sequences) and a weight-shared decoder performs reconstruction with discriminative learning, departing from the conventional 'late split' design where a single feature sequence encodes all speakers until the final stage. For long-sequence modeling, it replaces the widely adopted dual-path architecture (DPRNN and its successors) with direct global (EGA) and local (CLA) Transformer blocks that avoid chunking entirely, drawing inspiration from QDPN's downsampling strategy and Conformer's convolutional attention. The cross-speaker attention module and multi-loss progressive training are borrowed from attractor-based separation and multi-stage restoration literature, respectively, but are integrated into the weight-sharing decoder to enable discriminative learning without explicit speaker embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is a substantial efficiency gain: the tiny SepReformer (3.5M params, 10.4 G/s MACs) outperforms the much larger Sepformer (26M params, 86.9 G/s MACs) on WSJ0-2Mix, and the base model achieves SOTA with roughly 55% fewer MACs than comparable dual-path systems. The ESSD framework's generality—demonstrated by applying it to Conv-TasNet and Sepformer—suggests it could be a drop-in improvement for existing time-domain separators. However, the work is limited to 2-speaker mixtures with a known speaker count, and the authors acknowledge that extending to unknown or variable speaker counts remains open. The underlying mechanism by which weight-sharing in the decoder induces effective discriminative learning is not yet theoretically characterized, leaving room for deeper analysis.
