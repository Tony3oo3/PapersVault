## Problem

Traditional cover selection for image steganography relies on exhaustive searches over fixed image databases using heuristic metrics (DCT coefficient counts, visual quality scores, complexity estimates) that lack a clear connection to actual message-hiding efficacy. These methods also ignore the influence of the specific encoder-decoder pair on cover suitability and cannot generate a cover tailored to a particular steganographic task. The paper asks whether cover selection can be reframed as a differentiable optimization problem in the latent space of pretrained generative models, directly minimizing message recovery error.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256×3 RGB cover image and a secret binary message of shape H×W×B (where B is the payload in bits per pixel, tested at 1–4 bpp) as input. It produces an optimized cover image of the same dimensions that, when passed through a fixed pretrained LISO steganographic encoder-decoder, yields a steganographic image with significantly lower message recovery error than the original cover. The optimization operates on a latent representation (DDIM latent x_T or GAN latent z), not on pixel values directly, and all generative and steganographic model weights remain frozen throughout.

## Method

The core framework inverts a cover image into the latent space of a pretrained generative model (DDIM or BigGAN) and then optimizes that latent vector via gradient descent to minimize the binary cross-entropy loss between the secret message and the decoder's recovered estimate, with the LISO encoder-decoder and generative model held fixed. For the DDIM variant, a deterministic forward diffusion maps the image to latent x_T, and a stochastic backward diffusion (with σ_t > 0) generates candidate covers during optimization, yielding better results than a fully deterministic pipeline. A key interpretive finding is that the pretrained LISO encoder implicitly embeds messages in low-variance pixel regions, mirroring the waterfilling power allocation strategy for parallel Gaussian channels; the cover selection optimization increases the fraction of low-variance pixels (from 81.6% to 92.4% overlap with high-message regions), thereby giving the encoder more capacity for reliable embedding. The DDIM-based approach consistently outperforms the GAN-based variant in both error reduction and image quality, and the framework extends naturally to JPEG-compression robustness and steganalysis evasion by incorporating additional differentiable loss terms.

## Results

On ImageNet (B=4 bpp), DDIM-optimized cover images reduce error rates by over 50% for several classes (e.g., Robin: 2.48%→1.01%, Cheetah: 2.15%→1.53%, Lady's Slipper: 0.17%→0.07%) while simultaneously improving BRISQUE scores (e.g., Robin: 27.8→19.81, Hartebeest: 16.97→13.63). On CelebA-HQ at B=1 bpp, DDIM reduces error from 2.6E-04% to 1.5E-05% (an order of magnitude improvement). Under JPEG compression (B=1 bpp), DDIM-optimized images halve the error rate on CelebA-HQ (0.12%→0.06%) while improving PSNR (21.09→21.53 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two proposed methods (DDIM-based and GAN-based cover selection) against unoptimized original images, as well as against each other. DDIM consistently outperforms GAN on error rate across 8 out of 10 ImageNet classes (e.g., Robin: 1.01% vs 1.32%, Cheetah: 1.53% vs 2.02%) and on BRISQUE image quality in 9 out of 10 classes. Both methods substantially reduce error rates relative to originals, with DDIM achieving the largest gains on high-error classes (Robin: 2.48%→1.01%, a 59% reduction). A notable finding is that optimizing solely for error rate also improves no-reference image quality (BRISQUE) in most cases, a phenomenon the authors attribute to reduced entropy and edge density. For steganalysis (Scenario 2, AFHQ-Dog), DDIM-optimized images achieve lower detection rates than originals at all payloads (e.g., B=4: 8.6% vs 9.2% XuNet detection), confirming that the optimization does not compromise security.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes cover selection for image steganography—from a database-search problem over heuristic image metrics (DCT coefficient counts, visual quality scores, complexity estimates) to a latent-space optimization problem within pretrained generative models. It builds directly on DDIM inversion (Kim et al., 2022) for the diffusion-based pipeline and on LISO (Chen et al., 2022) as the fixed steganographic encoder-decoder, while also exploring a BigGAN (Brock et al., 2018) variant. The key departure from prior work is that the encoder-decoder pair is incorporated into the optimization loop rather than treated as a post-hoc evaluator, and the cover image is generated rather than selected from a fixed pool. The paper further connects its empirical findings to classical information theory by showing the learned encoder's behavior approximates the waterfilling solution for parallel Gaussian channels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The waterfilling analogy provides the first interpretive account of *why* neural steganographic encoders prefer low-variance pixels, bridging learned steganography and classical communication theory in a way that could inform future encoder design. Practically, the framework offers a plug-and-play way to improve message recovery and image quality simultaneously, with demonstrated robustness to JPEG compression and steganalysis evasion. Open questions include extending the analysis to correlated (rather than independent) Gaussian channels, addressing the quality degradation observed at lower payloads, and generalizing beyond LISO to other steganographic frameworks. The absence of error bars and the omission of direct comparisons against other cover selection baselines in the main tables limit the strength of the empirical claims.
