## Problem

LLMs remain unsatisfactory at mathematical reasoning, and it is unclear whether this deficiency is inherent or stems from insufficient exposure to high-quality math data. Existing data generation methods face a diversity-validity dilemma: prompt-based rephrasing produces varied problems but risks introducing errors, while template-based rewriting guarantees correctness at the cost of limited diversity and scale. This paper asks whether a principled data generation pipeline can resolve this tension and, if so, whether the resulting data can close the gap to state-of-the-art math reasoning models.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes seed problems from GSM8K (7,473) and MATH (7,500) training sets, first autoformalizing them into SMT-LIB 2.6 (a standard notation compatible with Z3, CVC5, MathSAT, SymPy, and SciPy). Mutation parameters—random arithmetic operators, interpreted functions (identity, log, exp, arcsin, etc.), and auxiliary variable domains—are drawn per problem to control difficulty. The output is a triple per generated example: a natural-language problem (informalized by GPT-4), a ground-truth answer (computed by symbolic solvers), and a step-by-step reasoning path (GPT-4-generated, then verified against the solver answer). In total, 860K problems are produced across five difficulty levels for GSM8K and four for MATH.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage neuro-symbolic generation loop: (1) formalize the seed problem into SMT-LIB, (2) mutate the formal problem in symbolic space, and (3) informalize the mutated problem back to natural language via GPT-4. Mutation comprises two complementary operations: simplification (applying SMT tactics such as simplify, gaussian_elim, elim_term_ite, and quantifier elimination to reduce expression or constraint complexity) and complication (introducing auxiliary variables coupled through random operators and interpreted functions, then sampling their values via projected MCMC—perturbing a random subset of variables and resolving the rest with symbolic solvers to guarantee both diversity and well-definedness). The informalization step uses GPT-4 with carefully designed prompt patterns (P1 for word problems, P2 for pure math), and consistency is enforced by checking that GPT-4's own solution matches the symbolic solver's answer, yielding a 90–97% consistency rate. The resulting 860K supervised dataset is used to fine-tune LLaMA-2 (7B/13B) and Mistral-7B, which then outperform prior open-source math models by 3.6–11.3% on GSM8K and MATH.

## Results

On GSM8K, the Mistral-7B fine-tuned model achieves 86.8% accuracy, surpassing GPT-3.5-Turbo (80.8%) by 6.0 percentage points. On MATH, the same model reaches 37.3%, outperforming GPT-3.5-Turbo (34.1%) by 3.2 points. Across all three base models (LLaMA-2 7B, LLaMA-2 13B, Mistral 7B), the method improves over the strongest open-source baseline (MetaMath) by 10.6–11.3 points on GSM8K and 4.3–11.3 points on MATH. The method also generalizes to out-of-domain benchmarks SVAMP and ASDiv, with gains of up to 24.4% over MetaMathQA at equal data budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all five baselines across every base model and both primary benchmarks. Against MetaMath (the strongest open-source competitor, 395K data), the 860K generated data yields +10.6/+10.6 (LLaMA-2 7B), +10.1/+11.3 (LLaMA-2 13B), and +3.6/+4.3 (Mistral 7B) on GSM8K/MATH respectively. WizardMath and MAmmoTH trail by even larger margins (15–29 points on GSM8K), while MuggleMATH is competitive on GSM8K for LLaMA-2 13B (74.0 vs. 84.1) but weak on MATH (9.4 vs. 33.7). Against the tool-based Tora model (LLaMA-2 7B), the method wins on GSM8K (79.2 vs. 68.8) but trails on MATH (28.8 vs. 40.1), consistent with the observation that tool-based models excel at complex calculations but underperform on knowledge-reasoning tasks. At equal data budgets (240K GSM8K, 155K MATH), the method still beats MetaMathQA by 2.1–24.4 points across four datasets, confirming data-quality superiority beyond mere scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant lines of math data generation—LLM-based rephrasing (MetaMathQA, WizardMath) and template-based rewriting—by casting problem mutation into the formal SMT-LIB symbolic space, where validity is guaranteed by solvers (Z3, CVC5, MathSAT, SymPy) and diversity is achieved via projected MCMC sampling [14, 15]. The neuro-symbolic pipeline (formalize → mutate → informalize) repurposes GPT-4 not as the generator of new problems but as a translator between symbolic and natural language, inverting the role LLMs play in prior bootstrapping methods. The mutation mechanism itself draws on SMT tactics (Gaussian elimination, quantifier elimination, simplification) and their inverses, making the difficulty of generated problems explicitly controllable rather than emergent from prompt engineering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence that LLMs' mathematical deficiency is substantially data-driven rather than architectural, since fine-tuning on 860K symbolically-verified problems lifts 7B models above GPT-3.5 Turbo on GSM8K. However, the approach is bounded by the expressiveness of SMT-LIB (geometry and precalculus remain poorly supported), the dependence on GPT-4 for informalization and reasoning-path generation, and a limited set of mutation operators that struggle to reach competition-level difficulty. Open questions include whether the GPT-4 dependency can be replaced by a fine-tuned informalizer, whether problem fusion can extend the difficulty ceiling, and whether the scalability trend (consistent gains vs. MetaMathQA's plateau) persists at 10M+ scale.
