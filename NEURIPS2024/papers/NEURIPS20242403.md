## Problem

Learning the spectral properties of Langevin dynamics from biased simulations (e.g., metadynamics, OPES) is fundamentally difficult for transfer operator approaches because the generator depends linearly on the bias potential while the transfer operator does not (the exponential of a sum is not the product of exponentials). This non-commutativity means reweighting biased data cannot recover the true transfer operator, and the problem of principled generator learning from biased data remained open. The paper proves that the infinitesimal generator, accessed through its resolvent, is the correct object for unbiased learning from biased dynamics, and provides the first method with provable convergence guarantees in this setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a biased simulation trajectory (states sampled from the perturbed process with known bias potential V), the diffusion coefficient β, and optionally the full potential U. A key practical detail is that the loss function can be evaluated using only the gradient of the neural network features (via the energy kernel form) rather than requiring the full potential, making it applicable when only the bias and temperature are known. The output consists of the leading eigenfunctions and eigenvalues of the unbiased generator, from which process timescales (as 1/|λ_i|) and metastable state boundaries are directly read off. The computational cost of the training loss is O(nm²d) in the naive form but reducible to O(nmd) with the two-sample estimator.

## Method

The core insight is the identity E_π^η[f,g] = E_π'^η[f√(dπ/dπ'), g√(dπ/dπ')], which allows the regularized energy kernel (used to define the resolvent regression risk) to be computed from biased samples via the reweighting factor w(x) = exp(βV(x)). The method learns the resolvent (ηI − L)^(−1) on a hypothesis space spanned by basis functions z_i, parameterized by a neural network with trainable eigenvalue weights λ_i^θ, by minimizing a loss that finds the best rank-m decomposition of the resolvent in the energy space. The loss combines an approximation term (measuring how well Z_θ Λ_θ^η Z_θ* approximates the resolvent), a constant offset, and an optional orthonormality penalty on the features in L²_π. After training, the eigenpairs of the unbiased generator are extracted via a ridge regression estimator Ĝ = (Ŵ + ηγI)^(−1)Ĉ computed from reweighted empirical covariances, with eigenvalues recovered as λ̂_i = η − 1/ν_i. The approach is distinguished from transfer operator methods by its linear dependence on the bias (avoiding the exponential non-commutativity) and from prior generator learning by its use of the resolvent with energy-based regularization, which bypasses the unbounded nature of the generator and yields provable spectral convergence (Theorem 1) and a loss landscape guarantee (Theorem 2).

## Results

The paper demonstrates that its infinitesimal generator approach recovers the spectral properties of the unbiased Langevin dynamics from biased simulations across four benchmarks of increasing complexity (1D double well, 2D Muller-Brown, 30D alanine dipeptide, and chignolin miniprotein). On the Muller-Brown potential, the method achieves the closest eigenvalue estimates to ground truth among all compared methods and produces eigenfunctions that are qualitatively similar or slightly better than baselines, particularly near the transition state. Remarkably, on alanine dipeptide Dataset 1 (biased on a suboptimal CV with only 2 observed transitions in 800 ns), the method still recovers a first eigenfunction that cleanly separates the two metastable states. For chignolin, training on just 1 μs of biased data yields eigenfunctions in very good agreement with those obtained from a 106 μs unbiased trajectory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: deepTICA [7] (a transfer operator / TICA-based method) and the neural PDE solver of Zhang et al. [50] (a generator learning approach based on Rayleigh quotients). Against deepTICA, the proposed method clearly outperforms on eigenfunction quality, especially near the transition state, because transfer operator approaches suffer from a fundamental incompatibility: the bias enters the generator linearly but not the transfer operator, so reweighting the covariance matrices only approximates the true operator for small lag-times. Against Zhang et al. [50], the proposed method achieves better eigenvalue estimates (closest to ground truth on Muller-Brown), which the authors attribute to having trainable eigenvalue coefficients in the loss rather than fixed hyperparameters; eigenfunction shapes are similar or slightly better. On the alanine dipeptide task, the method's ability to extract meaningful collective variables from a dataset with only 2 transitions (suboptimal ψ-angle biasing) is a capability neither baseline is designed to address, and the resulting first eigenfunction generalizes well to an independent dataset biased on the optimal φ angle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the resolvent-based generator learning framework of Kostic et al. [21] from kernel regression to nonlinear regression over a neural-network-parameterized dictionary, and adapts the deep feature learning paradigm of Kostic et al. [24] to the biased-simulation setting. The key conceptual departure is from transfer operator approaches (deepTICA [7], VAMPnets [30]) that rely on reweighted covariance matrices: the paper shows that the generator's linear dependence on the bias potential makes it fundamentally more amenable to unbiased recovery from biased data than the transfer operator, whose exponential relationship with the generator does not commute with the bias perturbation. It also contrasts with the Rayleigh-quotient-based generator learning of Zhang et al. [50], which requires manual tuning of per-eigenfunction weights that this paper makes trainable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Biased enhanced-sampling simulations (metadynamics, OPES) are the standard tool for exploring rare events in molecular dynamics, yet extracting unbiased kinetic information from them has been an open problem for generator-based methods. The demonstrated ability to recover meaningful eigenfunctions from datasets containing only a handful of transitions (e.g., 2 transitions in alanine dipeptide) suggests the method could re-analyze old, poorly converged metadynamics runs to extract previously inaccessible physical information. A key limitation is the current restriction to time-homogeneous bias potentials; extending to time-dependent biasing (as in standard metadynamics where the bias evolves) is identified as important future work. The approach also opens the door to applying generator learning to larger-scale problems like protein-ligand binding and catalytic processes.
