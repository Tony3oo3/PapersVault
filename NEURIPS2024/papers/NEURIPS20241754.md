## Problem

While Mamba-based state space models achieve linear complexity and strong supervised performance, their generalization under domain shifts remains unexplored. The paper identifies that Mamba's input-dependent matrices (B, C, Δ) accumulate and amplify domain-specific features during the recurrent process, causing overfitting to source domains. No prior work has provided a theoretical analysis of Mamba's domain generalization or a targeted mechanism to suppress domain-specific information within these matrices.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images, splits them into 4×4 patches (without flattening into a 1D sequence), and processes them through a VMamba-T backbone (22M parameters, 4 stages with 2-2-9-2 VSS layers) to produce a class label. During training, START selectively perturbs a subset of tokens (top 75% by saliency) in 50% of batch samples via style mixing, but the entire augmentation pipeline is disabled at inference, introducing zero additional parameters or inference latency.

## Method

START is built on a theoretical analysis proving that Mamba's S6 layer acts as a data-dependent self-attention whose input-dependent matrices (S_Δ, S_C, S_B) accumulate domain-specific features across the recurrent sequence (Proposition 1), and that perturbing domain-specific features in tokens these matrices focus on forces them to learn domain-invariant representations (Proposition 2). Two variants compute token saliency: START-M uses the product S_C(x_i)·softmax(S_Δ(x_i))·S_B(x_i)·x_i from the input-dependent matrices, while START-X approximates saliency directly from token activation values. A binary mask selects the top P_token% salient tokens, and only those tokens undergo style perturbation (mixing mean and variance with a randomly sampled batch-mate using a Beta(0.1,0.1) interpolation), leaving background tokens untouched to avoid activating spurious domain noise. The method is applied across all network layers during training and requires no architectural modification or additional parameters.

## Results

On PACS, START-M achieves 91.77% average accuracy, outperforming the VMamba strong baseline (89.94%) by 1.83% and the best CNN-based method GMDG (85.60%) by 6.17%. On TerraIncognita, START-X reaches 58.27%, surpassing the best CNN method AGFA (52.40%) by 5.87% and the previous SSM-based DGMamba (54.60%) by 3.67%. On DomainNet, START-X achieves 52.81%, exceeding the best ViT-based method DoPrompt (48.30%) by 4.51%. All results use the VMamba-T backbone (22M params) with linear complexity and no additional inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

START-M outperforms the closest SSM-based competitor DGMamba by 0.57% on PACS (91.77% vs. 91.20%) and 0.09% on OfficeHome (77.09% vs. 77.00%), confirming that saliency-driven token perturbation provides a meaningful gain over DGMamba's heuristic hidden-state approach. Against the strongest CNN-based methods, START-M leads GMDG by 6.17% on PACS (91.77% vs. 85.60%) and EoA by 4.59% on OfficeHome (77.09% vs. 72.50%), while START-X surpasses AGFA by 5.87% on TerraIncognita (58.27% vs. 52.40%) and SAGM by 1.32% on VLCS (81.32% vs. 80.00%). Among ViT/MLP-based methods, START-M exceeds GMoE-S by 5.07% on PACS (91.77% vs. 86.70%) and ViP-S by 4.71% on OfficeHome (77.09% vs. 73.38%), all with fewer parameters (22M vs. 34M/25M). On the large-scale DomainNet benchmark, START-X achieves 52.81%, beating the best ViT method DoPrompt by 4.51% (52.81% vs. 48.30%) and the best CNN method AGFA by 5.71% (52.81% vs. 47.10%). The VMamba strong baseline itself already surpasses all CNN methods by 4.44% on PACS, and START further adds 1.83% on top, demonstrating that the SSM backbone and the token-aware transformation are complementary gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mamba/VMamba line of work by providing the first theoretical analysis of how selective SSMs generalize under domain shifts, identifying that input-dependent matrices (B, C, Δ) accumulate and amplify domain-specific features during the recurrent process. It departs from prior DG methods that rely on CNNs (texture bias) or ViTs (quadratic complexity) by proposing an SSM-based architecture with linear complexity, and differentiates itself from DGMamba [54]—the first Mamba-based DG method—by grounding its design in a formal generalization error bound rather than heuristic observations. The token-level style perturbation mechanism adapts MixStyle's channel-level statistics mixing to a saliency-guided, token-selective operation, making it a targeted refinement of existing augmentation strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical identification of input-dependent matrices as the locus of domain-specific feature accumulation in SSMs provides a principled explanation for why Mamba-based models can overfit source domains, opening a path for future work to design SSM architectures with built-in domain-invariance. The method's parameter-free, training-only nature (no inference overhead) makes it a practical drop-in for existing Mamba pipelines, and its demonstrated applicability to ViM and ViT backbones suggests broader relevance. However, the gains over the already-strong VMamba baseline are modest (~1.8–2%), and the theoretical analysis is limited to a single S6 layer with simplifying assumptions (e.g., approximating S_C ratios as 1), leaving open whether the accumulation mechanism and the saliency-driven remedy generalize to deeper, multi-scale SSM stacks or to non-visual sequence domains.
