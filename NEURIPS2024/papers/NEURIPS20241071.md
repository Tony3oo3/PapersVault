## Problem

Existing spiking GNNs operate exclusively in Euclidean space, ignoring the inherent geometric structure of graphs that Riemannian manifolds (e.g., hyperbolic space for hierarchical structures) can capture. Additionally, training spiking GNNs via Back-Propagation-Through-Time with surrogate gradients incurs high latency from recurrent gradient computation over all time steps. No prior work has combined spiking neural networks with Riemannian manifold geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241071/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static graph with node features and adjacency matrix, and produces node representations as points on a Riemannian manifold (default: 32-dimensional hyperbolic space in the Lorentz model). Unusually, each layer maintains a parallel pair of representations: binary spike trains (from the IF/LIF neuron) and continuous manifold points, linked by a diffeomorphism. For downstream inference, the manifold output is mapped to Euclidean space via the logarithmic map for node classification, while tangent vectors are used directly for link prediction, avoiding expensive successive exponential maps.

## Method

MSG introduces a Manifold Spiking Neuron that simultaneously emits spike trains (via IF/LIF integrate-fire dynamics) and updates a manifold representation by applying the exponential map: the pooled incoming current becomes a tangent vector that pushes the manifold point along a geodesic with step size ε. For training, the authors propose Differentiation via Manifold (DvM), which derives closed-form backward gradients using pullback properties of the exponential map, enabling recurrence-free layer-by-layer backpropagation that avoids BPTT's per-time-step gradient recursion and eliminates the need for surrogate gradients. Theoretically, MSG is shown to approximate a dynamic chart solver of manifold ODEs, where each spiking layer defines a chart via the logarithmic map and the layer update corresponds to a first-order Euler step in the tangent space. The architecture is instantiated on any geodesically complete manifold (hyperbolic, hyperspherical, Euclidean, or their products) with closed-form Jacobians derived for the Lorentz and sphere models.

## Results

On node classification, MSG achieves the best accuracy among all spiking GNNs across all four datasets: 89.27% (Computers), 93.11% (Photo), 92.65% (CS), and 95.93% (Physics), also surpassing the strongest Riemannian ANN baselines (κ-GCN, HyboNet) on three of four datasets. On link prediction (AUC), MSG scores 94.65%, 96.75%, 95.19%, and 93.43% respectively, outperforming all Euclidean spiking baselines but remaining behind the best Riemannian ANN models (e.g., HyboNet at 98.46% on Physics). In terms of energy, MSG consumes at least 1/20 the energy of Riemannian ANN baselines and achieves the lowest energy cost among SNN-based models on three of four datasets (e.g., 0.047 mJ on Computers vs. 0.105 mJ for SpikeGCN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSG outperforms all four Euclidean spiking baselines (SpikeNet, SpikeGCN, SpikeGCL, SpikeGT) on node classification across every dataset, with the largest margin over SpikeGT (e.g., +8.27% on Computers) and a consistent 0.2–0.7% lead over the strongest spiking competitor SpikeGCL. Against Riemannian ANN baselines, MSG achieves the best NC accuracy on Computers (89.27% vs. κ-GCN 89.20%), Photo (93.11% vs. HyboNet 92.67%), and Physics (95.93% vs. HyboNet 95.56%), though it trails SAGE by 0.06% on CS. On link prediction, MSG beats all spiking baselines (e.g., 96.75% vs. SpikeGCL 95.58% on Photo) but remains behind the top Riemannian ANN models, particularly HyboNet (98.46% on Physics) and Q-GCN (96.98% on Computers). In energy cost, MSG uses 0.047 mJ on Computers versus 1.614–1.671 mJ for Riemannian ANN baselines (≈35× less) and 0.105 mJ for SpikeGCN, confirming its advantage as the most energy-efficient model in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three previously separate threads: spiking GNNs (SpikeGCN, SpikeGCL, SpikeNet), Riemannian GNNs (HGCN, κ-GCN, Q-GCN), and neural ODE solvers on manifolds (Lou et al., 2020). It repurposes the exponential map—already standard in Riemannian GNNs for neighborhood aggregation—as a diffeomorphism that bridges discrete spike trains and continuous manifold representations, a use not present in either prior line. The Differentiation via Manifold (DvM) training algorithm is a novel departure from the BPTT-with-surrogate-gradient paradigm used by all existing spiking GNNs, leveraging pullback and pushforward operations in the tangent space to achieve recurrence-free backpropagation. The theoretical framing of each spiking layer as a chart in a dynamic chart solver of a manifold ODE connects the architecture to the Neural Manifold ODE literature in a way that has no direct precedent in the spiking network domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to combine energy-efficient spiking neurons with the geometric expressiveness of Riemannian manifolds for graph learning, opening a research direction that neither community had explored. The DvM training algorithm addresses a practical bottleneck (BPTT latency) that has constrained spiking GNNs, and the manifold ODE interpretation provides a principled understanding of what the architecture computes. However, the authors acknowledge the work is primarily theoretical, limited to undirected homophilous graphs, and note implementation complexity as a barrier to adoption. Open questions include extending to directed/heterophilous graphs, dynamic graphs, and whether the ODE-solver interpretation enables new architectural designs or convergence guarantees.
