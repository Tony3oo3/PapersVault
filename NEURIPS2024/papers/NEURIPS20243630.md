## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models (MLLMs) are trained for image-based referring or holistic video encoding, leaving them unable to describe what a specific target—identified by a single bounding box in one frame—does across an entire 20–30 second complex video. The core difficulty is that dense video features are highly redundant and computationally expensive, making it hard for an MLLM to locate and summarize target-specific spatiotemporal information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243630/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a video clip (20–30 s, encoded at 224×224 via CLIP ViT-L/14), a natural-language question (e.g., 'What is the <region> doing during this video?'), and a bounding box (t; x₁, y₁, x₂, y₂) in one arbitrary frame. The output is a free-form sentence describing the target's full action sequence across the video. Notably, the concrete object class is not provided in the prompt—the model must infer identity purely from the visual region. The video features are decomposed into three complementary slices: spatial (frame-averaged), temporal (space-averaged), and target-specific (RoI features from tracked and selected regions).

## Method

Artemis builds on Vicuna-7B with a CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder and a 2-layer MLP projector (1024→4096→4096), but its key contribution is an RoI tracking-and-selection pipeline that produces compact target-specific features: HQTrack localizes the referred object in every frame, and K-means clustering over the tracked bounding boxes selects M representative RoIs (default M=4) to balance diversity and redundancy. These RoIs are encoded via RoIAlign and projected by a separate Linear layer, while spatial and temporal features use the shared MLP. Training follows a three-stage schedule—(1) video-text pre-training of the MLP on 1.26M image/video pairs, (2) instruction tuning of LLM+MLP on 765K pairs, and (3) video-based referring fine-tuning with LoRA (rank 16) on the 45K VideoRef45K dataset—requiring only 28 hours total on 8×A800 GPUs. The design shows that native video-based referring training outperforms adapting image-based MLLMs with post-hoc summarization, and the compact RoI features transfer to general video QA and enable integration with grounding and summarization tools for multi-round and long-video understanding.

## Results

On the HC-STVG test set for video-based referring, Artemis achieves a BERT Score of 0.9135, BLEU@4 of 15.5, METEOR of 18.0, ROUGE_L of 40.8, CIDEr of 53.2, and SPICE of 25.4, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The most dramatic margins are on BLEU@4 (15.5 vs. 3.3 for the next-best Merlin) and CIDEr (53.2 vs. 10.5 for Merlin). On zero-shot video QA benchmarks, Artemis achieves 72.1% accuracy on MSVD-QA and 56.7% on MSRVTT-QA, both state-of-the-art among compared MLLMs, though it trails Valley-v3 on ActivityNet-QA (39.3% vs. 45.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Artemis decisively outperforms all six baselines on the primary video-based referring task (HC-STVG), winning on all six metrics. The gap is most pronounced against Merlin (the closest competitor, which also uses multi-frame input): Artemis achieves 4.7× higher BLEU@4 (15.5 vs. 3.3) and 5.1× higher CIDEr (53.2 vs. 10.5), indicating far more comprehensive and accurate action descriptions. Image-based referring models (Osprey, Ferret-13B, Shikra-7B) perform poorly because they only perceive single-moment actions and rely on GPT-3.5-Turbo to stitch together 5 frame-level answers, often hallucinating. Video-based MLLMs (Video-ChatGPT, Video-LLaVA) encode the video holistically without target-specific features, resulting in BLEU@4 scores below 2. On the secondary video QA benchmarks, Artemis leads on Video-ChatGPT (all 5 sub-metrics), MSVD-QA (72.1% vs. 64.9% for Video-ChatGPT), and MSRVTT-QA (56.7% vs. 51.1% for Valley-v3), but loses to Valley-v3 on ActivityNet-QA (39.3% vs. 45.1% accuracy), suggesting the referring-trained model does not uniformly dominate all video understanding tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

Artemis extends the visual instruction tuning paradigm (LLaVA) and the spatial/temporal feature extraction design of Video-ChatGPT into a new video-based referential understanding setting. It departs from image-based referring models (Osprey, Ferret, Shikra) by requiring the model to track a target across an entire video rather than a single frame, and from Merlin's multi-frame input by accepting only a single bounding box in any frame. The core methodological contribution is an RoI tracking-and-selection pipeline (HQTrack + K-means clustering) that produces compact, target-specific features, addressing the redundancy problem inherent in dense video encodings that prior video MLLMs handle holistically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first benchmark (VideoRef45K) with box-level prompts spanning complex 20–30 second videos, filling a gap where prior video MLLMs could only perform holistic captioning or single-frame referring. The demonstration that image-based referring outputs cannot be post-hoc summarized into coherent video descriptions (via GPT-3.5-Turbo) validates the need for native video-based referring training. Key limitations include dependence on tracking quality (HQTrack errors propagate) and spatial-temporal aliasing, while open questions include whether more sophisticated feature selection or stronger video foundation models would yield larger gains, and how the approach scales to longer or more complex videos.
