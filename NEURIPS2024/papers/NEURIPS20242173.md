## Problem

Prior work established that smoothed online learning is as easy as batch (PAC) learning for binary classification and scalar regression, but left open whether this equivalence extends to unbounded label spaces. This paper shows that it does not: PAC learnability of a hypothesis class is not sufficient for smoothed online learnability when |Y| is infinite, and it identifies the precise conditions under which the equivalence does hold.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a sequence of instances x_t drawn from σ-smooth distributions ν_t with respect to a known base measure μ, and must output a prediction ŷ_t ∈ Y before seeing the true label y_t. The adversary is oblivious, selecting all distributions ν_1,...,ν_T and all labels y_1,...,y_T before the game begins, with labels permitted to depend on the entire instance sequence. The output is a worst-case expected regret bound expressed in terms of the UBEME complexity C_{ε,σ}(H,μ), which measures the supremum over n and σ-smooth processes of the expected empirical covering number of H.

## Method

The separation result constructs a hypothesis class H where each hypothesis's output on a finite subset of X encodes its full identity via a tuple label, yielding a size-1 compression scheme (hence PAC learnable) but forcing any online learner to suffer Ω(T) regret because each round's label depends on a uniformly random bit the learner cannot predict. For the sufficiency direction, the paper introduces the UBEME property—uniform boundedness of expected empirical covering numbers over all σ-smooth processes—and proves it implies smoothed online learnability by running REWA over an ε-cover of H under d_μ, bounding the approximation error via Rademacher complexity of the symmetric-difference class, and controlling the change-of-measure term through σ-smoothness. For finite label spaces, a generalized Haussler packing lemma bounds the covering number in terms of the Graph dimension, recovering the known equivalence between PAC and smoothed online learnability.

## Results

The paper establishes a separation between PAC learnability and smoothed online learnability: Theorem 3.1 constructs a hypothesis class H with a size-1 compression scheme (hence PAC learnable) that suffers linear regret ≥ T/2 under μ=Uniform([0,1]) and σ=1. Theorem 3.3 quantifies the dependence on label space size, showing regret ≥ log(K)/(24·log log K) for |Y|=K, yielding separation whenever K ≥ 2^(T log T). On the positive side, Theorem 4.1 provides a sufficient condition (UBEME) yielding regret ≤ 6·inf_ε{εT/σ + √(T·log C_{ε²,σ}(H,μ))}, and Corollary 4.2 recovers the bound 12√(T·G(H)·log(41T|Y|/σ²)) for finite label spaces, matching Haghtalab [2018] up to constants when |Y|=2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines. The key comparison is to prior smoothed online learning results: Haghtalab [2018] and Haghtalab et al. [2020] showed that all VC classes (|Y|=2) are smoothed online learnable with regret O(√(T·VC(H)·log(T/σ))), and Block et al. [2022] extended this to real-valued regression via fat-shattering dimension. This paper shows these positive results do not extend to unbounded label spaces by constructing a PAC-learnable class with linear regret (Theorem 3.1). The sufficiency condition (UBEME) generalizes the VC/Graph dimension bounds: when |Y|=2, Haussler's packing lemma implies UBEME is automatically satisfied, recovering Haghtalab's result. For finite |Y|, the paper's Corollary 4.2 matches the prior rate up to a constant factor. Theorem 4.3 further shows that the natural candidate characterization (finiteness of metric entropy N(ε,H,d_μ)) fails in both directions, leaving the exact characterization of smoothed online learnability open.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the smoothed online learning framework of Haghtalab [2018], Haghtalab et al. [2020], and Block et al. [2022], which established that smoothed online learning is as easy as batch learning for binary classification (via VC dimension) and real-valued regression (via fat-shattering dimension). The key departure is extending the analysis to unbounded label spaces, where the paper shows the equivalence between PAC and smoothed online learnability breaks down. The proof technique draws on sample compression schemes (David et al. [2016]) to establish PAC learnability of the separating class, and on Haussler's packing lemma and Rademacher complexity arguments to prove the sufficiency condition (UBEME). The construction is inspired by Hanneke et al. [2024] but must overcome the additional constraint that the adversary can only draw from σ-smooth distributions rather than choosing examples deterministically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result challenges the prevailing narrative in the field that smoothing removes the fundamental hardness gap between adversarial online and batch learning. The clean separation—a class with a size-1 compression scheme (hence PAC learnable) suffering linear regret under even iid sampling—shows that unbounded label spaces introduce a qualitatively new difficulty. The UBEME sufficiency condition is a meaningful step toward characterizing smoothed online learnability, but the paper explicitly shows that the natural candidate (bounded metric entropy w.r.t. μ) is neither necessary nor sufficient, leaving the full characterization as an open problem. For practitioners, this matters in settings with massive label spaces (face recognition, protein structure prediction) where learning guarantees independent of label size are desirable.
