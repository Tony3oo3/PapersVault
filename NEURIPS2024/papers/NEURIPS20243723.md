## Problem

Interpretable conditional density estimation (CDE) methods are understudied: existing shallow CDE approaches rely on kernel-based models that are less accessible to non-statisticians, while the only tree-based CDE method (CADET) assumes a Gaussian distribution at each leaf, limiting its ability to capture multi-modal or skewed conditional densities. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a fully non-parametric, tree-based CDE model that is both more accurate and more interpretable than existing alternatives.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular datasets with a mix of continuous and binary (one-hot encoded categorical) feature columns plus a continuous target variable. It produces a full binary decision tree in which every leaf node stores a histogram model (equal-width bins over a globally fixed target range) that serves as the conditional density estimate for that region of feature space. The tree boundaries are set to the global target range plus a small constant to avoid zero-density predictions for out-of-range test points.

## Method

CDTree replaces the parametric leaf models of prior tree-based CDE with non-parametric histograms, and formalizes the joint optimization of tree structure and per-leaf bin counts under the Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle, eliminating all regularization hyperparameters. A key theoretical result (Proposition 1) proves that the NML regret for the full CDTree factorizes into a product of per-leaf one-dimensional histogram regrets, enabling efficient computation via known sub-linear-time algorithms for multinomial stochastic complexity. The learning algorithm is a greedy, top-down procedure that, at each iteration, simultaneously searches over which leaf to split, the splitting feature and quantile threshold (via a hierarchical granularity-level search over C·2^(d−1) equal-frequency quantiles), and the optimal number of histogram bins for both child nodes, selecting the combination that minimizes the MDL score. Tree size, structure (Catalan number), split conditions (Rissanen integer code for granularity level plus log-quantile cost), and histogram bin counts are all encoded losslessly, yielding a regularized maximum-likelihood criterion with no free tuning parameters.

## Results

On 14 UCI regression datasets, CDTree achieves the best average rank (2.07) among all interpretable CDE methods, with the lowest negative log-likelihood (NLL) in 6 out of 14 datasets. CDTree outperforms all tree-based baselines (CADET, CART-h, CART-k) on 12–13 out of 14 datasets, and its overall accuracy is on par with the black-box boosted-tree method LinCDE (average rank 4.0 vs. 4.46). CDTree also produces smaller trees (fewer leaves) than CADET and CART on most datasets, and is nearly immune to irrelevant features, with almost zero splits on noisy columns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the only prior interpretable tree-based CDE method, CADET, CDTree achieves lower NLL on 13 out of 14 datasets, with particularly large margins on high-dimensional data (e.g., 3.43 vs. 134.0 on forestfire, −1.02 vs. 94.4 on skillcraft). CDTree also beats the two CART-based baselines (CART-h and CART-k) on 12 out of 14 datasets, confirming that jointly optimizing the tree structure and histogram bins (rather than fitting histograms post-hoc) yields better density estimates. Among kernel-based interpretable methods, CKDE matches or beats CDTree on 6 low-dimensional datasets (≤7 features) but degrades sharply as dimensionality grows, while LSCDE and NKDE are consistently outperformed by CDTree. Against the black-box LinCDE boosted-tree model, CDTree is essentially on par in overall accuracy (average rank 4.0 vs. 4.46) despite using a single tree rather than an ensemble, and it produces a far more interpretable model. CDTree's key differentiator is robustness: when irrelevant Gaussian features are added, CDTree's NLL remains nearly unchanged and it uses almost zero splits on noisy features, whereas CART and CADET trees incorporate many irrelevant splits and their NLLs degrade noticeably.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MDL-based histogram density estimation framework (Kontkanen & Myllymäki, AISTATS 2007) from unconditional to conditional density estimation by embedding histograms as leaf models within a decision tree. It departs from the dominant kernel-based interpretable CDE methods (CKDE, NKDE, LSCDE) that have been the standard for 'shallow' models, arguing that tree-based methods are more interpretable to non-experts. The closest prior work is CADET (Cousins & Riondato, 2019), the only other single-tree CDE method, which assumes a Gaussian distribution per leaf; CDTree replaces this parametric assumption with non-parametric histograms and jointly optimizes tree structure and bin counts via MDL rather than fixing one before the other.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Interpretable CDE is critical for high-stakes domains like healthcare where understanding the full conditional distribution (not just the mean) is necessary for well-informed decisions, yet the field has been dominated by black-box models. The MDL formulation eliminates all regularization hyperparameters, and the demonstrated robustness to irrelevant features (near-zero splits on noise) is a prerequisite for trusting the intrinsic explanations the tree provides. Key limitations include the bounded-support assumption of histograms (unseen test points outside boundaries get zero density) and the greedy tree construction, which is not guaranteed to find the globally optimal tree. An open direction the authors highlight is using CDTree as a local surrogate for post-hoc explainability of black-box CDE models, for which no method currently exists.
