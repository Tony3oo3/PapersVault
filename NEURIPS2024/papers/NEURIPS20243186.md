## Problem

Existing time series domain adaptation methods either ignore frequency features or treat temporal and frequency features symmetrically, failing to exploit their distinct properties. The paper reveals that frequency features are more discriminative within a domain while temporal features are more transferable across domains, but no prior method leverages this asymmetry to jointly boost both transferability and discriminability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243186/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw multivariate time series (C variates × T time steps) in the temporal domain and derives a frequency-domain representation by segmenting the signal according to top-k significant periods, performing FFT on each segment, averaging across segments, and concatenating the results. Only amplitude values (not phases) of the complex frequency features are retained. The final prediction is a class label produced by the temporal classifier, with the frequency classifier serving as an auxiliary branch during training.

## Method

ACON (Adversarial CO-learning Networks) jointly models temporal and frequency features through three collaborative mechanisms. First, multi-period frequency feature learning segments the raw signal by top-k dominant periods, applies FFT per segment, and concatenates the averaged spectral patterns to capture multi-periodicity while filtering high-frequency noise. Second, temporal-frequency domain mutual learning uses asymmetric KL-divergence distillation: in the source domain the more discriminative frequency classifier guides the temporal classifier, while in the target domain the more transferable temporal classifier guides the frequency classifier. Third, domain adversarial learning operates in a temporal-frequency correlation subspace constructed via the outer product of temporal and (average-pooled) frequency features, so the domain discriminator must align both marginal distributions and their cross-domain correlations simultaneously. The overall training is a minimax game minimizing classification and mutual-learning losses while maximizing the adversarial loss over the feature extractors, with all loss weights set to 1 without tuning.

## Results

ACON achieves state-of-the-art average accuracy across all 8 datasets and 5 application tasks, winning on every single dataset. The most notable gains are on HHAR-D (+15.85% over CoDATS), WISDM (+10.70% over RAINCOAT), and HHAR-P (+10.15% over RAINCOAT), where the method reaches 65.04%, 84.80%, and 81.74% respectively. On the sleep staging task (CAP), ACON achieves 74.08% average accuracy, a 5.20% improvement over DIRT-T. The method also reports Macro-F1 improvements of over 4% on average across all datasets, confirming gains are not driven by class imbalance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACON outperforms all 9 baselines on every one of the 8 datasets. The strongest competitor is RAINCOAT (ICML 2023), the first method to incorporate frequency features into time series DA; ACON surpasses it by 10.15% on HHAR-P, 10.70% on WISDM, and 5.20% on CAP, but RAINCOAT remains competitive on UCIHAR (94.43% vs. 97.02%) and FD (86.75% vs. 91.74%). Among general-purpose DA methods, DIRT-T is the strongest on CAP (70.42%) and CDAN leads on FD (90.56%), yet ACON exceeds both by 5.20% and 1.30% respectively. Time-series-specific baselines CLUDA and AdvSKM consistently underperform, with CLUDA dropping to 54.69% on PCL and AdvSKM to 64.39% on CAP, suggesting that simple contrastive or kernel-matching strategies are insufficient for cross-domain time series. The largest absolute gap is on HHAR-D (device-based HAR), where ACON achieves 65.04% versus 56.27% for CoDATS, indicating that the temporal-frequency correlation subspace alignment is particularly effective under strong sensor heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of time series domain adaptation work that began with VRADA and CoDATS (adversarial/statistical alignment of temporal features) and was advanced by RAINCOAT (ICML 2023), which first introduced frequency features into the UDA pipeline via Sinkhorn divergence. ACON departs from RAINCOAT's treatment of temporal and frequency features as symmetric by empirically establishing their asymmetric roles—frequency features are more discriminative within a domain while temporal features are more transferable across domains—and designs a collaborative learning framework (multi-period FFT, KL-based mutual learning, and adversarial alignment in a temporal-frequency correlation subspace) that exploits this asymmetry rather than treating both feature types equally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that higher within-domain discriminability does not imply better cross-domain transferability is a useful corrective to the common assumption in representation learning that the most separable features are automatically the most transferable. The temporal-frequency correlation subspace for adversarial alignment (outer product + average pooling) is a lightweight mechanism that could inspire similar cross-representation alignment strategies in other multimodal settings. The authors acknowledge that the method remains unstable for time series with large variances, and the reliance on a fixed top-k period selection (determined before training) limits adaptability to non-stationary signals—both are open directions for future work.
