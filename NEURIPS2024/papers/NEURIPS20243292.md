## Problem

3D object detectors trained on one dataset (e.g., KITTI in Germany) suffer significant performance drops when deployed on new domains (e.g., Lyft in the USA), primarily due to mislocalization—incorrect box sizes, shapes, and orientations—rather than missed detections. Existing domain adaptation methods either require careful retraining on target-domain data or rely on simple scaling heuristics, leaving a persistent gap in localization accuracy that is hard to close without domain-specific supervision.

## Input / Output

Given a LiDAR point cloud and a set of noisy 7-DoF bounding box proposals (center [x,y,z], size [w,l,h], yaw θ) from any underadapted detector, DiffuBox outputs refined bounding boxes as a post-processing step requiring no retraining. The method operates on points within a context limit (4× box size) around each proposal, transforming them into a scale-invariant Normalized Box View (NBV) where coordinates are box-relative and confined to a [-1,1]³ cube. Inference runs 14 denoising steps per box, with initial noise level scaled by detector confidence, followed by NMS on the refined set.

## Method

DiffuBox trains a transformer-based point diffusion denoiser (12 self-attention layers, hidden size 1024) to model the distribution of LiDAR points relative to a well-localized bounding box in the Normalized Box View, where a homogeneous transformation maps each box to a unit cube, eliminating size priors. During training, Gaussian noise is applied to the bounding box parameters (not the point cloud) to simulate mislocalization, and the denoiser learns to predict the displacement from noisy to clean NBV points. At inference, the chain rule backpropagates the learned score function through the differentiable NBV transformation to obtain gradients with respect to the 7-DoF box parameters, which are then integrated via a probabilistic flow ODE (2nd-order Heun solver) over 14 denoising steps. An optional shape guidance term regularizes box dimensions toward target-domain size statistics, and the entire pipeline is detector-agnostic, operating as a plug-and-play refinement on any 3D detector's output.

## Results

On KITTI→Lyft cars (3D mAP@IoU 0.7, 0-80m), DiffuBox applied to the unadapted Direct detector achieves 35.56, a +23.5 mAP gain over the baseline (12.07), reaching performance comparable to retraining-based methods like ST3D (22.47) without any target-domain training. When combined with the strongest baseline SN (36.64), DiffuBox further improves to 38.72. The gains are especially pronounced at near range (0-30m), where Direct+DiffuBox reaches 62.94 3D mAP versus 25.76 for Direct alone. Across all five domain adaptation baselines and multiple detectors (PointPillar, SECOND, PV-RCNN, CenterPoint, DSVT), DiffuBox consistently improves 3D mAP by 17–24 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffuBox is evaluated as a post-processing refinement on top of five domain adaptation baselines: Direct (no adaptation), Output Transformation (OT), Statistical Normalization (SN), Rote-DA, and ST3D. Against Direct (12.07 3D mAP), DiffuBox yields the largest absolute gain of +23.5 mAP, bringing the unadapted detector to 35.56—surpassing ST3D (22.47) and approaching SN (36.64) without any retraining. Against OT (11.89), the gain is +19.3 mAP. Against the strongest baseline SN (36.64), DiffuBox still adds +2.1 mAP to reach 38.72. Against Rote-DA (28.63) and ST3D (22.47), gains of +22.4 and +17.3 mAP respectively are observed. The paper also shows DiffuBox consistently improves predictions from five different detector architectures (PointPillar, SECOND, PV-RCNN, CenterPoint, DSVT), confirming its detector-agnostic nature. Notably, the gains are largest at near range (0-30m) where more LiDAR points are available for the diffusion model to condition on.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffuBox sits at the intersection of diffusion-based generative modeling (Karras et al. 2022 for the training framework; Song et al. 2020 for the score-function/ODE formulation) and the domain-consistency observation from Luo et al. 2024 that LiDAR points relative to ground-truth boxes form a domain-invariant 'shape' distribution. Unlike prior diffusion-based detection frameworks (DiffusionDet, DiffRef3D) that integrate diffusion into the detection pipeline end-to-end, DiffuBox repurposes a point-cloud diffusion model as a detector-agnostic post-processing step that denoises bounding boxes via the chain rule through the Normalized Box View (NBV) transformation. This departs from existing domain adaptation methods (SN, Rote-DA, ST3D) that require retraining on source or target data, positioning DiffuBox as an orthogonal, training-free refinement layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a single trained diffusion model can be deployed off-the-shelf to refine boxes from any detector in any new domain without retraining, closing much of the gap to methods that require careful per-domain adaptation. The consistent gains across five architecturally diverse detectors (PointPillar through DSVT) and multiple target datasets (Lyft, Ithaca365, nuScenes) strengthen the generality claim. Key limitations remain: DiffuBox cannot recover completely missed detections (false negatives), and its gains diminish at longer ranges where fewer LiDAR points are available. The retraining extension (Table 5) hints at an iterative self-improvement loop, but the paper leaves open whether this converges or compounds errors over multiple rounds.
