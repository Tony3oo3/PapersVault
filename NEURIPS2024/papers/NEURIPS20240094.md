## Problem

Best-of-n (BoN) sampling is a simple and effective alignment strategy, but its relationship to training-based methods (RLHF, DPO) was unclear, and its n× inference cost made it impractical for deployment. The paper asks: (1) is BoN theoretically optimal among alignment policies, and (2) can we train a single-pass model to mimic the BoN distribution without the repeated sampling overhead?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240094/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a single text completion from a fine-tuned LLM (Pythia-2.8b), avoiding the n-sample draw-and-rank procedure of vanilla BoN. Training data is constructed by drawing n completions from a reference model per prompt, scoring them with a reward model, and retaining only the best and worst samples as positive/negative pairs. The reward model (an off-the-shelf model) serves as the ground-truth ranker for both training data construction and evaluation win-rate computation.

## Method

The paper first proves that BoN is essentially Pareto-optimal in the win-rate vs. KL-divergence trade-off by embedding both BoN and RLHF/DPO policies in a common class of reward-weighted tiltings of the reference distribution, then showing the optimal tilting (exponential in the CDF of the reward) is nearly identical to BoN's power-law tilting. For practical alignment, BoNBoN combines two objectives that both have the BoN policy as their minimizer: SFT-BoN (supervised fine-tuning on best-of-n samples, which anchors likelihoods) and IPO-BoN (a contrastive squared-loss on best/worst pairs with an analytically derived β_n* = 1/(2(n−1)H_{n−1}), which improves data efficiency by exploiting the worst-of-n samples). The SFT term prevents the contrastive objective from 'cheating' by pushing down both winning and losing likelihoods (which causes off-target drift like length inflation), while the IPO term makes use of all n samples rather than just the winner. The combined loss is nearly hyperparameter-free: β_n* is fixed by theory, and α (balancing the two terms) is set so both contribute equally to the total loss.

## Results

BoNBoN achieves approximately 85% win rate against the reference model on both the Anthropic HH dialogue task and the OpenAI TL;DR summarization task, while minimally affecting off-target attributes such as response length. In contrast, DPO and IPO configurations that reach comparable win rates (~85%) nearly double the average response length, indicating substantial off-target drift. Theoretically, the best-of-n policy achieves a win rate of n/(n+1) (≈88.9% for n=8) with KL divergence of log(n)−(n−1)/n (≈1.20 for n=8), and the maximum win-rate gap between BoN and the true optimal policy is less than 1 percentage point (at n=2). BoNBoN is nearly hyperparameter-free, using an analytically derived β_n* and a fixed α=0.005 to balance the SFT and IPO loss terms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares BoNBoN against DPO and IPO (both on original preference data and on best-and-worst-of-8 samples) across multiple β values. BoNBoN dominates both baselines on the win-rate vs. off-target-deviation trade-off: at comparable win rates (~85%), DPO and IPO produce responses that are nearly twice as long as the reference model, while BoNBoN keeps response length close to the reference. BoNBoN also outperforms SFT-BoN (which only uses best-of-n samples) and IPO-BoN (which only uses the contrastive objective) at all β values, demonstrating that the combined SFT+IPO loss is essential. The advantage is most pronounced on the off-target axis: DPO/IPO must push probability mass to very long responses to achieve the target win rate, whereas BoNBoN avoids this 'cheating' behavior by explicitly maintaining high likelihood on the best-of-n samples. The paper notes that BoNBoN's advantage over DPO/IPO is consistent across both the dialogue and summarization tasks and across all tested n values (2–8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies best-of-n (BoN) sampling and training-based alignment methods (RLHF, DPO, IPO) within a common class of reward-weighted tiltings of the reference model, showing that BoN is essentially Pareto-optimal for the win-rate vs. KL divergence trade-off. It builds directly on the IPO objective from Azar et al. (2024) and the DPO framework from Rafailov et al. (2023), but departs from both by eliminating the explicit KL-regularization hyperparameter: the BoNBoN loss targets the BoN distribution directly, with the IPO β analytically derived (Theorem 3) rather than tuned. The approach also connects to prior work on reward transformations for alignment (Wang et al. 2024, Azar et al. 2024) and on the optimality of BoN (Beirami et al. 2024, Yang et al. 2024), but adds the practical training procedure to amortize the n× inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that BoN is essentially optimal for win-rate vs. KL provides a clean theoretical justification for a simple, widely-used heuristic, and the BoNBoN method offers a nearly hyperparameter-free alternative to DPO/IPO that avoids the need to estimate and optimize against KL divergence. A notable practical finding is that estimated KL poorly captures salient off-target changes (e.g., response length), suggesting that explicit KL regularization may be a misleading proxy for the actual alignment goal. Open questions include whether the advantage over online RLHF methods persists at scale, whether win-rate is the right objective when rewards have absolute scale, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the small Pythia-2.8b model and two datasets used in experiments.
