## Problem

Previous federated algorithms for distributed online model selection (e.g., eM-KOFL, POF-MKL) were proposed without establishing whether collaboration among clients is actually necessary, and their regret bounds scale linearly with the number of clients M. This paper answers the question from a novel computational-constraints perspective, proving that collaboration is unnecessary when clients have unlimited computation but becomes necessary when per-client computation is restricted to o(K), where K is the number of candidate hypothesis spaces.

## Input / Output

Each of M clients observes a sequence of tabular instances x_t^(j) and labels y_t^(j) without sharing raw data, while a central server coordinates via model/gradient exchange. The server samples only J out of K hypotheses per round (J can be as small as 2, independent of K) and broadcasts only the selected model parameters to each client, reducing per-round download cost from O(K) to O(log K) bits per client. Clients return estimated losses and gradients for the J sampled hypotheses, from which the server reconstructs full K-dimensional estimators via importance weighting.

## Method

The proposed FOMD-OMS algorithm is built on a new federated framework, FOMD-No-LU, which removes local updating (eliminating client drift) and supports estimated rather than exact gradients. Two key techniques achieve o(K) communication: (i) decoupling model selection from prediction, so the server performs the sampling over the probability simplex Δ_K and only broadcasts the selected hypothesis parameters rather than the full K-dimensional distribution; and (ii) using the FOMD-No-LU update rule with a weighted negative entropy regularizer for the probability vector and a Euclidean regularizer for each hypothesis, both solved via a single binary search on the server. A new Bernstein's inequality for martingales with a random (data-dependent) variance term enables high-probability multi-scale regret bounds that adapt to the complexity of the individual optimal hypothesis space, improving upon prior bounds that depend only on the worst-case complexity across all K spaces.

## Results

FOMD-OMS achieves a per-round client time complexity of O(D) compared to O(DK) for both eM-KOFL and POF-MKL, yielding a K-fold speedup (K=8 kernels in experiments). On 8 regression datasets, FOMD-OMS (J=2) matches eM-KOFL in MSE (e.g., 0.00318 vs 0.00292 on elevators, 0.01917 vs 0.01942 on bank) while running 5–6× faster (e.g., 0.52s vs 2.67s on elevators). Against POF-MKL, FOMD-OMS achieves substantially lower MSE (e.g., 0.00318 vs 0.00806 on elevators, 0.02373 vs 0.05248 on calhousing) at a fraction of the running time. Theoretically, FOMD-OMS provides a multi-scale regret bound that adapts to the complexity 𝔠ᵢ of the individual optimal hypothesis space, whereas prior bounds depend on the worst-case 𝔠 = maxᵢ 𝔠ᵢ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against eM-KOFL [Hong and Chae, 2022], FOMD-OMS achieves comparable MSE across all 8 datasets (within 1–15% relative difference, e.g., 0.00318 vs 0.00292 on elevators, 0.01534 vs 0.01481 on Year) but reduces per-round client time from O(DK) to O(D), yielding a 5–6× speedup in total running time (e.g., 0.52s vs 2.67s on elevators, 4.82s vs 12.74s on Slice). The paper proves this speedup comes at no theoretical cost: the regret bound of FOMD-OMS matches or improves upon eM-KOFL when K = O((𝔠/𝔠ᵢ)·M·ln K), and the collaboration in eM-KOFL is shown to be unnecessary. Against POF-MKL [Ghari and Shen, 2022], FOMD-OMS dominates on both MSE and running time: MSE is 2–3× lower (e.g., 0.00318 vs 0.00806 on elevators, 0.02373 vs 0.05248 on calhousing) and running time is 5–6× faster. The paper attributes POF-MKL's inferiority to three factors: (i) it learns personalized rather than global probability distributions, (ii) it uniformly samples kernels rather than using the learned distribution, and (iii) it executes model selection on each client incurring O(DK) time. The theoretical comparison (Table 1) further shows FOMD-OMS achieves a regret bound that adapts to individual hypothesis space complexity 𝔠ᵢ, while both baselines depend on the worst-case 𝔠.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the federated online mirror descent framework (FedOMD, Mitra et al. 2021) and the intermittent communication protocol (Woodworth et al. 2021), but departs from both by removing local updating (yielding FOMD-No-LU) and by decoupling model selection from prediction to reduce per-round communication from O(K) to o(K). It extends the online model selection literature (Foster et al. 2017, Zhang and Liao 2018) to the decentralized setting and provides the first lower bounds on regret for both cooperative and noncooperative algorithms in this setting. The paper also introduces a new Bernstein's inequality for martingales with random variance, which enables high-probability multi-scale regret bounds that prior analyses could not achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear theoretical characterization of when federated collaboration is actually beneficial for online model selection: unnecessary without computational constraints, but necessary when per-client computation is limited to o(K). This resolves ambiguity in prior federated OMKL algorithms (eM-KOFL, POF-MKL) whose collaboration was shown to be superfluous. A key limitation is that the lower bound for the no-constraint case is asymptotic (T→∞); a non-asymptotic version would strengthen the result. The decoupling technique and the new Bernstein's inequality are of independent interest for the broader online learning and federated learning communities.
