## Problem

Permutation-based causal discovery in Linear Gaussian Acyclic Models (LiGAMs) requires repeatedly constructing the DAG G^π for candidate permutations, but existing score-based methods (BIC, BDeu, CV General) have prohibitive computational complexity—O(n⁵) for initial computation and O(n⁴d) for updates—making them infeasible for large variable sets. No prior approach achieved both theoretical soundness and a complexity independent of the number of samples N.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an observational data matrix D (N×n i.i.d. samples), a permutation π over [n], and a precomputed whitening matrix W derived from the SVD of Cov(X). It outputs the DAG G^π (the unique DAG compatible with π that best fits the data) and the orthogonal matrix Q. Crucially, QWO supports incremental updates: when only a block of d positions in π changes, only O(n²d) work is needed by reusing previously computed columns of Q outside the modified block, rather than recomputing from scratch.

## Method

QWO reformulates the G^π construction problem by characterizing the set of all LiGM coefficient matrices B(X) as {I − QW : Q orthogonal}, where W is the whitening matrix from the SVD of Cov(X). For a given permutation π, the unique B whose graph is compatible with π is found by solving for Q such that P_π QW P_π^T is upper triangular with identity diagonal. This is accomplished by applying the Gram-Schmidt algorithm to the columns of W in the reverse permutation order π(n), π(n−1), …, π(1), where each q_π(i) is set to the normalized residual of projecting w_π(i) onto the span of {w_π(i+1), …, w_π(n)}. The resulting G^π = G(I − QW) is then read off from the sparsity pattern of I − QW. This yields O(n³) initial and O(n²d) update complexity—both independent of N—giving an O(n²) speedup over the state-of-the-art BIC-based approach while preserving theoretical soundness under the SMR assumption.

## Results

On small real-world graphs (5–11 nodes, 500 samples), QWO with GRaSP achieves SKF1 scores of 0.85–1.0 and PSHD of 0–0.87, matching or closely approaching BIC (SKF1 0.93–1.0, PSHD 0–0.81) while running 10–20× faster (e.g., 0.04s vs. 0.39s on SACHS). BDeu and CV General are orders of magnitude slower (41–1326s) and often less accurate (BDeu SKF1 as low as 0.12). On high-dimensional ER graphs (10,000 samples), QWO maintains high accuracy while demonstrating a clear O(n²) speedup over BIC for both GRaSP and Hill-Climbing search strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QWO matches BIC in accuracy on most small graphs (e.g., both achieve SKF1=1.0 on CANCER and SURVEY with GRaSP) while being 10–20× faster (0.04s vs. 0.39s on SACHS; 0.002s vs. 0.02s on ER2). Against BDeu, QWO is dramatically superior on both accuracy and speed: BDeu achieves SKF1 as low as 0.12 (ER2) and PSHD up to 1.81, while taking 41–497s per run versus QWO's 0.002–0.05s. CV General is similarly outperformed, requiring 102–1326s and showing inconsistent accuracy (SKF1 dropping to 0.50–0.57 on SURVEY). The key trade-off is that BIC with GRaSP achieves slightly higher SKF1 on SACHS (0.93 vs. 0.88) and ASIA (1.00 vs. 0.87), but QWO's O(n²) speedup makes it the only practical option for high-dimensional settings where BDeu and CV General become infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits squarely within the permutation-based causal discovery framework for LiGAMs, building on the sparsest-permutation principle of Raskutti & Uhler (2018) and the ordering-based search space reduction of Teyssier & Koller (2005). Its key departure is replacing the score-based computation of G^π (via BIC, BDeu, or CV General with grow-shrink parent search) with a whitening-plus-Gram-Schmidt procedure grounded in the characterization of all LiGM coefficient matrices as I − QW for orthogonal Q (Theorem 4.4). This reframing, drawing on classical whitening results (Fukunaga 1990; Kessy et al. 2018), yields an O(n²) speedup over BIC and removes dependence on sample size N, while remaining a drop-in module for existing search strategies like GRaSP and hill-climbing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(n²) speedup and N-independence make permutation-based causal discovery in LiGAMs substantially more scalable, potentially enabling the use of consistent search methods (e.g., GRaSP) on larger variable sets that were previously intractable. The main limitation is the restriction to linear, Gaussian, acyclic models; the authors note that the orthogonal-matrix characterization extends to cyclic LiGMs (Eq. 13), but solving the resulting non-convex sparsification problem remains open. The oracle inverse-covariance experiments reveal that finite-sample covariance estimation, not the QWO module itself, is the dominant error source, pointing to improved covariance estimation as a complementary direction.
