## Problem

Existing long-tail recognition methods either adjust training procedures (re-weighting, re-sampling) or rely on external data and pre-trained models for augmentation, making them impractical in real-world scenarios where specialized data is scarce and closely guarded. This paper asks whether a diffusion model trained entirely from scratch on the long-tailed dataset alone can generate useful synthetic samples to rebalance the distribution, and identifies which generated samples actually help classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243908/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long-tailed RGB image dataset (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT) with imbalance ratios of 10, 50, or 100, and produces two outputs: synthetic images for tail classes (generated up to a per-class threshold N_t of 500 for CIFAR-100-LT, 5000 for CIFAR-10-LT, 300 for ImageNet-LT) and a final classifier (ResNet-32/10/50) that maps images to class labels. The diffusion model and feature extractor are both initialized randomly and trained solely on the imbalanced dataset, with no external data or pre-trained weights.

## Method

DiffuLT trains a conditional DDPM from scratch on the long-tailed dataset using a composite loss: the standard DDPM noise-prediction term, the CBDM class-balancing regularizer, and a novel AID loss (L_AID) that uses a separately trained feature extractor φ_0 to penalize deviations from a target distance of 1.5·d_f in feature space at each denoising step, thereby biasing generation toward approximately-in-distribution (AID) samples that blend head and tail class information. During generation, φ_0 filters out out-of-distribution samples, and the remaining synthetic images are combined with the original dataset to train a classifier via a weighted cross-entropy loss that down-weights generated samples (ω=0.3). The key empirical finding is that AID samples—those slightly deviating from the real data manifold—yield the highest per-sample accuracy gain, while OOD samples actively harm performance, and the diffusion model naturally produces more AID samples when trained with access to head-class data.

## Results

On CIFAR100-LT with imbalance ratio r=100, DiffuLT achieves 51.5% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline H2T (48.9%) by 2.6% and the CE baseline (38.3%) by 13.2%. On CIFAR10-LT (r=100), it reaches 84.7%, outperforming ADRW (83.6%) by 1.1%. On ImageNet-LT with ResNet-50, DiffuLT attains 56.4% overall accuracy, exceeding ADRW (54.1%) by 2.3% and RIDE (54.9%) by 1.5%, while improving the 'Few' class accuracy from 5.8% (CE) to 39.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffuLT outperforms all 18 baselines on CIFAR100-LT across all three imbalance ratios, with the largest margin (2.6%) over H2T at r=100 and a consistent 2.5–4.4% lead over the next-best methods (H2T, CSA, MisLAS) at r=50 and r=10. On CIFAR10-LT, it wins at r=100 (84.7% vs. ADRW's 83.6%) and r=50 (86.9% vs. CSA's 86.0%), but is marginally outperformed by CSA at r=10 (90.7% vs. 90.8%). On ImageNet-LT, DiffuLT with ResNet-10 achieves 50.4%, beating the best reported baseline RIDE (45.9%) by 4.5%; with ResNet-50 it reaches 56.4%, surpassing ADRW (54.1%) and RIDE (54.9%). The 'Few' class accuracy on CIFAR100-LT (r=100) jumps to 29.7%, an 8.3% improvement over the nearest competitor RIDE (23.9%) and a 20.6% gain over the CE baseline (9.1%), confirming the method's primary benefit lies in tail-class recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the Class-Balancing Diffusion Model (CBDM, Qin et al., 2023) to introduce a diffusion-based data augmentation pipeline for long-tail recognition. It departs from the dominant paradigm in long-tail learning—re-weighting, re-sampling, and architectural modifications—by instead modifying the training data itself, and further distinguishes itself from prior synthetic-data approaches (e.g., MosaicOS, DiffusionEngine) by training the generative model entirely from scratch on the long-tailed dataset without any external data or pre-trained weights. The key conceptual departure is the identification of approximately-in-distribution (AID) samples as the most beneficial generated samples, formalized through a novel L_AID loss that uses a feature extractor to guide the diffusion process toward controlled deviations in feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that a diffusion model trained solely on a long-tailed dataset can meaningfully improve classification without external resources, which is critical for real-world scenarios (military, medical) where data is scarce and proprietary. The AID sample analysis provides a mechanistic explanation for why generated samples help—blending head-class information into tail classes creates novel, diverse examples rather than mere copies—offering a reusable diagnostic for evaluating synthetic data quality. The primary limitation is computational cost (24 hours for CIFAR100-LT, ~6 days for ImageNet-LT), which constrains scalability to larger benchmarks like iNaturalist. An open question the authors raise is whether pre-trained diffusion models applied to previously unseen long-tail distributions could retain the fairness guarantee while dramatically reducing training time.
