## Problem

Visual prompt tuning and other parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods suffer significant performance degradation when the finetuning dataset has a substantial distributional disparity from the pretraining data (e.g., ImageNet-21K). Existing prompt tuning approaches focus predominantly on spatial-domain information, limiting their capacity to adapt to novel tasks whose image distributions deviate markedly from the pretrained backbone's training distribution. The paper asks whether integrating frequency-domain information via the Fast Fourier Transform can make prompt tuning generalize robustly across datasets with varying degrees of disparity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240180/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input, which are processed by a frozen Vision Transformer backbone (ViT-Base/16 or Swin-Base) into patch embeddings. Learnable prompt embeddings (d-dimensional vectors) are prepended to the patch token sequence at each encoder layer, and only these prompts are updated during finetuning (0.27%–1.14% of total parameters depending on backbone and task). The output is a class label for image classification across 19 VTAB-1k tasks (grouped into Natural, Specialized, and Structured) and 5 FGVC fine-grained tasks, with each task using 1000 training examples.

## Method

VFPT extends Visual Prompt Tuning by applying a 2D Fast Fourier Transform to a configurable fraction (α) of the learnable prompt embeddings at each encoder layer, along both the sequence-length and hidden dimensions. The real part of the FFT output is retained to preserve compatibility with the frozen self-attention modules, and the resulting Fourier-domain prompts are concatenated with the remaining spatial-domain prompts to form the integrated prompt vector. This design requires no architectural modifications to the backbone and only a few lines of code beyond standard VPT, adding a marginal 2.8% training overhead. The key insight is that frequency-domain prompts expand the search space for latent embeddings, enabling the frozen model to capture complementary features (e.g., luminance and noise decomposition) that spatial-domain prompts alone miss, particularly when pretrain-finetune data disparity is large. Empirically, VFPT achieves 73.20% mean accuracy on VTAB-1k with 0.66% tuned parameters, outperforming full fine-tuning in 22 of 24 tasks and VPT in 23 of 24, with loss landscape analyses showing flatter minima and greater convexity.

## Results

On VTAB-1k with ViT-Base/16 (ImageNet-21k pretrained), VFPT achieves a mean accuracy of 73.20%, outperforming full fine-tuning (65.57%) by 7.63% while tuning only 0.66% of parameters. The largest gains appear on the Structured group (60.19% vs. 47.64% for full fine-tuning), where data disparity is highest. On FGVC, VFPT reaches 89.24% mean accuracy, surpassing full fine-tuning (88.54%) and VPT-D (89.11%). VFPT outperforms full fine-tuning in 22 of 24 tasks and VPT in 23 of 24 tasks across both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VFPT outperforms full fine-tuning [92] in 22 of 24 tasks, with the most dramatic margin on VTAB-1k Structured (60.19% vs. 47.64%, +12.55%) where data disparity is greatest. Against the closest prompt-tuning baselines, VFPT beats VPT-D [4] by 3.77% mean accuracy on VTAB-1k (73.20% vs. 69.43%) and E²VPT [5] by 1.78% (73.20% vs. 71.42%), while using comparable or fewer parameters (0.66% vs. 0.73% and 0.39% respectively). Among extra-module methods, VFPT surpasses LoRA [35] (72.25%), AdaptFormer [95] (72.32%), and ARCatt [96] (72.32%) on mean VTAB-1k accuracy, and also exceeds Adapter [32] (62.41%), Bias [30] (62.05%), and Sidetune [31] (45.65%) by wide margins. On FGVC, VFPT (89.24%) narrowly trails LoRA (89.46%) but leads all other methods including full fine-tuning (88.54%). For self-supervised pretraining (Table 3), VFPT achieves the highest Number of Wins vs. full fine-tuning among PEFT methods (8/19 under MAE, 14/19 under MoCo v3), substantially outperforming VPT-D (e.g., 53.59% vs. 36.02% on MAE Natural) and GPT [101] (53.59% vs. 47.61%). Partial tuning baselines (Linear [92], Partial-1 [93], MLP-3 [94]) are consistently outperformed by 10–30+ points on Structured tasks, confirming that VFPT's frequency-domain integration is critical for high-disparity generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) by Jia et al. (ECCV 2022) through a minimal architectural modification: applying 2D Fast Fourier Transform to a subset of learnable prompt embeddings before concatenation with the remaining spatial-domain prompts. Conceptually, it draws from human visual cognition's multi-domain processing (spatial + frequency) and from the broader literature on FFT in vision (FourierFormer, FNet, FDA), but is distinct from those works in that it operates on prompt parameters rather than on input images or network weights. It positions itself against the full spectrum of PEFT methods (partial tuning, adapters, LoRA, other prompt variants like E2VPT and EXPRES) while emphasizing simplicity over architectural complexity, and differentiates from the concurrent Fourier prompt tuning work [77] by targeting cross-dataset generalization rather than modality-incomplete segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized but underexplored failure mode of PEFT—performance degradation under high pretrain-finetune data disparity—by showing that frequency-domain prompt information provides a general remedy without task-specific engineering. The optimization landscape analysis (flatter minima, higher convexity) and attention-map interpretability results offer mechanistic explanations that go beyond empirical gains, potentially guiding future prompt design. Limitations include evaluation restricted to image classification with ViT/Swin backbones, per-task tuning of the Fourier percentage, and the modest absolute improvement over the strongest prompt baseline (E2VPT). Open questions include whether the frequency-domain insight transfers to other modalities (the authors hint at language-domain extension in the appendix) and whether the approach composes with other prompt-tuning enhancements.
