## Problem

Current post-training quantization methods for LLMs compress weights to 4 bits but still rely on mixed-precision (16b×4b) multiplications with limited hardware support, and further compression to lower bit-widths causes severe accuracy degradation, particularly for sub-7B models. This paper tackles the extreme case of binarization to eliminate nearly all multiplications in favor of summations, while avoiding the catastrophic accuracy loss that direct 1-bit quantization incurs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240105/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weight matrices in fp16 precision (optionally pre-quantized to 4-bit via GPTQ or OmniQuant for progressive quantization) and random tokens that seed autoregressive generation of synthetic calibration data. It produces, per linear layer, N binary weight matrices (each element in {0,1} or {-1,1}) paired with fp16 per-channel scaling vectors, such that the layer output is computed as a weighted sum of N masked-selection-and-summation operations plus a single scaling-vector multiplication. The binary matrices are frozen after the iterative optimization phase; only the scaling vectors are updated during the final data-free calibration.

## Method

QBB decomposes each weight matrix W into a linear combination of N binary bases (W ≈ Σ αᵢBᵢ) through a three-stage pipeline. First, an analytical cascaded initialization sets B₁ = sign(W) and each subsequent Bᵢ = sign(Δᵢ) where Δᵢ is the residual after the previous bases, with scaling vectors computed via the ℓ₁ column norm. Second, a block-coordinate-descent procedure with a straight-through estimator iteratively optimizes one binary matrix at a time (all scaling vectors updated jointly) to minimize the ℓ₂ reconstruction error, optionally starting from 4-bit quantized weights for progressive quantization. Third, a data-free holistic calibration fine-tunes only the scaling vectors using a student-teacher distillation loss (MSE on logits plus per-block feature MSE), where the teacher generates synthetic sequences from random tokens, a progressive teacher-student block swap provides a curriculum, and a top-K filtering step retains only the most discrepant sequences for sample-efficient training.

## Results

On WikiText2 perplexity, QBB (W(4×1)A16g128) achieves 5.21 on LLaMA-2 7B, 4.63 on LLaMA-2 13B, and 3.20 on LLaMA-2 70B, outperforming the best 4-bit baseline (OmniQuant) by 0.37, 0.32, and 0.20 respectively. On the original LLaMA family, QBB reaches 5.69 (7B), 5.14 (13B), 4.19 (30B), and 3.64 (70B), matching or beating OmniQuant across all sizes. The method uses only 4 binary matrices (4×1-bit) per weight with group size 128, effectively removing all multiplications except the final scaling-vector multiply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QBB outperforms all four 4-bit baselines (GPTQ, AWQ, OmniQuant, QuIP#) on LLaMA-2 7B (5.21 vs. best 5.58), LLaMA-2 13B (4.63 vs. 4.95), and LLaMA-2 70B (3.20 vs. 3.40), with margins of 0.37, 0.32, and 0.20 perplexity respectively. On LLaMA 7B and 13B, QBB also leads (5.69 vs. 5.77; 5.14 vs. 5.17), while on LLaMA 30B it ties OmniQuant at 4.19. The only case where QBB trails is LLaMA 70B (3.64 vs. 3.62 for AWQ/OmniQuant), a negligible 0.02 gap. Notably, QBB achieves these results using 4 binary matrices (effectively 4-bit storage) while eliminating all multiplications in the linear layer, whereas the baselines retain mixed-precision 16b×4b multiply-accumulate operations.

## Relation to Prior Work

QBB sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: (1) the analytical binarization framework from XNOR-Net, which provides the closed-form scaling vector formula, and (2) the data-free calibration paradigm from LLM-QAT, which the paper adapts with a student-teacher distillation loop and a data-filtering heuristic. It departs from prior PTQ methods (GPTQ, AWQ, OmniQuant, QuIP#) that operate in the 3–4-bit regime with mixed-precision multiplications, and from prior LLM binarization work (BitNet, PB-LLM, OneBit) that requires full retraining or fine-tuning on labeled corpora. The key conceptual departure is representing weights as a sum of N binary matrices with per-channel scaling vectors, optimized via block-coordinate descent with a straight-through estimator, entirely post-training and without any external dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing inference to (nearly) pure summations, QBB opens a path to substantially lower energy consumption and smaller die sizes on custom hardware, which is increasingly relevant as AI energy footprints are projected to reach 85–135 TWh by 2027. However, the paper explicitly acknowledges that all efficiency gains are theoretical—no custom hardware implementation was constructed—and the method's practical advantage over well-supported 4-bit kernels remains unproven. Open questions include whether the summation-only advantage persists at scale (70B+), how the approach interacts with activation quantization, and whether the N=4 binary-base representation can be further compressed (e.g., to log₂(N+1) bits) without accuracy loss.
