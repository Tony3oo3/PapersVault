## Problem

Generating comprehensive literature surveys (tens of thousands of tokens) with LLMs is hindered by three coupled challenges: context window limits prevent ingesting hundreds of papers and producing long outputs in one pass, parametric knowledge causes hallucinated or outdated citations, and no reliable automated evaluation benchmark exists for survey quality. AutoSurvey addresses all three simultaneously through a structured multi-phase pipeline and a multi-LLM-as-judge evaluation framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243654/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-text survey topic and a pre-built embedding index of 530K arXiv computer-science papers (title + abstract embedded with nomic-embed-text-v1.5) as input. It produces a structured survey document of configurable length (8k–64k tokens) organized into 8 predetermined sections with subsections, where every factual claim carries a citation in [paper_title] format that is post-hoc mapped to a concrete arXiv entry. The output is scored on citation recall/precision (via an NLI model) and three content-quality dimensions (coverage, structure, relevance) on a 5-point rubric.

## Method

AutoSurvey is a four-phase pipeline that decomposes long-form survey writing into parallelizable sub-tasks to bypass context-window limits. Phase 1 retrieves 1,200 topic-relevant papers, splits them into 30K-token chunks, generates a candidate outline per chunk in parallel, and merges them into a single 8-section outline with per-section descriptions. Phase 2 drafts each section concurrently using Claude-3-Haiku, where each subsection retrieves its own 60 papers (up to 1,500 tokens of body text each) via the subsection description, and the model is instructed to cite paper titles inline. Phase 3 refines each section for local coherence (seeing adjacent sections) and corrects citations, then merges all sections into one document. Phase 4 runs a Multi-LLM-as-Judge evaluation (GPT-4, Claude-3-Haiku, Gemini-1.5-Pro) that scores N=2 candidate surveys on NLI-based citation recall/precision and a 5-point content rubric, selecting the highest-scoring document. The key design insight is hierarchical RAG at two granularities—topic-level for the global outline and subsection-level for focused drafting—combined with parallel generation and a dedicated reflection pass to maintain citation fidelity across the full document.

## Results

At 64k tokens, AutoSurvey achieves 82.25% citation recall and 77.41% precision, approaching human performance (86.33% recall, 77.78% precision) while substantially outperforming Naive RAG (68.79% recall, 61.97% precision). On content quality at 64k, AutoSurvey scores 4.73 coverage, 4.33 structure, and 4.86 relevance (avg 4.62), closely matching human scores (5.00, 4.66, 5.00; avg 4.88). In terms of speed, AutoSurvey generates 73.59 surveys per hour at 64k tokens, compared to 12.56 for Naive RAG and 0.07 for human writing. Citation quality remains stable across all survey lengths (8k–64k) for AutoSurvey, whereas Naive RAG degrades significantly (recall drops from 78.14% at 8k to 68.79% at 64k).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoSurvey is compared against four baselines: human-authored surveys from arXiv, Naive RAG (iterative prompting with the same reference papers), Naive RAG+Reflection, and Naive RAG+Query Rewriting. AutoSurvey decisively outperforms all RAG-based baselines on citation quality at every length; at 64k tokens it achieves 82.25% recall vs. 68.79% (Naive RAG), 73.12% (RAG+Reflection), and 69.77% (RAG+Query Rewriting). On structure, AutoSurvey's advantage over Naive RAG is most pronounced at longer lengths (4.33 vs. 3.66 at 64k), attributable to its outline-guided hierarchical generation. Against human writing, AutoSurvey trails slightly on citation recall (82.25% vs. 86.33%) and structure (4.33 vs. 4.66 at 64k), but matches or exceeds humans on speed by a factor of over 1000×. The ablation further shows that removing the retrieval mechanism drops recall to 60.11% and precision to 51.65%, confirming that RAG is the critical differentiator over parametric-knowledge-only generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoSurvey extends the RAG-based text generation paradigm (Lewis et al., Jiang et al.) and the LLM-as-Judge evaluation framework (Zheng et al., NeurIPS 2024) to the specific domain of long-form academic survey writing. It departs from prior automatic writing systems (STORM, PaperRobot, IRP) that targeted shorter texts (<4k tokens) by introducing a hierarchical outline-then-parallel-drafting architecture designed for 64k-token outputs, and from prior long-form generation methods (RecurrentGPT, Temp-Lora) by grounding content in retrieved papers rather than relying on parametric memory. The NLI-based citation recall/precision metrics are directly adopted from Gao et al. (EMNLP 2023), while the multi-LLM-as-judge evaluation extends single-model LLM-as-Judge to an ensemble calibrated against human expert rankings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AutoSurvey provides the first end-to-end system for generating comprehensive 64k-token academic surveys at a cost of ~$1.2 and 3 minutes, addressing a genuine bottleneck in fast-moving fields where literature outpaces human review capacity. The multi-LLM-as-judge evaluation methodology (Spearman's rho 0.5429 with human experts) offers a scalable proxy for quality assessment that could transfer to other long-form generation tasks. However, the dominant error type—overgeneralization (51%)—reveals that LLMs still lean on parametric knowledge to extend beyond what retrieved sources support, and structure scores remain below human levels, indicating that coherent long-range organization is not yet solved. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond LLM-centric topics and whether the evaluation rubric transfers to non-survey academic writing.
