## Problem

Diffusion-based offline RL methods like DQL achieve strong performance through expressive multimodal policies, but their reliance on iterative denoising sampling makes both training and inference computationally expensive. Existing acceleration strategies, such as KL-divergence-based score distillation (SRPO), reduce sampling cost but degrade policy quality because they encourage multi-modal coverage rather than the single high-reward mode that RL typically requires. The paper asks whether one can retain the expressiveness of a diffusion policy while eliminating all iterative denoising, without sacrificing performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state vectors from offline D4RL datasets (state-action-reward-next-state tuples) and produces action vectors constrained to [-1, 1] via a tanh activation. The one-step policy is a Gaussian that outputs both a mean and variance per state; at inference, 1024 candidate actions are sampled and one is selected via a stochastic max-Q trick (sampling proportionally to exp(Q(a,s))). The diffusion policy is a 4-layer MLP conditioned on the state and a sinusoidal timestep embedding, operating in a continuous-time EDM schedule with α_t=1 and σ_t=t.

## Method

DTQL employs a dual-policy architecture: a diffusion policy μ_φ trained purely for behavior cloning (standard denoising loss) and a one-step Gaussian policy π_θ for actual deployment. The key contribution is the diffusion trust region loss L_TR, which penalizes the one-step policy's generated actions by measuring how well they survive a forward-noise-then-denoise round-trip through the frozen diffusion model. Theorem 1 proves that minimizing L_TR is equivalent to maximizing the ELBO lower bound of the data distribution's mode, giving it a mode-seeking character that contrasts with KL-divergence distillation's multi-modal coverage. The one-step policy is jointly optimized with L_TR (weighted by α) and double Q-value maximization, while the Q-function is trained via IQL. Crucially, the gradient of L_TR includes a Jacobian term ∂ε_φ/∂z_t absent from SDS/SRPO, which is what enforces mode-seeking rather than diversity. No iterative denoising is performed at any point during training or inference, yielding ~10× faster inference and ~5× faster total training compared to DQL/IDQL.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark, DTQL achieves a Gym average normalized score of 88.7, Antmaze average of 73.6, Adroit average of 72.7, and Kitchen average of 71.8. On Gym tasks, DTQL reaches 99.6 on hopper-medium-v2 (vs. 95.5 for SRPO and 90.5 for DQL) and 89.4 on walker2d-medium-v2. On Adroit, DTQL scores 81.3 on pen-cloned-v1, substantially outperforming DQL (57.3) and IQL (37.3). In terms of efficiency, DTQL requires only 500 training epochs (vs. 1000 for DQL/SRPO and 3000 for IDQL) and achieves 10× faster inference than DQL/IDQL by eliminating iterative denoising.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTQL outperforms DQL (Wang et al., 2022) on all four domain averages (Gym 88.7 vs. 88.0, Antmaze 73.6 vs. 69.6, Adroit 72.7 vs. 65.1, Kitchen 71.8 vs. 69.0) while being 10× faster at inference. Against IDQL (Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023), DTQL wins on Gym (88.7 vs. 82.1), Adroit (72.7 vs. N/A), and Kitchen (71.8 vs. N/A), but trails on Antmaze (73.6 vs. 79.1), where IDQL's 5-step denoising with 32–128 candidate actions provides an advantage on navigation tasks. DTQL substantially outperforms SRPO (Chen et al., 2023) on Antmaze (73.6 vs. 30.1) and slightly on Gym (88.7 vs. 87.1), with the gap attributed to SRPO's use of SDS-based KL distillation rather than mode-seeking trust region regularization. Against conventional baselines, DTQL dominates: IQL (77.0 Gym, 63.0 Antmaze), CQL (77.6 Gym, 50.6 Antmaze), TD3+BC (75.3 Gym, 27.3 Antmaze), BCQ (56.5 Adroit, 11.7 Kitchen), and BEAR (12.8 Adroit, 20.1 Kitchen) are all clearly surpassed. The one notable exception is kitchen-complete-v0, where DQL (84.0) edges out DTQL (80.8), and on most Antmaze sub-tasks where IDQL's multi-candidate sampling strategy proves more effective for long-horizon navigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DQL (Wang et al., 2022a), which established diffusion models as expressive policy classes for offline RL, and on IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2021) for the Q-function training. Its key conceptual departure is from the KL-divergence-based distillation lineage (SDS, DMD, VSD, and their RL adaptation SRPO), which aims to match the full distribution of a diffusion model to a one-step generator. Instead, DTQL introduces a diffusion trust region loss that is provably mode-seeking (Theorem 1), creating a safe in-sample region within which a one-step policy can explore freely toward high-reward actions, rather than attempting to cover all modalities of the behavior distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating iterative denoising from both training and inference, DTQL achieves a 10× inference speedup and ~5× total training time reduction over DQL and IDQL while matching or exceeding their performance across D4RL benchmarks. The mode-seeking vs. mode-covering distinction is conceptually important: it clarifies why the standard distillation objective (KL matching) is fundamentally misaligned with RL's goal of finding a single optimal action per state, a point supported by both 2D toy experiments and full benchmark results. Open questions include extending the approach to online settings, image-based observations, and whether the one-step policy architecture (currently a simple Gaussian MLP) can be further improved.
