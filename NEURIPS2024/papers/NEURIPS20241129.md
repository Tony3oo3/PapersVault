## Problem

Existing histopathology image-text datasets provide only coarse, slide-level annotations (e.g., 'cancer' vs. 'healthy') that fail to capture the molecular heterogeneity within sub-tile regions of a tissue slide. While spatial transcriptomics (ST) technologies can measure 15,000–30,000 gene expression values at individual spatial spots, no large-scale dataset pairs these high-dimensional transcriptomic profiles with their corresponding histopathology sub-tile images, preventing effective training of multi-modal models that integrate visual and gene expression information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241129/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 4,293,195 pairs of RGB sub-tile images (cropped at spot-level resolution: 100 μm diameter for Spatial Transcriptomics, 55 μm for Visium, 8 μm bins for VisiumHD) with their corresponding 15,000–30,000 dimensional gene expression vectors, along with spot center coordinates and radius. For the empirical studies, gene expression is reduced to 128 highly variable genes (per-slide HVG) or 100 overlap-HVGs across slides before being fed into a single fully connected layer. Outputs from the fine-tuned models include 32-dimensional image embeddings for clustering/linear probing, predicted gene expression values (e.g., ERBB2), and cell segmentation masks from CellViT evaluated against RCTD-inferred cell type proportions.

## Method

The primary contribution is the curation of STimage-1K4M: 1,149 ST slides from Spatial Transcriptomics, Visium, and VisiumHD platforms, manually processed to ensure accurate coordinate mapping between gene expression spots and histopathology images, spanning 10 species and 50 tissue types. For empirical validation, the authors fine-tune the image encoders of pre-trained CLIP (ViT-B/32) and PLIP (ViT-L/14) via contrastive learning, replacing the original text encoder with a single fully connected gene expression encoder that compresses the high-dimensional input to a 32-dimensional embedding, while the image encoder is similarly projected to 32 dimensions. The contrastive objective maximizes cosine similarity between aligned image–gene expression pairs and minimizes it for unaligned pairs, trained for 15 epochs with batch size 1024 on a single A100 GPU. To manage cross-dataset gene incompatibility and batch effects, analyses are restricted to 12 human DLPFC slides (47,681 spots) from Maynard et al. (2021), with HVG selection performed either per-slide or on overlapping genes. Fine-tuned models outperform zero-shot CLIP and PLIP on linear probing (macro F1) and clustering quality metrics (Silhouette, Calinski-Harabasz, Davies-Bouldin), though they remain inferior to the larger UNI model.

## Results

For gene expression prediction of ERBB2 in HER2+ breast cancer, zero-shot PLIP achieved the highest Pearson correlation and lowest MSE among CLIP, PLIP, and UNI image embeddings. In linear probing on 12 human DLPFC slides (47,681 spots), fine-tuned CLIP and PLIP with highly variable genes (HVG) achieved higher mean macro F1 scores than their zero-shot counterparts, confirming that contrastive fine-tuning on STimage-1K4M improves pathology image representations. For cell segmentation, CellViT-256 achieved a Pearson correlation of 0.37 with RCTD-inferred cancer epithelial proportions, slightly outperforming CellViT-SAM (0.34). Fine-tuned embeddings also yielded improved Silhouette, Calinski-Harabasz, and Davies-Bouldin clustering scores, with t-SNE visualizations showing clearer separation of white matter from cortical layers L1–L6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks three pre-trained vision models (CLIP ViT-B/32, PLIP ViT-L/14, and UNI) in zero-shot mode for ERBB2 gene expression prediction, where PLIP outperforms both CLIP and UNI in correlation and MSE. After contrastive fine-tuning on STimage-1K4M (replacing the text encoder with a 32-dim fully connected gene expression encoder), both CLIP and PLIP improve their linear probing macro F1 over their respective zero-shot versions, though the fine-tuned models remain inferior to the larger zero-shot UNI model (which was not fine-tuned due to compute constraints). For cell segmentation, CellViT-256 marginally outperforms CellViT-SAM (Pearson r = 0.37 vs. 0.34) against RCTD-inferred cell type proportions as ground truth. The paper also notes that fine-tuning on data from other tissue types (beyond the Maynard et al. 2021 human brain cohort) yields sub-optimal performance, highlighting batch effects as a key limitation across the 1,149-slide dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP/PLIP contrastive learning paradigm from image-text pairs to image-gene expression pairs by replacing the text encoder with a fully connected gene expression encoder, adapting the framework to spatial transcriptomics data. It carves new ground at the intersection of two previously disconnected data ecosystems: vision-language histopathology datasets (OpenPath, Quilt-1M) that provide textual annotations, and spatial omics databases (SpatialDB, STOmicsDB, SOAR) that provide gene expression without paired images. The core contribution is the STimage-1K4M dataset itself, which bridges this gap by providing 4.3M sub-tile image-gene expression pairs from three sequencing-based ST platforms (Spatial Transcriptomics, Visium, VisiumHD), enabling the contrastive framework to be applied at a scale and granularity not previously available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STimage-1K4M addresses a critical bottleneck in computational pathology: the lack of large-scale paired image-molecular data for training multi-modal models. The dataset's scale (1,149 slides, 10 species, 50 tissue types) and inclusion of pathologist annotations for 71 slides make it a foundational resource for benchmarking spatial clustering, deconvolution, and gene expression prediction. However, significant open questions remain: batch effects across datasets severely limit cross-dataset generalization (t-SNE clusters by source rather than biology), the 128-HVG-to-32-dim compression is acknowledged as overly simplistic, and the fine-tuned models still underperform the larger UNI model. The paper raises important architectural questions about whether gene expression should be treated as additional image channels (~20,000), as positional encoding, or through other mechanisms, leaving the optimal multi-modal integration strategy unresolved.
