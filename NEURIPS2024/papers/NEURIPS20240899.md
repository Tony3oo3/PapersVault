## Problem

Autoregressive image models significantly underperform iterative models (LDMs, MIMs) despite sharing the same VQGAN latent space, a discrepancy absent in NLP where GPT dominates. The paper identifies that the reconstructive autoencoder latent space is unstable—small pixel perturbations cause large token changes—causing error accumulation in autoregressive decoding that iterative methods can correct but AR models cannot. This raises the question of whether reconstruction-optimal latent spaces are truly optimal for generative modeling.

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 RGB images from ImageNet and optionally class labels for conditional generation. Images are first encoded by a frozen DINOv2 SSL model into 16×16 patch features, which are then discretized via K-Means into a sequence of 256 tokens (vocabulary size 8192 for base, 16000 for large). The autoregressive model outputs this token sequence in raster order, which is then decoded into pixels by a separate VQGAN or MaskGIT decoder; for understanding tasks, the frozen transformer features are used directly for linear probing.

## Method

The core contribution is a discriminative image tokenizer that stabilizes the latent space by disentangling encoder and decoder training. Instead of jointly training a VQGAN-style autoencoder, the encoder is a pre-trained DINOv2 discriminative SSL model whose patch-level features are discretized via K-Means clustering (8192 or 16000 clusters) to form the codebook, while a separate VQGAN or MaskGIT decoder is trained to render pixels from these tokens. The autoregressive model is a GPT-2-style causal Transformer (219M or 732M parameters) trained with standard next-token prediction on the flattened token sequence. The theoretical analysis shows that reconstructive autoencoders optimize only the decoder distance D^H, whereas the optimal latent space must also minimize the generative-model distance D^G; discriminative SSL features, analogous to LDA, produce more separable and noise-robust representations than PCA-like autoencoder features, reducing token flip rates under perturbation by 30–40%.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet 256×256 generation, DiGIT (732M) achieves FID 3.39 and IS 205.96, outperforming LDM-4 (FID 10.56), DiT-XL/2 (FID 9.62), and GIVT-1.67B (FID 3.46) for the first time with a GPT-style autoregressive model. On class-unconditional generation, DiGIT (732M) reaches FID 4.59 and IS 141.29, surpassing MAGE-463M (FID 9.10) and GIVT-1.67B (FID 11.02). For image understanding via linear probing, DiGIT (219M) achieves 71.7% top-1 accuracy, and scaling to 732M reaches 80.3%, surpassing iGPT-XL (68.7%) and AIM (70.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiGIT (732M, FID 3.39) outperforms all diffusion-based baselines including LDM-4 (10.56), ADM (10.94), DiT-XL/2 (9.62), and even L-DiT-7B (6.09), establishing the first GPT-style AR model to beat LDMs on class-conditional generation. Against other AR models, DiGIT surpasses GIVT-1.67B (3.46) with fewer parameters, RQ-Trans-3.8B (7.55), and ViTVQGAN-1.7B (5.3), while VAR-600M with classifier-free guidance (2.95†) remains slightly ahead but uses a different training protocol. On class-unconditional generation, DiGIT (732M, FID 4.59) beats MAGE-463M (9.10), GIVT-1.67B (11.02), and VQGAN (24.38). For image understanding, DiGIT (219M, 71.7%) outperforms iGPT-L (60.3%), iGPT-XL (68.7%), VIM+ViT-VQGAN (65.1%), GIVT (65.1%), and AIM (70.5%), with the 732M model reaching 80.3%. Among SSL tokenizer comparisons, the discriminative DINOv2-based tokenizer (FID 16.81 for iBOT, 20.38 for MoCo, 45.51 for MAE) clearly outperforms reconstructive MAE and global-level MoCo, confirming that discriminative patch-level objectives are critical for latent space stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in latent-based image generation (LDM, MaskGIT, VQGAN-AR) where a reconstructive autoencoder (VAE/VQGAN) defines the latent space, arguing instead that latent space *stability*—not reconstruction fidelity—is the critical property for autoregressive models. It builds on DINOv2's discriminative self-supervised features as the encoder backbone and borrows the K-Means discretization idea from VQGAN, but applies it to SSL features rather than autoencoder latents. The theoretical framing unifies the autoencoder objective (minimizing D^H) and the generative-model objective (minimizing D^G) into a single latent-space design criterion, positioning the discriminative SSL encoder as optimizing the latter while a separate decoder handles the former.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a GPT-style autoregressive image model can outperform LDMs and exhibit GPT-like scaling behavior, which reopens the question of whether next-token prediction is a viable primary paradigm for vision. The practical recipe (DINOv2 features + K-Means + causal Transformer) is simple enough to be widely adopted, and the stability argument generalizes beyond autoregressive models to any setting where error accumulation is a concern. Key limitations include the need for a separate pixel decoder (VQGAN or MaskGIT) since discriminative tokens cannot directly render RGB, and all experiments are confined to ImageNet-1K at 256×256. Open questions include whether direct pixel generation from discriminative tokens is feasible, whether the approach transfers to video or 3D, and whether the stability advantage persists at higher resolutions or with larger vocabularies.
