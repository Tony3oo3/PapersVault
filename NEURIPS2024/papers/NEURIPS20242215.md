## Problem

Protein language models are typically trained by scaling model parameters on a fixed dataset until performance plateaus, without principled guidance on how to optimally balance model size against training tokens under a given compute budget. Unlike natural language, protein sequences form a distinct modality with a 20-amino-acid vocabulary, minimal redundancy, and non-smooth semantics, making it unclear whether existing LLM scaling laws transfer. This paper asks: what are the compute-optimal scaling behaviors for both Masked Language Models (MLM) and Causal Language Models (CLM) on protein sequences, and how should compute be allocated when training both objectives?

## Input / Output

The model consumes protein amino acid sequences (20-token vocabulary) at a fixed length of 1024, with sequences concatenated via an EOS delimiter; for MLM, 15% of tokens are masked using an 80-10-10 strategy. Beyond standard next-token or masked-token predictions, the primary novel output is a set of closed-form scaling-law coefficients (power-law exponents and constants) that, given a FLOP budget C, predict the optimal model size N(C) and training token count D(C) for each objective, as well as the Effectively Transferred Tokens D_t that quantify how much CLM pre-training compute is needed before switching to MLM fine-tuning.

## Method

The authors construct UniMeta200B (939M unique sequences, 194B tokens) by combining Uniref50/S, Uniref90/50, and metagenomic sources from ColabFoldDB (BFD, MGnify, SMAG, MetaEuk, TOPAZ, MGV, GPD) with 0.3-similarity deduplication, eliminating the overfitting and diminishing-returns effects observed when repeating Uniref. They then train over 300 Transformer models (3.5M–10.7B parameters) across 7 IsoFLOPs levels (10^18–10^21 FLOPs) for both CLM and MLM objectives, fitting distinct power-law scaling relations: N(C)=A·C^α and D(C)=B·C^β, where MLM has a steeper model-size exponent (α=0.776) but a much flatter data exponent (β=0.230) compared to CLM (α=0.578, β=0.422). A transfer scaling law is derived showing that pre-training CLM before MLM yields a compute saving of C_t ∝ C_s^0.89, with the optimal CLM pre-training budget corresponding to 10–20% of total compute (quantified via Effectively Transferred Tokens D_t = k·D_f^(-δ)·N^(-γ)). Validation is performed by re-allocating the FLOPs of PROGEN2-xlarge (6.4B) and ESM-2 (3B) into a 7.2B CLM and a 10.7B MLM respectively, which outperform the originals on protein generation (pLDDT, TM-score, diversity) and understanding tasks (contact prediction, fold classification, fluorescence) under equal or lower pre-training compute.

## Results

On protein understanding tasks (Table 6), the 10.7B MLM model outperforms ESM-2 (3B) on 7 of 8 evaluated tasks, achieving 0.72 fold classification accuracy (vs. 0.69), 0.69 fluorescence regression (vs. 0.65), and 0.91 contact prediction (tied), all under equivalent pre-training FLOPs (~1.68×10²²). On protein generation, the 7.2B CLM surpasses PROGEN2-xlarge (6.4B) with a mean pLDDT of 78.69 (vs. 74.33), higher Foldseek TM-scores (0.655 vs. 0.522), and greater sequence diversity (7,097 vs. 4,818 clusters at 50% identity). The transfer scaling strategy (470M CLM→MLM) also improves over 470M scratch training on contact prediction (0.80 vs. 0.78) and fold classification (0.66 vs. 0.65).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ESM-2 (3B) [Lin et al., Science 2023], the paper's 10.7B MLM model wins on 6 of 8 downstream tasks (fold classification +3 pts, fluorescence +4 pts, fitness prediction +2 pts, metal ion binding +1 pt, solubility +5 pts, stability +1 pt), ties on contact prediction (0.91), and loses slightly on sub-cellular localization (0.79 vs. 0.81), all under the same ~1.68×10²² FLOP budget. Against PROGEN2-xlarge (6.4B) [Nijkamp et al., Cell Systems 2023], the 7.2B CLM model dominates on every protein generation metric: lower OOD perplexity across all MaxID thresholds, higher mean pLDDT (78.69 vs. 74.33), better structural similarity to PDB (TM-score 0.655 vs. 0.522, SeqID 0.194 vs. 0.165), and substantially greater diversity (7,097 vs. 4,818 MMseqs2 clusters at 50% identity), despite using fewer training tokens (265B vs. 350B) and fewer FLOPs (1.14×10²² vs. 1.34×10²²). The transfer scaling result (470M CLM→MLM vs. 470M scratch) confirms that allocating ~20% of compute to CLM pre-training before MLM fine-tuning yields consistent but modest gains on structure-related tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chinchilla-style compute-optimal scaling framework (Hoffmann et al., 2022) from natural language to protein sequences, adapting the IsoFLOPs profiling methodology to account for the distinct statistical properties of amino acid data (20-token vocabulary, low redundancy). It builds on the transfer scaling methodology of Hernandez et al. (2021) and Zhang et al. (2024) to formalize an 'Effectively Transferred Tokens' concept for CLM-to-MLM knowledge transfer in protein models. The work departs from prior protein LM efforts (ESM-2, PROGEN2) that primarily scaled model size on fixed, repeatedly-used datasets, instead introducing a 194B-token metagenomic dataset (UniMeta200B) to enable single-epoch training and revealing that MLM and CLM follow fundamentally different power-law exponents in this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic derivation of scaling laws for protein language models covering both MLM and CLM objectives, providing practitioners with concrete formulas to allocate compute between model size and data. The finding that a 10.7B model trained on 260B tokens outperforms ESM-2's 3B model trained on 1T tokens at equal FLOPs directly challenges the prevailing practice of training smaller models over many epochs on limited data. Key limitations include the single-epoch assumption (multi-epoch MLM scaling remains uncharacterized), the absence of MSA-based augmentation, and the restriction to a specific dataset composition. Open questions include whether these laws generalize to other biological modalities (RNA, DNA), long-sequence architectures, and whether the CLM→MLM transfer advantage persists at the 10B+ scale where the token allocation ratio crosses 1:1.
