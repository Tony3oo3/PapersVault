## Problem

Diffusion policies have shown strong expressiveness and multimodality in offline RL and imitation learning, but their integration into online RL remains underexplored. The standard variational lower bound (VLO) training objective of diffusion models cannot be directly applied in online RL because high-quality action samples are unavailable, and existing online diffusion RL methods (DIPO, QSM) suffer from limited exploration or doubled approximation errors that prevent convergence to optimality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a state vector from MuJoCo continuous control environments (Hopper, Walker2d, Ant, HalfCheetah, Humanoid) and outputs a continuous action vector. During training, the diffusion policy generates N_d=64 action samples per state, from which the best sample is selected by Q-value for policy optimization; additionally N_e=10 samples from a uniform distribution are used for the entropy regularization term. At inference, the efficient behavior policy selects the best action among K=32 diffusion samples rather than using deterministic denoising, yielding a 6.2 ms inference time.

## Method

QVPO's core contribution is the Q-weighted VLO loss, which multiplies the standard DDPM denoising objective by the Q-value of each state-action pair and is theoretically proven to be a tight lower bound of the online RL policy objective (Theorem 1). To handle negative Q-values and the scarcity of high-Q samples, the paper introduces equivalent Q-weight transformation functions—specifically the 'qadv' function that uses the positive part of the advantage A(s,a)—and leverages the diffusion model's generative capacity to synthesize high-quality training samples. A nontrivial entropy regularization term is added by training the diffusion policy on uniform-distribution samples with a state-dependent weight, compensating for the inaccessibility of the diffusion policy's log-likelihood. Finally, an efficient behavior policy via K-best action selection (K_b=4 for behavior, K_t<K_b for target) reduces the large variance inherent in diffusion sampling, improving sample efficiency during online interaction.

## Results

QVPO achieves the highest episodic reward across all five MuJoCo locomotion benchmarks after 1M training steps: 3728.5 on Hopper-v3, 5191.8 on Walker2d-v3, 6425.1 on Ant-v3, 11385.6 on HalfCheetah-v3, and 5306.6 on Humanoid-v3. The method also exhibits the lowest variance among all compared algorithms on most tasks (e.g., std of 13.8 on Hopper-v3 and 14.5 on Humanoid-v3). Learning curves (Figure 3) further show that QVPO converges substantially faster than all baselines, confirming superior sample efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QVPO outperforms all six baselines on every MuJoCo locomotion task. Against the strongest traditional off-policy method SAC, QVPO gains +730.4 on HalfCheetah-v3 (11385.6 vs 10616.9) and +146.9 on Humanoid-v3 (5306.6 vs 5159.7), while also reducing variance by roughly 5×. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor DIPO, QVPO leads by +432.6 on Hopper-v3, +510.1 on Walker2d-v3, +759.2 on Ant-v3, +1795.1 on HalfCheetah-v3, and +361.1 on Humanoid-v3. QSM, the other diffusion-based online RL method, is notably weaker (e.g., 2154.7 on Hopper-v3) and fails entirely on Ant-v3. On-policy baselines PPO and SPO are consistently the weakest, with SPO particularly unstable (std up to 1443.5 on Walker2d-v3). QVPO also converges faster in wall-clock training time (6.8 h vs 10.5 h for DIPO on Ant-v3) while maintaining a competitive inference latency of 6.2 ms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: the variational lower bound (VLO) training objective of denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPM, Ho et al. 2020) and the policy improvement objective in online RL. While Diffusion-QL (Wang et al. 2022) established diffusion policies as an expressive policy class in offline RL, and DIPO (Yang et al. 2023) and QSM (Psenka et al. 2023) were the first to bring diffusion policies into online RL via gradient-based action updates and score-Q alignment respectively, QVPO departs from both by showing that the VLO loss itself—when Q-weighted—constitutes a tight lower bound of the RL policy objective, eliminating the approximation errors inherent in prior gradient-matching approaches. The method also draws on advantage-weighted regression (AWR) and reward-weighted regression (RWR) for its Q-weight transformation functions, but applies them in an on-policy diffusion training context rather than offline weighted regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QVPO provides the first theoretically grounded (tight lower bound) training objective for diffusion policies in online RL, which is a clean conceptual contribution that could inform future diffusion-based RL methods beyond locomotion. However, the practical impact is currently limited: experiments are confined to five MuJoCo locomotion tasks, inference time (~6 ms) is an order of magnitude slower than classical methods like SAC (~0.3 ms), and the entropy regularization is a fixed-coefficient heuristic rather than an adaptive mechanism. Key open questions include whether the Q-weighted VLO framework extends to high-dimensional or multimodal action spaces (e.g., dexterous manipulation), whether entropy can be integrated into the TD target for soft policy iteration as in SAC, and whether the approach scales to tasks requiring real-time control beyond 50 Hz.
