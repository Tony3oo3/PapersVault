## Problem

Graph Neural Networks achieve high accuracy in semi-supervised node classification but lack reliable, distribution-free uncertainty estimates. Existing conformal prediction methods for GNNs (e.g., DAPS, CF-GNN) only leverage one-hop structural neighbors to smooth non-conformity scores, ignoring the influence of distant nodes that share the same label. This paper shows that aggregating scores from label-similar global nodes can dramatically shrink prediction sets while preserving valid marginal coverage, and proposes a practical mechanism to identify such nodes without ground-truth labels.

## Input / Output

SNAPS is a post-processing layer that consumes the predicted probability vector from any trained GNN (treated as a black box) along with the raw node feature matrix and graph adjacency matrix. It constructs an auxiliary k-NN similarity graph via cosine similarity over node features (sampling M ≪ N candidate nodes for large graphs like OGBN Products with 2.4M nodes) and outputs corrected non-conformity scores that feed into standard split conformal prediction to produce prediction sets. When adapted to image classification, the structural neighborhood term is dropped and only the feature-similarity k-NN aggregation is applied over calibration-set image embeddings.

## Method

SNAPS corrects each node's basic non-conformity score (e.g., APS) by a weighted convex combination of three components: the node's own score (weight 1−λ−μ), the similarity-weighted average of scores from its k nearest feature-similar neighbors (weight λ, normalized by the k-NN degree), and the uniform average of scores from its one-hop graph neighbors (weight μ, normalized by structural degree). The key insight, validated empirically via an oracle experiment, is that nodes sharing the ego node's label have systematically lower APS scores for the true class, so pulling the ego score toward the mean of same-label nodes pushes true-class scores below the conformal quantile while pushing false-class scores above it, shrinking the prediction set. The paper proves that the aggregated scores remain exchangeable (Proposition 1) and, under the assumption that selected neighbors share the ego label, that SNAPS prediction sets are stochastically no larger than those from the base score (Proposition 2). Hyperparameters λ and μ are selected by grid search over [0,1] with λ+μ≤1, and the method is robust to these choices as well as to the k-NN parameter k.

## Results

On 10 graph datasets at α=0.05 with GCN and APS as the base score, SNAPS achieves an average prediction set Size of 3.17 and singleton hit ratio (SH) of 52.31%, while maintaining the target 95% marginal coverage. The most dramatic improvement is on OGBN Products, where SNAPS reduces the average Size from 14.92 (APS) to 7.68. On Coauthor CS, SNAPS achieves a Size of 1.08 and SH of 87.92%, compared to 1.91 and 66.17% for APS. When adapted to ImageNet image classification, SNAPS reduces the average Size from 14.09 (APS) to 4.83 at α=0.1, roughly a 3× reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNAPS consistently outperforms all three conformal prediction baselines (APS, RAPS, DAPS) on both Size and SH across the 10 graph datasets, with average Size reduced from 5.23 (APS) and 3.79 (DAPS) to 3.17, and average SH increased from 40.22% (APS) and 48.01% (DAPS) to 52.31%. The largest gains appear on high-cardinality or large-scale datasets: on OGBN Products, SNAPS nearly halves the Size of APS (7.68 vs. 14.92) and improves SH by 6.9 percentage points over DAPS. Against CF-GNN (Huang et al., 2023b), SNAPS achieves substantially smaller prediction sets (e.g., 1.68 vs. 2.60 on CoraML at α=0.05) while running ~200× faster (0.73 s vs. 142 s per trial), since SNAPS is a lightweight post-hoc score correction rather than a retrained topology-aware model. The only dataset where SNAPS does not strictly dominate on SH is CiteSeer, where DAPS edges out SNAPS by 0.67 percentage points (59.75% vs. 59.08%), though SNAPS still wins on Size (1.84 vs. 1.94).

## Relation to Prior Work

SNAPS is explicitly positioned as an enhanced version of DAPS (Zargarbashi et al., 2023), which smooths non-conformity scores using one-hop structural neighbors under the homophily assumption. The key conceptual departure is extending the aggregation from local structural neighbors to a broader set of global nodes identified by feature-space k-NN (cosine similarity), motivated by the empirical observation that same-label nodes have higher feature similarity. The method uses APS (Romano et al., 2020) as its base score function and is compared against CF-GNN (Huang et al., 2023b), which learns a topology-aware correction model rather than performing explicit score aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SNAPS provides a simple, model-agnostic post-processing step that can be plugged into any GNN pipeline to shrink prediction sets while preserving finite-sample coverage guarantees, making it practically attractive for high-stakes node classification. The extension to ImageNet (reducing average set size from ~19.6 to ~4.1) demonstrates that the feature-similarity aggregation principle is not limited to graph structure. However, the method is restricted to transductive settings and high-homophily graphs; the authors acknowledge that inductive learning, heterophilous networks, and more efficient/accurate node selection remain open problems that limit broader deployment.
