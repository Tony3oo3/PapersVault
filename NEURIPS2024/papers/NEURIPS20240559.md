## Problem

Diffusion-based inpainting models, when asked to remove an object, frequently hallucinate the removed object or insert contextually similar objects (e.g., another person on a surfboard, another plane at an airport) instead of filling the masked region with background. Existing solutions to this problem require specialized training datasets, costly manual annotations, or extensive before/after data collection, making them inflexible and hard to deploy across different diffusion backbones.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a binary object segmentation mask (dilated with a 5-pixel kernel to avoid residual object pixels) and produces a full-resolution inpainted image where the masked region is filled with realistic background content. It operates at the native resolution of the underlying diffusion pipeline (512×512 for SD-Inpaint) and is designed as a plug-and-play module that can be attached to any diffusion-based inpainting backbone (SD-Inpaint, Unipaint, Blended Latent Diffusion, SDXL-Inpaint) without retraining the backbone itself.

## Method

CLIPAway exploits AlphaCLIP's alpha-channel mechanism to produce two region-focused image embeddings: one conditioned on the foreground mask (e_f) and one on its inverse (e_b). A small 6-block MLP (768→1024 dimensions, trained on COCO with full-image alpha to match OpenCLIP ViT-H/14 embeddings) projects both into the IP-Adapter's embedding space. The key step is an orthogonal projection that subtracts the foreground component from the background embedding: e_final = e_b − ((e_b·e_f)/‖e_f‖²)·e_f, yielding a vector that is orthogonal to the foreground direction and thus encodes only background semantics. This final embedding is fed through IP-Adapter's decoupled attention into the diffusion UNet, guiding the denoising process to fill the masked region with background-consistent content. The entire pipeline requires no object-removal training data; only the lightweight MLP projection is trained (~7 GPU-hours on a single A40), making the approach a drop-in enhancement for any existing diffusion inpainting model.

## Results

On the COCO 2017 validation set, CLIPAway consistently improves all diffusion-based inpainting backbones. The most dramatic gains appear with SD-Inpaint, where CLIP@1 jumps from 70.45 to 84.82 (+14.37), CLIP@3 from 57.14 to 74.42 (+17.28), and CLIP@5 from 49.88 to 67.76 (+17.88), while FID drops from 59.21 to 57.32. With Unipaint, FID improves by 15.40 (77.58→62.18) and CLIP@1 reaches 88.26, the highest among all evaluated methods. In a user study with 20 participants, CLIPAway was preferred 71.75% of the time over ZITS++ (13.25%), SD-Inpaint (11.25%), and Unipaint (3.75%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GAN-based methods (ZITS++, MAT, LaMa), CLIPAway achieves substantially better FID scores (57–62 vs. 63–68) and higher CLIP removal accuracy, while GAN methods avoid object hallucination but produce blurry, unrealistic backgrounds. Compared to Blended Latent Diffusion, CLIPAway reduces FID by 10.58 and improves CLIP@3/CLIP@5 by ~3.9 points, though CLIP Distance slightly decreases (0.85→0.83). Against Unipaint, CLIPAway delivers the largest FID improvement (−15.40) and boosts CLIP@1 to 88.26, the best score in the table. The most striking comparison is SD-Inpaint, where CLIPAway nearly doubles CLIP@3 (57.14→74.42) and CLIP@5 (49.88→67.76), indicating that the base model frequently hallucinates the removed object while CLIPAway effectively suppresses this. In qualitative comparisons with instruction-based methods InstructPix2Pix and InstInpaint, CLIPAway produces sharper, more accurate removals without requiring specialized training datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLIPAway sits at the intersection of three threads: (1) latent diffusion inpainting (SD-Inpaint, Blended Latent Diffusion, Unipaint), which condition generation on masks and text but lack explicit background-focused guidance; (2) AlphaCLIP's region-aware embedding via an alpha channel, which the authors repurpose to produce separate foreground and background embeddings; and (3) IP-Adapter's decoupled attention mechanism, which the authors use as the injection point for a projected background-only embedding. The key departure from prior work is the orthogonal projection (Eq. 2) that subtracts the foreground component from the background embedding, combined with a lightweight MLP that bridges AlphaCLIP's ViT-L/14 space to OpenCLIP ViT-H/14 — all without any task-specific training data, unlike InstructPix2Pix, InstInpaint, MagicBrush, or ObjectDrop which each require purpose-built datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of CLIPAway makes it immediately applicable to any IP-Adapter-compatible diffusion inpainting pipeline, lowering the barrier for practitioners who need reliable object removal without curating paired datasets. The substantial CLIP-accuracy gains over SD-Inpaint (+14.37 at CLIP@1) and consistent improvements across multiple diffusion backbones and AlphaCLIP variants suggest the orthogonal-projection trick is a broadly useful inductive bias. Open questions include whether the approach handles multi-object scenes with overlapping masks, whether it extends to video inpainting, and whether the resolution sensitivity inherent to latent diffusion models can be mitigated without degrading the embedding alignment.
