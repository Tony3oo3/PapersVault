## Problem

Existing gaze following methods predict only the 2D pixel location of a gaze target, which is insufficient for real-world applications that require understanding *what* a person is looking at (e.g., object class or social gaze category). No prior work jointly predicts both the localization and the semantic label of the gaze target in an end-to-end manner, and post-hoc object detection is inefficient, misses uncountable targets, and struggles with multimodal heatmaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243857/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB scene image (256×256), a head crop (224×224), and 4D head bounding box coordinates as input, and produces a 64×64 gaze heatmap plus a 512-dimensional gaze label embedding that is matched against a frozen CLIP text encoder's vocabulary embeddings at inference. The scene is encoded once in a person-agnostic manner, while person-specific gaze tokens are decoded separately, enabling efficient multi-person inference. The label vocabulary is open-ended and can be swapped at inference time without retraining.

## Method

The architecture follows a promptable gaze following design inspired by SAM: a ViT-base image encoder (MultiMAE-pretrained) processes the scene once, while a ResNet-18 gaze encoder (Gaze360-pretrained) processes the head crop and projects bbox coordinates into a position-sensitive gaze token. A lightweight 2-block transformer decoder performs bidirectional cross-attention (gaze→image and image→gaze) to align gaze tokens with relevant image regions, with a learnable 'not gaze' token that lets background image tokens avoid being pulled toward gaze-relevant features. The heatmap is produced by a dot-product between projected gaze tokens and upscaled image tokens, while the label is predicted by a 6-layer MLP on gaze tokens, trained with an asymmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss against frozen CLIP text embeddings of pseudo-labels derived from open-world segmentation (RAM-Grounded-SAM + Semantic-Segment-Anything). This late-fusion design reduces parameter count by ~42% compared to a CLIP-based baseline while setting state-of-the-art localization on GazeFollow.

## Results

On the GazeFollow benchmark, the proposed architecture achieves a new state-of-the-art in gaze target localization with an Avg. Dist of 0.108 and Min. Dist of 0.051, surpassing the previous best (Tafasca et al., 0.113) by 4.4%. For gaze target recognition, the method achieves 44.7% Acc@1 and 64.2% Acc@3, outperforming all frozen baselines by over 20 percentage points while using 40% fewer parameters (116M vs. 200M) than the strongest fine-tuned baseline. On the newly introduced GazeHOI dataset, the model reaches 72.3% Gaze Accuracy and 58.3% Acc@1, with the fine-tuned baselines slightly ahead on recognition (64.6% Acc@1) due to the larger vocabulary mismatch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On localization, the proposed method outperforms all seven prior gaze-following methods on the GazeFollow benchmark, achieving 0.108 Avg. Dist versus 0.113 for the closest competitor (Tafasca et al. [49], a 4.4% improvement) and 0.118 for Jin et al. [27] (8.5% improvement). The gap over earlier methods is larger: Chong et al. [10] (0.137), Fang et al. [16] (0.124), Bao et al. [5] (0.122), Tafasca et al. [48] (0.125), and Jin et al. [26] (0.126) all trail by 0.010–0.029 in Avg. Dist. For recognition, the method beats all frozen baselines by a wide margin (e.g., 44.7% vs. 23.9% Acc@1 over the crop variant) but is slightly below the best fine-tuned baseline (heatmap weight: 46.6% Acc@1) by ~2 points, while using 42% fewer parameters (116M vs. 200M). On GazeHOI, the fine-tuned baselines (64.6% Acc@1) edge out the proposed model (58.3%), which the authors attribute to vocabulary mismatch and the small dataset size relative to CLIP's 400M training instances. The zero-shot transfer from GazeFollow to GazeHOI drops recognition to 30.6% Acc@1, highlighting the sensitivity to label vocabulary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gaze following task (predicting 2D gaze target coordinates) by adding simultaneous semantic class prediction of the gaze target, a dimension absent from all prior gaze following methods (Recasens et al., Tafasca et al., Jin et al., Chong et al.). Architecturally, it departs from the standard two-stream early-fusion design of prior gaze following work by adopting a promptable, late-fusion decoder inspired by SAM, where the scene is encoded once and person-specific gaze tokens query it. The recognition component is framed as a visual-text alignment problem using a frozen CLIP text encoder, drawing on contrastive learning (InfoNCE) rather than the object-detection-based auxiliary task used by the closest prior work (Tonini et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying localization and semantic recognition in a single end-to-end model, this work addresses a practical gap: real-world gaze applications (e.g., neurodevelopmental screening, human-robot interaction) need to know *what* a person is looking at, not just *where*. The new GazeHOI benchmark and pseudo-annotation pipeline provide the community with the first resources for evaluating semantic gaze following. Key open questions remain: the model cannot handle multi-label gaze targets (a person looking at both a knife and a cake), recognition performance is constrained by small dataset scale relative to CLIP's pretraining, and the pseudo-labels introduce noise that limits ceiling performance. The promptable design's efficient multi-person scaling (linear FLOPS growth vs. quadratic in early-fusion methods) is a practical advantage for deployment.
