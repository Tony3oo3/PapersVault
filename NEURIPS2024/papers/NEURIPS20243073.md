## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models produce realistic images but frequently fail on multi-object compositional prompts, generating objects in wrong positions or with incorrect counts. Spatial-aware models like GLIGEN fix compositionality by injecting layout conditions, but this significantly degrades image realism and aesthetic quality. No existing training-free method achieves a simultaneous balance between both properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243073/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and uses GPT-4 via in-context learning to automatically generate a scene layout (bounding boxes in normalized [0,1] coordinates) that pre-binds objects to spatial regions. The output is a compositional RGB image where object positions, counts, and attributes align with the prompt. The framework is plug-and-play: any pre-trained T2I model can be paired with any spatial-aware diffusion model (layout, keypoint, or segmentation-based) without retraining, and the T2I branch can be swapped for a stylized model to produce compositional stylized images.

## Method

RealCompo introduces a dynamic balancer that composes the predicted noise from a fidelity-aware T2I model and a spatial-aware L2I model at every denoising step. At each timestep, the balancer computes softmax-normalized coefficients for each model's noise, producing a pixel-wise weighted sum as the balanced noise. The coefficients are updated via gradient descent on a loss that measures how well each model's cross-attention maps align with the layout's binary masks, effectively rewarding the model whose attention better localizes objects within their designated regions. This update rule is training-free and requires no additional parameters. The framework generalizes to arbitrary spatial-aware conditions (keypoints, segmentation maps) through a transfer function that converts region-based conditions into binary masks, making the same balancer loss applicable across ControlNet-based variants.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench, RealCompo achieves state-of-the-art scores across all seven compositional tasks, with the largest gains in Color (0.7741 vs. 0.6886 for PixArt-α), Spatial (0.3173 vs. 0.2632 for GLIGEN), and Numeracy (0.6592 vs. 0.5762 for LMD+). On the COCO realism evaluation, RealCompo attains the highest CLIP score (0.334) and aesthetic score (5.742), surpassing the best T2I baseline SDXL (0.322 / 5.531) and the best L2I baseline GLIGEN (0.301 / 4.892). A user study with 39 participants (1,755 votes) confirms RealCompo's superiority in both realism and compositionality over individual T2I and L2I models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RealCompo outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest T2I models (PixArt-α, SDXL, DALL-E 2), RealCompo gains the most on spatial and numeracy tasks (e.g., Spatial 0.3173 vs. 0.2082 for PixArt-α; Numeracy 0.6592 vs. 0.5058), while also leading on realism-sensitive tasks like Color (0.7741 vs. 0.6886) and Texture (0.7427 vs. 0.7044). Against the strongest L2I models (GLIGEN, LMD+), RealCompo dramatically recovers the realism gap: GLIGEN scores only 0.4288 on Color and 0.3904 on Texture, whereas RealCompo reaches 0.7741 and 0.7427, while still exceeding GLIGEN on Spatial (0.3173 vs. 0.2632) and Numeracy (0.6592 vs. 0.4970). On the COCO realism benchmark (Table 2), RealCompo's CLIP score of 0.334 and aesthetic score of 5.742 surpass SDXL (0.322 / 5.531), TokenCompose v2.1 (0.323 / 5.067), and Layout Guidance (0.294 / 4.947), confirming that the dynamic balancer preserves fidelity while adding spatial control. The ablation (Fig. 9) further shows that removing the balancer causes objects to ignore layout constraints, validating that the gains over GLIGEN and LMD+ come specifically from the noise-composition mechanism rather than from the LLM-generated layout alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

RealCompo sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: text-to-image diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, SDXL) that excel at realism but struggle with multi-object composition, and spatial-aware controllable generation (GLIGEN, ControlNet, LMD+) that enforces layout/pose/segmentation constraints at the cost of image fidelity. The key departure from prior model-composition methods (eDiffi, Raphael) is that RealCompo operates entirely training-free, dynamically adjusting the relative influence of each model's predicted noise via a gradient-based update on cross-attention alignment with the layout, rather than requiring joint training or fixed weighting. The LLM-based layout generation follows LMD+ and LayoutGPT, but the dynamic balancer replaces their fixed controllers with an adaptive mechanism that responds to denoising-stage feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of the dynamic balancer makes it immediately applicable to any pair of T2I and spatial-aware models without retraining, lowering the barrier for compositional generation in practice. However, the computational overhead of running two models plus gradient computation at each denoising step limits real-time deployment, and the paper acknowledges this as a key limitation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, whether fixed (non-dynamic) coefficients could capture most of the benefit at lower cost, and how the method scales to very complex scenes with many interacting objects. The broader implication is that model ensembling via noise composition is a viable, general-purpose paradigm for combining complementary diffusion models.
