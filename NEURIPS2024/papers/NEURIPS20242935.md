## Problem

Risk-constrained reinforcement learning (RCRL) methods that use spectral risk measures such as CVaR face a fundamental difficulty: the nonlinearity of risk measures prevents existing policy gradient approaches from guaranteeing convergence to a global optimum. Prior methods (CVaR-CPO, CPPO, WCSAC-Dist, SDPO, SDAC) only ensure local convergence, leaving open whether an RCRL algorithm can provably reach the optimal policy while satisfying risk constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an augmented state vector that concatenates the original state, per-constraint discounted cost accumulators (e_i), and a running discount factor (b_t = γ^t), along with discretized spectrum parameters defining the risk measure and per-constraint thresholds. It outputs a β-conditioned policy network π_θ(a|s̄;β) mapping augmented states to action distributions, plus a learned sampler distribution ξ_φ over the dual variable β that concentrates mass on the optimal β*. The policy network is conditioned on β so that a single set of parameters encodes optimal policies for all dual variable values, and the sampler is implemented as a product of truncated normals with a stick-breaking sampling procedure to enforce the monotonicity constraint on β.

## Method

SRCPO decomposes the RCRL problem into a bilevel optimization using the dual form of spectral risk measures: the inner problem solves a safe RL subproblem with sub-risk measure constraints via a natural policy gradient that leverages novel risk value functions (integrals of g against the return distribution) exhibiting linearity in the performance difference between policies, while the outer problem selects the optimal dual variable β. For the inner problem, risk value functions are estimated using quantile distributional critics (L Dirac deltas trained with quantile regression loss), and the policy update uses a Fisher-information-scaled gradient with a two-branch rule that switches between reward-maximizing and constraint-recovery directions. For the outer problem, rather than directly optimizing the non-convex β, the method learns a sampler distribution ξ_φ over β via a cross-entropy loss whose gradient is a policy-gradient-like update on the sampler parameters, converging to a distribution that deterministically samples the optimal β*. The spectrum function is discretized into M piecewise-constant segments, parameterizing g as a sum of hinge functions with breakpoints β[j], and the approximation error is bounded by C_max·σ(1)/((1−γ)M). Convergence to the global optimum is proven in the tabular setting under the Robbins-Monro learning rate condition.

## Results

SRCPO is evaluated on Safety Gymnasium (point/car goal and button tasks) and legged robot locomotion (quadrupedal and bipedal) under CVaR constraints with α=0.75. Across all tasks, SRCPO achieves the highest reward among all methods that satisfy the cost-rate thresholds, as shown in training curves (Figures 2–3). WCSAC-Dist attains higher raw reward in Safety Gymnasium but its cost rates exceed the specified thresholds, indicating constraint violation. In the locomotion tasks, where initial policies struggle to stabilize balance and are more susceptible to local optima, SRCPO's advantage over all baselines is most pronounced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRCPO outperforms all four constraint-satisfying baselines (CVaR-CPO, CPPO, SDPO, SDAC) on reward in every task while maintaining cost rates below thresholds. WCSAC-Dist achieves the highest raw reward in Safety Gymnasium but violates the cost constraints, which the authors attribute to its state-wise risk estimation being lower than the true risk measure. In the locomotion tasks, SRCPO's lead is largest because the initial policy's difficulty stabilizing robot balance makes the problem more prone to local optima, an issue that SRCPO's convergence guarantee helps overcome. Among the constraint-satisfying methods, SDAC (which uses a Gaussian approximation of cost returns) generally performs closest to SRCPO, while CVaR-CPO and CPPO (which use auxiliary variables for CVaR) and SDPO (distributional critic) trail further behind. The paper also demonstrates that SRCPO generalizes to other spectral risk measures (Pow, Wang) beyond CVaR, with higher risk levels yielding lower cost rates at the expense of reward.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Bauerle and Glauner's bilevel optimization framework for spectral risk measures in MDPs, which established the duality-based decomposition but left the outer problem unsolved and relied on value-based methods. It departs from the dominant RCRL literature (CVaR-CPO, CPPO, WCSAC-Dist, SDPO, SDAC) that only guarantees local convergence, by exploiting the dual form of spectral risk measures to create a bilevel structure where the inner problem admits a policy gradient with global convergence guarantees. The generalized policy update rule also subsumes several existing primal-approach safe RL algorithms (CPO, PCPO, CRPO, P3O) as special cases, unifying them under a single convergence framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first RCRL algorithm to guarantee convergence to a global optimum, resolving a long-standing gap where even well-studied CVaR constraints only had local convergence guarantees. The bilevel decomposition and the 'sampler' distribution for the outer problem provide a principled way to handle the nonlinearity of risk measures that could inform future work on risk-sensitive RL. However, the convergence guarantee is restricted to tabular settings with finite β spaces, and extending these results to function approximation or linear MDPs remains an open problem that limits immediate practical impact. The empirical gains on continuous control tasks, while consistent, are largely expected given the optimality guarantee and do not reveal unexpected behavioral differences.
