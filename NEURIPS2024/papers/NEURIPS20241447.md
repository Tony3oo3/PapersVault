## Problem

Instruction finetuning (IFT) for LLMs still depends on extensive labeled task instances for training, limiting adaptability in real-world settings where such data is scarce. Existing hypernetwork-based methods (e.g., HINT, HyperTuning) generate adapters from instructions but lack explicit supervision over the generated parameters, resulting in a performance gap versus meta-trained baselines. The paper asks whether a model can learn to construct task-specific adapters purely from instructions—mimicking how humans follow guidelines rather than repeated practice—while matching the quality of instance-trained models.

## Input / Output

At inference, TAGI takes a single task instruction (a natural-language description, optionally with two few-shot positive examples) and a source text. The instruction is encoded exactly once by the hypernetwork's encoder and used to generate LoRA Q/V matrices for every attention layer via a lightweight two-layer MLP; the source text is then processed by the vanilla LLM with the generated LoRA inserted, producing the target text. Because the instruction is processed only once per task rather than concatenated to every sample, inference FLOPs drop to ~0.39× that of Tk-Instruct for 100 examples per task.

## Method

TAGI consists of a hypernetwork (encoder + hierarchical cross-attention layers + MLP adapter generator) that converts a task instruction into LoRA parameters, which are inserted into a frozen LLM to form the student model. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) hypernetwork pretraining on C4 where random text segments serve as pseudo-instructions to build general instruction-recognition ability, and (2) finetuning on meta-training tasks under a knowledge-distillation objective. The distillation loss has three components: a standard cross-entropy prediction loss (L_pred), a KL-divergence loss (L_kl) aligning the student's output logits with a teacher model (the LLM plus a LoRA module trained on that task's instances), and an L2/MSE parameter-alignment loss (L_ins) that directly minimizes the distance between the hypernetwork-generated LoRA and the teacher's instance-trained LoRA. The cross-attention mechanism fuses encoded instruction representations into each layer's self-attention output before decoding, enabling dynamic interaction between instruction and input rather than simple concatenation.

## Results

On Super-Natural Instructions (SNI) with T5-LM-XL (3B), TAGI achieves 48.4 ROUGE-L in zero-shot (Def) and 56.3 in few-shot (Def+2Pos), surpassing HINT (47.2/53.2) by ~2 points and HyperTuning (38.9/48.6) by ~8 points, while matching Tk-Instruct (48.0/54.0) with ~2.5× fewer FLOPs. On P3 (T5-LM-XL), TAGI reaches 58.9 average accuracy on MTest11, within 1.1 points of FiD-ICL (60.0) but at only ×0.88 inference time versus FiD-ICL's ×1.9. TAGI also outperforms decoder-only models GPT-2 XL (38.2) and OPT-13B (44.8) by 10+ and 7+ points respectively on SNI zero-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest meta-training baseline Tk-Instruct [37], TAGI matches or slightly exceeds performance (48.4 vs 48.0 zero-shot; 56.3 vs 54.0 few-shot on XL) while using only ×0.39 relative FLOPs versus ×1.0. Among hypernetwork-based methods, TAGI outperforms HINT [13] by 1.2–3.1 points and HyperTuning [27] by 7.5–9.7 points on SNI, and by 0.5–4.6 points on P3, attributed to the knowledge distillation losses (KL divergence + L2 parameter alignment) that explicitly supervise adapter generation. On P3, TAGI (58.9 MTest11, T5-LM-XL) trails FiD-ICL [41] (60.0) by 1.1 points but requires only ×0.88 inference time versus FiD-ICL's ×1.9, and does not need the 16-shot demonstrations that ICL methods require. Against decoder-only models, TAGI surpasses GPT-2 XL [28] by 10.2 points and OPT-13B [43] by 7.5 points on SNI zero-shot, confirming the advantage of encoder-decoder architectures in this meta-learning setting. Hypter [42] performs poorly (16.8 ROUGE-L on XL zero-shot) due to the absence of instruction fusion, highlighting the critical role of the cross-attention mechanism in TAGI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hypernetwork-to-adapter paradigm established by HyperTuning and HINT, which generate task-specific adapters from instructions or demonstrations. The key departure is introducing a knowledge distillation framework that supervises the hypernetwork's adapter generation through both KL divergence on output logits and L2 regularization on adapter parameters, framing the process as 'Learning with Instruction' rather than 'Training with Instance.' Additionally, TAGI replaces HINT's simple instruction concatenation with a hierarchical cross-attention mechanism for richer instruction-input interaction, and adds a two-stage training process (C4 pretraining followed by multi-task finetuning with distillation losses).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the ~60% reduction in inference FLOPs while matching or slightly exceeding meta-trained baselines, making instruction-based task adaptation more accessible for resource-constrained settings. However, the work is limited to encoder-decoder models (T5/T0) up to 3B parameters, with the 11B model undertrained and lacking parameter alignment due to resource constraints, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger or decoder-only architectures. The higher training cost (requiring LoRA tuning for each task plus distillation) is a notable trade-off. Future directions include scaling to larger models, multilingual settings, and cross-modal task generalization.
