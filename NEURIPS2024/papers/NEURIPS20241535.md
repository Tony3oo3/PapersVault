## Problem

Recurrent RL algorithms suffer from training instability, particularly over long trajectories, because the autoregressive nature of RNNs amplifies small parameter changes into large output variations that destabilize bootstrapped value targets. Prior work addressed this with truncated training sequences (causing train-deploy mismatch) or auxiliary losses (limiting RNN expressiveness), but no method identified the root cause or offered a simple, architecture-agnostic fix.

## Input / Output

The policy and critic each receive the current observation, last-step observation, last-step action, and last-step reward as inputs, processed through pre-encoder MLPs into a shared hidden dimension before being fed into the context encoder. The context encoder (Mamba or GRU) processes these inputs autoregressively over full-length trajectories (up to 1000 steps), producing a context embedding that is concatenated with the current observation for the MLP policy to output an action. During training, batches are constructed by stacking multiple full-length trajectories along the time axis with hidden-reset flags and zero-padding gaps between them, enabling GPU-parallel processing without per-trajectory loops.

## Method

The paper proves (Proposition 1) that under Lipschitz continuity, RNN output differences after a single gradient step grow as K_y(1-K_h^t)/(1-K_h)·ε + ε, converging to an amplification factor β = K_y/(1-K_h) — a constant-factor amplification distinct from gradient explosion. To counteract this, RESeL assigns a much lower learning rate (LR_CE ≈ 10⁻⁵) to the RNN context encoder while keeping a standard rate (LR_other ≈ 3×10⁻⁴) for all MLP layers, reducing ε for the RNN without slowing MLP convergence. The full algorithm builds on SAC with REDQ's 8-critic ensemble for stabilization, uses Mamba (a parallelizable SSM) as the context encoder for computational efficiency, and delays policy updates to every second critic update (inspired by TD3). Training uses full-length trajectories from a replay buffer with a stacked-batch sampling scheme that resets hidden states between trajectories, avoiding the train-deploy sequence-length mismatch of prior truncated approaches.

## Results

On 8 classic POMDP locomotion tasks (PyBullet), RESeL achieves an average return of 2471, surpassing the previous SOTA GPIDE-ESS (2010) by 22.9% and MF-RNN (1462) by 69%. On 5 dynamics-randomized MuJoCo tasks, RESeL reaches an average of 5945, outperforming the previous SOTA ESCP (4146) by 43.4%. On 5 classic MuJoCo MDP tasks, RESeL achieves an average of 9532 at 5M steps, within 4.8% of TD7 (10010) while surpassing SAC (7109) by 34.2%. In the Key-to-Door credit assignment task, RESeL matches MF-GPT at lengths 60–120 (near 100% success) and outperforms it at lengths 250 and 500.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RESeL outperforms all 8 baselines in the classic POMDP suite (Table 4), with the largest margins over MF-RNN (+69% avg), VRM, and A2C-GRU, and a 22.9% improvement over the previous SOTA GPIDE-ESS. In dynamics-randomized tasks (Table 5), RESeL beats ESCP by 43.4% on average, and substantially outperforms PEARL (+50%), EPI (+117%), OSI (+105%), and ProMP (+866%), while also surpassing SAC-GRU (+103%). In classic meta-RL tasks (Fig. 9), RESeL converges faster and achieves higher asymptotic returns than RL² and VariBad in AntDir, CheetahDir, and HalfCheetahVel, and matches MF-RNN in the simpler Wind task. In the Key-to-Door credit assignment task (Fig. 10), RESeL matches MF-GPT (Transformer-based) at credit assignment lengths 60 and 120 (both near 100% success rate) but surpasses it at lengths 250 and 500. On classic MuJoCo MDP tasks (Table 1), RESeL outperforms SAC by 34.2% on average and TD3 by 39.8%, but trails TD7 by 4.8% overall, though it surpasses TD7 on Hopper (+8.2%) and Walker2d (+8.2%). The ablation in Fig. 12 confirms that the context-encoder-specific learning rate generalizes across GRU and Transformer architectures, with RESeL-GRU and RESeL-Transformer performing comparably to RESeL-Mamba on 3 of 4 POMDP tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the recurrent off-policy RL line initiated by Ni et al. (MF-RNN, ICML 2022), which established design principles for RNN-based actor-critic methods, and on the SAC + REDQ framework for off-policy training stabilization. It departs from prior approaches to recurrent RL instability—truncated trajectories, auxiliary transition-model losses, and gradient clipping—by identifying a distinct failure mode: autoregressive amplification of RNN output variations (formalized via a Lipschitz-based bound in Proposition 1) rather than gradient explosion. The use of Mamba (a parallelizable SSM) as the context encoder, combined with a context-encoder-specific learning rate, represents a practical departure from the GRU/LSTM backbones used in most prior recurrent RL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical diagnosis that recurrent RL instability stems from output amplification (not gradient explosion) reframes how practitioners should think about training RNNs in RL, and the finding that standard RNN stabilization techniques (gradient clipping, truncated BPTT) are ineffective for this failure mode is practically important. The solution—a simple 1/30 learning-rate ratio for the context encoder—is easy to adopt and generalizes across Mamba, GRU, and Transformer backbones. Open questions include how to automatically determine the optimal LR_CE, whether the RNN component genuinely helps in fully-observable MDP tasks (where RESeL still outperforms SAC), and whether the approach extends to on-policy or model-based settings.
