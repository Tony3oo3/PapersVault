## Problem

DDIM inversion-based image editing methods (AIDI, FPI) formulate each inversion step as a fixed-point problem to reduce reconstruction errors, yet none provide a theoretical proof that fixed points actually exist at every sampling step. Additionally, the convergence criterion f(z_t)−z_t used by these methods only measures intra-path convergence, which the paper demonstrates is insufficient to guarantee that the computed point is the true unique fixed point.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240632/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in the latent space of Stable Diffusion, consuming a real RGB image (encoded to latent z_0) and a text prompt, and producing either an edited image (where cross-attention for target words is modified while background is preserved) or a dehazed image (where haze attention is replaced with null-text attention). Experiments use 50 sampling steps with a class-free guidance coefficient of 7.5, evaluated on PIE-Bench (700 images, 10 editing types) and RESIDE real-world hazy scenes.

## Method

The paper proves that the implicit function f(z_t) in DDIM inversion satisfies the contraction mapping condition with Lipschitz constant (1−k√ᾱ_t) ∈ (0,1), establishing via the Banach fixed-point theorem that a unique fixed point exists at every sampling step. Leveraging this uniqueness, the authors show that the standard intra-path loss f(z_t)−z_t converges prematurely (within 2–3 iterations) while inter-path numerical discrepancies persist, and propose replacing it with an inter-path loss f(z_t)−f(ẑ_t) where ẑ_t = z_t + ε, ε~N(0,I), which requires approximately 7 iterations for true convergence. This optimized criterion yields more accurate fixed points, improving visual quality in both P2P-based image editing and unsupervised dehazing via NTI augmented with fixed-point correction.

## Results

On PIE-Bench (700 images, 10 editing types), the proposed inter-path loss f(z_t)−f(ẑ_t) achieves the best Structure Distance (69.190×10⁻³ for editing), MSE (219.85×10⁻⁴), SSIM (71.15×10⁻²), CLIP Whole similarity (25.31), and CLIP Edited similarity (22.58), outperforming both P2P and the original intra-path loss f(z_t)−z_t. For autoregressive reconstruction, the optimized method yields the lowest Structure Distance (70.046×10⁻³) and highest CLIP Whole score (23.80). On the RESIDE dehazing benchmark, NTI with fixed points reduces BRISQUE from 27.59 to 24.13 and NIQE from 4.45 to 3.48, while fixed points without NTI degrade severely (BRISQUE 37.18, NIQE 7.70), confirming NTI's necessity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against P2P (Hertz et al., 2022), the proposed inter-path loss improves Structure Distance by ~0.76×10⁻³ (69.190 vs. 69.953) and CLIP Whole similarity by 0.30 (25.31 vs. 25.01) for editing, while matching PSNR (17.87) and LPIPS (208.88 vs. 208.89). Against the original intra-path loss f(z_t)−z_t used by AIDI (Pan et al., ICCV 2023) and FPI (Meiri et al., 2023), the key gains are in MSE (219.85 vs. 221.48, a ~0.7% reduction) and Structure Distance (69.190 vs. 69.521), confirming that the intra-path criterion converges prematurely and yields suboptimal fixed points. For dehazing on RESIDE, NTI with fixed points (Mokady et al., CVPR 2023) outperforms plain NTI by 3.46 BRISQUE points (24.13 vs. 27.59) and 0.97 NIQE points (3.48 vs. 4.45), while applying fixed points without NTI causes severe image collapse (BRISQUE 37.18, NIQE 7.70), demonstrating that null-text optimization is essential when replacing haze attention with null text at high guidance coefficients.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of DDIM-based image editing (P2P, NTI) and the fixed-point reformulation of DDIM inversion introduced by AIDI and FPI. While AIDI and FPI identified the fixed-point structure and proposed iterative solutions, they lacked a rigorous proof that fixed points actually exist at each time step. This paper fills that gap by proving the Lipschitz constant is less than one via the Banach fixed-point theorem, and then uses the resulting uniqueness to argue that the intra-path convergence criterion (f(z_t)−z_t) used by AIDI and FPI is insufficient, proposing instead an inter-path criterion (f(z_t)−f(ẑ_t)). The work also extends the fixed-point editing framework to unsupervised dehazing built on NTI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantee that fixed points exist and are unique in DDIM inversion removes a foundational uncertainty that prior fixed-point editing methods operated under, and the identification of the inter-path loss as the correct convergence signal is a practically useful correction (convergence takes ~7 iterations rather than the 2–3 reported by FPI). However, the numerical gains in image editing (Table 1) are modest, and the dehazing application still suffers from artifacts due to imprecise semantic attention maps, limiting transfer to fine-grained restoration tasks like deraining. Open questions include reducing the computational overhead of multi-path fixed-point computation and designing principled ways to incorporate image priors (dark channel, depth) into cross-attention layers.
