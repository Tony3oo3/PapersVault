## Problem

Existing local image editing methods either rely on text prompts that cannot capture fine visual details (logos, patterns, specific shoe soles) or require users to manually crop/mask the reference region, which is impractical for local parts that are semantically intertwined with their surrounding context. This paper introduces 'imitative editing,' where the user provides only a full in-the-wild reference image without any region annotation, and the system must automatically discover and transfer the relevant visual content.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 source image with a binary mask marking the region to edit, a full reference image (no mask or box required), and an optional depth map for shape-preserving texture transfer. The output is the edited image with the masked region filled. Notably, the reference image is consumed as a whole — the model internally localizes the semantically corresponding region rather than receiving it as a pre-cropped input, which distinguishes this from composition methods like AnyDoor or Paint-by-Example.

## Method

MimicBrush employs a dual diffusion U-Net architecture: an imitative U-Net (initialized from Stable Diffusion 1.5 inpainting, taking 13-channel input of image latent + mask + background latent + depth latent) and a reference U-Net (initialized from standard SD 1.5) that extracts multi-level features from the reference image. The reference U-Net's attention keys and values are concatenated with the imitative U-Net's in the middle and upper stages, enabling cross-image feature transfer. Training is fully self-supervised: two frames from the same video clip serve as source and reference, with strong augmentations (color jitter, rotation, random projection) to maximize visual variation while preserving semantic correspondence. A SIFT-guided masking strategy biases the masked region toward discriminative areas rather than uniform random grids, and an optional Depth Anything depth map (dropped with 50% probability during training) provides shape control for texture transfer tasks.

## Results

On the inner-ID track of the part composition benchmark (where ground truth is available), MimicBrush achieves SSIM 0.70, PSNR 17.54, and LPIPS 0.28, substantially outperforming Paint-by-Example (SSIM 0.51, PSNR 16.09) and AnyDoor with mask (SSIM 0.44, PSNR 14.09). On the inter-ID track, MimicBrush scores DINO-I 56.48, CLIP-I 84.30, and CLIP-T 30.08, trailing AnyDoor with mask (DINO-I 61.30, CLIP-I 86.08) on identity metrics but leading on text alignment. In the user study, MimicBrush is ranked best 55.8% of the time for fidelity, 45.2% for harmony, and 50.6% for overall quality, with the lowest average rank (2.11–2.34) across all three criteria.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Paint-by-Example (PbE), MimicBrush wins decisively on every metric: on inner-ID it improves SSIM from 0.51 to 0.70 and LPIPS from 0.48 to 0.28, and on inter-ID it gains ~14 points in DINO-I and ~3 points in CLIP-I. Against AnyDoor without mask, MimicBrush dominates across all six metrics (e.g., SSIM 0.70 vs 0.42, DINO-I 56.48 vs 43.41). The most nuanced comparison is AnyDoor with mask: AnyDoor actually surpasses MimicBrush on inter-ID identity metrics (DINO-I 61.30 vs 56.48; CLIP-I 86.08 vs 84.30), but this advantage comes from being explicitly given the reference region mask, whereas MimicBrush must discover the correspondence autonomously. On inner-ID, MimicBrush still clearly beats AnyDoor w/ Mask (SSIM 0.70 vs 0.44, PSNR 17.54 vs 14.09). In the user study, MimicBrush is preferred over AnyDoor w/ Mask by a wide margin on fidelity (55.8% vs 30.6% best picks) and harmony (45.2% vs 22.6%), indicating that the quantitative gap on inter-ID identity does not translate to perceived quality, likely due to AnyDoor's weaker blending and detail preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MimicBrush builds on the dual-U-Net cross-attention injection mechanism introduced by MagicAnimate [46], where a reference U-Net's keys and values are concatenated into an imitative U-Net to guide generation. It departs from prior image composition methods (AnyDoor, Paint-by-Example, ObjectStitch) that require the user to explicitly specify the reference region via a mask or bounding box, and from text-guided inpainting (SmartBrush, InstructPix2Pix) that cannot capture fine visual details like logos or textures. The key conceptual departure is formulating editing as a self-supervised video-frame completion problem, where the model learns to discover semantic correspondence between two frames without any paired mask annotations, eliminating the need for part-level mask pairs that are infeasible to collect at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a more natural interaction paradigm for local image editing—users simply provide a full reference image without specifying which region matters—making the tool more accessible for product design, fashion, and creative applications. The self-supervised video-based training pipeline is scalable and annotation-free, which is practically important for training at scale. However, the method fails when the target region is too small or when multiple candidate regions exist in the reference, and the 512×512 resolution limits real-world applicability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to higher resolutions, video editing, and whether the discovered correspondence can be made more controllable or interpretable for users.
