## Problem

Existing unsupervised domain adaptation and open-vocabulary segmentation methods fail to generalize across camera viewpoints (e.g., ground-level car views to aerial drone views) because they lack explicit cross-view geometric modeling. The significant change in camera position introduces large structural and topological differences in scene layouts that standard UDA losses (adversarial, entropy, self-supervised consistency) cannot capture, and open-vocab prompting mechanisms do not encode view-condition information.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unpaired RGB images from a source view (car, 512×512) and a target view (drone, 512×512), along with text prompts that include class names augmented with view-condition descriptors (e.g., 'captured from the car view' vs. 'captured from the drone view'). It produces per-pixel semantic segmentation masks for the target view. The source domain provides ground-truth masks for supervised training, while the target domain is entirely unlabeled; no cross-view image pairs are required during training.

## Method

EAGLE introduces a cross-view geometric constraint that enforces a linear proportionality (D_x = α·D_y) between structural changes measured in the image space and the segmentation space, applied to unpaired source-target samples. To compute these structural change metrics, the method projects images and segmentation maps onto subspaces (via PCA) and measures their correlation along a geodesic flow path on the Grassmann manifold, yielding a closed-form kernel matrix Q that encodes the manifold geometry between views. A view-condition prompting mechanism appends camera-view descriptors to text prompts, and an additional geometric constraint (D_p = γ·D_y) is imposed on the CLIP text embeddings of these prompts to align prompt representations across views. The full training objective combines a supervised open-vocab segmentation loss on the source domain with the cross-view geometric adaptation loss (weighted by λ_I for image-segmentation and λ_P for prompt-segmentation terms), all within a Mask2Former + FreeSeg + CLIP architecture.

## Results

On the SYNTHIA→UAVID benchmark, EAGLE with DAFormer achieves 53.1% mIoU, outperforming the prior best UDA method CROVIA (46.0%) by +7.1% and MIC (41.8%) by +11.3%. On GTA→UAVID, EAGLE with DAFormer reaches 40.7% mIoU versus CROVIA's 36.8% (+3.9%) and MIC's 32.5% (+8.2%). In the open-vocabulary setting with Mask2Former, EAGLE + View-Condition Prompting achieves 52.2% mIoU on SYNTHIA→UAVID and 48.5% on GTA→UAVID, surpassing FreeSeg + Cross-View (51.3% / 47.5%) and DenseCLIP + Cross-View (44.9% / 45.8%). On the real-to-real BDD→UAVID setting, EAGLE (open-vocab) reaches 50.7% mIoU, outperforming FreeSeg + Cross-View (47.9%) and DAFormer (41.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAGLE consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across both cross-view benchmarks. Against the closest prior cross-view method CROVIA [50], EAGLE gains +7.1% mIoU on SYNTHIA→UAVID (53.1% vs 46.0% with DAFormer) and +3.9% on GTA→UAVID (40.7% vs 36.8%). Against the strongest general UDA baselines, EAGLE surpasses MIC [25] by +11.3% (SYNTHIA→UAVID) and +8.2% (GTA→UAVID), and DAFormer [23] by +13.7% and +12.2% respectively. In the open-vocabulary setting, EAGLE + View-Condition Prompting beats FreeSeg [38] + Cross-View by +0.9% (SYNTHIA→UAVID) and +1.0% (GTA→UAVID), and DenseCLIP [40] + Cross-View by +7.3% and +3.7%. Notably, EAGLE outperforms depth-based DADA [59] (34.6% mIoU) without using any depth labels, and substantially exceeds the polar-transform geo-localization baseline [45] (19.3% mIoU) by over 30 points, confirming that explicit geometric structural modeling is critical for large viewpoint changes (car-to-drone) rather than simple spatial transforms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for semantic segmentation and open-vocabulary segmentation via vision-language models. It departs from prior UDA methods (AdvEnt, SAC, DAFormer, MIC) that assume similar camera geometry across domains (e.g., simulation-to-real, day-to-night) by explicitly modeling the large geometric structural changes inherent to cross-view shifts (car to drone). The Geodesic Flow kernel, originally introduced by Gong et al. (2012) for domain adaptation and later adapted by Simon et al. (2021) for incremental learning, is repurposed here as a structural-change metric on Grassmann manifolds to enforce a linear proportionality between image-space and segmentation-space geometric shifts. The view-condition prompting mechanism extends the prompting strategies of DenseCLIP and FreeSeg by injecting camera-view context into the textual prompt, a departure from prior work that only encodes class names or task descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-view adaptation (e.g., transferring ground-level segmentation to aerial/drone views) is a practical bottleneck for autonomous systems where re-annotating high-resolution UAV data is costly. The theoretical upper-bound result showing that the unpaired-data geometric constraint bounds the paired-data constraint provides a principled justification for training without cross-view pairs, which is the realistic deployment scenario. Key limitations include the linear proportionality assumption (α, γ) between image and segmentation structural changes, sensitivity to the subspace dimension and scale factors, and evaluation on only 5–6 classes across three source datasets. Open questions include whether non-linear or learned relations across views would further close the gap to supervised performance, and whether the approach generalizes to more diverse category sets or other open-vocab architectures beyond Mask2Former/FreeSeg.
