## Problem

The prior work on MNL mixture MDPs by Hwang and Oh (2023) achieves a regret bound of Õ(κ^{-1}dH²√K), where κ^{-1} scales polynomially with the number of reachable states U (up to U² in the worst case), effectively negating the benefit of function approximation for large state spaces. Their method also requires O(k) storage and time per episode, making it computationally intractable for long horizons. This paper asks whether both statistical and computational efficiency can be simultaneously achieved for RL with MNL function approximation.

## Input / Output

The learner interacts with an inhomogeneous episodic MDP over K episodes of length H, observing states s_{k,h} and selecting actions a_{k,h} at each stage. Transitions are parameterized by a known feature mapping φ(s'|s,a) with ||φ||₂≤1 and unknown bounded parameters θ_h* (||θ_h*||₂≤B), with the MNL/softmax structure guaranteeing valid probability distributions over reachable states. The output is a policy π_k per episode and a high-probability regret guarantee; the key structural constraint is that the number of reachable states U per (s,a,h) triple can be large, and the algorithm must avoid polynomial dependence on U in the dominant regret term.

## Method

The paper proposes two algorithms. UCRL-MNL-LL uses MLE for parameter estimation but constructs a κ-independent confidence set via a Bernstein-type concentration inequality and the self-concordant-like property of the log-loss, yielding a tighter radius that removes κ^{-1} from the dominant regret term. The optimistic value function is built by directly maximizing expected reward over the confidence set, leveraging a second-order Taylor expansion to capture local curvature. UCRL-MNL-OL achieves the same Õ(dH²√K + κ^{-1}d²H²) regret with O(1) storage and time per episode by replacing MLE with online mirror descent using a look-ahead Hessian as the local norm, and replacing the non-convex confidence-set maximization with a closed-form bonus derived from a second-order Taylor expansion around the current estimate. A first lower bound of Ω(dH√(Kκ*)) is established via a reduction from the logistic bandit problem, confirming optimality in d and K.

## Results

The paper establishes a regret bound of Õ(dH²√K + κ⁻¹d²H²) for both UCRL-MNL-LL and UCRL-MNL-OL, eliminating the problematic κ⁻¹ factor from the dominant term (previously Õ(κ⁻¹dH²√K)). UCRL-MNL-OL further achieves O(1) storage and time complexity per episode, compared to O(k) for the prior method. The first lower bound for MNL mixture MDPs is Ω(dH√(Kκ*)), confirming optimality in d and K (up to an H^{1/2} factor).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Hwang and Oh (AAAI 2023), the first work to study MNL mixture MDPs. The paper strictly improves upon it on three axes: (1) statistical efficiency — the regret bound drops from Õ(κ⁻¹dH²√K) to Õ(dH²√K + κ⁻¹d²H²), removing κ⁻¹ from the dominant √K-scaling term (since κ⁻¹ ≥ U², the old bound scaled polynomially with the number of reachable states, undermining the purpose of function approximation); (2) computational efficiency — UCRL-MNL-OL reduces both storage and per-episode time from O(k) to O(1) via online mirror descent with a tailored local norm and a closed-form bonus from second-order Taylor expansion; (3) generality — the setting is extended from homogeneous to inhomogeneous MDPs. The newly established lower bound Ω(dH√(Kκ*)) matches the upper bound in d and K (up to H^{1/2}), confirming that the improvement is not merely an artifact of a looser analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MNL mixture MDP framework introduced by Hwang and Oh (AAAI 2023), which was the first to model transitions via multinomial logit functions to guarantee valid probability distributions. It departs from their work in three key ways: (1) moving from homogeneous to inhomogeneous episodic MDPs, (2) replacing Hoeffding-type concentration with Bernstein-type inequalities and exploiting self-concordance of log-loss to eliminate the problematic κ^{-1} factor from the dominant regret term, and (3) adapting the online mirror descent with look-ahead Hessian design from Faury et al. (AISTATS 2022) and Zhang and Sugiyama (NeurIPS 2023) to achieve constant per-episode computational cost. The paper also identifies a technical flaw in Hwang and Oh's confidence set analysis (dependent noise violating sub-Gaussian assumptions) that, to the authors' knowledge, has gone unaddressed in the broader MNL bandit literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves the central limitation of the only prior algorithm for MNL mixture MDPs—its regret scaled polynomially with the number of reachable states, undermining the very motivation for function approximation in large state spaces. The first lower bound (via reduction to logistic bandits) confirms optimality in d and K, though an H^{1/2} gap remains open, and it is unclear whether the variance-based techniques that improved H-dependence for linear mixture MDPs (Zhou et al., 2021) can be adapted here given the non-linear value function structure. The computationally efficient variant (constant storage and time per episode) makes the approach practical for long-horizon settings, and the identification of the dependent-noise issue in prior MNL bandit analyses may prompt corrections across that literature.
