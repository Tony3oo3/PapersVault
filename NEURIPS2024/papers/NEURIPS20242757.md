## Problem

The key-value (KV) cache memory footprint scales linearly with both sequence length and batch size, becoming a dominant bottleneck in LLM serving at long contexts. While Multi-Query Attention (MQA) and Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) reduce KV cache size by sharing key/value heads across query heads within a single layer, no prior work had explored reducing the number of unique layers that must store KV activations. This paper asks whether KV activations can also be shared across layers to further shrink the cache without significant accuracy loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242757/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume BPE-tokenized text sequences (GPT-NeoX tokenizer) at 2048-token sequence length and produce standard next-token predictions for autoregressive language modeling. The architectural modification affects the KV cache structure during decoding: under CLA with sharing factor s, only ⌈L/s⌉ of the L transformer layers maintain their own KV cache entries, while the remaining layers attend over the KV activations of the nearest preceding layer that does compute a fresh KV projection. All models are trained self-supervised on the SlimPajama corpus (30B tokens at 1B scale, 100B tokens at 3B scale).

## Method

Cross-Layer Attention (CLA) modifies the transformer by computing key/value projections for only a subset of layers; layers without their own KV projection reuse the KV activations of the nearest preceding layer that does have one. The sharing factor (e.g., CLA2 for pairs of adjacent layers) controls how many layers share each KV projection, directly scaling the KV cache memory by that factor. CLA is orthogonal to the attention variant (MHA, GQA, or MQA) and can be combined with any of them. The authors use separately-learnable RMSNorm parameters for the KV and Q projection blocks in CLA layers to give the shared KV path its own normalization. Extensive pretraining at 1B and 3B parameter scales shows that MQA + CLA2 achieves the best accuracy/memory Pareto frontier, delivering a 2× KV cache reduction over plain MQA with only ~0.04–0.06 perplexity degradation at well-tuned learning rates, and outperforming larger sharing factors (CLA3, CLA4) and non-uniform sharing patterns.

## Results

At the 1B-parameter scale with tuned learning rates, H128-MQA-CLA2 achieves a validation perplexity of 12.43 and Wikitext perplexity of 19.29 while using only 5,120 KV bytes per token — half the KV cache of H128-MQA (10,240 bytes, PPL 12.39) and matching the footprint of H64-MQA (5,120 bytes, PPL 12.74) with a 0.31-point perplexity improvement. At the 3B-parameter scale, H64-MQA-CLA2 achieves a Wikitext perplexity of 12.99 at 4,096 KV bytes per token, compared to 12.94 for H64-MQA (8,192 bytes) and 13.34 for H32-MQA (4,096 bytes), confirming the 2× KV cache reduction with ≤0.05-point perplexity degradation. On downstream benchmarks (HellaSwag, PIQA, WinoGrande, SciQ, OpenBookQA, BoolQ, ARC-Easy), all compared models remain within 1–5 percentage points of each other.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MHA baseline (H128-MHA, 163,840 KV bytes/token, PPL 13.15), CLA models achieve substantially lower perplexity at a fraction of the memory: H128-MQA-CLA2 uses only 5,120 bytes/token (32× less) with PPL 13.60 in the design space exploration. Against GQA baselines, H128-MQA-CLA2 (5,120 bytes, PPL 13.60) matches H128-GQA2 (20,480 bytes, PPL 13.52) at 4× less memory, and H256-MQA-CLA2 (10,240 bytes, PPL 13.51) beats H128-GQA4 (40,960 bytes, PPL 13.36) at 4× less memory. The primary MQA comparison is the headline result: H128-MQA-CLA2 halves the KV cache of H128-MQA (10,240→5,120 bytes) with only a 0.04-point validation PPL increase (12.39→12.43) after learning-rate tuning, while at the same 5,120-byte budget it beats H64-MQA by 0.31 points (12.43 vs 12.74). Against the open-source TinyLlama-1.1B-105B checkpoint, the CLA2-retrained version improves Wikitext perplexity from 21.34 to 19.59 and gains on most downstream benchmarks (e.g., HellaSwag 43.54→45.83, SciQ 72.40→77.30, ARC-Easy 44.91→47.10), confirming CLA's benefits transfer to externally-trained architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KV-sharing paradigm established by Multi-Query Attention (MQA) and Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) along a new axis: rather than sharing key/value heads across query heads within a single layer, CLA shares KV activations across consecutive layers. The approach is explicitly orthogonal to MQA/GQA/MHA and composable with any of them, positioning CLA as a second independent knob for controlling KV cache size. The adaptation experiments in Section 4 also draw on the 'uptraining' recipe from Ainslie et al. (2023) for converting MHA models to GQA, applying an analogous mean-pooling strategy to initialize shared KV projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache memory is a primary bottleneck for long-context and large-batch LLM serving, and CLA offers a simple architectural modification that halves KV cache size with only ~0.05 perplexity degradation at both 1B and 3B scales. Because CLA is orthogonal to MQA/GQA, it can be stacked with existing techniques for multiplicative reductions. Key open questions include whether the benefits persist at 7B+ parameter scales, whether end-to-end serving latency actually improves (the paper does not evaluate system-level throughput), and whether the adaptation recipe can be improved to allow retrofitting CLA onto existing checkpoints without full retraining.
