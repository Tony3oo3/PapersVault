## Problem

Existing 3D floorplanning methods are constrained by heuristic representations (e.g., B*-tree, Corner Block List) that limit the solution space and prevent fine-grained position adjustments, while analytical methods cannot handle the non-differentiable cross-die alignment constraint. Learning-based approaches that directly place blocks have only addressed 2D scenarios, overlooking the multi-die layer decision, cross-die alignment, and variable aspect ratio requirements that are critical in 3D IC design. No prior learning-based method has simultaneously discarded heuristic search while handling all three properties (position, aspect ratio, alignment) in a 3D setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a block list (each with fixed area, hard/soft type, and pre-assigned layer), I/O port positions on specific dies, a netlist defining interconnections, and per-pair minimum alignment area requirements, all projected onto a 128×128 grid per die with 2 stacked dies. The output is a complete 3D floorplan: discrete 2D coordinates (x, y) and a discrete layer index z for each block, plus a continuous aspect ratio (Gaussian-sampled, bounded to [0.5, 2]) for soft blocks. The hybrid action space means each step simultaneously produces a discrete position decision, a discrete next-layer decision, and a continuous aspect-ratio decision for the following block.

## Method

FlexPlanner formulates 3D floorplanning as an episodic MDP solved with Hybrid PPO under an Actor-Critic framework, where the policy network has three sub-modules (position decider via CNN generator, layer decider via Transformer with cross-attention over multi-die sequences, and aspect-ratio decider via CNN outputting Gaussian parameters). The state is represented through three modalities: a vision modality of four masks (alignment, canvas, wire, position) concatenated as a multi-channel image, a graph modality processed by a 2-layer GAT over the netlist, and a sequence modality of per-die FIFO queues processed by a Transformer. A key architectural choice is the asynchronous layer decision mechanism, which uses self-attention over the full multi-die block sequence to determine which die's queue to pop next, avoiding the degeneration to synchronous die-by-die placing. The alignment mask serves a dual role: as an input feature for the policy and as a hard constraint that masks out positions violating the minimum alignment area. The reward function combines local advantage (step-to-step metric differences) with a global baseline (final episode score) to provide dense gradient signal for both the position and layer deciders, preventing the layer module from collapsing to a fixed order.

## Results

On the MCNC and GSRC benchmarks (8 circuits), FlexPlanner achieves an average alignment score of 0.940, nearly doubling the previous best (MaskPlace at 0.474). For wirelength, FlexPlanner reduces average HPWL to 249,053, a 16% improvement over the second-best method Wiremask-BBO (296,791). The method also achieves zero overlap (0.001 avg.) and zero out-of-bound violations, while maintaining competitive runtime (~4.3s average) comparable to other learning-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexPlanner dominates all six baselines on both alignment and HPWL across all 8 circuits. On alignment, the gap is most dramatic against heuristic methods: 3D-B*-SA (0.338 avg.) and RL-CBL (0.084 avg.) struggle severely as circuit size grows, dropping below 0.14 on n100+. Among learning-based baselines, MaskPlace (0.474) is the closest competitor but still trails by nearly 2×; GraphPlace (0.288) and DeepPlace (0.306) perform comparably to each other. On HPWL, Wiremask-BBO (296,791 avg.) is the strongest baseline, but FlexPlanner still achieves a 16% reduction (249,053). Notably, Wiremask-BBO's alignment score is only 0.222, confirming that it cannot simultaneously optimize both objectives. The heuristic 3D-B*-SA has the worst HPWL (379,257) and degrades sharply on larger circuits, while RL-CBL is the slowest learning-based method in runtime but still outperformed on quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RL-based floorplanning (GraphPlace, DeepPlace, MaskPlace) from 2D to 3D IC design, while simultaneously departing from the heuristic-representation paradigm (B*-tree, Corner Block List) that constrained prior 3D FP methods like 3D-B*-SA and RL-CBL. The hybrid action space formulation draws directly on Fan et al.'s (IJCAI 2019) parameterized action space framework, combining discrete position/layer decisions with a continuous aspect-ratio Gaussian. The multi-modality state representation (vision masks + GAT + Transformer sequence) integrates ideas from MaskPlace's visual encoding, GraphPlace's netlist graph, and standard sequence modeling, unified under a single Actor-Critic PPO pipeline with an asynchronous layer-decision mechanism that has no direct precedent in prior FP work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

3D IC integration (Memory-on-Logic, vertical buses) is becoming a mainstream manufacturing paradigm, making cross-die alignment a first-class design constraint that prior methods handled poorly (best prior score 0.474). FlexPlanner's near-doubling of alignment score (0.940) and 16% HPWL reduction demonstrate that learning-based methods can outperform both heuristic and analytical approaches when freed from heuristic representation constraints. The zero-shot transferability (training on n100, deploying on n300 with <6% HPWL degradation) is practically significant for EDA workflows where retraining per circuit is costly. Open questions include scaling beyond 2 dies, incorporating thermal and power constraints, and whether the grid-based discretization (128×128) limits precision for production-scale designs.
