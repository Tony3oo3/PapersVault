## Problem

Existing learned image compression methods rely on sophisticated encoders that are too computationally expensive to deploy on satellite embedded GPUs, which are constrained to roughly 15 W of power and limited FLOPs. This paper addresses the gap by designing a lightweight on-satellite encoder that achieves high compression ratios while offloading the quality-recovery burden to a ground-based diffusion model that compensates for the encoder's degraded feature extraction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242917/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 RGB satellite image patches (cropped from fMoW or tiled from 2306×2306 full images) along with 8-dimensional sensor metadata (coordinates, timestamp, GSD, cloud cover, off-nadir angle, target azimuth, sun azimuth, sun elevation) as input. On the satellite side it produces an entropy-coded latent representation for downlink; on the ground side it reconstructs the full image by combining the decoded latent with diffusion-generated compensation. For the tile test set, individual 256×256 patches are compressed independently and stitched back into a single high-resolution image for evaluation.

## Method

COSMIC has two components: a lightweight encoder on the satellite and a diffusion-based compensation decoder on the ground. The encoder replaces standard 5×5 convolutions with depthwise-separable lightweight convolution blocks (LCB) and substitutes quadratic self-attention with a convolution attention module (CAM) that uses two serial 1D convolutions to approximate global context, reducing on-satellite FLOPs by 2.6–5×. On the ground, a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion U-Net generates compensation features z₀′ from Gaussian noise, conditioned on (1) the discrete latent coding [y] injected additively into U-Net blocks to preserve structural consistency, and (2) sensor metadata encoded via sinusoidal embeddings and fed through Cross-Attention blocks. Training is two-stage: first the encoder, a separate compensation encoder, and the image decoder are jointly optimized with a rate-distortion loss; then those parameters are frozen while the noise prediction network is fine-tuned with a standard diffusion denoising loss to match the compensation encoder's output distribution.

## Results

On the tile test set, COSMIC achieves 4.9G on-satellite FLOPs — a 2.6–5× reduction over all baselines (11.87–26.4G) — while delivering the best perceptual quality: LPIPS of 0.0359 and FID of 13.50 at 0.59 bpp. In distortion metrics, COSMIC attains PSNR of 32.11 dB and MS-SSIM of 0.980, competitive with the strongest baselines (Elic: 33.31 dB / 0.983). The paper also reports that COSMIC outperforms all six baselines on LPIPS and FID across all tested bitrates on both the fMoW and tile test sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COSMIC dominates on perceptual metrics: it achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.0359) and FID (13.50) among all six baselines, outperforming the closest competitor Hific (LPIPS 0.0384, FID 27.01) and the diffusion-based CDC (LPIPS 0.0462, FID 45.49) by a wide margin. On distortion, Elic leads with PSNR 33.31 dB and MS-SSIM 0.983, edging out COSMIC (32.11 dB / 0.980) by ~1.2 dB and 0.003 respectively; however, Elic's perceptual quality is substantially worse (LPIPS 0.0683, FID 60.80). COSMIC clearly beats the VAE-based baselines COLIC and Hific on every metric (e.g., +3.07 dB PSNR over COLIC, −54% FID over Hific) while using roughly 5× fewer FLOPs. Against the remote-sensing-specific HL_RS, COSMIC improves PSNR by 0.81 dB, LPIPS by 54%, and FID by 51% at comparable bpp. The industrial JPEG2000 baseline shows the largest accuracy drop in downstream classification (−4.21% at 10 classes) versus COSMIC's −1.06%, confirming that the learned approach better preserves task-relevant structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

COSMIC builds on the classic variational image compression architecture (Ballé et al.) for its encoder design and on Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al.) for its compensation module, but departs from both in key ways. Unlike prior learned compression methods (Hific, COLIC, Elic) that invest complexity in the encoder to extract rich features, COSMIC deliberately simplifies the encoder for satellite deployment and shifts the reconstruction burden to a ground-based diffusion model. It also differs from prior diffusion-based compression (CDC, Yang & Mandt) which uses diffusion as the decoder itself; here diffusion generates auxiliary compensation information that is concatenated with the decoder's latent input, and is uniquely conditioned on satellite sensor metadata (coordinates, timestamp, GSD, cloud cover) treated as a text-to-image pairing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck in earth observation: satellite-to-ground bandwidth is limited while image volumes grow exponentially, and existing learned compressors are too computationally heavy for the 15W embedded GPUs on current satellites. The insight that sensor metadata provides a natural multi-modal condition for diffusion generation is creative and could generalize to other sensor-equipped imaging domains. However, the authors acknowledge that at extremely low bitrates (<0.1 bpp), the pretrained Stable Diffusion lacks sufficient satellite-specific priors, and training a domain-specific diffusion model or using historical imagery as reference remains an open direction. The tile-stitching evaluation also highlights a practically important but underexplored aspect of satellite image compression.
