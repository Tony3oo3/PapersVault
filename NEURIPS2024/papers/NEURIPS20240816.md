## Problem

While learning-augmented algorithms have successfully bypassed information-theoretic lower bounds in online optimization, it remained open whether machine-learned predictions can overcome computational hardness barriers (approximation hardness) for NP-hard offline problems. This paper answers a question posed by Svensson (SODA 2023): can an ε-correlated prediction of an optimal MAXCUT solution yield a polynomial-time approximation strictly better than the Goemans-Williamson bound α_GW ≈ 0.878, which is tight under the Unique Games Conjecture?

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a weighted undirected graph (symmetric adjacency matrix A with zero diagonal) together with either a noisy prediction vector Y ∈ {−1,+1}^n (each coordinate correct with probability 1/2+ε) or a partial prediction vector Y ∈ {−1,0,+1}^n (each coordinate correct with probability ε, zero otherwise). Only pairwise independence across vertices is assumed, and the bias ε is unknown to the algorithm. The output is a vertex bipartition (cut) for MAXCUT or a binary variable assignment for general 2-CSPs, with approximation guarantees that improve over the prediction-free baseline by a function of ε.

## Method

The core technique partitions vertices into Δ-wide (most weight spread over many neighbors) and Δ-narrow (concentrated on few heavy edges) sets. For Δ-wide graphs, the prediction vector is used to estimate the optimal neighborhood imbalance r* = Ax* via the rescaled prediction Z = Y/(2ε), then an LP is solved to find a fractional assignment that maximizes cut value while remaining within (ε′+2η)W of these estimates in ℓ₁ norm; randomized rounding (or deterministic pipeage rounding) yields the final cut. For Δ-narrow graphs, the algorithm adapts the FKL bounded-degree MAXCUT algorithm with a tighter ℓ₁/ℓ₂ norm analysis from Hsieh-Kothari, achieving a gain of Ω̃(η⁵/Δ²) over α_GW without using predictions. For the partial prediction model, the Raghavendra-Tan max-bisection rounding (which preserves marginals) is employed to convert the Ω(ε²) edge-level gain into an Ω(ε) approximation improvement. The framework extends to general 2-CSPs by replacing the quadratic cut objective with a multilinear predicate expansion in the LP.

## Results

For MAXCUT with noisy predictions (each vertex correct with probability 1/2+ε, pairwise independent), the paper achieves an approximation factor of α_GW + Õ(ε⁴) ≈ 0.878 + Õ(ε⁴), unconditionally breaking the Goemans-Williamson barrier. Under the partial predictions model (each vertex revealed with probability ε), the guarantee improves to α_RT + (1−α_RT−o(1))(2ε−ε²) ≈ 0.858 + 0.142·(2ε−ε²), yielding a linear Ω(ε) improvement over the base. For general 2-CSPs on Δ-wide instances, the algorithm achieves opt_I − 5η − O(ε′) with probability 0.98, lowering the density threshold required by Arora et al. [1999] for near-optimal approximations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the Goemans-Williamson SDP algorithm (α_GW ≈ 0.878), which is UGC-tight for MAXCUT; the noisy-prediction algorithm strictly exceeds this by Õ(ε⁴) for any ε > 0. The partial-prediction model uses the Raghavendra-Tan max-bisection rounding (α_RT ≈ 0.858) as its base, trading a slightly lower worst-case factor for a linear Ω(ε) improvement rather than the Õ(ε⁴) of the noisy model. For dense 2-CSPs, the result subsumes Arora et al. [1999]'s PTAS by requiring a weaker density condition (the threshold depends on ε and the approximation error, making it applicable to sparser instances). Compared to the concurrent work of Bampis et al. [2024], which accelerates PTAS running time for dense CSPs, this paper focuses on improving the approximation factor itself. Relative to Ghoshal et al. [2024], who assume full independence of predictions and obtain (1−O((ε√Δ)⁻¹)) approximations, this paper's pairwise-independence assumption is strictly weaker and more realistic for ML-generated predictions, though the dependence on ε differs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the learning-augmented algorithms paradigm—previously applied almost exclusively to online problems where predictions overcome information-theoretic barriers—into the offline NP-hard setting, where the barrier is computational (approximation hardness under UGC). For MAXCUT, it composes the Goemans-Williamson SDP framework with the Feige-Karpinski-Langberg / Hsieh-Kothari bounded-degree analysis for Δ-narrow graphs, and introduces a novel LP-based approach using predicted neighborhood imbalances for Δ-wide graphs. For dense 2-CSPs, it extends the Arora-Karger-Karpinski PTAS by showing that ε-correlated predictions lower the required density threshold. Two concurrent works (Bampis et al. 2024; Ghoshal et al. 2024) independently explore noisy predictions for MAXCUT/CSPs but under stronger independence assumptions or different algorithmic strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unconditional demonstration that even arbitrarily weak (ε-correlated) predictions can break the UGC-hard approximation barrier for MAXCUT, answering a question posed by Svensson at SODA 2023. However, the improvement is only Ω̃(ε⁴) for noisy predictions, which is practically negligible for realistic prediction quality, and the conjectured α_GW + O(ε) barrier (supported by an SDP integrality gap argument) suggests the gap may be hard to close. Key open questions include whether the exponent can be improved, whether results extend to minimization CSPs or other correlation structures beyond pairwise independence, and whether general lower bounds exist for the prediction models.
