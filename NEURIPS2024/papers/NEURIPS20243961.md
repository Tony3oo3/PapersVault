## Problem

Future-dependent value functions (FDVFs) were proposed to achieve polynomial-in-horizon OPE guarantees in POMDPs by operating on latent-state density ratios rather than exponential history ratios. However, the guarantees also depend on the boundedness of the FDVF itself and a conversion ratio IV(V), both of which can scale exponentially with the horizon, erasing the framework's advantage over standard importance sampling. This paper identifies these 'curses of future and history' and provides the missing coverage assumptions that make the guarantees fully polynomial.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n historical trajectories of observation-action-reward sequences collected under behavior policy π_b, along with the evaluation policy π_e and finite function classes V (over futures f_h = (o_h, a_h, ..., o_H, a_H)) and either Ξ (over histories τ_h = (o_1, a_1, ..., o_{h-1}, a_{h-1})) for the Bellman-completeness variant or W (also over histories) for the new MIS-style variant. The output is a point estimate of J(π_e) via E_D[V̂(f_1)], where V̂ is the learned FDVF. A key structural detail is the asymmetric split: value functions are indexed by futures (posterior information about s_h) while weight/residual functions are indexed by histories (prior information), reflecting the forward-backward duality of the POMDP.

## Method

The paper introduces two novel coverage assumptions tailored to POMDP structure: outcome coverage, which requires the weighted covariance matrix Σ_{F,h} (constructed from the outcome matrix M_{F,h} with Z_h-weighting) to cover the direction of V_S^{π_e}, and belief coverage, which requires the belief-state covariance Σ_{H,h} to cover the average belief vector b_h^{π_e}. For bounding the FDVF, the key technical insight is replacing the standard pseudo-inverse solution with a minimum weighted L2-norm solution (using Z_h = diag(1_S^T M_{F,h}) weighting) and, for the L∞ version, incorporating reward information via Z^R to recover V_F = R^+ in the on-policy case. For the conversion ratio, the paper introduces effective history weights w*(τ_h) = b(τ_h)^T Σ_{H,h}^{-1} b_h^{π_e} that jointly handle the π_b→π_e and B^H→B^S conversions, avoiding cumulative importance weights. A new MIS-style algorithm replaces Bellman completeness with weight realizability, minimizing |E_{π_b}[w(τ_h)(B^H V)(τ_h)]| over a weight class W, yielding a guarantee depending only on C_μ, C_{H,∞}, and C_{F,∞}.

## Results

The paper establishes fully polynomial estimation guarantees for OPE in POMDPs, eliminating the exponential-in-horizon dependence that plagues importance sampling and history-based MDP reductions. Theorem 7 shows that under L₂ belief coverage (C_{H,2}) and L∞ outcome coverage (C_{F,∞}), the original FDVF estimator achieves |J(π_e) − E_D[V̂(f₁)]| ≤ cH²(C_{F,∞}+1)√(C_{H,2}·C_μ·log(|V||Ξ|/δ)/n). Theorem 9 provides an analogous bound for a new MIS-like algorithm: |J(π_e) − E_D[V̂(f₁)]| ≤ ε_V + ε_W + cH²C_{H,∞}(C_{F,∞}+1)√(C_μ·log(|V||W|/δ)/n), where C_{H,∞} is the L∞ belief coverage parameter. In the on-policy case (π_b = π_e), all coverage parameters reduce to 1, recovering the trivially bounded V_F = R⁺ construction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or numerical comparisons. The comparisons are between different constructions of the future-dependent value function V_F and different algorithmic frameworks. The importance-sampling solution for V_F involves cumulative action importance weights (exponential in H), and the pseudo-inverse solution scales as 1/σ_min(M_{F,h}), which is also exponential under stochastic dynamics. The proposed weighted L₂ construction (Eq. 4) achieves polynomial bounds under L₂ outcome coverage but retains an undesirable √S dependence even in the on-policy case (C_{F,V} ≤ H√S). The L∞ construction (Eq. 5) eliminates the S dependence, requires no additional regularity assumption, and exactly recovers V_F = R⁺ when π_b = π_e (C_{F,∞} = 1). For algorithms, the original FDVF approach (Theorem 2) requires Bellman completeness and a helper class Ξ to handle double-sampling, while the new algorithm (Theorem 9) replaces this with a weight-realizability assumption on W, avoiding the double-sampling issue entirely at the cost of requiring L∞ belief coverage instead of the weaker L₂ version.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the future-dependent value function (FDVF) framework of Uehara et al. [2022a], which proposed using latent-state density ratios as the coverage condition for OPE in POMDPs to avoid the exponential cumulative importance weights of standard IS. The key departure is showing that the FDVF guarantee still hides exponential dependencies in the boundedness of the FDVF itself and in the IV(V) conversion term, and resolving this through two novel coverage assumptions: outcome coverage (concerning future dynamics) and belief coverage (concerning past dynamics). The new MIS-like algorithm in Section 5.3 also draws on the marginalized importance sampling lineage for MDPs (Liu et al. 2018, Uehara et al. 2020), adapting the weight-realizability structure to the POMDP setting via effective history weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the missing theoretical pieces that make the FDVF framework for POMDP OPE actually polynomial in the horizon, resolving what the authors call the 'curses of future and history.' The conceptual insight that L1/L∞ Hölder (exploiting the L1 normalization of belief and outcome vectors) is the correct tool rather than L2 Hölder (natural for linear MDPs) is a structural observation that may inform other POMDP analyses. Open questions include handling policies with rich structured memories (e.g., belief-state memories) without incurring a 'curse of memory,' and whether the coverage assumptions can be verified or estimated from data in practice. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation.
