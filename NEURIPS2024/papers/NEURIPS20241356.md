## Problem

While finiteness of the Daniely-Shalev-Shwartz (DS) dimension characterizes PAC learnability of multiclass concept classes, the optimal sample complexity remained unresolved with polylog factor gaps between upper and lower bounds. Specifically, the best known upper bound had a factor of (log(log(n)) + √d·log(d))·log²(n) beyond the conjectured Θ((d + log(1/δ))/ε) rate, and no explicit matching lower bound on sample complexity existed in the literature.

## Input / Output

The multiclass learner receives a labeled training sequence S = ((x₁,y₁),…,(xₙ,yₙ)) drawn i.i.d. from a realizable distribution over X×Y, along with a concept class H ⊆ Y^X, and outputs a single label prediction for a test feature. The intermediate list learner outputs a menu μ: X → {Y ⊆ Y : |Y| ≤ k}, i.e., a set of at most k candidate labels per feature, whose error is the probability of excluding the true label. The reduction splits the training sequence into thirds: the first third feeds the list learner, the last two thirds are used to count menu errors and resample for a final graph-dimension-based classifier.

## Method

The paper's main technical contribution is a reduction from multiclass learning to list learning (Theorem 2.7): a list learner is applied to the first third of samples to produce a menu, the number of menu errors N on the remaining two thirds is counted, and a restricted concept class H_{x'} of hypotheses making at most N+1 menu errors is learned via a graph-dimension-based algorithm on resampled data. To avoid the extra log(n/r) factor from standard sample compression, the authors extend da Cunha et al.'s randomized boosting to multiclass list learners (Algorithm 2), using the list sample compression scheme of Brukhim et al. as the weak learner, yielding a boosted list learner with error rate O(d^{3/2}·log(d)·log(n)/n) and menu size O((e√d)^{√d}·log(n)). As an alternative route to optimal rates, the authors introduce 'pivot shifting'—a multiclass generalization of Haussler's shifting that replaces only the last label in selected vertices to create a pivot—showing that if pivot shifting preserves DS dimension, the one-inclusion graph density is bounded by 2·dim(H), immediately implying Θ((d + log(1/δ))/ε) sample complexity. They also prove the optimal Θ(log(1/δ)/ε) rate for DS dimension 1 classes by showing their one-inclusion graphs are cycle-free.

## Results

The paper establishes a lower bound of Ω((d + log(1/δ))/ε) on the multiclass PAC sample complexity for any nondegenerate concept class of DS dimension d, and improves the upper bound from O(((d^{3/2} log d + d log log n) log² n + log(1/δ))/n) [Brukhim et al., 2022] to O((d^{3/2} log d log n + log(1/δ))/n), reducing the gap to a factor of √d log d log(d/ε). For concept classes of DS dimension 1, the paper proves the optimal Θ(log(1/δ)/ε) sample complexity. The improvement in the dependence on sample size n goes from O(log(log n) · log² n / n) to O(log n / n) when treating d as a constant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the state-of-the-art upper bound of Brukhim et al. (FOCS 2022), which achieved O(((d^{3/2} log d + d log log n) log² n + log(1/δ))/n). The new bound strictly improves the dependence on n from log(log n)·log² n to log n, and removes the d·log(log n) term entirely. The gap between the upper and lower bounds is narrowed from a factor of (log(log n) + √d log d)·log² n to √d log d log(d/ε). The paper also improves upon the list learning bound of Charikar and Pabbaraju (STOC 2023) by using their list sample compression scheme as a weak learner in a boosting framework (Algorithm 2), saving a log(d) factor compared to boosting the full multiclass learner directly. For DS dimension 1, the paper resolves the conjecture μ_H(n) ≤ 2·dim(H) and achieves the optimal Θ(log(1/δ)/ε) rate, which was not previously known.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of several threads in multiclass PAC learning theory. It builds directly on Brukhim et al. (FOCS 2022), who first characterized multiclass learnability via the DS dimension and provided the initial (but loose) upper bound, and on Charikar and Pabbaraju (STOC 2023), who extended the characterization to list learning via k-DS dimension. The paper's key technical move is a reduction from multiclass learning to list learning, combined with a sampled boosting scheme adapted from da Cunha et al. (2024) to the multiclass list setting, which together tighten the error rate dependence on sample size from O(log(log(n))·log²(n)/n) to O(log(n)/n). A second, independent route via one-inclusion graph density (following Daniely and Shalev-Shwartz 2014 and Aden-Ali et al. 2023) is pursued through a new 'pivot shifting' operator that generalizes Haussler's binary shifting to the multiclass case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a long-standing gap in multiclass PAC learning theory by proving the first matching lower bound Ω((d + log(1/δ))/ε) and reducing the upper-bound gap from polylogarithmic factors to a single log factor in the error parameter. Two open questions are posed that, if resolved positively, would yield the conjectured Θ((d + log(1/δ))/ε) sample complexity: (1) existence of a list learner with size and error rate independent of n, and (2) whether pivot shifting preserves the DS dimension. The result for DS dimension 1 classes (optimal Θ(log(1/δ)/ε) complexity) is a clean new theorem. However, the remaining d^{3/2}log(d)log(d/ε) gap means the optimal rate is still not achieved, and the pivot shifting approach remains unverified for general classes.
