## Problem

Contextual dynamic pricing requires a seller to set prices adaptively based on covariates while receiving only binary trade/no-trade feedback. Prior algorithms either imposed strong structural assumptions (log-concave noise CDF, quadratic reward behavior around optima, i.i.d. contexts) or achieved sub-optimal regret rates of Õ(T^{3/4}) in the linear valuation model, leaving open whether the minimax-optimal Õ(T^{2/3}) rate is attainable under minimal regularity (Lipschitz CDF only).

## Input / Output

At each round the algorithm receives a d-dimensional context vector x_t and, after posting a price, observes only a binary outcome o_t ∈ {0,1} indicating whether the trade occurred. The seller's price is constructed as p_t = ĝ(x_t) + δ_k, where ĝ is an online estimate of the expected valuation and δ_k is drawn from a finite grid of increments spaced by ε. The output is a sequence of posted prices whose cumulative regret against the best fixed pricing policy is bounded, with the bound depending on T, d, and the Hölder exponent β of the valuation function.

## Method

The VAPE (Valuation Approximation – Price Elimination) scheme adaptively alternates between two phases: a Valuation Approximation phase that posts uniformly random prices to obtain unbiased estimates of g(x_t) (via ridge regression for linear valuations or piecewise-constant estimators on a covering grid for Hölder valuations), and a Price Elimination phase that uses successive elimination with UCB/LCB confidence intervals over a discrete grid of price increments. The critical insight enabling optimal rates is cross-context information sharing: demand estimates D̂(δ_k) pool observations from all contexts that selected the same increment δ_k, decoupling the estimation of the demand function from the specific valuation level. This sharing, combined with an adaptive (rather than pre-specified) phase transition governed by an elliptical potential criterion, avoids the T^{3/4} bottleneck of independent per-context bandits or fixed explore-then-commit schemes, yielding Õ(T^{2/3}) regret for linear valuations and Õ(T^{(d+2β)/(d+3β)}) for β-Hölder non-parametric valuations under only Lipschitz-continuity of the noise CDF.

## Results

In the linear valuation model with only a Lipschitz CDF assumption on the noise, VAPE achieves a regret of $\tilde{O}(T^{2/3})$, matching the known minimax lower bound $\Omega(T^{2/3})$ and improving upon the previous best of $\tilde{O}(T^{3/4})$ under comparable or stronger assumptions. In the non-parametric model with a $\beta$-Hölder valuation function and Lipschitz noise CDF, VAPE achieves $\tilde{O}(T^{(d+2\beta)/(d+3\beta)})$ regret, the first result under such minimal assumptions (prior work [10] required quadratic behavior around optimal prices). The algorithm handles adversarial contexts without requiring i.i.d. context distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary empirical comparison is against the algorithm of Fan et al. [14] (JASA 2024), which also targets the Lipschitz-CDF linear pricing setting. In the stochastic setting (i.i.d. contexts in $\mathbb{R}^3$, truncated Gaussian noise), VAPE achieves lower regret rates at larger time horizons ($T$ up to 800,000), while [14]'s two-phase algorithm suffers from prohibitive computational costs that limit its evaluation to smaller horizons. In the adversarial setting (two orthogonal context vectors, fixed $\theta$), VAPE maintains consistent regret rates because its phases are adaptively defined, whereas [14]'s algorithm degrades significantly because its pre-committed exploration phase gathers information that becomes irrelevant in the exploitation phase. Theoretically, VAPE improves the regret rate from $\tilde{O}(T^{3/4})$ to $\tilde{O}(T^{2/3})$ in the linear model, matching the minimax lower bound, and provides the first non-parametric result under only Hölder continuity of the valuation and Lipschitz continuity of the noise CDF, removing the strong quadratic-behavior assumption required by [10].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the successive elimination framework (Even-Dar et al. 2006; Perchet & Rigollet 2013) for the price selection subroutine and on ridge regression concentration results from linear bandits (Abbasi-Yadkori et al. 2011) for valuation estimation. It departs from prior contextual dynamic pricing work (Cohen et al. 2020; Javanmard & Nazerzadeh 2019; Fan et al. 2024; Luo et al. 2022/2024; Xu & Wang 2022) by introducing the VAPE scheme, which adaptively alternates between valuation approximation and price elimination while sharing demand-function estimates across different valuation levels—a cross-learning principle not previously exploited in this setting. Unlike prior approaches that required known noise distributions, log-concavity, or quadratic behavior around optima, VAPE needs only a Lipschitz CDF, and unlike two-phase explore-then-commit methods, its phases are adaptive and handle adversarial contexts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For the linear valuation model, VAPE resolves an open problem by achieving the minimax-optimal T^{2/3} regret under minimal assumptions, matching the lower bound of Xu & Wang (2022) and improving on the T^{3/4} rates of all prior methods under comparable or weaker assumptions. For the non-parametric model, it provides the first regret bound under only Hölder-continuity of the valuation and Lipschitz-continuity of the noise CDF, though the rate T^{(d+2β)/(d+3β)} exceeds the T^{(d+β)/(d+2β)} rates known for non-parametric bandits with stronger assumptions, leaving optimality open. Key open questions include the optimal dimension dependence in the linear case, whether the non-parametric rate is tight, and extending results to non-i.i.d. or context-dependent noise distributions.
