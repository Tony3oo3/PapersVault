## Problem

Existing temporal action segmentation methods achieve strong accuracy but at prohibitive computational cost, driven by two factors: dense frame-wise processing throughout the network and multi-stage refinement with expensive post-processing (e.g., Viterbi decoding). This limits real-time and resource-constrained deployment. The paper asks whether TAS can be reframed as a sparse, query-based problem that preserves dense prediction quality while drastically reducing inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241192/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an untrimmed video V ∈ R^{T×3×H×W} and produces a frame-wise action label vector S ∈ R^T assigning one of K action classes to each frame. Internally, a frozen I3D encoder extracts 2048-dim per-frame features, which an 11-layer ASFormer decoder (output dim C=64) refines into dense frame embeddings. The Transformer decoder compresses these into M=100 (or 90 for GTEA) instance queries, each yielding a class probability vector, a T-length binary mask, and a shared T-length boundary probability vector from a global query aggregated via average pooling over instance queries.

## Method

BaFormer decouples frame-wise prediction into per-query mask classification and a class-agnostic boundary head, inspired by MaskFormer's mask-classification paradigm. A 10-layer Transformer decoder processes M trainable instance queries through Mask-Attention (soft-thresholded variant of Mask2Former's masked attention), Self-Attention, and FFN sub-layers, with multi-level features from the frame decoder injected at each layer. Training uses Hungarian matching between queries and ground-truth segments, with focal+dice mask loss and cross-entropy class loss, plus a Gaussian-smoothed boundary heatmap loss. At inference, the boundary head produces class-agnostic segment proposals via local peak detection; within each proposal, the query whose mask sums highest over that span is selected, and its predicted class is assigned to all frames in the segment—this boundary-aware query voting replaces expensive post-processing with a single O(M·N_b) pass, achieving 6% of DiffAct's inference time and 24% of UVAST's while matching or exceeding accuracy.

## Results

On 50Salads, BaFormer achieves 89.5% accuracy, 84.2 Edit score, and F1@50 of 83.9, while requiring only 0.139s inference time and 4.54G FLOPs. On GTEA, it reaches 83.0% accuracy and 88.7 Edit; on Breakfast, 76.6% accuracy and 77.3 Edit. Compared to the state-of-the-art multi-stage method DiffAct, BaFormer uses only 6% of the inference time (0.139s vs. 2.306s) and 10% of the FLOPs (4.54G vs. 43.94G) while delivering comparable or better accuracy across all three datasets. Against the single-stage UVAST with alignment decoder, BaFormer reduces inference time to 24% (0.139s vs. 0.577s) while improving accuracy by 20.8% on GTEA, 10% on 50Salads, and 8.4% on Breakfast.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BaFormer outperforms all multi-stage methods in accuracy on all three datasets: it surpasses ASFormer by 3.9% accuracy and 4.6% Edit on 50Salads, and exceeds DiffAct (the prior SOTA) by 0.6% accuracy on 50Salads while using only 6% of its inference time and 10% of its FLOPs. Against the closest single-stage competitor UVAST (with alignment decoder), BaFormer improves accuracy by 20.8% on GTEA (83.0% vs. 62.2%), 10% on 50Salads (89.5% vs. 79.5%), and 8.4% on Breakfast (76.6% vs. 68.2%), while running 4.2× faster (0.139s vs. 0.577s). On F1@50 for 50Salads, BaFormer (83.9) matches DiffAct (83.7) and exceeds ASFormer (76.0) by 7.9 points. On GTEA, BaFormer's 88.7 Edit score trails UVAST with Viterbi (92.1) but exceeds all other methods including DiffAct (89.6) and RTK (87.9). On Breakfast, BaFormer achieves the best accuracy (76.6%) among all single-stage methods and is competitive with multi-stage methods like DiffAct (76.4%) and RTK (73.3%). The key efficiency advantage is that BaFormer's single-stage design with boundary-aware query voting eliminates the need for iterative refinement or Viterbi decoding, making it the fastest method among those achieving >85% accuracy on 50Salads.

## Relation to Prior Work

BaFormer adapts the query-based mask classification paradigm from MaskFormer and Mask2Former (image segmentation) to temporal action segmentation, decoupling per-segment binary masks from class predictions via learnable instance queries. It departs from the dominant multi-stage TAS pipeline (MSTCN, ASFormer, DiffAct) that relies on repeating refinement modules and heavy post-processing (Viterbi, NMS), and from UVAST which uses query-based transcripts but still incurs costly sequence-to-sequence decoding. The key conceptual shift is reframing TAS as a sparse per-query detection problem with a class-agnostic global boundary query, rather than a dense per-frame classification problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BaFormer's 6% running time relative to DiffAct (while matching or exceeding accuracy) makes high-quality TAS feasible in real-time and resource-constrained settings, a practical gap that multi-stage methods have not addressed. The boundary-aware query voting mechanism is simple and interpretable, but the paper's own ablation (Table 5) reveals that boundary quality is the primary bottleneck: ground-truth boundaries yield a 6.4% accuracy gain over predicted ones, suggesting significant headroom if boundary prediction improves. Open questions include whether the query-based formulation scales to much longer videos, whether the slow training convergence of query-based Transformers can be mitigated, and whether the approach generalizes to datasets with more action classes or finer-grained temporal structure.
