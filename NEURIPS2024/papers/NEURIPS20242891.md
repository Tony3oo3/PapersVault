## Problem

Lip-syncing deepfakes (LipSync) forge only the lip region driven by audio without altering identity, making them far harder to detect than face-swapping deepfakes. Existing unimodal detectors miss the subtle artifacts, and prior multimodal methods naively align single-frame images with long-range audio clips, neglecting the fine-grained temporal inconsistencies that are the hallmark of LipSync forgeries. No dedicated dataset or detection method for this specific threat existed prior to this work.

## Input / Output

The model consumes T consecutive video frames (each convolved to 224×224) alongside their corresponding audio spectrogram, with each frame additionally cropped at three scales (full head at 1.0, face at 0.65, lip at 0.45) to capture multi-granularity visual cues. The output is a single binary real/fake probability produced by an MLP head. The temporal dimension is critical: the transformer encoder processes the full T-frame sequence jointly with the spectrogram rather than treating frames independently.

## Method

LipFD is a dual-headed architecture built on a CLIP-pretrained ViT-L/14 backbone. The Global Feature Encoder treats the lip-movement-to-spectrogram correlation analogously to long-range word dependencies in NLP, using self-attention over the T-frame sequence to flag temporally inconsistent lip shapes relative to the audio. The Global-Region Encoder (E_GR) processes the three crop scales (head, face, lip) and concatenates their features with the global embedding, while a Region Awareness module applies a sigmoid-gated weighted fusion that dynamically up-weights the lip crop for forged samples and the head/face crops for real ones. A custom Region Awareness loss (L_RA) explicitly penalizes the model for not assigning the highest weight to the lip region, encoding the prior that LipSync predominantly manipulates the lower face. Training minimizes a combined objective of binary cross-entropy classification loss and the region-awareness regularization, with the entire pipeline trained via transfer learning from CLIP weights.

## Results

LipFD achieves 95.27% accuracy and 93.08% AP on the proposed AVLips dataset, 95.10% accuracy on FF++, and 94.53% accuracy on DFDC, outperforming all six baselines on accuracy across all three benchmarks. The method also attains the lowest false negative rate (FNR) among all compared methods (0.04 on AVLips, 0.05 on FF++, 0.04 on DFDC), indicating strong detection sensitivity. In real-world WeChat video call scenarios with <100ms latency, LipFD reaches 90.18% accuracy on streaming media. Cross-manipulation generalization to unseen generators (MakeItTalk, Wav2Lip, TalkLip) yields accuracies of 96.93%, 95.27%, and 79.33% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LipFD outperforms the strongest multi-modal baseline RealForensics by 3.49% ACC on AVLips (95.27 vs 91.78), 1.53% on FF++ (95.10 vs 93.57), and 1.99% on DFDC (94.53 vs 92.54), while also achieving a lower FNR on all three datasets. Against the lip-specific baseline LipForensics, LipFD leads by 9.14% ACC on AVLips (95.27 vs 86.13) and 3.78% on DFDC (94.53 vs 90.75), but trails on FF++ ACC by 1.07% (95.10 vs 94.03). On the AP metric, LipFD surpasses RealForensics on AVLips (93.08 vs 90.14) but falls short on FF++ (76.98 vs 91.32) and DFDC (78.61 vs 91.62), which the authors attribute to those datasets emphasizing large-scale face swaps rather than subtle lip inconsistencies. Single-frame image-based detectors (UniversalFakeDetect, SelfBlendedImages) perform near chance level (~50% ACC) on all three datasets, confirming that temporal audio-visual cues are essential for LipSync detection. Video-based baselines CViT and DoubleStream achieve moderate accuracy (62–91%) but are consistently outperformed by LipFD, with the gap most pronounced on AVLips where CViT scores only 65.54%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a specific sub-problem within DeepFake detection: identifying LipSync forgeries that preserve identity and lack the visual artifacts exploited by unimodal detectors (CViT, DoubleStream, UniversalFakeDetect). It departs from prior multi-modal audio-visual inconsistency methods (RealForensics, audio-visual dissonance approaches) by shifting from coarse single-frame alignment to fine-grained *temporal* inconsistency between lip movements and spectrogram energy, and by introducing a Region Awareness mechanism that mimics human biological attention to lip-head coupling. The backbone leverages CLIP ViT-L/14 pretrained representations, repurposing vision-language transfer learning for a forensic detection task rather than its original retrieval purpose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of AVLips (340K audio-visual samples spanning four SOTA LipSync generators) fills a critical gap: no prior public dataset was dedicated to LipSync detection with synchronized audio. Practically, the demonstrated 90.18% accuracy in WeChat video call scenarios highlights an urgent real-world threat vector (financial fraud, impersonation) that general DeepFake detectors miss entirely. However, the paper reveals significant limitations: accuracy drops sharply for non-English speech (Chinese: 72.53% vs. English: 81.67% at 100ms latency) and degrades substantially under high network latency (>200ms), leaving multilingual and real-time deployment as open challenges that limit immediate broad applicability.
