## Problem

In Mixture-of-Experts models, the majority of well-trained experts remain inactive during inference under standard top-k routing, leaving their capacity underutilized. The paper shows that naively activating more experts does not reliably improve—and can even degrade—output quality, and that different routing strategies produce substantially divergent next-token distributions, particularly on reasoning-critical tokens. The central question is how to convert the negative or conflicting contributions of unchosen experts into a net positive for generation quality.

## Input / Output

At inference time, SCMoE takes a standard text prompt and a pre-trained MoE model (Mixtral 8x7B or DeepSeekMoE-16B) and produces a generated text response. Internally, for each decoding step the model is run twice—once under top-2 routing (strong activation) and once under rank-k routing (weak activation)—and the two sets of pre-softmax logits are combined into a single adjusted logit vector that drives the next-token selection. A vocabulary mask (α = 0.1) restricts the contrast to tokens whose top-2 logit is within a threshold of the maximum, keeping the search space focused.

## Method

SCMoE is a training-free, MoE-native decoding strategy that performs self-contrast between the model's own strong and weak activations. For each token position, the adjusted logit is computed as z_sc = (1+β)·z_top-2 − β·z_rank-k, where β controls the contrastive penalty strength and z_top-2 / z_rank-k are the pre-softmax logits from top-2 and rank-k routing respectively. The motivation comes from a KLD analysis showing that the distributional gap between top-2 and rank-k routing is largest on reasoning tokens (e.g., mathematical expressions) but small on function words, so the contrast selectively amplifies the model's reasoning signal. A vocabulary mask (α = 0.1) further restricts the contrast to high-confidence tokens to avoid introducing noise. The method requires no additional parameters, no separate amateur model, and incurs only ~1.3× the latency of greedy decoding, while consistently outperforming routing-based and search-based baselines across GSM8K, StrategyQA, MBPP, and HumanEval.

## Results

On Mixtral 8x7B, SCMoE achieves 66.94 accuracy on GSM8K (+5.15 over greedy), 76.29 on StrategyQA (+3.46), 48.80 pass@1 on MBPP (+2.60), and 41.46 pass@1 on HumanEval (+7.92). Combining SCMoE with self-consistency further improves major@20 accuracy on GSM8K from 75.59 to 78.31 (+2.72). In terms of efficiency, SCMoE incurs only a 1.30× latency overhead compared to greedy decoding (65.47s vs 50.32s per 512 tokens), which is lower than contrastive search (1.62×) and contrastive decoding (1.43×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCMoE outperforms all five baselines across all four benchmarks. Against Dynamic Routing (Huang et al.), SCMoE leads by +5.83 on GSM8K, +1.88 on StrategyQA, +1.00 on MBPP, and +3.05 on HumanEval. Against Contrastive Search (Su et al.), SCMoE gains +5.98 on GSM8K, +1.44 on StrategyQA, +2.60 on MBPP, and +4.87 on HumanEval. DoLa (Chuang et al.) performs worst among all baselines, falling below greedy on GSM8K (49.96 vs 61.79) and HumanEval (12.80 vs 33.54) due to generation degeneration, while SCMoE surpasses it by +16.98 and +28.66 respectively. Contrastive Decoding (Li et al.) with Mistral-7B as the amateur model only marginally beats greedy on GSM8K (+0.45) and actually underperforms on MBPP (−1.00), whereas SCMoE improves on all four tasks. Finally, SCMoE (67.94) matches or exceeds vanilla self-consistency major@5 (66.87) on GSM8K with a single forward pass, and when combined with self-consistency, boosts major@20 from 75.59 to 78.31.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCMoE sits at the intersection of MoE routing research and contrastive decoding. On the routing side, it departs from prior work on dynamic routing (Huang et al., 2024) and ensemble routing, which simply activate more experts, by showing that unchosen experts can be *harmful* when naively included. On the contrastive decoding side, it extends the lineage of Contrastive Decoding (Li et al., 2022), DoLa (Chuang et al., 2023), and Contrastive Search (Su et al., 2022), but replaces the external amateur model or premature layer with the MoE model's own alternative routing strategy (rank-k vs. top-2), making the contrast entirely self-contained and training-free. The KLD analysis connecting routing-strategy divergence to reasoning-token difficulty is a new diagnostic that motivates the method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCMoE offers a practical, training-free decoding trick that consistently improves MoE reasoning with only 1.3× latency overhead, and it generalizes across architectures (Mixtral 8x7B and DeepSeekMoE-16B). The finding that simply activating more experts degrades performance on some tasks challenges the common assumption that more capacity at inference is always beneficial. Key limitations include evaluation only on 7B–16B models (the authors note they could not test Mixtral 8x22B or DeepSeek-V2), and the method's benefit is most pronounced on tasks with explicit reasoning chains, leaving open whether it helps on broader knowledge-intensive benchmarks like MMLU. Whether the self-contrast signal remains informative at larger scale or with more experts per layer is an important open question.
