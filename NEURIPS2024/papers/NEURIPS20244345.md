## Problem

Despite rapid progress in SNNs for individual computer vision tasks, no unified spiking neural network has been demonstrated for the full autonomous driving pipeline (perception, prediction, and planning) under the strict 50–60 W/h energy budgets that real vehicles face. This paper addresses that gap by building the first end-to-end SNN that replaces all temporal-mixing operators (GRUs, 3D convolutions) with spiking neurons, targeting the energy and latency constraints that make neuromorphic hardware attractive for safety-critical automotive deployment.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a 6-camera surround-view RGB video sequence (3 past frames at 224×480 resolution, 1.0 s of context) and produces three coupled outputs: a spatiotemporal BEV segmentation map (drivable area, lanes, vehicles, pedestrians), future semantic and instance segmentation predictions for the next 4 frames (2.0 s horizon), and a continuous steering-and-acceleration trajectory. The encoder processes frames via Sequence Repetition (each frame repeated T times to create virtual timesteps, matching its ImageNet pre-training), while the decoder streams new frames sequentially via Sequential Alignment, giving the LIF neurons a genuine temporal-mixing role.

## Method

SAD integrates three SNN modules trained end-to-end with a composite loss. The perception encoder is a 12-layer Spiking Token Mixer (inverted-bottleneck spiking CNN, 12 M params, 72.1 % ImageNet top-1) that extracts feature and depth embeddings per camera, fused into a BEV frustum; the decoder uses an MS-ResNet spiking backbone with task-specific heads. The prediction module employs a novel dual-pathway of LIF neurons: one pathway models future multi-modality through a conditional Gaussian (mean and variance heads over the current BEV), and the other compensates for information gaps by concatenating all prior spiking outputs; the two streams are element-wise added after separate Conv2d+BN+LIF blocks. The planning module generates candidate trajectories via a bicycle model scored by a multi-component cost (occupancy, comfort, traffic rules), then refines the best trajectory with a Spiking Gated Recurrent Unit (SGRU) whose Heaviside-gated hidden state preserves binary sparsity. Training proceeds in three stages (perception → prediction → planning) with learnable loss weights, and the resulting system achieves 7.33× and 75.03× lower estimated energy than Freespace and ST-P3 respectively while remaining competitive in L2 trajectory error and collision rate.

## Results

On nuScenes, SAD achieves a mean BEV segmentation IoU of 35.62% in perception, outperforming VED (28.19%), VPN (30.36%), PON (30.52%), and Lift-Splat (34.61%) but trailing ST-P3 (42.69%). For planning, SAD reports L2 errors of 1.53/2.35/3.21 m at 1s/2s/3s and collision rates of 0.62/1.26/2.38%, comparable to ST-P3 (1.33/2.11/2.90 m; 0.23/0.62/1.27%). The headline energy result is 46.92 mJ per inference, which is 7.33× lower than Freespace (344.11 mJ) and 75.03× lower than ST-P3 (3520.40 mJ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In perception (Table 1), SAD's 35.62% mean IoU surpasses VED by 7.43 points, VPN by 5.26, PON by 5.10, and Lift-Splat by 1.01, but falls short of IVMP (36.76%), FIERY (40.18%), and ST-P3 (42.69%). In prediction (Table 2), SAD (32.74% IoU, 20.00% PQ) trails ST-P3 (38.63% IoU, 31.72% PQ) and FIERY (37.00% IoU, 30.20% PQ) on all metrics, though it slightly exceeds the static baseline (32.20% IoU). In planning (Table 3), SAD's L2 error is marginally worse than NMP and Freespace at 1 s but comparable to ST-P3 at 3 s; its 3 s collision rate (2.38%) is higher than ST-P3 (1.27%) and Freespace (1.64%). The decisive advantage is energy: SAD consumes 46.92 mJ versus 344.11 mJ for Freespace (7.33× reduction) and 3520.40 mJ for ST-P3 (75.03× reduction), validating the core claim that SNN-based end-to-end driving can match ANN planning quality at a fraction of the energy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SNN literature from individual vision tasks (classification, detection, segmentation) to the full autonomous driving pipeline, building on ST-P3's cost-function-based planning framework and the spiking patch-embedding ideas from Spike-driven Transformer and Spikformer. It departs from prior SNN-in-driving work, which was limited to PID controllers, lane-keeping, or collision avoidance in simulation, by replacing all temporal-mixing operators (GRUs, 3D convolutions) with LIF spiking neurons and introducing a novel dual-pathway prediction module with conditional Gaussian uncertainty. The Spiking Token Mixer encoder is a new architecture that substitutes dense layers for self-attention, achieving competitive ImageNet accuracy with fewer parameters than prior spiking transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAD demonstrates that a fully spiking network can handle the coupled perception–prediction–planning pipeline of autonomous driving, a first for neuromorphic computing in this domain, and reports a 75× theoretical energy reduction over ST-P3. However, prediction quality (PQ 20.0 vs. 31.7 for ST-P3) reveals a substantial gap that limits real-world safety, and the energy figures are derived from sparsity-based FLOP estimates rather than measured on neuromorphic hardware. The authors acknowledge that on-vehicle validation is still needed, leaving open whether the theoretical efficiency translates to practical deployment and whether SNNs can close the prediction gap without sacrificing their sparsity advantage.
