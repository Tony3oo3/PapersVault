## Problem

Pretrained video diffusion models are trained on short clips (typically 16–24 frames), making it difficult to generate longer videos without quality degradation. Existing chunked autoregressive approaches condition only on the last one or two frames, leading to temporal inconsistency and discontinuous motion across chunk boundaries. This paper asks whether a training-free inference technique can extend short-clip video diffusion models to generate arbitrarily long, temporally coherent videos.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt (or a sequence of prompts for multi-scene generation) and produces an arbitrarily long video, demonstrated up to 10,000+ frames at resolutions such as 320×512 or 512×512. It operates on a sliding window of f frames matching the pretrained model's training length (e.g., 16 for VideoCrafter2), generating exactly one decoded frame per iteration. Memory consumption remains constant at O(f) regardless of target video length N, and the method parallelizes across multiple GPUs via latent partitioning.

## Method

FIFO-Diffusion introduces diagonal denoising, which maintains a queue of f latents with monotonically increasing noise levels and, at each step, denoises them jointly before dequeuing the fully denoised frame at the head and enqueuing a new random-noise frame at the tail. To mitigate the training-inference gap (the model was trained on uniform noise levels), latent partitioning splits the queue into n blocks of size f, reducing the maximum noise-level spread per forward pass and enabling finer inference steps. Lookahead denoising further improves quality by updating only the noisier half of each block, so every frame is denoised with reference to at least f/2 cleaner preceding frames. The paper proves (Theorem 3.3) that the diagonal denoising error is bounded by the maximum noise-level difference, justifying latent partitioning, and shows empirically that lookahead denoising nearly eliminates the training-inference gap in noise prediction MSE.

## Results

On UCF-101, FIFO-Diffusion (using Latte as the base model with n=4 partitions) achieves an FVD₁₂₈ of 596.64 and an IS score of 74.44±1.17, outperforming PVDM-L (400-400s) which scores 648.4 FVD₁₂₈ and 74.40±1.25 IS, while requiring only 64 inference steps versus PVDM's 400. In terms of computational efficiency, FIFO-Diffusion maintains a constant 11,245 MB memory usage regardless of video length (128, 256, or 512 frames), compared to FreeNoise which grows from 26,163 MB to out-of-memory at 512 frames. With 8-GPU parallelism, FIFO-Diffusion achieves 1.84 s/frame inference time, faster than both FreeNoise (6.09 s/frame) and Gen-L-Video (22.07 s/frame).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIFO-Diffusion outperforms the strongest training-based baseline, PVDM-L (400-400s), by 51.8 points in FVD₁₂₈ (596.64 vs. 648.4) while matching its IS score (74.44 vs. 74.40), all without any additional training and with only 64 inference steps versus PVDM's 400. Against the training-free baselines, FIFO-Diffusion demonstrates a clear advantage in memory efficiency: it uses a constant 11,245 MB across all video lengths, whereas FreeNoise's memory grows linearly (26,163 MB at 128 frames to OOM at 512 frames) and Gen-L-Video, while nearly constant, is nearly 2× slower (22.07 vs. 12.37 s/frame on 1 GPU). In qualitative comparisons (Figure 7), FIFO-Diffusion produces smoother motion, higher frame quality, and greater scene diversity than FreeNoise, Gen-L-Video, and the LaVie+SEINE chunked autoregressive pipeline, which suffers from error accumulation and inter-chunk discontinuities. A user study with 70 evaluators confirms strong preference for FIFO-Diffusion over FreeNoise across all five criteria (overall preference, motion plausibility, motion magnitude, text fidelity, and aesthetic quality). On the MSR-VTT test set, optical flow analysis shows that over 65% of FreeNoise-generated videos fall in the lowest motion bin, while FIFO-Diffusion produces a much broader range of motion magnitudes.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIFO-Diffusion positions itself as a training-free inference technique that repurposes pretrained short-clip video diffusion models (VideoCrafter1/2, zeroscope, Open-Sora Plan, Latte) for arbitrarily long video generation, departing from the dominant chunked autoregressive paradigm (LaVie+SEINE, PVDM) that requires additional prediction subnetworks or fine-tuning. Conceptually, it is related to rolling diffusion models [20] and TEDI [36], which also use sliding windows with temporally varying noise levels, but those require training from scratch to accommodate heterogeneous noise levels. The key departure is the diagonal denoising mechanism—processing a FIFO queue of frames at monotonically increasing noise levels—which is applied purely at inference time, combined with latent partitioning and lookahead denoising to mitigate the resulting training-inference gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: FIFO-Diffusion enables infinite-length video generation with constant memory (O(f) regardless of target length N) and straightforward multi-GPU parallelism, removing the need for expensive retraining or additional prediction models. The authors acknowledge that the training-inference gap persists despite latent partitioning, and suggest integrating diagonal denoising into the training phase as future work, which could further close the gap. An open question is whether the approach generalizes to higher resolutions, longer temporal contexts, or interactive/conditional generation settings beyond the 16-frame pretrained models tested here.
