## Problem

The price of anarchy of first-price auctions in the autobidding world—where bidders are a mix of traditional utility maximizers and ROI-constrained value maximizers (autobidders)—was previously uncharacterized. While the PoA for second-price auctions with autobidders (1/2) and for first-price auctions with only utility maximizers (1−1/e²) were known, the interaction between the two bidder types under the first-price format remained an open question, particularly because value maximizers can overbid in ways that break standard utility-maximizer analyses.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input a set of n bidders partitioned into utility maximizers B_u and value maximizers B_v, their values v_{i,j} for each of m auctions, and optionally machine-learned reserve signals s_j ∈ [γ·max_i v_{i,j}, max_i v_{i,j}]. The output is a tight instance-wise PoA bound: the worst-case ratio of equilibrium welfare to first-best liquid welfare. In the mixed setting without reserves, the bound is the closed-form optimization min_{t∈[0,1]} (1+t ln t)/(2−t+t ln t) ≈ 0.457; with ML reserves of accuracy γ, the bound smoothly increases to 1 as γ→1.

## Method

The core technique is a novel local analysis (Lemma 3) that characterizes the value-payment tradeoff in a first-price auction when the rightful winner is a utility maximizer. It constructs a value-payment frontier H (the concave envelope of the deterministic frontier G) and uses a best-response characterization (Lemma 2) to show that H must be flat (slope ≤ 1) to the right of the equilibrium operating point, which forces the CDF of competing bids to have small slope and hence forces other bidders' payments to be large. This local tradeoff is then combined with a separate bound for value maximizers (Lemma 1) by decomposing welfare into four quantities—value to value maximizers, value to utility maximizers in their own auctions, payments in value-maximizer auctions, and payments by non-winners in utility-maximizer auctions—and solving a two-parameter optimization. A matching upper bound is constructed with just n=2 bidders and m=2 auctions. For the ML-advice extension, the reserve price r_j = s_j tightens both tradeoffs by a factor involving (1−γ), yielding a PoA bound that transitions monotonically from ≈0.457 (γ=0) to 1 (γ=1).

## Results

The paper establishes tight Price of Anarchy (PoA) bounds for first-price auctions in the autobidding world. With all bidders as value maximizers (full autobidding), the PoA is exactly 1/2, matching the known second-price auction bound. In the mixed autobidding world (both utility and value maximizers), the PoA degrades to min_{t∈[0,1]} (1+t ln t)/(2−t+t ln t) ≈ 0.457, which is strictly worse than the 1/2 bound for second-price auctions. With machine-learned reserves of accuracy γ, the PoA improves smoothly from ≈0.457 (γ=0) to 1 (γ=1), approximately linearly in γ.

| Mechanism | First-price auction | Second-price auction |
|---|---|---|
| Full autobidding | 1/2 | 1/2 [Aggarwal et al., 2019] |
| Mixed autobidding | min_{t∈[0,1]} (1+t ln t)/(2−t+t ln t) ≈ 0.457 | 1/2 [Balseiro et al., 2021b] |
| No autobidding | 1−1/e² ≈ 0.865 [Jin and Lu, 2022] | 1 [Vickrey, 1961] |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the second-price auction in the mixed autobidding world (Balseiro et al., 2021b, PoA = 1/2), the first-price auction is strictly less efficient at ≈0.457, revealing a format disadvantage when both bidder types coexist. In the full autobidding setting, both formats tie at PoA = 1/2, so the first-price auction is no worse than the second-price auction when all bidders are value maximizers. The paper complements Jin and Lu (2022), who showed PoA = 1−1/e² ≈ 0.865 for first-price auctions with only utility maximizers, by quantifying the efficiency loss (from 0.865 to 0.457) introduced by the presence of autobidders. Against the classic Vickrey (1961) result of PoA = 1 for second-price auctions with utility maximizers, the first-price auction already loses efficiency (0.865) even without autobidding, and the loss compounds further with autobidders. The paper also extends Liaw et al. (2022), who proved PoA = 1/2 for first-price auctions restricted to pure (deterministic) Nash equilibria in full autobidding, by showing the same 1/2 bound holds for general equilibria with randomized bidding strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent research threads: the autobidding literature (Aggarwal et al. 2019, Deng et al. 2021, Balseiro et al. 2021b) that analyzed second-price auctions with value maximizers, and the first-price auction PoA literature (Jin and Lu 2022, Liaw et al. 2022) that studied efficiency under different bidder compositions. It extends Liaw et al.'s result from pure Nash equilibria to general (possibly randomized) equilibria in the full autobidding setting, and introduces a novel local analysis via value-payment frontiers to handle the mixed autobidding world where both utility and value maximizers coexist. The machine-learned reserves section directly builds on Balseiro et al.'s signal model, adapting their second-price auction framework to first-price auctions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key finding that the mixed autobidding world yields a strictly worse PoA (~0.457) than either the full autobidding world (1/2) or the no-autobidding world (~0.865) is a non-monotonicity that has practical implications for ad platform design as the industry transitions between auction formats and bidding paradigms. The tight characterization (matching upper and lower bounds) and the smooth improvement from machine-learned reserves provide concrete guidance for sellers on how much efficiency they can recover with imperfect value predictions. Open questions include whether similar non-monotonicities arise in other auction formats (e.g., GSP), whether the results extend to budget-constrained autobidders, and whether the smoothness framework can be formally instantiated to simplify the analysis.
