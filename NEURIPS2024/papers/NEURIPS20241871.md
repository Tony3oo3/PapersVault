## Problem

Prior gradient-based training of two-layer neural networks for single-index models under Gaussian data required n ≳ d^Θ(p) samples, where p is the information exponent of the link function, leaving a large gap from the information-theoretic limit of n ≃ d. The correlational statistical query (CSQ) lower bound suggested this gap was fundamental for SGD with squared loss. This paper asks whether SGD with reused training data can escape the CSQ barrier and learn single-index polynomials near the information-theoretic limit.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. samples (x_i, y_i) where x_i ~ N(0, I_d) and y_i = σ_*(⟨x_i, θ⟩) + ζ_i, with σ_* an unknown degree-q polynomial link function and θ an unknown unit direction in R^d. The output is a two-layer network f_Θ(x) = (1/N)Σ a_j σ_j(⟨x, w_j⟩ + b_j) with polynomial activations σ_j (randomized sums of Hermite polynomials up to degree C_σ) that achieves population squared loss o_d(1). The algorithm requires only knowledge of the degree q (not the Hermite coefficients of σ_*), and the activation functions are randomized per neuron to satisfy sign conditions without knowing σ_* explicitly.

## Method

The core mechanism is a layer-wise SGD procedure where the same training example is reused across two consecutive gradient steps on the first-layer weights. This batch reuse generates a non-correlational term in the update that, via Taylor expansion of the activation derivative, effectively applies a monomial transformation y → y^I to the labels, reducing the information exponent from p to at most 2 (the generative exponent p_*). A negative-momentum interpolation step between iterates stabilizes the signal-to-noise ratio under normalization. Phase I runs normalized SGD for Õ(d) steps to achieve weak then strong recovery of the index direction θ; Phase II fits the second-layer coefficients via ridge regression with random bias units to approximate σ_*. The result is n = Õ(d) sample and runtime complexity for arbitrary single-index polynomials, matching the information-theoretic limit up to polylog factors.

## Results

For arbitrary polynomial single-index models (degree q, information exponent p), the paper establishes a sample and runtime complexity of n = T = Õ(d), which matches the information-theoretic limit up to polylogarithmic factors. More generally, for link functions with generative exponent p*, the sample complexity is n = Õ(d^{(p*-1)∨1}). This breaks the correlational statistical query (CSQ) lower bound of n ≳ d^{p/2} and improves upon prior SGD results of n ≳ d^{Θ(p)}, since p* ≤ 2 for all polynomials while p can be arbitrarily large.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against the CSQ lower bound (n ≳ d^{p/2}) and prior gradient-based learning results (n ≳ d^{Θ(p)}). For polynomial link functions, this work achieves n = Õ(d), matching the information-theoretic limit and the SQ algorithm of [CM20], while the CSQ lower bound would predict n ≳ d^{p/2} (e.g., n ≳ d^{3/2} for p=3). The key mechanism is that SGD with batch reuse implements non-correlational label transformations (monomial transformations via Taylor expansion of the activation), effectively reducing the information exponent to p* ≤ 2. For non-polynomial link functions with p* > 2, the paper's rate n = Õ(d^{p*-1}) matches the SQ lower bound of [DPVLB24] and can be viewed as an SQ analogue of the online SGD rate n = Õ(d^{p-1}) from [BAGJ21], but governed by the generative exponent p* rather than the information exponent p. The empirical results in Figure 1 confirm the theory: same-batch GD reaches low test error with n ≃ d samples for a p=3 target, while online SGD fails to achieve even weak recovery with n = Ω(d²) samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the observation from DTA+24 that reusing the same minibatch in two consecutive gradient steps generates non-correlational (non-CSQ) terms in the SGD update, which implicitly transform the labels. It extends this from a weak-recovery statement for specific link functions to a full statistical guarantee for arbitrary polynomial single-index models, by showing that the non-CSQ term implements a monomial transformation (via Taylor expansion of the activation) that reduces the information exponent to at most 2. The analysis draws on the SQ algorithm of CM20 (which achieves near-optimal sample complexity via label thresholding) and the generative exponent framework of DPVLB24, but shows that SGD with batch reuse can replicate the effect of these non-CSQ transformations without explicit label preprocessing. The layer-wise training strategy and random bias units follow the standard framework of DLS22, BES+22, and AAM23.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes the gap between the information-theoretic limit (n ≃ d) and what gradient-based training of neural networks can achieve for polynomial single-index models, disproving the conjecture in AAM23 that n ≃ d^{p/2} is optimal for ERM with SGD on squared loss. The broader conceptual implication—that SGD with data reuse can implement full SQ algorithms beyond the CSQ class—reframes how we think about the computational power of gradient descent. Key open questions include whether standard multi-pass SGD (without the specific layer-wise schedule and batch-reuse protocol) achieves the same efficiency, whether the result extends to multi-index or hierarchical targets, and whether the monomial transformation mechanism generalizes to other loss functions or network architectures.
