## Problem

Despite the widespread use of the Gauss-Newton (GN) matrix as a preconditioner in adaptive optimizers (K-FAC, Shampoo, Sophia), no prior work had provided a theoretical characterization of how the GN condition number depends on fundamental architectural choices such as depth, width, skip connections, and data covariance. This gap left practitioners without a principled understanding of why certain architectures (e.g., residual networks, wider layers) train more easily, and why ill-conditioning arises in deep networks.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input the singular value spectra of each layer's weight matrix (or products of consecutive layers), the empirical input data covariance Σ, and architectural hyperparameters (depth L, hidden width m, skip-connection weight β). It produces closed-form upper bounds on the pseudo-condition number of the GN matrix, expressed as a convex combination of per-layer condition numbers weighted by products of minimum singular values. The bounds are agnostic to specific parameter values, depending only on spectral quantities, and are validated empirically on whitened MNIST (d=196) and CIFAR-10 (d=64, grayscale) at initialization and throughout training.

## Method

The paper decomposes the GN matrix of an L-layer linear network into a sum of Kronecker products of weight-matrix products (building on Singh et al. 2021), then applies Weyl's and dual Weyl's inequalities to bound the largest and smallest eigenvalues separately, yielding a tight upper bound expressed as a convex combination of per-layer condition numbers (Lemma 2). The convex-combination structure is shown to be essential: a naive max bound is vacuous due to a 'self-balancing' effect where layers with large condition numbers simultaneously have small minimum singular values and thus receive small weights. For residual networks, the skip connection shifts each layer's singular values by β, provably reducing the condition number (Eq. 8). The analysis extends to two-layer Leaky-ReLU networks via a unit-network decomposition and a novel lower bound on sums of Kronecker products of PSD matrices, and to convolutional layers via Toeplitz matrix reformulation. Empirical validation on MNIST and CIFAR-10 confirms the bounds are tight at initialization and remain predictive throughout training.

## Results

The paper derives tight upper bounds on the pseudo-condition number of the Gauss-Newton matrix for linear networks of arbitrary depth L and width m. For a one-hidden layer network, the bound is κ(G_O) ≤ κ(Σ)·(σ²_max(W)+σ²_max(V))/(σ²_min(W)+σ²_min(V)), which under Kaiming initialization with m ≫ max{d,k} simplifies to κ(G_O) ≈ κ(Σ). For L-layer networks, the condition number grows quadratically with depth at fixed width, but scaling width proportionally with depth compensates for this growth. Empirically, the condition number remains in the range of 6–12 throughout 5000 epochs of training on CIFAR-10 (a ~50% change from initialization), and the derived convex-combination bound stays tight across the entire training trajectory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external method baselines; instead, it compares architectural variants to isolate the effect of each component on GN conditioning. Adding skip connections (residual networks with β=1) strictly reduces the condition number relative to the equivalent linear network, as shown both analytically (Eq. 8: each per-layer ratio (σ_max+β)/(σ_min+β) < σ_max/σ_min) and empirically on whitened MNIST and CIFAR-10. Batch Normalization similarly improves conditioning on non-whitened CIFAR-10 (d=64, n=1000), with the improvement persisting as width increases. The convex-combination bound in Lemma 2 is empirically much tighter than the naive maximum bound, thanks to a 'self-balancing' effect where layers with large condition numbers carry small weights γ_ℓ in the sum. Finally, replacing linear activations with Leaky ReLU (α=0.01) in a one-hidden layer network improves conditioning relative to the linear case, though the condition number then increases with width rather than decreasing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Singh et al. (2021), which established the GN matrix decomposition for linear networks and analyzed Hessian rank, by deriving the first tight upper bounds on the GN condition number as an explicit function of depth, width, and architectural components. It departs from prior Hessian spectrum analyses (Pennington & Bahri, Jacot et al.) that were limited to asymptotic or infinite-width settings, and from empirical studies of conditioning (Ghorbani et al., Yao et al.) that lacked theoretical guarantees. The authors explicitly position their work as filling a gap: 'a detailed theoretical study on the Gauss-Newton conditioning of neural networks has been absent,' with no prior work addressing tight finite-width bounds for arbitrary-depth networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled theoretical explanation for why residual connections, width scaling, and data normalization improve optimization conditioning, moving these from empirical heuristics to provable spectral-shift arguments. Key limitations include: bounds are derived only for linear networks at arbitrary depth (non-linear analysis is restricted to two layers), the bounds are agnostic to training dynamics (cannot distinguish initialization from converged solutions), and no analytic treatment of transformers or normalization layers is provided. Open questions include extending the framework to deep non-linear networks, incorporating training dynamics into the bounds, and generalizing to the Generalized Gauss-Newton matrix relevant for cross-entropy loss.
