## Problem

Standard probabilistic graphical models (Bayesian Networks, Markov Random Fields) can only encode conditional independencies and cannot represent functional dependencies or give meaningful semantics to cyclic dependency structures. The Probabilistic Dependency Graph (PDG) formalism extended graphical models to directed hypergraphs but left a fundamental question unanswered: what does it mean for a distribution to be compatible with a given hypergraph structure? This paper provides a precise, causally grounded definition of such compatibility.

## Input / Output

The method takes a joint probability distribution μ over finite discrete variables and a directed hypergraph A (where each hyperarc specifies a set of source variables and a set of target variables, with targets allowed to overlap across hyperarcs). It produces a QIM-compatibility witness—an extended distribution over the original variables plus one independent noise variable per hyperarc—along with a corresponding generalized randomized PSEM. Additionally, it yields a necessary information-theoretic constraint (IDef_A(μ) ≤ 0) and a non-negative scoring function QIMInc that quantifies the distance from full compatibility, with equality iff the distribution is compatible.

## Method

The core contribution is Definition 2 (QIM-compatibility): a distribution μ is compatible with hypergraph A iff there exists an extension to independent noise variables {U_a} such that each target T_a is a deterministic function of its sources S_a and noise U_a. For DAGs, this reduces exactly to the conditional independencies of the corresponding Bayesian Network (Theorem 1), while multiple hyperarcs targeting the same variable encode functional dependencies (Theorem 2). The paper establishes a tight correspondence between QIM-compatibility witnesses and generalized randomized PSEMs (Propositions 3–4), showing that demonstrating compatibility is essentially constructing a causal model, and that this correspondence extends to interventions via conditioning on a specific do-event (Theorem 6). On the information-theoretic side, Theorem 7 proves IDef_A(μ) ≤ 0 as a universal necessary condition, and the QIMInc scoring function (Proposition 8) provides an entropy-based measure of incompatibility that is bounded above and below by IDef on a transformed hypergraph A† (Theorem 9), linking the framework to PDG semantics.

## Results

The paper establishes several key theoretical equivalences rather than empirical benchmarks. Theorem 1 shows QIM-compatibility with a DAG's hypergraph is equivalent to satisfying the corresponding Bayesian network's conditional independencies. Theorem 7 provides a universal necessary condition: if μ is QIM-compatible with hypergraph A, then the information deficiency IDef_A(μ) ≤ 0, which subsumes all known incompatibility proofs. Theorem 9 bounds the QIMInc score (which equals zero iff μ is QIM-compatible) between IDef_A(μ) and IDef_{A†}(ν), connecting the new scoring function to the qualitative PDG framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or quantitative comparisons. The paper positions QIM-compatibility relative to existing frameworks by establishing equivalences: it reduces to standard BN independencies for acyclic graphs (Theorem 1), generalizes Pearl's causal models via generalized randomized PSEMs (Propositions 3–4), and subsumes the qualitative PDG scoring function IDef (Theorem 9). The paper does not benchmark against competing methods but instead shows that QIM-compatibility unifies and extends the semantics of BNs, PDGs, and causal models into a single framework that also handles cyclic structures and functional dependencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Pearl's causal modeling framework (SEMs/PSEMs) and the Probabilistic Dependency Graph (PDG) formalism of Richardson and Halpern, which introduced the IDef scoring function but left the fundamental question of distributional compatibility with a directed hypergraph unanswered. It also draws on the information-theoretic literature on interaction information (Williams & Beer, James & Crutchfield) and the classical theory of Bayesian Network independencies. The key departure is the introduction of QIM-compatibility as a precise semantic notion for directed hypergraphs, unifying causal mechanisms, functional dependencies, and information-theoretic constraints under a single definition that the prior PDG formalism lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the missing semantic foundation for PDGs and gives cyclic causal structures a rigorous distributional interpretation, resolving a longstanding gap in the literature. The most impactful open question is computational: determining whether a given distribution is QIM-compatible with a hypergraph appears to require solving a non-convex optimization over exponentially many variables, and no efficient algorithm is known. The finding that the XOR distribution (with negative interaction information) is nonetheless compatible with a purely pairwise causal structure challenges a widely-held intuition in information theory and suggests the information profile may capture more structural content than previously believed, though the converse of Theorem 7 does not hold, leaving the full characterization of compatible distributions open.
