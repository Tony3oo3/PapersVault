## Problem

Foster et al. (2021) proved that no algorithm can achieve polynomial sample complexity for offline RL under linear q^π-realizability and concentrability when data consists of individual transitions. The open question was whether this impossibility could be overcome if the offline data were provided as full trajectories rather than isolated transitions. This paper answers affirmatively, showing that trajectory data is sufficient for statistically efficient policy optimization in this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242648/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The learner receives n full-length trajectories (state, action, reward sequences from initial to terminal state) collected by a single unknown policy, plus d-dimensional feature vectors φ(s_h, a) for every action a at each visited state s_h (not just the taken action). It outputs a deterministic memoryless policy π′ that is greedy with respect to the selected q-value parameters. A key structural requirement is that trajectories must be complete (from s_1 to s_⊤), which enables the algorithm to simulate arbitrary-length state-skipping within a single trajectory sample.

## Method

The core mechanism is a probabilistic 'skipping' procedure that transforms the q^π-realizable MDP into an approximately linear MDP by automatically taking the data-collection policy's action at low-range states and summing rewards along skipped paths. Trajectory data is essential here: it allows constructing least-squares targets of the form E_τ[r_{h:τ−1} + f(s_τ)] that simulate skipping of arbitrary length within one sample, making these targets linearly realizable under the true skipping mechanism G̅. The algorithm solves an optimization problem over all possible skipping guesses G ∈ G, imposing a tightness constraint (Eq. 14) that rejects guesses producing inconsistent q-estimates; the true guess G̅ provably passes this constraint. Among feasible guesses, it selects the one maximizing the optimistic value at s_1, then outputs a greedy policy. The sample complexity is Õ(C_conc⁴ H⁷ d⁴ / ε²) trajectories, independent of state-space size.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1) establishes that under linear q^π-realizability, concentrability (coefficient C_conc), and full-length trajectory data, a dataset of n = Õ(C_conc⁴ H⁷ d⁴ / ε²) trajectories suffices to output an ε-optimal policy with probability at least 1−δ. This is the first positive sample complexity result in the setting of linear q^π-realizability + concentrability, overcoming the exponential lower bound of Foster et al. [2021] which applies to individual-transition data. The result is purely statistical (no computational efficiency guarantee is provided for solving the underlying optimization problem).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper's result directly overcomes the exponential lower bound of Foster et al. [2021], which showed that no algorithm can achieve polynomial sample complexity under q^π-realizability + concentrability when data consists of individual transitions; the key distinction is that trajectory data enables the 'skipping' mechanism central to the proof. Compared to Jia et al. [2024], which also uses trajectory data but shows an exponential lower bound for policy evaluation, this paper's q^π-realizability assumption is stronger (all memoryless policies vs. only the target policy), and the task is harder (policy optimization vs. evaluation). Against Xie and Jiang [2021], which achieves a positive result under q^π-realizability, this paper requires only standard concentrability rather than the stronger 'strong concentrability' condition, at the cost of requiring trajectory data. The results of Chen and Jiang [2019] and Xiong et al. [2022] are positive but rely on strictly stronger structural assumptions (Bellman completeness and linear MDP, respectively) that do not follow from q^π-realizability. Wang et al. [2020] shows a negative result under a weaker data condition (λ_min lower bound on feature-space coverage rather than state-action-space concentrability), confirming that the concentrability assumption used here is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Weisz et al. [2023], who showed that linearly q^π-realizable MDPs can be approximated by linear MDPs by 'skipping' low-range states, and on the ELEANOR algorithm of Zanette et al. [2020], whose optimistic optimization structure is adapted here. It departs from Foster et al. [2021], who proved an exponential lower bound for offline RL under the same structural assumptions (linear q^π-realizability + concentrability) but with individual transition data, by showing that the negative result is an artifact of the data format rather than the structural assumptions. The paper also contrasts with Xie and Jiang [2021], who achieved positive results under a stronger 'strong concentrability' condition, and with Jia et al. [2024], who showed negative results for policy evaluation with trajectory data but under a restricted realizability assumption (only the target policy is linear).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper resolves a concrete open problem by showing that trajectory data is sufficient to achieve polynomial sample complexity in offline RL under linear q^π-realizability and concentrability, regardless of state space size. The key conceptual insight—that the 'skipping' mechanism enabling linear MDP approximation is tractable only with full trajectories, not individual transitions—clarifies why the Foster et al. lower bound does not extend to trajectory data. However, computational efficiency of the resulting optimization problem remains open, the statistical rate's optimality is unproven, and the assumptions (single data-collection policy, full trajectories, known concentrability coefficient) are strong, limiting immediate practical applicability.
