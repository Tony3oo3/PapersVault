## Problem

Classical lossy compression under logarithmic loss assumes the reconstruction distribution matches the source distribution, but practical applications like image restoration and joint compression-retrieval require the output to follow a different, prescribed distribution. When a rate bottleneck is added to the minimum entropy coupling (MEC) framework to handle this distributional shift, the resulting problem—MEC-B—has no known efficient solution, and it is unclear how the encoder and decoder can be optimized separately without significant loss.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a source distribution p_X over an alphabet of size n, a target output distribution p_Y (which may differ from p_X), and a rate budget R ≤ H(X), and produces an encoder joint distribution p_XT and a decoder joint distribution p_YT that together maximize I(X;Y) subject to H(T) ≤ R. In the Markov Coding Game application, the input is a discrete message (uniformly drawn from an alphabet of size 512 or 1024) combined with the current MDP state, and the output is a compressed signal T transmitted each round plus a final decoded message estimate from the receiver. In the image restoration variant, the input is a low-resolution image from an unpaired dataset and the output is an upscaled image whose distribution is enforced via an adversarial discriminator.

## Method

The key insight is a decomposition via Lemma 1: I(X;Y) = I(X;T) + I(Y;T) − I(T;X,Y) ≥ I(X;T) + I(Y;T) − R, which allows the encoder to be optimized by solving Entropy-Bounded Information Maximization (EBIM) and the decoder by solving standard MEC independently. For EBIM, the paper proves that only deterministic mappings T = g(X) can achieve the upper bound R (Theorem 1), then provides an O(n log n) greedy algorithm (Algorithm 1) that merges the two smallest or largest probability masses iteratively, guaranteeing a gap of at most h(p₂) bits from optimal (Theorem 2). Theorem 3 further characterizes the optimal stochastic coupling in an infinitesimal neighborhood of any deterministic mapping by analyzing the ratio dI(X;T)/dH(T) under basis perturbations, enabling navigation across the I–R plane. In the MCG experiments, the marginal policy is learned via Soft Q-Value iteration with temperature β, the source compresses its message belief each round using the EBIM solver, and the agent constructs its conditional action policy via MEC between the signal distribution and the marginal policy's action distribution at the current state.

## Results

In the Minimum Entropy Coupling comparison (Table 1), the proposed Zero-Seeking Greedy algorithm achieves an average joint entropy of 2.937±0.058 over 100 random marginal distribution pairs, outperforming the Successive Linearization Algorithm (SLA) at 3.225±0.141 and the independent joint baseline at 5.443±0.101. The deterministic EBIM solver (Algorithm 1) runs in O(n log n) time with a guaranteed optimality gap bounded by h(p₂) ≤ 1 bit (Theorem 2). In Markov Coding Game experiments on a noisy 8×8 Grid World with a 512-symbol uniform message, the proposed EBIM-based compression consistently achieves higher receiver decoding accuracy than uniform quantization at equivalent rate budgets, while the trade-off between MDP reward and accuracy is navigated via the entropy-regularization parameter β.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines. Against the Shkel–Verdu single-shot encoder [3], the proposed EBIM solver (Algorithm 1) significantly outperforms in the low-rate regime by identifying more deterministic mappings, while both methods yield comparable mutual information at high rates (Figure 9, Appendix D.1). Against uniform quantization (Algorithm 5) in Markov Coding Game experiments, the proposed EBIM-based compression achieves higher receiver decoding accuracy at the same rate budget, as shown in Figure 4, because it adaptively merges the smallest-probability symbols rather than applying fixed-size bins. Against the Successive Linearization Algorithm (SLA) [38] for the Minimum Entropy Coupling sub-problem, both greedy variants (Max-Seeking and Zero-Seeking) achieve lower joint entropy (≈2.94 vs. 3.23), with the Zero-Seeking variant marginally outperforming Max-Seeking (2.937 vs. 2.946). The paper does not report a single unified metric across all comparisons; the MCG results are presented as trade-off curves (MDP reward vs. accuracy) rather than a single scalar, and the image restoration results (MNIST, SVHN) in Appendix E are qualitative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Minimum Entropy Coupling (MEC) framework by introducing an entropy bottleneck on the intermediate code, creating the MEC-B formulation that decomposes into Entropy-Bounded Information Maximization (EBIM) for the encoder and MEC for the decoder. The log-loss distortion measure draws on prior rate-distortion work by Courtade and Wesel, while the MCG application extends Sokota et al.'s Markov Coding Games by adding a rate limit between source and agent. The image restoration formulation in the appendix leverages the Variational Information Maximization approach of Barber and Agakov and the mutual information lower bound from InfoGAN (Chen et al.), positioning the work at the intersection of information-theoretic compression and generative modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MEC-B framework provides a principled decomposition for lossy compression when the reconstruction distribution differs from the source, a setting relevant to joint compression-retrieval and distributional shift scenarios. The guaranteed-gap greedy algorithm for EBIM and the characterization of optimal solutions near deterministic mappings offer practical tools, though the gap between separate encoder-decoder optimization and the joint optimum remains unquantified. Open questions include extending the framework to continuous distributions, designing neural architectures based on MEC-B, and applying EBIM to watermarking language models. The MCG experiments, while illustrative, are limited to a simple grid world, and the image restoration results are explicitly preliminary.
