## Problem

Self-supervised pretraining of Vision Transformers (e.g., MoCo-V3, SimCLR, DINO) demands substantial computational resources, with optimal performance requiring long training schedules that hinder practical deployment. Existing acceleration methods either modify the model architecture, introduce additional losses, or are not specifically tailored to the ViT's unique property of generalizing across variable input sequence lengths. This paper addresses the gap by proposing acceleration techniques that reduce gradient estimation cost while preserving the original architecture and training algorithm.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 or 240×240 resolution) split into patch grids, but during training the input sequence is dynamically compressed via token dropout and/or larger patch sizes, reducing the effective sequence length from the base 197 tokens down to as few as 20 tokens. At evaluation time (linear probe, fine-tuning, or nearest-neighbor), the full uncompressed sequence is used. The output is a pretrained ViT-Base backbone whose learned representations are assessed on ImageNet-100 and ImageNet-1k classification, with the key constraint that the acceleration only modifies the input sequence and leaves the encoder architecture and loss function unchanged.

## Method

The framework combines two sequence compression strategies—randomized token dropout (removing a random fraction of patch tokens) and flexible patch scaling (using larger patches via FlexiViT-style optimal projection of patch embedding weights)—to reduce the ViT input sequence length and thus the per-iteration cost. A cost-adjusted MSE (CA-MSE) analysis decomposes the gradient estimation error into bias and variance components, enabling principled comparison of compression configurations without full training runs. The key insight is that the optimal compression ratio shifts over training: aggressive compression (high dropout, large patches) is beneficial early when gradient magnitudes are large, but as the model converges and gradients shrink, the bias from compressed sequences dominates, necessitating a gradual relaxation toward smaller patches and lower dropout. An automated schedule derived from CA-MSE measurements at intermediate checkpoints adjusts the compression ratio to training progress, achieving 4× speedup in MoCo-V3, 3.3× in SimCLR, and 2.5× in DINO on ImageNet-1k while matching non-accelerated downstream accuracy.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, the proposed dynamic acceleration schedule achieves near-identical downstream performance to non-accelerated baselines while drastically reducing training budget: MoCo-V3 reaches 75.9% LP / 81.9% FT at 1542M units (vs. 76.0% / 81.8% at 6168M, a 4× speedup), SimCLR reaches 68.4% LP / 81.5% FT at 922M units (vs. 68.3% / 81.3% at 3075M, a 3.3× speedup), and DINO reaches 77.4% LP / 82.0% FT at 1138M units (vs. 77.4% / 81.8% at 2846M, a 2.5× speedup). On ImageNet-100, the accelerated MoCo-V3 reproduces the full-budget peak (70.0% NN, 84.4% LP, 90.6% FT) using only ~30% of the original training budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its accelerated training against the standard (non-accelerated) versions of three SSL frameworks on ImageNet-1K. For MoCo-V3 [7], the accelerated model matches the baseline almost exactly (75.9% vs. 76.0% LP; 81.9% vs. 81.8% FT) while using only 25% of the training budget (1542M vs. 6168M units), a 4× speedup. For SimCLR [5], the accelerated model slightly exceeds the baseline on all three metrics (68.4% vs. 68.3% LP; 81.5% vs. 81.3% FT; 50.7% vs. 50.2% NN) at roughly 30% of the budget (922M vs. 3075M), a 3.3× speedup. For DINO [4], the accelerated model ties on LP (77.4%) and marginally outperforms on FT (82.0% vs. 81.8%) while using only ~40% of the budget (1138M vs. 2846M), a 2.5× speedup. In all three cases, the accelerated model is competitive or superior despite the substantially reduced compute, confirming that the sequence-compression-based acceleration does not sacrifice representation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on FlexiViT's flexible patch embedding projection [3] to enable dynamic patch scaling, and repurposes the token-masking idea from masked image modeling (MAE, BEiT) for a different purpose: reducing gradient estimation cost rather than establishing reconstruction targets. It departs from prior acceleration work (Koçyiigit et al. for ResNets, Fast-MoCo's combinatorial patches, progressive token merging) by modifying only the input sequence length without altering the architecture or the underlying SSL algorithm. The key conceptual departure is the systematic gradient error analysis (CA-MSE) that provides a principled, data-driven schedule for when to compress aggressively versus conservatively, rather than relying on hand-crafted heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4×/3.3×/2.5× speedups across MoCo-V3, SimCLR, and DINO on ImageNet-1k make large-scale ViT SSL pretraining substantially more accessible, directly addressing reproducibility and carbon-footprint concerns. The CA-MSE framework offers a reusable diagnostic tool for choosing compression schedules, though it was validated only on ViT-Base and ImageNet, and the linear-complexity assumption breaks down for sequence lengths above ~400 tokens (relevant for higher-resolution or larger-patch settings). Open questions include whether the schedule generalizes to larger ViT variants (Large, Huge), non-ImageNet domains, or architectures without the flexible patch embedding mechanism, and whether the approach extends to other transformer-based SSL paradigms beyond augmentation invariance.
