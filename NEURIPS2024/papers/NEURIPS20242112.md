## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of adversarial imitation learning (AIL) are restricted to tabular or linear function approximation settings and rely on algorithmic designs (count-based or covariance-matrix-based bonuses) that are impractical to implement with neural networks. This creates a significant gap between provable guarantees and the general function approximation (e.g., deep networks) used in practice. The paper asks whether a single AIL algorithm can simultaneously achieve polynomial sample complexity under general function approximation and remain straightforward to implement with standard optimization tools.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N expert trajectories (each of horizon length H, generated by a near-optimal expert policy) and K environment interaction trajectories (collected online by the learning policy). The output is a policy π̄ uniformly sampled from the sequence of K policies generated during training, which is guaranteed to achieve an ε-imitation gap with high probability. In practice, the reward and Q-value functions are parameterized by neural networks and updated via stochastic gradient descent, with the policy derived as the greedy action of the learned Q-function.

## Method

OPT-AIL alternates two optimization steps per iteration: (1) a reward update that solves an online optimization problem via Follow-the-Regularized-Leader (FTRL) to maximize the value gap between the expert and all historical policies, and (2) a policy update that learns Q-value functions by minimizing an optimism-regularized Bellman error (squared temporal-difference loss minus a λ-scaled initial-state Q-value term) and then extracts the greedy policy. The key theoretical insight is framing reward recovery as a no-regret online optimization problem, which provides a principled justification for the off-policy reward learning style used in prior deep AIL methods. The analysis adapts the generalized eluder coefficient to the AIL setting where the reward function changes across iterations, yielding polynomial expert sample complexity Õ(H² log N(R)/ε²) and interaction complexity Õ(H⁴ d_GEC log(N(Q)N(R))/ε²). Practically, the algorithm only requires approximate optimization of two objectives, enabling a clean actor-critic implementation with delayed target networks.

## Results

OPT-AIL is evaluated on 8 DMControl tasks with 500k environment interactions and varying numbers of expert trajectories (1 to 100). The paper reports that OPT-AIL significantly outperforms BC across all tasks, verifying its theoretical advantage in mitigating compounding errors. With only 1 expert trajectory, OPT-AIL uniquely achieves expert or near-expert performance on Finger Spin, Walker Run, and Hopper Hop. In terms of interaction efficiency, OPT-AIL reaches near-expert performance with substantially fewer environment interactions than prior SOTA AIL methods on Hopper Hop and Walker Run.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPT-AIL is compared against five baselines on 8 DMControl tasks: BC [Pomerleau, 1991], IQLearn [Garg, NeurIPS 2021], PPIL [Viano, NeurIPS 2022], FILTER [Swamy, ICML 2023], and HyPE [Ren, ICML 2024]. OPT-AIL significantly outperforms BC on all tasks, confirming its theoretical advantage over behavioral cloning in mitigating compounding errors under general function approximation. Against the three prior SOTA deep AIL methods (IQLearn, FILTER, HyPE), OPT-AIL consistently matches or exceeds their performance across all 8 tasks. The most notable advantage appears in the low-data regime: with only 1 expert trajectory, OPT-AIL uniquely reaches expert or near-expert returns on Finger Spin, Walker Run, and Hopper Hop, while the baselines fall short. In interaction efficiency (Figure 2, 1 expert trajectory), OPT-AIL achieves near-expert performance with substantially fewer environment steps than IQLearn, FILTER, and HyPE on Hopper Hop and Walker Run. PPIL [Viano, 2022] is also outperformed or matched by OPT-AIL across the evaluated tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical AIL literature from restricted settings (tabular MDPs via MB-TAIL [64], linear MDPs via BRIG [57] and OGAIL [33]) to general function approximation, which is the regime actually used in practice with neural networks. The algorithmic design adapts the optimism-regularized Bellman error minimization framework from [32] (Liu et al.) to the AIL context, where the reward is not fixed but must be jointly inferred via a no-regret online optimization procedure. Unlike prior theoretical AIL methods that rely on setting-specific bonuses (count-based or covariance-matrix-based), OPT-AIL requires only approximate optimization of two objectives, making it directly implementable with deep networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OPT-AIL closes a notable theory-practice gap by providing the first polynomial sample complexity guarantees for AIL with general function approximation, while simultaneously delivering a practical algorithm that matches or exceeds SOTA deep AIL methods (IQLearn, FILTER, HyPE) on DMControl. Key limitations include the Bellman completeness assumption (Assumption 3) and the H² expert sample complexity, which is worse than the H^{3/2} achievable in the tabular setting. Open questions include removing Bellman completeness (recent RL techniques [4] may help), achieving the tabular-optimal H^{3/2} rate in the general setting, and establishing horizon-free imitation gap bounds analogous to [63].
