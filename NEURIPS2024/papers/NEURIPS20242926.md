## Problem

Rendering 3D humans from monocular in-the-wild videos frequently fails under occlusion, producing artifacts, floating elements, or incomplete body parts. Existing occlusion-aware methods (OccNeRF, Wild2Avatar) rely on NeRFs and require hours to train, while efficient Gaussian splatting methods like GauHuman assume full visibility. The paper addresses the gap of achieving both high-fidelity occluded human rendering and practical training speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242926/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse monocular video sequence (e.g., 100 frames sampled at rate 5 for ZJU-MoCap, 50 frames for OcMotion) along with precomputed per-frame segmentation masks (from SAM) and SMPL pose parameters (from HMR 2.0 or ground truth). It produces a complete 3D Gaussian human representation (initialized from SMPL mesh vertices and deformed via LBS) that renders novel views of the full human body including occluded regions. The entire three-stage pipeline trains in approximately 10 minutes on a single Titan RTX GPU, with rendering taking less than a second per frame.

## Method

OccFusion is a three-stage pipeline combining 3D Gaussian splatting with pose-conditioned Stable Diffusion 1.5 (via ControlNet) as a generative prior. In the Initialization stage, occluded binary human masks are inpainted using the diffusion model, with a key simplification of removing self-occluded joints from the 2D pose condition via a Z-buffer test to avoid generation failures on challenging poses. In the Optimization stage, 3D Gaussians are trained with photometric losses on visible pixels plus Score Distillation Sampling applied to rendered human occupancy maps (rather than RGB) in both posed and canonical Da-pose space, enforcing geometric completeness without introducing appearance inconsistency. In the Refinement stage, a novel in-context inpainting technique stacks the coarse rendering and input image as a single image input to the diffusion model (prompted as 'the same person standing in two different rooms') to generate plausible RGB for occluded regions, which then supervises fine-tuning with perceptual-loss-weighted objectives. This is the first method to combine Gaussian splatting with diffusion priors specifically for occluded human rendering.

## Results

On ZJU-MoCap (simulated occlusions), OccFusion achieves the best PSNR (23.96 dB) and LPIPS (32.34×10⁻³), outperforming the closest competitor OccGaussian (23.29 dB, 41.93×10⁻³) by 0.67 dB and ~9.6 LPIPS points respectively. On OcMotion (real-world occlusions), OccFusion achieves the best SSIM (0.8875) and LPIPS (82.42×10⁻³), though its PSNR (18.28 dB) is slightly below OccGauHuman (18.85 dB). The full pipeline trains in only 10 minutes on a single Titan RTX GPU, compared to days for NeRF-based methods like OccNeRF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three non-occlusion baselines (HumanNeRF, 3DGS-Avatar, GauHuman), OccFusion dominates across all metrics on both datasets, confirming that occlusion-aware training is essential. Among occlusion-specific methods, OccFusion beats OccGaussian [67] (concurrent work) by 0.67 dB PSNR and 9.6 LPIPS on ZJU-MoCap, and beats OccNeRF [64] by 1.56 dB PSNR and 10.7 LPIPS on the same dataset, though OccNeRF retains a narrow SSIM edge (0.9562 vs. 0.9548). On OcMotion, OccFusion surpasses Wild2Avatar [63] by 4.19 dB PSNR and 10.9 LPIPS, and edges out its own OccGauHuman baseline on SSIM and LPIPS despite a 0.57 dB PSNR gap. The paper notes that OccGaussian results were taken from published numbers using 5× more training frames, and Wild2Avatar used 2× more frames, making OccFusion's advantage under a fairer compute budget even more pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

OccFusion builds directly on GauHuman's 3D Gaussian splatting framework for articulated human rendering and on DreamFusion's Score Distillation Sampling for leveraging 2D diffusion priors in 3D optimization. It departs from NeRF-based occluded human rendering (OccNeRF, Wild2Avatar) by replacing the implicit radiance field with explicit Gaussians for a ~10× speedup, and from standard Gaussian splatting human methods (GauHuman, 3DGS-Avatar) by introducing a three-stage pipeline that injects pose-conditioned Stable Diffusion priors at both the geometric (SDS on occupancy maps) and appearance (in-context inpainting) levels. The concurrent OccGaussian also targets occluded humans with Gaussians but relies on an occlusion feature query rather than generative diffusion supervision, making OccFusion the first to explicitly fuse 3DGS with diffusion priors for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10-minute training time on a single GPU makes occluded human rendering practically deployable in VR/AR, telemedicine, and sports analytics settings where NeRF-based alternatives require days of training. The key design insight—applying SDS to rendered occupancy maps rather than RGB to avoid appearance inconsistency, and using in-context inpainting (stacking coarse renderings with input frames) to guide appearance recovery—provides a reusable recipe for combining reconstruction and generation in 4D human modeling. Open questions include whether the per-video optimization can be made generalizable, whether consistency-aware diffusion models finetuned on human data can eliminate the residual cross-frame incoherence, and whether the weak 2D-pose conditioning can be replaced with stronger 3D pose signals.
