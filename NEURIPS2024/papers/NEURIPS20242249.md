## Problem

Event-to-video (E2V) reconstruction is inherently ill-posed because event cameras only detect per-pixel intensity changes, capturing edge and motion information locally while neglecting semantic content in static regions. This causes reconstructed videos to suffer from artifacts and regional blur. The paper identifies that language naturally conveys abundant semantic information and explores whether text-conditional diffusion models can resolve this semantic ambiguity in E2V reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242249/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes asynchronous event streams (converted to voxel grids with B=5 temporal bins via bilinear interpolation) and natural language descriptions (generated by the RAM tagging model, since no event-text-video paired data exists) as input, and produces 16-frame video clips at 256×256 resolution. Reconstruction is autoregressive: each subsequent clip is conditioned on the last frame of the previous clip, and the first clip is generated directly from events and text. The event encoder projects voxel grids into the latent space of a pre-trained image encoder, and a CLIP text encoder provides semantic conditioning to the attention layers.

## Method

LaSe-E2V builds on Stable Diffusion 2.1-base initialized with a text-guided video diffusion model (Consisti2V) and introduces three key mechanisms to ensure spatio-temporal consistency. First, an Event-guided Spatio-temporal Attention (ESA) module injects event features into the U-Net via two distinct cross-attention paths: spatial cross-attention concatenates event latents along the spatial dimension to constrain structural details, while temporal cross-attention concatenates along the temporal dimension to enforce inter-frame coherence. Second, an event-aware mask loss directly supervises the inter-frame difference in non-event-activated regions, penalizing spurious motion where no events were detected. Third, an event-aware noise initialization strategy replaces random Gaussian noise at inference with noise added to accumulated event frames, bridging the train-test gap and providing structural layout guidance during denoising. The model is trained end-to-end with a combined ε-prediction loss and mask loss (λ=0.01) for 150k steps on 8 V100 GPUs, using classifier-free guidance (scale w=5) and DDIM sampling (50 steps) at inference.

## Results

On the ECD dataset, LaSe-E2V achieves MSE 0.023, SSIM* 0.629, and LPIPS 0.194, outperforming the previous best (HyperE2VID) by 30% on MSE and 9% on SSIM*. On MVSEC, LaSe-E2V reaches MSE 0.055, SSIM* 0.342, and LPIPS 0.461, leading all baselines. On HQF, LaSe-E2V attains SSIM* 0.548 and LPIPS 0.254 (best), though its MSE of 0.034 is marginally higher than HyperE2VID (0.031) and ET-Net (0.032). On temporal consistency (VBench), LaSe-E2V scores 84.25% subject consistency and 93.39% background consistency, substantially exceeding all baselines (next best: 55.49% and 86.85% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaSe-E2V outperforms all eight compared E2V methods on the majority of metrics across three datasets. On ECD, it leads HyperE2VID (the strongest prior method) by 30% on MSE (0.023 vs 0.033), 9% on SSIM* (0.629 vs 0.576), and 8% on LPIPS (0.194 vs 0.212). On MVSEC, the margins are similarly clear: MSE 0.055 vs 0.076 (HyperE2VID) and 0.062 (SSL-E2VID), with SSIM* 0.342 vs 0.315. On HQF, LaSe-E2V achieves the best SSIM* (0.548) and LPIPS (0.254), but its MSE (0.034) is slightly worse than HyperE2VID (0.031) and ET-Net (0.032), indicating a minor trade-off in pixel-level fidelity on this dataset. On temporal consistency (VBench), LaSe-E2V dramatically outperforms all baselines in subject consistency (84.25% vs. next-best 55.49% from ET-Net) and background consistency (93.39% vs. 86.85%), while matching them on motion smoothness (98.11% vs. 98.41%). The paper also reports that on the challenging HS-ERGB dataset (fast motion, sparse events), LaSe-E2V achieves MSE 0.078 vs 0.161 (HyperE2VID) and 0.199 (E2VID), confirming its advantage in extreme scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established event-to-video (E2V) reconstruction task by repurposing text-conditional latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, Consisti2V) as the generative backbone, departing from the CNN/hypernetwork architectures (E2VID, FireNet, HyperE2VID) that have dominated the field since 2019. The key conceptual departure is treating language as a semantic prior to resolve the inherent ill-posedness of event data, which prior methods addressed only through architectural inductive biases or data augmentation. The Event-guided Spatio-temporal Attention (ESA) module and event-aware mask loss are novel conditioning mechanisms that adapt the diffusion pipeline to the unique spatiotemporal structure of event streams, drawing loosely on controllable video generation techniques (VideoComposer, Gen1) but applied to a fundamentally different inverse problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a promising new direction for event-based vision by demonstrating that language can serve as a practical semantic prior to resolve the ambiguity in event data, with the added benefit of enabling language-driven video editing (e.g., changing lighting conditions via text). However, significant limitations remain: the method requires 50 denoising steps (~1.09s/frame) making it impractical for real-time applications, training data is limited to synthetic MS-COCO and 1k BS-ERGB sequences, and hallucination in sparse-event regions is acknowledged but not fully resolved. Open questions include whether faster samplers (consistency models, flow matching) can close the speed gap, whether event-based multi-modality models can replace external tagging models for text generation, and whether confidence maps based on event density can make the output safe for deployment in safety-critical systems.
