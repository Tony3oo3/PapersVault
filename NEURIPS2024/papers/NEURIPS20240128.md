## Problem

The VAE encoder in latent diffusion models is computationally expensive (e.g., 135.59 GFLOPS for Stable Diffusion 2.1's encoder vs. 86.37 for its diffusion UNet) and its design space remains largely unexplored. This overhead is especially problematic for video diffusion, on-the-fly data augmentation, and gradient-based applications like 3D score distillation. The paper asks whether the encoder can be made substantially lighter without sacrificing reconstruction quality.

## Input / Output

LiteVAE takes an RGB image (typically 128×128 or 256×256) and first decomposes it via a 3-level Haar DWT into four sub-bands per level (low, horizontal, vertical, diagonal), each at progressively coarser spatial resolution. The encoder then produces a latent code of shape H/8 × W/8 × n_z (e.g., 32×32×4 or 32×32×12), which retains an image-like structure analogous to SD-VAE latents. The decoder reconstructs the full-resolution image from this latent, and the model is trained end-to-end with reconstruction, KL, and adversarial losses.

## Method

LiteVAE replaces the heavy convolutional encoder with a wavelet-based pipeline: a 3-level 2D Haar DWT decomposes the input into multiscale sub-bands, each processed by a lightweight UNet-based feature-extraction module (without spatial down/upsampling), and the resulting features are combined by a feature-aggregation UNet to produce the latent code. The decoder is the standard SD-VAE decoder, but with self-modulated convolution (SMC) replacing group normalization to eliminate imbalanced feature-map magnitudes and improve training dynamics. Additional training improvements include high-frequency loss terms (Charbonnier loss on DWT sub-bands and ℓ₁ loss on Gaussian-blurred residuals), a UNet-based pixel-wise discriminator, a constant adversarial loss weight, and pretraining at 128×128 before fine-tuning at 256×256. The base LiteVAE-B model uses only 6.75M encoder parameters (vs. 34.16M for the standard VAE) while matching or exceeding reconstruction quality, and larger LiteVAE variants (M, L) outperform VAEs of comparable complexity across rFID, LPIPS, PSNR, and SSIM.

## Results

LiteVAE matches or exceeds the reconstruction quality of the standard SD-VAE across all evaluated datasets while using only 6.75M encoder parameters versus 34.16M (a 6× reduction). On FFHQ 256, LiteVAE achieves rFID 0.41 vs. 0.47 for the VAE; on ImageNet 256, rFID 0.87 vs. 0.89. The larger LiteVAE-L model (41.42M params) outperforms the standard VAE on all metrics (rFID 0.74 vs. 0.95, PSNR 29.94 vs. 29.25 on ImageNet 128). In terms of computational cost, LiteVAE-B requires only 3,155 MB GPU memory and achieves 129 img/sec throughput versus 8,860 MB and 68 img/sec for the VAE encoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard VAE encoder from Stable Diffusion (SD-VAE, Rombach et al., CVPR 2022), which uses 34.16M encoder parameters. LiteVAE-B (6.75M params) matches or slightly exceeds the VAE on rFID, PSNR, and SSIM across all four dataset/resolution combinations (e.g., rFID 0.41 vs. 0.47 on FFHQ 256; PSNR 26.02 vs. 25.83 on ImageNet 256), while using roughly one-sixth the encoder parameters. When scaled up, LiteVAE-M (32.75M) and LiteVAE-L (41.42M) clearly outperform the VAE on all metrics (rFID 0.79 and 0.74 vs. 0.95 on ImageNet 128), demonstrating superior compute/quality trade-offs. A naive downscaled VAE (VAE-Small, 6.75M) performs worse than LiteVAE-B at the same parameter budget (rFID 5.27 vs. 4.40 on ImageNet 128), confirming that the wavelet decomposition is essential for efficient encoding. For downstream diffusion generation, models trained in LiteVAE's latent space achieve comparable or slightly better FID than standard LDMs (8.03 vs. 8.11 on FFHQ; 5.73 vs. 5.92 on CelebA-HQ), confirming the latent space is equally suitable for generative modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiteVAE builds directly on the Stable Diffusion VAE (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) by replacing its heavy convolutional encoder with a multi-level Haar wavelet decomposition followed by lightweight UNet feature-extraction and aggregation blocks (architecturally inspired by the ADM model, Dhariwal & Nichol, NeurIPS 2021). The decoder is kept identical to SD-VAE, and the self-modulated convolution (SMC) is a direct modification of the modulated convolution from StyleGAN2 (Karras et al., CVPR 2020). While wavelets have appeared in GANs (SWAGAN) and pixel-space diffusion, their use as a preprocessing step to simplify the learned encoder in LDM autoencoders is novel, and the paper explicitly positions this as an underexplored design dimension in the LDM pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6× encoder parameter reduction (34.16M → 6.75M) with matched reconstruction quality has direct practical impact on LDM training throughput, GPU memory, and the feasibility of on-the-fly data augmentation during diffusion training. The finding that wavelet decomposition offloads most of the spatial-frequency work from the learned encoder suggests a broader principle: fixed signal-processing front-ends can substantially reduce the capacity needed by learned components. Open questions include whether the efficiency gains extend to video, 3D, or other modalities, and whether the approach integrates cleanly with tokenization-based generative schemes beyond LDMs.
