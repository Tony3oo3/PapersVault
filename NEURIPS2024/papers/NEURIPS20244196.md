## Problem

Stereo matching networks trained on synthetic data (e.g., SceneFlow) over-rely on superficial appearance cues and fail to generalize to unseen real-world domains. Existing domain generalization approaches enforce feature consistency or use robust architectures but do not directly exploit semantic structure to guide correspondence matching, leaving ambiguous regions (textureless areas, edge boundaries) poorly resolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244196/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on rectified RGB stereo image pairs (960×540 from SceneFlow) and additionally requires per-pixel object instance index maps and ground-truth disparity during training. It produces a dense disparity map via the underlying stereo matching pipeline (PSMNet, GwcNet, CFNet, or IGEV). The HODC framework is a training-time loss module that requires no architectural modification to the base network; at inference, only the standard stereo image pair is needed.

## Method

HODC segments object index maps into sub-object regions at multiple hierarchical scales using rectangular grids (N_h × N_w), then aggregates pixel features within each region to form object-aware regional representations. The left feature map is inverse-warped to the right using ground-truth disparity, with occluded pixels removed via left-right reprojection consistency. Two levels of contrastive loss are applied: intra-scale loss pulls same-scale left-right regional representations together while pushing apart non-corresponding regions, and inter-scale loss aligns finer local representations with their coarser parent regions to enforce global structural awareness. Hard negatives (top 10% most similar) are selected to sharpen discrimination, and the InfoNCE loss (τ=0.05) is combined with standard smooth-L1 disparity loss. The framework is plug-and-play, requiring only the object index map and ground-truth disparity available in synthetic training data.

## Results

Using only the synthetic SceneFlow dataset for training, HODC achieves state-of-the-art synthetic-to-real generalization across four realistic benchmarks. HODC-PSMNet attains D1 scores of 4.7 (KITTI-2015), 3.9 (KITTI-2012), 9.3 (Middlebury Half), 7.0 (Middlebury Quarter), and 5.4 (ETH3D), while HODC-IGEV reaches 4.5, 3.8, 7.0, 5.2, and 2.7 respectively. The method improves the original PSMNet baseline (16.3 on KITTI-2015) by over 11 points and outperforms the strongest prior domain-generalization methods (FC-Stereo, ITSA, HVT) by 0.1–4.1 D1 points across datasets. On the challenging DrivingStereo dataset with adverse weather, HODC-PSMNet achieves an average D1 of 4.6, outperforming fine-tuned PSMNet (6.2) and all other domain-generalization baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HODC-PSMNet outperforms all prior PSMNet-based domain-generalization methods: it beats FC-PSMNet (5.8→4.7) and ITSA-PSMNet (5.8→4.7) by 1.1 D1 on KITTI-2015, Graft-PSMNet (4.8→4.7) by 0.1, and HVT-PSMNet (4.9→4.7) by 0.2; the gap widens substantially on Middlebury Half (9.3 vs. 10.2–15.1) and ETH3D (5.4 vs. 6.9–9.8). HODC-GwcNet similarly dominates its counterparts, improving over FC-GwcNet by 0.5 on KITTI-2015 and 2.8 on ETH3D (4.3 vs. 8.0), and over HVT-GwcNet by 1.9 on ETH3D. HODC-CFNet achieves the best scores on KITTI-2012 (3.8), Middlebury (9.5/7.5), and ETH3D (4.2), though ITSA-CFNet edges it by 0.1 on KITTI-2015 (4.7 vs. 4.8). HODC-IGEV improves over the IGEV baseline by 1.1 on KITTI-2015 and 0.9 on ETH3D (2.7 vs. 3.6), setting new records on all four standard benchmarks. On the broader DrivingStereo evaluation, HODC-PSMNet (avg. 4.6) surpasses fine-tuned PSMNet (6.2), HVT-PSMNet (5.4), and FC-PSMNet (5.7), confirming robustness under adverse weather conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

HODC sits at the intersection of domain-generalized stereo matching and contrastive representation learning. It extends FC-Stereo's stereo contrastive loss (which enforced pixel-level left-right feature consistency) by introducing hierarchical object-aware regional representations and a dual-level (intra- and inter-scale) contrastive objective, thereby shifting from pixel-wise to semantically and structurally meaningful matching. Conceptually, it departs from multi-task approaches (SegStereo, EdgeStereo) that coupled semantic segmentation or edge detection with disparity estimation, instead using object index maps to directly construct region-level positive/negative pairs within a single training objective. The method is architecture-agnostic and plugs into existing pipelines (PSMNet, GwcNet, CFNet, IGEV) without structural modifications, positioning it as a training-stage enhancement rather than a new network design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that semantically and structurally driven matching is a critical but underexploited cue for synthetic-to-real generalization in stereo matching, and provides a practical, drop-in mechanism to inject this cue. A notable finding is that discriminability of hard negatives matters more for generalization than positive-pair consistency, challenging the assumption made by FC-Stereo. The plug-and-play nature and consistent gains across four architectures and five realistic datasets make the approach broadly applicable. Open questions include whether architectures natively designed around semantic/structural matching (rather than post-hoc contrastive supervision) could yield further gains, and how the method behaves when object index maps are unavailable or noisy in real deployment settings.
