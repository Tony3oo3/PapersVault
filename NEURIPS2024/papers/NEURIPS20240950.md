## Problem

Diffusion-based neural solvers for combinatorial optimization (e.g., DIFUSCO, T2T) require tens to hundreds of iterative denoising steps to generate a single solution, creating a severe inference bottleneck that limits their practical utility for time-constrained CO solving. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether the multi-step denoising process can be collapsed into a direct mapping from arbitrary noise levels to the optimal solution, enabling single-step generation without sacrificing quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240950/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a graph problem instance G (2D city coordinates for TSP, graph adjacency for MIS) together with a noisy solution vector x_t ∈ {0,1}^{N×2} (one-hot per decision variable) and a scalar timestep t, and outputs a Bernoulli probability distribution p_θ(x_0|x_t, G) ∈ [0,1]^{N×2} over N entries (edges for TSP, nodes for MIS). Unlike standard diffusion models that predict the next denoising step, the consistency model predicts the full solution distribution in one forward pass regardless of the input noise level. At test time, the output probabilities are converted to heatmaps and post-processed (e.g., 2-Opt for TSP) to yield a feasible tour or independent set.

## Method

Fast T2T introduces an optimization consistency training protocol where, for a given instance G with known optimal solution x*, the model is trained to map points from any noise level and any trajectory directly to δ(x − x*), using binary cross-entropy as the distance metric and a cosine-scheduled pair of timesteps per training step. The backbone is a 12-layer anisotropic GNN with edge gating that receives sinusoidal embeddings of the noisy solution entries, instance features (coordinates or adjacency), and the timestep. At test time, solving proceeds in two stages: (1) consistency sampling—drawing x_T from uniform noise and evaluating f_θ(x_T, T, G) in a single forward pass (or optionally multi-step via alternating noise injection and denoising); and (2) a consistency-based gradient search that updates latent probability parameters p_x = η·Q̄_{αT} via exponential gradient descent on a combined objective (consistency loss + task-specific objective l(·;G)), alternating noise injection and denoising to explore the learned solution distribution with instance-specific objective guidance.

## Results

On TSP-50/100 with greedy decoding, Fast T2T (T_s=1, T_g=1) achieves 0.03% and 0.10% optimality drop in 54s and 1m respectively, matching T2T (T_s=50, T_g=30) at 0.03%/0.11% in 26m/42m — a ~35× speedup at equal quality. On TSP-500/1000, Fast T2T (T_s=1, T_g=1) achieves 4.28%/6.42% drop in 36s/2m30s, outperforming T2T (T_s=50, T_g=30) at 5.61%/9.04% in 6m23s/19m21s. On MIS (RB-[200-300] and ER-[700-800]), Fast T2T (T_s=1, T_g=1) achieves 3.51%/10.30% drop in 1m18s/25s versus T2T's 5.49%/11.28% in 20m58s/7m7s. With more steps (T_s=5, T_g=5), Fast T2T reaches 0.01%/0.03% drop on TSP-50/100 and 0.39%/0.58% on TSP-500/1000 with 2Opt, approaching exact solver quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fast T2T (T_s=1, T_g=1) matches or slightly beats T2T (T_s=50, T_g=30) on TSP-50/100 (0.03% vs 0.03% and 0.10% vs 0.11% drop) while being ~35× faster (54s/1m vs 26m/42m). On TSP-500/1000, Fast T2T (T_s=1, T_g=1) clearly outperforms T2T (T_s=50, T_g=30) by 1.33 and 2.62 percentage points in drop (4.28% vs 5.61%, 6.42% vs 9.04%) with ~10× speedup. Against DIFUSCO (T_s=100), Fast T2T (T_s=1) achieves comparable or better quality with 82.8× average speedup on TSP-50/100 and 16.8× on TSP-500/1000. On MIS, Fast T2T (T_s=1, T_g=1) beats T2T (T_s=50, T_g=30) by 1.98 and 0.98 percentage points in drop with ~16× speedup. Fast T2T also surpasses the LKH3 heuristic in the early solving stage on TSP-100/1000, and with T_s=5, T_g=5 plus 2Opt post-processing, it reaches 0.39%/0.58% drop on TSP-500/1000, approaching the exact solver (0.00%) while remaining orders of magnitude faster than Concorde (37m/6.65h).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the consistency model paradigm (Song et al., 2023) from image generation to combinatorial optimization, defining a new 'optimization consistency' property where all noise trajectories conditioned on a problem instance must map to the optimal solution, rather than merely being self-consistent within a single trajectory. It builds directly on the T2T framework (Li et al., NeurIPS 2023), which introduced objective-guided gradient search over diffusion-based solution priors for CO, and on DIFUSCO (Sun & Yang, NeurIPS 2023) for the discrete diffusion noising process. The key departure is replacing the step-by-step denoising function with a single-step consistency mapping, and reformulating the T2T gradient search to operate on these consistency mappings via exponential gradient updates on latent Bernoulli probabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: Fast T2T achieves tens-of-times speedup over SOTA diffusion-based CO solvers while matching or exceeding their solution quality, making neural CO solvers viable for time-constrained applications where traditional heuristics like LKH3 dominate. The finding that one-step generation plus one-step gradient search suffices to outperform 100-step diffusion challenges the assumption that iterative denoising is necessary for high-quality CO solving. Limitations include doubled training cost (two forward passes per instance), diminishing speedup at larger scales due to serial post-processing overhead, and the need for more training data than diffusion baselines. Open questions include whether the optimization consistency formulation extends to other CO problem classes beyond edge/node-decision types, and whether combining the fast consistency backbone with more efficient traditional local search could close the remaining gap to exact solvers at scale.
