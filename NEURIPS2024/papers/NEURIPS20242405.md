## Problem

Diffusion-based image restoration methods like IR-SDE achieve strong general-purpose performance but require 100+ sampling steps, making them impractical for real-time applications. While consistency models can reduce inference to one or two steps, existing approaches either depend on a pre-trained teacher model (consistency distillation, causing performance degradation) or suffer from unstable training and mediocre solutions when trained independently (consistency training). This paper addresses how to achieve fast, standalone image restoration without sacrificing quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242405/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (rainy, noisy, blurred, or low-light) as input, which simultaneously serves as the mean-reverting target μ in the underlying SDE. It outputs a restored high-quality RGB image in one or two forward passes of a U-Net. During training, 128×128 patches are used with a batch size of 4; at inference, the model handles full-resolution images (tested up to 1280×720) with linear scaling of compute relative to image size.

## Method

IR-CM builds on the IR-SDE mean-reverting SDE framework and introduces three key components. First, an Origin-Estimated Consistency Function (OECF) replaces the standard consistency function by incorporating the analytical origin prediction from the SDE, yielding a more stable initial state and smaller solution space. Second, an Origin-Guided Loss (OG loss) directly penalizes the gap between the consistency function output and the true HQ image (with VGG16 perceptual terms), preventing the trivial solutions and pattern collapse that arise from the self-consistency loss alone. Third, a Linear-Nonlinear Decoupling Training Strategy splits training into two stages: a linear-fitting stage where x(0) is set equal to μ (eliminating the nonlinear image-content term and reducing the problem to pure noise fitting), followed by a nonlinear-fitting stage where x(0) is set to the HQ image to recover the full degradation mapping and enable multi-step sampling. This decoupling allows the model to be trained from scratch (or initialized from IR-SDE weights) without any teacher model, yet match or exceed the original IR-SDE performance with only 1–2 NFE.

## Results

On the LOLv2 low-light enhancement task, IR-CM-CT with 2-step inference achieves PSNR 30.20, SSIM 0.95, and LPIPS 0.021, setting a new state-of-the-art that surpasses the 100-step IR-SDE baseline (PSNR 27.05, LPIPS 0.087) by 3.15 dB in PSNR. On R100H deraining, 1-step IR-CM-CT reaches LPIPS 0.016 (vs. 0.047 for IR-SDE) and SSIM 0.92 (vs. 0.90), while on GoPro deblurring it achieves LPIPS 0.013 (vs. 0.064 for IR-SDE). Inference time drops from 7.3 s (IR-SDE, 100 NFE) to 0.073 s (IR-CM 1-step) or 0.145 s (IR-CM 2-step) on 256×256 images, a ~50–100× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IR-CM-CT consistently outperforms its base model IR-SDE [22] on the perceptual LPIPS metric across all four tasks (e.g., 0.016 vs. 0.047 on R100H, 0.013 vs. 0.064 on GoPro, 0.021 vs. 0.087 on LOLv2) while requiring only 1–2 NFE instead of 100, though it typically trails IR-SDE on PSNR by 1–4 dB. Against single-step CNN baselines, IR-CM-CT dominates on LPIPS: on R100H it achieves 0.016 vs. 0.086 (Restormer [5]) and 0.133 (MAXIM [4]); on GoPro it reaches 0.013 vs. 0.089 (MAXIM) and 0.112 (DBGAN [15]). On LOLv2, the 2-step IR-CM-CT surpasses all prior diffusion-based methods—DiffLL [21] (LPIPS 0.207, 10 NFE) and GlobalDiff [20] (LPIPS 0.095, 500 NFE)—as well as single-step Retinexformer [16] (0.097) and LLFlow [17] (0.099). On the real-world Raindrop dataset, IR-CM 2-step (LPIPS 0.041) edges out Refusion [30] (0.048, 100 NFE) and Transweather (0.051) while matching MAXIM [4] on PSNR. The consistency-distillation variant (IR-CM-CD) is uniformly weaker than the consistency-training variant (IR-CM-CT), confirming that the proposed linear-nonlinear decoupling strategy and OECF are critical for closing the gap with the 100-step teacher.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the consistency model framework (Song et al., 2023) to the IR-SDE mean-reverting SDE formulation (Luo et al., ICML 2023), which previously required ~100 sampling steps for inference. The key departure from standard consistency training is the proposed linear-nonlinear decoupling strategy, which splits the PF-ODE trajectory into a linear (noise-only) fitting stage and a nonlinear (image-degradation) fitting stage, enabling the student to surpass the teacher on perceptual metrics without distillation. The origin-estimated consistency function (OECF) further modifies the standard consistency function by incorporating the analytical origin prediction from the mean-reverting SDE, reducing the initial solution error from a fixed offset to a higher-order infinitesimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for real-time image restoration applications (autonomous driving, medical imaging) where 100-step SDE inference is infeasible; reducing to 1–2 steps while maintaining or improving perceptual quality is a meaningful efficiency win. The finding that consistency training without a teacher can outperform the teacher on LPIPS challenges the common assumption that distillation is necessary for best performance, though the evidence is primarily on perceptual metrics while PSNR often remains below the baseline. Open questions include whether the linear-nonlinear decoupling generalizes to other SDE formulations (e.g., VP/VE SDEs), whether the approach extends to unconditional generation or video restoration, and whether the two-stage training overhead is justified compared to simpler single-stage CT with the OECF alone.
