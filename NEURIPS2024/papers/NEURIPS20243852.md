## Problem

In MLaaS settings, users cannot access model parameters or training data, yet backdoor attacks can compromise inference on poisoned inputs. Existing black-box defenses either discard suspicious samples/models (hurting utility) or assume triggers are small and corner-located, limiting them to specific attack types. This paper identifies an unexploited correlation between trigger visibility and noise robustness to enable a general, model-independent purification strategy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single potentially poisoned RGB image (e.g., 32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for MS-Celeb-1M) and a pre-trained diffusion model (DDPM) as input, and outputs a detoxified image of the same resolution. No access to the classifier's parameters, predictions, or training data is required; the diffusion model is the only external dependency. The two hyperparameters t̄₁ and t̄₂ (recommended 20 and 120) control global and local noise intensity respectively, and DDIM with a 20× speed-up factor is used to keep inference overhead comparable to the undefended baseline.

## Method

SampDetox is a two-stage perturbation-based detoxification built on the forward/reverse DDPM process. Stage 1 (Global) applies lightweight Gaussian noise to the entire sample via the closed-form forward DDPM equation (x_t = √ᾱ_t·x₀ + √(1−ᾱ_t)·z) to destroy low-visibility triggers, then restores the sample using reverse DDPM denoising. Stage 2 (Local) computes a per-pixel SSIM map between the original and Stage-1 denoised image to localize high-visibility triggers (justified by Theorem 4.2, which proves poisoned regions deviate more from their denoised counterparts than clean regions), then applies pixel-specific intensive noise proportional to the SSIM discrepancy and denoises again. Theorem 4.1 provides the theoretical guarantee that the KL divergence between clean and poisoned sample distributions monotonically decreases along the forward diffusion, ensuring that sufficient noise erases the trigger's statistical signature. The approach makes no assumptions on trigger size, location, or model architecture, and requires no interaction with the target classifier.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with PreAct-ResNet18, SampDetox achieves the highest Clean Accuracy (CA) across all ten backdoor attacks, ranging from 80.72% (LC) to 92.78% (TrojanNN), while reducing Attack Success Rate (ASR) to 1.45%–11.03% depending on the attack. For 8 out of 10 attacks, SampDetox also attains the best Poisoned Accuracy (PA) and ASR simultaneously. On GTSRB, SampDetox reaches up to 97.51% CA (TrojanNN) with ASR as low as 1.20% (LC), and on Tiny-ImageNet it maintains 53–55% CA with ASR below 9% for all attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SampDetox consistently outperforms all three baselines on Clean Accuracy (CA) across every attack, with margins of 10–16% over Sancdifi (e.g., 89.57% vs. 76.59% on BadNets; 92.78% vs. 76.63% on TrojanNN) and 1–5% over BDMAE and ZIP. On Poisoned Accuracy (PA), SampDetox leads for 8 of 10 attacks; the two exceptions are BadNets (Sancdifi: 89.55% vs. 90.15%) and LC (ZIP: 74.92% vs. 74.36%), where the gaps are under 0.6%. On ASR, SampDetox achieves the lowest rate for 8 attacks; Sancdifi edges it out on BadNets (1.92% vs. 2.11%) and TrojanNN (1.81% vs. 1.86%), while ZIP is slightly better on LC (2.06% vs. 1.55% — actually SampDetox wins here). Critically, Sancdifi and BDMAE require repeated model queries for feedback, whereas SampDetox is fully model-independent, making it applicable in strict black-box MLaaS settings where no model access is granted.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020), repurposing its forward process as a controlled noise-injection mechanism for trigger destruction and its reverse process for sample restoration. It departs from prior sample purification defenses (Sancdifi, BDMAE, ZIP) that assume triggers are small, corner-located, or require model feedback, by introducing a two-stage global-then-local perturbation strategy that handles arbitrary trigger geometries without any model access. The key conceptual departure is the empirical observation that trigger visibility and noise-robustness are inversely correlated, which motivates the split between lightweight global noise (for invisible triggers) and targeted local noise (for visible triggers).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SampDetox provides a practical, model-agnostic black-box defense that works across 10 diverse attack types, 3 datasets, and 3 architectures without requiring training data, model parameters, or iterative model queries—addressing a real deployment gap in MLaaS settings. The visibility-robustness correlation, while somewhat intuitive, gives a principled justification for the two-stage design and could inform future trigger design or detection. Open questions include: how the method scales to video or 3D modalities, whether adaptive attacks can specifically resist diffusion-based purification, and whether the reliance on a pre-trained diffusion model (with performance drops under distribution shift) limits real-world deployment where such models may not be available.
