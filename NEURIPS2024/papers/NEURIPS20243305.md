## Problem

Existing neural operator architectures for PDE solving are restricted to a fixed, predetermined set of physical variables and uniform grids, making them unable to handle multiphysics systems where coupled PDEs introduce new variables or where geometries are irregular and time-varying. No prior foundation model for PDEs can adapt to novel physical variables not present during pretraining, nor can it seamlessly transfer knowledge between systems with different numbers of coupled equations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes vector-valued functions (e.g., velocity, pressure, displacement, temperature) sampled on potentially irregular, time-varying meshes whose point sets change between snapshots, and produces the predicted solution fields at the next time step on the same or a denser target mesh. A key design constraint is that the number of input functions (physical variables) can differ between pretraining and fine-tuning, and the model supports zero-shot super-resolution by querying the output on a mesh with more points than seen during training.

## Method

CoDA-NO tokenizes the input function along the codomain so that each physical variable becomes a 'token function,' then extends transformer self-attention to infinite-dimensional function space by implementing the key, query, and value projections as Fourier Neural Operators and computing attention weights via L² inner products over the domain. Variable-Specific Positional Encodings (learnable Fourier-based embeddings) are concatenated per variable to distinguish physical channels, and GNO layers serve as encoders/decoders that map between irregular meshes and a uniform latent grid. The architecture is permutation-equivariant across variables via shared operators, and a function-space normalization (an extension of instance norm using integral statistics) stabilizes training. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: self-supervised pretraining via masked reconstruction of velocity/pressure fields, followed by supervised fine-tuning where only newly introduced VSPEs and a lightweight predictor module are trained, enabling adaptation to new variables (e.g., displacement or temperature) with minimal additional parameters.

## Results

On the fluid-structure interaction (NS+EW) task at Re=400 with only 5 few-shot samples, pretrained CoDA-NO achieves an L2 error of 0.040, outperforming the best from-scratch baseline (GNN at 0.045) and GINO (0.122) by 11% and 67% respectively. At 100 samples, the gap widens: CoDA-NO reaches 0.003 vs. GINO's 0.043 and GNN's 0.009. On Rayleigh-Bénard convection (Ra=12×10³, 5 samples), pretrained CoDA-NO achieves 0.016 L2 error versus U-Net's 0.049 and FNO's 0.119, a ~3× improvement. On PDEBench, CoDA-NO (11M params) reduces test error by up to 43% over FNO (1.9B params) while using only ~0.6% of its parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDA-NO consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluation setting. Against GINO, the closest neural-operator baseline, pretrained CoDA-NO achieves 3× lower L2 error at 5 samples (0.040 vs. 0.122) and 14× lower at 100 samples (0.003 vs. 0.043) on NS+EW at Re=400. GNN is the strongest from-scratch competitor at higher sample counts (0.009 at 100 samples), but CoDA-NO still wins by 3× and pulls further ahead in the few-shot regime. U-Net and DeepONet are dramatically outperformed (by 89× and 12× respectively at 5 samples), confirming that architectures without operator structure struggle with irregular meshes and multiphysics coupling. On PDEBench, CoDA-NO (11M params) beats FNO (1.9B params) by up to 43% on prediction error while using only 0.6% of the parameters. Against DPOT (up to 500M params, pretrained on 12 PDE datasets for 1000 epochs), CoDA-NO (11M params, 35 epochs) achieves competitive results on the diffusion equation (0.0081 vs. 0.0073 for DPOT-L-500) but trails on the harder shallow water equation (0.0407 vs. 0.0017), which the authors attribute to insufficient fine-tuning epochs and the greater difficulty of that task.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoDA-NO builds directly on Fourier Neural Operators (FNO) and Geometry-Informed Neural Operators (GINO) as its internal integral and pointwise operators, but departs from them by replacing the fixed-channel mixing of FNO with a codomain-attention mechanism that treats each physical variable as a token function in an infinite-dimensional space. It extends the transformer architecture from finite-dimensional token sequences to function-space tokens, requiring redesigned positional encodings (Variable-Specific Positional Encoding), attention (L² dot product over the domain), and normalization (instance norm over the spatial domain). Unlike prior PDE foundation models (DPOT, MPP) that are restricted to predetermined PDEs with a fixed number of variables and uniform grids, CoDA-NO explicitly handles varying numbers of input functions and irregular geometries, enabling seamless adaptation to new physical variables during fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes the first neural operator architecture that can serve as a foundation model for multiphysics PDEs, where the number and identity of coupled physical variables can change between pretraining and deployment. The demonstrated ability to transfer from single-physics (Navier-Stokes) to multiphysics (fluid-structure interaction, Rayleigh-Bénard convection) with only a few dozen labeled samples and two additional variable encoders opens a practical pathway for reducing the cost of training PDE surrogates in domains where high-fidelity multiphysics simulations are prohibitively expensive. Key limitations include dependence on the number of few-shot examples, restriction to 2D problems in the main experiments, and the absence of physics-informed inductive biases that could further improve sample efficiency. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D multiphysics, whether the codomain-attention mechanism generalizes to PDEs with fundamentally different coupling structures (e.g., electromagnetics + fluid), and how the method performs when pretraining and target PDEs share no physical variables at all.
