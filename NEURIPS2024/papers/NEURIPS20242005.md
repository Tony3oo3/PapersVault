## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) require substantial computational resources at inference, making real-time deployment impractical. While post-training quantization (PTQ) offers a data-efficient path to low-bit inference, its applicability to DiTs remains unexplored due to two inherent difficulties: salient channels with extreme magnitudes in both weights and activations, and significant temporal variability in activation distributions across the multi-timestep denoising process.

## Input / Output

PTQ4DiT operates on pre-trained class-conditional DiT-XL/2 models, consuming a small calibration set (25 timesteps × 32 samples for 256×256; 10 timesteps × 32 samples for 512×512) to estimate salience statistics. The quantized DiT then takes a random noise latent and a class label as input and produces a generated RGB image at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution via a 250/100/50-step DDPM sampling loop, with weights and activations stored at 8-bit or 4-bit precision respectively.

## Method

PTQ4DiT introduces two complementary mechanisms. Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) exploits the observed complementarity that extreme magnitudes do not co-occur in the same channel of activation and weight, constructing diagonal Salience Balancing Matrices B^X and B^W that redistribute channel salience toward the geometric mean of activation and weight magnitudes, thereby reducing the overall quantization range. Spearman's ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) extends this to the temporal dimension by weighting per-timestep activation salience with normalized exponential factors derived from the inverse Spearman's ρ correlation between activation and weight salience, so that timesteps exhibiting stronger complementarity receive greater influence in the final balancing matrices. A re-parameterization scheme then absorbs B^W into the linear layer weights and B^X into the preceding adaLN MLPs or attention matrix multiplications offline, preserving mathematical equivalence (X·W unchanged) while eliminating any extra inference-time computation.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with 250 DDPM timesteps, PTQ4DiT at W8A8 achieves FID 4.63, sFID 17.72, IS 274.86, and Precision 0.8299, closely matching the full-precision DiT-XL/2 (FID 4.53, IS 278.50). At the more challenging W4A8 setting, PTQ4DiT achieves FID 7.09 and IS 201.91, a substantial improvement over the best baseline Q-Diffusion (FID 15.31, IS 134.71). On ImageNet 512×512 with W4A8 and 250 timesteps, PTQ4DiT reaches FID 17.55 and IS 123.49, outperforming the second-best method Q-Diffusion (FID 58.81) by a margin of 41.26 in FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTQ4DiT is compared against four baselines: PTQ4DM, Q-Diffusion, PTQD, and RepQ* (an enhanced version of RepQ-ViT adapted for DiTs). At W8A8, PTQ4DiT slightly trails RepQ* in FID (4.63 vs. 4.51) but surpasses it in IS (274.86 vs. 264.68) and Precision (0.8299 vs. 0.8076), while Q-Diffusion and PTQD lag behind by roughly 1.0 FID points. The gap widens dramatically at W4A8: PTQ4DiT achieves FID 7.09, outperforming Q-Diffusion (15.31) by 8.22, PTQD (16.45) by 9.36, RepQ* (23.21) by 16.12, and PTQ4DM (72.58) by 65.49. PTQ4DM degrades catastrophically at 4-bit weights (FID >70), rendering it essentially unusable. On the higher-resolution ImageNet 512×512 benchmark, PTQ4DiT's advantage is even more pronounced, with a 41.26 FID improvement over Q-Diffusion at W4A8 with 250 timesteps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the channel-wise magnitude redistribution idea from LLM quantization (SmoothQuant, Outlier Suppression) and ViT quantization (RepQ-ViT) to the DiT setting, but identifies two DiT-specific challenges absent in those prior works: extreme-magnitude salient channels in both activations and weights, and their temporal variability across denoising timesteps. The complementarity observation—that activation and weight salient channels do not coincide in the same layer—motivates the geometric-mean balancing, while Spearman's ρ introduces a novel temporal weighting mechanism not present in any prior PTQ method. Unlike U-Net diffusion PTQ methods (Q-Diffusion, PTQ4DM, PTQD), which treat each timestep's quantization more independently, PTQ4DiT explicitly models the multi-timestep distributional shift as a first-class design constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As DiTs become the dominant backbone for next-generation generative models (Sora, PixArt, etc.), an effective PTQ method is practically critical for real-time deployment; PTQ4DiT is the first to demonstrate viable W4A8 quantization for DiTs, reducing model size by 8× with modest quality loss. However, validation is limited to a single model (DiT-XL/2), a single dataset (ImageNet), and class-conditional generation, leaving open whether the complementarity property and temporal calibration generalize to text-to-image, video, or other modalities. The re-parameterization strategy (absorbing balancing matrices into adaLN MLPs and attention multiplications) is a clean engineering contribution that ensures zero inference overhead, making the method immediately deployable.
