## Problem

Egocentric perception research has been dominated by head-mounted cameras, leaving lower-body pose estimation and multi-perspective body tracking largely unexplored. No existing simulator or dataset supports synchronized multi-view body-worn cameras (wrists, knees, pelvis) with realistic motion artifacts from non-rigid mounting, creating a gap for training and evaluating vision-based body pose methods in this emerging setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243383/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes aligned RGB video from six body-worn cameras (head, pelvis, both wrists, both knees) at 118° FOV, originally 640×360 at 30 fps, downsampled to 224×224 at 10 fps in 5-second windows. It outputs per-frame SMPL-X pose parameters in 6D rotation representation, relative root rotation and translation, which are integrated via forward kinematics to yield global 3D body pose and joint positions. The model also receives SMPL-X shape parameters β as a per-person conditioning input.

## Method

The architecture is a multi-view Vision Transformer built on Sparse Video Tube ViTs: a shared sparse view tokenizer (SVT) with interpolated kernels extracts feature vectors from each camera's video, which are then augmented with fixed spatio-temporal position encodings and learnable per-camera view encodings before being concatenated with pose tokens (sinusoidal positional encoding plus a trainable token) and processed by a ViT encoder. The key design choice is predicting relative root transformations (rotation and translation w.r.t. the previous frame) rather than absolute global pose, reducing accumulation of training difficulty; a composite loss supervises SMPL angles, joint positions via forward kinematics, relative and global root pose, velocity, and an L2 regularization on the pose token. The sim-to-real pipeline pretrains on 119 hours of EgoSim-rendered synthetic data (AMASS motions in four Unreal Engine scenes with spring-damper camera mounts) then fine-tunes on 5 hours of real GoPro footage with Xsens ground truth, yielding 3–4× improvement in global MPJPE over training on real data alone.

## Results

On MultiEgoView, the multi-view pose transformer trained on synthetic data alone achieves 0.16 m Global MPJPE and 0.040 m PA-MPJPE on synthetic evaluation, but degrades to 0.77 m / 0.119 m when evaluated directly on real data. Training solely on real data yields 1.23 m Global MPJPE and 0.087 m PA-MPJPE. Pretraining on synthetic data followed by fine-tuning on 80% of real data reduces Global MPJPE to 0.33 m (a 3.7× improvement over real-only) and PA-MPJPE to 0.044 m, demonstrating effective sim-to-real transfer. Even with only 20% of real data for fine-tuning, the model reaches 0.40 m Global MPJPE, confirming the value of the large synthetic dataset for data-scarce real-world settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external pose estimation methods; instead, it ablates different training strategies to isolate the simulator's contribution. The key comparison is between real-only training (1.23 m Global MPJPE, 0.087 m PA-MPJPE) and synthetic-pretrained + real fine-tuning (0.33 m / 0.044 m with 80% real data), showing a 3.7× reduction in global position error and a 49% reduction in pose error. Direct sim-to-real transfer without fine-tuning (0.77 m / 0.119 m) is substantially worse than real-only training on global metrics, confirming that the domain gap is non-trivial but bridgeable with fine-tuning. The cross-participant setting (10 train / 3 test participants) yields 0.35 m Global MPJPE, slightly worse than the standard split, indicating that more participant diversity would further improve generalization. The ablation in Table 4 additionally shows that using all six cameras outperforms head-only (0.18 m vs. 0.345 m Global MPJPE) and head+wrists (0.293 m), validating the multi-body-worn-camera design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AirSim/Unreal Engine simulation platform (Shah et al., 2018) to model body-worn cameras with realistic non-rigid attachment via spring-damper systems, and builds its pose estimation baseline on Sparse Video Tube ViTs (Piergiovanni et al., CVPR 2023). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant egocentric pose estimation literature (Mo2Cap2, xR-EgoPose, EgoCap, UnrealEgo, EgoBody, ARES), which has focused almost exclusively on head-mounted fisheye or stereo cameras, by introducing a multi-body-worn-camera setting (head, pelvis, wrists, knees) that captures lower-body motion and environmental context simultaneously. It also extends the synthetic human-motion dataset line (BEDLAM, Agora, SynBody) by coupling AMASS-driven avatars with configurable wearable camera rigs and motion artifacts, filling a gap that prior simulators (AirSim, Habitat 3, EgoGen) did not address for wearable sensing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MultiEgoView is the first dataset providing synchronized footage from six body-worn cameras with ground-truth 3D poses, enabling research on lower-body tracking, multi-view ego-pose estimation, and activity recognition that head-only datasets cannot support. The sim-to-real transfer results (synthetic pretraining + 20% real fine-tuning achieving 0.40 m global MPJPE vs. 1.23 m for real-only training) demonstrate a practical pathway for training on scarce real data, though global root position estimation remains a clear weakness. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the four scenes, whether human-object and human-human interactions can be incorporated, and whether integrating SLAM or SfM-based localization can resolve the cumulative drift in root translation that limits long-sequence accuracy.
