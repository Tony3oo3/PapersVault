## Problem

Zero-shot vision-language models like CLIP rely on single class prototypes that fail to capture the diversity of visual representations, and they inherit label bias from imbalanced web-scale pre-training data. Existing prompt distribution learning methods require labeled downstream data, and prior debiasing approaches need access to pre-training data or labeled samples—both unavailable in true zero-shot settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240064/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of unlabeled test images and a set of text class descriptions (prompts from SuS-X and CuPL) as its only inputs, following InMaP's convention of including all test images. It outputs a class label per image via a fused and debiased logit vector. No labeled data, pre-training data access, or hyperparameter tuning on a validation set is required at any stage.

## Method

Frolic models each class prototype as a Gaussian with shared covariance, estimating the covariance label-free from the second moment of the marginal image distribution minus the mean of prototype outer products (Eq. 6). The resulting Gaussian discriminant scores are fused with original CLIP scores via confidence matching: a temperature τ_g is found by binary search so that the Gaussian model's average confidence equals CLIP's, eliminating the need for a mixing hyperparameter. Finally, a label-free logit adjustment removes pre-training label bias by iteratively solving (S−I)β=0 for the prior β using pseudo-labels from the fused model, where S is the matrix of expected softmax outputs per pseudo-class. The entire pipeline is training-free, label-free, and requires no hyperparameter search.

## Results

On 10 diverse image classification datasets, Frolic achieves 71.1% average accuracy with CLIP ViT-B/16 and 77.1% with ViT-L/14, surpassing the strongest baseline (CuPL-CLIP) by 2.5% and 2.4% respectively. On ImageNet and its five distribution-shift variants, Frolic reaches 64.4% (ViT-B/16) and 75.1% (ViT-L/14) average accuracy, outperforming CLIP by 4.3% and 3.5%. When combined with InMaP, Frolic further improves to 72.7% (ViT-B/16) and 78.9% (ViT-L/14) on the 10-dataset benchmark, representing a 2.6% average gain over the prior state-of-the-art. The method is fully training-free and requires no hyperparameter tuning, completing in 6.5 minutes on ImageNet versus 6 hours for TPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Frolic consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across both ViT-B/16 and ViT-L/14 backbones. Against the strongest prompt-based baselines, Frolic beats CuPL-CLIP by 2.5% (71.1% vs 68.6%) and GPT4-Prompt by 3.5% on the 10-dataset average with ViT-B/16, and by 2.4% and 3.4% respectively with ViT-L/14. On ImageNet and its distribution-shift variants, Frolic surpasses TDA (63.3%) by 1.1% and CuPL-CLIP (62.7%) by 1.7% in average accuracy with ViT-B/16. For label bias correction specifically, Frolic's logit adjustment outperforms the implicit method (Wang et al., 2022) by 1.6% and TDE (Tang et al., 2020) by 5.6% on average across 11 datasets. In cross-dataset generalization tests (Table 5), Frolic* (built on InMaP) dramatically outperforms CoOp and CoCoOp on fine-grained datasets like Aircraft (71.7% vs 64.5%/65.3%) and SUN (58.0% vs 41.9%/45.7%), demonstrating superior transferability without task-specific training. Against adapter-based methods, Frolic* achieves 64.4% average on ImageNet variants, exceeding LFA (62.5%) by 1.9% and Tip-Adapter (61.6%) by 2.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

Frolic unifies three previously separate lines of work—prompt distribution learning (Lu et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2024), logit adjustment for label bias (Menon et al., 2021; Zhu et al., 2023), and confidence-based model fusion (Kumar et al., 2022)—into a single training-free, label-free framework built on CLIP. The key departure from prior prompt distribution learning is that Frolic estimates the shared Gaussian covariance from the marginal second moment of unlabeled data rather than requiring per-class labels, and it corrects pre-training label bias via a linear system (S−I)β=0 solved iteratively on downstream images, avoiding the need for pre-training data access that plagues existing debiasing methods (TDE, implicit estimation, GLA).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for labeled data, pre-training data access, and hyperparameter tuning simultaneously, Frolic lowers the practical barrier to improving zero-shot CLIP performance, making it applicable in settings where annotations are unavailable or pre-training data is inaccessible due to privacy/copyright constraints. The Gaussian mixture assumption and reliance on the quality of the pre-trained backbone are acknowledged limitations; whether the covariance estimation remains stable with very large class spaces or highly non-Gaussian feature distributions remains an open question. The 1.0% gap between Frolic and its oracle (ground-truth pseudo-labels) version suggests room for improvement in the iterative pseudo-labeling procedure.
