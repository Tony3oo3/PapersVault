## Problem

In federated learning with heterogeneous clients, spectral model sharding partitions weight matrices via SVD to create smaller sub-models for resource-constrained devices. Existing methods like PriSM use heuristic random sampling of SVD terms, leaving the statistical properties of the resulting approximation opaque. This paper asks: what are the principled, optimally-derived sampling strategies for selecting SVD terms that minimize approximation error under different design constraints?

## Input / Output

The server performs SVD on each affine layer's weight matrix and, given each client's keep ratio r (determining n = ⌈Nr⌉ terms), computes optimal inclusion probabilities and auxiliary multipliers on the server side. Each client receives factorized matrices U ∈ R^{c_out×n} and V ∈ R^{c_in×n} (with singular values absorbed as √λ_i) plus a frozen diagonal scaling matrix Ω, trains locally for a fixed number of epochs, and returns updated U and V. The server then aggregates each SVD term separately across participating clients to reconstruct the full global weight matrix.

## Method

The paper derives two closed-form sampling strategies as solutions to constrained optimization problems over inclusion probabilities. The Unbiased strategy enforces E[Ŵ] = W (Horvitz-Thompson multipliers ω_i = π_i^{-1}) and minimizes expected Frobenius discrepancy, yielding inclusion probabilities where the top-t terms are always included and remaining terms are sampled with probability proportional to their singular values. The Collective strategy instead minimizes the Frobenius error of the server's averaged reconstruction from C i.i.d. client estimators, producing a different closed-form structure with bounded multipliers (1 ≤ ω_i ≤ C). Both strategies use Conditional Poisson Sampling to realize the joint distribution while preserving the computed marginals. For local training, the paper introduces learning-rate clipping (capping effective LR at τ=10× nominal) to counteract the large auxiliary multipliers that arise at low keep ratios, combined with Frobenius weight decay and SGD with momentum 0.9.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (ResNet, r=0.1, 1500 communication rounds), the Collective strategy achieves 37.83% accuracy versus 21.13% for Top-n (FedHM) and 18.45% for PriSM, while the Unbiased strategy reaches 35.12%. With extended training (5000 rounds), Collective reaches 60.26% and Unbiased 59.44%, both surpassing the modified PriSM+Wallenius+ClipLR baseline (60.02%). On CIFAR-10 (ResNet, r=0.1), both Unbiased and Collective achieve ~82.6% accuracy compared to 67.44% for Top-n and 71.15% for PriSM. The paper reports that the proposed strategies outperform both baselines on all datasets except Shakespeare, where Top-n shows a slight (non-significant) edge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Top-n (FedHM), both proposed strategies deliver large gains: on CIFAR-100 ResNet at r=0.1, Collective improves accuracy from 21.13% to 37.83% (+16.7 pp) and Unbiased from 21.13% to 35.12% (+14.0 pp); on CIFAR-10 ResNet at r=0.1, both reach ~82.6% versus Top-n's 67.44% (+15.2 pp). Top-n only retains an edge on Shakespeare, where the gap is not statistically significant. Against vanilla PriSM, the advantage is even more pronounced on CIFAR-100 (Collective 37.83% vs. PriSM 18.45% at r=0.1, 1500 rounds), though the ablated PriSM+Wallenius+ClipLR variant narrows the gap substantially (40.36% at 1500 rounds, 60.02% at 5000 rounds). The paper notes that the Collective strategy is more effective at small keep ratios while the Unbiased strategy is preferable at larger r, and that with more communication rounds the more explorative Collective strategy overtakes the modified PriSM baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral model sharding framework introduced by PriSM (Niu et al., 2023) and FedHM (Yao et al., 2022), which partition weight matrices via SVD to create client-specific sub-models in heterogeneous federated learning. While PriSM used a heuristic Wallenius-like sampling distribution and FedHM used deterministic top-n truncation, this work reframes the sampling design as a constrained optimization problem, yielding closed-form inclusion probabilities under two distinct objectives: unbiasedness (via Horvitz-Thompson estimation) and collective multi-client reconstruction. The practical training modifications—frozen auxiliary multipliers and effective learning rate clipping—depart from prior work that set all multipliers to 1, and the use of Conditional Poisson Sampling for the joint distribution is a deliberate choice over the opaque NumPy-based procedure used by PriSM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form solutions for optimal inclusion probabilities provide a principled alternative to heuristic SVD sampling, with all computation performed server-side so that resource-constrained clients bear no additional overhead. The collective estimator is a genuinely new perspective—treating all participating clients' sub-models as a joint reconstruction problem rather than optimizing each client independently—and its convergence to the unbiased estimator as C→∞ provides a useful theoretical bridge. Open questions include whether incorporating data distribution knowledge could further improve the collective strategy, whether the more explorative nature of these methods (which slows early convergence) can be mitigated, and whether the Frobenius norm objective is truly optimal versus Schatten p-norms for p>2 (the paper's numerical experiments suggest not, but the analysis is incomplete). The ANME diagnostic for quantifying explorative vs. exploitative behavior is a reusable analytical tool for the broader sub-model sampling literature.
