## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of dataset distillation (Izzo & Zou; Maalouf et al.) show that d, n, or p data points are required to recover the original model's parameters, creating a large gap with empirical evidence that one point per class often suffices. This paper closes that gap by proving that k distilled points (one per class) are necessary and sufficient for kernel ridge regression in many practical settings, and by deriving a closed-form algorithm that constructs the distilled dataset analytically rather than through iterative optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes the original dataset (X, Y), a fixed feature mapping φ (identity, FCNN, CNN, or Random Fourier Features with p ≤ d), the target number of distilled points m ≥ k, and a regularization λ_S. It outputs a distilled dataset (X_S, Y_S) of size m and the corresponding λ_S. The distilled data X_S can be generated in two modes: initialized from real training images (yielding visually realistic samples) or from random Gaussian noise (yielding privacy-preserving samples that contain no recoverable information about the original data).

## Method

The core result (Theorems 4.1/5.1) shows that for KRR with a surjective feature mapping, W_S = W if and only if the regularized features X_{λ_S}^+ equal D = Y_S^+ W + (I_m − Y_S^+ Y_S)Z for some free matrix Z, and X_S is then recovered via the SVD of D with singular values σ_i = (1 ± √(1−4λ_S σ_i′²))/(2σ_i′). Because φ is surjective, X_S is directly invertible from φ(X_S), making the entire construction a closed-form computation (Algorithm 1) with no iterative optimization. For non-surjective injective mappings (deep NNs), the paper proves k points are insufficient in general but k+1 suffice for deep linear networks (Theorem 5.3). The algorithm achieves up to 15840× speedup over KIP on CIFAR-100 while matching or exceeding its accuracy.

## Results

The proposed analytical distillation algorithm achieves test accuracy matching or exceeding KIP across all datasets while being orders of magnitude faster. On CIFAR-10 (IPC=1), it reaches 47.85% accuracy vs. KIP's 45.83% with a 10.7× speedup; on CIFAR-100 (IPC=1), it achieves 21.58% vs. 20.08% with a 30.8× speedup. At IPC=50 on CIFAR-100, the speedup reaches ~15,840× (25 GPU-seconds vs. ~396,000 GPU-seconds for KIP). The method also exactly recovers the original model's parameters in theory-verification experiments (Table 3), e.g., 93.89% on MNIST for a one-hidden-layer LeakyReLU FCNN with just 1 image per class.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single baseline, KIP (Nguyen et al., NeurIPS 2021), which optimizes a kernel-based loss via iterative gradient descent. The proposed method wins on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100: at IPC=1 it achieves 47.85% vs. 45.83% on CIFAR-10 (+2.02 pp) and 21.58% vs. 20.08% on CIFAR-100 (+1.50 pp). On MNIST the two methods are statistically indistinguishable (93.72% vs. 93.44% at IPC=1, within one standard deviation). The dominant advantage is computational: the analytical solution requires only a single SVD and pseudoinverse computation (~16–25 GPU-seconds regardless of IPC), whereas KIP's iterative optimization scales with IPC and exceeds 396,000 GPU-seconds at IPC=50 on CIFAR-100, yielding a ~15,840× speedup. The paper notes that using a pre-trained (rather than randomly initialized) feature mapping could further close the small accuracy gap on MNIST.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of dataset distillation initiated by Izzo and Zou (who showed d points suffice for LRR and n points for Gaussian-kernel KRR) and Maalouf et al. (who showed p points suffice for shift-invariant kernels via RFF). The key departure is proving that only k points (one per class) are necessary and sufficient for LRR and KRR with surjective feature mappings, a dramatic reduction from d, n, or p. The paper also generalizes beyond prior work by covering invertible NNs, FCNNs, CNNs, and RFF as surjective mappings, and by analyzing the non-surjective (injective) case for deep linear NNs where k+1 points suffice. Unlike prior kernel-based distillation algorithms (KIP, FRePo, RFAD) that rely on iterative bilevel optimization, this work derives closed-form analytical solutions via SVD and Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable guarantee that one data point per class recovers the original KRR model's parameters closes a significant gap between empirical observations (that IPC=1 often works) and theory, and provides a principled, 15840× faster alternative to iterative distillation methods. The differential privacy result (Theorem 6.2) adds a practical privacy-preserving dimension to dataset distillation. However, the analysis is restricted to KRR with fixed feature mappings, which differs from end-to-end neural network training where all parameters are learned; extending to learnable features and determining the minimum number of points for non-surjective deep nonlinear NNs remain open. The empirical validation, while clean, is limited to randomly initialized one-hidden-layer networks and standard small-scale datasets.
