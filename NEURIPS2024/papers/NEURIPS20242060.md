## Problem

End-to-end collaborative trajectory prediction frameworks designed for autonomous driving rely on Lift-Splat-Shoot depth estimation, which degrades severely under aerial perspectives where observation distances span orders of magnitude larger than ground-level ranges. Additionally, existing sparse collaboration strategies (e.g., Where2comm) select transmission regions solely via detection heads, overlooking environmental context that is equally critical for motion forecasting, while full feature exchange exceeds the constrained bandwidth of drone-to-drone links.

## Input / Output

Each drone ingests a 224×480 RGB crop (from 1600×900 originals) at 2 Hz, along with its camera intrinsics/extrinsics and a known flight altitude of 50 m; three historical frames (1 s) are processed jointly. The network outputs future BEV instance segmentation masks and per-instance flow vectors for four subsequent frames (2 s) over a 100 m × 100 m area at 0.5 m resolution (or 50 m × 50 m at 0.25 m), with height discretized into 100 bins over 0–10 m. Inter-drone messages are sparse BEV feature patches restricted to a 25 % transmission ratio.

## Method

The DHD framework comprises two key modules. The Ground-prior-based BEV Generation (GBG) module exploits the geometric fact that a drone's oblique viewing ray intersects the ground plane, yielding a per-pixel depth upper-bound D_upperbound computed from altitude, intrinsics, and extrinsics; rather than regressing full depth, it predicts a simpler per-pixel object height (0–10 m, 100 categorical bins) and derives depth as D_upperbound·(1 − h_pred/H), reducing the estimation range by a factor of k = H/h_max. The Sparse Interaction via Sliding Windows (SISW) module compresses each drone's BEV to a single channel, slides a 7×7 window to compute local feature discrepancy as an information-volume score, and transmits only the top-K regions where the sender's information is complementary to the receiver's (score = I_j ⊙ (1−I_k)), achieving 75 % bandwidth savings. A temporal U-Net decoder (following PowerBEV) then jointly predicts future segmentation and flow, trained end-to-end with cross-entropy and smooth-L1 losses under a temporal discount factor of 0.95.

## Results

On the Air-Co-Pred dataset, DHD achieves 61.4% IoU and 50.4% VPQ in the short-range (50 m × 50 m) setting, and 54.0% IoU and 46.2% VPQ in the long-range (100 m × 100 m) setting, using only 25% transmission ratio. For BEV generation, DHD reduces position deviation by over 20% compared to LSS (0.82 m vs. 1.08 m short-range; 1.46 m vs. 2.18 m long-range). On CoPerception-UAVs for collaborative 3D detection, DHD achieves 0.437 mAP, a 25.2% improvement over BEVDet (0.349) and 12.9% over DVDET (0.387).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DHD outperforms all partially connected intermediate collaboration baselines: it beats Where2comm by 3.9% IoU and 1.8% VPQ (short-range) and 5.8% IoU and 3.8% VPQ (long-range), and surpasses UMC by 4.1% IoU and 2.1% VPQ (short-range). Against fully connected methods, DHD matches V2X-ViT (61.4 vs. 61.5 IoU short) and V2VNet (54.0 vs. 53.8 IoU long) while transmitting only 25% of the data. In BEV generation (Table 1), DHD improves over vanilla LSS by 9.1% IoU and 12.1% VPQ (short-range) and reduces position deviation from 2.18 m to 1.46 m (long-range); it also outperforms DVDET in long-range settings where DVDET degrades relative to LSS. On CoPerception-UAVs 3D detection, DHD leads in mAP (0.437 vs. 0.387 DVDET, 0.371 BEVDet4D) and mATE (0.872 vs. 0.904 DVDET), though DVDET retains a slight edge in orientation error (0.844 vs. 0.869 mAOE). The key advantage of DHD over Where2comm and UMC stems from incorporating environmental context (not just foreground objects) into the sparse interaction selection via the sliding-window information volume mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LSS (Lift-Splat-Shoot) BEV generation paradigm to the aerial domain by replacing full depth estimation with a simpler height estimation guided by the geometric constraint that drone sight lines intersect the ground plane. For collaborative interaction, it departs from Where2comm's detection-head-based region selection by introducing sliding-window feature discrepancy to capture both foreground objects and surrounding environmental context relevant to motion prediction. The prediction decoder follows PowerBEV's temporal U-Net architecture, and the overall framework positions itself at the intersection of collaborative perception (V2VNet, V2X-ViT, UMC) and trajectory prediction, but specifically for the under-explored multi-drone aerial setting where observation geometry fundamentally differs from ground-level autonomous driving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of Air-Co-Pred, the first dataset for multi-drone collaborative trajectory prediction, fills a notable gap in the collaborative perception literature where prior drone datasets (CoPerception-UAVs, VisDrone) supported only detection or 2D tracking. The ground-prior insight—that estimating object height relative to a known ground plane is substantially easier than estimating full depth from aerial perspectives—offers a transferable geometric principle for any elevated-camera BEV generation. However, the work is entirely simulation-based, and the authors acknowledge that real-world challenges (flight turbulence, rough terrain, extrinsic noise, communication latency) remain unaddressed, leaving open whether the geometric prior's advantage persists under realistic sensor degradation.
