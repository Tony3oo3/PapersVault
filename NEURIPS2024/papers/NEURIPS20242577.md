## Problem

In lightweight Transformer-based SISR, each Swin Transformer layer must simultaneously optimize both deep image feature extraction and its own projection space for similarity modeling, a coupled optimization that is difficult under limited parameter budgets and suffers from co-adaptation. The paper observes that trained projection spaces across layers exhibit >0.95 CKA similarity, suggesting they are nearly redundant and can be unified without loss.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a low-resolution RGB image (or depth map, noisy image, or JPEG-compressed image) and produces a super-resolved output at ×2, ×3, or ×4 scale. The input is first mapped to a high-dimensional feature space by a shallow convolutional head, processed through N decoupled Swin Transformer layers with window-based attention, and reconstructed by a tail module. For depth SR, the model is evaluated zero-shot on NYU V2 without any depth-specific training, and for denoising/deblocking it operates on single-channel or RGB inputs at matched resolutions.

## Method

UPS replaces the per-layer learnable projection matrices (P_i^Q, P_i^K, P_i^V) in standard Swin Transformer layers with a single shared learnable matrix U^Q ∈ R^{D×C} used across all layers, while setting V_i = X_i (identity mapping). Self-similarity is computed via Cosine similarity on the projected features followed by a ReLU activation (instead of dot-product + SoftMax), yielding a sparser attention map that activates more non-local pixels. This decoupled design means each D-STL layer only optimizes its convolutional feature extraction, while the projection space is learned globally in a layer-invariant manner, reducing both parameter count and optimization complexity. The unified projection space also confers robustness: under noisy input features or unseen degradations (compression, blur, noise), UPS generalizes better than the coupled baseline because the shared projection is less susceptible to per-layer error accumulation.

## Results

On the ×2 SISR setting, UPS (824K params, 162.5G FLOPs) achieves 38.26/0.9642 PSNR/SSIM on Set5, 34.16/0.9232 on Set14, 32.42/0.9031 on BSD100, 33.08/0.9373 on Urban100, and 39.62/0.9800 on Manga109, outperforming the second-best method (Omni-SR) by 0.34 dB on Manga109. The lighter UPS-S variant (453K params, 90.6G FLOPs) surpasses SwinIR-S (497K params) by 0.50 dB on Manga109 while using fewer parameters and 16% fewer FLOPs. UPS reduces inference FLOPs by 33% relative to its SwinIR-light baseline (162.5G vs. 244.4G) while improving PSNR by up to 0.55 dB across scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UPS consistently outperforms all seven compared baselines across all five standard SISR benchmarks. Against SwinIR-light (the primary baseline), UPS gains 0.50 dB on Manga109 (×2), 0.55 dB (×3), and 0.47 dB (×4) while reducing FLOPs from 244.4G to 162.5G. UPS-S (453K params) surpasses SwinIR-S (497K params) by 0.50 dB on Manga109 ×2 with 16% fewer FLOPs, demonstrating that the decoupled projection sharing is particularly effective under parameter constraints. Compared to DLGSA-light and Omni-SR, UPS leads by 0.33–0.34 dB on Manga109 ×2 and by 0.14–0.19 dB on Urban100 ×2. In the robustness evaluation (Table 7), UPS outperforms both SwinIR-light and NGSwin by up to 0.36 dB on noise-degraded inputs and 0.28 dB on JPEG-compressed inputs without any retraining. In the appendix, UPS-enhanced variants of DRCT and HAT (Tables 5–6) improve over their respective baselines by 0.3–0.9 dB on Manga109 while reducing parameters by 10–17% and FLOPs by 9–19%, confirming the plug-and-play nature of the unified projection sharing across different Swin-Transformer backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SwinIR/Swin Transformer line of window-based attention for SISR (SwinIR, NGSwin, DLGSA, HAT, DRCT), which all employ layer-specific projection matrices for self-attention. The key conceptual departure is the observation that trained projection spaces across layers exhibit >0.95 CKA similarity, motivating a single shared (unified) projection matrix for all layers while retaining per-layer feature extraction. This decoupled optimization strategy is distinct from ShareFormer (which shares static similarity maps between adjacent layers) and from Skip-Attention/LaViT (which reduce attention computations), as UPS computes dynamic similarity maps in a layer-invariant space. The design also incorporates ReLUFormer's ReLU activation in place of Softmax for similarity scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled optimization principle addresses a practical bottleneck in lightweight SISR where limited parameter budgets make simultaneous per-layer feature extraction and projection optimization difficult, and the plug-and-play nature (demonstrated on SwinIR, HAT, DRCT) makes it broadly applicable. The robustness to unseen degradations and depth maps without retraining suggests the unified projection space learns more generalizable similarity structure. However, the paper acknowledges it has not explored large-scale UPS models, and the CKA observation is specific to lightweight configurations, leaving open whether the decoupling principle holds at scale. The 33% FLOPs reduction with consistent PSNR gains makes this a practically relevant efficiency technique for deployment-constrained settings.
