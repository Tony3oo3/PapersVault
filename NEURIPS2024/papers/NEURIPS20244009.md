## Problem

Representation Engineering (RepE) demonstrated that LLM internal hidden states encode high-level concepts, but its proposed editing mechanism—directly incorporating a single representation vector—is fragile, hyperparameter-sensitive, and can degrade general model performance. The paper addresses how to leverage representation-level insights for general, flexible, and bidirectional conceptual editing of LLMs without compromising baseline capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244009/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base decoder-only LLM, a set of target prompts (designed to trigger the desired concept, e.g., 'respond in an angry manner') and anti-target prompts (neutral prompts), plus a target concept label. Representations are extracted by concatenating hidden states from the last 5 layers at the final token position. The output is a LoRA-adapted LLM whose responses consistently exhibit the target concept across all inputs, with the editing being bidirectional (enhance or remove a concept) and requiring only ~100 prompts per category and 3–50 training epochs.

## Method

ARE frames model editing as a GAN-like adversarial game between the LLM (generator) and a lightweight 2-layer representation discriminator (512 hidden neurons). In each epoch, the discriminator is first trained via cross-entropy to classify concatenated hidden-layer representations as exhibiting the target concept or its negation; then the LLM is fine-tuned via LoRA on selected layers to maximize the discriminator's confidence that its generated representations belong to the target class, with the discriminator frozen during this step. This alternating loop iteratively sharpens the discriminator's decision boundary while forcing the generator to produce robust, concept-aligned representations rather than overfitting to a fixed vector. The dynamic, co-adaptive optimization targets also preserve text generation diversity and naturalness, avoiding the severe repetition artifacts observed in static editing baselines like Contrast Vector and Shadow Alignment.

## Results

On the AdvBench jailbreak attack task, ARE reduces the refusal rate of Llama-2-7B-Chat to 0.5% (vs. 5.9% for the best editing-based baseline, Contrast Vector), and to 0.0% on Vicuna-7B and Guanaco-7B. For defense, ARE fine-tuned Llama-2-7B-Chat maintains 99.0% refusal under AutoDAN and 89.7% under GCG, substantially outperforming no-defense (70.0%/51.3%) and ICD (92.3%/74.0%). On TruthfulQA hallucination reduction, ARE achieves 52.14% correct answer rate on Llama2-7B, surpassing ITI (36.84%) and Self-Reminder (34.27%). In text quality, ARE yields Self-BLEU of 0.078 and Repetition-4 of 7.53, closest to human-written text (0.008/1.10) among all editing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARE dominates all editing-based baselines on the attack task: it reduces Llama-2-7B-Chat refusal to 0.5% versus 5.9% (Contrast Vector/RepE [69]), 23.5% (Shadow Alignment [56]), and 20.0% (HEDA [37]), and achieves 0.0% on Vicuna-7B and Guanaco-7B where the best baseline still leaves 0.9–1.1% refusal. Against optimization-based attacks, ARE's defense fine-tuning raises Llama-2-7B-Chat refusal from 51.3% (GCG [71]) to 89.7% and from 70.0% (AutoDAN [29]) to 99.0%, outperforming ICD [50] (74.0%/92.3%) and Self-Reminder [52] (60.2%/85.6%). On hallucination reduction (TruthfulQA), ARE achieves 52.14% correct-answer rate on Llama2-7B, beating ITI [22] (36.84%) and Self-Reminder [52] (34.27%) by 15+ points. Template-based attacks (ICA [50], DeepInception [24]) are far less effective than ARE, with refusal rates of 94.6% and 99.3% on Llama-2-7B-Chat respectively. In text generation quality, ARE's Self-BLEU (0.078) and Repetition-4 (7.53) are substantially better than Shadow Alignment (0.215/23.60), HEDA (0.117/15.78), and Contrast Vector (0.503/22.92), approaching the unedited model (0.035/4.01).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Representation Engineering (RepE) by Zou et al., which demonstrated that high-level concepts (harmlessness, honesty) have identifiable low-rank signatures in LLM hidden states. However, the authors argue that RepE's proposed editing mechanism—adding a single contrast vector to hidden layers—is unreliable, overfitted, and can degrade general model performance. ARE departs from this by replacing the static vector with a learned discriminator trained in an adversarial loop with the LLM itself (analogous to GAN's generator-discriminator dynamic), combined with LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning. The framework also positions itself against ReFT, which fine-tunes representations but is not designed for bidirectional conceptual editing, and against prior editing-based jailbreak methods (Shadow Alignment, HEDA) that lack the robustness of adversarial training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARE provides a unified, interpretable framework for bidirectional conceptual editing—both strengthening and removing alignment—that outperforms existing editing-based methods by large margins (e.g., reducing Llama2 refusal rate from 20% to 0.5% under attack, and boosting defense refusal to 99% under AutoDAN). The adversarial training loop offers a principled explanation for why the editing is more robust than static vector injection, and the t-SNE visualizations provide transparency into how representations converge. Key limitations include dependence on carefully curated (or LLM-generated) target/anti-target prompt sets, which limits generalizability to concepts not easily elicited by simple suffixes, and the dual-use risk that the same framework can strip safety alignment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-concept or compositional editing, whether it scales beyond 7B models without additional tuning, and how to automate the dataset construction step without human or LLM curation.
