## Problem

Current RL research prioritizes solution quality through large, overparameterized networks, ignoring the financial, environmental, and computational costs of training and deploying such models. Existing sparse-training methods for RL (RiGL, RLx2) rely on gradient-based topology evolution and fail at high sparsity levels (95%) due to distribution shift during online training, while multi-task RL approaches focus on gradient manipulation or architecture modifications without exploring task-specific sparse sub-networks. This paper asks whether one can discover extremely sparse task-specific sub-networks (neural pathways) within a single shared network for both online and offline multi-task RL, achieving competitive or superior performance while using only 5% of the parameters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment state vectors (e.g., MuJoCo joint positions/velocities, MetaWorld robot observations) and the current neural network parameters, producing a binary mask of the same dimensionality that selects the top-k most important parameters (typically 5% of total) for each task. The mask is computed via gradient-based saliency on the most recent episode's transitions rather than the full replay buffer, and in the multi-task setting each task receives its own pair of masks (one for the actor, one for the critic) over the same shared base parameters. The final output is a sparse policy that maps states to continuous actions, with the sparsity enabling reduced FLOPs and energy consumption at inference.

## Method

The core contribution is Data Adaptive Pathway Discovery (DAPD), which adapts the SNIP-style saliency criterion |θ_q · ∂L/∂θ_q| to the non-stationary data distribution of online RL. At each episode boundary during a warm-up phase, the mask is recomputed using a K-length moving average of saliency scores evaluated only on the most recent episode's transitions (excluding historical data, which is harmful during early random exploration). Once the episodic return exceeds a threshold TH, the mask is frozen for the remainder of training, preventing oscillation among multiple lottery-ticket sub-networks and reducing run-to-run variance. For offline RL, a single-shot pathway discovery using the same criterion on the fixed dataset suffices, with tasks trained in parallel via Hogwild-style asynchronous gradient updates. The approach is integrated into SAC (online) and BCQ/IQL (offline), requiring no changes to the learning objective or gradient update rules—only the parameter subset being optimized changes per task.

## Results

On MuJoCo continuous control at 95% sparsity (5% active parameters), SAC-DAPD outperforms the dense SAC baseline on all four tasks: HalfCheetah-v2 (9028.02 vs. 8568.1), Walker2d-v2 (3846.3 vs. 2972.49), Hopper-v2 (3359.88 vs. 3228.5), and Ant-v2 (3916.65 vs. 3538.22) average episodic return over 1M steps. On MetaWorld MT10 (online multi-task), DAPD achieves 77% mean success rate using only 17k parameters and 16.9k FLOPs, compared to 49% for dense SAC (340k params, 339K FLOPs), representing a ~20× reduction in energy consumption. In the offline setting, BCQ+PD achieves a perfect 100% success rate on MetaWorld MT10 with 67k parameters, versus 81.5% for the dense multi-task baseline (1.34M params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAPD decisively outperforms both sparse-training baselines RiGL and RLx2 at 95% sparsity across all four MuJoCo tasks, with margins of 2–15× (e.g., Walker2d-v2: 3846 vs. 260 for RiGL and 518 for RLx2), confirming that gradient-based topology evolution methods fail at high sparsity levels. Surprisingly, DAPD also exceeds the dense SAC network on every MuJoCo task, which the authors attribute to sparsity acting as a regularizer that mitigates primacy bias. SNIP applied at initialization (single-shot, no adaptive updates) performs poorly, validating the necessity of DAPD's warm-up and adaptive reconfiguration. On MetaWorld MT10 (online multi-task), DAPD (77%) beats PCGrad (72%), SM (73%), and SAC+ME (74%) while using only 5% of the parameters, but is outperformed by CARE (84%), which relies on a pre-trained language model for task context. In the offline setting, BCQ+PD (100%) and IQL+PD (97.3%) both surpass the dense multi-task (81.5%, 79.1%) and multi-head multi-task (95.9%, 96.5%) baselines while using 20–25× fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SNIP pruning criterion (Lee et al., 2018) from a one-shot initialization technique into an adaptive, data-driven pathway discovery procedure (DAPD) for online RL, where the mask is periodically reconfigured during a warm-up phase to track distribution shifts before being frozen. It departs from prior sparse RL methods (RiGL, RLx2) that rely on gradient-based topology evolution and fail at 95% sparsity, and from multi-task RL approaches (PCGrad, CARE, SM) that modify gradients or architectures, by instead assigning each task to a distinct sparse sub-network of a shared base network. The offline setting builds directly on single-shot pruning for offline RL (Arnob et al., 2021), while the online setting introduces the warm-up-and-freeze strategy motivated by the lottery ticket hypothesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a claimed 20× energy reduction via sparse matrix multiplication, making RL policies deployable on edge devices and reducing the carbon footprint of training and inference. However, the energy savings are contingent on SpMM-aware hardware, and the method is validated only on MuJoCo continuous control and MetaWorld manipulation with a small number of seeds. Open questions include whether pathway discovery generalizes to discrete-action or vision-based tasks, how pathways interact when tasks are highly correlated, and whether the warm-up threshold hyperparameter can be eliminated. The finding that a 5%-parameter sub-network can outperform the full dense network challenges the assumption that sparsity necessarily trades off against performance in RL.
