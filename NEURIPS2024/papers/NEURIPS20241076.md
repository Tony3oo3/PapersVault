## Problem

LLMs exhibit a significant reasoning gap between English and non-English, especially low-resource languages. Existing remedies either fine-tune LLMs to relearn reasoning per language (relearning-based) or replace non-English inputs with English translations (replacement-based), both of which underutilize the LLM's built-in reasoning and its own imperfect but useful non-English language understanding. The paper asks how to preserve and leverage these built-in capabilities while still compensating for the LLM's weaker multilingual understanding.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query in any language and produces a free-form text response (e.g., a step-by-step mathematical solution). Unusually, the query is consumed in two parallel forms: the multilingual model encodes it into a dense hidden-state representation that is projected into the LLM's space, while the LLM simultaneously embeds the original tokens. These two representations are concatenated with a trainable separator token before being fed to the LLM, so the model sees both the external language-agnostic encoding and its own native token embeddings at inference time.

## Method

MindMerger couples a frozen multilingual encoder (e.g., mT5-xl) with a frozen LLM (e.g., Llama-2-7B) through a lightweight two-layer MLP mapping layer and a single trainable separator token; no parameters of either backbone are updated. The multilingual model's hidden states are projected into the LLM's representation space and concatenated with the LLM's own token embeddings (augmentation rather than replacement), allowing the LLM to draw on both external and built-in language understanding. Training proceeds in two stages: a mapping stage that uses general bilingual translation pairs to teach the LLM to interpret the external representation (using a replacement-based input to force reliance on the multilingual model), followed by an augmentation stage that uses query-translation task data to teach collaborative use of both representations. This design avoids the quality loss of autoregressive decoding inherent in translation-based baselines and keeps the LLM's reasoning capabilities intact by never fine-tuning its weights.

## Results

On the MGSM multilingual mathematical reasoning benchmark, MindMerger-Soft achieves 57.3% average accuracy across all 10 languages, outperforming the best baseline (Translate-En at 50.6%) by 6.7%. For low-resource languages (Bengali, Thai, Swahili), the improvement is even more pronounced at 8.0% over the best baseline (53.5% vs. 45.5%). On the out-of-domain MSVAMP dataset, MindMerger-Soft reaches 60.4% average accuracy (3.2% over the best baseline), and on the X-CSQA commonsense reasoning and XNLI natural language inference benchmarks, it leads by at least 4.1% and 1.9% respectively. Notably, MindMerger-Soft achieves these gains without updating any LLM parameters, training only a small mapping layer (~10M parameters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MindMerger-Soft outperforms all six baselines on every evaluated dataset. Against the relearning-based methods (MonoReason, MultiReason-Lora, MultiReason-Full, QAlign), MindMerger-Soft leads by 6.9–19.0% on MGSM average accuracy, with the largest gap over MonoReason (57.3% vs. 38.3%), which suffers from near-zero performance on low-resource languages (6.9% Lrl.). Against the replacement-based methods, MindMerger-Soft beats Translate-En (50.6%) by 6.7% and LangBridge (50.2%) by 7.1% on MGSM; the advantage is particularly stark on low-resource languages where MindMerger-Soft reaches 53.5% versus 41.2% (Translate-En) and 45.5% (LangBridge). MindMerger-Hard, which augments the LLM prompt with translated text rather than dense representations, also surpasses all baselines (54.7% on MGSM) but trails MindMerger-Soft by 2.6%, confirming that dense encoder representations are more effective than decoded text. On the XNLI language understanding benchmark, MindMerger-Soft achieves 78.4% average accuracy, exceeding LangBridge (76.5%) and Translate-En (75.1%), while MindMerger-Hard underperforms (63.2%), suggesting that the augmentation strategy is most beneficial when the LLM's own language understanding is leveraged alongside external representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

MindMerger departs from both dominant strategies for multilingual reasoning: relearning-based fine-tuning (MultiReason, QAlign) that overwrites the LLM's built-in reasoning, and replacement-based methods (Translate-En, LangBridge) that discard the LLM's native multilingual understanding in favor of English text or encoder hidden states. Conceptually, it extends the model-merging paradigm popularized in vision-language work (BLIP-2, MiniGPT-4) to the multilingual reasoning setting, but with a key distinction: rather than replacing the LLM input, it augments the original token embeddings with projected encoder representations, preserving both the LLM's reasoning and its (imperfect) language understanding. The two-stage training scheme—first mapping the external representation space via general bilingual pairs, then learning collaborative utilization via task-specific data—draws on the curriculum-learning intuition but is specifically designed to prevent catastrophic forgetting by freezing both the LLM and the multilingual encoder throughout.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LLMs retain useful, non-trivial multilingual understanding that is systematically wasted by replacement strategies, and that a lightweight mapping layer (10M parameters) can unlock this capability without any LLM parameter updates. This has practical implications for deploying multilingual reasoning in resource-constrained settings where fine-tuning a 7B+ LLM is infeasible. However, the effective boundaries remain unclear: the paper acknowledges that a multilingual model significantly weaker than the LLM may not help, and a much stronger one may render the LLM's built-in understanding redundant. Future work on extending MindMerger to code generation and other professional skills, as well as understanding when augmentation vs. replacement is optimal, would strengthen the contribution.
