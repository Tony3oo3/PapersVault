## Problem

It is unclear whether LLMs possess metacognitive knowledge of their own reasoning skills, and whether such knowledge can be extracted to improve their problem-solving. Existing math benchmarks partition problems using broad human-assigned topics (e.g., 'algebra,' 'probability'), and standard in-context example selection (fixed, random, or complexity-based) ignores the specific skill a problem actually requires, leaving a gap between what the model needs to do and what examples it is shown.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a new math question and a pre-built Skill Exemplar Repository (coarse skill labels paired with question/answer exemplars drawn from training data) as input, and produces both a skill label for the question and a solution (text-based CoT reasoning or code-based PAL program). The repository is constructed offline: GPT-4-0613 first assigns a fine-grained skill name to each of ~7,000 training questions, then semantically clusters them into 22 (GSM8K) or 117 (MATH) interpretable coarse skills, after which K exemplars per skill are retained.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage automated skill discovery pipeline: GPT-4 labels each training question with a fine-grained skill (yielding ~5,000 unique labels for MATH), then performs semantic clustering to consolidate these into coarse, human-interpretable skills (117 for MATH, 22 for GSM8K). A Skill Exemplar Repository maps each coarse skill to its corresponding question/answer pairs from the training set. At inference, the LLM identifies which coarse skill applies to a new question, and K randomly selected exemplars for that skill are injected as in-context examples, replacing the fixed or randomly chosen examples in standard CoT or PAL prompting. This skill-matched selection outperforms topic-based, complexity-based (ComplexCoT), and retrieval-based (Retrieval-RSD) alternatives, yielding 11.6% improvement over CoT on MATH and 7.52% over PAL for code generation. The approach transfers across models (GPT-4-derived skills improve Mixtral-8x7B) and datasets (GSM8K skills boost SVAMP, ASDIV, and MAWPS), and extends beyond math to alignment via in-context learning.

## Results

On the MATH dataset (competition-level problems), the proposed CoT+Skill-Based approach achieves 53.88% overall accuracy with GPT-4-0613, a 11.6% absolute improvement over standard CoT (42.2%) and 3.5% over both Complex CoT (50.30%) and Topic-Based (50.31%). On GSM8K, the method reaches 94.31% (95.38% with self-consistency maj@5) versus 93.00% for CoT. For program-aided prompting (PAL), skill-based exemplars yield a 7.52% improvement over the PAL baseline on MATH, and the approach transfers effectively to weaker models (Mixtral 8×7B: 32.44% with skill-based + SC vs. 28.4% for CoT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines. Against standard CoT, the Skill-Based approach wins by 11.6% on MATH (53.88% vs. 42.2%) and by 1.31% on GSM8K (94.31% vs. 93.00%). Against Complex CoT (which selects longer/more complex in-context examples), Skill-Based wins by 3.58% on MATH, demonstrating that skill-relevance matters more than example complexity. Against Topic-Based (grouping by broad MATH topics like 'algebra' or 'geometry'), Skill-Based wins by 3.57%, confirming that fine-grained skill labels outperform coarse topic categories. Against Retrieval-RSD (cosine-similarity-based example selection), Skill-Based achieves 82.03% vs. 76.8% on GSM8K with GPT-3.5-Turbo. Against PAL (program-aided prompting), the skill-based variant improves code-generation accuracy by 7.52% on MATH, and in cross-dataset transfer experiments, CoT+Skill-Based outperforms the CoT+PAL hybrid on 4 out of 6 datasets (SVAMP, SingleOP, SingleEQ, AddSub, MultiArith, ASDIV).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context learning and prompting literature (CoT, PAL, self-consistency) by replacing fixed or heuristically-selected in-context examples with skill-matched exemplars drawn from an LLM-constructed repository. Conceptually, it departs from ComplexCoT (which selects examples by reasoning-chain length) and Retrieval-RSD (which uses embedding similarity) by instead leveraging an LLM's own skill taxonomy, framed as 'metacognitive knowledge' borrowed from human pedagogy. The automated two-stage pipeline (fine-grained skill labelling followed by semantic clustering) is the core methodological contribution, distinguishing it from prior skill-induction work that operates on model parameters rather than black-box LLM interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 11.6% improvement over CoT on MATH and the demonstrated transferability to weaker models (Mixtral) and unseen datasets suggest that skill-aligned example selection is a broadly applicable, low-cost augmentation to existing prompting pipelines. The alignment experiment hints at domain-agnostic applicability, though improvements there are modest. Key limitations include reliance on GPT-4 for skill discovery (limiting reproducibility), single-skill-per-question assignment that misses compound-skill problems, and the fact that gains are in-context only with no fine-tuning explored. An open question is whether skill-annotated data could bootstrap model capabilities through fine-tuning, potentially reducing dependence on a single frontier model.
