## Problem

Large language models exhibit substantial accuracy drops on complex, multi-step mathematical reasoning tasks, and existing prompting methods like Tree of Thought and Graph of Thought, while improving performance, require hand-crafted, problem-specific prompts that limit their generalizability across different mathematical domains. This paper addresses both the insufficient reasoning capability and the poor generalizability of current prompting approaches simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241691/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single math problem statement as text input and produces a final numerical answer along with the derivation steps. Internally, the system first decomposes the problem into a structured condition list and an objective, then iteratively expands the condition list over up to five rounds before generating a step-by-step solution. The Executor agent leverages GPT-4 Turbo's code interpreter to perform exact arithmetic, so the final output is a verified numerical result rather than a free-form explanation.

## Method

MACM is a three-agent prompting framework (Thinker, Judge, Executor) that iteratively mines new conditions from a growing condition list until the objective is deemed achievable. The Thinker proposes candidate conditions derived from the current known conditions, the Judge validates each candidate with a binary True/False verdict (discarding incorrect ones), and the Executor executes the final computation steps using code. The key architectural insight is that by abstracting every problem into a generic condition-objective structure, the same fixed set of role prompts applies uniformly across algebra, geometry, number theory, and other domains without task-specific prompt engineering. Theoretically, the paper shows that mapping the full thought space onto the condition list preserves the probability of reaching the correct answer, and empirically MACM raises GPT-4 Turbo accuracy on MATH level-5 problems from 54.68% to 76.73% while outperforming ToT by 17 percentage points on the 24-point game using the same model.

## Results

On the MATH dataset, MACM raises GPT-4 Turbo's overall accuracy from 72.78% (I-O) to 87.92%, a 15.14-point gain. On the most challenging Level 5 problems, accuracy jumps from 54.68% to 76.73%. On the 24-point game, MACM with GPT-4 reaches 91% accuracy, surpassing ToT (b=5) at 74% by 17 percentage points, and with GPT-4 Turbo plus code verification achieves 99%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MACM outperforms all baselines on the MATH dataset overall: it beats I-O by 15.14 points (87.92% vs. 72.78%), CoT by 13.56 points, SC-CoT by 7.8 points, and the prior SOTA CSV+Voting by 3.6 points. On the 24-point game, MACM (GPT-4, no code) achieves 91% versus ToT (b=5) at 74% and ToT (b=1) at 45%, and even GPT-3.5 with MACM (67%) exceeds GPT-4 with ToT (b=1). On sequence sorting (64 elements), MACM with GPT-3.5 reaches 92% versus GoT's 89.06%, and GPT-4 Turbo with MACM achieves 100%. On TheoremQA, MACM (79.41%) surpasses PoT (55.88%) by over 23 points. On SciBench, GPT-4 Turbo+MACM leads all baselines across all three math sub-tasks (diff: 60.98%, stat: 77.78%, calc: 76.2%), exceeding the best prior method (GPT-4Turbo+CoT) by 7–21 points depending on sub-task.

## Relation to Prior Work

MACM sits at the end of the prompting-engineering lineage (I-O → CoT → SC-CoT → ToT → GoT) but departs from the hierarchical tree/graph search paradigm of ToT and GoT by replacing it with a flat, accumulating Condition List verified by a dedicated Judge agent. The multi-agent decomposition (Thinker for ideation, Judge for verification, Executor for computation) repurposes the general multi-agent coordination idea into a prompting context, and the key conceptual move is abstracting each problem into conditions and an objective so that a single set of generalized prompts can be reused across problem types—something ToT and GoT explicitly cannot do without task-specific prompt engineering. The theoretical framing of mapping the full thought space onto a compressed condition list provides a new analytical lens not present in prior prompting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 22-point jump on Level-5 MATH problems (54.68% → 76.73%) and the demonstrated cross-task generalizability (one prompt set for algebra, geometry, number theory, and even logic reasoning) address two practical pain points that ToT/GoT leave unresolved. However, geometry remains a clear weak spot (62.74% overall, 50% at Level 5), the method incurs substantial API-call overhead, and the absence of error bars or multi-seed runs (greedy decoding only) limits confidence in the exact margins. Open questions include whether the condition-mining loop scales to open-ended or proof-based mathematics, whether the approach transfers to non-English or multimodal settings, and whether the gains persist as base models improve natively at multi-step reasoning.
