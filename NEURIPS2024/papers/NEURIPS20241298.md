## Problem

Robot manipulation policies trained on limited cross-embodiment data suffer from suboptimal data utilization when they fit observations to actions without modeling the underlying physical dynamics. Existing GPT-style approaches (e.g., GR-1) predict video and actions autoregressively, but this next-frame paradigm captures only short-horizon transitions and cannot exploit the multi-frame, long-horizon dynamics awareness that video diffusion models naturally encode. The paper asks whether a video diffusion model's implicit understanding of environment dynamics can be directly repurposed to improve action prediction accuracy, particularly in data-scarce regimes.

## Input / Output

In Stage 1, the model consumes 256×256 third-person camera frames (2 historical + 2 future, sampled at interval 3) tokenized by VideoAutoencoderKL, plus a CLIP-encoded language instruction, and outputs a denoised future-frame prediction. In Stage 2, inputs expand to 224×224 third-person and wrist camera images, language, and proprioceptive state (fed as extra keys/values into the layer-wise adapter); the model outputs a sequence of 10–12 action steps, each comprising 7-DoF end-effector pose deltas (x, y, z, roll, pitch, yaw) and a binary gripper command, decoded by a small diffusion-based action head (L=100 steps).

## Method

VidMan adopts a two-stage paradigm inspired by dual-process theory. Stage 1 (Dynamics-aware Visionary) pre-trains an Open-Sora-based video diffusion transformer (STDiT-XL/2, 12 layers) on the Open X-Embodiment dataset to denoise future-frame embeddings conditioned on zero-padded historical frames (concatenated along the channel dimension) and language, learning a long-horizon dynamics prior. Stage 2 (Dynamics-modulated Action) converts the same VDT into an implicit inverse dynamics model by inserting a lightweight layer-wise self-attention adapter (multi-head attention + gated FFN, à la Flamingo) after every VDT block; h learnable action tokens are concatenated with each block's output and fused through the adapter to produce action embeddings. Crucially, the iterative denoising loop is disabled by fixing the diffusion step at k=K (pure Gaussian noise input), so the model extracts dynamics-modulated features in a single forward pass without generating future frames. A compact diffusion-based action head (L=100) then decodes the fused action embeddings into executable 7-DoF trajectories, keeping inference latency low for real-time control.

## Results

On the CALVIN zero-shot long-horizon benchmark, VidMan achieves an average task length of 3.42, outperforming the strongest 2D baseline GR-1 (3.06) by 11.7% relative improvement and the best 3D baseline 3D Diffuser Actor (3.35) by 2.1%. On the OXE offline evaluation, VidMan surpasses Octo-base by 5.6% on Bridge, 2.6% on Taco Play, 9.9% on Cable Routing, and 9.0% on Autolab UR5 in average xyz-angle accuracy. On RLBench-100, VidMan achieves 67.4% average success rate, exceeding Act3D (65.1%), RVT (62.9%), and PerAct (42.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VidMan outperforms all 13 baselines across the three evaluation suites. On CALVIN, it beats the closest 2D transformer-based competitor GR-1 by 0.36 in Avg. Len. (11.7% relative gain) and edges out the 3D Diffuser Actor (3.35) by 0.07, despite not using depth information. On OXE offline metrics, VidMan consistently surpasses Octo-base (the strongest open-source generalist policy) by 2.6–9.9% in average xyz-angle accuracy, with the largest gains on small-scale datasets (Cable Routing +9.9%, Autolab UR5 +9.0%), demonstrating superior data efficiency from the two-stage training. It also outperforms its own GPT-style ablation (VidMan-GPT) on all four OXE sub-datasets, confirming the benefit of multi-frame diffusion over autoregressive next-frame prediction. On RLBench-100, VidMan achieves 67.4% average success, surpassing Act3D (65.1%) and RVT (62.9%), though it trails Act3D on specific tasks like Stack Blocks (48 vs. 12) and Put in Cupboard (32.8 vs. 51). The gains are most pronounced in data-scarce settings, where the dynamics-aware pre-training provides a stronger inductive bias than co-training from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

VidMan builds directly on Open-Sora's video diffusion transformer (STDiT) as its backbone, repurposing a text-to-video generation model into a robot manipulation policy. It is most closely related to GR-1, which also uses a GPT-style framework to jointly predict video and actions, but VidMan departs by using multi-frame video diffusion (denoising all future frames simultaneously) rather than autoregressive next-token prediction, and by decoupling dynamics learning from action prediction into two distinct stages. The layer-wise self-attention adapter, inspired by Flamingo, serves as the bridge between the pre-trained video model and the diffusion-based action head, while the overall two-stage design is motivated by dual-process theory from cognitive neuroscience.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that implicit dynamics encoded in video diffusion pre-training can be transferred to action prediction without explicit future-frame generation at inference time, offering a practical path to data-efficient robot learning. The ablation showing that co-training (learning the joint distribution P(a_t, s_{t+1}|s_t)) substantially underperforms the two-stage inverse-dynamics formulation (P(a_t|s_t, s_{t+1})) provides a useful design principle for future video-to-action pipelines. Key limitations remain: the model is 2D-only without 3D perception, relies on a relatively weak CLIP text encoder, and has not been validated on real robots. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex multi-camera or dexterous manipulation settings and whether the pure-noise input trick in stage 2 generalizes beyond the tested architectures.
