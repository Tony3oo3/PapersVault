## Problem

Linear scalarizations are provably incapable of exploring concave regions of the Pareto frontier, and while various non-linear scalarizations have been proposed heuristically, no prior work characterized the finite-sample convergence rate at which any scalarization family approximates the full Pareto frontier. This paper asks: which simple, oblivious (non-adaptive) scalarization minimizes hypervolume regret, and what is the fundamental lower bound on how fast any algorithm can explore the Pareto frontier?

## Input / Output

The method consumes k objective values y ∈ R^k (or noisy linear reward observations Θ*·a + ξ in the bandit setting) along with weight vectors λ drawn i.i.d. from the uniform distribution on the positive orthant sphere S_+^{k-1}. It produces T Pareto-optimal points {y_1, …, y_T} whose dominated hypervolume approaches that of the full frontier at rate O(T^{-1/k}), and in the bandit setting, a sequence of actions a_1, …, a_T selected via a scalarized UCB rule. The reference point z and bounds B_l ≤ y_i − z ≤ B_u are required for the regret guarantee.

## Method

The core scalarization is s_λ^HV(y) = min_i (y_i/λ_i)^k, whose sharp level curves are tangent to the Pareto frontier exactly in the angular direction of λ, so uniform random weights yield a uniform angular spread of selected points. The convergence proof decomposes hypervolume regret into a uniform-concentration term (bounded via Rademacher complexity and Dudley's integral over the function class of max-over-set scalarizations, exploiting the L_λ-Lipschitz smoothness of s_λ^HV in λ) and a finite-sample estimation term, yielding O(T^{-1/(k+1)} + √(ln(1/δ))/√T). A novel packing argument on the Pareto sphere establishes a matching Ω(T^{-1/(k-1)}) lower bound for any algorithm. For the stochastic linear bandit setting, the EXPLOREUCB algorithm alternates between isotropic exploration steps and scalarized UCB exploitation, and a non-Euclidean ℓ_p regret analysis (setting p=∞ for the hypervolume scalarization) removes the polynomial k-dependence present in prior bounds, giving Õ(dT^{-1/2} + T^{-1/k}).

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is that hypervolume scalarizations with uniformly random weights achieve a sublinear hypervolume regret of O(T^{-1/k}) (Theorem 7), with a matching lower bound of Ω(T^{-1/k}) for any algorithm (Theorem 8), establishing asymptotic optimality. For the multiobjective stochastic linear bandit setting, the proposed EXPLOREUCB algorithm achieves hypervolume regret of Õ(dT^{-1/2} + T^{-1/k}) (Theorem 11), removing the polynomial dependence on k present in prior work. Empirically, across synthetic Pareto frontiers, linear bandits (k up to 10), and BBOB bi-objective benchmarks (d up to 24), the hypervolume scalarization with uniform weights consistently achieves faster hypervolume convergence than both linear and Chebyshev scalarizations, with the gap widening as the number of objectives increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EHVI (Hupkens et al., 2015) on BBOB bi-objective benchmarks, the UCB-hypervolume scalarization is competitive in cumulative hypervolume but produces more diverse Pareto points by exploring the extreme ends of the frontier, whereas EHVI tends to over-concentrate on the central portion, limiting its hypervolume gain—this gap becomes more pronounced at higher input dimensions (d=16, 24). Against the boxed weight distribution of Paria et al. (2018) in the linear bandit setting, the uniform S_+ distribution used with hypervolume scalarization consistently outperforms the boxed variant across all tested configurations (k=2, 6, 10 objectives). Within the scalarization family, the hypervolume scalarization dominates the linear scalarization on all frontier types (concave, convex, mixed) and generally outperforms the Chebyshev scalarization, particularly on concave frontiers and when k ≥ 5, where the non-uniform curvature of the Pareto frontier combined with a stationary weight distribution causes linear and Chebyshev methods to stall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Golovin and Zhang (2020), which introduced random hypervolume scalarizations for black-box multiobjective optimization, by providing the first finite-sample sublinear hypervolume regret bounds and proving their optimality via a novel packing-argument lower bound. It departs from the broader scalarization literature (linear, Chebyshev, piecewise-linear) by showing that the hypervolume scalarization with uniform random weights is provably optimal for hypervolume regret, a property no prior scalarization had been shown to possess. The linear bandit analysis introduces a non-Euclidean ℓ_p regret framework that removes the polynomial k-dependence present in Golovin and Zhang's earlier bounds, and the empirical comparisons position the method against EHVI, ParEgo-style bounding-box weight distributions, and standard multiobjective evolutionary algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The matching upper and lower bounds establish that a simple, fully parallelizable, non-adaptive ensemble of non-linear scalarizations is asymptotically optimal for hypervolume regret, meaning no adaptive or model-specific algorithm can improve the T^{-1/k} rate. This provides a clean theoretical benchmark for evaluating more complex multiobjective strategies (adaptive reweighting, EHVI, evolutionary methods) and suggests that for many practical settings, the added complexity of adaptive methods may not yield fundamental gains in frontier coverage. Open questions include whether the rate can be improved under structural assumptions on the Pareto set (e.g., smoothness, convexity), how the results extend to non-linear bandit or general stochastic optimization settings, and whether the k^{-1} degradation in the rate can be mitigated for the increasingly common high-k regimes in modern ML systems.
