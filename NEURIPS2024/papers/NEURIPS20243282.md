## Problem

Novel view synthesis from unconstrained tourist photo collections is challenged by variable appearances (different lighting, weather, camera settings) and transient occluders, which cause ghosting and over-smoothing artifacts in standard NeRF or 3DGS. While NeRF-based in-the-wild methods (NeRF-W, Ha-NeRF, CR-NeRF) handle these issues, their implicit volumetric ray-marching incurs prohibitive training times (60–100 hours) and sub-frame-per-second rendering. The gap is an approach that retains 3DGS's real-time efficiency while robustly modeling appearance variation and suppressing transient objects.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unconstrained photo collection (downsampled 2× from the Phototourism dataset), per-image camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and a single reference image that conditions the appearance of the rendered novel view. The reference image is consumed by a 2D Parsing UNet (ResNet-18 encoder) to produce a visibility mask and global appearance embedding, and is back-projected via rendered depth to build the triplane color maps. Output is a real-time novel view rendering (~225 FPS on a single RTX3090) plus the underlying 3D Gaussian scene, with the ability to swap the reference image at inference time for appearance or style transfer.

## Method

Wild-GS decomposes each Gaussian's appearance into three hierarchical components: a global appearance embedding (UNet features → global average pooling → MLP) capturing scene-wide illumination/tone, a local appearance embedding obtained by sampling a triplane at the Gaussian's 3D center (position-aware reflectance), and a learnable intrinsic feature encoding material properties invariant to environment. The triplane is constructed explicitly by back-projecting the masked reference image into 3D, projecting points onto six planes (three axes plus their reverses), and processing the concatenated projections with a 3D UNet; an AABB crop confines sampling to the region containing most Gaussians for efficiency. A shared fusion MLP concatenates all three components to predict per-Gaussian spherical harmonic coefficients for RGB rasterization, preserving the original 3DGS rendering pipeline. Transient objects are suppressed via an unsupervised 2D visibility mask (trained by masking the rendering loss), and geometry is stabilized with a depth regularization term using Depth Anything monocular estimates and a masked Pearson correlation loss.

## Results

On the Phototourism dataset, Wild-GS achieves the best PSNR across all three scenes: 29.65 (Brandenburg Gate), 24.99 (Sacre Coeur), and 24.45 (Trevi Fountain), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art CR-NeRF by approximately 3.1, 2.9, and 3.0 dB respectively. Wild-GS also attains the highest SSIM (0.9333, 0.8776, 0.8081) and lowest LPIPS (0.0951, 0.1270, 0.1622) on all scenes. In terms of efficiency, Wild-GS trains in 0.52 hours and renders at 227 FPS, compared to CR-NeRF's 101 hours and 0.02 FPS — roughly 200× faster training and 10,000× faster inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wild-GS outperforms all four baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all three Phototourism scenes. Against the strongest NeRF-based baseline CR-NeRF, Wild-GS gains ~3 dB PSNR (e.g., 29.65 vs. 26.53 on Brandenburg Gate) while training ~200× faster (0.52 h vs. 101 h) and rendering ~10,000× faster (227 FPS vs. 0.02 FPS). Compared to the original 3DGS, Wild-GS improves PSNR by 10+ dB (29.65 vs. 19.63 on Brandenburg Gate) at only ~4× the training cost (0.52 h vs. 0.12 h), confirming that the appearance modeling modules are essential for in-the-wild robustness. Against NeRF-W and Ha-NeRF, Wild-GS achieves 5–7 dB higher PSNR with orders-of-magnitude speedups in both training and inference. In the appendix comparison with the concurrent GS-W method on three additional scenes, Wild-GS leads by ~1.1 dB PSNR (e.g., 25.83 vs. 24.73 on Palace of Westminster) while training in ~32 minutes versus GS-W's 2 hours.

## Relation to Prior Work

Wild-GS adapts 3D Gaussian Splatting to the in-the-wild novel view synthesis problem previously addressed by NeRF-based methods (NeRF-W, Ha-NeRF, CR-NeRF), which rely on implicit radiance fields and costly ray-marching. Its key architectural departure is the explicit local appearance modeling via triplane representation (borrowed from EG3D), where each 3D Gaussian queries position-aware appearance features by sampling triplane maps constructed from back-projected reference-view colors. The hierarchical decomposition into global appearance embedding, triplane-based local embedding, and per-Gaussian intrinsic features extends the single global latent vector used in prior in-the-wild NeRF methods, adding positional-awareness that those approaches lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~200× training speedup and ~10000× inference speedup over CR-NeRF make in-the-wild view synthesis practically deployable for the first time, opening applications in tourism, AR, and content creation from casual photo collections. However, the paper acknowledges that unsupervised transient masks can incorrectly mask static regions with unusual appearance, ground/road geometry remains poorly reconstructed, and training still requires roughly 2× the time of vanilla 3DGS. Open questions include whether the triplane local appearance mechanism extends to dynamic or 4D scenes, and whether supervised segmentation could replace the current unsupervised mask prediction to resolve color-inconsistency artifacts.
