## Problem

Off-the-shelf deep learning architectures do not systematically exploit the Lorentz symmetry that governs relativistic particle kinematics, leading to poor data efficiency and limited scalability in high-energy physics. While a few Lorentz-equivariant architectures have been proposed, they are restricted to specific tasks (e.g., message-passing GNNs for jet tagging) and no Lorentz-equivariant generative model existed. The paper asks whether a single, scalable architecture can handle the diverse regression, classification, and generative modelling problems across the LHC analysis pipeline while respecting the underlying physics symmetries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes variable-length sets of particle tokens, where each token carries a four-momentum embedded in the grade-1 subspace of the geometric algebra G_{1,3} (a 16-dimensional vector space) and a one-hot particle-type embedding in scalar channels; for reconstruction-level data, the proton beam direction is appended as a symmetry-breaking input. Outputs are task-specific: a scalar QFT amplitude (regression), a binary top-quark label (classification), or a full set of particle four-momenta sampled from a learned density (generative modelling). The geometric algebra representation extends the natural four-vector inputs to higher-grade antisymmetric tensors, increasing expressivity without breaking equivariance.

## Method

L-GATr is a Transformer whose representations, linear layers, attention, and normalization are all equivariant under the Lorentz group SO⁺(1,3). The key architectural insight is that Lorentz-equivariant linear maps on G_{1,3} are fully characterized by ten scalar parameters per channel pair (Proposition 1), and the G_{1,3} inner product reduces to a pre-computed sign pattern times a Euclidean dot product, enabling use of Flash Attention backends for efficient scaling. A grade-wise absolute-value layer normalization handles the norm cancellations inherent in the Minkowski metric. For generative modelling, the paper constructs the first Lorentz-equivariant continuous normalizing flow by training an L-GATr vector field with Riemannian flow matching: probability paths are geodesics on a physically-motivated manifold parameterized by (y_m, y_p, η, φ), which hard-codes phase-space boundaries (e.g., p_T > p_T,min) directly into the training objective and compresses high-energy tails.

## Results

On the top tagging benchmark, L-GATr achieves 0.9423 accuracy and 0.9870 AUC, matching the best Lorentz-equivariant baselines (PELICAN: 0.9426 acc, 0.9870 AUC) while achieving the highest background rejection of 540±20 at ε_S=0.5 (vs. 500 for CGENN and 498±18 for LorentzNet). For QFT amplitude surrogates, L-GATr outperforms all baselines at 3 and 4 gluon multiplicities, where neural surrogates have the highest practical impact. In generative modelling of tt̄+n jets events, the L-GATr flow achieves the best negative log-likelihood across all jet multiplicities and the lowest classifier two-sample test ROC AUC (0.51–0.56, where 0.5 is ideal), compared to 0.58–0.85 for Transformer and MLP baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On top tagging, L-GATr ties with PELICAN and CGENN on accuracy and AUC (all ≈0.942, 0.987) but achieves the best background rejection at ε_S=0.5 (540±20 vs. 500 for CGENN, 498±18 for LorentzNet, 413±16 for ParT), and is competitive at ε_S=0.3 (2240±70 vs. 2250±75 for PELICAN). For amplitude surrogates, L-GATr slightly underperforms the DSI baseline at 1 gluon but clearly outperforms CGENN, the vanilla Transformer, and DSI at 3 and 4 gluons, where it scales best with particle multiplicity. In generative modelling, the L-GATr flow outperforms the E(3)-GATr flow, Transformer flow, MLP flow, and the autoregressive JetGPT on negative log-likelihood across all tt̄+n jet processes (n=0–4) and all training set sizes. The classifier two-sample test confirms this: L-GATr samples are hardest to distinguish from ground truth (ROC AUC 0.51–0.56) compared to Transformer (0.58–0.85) and MLP baselines. Riemannian flow matching provides a consistent NLL improvement of ~2.5 nats over Euclidean paths for all architectures tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

L-GATr generalizes the Geometric Algebra Transformer (GATr, Brehmer et al. 2023) from the Euclidean symmetry E(3) to the Lorentz group SO⁺(1,3), redesigning every layer—linear maps, attention, normalization, and geometric product—to operate in the Minkowski-space Clifford algebra G₁,₃. It departs from the closest prior Lorentz-equivariant architectures (CGENN, LorentzNet, PELICAN), which are all message-passing graph neural networks, by adopting a Transformer with dot-product attention, which the authors argue is better suited to the dense pairwise interactions inherent in LHC problems. The generative component is the first Lorentz-equivariant continuous normalizing flow, trained with Riemannian flow matching (Chen & Lipman 2023) using geodesic probability paths in physically motivated particle coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

High-energy physics is a domain where symmetries are fundamental yet equivariant architectures have been underutilized relative to off-the-shelf Transformers; L-GATr provides a scalable, multipurpose alternative that matches or beats domain-specific baselines across regression, classification, and generative tasks while being more data-efficient. The Riemannian flow matching formulation is notable for allowing phase-space boundaries to be hard-coded into the generative model, addressing a long-standing difficulty with sharp edges in collider distributions. Open questions include whether L-GATr can serve as a foundation model pretrained across multiple HEP problems, and whether the modest computational overhead over vanilla Transformers (an order of magnitude for small systems) becomes negligible at the scales of future LHC analyses.
