## Problem

Fine-grained hallucination annotation of LLM outputs requires intensive sentence-by-sentence human labor, making large-scale dataset construction prohibitively expensive. Existing automatic annotators, including GPT-4, produce unreliable results on this task, and no prior work has demonstrated a scalable pipeline that simultaneously grows the annotation dataset and improves annotator accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241915/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The annotator receives a triple of (question, target sentence, reference document) and produces a structured three-phase annotation: a factual existence judgment (whether the sentence contains verifiable facts), extracted reference points from the document, and a hallucination type label drawn from four categories (No Fact, No Hallucination, Contradictory Hallucination, Unverifiable Hallucination). In training, these three phases are formatted as a multi-turn dialogue rather than a single-turn prediction, and the final dataset spans ~3,172 topics, ~196K model responses, and ~822K annotated sentences in English and Chinese.

## Method

The framework is an iterative EM-based self-training loop: in the E-step, the current annotator samples K candidate annotations per input and applies a self-consistency selection (majority vote on hallucination type, then cosine-similarity-based consensus on reference points) to pick the most representative label; in the M-step, InternLM2-7B is fine-tuned on the union of all previously annotated data plus the new E-step labels. Three progressive stages scale the dataset along different dimensions—Stage 1 seeds from the ANAH human-annotated corpus, Stage 2 augments responses from 13 additional open-source LLMs on the same questions, and Stage 3 expands topic coverage from 800 to 3,172—each stage using the updated annotator to annotate the next batch. A key design choice is the three-phase multi-turn dialogue training format (factual existence → reference extraction → hallucination type), which decomposes the annotation into cognitively sequential steps rather than a single hybrid prediction, yielding a 7B annotator that surpasses GPT-4 in F1 (89.30% vs. 87.11%) on the ANAH test set.

## Results

On the in-domain ANAH test set, ANAH-v2-Stage3 achieves 89.30% F1 and 89.55% accuracy, surpassing GPT-4 (87.11% F1, 86.97% ACC) with only 7B parameters. Under zero-shot evaluation on third-party benchmarks, ANAH-v2-Stage3 attains new SOTA accuracy of 81.54% on HaluEval and 94.44% on HalluQA, substantially outperforming GPT-4 (65.05% and 62.81% respectively). For hallucination mitigation via re-ranking on HaluEval, the NLI metric increases from 25.00% to 37.01% for InternLM2-7B and from 31.25% to 36.03% for LLaMA2-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANAH-v2-Stage3 outperforms GPT-4 on the primary ANAH benchmark by 2.19 points in F1 (89.30 vs 87.11) and 2.58 points in accuracy (89.55 vs 86.97), despite using only 7B parameters versus GPT-4's undisclosed scale; the paper notes GPT-4's higher RougeL/BERTScore is an artifact of GPT-4 being used for pre-annotation in ANAH's construction. On HaluEval zero-shot, ANAH-v2-Stage3 (81.54%) beats KnowHalu with GPT-3.5 (80.30%) and KnowHalu with Starling-7B (80.70%) by 1.24 and 0.84 points respectively, and substantially outperforms GPT-4 zero-shot (65.05%) by 16.49 points. On HalluQA zero-shot, ANAH-v2-Stage3 (94.44%) exceeds GPT-4 (62.81%) by 31.63 points. Compared to the original ANAH annotators, ANAH-v2-Stage3 improves accuracy by 9.63 points over ANAH-7B (89.55 vs 79.92) and 8.54 points over ANAH-20B (89.55 vs 81.01), demonstrating that the multi-turn dialogue training strategy and iterative self-training are more effective than simply scaling model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ANAH fine-grained hallucination annotation framework [29] by replacing its single-turn hybrid annotation with a three-phase multi-turn dialogue process (factual existence → reference extraction → hallucination type) that better mirrors human judgment. The core methodological contribution is framing iterative self-training for hallucination annotation as an EM algorithm, where the E-step uses self-consistency [63] to robustly estimate annotations on scaled data and the M-step retrains the annotator. Unlike prior self-improvement work in vision (SAM) or reasoning (REST), this pipeline is self-sufficient, requiring only the annotator model and an initial seed dataset without external human labor or supplementary models. The progressive multi-dimensional scaling strategy (responses → topics) departs from prior data augmentation approaches by stabilizing the annotator across both familiar and unfamiliar model responses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework dramatically reduces the cost of scaling hallucination annotation datasets (4,128 USD vs. an estimated 177,237 USD for manual+GPT-4 annotation), making large-scale hallucination oversight practically feasible. The resulting 822k-sentence, 3k-topic dataset and the 7B annotator that surpasses GPT-4 provide the community with an accessible, automated benchmark for evaluating open-source LLMs. Open questions include whether the EM-based iterative process truly converges to a globally optimal annotator, how well the approach generalizes to other NLG tasks (e.g., dialogue generation) and languages, and whether different backbone architectures would yield comparable results. The simple re-ranking mitigation (NLI 25%→37%) suggests the annotator could serve as a reward signal for more advanced strategies like fine-grained RLHF.
