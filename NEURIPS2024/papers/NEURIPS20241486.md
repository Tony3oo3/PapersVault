## Problem

Existing dexterous grasp methods optimize for stability or a fixed set of predefined task functions, leaving the robot unable to respond to flexible, natural-language instructions from a human operator. Two practical barriers prevent progress: (1) no large-scale dataset pairs dexterous grasp poses with fine-grained language guidance, and (2) the standard object-penetration loss used to ensure physical feasibility actively degrades the learning of a diverse, intention-aligned grasp distribution, making it impossible for a single model to satisfy all three objectives simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full 3D object point cloud (completed from partial observations via SAM + point-completion in the real-robot pipeline) and a free-form natural-language instruction (e.g., 'To use a trigger sprayer, press the trigger with your forefinger and hold the bottle with your other fingers'). It outputs a complete dexterous hand pose—global rotation (SO(3)), translation (R³), and J joint angles (J=22 for Shadow Hand, 16 for Allegro)—that is both physically feasible (no object or self-penetration) and semantically consistent with the language intent. The language is encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 text encoder and the point cloud by a three-layer PointNet++, both fed as cross-attention conditions into the generation network.

## Method

DexGYSGrasp decomposes grasp generation into two progressive stages to resolve the conflict between penetration loss and distribution learning. The first stage, the Intention and Diversity Grasp Component (IDGC), is a conditional DDPM diffusion model (PointNet++ object encoder + CLIP language encoder + 4-layer Transformer decoder) trained with only regression and Chamfer losses—deliberately excluding the penetration loss—so it learns a diverse, intention-aligned grasp distribution without the optimization interference. The second stage, the Quality Grasp Component (QGC), is a regression network that takes the coarse pose, its hand point cloud, and the object point cloud as input and predicts a small pose delta, trained with the full loss suite (penetration, self-penetration, contact-map, joint-limit) in a tightly constrained local space so quality is refined without disturbing the already-aligned intention. The dataset DexGYSNet (50k grasp–language pairs over 1,800 objects) is built cost-effectively via a three-step Hand-Object Interaction Retargeting (HOIR) pipeline that transfers MANO human grasps to the dexterous hand while preserving fingertip alignment and contact maps, combined with a GPT-3.5-assisted annotation system that converts brief intentions and computed finger-contact descriptors into natural-language guidance.

## Results

On DexGYSNet, DexGYSGrasp achieves the best intention consistency across all metrics: FID of 6.538 (vs. 20.44 for SceneDiffuser), P-FID of 5.595, Chamfer distance of 1.198, and contact distance of 0.036. It also achieves the highest diversity by a large margin, with δt=6.118 cm, δr=55.68°, and δq=6.118° (compared to δt=0.346, δr=3.455, δq=0.387 for SceneDiffuser). In terms of grasp quality, it matches SceneDiffuser on Q1 (0.083) and slightly exceeds it on success rate (63.31% vs. 62.24%), while maintaining a penetration depth of 0.223 cm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DexGYSGrasp substantially outperforms all four baselines on intention consistency: it reduces FID from 20.44 (SceneDiffuser, the best baseline) to 6.538, and reduces Chamfer distance from 1.679 to 1.198. On diversity, the advantage is even more dramatic — δt improves from 0.346 (SceneDiffuser) to 6.118, and δr from 14.01 (DGTR) to 55.68, indicating that baselines produce near-identical grasps under the same language condition while DexGYSGrasp generates genuinely diverse poses. On grasp quality, DexGYSGrasp ties SceneDiffuser on Q1 (0.083) and slightly exceeds it on success rate (63.31% vs. 62.24%), while its penetration depth (0.223 cm) is slightly higher than DGTR's (0.163 cm) but far better than GraspCVAE's (0.551 cm). GraspTTA performs worst on intention (CD=12.19) despite moderate quality, and GraspCVAE is weakest overall on both intention and quality. The paper's plug-and-play experiments (Table 4) further confirm that removing penetration loss from SceneDiffuser and GraspCVAE improves their intention consistency, and adding the QGC component recovers quality, validating the progressive design as a general principle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of dexterous grasp generation and language-conditioned robotics, building on diffusion-based generative models (SceneDiffuser, SE(3)-DiffusionFields) for pose synthesis and on prior dexterous grasp methods (DGTR, UniDexGrasp) that focused on stability or fixed task/function specifications. It departs from this lineage by introducing natural language as the conditioning signal for dexterous hands, replacing the rigid task/function labels used in prior task-oriented and functional grasping work. The key technical departure is the progressive decomposition of the learning objective, motivated by the finding that the standard penetration loss—ubiquitous in dexterous grasp training—actively degrades distribution learning (intention alignment and diversity) when applied in a single generative stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: dexterous hand research has largely ignored the human-communication channel, and language-guided grasping has been confined to parallel grippers. The DexGYSNet dataset (50K pairs across 1,800 objects) and the cost-effective HOIR + LLM annotation pipeline lower the barrier for future language-conditioned dexterous work. However, real-world success rates remain moderate (3–9/10 across objects), and the framework requires full point clouds (reconstructed via a cascade of off-the-shelf models), limiting immediate deployment. Open questions include whether the progressive decomposition generalizes to more complex multi-step manipulation tasks and whether the dataset's household-object scope can be expanded to industrial or deformable-object settings.
