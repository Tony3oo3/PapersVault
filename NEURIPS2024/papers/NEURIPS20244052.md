## Problem

When deploying LLMs across diverse tasks, the optimal model varies per input sample, but existing routing methods require labeled data to train auxiliary scorers or identify the best-performing LLM. This paper asks whether high-quality routing is possible in a fully unsupervised setting—without any human annotations or models trained on labeled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244052/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given an unlabeled test set of n inputs and a pool of m LLMs, the method requires all n×m generations (one per LLM per sample) as input, which are then embedded via a sentence encoder (e.g., SentenceBERT all-mpnet-base-v2) into d-dimensional vectors. The output is a per-sample, per-LLM quality score θ̂_i(x), from which the router selects argmax_i θ̂_i(x) as the final generation. A variant (SMOOTHIE-TRAIN) can avoid generating outputs for test samples entirely by reusing precomputed generations from a held-out training set, making runtime independent of test set size.

## Method

SMOOTHIE constructs a latent variable graphical model where each LLM's embedding is modeled as a multivariate Gaussian centered at an unknown true-output embedding, with precision (inverse variance) parameterized by the LLM's quality score θ_i(x). A key algebraic property (Proposition 1) shows that the expected squared distance between any two LLM embeddings equals the sum of their individual expected squared distances to the true embedding, yielding a closed-form estimator θ_i^{jk}(x) = d / (δ_ij + δ_ik − δ_jk) that requires no gradient descent. SMOOTHIE-GLOBAL estimates δ_ij by averaging over the entire test set, while SMOOTHIE-LOCAL applies kernel smoothing over n_0 nearest neighbors in embedding space to produce sample-conditional scores. The final router simply selects the LLM with the highest estimated quality score for each input.

## Results

On multi-task routing (Table 2), SMOOTHIE-LOCAL achieves 58.7 accuracy on DISTR-ACC and 20.2 Rouge-2 on DISTR-ROUGE2 for the 3B ensemble, and 75.0 accuracy and 26.9 Rouge-2 for the 7B ensemble, outperforming all baselines in every setting. On single-task LLM selection (Table 1), SMOOTHIE-GLOBAL outperforms the RANDOM baseline by up to 12 points (accuracy tasks) and 7 points (Rouge-2 tasks), and matches or exceeds the labeled BEST-ON-VAL baseline on 4/7 (3B) and 5/7 (7B) tasks. SMOOTHIE-GLOBAL's learned quality scores correlate with ground-truth model performance at ρ=0.72 across NLG tasks, and it identifies the best LLM on 9/14 task-ensemble combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMOOTHIE-LOCAL (unsupervised) outperforms the labeled PAIRRM reward model [41] by 4.8–7.7 points across all four distribution/ensemble settings (e.g., 58.7 vs. 53.9 on 3B DISTR-ACC; 75.0 vs. 71.8 on 7B DISTR-ACC). It also beats the labeled LABELED-KNN baseline [48, 86] by 7.7–3.3 points (58.7 vs. 51.0 on 3B DISTR-ACC; 26.9 vs. 26.2 on 7B DISTR-ROUGE2). Against the unsupervised RANDOM baseline, SMOOTHIE-LOCAL gains 10.0 points on 3B DISTR-ACC and 9.6 points on 7B DISTR-ACC. SMOOTHIE-LOCAL also surpasses BEST-MODEL (selecting the single best LLM on average) by 6.4 points on 3B DISTR-ACC and 1.8 points on 7B DISTR-ACC, confirming that sample-conditional routing provides gains beyond population-level model selection. On single-task selection (Table 1), SMOOTHIE-GLOBAL matches or exceeds the labeled BEST-ON-VAL baseline [67] on 5/7 tasks for the 7B ensemble (e.g., 76.2 vs. 74.0 on SQuAD; 70.9 vs. 69.4 on Definition Extraction), while outperforming it on 4/7 for the 3B ensemble.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMOOTHIE adapts the multivariate Gaussian graphical model from Shin et al. (ICLR 2022, 'Universalizing weak supervision'), which estimated voter quality in structured-prediction weak supervision, to the LLM routing setting by applying it to SentenceBERT embeddings of model generations. It departs from the dominant line of LLM routing work (e.g., LLM-Blender, Orchestrallm, RouteLLM) that requires labeled data to train auxiliary scorers or reward models, instead deriving a closed-form quality estimator from the Gaussian structure that needs no labels at all. The sample-conditional variant (SMOOTHIE-LOCAL) introduces nearest-neighbor kernel smoothing to make the global quality scores input-dependent, a step not present in the original weak-supervision formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that engineers deploying LLM ensembles in multi-capability settings can now route samples without constructing expensive labeled validation sets, and the closed-form computation runs in seconds per 1000 samples. The method's generality is demonstrated by its extension to prompt selection, where it lets a 410M-parameter model match a 6.9B-parameter model. Open questions include whether the diagonal-covariance assumption (independent LLM errors) is adequate in practice, how to incorporate cost-quality tradeoffs, and whether richer embedding features or dependency-aware models could further improve routing accuracy.
