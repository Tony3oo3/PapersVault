## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models frequently fail at semantic binding, where objects are not correctly associated with their attributes or related sub-objects (e.g., a hat appearing on the wrong animal in a multi-object prompt). Existing remedies require intensive fine-tuning, explicit layout specifications from large language models, or complex attention-map manipulation, and they often break down in complex multi-object, multi-attribute scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244371/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt and produces a generated RGB image via the SDXL latent diffusion model. The prompt is parsed with SpaCy to identify noun entities and their associated attribute tokens, which are then merged into composite tokens before being encoded by the CLIP text encoder (77-token sequence). No auxiliary inputs such as bounding boxes, segmentation maps, or LLM-generated layouts are required, and the entire pipeline operates at inference time without modifying any model weights.

## Method

The core mechanism, Token Merging (ToMe), exploits the semantic additivity property of CLIP text embeddings by element-wise summing each object token with its attribute tokens into a single composite token, so that all related semantics share one unified cross-attention map rather than multiple misaligned ones. End Token Substitution (ETS) replaces the [EOT] padding tokens with those derived from a simplified subject-only prompt to eliminate attribute leakage carried by the end tokens. During the first 20% of denoising steps, composite tokens are iteratively refined with two auxiliary losses: an entropy loss that regularizes each token's cross-attention map to be spatially concentrated, and a semantic binding loss that aligns the UNet noise prediction of each composite token with that of the full original phrase. The entire approach is training-free, requiring no parameter updates to the diffusion model or text encoder.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench (SDXL base), ToMe achieves BLIP-VQA scores of 0.7656 (Color), 0.6894 (Texture), and 0.6051 (Shape), outperforming all training-free and fine-tuning baselines on Texture and Shape. On the proposed GPT-4o object binding benchmark, ToMe scores 0.9549, substantially exceeding the next-best method (SynGen at 0.6458) by over 30 points. Human-preference scores via ImageReward also favor ToMe (1.074 / 0.9281 / 0.5916 for Color/Texture/Shape), confirming better prompt alignment. Notably, ToMe is training-free, whereas most competitors (CoMat, ELLA, Ranni) require fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SDXL baseline, ToMe improves BLIP-VQA by +0.129 (Color), +0.126 (Texture), and +0.064 (Shape), and nearly doubles the GPT-4o object binding score (0.9549 vs. 0.4907). Compared to CoMat (the strongest fine-tuning baseline on SDXL), ToMe trades a marginal 0.012 Color deficit for large gains in Texture (+0.030), Shape (+0.079), and GPT-4o (0.9549 vs. not reported), all without any training. Versus ELLA (which replaces CLIP with Llama/T5-XL), ToMe wins on Texture (+0.021), Shape (+0.042), and GPT-4o (0.9549 vs. 0.6481 on SD1.5), while ELLA leads on Color by 0.040. Against SynGen (the only other training-free optimization method), ToMe dominates across every metric: +0.065 Color, +0.085 Texture, +0.098 Shape, and +0.309 GPT-4o. Playground v2, a stronger standalone T2I model, is outperformed by ToMe on all three BLIP-VQA subsets and by a wide margin on GPT-4o (0.9549 vs. 0.5417). Ranni (LLM-augmented layout method) is the weakest SDXL-based baseline, trailing ToMe by 0.076–0.112 on BLIP-VQA subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the four dominant streams for improving semantic alignment in T2I diffusion models—optimization-based attention manipulation (SynGen, Attend-and-Excite), layout-guided generation (BoxDiff, Ranni), LLM-augmented pipelines (MuLan, RPG), and fine-tuning approaches (ELLA, CoMat)—by proposing a training-free method that requires none of these external components. Conceptually, it builds on the observed semantic additivity of CLIP text embeddings (citing prior work on embedding compositionality) and the known property that [EOT] tokens aggregate global semantic information, repurposing these observations into a simple element-wise token summation strategy. The key departure is that instead of manipulating cross-attention maps or imposing external layout constraints, ToMe collapses subject-attribute token groups into a single composite token, thereby structurally eliminating the multi-token attention misalignment problem at its source.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ToMe offers a practical, zero-training solution to semantic binding that avoids the computational overhead of fine-tuning and the dependency on LLMs or explicit layout specifications, making it broadly applicable to any CLIP-conditioned diffusion model. The introduction of the GPT-4o object binding benchmark fills a gap in evaluation for multi-object multi-attribute scenarios that prior benchmarks (T2I-CompBench) underrepresented. However, the method inherits SDXL's limitations (artifacts, complex layout failures) and depends on the quality of the CLIP encoder's additivity property, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to non-CLIP text encoders or newer architectures like DiT-based models. The reliance on SpaCy for syntactic parsing to identify subject-attribute pairs also introduces a potential fragility for unusual prompt structures.
