## Problem

Despite widespread use of parametric dimensionality reduction methods (neural network projectors trained with neighborhood embedding losses), practitioners have largely assumed they produce embeddings equivalent to their non-parametric counterparts. This paper shows that parametrization systematically degrades local structure preservation—clusters become densely packed with indistinct boundaries—and identifies the root cause as insufficient repulsive forces on negative pairs, with NEG-style losses (UMAP, PaCMAP) being more amenable to parametrization than NCE/InfoNCE-style losses (t-SNE, NCVis).

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-dimensional data matrix X ∈ R^{n×D} (flattened images, TF-IDF text vectors, or scRNA-seq principal components) and learns a parametric mapping f_θ that produces a low-dimensional embedding Y ∈ R^{n×d} (typically d=2 or 3). Unlike non-parametric methods, the learned MLP projector generalizes to unseen data points without re-optimization, enabling incremental embedding of new samples. The network architecture is a 3-layer fully-connected MLP with 100 neurons per layer and SiLU activation, initialized with Kaiming initialization.

## Method

ParamRepulsor is built on ParamPaCMAP and introduces Hard Negative Mining using mid-near (MN) pairs—sampled by drawing 6 random points and selecting the second closest—as the repulsive negative samples, replacing uniform random sampling. This design provably reduces the false-negative probability to O(1/n²) versus O(1/n) for uniform sampling, while MN pairs are naturally harder negatives that produce larger gradients for local structure separation. The loss function is a generalized NEG loss with three weighted terms: an attractive term pulling nearest neighbors together (d²/(10+d²)), a repulsive term pushing MN hard negatives apart (d²/(1+d²) with weight w_MN), and a repulsive term for further pairs (d²/(1+d²) with weight w_FP). The paper also proves that PaCMAP's loss is a generalized NEG loss with separate similarity kernels for NN and FP pairs, explaining why NEG-based methods adapt better to parametrization than NCE/InfoNCE-based methods, whose negative-pair gradients are diluted across all pairs in the batch.

## Results

On the primary local structure metric (10-NN accuracy), ParamRepulsor achieves the highest score on 10 out of 14 datasets, including MNIST (0.969), Fashion-MNIST (0.778), COIL-100 (0.928), and COIL-20 (0.887). On SVM accuracy, ParamRepulsor leads on 9/14 datasets, and on 30-NN preservation it leads on 10/14. For global structure (centroid distance correlation), ParamPaCMAP achieves the highest correlation on 5 datasets while ParamRepulsor leads on 4, with both outperforming other parametric baselines on most datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParamRepulsor substantially outperforms Parametric UMAP (P-UMAP) on most datasets: e.g., COIL-100 (0.928 vs. 0.145), Fashion-MNIST (0.778 vs. 0.733), and COIL-20 (0.887 vs. 0.843), though P-UMAP edges ahead on 20 Newsgroups (0.505 vs. 0.460) and Circle (0.901 vs. 0.895). Against Parametric Info-NC-t-SNE (P-ItSNE) and Parametric NCVis (P-NCVis), ParamRepulsor wins on nearly every dataset where those methods report results, with particularly large gaps on COIL-100 (0.928 vs. no result) and 20NG (0.460 vs. 0.340/0.442). GeoAE, an autoencoder-based method, achieves the best 10-NN accuracy on Mammoth (0.962 vs. 0.938) but is far weaker on scRNA-seq datasets (e.g., 0.468 on KANG vs. 0.961) and 20NG (0.061 vs. 0.460). ParamPaCMAP (the ablation without Hard Negative Mining) is competitive but consistently trails ParamRepulsor on local structure, confirming the contribution of mid-near hard negative repulsion. On global structure (centroid distance correlation), both P-PaCMAP and P-Rep lead on 5 and 4 datasets respectively, outperforming P-UMAP and P-ItSNE on most benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parametric neighborhood embedding framework established by Damrich et al. (ICLR 2023), which introduced MLP projectors with mini-batch SGD to make t-SNE and UMAP generalizable to unseen data. It builds directly on PaCMAP's loss function (Wang et al., JMLR 2021), proving it is a generalized NEG loss (Theorem 3.1), and imports Hard Negative Mining from contrastive learning (Robinson et al., ICLR 2020) into the DR setting for the first time. The key departure from prior work is the systematic diagnosis of *why* parametrization degrades local structure—insufficient repulsive force on negative pairs—and the identification of NEG-style losses as inherently more compatible with parametric optimization than NCE/InfoNCE-style losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that parametric and non-parametric DR methods are not equivalent despite sharing the same loss functions corrects a widespread practitioner misconception and has direct implications for anyone deploying parametric DR on large or incrementally updated datasets (e.g., single-cell genomics). The NEG-vs-InfoNCE distinction provides a principled guideline for choosing loss functions in the parametric setting. Limitations include slower training than P-ItSNE, and open questions remain about optimal network architecture for the preservation-generalization trade-off and better evaluation metrics that capture both local and global fidelity simultaneously.
