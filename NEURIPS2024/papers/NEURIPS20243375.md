## Problem

Deep learning models trained on large visual datasets inherit spurious concept co-occurrence biases (e.g., landbirds always on land, urban cars always near traffic lights), but no existing pipeline both diagnoses these biases directly from the data and debiases them in an end-to-end fashion. Prior work either diagnoses without debiasing (REVISE), debiases without diagnosis (ALIA, which also relies on a confounding LLM to generate prompts), or operates in model feature space rather than intervening on the dataset itself.

## Input / Output

The method requires a labeled image dataset paired with ground-truth concept annotations (captions for Waterbirds, segmentation masks for UrbanCars and COCO-GB) and a pre-trained ResNet-50 classifier. It outputs a weighted concept knowledge graph, a ranked list of imbalanced class-concept cliques with their under-represented class, a set of synthetic images generated via Stable Diffusion inpainting (with CLIP-score ≥ 0.6 filtering), and a retrained classifier on the union of original and augmented data. The concept set must be pre-specified per dataset (64 concepts for Waterbirds, 17 for UrbanCars, 81 for COCO-GB), and clique sizes are constrained to ≤ 4 for interpretability.

## Method

CONBIAS constructs a bipartite concept graph G=(V,E,W) where nodes are class labels and concepts, edges connect co-occurring pairs, and edge weights are co-occurrence counts across the dataset. Diagnosis enumerates k-cliques (k=1..4) common to all classes, then computes the frequency gap of each clique across classes to identify imbalanced combinations and their under-represented class. Debiasing uses a top-down sampling algorithm (Algorithm 1) that iterates from largest to smallest cliques, computing the number of synthetic images needed to equalize each under-represented class's co-occurrence count with the maximum. Generated images are produced by prompting Stable Diffusion with the under-represented concept combination as background, then inpainting the original object (extracted via ground-truth masks) as foreground to preserve class identity, avoiding the object distortion issues of InstructPix2Pix-based editing.

## Results

CONBIAS achieves 77.9% class-balanced (CB) and 69.3% out-of-distribution (OOD) accuracy on Waterbirds, 78.3% CB and 52.9% OOD on UrbanCars, and 58.8% CB and 51.4% OOD on COCO-GB (binary classification accuracy, averaged over three runs). The largest gains appear on Waterbirds OOD (+21.1 pp over the ResNet-50 baseline) and UrbanCars OOD (+12.4 pp over baseline), where the method most effectively mitigates background and co-occurring object shortcuts. On COCO-GB, improvements over baselines are marginal, which the authors attribute to the small number of augmented samples (260) used in retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONBIAS outperforms the vanilla ResNet-50 baseline by 10.8 pp (CB) and 24.4 pp (OOD) on Waterbirds, and by 4.8 pp (CB) and 12.4 pp (OOD) on UrbanCars, demonstrating strong debiasing of background and co-occurring object shortcuts. Against RandAug, CONBIAS leads by 4.2 pp (CB) and 9.1 pp (OOD) on Waterbirds and by 2.0 pp (CB) and 6.8 pp (OOD) on UrbanCars; RandAug actually underperforms the baseline on COCO-GB. Against CutMix, CONBIAS wins by 10.0 pp (CB) and 23.7 pp (OOD) on Waterbirds and by 3.9 pp (CB) and 13.6 pp (OOD) on UrbanCars, while CutMix is essentially at baseline level on both datasets. Against the most recent state-of-the-art ALIA (which uses ChatGPT-4-generated prompts with InstructPix2Pix), CONBIAS leads by 8.3 pp (CB) and 21.1 pp (OOD) on Waterbirds and by 4.3 pp (CB) and 10.4 pp (OOD) on UrbanCars. On COCO-GB, CONBIAS is roughly on par with ALIA (58.8 vs. 58.7 CB; 51.4 vs. 52.4 OOD), a gap the authors close by scaling the number of augmented images (Figure 5 shows CONBIAS pulling ahead as more synthetic samples are added).

## Relation to Prior Work

CONBIAS carves new ground by unifying dataset diagnosis and debiasing into a single end-to-end pipeline, departing from prior work that either diagnoses biases at the model level (feature decorrelation, worst-group robustness) or performs data augmentation without first identifying what to fix (ALIA, CutMix, RandAug). The key conceptual departure is representing a visual dataset as a knowledge graph of concept co-occurrences and using clique enumeration to systematically identify imbalanced class-concept combinations, replacing ALIA's reliance on LLM-generated prompts with a structured, interpretable graph-theoretic procedure. The generative component (Stable Diffusion inpainting with CLIP filtering) is borrowed from the diffusion-based augmentation literature, but the graph-driven sampling of which concepts to generate is the novel contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework that both diagnoses and debiases visual datasets in a single pipeline, offering practitioners a principled, human-interpretable way to identify and correct object co-occurrence biases without relying on LLMs whose own biases are uncontrolled. The graph representation makes bias diagnosis transparent (specific concept combinations and their imbalances are directly readable), which is valuable for auditability in safety-critical applications. Key limitations include the exponential growth of clique enumeration (mitigated by capping clique size at 4), the requirement for ground-truth concept annotations, and the narrow focus on object co-occurrence bias. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other bias types (texture, shape, social), how it behaves when concept sets are partially unavailable, and whether more efficient graph algorithms can handle larger real-world datasets.
