## Problem

Existing federated group distributionally robust optimization (FGDRO) methods suffer from prohibitively high communication and sample complexity—O(1/ε^12) communication rounds for CVaR-constrained variants and O(1/ε^6) per-machine sample complexity for KL-regularized variants—because they either update the client weight vector only at global communication rounds or require large local batches to estimate compositional gradients. This paper closes the gap by showing that constraint-free reformulations combined with locally-updated moving average estimators can dramatically reduce both costs.

## Input / Output

Each of N clients holds a local dataset D_i with potentially heterogeneous size and distribution; the algorithm consumes per-sample losses ℓ(w; z) drawn from these local distributions. The constrained N-dimensional weight vector p on the simplex is eliminated: in the CVaR variant it is replaced by a single scalar threshold s (only clients with loss above s contribute to the update), and in the KL variant it is absorbed via KKT conditions into an exponential-of-loss formulation. The output is the trained model w, with auxiliary scalar s (CVaR) or global loss estimator v (KL) maintained alongside; all auxiliary variables are O(1)-dimensional, so the per-round communication overhead beyond the model itself is negligible.

## Method

The paper reformulates FGDRO into constraint-free compositional optimization problems and designs federated algorithms that update all auxiliary estimators locally, communicating only averaged values at round boundaries. For FGDRO-CVaR, the problem becomes a non-smooth two-level compositional function in (w, s); a moving average u tracks each client's local loss, and the subgradient is estimated via ∂f(u_i − s)∇ℓ(w; z), with s updated locally and averaged globally, achieving O(1/ε^4) communication. For FGDRO-KL, the KKT-based reformulation yields a smooth three-level compositional function; three moving averages (u for local loss, v for the global exponential-of-loss statistic, m for the weighted gradient) are updated locally and v, m are averaged at communication rounds, achieving O(1/ε^3) communication with O(1/ε^4) per-machine sample complexity using only O(1) batch sizes. FGDRO-KL-Adam further introduces a second-order momentum q updated locally and averaged globally, enabling coordinate-wise adaptive steps at local iterations while preserving the same O(1/ε^3) communication rate; the convergence proof carefully tracks the interplay between first- and second-order moment estimates under the compositional structure.

## Results

On the Pile language modeling task, FGDRO-KL-Adam achieves a worst log-perplexity of 3.608, dramatically outperforming FedAdam (7.242) and FedAvg (8.085), while also reducing average log-perplexity from 6.785 to 2.653. On CivilComments toxicity classification, FGDRO-KL achieves the best worst-group accuracy of 0.6921 (vs. 0.6415 for FedAvg and 0.6567 for FedAdam). On image tasks, FGDRO-KL attains the highest Macro F1 on iWildCam2020 (0.5201 vs. 0.4964 for FedAvg), and FGDRO-KL-Adam achieves the best worst-region Pearson correlation on PovertyMap (0.7827 vs. 0.7301 for FedAvg). Across all tasks, the proposed methods improve worst-case performance without degrading average-case performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGDRO-KL-Adam delivers the most dramatic gains on Pile, reducing worst log-perplexity from 7.242 (FedAdam) to 3.608 and average log-perplexity from 6.479 to 2.653, a roughly 2.5× improvement over the strongest baseline. On CivilComments, FGDRO-KL achieves the best worst-group accuracy (0.6921), outperforming FedAdam (0.6567) by 3.5 points and DRFA (0.6327) by 5.9 points. On iWildCam2020, FGDRO-KL leads in Macro F1 (0.5201) over FedAvg (0.4964) and FGDRO-KL-Adam (0.4489). However, FedAdam retains an edge on Camelyon17 accuracy (0.9493 vs. 0.9399 for FGDRO-KL-Adam) and PovertyMap average Pearson (0.8273 vs. 0.8225). Overall, the proposed methods consistently improve worst-case metrics across all six baselines (FedAvg, SCAFFOLD, FedProx, FedAdam, DRFA, DR-DSGD) while maintaining competitive average-case performance, with the KL-Adam variant showing the largest practical gains on language modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FGDRO line initiated by DRFA [11] (CVaR with K=1) and DR-DSGD [30] (KL regularization) by replacing the constrained primal-dual formulation with constraint-free compositional reformulations: a scalar threshold for CVaR and a log-sum-exp form for KL. The moving-average estimator technique for handling compositional structure with small batches builds on the STORM-style estimators in [22] and the KKT-based reformulation in [56], while the local Adam analysis follows the convergence framework of [16] but extends it to federated and compositional settings where prior work (e.g., FedAdam [59]) restricted adaptivity to global steps only.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The communication complexity reductions (O(1/ε⁴) vs. O(1/ε¹²) for CVaR; O(1/ε⁴) sample vs. O(1/ε⁶) for KL) make FGDRO practically viable in bandwidth-constrained federated deployments, and the demonstration that local Adam steps can be provably integrated into compositional federated optimization opens a path for adaptive methods beyond ERM. A key open question the authors flag is the absence of a provable Adam variant for the CVaR case, where non-smoothness prevents accurate moving-average subgradient estimation. The large Pile perplexity gap for FGDRO-KL-Adam (3.6 vs. 7.9) suggests adaptive local steps may be especially beneficial for language modeling, though this does not uniformly hold across all tasks.
