## Problem

Existing bilevel optimization algorithms for nonconvex upper-level functions with unbounded smoothness and strongly convex lower-level problems achieve only Õ(ε⁻⁴) oracle complexity. It remained unclear whether this rate could be improved to Õ(ε⁻³) when population-level smoothness properties hold almost surely for each stochastic realization, since the required high-probability analysis blocked the use of momentum-based variance reduction techniques that yield acceleration in the smooth case.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes stochastic samples from the upper-level distribution D_f and lower-level distribution D_g at each iteration, along with an initial point x_0 and a warm-start phase of Õ(ε⁻²) SNAG iterations. The hypergradient is estimated via a Neumann series approximation requiring S·Q Hessian/Jacobian-vector products per upper-level step. The output is an ε-stationary point satisfying (1/T)Σ E‖∇Φ(x_t)‖ ≤ 20ε within Õ(ε⁻³) total oracle calls, with the critical constraint that the lower-level stochastic gradient variance must be O(ε). Two implementation options exist: Option I (single-loop, for quadratic lower-level functions) and Option II (periodic lower-level updates every I iterations, for general strongly convex lower-level functions).

## Method

AccBO updates the upper-level variable via normalized stochastic gradient descent with recursive momentum (dividing by ‖m_t‖ to handle unbounded smoothness) and the lower-level variable via stochastic Nesterov accelerated gradient descent (SNAG) with exponential averaging. The key technical contribution is a novel high-probability tracking lemma for SNAG under distributional drift (Lemma 4.3), which shows the potential function decays at rate (1−√(μα)/4)^t with drift and noise terms, strictly improving over prior SGD-under-drift guarantees in the small-noise regime. The normalization in the upper-level update keeps the per-step drift Δ_t = η·l_{g,1}/μ small, enabling the SNAG tracking guarantee to hold with high probability at every iteration. The averaging step (τ = √(μα)) then upgrades the lower-level guarantee from O(ε) tracking error to O(ε²) consecutive-iterate difference, which is essential for controlling the hypergradient estimation error and achieving the Õ(ε⁻³) rate.

## Results

On the deep AUC maximization task (imbalanced Sentiment140, r=0.2, two-layer RNN), AccBO achieves the highest training and test AUC among all seven bilevel baselines over 25 epochs, and also reaches comparable AUC in less wall-clock time. On the data hypercleaning task (SNLI with label-flip noise p=0.1 and p=0.2, three-layer RNN), AccBO attains the highest training and test classification accuracy across both noise levels while maintaining superior runtime efficiency. The paper reports results as convergence curves (AUC/ACC vs. epochs and vs. running time) rather than tabulated endpoint numbers, but consistently shows AccBO dominating all baselines on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AccBO is compared against seven bilevel optimization baselines: StocBio [44], TTSA [41], SABA [21], MA-SOBA [12], SUSTAIN [46], VRBO [71], and BO-REP [38]. On deep AUC maximization (Sentiment140), AccBO outperforms all seven baselines in both training and test AUC over epochs, and reaches a higher AUC in less running time, indicating both better optimization quality and faster convergence. On data hypercleaning (SNLI, p=0.1 and p=0.2), AccBO again achieves the highest training and test accuracy among all baselines at both noise levels, with the advantage being particularly visible in the running-time plots where AccBO converges to its final accuracy faster. Notably, the two baselines most closely related to the unbounded-smoothness setting—BO-REP [38] (which uses periodic lower-level updates) and MA-SOBA [12] (which targets relaxed smoothness)—are both outperformed by AccBO, confirming the benefit of the proposed Nesterov acceleration for the lower-level variable. The smooth-setting baselines (SUSTAIN, VRBO, StocBio, TTSA, SABA) also trail AccBO, suggesting that the unbounded-smoothness-aware design does not sacrifice performance even when the underlying network exhibits the relaxed smoothness property.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unbounded-smoothness bilevel optimization framework established by Hao et al. (ICLR 2024) and Gong et al. (ICML 2024), which achieved Õ(ε⁻⁴) oracle complexity, by incorporating acceleration techniques from single-level nonconvex optimization. The upper-level update generalizes the momentum-based variance reduction of Cutkosky & Orabona (NeurIPS 2019) and Liu et al. (2023) to the bilevel setting, while the lower-level update introduces stochastic Nesterov accelerated gradient (SNAG) with averaging—a combination not previously explored in bilevel optimization. A key technical departure is the first high-probability analysis of SNAG under distributional drift, extending the SGD-under-drift framework of Cutler et al. (JMLR 2023) to the accelerated case, which is essential for controlling the lower-level tracking error when the upper-level variable shifts each iteration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Õ(ε⁻³) oracle complexity matches the state-of-the-art single-level relaxed-smooth rate and is nearly optimal in ε-dependency, closing a gap that existed for unbounded-smoothness bilevel problems. The SNAG-under-drift lemma is of independent interest for online and non-stationary optimization. However, two limitations constrain practical impact: Option I (single-loop) requires the lower-level to be quadratic, and the overall guarantee needs lower-level stochastic gradient variance of O(ε), which may not hold in many real bilevel problems. The open question of whether single-loop acceleration is possible for general strongly-convex lower-level functions without the small-variance assumption remains unresolved.
