## Problem

Existing time series imputation methods evaluate quality via reconstruction error against ground truth, but this does not guarantee better downstream forecasting performance—especially in forecasting where the time series serves as both input and label, making label noise far more damaging than input noise. No prior work has assessed imputation quality at the individual time-step level based on its impact on downstream task performance, and the natural approach of retraining the forecasting model for each candidate substitution is computationally infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two complete sets of imputed time series labels (e.g., mean-value imputation vs. SAITS imputation) over the same training samples, a trained downstream forecasting model f(·,θ), and a validation/test set. It produces a per-sample, per-time-step scalar gain I(i,l) indicating whether substituting the l-th imputed value from method (2) for method (1) would reduce test loss, plus a final combined imputation vector that selects the superior value at each time step. Notably, the method requires no ground-truth labels and operates at time-step granularity (e.g., 24-hour resolution), with the gain values thresholded at a configurable top-c% percentile to control how many substitutions are made.

## Method

The paper defines an indicator I(i,l) as the change in test-set loss when a single imputed label y_{i,l}^{(1)} is replaced by y_{i,l}^{(2)}, then approximates it via a first-order Taylor expansion that decomposes into a test-loss gradient, a label-to-prediction derivative ∂f(X_k^v,θ)/∂y_{i,l}, and the label difference. To avoid retraining, the label-to-prediction derivative is approximated by a kernel machine α_{i,l}^T K(X_i, X_k^v) trained in an RKHS, yielding a closed-form solution α_{i,l} = -(1/n) ∂²L(f(X_i,θ), y_i)/∂f(X_i,θ)∂y_{i,l} combined with the Neural Tangent Kernel as the similarity measure. A block-diagonal basis compression (Seg-N) reduces the NTK computation from O(mL₂P) to O(mrP) by exploiting the smoothness of time series outputs across time steps. The final ensemble step (Algorithm 1) selects, for each time step, the imputed value from whichever method yields the higher estimated gain, then retrains the forecasting model once on the combined labels.

## Results

On the GEF load forecasting dataset (40% missing rate), combining Mean imputation with SAITS via the proposed gain estimation reduces downstream MSE from 0.1750 (Mean alone) to 0.1653, outperforming SAITS alone (0.1980). On UCI Electricity, the same combination improves MSE from 0.1123 to 0.0957, and on Traffic from 0.4359 to 0.4147. The gain estimation method (Seq-sim) achieves higher sign-accuracy than the modified Influence Function across all tested percentages of data, while running in 46 seconds (MLP) versus >48 hours for full retraining. The accelerated Seg-4 variant reduces computation to 18 seconds with only marginal performance loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed gain-estimation ensemble consistently outperforms every individual imputation method (SAITS, BRITS, mRNN, GPVAE, USGAN, SPIN, ImputeFormer) on all six datasets when combined with the Mean baseline. For example, on GEF, Mean+SAITS achieves MSE 0.1653 versus 0.1980 for SAITS alone and 0.1750 for Mean alone; on Electricity, Mean+SAITS reaches 0.0957 versus 0.1176 (SAITS) and 0.1123 (Mean). Against the modified Influence Function (which discards the 10% most harmful samples), the gain-estimation approach wins on GEF (0.1653 vs. 0.1953 for SAITS+IF), Electricity (0.0957 vs. 0.1170), and Traffic (0.4147 vs. 0.4294), while IF-based discarding actually degrades performance on the small AIR dataset (0.5207 vs. 0.5006 for SAITS alone). The method also complements robust forecasting: combining RobustTSF with the proposed ensemble further reduces Electricity MSE from 0.050 (SAITS+RobustTSF) to 0.046 (SAITS+RobustTSF+ours). In terms of computational cost, the Seq-sim estimation takes 46 s (MLP) or 10 s (DLinear) versus >48 h for exhaustive retraining, while the Seg-4 acceleration cuts this to 18 s / 8 s with minimal accuracy loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the generalized representer framework (Tsai et al., NeurIPS 2024) and gradient-tracing influence estimation (Pruthi et al., NeurIPS 2020) to the specific problem of evaluating time series imputation quality at the time-step level for downstream forecasting. It departs from the standard reconstruction-error-based imputation evaluation and from the classical Influence Function (Koh & Liang, ICML 2017) by avoiding Hessian computation and instead using a kernel machine with NTK to approximate label influence without retraining. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that in forecasting, the time series serves as both input and label, so imputation quality must be assessed through its effect on training labels rather than input features alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first retrain-free, time-step-granular evaluation of imputation methods based on downstream task performance, enabling practitioners to combine multiple imputation strategies (e.g., mean + SAITS) to achieve better forecasting than any single method. The segmented acceleration technique makes the evaluation practical for long sequences. Limitations include testing only on univariate forecasting (not classification or anomaly detection), reliance on first-order approximation that may miss multi-step interactions, and the observation that the method's gains are most pronounced when the baseline imputation is weak. Open questions include extending to end-to-end evaluation, handling optimization-based downstream tasks, and validating on larger-scale real-world deployments where missing patterns are non-random.
