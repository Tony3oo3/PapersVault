## Problem

Robust routing problems with uncertain travel times under the min-max regret criterion are NP-hard and typically solved by exact or heuristic methods that treat each instance independently, becoming inefficient for large batches of structurally similar instances. This paper presents the first neural combinatorial optimization approach to the robust routing problem, leveraging deep reinforcement learning to exploit patterns across instances with shared uncertainty structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244284/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dual-weighted graph represented as two n×n matrices (upper-bound and lower-bound travel times) plus a budget parameter Γ controlling the number of simultaneously affected edges. It outputs a complete node permutation (route) and its max-regret value, computed as the difference between the route's upper-bound cost and the optimal TSP cost under the analytically derived worst-case scenario. The reward signal is sparse, assigned only upon route completion, mirroring the delayed-reward structure of the min-max regret objective.

## Method

The architecture is an encoder-decoder model where the encoder applies MatNet separately to the upper and lower bound matrices (treating each as a bipartite graph) and fuses the resulting node embeddings by summation. The decoder uses a masked multi-head cross attention mechanism with a context vector formed by concatenating the first and last visited node embeddings to select the next node autoregressively. Training combines REINFORCE with POMO (sampling n trajectories with different starting nodes per instance), and the key insight is that the inner TSP* in the regret reward is approximated by a pre-trained MatNet TSP solver rather than an exact solver, making the training loop tractable. The worst-case scenario for each generated solution is derived in closed form (edges in the solution take upper bounds, others take lower bounds), so the reward reduces to a single TSP evaluation on a modified cost matrix. At inference, POMO multi-start sampling with randomized one-hot embedding augmentation (up to 128 augmented instances) selects the best solution, and Gurobi is used to compute the exact objective for fair comparison.

## Results

On RTSP instances (R-20-100 to R-50-1000), the proposed method without augmentation achieves gaps of 0.21%–2.55% in 0.3–0.9 s per instance. With ×128 instance augmentation, gaps drop to 0%–0.70% while still completing in under 50 s, compared to 1,400–3,600+ s for the best heuristics (EGA, iDS) and up to 3,600 s for exact methods (BC, BD) on larger instances. On RCVRP (20 and 50 nodes), the method with ×32 augmentation reaches 0% gap in 2.7–25.9 s, outperforming Benders Decomposition which requires ~3,960 s and still leaves 3.66%–6.26% gaps on 50-node instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the exact Branch-and-Cut (BC) and Benders Decomposition (BD) methods from Montemanni et al. [8], the proposed method is orders of magnitude faster: on R-50-100, BC takes 1,467 s and BD hits the 3,600 s time limit, while the proposed method with ×128 augmentation finishes in 45.6 s with a 0.54% gap. Against the SA-based heuristic [10], the proposed method dominates on both quality and speed: SA-based achieves 9.23% gap on R-50-100 in 750 s, whereas the proposed method reaches 0.54% gap in 45.6 s. Against iDS [36] and EGA [11], which both hit the 3,600 s time limit on R-50-100 and achieve 0.16% and 0.00% gaps respectively, the proposed method with ×128 augmentation achieves a 0.54% gap in 45.6 s—slightly worse in quality but ~80× faster. With ×8 augmentation, the method already matches EGA's quality on R-20-100 (0.02% vs. 0.00%) in 0.7 s versus 1,407 s. On RCVRP, the proposed method with ×32 augmentation achieves 0% gap on both 20-node and 50-node instances in under 26 s, while BD requires ~3,960 s and still leaves 3.66%–6.26% gaps on 50 nodes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: neural combinatorial optimization (building on MatNet's matrix-encoding encoder [30] and POMO's multi-start policy optimization [27]) and robust routing under budget uncertainty (building on the worst-case scenario characterization from Montemanni et al. [8]). Prior neural CO work has focused almost exclusively on nominal (deterministic) routing, while prior robust routing work has relied on per-instance exact solvers (Branch-and-Cut, Benders Decomposition) or heuristics (simulated annealing, iterated dual substitution, edge generation). The paper's key methodological departure is treating the robust routing problem as a single end-to-end learning task: a dual-matrix encoder extracts features from the upper and lower bound travel time matrices, and a pre-trained TSP model is invoked during training to approximate the inner optimization in the regret computation, replacing what would otherwise be an expensive exact solve at every training step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a roughly three-orders-of-magnitude speedup (sub-second inference vs. hours for heuristics) while maintaining gaps below 0.7% with instance augmentation, making robust routing tractable for large batches of instances sharing similar uncertainty structures (e.g., daily traffic patterns). However, the method is currently capped at 50 nodes by GPU memory and is restricted to problem classes satisfying the max-regret theorem, limiting its generality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger instances, whether it extends to other robustness criteria (e.g., adjustable robustness, k-adaptability), and whether the simple summation-based fusion of upper/lower bound embeddings is truly optimal or merely sufficient at this scale.
