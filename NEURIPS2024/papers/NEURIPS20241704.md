## Problem

Gaudio, Rácz, and Sridhar (COLT 2022) determined the exact information-theoretic threshold for community recovery from two correlated stochastic block models, but left open the natural question of what happens with K ≥ 3 graphs. The central challenge is understanding how to optimally aggregate information across multiple graphs when no pairwise latent vertex correspondence can be exactly recovered, a regime where each additional graph contributes information in a non-trivial, interacting way.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241704/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K correlated SBM graphs on n vertices each, generated by subsampling a parent SBM with edge probabilities p = a·log(n)/n and q = b·log(n)/n at rate s, then applying independent random permutations to the vertex labels of all but the first graph. The output is an exact community labeling σ̂ (achieving overlap 1 with ground truth) and, as a secondary result, exact recovery of the latent permutation vector π*. The sharp threshold depends on the Chernoff-Hellinger divergence D_+(a,b) = (√a − √b)²/2 and the connectivity threshold T_c(a,b) = (a+b)/2, operating in the logarithmic average degree regime where these quantities are the natural scaling parameters.

## Method

The algorithm extends pairwise k-core matching to all C(K,2) graph pairs, then partitions vertices into 'good' (matched in at least two pairs) and 'bad' (matched in at most one pair) sets. Good vertices are labeled by majority vote on the union graph integrating all K graphs; bad vertices are labeled by majority vote on restricted subgraphs (e.g., edges in G_1 absent from G_2 and G_3), where the effective edge probability carries the factor s(1−s)^(K−1). The key technical contribution is bounding the size of intersections of bad sets across different k-core matchings, showing |F_12 ∩ F_13| = n^{1−s(1−(1−s)²)T_c(a,b)+o(1)} for K=3, which requires a novel decoupling argument (removing a vertex before applying Luczak expansion) to handle dependencies that break the two-graph analysis. The resulting threshold s(1−(1−s)^(K−1))T_c(a,b) + s(1−s)^(K−1)D_+(a,b) > 1 is proven tight via a MAP estimator impossibility argument, and it reveals a parameter regime where exact community recovery is possible from K graphs even though it is impossible from any K−1 of them and no pairwise matching is exactly recoverable.

## Results

The paper establishes the exact information-theoretic threshold for exact community recovery from K correlated SBMs: recovery is possible if and only if (1-(1-s)^K)D₊(a,b) > 1 AND s(1-(1-s)^(K-1))T_c(a,b) + s(1-s)^(K-1)D₊(a,b) > 1. For exact graph matching, the tight threshold is s(1-(1-s)^(K-1))T_c(a,b) > 1. The headline finding is that for every K ≥ 3, there exists a non-empty parameter region where exact community recovery succeeds with K graphs yet fails with any K−1 of them, and where no pairwise latent matching can be exactly recovered—demonstrating a genuine multi-graph synergy beyond pairwise aggregation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or datasets; comparisons are made against prior information-theoretic thresholds. For K=2, the threshold from Gaudio, Rácz, and Sridhar (COLT 2022) is s²T_c(a,b) + s(1-s)D₊(a,b) > 1. The new K-graph threshold s(1-(1-s)^(K-1))T_c(a,b) + s(1-s)^(K-1)D₊(a,b) > 1 is strictly weaker (i.e., permits recovery in a larger parameter region) than the naive pairwise condition s²T_c(a,b) > 1 from Rácz and Sridhar (NeurIPS 2021), which would be the best one could achieve by independently matching each pair. The improvement is most dramatic for small s: the graph-matching term improves from s²T_c to s(1-(1-s)^(K-1))T_c, which for K=3 and small s is approximately 3s²T_c versus s²T_c. The paper also identifies a regime (the 'Pink' and 'Yellow' regions in Figure 2) where community recovery is possible from three graphs even though it is impossible from any two, and where exact graph matching between any pair is also impossible—something that has no analogue in the two-graph setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the two-graph results of Gaudio, Rácz, and Sridhar (COLT 2022), who determined the sharp information-theoretic threshold for exact community recovery from two correlated SBMs and posed the open question of what happens with more than two graphs. The technical machinery builds on k-core matching (Cullina et al., 2020) and the Luczak expansion framework, but the key new contribution is extending the k-core analysis to handle intersections of 'bad sets' across multiple pairwise matchings simultaneously. The impossibility proof adapts the MAP analysis from Gaudio et al. to the multi-graph setting with carefully chosen additional information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a clean open problem and reveals a richer phase diagram for K ≥ 3 than the two-graph case: there exist parameter regimes where exact community recovery is possible with K graphs even though it is impossible with K−1 graphs and no pairwise matching is exactly recoverable. This quantifies the value of each additional correlated graph and shows that information can be aggregated across multiple inexact matchings in ways that are not reducible to pairwise analysis. Open questions include whether the information-theoretic threshold is achievable by polynomial-time algorithms (the k-core step is currently exponential), extension to general block models with more than two communities, and alternative correlation constructions that differ from the subsampling model for K ≥ 3.
