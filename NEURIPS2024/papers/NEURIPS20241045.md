## Problem

Vision Transformers have fixed architectures that cannot adapt to the diverse computational resources of real-world deployment devices. Existing approaches like knowledge distillation and model compression require N separate training runs to produce N differently-scaled models, making them inflexible and computationally expensive for generating a family of models tailored to different hardware constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241045/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on 224×224 RGB images and outputs class labels across 9 downstream classification benchmarks. The key I/O distinction is at the model level: the decomposition phase consumes a single pre-trained DeiT-Base (12 layers, 86M params) trained on ImageNet-1K, and produces 12 reusable learngenes. These learngenes are then recomposed to initialize ViTs with any combination of 2–12 layers and 1–12 learngenes, yielding 144 distinct model configurations without additional large-scale training.

## Method

The paper decomposes each ViT layer W_l as a linear combination of N shared learngenes A_n with pre-defined Chebyshev polynomial coefficients a(l,n) = T_n((l-1)/L), justified by the observation (via PCA) that well-trained ViT layer parameters exhibit an approximately linear trend with layer position. During decomposition, learngenes are trained incrementally (ICT): at each iteration only the newly added learngene is updated while all previously trained ones are frozen, using a combined cross-entropy and KL-distillation loss against a RegNet-16GF teacher. After decomposition, any subset of learngenes can be recomposed into a ViT of arbitrary depth via the same polynomial coefficients, with two downstream training modes: 'w/o const' (layers trained independently after initialization) and 'w/ const' (parameters remain constrained by the linear combination, so trainable parameters scale with learngene count rather than layer count). This single 500-epoch decomposition run replaces the ~43,200 epochs that would be needed to individually train all 144 model variants via distillation.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, the fully decomposed 12-learngene ViT achieves 83.70% top-1 accuracy, matching DeiT-Base trained as a whole. In downstream evaluation across 9 datasets, the 12-layer ViT initialized with a single learngene (7.8M params, 'w/ const') reaches 72.3% on iNaturalist-2019, surpassing Efficient-ViT (8.8M params) by 6.3%. The full 12-layer/12-learngene model (86M params) achieves 98.96% on CIFAR-10 and 91.96% on CIFAR-100, matching or exceeding DeiT-B (86M) at the same parameter budget. The method reduces training cost by ~80× when generating 144 diverse-scale model variants compared to training each individually.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pre-training & fine-tuning (DeiT-T/S/B), the 'w/ const' strategy with fewer learngenes achieves competitive or superior accuracy at lower parameter counts: the 12L-4LG model (28.7M) reaches 90.91% on CIFAR-100, exceeding DeiT-S (22M, 89.59%) by 1.3%, while the full 12L-12LG (86M) matches DeiT-B on CIFAR-10 (98.96 vs 98.92) and exceeds it on CIFAR-100 (91.96 vs 90.03). Against model compression, the 12L-1LG model (7.8M) outperforms Efficient-ViT (8.8M) by 6.3% on iNaturalist (72.3% vs 66.0%) and by 2.2% on CIFAR-100 (88.37% vs 86.63%), despite having fewer parameters. The 12L-6LG model (43M) matches Mini-DeiT (43M) on CIFAR-10 (98.75 vs 98.57) and exceeds it on CIFAR-100 (90.69 vs 90.29). Against SN-Net (stitchable networks), the decomposition approach achieves better ImageNet accuracy with lower total training cost (800 epochs vs 1150 epochs). The 'w/o const' strategy with fewer layers (e.g., 2L, 4L) underperforms baselines at the same parameter count, but converges to competitive results at 6+ layers, confirming that depth matters more than learngene count for unconstrained training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Learngene framework (Wang et al., 2022, 2023), which condenses knowledge from ancestry models into reusable components, by introducing a concrete linear decomposition mechanism for ViTs using pre-defined Chebyshev polynomial coefficients. It departs from standard model compression approaches (MiniViT, Efficient-ViT, SN-Net) that require separate training runs for each target scale, instead proposing a single decomposition pass that yields reusable learngenes for arbitrary recomposition. The key conceptual departure is the assumption—supported by PCA analysis—that ViT layer parameters follow an approximately linear trend with depth, enabling shared learngene spaces across layers with fixed polynomial weights rather than independently learned per-layer parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80× reduction in training cost for generating diverse-scale models addresses a real deployment bottleneck, and the 'w/ const' training mode (a 12-layer ViT with only 7.8M trainable parameters outperforming Efficient-ViT with 8.8M) offers a practical path for resource-constrained fine-tuning. However, the method is validated only on the DeiT architecture for image classification, and the linear-parameter assumption's generality across other transformer families (e.g., Swin, ConvNeXt-V2) or non-classification tasks remains untested. Open questions include whether the Chebyshev coefficient choice is truly optimal or merely convenient, whether the decomposition generalizes to deeper or wider ViTs, and whether the incremental training protocol can be parallelized without degrading the learned learngene hierarchy.
