## Problem

Deep survival models that use ranking losses to improve discrimination (e.g., C-index) typically degrade calibration, because these losses directly manipulate model outputs to enforce pairwise risk ordering. This trade-off limits clinical utility, since poorly calibrated predictions can lead to over- or under-treatment. The paper asks whether discrimination can be enhanced without directly altering the survival probability outputs that NLL training calibrates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete-time tabular survival data: each patient is a feature vector x with an observed time-to-event τ (or censoring time) and an event indicator δ, over a finite discrete time horizon T = {0, …, T_max}. The network produces two types of outputs: (i) a per-time-point conditional hazard λ̂(t|x) from which survival and risk functions are derived for the NLL loss, and (ii) a d-dimensional embedding z = g_ψ(f_θ(x)) used exclusively for the contrastive objective. Augmented (corrupted) copies of each sample serve as positive pairs in the embedding space, while all other batch members act as weighted negatives.

## Method

ConSurv combines a standard discrete-time NLL loss (for calibration) with a novel Survival Outcome-aware Noise-Contrastive Estimation (SNCE) loss (for discrimination) that operates entirely in the latent embedding space, never touching the hazard/survival outputs. The key mechanism is an importance-sampling weight w(τ⁻;τ) = 1 − exp(−|τ−τ⁻|/σ) applied to negative pairs: samples with large time-to-event differences receive high contrastive penalty, while those with similar outcomes (potential false negatives) receive near-zero penalty, encoding the inductive bias that similar event times imply similar clinical status. Right-censoring is handled by an indicator I_{i,j} that restricts comparisons to valid pairs (both uncensored, or one uncensored before the other's censoring time with a margin α). The architecture comprises a shared encoder f_θ, a projection head g_ψ for the contrastive space, and a hazard network f_ϕ for survival prediction; tabular augmentation follows SCARF's marginal corruption process. The total loss is L_NLL + β·L_SNCE, with β balancing the two objectives.

## Results

ConSurv achieves the best integrated time-dependent C-index (CI) across all six clinical datasets, reaching 0.688 on METABRIC, 0.729 on NWTCO, 0.696 on GBSG, 0.810 on FLCHAIN, 0.618 on SUPPORT, and 0.865 on SEER. On calibration, ConSurv attains the best or near-best integrated Brier score (IBS) on every dataset (e.g., 0.072 on FLCHAIN, 0.004 on SEER) and passes D-Calibration tests on 4 of 6 datasets (NWTCO: 10/10, FLCHAIN: 9/10, SEER: 10/10, METABRIC: 6/10). Notably, ConSurv outperforms ranking-loss-based models (DeepHit, DRSA) by large margins on both discrimination and calibration, while also surpassing calibration-focused models (DCS, X-CAL) on CI without explicitly incorporating a calibration loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConSurv outperforms all six baselines on CI (discrimination) across all six datasets. Against CoxPH and DeepSurv (partial-likelihood-based models), ConSurv gains 2.6–10.5 CI points (e.g., 0.810 vs. 0.800 on FLCHAIN, 0.865 vs. 0.857 on SEER) while matching or improving IBS. Against ranking-loss models DeepHit and DRSA, the advantage is substantial on both metrics: ConSurv improves CI by 5–11 points and IBS by 0.02–0.18 (e.g., on SUPPORT, CI 0.618 vs. 0.502 for DeepHit; IBS 0.192 vs. 0.272). Against calibration-focused models DCS and X-CAL, ConSurv achieves higher CI on every dataset (e.g., 0.688 vs. 0.598 for DCS on METABRIC) while remaining competitive on IBS and DDC, demonstrating that the survival-outcome-aware contrastive loss enhances discrimination without the calibration degradation typical of ranking losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConSurv builds directly on the importance-sampling NCE framework of Robinson et al. (2020), adapting it to survival analysis by replacing the generic hard-negative weighting with a survival-outcome-aware weight function based on time-to-event differences. It departs from the dominant paradigm in deep survival models—where ranking losses (DeepHit, DRSA) or explicit calibration losses (DCS, X-CAL) are added to NLL—by instead performing discrimination in the latent embedding space via contrastive learning while reserving model outputs solely for the NLL objective. The use of SCARF for tabular augmentation and the handling of right-censoring through a margin-based indicator for comparable pairs are practical extensions that distinguish it from prior contrastive-learning-for-regression work (e.g., Zha et al., Kerdabadi et al.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized clinical concern: ranking-loss-based survival models improve discrimination but degrade calibration, potentially leading to misallocated treatments. By showing that survival-outcome-aware contrastive learning in the embedding space can boost discrimination while preserving NLL-based calibration, ConSurv offers a principled alternative to the ranking-loss recipe. However, the gains are incremental (e.g., CI improvements of ~0.02–0.04 over NLL-only baselines), D-CAL pass rates are mixed across datasets (0/10 on GBSG), and the method's reliance on observed event times limits its applicability to heavily censored settings like SEER (98.9% censored). Open questions include whether the approach extends to continuous-time or competing-risks settings, and whether richer augmentation strategies for tabular survival data could further close the gap.
