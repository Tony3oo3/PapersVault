## Problem

Existing VLM-based open-vocabulary semantic segmentation methods all rely on additional explicit segmentation-specific networks (mask proposal networks or semantic decoders) to convert VLM's image-level knowledge into pixel-level predictions, incurring substantial training cost. The paper asks whether prompt learning alone can guide a VLM to directly produce pixel-level segmentation without any such auxiliary networks, and identifies that existing prompt learning methods fail because they provide only image-level granularity and lack an explicit image-text relationship needed for pixel-level visual classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242363/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 512×512) and a set of text class names formatted with templates (e.g., 'A photo of a {}'), encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder into class embeddings. It outputs a per-pixel class score map computed as the matrix product of projected patch embeddings and projected class embeddings, with no intermediate mask proposals or decoder. The entire system uses only ~3.2M trainable parameters (2% of the CLIP ViT-B/14 total), and the segmentation result is obtained directly from the last-layer VLM features without any segmentation-specific head.

## Method

The core contribution is the Relationship Prompt Module (RPM), inserted in parallel at every layer of a frozen CLIP ViT encoder, which generates a pixel-level relationship prompt from the current image embeddings and the last-layer class embeddings. RPM comprises three blocks: (1) a Multi-scale Mixture-of-Experts (M2oE) block that aggregates patch embeddings across scales via a sparsely-gated expert network; (2) an Image-to-Pixel semantic attention (ITP) block that computes a relationship attention map m = p·(t⊙g)ᵀ, where the Hadamard product of the [CLS] token and class embeddings provides image-level weighting and the subsequent matrix product with patch embeddings provides pixel-level weighting; and (3) an Adaptive Prompt Generation (APG) block that projects the attention map through a trainable parameter h to produce a high-dimensional prompt concatenated with the image embeddings for the next layer. A minimal Linear Projection Module (LPM) of two linear layers then maps the final image and text features into a shared space and computes their matrix product as the segmentation output. The key insight is the progressive image-to-pixel attention scheme: as layers deepen, the relationship attention map refines from coarse image-level focus to precise pixel-level semantics, eliminating the need for any explicit segmentation network.

## Results

In the zero-shot setting without self-training, RPN achieves 84.6% mIoUu on VOC, 42.8% mIoUu on COCO, and 58.7% mIoUu on Context, outperforming the strongest baselines (ZegCLIP, FreeSeg) by 6.0–6.8% mIoUu on VOC. With self-training, RPN reaches 93.6% mIoUu on VOC and 70.8% mIoUu on Context, surpassing ZegCLIP† by 3.7% and 2.3% respectively. In the open-vocabulary setting with ViT-L/14, RPN attains 14.9 mIoU on A-847, 36.4 on A-150, and 96.6 on PAS-20, achieving state-of-the-art on three of five benchmarks. All results are obtained with only 3.2M trainable parameters (2% of total), compared to 13.8M for ZegCLIP and 60.3M for ZegFormer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPN consistently outperforms all VLM-based baselines across zero-shot and open-vocabulary settings. In the zero-shot setting without self-training, RPN surpasses ZegCLIP [19] by +6.8% mIoUu on VOC (84.6 vs. 77.8) and +4.1% on Context (58.7 vs. 54.6), and beats FreeSeg [10] by +6.0% mIoUu on VOC. With self-training, RPN leads ZegCLIP† [19] by +3.7% mIoUu on VOC (93.6 vs. 89.9), +1.3% on COCO (61.2 vs. 59.9), and +2.3% on Context (70.8 vs. 68.5). In the open-vocabulary setting with ViT-L/14, RPN achieves the best mIoU on A-847 (14.9 vs. SED's 13.9), A-150 (36.4 vs. SED's 35.2), and PAS-20 (96.6, tied with CAT-Seg [52]), while trailing SED [54] slightly on PC-459 (22.1 vs. 22.6) and PC-59 (61.9 vs. 60.6). Compared to PEFT methods (LoRA, VPT, Adapter, etc.) in Table 10, RPN achieves substantially higher mIoUu (84.6 vs. 53.1 for LoRA on VOC) with fewer trainable parameters (3.2M vs. 4.0M), demonstrating that relationship prompt learning is fundamentally more effective than generic parameter-efficient fine-tuning for pixel-level segmentation. On efficiency, RPN uses only 3.2M trainable parameters and 84.2 GFLOPs on VOC, compared to 13.8M/110.4G for ZegCLIP and 60.3M/1829.3G for ZegFormer [11], while achieving the highest FPS (10.6 vs. 9.0 and 1.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in VLM-based open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, where methods like ZegFormer, ZegCLIP, and MaskCLIP all rely on explicit segmentation-specific networks (mask proposal networks or semantic decoders) to translate VLM's image-level knowledge into pixel-level predictions. Instead, it repurposes prompt learning—previously applied at image-level granularity (CoOp, VPT, MaPLe)—to generate pixel-level relationship prompts that directly guide a frozen VLM encoder toward OVSS. The design is conceptually inspired by LoRA's low-rank bypass, but restructured as a prefix-tuning mechanism that concatenates the relationship prompt with image embeddings rather than adding it to frozen weights, and incorporates a mixture-of-experts block for multi-scale aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a VLM with only ~3M trainable parameters (2% of total) and no segmentation-specific networks can match or exceed methods requiring 14–60M parameters, challenging the assumption that explicit decoders are necessary for pixel-level VLM adaptation. A notable finding is that even training-free projection modules (TFPM_b, TFPM_c) achieve SOTA when paired with RPM, suggesting the relationship prompt carries nearly all the task-specific signal. Limitations include dependence on CLIP's large-scale pre-training (ViT and MAE weights perform substantially worse), restriction to ViT-based encoders, and the authors' own acknowledgment that directly manipulating attention maps or dynamically reorganizing multi-head attention could yield further gains. Open questions include whether this prompt-only paradigm extends to panoptic or instance segmentation, and whether it generalizes beyond CLIP to other VLM architectures.
