## Problem

Prior causal disentanglement results require interventional data on latent factors, an assumption that is debatable since directly intervening on latent variables makes them non-latent and may be infeasible for ethical or cost reasons. This paper asks what can be identified from purely observational data alone, without any structural restrictions on the mixing function or the latent causal DAG, and provides a precise characterization of the identifiability limits in this setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes N observational samples X ∈ R^d generated by an unknown full-column-rank linear mixing of n latent factors (where n is itself learned), along with point-wise estimates of the score Jacobian J_X(x) obtained via any off-the-shelf score estimation method (e.g., second-order Stein or SSM-VR). The output is a two-level representation: latent causal factors Ẑ identified up to upstream-layer linear transformations (each variable is a linear combination of itself and all upstream variables), and exogenous noise variables Ê identified up to layer-wise linear transformations (each variable is a linear combination only within its own causal layer). The number of zero-variance diagonal entries in the estimated Jacobian equals the number of leaf nodes, providing a data-driven stopping criterion for the iterative layer recovery.

## Method

The core principle exploits the fact that under nonlinear additive Gaussian noise models, the i-th diagonal element of the latent score Jacobian J_Z has zero variance if and only if node i is a leaf in the causal DAG. Since the observed Jacobian relates to the latent one via a quadratic form J_Ẑ = βᵀ J_Z β, maximizing the number of zero-variance diagonal entries in J_Ẑ forces β to have a maximum number of columns with zeros at non-leaf indices, thereby disentangling leaf nodes from non-leaf nodes. This rank-constrained ℓ₀ optimization (Eq. 1) is NP-hard, so the paper decomposes it into a sequence of quadratically constrained quadratic programs (QCQPs) solved column-by-column, where each sub-problem enforces zero sample variance of a single diagonal term subject to orthogonality and unit-norm constraints. After recovering Ẑ up to upstream layers via iterative QCQP solving (Algorithm 1), a second step (Algorithm 2) recovers exogenous noise layer-by-layer by fitting nonlinear regressions of each layer's Ẑ representation on all upstream noise representations and taking residuals. The approach is modular: any score estimation method can be plugged in, and any QCQP solver (e.g., Gurobi with cutting planes) can be used.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is theoretical: under nonlinear additive Gaussian noise models with linear mixing, latent causal variables are identifiable up to upstream layers and exogenous noise variables up to their layers from purely observational data (Theorems 1 and 2). Empirically, using a perfect score oracle on 4-node causal graphs (line and Y-structure), the algorithm recovers exogenous noise variables with MAC of 0.999–1.0. With data-driven score estimation (Stein or SSM-VR), MAC drops to approximately 0.36–0.45 across sample sizes of 10³–5×10⁴, and accuracy improves monotonically with the signal-to-error ratio of the Jacobian estimates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other causal disentanglement methods; its empirical evaluation focuses on validating the theoretical identifiability guarantees and assessing sensitivity to score estimation quality. The primary comparison is between a perfect score oracle (MAC ≈ 1.0) and two data-driven score estimators: the second-order Stein estimator and sliced score matching with variance reduction (SSM-VR). SSM-VR slightly outperforms Stein at larger sample sizes (e.g., 0.43 vs. 0.367 MAC at 5×10⁴ samples), while Stein is marginally better at the smallest sample size (0.39 vs. 0.358 at 10³). Both estimators show high variance across runs (±0.1–0.23), reflecting the difficulty of second-order score estimation. The paper also demonstrates that MAC improves monotonically with the signal-to-error ratio of the Jacobian, with Stein operating at approximately SER ≈ 2 and SSM-VR at SER ≈ 6, suggesting that improved score estimation methods would directly translate to better disentanglement accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal disentanglement framework from Zhang et al. (NeurIPS 2024), which required soft interventions, to the purely observational setting, and builds on the score-matching-based causal discovery methods of Rolland et al. (ICML 2022) and Montagna et al. (2023) that operate on fully observed variables. It departs from prior purely-observational identifiability results (Cai et al., Kivva et al., Kong et al.) that all required structural restrictions on the mixing function (e.g., the pure-child assumption), by showing that nonlinear additive Gaussian noise asymmetries alone suffice for layer-wise identifiability with no such restrictions. The algorithmic contribution repurposes the Jacobian-variance principle from Rolland et al. into a sequence of QCQPs over estimated scores, a reduction not present in the fully-observed causal discovery literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first precise characterization of what is and is not learnable about latent causal factors from purely observational data without structural restrictions: latent variables are identifiable up to upstream layers, exogenous noise up to their own layer, and nothing finer. This delineates a clear boundary for the field and motivates future work on what minimal additional assumptions (interventions, multi-view data, faithfulness constraints) are needed for stronger guarantees. Practical limitations include the narrow empirical scope (4-node graphs only), significant accuracy degradation under realistic score estimation (MAC drops from ~1.0 to ~0.4), and the open question of whether the QCQP-based algorithm scales to larger latent dimensions.
