## Problem

Real-time HAR models maintain low latency by restricting network depth or per-layer connections, but this limits accuracy because the classifier only sees features from the final layer, losing the detailed temporal information preserved in earlier layers that is critical for distinguishing semantically similar activities (e.g., 'Sit' vs. 'Talk-Sit'). The paper asks whether all-layer feature aggregation can be achieved without increasing the computational cost beyond that of a conventional shallow CNN.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242216/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw 1D sensor time-series segments (accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer channels) of varying lengths (125–512 samples) and sampling rates (9–100 Hz) from wearable devices, with input channel counts ranging from a few to 117 (e.g., OPPORTUNITY, REALDISP). It outputs a single activity class label via a Softmax layer whose input size is fixed at N regardless of network depth L. The architecture is designed for on-device inference, with measured latency of ~2.25 ms per 3-second window on a CPU-only setup.

## Method

CALANet introduces two mechanisms to aggregate features from all L convolutional layers into the classifier at no extra asymptotic cost. First, Learnable Channel-wise Transformation Matrices (LCTMs) compress each channel's temporal feature vector x_m ∈ R^T into a short vector y_m ∈ R^N (N ≪ T) via a learned matrix, then combine channels linearly; the product B_m = a_m·A_m is directly trainable as a dense layer, making the per-layer aggregation cost O(TMN) rather than O(T²M). Second, the Scalable Layer Aggregation Pool (SLAP) applies grouped convolution with channel shuffle (inspired by ShuffleNet) with G = L groups, and divides the LCTM output dimension by L so the concatenated all-layer feature fed to Softmax remains N elements. The combined effect reduces the total time complexity to O(T·D_k·N²), independent of L, which the paper proves is equivalent to a 2-layer shallow CNN (Corollary 1) and to a 1-layer CNN when input channels ≈ output channels (Corollary 2).

## Results

CALANet achieves state-of-the-art F1-scores on all seven HAR benchmark datasets: 96.1 (UCI-HAR), 78.3 (UniMiB-SHAR), 90.0 (DSADS), 81.6 (OPPORTUNITY), 97.5 (KU-HAR), 79.4 (PAMAP2), and 98.2 (REALDISP). Crucially, it does so with the lowest FLOPs among all compared methods (e.g., 7.6M on UCI-HAR vs. 17.9M for Shallow ConvNet and 143.1M for RevAttNet). The actual inference time on an AMD Ryzen 7 5800X is 2.25 ms/window (mean), comparable to Shallow ConvNet (2.15 ms) despite being 9× deeper. On the MIT-BIH ECG dataset, CALANet achieves 98.2% average accuracy, comparable to dedicated ECG models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CALANet outperforms all three real-time CNN baselines (Shallow ConvNet, RepHAR, Res-GTSNet) on all seven datasets with significantly lower FLOPs; for example, on KU-HAR it achieves 97.5 F1 vs. 77.9 (Shallow ConvNet), 93.4 (RepHAR), and 94.5 (Res-GTSNet). Against accuracy-oriented recurrent/attention models, CALANet beats DeepConvLSTM and Bi-GRU-I on all seven datasets while using 5–20× fewer FLOPs. Compared to RevAttNet and IF-ConvTransformer, CALANet achieves comparable F1 (within 0.3–1.0 points on most datasets) but with 10–20× lower FLOPs (e.g., 29.6M vs. 335.3M on KU-HAR). CALANet slightly trails MILLET on UniMiB-SHAR (78.3 vs. 81.4) and KU-HAR (97.5 vs. 97.8), and RevAttNet on KU-HAR (97.5 vs. 97.7) and REALDISP (98.2 vs. 98.5), but with dramatically lower computational cost. Against TSC models (T-ResNet, T-FCN, DSN), CALANet wins on 5–6 of 7 datasets with 3–10× fewer FLOPs, confirming that all-layer aggregation in a 1D-CNN is sufficient for real-time HAR without the overhead of deeper or attention-based architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of real-time HAR research that has focused on either shallow CNNs (Ignatov, Zebin, Wan) or efficient convolution variants (RepHAR, GTSNet, selective kernel modules) to reduce inference cost. It departs from the standard practice of classifying solely on last-layer features by introducing a cheap all-layer aggregation mechanism, drawing conceptually from DenseNet-style feature reuse and Deeply-Supervised Nets, but making it computationally free via learnable channel-wise transformation matrices (LCTMs). The SLAP component directly adapts ShuffleNet's grouped convolution and channel shuffle to keep the aggregation cost constant regardless of depth, and the time-complexity formalization builds on the authors' prior GTSNet work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical proof that all-layer aggregation is computationally equivalent to a shallow CNN (even L=1 when M≈N) provides a principled justification for deeper architectures in resource-constrained wearable settings, removing the traditional accuracy-vs-latency tradeoff. The CALA structure's generality—demonstrated by its plug-and-play application to SqueezeNet and ECG classification—suggests broader utility beyond HAR. However, the paper acknowledges it does not account for device-specific runtime optimizations (memory access patterns, parallelism, quantization), and the F1 improvements over strong baselines are modest (1–3 points), leaving open whether the architectural complexity is justified when simpler efficiency tricks (e.g., GTSNet's temporal shift) already achieve comparable FLOPs.
