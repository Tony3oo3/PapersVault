## Problem

Orthogonal fine-tuning methods like OFT and BOFT use structured orthogonal matrices to adapt pretrained weights, but existing parametrizations face a trade-off: block-diagonal structures (OFT) are too restrictive, while butterfly factorizations (BOFT) require 5–6 stacked sparse matrices to form a dense orthogonal matrix, incurring significant computational and memory overhead. The paper asks whether dense orthogonal matrices can be constructed with fewer factors and fewer trainable parameters while preserving the expressiveness needed for effective fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

For fine-tuning, the method takes a frozen pretrained weight matrix W⁰ ∈ ℝ^{d×n} and learns a structured orthogonal matrix Q ∈ ℝ^{d×d} (initialized as identity) so that the adapted weight becomes QW⁰, with no inference-time overhead since Q can be merged into W⁰. For subject-driven generation, it consumes a text prompt plus a small set of reference images and outputs generated images via a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion UNet. For 1-Lipschitz classification, it takes an image tensor and outputs a class score, with the network's Jacobian constrained to be orthogonal at every layer.

## Method

The paper introduces GS-matrices of the form A = P_L(LPR)P_R, where L and R are block-diagonal matrices with r blocks of size b×b and P_L, P, P_R are fixed permutation matrices. The key structural choice is the permutation P_{(r,br)} (a reshape-transpose-vectorize operation), which Theorem 2 shows requires only m = 1 + ⌈log_b(r)⌉ such factors to form a dense matrix—reducing the count from 5–6 in BOFT to just 2 in practice. Orthogonality is enforced by applying the Cayley parametrization to each small block, and Theorem 1 proves this block-wise constraint is sufficient to cover all orthogonal GS-matrices. The resulting GSOFT method replaces BOFT's butterfly product with two GS factors, and a Double GSOFT variant applies orthogonal GS-matrices on both sides of W⁰ to adapt both left and right singular vectors. The construction is further extended to convolutional layers by replacing standard convolutions with grouped convolutions interleaved with channel-shuffle permutations, yielding GS orthogonal convolutions that are faster and use fewer parameters than Skew Orthogonal Convolution while maintaining 1-Lipschitz guarantees.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa-base, GSOFT (b=8, 1.42M params) achieves the best average score of 86.67, outperforming LoRA (86.53), OFT (86.27), and BOFT (85.84) with comparable parameter budgets. On subject-driven generation (DreamBooth + Stable Diffusion), GSOFT (r=32, 6.8M params) achieves the highest CLIP text similarity (0.256) while matching full fine-tuning on CLIP image similarity (0.805), using only ~7% of the parameters. On CIFAR-100 with LipConvnet-15, GS-SOC achieves 43.48% accuracy and 29.26% robust accuracy with 6.81M parameters and 1.64× speedup, slightly surpassing the SOC baseline (43.15% / 29.18%) while using 3.5× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), GSOFT achieves a slightly higher GLUE average (86.67 vs. 86.53) with a comparable parameter count (1.42M vs. 1.33M), and on DreamBooth GSOFT achieves higher CLIP text similarity (0.256 vs. 0.236) indicating better prompt adherence, though LoRA achieves higher CLIP image similarity (0.819 vs. 0.805). Against OFT (Qiu et al., 2023), GSOFT improves the GLUE average by 0.4 points (86.67 vs. 86.27) with the same parameter budget, primarily driven by gains on CoLA (+0.93) and MRPC (+0.25). Against BOFT (Liu et al., 2024), GSOFT improves the GLUE average by 0.83 points (86.67 vs. 85.84) and on DreamBooth uses half the parameters (6.8M vs. 13.6M) while training 25% faster (1.5h vs. 2.0h) and achieving higher text similarity (0.256 vs. 0.244). Against SOC (Singla et al., 2021) on CIFAR-100, GS-SOC marginally improves both accuracy (43.48% vs. 43.15%) and robust accuracy (29.26% vs. 29.18%) while reducing parameters from 24.1M to 6.81M and achieving a 1.64× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces GS-matrices (Group-and-Shuffle), a structured matrix class that unifies and generalizes both Monarch matrices (Dao et al., 2022) and Block Butterfly matrices (Chen et al., 2022) by removing the rigid block-size constraints that limit Monarch matrices and by choosing more effective permutations than those used in BOFT (Liu et al., 2024b). The key departure from BOFT is that GS-matrices require only 2 factors to form a dense orthogonal matrix (versus 5–6 for butterfly matrices), directly reducing both parameter count and computational cost in the orthogonal fine-tuning pipeline inherited from OFT (Qiu et al., 2023). The convolutional extension adapts the Skew Orthogonal Convolution (SOC) framework of Singla and Feizi (2021) by replacing full convolutions with grouped convolutions interleaved with channel shuffles, preserving the 1-Lipschitz property while cutting FLOPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from 5–6 stacked sparse matrices to just 2 is a concrete, well-motivated efficiency gain that makes orthogonal fine-tuning more practical for large models, and Theorem 1 (completeness of block-orthogonal parametrization within the GS class) provides a clean theoretical guarantee that the restricted parametrization does not lose expressivity relative to the full GS-orthogonal manifold. However, the method remains slower than LoRA during training, and the parametrization inherently trades off expressivity for efficiency, meaning it cannot represent arbitrary orthogonal matrices. Open questions include whether GS-matrices without orthogonality constraints yield useful structured layers for other tasks, and whether the approach scales to much larger architectures (e.g., 7B+ parameter LLMs) where the current experiments (RoBERTa-base, SD-2-base) may not be representative.
