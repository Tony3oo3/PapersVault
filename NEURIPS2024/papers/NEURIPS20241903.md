## Problem

State-of-the-art weight-only PTQ methods (QuIP#, AQLM) rely on vector quantization, whose codebook size grows exponentially in dimension, capping practical VQ dimensions at ≤8 and limiting quantization quality. This paper asks whether ultra-high-dimensional quantization (>100) can be achieved with tractable quantization cost and fast hardware-efficient inference, rather than being restricted to the low dimensions that VQ permits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241903/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes trained LLM weight matrices and a calibration set (used to estimate per-layer Hessian matrices via 6144 sequences of length 2048 from RedPajama). After incoherence processing via the random Hadamard transform, weights are quantized block-by-block (T_x × T_y tiles, typically 16×16) into k-bit sequences. The output is a bitstream per block plus a small initial state; at inference, each group of V weights is recovered by bitshifting the stream and applying a ≤4-instruction pseudorandom Gaussian decode, requiring no large codebook in cache.

## Method

QTIP replaces VQ with trellis-coded quantization (TCQ) on a hardware-efficient 'bitshift trellis' where node transitions are defined by a simple bit-shift-and-add rule, eliminating the need to store the trellis graph and enabling parallel decoding. Incoherence processing (random Hadamard transform) makes weights approximately i.i.d. Gaussian, reducing the problem to Gaussian source coding. Three novel compute-based codes (1MAD, 3INST, HYB) generate pseudorandom approximate Gaussian values from L-bit words in as few as 2–4 ALU instructions per weight, removing the need for a large lookup codebook while matching the decorrelating effect of RPTC's permuted codebook. A tail-biting approximation (two Viterbi calls) avoids wasting bits on the initial state for hardware word alignment. The entire TCQ quantizer is plugged into QuIP#'s BlockLDLQ rounding framework, achieving 256D effective quantization (32× higher than QuIP#'s 8D E8 lattice) at the same or better inference throughput.

## Results

On Llama 2-7B quantized to 2 bits, QTIP (hybrid code) achieves 5.86 WikiText-2 and 7.73 C4 perplexity, outperforming QuIP# (6.19 / 8.16) and AQLM (6.14 / 8.09). At 3 bits on Llama 2-70B, QTIP reaches 3.26 W2 / 5.08 C4 versus QuIP# (3.35 / 5.15) and AQLM (3.36 / 5.17). Inference throughput at 2 bits on a RTX 6000 Ada is 188 tok/s (7B) and 23.5 tok/s (70B), matching QuIP# (186 / 22.2) while being 2.3× faster than AQLM (81.5 / 8.78). On Llama 3-70B at 2 bits, QTIP achieves 4.97 W2 / 6.80 C4 perplexity versus QuIP#'s 5.77 / 7.46, a ~13% relative improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QTIP consistently outperforms both QuIP# and AQLM across all model sizes (Llama 1, 2, 3, 3.1) and bitrates (2, 3, 4 bits) on perplexity and zero-shot accuracy. At 2 bits on Llama 2-7B, QTIP reduces WikiText-2 perplexity by 0.33 over QuIP# (5.86 vs. 6.19) and 0.28 over AQLM (5.86 vs. 6.14); the gap widens at larger model sizes (e.g., 0.21 W2 on 70B vs. QuIP#). Against GPTVQ-2D on Llama 1, QTIP achieves lower perplexity at all bitrates (e.g., 6.52 vs. 6.86 W2 at 2 bits on 1-7B). On Llama 3.1 405B at 2 bits, QTIP (3.29 W2) outperforms PV-Tuning (5.70 W2) by a large margin, though PV-Tuning focuses on fine-tuning rather than quantizer design. In the Gaussian source coding comparison (Table 1), QTIP's computed codes (1MAD: 0.069 MSE, 3INST: 0.069 MSE) match the pure-lookup RPTC baseline (0.068 MSE) while eliminating the need to store a codebook, and all TCQ methods significantly outperform Lloyd-Max scalar quantization (0.118 MSE) and QuIP#'s 8D E8P VQ (0.089 MSE). Inference speed matches QuIP# (188 vs. 186 tok/s at 2-bit 7B) while being 2.3× faster than AQLM, confirming that QTIP's 256-dimensional quantization incurs no additional decoding cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

QTIP builds directly on the VQ-based PTQ lineage (QuIP#, AQLM) that established incoherence processing via random Hadamard transforms and BlockLDLQ rounding as the standard framework for high-quality LLM weight quantization. Its key departure is replacing vector quantization—which is hardware-limited to dimension ≤8 due to exponential codebook size—with trellis-coded quantization (TCQ), a source-coding technique from information theory (Marcellin & Fischer, 1990) that achieves linear cost in dimension via the Viterbi algorithm. The bitshift trellis structure (Mao & Gray, 2010) is repurposed for hardware-efficient parallel decoding, and the paper introduces novel compute-based pseudorandom Gaussian codes (1MAD, 3INST, HYB) that eliminate the need to store large codebooks during inference, which is the first lookup-free Gaussian trellis code construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QTIP demonstrates that quantization dimensionality, not bitrate, is the primary driver of compression quality: a 2-bit 256D TCQ model outperforms a 4-bit 8D VQ model, fundamentally shifting the design space for LLM PTQ. The compute-based codes (≤4 instructions per weight) make ultra-high-dimensional quantization practical on current hardware, achieving SOTA quality at inference speeds matching QuIP# while AQLM's large codebook prevents fast decoding. Open questions include whether the i.i.d. Gaussian assumption from RHT holds robustly across all model architectures and training regimes, whether the approach extends to activation or mixed-precision quantization, and how the method scales to future hardware with different cache hierarchies or instruction sets.
