## Problem

Multimodal LLMs can process images and 3D point clouds, but no existing model can directly ingest Neural Radiance Fields as a modality. Converting a NeRF into images or point clouds before feeding it to an MLLM introduces lossy discretization, viewpoint dependence, and resolution constraints that degrade output quality. This paper asks whether the raw weights of a NeRF's MLP can be processed directly by a language model to enable NeRF captioning, Q&A, and classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240036/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the raw weight matrices and biases of a NeRF MLP (stacked into a single matrix of shape S×H, where S depends on the number of hidden layers and units) along with a free-form text instruction. The NeRF is compressed into a single 1024-dimensional global embedding that is projected into the LLM's token space between two learned special tokens (<n_start>, <n_end>), so the entire 3D object is represented by one token in the sequence. Outputs are autoregressive text: brief or detailed captions, single- or multi-round Q&A answers, or a class label chosen from a provided list.

## Method

LLaNA uses the nf2vec meta-encoder (frozen, pre-trained on ShapeNet) to process NeRF MLP weights row-by-row through an MLP with batch normalization, producing S tokens that are max-pooled into a single 1024-dim global embedding. A trainable linear projection layer maps this embedding into LLaMA 2's word-embedding dimension, and the resulting vector is inserted between two learned special tokens in the input sequence. Training follows a two-stage protocol: first, only the projector and special-token embeddings are trained on brief descriptions with the LLM frozen (lr=0.002, 3 epochs); second, both the projector and the full LLM are instruction-tuned on detailed descriptions and multi-round Q&A (lr=0.0002, 3 epochs). The key insight is that NeRF weights encode holistic geometry and appearance from all viewpoints simultaneously, so a meta-network can extract complete object information without any rendering, marching-cubes extraction, or viewpoint selection—avoiding the information loss and design decisions inherent in discretizing the NeRF into images or point clouds.

## Results

On the ShapeNeRF-Text benchmark, LLaNA-7b achieves 68.63 Sentence-BERT similarity for brief captioning (vs. 61.00 for the best image baseline LLaVA-13b), 77.43 for detailed captioning (vs. 60.21 for LLaVA-13b multi-view), and 81.03 for single-round Q&A (vs. 74.70 for PointLLM-7b). On the human-validated HST dataset, LLaNA scores 59.20 S-BERT for brief captioning, outperforming all baselines. For zero-shot NeRF classification, LLaNA achieves 67.14% accuracy, trailing only LLaVA multi-view (73.45%) but surpassing all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaNA outperforms all five baseline families on brief captioning, detailed captioning, and single-round Q&A. Against LLaVA (v1.6), the strongest image-based baseline, LLaNA gains 7.6 S-BERT points on brief captioning and 17.2 points on detailed captioning, with the gap widening for multi-view LLaVA on detailed descriptions (77.43 vs. 60.21). Against PointLLM-7b, the strongest 3D baseline, LLaNA leads by 6.3 S-BERT points on Q&A (81.03 vs. 74.70) and by 18.4 points on detailed captioning (77.43 vs. 59.02). BLIP-2 and GPT4Point are consistently the weakest baselines, trailing LLaNA by 25–50 S-BERT points across tasks. The sole exception is zero-shot NeRF classification, where LLaVA multi-view (73.45%) edges out LLaNA (67.14%), though LLaNA still surpasses all point-cloud and mesh-based methods. The paper also shows that even when baselines are given ground-truth images or point clouds from the original ShapeNet meshes (an idealized upper bound), LLaNA still matches or exceeds them on most metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLaNA sits at the intersection of two recent research threads: meta-networks that process neural field weights (nf2vec, inr2vec) and multimodal LLMs that inject non-textual embeddings into a language model's token space (LLaVA, PointLLM). It repurposes nf2vec as a frozen NeRF-to-embedding encoder and adopts LLaVA's two-stage projector-then-instruction-tuning protocol, but applies both to a modality—raw NeRF MLP weights—that neither prior line had targeted for language generation. The paper explicitly departs from the dominant NeRF-language paradigm (text-to-NeRF synthesis, NeRF editing, NeRF retrieval via CLIP alignment) by treating the NeRF as an input to be *understood* rather than a target to be generated or edited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single global embedding of NeRF weights can drive captioning, Q&A, and classification more accurately than any rendered view or extracted point cloud, the paper validates NeRFs as a first-class language-understanding modality and provides the first benchmark (ShapeNeRF-Text) for evaluating such assistants. Key open questions remain: the nf2vec encoder was trained exclusively on synthetic ShapeNet objects, so generalization to real-world or scene-level NeRFs is untested; the framework is restricted to MLP-based NeRFs and cannot yet handle hash-grid architectures like InstantNGP; and the automatic annotation pipeline (LLaVA → LLaMA) introduces potential error propagation that limits ground-truth reliability. If NeRFs become a standard digital-twin format, a robust NeRF-language interface could enable natural-language querying of 3D assets without any rendering pipeline.
