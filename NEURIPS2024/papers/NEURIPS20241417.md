## Problem

Despite advances in tabular DNNs, they still underperform GBDTs on tabular data. The paper identifies a key structural gap: tree-based models achieve on-off sparse selection of columns (each tree uses only a subset of features), but existing tabular DNNs cannot replicate this—attention-based models produce dense softmax-weighted selections, and tree-inspired DNNs using entmax/sparsemax only achieve near-sparse effects. DOFEN addresses this by designing a differentiable mechanism for true binary (on/off) column selection within a neural network.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single tabular row containing a mix of numerical and categorical features. Numerical columns pass through linear sub-networks while categorical columns use embedding layers, each producing N_cond scalar 'soft conditions' per column. The output is a scalar prediction for regression or a class-probability vector for classification, obtained by averaging predictions across N_forest independently sampled rODT forests (default N_forest=100), which introduces stochasticity at inference time.

## Method

DOFEN relaxes Oblivious Decision Trees into differentiable rODTs by replacing the non-differentiable Heaviside function and predefined split criteria with sigmoid-activated sub-networks (Δ1) that produce soft conditions per column. A shuffle-then-reshape procedure randomly partitions the condition matrix into N_rODT rODTs of depth d, each using a distinct subset of columns—this is the on-off sparse selection. A two-level ensemble then aggregates predictions: the first level samples N_estimator rODTs (with their learnable embedding vectors) per forest, applies softmax-weighted summing to produce a forest embedding, and the second level averages predictions from N_forest such forests via a shared prediction head (Δ3). This design mirrors bagging at two granularities (tree sampling within forests, forest sampling for the final prediction) and is trained end-to-end with standard cross-entropy or MSE loss aggregated across all forests.

## Results

On the Tabular Benchmark (73 datasets), DOFEN achieves 0.7725 mean accuracy on medium-sized classification and 0.6611 mean R² on medium-sized regression with default hyperparameters, and 0.7805 / 0.6882 with optimal hyperparameters. Among all DNN-based models, DOFEN consistently ranks first or shares the top position across all four task/feature-type combinations. DOFEN also achieves the lowest FLOPs (0.1845M) and smallest parameter count (0.0140M) among all compared DNN models, though its inference time (0.0125s) is longer than FT-Transformer (0.0058s) and NODE (0.0041s) due to group convolution overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOFEN outperforms all tabular DNN baselines: it beats Trompt (0.7704), FT-Transformer (0.7662), NODE (0.7658), and GRANDE on medium-sized classification accuracy, and similarly leads on regression R² (0.6611 vs. Trompt 0.6541, FT-Transformer 0.6359, NODE 0.1080). Against tree-based models, DOFEN is competitive on numerical features—ranking within the top three alongside XGBoost (0.7717), LightGBM (0.7757), and CatBoost (0.7777) on classification—but trails them on heterogeneous features, where CatBoost and XGBoost maintain a clear advantage. On large-sized numerical classification, DOFEN surpasses CatBoost to become the overall top performer. In terms of efficiency, DOFEN uses ~290× fewer FLOPs than Trompt and ~18× fewer than FT-Transformer, but its inference time is roughly 2× that of FT-Transformer due to inefficient group convolution in PyTorch. The paper also shows that removing the two-level forest ensemble (i.e., using all rODTs in a single forest) causes severe overfitting, dropping classification accuracy from 0.7725 to 0.7362 and regression R² from 0.6605 to 0.3238.

## Relation to Prior Work

DOFEN extends the tree-inspired DNN lineage (Deep Forest, NODE, TabNet, GradTree, GRANDE) by directly relaxing Oblivious Decision Trees (Kohavi 1994) into differentiable 'rODTs' through random column selection and sigmoid-based soft conditions, rather than learning decision paths as NODE and GRANDE do. Its key conceptual departure is achieving true on-off sparse column selection via a shuffle-then-reshape procedure, in contrast to attention-based tabular DNNs (SAINT, FT-Transformer, Trompt) that perform dense softmax aggregation across columns. The two-level ensemble (randomly sampling rODTs into multiple forests, then bagging forest predictions) is a novel architectural choice not present in prior tree-inspired DNNs, which typically apply a single weighted sum over base model predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DOFEN establishes a new SOTA among DNN-based models on the Tabular Benchmark and narrows the gap with GBDTs, particularly on numerical-feature datasets, demonstrating that differentiable sparse column selection is a viable path for tabular deep learning. However, the group-convolution implementation bottleneck makes inference slower than competing DNNs despite having the fewest FLOPs, and the randomization steps slow convergence, suggesting the current sparse-selection strategy is a starting point rather than a final solution. Open questions include whether more efficient differentiable sparse-selection mechanisms exist, how to better handle heterogeneous (mixed numerical/categorical) features where DOFEN underperforms tree-based models, and whether the architecture can serve as a backbone for self-supervised or multi-modal tabular learning as the authors speculate.
