## Problem

Existing SNN-based sound source localization methods rely on computationally expensive Fourier Transform operations for interaural time difference (ITD) encoding, which undermines the energy efficiency that makes spiking architectures attractive for edge deployment. Additionally, most SNN-based SSL solutions focus exclusively on ITD cues while neglecting well-established biological auditory mechanisms—frequency band selectivity and short-term memory—that are critical for robust localization in noisy real-world environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243616/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes raw multi-channel audio (2-channel binaural at 16 kHz for HRTF/Single Words, 4-channel microphone array for SLoClas) segmented into 170 ms frames, and produces a continuous azimuth angle estimate (DoA) with 5° or 10° angular resolution depending on the dataset. The 4-channel SLoClas setup computes ITDs across all 6 microphone pairs, yielding a 6×40×51 spike tensor per frame (microphone pairs × frequency channels × coincidence detector delays) that feeds the back-end network.

## Method

The front-end RF-PLC method replaces FT with 512 Resonate-and-Fire neurons tuned across 0–8800 Hz (CQT-spaced), whose complex-valued dynamics perform resonance-based frequency decomposition during a silent stage; a phase-locking loop then fires a single spike at the first oscillatory peak (real part = 0, imaginary part = max), directly encoding ITD as spike timing without any phase transformation step. Coincidence detection neurons with 51 delay preferences (±25τ, τ = 0.0625 ms) convert these spike times into binary spike patterns. The back-end SNN (LIF neurons, 4 timesteps) incorporates a spike-driven Multi-Auditory Attention (MAA) module: a Frequency-Spatial Joint Attention (FSJA) component generates binary spike attention maps via LIF neurons to mask irrelevant frequency bands and spatial regions without MAC-based broadcasting, and a Short-Term Memory (ST-M) component balances adjacent time-step features through a threshold-adjusted residual connection (α folded into the firing threshold) that avoids all multiply-accumulate operations during inference.

## Results

On the SLoClas dataset (4-channel, 5° resolution), the proposed SNN-based SSL framework achieves a MAE of 0.33°±0.02° and 96.40%±0.3% accuracy with only 1.64M parameters and 4 timesteps, representing a ~50% reduction in MAE over the best prior SNN (Hybrid Coding, 0.60°). On the HRTF and Single Words datasets (2-channel, 10° resolution), the model reaches 99.84% and 99.63% accuracy respectively, surpassing the best prior SNN baselines by 2.46 and 3.33 percentage points. The model also demonstrates strong noise robustness, maintaining high accuracy even at low SNR conditions where competing methods show significant degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 11 baselines across all three datasets. On SLoClas, it reduces MAE from 0.60° (Hybrid Coding, the best prior SNN) to 0.33° and from 0.63° (FN-SSL, the best ANN) to 0.33°, while also achieving the highest accuracy at 96.40% vs. 95.61% and 95.40% respectively. Among SNN baselines, MTPC-CSNN with T=4 (1.23°) and T=8 (1.02°) and MTPC-RSNN (1.48°) are all substantially outperformed, with the gap widening as timestep count increases in those methods. On HRTF, the model beats MNTB (97.38%) by 2.46 points and LSO (74.56%) by 25.28 points; on Single Words it surpasses MSO/LSO (96.30%) by 3.33 points. Notably, the model achieves these gains with comparable parameter counts (~1.64M) and only 4 timesteps, whereas MTPC-RSNN requires 51 timesteps and Hybrid Coding requires 4.37, making the proposed approach both more accurate and more energy-efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SNN-based SSL line initiated by Pan et al. [40] (MTPC-CSNN), which relied on computationally expensive Fourier Transform operations to extract ITD cues from binaural audio. The key methodological departure is the RF-PLC front-end, which replaces FT with Resonate-and-Fire neuron resonance filtering and a phase-locking loop, preserving the fully spike-driven pipeline while eliminating MAC-heavy spectral decomposition. On the back-end, the proposed MAA module departs from existing SNN attention mechanisms (TA [65], TCJA [71], CA/SA [66]) that use full-precision squeeze-and-excitation with MAC-based broadcasting, instead employing binary spike maps and an asynchronous short-term memory structure to avoid additional multiply-accumulate operations. The biological grounding—frequency band selectivity and auditory short-term memory—draws on well-established auditory neuroscience but is integrated into a computational SSL framework for the first time in this form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that energy-efficient, fully spike-driven SSL can match or exceed ANN-based methods (0.33° MAE vs. 0.63° for FN-SSL on SLoClas) while maintaining noise robustness down to low SNR, which is critical for portable edge-based localization in robotics and autonomous systems. However, the energy savings remain theoretical (45nm FLOP-based estimates) with no actual neuromorphic hardware deployment, and validation is limited to three datasets with a small parameter budget (~1.64M). Open questions include whether the RF-PLC encoding generalizes to more complex reverberant or multi-source scenarios, whether the MAA module's biological priors transfer to other auditory tasks, and whether the approach scales to larger microphone arrays or higher angular resolutions without the current CQT-based frequency discretization becoming a bottleneck.
