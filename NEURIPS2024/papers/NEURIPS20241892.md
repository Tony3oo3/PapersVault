## Problem

Existing open-world sparse-view reconstruction models rely on triplane representations with large-scale transformers trained primarily via rendering losses, requiring over 100 GPUs and still struggling to extract high-quality meshes with fine-grained geometric details. NeRF-based pipelines further complicate mesh extraction, often necessitating complex multi-stage 'NeRF-to-mesh' training strategies that still leave artifacts. The paper asks whether incorporating explicit 3D-native structure, supervision, and input guidance can enable a single-stage, efficiently trainable model that directly produces high-quality textured meshes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241892/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MeshFormer takes a sparse set of posed multi-view RGB images (e.g., 6 views) along with corresponding multi-view normal maps, which can be predicted by 2D diffusion models such as Zero123++ with a ControlNet. The normal maps are expressed in camera coordinates and converted to a unified world frame before being processed identically to RGB through projection-aware cross-attention. The output is a high-quality 3D textured mesh extracted from a 512³ SDF volume via dual Marching Cubes, accompanied by a learned 3D normal texture that can be exported for rendering pipelines or used with a traditional vertex-adjustment algorithm to sharpen geometric details.

## Method

MeshFormer replaces the triplane representation with explicit 3D sparse voxels and introduces a two-stage UNet architecture (VoxelFormer at 64³ for coarse occupancy, then SparseVoxelFormer at 256³ for fine features) that interleaves 3D (sparse) convolutions with a deep transformer at the bottleneck, using projection-aware cross-attention to adaptively aggregate multi-view 2D features for each 3D voxel rather than relying on simple pooling. Training is unified in a single stage by combining high-resolution 512³ SDF supervision with differentiable surface rendering (NVDiffRast) on the extracted mesh, where the SDF loss acts as strong geometric regularization that eliminates the need for a separate NeRF initialization or multi-stage pipeline. The model also learns a separate 3D normal texture via an MLP, decoupling normal learning from mesh geometry for training stability, and at inference applies a traditional position-normal alignment algorithm as post-processing to generate sharp geometric details. This 3D-native design enables training on just 8 H100 GPUs in roughly two days while matching or exceeding baselines trained on 100+ GPUs.

## Results

On the GSO dataset, MeshFormer achieves the best F-Score (0.963), Chamfer Distance (0.031), and PSNR (21.47) among all compared methods, with LPIPS of 0.20. On OmniObject3D, it similarly leads in F-Score (0.914), CD (0.043), and PSNR (18.14), with LPIPS of 0.27. Under limited training resources (8×H100 for 48 hours), MeshFormer reaches F-Score 0.960 and CD 0.0317, substantially outperforming MeshLRM (F-Score 0.925, CD 0.0397) which fails to converge under the same budget. The model is trained with only 8 GPUs, whereas most baselines require over 100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeshFormer outperforms all six baselines on F-Score, Chamfer Distance, and PSNR across both GSO and OmniObject3D. The closest competitor is MeshLRM, which leads only on LPIPS (0.19 vs. 0.20 on GSO; 0.26 vs. 0.27 on OmniObject3D), while MeshFormer wins on the remaining three metrics by small margins (e.g., F-Score 0.963 vs. 0.956 on GSO). One-2-3-45++ and InstantMesh trail by roughly 0.02–0.03 in F-Score on GSO, while TripoSR, CRM, and LGM show substantially larger gaps (F-Score 0.776–0.896 on GSO). Under a constrained 8×H100 / 48-hour budget, MeshFormer (F-Score 0.960, CD 0.0317) dramatically outperforms MeshLRM (F-Score 0.925, CD 0.0397), which the authors note fails to converge, highlighting the training-efficiency advantage of the 3D voxel architecture over triplane-based transformers. All baselines except MeshFormer require over 100 GPUs for full training, whereas MeshFormer converges in approximately two days on 8 H100 GPUs.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshFormer departs from the dominant LRM-style paradigm (LRM, TripoSR, InstantMesh, MeshLRM) that pairs triplane representations with large-scale transformers trained primarily via volume rendering. Instead, it replaces triplanes with explicit 3D sparse voxels and introduces a hybrid architecture combining 3D (sparse) convolutions with transformer layers, leveraging projective bias for 3D-2D interaction. It also breaks from the NeRF-to-mesh multi-stage training pipeline by using SDF supervision to enable unified single-stage mesh training, and is the first to introduce multi-view normal maps as input to a feed-forward reconstruction network, building on the Nehab et al. position-normal alignment algorithm for post-hoc geometry enhancement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practical contribution is the dramatic reduction in training cost: 8 H100 GPUs for ~2 days versus 100+ GPUs for competing methods, while matching or exceeding their quality. This makes open-world sparse-view reconstruction accessible to smaller labs and enables faster iteration. The key limitation is reliance on 2D diffusion models (Zero123++) for multi-view RGB and normal generation, where inconsistencies in predicted normals still cause visible quality gaps. Open questions include whether the 3D-voxel + 3D-conv architecture generalizes to even higher resolutions or more complex scenes, and whether the single-stage SDF-supervised training strategy can be extended to dynamic or multi-object settings.
