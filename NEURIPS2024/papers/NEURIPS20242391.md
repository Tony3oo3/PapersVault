## Problem

The optimal sample complexity of agnostic PAC multiclass classification under bandit feedback (where the learner only observes whether a prediction was correct, not the true label) was unknown. Prior work treated the problem as a generic K-armed contextual bandit, yielding O(K/ε²) sample complexity, and it was an open question (raised by Daniely et al., 2011) whether the linear K factor was unavoidable. This paper shows it is not: the price of bandit feedback is only O(1) as ε→0, matching the full-information rate up to poly(K) additive terms.

## Input / Output

The learner interacts with an environment that draws i.i.d. (x,y) pairs from an unknown distribution; it sees x, predicts a label ŷ ∈ {1,…,K}, and receives only the binary indicator 1{ŷ=y} as feedback. The algorithm also requires access to a weighted ERM oracle O_H that, given weighted examples, returns the hypothesis in H minimizing the weighted zero-one loss. The output is a proper learner: a hypothesis ĥ ∈ H with L_D(ĥ) − L_D(h⋆) ≤ ε with probability ≥ 1−δ, achieved in O((poly(K)+1/ε²) log(N/δ)) interaction rounds for a finite class of size N.

## Method

The algorithm operates in two phases. In Phase 1, it collects a small labeled dataset by predicting uniformly at random, then solves a stochastic convex optimization problem—minimizing the log-barrier potential Φ(P) = E[−log(W_{x,y}^γ(P))] over the simplex Δ_N—using a stochastic Frank-Wolfe method with SPIDER variance-reduced gradient estimates. The key insight is that the gradient of Φ at P is proportional to the variance of the importance-weighted reward estimator, so an approximate minimizer P̂ induces O(1) variance (rather than O(K) from uniform exploration) uniformly over all hypotheses. In Phase 2, the learner samples from P̂ (mixed with uniform for exploration), computes importance-weighted loss estimates for all hypotheses, and calls the weighted ERM oracle to return the best hypothesis. A technical novelty is providing an L₁-smoothness analysis of the SPIDER-FW algorithm, since the log-barrier objective is smooth in L₁ but not in the standard L₂ sense.

## Results

The paper establishes a sample complexity of O((K⁹ + 1/ε²) log(N/δ)) for agnostic PAC bandit multiclass classification over finite hypothesis classes of size N (Theorem 1), and Õ((K⁹ + 1/ε²) d_N log(1/δ)) for general classes with Natarajan dimension d_N (Theorem 2). The leading 1/ε² dependence matches the optimal full-information rate, and the price of bandit feedback is O(1) as ε→0, in stark contrast to the Θ(K) multiplicative price in the realizable setting. The algorithm requires O(K⁴√log(N/δ)) calls to a weighted ERM oracle and runs in polynomial time in K, 1/ε, and log(N/δ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against the previously known O(K/ε²) sample complexity bound for bandit multiclass PAC learning, established via reduction to contextual bandits [4] and the Daniely et al. [10] bound of Õ(K d_N/ε²) for general classes. The new result eliminates the multiplicative K/ε² dependence, replacing it with an additive poly(K) + 1/ε² term, so that as ε→0 the rate matches the full-information optimum O((1/ε²) log(N/δ)) up to the additive K⁹ term. This resolves the open question posed by Daniely, Sabato, Ben-David, and Shalev-Shwartz (2011) about whether the Θ(K) price of bandit feedback in the realizable case extends to the agnostic setting — the answer is no, the agnostic price is only O(1). The paper also highlights a separation from the online setting, where the optimal regret rate Θ(√(KT log|H|)) converts via online-to-batch to Õ(K/ε²), which is strictly worse than the new PAC rate for large hypothesis classes. The K⁹ additive term is acknowledged as suboptimal; the authors conjecture the true bound is O(K/ε + 1/ε²).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SPIDER Frank-Wolfe algorithm of Yurtsever et al. [29] and the original Frank-Wolfe method [15], adapting them from the standard L2-smoothness setting to an L1-smoothness regime that is essential for the log-barrier potential used here. It extends the line of bandit multiclass classification work initiated by Kakade, Shalev-Shwartz, and Tewari [18] and Daniely et al. [10], which established O(K/ε²) sample complexity for the agnostic PAC setting, by exploiting the single-label structure of classification to achieve O(1/ε²) dependence. The algorithm also draws on SPIDER variance-reduced gradient estimates [14] and the Natarajan dimension framework [22] for extending finite-class results to general hypothesis classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves an open question from Daniely et al. (2011) by showing that the multiplicative price of bandit feedback in agnostic PAC multiclass classification is O(1) as ε→0, in stark contrast to the Θ(K) price in the realizable case and the online regret setting. This reveals a fundamental separation between the online and PAC formulations of bandit multiclass classification that was not previously understood. The main limitation is the K⁹ additive term in the sample complexity, for which the authors conjecture the optimal bound is O(K/ε + 1/ε²); closing this gap and understanding whether more adaptive exploration strategies can reduce the polynomial-in-K overhead remain open problems.
