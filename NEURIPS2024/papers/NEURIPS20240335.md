## Problem

Existing RAG benchmarks fail to capture the diverse and dynamic nature of real-world QA: they lack coverage of fast-changing facts, long-tail entities, structured KG retrieval, and complex question types such as multi-hop reasoning and false premises. This makes it difficult to assess whether RAG systems can be trusted in production settings where questions span seconds-to-years temporal scales and head-to-tail entity popularity.

## Input / Output

Each question is paired with up to 50 full HTML pages from the Brave Search API and access to 38 mock APIs over a 2.6M-entity knowledge graph, simulating realistic retrieval with deliberate noise (signal-to-noise ratio below 1/30). The system outputs a free-form answer scored on a four-level scale (perfect, acceptable, missing, incorrect) mapped to truthfulness values of 1, 0.5, 0, and −1, deliberately penalizing hallucinated answers more heavily than abstentions to reflect real-world harm.

## Method

CRAG constructs 4,409 QA pairs across five domains (Finance, Sports, Music, Movie, Open) and eight question types (simple, conditional, set, comparison, aggregation, multi-hop, post-processing-heavy, false-premise), with questions generated from both KG templates and annotator-written web queries, then validated through two-phase human auditing. The benchmark defines three progressively harder tasks — retrieval summarization (5 pages), KG+web retrieval (5 pages + mock APIs), and end-to-end RAG (50 pages + mock APIs) — to isolate answer generation, structured querying, and search-ranking capabilities respectively. Evaluation combines a two-step automatic mechanism (exact match followed by dual LLM-as-judge with ChatGPT and Llama 3, achieving 94.7% and 98.9% F1 against human grading) with manual grading for industry SOTA systems. The paper benchmarks straightforward RAG pipelines (up to 44% accuracy, ≤20% truthfulness) and five industry SOTA systems (up to 63% perfect answers, 51% truthfulness), revealing persistent gaps on dynamic, long-tail, and complex questions.

## Results

On CRAG's public test set (1,335 questions), the best LLM-only solution (GPT-4 Turbo) achieves only 33.5% accuracy with 20.0% truthfulness, while the best straightforward RAG solution (GPT-4 Turbo, Task 3 with 50 web pages + KG APIs) reaches 43.6% accuracy but only 13.4% truthfulness due to increased hallucination (30.1%). Among industry SOTA RAG systems, Copilot Pro achieves the highest human-eval truthfulness of 50.6% with 62.6% perfect answers, while the best hallucination-free rate is 63% (Copilot Pro). All systems show substantially lower performance on real-time/fast-changing facts, tail entities, and complex question types (aggregation, multi-hop, post-processing).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4 Turbo is the strongest LLM-only baseline (33.5% accuracy, 20.0% truthfulness) and also the best straightforward RAG system (43.6% accuracy on Task 3), outperforming Llama 3 70B Instruct (40.6%) by ~3 points in accuracy. Llama 2 70B Chat, Mixtral-8x7B, Falcon 40B, and FLAN-T5-XXL all perform substantially worse, with LLM-only accuracy ranging from 9.4% to 22.3% and negative truthfulness scores for most. Among industry SOTA systems, Copilot Pro leads with 50.6% human-eval truthfulness and 62.6% perfect answers, followed by Gemini Advanced (49.3%) and ChatGPT Plus (41.5%); Perplexity.ai trails at 34.9% truthfulness. Notably, adding RAG increases accuracy but also increases hallucination rates (e.g., GPT-4 Turbo goes from 13.5% to 30.1% hallucination), and KG access (Task 2) improves truthfulness over web-only (Task 1) for both models, confirming the value of structured data. The gap between straightforward RAG (≤44% accuracy) and industry SOTA (≤63% perfect) highlights that production systems employ substantially more sophisticated retrieval and ranking than the fixed-window baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRAG builds on the RAG paradigm (Lewis et al. 2021) and the head-to-tail entity popularity framework of Sun et al. (2024) for sampling diverse entities, while extending multi-hop question construction from Talmor & Berant (2018). It departs from prior QA benchmarks (NQ, MS MARCO, TriviaQA, QALD-10) that consider only web or KG retrieval in isolation, and from newer RAG-specific benchmarks (FreshLLM, RGB) that target narrow capabilities, by unifying web search with full HTML pages, mock KG APIs, dynamic facts, and entity popularity into a single evaluation framework. A key methodological departure is the truthfulness scoring system that explicitly penalizes hallucinated answers more heavily than missing ones, replacing the matching-based metrics (ROUGE, F1) that prior benchmarks relied on and that are known to be ineffective for free-form LLM outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRAG reveals a substantial gap to trustworthy QA: even the best industry SOTA RAG systems (Copilot Pro, Gemini Advanced) achieve only 51% truthfulness and 63% perfect answers, while straightforward RAG barely lifts accuracy from 34% to 44% and paradoxically increases hallucination rates. The benchmark identifies concrete open challenges—handling retrieval noise, effectively leveraging structured KG data, and answering questions about dynamic or long-tail facts—that remain unsolved even by production systems. As the basis for KDD Cup 2024 with thousands of participants, and with a commitment to ongoing maintenance and expansion toward multi-lingual, multi-modal, and multi-turn settings, CRAG is positioned to become a standard reference testbed for RAG research.
