## Problem

Graph convolutional networks (GCNs) suffer from oversmoothing, where node features converge to identical vectors exponentially fast with depth, effectively limiting practical GNNs to a few layers. Prior theoretical proofs of inevitable oversmoothing made assumptions on weight matrix singular values that implicitly restricted analysis to the oversmoothing regime, leaving open whether a non-oversmoothing phase exists and how to reach it. This paper asks: under what conditions do GCNs avoid oversmoothing, and can this be characterized analytically?

## Input / Output

The analytical framework takes a graph's adjacency matrix, a row-stochastic shift operator (parameterized by g and d_max), and hyperparameters (weight variance σ_w², bias variance σ_b², activation function) as input, and produces the equilibrium covariance matrix K^eq, a set of propagation depths ξ_i (one per eigendirection of the linearized dynamics), and the critical weight variance σ_w,crit² marking the phase transition. For the classification task, the GCN GP consumes node feature vectors (e.g., 1433-dimensional binary word-presence vectors on Cora) and a small set of labeled training nodes, then outputs predicted class labels for unlabeled test nodes via Bayesian conditioning on the GP posterior.

## Method

The paper exploits the Gaussian process equivalence of infinitely wide GCNs to reduce the feature dynamics to a recursive covariance map K^(l+1) = T[K^(l)]. It then linearizes this map around the zero-distance fixed point (where all node features are identical) to obtain an N²×N² transition matrix H whose eigenvalues determine stability. The key insight is that the oversmoothing phase corresponds to all eigenvalues having |λ_i| < 1 (attractive fixed point), while a non-oversmoothing chaotic phase exists when max|λ_i| > 1, making the uniform-feature state unstable and allowing node features to retain distinct, topology-informative values at arbitrary depth. This transition is reachable by simply increasing the initialization weight variance σ_w² above a critical threshold, and the propagation depth ξ_i = -1/ln(λ_i) diverges at the transition, generalizing the DNN concept of information propagation depth to a set of graph-dependent values. The theory is validated by showing finite-size GCNs (d_l = 200) trained with only a linear readout layer match GP predictions on the Contextual Stochastic Block Model, and by achieving the original Kipf & Welling accuracy on Cora with GCN GPs beyond 100 layers.

## Results

On the Cora citation network (2,708 nodes, 7 classes), GCN GPs initialized near the transition to the non-oversmoothing phase (σ_w² = σ_w,crit² + 0.1, with σ_w,crit² ≈ 1) achieve accuracy that increases monotonically up to L = 100 layers, matching the accuracy reported by Kipf & Welling [19] for their 2-layer GCN. On the Contextual Stochastic Block Model (CSBM), GCN GPs in the chaotic (non-oversmoothing) phase remain informative beyond 1,000 layers, while oversmoothing-phase GCNs degrade to random-chance performance within a few layers. For finite-size GCNs (d_l = 200) with only the readout layer trained, performance peaks at L ≈ 15 layers near the critical point, confirming the GP predictions. The equilibrium feature distance in the non-oversmoothing phase encodes the underlying community structure of the graph, enabling node classification purely from topology at extreme depths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the original 2-layer GCN of Kipf & Welling [19], whose reported accuracy on Cora is shown as a grey dashed reference line in Figure 4b. The paper's GCN GP, initialized at σ_w² = σ_w,crit² + 0.1 (just past the non-oversmoothing transition), matches this accuracy by L ≈ 100 layers and continues to improve in generalization error beyond that depth. In the oversmoothing phase (σ_w² = σ_w,crit² − 1), both the GCN GP and finite-size GCNs degrade rapidly, with generalization error approaching random chance (MSE → 1) within a handful of layers. The paper also compares its theoretical predictions (GCN GP prior) against finite-size GCN simulations (d_l = 200, 50 realizations) on CSBM instances, finding good quantitative agreement in both the location of the phase transition and the layer-dependent feature distances, validating the infinite-width approximation at moderate feature dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the regular/chaotic phase and information propagation depth framework from Schoenholz et al. [37] (developed for fully-connected DNNs) to graph convolutional networks, where the graph structure couples all node pairs and turns the 1D DNN problem into a multi-dimensional eigenvalue problem. It builds on the GP equivalence of GCNs in the infinite-width limit established by Ng et al. [28] and Niu et al. [29], and studies the standard GCN architecture of Kipf & Welling [19]. Crucially, it departs from prior theoretical results [2, 45] that proved oversmoothing is inevitable in GCNs by showing those proofs impose boundedness constraints on weight matrix norms that explicitly exclude the chaotic, non-oversmoothing regime identified here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that oversmoothing is not fundamental but rather a consequence of typical initialization choices challenges a widely-held assumption in the GNN community and provides a trivially simple fix (increase weight variance at initialization) that enables GCNs with 1000+ layers to remain informative. Limitations include the infinite-width GP assumption (validated only at d_l=200), the O(N^6) cost of the eigenvalue analysis for large graphs, and preliminary finite-size experiments where only the readout layer is trained. Open questions include finite-width corrections, extension to other architectures (GATs, message-passing variants), and whether the non-oversmoothing phase persists under full end-to-end training on larger, more diverse real-world benchmarks.
