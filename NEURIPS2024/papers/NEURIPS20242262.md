## Problem

Previous regret bounds for generalized linear bandits (GLBs) either assumed subgaussian or bounded reward distributions, or paid an exponential price in the leading term for the magnitude of the unknown parameter. No self-concordance characterization existed for NEFs with subexponential tails, and consequently no regret bound was available for GLBs with subexponential rewards (e.g., Poisson, exponential, gamma bandits).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20242262/cropped_page40_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes arm features x ∈ R^d, a base distribution Q from a single-parameter NEF with subexponential tails (characterized by tail parameters c₁, C₁, c₂, C₂), a parameter space Θ, and past observations (X_s, Y_s) to produce an arm selection X_t and a confidence set C_t per round. The key theoretical output is a non-constant stretch function Γ(u) that grows polynomially (quadratically for subexponential, linearly for subgaussian) in the inverse distance to the boundary of the natural parameter space, and a second-order regret bound whose leading term scales with the variance of the optimal arm's reward distribution rather than the parameter magnitude.

## Method

The core contribution proves that single-parameter NEFs with subexponential base distributions are self-concordant by bounding the ratio |μ''(u)|/μ'(u) — the third-to-second central moment ratio of the tilted distribution Q_u. The proof splits the third moment integral at an optimized threshold B, controls the tail of Q_u via the subexponential property of Q (yielding quadratic growth in 1/(c₁−u)), and lower-bounds the variance using a fixed-mass interval argument. For subgaussian bases, a parallel argument with Gaussian tail bounds yields linear growth in |u|, which is shown tight via a constructed discrete distribution. In the bandit application, the self-concordance property bounds the CGF of tilted distributions (Lemma 5), enabling confidence set construction for the OFU-GLB algorithm and a second-order regret bound Õ(d√(μ'(x*⊤θ*)T) + dκ(θ*)) that is the first for subexponential GLBs and matches the logistic bandit lower bound.

## Results

The paper establishes that single-parameter NEFs with subexponential base distributions are self-concordant with a stretch factor growing at most quadratically in the inverse distance to the boundary of the natural parameter space (Theorem 7). For subgaussian base distributions, the stretch factor grows linearly, Γ_Q(u) = O(|u|), and this rate is shown to be tight (Theorems 9–10). The main application yields a regret bound for the OFU-GLB algorithm: Regret(T) = Õ(d√(μ̇(x⋆ᵀθ⋆)·T) + d·κ(θ⋆)), where the leading term scales with the variance of the optimal arm's reward distribution and is free of any exponential dependence on the problem parameter — the first such bound for GLBs with subexponential rewards (Poisson, exponential, gamma).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines. In terms of prior theoretical results, the paper improves upon several lines of work. Earlier GLB analyses [Fil+10; LLZ17; SPL23] used a naive first-order Taylor approximation, incurring an exponential dependence on the parameter norm κ in the leading regret term. [Fau+20] first exploited self-concordance for logistic bandits to remove this exponential dependence, and [AFC21] obtained matching second-order bounds for logistic bandits. [Jan+24] extended self-concordant analysis to general GLBs but required the MGF to be defined over all of ℝ, excluding distributions with exponential or heavier tails. [Rus+21] also employed self-concordance in GLBs but assumed bounded rewards and their bounds still scale with κ in the leading term. The present work is the first to handle subexponential (not just subgaussian) base distributions, broadening the applicable class to include Poisson, exponential, and gamma bandits, while maintaining a leading term free of exponential parameter dependence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-concordance-based analysis of generalized linear bandits initiated by Faury et al. (ICML 2020) for logistic bandits and generalized by Janz et al. (AISTATS 2024) to GLBs with subgaussian rewards. The key departure is proving that self-concordance holds for the broader class of subexponential NEFs (Poisson, exponential, gamma, negative binomial), where prior works either assumed bounded rewards or required the MGF to be defined on all of ℝ. The stretch function analysis builds on the self-concordant framework of Bach (2010) and Nesterov-Nemirovski, but introduces a non-constant stretch factor that grows polynomially near the boundary of the natural parameter space, which is necessary for distributions like the exponential where no constant stretch factor exists.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that all subexponential NEFs are self-concordant is 'almost free' in the sense that it requires no additional structural assumptions beyond light tails, immediately yielding second-order regret bounds for a wide class of bandit problems (Poisson, exponential, gamma) that were previously unaddressed. The tightness result (Theorem 10) showing linear growth of the stretch factor is necessary for subgaussian NEFs rules out the hope of a uniform constant, clarifying the fundamental limits of self-concordant analysis. Open questions include whether the quadratic growth bound for subexponential NEFs can be improved to match the linear rate seen in specific examples like the Laplace distribution, and whether these techniques extend to multi-parameter exponential families beyond the single-parameter NEF setting.
