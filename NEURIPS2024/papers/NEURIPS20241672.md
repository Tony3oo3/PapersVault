## Problem

Existing offline safe RL algorithms either require all-policy concentrability (impractical because safe data collection cannot cover all dangerous state-action pairs) or fail to guarantee that the learned policy is both safe and better than the behavior policy. No prior work simultaneously achieves single-policy ℓ₂ concentrability, an optimal 1/√N statistical rate, and a provable safe robust policy improvement (SRPI) guarantee under function approximation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite offline dataset of N i.i.d. tuples (s, a, r, c, s′) drawn from a behavior policy's discounted visitation distribution μ, together with a reference policy π_ref (which can be the behavior policy itself or extracted via behavior cloning). It outputs a safe policy π̄ as a uniform mixture over K iterations, along with learned reward and cost Q-function approximations. The key structural constraint is that the importance-weight function class W must contain the ratio w^{π_ref} = d^{π_ref}/μ, and all weight functions in W must be bounded in ℓ₂(μ) norm by C_{ℓ₂}^*.

## Method

WSAC formulates offline safe RL as a two-player Stackelberg game: the actor maximizes an aggression-limited objective f_r(s,π) − λ·{f_c(s,π) − f_c(s,π_ref)}₊, which penalizes cost only when it exceeds the reference policy's cost, while two adversarially trained critics minimize importance-weighted Bellman errors with pessimistic (reward) and optimistic (cost) regularization. The critical design choice is the rectified penalty [·]₊ rather than a primal-dual Lagrangian, which eliminates the need for dual-variable tuning and thus avoids the all-policy concentrability requirement that plagues primal-dual approaches. The critics are trained with a weighted average Bellman error max_{w∈W}|E_μ[w(s,a)(f − T^π f)(s,a)]|, which is an unbiased estimator that sidesteps the Bellman completeness assumption needed by squared-error methods. A no-regret policy optimization oracle (e.g., mirror descent or natural policy gradient) updates the actor, and the final policy is the uniform average over all K iterates, yielding the SRPI guarantee with optimal 1/√N rate under only single-policy ℓ₂ concentrability.

## Results

On four continuous-control safe offline RL benchmarks (BallCircle, CarCircle, PointButton, PointPush), WSAC achieves the lowest average normalized cost (0.51) while maintaining a competitive average normalized reward (0.41). Critically, WSAC is the only method that keeps all four environments safe (normalized cost < 1); in PointButton, where most baselines exceed the cost threshold (e.g., CPQ at 3.10, COptiDICE at 1.92), WSAC achieves a cost of 0.67. WSAC attains the best reward in BallCircle (0.75) and CarCircle (0.68), and the best cost in every environment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WSAC outperforms all five baselines on normalized cost in every environment, with the largest margins in PointButton (0.67 vs. CPQ's 3.10 and COptiDICE's 1.92) and CarCircle (0.59 vs. BEARL's 1.87). On reward, WSAC is competitive but not uniformly best: BCQL achieves higher reward on CarCircle (0.79 vs. 0.68) and BEARL on CarCircle (0.84 vs. 0.68), while CPQ leads on PointButton reward (0.43 vs. 0.13). However, these higher-reward baselines are all unsafe in at least one environment (cost > 1), whereas WSAC remains safe across all four. COptiDICE, the strongest safe baseline, is outperformed by WSAC on both reward and cost in BallCircle (0.75/0.27 vs. 0.71/1.13) and CarCircle (0.68/0.59 vs. 0.49/1.52). In the extended evaluation under varying cost limits (Table 4), WSAC maintains the lowest average cost (0.56) while matching or exceeding the best safe baseline's reward, confirming robustness across difficulty levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

WSAC extends the adversarial actor-critic framework of Cheng et al. (2022) and the importance-weighted Bellman error of Zhu et al. (2023) from unconstrained offline RL to the safe (constrained) setting, using the performance difference lemma of Kakade & Langford (2002) as a core analytical tool. The key departure is replacing the primal-dual formulation (used by Le et al. 2019 and Hong et al. 2024, which provably requires all-policy concentrability) with a rectified penalty-based aggression-limited objective that only needs single-policy ℓ2 concentrability. This is framed as a two-player Stackelberg game where the actor optimizes against two adversarially trained critics, achieving the first provable Safe Robust Policy Improvement (SRPI) guarantee in offline safe RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SRPI guarantee is practically critical: it ensures that deploying a learned policy will never be worse than the existing (safe) behavior policy in both reward and cost, which is essential for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving where the expert-collected dataset is the only safe reference. The relaxation from all-policy to single-policy ℓ2 concentrability removes an assumption that is essentially impossible to satisfy in safe RL (it would require data from every dangerous state-action pair). Open questions include extending to multiple coupled constraints, multi-agent settings, and combining with online exploration under safety guarantees, as the authors note. The empirical validation, while positive, is limited to four 2D continuous-control environments from a single benchmark, leaving open whether the advantages persist in higher-dimensional or more complex safe RL tasks.
