## Problem

Sliced Wasserstein (SW) and Generalized Sliced Wasserstein (GSW) distances are only defined between distributions supported on a single homogeneous domain, making them inapplicable to heterogeneous joint distributions whose marginals live on different metric spaces (e.g., points in ℝ³ paired with normals on S²). Applying SW or GSW directly to the joint product space fails because the homogeneous slicing operators (Radon Transform, Generalized Radon Transform) cannot separately capture within-marginal and between-marginal structure, yielding a meaningless comparison.

## Input / Output

The method takes two joint distributions whose marginals are supported on potentially different metric spaces — for 3D shapes, 10,000 or 2,048 point clouds in ℝ³ paired with corresponding unit surface normals on S²; for dataset comparison, Euclidean feature vectors paired with labels embedded in a Lorentz (hyperbolic) model. The output is a scalar H2SW distance estimated via Monte Carlo averaging over L random projection tuples (θ₁, θ₂, ψ), where θ₁, θ₂ are domain-specific projection directions and ψ is a weight vector on the unit sphere. The distance is computed in O(Ln log n) time and O(Ln) memory, matching the scalability of standard SW.

## Method

The core contribution is the Hierarchical Hybrid Radon Transform (HHRT), a two-level slicing operator that first applies a Partial Generalized Radon Transform (PGRT) with a domain-specific defining function to each marginal independently (e.g., linear projection for ℝ³, circular projection for S², Busemann function for hyperbolic space), then applies a Partial Radon Transform on the jointly transformed arguments with random weights ψ ∈ 𝕊 to aggregate inter-marginal information. The H2SW distance averages the one-dimensional Wasserstein distance over random tuples (θ₁, θ₂, ψ) drawn from the product of the respective projection spaces and the unit sphere. The paper proves HHRT is injective (hence H2SW is a valid metric), establishes the same O(n^{-1/2}) sample complexity as SW with no curse of dimensionality, and shows H2SW is bounded above by the sum of per-marginal GSW distances. In the Hadamard manifold setting, H2SW treats the Busemann projection weights as a random variable over the sphere rather than a fixed hyperparameter, making it a true metric where the prior Cartan-Hadamard Sliced-Wasserstein is only a pseudo-metric.

## Results

On 3D mesh deformation (sphere→Armadillo, L=100 projections), H2SW achieves a final joint Wasserstein distance of 1.086 at step 5000, outperforming SW (1.171) and GSW (3.226). On the deep 3D mesh autoencoder task (ShapeNet, L=1000, epoch 2000), H2SW yields the lowest reconstruction error of 129.87 (×100) versus GSW (130.76) and SW (130.93). On dataset comparison over the product of Hadamard manifolds (L=2000), H2SW achieves a relative error of 3.998 to the joint Wasserstein ground truth, beating CHSW (4.059) and SW (4.449).

## Baselines & Comparisons

H2SW consistently outperforms all three baselines across all three experimental tasks. Against SW (Bonneel et al., 2015), H2SW achieves lower joint Wasserstein distance in 3D mesh deformation (1.086 vs. 1.171 at step 5000, L=100) and lower autoencoder reconstruction error (129.87 vs. 130.93 at epoch 2000, L=1000). Against GSW (Kolouri et al., 2019), the advantage is most pronounced in deformation: H2SW reaches 1.086 vs. GSW's 3.226 at step 5000 (L=100), a roughly 3× improvement, because GSW's single homogeneous defining function cannot separately capture Euclidean point and spherical normal structure. Against CHSW (Bonet et al., 2024) on the product-of-Hadamard-manifolds dataset comparison task, H2SW achieves a relative error of 3.998 vs. CHSW's 4.059 (L=2000), with the gap widening at lower projection counts (e.g., 4.618 vs. 4.198 at L=100). The gains are largest when the number of projections L is small, consistent with H2SW's hierarchical structure providing more informative slices per projection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sliced Wasserstein (SW) and generalized sliced Wasserstein (GSW) framework, which previously required all data to live on a single homogeneous domain, to the setting of heterogeneous joint distributions where marginals are supported on different domains (e.g., R³ × S² for 3D point clouds with normals). The key conceptual move is the Hierarchical Hybrid Radon Transform (HHRT), which composes domain-specific Partial Generalized Radon Transforms (PGRTs) on each marginal with a final Partial Radon Transform on the joint projected arguments, thereby capturing both within-marginal and between-marginal structure. This contrasts with the Cartan-Hadamard Sliced-Wasserstein (CHSW) of Bonet et al. [9], which handles product manifolds but uses a fixed weighted combination of Busemann projections rather than a distributed one, and with GSW [26], which applies a single defining function uniformly across all arguments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

H2SW provides the first scalable (O(n log n) time) sliced Wasserstein metric specifically designed for heterogeneous joint distributions, a setting that arises naturally in 3D shape processing, domain adaptation, and dataset comparison. The theoretical guarantees (valid metric, no curse of dimensionality, sample complexity matching SW) make it a drop-in replacement for joint Wasserstein in optimization-based pipelines where the full OT cost is prohibitive. Limitations include the injectivity proof only covering linear, circular, and odd-degree polynomial defining functions, and the sample complexity rate being slightly worse than the best-known SW bounds due to the circular defining functions. Open questions include extending to non-manifold marginal domains (images, functions), non-uniform slicing distributions, and more complex hierarchical structures beyond two marginals.
