## Problem

Instruction-guided 3D scene editing via 2D diffusion distillation suffers from multi-view inconsistency because a single text instruction admits a large feasible output space (FOS) of plausible edits, causing the scene representation to fail to converge on consistent edited views. Prior methods address this with inconsistency-robust distillation losses or additional 3D-consistency components, but these add-ons still fail when the FOS is large, particularly for geometry-changing edits. The paper identifies the large FOS as the root cause and proposes controlling it directly through task decomposition rather than tolerating or filtering inconsistency after the fact.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D scene in either NeRF or 3DGS representation along with a free-form text editing instruction (e.g., 'Give the person a hat') and produces an edited 3DGS scene. A distinctive output feature is that every intermediate subtask yields a valid, renderable scene at a specific 'aggressivity' level, allowing users to preview, compare, and select partial edits (e.g., 40% completion) without re-running the pipeline. The diffusion model (Instruct-Pix2Pix) operates on rendered multi-view images at SDEdit denoising timesteps 450–850 with HiFA annealing, and the 3DGS backbone (Splatfacto) accepts full-image supervision rather than per-ray supervision, simplifying the iterative dataset update loop.

## Method

ProEdit decomposes a full editing task into a sequence of subtasks by linearly interpolating the instruction's text embedding with the empty-prompt embedding at ratios r ∈ [0,1], so each subtask S(s, r) represents a partial completion of the edit. A difficulty-aware scheduler recursively bisects the [0,1] interval using an LPIPS-based difficulty metric computed between 2D diffusion outputs at different interpolation ratios, ensuring each subtask's FOS stays below a threshold and all subtasks have comparable difficulty. Each subtask is then solved via iterative dataset updates (generate edited views with the diffusion model, train 3DGS on them), with an adaptive Gaussian creation strategy that controls opacity culling and gradient-based splitting/duplication thresholds to prevent overfitting to residual multi-view inconsistencies. A dual-GPU pipeline decouples the slow diffusion inference (~2 s/generation) from the fast 3DGS training (<0.02 s/iteration), and subtask completion is detected when the running mean training loss plateaus.

## Results

On the IN2N and ScanNet++ datasets, ProEdit achieves a User Study of Overall Quality (USO) score of 87.96, substantially outperforming IN2N (51.35) and ConsistDreamer (68.65). ProEdit also leads on 3D consistency (US3D: 80.23 vs. 75.23 for ConsistDreamer) and GPT evaluation score (81.00 vs. 74.40), while achieving comparable CLIP-based metrics (CTIDS 0.0844, CDC 0.3833) at only 1/3 of ConsistDreamer's running time (1–4 h vs. 12–24 h). The ablation removing subtask decomposition (ND variant) drops USO from 92.70 to 68.46 and US3D from 90.48 to 61.72, confirming that progressive decomposition is the key driver of quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProEdit decisively outperforms IN2N [5] across every metric (USO 87.96 vs. 51.35; US3D 80.23 vs. 65.45; GPT 81.00 vs. 45.32), with the gap attributed to IN2N's inability to handle large feasible output spaces without subtask decomposition. Against the strongest baseline ConsistDreamer [9], ProEdit wins on all three human/LLM-judged quality metrics (USO +19.3, US3D +5.0, GPT +6.6) while trailing slightly on CLIP-based text-image alignment (CTIDS 0.0844 vs. 0.0912; CDC 0.3833 vs. 0.3912), yet runs in roughly one-third of the wall-clock time. The remaining baselines—ViCA-NeRF [41], CSD [6], PDS [7], EN2N [37], DreamEditor [46], and Instruct-GS2GS [45]—are compared primarily through qualitative visualizations sourced from their respective papers, since they do not share a common evaluation protocol or publicly available code; ProEdit is reported to produce clearer textures, more precise geometry, and better instruction fidelity in these side-by-side comparisons (Figs. 3–5). Notably, ProEdit matches or exceeds ConsistDreamer's visual quality on ScanNet++ scenes despite using no 3D-consistency-enforcing components, validating the paper's core claim that controlling FOS via subtask decomposition is more effective than explicit consistency add-ons.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProEdit builds on the iterative dataset update paradigm from Instruct-NeRF2NeRF (IN2N) and the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, but departs fundamentally from prior approaches to multi-view inconsistency. While methods like CSD, PDS, and ConsistDreamer address inconsistency by adding robustness-tolerant distillation losses or explicit 3D consistency-enforcing components, ProEdit identifies the root cause as the diffusion model's large feasible output space (FOS) and instead reduces it through progressive subtask decomposition with text-embedding interpolation. This reframes the problem from 'tolerate or select from inconsistency' to 'prevent inconsistency at the source by constraining the solution space at each step,' eliminating the need for the expensive add-ons that prior work relies on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple progressive decomposition strategy can match or exceed sophisticated multi-component methods (e.g., ConsistDreamer with three add-ons) at roughly one-third the running time, suggesting that the field may have been over-engineering solutions to a problem that is fundamentally about task difficulty. The novel aggressivity controllability—letting users preview and select intermediate editing stages—opens a practical dimension absent from prior work. However, the method remains constrained by the underlying 2D diffusion model's capabilities, inherits 3DGS limitations for unbounded outdoor scenes, and can still produce multi-face artifacts, leaving open questions about whether semantic subtask construction or video-generation-based transitions could push the framework further toward 4D scene creation.
