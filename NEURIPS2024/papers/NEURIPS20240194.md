## Problem

Representation-based boundary detection methods rely on the assumption that consecutive distances between representations are larger at inter-segment points than at intra-segment points. This assumption breaks down for gradual class transitions (e.g., stair up vs. stair down) where only the direction of motion differs, making distance-based metrics unable to distinguish boundaries. The paper asks whether a geometric property of the representation trajectory—curvature—can serve as a more robust change metric that captures both gradual and abrupt transitions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a univariate or multivariate time series as a sequence of d-dimensional points indexed by timestamps, where each point is represented by a window of 2I consecutive points (I ≈ sampling rate) centered at that timestamp. A self-supervised TCN encoder maps each window to a d′-dimensional representation (d′=8 for low-dimensional sensor data, d′=32 for high-dimensional features). The output is a per-timestamp change metric score in [0,1] (higher = more likely a boundary), from which boundary timestamps are extracted by thresholding; the threshold is set either by matching the estimated number of boundaries (total timestamps / mean segment length) or by maximizing F1 on a validation set.

## Method

RECURVE reinterprets the sequence of learned representations as a trajectory in representation space and computes the curvature at each timestamp as the turning angle between two difference vectors (spanning w timestamps before and after) divided by their combined length. The key geometric insight is that intra-segment points are confined within a class-specific hypersphere of small radius R_intra, forcing their trajectory to make frequent sharp turns (high curvature), whereas inter-segment points traverse between two class balls enclosed by a larger hypersphere of radius R_inter, producing straighter paths (low curvature). This is formalized via an equilateral random-walk assumption, under which the mean curvature is proven to be a strictly decreasing function of the confining radius, guaranteeing K_intra > K_inter. The final change metric is 1 minus the min-max-normalized curvature, smoothed by a central moving average, and is agnostic to the underlying representation learning method (TPC or TNC with InfoNCE loss). The hyperparameter w is set to 5% of the mean segment length to balance noise sensitivity against loss of discriminative power.

## Results

RECURVE achieves the highest AUC across all four datasets at p=10, with RECURVE+TPC scoring 0.901 (WISDM), 0.913 (HAPT), 0.954 (mHealth), and 0.723 (50salads), while RECURVE+TNC reaches 0.980 on mHealth. The headline improvement is up to 12.7% AUC over the second-best method on mHealth (p=20). On the LOC measure (lower is better), RECURVE+TPC achieves 34.28 on HAPT and 93.76 on 50salads, substantially outperforming TS-CP² (404.2 and 129.4) and RuLSIF (156.0 and 189.2). The method is particularly effective for gradual changes, showing a 32% AUC improvement over TS-CP² on HAPT for gradual transitions versus 21% for abrupt ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECURVE+TPC outperforms TS-CP² (the closest distance-based representation method) on all four datasets, with the largest gap on mHealth (0.954 vs. 0.561 AUC) where gradual class transitions cause the consecutive-distance metric to fail. Against KL-CPD, RECURVE+TPC wins on all datasets (e.g., 0.913 vs. 0.873 on HAPT, 0.954 vs. 0.849 on mHealth), while RECURVE+TNC achieves the single best score of 0.980 on mHealth. RuLSIF is consistently the weakest baseline, scoring below 0.61 on WISDM and 50salads. The curvature-based metric is notably insensitive to the degree of change: on HAPT, RECURVE+TPC improves AUC by 32% for gradual changes versus 21% for abrupt changes relative to TS-CP², confirming that curvature captures subtle transitions that distance-based methods miss. On 50salads (high-dimensional video features), RECURVE+TPC still leads (0.723) but RECURVE+TNC underperforms (0.595), suggesting representation learning method choice matters more for high-dimensional inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

RECURVE builds directly on the representation-based boundary detection pipeline of TS-CP² (Deldari et al., WWW 2021), which trains a temporal convolutional network via contrastive predictive coding (TPC) and then flags boundaries using consecutive cosine-distance differences. The key departure is replacing this distance-based change metric with the curvature of the representation trajectory, a geometric quantity measuring the rate of direction change rather than magnitude of displacement. The theoretical justification draws on random-walk-in-confinement results from Diao et al. (2013), which had not previously been applied to time-series segmentation. The method is also compatible with alternative representation learners such as TNC (Tonekaboni et al., ICLR 2021), positioning it as a plug-in metric rather than a full pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical contribution is that curvature is insensitive to the degree of inter-class change, whereas distance-based metrics degrade sharply for gradual transitions (e.g., stair-up vs. stair-down), a common failure mode in real sensor data. This makes RECURVE a more robust drop-in replacement for the change-metric stage of any representation-based boundary detector. A noted limitation is that very short segments (shorter than the hyperparameter w) can produce false positives, and the authors flag segment-length variance as an open question. The modularity of the approach—curvature can be computed on top of any self-supervised representation—suggests it could be adopted broadly across time-series segmentation applications without retraining the backbone.
