## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for de novo peptide sequencing struggle with two data biases: post-translational modifications (PTMs) are far less frequent than canonical amino acids in training data, leading to poor identification of modified residues, and noise/missing peaks in mass spectra make many peptide-spectrum match (PSM) labels unreliable. No prior method explicitly accounts for these biases during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240057/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a variable-length set of MS2 peaks (each a 2-tuple of m/z and intensity) plus a precursor descriptor (total mass and integer charge state 1–10), and autoregressively generates a variable-length peptide sequence over a vocabulary of 20 canonical amino acids, 3 PTM types (oxidation of Met, deamidation of Asn/Gln), and a [stop] token. Peak embeddings are constructed via sinusoidal positional encoding on m/z and a linear projection on intensity, following the Casanovo convention.

## Method

AdaNovo builds on a Transformer encoder-decoder architecture (9 layers, 512 FFN dim) but introduces a second peptide decoder that predicts only p(y_j | y_<j) without spectrum conditioning, enabling computation of the conditional mutual information I_j = log[p(y_j|x,z,y_<j) / p(y_j|y_<j)] for each amino acid position. This CMI yields two adaptive re-weighting signals: an amino acid-level weight (Z-score normalized within each peptide) that up-weights positions where the spectrum carries strong evidence (e.g., PTM mass shifts), and a PSM-level weight (Z-score normalized across the mini-batch) that down-weights poorly matched spectrum-peptide pairs caused by noise or missing peaks. The final training loss multiplies both weights onto each amino acid's cross-entropy term, while the second decoder is trained with standard cross-entropy to maintain a reliable p(y_j|y_<j) baseline. At inference, only the first decoder is used with beam search, so the added decoder incurs no extra inference cost.

## Results

On the 9-species benchmark, AdaNovo achieves an average peptide-level precision of 0.499, surpassing Casanovo (rep.) at 0.459, PointNovo at 0.439, and DeepNovo at 0.376. At the amino acid level, AdaNovo matches or exceeds the best baselines on most species (e.g., 0.612 on Mouse vs. 0.626 for PointNovo; 0.753 on Yeast vs. 0.779 for PointNovo). Most notably, AdaNovo improves PTM identification precision by 3.6%–9.4% over all competitors, with the largest gains on Human (0.483 vs. 0.300 for Casanovo) and Yeast (0.665 vs. 0.552 for Casanovo).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaNovo outperforms all three baselines on peptide-level precision across 8 of 9 species, with the largest margin over DeepNovo (0.499 vs. 0.376 average, +12.3 pp) and a consistent 4–10 pp advantage over PointNovo and Casanovo (rep.). Against PointNovo, the closest competitor, AdaNovo gains +6.0 pp on average peptide-level precision (0.499 vs. 0.439), though PointNovo retains a slight edge on amino acid-level precision for a few species (e.g., Yeast 0.779 vs. AdaNovo's reported values). Against Casanovo (rep.), AdaNovo improves peptide-level precision by +4.0 pp on average and dramatically outperforms it on PTM identification (e.g., 0.483 vs. 0.300 on Human, 0.665 vs. 0.552 on Yeast). The ablation in Table 2 confirms both the amino acid-level CMI weighting (critical for PTM precision: 0.483 → 0.314 when removed) and the PSM-level MI weighting (critical for noise robustness: peptide precision drops from 0.397 to 0.336 on noisy Clam bacteria data) are necessary for AdaNovo's full performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaNovo builds directly on Casanovo (Yilmaz et al., ICML 2022), adopting its Transformer encoder-decoder architecture and peak embedding scheme, but introduces a second decoder (Peptide Decoder #2) that predicts amino acids conditioned only on the preceding sequence rather than the spectrum. This dual-decoder design enables computation of conditional mutual information (CMI) between each amino acid and the mass spectrum, which is then used to adaptively re-weight the training loss. Conceptually, the approach draws on long-tailed classification ideas (emphasizing rare classes) but departs from them by grounding the re-weighting in domain-specific information-theoretic reasoning: PTM mass shifts are only observable in spectra, so the CMI naturally up-weights PTM positions without requiring explicit frequency statistics that would not transfer across species.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.6%–9.4% gain in PTM identification precision addresses a practical bottleneck in proteomics, where PTMs are central to understanding protein function and disease mechanisms yet are systematically under-identified due to their rarity in training data. The clean separation of concerns—AA-level CMI for PTM identification and PSM-level MI for noise robustness—provides a principled template for handling multiple data biases simultaneously in sequence prediction tasks. A key open question is generalization to previously unseen PTMs, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation; the CMI mechanism relies on the model having learned the spectral signature of a modification, so truly novel PTMs remain out of scope.
