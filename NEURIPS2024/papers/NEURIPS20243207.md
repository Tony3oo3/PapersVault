## Problem

Existing open-world 3D representation learning methods rely on CAD-rendered images for multimodal alignment, but these renders lack realistic texture variation and environmental effects, limiting alignment robustness. Additionally, training a separate 3D encoder from scratch to bridge the modality gap is hampered by the massive volume discrepancy between 3D datasets (<1M objects) and the 2D image-text corpora (~5B images) used to pre-train VLMs like CLIP, hindering effective knowledge transfer.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (10,000 points for ModelNet40/Objaverse-LVIS, 2,048 for ScanObjectNN, 4,096 for OmniObject3D) and projects it into 10 multi-view depth maps at 224×224×3 along the x-axis (30°–330°). At inference, these depth maps are encoded by both a pre-trained and a fine-tuned CLIP ViT encoder, and the resulting multi-view embeddings are matched against 80 depth-specific text prompts per category (augmented with keywords like 'depth map' and 'silhouette of [CLASS]') to produce aggregated classification logits. For retrieval, image queries are also encoded and combined with text queries via embedding averaging.

## Method

OpenDlign generates depth-aligned images by feeding contour-aware projected depth maps (via a Quantize–Densify–Smooth–Squeeze pipeline using bilateral filtering for edge preservation) into ControlNet v1.1 with the CAD model's semantic metadata as text prompt, producing 524,700 images with diverse textures while preserving geometric consistency. For multimodal alignment, only 6M parameters are fine-tuned: the attention layers of a single transformer block residually connected to the last layer of the frozen CLIP image encoder, trained with a composite contrastive + feature-distance loss between depth map embeddings and depth-aligned image embeddings. At zero-shot inference, a dual-encoder strategy splits the 10 views between the pre-trained and fine-tuned encoders to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, and multi-view logits are summed for final prediction. Depth-specific text prompts (selected via CLIP Interrogator) and the logit aggregation strategy further boost zero-shot accuracy without additional training.

## Results

On zero-shot 3D classification, OpenDlign (ViT-H-14) achieves 82.6% Top-1 on ModelNet40, 59.5% on ScanObjectNN, and 31.3% on OmniObject3D, surpassing the previous best (TAMM-SparseConv) by 8.0%, 1.6%, and 16.4% respectively. On the long-tail Objaverse-LVIS benchmark, OpenDlign reaches 35.0% Top-1, outperforming the prior SOTA by 21.3%. For zero-shot 3D object detection on ScanNet V2, OpenDlign achieves 50.72% AP25 and 37.97% AP50, exceeding PointCLIP V2 by over 31.75% and 26.44% respectively. In few-shot classification, OpenDlign leads all baselines from 1-shot to 16-shot, with an 11.8% advantage over TAMM on OmniObject3D at 8-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenDlign outperforms all depth-based baselines by large margins: PointCLIP (19.3% on ModelNet40) and PointCLIP V2 (63.6%) are surpassed by 63.3% and 19.0% respectively, while CLIP2Point (49.5%) trails by 33.1%. Among point-based methods, OpenDlign beats ULIP-PointBERT (60.4%) by 22.2% on ModelNet40 and OpenShape-SparseConv (72.9%) by 9.7%. The closest competitor is TAMM-SparseConv (74.6%), which OpenDlign exceeds by 8.0% on ModelNet40 and 1.6% on ScanObjectNN; on OmniObject3D, the gap widens dramatically to 16.4% over TAMM-PointBERT (14.9%). Notably, even the lightest OpenDlign variant (ViT-B-32, 68.4%) already surpasses ULIP and approaches OpenShape on ModelNet40, while matching or exceeding all baselines on OmniObject3D. For 3D object detection, OpenDlign (50.72% AP25) substantially outperforms PointCLIP V2 (18.97%) and is comparable to the purpose-built CoDA method (19.32% mean AP in the OV-3DET protocol), despite not being specifically designed for detection. The paper further shows that replacing CAD-rendered images with depth-aligned images in OpenShape and TAMM consistently improves their performance (e.g., TAMM-PointBERT gains 4.8% on ScanObjectNN), validating the broader utility of the proposed depth-aligned image strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenDlign sits at the intersection of depth-based 3D open-world methods (PointCLIP, PointCLIP V2, CLIP2Point) and point-based methods (OpenShape, TAMM, ULIP), but departs from both by eliminating the need for a separate 3D encoder. Instead of training a new point or depth encoder from scratch, it fine-tunes only 6M parameters of the CLIP image encoder using a residual transformer block, while replacing the standard CAD-rendered alignment images with diffusion-model-generated 'depth-aligned images' via ControlNet. The contour-aware projection pipeline is explicitly inspired by PointCLIP V2's quantize-densify-smooth-squeeze approach, and the dual-encoder logit aggregation at inference draws on the IMM-Mean continual learning strategy to mitigate catastrophic forgetting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that texture diversity in alignment images (from stochastic diffusion generation) matters more than geometric consistency (from CAD renders) for robust 3D representation learning, and that this insight can be plugged into existing architectures to improve them. The efficiency of the approach—fine-tuning only 6M parameters on ShapeNet's 52K objects to outperform methods trained on multi-dataset ensembles—suggests the bottleneck in prior 3D open-world methods was data quality rather than model scale. Open questions include whether depth-aligned images can scale to the ~1M-object Objaverse dataset (currently infeasible due to generation cost), whether a data filtering strategy can reliably remove the low-quality generations the authors acknowledge, and whether the approach extends naturally to 3D scene understanding beyond individual objects.
