## Problem

In federated offline RL, distributed agents must collaboratively learn a high-quality policy from small pre-collected datasets generated by heterogeneous, unknown behavior policies without sharing data. Naively applying FedAvg to offline RL-trained actors and critics produces federated policies that perform worse than individual local policies, due to three unaddressed challenges: ensemble heterogeneity (policies of varying quality should not be uniformly averaged), pessimistic value computation (local critics are overly conservative toward actions well-represented in other clients' data), and data heterogeneity (multiple local gradient steps cause client drift). No prior work systematically identified or tackled all three issues simultaneously.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local offline dataset of transitions (s, a, r, s') generated by a client-specific behavior policy of unknown quality; the underlying MDP (transition kernel and reward) is assumed identical across all clients. The server and clients have no access to the environment or the true MDP model. The method outputs a federated deterministic control policy (state-to-action mapping) and a federated Q-function, both parameterized by neural networks with two hidden layers of 256 units. Federation proceeds in rounds where a random subset of clients (e.g., 20 of 50) participates, each performing K local gradient steps (≈380 steps) before sending updated parameters to the server.

## Method

FEDORA replaces FedAvg's dataset-size weighting with entropy-regularized soft-max weights computed from each client's local policy performance J_i^t = E[Q_i^t(s, π_i^t(s))], which serves as a proxy for the offline RL objective, so that higher-performing policies contribute more to the federated policy while the entropy term (temperature β) prevents weight collapse onto a single client. For critic training, FEDORA introduces 'federated optimism': the local critic's Bellman target is set to max(Q_local, Q_fed), ensuring the local critic learns ambitious but feasible value estimates that promote high-value actions. The local actor update combines a TD3-BC loss (policy improvement plus behavior-cloning regularization on local data) with a proximal term penalizing deviation from the federated policy, mitigating client drift under heterogeneous data. Finally, a decaying mechanism reduces the weight of the local data loss when the federated policy already outperforms the locally updated policy, preventing irrelevant local data from degrading the learned policy.

## Results

On MuJoCo environments (Hopper, HalfCheetah, Walker2D) with 50 clients (25 expert, 25 medium data, 5000 transitions each), FEDORA outperforms all federated baselines and achieves cumulative episodic reward equivalent to or exceeding centralized TD3-BC on pooled data. On the CityLearn urban energy management benchmark, FEDORA similarly surpasses both federated and centralized baselines. In the real-world TurtleBot navigation task, only FEDORA's learned policy successfully reaches the target waypoint while avoiding an obstacle, whereas all baselines fail. FEDORA remains effective even when only 20% of clients hold expert-quality data and when as few as 20% of clients participate in a given federation round.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEDORA outperforms all seven baselines across all evaluated environments. Against the naive federation baselines (Fed-A, Fed-AC, Fed-AC-Prox), FEDORA achieves substantially higher cumulative reward; these baselines plateau at low performance levels even after many federation rounds, with Fed-AC-Prox (adding a FedProx-style proximal term) offering only marginal improvement over plain FedAvg. HDAFL, which splits the actor into generic and client-specific parts, also underperforms FEDORA. Against centralized offline RL on pooled data (TD3-BC, CQL, IQL), FEDORA matches or exceeds performance, confirming that simply combining heterogeneous data is detrimental due to distribution shift. TD3-BC with data rebalancing (TD3-BC_RB) improves over plain centralized training but still falls short of FEDORA, which the authors attribute to FEDORA's superior entropy-regularized weighting that evaluates learned policy quality rather than raw dataset reward. In the real-world TurtleBot experiment, FEDORA is the only method that successfully navigates to the goal while avoiding the obstacle.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEDORA builds on the TD3-BC offline RL algorithm (Fujimoto & Gu, 2021) as its client-level training objective and on the FedAvg federation structure (McMahan et al., 2017) as its communication protocol, but departs from both in critical ways. Rather than the standard dataset-size-weighted averaging of FedAvg, it replaces weights with an entropy-regularized softmax over each client's estimated policy performance, and introduces federated optimism (max of local and federated critic) plus proximal regularization to the federated policy. The closest prior work, Zhou et al. (2022), formulates federated offline RL but assumes linear MDPs and performs only a single federation step; FEDORA is positioned as the first general (non-linear) federated offline RL algorithm for heterogeneous-data clients with iterative local updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically demonstrates that naively combining offline RL with standard federation (FedAvg) produces policies worse than individual client training, and that centralized training on pooled heterogeneous-policy data is also detrimental—both counterintuitive results that reshape how practitioners should think about federated offline RL. The practical relevance is clear for multi-robot and multi-agent settings where data quality varies and privacy prevents sharing. Key limitations include the assumption of a shared MDP across all clients (same transition and reward functions), evaluation primarily on MuJoCo benchmarks plus one real-robot task, and the use of a single offline RL algorithm (TD3-BC) as the client objective, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other offline RL methods or to clients with genuinely different environment dynamics.
