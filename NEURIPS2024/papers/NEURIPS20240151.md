## Problem

Classical compressive sensing methods (Lasso, SBL) impose sparsity priors but cannot adapt to specific signal statistics, while modern generative-model-based CS approaches require large ground-truth training sets and lose the explicit sparsity structure. This paper asks whether one can design a prior that is simultaneously sparsity-inducing and data-adaptive, trainable from only a few compressed and noisy observations without any ground-truth information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240151/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is trained on a set of compressed noisy observations Y = {y_i} (each y_i ∈ R^M with M << N), optionally with a known measurement matrix A and dictionary D; no ground-truth signals are required. At inference, a single new observation y is fed through the encoder (or its least-squares estimate if A varies per sample), and the decoder produces conditional variances γ_θ(z) that, combined with the closed-form posterior mean, yield the reconstructed signal x̂ ∈ R^N via a Monte-Carlo CME approximation. The encoder's output distribution also provides a differential entropy h(z|y) as an uncertainty estimate, enabling out-of-distribution detection.

## Method

The proposed sparse Bayesian generative model factorizes as p(y|s) = N(y; ADs, σ²I), p(s|z) = N(s; 0, diag(γ_θ(z))), p(z) = p_δ(z), where the zero-mean diagonal-covariance conditional Gaussian on s|z preserves SBL's conjugate-prior structure so that p(s|z,y) has a closed form independent of the specifics of γ_θ(z) and p_δ(z). Theorem 3.1 proves that the marginal p(s) = ∫ p_δ(z) N(s; 0, diag(γ_θ(z))) dz is bounded above by the improper sparsity-inducing prior t(s) = C·∏ 1/|s_i|, making the model's log-evidence a variational lower bound of a sparsity-inducing log-evidence. Two concrete instantiations are derived: CSVAE (continuous z ~ N(0,I), NN encoder/decoder, trained by maximizing an adapted ELBO via stochastic gradient descent) and CSGMM (discrete z with K components, trained by a tailored EM algorithm whose m-step has a closed-form solution). Inference requires no iterative optimization—only a feed-forward pass through the encoder/decoder plus a small number of Monte-Carlo samples—yielding reconstruction times of ~2 ms versus ~294 ms for SBL.

## Results

On MNIST (M=200, N_t=20000), the proposed CSVAE and CSGMM achieve the best nMSE and SSIM across all observation dimensions M and training set sizes N_t, outperforming Lasso, SBL, and CSGAN. The methods can effectively learn from as few as a few hundred compressed and noisy samples without any ground-truth information. Inference is dramatically faster than baselines: CSVAE reconstructs in 2.02 ms and CSGMM in 2.64 ms on average, compared to 480.81 ms for CSGAN and 294.17 ms for SBL. Training time is also substantially reduced (CSGMM: 45 min, CSVAE: 88 min vs. CSGAN: 1481 min on MNIST).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Lasso and SBL (non-learnable baselines), both CSVAE and CSGMM consistently achieve lower nMSE and higher SSIM across all tested M values on MNIST, piecewise smooth functions, and CelebA, demonstrating the benefit of learning statistical structure from compressed data. Against CSGAN (the strongest learnable baseline), CSVAE and CSGMM outperform it in both nMSE and SSIM, particularly as M increases; CSGAN's manifold-constrained estimation limits its ability to recover fine details not present in training, while the proposed methods infer conditional distributions over the full linear space. On piecewise smooth functions with 10 dB SNR, CSVAE outperforms CKSVD+OMP in nMSE. In terms of computational efficiency, the proposed methods are ~200× faster at inference than CSGAN (2.02 ms vs. 480.81 ms) and ~15× faster than SBL, while training is ~17× faster than CSGAN (88 min vs. 1481 min for CSVAE on MNIST).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: sparse Bayesian learning (SBL) for compressive sensing, which provides a conjugate Gaussian prior with provable sparsity-inducing properties, and latent-variable generative models (VAEs, GMMs), which provide data adaptability through conditional Gaussianity. The key conceptual move is to condition SBL's diagonal-covariance Gaussian prior on a latent variable z, yielding a family of 'sparse Bayesian generative models' that inherit both the sparsity guarantee (proven via Theorem 3.1) and the learnability of VAEs/GMMs. This departs from generative-model-based CS (CSGAN, VAE-based CS) by keeping the dictionary fixed and learning statistics in the compressible domain rather than learning a generative mapping in the signal domain, and from compressive dictionary learning (CKSVD) by learning a probabilistic model rather than a dictionary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling effective CS reconstruction from only a few hundred corrupted (compressed + noisy) samples without any ground-truth training data, which is critical in domains like electron microscopy, wearable ECG, and wireless channel estimation where clean data is prohibitively expensive. The inference phase requires no iterative optimization (just a feed-forward pass), yielding ~200× speedup over CSGAN at reconstruction time. Key limitations include the zero-mean constraint on the compressible representation (which introduces bias), the reliance on a fixed dictionary (no learnable dictionary yet), and the computational overhead of inverting S×S posterior covariance matrices during training for high-dimensional signals. Open questions include extending to learnable dictionaries, perception-emphasizing estimators beyond the CME approximation, and scaling to very high-dimensional problems.
