## Problem

Existing watermarking methods for diffusion models face a fundamental trade-off between robustness and concealment: they passively limit watermark strength to avoid visible artifacts, which inherently weakens the watermark's resilience to image manipulations. In-processing alternatives that learn this balance require expensive model retraining or introduce unintended semantic alterations. The paper asks whether an explicit, active hiding process can decouple watermark strength from invisibility, enabling the embedding of much stronger watermarks without degrading image quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a frequency-domain watermark pattern (concentric rings covering ~70% of the latent's frequency domain) as inputs, and produces a watermarked image indistinguishable from the unwatermarked counterpart. Verification takes a possibly distorted image (after blur, noise, JPEG compression, rotation, cropping, or combinations thereof) and reverses the diffusion process only to the intermediate injection step (between steps 200–300) to extract and compare the watermark via L1 distance. The scheme requires a reversible sampler (DPM-Solver, DDIM, PNDM, etc.) and operates on both latent-space models (Stable Diffusion, 512×512) and pixel-space models (ImageNet diffusion, 256×256).

## Method

ROBIN implants a robust frequency-domain watermark into an intermediate diffusion latent state (after semantic formation, between steps 300 and 200) and then actively guides the model to conceal it in the final image using an optimized prompt embedding signal w_p. The watermark w_i and the hiding prompt w_p are co-optimized via an alternating adversarial procedure: w_i is pushed to maximum strength (maximizing ‖w_i‖) while w_p minimizes the MSE between the watermarked and unwatermarked final images plus a constraint term that limits DDIM inversion loss. The prompt guidance is injected through the classifier-free guidance mechanism as a weighted combination of the original text condition, the hiding prompt, and the null condition. Verification reverses only the limited number of diffusion steps from the final image back to the injection point, extracting the watermark from the Fourier domain of the recovered latent, which is substantially faster than Tree-Ring's full-trajectory inversion.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion, ROBIN achieves an average AUC of 0.983 across six attack types (blur, noise, JPEG, brightness, rotation, cropping), outperforming the closest in-processing baseline Tree-Ring (0.975) and all post-processing methods (0.574–0.854). Verification time is 0.531s per image, roughly 5× faster than Tree-Ring (2.599s) while maintaining higher robustness. On the ImageNet pixel-level diffusion model, ROBIN reaches 0.988 average AUC versus Tree-Ring's 0.966, with a verification time of 0.986s versus 3.963s. Under combined multi-attack scenarios (up to 6 simultaneous transformations), ROBIN retains 0.556 AUC compared to Tree-Ring's 0.509.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROBIN outperforms all five baselines on average AUC for Stable Diffusion (0.983 vs. 0.574–0.975). Against the closest competitor Tree-Ring [44], ROBIN gains +0.008 average AUC and is ~5× faster in verification (0.531s vs. 2.599s), while also achieving higher image quality (PSNR 24.03 vs. 15.37, SSIM 0.768 vs. 0.568). Among post-processing methods, RivaGAN [52] (avg 0.854) and StableSig [14] (avg 0.845) are the strongest but both suffer severe degradation under rotation (0.173 and 0.658 respectively), whereas ROBIN maintains 0.957. DwtDctSvd [26] (avg 0.702) and DwtDct [4] (avg 0.574) are the weakest, with DwtDct dropping below 0.5 AUC under noise and JPEG attacks. On the ImageNet pixel-level model, ROBIN (0.988 avg) again surpasses Tree-Ring (0.966) and all post-processing baselines (0.536–0.882), confirming generality across latent and pixel diffusion architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROBIN extends the in-processing watermarking paradigm for diffusion models, building directly on DDIM's deterministic inversion properties and the classifier-free guidance framework. Its closest predecessor is Tree-Ring (Wen et al., 2023), which encodes watermarks by modifying the initial noise; ROBIN departs by implanting the watermark at an intermediate diffusion state and introducing an explicit adversarial 'hiding' process via an optimized prompt signal, rather than relying on the model to naturally obscure the watermark. This contrasts with post-processing methods (DwtDct, RivaGAN) that passively limit watermark strength for invisibility, and with retraining-based approaches (Stable Signature, training-data watermarking) that bake the watermark into model weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit hiding process decouples watermark strength from invisibility, enabling stronger, more robust watermarks without visible artifacts and without model retraining—a practical advantage for deploying watermarks on existing pretrained diffusion models. Verification is also faster than Tree-Ring since only a partial inversion (from step 0 to the injection point) is needed rather than a full reverse pass. Key limitations include the dependence on reversible samplers (DDIM, DPM-Solver) and inherent information loss during inversion; the paper also notes the unexpected finding that the optimized hiding prompt actually hinders watermark recovery during verification, an open question left for future work.
