## Problem

Current zero-shot denoising methods (DIP, Noise2Void, Zero-Shot Noise2Noise) train a new network from scratch for every noisy image, which is both time-consuming (minutes per image) and produces sub-optimal quality due to the limited information available from a single observation. The paper asks whether pre-training a model on vast natural images via masking can provide a universal, noise-agnostic starting point that simultaneously improves denoising quality and drastically reduces inference time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy RGB image (processed as 256×256 patches) with no prior knowledge of noise type or intensity, and produces a fully denoised image of the same size. During inference, the image is iteratively masked at the pixel level (30% mask ratio for synthetic noise, 80–95% for spatially correlated real noise), and only the predictions corresponding to masked regions are retained and assembled via exponential moving average across T iterations. For real-world noise with strong spatial correlation, a pixel-shuffle downsampling step is applied before processing to decorrelate adjacent noise pixels.

## Method

MPI (Masked Pre-train then Iterative fill) consists of two stages. First, a U-shaped hourglass network (same architecture as DIP) is pre-trained on ~48K ImageNet images using pixel-wise random masking: the network reconstructs only the masked pixels, learning the statistical distribution of natural images without any noise assumptions. Second, for zero-shot denoising, the pre-trained weights are loaded and further optimized on the single noisy image for T iterations (200–1000 steps), where each iteration applies a fresh random mask, computes loss only on masked regions, and updates an EMA ensemble of predictions. This mask-aligned optimization keeps inference consistent with pre-training, preventing premature over-fitting to noise and enabling high-quality results in ~10 seconds (faster variant) versus minutes for prior zero-shot methods. The pre-trained weights act as an implicit regularizer, and the pixel-wise masking strategy (as opposed to patch-level) captures fine-grained spatial structure critical for low-level denoising.

## Results

On CSet with Gaussian σ=25, MPI achieves 31.61 PSNR / 0.841 SSIM, outperforming the best zero-shot baseline ZS-N2N (29.55/0.765) by over 2 dB. The faster variant (β=0.9, 200 iterations) reaches 30.83/0.824 in just 10.1 s, compared to DIP's 451.9 s. On real-world SIDD validation, MPI scores 34.43/0.844, and on the generalization benchmark (Kodak, 6 unseen noise types) it achieves 33.30/0.890 average PSNR/SSIM, surpassing all supervised (SwinIR, Restormer) and unsupervised (Nb2Nb, B2U) methods trained on Gaussian σ=25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPI outperforms all zero-shot baselines (DIP, N2V*, N2S*, ZS-N2N, FasterDIP) across nearly all synthetic noise settings, with the largest gains at moderate-to-high noise (e.g., +2.06 dB over ZS-N2N on CSet σ=25, +3.53 dB over DIP on McMaster σ=50). On the generalization benchmark (Table 2), MPI's 33.30/0.890 average across 6 unseen noise types beats the best supervised method Restormer (29.73/0.777) by 3.57 dB and the best unsupervised method B2U (30.47/0.804) by 2.83 dB, demonstrating that pre-trained natural-image knowledge transfers better than task-specific training. On real-world SIDD, MPI (34.43/0.844) edges out DIP (33.68/0.802) and FasterDIP (33.55/0.795) while running 9× faster than DIP. Against diffusion-based methods (DDNM, DDPG) in Table 12, MPI wins on real noise (SIDD: 34.43 vs 28.11; FMD: 32.97 vs 28.99) but trails DDNM on Gaussian σ=10 (34.91 vs 36.22), where DDNM benefits from known variance. The faster variant (β=0.9) matches or exceeds ZS-N2N on most settings while being comparable in speed (10.1 s vs 16.8 s), and the full model (β=0.99) achieves the best quality at 51.6 s—still 8.7× faster than DIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

MPI sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: Deep Image Prior (DIP) and its architectural variants, which learn denoising by optimizing a network on a single noisy image from scratch, and Masked Image Modeling (MIM), which pre-trains representations by reconstructing randomly masked regions of natural images. The key departure is that prior zero-shot denoisers (DIP, N2V, N2S, ZS-N2N, FasterDIP) all initialize from random weights and rely on regularization or under-parameterization to prevent over-fitting, whereas MPI introduces a masked pre-training stage on ImageNet that provides a noise-agnostic initialization, followed by an iterative filling procedure that alternately reconstructs masked regions and assembles predictions via EMA. This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first application of pixel-wise masked pre-training specifically for zero-shot denoising, repurposing a technique originally designed for high-level semantic tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: MPI reduces inference time from ~450s (DIP) to ~10s while matching or exceeding quality, making zero-shot denoising feasible in real-world pipelines. The ability to generalize across six noise types (Gaussian, Poisson, NLF, speckle, salt-and-pepper, and real sensor noise) without knowing the noise distribution or intensity addresses a long-standing limitation of both supervised and prior zero-shot methods. A notable limitation is that the mask-based reconstruction objective cannot remove non-zero-mean noise, so severe color bias in extremely low-light images persists. Open questions include whether the pre-training benefit extends to other restoration tasks (inpainting, super-resolution) and whether the current U-shaped hourglass architecture is optimal or whether larger transformer-based backbones would further improve the pre-trained representation.
