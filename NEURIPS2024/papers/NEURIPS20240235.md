## Problem

Decoder-only Transformers store key-value caches at every layer for every token, making inference memory grow as O(L·N·D) and prefilling latency grow as O(N²). For long contexts (e.g., 512K tokens on a 65B model), KV caches alone can exceed 86 GB of GPU memory, and prefilling 1M tokens takes minutes, making long-context deployment impractical. The paper asks whether global attention capability can be retained while caching KV pairs only once across all layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240235/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text token sequence (up to 1M tokens) and autoregressively produces next-token predictions over a 100K vocabulary, behaving externally like a standard decoder-only Transformer. Internally, the input is processed in two stages: the self-decoder encodes the full sequence with efficient local attention to produce a single set of global KV caches, and the cross-decoder then attends to those shared caches for global context. The unusual aspect is that during prefilling, computation can early-exit after the self-decoder without altering the final output, since the cross-decoder only needs the self-decoder's output representations.

## Method

YOCO stacks L/2 cross-decoder layers on top of L/2 self-decoder layers. The self-decoder uses efficient self-attention (sliding-window attention or gated retention) with O(1) inference memory, producing a single set of global KV caches (K̂, V̂) from its final layer output. All cross-decoder layers then perform global cross-attention against these shared caches, recovering full-sequence attention without per-layer KV storage. This reduces KV cache memory from O(L·N·D) to O((N+L)·D) and prefilling time from O(L·N²·D) to O(L·N·D). The gated retention module (gRet) augments RetNet with a data-dependent sigmoid gate and unifies parallel, recurrent, and chunkwise-recurrent computation paradigms, enabling efficient training (chunkwise) and constant-memory inference (recurrent). The architecture is trained with standard next-token prediction loss using grouped-query attention, SwiGLU, and pre-RMSNorm, and is scaled from 160M to 13B parameters and extended to 1M context length.

## Results

On the LM Eval Harness zero-shot benchmark (8 tasks), YOCO-3B trained with 1T tokens achieves an average accuracy of 0.634, outperforming OpenLLaMA-3B-v2 (0.619) and StableLM-alpha-3B-v2 (0.612). After scaling to 1.6T tokens, YOCO-3B reaches 0.636 average, and extending context to 1M tokens further improves it to 0.645. On fine-grained LM perplexity (160M params), YOCO_gRet achieves 3.530 validation perplexity, beating Transformer (3.564), Mamba (3.645), RetNet (3.633), and H3 (3.591). For inference efficiency, YOCO reduces KV cache memory by ~80× for 65B models and cuts 512K prefilling latency from 180 s to <6 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three 3B Transformer baselines on the 8-task LM Eval Harness, YOCO-3B (1T) wins on average accuracy (0.634 vs. 0.619 for OpenLLaMA-3B-v2 and 0.612 for StableLM-alpha-3B-v2), with the largest gaps on ARC-C (+0.040 over OpenLLaMA) and OBQA (+0.038). On multi-needle retrieval at 128K length, YOCO-3B-1M (3B) substantially outperforms YaRN-Mistral-128K (7B; 0.98 vs. 0.02 at N=1) and ChatGLM3-128K (6B; 0.98 vs. 0.94 at N=1), and beats MiniCPM-128K (2.4B) at N=4 (0.84 vs. 0.54), while remaining competitive with the larger LWM-1M-text (7B; 0.98 vs. 1.00 at N=1, 0.98 vs. 0.90 at N=2). On fine-grained 160M perplexity, YOCO_gRet (3.530) beats Transformer (3.564), Mamba (3.645), RetNet (3.633), and H3 (3.591), with the advantage concentrated in the AR-Hit (associative recall) component (1.199 vs. 1.219 for Transformer). In inference profiling, YOCO reduces 1M-length inference memory to 12.4 GB versus 9.4× that for Transformer, and achieves 9.6× higher throughput at 512K context (43.1 vs. 4.5 token/s).

## Relation to Prior Work

YOCO reorganizes the decoder-only Transformer into a two-stage decoder-decoder stack, where a self-decoder (using efficient self-attention like gated retention or sliding-window attention) produces a single global KV cache that is then reused by all cross-decoder layers via cross-attention. This departs from prior KV-cache reduction strategies (sparse attention, cache dropping, quantization) by keeping one full global cache rather than approximating or pruning it, and from encoder-decoder designs by maintaining autoregressive generation end-to-end. The gated retention module directly extends RetNet [35] with a data-dependent gating mechanism, while the overall architecture draws on grouped-query attention, SwiGLU, and RMSNorm from the Llama/StableLM lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The KV cache memory bottleneck is the primary obstacle to deploying long-context LLMs in practice, and YOCO's ~80× cache reduction (for 65B models) plus 71.8× prefill speedup at 1M tokens represent a concrete path to making million-token contexts feasible on consumer hardware. The architecture's 'early exit' property for prefilling is a clean systems-level insight that could influence future model design. Open questions include whether the 1:1 self-to-cross-decoder ratio is optimal at scale, whether the approach extends naturally to non-autoregressive or multimodal settings, and whether the cross-decoder's O(N) cross-attention per generated token becomes a bottleneck at extreme lengths. The paper does not report error bars or multi-seed results, and the training corpus details are limited to 'similar to StableLM,' leaving some reproducibility gaps.
