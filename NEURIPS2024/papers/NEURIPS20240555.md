## Problem

Existing adaptor methods (LoRA, DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) fail to accurately depict out-of-distribution (OOD) concepts in text-to-image diffusion models, producing distorted textures, wrong colors, and incorrect anatomical details for concepts with CMMD scores above 3.5. The paper identifies that this failure is driven by low-quality training data containing disruptive objects, vague instances, or irrelevant elements, yet no systematic strategy exists to automatically locate the high-quality samples needed for faithful OOD concept adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240555/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt naming an OOD concept, a data pool of roughly 800–5000 real-world images per concept (collected from ImageNet, iNaturalist 2018, and IP102), and a frozen Stable Diffusion 2.0 backbone. It starts with only 20 randomly sampled images and iteratively adds 20 selected samples per cycle (5 cycles for single-concept, 10 for multi-concept), producing trained adaptor weights (LoRA, DreamBooth, or TI) that generate 100 evaluation images per concept. The aesthetic scorer is re-personalized per concept category each cycle, and the concept-matching scorer uses CLIP ViT-L/14 features with a VLAD-style codebook built from the current training set.

## Method

CATOD is an active-learning framework that alternates between high-quality data acquisition and scheduled adaptor training. Its core is a dual-scoring system: a personalized aesthetic scorer (fine-tuned via PA-IAA with concept-specific preference labels) and a VLAD-based concept-matching scorer that measures how well a candidate image's CLIP embedding aligns with the training-set codebook. A weighted acquisition score (Eq. 7) dynamically balances the two scorers by inversely weighting each based on the adaptor's current generative performance on that dimension, so the system preferentially selects samples that compensate for the adaptor's current weakness. A trigonometric quality indicator (Eq. 6) monitors the adaptor's balanced performance and drives a learning-rate schedule that reduces the rate and stops training when quality plateaus. The paper provides a theoretical grounding via an MMSE Pythagorean decomposition, showing that the first term corresponds to aesthetic preservation (gap between generated and ideal distributions) and the second to concept-matching (distortion of OOD information by the training set), justifying why both scores are necessary.

## Results

On 25 OOD concepts across 5 categories (insect, lizard, penguin, seafish, snake), CATOD achieves the best average CLIP score of 77.97 and lowest CMMD of 0.84 when combined with LoRA, outperforming LoRA+RAND (67.37 CLIP, 1.47 CMMD) by 10.60 CLIP points and 0.63 CMMD. Across all adaptor frameworks, CATOD delivers a 0.56–11.10 CLIP score boost and a 0.12–0.63 CMMD reduction over both random and CLIP-based sampling baselines. In multi-concept adaption (Table 2), CATOD+LoRA reaches 74.29 CLIP and 1.24 CMMD, still leading all baselines by 1.56–5.07 CLIP and 0.12–0.86 CMMD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CATOD is evaluated as a data-selection strategy plugged into three adaptor frameworks: DreamBooth, Textual Inversion (TI), and LoRA, each compared against random sampling (RAND) and CLIP-score-based sampling (CLIP). CATOD wins on both CLIP and CMMD across all nine adaptor×strategy combinations. The largest gains appear with LoRA: CATOD+LoRA achieves 77.97 CLIP vs. 73.06 (CLIP) and 67.37 (RAND), a 4.91 and 10.60 point improvement respectively, while reducing CMMD from 1.18/1.47 to 0.84. With DreamBooth, CATOD gains 3.25 CLIP over CLIP-sampling and 7.58 over RAND, cutting CMMD by 0.22 and 0.50. With TI, the CLIP improvement is 3.51 over CLIP and 7.04 over RAND, with CMMD dropping by 0.30 and 0.63. Notably, CLIP-based sampling already provides a meaningful boost over random sampling, but CATOD's weighted aesthetic+concept-matching scoring consistently adds a further 3–5 CLIP points on top, confirming that balancing both scores is critical for OOD concept fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the adaptor-based personalization line (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, LoRA) for latent diffusion models, but identifies a specific failure mode—degraded visual fidelity on out-of-distribution concepts (CMMD > 3.5)—that prior work largely ignores by focusing on in-distribution subjects. It repurposes the active learning paradigm (iterative data selection + model update) and combines it with two scoring mechanisms drawn from distinct subfields: personalized image aesthetics assessment (PIAA) and VLAD-based image retrieval for concept-matching. The theoretical grounding via MMSE decomposition (linking the LDM loss to an estimation problem and decomposing it into aesthetic-preservation and concept-distortion terms) is a novel framing not present in prior adaptor literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CATOD offers a practical, data-efficient recipe for handling OOD concepts without manual curation, which is a real bottleneck for deploying T2I models on long-tail or rare subjects. However, the evaluation is limited to 25 concepts across 5 narrow categories (insects, lizards, penguins, seafish, snakes), and the aesthetic scorer must be re-personalized per category, raising scalability questions. Open questions include whether the CMMD > 3.5 threshold generalizes across domains, whether the trigonometric weighting in Eq. (7) is optimal versus simpler alternatives, and whether the framework extends to more extreme OOD cases (e.g., fictional or composite concepts) where no real-world reference images exist.
