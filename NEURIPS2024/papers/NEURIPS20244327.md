## Problem

Existing knowledge graph embedding methods either rely solely on graph structure, missing the rich open-world knowledge surrounding entities, or fine-tune pre-trained language models with KG triples, which is computationally expensive, limited to small-scale PLMs, and fails to fully exploit the extensive knowledge within modern LLMs. The paper asks whether one can 'fine-tune the KG with the LLM' rather than 'fine-tune the LLM with the KG,' directly injecting LLM-derived semantic knowledge into lightweight structure-based embeddings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20244327/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes KG triples, GPT-4o (for entity description generation and hierarchy refinement), and text-embedding-3-large embeddings (for entity representation initialization). It produces fine-tuned entity and relation embeddings compatible with any standard KGE scoring function, plus an LLM-guided binary-tree entity hierarchy. Notably, the LLM is invoked only during a pre-computation phase (descriptions, clustering, hierarchy refinement); the actual fine-tuning loop operates purely on the pre-computed hierarchy and frozen text embeddings, making training 12× faster than the fastest PLM-based baseline.

## Method

KG-FIT is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (LLM-Guided Hierarchy Construction) generates per-entity descriptions via GPT-4o, concatenates entity and description embeddings, runs agglomerative clustering with a silhouette-score-optimized threshold to build a seed binary tree, then refines it bottom-up using LLM-suggested actions (cluster splitting, parent merge, leaf merge, include) to produce a semantically coherent hierarchy. Stage 2 (KG Fine-Tuning) initializes entity embeddings as a convex combination of random vectors and sliced text embeddings, then optimizes a composite loss combining three constraints: a hierarchical clustering loss (cluster cohesion, inter-level separation, and exponentially-decayed hierarchical distance maintenance), a semantic anchoring loss that pins embeddings to their text-embedding origins, and a standard margin-based link prediction loss from the chosen base KGE model (TransE, RotatE, HAKE, etc.). The key design choice is that the LLM's global semantic knowledge enters through the frozen hierarchy structure and text embeddings, while local KG structure is captured through the link prediction objective, enabling the framework to be model-agnostic and to evolve with future LLM improvements without retraining the language model.

## Results

On FB15K-237, KG-FIT_HAKE achieves MRR 0.362 and H@10 0.568, surpassing the best PLM-based method CSProm-KG (H@10 0.538) by 6.3% and the base HAKE model (H@10 0.538) by 6.1%. On YAGO3-10, KG-FIT_RotatE reaches MRR 0.563 and H@10 0.722, outperforming CSProm-KG (H@10 0.675) by 7.0%. On PrimeKG, KG-FIT_ComplEx attains MRR 0.651 and H@10 0.835, exceeding PKGC (H@10 0.625) by 11.0% and the base ComplEx (H@10 0.789) by 5.8%. The paper reports overall H@10 improvements of 14.4%, 13.5%, and 11.9% over state-of-the-art PLM-based methods on the three datasets, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KG-FIT consistently outperforms all PLM-based baselines (KG-BERT, StAR, PKGC, C-LMKE, KGT5, KG-S2S, SimKGC, CSProm-KG) on FB15K-237, YAGO3-10, and PrimeKG. On FB15K-237, KG-FIT_HAKE (H@10 0.568) beats the strongest PLM baseline CSProm-KG (0.538) by 6.3% and KG-BERT (0.420) by 35.2%. On YAGO3-10, KG-FIT_RotatE (H@10 0.722) surpasses CSProm-KG (0.675) by 7.0% and PKGC (0.660) by 9.4%. On PrimeKG, KG-FIT_ComplEx (H@10 0.835) exceeds PKGC (0.625) by 11.0% and SimKGC (0.742) by 12.5%. Against structure-based base models, KG-FIT yields gains of 12.6%, 6.7%, and 17.7% in H@10 on FB15K-237, YAGO3-10, and PrimeKG respectively (e.g., KG-FIT_HAKE 0.568 vs. HAKE base 0.538 on FB15K-237; KG-FIT_ComplEx 0.835 vs. ComplEx base 0.789 on PrimeKG). However, on the lexical dataset WN18RR, KG-FIT does not surpass C-LMKE (MRR 0.619) or SimKGC (MRR 0.666), though combining KG-FIT_pRotatE as a recall model with PKGC re-ranking achieves MRR 0.625, approaching these PLM-based leaders.

## Relation to Prior Work

KG-FIT inverts the prevailing paradigm in PLM-based knowledge graph completion: rather than fine-tuning a language model on KG triples (as in KG-BERT, PKGC, SimKGC, CSProm-KG), it fine-tunes structure-based KG embeddings (TransE, RotatE, HAKE, etc.) using knowledge extracted from LLMs. The method draws on agglomerative hierarchical clustering and silhouette-score optimization for seed hierarchy construction, then introduces LLM-guided refinement (cluster splitting and bottom-up triple updates) to produce a semantically coherent entity hierarchy. This hierarchy, combined with pre-trained text embeddings, is injected into the fine-tuning process via novel hierarchical clustering and semantic anchoring constraints, positioning KG-FIT as a bridge between the efficiency of structure-based KGE and the open-world knowledge of modern LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency-performance tradeoff: KG-FIT achieves 12× faster training than the fastest PLM-based baseline while outperforming all of them, and it can leverage any LLM (including GPT-4o-class models) without the prohibitive cost of fine-tuning them. This makes LLM-informed KG embeddings accessible at scale, particularly for large biomedical or enterprise KGs. Key limitations include underperformance on lexical datasets like WN18RR where contrastive PLM methods excel, sensitivity to seed hierarchy quality, and the single-operation-per-triple refinement that trades expressiveness for speed. Open questions include whether contrastive learning can be integrated to close the WN18RR gap, and whether multi-operation hierarchy updates can further improve without prohibitive LLM call costs.
