## Problem

Vision-language (VL) trackers underperform state-of-the-art visual trackers on mainstream benchmarks because they depend on manually annotated text descriptions that are often ambiguous, inaccurate, or lack contextual information about background objects. The paper asks whether the world knowledge in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) can be harnessed to generate higher-quality language descriptions that actually improve tracking, and how to bridge the semantic mismatch between MLLM-generated text and the VL tracker's latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241241/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sequence of RGB frames and an initial bounding box marking the target in the first frame. It produces per-frame target bounding boxes as the primary tracking output, along with MLLM-generated foreground and background text descriptions that persist across the sequence. The MLLM receives a template image with a green bounding box drawn on the target plus a prompt template; the GVLM (GroundingDINO-T) receives each frame image concatenated with the foreground and background text tokens to yield region proposals and per-token alignment scores. The framework is designed as a plug-and-play module that can be attached to existing visual trackers (MixFormer, ARTrack, OSTRack, TransT, STARK) without modifying their internal architecture.

## Method

ChatTracker comprises three modules: (1) a Reflection-based Prompt Optimization (RPO) module that iteratively refines MLLM-generated foreground descriptions by feeding back GVLM grounding results—words whose grounded regions overlap the target above an IoU threshold are marked positive, those that do not are marked negative—and prompting the MLLM to regenerate text incorporating this feedback until the maximum IoU between grounded proposals and the ground-truth box exceeds a threshold; (2) a Semantic Tracking module that uses a GVLM to ground both foreground and background text descriptions into per-frame region proposals, classifying proposals into foreground and background sets via fixed token-to-category assignments determined on the template image, and augmenting foreground proposals with an off-the-shelf visual tracker's prediction; (3) a Foreground Verification module that scores each foreground proposal by combining a learned template-proposal similarity (cosine distance in a discriminative embedding space) with a background penalty (maximum IoU against all background proposals), selecting the highest-scoring proposal as the final tracking result. The key insight is that iterative reflection with tracking feedback resolves both MLLM hallucination and the vocabulary mismatch between MLLM outputs and the VL tracker's grounding space, while background descriptions provide disambiguation context absent from manual annotations.

## Results

ChatTracker-L achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three main benchmarks: 74.1% AUC on LaSOT (surpassing the best visual tracker ARTrack-384 at 72.6% by 1.5 points), 86.1% AUC on TrackingNet (beating SeqTrack-L384 at 85.5% by 0.6 points), and 65.4% AUC on TNL2K (outperforming the best VL tracker UVLTrack-B at 63.1% by 2.3 points). The lighter ChatTracker-B variant also leads on LaSOT (71.7% AUC) and TrackingNet (83.6% AUC) while remaining competitive on TNL2K (59.6% AUC). On the VL-specific TNL2K benchmark, ChatTracker-L surpasses JointNLT by 12.1% in precision, demonstrating the benefit of MLLM-generated descriptions over manual annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChatTracker-L outperforms all 13 visual and VL tracker baselines on every reported metric across LaSOT, TrackingNet, and TNL2K. Against the strongest visual trackers, it leads ARTrack-384 by 1.5 AUC points on LaSOT (74.1 vs 72.6) and by 1.0 on TrackingNet (86.1 vs 85.1), and surpasses SeqTrack-L384 by 0.6 AUC on TrackingNet (86.1 vs 85.5). Among VL trackers, the margin is substantially larger: ChatTracker-L exceeds UVLTrack-B by 4.7 AUC on LaSOT (74.1 vs 69.4) and by 2.3 AUC on TNL2K (65.4 vs 63.1), and beats JointNLT by 13.7 AUC on LaSOT (74.1 vs 60.4). The plug-and-play integration experiments (Table 2) further show that adding ChatTracker-B to OSTRack, TransT, and STARK yields consistent 1–3 AUC gains across LaSOT, TNL2K, and OTB-lang, confirming generality beyond the specific base trackers used in the main comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior vision-language trackers (JointNLT, CiteTracker, UVLTrack) that rely on manually annotated text or predefined dictionaries by instead leveraging MLLMs (GPT-4V, Gemini, LLaVA) to generate target descriptions. It repurposes the 'reflection' paradigm from LLM self-improvement literature (Self-Refine, RE3) into the tracking domain, using GVLM grounding feedback to iteratively refine MLLM prompts. The plug-and-play design also distinguishes it from prior VL trackers that require co-designed vision-language architectures, positioning ChatTracker as an orthogonal enhancement layer over existing visual trackers like MixFormer and ARTrack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ChatTracker demonstrates that MLLM-generated descriptions can outperform human annotations for tracking, suggesting a path toward reducing manual annotation burden in VL tracking datasets. However, key limitations remain: the method requires API access (or local MLLM deployment), does not update descriptions over time to handle appearance changes, and struggles with low-resolution targets. Open questions include whether temporal description updates can be made computationally feasible, whether the approach generalizes to multi-object or video-level tracking, and how to handle the knowledge gap between MLLM and downstream tracker without iterative refinement overhead.
