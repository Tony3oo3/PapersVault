## Problem

Unconditional image generation—the task of modeling data distributions without human-annotated labels—has long stagnated relative to its conditional counterpart, with the performance gap attributed to the absence of semantic information that labels provide. This limitation prevents generative models from fully exploiting the vast abundance of unlabeled data, a central goal in modern deep learning. The paper asks whether this gap can be closed without resorting to human annotations.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes standard Gaussian noise as its sole input and produces 256×256 RGB images, requiring no human labels at any stage. Internally, the noise is first mapped to a 256-dimensional self-supervised representation by a frozen MoCo v3 ViT encoder, which then conditions the image generator. The representation generator is a lightweight fully-connected diffusion model, and the image generator can be any modern conditional architecture (MAGE, ADM, DiT, or LDM) with its original label conditioning replaced by the generated representation.

## Method

RCG decomposes the complex task of modeling the full image distribution into two simpler sub-problems: modeling a compact low-dimensional representation distribution, and modeling the image distribution conditioned on that representation. A pre-trained self-supervised encoder (MoCo v3 ViT) maps images to a 256-dimensional representation space that is both simple enough to model unconditionally and rich in semantic content. A lightweight representation diffusion model (RDM) with 12 fully-connected residual blocks (1536 hidden dimension) trained via DDIM generates representations from Gaussian noise, and the image generator (e.g., MAGE) is trained to reconstruct images from masked tokens conditioned on the generated representation. Classifier-free guidance on the representation conditioning further boosts fidelity, and the framework is architecture-agnostic: the same RDM works with diffusion-based (ADM, DiT, LDM) and parallel-decoding (MAGE) generators, reducing FID by 51–82% across all tested backbones.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, RCG-G(MAGE-L) achieves a new state-of-the-art unconditional FID of 2.15 and IS of 253.4, reducing the previous best unconditional FID of 5.91 (RDM-IN†) by 64% relatively. Without guidance, RCG(MAGE-L) reaches FID 3.44 and IS 186.9. RCG also dramatically improves each underlying generator: reducing FID by 71% for LDM-8 (39.13→11.30), 76% for ADM (26.21→6.24), 82% for DiT-XL/2 (27.32→4.89), and 51% for MAGE-L (7.04→3.44). On CIFAR-10, RCG improves Improved DDPM from FID 3.29 to 2.62; on iNaturalist 2021, it improves MAGE-B from 8.64 to 4.49.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCG-G(MAGE-L) (FID 2.15) decisively outperforms all prior unconditional methods: BigGAN (38.61), ADM (26.21), MaskGIT (20.72), RCDM† (19.0), IC-GAN† (15.6), ADDP (8.9), MAGE-L (7.04), and the previous best RDM-IN† (5.91). Against leading class-conditional methods, RCG-G(MAGE-L) at FID 2.15 is competitive with DiT-XL/2 (2.27), VDM++ (2.12), and U-ViT-H/2-G (2.29), though slightly behind MDTV2-XL/2 (1.58) and DiffiT (1.73). In an apple-to-apple comparison (Table 4), RCG's unconditional generation actually outperforms the class-conditional versions of the same backbones: LDM-8 (11.30 vs 17.41), ADM (6.24 vs 10.94), and DiT-XL/2 (4.89 vs 9.62), suggesting self-supervised representations provide richer guidance than class labels alone. On CIFAR-10, RCG with class labels (FID 2.89) slightly trails RCG without labels (2.62), while on iNaturalist 2021 both RCG variants (4.49 unconditional, 4.55 conditional) substantially beat the MAGE-B baseline (8.64).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of self-supervised representation learning (MoCo v3, DINO) and modern generative models (diffusion, masked generative transformers), proposing a decomposition of unconditional generation into two sub-problems: generating a self-supervised representation via a lightweight diffusion model (RDM, built on DDIM), then generating an image conditioned on that representation. It departs from prior attempts to close the unconditional-conditional gap that relied on clustering (using cluster indices as pseudo-labels) or retrieval-augmented methods requiring ground-truth images at inference. The guidance mechanism is adapted from classifier-free guidance, but applied to representation conditioning rather than class labels, enabling guidance without any human annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RCG demonstrates that the long-standing performance gap between unconditional and conditional generation can be effectively bridged without any labels, with the striking finding that unconditional RCG outperforms the class-conditional versions of the same models (e.g., ADM: 6.24 vs. 10.94 FID). The modular design—where only the lightweight 63M-parameter RDM needs retraining for new conditioning tasks while the image generator remains frozen—suggests a practical path toward pre-training on large-scale unlabeled data and adapting to downstream tasks with minimal overhead. Open questions include whether this framework extends to other modalities (audio, 3D, video), whether the reliance on a strong pre-trained SSL encoder limits applicability to domains where such encoders are unavailable, and whether the representation space can support more fine-grained compositional control.
