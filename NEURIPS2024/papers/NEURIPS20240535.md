## Problem

The Forward-Backward Euler discretization of Wasserstein gradient flow lacks convergence guarantees when the objective is nonconvex along generalized geodesics, a setting that encompasses sampling from non-log-concave distributions and many Bayesian inference problems. Prior convergence results (e.g., Salim et al. 2020) require the potential to be convex, leaving the 'convex + concave' (difference-of-convex) structure entirely unaddressed in the Wasserstein space.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes an initial absolutely continuous probability measure in P_{2,abs}(R^d), a DC decomposition F = G − H of the potential (both convex with quadratic growth), a regularizer functional convex along generalized geodesics (e.g., negative entropy or interaction energy), and a step size η below a threshold η₀. It outputs a sequence of probability distributions; in the sampling instantiation, the JKO step is solved by optimizing an input-convex neural network (ICNN) parameterizing the optimal Monge map, yielding samples from the target distribution μ*(x) ∝ exp(−F(x)).

## Method

The paper proposes the semi Forward-Backward Euler, a subtle modification of the standard FB Euler that applies the forward (gradient) push-forward to the concave component −E_H via the map I + η∇H, and the backward JKO step to the convex composite E_G + H. This splitting is motivated by classical DC programming and is theoretically advantageous because the push-forward map I + η∇H is an optimal transport map (by Brenier's theorem, since H is convex), whereas the FB Euler's I − η∇F is generally non-optimal. The paper establishes a descent lemma, asymptotic convergence of cluster points to critical points, an O(N^{-1}) rate in the Wasserstein gradient mapping for the nonsmooth case, an O(N^{-1/2}) rate in Fréchet subdifferential distance under C² regularity of H, and—under a Lojasiewicz-type functional inequality—full sequence convergence to the global minimizer with explicit rates in both Wasserstein and KL distances.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with the main contributions being convergence guarantees for the proposed semi FB Euler scheme. In the general nonsmooth setting, the Wasserstein (sub)gradient mapping converges at rate O(N^{-1}) (Thm. 2). Under the additional assumption that H is C² with bounded Hessian, the distance to the Fréchet subdifferential converges at rate O(N^{-1/2}) (Thm. 3). Under a Lojasiewicz-type inequality with exponent θ ∈ (0, 1/2], both the objective gap and Wasserstein distance converge exponentially fast; for θ ∈ (1/2, 1), convergence is sublinear at rates O(n^{-1/(2θ-1)}) and O(n^{-(1-θ)/(2θ-1)}) respectively (Thms. 4–5). Empirically, on a Gaussian mixture target, semi FB Euler achieves KL divergence comparable to FB Euler, while on the relaxed von Mises-Fisher distribution (with non-differentiable log-density), FB Euler fails entirely and semi FB Euler produces valid samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares semi FB Euler against the standard FB Euler (Wibisono, COLT 2018) and the unadjusted Langevin algorithm (ULA) on two non-log-concave sampling targets. On the Gaussian mixture distribution, semi FB Euler matches FB Euler in KL divergence convergence speed (Figure 1b), confirming that the theoretical modification does not sacrifice practical performance. On the relaxed von Mises-Fisher distribution with a non-differentiable log-density, FB Euler fails to produce valid samples due to high curvature, while semi FB Euler successfully recovers the target density (Figure 1d). ULA is included as a reference horizontal line in the KL divergence plot, showing that both Euler-based methods outperform it in the final KL value. The key advantage of semi FB Euler is thus not a speedup but a correctness guarantee: it is the only method among those compared that has proven convergence in the non-geodesically-convex, possibly nonsmooth regime, and it is the only one that empirically succeeds on the harder non-smooth target.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Wasserstein gradient flow discretization framework of Wibisono [70] (FB Euler) and the convergence analysis of Salim et al. [65] (Wasserstein proximal gradient) from the geodesically convex setting to the non-geodesically-convex, difference-of-convex (DC) regime. The algorithmic design directly ports the forward-backward splitting principle from classical DC programming [60]—applying the forward step to the concave component and the backward (JKO) step to the convex component—into the Wasserstein geometry, yielding the 'semi FB Euler' scheme. The key departure from prior work is that the standard FB Euler, which applies the forward step to the full nonconvex potential E_{G-H}, lacks any convergence theory in this nonconvex setting, whereas the semi FB Euler's subtle re-splitting (forward on -E_H, backward on H+E_G) makes the push-forward step optimal via Brenier's theorem and unlocks a full convergence analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first convergence theory for non-geodesically-convex optimization in the Wasserstein space with DC structure, directly addressing the long-standing gap in non-log-concave sampling guarantees. The multi-regime convergence rates (O(N^{-1}) gradient mapping, O(N^{-1/2}) Fréchet stationarity, and fast rates under Lojasiewicz/LSI) provide a comprehensive toolkit, and the connection between LSI and the Lojasiewicz condition in Wasserstein space is a useful theoretical bridge. Open questions include: (1) whether the standard FB Euler can be shown to converge in the nonconvex setting, (2) extending to full DC programming where both components are general functionals (not just potential energies), and (3) adapting to different OT cost functions or submanifolds like Bures-Wasserstein space. Practical adoption is currently limited by the cubic-in-dimension cost of the ICNN-based JKO step.
