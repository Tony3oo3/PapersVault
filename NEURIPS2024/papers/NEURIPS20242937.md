## Problem

Existing Stable Diffusion-based Real-ISR methods require tens to hundreds of diffusion steps for inference and start from random Gaussian noise, introducing unwanted randomness into the restored output. This makes them computationally expensive and unreliable for restoration tasks where deterministic, faithful reconstruction is desired.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-quality RGB image (tested at 512×512 and 128×128) and automatically extracts a short degradation-aware text prompt from it via the DAPE module (no user-provided caption needed). The output is a single high-quality super-resolved RGB image produced in exactly one forward pass through the diffusion network, with no iterative denoising loop.

## Method

OSEDiff directly feeds the LQ image's latent encoding (via a LoRA-finetuned VAE encoder) into the pre-trained SD UNet at the final diffusion timestep T, performing a single denoising step without any random noise injection. The UNet is adapted with rank-4 LoRA layers while the VAE decoder remains frozen to keep the output in the original latent space. For regularization, the paper applies Variational Score Distillation (VSD) in latent space: a frozen pre-trained SD and a LoRA-finetuned copy serve as two score estimators whose difference provides a KL-divergence gradient that aligns the one-step output distribution with natural HQ image priors. The total training objective combines MSE + LPIPS data loss with the VSD regularization loss, and the finetuned regularizer is updated separately with a standard diffusion denoising loss on the generator's outputs.

## Results

On the DrealSR benchmark, OSEDiff (1 step) achieves the best LPIPS (0.2968), DISTS (0.2165), FID (135.30), SSIM (0.7835), and CLIPIQA (0.6963) among all compared methods, outperforming multi-step baselines like SeeSR (50 steps) and StableSR (200 steps). On RealSR, OSEDiff similarly leads in LPIPS (0.2921), DISTS (0.2128), FID (123.49), and CLIPIQA (0.6693). In terms of efficiency, OSEDiff requires only 0.11 s inference time and 8.5M trainable parameters, making it ~105× faster than StableSR (11.50 s, 150M params) and ~39× faster than SeeSR (4.30 s, 749.9M params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSEDiff (1 step) achieves the best LPIPS, DISTS, FID, and CLIPIQA scores across all three benchmarks (DIV2K-Val, DrealSR, RealSR), beating multi-step SD-based methods like SeeSR (50 steps), StableSR (200 steps), PASD (20 steps), and DiffBIR (50 steps) on these perceptual and distribution-alignment metrics. On DrealSR, OSEDiff also leads in SSIM (0.7835 vs. SeeSR's 0.7691) and FID (135.30 vs. SeeSR's 147.39). However, OSEDiff trails ResShift and SinSR on PSNR (e.g., 27.92 vs. 28.46 on DrealSR) because those methods train a diffusion model from scratch specifically for restoration rather than fine-tuning a pre-trained T2I model. OSEDiff also lags behind SeeSR and PASD on no-reference metrics like NIQE, MUSIQ, and MANIQA, which the authors attribute to multi-step methods generating richer (sometimes excessive) details that no-reference metrics favor. In terms of efficiency, OSEDiff uses only 8.5M trainable parameters (vs. 150M for StableSR, 749.9M for SeeSR) and 0.11 s inference time (vs. 11.50 s for StableSR, 4.30 s for SeeSR), yielding a >100× speedup over StableSR while matching or exceeding its quality on most perceptual metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSEDiff builds directly on the Stable Diffusion architecture (VAE encoder, UNet, VAE decoder) and repurposes it for Real-ISR by replacing the conventional random-noise starting point with the LQ image itself, reducing the diffusion process from 20–200 steps to a single forward pass. For regularization, it adapts Variational Score Distillation (VSD) from text-to-3D generation and follows DMD's formulation to compute the KL-divergence loss in latent space rather than image space, avoiding costly encode/decode cycles. The DAPE prompt extractor from SeeSR is adopted to provide text conditioning, and LoRA is used for parameter-efficient fine-tuning of both the VAE encoder and the UNet, keeping trainable parameters to just 8.5M compared to 150–750M in prior SD-based Real-ISR methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100×+ inference speedup over multi-step methods like StableSR while maintaining comparable perceptual quality makes diffusion-based Real-ISR practically deployable in real-time or resource-constrained settings, which was previously infeasible. The conceptual insight that the LQ image already encodes sufficient information to serve as the diffusion starting point—eliminating the randomness and uncertainty of Gaussian noise sampling—is a clean simplification that could inform other inverse-problem applications of diffusion models. Open questions remain around fine-scale structure reconstruction (e.g., small text) and whether the one-step formulation can be further improved in detail generation, as acknowledged by the authors.
