## Problem

While noisy questions in in-context learning have been studied, the impact of noisy rationales—irrelevant or factually inaccurate reasoning steps within chain-of-thought demonstrations—remains unexplored. This paper shows that current LLMs are severely degraded by such noise (GPT-3.5 drops up to 40.4% accuracy with inaccurate rationales) and that existing robust methods like self-correction and self-consistency offer only limited protection, with some even performing worse than the base model.

## Input / Output

The method takes K noisy CoT prompting examples (each with a question, noisy rationale, and correct answer), one clean CoT demonstration serving as the minimal external supervision, and a test question. It produces denoised rationales through contrastive rephrasing and a final answer via majority voting over D diverse reasoning paths. The clean demonstration is the only additional information required, making the approach practical for real-world settings where ground-truth test answers or noise positions are unavailable.

## Method

CD-CoT (Contrastive Denoising with noisy CoT) follows an exploration-then-exploitation principle across input and output spaces. In the input space, it rephrases each noisy rationale by contrasting it with the clean demonstration (generating N candidates per example), then selects M rephrased examples whose answers remain unchanged to form a refined candidate pool. In the output space, it constructs M distinct K-shot inputs by allocating rephrased rationales across inputs, concatenates each with the clean example and test question, and generates D total answers via repeated LLM reasoning. The final answer is determined by majority vote over all D answers. A theoretical analysis based on in-context learning distinguishability shows that noisy demonstrations reduce the model's ability to match the clean-prompt distribution, which CD-CoT mitigates through its contrastive rephrasing step.

## Results

CD-CoT achieves an average improvement of 17.8% in accuracy over the base model under noisy rationale settings across all five NoRa sub-tasks. On the most challenging Math Base-9 task with inaccurate rationales, CD-CoT attains 48.4% average accuracy (vs. 13.1% for the base model), with only a 7.0% decline from its clean-rationale accuracy of 55.4%, compared to 28.8% and 26.3% declines for SCO and BT respectively. CD-CoT outperforms SCO in 20 out of 30 evaluation settings and surpasses both BT and CC in all 30 settings, while requiring only a single clean demonstration as external supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CD-CoT is compared against three methods requiring external supervision (SCO, BT, CC) and five intrinsic robustness methods (ISC, SP, SM, SD, SC). Against SCO [29], which uses ground-truth test answers (unavailable in practice), CD-CoT wins in 20 of 30 settings, trailing only on Symbolic-Longer and Commonsense where SCO's oracle access provides a slight edge. Against BT [81], which reveals the position of the first noisy thought, CD-CoT surpasses it in all 30 settings, with particularly large margins on Math Base-9 (53.7% vs. 20.9% inaccurate avg) and Commonsense (49.7% vs. 12.8%). Against CC [9], which also uses a clean demonstration but performs direct reasoning without explicit denoising, CD-CoT wins all 30 settings, gaining up to 20.5% on Math Base-9 inaccurate. Among intrinsic methods, self-consistency (SC [83]) provides the best noise resilience but still lags CD-CoT by 11–35% on mathematical tasks; self-correction methods (ISC, SP) and self-denoising (SD) often perform worse than the base model on noisy rationales, confirming that intrinsic denoising is insufficient without external supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by shifting the focus from the well-studied noisy questions (Noisy-Q) problem in in-context learning to the underexplored noisy rationales (Noisy-R) problem, where the noise resides in the intermediate reasoning steps of chain-of-thought demonstrations rather than in the questions or answers themselves. Conceptually, it repurposes contrastive learning principles (comparing a noisy example against a clean one) for rationale denoising, combined with self-consistency-style diverse sampling and voting. The method builds on the observation from prior work that LLMs struggle with self-correction without external feedback (Huang et al., 2024; Tyen et al., 2023), but departs by requiring only a single clean CoT demonstration rather than oracle answers or noise position labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NoRa dataset (26,391 questions across mathematical, symbolic, and commonsense reasoning) provides the first systematic benchmark for evaluating LLM robustness to noisy rationales, a practically relevant scenario given that rationales come from crowd-sourced, dialogue, or machine-generated sources. The finding that self-correction methods perform worse than the base model and that self-consistency methods can degenerate to near 0% on harder tasks underscores a fundamental limitation of current LLMs' intrinsic denoising ability. Key limitations include dependence on human-annotated clean rationales, evaluation primarily on GPT-3.5 with limited cross-model generalization, and restriction to text-only reasoning. Open questions include self-supervised denoising variants, extension to inductive reasoning and multi-modal settings, and whether knowledge-enhanced or retrieval-augmented frameworks can reduce the supervision requirement.
