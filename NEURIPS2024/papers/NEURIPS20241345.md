## Problem

Native Transformers were designed for variable-length sequences and lack inductive bias for fixed-length inputs where each token carries a specific structural meaning (e.g., a particular IMU or time frame). In sparse inertial pose estimation, prior methods model only temporal dependencies while neglecting the spatial correlations among body joints, and even Transformer-based approaches produce unacceptable jitter and inaccurate postures because they fail to explicitly exploit the inherent spatial and temporal structure of the fixed-length IMU sequences.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 12-dimensional readings (3D linear acceleration + 9-element rotation matrix) from 6 fixed IMUs (left/right forearms, left/right lower legs, head, pelvis) over a window of T=30 past frames, forming a tensor of shape R^{30×6×12}. It outputs full-body joint rotations in 6D representation for all SMPL skeleton joints across the T frames, with only the last frame used at inference for real-time prediction. The fixed spatial dimension (N=6) and temporal dimension (T=30) are central to the method's design, as the structural matrices are sized accordingly.

## Method

The core contribution is a Sequence Structure Module (SSM) that modulates transformer input features via a structural matrix S independent of the input content: X̃ = MLP(LN(SX)). Two SSM variants are deployed in a spatial-temporal transformer framework: SSM-S injects spatial structure (correlation of joint rotations computed from AMASS, plus a learnable offset P) before the spatial transformer that models inter-IMU correlations within a frame, while SSM-T injects a temporal smoothness prior (a linearly decaying distance matrix with hyperparameter σ=10) before the temporal transformer that captures inter-frame dependencies. The key distinction from self-attention is that S's elements are input-independent, encoding structural priors universally applicable to all sequences rather than content-dependent pairwise relationships. The spatial structure uses an Explicit-Implicit Hybrid (EIHS) formulation allowing data-driven adaptation to different motion distributions, while the temporal structure uses a purely Explicit (ES) formulation based on the smoothness prior that adjacent frames are more correlated than distant ones.

## Results

On DIP-IMU, the proposed method achieves 7.90° SIP error, 6.06° angular error, 3.12 cm positional error, 3.78 cm mesh error, and 0.07 jitter, outperforming the runner-up (DynaIP) by 44% on SIP error and 13% on angular error. On TotalCapture, it reaches 7.00° SIP error, 6.82° angular error, 3.36 cm positional error, 4.00 cm mesh error, and 0.09 jitter, reducing angular error by 38% over DynaIP and achieving the lowest jitter among all methods. On the Xsens-skeleton datasets (AnDy and CIP), the method similarly leads on SIP error (4.56° and 8.14°) and positional error (1.73 cm and 2.57 cm), though DynaIP retains a slight edge on angular error for CIP (4.54° vs. 5.49°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on every metric across both SMPL-skeleton datasets (DIP-IMU and TotalCapture). The most dramatic gains are in SIP error: 7.90° on DIP-IMU vs. 13.71° (PNP, the closest competitor) and 7.00° on TotalCapture vs. 10.89° (PNP), representing 42–49% improvements over the runner-up. On angular error, the method achieves 6.06° (DIP-IMU) and 6.82° (TotalCapture), beating DynaIP (7.00°/11.06°) and PNP (8.75°/10.45°) by 13% and 38% respectively. Jitter is also lowest for the proposed method (0.07 and 0.09), roughly 2–3× lower than the next-best (PIP at 0.14/0.18), attributed to the temporal structure module SSM-T. On the Xsens-skeleton datasets (AnDy, CIP), the method leads on SIP error and positional error but DynaIP retains a narrow angular-error advantage on CIP (4.54° vs. 5.49°), suggesting the spatial structure prior transfers less cleanly to industrial motion distributions. The earliest baseline, DIP [18], is outperformed by a wide margin on all metrics, reflecting the gap between the original RNN-based approach and the current spatial-temporal transformer with structural modulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of sparse inertial pose estimation from DIP-IMU (2018) through TransPose, TIP, PIP, and DynaIP, all of which model only the temporal dimension using RNNs or Transformers. It departs from this lineage in two ways: (1) introducing a spatial-temporal Transformer framework that independently models inter-joint correlations within a frame, and (2) proposing the Sequence Structure Module (SSM), which injects a content-independent structural matrix into input features before the Transformer encoder. The SSM conceptually connects to positional encoding modifications in Transformer variants (Transformer-XL, Informer, etc.) but targets a different gap: not long-range efficiency but the absence of inductive bias for fixed-length sequences with known token semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SSM is a simple, general-purpose module that could transfer to any fixed-length sequence task (e.g., multi-sensor fusion, structured time-series forecasting), making it potentially broadly useful beyond inertial pose estimation. The clean ablation decomposition—SSM-S primarily reduces angular error while SSM-T primarily reduces jitter—gives practitioners a practical knob for trading off accuracy vs. steadiness. Key limitations include the absence of global translation (IMU drift) and the task-specific nature of explicit structure construction, leaving open how to automate or generalize S_E design across domains.
