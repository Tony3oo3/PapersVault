## Problem

Existing sign language translation (SLT) systems are constrained to narrow domains and a handful of sign languages, showing limited effectiveness in open-domain settings. This paper asks whether the frontier of open-domain SLT can be pushed forward by systematically scaling pretraining data, model size, and the number of translation directions, and whether cross-lingual and cross-modal knowledge transfer from machine translation can enable zero-shot SLT.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20243620/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a sequence of 255-dimensional MediaPipe Holistic landmark vectors (one per frame, with every second frame discarded) concatenated with a task-specific prompt containing control tokens for the sign language, target spoken language, and task type. The decoder autoregressively generates the target-language text token by token, with maximum input/output lengths of 512 tokens. Training operates at the clip level: a random N-second window is sampled from a sign video, and the model predicts all caption texts and their start/end timestamps fully contained within that clip.

## Method

The approach initializes (m/By)T5 encoder-decoder models (Base, Large, XL) and performs large-scale multi-task SLT pretraining by mixing three data sources: ~6,600 hours of noisy multilingual YouTube sign videos, parallel MT corpora from MADLAD-400 (up to 41 spoken languages), and augmented SLT data where target captions are translated into additional languages via off-the-shelf MT models. Tasks are distinguished by prompt-level control tokens (SLT, alignment, MT, augmented SLT) and trained jointly with maximum likelihood, with MT sampled at a high probability (p_mt=0.9) to maximize cross-modal transfer. A key finding is that ByT5 consistently outperforms T5 and mT5 backbones, likely due to its byte-level spelling capability reducing the input-length gap between text and landmark sequences, and that data augmentation plus model scaling to XL are both necessary to accommodate 40+ target languages. After pretraining, models are finetuned on downstream benchmarks using only the SLT task on aligned (video segment, translation) pairs.

## Results

The best model (ByT5 XL with augmented SLT + MT-Large pretraining, model 8) achieves an average BLEURT of 49.60 across five downstream benchmarks, substantially surpassing the previous state-of-the-art. On How2Sign (ASL→En), it reaches 55.69 BLEURT vs. the prior SOTA of 50.80; on WMT23 DSGS SS it reaches 57.96 vs. 37.70; and on WMT23 LIS-CH it reaches 51.94 vs. 25.20. The multilingual finetuning variant (model 9) achieves 48.38 average BLEURT while simultaneously handling all five sign languages in a single model. Pretraining alone (without finetuning) on FLEURS-ASL#0 shows ByT5 Base reaching 52.48 BLEURT for ASL→En, and data augmentation + ByT5 XL pushes zero-shot ASL-to-42-language average BLEURT to 48.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's best model (8) outperforms all four previous SOTA baselines by wide margins across every benchmark. On How2Sign, it surpasses Tanzer [43] (50.80) by +4.89 BLEURT to reach 55.69. On WMT23 SRF (DSGS), it beats Müller et al. [28] (24.60) by +9.34 BLEURT to reach 33.94. On WMT23 LIS-CH and LSF-CH, it exceeds Tanzer & Zhang [44] by +26.74 and +22.34 BLEURT respectively (51.94 vs. 25.20; 41.14 vs. 18.80). On WMT23 SS, it surpasses the prior best (37.70) by +20.26 BLEURT to reach 57.96. Even the vanilla ByT5 Base without any SLT pretraining (model 1, avg 21.49) already exceeds the previous SOTA on several benchmarks, underscoring the strength of the ByT5 backbone. The multilingual finetuning model (9) also beats all prior SOTA on nearly all benchmarks while using a single model for all five sign languages, though it trails the bilingual model (8) by 1.22 BLEURT on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified encoder-decoder SLT framework established by YouTube-ASL [46], FLEURS-ASL [42], YouTube-SL-25 [44], and Fingerspelling [43], and builds directly on SLTUNet [58], which first demonstrated that jointly training SLT with machine translation data enables positive knowledge transfer. The key departure is a systematic scaling study: the authors scale pretraining data from ~2,800 hours of ASL to ~6,600 hours of multilingual sign language video, expand from a single sign language to up to 80 sign/spoken languages, and introduce data augmentation via off-the-shelf MT models to create synthetic multiway SLT pairs. They also shift the backbone from T5/mT5 to ByT5 (byte-level), which they find consistently outperforms token-based variants for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that data, model, and language scaling together are the primary levers for improving open-domain, gloss-free SLT, setting new SOTA across five benchmarks and five sign languages. However, the authors are candid that quality remains 'far from usable,' and the multilingual finetuning setting still underperforms bilingual finetuning due to cross-lingual interference. Open questions include whether the modality gap can be further closed with vision pretraining or multimodal approaches, whether standardized multilingual SLT benchmarks exist to properly evaluate the 80-language capability, and how to balance modeling capacity across many sign languages in a single model without negative transfer.
