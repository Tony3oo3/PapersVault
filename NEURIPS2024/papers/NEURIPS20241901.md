## Problem

Current RAG systems encode passages in isolation, making them unable to integrate knowledge across passage boundaries for tasks like multi-hop QA or path-finding questions where relevant information is scattered across documents. While iterative methods like IRCoT partially address this, they are 10–30× more expensive and 6–13× slower at inference, and still fail on questions requiring simultaneous exploration of many candidate paths rather than following a single chain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20241901/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Offline, the method consumes a corpus of text passages and produces a schemaless knowledge graph of noun-phrase nodes, OpenIE relation edges, and dense-encoder synonymy edges, along with a node-to-passage incidence matrix. Online, a single natural-language query is processed to yield a ranked list of passages; no iterative retrieval or generation loop is required. The KG is incrementally updatable—new passages simply add nodes and edges without re-encoding existing content.

## Method

HippoRAG mirrors the hippocampal memory indexing theory: an LLM (GPT-3.5 or Llama-3.1) acts as the neocortex, performing zero-shot OpenIE to extract noun-phrase triples from each passage; a dense retrieval encoder (Contriever or ColBERTv2) acts as the parahippocampal region, adding synonymy edges between semantically similar nodes; and the resulting KG serves as the hippocampal index. At query time, the LLM extracts named entities, the encoder links them to KG nodes, and Personalized PageRank (PPR) with those nodes as seeds propagates probability mass across the graph in a single step, effectively performing multi-hop reasoning without iteration. A node-specificity term (inverse document frequency computed locally per node) modulates seed probabilities before PPR to down-weight overly common entities. Passage scores are obtained by aggregating post-PPR node probabilities over the node-to-passage incidence matrix, yielding a retrieval ranking that outperforms iterative baselines while being an order of magnitude cheaper.

## Results

On single-step retrieval, HippoRAG(ColBERTv2) achieves an average R@2 of 57.4 and R@5 of 72.9 across MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and HotpotQA, outperforming the strongest baseline ColBERTv2 (53.9 / 65.6) by 3.5 and 7.3 points respectively. The gains are most dramatic on 2WikiMultiHopQA, where HippoRAG improves R@2 by 11.5 points (70.7 vs. 59.2) and R@5 by 20.9 points (89.1 vs. 68.2). On the less challenging HotpotQA, HippoRAG is slightly below ColBERTv2 (60.5 vs. 64.7 R@2), attributed to lower knowledge-integration requirements. In the multi-step setting, IRCoT+HippoRAG(ColBERTv2) reaches 62.7 R@2 and 78.2 R@5 on average, while single-step HippoRAG matches or exceeds IRCoT(ColBERTv2) (57.9 / 70.0) at 10–30× lower cost and 6–13× lower latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HippoRAG decisively outperforms all single-step retrieval baselines on the two harder multi-hop datasets: on 2WikiMultiHopQA it gains +11.5 R@2 over ColBERTv2 (70.7 vs. 59.2) and +12.3 R@2 over GTR (70.7 vs. 60.2), and on MuSiQue it leads ColBERTv2 by +3.0 R@2 (40.9 vs. 37.9). On HotpotQA, which requires less cross-passage integration, HippoRAG trails ColBERTv2 by 4.2 R@2 (60.5 vs. 64.7) and GTR by 1.1 R@2, confirming that its advantage is specific to knowledge-integration-heavy tasks. Against the LLM-augmented baselines RAPTOR and Propositionizer, HippoRAG wins on every dataset by 3–25 points in R@2. In the multi-step setting, single-step HippoRAG matches or exceeds IRCoT(ColBERTv2) on MuSiQue (40.9 vs. 41.7 R@2) and 2Wiki (70.7 vs. 64.1 R@2) while being 10–30× cheaper ($0.10 vs. $1–3 per 1,000 queries) and 6–13× faster (3 min vs. 20–40 min). Combining HippoRAG as the retriever inside IRCoT yields further complementary gains of up to +4 R@5 on MuSiQue and +18 R@5 on 2Wiki over IRCoT alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

HippoRAG sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) RAG as long-term memory for LLMs, where it departs from the standard encode-each-passage-in-isolation paradigm by building a global schemaless knowledge graph via LLM-driven OpenIE; (2) graph-augmented retrieval, where unlike prior work that traverses Wikipedia hyperlink graphs with supervised re-rankers, it constructs KGs from scratch with LLMs and performs unsupervised multi-hop retrieval via Personalized PageRank; and (3) iterative multi-step retrieval (IRCoT), which it matches or exceeds in a single retrieval step. The neurobiological framing from hippocampal memory indexing theory (Teyler & Discenna, 1986) provides the architectural motivation—neocortex as LLM, hippocampus as KG index, PHR as retrieval encoders—but the core algorithmic contribution is the PPR-based graph search over an LLM-built KG, which is a repurposing of a well-known graph algorithm in a new retrieval context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: HippoRAG achieves comparable or better retrieval than iterative methods like IRCoT while being 10–30× cheaper and 6–13× faster in online retrieval, making multi-hop retrieval viable for high-throughput serving. The introduction of path-finding multi-hop questions (finding one entity among many candidates by integrating disjoint attributes) identifies a retrieval capability gap that no prior single-step or iterative method addresses. Key open questions include scalability of the KG index beyond benchmark-scale corpora, the 48% error rate attributable to NER limitations, and whether relation-guided graph traversal or component fine-tuning can close the remaining gap. The continuously updating nature (adding edges without re-summarizing) also positions it favorably against summarization-based approaches like RAPTOR and GraphRAG for dynamic knowledge bases.
