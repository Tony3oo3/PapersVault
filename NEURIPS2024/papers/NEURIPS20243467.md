## Problem

Interpretability studies measure component importance by ablating that component and comparing model performance, but there is no consensus on how to set the replacement value, and prior methods (zero, mean, resample, counterfactual) all introduce 'spoofing' effects where the replacement value misleads downstream computation and inflates the measured importance gap. The paper formalizes the question 'what is the best performance the model could have achieved without component A?' and shows it has a unique, computable answer.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained transformer model, a subtask distribution of input-label pairs (e.g., IOI prompts, Greater-Than prompts, factual recall prompts), and a target component at varying granularity (single neuron, attention head, MLP block, or a specific computational edge). It produces an ablation loss gap Δ_opt as the importance score, and downstream applications yield sparse circuits (edge subsets with associated ablation constants), AIE localization scores per layer, or latent logit predictions. Operationally, OA requires a short SGD optimization to find the optimal constant a* for each component, initialized at the subtask mean, which is a per-component training step applied to the ablation value rather than the model weights.

## Method

Optimal ablation (OA) replaces a component's value with a constant a* defined as argmin_a E_{X~D} L(M_A(X,a), M(X)), and the paper proves (Proposition 2.3) that OA yields the lowest ablation loss gap among all total ablation methods because it minimizes the spoofing contribution to performance degradation while preserving the full deletion effect. For circuit discovery, the authors propose UGS (Uniform Gradient Sampling), a new search algorithm that jointly optimizes Bernoulli edge-inclusion probabilities and per-vertex ablation constants via SGD with a continuous sparsity regularizer, achieving Pareto dominance over ACDC and EAP. For factual recall, OA replaces Gaussian noise in causal tracing (yielding OAT), producing much sharper localization with single-layer AIEs exceeding 8% versus near-uniform small values under GNT. For latent prediction, OCA lens ablates attention layers i+1 through N with OA constants, leveraging the model's own MLP layers as a nonlinear translation between residual stream activations with O(Nd_model) parameters versus O(d_model²) for tuned lens, achieving lower prediction loss and better causal faithfulness.

## Results

On the IOI subtask with GPT-2-small, optimal ablation (OA) reduces the median ablation loss gap to 11.1% of zero ablation, 33.0% of mean ablation, and 17.7% of resample ablation, while achieving the highest rank correlation with counterfactual ablation (0.907). For circuit discovery, UGS with OA identifies a 385-edge IOI circuit achieving Δ_CF = 0.220 (52% fewer edges than the smallest comparable ACDC circuit), and at 1,000 edges OA enables 32% lower Δ than CF, 62% lower than mean, and 88% lower than resample ablation. For factual recall on GPT-2-XL, OAT localizes components with peak AIE of 42.6% (vs. 20.2% for GNT) while suppressing spurious signals in irrelevant layers to 1.7% (vs. 7.0% for GNT). OCA lens achieves predictive loss below 0.01 by layer 35 of GPT-2-XL, a threshold tuned lens never reaches, and increases elicitation accuracy boost on 12/15 classification datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OA dominates all subtask-agnostic ablation baselines: it reduces the median ablation gap to 11.1% of zero ablation, 33.0% of mean ablation, and 17.7% of resample ablation on IOI, indicating that prior methods' measured importance is largely driven by spoofing rather than true deletion. Among subtask-specific methods, OA achieves the highest rank correlation with counterfactual ablation (0.907 vs. 0.833 for CF-mean), and its Δ_opt is 88.9% of Δ_CF in median, confirming it captures the same deletion signal without requiring hand-crafted counterfactual mappings. For circuit discovery, UGS Pareto-dominates both ACDC and EAP across all ablation methods on IOI and Greater-Than, finding circuits with 52% fewer edges at comparable loss or 48% lower loss at comparable size. In factual recall, OAT outperforms GNT (Gaussian noise tracing) by producing sharper localization: peak AIE doubles (42.6% vs. 20.2%) while irrelevant-layer AIE drops by 4× (1.7% vs. 7.0%), and OAT can resolve single-layer contributions that GNT cannot. For latent prediction, OCA lens beats tuned lens on both predictive loss (reaching <0.01 by layer 35 vs. never reaching it) and both causal faithfulness metrics (magnitude correlation and direction similarity) across all layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces optimal ablation (OA) as a principled replacement for the fragmented landscape of ad-hoc ablation methods (zero, mean, resample, counterfactual) used to measure component importance in transformer interpretability. Conceptually, OA is positioned as the canonical total ablation method: it learns a constant replacement value that minimizes expected loss, provably yielding the lowest ablation gap among all total ablation methods (Proposition 2.3). For circuit discovery, the paper adapts the HardConcrete sampling framework from Louizos et al. (2018) to edge-level pruning and proposes a new search algorithm (UGS) that jointly optimizes circuit selection and ablation constants. For factual recall and latent prediction, OA directly replaces the Gaussian-noise intervention of Meng et al. (2022) and the linear tuned lens of Belrose et al. (2023), respectively, while retaining the same downstream evaluation protocols.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that Δ_opt is only ~11% of Δ_zero for median components on IOI suggests that widely-used ablation methods have been systematically overestimating component importance by 5–9×, which could invalidate conclusions in a large body of prior interpretability work. OA's subtask-agnostic nature (no need to engineer counterfactual mappings π) makes it broadly applicable, and its demonstrated improvements across circuit discovery, factual recall localization, and latent prediction suggest it could become a standard tool in the interpretability toolkit. Open questions include whether OA's advantage persists at frontier-model scale, whether the circuits it discovers elicit previously dormant mechanisms rather than the 'true' computational path, and whether improved localization translates to better surgical model edits.
