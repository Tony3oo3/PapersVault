## Problem

LoRA adapters, when fused into a base model, modify the entire weight tensor, making rapid adapter switching infeasible on memory-constrained edge devices, while unfused inference incurs up to 30% additional latency. Additionally, naively merging multiple LoRA adapters causes concept loss where one or more styles are lost. The paper asks whether a fundamentally different, sparser adaptation paradigm could simultaneously enable zero-overhead inference, rapid switching, and reduced concept loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/NEURIPS20240438/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For vision tasks, the method takes text prompts (e.g., 'a bird in paintings style with blue fire') and produces generated images via Stable Diffusion or SDXL; for language tasks, it takes commonsense reasoning questions and produces text completions via LLaMA/LLaMA-2. The adapter artifact itself is not a separate low-rank matrix but a set of sparse weight values paired with their indices into the base model (only 1–2% of parameters), which are loaded directly onto the pretrained weights via a scatter operation rather than a matrix multiplication fusion step.

## Method

SHiRA trains by applying a static sparse mask (98–99% zeros) to gradients during backpropagation, so that only a small subset of the original pretrained weights are updated—no new parameters are added to the forward pass. Five mask strategies are explored: structured (rows/columns + diagonal, yielding a rank-1 + diagonal adapter), random, weight-magnitude (top-K by |W|), gradient-magnitude (top-K by |∇L|), and SNIP (top-K by |W·∇L|). Because the adapter is high-rank yet extremely sparse, it can be stored as sparse values + indices and loaded onto the base model via scatter_op, achieving 5–16× faster switching than LoRA fusion on CPU. Theoretically, the paper shows that non-overlapping SHiRA-Struct adapters are nearly orthogonal (AWOR bounded by the sum of individual sparsities), which empirically reduces concept loss in multi-adapter fusion. A PEFT-based implementation retains only the sparse trainable parameters in the optimizer, training at nearly LoRA speed while consuming 16% less peak GPU memory.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B commonsense reasoning (8 benchmarks), SHiRA-SNIP achieves 77.4% average accuracy, outperforming LoRA (74.7%) by 2.7% while modifying only 1% of base parameters versus LoRA's 66.72%. On LLaMA2-7B, SHiRA-SNIP reaches 79.28% vs. LoRA's 77.61% (+1.67%). For vision style transfer on Stable Diffusion, SHiRA-Struct achieves 31.2 HPSv2 on Paintings vs. LoRA's 24.7, and 34.2 on Bluefire vs. LoRA's 32.6. In multi-adapter fusion on LLMs, SHiRA-WM-Overlap achieves 74.02% average vs. LoRA's 67.33% (+6.69%), with only 4.43% accuracy drop versus LoRA's 11.08% drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA, SHiRA consistently wins: on LLaMA-7B commonsense reasoning, SHiRA-SNIP outperforms LoRA by 2.7% average accuracy (77.4% vs. 74.7%) while changing only 1% of parameters versus LoRA's 66.72%. On vision (HPSv2), SHiRA-Struct beats LoRA by 6.5 points on Paintings (31.2 vs. 24.7) and 1.6 on Bluefire (34.2 vs. 32.6). In multi-adapter fusion, SHiRA-WM-Overlap outperforms multi-LoRA by 6.69% average accuracy (74.02% vs. 67.33%) with less than half the accuracy drop. Against DoRA, SHiRA-WM-DoRA achieves within 0.3% of DoRA on LLaMA-7B (77.8% vs. 78.1%) while changing only 1% of parameters at inference instead of 66.72%, and SHiRA-SNIP is within 0.4% of DoRA on LLaMA2-7B (79.28% vs. 79.68%). Against SoRA on GLUE, SHiRA matches SoRA's accuracy (88.29% vs. 88.34%) while using a 30% smaller adapter (636K vs. 910K parameters), and unlike SoRA, SHiRA enables rapid adapter switching.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHiRA departs from the dominant LoRA paradigm (which adds low-rank projection matrices to the forward pass) by instead directly finetuning a sparse subset (1-2%) of the base model's existing weights via gradient masking, making it a high-rank but highly sparse adapter. Conceptually, it revives pre-LoRA partial finetuning ideas (fixed sparse masks, weight-magnitude selection) and the SNIP pruning metric, but makes them practical for modern LLMs and diffusion models through a memory-efficient PEFT implementation that trains at LoRA speed with 16% lower peak GPU memory. The paper positions SHiRA as orthogonal to advanced LoRA variants like DoRA (demonstrating SHiRA-WM-DoRA combinations) and distinct from the concurrent SpIEL work by using static masks that require no custom GPU kernels, while also providing the first theoretical analysis of how sparsity induces near-orthogonality between adapters to reduce concept loss in multi-adapter fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SHiRA addresses a critical deployment bottleneck: on edge/mobile devices, LoRA fusion modifies 66%+ of weights (prohibiting rapid switching) while unfused LoRA incurs up to 30% inference latency overhead; SHiRA overwrites only 1-2% of weights via scatter operations, achieving 5-16× faster adapter switching on CPU with zero inference overhead. The finding that basic masking heuristics (SNIP, weight magnitude) outperform LoRA across both vision and language domains challenges the field's assumption that low-rank structure is necessary for effective adaptation. Open questions include optimal mask construction for specific tasks (the paper notes non-overlapping masks sacrifice single-adapter accuracy), hardware-software co-design for true mobile deployment, and whether subspace-level orthogonality (beyond weight-level) further improves multi-adapter fusion.
