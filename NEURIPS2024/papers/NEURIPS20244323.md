## Problem

Existing graph anomaly detection methods are specialized to a single object level (node, edge, or graph) and ignore the inherent correlations across levels—for example, a fraudulent transaction involves both an abnormal account and its surrounding community. No prior model jointly detects anomalies at multiple graph levels, and existing multi-task graph learning approaches (prompt-based or LLM-based) either require text attributes unavailable in GAD settings or use sampling strategies that erase critical anomaly information.

## Input / Output

The method accepts an attributed graph with anomaly labels at any arbitrary subset of node, edge, and graph levels (not all three required simultaneously), enabling flexible training and testing configurations. For multi-dimensional node features, dimensions are normalized and combined via 1-norm to produce a scalar composite feature used by the sampler. The model outputs three independent anomaly probability scores (p^N, p^E, p^G) per object, computed through separate MLP heads after the GraphStitch fusion layer.

## Method

UniGAD unifies multi-level GAD through two key modules. First, the MRQSampler converts node- and edge-level tasks into graph-level tasks by selecting, from a k-hop rooted subtree, the connected subgraph that maximizes the Rayleigh quotient (accumulated spectral energy), solved exactly via a dynamic programming algorithm with O(N log N) complexity and a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2) that the selected subgraph contains the maximum anomaly information. Second, the GraphStitch Network trains separate but identically structured GNN encoders for each level and fuses their outputs through a learnable cross-level mixing matrix (analogous to cross-stitch units in CV), with Gradient Surgery projecting conflicting multi-task gradients onto each other's normal planes to prevent interference. A shared GNN backbone (GCN or BWGNN) pre-trained via GraphMAE provides generalized node representations, and weighted cross-entropy loss with adaptive per-level weights balances the multi-level training objective.

## Results

UniGAD achieves state-of-the-art AUROC across nearly all 14 GAD datasets in both unified node+edge and node+graph settings. In the node+edge setting (Table 2), UniGAD-BWG attains the best combined performance, e.g., 96.49/94.32 on T-Finance (vs. best single-level baselines 96.03/92.27) and 99.07/99.10 on Weibo. In the node+graph setting (Table 3), UniGAD-GCN reaches 99.75/94.29 on BM-MN and 99.99/99.61 on MNIST0, while UniGAD-BWG achieves 99.99/99.98 on MNIST1. Multi-task prompt baselines (GraphPrompt-U, All-in-One-U) perform near random (~50% AUROC) or time out (OOT) on large datasets like T-Finance and T-Group.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniGAD outperforms all 9 node-level GNN baselines (GCN, GIN, GraphSAGE, SGC, GAT, BernNet, PNA, AMNet, BWGNN) on the majority of datasets; for example, on T-Finance, UniGAD-BWG achieves 96.49 AUROC vs. the best single-level baseline BWGNN at 96.03, and on Weibo, UniGAD-GCN reaches 99.02 vs. GCN's 97.97. Similarly, UniGAD surpasses all 9 edge-level baselines (GCNE through BWE) on most datasets, with UniGAD-BWG scoring 94.32 on T-Finance edge vs. BWE's 92.27. Against the 6 graph-level baselines (OCGIN, OCGTL, GLocalKD, iGAD, GmapAD, RQGNN), UniGAD-GCN achieves 99.75/94.29 on BM-MN node/graph, beating RQGNN (98.79) and iGAD (91.68/96.68). The multi-task prompt baselines GraphPrompt-U and All-in-One-U perform near random (49–55% AUROC) or time out entirely on large datasets, confirming that generic prompt-based multi-task learning is ill-suited for anomaly detection. UniGAD-GCN slightly underperforms the best single-level edge baseline on Reddit (65.46 vs. GSAGEE's 67.52), illustrating the trade-off of unified training, but UniGAD-BWG compensates with strong node-level results on the same dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate lines of graph anomaly detection (node-level, edge-level, graph-level) into a single framework, departing from the dominant paradigm where each object type is handled by specialized models. The MRQSampler builds on spectral anomaly detection insights (Rayleigh quotient as anomaly indicator) and GNN message-passing theory (rooted subtree structures), but introduces a novel dynamic programming solution to an NP-hard subgraph selection problem. The GraphStitch Network adapts cross-stitch networks from computer vision (Misra et al., 2016) to the graph domain, combined with gradient surgery (Yu et al., 2020) for multi-task loss balancing. Unlike prompt-based multi-task graph learning methods (GraphPrompt, All-in-One) that use generic k-hop induced subgraphs, UniGAD's spectral sampling is specifically designed to preserve anomaly-relevant information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly detect anomalies across node, edge, and graph levels, addressing a practical gap where real-world anomalies (e.g., money laundering) inherently span multiple graph object types. The zero-shot transferability—where a model trained only on node labels predicts edge anomalies better than some supervised edge-level baselines—suggests that cross-level information transfer is a previously underexploited signal in GAD. Key limitations include the node-centric GNN encoder potentially losing graph-level structural information, and the reliance on a shared unsupervised pre-training step (GraphMAE) whose interaction with multi-level supervision remains unexplored. Open questions include whether multi-level pre-training could further improve representations, and whether the spectral sampling principle generalizes beyond the tree-structured rooted subtrees assumed here.
