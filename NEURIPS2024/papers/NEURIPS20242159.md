## Problem

Existing Python programming benchmarks like HumanEval+ are saturating (GPT-4-turbo reaches 86.6% pass@1), and standard generative approaches for creating new problems merely sample from a stationary distribution, producing puzzles no harder or more diverse than those in the training data. The paper asks whether one can automate the generation of a diverse set of challenging-yet-solvable programming puzzles by steering a generative model with explicit goals over both difficulty and semantic diversity.

## Input / Output

ACES takes as input a target skill combination (up to 5 tags from a fixed list of 20 programming skills, yielding 21,700 possible niches) and three previously generated puzzles sampled from neighboring archive cells, all formatted as P3-style Python code blocks with difficulty scores. It outputs a set of five new P3-format puzzles (a test function f and solution function g such that f(g())==True), each accompanied by LLM-generated skill labels and an empirical difficulty score computed as the negative pass@1 over 50 solver attempts, rescaled to 0–100. Puzzles never solved in 50 attempts are discarded as unsolvable, and the valid ones are added to the archive at their labeled skill niche.

## Method

ACES is a goal-directed quality-diversity algorithm that extends Map-Elites by replacing undirected mutation with explicit goal sampling: at each iteration it uniformly samples a target skill-combination cell, identifies the three nearest filled cells, and selects one puzzle from each via a softmax over normalized fitness scores (temperature 0.2) to bias toward harder examples. These three puzzles, annotated with their difficulty scores, are given as in-context examples to a generator LLM (Llama-3-70B) prompted to produce five new puzzles that achieve the target skill combination and reach a difficulty score of 90–100. Each candidate is validated by running a solver LLM 50 times; solvable puzzles are independently described (to prevent label hacking) and then tagged with skill labels by a separate LLM call before being inserted into the archive. The key distinction from the ELM baseline is that ACES conditions generation on a specific target niche and prioritizes harder examples, creating a positive feedback loop where the growing archive of diverse, difficult puzzles provides increasingly strong in-context guidance for subsequent generations.

## Results

ACES generates puzzles that are on average three times more challenging than those in existing Python programming benchmarks across 11 state-of-the-art code LLMs. The best solver (Mixtral-8x22B-Instruct-v0.1) achieves only 47.3% pass@1 on ACES-generated problems, compared to 86.6% for GPT-4-turbo on HumanEval+. ACES fills up to 700 unique skill-combination niches (out of 21,700 possible), substantially outperforming all baselines in semantic diversity, average fitness, and QD score. In finetuning experiments, Llama-3-8B trained on ACES-ELM data achieves 53.3% pass@1 on the hardest test set, versus 49.4% (WizardCoder) and 41.6% (StaticGen).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACES and its variant ACES-ELM outperform all four baselines on both diversity and difficulty metrics. Against ELM (semantic), ACES fills significantly more niches (~700 vs. far fewer) and achieves higher average fitness and QD score, demonstrating that goal-directed exploration is more effective than undirected mutation for diversity. Against ELM-CVT (which optimizes diversity in codet5p embedding space), ACES wins on semantic diversity and deepseek-coder embedding diversity, while ELM-CVT only matches or exceeds ACES in its own codet5p embedding space — showing that ACES's semantic diversity transfers across representation spaces. Against Static Gen (random few-shot prompting without archive reuse), ACES dominates on all metrics: Static Gen consistently produces low-fitness puzzles and fills very few niches. In the finetuning comparison, ACES-ELM-generated data yields a 53.3% pass@1 for Llama-3-8B on the hardest test set, outperforming WizardCoder (49.4%) and StaticGen (41.6%), with the gap widening as test difficulty increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACES combines three research threads: the Map-Elites quality-diversity archive (Mouret & Clune, 2015), LLM-based evolutionary mutation (ELM, Lehman et al., 2022), and autotelic goal-directed exploration (Colas et al., 2022). The key departure from ELM is replacing undirected mutation with explicit goal sampling over a semantic skill-descriptor space, and replacing LLM-as-judge quality signals with a grounded empirical difficulty metric (pass@1 over 50 solver attempts). Compared to static few-shot generation methods (Unnatural Instructions, WizardCoder), ACES iteratively reuses its own archive as increasingly challenging in-context examples, creating a self-improving generation loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With HumanEval+ approaching saturation (GPT-4-turbo at 86.6% pass@1), the field needs self-calibrating benchmarks that scale in difficulty as models improve. ACES demonstrates that LLM-generated puzzles can be made 3× harder than existing benchmarks across 11 state-of-the-art solvers, and that difficulty transfers across models (correlation 0.83 with LiveCodeBench vs. 0.46 with HumanEval, suggesting HumanEval contamination). Open questions include the heredity assumption (whether harder parents reliably produce harder children), the cost of the 50-attempt difficulty evaluation, and whether difficulty alone captures meaningful puzzle quality beyond solvability.
