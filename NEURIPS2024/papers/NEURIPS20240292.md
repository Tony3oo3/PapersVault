## Problem

Prior multi-frame optical flow methods estimate consecutive flows in a pairwise manner, requiring multiple overlapping forward passes to derive T−1 flows from T frames, which introduces substantial computational redundancy. This redundancy is particularly costly for video sequences where continuous flow estimation is needed. The paper addresses how to perform effective spatiotemporal modeling under the constraint of non-overlapping in-batch estimation while maintaining efficiency comparable to two-frame methods.

## Input / Output

The method takes T consecutive video frames (typically 3 or 4) as input and produces all T−1 consecutive unidirectional optical flows between adjacent frame pairs in a single forward pass. For a sequence of N frames, the SIM pipeline divides them into non-overlapping groups of size T (sharing only the first frame of each group), reducing the number of forward passes from N−T+1 to ⌈(N−1)/(T−1)⌉. Cost volumes are computed only between adjacent frames rather than all frame pairs, and flows are initialized to zeros before iterative refinement over 12 decoder updates.

## Method

StreamFlow combines three key components: (1) the SIM pipeline, which eliminates redundant overlapping computation by predicting all unidirectional flows within a non-overlapping group in one pass; (2) the ISC module in the encoder, which reorganizes patch embeddings from consecutive frames along the spatial dimension and applies the existing Twins self-attention and feed-forward layers to the resulting spatiotemporal graph, introducing zero additional parameters; and (3) the GTR decoder, which uses a lightweight temporal transformer block to extract global temporal features from motion features of all frames, combined with spatial cross-attention and SKFlow's super convolution kernels for iterative flow refinement. The architecture uses a Twins transformer encoder and a CNN-based iterative decoder, trained with a weighted L1 loss across all flows in a group without self-supervised pre-training or bidirectional flow estimation.

## Results

Under the (C+)T cross-dataset generalization schedule, StreamFlow achieves 0.87 EPE on Sintel Clean and 2.11 on Sintel Final, outperforming the previous best (VideoFlow-BOF at 1.03 and 2.19) by 15.5% and 3.7% respectively. On KITTI, StreamFlow reaches 3.85 Fl-EPE and 12.6 Fl-all, surpassing TransFlow (3.98/14.4) and VideoFlow-BOF (3.96/15.3). Under the full (C+)T+S+K+H fine-tuning schedule, StreamFlow further improves to 0.28/0.38 EPE on Sintel Clean/Final and 4.24 Fl-all on KITTI, setting new state-of-the-art on all four test benchmarks. On the Spring dataset, StreamFlow achieves the best total score of 4.15 and EPE of 0.47 after fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreamFlow outperforms all 24 compared baselines on every reported test metric across Sintel, KITTI, and Spring. Against the closest multi-frame competitors, StreamFlow reduces Sintel Clean EPE by 0.16 (vs. TransFlow's 0.93) and 0.16 (vs. VideoFlow-BOF's 1.03) under the (C+)T schedule, and by 0.09 and 0.09 respectively under the full fine-tuning schedule. On KITTI Fl-all, StreamFlow achieves 12.6 under (C+)T, a 12.5% improvement over TransFlow (14.4) and 17.6% over VideoFlow-BOF (15.3), while also being 44.43% faster in inference latency (85.53 ms vs. 122.37 ms for VideoFlow-BOF). On the Spring benchmark, StreamFlow attains the best total score (4.15) and lowest EPE (0.47) among fine-tuned methods, surpassing MemFlow (4.48/0.47) and CroCo-Flow (4.57/0.50). In the occlusion analysis, StreamFlow improves unmatched-region EPE by 15.53% (clean) and 10.77% (final) over its own two-frame baseline Twins-SKFlow, and also beats TransFlow (6.77/10.96) and VideoFlow-BOF on unmatched regions. Notably, StreamFlow achieves these gains without self-supervised pre-training or bidirectional flow estimation, which TransFlow and VideoFlow-BOF respectively require.

## Relation to Prior Work

StreamFlow builds directly on the SKFlow decoder (super convolution kernels for motion encoding/updating) and the Twins transformer encoder, but departs fundamentally from prior multi-frame optical flow methods (VideoFlow, TransFlow, MFCFlow, MFRFlow) that estimate flows in overlapping pairwise groups with substantial redundant computation. The key conceptual departure is the SIM pipeline, which restructures the estimation into non-overlapping groups so that all consecutive unidirectional flows are predicted in a single forward pass, eliminating the T-1 redundant decoder passes. For temporal modeling under this constraint, StreamFlow introduces the parameter-free ISC module (reorganizing patches along the spatial dimension to exploit existing self-attention) and a lightweight GTR decoder, avoiding the heavy temporal transformers or self-supervised pre-training that TransFlow and VideoFlow rely on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 44.43% speedup while simultaneously improving accuracy (especially on occluded regions by ~15% on Sintel clean) makes multi-frame optical flow practically viable for real-time video applications like generation, editing, and compression, where latency is critical. The finding that effective spatiotemporal modeling can be achieved with zero additional parameters (ISC) challenges the assumption that temporal cues require dedicated heavy modules or pre-training. Open questions remain around inter-group information utilization (the SIM pipeline is confined to intra-group cues) and GPU memory during training (~40 GB for 4-frame settings), which limits scalability to longer sequences or higher resolutions.
